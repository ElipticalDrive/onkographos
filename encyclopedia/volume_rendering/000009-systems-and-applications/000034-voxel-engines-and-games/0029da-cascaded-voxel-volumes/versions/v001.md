# Cascaded Voxel Volumes

Also called: Cascaded Voxelization, Centered Voxels

## Overview
Cascaded Voxel Volumes is a method for Voxelized Global Illumination (VXGI) that extends the concept of cascaded shadow maps to 3D voxel grids. It uses multiple volumes with identical resolution but different scales, centered around the player, to vary coverage and spatial resolution [S1, unofficial]. Unlike single-volume approaches, it prioritizes high-fidelity voxelization near the eye while maintaining lower-detail coverage for distant indirect illumination [S2, unofficial]. It is distinct from [Voxel Space](3074-voxel-space.md) by explicitly managing multiple discrete resolution tiers rather than a single unified or sparse structure.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera position, camera orientation, scene geometry, number of cascades $N$, base voxel resolution $R$ (e.g., $128^3$), and cascade scale factors $s_i$ for each cascade $i \in [0, N-1]$.
*   **Output:** A set of $N$ 3D textures (voxel grids), each of size $R \times R \times R$, containing density and color data for the surrounding scene.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Cascade Grid:** An array of $N$ 3D textures. Each texture stores voxel data (e.g., density, albedo) at fixed resolution $R$.
*   **Cascade Transform:** For each cascade $i$, an axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) or orthographic projection frustum centered on the camera, scaled by $s_i$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Define Cascade Bounds:** For each cascade $i$, define a world-space volume centered at the camera position. The volume size is proportional to a scale factor $s_i$, where $s_0 < s_1 < \dots < s_{N-1}$. The innermost cascade ($i=0$) covers the closest region with highest detail; outer cascades cover progressively larger areas with the same voxel count, resulting in lower spatial resolution [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Voxelize Geometry:** For each cascade $i$:
    *   Render the scene geometry into an orthographic projection aligned with the cascade's axes.
    *   Rasterize fragments into the $R \times R \times R$ voxel grid.
    *   **Pitfall Mitigation (Color Swimming):** To prevent voxel colors from "swimming" as the camera moves (caused by slight camera shifts changing which fragments are rasterized), clamp the axis-aligned orthographic projection coordinates to world-space voxel-sized increments [S2, unofficial]. This stabilizes the voxelization grid relative to the world.
3.  **Handle Cascade Edges:**
    *   **Pitfall:** Discontinuities may appear at the boundaries between cascades.
    *   **Mitigation:** Apply linear interpolation between the values of adjacent cascades at their overlapping boundaries to smooth transitions [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Update Strategy:**
    *   Stagger updates of each cascade across multiple frames to reduce per-frame computational load [S1, unofficial].
    *   Prioritize updating the cascade closest to the player ($i=0$) to maintain visual quality where it matters most [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of cascades. Typical values depend on scene size and performance budget.
*   $R$: Resolution of each voxel grid. Commonly $128^3$ or $256^3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $s_i$: Scale factors for each cascade. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Color Swimming:** Caused by camera movement altering rasterization fragments. Fixed by clamping projections to voxel-sized world-space increments [S2, unofficial].
*   **Cascade Boundaries:** Discontinuities between different resolution levels. Mitigated by linear interpolation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Behind Camera:** Some voxels may remain behind the eye if objects in front cast indirect light from behind. This is acceptable as it contributes to global illumination [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Centered Voxels (Cascaded Voxelization)
A specific implementation variant where multiple 3D textures create cascades surrounding the eye, voxelizing only the geometry around the viewer rather than the entire scene [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by focusing on efficiency for open-world games by avoiding full-scene voxelization. Choose this when performance is critical and the scene is large. It shares the core definition with Cascaded Voxel Volumes but emphasizes the "centered" aspect and the specific fix for color swimming [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot G)$, where $N$ is the number of cascades and $G$ is the number of geometry primitives rendered per cascade. Staggering updates reduces effective per-frame cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot R^3)$, where $R$ is the resolution of each voxel grid. Since all cascades have identical resolution, memory usage scales linearly with $N$.
*   **Measured Performance:** Used in *The Tomorrow Children* on PS4 and NVIDIA’s VXGI in Unreal Engine 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient coverage of large scenes; high detail near the player; lower detail far away is acceptable for indirect illumination [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Discontinuities at cascade boundaries; color swimming artifacts if not clamped; complex implementation of staggered updates and edge interpolation [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Open-world games or large scenes where full-scene voxelization is too expensive, and specular/indirect illumination quality degrades with distance [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Small, contained scenes where a single high-resolution volume suffices, or when strict alias-free voxelization is required without interpolation artifacts.

## Background
The method was inspired by cascaded shadow maps, which center multiple shadow maps of increasing size around the eye to balance detail and coverage [S2, unofficial]. It was applied to VXGI by Q-Games for *The Tomorrow Children* on PS4 and by NVIDIA for their VXGI implementation in Unreal Engine 4 [S1, unofficial]. The technique emerged around 2012-2015 as a solution to the memory and performance constraints of full-scene voxelization in real-time rendering [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
