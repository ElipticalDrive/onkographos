# Volume Rendering based CGH Calculation

Also called: Direct volume rendering method for holograms, Approximate Volume Rendering Based CGH Calculation, Approximate Volume Rendering Based CGH Calculation with Elemental Holograms, Approximate volume rendering based CGH calculation method with elemental holograms

## Overview
Volume Rendering based CGH Calculation generates Computer-Generated Holograms (CGH) for 3D volume data by treating voxels as transparent objects with diffuse reflection properties [S3, unofficial]. Unlike Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) methods which only display objects with maximum voxel values, this approach simulates light propagation from a source to the voxel, diffuse reflection at the voxel, and propagation from the voxel to the viewpoint, allowing for the generation of clear holograms for transparent and multi-layer objects [S3, unofficial]. It serves as an alternative to polygon-based methods, offering superior detail for volumetric data at the cost of significantly higher computational complexity [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The method calculates the complex amplitude at the hologram plane by summing contributions from all voxels, modeled as point light sources after rendering. The process involves initializing voxel properties, simulating light transport with attenuation and diffuse reflection, and finally computing the holographic interference pattern.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Volume data grid of size $M \times M \times M$ (or general dimensions), where each voxel $i$ has a scalar value $I_i$.
    *   Color map and Alpha ($\alpha$) map definitions.
    *   Light source position and intensity $I_{d0}$.
    *   Viewpoint position (for the standard method) or Elemental Hologram grid parameters (for the approximate method).
    *   Wavelength $\lambda$ and wavenumber $k = 2\pi/\lambda$.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Complex amplitude distribution $\mu(\xi, \eta)$ for each pixel $(\xi, \eta)$ on the hologram plane.

### Data Structures
*   **Voxel Array:** Stores $R, G, B, \alpha$ for each voxel.
*   **Hologram Plane:** 2D grid of complex numbers.
*   **Elemental Holograms (Approximate Method):** A subset of the hologram plane, where each elemental hologram is represented by its center pixel for propagation calculations.

### Steps

1.  **Initialize Color and Opacity:**
    For each voxel $x$ with value $I_x$, calculate its color components $C_x$ (where $C \in \{R, G, B, \alpha\}$) using linear interpolation on the provided maps [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ C_x = C_3 - (C_3 - C_2) \frac{I_3 - I_x}{I_3 - I_2} $$
    where $C_2, C_3$ are known parameters of the color/alpha map at endpoints with voxel values $I_2, I_3$.

2.  **Light Propagation to Voxel (Attenuation):**
    For a voxel $eli$, calculate the incident light intensity $I_{di}$ from the light source. Light attenuates as it passes through preceding voxels $j$ along the path [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ I_{di} = I_{d0} \prod_{j=0} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $I_{d0}$ is the source intensity and $\alpha_j$ is the opacity of voxel $j$.

3.  **Diffuse Reflection Calculation:**
    Calculate the reflected light intensity $I_{ri}$ using the Lambertian reflection model [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ I_{ri} = k_{di} I_{di} \max(0, \mathbf{L} \cdot \mathbf{N}) $$
    where:
    *   $k_{di}$ is the ratio of Lambert light.
    *   $\mathbf{L}$ is the unit vector pointing from the voxel to the light source.
    *   $\mathbf{N}$ is the normal unit vector, derived from the gradient of the $\alpha$ map in $x, y, z$ directions.

4.  **Light Propagation to Viewpoint (Attenuation):**
    Calculate the final reflected light intensity $I_{rLi}$ reaching the viewpoint from voxel $eli$. Light attenuates through subsequent voxels $j$ between the voxel and the viewpoint [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ I_{rLi} = I_{ri} \prod_{j=i+1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    Note: $I_{di}$ and $I_{ri}$ are independent of the viewpoint direction, but $I_{rLi}$ must be recalculated for each viewpoint.

5.  **CGH Calculation (Point Light Source Method):**
    Treat each voxel as a point light source. The complex amplitude $\mu_i(\xi, \eta)$ contributed by voxel $i$ at hologram pixel $(\xi, \eta)$ is [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ \mu_i(\xi, \eta) = \frac{\sqrt{I_{rLi}}}{r_i} \exp\left(-j(k r_i + \phi_i)\right) $$
    where $r_i$ is the distance from voxel $i$ to the hologram pixel, and $\phi_i$ is the initial phase.
    The total complex amplitude $U_h(\xi, \eta)$ is the superposition of all voxels:
    $$ U_h(\xi, \eta) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \mu_i(\xi, \eta) $$

6.  **Approximate Calculation with Elemental Holograms (Optional Optimization):**
    To reduce computation, divide the hologram plane into $N_e$ elemental holograms. For each elemental hologram $k$, use only the center pixel to represent the entire elemental hologram for the final light propagation calculation [S3, unofficial]. The amplitude for elemental hologram $k$ is $I_{ek_{rLi}}$. The complexity is reduced from $O(M^2 N)$ to $O(M^2 N_e)$ for the propagation step [S3, unofficial].

    Calculate CGH using Volume Rendering based method.

        volume_data: 3D array of voxel values I.

        alpha_map: Function mapping voxel value I to Alpha alpha.

        # I_ri = k_di * I_di * max(0, L . N)

        k_di = 1.0

        # [G] Assume ray_march_attenuation handles the path from voxel to viewpoint

            'pos': pos,
            'intensity': I_rLi,
            'alpha': alpha # Needed for elemental hologram approximation if used
        })
        
    # Step 5: CGH Calculation
    # Superposition of wavefields
    for y in range(hologram_plane.shape[0]):
        for x in range(hologram_plane.shape[1]):
            pixel_pos = get_hologram_pixel_position(x, y)
            total_amplitude = 0.0 + 0.0j
            
            for vc in voxel_contributions:
                v_pos = vc['pos']
                I_rLi = vc['intensity']
                
                # Distance from voxel to hologram pixel

### Parameters
*   **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** Determines the wavenumber $k$. Typical values depend on the laser source (e.g., 532 nm for green).
*   **Color/Alpha Maps:** Define the mapping from voxel intensity to RGB and opacity.
*   **Number of Viewpoints ($n$):** The method calculates intensity for each voxel and each viewpoint.
*   **Number of Elemental Holograms ($N_e$):** Used in the approximate method. If $N_e$ is small relative to $N$, calculation time is greatly shortened [S3, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Computational Complexity:** The calculation amount is very large, making the method computationally expensive compared to polygon-based or MIP-based methods [S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Attenuation products $\prod (1-\alpha_j)$ can underflow if many semi-transparent voxels are traversed.
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Ray marching must handle voxels outside the volume grid gracefully.

### [View Volume Transformation Based Layered Method](12609-view-volume-transformation-based-layered-method.md)
A layered rendering method for generating computer-generated holograms (CGH) of 3D objects that uses view volume transformation to avoid shape distortion and size deviation in the reconstructed image, preserving the real size of the original model. Unlike conventional layer-based methods where sampling pitch in Fresnel diffraction is proportional to reconstruction distance (causing inconsistent sizes), this method preserves the real size of the original model.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:**
    *   Incident light calculation: $O(M^2)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Reflected light calculation: $O(M)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Final light propagation: $O(M^2 \cdot N_e)$ for the approximate method with elemental holograms, or $O(M^2 \cdot N)$ for the standard method [S3, unofficial].
    *   Total CGH calculation complexity: $O(M^2 \cdot N)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the number of voxels $M$ and hologram pixels $N$.
*   **Measured Performance:** In experiments, the proposed method took 24.3 hours for rendering and 3.8 minutes for CGH calculation, compared to 1.5 hours/0.3 seconds for polygon-based and 0.2 hours/0.6 seconds for MIP-based methods. The approximate method with elemental holograms reduced rendering time to 3.6 hours and CGH calculation to 3.7 minutes [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Generates clear holograms for transparent and multi-layer objects; better detail than polygon-based methods [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Extremely high computational cost; requires special lighting conditions for reconstruction (point monochrome light source at a specific angle) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Holography is a form of lensless photography based on the recording of amplitude and phase of light illuminating an object [S1, unofficial]. Computed holograms are created by calculating the interference pattern and recording it on film [S1, unofficial]. However, output devices often cannot transfer the pattern to film at the required resolution, and fully computed holograms are difficult and expensive to record [S1, unofficial]. This method was proposed to address the limitations of MIP-based methods, which only show objects with maximum voxel values, by applying direct volume rendering with transparency and diffuse reflection [S3, unofficial].
