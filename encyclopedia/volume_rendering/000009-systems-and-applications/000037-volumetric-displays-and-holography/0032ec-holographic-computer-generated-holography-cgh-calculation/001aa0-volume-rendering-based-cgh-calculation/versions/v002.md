# Volume Rendering based CGH Calculation

Also called: Direct volume rendering method for holograms, Approximate Volume Rendering Based CGH Calculation, Approximate Volume Rendering Based CGH Calculation with Elemental Holograms, Approximate volume rendering based CGH calculation method with elemental holograms

## Overview
This method generates Computer-Generated Holograms (CGHs) for volumetric data by treating voxels as transparent objects with diffuse reflection, enabling the reconstruction of transparent and multi-layer objects [S3, unofficial]. It differs from Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP)-based methods, which only display objects with maximum voxel values, by simulating light propagation and attenuation to preserve depth and transparency cues [S3, unofficial]. The approximate variant reduces computational cost by dividing the hologram into elemental holograms and calculating final light propagation only once per elemental hologram using its center pixel [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume data: A 3D grid of voxels with scalar values $I$ [S3, unofficial].
*   Color map and Alpha ($\alpha$) map: Lookup tables mapping scalar values to RGB color components and opacity [S3, unofficial].
*   Hologram parameters: Number of hologram pixels $N$, number of voxels $M$, number of viewpoints $n$, and number of elemental holograms $N_e$ (for the approximate method) [S3, unofficial].
*   Optical parameters: Light source intensity $I_{d0}$, wavelength (implicit in phase calculation), and geometry (light source position, viewpoint positions) [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Complex amplitude distribution $\mu(\xi, \eta)$ for the CGH, which is typically quantized to phase values $0$ to $2\pi$ for display on an LCOS-SLM [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   Voxel Grid: 3D array of size $D_x \times D_y \times D_z$ storing scalar values $I$ [S3, unofficial].
*   Color/Alpha Maps: Arrays mapping scalar indices to $(R, G, B, \alpha)$ tuples [S3, unofficial].
*   CGH Plane: 2D array of complex numbers or phase values [S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialize Color and Opacity:**
    For each voxel $x$ with value $I_x$, calculate its color components $C_x$ (where $C \in \{R, G, B, \alpha\}$) using linear interpolation between two known endpoints $(I_2, C_2)$ and $(I_3, C_3)$ from the map [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ C_x = C_3 - \frac{(C_3 - C_2)(I_3 - I_x)}{I_3 - I_2} $$
    This redefines each voxel as a transparent object with specific RGB and $\alpha$ values [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Render Each Voxel (Diffuse Reflection Model):**
    For each voxel $i$ and each viewpoint, calculate the light intensity in three parts [S3, unofficial]:

    *   **Part 1: Light Propagation from Source to Voxel:**
        Calculate the incident light intensity $I_{di}$ reaching voxel $i$ after attenuation by preceding voxels $j$ along the path from the light source [S3, unofficial]:
        $$ I_{di} = I_{d0} \times \prod_{j} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
        where $I_{d0}$ is the source intensity and $\alpha_j$ is the opacity of voxel $j$ [S3, unofficial]. Note that $I_{di}$ and the reflected intensity $I_{ri}$ are independent of the viewpoint direction [S3, unofficial].

    *   **Part 2: Calculation of Reflected Light:**
        Calculate the intensity of the reflected light $I_{ri}$ using the Lambertian diffuse reflection model [S3, unofficial]:
        $$ I_{ri} = k_{di} I_{di} \max(0, \mathbf{L} \cdot \mathbf{N}) $$
        where $k_{di}$ is the Lambert light ratio, $\mathbf{L}$ is the unit vector pointing to the light source, and $\mathbf{N}$ is the normal unit vector derived from the gradient of $\alpha$ in the $x, y, z$ directions [S3, unofficial].

    *   **Part 3: Light Propagation from Voxel to Viewpoint:**
        Calculate the final reflected light intensity $I_{rLi}$ reaching the viewpoint, accounting for attenuation by subsequent voxels $j$ between the voxel and the viewpoint [S3, unofficial]:
        $$ I_{rLi} = I_{ri} \times \prod_{j=i+1}^{M} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
        This step must be recalculated for each viewpoint [S3, unofficial].

3.  **Calculate Hologram Data (Point Light Source Method):**
    Treat each voxel as a point light source [S3, unofficial]. For a point light source $i$, calculate the complex amplitude distribution $\mu(\xi, \eta)$ on the hologram plane [S3, unofficial]:
    $$ \mu_i(\xi, \eta) = \frac{\sqrt{I_{rLi}}}{r_i} \exp(-j(k r_i + \phi_i)) $$
    where $I_{rLi}$ is the final intensity calculated in Step 2, $r_i$ is the distance from voxel $i$ to the hologram pixel, $k$ is the wavenumber, and $\phi_i$ is the initial phase [S3, unofficial]. The total CGH is the superposition of amplitudes from all voxels [S3, unofficial].

4.  **Approximate Calculation with Elemental Holograms (Optional):**
    To reduce complexity, divide the hologram into $N_e$ elemental holograms [S3, unofficial]. Calculate the final light propagation $I_{rLi}$ only once for each elemental hologram using the center pixel of that elemental hologram to represent the entire region [S3, unofficial]. The amplitude for voxel $i$ in elemental hologram $k$ is $I_{ek}^{rLi}$ [S3, unofficial]. For all pixels in elemental hologram $k$, the amplitude of point light source $i$ is assumed constant [S3, unofficial]. If $N_e$ is small enough relative to $N$, the calculation time of final light propagation is greatly shortened [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cgh(voxel_grid, color_map, alpha_map, light_source_pos, viewpoint_pos, N, Ne):
    M = len(voxel_grid)
    cg_h_plane = np.zeros((N, N), dtype=complex)
    # Step 1: Initialize color and opacity
    for x in voxel_grid:
        x.color = interpolate_color(x.value, color_map)
        x.alpha = interpolate_alpha(x.value, alpha_map)
    # Step 2 & 3: Render and calculate hologram
    for i in range(M):
        # Part 1: Incident light
        I_di = I_d0
        for j in range(i): # voxels between source and i
            I_di *= (1 - voxel_grid[j].alpha)
        # Part 2: Reflected light
        L = normalize(light_source_pos - voxel_grid[i].pos)
        N_vec = gradient_alpha(voxel_grid[i].pos)
        I_ri = k_di * I_di * max(0, dot(L, N_vec))
        # Part 3: Final propagation to viewpoint
        I_rLi = I_ri
        for j in range(i+1, M): # voxels between i and viewpoint
            I_rLi *= (1 - voxel_grid[j].alpha)
        # CGH superposition
        r_i = distance(voxel_grid[i].pos, hologram_pixel)
        cg_h_plane += (sqrt(I_rLi) / r_i) * exp(-1j * (k * r_i + phi_i))
    return cg_h_plane
```

**Parameters:**
*   Number of iterations for CGH optimization (e.g., Gerchberg-Saxton): 50 [S2].
*   Maximum spatial frequency: 4 lp/mm [S2].
*   Reflection Model: Lambertian diffuse reflection [S3, unofficial].
*   Number of viewpoints $n$: Varies by application [S3, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   The calculation amount is very large, making the method computationally expensive compared to polygon-based or MIP-based methods [S3, unofficial].
*   Output devices may not transfer the pattern to film at the required resolution, necessitating multiplex holograms or elemental hologram approximations [S1, unofficial].
*   Holograms may require special lighting conditions, specifically a point monochrome light source at a specific angle, for reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:**
    *   Standard Method: Incident light $O(M^2)$, Reflected light $O(M)$, Final light propagation $O(M^2 \cdot N)$, CGH calculation $O(M \cdot N)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Approximate Method: Incident light $O(M^2)$, Reflected light $O(M)$, Final light propagation $O(M^2 \cdot N_e)$, CGH calculation $O(M^2 \cdot N)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Note: Sources [S3, unofficial] list CGH calculation complexity as $O(M \cdot N)$ for the standard method and $O(M^2 \cdot N)$ for the approximate method, which appears inconsistent with the text description of superposition [S3, unofficial]. The text states "If $N_e$ is small enough relative to $N$, the calculation time of final light propagation is greatly shortened. Generally, this method is 5 to 6 times faster than the standard proposed method" [S3, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   Standard proposed method: 24.3 hours rendering, 3.8 minutes CGH calculation [S3, unofficial].
    *   Approximate method with Elemental Holograms: 3.6 hours rendering, 3.7 minutes CGH calculation [S3, unofficial].
    *   Comparison: Polygon-based (1.5s rendering, 0.3s CGH) and MIP-based (0.2s rendering, 0.6s CGH) are significantly faster but lack transparency/multi-layer detail [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Generates clear holograms for transparent and multi-layer objects [S3, unofficial].
    *   Parallel generation of focal points increases voxel density per frame (e.g., 4 focal points at 20 fps) [S2].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   High computational cost [S3, unofficial].
    *   Requires high-resolution output devices or multiplexing/elemental approximations [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Holography is a form of lensless photography based on recording the amplitude and phase of light [S1, unofficial]. Computed holograms are created by calculating interference patterns, but output devices often cannot transfer these patterns to film at the required resolution [S1, unofficial]. Multiplex holograms overcome this by rendering a series of perspective images stored on the same hologram using multiple exposures, with Benton's Alcove holograms using approximately 1000 images [S1, unofficial]. The volume rendering based CGH calculation method was proposed to generate holograms for volume data using direct volume rendering and a point light source method, addressing the limitations of MIP-based methods which only show maximum voxel values [S3, unofficial]. The approximate method with elemental holograms was introduced to reduce the huge computational load, based on the elemental hologram concept proposed by Dr. Ichikawa [S3, unofficial].
