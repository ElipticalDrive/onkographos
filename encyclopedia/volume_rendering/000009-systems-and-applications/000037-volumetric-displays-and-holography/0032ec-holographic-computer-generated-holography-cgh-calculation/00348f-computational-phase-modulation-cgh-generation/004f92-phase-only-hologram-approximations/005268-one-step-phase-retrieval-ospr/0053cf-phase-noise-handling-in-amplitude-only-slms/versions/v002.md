# Phase Noise Handling in Amplitude-Only SLMs

Also called: Phase Noise Mitigation in Amplitude-Only SLMs

## Overview
This method models and mitigates phase noise inherent to liquid crystal amplitude-only Spatial Light Modulators (SLMs) by analyzing the coupling between amplitude modulation and unwanted phase modulation [S1]. It establishes a mathematical framework using Jacobi-Anger expansions to separate signal terms from noise terms in the Fourier domain, allowing for the design of grating frequencies that prevent noise overlap with the reconstructed signal [S1]. It sets it apart from sibling methods by providing a rigorous analytical derivation of noise spectra based on the specific physical properties of the liquid crystal, rather than relying solely on empirical averaging or iterative phase retrieval [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Input**: The amplitude modulation function $f(x)$ applied to the SLM pixels [S1].
- **Output**: A derived expression for the wave field at the SLM plane identifying signal and noise components, and a constraint on the grating phase frequency to avoid aliasing [S1].

**Data Structures**
- **Amplitude Function**: Represented as a Fourier series $f(x) = \sum_k F_k \cos(kx + \phi_k)$, where $F_k$ are amplitude coefficients and $\phi_k$ are phase coefficients for spatial frequency $k$ [S1].
- **Phase Noise Model**: Unwanted phase modulation is modeled as a second-order polynomial of the amplitude: $\exp(i p_1 f(x) + i p_2 f(x)^2)$, where $p_1$ and $p_2$ are coefficients characterizing the liquid crystal's phase-amplitude coupling [S1].

**Steps**
1. **Model the Wave Field**: Define the complex wave field $U(x)$ at the SLM plane as the product of the amplitude modulation and the unwanted phase modulation:
   $$ U(x) = f(x) e^{i p_1 f(x) + i p_2 f(x)^2} $$
   [S1]

2. **Expand Phase Terms**: Apply the Jacobi-Anger expansion, $e^{iz \cos \theta} = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} i^n J_n(z) e^{in\theta}$, to the exponential terms, where $J_n(z)$ is the $n$-th Bessel function of the first kind [S1]. (Note: The source text contains an OCR typo in the summation limits; the standard identity is used here.)

3. **Substitute Fourier Series**: Substitute the Fourier series expansion of $f(x)$ into the phase terms [S1].

4. **Derive Expanded Expression**: The full expanded wave field is given by:
   $$ U(x) = f(x) \prod_k \sum_n i^n J_n(p_1 F_k) e^{in(kx + \phi_k)} \prod_{k,l} \sum_n i^n J_n\left(\frac{p_2 F_k F_l}{2}\right) e^{in((k+l)x + \phi_k + \phi_l)} \prod_{k,l} \sum_n i^n J_n\left(\frac{p_2 F_k F_l}{2}\right) e^{in((k-l)x + \phi_k - \phi_l)} $$
   [S1]

5. **Approximate for Small Parameters**: In typical experimental conditions, $p_1, p_2, F_k < 1$ and $|n| \ll 1$ [S1]. Under these conditions, higher-order Bessel functions $J_n(z)$ for $|n| \gg 1$ are negligible [S1]. The expression approximates to:
   $$ U(x) \approx f(x) \prod_k J_0(p_1 F_k) \prod_{k,l} J_0\left(\frac{p_2 F_k F_l}{2}\right) \left[ \sum_m J_1(p_1 F_m) e^{i(mx + \phi_m)} + \sum_{m,n} 2i J_1\left(\frac{p_2 F_m F_n}{2}\right) e^{i((m+n)x + \phi_m + \phi_n)} + O(p_1 F_k^2) + O(p_2 F_k^2) \right] $$
   [S1]
   *Note: The source's Eq. (6) contains typographical errors in Bessel indices and Fourier coefficients; the corrected structure above preserves the derivation's intent for implementation.*

6. **Identify Noise Terms**: Analyze the frequency components of the expanded expression [S1]. The signal term is typically the first-order diffraction $e^{ik_{prism}x}$ [S1]. Noise terms arise from higher-order interactions, such as $e^{ik_{pitch}x - 2ik_{prism}x}$, where $k_{pitch}$ is the wavenumber of the SLM pixel pitch and $k_{prism}$ is the grating frequency [S1].

7. **Set Grating Frequency Constraint**: To ensure noise terms do not overlap with the signal term in the Fourier domain, set the grating phase frequency $k_{prism}$ to one-quarter or less of the spatial frequency of the pixel pitch $k_{pitch}$:
   $$ k_{prism} \le \frac{1}{4} k_{pitch} $$
   [S1]

**Parameters**
- **$p_1, p_2$**: Phase noise coefficients. Values are typically $< 1$ in experimental setups [S1].
- **$F_k$**: Fourier coefficients of the amplitude pattern. Values are typically $< 1$ [S1].
- **Grating Frequency**: Must be $\le 1/4$ of the pixel pitch spatial frequency [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Noise Overlap**: If the grating phase frequency is set to one-third of the spatial frequency of the pixel pitch, the signal term $e^{ik_{prism}x}$ overlaps with the noise term $e^{ik_{pitch}x - 2ik_{prism}x}$, making the noise unfilterable [S1].
- **High-Order Terms**: If $p_1 F_k$ or $p_2 F_k F_l$ are not small, the approximation in Step 5 fails, and higher-order Bessel terms must be retained, increasing computational complexity [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import jv

def calculate_wave_field_spectrum(fourier_coeffs, p1, p2, k_max):
    """
    Calculate spectral components of the wave field at an amplitude-only SLM
    with phase noise, using the Jacobi-Anger expansion.
    
    Parameters:
    fourier_coeffs: dict mapping spatial frequency k to (amplitude F_k, phase phi_k)
    p1: Phase noise linear coefficient
    p2: Phase noise quadratic coefficient
    k_max: Maximum spatial frequency to consider for truncation
    
    Returns:
    spectrum: dict mapping spatial frequency k to complex amplitude
    """
    spectrum = {}
    ks = [k for k in fourier_coeffs.keys() if abs(k) <= k_max]
    
    # Global scaling factor from zero-order Bessel terms in the approximation [S1]
    # U_0 = prod_k J_0(p1 * F_k) * prod_{k,l} J_0(p2 * F_k * F_l / 2)
    # This factor scales all spectral components uniformly.
    
    for k in ks:
        F_k, phi_k = fourier_coeffs[k]
        
        # Linear phase noise term (n=1 dominant) [S1]
        if abs(p1 * F_k) < 1.0:
            coeff = jv(1, p1 * F_k)
            freq = k
            phase = phi_k
            spectrum[freq] = spectrum.get(freq, 0) + coeff * np.exp(1j * phase)
            
        # Quadratic phase noise terms (n=1 dominant) [S1]
        for l in ks:
            F_l, phi_l = fourier_coeffs[l]
            arg = p2 * F_k * F_l / 2
            if abs(arg) < 1.0:
                coeff = jv(1, arg)
                
                # (k+l) term
                freq_sum = k + l
                phase_sum = phi_k + phi_l
                spectrum[freq_sum] = spectrum.get(freq_sum, 0) + 2j * coeff * np.exp(1j * phase_sum)
                
                # (k-l) term
                freq_diff = k - l
                phase_diff = phi_k - phi_l
                spectrum[freq_diff] = spectrum.get(freq_diff, 0) + 2j * coeff * np.exp(1j * phase_diff)
                
    return spectrum

def check_grating_constraint(k_prism, k_pitch):
    """
    Verify grating frequency avoids noise overlap [S1].
    """
    return k_prism <= 0.25 * k_pitch
```

## Subtopics

### One Step Phase Retrieval (OSPR)
A method to obtain amplitude in a phase-only device by playing back several holograms with randomized object phases in random succession, allowing noise to average out [G]. It sets itself apart by relying on temporal averaging rather than analytical spectral separation [G]. Choose it when hardware supports rapid sequential hologram display and phase noise is stochastic rather than deterministic [G]. [Link](14717-one-step-phase-retrieval-ospr.md)

### Diffraction-Engineered Holography
A broader approach that uses the physical properties of the SLM and optical system to engineer the diffraction pattern, often incorporating phase noise models [S1]. It sets itself apart by treating the SLM's physical response as part of the optical transfer function rather than a defect to be filtered [S1]. Choose it when designing custom holographic displays with specific SLM hardware constraints [S1]. [Link](14717-one-step-phase-retrieval-ospr.md)

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: The analytical derivation is $O(1)$ [G]. The numerical evaluation of the spectrum depends on the number of Fourier components $N$ [G]. The pseudocode is $O(N^2)$ due to the double loop over $k$ and $l$ [G].
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(N)$ to store the Fourier coefficients and the resulting spectrum [G].
- **Measured Performance**: In experiments using an IRIS-U62 LCoS SLM (3840 × 2160 resolution, 3.6 μm pixel pitch, binned 2×2 to 7.2 μm effective pitch), the method successfully mitigated phase noise by setting the grating frequency to $\le 1/4$ of the pixel pitch spatial frequency [S1]. The distance between minimum and maximum depths was set to 2 mm, with dispersion diameters of 25 (red), 20 (green), and 18 pixels (blue) under 2 mm light propagation [S1].
- **Strengths**: Provides a rigorous physical model for phase noise; allows for precise design of grating frequencies to avoid aliasing; analytical solution avoids iterative computation [S1].
- **Limitations**: Relies on the assumption that $p_1, p_2, F_k < 1$; higher-order terms may become significant for large amplitude modulations or strong phase coupling; requires accurate characterization of $p_1$ and $p_2$ for the specific SLM [S1].
- **When to Use**: When using amplitude-only SLMs for holography and phase noise is a significant artifact; when analytical design of the grating frequency is preferred over empirical tuning [G].
- **When Not to Use**: When the SLM has negligible phase noise; when the amplitude modulation is large enough to violate the small argument approximation for Bessel functions [G].

## Background
This method originates from research into diffraction-engineered holography, specifically addressing the limitations of amplitude-only SLMs in volumetric displays and holographic computer-generated holography [S1]. The work was published in *Nature Communications* in 2022 by researchers investigating the depth representation limit of holographic displays [S1].
