# Digital Hologram Numerical Reconstruction
Also called: ASM, Angular Spectrum Method, Fresnel Method

## Overview
Digital Hologram Numerical Reconstruction computes the complex wavefield at a specific reconstruction plane from a loaded digital hologram matrix [S1]. It solves the problem of simulating optical propagation to retrieve the amplitude and phase of a 3D scene from its 2D holographic recording. It differs from physical reconstruction [Holographic Reconstruction System Setup](13993-holographic-reconstruction-system-setup.md) by performing these calculations via algorithms rather than physical light propagation through lenses and filters.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves propagating the complex field $U_h(x,y)$ defined on the hologram plane to a target plane at distance $z$. The propagation is modeled using either the Angular Spectrum Method (ASM) or the Fresnel approximation.

**Angular Spectrum Method (ASM):**
The ASM is an exact solution to the scalar wave equation under the paraxial approximation. It operates in the spatial frequency domain.
1.  Compute the 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) of the input hologram $U_h(x,y)$ to obtain the angular spectrum $A(f_x, f_y)$:
    $$ A(f_x, f_y) = \mathcal{F}\{U_h(x,y)\} $$
2.  Multiply by the transfer function $H(f_x, f_y, z)$, which represents the propagation of each spatial frequency component over distance $z$:
    $$ H(f_x, f_y, z) = \exp\left(i 2\pi z \sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda^2} - f_x^2 - f_y^2}\right) $$
    where $\lambda$ is the wavelength, and $f_x, f_y$ are spatial frequencies. If the term under the square root is negative (evanescent waves), the component is typically set to zero.
3.  Compute the inverse FFT to obtain the reconstructed field $U_z(x,y)$:
    $$ U_z(x,y) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\{A(f_x, f_y) H(f_x, f_y, z)\} $$

**Fresnel Method:**
The Fresnel approximation assumes that the propagation distance $z$ is large compared to the wavelength and the transverse dimensions, allowing the square root in the ASM transfer function to be approximated:
$$ \sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda^2} - f_x^2 - f_y^2} \approx \frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{\lambda}{2}(f_x^2 + f_y^2) $$
The transfer function becomes:
$$ H_{Fresnel}(f_x, f_y, z) = \exp\left(i \frac{2\pi z}{\lambda}\right) \exp\left(-i \pi \lambda z (f_x^2 + f_y^2)\right) $$
The reconstruction is performed via:
$$ U_z(x,y) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{ \mathcal{F}\{U_h(x,y)\} \cdot H_{Fresnel}(f_x, f_y, z) \right\} $$
Alternatively, this can be computed using a convolution integral in the spatial domain, but the FFT-based approach is standard for computational efficiency.

**Parameters:**
*   **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** Must match the recording or intended reconstruction wavelength [S1].
*   **Pixel Pitch ($\Delta x$):** Determines the spatial frequency sampling and the field of view in the reconstruction plane [S1].
*   **Reconstruction Distance ($z$):** The axial distance from the hologram plane to the reconstruction plane [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Holographic Reconstruction System Setup](13993-holographic-reconstruction-system-setup.md)
This method defines the physical optical configuration required to reconstruct complex wavefields from computer-generated holograms (CGH) projected onto spatial light modulators (SLMs). It solves the problem of converting digital hologram data into a visible 3D image by managing illumination, diffraction order separation, and aberration correction. It differs from numerical reconstruction by handling the physical propagation of light through lenses and filters rather than simulating it via algorithms. See [Holographic Reconstruction System Setup](13993-holographic-reconstruction-system-setup.md).

### Complex Magnitude Extraction & Histogram Enhancement
This minor variant focuses on post-processing the reconstructed complex field to produce a visible intensity image. It extracts the complex magnitude $|U_z(x,y)|$ from the reconstructed wavefield. To enhance contrast, it applies linear histogram stretching after clipping extreme intensity values at a user-defined percentile threshold. This removes outliers and improves the visibility of the reconstructed features. See [S1].

## Background
Digital Hologram Numerical Reconstruction is a fundamental technique in computer-generated holography (CGH) and digital holography. It allows for the computational retrieval of 3D information from 2D holograms without the need for physical reference beams during reconstruction. The Angular Spectrum Method and Fresnel approximation are standard tools in computational optics, widely implemented in software tools for holographic processing. Recent advances have integrated these methods into configurable pipelines, allowing users to select between ASM and Fresnel methods based on accuracy and performance requirements [S1].
