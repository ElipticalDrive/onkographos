# Holographic Reconstruction System Setup

Also called: Experimental setup for holographic reconstruction, Optical setup for holographic reconstruction, Phase-Only Holographic Display

## Overview
This method defines the physical optical configuration required to reconstruct complex wavefields from computer-generated holograms (CGH) projected onto spatial light modulators (SLMs). It solves the problem of converting digital hologram data into a visible 3D image by managing illumination, diffraction order separation, and aberration correction. It differs from numerical reconstruction [Digital Hologram Numerical Reconstruction](13798-digital-hologram-numerical-reconstruction.md) by handling the physical propagation of light through lenses and filters rather than simulating it via algorithms.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Laser light source(s) with specific wavelengths.
*   Digital hologram matrix (complex or phase-only).
*   SLM device parameters (resolution, pixel pitch).
*   Calibration coefficients (if aberrations are present).

**Outputs:**
*   Reconstructed complex wavefront captured by a camera or observed by an eye.
*   Intensity distribution $|U(x,y)|$ at the reconstruction plane.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Hologram Matrix:** 2D array of pixel values. For phase-only SLMs, complex values are encoded into phase using Burch encoding [S2].
*   **Calibration Coefficients:** A set of parameters $a_{jo}$ associated with specific object points or focal positions, used to correct display aberrations [S3].

**Steps:**

1.  **Illumination Preparation:**
    *   Filter the laser beam using a pinhole to remove spatial noise.
    *   Collimate the beam using a lens to ensure plane wave illumination [S1].
    *   If using multiple wavelengths (e.g., RGB), ensure sequential or simultaneous illumination as supported by the SLM [S2].

2.  **SLM Configuration and Encoding:**
    *   Load the hologram onto the SLM.
    *   If the SLM is phase-only but the hologram is complex, apply Burch encoding to project the complex wavefield onto real phase values [S2].
    *   If the SLM has a small pixel pitch causing crosstalk, upsample the hologram by assigning the same pixel value to $2 \times 2$ neighboring pixels to behave as a lower-resolution amplitude-only device [S2].

3.  **Aberration Calibration (Optional but recommended for high fidelity):**
    *   Present a single object point $o_i$ to the user/camera.
    *   Adjust coefficients $a_{jo}$ of the hologram representation $f_z(\rho, \theta)_{o_i}$ until the spot is tightly focused [S3].
    *   Repeat for a sparse set of object points (e.g., 15 for highly aberrated systems, 1 for low aberration) [S3].
    *   Interpolate coefficients for other points, as aberrations vary smoothly [S3].
    *   For true 3D displays, calibrate at multiple focal positions (e.g., four) and interpolate across the focal range [S3].

4.  **Optical Propagation and Filtering:**
    *   Illuminate the SLM with the collimated laser beam.
    *   Pass the modulated light through a first lens group to form the Fourier plane [S2].
    *   At the Fourier plane, use an adjustable 2D slit to block the zeroth-order diffraction and higher-order diffractions generated by the grating phase [S2].
    *   Remove speckle noise by rotating a holographic diffuser at the focused spot of the laser beams [S2].

5.  **Reconstruction and Capture:**
    *   Allow the first-order diffraction to propagate to the reconstruction plane.
    *   Use a second lens group to image the reconstructed wavefront onto a digital camera [S2].
    *   Capture the intensity distribution at specific shooting distances (e.g., 155, 160, 165, 170 mm) to verify focus and 3D effect [S1].

**Parameters and Typical Values:**
*   **Wavelengths ($\lambda$):** 638 nm (Red), 515 nm (Green), 460 nm (Blue) [S2]. Or 532 nm (Green) [S1].
*   **SLM Resolution:** 3840x2160 (UHD) or 1920x1080 (FHD) [S2]. 2000x2000 [S1].
*   **Pixel Pitch ($\Delta x$):** 3.6 $\mu$m (native), 7.2 $\mu$m (effective in 1080p mode) [S2]. 3.74 $\mu$m [S1].
*   **Grating Period:** 0.25 of the maximum period to avoid noise [S2].
*   **Depth Range:** 2 mm distance between min and max depths [S2].
*   **Shooting Distances:** 155, 160, 165, 170 mm [S1].

**Formulas:**
The approximation for phase modulation when $p_1 F_k$ and $p_2 F_k$ are small ($\ll 1$) is given by [S2]:
$$
f(x)e^{ip_1 f(x) + ip_2 f(x)} \approx f(x) \prod_k J_0(p_1 F_k) \prod_{k,l} J_0(p_2 F_k F_l / 2) \left[ \sum_i J_i(p_1 F_1) e^{i(mx + \phi_m)} + \sum_{m,n} 2i J_m(p_1 F_1 F_n / 2) e^{i((m+n)x + \phi_m + \phi_n)} + e^{i((m-n)x + \phi_m - \phi_n)} \right] + O(p_1 F_k^2) + O(p_2 F_k^2)
$$
Where $J_n$ is the Bessel function of the first kind of order $n$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Pixel Crosstalk:** Small pixel pitches can cause crosstalk. Mitigated by binning pixels ($2 \times 2$) [S2].
*   **Diffraction Dispersion:** Different wavelengths disperse differently. Under 2 mm propagation, dispersion diameters are 25 pixels (red), 20 (green), 18 (blue) [S2].
*   **Minimum Propagation Distance:** Must be $> 1.7$ mm to ensure defocus blur diameter (max 15 pixels) is larger than dispersion blur [S2].
*   **Aberration Measurement:** It is impractical to measure coefficients for all millions of object points. Interpolation is required [S3].

## Subtopics

### Calibration via Coefficient Adjustment
A method to correct optical aberrations by user feedback. The user adjusts coefficients $a_{jo}$ for specific object points $o_i$ until the holographic spot is sharpest. This is done for a sparse set of points (15 for high aberration, 1 for low) and interpolated. For 3D displays, calibration is performed at multiple focal positions and interpolated across depth [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not applicable to the optical setup itself. Hologram calculation is complex (quadruple summation), taking seconds to tens of seconds on high-end GPUs for 2 million points [S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on hologram resolution (e.g., 2000x2000 or 3840x2160) [S1, S2].
*   **Strengths:** Enables true 3D reconstruction with depth cues; Burch encoding allows complex wavefield projection on phase-only SLMs [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires precise optical alignment; speckle noise from laser coherence; limited depth range due to diffraction dispersion and pixel pitch [S2].
*   **When to use:** When physical 3D visualization is required, especially for near-eye displays or volumetric displays.
*   **When not to use:** When computational efficiency is paramount and physical hardware is unavailable; use numerical reconstruction instead.

## Background
Holographic reconstruction systems have evolved from optical holography to computer-generated holography (CGH). Early systems used photographic plates; modern systems use Liquid Crystal on Silicon (LCoS) SLMs. The use of Burch encoding for phase-only SLMs and the challenge of diffraction dispersion in multi-wavelength displays are key developments in recent literature [S2, S3]. The specific setup described here is based on experimental implementations for near-eye displays and volumetric rendering [S1, S2, S3].
