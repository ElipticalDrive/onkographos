# Acousto-Optic Modulator R-Factor Analysis

Also called: AOM Eccentricity Calculation, Beam Spread Ratio Analysis

## Overview
This method evaluates the performance of an Acousto-Optic Modulator (AOM) by calculating the R-factor, defined as the ratio of the acoustical beam spread to the optical beam spread [S1, unofficial]. It determines the output beam eccentricity (ellipticity) to ensure optimal diffraction efficiency and beam quality in scanned volumetric displays [S1, unofficial]. It focuses on intrinsic beam shaping at the modulator stage rather than geometric alignment of multiple scanner channels [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Optical beam radius ($\omega_0$), acoustic center frequency ($f_a$), acoustic velocity ($V$), refractive index ($n$), and acoustic wavelength ($\lambda_a$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** R-factor ($R$), beam eccentricity ($e$), and beam diameter ratio ($d_x/d_y$) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
No persistent data structures are required [S1, unofficial]. The method is a direct calculation based on physical constants and input beam parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters and Constants**
Typical values for TeO$_2$ modulators used in NEOS scanners [S1, unofficial]:
*   Refractive index ($n$): 2.26 [S1, unofficial].
*   Acoustic velocity ($V$): 4.2 mm/$\mu$s [S1, unofficial].
*   Transducer width ($L$): 0.67 mm [S1, unofficial].
*   Acoustic center frequencies ($f_a$): 440 MHz or 260 MHz [S1, unofficial].
*   Acoustic wavelengths ($\lambda_a$): 442 nm, 514 nm, or 647 nm [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**
The R-factor is calculated as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ R = \frac{\lambda_a f_a}{n V \omega_0} $$
Where:
*   $\lambda_a$ is the acoustic wavelength [S1, unofficial].
*   $f_a$ is the acoustic center frequency [S1, unofficial].
*   $n$ is the refractive index [S1, unofficial].
*   $V$ is the acoustic velocity [S1, unofficial].
*   $\omega_0$ is the optical beam radius (waist) [S1, unofficial].

For the specific case of a 440-MHz modulator with 514-nm light, this simplifies to [S1, unofficial]:
$$ R = 0.0983 \omega_0 $$
Note: In this simplified equation, $\omega_0$ must be in microns to yield a dimensionless R-factor [S1, unofficial].

**Eccentricity Relationship**
The eccentricity $e$ is a function of $R$ [S1, unofficial]. Optimal R-factor range: 1.5 to 2.0 [S1, unofficial]. Corresponding optimal eccentricity: 0.90 to 0.95 [S1, unofficial]. An eccentricity of 1.0 (perfect circle) implies $R \approx 6.0$, which is impractical as it requires decreasing $f_a$ or increasing $\omega_0$ by 3-4x, causing bandwidth loss [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def calculate_aom_r_factor(omega_0_microns, f_a_mhz, lambda_a_nm, n=2.26, V_mm_us=4.2):
    """Calculate the R-factor from physical parameters."""
    # Convert to SI units for consistency
    omega_0_m = omega_0_microns * 1e-6
    f_a_hz = f_a_mhz * 1e6
    lambda_a_m = lambda_a_nm * 1e-9
    V_ms = V_mm_us * 1e3
    # R = (lambda_a * f_a) / (n * V * omega_0)
    return (lambda_a_m * f_a_hz) / (n * V_ms * omega_0_m)

def analyze_beam_quality(omega_0_microns, f_a_mhz, lambda_a_nm):
    R = calculate_aom_r_factor(omega_0_microns, f_a_mhz, lambda_a_nm)
    # Eccentricity is obtained from empirical graphs (Figure 1-21 in [S1, unofficial])
    # The sources do not provide a closed-form formula for eccentricity. [G]
    # eccentricity = lookup_eccentricity(R)
    return {"R_factor": R}
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
1.  **Beam Overfilling:** If the input beam diameter exceeds 80 microns for 440-MHz AOMs, it overfills the acoustic beam, causing a vertical aperturing effect and elliptical output [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Low R-Factor:** A very low R-factor (e.g., $R=0.5$) results in high eccentricity (e.g., 0.5), meaning a highly elliptical beam [S1, unofficial].
3.  **High R-Factor:** An eccentricity of 1.0 implies $R \approx 6.0$. Achieving this requires either decreasing the center frequency or increasing the spot size by 3-4x, which is impractical due to bandwidth loss [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Divergence Mismatch:** Ideal spot sizes for red scanners (e.g., 45 microns for 440-MHz) do not diverge rapidly enough to reach the required 3.2 mm beam diameter at the X-Y deflectors. This necessitates longer focal length lenses and a 5-10 inch longer scanner body [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ for calculation [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ [G].
*   **Performance:** The method relies on empirical graphs for eccentricity, so precision depends on the resolution of the source figures [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides a direct metric (R-factor) to optimize beam quality and bandwidth [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Does not account for lens aberrations or mechanical misalignments. Requires precise knowledge of the beam waist at the modulator [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** During the design phase of the AOM selection and laser coupling optics [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When diagnosing spatial artifacts in the final displayed volume (use convergence correction instead) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This analysis originates from the design of the NEOS Second-Generation 3-D Volumetric Display System [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the paper "Design Procedure for Wide Bandwidth Acousto-Optic Modulator" [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to optimize the trade-off between beam eccentricity and bandwidth for argon-ion and diode-pumped lasers in holographic displays [S1, unofficial].
