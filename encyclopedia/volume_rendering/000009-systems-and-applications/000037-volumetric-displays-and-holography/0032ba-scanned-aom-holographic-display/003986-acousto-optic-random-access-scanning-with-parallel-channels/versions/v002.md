# Acousto-Optic Random Access Scanning with Parallel Channels

Also called: AO Laser Scanner, Four-Channel Parallel Processing Scanner

## Overview
Acousto-Optic Random Access Scanning with Parallel Channels is a volumetric display technique that uses multiple acousto-optic (AO) deflectors operating in parallel to address voxels in a display volume, thereby increasing the total voxel count and maintaining image intensity [S1, unofficial]. It solves the bottleneck of finite acoustic wave travel time ("access time") in single-channel scanners, which limits the number of addressable voxels per frame at a fixed refresh rate [S1, unofficial]. Unlike single-channel scanners, which suffer from intensity drop-off as voxel density increases due to reduced on-time, this method couples several scanners to distribute the workload [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Four-Channel Scanner Convergence Correction](11734-four-channel-scanner-convergence-correction.md) by defining the parallel architecture itself, whereas the sibling method addresses the geometric alignment errors inherent in that architecture.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A single-mode laser beam (e.g., 1.6 mm diameter) and high-speed position/intensity data from a custom computer interface card [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A scanned light beam projecting voxels onto a helical display surface [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Control Data:** High-speed data packets sent to the scanner electronics independent of the host computer speed [S1, unofficial].
*   **Frequency Sets:** Each AO deflector is loaded with specific acoustic frequencies corresponding to desired beam deflection angles [S1, unofficial].

**Optical Layout and Components:**
1.  **Light Source:** An argon ion gas laser (up to 1 W) provides the initial beam [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Beam Splitting:** The single input beam is split into four paths (Channels A, B, C, D) using beam splitters [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Modulation:** Each beam is focused into a 440 MHz Tellurium Dioxide (TeO$_2$) Acousto-Optic Modulator (AOM) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Collimation:** Beams are recollimated to a diameter of 3 mm [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Deflection:** Beams are sent to x and y TeO$_2$ AO deflectors (50 MHz bandwidth) [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Combining:** Outputs from Channels A and B are combined using a half-wave plate and a polarizing beam splitter. Channels C and D are combined identically [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Projection:** The two combined outputs are projected downward onto the helix using a final lens pair, which increases angular magnification by ~2.5x [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Polarization Maintenance:** Maintain vertical polarization at the input beam to align parallel to the acoustic wave direction in the deflectors, avoiding corrective wave plates [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Beam Reduction:** Reduce the optical beam diameter in the AO deflectors from 6 mm to 3 mm [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Frequency Loading:** Load the x and y deflectors with desired frequencies. The deflectors are swept from 75 to 125 MHz [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Blanking:** During the "access time" (fill time), blank the laser beam with a modulator to prevent image smearing caused by multiple frequencies being present in the beam deflection path [S1, unofficial].
5.  **On-Time Activation:** Turn on the modulator for the specified "on-time" after the acoustic wave has traveled across the laser beam to achieve 100% deflection [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Channel Combination:** Combine A/B and C/D outputs via half-wave plates and polarizing beam splitters to ensure all four channels can overlap anywhere in the helix volume [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Projection:** Project the combined beams onto the helix using the final lens pair [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas and Parameters:**
*   **Time-Bandwidth Product ($TB$):**
    $$ TB = \Delta f \times \Delta t $$
    Where $\Delta f$ is the deflector sweep range (125 MHz - 75 MHz = 50 MHz) and $\Delta t$ is the sweep time (5 $\mu$sec).
    $$ TB = 50 \times 10^6 \text{ Hz} \times 5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ s} = 250 $$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Access Time Reduction:** Reducing beam diameter from 6 mm to 3 mm lowers access time from 10 $\mu$sec to 5 $\mu$sec [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Capacity:**
    *   Per Channel: Up to 10,000 voxels/frame [S1, unofficial].
    *   Total System: 4 channels $\times$ 10,000 voxels = 40,000 voxels per color [S1, unofficial].
*   **Refresh Rate:** 20 Hz [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Initialization
laser_power = 1.0  # Watts
input_beam_diameter = 1.6  # mm
target_beam_diameter = 3.0  # mm
deflector_sweep_start = 75  # MHz
deflector_sweep_end = 125  # MHz
sweep_time = 5.0  # microseconds
refresh_rate = 20  # Hz
num_channels = 4
voxels_per_channel = 10000

# Optical Path Setup
# 1. Split single laser beam into 4 channels (A, B, C, D)
channels = split_beam(input_beam, num_channels)

# 2. Focus each channel into 440MHz TeO2 Modulator
for channel in channels:
    channel = focus_into_modulator(channel, frequency=440e6)

# 3. Recollimate beams to 3mm diameter
for channel in channels:
    channel = recollimate(channel, diameter=target_beam_diameter)

# 4. Send to x and y TeO2 AO Deflectors
for channel in channels:
    channel = send_to_deflectors(channel, type='TeO2', bandwidth=50e6)

# 5. Combine Channels A/B and C/D
combined_ab = combine_channels(channels[0], channels[1], 
                               method='half_wave_plate_and_polarizing_beam_splitter')
combined_cd = combine_channels(channels[2], channels[3], 
                               method='half_wave_plate_and_polarizing_beam_splitter')

# 6. Project onto Helix
projection_lenses = create_lens_pair(magnification=2.5)
final_output = project(combined_ab, combined_cd, lenses=projection_lenses)

# Scanning Loop (per frame)
for frame in range(num_frames):
    # 1. Blank laser beam during access time
    blank_laser()
    
    # 2. Load deflectors with frequencies for current voxel positions
    # Deflectors sweep from 75 to 125 MHz in 5 microseconds
    load_deflectors(f_start=deflector_sweep_start, f_end=deflector_sweep_end, 
                    time=sweep_time)
    
    # 3. Wait for acoustic wave to travel across beam (fill time)
    wait(access_time=5.0)  # microseconds
    
    # 4. Turn on modulator for on-time
    turn_on_modulator()
    
    # 5. Display voxels
    display_voxels(voxels_per_channel)
    
    # 6. Turn off modulator
    turn_off_modulator()
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Access Time/Smearing:** Finite acoustic wave travel time causes an "access time" or "fill time." If the beam is not blanked during this period, image smearing occurs due to multiple frequencies in the beam path [S1, unofficial].
*   **Polarization Mismatch:** Misalignment of polarization degrades deflection efficiency. The system solves this by maintaining vertical polarization at the input beam [S1, unofficial].
*   **Intensity Drop-off:** In single-channel systems, increasing voxels decreases on-time and overall light level. Parallel coupling prevents this [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Four-Channel Scanner Convergence Correction](11734-four-channel-scanner-convergence-correction.md)
Four-Channel Scanner Convergence Correction is a geometric alignment method for four-channel acousto-optic (AO) holographic displays, ensuring that four scanned laser beams (organized as two pyramidal sources) intersect uniformly throughout the volumetric display space. It solves the problem of beam separation caused by the physical offset of output lenses, which would otherwise cause the beams to intersect at only a single point rather than throughout the entire volume. Unlike its sibling [Acousto-Optic Random Access Scanning with Parallel Channels](8508-acousto-optic-random-access-scanning-with-parallel-channels.md), which defines the parallel channel architecture, this method specifically addresses the spatial registration errors inherent in that architecture. It differs from [Acousto-Optic Modulator R-Factor Analysis](11746-acousto-optic-modulator-r-factor-analysis.md) by focusing on macroscopic geometric alignment rather than device physics limits.

### [Acousto-Optic Modulator R-Factor Analysis](11746-acousto-optic-modulator-r-factor-analysis.md)
This method evaluates the performance of an Acousto-Optic Modulator (AOM) by calculating the R-factor, defined as the ratio of the acoustical beam spread to the optical beam spread. It determines the output beam eccentricity (ellipticity) to ensure optimal diffraction efficiency and beam quality in scanned volumetric displays. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Four-Channel Scanner Convergence Correction](11734-four-channel-scanner-convergence-correction.md) by focusing on the intrinsic beam shaping at the modulator stage rather than the geometric alignment of multiple scanner channels.

### Four-Quadrant Helix Optical Layout
The Four-Quadrant Helix Optical Layout is an optical system design that distributes four scanner channels across four quadrants of the helix volume, enabling up to 10,000 green and 10,000 red voxels per quadrant, plus 10,000 blue voxels in one quadrant [S2, unofficial]. It differs from the single-quadrant design by making full use of the entire helix volume and improving viewability [S2, unofficial].
*   **Steps:** Combine red, green, and blue channels using standard dichroic mirrors before projection [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Green lasers are diode-pumped Nd:YAG (532 nm), 100 mW each (one pair for channels A/B, another for C/D). Red lasers are helium-neon (20 mW each), one per channel. Blue laser is argon ion (water-cooled) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Layout:** Projection optics consist of three lenses in series: Lens 1 (300 mm focal length) and Lens 3 (120 mm) provide rough expansion and focusing; Lens 2 (200 mm) acts as an intermediate relay/focusing element [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The system operates at a fixed refresh rate of 20 Hz [S1, unofficial]. The access time per deflection is 5 $\mu$sec, allowing for a time-bandwidth product of 250 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory/Storage:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** The system supports 40,000 voxels per color at 20 Hz refresh rate [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Scaling is achieved by coupling multiple scanners in parallel. A four-channel system was built, doubling the voxel count compared to a 2k single-channel system by reducing beam diameter [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Increases total voxel count without intensity drop-off; reduces throw distance via angular magnification; uses efficient laser sources (in later variants) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires complex optical alignment (convergence correction); limited by acoustic wave travel time (access time); requires beam blanking [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high voxel count and uniform intensity are required in a volumetric display [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When simplicity of optical alignment is prioritized over voxel count [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originated with NEOS Technologies, Inc., of Melbourne, Florida, which built a four-channel 4k voxel system [S1, unofficial]. It was developed as a "Second Generation" improvement to address the bottleneck of finite acoustic wave travel time in random-access scanning [S1, unofficial]. The design was further improved in 1997 to distribute channels across four quadrants of the helix volume, replacing argon ion lasers with diode-pumped Nd:YAG and helium-neon lasers for green and red channels, respectively [S2, unofficial].
