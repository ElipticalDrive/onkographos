# Mechanically Driven Mirrors

Also called: Galvanometer driven, Piezoelectrically driven

## Overview
Mechanically driven mirrors are beam deflectors used in volumetric displays to steer light beams toward specific points in a drawing volume. They solve the problem of directing light from a source to arbitrary $(x, y, z)$ coordinates within a 3D space by physically rotating mirrors. They are distinguished from electro-optic deflectors by being essentially lossless but suffering from slower response times [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A light source (typically a laser or collimated beam) and a command signal specifying a target deflection angle.
*   **Output:** A reflected light beam directed to a specific spatial coordinate.

**Data Structures:**
Not given in the sources.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Select Drive Type:** Choose between galvanometer or piezoelectric actuation based on required speed and precision [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Command Deflection:** Send a voltage/current signal to the actuator to rotate the mirror to the target angle.
    *   *Galvanometer:* Requires approaching the target angle from the same direction every time to mitigate hysteresis [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Piezoelectric:* Uses active position sensing to correct for hysteresis [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Dampen Oscillations:** Apply additional damping to the mechanical system to suppress underdamped oscillations about the mean orientation. This prevents enlargement of the apparent spot size and misregistration, at the cost of reduced drive response [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Reflect Beam:** The mirror reflects the incident light beam toward the target voxel in the volumetric display [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
Not given in the sources for the mirror mechanics itself. The general perspective projection for the resulting image is governed by the matrix $S$ defined in the parent article [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Lowest Natural Frequency (Piezoelectric):** Approximately 12 KHz [S1, unofficial].
*   **Lowest Natural Frequency (Galvanometer):** Approximately 3 KHz [S1, unofficial].
*   **Maximum Spot Capability (30/sec refresh):**
    *   Piezoelectric: 400 spots [S1, unofficial].
    *   Galvanometer: 100 spots [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hysteresis (Galvanometer):** Up to 4 percent [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def deflect_mirror(drive_type, target_angle, current_angle):
    """
    Deflect a mechanically driven mirror to a target angle.
    
    Args:
        drive_type: 'galvanometer' or 'piezoelectric'
        target_angle: The desired deflection angle in degrees.
        current_angle: The current mirror angle.
        
    Returns:
        None. Modifies mirror state.
    """
    if drive_type == 'galvanometer':
        # Galvanometer drives are susceptible to hysteresis effects
        # as large as 4 percent. To overcome this, it is necessary
        # to always approach a newly commanded deflection angle
        # from the same direction.
        # [G] Implementation note: The system must track the
        # direction of the last move (e.g., always move clockwise
        # to reach the target, or maintain a 'last_direction' flag).
        # This nearly doubles random access time.
        move_to_angle_with_directional_constraint(target_angle)
        
    elif drive_type == 'piezoelectric':
        # Piezoelectric drives circumvent hysteresis by using
        # active position sensing.
        # [G] Implementation note: Use a closed-loop controller
        # that reads the actual position via a sensor and adjusts
        # the drive signal to match the target.
        move_to_angle_closed_loop(target_angle)
    
    # High response mechanical mirror drives often operate in a
    # highly underdamped manner, producing slowly decaying oscillations.
    # Additional damping is required to prevent viewing degradation.
    apply_damping()
```

## Subtopics

### Beam deflector (BD) steering
A liquid crystal-based transmission phase array that optically tilts coherent backlight beams to expand the holographic viewing angle. It sets itself apart by using phase arrays rather than physical mirrors, allowing for scalable expansion of the spatial-bandwidth product (SBP) without increasing SLM pixels. Choose it when a compact, scalable holographic display with a wide viewing angle is needed. It operates at a wavelength of 520 nm with a maximum steering angle of ~15° and angular resolution of 0.02° [S2]. A key pitfall is that the maximum steering angle decreases as the light source area increases due to etendue constraints [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity/Response:** Limited by the natural frequency of the unit. Piezoelectric units (~12 KHz) are faster than galvanometer units (~3 KHz) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory/Storage:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** At a 30/sec refresh rate, piezoelectric units support a maximum of 400 spots, while galvanometer units support 100 spots [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Essentially lossless efficiency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow response time compared to electro-optic methods; galvanometer units suffer from hysteresis (up to 4%) requiring directional approach strategies that double random access time; high-speed units are underdamped, requiring additional damping that further reduces response speed [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high optical efficiency is critical and the required frame rate/spot count is moderate (e.g., <100 spots at 30Hz for galvanometers).
*   **When not:** When high-speed random access to many points is required without the overhead of directional constraints or when extreme speed is needed beyond the natural frequency limits.

## Background
Mechanically driven mirrors were studied as part of 3D volumetric display technologies in the early 1970s. They are described as the simplest of the available beam deflectors [S1, unofficial].
