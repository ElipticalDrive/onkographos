# Holographic Magnification via Diverging Light
Also called: Diverging Light Holographic Magnification

## Overview
This method provides a lensless magnification technique for holographic displays by using a diverging point light source to perform coarse beam steering, allowing a hologram to steer light at a finer angle to focus into an image [S1]. It solves the problem of achieving wide fields of view (60–100°) without the bulky refractive lenses required by conventional magnification [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A Spatial Light Modulator (SLM) displaying a hologram pattern; a diverging point light source positioned at $\vec{l}_e$; wavelength $\lambda$ [S1].
*   **Output:** A magnified virtual image with an expanded field of view and a reduced exit pupil [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **SLM Pattern:** A 2D array of phase values [S1]. The sub-hologram size corresponds to the effective exit pupil size [S1].
*   **Light Source:** Defined by position $\vec{l}_e$ relative to the SLM [S1].

**Core Principles:**
The hologram acts as a series of superimposed diffraction gratings [S1]. The light deflection angle is determined by the grating equation [S1]:
$$ d(\sin\theta_i + \sin\theta_m) = \lambda $$
where $d$ is the grating pitch, $\theta_i$ is the light incidence angle, $\theta_m$ is the diffraction angle, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength [S1].

The lens phase function for a sub-hologram is updated to handle diverging light [S1]. The phase $\phi$ at position $\vec{p}$ on the SLM, relative to the eye position $\vec{o}$ and light source $\vec{l}_e$, is calculated as [S1]:
$$ \phi(\vec{p}) = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \left( |\vec{p} - \vec{l}_e| + |\vec{p} - \vec{o}| \right) $$
This phase function accounts for the path length difference from the diverging source to the SLM pixel and from the pixel to the observer's eye [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **SLM Pixel Pitch:** Typically 8 µm [S1].
*   **Minimum Grating Pitch ($d$):** 16 µm (derived from Nyquist sampling theorem on 8 µm pixels) [S1].
*   **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** 532 nm (green light) [S1].
*   **Maximum Diffraction Angle ($\theta_m$):** $\pm 1.9^\circ$ for normally incident light with $d=16\mu m$ and $\lambda=532nm$ [S1].
*   **Field of View:** 60–100° or more, limited only by the divergence of the point light source [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Define Geometry:** Position the diverging point light source at $\vec{l}_e$ and the observer's eye at $\vec{o}$ relative to the SLM [S1].
2.  **Calculate Phase:** For each pixel $\vec{p}$ in the sub-hologram, calculate the phase $\phi(\vec{p})$ based on the path lengths from the light source to the pixel and from the pixel to the eye [S1].
3.  **Compute Hologram:** Generate the hologram pattern using the calculated phase values [S1].
4.  **Display:** Render the hologram on the SLM [S1].
5.  **Illumination:** Illuminate the SLM with the diverging light from $\vec{l}_e$ [S1].
6.  **Diffraction & Steering:** The hologram diffracts the light [S1]. The beam divergence performs coarse beam steering while the hologram steers light at a finer angle to focus the light into an image [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Exit Pupil Reduction:** As the projector is magnified with diverging light, the field of view increases, but the effective size of the exit pupil decreases [S1].
*   **Sub-hologram Size:** The sub-hologram size used in computation must match the size of the reduced exit pupil [S1]. Attempting to increase the sub-hologram size would require diffraction angles and spatial frequencies higher than the SLM can reproduce [S1].
*   **Etendue:** Unlike conventional displays, the etendue of a holographic projector is linked to the device resolution [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Performance:** Supports wide fields of view (60–100°) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Lensless magnification; avoids bulky refractive lenses; enables wide fields of view with smaller optics [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The exit pupil size decreases as magnification increases; etendue is limited by SLM resolution [S1].
*   **When to use:** When wide fields of view are required in VR/AR near-eye displays without bulky optics [S1].

## Background
This technique was developed to address the need for wide fields of view in VR and AR displays without the bulk of conventional refractive lenses [S1]. It builds on the principle that holographic displays can be magnified lenslessly using a diverging point light source, as cited from Makowski et al. [2012] and Qu et al. [2015] in [S1]. The method was detailed in the context of holographic near-eye displays for virtual and augmented reality [S1].
