# Photophoretic-Trap Volumetric Display

Also called: OTD, Optical Trap Display, Photophoretic optical trapping display, Smalley Photophoretic Trap Display

## Overview
A Photophoretic-Trap Volumetric Display (also known as the Optical Trap Display or OTD) is a free-space volumetric display that generates full-color, three-dimensional images in air using a single trapped particle illuminated by synchronized RGB lasers [S1, unofficial]. It solves the "clipping" limitation inherent to holographic and light-field displays, where image points are physically separated from the scattering surface, thereby restricting viewing angles and preventing geometries such as long-throw projections or wrap-around displays [S1, unofficial]. By co-locating the scattering surface (the particle) with the image point, the OTD provides a 360° in-plane viewing angle around every voxel at any depth, limited only by the optical axis itself [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** An absorbing, opaque particle (typically cellulose) with a diameter $d$ in the range of $5\text{--}100 \, \mu\text{m}$ [S1, unofficial]. A near-invisible trapping laser at $405 \, \text{nm}$ wavelength [S1, unofficial]. Collinear Red, Green, and Blue (RGB) lasers capable of varying power and position [S1, unofficial]. Air at standard pressure and room temperature [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A free-space 3D image composed of voxels visible from all angles (except the optical axis line) [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The system relies on the physical state of the particle and the optical configuration rather than digital data structures for the image itself, though the control software must manage:
1.  **Trajectory Data:** A time-parameterized 3D path $(x(t), y(t), z(t))$ for the trap center [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Illumination Map:** A time-synchronized sequence of RGB intensity values $(I_R(t), I_G(t), I_B(t))$ corresponding to the particle's position along the trajectory [S1, unofficial].
Exact field sizes and bit-packing layouts are not given in the sources.

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Isolation:** A cellulose particle is isolated and confined in the photophoretic trap created by the $405 \, \text{nm}$ laser and the aberration optics [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Scanning:** The trap (and the trapped particle) is scanned through the display volume [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Illumination:** A system of collinear RGB lasers illuminates the trapped particle [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Image Formation:** The combination of the scanned particle and synchronized RGB illumination forms a 3D image in free space [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
The trapping force is **photophoretic**, arising from thermal creep and radiometer effects due to uneven heating of the absorbing particle by the trap laser, rather than radiation pressure or gradient forces used in standard optical tweezers [S1, unofficial]. Because the particle diameter ($5\text{--}100 \, \mu\text{m}$) is much larger than the gas mean free path ($68 \, \text{nm}$ at standard pressure and room temperature), the system operates in the **continuous regime** [S1, unofficial].

The photophoretic force in the continuous regime is given by:

$$
F_{\text{cont}} = \frac{\pi d^2 R T}{3 \eta M p} \nabla T
$$

Where:
*   $F_{\text{cont}}$ is the photophoretic force [S1, unofficial].
*   $d$ is the diameter of the particle [S1, unofficial].
*   $R$ is the universal gas constant [S1, unofficial].
*   $T$ is the temperature of the gas [S1, unofficial].
*   $\eta$ is the viscosity of the gas [S1, unofficial].
*   $M$ is the molecular weight of the gas [S1, unofficial].
*   $p$ is the gas pressure [S1, unofficial].
*   $\nabla T$ is the gradient of the temperature [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Trap Wavelength:** $405 \, \text{nm}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Illumination:** RGB lasers [S1, unofficial].
*   **Particle Size:** Demonstrated resolution $\approx 10 \, \mu\text{m}$; early trials used diameters ranging from below $5 \, \mu\text{m}$ to above $100 \, \mu\text{m}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scanning Speed:** $10 \, \text{Hz}$ [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Optical Axis Occlusion:** The image is visible from every angle with the possible exception of the line forming the optical axis, where the trap laser and illumination beams may obscure the view or cause glare [S1, unofficial].
*   **Particle Size Variance:** Early trials used particles with diameters ranging from below $5 \, \mu\text{m}$ to above $100 \, \mu\text{m}$ [S1, unofficial]. Consistent image quality requires precise control over particle size to maintain the continuous regime and optimal scattering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gas Conditions:** The force model depends on gas pressure, temperature, and viscosity [S1, unofficial]. Changes in ambient conditions can alter the trap stiffness and particle dynamics [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode describes the control logic for generating a single frame of the volumetric image. It assumes a pre-defined trajectory and illumination map.

```python
def render_otd_frame(traj, illum_map, dt):
    """
    Simulates the control logic for generating a single frame of the OTD.
    
    Args:
        traj: List of 3D coordinates [(x1, y1, z1), (x2, y2, z2), ...] representing 
              the trap center position over time.
        illum_map: List of RGB tuples [(R1, G1, B1), (R2, G2, B2), ...] representing 
                   the laser intensities synchronized with traj.
        dt: Time step between trajectory points (seconds).
    Returns:
        A dictionary mapping 3D positions to accumulated RGB color values.
    """
    # Ensure trajectory and illumination map are synchronized
    if len(traj) != len(illum_map):
        raise ValueError("Trajectory and illumination map must have the same length.")

    # Initialize an empty dictionary to accumulate color at each spatial position
    image_buffer = {}

    # Scan the particle through the volume
    for i in range(len(traj)):
        pos = traj[i]
        color = illum_map[i]
        
        # Accumulate color at the current position to simulate persistence of vision
        if pos not in image_buffer:
            image_buffer[pos] = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
            
        # Simple averaging accumulation for the current frame
        current_color = image_buffer[pos]
        image_buffer[pos] = [
            (current_color[0] + color[0]) / 2.0,
            (current_color[1] + color[1]) / 2.0,
            (current_color[2] + color[2]) / 2.0
        ]

    return image_buffer
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The frame rate is limited by the mechanical or optical scanning speed of the trap [S1, unofficial]. Demonstrated scan rates were $10 \, \text{Hz}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not applicable in the traditional sense, as the image is generated physically in space [S1, unofficial]. However, control software requires memory proportional to the trajectory length and illumination map size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Demonstrated image points are approximately $10 \, \mu\text{m}$ in dimension [S1, unofficial]. Images exhibit full color, fine detail, and low apparent speckle [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** The system is capable of producing image geometries that are currently unobtainable with holographic and light-field technologies, such as long-throw projections, tall sandtables, and 'wrap-around' displays [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Free-space imaging without screens or scattering surfaces [S1, unofficial].
    *   Full-color, low-speckle images [S1, unofficial].
    *   Ability to create geometries impossible for holograms, such as long-throw projections, tall sandtables, and wrap-around displays [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Limited to single-particle scanning (though multiple particles could theoretically be trapped, the described system uses one) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Requires absorbing particles (cellulose) and specific gas conditions [S1, unofficial].
    *   The optical axis may be a blind spot [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Applications requiring unconstrained viewing angles, long-throw projection, or interaction with the image volume (e.g., touch interaction) where holographic clipping is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Applications requiring high pixel counts or static images without motion, or environments where gas conditions cannot be controlled [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Photophoretic-Trap Volumetric Display, also referred to as the Optical Trap Display (OTD), was introduced by Smalley et al. in 2018 [S1, unofficial]. It advances the state of the art by providing full-color, free-space images with fine detail, overcoming the limitations of earlier levitation displays that were too coarse or inertial [S1, unofficial]. The technology leverages photophoretic optical trapping, which is particularly useful for confining micrometer-diameter absorbing particles using thermal forces rather than radiation pressure [S1, unofficial]. The system was demonstrated producing images such as a butterfly and the Earth, with voxel dimensions of approximately $10 \, \mu\text{m}$ [S1, unofficial].
