# Acoustophoretic Volumetric Display

Also called: AVD, Acoustophoretic-trap free-space volumetric display

## Overview
An Acoustophoretic Volumetric Display (AVD) renders 3D graphics in free space by trapping a millimetric dielectric particle using acoustic radiation forces and moving it rapidly along a 3D path while synchronously illuminating it with colored light to exploit persistence of vision [S1]. Unlike holographic or light-field displays that suffer from clipping and limited field of view, or photophoretic displays that require high-power lasers, AVD uses safe, low-cost ultrasonic phased arrays to create a large operational region (up to half a meter) without optical hazards [S1]. It is distinguished from [Photophoretic-Trap Volumetric Display](6753-photophoretic-trap-volumetric-display.md) by its use of acoustic forces rather than photophoretic forces, allowing for simpler hardware but currently limiting high-speed rendering to smaller, simpler shapes [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A 3D graphic defined as a sequence of particle positions $(x, y, z)$ at time $t$ and corresponding RGB color values.
*   **Output:** A visible 3D volumetric image formed by the scattered light from the levitated particle.

**Data Structures and Hardware Layout**
The system consists of a control chain and an acoustic/optical hardware array [S1]:
1.  **Control Chain:** PC $\rightarrow$ UART (1.5 Mbauds) $\rightarrow$ FPGA (ALTERA CoreEp4CE6) $\rightarrow$ MOSFET Drivers (TC4427a) $\rightarrow$ Transducers.
2.  **Transducer Array:** 60 ultrasonic transducers (Murata MA40S4S) arranged as two opposed arrays of 30 transducers each.
3.  **Signal Format:** Square waves generated by the FPGA with a phase resolution of $\pi/64$ rad.
4.  **Color Signal:** 8-bit signal per RGB channel sent to the RGB LED.

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Path Definition:** Represent the 3D graphic as a sequence of target positions $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ at times $t_i$ and colors $C_i = (R_i, G_i, B_i)$ [S1].
2.  **Color Transmission:** Send the 8-bit RGB signal for each channel to the RGB LED [S1].
3.  **Phase Calculation:** Calculate the emission phases $\phi_j$ for each of the 60 transducers $j$ required to focus the acoustic field at the target position $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ [S1]. This creates an acoustic antinode at the focal point with nodes above and below to trap the particle [S1].
4.  **FPGA Control:** Send the calculated phases to the FPGA. The FPGA generates square waves with the specified phase resolution ($\pi/64$ rad) [S1].
5.  **Amplification:** The FPGA signals are sent to MOSFET drivers (TC4427a), which amplify the signals using an external DC power supply [S1].
6.  **Acoustic Emission:** The amplified signals drive the 60 ultrasonic transducers, creating a standing wave field that traps the particle at the target position [S1].
7.  **Illumination:** Illuminate the trapped particle with the target color $C_i$ [S1].
8.  **Motion:** Move the particle to the next position in the sequence. If the particle moves sufficiently fast while illuminated, persistence of vision creates a continuous 3D path [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Particle:** Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) sphere, diameter $\approx 1.5$ mm [S1].
*   **Working Volume:** Approximately $4 \times 5 \times 8$ cm$^3$ [S1].
*   **Communication:** UART at 1.5 Mbauds [S1].
*   **Phase Resolution:** $\pi/64$ rad [S1].
*   **Color Depth:** 8-bit per RGB channel [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Particle Size Constraints:** Particles $> 1.5$ mm invalidate the small particle assumptions in the Gor’kov model, causing the force per unit volume to decrease [S1]. Particles $< 0.5$ mm are swept out of traps by air currents because drag force scales with area while acoustic radiation force (Gor’kov) scales with volume [S1].
*   **Static Calibration Deviation:** Equilibrium points deviate from ideal focal points by $\approx 0.5$ mm due to scattering, temperature variations, manufacturing variations, or transducer surface inaccuracies [S1]. This affects image accuracy [S1].
*   **Dynamic Distortion:** Increasing particle velocity distorts the shape of the volumetric image and can eject the particle from the trap [S1]. Static calibration decreases in effectiveness as speed increases [S1].
*   **Speed Limitation:** At 10 Hz rendering frequency, only small and simple shapes of size 5–6 mm can be achieved [S1].

def calculate_acoustic_phases(target_x, target_y, target_z, transducer_positions):
    """
    Calculate the phase offset for each transducer to focus the acoustic field
    at the target position (target_x, target_y, target_z).
    This creates an antinode at the focal point.
    """
    phases = []
    # Implementation details for phase calculation depend on the specific
    # acoustic model and transducer geometry. The source notes state that
    # phases are calculated and sent to the FPGA [S1].
    # [G] Standard phased array beamforming logic: phase = -k * distance_to_target
    # where k is the wavenumber. The result is quantized to PHASE_RESOLUTION.
    for pos in transducer_positions:
        distance = sqrt((pos.x - target_x)**2 + (pos.y - target_y)**2 + (pos.z - target_z)**2)
        # Quantize phase to pi/64 resolution
        phase = quantize_phase(distance)
        phases.append(phase)
    return phases

def quantize_phase(phase):
    """Quantize phase to the nearest multiple of PI/64."""
    steps = round(phase / PHASE_RESOLUTION)
    return steps * PHASE_RESOLUTION

    led = init_rgb_led()

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** Renders simple figures in real time at 10 frames per second (fps) [S1]. Raster images are rendered at lower frame rates; draw times were 20 s for logos/stop signs and 5 s for a 3D wireframe airplane in static tests [S1].
*   **Limitations:** At 10 Hz rendering frequency, only small and simple shapes of size 5–6 mm can be achieved [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The operational region can be as large as half a meter, significantly larger than photophoretic displays [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Safe for humans (ultrasonic range), simple hardware using low-cost off-the-shelf components, 360-degree viewing without obstructions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Dynamic distortion increases with drawing speed; static calibration is less effective at high speeds; limited to small shapes at high speeds compared to photophoretic displays (which reach 1.8 m/s) [S1].
*   **When to use:** When a large, obstruction-free 3D display is needed without the hazards of high-power lasers.
*   **When not to use:** When high-speed rendering of complex, large-scale shapes is required.

## Background
Acoustophoretic volumetric displays are an active area of research for free-space 3D graphics [S1]. Previous attempts included Ochiai et al. showing crosses and uniform 2D grids [S1], Sahoo et al. presenting a screen of levitated dielectric particles flipped by electric fields [S1], and Uno et al. demonstrating levitation of an LED for long-exposure images [S1]. The specific AVD method described here uses a phased array of ultrasonic emitters to hold and move a millimetric particle, synchronously illuminating it with RGB light [S1]. It was published in 2019 [S1].
