# Particle Trapping and Manipulation
Also called: Free-space particle volumetric displays, Levitated particle displays

## Overview
Particle Trapping and Manipulation renders 3D images in free space by suspending microscopic or millimetric particles using optical or acoustic forces and illuminating them to exploit persistence of vision [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family relies on the synchronization of particle position and illumination color/intensity [S1, unofficial]. The core mechanism involves three stages:
1. **Trapping:** A force field (optical or acoustic) creates a potential well that confines a single particle against gravity and drag [S1, unofficial].
2. **Scanning:** The trap moves the particle along a 3D trajectory $(x(t), y(t), z(t))$ at a speed sufficient to blur the particle's motion into a continuous path via persistence of vision [S1, unofficial].
3. **Illumination:** A light source (lasers or LEDs) illuminates the particle synchronously with its position to generate specific voxels with defined colors [S1, unofficial].

The choice of particle material and size is dictated by the trapping force:
* **Optical Traps** use absorbing particles (e.g., cellulose) to generate photophoretic forces via thermal creep in gas [S1, unofficial].
* **Acoustic Traps** use dielectric particles (e.g., expanded polystyrene) to experience radiation forces in standing waves [S1, unofficial].

The control logic for frame generation follows a synchronized trajectory-illumination loop:
```python
def render_particle_trap_frame(traj, illum_map, dt):
    """
    Renders a single frame by synchronizing particle trajectory and illumination.
    Args:
        traj: List of 3D coordinates [(x1, y1, z1), ...] for trap center.
        illum_map: List of RGB tuples [(R1, G1, B1), ...] synchronized with traj.
        dt: Time step between points (seconds).
    """
    if len(traj) != len(illum_map):
        raise ValueError("Trajectory and illumination map must have the same length.")
    
    image_buffer = create_empty_3d_volume()
    
    for i in range(len(traj)):
        pos = traj[i]
        color = illum_map[i]
        # Particle is at 'pos' and illuminated with 'color'
        # Persistence of vision blurs discrete points into continuous paths
        set_voxel_color(image_buffer, pos, color)
        
    return image_buffer

def set_voxel_color(volume, position, rgb):
    voxel_idx = quantize_to_grid(position)
    volume[voxel_idx] = rgb

def create_empty_3d_volume():
    return {}

def quantize_to_grid(position):
    # Standard grid quantization; implementation depends on display resolution
    return tuple(int(coord) for coord in position)
```

## Subtopics

### [Photophoretic-Trap Volumetric Display](6753-photophoretic-trap-volumetric-display.md)
A free-space display using photophoretic forces to trap a microscopic particle [6753-photophoretic-trap-volumetric-display.md]. It is distinguished by its use of near-invisible trapping lasers and RGB illumination, enabling high-resolution voxels ($\approx 10 \mu m$) and unconstrained viewing angles [6753-photophoretic-trap-volumetric-display.md]. Choose this method for high-resolution, full-color 3D images where optical safety and hardware complexity are secondary to image quality [6753-photophoretic-trap-volumetric-display.md].

### [Acoustophoretic Volumetric Display](11093-acoustophoretic-volumetric-display.md)
A free-space display using acoustic radiation forces to trap a millimetric particle [11093-acoustophoretic-volumetric-display.md]. It is distinguished by its use of safe, low-cost ultrasonic phased arrays, allowing for a larger operational region (up to half a meter) without optical hazards [11093-acoustophoretic-volumetric-display.md]. Choose this method for larger-scale displays where safety and cost are priorities, accepting lower resolution and speed limitations compared to optical methods [11093-acoustophoretic-volumetric-display.md].

### Kumagai Volumetric Bubble Display
A method using bubbles excited by lasers in liquid as voxels [S1, unofficial]. By increasing liquid viscosity, slow-floating bubbles are generated and arranged to stack 2D cross-sections, realizing volumetric image display [S1, unofficial]. This system has a very long voxel residence time, requiring time for image refresh, but can express complex shapes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of trapping particles for volumetric display was pioneered by Smalley et al. in 2018, who used a $405 \text{ nm}$ laser to trap $\approx 10 \mu m$ cellulose particles in air, illuminating them with coaxial RGB lasers to generate voxels [S1, unofficial]. This was the first method to adopt computer-hologram-based light irradiation patterns for volumetric displays, demonstrating that holographic design improves voxel count and brightness [S1, unofficial]. Acoustic variants emerged earlier; in 2014, "Pixie Dust" proposed using 285 ultrasonic transducers across four speakers to create acoustic ponderomotive fields for levitating multiple particles [S1, unofficial]. In 2019, Hirayama et al. demonstrated particle trapping using standing acoustic waves [S1, unofficial]. The Kumagai method utilizes laser-excited bubbles in viscous liquids, offering long residence times for complex shape expression [S1, unofficial].
