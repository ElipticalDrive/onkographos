# Mixed Modality Visualization
## Overview
Mixed Modality Visualization is the visualization of mixed modality data, including imaging, genetics, and blood measurements, acquired from a group of subjects [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of analyzing complex medical cohort studies where diverse data types must be integrated to diagnose or predict clinical outcomes [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its sibling [Multi-Subject Medical Visualization](5715-multi-subject-medical-visualization.md) by its specific focus on integrating heterogeneous data types (imaging, genetics, blood) rather than just multiple subjects, though it relies on the same coupled view interface principles.
## Shared principles
The family relies on creating a highly interactive coupled view visualization interface [S1, unofficial]. This interface integrates both information and scientific visualization techniques [S1, unofficial]. The goal is to allow users to extract patterns and hypotheses from the whole data collection [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept emerged from work on the interactive visualization of multi-subject and mixed modality datasets acquired by medical cohort studies [S1, unofficial]. In these studies, mixed modality data, including imaging, genetics, and blood measurements, is acquired from a group of subjects in order to be analyzed for diagnosing or predicting the clinical outcome of that group [S1, unofficial]. It was demonstrated by Stenwijk to create a highly interactive coupled view visualization interface, integrating both information and scientific visualization techniques, with which patterns, and also hypotheses, could be extracted from the whole data collection [S1, unofficial]. The area of medical visualization is complex and, depending on the context, requires supplementary activities of medical doctors, medical physicists, biomedical engineers as well as technicians [S1, unofficial].
