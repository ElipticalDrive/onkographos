# Shear-Warp Algorithm

Also called: Shear-Warp Factorization, Lacroute and Levoy algorithm, Lacroute-Levoy algorithm

## Overview
The Shear-Warp Algorithm is a fast, software-based volume rendering technique that reduces the computational complexity of arbitrary viewing angles by factorizing the viewing transformation into a 3D shear and a 2D warp [S1, S2, S3, S8, S12, S23, S25, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficient resampling for non-axis-aligned views by transforming the volume into a "sheared object space" where viewing rays are parallel to a coordinate axis, allowing the use of fixed bilinear interpolation weights and efficient scanline traversal [S2, S3, S8, S12, unofficial][S18]. Unlike general ray-casting methods that require trilinear interpolation for every sample, Shear-Warp substitutes this with two 2D resampling steps (shear and warp), making it significantly faster for parallel projections on standard CPUs [S1, S6, S12, unofficial]. It is distinct from [GPU-based Shear-Warp](7425-gpu-based-shear-warp.md) in its reliance on software optimization and RLE structures, and from [Perspective Shear-Warp](6047-perspective-shear-warp.md) in its simpler handling of scaling, though both share the core factorization principle [S2, S25, unofficial].

## How it works
The algorithm decomposes the viewing transformation matrix $M_{view}$ into three components: a permutation $P$, a shear $S$, and a 2D warp $M_{warp2D}$ [S2, S5, S8, S12, unofficial][S18][S22, unofficial]:
$$ M_{view} = M_{warp2D} \cdot S \cdot P $$

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Volume data $V$ of dimensions $D_x \times D_y \times D_z$.
    *   Viewing transformation matrix $M_{view}$ (4x4).
    *   Transfer function mapping density to color/opacity.
    *   Image dimensions $W \times H$ (final output).
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Final 2D image buffer of size $W \times H$.

### Data Structures
1.  **Volume Data:** Stored as a 3D array or 3D texture. For optimization, three Run-Length Encoded (RLE) structures are pre-computed, one for each principal axis (X, Y, Z) [S2, S12, S17, S20, unofficial].
    *   **RLE Entry:** Contains `start_index`, `length`, and `is_transparent` (boolean). Transparent runs are skipped during compositing [S2, S12, S17, unofficial].
2.  **Intermediate Image:** A 2D buffer storing the composited result in sheared space.
    *   **Optimization:** Opaque pixels store an offset (pointer) to the next non-opaque pixel in the same scanline, enabling early ray termination/skipping of occluded voxels [S2, S11, unofficial].
3.  **Shear Parameters:** Pre-computed coefficients $s_x, s_y$ (and $s'_w$ for perspective) derived from $M_{view}$ [S2, S7, unofficial][S18][S22, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Permutation ($P$):**
    Determine the principal viewing axis (the axis most aligned with the view direction vector $v_o$) [S2, S5, S8, S12, unofficial].
    *   Calculate view direction $v_o$ from $M_{view}$:
        $$ v_o = \begin{bmatrix} m_{12}m_{23} - m_{22}m_{13} \\ m_{21}m_{13} - m_{11}m_{23} \\ m_{11}m_{22} - m_{21}m_{12} \end{bmatrix} $$
        where $m_{ij}$ are elements of $M_{view}$ [S7, S22, unofficial].
    *   Select the axis with the largest absolute component in $v_o$ as the principal axis (Z-axis in sheared space) [S2, S9, S12, unofficial].
    *   Select the corresponding RLE structure (RLE_X, RLE_Y, or RLE_Z) [S2, S12, S17, unofficial].
    *   Define $P$ to permute coordinates so the principal axis aligns with Z [S2, S12, unofficial][S18].

2.  **Shear ($S$):**
    Compute the shear matrix to align viewing rays with the Z-axis [S2, S8, S12, unofficial][S18].
    *   **Parallel Projection:**
        $$ S_{par} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & s_x & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & s_y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
        where $s_x = -v_{o,x}/v_{o,z}$ and $s_y = -v_{o,y}/v_{o,z}$ [S2, S7, S12, unofficial][S18][S22, unofficial].
    *   **Perspective Projection:**
        $$ S_{persp} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & s'_x & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & s'_y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & s'_w & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
        Additional scaling is applied to each slice to account for foreshortening [S2, S12, unofficial][S18][S25, unofficial].

3.  **Resampling and Compositing (Shear Phase):**
    Transform volume slices into the sheared object space and composite them into an intermediate 2D image [S2, S8, S12, unofficial][S18].
    *   Iterate through slices perpendicular to the principal axis [S2, S12, unofficial].
    *   For each slice, translate and resample using bilinear interpolation to align with the intermediate image grid [S2, S6, S12, unofficial].
    *   **Optimization:** Use RLE to skip transparent voxel runs [S2, S12, S17, unofficial].
    *   Composite slices in front-to-back order using the Porter-Duff "over" operator [S2, S8, S11, S12, unofficial]:
        $$ C_{new} = C_{slice} \cdot \alpha_{slice} + C_{accum} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{slice}) $$
        $$ \alpha_{new} = \alpha_{slice} + \alpha_{accum} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{slice}) $$
    *   **Early Ray Termination:** If $\alpha_{accum}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., 0.95), stop processing the current ray [S2, S11, unofficial][S19, abstract only, unofficial]. Use the intermediate image offset structure to skip occluded pixels [S2, S11, unofficial].

4.  **Warp ($M_{warp2D}$):**
    Transform the intermediate image to the final image space [S2, S8, S12, S22, unofficial].
    *   Compute the warp matrix:
        $$ M_{warp2D} = M_{view} \cdot S^{-1} \cdot P^{-1} $$
        [S2, S12, unofficial][S18][S22, unofficial].
    *   Resample the intermediate image using the warp matrix to produce the final image [S2, S6, S12, S25, unofficial].
    *   This step corrects the distortion introduced by the shear and applies the correct perspective/parallel projection [S2, S22, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def shear_warp_render(volume, M_view, image_width, image_height, transfer_function):
    # 1. Permutation
    v_o = compute_view_direction(M_view)
    principal_axis = argmax(abs(v_o)) # 0=x, 1=y, 2=z
    P = get_permutation_matrix(principal_axis)
    RLE_list = get_rle_for_axis(principal_axis, volume, transfer_function)
    
    # 2. Shear
    if is_parallel_projection(M_view):
        s_x = -v_o.x / v_o.z
        s_y = -v_o.y / v_o.z
        s_w = 0
    else:
        # Perspective shear parameters (simplified)
        s_x, s_y, s_w = compute_perspective_shear(M_view, v_o)
        
    S = create_shear_matrix(s_x, s_y, s_w)
    
    # 3. Resample and Composite
    intermediate_image = zeros((image_height, image_width, 4)) # RGBA
    intermediate_image_offsets = zeros((image_height, image_width)) # For early termination
    
    # Iterate slices in sheared space
    for slice_idx in range(volume_depth):
        # Get slice data and apply shear translation
        slice_data = get_slice_data(RLE_list, slice_idx)
        shear_offset_x = slice_idx * s_x
        shear_offset_y = slice_idx * s_y
        
        # Resample slice to intermediate image using bilinear interpolation
        # Note: In optimized implementations, this is done via scanline traversal
        # skipping transparent runs and occluded pixels
        composite_slice_to_intermediate(
            intermediate_image, 
            intermediate_image_offsets,
            slice_data, 
            shear_offset_x, 
            shear_offset_y,
            transfer_function
        )
        
        # Check for early termination optimization in intermediate image
        if check_early_termination(intermediate_image_offsets):
            break
            
    # 4. Warp
    M_warp = M_view * inverse(S) * inverse(P)
    final_image = warp_image(intermediate_image, M_warp, image_width, image_height)
    
    return final_image

def compute_view_direction(M_view):
    m = M_view.elements
    vx = m[1][2]*m[2][3] - m[2][2]*m[1][3]
    vy = m[2][1]*m[1][3] - m[1][1]*m[2][3]
    vz = m[1][1]*m[2][2] - m[2][1]*m[1][2]
    return Vector3(vx, vy, vz)

def create_shear_matrix(sx, sy, sw):
    return Matrix4x4([
        [1, 0, sx, 0],
        [0, 1, sy, 0],
        [0, 0, 1, 0],
        [0, 0, sw, 1]
    ])
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Interpolation:** Bilinear interpolation is used for resampling in both shear and warp phases [S2, S6, S12, unofficial].
*   **Sampling Rate:** Fixed for parallel projection; for perspective, individual slice scaling is required [S2, S6, S12, S25, unofficial].
*   **Ambiguous Slicing:** When the viewing direction is at 45 degrees to the axes, the principal axis choice is ambiguous; either axis can be chosen [S9, unofficial].
*   **Artifacts:** Stair-casing artifacts may appear near 45-degree viewing angles. Blurring may occur during zoom-in if the warp resampling is not adaptive [S1, unofficial].
*   **Clipping:** Clipping planes must be handled in the compositing step (limiting loops) because standard depth buffering is not available in the 2D composite [S25, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot M)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels and $M$ is the number of pixels, but optimized by RLE to skip transparent/occluded regions [S2, S12, S17, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V + I)$ where $V$ is volume size and $I$ is image size. Requires three RLE structures for all axes if pre-computed [S2, S12, S17, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Historically one of the fastest software-based volume rendering algorithms, achieving sub-second frame rates for moderate datasets (e.g., $256^3$ voxels) on standard CPUs [S1, S6, S25, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High speed for parallel projections, efficient memory access patterns, simple implementation on CPUs.
*   **Limitations:** Reduced image quality compared to ray-casting (aliasing, blurring), fixed sampling rate, artifacts near 45-degree angles, complex handling of perspective projection scaling [S1, S6, S25, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time visualization on CPUs where image quality is secondary to speed, or as a baseline for comparison.
*   **When Not to Use:** High-quality offline rendering, complex perspective views with large depth of field, or when hardware acceleration is available.

## Background
The Shear-Warp algorithm was proposed by Pierre Lacroute and Marc Levoy in 1994 [S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S12, unofficial][S18][S19, abstract only, unofficial][S25, unofficial]. It was designed to provide fast, interactive volume rendering on standard graphics workstations by exploiting spatial coherence and reducing the number of resampling passes from three to two [S1, S6, S12, unofficial]. The method combines advantages of image-order (ray-casting) and object-order (splatting) techniques [S2, S3, S9, unofficial][S14][S16, S17, unofficial]. It has been widely used in medical imaging and virtual environments, and served as the basis for hardware implementations like VolumePro [S1, S6, S25, unofficial].
