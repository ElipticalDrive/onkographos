# Warp-Space Affine Mixture

Also called: Affine mixture warp model

## Overview
Warp-Space Affine Mixture is a warping method where affine mixture weights are computed in warped space, outperforming models with no warping, convolutional warps, or world-space mixture [S1]. It addresses the problem of modeling non-linear bending in dynamic volumes by using a spatial mixture of affine warps, avoiding the overfitting and poor generalization associated with freely-varying template sample points [S1]. It sets itself apart from siblings by explicitly defining the weighting function in the warped coordinate system rather than world space, which better represents different parts of the scene [S1].

## How it works
The method constructs an inverse warp field $\mathbf{W}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})$ as a spatial mixture of affine transformations.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A point $\mathbf{x}$ in the target (warped) space.
- Output: The corresponding point in the source (original) space, $\mathbf{W}^{-1}(\mathbf{x})$.

**Data Structures:**
- **Affine Parameters:** For each warp $i$, parameters $\{R_i, s_i, t_i\}$ define rotation, scaling, and translation.
    - $R_i$: Rotation matrix derived from a normalized quaternion vector.
    - $s_i$: Scaling vector.
    - $t_i$: Translation vector.
- **Weighting Volume:** A 3D voxel grid $w_i(\mathbf{x})$ representing the weight for warp $i$.
    - Resolution: $32^3$ voxels [S1].
    - Values: Non-negative, ensured by applying $\exp(\cdot)$ to the output of fully-connected layers [S1].

**Formulas:**
The inverse warp is defined as [S1]:
$$ \mathbf{W}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i \mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x}) a_i(\mathbf{x}) $$

Where $\mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x})$ is the $i$-th affine transformation:
$$ \mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x}) = R_i(s_i \circ (\mathbf{x} - t_i)) $$
Here, $\circ$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S1].

The mixture weight $a_i(\mathbf{x})$ is computed by sampling the weight volume $w_i$ *after* warping:
$$ a_i(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{w_i(\mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x}))}{\sum_j w_j(\mathbf{A}_j(\mathbf{x}))} $$
This "warped weight" approach ensures the weighting function is defined in the warped space, which represents different parts of the scene [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Encode:** Use an encoder to produce a latent vector $\mathbf{z}$ [S1].
2.  **Compute Parameters:** Use 2 fully-connected layers after encoding $\mathbf{z}$ to produce transformation parameters $\{R_i, s_i, t_i\}$ and raw weight values for each warp $i$ [S1].
3.  **Normalize Rotation:** Produce a rotation quaternion vector from the fully-connected layers, normalize it, and transform it into a rotation matrix $R_i$ [S1].
4.  **Ensure Non-Negative Weights:** Apply $\exp(\cdot)$ to the output of the fully-connected layers for the weighting volume $w_i$ to ensure non-negative weights [S1].
5.  **Global Warp (Optional):** Learn an additional global warp $\{R_g, s_g, t_g\}$ produced by an MLP. Apply this to $\mathbf{x}$ *before* the warping field [S1].
6.  **Evaluate Warp:** For a given point $\mathbf{x}$:
    a. Compute $\mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x})$ for all $i$.
    b. Sample $w_i(\mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x}))$. If $\mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x})$ is outside the weighting volume grid, clamp the sample to the surface value [S1].
    c. Compute normalized weights $a_i(\mathbf{x})$.
    d. Compute $\mathbf{W}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i \mathbf{A}_i(\mathbf{x}) a_i(\mathbf{x})$.

**Parameters:**
- Number of warps: 16 provides sufficient expressiveness [S1].
- Warp field resolution: $32^3$ voxel grid [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Samples outside the weighting volume $w_i$ must be clamped to the surface to prevent warps from getting "stuck" early in training if they land outside the volume [S1].

## Subtopics

### Mixture of Affine Warps
A warp field decoder using a spatial mixture of affine warps to avoid overfitting and poor generalization associated with freely-varying template sample points [S1]. It computes transformation parameters using 2 fully-connected layers after encoding $z$, producing a normalized quaternion for rotation [S1]. Weights are ensured non-negative via $\exp(\cdot)$ [S1]. A global warp $\{R_g, s_g, t_g\}$ is learned via MLP and applied before the warping field [S1]. It uses 16 warps and a $32^3$ voxel grid for smoothness [S1]. Pitfall: Clamp samples outside the weighting volume to the surface to prevent warps from getting "stuck" early in training [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** The model outperforms all other evaluated warping methods (no warping, convolutional warp, no warp affine mixture) on datasets including moving hand, swinging hair, and dry ice smoke [S1]. Convolutional warps completely fail on the "hair swing" dataset [S1].
- **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Avoids overfitting and poor generalization; handles non-linear bending; outperforms convolutional warps on complex dynamics like hair swing; modeling weights in warp space is better than world space [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires learning additional parameters for warps and weights; convolutional warps fail on certain datasets [S1].
- **When to use:** When modeling dynamic volumes with non-linear bending and view-dependent appearance, especially when convolutional warps fail [S1].
- **When not:** When computational resources are extremely limited and simple affine transforms suffice, or when the scene does not exhibit significant non-linear deformation [S1].

## Background
Originated in "Neural Volumes: Learning Dynamic Renderable Volumes from Images" [S1]. The method is an extension of linear blend skinning applied to volumetric space [S1]. It was developed to address limitations in previous warping techniques, particularly convolutional warps, which failed on certain dynamic datasets like swinging hair [S1].
