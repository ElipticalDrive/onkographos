# Perspective Shear-Warp

Also called: Perspective Projection Rendering 1

## Overview
Perspective Shear-Warp extends the Shear-warp algorithm to handle perspective projection, where viewing rays diverge from a focal point [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of rendering volumetric data with perspective distortion by incorporating slice scaling into the shear phase, ensuring that the resampling footprint adapts to the increasing distance from the camera [S1, unofficial]. Unlike parallel Shear-warp, which assumes uniform ray spacing, this method accounts for the non-linear sampling density inherent in perspective views [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method follows the standard Shear-warp factorization $M_{view} = M_{warp2D} \cdot S \cdot P$, but modifies the shear and compositing steps to handle diverging rays [S2, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 3D volume data $V$, perspective view matrix $M_{view}$, focal length, image resolution.
*   **Output:** 2D perspective image $I_{final}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume:** Voxel grid with scalar values and opacity.
*   **Intermediate Image:** 2D array for front-to-back compositing.
*   **RLE Tables:** For skipping transparent voxel runs and opaque pixel runs in the intermediate image [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Permutation and Shear with Scaling:**
    The volume is permuted so the principal viewing axis aligns with Z. A shear matrix $M_{shear}$ is applied, but unlike parallel projection, each slice $z$ is scaled by a factor $s_z$ to account for perspective divergence [S2, unofficial].
    *   The shear coefficients $S_i, S_j$ are derived from the view direction, but the scaling factor $s_z$ depends on the distance from the focal point [S2, unofficial].
    *   Formula for scaling: $s_z = \frac{d}{d + z}$, where $d$ is the distance from the focal point to the near clipping plane (or similar reference), and $z$ is the slice depth in sheared space [S2, unofficial].
    *   This scaling ensures that farther slices are smaller, effectively increasing the sampling footprint of rays as they travel deeper into the volume [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Shear and Composite with Adaptive Area Sampling:**
    The volume is traversed slice-by-slice in the sheared space [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Resampling:** Each slice is resampled into the intermediate 2D image using bilinear interpolation [S2, unofficial]. Because of the scaling, more than two voxel scanlines may contribute to a single intermediate image scanline, and they may not traverse at the same rate [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Adaptive Area Sampling:** To handle the diverging rays, adaptive area sampling is used. Each sample point represents a larger portion of the volume as distance increases, preventing aliasing and ensuring correct density estimation [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Compositing:** Samples are composited front-to-back:
        $$ I_{int}[y, x] = I_{int}[y, x] + C \cdot \alpha \cdot (1 - A_{int}[y, x]) $$
        $$ A_{int}[y, x] = A_{int}[y, x] + \alpha \cdot (1 - A_{int}[y, x]) $$
        where $C$ is color, $\alpha$ is opacity, and $A_{int}$ is accumulated opacity [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Optimization:** RLE is used to skip transparent voxel runs and opaque pixel runs [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Warp:**
    The intermediate image is transformed to the final image space using the 2D warp matrix $M_{warp2D}$ [S2, unofficial]. This corrects the distortion introduced by the shear and scaling [S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Perspective rays diverge, making uniform sampling difficult and leading to aliasing if not handled with adaptive area sampling [S1, unofficial].
*   Voxel scanlines may not be traversed at the same rate as image scanlines, requiring careful interpolation logic [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Stereo Rendering via Similarity Utilization
This variant leverages the geometric similarities between left and right eye views in stereo rendering to accelerate the process [S2, unofficial]. By reusing computation or data from one eye's view for the other, rendering speed is increased [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when stereo visualization is required and computational resources are limited.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Generally $O(N)$ with respect to the number of voxels and pixels, but the adaptive sampling and scaling factors add overhead compared to parallel Shear-warp [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to parallel Shear-warp, dominated by the volume data and intermediate image buffers [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Correctly handles perspective distortion; adaptive sampling reduces aliasing in diverging rays [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** More complex resampling logic due to slice scaling and non-uniform scanline traversal [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When perspective projection is required for volumetric data.
*   **When not to use:** When parallel projection is sufficient, as it adds unnecessary complexity [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Perspective Shear-Warp algorithm is a direct extension of the Shear-warp algorithm by Lacroute and Levoy, adapted for perspective views [S2, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of parallel projection in virtual environments where perspective is necessary for correct spatial perception [S2, unofficial]. The method builds on the factorization principles established in earlier Shear-warp literature [S3, unofficial].
