# Projective Splat Rasterization with Depth Optimization

Also called: Adjoint Matrix Splat Rasterization, Inverse Projective Mapping for Splatting

## Overview
A rasterization technique for point-based rendering that uses the inverse of a projective mapping matrix to transform screen coordinates back to object space for kernel evaluation, while simultaneously computing window-space depth values via linear interpolation of homogeneous coordinates [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard perspective splatting by avoiding explicit matrix inversion through the use of adjoint matrices and optimizing depth computation by embedding the window-space depth formula directly into the homogeneous interpolation weights [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Splat center $\mathbf{p}_k$, tangent vectors $\mathbf{s}_k, \mathbf{t}_k$ in screen space, view frustum near/far constants $a, b$, and screen coordinates $(x, y)$ of the fragment.
*   **Output:** Object-space coordinates $(u, v)$ for kernel evaluation and window-space depth $d(x)$ for z-buffering.

**Data Structures:**
*   $\mathbf{M}_k$: A $3 \times 3$ matrix representing the forward projective mapping from parameter space $(u, v)$ to screen space.
*   $\mathbf{N}_k$: A $4 \times 3$ matrix (or $3 \times 3$ without depth optimization) representing the scaled inverse mapping.

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Forward Mapping Matrix $\mathbf{M}_k$:**
    Construct the matrix from the splat's screen-space center and tangent vectors:
    $$ \mathbf{M}_k = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{s}_k & \mathbf{t}_k & \mathbf{p}_k \end{bmatrix} $$
    where $\mathbf{s}_k, \mathbf{t}_k, \mathbf{p}_k$ are 3D vectors (typically 2D with $z=1$ or homogeneous 3D vectors depending on implementation, but the source defines them as components of the $3 \times 3$ matrix acting on $\begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$). The forward mapping is:
    $$ \begin{bmatrix} x_z \\ y_z \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{M}_k \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

2.  **Compute Adjoint Matrix:**
    Calculate the adjoint of $\mathbf{M}_k$ using three cross products. This avoids the computational cost of explicit matrix inversion [S1]:
    $$ \text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{t}_k \times \mathbf{p}_k \\ \mathbf{p}_k \times \mathbf{s}_k \\ \mathbf{s}_k \times \mathbf{t}_k \end{bmatrix} $$
    Let $\text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k)_3$ denote the third row of this matrix.

3.  **Compute Scaling Factor:**
    Calculate the scalar $\alpha$ to normalize the homogeneous coefficient $w$ such that it relates to the view-space depth $p_z$ (the $z$-component of $\mathbf{p}_k$) and the window-space depth constant $b$:
    $$ \alpha = \frac{b}{\text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k)_3 \cdot \mathbf{p}_k} $$
    Note: $\text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k)_3 \cdot \mathbf{p}_k$ is the dot product of the third row of the adjoint matrix and the center vector $\mathbf{p}_k$ [S1].

4.  **Construct Inverse Mapping Matrix $\mathbf{N}_k$:**
    Scale the adjoint matrix by $\alpha$. To optimize depth computation, extend $\mathbf{N}_k$ to a $4 \times 3$ matrix. The first three rows are $\alpha \cdot \text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k)$. The fourth row $(\mathbf{N}_k)_4$ is computed as:
    $$ (\mathbf{N}_k)_4 = \alpha \cdot \text{adj}(\mathbf{M}_k)_3 + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ a \end{bmatrix} $$
    This construction ensures that the homogeneous weight $w$ in the inverse mapping equals $b/z$, allowing the fourth component to directly yield the window-space depth $d(x) = a + b/z$ via linear interpolation [S1].

5.  **Rasterize and Interpolate:**
    Rasterize the splat's bounding box (or projected triangle). For each pixel $(x, y)$, compute the homogeneous coordinates by multiplying the inverse matrix $\mathbf{N}_k$ with the screen coordinate vector $\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$:
    $$ \begin{bmatrix} uw \\ vw \\ w \\ d \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{N}_k \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    The values $uw, vw, w$ are linearly interpolated across the primitive. The object-space coordinates are recovered by perspective division:
    $$ u = \frac{uw}{w}, \quad v = \frac{vw}{w} $$
    The window-space depth is directly available as the fourth component $d$ (if the $4 \times 3$ matrix is used) or computed as $a + b/(w/b) = a + b/z$ if only the $3 \times 3$ matrix is used [S1].

6.  **Kernel Evaluation:**
    Use the recovered $(u, v)$ to evaluate the splat's reconstruction kernel.

**Parameters:**
*   $a, b$: Constants derived from the near and far planes of the view frustum, mapping $1/z$ to the window space depth range $[0, 1]$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Matrix Inversion Cost:** Explicitly inverting $\mathbf{M}_k$ is computationally expensive. The method avoids this by using the adjoint matrix, which is equivalent for projective mappings up to a scalar multiple and requires only cross products [S1].
*   **Degenerate Splats:** If $\mathbf{p}_k, \mathbf{s}_k, \mathbf{t}_k$ are collinear or $\mathbf{p}_k$ is at the origin, the adjoint matrix may be singular or the scaling factor undefined. [G] In practice, check for zero determinant or small scaling factors and skip rasterization or fall back to a safe default.
*   **Precision:** Linear interpolation of homogeneous coordinates is valid for projective mappings. However, ensure that $w$ does not become zero or negative (behind the camera) during interpolation, which would cause division by zero or incorrect depth ordering. [G] Clip fragments where $w \le 0$.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat for matrix construction, $O(N)$ for rasterizing $N$ pixels within the splat's bounding box. The use of adjoint matrices and pre-scaled homogeneous weights minimizes per-pixel arithmetic cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per splat for storing $\mathbf{M}_k$ and $\mathbf{N}_k$.
*   **Performance:** Highly efficient on GPUs due to native support for cross products and 4-component vector instructions. The optimization of embedding depth computation into the homogeneous weights reduces per-pixel operations to a single addition and interpolation [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Perspective accurate, avoids expensive matrix inversion, integrates seamlessly with standard z-buffering via window-space depth interpolation.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of homogeneous weights to avoid division by zero. Performance may degrade if splats are very large or numerous, requiring efficient culling.

## Background
Originated in the context of point-based rendering, specifically for perspective-accurate splatting. The method is described in "Splat/Mesh Blending, Perspective Rasterization and Transparency for Point-Based Rendering" [S1]. It addresses the need for efficient, perspective-correct rasterization of splats without the overhead of full 3D-to-2D projection inversion per pixel.
