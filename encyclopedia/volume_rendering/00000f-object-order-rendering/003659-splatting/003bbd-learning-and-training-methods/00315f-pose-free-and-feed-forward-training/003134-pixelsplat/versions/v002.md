# pixelSplat

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splats from Image Pairs, Epipolar Transformer Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
pixelSplat is a feed-forward model that reconstructs a 3D radiance field parameterized by 3D Gaussian primitives from a pair of input images [S1]. It solves the problem of generalizable novel view synthesis by replacing the iterative, memory-intensive optimization of single-scene methods with a direct neural mapping, while avoiding the local minima inherent to sparse, locally-supported primitive representations [S1]. Unlike light-field transformers that produce black-box ray fields, pixelSplat generates an explicit, editable 3D representation that enables real-time rasterization-based rendering [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A pair of images $I_1, I_2$ and their associated camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) [S1].
**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\{G_i\}$, each defined by mean $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and scale $S_i$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The model predicts parameters for pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives. For each pixel $u$ in the reference view, a feature vector $\mathbf{F}[u]$ is computed. The prediction network $f$ outputs:
*   $\phi$: A scalar probability density value (logit or raw output before softmax/sigmoid, depending on implementation details not fully specified, but used to form a distribution).
*   $\boldsymbol{\delta}$: The depth offset along the camera ray for the mean $\mu$.
*   $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$: The covariance matrix (or parameters to construct it).
*   $\mathbf{S}$: The scale factor [S1].

The mean $\mu$ is constructed by projecting the pixel $u$ into 3D space using the camera pose and adding the predicted depth offset $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ along the ray direction [S1].

**Algorithm:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** Pass each reference image $I_j$ through a ResNet-50 and a ViT-B/8 vision transformer, both pre-trained with a DINO objective. Sum their pixel-wise outputs to generate initial features [S1].
2.  **Epipolar Encoding:** Perform two rounds of epipolar cross-attention between the features of the two views. This step utilizes frequency-based positional encoding of depth to resolve scale ambiguity [S1].
3.  **Probability Distribution Prediction:** For each pixel $u$ in the reference view, use the encoded feature $\mathbf{F}[u]$ to predict a dense probability distribution over 3D space along the camera ray. The network $f$ outputs $\phi, \boldsymbol{\delta}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}, \mathbf{S}$ conditioned on $\mathbf{F}[u]$ [S1].
    $$ \phi, \boldsymbol{\delta}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}, \mathbf{S} = f(\mathbf{F}[u]) $$
4.  **Differentiable Sampling (Reparameterization Trick):** Sample Gaussian means from the predicted dense probability distribution. The sampling operation is made differentiable via a reparameterization trick. This couples the density of a sampled Gaussian primitive to the probability of that location, allowing gradients to back-propagate through the sampling operation [S1].
    *   *Mechanism:* When a gradient indicates that increasing the opacity of a Gaussian at a specific 3D location would reduce loss, the model increases the probability that a Gaussian will be sampled at that location in future training steps. This implicitly spawns or deletes primitives without non-differentiable pruning/splitting [S1].
5.  **Rendering:** Render the predicted Gaussians using standard differentiable volume rendering/rasterization [S1].
6.  **Loss Calculation:** Minimize the combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) between the rendered image and the ground-truth image [S1].
7.  **Optimization:** Use the Adam optimizer [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Training resolution: $256 \times 256$ pixels [S1].
*   Loss weights: Not specified in sources.
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scale Ambiguity:** SfM poses are scaled by arbitrary unknown factors $s_i$. The model handles this by inferring the per-scene scale factor via the epipolar transformer, which finds cross-view pixel correspondences associated with positionally encoded depth values [S1]. Without this, ghosting artifacts occur [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Direct regression of Gaussian means fails because gradients vanish if the Gaussian is far from the correct location (local support) or if the path to the correct location traverses empty space (non-monotonic loss). The probabilistic sampling scheme avoids this [S1].
*   **Out-of-Distribution Views:** Reflective surfaces may appear transparent, and Gaussians may appear billboard-like when viewed from poses outside the training distribution [S1].

## Subtopics

### MVSplat
A method that builds upon pixelSplat by combining multi-view feature extraction through transformers and cost volumes, which are then processed by a U-Net [S3]. It extends the two-view input to multi-view inputs.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference cost is approximately 650 times less than the next-fastest baseline for encoding one scene and rendering 100 images [S1]. It accelerates rendering by 2.5 orders of magnitude compared to state-of-the-art light field transformers [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly less memory per ray at training time compared to baselines like pixelNeRF and GPNR [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time and memory-efficient rendering; explicit, editable 3D representation; fast 3D reconstruction at inference time; outperforms baselines on perceptual quality (LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Suffers from transparency on reflective surfaces and billboard artifacts on out-of-distribution views, similar to standard 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1]. Requires known camera poses (unlike NoPoSplat) [S3].
*   **When to use:** Wide-baseline novel view synthesis from image pairs where real-time rendering and explicit 3D structure are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When camera poses are unknown (use NoPoSplat) or when monocular reconstruction is required (use Flash3D) [S3].

## Background
pixelSplat was introduced by Charatan, Li, Tagliasacchi, and Sitzmann from MIT and Simon Fraser University/University of Toronto [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2024 [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of differentiable rendering (memory/time intensive) and light-field transformers (lack of editable 3D representation) by integrating 3D Gaussian primitives into a generalizable, feed-forward framework [S1]. It is benchmarked on the RealEstate10k and ACID datasets [S1].
