# Gradient of 2D Gaussian PDF (Conic)

Also called: Gradient of 2D Gaussian Probability Density (Normalized/Unnormalized Factor Only), Gradient of 2D Gaussian Probability Density from Conic and Rescale

## Overview
Computes the probability density value $p$ and its gradients with respect to the Gaussian mean ($\frac{dp}{d\_mean}$) and covariance ($\frac{dp}{d\_cov}$) for a 2D Gaussian distribution. This method provides three variants: normalized (including the $1/(2\pi\sqrt{\det})$ factor), unnormalized (excluding the factor), and conic-based (using the conic matrix representation). It builds on [2D Gaussian Probability Density (Standard)](3466-2d-gaussian-probability-density-standard.md) by providing the necessary derivatives for optimization, specifically for training Gaussian splats where gradients must flow back to mean and covariance parameters.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- `xy`: 2D point coordinates, vector $\mathbb{R}^2$.
- `gaussian_mean`: 2D mean vector, $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
- `gaussian_covariance`: 2x2 symmetric positive-definite covariance matrix, $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$.
- `conic_and_rescale`: (For conic variant) A 4D vector or struct containing the conic matrix elements and a rescale factor. The conic matrix $C$ is derived from the inverse covariance $\Sigma^{-1}$. The vector contains $[C_{00}, C_{01}, C_{11}, R]$ where $R$ is the rescale factor. Note: The source code implies a specific layout for the conic matrix elements.

**Outputs:**
- `p`: Scalar probability density value.
- `d_p_d_mean`: Gradient of $p$ with respect to `gaussian_mean`, vector $\mathbb{R}^2$.
- `d_p_d_cov`: Gradient of $p$ with respect to `gaussian_covariance`, matrix $\mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ (or flattened representation depending on implementation, but mathematically a matrix).

### Data Structures
- **Vector2**: 2D vector with components $x, y$.
- **Matrix2**: 2x2 matrix with elements $M_{00}, M_{01}, M_{10}, M_{11}$. For symmetric covariance, $M_{01} = M_{10}$.
- **ConicAndRescale**: A structure or vector of 4 floats. Based on the source code, the layout is:
  - `.x`: $C_{00}$
  - `.y`: $C_{01}$ (which equals $C_{10}$)
  - `.z`: $C_{11}$
  - `.w`: Rescale factor $R$

### Formulas
Let $\Delta = xy - gaussian\_mean$.

**1. Normalized Variant:**
$$ p = \frac{\exp(-0.5 \Delta^T \Sigma^{-1} \Delta)}{2\pi\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}} $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_mean} = p \cdot \Sigma^{-1} \Delta $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_cov} = -0.5 p (\Sigma^{-1} - \Sigma^{-1} \Delta \Delta^T \Sigma^{-1}) $$

**2. Unnormalized Variant:**
$$ p = \exp(-0.5 \Delta^T \Sigma^{-1} \Delta) $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_mean} = p \cdot \Sigma^{-1} \Delta $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_cov} = 0.5 p (\Sigma^{-1} \Delta \Delta^T \Sigma^{-1}) $$

**3. Conic Variant:**
Let $C$ be the conic matrix (inverse covariance) and $R$ be the rescale factor.
$$ p = \exp(-0.5 \Delta^T C \Delta) \cdot R $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_mean} = p \cdot C \Delta $$
$$ \frac{dp}{d\_cov} = 0.5 p (C \Delta \Delta^T C) $$
*Note: The gradient with respect to the rescale factor $R$ is not computed.*

### Steps

**For Normalized and Unnormalized Variants:**

1.  Compute the displacement vector: $\Delta = xy - gaussian\_mean$.
2.  Compute the determinant of the covariance matrix: $\det(\Sigma) = \Sigma_{00}\Sigma_{11} - \Sigma_{01}\Sigma_{10}$.
3.  Compute the inverse covariance matrix $\Sigma^{-1}$:
    $$ \Sigma^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(\Sigma)} \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{11} & -\Sigma_{01} \\ -\Sigma_{10} & \Sigma_{00} \end{bmatrix} $$
4.  Compute the term $\Sigma^{-1} \Delta$:
    $$ v = \Sigma^{-1} \Delta $$
5.  Compute the exponent term:
    $$ exponent = -0.5 (\Delta^T v) = -0.5 (\Delta_x v_x + \Delta_y v_y) $$
6.  Compute the exponential:
    $$ exp\_val = \exp(exponent) $$
7.  Compute $p$:
    -   **Normalized**: $p = \frac{exp\_val}{2\pi\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}}$
    -   **Unnormalized**: $p = exp\_val$
8.  Compute $\frac{dp}{d\_mean}$:
    $$ \frac{dp}{d\_mean} = p \cdot v $$
9.  Compute the outer product $\Delta \Delta^T$:
    $$ outer = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_x^2 & \Delta_x \Delta_y \\ \Delta_y \Delta_x & \Delta_y^2 \end{bmatrix} $$
10. Compute $\frac{dp}{d\_cov}$:
    -   **Normalized**: $\frac{dp}{d\_cov} = -0.5 p (\Sigma^{-1} - \Sigma^{-1} outer \Sigma^{-1})$
    -   **Unnormalized**: $\frac{dp}{d\_cov} = 0.5 p (\Sigma^{-1} outer \Sigma^{-1})$

**For Conic Variant:**

1.  Compute the displacement vector: $\Delta = xy - gaussian\_mean$.
2.  Construct the conic matrix $C$ from `conic_and_rescale`:
    $$ C = \begin{bmatrix} conic\_and\_rescale.x & conic\_and\_rescale.y \\ conic\_and\_rescale.y & conic\_and\_rescale.z \end{bmatrix} $$
3.  Compute the term $C \Delta$:
    $$ v = C \Delta $$
4.  Compute the exponent term:
    $$ exponent = -0.5 (\Delta^T v) = -0.5 (\Delta_x v_x + \Delta_y v_y) $$
5.  Compute the exponential:
    $$ exp\_val = \exp(exponent) $$
6.  Compute $p$:
    $$ p = exp\_val \cdot conic\_and\_rescale.w $$
7.  Compute $\frac{dp}{d\_mean}$:
    $$ \frac{dp}{d\_mean} = p \cdot v $$
8.  Compute the outer product $\Delta \Delta^T$:
    $$ outer = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta_x^2 & \Delta_x \Delta_y \\ \Delta_y \Delta_x & \Delta_y^2 \end{bmatrix} $$
9.  Compute $\frac{dp}{d\_cov}$ (gradient w.r.t. conic matrix elements, interpreted as covariance gradient in the source context):
    $$ \frac{dp}{d\_cov} = 0.5 p (C outer C) $$

### Pseudocode

```python
import math

def gradient_2d_gaussian_normalized(xy, gaussian_mean, gaussian_covariance):
    """
    Computes p, dp/d_mean, dp/d_cov for normalized 2D Gaussian.
    xy: (2,) array
    gaussian_mean: (2,) array
    gaussian_covariance: (2,2) array
    """
    # 1. Displacement
    delta = xy - gaussian_mean
    
    # 2. Determinant
    det_cov = gaussian_covariance[0,0] * gaussian_covariance[1,1] - gaussian_covariance[0,1] * gaussian_covariance[1,0]
    
    # 3. Inverse Covariance
    inv_cov = (1.0 / det_cov) * \
              [[gaussian_covariance[1,1], -gaussian_covariance[0,1]],
               [-gaussian_covariance[1,0], gaussian_covariance[0,0]]]
    
    # 4. inv_cov @ delta
    cov_inv_delta = [
        inv_cov[0][0] * delta[0] + inv_cov[0][1] * delta[1],
        inv_cov[1][0] * delta[0] + inv_cov[1][1] * delta[1]
    ]
    
    # 5. Exponent
    exponent = -0.5 * (delta[0] * cov_inv_delta[0] + delta[1] * cov_inv_delta[1])
    
    # 6. Exponential
    exp_val = math.exp(exponent)
    
    # 7. p (Normalized)
    p = exp_val / (2.0 * math.pi * math.sqrt(det_cov))
    
    # 8. dp/d_mean
    d_p_d_mean = [p * cov_inv_delta[0], p * cov_inv_delta[1]]
    
    # 9. Outer Product delta * delta^T
    outer = [
        [delta[0]*delta[0], delta[0]*delta[1]],
        [delta[1]*delta[0], delta[1]*delta[1]]
    ]
    
    # 10. dp/d_cov
    # Term: inv_cov @ outer @ inv_cov
    # First: inv_cov @ outer
    temp1 = [
        [inv_cov[0][0]*outer[0][0] + inv_cov[0][1]*outer[1][0], inv_cov[0][0]*outer[0][1] + inv_cov[0][1]*outer[1][1]],
        [inv_cov[1][0]*outer[0][0] + inv_cov[1][1]*outer[1][0], inv_cov[1][0]*outer[0][1] + inv_cov[1][1]*outer[1][1]]
    ]
    # Second: temp1 @ inv_cov
    temp2 = [
        [temp1[0][0]*inv_cov[0][0] + temp1[0][1]*inv_cov[1][0], temp1[0][0]*inv_cov[0][1] + temp1[0][1]*inv_cov[1][1]],
        [temp1[1][0]*inv_cov[0][0] + temp1[1][1]*inv_cov[1][0], temp1[1][0]*inv_cov[0][1] + temp1[1][1]*inv_cov[1][1]]
    ]
    
    # dp/d_cov = -0.5 * p * (inv_cov - temp2)
    d_p_d_cov = [
        [-0.5 * p * (inv_cov[0][0] - temp2[0][0]), -0.5 * p * (inv_cov[0][1] - temp2[0][1])],
        [-0.5 * p * (inv_cov[1][0] - temp2[1][0]), -0.5 * p * (inv_cov[1][1] - temp2[1][1])]
    ]
    
    return p, d_p_d_mean, d_p_d_cov

def gradient_2d_gaussian_unnormalized(xy, gaussian_mean, gaussian_covariance):
    """
    Computes p, dp/d_mean, dp/d_cov for unnormalized 2D Gaussian.
    """
    delta = xy - gaussian_mean
    
    det_cov = gaussian_covariance[0,0] * gaussian_covariance[1,1] - gaussian_covariance[0,1] * gaussian_covariance[1,0]
    
    inv_cov = (1.0 / det_cov) * \
              [[gaussian_covariance[1,1], -gaussian_covariance[0,1]],
               [-gaussian_covariance[1,0], gaussian_covariance[0,0]]]
    
    cov_inv_delta = [
        inv_cov[0][0] * delta[0] + inv_cov[0][1] * delta[1],
        inv_cov[1][0] * delta[0] + inv_cov[1][1] * delta[1]
    ]
    
    exponent = -0.5 * (delta[0] * cov_inv_delta[0] + delta[1] * cov_inv_delta[1])
    exp_val = math.exp(exponent)
    
    p = exp_val
    
    d_p_d_mean = [p * cov_inv_delta[0], p * cov_inv_delta[1]]
    
    outer = [
        [delta[0]*delta[0], delta[0]*delta[1]],
        [delta[1]*delta[0], delta[1]*delta[1]]
    ]
    
    temp1 = [
        [inv_cov[0][0]*outer[0][0] + inv_cov[0][1]*outer[1][0], inv_cov[0][0]*outer[0][1] + inv_cov[0][1]*outer[1][1]],
        [inv_cov[1][0]*outer[0][0] + inv_cov[1][1]*outer[1][0], inv_cov[1][0]*outer[0][1] + inv_cov[1][1]*outer[1][1]]
    ]
    temp2 = [
        [temp1[0][0]*inv_cov[0][0] + temp1[0][1]*inv_cov[1][0], temp1[0][0]*inv_cov[0][1] + temp1[0][1]*inv_cov[1][1]],
        [temp1[1][0]*inv_cov[0][0] + temp1[1][1]*inv_cov[1][0], temp1[1][0]*inv_cov[0][1] + temp1[1][1]*inv_cov[1][1]]
    ]
    
    # dp/d_cov = 0.5 * p * (inv_cov @ outer @ inv_cov)
    d_p_d_cov = [
        [0.5 * p * temp2[0][0], 0.5 * p * temp2[0][1]],
        [0.5 * p * temp2[1][0], 0.5 * p * temp2[1][1]]
    ]
    
    return p, d_p_d_mean, d_p_d_cov

def gradient_2d_gaussian_conic(xy, gaussian_mean, conic_and_rescale):
    """
    Computes p, dp/d_mean, dp/d_cov for conic 2D Gaussian.
    conic_and_rescale: [C00, C01, C11, R]
    """
    delta = xy - gaussian_mean
    
    C = [
        [conic_and_rescale[0], conic_and_rescale[1]],
        [conic_and_rescale[1], conic_and_rescale[2]]
    ]
    
    C_delta = [
        C[0][0] * delta[0] + C[0][1] * delta[1],
        C[1][0] * delta[0] + C[1][1] * delta[1]
    ]
    
    exponent = -0.5 * (delta[0] * C_delta[0] + delta[1] * C_delta[1])
    exp_val = math.exp(exponent)
    
    p = exp_val * conic_and_rescale[3]
    
    d_p_d_mean = [p * C_delta[0], p * C_delta[1]]
    
    outer = [
        [delta[0]*delta[0], delta[0]*delta[1]],
        [delta[1]*delta[0], delta[1]*delta[1]]
    ]
    
    temp1 = [
        [C[0][0]*outer[0][0] + C[0][1]*outer[1][0], C[0][0]*outer[0][1] + C[0][1]*outer[1][1]],
        [C[1][0]*outer[0][0] + C[1][1]*outer[1][0], C[1][0]*outer[0][1] + C[1][1]*outer[1][1]]
    ]
    temp2 = [
        [temp1[0][0]*C[0][0] + temp1[0][1]*C[1][0], temp1[0][0]*C[0][1] + temp1[0][1]*C[1][1]],
        [temp1[1][0]*C[0][0] + temp1[1][1]*C[1][0], temp1[1][0]*C[0][1] + temp1[1][1]*C[1][1]]
    ]
    
    # dp/d_cov = 0.5 * p * (C @ outer @ C)
    d_p_d_cov = [
        [0.5 * p * temp2[0][0], 0.5 * p * temp2[0][1]],
        [0.5 * p * temp2[1][0], 0.5 * p * temp2[1][1]]
    ]
    
    return p, d_p_d_mean, d_p_d_cov
```

### Parameters with Typical Values
- No specific typical values are given for `xy`, `mean`, or `cov` in the sources, as they depend on the scene and camera.
- The normalization factor is $2\pi\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}$.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Rescale Factor Gradient**: The gradient with respect to the rescale factor (`conic_and_rescale.w`) is **not** computed. This is an explicit design choice in the source code: "known caveat: we don't intend to differentiate w.r.t. rescale" [S1, unofficial].
- **Determinant Zero**: If $\det(\Sigma) = 0$, the inverse covariance is undefined. The source code does not explicitly handle this, implying it is assumed that the covariance is always positive definite. [G] In practice, a small epsilon should be added to the determinant to avoid division by zero.
- **Precision**: The computation involves exponentials and matrix inversions. For very large or small variances, numerical stability should be considered.

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
- **Time**: $O(1)$ per Gaussian evaluation. The operations involve fixed-size matrix multiplications (2x2) and vector operations.
- **Memory**: $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per evaluation.

### Strengths and Limitations
- **Strengths**: Provides exact gradients for optimization. The conic variant avoids explicit matrix inversion, which can be more stable and efficient if the conic matrix is already available.
- **Limitations**: The conic variant does not provide gradients for the rescale factor. The normalized variant requires computing the determinant and its square root, which can be slightly more expensive than the unnormalized variant.

### When to Use
- Use the **normalized** variant when the absolute probability density value is required for loss functions or normalization.
- Use the **unnormalized** variant when the normalization constant is handled separately or is irrelevant for the optimization (e.g., if it cancels out in the gradient).
- Use the **conic** variant when the conic matrix is already computed (e.g., from a previous step in the rendering pipeline) to avoid redundant matrix inversion.
