# Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering, Vanilla 3DGS rendering

## Overview
Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a novel-view synthesis method that represents a scene as an explicit collection of anisotropic 3D Gaussians carrying opacity and view-dependent color, optimized directly from calibrated images [S5, unofficial]. It solves the computational bottleneck of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by replacing implicit neural queries with explicit primitives rendered via a tile-based differentiable rasterizer, achieving real-time performance without ray marching [S5, unofficial]. Unlike NeRF, which requires supervising empty space and hundreds of MLP evaluations per pixel, 3DGS initializes Gaussians on geometry (SfM points), avoiding costly queries in empty space and enabling efficient rasterization [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of posed input images $\{I_i\}$ and their corresponding camera poses.
**Outputs:** A rendered image $I_f$ for a given camera view, and a set of optimized Gaussian parameters $\theta$.

**Data Structures:**
The scene is represented by a set of $M$ 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by the following parameters [S1][S12, unofficial]:
*   $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position in world space.
*   $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix describing shape and orientation. To ensure positive semi-definiteness during optimization, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ is parameterized via factorization [S5, unofficial]:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^\top \mathbf{R}_i^\top $$
    where $\mathbf{R}_i$ is a rotation matrix derived from a unit quaternion $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, and $\mathbf{S}_i$ is a diagonal scaling matrix derived from a scale vector $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S5, S12, unofficial]. The scaling vector is typically optimized in log space [S4, unofficial].
*   $\sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity (logit). This is a scalar value assigned to the primitive, distinct from integrated extinction [S2].
*   $\mathbf{h}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 3}$: Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients (up to band 3, i.e., 48 coefficients) encoding view-dependent color [S4, S13, unofficial].

**Rendering Pipeline:**
The rendering process projects 3D Gaussians to 2D screen-space ellipses and composites them via alpha blending [S2].

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussian centers $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ and colors from a sparse point cloud obtained via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1][S5, unofficial]. Random initialization is avoided as it introduces artifacts and "floaters" [S5, unofficial].
2.  **3D-to-2D Projection (EWA Splatting):**
    *   Transform the 3D Gaussian center to camera coordinates: $\boldsymbol{\mu}'_i = \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, where $\mathbf{W}$ is the camera pose matrix [S12, unofficial].
    *   Approximate the non-linear perspective projection $\phi_{\mathbf{K}}$ using a first-order Taylor expansion (Jacobian $\mathbf{J} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 3}$) around the center [S1][S12, unofficial]:
        $$ \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i := \phi_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\mu}_i) $$
        $$ \tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_i := \mathbf{J} \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{J}^\top $$
        Here, $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i$ is the 2D mean and $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_i$ is the projected 2D covariance [S1][S12, unofficial].
    *   The opacity and color attributes are preserved: $\tilde{\sigma}_i = \sigma_i$ and $\tilde{c}_i = c_i$ [S1].
3.  **Tile-Based Sorting:**
    *   Partition the image into tiles (typically $16 \times 16$ pixels) [S8].
    *   Identify Gaussians intersecting the view frustum and assign them to overlapping tiles [S8].
    *   Sort the Gaussians within each tile by depth (distance from camera) using a GPU-optimized Radix sort [S8]. This global sorting step is an approximation that can cause popping artifacts [S2][S13, unofficial].
4.  **Alpha Compositing (Rasterization):**
    *   For each pixel, traverse the sorted list of overlapping Gaussians.
    *   Compute the 2D Gaussian value at pixel $\mathbf{p}$:
        $$ \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_i(\mathbf{p}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{p} - \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i)^\top \tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_i^{-1} (\mathbf{p} - \tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i)\right) $$
    *   Compute the effective opacity $\alpha_i$ for the splat at $\mathbf{p}$:
        $$ \alpha_i = \tilde{\sigma}_i \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_i(\mathbf{p}) $$
        Note: The maximum alpha is often clamped to 0.99 to prevent full opacity from blocking subsequent splats entirely [S11, unofficial].
    *   Accumulate color using alpha blending. Let $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ be the transmittance. The final color $C_{\mathbf{p}}$ is:
        $$ C_{\mathbf{p}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_i \alpha_i T_i $$
        where $c_i$ is the color of the $i$-th Gaussian evaluated from its SH coefficients using the viewing direction [S7, S13, unofficial].
    *   Include background color $c_{\text{BG}}$ and background opacity $\alpha_{\text{BG}}$ for numerical stability [S10, unofficial]:
        $$ \hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i + \alpha_{\text{BG}} $$
        $$ C_{\text{final}} = \frac{1}{\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{p})} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i c_i + \alpha_{\text{BG}} c_{\text{BG}} \right) $$
        If $\hat{\alpha}(\mathbf{p}) \approx 0$, use $c_{\text{BG}}$ [S10, unofficial].

**Optimization:**
*   Minimize photometric loss between rendered image $I_{\theta,i}$ and input view $I_i$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \|\mathbf{I}_{\theta,i} - \mathbf{I}_i\|^2 + \lambda_{\text{SSIM}} \cdot \text{SSIM}(\mathbf{I}_{\theta,i}, \mathbf{I}_i) $$
    [S6, S12, unofficial].
*   **Adaptive Density Control:** During training, dynamically adjust the number of Gaussians [S1][S12, unofficial]:
    *   **Split:** Split Gaussians that are too large (based on screen-space gradient magnitude).
    *   **Clone:** Clone Gaussians that underfit local detail (high gradient magnitude but small size).
    *   **Prune:** Remove Gaussians with persistently low opacity.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def render_gaussian_splats(gaussians, camera, image_width, image_height, tile_size=16):
    """
    Renders a scene of 3D Gaussians using tile-based rasterization.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of dicts, each containing:
            'mu': (3,) array, center position
            'q': (4,) array, unit quaternion for rotation
            's': (3,) array, scale vector (log space)
            'opacity': float, opacity logit
            'sh_coeffs': (48,) array, SH coefficients (band 3)
        camera: Dict containing 'W' (4x4 view matrix), 'K' (3x3 intrinsics).
        image_width, image_height: int
        tile_size: int
        
    Returns:
        image: (H, W, 3) array, rendered RGB image
    """
    # 1. Precompute 3D Covariance Matrices
    # Sigma = R * S * S^T * R^T
    # R is derived from quaternion q
    # S is diagonal matrix from scale s
    # This ensures Sigma is positive semi-definite
    for g in gaussians:
        R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(g['q'])
        S = np.diag(np.exp(g['s'])) # Scale is stored in log space
        g['Sigma'] = R @ (S @ S.T) @ R.T

    # 2. Project to 2D and Filter
    projected_gaussians = []
    for g in gaussians:
        # Transform center to camera space
        mu_cam = camera['W'] @ np.append(g['mu'], 1.0)
        mu_cam = mu_cam[:3] / mu_cam[2] # Perspective divide
        
        # Compute Jacobian J of projection at mu_cam
        # J approximates d(proj(x))/dx at x=mu_cam
        J = compute_projection_jacobian(mu_cam, camera['K'])
        
        # Project Covariance: Sigma_2d = J * W * Sigma_3d * W^T * J^T
        # Note: W here is the rotation part of the view matrix if Sigma is in world coords
        # Assuming Sigma is in world coords, we need the full transformation
        # Simplified: Sigma_2d = J * (Rotation_W) * Sigma_3d * (Rotation_W)^T * J^T
        # The source [S12, unofficial] uses W as camera pose matrix transforming to camera system.
        # Let's assume g['Sigma'] is in world space.
        # Rotation from world to camera is the upper 3x3 of camera['W']
        R_wc = camera['W'][:3, :3]
        Sigma_2d = J @ (R_wc @ g['Sigma'] @ R_wc.T) @ J.T
        
        # 2D Mean
        mu_2d = camera['K'] @ mu_cam
        mu_2d = mu_2d[:2] / mu_2d[2]
        
        # Compute 3-sigma bounding box
        # Eigenvalues of Sigma_2d give variance
        eigenvalues = np.linalg.eigvalsh(Sigma_2d)
        # 3-sigma extent
        extent_x = 3.0 * np.sqrt(max(eigenvalues[0], 1e-6))
        extent_y = 3.0 * np.sqrt(max(eigenvalues[1], 1e-6))
        
        # Bounding box in pixel coordinates
        min_x = int(np.floor(mu_2d[0] - extent_x))
        max_x = int(np.ceil(mu_2d[0] + extent_x))
        min_y = int(np.floor(mu_2d[1] - extent_y))
        max_y = int(np.ceil(mu_2d[1] + extent_y))
        
        # Clip to image bounds
        min_x = max(0, min_x)
        min_y = max(0, min_y)
        max_x = min(image_width, max_x)
        max_y = min(image_height, max_y)
        
        if min_x >= max_x or min_y >= max_y:
            continue
            
        projected_gaussians.append({
            'mu_2d': mu_2d,
            'Sigma_2d': Sigma_2d,
            'opacity': g['opacity'],
            'sh_coeffs': g['sh_coeffs'],
            'mu_cam_z': mu_cam[2], # For sorting
            'bbox': (min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y)
        })

    # 3. Tile-based Sorting
    # Assign to tiles
    tiles = {}
    for i, pg in enumerate(projected_gaussians):
        min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y = pg['bbox']
        # Assign to all tiles the bbox overlaps
        for ty in range(min_y // tile_size, (max_y + tile_size - 1) // tile_size + 1):
            for tx in range(min_x // tile_size, (max_x + tile_size - 1) // tile_size + 1):
                tile_key = (tx, ty)
                if tile_key not in tiles:
                    tiles[tile_key] = []
                tiles[tile_key].append(i)
                
    # Sort each tile by depth (z)
    for tile_key in tiles:
        tiles[tile_key].sort(key=lambda idx: projected_gaussians[idx]['mu_cam_z'])

    # 4. Rasterization
    image = np.zeros((image_height, image_width, 3))
    alpha_accum = np.zeros((image_height, image_width))
    
    # Background color (white)
    bg_color = np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0])
    bg_alpha = 0.0 # Or a small value for stability
    
    for (tx, ty), indices in tiles.items():
        x_start = tx * tile_size
        y_start = ty * tile_size
        x_end = min(x_start + tile_size, image_width)
        y_end = min(y_start + tile_size, image_height)
        
        for y in range(y_start, y_end):
            for x in range(x_start, x_end):
                # Current accumulated color and alpha
                current_color = image[y, x]
                current_alpha = alpha_accum[y, x]
                
                for idx in indices:
                    pg = projected_gaussians[idx]
                    mu_2d = pg['mu_2d']
                    Sigma_2d = pg['Sigma_2d']
                    opacity_logit = pg['opacity']
                    sh_coeffs = pg['sh_coeffs']
                    
                    # Compute 2D Gaussian value at (x, y)
                    # power = -0.5 * (x - mu)^T * Sigma^-1 * (x - mu)
                    # Using conic matrix C = Sigma^-1
                    try:
                        C = np.linalg.inv(Sigma_2d)
                    except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
                        continue
                        
                    dx = x - mu_2d[0]
                    dy = y - mu_2d[1]
                    power = -0.5 * (C[0,0]*dx*dx + C[1,1]*dy*dy + 2*C[0,1]*dx*dy)
                    
                    if power > 0.0:
                        continue # Outside effective area
                        
                    gaussian_val = np.exp(power)
                    
                    # Effective alpha
                    # Opacity is stored as logit, convert to [0,1]
                    # Note: Source [S1] says opacity is preserved. 
                    # Source [S11, unofficial] says alpha = sigma * exp(...). 
                    # Source [S4, unofficial] says opacity is stored as logit.
                    # Let's assume 'opacity' in gaussians is the logit sigma.
                    sigma = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-opacity_logit)) # Sigmoid
                    
                    alpha = sigma * gaussian_val
                    alpha = min(alpha, 0.99) # Clamp max alpha
                    
                    if alpha < 1.0/255.0:
                        continue
                        
                    # Compute color from SH
                    # Viewing direction from camera to Gaussian center
                    # Approximate with ray direction to pixel center or Gaussian center
                    # For simplicity, use direction from camera origin to mu_cam
                    view_dir = mu_2d # This is 2D, need 3D view dir
                    # Reconstruct 3D view dir from mu_cam
                    # This is an approximation. Ideally use the original 3D mu_cam
                    # Let's assume we have access to the 3D view direction
                    # For this pseudocode, we'll use a placeholder for SH evaluation
                    color = evaluate_sh(sh_coeffs, view_dir) # Placeholder
                    
                    # Alpha blending
                    # C_new = (1 - alpha) * C_old + alpha * C_splat
                    # But we need to account for transmittance
                    # T = product(1 - alpha_j)
                    # C = sum(alpha_i * c_i * T_i)
                    
                    # Standard alpha blending:
                    # C_out = C_in * (1 - alpha) + C_splat * alpha
                    # A_out = A_in + alpha * (1 - A_in)
                    
                    # However, the volume rendering equation is:
                    # C = sum(c_i * alpha_i * prod(1-alpha_j))
                    
                    # Let's implement the volume rendering accumulation directly
                    # T = 1.0 - current_alpha (transmittance so far)
                    # contribution = color * alpha * T
                    # current_color += contribution
                    # current_alpha += alpha * T
                    
                    T = 1.0 - current_alpha
                    contribution = color * alpha * T
                    current_color += contribution
                    current_alpha += alpha * T
                    
                    if current_alpha >= 0.999:
                        break # Early termination
                
                # Apply background
                # If current_alpha is not 1, add background
                if current_alpha < 0.999:
                    T_bg = 1.0 - current_alpha
                    current_color += bg_color * bg_alpha * T_bg # Assuming bg_alpha is separate
                    # Actually, bg_alpha is usually added to the sum of alphas
                    # C_final = (sum(alpha_i * c_i) + alpha_bg * c_bg) / (sum(alpha_i) + alpha_bg)
                    # This is handled in the final step or by treating bg as a final splat
                
                image[y, x] = current_color
                alpha_accum[y, x] = current_alpha

    # Final background handling
    # For pixels where alpha_accum < 1, blend with background
    # This is a simplified version. The exact formula is in [S10, unofficial]
    for y in range(image_height):
        for x in range(image_width):
            alpha_total = alpha_accum[y, x]
            if alpha_total < 0.999:
                # Blend with background
                # Assuming bg_alpha is 0 for simplicity in this basic impl, 
                # or handled by the bg_color term in the loop if bg_alpha > 0
                pass
                
    return image

def quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q):
    # q = [w, x, y, z]
    w, x, y, z = q
    return np.array([
        [1 - 2*(y*y + z*z), 2*(x*y - w*z), 2*(x*z + w*y)],
        [2*(x*y + w*z), 1 - 2*(x*x + z*z), 2*(y*z - w*x)],
        [2*(x*z - w*y), 2*(y*z + w*x), 1 - 2*(x*x + y*y)]
    ])

def compute_projection_jacobian(mu_cam, K):
    # mu_cam = (x, y, z)
    x, y, z = mu_cam
    # Projection: u = K * [x/z, y/z, 1]
    # Jacobian of proj(x) w.r.t x in 3D
    # d(u)/dx = d(K * [x/z, y/z, 1])/dx
    # This is a 2x3 matrix
    # u = (K[0,0]*x/z + K[0,2]), v = (K[1,1]*y/z + K[1,2])
    # du/dx = K[0,0]/z, du/dy = 0, du/dz = -K[0,0]*x/z^2
    # dv/dx = 0, dv/dy = K[1,1]/z, dv/dz = -K[1,1]*y/z^2
    J = np.array([
        [K[0,0]/z, 0, -K[0,0]*x/(z*z)],
        [0, K[1,1]/z, -K[1,1]*y/(z*z)]
    ])
    return J
```

## Subtopics

### [Unnormalized 3D Gaussian Function](1026-unnormalized-3d-gaussian-function.md)
Parameterizes the Gaussian via its peak value (opacity) rather than its integral, making $a$ the intensity at the center.

### [Row-constant hoisting for Gaussian Splatting Rasterization](1195-row-constant-hoisting-for-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md)
Hoists row-constant terms outside the inner pixel loop to minimize arithmetic operations per pixel.

### [2D Gaussian Projection with Covariance Stabilization](1198-2d-gaussian-projection-with-covariance-stabilization.md)
Uses a local affine transformation (Jacobian) to project 3D Gaussians to 2D ellipses, preserving the Gaussian form and differentiability.

### [Gaussian in the Wild (GS-W)](1386-gaussian-in-the-wild-gs-w.md)
Separates scene appearance into intrinsic material properties and dynamic environmental factors, avoiding global latent embeddings.

### [4D Gaussian Splatting](1458-4d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Extends 3DGS to dynamic scenes using a unified 4D covariance matrix to capture space-time motion, avoiding jitter from independent temporal weighting.

### [3DGUT](1474-3dgut.md)
Uses an unscented transform to improve the affine approximation of the projection operator, reducing approximation errors in the 2D covariance.

### [Transformer-based Reconstruction](1794-transformer-based-reconstruction.md)
Predicts 3DGS parameters directly from 2D images using a feed-forward network, bypassing iterative optimization [G].

### [3D Gaussian Ellipsoid Rendering](1869-3d-gaussian-ellipsoid-rendering.md)
Replaces rasterization with ray tracing to compute exact volume density distributions, solving occlusion ambiguity.

### [Stochastic Gaussian Splatting](6599-stochastic-gaussian-splatting.md)
Uses stochastic sampling and atomic operations to eliminate sorting bottlenecks, enabling real-time rendering of hundreds of millions of Gaussians.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering is $O(M)$ where $M$ is the number of Gaussians, but effectively $O(N \cdot K)$ per pixel where $N$ is the number of tiles and $K$ is the average number of Gaussians per tile. Sorting is $O(M \log M)$ [S8].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(M)$ to store Gaussian parameters. Typical scenes require 200,000–500,000 Gaussians [S12, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves real-time rendering (≥30 fps at 1080p) on consumer hardware [S5, unofficial]. On the Mip-NeRF360 benchmark, it reaches 27.21 dB PSNR and 0.214 LPIPS at 134 fps [S5, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering, high visual fidelity, explicit representation allows for easy editing and compression.
*   **Limitations:** Approximations in visibility (global sorting) cause popping artifacts [S2][S13, unofficial]. Self-attenuation is ignored [S2]. Standard anti-aliasing (dilation-based) fails with varying focal length [S12, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Novel-view synthesis, virtual walkthroughs, digital twins, VR/AR content capture [S5, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring accurate volumetric scattering or self-attenuation [S2]. Highly reflective surfaces may suffer due to the emission-absorption model [S13, unofficial].

## Background
Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S5, unofficial]. It builds on EWA Volume Splatting [S2][S3, unofficial] and point-based rendering methods [S5, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of NeRF (slow rendering) and explicit grids (ray marching) by combining volumetric image formation with rasterization [S5, unofficial].
