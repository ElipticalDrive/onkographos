# EWA Splatting

Also called: EWA Volume Splatting, Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) Splatting, Elliptical Weighted Average Volume Splatting, Zwicker EWA Volume Splatting, Zwicker EWA splatting

## Overview
EWA Splatting is a high-quality volume rendering technique that eliminates aliasing artifacts in splatting by replacing the standard footprint function with a band-limited resampling filter [S1, unofficial][S2][S8, S10, unofficial]. It solves the problem of incorrect visibility determination and high-frequency sampling errors caused by perspective projection, which standard splatting methods ignore by assuming non-overlapping kernels [S1, S8, S15, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent [Splatting](23-splatting.md) and siblings like [Splatting Without the Blur](1157-splatting-without-the-blur.md) by explicitly combining an elliptical Gaussian reconstruction kernel with a Gaussian low-pass filter to derive an analytic, aliasing-free footprint in screen space [S2][S5, S6, S10, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Volume data represented as a set of particles (voxels), each defined by position $\mathbf{p}_k$, extinction coefficient $g_k$, emission color $c_{\lambda k}$, and a 3D elliptical Gaussian reconstruction kernel with variance matrix $\mathbf{V}_k$ in object space [S6, S8, unofficial][S11].
*   **Output:** A 2D image where each pixel color is the accumulated contribution of splats composited back-to-front [S6, S8, unofficial][S11].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Data:** Each voxel $k$ stores:
    *   $\mathbf{p}_k$: 3D position in object space.
    *   $g_k$: Extinction coefficient (scalar).
    *   $c_{\lambda k}$: Emission color (RGB).
    *   $\mathbf{V}_k$: 3x3 symmetric positive-definite variance matrix of the reconstruction kernel in object space [S6, S8, unofficial][S11].
*   **Ray Space Coordinates:** $\mathbf{x} = (x_0, x_1, x_2)^T$, where $x_0, x_1$ define a point on the projection plane and $x_2$ is the distance from the center of projection [S8, unofficial].
*   **Camera Space Coordinates:** $\mathbf{u} = (u_0, u_1, u_2)^T$, with origin at the center of projection and image plane at $u_2 = 1$ [S6, unofficial][S11].
*   **Image Space Coordinates:** $\mathbf{x} = (x_0, x_1)^T$ (often denoted $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$) [S8, unofficial][S11].
*   **1D Footprint Table:** A 1D texture or array storing values of the exponential function $e^{-r^2/2}$ or similar, used for efficient rasterization via forward differencing [S8, S12, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Cutoff radius $r$:** The Gaussian kernel is truncated where $(\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{p})^T \mathbf{V}^{-1} (\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{p}) \leq r^2$. Typical values: $1 \leq r \leq 3$ [S6, unofficial][S11].
*   **Low-pass filter variance $\mathbf{V}_h$:** A 2x2 identity matrix is typically used [S6, unofficial][S11].
*   **Assumptions:**
    1.  Reconstruction kernels do not overlap along a viewing ray (for compositing order) [S8, S15, unofficial].
    2.  Perspective transformation is linearly approximated by its Jacobian [S6, unofficial][S11].
    3.  Emission coefficient is constant within the support of each footprint [S8, unofficial].
    4.  Attenuation factor is approximately constant within the support of each footprint [S8, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Transform to Camera Space:**
    For each voxel $k$, compute its camera space coordinates $\mathbf{u}_k$ and transform its object space variance matrix $\mathbf{V}_k$ to camera space:
    $$ \mathbf{V}_k^{cam} = \mathbf{W} \mathbf{V}_k \mathbf{W}^T $$
    where $\mathbf{W}$ is the rotational part of the viewing transformation mapping object space to camera space [S6, unofficial][S11].

2.  **Compute Jacobian:**
    Calculate the Jacobian matrix $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{u}_k}$ of the perspective projection at $\mathbf{u}_k$ in camera space to image space:
    $$ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{u}_k} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{u_{k_2}} & 0 & -\frac{u_{k_0}}{u_{k_2}^2} \\ 0 & \frac{1}{u_{k_2}} & -\frac{u_{k_1}}{u_{k_2}^2} \\ \frac{u_{k_0}}{\|\mathbf{u}_k\|} & \frac{u_{k_1}}{\|\mathbf{u}_k\|} & \frac{u_{k_2}}{\|\mathbf{u}_k\|} \end{pmatrix} $$
    where $\|\mathbf{u}_k\| = \sqrt{u_{k_0}^2 + u_{k_1}^2 + u_{k_2}^2}$ [S6, unofficial][S11][S15, unofficial].

3.  **Transform to Image Space:**
    Transform the variance matrix to image space (ray space projection):
    $$ \mathbf{V}_k^{img} = \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{u}_k} \mathbf{V}_k^{cam} \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{u}_k}^T $$
    Extract the 2x2 variance matrix $\hat{\mathbf{V}}_k$ by skipping the third row and column of $\mathbf{V}_k^{img}$ [S6, unofficial][S11][S15, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Resampling Filter Parameters:**
    Combine the projected reconstruction variance with the low-pass filter variance $\mathbf{V}_h$ (usually identity):
    $$ \mathbf{M}_k = (\hat{\mathbf{V}}_k + \mathbf{V}_h)^{-1} $$
    Compute the normalization factor:
    $$ \text{norm}_k = \frac{1}{2\pi |\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{u}_k}^{-1}| |\mathbf{W}^{-1}| |\hat{\mathbf{V}}_k + \mathbf{V}_h|^{\frac{1}{2}}} $$
    where $|\cdot|$ denotes the determinant [S6, unofficial][S11][S15, unofficial].

5.  **Project Center:**
    Project the voxel center $\mathbf{u}_k$ to screen coordinates $\mathbf{x}_k$ by perspective division:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_k = \left( \frac{u_{k_0}}{u_{k_2}}, \frac{u_{k_1}}{u_{k_2}} \right)^T $$
    [S8, unofficial][S11].

6.  **Rasterize Splat:**
    For each pixel $(x, y)$ within the bounding box of the splat (determined by $\mathbf{M}_k$ and cutoff radius $r$):
    a. Compute distance squared: $d^2 = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_k)^T \mathbf{M}_k (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_k)$.
    b. If $d^2 > r^2$, skip.
    c. Compute footprint value: $\rho_k(\mathbf{x}) = \text{norm}_k \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2} d^2}$.
    d. Compute opacity contribution: $\alpha_k = g_k \cdot \rho_k(\mathbf{x})$.
    e. Composite back-to-front:
       $$ C_{out} = C_{in} (1 - \alpha_k) + c_{\lambda k} \alpha_k $$
       $$ \alpha_{out} = \alpha_{in} + (1 - \alpha_{in}) \alpha_k $$
    [S6, S8, unofficial][S11][S15, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def ewa_splatting(volume_particles, view_matrix, proj_matrix, image_width, image_height):
    """
    Renders volume particles using EWA Splatting.
    
    Args:
        volume_particles: List of dicts with keys:
            'pos': (3,) object space position
            'extinction': scalar g_k
            'color': (3,) emission color c_lambda_k
            'variance': (3,3) object space variance matrix V_k
        view_matrix: (4,4) Model-View matrix
        proj_matrix: (4,4) Projection matrix
        image_width: int
        image_height: int
        
    Returns:
        image: (image_height, image_width, 3) rendered image
    """
    # Initialize output buffers
    color_buffer = np.zeros((image_height, image_width, 3))
    alpha_buffer = np.zeros((image_height, image_width))
    
    # Extract rotation W and translation from view matrix
    # Assuming view_matrix transforms object to camera space
    # Camera space origin is at center of projection
    W = view_matrix[:3, :3]
    
    # Precompute inverse Jacobian determinant factor and W inverse determinant factor
    # Note: |J^-1| * |W^-1| = 1 / (|J| * |W|)
    # However, the formula uses |J^-1| and |W^-1| directly in denominator.
    # We will compute the full normalization factor per particle.
    
    # Sort particles back-to-front in camera space
    # Compute camera space positions
    cam_positions = []
    for p in volume_particles:
        pos_obj = p['pos']
        # Homogeneous coordinate for transformation
        pos_obj_h = np.append(pos_obj, 1.0)
        pos_cam_h = view_matrix @ pos_obj_h
        pos_cam = pos_cam_h[:3]
        cam_positions.append(pos_cam)
        
    # Sort by u2 (depth in camera space, assuming u2 > 0 for front)
    # Higher u2 is further away in standard OpenGL camera space? 
    # The text says "origin u=0 is at center of projection and image plane is u2=1".
    # Usually, u2 increases away from camera. Back-to-front means sorting by u2 descending.
    indices = np.argsort([p[2] for p in cam_positions])[::-1]
    
    for idx in indices:
        p = volume_particles[idx]
        u_k = cam_positions[idx]
        V_k_obj = p['variance']
        g_k = p['extinction']
        c_k = p['color']
        
        # Step 1: Transform Variance to Camera Space
        V_k_cam = W @ V_k_obj @ W.T
        
        # Step 2: Compute Jacobian J_u_k
        u0, u1, u2 = u_k
        norm_u = np.linalg.norm(u_k)
        
        # J matrix as defined in [S6, unofficial][S11]
        J = np.array([
            [1.0/u2, 0, -u0/(u2**2)],
            [0, 1.0/u2, -u1/(u2**2)],
            [u0/norm_u, u1/norm_u, u2/norm_u]
        ])
        
        # Step 3: Transform to Image Space
        V_k_img = J @ V_k_cam @ J.T
        
        # Extract 2x2 submatrix (skip 3rd row/col)
        V_hat_k = V_k_img[:2, :2]
        
        # Step 4: Compute Resampling Filter Params
        V_h = np.eye(2) # Identity low-pass filter
        M_k = np.linalg.inv(V_hat_k + V_h)
        
        # Normalization factor
        # |J^-1| * |W^-1| * |V_hat_k + V_h|^(1/2)
        # Note: The formula in [S6, unofficial] is 1 / (2*pi * |J^-1| * |W^-1| * |V_hat + V_h|^(1/2))
        # We need determinants.
        det_J = np.linalg.det(J)
        det_W = np.linalg.det(W)
        det_V_sum = np.linalg.det(V_hat_k + V_h)
        
        norm_factor = 1.0 / (2.0 * np.pi * abs(det_J) * abs(det_W) * np.sqrt(det_V_sum))
        
        # Project center to screen
        x_k = np.array([u0/u2, u1/u2])
        
        # Determine bounding box for rasterization
        # The splat is an ellipse defined by M_k.
        # We can approximate the bounding box by the eigenvalues of M_k or a simple conservative box.
        # For implementation simplicity, we iterate over a bounding box derived from the cutoff radius r.
        r = 2.0 # Typical cutoff radius
        # The extent in screen space is roughly sqrt(r^2 * eigenvalues of inv(M_k))
        # Or simpler: iterate over pixels and check distance.
        # To optimize, compute bounding box of the ellipse.
        # Eigen decomposition of M_k
        eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(M_k)
        # Semi-axes lengths
        a = r / np.sqrt(eigenvalues[0])
        b = r / np.sqrt(eigenvalues[1])
        
        # Bounding box in screen coordinates
        # Rotate semi-axes by eigenvectors
        # This is complex. A simpler approach for the article is to iterate over a square box
        # defined by the max extent.
        max_extent = max(a, b)
        x_min = int(max(0, x_k[0] - max_extent))
        x_max = int(min(image_width, x_k[0] + max_extent))
        y_min = int(max(0, x_k[1] - max_extent))
        y_max = int(min(image_height, x_k[1] + max_extent))
        
        for y in range(y_min, y_max):
            for x in range(x_min, x_max):
                # Pixel center in screen space
                x_pix = np.array([x + 0.5, y + 0.5])
                
                # Distance squared
                diff = x_pix - x_k
                d_sq = diff @ M_k @ diff
                
                if d_sq > r**2:
                    continue
                
                # Footprint value
                rho = norm_factor * np.exp(-0.5 * d_sq)
                
                # Opacity contribution
                alpha_k = g_k * rho
                
                # Composite
                # C_out = C_in * (1 - alpha_k) + c_k * alpha_k
                # alpha_out = alpha_in + (1 - alpha_in) * alpha_k
                
                alpha_in = alpha_buffer[y, x]
                if alpha_in >= 1.0:
                    continue # Fully opaque, skip
                
                color_in = color_buffer[y, x]
                
                color_out = color_in * (1.0 - alpha_k) + c_k * alpha_k
                alpha_out = alpha_in + (1.0 - alpha_in) * alpha_k
                
                color_buffer[y, x] = color_out
                alpha_buffer[y, x] = alpha_out
                
    return color_buffer
```

## Subtopics

### EWA Surface Splatting
EWA Surface Splatting is a variant where EWA volume splats are reduced to surface splats suitable for high-quality iso-surface rendering by flattening the 3D Gaussian kernel along the volume gradient [S9, unofficial]. It differs from EWA Volume Splatting by treating the data as a point-sampled surface rather than a volumetric density field, resulting in sharper, anisotropically filtered textures for surfaces [S7, abstract only, unofficial][S9, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The computation of the resampling filter per voxel is $O(1)$ (matrix inversions and determinants of fixed size 3x3/2x2). Rasterization is proportional to the number of pixels covered by the splats. The total time is dominated by the rasterization pass.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing particle data, where $N$ is the number of voxels/particles. The image buffer is $O(W \times H)$.
*   **Measured Performance:** Early software implementations took approximately 11 seconds per frame for the UNC head dataset on an 866 MHz Pentium-III [S1, S8, S10, unofficial]. Hardware acceleration significantly improves this, but the method is computationally expensive due to the per-pixel evaluation of the Gaussian and matrix operations [S6, unofficial][S11].
*   **Strengths:** Provides high-quality, aliasing-free rendering without excessive blurring. Handles arbitrary elliptical kernels and perspective projection correctly [S2][S5, S8, S10, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to the complex footprint calculation. Assumes non-overlapping kernels for compositing, which can lead to artifacts if violated [S1, S8, S15, unofficial]. Numerical instability can occur when the projection approaches a line (condition number of inverse mapping increases) [S12, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high visual quality and anti-aliasing are critical, and performance constraints allow for complex per-pixel calculations.
*   **When not to use:** For real-time applications on low-end hardware where simpler splatting or ray-casting approximations are preferred.

## Background
EWA Splatting was introduced by Matthias Zwicker, Hanspeter Pfister, Jeroen van Baar, and Markus Gross in 2001 [S8, unofficial]. It is named after Heckbert's Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) filter for texture mapping [S7, abstract only, unofficial][S8, S10, unofficial]. The method was developed to address aliasing artifacts in volume splatting caused by perspective projection and the assumption of non-overlapping kernels [S1, S8, S15, unofficial]. It builds on Westover's original splatting framework but introduces a resampling filter combining a reconstruction kernel with a low-pass filter [S6, S8, S10, unofficial].
