# Splatting

Also called: Object-order volume rendering, Element-tossing, Gaussian splatting (in modern context)

## Overview
Splatting is an object-order volume rendering algorithm that projects volumetric primitives (typically voxels or particles) onto the 2D image plane as overlapping, smooth footprints (kernels), which are then accumulated to form the final image [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, unofficial][S6][S7, book][S8, S10, unofficial][S12]. It solves the computational bottleneck of image-order methods (like ray casting) by precomputing the reconstruction and integration for each voxel, allowing for cache-coherent traversal and efficient hardware acceleration [S1, S8, S10, unofficial]. Unlike ray casting, which maps pixels into the data set, splatting maps data elements into the image plane, treating the volume as a collection of discrete samples that are convolved with a filter to distribute their energy to neighboring pixels [S5, S10, unofficial][S12].

## Shared principles
The splatting family shares the fundamental mechanism of treating volumetric data as a set of geometric or parametric primitives that are transformed and rasterized [S1, S3, S4, unofficial].

1.  **Primitive Representation:** The volume is decomposed into primitives. These may be explicit geometric shapes (cubes, cells, splats) or implicit functions sampled via texture hardware [S1, S3, S4, unofficial]. Each primitive $k$ is defined by its position $\mathbf{p}_k$, density/extinction coefficient $g_k$ or $\rho_k$, and a reconstruction kernel $h$ (typically a Gaussian) [S1, unofficial][S6][S10, unofficial].
2.  **Transformation:** Primitives are transformed from volume space to screen space using the Model-View-Projection (MVP) matrix or specific factorizations (e.g., shear-warp) [S2, S3, S4, unofficial]. For perspective projections, the kernel shape may be distorted (e.g., EWA splatting) to account for foreshortening [S6][S10, unofficial].
3.  **Rasterization:** The transformed primitives are projected onto the 2D image plane. This step leverages hardware-accelerated scan-conversion, depth testing, and blending [S1, S2, S3, S4, unofficial]. The contribution of a voxel $D$ to a screen point $(x,y)$ is determined by the footprint function $F(x,y)$, which is the integral of the 3D kernel along the view direction [S2, unofficial][S6][S10, unofficial]:
    $$ F(x,y) = \int h(x-x_D, y-y_D, z) \, dz $$
    The intensity written to the pixel is modulated by the voxel density:
    $$ P(x,y) = \text{weight}(x,y) \cdot \rho(D) $$
    where $\text{weight}(x,y) = F(x-x_D, y-y_D)$ [S10, unofficial].
4.  **Compositing:** Contributions are accumulated to form the final image. This is typically done using alpha blending in back-to-front or front-to-back order to handle transparency correctly [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S7, book]. Splatting compositing is performed along sheets (all pixels at once for a set of voxels), whereas ray-casting composites along rays (one sample at a time) [S10, unofficial].
5.  **Hardware Acceleration:** Most methods in this family rely on GPU rasterization pipelines for performance, enabling real-time interactivity [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Footprint Evaluation](1180-footprint-evaluation.md)
A forward-mapping algorithm that projects each voxel independently, distributing its energy via a reconstruction kernel to form a "splat" or footprint. It solves the problem of efficiently rendering regular volumetric grids by avoiding per-pixel ray traversal, reducing per-voxel overhead from $O(k^3)$ to $O(k^2)$. It sets itself apart by pre-computing the integration of the 3D kernel into a 2D footprint table.

### [EWA Splatting](1069-ewa-splatting.md)
A high-quality, aliasing-free technique that projects 3D elliptical Gaussian kernels onto the 2D image plane as pre-integrated footprints. It solves aliasing artifacts inherent in basic splatting by convolving the reconstruction kernel with a Gaussian low-pass filter. It sets itself apart from [Ray-Driven Splatting](1156-ray-driven-splatting.md) by using object-space kernels projected to screen space rather than ray-space sampling.

### [Ray-Driven Splatting](1156-ray-driven-splatting.md)
A hybrid technique that combines precomputed voxel contributions via splatting with pixel accumulation logic of ray casting. It solves performance bottlenecks of traditional voxel-driven splatting, particularly for perspective projections. Unlike standard splatting, it suspends splats in object space and intersects them with rays, enabling ray-tracing optimizations like early termination.

### [Splatting Without the Blur](1157-splatting-without-the-blur.md)
A method that performs classification and shading after voxels are projected onto the screen to avoid blurring object edges, which occurs when classification is done prior to projection.

### [Rendering Pipelines and Passes](1411-rendering-pipelines-and-passes.md)
Defines the structural organization of volume rendering into logical stages (data traversal, interpolation, shading, compositing). It differs from monolithic approaches by isolating geometric projection from visual appearance, enabling techniques like deferred shading.

### [Stop-the-pop](1472-stop-the-pop.md)
A technique to mitigate popping artifacts in volume splatting caused by sorting procedures of Gaussians along the ray. It uses hierarchical or pixel-resorting to remove artifacts without sacrificing speed.

### [SpotlessSplats](1476-spotlesssplats.md)
A robust 3D Gaussian Splatting method that detects and ignores transient distractors in casual captures. It enhances robustness by identifying outliers in a pre-trained feature space, distinguishing them from static scene geometry.

### [Anti-Aliasing and Filtering](1574-anti-aliasing-and-filtering.md)
Addresses aliasing artifacts inherent in object-order splatting by modifying the splat footprint, opacity, or pipeline to account for sampling rate and motion. Unlike [Ray-Driven Splatting](1156-ray-driven-splatting.md), which integrates samples along rays, this family explicitly accounts for the discrete pixel grid.

### [Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction](1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md)
Reconstructs animatable, dynamic 3D avatars from video using 3D Gaussians. It is distinguished from [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) by its explicit dependency on a parametric structural prior (e.g., SMPL/FLAME) to drive deformation.

### [Splat Extent and Projection](1792-splat-extent-and-projection.md)
Addresses the geometric problem of determining the screen-space position and spatial extent of a projected 3D kernel to optimize rasterization and culling. It sets itself apart by focusing on the analytical derivation of footprint boundaries rather than density evaluation.

### [CPU-Based Splat Sorting](2097-cpu-based-splat-sorting.md)
Orders 3D Gaussian splats for correct front-to-back alpha blending by performing the sort on the CPU. It solves the latency of GPU-based sorting by offloading $O(N \log N)$ work to the CPU, allowing the GPU to focus on rendering.

### [Slice-by-Slice (Sheet Buffer) Splatting](2110-slice-by-slice-sheet-buffer-splatting.md)
Organizes splats into cache-sheets aligned parallel to the volume face most closely parallel to the image plane, then composites these sheets slice-by-slice. It solves visibility determination errors inherent in "splat-every-sample" composition by ensuring correct depth ordering within each sheet.

### [Splat/Mesh Blending with Transition Smoothing](2145-splat-mesh-blending-with-transition-smoothing.md)
Solves visual discontinuities when rendering surfaces using both polygonal meshes and point-based splats simultaneously. It achieves smooth transitions by reusing the natural weight falloff of the splat representation to linearly blend between results.

### [Order Independent Transparency for Point-Based Models](2150-order-independent-transparency-for-point-based-models.md)
Renders transparent point-based models on GPUs in an order-independent manner by extending depth-peeling techniques to deferred-shaded multi-pass splatting. It solves the expensive $O(N \log N)$ depth sorting requirement.

### [Line Capsule Primitive](3131-line-capsule-primitive.md)
A screen-space rendering primitive used in object-order splatting to represent line segments as rotationally symmetric profiles of the 2D point-to-segment distance. It offers visual quality comparable to exact volumetric primitives at lower computational cost.

### [Ultrafast Spatial Texture-Atlas Splatting](3245-ultrafast-spatial-texture-atlas-splatting.md)
A learned sparse scene representation that eliminates runtime neural network inference by baking view-independent high-frequency texture into a hardware-accelerated 2D texture atlas. It represents the scene as a sparse set of oriented 2D Beta kernels, separating low-frequency view-dependent appearance from high-frequency spatial texture.

### [Lighting and Material Interaction](3851-lighting-and-material-interaction.md)
Defines how illumination models and material properties are applied to splatted primitives, bridging the gap between raw geometric density and photorealistic appearance. It differs from siblings by focusing on light transport computation rather than geometric projection.

### [Anti-Aliasing for Splatting](4471-anti-aliasing-for-splatting.md)
Prevents sample-rate aliasing artifacts in splatting-based volume rendering by explicitly accounting for the integration grid sampling frequency relative to the volume raster sampling rate. It distinguishes itself from volume ray-casting, which avoids aliasing by using reconstruction kernels that expand as rays diverge.

### [Multi-View Splatting Pipeline with Fill-Rate Filtering](4689-multi-view-splatting-pipeline-with-fill-rate-filtering.md)
A pipeline that generates multi-view 2D images from a 3D point cloud, filtering out sparse views based on fill rate. It differs from standard splatting by explicitly discarding camera poses that do not provide sufficient pixel coverage.

### [EWA Splatting with kNN Covariance Estimation](4690-ewa-splatting-with-knn-covariance-estimation.md)
Uses per-point 3D covariance estimated from k-nearest neighbours to project an elliptical weighted average (EWA) shape onto the 2D image plane for denser coverage than simple disk splatting.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods](5439-3d-gaussian-splatting-lod-methods.md)

### [Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting](5618-hierarchical-and-multi-resolution-splatting.md)

### [Mueller's Splatting Approach](5752-mueller-s-splatting-approach.md)
An object-order method that treats a gray-scale volume as a 3D discrete density function, calculating the influence of each voxel on multiple output pixels to accelerate DVR. It distinguishes itself from ray-casting by exploiting cache coherency through sequential memory traversal and supporting incremental rendering, though it introduces blurring due to spherically symmetric reconstruction kernels.

### [Primitive Geometry](6264-primitive-geometry.md)

### [Edge-Preserving Splatting with Compression/Stretching](6574-edge-preserving-splatting-with-compression-stretching.md)
Preserves sharp edges by applying non-linear compression and stretching functions to the standard splatting kernel, ensuring C1 continuity across splat boundaries.

### Early Splat Termination
An optimization for volume splatting that skips processing of voxels when the accumulated opacity at the target screen pixels is already full, analogous to early ray termination in ray casting [S10, unofficial]. It projects two volumes: the density volume and an opacity volume. For each projected voxel, it computes a 2D bounding box around the pixels that cover the splat. If all pixels inside the computed bounding box have full opacity (1.0 or near 1.0), the current voxel is skipped [S10, unofficial].

### Extinction-based splatting
A rendering solution that uses extinction (referred to as 'density' in NeRF literature) instead of the learned opacity value used in 3DGS, to analyze the difference between opacity-based and accurate volumetric rendering [S13]. For small numbers of primitives, extinction-based splatting performs better than opacity-based splatting. However, with more primitives, opacity splatting performs best [S13].

### Parallel Splatting
Iterates splats in parallel using `rayon::par_iter().filter_map()`. For each splat, it runs projection and bounding box computation, returning `Some(Projected)` if valid, `None` otherwise. If a `rayon::ThreadPool` is provided, the projection closure is installed into the pool; otherwise, it runs sequentially [S14, unofficial].

### Splat Radius Adjustment Based on Normal Angle
A method to adjust splat radii to prevent holes between splats when samples are elevated in the normal direction of the base plane, by scaling the radius based on the angle between the splat normal and the base plane normal [S15, unofficial]. The radius $r$ is calculated as:
$$ r = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\mu}{0.1 + 0.9\langle n, n_B \rangle} $$
where $\mu$ is the sampling step size, $n$ is the splat normal, and $n_B$ is the base plane normal [S15, unofficial]. The constant 0.1 prevents division by zero, and 0.9 controls the maximal radius increase [S15, unofficial]. If samples are elevated, holes may occur if radii are not adjusted, as large angles indicate large variance between neighboring elevation values [S15, unofficial].

### Scatter Samples to Image
A post-processing step that scatters computed sample values from arbitrary screen-space positions into a regular image grid [S16, unofficial]. Samples located outside the image boundaries are ignored. Pixels in the output image that receive no samples are left untouched (no default value overwriting) [S16, unofficial]. Input `sample_positions` is a tensor of shape 2*N, and `samples` is a tensor of shape Channels*N [S16, unofficial].

### 3D Point Splatting with Dirichlet Kernel
A method that splats a collection of weighted 3D points onto a volumetric grid using a Dirichlet kernel (described as Lanczos/sinc filtering) [S17, unofficial]. For each point $(x, y, z)$ with weight $w$, it computes the integer floor coordinates and the bounding box for the splat. It iterates over the bounding box indices and adds the computed weight to the grid cell [S17, unofficial]. If a different kernel is desired after splatting, a convolution via FFT can be applied [S17, unofficial].

### Splat Size Culling
A staging optimization that discards splats smaller than a certain threshold during staging to reduce workload, trading off visual quality for real-time frame rates [S18]. On the GardenF scene, culling reduces staged splats from 5.79M to 1.25M and fragments from 8.16M to 1.99M, decreasing render time from 9.7 ms to 2.1 ms [S18]. This approach does not work with scenes comprising solely small splats, such as models constructed via Mesh2Splat [S18].

### Splat-Every-Sample Composition
A composition method where every sample is splatted, compared against slice-by-slice (sheet buffer) splatting [S19, unofficial]. Splat-every-sample produces darker images due to incorrect visibility determination. It achieves better performance by avoiding per-slice additions [S19, unofficial].

### Splat State Encoding
Storing a 32-bit integer for each splat where each potential state (highlighted, selected, deleted) is encoded inside a single bit, serving as an acceleration for transformation of selected splats [S18].

### Beta-Splatting Rendering
Loads the Beta-Splatting model from `point_cloud/iteration_{iteration}/point_cloud.ply` or `point_cloud/iteration_{iteration}/png/` directory. It iterates over held-out test cameras obtained via `scene.getTestCameras()`, renders the scene for each camera using `beta_model.render(camera)`, clamps the rendered image and ground truth to `[0.0, 1.0]`, and saves the render and ground truth images as PNGs in `renders/` and `gt/` directories [S20, unofficial].

### Particle Splatting for Density Field Construction
A 3D density field representing a wax surface is constructed on the CPU every frame by splatting interpolated particles into a 3D grid aligned with the container [S21, unofficial]. Temporal smoothing is applied in-place using the update rule: $keep = \text{exp}(-dt/\tau)$, where $\tau = 0.04$ seconds [S21, unofficial]. The grid dimensions are 48×94×48 voxels, using a separable kernel defined as $(1 - u^2)^3$. Two channels are stored per voxel: (1) density, (2) temperature × density. Density is normalized to approximately 1 at the core of the wax [S21, unofficial]. GPU Texture Format is Data3DTexture with RG channel layout, internal format RG32F (or RG16F if 32-bit float filtering is unsupported) [S21, unofficial]. CPU splatting takes 1.1 to 1.25 ms median, and 1.3 to 1.9 ms p95, measured on a Xeon 2.1 GHz processor [S21, unofficial].

### Seed Generation for Initial Splats
Initial splat positions derived from the volume using a combination of gradient-based and uniform grid sampling [S22, unofficial]. Sobel gradients are computed across all dimensions. Voxels with normalized gradient magnitude > 0.1 are sampled using gradient-weighted Poisson-disk sampling with a 2-voxel exclusion radius. Uniform grid seeds are generated to cover smooth regions, with grid spacing set so 40% share tiles the volume: $(N_{vox} / N_{grid})^{1/D}$, at least 2 voxels [S22, unofficial]. Seed target is $\max(100, \lfloor N_{vox} / 2^{1/D} \rfloor)$ capped at 10,000. Alternative seeders include scale-hierarchical decomposition seeder and intensity-weighted peaks seeder [S22, unofficial].

### Hardware-Accelerated Axis-Aligned Splatting Framework
A rendering framework for regular or rectilinear volumes using axis-aligned splatting with three traversal orders, processing voxels in slices perpendicular to the axis most parallel to the viewing direction [S23]. It attaches a textured quad (proxy geometry) representing a splat at each voxel. Processing occurs in two stages: First, add contributions from splats in one slice to an intermediate buffer. Second, perform back-to-front or front-to-back composition of each slice in an accumulation buffer to yield final results (slice-by-slice composition) [S23]. Slice-by-slice composition uses an intermediate buffer and accumulation buffer. Splat-every-sample composition is an alternative that does not use an intermediate buffer [S23].

## Background
Splatting was first proposed by Westover in the late 1980s and early 1990s as an object-order alternative to ray casting [S3, unofficial][S7, book][S10, unofficial][S12]. The term "splatting" is derived from a non-technical description of the feed-forward volume-rendering process, likened to throwing snowballs at a wall, where each snowball leaves a fuzzy circular mark (footprint) [S5, S10, unofficial]. Early methods suffered from aliasing and popping artifacts, particularly when changing viewpoints or when splats overlapped incorrectly [S3, unofficial][S7, book][S10, unofficial]. The introduction of Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) splatting significantly improved image quality by combining elliptical Gaussian reconstruction kernels with Gaussian low-pass filters [S3, unofficial][S6][S7, book]. In recent years, splatting has seen a resurgence in the form of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which uses differentiable rendering to optimize millions of 3D Gaussians for real-time novel view synthesis [S4, unofficial][S6][S11, unofficial]. This has expanded the application of splatting from traditional volume rendering to surface reconstruction and neural radiance fields [S4, unofficial][S6].
