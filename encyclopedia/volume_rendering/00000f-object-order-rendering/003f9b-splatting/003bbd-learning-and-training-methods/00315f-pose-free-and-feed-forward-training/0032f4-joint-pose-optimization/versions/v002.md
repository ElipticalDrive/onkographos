# Joint Pose Optimization

Also called: BARF, Unposed images methods

## Overview
Joint Pose Optimization refers to methods that simultaneously optimize camera poses and the underlying 3D radiance field representation, eliminating the strict dependency on external Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines for initial pose estimation [S1]. Unlike standard differentiable rendering which assumes known camera parameters, this approach treats poses as learnable variables optimized via photometric loss [S1]. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [InstantSplat](6136-instantsplat.md) and [pixelSplat](6464-pixelsplat.md) by its reliance on iterative gradient-based optimization of pose parameters rather than direct feed-forward regression or pre-computed depth priors.

## Shared principles
The family operates under the principle that camera extrinsics and the scene representation are coupled and must be solved jointly.

1.  **Input:** A set of uncalibrated images $\{I_i\}$ with known intrinsics but unknown extrinsics (poses) [S1].
2.  **Learnable Parameters:**
    *   **Scene Representation:** The radiance field parameters (e.g., neural network weights or Gaussian splatting attributes).
    *   **Camera Poses:** A set of pose parameters $P = \{T_i\}$ for each image, where $T_i \in SE(3)$ represents the transformation from world to camera coordinates.
3.  **Optimization Objective:** The method minimizes the photometric reconstruction error between the rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and the input image $I_i$ [S1]. The loss function is defined as:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo}(\hat{I}_i, I_i) $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ is typically a combination of L1 loss and perceptual metrics (e.g., MS-SSIM or LPIPS) [S1, 6136-instantsplat.md, 6464-pixelsplat.md].
4.  **Differentiability:** The rendering process is differentiable with respect to both the scene representation and the pose parameters, allowing gradients to flow from the photometric loss to update both [S1, 6136-instantsplat.md, 6464-pixelsplat.md].
5.  **Initialization Requirement:** These methods generally require a rough pose initialization (e.g., from a coarse SfM run or random initialization with constraints) and are limited to optimizing small motions relative to this initialization [S1].

## Subtopics

### [InstantSplat](6136-instantsplat.md)
Uses a feed-forward network to predict poses and Gaussians jointly, leveraging temporal continuity or depth priors for initialization. It avoids iterative pose optimization per image, favoring speed and generalization.

### [pixelSplat](6464-pixelsplat.md)
Focuses on feed-forward estimation of poses and scene representations from sparse views, often utilizing stereo-matching networks or depth estimation priors to guide the joint learning process without explicit iterative pose refinement.

## Background
The need for Joint Pose Optimization arises from the limitations of traditional pipelines that rely on off-the-shelf pose estimation methods, which can fail in texture-less regions or when images lack sufficient overlap [S1]. Early works in this area, such as BARF (Blending Artificially Realistic Frequency), proposed jointly optimizing camera poses and neural radiance fields [S1]. Subsequent research has extended these ideas to both static [HJS∗ 23, WWX∗ 21, CL23a, LMTL21, SCW∗ 25] and dynamic settings [LGM∗ 23]. While effective, these iterative optimization methods are computationally expensive and sensitive to initialization, often requiring rough pose estimates and being limited to small motions [S1]. More recent approaches attempt to mitigate these issues by leveraging mono-depth priors [BWL∗ 23] or temporal continuity [FLK∗ 24] to optimize camera parameters incrementally. Some works have also extended this concept to generalizable representations, enabling joint pose prediction and representation learning from sparse image collections [CL23b, HJS∗ 23, XLC∗ 25]. Ye et al. [YLX∗ 25] demonstrated that pose estimation and novel-view synthesis could be performed from unposed images using only photometric loss, though this remains limited to static scenes [S1].
