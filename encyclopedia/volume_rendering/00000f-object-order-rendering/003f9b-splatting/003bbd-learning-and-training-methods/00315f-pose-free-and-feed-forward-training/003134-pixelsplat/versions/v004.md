# pixelSplat

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splats from Image Pairs, Epipolar Transformer Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
pixelSplat is a feed-forward model that reconstructs a 3D radiance field parameterized by 3D Gaussian primitives from a pair of input images [S1]. It solves the problem of generalizable novel view synthesis by predicting an explicit, editable 3D representation in a single forward pass, avoiding the iterative optimization loops of single-scene methods [S1]. Unlike light-field transformers which output black-box ray colors, pixelSplat generates a primitive-based representation that enables real-time rasterization-based rendering [S1]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like InstantSplat by using a probabilistic sampling scheme to overcome local minima in primitive regression and an epipolar transformer to resolve scale ambiguity inherent in SfM poses [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A pair of images $\{I_1, I_2\}$ and their associated camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) [S1]. The camera poses are typically from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and thus scaled by an unknown factor $s_i$ [S1].

**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\{G_u\}$ for each pixel $u$ in the reference image, defined by mean $\mu_u$, covariance $\Sigma_u$, opacity $\alpha_u$, and scale $S_u$ [S1]. The spherical harmonics coefficients $c_{u,lm}$ are not explicitly detailed in the source for the prediction head, but the output parameters are $\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, S$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Image Features:** For each pixel $u$, a feature vector $\mathbf{F}[u]$ is computed by summing the outputs of a ResNet-50 and a ViT-B/8 (pre-trained with DINO) [S1].
*   **Gaussian Parameters:**
    *   $\mu_u \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
    *   $\Sigma_u \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix.
    *   $\alpha_u \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
    *   $S_u \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector [S1].
*   **Probability Distribution:** A dense probability distribution $p(\mathbf{x})$ over 3D space is predicted to sample Gaussian means [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** Pass each reference image $I_j$ ($j \in \{1, 2\}$) through a ResNet-50 and a ViT-B/8 vision transformer, both pre-trained using a DINO objective. Sum their pixel-wise outputs to generate initial features [S1].
2.  **Epolar Transformer Encoding:** Perform two rounds of epipolar cross-attention to encode features between the two views. This step utilizes frequency-based positional encoding of depth to resolve scale ambiguity [S1]. The transformer infers a per-scene scale factor $s_i$ by finding cross-view pixel correspondences [S1].
3.  **Dense Probability Distribution Prediction:** Predict a dense probability distribution $p(\mathbf{x})$ over 3D space conditioned on the epipolar-encoded features [S1].
4.  **Differentiable Sampling (Reparameterization Trick):** Sample Gaussian means $\mu_u$ from the predicted probability distribution $p(\mathbf{x})$. The sampling operation is made differentiable via a reparameterization trick that couples the density of a sampled Gaussian primitive to the probability of that location [S1]. This allows gradients to back-propagate through the Gaussian splatting representation [S1].
    *   *Mechanism:* When receiving a gradient that would increase the opacity of a Gaussian at a 3D location, the model increases the probability that a Gaussian will be sampled at that location again in the future, implicitly spawning or deleting primitives during training [S1].
5.  **Gaussian Parameter Prediction:** Use a neural network $f$ conditioned on the feature $\mathbf{F}[u]$ for each pixel $u$ to predict the remaining Gaussian parameters [S1]:
    $$ \phi, \boldsymbol{\delta}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}, \mathbf{S} = f(\mathbf{F}[u]) $$
    where $\phi$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ likely relate to the mean $\mu$ (or its components) and orientation, while $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is the covariance, and $\mathbf{S}$ is the scale [S1]. The opacity $\alpha$ is also predicted [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Render the predicted 3D Gaussians using standard differentiable volume rendering/rasterization to produce the output image [S1].

**Loss Function:**
Minimize a combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Perceptual Distance (LPIPS) [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{MSE} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{LPIPS} $$
[ S1 ]

**Parameters:**
*   **Training Resolution:** $256 \times 256$ pixels [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Scale Ambiguity Handling:** The epipolar transformer infers the scale factor $s_i$ associated with the SfM poses [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scale Ambiguity:** SfM poses are scaled by an arbitrary factor $s_i$, making metric depth prediction from a single image impossible [S1]. The epipolar transformer resolves this by associating positionally encoded depth values with cross-view correspondences [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Gaussian primitives have local support; gradients vanish if the distance to the correct location exceeds a few standard deviations [S1]. The probabilistic sampling scheme avoids this by allowing gradients to flow through the probability distribution, unlike non-differentiable pruning/splitting [S1].
*   **Out-of-Distribution Views:** Reflective surfaces may appear transparent, and Gaussians may appear billboard-like when viewed from out-of-distribution camera poses [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class PixelSplat:
    def __init__(self, encoder, transformer, predictor):
        self.encoder = encoder # ResNet-50 + ViT-B/8
        self.transformer = transformer # Epipolar Transformer
        self.predictor = predictor # Neural network f

    def forward(self, images, camera_params):
        """
        images: List[Tensor] of shape (2, C, H, W)
        camera_params: Dict containing intrinsics and extrinsics (scaled)
        """
        # Step 1: Feature Extraction
        # Sum outputs of ResNet-50 and ViT-B/8
        features = []
        for img in images:
            resnet_feat = self.encoder.resnet(img)
            vit_feat = self.encoder.vit(img)
            features.append(resnet_feat + vit_feat)

        # Step 2: Epipolar Transformer Encoding
        # Two rounds of epipolar cross-attention with frequency-based depth encoding
        encoded_features = self.transformer(features, camera_params)

        # Step 3 & 4: Dense Probability Distribution and Differentiable Sampling
        # Predict probability distribution p(x) over 3D space
        prob_dist = self.predictor.predict_probability_distribution(encoded_features)
        
        # Sample Gaussian means mu from p(x) using reparameterization trick
        # This makes sampling differentiable
        mu = self.predictor.sample_means(prob_dist)

        # Step 5: Predict remaining Gaussian parameters
        # f(F[u]) -> phi, delta, Sigma, S
        # phi, delta likely define mean orientation/position components
        # Sigma is covariance, S is scale
        phi, delta, Sigma, S = self.predictor.predict_parameters(encoded_features)
        
        # Opacity alpha is also predicted
        alpha = self.predictor.predict_opacity(encoded_features)

        # Construct Gaussian primitives
        gaussians = {
            'mu': mu,
            'Sigma': Sigma,
            'alpha': alpha,
            'S': S
        }

        # Step 6: Render
        rendered_image = render_gaussians(gaussians, camera_params)
        return rendered_image, gaussians
```

## Subtopics

### MVSplat
MVSplat builds upon pixelSplat by combining multi-view feature extraction through transformers and cost volumes, which are then processed by a U-Net [S3]. It extends the two-view approach to multi-view settings [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference cost is approximately 650 times less than the next-fastest baseline for encoding one scene and rendering 100 images [S1]. It accelerates rendering by 2.5 orders of magnitude compared to state-of-the-art light field transformers [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses significantly less memory per ray at training time compared to baselines [S1].
*   **Performance:** Outperforms baselines (pixelNeRF, GPNR, Du et al.) on RealEstate10k and ACID datasets, with significant improvements in perceptual distance (LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time and memory-efficient rendering; explicit, editable 3D representation; fast 3D reconstruction at inference time; handles scale ambiguity; avoids local minima via probabilistic sampling [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Reflective surfaces may appear transparent; Gaussians appear billboard-like from out-of-distribution views; requires a pair of images with known (scaled) camera poses [S1].
*   **When to use:** Wide-baseline novel view synthesis from image pairs where real-time rendering and explicit 3D representation are desired [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When metric depth is required without scale inference; when out-of-distribution views are critical and billboard artifacts are unacceptable [S1].

## Background
pixelSplat was introduced by Charatan, Li, Tagliasacchi, and Sitzmann from MIT and Simon Fraser University/University of Toronto [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2024 [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of iterative optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting for generalizable novel view synthesis by introducing a feed-forward architecture [S1]. It builds on the concept of 3D Gaussian primitives for real-time rendering [S1] and the use of epipolar transformers for feature generalization [S1].
