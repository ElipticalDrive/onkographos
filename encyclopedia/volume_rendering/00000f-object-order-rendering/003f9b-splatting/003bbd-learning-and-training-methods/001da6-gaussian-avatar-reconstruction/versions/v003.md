# Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction


## Overview
Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction is a family of methods that reconstruct animatable, dynamic 3D human or head avatars from video by representing them as collections of 3D Gaussians [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, real-time renderable avatars that can be driven by parametric body models (SMPL/FLAME) or pose features, serving as a dynamic alternative to static Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or traditional mesh-based animation [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its sibling [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) by its explicit dependency on a parametric structural prior to drive deformation, and from [Gaussian Avatars](1477-gaussian-avatars.md) which typically focus on head-only reconstruction using the FLAME model [S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family builds on [Splatting](23-splatting.md) by treating volumetric data as a set of parametric primitives that are transformed and rasterized [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
1. **Parametric Prior:** The geometry and deformation of the avatar are constrained by a parametric model (e.g., SMPL for full-body, FLAME for heads) [S2, unofficial].
2. **Pose-Conditioned Deformation:** Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scale) are functions of the parametric model's pose and shape parameters [S2, unofficial].
3. **Differentiable Rendering:** The rendering process uses differentiable splatting to compute gradients from the rendered image to the Gaussian parameters and/or the deformation network weights [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
4. **Optimization:** The model is trained by minimizing photometric loss between rendered images and input video frames [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md)
Represents a scene as an explicit collection of anisotropic 3D Gaussians with adaptive density control. Sets it apart by optimizing parameters directly from images without pose constraints, treating the scene as static. Choose for static novel-view synthesis.

### [SpotlessSplats](1476-spotlesssplats.md)
Robust reconstruction that ignores transient distractors via feature-space outlier rejection. Sets it apart by adding feature-based outlier rejection to the standard pipeline. Choose for casual captures with noise and occlusions.

### [Gaussian Avatars](1477-gaussian-avatars.md)
Photorealistic animatable head avatars by associating Gaussians with FLAME mesh triangles. Sets it apart by explicit triangle-constrained initialization for bust-level reconstruction. Choose for head-only riggable animation.

### [Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars](1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md)
Animatable head avatars supporting dynamic relighting. Sets it apart by explicitly modeling lighting interactions via a relightable BRDF. Choose for novel lighting conditions.

### [NoPoSplat](1484-noposplat.md)
Novel view synthesis from sparse or unposed images. Sets it apart by eliminating known camera pose requirements using intrinsics token embeddings. Choose for sparse views with minimal overlap.

### [SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md)
Animatable full-body avatars initialized on SMPL/SMPL-X. Sets it apart by introducing pose-dependent geometry updates for full-body humans. Choose for dynamic full-body rendering.

### GaussianShader
Enriches Gaussians with material parameters (diffusiveness, specularity, roughness, normals) to handle specular/reflective objects [S1, unofficial]. Choose for physically accurate simulation of reflective surfaces.

### Codec Avatar 3D
Production-grade GS-based head model for volumetric telepresence by Meta [S2, unofficial]. Choose for real-time telepresence applications.

### OmniRe
Holistic scene reconstruction including humans, vehicles, and static background [S2, unofficial]. Choose for comprehensive scene capture including dynamic actors.

### SplatArmor
Leverages two MLPs to predict large motions on SMPL/canonical space with SE(3) fields [S3]. Choose for detailed full-body motion capture.

## Background
The method originates from the application of 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic human reconstruction [S2, unofficial]. Early works introduced SMPL-driven body Gaussians [S2, unofficial]. Subsequent methods such as 3DGS-Avatar, GaussianAvatars, GoMAvatar, and AnimatableGaussians refined the approach using SMPL-X or FLAME models with various deformation strategies [S2, unofficial]. Relightable variants like PSAvatar added BRDF modeling for lighting synthesis [S2, unofficial]. Meta's Codec Avatar 3D represents a production-grade implementation for telepresence [S2, unofficial]. The field has expanded to holistic scene reconstruction (OmniRe) and SLAM applications (SplaTAM, MonoGS) [S2, unofficial].
