# Reconstruction-based Generation
Also called: LGM
## Overview
Reconstruction-based Generation decomposes 3D generation into two distinct stages: multi-view image synthesis followed by 3D reconstruction [S1, S2]. Unlike direct prediction methods, it leverages pre-trained diffusion models to generate posed multi-view images, which then serve as supervision for a Gaussian Splatting (GS) reconstructor [S1]. This approach reuses mature reconstruction pipelines, making system-level engineering straightforward, but inherits the consistency limitations of the multi-view generator [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Text prompt or single reference image.
- Output: Set of 3D Gaussian parameters ($\mu, \Sigma, c, \alpha$).

**Data Structures:**
- Multi-view images: A small set of posed 2D images generated by a diffusion model.
- Asymmetric U-Net: A network architecture designed to accept high-resolution image inputs while limiting the output Gaussian count [S2].

**Steps:**
1. **Multi-View Synthesis:** An off-the-shelf multi-view or video diffusion model generates a set of posed views from the text or single image prompt [S1, S2].
2. **GS Reconstruction Training:** A U-Net-based network is trained to predict 3D Gaussian parameters directly from the generated multi-view images [S2].
3. **Inference:** The trained U-Net processes the multi-view images to output the final 3D Gaussian representation [S2].

**Parameters:**
- The U-Net is asymmetric, allowing high-resolution image input while limiting the number of output Gaussians [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Imperfect cross-view consistency in the generated images can lead to noisy or inconsistent 3D reconstructions [S1].
- The method inherits limitations such as Janus ambiguity, over-smoothing, or texture oversaturation if the multi-view generator lacks additional regularizers or geometry-aware constraints [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Strengths:** Reuses mature reconstruction pipelines; straightforward system-level engineering [S1].
- **Limitations:** Prone to Janus ambiguity, over-smoothing, or texture oversaturation without additional regularizers [S1]. Inherits fundamental limitations of the multi-view generator, such as imperfect cross-view consistency leading to noisy results [S1].
- **Performance:** Computational cost is high (minutes to hours per prompt) [S1].
- **When to use:** When leveraging existing diffusion models and reconstruction pipelines is preferred over training end-to-end direct prediction models.
- **When not to use:** When strict cross-view consistency is critical and the multi-view generator cannot provide sufficient geometric constraints [S1].
## Background
Originating from the observation that direct generation is prone to artifacts, this method was popularized by models like LGM [S1, S2]. It builds on the idea that separating generation into synthesis and reconstruction allows for better reuse of existing technologies [S1]. LGM first exploited off-the-shelf models for multi-view generation and then trained a U-Net-based network for GS reconstruction [S2].
