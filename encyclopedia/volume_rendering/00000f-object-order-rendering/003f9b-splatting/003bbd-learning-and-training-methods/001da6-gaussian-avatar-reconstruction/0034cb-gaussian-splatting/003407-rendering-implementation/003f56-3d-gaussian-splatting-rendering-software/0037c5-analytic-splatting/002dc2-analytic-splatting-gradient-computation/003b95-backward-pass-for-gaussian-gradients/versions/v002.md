# Backward Pass for Gaussian Gradients

Also called: Gaussian Splatting Backpropagation, Gradient Computation for Gaussian Parameters

## Overview
Computes the exact gradient of a scalar loss $L$ with respect to the input 3D Gaussian parameters (color $c$, opacity $o$, mean $\mu$, scale $s$, rotation $q$) by propagating pixel gradients backward through the alpha-compositing rasterizer [S1, unofficial]. It enables differentiable training of Gaussian splat parameters by providing gradients for all learnable variables required for optimization [S1, unofficial]. Unlike the forward pass which composites colors front-to-back, this method propagates gradients back-to-front to correctly account for occlusion and transparency [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Gradient of the loss with respect to the final pixel color, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial C_i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, for each pixel $i$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Output:** Gradients with respect to Gaussian parameters: $\frac{\partial L}{\partial c_n}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial o_n}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu'_n}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \Sigma'_n}$, and subsequently $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu_n}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial R_n}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial S_n}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian Parameters:** Each Gaussian $n$ has color $c_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$, opacity $o_n \in \mathbb{R}$, 3D mean $\mu_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scale $s_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and rotation $q_n \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion) [S1, unofficial].
- **2D Projection:** For each Gaussian contributing to a pixel, the 2D mean $\mu'_n \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and 2D covariance $\Sigma'_n \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ are computed during the forward pass [S1, unofficial].
- **Accumulators:** $T_n$ (transmittance) and $S_n$ (accumulated color) are stored or recomputed during the backward pass [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1. **Initialize Backward Accumulators:**
   Let $N$ be the number of Gaussians contributing to the pixel, ordered from front to back in the forward pass [S1, unofficial]. In the backward pass, iterate $n$ from $N$ down to $1$ [S1, unofficial].
   Initialize $T_N$ using the final transmittance saved from the forward pass [S1, unofficial].
   Initialize $S_N(k) = 0$ for each color channel $k \in \{R, G, B\}$ [S1, unofficial].

2. **Iterate Backward from $n = N$ to $1$:**
   For each Gaussian $n$ contributing to the pixel:

   a. **Compute Transmittance and Accumulated Color Gradients:**
      Compute $T_{n-1}$ using the saved $T_n$ and the computed $\alpha_{n-1}$:
      $$ T_{n-1} = \frac{T_n}{1 - \alpha_{n-1}} \quad (23) $$
      Update the accumulated color sum $S_{n-1}$:
      $$ S_{n-1}(k) = c_n(k) \alpha_n T_n + S_n(k) \quad (25) $$
      where $S_n(k) = \sum_{m > n} c_m(k) \alpha_m T_m$ [S1, unofficial].

   b. **Compute Gradients for Color and Opacity:**
      The gradient of the pixel color $C_i(k)$ with respect to the Gaussian's color $c_n(k)$ is:
      $$ \frac{\partial C_i(k)}{\partial c_n(k)} = \alpha_n T_n \quad (22) $$
      Thus, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial c_n(k)} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C_i(k)} \alpha_n T_n$ [S1, unofficial].

      The gradient with respect to opacity $\alpha_n$ is:
      $$ \frac{\partial C_i(k)}{\partial \alpha_n} = c_n(k) T_n - \frac{S_n(k)}{1 - \alpha_n} \quad (24) $$
      Then, propagate to opacity parameter $o_n$ and the exponential term $\sigma_n$:
      $$ \frac{\partial \alpha_n}{\partial o_n} = \exp(-\sigma_n), \quad \frac{\partial \alpha_n}{\partial \sigma_n} = -o_n \exp(-\sigma_n) \quad (26) $$
      where $\sigma_n = \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{p} - \boldsymbol{\mu}'_n)^T (\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_n)^{-1} (\mathbf{p} - \boldsymbol{\mu}'_n)$ [S1, unofficial].

   c. **Compute Gradient for 2D Mean $\mu'_n$:**
      The gradient of $\sigma_n$ with respect to $\mu'_n$ is:
      $$ \frac{\partial \sigma_n}{\partial \mu'_n} = \Sigma'^{-1}_n \Delta_n \quad (27) $$
      where $\Delta_n = \mathbf{p} - \boldsymbol{\mu}'_n$ [S1, unofficial].
      Combine with $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \sigma_n}$ to get $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu'_n}$ [S1, unofficial].

   d. **Compute Gradient for 2D Covariance $\Sigma'_n$:**
      Let $Y = (\Sigma'_n)^{-1}$. The Jacobian of $\sigma_n$ with respect to $Y$ is:
      $$ \frac{\partial \sigma_n}{\partial Y} = \frac{1}{2} \Delta_n \Delta_n^\top \quad (28) $$
      Using the identity $\frac{\partial Y}{\partial x} = -Y \frac{\partial \Sigma'_n}{\partial x} Y$, the gradient with respect to $\Sigma'_n$ is:
      $$ \frac{\partial \sigma_n}{\partial \Sigma'_n} = -\frac{1}{2} \Sigma'^{-1}_n \Delta_n \Delta_n^\top \Sigma'^{-1}_n \quad (31) $$
      Combine with $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \sigma_n}$ to get $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \Sigma'_n}$ [S1, unofficial].

   e. **Propagate to 3D Parameters:**
      The gradients $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu'_n}$ and $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \Sigma'_n}$ must be propagated to the 3D mean $\mu_n$, rotation $R_n$, and scale $S_n$ [S1, unofficial].
      
      First, compute the gradient with respect to the camera coordinates $t$ (homogeneous coordinates of the projected mean) [S1, unofficial]. The contribution from the 2D mean is:
      $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mu'}}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{2} P^{\top} \begin{bmatrix} w/t_w & 0 & 0 & -w \cdot t_x / t_w^2 \\ 0 & h/t_w & 0 & -w \cdot t_y / t_w^2 \end{bmatrix}^{\top} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mu'} \quad (33) $$
      The contribution from the 2D covariance involves the Jacobians of the projection matrix $J$ with respect to $t_x, t_y, t_z$ (Eqs. 37-38 in [S1, unofficial]). Sum these to get $\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}$ [S1, unofficial].
      
      Then, compute gradients for the view matrix $T_{cw}$ and 3D mean $\mu$:
      $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial T_{\text{CW}}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial t} q^\top \quad (40) $$
      $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mu} = R_{\text{CW}}^\top \left[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial t_x} \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial t_y} \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial t_z} \right]^\top \quad (40) $$
      where $q = [\mu, 1]^\top$ and $t = T_{cw} q$ [S1, unofficial].

      Finally, decompose the 3D covariance $\Sigma = M M^\top$ (where $M = R S$) to get gradients for rotation $R$ and scale $S$:
      $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial R} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial M} S^T, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S} = R^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial M} \quad (44) $$
      The Jacobians of $R$ with respect to quaternion components $w, x, y, z$ are given by Eqs. 45-46 in [S1, unofficial] [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def backward_pass(gaussians, pixel_grad, cam_params, img_dims):
    """
    gaussians: list of dicts/objects with fields: color, opacity, mu_3d, cov_3d, mu_2d, cov_2d, alpha, sigma, T_forward
    pixel_grad: gradient of loss w.r.t. pixel color, shape (3,)
    cam_params: dict with keys: T_cw (4x4 view matrix), P (3x4 projection matrix), fx, fy, w, h
    img_dims: (width, height)
    Returns: list of gradient dicts for each Gaussian
    """
    import numpy as np
    
    N = len(gaussians)
    T_n = gaussians[N-1]['T_forward']
    S_n = np.zeros(3)
    grads = []
    
    for n in range(N-1, -1, -1):
        g = gaussians[n]
        delta = g['pixel_pos'] - g['mu_2d']
        Sigma_inv = np.linalg.inv(g['cov_2d'])
        sigma = 0.5 * delta.T @ Sigma_inv @ delta
        alpha = g['opacity'] * np.exp(-sigma)
        
        # 1. Gradients for color and opacity
        dL_dc = pixel_grad * alpha * T_n
        dL_dalpha = np.sum(pixel_grad * (g['color'] * T_n - S_n / (1 - alpha)))
        dL_do = dL_dalpha * np.exp(-sigma)
        dL_dsigma = dL_dalpha * (-g['opacity'] * np.exp(-sigma))
        
        # 2. Gradients for 2D mean and covariance
        dL_dmu_2d = dL_dsigma * (Sigma_inv @ delta)
        dL_dSigma_2d = dL_dsigma * (-0.5 * Sigma_inv @ np.outer(delta, delta) @ Sigma_inv)
        
        # 3. Propagate to 3D mean, rotation, scale
        # Compute gradient w.r.t camera coordinates t
        # Contribution from 2D mean (Eq 33)
        J_mu_term = np.array([[g['w']/2, 0, 0, -g['w']*g['t_x']/(2*g['t_w']**2)],
                              [0, g['h']/2, 0, -g['h']*g['t_y']/(2*g['t_w']**2)]])
        dL_dt_mu = 0.5 * cam_params['P'].T @ J_mu_term.T @ dL_dmu_2d
        
        # Contribution from 2D covariance (Eqs 37-38)
        # dJ/dt_x, dJ/dt_y, dJ/dt_z matrices
        dJ_dt_x = np.array([[0, 0, -cam_params['fx']/g['t_x']**2], [0, 0, 0]])
        dJ_dt_y = np.array([[0, 0, 0], [0, 0, -cam_params['fy']/g['t_y']**2]])
        dJ_dt_z = np.array([[-cam_params['fx']/g['t_z']**2, 0, 2*cam_params['fx']*g['t_x']/g['t_z']**3],
                            [0, -cam_params['fy']/g['t_z']**2, 2*cam_params['fy']*g['t_y']/g['t_z']**3]])
        dL_dt_sigma = np.zeros(4)
        dL_dt_sigma[:3] += dJ_dt_x.T @ (dL_dSigma_2d @ np.array([1, 0, 0]))
        dL_dt_sigma[:3] += dJ_dt_y.T @ (dL_dSigma_2d @ np.array([0, 1, 0]))
        dL_dt_sigma[:3] += dJ_dt_z.T @ (dL_dSigma_2d @ np.array([0, 0, 1]))
        
        dL_dt = dL_dt_mu + dL_dt_sigma
        
        # Then dL/dT_cw and dL/dmu (Eq 40)
        q = np.array([g['mu_3d'][0], g['mu_3d'][1], g['mu_3d'][2], 1.0])
        dL_dT_cw = np.outer(dL_dt, q)
        R_cw = cam_params['T_cw'][:3, :3]
        dL_dmu = R_cw.T @ dL_dt[:3]
        
        # Then dL/dR and dL/dS (Eq 44)
        # Assuming M = R @ S is available or decomposed
        dL_dM = dL_dSigma_2d # Simplified mapping for pseudocode clarity
        dL_dR = dL_dM @ g['S'].T
        dL_dS = R_cw.T @ dL_dM
        
        # Then dL/dq (Eqs 45-46)
        # Jacobians of R w.r.t w, x, y, z applied to dL_dR
        
        grads.append({
            'color': dL_dc,
            'opacity': dL_do,
            'mu_3d': dL_dmu,
            'R': dL_dR,
            'S': dL_dS
        })
        
        # Update T and S for next iteration (n-1)
        # T_{n-1} = T_n / (1 - alpha_{n-1}) -> computed at start of next iter or end of this using next alpha
        # S_{n-1} = c_n * alpha_n * T_n + S_n
        S_prev = g['color'] * alpha * T_n + S_n
        # T_prev will be computed using alpha of next Gaussian
        T_n = T_n # placeholder, will be updated in next iter
        S_n = S_prev
        
    return grads
```

## Subtopics

### Skip Invisible Gaussian Updates
An optional extension that skips parameter updates for invisible Gaussians to further accelerate training, based on the idea by Mallick et al. [S2, unofficial]. It omits parameter updates for Gaussians that are not visible in the current view during the backward pass [S2, unofficial]. During the backward pass, invisible Gaussians are identified and their parameter update computation is skipped [S2, unofficial]. This speeds up training and reduces VRAM usage but slightly degrades quality [S2, unofficial]. On Mip-NeRF360 outdoor scenes (RTX 4090), it reduces training time from 5m31s to 3m03s, VRAM from 6.0GiB to 5.1GiB, and Gaussian count from 3.87M to 3.34M, while PSNR drops from 24.72 to 24.59 [S2, unofficial]. Skipping updates can cause inconsistencies and performance regressions compared to the original 3DGS implementation [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Proportional to the number of Gaussians contributing to each pixel. The backward pass iterates through these Gaussians in reverse order, performing constant-time operations per Gaussian per pixel [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing intermediate values ($T_n, S_n$) for each Gaussian per pixel during the backward pass, or recomputing them. The fused implementation reduces VRAM usage by avoiding storing all intermediate gradients [S2, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** On an RTX 4090, the full implementation takes 5m31s on Mip-NeRF360 outdoor scenes. With the "Skip Invisible" optimization, this drops to 3m03s [S2, unofficial]. On an RTX 5090, training takes 163 seconds on average, a 5× improvement over the original implementation [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Provides exact gradients for all parameters, enabling stable optimization. The analytic approach avoids numerical differentiation errors [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Computationally intensive due to the need to propagate gradients through the projection and covariance decomposition steps. Skipping invisible Gaussians can lead to quality degradation [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The backward pass for Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper by Kerbl et al. [S1, unofficial]. It relies on the Frobenius inner product for matrix derivatives [S1, unofficial]. The method has been optimized in subsequent works like Faster-GS, which analyzes and improves the optimization process [S2, unofficial].
