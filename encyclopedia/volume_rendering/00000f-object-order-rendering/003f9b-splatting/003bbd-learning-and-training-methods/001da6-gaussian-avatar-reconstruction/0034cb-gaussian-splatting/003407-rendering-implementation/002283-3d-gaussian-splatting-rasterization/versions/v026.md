# 3D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), 3DGS Rasterization, Differentiable Gaussian Rasterization, TrimGS, gsplat.rasterization

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization is a differentiable rendering technique that represents a scene as a collection of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives and projects them onto a 2D image plane for real-time, photorealistic rendering [S1][S4, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently rendering continuous volumetric representations by converting each 3D Gaussian into a 2D splat, handling overlap via depth-sorted alpha compositing, and enabling gradient flow back to Gaussian parameters for training [S4, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings by providing the core differentiable forward and backward passes for 3D Gaussians, often implemented as optimized GPU kernels (e.g., CUDA or MLX), whereas alternatives like [Perspective-Correct 3D Gaussian Splatting via Plücker Coordinates](3403-perspective-correct-3d-gaussian-splatting-via-pl-cker-coordi.md) focus on specific projection corrections or [Analytic-Splatting Forward Shading](3459-analytic-splatting-forward-shading.md) on shading models.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $N$ Gaussians, each defined by mean position $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scale $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, rotation $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion), opacity $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients $c_{\ell m}$ (or direct RGB color) [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   Camera parameters: View matrix $V \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$, intrinsic matrix $K \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, image dimensions $W \times H$, near plane $z_{near}$, far plane $z_{far}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   Optional parameters: `render_mode` (e.g., "RGB", "D", "ED"), `rasterize_mode` ("classic", "antialiased"), `camera_model` ("pinhole", "ortho", "fisheye"), `sh_degree`, `tile_size` (default 16) [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Rendered image $C_{final} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ (and optionally depth, normals, or masks) [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** Stored in contiguous arrays or tensors.
    *   `means`: $[N, 3]$ float32.
    *   `quats`: $[N, 4]$ float32 (normalized).
    *   `scales`: $[N, 3]$ float32.
    *   `opacities`: $[N]$ float32.
    *   `colors`: $[N, D]$ float32 (where $D=3$ for RGB, or larger if SH coefficients are pre-computed) [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Projected Attributes (per camera):**
    *   `means_2D`: $[N, 2]$ float32.
    *   `conics`: $[N, 3]$ float32, storing the upper triangle of $\Sigma_{2D}^{-1}$ as $[\Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[0,0], \Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[0,1], \Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[1,1]]$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
    *   `radii`: $[N, 2]$ int32, bounding box in pixel space.
    *   `depths`: $[N]$ float32, camera-space $z$-depth.
*   **Tile Data:**
    *   `isect_offsets`: $[C, H_{tile}, W_{tile}]$ int32, encoding the start index of Gaussians intersecting each tile [S2, unofficial].
    *   `flatten_ids`: $[N_{active}]$ int32, mapping tile-local Gaussian indices to global Gaussian indices [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Input Validation & Parameter Setup:**
    Validate `render_mode`, `rasterize_mode`, and `camera_model`. If `sh_degree` is not None, compute view directions $d_i = \mu_i - \text{campos}$ and evaluate SH to get colors $c_i$ [S2, unofficial]. If `render_mode` requires depth, compute camera-space means $\mu'_i = V \mu_i$ and hit distances $d_{hit} = \|\mu'_i\|$ [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Covariance Construction:**
    Compute the 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_{3D}$ from quaternions and scales:
    $$ \Sigma_{3D} = R S S^T R^T $$
    where $R$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q$, and $S = \text{diag}(s)$ [S1][S3, unofficial].

3.  **Projection to 2D:**
    Transform means to camera space $\mu' = V \mu$. Transform covariance to camera space $\Sigma' = V_{1:3, 1:3} \Sigma_{3D} V_{1:3, 1:3}^T$. Compute the 2D screen-space covariance $\Sigma_{2D}$ using the Jacobian $J$ of the projective projection:
    $$ \Sigma_{2D} = J \Sigma' J^T $$
    where $J = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{f_x}{\mu'_z} & 0 & -\frac{f_x \mu'_x}{\mu'_z^2} \\ 0 & \frac{f_y}{\mu'_z} & -\frac{f_y \mu'_y}{\mu'_z^2} \end{pmatrix}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
    Compute the conic vector $C = [\Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[0,0], \Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[0,1], \Sigma_{2D}^{-1}[1,1]]$ [S2, S3, unofficial]. If `rasterize_mode` is "antialiased", compute compensation factors [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Culling:**
    Filter Gaussians where $\mu'_z < z_{near}$ or outside the view frustum [S3, S4, unofficial].

5.  **Sorting:**
    Sort remaining Gaussians by camera-space depth $z$ (front-to-back) [S3, S4, unofficial]. *Pitfall:* Sorting millions of Gaussians is a primary bottleneck; optimized radix sort (e.g., CUB) is typically used [S4, unofficial].

6.  **Tile Intersection:**
    Divide the image into tiles of size `tile_size` $\times$ `tile_size`. For each Gaussian, compute its 2D bounding box. Identify which tiles intersect with each Gaussian. Encode these intersections into `isect_offsets` and `flatten_ids` [S2, unofficial].

7.  **Rasterization (Pixel-wise Compositing):**
    For each pixel $(u, v)$ in each tile:
    *   Initialize accumulated color $C_{acc} = 0$, transmittance $T = 1.0$, and mask $M = 0$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Iterate over Gaussians $i$ that intersect the tile and cover the pixel (using `flatten_ids`):
        *   Compute distance vector $\mathbf{x} = [u - \mu'_{2D,i,x}, v - \mu'_{2D,i,y}]^T$ [S3, unofficial].
        *   Compute alpha contribution:
            $$ \alpha_i = \alpha_i^{global} \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \mathbf{x}^T \Sigma_{2D,i}^{-1} \mathbf{x}\right) $$
            If `rasterize_mode` is "antialiased", multiply by compensation factor [S2, unofficial].
        *   Update compositing:
            $$ C_{acc} \leftarrow C_{acc} + T \cdot \alpha_i \cdot c_i $$
            $$ T \leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_i) $$
            $$ M \leftarrow M + T_{old} \cdot \alpha_i $$
        *   If $T < \epsilon$ (e.g., $10^{-4}$), break early [S3, unofficial].
    *   Composite background: $C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - M) \cdot C_{bg}$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   If depth is requested, compute weighted depth: $D_{final} = \frac{\sum T_{old,i} \cdot \alpha_i \cdot z_i}{M}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def rasterize_gaussians(means_3d, quats, scales, opacities, colors, viewmats, Ks, width, height, backgrounds, sh_degree=None, tile_size=16, near_plane=0.01, far_plane=1e10, render_mode="RGB"):
    """
    Renders 3D Gaussians to 2D images.
    """
    C = viewmats.shape[0] # Number of cameras
    N = means_3d.shape[0] # Number of Gaussians
    
    # 1. Compute 3D Covariance
    # R is rotation matrix from quat, S is diag(scales)
    cov_3d = quat_scale_to_covar(quats, scales) # Shape [N, 3, 3]
    
    # 2. Project to 2D
    # Linearized projection using camera intrinsics/extrinsics
    means_2d, cov_2d, radii, depths, conics = linearized_project(
        means_3d, cov_3d, viewmats, Ks, width, height, near_plane, far_plane
    ) # Returns [C, N, 2], [C, N, 3, 3], [C, N, 2], [C, N], [C, N, 3]
    
    # 3. Handle Colors (SH or Direct)
    if sh_degree is not None:
        # Compute view directions
        campos = viewmats[:, :3, 3]
        dirs = means_3d[None, :, :] - campos[:, None, :]
        dirs_norm = mx.maximum(mx.linalg.norm(dirs, axis=-1, keepdims=True), 1e-8)
        dirs = dirs / dirs_norm
        # Broadcast SH coefficients and evaluate
        coeffs = broadcast_sh_coeffs(sh_degree, N, C)
        rgb = spherical_harmonics(sh_degree, dirs, coeffs)
        colors = mx.maximum(rgb + 0.5, 0.0) # Clamp
    else:
        # Broadcast direct colors [N, D] to [C, N, D]
        colors = mx.broadcast_to(colors[None, :, :], (C, N, colors.shape[-1]))
        
    # 4. Culling and Sorting
    # Filter by near plane and frustum (implementation specific)
    # Sort by depth
    sorted_indices = argsort_depth(depths) # Shape [C, N]
    
    # 5. Tile Intersection
    tile_width = ceil(width / tile_size)
    tile_height = ceil(height / tile_size)
    tiles_per_gauss, isect_ids, flatten_ids = isect_tiles(
        means_2d, radii, depths, tile_size, tile_width, tile_height
    )
    isect_offsets = isect_offset_encode(isect_ids, C, tile_width, tile_height)
    
    # 6. Rasterize to Pixels
    # Call fused kernel or loop over tiles
    render_colors, render_alphas = rasterize_to_pixels(
        means_2d, conics, colors, opacities, width, height, 
        tile_size, isect_offsets, flatten_ids, backgrounds
    )
    
    # 7. Post-processing (Depth/Expected Depth)
    if "D" in render_mode or "ED" in render_mode:
        # Compute depth if not already in colors
        # Normalize by alpha for expected depth
        depth_channel = compute_depth(depths, render_alphas, sorted_indices)
        if "ED" in render_mode:
            depth_channel = depth_channel / mx.maximum(render_alphas, 1e-10)
        # Concatenate to colors
        render_colors = concatenate([render_colors, depth_channel], axis=-1)
        
    return render_colors, render_alphas
```

### Temporal Marginal Probability Filtering
Pre-rasterization culling step that removes 4D Gaussians with negligible temporal support at the current frame timestamp, stabilizing rendering speed across video length [S6, unofficial]. It computes the marginal probability $p_i(t)$ for each 4D Gaussian $i$ at the current frame's timestamp $t$ and discards those with negligible $p_i(t)$ before frustum culling [S6, unofficial]. Unlike methods where Gaussian count scales with video length, this keeps active Gaussians constant, making it friendly to long videos [S6, unofficial].

### t-distribution truncation for splatting
A projection truncation technique that replaces the fixed three-sigma rule with a $\nu$-dependent critical value interpolation to handle fat-tailed Student's t-distributions [S7]. For thin tails (high $\nu$), it applies the standard three-sigma rule; for fat tails (low $\nu$), it interpolates critical values from reference [9] to compute the truncation threshold [S7]. Using a fixed threshold on fat-tailed distributions causes excessive truncation or leakage, which this method resolves [S7].

### Progressive Normal Propagation
Uses progressive propagation along normals to fill texture-less regions [S5, unofficial].

### Splat TBO Upload & Layout
Packs per-splat data (center, covariance, DC color/alpha, optional SH) into Texture Buffer Objects (TBOs) for GPU consumption [S8, unofficial]. The Base TBO uses 2 `uvec4` texels per splat (32 B): Texel 0 stores `center.xyz` (raw float bits) and `packHalf2x16(cov00, cov01)`; Texel 1 stores `packHalf2x16(cov02, cov11)`, `packHalf2x16(cov12, cov22)`, and `packUnorm4x8(DC-baked RGBA)` [S8, unofficial]. The SH TBO uses `GL_RGBA16F` format, with layout `[splat0_c1, splat0_c2, …, splat1_c1, splat1_c2, …]` [S8, unofficial]. Memory usage is 32 B/splat for Base TBO + 8 B $\times$ `sh_coeffs_per_splat_` $\times$ `splat_count_` for SH TBO, where `sh_coeffs_per_splat_` = $(active\_sh\_degree\_ + 1)^2 - 1$ [S8, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by sorting $N$ Gaussians ($O(N \log N)$) and tile-based rasterization ($O(N \cdot A)$ where $A$ is average splat area in pixels) [S4, unofficial]. The original implementation uses CUB device radix sort for efficiency [S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for Gaussian attributes. High VRAM usage: ~4GB to view, ~12GB to train [S4, unofficial]. Large disk size: 1GB+ for a scene [S4, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Enables real-time rendering (60+ FPS) for scenes with millions of Gaussians on modern GPUs [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High visual fidelity, real-time performance, differentiable training [S4, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High VRAM/disk usage, static scenes (original), sorting bottleneck on non-CUDA hardware, incompatible with traditional triangle pipelines without adaptation [S4, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of high-quality static or dynamic scenes from sparse images.
*   **When not to use:** When strict compatibility with existing triangle-based pipelines is required without adaptation, or when memory is severely constrained.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the paper "3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering" [S4, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of representing scenes as collections of 3D Gaussians, enabling real-time rendering through efficient rasterization [S1]. The method uses Structure from Motion (SfM) to initialize Gaussians from point clouds, followed by differentiable rasterization and Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) to optimize Gaussian parameters [S4, unofficial]. It has become a standard technique for novel view synthesis and 3D scene representation [S1].
