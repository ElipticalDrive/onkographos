# Centralized Conics Approach

Also called: Botsch et al. Splat Extent Approximation, Botsch et al.'s approximation, Zwicker et al. Centralized Conics Approach

## Overview
The Centralized Conics Approach is an efficient approximation method for computing the screen-space position and extents of a splat in point-based rendering [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of determining the bounding region of a projected Gaussian kernel to optimize rasterization or raycasting. It sets itself apart from [Exact Half-Space Clipping](3395-exact-half-space-clipping.md) by using a simplified projective mapping approximation rather than exact geometric clipping, prioritizing computational speed over geometric precision [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes the screen-space bounding box of a splat by approximating the projection of its parameter space (defined by the splat center and two spanning vectors) onto the image plane [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Splat parameters (center position, two vectors spanning the ellipse in parameter space), camera projection matrix.
*   **Output:** Screen-space position and extents (bounding box) of the splat.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on a 2D-to-2D projective mapping matrix derived from the splat's geometry and the camera projection. No specific bit-packing is mandated, but floating-point precision is critical due to numerical instability issues [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Construct the 2D-to-2D projective mapping matrix that transforms the splat's parameter space to screen space. This involves combining the splat's local basis vectors with the global camera projection [S1, unofficial].
2.  Compute the inverse of this projective mapping matrix [S1, unofficial].
3.  Apply the inverse mapping to the boundary of the splat's parameter space (typically the unit circle or cutoff boundary) to determine the screen-space extents [S1, unofficial].
4.  Derive the bounding box from the transformed boundary points [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
Let $M$ be the 2D-to-2D projective mapping matrix from parameter space to screen space. The screen-space coordinates $x_s$ are related to parameter space coordinates $x_p$ by:
$$ x_s \approx M x_p $$
The extents are computed using the inverse:
$$ x_p \approx M^{-1} x_s $$
The exact formula for $M$ is not provided in the sources, but it is derived from the splat's center and spanning vectors [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Splat cutoff threshold: Not given in the sources.
*   Precision: High precision is required to mitigate roundoff errors [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Instability:** The method is numerically unstable when the projection of a splat approaches a line. In this case, the condition number of the mapping increases rapidly, leading to large roundoff errors [S1, unofficial].
*   **Underestimation:** Under certain conditions, the screen-space extents are underestimated by this approximation, which can cause rendering artifacts [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Efficient to compute compared to exact methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it does not require complex data structures for exact clipping [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Considered the most performant way on NVIDIA hardware when drawing point primitives as screen-space squares and casting rays for fragment testing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Computational efficiency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Numerical instability near line projections; potential for underestimation leading to artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When performance is critical and the splat projections are unlikely to approach degenerate line cases [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When high geometric accuracy is required or when splats are projected near parallel to the view plane [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method is attributed to Zwicker et al. and is also associated with Botsch et al. [S1, unofficial]. It was proposed as an efficient alternative to exact screen-space extent computation. Sigg et al. later noted the limitations of this approach, particularly the reliance on the inverse of the projective mapping, and suggested alternative robust solutions [S1, unofficial].
