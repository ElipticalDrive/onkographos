# Splat Extent and Projection
## Overview
This family addresses the geometric problem of determining the screen-space position and spatial extent (bounding region) of a projected 3D kernel (splat) to optimize rasterization, culling, and clipping [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [EWA Splatting](1069-ewa-splatting.md) by focusing specifically on the analytical or approximate derivation of the splat's footprint boundaries rather than the density evaluation or compositing order [G].

## Shared principles
Builds on [Splat Kernels and Footprints](8724-splat-kernels-and-footprints.md) [G].

## Subtopics

### [Perspective-Correct Point Splatting Vertex Shader](1082-perspective-correct-point-splatting-vertex-shader.md)
This method renders point clouds or voxel centers as splats in a vertex shader, applying size attenuation based on perspective projection to ensure the visual size of points corresponds to their world-space radius. It solves the problem of points appearing incorrectly sized at varying depths, which occurs when using fixed viewport sizes or naive depth scaling. It sets itself apart from siblings like the [Centralized Conics Approach](1243-centralized-conics-approach.md) by operating at the primitive level (vertex/fragment) rather than computing exact geometric intersections, and from [Exact Half-Space Clipping](3395-exact-half-space-clipping.md) by relying on hardware-accelerated point rasterization rather than explicit clipping logic.

### [Footprint Evaluation](1180-footprint-evaluation.md)
Footprint Evaluation is a forward-mapping object-order volume rendering algorithm that projects each voxel independently onto the 2D image plane, distributing its energy via a reconstruction kernel to form a "splat" or footprint. It solves the problem of efficiently rendering regular volumetric grids by avoiding the per-pixel ray traversal of image-order methods, instead building the image voxel-by-voxel. It sets itself apart from ray-casting by pre-computing the integration of the 3D kernel into a 2D footprint, reducing the per-voxel computational overhead from $O(k^3)$ to $O(k^2)$, where $k$ is the filter dimension.

### [Centralized Conics Approach](1243-centralized-conics-approach.md)
The Centralized Conics Approach is an efficient approximation for computing the screen-space position and extents of a splat. It sets itself apart from [Exact Half-Space Clipping](3395-exact-half-space-clipping.md) and [Mueller's Splatting Approach](5752-mueller-s-splatting-approach.md) by relying on a projective mapping approximation rather than exact geometric clipping or ray-based evaluation, prioritizing computational speed over strict geometric precision.

### [Exact Half-Space Clipping](3395-exact-half-space-clipping.md)
Exact Half-Space Clipping (implemented as XClipGS) is a differentiable operator for interactive volume rendering that computes the exact retained ray integral of a 3D Gaussian primitive when intersected by a clipping plane. It solves the problem of binary keep/drop decisions (Hard Cull) and approximate moment-matching, which cause aliasing, popping artifacts, and silhouette errors when clipping planes expose hidden anatomy. It sets itself apart from [ClipGS](1082-clipgs.md) by avoiding learned binary decisions and MLP deformations, and from [RaRa](1082-rara.md) by providing a closed-form backward pass for clip-aware training, all without auxiliary networks.

### [Mueller's Splatting Approach](5752-mueller-s-splatting-approach.md)
Mueller's Splatting Approach is an object-order volume rendering method that treats a gray-scale volume as a 3D discrete density function, calculating the influence of each voxel on multiple output pixels to accelerate Direct Volume Rendering (DVR). It distinguishes itself from ray-casting by exploiting cache coherency through sequential memory traversal and supporting incremental rendering, though it introduces blurring due to spherically symmetric reconstruction kernels.

### Splat Extent Calculation (Polygon Bounding)
A method to compute screen-space position and extents of a splat by bounding it with a polygon in parameter space, transforming to clip space, clipping against the view-frustum, and performing perspective division [S1, unofficial]. The steps are: bound the splat by a polygon in its parameter space, transform the polygon to clip space, clip the polygon by the view-frustum using the Sutherland-Hodgman algorithm, perform perspective division on each vertex to yield the clipped polygon in screen-space, and compute desired quantities (position/extents) from the screen-space polygon [S1, unofficial]. The method is not exact but produces a correct upper bound [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The problem of accurately determining splat extents is critical for efficient rasterization and correct handling of perspective projection artifacts [S1, unofficial]. Early methods relied on approximations that could fail near the near plane or for large splats [S1, unofficial]. The Polygon Bounding method, introduced by Lipponer, provides a robust upper bound by leveraging clip-space clipping [S1, unofficial]. Concurrently, the Centralized Conics Approach offers a faster, albeit less precise, approximation using projective mapping matrices [S1, unofficial]. Perspective-correct vertex shaders ensure that point-based splats maintain correct visual scaling in 3D space [S1, unofficial].
