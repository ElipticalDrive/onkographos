# Resonant Brane Splatting

Also called: Brane, Brane Primitive, Brane Splatting, Resonant Brane

## Overview
Resonant Brane Splatting (RBS) is a feedforward arbitrary-scale super-resolution (ASR) framework that replaces standard flat, single-color 2D Gaussian splats with "Branes," expressive primitives that emit spatially varying colors [S1, unofficial]. By augmenting a Gaussian spatial envelope with internal Gaussian-Hermite modes, each having distinct RGB coefficients, a single Brane can model local contrast, sharp edges, and complex textures without requiring dense overlapping splats [S1, unofficial]. This distinguishes RBS from variants like 2DGH, which use Hermite functions only to modulate the spatial opacity/support profile while keeping color constant [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A low-resolution image $I_{LR} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ and a continuous target scale factor $s \in \mathbb{R}^+$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A super-resolved image $I_{SR} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_{SR} \times W_{SR} \times 3}$, where $H_{SR} = \lfloor Hs \rfloor$ and $W_{SR} = \lfloor Ws \rfloor$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each Brane primitive $k$ is defined by a state vector $\Phi_k$ predicted from a feature vector $\mathbf{f}_k$ [S1, unofficial]. The layout is:
$$ \Phi_k = [\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k, \sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}, \rho_k, \alpha_k, \mathbf{C}_{0,0,k}, \dots, \mathbf{C}_{N,M,k}] $$
Fields:
*   $\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k$: Anchoring offsets (float).
*   $\sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}$: Scale parameters (float).
*   $\rho_k$: Rotation angle (float).
*   $\alpha_k$: Opacity (float).
*   $\mathbf{C}_{n,m,k} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Color coefficients for each mode $(n,m)$ (float).

**Parameters:**
*   $N, M$: Maximum degrees of Gaussian-Hermite modes in x and y directions. Typical value: $N = M = 3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $u$: Upsampling factor determining the number of generated primitives ($K = u\hat{H} \times u\hat{W}$). Tested values: $\{1, 2, 4\}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $d_{max}$: Bounding box fraction in normalized space used for culling candidate Branes [S1, unofficial].
*   $R_{max}$: Canonical space boundary radius for culling, defined as $\sqrt{2n_{max} + 1}$ where $n_{max} = \max(N, M)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction and Upsampling:**
    A neural backbone extracts a dense feature map $\hat{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{\hat{H} \times \hat{W} \times C}$ from $I_{LR}$ [S1, unofficial]. This map is optionally upsampled by factor $u$ to $F \in \mathbb{R}^{H' \times W' \times C}$, where $H' = u\hat{H}$ and $W' = u\hat{W}$ [S1, unofficial]. Let $K = H'W'$ be the total number of primitives [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Prediction of Brane State:**
    For each feature vector $\mathbf{f}_k$ in $F$, an MLP head predicts the full Brane state $\Phi_k$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Anchoring:** The $k$-th Brane is initially anchored at the center of its feature grid cell:
        $$ (x_k^{\text{ref}}, y_k^{\text{ref}}) = \left( \frac{j + 0.5}{W'}, \frac{i + 0.5}{H'} \right) $$
        An MLP predicts continuous offsets $(\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k) = \text{MLP}_{\theta_1}(\mathbf{f}_k)$ [S1, unofficial]. The final center is:
        $$ x_k = x_k^{\text{ref}} + \frac{\Delta x_k}{W'}, \quad y_k = y_k^{\text{ref}} + \frac{\Delta y_k}{H'} $$
    *   **Footprint and Opacity:**
        $$ (\sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}) = \text{sigmoid}(\text{MLP}_{\theta_3}(\mathbf{f}_k)) $$
        $$ \rho_k = \pi \tanh(\text{MLP}_{\theta_4}(\mathbf{f}_k)) $$
        $$ \alpha_k = \text{sigmoid}(\text{MLP}_{\theta_5}(\mathbf{f}_k)) $$
    *   **Mode Colors:**
        Let $L = (N + 1)(M + 1)$ be the total number of modes [S1, unofficial]. A dedicated head predicts raw mode colors $\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_k = \text{MLP}_{\theta_2}(\mathbf{f}_k) \in \mathbb{R}^{3L}$ [S1, unofficial]. Reshape into $L$ coefficients $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial]. Apply activation:
        $$ \mathbf{c}_k^{n,m} = \begin{cases} \text{sigmoid}(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m}), & n=0, m=0 \\ \tanh(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m}), & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        The zero-order mode $\mathbf{c}_{0,0,k}$ defines the base color; higher-order modes are signed residuals [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Rasterization (Culling and Evaluation):**
    For each target pixel $(u, v)$ in the output image:
    *   Map to normalized coordinates:
        $$ (u_{\text{norm}}, v_{\text{norm}}) = \left( \frac{u + 0.5}{W_{SR}}, \frac{v + 0.5}{H_{SR}} \right) $$
    *   **Culling:** Identify candidate Branes. A Brane is considered if its center $(x_k, y_k)$ satisfies $|u_{\text{norm}} - x_k| < d_{max}$ and $|v_{\text{norm}} - y_k| < d_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Quantum Turning Point Check:** For each candidate, compute the squared distance in canonical space. If $x_k'^2 + y_k'^2 > R_{max}^2$, skip the Brane [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Coordinate Transformation:** Compute displacement $(\delta x_k, \delta y_k) = (u_{\text{norm}} - x_k, v_{\text{norm}} - y_k)$ [S1, unofficial]. Map to local canonical coordinates $(x'_k, y'_k)$:
        $$ \begin{pmatrix} x'_k \\ y'_k \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{\sigma_{x,k}} & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma_{y,k} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \cos \rho_k & \sin \rho_k \\ -\sin \rho_k & \cos \rho_k \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \delta x_k \\ \delta y_k \end{pmatrix} $$
    *   **Color Accumulation:** Compute the contribution $\mathcal{B}_k(x'_k, y'_k)$:
        $$ \mathcal{B}_k(x'_k, y'_k) = \alpha_k E(x'_k, y'_k) \sum_{n=0}^N \sum_{m=0}^M \mathbf{c}_{n,m,k} \bar{H}_n(x'_k) \bar{H}_m(y'_k) $$
        where $E(x, y) = \exp\left(-\frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}\right)$ and $\bar{H}_n(z)$ are normalized Hermite polynomials [S1, unofficial].
    *   Sum contributions:
        $$ \mathbf{C}(u, v) = \sum_{k=1}^K \mathcal{B}_k(x'_k, y'_k) $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def hermite_polynomial(n, z):
    """Compute physicist's Hermite polynomial H_n(z) recursively."""
    if n == 0:
        return 1.0
    if n == 1:
        return 2.0 * z
    h_prev2 = 1.0
    h_prev1 = 2.0 * z
    for i in range(2, n + 1):
        h_curr = 2.0 * z * h_prev1 - 2.0 * (i - 1) * h_prev2
        h_prev2 = h_prev1
        h_prev1 = h_curr
    return h_prev1

def normalized_hermite(n, z):
    """Compute normalized Hermite polynomial H_bar_n(z)."""
    if n == 0:
        return 1.0
    # sqrt(2^n * n!)
    denom = math.sqrt((2 ** n) * math.factorial(n))
    return hermite_polynomial(n, z) / denom

def sigmoid(x):
    return 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-x))

def tanh(x):
    return math.tanh(x)

def render_rbs(branes, h_sr, w_sr, d_max, n_max, n_hermite, m_hermite):
    """
    Render image using Resonant Brane Splatting.
    
    Args:
        branes: List of dicts, each containing:
            - x, y: center coordinates
            - sigma_x, sigma_y: scales
            - rho: rotation angle
            - alpha: opacity
            - colors: dict mapping (n, m) tuple to RGB vector (array-like)
        h_sr, w_sr: Output image dimensions
        d_max: Bounding box fraction for culling
        n_max: max(n_hermite, m_hermite)
        n_hermite, m_hermite: Max Hermite degrees
        
    Returns:
        image: 2D array of shape (h_sr, w_sr, 3)
    """
    r_max_sq = 2 * n_max + 1
    image = [[(0.0, 0.0, 0.0) for _ in range(w_sr)] for _ in range(h_sr)]
    
    for u in range(h_sr):
        for v in range(w_sr):
            # Normalized coordinates
            u_norm = (u + 0.5) / w_sr
            v_norm = (v + 0.5) / h_sr
            
            color_sum = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
            
            for k, brane in enumerate(branes):
                # Culling 1: Bounding box
                dx = u_norm - brane['x']
                dy = v_norm - brane['y']
                
                if abs(dx) >= d_max or abs(dy) >= d_max:
                    continue
                
                # Coordinate transformation
                # Rotation matrix inverse: [[cos, sin], [-sin, cos]] applied to delta
                cos_r = math.cos(brane['rho'])
                sin_r = math.sin(brane['rho'])
                
                # Apply rotation
                rot_x = cos_r * dx + sin_r * dy
                rot_y = -sin_r * dx + cos_r * dy
                
                # Apply scaling (inverse scaling in canonical space definition)
                # Formula: [1/sigma_x, 0; 0, sigma_y] * [rot_x; rot_y]
                x_prime = rot_x / brane['sigma_x']
                y_prime = rot_y * brane['sigma_y']
                
                # Culling 2: Quantum turning point
                if x_prime**2 + y_prime**2 > r_max_sq:
                    continue
                
                # Gaussian envelope
                e_val = math.exp(-(x_prime**2 + y_prime**2) / 2.0)
                
                # Hermite sum
                hermite_sum = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
                for n in range(n_hermite + 1):
                    for m in range(m_hermite + 1):
                        h_n = normalized_hermite(n, x_prime)
                        h_m = normalized_hermite(m, y_prime)
                        coeff = brane['colors'][(n, m)]
                        
                        # Accumulate: coeff * h_n * h_m
                        hermite_sum = (
                            hermite_sum[0] + coeff[0] * h_n * h_m,
                            hermite_sum[1] + coeff[1] * h_n * h_m,
                            hermite_sum[2] + coeff[2] * h_n * h_m
                        )
                
                # Brane emission
                brane_color = (
                    brane['alpha'] * e_val * hermite_sum[0],
                    brane['alpha'] * e_val * hermite_sum[1],
                    brane['alpha'] * e_val * hermite_sum[2]
                )
                
                # Additive blending
                color_sum = (
                    color_sum[0] + brane_color[0],
                    color_sum[1] + brane_color[1],
                    color_sum[2] + brane_color[2]
                )
            
            image[u][v] = color_sum
            
    return image
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Instability:** Higher-order Hermite polynomials $H_n(z)$ grow rapidly. Using normalized Hermite polynomials $\bar{H}_n(z)$ removes the dominant degree-dependent growth, ensuring stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Aliasing:** Training strictly on scales $s \in U(1, 4)$ fails to suppress oscillations when generalizing to smooth regions at higher scales, causing aliasing artifacts [S1, unofficial]. This is mitigated by fine-tuning on a wider scale distribution $s \in U(1, 8)$ or using sparsity loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **Color Clipping:** Hard clipping during additive blending is avoided; end-to-end L1 supervision naturally bounds the final rendering within $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Adaptive Mode Culling
A rasterization optimization that skips the evaluation of Hermite basis functions and color accumulation for Branes whose high-order mode coefficients are negligible, reducing computational load without affecting visual quality [S1, unofficial]. For each Brane, if the magnitude of an RGB coefficient for a specific mode $(n, m)$ is less than $10^{-4}$, that mode is skipped [S1, unofficial]. This exploits sparsity induced by fine-tuning or sparsity loss, where unneeded coefficients are driven to zero [S1, unofficial].

### Sparsity Loss (Resonant Brane Splatting)
An explicit regularization term added to the training objective to enforce sparsity in the Hermite mode coefficients, preventing excessive vibration (aliasing) in smooth regions [S1, unofficial]. The loss is computed as:
$$ L_{\text{sparse}} = \lambda_{\text{sparse}} \cdot \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{n+m>0} \|\mathbf{c}_{n,m,k}\|_1 $$
where $\lambda_{\text{sparse}}$ is the weight, $K$ is the number of channels (RGB), and $\mathbf{c}_{n,m,k}$ are the Hermite coefficients [S1, unofficial]. A tuned value of $\lambda_{\text{sparse}} = 0.0005$ yields results virtually identical to fine-tuning, while an aggressive value of $0.1$ stifles details [S1, unofficial]. This explicitly enforces energy awareness otherwise achieved implicitly through fine-tuning [S1, unofficial].

### Resonant Brane Splatting Regularization via Fine-Tuning
A training strategy to mitigate aliasing artifacts in smooth regions at large scales by fine-tuning the model on a wider range of scales, allowing it to naturally dampen high-order coefficients [S1, unofficial]. The model is fine-tuned using a scale distribution $s \in U(1, 8)$ instead of the initial training range [S1, unofficial]. The model learns to dampen coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{n,m}$ to 0 for $n+m > 0$ in flat/smooth areas [S1, unofficial]. Training strictly on scales $s \in U(1, 4)$ fails to suppress oscillations when generalizing to smooth regions at higher scales, causing aliasing artifacts due to over-expressing modes [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Naive evaluation requires $O(K H_{SR} W_{SR})$ operations. Optimized via a CUDA rasterizer with dual culling (bounding box and quantum turning point) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Rmax culling accelerates rendering without quality loss, proving Branes resolve complexity without dense overlap [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly reduces the number of overlapping primitives needed to model high frequencies compared to standard Gaussian Splatting. Provides superior speed-quality trade-off than prior GS methods in ASR tasks [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Predicting excessive modes in a single pass can hamper generalization and cause smooth-region artifacts if not properly regularized [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Arbitrary-Scale Super-Resolution tasks requiring high-frequency detail reconstruction with limited primitive counts [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Standard fixed-resolution rendering where standard Gaussians are sufficient, or when computational resources are extremely limited and culling overhead is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Resonant Brane Splatting (RBS) was introduced as a feedforward Arbitrary-Scale Super-Resolution (ASR) framework [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the expressive Gaussian-Hermite primitive but differs in appearance representation from 2DGH [S1, unofficial]. RBS assigns an RGB-valued coefficient to each Hermite mode, making the emitted color vary spatially within the primitive footprint, unlike 2DGH which uses Hermite functions only for the spatial opacity/support profile [S1, unofficial]. It is used in ASR benchmarks, compared against implicit (Meta-SR, LIIF) and explicit (GaussianSR, GSASR) methods [S1, unofficial].
