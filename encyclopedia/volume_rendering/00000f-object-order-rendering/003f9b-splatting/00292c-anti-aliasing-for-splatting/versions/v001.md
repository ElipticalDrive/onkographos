# Anti-Aliasing for Splatting
Also called: Perspective Splatting Anti-Aliasing, Region-Based Anti-Aliasing

## Overview
This method prevents sample-rate aliasing (prealiasing) artifacts in splatting-based volume rendering that arise when the integration grid sampling frequency is insufficient to represent the volume raster data [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from volume ray-casting, which typically avoids aliasing by using reconstruction kernels that expand as rays diverge in eye space; splatting performs reconstruction in perspective space and lacks this equivalent mechanism, requiring explicit spatial partitioning to apply low-pass filtering where necessary [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Volume raster with grid spacing $s$ and sampling rate $p = 1/s$; perspective projection parameters defining the integration grid sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ as a function of distance $d$ along the z-axis; volume bandlimit frequency $\omega$.
*   **Output:** Aliasing-free rendered image.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Raster:** Regular 3D grid with spacing $s$. Assumed to be bandlimited at frequency $\omega$.
*   **Integration Grid:** The screen-space or eye-space sampling lattice defined by the camera projection.
*   **Distance Threshold $k$:** A scalar value representing the distance along the +z axis in eye space where the volume raster sampling rate equals the integration grid sampling rate ($p = \phi(k)$).

**Steps:**
1.  **Verify Volume Bandlimiting:** Ensure the volume raster sampling rate $p$ satisfies the Nyquist criterion for the volume's highest frequency content $\omega$:
    $$ p > 2\omega \quad [S1, \text{unofficial}, unofficial] $$
    If this condition is not met, the volume raster itself contains aliasing that cannot be removed by the splatting process.

2.  **Calculate Threshold Distance $k$:** Determine the distance $k$ along the +z axis in eye space where the integration grid sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ equals the volume raster sampling rate $p$:
    $$ \phi(k) = p \quad [S1, \text{unofficial}, unofficial] $$
    For orthographic projections, $\phi(d)$ is constant, so $k$ may be undefined or infinite if $p$ is always greater than $\phi$. For perspective projections, $\phi(d)$ decreases as $d$ increases.

3.  **Partition Space into Regions:** Divide the volume along the z-axis into two regions based on $k$:
    *   **Region 1 ($d < k$):** The integration grid sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ is greater than the volume raster sampling rate $p$. Since $p > 2\omega$, it follows that $\phi(d) > 2\omega$. No sample-rate aliasing occurs in this region.
    *   **Region 2 ($d > k$):** The integration grid sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ is less than the volume raster sampling rate $p$. Here, $\phi(d)$ may drop below $2\omega$, risking sample-rate aliasing.

4.  **Apply Anti-Aliasing Filtering:**
    *   **In Region 1:** Render normally without additional low-pass filtering. The integration grid is dense enough to capture the volume data without aliasing.
    *   **In Region 2:** Apply a low-pass filter to the volume raster data before splatting. This filtering reduces the effective bandwidth of the volume data to match the lower sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ of the integration grid in this region, preventing energy from alias spectra from spilling into the primary spectrum [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $s$: Volume raster grid spacing [S1, unofficial].
*   $p$: Volume raster sampling rate, $p = 1/s$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\omega$: Bandlimit frequency of the volume data [S1, unofficial].
*   $\phi(d)$: Sampling frequency of the integration grid at distance $d$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $k$: Distance where $p = \phi(k)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-Bandlimited Volume:** If the volume is not bandlimited, aliasing is present regardless of $p$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Entirely in One Region:** Depending on viewing parameters, the volume raster might lie entirely in Region 1 (no filtering needed) or entirely in Region 2 (filtering needed everywhere) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Orthographic Projection:** $\phi(d)$ is constant. If $p > \phi$, the entire volume is in Region 2. If $p \le \phi$, the entire volume is in Region 1.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds a preprocessing step to identify Region 2 and apply low-pass filtering. The filtering cost depends on the filter kernel size and the volume of Region 2.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the filtered volume data in Region 2, or a mechanism to filter on-the-fly.
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates prealiasing artifacts in perspective splatting where the integration grid becomes sparse.
*   **Limitations:** Requires determining $k$ and applying spatially varying filtering. Postaliasing (from non-ideal reconstruction filters) is not addressed by this method and remains a tradeoff between aliasing and blurring [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method was proposed in "Perspective Splatting" (DTIC ADA363073) to address the lack of an equivalent anti-aliasing mechanism in splatting algorithms compared to volume ray-casting [S1, unofficial]. Volume ray-casting algorithms generally perform reconstruction in eye space and avoid aliasing by using reconstruction kernels that become larger as rays diverge [S1, unofficial]. Splatting algorithms generally perform reconstruction in perspective space and lack this equivalent mechanism, requiring the proposed anti-aliasing method [S1, unofficial].
