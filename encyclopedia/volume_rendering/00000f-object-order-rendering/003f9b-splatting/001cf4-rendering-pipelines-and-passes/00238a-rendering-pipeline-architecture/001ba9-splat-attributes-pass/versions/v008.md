# Splat Attributes Pass

Also called: Blending Pass, G-Buffer Pass

## Overview
The Splat Attributes Pass is the fragment processing stage in hardware-accelerated surface splatting that reconstructs continuous and cardinal attributes from overlapping splats into a reconstruction buffer (G-Buffer) [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of accumulating per-fragment contributions (such as normals, colors, and weights) from overlapping geometric primitives by performing weighted blending and accumulation [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling passes like the [Splat Depth Pass](1078-splat-depth-pass.md) (which only computes visibility) and [Attribute Accumulation and Blending](13190-attribute-accumulation-and-blending.md) (which handles final resolution with ternary depth tests) by operating as the primary accumulation layer for continuous attributes using simple additive blending of weighted values [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of projected splats, each defined by screen-space center, radius, world-space position, color, and normal. The current state of the G-Buffer textures.
*   **Output:** Updated G-Buffer textures containing accumulated weighted attributes.

**Data Structures:**
*   **G-Buffer Textures:**
    *   `g_normal`: `vec4` texture at location 0. Stores weighted normals in RGB channels. The Alpha channel is unused or reserved.
    *   `g_color`: `vec4` texture at location 1. Stores weighted RGB color in RGB channels and accumulated weight in the Alpha channel.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Fragment Position Calculation:** For each fragment generated by the rasterizer, calculate the distance from the splat center in screen space.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{offset} = \mathbf{p}_{frag} - \mathbf{p}_{center} $$
    where $\mathbf{p}_{frag}$ is the fragment position and $\mathbf{p}_{center}$ is the splat center (typically $(0.5, 0.5)$ in normalized point coordinates).
2.  **Discard Outside Radius:** Calculate the squared magnitude of the offset. If the fragment lies outside the splat's circular boundary, discard it.
    $$ m^2 = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{offset} $$
    If $m^2 > 0.25$, terminate processing for this fragment [S1, unofficial].
3.  **World-Space Offset Conversion:** Convert the screen-space offset to a world-space offset using the splat's radius $r$.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{wld} = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \cdot 2r $$
4.  **Depth Offset Calculation:** Calculate the depth offset from the sphere surface to the fragment. This assumes the splat is a sphere of radius $r$.
    $$ d_{offset} = \sqrt{r^2 - \mathbf{p}_{wld} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{wld}} $$
5.  **Fragment Depth in View Space:** Calculate the fragment's depth in view space by subtracting the depth offset from the linear depth of the splat center.
    $$ z_{frag} = z_{linear} - d_{offset} $$
    where $z_{linear}$ is the linear depth of the splat center derived from `gl_FragCoord.z` or uniform data [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Visibility Test:** Sample the depth buffer to retrieve the surface depth $z_{surf}$ at the fragment's screen coordinates.
    $$ z_{surf} = \text{linearDepth}(\text{texture}(g_{depth}, \mathbf{uv})) $$
    where $\mathbf{uv} = \mathbf{p}_{frag} / \text{viewport\_size}$. If $z_{frag} > z_{surf}$, the splat is behind the existing geometry; discard the fragment [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Weight Calculation:** Compute two falloff weights and combine them.
    *   **Radial Falloff ($w_1$):** Based on the distance from the center.
        $$ w_1 = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{m^2}}{0.5} $$
    *   **Depth-Proximity Falloff ($w_2$):** Based on the difference between the surface depth and the splat fragment depth, scaled by a small constant $\epsilon$.
        $$ w_2 = \frac{z_{surf} - z_{frag}}{\epsilon} $$
    *   **Combined Weight ($w$):**
        $$ w = w_1 \cdot w_2 $$
    The parameter $\epsilon$ is a small constant, typically $0.25$ [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Normal Computation:** Calculate the view-space normal for the fragment based on the geometry of the sphere intersection.
    $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{p}_{wld}, d_{offset}) $$
    Note: The third component of the vector passed to normalize is the depth offset $d_{offset}$ [S1, unofficial].
9.  **Attribute Accumulation:** Accumulate the weighted attributes into the G-Buffer textures using additive blending.
    *   **Normal Accumulation:**
        $$ \mathbf{g}_{normal}.xyz \leftarrow \mathbf{g}_{normal}.xyz + w \cdot \mathbf{n} $$
    *   **Color Accumulation:**
        $$ \mathbf{g}_{color}.rgb \leftarrow \mathbf{g}_{color}.rgb + w \cdot \mathbf{c} $$
        where $\mathbf{c}$ is the splat's color.
    *   **Weight Accumulation:**
        $$ \mathbf{g}_{color}.a \leftarrow \mathbf{g}_{color}.a + w $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Fragment Shader Logic for Splat Attributes Pass
# Inputs:
#   gl_FragCoord: current fragment screen position
#   gl_PointCoord: normalized coordinates within the point (0.0 to 1.0)
#   i.radius: splat radius in world units
#   i.color: splat color (vec3)
#   i.linearDepth: linear depth of splat center in view space
#   g_depth: depth buffer texture
#   g_normal: G-Buffer texture for normals (vec4)
#   g_color: G-Buffer texture for color/weight (vec4)
#   viewport_size: (width, height) of the viewport
#   epsilon: small constant for depth falloff (default 0.25)

def process_fragment():
    # 1. Calculate distance from center in screen space
    from_center_pt_spc = gl_PointCoord - vec2(0.5, 0.5)
    
    # 2. Discard fragments outside the point circle
    mag_sq = dot(from_center_pt_spc, from_center_pt_spc)
    if mag_sq > 0.25:
        discard()
        
    # 3. Convert screen-space offset to world-space offset
    from_center_wld_spc = from_center_pt_spc * 2 * i.radius
    
    # 4. Calculate depth offset from the sphere surface
    depth_offset = sqrt(i.radius * i.radius - dot(from_center_wld_spc, from_center_wld_spc))
    
    # 5. Calculate fragment depth in view space
    # Note: linearDepth converts normalized depth to linear view space depth
    depth = linearDepth(gl_FragCoord.z) - depth_offset
    
    # 6. Sample the depth buffer to get surface depth
    tex_coords = gl_FragCoord.xy / viewport_size
    surface_depth = linearDepth(texture(g_depth, tex_coords).x)
    
    # 7. Discard if splat is behind the surface
    if depth > surface_depth:
        discard()
        
    # 8. Calculate radial falloff weight
    w1 = 1 - (sqrt(mag_sq) / 0.5)
    
    # 9. Calculate depth-proximity falloff weight
    w2 = (surface_depth - depth) / epsilon
    
    # 10. Combine weights
    weight = w1 * w2
    
    # 11. Compute view-space normal from geometry
    normal = normalize(vec3(from_center_wld_spc, depth_offset))
    
    # 12. Accumulate weighted normal into g_normal.xyz
    # In a real shader, this requires atomicAdd or render-to-texture with blending
    g_normal.xyz += weight * normal
    
    # 13. Accumulate weighted color into g_color.rgb
    color = weight * i.color
    g_color.rgb += color
    
    # 14. Accumulate weight into g_color.a
    g_color.a += weight
```

**Parameters:**
*   `epsilon`: A small constant used in depth-proximity falloff calculation. Typical value: $0.25$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Linear depth conversion must be consistent between the vertex/geometry stage and the fragment stage to avoid z-fighting or incorrect discard decisions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The radial falloff $w_1$ becomes zero at the exact edge of the splat ($m=0.5$), ensuring smooth transitions if blending is enabled, but the depth falloff $w_2$ may introduce artifacts if the splat is nearly tangent to the surface [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the splat radius is zero or negative, the square root in step 4 may fail or produce NaNs. Guard against non-positive radii [G].

## Subtopics

### Front-to-Back Blending Setup
This variant configures the OpenGL state for the blending pass. It clears the color buffer with RGBA(0,0,0,0) to ensure zero initial alpha. Depth testing is disabled. Blending is enabled with separate blend factors: RGB uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`, and Alpha uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`. The blend equation is set to `GL_FUNC_ADD` for both RGB and Alpha [S2, unofficial]. It draws instanced quads using `GL_TRIANGLE_FAN` with 4 vertices per instance, passing `splatCount` instances. Vertex attributes include 'position' (2 floats) and 'index' (1 unsigned int, instanced with divisor 1) [S2, unofficial]. Uniforms passed to the shader include projection (mat4), view (mat4), focal (vec2: fx, fy), viewport (vec2: width, height), and u_texture (sampler2D at unit 0) [S2, unofficial].

### sRGB to Linear Composite Pass
This variant is a full-screen triangle pass that reads an intermediate sRGB-blended splat accumulation texture, converts it to linear space, and outputs for premultiplied alpha blending onto SceneColor [S3, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself by handling color space conversion as a final compositing step. The pixel shader parameters include `IntermediateTexture` (Texture2D) and `IntermediateSampler` (SamplerState) [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of splats and $K$ is the average number of fragments per splat (determined by the splat radius in screen space).
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H)$ for the G-Buffer textures, where $W$ and $H$ are the viewport dimensions.
*   **Measured Performance:** Performance is heavily dependent on fill rate and the number of overlapping splats. Hardware-accelerated blending is efficient, but the depth buffer sampling and discard logic can cause divergence in warp execution if splat coverage is sparse [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Allows for real-time rendering of large point clouds by leveraging GPU rasterization pipelines. The separation of depth testing and attribute accumulation allows for flexible compositing strategies [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The additive blending of normals and colors in the G-Buffer requires normalization or division by the accumulated weight in a subsequent pass to recover correct values. This pass does not handle occlusion culling itself; it relies on the depth buffer populated by previous passes or the depth test within the pass [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Use when rendering large volumetric datasets or point clouds where hardware rasterization is preferred over ray-marching, and when continuous attribute reconstruction (normals, colors) is required [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Avoid if strict front-to-back alpha blending with correct occlusion is required without post-processing normalization, or if the scene has very few splats where ray-casting might be more efficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Splat Attributes Pass is part of the broader splatting family of volume rendering techniques, which treat volumetric data as a set of geometric primitives (splats) rasterized onto the screen [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial]. The specific implementation details cited here originate from modern GPU-based point cloud and Gaussian Splatting rendering pipelines [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. The method builds on the principle of hardware-accelerated rasterization for real-time interactivity [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
