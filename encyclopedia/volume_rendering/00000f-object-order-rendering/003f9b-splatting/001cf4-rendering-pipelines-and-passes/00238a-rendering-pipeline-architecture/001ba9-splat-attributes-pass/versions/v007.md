# Splat Attributes Pass

Also called: Blending Pass, G-Buffer Pass

## Overview
The Splat Attributes Pass is a fragment-shader-based rendering pass that rasterizes volumetric splats (typically Gaussian primitives) to accumulate weighted attributes—such as color, normal, and opacity—into a G-Buffer for subsequent lighting or compositing stages [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of correctly blending overlapping transparent splats by discarding fragments occluded by the front surface of the splat sphere and weighting contributions by both radial distance from the splat center and depth proximity to the visible surface [S1, unofficial]. It differs from the [Single-Pass Splat Rendering Pipeline](1254-single-pass-splat-rendering-pipeline.md) by explicitly writing to intermediate G-Buffer textures rather than directly to the final color buffer, and from the [Three-Pass GPU Point Rendering Pipeline](2837-three-pass-gpu-point-rendering-pipeline.md) by relying on fragment-shader depth calculations rather than pre-sorted geometry passes for visibility determination [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of splats, each defined by position, radius, color, and depth information [S1, unofficial].
- A depth buffer (`g_depth`) containing the surface depth of the scene geometry [S1, unofficial].
- Viewport dimensions [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `g_normal`: A `vec4` texture (location 0) storing the accumulated weighted normal vector in RGB and unused alpha [S1, unofficial].
- `g_color`: A `vec4` texture (location 1) storing the accumulated weighted color in RGB and the accumulated weight (opacity) in the Alpha channel [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- Fragment shader inputs (`in`):
  - `gl_FragCoord`: Current fragment coordinates [S1, unofficial].
  - `gl_PointCoord`: Coordinates within the point primitive (0.0 to 1.0) [S1, unofficial].
  - `i.radius`: Radius of the splat in world space [S1, unofficial].
  - `i.color`: Color of the splat [S1, unofficial].
  - `linearDepth`: Linear depth of the splat center in view space [S1, unofficial].
- Uniforms/Constants:
  - `viewport_size`: Width and height of the viewport [S1, unofficial].
  - `epsilon`: Small constant for depth falloff, typically 0.25 [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Screen-Space Offset:** Compute the vector from the center of the point in screen space.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{offset} = \text{gl\_PointCoord} - [0.5, 0.5] $$
2.  **Check Boundary:** Calculate the squared magnitude of the offset. If $|\mathbf{p}_{offset}|^2 > 0.25$, discard the fragment [S1, unofficial].
    $$ m^2 = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{offset} $$
3.  **Convert to World-Space Offset:** Scale the screen-space offset by the splat's radius to get the world-space offset vector in the X-Y plane.
    $$ \mathbf{w}_{offset} = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \times 2 \times \text{i.radius} $$
4.  **Calculate Depth Offset:** Compute the Z-offset from the center of the sphere to the fragment's position on the sphere surface.
    $$ d_{offset} = \sqrt{\text{i.radius}^2 - (\mathbf{w}_{offset} \cdot \mathbf{w}_{offset})} $$
5.  **Calculate Fragment Depth:** Determine the fragment's depth in view space by subtracting the depth offset from the splat center's linear depth.
    $$ d_{frag} = \text{linearDepth} - d_{offset} $$
6.  **Sample Surface Depth:** Sample the depth buffer at the fragment's screen coordinates to get the depth of the geometry in front of the splat.
    $$ d_{surf} = \text{linearDepth}(\text{texture}(g_{depth}, \frac{\text{gl\_FragCoord.xy}}{\text{viewport\_size}}).x) $$
7.  **Visibility Test:** If the fragment is behind the surface ($d_{frag} > d_{surf}$), discard it [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Calculate Radial Weight:** Compute the falloff weight based on the distance from the center.
    $$ w_1 = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{m^2}}{0.5} $$
9.  **Calculate Depth-Proximity Weight:** Compute the weight based on how close the fragment is to the visible surface.
    $$ w_2 = \frac{d_{surf} - d_{frag}}{\epsilon} $$
10. **Combine Weights:**
    $$ w = w_1 \times w_2 $$
11. **Compute Normal:** Calculate the view-space normal vector from the world-space offset and depth offset.
    $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{normalize}([\mathbf{w}_{offset}, d_{offset}]) $$
12. **Accumulate Attributes:** Add the weighted attributes to the G-Buffer textures using additive blending (or explicit accumulation in shader) [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ g_{normal}.xyz += w \times \mathbf{n} $$
    $$ g_{color}.rgb += w \times \text{i.color} $$
    $$ g_{color}.a += w $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Fragment Shader Logic
def splat_attributes_pass(gl_FragCoord, gl_PointCoord, i_radius, i_color, linear_depth, viewport_size, epsilon, g_depth):
    # 1. Screen-space offset
    from_center_pt_spc = gl_PointCoord - vec2(0.5, 0.5)
    
    # 2. Boundary check
    mag_sq = dot(from_center_pt_spc, from_center_pt_spc)
    if mag_sq > 0.25:
        discard()
        
    # 3. World-space offset (X-Y plane)
    from_center_wld_spc = from_center_pt_spc * 2.0 * i_radius
    
    # 4. Depth offset (Z component)
    depth_offset = sqrt(i_radius * i_radius - dot(from_center_wld_spc, from_center_wld_spc))
    
    # 5. Fragment depth in view space
    depth = linear_depth - depth_offset
    
    # 6. Sample surface depth
    tex_coords = gl_FragCoord.xy / viewport_size
    surface_depth = linear_depth(texture(g_depth, tex_coords).x)
    
    # 7. Visibility test
    if depth > surface_depth:
        discard()
        
    # 8. Radial weight
    w1 = 1.0 - (sqrt(mag_sq) / 0.5)
    
    # 9. Depth-proximity weight
    w2 = (surface_depth - depth) / epsilon
    
    # 10. Combined weight
    weight = w1 * w2
    
    # 11. Normal calculation
    normal = normalize(vec3(from_center_wld_spc, depth_offset))
    
    # 12. Accumulate
    # Note: In a real implementation, this requires atomicAdd or render-to-texture with additive blending
    g_normal.xyz += weight * normal
    g_color.rgb += weight * i_color
    g_color.a += weight
```

## Subtopics

### Front-to-Back Blending Setup
Configures the GPU rasterization state to correctly accumulate splat attributes in the G-Buffer. It clears the color buffer to RGBA(0,0,0,0) to ensure zero initial alpha, disables depth testing, and enables blending with separate factors: RGB uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`, and Alpha uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`, with `GL_FUNC_ADD` for both [S2, unofficial]. It typically draws instanced quads using `GL_TRIANGLE_FAN` with 4 vertices per instance, passing splat count as instances, with vertex attributes for position (2 floats) and index (1 unsigned int, instanced divisor 1) [S2, unofficial]. Uniforms passed include projection, view, focal (fx, fy), viewport (width, height), and the splat texture sampler [S2, unofficial].

### sRGB to Linear Composite Pass
A full-screen triangle pass that reads an intermediate sRGB-blended splat accumulation texture, converts it to linear space, and outputs the result for premultiplied alpha blending onto the final SceneColor buffer [S3, unofficial]. It takes parameters including a `Texture2D` (IntermediateTexture) and a `SamplerState` (IntermediateSampler) [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of splats and $K$ is the number of fragments covered by each splat's footprint. The per-fragment cost is constant.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H)$ for the G-Buffer textures (`g_normal`, `g_color`) and depth buffer, where $W, H$ are viewport dimensions [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Relies on hardware-accelerated rasterization and blending, enabling real-time interactivity [S1, unofficial]. Performance is heavily dependent on fill rate and the number of overlapping splats per pixel.
- **Strengths:** Handles transparency and occlusion correctly via depth testing; allows for complex attribute accumulation (normals, colors) in a single pass; leverages GPU parallelism [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires multiple passes if sorting is needed for correct alpha blending (see [Radix Sort for Splat Depth](1207-radix-sort-for-splat-depth.md)); precision issues may arise with very small epsilon values or large depth ranges [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When volumetric data needs to be rendered as a G-Buffer for deferred lighting or complex compositing, and splat overlap is significant [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When simple opaque rendering is sufficient, or when memory bandwidth is a severe bottleneck and G-Buffer storage is prohibitive.

## Background
The Splat Attributes Pass is part of the broader family of splatting-based volume rendering techniques, which treat volumetric data as a set of geometric primitives (splats) rasterized onto the screen [S1, S3, S4, unofficial]. The specific implementation details cited here are derived from modern GPU-based point cloud and Gaussian splatting rendering pipelines [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The method builds on the fundamental principle of transforming primitives from volume space to screen space and rasterizing them with hardware acceleration [S1, S3, S4, unofficial].
