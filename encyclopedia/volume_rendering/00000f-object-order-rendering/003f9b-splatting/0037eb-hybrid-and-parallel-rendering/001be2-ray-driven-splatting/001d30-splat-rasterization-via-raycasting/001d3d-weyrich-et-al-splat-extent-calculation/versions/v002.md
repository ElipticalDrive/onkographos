# Weyrich et al. Splat Extent Calculation

Also called: Projective bounding rectangle, Splat bounding box computation, Tangent plane method, Tight Axis-Aligned Bounding Rectangle for EWA Splatting

## Overview
This method computes a tight axis-aligned bounding rectangle (AABB) for a projected Ellipsoid-Wise Adaptive (EWA) splat in screen space, along with its depth range, without using matrix inversions or conic centralization [S1]. It solves the problem of numerical instability and excessive overestimation found in previous bounding box algorithms, particularly near object silhouettes or for near-degenerate splats [S1]. Unlike conic-based approaches that suffer from severe instabilities or square approximations that overestimate area by a factor of four, this tangent plane method remains stable and tight [S1]. It yields results in normalized device coordinates (NDC), meaning the bounding box cannot be clipped against the view frustum in screen space [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method treats the splat as a degenerate ellipsoid in local coordinates and exploits the property that normal vectors transform via the inverse transpose of a mapping to find tangent planes in screen space [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
*   Splat center in camera space: $\mathbf{c}$ (3D vector).
*   Splat orientation vectors in camera space: $\mathbf{u}_0, \mathbf{v}_0$ (3D vectors).
*   Projective transformation matrix: $P$ (4x4 matrix).
*   Splat scaling/rotation matrix in camera space: $S$ (4x4 matrix, constructed from $\mathbf{u}_0, \mathbf{v}_0, \mathbf{c}$).

**Outputs:**
*   Screen-space bounding box: $(x_{min}, y_{min}, x_{max}, y_{max})$ in NDC.
*   Depth range: $(z_{min}, z_{max})$ in NDC.

**Data Structures:**
*   Homogeneous plane representation: $\mathbf{h} = (a, b, c, d)^T$ representing the plane equation $ax+by+cz+d=0$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Construct the Splat Matrix $S$**:
    The splat in clip space is defined by mapping the unit sphere under the projective mapping $P$ with matrix $S$. The matrix $S$ is constructed from the splat's center $\mathbf{c}$ and orientation vectors $\mathbf{u}_0, \mathbf{v}_0$.
    $$ S = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{u}' & \mathbf{v}' & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{c}' \end{pmatrix} $$
    Where $\mathbf{u}', \mathbf{v}', \mathbf{c}'$ are the homogeneous representations of the vectors. As an optimization, the third column (corresponding to the degenerate dimension $w_0$ reduced to zero length) is removed or ignored [S1].

2.  **Define Clip-Space Tangent Planes**:
    Identify the planes in clip space that are tangent to the splat's extent along the screen axes. For the x and y extents, define planes $\mathbf{h}_x$ and $\mathbf{h}_y$:
    $$ \mathbf{h}_x = (-1, 0, 0, x)^T $$
    $$ \mathbf{h}_y = (0, -1, 0, y)^T $$
    Here, $x$ and $y$ are the unknown clip-space coordinates of the tangent planes. Similarly, for depth, define $\mathbf{h}_z = (0, 0, -1, z)^T$ [S1].

3.  **Map Planes to Local Splat Coordinates**:
    Transform the clip-space planes $\mathbf{h}_c$ (for $c \in \{x, y, z\}$) into the local splat coordinate system $(u_0, v_0, w_0)$. This is done by multiplying by the inverse transpose of the combined transformation $(PS)$. Let $\bar{\mathbf{h}}_c$ be the transformed plane:
    $$ \bar{\mathbf{h}}_c = (PS)^T \mathbf{h}_c $$
    This step avoids explicit inversion of $S$ or $P$, ensuring stability for near-degenerate cases [S1][S2, unofficial].

4.  **Solve for Extents**:
    The transformed planes $\bar{\mathbf{h}}_x$ and $\mathbf{h}_y$ must be tangent to the unit sphere in the local splat coordinate system. A plane $\mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{x} + d = 0$ is tangent to the unit sphere if the distance from the origin to the plane is 1.
    For $\bar{\mathbf{h}}_x = (a_x, b_x, c_x, d_x)^T$, the condition is:
    $$ \frac{|d_x|}{\sqrt{a_x^2 + b_x^2 + c_x^2}} = 1 \implies d_x^2 = a_x^2 + b_x^2 + c_x^2 $$
    Since $d_x$ depends linearly on the unknown $x$ (from $\mathbf{h}_x$), this results in a quadratic equation in $x$. Solving this quadratic yields two values for $x$, corresponding to $x_{min}$ and $x_{max}$.
    Repeat the same process for $\mathbf{h}_y$ to find $y_{min}$ and $y_{max}$, and for $\mathbf{h}_z$ to find $z_{min}$ and $z_{max}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def compute_splat_extent(c, u0, v0, P):
    """
    Computes the tight AABB and depth range for a splat.
    
    Args:
        c: Camera-space center (3,)
        u0: Camera-space u-vector (3,)
        v0: Camera-space v-vector (3,)
        P: 4x4 Projective matrix
        
    Returns:
        (x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max, z_min, z_max) in NDC
    """
    # 1. Construct S matrix (4x4)
    # S columns: u', v', 0, c'
    # u', v', c' are homogeneous (w=0 for vectors, w=1 for point)
    u_h = np.append(u0, 0.0)
    v_h = np.append(v0, 0.0)
    c_h = np.append(c, 1.0)
    
    # S = [u_h, v_h, [0,0,0,0]^T, c_h]
    # Note: The third column is degenerate (zero vector in local space)
    S = np.column_stack([u_h, v_h, np.zeros(4), c_h])
    
    # Combined transformation M = P @ S
    M = P @ S
    
    # 2. Helper to solve quadratic for extent
    def solve_extent(plane_normal_idx, plane_offset_idx):
        """
        Solves for the clip-space coordinate 'val' such that the plane
        h = (..., val, ...) is tangent to the unit sphere in local space.
        plane_normal_idx: index in h where the variable is the constant term? 
        No, h = (nx, ny, nz, d). 
        For x-extent: h_x = (-1, 0, 0, x). Variable is d.
        For y-extent: h_y = (0, -1, 0, y). Variable is d.
        For z-extent: h_z = (0, 0, -1, z). Variable is d.
        """
        # Define the base plane vector h_base where the variable part is 0
        # and the fixed part is set.
        # h_x: (-1, 0, 0, 0) + x * (0,0,0,1)
        # h_y: (0, -1, 0, 0) + y * (0,0,0,1)
        # h_z: (0, 0, -1, 0) + z * (0,0,0,1)
        
        # We need to map h = h_base + var * h_var to local space
        # h_local = M^T @ h
        
        # Let h = h_base + k * h_var
        # h_local = M^T @ h_base + k * (M^T @ h_var)
        # Let A = M^T @ h_base, B = M^T @ h_var
        # h_local = A + k * B = (A_x + k*B_x, A_y + k*B_y, A_z + k*B_z, A_w + k*B_w)
        
        # Tangency condition: distance from origin to plane is 1
        # |A_w + k*B_w| / sqrt((A_x+k*B_x)^2 + (A_y+k*B_y)^2 + (A_z+k*B_z)^2) = 1
        # (A_w + k*B_w)^2 = (A_x+k*B_x)^2 + (A_y+k*B_y)^2 + (A_z+k*B_z)^2
        
        # This is a quadratic equation: a*k^2 + b*k + c = 0
        
        if plane_normal_idx == 0: # X extent
            h_base = np.array([-1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0])
            h_var = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
        elif plane_normal_idx == 1: # Y extent
            h_base = np.array([0.0, -1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
            h_var = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
        else: # Z extent
            h_base = np.array([0.0, 0.0, -1.0, 0.0])
            h_var = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
            
        Mt = M.T
        A = Mt @ h_base
        B = Mt @ h_var
        
        # Coefficients for k^2, k, constant
        # LHS: (Aw + k*Bw)^2 = Aw^2 + 2*Aw*Bw*k + Bw^2*k^2
        # RHS: sum((Ai + k*Bi)^2) = sum(Ai^2) + 2*k*sum(Ai*Bi) + k^2*sum(Bi^2)
        
        Aw, Bw = A[3], B[3]
        Ax, Ay, Az = A[0], A[1], A[2]
        Bx, By, Bz = B[0], B[1], B[2]
        
        # a*k^2 + b*k + c = 0
        # k^2: Bw^2 - (Bx^2 + By^2 + Bz^2)
        a = Bw**2 - (Bx**2 + By**2 + Bz**2)
        
        # k: 2*Aw*Bw - 2*(Ax*Bx + Ay*By + Az*Bz)
        b = 2 * (Aw * Bw - (Ax * Bx + Ay * By + Az * Bz))
        
        # const: Aw^2 - (Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Az^2)
        c = Aw**2 - (Ax**2 + Ay**2 + Az**2)
        
        # Solve quadratic
        if abs(a) < 1e-9:
            # Linear case, should not happen for valid splats but handle gracefully
            if abs(b) < 1e-9:
                return 0.0, 0.0
            k = -c / b
            return k, k
            
        discriminant = b**2 - 4*a*c
        if discriminant < 0:
            # No real solution, splat is degenerate or behind camera in a way that breaks projection
            # Return a small default or handle error
            return 0.0, 0.0
            
        sqrt_d = math.sqrt(discriminant)
        k1 = (-b - sqrt_d) / (2 * a)
        k2 = (-b + sqrt_d) / (2 * a)
        
        return min(k1, k2), max(k1, k2)

    x_min, x_max = solve_extent(0, 0)
    y_min, y_max = solve_extent(1, 0)
    z_min, z_max = solve_extent(2, 0)
    
    return (x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max, z_min, z_max)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Parabola/Hyperbola Projection:** If the projected ellipse becomes a parabola or hyperbola (e.g., splat intersects the view plane), the method fails because the quadratic equation may not yield a valid bounding box for an open curve [S2, unofficial].
*   **View Frustum Clipping:** The output is in NDC. The bounding box cannot be clipped against the view frustum in screen space because the frustum is not axis-aligned in NDC [S2, unofficial]. Implementations often pre-filter splats by checking if their bounding sphere intersects the view frustum in camera space [S2, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Splats:** The method is designed to remain stable for near-degenerate splats (where the splat is very thin or flat) by avoiding matrix inversion [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat. It involves a fixed number of matrix-vector multiplications and solving two quadratic equations.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory.
*   **Performance:** Significantly faster than conic-based approaches due to the avoidance of matrix inversions and conic centralization [S1]. It provides tighter bounds than square approximations, reducing overdraw and rasterization cost [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Numerical stability for near-degenerate splats; no matrix inversion required; tight bounds.
*   **Limitations:** Fails if the projection results in a parabola or hyperbola; bounding box is in NDC and cannot be frustum-clipped in screen space [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering EWA splats on GPUs where setup cost is critical and numerical stability is required for thin/degenerate primitives.
*   **When not to use:** If the splats are likely to intersect the view plane causing parabolic/hyperbolic projections, or if frustum clipping in screen space is required.

## Background
The method was introduced by Weyrich et al. in their work on hardware architecture for surface splatting [S1]. It was adapted from a general technique for raycasting quadric surfaces noted by Sigg et al., which uses the inverse transpose of the mapping to find tangent planes to a unit sphere [S2, unofficial]. Previous methods included centralized conic representations [Zwicker et al. 2004] and conservative square approximations [Botsch et al. 2004], which suffered from overestimation or instability [S1].
