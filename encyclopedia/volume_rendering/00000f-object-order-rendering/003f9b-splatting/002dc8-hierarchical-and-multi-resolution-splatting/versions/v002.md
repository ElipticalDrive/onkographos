# Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting

Also called: Multi-Resolution Splatting, Hierarchical Splatting, LOD Splatting

## Overview
Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting is a family of techniques that optimize volume rendering performance by adapting the resolution of splatted primitives to the viewing distance, screen-space footprint, or user-defined precision thresholds. It solves the computational bottleneck of rendering large datasets or dense point clouds by replacing many small primitives with fewer, larger ones or by skipping low-detail levels of a precomputed pyramid. It sets itself apart from standard splatting by introducing spatial or hierarchical structure to manage detail levels, and from [Splatting Without the Blur](1157-splatting-without-the-blur.md) by explicitly managing resolution trade-offs rather than just kernel shape.

## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of decomposing the rendering workload into multiple levels of detail (LOD) or spatial clusters, where the selection of which primitives to render is determined by a metric of visual importance or error.

1.  **Hierarchical or Clustered Representation:** The input data (voxels, points, or Gaussians) is organized into a structure that allows for aggregation. Common structures include:
    *   **Pyramids:** A sequence of volumes where level $k$ is derived from level $k-1$ by averaging $2 \times 2 \times 2$ blocks [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   **Octrees:** Spatial partitioning where nodes contain clusters of primitives. A node can be represented by a single aggregated primitive if it meets a quality threshold [S1, S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Sparse Voxel Lists (SVL):** Contiguous buffers generated by iteratively downsampling occupancy masks to extract simplified geometry for coarser levels [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Error or Importance Metric:** A criterion determines when a group of primitives can be merged or when a coarser level is sufficient.
    *   **Precision Threshold:** For voxel pyramids, a user-supplied threshold determines if the error between the aggregated cell and the underlying samples is acceptable [S1, unofficial].
    *   **RMS Error:** For point clouds, the Root Mean Square error of the cluster is compared against a user-defined $\epsilon$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Screen-Space Footprint:** For 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), the projected size of the Gaussian or cluster on the screen determines the LOD [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Adaptive Selection:** During rendering, the system traverses the hierarchy to select the finest level of detail that satisfies the error metric or fits within the screen-space constraint.
    *   **Back-to-Front Scanning:** In voxel-based methods, levels are scanned in back-to-front order, with the resolution level selected based on desired quality [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Culling:** Occlusion culling is often combined with LOD selection. Visibility maps (e.g., quadtrees) track occluded regions to skip processing of hidden nodes [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Primitive Aggregation:** When a node or cell is selected for rendering, it is represented by a single primitive.
    *   **Voxels:** The cell is approximated as an RGBA Gouraud-shaded polygon with size matching the projected volume cell [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Points:** A cluster of points is replaced by a single larger splat with averaged properties [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Gaussians:** A cluster of Gaussians is merged into a single "super-Gaussian" or the system switches to a coarser cluster node [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md)
Hierarchical splatting is a progressive refinement algorithm for volume rendering that uses a pyramidal volume representation to achieve real-time interactive rendering. It solves the problem of rendering large datasets efficiently by allowing the user to start with a low-resolution approximation and progressively refine the image as needed. It sets itself apart from standard splatting by using a multi-resolution pyramid structure to adapt the rendering resolution to the desired image quality, and from [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md) by explicitly constructing a hierarchical pyramid of averaged volume cells rather than just selecting points based on density or distance.

### [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md)
Multi-Resolution Points is a technique to render one large point in place of many small points to reduce rasterization cost and storage. It addresses the high computational cost of rendering large point clouds by adaptively merging nearby points into larger splats based on a local error metric. Unlike [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md) which often relies on fixed hierarchical levels, this method explicitly controls point size via a user-defined error threshold to balance visual fidelity and performance.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods](5439-3d-gaussian-splatting-lod-methods.md)
3D Gaussian Splatting Level of Detail (LOD) methods address the scalability and memory constraints of rendering large-scale scenes with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by reducing the number of primitives or their attribute precision based on spatial distance, resolution, or redundancy. Unlike standard 3DGS which renders all Gaussians uniformly, these methods employ hierarchical structures, spatial clustering, or attribute compression to achieve significant size reductions (up to 99% at far distances) while maintaining interactive frame rates. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md) by specifically targeting the parametric nature of Gaussians (position, scale, opacity, spherical harmonics) rather than just voxel density, and from [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md) by utilizing learned Gaussian representations rather than raw point clouds.

### [Splat List (SVL) Generation with Multi-Level Downsampling](5767-splat-list-svl-generation-with-multi-level-downsampling.md)
Splat List (SVL) Generation with Multi-Level Downsampling is a method for constructing a hierarchical representation of volumetric data by generating a sparse voxel list (SVL) of splat primitives across multiple levels of detail (LOD). It solves the problem of efficiently rendering large volumetric datasets by iteratively downsampling a voxel occupancy mask to extract simplified splat geometry for coarser levels, thereby reducing the number of primitives sent to the GPU for distant or less detailed views. It differs from standard hierarchical splatting by explicitly managing a contiguous SVL buffer populated through a multi-level downsampling pass rather than relying solely on a pre-built tree structure for traversal.

### RBF Splatting with Octree and Fragment Programs
- [S1, unofficial] A splatting implementation using Rational Basis Functions (RBFs) located by an octree, evaluated via fragment programs on cache-sheets.
- [S1, unofficial] Add an octree to locate the Rational Basis Functions (RBFs) intersecting the cache-sheets. For each sheet plane pixel, use a fragment program to evaluate the exponential Gaussian function of the RBFs.
- [S1, unofficial] Performance: 7 to 70 fps for Blunt-fin rendering at 400^2 resolution, caused by large variation of RBFs. Hardware: 2.8 GHz Pentium-IV, nVidia GeForce FX 5900.

### Hierarchical Splat Culling
- [S2, unofficial] Splats are culled prior to sorting and rendering using a custom octree to optimize performance.

### TreeSplat
- [S3, unofficial] A method that organizes deformation as a mergeable tree, where each node represents a rigid part of an articulated object, and the overall deformation is computed by traversing the kinematic chain.

### Depth-Aware Multi-Layer Splatting
- [S4] A splatting strategy that converts rasterized surface points into Gaussian splats and uses a multi-layer accumulation strategy based on depth to handle occlusion boundaries and disocclusions, producing smooth derivatives.
- [S4] Render multiple layers of G-buffers. Maintain three accumulation buffers during splatting: $S^+$ for splats occluding the target pixel, $S^-$ for occluded splats, and $S^o$ for splats at the same depth as the target pixel.
- [S4] To determine splat depth relation, pair up multi-layer surface intersections at splat center $p$ with closest intersections at target pixel $q$ in depth. The $p$ layer paired with the front-most $q$ layer is assigned to $S^o$, layers in front of it to $S^+$, and the rest to $S^-$.
- [S4] After all splats are rendered, buffers are separately normalized following Eq. 3 and composited in $S^-$, $S^o$, $S^+$ order using over-compositing to produce the final result $S$.
- [S4] Single-layer splatting treats all splats at the same depth, ignoring occlusions and producing spurious derivatives for occluded objects. Multi-layer splatting corrects this by distinguishing front, back, and coincident depths.

### Splat Downscaling Optimization

### Octree-based Splatting
- [S6, unofficial] Change the size of a splat based upon the cell it represents in an octree representation of the volume.
- [S6, unofficial] Cull voxels using an octree structure to accelerate rendering.

## Background
Hierarchical splatting was introduced to address the performance limitations of standard splatting when rendering large volumetric datasets. The pyramidal approach, described in early works on interactive volume rendering, allows for progressive refinement [S1, S2, unofficial]. The use of octrees for culling and multi-resolution point representation was pioneered by Laur and Hanrahan, who demonstrated that merging points based on error metrics could significantly reduce rasterization cost [S1, S6, unofficial]. More recently, with the rise of 3D Gaussian Splatting, LOD methods have been adapted to handle the parametric nature of Gaussians, focusing on memory efficiency and scalability for large-scale scenes [S2, S3, unofficial].
