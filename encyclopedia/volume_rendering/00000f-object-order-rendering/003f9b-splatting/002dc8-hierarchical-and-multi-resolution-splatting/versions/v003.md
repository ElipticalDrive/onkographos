# Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting

Also called: Multi-Resolution Splatting, LOD Splatting, Hierarchical Volume Splatting

## Overview
Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting is a family of techniques that accelerate volume rendering by decomposing the dataset or primitive set into multiple levels of detail (LOD), allowing the renderer to select the appropriate resolution based on screen-space footprint, distance, or user-defined precision thresholds. It solves the performance bottleneck of rendering large-scale volumetric data or dense point clouds by reducing the number of primitives processed per frame. It sets itself apart from standard splatting by dynamically adapting the geometric complexity of the representation, and from [Splatting Without the Blur](1157-splatting-without-the-blur.md) by explicitly managing resolution hierarchy rather than just kernel shape.

## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of constructing a multi-resolution representation where coarser levels approximate finer levels, enabling culling or substitution of primitives based on visibility and error metrics.

1.  **Multi-Resolution Construction:** The input data (voxels, points, or Gaussians) is organized into a hierarchy. Common structures include pyramids (for voxel grids) or trees (octrees/k-d trees for points/Gaussians) [S1, S2, S3, S6, unofficial].
    *   **Pyramidal Volumes:** For voxel data, level $k$ is generated by averaging $2 \times 2 \times 2$ blocks from level $k-1$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   **Tree Structures:** For points or Gaussians, space is recursively subdivided. Internal nodes aggregate child properties (mean position, color, covariance) [S1, S3, unofficial].
2.  **Error/Importance Metrics:** A metric determines whether a coarse representation is sufficient or if refinement is needed.
    *   **Voxel Error:** Variance or difference between the cell average and underlying samples [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Point/Gaussian Error:** RMS error between aggregated properties and original primitives [S1, unofficial]. For Gaussians, importance scores derived from loss gradients or projected area are used for pruning [S3, unofficial].
3.  **LOD Selection:** During rendering, the algorithm selects the finest LOD level that satisfies the error threshold or fits within the pixel grid.
    *   **Screen-Space Footprint:** Primitives with small projected areas are rendered at coarser levels or culled [S2, S3, unofficial].
    *   **Precision Threshold:** User-defined or adaptive thresholds determine the minimum cell size or maximum error [S1, S2, unofficial].
4.  **Aggregation/Pruning:**
    *   **Aggregation:** Multiple primitives are merged into a single representative primitive (e.g., a "super-Gaussian" or aggregated point) [S1, S3, unofficial].
    *   **Pruning:** Low-importance or redundant primitives are removed entirely [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Rendering:** The selected primitives are rasterized using standard splatting techniques. Hierarchical culling is often applied to skip subtrees that are fully occluded or exceed error bounds [S1, S2, S3, S6, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md)
A progressive refinement algorithm for volume rendering that uses a pyramidal volume representation to achieve real-time interactive rendering. It sets itself apart from standard splatting by using a multi-resolution pyramid structure to adapt the rendering resolution to the desired image quality, and from [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md) by explicitly constructing a hierarchical pyramid of averaged volume cells rather than just selecting points based on density or distance. Choose it when rendering large static voxel datasets where user-controlled quality/performance trade-offs are required.

### [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md)
A technique to render one large point in place of many small points to reduce rasterization cost and storage. It builds on [Hierarchical and Multi-Resolution Splatting](5618-hierarchical-and-multi-resolution-splatting.md) by organizing points into a tree and using a local error metric to decide on point size. Choose it for point cloud rendering where spatial clustering can significantly reduce draw calls without excessive visual loss.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods](5439-3d-gaussian-splatting-lod-methods.md)
3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods reduce the computational and memory burden of rendering large-scale scenes by selecting a subset of Gaussians or aggregating them into coarser representations based on spatial proximity, screen-space footprint, or redundancy metrics. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) which renders all primitives, these methods employ hierarchical structures, clustering, or pruning to achieve significant size reductions (up to 99% at far distances) while maintaining interactive frame rates. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Multi-Resolution Points](4784-multi-resolution-points.md) by operating directly on the anisotropic Gaussian primitives rather than isotropic points, and from [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md) by utilizing specific Gaussian aggregation or pruning strategies tailored to the splatting rasterizer. Choose it for large-scale NeRF/Gaussian reconstructions requiring high performance.

### [Splat List (SVL) Generation with Multi-Level Downsampling](5767-splat-list-svl-generation-with-multi-level-downsampling.md)
This method generates a Sparse Voxel List (SVL) containing splat geometry across multiple levels of detail (LOD) by iteratively downsampling a voxel occupancy mask and extracting simplified splat primitives for each level. It differs from standard hierarchical splatting by explicitly constructing a contiguous buffer of geometry items for all LOD levels simultaneously during the meshing phase, rather than traversing a tree structure at render time to select primitives. Choose it when a fixed, pre-computed LOD structure is preferred for efficient GPU buffer management.

#### RBF Splatting with Octree and Fragment Programs
- [S1, unofficial] A splatting implementation using Rational Basis Functions (RBFs) located by an octree, evaluated via fragment programs on cache-sheets.
- [S1, unofficial] Add an octree to locate the Rational Basis Functions (RBFs) intersecting the cache-sheets.
- [S1, unofficial] For each sheet plane pixel, use a fragment program to evaluate the exponential Gaussian function of the RBFs.
- [S1, unofficial] Performance: 7 to 70 fps for Blunt-fin rendering at 400^2 resolution, caused by large variation of RBFs. Hardware: 2.8 GHz Pentium-IV, nVidia GeForce FX 5900.

#### Hierarchical Splat Culling
- [S2, unofficial] Splats are culled prior to sorting and rendering using a custom octree to optimize performance.

#### TreeSplat
- [S3, unofficial] A method that organizes deformation as a mergeable tree, where each node represents a rigid part of an articulated object, and the overall deformation is computed by traversing the kinematic chain.

#### Depth-Aware Multi-Layer Splatting
- [S4] A splatting strategy that converts rasterized surface points into Gaussian splats and uses a multi-layer accumulation strategy based on depth to handle occlusion boundaries and disocclusions, producing smooth derivatives.
- [S4] Render multiple layers of G-buffers. Maintain three accumulation buffers during splatting: $S^+$ for splats occluding the target pixel, $S^-$ for occluded splats, and $S^o$ for splats at the same depth as the target pixel.
- [S4] To determine splat depth relation, pair up multi-layer surface intersections at splat center $p$ with closest intersections at target pixel $q$ in depth. The $p$ layer paired with the front-most $q$ layer is assigned to $S^o$, layers in front of it to $S^+$, and the rest to $S^-$.
- [S4] After all splats are rendered, buffers are separately normalized following Eq. 3 and composited in $S^-$, $S^o$, $S^+$ order using over-compositing to produce the final result $S$.
- [S4] Single-layer splatting treats all splats at the same depth, ignoring occlusions and producing spurious derivatives for occluded objects. Multi-layer splatting corrects this by distinguishing front, back, and coincident depths.

#### Splat Downscaling Optimization
- [S5, unofficial] Reduces Gaussian splat sizes to improve rendering speed while minimizing visual quality impact.
- [S5, unofficial] SplatDownscaleFactor: Float parameter in range [0, 1] controlling the degree of size reduction.

#### Octree-based Splatting
- [S6, unofficial] Change the size of a splat based upon the cell it represents in an octree representation of the volume.
- [S6, unofficial] Cull voxels using an octree structure to accelerate rendering.

## Background
Hierarchical splatting originated in the mid-1990s as a solution to the high computational cost of volume rendering. Laur and Hanrahan [S6, unofficial] introduced the use of octrees to cull voxels and adjust splat sizes, while Cabral et al. [S1, unofficial] explored texture mapping and pyramidal representations. The technique has evolved to support modern real-time rendering, including GPU-accelerated implementations [S1, S2, unofficial] and integration with neural rendering methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting [S3, unofficial]. It is widely used in scientific visualization, medical imaging, and increasingly in real-time graphics for large-scale scene rendering.
