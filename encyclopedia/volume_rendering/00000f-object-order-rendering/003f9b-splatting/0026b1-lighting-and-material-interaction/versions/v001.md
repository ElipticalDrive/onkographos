# Lighting and Material Interaction


## Overview
This family defines how illumination models and material properties are applied to splatted primitives, bridging the gap between raw geometric density and photorealistic appearance. It solves the problem of integrating dynamic lighting, shadowing, and surface normals into splat-based rendering, which traditionally relies on pre-computed or baked appearance. It differs from its siblings by focusing specifically on the computation of light transport (via photon mapping or analytical shading) rather than the geometric projection, sorting, or anti-aliasing mechanics of the splatting pipeline itself.

## Shared principles
The splatting framework treats the volume as a collection of smooth kernels (typically Gaussians) centered at voxel positions [S10, unofficial]. Lighting and material interaction operate on these kernels by associating photometric data with the splat's geometric center or footprint.

1.  **Data Association:** Each splat carries attributes beyond position and covariance, such as color, opacity, and optionally, surface normals or ambient occlusion values. These attributes are stored in buffers or textures (e.g., `splat_lighting`) and accessed during the rendering or compositing pass [S10, unofficial].
2.  **Shading Timing:** Shading can occur at different stages:
    *   **Pre-shading:** Lighting is computed during the training or reconstruction phase, baking the result into the splat's color attribute. This is fast to render but static [S10, unofficial].
    *   **Post-shading/Per-Splat:** Lighting is computed dynamically during rendering using the splat's world position and normal. This allows for dynamic lights and shadows but requires additional computation per splat [S10, unofficial].
3.  **Normal Estimation:** For surface-based splatting, normals are critical for lighting. They may be derived from the gradient of the density field (central differences on the current buffer) [S10, unofficial] or explicitly stored as part of the splat's attribute set (e.g., octahedral encoding) [S1, 3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
4.  **Compositing with Lighting:** The final pixel color is a result of alpha blending the shaded splats. The shading model (diffuse, specular, etc.) determines the RGB contribution of each splat before it is blended into the accumulation buffer [S7, book].

## Subtopics

### [Photon Splatting](2832-photon-splatting.md)
Photon Splatting captures direct illumination into a sparse voxel octree by rasterizing a light-view map and accumulating photon data into leaf nodes [2832-photon-splatting.md]. It solves the problem of efficiently storing incoming radiance as a direction distribution and energy proportional to the subtended solid angle of the light source. It differs from [Per-Splat Relighting](3371-per-splat-relighting.md) by focusing on the initial capture of direct light into the voxel structure rather than relighting existing splats.

*   **Inputs:** Light-view map (world position map from light perspective), Sparse Voxel Octree.
*   **Outputs:** Updated octree leaves containing photon data.
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  Render a light-view map from the light's perspective. Each pixel corresponds to a photon [2832-photon-splatting.md].
    2.  In a fragment shader, determine the leaf node in the octree corresponding to the pixel's world position [2832-photon-splatting.md].
    3.  Calculate energy $E$ proportional to the subtended solid angle $\Omega$ of the pixel as seen from the light: $E \propto \Omega$ [2832-photon-splatting.md].
    4.  Accumulate the photon's direction and energy into the leaf node using atomic additions on 16-bit normalized textures [2832-photon-splatting.md].
    5.  Transfer values to neighboring bricks to handle voxel repetition at boundaries, avoiding thread collisions [2832-photon-splatting.md].
*   **Parameters:** Light-view map resolution is typically higher than the lowest level of the voxel grid. Texture format is 16-bit normalized [2832-photon-splatting.md].
*   **Pitfalls:** Atomic adds are restricted to integer textures, requiring denormalization. Direct copying to adjacent bricks causes thread collisions and bandwidth issues [2832-photon-splatting.md].

### [Per-Splat Relighting](3371-per-splat-relighting.md)
Per-Splat Relighting computes a dynamic lighting multiplier for individual Gaussian splats based on scene lights, surface normals, ambient occlusion, and confidence values [3371-per-splat-relighting.md]. It differs from pre-baked lighting by allowing real-time changes to light positions or intensities without retraining or resplatting the geometry, and differs from Photon Splatting by computing direct illumination analytically per-splat rather than storing photon densities.

*   **Inputs:** `splat_lighting` (packed RGBA: RG=octahedral normal, B=AO, A=confidence), `light_rig` (flat array of light params), `world_position`, `ambient`, `gain`, `unlit_level` [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  Unpack the octahedral normal from `splat_lighting`. Normalize it; default to $(0,0,1)$ if length is zero [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
    2.  Iterate over each light in `light_rig`.
    3.  Calculate attenuation based on light type (Omni or Spot) [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
        *   Omni: $attenuation = \max(1.0 - (d \cdot inv\_range)^4, 0)^2 \cdot d^{-decay}$ [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
        *   Spot: Apply cone cosine and rim falloff [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
    4.  Compute direct lighting contribution: $direct += tint \cdot attenuation \cdot \max(dot(normal, to\_light), 0)$ [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
    5.  Compute final irradiance: $irradiance = ambient \cdot ao + direct \cdot confidence$ [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
    6.  Return result: $result = unlit\_level + gain \cdot irradiance$ [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].
*   **Data Structures:** `light_rig` stores 4 floats per light: direction/position, tint/type, setup params, spot direction [3371-per-splat-relighting.md].

### Splat Painting
Splat Painting allows for the manual recolorization or shadow addition on splat models, addressing the need for artistic control or correction of baked lighting.

*   **Definition:** Recolorization or shadow additions are performed by checking if a splat center intersects with a spherical brush and adjusting its color value [S1].
*   **Parameters:** Color channels are stored in 16-bit format to support brushes with smooth falloff and low opacity [S1].
*   **Pitfalls:** Splat models often do not lend themselves well to painting detailed features (text, lines, symbols) because they represent sparse geometry. Models reconstructed from images use few splats on clean surfaces, preventing detailed color changes. Color leaks may occur into differently colored parts of a surface if the reconstruction lacks clean, opaque surfaces [S1].

## Background
The concept of lighting splatted primitives evolved alongside 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md]. Early 3DGS implementations relied on pre-shaded colors learned during optimization. As the field matured, methods like Per-Splat Relighting [3371-per-splat-relighting.md] and Photon Splatting [2832-photon-splatting.md] were developed to enable dynamic lighting and physically-based interactions without retraining. Painting tools like Splatshop [S1] emerged to address the limitations of editing sparse, pre-shaded geometry.
