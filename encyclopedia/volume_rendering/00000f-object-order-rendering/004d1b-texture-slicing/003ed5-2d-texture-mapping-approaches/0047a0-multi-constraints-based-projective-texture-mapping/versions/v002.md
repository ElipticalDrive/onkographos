# Multi-constraints-based projective texture mapping

Also called: 2.5D texture engine, Continuous space mapping for volume texturing, Multi-Image-Based Texture Mapping, Multi-constraint-based forward projection, Projective Texture Mapping for Volume Animation, Three-part texture mapping

## Overview
Multi-constraints-based projective texture mapping is a method for placing textures on iso-surfaces of volume datasets by projecting the surface onto a 2D intermediate template using a plenoptic surface, warping a texture image to align with user-defined key points, and rendering via ray-casting [S1]. It solves the problem of ill-conditioned direct projection of 2D textures to 3D voxels by using spatial and semantic constraints (transfer functions) to control texture position [S1]. Unlike traditional mesh-based texture mapping, it does not require filling topological holes; regions with artefacts receive the same texture as their radial neighborhood [S1]. It differs from [2D Texture-Based Volume Rendering](916-2d-texture-based-volume-rendering.md) by synthesizing/warping detail to match local data and surface geometry using key points, rather than reconstructing existing detail [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in three passes: texture warping (preparation), inverse mapping to a plenoptic surface, and inverse mapping to the warped texture for sampling [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   Volume dataset $V$.
    *   Iso-surface $S$ identified from $V$ [S1].
    *   Source texture image $I_{src}$ [S1].
    *   Bounding shape $P$ (sphere, cylinder, or box) enclosing the volume [S1].
    *   Key points on the intermediate template $T$ and corresponding points on $I_{src}$ [S1].
    *   Spatial transfer functions for multi-texture constraints [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs**:
    *   Warped texture image $I_{warped}$ [S1].
    *   Rendered image with texture applied to $S$ [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Intermediate Template $T$**: A 2D rectangular image generated by projecting $S$ onto a plane via the chosen projection (spherical, cylindrical, or cubical). Resolution is chosen for user interaction convenience [S1].
*   **Warped Texture $I_{warped}$**: A 2D image of the same resolution as $T$, resulting from warping $I_{src}$ to align key points [S1].
*   **Plenoptic Surface $P$**: An implicit or explicit representation of the bounding shape (e.g., sphere equation, cylinder axis/radius, box planes) [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Identify Surface**: Identify the iso-surface $S$ to be textured [S1].
2.  **Choose Projection**: Select the projection type (spherical, cylindrical, or cubical) based on the bounding shape $P$ [S1]. The center of projection is located within the volume data [S1].
3.  **Generate Template**: Project $S$ onto a 2D intermediate template $T$ using the chosen projection. The volume is projected outwards onto the interior of $P$, and $P$ is unwound to a plane [S1].
4.  **Identify Key Points**: Manually identify key points on $T$ and corresponding points on the source texture image $I_{src}$ [S1].
5.  **Warp Texture**: Warp $I_{src}$ to produce $I_{warped}$ such that the corresponding key points align [S1]. This step uses constrained interpolation to synthesize detail [S1].
6.  **Render (Ray-Casting)**: For each pixel in the output image:
    a.  Cast a ray through the pixel to intersect the iso-surface $S$ at point $s$ [S1].
    b.  Construct a direction vector from the center of the texture projection through $s$ [S1].
    c.  Follow this direction to determine the intersection point $p$ on the plenoptic surface $P$ [S1]. This yields continuous coordinates on $P$ [S1].
    d.  Map the continuous coordinates of $p$ onto the warped texture image $I_{warped}$ to retrieve a color [S1].
    e.  Interpolate the discrete pixels of $I_{warped}$ around the mapped coordinates to estimate the color at $s$ [S1].
    f.  Apply lighting calculations to the estimated color [S1].

### Multi-Texture Constraints
To apply multiple textures to one surface, regions on the template $T$ are identified for each texture using polygons or image mattes [S1]. Spatial transfer functions control the position of texture mapping, ensuring color information from a specific morphed texture image is only assigned to specific voxels/positions [S2, unofficial]. For example, the top half of a head can be textured with one spherical image and the bottom half with another [S2, unofficial]. The surface of the earth can be textured with a spherical image while the core uses a cylindrical image [S2, unofficial].

### Interpolation
The method uses a fast, data-dependent triangulation method to retain edge quality during texture sampling [S1]. This preserves edge details by keeping edges sharp and smooth areas smooth, superior to standard bilinear interpolation [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def multi_constraints_projective_texture_mapping(volume, iso_surface, texture_image, bounding_shape, key_points_template, key_points_texture):
    """
    volume: Volume dataset V
    iso_surface: Iso-surface S
    texture_image: Source texture image I_src
    bounding_shape: Bounding shape P (sphere, cylinder, box)
    key_points_template: List of (x, y) coordinates on template T
    key_points_texture: List of (x, y) coordinates on texture_image
    """
    
    # Step 1-3: Generate Intermediate Template
    # Project iso_surface S onto 2D template T using bounding_shape P
    # The center of projection is within the volume, projecting outwards onto P
    # P is unwound to a plane to form T
    template_T = project_surface_to_template(iso_surface, bounding_shape)
    
    # Step 4-5: Warp Texture
    # Warp texture_image to align key_points_texture with key_points_template
    warped_texture = warp_texture(texture_image, key_points_template, key_points_texture)
    
    # Step 6: Render
    output_image = empty_image(output_width, output_height)
    
    for y in range(output_height):
        for x in range(output_width):
            # 6a: Ray-cast to iso-surface
            ray = cast_ray_through_pixel(x, y)
            intersection_s = intersect_ray_with_surface(ray, iso_surface)
            
            if intersection_s is None:
                continue
                
            # 6b: Map to Plenoptic Surface
            # Direction from center of projection through intersection_s
            center = get_projection_center(bounding_shape)
            direction = normalize(intersection_s - center)
            
            # Find intersection with bounding shape P
            intersection_p = intersect_ray_with_shape(direction, center, bounding_shape)
            
            # 6c: Map to Warped Texture
            # Convert continuous coordinates on P to 2D coordinates on warped_texture
            # This depends on the unwinding method of P to T
            texture_coords = map_plenoptic_to_texture(intersection_p, bounding_shape, template_T)
            
            # 6d: Sample Texture with Data-Dependent Interpolation
            color = sample_warped_texture_with_edge_preservation(warped_texture, texture_coords)
            
            # 6e: Lighting
            final_color = apply_lighting(color, intersection_s, normal_at(intersection_s))
            
            output_image[y, x] = final_color
            
    return output_image

def sample_warped_texture_with_edge_preservation(texture, coords):
    """
    Uses fast, data-dependent triangulation to retain edge quality.
    """
    # Implementation detail: Data-dependent triangulation interpolation
    # Preserves edges better than bilinear interpolation
    return triangulation_interpolate(texture, coords)
```

### Parameters
*   **Projection Shape $P$**: A conveniently regular shape enclosing the volume, such as a sphere, cylinder, or box, chosen heuristically to match the object's general shape [S1].
*   **Template Resolution**: Chosen to be large enough on screen for user interaction in the key-point identification step [S1].

### Pitfalls
*   **Texture Stretching**: When the underlying surface differs greatly in slope from the plenoptic surface, a single texel may stretch across a significant area, causing a soft, smeared effect. The key-point warping helps mitigate this by ensuring sufficient texels cover the area [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths**:
    *   Does not degrade visual effects if topological artefacts exist; regions with artefacts receive the same texture as their radial neighborhood [S1].
    *   Allows volume objects to be animated while maintaining texture alignment via key points [S1].
    *   Can synthesize detail to match local data and surface geometry [S1].
    *   Supports multiple textures on one surface using spatial transfer functions [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**:
    *   Requires manual identification of key points [S1].
    *   Texture stretching can occur if surface slope differs greatly from the plenoptic surface, though warping mitigates this [S1].
*   **When to use**: When texturing volume characters or objects with topological artefacts, or when animation requires texture alignment [S1]. Suitable for film special effects [S1].
*   **When not to use**: When automatic texture placement without user interaction is required [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Wang and Mueller [S1]. It builds on Winter's method for projective texture mapping [Winter 2002] and uses volume rendering methods such as DSR [Jones 1999], DVR [Kajiya and Hersen 1984], and Winter's method [Winter 2002] for implementation [S1]. It differs from edge-reconstruction methods like Froumentin et al. [Froumentin et al. 2000] and Su and Willis [Su and Willis 2004] by synthesizing detail rather than reconstructing existing detail [S1]. It also differs from mesh-based methods that require filling holes [Wood et al. 2004, Guskov and Wood 2001] [S1].
