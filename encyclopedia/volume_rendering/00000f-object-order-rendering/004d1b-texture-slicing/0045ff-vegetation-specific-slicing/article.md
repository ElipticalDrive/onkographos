# Vegetation-Specific Slicing
## Overview
Vegetation-Specific Slicing is a family of volume rendering techniques tailored for the efficient visualization of plant structures, such as forests and individual trees, by decomposing the geometry into planar slices or fiber surfaces. It solves the problem of high geometric complexity in vegetation rendering by replacing detailed polygonal meshes with image-based representations (slices or textured surfaces) that exploit hardware compositing and texture mapping. It is distinguished from general texture slicing by its specific handling of vegetation topology (fibers in tetrahedral meshes) and large-scale environmental constraints (ground-aligned texcells with non-linear pre-filtering and grazing angle corrections).
## Shared principles
The Vegetation-Specific Slicing family shares the following mechanism, terms, and mathematical foundations:

1.  **Vegetation Decomposition:** The volume or geometry is decomposed based on structural characteristics. For forests, this involves ground-aligned planar slices (texcells) [S1, child]. For individual trees, it involves separating solid parts (trunk/limbs) from sparse parts (twigs/leaves), where sparse parts are represented as parallel planar layers [S1, child].
2.  **Image-Based Representation:** Sparse or volumetric vegetation parts are represented using textures rather than explicit geometry. This includes 1D textures for fiber paths [S1, child] and 2D alpha-premultiplied textures for slice layers [S1, child].
3.  **Hardware Compositing:** The slices or textured polygons are composited using standard alpha blending equations, often requiring back-to-front rendering order to ensure correct transparency accumulation [S1, child].
4.  **Texture Coordinate Mapping:** Specific parameterization strategies are used to map 3D structural information to 2D or 1D texture coordinates. This includes mapping global fiber path length to 1D textures [S1, child] and using MIP-mapped 2D textures for LOD [S1, child].
## Subtopics

### [Fiber Surface Texturing](5672-fiber-surface-texturing.md)
Fiber Surface Texturing is a method for visualizing the internal structure of fiber surfaces (specifically Fiber Surface Cross-Section Polygons, or FSCP) within tetrahedral meshes by mapping a 1D texture pattern onto the surface geometry. It solves the problem of displaying continuous fiber paths and their topological changes across a discretized surface without requiring complex per-vertex shading logic. It is distinguished from standard surface texturing by its specific parameterization strategy, which maps the global fiber path length onto a single texture coordinate, allowing a "dotted line" texture to reveal fiber presence and continuity. See [Fiber Surface Texturing](5672-fiber-surface-texturing.md).

### [Regular Texcell Slicing with Non-Linear Pre-filtering](9823-regular-texcell-slicing-with-non-linear-pre-filtering.md)
Regular Texcell Slicing is a volume rendering technique that represents volumetric data (specifically forest scenes) as a stack of parallel, ground-aligned planar slices, enabling efficient hardware compositing and non-linear pre-filtering. It solves the problem of rendering large-scale volumetric textures with high performance and correct anti-aliasing by constructing a MIP-map pyramid of 2D textures, where coarser levels are generated by blending finer levels using the volume rendering transparency equation. Unlike view-aligned or silhouette-specific methods, regular texcells maintain slices parallel to the ground plane, which avoids CPU-side polygon generation for the bulk of the scene but requires specific opacity adjustments at grazing angles to prevent sampling artifacts. See [Regular Texcell Slicing with Non-Linear Pre-filtering](9823-regular-texcell-slicing-with-non-linear-pre-filtering.md).

### Slicing and Blending for Vegetation Rendering
A simplified image-based rendering approach for vegetation that uses alpha-blended textured polygons to represent sparse parts (twigs and leaves) as a set of parallel planar layers called slices, while using ordinary mesh-based rendering for solid parts (trunk and limbs) [S1]. It renders dozens of detailed trees in real-time with off-the-shelf hardware, providing significantly improved image quality over existing real-time techniques [S1]. The method renders solid parts using ordinary mesh-based rendering and represents sparse parts with a set of slices, where each slice is a planar layer represented with an ordinary alpha or color-keyed texture [S1]. Rendering from an arbitrary viewpoint in a 360-degree circle around the tree center is achieved by blending between the nearest two slicings [S1]. In implementation, only 6 slicings with 5 slices each are sufficient to visualize a tree for a moving or stationary observer with perceptually similar quality to the original model [S1].

## Background
The techniques within this family address the challenges of interactive 3D rendering of vegetation, which is computationally expensive due to large geometric complexity even for simple plants [S1]. The foundational work on "Slicing and Blending for Vegetation Rendering" was introduced to provide a simplified image-based rendering approach based on the limitations of human perception of complex geometry [S1]. This allows for real-time rendering of dozens of detailed trees using standard hardware [S1]. The specific methods for fiber surface texturing and regular texcell slicing extend these principles to handle specific data structures (tetrahedral meshes for fibers) and large-scale environments (forests with LOD requirements) [S1, child].
