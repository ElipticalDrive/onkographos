# Near Plane Clipping Correction

Also called: Camera Inside Volume Handling, Inside-Volume Volume Rendering via Backface Raycasting, Near Clipping Plane Hole Filling

## Overview
Near Plane Clipping Correction is a technique for rendering volumetric data when the camera viewpoint is located inside the volume bounds, a scenario where standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume, creating holes in the ray entry data [S1, S2, unofficial]. It extends the image-based ray setup by intersecting rays with explicit polygonal geometry to correctly determine entry points and clip internal surfaces, allowing for hybrid rendering of isosurfaces and direct volume rendering [S1, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera position in world coordinates, volume bounding box (defined by axes and sizes), RayData texture (precomputed ray entry points and marching distances), near clipping plane geometry [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Corrected ray origins and entry distances for pixels where the camera is inside the volume or the near plane clips the front faces [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Box Matrix ($M_{vol}$):** A $4 \times 4$ matrix defining the local coordinate system of the volume [S1, unofficial]. Columns 0-2: `vec4(axis_direction * length, 0)` for x, y, and z axes respectively [S1, unofficial]. Column 3: `vec4(tx, ty, tz, 1)` representing the box position [S1, unofficial].
*   **Inverse Volume Box Matrix ($M_{inv}$):** The inverse of $M_{vol}$, used to transform world coordinates to the local unit cube $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **RayData Texture:** A texture storing ray entry positions and depths [S2, unofficial]. Cleared to zero before rendering [S2, unofficial].
*   **AABB Structure:** Defines the unit bounding box with `min` at `(0,0,0)` and `max` at `(1,1,1)` [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Coordinate System Transformation:** Construct $M_{vol}$ using the volume box axes and their lengths [S1, unofficial]. Compute $M_{inv} = M_{vol}^{-1}$ [S1, unofficial]. Transform the camera position from world coordinates to the local unit cube coordinate system: $\mathbf{p}_{local} = M_{inv} \cdot [\mathbf{p}_{cam}, 1]^T$ [S1, unofficial]. The resulting $\mathbf{p}_{local}$ is passed to the shader as `LOCAL_CAM_POS` [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Marking Clipped Regions (Back-face Rasterization):** Clear the RayData texture to zero [S2, unofficial]. Render only the back faces of the volume bounding box (vertices at $(0,0,0)$ to $(1,1,1)$ in the local system) [S1, unofficial]. Pass the interpolated local unit texture coordinates (`vLocalUnitTexCoord`) to the shader [S1, unofficial]. In the fragment shader, write a negative color value into the green channel of the RayData texture for these pixels [S2, unofficial]. This marks pixels where back faces were rendered but front faces were clipped by the near plane [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Rasterization Sequence for Depth Correction:** [S3, unofficial] describes a multi-pass rasterization approach to fill holes using depth buffer information. Step 3.1: Disable depth buffer, enable color buffer. Rasterize the entire near clipping plane [S3, unofficial]. Step 3.2: Enable depth buffer, disable color buffer. Rasterize the nearest back faces of active bricks [S3, unofficial]. Step 3.3: Enable depth buffer, enable color buffer. Rasterize the nearest front faces of active bricks [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Shader Correction for Marked Pixels:** Identify pixels where the green channel is negative (indicating clipped front faces) [S2, unofficial]. For these pixels, explicitly initialize the ray entry position to the texture-space position of the corresponding point on the near plane [S2, unofficial]. Correct the depth value read from the RayData texture by subtracting the distance from the eye to the corresponding point on the near plane [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Ray Setup and Marching Start Position:** Define the ray origin as `LOCAL_CAM_POS` [S1, unofficial]. Define the ray direction as the normalized vector from the camera to the back-face vertex coordinate [S1, unofficial]. Calculate the entry distance $t$ to the unit AABB $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$ using the slab method [S1, unofficial]. Determine the start position for the raymarching loop by mixing between the camera position and the front-face intersection point based on the sign of $t$ [S1, unofficial]. If $t > 0$ (camera outside), use the front face intersection; if $t \le 0$ (camera inside), use the camera position [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   $\mathbf{r}_{dir} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{v}_{LocalUnitTexCoord} - \mathbf{LOCAL\_CAM\_POS})$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{r}_{InvDir} = 1.0 / \mathbf{r}_{dir}$ (with zero-component handling) [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{t}_{Minima} = (\mathbf{b}_{min} - \mathbf{r}_{origin}) \cdot \mathbf{r}_{InvDir}$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{t}_{Maxima} = (\mathbf{b}_{max} - \mathbf{r}_{origin}) \cdot \mathbf{r}_{InvDir}$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{t}_{Entries} = \min(\mathbf{t}_{Minima}, \mathbf{t}_{Maxima})$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $t_{MaxEntry} = \max(\max(\mathbf{t}_{Entries}.xy), \max(\mathbf{t}_{Entries}.yz))$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{p}_{front} = \mathbf{r}_{origin} + \mathbf{r}_{dir} \cdot t$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{useFrontCoord} = 0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \text{sign}(t)$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\mathbf{startPos} = \text{mix}(\mathbf{LOCAL\_CAM\_POS}, \mathbf{p}_{front}, \mathbf{useFrontCoord})$ [S1, unofficial]
Where $\mathbf{r}_{origin}$ is `LOCAL_CAM_POS`, $\mathbf{v}_{LocalUnitTexCoord}$ is the interpolated back-face coordinate, $\mathbf{b}_{min}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{max}$ are the unit AABB bounds, and $\text{sign}(t)$ returns $1$ for $t>0$, $0$ for $t=0$, and $-1$ for $t<0$.

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   **Volume Box:** Defined by axes and lengths. The local coordinate system maps to $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **RayData Texture:** Cleared to zero before rendering [S2, unofficial].
*   **Green Channel Marker:** Negative value used to indicate clipped front faces [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Inside Volume:** Standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces, creating holes in the ray entry data [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The slab method for AABB intersection must handle cases where ray direction components are zero or near-zero to avoid division by zero or precision issues [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the camera is exactly on the boundary of the volume, the sign of $t$ determines the behavior. The `mix` function with `sign(t)` handles this by preferring the camera position if $t \le 0$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

def get_unit_aabb_entry(origin, direction):
    # Handle zero direction components to avoid division by zero
    inv_dir = [1.0 / d if d != 0.0 else 1e30 for d in direction]
    b_min = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    b_max = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0]
    t_minima = [(b_min[i] - origin[i]) * inv_dir[i] for i in range(3)]
    t_maxima = [(b_max[i] - origin[i]) * inv_dir[i] for i in range(3)]
    t_entries = [min(t_minima[i], t_maxima[i]) for i in range(3)]
    # Find first real entry value: max of minimums
    t_max_entry_candidates = [max(t_entries[0], t_entries[1]), max(t_entries[1], t_entries[2])]
    t_max_entry = max(t_max_entry_candidates)
    return t_max_entry

def get_closer_pos(camera, front_face_intersection, t):

## Subtopics

### [Viewpoint Inside Volume Ray Setup](8369-viewpoint-inside-volume-ray-setup.md)
This method enables volume ray casting when the camera viewpoint is located inside the volume (e.g., virtual colonoscopy), a scenario where standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume, creating holes in the ray entry data. It extends the image-based ray setup by intersecting rays with explicit polygonal geometry to correctly determine entry points and clip internal surfaces, allowing for hybrid rendering of isosurfaces and direct volume rendering.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The correction adds a constant number of rasterization passes (back faces, near plane, front faces) and a simple shader calculation per pixel [S3, unofficial]. The complexity is dominated by the raymarching loop, which is unchanged [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires the RayData texture to store entry points and depths [S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Scales with the resolution of the RayData texture and the number of samples in the raymarching loop.
*   **Strengths:** Correctly handles camera positions inside the volume and near-plane clipping artifacts, enabling immersive viewing experiences like virtual colonoscopy [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires explicit rasterization of the volume bounding box and near clipping plane [S2, S3, unofficial]. May introduce artifacts if the near plane is very close to the camera or if the volume geometry is complex [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When the camera viewpoint is inside the volume or when the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the camera is always outside the volume and the near plane does not intersect the front faces [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The technique is described in the context of GPU-based volume raycasting, particularly for handling fluid volumes and immersive viewpoints [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **History and Development:** The method is detailed in "Real-Time Simulation and Rendering of 3D Fluids" (GPU Gems 3) and "Advanced Illumination Techniques for GPU-Based Volume Raycasting" [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Where it is Used:** Virtual colonoscopy, immersive fluid visualization, and any application requiring camera movement inside volumetric data [S1, S3, unofficial].
