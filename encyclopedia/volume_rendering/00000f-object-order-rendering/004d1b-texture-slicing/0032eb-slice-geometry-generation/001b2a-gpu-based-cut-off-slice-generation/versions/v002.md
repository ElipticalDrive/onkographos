# GPU-based Cut-off Slice Generation

Also called: Rezk-Salama method, Vertex Shader Slice Generation, View-Aligned Slicing (Proxy Geometry Generation), Viewport-Aligned Slices Intersection Algorithm

## Overview
GPU-based Cut-off Slice Generation is an object-order volume rendering technique that computes proxy geometry (polygons) representing the intersection of slicing planes with the volume's bounding box directly on the GPU [S1, unofficial][S2, book][S4]. It solves the performance bottleneck of CPU-based slice generation by offloading the calculation of intersection points, polygon sorting, and tessellation to the vertex shader [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Plane Slicing](845-plane-slicing.md) which may compute geometry on the CPU, this method ensures real-time interactivity by balancing workload between vertex and fragment shaders, specifically addressing the constraint that vertex shaders cannot insert or remove vertices [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D texture containing volumetric data, modelview matrix, camera parameters (view direction, viewport), number of slices, voxel size, and sampling rate [S1, unofficial][S2, book][S4].
*   **Outputs:** A sequence of triangle strips or fans representing the proxy polygons, rasterized and composited into the frame buffer [S2, book][S4].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Bounding Box Vertices:** 8 vertices defining the volume extent in object space [S1, unofficial][S2, book].
*   **Edge Flags:** A set of 6 boolean flags per edge, corresponding to the 6 faces of the bounding box (Top, Bottom, Left, Right, Front, Back) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Vertex List:** A temporary array of maximum size 6 to store intersection points for a single slice plane [S2, book][S4].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Bind the volumetric data as a 3D texture [S1, unofficial]. Setup rendering parameters: camera position, view direction, viewport, number of slices, and rendering mode [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Transform Bounding Box:** Transform the 8 vertices of the volume bounding box from object space to view coordinates using the modelview matrix [S2, book][S4].
3.  **Determine Slicing Planes:** Find the minimum ($z_{min}$) and maximum ($z_{max}$) z-coordinates of the transformed vertices [S2, book][S4]. Compute the number of sampling planes and their positions using equidistant spacing from the view origin. The sampling distance is derived from the voxel size and current sampling rate [S2, book][S4].
4.  **Iterate Slices:** For each plane in front-to-back or back-to-front order:
    a. **Compute Intersections:** Test for intersections between the slicing plane and the 12 edges of the bounding box.
    b. **Validate Intersections:** Calculate the intersection parameter $\lambda$. If $\lambda \in [0, 1]$, the point is valid. If the denominator is zero (edge parallel to plane normal), mark as invalid ($-1.0$) [S1, unofficial].
    c. **Eliminate Invalid Points:** Apply a bounding sphere test to discard points outside the bounding sphere. Merge double points coinciding with corner vertices [S3, unofficial].
    d. **Sort Vertices:** Compute the center of the proxy polygon by averaging the valid intersection points. Sort vertices clockwise or counter-clockwise by projecting onto the x-y plane and computing a "pseudo-angle" (tangent of angle combined with sign of coordinates) to avoid trigonometry [S2, book][S4].
    e. **Tessellate:** Tessellate the sorted polygon into triangles (using a triangle strip or fan from the center) [S2, book][S4].
    f. **Output:** Add vertices to the output vertex array. Transform back to object space if required by the rendering pipeline [S2, book][S4].
5.  **Render:** Rasterize the proxy geometry. The fragment shader samples the 3D texture and applies a transfer function (2D texture lookup) to map scalar values to RGBA [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Composite:** Blend the fragments using hardware alpha blending [S1, S3, unofficial].
7.  **Update:** Respond to interaction changes (e.g., rotation, translation) by repeating steps 2–6 [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**

*   **Edge Representation:**
    $$ \vec{x} = \vec{V}_i + \lambda \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} $$
    where $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ and $\vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} = \vec{V}_j - \vec{V}_i$ [S1, unofficial].

*   **Clipping Plane (Hessian Normal Form):**
    $$ \overrightarrow{n}_p \cdot \overrightarrow{x} = d $$
    where $\overrightarrow{n}_p$ is the plane normal (viewing direction for view-aligned slices) and $d$ is the distance to the origin [S1, unofficial].

*   **Intersection Parameter $\lambda$:**
    $$ \lambda = \begin{cases} \frac{d - \vec{n}_p \cdot \vec{V}_i}{\vec{n}_p \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j}}, & \vec{n}_p \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} \neq 0 \\ -1.0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

*   **Texture Space Coordinate Translation:**
    $$ PI \cdot \vec{k} = \begin{cases} \frac{V_i \vec{k} - min(B_{\vec{k}})}{max(B_{\vec{k}}) - min(B_{\vec{k}})} & \vec{k} \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} = 0 \\ \lambda & \vec{k} \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} > 0 \\ 1 - \lambda & \vec{k} \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} < 0 \end{cases} $$
    where $\vec{k}$ is the x, y, or z unit vector in texture space, and $B_{\vec{k}}$ is the bounding box length in direction $\vec{k}$ [S1, unofficial].

*   **Polygon Validity Check (Edge Flags):**
    Compute bitwise OR of edge flags for adjacent points. If result is nonzero, a common face exists, and the sequence is valid [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def generate_slices(volume_texture, modelview_matrix, num_slices, voxel_size, sampling_rate):
    # Step 1: Transform bounding box vertices to view space
    view_vertices = transform_vertices(volume_texture.bounding_box.vertices, modelview_matrix)
    
    # Step 2: Determine slice planes
    z_min = min(v.z for v in view_vertices)
    z_max = max(v.z for v in view_vertices)
    sampling_distance = voxel_size * sampling_rate
    num_planes = int((z_max - z_min) / sampling_distance)
    
    output_vertices = []
    
    for i in range(num_planes):
        z_plane = z_min + i * sampling_distance
        
        # Step 3: Compute intersections with bounding box edges
        intersections = []
        for edge in volume_texture.bounding_box.edges:
            # Edge: V_i -> V_j
            V_i = view_vertices[edge.start]
            V_j = view_vertices[edge.end]
            e_vec = V_j - V_i
            
            # Plane normal is view direction (z-axis in view space)
            n_p = vec3(0, 0, 1)
            d = z_plane
            
            denom = dot(n_p, e_vec)
            if denom == 0:
                continue # Edge parallel to plane
            
            lambda_val = (d - dot(n_p, V_i)) / denom
            
            if 0 <= lambda_val <= 1:
                intersection_point = V_i + lambda_val * e_vec
                intersections.append({
                    'point': intersection_point,
                    'lambda': lambda_val,
                    'edge_flags': edge.flags,
                    'edge_type': edge.type # Red, Green, Blue, Yellow, Cyan, Purple
                })
        
        # Step 4: Sort and Tessellate
        if len(intersections) < 3:
            continue # Not enough points for a polygon
            
        # Compute center
        center = sum(p['point'] for p in intersections) / len(intersections)
        
        # Sort using pseudo-angle
        intersections.sort(key=lambda p: pseudo_angle(p['point'] - center))
        
        # Handle duplicate vertices for < 6 vertex polygons (Rezk-Salama specific logic)
        # If polygon has fewer than 6 vertices, duplicate existing vertices to fill 6 slots
        # to satisfy vertex shader constraints [S1, unofficial]
        final_vertices = handle_duplicate_vertices(intersections)
        
        # Tessellate into triangles (fan from center)
        triangles = tessellate_polygon(final_vertices, center)
        
        # Step 5: Transform back to object space if needed
        output_vertices.extend(transform_vertices(triangles, inverse(modelview_matrix)))
        
    return output_vertices

def pseudo_angle(point):
    # Tangent of angle combined with sign of coordinates [S2, book][S4]
    x, y = point.x, point.y
    if x == 0 and y == 0:
        return 0
    # Simplified pseudo-angle calculation
    if x >= 0:
        if y >= 0:
            return y / (x + y) # Quadrant 1
        else:
            return 2.0 - x / (x - y) # Quadrant 2
    else:
        if y >= 0:
            return 4.0 + y / (-x + y) # Quadrant 3
        else:
            return 6.0 - x / (-x - y) # Quadrant 4
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Vertex Shader Constraints:** Vertex shaders cannot insert or remove vertices. The algorithm must output exactly 6 vertices for every intersection polygon, even if the geometric polygon has fewer vertices. Duplicate vertices must be generated to fill the slots [S1, unofficial].
*   **Invalid Intersections:** If $\lambda \notin [0, 1]$, the intersection is not on the bounding box edge and must be discarded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parallel Edges:** If the denominator in the $\lambda$ calculation is zero, the edge is parallel to the clipping plane. This case is handled by marking $\lambda = -1.0$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Texture Coordinates:** If the edge is perpendicular to the texture axis ($\vec{k} \cdot \vec{e}_{i \rightarrow j} = 0$), the texture coordinate is 0 or 1. If the edge direction is opposite to the texture axis, use $1 - \lambda$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Incremental Intersection and Texture Coordinates](997-incremental-intersection-and-texture-coordinates.md)
A method to compute intersection points and texture coordinates for axis-aligned boxes against parallel slicing planes incrementally, avoiding the bottleneck of recomputing intersections for every sub-volume and plane. It sets itself apart from naive per-plane intersection by leveraging the uniform spacing and parallel nature of slicing planes to update positions via constant deltas, maintaining an `ActiveIntersectionList` to track active edge intersections.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The intersection calculation is $O(1)$ per slice plane (constant number of edges and vertices). Sorting is $O(1)$ (max 6 points). Total time is linear with the number of slices [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the 3D texture and temporary vertex lists of size 6 per slice [S1, unofficial][S2, book].
*   **Performance:** Offloading slice generation to the GPU significantly improves performance compared to CPU-based methods, enabling real-time interactivity (response time < 100ms) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance, interactive rotation and slicing, efficient use of GPU parallelism [S1, unofficial][S2, book].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to view-aligned slicing (or requires modelview matrix modification for other axes). Cannot rotate more than 180 degrees in one arc-ball drag operation [S1, unofficial]. Requires careful handling of duplicate vertices due to vertex shader constraints [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was proposed by Rezk-Salama et al. [50] to move the calculation of cut-off slices from the CPU to the GPU using vertex shaders [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the work of Moret and Shapiro (1991) for pseudo-angle sorting [S2, book][S4]. The approach is described in detail in [S1, unofficial] (thesis) and [S2, book][S4] (GPU Gems). It is a variant of [View-Aligned Slicing with 3D Textures](6687-view-aligned-slicing-with-3d-textures.md).
