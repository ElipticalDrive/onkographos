# Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting

Also called: SA-GS

## Overview
Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting (SA-GS) is a training-free, test-time plugin for 3D Gaussian Splatting that improves anti-aliasing by applying 2D scale-adaptive filters to each Gaussian [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of scale mismatch between training and testing frequencies, where observing Gaussians at different resolutions or camera distances causes jagged artifacts [S2, unofficial]. Unlike Mip-Splatting, which modifies the training procedure and uses filters unaware of testing frequency, SA-GS operates at test time and adapts filters based on the specific rendering resolution and camera distance [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting field (Gaussians with position, covariance, opacity, and SH coefficients); camera parameters (extrinsics, intrinsics, focal length); rendering resolution ($W \times H$).
*   **Outputs:** Anti-aliased 2D image.

**Data Structures:**
The method uses the standard 3DGS Gaussian representation. For each Gaussian $i$, the projected 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'$ is computed in screen space. The method modifies the filtering kernel applied during rasterization.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Testing Frequency:** Determine the effective sampling frequency based on the camera distance ($d$) and rendering resolution ($W, H$). The dilation scale (filter width) is adjusted according to the ratio of Gaussian scales before and after temporal changes or spatial scaling [S1].
2.  **Apply 2D Scale-Adaptive Filter:** For each Gaussian, compute a 2D low-pass filter kernel. The filter width is scaled adaptively to match the Gaussian's projected scale relative to the pixel size. This ensures the Gaussian primitive distribution remains consistent across different testing frequencies [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Integrate Projected Gaussian:** During rasterization, integrate the projected 2D Gaussian within each pixel. This integration acts as a limiting case of super-sampling, handling sampling rates smaller than the scene frequency [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Composite:** Accumulate the filtered contributions of all Gaussians sorted by depth.

**Formulas:**
Let $\Sigma'$ be the projected 2D covariance matrix of a Gaussian. The standard 3DGS rasterization uses a fixed kernel. SA-GS modifies the kernel width $\sigma_{filter}$ based on the testing frequency.
The dilation scale is adapted using the ratio of Gaussian scales [S1].
The integration within a pixel $p$ is computed as:
$$ C_p = \sum_{i \in \text{visible}} \alpha_i \prod_{j < i} (1 - \alpha_j) c_i $$
where $\alpha_i$ is the alpha value computed using the scale-adaptive filter on $\Sigma'$ [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Super-sampling and integration are only effective when the scale-adaptive filtering is activated; without it, scale inconsistency leads to jaggedness at low sampling rates [S2, unofficial].
*   Precision issues may arise if the adaptive scale ratio is extreme, though the method is designed to handle various bounded and unbounded scenes [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Alias-free 4D Gaussian Splatting](9872-alias-free-4d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Eliminates high-frequency artifacts in dynamic scenes by introducing a 4D scale-adaptive filter and scale loss. It adapts dilation scale using the ratio of Gaussian scales before and after temporal changes, unlike Mip-Splatting's fixed dilation scale.

### [Adaptive-Scale Color Offset](13019-adaptive-scale-color-offset.md)
A training-stage module for multi-scale 3DGS that corrects scale-dependent color mismatch by applying a learnable, scale-specific RGB offset to the SH DC coefficient, keeping geometry and higher-order SH coefficients unchanged.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds minimal overhead to the rendering pipeline as it is a test-time plugin. The adaptive filter computation is lightweight compared to training modifications.
*   **Memory Complexity:** No additional memory required for training data structures; operates on existing Gaussian fields.
*   **Performance:** Performs comparably with or better than Mip-Splatting across various settings and both bounded and unbounded scenes [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Training-free; works as a plugin on any pretrained Gaussian splatting field; effective anti-aliasing at low sampling rates.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful implementation of the adaptive scale ratio; relies on correct camera parameter estimation.
*   **When to use:** When anti-aliasing is needed without retraining, or when testing at resolutions/frequencies different from training.
*   **When not to use:** When training-time optimization is preferred or when computational resources for training are available and Mip-Splatting is acceptable.

## Background
SA-GS was introduced as a response to the limitations of Mip-Splatting, which requires modifying the training procedure and uses filters unaware of testing frequency [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the observation that observing Gaussians at different frequencies leads to scale mismatches [S2, unofficial]. The method leverages the fact that integrating projected Gaussians within pixels acts as a limiting case of super-sampling [S2, unofficial].
