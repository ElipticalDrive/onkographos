# 3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods

Also called: RTGS, LODGE, EAGLES, SOGS, Compact3D, Unreachable Gaussian Handling

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting Level of Detail (LOD) methods address the memory bottlenecks and rendering latency inherent in large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by reducing the number of primitives or compressing their attributes [S1, unofficial][S2]. These methods solve the problem of excessive VRAM consumption and slow rasterization in scenes containing millions of Gaussians, which standard rasterizers cannot handle efficiently [S1, S3, unofficial]. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md) is the focus on attribute-level compression, spatial partitioning, or dynamic redundancy reduction rather than purely hierarchical tree traversal for culling [S1, unofficial][S2]. Trade-offs include potential quality degradation from quantization or pruning, and the complexity of maintaining consistency between LOD levels [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
The methods in this category operate through distinct mechanisms: redundancy reduction, quantization/compression, and hierarchical memory management.

### Redundancy Reduction (RTGS)
RTGS applies a multi-level redundancy reduction strategy to remove Gaussians based on spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Input:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}$ with position $\mathbf{p}_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$.
2.  **Spatial Overlap Check:** For each Gaussian $G_i$, calculate the spatial overlap with neighbors. If $G_i$ is fully covered by a union of neighbors within a threshold, it is marked for removal [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Temporal Consistency:** In dynamic scenes, compare Gaussian attributes across frames. Gaussians with stable attributes and low contribution variance are candidates for removal [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Pruning:** Remove marked Gaussians. The remaining Gaussians are re-optimized to fill any visual gaps [S1, unofficial].

### Quantization and Compression (EAGLES, SOGS, Compact3D)
These methods compress Gaussian attributes to reduce disk size and memory bandwidth.

**EAGLES** quantizes all Gaussian attributes: color, position, opacity, rotation, and scaling [S2].
1.  **Quantization:** Map continuous attributes to discrete codebooks.
2.  **Opacity Quantization:** Specifically quantize opacity. This reduces "floaters" (spurious low-opacity Gaussians) and visual artifacts in novel view synthesis [S2].
3.  **Storage:** Store indices into codebooks.
4.  **Performance:** On MipNeRF360, achieves SSIM 0.81, PSNR 27.15, LPIPS 0.24, and size 68 MB [S2].

**SOGS (Sparse Orthographic Grid Splatting)** arranges attributes into 2D grids.
1.  **Grid Mapping:** Project 3D Gaussian attributes onto multiple 2D grids.
2.  **Sorting:** Sort grid pixels to group similar values.
3.  **Regularization:** Apply smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values significantly different from their local neighborhood on the 2D grid [S2].
4.  **Compression:** Compress the RGB grid with JPEG XL. Store other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression [S2].
5.  **Performance:** On MipNeRF360, achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, and size 28.80 MB [S2].

**Compact3D** avoids quantizing opacity and position.
1.  **Constraint:** Do not quantize or share opacity and position attributes.
2.  **Reason:** Sharing these attributes results in overlapping Gaussians, which degrades rendering quality [S2].
3.  **Performance:** On MipNeRF360, achieves SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16, LPIPS 0.228. Size is not specified in the sources [S2].

### Hierarchical Memory Management (SPTs, Unreachable Gaussian Handling)
Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) enable dynamic LOD selection [S3, unofficial].
1.  **Hierarchy Construction:** Build a tree where parent nodes summarize children.
2.  **Cut Condition:** Determine the LOD cut based on camera distance and Gaussian scale. A parent Gaussian fulfills the cut condition if it covers the screen area of its children sufficiently [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Unreachable Gaussian Handling:**
    *   **Identification:** Identify Gaussians where a child's scale is larger than its parent's, violating the SPT cut condition ($\sigma_{child} > \sigma_{parent}$) [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Action:** Leave these Gaussians in RAM but do not render or transfer them. Do not rebalance the hierarchy [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Pitfall:** Rebalancing is too costly for little benefit. Generating improper cuts to fix this derails training [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Impact:** Affects <10% of Gaussians [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md)
CityGaussian, VastGaussian, and DoGaussian are methods for rendering city-scale (square-kilometer) 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes by partitioning the environment into spatial blocks, training independent Gaussian models per block, and merging them at render time. They solve the memory and performance bottlenecks of monolithic 3DGS in large scenes by enabling level-of-detail (LoD) selection, streaming, and distributed training. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) by relying on tile-based training and merging rather than a single unified hierarchical structure, and from [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md) by focusing on spatial partitioning rather than pure compression.

### [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md)
LightGaussian is a compression method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reduces model size and improves rendering speed by combining structured pruning with Spherical Harmonic (SH) factorization and vector quantization. It achieves significant size reduction (up to 15$\times$) and high frame rates ($>200$ FPS) with minimal quality degradation ($<0.5$ dB drop). It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by focusing on attribute-level compression (SH factorization, vector quantization, and G-PCC position compression) rather than purely spatial clustering or octree-based spatial pruning.

### TRIPS
TRIPS is a point-based radiance field rendering technique that rasterizes points into a screen-space image pyramid using trilinear splats, followed by a neural reconstruction network to produce hole-free, crisp images [S2]. It solves the blurriness and cloudy artifacts of 3D Gaussian Splatting and the temporal instability and large-gap handling issues of ADOP by projecting neural color descriptors into an image pyramid using trilinear writes to two neighboring resolution layers based on projected point size, avoiding overdraw by rendering only 2x2 splats [S2].

### [Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting](8085-scale-adaptive-gaussian-splatting.md)
Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting (SA-GS) is a rendering optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates aliasing artifacts by applying an adaptive 2D low-pass filter during the splatting process. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Alias-free 4D Gaussian Splatting](9872-alias-free-4d-gaussian-splatting.md) by performing this filtering at test time based on immediate rendering parameters, rather than modifying the Gaussian geometry or training loss.

### [Multi-scale filtering](8244-multi-scale-filtering.md)
Multi-scale filtering is a family of techniques in 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates aliasing and geometric artifacts by applying low-pass filters to the Gaussian representation. It addresses rendering quality degradation when the camera zooms out (causing high-frequency aliasing) or zooms in (causing dilation artifacts due to under-sampling) relative to the training resolution. Unlike sibling methods that rely on hierarchical data structures or clustering to reduce Gaussian count, multi-scale filtering modifies the Gaussian's covariance and opacity directly to match the physical sampling limits of the camera sensor [G].

### [Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting](8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vmem) enables the rendering of large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes that exceed available GPU memory by applying virtual texturing concepts. It groups Gaussians into fixed-size pages, uses a proxy mesh for visibility culling, and streams only visible pages into a limited GPU buffer. Unlike standard 3DGS which loads all Gaussians or relies on simple distance culling, Vmem separates virtual and physical address spaces, allowing dynamic LOD selection and just-in-time memory allocation to maintain stable frame rates in memory-constrained environments.

### [Gaussian clustering and merging](8436-gaussian-clustering-and-merging.md)
Gaussian clustering and merging is a family of Level of Detail (LOD) and memory management techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that reduces the number of splats by grouping spatially or attribute-similar Gaussians into representative clusters. It solves the problem of excessive memory consumption and rendering latency in large-scale scenes by providing coarser representations for distant or less significant regions. What sets this family apart from simple culling is that it retains geometric information through aggregation (merging) rather than discarding Gaussians, allowing for smooth transitions between detail levels.

### [Iterative Gaussian Synopsis](9289-iterative-gaussian-synopsis.md)
Iterative Gaussian Synopsis is a top-down strategy for constructing a hierarchy of Level of Detail (LOD) representations for 3D Gaussian Splatting [1]. It begins with a full-fidelity model and progressively derives coarser levels through adaptive, learnable pruning of anchors and downsampling of hierarchical grids, ensuring coherent simplification without the error accumulation inherent in bottom-up training. It sets itself apart from siblings like [LapisGS](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) by deriving coarse layers from fine ones rather than building up from coarse, and from [H-3DGS](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) by using a unified optimization with lightweight codebook/MLP updates rather than independent hierarchical structures.

### [Budgeted Gaussian Splatting](9672-budgeted-gaussian-splatting.md)
Budgeted Gaussian Splatting (BCT) is a training and deployment method for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that enables variable primitive counts (Level of Detail) without retraining, using a continuous budget-quality curve to select primitives. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) and [Octree-GS](7341-octree-gs.md) by decoupling the LOD selection from the training process, allowing a single trained model to be exported to any standard rasterizer with different budgets, achieving near-lossless quality at low compression ratios.

### [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md)
Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS) is a method for large-scale 3D reconstruction that mitigates the memory bottlenecks of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by training scene chunks independently and merging them into a global Level-of-Detail (LoD) hierarchy. It sets itself apart from siblings like [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) and [Octree-GS](7341-octree-gs.md) by using a unified training and rendering pipeline with external memory management to handle ultra-large scenes, whereas alternatives often rely on post-processing, manual threshold tuning, or different spatial partitioning strategies.

### [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md)
Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) are a hierarchical data structure originally developed for point cloud rendering, adapted here for efficient, view-dependent Level-of-Detail (LoD) selection in 3D Gaussian Splatting without requiring full tree traversal. Unlike sibling methods that rely on static spatial partitioning or post-training hierarchy construction, SPTs enable dynamic LoD hierarchies that evolve continuously during optimization. They are primarily used within the Hierarchical SPT (HSPT) framework to compute the correct LoD cut efficiently for individual views and camera paths.

### FilterGS
FilterGS is a rendering acceleration method for large-scale Level-of-Detail (LoD) 3D Gaussian Splatting that eliminates inefficient serial tree traversal and reduces redundant Gaussian-tile pairs through parallel filtering and adaptive shrinking [S2]. It differs from its siblings, such as [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) and [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md), by replacing depth-dependent serial traversal with a traversal-free, parallel filtering structure that selects a single scale for each Gaussian via a learned network, avoiding expensive tree traversal at render time [S1, unofficial][S2].

### LODGE
LODGE is a level-of-detail framework for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that enables efficient rendering by dynamically selecting appropriate detail levels [S1, unofficial].

### EAGLES
EAGLES is a method that quantizes all Gaussian attributes including color, position, opacity, rotation, and scaling [S2]. Quantization of opacity leads to fewer floaters or visual artifacts in the novel view synthesis task [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, EAGLES achieves SSIM 0.81, PSNR 27.15, LPIPS 0.24, and a size of 68 MB [S2].

### SOGS (Sparse Orthographic Grid Splatting)
SOGS is a method that arranges Gaussian attributes into multiple 2D grids, sorts them, and applies a smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values very different from their local neighborhood on the 2D grid [S2]. For storage, SOGS compresses the RGB grid with JPEG XL and stores all other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, and a size of 28.80 MB [S2].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression
This compression method does not quantize opacity and position because sharing them results in overlapping Gaussians [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, Compact3D achieves SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16, LPIPS 0.228, and a size of / MB (unspecified) [S2].

### Unreachable Gaussian Handling
This strategy tolerates cases where a child Gaussian becomes larger than its parent, making it impossible for the parent to fulfill the SPT cut condition, by leaving these Gaussians in RAM but not rendering them [S3, unofficial].
1.  Identify Gaussians where the child's scale is larger than the parent's, violating the SPT cut condition [S3, unofficial].
2.  Do not rebalance the hierarchy. Leave these Gaussians in RAM, but never render or transfer them [S3, unofficial].
Rebalancing the hierarchy during training was experimented with but found to be too costly for little benefit. Generating improper cuts to fix this is considered worse as it derails training [S3, unofficial]. This inefficiency affects less than 10% of Gaussians in practice [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory:** Quantization methods (EAGLES, SOGS) significantly reduce disk and memory footprint (e.g., SOGS to 28.80 MB on MipNeRF360) [S2]. Hierarchical methods (H-3DGS, SPTs) reduce VRAM usage by streaming or culling [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** RTGS and pruning methods (LightGaussian) increase rendering FPS (e.g., >200 FPS) by reducing primitive count [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Quality:** Quantization may introduce artifacts if not carefully tuned (e.g., opacity quantization in EAGLES reduces floaters) [S2]. Compact3D avoids position/opacity quantization to prevent overlapping artifacts [S2].
*   **Complexity:** Hierarchical methods require complex tree management and culling logic [S3, unofficial]. Quantization requires codebook training or optimization [S2].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) was introduced by Kerbl et al. [5] as a real-time rendering technique. As applications expanded to large-scale scenes (cityscapes, autonomous driving), memory and performance bottlenecks emerged [S1, unofficial][S2]. Methods like RTGS [84], LightGaussian [83], and LODGE [144] were developed to address these issues through redundancy reduction, compression, and hierarchical LOD [S1, unofficial]. SPTs and related hierarchical structures were adapted from point cloud rendering to handle ultra-large scale reconstruction with external memory [S3, unofficial].
