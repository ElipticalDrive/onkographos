# 3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods

Also called: RTGS

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting Level of Detail (LOD) methods address the memory and performance bottlenecks of monolithic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) in large-scale scenes by reducing redundancy and enabling efficient rendering [S1, S3, unofficial]. These methods solve the problem of excessive VRAM consumption and rendering latency by applying multi-level redundancy reduction, spatial partitioning, or attribute compression [S1, S3, unofficial]. What sets them apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical Splatting](1168-hierarchical-splatting.md) is the specific focus on Gaussian attribute optimization (pruning, quantization, clustering) rather than purely spatial tree traversal or point-based rasterization [S2][S3, unofficial]. Trade-offs include potential quality degradation from aggressive pruning or quantization, and the complexity of managing hierarchical structures or external memory [S1, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The core mechanism involves identifying redundant or less significant Gaussians and removing or compressing them based on spatial overlap, temporal consistency, or importance scores [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:** A trained set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$, where each Gaussian $g_i$ has position $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$ (or scale/rotation), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color $c_i$.
**Outputs:** A reduced or compressed set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ suitable for efficient rendering.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian List:** An array of Gaussian attributes.
*   **Hierarchy (Optional):** For methods like [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) or [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md), a tree structure where nodes represent merged Gaussians summarizing radiance and opacity of children [S1, unofficial].
*   **Grids (Optional):** For methods like [SOGS](8085-scale-adaptive-gaussian-splatting.md), 2D grids storing sorted attributes [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Importance Scoring:** Calculate an importance score for each Gaussian based on its contribution to the rendered image or its spatial density [S2].
2.  **Reduction/Pruning:** Remove Gaussians with low importance scores or high spatial overlap [S1, unofficial].
    *   *RTGS:* Applies a multi-level redundancy reduction strategy removing Gaussians based on spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial].
    *   *LightGaussian:* Prunes Gaussians with smaller importance scores and applies structured pruning to Spherical Harmonic (SH) parameters [S1, S3, unofficial].
3.  **Quantization/Compression:**
    *   *EAGLES:* Quantizes all attributes (color, position, opacity, rotation, scaling) [S2].
    *   *Compact3D:* Does not quantize opacity and position to avoid overlapping Gaussians [S2].
    *   *SOGS:* Arranges attributes into 2D grids, sorts them, and applies smoothness regularization [S2].
4.  **Hierarchy Construction (if applicable):**
    *   *H-3DGS/SPTs:* Train scene chunks independently and merge them into a global LoD hierarchy [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Iterative Gaussian Synopsis:* Derive coarser levels from fine ones through adaptive pruning [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Rendering:**
    *   *Standard:* Rasterize the reduced set of Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Hierarchical:* Select the appropriate LOD level based on camera distance or budget [S1, S3, unofficial].
    *   *FilterGS:* Use a parallel filtering structure to select a single scale for each Gaussian via a learned network, avoiding tree traversal [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Formulas:**
*   **Importance Score:** Not explicitly defined in sources, but used for pruning in LightGaussian and Mini-Splatting [S2].
*   **Smoothness Regularization (SOGS):** Penalizes pixels with values very different from their local neighborhood on the 2D grid [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Pruning Threshold:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Quantization Bits:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Grid Size (SOGS):** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Unreachable Gaussians:** In SPT-based methods, a child Gaussian may become larger than its parent, violating the cut condition [S3, unofficial]. This affects <10% of Gaussians [S3, unofficial]. The strategy is to leave them in RAM but not render them, as rebalancing is too costly [S3, unofficial].
*   **Quantization Artifacts:** Quantizing opacity in EAGLES leads to fewer floaters or visual artifacts [S2].
*   **Overlapping Gaussians:** Quantizing position and opacity in Compact3D is avoided because it results in overlapping Gaussians [S2].

## Subtopics

### [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md)
CityGaussian, VastGaussian, and DoGaussian are methods for rendering city-scale (square-kilometer) 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes by partitioning the environment into spatial blocks, training independent Gaussian models per block, and merging them at render time. They solve the memory and performance bottlenecks of monolithic 3DGS in large scenes by enabling level-of-detail (LoD) selection, streaming, and distributed training. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) by relying on tile-based training and merging rather than a single unified hierarchical structure, and from [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md) by focusing on spatial partitioning rather than pure compression.

### [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md)
LightGaussian is a compression method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reduces model size and improves rendering speed by combining structured pruning with Spherical Harmonic (SH) factorization and vector quantization. It achieves significant size reduction (up to 15$\times$) and high frame rates ($>200$ FPS) with minimal quality degradation ($<0.5$ dB drop). It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by focusing on attribute-level compression (SH factorization, vector quantization, and G-PCC position compression) rather than purely spatial clustering or octree-based spatial pruning.

### [TRIPS](8039-trips.md)
TRIPS is a point-based radiance field rendering technique that rasterizes points into a screen-space image pyramid using trilinear splats, followed by a neural reconstruction network to produce hole-free, crisp images. It solves the blurriness and cloudy artifacts of 3D Gaussian Splatting and the temporal instability and large-gap handling issues of ADOP by projecting neural color descriptors into an image pyramid using trilinear writes to two neighboring resolution layers based on projected point size, avoiding overdraw by rendering only 2x2 splats.

### [Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting](8085-scale-adaptive-gaussian-splatting.md)
Scale-Adaptive Gaussian Splatting (SA-GS) is a rendering optimization for 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates aliasing artifacts by applying an adaptive 2D low-pass filter during the splatting process. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Alias-free 4D Gaussian Splatting](9872-alias-free-4d-gaussian-splatting.md) by performing this filtering at test time based on immediate rendering parameters, rather than modifying the Gaussian geometry or training loss.

### [Multi-scale filtering](8244-multi-scale-filtering.md)
Multi-scale filtering is a family of techniques in 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates aliasing and geometric artifacts by applying low-pass filters to the Gaussian representation. It addresses rendering quality degradation when the camera zooms out (causing high-frequency aliasing) or zooms in (causing dilation artifacts due to under-sampling) relative to the training resolution. Unlike sibling methods that rely on hierarchical data structures or clustering to reduce Gaussian count, multi-scale filtering modifies the Gaussian's covariance and opacity directly to match the physical sampling limits of the camera sensor [G].

### [Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting](8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vmem) enables the rendering of large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes that exceed available GPU memory by applying virtual texturing concepts. It groups Gaussians into fixed-size pages, uses a proxy mesh for visibility culling, and streams only visible pages into a limited GPU buffer. Unlike standard 3DGS which loads all Gaussians or relies on simple distance culling, Vmem separates virtual and physical address spaces, allowing dynamic LOD selection and just-in-time memory allocation to maintain stable frame rates in memory-constrained environments.

### [Gaussian clustering and merging](8436-gaussian-clustering-and-merging.md)
Gaussian clustering and merging is a family of Level of Detail (LOD) and memory management techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that reduces the number of splats by grouping spatially or attribute-similar Gaussians into representative clusters. It solves the problem of excessive memory consumption and rendering latency in large-scale scenes by providing coarser representations for distant or less significant regions. What sets this family apart from simple culling is that it retains geometric information through aggregation (merging) rather than discarding Gaussians, allowing for smooth transitions between detail levels.

### [Iterative Gaussian Synopsis](9289-iterative-gaussian-synopsis.md)
Iterative Gaussian Synopsis is a top-down strategy for constructing a hierarchy of Level of Detail (LOD) representations for 3D Gaussian Splatting [1]. It begins with a full-fidelity model and progressively derives coarser levels through adaptive, learnable pruning of anchors and downsampling of hierarchical grids, ensuring coherent simplification without the error accumulation inherent in bottom-up training. It sets itself apart from siblings like [LapisGS](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) by deriving coarse layers from fine ones rather than building up from coarse, and from [H-3DGS](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) by using a unified optimization with lightweight codebook/MLP updates rather than independent hierarchical structures.

### [Budgeted Gaussian Splatting](9672-budgeted-gaussian-splatting.md)
Budgeted Gaussian Splatting (BCT) is a training and deployment method for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that enables variable primitive counts (Level of Detail) without retraining, using a continuous budget-quality curve to select primitives. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) and [Octree-GS](7341-octree-gs.md) by decoupling the LOD selection from the training process, allowing a single trained model to be exported to any standard rasterizer with different budgets, achieving near-lossless quality at low compression ratios.

### [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md)
Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS) is a method for large-scale reconstruction that mitigates the memory bottlenecks of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by training scene chunks independently and merging them into a global Level-of-Detail (LoD) hierarchy. It sets itself apart from siblings like [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) and [Octree-GS](7341-octree-gs.md) by using a unified training and rendering pipeline with external memory management to handle ultra-large scenes, whereas alternatives often rely on post-processing, manual threshold tuning, or different spatial partitioning strategies.

### [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md)
Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) are a hierarchical data structure originally developed for point cloud rendering, adapted here for efficient, view-dependent Level-of-Detail (LoD) selection in 3D Gaussian Splatting without requiring full tree traversal. Unlike sibling methods that rely on static spatial partitioning or post-training hierarchy construction, SPTs enable dynamic LoD hierarchies that evolve continuously during optimization. They are primarily used within the Hierarchical SPT (HSPT) framework to compute the correct LoD cut efficiently for individual views and camera paths.

### [FilterGS](12631-filtergs.md)
FilterGS is a rendering acceleration method for large-scale Level-of-Detail (LoD) 3D Gaussian Splatting that eliminates inefficient serial tree traversal and reduces redundant Gaussian-tile pairs through parallel filtering and adaptive shrinking. It differs from its siblings, such as [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) and [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md), by replacing depth-dependent serial traversal with a traversal-free, parallel filtering structure that selects a single scale for each Gaussian via a learned network, avoiding expensive tree traversal at render time.

### LODGE
LODGE is a level-of-detail framework for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that enables efficient rendering by dynamically selecting appropriate detail levels [S1, unofficial].

### EAGLES
EAGLES is a method that quantizes all Gaussian attributes including color, position, opacity, rotation, and scaling [S2]. Quantization of opacity leads to fewer floaters or visual artifacts in the novel view synthesis task [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, EAGLES achieves SSIM 0.81, PSNR 27.15, LPIPS 0.24, and a size of 68 MB [S2].

### SOGS (Sparse Orthographic Grid Splatting)
SOGS is a method that arranges Gaussian attributes into multiple 2D grids, sorts them, and applies a smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values very different from their local neighborhood on the 2D grid [S2]. For storage, SOGS compresses the RGB grid with JPEG XL and stores all other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, and a size of 28.80 MB [S2].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression
This compression method does not quantize opacity and position because sharing them results in overlapping Gaussians [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, Compact3D achieves SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16, LPIPS 0.228, and a size of / MB (unspecified) [S2].

### Unreachable Gaussian Handling
This strategy tolerates cases where a child Gaussian becomes larger than its parent, making it impossible for the parent to fulfill the SPT cut condition, by leaving these Gaussians in RAM but not rendering them [S3, unofficial]. Steps: Identify Gaussians where the child's scale is larger than the parent's, violating the SPT cut condition; do not rebalance the hierarchy; leave these Gaussians in RAM, but never render or transfer them [S3, unofficial]. Pitfall: Rebalancing the hierarchy during training was experimented with but found to be too costly for little benefit. Generating improper cuts to fix this is considered worse as it derails training [S3, unofficial]. This inefficiency affects less than 10% of Gaussians in practice [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Varies by method. RTGS reduces redundancy based on spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial]. LightGaussian achieves over 200 FPS with slight quality loss [S1, unofficial]. EAGLES achieves a size of 68 MB on MipNeRF360 [S2]. SOGS achieves 28.80 MB [S2].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **EAGLES:** SSIM 0.81, PSNR 27.15, LPIPS 0.24, Size 68 MB [S2].
    *   **SOGS:** SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, Size 28.80 MB [S2].
    *   **Compact3D:** SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16, LPIPS 0.228, Size unspecified [S2].
    *   **LightGaussian:** >200 FPS, slight quality loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Methods like CityGaussian and VastGaussian enable rendering of city-scale scenes [S1, S3, unofficial]. Virtual Memory enables rendering of scenes exceeding GPU memory [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduced memory footprint, faster rendering, ability to handle large-scale scenes [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Potential quality degradation from pruning/quantization, complexity of implementation, potential artifacts from quantization [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Large-scale scenes, memory-constrained environments, real-time rendering requirements [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Small scenes where standard 3DGS is sufficient, high-fidelity requirements where pruning artifacts are unacceptable [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods originated from the need to scale 3DGS to large environments [S1, S3, unofficial]. RTGS applies a multi-level redundancy reduction strategy [S1, unofficial]. LightGaussian compresses Gaussians by factorizing SH parameters and applying structured pruning [S1, unofficial]. Google's LODGE introduces a level-of-detail framework for large-scale 3DGS [S1, unofficial]. Nvidia's HiGS proposes a hierarchical rendering architecture [S1, unofficial]. FilterGS proposes a traversal-free, parallel filtering structure [S1, unofficial]. These methods are used in urban scenes, autonomous driving, and large-scale reconstruction [S1, S3, unofficial].
