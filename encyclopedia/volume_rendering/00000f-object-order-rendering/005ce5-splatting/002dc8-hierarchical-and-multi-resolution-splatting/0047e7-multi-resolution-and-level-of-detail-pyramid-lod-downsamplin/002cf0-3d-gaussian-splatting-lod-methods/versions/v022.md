# 3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods

Also called: RTGS

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods reduce the memory footprint and rendering cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by removing redundant primitives or compressing their attributes, addressing the scalability limits of standard 3D-GS which stores full-precision data for millions of Gaussians [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on hierarchical spatial structures [1168-hierarchical-splatting.md], these methods primarily focus on attribute-level compression, structured pruning, and redundancy reduction based on spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
The core mechanism involves identifying and eliminating redundant Gaussians or compressing their high-dimensional attributes (position, covariance, opacity, color) to fit within constrained memory budgets while preserving visual fidelity [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Inputs:** A trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene consisting of $N$ Gaussians, each defined by position $\mathbf{p}$, covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ (or scale/rotation), opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients for color [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Outputs:** A compressed set of Gaussians $\{G'_i\}$ with reduced attribute precision or count, and a corresponding rendering pipeline capable of interpreting the compressed data [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Data Structures:**
1. **Gaussian List:** A flat array of Gaussian attributes [G]. In compression methods like SOGS, attributes are rearranged into 2D grids [S2].

**Steps:**
1. **Redundancy Identification:** Analyze Gaussians for spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial].
2. **Pruning/Selection:** Remove marked Gaussians based on computed importance or overlap metrics [G]. Methods like RTGS apply multi-level redundancy reduction based on spatial overlap and temporal consistency [S1, unofficial]. Methods like LightGaussian use structured pruning combined with SH factorization [S1, S3, unofficial].
3. **Attribute Compression:**
   * **Quantization:** Compress color, rotation, and opacity into latent vectors decoded by a small MLP (EAGLES) or use vector quantization (LightGaussian) [S2][S1, unofficial].
   * **Grid Sorting (SOGS):** Arrange attributes into 2D grids, sort them, and apply smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values differing significantly from their local neighborhood [S2].
   * **Encoding:** Compress RGB grids using JPEG XL and store other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression (SOGS) [S2].
4. **Rendering:** Rasterize the remaining/compressed Gaussians using standard screen-space splatting [G].

**Formulas:**
* **Smoothness Regularization (SOGS):** Penalizes differences between a grid cell's attribute value and the mean of its local neighborhood in the 2D grid [S2].

**Parameters:**
* **Pruning Threshold:** Not given in the sources [G].
* **Grid Resolution (SOGS):** Not given in the sources [G].
* **Quantization Bits:** Not given in the sources [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* **Unreachable Gaussians:** In Sequential Point Trees (SPTs), a child Gaussian may become larger than its parent, violating the SPT cut condition ($\text{scale}(\text{parent}) < \text{scale}(\text{child})$) [S3, unofficial]. These Gaussians are left in RAM but not rendered [S3, unofficial]. Rebalancing the hierarchy is too costly [S3, unofficial].
* **Overlapping Gaussians:** Compact3D avoids quantizing position and opacity to prevent overlapping Gaussians and visual artifacts [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### EAGLES [7990-eagles.md]
EAGLES optimizes 3D-GS by reducing memory and training time through attribute quantization, progressive coarse-to-fine training, and aggressive pruning [S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling LOD methods by focusing on attribute-level compression and training-time acceleration rather than hierarchical spatial structures [S3, unofficial]. Choose it when training time and storage size are primary constraints [S3, unofficial].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression [8035-3d-gaussian-splatting-compression.md]
This class of methods reduces the storage footprint of 3D-GS scenes by compressing geometric and attribute data [S2]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by focusing on bitstream reduction rather than hierarchical rendering structures [G]. Choose it when disk space or network bandwidth limits scene distribution [G].

### LightGaussian [8036-lightgaussian.md]
LightGaussian reduces model size and improves rendering speed by combining structured pruning with Spherical Harmonic (SH) factorization and vector quantization [S1, S3, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by focusing on attribute-level compression rather than purely spatial clustering or octree-based spatial pruning [S2][S3, unofficial]. Choose it when high frame rates and extreme size reduction are required [S1, S3, unofficial].

### TRIPS [8039-trips.md]
TRIPS is a point-based radiance field rendering technique that rasterizes points into a screen-space image pyramid using trilinear splats, followed by a neural reconstruction network to produce hole-free, crisp images [S2]. It sets itself apart by solving the blurriness and cloudy artifacts of 3D-GS and the temporal instability of ADOP through neural reconstruction [S2]. Choose it when visual crispness and temporal stability outweigh the added neural network overhead [S2].

### Multi-scale filtering [8244-multi-scale-filtering.md]
Multi-scale filtering mitigates aliasing artifacts and optimizes rendering performance by adapting the Gaussian representation to the camera's viewing frequency or resolution [G]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D-GS by explicitly managing the spectral content of the primitives through training-time aggregation, test-time covariance regularization, or runtime hierarchical pruning [G]. Choose it when rendering at varying resolutions or when aliasing is a concern [G].

### Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting [8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md]
Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vmem) enables the rendering of large-scale scenes that exceed available GPU memory by applying virtual texturing concepts [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D-GS by separating virtual and physical address spaces, allowing dynamic LOD selection and just-in-time memory allocation [S1, unofficial]. Choose it when scene size exceeds GPU VRAM limits [S1, unofficial].

### Gaussian clustering and merging [8436-gaussian-clustering-and-merging.md]
This family of techniques reduces computational and memory cost by grouping individual splats into representative clusters or hierarchical nodes [G]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by explicitly constructing cluster structures and aggregating Gaussian parameters to form fewer, larger primitives, rather than merely filtering or paging existing data [G]. Choose it when reducing primitive count for culling or LOD generation is prioritized [G].

### Budgeted Gaussian Splatting [9672-budgeted-gaussian-splatting.md]
Budgeted Gaussian Splatting (BCT) enables continuous, variable primitive counts (Level of Detail) without retraining [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by providing a single, global primitive ordering whose prefixes define successive approximations of the scene, rather than camera-dependent hierarchical selectors or discrete hierarchy methods [S1, unofficial]. Choose it when dynamic, continuous LOD adjustment without retraining is required [S1, unofficial].

### Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS) [10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md]
Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS) is a Level-of-Detail method that organizes 3D Gaussian primitives into a hierarchical structure to enable efficient rendering of large-scale scenes by dynamically selecting appropriate detail levels based on viewing distance [S1, unofficial][S2]. It sets itself apart by training scene chunks independently and merging them into a global LoD hierarchy [S1, unofficial]. Choose it for large-scale outdoor or indoor scenes requiring distance-based LOD [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) [10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md]
Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) are a hierarchical data structure originally developed for point cloud rendering, adapted here for efficient, view-dependent Level-of-Detail selection in 3D Gaussian Splatting without requiring full tree traversal [S1, unofficial][S2]. They set themselves apart by enabling dynamic LoD hierarchies that evolve continuously during optimization [S2]. Choose them when dynamic, training-time hierarchy evolution is preferred over static partitioning [S2].

### SOGS (Sparse Orthographic Grid Splatting)
SOGS arranges Gaussian attributes into multiple 2D grids, sorts them, and applies smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values very different from their local neighborhood [S2]. It sets itself apart from vector quantization methods by using grid-based sorting and JPEG XL/OpenEXR encoding [S2]. Choose it when disk storage size is the primary constraint [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, and a size of 28.80 MB [S2].

### Unreachable Gaussian Handling
A strategy to tolerate cases where a child Gaussian becomes larger than its parent, making it impossible for the parent to fulfill the SPT cut condition [S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by accepting a small RAM inefficiency rather than performing costly rebalancing or generating improper cuts that derail training [S3, unofficial]. Choose it when using SPTs and encountering scale inversions during training [S3, unofficial]. This inefficiency affects less than 10% of Gaussians in practice [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Varies by method. Structured pruning (LightGaussian) and redundancy reduction (RTGS) add preprocessing time but reduce rendering time by decreasing primitive count [S1, unofficial]. Grid sorting (SOGS) adds compression/decompression overhead [S2].
* **Memory Complexity:** Significantly reduced [G]. LightGaussian achieves up to 15$\times$ size reduction [S1, S3, unofficial]. SOGS achieves 28.80 MB on MipNeRF360 [S2].
* **Measured Performance:** LightGaussian achieves >200 FPS with <0.5 dB quality drop [S1, S3, unofficial]. SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238 on MipNeRF360 [S2].
* **Strengths:** Reduced memory footprint, faster rendering, ability to handle large-scale scenes [G].
* **Limitations:** Potential quality loss if compression is too aggressive. Unreachable Gaussians in SPTs waste RAM [S3, unofficial]. Rebalancing is costly [S3, unofficial].
* **When to Use:** When memory or bandwidth is constrained, or for large-scale scenes [G].
* **When Not to Use:** When maximum visual fidelity is required and resources are abundant [G].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) was introduced by Kerbl et al. [S2]. LOD methods for 3D-GS have been developed to address its high memory and computational costs [G]. RTGS applies multi-level redundancy reduction [S1, unofficial]. LightGaussian combines structured pruning with SH factorization [S1, S3, unofficial]. SOGS uses grid-based compression [S2]. H-3DGS and SPTs introduce hierarchical structures for LOD [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
