# 3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods

Also called: RTGS

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods address the computational and memory bottlenecks of rendering millions of 3D Gaussian primitives by reducing redundancy, compressing attributes, or organizing primitives into hierarchical structures for view-dependent selection. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS), which renders all primitives, these methods employ strategies such as spatial overlap removal, attribute quantization, or tree-based culling to maintain visual fidelity while significantly lowering storage size and increasing frame rates [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
The methods in this family operate through distinct mechanisms: redundancy reduction, attribute compression, or hierarchical spatial partitioning.

### Redundancy Reduction (RTGS)
RTGS reduces the number of Gaussians by identifying and removing redundant primitives based on spatial and temporal criteria [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Input:** A set of optimized 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$ and a sequence of training images.
2.  **Spatial Overlap Analysis:** For each Gaussian $g_i$, calculate its spatial overlap with neighboring Gaussians. Gaussians with high overlap and low unique contribution are marked for removal.
3.  **Temporal Consistency:** In dynamic scenes, analyze the temporal consistency of Gaussian attributes. Gaussians that do not contribute significantly to the change in appearance over time are candidates for pruning.
4.  **Pruning:** Remove the marked Gaussians from the scene representation.
5.  **Output:** A sparse set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}' \subset \mathcal{G}$ with reduced count.

### Attribute Compression (Compact3D, SOGS, LightGaussian, EAGLES)
These methods compress the attributes (position, covariance, opacity, rotation, color) of the Gaussians to reduce storage and memory bandwidth [S2].

1.  **Importance Scoring:** Calculate an importance score for each Gaussian based on its contribution to the rendered image (e.g., based on opacity, scale, or gradient magnitude) [S2].
2.  **Pruning/Sampling:**
    *   **LightGaussian:** Prunes Gaussians with low importance scores [S2].
    *   **Mini-Splatting:** Samples Gaussians instead of pruning to avoid artifacts [S2].
3.  **Attribute Compression:**
    *   **Compact3D:** Does not quantize opacity and position to avoid overlapping Gaussians. Other attributes are quantized [S2].
    *   **SOGS:** Arranges Gaussian attributes into multiple 2D grids. Sorts the grids and applies smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values different from their local neighborhood. Compresses RGB grid with JPEG XL and other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression [S2].
    *   **EAGLES:** Quantizes all attributes (color, position, opacity, rotation, scaling) into latent vectors decoded by a small MLP. Uses influence-based pruning [S2].
    *   **LightGaussian:** Factorizes Spherical Harmonic (SH) parameters, applies structured pruning, and uses G-PCC for position compression [S1, unofficial][S2].
4.  **Output:** Compressed Gaussian representation.

### Hierarchical Spatial Partitioning (H-3DGS, SPTs, Vmem, Budgeted GS)
These methods organize Gaussians into hierarchical structures to enable efficient LOD selection and memory management [S1, S3, unofficial].

1.  **Hierarchy Construction:**
    *   **H-3DGS:** Builds a cluster-based octree-like tree structure. Clusters are formed by grouping nearby Gaussians [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   **SPTs:** Constructs Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) that evolve continuously during optimization. The tree defines a global ordering of primitives [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Budgeted GS:** Constructs a Morton-ordered tree of primitives with a coverage-preserving prefix cut [S1, unofficial].
2.  **LOD Selection:**
    *   **H-3DGS:** Selects appropriate detail levels based on viewing distance [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   **SPTs:** Computes the correct LoD cut efficiently for individual views without full tree traversal [S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Budgeted GS:** Uses the prefix cut to define successive approximations of the scene [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Memory Management (Vmem):**
    *   Groups Gaussians into fixed-size pages.
    *   Uses a proxy mesh for visibility culling.
    *   Streams only visible pages into GPU memory [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Unreachable Gaussian Handling (SPTs):**
    *   Identify Gaussians where the child's scale is larger than the parent's, violating the SPT cut condition [S3, unofficial].
    *   Leave these Gaussians in RAM but do not render or transfer them [S3, unofficial].
    *   Do not rebalance the hierarchy during training as it is too costly [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Output:** Hierarchical structure with LOD-aware rendering.

### Multi-scale Filtering
Mitigates aliasing artifacts by adapting the Gaussian representation to the camera's viewing frequency or resolution [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Covariance Regularization:** Adjusts the covariance matrix of Gaussians at runtime based on the projected screen size [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Training-time Aggregation:** Aggregates Gaussians during training to ensure consistent spectral content across scales [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Output:** Rendered image with reduced aliasing.

### Gaussian Clustering and Merging
Reduces computational cost by grouping individual splats into representative clusters [S2].

1.  **Clustering:** Groups Gaussians based on spatial proximity and attribute similarity.
2.  **Aggregation:** Computes representative parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) for each cluster.
3.  **Output:** A reduced set of cluster primitives [S2].

## Subtopics

### EAGLES
EAGLES is a method for optimizing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) representations by reducing memory footprint, training time, and storage size while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality [S2]. It achieves this through attribute quantization, progressive coarse-to-fine training, and aggressive pruning [S2]. Unlike standard 3D-GS, which stores full-precision attributes for millions of Gaussians, EAGLES compresses color, rotation, and opacity into latent vectors decoded by a small MLP, and reduces the Gaussian count via influence-based pruning [S2]. It sets itself apart from sibling LOD methods like H-3DGS by focusing on attribute-level compression and training-time acceleration rather than hierarchical spatial structures [S2].

### [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md)
LightGaussian is a compression method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reduces model size and improves rendering speed by combining structured pruning with Spherical Harmonic (SH) factorization and vector quantization. It achieves significant size reduction (up to 15$\times$) and high frame rates ($>200$ FPS) with minimal quality degradation ($<0.5$ dB drop). It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by focusing on attribute-level compression (SH factorization, vector quantization, and G-PCC position compression) rather than purely spatial clustering or octree-based spatial pruning.

### TRIPS
TRIPS is a point-based radiance field rendering technique that rasterizes points into a screen-space image pyramid using trilinear splats, followed by a neural reconstruction network to produce hole-free, crisp images [S2]. It solves the blurriness and cloudy artifacts of 3D Gaussian Splatting and the temporal instability and large-gap handling issues of ADOP by projecting neural color descriptors into an image pyramid using trilinear writes to two neighboring resolution layers based on projected point size, avoiding overdraw by rendering only 2x2 splats [S2].

### [Multi-scale filtering](8244-multi-scale-filtering.md)
Multi-scale filtering refers to a family of techniques in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that mitigate aliasing artifacts and optimize rendering performance by adapting the Gaussian representation to the camera's viewing frequency or resolution. It sets itself apart from standard 3DGS by explicitly managing the spectral content of the primitives—either through training-time aggregation, test-time covariance regularization, or runtime hierarchical pruning—to ensure the rendered image remains consistent across varying scales.

### [Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting](8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vmem) enables the rendering of large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes that exceed available GPU memory by applying virtual texturing concepts. It groups Gaussians into fixed-size pages, uses a proxy mesh for visibility culling, and streams only visible pages into a limited GPU buffer. Unlike standard 3DGS which loads all Gaussians or relies on simple distance culling, Vmem separates virtual and physical address spaces, allowing dynamic LOD selection and just-in-time memory allocation to maintain stable frame rates in memory-constrained environments.

### [Gaussian clustering and merging](8436-gaussian-clustering-and-merging.md)
Gaussian clustering and merging refers to a family of techniques that reduce the computational and memory cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by grouping individual splats into representative clusters or hierarchical nodes. These methods solve the problem of rendering large-scale scenes with millions of Gaussians by enabling Level of Detail (LOD) generation, spatial culling, and memory management. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [Multi-scale filtering](8244-multi-scale-filtering.md) or [Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting](8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) is the explicit construction of cluster structures (flat or hierarchical) and the aggregation of Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) to form fewer, larger primitives, rather than merely filtering or paging existing data.

### [Budgeted Gaussian Splatting](9672-budgeted-gaussian-splatting.md)
Budgeted Gaussian Splatting (BCT) is a training and deployment method for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that enables continuous, variable primitive counts (Level of Detail) without retraining. It solves the problem of fixed-budget compression by constructing a Morton-ordered tree of primitives and a coverage-preserving prefix cut, allowing for near-lossless quality at low compression ratios. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) and [Iterative Gaussian Synopsis](9289-iterative-gaussian-synopsis.md) by providing a single, global primitive ordering whose prefixes define successive approximations of the scene, rather than camera-dependent hierarchical selectors or discrete hierarchy methods.

### [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md)
Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS) is a Level-of-Detail (LoD) method that organizes 3D Gaussian primitives into a hierarchical structure to enable efficient rendering of large-scale scenes by dynamically selecting appropriate detail levels based on viewing distance. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Octree-GS](7341-octree-gs.md) by using a cluster-based octree-like tree structure rather than an explicit anchor-based voxel grid, and from [CityGaussian / VastGaussian / DoGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) by employing a unified top-down or bottom-up hierarchy generation rather than independent block-wise training.

### [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md)
Sequential Point Trees (SPTs) are a hierarchical data structure originally developed for point cloud rendering, adapted here for efficient, view-dependent Level-of-Detail (LoD) selection in 3D Gaussian Splatting without requiring full tree traversal. Unlike sibling methods that rely on static spatial partitioning or post-training hierarchy construction, SPTs enable dynamic LoD hierarchies that evolve continuously during optimization. They are primarily used within the Hierarchical SPT (HSPT) framework to compute the correct LoD cut efficiently for individual views and camera paths.

### SOGS (Sparse Orthographic Grid Splatting)
SOGS is a method that arranges Gaussian attributes into multiple 2D grids, sorts them, and applies a smoothness regularization to penalize pixels with values very different from their local neighborhood on the 2D grid [S2]. For storage, SOGS compresses the RGB grid with JPEG XL and stores all other attributes as 32-bit OpenEXR images with zip compression [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98, LPIPS 0.238, and a size of 28.80 MB [S2].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting Compression
This refers to compression methods that do not quantize opacity and position because sharing them results in overlapping Gaussians [S2]. On the MipNeRF360 dataset, Compact3D achieves SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16, LPIPS 0.228, and a size of / MB (unspecified) [S2].

### Unreachable Gaussian Handling
A strategy to tolerate cases where a child Gaussian becomes larger than its parent, making it impossible for the parent to fulfill the SPT cut condition, by leaving these Gaussians in RAM but not rendering them [S3, unofficial]. The steps are: identify Gaussians where the child's scale is larger than the parent's, violating the SPT cut condition; do not rebalance the hierarchy; leave these Gaussians in RAM, but never render or transfer them [S3, unofficial]. Rebalancing the hierarchy during training was experimented with but found to be too costly for little benefit. Generating improper cuts to fix this is considered worse as it derails training [S3, unofficial]. This inefficiency affects less than 10% of Gaussians in practice [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory:** LOD methods significantly reduce memory footprint. RTGS reduces the number of Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. Compression methods like LightGaussian achieve up to 15$\times$ size reduction [S1, S3, unofficial]. Vmem enables rendering of scenes exceeding GPU memory [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** LOD methods increase rendering speed. LightGaussian achieves $>200$ FPS [S1, S3, unofficial]. RTGS accelerates rendering by reducing primitive count [S1, unofficial].
*   **Quality:** Most methods maintain high quality with minimal degradation. LightGaussian has $<0.5$ dB drop [S1, S3, unofficial]. SOGS achieves SSIM 0.801, PSNR 26.98 on MipNeRF360 [S2]. Compact3D achieves SSIM 0.808, PSNR 27.16 [S2].
*   **Complexity:** Hierarchical methods (H-3DGS, SPTs) add complexity to training and rendering due to tree construction and traversal [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial]. Compression methods add complexity to attribute encoding/decoding [S2].
*   **Scalability:** LOD methods enable rendering of large-scale scenes. Vmem handles scenes exceeding GPU memory [S1, unofficial]. Budgeted GS enables continuous LOD without retraining [S1, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) was introduced by Kerbl et al. [S2]. The need for LOD methods arose due to the high computational and memory costs of rendering millions of Gaussians. RTGS was proposed to reduce redundancy [S1, unofficial]. Various compression methods (Compact3D, EAGLES, LightGaussian, SOGS) were developed to reduce storage and memory bandwidth [S2]. Hierarchical methods (H-3DGS, SPTs, Vmem) were developed to enable efficient rendering of large-scale scenes [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial].
