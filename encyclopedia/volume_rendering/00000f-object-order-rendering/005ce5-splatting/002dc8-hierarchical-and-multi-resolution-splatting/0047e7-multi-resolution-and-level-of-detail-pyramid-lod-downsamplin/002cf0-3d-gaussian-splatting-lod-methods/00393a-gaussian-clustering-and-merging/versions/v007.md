# Gaussian clustering and merging

Also called: Gaussian clustering, Gaussian merging, Gaussian reduction

## Overview
Gaussian clustering and merging reduces the computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by grouping individual splats into representative clusters or merged Gaussians, thereby decreasing the number of primitives rendered per frame [S1, S2, unofficial]. This family of methods addresses the scalability limits of large-scale scenes by enabling Level of Detail (LOD) selection, virtual memory management, and spatial culling. It differs from sibling methods like [CityGaussian](6143-citygaussian-vastgaussian-dogaussian.md) (which focuses on training-time pruning) and [LightGaussian](8036-lightgaussian.md) (which optimizes spherical harmonics) by operating on the spatial and attribute structure of the Gaussian set to create hierarchical or reduced representations for rendering efficiency [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of 3D Gaussians $G_i$, each defined by position $\mathbf{t}_i$, covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_i$ (or scale/rotation), opacity $\alpha_i$, and color coefficients $c_i$ (typically spherical harmonics). The core mechanism involves two phases: **Clustering** and **Merging**.

1.  **Clustering:** Gaussians are grouped into clusters $C_k$ based on spatial proximity, attribute similarity, or hierarchical structure. Common clustering strategies include:
    *   **Spatial Clustering:** Grouping by world-space distance or Morton code ordering [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   **Attribute Clustering:** Grouping by similarity in position, color, and scale [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Hierarchical Clustering:** Building a tree structure (e.g., Octree, VDB-like) where leaf nodes contain raw Gaussians and internal nodes contain merged representatives [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Merging:** Each cluster $C_k = \{G_i\}_{i \in C_k}$ is replaced by a single representative Gaussian $G'_k$. The parameters of $G'_k$ are derived from the members of $C_k$. The specific merging formula varies by method:
    *   **Moment Matching:** Preserving the first and second moments of the cluster to maintain spatial extent and center of mass [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Arithmetic Averaging:** Computing the mean of position, color, and opacity [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   **L2-Optimal Amplitude:** Optimizing the amplitude of the merged Gaussian to minimize the integrated squared error with respect to the cluster members [S1, unofficial].

The output is a reduced set of Gaussians that approximates the original scene's appearance, often organized into LOD levels or virtual memory pages [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude](5440-k-wise-moment-matched-merging-with-l2-optimal-amplitude.md)
A level-of-detail merging technique that combines $K$ splats into a single representative splat by matching moments and optimizing the resulting amplitude via L2 loss. It differs from simple arithmetic averaging by explicitly minimizing the integrated squared error between the merged Gaussian and the cluster members, ensuring the amplitude is optimal for the L2 metric rather than a heuristic average.

### [Hierarchical Gaussian Merging](8358-hierarchical-gaussian-merging.md)
Hierarchical Gaussian Merging generates multiple levels of detail (LOD) for 3D Gaussian Splatting by iteratively clustering and averaging Gaussian parameters to reduce scene complexity. It differs from methods using moment-matching or L2-optimal amplitude preservation by employing a simple arithmetic average for position, color, and opacity, while explicitly excluding scale from this averaging process to prevent geometric distortion. This approach enables efficient virtual memory management by grouping Gaussians into pages that can be loaded or unloaded based on visibility.

### [LOD Generation for 3DGS](8408-lod-generation-for-3dgs.md)
This method reduces the number of 3D Gaussians copied, stored, sorted, and rendered by merging Gaussians into clusters based on similar attributes, minimizing distant appearance impact without altering scene creation. It differs from virtual texturing where mip levels are uniform-sized pages; this method reduces Gaussian count with each LOD level, allowing multiple virtual pages within one physical page. It builds on [Gaussian clustering and merging](8436-gaussian-clustering-and-merging.md).

### [Cluster Culling (Nanite-style)](10402-cluster-culling-nanite-style.md)
Cluster Culling is a hierarchical frustum culling and Level of Detail (LOD) selection mechanism for 3D Gaussian Splatting, designed to emulate the spatial clustering and culling efficiency of Nanite. It operates on pre-computed Gaussian clusters, testing their bounding volumes against the view frustum and selecting the appropriate LOD based on screen-space error metrics. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by integrating visibility culling, LOD selection, and indirect draw argument generation into a unified GPU compute pipeline, optimizing for high-performance rendering of large-scale Gaussian scenes.

### [World Space Clustering](13206-world-space-clustering.md)
World Space Clustering is a preprocessing step in 3D Gaussian Splatting that groups sampled world-space pixels into clusters to remove foreground or background noise before projecting 2D Gaussian kernels. It differs from attribute-based clustering by operating on geometric proximity in world space rather than color or scale attributes, using single linkage merging to identify coherent spatial structures.

### Inter-leaf node clustering
#### definition
- [S2, unofficial] A post-processing step that merges neighboring Gaussian blocks across leaf node boundaries to form larger Gaussians for homogeneous regions.
#### step
- [S2, unofficial] Store intermediate LOD refinement results as if they were individual leaf nodes.
- [S2, unofficial] Run spatial variance estimation on these results sequentially in groups of 26 neighbors (considering each 2x2x2 node has 26 neighbors: faces, edges, corners).
- [S2, unofficial] If computed variance is below a predefined threshold, replace the entire group with a single merged Gaussian.
- [S2, unofficial] Calculate the new Gaussian position as the merged centroid and axis-aligned radii/opacity using methods described in previous variants.
#### formula
- [S2, unofficial] bboxmin = min_i(cmin_i), bboxmax = max_i(cmax_i), where c_i are the corner coordinates of the bounding boxes of the regions being merged.

## Background
The need for clustering and merging in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the high memory and computational costs associated with rendering millions of individual splats, particularly in large-scale scenes. Early 3DGS implementations rendered all splats without hierarchy, leading to scalability issues. Subsequent research introduced hierarchical structures and LOD techniques to mitigate this.

*   **Hierarchical Structures:** Methods like [Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Splatting (H-3DGS)](10279-hierarchical-3d-gaussian-splatting-h-3dgs.md) and [Sequential Point Trees (SPTs)](10290-sequential-point-trees-spts.md) introduced tree-based hierarchies to enable efficient culling and LOD selection.
*   **Virtual Memory:** Techniques such as [Virtual Memory for 3D Gaussian Splatting](8403-virtual-memory-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) and [Hierarchical Gaussian Merging](8358-hierarchical-gaussian-merging.md) use clustering to group Gaussians into pages, allowing for dynamic loading and unloading based on visibility.
*   **Scientific Visualization:** In scientific visualization, Gaussian approximations of VDB datasets have been used to represent complex volumetric data, with clustering techniques employed to reduce the number of Gaussians while preserving visual fidelity [S2, unofficial].
*   **Nanite-style Culling:** Inspired by Unreal Engine's Nanite, methods like [Cluster Culling (Nanite-style)](10402-cluster-culling-nanite-style.md) apply hierarchical frustum culling and LOD selection to Gaussian clusters, enabling real-time rendering of massive scenes.

The specific algorithms for merging (moment matching, L2 optimization, arithmetic averaging) have evolved to balance visual quality with computational efficiency. Moment matching preserves spatial properties better than simple averaging, while L2-optimal amplitude ensures the merged Gaussian contributes correctly to the final image intensity [S1, unofficial].
