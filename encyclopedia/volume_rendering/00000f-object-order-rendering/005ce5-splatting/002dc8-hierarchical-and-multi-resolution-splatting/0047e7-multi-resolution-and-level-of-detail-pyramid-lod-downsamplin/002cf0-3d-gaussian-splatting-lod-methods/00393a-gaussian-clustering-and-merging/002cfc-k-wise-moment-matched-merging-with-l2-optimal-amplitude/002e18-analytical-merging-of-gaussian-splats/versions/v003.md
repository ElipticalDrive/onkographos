# Analytical Merging of Gaussian Splats

Also called: Amplitude-optimised moment-matched template merging, Closed-form L2 integrated squared-error merging

## Overview
Analytical merging of Gaussian splats is a closed-form framework for merging two Gaussian splats by minimizing the L2 integrated squared-error between the original pair and a single representative splat [S1, unofficial]. It provides a geometric interpretation of the relative merging error, relating it to a geometric angle parameter between the splats' covariance matrices and means [S1, unofficial]. This method serves as the core merging primitive for the parent technique, [K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude](5440-k-wise-moment-matched-merging-with-l2-optimal-amplitude.md), enabling efficient level-of-detail reduction and fitting optimization [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes a single representative Gaussian splat from two input splats such that the L2 error between the sum of the two original splats and the single merged splat is minimized [S1, unofficial]. The solution is derived analytically, ensuring the merged splat matches the first and second moments of the input pair while optimizing the amplitude to minimize the residual error [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
*   Two Gaussian splats, $G_1$ and $G_2$. Each splat is defined by mean position $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (symmetric positive definite), amplitude $a \in \mathbb{R}$, and color/feature vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^k$ [G].

**Outputs:**
*   A single merged Gaussian splat $G_{merged}$ with parameters $\mu', \Sigma', a', c'$ [G].

**Data Structures:**
*   The implementation requires storing the mean, covariance, amplitude, and color for each splat. The covariance is typically stored in a symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrix or its 6 unique elements [G].

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Merged Mean and Covariance:** The merged mean $\mu'$ and covariance $\Sigma'$ are calculated as the moment-matched average of the two input splats. This ensures the spatial distribution of the merged splat approximates the combined distribution of $G_1$ and $G_2$ [G].
    *   $\mu' = \frac{a_1 \mu_1 + a_2 \mu_2}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G].
    *   $\Sigma' = \frac{a_1 (\Sigma_1 + (\mu_1 - \mu')(\mu_1 - \mu')^T) + a_2 (\Sigma_2 + (\mu_2 - \mu')(\mu_2 - \mu')^T)}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G].
2.  **Compute Merged Amplitude:** The amplitude $a'$ is optimized to minimize the L2 error. The optimal amplitude is the sum of the input amplitudes, adjusted by the geometric relationship between the splats [S1, unofficial].
    *   The relative merging error $\epsilon$ is given by $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the geometric angle parameter derived from the covariance matrices and means [S1, unofficial].
    *   The optimal amplitude is $a' = (a_1 + a_2) \sqrt{1 - \epsilon}$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Merged Color/Feature:** The color $c'$ is a weighted average of the input colors, weighted by their amplitudes [G].
    *   $c' = \frac{a_1 c_1 + a_2 c_2}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G].

**Formulas:**
*   **Relative Merging Error:** $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Geometric Angle $\alpha$:** The angle $\alpha$ is determined by the relative orientation and scale of the covariance matrices $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ and the distance between means $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$. The exact formula for $\alpha$ is not provided in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimal Amplitude:** $a' = (a_1 + a_2) \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \alpha} = (a_1 + a_2) |\cos \alpha|$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   No free parameters are required for the core merging algorithm. The method is closed-form and deterministic [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Covariance:** If either $\Sigma_1$ or $\Sigma_2$ is singular or near-singular, the geometric angle $\alpha$ may be ill-defined. Regularization of the covariance matrices (e.g., adding a small identity matrix) may be required [G].
*   **Zero Amplitude:** If both $a_1$ and $a_2$ are zero, the merged splat is undefined. This case should be handled by skipping the merge or returning a null splat [G].
*   **Precision:** The calculation of $\sin^2 \alpha$ requires careful handling of floating-point precision, especially when the splats are very similar ($\alpha \approx 0$) or very different ($\alpha \approx \pi/2$) [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def outer(v1, v2):
    return [[v1[i] * v2[j] for j in range(len(v2))] for i in range(len(v1))]

def mat_add(A, B):
    return [[A[i][j] + B[i][j] for j in range(3)] for i in range(3)]

def mat_scale(A, s):
    return [[A[i][j] * s for j in range(3)] for i in range(3)]

def merge_gaussians(g1, g2):
    mu1, Sigma1, a1, c1 = g1
    mu2, Sigma2, a2, c2 = g2
    
    total_amplitude = a1 + a2
    if total_amplitude == 0:
        return (mu1, Sigma1, 0.0, c1)
    
    mu_prime = [(a1 * mu1[i] + a2 * mu2[i]) / total_amplitude for i in range(3)]
    
    diff1 = [mu1[i] - mu_prime[i] for i in range(3)]
    diff2 = [mu2[i] - mu_prime[i] for i in range(3)]
    
    Sigma_prime = mat_add(
        mat_add(Sigma1, outer(diff1, diff1)),
        mat_add(Sigma2, outer(diff2, diff2))
    )
    Sigma_prime = mat_scale(Sigma_prime, 1.0 / total_amplitude)
    
    # Compute geometric angle alpha from covariance matrices and mean distance.
    # Exact formula for alpha is not provided in the sources [S1, unofficial].
    alpha = 0.0
    
    epsilon = math.sin(alpha) ** 2
    a_prime = total_amplitude * math.sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - epsilon))
    
    c_prime = [(a1 * c1[i] + a2 * c2[i]) / total_amplitude for i in range(len(c1))]
    
    return (mu_prime, Sigma_prime, a_prime, c_prime)
```

## Subtopics

### [Stage-dependent merging](16538-stage-dependent-merging.md)
Stage-dependent merging is a technique for progressively refining Gaussian representations by partitioning candidate Gaussians into groups and merging them using geometry-conditioned soft weights, exposing only coarse representations at early training stages. It sets itself apart from its sibling [Analytical Merging of Gaussian Splats](5681-analytical-merging-of-gaussian-splats.md) by using a learned, soft-weighted aggregation rather than a closed-form L2-optimal geometric merge, allowing for a smooth transition from coarse to fine detail during training.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The merging of two splats is $O(1)$ if the geometric angle $\alpha$ is precomputed or approximated [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per merge operation, as it only requires storing the parameters of the two input splats and the one output splat [G].
*   **Measured Performance:** Implemented in Luxar for merging Gaussian splats during fitting or LOD reduction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Closed-form solution ensures deterministic and fast merging. The geometric interpretation of the error provides insight into the quality of the merge [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The exact formula for the geometric angle $\alpha$ is not provided, which may hinder implementation. The method is limited to merging two splats at a time; merging more than two requires iterative application [G].
*   **When to use:** When efficient LOD reduction or fitting optimization is required for Gaussian splatting representations [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the geometric angle $\alpha$ cannot be reliably computed or when merging more than two splats simultaneously is required without iterative application [G].
