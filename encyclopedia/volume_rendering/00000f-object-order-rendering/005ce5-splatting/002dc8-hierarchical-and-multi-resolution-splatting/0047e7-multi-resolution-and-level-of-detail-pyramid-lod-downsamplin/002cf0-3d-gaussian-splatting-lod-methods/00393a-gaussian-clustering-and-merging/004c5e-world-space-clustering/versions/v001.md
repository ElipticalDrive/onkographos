# World Space Clustering

Also called: Single linkage merging

## Overview
World Space Clustering is a preprocessing technique used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to organize spatial data before projection. It groups pixels or Gaussian primitives based on their world-space coordinates to remove foreground or background noise and establish spatial coherence [S1, unofficial]. It differs from attribute-based methods like [Coarse-Level Color-Based Segmentation](8408-lod-generation-for-3dgs.md) by relying strictly on geometric proximity rather than color or semantic features [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of world-space coordinates $\{\mathbf{p}_k\}_{k=1}^M$ corresponding to pixels covered by Gaussian kernels.
**Outputs:** A set of disjoint clusters $C_j$, where each cluster contains coordinates within a specific spatial threshold.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Clusters:** A list of sets, where each set contains indices of points belonging to that cluster.
*   **Distance Matrix:** Precomputed or on-the-fly Euclidean distances between points.

**Parameters:**
*   $\delta_d$: The world-space distance between two adjacent pixels at the depth of the Gaussian kernel’s central pixel.
*   $T_{init}$: Initial termination threshold, typically $5 \cdot \delta_d$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $T_{fail}$: Failure multiplier, typically $1.5$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Create $M$ singleton clusters, each containing one point $\mathbf{p}_k$.
2.  **Distance Calculation:** Compute the minimum distance between any two points in distinct clusters. For clusters $C_a$ and $C_b$, the distance is:
    $$ d(C_a, C_b) = \min_{\mathbf{p}_i \in C_a, \mathbf{p}_j \in C_b} \|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\|_2 $$
3.  **Merging:** Identify the pair of clusters $(C_a, C_b)$ with the minimum distance $d(C_a, C_b)$.
4.  **Threshold Check:** If $d(C_a, C_b) \le T$, merge $C_a$ and $C_b$ into a single cluster. If $d(C_a, C_b) > T$, stop merging.
5.  **Iteration:** Repeat steps 3–4 until only one cluster remains or the threshold condition fails.
6.  **Failure Handling:** If the process fails to produce a useful clustering (e.g., too many small clusters or singletons), update $T \leftarrow T \cdot T_{fail}$ and restart from step 2 [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Coarse-Level Color-Based Segmentation
A variant that groups 3D Gaussians based on approximate view-independent colors rather than spatial proximity [S2]. It averages view-dependent colors across sampled directions to compute a representative color $\tilde{c}$ for each Gaussian:
$$ \tilde{c} \approx \frac{1}{\|D\|} \sum_{d \in D} c_d $$
where $D$ is the set of sampled viewing directions and $c_d$ is the color in direction $d$ [S2]. Clustering is then performed using k-means with Euclidean distance in RGB space [S2]. Outlier removal is applied by checking neighbor counts within a small-radius sphere; Gaussians with fewer neighbors than a threshold are removed [S2]. This method is used for coarse-level decomposition in volumetric segmentation tasks [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(M^2)$ or $O(M^3)$ depending on implementation, as it requires computing pairwise distances between points in clusters. Single linkage merging is computationally expensive for large $M$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(M^2)$ to store distance matrices or $O(M)$ if computed on-the-fly [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effective at removing spatial noise (foreground/background) and establishing spatial coherence for subsequent rendering or optimization steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive for large numbers of points. Sensitive to the choice of threshold $T$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When preprocessing spatial data for Gaussian Splatting to reduce noise or organize primitives for hierarchical rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When color or semantic similarity is more important than spatial proximity [S2].

## Background
The method is based on single linkage merging, a hierarchical clustering algorithm introduced by Sibson in 1973 [S1, unofficial]. It was adapted for 3D Gaussian Splatting in the context of Augmented Radiance Fields to preprocess pixel coordinates before projection [S1, unofficial].
