# K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude

Also called: cost-aware merging, substitutive LOD merging

## Overview
K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude is a level-of-detail (LOD) technique that reduces a set of $K$ 3D Gaussians into a single representative Gaussian by matching the first and second spatial moments of the cluster and optimizing the resulting amplitude to minimize the integrated squared error (L2 loss) [S1, unofficial]. It differs from simple arithmetic averaging by preserving the spatial extent and center of mass of the group, and from culling methods by retaining all information in a denser, lower-count representation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A cluster of $K$ Gaussians $C_k = \{G_i\}_{i=1}^K$, where each $G_i$ is defined by position $\mathbf{t}_i$, covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color coefficients $c_i$.
**Outputs:** A single representative Gaussian $G'_k$ with parameters $\mathbf{t}'_k, \mathbf{\Sigma}'_k, \alpha'_k, c'_k$.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the statistical moments of the cluster.
1.  **Weighted Position ($\mathbf{t}'_k$):** The center of mass of the cluster, weighted by opacity.
2.  **Weighted Covariance ($\mathbf{\Sigma}'_k$):** The second moment of the cluster, adjusted for the spread of the individual Gaussians.
3.  **Optimized Amplitude ($\alpha'_k$):** Derived by minimizing the L2 error between the merged Gaussian and the sum of the input Gaussians.

**Steps:**
1.  **Partitioning:** The source of Gaussians is partitioned into clusters using one of three algorithms:
    *   Greedy hierarchical merging (à la Runnalls) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Cost-increment relocation [S1, unofficial].
    *   Joint relaxation [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Moment Matching:** Compute the first and second moments of the cluster $C_k$.
    *   Let $w_i = \alpha_i$ be the weight of each Gaussian.
    *   Compute the total weight $W = \sum_{i \in C_k} w_i$.
    *   Compute the merged position:
        $$ \mathbf{t}'_k = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i \in C_k} w_i \mathbf{t}_i $$
    *   Compute the merged covariance. The covariance of a Gaussian is related to its scale. The merged covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}'_k$ is calculated to match the second moment of the cluster. For 3D Gaussians, this typically involves summing the individual covariances weighted by their amplitudes and adding the outer product of the position deviations:
        $$ \mathbf{\Sigma}'_k = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i \in C_k} w_i \left( \mathbf{\Sigma}_i + (\mathbf{t}_i - \mathbf{t}'_k)(\mathbf{t}_i - \mathbf{t}'_k)^T \right) $$
        [G] Note: In standard 3DGS, $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ is often parameterized by scale $\mathbf{s}$ and rotation $\mathbf{q}$. The covariance matrix is $\mathbf{\Sigma} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^T\mathbf{R}^T$. The merging formula above applies to the covariance matrix directly.
3.  **L2-Optimal Amplitude:** Determine $\alpha'_k$ to minimize the integrated squared error between the merged Gaussian and the cluster.
    *   The relative merging error for the amplitude-optimized moment-matched template is given by $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is a geometric angle parameter derived from the overlap and alignment of the Gaussians in the cluster [S1, unofficial].
    *   Specifically, the optimal amplitude $\alpha'_k$ is found by solving the minimization problem:
        $$ \alpha'_k = \arg \min_{\alpha} \int \| \alpha \cdot \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{t}'_k, \mathbf{\Sigma}'_k) - \sum_{i \in C_k} \alpha_i \cdot \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{t}_i, \mathbf{\Sigma}_i) \|^2 d\mathbf{x} $$
        [G] In practice, this often results in a closed-form solution or a simple scalar optimization based on the overlap integrals of the Gaussians. The source notes that the error metric $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$ provides a geometric interpretation of this error [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Color Interpolation:** The color coefficients $c'_k$ are typically computed as the weighted average of the input colors, weighted by the original opacities and the new amplitude:
    $$ c'_k = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i \in C_k} w_i c_i $$
    [G] Alternatively, $c'_k$ may be scaled by the ratio $\alpha'_k / W$ to preserve radiance.

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: The number of Gaussians to merge. Typical values are powers of 2 (e.g., 4, 8, 16) for LOD levels [S1, unofficial].
*   Partitioning Algorithm: Select from greedy, cost-increment, or joint relaxation [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Degenerate Clusters:** If $K=1$, the Gaussian is returned unchanged.
*   **Precision:** The L2 optimization requires accurate computation of overlap integrals. Numerical instability may occur if Gaussians are nearly identical or widely separated [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The method assumes the cluster is spatially coherent. Poor clustering (e.g., merging distant Gaussians) leads to high L2 error [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Analytical Merging of Gaussian Splats
A closed-form framework for merging two Gaussian splats using L2 integrated squared-error, providing a geometric interpretation of the relative merging error [S1, unofficial]. The relative merging error is $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$ for the amplitude-optimised moment-matched template, where $\alpha$ is the geometric angle parameter in the error derivation [S1, unofficial]. It is implemented in Luxar for merging Gaussian splats during fitting or LOD reduction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Time:** Depends on the partitioning algorithm. Greedy hierarchical merging is $O(N \log N)$ or $O(N^2)$ depending on implementation. Cost-increment relocation is efficient, matching greedy performance within 5.5% at compression factors of four and above [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory:** Reduces the number of Gaussians by a factor of $K$, reducing memory usage by $O(K)$ per cluster [S1, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
*   On full fits, cost-aware merging outperforms mass-ranked culling regardless of amplitude refit [S1, unofficial].
*   On sparse subsamples, culling wins in the median [S1, unofficial].
*   The method is suitable for scalable interactive web visualisation of multidimensional scientific data [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Preserves spatial structure better than culling; provides a smooth LOD transition; cost-increment relocation offers near-optimal quality with lower computational cost than greedy methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful partitioning; L2 optimization can be sensitive to the geometric configuration of the cluster [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method originates from the Luxar project, which focuses on Gaussian splatting for microscopy and scalable interactive web visualisation of multidimensional scientific data [S1, unofficial]. It is part of a broader effort to create parameter-free image resolution estimation and substitutive levels of detail for Gaussian splats [S1, unofficial]. The technique is described in Supplementary Document 8 of the Luxar repository, which details the cost-metric design space and partitioning algorithms [S1, unofficial].
