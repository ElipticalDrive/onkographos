# Analytical Merging of Gaussian Splats

Also called: Amplitude-optimised moment-matched template merging, Closed-form L2 integrated squared-error merging

## Overview
Analytical merging of Gaussian splats is a closed-form framework for merging two Gaussian splats by minimizing the L2 integrated squared-error between the original pair and the merged representative. It provides a geometric interpretation of the relative merging error, specifically relating it to the angle between the splats' parameters. This method serves as the core merging primitive for the parent technique, [K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude](5440-k-wise-moment-matched-merging-with-l2-optimal-amplitude.md), enabling efficient level-of-detail reduction and fitting optimization.

## How it works
The method computes a single representative Gaussian splat from two input splats such that the L2 error between the sum of the two original splats and the single merged splat is minimized. The solution is derived analytically, ensuring the merged splat matches the first and second moments of the input pair while optimizing the amplitude (opacity/scale factor) to minimize the residual error.

**Inputs:**
*   Two Gaussian splats, $G_1$ and $G_2$. Each splat is defined by:
    *   Mean position $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (symmetric positive definite).
    *   Amplitude (opacity/scale) $a \in \mathbb{R}$.
    *   Color/Feature vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^k$.

**Outputs:**
*   A single merged Gaussian splat $G_{merged}$ with parameters $\mu', \Sigma', a', c'$.

**Data Structures:**
*   The implementation requires storing the mean, covariance, amplitude, and color for each splat. The covariance is typically stored in a symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrix or its 6 unique elements.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Merged Mean and Covariance:** The merged mean $\mu'$ and covariance $\Sigma'$ are calculated as the moment-matched average of the two input splats. This ensures the spatial distribution of the merged splat approximates the combined distribution of $G_1$ and $G_2$.
    *   $\mu' = \frac{a_1 \mu_1 + a_2 \mu_2}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G: Standard weighted average for moment matching].
    *   $\Sigma' = \frac{a_1 (\Sigma_1 + (\mu_1 - \mu')(\mu_1 - \mu')^T) + a_2 (\Sigma_2 + (\mu_2 - \mu')(\mu_2 - \mu')^T)}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G: Parallel axis theorem application for covariance merging].

2.  **Compute Merged Amplitude:** The amplitude $a'$ is optimized to minimize the L2 error. The optimal amplitude is the sum of the input amplitudes, adjusted by the geometric relationship between the splats.
    *   $a' = a_1 + a_2$ [G: Base amplitude before error correction].
    *   The relative merging error $\epsilon$ is given by $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the geometric angle parameter derived from the covariance matrices and means [S1, unofficial].
    *   The optimal amplitude is $a' = (a_1 + a_2) \sqrt{1 - \epsilon}$ [G: Derived from minimizing L2 error with respect to amplitude].

3.  **Compute Merged Color/Feature:** The color $c'$ is a weighted average of the input colors, weighted by their amplitudes.
    *   $c' = \frac{a_1 c_1 + a_2 c_2}{a_1 + a_2}$ [G: Standard weighted average].

**Formulas:**
*   **Relative Merging Error:** $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Geometric Angle $\alpha$:** The angle $\alpha$ is determined by the relative orientation and scale of the covariance matrices $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ and the distance between means $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$. The exact formula for $\alpha$ is not explicitly provided in the sources, but it is described as a geometric parameter in the error derivation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimal Amplitude:** $a' = (a_1 + a_2) \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \alpha} = (a_1 + a_2) |\cos \alpha|$ [G: Simplified from $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$].

**Parameters:**
*   No free parameters are required for the core merging algorithm. The method is closed-form and deterministic.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Covariance:** If either $\Sigma_1$ or $\Sigma_2$ is singular or near-singular, the geometric angle $\alpha$ may be ill-defined. Regularization of the covariance matrices (e.g., adding a small identity matrix) may be required [G].
*   **Zero Amplitude:** If both $a_1$ and $a_2$ are zero, the merged splat is undefined. This case should be handled by skipping the merge or returning a null splat.
*   **Precision:** The calculation of $\sin^2 \alpha$ requires careful handling of floating-point precision, especially when the splats are very similar ($\alpha \approx 0$) or very different ($\alpha \approx \pi/2$).

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def merge_gaussians(g1, g2):
    """
    Merges two Gaussian splats using closed-form L2 optimal amplitude.
    
    Args:
        g1: Tuple (mu1, Sigma1, a1, c1)
        g2: Tuple (mu2, Sigma2, a2, c2)
        
    Returns:
        Tuple (mu', Sigma', a', c')
    """
    mu1, Sigma1, a1, c1 = g1
    mu2, Sigma2, a2, c2 = g2
    
    # Step 1: Compute merged mean
    total_amplitude = a1 + a2
    if total_amplitude == 0:
        return (mu1, Sigma1, 0.0, c1) # Handle degenerate case
    
    mu_prime = (a1 * mu1 + a2 * mu2) / total_amplitude
    
    # Step 2: Compute merged covariance
    # Using parallel axis theorem
    Sigma_prime = (a1 * (Sigma1 + np.outer(mu1 - mu_prime, mu1 - mu_prime)) + 
                   a2 * (Sigma2 + np.outer(mu2 - mu_prime, mu2 - mu_prime))) / total_amplitude
    
    # Step 3: Compute geometric angle alpha and error epsilon
    # Note: The exact formula for alpha is not provided in the sources.
    # This is a placeholder for the geometric computation described in [S1, unofficial].
    alpha = compute_geometric_angle(mu1, Sigma1, mu2, Sigma2)
    epsilon = np.sin(alpha)**2
    
    # Step 4: Compute optimal amplitude
    a_prime = total_amplitude * np.sqrt(1 - epsilon)
    
    # Step 5: Compute merged color
    c_prime = (a1 * c1 + a2 * c2) / total_amplitude
    
    return (mu_prime, Sigma_prime, a_prime, c_prime)
```

## Subtopics

### Stage-dependent merging
A technique to progressively refine Gaussian representations by partitioning candidate Gaussians into groups and merging them using geometry-conditioned soft weights, exposing only coarse representations at early training stages [S2]. It partitions 16 candidates for a token into $G = 2^s$ groups, where each group has size $b_s = 16 / G$ [S2]. For each group $g$, normalized gate weights are calculated as $w_{p,g,i} = \exp(\ell_{p,g,i}/\tau) / \sum_{j=1}^{b_s} \exp(\ell_{p,g,j}/\tau)$, where $\ell_{p,g,i}$ is the gate logit and $\tau$ is a temperature parameter [S2]. The merged output for an attribute $z$ is $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{p,g} = \sum_{i=1}^{b_s} w_{p,g,i} \mathbf{z}_{p,g,i}$ [S2]. To avoid abrupt transitions, linear interpolation is used: $\mathbf{z}^{(s)} = (1-\lambda) \mathbf{z}^{(s-1)} + \lambda \bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(s)}$, where $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ is the transition coefficient [S2]. This method does not introduce new candidates but progressively relaxes the merging applied to the same underlying set of predictions [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The merging of two splats is $O(1)$ if the geometric angle $\alpha$ is precomputed or approximated. The computation of $\alpha$ itself depends on the eigenvalue decomposition of the covariance matrices, which is $O(1)$ for fixed dimensionality (3D).
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per merge operation, as it only requires storing the parameters of the two input splats and the one output splat.
*   **Measured Performance:** Implemented in Luxar for merging Gaussian splats during fitting or LOD reduction [S1, unofficial]. The method provides a systematic rate-distortion analysis across benchmark volumes, showing effective resolution analysis and noise floor estimation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Closed-form solution ensures deterministic and fast merging. The geometric interpretation of the error provides insight into the quality of the merge.
*   **Limitations:** The exact formula for the geometric angle $\alpha$ is not provided, which may hinder implementation. The method is limited to merging two splats at a time; merging more than two requires iterative application.
*   **When to use:** When efficient LOD reduction or fitting optimization is required for Gaussian splatting representations.
*   **When not to use:** When the geometric angle $\alpha$ cannot be reliably computed or when merging more than two splats simultaneously is required without iterative application.
