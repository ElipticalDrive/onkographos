# K-wise moment-matched merging with L2-optimal amplitude

Also called: cost-aware merging, substitutive LOD merging

## Overview
A level-of-detail merging technique that combines $K$ splats into a single representative splat by matching moments and optimizing the resulting amplitude via L2 loss [S1, unofficial]. It differs from simple arithmetic averaging by explicitly minimizing the integrated squared error between the merged Gaussian and the cluster members, ensuring the amplitude is optimal for the L2 metric rather than a heuristic average [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A cluster of $K$ Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^K$, where each $G_i$ is defined by position $\mathbf{t}_i$, covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_i$, and amplitude $a_i$ (related to opacity/color).
*   **Output:** A single representative Gaussian $G'$ with position $\mathbf{t}'$, covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}'$, and amplitude $a'$.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the parameters of the input Gaussians. No specific bit-packing is mandated by the core algorithm, though practical implementations may use quantized representations (e.g., uint16 centers, uint8 Cholesky factors) for storage [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Partitioning:** The input set of splats is partitioned into clusters using one of three algorithms:
    *   Greedy hierarchical (à la Runnalls) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Cost-increment relocation [S1, unofficial].
    *   Joint relaxation [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Moment Matching:** For a given cluster, compute the merged covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}'$ and position $\mathbf{t}'$ by matching the first and second moments of the cluster members. This ensures the spatial extent and center of mass are preserved [S1, unofficial].
3.  **L2-Optimal Amplitude:** Compute the amplitude $a'$ by minimizing the L2 integrated squared error between the merged Gaussian $G'$ and the sum of the original Gaussians in the cluster. This step distinguishes the method from standard moment-matching which might use a simple sum or average of amplitudes [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
Let the merged Gaussian be $G'(\mathbf{x}) = a' \exp(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t}')^T \mathbf{\Sigma}'^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t}'))$.
The position $\mathbf{t}'$ and covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}'$ are derived from the moment-matching constraints of the cluster $\{G_i\}$ [S1, unofficial].
The amplitude $a'$ is the solution to the L2 optimization problem:
$$ a' = \arg \min_a \int \| a G'(\mathbf{x}) - \sum_{i=1}^K G_i(\mathbf{x}) \|^2 d\mathbf{x} $$
For the specific case of merging two Gaussians, the relative merging error $\epsilon$ for the amplitude-optimised moment-matched template is given by:
$$ \epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha $$
where $\alpha$ is a geometric angle parameter derived from the relative orientation and scale of the two splats [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: The number of splats to merge. Typical values depend on the desired compression factor (e.g., $K=4$ for 4x compression) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The L2 optimization requires careful handling of numerical stability, especially when splats are nearly identical or very far apart [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The method assumes a continuous domain for integration; discrete voxelization errors may arise if not accounted for [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Analytical Merging of Gaussian Splats
A closed-form framework for merging two Gaussian splats using L2 integrated squared-error, providing a geometric interpretation of the relative merging error [S1, unofficial]. The relative merging error is $\epsilon = \sin^2 \alpha$ for the amplitude-optimised moment-matched template, where $\alpha$ is the geometric angle parameter [S1, unofficial]. It is implemented in Luxar for merging Gaussian splats during fitting or LOD reduction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the partitioning algorithm. Greedy hierarchical is generally $O(N \log N)$ or $O(N^2)$ depending on implementation. Cost-increment relocation is noted to match greedy merging performance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of splats $N$ for storage, plus overhead for the partitioning structure.
*   **Measured Performance:** Cost-increment relocation matches greedy merging performance within 5.5% at compression factors of four and above [S1, unofficial]. On full fits, cost-aware merging outperforms mass-ranked culling regardless of amplitude refit; on sparse subsamples, culling wins in the median [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides a theoretically grounded amplitude that minimizes L2 error, leading to better reconstruction quality for a given splat count compared to simple averaging.
*   **Limitations:** More computationally intensive than simple averaging due to the L2 optimization step. The general closed-form for $K>2$ is not explicitly detailed in the provided sources, potentially requiring numerical methods.

## Background
Originated in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting LOD methods, specifically within the Luxar project for microscopy and scalable interactive web visualisation [S1, unofficial]. The method is part of a broader effort to create substitutive levels of detail for Gaussian splats, addressing the need for efficient compression and rendering of large-scale volumetric data [S1, unofficial].
