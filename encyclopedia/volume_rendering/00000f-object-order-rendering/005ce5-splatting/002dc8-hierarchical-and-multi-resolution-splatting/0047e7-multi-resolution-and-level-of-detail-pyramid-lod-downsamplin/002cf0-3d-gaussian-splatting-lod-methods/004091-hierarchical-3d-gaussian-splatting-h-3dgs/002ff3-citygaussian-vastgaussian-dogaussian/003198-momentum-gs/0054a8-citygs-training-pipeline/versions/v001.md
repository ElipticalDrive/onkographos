# CityGS Training Pipeline

Also called: Block-wise Parallel Training with Global Prior, CityGaussian Training

## Overview
The CityGS Training Pipeline is a method for training large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting models by dividing the scene into spatial blocks for parallel processing, using a coarse global Gaussian prior to ensure geometric consistency and prevent floating artifacts during fusion [S1]. It differs from standard block-wise training by introducing a contraction-based partitioning scheme and a dual-criteria data assignment strategy, which together enable reliable parallel finetuning and direct concatenation of block results without complex alignment [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of calibrated images with intrinsics $\kappa$ and poses $\tau_i$ (world-to-camera transformation matrices).
*   An initial point cloud generated by Structure-from-Motion (SfM), typically via COLMAP [S1].
*   Hyperparameters:
    *   $T_{prior} = 30,000$: Iterations for coarse prior training [S1].
    *   $\epsilon$: SSIM loss threshold for data assignment [S1].
    *   $K_{min}$: Minimum Gaussian count threshold for empty block enlargement [S1].
    *   $\lambda_{L1}, \lambda_{SSIM}$: Weights for L1 and SSIM losses [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A concatenated set of finetuned 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_{final}$ representing the full scene [S1].

### Data Structures
The method operates on sets of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_K = \{G_k\}_{k=1}^K$. Each Gaussian $G_k$ contains:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   Opacity $\alpha_k \in [0, 1]$ [S1].
*   Covariance parameters (scaling and rotation) [S1].
*   Spherical Harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{f}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 16}$ for view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

Blocks are defined by spatial bounds $\mathbf{b}_{j,min}, \mathbf{b}_{j,max} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ in the contracted coordinate space [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Generate Coarse Global Prior:**
    Train the initial COLMAP point cloud using all available image observations for $T_{prior} = 30,000$ iterations. This yields a coarse Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_K$ that approximates the global geometry distribution [S1].

2.  **Contract Global Prior:**
    To enable balanced partitioning, map the foreground region of $\mathcal{G}_K$ to a normalized space $[-1, 1]^3$.
    *   Identify the bounding box corners $\mathbf{p}_{min}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{max}$ of the foreground points in $\mathcal{G}_K$.
    *   Normalize positions $\mathbf{p}_k$ to $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k$:
        $$ \hat{\mathbf{p}}_k = \frac{\mathbf{p}_k - \mathbf{p}_{min}}{\mathbf{p}_{max} - \mathbf{p}_{min}} \times 2 - 1 $$
    *   Apply non-linear contraction to $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k$ to handle points outside the unit cube:
        $$ \text{contract}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k) = \begin{cases} \hat{\mathbf{p}}_k & \text{if } \|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k\|_\infty \le 1 \\ \left(2 - \frac{1}{\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k\|_\infty}\right) \frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k}{\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k\|_\infty} & \text{if } \|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k\|_\infty > 1 \end{cases} $$
        where $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is the infinity norm [S1].

3.  **Partition Space and Assign Primitives:**
    *   Evenly partition the contracted cubic space into $J$ blocks. For each block $j$, define bounds $\mathbf{b}_{j,min}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{j,max}$ in the contracted space [S1].
    *   Assign Gaussians from $\mathcal{G}_K$ to block $j$ if their contracted positions fall within the block bounds:
        $$ \mathcal{G}_{K_j} = \{ G_k \in \mathcal{G}_K \mid \mathbf{b}_{j,min} \le \text{contract}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_k) < \mathbf{b}_{j,max} \} $$
    *   **Handle Empty Blocks:** If a block $j$ contains fewer than $K_{min}$ Gaussians, enlarge its bounds $\mathbf{b}_{j,min}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{j,max}$ iteratively until $|\mathcal{G}_{K_j}| > K_{min}$. This adjustment is used for data assignment but does not restrict the final spatial filtering [S1].

4.  **Assign Training Data (Poses):**
    For each block $j$ and camera pose $\tau_i$, determine if the pose should be included in the training set for that block using two criteria combined via logical OR (sum of binary indicators):
    *   **Criterion 1 (Visibility/Contribution):** Calculate the SSIM loss between the image rendered by the full global prior $\mathcal{G}_K$ and the image rendered by the global prior excluding block $j$ ($\mathcal{G}_K \setminus \mathcal{G}_{K_j}$). If the loss exceeds $\epsilon$, the block contributes significantly to the view.
        $$ \boldsymbol{B}_1(\tau_i, \mathcal{G}_{K_j}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } L_{SSIM}(I_{\mathcal{G}_K}(\tau_i), I_{\mathcal{G}_K \setminus \mathcal{G}_{K_j}}(\tau_i)) > \epsilon \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    *   **Criterion 2 (Proximity):** Check if the camera position $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{\tau_i}$ (world coordinate of pose $\tau_i$) falls within the block's contracted bounds.
        $$ \boldsymbol{B}_2(\tau_i, \mathcal{G}_{K_j}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \mathbf{b}_{j,min} \le \text{contract}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{\tau_i}) < \mathbf{b}_{j,max} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    *   **Final Assignment:**
        $$ \boldsymbol{B}(\tau_i, \mathcal{G}_{K_j}) = \boldsymbol{B}_1(\tau_i, \mathcal{G}_{K_j}) + \boldsymbol{B}_2(\tau_i, \mathcal{G}_{K_j}) $$
        If $\boldsymbol{B} > 0$, pose $\tau_i$ is assigned to block $j$ [S1].

5.  **Parallel Finetuning:**
    *   Initialize each block's Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_{K_j}$ with the corresponding subset of the coarse global prior $\mathcal{G}_K$.
    *   Train each block in parallel using the original, uncontracted coordinate space.
    *   Use the assigned poses for each block.
    *   Optimize the loss function, which is a weighted sum of L1 loss and SSIM loss, following the standard 3DGS approach [S1].

6.  **Post-processing and Fusion:**
    *   After training, filter the Gaussians in each block $\mathcal{G}_{K_j}$ to retain only those whose positions $\mathbf{p}_k$ lie within the block's original spatial bounds (not the contracted bounds).
    *   Concatenate the filtered Gaussians from all blocks to form the final model $\mathcal{G}_{final}$ [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def citygs_training_pipeline(images, poses, initial_points, epsilon, k_min, lambda_l1, lambda_ssim):
    """
    images: List of image tensors
    poses: List of camera pose matrices (world-to-camera)
    initial_points: COLMAP point cloud (positions, colors)
    epsilon: SSIM threshold for data assignment
    k_min: Minimum Gaussian count for empty block handling
    lambda_l1, lambda_ssim: Loss weights
    """
    
    # Step 1: Generate Coarse Global Prior
    # Train initial_points with all images for 30,000 iterations
    global_prior = train_gaussians(initial_points, images, poses, iterations=30000)
    
    # Step 2: Contract Global Prior
    # Find bounding box of foreground in global_prior
    p_min, p_max = compute_bounding_box(global_prior.positions)
    
    # Normalize positions to [-1, 1]
    normalized_positions = (global_prior.positions - p_min) / (p_max - p_min) * 2 - 1
    
    # Apply non-linear contraction
    contracted_positions = contract_positions(normalized_positions)
    
    # Step 3: Partition Space
    # Define J blocks in contracted space [-1, 1]^3
    blocks = create_even_partitions(contracted_positions, num_blocks=J)
    
    # Assign primitives to blocks
    block_primitives = {}
    for j, block in enumerate(blocks):
        # Select Gaussians whose contracted positions are within block bounds
        mask = (block.min <= contracted_positions) & (contracted_positions < block.max)
        block_primitives[j] = [g for g, m in zip(global_prior, mask) if m]
        
        # Handle empty blocks: enlarge bounds until count > k_min
        # Note: This modifies the assignment logic for data, not the final spatial filter
        while len(block_primitives[j]) < k_min:
            block.expand_bounds()
            # Re-assign primitives based on expanded bounds
            mask = (block.min <= contracted_positions) & (contracted_positions < block.max)
            block_primitives[j] = [g for g, m in zip(global_prior, mask) if m]

    # Step 4: Assign Data (Poses)
    block_data = {}
    for j, block in enumerate(blocks):
        assigned_poses = []
        for i, pose in enumerate(poses):
            # Criterion 1: SSIM difference
            # Render image with full global prior
            img_full = render(global_prior, poses[i])
            # Render image with global prior excluding block j
            # Note: global_prior \ block_primitives[j]
            prior_without_j = [g for g in global_prior if g not in block_primitives[j]]
            img_without_j = render(prior_without_j, poses[i])
            
            ssim_loss = compute_ssim_loss(img_full, img_without_j)
            b1 = 1 if ssim_loss > epsilon else 0
            
            # Criterion 2: Camera position proximity in contracted space
            # Get world position of camera from pose
            cam_pos_world = get_camera_world_position(pose)
            # Normalize and contract camera position
            cam_pos_norm = (cam_pos_world - p_min) / (p_max - p_min) * 2 - 1
            cam_pos_contracted = contract_positions(cam_pos_norm)
            
            b2 = 1 if (block.min <= cam_pos_contracted) & (cam_pos_contracted < block.max) else 0
            
            if b1 + b2 > 0:
                assigned_poses.append(i)
        block_data[j] = assigned_poses

    # Step 5: Parallel Finetuning
    finetuned_blocks = []
    for j in range(J):
        # Initialize block with coarse prior subset
        # Note: Training happens in original uncontracted space
        block_gaussians = copy_gaussians(block_primitives[j])
        
        # Train using assigned poses
        for epoch in range(num_finetune_epochs):
            for idx in block_data[j]:
                image = images[idx]
                pose = poses[idx]
                
                # Render current block
                rendered_img = render(block_gaussians, pose)
                
                # Compute loss
                l1_loss = compute_l1_loss(rendered_img, image)
                ssim_loss = compute_ssim_loss(rendered_img, image)
                loss = lambda_l1 * l1_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_loss
                
                # Backpropagate and update block_gaussians
                update_gaussians(block_gaussians, loss)
        
        # Step 6: Post-processing
        # Filter Gaussians to keep only those within original spatial bounds of the block
        # Note: Use original bounds, not contracted ones. 
        # Assuming blocks were defined in contracted space, we need to map back or 
        # store original bounds. The source says "filter out ... contained within its spatial bound".
        # We assume block bounds are stored in original space or mapped back.
        # For implementation, we use the bounds derived from the contracted partition 
        # but evaluated in original space? No, the source says "spatial bound".
        # Given the contraction is for partitioning, the final filter likely uses the 
        # original coordinate bounds corresponding to the contracted block.
        # Let's assume we have original bounds for each block.
        original_block_bounds = map_contracted_to_original(block.min, block.max, p_min, p_max)
        
        filtered_gaussians = [g for g in block_gaussians 
                              if (original_block_bounds.min <= g.position) & 
                                 (g.position < original_block_bounds.max)]
        finetuned_blocks.append(filtered_gaussians)

    # Concatenate all blocks
    final_gaussians = concatenate(finetuned_blocks)
    return final_gaussians

def contract_positions(positions):
    contracted = []
    for p in positions:
        inf_norm = max(abs(p.x), abs(p.y), abs(p.z))
        if inf_norm <= 1.0:
            contracted.append(p)
        else:
            # (2 - 1/||p||_inf) * (p / ||p||_inf)
            scale = (2.0 - 1.0 / inf_norm) / inf_norm
            new_p = Vector3(p.x * scale, p.y * scale, p.z * scale)
            contracted.append(new_p)
    return contracted
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Empty Blocks:** Extremely uneven Gaussian distributions may result in blocks with no assigned primitives. The method handles this by enlarging block bounds during data assignment to ensure sufficient training data, preventing overfitting [S1].
*   **Precision:** The contraction function uses the infinity norm. Floating-point precision in the contraction step should be maintained to ensure consistent boundary checks [S1].
*   **Boundary Definitions:** The final filtering step uses "spatial bounds." It is critical to distinguish between the contracted bounds used for partitioning and the original coordinate bounds used for final filtering to avoid discarding valid Gaussians [S1].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** The initial coarse prior training takes $O(K \cdot T_{prior} \cdot N)$, where $K$ is the number of Gaussians, $T_{prior}$ is iterations, and $N$ is the number of images. The block-wise finetuning is parallelized, reducing wall-clock time significantly compared to sequential training, though total compute remains high.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the coarse global prior and all block primitives. Memory usage scales with the scene size but is manageable due to block-wise processing [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables parallel training of large scenes; the global prior reduces geometric inaccuracies and floating artifacts; direct concatenation simplifies fusion [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a high-quality initial point cloud; the contraction and partitioning may introduce slight discontinuities at block boundaries if not handled carefully (though the prior mitigates this) [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Large-scale scenes where standard 3DGS training is too slow or memory-intensive [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Small scenes where the overhead of partitioning and prior generation is unnecessary [S1].

## Background

The CityGS Training Pipeline was introduced in the paper "CityGaussian: Real-Time High-Quality Large-Scale Scene Rendering with Gaussians" by Y. Liu et al. [S1]. It addresses the challenges of training 3D Gaussian Splatting on large-scale urban environments, where standard methods suffer from long training times and fusion artifacts. The method builds on the original 3DGS framework [S1] and integrates with the Level of Detail (LoD) rendering pipeline described in the same paper [S1].
