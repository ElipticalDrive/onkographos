# CityGS-X

Also called: Anchor-Based Octree 3D Gaussian Splatting (Baseline), CityGS-X backbone, UrbanGS

## Overview
CityGS-X is a large-scale urban reconstruction method that extends block-based partitioning with a geometry-aware architecture, refining block boundaries according to building footprints to achieve high accuracy [S1, unofficial]. It builds on parent methods like CityGaussian and VastGaussian by replacing fixed grids with an anchor-based octree where visible anchors generate "Neural Gaussians" via shared MLPs [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Sparse point cloud from Structure-from-Motion (SfM); camera poses and intrinsics [S2, unofficial].
**Outputs:** Rasterized image; optimized anchor features and Gaussian parameters [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
1. **Octree:** Branching factor $b$. Levels $l \in \{0, 1, \dots, L_{max}\}$, where $l=0$ is the coarsest resolution [S2, unofficial]. Voxel side length at level $l$ is $v_l = v_0 / b^l$, where $v_0$ is determined by the scene scale [S2, unofficial].
2. **Anchors:** Each non-empty voxel at every level instantiates one anchor at its center, yielding $N$ anchors organized into $L_{max} + 1$ levels [S2, unofficial].
    * **Layout per anchor $a_i$:**
        * $f_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$: Learnable feature vector.
        * $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Position at voxel center.
        * $l_i$: Level index.
        * $\{\delta^k_i\}_{k=1}^K$: Set of $K$ learnable offset vectors.
        * Per-anchor scaling, rotation, and opacity parameters (base attributes) [S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Octree Construction:** Build an octree with branching factor $b$ over the scene bounding volume from a sparse point cloud obtained from Structure-from-Motion [S2, unofficial].
2. **Anchor Instantiation:** Instantiate an anchor at the center of each non-empty voxel at every level, yielding $N$ anchors organized into $L_{max} + 1$ levels indexed by $l \in \{0, 1, \dots, L_{max}\}$ [S2, unofficial].
3. **Visibility Filtering:** Apply Level-of-detail filtering: retain anchor at level $l_i$ only if $l_i \leq \hat{l}(p_i, c)$, where $c$ is the current training camera and $\hat{l}$ depends on anchor-to-camera distance and a scene-dependent reference distance [S2, unofficial]. Apply view-frustum culling to remove anchors outside the camera frustum, resulting in visible anchor set $V$ [S2, unofficial].
4. **Neural Gaussian Generation:** For each visible anchor, generate $k$ Neural Gaussians whose attributes are decoded by three shared MLPs ($MLP_{op}$, $MLP_{cov}$, $MLP_{col}$) from the anchor feature $f_i$, viewing direction $d_i$, and camera-relative distance [S2, unofficial].
5. **Rasterization:** Rasterize the resulting Gaussians via differentiable tile-based splatting to produce the final image [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
* $b$: Octree branching factor. Not given in the sources.
* $L_{max}$: Maximum octree depth. Not given in the sources.
* $v_0$: Initial voxel side length. Determined by scene scale [S2, unofficial].
* $K$: Number of Neural Gaussians per anchor. Not given in the sources.
* $d$: Dimension of anchor feature vector $f_i$. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases:**
* **Empty Voxels:** Anchors are only instantiated in non-empty voxels, reducing memory overhead [S2, unofficial].
* **Far-away Anchors:** LoD filtering prevents rendering sub-pixel Gaussians for distant anchors, improving performance [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Rendering time depends on the number of visible anchors $|V|$ and the number of Neural Gaussians $K$ per anchor, scaling as $O(|V| \cdot K)$ [S2, unofficial].
* **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the total number of anchors $N$ and the feature dimension $d$, scaling as $O(N \cdot d)$ [S2, unofficial].
* **Strengths:** High accuracy in urban reconstruction due to geometry-aware block refinement and efficient rendering via LoD and frustum culling [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
* **Limitations:** Relies on SfM sparse point cloud for initialization, which may be sparse in textureless urban areas [S2, unofficial]. Independent optimization of features across levels may lead to inconsistencies [S2, unofficial].
* **When to use:** Large-scale urban scenes with distinct building facades and road surfaces [S1, unofficial].
* **When not to use:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
CityGS-X originates from the need to scale 3D Gaussian Splatting to city-scale environments [S1, unofficial]. It builds on block-based partitioning methods like VastGaussian and CityGaussian, which divide scenes into overlapping tiles [S1, unofficial]. CityGS-X extends this by incorporating a geometry-aware architecture to refine block boundaries according to building footprints [S1, unofficial]. It serves as the baseline for "Anchor-Based Octree 3D Gaussian Splatting" methods, where anchors in an octree generate Gaussians via neural networks [S2, unofficial]. Semantic segmentation for facade and road allocation is attributed to the sibling method UrbanGS, not CityGS-X [S1, unofficial].
