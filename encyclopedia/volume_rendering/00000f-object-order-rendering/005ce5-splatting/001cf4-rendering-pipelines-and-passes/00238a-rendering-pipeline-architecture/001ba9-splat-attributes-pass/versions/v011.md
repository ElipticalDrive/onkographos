# Splat Attributes Pass

Also called: Blending Pass, G-Buffer Pass

## Overview
The Splat Attributes Pass is the fragment processing stage in hardware-accelerated surface splatting that reconstructs continuous attributes from overlapping splats into a reconstruction buffer (G-Buffer) [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of correctly accumulating per-fragment data (such as normals and colors) from translucent, overlapping point sprites by applying a weighted blending scheme based on radial distance and depth proximity [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard alpha blending by incorporating a depth-proximity falloff factor to prioritize splats closer to the visible surface [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The pass processes each fragment generated by the rasterization of a splat (point sprite) [S1, unofficial]. It calculates a composite weight for the splat based on its position within the sprite and its depth relative to the scene geometry, then accumulates weighted attributes into the G-Buffer textures [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Splat Data:** Per-splat attributes including center position, radius, color, and linear depth in view space [S1, unofficial].
*   **Depth Buffer:** The current depth buffer containing the depth of the closest visible surface at each pixel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Viewport Size:** The dimensions of the render target, used to normalize fragment coordinates for depth buffer sampling [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   **G-Buffer Textures:**
    *   `g_normal` (vec4, location 0): Stores the accumulated weighted normal vector in the RGB channels [S1, unofficial].
    *   `g_color` (vec4, location 1): Stores the accumulated weighted color in the RGB channels and the accumulated weight in the Alpha channel [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The G-Buffer is implemented as two 2D textures (or render targets):
1.  `g_normal`: Format `RGBA` (or `RGB` with unused alpha), type `float`. Layout: `xyz` = weighted normal sum, `w` = unused or padding [S1, unofficial].
2.  `g_color`: Format `RGBA`, type `float`. Layout: `rgb` = weighted color sum, `a` = weight sum [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   `epsilon`: A small constant used to normalize the depth-proximity falloff. Typical value: `0.25` [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Fragment Position Calculation:** Calculate the 2D offset of the fragment from the center of the point sprite in screen space [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{offset} = \text{gl\_PointCoord} - (0.5, 0.5) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

2.  **Radius Check:** Calculate the squared magnitude of the offset [S1, unofficial].
    $$ m^2 = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{offset} $$
    If $m^2 > 0.25$, discard the fragment [S1, unofficial].

3.  **World-Space Offset Conversion:** Convert the screen-space offset to a world-space offset proportional to the splat's radius [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{world\_offset} = \mathbf{p}_{offset} \times 2 \times r $$
    where $r$ is the splat radius [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Depth Offset Calculation:** Calculate the depth offset from the center of the splat sphere to the fragment's position on the sphere surface [S1, unofficial].
    $$ d_{offset} = \sqrt{r^2 - \mathbf{p}_{world\_offset} \cdot \mathbf{p}_{world\_offset}} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

5.  **Fragment Depth in View Space:** Calculate the depth of the fragment on the splat surface in view space by subtracting the depth offset from the linear depth of the splat center [S1, unofficial].
    $$ z_{frag} = z_{linear} - d_{offset} $$
    where $z_{linear}$ is the linear depth of the splat center [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Surface Depth Retrieval:** Sample the depth buffer at the fragment's screen coordinates to get the depth of the visible surface [S1, unofficial].
    $$ z_{surface} = \text{LinearDepth}(\text{Texture}(\text{DepthBuffer}, \frac{\text{gl\_FragCoord.xy}}{\text{ViewportSize}}).x) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

7.  **Occlusion Test:** If the splat fragment is behind the visible surface ($z_{frag} > z_{surface}$), discard the fragment [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Weight Calculation:** Compute two falloff weights and combine them [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Radial Falloff ($w_1$):** Decreases with distance from the splat center [S1, unofficial].
        $$ w_1 = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{m^2}}{0.5} $$
        [S1, unofficial]
    *   **Depth-Proximity Falloff ($w_2$):** Increases as the splat surface approaches the visible surface (smaller gap) [S1, unofficial].
        $$ w_2 = \frac{z_{surface} - z_{frag}}{\epsilon} $$
        where $\epsilon$ is the epsilon parameter (e.g., 0.25) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Combined Weight ($w$):**
        $$ w = w_1 \times w_2 $$
        [S1, unofficial]

9.  **Normal Calculation:** Compute the view-space normal at the fragment's position on the splat sphere [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{p}_{world\_offset}, d_{offset}) $$
    The reference code constructs the normal vector from the world-space offset and the depth offset component [S1, unofficial].

10. **Attribute Accumulation:** Accumulate the weighted attributes into the G-Buffer using additive blending (or manual accumulation in a compute shader) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Normal:**
        $$ \mathbf{g\_normal.xyz} \leftarrow \mathbf{g\_normal.xyz} + w \cdot \mathbf{n} $$
        [S1, unofficial]
    *   **Color:**
        $$ \mathbf{g\_color.rgb} \leftarrow \mathbf{g\_color.rgb} + w \cdot \mathbf{c} $$
        where $\mathbf{c}$ is the splat color [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Weight:**
        $$ \mathbf{g\_color.a} \leftarrow \mathbf{g\_color.a} + w $$
        [S1, unofficial]

### Pseudocode
```python
import math
import numpy as np

def linear_depth(depth_value):
    """Converts depth buffer value to linear view space depth [G]."""
    return depth_value

def sample_texture(depth_buffer, coords):
    """Samples the depth buffer texture at normalized coordinates [G]."""
    return depth_buffer[coords]

def compute_splat_fragment(gl_PointCoord, radius, linearDepth, color, depth_buffer, viewport_size, epsilon, gl_FragCoord):
    # 1. Fragment position calculation
    from_center_pt_spc = np.array(gl_PointCoord) - 0.5

    # 2. Radius check
    mag_sq = np.dot(from_center_pt_spc, from_center_pt_spc)
    if mag_sq > 0.25:
        return None  # Discard fragment

    # 3. World-space offset conversion
    from_center_wld_spc = from_center_pt_spc * 2.0 * radius

    # 4. Depth offset calculation
    depth_offset = math.sqrt(radius * radius - np.dot(from_center_wld_spc, from_center_wld_spc))

    # 5. Fragment depth in view space
    depth = linearDepth - depth_offset

    # 6. Surface depth retrieval
    tex_coords = np.array(gl_FragCoord) / viewport_size
    surface_depth = linear_depth(sample_texture(depth_buffer, tex_coords))

    # 7. Occlusion test
    if depth > surface_depth:
        return None  # Discard fragment

    # 8. Weight calculation
    w1 = 1.0 - (math.sqrt(mag_sq) / 0.5)
    w2 = (surface_depth - depth) / epsilon
    weight = w1 * w2

    # 9. Normal calculation
    normal = np.array(list(from_center_wld_spc) + [depth_offset])
    normal = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)

    # 10. Attribute accumulation values
    normal_contrib = weight * normal
    color_contrib = weight * color
    weight_contrib = weight

    return normal_contrib, color_contrib, weight_contrib
```

## Subtopics

### Front-to-Back Blending Setup
This configuration sets up the blending state for the Splat Attributes Pass to ensure correct accumulation of translucent splats [S2, unofficial]. It involves clearing the color buffer to RGBA(0,0,0,0) to ensure zero initial alpha, disabling depth testing, and enabling blending with separate blend factors: RGB uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`, and Alpha uses `GL_ONE_MINUS_DST_ALPHA` / `GL_ONE`, with the blend equation set to `GL_FUNC_ADD` for both [S2, unofficial]. This setup is typically used when rendering instanced quads representing splats [S2, unofficial].

### sRGB to Linear Composite Pass
A full-screen triangle pass that reads an intermediate sRGB-blended splat accumulation texture, converts it to linear space, and outputs the result for premultiplied alpha blending onto the final SceneColor buffer [S3, unofficial]. It takes parameters including an intermediate texture (`IntermediateTexture`) and a sampler (`IntermediateSampler`) [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot F)$, where $N$ is the number of splats and $F$ is the average number of fragments per splat (determined by the splat radius and screen resolution) [G]. The depth buffer sampling and discard operations are constant time per fragment [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \cdot H)$ for the G-Buffer textures, where $W$ and $H$ are the viewport dimensions [G].
*   **Performance:** The pass is highly parallelizable and benefits from hardware-accelerated rasterization and blending [G]. The depth test and discard logic can reduce the effective workload if splats are largely occluded [G].
*   **Strengths:** Provides physically plausible attribute reconstruction (normals, colors) for surface splatting by considering depth proximity [S1, unofficial]. It is efficient as it leverages standard GPU fragment processing [G].
*   **Limitations:** The accuracy of the normal and color reconstruction depends on the density of splats and the correctness of the depth buffer [G]. If splats are too sparse, the G-Buffer may contain gaps or inaccurate normals [G].
*   **When to use:** When surface splatting is required for rendering point clouds or Gaussian splats, and accurate per-pixel normals and colors are needed for lighting or further processing [G].
*   **When not to use:** If only a simple color accumulation is needed without normal reconstruction, or if the depth buffer is not available or unreliable [G].

## Background
The Splat Attributes Pass is part of the rendering pipeline for surface splatting, a technique used to render point clouds and Gaussian splats as smooth surfaces [S1, unofficial]. The method of using depth-proximity falloff to weight attributes is described in the context of point cloud rendering using OpenGL [S1, unofficial]. The broader context of splatting as a volume rendering technique involves treating volumetric data as geometric primitives rasterized onto the screen [S1, S3, unofficial]. The specific implementation details, including the G-Buffer layout and weight calculation, are derived from open-source point cloud rendering projects [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
