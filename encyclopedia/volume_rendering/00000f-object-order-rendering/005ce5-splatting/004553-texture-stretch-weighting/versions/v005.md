# Texture Stretch Weighting

Also called: wTS Calculation

## Overview
Texture Stretch Weighting ($w_{TS}$) is a weighting term used in point-based neural rendering to compensate for artifacts introduced by weighted average pooling when handling view-dependent effects [S1]. It specifically penalizes pixels at grazing angles, thereby increasing the influence of more front-facing views in the final composite [S1]. It operates as a scalar weight modifier rather than a geometric projection technique, distinguishing it from sibling methods like EWA Splatting [S1, 1069-ewa-splatting.md].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** The covariance matrix of the splat kernel $G$ for a point reprojected into the novel view [S1].
*   **Output:** A scalar weight $w_{TS}$ representing the penalty for texture stretch [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix derived from the projection of the point's 3D covariance into the image plane [G]. Let the two eigenvalues be $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ [G].

**Formula:**
The weight is calculated as the ratio between the two eigenvalues of the covariance matrix [S1]:
$$ w_{TS} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{minor}}}{\lambda_{\text{major}}} $$
Where $\lambda_{\text{major}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{minor}}$ are the larger and smaller eigenvalues, respectively [G].

**Steps:**
1. Obtain the 2D covariance matrix for the splat kernel $G$ at the current pixel location in the novel view [S1].
2. Compute the two eigenvalues of the covariance matrix [G].
3. Calculate $w_{TS}$ using the ratio of the two eigenvalues [S1].
4. Combine $w_{TS}$ with the covariance smoothing weight $w_{CS}$ and the probabilistic depth weight $w_{PD}$ to form the final weight $w$ [S1]:
    $$ w = w_{CS} \cdot w_{TS} \cdot w_{PD} $$

**Parameters:**
*   No explicit tuning parameters are defined for $w_{TS}$ itself; it is derived directly from the geometry [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Splat:** If the covariance matrix has zero extent, the ratio is undefined. A small epsilon is added to the denominator to prevent division by zero [G].
*   **Grazing Angles:** As the view becomes grazing, the splat stretches, causing the ratio to approach zero. This drives $w_{TS}$ toward 0, effectively discarding the contribution of that point/view, which is the intended behavior [S1].

## Subtopics

### Final View Weighting
This is not a separate algorithmic variant but the aggregation step where $w_{TS}$ is multiplied with other weights [S1]. The final weight $w$ is the product of the covariance smoothing weight $w_{CS}$, the texture stretch weight $w_{TS}$, and the probabilistic depth weight $w_{PD}$ [S1]. This product is used in the weighted average pooling operation to encode inductive bias for visibility and allow for temporal smoothing [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The computation is performed per splat per pixel and requires constant time and minimal memory overhead [G].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** It effectively mitigates artifacts in weighted average pooling caused by view-dependent effects by down-weighting stretched (grazing) splats [S1]. It improves temporal stability in interactive scenarios [S1].
*   **Limitations:** It relies on the accuracy of the covariance estimation. It does not handle occlusion directly, which is handled by $w_{PD}$ [S1].

**When to use it:**
Use this method when performing point-based neural rendering with per-view optimization where view-dependent effects are present and weighted average pooling is used to aggregate features [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Georgios Kopanas, Julien Philip, Tobias Leimkühler, and George Drettakis in the paper "Point-Based Neural Rendering with Per-View Optimization" [S1]. It addresses the issue that standard weighted average pooling can lead to artifacts when view-dependent effects are present, by introducing a penalty for texture stretch [S1].
