# Analytic Cylinder Shading


## Overview
Analytic Cylinder Shading is a technique for rendering streamlines as volumetric entities by computing the average color integrated across the width of a projected cylinder [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of representing line-based flow data with perceived volume and lighting interaction without the computational cost of rendering fully modeled 3D tubes [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from [Streamline Rendering (Lines)](7718-streamline-rendering-lines.md) by explicitly modeling the cross-sectional integration of light, and from [Cylindrical Shading via Texture Map](#cylindrical-shading-via-texture-map) by using closed-form mathematical evaluation rather than texture lookup [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Streamline geometry (start point, end point, direction).
*   Viewing direction vector ($\mathbf{v}$).
*   Lighting direction vector ($\mathbf{l}$).
*   Surface albedo or base color ($C_{base}$).

**Outputs:**
*   Shaded color ($C_{shaded}$) for the streamline pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   No complex data structures required. The method relies on vector operations and scalar integration results. [G] The streamline is typically treated as a line segment in screen space for the projection.

**Steps:**
1.  **Project Geometry:** Determine the screen-space projection of the streamline segment.
2.  **Compute Directions:** Calculate the normalized viewing vector $\mathbf{v}$ and lighting vector $\mathbf{l}$ at the streamline's position.
3.  **Evaluate Analytic Formula:** Compute the average color by integrating the shading model across the cylinder's width. The integration accounts for the variation of the dot product between the surface normal and light/view directions across the circular cross-section of the projected cylinder.
    *   The formula derives from integrating the Lambertian or similar shading model over the visible hemisphere of the cylinder's cross-section [S1, unofficial].
    *   Let $N$ be the surface normal at a point on the cylinder. The integration averages $C_{base} \cdot (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})$ over the width.
    *   [G] The specific analytic solution involves terms dependent on the angle between the viewing direction and the streamline axis, and the lighting direction relative to the surface.
4.  **Alternative Lookup:** If precomputed, retrieve the average color from a precomputed table or texture based on the angles between $\mathbf{v}$, $\mathbf{l}$, and the streamline tangent [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Cylinder radius: Implicitly defined by the visual width of the streamline. Not given in the sources.
*   Lighting model: Typically Lambertian or Phong, but the analytic integration assumes a specific model. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Viewing Angle:** The analytic formula may become singular or inaccurate when the viewing direction is parallel to the streamline axis, as the projected width approaches zero.
*   **Lighting Extremes:** If the light source is behind the viewer or the streamline, the integration bounds change.
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision in the integration formula must be handled to avoid artifacts at grazing angles.

## Subtopics

### Cylindrical Shading via Texture Map
Simulates cylindrical shading on streamlines using a texture map instead of fully modeled geometry [S1, unofficial]. This variant avoids the computational cost of analytic integration or 3D tube rendering by mapping a pre-shaded cylindrical texture onto the 2D line primitive [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when performance is critical and the lighting conditions are static or can be approximated by a fixed texture. It differs from the analytic method by relying on spatial sampling rather than mathematical evaluation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per streamline segment for the analytic method, assuming constant-time vector operations. The texture map variant is also $O(1)$ but involves texture lookup overhead.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the analytic method. The texture map variant requires memory for the precomputed texture table.
*   **Performance:** Faster than rendering fully modeled cylindrical tubes, which require more vertices and rasterization overhead [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides a volumetric appearance with low computational cost; avoids the "flat" look of simple lines.
*   **Limitations:** Requires precomputation or complex analytic formulas; may not handle dynamic lighting changes as easily as fully modeled geometry without recomputation.
*   **When to use:** When visualizing flow fields with perceived volume is desired but full 3D tube rendering is too expensive.
*   **When not to use:** When dynamic, complex lighting interactions are required that cannot be captured by the analytic approximation or static texture.

## Background
The technique was discussed by Schussman [19] and cited in the context of scientific visualization [S1, unofficial]. It is part of the broader effort to improve the visual clarity of streamline rendering by adding perceived volume and lighting cues [S1, unofficial]. The method is an alternative to rendering lines as fully modeled tubes or using simple texture maps [S1, unofficial].
