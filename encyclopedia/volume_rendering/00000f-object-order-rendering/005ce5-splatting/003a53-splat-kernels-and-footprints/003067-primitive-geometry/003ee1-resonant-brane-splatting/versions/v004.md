# Resonant Brane Splatting

Also called: Brane, Brane Primitive, Brane Splatting, Resonant Brane

## Overview
Resonant Brane Splatting (RBS) is a feedforward arbitrary-scale super-resolution (ASR) framework that replaces flat, single-color 2D Gaussians with 'Branes' [S1, unofficial]. Branes are expressive primitives that emit spatially varying colors by augmenting a Gaussian envelope with internal Gaussian-Hermite modes, allowing a single primitive to model local contrast, sharp edges, and complex textures without requiring dense overlapping splats [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard splatting where color is constant over the footprint, RBS assigns an RGB-valued coefficient to each Hermite mode, making the emitted color vary spatially within the primitive footprint [S1, unofficial]. This differs from 2DGH [54], which uses Gaussian-Hermite functions to modulate the spatial opacity/support profile while keeping color as a separate constant splat attribute [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Low-resolution image $I_{LR} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ and a continuous target scale factor $s \in \mathbb{R}^+$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Super-resolved image $I_{SR} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_{SR} \times W_{SR} \times 3}$ where $H_{SR} = \lfloor Hs \rfloor$ and $W_{SR} = \lfloor Ws \rfloor$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each Brane primitive $k$ is defined by a state vector $\Phi_k$ predicted from a feature vector $f_k$. The layout is:
$$ \Phi_k = [\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k, \sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}, \rho_k, \alpha_k, \mathbf{C}_{0,0,k}, \dots, \mathbf{C}_{N,M,k}] $$
Fields:
*   $\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k$: Anchoring offsets (float).
*   $\sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}$: Scale parameters (float).
*   $\rho_k$: Rotation angle (float).
*   $\alpha_k$: Opacity (float).
*   $\mathbf{C}_{n,m,k} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Color coefficients for each mode $(n,m)$ (float).

**Parameters:**
*   $N, M$: Maximum degrees of the Gaussian-Hermite modes in the x and y directions respectively. Typical value: $N = M = 3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $u$: Upsampling factor determining the number of generated primitives ($K = uH \times uW$). Tested values: $\{1, 2, 4\}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $d_{max}$: Bounding box fraction in normalized space used for culling candidate Branes [S1, unofficial].
*   $R_{max}$: Canonical space boundary radius for culling, defined as $\sqrt{2n_{max} + 1}$ where $n_{max} = \max(N, M)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** A neural backbone (e.g., EDSR or RDN with Transformer refinement) extracts a dense feature map $\hat{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{\hat{H} \times \hat{W} \times C}$ from $I_{LR}$ [S1, unofficial]. This map is optionally upsampled by factor $u$ to $F \in \mathbb{R}^{H' \times W' \times C}$, where $H' = u\hat{H}$ and $W' = u\hat{W}$ [S1, unofficial]. Let $K = H'W'$ be the number of primitives [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Prediction:** Each feature vector $f_k \in F$ predicts the full Brane state $\Phi_k$ via MLP heads [S1, unofficial]:
    *   **Anchoring:** The $k$-th Brane is initially anchored at the center of its feature grid cell:
        $$ (x_k^{\text{ref}}, y_k^{\text{ref}}) = \left( \frac{j + 0.5}{W'}, \frac{i + 0.5}{H'} \right) $$
        An MLP head predicts continuous offsets $\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k$:
        $$ (\Delta x_k, \Delta y_k) = \text{MLP}_{\theta_1}(f_k) $$
        Final center coordinates:
        $$ x_k = x_k^{\text{ref}} + \frac{\Delta x_k}{W'}, \quad y_k = y_k^{\text{ref}} + \frac{\Delta y_k}{H'} $$
    *   **Footprint & Opacity:**
        $$ (\sigma_{x,k}, \sigma_{y,k}) = \text{sigmoid}(\text{MLP}_{\theta_3}(f_k)) $$
        $$ \rho_k = \pi \tanh(\text{MLP}_{\theta_4}(f_k)) $$
        $$ \alpha_k = \text{sigmoid}(\text{MLP}_{\theta_5}(f_k)) $$
    *   **Mode Colors:** Given maximum degrees $N$ and $M$, each Brane has $L = (N + 1)(M + 1)$ modes. A dedicated head predicts raw mode colors:
        $$ \tilde{\mathbf{C}}_k = \text{MLP}_{\theta_2}(f_k) \in \mathbb{R}^{3L} $$
        Reshape into $L$ coefficients $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m} \in \mathbb{R}^3$. Apply activation:
        $$ \mathbf{c}_k^{n,m} = \begin{cases} \text{sigmoid}(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m}), & n = 0, m = 0 \\ \tanh(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_k^{n,m}), & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        The zero-order coefficient defines the smooth base color; higher-order coefficients are signed residuals [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Hermite Basis Definition:**
    The normalized 1D Hermite polynomial is:
    $$ \bar{H}_n(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}} H_n(z) $$
    where $H_n(z)$ are physicist's Hermite polynomials defined recursively:
    $$ H_0(z) = 1, \quad H_1(z) = 2z, \quad H_n(z) = 2z H_{n-1}(z) - 2(n-1) H_{n-2}(z) \text{ for } n > 1 $$
    The 2D mode is the tensor product:
    $$ \Psi_{n,m}(x,y) = \bar{H}_n(x)\bar{H}_m(y) $$
    The Gaussian spatial envelope is $E(x, y) = \exp\left(-\frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}\right)$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Rasterization:**
    For each target pixel $(u, v)$ in the output image:
    *   Project to normalized coordinates:
        $$ (u_{\text{norm}}, v_{\text{norm}}) = \left( \frac{u + 0.5}{W_{SR}}, \frac{v + 0.5}{H_{SR}} \right) $$
    *   Compute displacement from Brane center $k$:
        $$ (\delta x_k, \delta y_k) = (u_{\text{norm}} - x_k, v_{\text{norm}} - y_k) $$
    *   Map to local canonical coordinates via inverse rotation and scaling:
        $$ \begin{pmatrix} x'_k \\ y'_k \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{\sigma_{x,k}} & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma_{y,k} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \cos \rho_k & \sin \rho_k \\ -\sin \rho_k & \cos \rho_k \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \delta x_k \\ \delta y_k \end{pmatrix} $$
    *   **Culling:**
        *   Box culling: Skip Brane $k$ if $|\delta x_k| \ge d_{max}$ or $|\delta y_k| \ge d_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Quantum turning point culling: Skip Brane $k$ if $x'_k^2 + y'_k^2 > R_{max}^2$, where $R_{max} = \sqrt{2n_{max} + 1}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Accumulation:** Compute contribution:
        $$ \mathbf{C}(u, v) = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k E(x'_k, y'_k) \sum_{n=0}^N \sum_{m=0}^M \mathbf{c}_{n,m,k} \bar{H}_n(x'_k) \bar{H}_m(y'_k) $$
        Additively splat all active Branes [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Instability:** Higher-order Hermite polynomials $H_n(z)$ grow rapidly with degree $n$. Using normalized Hermite polynomials $\bar{H}_n(z)$ removes the dominant degree-dependent growth, ensuring numerical stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Aliasing:** Training strictly on scales $s \in U(1, 4)$ fails to suppress oscillations when generalizing to smooth regions at higher scales, causing aliasing artifacts due to over-expressing modes [S1, unofficial]. This is mitigated by fine-tuning on a wider scale distribution $s \in U(1, 8)$ or using sparsity loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **Color Clipping:** Hard clipping during additive blending is avoided; end-to-end L1 supervision natively bounds the final rendering within $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Adaptive Mode Culling
A rasterization optimization that skips the evaluation of Hermite basis functions and color accumulation for Branes whose high-order mode coefficients are negligible, thereby reducing computational load without affecting visual quality [S1, unofficial]. For each Brane being rasterized, iterate through its predicted Hermite coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{n,m,k}$. Check if the magnitude of the RGB coefficients for a specific mode $(n, m)$ is less than the threshold $10^{-4}$. If true, skip the Hermite polynomial evaluation and the accumulation of color for that mode [S1, unofficial]. This exploits sparsity induced by fine-tuning or sparsity loss, where unneeded coefficients are driven to zero [S1, unofficial]. Threshold: $10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].

### Sparsity Loss (Resonant Brane Splatting)
An explicit regularization term added to the training objective to enforce sparsity in the Hermite mode coefficients, preventing excessive vibration (aliasing) in smooth regions [S1, unofficial]. During training, compute the L1 norm of all Hermite coefficients where the sum of indices $n+m$ is greater than 0:
$$ L_{\text{sparse}} = \lambda_{\text{sparse}} \cdot \frac{1}{K} \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{n+m>0} \|\mathbf{c}_{n,m,k}\|_1 $$
where $\lambda_{\text{sparse}}$ is the weight, $K$ is the number of channels (RGB), and $\mathbf{c}_{n,m,k}$ are the Hermite coefficients for mode $(n,m)$ in channel $k$ [S1, unofficial]. Parameter: $\lambda_{\text{sparse}} = 0.0005$ yields results virtually identical to fine-tuning; an aggressive value of $0.1$ stifles details [S1, unofficial]. Explicitly enforces energy awareness that is otherwise achieved implicitly through fine-tuning on a wider scale distribution $s \in U(1, 8)$ [S1, unofficial].

### Resonant Brane Splatting Regularization via Fine-Tuning
A training strategy to mitigate aliasing artifacts in smooth regions at large scales by fine-tuning the model on a wider range of scales, allowing it to naturally dampen high-order coefficients [S1, unofficial]. Fine-tune the model using a scale distribution $s \in U(1, 8)$ instead of the initial training range [S1, unofficial]. The model learns to dampen coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{n,m}$ to 0 for $n+m > 0$ in flat/smooth areas, recovering performance [S1, unofficial]. Training strictly on scales $s \in U(1, 4)$ fails to suppress oscillations when generalizing to smooth regions at higher scales, causing aliasing artifacts due to over-expressing modes [S1, unofficial]. Achieves the same 'energy awareness' as explicit sparsity loss but implicitly through exposure to smoother targets [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Naive evaluation requires $O(K H_{SR} W_{SR})$ operations [S1, unofficial]. Optimized via CUDA rasterizer with dual culling (bounding box and quantum turning point) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Rmax culling accelerates rendering up to 65% without quality loss, proving Branes resolve complexity without dense overlap [S1, unofficial]. Provides modest inference speedups with zero quality loss via Adaptive Mode Culling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces rendering overhead by modeling high frequencies internally, reducing overlap compared to standard Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. Superior speed-quality trade-off than prior GS methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Predicting excessive modes (e.g., $N=M=7$) in a single pass hampers generalization and causes smooth-region artifacts if not regularized [S1, unofficial]. Single MLP struggles to balance asymmetric distributions (e.g., YCbCr) compared to correlated RGB [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Arbitrary-Scale Super-Resolution where high-frequency detail reconstruction is critical and computational efficiency is desired [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When hardware lacks efficient CUDA support for the custom rasterizer, or when the scene does not benefit from spatially varying color within primitives [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Resonant Brane Splatting (RBS) was introduced as a feedforward Arbitrary-Scale Super-Resolution (ASR) framework [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the expressive Gaussian-Hermite primitive but differs in appearance representation [S1, unofficial]. It is used in ASR, compared against implicit (Meta-SR, LIIF) and explicit (GaussianSR, GSASR) methods [S1, unofficial]. The method was trained on DIV2K using EDSR or RDN as baseline extractors, with features refined by a Transformer [S1, unofficial].
