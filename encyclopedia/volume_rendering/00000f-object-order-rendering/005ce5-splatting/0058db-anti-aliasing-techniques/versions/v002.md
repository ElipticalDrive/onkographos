# Anti-Aliasing Techniques
Also called: Splat Anti-Aliasing, Volume Splatting Anti-Aliasing

## Overview
Anti-aliasing techniques for splatting address the sample-rate aliasing artifacts (pre-aliasing) that occur when the integration grid sampling frequency is insufficient relative to the volume raster during the resampling phase . Unlike volume ray-casting, which performs reconstruction in eye space and naturally adapts kernel size as rays diverge, splatting performs reconstruction in perspective space, requiring explicit mechanisms to maintain the Nyquist criterion . These methods distinguish themselves from sibling techniques by either dynamically scaling splat size and energy for distant voxels [4471-anti-aliasing-for-splatting.md], or by hybridizing polygonal slicing with ellipsoidal splats to handle geometric detail and low-density regions simultaneously [829-splatting-for-anti-aliasing-small-cells.md][1447-threshold-slicing-and-anti-aliasing.md].

## Shared principles
The splatting family shares the fundamental mechanism of treating volumetric data as a set of geometric or parametric primitives that are transformed and rasterized [G].

1.  **Primitive Representation:** The volume is decomposed into primitives [G]. Each primitive $k$ is defined by its position $\mathbf{p}_k$, density/extinction coefficient $g_k$ or $\rho_k$, and a reconstruction kernel $h$ (typically a Gaussian) .
2.  **Transformation:** Primitives are transformed from volume space to screen space using the Model-View-Projection (MVP) matrix or specific factorizations (e.g., shear-warp) . For perspective projections, the kernel shape may be distorted (e.g., EWA splatting) to account for foreshortening .
3.  **Rasterization:** The transformed primitives are projected onto the 2D image plane [G]. The contribution of a voxel $D$ to a screen point $(x,y)$ is determined by the footprint function $F(x,y)$, which is the integral of the 3D kernel along the view direction :
    $$ F(x,y) = \int h(x-x_D, y-y_D, z) \, dz $$
    The intensity written to the pixel is modulated by the voxel density:
    $$ P(x,y) = \text{weight}(x,y) \cdot \rho(D) $$
4.  **Compositing:** Contributions are accumulated to form the final image [G]. This is typically done using alpha blending in back-to-front or front-to-back order to handle transparency correctly . Splatting compositing is performed along sheets (all pixels at once for a set of voxels), whereas ray-casting composites along rays (one sample at a time) .
5.  **Hardware Acceleration:** Most methods in this family rely on GPU rasterization pipelines for performance, enabling real-time interactivity .

## Subtopics

### [Splatting for Anti-aliasing Small Cells](829-splatting-for-anti-aliasing-small-cells.md)
Prevents loss of geometric detail in adaptive mesh volume rendering by replacing small, easily missed polyhedral cells with anti-aliased ellipsoidal splats. It sets itself apart by explicitly using ellipsoidal splats convolved with a Gaussian presampling filter to handle anti-aliasing, rather than relying solely on polygon rasterization. Choose this when rendering unstructured or curvilinear grids with significant cell size variation.

### [Threshold Slicing and Anti-Aliasing](1447-threshold-slicing-and-anti-aliasing.md)
A hybrid technique for curvilinear and unstructured grids that partitions cells based on a scalar value threshold to balance geometric precision and performance. It solves aliasing in large datasets by rendering high-value cells as exact projected polygons and low-value cells as anti-aliased ellipsoidal splats. Choose this when exact boundaries for significant features are required alongside smooth representation of low-density regions.

### [Anti-Aliasing for Splatting](4471-anti-aliasing-for-splatting.md)
Prevents sample-rate aliasing artifacts during resampling by dynamically scaling splat size and attenuating energy for splats beyond a critical distance $k$. It distinguishes itself by maintaining constant energy per unit area on the view plane and satisfying the Nyquist criterion for the integration grid. Choose this for standard volume rasters where perspective projection causes sampling rate mismatches at depth.

## Background
The problem of aliasing in volume rendering, particularly during resampling, was formally addressed in the context of splatting by Westover . The distinction between eye-space reconstruction (ray casting) and perspective-space reconstruction (splatting) highlights the unique challenges of maintaining the Nyquist criterion in splatting-based pipelines . Subsequent developments introduced hybrid approaches combining polygonal slicing with splatting to handle unstructured grids and adaptive meshes, such as those by Kindlmann and Westover  and later refinements by others .
