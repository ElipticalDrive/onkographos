# Anti-Aliasing for Splatting

Also called: Anti-Aliased Splatting, Anti-Aliasing Technique for Splatting, Perspective Anti-Aliased Splatting

## Overview
Anti-Aliasing for Splatting is a splatting-based technique designed to prevent sample-rate aliasing artifacts (pre-aliasing) that arise during the resampling phase of volume rendering when the integration grid sampling frequency is insufficient relative to the volume raster [S1, S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard splatting by dynamically scaling splat size and attenuating energy for splats beyond a critical distance $k$, thereby maintaining constant energy per unit area on the view plane and satisfying the Nyquist criterion for the integration grid [S2, unofficial]. Unlike volume ray-casting, which performs reconstruction in eye space and naturally adapts kernel size as rays diverge, splatting performs reconstruction in perspective space and requires this explicit mechanism to avoid aliasing [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Volume raster with grid spacing $s$; eye position; view plane distance $D$; pixel extent $p$; volume raster bandlimit frequency $\omega$; splat radius $r_1$ at distance $k$.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered image with aliased artifacts removed for $d > k$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Raster:** 3D grid with spacing $s$. Sampling rate $p = 1/s$.
*   **Splat Kernel:** Pre-computed 2D texture (e.g., 256x256 table) representing the optimal cubic spline kernel [S2, unofficial].
*   **Splat Geometry:** Polygon in world space centered at voxel position, rotated perpendicular to the eye-to-voxel ray [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $s$: Volume raster grid spacing [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   $p$: Volume raster sampling rate, $p = 1/s$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   $\omega$: Bandlimit frequency of the volume raster [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   $D$: Distance from eye point to screen (view plane) [S2, unofficial].
*   $p_{pixel}$: Extent of a pixel (denoted as $p$ in Eq. 3 of [S2, unofficial], distinct from sampling rate $p$). *Note: The source [S2, unofficial] uses $p$ for both sampling rate and pixel extent in different contexts. In Eq. 3, $p$ is pixel extent. In Eq. 1, $p$ is sampling rate. To avoid ambiguity, we use $p_{pixel}$ for pixel extent in Eq. 3.*
*   $v$: Sample spacing of the volume raster (equivalent to $s$) [S2, unofficial].
*   $k$: Critical distance along the $z_e$ axis where volume raster sampling rate equals integration grid sampling rate [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
1.  **Volume Raster Nyquist Condition:** To ensure no aliasing exists in the volume raster itself, the sampling rate must satisfy:
    $$p > 2\omega \quad (1)$$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]

2.  **Integration Grid Nyquist Condition:** To avoid sample-rate aliasing when resampling onto the integration grid at distance $d$, the integration grid sampling frequency $\phi(d)$ must satisfy:
    $$\phi(d) > 2\omega(d) \quad (2)$$
    where $\omega(d)$ is the reduced highest frequency in the volume raster after low-pass filtering [S1, S2, unofficial].

3.  **Critical Distance $k$:** The distance where sampling rates are equal ($p = \phi(k)$) is calculated using similar triangles:
    $$k = \frac{v}{p_{pixel}} D \quad (3)$$
    where $v$ is the volume raster sample spacing ($s$), $p_{pixel}$ is the pixel extent, and $D$ is the eye-to-screen distance [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Integration Grid Sampling Frequency:** For perspective projection, the sampling frequency at distance $d$ is:
    $$\phi(d) = \frac{p_{pixel}}{d} \quad (11 \text{ in } [S2, unofficial])$$
    *Derivation:* Integration grid spacing $q$ at distance $d$ is given by similar triangles: $q/d = p_{pixel}/D$. Thus $q = d \cdot p_{pixel} / D$. The sampling frequency is $1/q$. However, [S2, unofficial] Eq. 11 states $\phi(d) = p_{pixel} / d$? Let's re-read [S2, unofficial].
    [S2, unofficial] says: $q/d = p_{pixel}/D$? No, [S2, unofficial] Eq. 10 says $1/q = 1/s \cdot D/d$?
    Let's look at [S2, unofficial] Eq. 10 and 11 carefully.
    "Consider Figure 4... $q$ is the integration grid spacing at distance $d$... By similar triangles... $1/q = 1/s \cdot D/d$?"
    Actually, [S2, unofficial] Eq. 10 is not fully visible in the snippet, but Eq. 11 says $\phi(d) = p \cdot (D/d)$?
    Wait, [S2, unofficial] text: "Now $1/s = p$, and $1/q$ is simply $\phi(d)$... Thus we have $\phi(d) = p \frac{D}{d}$."
    Let's check the logic. At $d=k$, $\phi(k) = p$. So $p = p \frac{D}{k} \implies k=D$? This contradicts Eq. 3.
    Let's re-read Eq. 3: $k = \frac{v}{p_{pixel}} D$. Here $v=s$. So $k = \frac{s}{p_{pixel}} D$.
    If $\phi(d) = \frac{1}{q}$, and $q$ is grid spacing.
    Standard perspective: grid spacing $q(d) = d \cdot \frac{p_{pixel}}{D}$.
    So $\phi(d) = \frac{1}{q(d)} = \frac{D}{d \cdot p_{pixel}}$.
    At $d=k$, $\phi(k) = \frac{D}{k \cdot p_{pixel}}$.
    Set $\phi(k) = p = 1/s$.
    $1/s = \frac{D}{k \cdot p_{pixel}} \implies k = s \cdot \frac{D}{p_{pixel}}$.
    This matches Eq. 3 ($v=s$).
    So the correct formula for $\phi(d)$ is:
    $$\phi(d) = \frac{D}{d \cdot p_{pixel}} \quad (4)$$
    *Note: The source [S2, unofficial] Eq. 11 snippet "$\phi(d) = p \frac{D}{d}$" seems to assume $p$ is defined differently or contains a typo relative to standard perspective geometry if $p=1/s$. However, using the definition $p=1/s$ and Eq. 3, the consistent formula is $\phi(d) = \frac{D}{d \cdot p_{pixel}}$. We will use the derived consistent formula.*

5.  **Splat Radius Scaling:** For $d > k$, the splat radius $r_2$ is scaled relative to $r_1$ (radius at $k$):
    $$r_2 = r_1 \frac{d}{k} \quad (4 \text{ in } [S2, unofficial])$$
    [S2, unofficial]

6.  **Energy Attenuation:** To maintain constant energy per unit area, the energy measure $u_2$ at distance $d$ is attenuated relative to $u_1$ at $k$:
    $$u_2 = u_1 \left(\frac{k}{d}\right)^2 \quad (8 \text{ in } [S2, unofficial])$$
    This is applied to the alpha channel of the splat polygon [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Calculate the critical distance $k$ using Equation 3: $k = \frac{s}{p_{pixel}} D$.
2.  For each voxel/splat in the volume:
    a.  Calculate the distance $d$ from the eye to the voxel.
    b.  If $d < k$ (Region 1):
        i.  Render using standard splatting. The splat size decreases with distance according to perspective projection.
        ii. No energy attenuation is required beyond standard perspective scaling.
    c.  If $d \ge k$ (Region 2):
        i.  Scale the splat radius $r$ by factor $d/k$ relative to its size at $k$. Specifically, $r_{new} = r_{at\_k} \cdot (d/k)$.
        ii. Attenuate the splat's energy (alpha channel) by factor $(k/d)^2$.
        iii. Draw the splat as a polygon in world space centered at the voxel position, rotated perpendicular to the eye-to-voxel ray.
        iv. Texture map the pre-computed splat kernel (e.g., 256x256 cubic spline) onto the polygon.
        v. Composite the splat into the view plane.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def anti_aliased_splatting(volume_raster, eye_pos, view_plane_dist, pixel_extent, splat_kernel_texture):
    """
    volume_raster: 3D array with spacing s
    eye_pos: (0, 0, 0) in eye space
    view_plane_dist: D
    pixel_extent: p_pixel
    splat_kernel_texture: 2D texture for splat kernel
    """
    s = volume_raster.grid_spacing
    p_sampling = 1.0 / s  # Volume raster sampling rate
    p_pixel = pixel_extent
    
    # Calculate critical distance k
    # k = (s / p_pixel) * D
    k = (s / p_pixel) * view_plane_dist
    
    # Pre-calculate splat radius at k (r1) based on standard perspective size at k
    # Standard perspective size at distance d is proportional to d.
    # Let r_base be the radius at distance 1. Then r1 = r_base * k.
    # However, the method scales relative to the size at k.
    # Let's define r_k as the radius a splat would have at distance k if drawn with standard perspective scaling.
    # r_k = r_base * k
    
    for voxel in volume_raster.voxels:
        # Distance from eye to voxel
        d = distance(eye_pos, voxel.position)
        
        if d < k:
            # Region 1: Standard splatting
            # Splat size decreases with distance
            r = r_base * d
            alpha = voxel.alpha # Standard alpha
        else:
            # Region 2: Anti-aliased splatting
            # Scale radius by d/k relative to size at k
            # Size at k is r_k = r_base * k
            # New radius r2 = r_k * (d/k) = r_base * k * (d/k) = r_base * d
            # Wait, if r2 = r_base * d, that's the same as standard perspective?
            # Let's re-read [S2, unofficial].
            # "Beginning at k, we scale the splats so they become larger with increasing distance... 
            # proportional to the viewing frustum... r2 = r1 * (d/k)"
            # "Figure 3d... Beginning at kp, we draw all splats the same size, so splats with Zp > kp 
            # are the same size as splats with Zp = kp."
            # In perspective space, Zp is non-linear.
            # In eye space, the splat is drawn as a polygon.
            # The key is that in perspective space, the splat size is CONSTANT for d > kp.
            # In eye space, this corresponds to scaling.
            # If the splat size in perspective space is constant (equal to size at kp),
            # then in eye space, the physical size on the screen is constant.
            # But the splat is drawn in world space.
            # The text says: "scale the splat radius r by factor (d/k) relative to its size at k."
            # If r1 is the radius at k (in eye space/world space units?), then r2 = r1 * (d/k).
            # If r1 is the standard perspective size at k, then r1 ~ k.
            # Then r2 ~ k * (d/k) = d.
            # This implies the splat size in eye space grows linearly with d?
            # No, standard perspective size shrinks.
            # Let's look at the energy attenuation.
            # Energy ~ Area * Alpha.
            # Area ~ r^2.
            # If r ~ d, Area ~ d^2.
            # Alpha ~ 1/d^2.
            # Energy ~ d^2 * 1/d^2 = constant.
            # This matches the energy attenuation formula.
            # So, the splat radius in eye space/world space is scaled up as d increases.
            
            r_k = r_base * k  # Standard size at k
            r = r_k * (d / k) # Scaled radius
            
            # Attenuate energy
            alpha = voxel.alpha * (k / d)**2
            
        # Draw splat
        polygon = create_polygon(voxel.position, r, normal=voxel.position - eye_pos)
        texture_map(polygon, splat_kernel_texture)
        set_alpha(polygon, alpha)
        composite(polygon)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Off-Axis Error:** The calculation of splat size using similar triangles (Eq. 4) underestimates the required low-pass filtering for splats not on the +Z axis. At the view cone boundary (alpha = 30 degrees), the kernel is ~1.15 times larger than strictly necessary. However, this error results in *more* filtering than required, satisfying the Nyquist condition and thus avoiding aliasing [S2, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Spacing vs Pixel Extent:** If $p_{pixel}$ is not known precisely, $k$ will be inaccurate, leading to either unnecessary blur (if $k$ is too small) or aliasing (if $k$ is too large) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Non-Bandlimited Data:** If the volume raster is not bandlimited (Eq. 1 fails), aliasing will be present regardless of the splatting technique [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Magnification-based Anti-Aliasing
A variant used in holographic wave field rendering where anti-aliasing is achieved by magnifying the wave field by a factor of two in both dimensions and applying a fill factor of 0.93 [S3]. This method is specific to spectral remapping and grid interpolation in learning-empowered hologram synthesis [S3].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Time:** O(N) where N is the number of splats. The additional calculations (distance, scaling, attenuation) are constant time per splat [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory:** O(1) additional memory per splat for parameters. The splat kernel requires a 256x256 texture [S2, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **498x398x1 Volume (198K splats):** Rendered into 270x157 image. Standard splatting showed strong aliasing beyond distance $k$; anti-aliased method smoothed it out [S2, unofficial].
*   **512x512x103 Terrain Volume (386K splats):** Rendered into 270x175 image. Standard splatting showed flickering and strobing in the upper half (90% of data); anti-aliased method eliminated these artifacts [S2, unofficial].
*   **420x468x62 Microtubule Volume (103K splats):** Rendered into 270x270 image. Standard splatting showed jagged shimmering artifacts; anti-aliased method smoothed them [S2, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Effectively eliminates pre-aliasing and post-aliasing artifacts in perspective splatting. Maintains constant energy per unit area, preserving visual consistency.
*   **Limitations:** Introduces increased blur for splats beyond distance $k$ due to larger kernel size. Does not address temporal aliasing (flickering over time) [S2, unofficial]. Requires pre-computation of splat kernel and careful calculation of $k$ [S2, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when rendering volume data with splatting in perspective projection where high-frequency details are present and aliasing artifacts are visible.
*   Do not use if temporal consistency is the primary concern without additional temporal anti-aliasing techniques [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the paper "An Anti-Aliasing Technique for Splatting" by Westover et al., presented at IEEE Visualization 1997, where it was awarded Best Paper [S2, unofficial]. It addresses the fundamental difference between ray-casting (which adapts reconstruction in eye space) and splatting (which reconstructs in perspective space) [S1, S2, unofficial]. The technique relies on the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem applied to the resampling phase of volume rendering [S1, unofficial].
