# FCC/BCC Subsampling via Voronoi Filtering

Also called: Granular 3D Multiresolution Transform, Granular Multiresolution Transform, Lattice-based Subsampling

## Overview
This method provides a 3D multi-resolution scheme that represents volumetric data at resolutions that are powers of two (1/2, 1/4, 1/8 of original), resolving the rigidity of Cartesian schemes that only allow resolution changes by a factor of eight [S1]. It achieves this by subsampling a Cartesian volume onto Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) and Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) lattices using discrete filters designed to match the Nyquist regions of the target lattices [S1]. Unlike standard Cartesian downsampling, which loses information or requires larger kernels, this approach leverages the geometric properties of FCC and BCC lattices to maintain lossless compression for spherically bandlimited signals while reducing sample counts by half and quarter, respectively [S1, Parent].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A volumetric dataset sampled on a 3D Cartesian lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^3$) at resolution $N^3$.
*   Target resolution levels: FCC (1/2 density), BCC (1/4 density), and Cartesian (1/8 density).

**Outputs:**
*   Three volumes stored in compacted-Cartesian memory space:
    1.  FCC volume at $N^3/2$ samples.
    2.  BCC volume at $N^3/4$ samples.
    3.  Cartesian volume at $N^3/8$ samples.

### Data Structures
The data is stored in a 1-dimensional array (or 3D array) representing **compacted-Cartesian-space**.

**Canonical Lattice Spacing:**
*   Cartesian: Distance between neighboring points is 1.
*   FCC/BCC: Distance between neighboring points is 2 [S1].

**Compacted-Cartesian Layout:**
To store non-Cartesian lattices efficiently, coordinates are transformed into a standard Cartesian grid:
1.  **BCC Storage:**
    *   Odd-numbered $z$-slices are shifted by an offset of 1 along the $x$ and $y$ axes to align with even slices.
    *   The $x$ and $y$ axes are compacted (scaled) by a factor of 2.
    *   Result: A standard Cartesian grid where every other voxel corresponds to a BCC point [S1].
2.  **FCC Storage:**
    *   Each $z$-slice is decomposed into two sub-slices.
    *   The second sub-slice is pushed halfway between the current $z$-slice and the one behind it.
    *   All sub-slices are aligned along the $z$-axis.
    *   Scaling is applied to transform into a canonical-Cartesian-lattice.
    *   Result: A standard Cartesian grid where specific indices map to FCC points [S1].

### Mathematical Background

**Lattice Definitions:**
*   **FCC Lattice:** A sub-lattice of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ where the sum of coordinates is even. Density is $1/2$ of Cartesian [S1].
*   **BCC Lattice:** A sub-lattice of $\mathbb{Z}^3$ where all three coordinates have the same parity (all even or all odd). Density is $1/4$ of Cartesian [S1].
*   **Duality:** FCC and BCC are dual lattices of each other [S1].

**Sampling Density:**
The sampling density is the inverse of the volume of the Voronoi cell:
$$ \text{Density} = \frac{1}{\text{Volume}(\text{Voronoi Cell})} $$
*   FCC Voronoi Cell: Rhombic dodecahedron.
*   BCC Voronoi Cell: Truncated octahedron [S1].

**Nyquist Regions:**
To subsample properly, the original spectrum must be band-limited to the Voronoi cell of the *dual* lattice.
*   **FCC Subsampling:** The Nyquist region is the Voronoi cell of the BCC lattice (truncated octahedron). This occupies exactly half the volume of the original cubic spectrum [S1].
*   **BCC Subsampling:** The Nyquist region is the Voronoi cell of the FCC lattice (rhombic dodecahedron). This occupies exactly one-quarter the volume of the original cubic spectrum [S1].

### Filtering Process

**Goal:** Design a bandpass filter whose support in the frequency domain resembles the Voronoi cell of the dual lattice of the subsampling lattice. Ideal filters are band-limited but have infinite spatial support, so compact support filters derived from reconstruction kernels are used [S1].

**Filters:**
1.  **FCC Linear Filter:** Linear filter on the first neighbors cell of the FCC (a cub-octahedron) [S1].
2.  **FCC Cubic Filter:** Convolution of the linear element FCC with itself [S1].
3.  **BCC Linear Filter:** Linear box spline BCC filter whose support is a rhombic dodecahedron [S1].
4.  **BCC Cubic Filter:** Convolution of the linear box spline with itself [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Reconstruct Continuous Volume:**
    Interpolate the original Cartesian volume to a continuous function using a high-quality filter (e.g., tri-cubic B-spline) [S1].

2.  **Band-Limit for FCC (1/2 Resolution):**
    *   Apply a filter to the continuous volume such that the resulting spectrum is contained within the truncated octahedron (BCC Voronoi cell).
    *   Resample the filtered continuous volume onto the FCC lattice points.
    *   *Implementation Note:* The filter size must be chosen to ensure proper band-limiting before resampling [S1].

3.  **Band-Limit for BCC (1/4 Resolution):**
    *   Apply a filter to the continuous volume such that the resulting spectrum is contained within the rhombic dodecahedron (FCC Voronoi cell).
    *   Resample the filtered continuous volume onto the BCC lattice points.
    *   *Implementation Note:* Similar computation to FCC, but with the stricter quarter-volume constraint [S1].

4.  **Cartesian Subsampling (1/8 Resolution):**
    *   Standard Cartesian downsampling (typically by factor of 2 in each dimension) is used for the 1/8 resolution level, as FCC/BCC nesting does not provide a direct 1/8 sub-lattice [S1].

5.  **Coordinate Transformation (Storage):**
    *   Transform the sampled points from canonical-lattice-space to compacted-Cartesian-space.
    *   Map coordinates to 1-dimensional indices for file storage [S1].

def granular_multiresolution_transform(volume_cartesian):
    """
    volume_cartesian: 3D array of shape (N, N, N) on Cartesian lattice.
    Returns:
        volume_fcc: 3D array of shape (N, N, N/2) in compacted-Cartesian space.
        volume_bcc: 3D array of shape (N/2, N/2, N/2) in compacted-Cartesian space.
        volume_cart_1_8: 3D array of shape (N/2, N/2, N/2) in Cartesian space.
    """
    
    # 1. Reconstruct continuous volume from Cartesian samples
    # Use a tri-cubic B-spline filter for high-quality reconstruction
    # This step effectively creates a continuous function f(x,y,z)
    continuous_vol = reconstruct_continuous(volume_cartesian, filter_type='tri_cubic_b_spline')
    
    # 2. Generate FCC samples (1/2 resolution)
    # FCC points are those (x,y,z) in Z^3 where (x+y+z) is even.
    # Canonical spacing is 2.
    fcc_points = generate_fcc_lattice_points(original_size=N, spacing=2)
    
    # Filter for FCC: Band-limit to BCC Voronoi cell (Truncated Octahedron)
    # Use FCC Linear Filter (support: cub-octahedron) or Cubic Filter
    filtered_fcc = apply_filter(continuous_vol, fcc_points, filter_type='fcc_linear')
    
    # Store in compacted-Cartesian space
    volume_fcc = compact_to_cartesian_fcc(filtered_fcc)
    
    # 3. Generate BCC samples (1/4 resolution)
    # BCC points are those (x,y,z) in Z^3 where x,y,z have same parity.
    # Canonical spacing is 2.
    bcc_points = generate_bcc_lattice_points(original_size=N, spacing=2)
    
    # Filter for BCC: Band-limit to FCC Voronoi cell (Rhombic Dodecahedron)
    # Use BCC Linear Filter (support: rhombic dodecahedron) or Cubic Filter
    filtered_bcc = apply_filter(continuous_vol, bcc_points, filter_type='bcc_linear')
    
    # Store in compacted-Cartesian space
    volume_bcc = compact_to_cartesian_bcc(filtered_bcc)
    
    # 4. Generate Cartesian samples (1/8 resolution)
    # Standard downsampling by factor of 2 in each dimension
    volume_cart_1_8 = downsample_cartesian(volume_cartesian, factor=2)
    
    return volume_fcc, volume_bcc, volume_cart_1_8

def generate_fcc_lattice_points(size, spacing):
    """
    Generates FCC lattice points within the volume bounds.
    FCC: sum of coordinates is even.
    """
    points = []
    # Iterate over canonical grid with spacing 2
    # Note: Implementation details of 'generate' depend on specific lattice generation algorithm
    # [G] Standard approach: iterate x,y,z from 0 to size/spacing, check parity
    for x in range(0, size, spacing):
        for y in range(0, size, spacing):
            for z in range(0, size, spacing):
                if (x + y + z) % 2 == 0:
                    points.append((x, y, z))
    return points

def generate_bcc_lattice_points(size, spacing):
    """
    Generates BCC lattice points within the volume bounds.
    BCC: all coordinates have same parity (all even or all odd relative to origin).
    """
    points = []
    for x in range(0, size, spacing):
        for y in range(0, size, spacing):
            for z in range(0, size, spacing):
                # Check if x, y, z are all even or all odd
                if (x % 2 == y % 2 == z % 2):
                    points.append((x, y, z))
    return points

def compact_to_cartesian_fcc(samples):
    """
    Transforms FCC samples to compacted-Cartesian storage.
    1. Decompose each z-slice into two sub-slices.
    2. Push second sub-slice halfway between current and previous z-slice.
    3. Align all sub-slices along z-axis.
    4. Scale to canonical Cartesian.
    """

    for (x, y, z) in samples:

        # Logic:
        # z is split. If z is even, it goes to one set of slices.

def compact_to_cartesian_bcc(samples):
    """
    Transforms BCC samples to compacted-Cartesian storage.
    1. Shift odd z-slices by offset 1 in x and y.
    2. Compact x and y axes by factor of 2.
    """
    # [G] Specific indexing logic from [S1] Figure 5.
    # The output array shape will be (N/2, N/2, N/2) if original was N^3.
    output_shape = (N // 2, N // 2, N // 2)
    output_array = zeros(output_shape)
    
    for (x, y, z) in samples:
        # Determine if z is even or odd
        if z % 2 == 1:
            # Shift odd slices
            x_adj = x - 1
            y_adj = y - 1
        else:
            x_adj = x
            y_adj = y
            
        # Compact by factor of 2
        ix = x_adj // 2
        iy = y_adj // 2
        iz = z // 2
        
        output_array[ix, iy, iz] = samples[(x, y, z)]
        
    return output_array

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Infinite Support:** Ideal subsampling filters are band-limited in frequency but have infinite support in space. Practical implementations must use compact support filters (like the linear/cubic filters described), which may introduce approximation errors [S1].
*   **Girdering Artifacts:** The linear FCC filter can exhibit "girdering" artifacts. Cubic filters or other specialized filters may be required to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Precision:** Ensure that the filter size is sufficient to capture the necessary frequency content for proper band-limiting before resampling [S1].

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Memory:** The method reduces memory usage by half (FCC) and quarter (BCC) compared to the original Cartesian volume at those respective levels. Storage is efficient due to compacted-Cartesian layout [S1].
*   **Time:** The filtering step involves convolution with compact support filters (linear/cubic). The complexity depends on the filter kernel size. Resampling is linear in the number of output samples [S1].

**Performance:**
*   **Quality:** The FCC/BCC pipeline preserves more detail than a comparable Cartesian multi-resolution pipeline at the same reduction ratios (1/2, 1/4) [S1].
*   **Benchmark:** Tested on ML dataset ($40^3$) and Skull dataset ($128^3$). The FCC volume has half the samples, and BCC has quarter samples of the original [S1].

**Strengths:**
*   Allows resolution changes by powers of two (1/2, 1/4, 1/8) in 3D, unlike Cartesian schemes which typically jump by 1/8 [S1].
*   Superior representation accuracy compared to Cartesian resampling [S1].

**Limitations:**
*   Requires complex coordinate transformations for storage (compaction) [S1].
*   Linear FCC filters may produce artifacts [S1].

## Background

**Origin:**
The method was introduced by Alireza Entezari, Tai Meng, Steven Bergner, and Torsten Möller in the paper "A Granular Three Dimensional Multiresolution Transform" (EuroVis 2006) [S1].

**History:**
The work addresses the limitations of separable Cartesian multi-resolution schemes in 3D, which are rigid and only allow resolution changes by powers of eight. By leveraging the properties of FCC and BCC lattices, which are dual to each other and have higher packing densities, the authors created a more flexible multi-resolution framework [S1].

**Usage:**
Used in volume rendering pipelines where efficient multi-resolution representation is needed for ray-casting. It enables rendering of continuous volumes from sampled data on FCC, BCC, and Cartesian lattices using appropriate reconstruction kernels (linear/cubic element FCC, linear/cubic box spline BCC) [S1].
