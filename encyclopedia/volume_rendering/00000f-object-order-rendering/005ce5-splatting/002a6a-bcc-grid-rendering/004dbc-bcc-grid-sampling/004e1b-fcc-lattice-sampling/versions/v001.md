# FCC Lattice Sampling

Also called: FCC Lattice Storage, FCC dataset lattice, FCC lattice, Face Centered Cubic (FCC) Lattice, Face-centered cartesian lattice

## Overview
FCC Lattice Sampling is a 3D sampling method that defines a volume grid as a subset of the Cartesian lattice, offering superior sampling efficiency and optimality for signals sampled at a depressed rate compared to standard Cubic Cartesian (CC) grids. It is distinguished from its sibling [BCC Grid Sampling](13572-bcc-grid-sampling.md) by its optimality for depressed-rate signals, whereas BCC is optimal for band-limited and isotropic signals.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D signal (volume data) defined over spatial coordinates.
*   **Output:** A set of sample points located on the FCC lattice, which can be used for storage, interpolation, or rendering.

**Data Structures:**
The lattice is defined mathematically rather than as a dense array. Implementation requires a generator matrix $G$ or a parity check condition to determine valid sample indices.
*   **Generator Matrix ($G$):** A $3 \times 3$ matrix where $\det(G) \neq 0$.
*   **Lattice Points:** Defined as integer linear combinations of the columns of $G$.

**Formulas:**
The general definition of a 3D lattice $L$ is given by [S1]:
$$ L = \{ G \cdot \mathbf{v} : \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{Z}^3, G \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}, \det(G) \neq 0 \} \quad \text{(Eq 1)} $$

For the FCC lattice, the specific generator matrix $G$ is [S1]:
$$ G = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{(Eq 3)} $$

Alternatively, the FCC lattice $L_{FCC}$ can be defined as the subset of the Cartesian lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$ where the sum of the coordinates is even [S1]:
$$ L_{FCC} = \{ (x, y, z) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : x + y + z \text{ is even} \} \quad \text{(Eq 4)} $$

**Parameters:**
*   **Generator Matrix $G$:** Must be non-singular. The specific matrix above yields the standard FCC lattice.
*   **Applicability:** The FCC lattice is optimal if the frequency spectrum of the signal is spherical [S2, book].

**Algorithm:**
To generate or iterate over FCC lattice points within a bounding box:

```python
def generate_fcc_points(min_x, max_x, min_y, max_y, min_z, max_z):
    """
    Generates all integer coordinates (x, y, z) within the specified bounds
    that satisfy the FCC lattice condition: x + y + z is even.
    """
    points = []
    for x in range(min_x, max_x + 1):
        for y in range(min_y, max_y + 1):
            for z in range(min_z, max_z + 1):
                if (x + y + z) % 2 == 0:
                    points.append((x, y, z))
    return points
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Conditions:** When mapping continuous volume data to the FCC lattice, care must be taken to ensure the bounding box includes all valid lattice points that intersect the volume of interest.
*   **Interpolation:** Standard trilinear interpolation (designed for CC grids) is invalid. Interpolation requires box splines or other basis functions defined on the FCC lattice [S1].

## Subtopics

### Box Spline Interpolation
The standard interpolation method for FCC datasets, providing smooth reconstruction from the sparse samples. It is chosen when high-quality rendering of FCC-stored data is required on GPUs. See [Box Spline Interpolation](link-to-box-spline.md).

### FCC vs BCC Storage
FCC is optimal for depressed-rate signals, while BCC (the dual lattice) is optimal for band-limited isotropic signals. BCC requires ~71% of the samples of a CC grid, while FCC offers similar density benefits with different spectral properties. See [BCC Grid Sampling](13572-bcc-grid-sampling.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Sampling Efficiency:** FCC is more efficient than the Cartesian lattice for sampling [S1]. It provides the densest packing of equal-sized spheres among regular lattices [S2, book].
*   **Optimality:** FCC is optimal for signals sampled at a depressed rate [S1]. BCC is optimal for band-limited and isotropic signals [S1].
*   **Memory:** Reduces the number of samples required compared to a full Cartesian grid, though the exact percentage reduction is not explicitly stated in the sources for FCC alone (BCC is cited as 71% of CC) [S2, book].
*   **Usage:** Suitable for medical and scientific datasets, GPU ray casting, and mobile volume rendering [S1].

## Background
The FCC lattice was introduced in the context of volume rendering and signal processing as an alternative to the Cartesian lattice. It is based on the mathematical property that it provides the densest sphere packing in 3D space [S2, book]. The connection to box splines was established by de Boor et al. [S1][S2, book]. The method has been applied in real-time GPU iso-surface ray casting [S1] and mobile volume rendering [S1].
