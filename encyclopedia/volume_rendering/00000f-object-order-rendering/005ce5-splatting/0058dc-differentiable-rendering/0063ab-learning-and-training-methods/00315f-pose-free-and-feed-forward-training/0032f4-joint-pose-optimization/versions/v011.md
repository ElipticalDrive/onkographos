# Joint Pose Optimization

Also called: BARF, Unposed images methods

## Overview
Joint Pose Optimization (JPO) refers to methods that simultaneously optimize camera poses and the scene representation (neural or Gaussian) to eliminate the dependency on pre-computed external Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines [S1]. By estimating pose and performing novel-view synthesis from unposed images using only photometric loss, these methods relax the strict requirement for known camera parameters [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on incremental approaches or mono-depth priors, JPO treats pose and geometry as co-variables in a single differentiable optimization loop [S1].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on differentiable rendering to compute gradients from the rendered image to the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics) and/or network weights [S1, 6136-instantsplat.md, 6464-pixelsplat.md]. The rendering process remains differentiable with respect to the scene representation [S1, 6136-instantsplat.md, 6464-pixelsplat.md]. Loss functions typically combine photometric reconstruction error, such as $\mathcal{L}_{L1}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}$, with perceptual or structural metrics like $\mathcal{L}_{MS-SSIM}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{LPIPS}$ [6136-instantsplat.md][6464-pixelsplat.md].

## Subtopics

### [1D Signal Alignment for Joint Optimization Analysis](8423-1d-signal-alignment-for-joint-optimization-analysis.md)
1D Signal Alignment is a simplified analytical framework that reduces the 3D joint optimization of camera pose and scene reconstruction into a 1D signal alignment problem to analyze the effect of signal spectrum on convergence. It serves as a pilot study to understand the local phenomena of joint alignment, specifically how high-frequency components in the signal spectrum can cause gradient noise and local optima traps during iterative optimization. Unlike the full 3D method, this 1D counterpart isolates the spectral properties of the signal to demonstrate that Gaussian filtering can mitigate sign-alternating issues in the optimization gradient.

### Iterative Pose Optimization Network (IPO-Net)
IPO-Net is a variant that extracts features $F_r$ and $F_t$ from reference images $I_r$ and target image $I_t$ using a Shared Image Encoder (ResNet backbone pre-trained with DINO augmented with FPN) [S2]. It iteratively updates pose and depth for $K$ iterations using a Conv-GRU module: $T_{r \rightarrow t}^{(k)}=T_{r \rightarrow t}^{(k-1)}+\Delta T_{r \rightarrow t}$ and $D_t^{(k)}=D_t^{(k-1)}+\Delta D_t$, where $\Delta T$ and $\Delta D$ are predicted differences [S2].

## Background
Joint Pose Optimization emerged as a solution to the limitations of traditional pipelines that depend on off-the-shelf pose estimation methods, which increase inference time and may fail in texture-less regions or when images lack overlap [S1]. Early works proposed jointly optimizing camera poses and neural representations for both static [HJS∗ 23,WWX∗ 21,CL23a,LMTL21,SCW∗ 25] and dynamic settings [LGM∗ 23] [S1]. However, these initial methods still required rough pose initialization and were limited to small motions [S1]. Alternative solutions followed incremental approaches, leveraging mono-depth priors [BWL∗ 23] or temporal continuity [FLK∗ 24] to optimize camera parameters [S1]. More recent extensions have enabled generalizable representations [CL23b,HJS∗ 23,XLC∗ 25], allowing joint pose prediction and representation learning from sparse image collections [S1]. Ye et al. [YLX∗ 25] demonstrated that it is possible to efficiently estimate pose and perform novel-view synthesis from unposed images and intrinsic camera parameters using only photometric loss, though this approach is limited to static scenes [S1].
