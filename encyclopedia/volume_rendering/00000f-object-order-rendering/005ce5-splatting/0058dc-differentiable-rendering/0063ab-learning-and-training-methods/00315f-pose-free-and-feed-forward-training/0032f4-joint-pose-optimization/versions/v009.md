# Joint Pose Optimization

Also called: BARF, Unposed images methods

## Overview
Joint Pose Optimization (JPO) eliminates the dependency on external Structure-from-Motion pipelines by simultaneously optimizing camera poses and the scene representation from unposed images [S1]. Methods in this family estimate pose and perform novel-view synthesis using only photometric loss [S1].

## Shared principles
This family builds on the differentiable rendering framework and loss formulation defined in [Pose-free and Feed-forward Training](6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md).

## Subtopics

### [1D Signal Alignment for Joint Optimization Analysis](8423-1d-signal-alignment-for-joint-optimization-analysis.md)
A simplified analytical framework used to study the convergence behavior of joint camera pose and scene reconstruction optimization. It reduces the complex 3D joint optimization problem into a 1D signal alignment task to analyze how the signal spectrum affects the gradient landscape. Unlike the full 3D method, this approach isolates the "local phenomenon" of joint alignment, revealing that high-frequency signals cause gradient noise and local optima, which can be mitigated by Gaussian filtering.

## Background
Dependence on off-the-shelf pose estimation methods increases inference time and may fail in texture-less regions or when images lack overlap [S1]. To eliminate the need for known camera parameters, several works propose to jointly optimize camera poses and neural representation, both in static [HJS∗ 23, WWX∗ 21, CL23a, LMTL21, SCW∗ 25] and dynamic settings [LGM∗ 23] [S1]. However, these methods still require rough pose initialization and are limited to small motions [S1]. Alternative solutions follow incremental approaches, leveraging mono-depth prior [BWL∗ 23] or temporal continuity [FLK∗ 24] to optimize camera parameters [S1]. Several works have extended this idea to generalizable representations, enabling joint pose prediction and representation learning from sparse image collections [CL23b, HJS∗ 23, XLC∗ 25] [S1]. Ye et al. [YLX∗ 25] demonstrated that it was possible to efficiently estimate pose and perform novel-view synthesis from unposed images and intrinsic camera parameters using only photometric loss, though this approach is limited to static scenes [S1].
