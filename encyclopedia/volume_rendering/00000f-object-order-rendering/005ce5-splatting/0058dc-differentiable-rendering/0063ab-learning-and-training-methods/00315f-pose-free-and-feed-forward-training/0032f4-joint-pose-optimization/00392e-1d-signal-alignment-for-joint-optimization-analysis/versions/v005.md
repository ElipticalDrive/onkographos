# 1D Signal Alignment for Joint Optimization Analysis

Also called: 1D Signal Alignment, 1D Signal Alignment Pilot Study

## Overview
1D Signal Alignment is a simplified analytical model used to investigate the local convergence behavior of joint camera pose and scene reconstruction optimization [S1][S2, unofficial]. It reduces the complex 3D joint optimization problem into a 1D signal alignment task to analyze how the signal spectrum affects gradient stability [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike full 3D joint optimization methods which suffer from high-frequency spectral noise causing local optima, this method demonstrates that applying a Gaussian filter to the signal spectrum stabilizes gradients and ensures convergence to the global optimum within a specific initial range [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Ground truth 1D signal $f_{GT}$: Continuous, bounded, with finite support [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Translated versions $f_1, f_2$: Generated by translating $f_{GT}$ by unknown parameters $p_1, p_2$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Gaussian kernel $N$: Used for spectral filtering (default variance $\sigma^2 = 4$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Estimated signal $g$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Estimated translation parameters $q_1, q_2$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Relative pose error $u = (p_1 - p_2) - (q_1 - q_2)$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method operates on continuous 1D signals [S1][S2, unofficial]. For implementation, these are discretized into arrays [G].
*   **Signal Array:** 1D array representing $f_{GT}$, $g$, $f_1$, $f_2$ [G].
*   **Parameters:** Scalars $q_1, q_2$ (or their difference $u$) [G].
*   **Frequency Domain:** Arrays for Fourier transforms $\mathcal{F}[f_{GT}]$ and $\mathcal{F}[N]$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Define Ground Truth and Targets:**
    Define the ground truth signal $f_{GT}(x)$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. Generate two target signals $f_1$ and $f_2$ by translating $f_{GT}$ by random offsets $p_1$ and $p_2$:
    $$ f_i(x) = f_{GT}(x - p_i), \quad i \in \{1, 2\} $$
    where the translation operator is $W_{1d}(f, p)(x) = f(x - p)$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

2.  **Initialize Estimates:**
    Initialize the estimated signal $g(x)$ and the estimated translation parameters $q_1, q_2$ [G].

3.  **Define Reconstruction Loss:**
    The loss function $L_{1d}$ measures the squared $L_2$ distance between the translated estimate and the targets:
    $$ L_{1d}(g, q_1, q_2) = \sum_{i \in [1,2]} \int \| W_{1d}(g, q_i)(x) - f_i(x) \|^2 dx $$
    Substituting the translation definition:
    $$ L_{1d}(g, q_1, q_2) = \sum_{i \in [1,2]} \int \| g(x) - f_{GT}(x - p_i + q_i) \|^2 dx $$
    The global optimum is achieved when $q_1 - q_2 = p_1 - p_2$ and $g = W_{1d}(f_{GT}, p_1 - q_1)$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

4.  **Spectral Analysis of Gradient:**
    The gradient of the loss with respect to the relative pose error $u = (p_1 - p_2) - (q_1 - q_2)$ is derived in the frequency domain [S1][S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{d L_{1d}}{du} = \int \| \mathcal{F}[f_{GT}] \|^2 \cdot H(u, k) \, dk $$
    where $k$ is the wavenumber, $\mathcal{F}[f_{GT}]$ is the Fourier transform of the ground truth signal, and:
    $$ H(u, k) = 4\pi k \sin(2\pi k u) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

5.  **Apply Gaussian Filtering (Mitigation):**
    High-frequency components cause $H(u, k)$ to alternate in sign and grow in magnitude, leading to noisy gradients and local optima [S1][S2, unofficial]. To mitigate this, convolve $f_{GT}$ with a Gaussian kernel $N$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. This is equivalent to modulating $H(u, k)$ by the squared magnitude of the kernel's Fourier transform [S1][S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{d \tilde{L}_{1d}}{du} = \int \| \mathcal{F}[f_{GT}] \|^2 \cdot \tilde{H}(u, k) \, dk $$
    where:
    $$ \tilde{H}(u, k) = \| \mathcal{F}[N] \|^2 \cdot H(u, k) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

6.  **Iterative Optimization:**
    Perform gradient descent on $g, q_1, q_2$ (or directly on $u$) using the filtered gradient $\frac{d \tilde{L}_{1d}}{du}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Formulas and Definitions

*   **Translation Operator:**
    $$ W_{1d}(f, p)(x) = f(x - p) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

*   **Loss Function:**
    $$ L_{1d}(g, q_1, q_2) = \sum_{i=1}^{2} \int \| g(x) - f_{GT}(x - p_i + q_i) \|^2 dx $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

*   **Unfiltered Gradient Kernel:**
    $$ H(u, k) = 4\pi k \sin(2\pi k u) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

*   **Filtered Gradient Kernel:**
    $$ \tilde{H}(u, k) = \| \mathcal{F}[N] \|^2 \cdot H(u, k) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

*   **Optimality Condition:**
    $$ q_1 - q_2 = p_1 - p_2 \quad \text{and} \quad g = f_{GT}(x - (p_1 - q_1)) $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Gaussian Variance:** The variance of the Gaussian kernel $N$ is set to 4 in the pilot study [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Convergence Radius:** With variance 4, gradient descent converges to $u=0$ if the initial magnitude $|u| < 6.0$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **High-Frequency Signals:** Signals with spreading high-frequency spectra cause $H(u, k)$ to alternate signs rapidly as $k$ increases [S1][S2, unofficial]. This results in large, noisy gradients that trap optimization in local optima [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Initial Pose Error:** Without filtering, the "well-behaved" region of the gradient is narrow [S1][S2, unofficial]. If the initial relative pose error $u$ is large, the optimizer may fail to converge [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Saddle Points:** A suitable learning rate is required to prevent getting stuck on saddle points, although the filtered region is quasi-convex [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np

def gaussian_kernel_1d(size, variance):
    """
    Creates a 1D Gaussian kernel.
    Args:
        size: Length of the kernel.
        variance: Variance (sigma^2) of the Gaussian.
    Returns:
        Normalized 1D numpy array.
    """
    x = np.linspace(-size//2, size//2, size)
    kernel = np.exp(-x**2 / (2 * variance))
    return kernel / np.sum(kernel)

def compute_gradient_filtered(u, f_gt_fft, variance, n):
    """
    Computes the gradient of the 1D alignment loss with respect to u,
    using Gaussian filtering to stabilize high frequencies.
    
    Args:
        u: Relative pose error (scalar).
        f_gt_fft: FFT of the ground truth signal f_GT.
        variance: Variance of the Gaussian filter N.
        n: Length of the signal array.
    Returns:
        Gradient dL_tilde_1d / du.
    """
    # Frequency domain coordinates (cycles per unit domain)
    k = np.fft.fftfreq(n)
    dk = k[1] - k[0] if n > 1 else 1.0

    # H(u, k) = 4 * pi * k * sin(2 * pi * k * u)
    H = 4.0 * np.pi * k * np.sin(2.0 * np.pi * k * u)

    # Fourier transform magnitude squared of Gaussian kernel N
    # Standard FT of Gaussian with variance sigma^2: ||F[N]||^2 = 2*pi*sigma^2 * exp(-2*pi^2*sigma^2*k^2) [G]
    F_N_sq = 2.0 * np.pi * variance * np.exp(-2.0 * np.pi**2 * variance * k**2)

    # Modulated kernel H_tilde
    H_tilde = F_N_sq * H

    # Integrate ||F[f_GT]||^2 * H_tilde over k
    integrand = np.abs(f_gt_fft)**2 * H_tilde
    gradient = np.sum(integrand) * dk
    return gradient

def optimize_1d_alignment(f_gt, p1, p2, variance=4.0, lr=0.01, steps=1000):
    """
    Simulates the 1D Signal Alignment optimization.
    
    Args:
        f_gt: 1D numpy array representing ground truth signal.
        p1: True translation for signal 1.
        p2: True translation for signal 2.
        variance: Variance of the Gaussian filter.
        lr: Learning rate.
        steps: Number of optimization steps.
    Returns:
        Estimated relative pose error u.
    """
    n = len(f_gt)
    f_gt_fft = np.fft.fft(f_gt)

    # Initialize relative pose error u = (p1 - p2) - (q1 - q2)
    # Sources do not specify initialization strategy [G]
    u = np.random.uniform(-5.0, 5.0)

    for _ in range(steps):
        grad = compute_gradient_filtered(u, f_gt_fft, variance, n)
        u = u - lr * grad
    return u
```

### Subtopics

#### 2D Planar Image Alignment
Extends the 1D analysis to 2D planar images to further validate the spectral effects on joint optimization. It sets itself apart by operating on image planes rather than cross-sectional signals, and is chosen when validating convergence behavior on full 2D renderings. See [2D Planar Image Alignment](6889-2d-planar-image-alignment.md).

#### Joint Pose Optimization
The parent method that 1D Signal Alignment analyzes [6888-joint-pose-optimization.md]. It jointly optimizes camera poses and neural representations. It sets itself apart by handling full 3D scene reconstruction and pose estimation simultaneously, and is chosen when external camera parameters are unavailable. See [Joint Pose Optimization](6888-joint-pose-optimization.md).

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Provides theoretical insight into why coarse-to-fine training schedules work [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Demonstrates that Gaussian filtering stabilizes gradients by suppressing high-frequency noise [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Guarantees convergence to the global optimum if the initial error is within a specific radius ($|u| < 6.0$ with variance 4) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   It is a simplified 1D model and does not directly replace 3D joint optimization [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Requires careful tuning of the Gaussian variance to balance smoothing and gradient accuracy [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Use this analysis to understand convergence issues in joint pose optimization or to justify the use of spectral filtering/coarse-to-fine schedules in 3D neural rendering [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background

*   **Origin:** Introduced in the paper "Improving Robustness for Joint Optimization of Camera Pose and Decomposed Low-Rank Tensorial Radiance Fields" (AAAI 2024) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Purpose:** The authors conducted this pilot study to analyze the effect of the signal spectrum on the joint optimization process, specifically addressing the instability caused by high-frequency components [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Development:** The work builds on the observation that joint optimization is highly influenced by the structural bias of the underlying representation [S2, unofficial]. It aligns with motivations behind coarse-to-fine training schedules in prior works like [Lin et al. 2021] and [Heo et al. 2023] [S1][S2, unofficial].
