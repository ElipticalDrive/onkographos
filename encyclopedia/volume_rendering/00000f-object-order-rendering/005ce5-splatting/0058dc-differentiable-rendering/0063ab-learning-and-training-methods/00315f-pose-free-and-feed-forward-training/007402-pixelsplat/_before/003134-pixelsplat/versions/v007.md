# pixelSplat

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splats from Image Pairs, Epipolar Transformer Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
pixelSplat is a feed-forward model that learns to reconstruct 3D radiance fields parameterized by 3D Gaussian primitives from pairs of images, enabling real-time and memory-efficient rendering and fast 3D reconstruction at inference time [S1]. It solves the problem of local minima inherent to sparse and locally supported representations in generalizable novel view synthesis, where non-differentiable pruning/splitting (used in single-scene optimization) cannot be used because gradients must back-propagate through the representation [S1]. Unlike light-field transformers which do not reconstruct editable 3D scene representations, pixelSplat generates an explicit 3D representation that is renderable in real time, remains editable, and is cheap to train [S1].

## How it works
pixelSplat consists of a two-view image encoder and a pixel-aligned Gaussian prediction module [S1]. The method predicts parameters of pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives from image features, incorporating an epipolar transformer to generalize features between two views [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A pair of images $I_1, I_2$.
*   Associated camera parameters (poses) for the two images. Note that in real-world datasets, these poses are computed using structure-from-motion (SfM) and are scaled by an arbitrary unknown scalar $s_i$ per scene [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A set of 3D Gaussian primitives parameterized by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, opacity $\alpha$, and scale $S$ [S1].

### Data Structures
The model predicts a dense probability distribution over 3D space. For each pixel $u$, the network outputs a feature vector $\mathbf{F}[u]$ which is conditioned on to predict Gaussian parameters [S1]. The specific internal layout of the probability distribution is not explicitly defined beyond being a "dense probability distribution" from which means are sampled [S1].

### Steps
1.  **Image Encoding:** Pass each reference image through a ResNet-50 and a ViT-B/8 vision transformer, both pre-trained using a DINO objective, and sum their pixel-wise outputs to generate features [S1].
2.  **Epolar Feature Encoding:** Perform two rounds of epipolar cross-attention to encode features, utilizing frequency-based positional encoding of depth to resolve scale ambiguity [S1].
3.  **Gaussian Parameter Prediction:** Predict Gaussian parameters using a neural network $f$ conditioned on the feature $\mathbf{F}[u]$ for each pixel $u$, outputting mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, opacity $\alpha$, and scale $S$ [S1]. The prediction is formulated as:
    $$ \phi, \boldsymbol{\delta}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}, \mathbf{S} = f(\mathbf{F}[\mathbf{u}]) $$
    where $\phi$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ relate to the mean position [S1].
4.  **Probabilistic Sampling:** Predict a dense probability distribution over 3D space [S1]. Sample Gaussian means from the predicted dense probability distribution [S1].
5.  **Differentiable Sampling:** Make the sampling operation differentiable via a reparameterization trick that couples the density of a sampled Gaussian primitive to the probability of that location, allowing back-propagation of gradients through the Gaussian splatting representation [S1].
6.  **Implicit Spawning/Deletion:** When receiving a gradient that would increase the opacity of a Gaussian at a 3D location, increase the probability that the Gaussian will be sampled at that location again in the future, implicitly spawning or deleting primitives during training [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Training Resolution:** $256 \times 256$ pixels [S1].
*   **Loss Function:** Combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Perceptual Distance (LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Scale Ambiguity:** SfM reconstructs scenes only up to an arbitrary scale factor $s_i$, making it impossible to predict metric depth from a single image. The model handles this by inferring the scale via an epipolar transformer that finds cross-view pixel correspondences and associates them with positionally encoded depth values [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Gaussian primitives have local support, so gradients vanish if distance to correct location exceeds a few standard deviations. Also, loss may not decrease monotonically along the path to the final location (e.g., traversing empty space). pixelSplat avoids this by using differentiable sampling from a dense probability distribution instead of non-differentiable pruning/splitting [S1].
*   **Out-of-Distribution Views:** Reflective surfaces often appear transparent and Gaussians appear billboard-like when viewed from out-of-distribution camera poses [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** pixelSplat accelerates rendering by 2.5 orders of magnitude compared to state-of-the-art light field transformers [S1]. Compared to the next-fastest baseline, the cost to infer a single scene (encoding) and then render 100 images is about 650 times less [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** pixelSplat uses significantly less memory per ray at training time compared to baselines [S1].
*   **Performance:** Benchmarked on wide-baseline novel view synthesis on the real-world RealEstate10k and ACID datasets [S1]. It outperforms baselines like pixelNeRF and GPNR, producing higher perceptual quality (lower LPIPS) and avoiding ghosting artifacts caused by incorrect depth predictions found in models without epipolar encoding [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time and memory-efficient rendering; fast 3D reconstruction at inference time; explicit 3D representation that is editable; cheap to train [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Struggles with reflective surfaces (transparency) and out-of-distribution views (billboard-like artifacts) [S1].

## Background
pixelSplat was introduced by Charatan, Li, Tagliasacchi, and Sitzmann from MIT and Simon Fraser University/University of Toronto [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2024 [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of generalizable novel view synthesis by integrating 3D Gaussian primitives, which allow for real-time rendering and interpretable 3D structure, into a feed-forward framework [S1].
