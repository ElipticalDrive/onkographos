# FreeSplat

Also called: FreeSplat: Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
FreeSplat is a feed-forward framework that reconstructs geometrically consistent global 3D Gaussian Splatting from long, sparse image sequences to support free-view synthesis across wide view ranges [S1]. It builds on [AnySplat](6137-anysplat.md) by introducing adaptive cost volumes between nearby views and a Pixel-aligned Triplet Fusion (PTF) module to eliminate redundancy in overlapping regions [S1]. Unlike [pixelSplat](52-pixelsplat.md) and [MVSplat](53-mvsplat.md), which are limited to narrow-range interpolation due to heavy transformer backends, FreeSplat uses pure CNN-based backbones and progressive feature aggregation to handle arbitrary view counts and wide baselines [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sparse sequence of $T$ posed RGB images $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ with known camera poses $\{P_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
**Outputs:** Global 3D Gaussian primitives $\{\Sigma, \alpha, s\}$ and depths $d$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Local Gaussian Triplet Map ($F_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{C \times H \times W}$. Contains latent features for each pixel.
*   **Local Gaussian Centers ($\mu_{tl}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{3 \times H \times W}$.
*   **Local Gaussian Weights ($\omega_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{1 \times H \times W}$, values in $(0, 1)$.
*   **Local Gaussian Features ($f_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{(C-1) \times H \times W}$.
*   **Global Gaussian Centers ($\mu_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{3 \times M}$, where $M$ is the number of global Gaussians.
*   **Global Gaussian Weights ($\omega_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{1 \times M}$.
*   **Global Gaussian Features ($f_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{C \times M}$.
*   **Cost Volume ($F_{cv}^t$):** Tensor of shape $R^{K \times 4 \times 4}$ (or similar channel dimension depending on $f_\theta$).

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of nearby views selected for cost volume construction.
*   $K$: Number of virtual depth planes.
*   $[d_{near}, d_{far}]$: Depth range for virtual planes.
*   $\delta$: Ratio threshold for pixel-wise alignment depth difference check. Value not specified in sources [S1].
*   $T$: Number of context views sampled during Free-View Training. Typical value: between 2 and 8 [S1].
*   Learning Rate: $1e-4$ (Adam optimizer) [S1].
*   Image Resolution: $384 \times 512$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **2D Feature Extraction:**
    Feed the sparse sequence of posed images $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ into a shared CNN-based 2D backbone to extract multi-scale embeddings $F_{et}$ and matching features $F_m$ [S1].

2.  **Adaptive Cost Volume Construction:**
    For the current view $I_t$ with pose $P_t$ and matching feature $F_m$:
    a. Select $N$ nearby views $\{I_{tn}\}_{n=1}^N$ based on pose proximity [S1].
    b. Define $K$ virtual depth planes $\{d_k\}_{k=1}^K$ uniformly spaced in $[d_{near}, d_{far}]$ [S1].
    c. Warp nearby view features to each depth plane: $\tilde{F}_m^{tn,k} = \text{Trans}(P_{tn}, P_t)F_m$, where $\text{Trans}$ denotes the transformation from pose $P_{tn}$ to $P_t$ [S1].
    d. Compute the cost volume $F_{cv}^t \in R^{K \times 4 \times 4}$:
       $$F_{cv}^t[k] = f_\theta\left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \cos(F_m, \tilde{F}_m^{tn,k}) \oplus \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \tilde{F}_m^{tn,k} \right)$$
       where $\cos(\cdot)$ is cosine similarity, $\oplus$ is feature-wise concatenation, and $f_\theta(\cdot)$ is a $1 \times 1$ CNN mapping to dimension 1 [S1].

3.  **Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation:**
    Pass $F_{cv}^t$ through a multi-scale encoder-decoder structure (UNet++-like). Concatenate encoder output with multi-scale image features $\{F_{st}\}$ before the decoder to upsample to full resolution. Predict a depth candidates map $D_{ct} \in R^{K \times H \times W}$ and a Gaussian triplet map $F_{lt} \in R^{C \times H \times W}$ [S1].

4.  **Depth Prediction:**
    Predict the depth map $D_t$ via soft-argmax to bound depth between near and far:
    $$D_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \text{softmax}(D_{ct})_k \cdot d_k$$
    [S1].

5.  **Unprojection:**
    Unproject the pixel-aligned Gaussian triplet map $F_{lt}$ to 3D Gaussian triplets $\{\mu_{tl}, \omega_{lt}, f_{lt}\}$, where $\mu_{tl} \in R^{3 \times HW}$ are centers, $\omega_{lt} \in R^{1 \times HW}$ are weights in $(0,1)$, and $f_{lt} \in R^{(C-1) \times HW}$ are features [S1].

6.  **Pixel-wise Triplet Fusion (PTF):**
    Initialize global Gaussians as empty. For $t=1$ to $T$:
    a. **Global Projection:** Project global Gaussian centers $\mu_{t-1}^g \in R^{3 \times M}$ onto the $t$-th view:
       $$p_{tg} := \{x_{tg}, y_{tg}, d_{tg}\} = P_t \mu_{t-1}^g$$
       where $[x_{tg}, y_{tg}, d_{tg}] \in R^{3 \times M}$ are projected 2D coordinates and depths [S1].
    b. **Correspondence:** For the $i$-th local Gaussian with 2D coordinate $[x_{tl}(i), y_{tl}(i)]$ and depth $d_{tl}(i)$, find the intra-pixel global projection set $S_{ti}$:
       $$S_{ti} := \{j \mid \lfloor x_{tg}(j) \rfloor = \lfloor x_{tl}(i) \rfloor, \lfloor y_{tg}(j) \rfloor = \lfloor y_{tl}(i) \rfloor\}$$
       where $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$ is the rounding operator. Find valid correspondence $m_i$:
       $$m_i = \begin{cases} \arg \min_{j \in S_{ti}} d_{tg}(j) & \text{if } |d_{tl}(i) - \min_{j \in S_{ti}} d_{tg}(j)| < \delta \cdot d_{tl}(i) \\ \emptyset & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
       Define the valid correspondence set $\mathcal{F}_t := \{(i, m_i) \mid i = 1, \dots, HW; m_i \neq \emptyset\}$ [S1].
    c. **Gaussian Triplet Fusion (Geometric):** For each pair $(i, m_i) \in \mathcal{F}_t$, compute weighted sum of centers and sum weights:
       $$\mu_{tg}(m_i) = \frac{\omega_{lt}(i)\mu_{tl}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)\mu_{t-1}^g(m_i)}{\omega_{lt}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)}$$
       $$\omega_{t}^g(m_i) = \omega_{lt}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)$$
       [S1].
    d. **Gaussian Triplet Fusion (Feature):** Aggregate aligned local and global Gaussian latent features through a lightweight GRU:
       $$f_{gt}(m_i) = \text{GRU}(f_{lt}(i), f_{t-1}^g(m_i))$$
       Append unaligned local Gaussian triplets to the global set [S1].

7.  **Gaussian Primitives Decoding:**
    Decode global Gaussian triplets into primitives:
    $$\Sigma, \alpha, s = \text{MLP}_d(f_g^T)$$
    $$\mu = \mu_g^T$$
    [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is fast due to pure CNN-based backbones, avoiding the quadratically expensive computations of patch-wise transformers [S1]. On ScanNet with 2 views, FreeSplat-spec takes 0.103s, which is 300x faster than NeuRay (3.103s) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The PTF module reduces the number of Gaussians by around 55.0% compared to methods without it, significantly lowering memory usage for long sequences [S1].
*   **Performance:** On ScanNet with 2 views, FreeSplat-spec achieves PSNR 28.08, SSIM 0.837, LPIPS 0.211 [S1]. With 3 views, PSNR 27.45, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.222 [S1]. With 10 views, FreeSplat-3views achieves View Interpolation PSNR 25.15, SSIM 0.800, LPIPS 0.278, and View Extrapolation PSNR 23.78, SSIM 0.774, LPIPS 0.309 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Supports wide view range and long sequences; efficient CNN backbone; effective redundancy removal via PTF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate depth estimation from cost volumes; performance may degrade if pose estimation is poor [S1].
*   **When to use:** Free-view synthesis of indoor scenes from sparse, uncalibrated or loosely calibrated image sequences [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Narrow-range stereo interpolation where simpler methods like pixelSplat might suffice; scenes with very few views where cost volume construction is ambiguous [S1].

## Background
FreeSplat was introduced in 2024 by Wang et al. in the paper "FreeSplat: Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting Towards Free-View Synthesis of Indoor Scenes" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of existing generalizable 3DGS methods, which are confined to narrow-range interpolation due to heavy backends [S1]. The method was trained on the ScanNet dataset (100 scenes train, 8 test) and evaluated zero-shot on the Replica dataset (8 scenes test) [S1].
