# FreeSplat

Also called: FreeSplat Plus Plus

## Overview
FreeSplat is a feed-forward framework that reconstructs geometrically consistent global 3D Gaussian Splatting from long, sparse image sequences to support free-view synthesis across wide view ranges [S2]. It differs from pixelSplat and MVSplat, which are limited to narrow-range interpolation due to heavy transformer backbones, by using pure CNN-based backbones and a Pixel-aligned Triplet Fusion (PTF) module to handle arbitrary view counts and wide baselines [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sparse sequence of $T$ posed RGB images $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ with known camera poses $\{P_t\}_{t=1}^T$ [S2].
**Outputs:** Global 3D Gaussian primitives $\{\Sigma, \alpha, s\}$ and depths $d$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Local Gaussian Triplet Map ($F_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{C \times H \times W}$ containing latent features for each pixel [S2].
*   **Local Gaussian Centers ($\mu_{tl}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{3 \times H \times W}$ [S2].
*   **Local Gaussian Weights ($\omega_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{1 \times H \times W}$, values in $(0, 1)$ [S2].
*   **Local Gaussian Features ($f_{lt}$):** Tensor of shape $R^{(C-1) \times H \times W}$ [S2].
*   **Global Gaussian Centers ($\mu_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{3 \times M}$, where $M$ is the number of global Gaussians [S2].
*   **Global Gaussian Weights ($\omega_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{1 \times M}$ [S2].
*   **Global Gaussian Features ($f_g$):** Tensor of shape $R^{C \times M}$ [S2].
*   **Cost Volume ($F_{cv}^t$):** Tensor of shape $R^{K \times 4 \times 4}$ (or similar channel dimension depending on $f_\theta$) [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of nearby views selected for cost volume construction [S2].
*   $K$: Number of virtual depth planes [S2].
*   $[d_{near}, d_{far}]$: Depth range for virtual planes [S2].
*   $\delta$: Ratio threshold for pixel-wise alignment depth difference check. Value not specified in sources [S2].
*   $T$: Number of context views sampled during Free-View Training. Typical value: between 2 and 8 [S2].
*   Learning Rate: $1e-4$ (Adam optimizer) [S2].
*   Image Resolution: $384 \times 512$ [S2].

**Steps:**

1.  **2D Feature Extraction:**
    Feed the sparse sequence of posed images $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ into a shared CNN-based 2D backbone to extract multi-scale embeddings $F_{et}$ and matching features $F_m$ [S2].

2.  **Adaptive Cost Volume Construction:**
    For the current view $I_t$ with pose $P_t$ and matching feature $F_m$:
    a. Select $N$ nearby views $\{I_{tn}\}_{n=1}^N$ based on pose proximity [S2].
    b. Define $K$ virtual depth planes $\{d_k\}_{k=1}^K$ uniformly spaced in $[d_{near}, d_{far}]$ [S2].
    c. Warp nearby view features to each depth plane: $\tilde{F}_m^{tn,k} = \text{Trans}(P_{tn}, P_t)F_m$, where $\text{Trans}$ denotes the transformation from pose $P_{tn}$ to $P_t$ [S2].
    d. Compute the cost volume $F_{cv}^t \in R^{K \times 4 \times 4}$:
       $$F_{cv}^t[k] = f_\theta\left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \cos(F_m, \tilde{F}_m^{tn,k}) \oplus \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \tilde{F}_m^{tn,k} \right)$$
       where $\cos(\cdot)$ is cosine similarity, $\oplus$ is feature-wise concatenation, and $f_\theta(\cdot)$ is a $1 \times 1$ CNN mapping to dimension 1 [S1].

3.  **Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation:**
    Pass $F_{cv}^t$ through a multi-scale encoder-decoder structure (UNet++-like). Concatenate encoder output with multi-scale image features $\{F_{st}\}$ before the decoder to upsample to full resolution. Predict a depth candidates map $D_{ct} \in R^{K \times H \times W}$ and a Gaussian triplet map $F_{lt} \in R^{C \times H \times W}$ [S2].

4.  **Depth Prediction:**
    Predict the depth map $D_t$ via soft-argmax to bound depth between near and far:
    $$D_t = \sum_{k=1}^K \text{softmax}(D_{ct})_k \cdot d_k$$
    [S2].

5.  **Unprojection:**
    Unproject the pixel-aligned Gaussian triplet map $F_{lt}$ to 3D Gaussian triplets $\{\mu_{tl}, \omega_{lt}, f_{lt}\}$, where $\mu_{tl} \in R^{3 \times HW}$ are centers, $\omega_{lt} \in R^{1 \times HW}$ are weights in $(0,1)$, and $f_{lt} \in R^{(C-1) \times HW}$ are features [S2].

6.  **Pixel-wise Triplet Fusion (PTF):**
    Initialize global Gaussians as empty. For $t=1$ to $T$:
    a. **Global Projection:** Project global Gaussian centers $\mu_{t-1}^g \in R^{3 \times M}$ onto the $t$-th view:
       $$p_{tg} := \{x_{tg}, y_{tg}, d_{tg}\} = P_t \mu_{t-1}^g$$
       where $[x_{tg}, y_{tg}, d_{tg}] \in R^{3 \times M}$ are projected 2D coordinates and depths [S2].
    b. **Correspondence:** For the $i$-th local Gaussian with 2D coordinate $[x_{tl}(i), y_{tl}(i)]$ and depth $d_{tl}(i)$, find the intra-pixel global projection set $S_{ti}$:
       $$S_{ti} := \{j \mid \lfloor x_{tg}(j) \rfloor = \lfloor x_{tl}(i) \rfloor, \lfloor y_{tg}(j) \rfloor = \lfloor y_{tl}(i) \rfloor\}$$
       where $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$ is the rounding operator. Find valid correspondence $m_i$:
       $$m_i = \begin{cases} \arg \min_{j \in S_{ti}} d_{tg}(j) & \text{if } |d_{tl}(i) - \min_{j \in S_{ti}} d_{tg}(j)| < \delta \cdot d_{tl}(i) \\ \emptyset & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
       Define the valid correspondence set $\mathcal{F}_t := \{(i, m_i) \mid i = 1, \dots, HW; m_i \neq \emptyset\}$ [S2].
    c. **Gaussian Triplet Fusion (Geometric):** For each pair $(i, m_i) \in \mathcal{F}_t$, compute weighted sum of centers and sum weights:
       $$\mu_{tg}(m_i) = \frac{\omega_{lt}(i)\mu_{tl}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)\mu_{t-1}^g(m_i)}{\omega_{lt}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)}$$
       $$\omega_{t}^g(m_i) = \omega_{lt}(i) + \omega_{t-1}^g(m_i)$$
       [S2].
    d. **Gaussian Triplet Fusion (Feature):** Aggregate aligned local and global Gaussian latent features through a lightweight GRU:
       $$f_{gt}(m_i) = \text{GRU}(f_{lt}(i), f_{t-1}^g(m_i))$$
       Append unaligned local Gaussian triplets to the global set [S2].

7.  **Gaussian Primitives Decoding:**
    Decode global Gaussian triplets into primitives:
    $$\Sigma, \alpha, s = \text{MLP}_d(f_g^T)$$
    $$\mu = \mu_g^T$$
    [S2].

import torch.nn as nn

class FreeSplat:
    def __init__(self, num_nearby_views=4, num_depth_planes=64, near=0.1, far=100.0, 
                 feature_dim=64):
        self.N = num_nearby_views
        self.K = num_depth_planes
        self.d_near = near
        self.d_far = far
        self.C = feature_dim
        self.delta = 0.1  # Value not specified in sources
        
        # CNN Backbone for 2D features
        self.backbone = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(3, 64, 7, stride=2, padding=3),
            nn.ReLU()
            # Additional conv layers not specified in sources
        )
        
        # 1x1 CNN for cost volume aggregation
        self.cost_aggregator = nn.Conv2d(2 * 64, 1, 1)
        
        # GRU for feature fusion
        self.gru = nn.GRUCell(input_size=feature_dim - 1, hidden_size=feature_dim - 1)
        
        # MLP for decoding primitives
        self.primitive_decoder = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(feature_dim - 1, 128),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 7) # Sigma(3), Alpha(1), Scale(3)
        )

    def forward(self, images, poses):
        T = len(images)
        H, W = images[0].shape[2], images[0].shape[3]
        
        # 1. 2D Feature Extraction
        features = [self.backbone(img) for img in images]
            
        # Initialize global Gaussians
        global_centers = torch.empty(0, 3)
        global_weights = torch.empty(0, 1)
        global_features = torch.empty(0, self.C - 1)
        
        for t in range(T):
            # 2. Adaptive Cost Volume Construction
            nearby_indices = self.select_nearby_views(t, poses, self.N)
            
            cost_volume = []
            for k in range(self.K):
                d_k = self.d_near + k * (self.d_far - self.d_near) / (self.K - 1)
                warped_to_depth = []
                for n in nearby_indices:
                    w = self.warp_to_depth_plane(features[n], poses[n], poses[t], d_k)
                    warped_to_depth.append(w)
                    
                cos_sim = []
                mean_feat = []
                for w in warped_to_depth:
                    cos_sim.append(F.cosine_similarity(features[t], w, dim=1))
                    mean_feat.append(w)
                    
                cos_sim = torch.stack(cos_sim, dim=0).mean(dim=0)
                mean_feat = torch.stack(mean_feat, dim=0).mean(dim=0)
                
                concat = torch.cat([cos_sim.unsqueeze(0), mean_feat], dim=0)
                cv_k = self.cost_aggregator(concat)
                cost_volume.append(cv_k)
                
            cost_volume = torch.cat(cost_volume, dim=0)
            
            # 3. Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation
            depth_candidates, triplet_map = unet_like_encoder_decoder(cost_volume, features[t])
            
            # 4. Depth Prediction
            softmax_d = F.softmax(depth_candidates, dim=0)
            depth_map = torch.zeros(H, W).to(depth_candidates.device)
            for k in range(self.K):
                d_k = self.d_near + k * (self.d_far - self.d_near) / (self.K - 1)
                depth_map += softmax_d[k] * d_k
                
            # 5. Unprojection
            centers, weights, features_local = self.unproject(triplet_map, depth_map, poses[t], H, W)
            
            # 6. PTF
            if global_centers.numel() > 0:
                projected = self.project_to_view(global_centers, poses[t])
                x_g, y_g, d_g = projected[:, 0], projected[:, 1], projected[:, 2]
            else:
                x_g, y_g, d_g = torch.empty(0), torch.empty(0), torch.empty(0)
                
            new_global_centers = []
            new_global_weights = []
            new_global_features = []
            
            valid_pairs = []
            for i in range(H * W):
                x_l, y_l = (i % W).float(), (i // W).float()
                d_l = depth_map[i // W, i % W]
                
                x_g_int = torch.round(x_g).long()
                y_g_int = torch.round(y_g).long()
                
                mask = (x_g_int == x_l) & (y_g_int == y_l)
                indices_in_pixel = torch.where(mask)[0]
                
                if indices_in_pixel.numel() == 0:
                    continue
                    
                d_g_in_pixel = d_g[indices_in_pixel]
                min_d_g = d_g_in_pixel.min()
                
                if torch.abs(d_l - min_d_g) < self.delta * d_l:
                    best_idx = indices_in_pixel[torch.argmin(d_g_in_pixel)]
                    valid_pairs.append((i, best_idx.item()))
                    
            for i, m_i in valid_pairs:
                w_l = weights[0, i // W, i % W]
                mu_l = centers[:, i // W, i % W]
                f_l = features_local[:, i // W, i % W]
                
                w_g = global_weights[0, m_i]
                mu_g = global_centers[m_i]
                f_g = global_features[m_i]
                
                new_w = w_l + w_g
                new_mu = (w_l * mu_l + w_g * mu_g) / new_w
                new_f = self.gru(f_l, f_g)
                
                new_global_centers.append(new_mu)
                new_global_weights.append(new_w)
                new_global_features.append(new_f)
                
            for i in range(H * W):
                if i not in [p[0] for p in valid_pairs]:
                    new_global_centers.append(centers[:, i // W, i % W])
                    new_global_weights.append(weights[0, i // W, i % W])
                    new_global_features.append(features_local[:, i // W, i % W])
                    
            if new_global_centers:
                global_centers = torch.stack(new_global_centers, dim=0)
                global_weights = torch.stack(new_global_weights, dim=0)
                global_features = torch.stack(new_global_features, dim=0)
            else:
                global_centers = torch.empty(0, 3)
                global_weights = torch.empty(0, 1)
                global_features = torch.empty(0, self.C - 1)

        # 7. Decoding
        decoded = self.primitive_decoder(global_features)
        sigma = decoded[:, :3]
        alpha = decoded[:, 3:4]
        scale = decoded[:, 4:]
        
        return {
            'centers': global_centers,
            'weights': global_weights,
            'features': global_features,
            'sigma': sigma,
            'alpha': alpha,
            'scale': scale,
            'depths': depth_map
        }

    def select_nearby_views(self, t, poses, N):
        # Not specified in sources. Select N views with smallest distance in pose space.
        return list(range(max(0, t - N // 2), min(len(poses), t + N // 2 + 1)))

    def warp_to_depth_plane(self, feat, pose_src, pose_dst, depth):
        # Not specified in sources. Warp feature from src to dst at given depth.
        return feat  # Placeholder for geometric warping logic

    def unproject(self, triplet_map, depth_map, pose, H, W):
        # Not specified in sources. Unproject 2D map to 3D using depth and pose.
        # Returns centers (3, HW), weights (1, HW), features (C-1, HW)
        return torch.empty(3, H*W), torch.empty(1, H*W), torch.empty(self.C-1, H*W)

    def project_to_view(self, centers, pose):
        # Not specified in sources. Project 3D centers to 2D with depth.
        return torch.empty(0, 3)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is fast due to pure CNN-based backbones, avoiding the quadratically expensive computations of patch-wise transformers [S2]. On ScanNet with 2 views, FreeSplat-spec takes 0.103s, which is 300x faster than NeuRay (3.103s) [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The PTF module reduces the number of Gaussians by around 55.0% compared to methods without it, significantly lowering memory usage for long sequences [S2].
*   **Performance:** On ScanNet with 2 views, FreeSplat-spec achieves PSNR 28.08, SSIM 0.837, LPIPS 0.211 [S2]. With 3 views, PSNR 27.45, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.222 [S2]. With 10 views, FreeSplat-3views achieves View Interpolation PSNR 25.15, SSIM 0.800, LPIPS 0.278, and View Extrapolation PSNR 23.78, SSIM 0.774, LPIPS 0.309 [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Supports wide view range and long sequences; efficient CNN backbone; effective redundancy removal via PTF [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate depth estimation from cost volumes; performance may degrade if pose estimation is poor [S2].
*   **When to use:** Free-view synthesis of indoor scenes from sparse, uncalibrated or loosely calibrated image sequences [S2].
*   **When not to use:** Narrow-range stereo interpolation where simpler methods like pixelSplat might suffice; scenes with very few views where cost volume construction is ambiguous [S2].

## Background
FreeSplat was introduced in 2024 by Wang et al. in the paper "FreeSplat: Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting Towards Free-View Synthesis of Indoor Scenes" [S2]. It addresses the limitation of existing generalizable 3DGS methods, which are confined to narrow-range interpolation due to heavy backends [S2]. The method was trained on the ScanNet dataset (100 scenes train, 8 test) and evaluated zero-shot on the Replica dataset (8 scenes test) [S2].
