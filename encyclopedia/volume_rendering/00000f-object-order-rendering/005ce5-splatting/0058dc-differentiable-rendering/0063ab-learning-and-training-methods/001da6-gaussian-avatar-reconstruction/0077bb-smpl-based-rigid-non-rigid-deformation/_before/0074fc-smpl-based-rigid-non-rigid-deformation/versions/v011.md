# SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation


## Overview
This family of methods reconstructs dynamic human avatars by anchoring 3D Gaussians to the surface of a parametric mesh (e.g., SMPL, SMPL-X) and deforming them to a target pose $\theta$ and expression $\beta$ [1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md]. It solves the problem of dynamic human reconstruction by providing a geometric prior that ensures topological consistency and enables real-time novel view synthesis and animation [S1, 1479-gaussian-avatar.md]. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) by its explicit reliance on a parametric mesh for initialization and deformation, and from [Gaussian Avatar](1479-gaussian-avatar.md) by its use of explicit rigid/non-rigid transformation pipelines (LBS + residual) rather than purely feedforward parameter prediction [1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md].

## Shared principles
This family builds on the splatting principles of primitive representation, transformation, rasterization, and compositing [G]. It relies on parametric anchoring, a deformation pipeline combining rigid and non-rigid transformations, covariance update via Jacobian, differentiable rendering, and a photometric/perceptual loss function, as detailed in the parent article [Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction](1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md).

## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Avatar](1479-gaussian-avatar.md)
Gaussian Avatar reconstructs animatable full-body human avatars from video by leveraging the SMPL parametric model as a geometric prior. It solves the problem of dynamic human reconstruction by using a feedforward network to predict Gaussian parameters directly from pose features, enabling real-time novel view synthesis and animation. It is distinguished from siblings like 3DGS-Avatar by its reliance on a feedforward prediction of parameters via pose features rather than explicit per-Gaussian rigid/non-rigid transformation pipelines during inference [1479-gaussian-avatar.md].

### [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md)
3DGS-Avatar reconstructs full-body human avatars by combining 3D Gaussians with rigid and non-rigid transformations of an underlying SMPL model [1480-3dgs-avatar.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by replacing spherical harmonics with a shallow MLP to model per-Gaussian color, enabling pose-dependent appearance modeling [1480-3dgs-avatar.md].

### [Animatable Gaussians](6157-animatable-gaussians.md)
Animatable Gaussians reconstructs animatable 3D human avatars using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) driven by the SMPL-X parametric model. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, real-time renderable human avatars from monocular video by explicitly modeling pose-conditioned deformation offsets. It is distinguished from sibling methods like 3DGS-Avatar, which uses per-Gaussian deformation MLPs, and Gaussian Avatar, which focuses on head-driven avatars using the FLAME model [6157-animatable-gaussians.md].

### [UV-based motion parameterization](11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md)
UV-based motion parameterization models dynamic human deformation by reparameterizing 3D spatial coordinates into 2D surface-level UV coordinates combined with time. It solves the problem of high-dimensional motion field learning by exploiting the prior that human motion occurs primarily at the surface level, reducing the input dimensionality from 4D space-time $(x, y, z, t)$ to 3D surface-time $(u, v, t)$ [11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md]. This approach is distinct from volumetric parameterizations because it leverages the parametric mesh's surface topology to enforce spatial coherence among nearby surface points, resulting in significantly lower memory usage and faster convergence [11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md]. It builds on [SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md) by predicting residual offsets for surface points [11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md].

### [Full-body Avatar Modeling](11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md)
Full-body Avatar Modeling anchors 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and deforms them using parametric body models (e.g., SMPL) to reconstruct dynamic human avatars from multi-view images. It distinguishes itself from head-centric modeling by focusing on full-body skeletal rigidity and secondary non-rigid effects (e.g., muscle motion) rather than fine-grained facial expressions [11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md]. It builds on [SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md) by applying a hybrid deformation strategy specifically optimized for full-body dynamics [11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md].

### [Skeleton Representation for Articulated NeRF](17005-skeleton-representation-for-articulated-nerf.md)
Skeleton Representation for Articulated NeRF (A-NeRF) encodes human shape, appearance, and pose by mapping 3D query points relative to an articulated skeleton structure rather than absolute world coordinates. It solves the problem of modeling dynamic articulated human motion in neural radiance fields by replacing the parametric surface model of SMPL with a pure skeletal hierarchy, allowing the method to be initialized with any skeleton pose estimator [17005-skeleton-representation-for-articulated-nerf.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on SMPL mesh topology for deformation, this approach explicitly incorporates domain knowledge of bone connectivity and static bone lengths to reparameterize query locations and view directions before density and color evaluation [17005-skeleton-representation-for-articulated-nerf.md].

### Skinning Weight Regularization via SMPL Proximity
#### definition
- [S2] Regularization applied to LBS weights W to prevent artifacts with unseen poses by aligning predicted weights with those obtained from SMPL using an l2 loss.
#### step
- [S2] For individual Gaussians pi, retrieve their k=6 nearest vertices on the SMPL mesh.
#### formula
- [S2] The distance-weighted average Ŵi is calculated as: Ŵi = Σ(j∈Ni) (ωj/ω) Wj, where ωj = exp(-||pi - pj|| ||Wi - Wj|| / (2σ^2)) and ω = Σ(j∈Ni) ωj. Ni is the set of k nearest neighbors.
#### parameter
- [S2] k: Number of nearest neighbors on SMPL mesh used for weight query. Value: 6.
#### code
- [S2] We use the efficient CUDA k-nn implementation of PyTorch3D library [56].
#### pitfall
- [S2] Points around the intersection of joints can have ambiguous k-nn results, leading to artifacts if SMPL weights are used directly without learning.

### Spatial-temporal Texture Completion
#### definition
- [S4] A method to generate a texture-completed proxy in canonical human space to resolve severe artifacts caused by missing texture due to occlusion in free-viewpoint rendering.
#### step
- [S4] Use non-rigid deformation to register an up-sampled SMPL model (41330 vertices) with the captured human meshes.
- [S4] For each point on the proxy, find the nearest visible points along with all views and all frames, then assign an interpolation color to this point to generate a canonical human space with fused texture.
- [S4] Render the texture-completed image for the occluded part of human in novel view and blend it with the neural rendering results from Section 4.2 (c).
#### parameter
- [S4] SMPL model vertex count: 41330 vertices.

### Mesh-guided Simulation
#### step
- [S5, unofficial] Deformation: Apply physics-based priors (soft-body, ARAP, SMPL) to the reconstructed mesh to produce a new guide mesh.
- [S5, unofficial] Inheritance: Mesh-adsorbed Gaussians are inherited (adsorbed to their corresponding facets) from the previous state.
- [S5, unofficial] Refinement: Use MPE-Net, RMD-Net, and RGD-Net to yield Final Deformed Gaussians from the new guide mesh and inherited Gaussians.
- [S5, unofficial] Rendering: Render the Final Deformed Gaussians to generate the final image.
- [S5, unofficial] Offset Application: Apply offsets $\Delta v$ calculated by RMD-Net directly to the vertices $v$ of the guide mesh to obtain the optimized deformed mesh.

### Pose transformation matrix derivation
#### definition
- [S6] Represents the human skeleton as joint locations and relative rotations to compute a transformation matrix that maps geometry from a canonical pose to a target pose.
#### formula
- [S6] G_k = A_k(J, θ_t) A_k(J, θ_c)^{-1}
#### parameter
- [S6] K: Number of body parts/joints. Default value is 24 for the SMPL skeleton.
- [S6] θ: Vector of relative rotations θ ∈ R^{3(K+1)×1} = [ω_0^T, ..., ω_K^T] using axis-angle representation.
- [S6] R(ω_i): 3×3 rotation matrix converted from axis-angle vector ω_i via the Rodrigues formula.
- [S6] j_i: The 3D center coordinate of the i-th joint.
- [S6] P(k): Ordered set of parent joints of joint k.

### ICON
#### step
- [S7] Infer SMPL parameters and simultaneously estimate front-view and back-view normal maps using the SMPL model.
- [S7] Infer the SDF for observable and unobservable points using the estimated normal maps.

### Mesh Pose Embedding Network
#### definition
- [S5, unofficial] A network that extracts pose information from a coarse guide mesh to replace timestamp-based temporal cues, enabling simulation without temporal input.
#### step
- [S5, unofficial] Input Preparation: Sample handle vertices $H$ and their normals $N$ using Poisson disk sampling [62] to uniformly sample points, then map to mesh vertices using k-nearest neighbors (KNN).
#### formula

## Background
The integration of parametric body models like SMPL with 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a solution to the instability and ambiguity of reconstructing dynamic humans from monocular or sparse multi-view inputs [S1, 1479-gaussian-avatar.md]. Early works like [Gaussian Avatar](1479-gaussian-avatar.md) and [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md) established the paradigm of anchoring Gaussians to the SMPL mesh to leverage its topological priors [S1, 1479-gaussian-avatar.md, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md]. Subsequent methods such as [Animatable Gaussians](6157-animatable-gaussians.md) and [Full-body Avatar Modeling](11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md) refined the deformation pipeline by explicitly modeling non-rigid residuals to capture muscle motion and clothing dynamics that LBS alone cannot represent [S1, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md, 11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md]. The approach has been extended to head avatars (e.g., [Rig3DGS](17005-skeleton-representation-for-articulated-nerf.md)) and specialized editing tasks (e.g., [PortraitGen](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md)), demonstrating its versatility in high-fidelity, real-time human rendering [S1][S3, unofficial].
