# SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation

Also called: SMPL-based deformation, Parametric Gaussian Deformation

## Overview
This family of methods reconstructs animatable 3D human avatars by anchoring 3D Gaussians to a parametric body model, typically SMPL or SMPL-X [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md, 11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md]. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, pose-controllable avatars from monocular or multi-view video by explicitly modeling the deformation of Gaussians from a canonical rest pose to a target pose [S1, 8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) by incorporating parametric priors for geometric consistency, and from [Gaussian Avatar](1477-gaussian-avatars.md) by providing the specific rigid/non-rigid deformation pipeline that children like [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md) and [Animatable Gaussians](6157-animatable-gaussians.md) implement with varying degrees of explicitness [S1, 1479-gaussian-avatar.md, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md].

## Shared principles
The family shares the following mechanisms, terms, and math, building on the splatting principles of primitive representation, transformation, rasterization, and compositing [23-splatting.md].

**Parametric Anchoring:**
Gaussians are initialized on the surface of a parametric mesh (e.g., SMPL, FLAME) in a canonical rest pose [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md]. Each Gaussian $i$ is associated with a specific triangle $t$ or vertex of the mesh [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md].

**Deformation Pipeline:**
To animate the avatar, Gaussians are deformed from the canonical pose to a target pose $\theta$ and expression $\beta$ [S1, 8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md].
1.  **Rigid Deformation:** The canonical position $\mathbf{p}_i$ is transformed using Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) [S1, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md]:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{rigid} = \sum_{j=1}^{N_{bones}} w_{i,j} \mathbf{T}_j(\theta) \mathbf{p}_i $$
    where $w_{i,j}$ are skinning weights and $\mathbf{T}_j(\theta)$ are bone transformation matrices [S1, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md].
2.  **Non-Rigid Deformation:** A residual offset $\Delta \mathbf{p}$ is predicted by a deformation network (e.g., MLP) conditioned on pose and/or canonical position [S1, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md]. The final position is:
    $$ \mathbf{p}'_i = \mathbf{p}_{rigid} + \Delta \mathbf{p} $$
    Some methods predict parameters directly via a feedforward network, implicitly handling deformation [S1, 1479-gaussian-avatar.md].
3.  **Covariance Update:** The covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ is transformed using the Jacobian $J$ of the deformation map with respect to the canonical position [G]:
    $$ \Sigma'_i = J \Sigma_i J^T + \sigma^2 I $$
    where $\sigma^2 I$ is a small regularization term to ensure positive definiteness [G]. The Jacobian $J$ includes contributions from both LBS and the non-rigid deformation network [G, 11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md].

**Differentiable Rendering:**
The deformed Gaussians are rendered using differentiable 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md, 11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md]. Gradients are computed from the rendered image to the Gaussian parameters and network weights [S1, 6136-instantsplat.md, 6464-pixelsplat.md].

**Loss Function:**
Training minimizes a combination of photometric and perceptual losses [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{L1} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{MS-SSIM} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{L1}$ is the L1 loss between rendered and ground truth images, and $\mathcal{L}_{MS-SSIM}$ is the multi-scale structural similarity loss [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md].

## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Avatar](1479-gaussian-avatar.md)
Gaussian Avatar reconstructs animatable full-body human avatars by anchoring 3D Gaussians to the SMPL parametric mesh prior. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, pose-controllable avatars from monocular video by predicting Gaussian parameters via a feedforward network conditioned on pose features. It sets itself apart from siblings like [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md) by relying primarily on a feedforward network for parameter prediction rather than explicit per-Gaussian deformation MLPs or rigid/non-rigid hybrid transformations for position updates.

### [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md)
3DGS-Avatar reconstructs photorealistic, animatable full-body human avatars by anchoring 3D Gaussians to an underlying SMPL model. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by replacing spherical harmonics with a shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to model per-Gaussian color, enabling the capture of pose-dependent cloth deformation and novel pose generalization.

### [Animatable Gaussians](6157-animatable-gaussians.md)
Animatable Gaussians is a method for reconstructing animatable 3D human avatars from multi-view video by binding 3D Gaussians to an SMPL-X parametric model. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, pose-dependent geometry by using explicit pose-conditioned offsets rather than implicit deformation fields. It is distinguished from siblings like [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md) by its use of explicit offsets instead of per-Gaussian MLPs, and from [Gaussian Avatar](1479-gaussian-avatar.md) by its reliance on the SMPL-X rig rather than FLAME for full-body representation.

### [UV-based motion parameterization](11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md)
UV-based motion parameterization is a method for modeling dynamic human deformation by reparameterizing 3D spatial coordinates into 2D surface-level UV coordinates combined with time. It solves the problem of high-dimensional motion field learning by exploiting the prior that human motion occurs primarily at the surface level, reducing the input dimensionality from 4D space-time $(x, y, z, t)$ to 3D surface-time $(u, v, t)$. This approach is distinct from volumetric parameterizations (e.g., using raw 3D coordinates) because it leverages the parametric mesh's surface topology to enforce spatial coherence among nearby surface points, resulting in significantly lower memory usage and faster convergence.

### [Full-body Avatar Modeling](11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md)
Full-body Avatar Modeling anchors 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and deforms them using parametric body models (e.g., SMPL) to reconstruct dynamic human avatars from multi-view images. It distinguishes itself from head-centric modeling by focusing on full-body skeletal rigidity and secondary non-rigid effects (e.g., muscle motion) rather than fine-grained facial expressions. It builds on [SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md) by applying a hybrid deformation strategy specifically optimized for full-body dynamics.

### [Skeleton Representation for Articulated NeRF](17005-skeleton-representation-for-articulated-nerf.md)
Skeleton Representation for Articulated NeRF (A-NeRF) encodes human shape, appearance, and pose by mapping 3D query points relative to an articulated skeleton structure rather than absolute world coordinates. It solves the problem of modeling dynamic articulated human motion in neural radiance fields by replacing the parametric surface model of SMPL with a pure skeletal hierarchy, allowing the method to be initialized with any skeleton pose estimator. Unlike sibling methods that rely on SMPL mesh topology for deformation, this approach explicitly incorporates domain knowledge of bone connectivity and static bone lengths to reparameterize query locations and view directions before density and color evaluation.

## Background
The approach originates from the integration of parametric human models (SMPL) with neural rendering techniques. Early works like [Gaussian Avatar](1477-gaussian-avatars.md) established the paradigm of anchoring Gaussians to SMPL for animatable avatars [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md]. Subsequent methods such as [3DGS-Avatar](1480-3dgs-avatar.md) and [Animatable Gaussians](6157-animatable-gaussians.md) refined the deformation pipeline, exploring explicit vs. implicit deformation fields and color representation [S1, 1480-3dgs-avatar.md, 6157-animatable-gaussians.md]. The family has expanded to include UV-based parameterization for efficiency [11653-uv-based-motion-parameterization.md] and full-body modeling with secondary effects [11805-full-body-avatar-modeling.md]. It is widely used in virtual try-on, avatar creation, and 3D video editing [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md].
