# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Simultaneous Localization and Mapping, Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to methods that simultaneously estimate camera poses and reconstruct a scene representation using 3D Gaussians or hybrid neural fields, enabling real-time rendering and localization from monocular, stereo, or RGB-D inputs. Unlike offline training methods that assume known camera poses, SLAM methods must jointly optimize the Gaussian parameters and camera trajectory in an online or incremental fashion. This family distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by handling the temporal consistency and pose estimation challenges inherent in continuous video streams, often integrating uncertainty estimation, loop closure, and active exploration strategies.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS-based SLAM involves maintaining a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ and a sequence of camera poses $T$. The system operates in a loop:
1.  **Rendering:** Given current pose estimates, render the scene to produce color $\hat{C}$ and depth $\hat{D}$ images using differentiable rasterization [S1, unofficial][S4].
2.  **Tracking:** Optimize the current camera pose $T_t$ by minimizing a loss between the rendered image $\hat{C}_t$ and the input image $C_t$, often incorporating depth constraints if available [S4].
3.  **Mapping:** Update the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) based on the gradients from the rendering loss. This often includes densification (splitting/cloning) in regions of high error or uncertainty [S1, unofficial][S4].
4.  **Loop Closure/Global Optimization:** Detect revisited locations and optimize the global pose graph and Gaussian map to correct drift [S2].

Key shared concepts include:
*   **Differentiable Rasterization:** The ability to backpropagate pixel errors to Gaussian parameters and camera poses [S1, unofficial].
*   **Uncertainty Estimation:** Quantifying confidence in depth or pose to guide densification and weighting of losses [S1, unofficial][S4].
*   **Incremental Update:** The map is updated frame-by-frame or keyframe-by-keyframe, requiring efficient data structures for Gaussian management [S2].

## Subtopics

### [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)
CG-SLAM is a 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM method that enhances reconstruction quality by integrating an uncertainty map derived from rendered depth into the training process. It distinguishes itself from standard 3DGS SLAM by explicitly modeling depth uncertainty to guide densification and optimization, addressing misalignment issues common in explicit geometry representations.
*   **When to choose:** Use when high-fidelity reconstruction is required in scenes with reflective surfaces or textureless regions where standard depth-based densification fails.
*   **Link:** [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scenes or frames for the purpose of fusion, editing, or SLAM initialization. They solve the problem of establishing spatial and semantic consistency when direct geometric alignment is ambiguous or unavailable. Unlike sibling methods that focus on the continuous representation of a single scene, mapping strategies specifically address the *inter-scene* or *inter-frame* registration step, ranging from naive pixel identity to dense semantic feature matching.
*   **When to choose:** Use when fusing multiple maps, performing cross-scene editing, or initializing SLAM in environments with low geometric texture but high semantic distinctiveness.
*   **Link:** [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)

### [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md)
Semantic-aware mapping integrates semantic information into the SLAM pipeline to improve robustness and map quality.
*   **When to choose:** Use in scenes with repetitive textures or large uniform areas where geometric cues are insufficient.
*   **Link:** [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md)

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation refers to a family of methods that use neural networks to implicitly encode 3D scenes for novel view synthesis or real-time rendering. This article covers two distinct but related implementations found in the source material: **Photon Field Networks** (an implicit method for global illumination in volumetric media) and **3D Gaussian Splatting** (an explicit method using differentiable rasterization of Gaussian blobs). While standard NeRF methods map spatial coordinates and view directions to color and density via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), 3D Gaussian Splatting abandons the implicit field evaluation for real-time performance, storing the scene explicitly as millions of 3D Gaussians. Photon Fields specifically target indirect global illumination in heterogeneous media, whereas Gaussian Splatting focuses on high-fidelity, real-time rendering of general scenes.
*   **When to choose:** Use implicit NeRFs for global illumination effects in volumetric media; use 3DGS for real-time rendering of general scenes.
*   **Link:** [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
Combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting. On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), ATE RMSE (m): ORB-SLAM3 0.052, Ours 3.190. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences. ORB-SLAM achieves best ATE, but combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. Unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S1, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
A neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure. Uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization. Employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly. Integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S2].

### MNE-SLAM
The first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange. Uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module. Introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S2].

### HERO-SLAM
A SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings. Uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S2].

### PIN-SLAM
A globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation. Replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure. Mapping alternates with odometry. Mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S2].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
Initialization: Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates Sg from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model M(c) and agent state s0, append initial image to database {I}. Global Policy: Select next-best-view s*g from goal candidates Sg using criterion: s*g = argmin_{sg ∈ Sg} (λ_cp l_cp(x_sg) - λ_ig E_s(sg)), where x_sg is 3D location of sg. Local Policy: Execute planning optimization (Sec 4.2) to obtain optimized trajectory S* and local next-best view s*. Action: Move agent to s*. Observation: Obtain new image observation I_t. Update Database: Add new observation to database {I}_{i=0}^{t+1}. Mapping Optimization: Optimize DVGO model M_t to M_{t+1} by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume V(E,c). Stop Check: Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold. Fine Stage: After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model M(f) with trained coarse model M_T(c). Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database {I}_i=0^T for N iterations, optimizing every m iterations [S3, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison. 1. Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$. 2. Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity. 3. Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$. 4. Discard the L channel. 5. Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences. Formula: $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth. Parameter: $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S4].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM). Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration. Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent. Compute EIG for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration. Formula: $P_j = arg max_{P_j} tr ( \sum_{x_acq_i \in P_j} H''[y_i_acq|x_acq_i, w*] ) H''[w*|D_train]^{-1}$ [S5].

## Background
The integration of SLAM with 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a natural extension of the original 3DGS paper, which provided a fast, differentiable rendering pipeline but assumed known camera poses. Early works like [S1, unofficial] demonstrated that 3DGS could be used for online SLAM, leveraging its explicit representation for efficient densification. Concurrently, hybrid neural field methods (NeRF-based SLAM) such as [S2] explored implicit representations for robustness. The field has since branched into specialized areas including multi-agent SLAM [S2], active mapping [S3, unofficial][S5], and robust tracking under lighting changes [S4]. The shift from implicit to explicit representations in SLAM aims to combine the robustness of neural fields with the real-time rendering capabilities of Gaussian Splatting.
