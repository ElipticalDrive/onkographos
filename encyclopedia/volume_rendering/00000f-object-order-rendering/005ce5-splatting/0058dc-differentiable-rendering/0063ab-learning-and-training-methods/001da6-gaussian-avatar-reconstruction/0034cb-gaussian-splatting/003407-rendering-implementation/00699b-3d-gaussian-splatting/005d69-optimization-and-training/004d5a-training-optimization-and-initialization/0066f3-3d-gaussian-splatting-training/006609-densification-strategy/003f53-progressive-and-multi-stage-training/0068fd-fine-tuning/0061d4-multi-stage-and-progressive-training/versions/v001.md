# Multi-stage and Progressive Training

Also called: Two-stage Optimization, On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training

## Overview
Multi-stage and Progressive Training encompasses training protocols that decouple optimization into distinct phases or incremental steps to address specific challenges such as training instability, catastrophic forgetting, or the need for real-time scene updates. It solves the problem where simultaneous optimization of coupled parameters (e.g., geometry and quantization bottlenecks) or sequential data acquisition leads to convergence failure or overfitting [S1]. It sets itself apart from single-stage training by isolating foundational representation learning from attribute-specific refinement or by dynamically allocating resources based on image overlap hierarchies [S1].

## Shared principles
The family shares a core mechanism of temporal or structural decoupling. In multi-stage approaches, the optimization domain is split into a "base" phase and an "attribute" phase, where the base parameters are frozen or constrained during the second phase to prevent interference . In progressive approaches, the optimization domain is split temporally, with new data integrated incrementally using hierarchical weighting and adaptive learning rates to balance stability and plasticity [S1]. Both methods rely on specific loss functions (e.g., load-balancing or rate-distortion) to guide the specific phase's objective.

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
Decouples base representation learning from secondary attribute optimization (e.g., compression or materials) to prevent instability. Choose when optimizing complex, coupled parameters like codecs or physically based materials.

### [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)
Optimizes 3DGS fields incrementally as new images arrive, using hierarchical weighting and adaptive learning rates. Choose for sequential SLAM-based reconstruction or real-time updates.

## Background
The concept of decoupling optimization stages originates from the need to stabilize training in complex differentiable rendering pipelines, particularly when introducing non-differentiable bottlenecks like quantization codecs . Progressive training methods emerged from the requirements of dynamic scene reconstruction and SLAM systems, where retraining from scratch is computationally prohibitive [S1].
