# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3DGS Learning, G Splats

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting Training is the iterative optimization process that adjusts the parameters of a set of 3D Gaussian primitives to minimize the photometric difference between rendered images and ground truth views [S2][S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of reconstructing high-fidelity radiance fields from sparse or dense image collections by explicitly optimizing position, rotation, scale, opacity, and appearance (spherical harmonics) [S5, S6, unofficial]. Unlike implicit neural radiance fields (NeRFs) which require per-pixel ray marching and network inference, 3DGS training relies on differentiable rasterization of explicit Gaussian blobs, enabling significantly faster convergence and real-time rendering capabilities [S2][S5, unofficial]. It builds on [Training Optimization and Initialization](13464-training-optimization-and-initialization.md) by defining the specific loss functions, densification heuristics, and gradient propagation mechanics used in the base method.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images with known camera poses (extrinsics $R_{cw}, t_{cw}$ and intrinsics $K$) and initial 3D Gaussian estimates derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S2][S6, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters: positions $\mathbf{p}_i$, rotations $q_i$, scales $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacities $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $h_i$ [S6, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian primitive $i$ stores:
*   $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position.
*   $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Unit quaternion for rotation.
*   $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector (standard deviations).
*   $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
*   $h_i$: Spherical Harmonic coefficients for view-dependent color.
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$: View-independent color (or derived from $h_i$).

**Initialization:**
1.  **Position:** Initialize $\mathbf{p}_i$ using sparse 3D points from SfM (e.g., COLMAP) [S2][S6, unofficial].
2.  **Rotation:** Initialize $q_i$ as unit quaternions (identity or random) [S2].
3.  **Scale:** Initialize $\mathbf{s}_i$ with values between 0 and 1, often derived from the distance to nearest neighbors in the SfM cloud [S2][S6, unofficial].
4.  **Opacity:** Initialize $\alpha_i$ to a small constant (e.g., 0.1) or derived from SfM confidence [S6, unofficial].
5.  **Color:** Initialize $h_i$ (or $\mathbf{c}_i$) using the average color of the SfM points or the first view [S6, unofficial].

**Loss Function:**
The training minimizes a composite loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered image $\gamma$ and ground truth $\gamma_{gt}$ [S6, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}
$$
where $\lambda$ is a weighting factor (typically 0.2) [S6, unofficial]. $\mathcal{L}_1$ is the L1 loss and $\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ is the differentiable Structural Similarity index [S6, unofficial].

**Rendering Forward Pass:**
1.  **Projection:** Transform 3D Gaussians to camera space. Compute the 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'$ in screen space using the Jacobian of the affine approximation of the projection [S6, unofficial].
2.  **Sorting:** Sort Gaussians by depth for each pixel or tile to ensure correct alpha blending [S2].
3.  **Rasterization:** For each pixel $j$, compute the color $\gamma_j$ via alpha blending of sorted Gaussians $i \in N_j$:
    $$
    \gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N_j} \alpha'_{ij} c_i \tau_{ij}, \quad \tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_{kj})
    $$
    where $\alpha'_{ij}$ is the effective opacity of Gaussian $i$ at pixel $j$, and $c_i$ is its color [S6, unofficial].
    The effective opacity is $\alpha'_{ij} = g \alpha_i$, where $g = \exp(-0.5 \omega'_0 d_0^2 - 0.5 \omega'_2 d_1^2 - \omega'_1 d_0 d_1)$, with $\omega'$ being elements of the inverse 2D covariance matrix and $d$ being the distance from the pixel center to the Gaussian center [S6, unofficial].

**Backpropagation and Gradient Computation:**
Gradients are computed via the chain rule for each parameter [S6, unofficial]:
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i}
    $$
*   **Scale ($s_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i}
    $$
*   **Color ($h_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i}
    $$
*   **Opacity ($\alpha_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i}
    $$
*   **Position ($\mathbf{p}_{w,i}$):**
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} &= \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} \\
    &+ \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} \\
    &+ \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}}
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    where $u_i$ is the 2D center, $\mathbf{p}_{c,i}$ is the 3D center in camera space, and $\sigma_i$ is the 3D covariance [S6, unofficial].

**Adaptive Density Control (Densification):**
Performed periodically (e.g., every 100–500 steps) to manage Gaussian count [S2]:
1.  **Pruning:** Remove Gaussians with $\alpha_i < \epsilon$ (e.g., 0.005) or those that are too large ($||\mathbf{s}_i|| > \tau_S$) [S2].
2.  **Densification Check:** If the positional gradient magnitude $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{p}} \mathcal{L}\| > \tau_p$ (e.g., 0.0002):
    *   **Over-reconstruction:** If the Gaussian is large ($||\mathbf{s}_i|| > \tau_S$), **split** it into two. The new positions are perturbed by noise, and scales are adjusted [S2].
    *   **Under-reconstruction:** If the Gaussian is small ($||\mathbf{s}_i|| \le \tau_S$), **clone** it. The clone is placed at the same position with slightly perturbed parameters [S2].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_3dgs(input_images, camera_poses, initial_gaussians, config):
    """
    input_images: List of ground truth RGB images.
    camera_poses: List of camera extrinsics/intrinsics.
    initial_gaussians: List of Gaussian objects with p, q, s, alpha, h.
    config: Dictionary of hyperparameters.
    """
    gaussians = initial_gaussians
    optimizer = Adam(gaussians.parameters(), lr=config.initial_lr)
    
    # Spherical Harmonics degree scheduling
    current_sh_degree = 0
    max_sh_degree = config.sh_degree
    
    for step in range(1, config.num_iters + 1):
        # 1. Camera Selection
        cam_idx = get_next_camera_index(step, len(camera_poses))
        cam = camera_poses[cam_idx]
        gt_image = input_images[cam_idx]
        
        # 2. Resolution Scheduling
        downscale_factor = get_downscale_factor(step, config.resolution_schedule)
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, cam, downscale_factor)
        
        # 3. Loss Computation
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, config.ssim_weight)
        
        # 4. Backpropagation
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # 5. SH Degree Increase
        if step % config.sh_degree_interval == 0 and current_sh_degree < max_sh_degree:
            current_sh_degree += 1
            update_sh_degree(gaussians, current_sh_degree)
            
        # 6. Adaptive Density Control (Densification)
        if is_refinement_iteration(step, config.densify_from, config.densify_until, config.refine_every):
            densify_and_prune(gaussians, config)
            
        # 7. Learning Rate Scheduling
        update_learning_rate(optimizer, step, config.warmup_length)
        
    return gaussians

def densify_and_prune(gaussians, config):
    for g in gaussians:
        # Pruning
        if g.alpha < config.opacity_threshold or g.scale_norm > config.size_threshold:
            gaussians.remove(g)
            continue
            
        # Densification
        if g.gradient_norm > config.grad_threshold:
            if g.scale_norm > config.split_threshold:
                # Split: Create two new Gaussians from this one
                new_g1, new_g2 = split_gaussian(g)
                gaussians.add(new_g1)
                gaussians.add(new_g2)
                gaussians.remove(g)
            else:
                # Clone: Create a copy with perturbed position
                new_g = clone_gaussian(g)
                gaussians.add(new_g)
```

## Subtopics

### [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md)
Replaces standard SH color and empirical opacity with physical quantities (extinction, albedo, scattering asymmetry) to enable dynamic relighting by solving the radiative transfer equation [4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md].

### [WildGaussians](6064-wildgaussians.md)
Models image-dependent appearance variations via a lightweight MLP and separates static scene geometry from transient occluders using learnable opacity variations, handling "in-the-wild" photo collections [6064-wildgaussians.md].

### [Non-Pinhole Camera Training](6088-non-pinhole-camera-training.md)
Adapts the training loop to non-pinhole cameras (e.g., with lens distortion) by using a differentiable rasterizer that accommodates arbitrary camera projection models [6088-non-pinhole-camera-training.md].

### AbsGS (Absolute Positional Gradients)
Uses absolute sums of positional gradients across multiple views to prevent gradient cancellation, improving the detection of "fence-sitting" Gaussians and sharpening edges [6127-absgs.md].

### [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md)
Dynamically manages Gaussian cardinality via splitting, cloning, and pruning based on gradient magnitude and opacity to balance quality and cost [6234-densification-strategy.md].

### [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md)
Introduces auxiliary losses (e.g., normal consistency) to suppress floaters and enforce geometric plausibility, distinct from physical material optimization [6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md].

### [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md)
Initializes geometric attributes with an explicit normal attribute optimized via normal consistency loss to accelerate convergence of editable Gaussian optimization [7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md].

### [GSP Training Loss](9156-gsp-training-loss.md)
Extends the standard loss with normal consistency and anisotropy regularization to prevent excessive kernel elongation in dynamic or physically-based contexts [9156-gsp-training-loss.md].

### [GPS-Gaussian](9197-gps-gaussian.md)
Eliminates iterative optimization by regressing Gaussian parameters directly from sparse views using Gaussian parameter maps and a depth estimation module [9197-gps-gaussian.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training typically requires 15,000–30,000 iterations [S3, S4, unofficial]. Convergence is significantly faster than NeRFs, often completing in minutes to hours depending on scene size and hardware [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians $N$. The default limit is often 5,000,000 Gaussians [S3, unofficial]. Each Gaussian stores position (12B), rotation (16B), scale (12B), opacity (4B), and SH coefficients (variable, e.g., 45 floats for degree 3) [S6, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves real-time rendering (30–100+ FPS) on consumer GPUs after training [S5, unofficial]. Training speed is highly dependent on GPU acceleration; CPU execution is ~100x slower than GPU [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast training, real-time rendering, high visual fidelity, explicit representation allows for easy editing and compression [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce "floaters" or over-reconstruction artifacts in textureless regions or dynamic scenes without regularization [S2][S6, unofficial]. Memory consumption can be high for large scenes [S7].
*   **When to use:** Static or slowly changing scenes where real-time novel view synthesis is required [S5, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with significant transient objects or complex dynamic lighting without extensions like WildGaussians or Dynamic Modeling [6064-wildgaussians.md, 9265-dynamic-and-temporal-modeling.md].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S2]. It builds on the concept of splatting (rasterizing 3D primitives to 2D) and differentiable rendering. The method initializes Gaussians using sparse point clouds from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) tools like COLMAP [S2][S6, unofficial]. It differs from NeRFs by using explicit Gaussian primitives instead of implicit neural networks, allowing for faster training and rendering [S2][S5, unofficial]. The training process uses the Adam optimizer and adaptive density control to manage the number of Gaussians [S2][S6, unofficial].
