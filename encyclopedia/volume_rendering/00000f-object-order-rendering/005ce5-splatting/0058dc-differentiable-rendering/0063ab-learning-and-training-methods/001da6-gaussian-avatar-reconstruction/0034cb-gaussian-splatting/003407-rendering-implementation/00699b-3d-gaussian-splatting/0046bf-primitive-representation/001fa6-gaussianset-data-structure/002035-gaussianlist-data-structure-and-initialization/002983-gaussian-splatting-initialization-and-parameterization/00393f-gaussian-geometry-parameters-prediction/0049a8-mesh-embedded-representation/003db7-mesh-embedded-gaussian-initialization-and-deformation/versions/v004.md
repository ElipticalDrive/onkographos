# Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation

Also called: Gaussian Embedding, Triangle Embedding

## Overview
This method anchors 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh using barycentric coordinates and normal displacement, allowing the Gaussians to track mesh deformation through topology rather than direct vertex positions [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly modeling the continuous embedding space $E_i = \{k, u, v, d\}$ to compute posed positions, rotations, and scales via interpolation and area-change ratios, ensuring differentiability across the mesh surface [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Canonical triangle mesh with vertices $V$, per-vertex normals $n$, and per-vertex quaternions $q$ (computed for rotation tracking).
*   **Output:** Posed Gaussian parameters (position $\mu_{pose}$, rotation $q_{pose}$, scale $s_{pose}$) for a given frame $t$.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian $i$ stores embedding parameters $E_i$:
*   $k$: Integer triangle index.
*   $u, v$: Float barycentric coordinates ($u \ge 0, v \ge 0, u+v \le 1$).
*   $d$: Float normal displacement.
*   $\bar{q}_i$: Quaternion representing the canonical rotation of the Gaussian.
*   $\bar{s}_i$: Scalar representing the canonical scale of the Gaussian.

**Initialization:**
1.  Randomly select 10,000 pairs of triangle indices and barycentric coordinates on the canonical mesh [S1, unofficial].
2.  Set the initial barycentric coordinates $(u, v)$ to the sampled values.
3.  Initialize all normal displacements $d$ to zero [S1, unofficial].
4.  Initialize canonical rotations $\bar{q}_i$ and scales $\bar{s}_i$ based on the local geometry or default values.

**Canonical Position and Normal:**
Compute the canonical position $\mu_{cano}$ and normal $n_{cano}$ using Phong interpolation :
$$
\mu_{cano} = \mathcal{V}(k, u, v) = u V_1 + v V_2 + (1 - u - v) V_3
$$
$$
n_{cano} = \mathcal{N}(k, u, v) = u n_1 + v n_2 + (1 - u - v) n_3
$$
where $V_1, V_2, V_3$ are the vertices of triangle $k$, and $n_1, n_2, n_3$ are the per-vertex normals [S1, unofficial].

**Deformation:**
The posed position $\mu_{pose}$ is obtained by applying mesh deformation to the displaced canonical position:
$$
\mu_{pose} = \text{Deform}(\mu_{cano} + d \cdot n_{cano})
$$
where $\text{Deform}(\cdot)$ applies skeletal or blend-shape animation to the mesh vertices .

**Rotation:**
The posed rotation $q_{pose}$ is interpolated from per-vertex quaternions and combined with the canonical rotation :
$$
\delta q_{i,t} = u q_1 + v q_2 + (1 - u - v) q_3
$$
$$
q_{pose} = \delta q_{i,t} * \bar{q}_i
$$
where $q_1, q_2, q_3$ are quaternions on the corresponding triangle vertices at frame $t$, and $*$ denotes quaternion multiplication [S1, unofficial]. The per-vertex quaternions $q_V$ are calculated by area-weighted average from surrounding neighbor triangles [S1, unofficial].

**Scale:**
The posed scale $s_{pose}$ is derived from the area change of the embedded triangle relative to the canonical state :
$$
s_{pose} = \left( \frac{A_{pose}}{A_{cano}} \right) \bar{s}_i
$$
where $A_{pose}$ and $A_{cano}$ are the areas of the triangle in the posed and canonical states, respectively [S1, unofficial].

**Color:**
Spherical harmonics are turned off for simplicity [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Cross-Triangle Handling:** Barycentric coordinates are only differentiable within a single triangle. If an optimization step moves the embedding point across a triangle boundary, the embedding must be re-expressed in the neighboring triangle to maintain differentiability . Failure to handle this causes Gaussians to stick to boundaries and pile up .

## Subtopics

### Walking on Triangle
A mechanism that constrains Gaussians to move within their corresponding mesh triangles by clipping UV values to prevent movement beyond triangle boundaries [S1, unofficial]. Disabling this mechanism causes Gaussians to stick and pile up on boundaries, leading to artifacts in novel poses [S1, unofficial]. Performance impact: For the 'bala' head avatar, disabling reduces PSNR from 30.04 to 29.91, SSIM from 0.938 to 0.933, and increases LPIPS from 0.062 to 0.070. For 'male-3-casual', disabling reduces PSNR from 33.01 to 32.48, SSIM from 0.982 to 0.979, and increases LPIPS from 0.020 to 0.024 [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Position and normal computation is $O(1)$ per Gaussian per frame. Rotation and scale computation is $O(1)$ per Gaussian per frame.
*   **Memory:** Requires storing embedding parameters ($k, u, v, d$) and canonical properties ($\bar{q}, \bar{s}$) per Gaussian.
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently tracks mesh deformation without updating Gaussian positions directly; maintains smoothness via interpolation.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of triangle boundaries to avoid artifacts; relies on accurate mesh deformation and per-vertex quaternion computation.
*   **When to use:** When rendering animated avatars or deformable meshes where Gaussians need to follow surface deformation.
*   **When not to use:** For static scenes or scenes with non-mesh-based geometry where embedding coordinates are not applicable.

## Background
Originated in SplattingAvatar [S1, unofficial], this method builds on the concept of embedding Gaussians on a canonical mesh to facilitate deformation tracking. It addresses the challenge of maintaining differentiability and smoothness when animating Gaussian splats on deformable surfaces.
