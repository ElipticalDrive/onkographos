# Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control

Also called: Complexity-Aware Density Control, Complexity-Aware Level of Details, Complexity-Guided Relocation

## Overview
Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control is a training optimization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that replaces opacity-only heuristics with a composite metric combining opacity, spatial scale, and local image complexity [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of inefficient primitive allocation by biasing densification and pruning toward regions with high reconstruction error (surrogate by complexity) while preserving large primitives in simple regions to maintain uniform degradation across different levels of detail (LoD) [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods like [Multi-View Density Control Criterion](5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md) by explicitly modeling the spatial footprint of primitives via scale and using a complexity field to modulate the importance score, rather than relying solely on view-dependent gradient accumulation or opacity [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates during the training phase of 3D Gaussian Splatting, specifically managing the relocation (splitting/cloning) and retention (pruning) of Gaussian primitives based on a learned complexity field.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Set of 3D Gaussians $G = \{g_j\}$, where each $g_j$ has mean $\mu_j$, opacity $\alpha_j$, scale $s(g_j)$, and covariance.
    *   Camera views $\Pi_i$ with associated ground truth images.
    *   Training budget factor $\tau \in (0, 1]$, where $\tau=1$ is full capacity and $\tau<1$ is a reduced budget.
    *   Hyperparameters: EMA learning rate $\eta \in (0, 1]$, complexity dropout probability $\delta = 0.2$, scale weight $\lambda = 2$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Updated set of Gaussians with refined positions, opacities, and scales.
    *   Updated complexity field $C_j$ for each Gaussian.

### Data Structures
*   **Complexity Field $C_j$:** A scalar value stored per Gaussian $g_j$. Initialized to 0. Updated via exponential moving average.
*   **Normalized Complexity $\tilde{C}_j$:** A scalar in $[0, 1]$ derived from $C_j$ using min-max normalization over the current training step or epoch.
*   **Importance Score $S(g_j)$:** A scalar computed per Gaussian for pruning decisions.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Complexity Map Computation:** For each training view $\Pi_i$, compute a 2D complexity map $C_i^{2D}(u)$ at pixel coordinates $u$. The source specifies this is computed using "delentropy" [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Gaussian Projection and Complexity Update:**
    *   Project the mean $\mu_j$ of each contributing Gaussian $g_j$ into the image plane of view $\Pi_i$ to obtain pixel coordinate $\pi_i(\mu_j)$.
    *   Only update $C_j$ if $\pi_i(\mu_j)$ lies within the view's frustum.
    *   Update the complexity using exponential moving average:
        $$ C_j \leftarrow (1 - \eta) C_j + \eta C_i^{2D}(\pi_i(\mu_j)) $$
    *   Over time, $C_j$ converges to a multi-view average complexity [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Relocation Sampling (Densification):**
    *   To decide where to spawn new primitives, sample a source Gaussian $g_j$ with probability $p(g_j)$:
        $$ p(g_j) = \alpha_j \cdot \exp(\tilde{C}_j) $$
    *   Apply **Complexity Dropout**: With probability $\delta = 0.2$, set $\tilde{C}_j = 0$ for that candidate. This forces the score to rely solely on opacity $\alpha_j$, preventing the system from collapsing exclusively onto fixed complex regions [S1, unofficial].
    *   The exponential term $\exp(\tilde{C}_j)$ mimics the observed exponential growth of particle count required in high-complexity areas [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Importance Score Computation (Pruning/LoD):**
    *   Compute the importance score $S(g_j)$ for retaining primitives under a budget $\tau < 1$:
        $$ S(g_j) = \alpha_j + \lambda \cdot \frac{s(g_j)}{\exp(\tilde{C}_j(\mu_j))} $$
    *   Here, $s(g_j)$ is the max-normalized scale of the Gaussian.
    *   **High Complexity ($\tilde{C}_j$ high):** $\exp(\tilde{C}_j)$ is large, attenuating the scale term. The score approaches $\alpha_j$, prioritizing opacity where optimization pressure is highest and avoiding large, blurring primitives [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Low Complexity ($\tilde{C}_j$ low):** $\exp(\tilde{C}_j) \to 1$. The score becomes $\alpha_j + \lambda \cdot s(g_j)$. This restores weight to large primitives that efficiently cover simple areas despite moderate opacity [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Variance Loss Minimization:**
    *   To ensure uniform degradation across LoDs, minimize the spatial variance of the reconstruction error residual.
    *   Compute residual $\Delta_i(u)$ at pixel $u$:
        $$ \Delta_i(u) = E(I_i^{\text{full}})(u) - E(I_i^{\tau})(u) $$
        where $E(I_i^{\text{full}})$ is the error at full capacity ($\tau=1$) and $E(I_i^{\tau})$ is the error at budget $\tau$.
    *   Compute variance loss:
        $$ L_{var} = \text{Var}_{u \in \Omega} \Delta_i(u) $$
    *   The total training loss combines L1+D-SSIM on both full and budgeted renders with this variance term:
        $$ \text{Total Loss} = \text{Average}(\text{L1} + \text{D-SSIM}) + \lambda_{var} L_{var} $$
    *   The optimizer varies $\tau$ during training to enforce this uniformity [S1, unofficial].

```python
def update_complexity(gaussians, view, eta):
    """
    Updates the complexity field C_j for Gaussians projecting into the current view.
    """
    # Project Gaussian means to image plane

    # Compute 2D complexity map for the current view using delentropy
    # Note: 'delentropy' implementation details are not provided in source [S1, unofficial]
    complexity_map = compute_delentropy_complexity(view.image, view.depth) 
    
    for i, g in enumerate(gaussians):
        pixel = projected_pixels[i]
        if is_in_frustum(pixel, view):
            # Get complexity value at the projected pixel
            c_2d = complexity_map[pixel.y, pixel.x]
            # Exponential Moving Average update
            g.complexity = (1 - eta) * g.complexity + eta * c_2d

def compute_relocation_probability(gaussians, normalized_complexity, dropout_prob=0.2):
    """
    Computes sampling probability for densification/relocation.
    """
    probs = []
    for g in gaussians:
        c_norm = normalized_complexity[g.id]
        
        # Apply complexity dropout
        if np.random.random() < dropout_prob:
            c_norm = 0.0
            
        # p(g) = alpha * exp(C_tilde)
        p = g.opacity * np.exp(c_norm)
        probs.append(p)
        
    return np.array(probs)

def compute_importance_score(gaussians, normalized_complexity, lambda_scale=2.0):
    """
    Computes importance score for pruning/LoD selection.
    """
    scores = []
    for g in gaussians:
        c_norm = normalized_complexity[g.id]
        scale_term = g.max_normalized_scale / np.exp(c_norm)
        
        # S(g) = alpha + lambda * (scale / exp(C_tilde))
        score = g.opacity + lambda_scale * scale_term
        scores.append(score)
        
    return np.array(scores)

def compute_variance_loss(full_render, budgeted_render, tau):
    """
    Computes L_var to ensure uniform degradation.
    """
    # E(I) is reconstruction error, e.g., L1 or SSIM difference from GT
    error_full = compute_error(full_render, ground_truth)
    error_budget = compute_error(budgeted_render, ground_truth)
    
    # Residual
    residual = error_full - error_budget
    
    # Variance over the image domain Omega
    l_var = np.var(residual)
    return l_var
```

### Parameters
*   $\eta$: EMA learning rate for complexity update. Range $(0, 1]$. [S1, unofficial]
*   $\delta$: Complexity dropout probability. Default $0.2$. [S1, unofficial]
*   $\lambda$: Weight for the scale term in importance score. Default $2$. [S1, unofficial]
*   $\tau$: Training budget factor. Varies during training. [S1, unofficial]
*   $\lambda_{var}$: Weight for the variance loss. [S1, unofficial]

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Collapse on Complex Regions:** Without complexity dropout ($\delta=0$), the system may over-concentrate primitives in high-complexity areas, ignoring simpler regions. The source ablates this, showing $\delta=0.2$ prevents this collapse [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision in Normalization:** $\tilde{C}_j$ must be min-max normalized to $[0, 1]$ before exponentiation to ensure stable gradients and consistent behavior across different scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Frustum Culling:** Complexity updates should only occur for Gaussians projecting into the current view's frustum to avoid noise from out-of-view contributions [S1, unofficial].
*   **LoD Variance:** The variance loss $L_{var}$ requires rendering at both full capacity and the current budget $\tau$ simultaneously, increasing memory and compute overhead during training [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead proportional to the number of Gaussians for complexity updates and importance score computations. The variance loss requires dual renders (full and budgeted), effectively doubling the rendering cost per step for the loss calculation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the complexity field $C_j$ per Gaussian and maintaining two render buffers (full and budgeted) for variance loss calculation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** At full capacity ($\tau=1$), performance is on par with standard 3DGS. At lower budgets (e.g., 10%, 1%), it significantly outperforms opacity-only pruning methods in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS by preserving structurally important large primitives in simple regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides uniform quality degradation across LoDs; efficiently allocates primitives to complex regions; prevents blurring in high-complexity areas.
*   **Limitations:** Requires implementation of "delentropy" for complexity maps; increased training time due to dual rendering and EMA updates; sensitive to the choice of $\lambda$ and $\delta$ [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control was introduced in the paper "KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian Splatting" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses limitations in standard 3DGS training where opacity serves as a proxy for error, leading to under-served unresolved regions and non-uniform degradation at lower budgets. The method builds on the observation that particle count grows exponentially with image complexity and integrates this into the density control mechanism [S1, unofficial].
