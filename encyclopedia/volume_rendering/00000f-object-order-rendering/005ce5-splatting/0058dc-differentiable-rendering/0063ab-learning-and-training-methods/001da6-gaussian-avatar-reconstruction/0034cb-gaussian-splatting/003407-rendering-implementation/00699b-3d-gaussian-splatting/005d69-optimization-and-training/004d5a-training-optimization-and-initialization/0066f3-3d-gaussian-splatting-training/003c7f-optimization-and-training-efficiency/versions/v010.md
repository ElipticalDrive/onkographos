# Optimization and Training Efficiency

Also called: Training Efficiency, Optimization Strategies

## Overview
Optimization and Training Efficiency encompasses the computational strategies, memory management techniques, and hyperparameter configurations required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) efficiently at scale. It addresses the high memory and compute costs of differentiable rasterization by introducing distributed rendering, memory-aware caching, gradient checkpointing, and fused optimizer kernels, while stabilizing convergence through parameter-specific learning rates. It sets itself apart from standard optimization by focusing on the infrastructure and algorithmic variants that enable training on large-scale scenes or accelerate convergence, rather than the core loss minimization mechanics [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Shared principles
The optimization and parallelization methods share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

### Faster-GS
Addresses oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters caused by a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio [S1, unofficial]. It rescales the scaling gradient with the inverse of the current scale to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial]. This cuts training time by nearly half compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Reduced Iteration Training
Reduces training iterations from the original 30,000 to 1,000 for the Neural3DVideo dataset and to 1,500 for the LongVolumetricVideo dataset, keeping all other parameters identical to the original 3DGS implementation [S2]. Using the original 30,000 iterations on sparse input data causes overfitting [S2]. This reduces training time and improves novel view synthesis by approximately 4 PSNR [S2].

### Disk-Based Training Image Loading
Loading ground truth images from disk every iteration instead of storing them in VRAM, to enable training on consumer hardware with limited VRAM for ultra-large-scale datasets [S3, unofficial]. Standard 3DGS stores the entire training dataset in VRAM for speed; this method saves VRAM but reduces training performance [S3, unofficial].

### Sampling Gap Control
Controlling the sampling rate by adjusting the sampling gap value to influence training speed and rendering quality [S4, unofficial]. Increasing the sampling gap value decreases the sampling rate, which is inversely proportional to the sampling rate [S4, unofficial]. Increasing the sampling gap increases pc time and makes the rendering result blurrier [S4, unofficial]. On the truck dataset with 3000 training steps, increasing the sampling gap from 2 to 500 increases pc time from 0.17739 to 0.20525 [S4, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. The original implementation prioritized training speed by storing the entire training dataset in VRAM, which is infeasible for ultra-large-scale datasets on consumer-grade hardware [S3, unofficial]. Subsequent research has focused on reducing memory footprints, accelerating convergence, and stabilizing the optimization process through various efficiency techniques [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
