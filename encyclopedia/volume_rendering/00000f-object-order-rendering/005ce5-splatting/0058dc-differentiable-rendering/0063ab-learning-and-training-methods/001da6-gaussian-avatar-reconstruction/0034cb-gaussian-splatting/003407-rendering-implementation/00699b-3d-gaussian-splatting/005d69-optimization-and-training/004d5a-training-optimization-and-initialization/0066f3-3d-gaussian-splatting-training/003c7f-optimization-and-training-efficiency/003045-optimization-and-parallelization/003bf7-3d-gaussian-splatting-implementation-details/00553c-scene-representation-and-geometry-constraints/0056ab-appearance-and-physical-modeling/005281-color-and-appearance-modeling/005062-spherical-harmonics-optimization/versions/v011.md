# Spherical Harmonics Optimization

Also called: SH Optimization, SH Compression, SH Distillation, SH Quantization

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Optimization encompasses techniques to reduce the memory footprint, computational cost, and training time of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by modifying the SH representation of Gaussian radiance. It addresses the high parameter count (48 coefficients per Gaussian for degree 3) and the redundancy of higher-order coefficients in standard training. It sets itself apart from siblings like [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md) and [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md) by focusing on compression and efficiency of the existing SH basis rather than introducing new appearance conditioning or factorized illumination models.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ of each Gaussian $i$. The rendering equation remains:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
where $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the view-dependent color computed from SH coefficients. The variations involve:
1.  **Dimensionality Reduction:** Reducing the SH degree (e.g., from 3 to 1 or 0) to decrease coefficient count from 48 to 12 or 3 per Gaussian.
2.  **Quantization:** Replacing continuous SH vectors with discrete indices from a shared palette.
3.  **Distillation:** Using a high-fidelity teacher model to guide the optimization of a lower-dimensional student model.

## Subtopics

### [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md)
Replaces third-order SH with first-order SH using knowledge distillation from a pretrained teacher model. It preserves rendering fidelity better than direct degree reduction by compensating for lost high-frequency information via the distillation loss. Choose this when high compression is needed without significant quality loss.

### [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md)
Derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to Gaussian primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking. It solves the computational overhead of automatic differentiation in differentiable rasterization. Choose this for SLAM or camera tracking applications where gradient efficiency is critical.

### [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Enables stable optimization by training on multiple images per iteration, reducing gradient variance. It modifies Adaptive Density Control (ADC) to be compatible with multi-view gradients. Choose this when training stability and convergence speed are prioritized over single-view efficiency.

### [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md)
Compresses SH coefficients by clustering them into a shared palette of $K$ vectors, storing only indices. It reduces memory footprint significantly at the cost of minor lighting artifacts. Choose this when memory bandwidth is the primary bottleneck.

### SH Degree Zero Optimization
#### definition
Setting the maximum degree of Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to 0 to remove superfluous operations and improve training speed, as higher-degree SH coefficients for color remain unchanged during training [S1, unofficial].
#### step
Modify the model code to delete operations relative to the max degree in the model and set the max degree parameter to 0 [S1, unofficial].
#### pitfall
In the original 3D Gaussian Splatting model, features represented by SH coefficients with degree > 0 are unchanged during all training, making them useless [S1, unofficial].
#### number
Directly changing the max degree to 0 yields a 16.25% improvement compared to the default. With specific changing code, it yields a 16.57% improvement but increases CPU-GPU process time [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated from observations in [I3DGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06408v1) that higher-order SH coefficients in standard 3DGS training do not update significantly, suggesting redundancy. This led to optimizations such as degree reduction, quantization, and distillation to improve efficiency.
