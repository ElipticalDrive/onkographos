# Optimization and Training Efficiency


## Overview
Optimization and Training Efficiency encompasses the computational strategies, memory management techniques, and hyperparameter configurations required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) efficiently at scale. It addresses the high memory and compute costs of differentiable rasterization by introducing distributed rendering, memory-aware caching, gradient checkpointing, and fused optimizer kernels, while also stabilizing convergence through parameter-specific learning rates and regularization. It sets itself apart from standard optimization by focusing on the infrastructure and algorithmic variants that enable training on large-scale scenes or accelerate convergence, rather than the core loss minimization mechanics [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Shared principles
The optimization and parallelization methods share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer defined in [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md). The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants (e.g., MaskGaussian, Grendel-GS) modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training > Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md)
Assigns distinct learning rates to different groups of Gaussian attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) within a single Adam optimizer. This stabilizes training by preventing parameters with high sensitivity (such as color or scale) from diverging due to large gradients, while allowing slower-converging parameters (such as position) to update appropriately. It sets itself apart from standard uniform learning rate approaches by decoupling the update magnitudes per attribute, addressing the varying gradient scales inherent in the differentiable rasterization pipeline.

### [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md)
Encompasses the computational strategies required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) efficiently at scale. It addresses the high memory and compute costs of differentiable rasterization by introducing distributed rendering across multiple GPUs, restructuring the backward pass for per-splat parallelization, and computing specialized gradients for adaptive representation parameters like probabilistic masks. It distinguishes itself from standard optimization by focusing on the infrastructure and algorithmic variants that enable training on large-scale scenes or accelerate convergence, rather than the core loss minimization mechanics.

### [CLM](7351-clm.md)
Removes the GPU memory barrier by moving Gaussian states between CPU and GPU memory during optimization. It employs a smart caching policy to keep the most frequently updated Gaussians on the GPU.

### [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md)
Re-engineers the 3D Gaussian Splatting training loop to accelerate optimization by combining dynamic sparse rasterization with fast gradient checkpointing. It sets itself apart from standard training by skipping tiles with negligible transmittance during rasterization and recomputing only necessary intermediate values during the backward pass, rather than storing all activations.

### [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md)
Integrates the parameter update step of the optimizer directly into the backward pass of the differentiable rasterizer. It solves the problem of high VRAM usage and computational overhead associated with separate optimizer kernels by eliminating the need for intermediate buffers to store parameter states during the gradient computation phase.

### Faster-GS
- **Definition**: Addresses oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters caused by a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio [S1, unofficial].
- **Step**: Rescales the scaling gradient with the inverse of the current scale to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial].
- **Impact**: Cuts training time by nearly half compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Reduced Iteration Training
- **Step**: Reduces training iterations from the original 30,000 to 1,000 for the Neural3DVideo dataset and to 1,500 for the LongVolumetricVideo dataset [S2]. Keeps all other parameters identical to the original 3DGS implementation [S2].
- **Pitfall**: Using the original 30,000 iterations on sparse input data causes overfitting [S2].
- **Impact**: Reduces training time and improves novel view synthesis by approximately 4 PSNR [S2].

### Disk-Based Training Image Loading
- **Definition**: Loading ground truth images from disk every iteration instead of storing them in VRAM, to enable training on consumer hardware with limited VRAM for ultra-large-scale datasets [S3, unofficial].
- **Comparison**: Standard 3DGS stores the entire training dataset in VRAM for speed. This method loads images from disk every iteration, saving VRAM but reducing training performance [S3, unofficial].

### Sampling Gap Control
- **Definition**: Controlling the sampling rate by adjusting the sampling gap value to influence training speed and rendering quality [S4, unofficial].
- **Step**: Increase the sampling gap value to decrease the sampling rate, which is inversely proportional to the sampling rate [S4, unofficial].
- **Pitfall**: Increasing the sampling gap increases pc time and makes the rendering result blurrier [S4, unofficial].
- **Impact**: On the truck dataset with 3000 training steps, increasing the sampling gap from 2 to 500 increases pc time from 0.17739 to 0.20525 [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The original 3D Gaussian Splatting implementation [22] established a baseline training loop that stored the entire training dataset in VRAM and used a fixed number of iterations (30,000) [S2]. Subsequent research identified bottlenecks in memory usage, training speed, and convergence stability. Methods like Faster-GS [79] addressed gradient oscillation in scaling and rotation parameters, while others like ABSGS [80] introduced high-pass filtering for fine detail recovery. To handle ultra-large-scale datasets, approaches such as LoD of Gaussians [S3, unofficial] introduced disk-based image loading and CPU-side Morton Z-ordering to manage memory. Further optimizations like FastGS [S1, unofficial] and CLM [S1, unofficial] focused on reducing memory footprints through gradient checkpointing and CPU-GPU caching, respectively. The integration of fused backward passes and parameter-specific learning rates has become standard practice to balance VRAM consumption with training stability and speed [S1, unofficial][4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r].
