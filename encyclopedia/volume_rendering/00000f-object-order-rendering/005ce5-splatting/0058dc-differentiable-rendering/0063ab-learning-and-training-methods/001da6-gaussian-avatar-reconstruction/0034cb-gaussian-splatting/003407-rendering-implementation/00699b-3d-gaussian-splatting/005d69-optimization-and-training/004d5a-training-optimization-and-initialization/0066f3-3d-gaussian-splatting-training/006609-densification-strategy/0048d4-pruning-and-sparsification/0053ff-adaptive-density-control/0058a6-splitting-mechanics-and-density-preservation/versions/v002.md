# Splitting Mechanics and Density Preservation

Also called: Adaptive Density Control (ADC) splitting logic, Density-preserving splitting

## Overview
This family defines the geometric operations used to refine the primitive distribution during 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization. It addresses the problem of static initialization failing to capture complex scene details, either by under-reconstructing fine structures or over-reconstructing smooth regions. It differs from sibling methods like [MCMC Densification](9133-mcmc-densification.md) by using deterministic gradient-based criteria rather than stochastic sampling, and from [Importance-Weighted Stochastic Sampling](9243-importance-weighted-stochastic-sampling-for-gaussian-simplif.md) by focusing on densification (splitting/cloning) rather than simplification.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of 3D Gaussians $G = \{g_k\}$, where each $g_k$ is defined by position $\mu_k$, scale $s_k$ (or covariance $\Sigma_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, rotation $q_k$, and spherical harmonics coefficients $c_k$. The core mechanism involves replacing a parent Gaussian with two child Gaussians (Splitting) or duplicating a parent (Cloning) to adjust the local density of the representation.

The decision to split or clone is typically driven by the magnitude of the view-space positional gradient $\|\nabla_{\mu_{2D}} \mathcal{L}\|$ accumulated over a training interval [S1, unofficial]. Gaussians with high average gradients are marked for densification. The geometric transformation must preserve the visual contribution of the parent to prevent loss function perturbations and artifact generation (e.g., floaters or clustering) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [3DGS Adaptive Density Control](7847-3dgs-adaptive-density-control.md)
A dynamic optimization strategy that adjusts the number and distribution of Gaussians during training. It distinguishes itself by using gradient magnitude thresholds to trigger densification, rather than explicit density preservation constraints. It employs both splitting (for "large" Gaussians) and cloning (for "small" Gaussians), along with pruning and opacity resets.

### [Density-preserving Gaussian splitting](9290-density-preserving-gaussian-splitting.md)
A densification strategy that modifies the parameters of split Gaussian children to maintain the volumetric density of the parent. Unlike standard 3DGS, it discards cloning entirely and relies exclusively on splitting with learned opacity and size adaptations to prevent loss perturbations and Gaussian clustering.

### Adaptive Density Control (Split & Clone)
#### definition
A mechanism to adjust the density of 3D Gaussians during optimization to match local scene complexity by duplicating or subdividing Gaussians based on their coverage relative to the target region [S1, unofficial].

#### step
During optimization, evaluate each Gaussian's coverage area against the target reconstruction region. If the Gaussian is too small (Under-Reconstruction), duplicate it (Clone). If the Gaussian is too large (Over-Reconstruction), split it into smaller Gaussians [S1, unofficial].

#### number
On the Average-30K dataset, Full achieves PSNR 26.05. No-Split drops to 23.90. No-Clone drops to 25.91 [S1, unofficial].

#### comparison
Split removal primarily degrades background quality, causing large blurry regions. Clone removal primarily breaks thin structures (e.g., bicycle spokes), causing them to appear fragmented [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of Adaptive Density Control (ADC) originates from the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper by Kerbl et al. (2023) . The specific ablation study comparing Split vs. Clone contributions, including the PSNR metrics on the Average-30K dataset, is documented in an unofficial analysis of the paper's ablation results [S1, unofficial]. The Density-preserving splitting variant was introduced to address artifacts caused by standard splitting operations, such as loss perturbations and clustering, by enforcing density conservation during the split operation [S1, unofficial].
