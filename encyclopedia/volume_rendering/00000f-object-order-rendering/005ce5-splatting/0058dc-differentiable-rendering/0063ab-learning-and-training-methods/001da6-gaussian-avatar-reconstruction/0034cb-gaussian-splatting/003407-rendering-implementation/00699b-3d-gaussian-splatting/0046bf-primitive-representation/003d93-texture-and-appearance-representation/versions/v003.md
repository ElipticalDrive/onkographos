# Texture and Appearance Representation

Also called: View-Dependent Appearance, Neural Shaders, Textured Gaussians

## Overview
This family extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting primitive representation to model high-frequency spatial details and view-dependent optical effects that static isotropic color vectors (Spherical Harmonics) cannot capture [S1][S2, unofficial]. It replaces or augments the per-Gaussian color attribute with explicit textures, neural networks, or directional basis functions, enabling the reconstruction of complex materials, specularities, and fine surface patterns [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike the base [3D Gaussian Splatting](2812-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) method which assumes uniform color per primitive, these methods decouple geometry from appearance or enhance the appearance model with directional sensitivity [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the following mechanism and data structure extensions:

1.  **Extended Primitive Attributes**: Each 3D Gaussian primitive $i$ stores additional appearance attributes $\mathbf{a}_i$ alongside the standard geometric parameters (position $\mathbf{\mu}_i$, covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **View-Dependent Evaluation**: The final color $C(p)$ at pixel $p$ is computed by querying $\mathbf{a}_i$ with the viewing direction $\mathbf{v}$ and potentially other parameters (e.g., light direction $\mathbf{l}$, surface normal $\mathbf{n}$), rather than using a static isotropic color vector [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Alpha-Blending**: The rendering equation remains a front-to-back alpha blending process, but the contribution $c_i$ of each Gaussian is modulated by the appearance function:
    $$ C(p) = \sum_{i \in \text{sorted}} c_i(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{a}_i) \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $c_i$ is the color contribution derived from the specific appearance representation [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Trade-off**: The choice of representation involves a trade-off between storage bandwidth, computational cost during decoding, and the complexity of the appearance being modeled [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Textured Gaussians](8061-textured-gaussians.md)
[Textured Gaussians](8061-textured-gaussians.md) replaces spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients with explicit 2D textures mapped onto each 3D Gaussian primitive to resolve artifacts in semi-transparent scenes and enable high-frequency appearance details without increasing the number of primitives. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting which uses low-order SH for view-dependent color, this method decouples geometric representation from high-fidelity appearance, allowing for detailed texture sampling based on local UV coordinates.

### [View-Dependent Appearance Representations](14602-view-dependent-appearance-representations.md)
[View-Dependent Appearance Representations](14602-view-dependent-appearance-representations.md) encompasses methods for modeling view-dependent appearance within 3D Gaussian splats, addressing the limitation of static isotropic color vectors in standard Gaussian Splatting. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Spec-Gaussian](8087-spec-gaussian.md) by providing a modular framework for various encoding strategies, including fixed basis functions, neural feature vectors, explicit caching structures, and codebook quantization.

#### View-Dependent Gaussian Splatting
[View-Dependent Gaussian Splatting](14602-view-dependent-appearance-representations.md) models 3DGS to represent 3D shapes and predicts attributes like view-dependent color and opacity using a NeRF-like neural network, replacing the Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients used in original 3DGS.

#### Neural BRDF for 3D Gaussian Splatting
[Neural BRDF for 3D Gaussian Splatting](14602-view-dependent-appearance-representations.md) simplifies the BRDF to a neural shader that predicts specular and diffuse components directly from the view direction and a learnable material feature.

#### Neural Texture Splatting
[Neural Texture Splatting](14602-view-dependent-appearance-representations.md) encodes textures as small neural networks evaluated at Gaussian coordinates, which can be deformed to model dynamic appearances and fine geometric displacements.

## Background
The original 3D Gaussian Splatting method [Kerbl et al., 2023] uses Spherical Harmonics (SH) to represent view-dependent color. While efficient, SH has limited capacity for high-frequency details and complex specularities, often leading to "popping" artifacts or blurry textures [S1]. To address this, subsequent works introduced explicit textures [S2, unofficial], neural networks for view-dependent effects [S1], and neural BRDFs [S2, unofficial]. These methods leverage the differentiable rasterization pipeline of 3DGS to optimize these richer appearance representations jointly with geometry [S1][S2, unofficial].
