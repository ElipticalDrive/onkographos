# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM, Neural SLAM, Active Mapping with 3DGS

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the simultaneous estimation of camera poses and the construction of a 3D scene representation using differentiable Gaussian primitives. It solves the problem of real-time, high-fidelity reconstruction and localization from monocular, stereo, or RGB-D video streams. Unlike traditional explicit geometry methods (e.g., [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)) that rely on feature points and planar surfaces, or implicit neural fields (e.g., [NeRF Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)) that use continuous volumetric functions, 3DGS SLAM leverages the explicit, differentiable nature of Gaussians for faster training and rendering. It distinguishes itself by integrating Gaussian densification and optimization directly into the SLAM loop, allowing for dynamic adaptation of the scene representation as new views are observed.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS SLAM involves a coupled optimization loop where camera poses and Gaussian parameters are updated simultaneously or alternately.

1.  **Differentiable Rendering:** The scene is represented as a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$. For a given camera pose $T$, the renderer computes the projected 2D mean $\mu_{ij}$ and covariance $\Sigma_{ij}$ for each Gaussian $j$ in the view frustum of camera $i$. The color $C_{ij}$ is evaluated using spherical harmonics. The final pixel color is computed via alpha compositing:
    $$ C_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{O}_i} \alpha_{ij} \prod_{k=1}^{j-1} (1 - \alpha_{ik}) C_{ij} $$
    where $\alpha_{ij}$ is the opacity of Gaussian $j$ at pixel $i$, and $\mathcal{O}_i$ is the set of Gaussians sorted by depth in the view [S2].

2.  **Pose and Structure Optimization:** The system minimizes a photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ between the rendered image $\hat{C}$ and the ground truth image $C$. Gradients are backpropagated to update both the camera pose parameters (translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$) and the Gaussian attributes (position $\mu$, scale $S$, rotation $R$, opacity $\alpha$, and SH coefficients $c$).
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo}(\hat{C}, C) + \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ includes regularization terms for opacity and scale to prevent degenerate Gaussians [S4].

3.  **Densification and Culling:** To maintain reconstruction quality, the Gaussian set is dynamically adjusted. New Gaussians are spawned (cloned or split) in regions with high reprojection error or low opacity, while redundant Gaussians are pruned. This process is guided by the accumulated gradient magnitude of the opacity [Parent](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md).

4.  **Uncertainty and Mapping:** Advanced variants incorporate uncertainty estimation to guide densification and pose correction. For instance, depth uncertainty maps can be derived from the rendered depth variance, influencing the weight of depth constraints in the optimization [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Subtopics

### [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)
CG-SLAM is a 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM method that enhances reconstruction quality by integrating an uncertainty map derived from rendered depth into the training process. It distinguishes itself from standard 3DGS SLAM by explicitly modeling depth uncertainty to guide densification and optimization, addressing misalignment issues common in explicit geometry representations.
- **Inputs:** Input image sequence, initial camera poses, initial 3D Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$.
- **Outputs:** Optimized camera poses, refined 3D Gaussian parameters, uncertainty map.
- **Key Mechanism:** Computes an uncertainty map $U$ from rendered depth $\hat{D}$ and integrates it into the loss function to modulate gradient updates, preventing overfitting to unreliable depth regions.
- **When to choose:** When dealing with scenes containing degenerate depth regions (e.g., reflective surfaces) where standard photometric loss may fail.

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scenes or frames for the purpose of fusion, editing, or SLAM initialization. They solve the problem of establishing spatial and semantic consistency when direct geometric alignment is ambiguous or unavailable. Unlike sibling methods that focus on the continuous representation of a single scene or semantic enrichment, mapping strategies specifically address the *inter-scene* or *inter-frame* registration step, ranging from naive pixel identity to dense semantic feature matching.
- **Identity Mapping:** Assumes direct pixel correspondence $M(u, v) = (u, v)$. Computationally trivial but lacks spatial alignment accuracy.
- **Semantic Correspondence-based Mapping:** Uses deep learning models (e.g., DINO, ViT) to extract dense semantic features and match them via similarity matrices. Improves robustness in textureless regions.
- **Co-segmentation-based Mapping:** Refines alignment by segmenting images into semantically meaningful parts using clustering, then mapping within segments to preserve boundaries.
- **Density-based Resampling:** Used for geometry fusion; aligns sampling points along rays based on density peaks to ensure color attributes fuse with consistent geometry.

### [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md)
Semantic-aware mapping integrates semantic labels or features into the SLAM loop to improve robustness and enable semantic understanding of the reconstructed scene. It addresses the interdependence of appearance, geometry, and semantics, often using cross-attention mechanisms or hierarchical semantic encoding to ensure mutual reinforcement between modalities.

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation is a method for encoding 3D scenes as continuous volumetric functions using neural networks, enabling high-quality novel view synthesis from sparse 2D images. While standard NeRF implicitly maps position and view direction to color and density via a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), variants like Photon Field Networks extend this to model indirect global illumination by mapping position, view direction, and phase function coefficients to radiance. In the context of the parent tree (Gaussian Splatting), the term "NeRF Representation" often refers to the implicit baseline against which explicit Gaussian representations are compared, or specifically to the INGP architecture used in SLAM systems for efficient neural mapping.
- **Structure:** Hashgrid encoding followed by an MLP (e.g., 16 grid levels, 8 features/level, 5-layer MLP with 64 neurons).
- **Photon Fields:** Uses a KD-tree on a photon map and a phase coefficient $g$ to model indirect illumination, outputting indirect radiance $L_i$.
- **Comparison:** NeRFs are generally slower to train and render than 3DGS but offer smoother geometry and better handling of transparent/refractive surfaces without explicit primitives.

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
Combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting. On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), ATE RMSE (m): ORB-SLAM3 0.052, Ours 3.190. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences. ORB-SLAM achieves best ATE, but combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. Unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S1, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
A neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure. Uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization. Employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly. Integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S2].

### MNE-SLAM
The first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange. Uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module. Introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S2].

### HERO-SLAM
A SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings. Uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning. Extensive evaluations show superior performance and robustness over prior implicit field-based SLAM methods, especially under challenging conditions [S2].

### PIN-SLAM
A globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation. Replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure. Mapping alternates with odometry. The mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S2].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
An active mapping approach that autonomously selects next-best-views to maximize information gain.
- **Initialization:** Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M(c)$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$.
- **Global Policy:** Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$.
- **Local Policy:** Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$.
- **Action:** Move agent to $s^*$.
- **Observation:** Obtain new image observation $I_t$.
- **Update Database:** Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$.
- **Mapping Optimization:** Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$.
- **Stop Check:** Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold.
- **Fine Stage:** After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M(f)$ with trained coarse model $M_T(c)$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S3, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison.
- **Step:** 1. Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$. 2. Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity. 3. Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$. 4. Discard the L channel. 5. Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences.
- **Formula:** $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth.
- **Parameter:** $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S4].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
Extends passive mapping to active exploration by selecting informative paths.
- **Step:** Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM). Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration. Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent. Compute EIG for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration.
- **Formula:** $P_j = \arg \max_{P_j} \text{tr} \left( \sum_{x_{acq_i} \in P_j} H''[y_{i\_acq}|x_{acq_i}, w^*] \right) H''[w^*|D_{train}]^{-1}$ [S5].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 as a real-time explicit scene representation method [Parent](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md). The integration of 3DGS into SLAM systems began shortly after, with methods like SplaTAM [S4] and GS-SLAM [S4] demonstrating the feasibility of using Gaussians for simultaneous localization and mapping. Early approaches often combined traditional SLAM backends (like ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping [S1, unofficial], while subsequent works focused on unified differentiable frameworks that jointly optimize poses and Gaussians. The field has expanded to include multi-agent systems (CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM) [S2], active mapping strategies (ActiveRMAP, FisherRF) [S3, unofficial][S5], and hybrid representations combining neural fields with Gaussians (GPS-SLAM) [S2].
