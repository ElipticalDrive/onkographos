# TangoGS

Also called: Gauss What You Need

## Overview
TangoGS is a density controller for 3D Gaussian Splatting that automatically scales the number of Gaussian primitives across different capture sizes without per-capture retuning [S1, unofficial]. It combines a capture-derived learning allowance (model sizing) with training-based adaptation (quality feedback) to determine the target Gaussian population [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on fixed defaults or per-capture tuning, TangoGS ensures the model budget is invariant to redundant views by using track-adjusted pixels normalized against standard scenes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
TangoGS operates by first calculating a target Gaussian count based on the capture characteristics, then dynamically adjusting the actual number of Gaussians during training based on reconstruction quality.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Capture metadata: number of images $N_{\text{img}}$ (including held-out), resolution $W \times H$, and sparse reconstruction track data (mean track length) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Training metrics: mean per-image PSNR $P$ from the preceding calibration epoch [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Target Gaussian count $T_{\text{adapt}}$ for densification [S1, unofficial].
    *   Keep fraction $\rho$ for pruning [S1, unofficial].
    *   Final Gaussian population $N_G$ at the end of training [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Population:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$. Parameters and Adam optimizer buffers are stored in contiguous memory [S1, unofficial].
*   **Morton-Ordered Groups:** To align with batch removal operations, parameters and buffers are clustered into groups of 128 elements ordered by Morton codes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sensitivity Scores:** An accumulator for each Gaussian, storing the opacity-weighted effect on training views, computed via a backward pass with a unit upstream image multiplier [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas and Constants
The method uses the following constants [S1, unofficial]:
*   $g \approx 0.063362106$: Effective Gaussian count scaling factor.
*   $T_{\text{ref}} = 2,462,476$: Reference count, the rounded geometric mean of the 13 standard linear allowances.
*   $s = 1.7$: Scaling factor for final selection.
*   $c = 0.0075$: Coefficient for PSNR-based pruning.
*   $P^* = 20 \text{ dB}$: Threshold PSNR for no-removal.

**1. Learning Allowance Calculation**
The linear allowance $T_{\text{lin}}$ is derived from the capture image count $N_{\text{img}}$ [S1, unofficial]:
$$ T_{\text{lin}} = \text{round} \left[ 0.90 \cdot \text{round}(0.07040234 \cdot N_{\text{img}}) \right] $$
Nested rounding implements the effective scaling factor $g$ to integer precision [S1, unofficial].

The capped allowance $T_{\text{cap}}$ scales with the square root of the linear allowance to reduce spread between scenes [S1, unofficial]:
$$ T_{\text{cap}} = \text{round} \left[ T_{\text{ref}} \cdot \left( \frac{T_{\text{lin}}}{T_{\text{ref}}} \right)^{0.5} \right] $$

**2. Quality Feedback and Pruning Threshold**
At each growth or pruning event, the mean per-image training PSNR $P$ from the preceding epoch is used to compute the keep fraction $\rho$ [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 \cdot P $$
Rounding the intercept places the final no-removal threshold at approximately 19.995 dB [S1, unofficial]. If $P \le P^*$, $\rho$ is effectively capped such that no removal occurs in the final selection stage [S1, unofficial].

**3. Final Selection**
After densification ends, a single prune selects the final population $N_G$ from the pre-selection population $N_{\text{pre}}$ [S1, unofficial]:
$$ f = \min(1, s \cdot \rho) $$
$$ N_G = N_{\text{pre}} \cdot f^h $$
where $h$ is the number of remaining events in a reference pruning schedule spaced 8% of total training iterations apart [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Split/Clone Threshold:** 0.001 (maximum allowed gradient magnitude) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Opacity Decay:** Factor 0.5 per step, floor 1/128 [S1, unofficial].
*   **SH-rest Learning-Rate Divisor:** 20 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Epoch Spacing Unit:** 150 epochs (for runs $\ge$ 150 epochs) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Iteration Counts:** 30,000 for standard scenes, 60,000 for large captures [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Seeds:** 4114 and 4115 [S1, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Pre-Training Setup:**
    *   Calculate $T_{\text{lin}}$ using $N_{\text{img}}$.
    *   Calculate $T_{\text{cap}}$ using $T_{\text{lin}}$ and $T_{\text{ref}}$.
    *   Initialize Gaussian population with initial point count (e.g., from SfM).

2.  **Training Loop (per iteration):**
    *   Render image from current view.
    *   Compute photometric loss.
    *   Backward pass: Accumulate sensitivity scores for all Gaussians using a unit upstream image multiplier [S1, unofficial].
    *   Update Gaussian parameters using Adam optimizer.

3.  **Epoch End (Calibration):**
    *   Compute mean per-image PSNR $P$ over the just-completed training epoch [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Growth/Pruning Events (Triggered periodically):**
    *   **Check Schedule:**
        *   Densification starts at 2% of total iterations, ends at 80% [S1, unofficial].
        *   Soft prunes begin at ~9% of total iterations [S1, unofficial].
        *   Growth events are spaced 5 epochs apart (scaled proportionally for runs < 150 epochs, min 1 epoch) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Compute Target:**
        *   Calculate $\rho$ using current $P$.
        *   Calculate $T_{\text{adapt}} = \rho \cdot T_{\text{cap}}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Budgeted Densification:**
        *   Ramp linearly from current count to $T_{\text{adapt}}$ over the growth window [S1, unofficial].
        *   Add Gaussians via splitting/cloning based on gradient magnitude threshold (0.001) [S1, unofficial].
        *   Newly created Gaussians are exempt from immediate pruning [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Soft Pruning (if applicable):**
        *   Rank Gaussians present *before* growth using accumulated sensitivity scores [S1, unofficial].
        *   Keep top fraction $\rho$ of these Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
        *   Remove the rest.
        *   **Guard:** If removal count > 90% of population, abort event [S1, unofficial].
        *   **Alignment:** Round removal count down to nearest multiple of 128 [S1, unofficial].
        *   **Buffer Management:** Uncluster, filter, and recluster remaining Gaussians into Morton-ordered groups of 128 [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Final Selection (at 80% iterations):**
    *   Let $N_{\text{pre}}$ be the population entering this event.
    *   Calculate $f = \min(1, s \cdot \rho)$ using $P$ from the preceding epoch [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate $N_G = N_{\text{pre}} \cdot f^h$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Rank all $N_{\text{pre}}$ Gaussians by sensitivity score.
    *   Keep the top $N_G$ Gaussians.
    *   Optimize this final population for the remainder of training [S1, unofficial].

```python
def tango_gs_training(capture_metadata, total_iterations, initial_gaussians):
    """
    Main training loop for TangoGS.
    capture_metadata: dict with 'N_img', 'mean_track_length', etc.
    total_iterations: int, e.g., 30000 or 60000
    initial_gaussians: list of Gaussian objects
    """
    # Constants
    T_ref = 2462476
    g_effective = 0.063362106
    s_final = 1.7
    c_prune = 0.0075
    P_star = 20.0
    split_clone_threshold = 0.001
    opacity_decay_factor = 0.5
    opacity_floor = 1.0 / 128.0
    sh_lr_divisor = 20
    group_size = 128
    
    # 1. Pre-Training: Calculate Allowance
    N_img = capture_metadata['N_img']
    T_lin = round(0.90 * round(0.07040234 * N_img))
    T_cap = round(T_ref * (T_lin / T_ref) ** 0.5)
    
    # Schedule parameters
    densification_start = 0.02 * total_iterations
    densification_end = 0.80 * total_iterations
    soft_prune_start = 0.09 * total_iterations
    
    # Epoch spacing logic (assuming 1 epoch = N_views iterations)
    # For simplicity, we assume epoch_count is tracked. 
    # Spacing is 5 epochs for runs >= 150 epochs, scaled down for shorter runs.
    # Let's assume a function get_epoch_spacing(total_epochs) exists or is calculated.
    # Here we use a placeholder for epoch-based scheduling logic.
    
    gaussians = initial_gaussians
    current_iteration = 0
    
    # Sensitivity score buffer
    sensitivity_scores = {g.id: 0.0 for g in gaussians}
    
    while current_iteration < total_iterations:
        # Standard 3DGS training step
        view = get_next_training_view(current_iteration)
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, view)
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, view.target_image)
        
        # Backward pass with sensitivity scoring
        # LiteGS kernel substitutes unit upstream image multiplier
        gradients = backward_pass(loss, gaussians, unit_upstream_multiplier=True)
        
        # Accumulate sensitivity scores
        for g in gaussians:
            # Sensitivity is opacity-weighted effect on training views
            # Simplified: accumulate gradient magnitude weighted by opacity
            sensitivity_scores[g.id] += g.opacity * norm(gradients[g.id].position)
            
        # Update parameters
        update_gaussians(gaussians, gradients, sh_lr_divisor=sh_lr_divisor)
        
        # Opacity decay
        for g in gaussians:
            if g.opacity > opacity_floor:
                g.opacity *= opacity_decay_factor
            else:
                g.opacity = opacity_floor
                
        current_iteration += 1
        
        # Check for Growth/Pruning Events
        # Note: Exact epoch calculation depends on view count per epoch.
        # We approximate event triggers based on iteration fractions for clarity.
        
        is_densification_phase = densification_start <= current_iteration <= densification_end
        is_soft_prune_phase = current_iteration >= soft_prune_start
        
        # Trigger growth/prune every 5 epochs (scaled)
        # For implementation, we check if current iteration aligns with epoch boundaries
        if is_event_trigger(current_iteration):
            
            # Calibrate Quality Feedback
            P = compute_mean_per_image_psnr(gaussians, validation_set_or_recent_views)
            
            # Calculate Keep Fraction
            rho = 0.7382 - c_prune * P
            
            # Budgeted Densification
            if is_densification_phase:
                # Ramp target from initial count to T_adapt
                # T_adapt depends on rho and T_cap
                T_adapt = rho * T_cap
                
                # Linear ramp logic:
                # progress = (current_iteration - densification_start) / (densification_end - densification_start)
                # target_count = initial_count + (T_adapt - initial_count) * progress
                # Add Gaussians via split/clone if count < target_count
                # Split/Clone based on gradient threshold (0.001)
                gaussians = densify_gaussians(gaussians, T_adapt, split_clone_threshold)
                
            # Soft Pruning
            if is_soft_prune_phase:
                # Rank Gaussians present BEFORE growth
                # Note: In joint events, growth anticipates removal; only pre-growth Gaussians are pruned
                pre_growth_gaussians = gaussians # Assuming densify_gaussians returns new list
                
                # Sort by sensitivity score
                sorted_gaussians = sort_by_sensitivity(pre_growth_gaussians, sensitivity_scores)
                
                # Keep top fraction rho
                keep_count = int(len(sorted_gaussians) * rho)
                
                # Guard against catastrophic collapse
                if keep_count < 0.10 * len(sorted_gaussians):
                    print("Guard abort: removal > 90%")
                    keep_count = int(0.10 * len(sorted_gaussians))
                    
                retained_gaussians = sorted_gaussians[:keep_count]
                
                # Alignment: Round down to multiple of 128
                aligned_keep_count = (keep_count // group_size) * group_size
                retained_gaussians = retained_gaussians[:aligned_keep_count]
                
                # Buffer Management: Recluster into Morton-ordered groups of 128
                gaussians = recluster_morton(retained_gaussians, group_size)
                
                # Reset sensitivity scores for next epoch
                sensitivity_scores = {g.id: 0.0 for g in gaussians}
                
        # Final Selection at 80%
        if current_iteration == densification_end:
            N_pre = len(gaussians)
            f = min(1.0, s_final * rho)
            # h is number of remaining events in reference schedule (8% spacing)
            # For simplicity, assume h is a constant or calculated from schedule
            h = calculate_h(total_iterations, current_iteration)
            
            N_G = int(N_pre * (f ** h))
            
            # Rank all N_pre Gaussians
            sorted_gaussians = sort_by_sensitivity(gaussians, sensitivity_scores)
            
            # Keep top N_G
            final_gaussians = sorted_gaussians[:N_G]
            
            # Optimize final population
            gaussians = final_gaussians
            sensitivity_scores = {g.id: 0.0 for g in gaussians}
            
    return gaussians
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Catastrophic Collapse:** If a pruning event would remove more than 90% of the Gaussian population, the event is aborted [S1, unofficial].
*   **Buffer Alignment:** Removal counts are rounded down to the nearest multiple of 128 to maintain group alignment for Morton-ordered clusters [S1, unofficial].
*   **New Gaussians:** Newly created Gaussians during densification are exempt from pruning until they have trained [S1, unofficial].
*   **Joint Events:** In joint growth/pruning events, growth is scheduled to anticipate the following removal; only Gaussians scored before the growth phase are eligible for pruning [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Capture-Conditioned Learning Allowance](10214-capture-conditioned-learning-allowance.md)
A method to determine the target Gaussian population size for 3D Gaussian Splatting based on capture characteristics, specifically using track-adjusted pixels normalized against standard scenes, to replace fixed defaults. It sets apart from sibling methods by ensuring the model budget is invariant to redundant views (e.g., duplicated images), whereas alternatives like total pixel count scale with view count and SfM point counts scale with matched-observation density rather than capture scale.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time scales with the number of Gaussians and iterations. TangoGS uses a shared configuration across all 21 captures, whereas baselines require per-capture tuning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is proportional to the final Gaussian count $N_G$. TangoGS matches the mean PSNR of the best-performing evaluated baseline, LeGS, with 48% fewer Gaussians on 13 standard benchmark scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On 13 standard benchmark scenes, TangoGS matches the mean PSNR of LeGS with 48% fewer Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. On 8 large captures, the same configuration automatically scales to larger models when necessary [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Automatic scaling across different capture sizes without retuning; robustness to redundant views; reduced model size compared to baselines.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate capture metadata (image count, track data); sensitivity scoring adds overhead to the backward pass (though LiteGS implements it efficiently without additional renders) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When deploying 3D Gaussian Splatting across diverse capture sizes without manual hyperparameter tuning.
*   **When Not to Use:** When per-capture optimization is feasible and maximum possible PSNR is required regardless of model size.

## Background
TangoGS was introduced in the paper "Gauss What You Need: Compact Gaussian Splatting Across Scene Scales" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework and integrates concepts from LiteGS for sensitivity scoring [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the challenge of scaling Gaussian Splatting to different capture sizes without manual intervention, a common issue in inverse volume rendering.
