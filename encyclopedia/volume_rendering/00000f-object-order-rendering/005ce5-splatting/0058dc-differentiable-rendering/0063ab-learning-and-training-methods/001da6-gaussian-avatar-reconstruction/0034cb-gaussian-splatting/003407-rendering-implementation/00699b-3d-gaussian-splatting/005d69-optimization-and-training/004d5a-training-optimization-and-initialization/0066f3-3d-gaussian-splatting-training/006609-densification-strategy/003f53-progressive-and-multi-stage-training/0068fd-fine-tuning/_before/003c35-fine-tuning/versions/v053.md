# Fine-tuning

Also called: 3DGS Fine-tuning, Fine-tuning Strategy, Gaussian Splatting Fine-tuning

## Overview
Fine-tuning in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the phase of optimization where a pre-initialized or pre-trained set of Gaussian primitives is further refined to improve rendering fidelity, adapt to specific data distributions, or correct initialization artifacts. It serves as a bridge between coarse initialization (e.g., from Structure-from-Motion, feed-forward networks, or grid volumes) and the final high-quality radiance field. Unlike standard training from scratch, fine-tuning leverages existing structural priors, allowing for faster convergence and higher quality results [S2, S5]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) by focusing on the refinement of an already established primitive set rather than the hierarchical construction of the scene representation, and from [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md) by often implying a specific phase or strategy applied after initial convergence or during specialized adaptation tasks [S2][S6, unofficial].

## How it works
The core mechanism of fine-tuning involves optimizing the parameters of an existing set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ using gradient-based descent, often with specific constraints or auxiliary losses depending on the variant. The process typically follows these steps:

1.  **Initialization:** A set of 3D Gaussians is provided. This may come from a feed-forward network predicting geometry and color [S2], a grid-based volume [S4], or a pre-trained backbone [S16908-backbone-fine-tuning.md].
2.  **Optimization Loop:** The parameters of the Gaussians (means $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$ (scale $s$ and rotation $q$), opacities $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}$) are updated by minimizing a rendering loss.
3.  **Densification/Pruning (Optional):** Depending on the variant, the number of Gaussians may be adjusted. Standard 3DGS fine-tuning enables growing and pruning during this phase to remove redundant primitives and fill gaps [S2]. Other variants use rendering loss to identify holes and high-error regions for densification [S3].
4.  **Convergence:** The process continues until the rendering quality stabilizes or a fixed number of iterations is reached.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Initial set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_{init}$ [S2, S4].
    *   Training images (and optionally depth maps) $\{I_v, D_v\}$ [S3][S6, unofficial].
    *   Camera poses $P_v$ [S3].
    *   Loss weights (e.g., $\lambda_{color}, \lambda_{depth}, \lambda_{SSIM}$) [S3].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Refined set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_{final}$ [S2][S6, unofficial].
    *   Rendered images with improved fidelity [S2, S3].

### Data Structures
The data structure remains the standard 3D Gaussian representation as defined in the parent article [Parent]. Each Gaussian $g_k$ contains:
*   $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
*   $\Sigma_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix, parameterized by scale $s_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation $q_k \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion).
*   $\alpha_k \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
*   $\mathbf{c}_k$: Spherical Harmonic coefficients.

### Formulas
The total loss function for fine-tuning typically combines color and depth errors, often with an SSIM term for perceptual quality [S3]:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{mapping} = \lambda_{color} \| \hat{C} - C \|_1 + \lambda_{depth} \| \hat{D} - D \|_1 + \lambda_{SSIM} SSIM(\hat{C}, C)
$$

Where:
*   $\hat{C}, \hat{D}$ are the rendered color and depth images.
*   $C, D$ are the ground truth images.
*   $SSIM(\cdot)$ is the Structural Similarity Index Measure.

For opacity-based densification, the rendered opacity $O(x,y)$ at pixel $(x,y)$ is calculated as [S3]:

$$
O = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$

Where $N$ is the set of 2D Gaussians overlapping the pixel, and $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian.

### Parameters
*   **Learning Rate:** Typically $1 \times 10^{-3}$ for Adam optimizer [S2, S5].
*   **Iterations:** Varies by task. Naive fine-tuning may use 10,000 steps [S5]. EVolSplat uses standard 3DGS training iterations [S2].
*   **Thresholds for Densification:**
    *   $\tau_{opa}$: Opacity threshold for hole detection [S3].
    *   $\tau_{color}$: Color error threshold [S3].
    *   $\tau_{depth}$: Normalized depth error threshold [S3].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode outlines a generic fine-tuning loop with rendering-guided densification [S3] and standard optimization [S2].

```python
def fine_tuning_3dgs(G_init, images, depths, poses, config):
    """
    G_init: Initial set of 3D Gaussians
    images: List of ground truth color images
    depths: List of ground truth depth images
    poses: List of camera poses
    config: Dictionary containing hyperparameters
    """
    G = G_init
    optimizer = Adam(G.parameters(), lr=config.lr)
    
    for iteration in range(config.total_iterations):
        # 1. Sample a random view
        idx = random_index(len(images))
        C_gt = images[idx]
        D_gt = depths[idx]
        P = poses[idx]
        
        # 2. Render current Gaussians
        C_pred, D_pred = render(G, P)
        
        # 3. Compute Loss
        loss_color = L1(C_pred, C_gt)
        loss_depth = L1(D_pred, D_gt)
        loss_ssim = SSIM(C_pred, C_gt)
        loss = config.lambda_color * loss_color + \
               config.lambda_depth * loss_depth + \
               config.lambda_ssim * loss_ssim
        
        # 4. Backpropagate and Update
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # 5. Densification (if enabled and at interval)
        if iteration % config.densify_interval == 0:
            # Identify regions for densification
            # Opacity holes
            opacity_map = compute_opacity_map(G, P)
            holes = opacity_map < config.threshold_opa
            
            # High color error
            color_error = abs(C_pred - C_gt)
            high_color_err = color_error > config.threshold_color
            
            # High depth error (normalized)
            depth_error = abs(D_pred - D_gt)
            normalized_depth_err = depth_error / D_pred
            high_depth_err = normalized_depth_err > config.threshold_depth
            
            # Union of regions to densify
            densify_mask = holes | high_color_err | high_depth_err
            
            if densify_mask.any():
                # Add new Gaussians in densified regions
                # For depth images, project corresponding depths directly [S3]
                new_Gaussians = create_gaussians_from_mask(densify_mask, P, D_gt)
                G = G + new_Gaussians
            
            # Pruning (optional, based on opacity)
            G = prune_low_opacity(G, config.opacity_threshold)
            
    return G
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Forgetting:** In continuous mapping or incremental fine-tuning, Gaussians may overfit to the latest frame, causing quality drop for previous frames [S3]. Regularization or replay buffers are needed.
*   **Redundancy:** Feed-forward initialization may produce redundant Gaussians [S2]. Pruning is essential to reduce memory consumption.
*   **Scale Ambiguity:** Depth error thresholds must be normalized by rendered depth to handle varying scales [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Exponentially Ascending Gradient Threshold](9684-exponentially-ascending-gradient-threshold.md)
A densification control mechanism that dynamically adjusts gradient thresholds based on training progress, allowing aggressive densification early and restricting it later to prevent overfitting.

### [SplatFormer Training Objective & Refinement](11745-splatformer-training-objective-refinement.md)
A learning-based refinement module that applies a point transformer to predict attribute residuals, improving generalization to out-of-distribution views.

### [MCMC-based 3DGS / SGLD for 3DGS](11917-mcmc-based-3dgs-sgld-for-3dgs.md)
Reformulates 3DGS updates as Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, removing the need for manual densification heuristics by introducing noise for exploration.

### [Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting](12318-deblurring-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Uses an MLP to manipulate Gaussian covariance and means during training to reconstruct sharp scenes from blurry inputs.

### [Supplementary Point Addition for Sparse Point Clouds](12323-supplementary-point-addition-for-sparse-point-clouds.md)
Injects synthetic points into sparse regions after fixed iterations to compensate for poor SfM extraction in defocused areas.

### [Augmented Radiance Field](13218-augmented-radiance-field.md)
Adds Gaussian kernels with view-dependent opacity to model specular components separately from diffuse radiance.

### [Frequency Regularization for Densification](13484-frequency-regularization-for-densification.md)
Minimizes frequency spectrum discrepancy in Fourier space to guide densification in over-reconstructed regions.

### [Error-Guided Redistribution](14037-error-guided-redistribution.md)
Dynamically adjusts Gaussian distribution based on rendering errors, targeting hard gradients and coarse-scale errors.

### [CoherentGS](14072-coherentgs.md)
Enforces structural coherency in 2D image space to stabilize optimization for sparse novel view synthesis.

### [Pruning and Sparsity Control](14431-pruning-and-sparsity-control.md)
Reduces active Gaussians using geometric or volume-based criteria to lower memory and storage.

### [Rendering-Free Pruning via Hessian Importance](14447-rendering-free-pruning-via-hessian-importance.md)
Uses an analytically approximated Hessian field to identify and remove unimportant primitives without rendering.

### [GoF-based Dynamic 3DGS Video Construction](14770-gof-based-dynamic-3dgs-video-construction.md)
Segments video into Groups of Frames and trains independent deformation fields to prevent motion drift.

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
Decouples geometric and appearance optimization from material and lighting decomposition in inverse rendering.

### [TIP-Editor](14859-tip-editor.md)
Enables local editing of 3DGS scenes using text and image prompts with stepwise 2D personalization.

### [StylizedGS](14887-stylizedgs.md)
Performs color matching with a reference style image while preserving geometric structure.

### [SGD (Street View Synthesis with Gaussian Splatting and Diffusion Prior)](15522-sgd-street-view-synthesis-with-gaussian-splatting-and-diffus.md)
Fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion model to regularize 3DGS training for sparse-view street scene synthesis.

### [Backbone Fine-tuning](16908-backbone-fine-tuning.md)
Adapts the weights of a pre-trained 3D generator backbone alongside the decoder to generate Gaussian attributes.

### [Equivalent Distribution Reorganization](16968-equivalent-distribution-reorganization.md)
Rebuilds Gaussian centers and covariances from the empirical distribution to resolve parameterization degeneration.

### [S2Gaussian](17154-s2gaussian.md)
A two-stage framework initializing high-resolution Gaussians from low-resolution representations and refining with super-resolved supervision.

### [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)
Optimizes 3DGS fields incrementally as new images are acquired, using hierarchical weighting and load-balancing losses.

### [FreeFix](17532-freefix.md)
Improves extrapolated views using pretrained 2D image diffusion models with per-pixel confidence masks.

### [Localized Point Management (LPM)](17850-localized-point-management-lpm.md)
Identifies error-contributing zones via cross-view feature mapping and performs localized point addition and pruning.

### Gaussian Densification via Rendering Loss
A strategy to add new 3D Gaussians to the map by identifying unobserved regions (holes) and regions with high rendering error, using opacity, color, and depth thresholds [S3]. It identifies holes where rendered opacity $O(x,y) < \tau_{opa}$, and densifies regions where normalized depth error $E_{depth}(x,y)/D(x,y) > \tau_{depth}$ or color error $E_{color}(x,y) > \tau_{color}$ [S3]. For depth images, corresponding depths are projected directly [S3].

### Point Densification
A process in original 3DGS that adaptively controls the number of Gaussian points through clone and split operations to convert a sparse set into a denser one [S4]. In HO-Gaussian, this is performed at regular intervals to provide new positions to the Gaussian pipeline, populating problematic regions that were not covered by initial SfM points [S4].

### Naive Fine-tuning
A generator fine-tuning method identical to model rewriting except freely optimizing all weights of the generator [S5]. It is optimized over 10,000 steps with a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ [S5].

### Image-space Gaussian Fine-tuning
A fine-tuning process to account for different visual modes in the volume by generating multiple radiance fields using different transfer functions and optimizing the Gaussian set against rendered images [S6, unofficial]. It selects a series of transfer functions $\{TF_h\}$, generates radiance fields $RF_h = TF_h(F)$, renders 64 images for supervision, and backpropagates to update the Gaussian set $G_h$ [S6, unofficial]. In practice, 5 transfer functions are selected for each volume [S6, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Fine-tuning typically requires fewer iterations than training from scratch if good initialization is provided [S2]. However, densification operations (splitting, cloning) add overhead proportional to the number of Gaussians [Parent].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians. Fine-tuning with pruning can reduce memory consumption compared to initial feed-forward outputs [S2].
*   **Performance:** Fine-tuning can achieve photorealism on par with or surpassing other methods, leveraging pretrained weights for faster convergence [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Faster convergence, higher quality results, ability to adapt to specific data distributions [S2, S5].
*   **Limitations:** Risk of overfitting to training views, forgetting in incremental settings [S3], dependency on initialization quality [S4].
*   **When to use:** When a good initial set of Gaussians is available, or when adapting a pre-trained model to a new domain [S2, S5].
*   **When not to use:** When initialization is poor and densification cannot recover missing geometry [S4], or when computational resources are extremely limited and densification overhead is prohibitive.

## Background
Fine-tuning is a common strategy in deep learning, originally applied to CNNs where lower layers are fixed and higher layers are trained on specific datasets [S1]. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, it has been adapted to refine Gaussian primitives for better rendering quality, handle specific tasks like SLAM [S3], urban scene reconstruction [S4], and volume visualization [S6, unofficial]. The concept of "naive fine-tuning" in generative models involves optimizing all weights of a generator [S5]. In 3DGS, fine-tuning often involves enabling growing and pruning during the optimization phase [S2].
