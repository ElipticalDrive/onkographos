# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) stabilizes the optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting by minimizing the spectral discrepancy between rendered images and ground truth, specifically addressing the problem of over-reconstruction where redundant Gaussians form in already well-reconstructed regions [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which yields small gradients in over-reconstructed areas and stalls densification, FR increases gradients in these regions by explicitly disentangling amplitude and phase components, thereby guiding adaptive densification toward high-frequency detail [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, where $H$ and $W$ are height and width.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar regularization loss terms $d_a$ (amplitude discrepancy) and $d_p$ (phase discrepancy), which are added to the total training loss.

**Data Structures**
*   **Frequency Domain:** Complex-valued matrices $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ of size $H \times W$.
*   **Amplitude/Phase:** Real-valued matrices of size $H \times W$.

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Fourier Transform:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for both the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth $I$. For a pixel at coordinates $(x, y)$ and frequency coordinates $(u, v)$:
    $$
    F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    $$
    \hat{F}(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \hat{I}(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    where $i$ is the imaginary unit [S2].

2.  **Decompose into Amplitude and Phase:** For each frequency component $(u, v)$, calculate the real part $Re(u, v)$ and imaginary part $Im(u, v)$ of the complex frequency value.
    *   **Amplitude:**
        $$
        |F(u, v)| = \sqrt{Re(u, v)^2 + Im(u, v)^2}
        $$
        $$
        |\hat{F}(u, v)| = \sqrt{\hat{Re}(u, v)^2 + \hat{Im}(u, v)^2}
        $$
    *   **Phase:**
        $$
        \angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{Im(u, v)}{Re(u, v)}\right)
        $$
        $$
        \angle \hat{F}(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\hat{Im}(u, v)}{\hat{Re}(u, v)}\right)
        $$
    [S2]. Note: In implementation, use `atan2(Im, Re)` to handle quadrants correctly [G].

3.  **Compute Discrepancies:** Calculate the root-mean-square error (Euclidean metric) for amplitude and phase across all frequency components.
    *   **Total Amplitude Discrepancy ($d_a$):**
        $$
        d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2}
        $$
    *   **Total Phase Discrepancy ($d_p$):**
        $$
        d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2}
        $$
    [S2].

4.  **Loss Integration:** The total frequency regularization loss is typically a weighted sum of $d_a$ and $d_p$. This loss is backpropagated through the differentiable rasterization pipeline to update Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients) [S2].

**Parameters**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer [S2].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
*   **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS are retained [S2].
*   **Typical Values:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Over-reconstruction Bias:** Naively applying FR without distinguishing frequency bands can bias optimization toward undesirable artifacts and restrict densification [S2]. This is often mitigated by "Frequency Annealing," which uses low-pass and dynamic high-pass filters to progressively regularize low-to-high frequencies [S2].
*   **Gradient Vanishing:** In regions already well-reconstructed, L1 loss gradients are small. FR is specifically designed to counteract this by maintaining significant gradients in these regions, preventing the stagnation of densification [S2].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def frequency_regularization_loss(rendered_image: torch.Tensor, ground_truth: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """
    Computes the frequency regularization loss (amplitude and phase discrepancy)
    between rendered and ground truth images.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (C, H, W). 
                        Assumes normalized pixel values [0, 1] or [-1, 1].
        ground_truth: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (C, H, W).
        
    Returns:
        Scalar tensor representing the total frequency regularization loss.
    """
    # Ensure input is (C, H, W) for FFT
    if rendered_image.dim() == 3 and rendered_image.shape[0] != 3:
        rendered_image = rendered_image.permute(2, 0, 1)
        ground_truth = ground_truth.permute(2, 0, 1)
    
    # Compute 2D FFT for each channel
    # torch.fft.fft2 expects (H, W) or (..., H, W). 
    # We apply it to the spatial dimensions.
    F_rendered = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image)
    F_gt = torch.fft.fft2(ground_truth)
    
    # Extract Real and Imaginary parts
    Re_r = F_rendered.real
    Im_r = F_rendered.imag
    Re_g = F_gt.real
    Im_g = F_gt.imag
    
    # Compute Amplitude
    Amp_r = torch.sqrt(Re_r**2 + Im_r**2)
    Amp_g = torch.sqrt(Re_g**2 + Im_g**2)
    
    # Compute Phase using atan2 for numerical stability
    Phase_r = torch.atan2(Im_r, Re_r)
    Phase_g = torch.atan2(Im_g, Re_g)
    
    # Compute Discrepancies (RMSE)
    # Note: The source formula sums over x,y but uses u,v indices. 
    # In discrete FFT, the output grid is the same size. 
    # We average over all spatial/frequency bins.
    
    # Amplitude Discrepancy
    amp_diff = Amp_r - Amp_g
    d_a = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(amp_diff**2))
    
    # Phase Discrepancy
    phase_diff = Phase_r - Phase_g
    d_p = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(phase_diff**2))
    
    # Total Loss (weights not specified in source, assume equal or configurable)
    # [G]: In practice, weights might be tuned. Source cites d_a and d_p as the components.
    total_loss = d_a + d_p
    
    return total_loss
```

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

### DWTGS
DWTGS rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The addition of 2D FFT and inverse operations (if used for annealing) per iteration increases computational overhead compared to pure spatial L1 loss. FFT complexity is $O(N \log N)$ where $N = H \times W$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing complex frequency matrices of size $H \times W$ for both rendered and ground truth images.
*   **Measured Performance:** In the "Bicycle" scene, FR significantly increased pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to the baseline method (equivalent to 3D-GS with L1 loss), enabling better adaptive densification [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively alleviates over-reconstruction; improves Gaussian densification by revealing over-reconstructed regions that L1 loss misses; pushes Gaussians toward high-frequency regions [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Naive application can bias optimization toward artifacts if frequency bands are not annealed [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene exhibits over-reconstruction (blurry or redundant Gaussians in simple areas) or under-reconstruction in detail-rich regions.
*   **When not to use:** When computational budget is extremely tight and spatial L1 loss is sufficient for coarse reconstruction.

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced as a method to improve 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization by addressing convergence speed, visual artifacts, and improper Gaussians [S1]. It was notably implemented in "FreGS: 3D Gaussian Splatting with Progressive Frequency Regularization" [S2], which demonstrated that minimizing the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth alleviates over-reconstruction. The technique is part of a broader family of regularization methods including geometry constraints and depth supervision [S1].
