# Fine-tuning

Also called: 3DGS Fine-tuning, Fine-tuning Strategy, Gaussian Splatting Fine-tuning

## Overview
Fine-tuning in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the phase of optimization where a pre-initialized or pre-trained representation is refined to achieve high-fidelity rendering, often by adapting the Gaussian primitives' attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients) to specific scene data [S2, S6]. It solves the problem of initializing a valid geometric backbone and then iteratively densifying and optimizing primitives to match input views, distinguishing itself from end-to-end training by leveraging prior knowledge or coarse initialization to accelerate convergence and improve stability [S2]. Unlike naive optimization which may overfit or fail to generalize, fine-tuning strategies often decouple geometry and appearance or employ specific regularization to prevent error coupling and forgetting [S2, S6].

## How it works
The fine-tuning process typically follows an initialization phase and involves iterative optimization of the Gaussian set $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$. The exact steps vary depending on whether the method is a direct feed-forward fine-tuning, a backbone adaptation, or a densification-guided refinement.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of initialized 3D Gaussian primitives (from SfM, feed-forward prediction, or grid-based volume), input images (RGB/Depth), and camera poses [S2, S3, S4].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters: mean position $\mu_k$, covariance $\Sigma_k$ (derived from scale $s_k$ and rotation $q_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k$ [Parent, S2].

### Data Structures
The Gaussian set is stored as a contiguous array or list of structures. Each Gaussian $g_k$ contains:
*   $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
*   $s_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale (log-space for optimization stability).
*   $q_k \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation (quaternion).
*   $\alpha_k \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity (sigmoid output).
*   $\mathbf{c}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times (D+1)^2}$: SH coefficients for color, where $D$ is the degree of spherical harmonics [S2].

### Steps

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Generate initial Gaussians. This can be done via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points [S4], a direct feed-forward network predicting geometry and color [S2], or a grid-based volume [S4].
    *   If using a pre-trained backbone (e.g., for generative models), create a copy of the pre-trained 3D generator and optimize its weights alongside the decoder [S6].

2.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   For each training step $t$:
        *   Render the scene using differentiable rasterization to produce color $\hat{C}$ and depth $\hat{D}$ [S3].
        *   Compute loss functions. Common losses include L1 loss between rendered and ground truth images, SSIM, and entropy regularization [S2, S6].
        *   Update Gaussian parameters using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [S2].
        *   If using rendering-guided densification (e.g., in SLAM contexts), check for holes or high error regions [S3].

3.  **Densification and Pruning (Conditional):**
    *   Enable growing and pruning of primitives during fine-tuning to reduce redundancy and fill gaps [S2].
    *   **Hole Filling:** If rendered opacity $O(x,y) < \tau_{opa}$, mark region for densification [S3].
    *   **Error-Based Densification:** If color error $E_{color}(x,y) > \tau_{color}$ or normalized depth error $E_{depth}(x,y)/D(x,y) > \tau_{depth}$, add new Gaussians [S3].
    *   **Point Densification:** Perform clone and split operations at regular intervals to populate problematic regions, converting sparse sets to denser ones [S4].

4.  **Backbone Adaptation (If applicable):**
    *   Optimize the weights of the pre-trained 3D generator (backbone) to adapt tri-plane features for Gaussian attributes, which have scale and rotation influencing adjacent regions unlike NeRF's local color/density [S6].

### Formulas

**Opacity Rendering:**
The opacity at pixel $(x,y)$ is computed as [S3, S4]:
$$ O(x,y) = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $N$ are the 2D Gaussians overlapping the pixel, and $\alpha_i$ is the alpha value of the $i$-th Gaussian.

**Color Rendering:**
$$ \mathbf{C}(x,y) = \sum_{i \in N} \mathbf{c}_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color of the $i$-th Gaussian [S4].

**Rendering Loss (SLAM Context):**
$$ \mathcal{L}_{mapping} = \lambda_{color} \|\hat{C} - C\|_1 + \lambda_{depth} \|\hat{D} - D\|_1 + \lambda_{SSIM} \text{SSIM}(\hat{C}, C) $$
where $C$ and $D$ are ground truth color and depth, and $\hat{C}, \hat{D}$ are rendered values [S3].

**Error Thresholds for Densification:**
Densify if [S3]:
$$ O(x,y) < \tau_{opa} \quad \text{OR} \quad E_{color}(x,y) > \tau_{color} \quad \text{OR} \quad \frac{E_{depth}(x,y)}{D(x,y)} > \tau_{depth} $$
where $E_{color}(x,y) = \|\hat{C}(x,y) - C(x,y)\|$ and $E_{depth}(x,y) = \|\hat{D}(x,y) - D(x,y)\|$ [S3].

### Parameters
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ for Adam optimizer [S2].
*   **Naive Fine-tuning Steps:** 10,000 steps [S5].
*   **Naive Fine-tuning Learning Rate:** $10^{-3}$ [S5].
*   **Opacity Threshold ($\tau_{opa}$):** Predefined threshold for hole detection [S3].
*   **Color Error Threshold ($\tau_{color}$):** Predefined threshold for color error [S3].
*   **Depth Error Threshold ($\tau_{depth}$):** Predefined threshold for normalized depth error [S3].
*   **SH Degree:** Typically set to 3 or 4 for high fidelity [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Forgetting:** In continuous mapping, parameters may overfit to the latest frame, causing quality drop for previous frames. Regularization is needed to alleviate this [S3].
*   **Sparse Initialization:** SfM points often have holes in sky, distant, or low-texture areas. Naive densification may fail to populate these regions without supplementary point addition or grid-based initialization [S4].
*   **Redundancy:** Initial feed-forward Gaussians may be redundant. Fine-tuning must include pruning to reduce memory consumption while maintaining fidelity [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
def fine_tune_gaussians(initial_gaussians, images, poses, config):
    """
    Fine-tune 3D Gaussian Splatting from initial primitives.
    
    Args:
        initial_gaussians: List of Gaussian objects with mu, s, q, alpha, c.
        images: List of input RGB/Depth images.
        poses: List of camera poses.
        config: Configuration dictionary with thresholds and learning rate.
        
    Returns:
        Optimized list of Gaussian objects.
    """
    gaussians = initial_gaussians
    optimizer = Adam(gaussians.parameters(), lr=config['learning_rate'])
    
    for step in range(config['total_steps']):
        # 1. Sample a random image and pose
        idx = random.randint(0, len(images) - 1)
        image = images[idx]
        pose = poses[idx]
        
        # 2. Render scene
        rendered_color, rendered_depth, alpha_map = render(gaussians, pose)
        
        # 3. Compute Loss
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_color, image.color, rendered_depth, image.depth)
        
        # 4. Backpropagate and Update
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # 5. Densification (if enabled and periodic)
        if step % config['densify_interval'] == 0:
            densify_gaussians(gaussians, alpha_map, rendered_color, rendered_depth, 
                              image.color, image.depth, config)
            
        # 6. Pruning (if enabled)
        if step % config['prune_interval'] == 0:
            prune_gaussians(gaussians, config)
            
    return gaussians

def densify_gaussians(gaussians, alpha_map, rendered_color, rendered_depth, 
                      gt_color, gt_depth, config):
    """
    Add new Gaussians based on rendering error and opacity holes.
    """
    tau_opa = config['tau_opa']
    tau_color = config['tau_color']
    tau_depth = config['tau_depth']
    
    new_gaussians = []
    
    for x, y in pixels:
        # Check for holes
        if alpha_map[x, y] < tau_opa:
            new_gaussians.append(create_gaussian_from_depth(rendered_depth[x, y]))
            continue
            
        # Check for color error
        e_color = abs(rendered_color[x, y] - gt_color[x, y])
        if e_color > tau_color:
            new_gaussians.append(create_gaussian_from_gradient(x, y, gaussians))
            continue
            
        # Check for depth error (normalized)
        e_depth = abs(rendered_depth[x, y] - gt_depth[x, y])
        if gt_depth[x, y] > 0 and (e_depth / gt_depth[x, y]) > tau_depth:
            new_gaussians.append(create_gaussian_from_depth(rendered_depth[x, y]))
            
    gaussians.extend(new_gaussians)

def compute_loss(rendered_color, gt_color, rendered_depth, gt_depth):
    """
    Compute L1 and SSIM loss.
    """
    l1_color = l1_loss(rendered_color, gt_color)
    l1_depth = l1_loss(rendered_depth, gt_depth)
    ssim = ssim_loss(rendered_color, gt_color)
    
    lambda_color = 1.0
    lambda_depth = 0.5
    lambda_ssim = 0.2
    
    return lambda_color * l1_color + lambda_depth * l1_depth + lambda_ssim * ssim
```

## Subtopics

### [Exponentially Ascending Gradient Threshold](9684-exponentially-ascending-gradient-threshold.md)
A densification control mechanism that uses an exponentially ascending gradient threshold to regulate splitting and cloning, solving the trade-off between early-stage convergence speed and late-stage overfitting.

### [SplatFormer Training Objective & Refinement](11745-splatformer-training-objective-refinement.md)
A learning-based, feed-forward module that refines initial 3DGS representations to enable robust novel view synthesis from out-of-distribution camera angles by predicting attribute residuals.

### [MCMC-based 3DGS / SGLD for 3DGS](11917-mcmc-based-3dgs-sgld-for-3dgs.md)
Reformulates 3DGS as MCMC samples, converting updates into Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) to remove the need for manual densification heuristics.

### [Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting](12318-deblurring-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
A real-time deblurring framework that reconstructs sharp 3D scenes from blurry input images by manipulating covariance matrices and means using a small MLP.

### [Supplementary Point Addition for Sparse Point Clouds](12323-supplementary-point-addition-for-sparse-point-clouds.md)
A densification technique that injects synthetic points into empty or sparse regions after a fixed number of training iterations to compensate for poor SfM extraction in far-end scenes.

### [Augmented Radiance Field](13218-augmented-radiance-field.md)
A post-enhancement framework that adds Gaussian kernels with view-dependent opacity to explicitly model specular components, solving the decoupling issue of diffuse and specular radiance.

### [Frequency Regularization for Densification](13484-frequency-regularization-for-densification.md)
Addresses over-reconstruction by minimizing the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of the rendered image and the ground truth in Fourier space.

### [Error-Guided Redistribution](14037-error-guided-redistribution.md)
A family of strategies that dynamically adjusts the spatial distribution of Gaussian primitives based on rendering errors or gradient magnitudes, targeting regions with persistent coarse-scale errors.

### [CoherentGS](14072-coherentgs.md)
A regularized optimization method for sparse novel view synthesis that introduces structural coherency by constraining Gaussian movement in 2D image space.

### [Pruning and Sparsity Control](14431-pruning-and-sparsity-control.md)
Methods that reduce the number of active 3D Gaussians to lower memory consumption while maintaining rendering quality, using explicit geometric or volume-based criteria.

### [Rendering-Free Pruning via Hessian Importance](14447-rendering-free-pruning-via-hessian-importance.md)
A post-processing framework that identifies and removes unimportant Gaussian primitives without performing forward rendering passes, using an analytically approximated Hessian field.

### [GoF-based Dynamic 3DGS Video Construction](14770-gof-based-dynamic-3dgs-video-construction.md)
Constructs dynamic 3DGS video by temporally segmenting input sequences into Groups of Frames (GoFs) and independently training Gaussian deformation fields within each segment.

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
A training protocol that decouples geometric and appearance optimization from material and lighting decomposition, preventing entanglement of physical properties.

### [TIP-Editor](14859-tip-editor.md)
A method for editing existing 3DGS scenes using text and image prompts, combining stepwise 2D personalization with score distillation sampling.

### [StylizedGS](14887-stylizedgs.md)
A controllable 3D stylization method that performs color matching with a reference style image while utilizing a 3DGS filter to remove artifacts.

### [SGD (Street View Synthesis with Gaussian Splatting and Diffusion Prior)](15522-sgd-street-view-synthesis-with-gaussian-splatting-and-diffus.md)
Enhances 3DGS by fine-tuning a Stable Diffusion model on driving data to regularize training at unseen viewpoints, addressing sparse-view reconstruction.

### Gaussian Densification via Rendering Loss
A strategy to add new 3D Gaussians to the map by identifying unobserved regions (holes) and regions with high rendering error, using opacity, color, and depth thresholds. It identifies holes where rendered opacity is below a threshold, normalizes depth error by rendered depth to compensate for scale, and adds new Gaussians to densified regions, projecting corresponding depths directly for depth images [S3].

### Point Densification
A process in original 3DGS that adaptively controls the number of Gaussian points through clone and split operations to convert a sparse set into a denser one. In HO-Gaussian, point densification is performed at regular intervals to provide new positions to the Gaussian pipeline, populating problematic regions [S4].

### Naive Fine-tuning
A naive generator fine-tuning method identical to model rewriting except freely optimizing all weights of the generator. It is optimized over 10,000 steps with a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ [S5].

### Backbone Fine-tuning
Optimizing the weights of the pre-trained 3D generator (backbone) alongside the decoder to adapt tri-plane features for Gaussian Splatting attributes, which have scale and rotation influencing adjacent regions unlike NeRF's local color/density. It involves creating a copy of the pre-trained 3D generator and optimizing its weights during the training process [S6].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians $N$ and the number of pixels $P$. Rasterization is $O(N \cdot P)$ in the worst case, but optimized implementations use spatial hashing to reduce this. Fine-tuning iterations are typically 10,000 steps for naive methods [S5] or fewer with pre-trained priors [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing Gaussian parameters. Fine-tuning with densification increases $N$, potentially requiring more memory. Pruning strategies can mitigate this [S2].
*   **Measured Performance:** EVolSplat achieves photorealism on par with or surpassing other methods, leveraging pretrained weights to accelerate network training and enable faster convergence [S2]. High-Fidelity SLAM using Gaussian Splatting achieves state-of-the-art results on synthetic datasets and competitive results on real-world datasets [S3].
*   **Scaling:** Fine-tuning scales well with pre-trained models, as they provide a good initial representation [S2]. However, densification can lead to memory issues if not controlled [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates convergence, improves stability, allows for high-fidelity rendering, and can handle sparse initialization with supplementary techniques [S2, S4].
*   **Limitations:** May suffer from forgetting in continuous mapping [S3], requires careful tuning of densification thresholds [S3], and may overfit to input views if not regularized .
*   **When to Use:** When high-fidelity rendering is required, when initialization is sparse or noisy, or when adapting a pre-trained model to a specific domain [S2, S6].
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and pre-training is not feasible, or when the scene is static and simple enough that direct optimization from SfM points is sufficient [S4].

## Background
Fine-tuning in deep learning generally refers to transferring knowledge from a pre-trained model to a new task by adjusting the model's weights [S1]. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, fine-tuning has emerged as a critical strategy to leverage pre-trained priors (e.g., from generative models or large-scale datasets) to improve initialization and convergence [S2, S6]. The concept of fine-tuning layers in CNNs, such as fixing early layers and training later layers, has been adapted to 3DGS to stabilize training and improve generalization [S1, S6]. Recent works have extended this to include densification strategies guided by rendering loss and backbone adaptation for generative models [S2, S3, S6].
