# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: GS-IR Training Process

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy for inverse rendering with 3D Gaussians that decouples geometric/appearance optimization from material decomposition [S1]. It solves the problem of jointly optimizing conflicting parameters (geometry, appearance, and material) by first establishing a stable geometric and appearance baseline, then fixing those to optimize material properties [S1]. It sets itself apart from single-stage joint optimization by reducing optimization interference, allowing for specialized loss functions like normal smoothing in stage 1 and material/lighting total variation in stage 2 [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view images, initial 3D Gaussian point cloud.
**Outputs:** Optimized geometric parameters $T$, appearance parameters $A$, and material parameters $M$.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T = \{p, s, q, n\}$: Geometric properties. $p$ is position, $s$ is scale, $q$ is rotation (quaternion), $n$ is normal vector [S1].
*   $A$: Appearance parameters (e.g., spherical harmonic coefficients) [S1].
*   $M = \{a, \rho, m\}$: Material properties. $a$ is albedo, $\rho$ is roughness, $m$ is metalness [S1].
*   $V_{occl}$: Occlusion volume for caching [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1].
*   Stage 1 iterations: 30,000 [S1].
*   Stage 2 iterations: 10,000 [S1].
*   $\lambda_{n-TV}$: Normal smoothing weight = 5.0 [S1].
*   $\lambda_M$: Material smoothing weight = 1.0 [S1].
*   $\lambda_E$: Light smoothing weight = 0.01 [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Stage 1: Initial Optimization**
    Minimize the initial loss $L_{init}$ to optimize $T$ and $A$. $M$ is not optimized or is set to a default [S1].
    $$L_{init} = L_c + L_n$$
    Where $L_c$ is the L1 color reconstruction loss [S1].
    $L_n$ is the normal loss:
    $$L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} TV_{normal}$$
    $L_{n-p}$ is the point-wise normal consistency loss [S1].
    $TV_{normal}$ is the total variation of the predicted normal map $\hat{N}$:
    $$TV_{normal} = \frac{1}{|\hat{N}|} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{(\hat{N}_{i,j} - \hat{N}_{i-1,j})^2 + (\hat{N}_{i,j} - \hat{N}_{i,j-1})^2}$$
    [S1] (Note: The source formula for $TV_{normal}$ is implied by the structure of $TV_{mat}$ and $TV_{light}$ and the variable name, but the exact pixel-wise formula is not explicitly written out in the provided text snippet, only the structure $L_{n-p} + \lambda TV$. However, $TV_{mat}$ and $TV_{light}$ are explicit. We assume standard TV definition for normals).

2.  **Stage 2: Decomposition Optimization**
    Fix $T$ and $A$. Optimize $M$ (and potentially lighting/environment maps $E$) by minimizing the decomposition loss $L_d$ [S1].
    $$L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light}$$
    $L_{shade}$ is the shading loss:
    $$L_{shade} = \| I - \hat{I}_{shade}(M, E, V_{illu}) \|_1$$
    Where $I$ is the input image, $\hat{I}_{shade}$ is the rendered shading using material $M$, environment $E$, and illumination volume $V_{illu}$ [S1].
    $TV_{mat}$ is the total variation of the predicted material map $\hat{M}$:
    $$TV_{mat} = \frac{1}{|\hat{M}|} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{(\hat{M}_{i,j} - \hat{M}_{i-1,j})^2 + (\hat{M}_{i,j} - \hat{M}_{i,j-1})^2}$$
    [S1]
    $TV_{light}$ is the total variation of the predicted light map $\hat{E}$:
    $$TV_{light} = \frac{1}{|\hat{E}|} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{(\hat{E}_{i,j} - \hat{E}_{i-1,j})^2 + (\hat{E}_{i,j} - \hat{E}_{i,j-1})^2}$$
    [S1]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Initialization
gaussians = initialize_3d_gaussians(point_cloud)
T = {p: gaussians.position, s: gaussians.scale, q: gaussians.rotation, n: gaussians.normal}
A = gaussians.sh_coefficients
M = {a: 0.5, rho: 0.5, m: 0.0} # Default material
E = initialize_lighting()
optimizer = Adam([T, A, M, E], lr=0.001)

# Stage 1: Initial Optimization
for i in range(30000):
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    # Render color and normals using T and A
    rendered_color, rendered_normal = render(T, A)
    
    # Calculate losses
    Lc = L1_loss(rendered_color, target_image)
    Ln_p = NormalConsistencyLoss(rendered_normal, target_normal)
    Tv_normal = TotalVariation(rendered_normal)
    Ln = Ln_p + 5.0 * Tv_normal
    
    L_init = Lc + Ln
    L_init.backward()
    optimizer.step()

# Stage 2: Decomposition Optimization
# Fix T and A
for i in range(10000):
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    # Render shading using T, A, M, E
    rendered_shading = render_shading(T, A, M, E)
    
    # Calculate losses
    L_shade = L1_loss(rendered_shading, target_image)
    Tv_mat = TotalVariation(M)
    Tv_light = TotalVariation(E)
    
    L_d = L_shade + 1.0 * Tv_mat + 0.01 * Tv_light
    L_d.backward()
    optimizer.step()
```

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:** The method requires two distinct training phases, effectively doubling the iteration count compared to a single-stage approach if the second stage is comparable in cost. Stage 1 is 30,000 iterations, Stage 2 is 10,000 iterations [S1]. Memory usage includes storing geometric, appearance, and material parameters, plus occlusion volumes $V_{occl}$ for caching [S1].
**Measured Performance:** Tested on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU [S1].
**Strengths:** Decoupling geometry and material reduces optimization instability. Normal smoothing in stage 1 improves geometric consistency. Material/lighting smoothing in stage 2 reduces noise in decomposed maps [S1].
**Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of smoothing weights ($\lambda_{n-TV}, \lambda_M, \lambda_E$) [S1]. Fixing parameters in stage 2 may prevent global refinement.

## Background
Originated in GS-IR (3D Gaussian Splatting for Inverse Rendering) [S1]. The method was developed to address the challenges of inverse rendering with 3D Gaussians, specifically the difficulty of jointly optimizing geometry, appearance, and material properties [S1].
