# Mesh-embedded representation
Also called: Mesh-embedded Gaussian Splatting, Mesh-embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation, GaMeS, Mesh to DRK Conversion
## Overview
Mesh-embedded representations parameterize 3D Gaussians using embedding coordinates on a canonical triangle mesh, disentangling motion (handled by the mesh) from appearance (handled by the Gaussians) . It solves the problem of animating high-fidelity, detailed human avatars by allowing classical mesh operations or skeletal animations to induce coherent changes in Gaussian position, scale, and orientation, avoiding the need for MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) fields [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on implicit deformation fields, this approach explicitly controls Gaussian rotation and translation via the mesh, enabling compatibility with skeletal animation, blend shapes, and direct mesh editing .
## Shared principles
All methods in this family anchor 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ with vertices $V$, normals $n$, and optionally per-vertex quaternions $q$. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by embedding parameters $E_i$ and invariant appearance/geometry parameters.

**Embedding Parameters ($E_i$):**
*   $k$: Integer triangle index.
*   $(u, v)$: Floats representing barycentric coordinates within triangle $k$ ($u \ge 0, v \ge 0, u+v \le 1$).
*   $d$: Float representing normal displacement along the interpolated normal.

**Invariant Parameters:**
*   $\bar{q}$: Canonical rotation quaternion (pose-invariant).
*   $\bar{s}$: Canonical scale (pose-invariant).
*   $o$: Opacity (pose-invariant).
*   $c$: Color (pose-invariant).

**Canonical Position and Normal:**
The canonical position $\mu_{cano}$ and normal $n_{cano}$ are computed via Phong interpolation :
$$
\mu_{cano} = \mathcal{V}(k, u, v) = u V_1 + v V_2 + (1 - u - v) V_3
$$
$$
n_{cano} = \mathcal{N}(k, u, v) = u n_1 + v n_2 + (1 - u - v) n_3
$$
where $V_1, V_2, V_3$ are the vertices of triangle $k$, and $n_1, n_2, n_3$ are the per-vertex normals .

**Deformation:**
For a posed frame, the posed position $\mu_{pose}$ is obtained by applying mesh deformation to the canonical position and adding normal displacement:
$$
\mu_{pose} = \text{Deform}(\mu_{cano} + d \cdot n_{cano})
$$
where $\text{Deform}(\cdot)$ applies skeletal or blend-shape animation to the mesh vertices .

**Rotation:**
The posed rotation $q_{pose}$ is interpolated from per-vertex quaternions using barycentric weights and combined with the canonical rotation :
$$
q_{pose} = \delta q * \bar{q}
$$
where $\delta q = u q_1 + v q_2 + (1 - u - v) q_3$ is the interpolated rotation from the mesh vertices, and $*$ denotes quaternion multiplication . The per-vertex quaternions $q_V$ are calculated by area-weighted average from surrounding neighbor triangles [S1, unofficial].

**Scale:**
The posed scale $s_{pose}$ is derived from the area change of the embedded triangle relative to the canonical state :
$$
s_{pose} = \left( \frac{A_{pose}}{A_{cano}} \right) \bar{s}
$$
where $A_{pose}$ and $A_{cano}$ are the areas of the triangle in the posed and canonical states, respectively .

**Cross-Triangle Handling:**
Barycentric coordinates are only differentiable within a single triangle. If an optimization step moves the embedding point across a triangle boundary, the embedding must be re-expressed in the neighboring triangle to maintain differentiability . Failure to handle this causes Gaussians to stick to boundaries and pile up .

## Subtopics

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md)
Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting is a hybrid 3D representation that parameterizes 3D Gaussians using embedding coordinates on a canonical triangle mesh, disentangling motion (handled by the mesh) from appearance (handled by the Gaussians). It solves the problem of animating high-fidelity, detailed human avatars by allowing classical mesh operations or skeletal animations to induce coherent changes in Gaussian position, scale, and orientation, avoiding the need for MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) fields. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on implicit deformation fields, this approach explicitly controls Gaussian rotation and translation via the mesh, enabling compatibility with skeletal animation, blend shapes, and direct mesh editing.

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation](9559-mesh-embedded-gaussian-initialization-and-deformation.md)
This method anchors 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh using barycentric coordinates and normal displacement, allowing the Gaussians to track mesh deformation through topology rather than direct vertex positions. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly modeling the continuous embedding space $E_i = \{k, u, v, d\}$ to compute posed positions, rotations, and scales via interpolation and area-change ratios, ensuring differentiability across the mesh surface.

### [GaMeS (Gaussian Mesh Splatting)](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md)
GaMeS is a hybrid representation that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with mesh topology, allowing Gaussian components to be parameterized by the vertices of mesh faces. It solves the problem of conditioning and editing large numbers of independent Gaussians by tying their mean, rotation, and scale directly to the geometry of a canonical triangle mesh. Unlike sibling methods such as [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) which may rely on expensive pre-processing or SDF regularization, GaMeS directly constructs Gaussians on mesh faces or a "Triangle Soup" derived from flat splats, enabling automatic adaptation of Gaussian parameters when the underlying mesh is deformed.

### [Mesh to DRK Conversion](13700-mesh-to-drk-conversion.md)
Mesh to DRK Conversion is a training-free procedure that converts traditional triangle and polygon mesh faces into Deformable Radial Kernel (DRK) primitives for seamless integration into radiance field scenes. Unlike existing splatting methods (2D-GS, 3D-GS, 3D-HGS, GES) which fail to integrate traditional assets seamlessly, this method provides a direct, training-free conversion pipeline. It bridges the gap between millions of traditional 3D assets and fast, high-quality reconstruction representations, enabling unified rendering, VR digital world creation, and AR scene enhancement.

### SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting
SuGaR aligns Gaussians to a surface and extracts it via Poisson reconstruction; Frosting binds Gaussians to a mesh surface for hybrid rendering [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Origin: SuGaR and Gaussian Frosting are described in "Neural Rendering — Technologies & Tricks" [S1, unofficial].
History and development: The family builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [G] to handle dynamic scenes and mesh-based animation.
Where it is used: Animating high-fidelity human avatars, VR digital world creation, AR scene enhancement.
