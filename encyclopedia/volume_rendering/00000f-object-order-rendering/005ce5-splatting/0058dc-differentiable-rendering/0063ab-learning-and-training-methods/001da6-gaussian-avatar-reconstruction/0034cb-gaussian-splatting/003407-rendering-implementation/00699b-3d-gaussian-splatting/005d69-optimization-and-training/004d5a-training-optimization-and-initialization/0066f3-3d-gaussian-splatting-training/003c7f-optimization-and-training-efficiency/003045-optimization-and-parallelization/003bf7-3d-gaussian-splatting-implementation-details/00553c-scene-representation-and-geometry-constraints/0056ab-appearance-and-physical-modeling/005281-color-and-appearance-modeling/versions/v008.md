# Color and Appearance Modeling

Also called: Appearance Modeling, View-Dependent Color Modeling, Appearance Conditioning

## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting addresses the limitations of standard view-dependent radiance representation (typically Spherical Harmonics) when handling complex lighting, transient objects, or unstructured data. It solves the problem where static Gaussian attributes cannot capture appearance variations caused by changing illumination, camera ISP processing, or dynamic occluders. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by offering diverse strategies: post-processing image transformations ([DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)), neural field substitution ([Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)), factorized illumination ([3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)), and image-conditioned modulation ([SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)), rather than relying solely on optimizing SH coefficients ([Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)).

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering equation, where the pixel color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is computed via alpha-blending of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
where $T_i$ is the transmittance, $g_i$ is the 2D projected Gaussian density, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, and $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the view-dependent outgoing radiance. The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md).
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md).
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md).

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
A plug-and-post appearance modeling method that mitigates floaters and color distortions by transforming rendered images at the image level using 3D consistent features. It decouples global view-dependent appearance from local spatial changes, ensuring 3D consistency while maintaining real-time rendering speeds by discarding the appearance module after optimization. Choose it when handling daylight changes or ISP processing artifacts without slowing down inference.

### [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)
Replaces per-Gaussian Spherical Harmonics coefficients with a shared grid-based neural field to represent view-dependent color. It significantly reduces parameter count and addresses disk space explosion caused by high-order SH storage. Choose it when memory efficiency and generalization to novel views are prioritized over explicit BRDF modeling.

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
Models outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes. It explicitly separates diffuse and specular components via a neural renderer. Choose it when accurate specular highlights and physically plausible lighting interactions are required.

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
A family of techniques that modify the representation, storage, or training dynamics of the view-dependent radiance coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ to improve rendering quality, training stability, or inference efficiency. It focuses on the SH coefficients themselves (via distillation, quantization, or degree reduction) or the optimization infrastructure, rather than replacing the SH representation entirely. Choose it when retaining the standard 3DGS pipeline is necessary but SH artifacts or instability are problematic.

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
Designed for unstructured, in-the-wild image collections, it models appearance variations and handles transient objects without supervision. It uses a per-image embedding vector and an MLP to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, disentangling static scene geometry from dynamic occluders and lighting conditions. Choose it for training on diverse, uncurated datasets with transient elements.

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting method uses third-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) to encode view-dependent color. While efficient, SH is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and prone to artifacts (e.g., ringing) or instability in specific training regimes, particularly under varying lighting or with transient objects [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md). Subsequent works have explored alternative representations to handle these limitations. DAVIGS addresses appearance variations from lighting and ISP processing [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md). Compact View-dependent Color reduces parameter count by substituting SH with neural fields [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md). 3iGS introduces factorized illumination for specular modeling [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md). SWAG handles in-the-wild data with transient objects [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).
