# Mesh-embedded representation

Also called: Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting, Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation, GaMeS, SuGaR, Gaussian Frosting, Mesh to DRK Conversion, Gaussian Extraction Algorithm, GauS

## Overview
Mesh-embedded representations hybridize 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with explicit triangle mesh topology to enable coherent motion transfer, interactive editing, and explicit surface modeling [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3DGS, which treats Gaussians as independent entities in free space, this family ties Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scale) to mesh geometry via barycentric coordinates or vertex parameterization, allowing deformations to propagate naturally from the mesh to the splats . This approach solves the problem of Gaussian drift and incoherent deformation during animation, while also facilitating the extraction of textured meshes from optimized radiance fields without requiring NeRF-based re-training [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves parameterizing 3D Gaussians relative to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ with vertices $V$, normals $n$, and faces $F$. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by an embedding tuple $E_i = \{k, u, v, d\}$ , where:
*   $k$ is the index of the triangle $k \in F$ in the canonical mesh.
*   $(u, v)$ are the barycentric coordinates within triangle $k$, such that $u \ge 0, v \ge 0, u+v \le 1$.
*   $d$ is the normal displacement from the mesh surface.

The canonical position $\mu_{cano}$ and normal $n_{cano}$ are computed via Phong interpolation on the triangle :
$$
\mu_{cano} = \mathcal{V}(k, u, v) = u V_1 + v V_2 + (1 - u - v) V_3
$$
$$
n_{cano} = \mathcal{N}(k, u, v) = u n_1 + v n_2 + (1 - u - v) n_3
$$
where $V_j$ and $n_j$ are the vertices and per-vertex normals of triangle $k$ [Parent].

Pose-dependent properties are derived dynamically from the mesh pose :
1.  **Position:** The posed mean $\mu_{pose}$ is obtained by applying a mesh deformation function $\text{Deform}(\cdot)$ to the displaced canonical position [Parent]:
    $$
    \mu_{pose} = \text{Deform}(\mu_{cano} + d \cdot n_{cano})
    $$
2.  **Rotation:** The posed rotation $q_{pose}$ combines a canonical rotation $\bar{q}$ with an interpolated per-vertex rotation $\delta q$ [Parent]:
    $$
    q_{pose} = \delta q * \bar{q}, \quad \delta q = u q_1 + v q_2 + (1 - u - v) q_3
    $$
    where $q_j$ are per-vertex quaternions, typically computed via area-weighted averaging from surrounding neighbor triangles [Parent].
3.  **Scale:** The posed scale $s_{pose}$ scales the canonical scale $\bar{s}$ by the ratio of the triangle's area in the posed state $A_{pose}$ to its canonical area $A_{cano}$ [Parent]:
    $$
    s_{pose} = \left( \frac{A_{pose}}{A_{cano}} \right) \bar{s}
    $$

**Cross-Triangle Handling:** Barycentric coordinates are differentiable only within a single triangle. If optimization moves an embedding point across a boundary ($u < 0$, $v < 0$, or $u+v > 1$), the embedding must be re-expressed in the neighboring triangle to maintain differentiability [Parent, S2, unofficial]. Failure to handle this causes Gaussians to stick to boundaries and pile up [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md)
A hybrid representation that defines 3D Gaussians over an explicit triangle mesh to enable large-scale, interactive deformations driven by mesh editing. It solves the problem of coherent motion transfer in 3DGS by tying Gaussian parameters to mesh geometry via joint refinement, where GS rendering guides adaptive splitting and mesh constraints regularize Gaussian shapes. It sets itself apart from its siblings by utilizing a reconstructed explicit mesh for initialization and employing specific face-split and normal-guidance strategies to handle large deformations without relying on implicit MLP-based skinning fields. Unlike methods that use MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) for motion, this approach controls Gaussian rotation and translation directly by the mesh, enabling compatibility with skeletal animation, blend shapes, and mesh editing.

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation](9559-mesh-embedded-gaussian-initialization-and-deformation.md)
This method anchors 3D Gaussians to a triangle mesh using barycentric coordinates and normal displacement, enabling deformation tracking through mesh topology rather than direct vertex positions. It solves the problem of Gaussians drifting off-mesh or piling up at triangle edges during optimization and animation by defining a local triangle space. Unlike methods relying on precise mesh accuracy, it ensures robust results even with inaccurate meshes by binding Gaussians to the surface.

### [GaMeS (Gaussian Mesh Splatting)](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md)
GaMeS is a hybrid representation that integrates Gaussian Splatting (GS) with mesh topology by parameterizing each Gaussian component using the vertices of a mesh face. It solves the problem of conditioning and editing Gaussian splats by ensuring that means, rotations, and scales automatically adapt when the underlying mesh geometry is modified. Unlike sibling methods such as SuGaR, GaMeS avoids expensive pre-processing stages or SDF-based regularization, directly embedding Gaussians onto the mesh surface or a "Triangle Soup" structure.

### [SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting](13542-sugar-gaussian-frosting.md)
SuGaR (Surface-Aligned Gaussian Splatting) and Gaussian Frosting are methods that bridge the gap between implicit Gaussian representations and explicit mesh geometry. SuGaR aligns optimized 3D Gaussians to a surface and extracts a base mesh via Poisson reconstruction. Gaussian Frosting builds on this by binding Gaussians to the extracted mesh surface to enable hybrid rendering, combining the editing capabilities of meshes with the high-fidelity rendering quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It introduces an adaptive Gaussian layer with variable thickness to enhance detail and volumetric effects.

### [Mesh to DRK Conversion](13700-mesh-to-drk-conversion.md)
Mesh to DRK Conversion is a training-free procedure that converts traditional triangle and polygon mesh faces into Deformable Radial Kernel (DRK) primitives for seamless integration into radiance field scenes. It differs from sibling methods like [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) and [SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting](13542-sugar-gaussian-frosting.md), which typically require optimization or learning-based initialization, by providing a direct, deterministic mapping that eliminates the need for training data rendering or model optimization.

### [Gaussian Extraction Algorithm](15577-gaussian-extraction-algorithm.md)
The Gaussian Extraction Algorithm extracts discrete 3D Gaussian primitives from continuous Gaussian Splatting Functions (GauPF) at arbitrary densities. It solves the problem of converting a continuous, neural-network-parameterized probability field into a finite set of splattable Gaussians, analogous to how Marching Cubes extracts meshes from Signed Distance Functions. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its use of an octree-guided progressive sampling strategy to locate high-probability regions, followed by gradient-based optimization of proxy points, rather than fixed-grid voxelization or direct mesh embedding.

#### GauS (Gaussian Splatting Surface Reconstruction)
**Definition:** GauS is a render-then-fuse approach for high-fidelity mesh reconstruction from 3DGS inputs without fine-tuning. It uses volumetric fusion to convert 3D Gaussians into textured triangle meshes. It is a plug-and-play module compatible with various GS-based methods [S1, unofficial].
**Comparison:** Unlike NeRF-based re-training or further fine-tuning required for explicit surface modeling in other methods, GauS converts optimized Gaussians directly into textured meshes serving as a versatile plug-and-play solution [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of embedding Gaussian primitives onto explicit geometry emerged to address the limitations of free-space 3DGS in handling articulated motion and explicit surface editing. Early works like [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) and [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation](9559-mesh-embedded-gaussian-initialization-and-deformation.md) introduced barycentric parameterization to tie Gaussians to mesh topology, enabling deformation tracking [S1, unofficial]. Subsequent developments such as [GaMeS](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md) and [SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting](13542-sugar-gaussian-frosting.md) refined this by aligning Gaussians to extracted meshes or using triangle parameterization for editing [S1, unofficial]. The family also includes training-free conversions like [Mesh to DRK Conversion](13700-mesh-to-drk-conversion.md) [S1, unofficial] and extraction from neural fields like the [Gaussian Extraction Algorithm](15577-gaussian-extraction-algorithm.md) [S1, unofficial]. Surface reconstruction from Gaussians without re-training is further advanced by [GauS](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) within the GauStudio framework [S1, unofficial].
