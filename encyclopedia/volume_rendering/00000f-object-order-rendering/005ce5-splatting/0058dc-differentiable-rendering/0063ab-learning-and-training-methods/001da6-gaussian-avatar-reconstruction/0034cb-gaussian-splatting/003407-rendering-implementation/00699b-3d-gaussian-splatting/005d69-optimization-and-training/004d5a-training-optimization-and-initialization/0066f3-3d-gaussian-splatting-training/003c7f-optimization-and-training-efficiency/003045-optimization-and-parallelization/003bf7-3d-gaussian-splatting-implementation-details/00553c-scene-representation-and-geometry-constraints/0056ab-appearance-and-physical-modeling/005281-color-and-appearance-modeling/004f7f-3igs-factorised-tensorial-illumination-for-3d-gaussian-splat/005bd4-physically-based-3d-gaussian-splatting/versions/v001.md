# Physically-Based 3D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 3DGS PBR Framework, PBR Color Computation, PBR with 3D Gaussians, Physically-Based Rendering (PBR) with 3D Gaussians, Physically-based Gaussian Rendering

## Overview
Physically-Based 3D Gaussian Splatting (PBR 3DGS) is a framework that assigns physically interpretable rendering attributes to Gaussian primitives and evaluates the rendering equation to produce per-Gaussian PBR colors, replacing the view-dependent spherical harmonics used in vanilla 3DGS with a physical Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of inverse rendering and relighting by enabling the simulation of complex lighting interactions, such as indirect illumination and shadows, which vanilla 3DGS cannot natively represent due to its lack of physical material definitions [S1]. Unlike vanilla 3DGS, which uses $k$-order spherical harmonics for view-dependent effects that lack physical interpretability, this method provides physical interpretability by explicitly modeling albedo, roughness, and metalness [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian points $\{G_i\}$, each defined by position $\mu_i$, scale $S_i$, rotation $R_i$, and opacity $\alpha_i$ [S1]. The method requires a learnable environment light probe $L(\omega_i)$ [S2].
*   **Outputs:** Final rendered images via alpha compositing of shaded Gaussian points [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** Each Gaussian $i$ stores learnable PBR attributes queried from multi-resolution hash grids based on its position $\mu_i$ [S1].
    *   **Diffuse Albedo ($a_i$):** Queried from hash grid $\mathcal{E}_d$ [S1].
    *   **Roughness ($\rho_i$) and Metalness ($m_i$):** Queried from hash grid $\mathcal{E}_s$ [S1].
    *   **Normal ($n_i$):** Derived from the Gaussian's geometry or mesh surface [S1, S2].
*   **Light Probe:** A learnable environment light $L(\omega_i)$ stored in latitude-longitude format with dimensions $32 \times 64 \times 1$ (or $R^{32 \times 64 \times 1}$) [S2].
*   **Visibility ($v_i$):** Modeled as a 3-degree Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficient set to represent occlusion [S2].

**Formulas:**
The core of the method is the evaluation of the rendering equation [S1, S2]:
$$ L_o(x, \omega_o) = \int_{\mathcal{H}} f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o) L_i(x, \omega_i) |n \cdot \omega_i| d\omega_i $$
Where:
*   $L_o(x, \omega_o)$ is the outgoing radiance in direction $\omega_o$.
*   $f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o)$ is the BRDF.
*   $L_i(x, \omega_i)$ is the incoming light.
*   $n$ is the surface normal.
*   $\omega_i$ is the incoming light direction.
*   $\mathcal{H}$ is the hemisphere.

The BRDF $f_r$ is modeled using the GGX microfacet model (Disney BRDF) [S1, S2]:
$$ f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o) = (1 - m) \frac{a}{\pi} + \frac{D \cdot F \cdot G}{4 |n \cdot \omega_i| |n \cdot \omega_o|} $$
Where:
*   $m$ is metalness.
*   $a$ is albedo.
*   $D$ is the microfacet distribution function (influenced by roughness $\rho$).
*   $F$ is the Fresnel reflection term.
*   $G$ is the geometric shadowing factor (influenced by roughness $\rho$).
*   $D \cdot F \cdot G$ is collectively referred to as $DFG$ in some implementations [S1].

The incoming light $L_i$ is computed as:
$$ L_i(x, \omega_i) = v(x, \omega_i) L(\omega_i) $$
Where $v(x, \omega_i)$ is the visibility (occlusion) and $L(\omega_i)$ is the environment light [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Environment Light Resolution:** $32 \times 64$ latitude-longitude map [S2].
*   **Visibility SH Degree:** 3-degree [S2].
*   **Metalness:** Can be manually set to zero to simplify modeling in avatar reconstruction [S2].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Attribute Query:** For each Gaussian point $i$ with position $\mu_i$, query the multi-resolution hash grids to obtain PBR attributes:
    $$ a_i = \mathcal{E}_d(\mu_i) $$
    $$ (\rho_i, m_i) = \mathcal{E}_s(\mu_i) $$
    [S1]
2.  **Light Transport Evaluation:** Evaluate the incident lighting $L_i(x, \omega_i)$ by querying the learnable environment light probe $L(\omega_i)$ and applying the visibility term $v(x, \omega_i)$ [S2].
3.  **BRDF Computation:** Compute the BRDF $f_r$ using the queried attributes ($a_i, \rho_i, m_i$) and the normal $n_i$ via the GGX model [S1, S2].
4.  **Rendering Equation Solution:** Differentiably solve the rendering equation (Eq. 2) using Monte Carlo sampling to obtain the PBR color (outgoing radiance $L_o$) for each Gaussian point [S1].
5.  **Rasterization:** Process the shaded Gaussian points using standard 3DGS rasterization [S1].
6.  **Compositing:** Render the final images via alpha compositing of the shaded Gaussians [S1].
7.  **Optimization:** Apply backpropagation to optimize the PBR attributes (hash grid values, light probe, visibility) through photometric supervision [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_pbr_color(gaussian, hash_grids, light_probe, visibility_sh):
    """
    Computes the PBR color for a single Gaussian point.
    
    Args:
        gaussian: Object containing position mu, normal n, and other geometric props.
        hash_grids: Dictionary containing Ed (diffuse) and Es (specular) multi-resolution hash grids.
        light_probe: 3D array of shape (32, 64, 1) representing environment light in lat-long.
        visibility_sh: 3-degree Spherical Harmonic coefficients for visibility.
        
    Returns:
        Lo: Outgoing radiance (color) for the Gaussian.
    """
    # Step 1: Query PBR attributes from hash grids
    # Ed returns albedo (scalar or RGB depending on implementation, assumed RGB for color)
    albedo = hash_grids['Ed'](gaussian.mu)
    
    # Es returns roughness and metalness
    roughness, metalness = hash_grids['Es'](gaussian.mu)
    
    # Step 2: Sample incoming light directions and compute Li
    # Monte Carlo sampling over the hemisphere H
    # Note: In practice, this involves sampling directions omega_i and querying the light probe
    # For a simplified implementation description:
    
    Lo = 0.0
    num_samples = 1024 # Typical MC sample count
    
    for _ in range(num_samples):
        # Sample direction omega_i from hemisphere around normal n
        omega_i = sample_hemisphere(gaussian.n)
        
        # Query environment light L(omega_i)
        # Convert omega_i to lat-long coordinates to index light_probe
        lat, lon = direction_to_lat_lon(omega_i)
        Li = light_probe[lat, lon, :]
        
        # Compute visibility v(omega_i) using SH coefficients
        # v is a scalar occlusion term
        v = evaluate_sh(visibility_sh, omega_i)
        
        # Effective incoming radiance
        Li_effective = v * Li
        
        # Compute BRDF fr
        # Diffuse term
        diffuse_term = (1 - metalness) * (albedo / pi)
        
        # Specular term (GGX)
        # D, F, G are functions of roughness, metalness, and angles
        D = ggx_distribution(roughness, gaussian.n, omega_i)
        F = fresnel(metalness, albedo, gaussian.n, omega_i)
        G = geometric_shadowing(roughness, gaussian.n, omega_i)
        
        # Denominator term
        denom = 4 * abs(dot(gaussian.n, omega_i)) * abs(dot(gaussian.n, omega_o))
        
        specular_term = (D * F * G) / denom
        
        fr = diffuse_term + specular_term
        
        # Cosine term
        cos_theta = abs(dot(gaussian.n, omega_i))
        
        # Accumulate radiance
        Lo += fr * Li_effective * cos_theta
        
    # Average over samples
    Lo = Lo / num_samples
    
    return Lo
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Monte Carlo sampling introduces noise; sufficient samples are required for clean gradients during optimization [S1].
*   **Boundaries:** The hash grid queries must handle out-of-bounds positions gracefully, typically by clamping or using periodic boundary conditions inherent to hash grids [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the Gaussian normal is undefined (e.g., at initialization), the BRDF evaluation may fail; a default normal or fallback to spherical harmonics might be needed [G].

## Subtopics

### Geometry Guided Gaussian Points Generation
Generates Gaussian points on a mesh surface extracted via FlexiCubes, ensuring geometric consistency. This sets it apart from random initialization by providing a deterministic geometric bound [S1].

### Light Transport Modeling
Uses BVH-accelerated ray tracing on the extracted mesh to evaluate light transport and occlusion, distinguishing it from methods that approximate occlusion with contiguous forms [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the Monte Carlo integration for each Gaussian point during the shading step, plus the standard $O(N)$ rasterization of 3DGS. The hash grid queries are $O(1)$ on average.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the hash grids ($\mathcal{E}_d, \mathcal{E}_s$), the light probe ($32 \times 64 \times 1$), and the Gaussian attributes. The light probe is small, but the hash grids can grow with scene complexity [S1, S2].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Provides physically interpretable materials (albedo, roughness, metalness) and enables relighting and inverse rendering, which vanilla 3DGS cannot do [S1].
*   **Limitations:** More computationally expensive than vanilla 3DGS due to the BRDF evaluation and Monte Carlo sampling. Requires careful tuning of sampling rates and hash grid resolutions [S1].
*   **When to use:** When physically accurate lighting, relighting, or material estimation is required.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and physical accuracy is not needed; vanilla 3DGS is faster [S1].

## Background
The method originates from the need to extend 3D Gaussian Splatting beyond view-dependent appearance modeling to physically based inverse rendering. Vanilla 3DGS uses spherical harmonics for view-dependent effects, which lack physical interpretability [S1]. GeoSplatting introduced the use of the rendering equation with GGX BRDF for physical interpretability [S1]. The specific framework described here utilizes multi-resolution hash grids for efficient attribute querying and learnable environment lights for relighting [S1, S2].
