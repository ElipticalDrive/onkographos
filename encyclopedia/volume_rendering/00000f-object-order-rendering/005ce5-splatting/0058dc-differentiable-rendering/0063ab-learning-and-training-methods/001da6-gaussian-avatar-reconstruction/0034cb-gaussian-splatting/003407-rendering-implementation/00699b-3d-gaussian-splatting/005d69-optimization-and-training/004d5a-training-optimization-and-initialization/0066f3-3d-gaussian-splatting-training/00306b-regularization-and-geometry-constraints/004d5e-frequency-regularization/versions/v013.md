# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which fails to differentiate between over-reconstructed and well-reconstructed regions, FR explicitly disentangles frequency components to increase pixel gradients in over-reconstructed areas, thereby guiding adaptive densification toward high-frequency details [S2][S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from geometric constraints by operating in the Fourier domain rather than the spatial or 3D geometric domain [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, where $H$ and $W$ are height and width.
*   **Outputs:** Regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{FR}$, which is added to the total loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   Complex frequency values $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ for each frequency coordinate $(u, v)$.
*   Amplitude $|F(u, v)|$ and Phase $\angle F(u, v)$ derived from the complex values.

**Steps:**
1.  **Fourier Transform:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for both the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I$ to obtain their frequency representations $\hat{F}$ and $F$.
    $$
    F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    $$
    \hat{F}(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \hat{I}(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    where $(x, y)$ are spatial coordinates, $(u, v)$ are frequency coordinates, and $i$ is the imaginary unit [S2].

2.  **Compute Amplitude and Phase:** For each frequency component, calculate the amplitude and phase for both the ground truth and rendered images.
    *   Amplitude:
        $$
        |F(u, v)| = \sqrt{\text{Re}(u, v)^2 + \text{Im}(u, v)^2}
        $$
        $$
        |\hat{F}(u, v)| = \sqrt{\hat{\text{Re}}(u, v)^2 + \hat{\text{Im}}(u, v)^2}
        $$
    *   Phase:
        $$
        \angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(u, v)}{\text{Re}(u, v)}\right)
        $$
        $$
        \angle \hat{F}(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\hat{\text{Im}}(u, v)}{\hat{\text{Re}}(u, v)}\right)
        $$
    where $\text{Re}$ and $\text{Im}$ denote the real and imaginary components, respectively [S2].

3.  **Calculate Discrepancies:** Compute the total amplitude discrepancy $d_a$ and total phase discrepancy $d_p$ using the Euclidean metric over all frequency components.
    $$
    d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \left( |F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)| \right)^2}
    $$
    $$
    d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \left( \angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v) \right)^2}
    $$
    Note: The summation indices in the source formulas [S2] use $x, y$ but refer to frequency coordinates $u, v$. In implementation, the sum iterates over all valid frequency bins $(u, v)$ in the spectrum [S2].

4.  **Compute Loss:** The frequency regularization loss is typically a weighted sum of these discrepancies. While the exact weighting formula for $\mathcal{L}_{FR}$ is not explicitly defined as a single equation in the provided notes, it is derived from $d_a$ and $d_p$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer [S2].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
*   **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS are retained [S2].
*   **Typical Values:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-reconstruction vs. Well-reconstruction:** Naive L1 loss results in small gradients in over-reconstruction regions, preventing densification. FR increases these gradients, revealing over-reconstruction regions [S2].
*   **Frequency Annealing:** Naively adopting amplitude and phase discrepancies without distinguishing between low and high frequencies can restrict Gaussian densification and bias the model toward undesirable artifacts. Progressive frequency regularization (annealing) is used to mitigate this [S2].
*   **Precision:** The use of `arctan` for phase calculation requires handling cases where the real part is zero to avoid division by zero or discontinuities [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import torch
import torch.fft

def frequency_regularization_loss(rendered_image: torch.Tensor, ground_truth_image: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """
    Computes the frequency regularization loss based on amplitude and phase discrepancies.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (C, H, W). Assuming (H, W, C) for DFT.
        ground_truth_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C).
        
    Returns:
        Scalar loss tensor.
    """
    # Ensure inputs are float32 for FFT stability
    rendered_image = rendered_image.float()
    ground_truth_image = ground_truth_image.float()
    
    # Compute 2D FFT. torch.fft.fft2 expects (H, W) or (..., H, W).
    # If C > 1, we typically compute FFT per channel or on luminance. 
    # The source implies standard 2D DFT on the image representation.
    # We assume the input is processed per channel or converted to a scalar representation if necessary.
    # For multi-channel, we sum the losses or average them.
    
    # Compute FFT for Ground Truth
    # torch.fft.fft2 returns complex tensor. 
    # Note: torch.fft.fft2 output is not shifted. The formula uses standard DFT definition.
    F_gt = torch.fft.fft2(ground_truth_image, dim=(-2, -1))
    
    # Compute FFT for Rendered Image
    F_render = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image, dim=(-2, -1))
    
    # Compute Amplitude
    amp_gt = torch.abs(F_gt)
    amp_render = torch.abs(F_render)
    
    # Compute Phase
    # torch.angle returns phase in radians [-pi, pi]
    phase_gt = torch.angle(F_gt)
    phase_render = torch.angle(F_render)
    
    # Calculate Amplitude Discrepancy (da)
    # Formula: sqrt( (1/HW) * sum( (|F| - |F_hat|)^2 ) )
    # Note: The source formula [S2] has a summation over x,y but refers to frequency u,v.
    # We sum over the spatial dimensions of the frequency spectrum (H, W).
    h, w = F_gt.shape[-2], F_gt.shape[-1]
    
    amp_diff_sq = (amp_gt - amp_render) ** 2
    da = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(amp_diff_sq))
    
    # Calculate Phase Discrepancy (dp)
    # Formula: sqrt( (1/HW) * sum( (angle(F) - angle(F_hat))^2 ) )
    phase_diff_sq = (phase_gt - phase_render) ** 2
    dp = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(phase_diff_sq))
    
    # Combine discrepancies. The exact weighting lambda is not specified in the core definition.
    # We assume equal weighting or a hyperparameter lambda_fr.
    # [G] Defaulting to equal weighting as no specific lambda is provided in the source text for the base FR loss.
    loss = da + dp
    
    return loss
```

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

### DWTGS
DWTGS rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The addition of 2D FFT operations increases the computational cost per iteration compared to standard L1 loss. The complexity is dominated by the FFT, which is $O(HW \log(HW))$ per channel [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the frequency representations $F$ and $\hat{F}$, which are complex tensors of size $H \times W \times C$. This adds memory overhead proportional to the image resolution [S2].
*   **Measured Performance:** In the "Bicycle" scene, FR significantly increased pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to the baseline method without FR [S2]. It alleviates over-reconstruction and improves Gaussian densification [S2].
*   **Scaling:** The method is implemented using PyTorch and custom CUDA kernels for rasterization, allowing for efficient computation on GPUs [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively identifies over-reconstructed regions that L1 loss misses, leading to better adaptive densification [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Naive application without frequency annealing can restrict densification and introduce artifacts [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene exhibits over-reconstruction or when standard densification fails to capture high-frequency details [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of FFT is prohibitive, or when the scene is purely low-frequency and does not benefit from high-frequency regularization [S2].

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced as part of the FreGS method to improve 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization [S2]. It addresses the problem of over-reconstruction and improper Gaussian distribution by leveraging the frequency domain [S1, S2]. The technique builds on the observation that L1 loss is insufficient for guiding densification in over-reconstructed areas [S2]. It is used in conjunction with Adam optimizer and PyTorch framework [S2].
