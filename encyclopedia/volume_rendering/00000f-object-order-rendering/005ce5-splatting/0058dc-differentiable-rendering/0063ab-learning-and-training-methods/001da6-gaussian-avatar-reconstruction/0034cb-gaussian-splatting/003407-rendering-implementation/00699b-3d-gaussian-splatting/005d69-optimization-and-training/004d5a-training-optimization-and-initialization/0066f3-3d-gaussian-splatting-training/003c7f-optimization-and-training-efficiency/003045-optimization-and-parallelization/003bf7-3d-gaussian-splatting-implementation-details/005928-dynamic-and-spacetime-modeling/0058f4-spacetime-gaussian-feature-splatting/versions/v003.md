# Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting

Also called: STG, Spacetime Gaussians

## Overview
Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting (STG) is a dynamic scene representation that extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to the 4D spacetime domain, enabling the modeling of static, dynamic, and transient content in real-time [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by replacing spherical harmonics with a compact neural feature vector and a shallow MLP, and by employing a polynomial motion model rather than linear deformation or grid-based factorization [S1]. This approach achieves high-fidelity novel view synthesis with compact storage and real-time rendering speeds, addressing the limitations of grid-like representations that struggle with delicate dynamic details [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Multi-view video sequences (images at various timestamps and viewpoints).
*   **Output:** A set of Spacetime Gaussians (STGs) and a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) $\Phi$, allowing rendering of novel views at arbitrary timestamps $t$.

**Data Structures:**
Each STG $i$ stores the following parameters:
1.  **Spatial Opacity:** $\sigma_{is} \in \mathbb{R}$ (time-independent base opacity).
2.  **Temporal Opacity Parameters:** $s_{\tau i} \in \mathbb{R}$ (temporal scaling), $\mu_{\tau i} \in \mathbb{R}$ (temporal center/timestamp of peak visibility).
3.  **Motion Parameters:** Polynomial coefficients $\{b_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{n_p}$ for position, where $b_{i,k} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
4.  **Rotation Parameters:** Polynomial coefficients $\{c_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{n_q}$ for quaternion rotation, where $c_{i,k} \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
5.  **Scaling:** $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (3D scaling vector, time-independent).
6.  **Features:** A feature vector $f_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}^9$ composed of:
    *   $f_{base} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Base RGB color.
    *   $f_{dir} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: View-direction dependent features.
    *   $f_{time} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Time-dependent features.
7.  **Covariance:** $\Sigma_i$ is derived from $s_i$ and a quaternion $q_i$ (derived from $\{c_{i,k}\}$), but the raw quaternion coefficients are optimized, not the matrix directly.

**Parameters:**
*   Polynomial degree for motion: $n_p = 3$ [S1].
*   Polynomial degree for rotation: $n_q = 1$ [S1].
*   Feature dimension: 9 (vs. 48 for 3-degree Spherical Harmonics) [S1].
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1].
*   Loss: Combination of L1 loss and D-SSIM loss [S1].

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    Initialize STGs using Structure-from-Motion (SfM) sparse point clouds collected from all available timestamps in the input video [S1].

2.  **Temporal Opacity Calculation:**
    For a given time $t$, compute the temporal opacity $\sigma_i(t)$ using a 1D Gaussian function:
    $$ \sigma_i(t) = \sigma_{is} \exp \left( -s_{\tau i} |t - \mu_{\tau i}|^2 \right) $$
    where $\sigma_{is}$ is the spatial opacity, $s_{\tau i}$ controls the duration, and $\mu_{\tau i}$ is the timestamp where the Gaussian is most visible [S1].

3.  **Motion and Rotation Calculation:**
    Compute the time-dependent position $\mu_i(t)$ and rotation quaternion $q_i(t)$ using polynomial functions centered at $\mu_{\tau i}$:
    $$ \mu_i(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n_p} b_{i,k} (t - \mu_{\tau i})^k $$
    $$ q_i(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n_q} c_{i,k} (t - \mu_{\tau i})^k $$
    Note: $q_i(t)$ must be normalized to unit length after computation [S1]. The scaling $s_i$ remains constant over time [S1].

4.  **Covariance Construction:**
    Construct the 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i(t)$ from the rotation matrix $R_i(t)$ (derived from $q_i(t)$) and the diagonal scaling matrix $S_i$ (derived from $s_i$):
    $$ \Sigma_i(t) = R_i(t) S_i S_i^T R_i(t)^T $$
    where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ [S1].

5.  **Projection to 2D:**
    Project the 3D Gaussian to the image plane for a specific camera view. Let $W$ be the viewing transformation and $K$ be the projection matrix.
    Compute the 2D center $\mu^{2D}_i$:
    $$ \mu^{2D}_i = \left( K \left( \frac{W \mu_i(t)}{(W \mu_i(t))_z} \right) \right)_{1:2} $$
    Compute the 2D covariance $\Sigma^{2D}_i$ using the Jacobian $J$ of the projective transformation:
    $$ \Sigma^{2D}_i = \left( J W \Sigma_i(t) W^T J^T \right)_{1:2,1:2} $$
    [S1].

6.  **Feature Splatting:**
    Instead of computing color directly, splat the feature vector $f_i(t)$ to the image plane. The feature vector is defined as:
    $$ f_i(t) = [f_{base}, f_{dir}, (t - \mu_{\tau i}) f_{time}]^T $$
    During rasterization, accumulate the splatted features $F_{base}, F_{dir}, F_{time}$ at each pixel weighted by the 2D opacity $\alpha^{2D}_i$ and depth order, similar to standard alpha blending:
    $$ I_{feat} = \sum_{i \in N} f_i(t) \alpha^{2D}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha^{2D}_j) $$
    where $N$ is the set of Gaussians sorted by depth [S1].

7.  **Color Reconstruction:**
    Pass the accumulated features through a shallow MLP $\Phi$ along with the view direction $r$ to produce the final color $C$:
    $$ C = f_{base} + \Phi(F_{dir}, F_{time}, r) $$
    [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Optimize all STG parameters ($\sigma_{is}, s_{\tau i}, \mu_{\tau i}, \{b_{i,k}\}, \{c_{i,k}\}, s_i, f_{base}, f_{dir}, f_{time}$) and the MLP $\Phi$ using the Adam optimizer. The loss function is:
    $$ L = L_{L1} + \lambda L_{D-SSIM} $$
    where $L_{L1}$ is the L1 loss between rendered and ground truth images, and $L_{D-SSIM}$ is the differentiable SSIM loss [S1].

9.  **Density Control and Guided Sampling:**
    *   **Pruning:** Perform more aggressive pruning than standard 3DGS to reduce Gaussian count and model size [S1].
    *   **Guided Sampling:** After training loss stabilizes, identify pixels with large errors. Aggregate errors patch-wise. Use a coarse depth map (generated during splatting) to determine the depth range. Sample new Gaussians uniformly along rays from the center of high-error patches within this depth range, adding small noise to centers. This is performed no more than 3 times [S1].

import numpy as np

def compute_temporal_opacity(sigma_is, s_tau, mu_tau, t):
    """
    Computes temporal opacity sigma_i(t).
    """
    return sigma_is * np.exp(-s_tau * (t - mu_tau)**2)

def compute_motion(mu_tau, b_coeffs, t, n_p=3):
    """
    Computes position mu_i(t) using polynomial coefficients.
    b_coeffs: list of numpy arrays [b_0, b_1, ..., b_n_p], each shape (3,)
    """
    mu_t = np.zeros(3)
    for k in range(n_p + 1):
        mu_t += b_coeffs[k] * (t - mu_tau)**k
    return mu_t

def compute_rotation(mu_tau, c_coeffs, t, n_q=1):
    """
    Computes quaternion q_i(t) using polynomial coefficients.
    c_coeffs: list of numpy arrays [c_0, c_1, ..., c_n_q], each shape (4,)
    Returns normalized quaternion.
    """
    q_t = np.zeros(4)
    for k in range(n_q + 1):
        q_t += c_coeffs[k] * (t - mu_tau)**k
    # Normalize quaternion
    q_t = q_t / np.linalg.norm(q_t)
    return q_t

def quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q):
    """
    Converts a unit quaternion [w, x, y, z] to a 3x3 rotation matrix.
    """
    w, x, y, z = q[0], q[1], q[2], q[3]
    R = np.array([
        [1 - 2*y*y - 2*z*z, 2*x*y - 2*w*z, 2*x*z + 2*w*y],
        [2*x*y + 2*w*z, 1 - 2*x*x - 2*z*z, 2*y*z - 2*w*x],
        [2*x*z - 2*w*y, 2*y*z + 2*w*x, 1 - 2*x*x - 2*y*y]
    ])
    return R

def render_stg(stgs, camera_params, t, mlp):

        mu_t = compute_motion(g.mu_tau, g.b_coeffs, t)
        q_t = compute_rotation(g.mu_tau, g.c_coeffs, t)
        
        # Covariance

        S = np.diag(g.s_i)
        Sigma_t = R_t @ S @ S.T @ R_t.T
        
        # Projection

        # Note: Standard 3DGS projection approximation is used here conceptually.

    # 2. Sort by depth
    projected_gaussians.sort(key=lambda x: x['depth'])

def guided_sampling(stgs, error_map, coarse_depth_map, num_samples=3):
    """
    Samples new Gaussians based on training error and depth.
    """
    for _ in range(num_samples):
        # Aggregate errors patch-wise
        patches = aggregate_errors_patchwise(error_map)

        for patch in high_error_patches:
            # Get ray from center of patch

## Operating characteristics
*   **Rendering Speed:** Achieves 140 FPS at 1352 × 1014 resolution on the Neural 3D Video Dataset [S1]. The lite-version (without MLP) renders 8K video at 66 FPS on an Nvidia RTX 4090 GPU [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 40-60 minutes for a 50-frame sequence on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory/Size:** Uses 9 parameters per STG for features, significantly fewer than the 48 parameters required for 3-degree Spherical Harmonics [S1]. Aggressive pruning keeps the model size compact [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time high-resolution rendering, compact storage, ability to model transient content (emergence/vanishing), and better handling of delicate dynamic details compared to grid-based methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires multi-view video input. The polynomial motion model may be less efficient for extremely long, complex motions compared to deformation fields, though it offers higher quality than linear models [S1].

## Background
Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting was introduced by Zhan Li, Zhang Chen, Zhong Li, and Yi Xu from OPPO US Research Center and Portland State University in the paper "Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting for Real-Time Dynamic View Synthesis" (arXiv:2312.16812v2, published April 4, 2024) [S1]. It builds upon the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [41] and addresses the challenges of dynamic scene representation by incorporating temporal components and neural features.
