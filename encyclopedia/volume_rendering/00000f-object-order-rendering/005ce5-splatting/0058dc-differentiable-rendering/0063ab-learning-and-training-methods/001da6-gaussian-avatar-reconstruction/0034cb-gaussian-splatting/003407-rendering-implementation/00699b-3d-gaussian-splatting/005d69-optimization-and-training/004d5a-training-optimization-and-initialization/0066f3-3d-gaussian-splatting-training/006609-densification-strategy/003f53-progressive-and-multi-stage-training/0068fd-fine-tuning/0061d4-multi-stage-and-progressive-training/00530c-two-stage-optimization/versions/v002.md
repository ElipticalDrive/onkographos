# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: GS-IR Training Process

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training protocol for inverse rendering with 3D Gaussians that decouples geometric and appearance optimization from material and lighting decomposition [S1]. It solves the problem of jointly recovering geometry, material properties (albedo, roughness), and lighting from static multi-view images by preventing the entanglement of these distinct physical properties during early training [S1]. It sets itself apart from vanilla 3DGS by introducing normal gradient-based densification and PBR-specific loss terms, while differing from neural relightable methods by maintaining a discrete point-based representation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view images, initial 3D point cloud.
**Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussians with geometry $T$, appearance $A$, and material $M$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Geometry $T$:** Position $p$, scale $s$, rotation $q$, normal $n$.
*   **Appearance $A$:** Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients for view-dependent color.
*   **Material $M$:** Albedo $a$, roughness $\rho$, metalness $m$.
*   **Lighting:** Environment map or light sources (optimized in Stage 2).

**Steps:**

1.  **Stage 1: Initial Optimization (Geometry & Appearance)**
    *   Optimize $T$ and $A$ for 30,000 iterations using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   Minimize the initial loss $L_{init}$:
        $$ L_{init} = L_c + L_n $$
    *   $L_c$ is the L1 color reconstruction loss between rendered image $I$ and ground truth $I_{gt}$ [S1].
    *   $L_n$ is the normal loss, defined as:
        $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} TV_{normal} $$
        where $L_{n-p}$ is the point-wise normal consistency loss and $TV_{normal}$ is the total variation of the normal field [S1].
    *   The smoothing weight $\lambda_{n-TV}$ is set to 5.0 [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Decomposition Optimization (Material & Lighting)**
    *   Fix $T$ and $A$. Optimize $M$ (and lighting parameters) for 10,000 iterations using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   Minimize the decomposition loss $L_d$:
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
    *   $L_{shade}$ is the shading loss, measuring the difference between the rendered image $I$ and the physically based shading model output $\hat{I}_{shade}(M, E, V_{illu})$ [S1]:
        $$ L_{shade} = \| I - \hat{I}_{shade}(M, E, V_{illu}) \|_1 $$
    *   $TV_{mat}$ is the total variation of the predicted material map $\hat{M}$:
        $$ TV_{mat} = \frac{1}{|\hat{M}|} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{(\hat{M}_{i,j} - \hat{M}_{i-1,j})^2 + (\hat{M}_{i,j} - \hat{M}_{i,j-1})^2} $$
    *   $TV_{light}$ is the total variation of the estimated lighting $\hat{E}$:
        $$ TV_{light} = \frac{1}{|\hat{E}|} \sum_{i,j} \sqrt{(\hat{E}_{i,j} - \hat{E}_{i-1,j})^2 + (\hat{E}_{i,j} - \hat{E}_{i,j-1})^2} $$
    *   Smoothing weights are $\lambda_M = 1.0$ and $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1].
*   Stage 1 Iterations: 30,000 [S1].
*   Stage 2 Iterations: 10,000 [S1].
*   $\lambda_{n-TV}$: 5.0 [S1].
*   $\lambda_M$: 1.0 [S1].
*   $\lambda_E$: 0.01 [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
*   The method assumes the initial geometry is sufficiently accurate for Stage 1 to converge before material decomposition begins [S1].
*   Precision issues may arise in $TV_{mat}$ and $TV_{light}$ calculations if material or lighting values are near zero; the denominator $|\hat{M}|$ and $|\hat{E}|$ normalizes the total variation [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage training procedure for Relightable 3D Gaussians](15262-two-stage-training-procedure-for-relightable-3d-gaussians.md)
This method defines the optimization protocol for Relightable 3D Gaussians (R3DG), a technique that reconstructs relightable scenes from multi-view images by combining standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering with physically based rendering (PBR) and BRDF decomposition. It solves the problem of recovering geometry, material properties (albedo, roughness), and lighting from static multi-view inputs, enabling novel view synthesis and relighting. It sets itself apart from vanilla 3DGS by introducing normal gradient-based densification in the first stage and PBR-specific loss terms in the second stage, while differing from neural relightable methods by maintaining a discrete point-based representation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method requires two distinct optimization phases. Stage 1 runs for 30,000 iterations, and Stage 2 for 10,000 iterations, totaling 40,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing geometry, appearance, and material attributes for each Gaussian, plus caching for occlusion volumes in the baking stage [S1].
*   **Performance:** Tested on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Decouples geometry and material optimization, leading to more stable convergence for inverse rendering tasks [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of smoothing weights ($\lambda_{n-TV}, \lambda_M, \lambda_E$) to balance reconstruction accuracy and smoothness [S1].

## Background
The method originates from GS-IR (3D Gaussian Splatting for Inverse Rendering) [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of standard 3DGS in inverse rendering scenarios, where material and lighting properties need to be disentangled from geometry and appearance [S1].
