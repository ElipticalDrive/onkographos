# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed 3D Gaussian Splatting, Per-Splat Backpropagation, Masked Gradient Computation

## Overview
This family encompasses the computational strategies required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models at scale, addressing memory limits, gradient computation bottlenecks, and numerical stability. It solves the problem of efficiently optimizing billions of Gaussian primitives across multiple GPUs and accelerating the differentiable rasterization backward pass. It is distinguished from standard optimization by its focus on distributed data parallelism, warp-level parallelization of gradients, and specialized gradient formulations for masked rendering.

## Shared principles
The optimization and parallelization methods share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer defined in [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md). The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants (e.g., MaskGaussian, Grendel-GS) modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing the gradient of the loss with respect to probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to 3D Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly accounting for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground Gaussian color accumulation and the background transmittance path, solving the backpropagation challenge in masked alpha-blending. Choose this when training with explicit occlusion masks or probabilistic visibility.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself by adapting the representation density to scene complexity via axis-slice rendering and gradient-based refinement criteria. Choose this as the standard training backbone for 3DGS.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It addresses the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in per-pixel accumulation by iterating over Gaussians and updating the gradients of the pixels they cover. Choose this to reduce training time by 4–5× while maintaining numerical equivalence.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs to overcome single-device memory limits. Unlike [TideGS](7350-tidegs.md), which streams data from disk, Grendel-GS distributes the parameter table across a GPU cluster. Choose this for scenes with billions of primitives that exceed single-GPU VRAM.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details the specific hyperparameters, data structures, and optimization schedules required to implement the 3DGS training loop. It sets itself apart by providing concrete values for densification thresholds, loss weighting, and hardware-specific kernel configurations critical for reproducibility. Choose this for engineering the stable, high-performance software implementation.

## Background
The optimization and parallelization techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged following the initial publication of 3DGS in 2023 . As the method demonstrated high fidelity, scalability challenges arose regarding memory consumption for large scenes and training speed. Early implementations relied on single-GPU training with adaptive density control [9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md]. Subsequent research introduced distributed training frameworks like Grendel-GS to handle billion-primitive scenes [7349-grendel-gs.md] and optimized backward passes to mitigate atomic contention bottlenecks [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md]. Specialized gradient computations, such as those for MaskGaussian, were developed to support advanced rendering features like explicit masking [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md]. These methods collectively enable the training of 3DGS on large-scale datasets and complex scenes.
