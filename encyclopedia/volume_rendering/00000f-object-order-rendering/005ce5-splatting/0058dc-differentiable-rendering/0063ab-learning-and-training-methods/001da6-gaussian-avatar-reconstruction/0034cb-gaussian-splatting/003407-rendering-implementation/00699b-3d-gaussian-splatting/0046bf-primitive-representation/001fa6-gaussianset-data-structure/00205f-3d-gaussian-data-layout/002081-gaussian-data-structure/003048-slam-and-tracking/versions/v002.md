# SLAM and Tracking


## Overview
SLAM and Tracking in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the simultaneous estimation of camera poses and the optimization of the Gaussian scene representation from a stream of images or video [G]. It solves the problem of building a consistent, real-time 3D model while tracking the camera's trajectory in dynamic or static environments [G]. What sets this family apart from standard offline 3DGS reconstruction is the online, incremental nature of the optimization, requiring efficient keyframe management, robust tracking against dynamic objects, and real-time rendering feedback for pose correction [G].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves coupling a visual odometry or SLAM backend with the differentiable rasterization of 3D Gaussians [G]. Unlike traditional SLAM systems that rely on sparse feature points or dense depth maps for pose estimation, Gaussian SLAM uses the rendered image or depth from the Gaussian representation as the primary observation model [G].

1. **Differentiable Rendering as Observation Model**: The system renders a predicted image $I_{pred}$ from the current camera pose $P$ and Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$. The error between $I_{pred}$ and the observed image $I_{obs}$ drives the optimization [G].
2. **Joint Optimization**: Camera poses and Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) are optimized jointly or alternately [G]. The gradient of the loss with respect to the camera pose is derived via analytical Jacobians for the camera pose optimisation in a similar manner to Eq. 3, 4 [S2, unofficial].
3. **Incremental Mapping**: New Gaussians are inserted to cover unobserved or poorly reconstructed regions, and existing Gaussians are pruned or densified based on visibility and error metrics [G].
4. **Keyframe Management**: To avoid the computational cost of optimizing all historical data, a sliding window of keyframes is maintained [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### WildGS-SLAM
A robust monocular RGB 3DGS-SLAM for dynamic in-the-wild scenes [S1, unofficial]. It uses a shallow MLP over DINOv2 features to predict an uncertainty map that guides dynamic-object removal during tracking and mapping, feeding uncertainty-aware dense bundle adjustment and Gaussian-map optimization [S1, unofficial]. Input: DINOv2 features [S1, unofficial]. Marked as 2026 SOTA update [S1, unofficial].

### Keyframe Management
Maintains a small window $W_k$ of keyframes based on inter-frame covisibility to avoid joint optimization overhead [S2, unofficial]. Gaussian covisibility estimation uses visibility ordering: a Gaussian is visible if used in rasterization and accumulated $\alpha$ has not reached 0.5 [S2, unofficial]. Measure covisibility by calculating the intersection over union (IoU) of observed Gaussians between current frame $i$ and last keyframe $j$ [S2, unofficial]. Register frame $i$ as a keyframe if covisibility drops below a threshold OR if relative translation $t_{ij}$ is large with respect to median depth [S2, unofficial]. Remove a keyframe from window $W_k$ if its overlap coefficient with the latest keyframe drops below a threshold [S2, unofficial].

### GPS-SLAM
An RGB-D SLAM method that represents a scene with a truncated SDF volume and a thin layer of 3D Gaussians near the surface for high-frequency appearance residuals [S3]. At render time, raycasts the SDF for depth plus a base color [S3]. Splats only the surface-near Gaussians with SDF-guided depth culling and order-independent blending [S3]. Mixes the two images to avoid expensive NeRF-style volumetric sampling and remove the Gaussian depth sorting step [S3].

## Background
The integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting into SLAM systems emerged as a response to the limitations of NeRF-based SLAM (e.g., NICE-SLAM, NeRF-SLAM), which struggled with real-time performance and local editing capabilities [S1, unofficial]. Early Gaussian SLAM systems adapted keyframe management and optimization strategies from traditional visual SLAM (like DSO) to the differentiable Gaussian representation [S2, unofficial]. The use of DINOv2 features for dynamic object handling, as seen in WildGS-SLAM, represents a more recent advancement aimed at robustness in unstructured environments [S1, unofficial]. GPS-SLAM illustrates the trend of hybridizing implicit fields (SDF) with explicit Gaussian representations to leverage the strengths of both for geometry and appearance [S3].
