# Deformable 3D Gaussians

Also called: Deformable-GS

## Overview
Deformable 3D Gaussians is a framework for modeling monocular dynamic scenes that extends static 3D Gaussian Splatting by learning time-independent 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and using a learned deformation field to model time-varying geometry [S1]. It solves the problem of high-fidelity, real-time rendering of dynamic scenes where implicit methods suffer from slow convergence and overfitting, and entangled methods lack spatiotemporal continuity [S1]. Unlike entangled methods that condition the radiance field directly on time, this method decouples the deformation field from the radiance field, mapping point coordinates to a canonical space [S1]. It introduces an Annealing Smooth Training (AST) mechanism to mitigate temporal jitter caused by inaccurate camera poses in real-world datasets [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of images of a monocular dynamic scene, time labels $t$, camera poses calibrated by Structure-from-Motion (SfM), and a sparse point cloud generated by SfM [S1].
**Outputs:** A set of deformed 3D Gaussians rendered into 2D images via differentiable rasterization [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **3D Gaussians ($G$):** Defined in canonical space by:
    *   Center position $\mathbf{x}$
    *   Opacity $\sigma$
    *   Rotation quaternion $\mathbf{r}$
    *   Scaling vector $\mathbf{s}$
    *   View-dependent color represented via Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients [S1].
    *   The 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is derived from $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{s}$ as $\Sigma = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^T\mathbf{R}^T$, where $\mathbf{R}$ is the rotation matrix from $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ is the diagonal scaling matrix from $\mathbf{s}$ [S1].
2.  **Deformation Network ($F_\theta$):** An MLP with depth $D=8$ and hidden layer dimension $W=256$ [S1].
    *   Input: Positional encoding of canonical position $\gamma(\text{sg}(\mathbf{x}))$ and time $\gamma(t)$ [S1].
    *   Output: Offsets $(\delta\mathbf{x}, \delta\mathbf{r}, \delta\mathbf{s})$ [S1].
3.  **Positional Encoding ($\gamma$):**
    *   $\gamma(\mathbf{p}) = (\sin(2^k \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^k \pi \mathbf{p}))_{k=0}^{L-1}$ [S1].
    *   Frequency bands $L=10$ for position $\mathbf{x}$ and $L=6$ for time $t$ in synthetic scenes; $L=10$ for both in real scenes [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize 3D Gaussians $G(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$ from the SfM sparse point cloud or random generation [S1].
2.  **Warm-up Phase (Iterations $0$ to $3k$):** Optimize only the parameters of the 3D Gaussians (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH coefficients) using the differential Gaussian rasterization pipeline. The deformation field parameters are frozen [S1].
3.  **Joint Optimization (Iterations $3k$ to $40k$):**
    *   Compute the deformed Gaussian parameters:
        $$ \mathbf{x}_{\text{deformed}} = \mathbf{x} + \delta\mathbf{x} $$
        $$ \mathbf{r}_{\text{deformed}} = \mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r} $$
        $$ \mathbf{s}_{\text{deformed}} = \mathbf{s} + \delta\mathbf{s} $$
        where $(\delta\mathbf{x}, \delta\mathbf{r}, \delta\mathbf{s}) = F_\theta(\gamma(\text{sg}(\mathbf{x})), \gamma(t))$ [S1].
    *   **Annealing Smooth Training (AST):** During joint optimization, apply noise to the time input to handle pose inaccuracies. The deformation field computes:
        $$ \Delta = F_\theta(\gamma(\text{sg}(\mathbf{x})), \gamma(t + X(i))) $$
        where the noise term $X(i)$ is:
        $$ X(i) = \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta t \cdot \left(1 - \frac{i}{\tau}\right) $$
        Here, $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ is standard Gaussian noise, $\beta=0.1$, $\Delta t$ is the mean time interval between frames, $\tau=20,000$ iterations, and $i$ is the current iteration index [S1].
    *   **Rasterization:** Project the deformed Gaussians to 2D. The 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'$ is calculated as:
        $$ \Sigma' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{V} \Sigma \mathbf{V}^T \mathbf{J}^T $$
        where $\mathbf{V}$ is the view matrix, $\Sigma$ is the 3D covariance matrix of the deformed Gaussian, and $\mathbf{J}$ is the Jacobian of the affine approximation of the projective transformation [S1].
    *   **Rendering:** Render pixel color $C(\mathbf{p})$ using alpha blending:
        $$ C(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
        where $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$, $\alpha_i = \sigma_i e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)^T \Sigma'^{-1} (\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)}$, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ is the projected 2D mean, and $c_i$ is the color [S1].
    *   **Loss and Backpropagation:** Minimize L1 loss and D-SSIM loss between rendered and ground-truth images. Gradients flow through the rasterizer to update both 3D Gaussian parameters and deformation network weights [S1].
    *   **Adaptive Density Control:**
        *   **Pruning:** Remove Gaussians with opacity $\sigma$ below a threshold [S1].
        *   **Densification:** Clone small Gaussians and move them in the direction of positional gradients if the gradient magnitude exceeds $t_{\text{pos}} = 0.0002$ [S1].
        *   **Splitting:** Split large, overlapping Gaussians by dividing their scale by $\xi = 1.6$ [S1].
4.  **Optimization Settings:** Use the Adam optimizer. The learning rate for 3D Gaussians follows the standard 3D-GS implementation. The learning rate for the deformation network decays exponentially from $8 \times 10^{-4}$ to $1.6 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].

class PositionalEncoding(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, L):
        super().__init__()
        self.L = L

    def forward(self, p):
        # p shape: (..., 1)
        # Output shape: (..., 2*L)
        freq_bands = 2.0 ** torch.arange(self.L, device=p.device).float() * torch.pi
        # sin(2^k pi p), cos(2^k pi p)
        sin_vals = torch.sin(freq_bands * p)
        cos_vals = torch.cos(freq_bands * p)
        return torch.cat([sin_vals, cos_vals], dim=-1)

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Pose Inaccuracy:** Real-world datasets often have inaccurate SfM poses, leading to temporal jitter. The AST mechanism is required to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Quaternion Representation:** The source specifies $\mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r}$. Care must be taken to ensure the resulting quaternion remains normalized if interpreted strictly as a quaternion addition, or that $\delta\mathbf{r}$ represents a small rotation vector in a local frame [S1].
*   **Warm-up:** Skipping the warm-up phase may lead to instability as the deformation field and Gaussians compete for optimization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires $40,000$ iterations. The first $3,000$ iterations optimize only Gaussians; the remaining $37,000$ optimize both Gaussians and the deformation field [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of 3D Gaussians and the size of the deformation network ($D=8, W=256$) [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves real-time rendering speeds and high-fidelity reconstruction, outperforming existing implicit and entangled methods in both quality and speed [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Decouples time and radiance field, enabling efficient differentiable rendering. AST improves temporal smoothness without extra computational overhead during inference [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate SfM poses for initialization; while AST mitigates pose errors, severe inaccuracies may still impact results. Performance on scenes with dramatic action variations may be limited compared to entangled methods [S1].

## Background
Deformable 3D Gaussians was proposed to address the limitations of implicit representations (slow convergence, overfitting) and entangled methods (lack of spatiotemporal continuity) in dynamic scene modeling [S1]. It builds on the seminal 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [15] and extends it to dynamic scenes by introducing a deformation field and canonical space representation [S1]. The method was introduced in the paper "Deformable 3D Gaussians for High-Fidelity Monocular Dynamic Scene Reconstruction" [S1].
