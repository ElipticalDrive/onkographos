# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Coarse-to-Fine Training, Curriculum Learning for 3DGS

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to optimization strategies that decompose the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into distinct phases or stages, rather than optimizing the primitive set in a single continuous pass [S1, unofficial]. This approach solves the problem of converging to local minima, producing blurry details, or suffering from instability that often results from single-stage training of sparse primitives [S1, unofficial]. By alternating between coarse initialization and fine-grained densification, or by decoupling geometry and pose learning, it yields significantly higher rendering quality and stability than single-stage baselines [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, where each $g_k$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$ (parameterized by scale $s_k$ and rotation $q_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k$ .

The core mechanism involves periodic evaluation of a metric per Gaussian to determine if the current representation is insufficient or redundant in a specific region. Common metrics include:
1.  **Gradient Magnitude:** The accumulated 2D positional gradient $\tau_k = \frac{1}{N_{\text{views}}} \sum_{v} \| \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu_v^k} \|_2$ [S4]. High gradients indicate that the Gaussian's position is not adequately minimizing the loss, suggesting a need for refinement or replacement.
2.  **Opacity:** Low opacity $\alpha_k < \alpha_{\min}$ indicates a primitive contributes negligibly to the rendered image and is a candidate for pruning [S1, unofficial][S2].
3.  **Scale:** Excessively large scales $max(s_k)$ relative to the scene extent $e_{scene}$ indicate coarse approximations that may blur details or occlude nearby geometry .

Densification operations are typically triggered every $N$ iterations (e.g., `refineEvery` = 100) to allow the optimizer to stabilize . The primary operations are:
*   **Splitting:** Replacing a parent Gaussian $g_k$ with $N_{children}$ children. The children's means $\mu_{child}$ are sampled from a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ . The scale is typically reduced, e.g., $s_{child} = s_k \cdot \frac{5}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{children}}}$, to increase local density .
*   **Cloning:** Creating an exact copy of $g_k$ (same $\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, \mathbf{c}$) to increase sampling frequency in high-error regions without changing the spatial footprint .
*   **Pruning:** Removing Gaussians that fail to meet opacity or significance thresholds [S1, unofficial][S2].

Buffer management is critical: pruned or split Gaussians require buffer compaction or flag-based marking to maintain contiguous memory access for rasterization [Parent].

### Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction
Separates the joint optimization of camera poses and 3D Gaussians into distinct phases to isolate pose errors from geometry errors.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial]. It expands the Gaussian cloud in a fronto-parallel manner [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
*   **Step:** Stage 1 constructs RGB point clouds for each input view, projects them to the target view using a differentiable point cloud renderer, aggregates all point clouds to render RGB images at the target view, and optimizes the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and ground truth target images. Stage 2 freezes or continues training the initialized depth network and jointly trains the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) [S3].
*   **Parameter:** Performance improvement is approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

### Free-View Training (FVT)
A training strategy that disentangles generalizable 3DGS from a specific number of views, enabling robust view synthesis across broader view ranges regardless of input view count [S4].
*   **Step:** Train the model on long sequences to disentangle generalizable 3DGS from specific view counts [S4].

## Background
The concept of multi-stage or progressive training in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as researchers identified limitations in single-stage optimization, particularly regarding detail reconstruction and stability in complex scenes. Early works like GaussianPro introduced progressive propagation to ensure smooth expansion of the Gaussian cloud [S1, unofficial]. Subsequent methods such as MeshGS [S1, unofficial] and FineSplat [S2] formalized multi-stage approaches to handle specific constraints like mesh alignment and pose-geometry coupling. The FWD-U method demonstrated the benefits of decoupling depth network initialization [S3], while FreeSplat explored generalizable training over long sequences [S4]. These methods collectively address the instability and local minima issues inherent in optimizing sparse primitives directly from raw image data.
