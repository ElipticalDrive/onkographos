# Mesh-embedded representation
Also called: Mesh-embedded Gaussian Splatting, Mesh-GS, Gaussian-Mesh hybrid

## Overview
Mesh-embedded representation anchors 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ to disentangle motion from appearance . It solves the problem of coherent, editable deformation in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by parameterizing Gaussian positions via barycentric coordinates and normal displacement on mesh faces, allowing explicit motion control through mesh editing or skeletal animation . It sets itself apart from nearest alternatives like [GaMeS](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md) by explicitly controlling Gaussian rotation and translation directly via the mesh geometry rather than relying on implicit MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) fields, ensuring compatibility with standard animation techniques .

## Shared principles
All methods in this family define the canonical position $\mu_{cano}$ and normal $n_{cano}$ of a Gaussian via Phong interpolation  over a triangle $k$ with vertices $V_1, V_2, V_3$ and normals $n_1, n_2, n_3$, using barycentric coordinates $(u, v)$:
$$
\mu_{cano} = u V_1 + v V_2 + (1 - u - v) V_3
$$
$$
n_{cano} = u n_1 + v n_2 + (1 - u - v) n_3
$$
The posed position $\mu_{pose}$ is obtained by applying a mesh deformation function $\text{Deform}(\cdot)$ (e.g., skeletal or blend-shape animation) to the displaced canonical position :
$$
\mu_{pose} = \text{Deform}(\mu_{cano} + d \cdot n_{cano})
$$
where $d$ is the normal displacement .

The posed rotation $q_{pose}$ is computed by interpolating per-vertex quaternions $q_1, q_2, q_3$ using barycentric weights to get $\delta q$, then combining with a canonical rotation $\bar{q}$ :
$$
q_{pose} = \delta q * \bar{q}, \quad \delta q = u q_1 + v q_2 + (1 - u - v) q_3
$$
The per-vertex quaternions $q_V$ are calculated by area-weighted average from surrounding neighbor triangles .

The posed scale $s_{pose}$ is derived from the area change of the embedded triangle relative to the canonical state :
$$
s_{pose} = \left( \frac{A_{pose}}{A_{cano}} \right) \bar{s}
$$
where $A_{pose}$ and $A_{cano}$ are the areas of the triangle in the posed and canonical states, respectively .

Barycentric coordinates are only differentiable within a single triangle. If an optimization step moves the embedding point across a triangle boundary ($u < 0$, $v < 0$, or $u+v > 1$), the embedding must be re-expressed in the neighboring triangle to maintain differentiability . Failure to handle this causes Gaussians to stick to boundaries and pile up .

## Subtopics

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md)
Anchors Gaussians to a canonical mesh to disentangle motion from appearance. Sets itself apart by explicitly controlling rotation/translation via mesh geometry rather than implicit LBS fields. Choose when explicit mesh animation is required.

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation](9559-mesh-embedded-gaussian-initialization-and-deformation.md)
Defines specific initialization and deformation rules for the embedding. Sets itself apart by explicitly parameterizing position via displacement $d$ along the interpolated normal, allowing representation of off-surface geometry. Choose when tight surface fitting or hair-like geometry is needed.

### [GaMeS (Gaussian Mesh Splatting)](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md)
Integrates GS with mesh topology by parameterizing components using mesh face vertices. Sets itself apart by avoiding expensive pre-processing or SDF regularization, directly embedding onto mesh or "Triangle Soup". Choose for conditioning and editing without complex initialization.

### [SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting](13542-sugar-gaussian-frosting.md)
Bridges implicit Gaussian representations and explicit mesh geometry via Poisson reconstruction and binding. Sets itself apart by introducing an adaptive Gaussian layer with variable thickness for volumetric effects. Choose for hybrid rendering with high-fidelity detail.

### [Mesh to DRK Conversion](13700-mesh-to-drk-conversion.md)
Converts mesh faces into Deformable Radial Kernel (DRK) primitives. Sets itself apart by being a training-free, deterministic mapping that eliminates optimization. Choose for direct integration without training data.

## Background
The concept of embedding Gaussian primitives onto mesh topology emerged to address the limitations of free-space 3D Gaussian Splatting regarding deformation and editability. Standard 3DGS lacks explicit geometric structure, making coherent animation difficult. The family of mesh-embedded methods leverages the well-established mathematical framework of barycentric coordinates and Phong interpolation to bind radiance field primitives to explicit surface geometry . Early works like SuGaR demonstrated the viability of aligning Gaussians to extracted meshes , while subsequent methods like Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting and GaMeS refined this by providing direct parameterizations for deformation and initialization . The approach allows standard animation techniques (skeletal, blend-shape) to drive the radiance field, bridging the gap between static scene reconstruction and dynamic character rendering .
