# Human and Articulated Avatars

Also called: Animatable Gaussian Avatars, Dynamic Human/Animal Splatting

## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct and render animatable avatars of humans and articulated non-human models from multi-view video. It solves the problem of simulating geometrically changing bodies in continuous movements while rendering rich textures, addressing the limitation of standard 3DGS which assumes static scenes. It sets itself apart from static rendering methods by integrating deformation pipelines (such as mesh binding, tetrahedral cages, or neural motion fields) that drive canonical Gaussians into target poses, enabling real-time novel view synthesis of dynamic subjects.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share a canonical space representation where 3D Gaussians are initialized in a reference pose (e.g., T-pose or neutral expression). Deformation is achieved by mapping these canonical Gaussians to a target pose defined by parameters (e.g., skeleton joints, expression coefficients). The rendering pipeline remains differentiable, allowing optimization of Gaussian attributes and deformation parameters via photometric loss.

## Subtopics

### [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md)
GaussianHead reconstructs animatable 3D head avatars using a tri-plane representation and a motion field network. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [GART](8093-gart.md) and [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md) by utilizing implicit neural deformation rather than explicit mesh binding or cage deformations. It is chosen when high-fidelity facial details (skin, hair) and continuous movement simulation are required. See [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md).

### [GART](8093-gart.md)
GART animates non-human articulated models (e.g., animals) by extending the deformation pipeline to general articulated skeletons. It distinguishes itself from [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md) and [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md) by focusing on generalization to non-human topologies rather than specialized human modeling. It is chosen for diverse biological models with complex joint hierarchies. See [GART](8093-gart.md).

### [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md)
D3GA reconstructs animatable full-body human avatars using tetrahedral cage deformations. Unlike methods relying on linear blend skinning or neural motion fields, D3GA uses explicit tetrahedral cages to drive deformation, providing robust geometry modeling. It is chosen when explicit volumetric constraints are preferred for body dynamics. See [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md).

### 3D Gaussian Splatting for Human101
Pushes the speed of high-fidelity dynamic human creation to 100 seconds using a fixed-perspective camera [S1][S2, unofficial].

### SplattingAvatar / GoMAvatar
Embeds Gaussians onto a canonical human body mesh. The position of a Gaussian is determined by the barycenter and displacement along the normal direction [S1][S2, unofficial].

### HeadGas
Attributes 3DGS with a base of latent features weighted by the expression vector from 3DMMs, allowing real-time reconstruction of animatable heads [S2, unofficial].

### 3DPSHR
Combines hand geometry priors (MANO) with 3DGS to provide the first real-time hand reconstruction [S2, unofficial].

### MonoGaussianAvatar
Applies 3DGS to dynamic head reconstruction using canonical space modeling and deformation prediction [S2, unofficial].

### MANUS
Explores the interaction between hands and objects using 3DGS [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The application of 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic human and articulated avatars emerged as a solution to the static nature of original 3DGS. Early works like GaussianAvatar incorporated optimizable tensors with dynamic appearance networks [S1]. Human101 further optimized training speed to 100 seconds [S1][S2, unofficial]. Methods like SplattingAvatar and GoMAvatar introduced mesh-embedded Gaussians [S1][S2, unofficial]. Head-specific methods such as GaussianHead and HeadGas addressed facial complexity using tri-planes and 3DMMs [S2, unofficial]. Non-human articulation was addressed by GART, extending the paradigm to animals [S1][S2, unofficial]. Hand and hair details were later specialized in works like 3DPSHR and MANUS [S2, unofficial].
