# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which fails to differentiate between over-reconstructed and well-reconstructed regions, FR increases pixel gradients in over-reconstructed areas by explicitly disentangling frequency components (amplitude and phase) [S2]. This enables adaptive densification by pushing Gaussians toward high-frequency regions where detail is missing [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Image dimensions $H, W$.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar loss values $d_a$ (amplitude discrepancy) and $d_p$ (phase discrepancy), which are combined to update Gaussian parameters via an optimizer (typically Adam) [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Frequency Domain:** Complex-valued matrices $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ of size $H \times W$ (per channel, or combined if processed jointly).
*   **Amplitude/Phase:** Real-valued matrices $|F(u, v)|$ and $\angle F(u, v)$ of size $H \times W$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Fourier Transform:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for both the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I$. For a pixel at coordinates $(x, y)$ and frequency coordinates $(u, v)$:
    $$
    F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    $$
    \hat{F}(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \hat{I}(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    where $I(x, y)$ and $\hat{I}(x, y)$ are the pixel values [S2].

2.  **Compute Amplitude and Phase:** Extract the magnitude and angle for each frequency component for both images:
    $$
    |F(u, v)| = \sqrt{\text{Re}(F(u, v))^2 + \text{Im}(F(u, v))^2}
    $$
    $$
    \angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(F(u, v))}{\text{Re}(F(u, v))}\right)
    $$
    Similarly compute $|\hat{F}(u, v)|$ and $\angle \hat{F}(u, v)$ [S2].

3.  **Calculate Discrepancies:** Compute the root-mean-square error for amplitude and phase across all frequency components:
    $$
    d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2}
    $$
    $$
    d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2}
    $$
    where $F$ and $\hat{F}$ denote the ground truth and rendered frequency values respectively [S2].

4.  **Optimization:** Combine $d_a$ and $d_p$ (typically with weighting coefficients) and backpropagate through the rendering pipeline to update Gaussian parameters. The method is implemented using the PyTorch framework with custom CUDA kernels for rasterization [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S2].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
*   **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS [S2].
*   **Frequency Annealing (Optional Variant):** Uses a low-pass filter $H_l$ and dynamic high-pass filter $H_h$ to progressively regularize from low to high frequencies, achieving coarse-to-fine densification [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-reconstruction Bias:** Naively applying FR without distinguishing between low and high frequencies can bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts and restrict densification [S2].
*   **Gradient Vanishing in L1:** Standard L1 loss results in small gradients in over-reconstructed regions, preventing densification. FR specifically addresses this by increasing gradients in these regions [S2].
*   **Implementation:** The arctangent function must handle quadrants correctly (e.g., using `atan2`) to ensure phase continuity [G].

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

### DWTGS
DWTGS rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The addition of 2D FFT operations per training step increases computational overhead compared to standard spatial-domain losses. The complexity is dominated by the FFT, $O(HW \log(HW))$ per image [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency domain representations for both rendered and ground truth images, adding $O(HW)$ memory overhead per channel [S2].
*   **Performance:** FR significantly increases pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to L1 loss, enabling more effective Gaussian densification [S2]. It mitigates over-reconstruction and improves visual fidelity [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Explicitly disentangles frequency components, allowing targeted densification in high-frequency (detail) regions [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Naive application can lead to artifacts if frequency annealing is not used [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene suffers from over-reconstruction or insufficient detail in high-frequency areas during optimization [S2].

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced to address convergence speed, visual artifacts, and improper Gaussian distributions in 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization [S1]. It was proposed as part of the FreGS method, which aims to boost 3D Gaussian splatting from a frequency perspective [S2]. The technique leverages the fact that amplitude and phase capture different information, allowing for more accurate assessment of reconstruction disparities than spatial domain losses alone [S2].
