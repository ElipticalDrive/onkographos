# DashGaussian

Also called: Computational resource allocation for 3DGS

## Overview
DashGaussian is a scheduling scheme for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) optimization that accelerates training by reformulating the process as a progressive fitting of higher-frequency components [S1]. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count to strip redundant complexity, distinguishing itself from vanilla 3DGS and other schedulers by producing a concave-up primitive growth curve that spans the entire optimization process [S1].

## How it works
DashGaussian reduces per-iteration elapsed time for forward rendering, gradient back-propagation, and primitive optimization by dynamically adjusting two parameters: rendering resolution and the number of Gaussian primitives [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs:** Training views, initial 3DGS primitives, optimization hyperparameters (learning rate, densification schedule).
- **Outputs:** Optimized 3DGS primitives (positions, covariances, opacities, colors) with reduced total optimization time [S1].

### Data Structures
The method operates on the standard 3DGS primitive set. Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
- Position $\mathbf{p}_i$
- Covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (or rotation $\mathbf{q}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{s}_i$)
- Opacity $o_i$
- Spherical harmonics coefficients for color $c_i$

The rendering resolution is defined by a scalar factor $r(i)$ at iteration $i$ [S1].

### Formulas
The standard 3DGS rendering equation for a pixel $u$ is [S1]:
$$ C(u) = \sum_{i=1}^P T_i(u) \cdot w_i(u) \cdot c_i $$
where:
- $P$ is the total number of primitives.
- $T_i(u) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - w_j(u))$ is the transmittance.
- $w_i(u) = o_i G_{i2D}(u)$ is the weight, with $G_{i2D}(u)$ being the 2D Gaussian splatted to the image plane.

The rendering resolution at iteration $i$, denoted $H_r \times W_r$, is derived from the original resolution $H \times W$ via a downsampling factor $r(i)$ [S1]:
$$ H_r = \lfloor H / r(i) \rfloor, \quad W_r = \lfloor W / r(i) \rfloor $$
In practice, $r(i)$ is a continuous scalar modulated by floor operation to ensure integer pixel dimensions [S1].

Anti-aliasing downsampling is required to prevent 3D aliasing artifacts when reducing resolution. This involves applying a low-pass filter to the frequency domain representation of the image before downsampling [S1]. Specifically, the frequency domain $F$ is center-cropped to size $(H/r, W/r)$, scaled by $1/r^2$, and inversely transformed [S1].

### Parameters
- **Target Optimization Time:** 200 seconds (achieved on Taming-3DGS backbone) [S1].
- **Acceleration Rate:** 45.7% average across tested backbones [S1].
- **Time Reduction:** 41.3% reduction for Taming-3DGS backbone [S1].

### Steps
1.  **Frequency Formulation:** Reformulate 3DGS optimization as progressively fitting the 3DGS to higher levels of frequency components in the training views. Increasing resolution during optimization is equivalent to fitting higher frequencies [S1].
2.  **Dynamic Resolution Scheduling:** Apply a scene-adaptive frequency-based resolution scheduling scheme. The resolution starts low and increases, reducing optimization complexity in early stages while capturing high-frequency details later [S1].
3.  **Primitive Growth Synchronization:** Schedule the growth of primitives to synchronize with the rendering resolution. This is achieved by coupling primitive growth with a numerical constraint that ensures the primitive count matches the information capacity of the current resolution [S1].
4.  **Momentum-Based Budgeting:** Use a momentum-based primitive budgeting method to adaptively estimate the maximum number of primitives in the growth curve, avoiding reliance on dataset priors [S1].
5.  **Optimization Loop:** Perform standard 3DGS optimization steps (forward pass, loss computation, backward pass, parameter update) using the current resolution and primitive set. The primitive set is updated via standard densification (splitting/cloning) and pruning, but gated by the scheduler's constraints [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **3D Aliasing:** Simply reducing resolution without anti-aliasing causes 3D aliasing, sacrificing rendering fidelity at the original resolution [S1].
- **Artifact Introduction:** Improper downsampling can disturb recoverable low-frequency components and introduce artifacts [S1].
- **Resolution Modulation:** The source notes that modulating the continuous resolution factor $r(i)$ with a floor operation encourages primitives to grow relatively faster, optimizing each primitive more sufficiently [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Reduces per-iteration elapsed time for forward rendering, gradient back-propagation, and primitive optimizing by lowering resolution in early stages [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Uses fewer primitives overall compared to vanilla 3DGS, reducing memory footprint [S1].
- **Performance:** Achieves an average acceleration rate of 45.7% across tested backbones (Mip-NeRF 360, Deep Blending, Tanks and Temples) [S1]. Specifically reduces optimization time of Taming-3DGS by 41.3% [S1].
- **Quality:** Preserves or improves rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) compared to unscheduled baselines [S1].
- **Strengths:** Plug-and-play integration with existing 3DGS backbones (3DGS, Mip-Splatting, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS) [S1]. Scene-adaptive scheduling [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful implementation of anti-aliasing downsampling [S1]. Performance depends on the effectiveness of the scene-adaptive resolution scheduler [S1].

## Background
DashGaussian was proposed by Youyu Chen, Junjun Jiang, Kui Jiang, Xiao Tang, Zhihao Li, Xianming Liu, and Yinyu Nie from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab [S1]. It was published in CVPR 2025 [S1]. The method addresses the high computational cost of 3DGS optimization by treating it as a resource allocation problem, building on the observation that increasing resolution is equivalent to fitting higher frequency components [S1].
