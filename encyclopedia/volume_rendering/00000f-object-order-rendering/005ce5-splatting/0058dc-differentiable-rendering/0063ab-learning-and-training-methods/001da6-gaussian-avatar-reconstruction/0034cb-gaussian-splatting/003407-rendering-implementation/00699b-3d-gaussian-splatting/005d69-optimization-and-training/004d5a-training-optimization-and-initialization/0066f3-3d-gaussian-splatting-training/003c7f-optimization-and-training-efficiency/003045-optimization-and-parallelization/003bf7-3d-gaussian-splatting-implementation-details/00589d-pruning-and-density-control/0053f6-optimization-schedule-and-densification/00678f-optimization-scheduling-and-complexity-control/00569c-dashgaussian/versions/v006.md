# DashGaussian

Also called: Computational resource allocation for 3DGS

## Overview
DashGaussian is a scheduling scheme for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) optimization that accelerates training by reformulating the process as a frequency component fitting task [S1]. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count to strip redundant computational complexity, distinguishing itself from vanilla 3DGS and other schedulers by using a scene-adaptive, concave-up growth curve for primitives that spans the entire optimization process [S1]. Unlike methods that peak in primitive count at half-iteration or use concave-down curves, DashGaussian synchronizes primitive growth with increasing resolution to fit higher-frequency details progressively [S1].

## How it works
DashGaussian operates on the standard 3DGS primitive representation defined in [Optimization Schedule and Densification](15107-optimization-schedule-and-densification.md). The method modulates the optimization complexity, defined as the product of rendering resolution and the number of active Gaussian primitives, which dominates the time cost of forward rendering, gradient back-propagation, and primitive optimization [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training views (images), initial set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position $\mathbf{p}_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{q}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$), and total optimization iterations $T$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives with reduced final count and optimized parameters, achieved in significantly fewer iterations or less time than vanilla 3DGS.

### Data Structures
The method relies on the standard 3DGS primitive list. Additionally, it maintains:
*   **Resolution Scheduler State:** A continuous scalar $r(i)$ representing the downsampling factor at iteration $i$.
*   **Primitive Budget State:** A momentum-based accumulator used to estimate the maximum primitive count dynamically.

### Formulas
The rendering equation for a pixel $u$ remains standard [S1]:
$$ C(u) = \sum_{i=1}^P T_i(u) \cdot w_i(u) \cdot c_i $$
where $T_i(u) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - w_j(u))$ is the transmittance, $w_i(u) = o_i G_{i2D}(u)$ is the weight, $o_i$ is opacity, and $G_{i2D}(u)$ is the 2D Gaussian splatted to the image plane.

The core innovation lies in the scheduling of resolution $r(i)$ and primitive count $P(i)$.

1.  **Frequency Formulation:** Increasing rendering resolution during optimization is equivalent to gradually fitting 3DGS to higher levels of frequency components in the training views [S1]. The difference between a high-resolution image $I$ and a downsampled image $I_r$ lies in the residual high-frequency components [S1].
2.  **Dynamic Rendering Resolution:** To avoid 3D aliasing caused by simple spatial downsampling, an anti-aliasing low-pass filter is applied before downsampling [S1]. The resolution factor $r(i)$ is modulated in practice as $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ to encourage faster primitive growth relative to the continuous case, ensuring sufficient optimization of each primitive [S1].
3.  **Primitive Scheduler:** The growth of primitives is coupled with the rendering resolution via a numerical constraint to balance computing redundancy and fitting quality [S1]. This produces a concave-up growth curve for the primitive count $P(i)$ that spans the entire optimization process [S1].
4.  **Momentum-Based Budgeting:** The maximum number of primitives in the growth curve is estimated adaptively using a momentum-based method, avoiding reliance on dataset priors [S1].

    """
    Optimizes 3D Gaussian primitives using DashGaussian scheduling.
    
    Args:
        training_views: List of training images and camera poses.
        initial_primitives: List of 3D Gaussian primitives (p, s, q, alpha, c).

        optimized_primitives: Final list of 3D Gaussian primitives.

    # Momentum-based budgeting state

    r_start = 2.0

        # Linear interpolation of the continuous resolution factor r(i)
        progress = i / T

        # In practice, this involves:

        #    rate of densification to follow the concave-up curve.
        
        # Simplified logic for the scheduler's effect on primitive count:
        # The scheduler ensures that primitive growth accelerates (concave-up)
        # as resolution increases (linearly or near-linearly).

        primitives = apply_primitive_scheduler(primitives, resolution_factor, momentum_buffer, momentum_decay)

        momentum_buffer = momentum_decay * momentum_buffer + (1 - momentum_decay) * len(primitives)

### Parameters
*   **Target Optimization Time:** 200 seconds when applied to the Taming-3DGS backbone on a consumer-grade GPU [S1].
*   **Resolution Downsampling Factor $r(i)$:** A continuous scalar modulated to $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ in practice [S1].
*   **Momentum Decay:** Not explicitly given in the sources [G].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **3D Aliasing:** Simply reducing resolution in training causes 3D aliasing, which sacrifices rendering fidelity at the original resolution [S1].
*   **Anti-Aliasing Requirement:** Anti-aliasing downsampling requires applying a low-pass filter before downsampling to avoid disturbing recoverable low-frequency components and introducing artifacts [S1].
*   **Modulation Effect:** Not modulating $r(i)$ with $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ results in slower primitive growth relative to the continuous case, potentially leading to less sufficient optimization of each Gaussian primitive [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** DashGaussian reduces the per-iteration elapsed time for forward rendering, gradient back-propagation, and primitive optimizing by lowering the effective resolution and controlling primitive count in early stages [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** By using fewer primitives on average, it reduces memory usage compared to vanilla 3DGS [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   Average acceleration rate across tested backbones: 45.7% [S1].
    *   Optimization time reduction for Taming-3DGS backbone: 41.3% [S1].
    *   Achieves optimization in 200 seconds for models with millions of primitives on consumer-grade GPUs [S1].
*   **Rendering Quality:** Preserves or improves rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM) while using fewer primitives [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Scene-adaptive scheduling; no reliance on dataset priors for primitive budgeting; plug-and-play compatibility with various backbones (Mip-Splatting, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the resolution downsampling range and momentum parameters [G]. The method assumes that the frequency content of the scene can be effectively captured by the resolution progression [S1].

## Background
DashGaussian was proposed by Youyu Chen, Junjun Jiang, Kui Jiang, et al. from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab [S1]. It was published in March 2025 (arXiv:2503.18402v2) [S1]. The work addresses the high computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization by treating it as a resource allocation problem, building on the observation that increasing resolution is equivalent to fitting higher frequency components [S1]. It improves upon prior works that use concave-down growth curves or peak primitive counts at half-iterations [S1].
