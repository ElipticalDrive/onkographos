# Human and Articulated Avatars

Also called: Animatable Gaussian Avatars, Dynamic Human/Animal Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct animatable human and articulated animal avatars from multi-view video or image data. It solves the problem of dynamic appearance and geometry reconstruction by maintaining a canonical 3D Gaussian field and applying differentiable deformation to map primitives to target poses. It is distinguished from [Semantic and Structural Extensions](17384-semantic-and-structural-extensions.md) by its focus on geometric articulation (skeleton/mesh-driven) rather than semantic feature accumulation, and from [Gaussian Splatting](52-gaussian-splatting.md) by its explicit optimization of deformation parameters alongside Gaussian attributes.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on a canonical space representation where 3D Gaussians are initialized in a reference pose (e.g., T-pose or neutral expression). Deformation is achieved by mapping these canonical Gaussians to a target pose defined by parameters (e.g., skeleton joints, expression coefficients). The rendering pipeline remains differentiable, allowing optimization of Gaussian attributes and deformation parameters via photometric loss.

For a pixel $p$, the rendered color $\mathbf{C}(p)$ is computed by sorting overlapping deformed Gaussians $\mathcal{O}_p$ front-to-back and applying:

$$
\mathbf{C}(p) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}_p} \mathbf{c}_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$

where:
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color of the $i$-th deformed Gaussian.
*   $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th deformed Gaussian.
*   $\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance from all preceding Gaussians $j < i$.
*   If $\mathcal{O}_p$ is empty, $\mathbf{C}(p) = \mathbf{0}$.

The deformation function $D(\cdot)$ maps a canonical Gaussian $i$ with position $\mathbf{p}_i^{canonical}$ to a target position $\mathbf{p}_i^{target}$ based on pose parameters $\theta$:
$$
\mathbf{p}_i^{target} = D(\mathbf{p}_i^{canonical}, \theta)
$$
The rotation and scale of the Gaussian may also be transformed by the deformation function to maintain physical consistency [G].

## Subtopics

### [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md)
A method for animatable head reconstruction using a tri-plane representation and motion fields. It decouples static geometric structure from dynamic deformation to render rich textures like skin and hair. Choose this for high-fidelity head avatars with complex hair.

### [GART](8093-gart.md)
Extends human avatar reconstruction to articulated non-human models (e.g., animals). It generalizes the canonical-space deformation framework from bipedal humans to other articulated species. Choose this for non-human articulated subjects.

### [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md)
Creates animatable human avatars from multi-view video by combining drivable 3D Gaussians with tetrahedral cages. It explicitly utilizes tetrahedral cages to drive deformation, enabling full-body articulation. Choose this for full-body dynamic modeling with explicit cage-based deformation.

### 3D Gaussian Splatting for Human101
Pushes the speed of high-fidelity dynamic human creation to 100 seconds using a fixed-perspective camera [S1][S2, unofficial].

### SplattingAvatar / GoMAvatar
Embeds Gaussians onto a canonical human body mesh. The position of a Gaussian is determined by the barycentric point and the displacement along the normal direction [S1][S2, unofficial].

### HeadGas
Attributes the 3DGS with a base of latent features that are weighted by the expression vector from 3DMMs. This allows reconstruction in real-time of animatable heads [S2, unofficial].

### 3DPSHR
Combines hand geometry priors (MANO) with 3DGS to provide the first real-time hand reconstruction [S2, unofficial].

### MonoGaussianAvatar
Applies 3DGS to dynamic head reconstruction using canonical space modeling and deformation prediction [S2, unofficial].

### MANUS
Explores the interaction between hands and objects using 3DGS [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The field emerged from the need to animate 3D Gaussian Splatting representations. Early works like GaussianAvatar [S1] incorporated optimizable tensors with dynamic appearance networks. Human101 [S1][S2, unofficial] significantly accelerated training to 100 seconds. SplattingAvatar and GoMAvatar [S1][S2, unofficial] introduced mesh-embedded Gaussians. Head-focused methods like GaussianHead [S2, unofficial] and HeadGas [S2, unofficial] specialized in facial details and real-time expression control. Full-body and animal extensions like GART [S2, unofficial] and D3GA [S1] broadened the scope to general articulation.
