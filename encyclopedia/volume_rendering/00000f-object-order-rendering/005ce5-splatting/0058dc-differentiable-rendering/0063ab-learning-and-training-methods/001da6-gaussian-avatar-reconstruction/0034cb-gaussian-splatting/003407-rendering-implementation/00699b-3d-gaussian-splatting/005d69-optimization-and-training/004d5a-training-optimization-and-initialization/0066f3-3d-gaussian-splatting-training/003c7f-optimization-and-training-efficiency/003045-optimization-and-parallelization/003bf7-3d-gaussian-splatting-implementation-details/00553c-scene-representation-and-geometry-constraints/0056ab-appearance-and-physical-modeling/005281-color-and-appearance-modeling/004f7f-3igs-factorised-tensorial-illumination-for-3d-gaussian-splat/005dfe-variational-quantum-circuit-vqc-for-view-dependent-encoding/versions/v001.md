# Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) for View-Dependent Encoding

Also called: Bloch-encoded VQC, QuantumGS VQC

## Overview
This method replaces or modulates classical Spherical Harmonics (SH) with a Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) to model view-dependent radiance and opacity for 3D Gaussians, specifically targeting high-frequency specular effects and reducing artifacts like "floaters" [S1]. It sets itself apart from its parent [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md) by using quantum-inspired entanglement and Bloch sphere mapping for directional encoding, and from its sibling [Physically-Based 3D Gaussian Splatting](16738-physically-based-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by relying on learned quantum parameterizations rather than explicit physical scattering models [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   3D Gaussian primitives $G$ with mean position $\mu_G$, covariance, base SH color coefficients, and base opacity.
*   Viewing direction $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (normalized).
*   Hashed position features $hash(\mu_G)$ derived from multi-resolution hash encoding.

**Outputs:**
*   Final color $c_{final}(d)$ and opacity $\alpha_{final}(d)$ for each Gaussian.
*   Rendered image via differentiable rasterization.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Hash Encoding Configuration:** Multi-resolution hash grid with $L_{hash}=16$ levels, feature dimension $F=2$, and maximum hash table size $T=2^{19}$ [S1].
2.  **VQC State:** A 3-qubit register initialized to $|000\rangle$.
3.  **VQC Parameters:** Rotation angles $\theta = (\theta_{j,\ell}, \phi_{j,\ell})$ for qubit $j \in \{0,1,2\}$ and layer $\ell \in \{1,\dots,L\}$.
4.  **Hypernetwork (Pipeline I):** Classical MLP with two hidden layers of 64 units, residual connections, GeLU activation, and dropout [S1].
5.  **Decoding MLP:** Lightweight MLP. Input size 3, single hidden layer of 3 neurons, output size $N_{out}$ (49 for full SH+opacity modulation, 1 for opacity-only) [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Direction Encoding:** Map the viewing direction $d$ to a quantum state $|\psi_{enc}\rangle$ on the Bloch sphere. This involves encoding the spherical coordinates of $d$ into the amplitudes/phases of the 3-qubit register [S1].
2.  **VQC Processing:** Process $|\psi_{enc}\rangle$ through a VQC ansatz $U(\theta; L)$ consisting of $L=4$ layers [S1]. For each layer $\ell$:
    *   Apply parameterized local rotations to the 3-qubit register. For the $j$-th qubit ($j=0, 1, 2$), apply:
        $$ R_j(\theta_{j,\ell}, \phi_{j,\ell}) = R_z^{(j)}(\phi_{j,\ell}) R_y^{(j)}(\theta_{j,\ell}) $$
        where $R_y$ and $R_z$ are standard quantum rotation gates defined by matrices:
        $$ R_y(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \\ \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix}, \quad R_z(\phi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{-i\phi/2} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{i\phi/2} \end{pmatrix} $$
        [S1].
    *   Apply multi-qubit entanglement gates between qubits to introduce non-linearities and correlations [S1].
3.  **Parameter Generation (Pipeline I - Per-Gaussian):** For each Gaussian $G$, generate unique VQC parameters $\theta_G$ and MLP weights $W_{MLP}^G$ using a hypernetwork $H$ conditioned on the hashed position $hash(\mu_G)$:
    $$ (\theta_G, W_{MLP}^G) = H(hash(\mu_G)) $$
    [S1].
    *(Note: Pipeline II uses shared VQC parameters conditioned on global spatial/directional hashgrids via projection networks [S1].)*
4.  **Attribute Modulation:** Apply the VQC output and MLP weights to generate view-dependent multiplicative updates $\Delta c(d)$ and $\Delta \alpha(d)$. Calculate final attributes:
    $$ c_{final}(d) = c(d) \cdot \Delta c(d) $$
    $$ \alpha_{final}(d) = \alpha(d) \cdot \Delta \alpha(d) $$
    [S1].
5.  **Rendering:** Render the scene using a differentiable Gaussian rasterizer with the refined attributes [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Minimize the combined loss function:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_{1} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM} $$
    where $\lambda \in (0, 1)$ [S1].

## Subtopics

### Pipeline I (Hyper-Quantum)
Uses per-Gaussian unique VQC parameters generated by a hypernetwork. Sets itself apart by maximizing local expressivity for intricate optical effects. Choose for synthetic scenes prioritizing high PSNR [S1].

### Pipeline II (Joint-Hash Global)
Uses shared VQC parameters conditioned on spatial and directional hashgrids via projection networks. Sets itself apart by offering scene-scale scalability and memory efficiency. Choose for large-scale real-world scenes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Higher than vanilla 3DGS due to the simulation of quantum circuits (entanglement) on classical hardware [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Pipeline I requires storing unique VQC parameters per Gaussian, increasing memory usage compared to shared parameters [S1].
*   **Performance:** On NeRF Synthetic, QuantumGS achieves PSNR 33.98 and SSIM 0.970, outperforming standard 3DGS (PSNR 33.30, SSIM 0.969) and VDGS (PSNR 33.37, SSIM 0.969) [S1]. FPS is significantly lower (12.64 FPS) compared to 3DGS (733.00 FPS) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates "floater" artifacts, recovers high-frequency specular details, and maintains geometric robustness in complex scenes (e.g., Ship, Room, LEGO) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computational cost prevents real-time speeds comparable to vanilla 3DGS. Training efficiency is lower due to hypernetwork complexity [S1].
