# D3GA
Also called: D3GA, A method that creates animatable human avatars using drivable 3D Gaussians and tetrahedral cages for full-body modeling

## Overview
D3GA is a method for reconstructing animatable full-body human avatars from multi-view video inputs [S1]. It addresses the challenge of modeling dynamic human geometry and appearance by combining 3D Gaussian Splatting with tetrahedral cage deformations [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on linear blend skinning or neural motion fields, D3GA utilizes explicit tetrahedral cages to drive the deformation of canonical 3D Gaussians, providing robust geometry and appearance modeling [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view video sequences of a human subject in various poses.
*   **Outputs:** An animatable 3D Gaussian avatar representation that can be rendered in arbitrary poses.

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Gaussians:** Standard 3D Gaussian representation including center, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients in a canonical space [S1].
*   **Tetrahedral Cages:** A volumetric decomposition of the human body space into tetrahedra, used to define the deformation field [S1].

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Canonical Initialization:** Initialize 3D Gaussians in a reference canonical pose [S1].
2.  **Tetrahedral Cage Construction:** Construct tetrahedral cages that encompass the human body geometry [S1].
3.  **Deformation Mapping:** Map the canonical 3D Gaussians to the target pose using the drivable tetrahedral cages [S1]. The cages provide the geometric constraints for deformation [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Rendering:** Render the deformed Gaussians using differentiable splatting [S1].
5.  **Optimization:** Optimize the Gaussian parameters and cage deformation parameters to minimize photometric loss against input images [S1].

## Background
D3GA was introduced as one of the first methods to apply 3D Gaussian Splatting to full-body human modeling using tetrahedral cages [S1]. It builds on the high efficiency of 3DGS, which has spurred research in digital human creation [S1]. The method is cited in recent surveys on 3D Gaussian Splatting advances [S1].
