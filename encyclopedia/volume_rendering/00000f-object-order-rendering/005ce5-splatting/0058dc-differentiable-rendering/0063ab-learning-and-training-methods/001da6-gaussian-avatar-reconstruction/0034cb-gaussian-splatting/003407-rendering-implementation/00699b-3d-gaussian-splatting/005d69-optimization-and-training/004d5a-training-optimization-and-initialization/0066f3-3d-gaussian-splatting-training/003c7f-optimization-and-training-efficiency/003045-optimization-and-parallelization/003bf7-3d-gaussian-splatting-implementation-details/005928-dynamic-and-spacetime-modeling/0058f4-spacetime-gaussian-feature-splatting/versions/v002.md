# Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting

Also called: STG, Spacetime Gaussians

## Overview
Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting (STG) is a dynamic scene representation that extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to the 4D spacetime domain to model emerging, vanishing, and deforming content in real-time [S1]. It distinguishes itself from static 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing time-dependent opacity, parametric polynomial motion/rotation, and neural features, while outperforming grid-based dynamic methods like K-Planes in adapting to delicate scene structures and achieving higher rendering speeds [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Multi-view video sequences (sparse point clouds from Structure-from-Motion at all timestamps, camera poses, and ground-truth images).
*   **Output:** A set of Spacetime Gaussians (STGs) and a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) $\Phi$, enabling real-time novel view synthesis at arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps.

**Data Structures:**
Each STG $i$ stores the following parameters:
1.  **Spatial Opacity:** $\sigma_{is}$ (scalar, time-independent).
2.  **Temporal Opacity:** $\sigma_{\tau i}$ (scalar, scaling factor), $\mu_{\tau i}$ (scalar, temporal center).
3.  **Motion (Position):** Polynomial coefficients $\{b_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{n_p}$ where $b_{i,k} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
4.  **Rotation:** Polynomial coefficients $\{c_{i,k}\}_{k=0}^{n_q}$ where $c_{i,k} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternions).
5.  **Scaling:** $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (time-independent diagonal scaling vector).
6.  **Features:** $f_{base} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $f_{dir} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $f_{time} \in \mathbb{R}^3$. Total feature dimension is 9.

**Formulas:**
The opacity of an STG at spacetime point $(x, t)$ is defined as [S1]:
$$ \alpha_i(t) = \sigma_i(t) \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu_i(t))^T \Sigma_i(t)^{-1} (x - \mu_i(t)) \right) $$
where $\sigma_i(t)$ is the temporal opacity, $\mu_i(t)$ is the time-dependent position, and $\Sigma_i(t)$ is the time-dependent covariance.

**Temporal Opacity:**
Modeled as a 1D Gaussian in time [S1]:
$$ \sigma_i(t) = \sigma_{is} \exp \left( -s_{\tau i} |t - \mu_{\tau i}|^2 \right) $$

**Polynomial Motion:**
Position $\mu_i(t)$ is modeled using a polynomial trajectory [S1]:
$$ \mu_i(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n_p} b_{i,k} (t - \mu_{\tau i})^k $$
In implementation, $n_p = 3$ [S1].

**Polynomial Rotation:**
Rotation $q_i(t)$ is modeled as a polynomial of quaternions [S1]:
$$ q_i(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{n_q} c_{i,k} (t - \mu_{\tau i})^k $$
The rotation matrix $R_i(t)$ is derived from $q_i(t)$. In experiments, $n_q = 1$ [S1].

**Covariance:**
The covariance matrix $\Sigma_i(t)$ is constructed from the time-independent scaling $S_i$ and time-dependent rotation $R_i(t)$ [S1]:
$$ \Sigma_i(t) = R_i(t) S_i S_i^T R_i(t)^T $$
where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ [S1].

**Projection:**
The 3D Gaussian is projected to 2D image space using a first-order approximation of the perspective transformation [S1]:
$$ \mu^{2D}_i = \left( K \left( \frac{W \mu_i(t)}{(W \mu_i(t))_z} \right) \right)_{1:2} $$
$$ \Sigma^{2D}_i = \left( J W \Sigma_i(t) W^T J^T \right)_{1:2,1:2} $$
where $W$ is the viewing transformation, $K$ is the projection matrix, and $J$ is the Jacobian of the projective transformation [S1].

**Feature Splatting and Rendering:**
Instead of spherical harmonics, each STG stores features $f_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}^9$ [S1]:
$$ f_i(t) = [f_{base}, f_{dir}, (t - \mu_{\tau i})f_{time}]^T $$
During rendering, features are splatted to the image plane. At each pixel, the splatted features are split into $F_{base}, F_{dir}, F_{time}$. The final color $I$ is computed by passing these features through a tiny MLP $\Phi$ along with the view direction $r$ [S1]:
$$ I = F_{base} + \Phi(F_{dir}, F_{time}, r) $$
The volume rendering equation for the accumulated color remains [S1]:
$$ I = \sum_{i \in N} c_i \alpha^{2D}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha^{2D}_j) $$
where $N$ is the set of Gaussians sorted in depth order, and $c_i$ is the feature vector (which is processed by $\Phi$) [S1].

**Optimization:**
Parameters optimized include $\sigma_{is}, s_{\tau i}, \mu_{\tau i}, \{b_{i,k}\}, \{c_{i,k}\}, s_i, f_{base}, f_{dir}, f_{time}$ and the MLP $\Phi$ [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Loss:** L1 term + D-SSIM term comparing rendered images to ground truth [S1].
*   **Density Control:** Interleaved with training. More aggressive pruning than standard 3DGS is used to reduce Gaussian count [S1].
*   **Scaling Constraint:** $S_i$ is kept time-independent because time-conditioned scaling did not improve rendering quality [S1].

**Guided Sampling:**
To address sparse coverage in distant areas, new Gaussians are sampled after training loss stabilizes [S1]:
1.  Aggregate training errors patch-wise to identify substantial errors.
2.  Select rays from the center of high-error patches.
3.  Use a coarse depth map (generated during splatting) to determine a specific depth range.
4.  Sample new Gaussian centers uniformly within this depth range along the rays, adding small noise [S1].
5.  This process is conducted no more than 3 times per scene [S1].

**Lite-Version:**
For maximum speed, the MLP $\Phi$ can be dropped, using only $F_{base}$ for rendering [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def initialize_stgs(point_clouds):
    """
    Initialize Spacetime Gaussians from SfM sparse point clouds.
    Returns list of STG objects with random initial parameters.
    """
    stgs = []
    for point in point_clouds:
        stg = STG()
        stg.position = point.position
        stg.opacity = random_positive_scalar()
        stg.scaling = random_vector_3d()
        stg.rotation = identity_quaternion()
        stg.temporal_center = random_timestamp()
        stg.temporal_scaling = random_positive_scalar()
        stg.spatial_opacity = random_positive_scalar()
        # Polynomial coefficients for motion (degree 3)
        stg.motion_coeffs = [random_vector_3d() for _ in range(4)]
        # Polynomial coefficients for rotation (degree 1)
        stg.rotation_coeffs = [random_quaternion() for _ in range(2)]
        # Features
        stg.f_base = random_vector_3d()
        stg.f_dir = random_vector_3d()
        stg.f_time = random_vector_3d()
        stgs.append(stg)
    return stgs

def get_stg_state(stg, t):
    """
    Compute time-dependent state of an STG at time t.
    """
    # Temporal Opacity
    sigma_t = stg.spatial_opacity * exp(-stg.temporal_scaling * (t - stg.temporal_center)**2)
    
    # Position
    pos = vector_3d(0,0,0)
    for k, coeff in enumerate(stg.motion_coeffs):
        pos += coeff * (t - stg.temporal_center)**k
        
    # Rotation Matrix
    q = quaternion(0,0,0,1)
    for k, coeff in enumerate(stg.rotation_coeffs):
        q += coeff * (t - stg.temporal_center)**k
    q = normalize(q)
    R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q)
    
    # Covariance
    S = diag(stg.scaling)
    Sigma = R @ S @ S.T @ R.T
    
    return pos, Sigma, sigma_t

def feature_splatting(stgs, camera, t):
    """
    Render image using feature splatting.
    """
    image = zeros_like(target_image)
    alpha_accum = zeros_like(target_image)
    
    # Sort STGs by depth
    sorted_stgs = sort_by_depth(stgs, camera, t)
    
    for stg in sorted_stgs:
        pos, Sigma, sigma_t = get_stg_state(stg, t)
        
        # Project to 2D
        mu_2d = project_2d(pos, camera.W, camera.K)
        Sigma_2d = project_covariance(Sigma, camera.W, camera.J)
        
        # Compute 2D Gaussian alpha
        alpha_2d = compute_2d_alpha(mu_2d, Sigma_2d, sigma_t)
        
        # Compute Feature
        f_base = stg.f_base
        f_dir = stg.f_dir
        f_time = stg.f_time
        f_splat = f_base + f_dir * view_direction + f_time * (t - stg.temporal_center)
        
        # Composite
        weight = alpha_2d * (1 - alpha_accum)
        image += f_splat * weight
        alpha_accum += alpha_2d * (1 - alpha_accum)
        
        if alpha_accum > 0.99:
            break
            
    return image, alpha_accum

def guided_sampling(stgs, camera, error_map, depth_map, t):
    """
    Sample new Gaussians in high-error regions.
    """
    patches = find_high_error_patches(error_map, threshold=0.1)
    new_stgs = []
    for patch in patches:
        ray = get_ray_from_patch_center(patch, camera)
        depth_min, depth_max = get_depth_range_from_map(depth_map, ray)
        num_samples = 5 # Example constant
        for _ in range(num_samples):
            depth = uniform_random(depth_min, depth_max)
            pos = ray.origin + ray.direction * depth
            pos += small_noise()
            new_stg = create_new_stg(pos, t)
            new_stgs.append(new_stg)
    return new_stgs

def train_step(stgs, cameras, gt_images, t):
    """
    Single training iteration.
    """
    pred_images = []
    for camera, gt in zip(cameras, gt_images):
        pred, _ = feature_splatting(stgs, camera, t)
        pred_images.append(pred)
        
    loss = 0
    for pred, gt in zip(pred_images, gt_images):
        loss += L1_loss(pred, gt)
        loss += D_SSIM_loss(pred, gt)
        
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    
    # Density control
    stgs = prune_low_opacity(stgs, threshold=0.005)
    stgs = split_large_gradients(stgs)
    
    return loss
```

Addresses omnidirectional or wide-baseline dynamic scenes, while STG is optimized for multi-view video setups with specific temporal polynomial modeling [12929-360-gaussian-splatting.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is 40-60 minutes for a 50-frame sequence on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Model size is compact. Feature dimension is 9 per STG, compared to 48 for 3-degree Spherical Harmonics [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   140 FPS at 1352 × 1014 resolution on the Neural 3D Video Dataset [S1].
    *   66 FPS at 8K resolution on an Nvidia RTX 4090 GPU [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Rendering speed is high due to the shallow/narrow MLP and efficient splatting [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time high-resolution rendering, compact storage, handles emerging/vanishing content, adaptive to scene structures [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Polynomial motion may be less expressive than deformation fields for complex non-rigid deformations (though better than linear models) [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time dynamic view synthesis with multi-view video input [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring extremely complex non-rigid deformation where polynomial motion is insufficient, or when deformation fields are preferred [S1].

## Background
Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting was introduced by Zhan Li, Zhang Chen, Zhong Li, and Yi Xu from OPPO US Research Center and Portland State University in the paper "Spacetime Gaussian Feature Splatting for Real-Time Dynamic View Synthesis" (arXiv:2312.16812v2, Apr 4 2024) [S1]. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [41] and addresses the challenges of dynamic scene representation by combining temporal opacity, polynomial motion, and neural features [S1].
