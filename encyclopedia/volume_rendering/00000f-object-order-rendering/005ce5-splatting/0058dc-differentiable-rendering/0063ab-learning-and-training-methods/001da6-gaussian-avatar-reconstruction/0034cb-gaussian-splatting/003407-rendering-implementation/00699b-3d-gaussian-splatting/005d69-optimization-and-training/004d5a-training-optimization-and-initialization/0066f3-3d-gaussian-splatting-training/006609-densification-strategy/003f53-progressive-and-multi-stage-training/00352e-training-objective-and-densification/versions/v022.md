# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the loss function and densification strategy for **MeshGS** [S1], a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It solves the problem of Gaussian splats drifting away from geometry or expanding excessively by introducing surface-aware regularization and projection losses. It differs from standard 3DGS [22] by splitting the Gaussian cloud into **tightly-bound** ($G^t$) and **loosely-bound** ($G^l$) subsets, applying mesh-aligned constraints only to the former, while retaining the original densification logic for the latter.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and ground truth image $I_i$.
    *   Set of tightly-bound Gaussians $G^t$ and loosely-bound Gaussians $G^l$.
    *   Mesh $M$ consisting of faces $F$, where each face $f_j$ has vertices $v_i$ and normal $n_f$.
    *   Gaussian parameters: center $P_i$, scale $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$, rotation $R_i$, and opacity.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Gradient updates for Gaussian parameters based on the total loss $L$.
    *   New Gaussian splats generated via densification.

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Primitive:** Contains position $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scale $S_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, rotation $R_i \in SO(3)$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color $C_i$.
*   **Mesh Face:** Defined by three vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$ and a computed face normal $n_f$.
*   **Distance Threshold:** $d_{th} = 0.01$ [S1]. Used to classify Gaussians as tightly-bound ($d \le d_{th}$) or loosely-bound ($d > d_{th}$).

### Formulas
The total loss $L$ is a weighted sum of four terms [S1]:

$$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($L_{img}$):** Combines L1 loss and D-SSIM [S1].
    $$ L_{img} = (1 - \lambda_{img}) L_1(\hat{I}_i, I_i) + \lambda_{img} SSIM(\hat{I}_i, I_i) $$
    Where $\hat{I}_i$ is the rendered image, $I_i$ is the ground truth, and $\lambda_{img}$ is the SSIM weight.

2.  **Normal Consistency Loss ($L_{nc}$):** Aligns Gaussian normals with mesh face normals [S1].
    $$ L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^t, f \in F} (1 - n_i \cdot n_f) $$
    Where $n_i$ is the estimated normal of Gaussian $i$, and $n_f$ is the normal of the closest mesh face $f$.

3.  **Scale Regularization Loss ($L_{scale}$):** Constrains the scale of tightly-bound Gaussians to match the mesh surface thickness [S1].
    $$ L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \left( \lambda_{min} |\min(s_1, s_2, s_3)| + \lambda_{max} |\max(s_1, s_2, s_3) - \rho| \right) $$
    Where $\rho$ is the target maximum scale threshold.

4.  **Projection Loss ($L_{proj}$):** Pulls Gaussian centers toward the mesh surface [S1].
    $$ s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j $$
    $$ L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \| s_i - P_i \|_2 $$
    Where $s_i$ is the projection of $P_i$ onto the plane defined by face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$. Note: The source formula implies projection onto the face plane; in practice, this should be the closest point on the triangle, but the source explicitly defines it via the face plane equation [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{img} = 0.001$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{nc} = 0.1$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{min} = 0.1$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{max} = 10$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{proj} = 50$ [S1]
*   $\rho = 0.1$ (threshold for max scale) [S1]
*   $d_{th} = 0.01$ (distance threshold for binding) [S1]
*   Training iterations: 30,000 [S1]

### Steps and Densification Strategy
The method distinguishes between tightly-bound ($G^t$) and loosely-bound ($G^l$) Gaussians.

1.  **Classification:** For each Gaussian $i$, calculate the distance to the nearest mesh surface. If distance $\le d_{th}$, classify as $G^t$; otherwise, $G^l$ [S1].
2.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute $L_{img}$ for all Gaussians.
    *   For $i \in G^t$:
        *   Estimate normal $n_i$: Identify the axis of $S_i$ with the minimum scale value. Apply rotation $R_i$ to this axis vector to get $n_i$ in world coordinates [S1].
        *   Find the closest mesh face $f_j$ for each $i \in G^t$.
        *   Compute $L_{nc}$ using $n_i$ and $n_f$ of $f_j$ [S1].
        *   Compute $L_{scale}$ using $S_i$ and $\rho$ [S1].
        *   Compute $L_{proj}$ by projecting $P_i$ onto the plane of $f_j$ [S1].
    *   Sum losses to get $L$ [S1].
3.  **Densification (Cloning/Splitting):**
    *   **For $G^l$:** Follow the original 3DGS densification strategy (gradient-based cloning and splitting) [S1].
    *   **For $G^t$:** Use mesh-aligned densification [S1]:
        *   Identify the mesh triangle close to the Gaussian to be densified.
        *   **Cloning:** Sample a new point from the triangle surface. Copy all attributes (position, scale, rotation, color, opacity) from the parent Gaussian to the new Gaussian. Initialize the new Gaussian's position at the sampled point [S1].
        *   **Splitting:** Sample a new point from the triangle surface. Divide the parent Gaussian's scale by a factor of 1.6. Initialize the new Gaussian's position at the sampled point. Copy other attributes (color, opacity) [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_loss_and_densify(Gaussians, Mesh, Images, GT_Images, Iteration):
    # Parameters
    lambda_img = 0.001
    lambda_nc = 0.1
    lambda_min = 0.1
    lambda_max = 10
    lambda_proj = 50
    rho = 0.1
    d_th = 0.01
    
    G_t = []
    G_l = []
    
    # 1. Classify Gaussians
    for g in Gaussians:
        dist = distance_to_mesh(g.position, Mesh)
        if dist <= d_th:
            G_t.append(g)
        else:
            G_l.append(g)
            
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # 2. Compute Image Loss for all
    rendered_img = render(Gaussians, Images)
    l1_loss = L1(rendered_img, GT_Images)
    ssim_loss = SSIM(rendered_img, GT_Images)
    L_img = (1 - lambda_img) * l1_loss + lambda_img * ssim_loss
    total_loss += L_img
    
    # 3. Compute Mesh-Aligned Losses for Tightly-Bound Gaussians
    L_nc = 0.0
    L_scale = 0.0
    L_proj = 0.0
    
    for g in G_t:
        # Estimate normal: min scale axis rotated to world
        min_scale_idx = argmin(g.scale)
        local_axis = zero_vector()
        local_axis[min_scale_idx] = 1.0
        g.normal = rotate_vector(local_axis, g.rotation)
        
        # Find closest face
        face = find_closest_face(g.position, Mesh)
        face_normal = face.normal
        face_vertices = face.vertices
        
        # Normal Consistency
        dot_product = dot(g.normal, face_normal)
        L_nc += (1.0 - dot_product)
        
        # Scale Regularization
        min_s = min(g.scale)
        max_s = max(g.scale)
        L_scale += lambda_min * abs(min_s) + lambda_max * abs(max_s - rho)
        
        # Projection Loss
        # Project P_i onto the plane defined by face
        # Formula from source: s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) . f_j) * f_j
        # Note: This projects onto the plane normal to f_j passing through v_i
        # However, standard projection to plane uses the plane normal. 
        # The source formula seems to imply projection along the face normal?
        # Let's follow the source formula literally:
        # s_i = P_i + dot(P_i - v_i, face_normal) * face_normal
        # This is the orthogonal projection of P_i onto the plane defined by face_normal and v_i.
        v_i = face_vertices[0]
        projection_offset = dot(g.position - v_i, face_normal) * face_normal
        s_i = g.position + projection_offset
        L_proj += norm(s_i - g.position)
        
    total_loss += lambda_nc * L_nc + L_scale + lambda_proj * L_proj
    
    # 4. Densification
    new_gaussians = []
    
    # Loosely-bound: Original 3DGS strategy
    for g in G_l:
        if needs_cloning(g):
            new_g = clone_gaussian(g)
            new_gaussians.append(new_g)
        if needs_splitting(g):
            new_g = split_gaussian(g, factor=1.6)
            new_gaussians.append(new_g)
            
    # Tightly-bound: Mesh-aligned strategy
    for g in G_t:
        if needs_cloning(g):
            # Sample point from closest triangle
            sample_point = sample_from_triangle(g.closest_triangle)
            new_g = clone_gaussian(g)
            new_g.position = sample_point
            new_gaussians.append(new_g)
            
        if needs_splitting(g):
            # Sample point from closest triangle
            sample_point = sample_from_triangle(g.closest_triangle)
            new_g = split_gaussian(g, factor=1.6)
            new_g.position = sample_point
            new_gaussians.append(new_g)
            
    return total_loss, new_gaussians
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Projection Formula Ambiguity:** The source formula for $L_{proj}$ projects the Gaussian center onto the *plane* of the face, not necessarily the *triangle* itself. This may allow Gaussians to project outside the mesh boundary if the face is part of a larger planar surface. [S1]
*   **Normal Estimation:** Using the minimum scale axis to estimate the normal assumes the Gaussian is flattened against the surface. If the Gaussian is not yet aligned, this normal estimate may be inaccurate, leading to unstable $L_{nc}$ gradients. [S1]
*   **Scale Threshold $\rho$:** If $\rho$ is set too low, tightly-bound Gaussians may be penalized for being too large, potentially causing them to vanish or become unstable. If too high, they may penetrate the mesh. [S1]

## Subtopics

### [Budget-Centered Training](9682-budget-centered-training.md)
Constrains the primitive count to a target budget $N^*$ from initialization, replacing gradient-based pruning with a redundancy map. It differs by treating the budget as a hard constraint rather than a post-hoc optimization goal.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The addition of mesh proximity queries and per-Gaussian normal/face alignment calculations increases the per-iteration cost compared to standard 3DGS. The densification step for $G^t$ requires sampling from triangles, which is more expensive than gradient-based splitting.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the mesh geometry (vertices, faces, normals) in addition to Gaussian parameters.
*   **Performance:** Evaluated on Mip-NeRF360 and Deep Blending datasets for 30k iterations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Ensures Gaussians remain aligned with the mesh surface, reducing artifacts and improving geometry reconstruction quality for mesh-based scenes.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the input mesh. If the mesh is noisy or incomplete, the projection and normal losses may misguide the optimization. The projection formula may not respect triangle boundaries.

## Background
This method originates from **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting for High-Quality Rendering) [S1], published in 2024. It builds on the original 3DGS [22] by integrating explicit mesh geometry into the training loop. The approach addresses the limitation of standard 3DGS, which does not inherently respect existing 3D geometry, by binding a subset of Gaussians to a mesh and guiding their densification and regularization accordingly.
