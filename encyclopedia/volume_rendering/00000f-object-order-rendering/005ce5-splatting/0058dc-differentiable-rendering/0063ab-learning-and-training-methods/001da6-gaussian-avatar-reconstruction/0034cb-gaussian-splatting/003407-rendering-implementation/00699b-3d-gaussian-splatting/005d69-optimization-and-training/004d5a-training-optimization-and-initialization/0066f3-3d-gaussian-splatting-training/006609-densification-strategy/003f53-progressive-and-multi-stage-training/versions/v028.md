# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Curriculum Learning for 3DGS, Stage-wise Optimization

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training structures the optimization of 3D Gaussian primitives into discrete phases to establish a valid geometric backbone before refining details [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike single-stage training, which often converges to suboptimal local minima or exhibits poor structural fidelity, this approach decouples initialization from refinement, yielding significantly higher rendering quality [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md) by focusing on the topological evolution of the primitive set (mesh or point cloud) rather than the supervision signal itself.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, where each $g_k$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$ (parameterized by scale $s_k$ and rotation $q_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k$ [Parent].

The core mechanism involves periodic evaluation of a metric per Gaussian to determine if the current representation is insufficient or redundant in a specific region. Common metrics include:
1.  **Gradient Magnitude:** The accumulated 2D positional gradient $\tau_k = \frac{1}{N_{\text{views}}} \sum_{v} \| \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu_v^k} \|_2$ [S4]. High gradients indicate that the Gaussian's position is not adequately minimizing the loss, suggesting a need for refinement or replacement.
2.  **Opacity:** Low opacity $\alpha_k < \alpha_{\min}$ indicates a primitive contributes negligibly to the rendered image and is a candidate for pruning [S1, unofficial][S2].
3.  **Scale:** Excessively large scales $max(s_k)$ relative to the scene extent $e_{scene}$ indicate coarse approximations that may blur details or occlude nearby geometry [Parent].

Densification operations are typically triggered every $N$ iterations (e.g., `refineEvery` = 100) to allow the optimizer to stabilize . The primary operations are:
*   **Splitting:** Replacing a parent Gaussian $g_k$ with $N_{children}$ children. The children's means $\mu_{child}$ are sampled from a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ [Parent]. The scale is typically reduced, e.g., $s_{child} = s_k \cdot \frac{5}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{children}}}$, to increase local density [Parent].
*   **Cloning:** Creating an exact copy of $g_k$ (same $\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, \mathbf{c}$) to increase sampling frequency in high-error regions without changing the spatial footprint [Parent].
*   **Pruning:** Removing Gaussians that fail to meet opacity or significance thresholds [S1, unofficial][S2].

Buffer management is critical: pruned or split Gaussians require buffer compaction or flag-based marking to maintain contiguous memory access for rasterization [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
Structures optimization into discrete stages to establish a valid geometric backbone before refining details. Unlike single-stage training, which often converges to suboptimal local minima or exhibits poor structural fidelity, this approach decouples initialization from refinement, yielding significantly higher rendering quality. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md) by focusing on the topological evolution of the primitive set (mesh or point cloud) rather than the supervision signal itself.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the loss function and densification strategy for **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting), a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It solves the problem of geometric misalignment and excessive volume expansion in standard Gaussian splatting by introducing mesh-aware regularization and projection losses. It differs from standard 3DGS [Parent] by splitting the Gaussian set into **tightly-bound** ($G^t$) and **loosely-bound** ($G^l$) subsets, applying distinct densification rules and loss terms to each, thereby enforcing surface adherence while maintaining rendering flexibility.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines a pre-initialized or pre-trained representation to achieve high-fidelity rendering, often by adapting the Gaussian primitives' attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients) to specific scene data. It solves the problem of initializing a valid geometric backbone and then iteratively densifying and optimizing primitives to match input views, distinguishing itself from end-to-end training by leveraging prior knowledge or coarse initialization to accelerate convergence and improve stability. Unlike naive optimization which may overfit or fail to generalize, fine-tuning strategies often decouple geometry and appearance or employ specific regularization to prevent error coupling and forgetting.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Addresses the instability and incomplete volumetric coverage inherent in optimizing 3D Gaussian representations for volume rendering by structuring training into a curriculum of distinct supervision phases. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) by explicitly decoupling global spatial guidance (via sparse voxels) from local structural continuity (via slices), ensuring that the Gaussian primitives learn both the coarse volumetric backbone and fine anatomical details without the destabilization caused by random sampling or the lack of global context seen in slice-only approaches.

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
A curriculum learning strategy for feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting that decouples geometry optimization from pose estimation to stabilize training. It addresses the error coupling between inaccurate initial camera poses and Gaussian geometry by first training a pose-predicting backbone, then refining geometry with ground-truth poses, and finally fine-tuning the entire system in a fully pose-free setting. This approach sets itself apart from siblings like [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) by explicitly freezing the pose estimator during the geometric refinement phase to prevent pose errors from corrupting the Gaussian distribution.

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
- [S3] A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance.
- [S3] Stage 1: Construct RGB point clouds for each input view.
- [S3] Stage 1: Project the constructed point clouds to the target view using the differentiable point cloud renderer.
- [S3] Stage 1: Aggregate all point clouds from the various input views to render RGB images at the target view.
- [S3] Stage 1: Optimize the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and the ground truth target images.
- [S3] Stage 2: Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module).
- [S3] Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain.
- [S3] Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability.

## Background
The concept of progressive training in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a response to the instability of optimizing high-dimensional Gaussian parameters from sparse or noisy initializations. Early works noted that naive densification from random or SfM-initialized points often led to artifacts or incomplete coverage [S1, unofficial][S2]. The Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy [S1, unofficial][S2] and MeshGS [S1, unofficial] formalized the separation of geometric initialization from appearance refinement. Concurrently, feed-forward methods like FreeSplat [S4] and pose-predictive models [S1, unofficial] adopted multi-stage curricula to decouple camera pose estimation from geometry learning, preventing error coupling. The technique is now standard in high-fidelity 3DGS pipelines, particularly for dynamic scenes [S2] and large-scale reconstruction [S4].
