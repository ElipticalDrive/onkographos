# Planar Gaussian Splatting

Also called: PGSR, Planar GS

## Overview
Planar Gaussian Splatting (PGS) is an unsupervised neural rendering framework that reconstructs 3D geometry and explicitly parses 3D planar surfaces from multiple RGB images without requiring 3D plane labels or depth supervision [S1]. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing discriminative 3D plane descriptors and a hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Tree (GMT) to group Gaussian primitives into distinct planar instances [S1]. Unlike post-hoc mesh extraction methods, PGS learns planarity directly during optimization via local planar alignment and holistic separability constraints [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multiple RGB images of a scene with known camera poses.
**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives with optimized parameters (position, covariance, color, opacity, spherical harmonics, surface normal, and plane descriptor) and a hierarchical tree structure grouping these primitives into 3D planes.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Primitive:** Contains standard 3DGS parameters plus:
    *   $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Surface normal vector at the center.
    *   $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^d$: Learnable plane descriptor vector with unit norm ($||\mathbf{z}||_2 = 1$) [S1].
2.  **Gaussian Mixture Tree (GMT):** A tree-structured arrangement where leaf nodes correspond to individual Gaussian primitives (or clusters derived from SAM masks) and parent nodes are formed by probabilistically merging child nodes [S1]. The root's children represent distinct 3D planes [S1].
3.  **Region Adjacency Graph (RAG):** Used to merge 2D segments belonging to the same planar surface. Nodes are segments; edges exist if segments are adjacent. Edges are cut if planar distance > 10 cm or surface normal cosine distance > 20 degrees [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize Gaussian locations, colors, and opacities using a sparse point cloud obtained from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) on training images [S1].
    *   Initialize plane descriptors $\mathbf{z}$ and normals $\mathbf{n}$ [S1].

2.  **Leaf Node Generation (GMT Construction):**
    *   Prompt Segment Anything Model (SAM) with a $32 \times 32$ regular grid of points on each input 2D image to generate masks [S1].
    *   Ignore segments with high variance in normal vectors as invalid regions [S1].
    *   Merge segments using the RAG to handle multi-view consistency and variable mask counts [S1].
    *   Lift the boundary points of each refined SAM mask proposal into 3D space using the rendered depth map and camera parameters [S1].
    *   For each set of boundary 3D points, compute the mean and covariance matrix of a Gaussian distribution. These distributions form the leaf nodes of the GMT [S1].

3.  **Plane Descriptor Learning via 2D SAM Lifting:**
    *   Render a 2D descriptor image for each camera view by blending the 3D descriptors $\mathbf{z}$ of the Gaussian field [S1].
    *   Solve for linear regression weights $\hat{\mathbf{W}}$ to map rendered 3D descriptors to 2D segment labels (one-hot vectors $\mathbf{y}$) using the closed-form solution:
        $$ \hat{\mathbf{W}} = (\mathbf{Z}^T \cdot \mathbf{Z})^{-1} \cdot \mathbf{Z}^T \cdot \mathbf{Y} $$
        where $\mathbf{Z}$ is the matrix of rendered 3D descriptors augmented with a column of 1s, and $\mathbf{Y}$ is the matrix of one-hot labels [S1].
    *   Compute segmentation loss:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{seg} = \sum_i ||\mathbf{y}_i - \hat{\mathbf{y}}_i||_1 $$
        where $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i = [\mathbf{z}_i | 1] \cdot \hat{\mathbf{W}}$ [S1].
    *   This regression is recomputed for each camera view [S1].

4.  **Local Planar Alignment:**
    *   Compute K Nearest Neighbors (KNN) indices for each Gaussian (K=30) [S1].
    *   Compute the $3 \times 3$ covariance matrix of the KNN Gaussian centers [S1].
    *   Perform Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on the covariance matrix. The local tangent plane is defined by the two eigenvectors corresponding to the two largest eigenvalues [S1].
    *   Project Gaussian centers onto their local tangent planes to enforce surface alignment [S1].
    *   Apply Laplacian smoothing on learned normal and descriptor features by averaging over KNN neighbors [S1].
    *   Recompute KNN after any refinement (cloning, splitting, culling) [S1].

5.  **Holistic Separability (Mean-Shift):**
    *   Apply recurrent mean-shift to enhance separability among descriptors corresponding to distinct planes [S1].
    *   Update rule for descriptor matrix $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$:
        $$ \mathbf{Z} \leftarrow \mathbf{Z} \cdot (\eta \cdot \mathbf{K} \cdot \mathbf{D}^{-1} + (1-\eta) \cdot \mathbf{I}) $$
        where $\mathbf{K}$ is the pairwise distance kernel using von Mises-Fisher, $\mathbf{D}$ is the diagonal matrix of row sums of $\mathbf{K}$, $\mathbf{I}$ is the identity matrix, and $\eta$ is a mixing parameter [S1].
    *   **Efficient Kernel Estimation:** To avoid quadratic memory cost of $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, estimate $\mathbf{K}$ on a random subset of $M$ points ($M \ll N$) without replacement. Propagate and share the estimated kernel and update vectors across all KNN samples within the chosen set. Continue sampling until an update vector is computed for all samples or their neighbors [S1].

6.  **Gaussian Mixture Tree Merging:**
    *   Similar Gaussians are progressively merged probabilistically in the tree-structured Gaussian mixtures to identify distinct 3D plane instances [S1].
    *   Parent nodes are recursively formed by merging Gaussian nodes [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Training iterations: 15,000 [S1].
*   Gradient densification threshold: 0.001 [S1].
*   Spherical harmonics degrees: 2 [S1].
*   RAG cutting edge threshold (planar distance): 10 cm [S1].
*   RAG cutting edge threshold (surface normal cosine distance): 20 degrees [S1].
*   Recursive mean-shift frequency: Once after 2,000 iterations, then every 100 iterations [S1].
*   Mean-shift update steps: 10 steps per run [S1].
*   Mean-shift gamma ($\gamma$): 60 [S1].
*   KNN neighbours (K): 30 [S1].
*   Local planar alignment frequency: Every 500 iterations [S1].
*   Holistic separability frequency: Every 100 iterations [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def train_pgs(images, camera_poses):
    # 1. Initialization
    gaussians = initialize_from_sfm(images, camera_poses)
    for g in gaussians:
        g.z = random_unit_vector() # Plane descriptor
        g.n = random_normal()      # Surface normal

    # 2. Pre-compute SAM masks and RAG
    sam_masks = prompt_sam(images, grid_size=32)
    rag = build_rag(sam_masks, planar_dist_thresh=0.1, normal_cos_thresh=20)
    refined_masks = merge_rag(rag)

    for iteration in range(15000):
        # 3. Standard 3DGS Optimization Step
        render_images = render(gaussians, camera_poses)
        loss = photometric_loss(render_images, images)
        loss.backward()
        optimize_gaussians(gaussians, lr=0.001)
        
        # Densification with higher threshold
        if iteration % densify_interval == 0:
            densify(gaussians, threshold=0.001)

        # 4. Plane Descriptor Learning (Every iteration or specific interval)
        for cam in camera_poses:
            Z_rendered = render_descriptors(gaussians, cam)
            Y_labels = get_sam_labels(refined_masks, cam)
            # Solve linear regression: W = (Z^T Z)^-1 Z^T Y
            W = solve_linear_regression(Z_rendered, Y_labels)
            # Update descriptors based on gradient of L_seg
            update_descriptors(gaussians, W)

        # 5. Local Planar Alignment (Every 500 iterations)
        if iteration % 500 == 0:
            knn_indices = compute_knn(gaussians, k=30)
            for g in gaussians:
                neighbors = get_knn_neighbors(g, knn_indices)
                cov = compute_covariance([n.center for n in neighbors])
                eigvecs = svd(cov).eigenvectors
                tangent_plane = eigvecs[:, :2] # Two largest eigenvalues
                g.center = project_on_plane(g.center, tangent_plane)
                # Laplacian smoothing
                g.n = average_normals([n.n for n in neighbors])
                g.z = average_descriptors([n.z for n in neighbors])
            # Recompute KNN after refinement
            knn_indices = compute_knn(gaussians, k=30)

        # 6. Holistic Separability (Every 100 iterations)
        if iteration % 100 == 0:
            # Efficient Mean-Shift
            Z = get_all_descriptors(gaussians)
            # Estimate kernel on subset M
            M = random_sample(gaussians, size=M_subset)
            K_subset = compute_vmf_kernel(M)
            D_subset = diag(sum(K_subset, axis=1))
            # Propagate updates
            for g in gaussians:
                if g in M or has_neighbor_in_M(g, knn_indices):
                    update = g.z @ (eta * K_subset @ inv(D_subset) + (1-eta) * I)
                    g.z = normalize(update)

        # 7. GMT Merging (Probabilistic merging of similar Gaussians)
        # This is implicit in the descriptor clustering/separability
        # Explicit tree construction happens post-training or periodically
        if iteration % 1000 == 0:
            build_gaussian_mixture_tree(gaussians)

    return gaussians, gmt
```

## Subtopics

### Plane Descriptor Learning via 2D SAM Lifting
Learns 3D plane descriptors $\mathbf{z}$ by lifting 2D segmentation masks from SAM to 3D using linear regression to map rendered 3D descriptors to 2D segment labels. It resolves the lack of multi-view proposal association and variable numbers of masks by solving a linear regression problem for each view [S1].

### Local Planar Alignment of 3DGS
Enforces Gaussian centers to lie on object surfaces by projecting them onto local tangent planes, computed from K Nearest Neighbors (KNN) covariance matrices. It also applies Laplacian smoothing on normal and descriptor features [S1].

### Gaussian Mixture Tree
A probabilistic approach for planar construction of the scene geometry in a bottom-up modeling. It models the scene as a tree structure where leaf nodes are Gaussians and parent nodes are formed by merging similar Gaussians, allowing the identification of distinct 3D plane instances [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The mean-shift update is optimized to take approximately one second on the GPU for millions of points using subset sampling [S1]. Holistic separability overhead is minor, occurring every 100 iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Computing the full kernel $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is quadratic in memory and infeasible for millions of points; thus, subset sampling is required [S1].
*   **Performance:** On ScanNet, PGS achieves an Accuracy of 0.137 and Completeness of 0.118, outperforming PlanarRecon (0.154 Accuracy, 0.187 Completeness) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Unsupervised, does not require 3D labels or depth supervision, state-of-the-art planar reconstruction, generalizable across datasets [S1].
*   **Limitations:** High-quality rendering is not the primary objective (uses SH degree 2), relies on SAM for initialization which may propagate errors if masks are poor [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Tested on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 Ti [S1].

## Background
Planar Gaussian Splatting (PGS) was proposed by Farhad G. Zanjani, Hong Cai, Hanno Ackermann, Leila Mirvakhabova, and Fatih Porikli from Qualcomm AI Research [S1]. It was presented at WACV 2025 (arXiv:2412.01931v1, Dec 2024) [S1]. The method builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [16] and leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [17] for 2D segmentation priors [S1].
