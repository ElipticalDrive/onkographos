# Optimization Schedule and Densification

Also called: Densification Scheduling, Primitive Growth Control

## Overview
This family of methods addresses the inefficiency and instability of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training by controlling the rate of primitive creation (densification) and the resolution at which the scene is optimized. Standard 3DGS uses fixed thresholds for splitting and cloning, often leading to redundant primitives or slow convergence. These methods differ from siblings like [Pruning and Density Control](16215-pruning-and-density-control.md) by focusing on the *schedule* and *criteria* for adding primitives, and from [3D Gaussian Splatting Training Loop](15139-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-loop.md) by providing specific algorithms for dynamic resolution and adaptive budgeting rather than just the loop structure.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of optimizing the trade-off between rendering fidelity and computational cost during the training of 3D Gaussian primitives. They operate on the standard 3DGS primitive representation:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ (Spherical Harmonics)

The core mechanism involves modulating the number of active primitives $P$ and the rendering resolution $r$ over the course of optimization iterations $t$. The optimization complexity is defined as the product of rendering resolution and primitive count, which dominates time costs [S5]. Methods typically employ a "concave-up" growth curve for primitives, where growth accelerates over time, synchronized with increasing rendering resolution to fit high-frequency details only after low-frequency structures are established [S5].

## Subtopics

### [Error-Driven 2D Gaussian Training](13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md)
A densification strategy that initializes view-dependent Gaussian kernels by optimizing 2D Gaussian primitives in image space before back-projecting them to 3D [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md]. It solves the problem of initializing complex specular radiance by leveraging pre-rendered images as fixed backgrounds, distinguishing it from standard 3DGS densification which operates directly in 3D space. It allocates supplementary Gaussians proportional to rendering loss and samples positions via multinomial distribution based on composite $L_1$ and SSIM loss [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md].

### [DashGaussian](15742-dashgaussian.md)
A scheduling scheme that accelerates training by reformulating optimization as progressive fitting of higher frequency components [15742-dashgaussian.md]. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count, producing a concave-up primitive growth curve that spans the entire optimization process. Unlike methods that peak in primitive count at half-iteration, DashGaussian synchronizes primitive growth with dynamically increasing rendering resolution to balance computing redundancy and fitting quality [15742-dashgaussian.md].

### [Training Configuration and Hyperparameters](16385-training-configuration-and-hyperparameters.md)
Defines specific hyperparameters, optimization schedules, and data sampling strategies for 3D-aware generative models and downstream tasks [16385-training-configuration-and-hyperparameters.md]. It differs from the parent by focusing on global training loop parameters (iterations, LR, batch size) and camera sampling distributions rather than the dynamic primitive management (splitting, cloning) that occurs within the loop. It specifies distinct protocols for generative decoder training (e.g., 500k iterations, Adam LR 0.0009) and downstream classification (e.g., AdamW, specific group sizes) [16385-training-configuration-and-hyperparameters.md].

### QuickSplat Optimization Process
An optimization framework that uses learned networks for initialization, densification, and parameter updates to accelerate surface reconstruction [S1]. It iterates for $t = 0$ to $T-1$, accumulating gradients $\nabla G_t$ from all images, and generates new Gaussians $\hat{G}_t$ using a densifier network $f_D$ on current Gaussians, accumulated gradients, and time step $t$: $\hat{G}_t \leftarrow f_D(G_t, \nabla G_t, t)$ [S1].

### BlitzGS Optimization Schedule
A schedule designed to reduce active workload by managing the "LOD gate" and density control windows [S2, unofficial]. It disables the LOD gate during the density-control window (iterations 2,000–32,000) and engages it after the first importance-scoring pass ($T_1$). After this pass, density-control gradients are reweighted using the per-Gaussian visibility factor $\phi_i$ for the remainder of the window [S2, unofficial]. Extending training iterations (e.g., 500k–1000k) does not significantly improve quality on aerial scenes, indicating deficits stem from representation structure rather than insufficient budget [S2, unofficial].

### Normal Gradient Based Densification
A densification criterion to improve normal recovery in thin regions by introducing an additional criterion on the gradient of normals, beyond the vanilla 3DGS gradient of view space points [S3]. It densifies Gaussians whose normal gradient exceeds a threshold $T_n$. The typical value for $T_n$ is $2 \times 10^{-9}$ [S3].

### Global-to-local densification
A strategy that decouples split and clone operations across densification phases to accelerate optimization [S4, unofficial]. It uses split for global spread at low resolution and suppresses clone, then reintroduces clone for local refinement at full resolution. The norm of the average position gradient is calculated every 100 iterations; if it exceeds a threshold, the Gaussian is split if its maximum scale exceeds a scale threshold, otherwise it is cloned [S4, unofficial].

### Resolution Guided Primitive Scheduler
A scheduler that uses a momentum-based budgeting approach to control primitive growth [S5]. The final primitive count $P_{fin}^{(i)}$ is calculated as:
$$ P_{fin}^{(i)} = \max(P_{fin}^{(i-1)}, \gamma \cdot P_{fin}^{(i-1)} + \eta \cdot P_{add}) $$
where $P_{add}$ is the number of primitives densified in step $i$, and $P_{fin}^{(0)}$ is initialized as $5 \cdot P_{init}$ [S5]. The power factor in primitive growth starts at 2 and gradually decreases to 1 to suppress early growth and encourage mid-stage growth. Hyperparameters are $\gamma = 0.98$ and $\eta = 1$ [S5].

### 3D Refinement View Sampling Strategy
A staged sampling schedule for supervising 3D refinement that balances fitting the current refined view with preserving rendering ability in original and previously refined views [S6]. It divides total 3D refinement steps into three equal phases. In the first third, every 3 steps are 'current-refine steps'. In the subsequent third, every 5 steps are 'current-refine steps'. In the final third, every 8 steps are 'current-refine steps'. For non-current-refine steps, views are sampled from the training set $S_{train}$ and previous refined set $F_{i-1}$, with lower selection weight for $F_{i-1}$ [S6].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [26] introduced a novel explicit scene representation using differentiable Gaussian primitives. However, the original training procedure, which relies on fixed thresholds for densification (splitting and cloning) and full-resolution rendering throughout, can be computationally expensive and prone to redundancy. Subsequent works have focused on optimizing this process. QuickSplat [S1] introduced learned networks for densification. DashGaussian [S5] proposed dynamic resolution and concave-up primitive growth. BlitzGS [S2, unofficial] focused on city-scale efficiency with LOD gating. Other works like [S3] and [S4, unofficial] addressed specific geometric issues (normal recovery, global vs local spread) through modified densification criteria.
