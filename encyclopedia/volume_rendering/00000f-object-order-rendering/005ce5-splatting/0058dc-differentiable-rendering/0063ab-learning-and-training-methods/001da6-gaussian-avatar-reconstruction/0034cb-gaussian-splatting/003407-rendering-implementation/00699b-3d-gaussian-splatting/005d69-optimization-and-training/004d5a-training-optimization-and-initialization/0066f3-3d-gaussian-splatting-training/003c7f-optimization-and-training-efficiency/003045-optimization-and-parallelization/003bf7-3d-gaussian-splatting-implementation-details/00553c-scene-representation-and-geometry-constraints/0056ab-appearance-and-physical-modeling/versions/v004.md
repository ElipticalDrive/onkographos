# Appearance and Physical Modeling


## Overview
Appearance and Physical Modeling extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to handle complex lighting, material properties, and non-vacuum media by modifying the definition, storage, and optimization of view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and opacity $\alpha_i$. It solves the limitations of static Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients, which struggle with high-frequency specularities, complex lighting variations, and volumetric light transport in participating media. This family sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md) and [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) by focusing on the radiometric and physical properties of the primitives rather than their geometric density or surface extraction. It distinguishes between [Color and Appearance Modeling](14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md), which synthesizes appearance in clear space, and [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md), which models volumetric attenuation in scattering media.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [Color and Appearance Modeling](14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md)
Addresses the limitations of the base representation, where view-dependent color is stored as fixed Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients per Gaussian. This approach struggles with high-frequency specularities, complex lighting variations, and transient objects in unstructured datasets. The family solves this by introducing dynamic mechanisms to compute or modulate the outgoing radiance $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and opacity $\alpha_i$, moving beyond static SH storage. It sets itself apart from [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md) by focusing on appearance synthesis and material-light interaction rather than volumetric light transport in participating media.

### [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md)
Extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to handle non-vacuum media and physically consistent surface extraction by modifying the rendering equation or the underlying density field. It solves the problem of light transport in scattering media (e.g., underwater fog) and the geometric inconsistency of discrete alpha-blending for surface reconstruction. Unlike [Color and Appearance Modeling](14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md), which focuses on view-dependent shading and material properties in clear space, this family explicitly models volumetric attenuation and backscatter or reformulates the Gaussian primitive as a continuous attenuation field.

### Relightable 3D Gaussians
A differentiable point-based rendering framework that enhances vanilla 3D Gaussians by associating extra properties (normal vectors, BRDF parameters, and incident lighting) to achieve photo-realistic relighting and BRDF decomposition from multi-view images [S1].

**Definition:**
The method assigns a normal vector $\mathbf{n}$, BRDF values, and incident light information to each 3D Gaussian point to model per-point light reflectance [S1]. It splits the incident light into a global environment map and an indirect incident light field for robust material and lighting estimation [S1]. To produce plausible shadow effects, it introduces an innovative point-based ray tracing with bounding volume hierarchies (BVH) for efficient visibility pre-computation [S1]. Proper regularizations are introduced to enhance geometry and mitigate material-lighting ambiguity, including constraints on depth distribution, smoothness priors, and a lighting regularization [S1].

**Step:**
1.  Assign a normal vector $\mathbf{n}$, BRDF values, and incident light information to each 3D Gaussian point to model per-point light reflectance [S1].
2.  Apply physically based rendering (PBR) to get a PBR color for each 3D Gaussian point, which is then alpha-composited to obtain the rendered color for the corresponding image pixel [S1].
3.  Split the incident light into a global environment map and an indirect incident light field for robust material and lighting estimation [S1].
4.  Introduce proper regularizations to enhance geometry and mitigate material-lighting ambiguity, including constraints on depth distribution, smoothness priors, and a lighting regularization [S1].

## Background
Originating from the need to overcome the static nature of Spherical Harmonics in 3D Gaussian Splatting, this family emerged as researchers sought to incorporate physically based rendering (PBR) and volumetric effects into point-based representations. The "Relightable 3D Gaussians" method, published by Gao et al. in 2024, represents a key development in this area, introducing BVH-based ray tracing for visibility and BRDF decomposition [S1]. These methods are used in applications requiring realistic relighting, material editing, and rendering in non-vacuum environments, extending the utility of 3DGS beyond static novel view synthesis.
