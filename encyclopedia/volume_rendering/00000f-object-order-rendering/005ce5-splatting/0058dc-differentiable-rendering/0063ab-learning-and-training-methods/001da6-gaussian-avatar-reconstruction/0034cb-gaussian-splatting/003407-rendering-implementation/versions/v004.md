# Rendering Implementation

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization Pipeline, 3DGS Rendering

## Overview
Rendering Implementation defines the computational pipeline that transforms a set of 3D Gaussian primitives into a 2D image via screen-space rasterization and alpha compositing . It solves the real-time rendering bottleneck of volumetric methods by replacing ray marching with a differentiable rasterization pipeline, allowing gradients to flow from the rendered image back to Gaussian parameters . It sets itself apart from its siblings by relying on full 3D covariance projection and tile-based depth sorting to handle arbitrary 3D geometry and occlusion .

## Shared principles
All rendering implementations for 3D Gaussian Splatting share the following core mechanism:

1.  **Activation and Covariance Construction**:
    *   Rotations $q$ are normalized: $q_{norm} = q / \|q\|$ .
    *   Scales $s$ are exponentiated to ensure positivity: $s_{eff} = \exp(s)$ .
    *   Opacities $\alpha$ are sigmoided: $\alpha_{eff} = \sigma(\alpha)$ .
    *   The 3D covariance matrix is constructed from the rotation matrix $R$ (derived from $q_{norm}$) and scaling matrix $S = \text{diag}(s_{eff})$:
        $$ \Sigma_{3D} = R S S^T R^T $$
        .

2.  **Camera Space Transformation**:
    *   Means are transformed to camera space: $\mu' = V \mu$ .
    *   Covariance is transformed to camera space using the upper-left $3 \times 3$ submatrix of the view matrix $V$:
        $$ \Sigma' = V_{1:3, 1:3} \Sigma_{3D} V_{1:3, 1:3}^T $$
        .

3.  **Projection to Screen Space**:
    *   The Jacobian $J$ of the projective projection is computed at $\mu'$:
        $$ J = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{f_x}{\mu'_z} & 0 & -\frac{f_x \mu'_x}{\mu'_z^2} \\ 0 & \frac{f_y}{\mu'_z} & -\frac{f_y \mu'_y}{\mu'_z^2} \end{pmatrix} $$
        .
    *   The 2D screen-space covariance is computed:
        $$ \Sigma_{2D} = J \Sigma' J^T $$
        .
    *   The 2D mean is computed:
        $$ \mu_{2D} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{f_x \mu'_x}{\mu'_z} + c_x \\ \frac{f_y \mu'_y}{\mu'_z} + c_y \end{pmatrix} $$
        .

4.  **Culling and Sorting**:
    *   Gaussians are culled if they are behind the near plane ($\mu'_z < z_{near}$) or outside the view frustum .
    *   Remaining Gaussians are sorted by camera-space depth $\mu'_z$ to enable correct front-to-back alpha compositing .

5.  **Rasterization and Compositing**:
    *   Gaussians are rasterized as 2D ellipses. The alpha contribution at a pixel offset $\mathbf{x}$ from the mean is:
        $$ \alpha_{pixel} = \alpha_{eff} \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \mathbf{x}^T \Sigma_{2D}^{-1} \mathbf{x}\right) $$
        .
    *   Colors are accumulated using front-to-back blending:
        $$ C_{acc} \leftarrow C_{acc} + T \cdot \alpha_{pixel} \cdot c $$
        $$ T \leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_{pixel}) $$
        .
    *   Early termination is applied when transmittance $T$ drops below a threshold $\tau$ .

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering (CPU-Sorted, Instanced)](2058-3d-gaussian-splatting-rendering-cpu-sorted-instanced.md)
A display-only technique that projects Gaussians into 2D quads, sorts them by depth on the CPU, and renders them using hardware-instanced drawing with standard alpha blending. It sets itself apart by relying on CPU-side depth sorting to resolve the non-commutative nature of alpha blending, avoiding complex GPU compute shaders. Unlike [SortFree Weighting](8006-sortfree-weighting.md), it maintains strict physical accuracy of the compositing order at the cost of sorting overhead.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering (Software)](2805-3d-gaussian-splatting-rendering-software.md)
A novel view synthesis method that replaces ray marching with a tile-based, differentiable software rasterization pipeline. It sets itself apart by using a specific object-order sorting strategy followed by tile-based rasterization, distinct from the CPU-sorted instanced approach [2058-3d-gaussian-splatting-rendering-cpu-sorted-instanced.md] or the pure rasterization kernel focus [2812-3d-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md].

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization](2812-3d-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md)
A screen-space rendering method that projects 3D Gaussians into 2D ellipses and accumulates color/opacity via alpha compositing. It differs from siblings like [2D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization](4815-2d-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md) by relying on full 3D covariance projection and tile-based depth sorting to handle arbitrary 3D geometry and occlusion.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . It addresses the computational cost of evaluating the volume rendering equation via quadrature, which requires querying points outside Gaussian supports [Parent]. The method uses volume splatting for efficient rasterization [Parent]. It is widely used for real-time novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction .
