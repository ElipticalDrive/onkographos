# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive geometric regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without this adaptation [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on complex scheduling or pre-trained feature extractors, this method adapts multi-scale voxel geometry by leveraging low-frequency components early in training and high-frequency details later [S1, Parent].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Training views with known camera poses (rotation $R_i$, translation $t_i$) and intrinsics $K_i$; a multi-scale voxel representation where each scale $l$ produces a rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ and RGB color $C_i(p_i)$ for pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$; a set of training rays $R_{train}$.
*   **Outputs:** A geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ used to update the voxel parameters (depths/colors) during optimization.

**Data Structures**
The method operates on a multi-scale voxel grid. Let $L$ be the number of scales (e.g., Low, Mid, High). For each scale $l \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$, the system maintains rendered depth maps $D_l$ and color maps $C$. The specific voxel layout is defined by the underlying multi-scale voxel framework (see [Parent](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md)).

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Iterate over training rays:** For each ray $r_{train} \in R_{train}$ originating from pixel $p_i$ in training frame $i$:
2.  **Compute 3D points per scale:** For each scale $l$:
    *   Retrieve the rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ from volume rendering at scale $l$.
    *   Compute the 3D point in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$, denoted $x_{li}$:
        $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
        where $\tilde{p}_i$ is the homogeneous augmentation of pixel coordinate $p_i$ [S1].
    *   Alternatively, this point can be computed directly from the ray origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
        $$ x_{li} = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
        [S1]
3.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s pose $(R_i, t_i)$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]
4.  **Transform to Target Camera Coordinates:** Select a target frame $j$ (typically a different training view). Transform $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s pose $(R_j, t_j)$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]
5.  **Reproject to 2D:** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$, denoted $\tilde{p}_{li \to j}$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
6.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the color difference between the original pixel and the warped pixel:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]
7.  **Select Pseudo-Ground Truth Depth:** Identify the scale $l^*$ with the minimal reprojection error magnitude:
    $$ l^* = \arg \min_l (e_l(p_i)) $$
    [S1]
    The depth at this optimal scale, $D_{il^*}(p_i)$, serves as the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for the geometric adaptation loss [S1].
8.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the current depth at scale $l$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth across all training rays and scales:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L-1} \sum_{r_{train} \in R_{train}} \left( D_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \right)^2 $$
    [S1]

**Pseudocode**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geo_loss(voxels, training_views, rays):
    """
    Computes the cross-scale geometric adaptation loss Lgeo.
    
    Args:
        voxels: Multi-scale voxel structure providing render_depth(scale, ray) and render_color(scale, ray).
        training_views: List of view objects, each containing:
            - id: unique identifier
            - K: 3x3 camera intrinsic matrix
            - R: 3x3 rotation matrix
            - t: 3x1 translation vector
            - C: RGB image map
        rays: List of ray objects, each containing:
            - p_i: 2D pixel coordinate in source frame
            - source_view_id: ID of the frame where the ray originates
            - origin: 3D origin point in world coordinates
            - direction: 3D direction vector
            
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar tensor representing Lgeo.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    num_scales = voxels.num_scales()
    
    for ray in rays:
        src_view = training_views[ray.source_view_id]
        p_i = ray.p_i
        K_i = src_view.K
        R_i = src_view.R
        t_i = src_view.t
        
        # Homogeneous coordinate for reprojection
        p_i_hom = np.array([p_i[0], p_i[1], 1.0])
        
        # Precompute inverse intrinsics term for camera space depth
        # x_li = D * K^-1 * p_hom
        # Note: K^-1 * p_hom gives the unnormalized ray direction in camera space
        cam_ray_dir = np.linalg.inv(K_i) @ p_i_hom
        
        min_errors = []
        depths_per_scale = []
        
        # Step 1-3: Compute 3D points in world space for each scale
        for l in range(num_scales):
            # Get depth from volume rendering at scale l
            D_il = voxels.render_depth(l, ray)
            
            # Compute 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
            # x_li = D_il * (K_i^-1 * p_i_hom)
            x_li = D_il * cam_ray_dir
            
            # Transform to world coordinates
            # x_l = R_i * x_li + t_i
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            depths_per_scale.append(D_il)
            
            # Step 4-5: Reproject to other views to find min error
            # We need to compare against other training views. 
            # For simplicity, assume we iterate all other views j != i.
            # In practice, a subset or specific pairing might be used.
            # Here we compute error for all j and take the min across l and j.
            
            view_errors = []
            for other_view in training_views:
                if other_view.id == src_view.id:
                    continue
                
                K_j = other_view.K
                R_j = other_view.R
                t_j = other_view.t
                
                # Transform world point to camera j
                # x_li_to_j = R_j^T * (x_l - t_j)
                x_li_to_j = R_j.T @ (x_l - t_j)
                
                # Project to 2D
                # p_li_to_j = pi(K_j * x_li_to_j)
                proj = K_j @ x_li_to_j
                if proj[2] == 0:
                    continue # Avoid division by zero
                p_li_to_j = np.array([proj[0]/proj[2], proj[1]/proj[2]])
                
                # Sample color from frame j at p_li_to_j
                # C_j(p_li_to_j)
                C_j_val = voxels.render_color_at_pixel(other_view.id, p_li_to_j)
                
                # Reprojection error
                # e_l = C_i(p_i) - C_j(p_li_to_j)
                C_i_val = voxels.render_color_at_pixel(src_view.id, p_i)
                error = np.linalg.norm(C_i_val - C_j_val)
                view_errors.append(error)
            
            if view_errors:
                min_errors.append(min(view_errors))
            else:
                min_errors.append(float('inf'))
        
        # Step 7: Select scale with minimal reprojection error
        # Note: The source defines D'(r_train) as the depth of the scale with min error.
        # The loss is computed against this pseudo-GT.
        if min_errors:
            best_scale_idx = np.argmin(min_errors)
            pseudo_gt_depth = depths_per_scale[best_scale_idx]
            
            # Step 8: Compute MSE loss for all scales against the pseudo-GT
            # L_geo = sum_l sum_r (D_l(r) - D'(r))^2
            for l in range(num_scales):
                D_l = depths_per_scale[l]
                diff = D_l - pseudo_gt_depth
                total_loss += diff ** 2
                
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters and Edge Cases**
*   **Scales:** The method typically uses 3 scales (Low, Mid, High) [S1].
*   **Early Training:** Low-resolution voxels are used as pseudo-ground truth to guide coarse geometry learning [S1].
*   **Late Training:** Medium- and high-resolution voxels refine details [S1].
*   **Edge Cases:** If reprojection fails (e.g., $z=0$), the error is ignored or penalized heavily [G]. The method assumes accurate camera poses; significant viewpoint changes or sparse views may challenge generalization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method adds a computational overhead proportional to the number of scales and the number of target views used for reprojection. For each ray, it computes 3D points for all scales and reprojects them to other views.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. It achieves lower final depth error than using a simple depth prior ($\mathcal{L}_d$) alone [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive; does not require complex scheduling or external depth priors; robust to geometric noise due to multi-scale voxel structure [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses; performance may degrade in few-shot scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in the context of FrugalNeRF, a framework for extreme few-shot novel view synthesis [S1]. It was introduced to accelerate and regularize training by using weight-sharing voxels across scales and guiding multi-scale geometry via reprojection errors [S1]. The method addresses the issue of floaters and sub-optimal convergence in multi-scale representations by providing a stronger and more robust constraint than traditional depth priors [S1].
