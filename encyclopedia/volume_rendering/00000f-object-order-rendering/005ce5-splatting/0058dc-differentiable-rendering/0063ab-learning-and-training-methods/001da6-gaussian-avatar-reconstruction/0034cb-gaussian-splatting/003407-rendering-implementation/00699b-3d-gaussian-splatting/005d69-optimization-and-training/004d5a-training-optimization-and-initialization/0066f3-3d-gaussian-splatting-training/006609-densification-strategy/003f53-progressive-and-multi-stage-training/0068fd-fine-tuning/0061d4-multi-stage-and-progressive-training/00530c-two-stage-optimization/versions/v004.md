# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: GS-IR Training Process

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy that decouples the optimization of geometric/appearance parameters from material or compression parameters to prevent interference and instability [S1][S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem where jointly optimizing complex attribute sets (such as PBR materials or entropy-coded latents) leads to convergence issues or suboptimal rate-distortion performance [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from single-stage training by freezing base parameters during the second phase, and from other multi-stage methods by specifically targeting the separation of "codec-free" or "geometric" stability from "codec-aware" or "material" refinement [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of two sequential phases. The specific parameters optimized and the loss functions used depend on the variant (GS-IR or CodecSplat), but the structural logic remains consistent: Stage 1 establishes a stable base representation, and Stage 2 refines specific attributes while keeping the base fixed.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images and poses), target view images (for supervision), and initial Gaussian primitives (often predicted by a feed-forward network).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ with refined attributes (geometry, appearance, material, or compressed latents).

### Data Structures
The Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ contains primitives defined by:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}$, Scale $\mathbf{s}$, Rotation $\mathbf{q}$ (quaternion), Opacity $\alpha$.
*   Appearance: Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients $\mathbf{c}$ or color $\mathbf{c}$.
*   Material (GS-IR variant only): Albedo $\mathbf{a}$, Roughness $\rho$, Metallic $m$.
*   Latents (CodecSplat variant only): Feature tensor $\mathbf{F}$, encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$, decoder-side depth $S$.

### Numbered Steps

#### Stage 1: Base Optimization (Codec-Free / Initial)
1.  **Initialize:** Predict initial Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ (codec-free) or base attributes $T$ (geometry) and $A$ (appearance) using a feed-forward backbone [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Render:** Render the target view $\hat{Y}_{t}$ using the current Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$, camera intrinsics $K_t$, and extrinsics $T_t$:
    $$ \hat{Y}_{t} = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}, K_t, T_t) $$
3.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the reconstruction loss $L_{stage1}$ based on the difference between the rendered image $\hat{Y}_{t}$ and the ground truth target image $Y_t$ [S2, unofficial]. For GS-IR, this includes color and normal losses [S1].
4.  **Optimize:** Update the base parameters ($T, A$ or the backbone weights predicting $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$) using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [S1][S2, unofficial].
5.  **Iterate:** Repeat steps 2–4 for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 30,000 for GS-IR) [S1].

#### Stage 2: Attribute Decomposition / Codec-Aware Optimization
1.  **Freeze Base:** Fix the parameters optimized in Stage 1 ($T, A$ or the backbone weights) [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Enable Advanced Modules:**
    *   **GS-IR:** Enable material optimization $M$ (albedo, roughness, metalness) and lighting estimation [S1].
    *   **CodecSplat:** Enable the feature codec (quantization/entropy coding) and post-codec refiner [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Render:** Render the target view $\hat{Y}_{t}$ using the updated Gaussian set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ (decoded from latents or with updated materials) [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the composite loss $L_{stage2}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   **CodecSplat Loss:**
        $$ L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ D_{render}(\hat{Y}_t, Y_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} + \beta R \right] $$
        Where $D_{render}$ is the rendering reconstruction loss, $D_{codec-depth}$ is the codec-side depth loss, $R$ is the estimated rate, $\gamma$ is the depth supervision weight, $\beta$ is the rate penalty weight, and $\lambda$ is the rate parameter [S2, unofficial].
    *   **GS-IR Loss:**
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
        Where $L_{shade}$ is the shading loss, $TV_{mat}$ is the total variation loss for material, and $TV_{light}$ is the total variation loss for light [S1].
5.  **Optimize:** Update only the attribute parameters ($M$ or codec modules) [S1][S2, unofficial].
6.  **Iterate:** Repeat for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 10,000 for GS-IR) [S1].

### Formulas

**CodecSplat Specifics:**
*   **Codec-side Depth Loss:**
    $$ D_{codec-depth} = \| S - S_{enc} \|_1 $$
    Where $S$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $S_{enc}$ is the encoder-side normalized inverse-depth map [S2, unofficial].
*   **Encoder-side Normalized Inverse-Depth:**
    $$ S_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{D_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
    Where $D_{enc,i}(p)$ is the encoder-side depth, $n_i$ and $f_i$ are the near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S2, unofficial].

**GS-IR Specifics:**
*   **Normal Loss:**
    $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} TV_{normal} $$
    Where $TV_{normal}$ is a total variation smoothing term on the predicted normal map [S1].
*   **Decomposition Loss:**
    $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
    Where $L_{shade}$ measures the difference between rendered and target shading, and $TV_{mat}, TV_{light}$ are total variation losses for material and light maps [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Stage 1 Iterations:** 30,000 (GS-IR) [S1]. Not given in CodecSplat sources.
*   **Stage 2 Iterations:** 10,000 (GS-IR) [S1]. Not given in CodecSplat sources.
*   **Rate Parameter ($\lambda$):** Sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial]. Fixed at test time [S2, unofficial].
*   **Max Rate ($\lambda_{max}$):** 1024 (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Depth Supervision Weight ($\gamma$):** 0.1 (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Rate Penalty Weight ($\beta$):** $10^{-4}$ for DL3DV, $4 \times 10^{-4}$ for RE10K (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Smoothing Weight ($\lambda_{n-TV}$):** 5.0 (GS-IR) [S1].
*   **Material Smoothing Weight ($\lambda_M$):** 1.0 (GS-IR) [S1].
*   **Light Smoothing Weight ($\lambda_E$):** 0.01 (GS-IR) [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck (CodecSplat) is unstable; the two-stage approach is required to stabilize Gaussian generation first [S2, unofficial].
*   **Interference:** Fixing base parameters in Stage 2 prevents the optimization of materials or latents from degrading the geometric accuracy established in Stage 1 [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Rate Control:** The rate parameter $\lambda$ must be sampled during training to ensure robustness across different bitrates, but fixed at inference [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage training procedure for Relightable 3D Gaussians](15262-two-stage-training-procedure-for-relightable-3d-gaussians.md)
Defines the optimization for Relightable 3D Gaussians (R3DG), separating geometry/appearance optimization from material/lighting decomposition. It uses normal gradient-based densification in Stage 1 and PBR-specific losses in Stage 2. Choose this when relighting is required.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Proportional to the sum of iterations in Stage 1 and Stage 2. Stage 1 typically dominates due to the larger number of iterations (e.g., 30,000 vs 10,000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Standard 3DGS memory usage plus overhead for codec modules (if applicable) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** CodecSplat achieves KiB-level scene bitstreams while preserving structure, outperforming MiB-level compressed baselines [S2, unofficial]. GS-IR enables novel view synthesis and relighting with high fidelity [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Tested on single NVIDIA V100 (GS-IR) [S1] and multiple NVIDIA RTX 5880 (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Stable convergence for complex attribute optimization; effective rate-distortion control; decoupled optimization prevents interference.
*   **Limitations:** Requires two distinct training phases; tuning of multiple loss weights ($\lambda, \gamma, \beta, \lambda_M, \lambda_E$) is necessary.

## Background
*   **Origin:** The concept is applied in two distinct lines of research: GS-IR (Inverse Rendering with 3DGS) [S1] and CodecSplat (Ultra-Compact Latent Coding) [S2, unofficial].
*   **History:** GS-IR introduced the two-stage process for inverse rendering in 2024 [S1]. CodecSplat adapted the two-stage strategy for feed-forward 3DGS compression in 2026 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Usage:** Used in relightable scene reconstruction [S1] and ultra-compact 3DGS transmission/storage [S2, unofficial].
