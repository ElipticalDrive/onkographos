# Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Inner Gaussian Splatting, InnerGS

## Overview
Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting (InnerGS) is a framework for direct volumetric inference that models continuous volumetric density using 3D Gaussians to reconstruct internal structures from sparse, pose-free sliced data, eliminating the need for camera poses [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of modeling internal volumes where standard projection-based 3D Gaussian Splatting fails because learned Gaussians concentrate on the object's surface, leaving the interior volume empty, and because it relies on camera parameters (view/projection matrices) which are absent in sliced data [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by using **Conditional Splatting**, which adapts the bounding box per slice based on conditional Gaussians, whereas alternatives like 3D Ellipsoid Projection compute a cube and project identical bounding boxes across slices [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Sparse, pose-free sliced data (e.g., medical CT/MRI slices or material cross-sections).
**Outputs:** A 3D Gaussian representation of the internal volumetric density and color, allowing for tile-based rasterization to reconstruct smooth and detailed internal structures.

**Data Structures:**
The scene is represented as a cloud of 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by:
*   **Position:** $\mu = (\mu_x, \mu_y, \mu_z)$ defining the center.
*   **Covariance:** $\Sigma$, decomposed into scaling $S$ and rotation $R$: $\Sigma = RSS^\top R^\top$.
    *   $S$ is a diagonal matrix with scale factors $s_x, s_y, s_z$.
    *   $R$ is derived from a unit quaternion $q$ encoding rotation.
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i$.
*   **Color:** $c_i$, modeled using Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to capture view-dependent variations.
*   **Pixel Coordinates:** $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, W - 1]$ and $v \in [0, H - 1]$ for an image of width $W$ and height $H$.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Conditional 2D Gaussian Splatting:** For each depth slice $t$, compute conditional 2D Gaussian splats. These splats determine the 2D Gaussian center and influence radii in the image plane for that specific slice. This step adapts the bounding box per slice based on the conditional Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Marginal 1D Gaussian Combination:** Combine the conditional 2D Gaussians with a marginal 1D Gaussian along the depth axis to formulate the complete 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) density. This factorization allows the representation of continuous volumetric density without explicit 3D projection matrices [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Tile-based Rasterization:** Perform tile-based rasterization using the formulated 3DGS density. The rendering pipeline processes each slice independently but coherently to reconstruct the internal structures [S1, unofficial].

**Rendering Formula:**
The screen-space contribution of the $i$-th splat at pixel $(u, v)$ in standard 3DGS is computed as density $p_i(u, v)$ multiplied by opacity $\alpha_i$ and color $c_i$, accumulated front-to-back [S1, unofficial]:
$$ I(u, v) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(u, v)\alpha_i c_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(u, v)\alpha_j) $$

In InnerGS, the final color of a pixel at coordinates $(u, v)$ on a slice at depth $t$ is computed as:
$$ I(u, v, t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(u, v, t)\alpha_i c_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(u, v, t)\alpha_j) $$
where $p_i(u, v, t)$ is the density of the $i$-th Gaussian at pixel $(u, v)$ for slice $t$, derived from the conditional 2D and marginal 1D Gaussian combination [S1, unofficial].

```python
    Render internal volumetric structures from sparse slices using InnerGS.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of Gaussian objects, each with attributes:
            - mu: (mu_x, mu_y, mu_z) position vector

            - t: depth index
            - conditional_gaussians: 2D Gaussians specific to this slice

    """

        conditional_gs = slice_data['conditional_gaussians']
        
        # Initialize output image for this slice
        image = zeros((height, width, 3))

def compute_2d_gaussian_density(gs, u, v):
    """
    Compute the density of a 2D Gaussian splat at pixel (u, v).
    """
    # Extract 2D mean and covariance from the conditional Gaussian
    # The conditional Gaussian is derived from the 3D Gaussian parameters
    # projected/conditioned on the slice t.
    mean_2d = gs.mean_2d
    cov_2d = gs.cov_2d
    
    # Unnormalized Gaussian function: p(x) = exp(-0.5 * (x-mu)^T Sigma^-1 (x-mu))
    diff = array([u - mean_2d[0], v - mean_2d[1]])
    inv_cov = inverse(cov_2d)
    exponent = -0.5 * dot(diff, dot(inv_cov, diff))
    
    # Normalization factor is typically absorbed into the optimization or
    # handled by the rasterizer's tile-based accumulation.
    density = exp(exponent)
    
    return density
```

*   **Strengths:** Eliminates the need for camera poses; plug-and-play; inherently compatible with any data modalities; enables high-fidelity reconstruction of complex internal structures from sparse, pose-free sliced data; efficient, slice-based rendering pipeline improves computational efficiency and reconstruction accuracy [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption that internal structures can be effectively modeled by factorized Gaussians; performance may degrade with extremely sparse slice data [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When reconstructing internal volumetric structures from sparse, pose-free sliced data (e.g., medical imaging, material science) where camera poses are unavailable or unreliable.
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality external surface reconstruction is the primary goal and camera poses are available, as standard 3DGS may be more appropriate.

## Background
Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting was proposed by Shuxin Liang et al. in the paper "InnerGS: Internal Scenes Reconstruction and Segmentation via Factorized 3D Gaussian Splatting" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), which require heavy multi-layer perceptrons that are costly to train and slow to render, and standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which is projection-based and unsuitable for internal volumes without camera poses [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework but introduces Conditional Splatting and factorized density modeling to handle internal scenes [S1, unofficial].
