# Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting with Backscatter Loss

Also called: 3DGS adapted to aquatic environments

## Overview
This method adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to underwater environments by integrating ocean optics models, specifically learning direct attenuation ($B_d$), infinite backscatter color ($B_\infty$), and backscatter coefficient ($B_b$) [S1]. It solves the problem of color cast and visibility loss in underwater 3D reconstruction by modeling the physical interaction of light with water [S1]. Unlike vanilla 3DGS, which fails to handle the dominant blue/green palette and distance-dependent attenuation, this method maintains high speed while significantly improving reconstruction quality in aquatic scenes [S1]. It is significantly faster than NeRF-based alternatives like STNeRF and STNeRFacto [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$ initialized from COLMAP sparse points, a set of $K$ input images $\{I_k^{gt}\}_{k=1}^K$ with known camera poses, and initial depth estimates derived from the scene geometry [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters $\Theta$ (means, covariances, opacities, colors), medium parameters $B_d, B_\infty, B_b$, and rendered novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene is represented by the standard 3DGS structure:
*   $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
*   $\mathbf{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Rotation matrix.
*   $\mathbf{S}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector.
*   $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
*   $\mathbf{c}_i \in [0, 1]^3$: View-dependent color (SH coefficients).
*   $B_d \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Direct attenuation coefficient per channel.
*   $B_\infty \in [0, 1]^3$: Infinite backscatter color per channel.
*   $B_b \in [0, 5]$: Backscatter coefficient (scalar per channel or shared, sources imply per-channel fitting but scalar bound) [S1].

**Rendering Equation:**
The rendering process modifies the standard alpha-blending to incorporate medium properties. For a ray passing through a medium, the transmittance $T$ is modulated by direct attenuation $e^{-B_d z}$, where $z$ is the depth along the ray. The backscatter signal is modeled as $B_\infty (1 - e^{-B_b z})$ [S1]. The rendered image $I^r$ is a function of $\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initial Parameter Estimation:**
    *   Extract depth map $z$ from the current 3DGS representation. The depth is mapped to $[0, 1.0)$ using a shifted and scaled logistic function: $z_{norm} = \frac{2}{1 + e^{-0.1 \cdot z}} - 1$ [S1].
    *   Cluster the depth values into 10 evenly-spaced clusters covering the range from minimal to maximal $z$ [S1].
    *   Within each cluster, identify pixels in the ground-truth image $I_k^{gt}$ whose RGB triplets fall below the bottom 1st percentile [S1].
    *   These pixels serve as an overestimate of the backscatter signal $B_s$ [S1].
    *   Using the pairs of $(B_s, z)$ values, perform nonlinear least squares fitting to estimate initial parameters $\hat{B}_\infty$ and $\hat{B}_b$ for each RGB channel, ensuring $\hat{B}_\infty \in [0, 1]$ and $\hat{B}_b \in [0, 5]$ [S1].

2.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   Minimize the total loss $L$ over $\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   The learning rate for $B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ is identical to that of the Gaussian color parameters $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
    *   **Alternating Refinement:** Every 500 iterations, re-compute the initial estimates $\hat{B}_\infty$ and $\hat{B}_b$ using the updated depth map and the nonlinear least squares procedure described in Step 1. These new estimates are used to update the guidance term in the loss function [S1].

3.  **Loss Function:**
    The total loss is defined as:
    $$ L(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b) = \sum_{k=1}^K (1 - \lambda_1) \|I_k^{gt} - I_k^r(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b)\|_{\ell_1} + \lambda_1 L_{D-SSIM}(I_k^{gt}, I_k^r(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b)) + \lambda_2 L_{bs}(B_\infty, B_b) $$
    where:
    *   $\lambda_1 = 0.3$ (weight for D-SSIM) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_2 = 0.1$ (weight for backscatter loss) [S1].
    *   $L_{bs}(B_\infty, B_b) = \|B_\infty - \hat{B}_\infty\|_{\ell_1} + \|B_b - \hat{B}_b\|_{\ell_1}$ [S1].

4.  **Constraints:**
    *   $B_\infty \in [0, 1]$ [S1].
    *   $B_b \in [0, 5]$ [S1].
    *   $B_d$ follows the normalized RGB image range and is constrained to be physically non-negative [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import least_squares

def underwater_3dgs_training(Gaussians, Images, CameraPoses, iterations=30000):
    """
    Main training loop for Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting with Backscatter Loss.
    """
    # Hyperparameters
    lambda_1 = 0.3
    lambda_2 = 0.1
    lr = get_learning_rate_for_colors() # Inherited from original 3DGS
    
    # Initialize medium parameters with bounds
    B_d = np.zeros(3) # Direct attenuation, initialized to 0 or small positive
    B_inf = np.ones(3) * 0.5 # Infinite backscatter, initialized to mid-range [0,1]
    B_b = 1.0 # Backscatter coefficient, initialized to mid-range [0,5]
    
    # Optimizer setup (Adam)
    optimizer = AdamOptimizer([Gaussians, B_d, B_inf, B_b], lr=lr)
    
    for step in range(1, iterations + 1):
        # 1. Render current scene
        # Renders I_r based on Gaussians, B_d, B_inf, B_b
        I_r = render_underwater(Gaussians, B_d, B_inf, B_b, CameraPoses)
        
        # 2. Compute Photometric and Structural Losses
        loss_photometric = 0
        loss_ssim = 0
        for k in range(len(Images)):
            gt = Images[k]
            loss_photometric += np.mean(np.abs(gt - I_r[k]))
            loss_ssim += compute_d_ssim(gt, I_r[k])
            
        loss_photometric /= len(Images)
        loss_ssim /= len(Images)
        
        # 3. Compute Backscatter Guidance Loss
        # Note: B_inf_hat and B_b_hat are updated every 500 steps
        loss_bs = np.mean(np.abs(B_inf - B_inf_hat)) + np.mean(np.abs(B_b - B_b_hat))
        
        # 4. Total Loss
        total_loss = (1 - lambda_1) * loss_photometric + lambda_1 * loss_ssim + lambda_2 * loss_bs
        
        # 5. Backpropagate and Update
        total_loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        
        # 6. Alternating Refinement of Backscatter Estimates
        if step % 500 == 0:
            # Extract depth map from current Gaussians
            depth_map = extract_depth(Gaussians)
            # Normalize depth to [0, 1)
            z_norm = 2 / (1 + np.exp(-0.1 * depth_map)) - 1
            
            # Identify backscatter pixels (bottom 1st percentile in clusters)
            # This requires clustering z_norm and finding low-RGB pixels in corresponding image regions
            backscatter_pixels = find_backscatter_pixels(Images, z_norm)
            
            # Fit B_inf and B_b using nonlinear least squares
            # Model: B_s = B_inf * (1 - exp(-B_b * z))
            def backscatter_residual(params, z_vals, b_s_vals):
                b_inf, b_b = params
                # Ensure bounds
                b_inf = np.clip(b_inf, 0, 1)
                b_b = np.clip(b_b, 0, 5)
                model = b_inf * (1 - np.exp(-b_b * z_vals))
                return b_s_vals - model
            
            # Flatten arrays for fitting
            z_vals = backscatter_pixels['z']
            b_s_vals = backscatter_pixels['rgb'] # RGB triplets
            
            # Fit for each channel or jointly? Sources imply per-channel fitting for B_inf
            # Simplified: Fit scalar B_b and vector B_inf
            # Note: Implementation detail for joint fitting of B_inf (3D) and B_b (scalar)
            # is complex; sources state "nonlinear least squares fitting to estimate parameters... for each RGB channel"
            # implying B_b might be shared or per-channel. We assume per-channel B_inf and shared B_b for simplicity 
            # or per-channel B_b if sources allow. Sources say "B_b ... for each RGB channel" in one spot, 
            # but "B_b \in [0,5]" (scalar bound) in another. We follow the bound constraint.
            
            # Fitting B_inf per channel
            B_inf_hat = np.zeros(3)
            for c in range(3):
                z_c = z_vals
                b_s_c = b_s_vals[:, c]
                # Initial guess for fitting
                x0 = [0.5, 1.0] 
                res = least_squares(backscatter_residual, x0, args=(z_c, b_s_c), 
                                    bounds=([0, 0], [1, 5]))
                B_inf_hat[c] = res.x[0]
                B_b_hat = res.x[1] # Assuming shared B_b or taking mean/last
            
            # Update guidance targets
            B_inf_hat = np.clip(B_inf_hat, 0, 1)
            B_b_hat = np.clip(B_b_hat, 0, 5)

    return Gaussians, B_d, B_inf, B_b
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Depth normalization using the logistic function is critical for stable clustering [S1].
*   **Boundaries:** Parameters must be clipped to their physical bounds during optimization to prevent non-physical values (e.g., negative attenuation) [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The method relies on the existence of pixels with low RGB values (deep water/backscatter). If the scene is entirely shallow and clear, the initial estimation may fail or provide poor guidance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes only several minutes, significantly faster than NeRF-based methods [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Approximately 500 MB per scene over 30,000 steps on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU [S1]. This is slightly less than STNeRF's ~600 MB [S1].
*   **Inference Speed:** ~140 FPS average across multiple datasets, enabling real-time novel-view synthesis [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High speed, real-time rendering, improved reconstruction quality in underwater scenes compared to vanilla 3DGS, better handling of distant objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance may degrade if the initial depth estimation is poor, affecting the backscatter guidance [S1]. Relies on the assumption that backscatter can be estimated from dark pixels, which may not hold in very bright or cluttered shallow scenes [S1].
*   **When to use:** Underwater 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and color-reconstruction applications where speed and physical plausibility are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Non-underwater scenes where standard 3DGS is sufficient and faster to train without the overhead of medium parameter optimization [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in a 2026 paper published in *Scientific Reports* [S1]. It builds on the foundational 3DGS framework [S1] and integrates established formulations of underwater image formation [S1]. The approach addresses the limitations of vanilla 3DGS in aquatic environments, where color casts and attenuation are dominant [S1]. It was evaluated on the newly captured TableDB dataset (172 images, 1384×918 resolution) and public underwater datasets from the Red Sea, Curaçao, and Panama [S1].
