# Training Configuration and Hyperparameters

Also called: Decoder Training Setup, Training Configuration for Gaussian Splatting Decoder

## Overview
This article defines the standardized hyperparameters and optimization schedules for training 3D Gaussian Splatting decoders and classifiers, specifically addressing downstream tasks like classification, part segmentation, and integration with 3D-aware Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It differs from general radiance-field optimization by focusing on fixed-attribute Gaussian encodings and specific dataset protocols rather than dynamic densification schedules. It complements [Optimization Schedule and Densification](15107-optimization-schedule-and-densification.md) by providing the static configuration values used when densification is either disabled (for classification) or handled via decoder-specific constraints.

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Pre-trained Gaussian representations (e.g., from Gaussian-MAE or Gaussian-JEPA) or a target GAN latent space.
- Dataset-specific camera sampling distributions.
- Hyperparameter configuration (learning rate, batch size, epochs/iterations).

**Outputs:**
- Fine-tuned Gaussian attributes (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) or a trained decoder mapping latent vectors to Gaussian parameters.

### Data Structures
The Gaussian representation follows the standard 3DGS layout but is grouped for specific downstream tasks:
- **Gaussian Grouping:** Gaussians are organized into groups to facilitate attribute handling.
  - *ModelNet Protocol:* 1,024 Gaussians total, organized into 64 groups of 32 Gaussians each. All 14 attributes are used.
  - *ShapeNet-Part Protocol:* 2,048 Gaussians total, organized into 128 groups of 32 Gaussians each.
- **Attributes:** Standard 3DGS attributes (position $\mathbf{p}$, rotation $\mathbf{q}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}$). The specific count of attributes is fixed per protocol (e.g., 14 attributes for ModelNet).

### Numbered Steps
1. **Initialization:** Load pre-trained Gaussian encodings. For classification tasks, initialize the classifier head (linear or MLP). For decoder tasks, initialize the Gaussian Splatting decoder network.
2. **Camera Sampling:**
   - For full 360° view synthesis (e.g., PanoHead): Sample random cameras uniformly around the object (360°).
   - For frontal view synthesis (e.g., EG3D): Sample cameras with mainly frontal views, applying small vertical and horizontal rotations.
3. **Optimization Loop:**
   - Render images from sampled cameras.
   - Compute loss (e.g., MSE, LPIPS, ID similarity, structural similarity for GANs; cross-entropy for classification).
   - Backpropagate gradients.
   - Update parameters using the specified optimizer.
4. **Probing/Fine-tuning Modes:**
   - *Full Fine-tuning:* Update all Gaussian attributes and classifier/decoder parameters.
   - *Linear/MLP Probing:* Freeze the Gaussian encoder (pre-trained features) and update only the classifier head.

### Formulas
- **Loss Weights (Decoder):** For GAN decoder training, the total loss is a weighted sum:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_2 + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_3 + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_4 + \lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_5 $$
  Typical weights: $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4, \lambda_5) = (0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 1.0, 0.2)$ [S2].

### Parameters
- **Optimizer:** AdamW [S1, unofficial] or Adam [S2].
- **Learning Rate:**
  - ModelNet (Classification): $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1, unofficial].
  - ShapeNet-Part (Segmentation): $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
  - GAN Decoder: $9 \times 10^{-4}$ [S2].
- **Weight Decay:** $0.05$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Batch Size:**
  - ModelNet: 256 [S1, unofficial].
  - ShapeNet-Part: 16 [S1, unofficial].
- **Epochs/Iterations:**
  - Classification/Segmentation: 300 epochs [S1, unofficial].
  - GAN Decoder: 500,000 iterations [S2].
- **Warm-up:** 5 epochs [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Run-to-Run Variance:** ModelNet fine-tuning exhibits appreciable run-to-run variation, particularly on ModelNet40. Results should be interpreted with caution, and provenance must be tracked [S1, unofficial].
- **Protocol Matching:** When comparing methods, ensure the Gaussian count and attribute sets match exactly (e.g., 1K Gaussians for both pretraining and transfer) to avoid confounding factors [S1, unofficial].
- **Camera Bias:** EG3D focuses on frontal views, while PanoHead supports 360°. Training configurations must align with the target view distribution to avoid artifacts in unseen angles [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Training time is dominated by the number of iterations (500k for decoders) and batch size. Classification tasks are shorter (300 epochs) but require careful handling of variance [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians (1K or 2K) and attributes. Grouping (32 Gaussians/group) may facilitate memory-efficient updates but does not change the total memory footprint significantly [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Standardized protocols allow for fair comparison across different Gaussian-based methods. Fixed grouping simplifies implementation for downstream tasks.
- **Limitations:** High variance in ModelNet results can obscure method improvements. Frontal-view bias in EG3D limits applicability to full 3D space rendering compared to PanoHead [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **When to Use:** Use for fine-tuning pre-trained Gaussian encoders for classification/segmentation or for training Gaussian decoders for GANs. Not suitable for general novel-view synthesis without adaptation.

## Background
The training configurations described originate from studies on Gaussian-JEPA and Gaussian-MAE for 3D Gaussian splats, as well as Gaussian Splatting Decoders for 3D-aware GANs. These works established standardized protocols for evaluating Gaussian representations on downstream tasks, addressing the need for reproducible benchmarks in the rapidly evolving field of Gaussian-based rendering [S1, unofficial][S2]. The distinction between frontal (EG3D) and 360° (PanoHead) camera sampling highlights the adaptation of Gaussian splatting to different generative modeling constraints [S2].
