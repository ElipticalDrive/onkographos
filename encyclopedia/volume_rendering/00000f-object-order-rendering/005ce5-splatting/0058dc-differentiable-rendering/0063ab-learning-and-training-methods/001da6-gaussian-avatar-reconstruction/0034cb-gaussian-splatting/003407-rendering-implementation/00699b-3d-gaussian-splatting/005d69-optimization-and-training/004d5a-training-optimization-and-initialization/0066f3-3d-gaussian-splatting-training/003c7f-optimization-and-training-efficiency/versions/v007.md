# Optimization and Training Efficiency

Also called: Training Efficiency, Optimization Strategies

## Overview
This family encompasses the computational strategies, memory management techniques, and hyperparameter configurations required to efficiently train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models. It addresses the high memory and compute demands of differentiable rasterization by optimizing the backward pass, managing GPU/CPU memory transfers, and stabilizing gradient updates. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by focusing on the infrastructure and efficiency of the training loop itself, rather than specific parameter updates, loss variants, or representation modifications.

## Shared principles
The training process relies on a differentiable rasterizer that renders images from a set of 3D Gaussians. The core optimization mechanism involves computing the gradient of a loss function $\mathcal{L}$ (e.g., L1, SSIM) with respect to the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color, SH coefficients) and any auxiliary parameters.

Key shared mathematical components include:
1.  **Alpha Blending**: The color contribution of a Gaussian $i$ is modulated by its opacity $\alpha_i$ and transmittance $T_i$ from preceding Gaussians.
2.  **Gradient Propagation**: Gradients flow from the loss through the pixel color, through the alpha-blending chain, to the individual Gaussian attributes.
3.  **Parallelization Granularity**: Computation is parallelized across pixels, splats (Gaussians), or batches of views. The standard approach is per-pixel backpropagation, but alternatives exist [S2].
4.  **Memory Management**: Large scenes require managing the Gaussian list in memory, potentially across multiple GPUs or via out-of-core techniques.

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training > Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md)
Assigns distinct learning rates to different groups of Gaussian attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) within a single Adam optimizer. This stabilizes training by preventing parameters with high sensitivity (such as color or scale) from diverging due to large gradients, while allowing slower-converging parameters (such as position) to update appropriately. It sets itself apart from standard uniform learning rate approaches by decoupling the update magnitudes per attribute, addressing the varying gradient scales inherent in the differentiable rasterization pipeline.

### [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md)
Covers the broad infrastructure of training, including pipelines, distributed systems, and gradient computation optimization. It differs from sibling methods by covering the general computational strategies required to efficiently train 3DGS models, focusing on the optimization loop and parallel execution across hardware, rather than specific parameter updates or loss variants.

### [CLM](7351-clm.md)
Removes the GPU memory barrier by moving Gaussian states between CPU and GPU memory during optimization. It employs a smart caching policy to keep the most frequently updated Gaussians on the GPU.

### [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md)
Re-engineers the training loop to accelerate optimization by combining dynamic sparse rasterization with fast gradient checkpointing. It sets itself apart from standard training by skipping tiles with negligible transmittance during rasterization and recomputing only necessary intermediate values during the backward pass, rather than storing all activations.

### [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md)
Integrates the parameter update step of the optimizer directly into the backward pass of the differentiable rasterizer. It solves the problem of high VRAM usage and computational overhead associated with separate optimizer kernels by eliminating the need for intermediate buffers to store parameter states during the gradient computation phase.

### Faster-GS
Addresses oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters caused by a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio [S1, unofficial]. It rescales the scaling gradient with the inverse of the current scale to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial]. This cuts training time by nearly half compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Reduced Iteration Training
Reduces training iterations from the original 30,000 to 1,000 for the Neural3DVideo dataset and to 1,500 for the LongVolumetricVideo dataset [S2]. It keeps all other parameters identical to the original 3DGS implementation [S2]. Using the original 30,000 iterations on sparse input data causes overfitting [S2]. This reduces training time and improves novel view synthesis by approximately 4 PSNR [S2].

### Disk-Based Training Image Loading
Loads ground truth images from disk every iteration instead of storing them in VRAM, to enable training on consumer hardware with limited VRAM for ultra-large-scale datasets [S3, unofficial]. Standard 3DGS stores the entire training dataset in VRAM for speed. This method saves VRAM but reduces training performance [S3, unofficial].

### Sampling Gap Control
Controls the sampling rate by adjusting the sampling gap value to influence training speed and rendering quality [S4, unofficial]. Increasing the sampling gap value decreases the sampling rate, which is inversely proportional to the sampling rate [S4, unofficial]. Increasing the sampling gap increases pc time and makes the rendering result blurrier [S4, unofficial]. On the truck dataset with 3000 training steps, increasing the sampling gap from 2 to 500 increases pc time from 0.17739 to 0.20525 [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting training has evolved from the original implementation [S2] to address memory constraints and training stability. Early observations noted that gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters often oscillate due to a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio, leading to methods like Faster-GS [S1, unofficial]. As datasets grew larger, memory management became critical, leading to techniques like CLM [S1, unofficial] and disk-based loading [S3, unofficial]. The trade-off between memory usage and computational speed has driven innovations like FastGS [S1, unofficial] and fused backward passes [S1, unofficial].
