# Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control

Also called: Complexity-Aware Density Control, Complexity-Aware Level of Details, Complexity-Guided Relocation

## Overview
Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control is a training optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that replaces opacity-only metrics with a composite score incorporating local image complexity to guide Gaussian relocation and pruning [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem where standard opacity-based densification concentrates primitives in already-resolved regions while leaving complex, high-frequency areas under-served, by using complexity as a surrogate for reconstruction error to bias sampling and retention [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that rely solely on opacity or multi-view density, this approach explicitly decouples spatial footprint from photometric importance, allowing efficient coverage of simple regions and precise detail in complex ones [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates by maintaining a per-Gaussian complexity field, updating it online during training, and using it to modulate two key processes: the probability of selecting a Gaussian for relocation (splitting/duplication) and the importance score for retaining Gaussians under a limited budget (Level of Details).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Set of active 3D Gaussians $G = \{g_j\}$, where each $g_j$ has mean $\mu_j$, opacity $\alpha_j$, scale $s(g_j)$, and rotation.
    *   Current training view $i$ with image plane $\Pi_i$.
    *   Complexity map $C_i^{2D}(u)$ for view $i$, computed via delentropy [S1, unofficial].
    *   Budget factor $\tau \in (0, 1]$ for Level of Details (LoD) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Hyperparameters: EMA learning rate $\eta \in (0, 1]$, scalar weight $\lambda = 2$, complexity dropout probability $\delta = 0.2$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Updated complexity values $C_j$ for each Gaussian.
    *   Relocation destination probabilities $p(g_j)$.
    *   Importance scores $S(g_j)$ for sorting and pruning.

### Data Structures
*   **Complexity Field:** A scalar value $C_j$ stored per Gaussian. Initialized to 0 or a small constant.
*   **Normalized Complexity:** $\tilde{C}_j \in [0, 1]$, derived from min-max normalization of $C_j$ across all active Gaussians or a sliding window [S1, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Complexity Update:**
    After rendering a view $\Pi_i$ with full capacity, project the mean $\mu_j$ of every contributing Gaussian into the image plane. For each Gaussian $g_j$ whose center projects within the view's frustum, update its complexity $C_j$ using an exponential moving average with the 2D complexity map $C_i^{2D}$ at the projected pixel location [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    C_j \leftarrow (1 - \eta) C_j + \eta C_i^{2D}(\pi_i(\mu_j))
    $$
    Gaussians outside the frustum retain their previous $C_j$ [S1, unofficial]. Over training, $C_j$ converges to a multi-view average complexity [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Normalization:**
    Compute $\tilde{C}_j$ by min-max normalizing $C_j$ across the current set of Gaussians to the range $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Relocation Sampling (Densification):**
    When selecting a Gaussian $g_j$ as a candidate for relocation (splitting or duplication), sample with probability $p(g_j)$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    p(g_j) = \alpha_j \cdot \exp(\tilde{C}_j)
    $$
    To prevent collapse onto fixed complex regions, apply complexity dropout: with probability $\delta = 0.2$, set $\tilde{C}_j = 0$ before computing the exponential, effectively scoring that candidate by opacity alone [S1, unofficial]. The exponential term mimics the observed exponential growth of particle count with complexity [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Importance Scoring (Pruning/LoD):**
    For Level of Details control under a budget $\tau < 1$, rank Gaussians by importance score $S(g_j)$ to determine which survive pruning [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    S(g_j) = \alpha_j + \lambda \cdot \frac{s(g_j)}{\exp(\tilde{C}_j(\mu_j))}
    $$
    where $s(g_j)$ is the max-normalized scale of the Gaussian and $\lambda = 2$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   In high-complexity regions ($\tilde{C}_j \to 1$), $\exp(\tilde{C}_j)$ attenuates the scale term, reducing the score toward opacity $\alpha_j$, prioritizing precise, high-opacity primitives [S1, unofficial].
    *   In low-complexity regions ($\tilde{C}_j \to 0$), $\exp(\tilde{C}_j) \to 1$, restoring weight to the scale term $\lambda \cdot s(g_j)$, allowing large primitives to efficiently cover simple areas despite moderate opacity [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Variance Loss Minimization:**
    To ensure uniform degradation across LoDs, compute the residual $\Delta_i(u)$ between the full-capacity reconstruction error $E(I_i^{full})$ and the budgeted reconstruction error $E(I_i^{\tau})$ at pixel $u$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \Delta_i(u) = E(I_i^{full}) - E(I_i^{\tau})
    $$
    Minimize the spatial variance of this residual over the image domain $\Omega$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    L_{var} = \text{Var}_{u \in \Omega} \Delta_i(u)
    $$
    The total training loss combines the standard L1+D-SSIM loss on both full and budgeted renders with this variance term [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \text{Total Loss} = \text{Average}(\text{L1} + \text{D-SSIM}) + \lambda_{var} L_{var}
    $$

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np

def update_complexity(gaussians, view, eta):
    """
    Update complexity C_j for Gaussians visible in the current view.
    """
    # Project Gaussian means to 2D image coordinates
    projected_means = project_to_image(gaussians.means, view.camera_matrix)
    
    # Get 2D complexity map for the view (computed via delentropy)
    complexity_map = compute_delentropy_complexity(view.image)
    
    for j, g in enumerate(gaussians):
        if is_in_frustum(projected_means[j], view.bounds):
            # Sample complexity at projected pixel location
            c_2d = sample_complexity_map(complexity_map, projected_means[j])
            # Exponential moving average update
            g.complexity = (1 - eta) * g.complexity + eta * c_2d

def normalize_complexity(gaussians):
    """
    Min-max normalize complexity values to [0, 1].
    """
    c_values = np.array([g.complexity for g in gaussians])
    c_min, c_max = c_values.min(), c_values.max()
    if c_max == c_min:
        return np.zeros_like(c_values)
    normalized = (c_values - c_min) / (c_max - c_min)
    for j, g in enumerate(gaussians):
        g.normalized_complexity = normalized[j]

def sample_relocation_candidate(gaussians, dropout_prob=0.2):
    """
    Select a Gaussian for relocation based on opacity and complexity.
    """
    n = len(gaussians)
    weights = np.zeros(n)
    for j, g in enumerate(gaussians):
        c_tilde = g.normalized_complexity
        # Apply complexity dropout
        if np.random.rand() < dropout_prob:
            c_tilde = 0.0
        # Compute probability weight
        weights[j] = g.opacity * np.exp(c_tilde)
    
    # Normalize to probability distribution
    weights_sum = weights.sum()
    if weights_sum == 0:
        return np.random.randint(n)
    probs = weights / weights_sum
    return np.random.choice(n, p=probs)

def compute_importance_score(gaussians, lambda_weight=2.0):
    """
    Compute importance score for LoD pruning.
    """
    scores = np.zeros(len(gaussians))
    # Max-normalize scales for the batch
    scales = np.array([g.scale for g in gaussians])
    scale_max = scales.max()
    if scale_max == 0:
        scale_max = 1.0
    normalized_scales = scales / scale_max
    
    for j, g in enumerate(gaussians):
        c_tilde = g.normalized_complexity
        # Formula: alpha + lambda * scale / exp(C)
        scores[j] = g.opacity + lambda_weight * (normalized_scales[j] / np.exp(c_tilde))
    return scores

def compute_variance_loss(full_render, budgeted_render, tau):
    """
    Compute spatial variance of reconstruction error residuals.
    """
    # Compute errors (e.g., L1 difference from ground truth)
    # Note: E(I) is the error metric, not the image itself. 
    # Assuming ground truth is available or using full render as reference if GT unavailable.
    # Standard implementation uses GT. Here we assume GT is available for error calculation.
    # However, the source defines Delta as difference between full and budgeted error.
    # Let's assume E(I) is L1 error against GT.
    gt = get_ground_truth()
    error_full = np.abs(full_render - gt)
    error_budgeted = np.abs(budgeted_render - gt)
    
    # Residual
    residual = error_full - error_budgeted
    
    # Spatial variance
    var_loss = np.var(residual)
    return var_loss
```

### Parameters
*   $\eta$: EMA learning rate for complexity update, $\eta \in (0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Scalar weight for the scale term in importance score, fixed at $\lambda = 2$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\delta$: Complexity dropout probability, fixed at $\delta = 0.2$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\tau$: Budget factor for LoD, $\tau < 1$ for reduced capacity, $\tau = 1$ for full capacity [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{var}$: Weight for the variance loss term [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Frustum Culling:** Only Gaussians projecting within the current view's frustum should have their complexity updated; others retain their previous value [S1, unofficial].
*   **Complexity Collapse:** Without dropout ($\delta=0$), Gaussians may collapse onto fixed complex regions; dropout ensures opacity-only scoring for a fraction of candidates [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scale Normalization:** The scale $s(g_j)$ must be max-normalized across the current set of Gaussians to ensure consistent scoring [S1, unofficial].
*   **LoD Variance:** The scoring alone does not guarantee uniform degradation; the variance loss $L_{var}$ is required to enforce it [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for computing the 2D complexity map (delentropy) and updating per-Gaussian complexity fields. The relocation and scoring steps are $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing a scalar complexity value $C_j$ per Gaussian [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Outperforms opacity-only baselines (e.g., standard 3DGS) and other LoD-aware methods (e.g., OctreeGS, FlexGS) at lower capacities (10%, 1%, 0.1%) while maintaining comparable quality at full capacity (100%) [S1, unofficial]. For example, on the Tanks&Temples dataset at 1% capacity, it achieves higher PSNR (17.81) and SSIM (0.720) than LightGaussian (15.26, 0.675) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently allocates Gaussian budget by preserving large primitives in simple regions and focusing detail in complex areas. Provides smooth Level of Details transitions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires computing 2D complexity maps, which adds computational cost. Performance may vary across LoD targets, requiring careful tuning of $\tau$ [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control was introduced in the paper "KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian Splatting" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses limitations in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting training where opacity acts as a proxy for error, leading to under-served complex regions. The method builds on the principle that complexity is a surrogate for reconstruction error, as shown in pilot studies [S1, unofficial]. It is used in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization to improve quality at reduced capacities and enable continuous Level of Details [S1, unofficial].
