# SLAM and Tracking

Also called: Gaussian SLAM, 3DGS-SLAM, Online Gaussian Optimization

## Overview
SLAM and Tracking in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the simultaneous estimation of camera poses and the incremental construction/refinement of a Gaussian map from a stream of images [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of real-time, online reconstruction where the scene model must be updated dynamically as new views arrive, unlike offline batch optimization. It differs from offline methods by maintaining a sliding window of keyframes and using visibility-based metrics for efficient joint optimization, and from NeRF-based SLAM by leveraging the explicit, rasterizable nature of Gaussians for faster tracking and editing [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family relies on the explicit representation of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$, where each Gaussian $g$ is defined by position $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$ (or scale/rotation), opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics coefficients for color. The core mechanism involves:
1.  **Visibility Determination:** A Gaussian is considered visible from a camera view if it contributes to the rasterization process and the accumulated alpha $\alpha$ along the ray has not reached 0.5 [S1, unofficial]. This leverages the inherent depth sorting of 3DGS rasterization to handle occlusions without additional heuristics [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Covisibility Metric:** The overlap between views is quantified by the Intersection over Union (IoU) of visible Gaussians:
    $$ \text{Covisibility}(i, j) = \frac{|V_i \cap V_j|}{|V_i \cup V_j|} $$
    where $V_i$ is the set of visible Gaussians for frame $i$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Keyframe Window Management:** Optimization is restricted to a small sliding window $W_k$ of camera poses and corresponding Gaussian states to ensure computational feasibility while maintaining multiview constraints [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Joint Optimization:** Camera poses and Gaussian parameters are optimized jointly, often using dense bundle adjustment, within the keyframe window [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Keyframe Management](10081-keyframe-management.md)
Keyframe management maintains a small sliding window $W_k$ of camera poses and corresponding Gaussian representations to enable efficient joint optimization in online Gaussian SLAM systems. By limiting optimization to a subset of non-redundant frames, it avoids the computational infeasibility of processing all historical data while ensuring sufficient multiview constraints for accurate pose estimation and map refinement. It differs from fixed-interval keyframing by using covisibility metrics to select frames that span wide baselines and observe distinct scene areas. See [Keyframe Management](10081-keyframe-management.md) for algorithmic details.

### WildGS-SLAM
A robust monocular RGB 3DGS-SLAM for dynamic in-the-wild scenes [S1, unofficial]. It uses a shallow MLP over DINOv2 features to predict an uncertainty map that guides dynamic-object removal during tracking and mapping, feeding uncertainty-aware dense bundle adjustment and Gaussian-map optimization [S1, unofficial]. It reconstructs a clean static Gaussian map while ignoring moving distractors [S1, unofficial]. Marked as a 2026 SOTA update [S1, unofficial].

### GPS-SLAM
An RGB-D SLAM method that represents a scene with a truncated SDF volume and a thin layer of 3D Gaussians near the surface for high-frequency appearance residuals [S2]. At render time, it raycasts the SDF for depth plus a base color, then splats only the surface-near Gaussians with SDF-guided depth culling and order-independent blending, and mixes the two images [S2]. This avoids expensive NeRF-style volumetric sampling and removes the Gaussian depth sorting step, achieving real-time to high-frame-rate operation while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality relative to Gaussian-only RGB-D SLAM baselines [S2].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 as a fast, explicit alternative to NeRFs [S1, unofficial]. SLAM systems leveraging this representation emerged shortly after, aiming to combine the rendering speed of 3DGS with the online capabilities of visual SLAM. Early works like [S1, unofficial] highlighted the ability to use the same map for photorealistic rendering, ICP-style tracking, and local editing, addressing limitations of NeRF-SLAM methods like NICE-SLAM and Co-SLAM which struggled with local editing [S1, unofficial]. Recent developments include handling dynamic scenes (WildGS-SLAM) and hybrid geometric representations (GPS-SLAM) [S1, unofficial][S2].
