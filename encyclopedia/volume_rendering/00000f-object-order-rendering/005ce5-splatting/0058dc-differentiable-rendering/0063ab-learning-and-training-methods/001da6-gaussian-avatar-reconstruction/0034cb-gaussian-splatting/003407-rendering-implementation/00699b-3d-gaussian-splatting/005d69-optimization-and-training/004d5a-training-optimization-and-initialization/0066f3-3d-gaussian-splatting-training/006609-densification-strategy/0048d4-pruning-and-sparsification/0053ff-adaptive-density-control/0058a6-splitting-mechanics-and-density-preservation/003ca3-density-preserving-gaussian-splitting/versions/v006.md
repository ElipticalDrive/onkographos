# Density-preserving Gaussian splitting

Also called: BOGausS splitting, density-preserving split

## Overview
Density-preserving Gaussian splitting is a refinement of the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting densification strategy that modifies the parameters of child Gaussians to match the integrated density of the parent Gaussian [S1]. It solves the problem of loss function perturbations and Gaussian cluster formation caused by naive splitting or cloning operations [S1]. Unlike standard 3DGS which resets opacity and uses cloning, this method discards cloning entirely and relies solely on splitting with an adaptation function that accounts for opacity variations [S1].

## How it works
The method replaces one parent Gaussian $g_{mother}$ with two child Gaussians $g_{child,1}$ and $g_{child,2}$. The process ensures the sum of the children's densities approximates the parent's density, preventing artifacts during optimization [S1].

**Inputs:**
*   Parent Gaussian $g_{mother}$ with mean $\mu_{mother}$, scale $\sigma_{mother}$ (vector of 3 principal axes), opacity $o_{mother}$, rotation $R_{mother}$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $C_{mother}$.
*   Splitting constant $\alpha$ (typically 0.3) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Two child Gaussians $g_{child,1}$ and $g_{child,2}$ with updated means, scales, opacities, and inherited rotation/SH coefficients.

**Data Structures:**
*   Gaussian parameters are stored as:
    *   $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
    *   $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale along principal axes (positive values).
    *   $o \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
    *   $R \in SO(3)$: Rotation matrix (or quaternion).
    *   $C$: Spherical Harmonic coefficients.

**Steps:**

1.  **Identify Splitting Axis:**
    Find the index $k$ corresponding to the largest scale value in $\sigma_{mother}$:
    $$ k = \arg\max_i (\sigma_{mother}[i]) $$
    Let $\sigma_{max} = \sigma_{mother}[k]$ be the scale along this axis.

2.  **Determine Direction Vector:**
    Let $\mathbf{u}$ be the unit vector corresponding to the $k$-th principal axis in the parent's local coordinate system. In the global coordinate system, this direction is given by the $k$-th column of the rotation matrix $R_{mother}$:
    $$ \mathbf{u}_{global} = R_{mother}[:, k] $$

3.  **Compute Mean Shift:**
    Calculate the shift magnitude $\Delta$:
    $$ \Delta = \alpha \cdot \sigma_{max} $$
    Compute the new means for the two children:
    $$ \mu_{child,1} = \mu_{mother} + \Delta \cdot \mathbf{u}_{global} $$
    $$ \mu_{child,2} = \mu_{mother} - \Delta \cdot \mathbf{u}_{global} $$
    The means along the other two axes remain unchanged relative to the parent's center.

4.  **Adapt Scales and Opacity:**
    Compute the new scales $\sigma_{child}$ and opacity $o_{child}$ for both children using the adaptation function:
    $$ (\sigma_{child}, o_{child}) = \text{SplitAdaptation}(\sigma_{mother}, o_{mother}) $$
    This function adjusts the sizes and opacity such that the resulting density of the sum of the two children is close to the density of the mother Gaussian [S1]. The adaptation explicitly takes into account opacity variations, unlike some prior methods [S1].

5.  **Inherit Other Parameters:**
    Copy the rotation matrix and spherical harmonic coefficients from the parent to both children:
    $$ R_{child,1} = R_{child,2} = R_{mother} $$
    $$ C_{child,1} = C_{child,2} = C_{mother} $$

**Parameters:**
*   $\alpha$: Constant controlling the separation distance. Typical value: 0.3 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Loss Perturbations:** Naive splitting causes perturbations in the loss function. This method mitigates this by ensuring density preservation [S1].
*   **Cluster Formation:** The method prevents the formation of Gaussian clusters, which is a known issue in original splitting operations [S1].
*   **Cloning Discard:** This method discards cloning operations entirely to limit perturbations and prevent clusters [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Similar to standard splitting, $O(1)$ per split operation, plus the cost of the `SplitAdaptation` function.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory per split.
*   **Strengths:** Prevents loss perturbations and Gaussian cluster formation [S1]. Accounts for opacity variations during splitting, unlike some prior methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires an adaptation function (potentially learned offline) which may not be available in all implementations [S1]. Discards cloning, which might reduce flexibility in some scenarios [S1].
*   **When to use:** When using BOGausS or similar variants that prioritize stability and density preservation over the original 3GS densification strategy [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When using standard 3GS with opacity reset and cloning [S1].

## Background
Density-preserving Gaussian splitting was proposed in the BOGausS paper [S1]. It addresses issues identified in earlier works [5, 9] regarding loss perturbations during splitting [S1]. The method is inspired by [16] and differs from [17] by accounting for opacity variations [S1]. It replaces the opacity reset mechanism used in standard 3GS with a pruning strategy based on opacity thresholds [S1].
