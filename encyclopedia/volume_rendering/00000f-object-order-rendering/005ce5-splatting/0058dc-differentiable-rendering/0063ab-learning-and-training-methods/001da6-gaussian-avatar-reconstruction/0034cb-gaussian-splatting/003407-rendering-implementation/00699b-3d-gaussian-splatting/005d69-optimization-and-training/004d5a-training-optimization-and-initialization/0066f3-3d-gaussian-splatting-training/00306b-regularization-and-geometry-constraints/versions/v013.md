# Regularization and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevent overfitting, reduce floaters, and enforce geometric plausibility by modifying the optimization trajectory or loss landscape [S1, S2]. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md) which relies on photometric consistency alone, these methods introduce explicit priors—such as frequency domain alignment, depth consistency, or shape flattening—to stabilize convergence and improve surface coherence [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of constraining the 3D Gaussian parameters ($\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, c$) beyond the standard photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Decoupling Geometry and Appearance:** Separating the optimization of shape/position from color to prevent geometric priors from blurring high-frequency details [S1].
2.  **Multi-Scale or Multi-Frequency Supervision:** Using low-frequency signals for coarse alignment and high-frequency signals for detail refinement, often via annealing or filtering [S2].
3.  **Structural Priors:** Enforcing physical constraints such as planarity (flattening), normal alignment, or signed distance field (SDF) consistency to eliminate "dust" and floaters [S1, S2].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum (amplitude and phase) of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and guide adaptive densification. It sets itself apart by operating in the Fourier domain to increase gradients in over-reconstructed regions, unlike pixel-wise L1 loss.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Stabilizes joint optimization of Gaussian parameters and camera poses by blurring 2D training images with a scheduled set of 2D Gaussian kernels. It solves the problem of noisy, high-frequency gradient signals that destabilize camera alignment, distinct from Frequency Regularization which modifies the loss weights or encoding.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
Modifies the covariance structure to enforce half-ellipsoid geometry, aligning splats with surface normals to model sharp transitions. It sets itself apart by directly transforming the covariance matrix into a local basis defined by the surface normal, rather than relying solely on auxiliary loss terms.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Prevents the degradation of high-frequency color details caused by enforcing smooth monocular depth priors by freezing shape parameters and decoupling center/opacity optimization into two passes. It sets itself apart by explicitly freezing scale and rotation, whereas other methods modify the covariance directly.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
A self-adaptive method using reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It sets itself apart by avoiding scale mismatches inherent in single-scale depth priors, leveraging low-frequency components for coarse geometry and high-resolution voxels for detail.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Improves geometric plausibility by constraining the shape and orientation of Gaussian kernels (flattening and normal alignment). It sets itself apart from depth supervision by enforcing structural priors in the spatial domain to reduce floaters and improve surface coherence.

### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.
- [S2] Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.
- [S2] $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

### FreGS
- [S4, unofficial] Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training.

## Background
The need for regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the tendency of the optimization to produce unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces, leading to floaters and artifacts [S1, S3]. Early works like [S2] (FreGS) and [S1] (Geometry Regularization) introduced frequency-domain and spatial-domain constraints, respectively, to stabilize training. Subsequent methods like [S1] (Hard and Soft Depth) and [S1] (Cross-scale geometric adaptation) addressed the conflict between geometric priors and photometric detail, while [S1] (Wild-GS) and [S3] (SDF-Regularized) integrated advanced priors for robustness against transients and improved surface fidelity.
