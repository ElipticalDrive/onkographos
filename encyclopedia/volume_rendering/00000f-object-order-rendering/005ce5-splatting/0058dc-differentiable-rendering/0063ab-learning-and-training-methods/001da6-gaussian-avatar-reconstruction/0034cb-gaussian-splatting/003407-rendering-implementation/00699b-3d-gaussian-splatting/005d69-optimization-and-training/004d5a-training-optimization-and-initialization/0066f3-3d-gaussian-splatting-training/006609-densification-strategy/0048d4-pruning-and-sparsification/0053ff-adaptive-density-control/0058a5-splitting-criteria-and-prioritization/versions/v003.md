# Splitting Criteria and Prioritization

Also called: Splitting Criteria, Splitting Prioritization

## Overview
Splitting Criteria and Prioritization determines which 3D Gaussian primitives are selected for densification (splitting or cloning) during the training of Gaussian Splatting. It solves the problem of inefficient primitive allocation by identifying regions with high reconstruction error or complexity, ensuring that new primitives are spawned where they provide the maximum quality gain. It sets itself apart from its siblings by defining the specific metric used to rank Gaussians for splitting, whereas [Splitting Mechanics and Density Preservation](16227-splitting-mechanics-and-density-preservation.md) defines the geometric transformation applied to the selected primitives.

## Shared principles
The family operates on the principle that densification should be driven by a scalar metric $M(G)$ computed for each Gaussian $G$. This metric aggregates information from multiple training views to estimate the local error or complexity. The selection process involves:
1.  **Metric Computation:** Calculating $M(G)$ for all active Gaussians.
2.  **Thresholding/Sorting:** Comparing $M(G)$ against a threshold or sorting Gaussians by $M(G)$ descending.
3.  **Selection:** Choosing the top-$k$ Gaussians or those exceeding the threshold for splitting.
4.  **Density Preservation:** The actual splitting operation (creating two children from one parent) is handled by [Splitting Mechanics and Density Preservation](16227-splitting-mechanics-and-density-preservation.md), ensuring the total density and opacity are preserved or appropriately scaled.

## Subtopics

### [Multi-View Density Control Criterion](5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md)
A densification strategy for multi-view training that resolves the invalid vector arithmetic of summing 2D positional gradients from different image planes. It replaces vector addition with the summation of squared norms of gradients, ensuring geometric consistency. It is chosen when training with multiple viewpoints to prevent gradient cancellation errors.

### [Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control](16126-complexity-aware-adaptive-density-control.md)
A strategy that replaces opacity-only heuristics with a composite metric combining opacity, spatial scale, and a learned complexity field. It biases densification toward high-complexity regions while preserving large primitives in simple areas to maintain uniform Level-of-Detail (LoD) degradation. It is chosen when uniform quality degradation under a fixed primitive budget is required.

### Distortion-based Gaussian Splitting Prioritization
A criterion for prioritizing which Gaussians to split, based on rate-distortion optimization to maximize PSNR gain [S1]. It associates a reconstruction Squared Error $SE_{c,i}$ to each Gaussian $i$ in camera view $c$, defined as:
$$ SE_{c,i} = \sum_{p \in I_c} w_i^{(c)} (R^{(c)}(p) - I^{(c)}(p))^2 $$
where $I^{(c)}$ is the ground-truth image, $R^{(c)}(p)$ is the rendered value at pixel $p$, and $w_i^{(c)}$ is the contribution weight of Gaussian $i$ to pixel $p$ [S1]. Gaussians with the highest $SE_{c,i}$ are prioritized for splitting to reduce local reconstruction error [S1]. This approach is inspired by rate-distortion optimization and aims to maximize PSNR gain by densifying areas with high residual error [S1].

## Background
The concept of adaptive density control in Gaussian Splatting originated with the foundational work on 3D Gaussian Splatting [G]. The standard approach uses 2D positional gradients from a single view to determine splitting. As multi-view training and complexity-aware optimizations emerged, variants like the Multi-View Density Control Criterion and Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control were developed to address specific limitations in gradient aggregation and primitive allocation efficiency. The distortion-based prioritization method draws from rate-distortion theory to optimize for perceptual quality metrics like PSNR [S1].
