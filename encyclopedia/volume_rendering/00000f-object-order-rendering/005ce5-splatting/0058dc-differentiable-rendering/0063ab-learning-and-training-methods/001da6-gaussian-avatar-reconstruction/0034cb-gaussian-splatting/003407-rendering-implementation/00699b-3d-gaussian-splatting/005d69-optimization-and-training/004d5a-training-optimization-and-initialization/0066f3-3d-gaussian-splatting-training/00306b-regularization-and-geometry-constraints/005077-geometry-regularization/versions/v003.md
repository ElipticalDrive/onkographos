# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the structural integrity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian splats by constraining their shape and orientation, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains position [S1, S2, unofficial]. It addresses the issue of "floaters" and noisy geometry by enforcing planarity and normal alignment [S1, S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating directly on the Gaussian covariance parameters rather than frequency-domain signals, and from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by focusing on surface orientation and flattening rather than just depth value consistency.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian scaling factors $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, per-Gaussian normals $n_g$, rendered depth map $D$, and rendered normal map $n_r$.
*   **Outputs:** Regularized gradients for Gaussian parameters (specifically scaling and orientation).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Parameters:** Each Gaussian $i$ has a scaling vector $s_i = [s_{i,x}, s_{i,y}, s_{i,z}]^T$ representing the extent along its local axes.
*   **Normals:** Per-Gaussian normal $n_{g,i}$ derived from the rotation matrix of the Gaussian covariance. Rendered normals $n_r$ derived from the gradient of the rendered depth map.

**Steps:**
1.  **Enable Geometry Regularization:** The method is controlled by a boolean flag `use_normal_loss` in the optimization parameters [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Depth Normal Consistency:**
    *   Compute the surface normal $n_r$ from the rendered depth map $D$ at each pixel [S1, unofficial].
    *   Retrieve the per-Gaussian normal $n_g$ for the Gaussian contributing to that pixel [S1, unofficial].
    *   Enforce alignment between $n_r$ and $n_g$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Flattening Surface:**
    *   Identify the smallest component of the scaling factor $s$ for each Gaussian [S2, unofficial].
    *   Minimize this smallest component towards zero during optimization [S2, unofficial]. This flattens the Gaussian into a surfel aligned with the surface [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Flattening Loss:** Let $s_{min} = \min(s_x, s_y, s_z)$. The loss encourages $s_{min} \to 0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Consistency:** Align rendered normal $n_r$ with Gaussian normal $n_g$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean. Enables the geometry regularization pipeline [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Refitting:** Depth maps must be refitted against a prior point cloud to the same scale before use. Depth maps that fail refitting are discarded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sky/Background:** Geometry regularization may pull geometry onto itself in open environments. Using a hemisphere instead of a full sphere for sky domes can mitigate this [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A technique to flatten Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels aligned with the surface by minimizing the smallest component of the scaling factor towards zero [S2, unofficial]. It aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when explicit surfel representation is desired to improve surface definition [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for normal computation and scaling factor analysis per Gaussian [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing rendered depth and normal maps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces floaters, improves geometry smoothness, and enforces planarity [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May compromise the number of remaining points if not balanced with pruning [S2, unofficial]. Requires careful handling of depth map scaling [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality surface geometry is required and floaters are prevalent [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are extremely limited or when depth maps are unreliable [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated from techniques to improve Gaussian Splatting geometry, specifically referenced in Julia-based implementations [S1, unofficial] and modular frameworks like GauStudio [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the idea that constraining shape (flattening) and orientation (normal consistency) provides better geometric priors than depth alone [S1, S2, unofficial].
