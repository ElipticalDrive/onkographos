# BlitzGS

Also called: BlitzGS: City-Scale Gaussian Splatting at Lightning Speed

## Overview
BlitzGS is a distributed 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework designed to accelerate city-scale training by reducing the active Gaussian workload at three coupled levels: system-level sharding, model-level population control, and view-level filtering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike spatial block partitioning which suffers from cross-block visibility waste and imbalanced shards, BlitzGS shards Gaussians by index parity and uses a single all-to-all communication step to distribute rendering tiles, ensuring balanced loads without a final merge step [S1, unofficial]. It achieves nearly an order-of-magnitude speedup over baselines like Momen.GS and CityGS-X while maintaining high reconstruction quality [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of calibrated images with known camera poses and intrinsics; a global set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ initialized from SfM points.
**Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) and rendered novel views.
**Hardware:** Demonstrated on 4x NVIDIA A6000 GPUs [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Global Gaussian Set $\mathcal{G}$:** The complete set of $N$ Gaussians.
*   **Local Shard $\mathcal{G}^{(m)}$:** The subset of Gaussians assigned to GPU $m$, where $m \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$.
*   **Optimizer State:** Maintained locally for each Gaussian in $\mathcal{G}^{(m)}$.
*   **Tile Assignment:** A mapping from image tiles to GPU owners, determined by the all-to-all exchange.

**Method Steps:**

1.  **System-Level Sharding (Index Parity):**
    The global set $\mathcal{G}$ is partitioned across $M$ GPUs based on the parity of the Gaussian index $i$. GPU $m$ stores Gaussians where $i \pmod M = m$ (or similar parity scheme ensuring even/odd distribution) [S1, unofficial]. This avoids the spatial locality issues of block partitioning and ensures each GPU holds approximately $|\mathcal{G}|/M$ Gaussians and their optimizer states [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Local Projection:**
    Each GPU $m$ projects only its local shard $\mathcal{G}^{(m)}$ into the camera space of the current training view [S1, unofficial]. This generates screen-space bounding boxes and depth information for the local Gaussians.

3.  **Communication (All-to-All):**
    A single cross-GPU all-to-all exchange routes each projected Gaussian to the GPU responsible for the image tiles it covers [S1, unofficial]. This ensures that every GPU receives the subset of Gaussians necessary to composite its assigned tiles, balancing the workload dynamically based on visibility [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Model-Level Population Control:**
    *   **Importance Scoring:** A scoring pass computes an importance signal for each Gaussian relative to the current view [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Pruning:** Gaussians with low importance are pruned to reduce redundancy [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Spawn Gating:** A lightweight gate predicts whether a candidate Gaussian (from densification) will survive; candidates predicted not to survive are withheld before spawning, limiting peak population [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Gradient Reweighting:** The importance signal is used to reweight the density control gradients during optimization [S1, unofficial].

5.  **View-Level Filtering:**
    *   **Distance-based LOD Gate:** Gaussians that are too fine-grained for the current camera distance are dropped [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Importance Mask:** Gaussians with negligible cross-view contribution are masked out, skipping them during rasterization [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Rasterization and Update:**
    Each GPU composites the pixels for its assigned tiles using the received Gaussians. Gradients are backpropagated and used to update the parameters of the local shard $\mathcal{G}^{(m)}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

    # Partition G into M shards based on index parity

    # Initialize optimizer states for each shard
    optimizers = {m: Optimizer(shards[m]) for m in range(M)}
    
    for step in range(num_steps):
        # 2. Local Projection
        projected_gaussians = {}
        for m in range(M):
            # Project local shard to camera space
            projected_gaussians[m] = project(shards[m], cameras[step])
        
        # 3. Communication (All-to-All)
        # Determine tile ownership and route Gaussians
        # This step is abstracted as a single exchange
        received_gaussians = all_to_all_exchange(projected_gaussians)
        
        # 4. Model-Level Population Control
        # Compute importance scores
        importance_scores = compute_importance(received_gaussians, cameras[step])
        
        # Prune redundant Gaussians
        shards = prune(shards, importance_scores)
        
        # Spawn gating (during densification phase)
        if densification_active:
            candidates = generate_candidates(shards)
            survival_predictions = predict_survival(candidates)
            valid_candidates = [c for c, pred in zip(candidates, survival_predictions) if pred]
            shards = spawn(shards, valid_candidates)
        
        # 5. View-Level Filtering
        # Apply LOD and importance masks
        filtered_gaussians = {}
        for m in range(M):
            # Distance-based LOD gate
            lod_mask = apply_lod_gate(received_gaussians[m], cameras[step])
            # Importance-based mask
            imp_mask = apply_importance_mask(received_gaussians[m], importance_scores[m])
            filtered_gaussians[m] = received_gaussians[m][lod_mask & imp_mask]
        
        # 6. Rasterization and Update
        for m in range(M):
            # Composite pixels for assigned tiles
            loss = rasterize_and_compute_loss(filtered_gaussians[m], cameras[step])
            
            # Reweight gradients using importance signal
            gradients = reweight_gradients(loss.grad, importance_scores[m])
            
            # Update local shard
            optimizers[m].step(shards[m], gradients)

**Parameters:**
*   **Number of GPUs ($M$):** 4 (as used in benchmarks) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sharding Strategy:** Index parity (even/odd indices) [S1, unofficial].
*   **LOD Threshold:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Importance Threshold:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Imbalanced Shards:** Index parity sharding is chosen specifically to avoid the imbalanced shards common in spatial block partitioning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Cross-Block Redundancy:** By sharding by index rather than space, cross-block visibility waste is eliminated [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Not given in the sources.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is significantly reduced compared to baselines. On the Residence scene, BlitzGS takes 38 minutes (PSNR 24.66 dB) compared to Momen.GS (5h 03min, PSNR 31.81 dB) and CityGS-X (4h 45min, PSNR 20.12 dB) [S1, unofficial]. On the Rubble scene, it takes 39 minutes (PSNR 33.79 dB) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Each GPU stores only $|\mathcal{G}|/M$ Gaussians and their optimizer states, scaling linearly with the number of GPUs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Both storage and per-step compute scale with the GPU count due to the distributed projection and rasterization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Near-order-of-magnitude speedup on city-scale scenes; high quality comparable to baselines; no final merge step required; balanced workload via all-to-all exchange.
*   **Limitations:** Requires efficient all-to-all communication; relies on accurate importance scoring and survival prediction.
*   **When to use:** Large-scale, city-scale 3DGS training where speed is critical and high-quality reconstruction is required.
*   **When not to use:** Small-scale scenes where the overhead of distributed communication may outweigh benefits; hardware with limited inter-GPU bandwidth.

## Background
BlitzGS was introduced by Zhongtao Wang, Huishan Au, Yisong Chen, Meng Gai, Yilong Li, Fei Zhu, Mai Su, Haojie Jin, and Guoping Wang in the paper "BlitzGS: City-Scale Gaussian Splatting at Lightning Speed" (arXiv:2605.13794, 2026) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the high computational cost of city-scale 3D reconstruction, which often involves thousands of images and results in massive Gaussian sets that exceed single-GPU memory and require extensive preprocessing, communication, and rasterization [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on insights from prior work on distributed 3DGS [S1, unofficial].
