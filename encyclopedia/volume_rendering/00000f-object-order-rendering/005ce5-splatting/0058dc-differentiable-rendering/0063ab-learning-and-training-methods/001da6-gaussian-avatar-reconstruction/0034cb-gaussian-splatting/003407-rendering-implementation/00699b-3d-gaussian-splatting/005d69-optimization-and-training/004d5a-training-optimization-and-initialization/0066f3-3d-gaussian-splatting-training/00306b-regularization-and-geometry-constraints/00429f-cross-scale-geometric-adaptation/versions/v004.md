# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth or complex scheduling [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on pre-trained feature extractors or fixed depth priors, this approach adapts to diverse scenes by leveraging low-frequency components from low-resolution voxels for coarse geometry early in training, while medium- and high-resolution voxels refine details as training advances [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses (rotation $R_i, R_j$ and translation $t_i, t_j$), multi-scale voxel representations (low, mid, high resolution), and RGB maps $C_i, C_j$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for each training ray $r_{train}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxels:** A set of voxel grids at different resolutions (low, mid, high). Each scale $l$ provides a depth value $D_{il}(p_i)$ for a pixel $p_i$ via volume rendering [S1].
*   **Ray Representation:** A training ray $r_{train}$ is defined by its origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$ in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling:** For each training ray $r_{train}$ sampled from frame $i$, identify the 2D pixel coordinate $p_i$ [S1].
2.  **Homogeneous Augmentation:** Compute the homogeneous coordinate $\tilde{p}_i$ from $p_i$ [S1].
3.  **3D Point in Camera Coordinates (Frame $i$):** Calculate the 3D point $x_{li}$ in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$ at scale $l$ using the depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ obtained from volume rendering and camera intrinsics $K_i$:
    $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    Alternatively, this point can be determined directly from the ray:
    $$ x_{li} = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
    [S1]
4.  **Transformation to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]
5.  **Transformation to Camera Coordinates (Frame $j$):** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]
6.  **Reprojection to 2D:** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
7.  **Reprojection Error Calculation:** Compute the reprojection error $e_l(p_i)$ for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel $C_i(p_i)$ and the warped pixel $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]
8.  **Pseudo-Ground Truth Selection:** Select the scale $l$ with the minimum reprojection error as the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]
9.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth $D_l(r_{train})$ at the selected scale and the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ over all training rays $R_{train}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    Note: The source formula (19) sums over scales $l$ and rays $r_{train}$, but the text implies selecting the best scale per ray. The implementation should sum the squared difference between the depth at the *selected* best scale and the pseudo-GT depth for each ray, or sum over all scales if the pseudo-GT is defined per scale. Based on Eq 19, it appears to be a sum over scales of the difference between the rendered depth at scale $l$ and the *best* pseudo-GT depth. However, standard practice suggests comparing the selected scale's depth to the pseudo-GT. The source states: "the pseudo-GT depth of the scale with the minimum reprojection error is obtained... utilized to compute the geometric adaptation loss (MSE)". Eq 19 shows a sum over $l$. This implies the loss penalizes all scales, but the pseudo-GT is fixed per ray. Let's stick to the explicit formula provided:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo} = \sum_{r_{train} \in R_{train}} \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Voxel Scales:** Typically 3 scales (low, mid, high) [S1].
*   **Reference Frames:** Not explicitly defined in the notes, but requires at least one other view $j$ for reprojection [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusions:** If frame $j$ does not see the surface point visible in frame $i$, the reprojection error will be large, potentially leading to incorrect pseudo-GT selection. The method relies on multi-view consistency [S1].
*   **Depth Ambiguity:** In textureless regions, depth estimation may be unstable, affecting the reprojection error [S1].
*   **Boundary Checks:** Reprojected coordinates $\tilde{p}_{li \to j}$ may fall outside the image bounds of frame $j$, requiring validation [S1].
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision in matrix operations and reprojection can introduce small errors [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for multi-scale volume rendering and reprojection calculations for each training step. The source notes that FrugalNeRF achieves state-of-the-art results with shorter training times compared to other few-shot methods, suggesting the regularization is efficient [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel grids and RGB maps for reference frames [S1].
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Provides a stronger and more robust constraint than the depth prior ($\mathcal{L}_d$), achieving lower final depth error [S1]. Combining $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ with $\mathcal{L}_d$ yields the best results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no complex scheduling, no explicit depth ground truth required, robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warping methods like GeCoNeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses. May face challenges in generalization with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views (e.g., 360° datasets) [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in **FrugalNeRF** by [S1] (CVPR 2025). It is designed for extreme few-shot novel view synthesis, accelerating and regularizing training by using weight-sharing voxels across scales and geometric adaptation via reprojection errors. It addresses the issue of floaters and sub-optimal convergence in few-shot scenarios by providing a self-adaptive supervisory signal for geometry [S1].
