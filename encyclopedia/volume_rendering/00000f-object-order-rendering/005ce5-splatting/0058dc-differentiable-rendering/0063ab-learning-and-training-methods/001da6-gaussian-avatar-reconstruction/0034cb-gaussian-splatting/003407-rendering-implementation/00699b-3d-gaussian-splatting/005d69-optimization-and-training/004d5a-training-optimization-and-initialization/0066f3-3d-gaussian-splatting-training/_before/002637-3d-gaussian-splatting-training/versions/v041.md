# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3DGS Learning, G Splats

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Training is the iterative optimization process that adjusts the parameters of a set of 3D Gaussian primitives to minimize the difference between rendered images and ground truth views [S2][S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of reconstructing high-fidelity 3D scenes from 2D images by replacing implicit neural fields with explicit, differentiable geometric primitives [S5, unofficial]. Unlike NeRFs, which require iterative per-pixel ray marching and neural network inference, 3DGS training optimizes a fixed set of Gaussian attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity, and color) using standard gradient descent, enabling significantly faster training and real-time rendering [S2][S5, unofficial]. The method distinguishes itself through its use of adaptive density control, which dynamically splits, clones, and prunes Gaussians during optimization to balance reconstruction fidelity and computational cost [S2].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images (or video frames) with known camera poses (rotation $R_{cw}$, translation $t_{cw}$, and intrinsic matrix $K$) [S6, unofficial]. Initial 3D Gaussian parameters are derived from Structure from Motion (SfM) point clouds (e.g., from COLMAP) [S2][S6, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained set of 3D Gaussians defined by position $\mathbf{p}_i$, rotation $q_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $h_i$, typically exported as a PLY file [S4, S6, unofficial].

### Data Structures
Each Gaussian primitive $i$ is stored with the following fields:
*   **Position:** $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (3D center) [S6, unofficial].
*   **Rotation:** $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion, $q_r, q_i, q_j, q_k$) [S6, unofficial].
*   **Scale:** $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (standard deviations for x, y, z axes) [S6, unofficial].
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$ [S6, unofficial].
*   **Color:** Spherical Harmonic coefficients $h_i$ (degree $D$, typically $D=3$) [S4, S6, unofficial].
*   **Covariance:** $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, computed from rotation and scale [S6, unofficial].

### Initialization
1.  **Point Cloud Generation:** Use SfM to generate a sparse 3D point cloud from input images [S5, S6, unofficial].
2.  **Position:** Initialize $\mathbf{p}_i$ from the SfM points [S6, unofficial].
3.  **Rotation:** Initialize $q_i$ as a unit quaternion (e.g., identity or random) [S2][S6, unofficial].
4.  **Scale:** Initialize $\mathbf{s}_i$ with values between 0 and 1 [S2][S6, unofficial].
5.  **Opacity:** Initialize $\alpha_i$ to a constant value (e.g., 0.1 or 0.5) [S6, unofficial].
6.  **Color:** Initialize $h_i$ from the average color of the SfM points or zero [S6, unofficial].

### Rendering Forward Pass
For each training step, a camera view $V$ is selected, and the scene is rendered to produce image $\gamma$:
1.  **Projection:** Project each 3D Gaussian to 2D screen space. The 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ is transformed into the camera coordinate system and then projected to 2D, resulting in a 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'_i$ [S6, unofficial].
2.  **Sorting:** Sort Gaussians by depth relative to the camera [S2].
3.  **Alpha Blending:** For each pixel $j$, compute the color $\gamma_j$ by alpha-compositing the projected Gaussians $i$ sorted by depth:
    $$ \gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha'_{ij} c_i \tau_{ij} $$
    where $\tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_{kj})$ is the accumulated transmittance, $c_i$ is the color of Gaussian $i$, and $\alpha'_{ij}$ is the effective opacity of Gaussian $i$ at pixel $j$ [S6, unofficial].
4.  **Effective Opacity:** The effective opacity is calculated as:
    $$ \alpha'_{ij} = g \alpha_i $$
    where $g = \exp(-0.5 \omega'_0 d_0^2 - 0.5 \omega'_2 d_1^2 - \omega'_1 d_0 d_1)$, $\omega'$ are the upper triangular elements of $\Sigma'^{-1}$, and $d$ are the components of $u_i - x_j$ (difference between Gaussian center and pixel center) [S6, unofficial].

### Loss Function
The optimization minimizes a composite loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered image $\gamma$ and the ground truth image $\gamma_{gt}$:
$$ \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM} $$
where $\lambda$ is a weight (typically 0.2) [S6, unofficial]. $\mathcal{L}_1$ is the L1 loss, and $\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ is the differentiable Structural Similarity index loss [S6, unofficial].

### Backward Pass (Gradient Computation)
Gradients are computed via backpropagation using the chain rule. The Jacobians for each parameter are:
*   **Rotation $q_i$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i} $$
    The rotation matrix $R(q)$ is derived from the quaternion:
    $$ R(q) = 2 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} - (q_j^2 + q_k^2) & q_i q_j - q_r q_k & q_i q_k + q_r q_j \\ q_i q_j + q_r q_k & \frac{1}{2} - (q_i^2 + q_k^2) & q_j q_k - q_r q_i \\ q_i q_k - q_r q_j & q_j q_k + q_r q_i & \frac{1}{2} - (q_i^2 + q_j^2) \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S6, unofficial].
*   **Scale $s_i$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i} $$
    [S6, unofficial].
*   **Color $h_i$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i} $$
    [S6, unofficial].
*   **Opacity $\alpha_i$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i} $$
    [S6, unofficial].
*   **Position $\mathbf{p}_{w,i}$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{p}_{c,i}}{\partial \mathbf{p}_{w,i}} $$
    [S6, unofficial].

### Adaptive Density Control
To manage the number of Gaussians, densification and pruning are performed periodically (e.g., every 100–500 steps) [S2][S4, unofficial]:
1.  **Pruning:** If $\alpha_i < \epsilon$ (opacity threshold) or the Gaussian is too large, remove it [S2].
2.  **Densification Check:** If the positional gradient magnitude $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{p}} \mathcal{L}\| > \tau_p$ (gradient threshold):
    *   **Over-reconstruction:** If the scale norm $\|\mathbf{s}_i\| > \tau_s$ (size threshold), split the Gaussian into two clones with perturbed positions and halved scales [S2].
    *   **Under-reconstruction:** If $\|\mathbf{s}_i\| \le \tau_s$, clone the Gaussian [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
def train_3dgs(input_images, camera_poses, num_iters=30000, sh_degree=3):
    # 1. Initialization
    gaussians = initialize_from_sfm(input_images, camera_poses)
    # gaussians contains: positions, quaternions, scales, opacities, sh_coeffs
    
    optimizer = Adam(gaussians.parameters(), lr=1.6e-4)
    
    # 2. Training Loop
    for step in range(1, num_iters + 1):
        # Camera Selection
        camera = select_next_camera(camera_poses, step)
        
        # Rendering
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, camera)
        
        # Loss Computation
        gt_image = get_ground_truth(camera, step)
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, sh_degree)
        
        # Backpropagation
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # Adaptive Density Control (every refine_every steps)
        if step % refine_every == 0:
            gaussians = adaptive_density_control(gaussians, step)
            
        # Update SH Degree
        if step % sh_degree_interval == 0 and sh_degree < max_sh_degree:
            increase_sh_degree(gaussians)
            
    return gaussians

def adaptive_density_control(gaussians, step):
    # Pruning
    for g in gaussians:
        if g.opacity < opacity_threshold or g.scale_norm > max_scale_threshold:
            remove_gaussian(g)
            
    # Densification
    for g in gaussians:
        if g.position_gradient_norm > grad_threshold:
            if g.scale_norm > size_threshold:
                split_gaussian(g)
            else:
                clone_gaussian(g)
                
    return gaussians
```

Trains a 3DGS to render stereo-calibrated novel views, applies stereo depth estimation on the rendered views, and fuses the estimated dense depth maps using a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) to form a triangular mesh [S1].

### Pseudo Ground Truth Acquisition for Gaussians
A labeling pipeline that generates Gaussian-specific pseudo ground truth labels from original point cloud labels, accounting for the densification and pruning processes of 3D-GS optimization [S8, unofficial].

### Cross-View Pruning Mechanism
A mechanism comprising a Cross-View Consistency Loss and adaptive pruning to reduce Gaussian redundancy and ensure geometric validity. It selects adjacent view pairs with SSIM > 0.6, computes a consistency loss to penalize residual discrepancies, and prunes Gaussians based on depth/distance consistency thresholds [S9].

### Single-view Mini-batch Strategy
A training data sampling strategy where data is organized per viewpoint, and a mini-batch consists of data from only a single viewpoint. This involves collecting pixel-color pairs for each viewpoint $i$ into dataset $D_i$, organizing them into $D = \{D_i\}$, and sampling a mini-batch $B_{3DGS}$ from a single $D_i$ [S10, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is significantly faster than NeRFs, typically completing in minutes to hours depending on scene complexity and resolution [S2][S5, unofficial]. The rendering time is $O(N \cdot P)$ where $N$ is the number of Gaussians and $P$ is the number of pixels, but optimized rasterization makes it real-time [S6, S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians $N$. The original 3DGS can require several GBs of VRAM for high-resolution scenes [S4, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves comparable or superior image quality (PSNR, SSIM) to NeRFs with much faster training and inference [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering, fast training, high visual fidelity, explicit representation allows for easy editing and compression [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce "floaters" in empty space, sensitive to initialization, and may require significant VRAM for large scenes [S2][S4, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When real-time rendering and fast training are required, and the scene can be represented by a set of Gaussians [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When explicit geometry (watertight mesh) is required without post-processing, or when memory is extremely constrained [S1, S2].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S2]. It builds on the concept of radiance fields but uses explicit 3D Gaussians instead of implicit neural networks. The method was inspired by the need for real-time rendering of complex scenes, which NeRFs could not achieve efficiently [S5, unofficial]. The original implementation used CUDA for GPU acceleration [S2]. The method has since been extended to various domains including SLAM, dynamic scenes, and physical parameter optimization [S1].
