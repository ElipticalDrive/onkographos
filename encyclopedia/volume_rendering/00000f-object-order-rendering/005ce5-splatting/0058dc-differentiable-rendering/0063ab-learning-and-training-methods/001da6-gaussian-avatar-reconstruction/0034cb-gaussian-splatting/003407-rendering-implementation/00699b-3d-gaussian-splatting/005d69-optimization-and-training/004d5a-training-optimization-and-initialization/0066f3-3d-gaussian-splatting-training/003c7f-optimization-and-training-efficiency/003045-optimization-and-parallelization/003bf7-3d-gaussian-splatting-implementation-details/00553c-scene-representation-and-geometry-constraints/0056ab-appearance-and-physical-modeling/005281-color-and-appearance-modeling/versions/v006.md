# Color and Appearance Modeling

Also called: Appearance Modeling, View-Dependent Color Modeling

## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting addresses the limitation of base 3DGS, which uses fixed Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients per Gaussian to represent view-dependent radiance. This approach struggles with complex lighting, transient objects, and large photometric variations in unstructured "in-the-wild" datasets. This family of methods decouples appearance from static geometry or enhances the radiance representation to support physically-based shading, global view-dependent effects (e.g., daylight), and transient occluders, while maintaining real-time rendering speeds.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering equation, where the pixel color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is computed via alpha-blending of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
where $T_i$ is the transmittance, $g_i$ is the 2D projected Gaussian density, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, and $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the view-dependent outgoing radiance. The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md).
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md).
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [Compact View-dependent Color].

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
A plug-and-play method that mitigates floaters and color distortions by applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image, decoupling global view-dependent variations from local spatial changes. Choose when you need to handle significant global lighting changes (e.g., daylight) without slowing down rendering.

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
Models outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes. Choose when accurate specular view-dependent effects and physically-based shading are required.

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
Focuses on optimizing SH coefficients through initialization, compression, distillation, or gradient handling to reduce memory cost and improve training stability. Choose when you want to stick to the standard SH representation but need better efficiency or stability.

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
Decouples appearance from static geometry using an image-conditioned MLP and concrete distribution for transient detection. Choose when reconstructing scenes from unstructured, in-the-wild images with transient occluders (tourists, clouds) and varying lighting.

### Compact View-dependent Color
- **Definition:** A method to represent view-dependent color using a grid-based neural field instead of storing Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients per Gaussian, reducing parameters from 48 to a shared grid [S1].
- **Step:** Contract unbounded positions $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ to a bounded range using the contraction function from mip-NeRF 360. Input positions into hash grids, then feed the resulting feature and view direction into a tiny MLP. Use 0-degree components of SH (same channels as RGB, not view-dependent) and convert to RGB colors [S1].
- **Formula:** Contraction function: $\text{contract}(p_n) = p_n$ if $||p_n|| \le 1$, else $\frac{p_n}{||p_n||} (2 - ||p_n||)$. View-dependent color $c_n(d)$ is computed as: $c_n(d) = f(\text{contract}(p_n), d; \theta)$, where $f$ is a tiny MLP with parameters $\theta$ [S1].
- **Comparison:** Original 3DGS uses 48 parameters per Gaussian for SH (max 3 degrees) to model view-dependent color; this method uses a shared grid-based neural field [S1].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) introduced a novel explicit representation for radiance fields, using 3D Gaussians to model scene geometry and appearance. The original method used Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to encode view-dependent color, which is efficient but limited in modeling complex lighting and specularities. Subsequent research identified issues with "floaters" (artifacts in transparent or low-texture regions) and poor performance on unstructured datasets with transient objects. Methods like DAVIGS, 3iGS, and SWAG were developed to address these specific appearance modeling challenges, leveraging neural fields, factorized lighting, and image-conditioned modulations. The "Compact" approach addresses the parameter inefficiency of SH by replacing it with a shared neural field.
