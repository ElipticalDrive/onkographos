# Geometry and orientation extension


## Overview
This family extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation by introducing explicit geometric orientation or asymmetric density distributions to primitives, enabling the extraction of consistent normal fields, watertight meshes, or the modeling of sharp, one-sided boundaries that symmetric Gaussians cannot capture. It differs from standard Gaussian Splatting, which relies on implicit opacity for geometry, by restoring reciprocity in attenuation or introducing skewness parameters, thereby allowing the derivation of explicit geometric fields (normals and occupancy) via frameworks like Objects as Volumes [S1].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family augment the standard 3D Gaussian primitive, defined by center $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color $c_i$, with additional parameters that define orientation or asymmetry.

1.  **Orientation/Asymmetry Parameterization**:
    *   **Oriented Variants**: Introduce a learnable normal vector $n_i \in S^2$ per Gaussian. The attenuation is restricted to a half-space defined by $n_i$ to restore reciprocity $\sigma(x, w) = \sigma(x, -w)$, which is broken by standard isotropic attenuation [S1].
    *   **Skew-Normal Variants**: Replace the symmetric Gaussian kernel with the Azzalini Skew-Normal distribution. This introduces a learnable skewness parameter (magnitude $m_k$ and direction $d_k$) allowing continuous interpolation between symmetric Gaussians and half-ellipsoids [S1].

2.  **Geometric Field Derivation**:
    *   For oriented Gaussians, the vacancy field $v(\mathbf{x})$ is approximated by maximizing the product of unobstructed transmittance over training rays. The normal field $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x})$ is derived as the normalized gradient of the log-vacancy:
        $$ \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\nabla \log v(\mathbf{x})}{\|\nabla \log v(\mathbf{x})\|} $$
        where $\nabla \log v(\mathbf{x})$ sums the gradients of Gaussian components oriented towards the query point [S1].
    *   For skew-normal variants, the spatial mean $\mu$ does not coincide with the distribution's center. A bounding box correction $\Delta$ is applied to locate the true spatial mean for rasterization [S1].

3.  **Rendering and Optimization**:
    *   Rendering incorporates the orientation or skewness into the 2D projection and blending process.
    *   Optimization includes geometric consistency losses, such as normal alignment between rendered normals and depth gradients, or depth-normal consistency [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Oriented Gaussian Splatting](9621-oriented-gaussian-splatting.md)
A variant where each primitive is assigned a learnable oriented normal vector $n_i$ to create a reciprocal attenuation model. It differs from standard Gaussian Splatting by restoring reciprocity, enabling the extraction of watertight meshes and consistent normals via the Objects as Volumes framework. It is chosen when explicit, consistent surface normals and mesh extraction are required from the Gaussian representation.

### [3D Skew-Normal Splatting](10045-3d-skew-normal-splatting.md)
Replaces the standard symmetric 3D Gaussian kernel with the Azzalini Skew-Normal distribution to model asymmetric structures, such as sharp boundaries and one-sided surfaces. It sets apart from standard Gaussian Splatting and its symmetric variants by introducing a learnable skewness parameter that allows continuous interpolation between symmetric Gaussians and half-ellipsoids, enabling a single primitive to represent complex geometries that would otherwise require multiple overlapping kernels.

### 6D Gaussian Splatting
An extension of 3DGS that models view-dependent effects by adding directional dimensions, representing scene elements as 6D Gaussians (space+direction) [S1]. The full 6D covariance is parameterized via Cholesky decomposition $\Sigma = LL^\top$ with $L$ lower-triangular and positive diagonal entries to guarantee positive definiteness [S1]. Rendering conditions the 6D Gaussian on the viewing direction $d$ to yield a conditional 3D Gaussian for the spatial component, with opacity modulated by the alignment between the view direction and the Gaussian’s preferred direction [S1].

### Anisotropic Covariance
Allows 3D Gaussians to scale anisotropically along the x, y, and z axes and rotate in 3D space, rather than remaining spherical, to better fit scene geometry [S2, unofficial]. Each Gaussian optimizes 3 scale parameters (x, y, z expansion) and 4 rotation parameters (quaternion) [S2, unofficial]. On Average-30K, the Anisotropic variant achieves a PSNR of 26.05, compared to 25.23 for the Isotropic variant [S2, unofficial]. Isotropic forces Gaussians to remain spherical, causing blurry, rounded artifacts (e.g., tree trunks/leaves appear as round dots), whereas Anisotropic allows elongated/rotated shapes matching scene geometry [S2, unofficial].

### Barycentric Splat Parameterization
A method to constrain 2D Gaussian Splat centers to lie on triangular mesh faces using barycentric coordinates, allowing splats to move freely on the face while preserving per-splat attributes [S3]. For each mesh face with vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$, the splat center $m$ is computed as:
$$ m = u \cdot v_1 + w \cdot v_2 + (1 - u - w) \cdot v_3 $$
where $u, w \in [0, 1]$ with $u + w \le 1$ are the barycentric coordinates [S3].

### In-Plane Rotation for Splats
A parameterization for the orientation of Gaussian Splats on the face plane using an in-plane rotation parameter $R_{2d}$, transformed into world space [S3]. The final rotation matrix $R$ used for rasterization is computed as:
$$ R = R_s \cdot R_{2d} $$
where $R_s$ is the rotation matrix of the triangle faces [S3].

## Background
The concept of extending Gaussian Splatting with geometric orientation stems from the limitation of standard 3DGS in providing explicit geometric fields. Standard attenuation breaks reciprocity, making it difficult to extract consistent normals or watertight meshes. Oriented Gaussian Splatting addresses this by introducing reciprocal attenuation [S1]. Concurrently, 6D Gaussian Splatting was introduced to model view-dependent effects by adding directional dimensions, parameterizing the covariance via Cholesky decomposition for stability [S1]. Skew-Normal Splatting addresses the inability of symmetric Gaussians to model sharp, one-sided boundaries by introducing skewness [S1]. Anisotropic covariance is a foundational optimization strategy in 3DGS to allow elongated shapes, improving reconstruction quality over isotropic variants [S2, unofficial]. Barycentric parameterization and in-plane rotation are techniques used in mesh-constrained Gaussian representations to ensure splats adhere to surface geometry [S3].
