# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed 3D Gaussian Splatting, Per-Splat Backpropagation, MaskGaussian Gradients

## Overview
This family encompasses methods that accelerate the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and enable its application to scenes exceeding single-GPU memory limits. It addresses the computational bottleneck of the differentiable rasterization backward pass and the memory constraints of large-scale scene reconstruction. It is distinguished from standard optimization by restructuring the gradient computation from a per-pixel atomic accumulation to a per-splat approach [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md], partitioning primitives across multiple GPUs [7349-grendel-gs.md], or introducing specialized gradient paths for probabilistic masks [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md].

## Shared principles
The optimization and parallelization methods share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer defined in [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md). The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants (e.g., MaskGaussian, Grendel-GS) modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
Computes gradients for probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ by decomposing the pixel color into Gaussian and background paths. It is chosen when occlusion or transparency modeling requires differentiable mask modulation.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative loop integrating dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification) with the optimization step. It is the standard framework for adapting representation density to scene complexity.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
Replaces per-pixel atomic updates with per-splat gradient accumulation to eliminate warp contention. It is chosen to reduce training time by 4–5× while maintaining numerical equivalence.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed framework partitioning Gaussians across a GPU cluster. It is chosen for scenes with billions of primitives that exceed single-device VRAM.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Provides concrete hyperparameters, data structures, and stability fixes (e.g., scene extent correction) required for reproducible high-performance implementation.

## Background
The methods originate from the initial 3D Gaussian Splatting paper [Kerbl et al., 2023] and subsequent works addressing its scalability and training efficiency. Key developments include the per-splat backward pass optimization [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md], distributed training frameworks like Grendel-GS [7349-grendel-gs.md], and specialized gradient computations for masked rendering [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md]. These methods are primarily used in novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and large-scale scene modeling.
