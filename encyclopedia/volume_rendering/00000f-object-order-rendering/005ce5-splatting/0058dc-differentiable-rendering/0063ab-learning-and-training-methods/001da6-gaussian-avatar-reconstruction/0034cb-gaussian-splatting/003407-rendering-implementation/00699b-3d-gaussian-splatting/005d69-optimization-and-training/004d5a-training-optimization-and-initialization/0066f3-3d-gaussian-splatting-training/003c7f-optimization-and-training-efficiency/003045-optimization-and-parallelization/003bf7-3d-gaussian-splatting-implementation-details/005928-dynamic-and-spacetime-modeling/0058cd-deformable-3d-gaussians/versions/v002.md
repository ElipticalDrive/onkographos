# Deformable 3D Gaussians

Also called: Deformable-GS

## Overview
Deformable 3D Gaussians is a framework for modeling monocular dynamic scenes that extends static 3D Gaussian Splatting by learning 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and using a learned deformation field to model time-varying geometry [S1]. It decouples the radiance field from the deformation field, allowing for real-time rendering and high-fidelity reconstruction of dynamic scenes [S1]. Unlike entangled methods that condition implicit representations (like NeRF) directly on time, or methods that suffer from slow convergence and overfitting associated with implicit representations, this method uses discrete 3D Gaussians for faster training and real-time rendering [S1]. It also incorporates an Annealing Smooth Training (AST) mechanism to mitigate jitter in temporal sequences caused by inaccurate camera poses in real-world datasets [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of images of a monocular dynamic scene, time labels for each image, camera poses calibrated by Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [37], and a sparse point cloud generated by SfM [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of time-varying 3D Gaussians that can be differentially rasterized to render images at arbitrary times $t$ and camera poses [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **3D Gaussians ($G$):** Defined in canonical space by:
    *   Center position $\mathbf{x}$
    *   Opacity $\sigma$
    *   Rotation quaternion $\mathbf{r}$
    *   Scaling vector $\mathbf{s}$
    *   View-dependent color represented via spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients [S1].
    *   The 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is constructed from $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{s}$ as $\Sigma = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^T\mathbf{R}^T$, where $\mathbf{R}$ is the rotation matrix derived from $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ is the scaling matrix derived from $\mathbf{s}$ [S1].
2.  **Deformation Network ($F_\theta$):** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with depth $D=8$ and hidden layer dimension $W=256$ [S1]. It takes encoded canonical position and time as input and outputs offsets $(\delta\mathbf{x}, \delta\mathbf{r}, \delta\mathbf{s})$ [S1].

**Positional Encoding:**
Inputs to the deformation network are processed using positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$:
$$ \gamma(\mathbf{p}) = (\sin(2^k \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^k \pi \mathbf{p}))_{k=0}^{L-1} $$
*   For canonical position $\mathbf{x}$: $L=10$ [S1].
*   For time $t$: $L=6$ in synthetic scenes, $L=10$ in real scenes [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the set of 3D Gaussians $G(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$ from the SfM sparse point cloud. The initial positions $\mathbf{x}$ are derived from the SfM points. Opacity, rotation, and scale are initialized according to standard 3D Gaussian Splatting practices [S1].

2.  **Warm-up Phase (Iterations $0$ to $3000$):**
    *   Optimize only the parameters of the 3D Gaussians ($\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma$, SH coefficients).
    *   The deformation field $F_\theta$ is **not** optimized during this phase [S1].
    *   This allows the 3D Gaussians to attain relatively stable positions and shapes in the canonical space [S1].

3.  **Joint Optimization (Iterations $3000+$):**
    *   Perform joint optimization of the deformation field $F_\theta$ and the 3D Gaussians using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   **Learning Rates:**
        *   3D Gaussians: Standard learning rates (same as official 3D Gaussian Splatting implementation) [S1].
        *   Deformation Network: Exponential decay from $8 \times 10^{-4}$ to $1.6 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].
        *   Adam parameters: $\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$ [S1].
    *   **Deformation Field Forward Pass:**
        For each Gaussian $i$ with canonical position $\mathbf{x}_i$ and current time $t$:
        $$ (\delta\mathbf{x}, \delta\mathbf{r}, \delta\mathbf{s}) = F_\theta(\gamma(\text{sg}(\mathbf{x}_i)), \gamma(t)) $$
        where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator, ensuring gradients do not flow back to update the canonical positions $\mathbf{x}$ through the deformation network [S1].
    *   **Deformed Gaussians:**
        The deformed parameters are computed as:
        $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \delta\mathbf{x} $$
        $$ \mathbf{r}' = \mathbf{r} + \delta\mathbf{r} $$
        $$ \mathbf{s}' = \mathbf{s} + \delta\mathbf{s} $$
        The deformed Gaussian is $G(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{r}', \mathbf{s}', \sigma)$ [S1].
    *   **Rendering:**
        Project the deformed 3D Gaussians to 2D using the covariance matrix $\Sigma'$:
        $$ \Sigma' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{V} \Sigma \mathbf{V}^T \mathbf{J}^T $$
        where $\mathbf{V}$ is the view matrix, $\Sigma$ is the 3D covariance matrix of the deformed Gaussian, and $\mathbf{J}$ is the Jacobian of the affine approximation of the projective transformation [S1].
        Render pixel color $C(\mathbf{p})$ using alpha blending:
        $$ C(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
        where $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$, $\alpha_i = \sigma_i e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)^T \Sigma'^{-1} (\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_i)}$, $c_i$ is the color, and $\mathcal{N}$ is the set of Gaussians along the ray [S1].
    *   **Backpropagation:**
        Gradients for both the deformation field and the 3D Gaussians are derived from the customized differential Gaussian rasterization pipeline [S1].

4.  **Annealing Smooth Training (AST) Mechanism:**
    To mitigate jitter caused by inaccurate poses in real-world datasets, Gaussian noise is added to the time input during training. This noise decays linearly over iterations [S1].
    *   The deformation field input becomes:
        $$ \Delta = F_\theta(\gamma(\text{sg}(\mathbf{x})), \gamma(t + X(i))) $$
    *   The noise term $X(i)$ is defined as:
        $$ X(i) = \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta t \cdot (1 - i/\tau) $$
        where:
        *   $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ is standard Gaussian noise [S1].
        *   $\beta = 0.1$ (scaling factor) [S1].
        *   $\Delta t$ is the mean time interval between frames [S1].
        *   $\tau = 20,000$ iterations (annealing threshold) [S1].
        *   $i$ is the current training iteration [S1].
    *   This mechanism enhances temporal generalization in early training and prevents excessive smoothing in later stages [S1].

5.  **Adaptive Density Control:**
    Applied during joint optimization to maintain representation quality [S1]:
    *   **Pruning:** Remove Gaussians with opacity $\sigma$ below a threshold [S1].
    *   **Densification:** For Gaussians with positional gradient magnitude exceeding $t_{pos} = 0.0002$:
        *   If the Gaussian is small (insufficient for geometric details): Clone the Gaussian and move the clone in the direction of the positional gradient [S1].
        *   If the Gaussian is large and overlapping: Split the Gaussian by dividing its scale by $\xi = 1.6$ [S1].

def positional_encoding(p, L):
    """
    Computes positional encoding for input p.
    p: tensor of shape (..., 1)
    L: number of frequency bands
    Returns: tensor of shape (..., 2*L)
    """
    freq_bands = 2.0 ** torch.arange(L, device=p.device) * math.pi
    # sin(2^k * pi * p) and cos(2^k * pi * p) for k in 0..L-1
    # Note: The formula is sin(2^k pi p). 
    # We generate frequencies k=0 to L-1.
    k = torch.arange(L, device=p.device)
    freqs = (2.0 ** k) * math.pi
    # Expand dims for broadcasting
    freqs = freqs.view(1, -1)
    p_expanded = p.unsqueeze(-1) # (..., 1, 1)
    # Compute sin and cos
    sin_vals = torch.sin(freqs * p_expanded)
    cos_vals = torch.cos(freqs * p_expanded)
    # Concatenate along the last dimension
    return torch.cat([sin_vals, cos_vals], dim=-1)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training involves joint optimization of 3D Gaussians and a deformation MLP. The deformation network has $D=8$ layers and $W=256$ hidden units, which is relatively lightweight compared to full NeRFs. Rendering is real-time due to the tile-based differentiable Gaussian rasterization [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is dominated by the number of 3D Gaussians, which is adaptive. The deformation network memory is negligible compared to the Gaussian attributes [S1].
*   **Performance:** Experiments show higher rendering quality and real-time rendering speed compared to existing implicit methods and other dynamic scene modeling techniques [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Decouples time and radiance field, enabling efficient representation of non-dramatic action variations. Real-time rendering capability. AST mechanism handles pose inaccuracies without extra computational overhead during inference [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate SfM poses for initialization; while AST mitigates jitter, it does not correct pose errors. Performance may degrade with highly dynamic scenes with dramatic action variations, as the canonical space assumption may break down [S1].
*   **When to use:** Monocular dynamic scene reconstruction where real-time rendering is required and SfM poses are available. Suitable for scenes with non-dramatic motion.
*   **When not to use:** Multi-view dynamic scenes with significant camera motion (though SfM handles this, the method is designed for monocular). Scenes with extreme, non-rigid deformations that cannot be captured by a local deformation field [S1].

## Background
Deformable 3D Gaussians was proposed to address the limitations of implicit representations (like NeRF) in dynamic scene modeling, which suffer from slow convergence, overfitting, and lack of real-time rendering capabilities [S1]. It builds on the seminal work of 3D Gaussian Splatting for static scenes [15] and extends it to dynamic scenes by introducing a deformation field [S1]. The method was introduced in the paper "Deformable 3D Gaussians for High-Fidelity Monocular Dynamic Scene Reconstruction" [S1]. It is part of the broader family of dynamic and spacetime modeling methods that seek to represent time-varying scenes efficiently [S1].
