# DaReNeRF

Also called: DaReNeRF architecture, Direction-Aware Representation

## Overview
DaReNeRF is a neural radiance field method for novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes that replaces standard 2D discrete wavelet transforms (DWT) with a direction-aware representation based on the dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT) [S1]. It solves the checkerboard artifacts and direction ambiguity inherent in 2D DWT by modeling features from six specific directions using real and imaginary wavelet coefficients, while maintaining memory efficiency through plane-based 4D volume decomposition [S1]. Unlike [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md), which focuses on multi-input multi-output dynamic optimization, DaReNeRF specifically targets the structural fidelity of the spatial-temporal representation to eliminate directional artifacts [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of posed images with timestamps.
**Outputs:** Rendered images at arbitrary poses and timestamps.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Plane-based Representations ($M^{AB}_r$):** For each pair of axes $(A, B) \in \{X, Y, Z, T\}^2$ with $A \neq B$, and for each rank $r$, a 2D plane representation is stored.
2.  **Wavelet Coefficients:** Each plane representation $M^{AB}_r$ is decomposed into:
    *   Approximation coefficient: $W^{AB}_{a,r} \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2^{l-1}, n/2^{l-1}}$.
    *   Real direction-aware coefficients: $\{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6 \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2^{l-1}, n/2^{l-1}}$.
    *   Imaginary direction-aware coefficients: $\{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6 \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2^{l-1}, n/2^{l-1}}$.
    *   Here, $l$ is the DTCWT transformation level, and $m, n$ are the spatial resolutions.
3.  **Vector-based Representations ($v^r_C$):** For static decomposition, vectors $v^r_C \in \mathbb{R}^C$ are stored along the remaining axis $C$.
4.  **Masks ($M^{AB}$):** Learned masks used to sparsify the wavelet coefficients.
5.  **Voxel Grid:** A small 3D voxel grid indicating emptiness, storing the maximum opacity across time steps for each spatial location.
6.  **$V_{RF}$:** A 2D tensor stacking all learned vectors $v^r_i$.

**Parameters:**
*   Spatial grid size: 512 [S1].
*   Temporal grid size: 300 [S1].
*   Batch size: 4,096 [S1].
*   Coordinate system: Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) [S1].
*   Hardware: Single A100 GPU [S1].
*   Loss weights: $\lambda_{reg}$ (total variation), $\lambda_m$ (mask sparsity). Values not given in the sources.

**Steps:**

1.  **Static Scene Decomposition (Optional):** If modeling a static scene, decompose the density field $D$ using TensorRF-like decomposition [S1]:
    $$ D = \sum_{r=1}^{R1} M^{XY}_r \circ v^r_Z \circ v^{r1} + \sum_{r=1}^{R2} M^{XZ}_r \circ v^r_Y \circ v^{r2} + \sum_{r=1}^{R3} M^{YZ}_r \circ v^r_X \circ v^{r3} $$
    where $R1, R2, R3$ are the number of low-rank components, and $\circ$ denotes outer product or appropriate tensor contraction [S1].

2.  **Direction-Aware Representation Reconstruction:** For a dynamic scene, reconstruct the plane-based representation $M^{AB}_r$ from its wavelet coefficients using the Inverse Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (IDTCWT) [S1]:
    $$ M^{AB}_r = \text{IDTCWT}([W^{AB}_{a,r}, \{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6, \{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6]) $$
    The DTCWT uses two complex wavelets satisfying Hilbert transform properties, providing 6 directional coefficients (3 real, 3 imaginary pairs) to eliminate checkerboard effects [S1].

3.  **Masking for Sparsity:** Apply learned masks to the wavelet coefficients to encourage sparsity and remove empty regions [S1]:
    $$ \tilde{W}^{AB} = \text{sg}(H(M^{AB}) - \text{sigmoid}(M^{AB})) \odot W^{AB} $$
    where $\text{sg}$ is the stop-gradient operator, $H$ is the Heaviside step function, and $\odot$ is element-wise multiplication. The mask $M^{AB}$ is learned during training [S1].

4.  **Feature Extraction:**
    *   Extract feature vectors from paired planes (e.g., XY and ZT) [S1].
    *   Multiply the feature vectors from the paired planes element-wise [S1].
    *   Concatenate the multiplied results into a single vector [S1].
    *   Multiply the concatenated vector by $V_{RF}$ (stacking all $v^r_i$ into a 2D tensor) to obtain density and appearance features [S1].

5.  **Density and Color Query:**
    *   Query point opacities directly from the density features [S1].
    *   Regress RGB color values using a compact Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The MLP inputs are the appearance features and the view direction [S1].

6.  **Volumetric Rendering:** Synthesize images via differentiable volumetric rendering along rays [S1].

7.  **Emptiness Voxel Check:** Before rendering, check the small 3D voxel grid. If a voxel indicates emptiness (aggregated maximum opacity across time steps is below a threshold), skip the points in that region to accelerate computation [S1].

8.  **Optimization:** Minimize the total loss $L$ [S1]:
    $$ L = \frac{1}{|R|} \sum_{r \in R} ||C(r) - \hat{C}(r)||^2 + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg} + \lambda_m L_m $$
    where $C(r)$ is the rendered color, $\hat{C}(r)$ is the ground-truth color, $L_{reg}$ is the total variation loss on the direction-aware representation, and $L_m$ is the mask sparsity loss (sum of all masks) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced from $O(N^3 T F)$ to $O(R N^2 T F)$, where $R = R1 + R2 + R3 \ll N$ is the number of low-rank components, $N$ is the spatial resolution, $T$ is the temporal resolution, and $F$ is the feature dimension [S1].
*   **Performance:** Trained on a single A100 GPU with a batch size of 4,096 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates checkerboard effects and direction ambiguity present in 2D DWT; efficient memory usage for dynamic scenes; handles both dynamic and static scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of mask sparsity and regularization weights; complexity of DTCWT implementation compared to standard DWT [S1].
*   **When to use:** Novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes where directional fidelity is important and memory efficiency is required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Static scenes with simple geometry where standard NeRF or TensorRF might be sufficient; scenes with very high temporal frequency where the temporal grid size of 300 is insufficient [S1].

## Background
DaReNeRF was introduced in the paper "DaReNeRF: Direction-aware Representation for Dynamic Scenes" [S1]. It builds upon the plane-based 4D volume decomposition and draws inspiration from the dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT) [S1]. The method was evaluated on the Plenoptic Video and D-NeRF datasets for novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes [S1]. It addresses the limitations of 2D DWT, which suffers from direction ambiguity and checkerboard artifacts, by using DTCWT with 6 directional coefficients [S1].
