# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and reducing floaters more robustly than single-scale depth priors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views $i$ and $j$ (where $j$ is a neighboring or novel view), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$, RGB maps $C_i, C_j$, and a multi-scale voxel representation with render depths $D_{il}(p_i)$ at scale $l$ for pixel $p_i$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for each training ray $r_{train}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxels:** A hierarchy of voxel grids at low, medium, and high resolutions. The method assumes a shared or weight-sharing voxel structure across scales to capture varying scene frequencies [S1].
*   **Ray Data:** For each training ray $r_{train}$, the system stores the 2D pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Reprojection to 3D (Frame $i$):** For a pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$, compute its homogeneous coordinate $\tilde{p}_i$. Calculate the 3D point in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$ ($x_{li}$) using the depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ at scale $l$ and camera intrinsics $K_i$:
    $$
    x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i
    $$
    Alternatively, the 3D point can be determined directly from the ray origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
    $$
    x_l = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i)
    $$
2.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$
    x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i
    $$
3.  **Reproject to Frame $j$:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$
    x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j)
    $$
4.  **Project to 2D (Frame $j$):** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$ using intrinsics $K_j$ and the projection function $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$:
    $$
    \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j})
    $$
5.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the error $e_l(p_i)$ for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel $C_i(p_i)$ and the warped RGB pixel $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$ from frame $j$:
    $$
    e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})
    $$
6.  **Select Pseudo-Ground Truth:** Identify the scale $l$ that minimizes the reprojection error for the given ray. This scale's depth is selected as the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$:
    $$
    D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train}))
    $$
7.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth $D_l(r_{train})$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ across all training rays $R_{train}$:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2
    $$
    *(Note: The source formula [S1] sums over scales $l$ and rays $r_{train}$. The exact aggregation over rays is implied by the summation notation.)*

**Parameters:**
*   **Scales:** Typically includes Low, Medium, and High resolution voxels [S1].
*   **Weighting Factor $\lambda$:** Part of the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{geo}$ (implied by parent structure [S1]). Value not given in sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sparse Views:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views (e.g., 360° datasets), generalization may be challenging [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Scale Mismatch:** Using a single scale depth prior ($\mathcal{L}_d$) alone can suffer from higher error later in training due to scale mismatches, whereas $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ refines depth progressively [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geo_loss(
    rays, 
    frames, 
    voxel_grids, 
    intrinsics, 
    poses
):
    """
    rays: List of training rays, each containing pixel coordinate p_i in frame i.
    frames: List of frame objects, each containing RGB map C, rotation R, translation t.
    voxel_grids: List of voxel grids at different scales (low, mid, high).
    intrinsics: Dictionary of camera intrinsics K for each frame.
    poses: Dictionary of camera poses (R, t) for each frame.
    
    Returns:
    loss: Scalar geometric adaptation loss.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        p_i = ray.pixel_coordinate
        frame_i = ray.source_frame
        frame_j = ray.target_frame # Neighbor or novel view
        
        K_i = intrinsics[frame_i.id]
        K_j = intrinsics[frame_j.id]
        R_i, t_i = poses[frame_i.id]
        R_j, t_j = poses[frame_j.id]
        
        C_i = frame_i.rgb_map
        C_j = frame_j.rgb_map
        
        min_error = infinity
        best_scale_depth = None
        best_scale_idx = -1
        
        # Iterate over all voxel scales
        for l, voxel_grid in enumerate(voxel_grids):
            # 1. Get depth at scale l for pixel p_i in frame i
            # D_il(p_i) is obtained via volume rendering from the voxel grid
            D_il = voxel_grid.render_depth(p_i, frame_i.camera_origin, frame_i.camera_direction)
            
            # 2. Reproject to 3D in camera coordinates of frame i
            # Homogeneous coordinate
            p_i_hom = [p_i.x, p_i.y, 1.0]
            # Inverse intrinsic multiplication
            inv_K_i = np.linalg.inv(K_i)
            vec = inv_K_i @ p_i_hom
            # Scale by depth
            x_li = D_il * vec
            
            # 3. Transform to World Coordinates
            # x_l = R_i * x_li + t_i
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            # 4. Transform to Camera Coordinates of frame j
            # x_li_j = R_j^T * (x_l - t_j)
            x_li_j = R_j.T @ (x_l - t_j)
            
            # 5. Project to 2D in frame j
            # pi([x,y,z]) = [x/z, y/z]
            if x_li_j[2] == 0:
                continue # Avoid division by zero
            p_j = [x_li_j[0] / x_li_j[2], x_li_j[1] / x_li_j[2]]
            
            # 6. Compute Reprojection Error
            # e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(p_j)
            # Assuming C_j is sampled at p_j (with interpolation if needed)
            C_j_val = sample_rgb(C_j, p_j)
            error = np.linalg.norm(C_i[p_i] - C_j_val)
            
            if error < min_error:
                min_error = error
                best_scale_depth = D_il
                best_scale_idx = l
        
        # 7. Compute Loss against best scale depth
        # L_geo = sum || D_l - D' ||^2
        # Here D' is best_scale_depth. 
        # The source sums over scales l, comparing rendered depth D_l to D'.
        # We assume D_l is the depth rendered at scale l for this ray.
        for l, voxel_grid in enumerate(voxel_grids):
            D_l = voxel_grid.render_depth(p_i, frame_i.camera_origin, frame_i.camera_direction)
            diff = D_l - best_scale_depth
            total_loss += diff ** 2
            
    return total_loss
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Increases with the number of scales and the cost of reprojection for each scale per ray. Requires rendering depth at multiple scales and performing coordinate transformations for each.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel grids and RGB maps for training views.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Achieves lower final depth error than single-scale depth priors ($\mathcal{L}_d$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive; does not require complex scheduling or external depth priors. Robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warping methods like GeCoNeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses. Performance may degrade in few-shot scenarios with sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed in **FrugalNeRF** [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to accelerate and regularize training in extreme few-shot novel view synthesis without learned priors. It addresses the issue of floaters and sub-optimal convergence in multi-scale voxel-based NeRFs [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in FrugalNeRF for few-shot NeRF training, often combined with novel view regularization and weight-sharing voxels [S1].
