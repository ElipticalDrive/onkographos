# Analytic Transmittance 3DGS

Also called: Analytic transmittance computation for 3DGS, Volumetrically Consistent 3D Gaussian Rasterization, Volumetrically Consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Analytic Transmittance 3DGS is a rasterization-based rendering method that replaces the approximate alpha computation of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with an analytic integration of the volume rendering equation along the camera ray [S1]. It computes physically accurate transmittance and opacity values without projecting 3D Gaussians to 2D splats, thereby avoiding the linearization and self-occlusion approximations inherent in splatting [S1]. This method achieves higher accuracy for opaque surfaces and better view synthesis quality (SSIM, LPIPS) while maintaining rasterization speeds [S1].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians, each defined by:
    *   $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D mean (center).
    *   $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: 3D covariance matrix.
    *   $c_i$: View-dependent color.
    *   $\theta_i \in [0, 1]$: Learnable parameter controlling density.
    *   $s_{x,i}, s_{y,i}, s_{z,i}$: Scales.
*   Camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the direction.
*   A list of Gaussians sorted front-to-back along the ray.

**Outputs:**
*   Pixel color $C(p)$ and accumulated opacity.

### Data Structures
The method uses the standard 3DGS Gaussian representation but modifies the density parameterization and alpha computation.
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i, c_i, \theta_i, s_{x,i}, s_{y,i}, s_{z,i}$.
*   **Precomputed Density:** $\kappa_i$ is derived from $\theta_i$ and scales (see Formula section).
*   **Sorting:** Gaussians must be sorted by their intersection distance with the ray or depth, ensuring front-to-back order.

### Formulas and Definitions
The volume rendering equation for a ray $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is:
$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_0^\infty T(0, t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) c(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt $$
where transmittance is:
$$ T(a, b) = \exp \left( - \int_a^b \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) ds \right) $$
The density field is a sum of weighted 3D Gaussians:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \kappa_i G_i(\mathbf{x}) $$
with the unnormalized 3D Gaussian:
$$ G_i(\mathbf{x}) = \exp \left\{ -\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i)^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i^{-1}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i) \right\} $$

**1. Reparameterization of Density $\kappa$**
To promote high density for small Gaussians and aid optimization, $\kappa$ is reparameterized using $\theta \in [0, 1]$:
$$ \kappa = -\log(1 - 0.99\theta) \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{1}{s_x} + \frac{1}{s_y} + \frac{1}{s_z} \right) $$
[S1]

**2. 1D Gaussian Projection along Ray**
For a specific Gaussian $j$, the 1D distribution $g_j(t)$ along the ray $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is derived by substituting the ray equation into the 3D Gaussian. The exponent argument $\Delta$ expands to:
$$ \Delta = (t\mathbf{d} - (\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o}))^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} (t\mathbf{d} - (\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o})) $$
$$ \Delta = t^2 \mathbf{d}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} \mathbf{d} - 2t(\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} \mathbf{d} + (\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} (\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o}) $$
This results in a 1D Gaussian form:
$$ g_j(t) = G_j(\gamma_j \mathbf{d}) \exp \left\{ -\frac{(t - \gamma_j)^2}{2\beta_j^2} \right\} $$
where the mean $\gamma_j$ and variance $\beta_j$ are:
$$ \gamma_j = \frac{(\boldsymbol{\mu}_j - \mathbf{o})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} \mathbf{d}}{\mathbf{d}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} \mathbf{d}}, \quad \beta_j = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbf{d}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j^{-1} \mathbf{d}}} $$
$G_j(\gamma_j \mathbf{d})$ is the maximum value of the Gaussian along the ray.
[S1]

**3. Analytic Transmittance $\overline{T}_j$**
The accumulated transmittance across Gaussian $j$ from entry $t_{jn}$ to exit $t_{jf}$ is:
$$ \overline{T}_j = \exp \left( - \int_{t_{jn}}^{t_{jf}} \kappa_j g_j(t) dt \right) $$
The integral of the 1D Gaussian is:
$$ \int_{t_{jn}}^{t_{jf}} g_j(t) dt = G_j(\gamma_j \mathbf{d}) \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}} \beta_j \left[ \text{erf} \left( \frac{t - \gamma_j}{\sqrt{2}\beta_j} \right) \right]_{t_{jn}}^{t_{jf}} $$
*Note: The source text provides the indefinite integral result as $\text{erf} \left( \frac{t - \gamma_j}{2\beta_j} \right)$ in Eq. 17, but standard Gaussian integration yields $\sqrt{2}\beta_j$ in the denominator. The source Eq. 19 assumes infinite support ($t_{jn} \to -\infty, t_{jf} \to \infty$), where the error function term becomes 1. The source formula for infinite support is:*
$$ \overline{T}_j = \exp \left( -\kappa_j G_j(\gamma_j \mathbf{d}) \sqrt{2\pi} \beta_j \right) $$
[S1]

**4. Alpha and Color Computation**
Assuming non-overlapping, sorted Gaussians, the opacity $\alpha_i$ of the $i$-th Gaussian is:
$$ \alpha_i = 1 - \overline{T}_i $$
The final pixel color is computed via alpha blending:
$$ C(p) = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
[S1]

### Pseudocode
```python
import math

def compute_analytic_alpha(mu, Sigma_inv, d, o, kappa, G_peak):
    """
    Computes the accumulated transmittance and alpha for a single 3D Gaussian
    along a ray, assuming infinite support for the analytic approximation.
    
    Args:
        mu: 3D mean vector (numpy array)
        Sigma_inv: Inverse 3D covariance matrix (numpy array)
        d: Ray direction vector (numpy array)
        o: Ray origin vector (numpy array)
        kappa: Density weight
        G_peak: Maximum value of the Gaussian along the ray, G(mu_proj)
        
    Returns:
        alpha: Accumulated opacity (1 - transmittance)
    """
    # Compute d^T * Sigma_inv * d
    # This is a scalar
    denom = d.dot(Sigma_inv.dot(d))
    
    # Avoid division by zero
    if denom < 1e-8:
        return 0.0
        
    # Compute beta_j = 1 / sqrt(d^T Sigma_inv d)
    beta_j = 1.0 / math.sqrt(denom)
    
    # Compute the exponent argument for transmittance:
    # exp_term = kappa * G_peak * sqrt(2 * pi) * beta_j
    # Note: Source Eq. 19 uses sqrt(2*pi). 
    # Standard integral of exp(-x^2/(2b^2)) is b*sqrt(2*pi).
    # Here the 1D Gaussian is G_peak * exp(-(t-gamma)^2 / (2*beta^2)).
    # Integral is G_peak * beta * sqrt(2*pi).
    
    exponent = kappa * G_peak * math.sqrt(2.0 * math.pi) * beta_j
    
    # Transmittance T = exp(-integral)
    transmittance = math.exp(-exponent)
    
    # Alpha = 1 - T
    alpha = 1.0 - transmittance
    
    return alpha

def render_pixel_analytic(ray_o, ray_d, gaussians_sorted):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using analytic transmittance.
    
    Args:
        ray_o: Ray origin
        ray_d: Ray direction (normalized)
        gaussians_sorted: List of Gaussian objects sorted front-to-back.
                          Each object has: mu, Sigma_inv, color, kappa, G_peak.
                          G_peak is precomputed as exp(-0.5 * (mu_proj - mu)^T Sigma_inv (mu_proj - mu))
                          where mu_proj is the projection of mu onto the ray.
                          
    Returns:
        color: Rendered RGB color
        alpha: Total accumulated alpha
    """
    accumulated_transmittance = 1.0
    final_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    
    for g in gaussians_sorted:
        # Compute alpha for this Gaussian
        alpha_i = compute_analytic_alpha(
            g.mu, g.Sigma_inv, ray_d, ray_o, g.kappa, g.G_peak
        )
        
        # Contribution: c_i * alpha_i * T_prev
        # T_prev is accumulated_transmittance
        contribution = [
            g.color[0] * alpha_i * accumulated_transmittance,
            g.color[1] * alpha_i * accumulated_transmittance,
            g.color[2] * alpha_i * accumulated_transmittance
        ]
        
        final_color[0] += contribution[0]
        final_color[1] += contribution[1]
        final_color[2] += contribution[2]
        
        # Update accumulated transmittance
        accumulated_transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha_i)
        
        # Early exit if fully opaque (optional optimization)
        if accumulated_transmittance < 1e-4:
            break
            
    return final_color, 1.0 - accumulated_transmittance
```

### Parameters
*   $\theta \in [0, 1]$: Learnable density parameter.
*   $s_x, s_y, s_z$: Scales of the Gaussian.
*   Constant $0.99$: Used in the log reparameterization to promote high density for small Gaussians [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Overlap and Sorting:** The method assumes Gaussians are sorted front-to-back and do not overlap along the ray. If these assumptions are violated, the separation of transmittance terms is invalid, leading to artifacts [S1].
*   **Infinite Support Approximation:** The analytic formula $\overline{T}_j = \exp(-\kappa_j G_j(\gamma_j \mathbf{d}) \sqrt{2\pi} \beta_j)$ assumes infinite support. For very small Gaussians or specific ray intersections, finite bounds might be more accurate, but the source uses the infinite approximation for consistency and speed [S1].
*   **Optimization Heuristics:** Standard 3DGS opacity reset is disabled. Density $\kappa$ is halved after splitting and cloning. Points with high $\kappa$ are split; points with low $\theta$ (density before reparameterization) are pruned [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Similar to 3DGS rasterization, as it avoids the overhead of 2D Gaussian projection and blending but adds the cost of computing $\gamma_j, \beta_j$ and the exponential/erf-like operations. The source reports 136 FPS on MipNeRF-360 with an Nvidia 3090-Ti, compared to 159 FPS for standard 3DGS [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Same as 3DGS, storing $N$ Gaussians with additional precomputed values if necessary.
*   **Performance:** On MipNeRF-360, the method achieves PSNR 27.30, SSIM 0.813, LPIPS 0.209 [S1]. It outperforms 3DGS in SSIM and LPIPS, particularly for opaque surfaces and piecewise constant textures [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Physically accurate transmittance, better representation of opaque surfaces, no self-occlusion artifacts from linearization, compatible with tomography pipelines [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on non-overlapping assumption; slightly slower than 3DGS due to analytic integration cost [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in "Volumetrically Consistent 3D Gaussian Rasterization" (2024) [S1]. It addresses the approximations in 3DGS splatting (linearization of exponential, no-self occlusion, linearization of covariance) by analytically integrating 3D Gaussians along rays. It builds on the volume rendering equation and the 3DGS representation, offering a more physically accurate alternative within a rasterization framework [S1].
