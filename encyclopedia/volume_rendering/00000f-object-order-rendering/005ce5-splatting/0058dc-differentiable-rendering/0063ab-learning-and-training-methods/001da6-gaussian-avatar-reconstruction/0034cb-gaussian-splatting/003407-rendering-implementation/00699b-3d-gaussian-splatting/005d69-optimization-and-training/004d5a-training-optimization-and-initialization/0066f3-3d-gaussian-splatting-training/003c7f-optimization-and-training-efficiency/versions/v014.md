# Optimization and Training Efficiency

Also called: Training Optimization, Training Efficiency

## Overview
Optimization and Training Efficiency in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) addresses the computational bottlenecks of differentiable rasterization and gradient accumulation during the training of large-scale scenes [6233-optimization-and-parallelization]. It encompasses strategies for accelerating the training loop through hardware-aware parallelism, memory management, and parameter-specific learning rate configurations [6233-optimization-and-parallelization], [4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md]. Unlike methods that modify the Gaussian representation or the rasterization core, this family focuses on the *mechanism* of computation and optimizer behavior to reduce latency and VRAM usage without sacrificing fidelity [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### Parameter-Specific Learning Rates
Parameter-Specific Learning Rates configures the Adam optimizer with distinct learning rates for each Gaussian attribute group (means, opacities, scales, colors) to stabilize training and prevent rapid divergence [4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md]. Unlike a uniform learning rate, this method assigns higher rates to attributes requiring aggressive adaptation (e.g., colors) and lower rates to stable geometric parameters (e.g., means), addressing the varying sensitivity of different Gaussian properties during differentiable rasterization [4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [CLM](7351-clm.md) or [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md) by focusing exclusively on the optimizer's parameter grouping strategy rather than modifying the rasterization core or representation density [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

### [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md)
Optimization and Parallelization refers to the strategies for accelerating the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its variants through hardware-aware parallelism and distributed computing. It solves the computational bottleneck of differentiable rasterization and gradient accumulation when scaling to large scenes or high-resolution datasets. It is distinguished from standard optimization by focusing on the *mechanism* of computation (e.g., per-splat vs. per-pixel backward passes, multi-GPU partitioning) rather than the *optimization algorithm* (e.g., Adam) or *representation* (e.g., densification) [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

### CLM
CLM is a training optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that eliminates the performance bottleneck of frequent GPU memory barriers by dynamically migrating Gaussian states between CPU and GPU memory [7351-clm.md]. It solves the problem of VRAM exhaustion and transfer latency during optimization by employing a smart caching policy that retains only the most frequently updated Gaussians on the GPU [7351-clm.md]. Unlike [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md) which focuses on kernel fusion, or [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md) which optimizes the compute graph, CLM optimizes data placement [7351-clm.md].

### FastGS
FastGS is a training optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that accelerates the optimization loop by re-engineering the rasterization and gradient computation pipelines [9177-fastgs.md]. It addresses the high computational cost of differentiable volume rendering by employing dynamic sparse rasterization to skip tiles with negligible transmittance and a fast gradient checkpointing scheme to reduce memory overhead during backpropagation [9177-fastgs.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [CLM](7351-clm.md) and [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md) by focusing on algorithmic sparsity and checkpointing efficiency rather than kernel fusion or representation compression [9177-fastgs.md].

### [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md)
Fused Backward and Optimizer is a training optimization technique for 3D Gaussian Splatting that integrates the Adam optimizer step directly into the backward pass of the differentiable rasterizer [9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md]. It solves the bottleneck where parameter updates consume 40–60% of training time by eliminating separate optimizer kernels and reducing VRAM usage through the avoidance of intermediate parameter buffers [9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md]. It differs from sibling methods like [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md) by prioritizing memory efficiency and correctness over maximum speed, whereas [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md) and [CLM](7351-clm.md) focus on skipping computations for invisible Gaussians to achieve higher speedups at the cost of potential quality degradation or inconsistency [9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md].

### Faster-GS
- **Definition**: Addresses oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters caused by a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio [S1, unofficial].
- **Step**: Rescales the scaling gradient with the inverse of the current scale to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial].
- **Impact**: Cuts training time by nearly half compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Reduced Iteration Training
- **Step**: Reduces training iterations from the original 30,000 to 1,000 for the Neural3DVideo dataset and to 1,500 for the LongVolumetricVideo dataset, keeping all other parameters identical to the original 3DGS implementation [S2].
- **Pitfall**: Using the original 30,000 iterations on sparse input data causes overfitting [S2].
- **Impact**: Reduces training time and improves novel view synthesis by approximately 4 PSNR [S2].

### Disk-Based Training Image Loading
- **Definition**: Loads ground truth images from disk every iteration instead of storing them in VRAM, to enable training on consumer hardware with limited VRAM for ultra-large-scale datasets [S3, unofficial].
- **Comparison**: Standard 3DGS stores the entire training dataset in VRAM for speed. This method saves VRAM but reduces training performance [S3, unofficial].

### Sampling Gap Control
- **Definition**: Controls the sampling rate by adjusting the sampling gap value to influence training speed and rendering quality [S4, unofficial].
- **Step**: Increases the sampling gap value to decrease the sampling rate, which is inversely proportional to the sampling rate [S4, unofficial].
- **Pitfall**: Increasing the sampling gap increases per-compute (pc) time and makes the rendering result blurrier [S4, unofficial].
- **Impact**: On the truck dataset with 3000 training steps, increasing the sampling gap from 2 to 500 increases pc time from 0.17739 to 0.20525 [S4, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. The initial implementation stored the entire training dataset in VRAM, which limited scalability to ultra-large scenes on consumer hardware [S3, unofficial]. Subsequent research focused on optimizing the training loop to reduce memory footprint and latency. Methods like CLM [7351-clm.md] and FastGS [9177-fastgs.md] addressed VRAM exhaustion and computation bottlenecks, respectively. Variants such as Faster-GS [S1, unofficial] and Reduced Iteration Training [S2] further refined convergence stability and training duration. Disk-based loading [S3, unofficial] and sampling gap control [S4, unofficial] were introduced to handle specific hardware constraints and dataset characteristics.
