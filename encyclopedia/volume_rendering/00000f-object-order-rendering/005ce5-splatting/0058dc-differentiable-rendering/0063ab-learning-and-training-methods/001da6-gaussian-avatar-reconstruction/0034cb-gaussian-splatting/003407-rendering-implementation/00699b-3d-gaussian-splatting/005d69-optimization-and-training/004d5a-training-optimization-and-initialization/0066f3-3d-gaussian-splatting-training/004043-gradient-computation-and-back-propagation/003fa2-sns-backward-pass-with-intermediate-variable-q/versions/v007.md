# SNS Backward Pass with Intermediate Variable Q

Also called: SNS Gradient Computation, Skew-Normal 2D Gradient Computation

## Overview
The SNS Backward Pass with Intermediate Variable Q computes exact gradients for 3D Skew-Normal Splatting (SNS) parameters by decoupling the complex interaction between rotation and scale matrices. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) which computes gradients with respect to the inverse covariance matrix $\Omega^{-1}$ before backpropagating to rotation $R$ and scale $S$, this method introduces an intermediate variable $Q = ARS$ to reformulate the 2D Skew-Normal density. This allows for direct, closed-form gradient computation of the skew parameter $k$, the mean $\mu$, and the shape matrix $Q$, avoiding the high computational complexity of differentiating the coupled $RS$ terms directly [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The backward pass follows the standard 3DGS architecture, divided into a **rendering stage** and a **preprocessing stage** [S1, unofficial]. The rendering stage evaluates shared auxiliary algebraic terms, and the preprocessing stage uses these terms to compute exact gradients for 3D learnable parameters using derived closed-form expressions [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Loss gradient w.r.t. rendered color: $\frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (per pixel).
    *   2D pixel coordinate: $u \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
    *   2D mean: $\mu^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
    *   Intermediate shape matrix: $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$, where $Q = ARS$ ($A$ is projection, $R$ rotation, $S$ scale).
    *   Skew parameter: $k \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
    *   Opacity: $o \in \mathbb{R}$.
    *   Color: $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Transmittance up to previous Gaussian: $T_{k-1} = \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} (1 - a_j(u))$.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Gradients: $\frac{\partial L}{\partial c}, \frac{\partial L}{\partial o}, \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu}, \frac{\partial L}{\partial Q}, \frac{\partial L}{\partial k}$.

### Data Structures and Definitions
Let $\Delta = u - \mu^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
Let $\Omega^{2D} = Q Q^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ be the 2D covariance matrix.
Let $G_2(\Delta; \Omega^{2D}) = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{\det(\Omega^{2D})}} \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \Delta^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta\right)$ be the standard 2D Gaussian component.
Let $\Phi(z) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 + \text{erf}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \right]$ be the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution.
Let $m = m^{2D} = \frac{(\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k}{\sqrt{1 + k^\top k - k^\top Q^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k}} \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
Let $D = \sqrt{1 + k^\top k - k^\top Q^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k}$.

The 2D Skew-Normal density is:
$$ SN^{2D}(u) = G_2(\Delta; \Omega^{2D}) \cdot \Phi(m^\top \Delta) $$
The opacity is $a(u) = o \cdot SN^{2D}(u)$.

### Auxiliary Variables
To simplify gradient expressions, four auxiliary variables are computed in the rendering stage [S1, unofficial]:

1.  $c_\Delta = \Phi \cdot G_2 \cdot (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta \in \mathbb{R}^2$
2.  $M_\Delta = \Phi \cdot G_2 \cdot ((\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta) ((\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$
3.  $s_\Delta = \frac{G_2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} (m^\top \Delta)^2 \right) \in \mathbb{R}$
4.  $p_\Delta = \frac{G_2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} (m^\top \Delta)^2 \right) \cdot (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta \in \mathbb{R}^2$

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Compute Transmittance and Opacity Gradient:**
    Calculate the contribution of the current Gaussian to the loss. Let $\frac{\partial L}{\partial a}$ be the gradient of the loss with respect to opacity $a$.
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial a} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot c \cdot T_{k-1} $$
    (Note: $\frac{\partial L}{\partial C}$ is a vector, $c$ is a vector. This is a dot product if scalar loss, or element-wise if vector loss. Standard 3DGS assumes scalar loss per pixel or batch, resulting in a scalar $\frac{\partial L}{\partial a}$).

2.  **Compute Gradient w.r.t. Skew-Normal Density:**
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial SN^{2D}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a} \cdot o $$

3.  **Compute Gradient w.r.t. Mean $\mu^{2D}$:**
    Using the auxiliary variables $c_\Delta$ and $s_\Delta$:
    $$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial \mu^{2D}} = c_\Delta - s_\Delta \cdot m $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu^{2D}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial \mu^{2D}} $$
    Note: Since $\Delta = u - \mu^{2D}$, $\frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial \mu^{2D}} = -I$. The derivation in [S1, unofficial] accounts for this sign in the final expression $c_\Delta - s_\Delta m$.

4.  **Compute Gradient w.r.t. Skew Parameter $k$:**
    Using auxiliary variables $p_\Delta$, $m$, $k$, $Q$, $\Omega^{2D}$, and scalar $D$:
    $$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial k} = \frac{1}{D} Q^\top \left( p_\Delta + (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m \right) - \frac{1}{D^2} \cdot (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot k $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial k} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial k} $$

5.  **Compute Gradient w.r.t. Shape Matrix $Q$:**
    Using auxiliary variables $M_\Delta$, $p_\Delta$, $m$, $k$, $Q$, and scalar $D$:
    $$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial Q} = \left( M_\Delta - p_\Delta m^\top - m p_\Delta^\top - (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m m^\top \right) Q + \frac{1}{D} \left( p_\Delta + (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m \right) k^\top $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial Q} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial Q} $$

6.  **Compute Gradient w.r.t. Color $c$ and Opacity $o$:**
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial c} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot a(u) \cdot T_{k-1} $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial o} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot SN^{2D}(u) \cdot T_{k-1} $$

7.  **Map $Q$ Gradients to $R$ and $S$ (Preprocessing Stage):**
    Since $Q = ARS$, and $A$ is constant, gradients w.r.t $R$ and $S$ are derived from $\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q}$.
    Let $\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q}$ be the computed gradient.
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial S} = A^\top \frac{\partial L}{\partial Q} R^\top $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial R} = A^\top \frac{\partial L}{\partial Q} S^\top $$
    (Note: The exact mapping depends on the parameterization of $R$ and $S$. If $R$ is a quaternion or axis-angle, further chain rule steps are required. The source [S1, unofficial] states gradients for $R$ and $S$ are computed via Eq. 25, 28, 38 logic, but explicitly defines $Q=ARS$. The gradient $\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q}$ is the primary output of the SNS-specific backward pass).

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_sns_gradients(
    grad_L_C,       # Shape (3,) or (N, 3) - Gradient of Loss w.r.t Rendered Color
    c,              # Shape (3,) - Color
    o,              # Scalar - Opacity
    u,              # Shape (2,) - Pixel coordinate
    mu_2d,          # Shape (2,) - 2D Mean
    Q,              # Shape (2, 2) - Intermediate shape matrix ARS
    k,              # Shape (2,) - Skew parameter
    T_prev          # Scalar - Transmittance from previous Gaussians
):
    """
    Computes gradients for SNS backward pass.
    Returns: grad_c, grad_o, grad_mu, grad_Q, grad_k
    """
    
    # 1. Basic quantities
    Delta = u - mu_2d  # Shape (2,)
    Omega_2D = Q @ Q.T # Shape (2, 2)
    Omega_inv = np.linalg.inv(Omega_2D)
    
    # Gaussian component G2
    # det(Omega_2D)
    det_Omega = np.linalg.det(Omega_2D)
    exponent = -0.5 * (Delta @ Omega_inv @ Delta)
    G2 = (1.0 / (2.0 * np.pi * np.sqrt(det_Omega))) * np.exp(exponent)
    
    # Skew term m
    # m = (Omega_inv @ Q @ k) / D
    numerator_m = Omega_inv @ Q @ k
    D_squared = 1.0 + k.T @ k - k.T @ Q.T @ Omega_inv @ Q @ k
    D = np.sqrt(D_squared)
    m = numerator_m / D
    
    # Phi(m^T Delta)
    z = m.T @ Delta
    Phi = 0.5 * (1.0 + erf(z / np.sqrt(2.0)))
    
    # 2. Auxiliary Variables
    # c_delta = Phi * G2 * Omega_inv @ Delta
    c_delta = Phi * G2 * (Omega_inv @ Delta)
    
    # M_delta = Phi * G2 * (Omega_inv @ Delta) @ (Omega_inv @ Delta).T
    v = Omega_inv @ Delta
    M_delta = Phi * G2 * np.outer(v, v)
    
    # s_delta = (G2 / sqrt(2pi)) * exp(-0.5 * (m^T Delta)^2)
    s_delta = (G2 / np.sqrt(2.0 * np.pi)) * np.exp(-0.5 * z**2)
    
    # p_delta = s_delta * Omega_inv @ Delta
    p_delta = s_delta * v
    
    # 3. Gradient w.r.t SN2D
    # a = o * SN2D
    # dL/da = dL/dC . c * T_prev
    # Assuming grad_L_C is (3,) and c is (3,), dot product.
    # If batched, this needs adjustment. Assuming single pixel for this function.
    dL_da = np.dot(grad_L_C, c) * T_prev
    dL_dSN2D = dL_da * o
    
    # 4. Gradient w.r.t mu_2d
    # dSN2D/dmu = c_delta - s_delta * m
    dSN2D_dmu = c_delta - s_delta * m
    grad_mu = dL_dSN2D * dSN2D_dmu
    
    # 5. Gradient w.r.t k
    # term1 = p_delta + (p_delta^T Omega_2D m) * m
    term_p_Om_m = np.dot(p_delta, Omega_2D @ m)
    term1 = p_delta + term_p_Om_m * m
    
    # grad_k = (1/D) * Q.T @ term1 - (1/D^2) * term_p_Om_m * k
    grad_k = (1.0 / D) * (Q.T @ term1) - (1.0 / D**2) * term_p_Om_m * k
    grad_k = dL_dSN2D * grad_k
    
    # 6. Gradient w.r.t Q
    # term_mm = (p_delta^T Omega_2D m) * m @ m.T
    term_mm = term_p_Om_m * np.outer(m, m)
    
    # Matrix part: (M_delta - p_delta @ m.T - m @ p_delta.T - term_mm) @ Q
    matrix_part = M_delta - np.outer(p_delta, m) - np.outer(m, p_delta) - term_mm
    grad_Q_part1 = matrix_part @ Q
    
    # Vector part: (1/D) * (p_delta + term_p_Om_m * m) @ k.T
    grad_Q_part2 = (1.0 / D) * np.outer(term1, k)
    
    grad_Q = dL_dSN2D * (grad_Q_part1 + grad_Q_part2)
    
    # 7. Gradient w.r.t c and o
    SN2D_val = G2 * Phi
    a_val = o * SN2D_val
    
    grad_c = dL_da * T_prev # dL/dC * a * T_prev / c? No.
    # C = sum c_i a_i T_{i-1}
    # dL/dc_i = dL/dC * a_i * T_{i-1}
    # dL_da = dL/dC . c * T_{i-1}
    # So dL/dc = dL/dC * a * T_{i-1}
    grad_c = grad_L_C * a_val * T_prev
    
    grad_o = grad_L_C * SN2D_val * T_prev
    
    return grad_c, grad_o, grad_mu, grad_Q, grad_k

def erf(x):
    """Error function approximation for numpy arrays."""
    return special.erf(x) # Requires scipy.special or implement manually
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Singularity in $D$:** If $D \approx 0$, the division by $D$ and $D^2$ in gradients for $k$ and $Q$ becomes unstable. This occurs when $1 + k^\top k - k^\top Q^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k \approx 0$. This implies the skew parameter is extremely large relative to the covariance, potentially leading to degenerate skew-normal distributions. A small epsilon should be added to $D$ or $D^2$ for numerical stability [G].
*   **Covariance Inversion:** $(\Omega^{2D})^{-1}$ must be computed stably. If $\Omega^{2D}$ is near-singular (e.g., scale is very small), use a regularized inverse or SVD-based inversion [G].
*   **Sign of $\mu$ Gradient:** The derivation $\frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial \mu^{2D}} = -I$ is critical. The final formula $c_\Delta - s_\Delta m$ already incorporates this sign. Implementers must ensure they do not negate it again.
*   **Batching:** The pseudocode assumes a single pixel. For batched rendering, operations must be vectorized (e.g., using `np.einsum` or batched matrix multiplications).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The auxiliary variables require $O(1)$ operations per Gaussian per pixel (matrix inversions are $2 \times 2$). The gradient computations are also $O(1)$. This is comparable to standard 3DGS but avoids the iterative or complex chain-rule steps for $R$ and $S$ inversion.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing auxiliary variables $c_\Delta, M_\Delta, s_\Delta, p_\Delta$ per Gaussian per pixel during the rendering stage. This is slightly higher than standard 3DGS but allows for efficient reuse in the preprocessing stage.
*   **Strengths:** Decouples $R$ and $S$ optimization, leading to more stable gradients for shape parameters. Provides closed-form solutions for skew parameter $k$, which is absent in standard 3DGS.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of the skew parameter $k$ and the intermediate variable $Q$. The complexity of the gradient expressions for $Q$ is higher than standard 3DGS covariance gradients.

## Background
The SNS (Skew-Normal Splatting) method was introduced to address the limitations of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting in modeling asymmetric shapes. Standard 3DGS uses symmetric 2D Gaussians, which cannot capture skewness. The SNS method introduces a 2D Skew-Normal distribution. The backward pass with intermediate variable $Q$ was proposed to simplify the gradient computation for the shape parameters (rotation and scale) which are coupled in the covariance matrix. This approach was detailed in the paper "3D Skew-Normal Splatting" [S1, unofficial].
