# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, 3DGS SLAM, Gaussian Splatting SLAM

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the simultaneous estimation of camera poses and the construction of a 3D scene representation using Gaussian primitives or hybrid neural fields, enabling real-time novel view synthesis and localization [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike traditional SLAM systems that rely on sparse feature points or dense depth maps, 3DGS-based methods leverage the differentiable rendering of Gaussians to optimize geometry and appearance jointly [S1]. This family is distinguished from sibling methods like [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by its integration of tracking loops, loop closure, and incremental mapping strategies required for continuous operation, rather than offline batch training.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS SLAM involves a coupled optimization loop where camera poses and Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) are updated simultaneously or alternately.

1.  **Representation:** The scene is represented as a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_i$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (parameterized by a rotation matrix $R_i$ and a scaling vector $s_i$), an opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ for view-dependent color [S1].
2.  **Rendering:** The image is rendered via differentiable rasterization. For each pixel, the Gaussians are sorted by depth, and alpha compositing is applied:
    $$ C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $\mathcal{N}$ is the set of Gaussians projected onto the pixel [S1].
3.  **Optimization:** The system minimizes a loss function $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I_{gt}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{rec}(\hat{I}, I_{gt}) + \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{rec}$ is typically a combination of L1 loss and SSIM [S2, unofficial]. Gradients are backpropagated to update both the Gaussian parameters and the camera pose $T$ [S1].
4.  **Densification:** To maintain detail, new Gaussians are spawned from existing ones based on gradient magnitude or depth uncertainty, and redundant Gaussians are pruned [S1]. This process is adapted for SLAM to handle dynamic scenes and incremental updates.

## Subtopics

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scene representations, typically for tasks such as 3D editing, style transfer, or multi-scene fusion. Unlike standard SLAM mapping which updates a single scene's geometry and appearance, these strategies solve the problem of transferring visual attributes (density, radiance, color) from a source scene to a target scene while maintaining structural fidelity. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md) by their focus on explicit pixel-to-pixel or region-to-region correspondence algorithms rather than high-level semantic label propagation. See [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md).

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation is a method for implicitly encoding a 3D scene as a continuous volumetric function using a neural network, enabling high-fidelity novel view synthesis by differentiating through the volume rendering equation. It differs from explicit representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting by storing scene geometry and appearance within a compact network weight set rather than millions of discrete primitives, trading higher memory usage for implicit smoothness and avoiding the floating artifacts associated with sparse point clouds. See [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md).

### CG-SLAM
CG-SLAM improves reconstruction quality by introducing an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1]. This uncertainty guides the densification and optimization steps, allowing the system to focus resources on areas with high geometric ambiguity.

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
This approach combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers camera poses, and 3DGS is trained using these poses and sensor depth from keyframes, effectively performing offline splatting [S2, unofficial]. On the EuRoC Machine Hall dataset with stereo depth, the combined approach achieved an ATE RMSE of 3.190 m, compared to 0.052 m for ORB-SLAM3. While ORB-SLAM provides superior tracking accuracy, the rendering metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) show no significant difference compared to unified 3DGS SLAM methods. However, the unified approach is preferred for exploring the full capabilities of 3DGS within the algorithm [S2, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
CP-SLAM is a neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system that enables multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure [S3]. It uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization. It employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly. The system integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S3].

### MNE-SLAM
MNE-SLAM is the first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange [S3]. It uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module. It introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss. It also introduces the INS dataset for benchmarking [S3].

### HERO-SLAM
HERO-SLAM employs a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings [S3]. It uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning. It demonstrates superior performance and robustness over prior implicit field-based SLAM methods, especially under challenging conditions [S3].

### PIN-SLAM
PIN-SLAM is a globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation [S3]. It replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure. Mapping alternates with odometry. The mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S3].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
This algorithm enables autonomous 3D reconstruction by actively selecting next-best views. It initializes a 3D bounding box and samples goal-state candidates. A global policy selects the next-best view $s^*_g$ using the criterion:
$$ s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g)) $$
where $x_{s_g}$ is the 3D location of the candidate [S4, unofficial]. A local policy optimizes the trajectory. The agent moves, observes, and updates the database. The mapping module (DVGO model) is optimized by sampling pixels from the database, synchronizing the entropy volume. Exploration stops if the change in entropy volume or the maximum information gain falls below a threshold. A fine stage then refines the model using stochastic training [S4, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
This method decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison [S5]. Camera pose is parameterized as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$. The tracking loss is:
$$ L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D| $$
where $\rho$ converts RGB to LAB, $C$ and $\hat{C}$ are input and rendered images, and $D$ and $\hat{D}$ are input and rendered depths. The weights are $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$ and $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$ [S5].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced as a real-time explicit scene representation method [S1]. Its application to SLAM emerged as a natural extension, leveraging the differentiable nature of Gaussian splatting for joint pose and map optimization. Early works combined traditional SLAM backends with 3DGS renderers, while later approaches developed unified frameworks that integrate tracking, mapping, and loop closure directly within the Gaussian optimization loop [S2, unofficial]. Hybrid neural field approaches (NeRF-based SLAM) also evolved in parallel, offering implicit representations that complement explicit Gaussian methods [S3]. The field has expanded to include multi-agent systems, active reconstruction, and robust tracking under varying lighting conditions [S3][S4, unofficial][S5].
