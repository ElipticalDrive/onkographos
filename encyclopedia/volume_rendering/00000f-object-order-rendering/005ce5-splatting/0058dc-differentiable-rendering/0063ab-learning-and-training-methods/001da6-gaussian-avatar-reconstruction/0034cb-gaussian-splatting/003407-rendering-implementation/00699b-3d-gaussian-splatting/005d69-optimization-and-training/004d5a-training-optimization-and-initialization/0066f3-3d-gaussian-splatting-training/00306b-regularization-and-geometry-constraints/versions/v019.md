# Regularization and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time optimization techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that improve geometric fidelity, surface smoothness, and visual quality by constraining the shape, orientation, and frequency content of Gaussian primitives [S1, S2]. Unlike standard photometric losses that only minimize pixel-wise color differences, these methods explicitly penalize geometric inconsistencies, over-reconstruction, and high-frequency artifacts, ensuring that the learned Gaussians align with physical surface properties rather than merely fitting appearance [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of stabilizing the optimization of 3D Gaussians by introducing constraints beyond the standard L1/L2 photometric loss. These constraints typically operate on one or more of the following aspects:
1.  **Geometric Consistency:** Aligning Gaussian orientations (normals) and shapes (covariances) with estimated surface properties derived from depth maps or rendered normals [S1, S2].
2.  **Frequency Control:** Managing the spectral content of the rendered image to prevent overfitting to high-frequency noise or underfitting in detail-rich regions [S2][S4, unofficial].
3.  **Depth Supervision:** Using monocular or multi-view depth estimates as pseudo-ground truth to guide the spatial placement and scale of Gaussians, often decoupling depth fitting from color fitting to preserve detail [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction. It increases pixel gradients in over-reconstructed areas by disentangling amplitude and phase, enabling adaptive densification toward high-frequency regions.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision stabilizes joint optimization of camera poses and scene representation by blurring 2D training images with scheduled Gaussian kernels. It solves noisy gradients and high-frequency overfitting during early training, restricting the spectrum of the learned scene.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method modifies the covariance matrix to enforce a "half-Gaussian" shape, cutting the ellipsoid along the local surface normal. It reduces "ghosting" artifacts and improves geometric fidelity in regions with sharp transitions, differing from siblings by explicitly altering kernel support geometry.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
This strategy prevents geometric detail loss by decoupling coarse depth fitting from fine color appearance. It freezes shape parameters (scale/rotation) and encourages spatial reshaping through independent updates to centers and opacities, addressing the conflict where anisotropic Gaussians conform to smooth depth maps.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
A self-adaptive method using reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error as pseudo-labels, suppressing floaters and improving convergence by relying on multi-scale consistency.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Improves geometric fidelity by constraining the shape and orientation of Gaussian kernels, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains spatial location. It directly manipulates the covariance structure (via flattening) and enforces alignment between rendered geometric cues and Gaussian primitives.

### FreGS
- [S4, unofficial] Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training.

## Background
The need for regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the fact that standard optimization focuses on photometric loss, often resulting in unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces [S3]. Early methods like FreGS [S4, unofficial] and ABSGS  addressed high-frequency artifacts and fine detail recovery. Subsequent works introduced geometric priors, such as SDF regularization [S3] and depth-guided sampling [S2], to enforce physical constraints. Wild-GS [S1] and others further refined this by integrating visibility maps and depth regularization to handle transient effects. The field has evolved from simple frequency smoothing to complex multi-scale geometric adaptation and half-Gaussian covariance transformations to achieve high-fidelity, geometrically consistent reconstructions [S1, S2, S3][S4, unofficial].
