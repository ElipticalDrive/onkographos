# Optimization and Training Efficiency

Also called: Training Optimization, 3DGS Training Efficiency

## Overview
This family encompasses the computational strategies, memory management techniques, and hyperparameter configurations required to efficiently train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models at scale. It addresses the high memory and compute demands of differentiable rasterization by optimizing gradient computation granularity, distributing parameters across memory hierarchies, and refining learning rate schedules. It sets itself apart from standard optimization by focusing on architectural parallelization, memory-bound bottlenecks, and specific attribute sensitivities rather than solely on loss function design.

## Shared principles
The training process relies on a differentiable rasterizer that renders images from a set of 3D Gaussians. The core optimization mechanism involves computing the gradient of a loss function $\mathcal{L}$ (e.g., L1, SSIM) with respect to the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color, SH coefficients) and any auxiliary parameters.

Key shared mathematical components include:
1.  **Alpha Blending**: The color contribution of a Gaussian $i$ is modulated by its opacity $\alpha_i$ and transmittance $T_i$ from preceding Gaussians.
2.  **Gradient Propagation**: Gradients flow from the loss through the pixel color, through the alpha-blending chain, to the individual Gaussian attributes.
3.  **Parallelization Granularity**: Computation is parallelized across pixels, splats (Gaussians), or batches of views. The standard approach is per-pixel backpropagation, but alternatives exist.
4.  **Memory Management**: Large scenes require managing the Gaussian list in memory, potentially across multiple GPUs or via out-of-core techniques.

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training > Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md)
[Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md) configures the Adam optimizer to handle multiple parameter groups, each with its own learning rate. It stabilizes training by preventing parameters with high sensitivity (such as color or scale) from diverging due to large gradients, while allowing slower-converging parameters (such as position) to update appropriately. Choose this when standard uniform learning rates cause instability or slow convergence in specific attribute groups.

### [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md)
[Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md) encompasses the computational strategies required to efficiently train 3DGS models at scale. It addresses high memory and compute demands by optimizing gradient computation granularity, distributing parameters across multiple GPUs, and refining gradient paths for auxiliary parameters. Choose this when scaling to large scenes or multi-GPU setups.

### [CLM](7351-clm.md)
[CLM](7351-clm.md) removes the GPU memory barrier by moving Gaussian states between CPU and GPU memory during optimization. It employs a smart caching policy to keep the most frequently updated Gaussians on the GPU. Choose this when GPU VRAM is insufficient to hold the entire Gaussian dataset.

### [FastGS](9177-fastgs.md)
[FastGS](9177-fastgs.md) re-engineers the training loop to accelerate optimization by combining dynamic sparse rasterization with fast gradient checkpointing. It sets itself apart by skipping tiles with negligible transmittance during rasterization and recomputing only necessary intermediate values during the backward pass. Choose this when memory bandwidth or VRAM limits training speed.

### [Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md)
[Fused Backward and Optimizer](9597-fused-backward-and-optimizer.md) integrates the parameter update step of the optimizer directly into the backward pass of the differentiable rasterizer. It solves the problem of high VRAM usage and computational overhead associated with separate optimizer kernels by eliminating the need for intermediate buffers to store parameter states during the gradient computation phase. Choose this to reduce memory footprint and kernel launch overhead.

### Faster-GS
- **Definition**: Addresses oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters caused by a poorly conditioned learning rate ratio [S1, unofficial].
- **Step**: Rescales the scaling gradient with the inverse of the current scale to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial].
- **Impact**: Cuts training time by nearly half compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Reduced Iteration Training
- **Step**: Reduce training iterations from the original 30,000 to 1,000 for the Neural3DVideo dataset and to 1,500 for the LongVolumetricVideo dataset [S2]. Keep all other parameters identical to the original 3DGS implementation [S2].
- **Pitfall**: Using the original 30,000 iterations on sparse input data causes overfitting [S2].
- **Impact**: Reduces training time and improves novel view synthesis by approximately 4 PSNR [S2].

### Disk-Based Training Image Loading
- **Definition**: Loading ground truth images from disk every iteration instead of storing them in VRAM, to enable training on consumer hardware with limited VRAM for ultra-large-scale datasets [S3, unofficial].
- **Comparison**: Standard 3DGS stores the entire training dataset in VRAM for speed. This method loads images from disk every iteration, saving VRAM but reducing training performance [S3, unofficial].

### Sampling Gap Control
- **Definition**: Controlling the sampling rate by adjusting the sampling gap value to influence training speed and rendering quality [S4, unofficial].
- **Step**: Increase the sampling gap value to decrease the sampling rate, which is inversely proportional to the sampling rate [S4, unofficial].
- **Pitfall**: Increasing the sampling gap increases pc time and makes the rendering result blurrier [S4, unofficial].
- **Impact**: On the truck dataset with 3000 training steps, increasing the sampling gap from 2 to 500 increases pc time from 0.17739 to 0.20525 [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting training has evolved from the initial implementation which stored the entire training dataset in VRAM [S3, unofficial]. Early challenges included oscillation in gradient updates for scaling and rotation parameters, addressed by methods like Faster-GS [S1, unofficial]. As datasets grew to ultra-large scales, memory constraints led to techniques like CLM and disk-based loading [S3, unofficial]. Concurrently, efficiency improvements such as FastGS and fused backward passes were developed to mitigate the computational overhead of differentiable rasterization [S1, unofficial]. The reduction of training iterations for specific sparse datasets, such as Neural3DVideo, has also been shown to prevent overfitting while improving quality [S2].
