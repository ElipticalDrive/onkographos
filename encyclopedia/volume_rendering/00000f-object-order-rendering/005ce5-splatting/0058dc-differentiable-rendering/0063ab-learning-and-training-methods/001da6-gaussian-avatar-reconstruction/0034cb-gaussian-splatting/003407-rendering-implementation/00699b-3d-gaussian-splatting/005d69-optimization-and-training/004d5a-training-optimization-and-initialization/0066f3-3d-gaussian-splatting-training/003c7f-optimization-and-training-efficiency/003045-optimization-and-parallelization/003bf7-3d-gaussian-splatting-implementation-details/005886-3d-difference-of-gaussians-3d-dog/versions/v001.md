# 3D Difference-of-Gaussians (3D-DoG)

Also called: 3D Difference-of-Gaussians (3D-DoG) primitive, 3D-DoG

## Overview
3D Difference-of-Gaussians (3D-DoG) is a volumetric primitive variant designed to enhance the representation of fine geometric details and edges in compact 3D Gaussian Splatting models [S1, unofficial]. It solves the limitation of standard smooth Gaussian kernels, which struggle to capture high-frequency details when the number of primitives is aggressively reduced [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussians that only contribute positive density, the 3D-DoG models both positive and negative spatial responses, allowing it to implement color subtraction in $\alpha$-blending to sharpen boundaries [S1, unofficial]. It is distinct from sibling optimization techniques like [DeSplat](12925-desplat.md) or [TurboGS](15697-turbogs.md) by introducing a specific mathematical primitive structure rather than altering the rendering pipeline or training schedule directly.

## How it works
The 3D-DoG primitive is defined as the difference between a primary Gaussian $G(\mathbf{x})$ and a pseudo-Gaussian $G_p(\mathbf{x})$ [S1, unofficial]. The primary Gaussian retains standard parameters, while the pseudo-Gaussian shares the same center coordinates but has modified opacity and scale parameters [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** The parameters of the primary 3D Gaussian $\mathbf{G}$ (center $\mathbf{c}$, rotation $\mathbf{R}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, opacity $\alpha$, color $\mathbf{c}_{color}$) and the new learnable parameters for the pseudo-Gaussian.
*   **Output:** A composite primitive that renders with a positive-density lobe and a negative-density ring, effectively subtracting color from overlapping regions to enhance contrast [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The 3D-DoG primitive extends the standard 3D Gaussian parameter set with four additional learnable parameters per primitive [S1, unofficial]:
1.  Opacity factor: $f_\alpha$
2.  Scaling factors: $f_{xs}, f_{ys}, f_{zs}$

These parameters are typically initialized to values less than 1.0 [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
The 3D-DoG primitive $\text{DoG}(\mathbf{x})$ is calculated as [S1, unofficial]:

$$
\text{DoG}(\mathbf{x}) = G(\mathbf{x}) - G_p(\mathbf{x})
$$

Where $G(\mathbf{x})$ is the primary Gaussian and $G_p(\mathbf{x})$ is the pseudo-Gaussian [S1, unofficial].

The pseudo-Gaussian $G_p(\mathbf{x})$ shares the same center and rotation as $G(\mathbf{x})$ but uses a different scaling matrix $\mathbf{S}_p$ and opacity $\alpha_p$ [S1, unofficial].

The scaling matrix $\mathbf{S}_p$ is calculated by element-wise multiplication of the primary Gaussian's scaling matrix and the transpose of the pseudo-Gaussian's scaling factors matrix [S1, unofficial]:

$$
\mathbf{S}_p = \begin{bmatrix} s_x & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & s_y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & s_z \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} f_{xs} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & f_{ys} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & f_{zs} \end{bmatrix}^T
$$

Note: Since the scaling factors matrix is diagonal, the transpose operation does not change the matrix structure, but the formula explicitly denotes the element-wise product of the primary scales and the factors [S1, unofficial].

The opacity $\alpha_p$ of the pseudo-Gaussian is calculated as [S1, unofficial]:

$$
\alpha_p = f_\alpha \cdot \alpha
$$

Where $\alpha$ is the opacity of the primary Gaussian [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   $f_\alpha$: Opacity factor for the pseudo-Gaussian. Set to values less than 1.0 [S1, unofficial].
*   $f_{xs}, f_{ys}, f_{zs}$: Scaling factors for the pseudo-Gaussian. Set to values less than 1.0 [S1, unofficial].
*   These four parameters are learnable during optimization [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Pruning Phase:** Perform standard 3D Gaussian densification and pruning. The 3D-DoG is **not** introduced during initial densification [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Activation:** After the pruning process finishes, activate 3D-DoG primitives in the compact model [S1, unofficial]. This is done by enabling the parameters $[f_{xs}, f_{ys}, f_{zs}]$ and $\alpha_p$ for all primitives [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Optimization:** Continue training with the same loss functions. The primary Gaussian and pseudo-Gaussian parameters are jointly optimized [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Density Control:** Evaluate $\alpha_p$ across all primitives to identify those with the lowest values, which indicate marginal impact of the pseudo-Gaussian representation [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Degeneration:** If $\alpha_p$ falls below a predefined threshold (or reaches 0), the 3D-DoG degenerates into a standard 3D Gaussian, as the negative component vanishes [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Proportion Optimization:** Optimize the overall proportion of 3D-DoG primitives in the point cloud after pruning [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
class Gaussian3DDoG:
    def __init__(self, primary_gaussian, f_alpha, f_xs, f_ys, f_zs):
        """
        Initialize a 3D-DoG primitive.
        
        Args:
            primary_gaussian: Object containing standard 3D Gaussian params
                               (center, rotation, scale, opacity, color)
            f_alpha: Opacity factor for pseudo-Gaussian (< 1.0)
            f_xs, f_ys, f_zs: Scaling factors for pseudo-Gaussian (< 1.0)
        """
        self.primary = primary_gaussian
        self.f_alpha = f_alpha
        self.f_xs = f_xs
        self.f_ys = f_ys
        self.f_zs = f_zs
        
        # Calculate pseudo-Gaussian parameters
        # Sp = diag(sx, sy, sz) * diag(fxs, fys, fzs)^T
        # Since diagonal, this is element-wise multiplication
        self.pseudo_scale_x = primary_gaussian.scale_x * f_xs
        self.pseudo_scale_y = primary_gaussian.scale_y * f_ys
        self.pseudo_scale_z = primary_gaussian.scale_z * f_zs
        
        # alpha_p = f_alpha * alpha
        self.pseudo_opacity = f_alpha * primary_gaussian.opacity
        
        # Pseudo-Gaussian shares center and rotation with primary
        self.pseudo_center = primary_gaussian.center
        self.pseudo_rotation = primary_gaussian.rotation

    def evaluate(self, x):
        """
        Evaluate the 3D-DoG primitive at point x.
        DoG(x) = G(x) - Gp(x)
        """
        # Evaluate primary Gaussian G(x)
        g_primary = self.primary.evaluate(x)
        
        # Evaluate pseudo-Gaussian Gp(x)
        # Gp uses same center/rotation but different scale and opacity
        g_pseudo = Gaussian3D(
            center=self.pseudo_center,
            rotation=self.pseudo_rotation,
            scale=(self.pseudo_scale_x, self.pseudo_scale_y, self.pseudo_scale_z),
            opacity=self.pseudo_opacity,
            color=self.primary.color # Color is typically shared or derived
        ).evaluate(x)
        
        return g_primary - g_pseudo

    def is_standard_gaussian(self):
        """
        Check if the 3D-DoG has degenerated to a standard Gaussian.
        This occurs when alpha_p is effectively 0.
        """
        return self.pseudo_opacity <= 0.0
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degeneration:** When $\alpha_p = 0$, the 3D-DoG becomes equivalent to a standard 3D Gaussian [S1, unofficial]. This is a valid state and should be handled by the renderer to avoid unnecessary computation of the negative component.
*   **Negative Density:** The negative-density component implements color subtraction in $\alpha$-blending [S1, unofficial]. Implementers must ensure the rendering pipeline supports negative opacity contributions or subtracts colors appropriately during compositing.
*   **Activation Timing:** 3D-DoG is only introduced after the pruning phase, not during initial densification [S1, unofficial]. Activating it too early may destabilize training or lead to suboptimal primitive distribution.
*   **Computational Overhead:** 3D-DoG introduces additional computational overhead compared to standard Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. It is recommended for compact models where the benefit of sharper details outweighs the cost.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Increases per-primitive rendering cost due to the evaluation of two Gaussians (primary and pseudo) and their subtraction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Adds 4 learnable parameters ($f_\alpha, f_{xs}, f_{ys}, f_{zs}$) per primitive [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Improves reconstruction quality for fine details and edges in compact models [S1, unofficial]. It allows sharper structures to be preserved under aggressive pruning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances boundary and texture representation; more responsive to fine geometric details than standard Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Higher computational cost; not beneficial for smooth regions where standard Gaussians suffice; requires careful tuning of the pruning schedule to activate at the right time [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** In compact 3D Gaussian Splatting models where memory is constrained but high-frequency detail preservation is critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** In initial training phases or when the model is not being aggressively pruned, as the overhead may not be justified [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The 3D Difference-of-Gaussians (3D-DoG) primitive was introduced in the paper "Prune Wisely, Reconstruct Sharply: Compact 3D Gaussian Splatting via Adaptive Pruning and Difference-of-Gaussian Primitives" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of compact 3D Gaussian Splatting, where standard smooth Gaussian kernels fail to capture fine details after aggressive pruning [S1, unofficial]. The method is part of a broader framework that includes a Reconstruction-aware Pruning Scheduler (RPS) and Spatio-spectral Pruning Score (SPS) [S1, unofficial].
