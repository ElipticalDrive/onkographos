# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM, 3DGS SLAM

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) integrates simultaneous localization and mapping with the explicit, differentiable representation of 3D Gaussians. It solves the problem of reconstructing a 3D scene and estimating camera poses simultaneously in real-time, leveraging the fast rendering and gradient-based optimization capabilities of 3DGS [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike traditional SLAM methods that rely on sparse feature points or dense depth maps, or NeRF-based SLAM methods that use implicit neural fields, 3DGS-based SLAM explicitly optimizes a set of 3D Gaussian primitives for geometry and appearance while jointly optimizing camera poses [S1][S2, unofficial]. This approach offers real-time rendering speeds and flexible reprojection, leading to better reconstruction quality compared to NeRF-based methods [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS SLAM involves a coupled optimization loop where camera poses and Gaussian parameters are updated iteratively.

1.  **Representation:** The scene is represented as a collection of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by:
    *   Mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Covariance matrix $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, parameterized by a rotation matrix $R_i$ and a scaling vector $s_i$ such that $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T$, where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$.
    *   Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ for view-dependent color [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Rendering:** Given a camera pose $T$ (rotation $R_c$ and translation $t_c$), each 3D Gaussian is projected to 2D. The 2D covariance $\Sigma'$ is computed using the camera transformation and the Jacobian of the projection [S2, unofficial]. Gaussians are sorted by depth and rendered via alpha compositing to produce an image $C(\mathbf{r})$ [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Optimization:** The system minimizes a loss function between the rendered image and the observed image. Common losses include L1 loss and SSIM [S2, unofficial]. Gradients are backpropagated to update both the Gaussian parameters ($\mu_i, \Sigma_i, \alpha_i, \mathbf{c}_i$) and the camera pose $T$ [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Densification:** To maintain reconstruction quality, the density of Gaussians is adapted. Gaussians are cloned or split in regions with high reprojection error or low opacity, and pruned if they become too transparent [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scene representations, typically for tasks such as 3D editing, style transfer, or multi-scene fusion. Unlike standard SLAM mapping which updates a single scene's geometry and appearance, these strategies solve the problem of transferring visual attributes (density, radiance, color) from a source scene to a target scene while maintaining structural fidelity. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md) by their focus on explicit pixel-to-pixel or region-to-region correspondence algorithms rather than high-level semantic label propagation. See [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md).

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation refers to two distinct but related approaches for 3D scene modeling: the original implicit neural field formulation and the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation. The original NeRF implicitly represents a 6-dimensional illumination volume using a neural network to map position, view direction, and phase function coefficients to radiance, solving the problem of high-quality novel view synthesis by replacing expensive volumetric integration with constant-time network inference. In contrast, 3D Gaussian Splatting explicitly represents a scene as millions of tiny colored semi-transparent 3D Gaussian blobs, rendering them via differentiable rasterization to achieve real-time performance, thereby avoiding neural network evaluation during rendering. While NeRF offers compact memory usage, Gaussian Splatting provides significantly faster training and rendering speeds at the cost of higher memory consumption. See [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md).

### CG-SLAM
CG-SLAM is a method that greatly improves reconstruction quality by introducing an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1].

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
This approach combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting [S2, unofficial]. On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), the ATE RMSE for ORB-SLAM3 is 0.052 m, while the combined approach achieves 3.190 m. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences [S2, unofficial]. While ORB-SLAM achieves the best ATE, the combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. The unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S2, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
CP-SLAM is a neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure [S3]. It uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization [S3]. It employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly [S3]. It integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S3].

### MNE-SLAM
MNE-SLAM is the first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange [S3]. It uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module [S3]. It introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S3].

### HERO-SLAM
HERO-SLAM is a SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings [S3]. It uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S3].

### PIN-SLAM
PIN-SLAM is a globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation [S3]. It replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure [S3]. Mapping alternates with odometry [S3]. The mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders [S3]. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization [S3]. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates [S3]. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S3].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
This algorithm performs active 3D reconstruction using a DVGO (Direct Voxel Grid Optimization) model [S4, unofficial].
*   **Initialization:** Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M^{(c)}$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Global Policy:** Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Local Policy:** Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Action:** Move agent to $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Observation:** Obtain new image observation $I_t$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Update Database:** Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Mapping Optimization:** Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$ [S4, unofficial].
*   **Stop Check:** Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold [S4, unofficial].
*   **Fine Stage:** After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M^{(f)}$ with trained coarse model $M_T^{(c)}$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S4, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
This method decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison [S5].
*   **Step 1:** Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$ [S5].
*   **Step 2:** Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity [S5].
*   **Step 3:** Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$ [S5].
*   **Step 4:** Discard the L channel [S5].
*   **Step 5:** Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences [S5].
*   **Formula:** $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth [S5].
*   **Parameters:** $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss [S5]. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S5].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
This method extends passive mapping to active exploration using 3D Gaussians [S6].
*   **Step 1:** Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM) [S6].
*   **Step 2:** Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration [S6].
*   **Step 3:** Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent [S6].
*   **Step 4:** Compute Expected Information Gain (EIG) for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration [S6].
*   **Formula:** $P_j^* = \arg\max_{P_j} \text{tr} \left( \sum_{x_{acq_i} \in P_j} H''[y_{i\_acq}|x_{acq_i}, w^*] \right) H''[w^*|D_{train}]^{-1}$ [S6].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced as a real-time explicit scene representation [S2, unofficial]. Its integration into SLAM systems began with methods like SplaTAM, which combined 3DGS with visual-inertial odometry [S2, unofficial]. Subsequent works explored various aspects, including uncertainty-aware training (CG-SLAM) [S1], hybrid approaches with classical SLAM (Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS) [S2, unofficial], multi-agent cooperative SLAM (CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM) [S3], and active mapping strategies (Active 3D Reconstruction, FisherRF) [S4, unofficial][S6]. These methods leverage the differentiable nature of 3DGS to jointly optimize geometry, appearance, and camera poses, offering advantages in speed and reconstruction quality over traditional and NeRF-based SLAM methods [S1][S2, unofficial].
