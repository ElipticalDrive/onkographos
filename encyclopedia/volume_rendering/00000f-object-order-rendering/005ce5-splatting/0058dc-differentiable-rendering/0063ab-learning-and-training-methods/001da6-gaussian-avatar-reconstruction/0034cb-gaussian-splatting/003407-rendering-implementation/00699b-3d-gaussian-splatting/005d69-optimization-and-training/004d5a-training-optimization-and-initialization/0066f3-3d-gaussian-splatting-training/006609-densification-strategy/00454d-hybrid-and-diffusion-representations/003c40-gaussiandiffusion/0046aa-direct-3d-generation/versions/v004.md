# Direct 3D Generation

Also called: Feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting, Diffusion-based 3DGS Generation

## Overview
Direct 3D Generation refers to methods that learn a feedforward network or diffusion model to output 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) fields directly from conditioning signals such as text prompts, single images, or partial inputs, bypassing the iterative optimization required by standard 3DGS [S1]. Unlike implicit NeRF feature fields, these methods operate on explicit Gaussian primitives, enabling high-fidelity geometry recovery and direct manipulation while amortizing reconstruction costs to allow near-real-time sampling at test time [S1]. This approach addresses the challenges of cross-view consistency and occluded regions by leveraging large-scale training corpora to promote multi-view coherence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Conditioning signal $C$ (text, image, or partial 3DGS), and potentially a noise vector $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ for diffusion-based variants.
**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives defined by position $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$ (or rotation $R$ and scale $S$), color $c$, and opacity $\alpha$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Primitives:** A list of $N$ Gaussians. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by:
    *   $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position.
    *   $R_i \in SO(3)$: Rotation matrix (or quaternion).
    *   $S_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector.
    *   $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Spherical harmonics coefficients or RGB color.
    *   $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$: Opacity.
2.  **Latent Space (for Diffusion/VAE variants):** A compressed latent vector $z$ representing the Gaussian field, often disentangled into geometry, color, and transform functions [S3].

**Steps:**
1.  **Training Phase (if applicable):**
    *   Encode input 3DGS representations into a latent space using a Gaussian VAE [S3].
    *   Train a diffusion model (e.g., LDM) to predict noise or reverse the diffusion process on latent vectors $z_k$ derived from the VAE, conditioned on $C$ [S3].
    *   Alternatively, train a feedforward network to map $C$ directly to Gaussian attributes [S1].
2.  **Generation Phase:**
    *   **Diffusion Variant:** Start with noise $z_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Iteratively denoise from step $K$ to $0$ using the trained diffusion model conditioned on $C$ to obtain $z_0$ [S3].
    *   **Decoding:** Decode $z_0$ into Gaussian functions (GauPF for probability/geometry, GauCF for color, GauTF for transform) [S3].
    *   **Geometry Extraction:** Sample 3D locations with highest Gaussian probabilities from GauPF [S3].
    *   **Attribute Prediction:** Predict color and transform attributes for the sampled locations using GauCF and GauTF [S3].
    *   **Discretization:** Extract final Gaussians at arbitrary numbers via octree-guided sampling and optimization [S3].
3.  **Rendering:** Render the generated Gaussians using standard differentiable rasterization [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Diffusion steps $K$: Not given in the sources.
*   Number of Gaussians $N$: Arbitrary, determined by discretization [S3].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **High-dimensional Output Space:** The structured nature of Gaussian attributes (anisotropic covariance, etc.) poses challenges for model stability [S1].
*   **Data Scarcity:** Requires large, curated 3D training corpora, which are often synthetic or reconstructed [S1].
*   **Controllability:** Disentangling geometry from appearance while scaling to diverse objects remains difficult [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Conditional 3D Gaussian Splatting Generation](15578-conditional-3d-gaussian-splatting-generation.md)
Conditional 3D Gaussian Splatting Generation refers to methods that synthesize 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations directly from conditioning signals such as text prompts, input images, or partial 3DGS inputs with large occlusions. Unlike standard 3DGS which requires multi-view images for optimization, these methods leverage diffusion models or feedforward networks to predict Gaussian attributes (position, covariance, color, opacity) conditioned on the input. This approach is distinct from implicit NeRF feature fields by operating on explicit Gaussian primitives, allowing for direct manipulation and high-fidelity geometry recovery.

#### Align Your Gaussians (AYG)
A method realizing text-to-4D synthesis and deformation, employing dynamic 3D Gaussians with deformation fields, starting with 3D-aware diffusion model and text-to-image model for initial 3D shape, then employing text-to-video diffusion model for motion and text-to-image model for visual quality [S2].
1. Start with 3D-aware diffusion model and text-to-image model to create initial 3D shape.
2. Employ text-to-video diffusion model to capture scene motion.
3. Utilize text-to-image model to maintain high visual quality for all frames [S2].

#### Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) for 3DGS
A diffusion model trained in the latent space of the Gaussian VAE to generate novel 3DGS shapes conditionally or unconditionally [S3].
*   **Training:** The LDM is trained to predict noise or reverse the diffusion process on latent vectors $z_k$ derived from the VAE [S3].
*   **Generation:** Starts from noise $N(0,1)$, iteratively denoises to $z_0$ (Step K to Step 0), conditioned on text, image, or partial GS if applicable [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Near-real-time sampling at test time, amortizing the reconstruction cost [S1]. Training requires large-scale data and computational resources [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians and the capacity of the feedforward/diffusion model [S1, S3].
*   **Strengths:** Promotes multi-view coherence, handles occlusions better than iterative methods, allows direct manipulation of explicit primitives [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires large curated 3D datasets, struggles with controllability (disentangling geometry/appearance), and faces challenges in the high-dimensional output space [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When rapid generation from text/image is needed, or when multi-view coherence is critical.
*   **When Not to Use:** When high-fidelity reconstruction from a specific set of multi-view images is required without pre-trained priors.

## Background
Direct 3D Generation emerged as a response to the computational cost and limitations of iterative 3DGS optimization. It builds on the success of diffusion models in 2D image generation and extends them to 3D explicit representations [S1, S3]. Key developments include the use of Gaussian VAEs for latent space compression [S3] and the integration of text-to-image/video models for dynamic content [S2].
