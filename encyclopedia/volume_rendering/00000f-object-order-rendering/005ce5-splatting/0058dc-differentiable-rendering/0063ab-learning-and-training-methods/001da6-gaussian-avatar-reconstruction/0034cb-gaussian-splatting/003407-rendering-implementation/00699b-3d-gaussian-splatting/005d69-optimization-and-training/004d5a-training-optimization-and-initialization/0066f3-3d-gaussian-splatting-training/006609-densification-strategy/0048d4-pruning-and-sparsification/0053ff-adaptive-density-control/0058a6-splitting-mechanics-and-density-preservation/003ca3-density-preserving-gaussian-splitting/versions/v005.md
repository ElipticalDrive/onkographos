# Density-preserving Gaussian splitting

Also called: BOGausS splitting, density-preserving split

## Overview
Density-preserving Gaussian splitting is a densification strategy that modifies the parameters of split Gaussian children so that their combined density approximates the density of the parent Gaussian. It solves the problem of loss function perturbations and Gaussian cluster formation caused by naive splitting or cloning operations [S1]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) which uses opacity reset and cloning, this method discards cloning entirely and relies solely on splitting with learned or fixed adaptation functions for size and opacity [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Parent Gaussian $g_{mother}$ with position $\mu_{mother}$, scale $\sigma_{mother}$ (diagonal covariance in local space), and opacity $o_{mother}$.
- Splitting constant $\alpha$.

**Outputs:**
- Two child Gaussians $g_{child,1}$ and $g_{child,2}$ with updated positions, scales, and opacities.

**Data Structures:**
- Gaussian parameters are stored as:
  - Position: $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$
  - Scale: $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (representing the standard deviation along the three principal axes)
  - Opacity: $o \in [0, 1]$

**Steps:**
1. **Identify Longest Axis:** Determine the principal axis with the largest scale value. Let $\sigma_{max}$ be the scale along this axis, and let $\mathbf{u}$ be the corresponding unit vector in the local coordinate system of the parent Gaussian.
2. **Shift Means:** Calculate the shift magnitude $\Delta = \alpha \sigma_{max}$. The means of the two child Gaussians are shifted along the longest principal axis:
   $$ \mu_{child,1} = \mu_{mother} + \Delta \mathbf{u} $$
   $$ \mu_{child,2} = \mu_{mother} - \Delta \mathbf{u} $$
   The other two axes' means remain unchanged.
3. **Adapt Size and Opacity:** Compute the new scale $\sigma_{child}$ and opacity $o_{child}$ for both children using the adaptation function:
   $$ (\sigma_{child}, o_{child}) = \text{SplitAdaptation}(\sigma_{mother}, o_{mother}) $$
   This function ensures the resulting density of the sum of the two children is close to the density of the mother Gaussian [S1]. The adaptation can be learned offline [S1].
4. **Copy Remaining Parameters:** Copy rotation and color attributes from the parent to both children.

**Parameters:**
- $\alpha$: Constant controlling the mean shift magnitude. Typical value: $0.3$ [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
- Naive splitting causes perturbations in the loss function [S1].
- Cloning operations are discarded to prevent Gaussian clusters [S1].
- Opacity variations must be accounted for during splitting adaptation, unlike some prior methods [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Splitting is $O(1)$ per Gaussian, but requires evaluating the adaptation function. If the adaptation is a learned model, inference time depends on the model size.
- **Memory Complexity:** Increases by one Gaussian per split operation. Pruning removes Gaussians with low opacity, mitigating memory growth [S1].
- **Strengths:** Prevents loss function perturbations and Gaussian clusters [S1]. Replaces opacity reset with a more principled density-preserving approach [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires offline learning or careful tuning of the adaptation function [S1]. Discards cloning, which may limit flexibility in some scenarios [S1].
- **When to use:** When training 3D Gaussian Splatting models and experiencing instability or cluster formation due to densification [S1].

## Background
Originated in BOGausS (Better Optimized Gaussian Splatting) [S1]. The method addresses issues identified in earlier works [5, 9] regarding loss perturbations during splitting. It builds on ideas from [16] and contrasts with [17] by incorporating opacity variations. The method replaces the opacity reset mechanism used in standard 3DGS [S1].
