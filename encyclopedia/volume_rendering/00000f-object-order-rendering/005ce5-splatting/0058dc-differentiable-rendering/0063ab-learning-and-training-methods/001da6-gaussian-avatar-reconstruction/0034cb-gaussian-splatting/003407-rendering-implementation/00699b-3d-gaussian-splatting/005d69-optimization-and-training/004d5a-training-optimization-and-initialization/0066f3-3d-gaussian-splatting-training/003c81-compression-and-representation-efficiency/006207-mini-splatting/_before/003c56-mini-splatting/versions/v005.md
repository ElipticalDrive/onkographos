# Mini-Splatting

Also called: Constrained Gaussian Representation, Hard-constrained 3DGS, Mixed Integer 3DGS

## Overview
Mini-Splatting is a training optimization and initialization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that explicitly constrains the total number of Gaussians to a hard limit $N_{max}$ to reduce memory footprint and accelerate rendering [S1, unofficial][S2]. It addresses the inefficient spatial distribution of vanilla 3DGS—specifically "overlapping" and "under-reconstruction" phenomena—by pivoting from direct simplification to spatial reorganization [S2]. Unlike its sibling [C3DGS](9184-c3dgs.md), which focuses on voxel-based pruning, Mini-Splatting leverages both screen-space and world-space information to densify around objects and simplify redundant overlaps, achieving comparable or superior visual quality with significantly fewer primitives [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A scene representation initialized with 3D Gaussians (position $\mathbf{p}$, rotation $\mathbf{R}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{lm}$) and a target maximum Gaussian count $N_{max}$.
*   **Output:** A sparse Gaussian field with exactly $N_{max}$ primitives, uniformly distributed around object surfaces, maintaining rendering fidelity [S2].

**Data Structures:**
The method maintains the standard 3DGS attribute set for each Gaussian $i$:
*   $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position.
*   $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation (quaternion).
*   $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale.
*   $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
*   $\mathbf{c}_{i, lm}$: Spherical Harmonic coefficients.
*   $score_i \in \mathbb{R}$: Importance score used for selection [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The optimization follows a 30,000-step schedule, divided into densification and simplification phases [S2].

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians using standard 3DGS densification (point cloud sampling) until iteration 15,000. Disable view-dependent colors (SH coefficients) during this phase to save resources, as they barely enhance densification [S2].
2.  **Densification (Iterations 0–15,000):**
    *   **Blur Split:** Detect blurring areas in the rendered image. Split Gaussians located in these regions to increase local density [S2].
    *   **Depth Reinitialization:** Use merged depth points to reinitialize scene geometry and Gaussian positions, ensuring coverage of under-reconstructed areas [S2].
    *   **Constraint:** The total count may exceed $N_{max}$ temporarily during densification.
3.  **Simplification (Iterations 15,000 and 20,000):**
    *   **Intersection Preserving:** Retain only Gaussians that have the maximal impact on the rendered image, discarding redundant overlaps [S2].
    *   **Gaussian Sampling:** Sample points to preserve the underlying geometric structure and original rendering quality [S2].
    *   **Hard Constraint:** Iteratively prune the least important Gaussians and split the most important ones to keep the total count constant at $N_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Refinement (Iterations 15,000–30,000):**
    *   Enable SH coefficients and increase the SH level after the first simplification pass (at 15K) [S2].
    *   Continue standard 3DGS optimization (opacity reset, etc.) for the remaining steps [S2].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def mini_splatting_training(scene_data, N_max, total_steps=30000):
    # Initialize Gaussians using standard 3DGS densification
    gaussians = initialize_3dgs(scene_data)
    
    # Disable view-dependent colors during densification
    gaussians.sh_degree = 0 
    
    for step in range(1, total_steps + 1):
        # Render current view and compute loss
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, camera)
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth)
        
        if step <= 15000:
            # Densification Phase
            # 1. Blur Split: Split Gaussians in blurring areas
            blur_mask = detect_blur(rendered_image)
            gaussians = split_gaussians(gaussians, blur_mask)
            
            # 2. Depth Reinitialization: Reinitialize positions using depth
            depth_points = get_merged_depth(scene_data)
            gaussians = reinitialize_positions(gaussians, depth_points)
            
            # Standard 3DGS densification (clone/split based on gradient)
            gaussians = standard_densification(gaussians)
            
        else:
            # Simplification Phase (triggered at 15K and 20K)
            if step in [15000, 20000]:
                # 1. Intersection Preserving: Keep high-impact Gaussians
                impact_scores = compute_rendering_impact(gaussians)
                gaussians = prune_low_impact(gaussians, impact_scores, N_max)
                
                # 2. Gaussian Sampling: Preserve geometry
                gaussians = sample_gaussians(gaussians, N_max)
                
            # Enable SH coefficients after first simplification
            if step > 15000:
                gaussians.sh_degree = 1 # Increase SH level
                
            # Standard optimization
            gaussians = standard_optimization(gaussians)
            
    return gaussians
```

**Parameters:**
*   $N_{max}$: Hard limit on Gaussian count. Typical value: ~0.49M for Mip-NeRF360 Outdoor (vs 3.36M for vanilla 3DGS) [S2].
*   Total Optimization Steps: 30,000 [S2].
*   Densification Stop: 15,000 steps [S2].
*   Simplification Steps: 15,000 and 20,000 [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Popping Artifacts:** Using fewer Gaussians results in larger Gaussians on average, which increases the prevalence of noticeable popping artifacts during camera movement [S4].
*   **SH Redundancy:** View-dependent colors (SH coefficients) provide minimal benefit during the densification phase; enabling them early wastes resources without improving spatial distribution [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Model Compression Pipeline for 3D Gaussian Splatting](9987-model-compression-pipeline-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
This method is a compression and rendering pipeline for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that reduces memory footprint and increases rendering speed while maintaining image quality. It addresses the high memory bandwidth and computational complexity of standard 3DGS, making it suitable for edge deployment, mobile VR/AR, and low-power hardware. It sets itself apart from its parent [Mini-Splatting](7850-mini-splatting.md) by not imposing a hard limit on the total number of Gaussians via mixed-integer optimization, but rather by iteratively pruning, quantizing attributes, and reducing spherical harmonic (SH) degrees to achieve high compression ratios (up to 51.6×) with minimal quality loss. It differs from LightGaussian by using iterative pruning with intermediate fine-tuning, progressive SH-degree reduction, and vector quantization (VQ) on all SH coefficients and colors, rather than only low-salience subsets.

### Mini-Splatting-D
A quality-prioritized variant of Mini-Splatting that omits the simplification process to maximize rendering quality [S2]. On Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor), Mini-Splatting-D uses 4.69M Gaussians, achieving SSIM 0.831, PSNR 27.51, LPIPS 0.176. It surpasses Zip-NeRF in SSIM (0.828) and LPIPS (0.189) on this dataset [S2]. Training time is 31m48s, Peak GPU Memory is 7.45GB, Rendering FPS is 83, Peak Rendering Memory is 3.12GB [S2].

### Mini-Splatting-C
Designed for scenarios requiring storage compression, this pipeline is directly based on Mini-Splatting. Given a pre-trained Mini-Splatting model, it stores Gaussian centers as float32, while other attributes (scales, SH coefficients) are processed using transform coding and standard zip compression [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Training Time:** Mini-Splatting reduces training time significantly. On Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor), it takes 17m56s compared to 30m8s for vanilla 3DGS [S2].
*   **Peak GPU Memory:** Reduced from 7.45GB (3DGS) to 2.61GB (Mini-Splatting) on Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor) [S2].
*   **Rendering FPS:** Increases from 98 FPS (3DGS) to 410 FPS (Mini-Splatting) on Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor) [S2].
*   **Peak Rendering Memory:** Reduced from 2.79GB (3DGS) to 0.40GB (Mini-Splatting) [S2].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor):** Mini-Splatting uses 0.49M Gaussians (7x fewer than 3DGS's 3.36M). Metrics: SSIM 0.822 (vs 0.815), PSNR 27.34 (vs 27.21), LPIPS 0.217 (vs 0.214) [S2].
*   **Mip-NeRF360 (General):** Another variant achieves SSIM 0.763, PSNR 25.83, LPIPS 0.273, with a model size of 18.2 MB [S3].
*   **Model Size:** Up to 10x reduction compared to original 3DGS [S4].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Efficient spatial distribution reduces redundancy; significant memory and speed improvements; comparable or better visual quality than dense 3DGS [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Fewer Gaussians lead to larger individual splats, increasing popping artifacts [S4]. The hard constraint requires careful balancing of densification and simplification schedules [S2].

**When to Use:**
Use Mini-Splatting when memory bandwidth, training time, or rendering FPS are critical constraints, and a slight increase in popping artifacts is acceptable. Use Mini-Splatting-D when quality is prioritized over memory/speed constraints.

## Background
Mini-Splatting was introduced by Fang and Wang in 2024 [S2]. It emerged from the observation that vanilla 3DGS suffers from inefficient spatial distribution, leading to overlapping and under-reconstruction. The method formulates the optimization as a mixed-integer problem to bound memory footprints explicitly [S1, unofficial]. It is part of the broader effort to make 3DGS viable for resource-constrained environments, such as mobile AR/VR and edge devices [S1, unofficial][S2].
