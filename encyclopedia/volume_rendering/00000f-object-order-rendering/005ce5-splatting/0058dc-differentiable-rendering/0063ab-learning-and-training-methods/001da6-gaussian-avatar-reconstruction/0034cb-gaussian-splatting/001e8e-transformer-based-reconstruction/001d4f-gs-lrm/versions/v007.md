# GS-LRM
Also called: Gaussian Large Reconstruction Model
## Overview
GS-LRM is a feed-forward transformer-based method that reconstructs 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations from a small set of input images. It solves the sparse-view reconstruction problem by tokenizing images and camera parameters, processing them through a transformer, and decoding the output into Gaussian parameters. It is distinguished from its siblings [Flash3D](1481-flash3d.md) and [Large Reconstruction Model (LRM)](5959-large-reconstruction-model-lrm.md) by its specific focus on predicting Gaussians aligned along camera-to-pixel rays using a direct transformer decoder, rather than latent volumes or mesh-based outputs [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of 2 to 4 input images and their corresponding camera parameters (extrinsics and intrinsics).
**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, color, opacity) aligned along the rays from the camera centers to the pixel coordinates of the input views.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Input Tokens:** Each image is processed into visual tokens. Camera parameters are encoded into token embeddings.
2.  **Concatenated Sequence:** Visual tokens and camera parameter tokens are concatenated into a single sequence.
3.  **Transformer Block:** The concatenated sequence is passed through a transformer encoder/decoder block.
4.  **Decoded Tokens:** The output tokens from the transformer block are mapped to Gaussian parameters.

**Steps:**
1.  **Tokenization:** Encode the 2-4 input images into feature tokens. Encode the associated camera parameters (extrinsics and intrinsics) into token embeddings [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Concatenation:** Concatenate the image tokens and camera parameter tokens into a single sequence [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Transformer Processing:** Pass the concatenated sequence through a transformer block to capture spatial and geometric relationships [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Decoding:** Decode the output tokens of the transformer block into 3D Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, color, opacity) [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Ray Alignment:** Ensure the predicted Gaussians are aligned along the camera-to-pixel rays corresponding to the input views [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Number of input images: 2 to 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   Typical values for other hyperparameters are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Pose Accuracy:** The method relies on known camera poses. Incorrect poses may lead to misaligned Gaussians.
*   **View Overlap:** Performance may degrade with minimal view overlap, though specific mitigation strategies are not detailed in the primary source [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Standard floating-point precision is assumed for transformer operations and Gaussian parameter decoding.

## Subtopics

### Large 4D Gaussian Model (L4GM)
L4GM is a feed-forward 4D large reconstruction model that outputs per-frame 3DGS from a single-view video in one ~1s forward pass. It upsamples to higher fps via temporal self-attention for temporal smoothness. It is built on LGM and trained on a new Objaverse-derived multiview-video dataset (44K objects, 12M videos, 300M frames). It is marked as a 2026 SOTA update [S2, unofficial].

*   **When not to use:** When camera poses are unknown or when high temporal smoothness from a single video is required (consider L4GM instead) [S2, unofficial].
## Background
GS-LRM was introduced as part of the evolution of feed-forward 3D reconstruction models. It builds upon earlier methods like PixelSplat and MVSplat by combining multi-view feature extraction through transformers and cost volumes, processed by a U-Net, but specifically focuses on direct Gaussian parameter prediction via a transformer block [S1, unofficial]. It is cited in the context of "From Volume Rendering to 3D Gaussian Splatting" [S1, unofficial].
