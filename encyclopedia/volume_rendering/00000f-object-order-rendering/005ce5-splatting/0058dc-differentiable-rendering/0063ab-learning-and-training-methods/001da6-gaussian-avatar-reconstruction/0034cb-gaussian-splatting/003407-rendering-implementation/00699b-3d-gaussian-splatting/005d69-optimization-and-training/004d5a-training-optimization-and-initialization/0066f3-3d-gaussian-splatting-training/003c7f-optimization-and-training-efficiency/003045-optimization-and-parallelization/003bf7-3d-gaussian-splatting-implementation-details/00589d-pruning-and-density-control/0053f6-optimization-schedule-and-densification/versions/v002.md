# Optimization Schedule and Densification

Also called: Densification Scheduling, Primitive Growth Control

## Overview
This family of methods addresses the inefficiency and instability of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) densification by decoupling the rate of primitive growth from the optimization timeline or by optimizing in alternative spaces. Standard 3DGS uses fixed intervals for splitting and cloning, which can lead to redundant primitives or insufficient coverage [S4, unofficial][S5]. These methods solve the problem of balancing rendering fidelity with training speed by dynamically adjusting resolution, primitive budgets, or optimization domains. They differ from [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md) by introducing explicit scheduling logic for densification events, and from [Pruning and Density Control](16215-pruning-and-density-control.md) by focusing on the *growth* phase rather than just removal.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of controlling the number and distribution of 3D Gaussian primitives during training to accelerate convergence or improve quality. They typically modify the standard 3DGS densification loop, which consists of:
1.  **Gradient Accumulation:** Computing view-space position gradients $\nabla_{pos}$ for each Gaussian.
2.  **Densification Criteria:** Checking if $\nabla_{pos}$ exceeds a threshold $\tau_{grad}$ and if opacity $o_i$ is below a threshold $\tau_{opacity}$ (for cloning) or above a threshold (for splitting).
3.  **Operations:** Splitting a Gaussian into two (copying parameters, halving scale, resetting opacity) or cloning a Gaussian (copying parameters, resetting opacity).
4.  **Pruning:** Removing Gaussians with low opacity.

The shared deviation from standard 3DGS is the introduction of a **scheduler** or **constraint** that gates these operations. This gate may be based on:
*   **Iteration Count:** Fixed or adaptive intervals for densification [S2, S4, unofficial][S5].
*   **Resolution:** Coupling primitive growth to the current rendering resolution [S5].
*   **Optimization Space:** Performing densification in 2D image space before back-projection [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md].
*   **Gradient Type:** Using normal gradients or accumulated momentum instead of raw view-space gradients [S3, S5].

## Subtopics

### [Error-Driven 2D Gaussian Training](13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md)
A densification strategy that optimizes 2D Gaussian kernels in image space and back-projects them to 3D. It solves the problem of reconstructing complex specular radiance and high-frequency details where standard 3DGS initialization fails [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md]. Unlike standard densification which operates directly in 3D, this method optimizes in 2D image space using pre-rendered images as fixed backgrounds, allowing for more precise error-driven placement before projection [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md].

### [DashGaussian](15742-dashgaussian.md)
A scheduling scheme that accelerates training by reformulating optimization as a progressive fitting of higher-frequency components. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count to strip redundant complexity, distinguishing itself from vanilla 3DGS and other schedulers by producing a concave-up primitive growth curve that spans the entire optimization process [15742-dashgaussian.md].

### QuickSplat Optimization Process
An optimization framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting that uses learned networks for initialization, densification, and parameter updates to accelerate surface reconstruction [S1].
*   **Step:** Iterate for $t = 0$ to $T - 1$. Reset gradient accumulator $\nabla G_t \leftarrow 0$. For all images, compute rendering loss $L$, compute gradient $\delta G_t$, and accumulate: $\nabla G_t \leftarrow \nabla G_t + \delta G_t$. Generate new Gaussians $\hat{G}_t$ using a densifier network $f_D$ on current Gaussians $G_t$, accumulated gradients $\nabla G_t$, and time step $t$: $\hat{G}_t \leftarrow f_D(G_t, \nabla G_t, t)$ [S1].

### BlitzGS Optimization Schedule
A schedule designed to reduce active workload by managing the density-control window and Level-of-Detail (LOD) gates.
*   **Step:** Disable the LOD gate during the density-control window (iterations 2,000–32,000). Engage the LOD gate after the first importance-scoring pass ($T_1$). After the importance-scoring pass, reweight density-control gradients using the per-Gaussian visibility factor $\phi_i$ for the remainder of the density-control window [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Density control interval: runs every 1,000 steps from iteration 2,000 up to 32,000 (last event at 31,000) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Extending training iterations (e.g., 500k–1000k) does not significantly improve quality on aerial scenes (e.g., Building 19.27→19.50 dB), indicating deficits stem from representation structure rather than insufficient budget [S2, unofficial].

### Normal Gradient Based Densification
A densification criterion to improve normal recovery in thin regions by introducing an additional densification criterion on the gradient of normals, beyond the vanilla 3DGS gradient of view space points [S3].
*   **Definition:** An additional densification criterion for 3D Gaussians that improves normal recovery in thin regions by densifying Gaussians whose normal gradient exceeds a threshold $T_n$ [S3].
*   **Step:** Densify Gaussians whose normal gradient exceeds a threshold $T_n$ [S3].
*   **Parameter:** $T_n$: The threshold for normal gradient magnitude used to trigger densification. Typical value: $2 \times 10^{-9}$ [S3].

### Global-to-local densification
A densification strategy that decouples split and clone operations across densification phases to accelerate optimization. It uses split for global spread at low resolution and suppresses clone, then reintroduces clone for local refinement at full resolution [S4, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Calculate the norm of the average position gradient of each Gaussian primitive every 100 iterations. If the gradient norm exceeds a predefined threshold, check the maximum scale of the Gaussian primitive. If the maximum scale exceeds a given scale threshold, split the Gaussian into smaller components. Otherwise, clone the Gaussian with the same parameters. During low-resolution training, promote Gaussian primitives' global spread with the split operation and suppress clone operations to prevent premature local clustering. When transitioning to full-resolution training, reintroduce clone operations to refine high-frequency details [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Gradient calculation frequency: 100 iterations [S4, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Unlike standard 3DGS which applies split and clone simultaneously, this method decouples them across phases (global vs local) to address inefficient spatial spread and redundant primitives [S4, unofficial].

### Resolution Guided Primitive Scheduler
A scheduler that uses a momentum-based budgeting method to dynamically update the maximum number of primitives, avoiding reliance on dataset priors [S5].
*   **Formula:** $P_{fin}^{(i)} = \max(P_{fin}^{(i-1)}, \gamma \cdot P_{fin}^{(i-1)} + \eta \cdot P_{add})$, where $P_{add}$ is the number of primitives densified in step $i$. $P_{fin}^{(0)}$ is initialized as $5 \cdot P_{init}$ [S5].
*   **Parameter:** Power factor in primitive growth: Starts at 2 and gradually decreases to 1 along the optimization process to suppress early growth and encourage mid-stage growth [S5]. Hyperparameter $\gamma$: Momentum decay factor for primitive budget. Default value: 0.98 [S5]. Hyperparameter $\eta$: Force scaling factor for primitive budget. Default value: 1 [S5]. Resolution modulation: The continuous scalar $r(i)$ is modulated to $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ in practice to encourage faster primitive growth [S5].
*   **Pitfall:** Too much primitive growth at the end of optimization results in poor convergence. Mitigated by the concave-up scheduling function and momentum-based budgeting [S5].

### 3D Refinement View Sampling Strategy
A staged sampling schedule for supervising 3D refinement that balances fitting the current refined view with preserving rendering ability in original and previously refined views [S6].
*   **Definition:** A staged sampling schedule for supervising 3D refinement that balances fitting the current refined view with preserving rendering ability in original and previously refined views [S6].
*   **Step:** Divide the total 3D refinement steps into three equal phases (first third, subsequent third, final third). In the first third, designate every 3 steps as 'current-refine steps' to optimize using the current refined image $\hat{I}^{i,e,f}_{ie}$. In the subsequent third, designate every 5 steps as 'current-refine steps'. In the final third, designate every 8 steps as 'current-refine steps'. For all non-current-refine steps, randomly sample views from the training set $S_{train}$ and the previous refined set $F_{i-1}$ [S6].
*   **Parameter:** Selection weight for $F_{i-1}$ is lower than for $S_{train}$ to prioritize original training data over previously refined views [S6].

## Background
The original 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) paper [26] introduced a fixed densification schedule: splitting and cloning occur every 100 iterations for the first 3,000 iterations, and pruning occurs every 100 iterations thereafter. This static schedule often leads to over-densification in simple regions or under-densification in complex areas, wasting computation [S4, unofficial][S5].

Subsequent works identified that the split operation primarily drives global spatial spread, while clone operations drive local refinement [S4, unofficial]. This insight led to strategies like Global-to-local densification [S4, unofficial] and DashGaussian [S5], which decouple these operations or couple them to resolution.

The concept of optimizing in 2D image space was explored in methods like Error-Driven 2D Gaussian Training [13985-error-driven-2d-gaussian-training.md], which leverages differentiable rasterization to place Gaussians based on pixel-wise error, back-projecting them to 3D. This addresses the limitation of 3D-only densification in capturing high-frequency specular details.

QuickSplat [S1] further accelerated this by replacing heuristic densification with learned networks ($f_D$) that predict new Gaussian parameters based on accumulated gradients. BlitzGS [S2, unofficial] focused on system-level efficiency, managing density control windows and LOD gates to reduce active workload in large-scale scenes.

Normal Gradient Based Densification [S3] addressed geometric fidelity, specifically normal recovery in thin regions, by adding a criterion based on the gradient of normals.

The Resolution Guided Primitive Scheduler [S5] formalized the relationship between rendering resolution and primitive count, introducing a momentum-based budgeting formula to dynamically limit primitive growth, preventing the "over-dense" state that hinders convergence.

3D Refinement View Sampling [S6] addressed the challenge of fine-tuning pre-trained models, proposing a staged sampling strategy to balance fitting new views with preserving generalization.
