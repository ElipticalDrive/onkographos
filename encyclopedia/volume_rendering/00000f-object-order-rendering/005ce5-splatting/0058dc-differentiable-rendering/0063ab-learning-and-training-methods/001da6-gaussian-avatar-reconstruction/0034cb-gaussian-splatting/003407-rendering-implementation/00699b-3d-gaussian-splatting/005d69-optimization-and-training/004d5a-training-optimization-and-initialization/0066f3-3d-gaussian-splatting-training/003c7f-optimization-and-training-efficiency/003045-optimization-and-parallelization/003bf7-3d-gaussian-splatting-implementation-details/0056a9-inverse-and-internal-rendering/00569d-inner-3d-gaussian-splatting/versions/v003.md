# Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Inner Gaussian Splatting, InnerGS

## Overview
Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting (InnerGS) is a framework for direct volumetric inference that models continuous volumetric density using 3D Gaussians to reconstruct internal structures from sparse, pose-free sliced data [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of reconstructing internal volumes from slice-based inputs (e.g., medical imaging or material scans) where camera poses and projection matrices are unavailable [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which relies on camera projection and tends to concentrate Gaussians on object surfaces leaving interiors empty, InnerGS uses **Conditional Splatting** to adaptively determine Gaussian influence per depth slice [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like GS-IR by explicitly factorizing the 3D density into conditional 2D splats and a marginal 1D depth Gaussian, enabling tile-based rasterization without external camera parameters [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Sparse set of 2D slices (images) representing cross-sections of the volume.
*   No camera poses or projection matrices are required.

**Outputs:**
*   A set of 3D Gaussians representing the volumetric density and color of the internal structure.
*   Reconstructed color images for any depth slice $t$.

**Data Structures:**
Each 3D Gaussian $i$ is defined by the following attributes, consistent with the parent [Inverse and Internal Rendering](15764-inverse-and-internal-rendering.md):
*   **Mean position:** $\mu_i = (\mu_{x,i}, \mu_{y,i}, \mu_{z,i}) \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Covariance matrix:** $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, decomposed into scaling $S_i$ and rotation $R_i$:
    $$ \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^\top R_i^\top $$
    where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_{x,i}, s_{y,i}, s_{z,i})$ and $R_i$ is derived from a unit quaternion $q_i$.
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$.
*   **Color:** Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients $c_i$ to model view-dependent color variations [S1, unofficial].

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Conditional 2D Gaussian Splatting:**
    For a target depth slice at position $t$, compute conditional 2D Gaussian splats. This step determines the 2D Gaussian center and influence radii in the image plane $(u, v)$ specifically for that slice [S1, unofficial]. This replaces the standard projection-based mechanism which fails for internal structures [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Marginal 1D Gaussian Combination:**
    Combine the conditional 2D Gaussians with a marginal 1D Gaussian along the depth axis to formulate the complete 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) density [S1, unofficial]. This factorization allows the model to capture continuous volumetric information between sparse slices [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Tile-Based Rasterization:**
    Perform tile-based rasterization using the formulated 3DGS density. The rendering pipeline accumulates contributions from the Gaussians to reconstruct smooth and detailed internal structures [S1, unofficial].

**Rendering Formula:**
The final color of a pixel at coordinates $(u, v)$ on a slice at depth $t$ is computed as [S1, unofficial]:

$$ I(u, v, t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(u, v, t)\alpha_i c_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(u, v, t)\alpha_j) $$

Where:
*   $N$ is the total number of Gaussians.
*   $p_i(u, v, t)$ is the screen-space density contribution of the $i$-th splat at pixel $(u, v)$ for slice $t$.
*   $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian.
*   $c_i$ is the color of the $i$-th Gaussian (derived from SH coefficients).
*   The product term $\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(u, v, t)\alpha_j)$ represents the accumulated transparency from previously rendered (front-to-back) Gaussians.

**Parameters:**
*   **Pixel Coordinates:** $u \in [0, W - 1]$ and $v \in [0, H - 1]$ for an image of width $W$ and height $H$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Depth Slice:** $t$ represents the specific depth plane being rendered [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Surface Concentration:** Standard 3DGS projection-based mechanisms fail for internal volumetric structures because learned Gaussians concentrate on the object's surface, leaving the interior volume empty [S1, unofficial]. InnerGS mitigates this via conditional splatting.
*   **Missing Camera Data:** Standard 3DGS relies on view/projection matrices which are absent in sliced data [S1, unofficial]. InnerGS does not require these parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Bounding Box Adaptation:** Unlike 3D Ellipsoid Projection which computes a cube and projects identical bounding boxes across slices, InnerGS adapts the bounding box per slice based on conditional Gaussians, improving accuracy for varying internal structures [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The method utilizes tile-based rasterization similar to standard 3DGS, suggesting linear scaling with the number of Gaussians and pixels per tile, but specific complexity metrics are not provided [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Storage is proportional to the number of Gaussians $N$ and their attributes ($\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, c$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Eliminates the need for camera poses [S1, unofficial].
    *   Plug-and-play compatibility with any data modalities [S1, unofficial].
    *   Enables high-fidelity reconstruction of complex internal structures from sparse, pose-free sliced data [S1, unofficial].
    *   Improves computational efficiency and reconstruction accuracy through dynamic adaptation of Gaussian sampling per slice [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the assumption that data can be represented as slices.
    *   Performance on extremely sparse slice distributions is not quantified in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Use when reconstructing internal volumes from sparse, pose-free sliced data (e.g., medical imaging, material science) where camera parameters are unknown or irrelevant.
*   **When not to use:** Use standard 3DGS or NeRF when camera poses are available and the goal is external surface reconstruction or novel-view synthesis of external scenes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Inner 3D Gaussian Splatting was proposed to address the limitations of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and standard 3D Gaussian Splatting in the context of internal scene reconstruction. NeRFs require heavy multi-layer perceptrons that are costly to train and slow to render [S1, unofficial]. Standard 3DGS, while fast, is projection-based and optimized for external views, failing to capture internal volumetric details [S1, unofficial]. InnerGS was introduced as a novel framework leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting for direct volumetric inference [S1, unofficial]. It was developed by Shuxin Liang et al. and published as a preprint in 2026 [S1, unofficial]. The method is designed to be inherently compatible with any data modalities and provides a CUDA implementation [S1, unofficial].
