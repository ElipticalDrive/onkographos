# Mesh-embedded representation
Also called: Mesh-embedded Gaussian Splatting, Mesh-GS, Gaussian-Mesh Hybrid
## Overview
Mesh-embedded representation methods anchor 3D Gaussian primitives to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ to disentangle geometric deformation from appearance . This family solves the problem of coherent, editable animation in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by parameterizing Gaussian positions, rotations, and scales via mesh topology (barycentric coordinates and normal displacement) rather than independent vertex optimization . It sets itself apart from nearest alternatives like [GaMeS](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md) by explicitly controlling Gaussian rotation and translation directly via the mesh geometry rather than relying on implicit MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) fields, ensuring compatibility with standard animation techniques .
## Shared principles
All methods in this family define a Gaussian $i$ by embedding parameters $E_i$ that map it to a canonical mesh $\mathcal{M}$ with vertices $V$, normals $n$, and optionally per-vertex quaternions $q_V$. The canonical position $\mu_{cano}$ and normal $n_{cano}$ are computed via Phong interpolation :
$$
\mu_{cano} = \mathcal{V}(k, u, v) = u V_1 + v V_2 + (1 - u - v) V_3
$$
$$
n_{cano} = \mathcal{N}(k, u, v) = u n_1 + v n_2 + (1 - u - v) n_3
$$
where $k$ is the triangle index, $(u, v)$ are barycentric coordinates ($u \ge 0, v \ge 0, u+v \le 1$), and $V_1, V_2, V_3$ are the triangle vertices .

Deformation to a posed frame is achieved by applying a mesh deformation function $\text{Deform}(\cdot)$ (e.g., skeletal or blend-shape animation) to the displaced canonical position :
$$
\mu_{pose} = \text{Deform}(\mu_{cano} + d \cdot n_{cano})
$$
where $d$ is the normal displacement .

Rotation $q_{pose}$ is interpolated from per-vertex quaternions using barycentric weights and combined with a canonical rotation $\bar{q}$ :
$$
q_{pose} = \delta q * \bar{q}
$$
where $\delta q = u q_1 + v q_2 + (1 - u - v) q_3$ is the interpolated rotation, and $*$ denotes quaternion multiplication . Per-vertex quaternions $q_V$ are calculated by area-weighted average from surrounding neighbor triangles .

Scale $s_{pose}$ is derived from the area change of the embedded triangle relative to the canonical state :
$$
s_{pose} = \left( \frac{A_{pose}}{A_{cano}} \right) \bar{s}
$$
where $A_{pose}$ and $A_{cano}$ are the areas of the triangle in the posed and canonical states, respectively .

A critical implementation detail is cross-triangle handling: barycentric coordinates are only differentiable within a single triangle. If optimization moves an embedding point across a boundary ($u < 0$, $v < 0$, or $u+v > 1$), the embedding must be re-expressed in the neighboring triangle to maintain differentiability . Failure to handle this causes Gaussians to stick to boundaries and pile up .
## Subtopics

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md)
A hybrid representation that anchors 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ to disentangle motion from appearance. It solves the problem of coherent, editable deformation in 3DGS by parameterizing Gaussian positions via barycentric coordinates and normal displacement on mesh faces, allowing explicit motion control through mesh editing or skeletal animation. It sets itself apart from nearest alternatives like [GaMeS](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md) by explicitly controlling Gaussian rotation and translation directly via the mesh geometry rather than relying on implicit MLP-based linear blend skinning (LBS) fields, ensuring compatibility with standard animation techniques.

### [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Initialization and Deformation](9559-mesh-embedded-gaussian-initialization-and-deformation.md)
This method anchors 3D Gaussians to a canonical triangle mesh $\mathcal{M}$ using barycentric coordinates and normal displacement, enabling deformation tracking through mesh topology rather than direct vertex positions. It differs from sibling methods like [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) by explicitly parameterizing the Gaussian position via a displacement $d$ along the interpolated normal, allowing Gaussians to represent off-surface geometry (e.g., hair) or fit tightly to the surface. It builds on [Mesh-embedded representation](12559-mesh-embedded-representation.md) by defining the specific initialization and deformation rules for the embedding parameters.

### [GaMeS (Gaussian Mesh Splatting)](12542-games-gaussian-mesh-splatting.md)
GaMeS is a hybrid representation that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with mesh topology, allowing Gaussian components to be parameterized by the vertices of mesh faces. It solves the problem of conditioning and editing large numbers of independent Gaussians by tying their mean, rotation, and scale directly to the geometry of a canonical triangle mesh. Unlike sibling methods such as [Mesh-Embedded Gaussian Splatting](9553-mesh-embedded-gaussian-splatting.md) which may rely on expensive pre-processing or SDF regularization, GaMeS directly constructs Gaussians on mesh faces or a "Triangle Soup" derived from flat splats, enabling automatic adaptation of Gaussian parameters when the underlying mesh is deformed.

### [SuGaR / Gaussian Frosting](13542-sugar-gaussian-frosting.md)
SuGaR (Surface-Aligned Gaussian Splatting) and Gaussian Frosting are methods that extract a parametric mesh from an optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to enable mesh-based editing while retaining high-fidelity rendering. SuGaR aligns Gaussians to a surface and extracts a base mesh via Poisson reconstruction. Gaussian Frosting extends this by binding Gaussians to the extracted mesh surface as an adaptive layer with variable thickness, allowing the Gaussians to automatically adjust during base mesh manipulation. This approach combines the editing capabilities of explicit meshes with the visual quality of 3DGS.

### [Mesh to DRK Conversion](13700-mesh-to-drk-conversion.md)
Mesh to DRK Conversion is a training-free procedure that converts traditional triangle and polygon mesh faces into Deformable Radial Kernel (DRK) primitives for seamless integration into radiance field scenes. Unlike existing splatting methods (2D-GS, 3D-GS, 3D-HGS, GES) which fail to integrate traditional assets seamlessly, this method provides a direct, training-free conversion pipeline. It bridges the gap between millions of traditional 3D assets and fast, high-quality reconstruction representations, enabling unified rendering, VR digital world creation, and AR scene enhancement.

## Background
Mesh-embedded representations emerged as a solution to the limitations of independent Gaussian optimization in 3DGS, particularly for dynamic scenes and editable assets. The core idea of anchoring Gaussians to mesh topology allows for efficient deformation via standard animation pipelines (skeletal or blend-shape) . The specific formulation using barycentric coordinates and normal displacement was introduced to ensure differentiability and coherent motion .
