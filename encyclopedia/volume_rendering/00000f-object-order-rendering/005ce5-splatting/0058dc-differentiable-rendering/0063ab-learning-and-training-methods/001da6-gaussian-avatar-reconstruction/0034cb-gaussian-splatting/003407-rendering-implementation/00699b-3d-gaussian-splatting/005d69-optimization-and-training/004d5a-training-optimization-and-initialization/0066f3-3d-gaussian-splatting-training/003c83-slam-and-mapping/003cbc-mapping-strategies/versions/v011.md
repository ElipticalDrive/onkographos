# Mapping Strategies

Also called: Pixel mapping strategies, Scene alignment strategies, Joint mapping and pose update

## Overview
Mapping strategies define the mechanism for aligning pixels or features between two scenes or observations to facilitate spatial correspondence, pose estimation, or scene fusion [S1, S2]. They solve the problem of establishing accurate geometric and semantic links between distinct viewpoints or temporal states in SLAM and 3D reconstruction pipelines [S1, S2]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on discrete feature matching or dense neural field optimization alone, these strategies range from direct pixel identity assumptions to semantic-aware correspondences and joint optimization windows [S1, S2].

## How it works
The implementation of mapping strategies varies significantly depending on the specific variant (Identity, Semantic, Co-segmentation, or Joint Mapping). Below are the distinct algorithms for each.

### Identity Mapping
This is the baseline strategy assuming direct pixel correspondence.

1.  **Input:** Two images $I_A$ and $I_B$ from different scenes or viewpoints.
2.  **Process:** For each pixel $(u, v)$ in $I_A$, the corresponding pixel in $I_B$ is assumed to be at the same coordinates $(u, v)$ or transformed via a known rigid pose.
3.  **Output:** A set of pixel pairs $(p_A, p_B)$ where $p_A = p_B$ under the assumption of perfect alignment.
4.  **Pitfalls:** Lacks spatial alignment accuracy when there is significant viewpoint change or non-rigid deformation [S1].

### Semantic Correspondence-Based Mapping
This strategy uses deep learning to extract dense semantic features for robust matching.

1.  **Input:** Two images $I_A$ and $I_B$.
2.  **Feature Extraction:**
    *   Pass $I_A$ and $I_B$ through a Vision Transformer (ViT) [Dosovitskiy 2020] and DINO [Amir et al. 2021] encoders to extract dense semantic feature maps $F_A$ and $F_B$.
    *   Let $F_A \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ and $F_B \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, where $C$ is the feature dimension.
3.  **Matching:**
    *   Compute the cosine similarity between feature vectors at corresponding spatial locations or across a search window.
    *   Identify matches where the semantic similarity exceeds a threshold.
4.  **Output:** A set of pixel correspondences $(p_A, p_B)$ aligned by semantic content rather than raw intensity.
5.  **Benefit:** Improves spatial matching accuracy in textureless or repetitive regions compared to identity mapping [S1].

### Co-segmentation-Based Mapping
This strategy refines alignment by segmenting images into semantically meaningful parts.

1.  **Input:** Two images $I_A$ and $I_B$.
2.  **Clustering:**
    *   Use DINO’s clustering capabilities to segment $I_A$ and $I_B$ into $K$ semantically meaningful parts.
    *   Let $S_A = \{s_{A,1}, ..., s_{A,K}\}$ and $S_B = \{s_{B,1}, ..., s_{B,K}\}$ be the sets of segmented regions.
3.  **Refinement:**
    *   Establish correspondences between regions $s_{A,i}$ and $s_{B,j}$ based on semantic label similarity.
    *   Within matched regions, refine pixel-level correspondence.
4.  **Output:** High-fidelity pixel correspondences that maintain semantic boundaries.
5.  **Benefit:** Yields finer correspondence than semantic-only mapping by enforcing structural consistency within object categories [S1].

### Joint Mapping and Pose Update
This variant fuses tracked RGB-D frames into an existing scene map by jointly optimizing geometry, poses, and features [S2].

1.  **Input:**
    *   Current tracked RGB-D frame $F_{curr}$.
    *   Existing scene map (sparse voxels/octree).
    *   Previous keyframes.
2.  **Parameters:**
    *   $N_{kf}$: Number of keyframes randomly selected for the optimization window.
    *   $N_m$: Number of rays randomly sampled for each frame in the optimization window.
3.  **Steps:**
    1.  **Window Construction:** Randomly select $N_{kf}$ keyframes from the existing map. Combine these with $F_{curr}$ to form an optimization window.
    2.  **Ray Sampling:** For each frame in the optimization window, randomly sample $N_m$ rays.
    3.  **World Transformation:** Transform the sampled rays into the world coordinate system using the estimated camera poses for each frame in the window.
    4.  **Voxel Sampling:** Sample points within the sparse voxels (octree leaf nodes) intersected by these rays.
    5.  **Rendering and Loss:** Render pixels from the sample points and calculate loss functions (e.g., photometric, geometric) as defined in the underlying rendering method (Section 4.1 of [S2]).
    6.  **Optimization:** Update the scene geometry (voxel/implicit field parameters), camera poses, and feature embeddings simultaneously to minimize the loss.
4.  **Edge Cases:** Online incremental learning is prone to network forgetting; the sliding window approach mitigates this by retaining recent context [S2].

## Subtopics

### [iMAP](11979-imap.md)
iMAP is the first NeRF-based dense online SLAM model that jointly optimizes camera pose and an implicit scene representation. It sets itself apart from its siblings by being the foundational method that introduced the iterative two-step approach of tracking and mapping to NeRF-based SLAM, upon which NICE-SLAM and subsequent methods build. It treats the scene as a continuous radiance field rather than a discrete mesh or point cloud. [iMAP](11979-imap.md)

### [RNR-Map Construction](16526-rnr-map-construction.md)
RNR-Map Construction embeds visual information from an RGB-D image into a 2D grid map (RNR-Map) using a pretrained encoder and inverse camera projection. It differs from sibling methods like [iMAP](11979-imap.md) by operating directly on latent codes to bypass the computationally expensive differentiable rendering step required for alignment, enabling high-frequency mapping. [RNR-Map Construction](16526-rnr-map-construction.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Identity Mapping:** Fastest computation ($O(1)$ per pixel), but lowest accuracy in misaligned scenes [S1].
*   **Semantic/Co-segmentation Mapping:** Higher computational cost due to ViT/DINO inference ($O(H \cdot W \cdot C)$), but robust to appearance changes and lack of texture [S1].
*   **Joint Mapping and Pose Update:** High computational cost due to ray sampling, voxel traversal, and differentiable rendering optimization within a sliding window [S2]. Requires sparse voxel structures (e.g., octrees) for efficient memory usage [S2].
*   **Scalability:** Joint mapping scales with the number of keyframes $N_{kf}$ and sampled rays $N_m$ [S2]. Semantic mapping scales with image resolution and feature dimension [S1].

## Background
Mapping strategies in the context of 3D reconstruction and SLAM have evolved from simple geometric correspondences to semantic-aware and jointly optimized neural representations. The "Identity" and "Semantic" strategies are highlighted in the context of multi-scene radiance field editing and view-consistent 3D editing, where aligning pixels between different scenes is crucial for style transfer and structural fidelity [S1]. The "Joint Mapping and Pose Update" strategy originates from voxel-based neural implicit representations for dense tracking and mapping, addressing the issue of network forgetting in online incremental learning by using a sliding window optimization approach [S2].
