# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Gradient Parallelism, Warp-Synchronous Backprop

## Overview
This family encompasses methods that accelerate the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by optimizing the computational efficiency of the differentiable rasterization backward pass and enabling distributed training across multiple GPUs. It solves the bottleneck where gradient accumulation via atomic operations in per-pixel backpropagation limits scalability, and where single-GPU memory constraints prevent training on scenes with billions of primitives. It is distinguished from sibling methods by focusing on system-level parallelism (multi-GPU partitioning) and algorithmic restructuring (per-splat vs. per-pixel gradients) rather than parameter-specific learning rates or fused kernel optimizations.

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing the gradient of the loss with respect to probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to 3D Gaussians. It explicitly accounts for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground Gaussian color accumulation and the background transmittance path, distinguishing it from standard backpropagation which treats opacity as a fixed scalar. Choose this when training requires learnable masking or occlusion handling.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself by adapting the representation density to scene complexity during optimization, rather than treating the Gaussian set as static. Choose this as the standard baseline for 3DGS reconstruction.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It iterates over Gaussians and updates the gradients of the pixels they cover, addressing the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in the per-pixel approach. It yields a 4–5× reduction in training time while maintaining numerical equivalence. Choose this for single-GPU training speedups.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs to overcome single-device memory limits. Unlike [TideGS](7350-tidegs.md), which streams data from disk on a single machine, Grendel-GS distributes the parameter table across a GPU cluster. Choose this for scenes with billions of primitives that exceed single-GPU VRAM.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details the specific algorithmic steps, data structures, and hyperparameters for 3DGS training, including densification logic, gradient thresholding, and half-precision rasterization. It distinguishes the standard implementation from generic volume rendering by specifying the exact conditions for Gaussian splitting and pruning. Choose this for implementing the core training loop.

## Background
The optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting training emerged alongside the original 3DGS paper [Kerbl et al., 2023], which introduced the differentiable rasterizer and adaptive densification. Early implementations suffered from slow convergence due to the memory bandwidth bottleneck of atomic operations during gradient accumulation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization]. Subsequent work focused on parallelizing the backward pass [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation] and scaling to large scenes via distributed computing [7349-grendel-gs]. The integration of mask-based gradients [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation] and gradient flag optimizations [9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details] further refined the stability and efficiency of the training pipeline.
