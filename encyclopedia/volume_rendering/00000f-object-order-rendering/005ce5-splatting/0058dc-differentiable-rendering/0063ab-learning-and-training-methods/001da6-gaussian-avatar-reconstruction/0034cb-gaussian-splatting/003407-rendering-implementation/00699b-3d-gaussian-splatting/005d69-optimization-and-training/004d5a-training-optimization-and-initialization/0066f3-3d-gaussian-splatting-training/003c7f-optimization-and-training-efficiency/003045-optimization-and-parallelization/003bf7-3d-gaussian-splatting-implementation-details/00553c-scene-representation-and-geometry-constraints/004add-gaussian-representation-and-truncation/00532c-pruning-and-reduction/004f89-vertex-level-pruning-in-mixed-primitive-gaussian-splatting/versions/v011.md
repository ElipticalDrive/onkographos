# Vertex-level Pruning in Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Mixed-Primitive Vertex Pruning

## Overview
Vertex-level pruning is a refinement mechanism for mixed-primitive Gaussian Splatting that removes redundant vertices from Gaussian triangles and lines during training to simplify the representation [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting which only performs primitive-level opacity pruning, this method dynamically converts complex primitives (triangles) into simpler ones (lines or ellipses) based on vertex geometry, reducing storage overhead and representation clutter [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A set of mixed-primitive Gaussians, including Gaussian triangles (3 vertices) and Gaussian lines (2 vertices), along with their current vertex positions $\mu$.
*   **Output:** A refined set of primitives where redundant vertices are removed or primitives are downgraded in complexity.

**Data Structures**
The method operates on the vertex attributes of the primitives defined in [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md).
*   **Gaussian Triangle:** Defined by three vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3 \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Gaussian Line:** Defined by two vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2 \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Gaussian Ellipse:** Defined by a single center vertex $\mu_1 \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a covariance matrix (implicitly derived from the original triangle/line parameters or treated as a point-centered primitive with anisotropic scaling).

**Parameters**
*   $\omega_{dist}$: Threshold for vertex proximity. Default: $0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\omega_{pear}$: Threshold for collinearity detection. Default: $0.9$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm**
Vertex-level pruning is triggered concurrently with primitive-level pruning [S1, unofficial]. For each active primitive, the following checks are performed:

1.  **Gaussian Triangle to Ellipse:** If all three vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$ are close to each other, prune $\mu_2$ and $\mu_3$, converting the primitive into a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$.
    *   Condition: $\max(\|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|_2, \|\mu_1 - \mu_3\|_2, \|\mu_2 - \mu_3\|_2) < \omega_{dist}$.
2.  **Gaussian Triangle to Line:** If the vertices are not close (per $\omega_{dist}$) but are nearly collinear, convert the primitive into a Gaussian line.
    *   Condition: The absolute value of the Pearson correlation coefficient computed from the three vertices exceeds $\omega_{pear}$.
3.  **Gaussian Line to Ellipse:** If the two vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2$ are close to each other, prune $\mu_2$, reducing the primitive to a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$.
    *   Condition: $\|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|_2 < \omega_{dist}$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Precision:** The distance threshold $\omega_{dist}$ is absolute. In scenes with varying scales, this may require normalization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If vertices are exactly identical, the distance is 0, triggering ellipse conversion.
*   **Collinearity Metric:** The exact formula for "Pearson correlation coefficient computed from these vertices" is not explicitly defined in the source text [S1, unofficial]. Implementers must determine the specific statistical computation intended (e.g., correlation of projected coordinates) or use a standard collinearity metric if the source's specific metric is unavailable.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Storage:** Reduces average storage size for vertex coordinates by 24% compared to the variant without vertex pruning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The source does not provide specific timing benchmarks for the pruning step itself, but notes that the mechanism refines the representation into "less messy" ones, potentially improving rendering efficiency by reducing primitive complexity [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The pruning is coupled with primitive-level pruning. It does not reduce the representation to only point-centered ellipses because densification (cloning/splitting) continues during training [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method is part of the Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting (MP-GS) framework, which extends standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by using Gaussian triangles and lines in addition to ellipses [S1, unofficial]. The vertex-level pruning strategy was introduced to handle the increased complexity of mixed primitives, where redundant vertices can arise during optimization [S1, unofficial].
