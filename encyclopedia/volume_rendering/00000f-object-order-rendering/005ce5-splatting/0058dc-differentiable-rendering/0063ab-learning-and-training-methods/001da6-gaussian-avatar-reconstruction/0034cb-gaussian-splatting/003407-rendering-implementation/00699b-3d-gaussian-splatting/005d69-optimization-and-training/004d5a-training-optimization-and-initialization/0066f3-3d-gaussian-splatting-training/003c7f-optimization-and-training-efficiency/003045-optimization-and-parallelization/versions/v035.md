# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed 3DGS Training, Per-Splat Backpropagation, Masked Gradient Computation

## Overview
This family encompasses methods that accelerate the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and enable the optimization of extended representations, such as probabilistic masks or distributed parameter sets. It solves the computational bottlenecks of the standard differentiable rasterization pipeline, specifically the $O(N \cdot P)$ complexity of the backward pass and the memory limits of single-GPU training . It is distinguished from standard optimization by restructuring the gradient flow (per-splat vs. per-pixel), extending the differentiable core to non-Gaussian parameters (masks), and distributing the state across hardware clusters .

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
Computes gradients for probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly backpropagating through the mask-modulated opacity in both the foreground accumulation and background transmittance paths.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative loop integrating dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, cloning, splitting) within the optimization steps. It distinguishes itself by coupling representation density adaptation directly with the gradient descent loop.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
Replaces the per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat approach. It distinguishes itself by iterating over Gaussians to update pixel gradients, eliminating atomic contention and reducing training time by 4–5×.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed framework partitioning Gaussians across multiple GPUs. It distinguishes itself from single-machine streaming methods by enabling training on billions of primitives via inter-GPU communication.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Provides concrete implementation parameters, including half-precision handling, specific densification schedules, and variants for camera localization and multi-scale constraints.

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization pipeline was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . Subsequent work addressed its scalability and flexibility: MaskGaussian introduced mask-based gradients , Parallelization for Backpropagation optimized the backward pass , and Grendel-GS extended training to multi-GPU clusters . Implementation details and variants (DiffGS, GraphiXS, etc.) have been documented in various technical reports and papers .
