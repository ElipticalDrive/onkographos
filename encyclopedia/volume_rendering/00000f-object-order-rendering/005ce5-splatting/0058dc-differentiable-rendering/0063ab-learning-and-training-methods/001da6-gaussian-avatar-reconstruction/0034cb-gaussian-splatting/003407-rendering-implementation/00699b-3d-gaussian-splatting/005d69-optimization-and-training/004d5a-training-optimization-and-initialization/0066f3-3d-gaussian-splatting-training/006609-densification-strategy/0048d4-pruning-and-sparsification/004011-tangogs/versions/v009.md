# TangoGS

Also called: Gauss What You Need

## Overview
TangoGS is a density control strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that automatically scales the number of Gaussian primitives to match the complexity of the input capture without per-scene tuning [S1, unofficial]. It combines a capture-derived "learning allowance" (model sizing based on track-adjusted pixel count) with training-based quality feedback (PSNR-driven pruning) to determine the final Gaussian population [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on fixed thresholds or per-capture calibration, TangoGS uses a shared configuration across diverse scene scales, achieving comparable or better PSNR with significantly fewer Gaussians than baselines like LeGS or 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
TangoGS operates on the set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ defined by the parent [Parent]. The method manages the population size through three phases: allowance calculation, budgeted densification with soft pruning, and final selection.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Capture metadata: number of images $N_{\text{img}}$ (including held-out), mean track length, sparse model point count.
    *   Training metrics: mean per-image PSNR $P$ from the preceding epoch.
    *   Hyperparameters: $T_{\text{ref}}$, $g$, $s$, $c$, $P^*$, $h$.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   A pruned subset of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_{final}$ with size $N_G$.

### Data Structures and Layout
*   **Gaussian Parameters:** Unclustered, then filtered, then reclustered into Morton-ordered groups of 128 elements to align with batch removal operations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sensitivity Scores:** Accumulated per-Gaussian scores from the LiteGS kernel, mapped back to original indices via a culling map [S1, unofficial].
*   **Adam Buffers:** Reclustered alongside parameters to maintain contiguous memory access [S1, unofficial].

### Constants and Parameters
*   $T_{\text{ref}} = 2,462,476$: Reference count, rounded geometric mean of linear allowances over 13 standard scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   $g \approx 0.063362106$: Effective scaling factor from track-adjusted pixels to Gaussians, implemented via nested rounding [S1, unofficial].
*   $s = 1.7$: Scaling factor for the final selection keep fraction [S1, unofficial].
*   $c = 0.0075$: Coefficient for PSNR-based threshold adjustment [S1, unofficial].
*   $P^* = 20 \text{ dB}$: Threshold PSNR above which no removal occurs [S1, unofficial].
*   $h$: Number of remaining events in a reference pruning schedule spaced 8% of total training iterations apart [S1, unofficial].
*   Split/Clone Threshold: $0.001$ (maximum gradient magnitude) [S1, unofficial].
*   Opacity Decay: Factor $0.5$ per step, floor $1/128$ [S1, unofficial].
*   SH Learning Rate Divisor: $20$ [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Calculate Learning Allowance ($T_{\text{cap}}$):**
    Compute the linear allowance $T_{\text{lin}}$ based on the capture's track-adjusted pixel count $\mathcal{A}$ (derived from $N_{\text{img}}$ and track data):
    $$ T_{\text{lin}} = \text{round} \left[ 0.90 \cdot \text{round}(0.07040234 \cdot \mathcal{A}) \right] $$
    Compute the capped allowance:
    $$ T_{\text{cap}} = \text{round} \left[ T_{\text{ref}} \cdot \left( \frac{T_{\text{lin}}}{T_{\text{ref}}} \right)^{0.5} \right] $$
    [S1, unofficial]

2.  **Initialize Training Schedule:**
    *   Densification starts at 2% of total training iterations.
    *   Soft pruning begins at ~9% of total training iterations.
    *   Densification ends at 80% of total training iterations.
    *   Growth events are spaced 5 epochs apart for runs $\ge 150$ epochs; for shorter runs, spacing is reduced proportionally to a minimum of 1 epoch [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Budgeted Densification (at each growth event):**
    *   Calculate the adaptation target $T_{\text{adapt}}$ using the mean per-image PSNR $P$ from the preceding epoch:
        $$ \rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 \cdot P $$
        $$ T_{\text{adapt}} = \rho \cdot T_{\text{cap}} $$
        Note: The intercept $0.7382$ results from rounding the main paper's formula $\rho = \frac{1}{s} - c(P - P^*)$ with $s=1.7, c=0.0075, P^*=20$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Ramp the Gaussian count linearly from the initial point count to $T_{\text{adapt}}$ over the growth window using standard splitting/cloning based on gradient magnitude $> 0.001$ [S1, unofficial][Parent].
    *   Newly created Gaussians are exempt from pruning until they have trained [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Soft Pruning (at each growth event, if applicable):**
    *   Rank Gaussians present *before* the growth phase using sensitivity scores (accumulated over the epoch via LiteGS kernel) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Keep the top fraction $\rho$ of Gaussians.
    *   Remove the rest. Removal counts are rounded down to the nearest multiple of 128 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Guard: If removal would exceed 90% of the population, abort the prune [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Final Selection (at 80% of training iterations):**
    *   Let $N_{\text{pre}}$ be the population entering this event.
    *   Calculate the final keep fraction:
        $$ f = \min(1, s \cdot \rho) $$
        where $\rho$ is recalibrated from the preceding epoch's PSNR [S1, unofficial].
    *   The retained count is:
        $$ N_G = N_{\text{pre}} \cdot f^h $$
    *   Rank all $N_{\text{pre}}$ Gaussians by sensitivity score.
    *   Keep the top $N_G$ Gaussians.
    *   Optimize this final population for the remainder of training [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def tango_gs_training(gaussians, capture_metadata, total_iterations):
    """
    Main training loop for TangoGS.
    """
    # Constants
    T_REF = 2462476
    G = 0.063362106
    S = 1.7
    C = 0.0075
    P_STAR = 20.0
    INTERCEPT = 0.7382  # Rounded from 1/1.7 - 0.0075*(20-20)
    
    # 1. Calculate Allowance
    # A is track-adjusted pixel count derived from capture_metadata
    A = calculate_track_adjusted_pixels(capture_metadata)
    T_lin = round(0.90 * round(0.07040234 * A))
    T_cap = round(T_REF * (T_lin / T_REF)**0.5)
    
    # Schedule parameters
    densify_start = 0.02 * total_iterations
    prune_start = 0.09 * total_iterations
    densify_end = 0.80 * total_iterations
    
    # Epoch spacing logic (simplified)
    epoch_spacing = 5
    if total_epochs < 150:
        epoch_spacing = max(1, 5 * (total_epochs / 150))
        
    current_gaussian_count = len(gaussians)
    initial_count = current_gaussian_count
    
    # 2. Training Loop
    for iter in range(total_iterations):
        # Standard 3DGS training step (forward, loss, backward)
        loss = render_and_compute_loss(gaussians)
        optimizer.step()
        
        # Quality Feedback: Calculate mean per-image PSNR P
        P = compute_mean_per_image_psnr(gaussians)
        
        # 3. Growth Events
        if iter >= densify_start and iter <= densify_end:
            if is_growth_event(iter, epoch_spacing):
                # Calculate adaptation target
                rho = INTERCEPT - C * P
                T_adapt = rho * T_cap
                
                # Budgeted densification: ramp up to T_adapt
                # This involves splitting/cloning existing Gaussians
                # based on gradient magnitude > 0.001
                target_count = interpolate_count(initial_count, T_adapt, iter, densify_start, densify_end)
                densify_gaussians(gaussians, target_count, threshold=0.001)
                
                # Soft Pruning (if after prune_start)
                if iter >= prune_start:
                    # Rank Gaussians present BEFORE growth by sensitivity score
                    scores = compute_sensitivity_scores(gaussians)
                    keep_fraction = rho
                    keep_count = round(len(gaussians) * keep_fraction)
                    
                    # Guard against catastrophic collapse
                    if keep_count < 0.1 * len(gaussians):
                        abort_prune()
                    else:
                        # Remove bottom (1-rho) Gaussians
                        # Round removal count to multiple of 128
                        remove_count = len(gaussians) - keep_count
                        remove_count = (remove_count // 128) * 128
                        prune_gaussians(gaussians, remove_count, scores)
                        
        # 4. Final Selection
        if iter == densify_end:
            N_pre = len(gaussians)
            # Recalibrate rho from preceding epoch
            rho_final = INTERCEPT - C * P
            f = min(1.0, S * rho_final)
            
            # Calculate retained count
            # h is number of remaining events in reference schedule (8% spacing)
            # For implementation, h is a constant derived from total_iterations
            N_G = round(N_pre * (f ** h))
            
            # Rank and keep top N_G
            scores = compute_sensitivity_scores(gaussians)
            final_gaussians = select_top_k(gaussians, scores, N_G)
            
            # Optimize final population
            gaussians = final_gaussians
            break

    return gaussians
```

## Subtopics

### [Capture-Conditioned Learning Allowance](10214-capture-conditioned-learning-allowance.md)
Determines the target Gaussian population size based on capture characteristics (track-adjusted pixels) to replace fixed defaults. It ensures model budget invariance to redundant views, unlike sibling methods that scale with view count or SfM density.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to standard 3DGS, with minor overhead for sensitivity score accumulation and periodic pruning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly lower than standard 3DGS and baselines like LeGS. On 13 standard benchmark scenes, TangoGS uses 48% fewer Gaussians while matching mean PSNR [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Matches or exceeds baseline PSNR on 21 captures (13 standard, 8 large) with a single shared configuration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Automatic scaling across scene sizes; no per-capture tuning required; robust to redundant views.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate track data and sparse models for allowance calculation; sensitivity scoring adds minor computational overhead [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When deploying 3DGS on diverse captures without manual hyperparameter tuning.
*   **When not to use:** When strict real-time training constraints prohibit the sensitivity score accumulation or when capture metadata is unavailable.

## Background
TangoGS was introduced in the paper "Gauss What You Need: Compact Gaussian Splatting Across Scene Scales" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on LiteGS for sensitivity scoring and integrates with standard 3DGS optimization. The method addresses the limitation of fixed Gaussian counts or per-scene tuning in previous density control strategies [S1, unofficial].
