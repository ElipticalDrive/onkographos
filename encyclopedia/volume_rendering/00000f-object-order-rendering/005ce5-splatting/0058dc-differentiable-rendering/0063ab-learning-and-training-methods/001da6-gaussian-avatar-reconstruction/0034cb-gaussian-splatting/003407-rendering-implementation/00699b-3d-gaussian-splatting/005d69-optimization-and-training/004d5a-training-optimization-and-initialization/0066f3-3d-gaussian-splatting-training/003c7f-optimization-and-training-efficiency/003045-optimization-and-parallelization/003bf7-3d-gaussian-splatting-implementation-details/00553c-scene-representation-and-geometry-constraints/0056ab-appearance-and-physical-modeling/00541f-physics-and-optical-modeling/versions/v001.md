# Physics and Optical Modeling


## Overview
Physics and Optical Modeling extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) image formation pipeline to account for physical light transport phenomena, specifically volumetric attenuation and scattering. It solves the problem of inaccurate rendering in non-vacuum media (such as underwater environments) or the need for geometrically consistent surface extraction by reformulating the discrete alpha-blending rasterization into a continuous volumetric integration framework. It distinguishes itself from standard 3DGS by introducing learnable medium parameters (attenuation and backscatter coefficients) or by deriving continuous attenuation fields from Gaussian densities to ensure unbiased surface extraction, thereby addressing color distortion and depth inconsistency issues inherent in the base method.

## Shared principles
The family shares the foundational assumption that the scene is represented by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$, where each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (or rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\mathbf{w})$. The rendering process involves evaluating the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{w}$ and integrating the contribution of these primitives.

The core mathematical mechanism involves the Gaussian density function:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$
where $\mathbf{x}$ is a point in 3D space.

In the context of optical modeling, the standard alpha-blending equation is modified or replaced by a volumetric rendering equation that incorporates medium properties. The general form for a ray passing through a medium with direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$ integrates the Gaussian contributions weighted by the medium's transmittance. The transmittance $T$ along a ray segment of length $z$ is modulated by the direct attenuation factor $e^{-B_d z}$. The backscatter signal, which represents light scattered into the camera ray from the medium itself, is modeled as $B_\infty (1 - e^{-B_b z})$, where $B_\infty$ is the infinite backscatter color and $B_b$ is the backscatter coefficient.

The optimization process for these physical parameters typically involves minimizing a loss function that combines photometric error (e.g., $\ell_1$ norm) and structural similarity (D-SSIM), often augmented with a guidance loss term that constrains the learned medium parameters to be physically plausible or consistent with depth-dependent observations.

### [Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting with Backscatter Loss](13980-underwater-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-backscatter-loss.md)
This method adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting to underwater environments by integrating ocean optics models, specifically learning direct attenuation ($B_d$), infinite backscatter ($B_\infty$), and backscatter coefficient ($B_b$) alongside scene geometry. It solves the problem of color distortion and visibility loss in aquatic scenes. Unlike vanilla 3DGS, which struggles with underwater color palettes, or NeRF-based methods, this approach maintains real-time inference speeds while significantly improving reconstruction quality at large distances. ### Backscatter Estimation and Optimization
This minor variant details the specific algorithmic steps for estimating backscatter parameters in underwater scenes.
- **Step**: The depth map (from the current scene estimate) is divided into 10 evenly-spaced clusters covering the range from $\min(z)$ to $\max(z)$. Within each cluster, pixels in the ground-truth image whose RGB triplets are below the bottom 1st percentile are identified. These triplets serve as an overestimate of the backscatter $B_s$. Nonlinear least squares fitting is then used on pairs $(B_s, z)$ to estimate parameters $B_\infty$ and $B_b$. This estimation is re-performed every 500 optimization steps to guide convergence [S1].
- **Formula**: The optimization minimizes the loss $L = \sum_{k=1}^K (1 - \lambda_1) \| I_k^{gt} - I_k^r(\Theta) \|_{\ell_1} + \lambda_1 L_{D-SSIM}(I_k^{gt}, I_k^r(\Theta))$, where $K$ is the number of images and $\lambda_1 \in [0,1]$ [S1].
- **Parameter**: The backscatter parameters are bounded: $B_\infty \in [0, 1]$ and $B_b \in [0, 5]$ [S1].
- **Pitfall**: The camera sensor must have a linear response to light. In-camera processed .jpg images or gamma-corrected images cannot be used unless the non-linearities can be reversed, as the physical model relies on linear light intensity [S1].

## Background
The concept of modeling physics in Gaussian Splatting emerged as the technique was applied to challenging environments where the vacuum assumption of standard 3DGS fails. The underwater adaptation, referred to as "Gaussian Splashing," was introduced to handle the significant color attenuation and backscatter present in aquatic scenes [S1]. The continuous attenuation model was developed to bridge the gap between the discrete rasterization of 3DGS and the continuous volumetric rendering required for accurate surface extraction, addressing inconsistencies found in earlier approximations.
