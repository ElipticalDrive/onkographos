# Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction
Also called: Animatable Gaussian Avatars, Mesh-Anchored Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction refers to a family of methods that reconstruct animatable, photorealistic 3D human or head avatars from video data by representing the subject as a collection of 3D Gaussians anchored to a parametric mesh prior (e.g., SMPL for bodies, FLAME for heads) [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md, unofficial]. It solves the challenge of maintaining topological consistency and rigging capabilities in dynamic reconstruction, which standard [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) lacks due to its unstructured primitive representation [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, unofficial]. Unlike implicit neural radiance fields (NeRFs) or fixed geometric meshes, this family explicitly binds learnable Gaussian primitives to a parametric model, enabling high-fidelity rendering and pose-controllable animation [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares the following mechanisms, terms, and math, building on the splatting principles of primitive representation, transformation, rasterization, and compositing [23-splatting.md].

**1. Parametric Anchoring**
Gaussians are initialized on the surface of a parametric mesh (SMPL/FLAME) in a canonical rest pose [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md, unofficial]. Each Gaussian $i$ is associated with a specific triangle $t$ or vertex of the mesh [1477-gaussian-avatars.md]. The position $\mathbf{p}_i$ is constrained to lie within the barycentric coordinates of its associated triangle:
$$ \mathbf{p}_i = u \mathbf{v}_1 + v \mathbf{v}_2 + w \mathbf{v}_3 $$
where $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3$ are mesh vertices and $u+v+w=1, u,v,w \ge 0$ [1477-gaussian-avatars.md].

**2. Deformation Pipeline**
To animate the avatar, Gaussians are deformed from the canonical pose to a target pose $\theta$ and expression $\beta$ [S1, 1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md, unofficial].
*   **Rigid Deformation:** Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) transforms the canonical position $\mathbf{p}_i$ using bone transformations $\mathbf{T}_j(\theta)$ and skinning weights $w_{i,j}$ [8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md]:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{rigid} = \sum_{j=1}^{N_{bones}} w_{i,j} \mathbf{T}_j(\theta) \mathbf{p}_i $$
*   **Non-Rigid Deformation:** A residual offset $\Delta \mathbf{p}$ is predicted by a deformation network (e.g., MLP) conditioned on pose and canonical position to capture skin deformation [8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md]:
    $$ \mathbf{p}'_i = \mathbf{p}_{rigid} + \Delta \mathbf{p} $$
*   **Covariance Update:** The covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ is transformed using the Jacobian $J$ of the deformation map to maintain volume preservation and orientation [8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md]:
    $$ \Sigma'_i = J \Sigma_i J^T + \sigma^2 I $$
    where $\sigma^2 I$ is a small regularization term [G].

**3. Differentiable Rendering and Optimization**
The deformed Gaussians are rendered using differentiable 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, unofficial]. The training loop minimizes a loss function combining photometric error ($\mathcal{L}_{L1}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}$) and perceptual metrics ($\mathcal{L}_{MS-SSIM}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{LPIPS}$) [8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md]. Gradients flow from the rendered image to the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, SH coefficients) and network weights [8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md].

## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md)
The foundational method for 3D scene representation using anisotropic 3D Gaussians optimized directly from images. It sets the rendering and optimization baseline for this family but lacks explicit rigging.

### [GaussianShader](1470-gaussianshader.md)
Extends the Gaussian representation with physical material parameters (diffusiveness, specularity, roughness, normals) to enable physically-based shading of specular objects. It builds on this family by adding appearance complexity beyond simple color.

### [SpotlessSplats](1476-spotlesssplats.md)
Enhances robustness in casual captures by detecting and ignoring transient distractors (e.g., moving people, shadows) using outlier detection in a pre-trained feature space. It filters noise that standard avatar reconstruction might interpret as geometry.

### [Gaussian Avatars](1477-gaussian-avatars.md)
Reconstructs photorealistic, animatable head avatars by anchoring 3D Gaussians to a FLAME mesh. It solves topological consistency by constraining Gaussians to mesh triangles, enabling high-fidelity rendering and relighting.

### [Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars](1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md)
Supports dynamic relighting under novel illumination by decoupling geometry (FLAME-rigged Gaussians) from appearance (relightable BRDF). It distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling surface reflectance for interactive relighting.

### [NoPoSplat](1484-noposplat.md)
Eliminates the requirement for known camera poses by anchoring the scene to a canonical coordinate system and using intrinsics token embeddings. While primarily for general scenes, its pose-agnostic approach is relevant for avatar reconstruction from uncalibrated video.

### [SMPL-based rigid/non-rigid deformation](8385-smpl-based-rigid-non-rigid-deformation.md)
Provides the underlying deformation mechanism (rigid LBS + non-rigid offsets) that many children implement or extend. It ensures structural integrity during animation.

- [S1, unofficial] A holistic scene reconstruction method including humans, vehicles, and static background.

### SplatArmor

### BakedAvatar
- [S3] A representation for real-time 4D head avatar rendering targeting photorealism and efficiency on commodity devices.
- [S3] Introduces a learned manifold closely aligned with the head surface.
- [S3] Extracts layered mesh proxies from the manifold to approximate volumetric rendering while enabling fast rasterization.

## Background
Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction emerged as a solution to the limitations of NeRFs and static Gaussian Splatting in dynamic human modeling. Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting [946-gaussian-splatting.md] provides real-time rendering but lacks the structural priors needed for consistent animation [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, unofficial]. By anchoring Gaussians to parametric models like SMPL and FLAME, methods in this family achieve pose-controllable avatars with high fidelity [S1, 1477-gaussian-avatars.md, unofficial]. The field has evolved to include relighting capabilities [1482-relightable-gaussian-codec-avatars.md] and robustness to casual captures [1476-spotlesssplats.md]. Production-grade implementations, such as Meta's Codec Avatars 3D, demonstrate the viability of GS-based avatars for telepresence [S1, unofficial].
