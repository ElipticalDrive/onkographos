# 4D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 4D Gaussian Representation, 4D-GS

## Overview
4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to model dynamic scenes by treating time as a fourth dimension, enabling the reconstruction and real-time rendering of 3D videos from arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. Unlike static 3DGS which assumes a fixed scene, 4DGS captures spatiotemporal deformations of position, rotation, scale, and appearance, allowing for the generation of novel views at any continuous time $t$ [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from deformation-field methods (e.g., Dynamic3DGS) by maintaining a canonical set of Gaussians transformed by a learned field, and from naive temporal extensions by using coherent 4D covariance structures or explicit spatiotemporal encoders to ensure smooth motion and appearance interpolation [S5][S14, unofficial].

## How it works
4DGS methods generally follow one of two architectural paradigms: **Native 4D Gaussian Slicing** (Yang et al.) or **Canonical Deformation Fields** (Wu et al.). Both aim to render a 2D image $\mathcal{I}(u,v,t)$ for a given camera pose and timestamp $t$.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Camera extrinsics/intrinsics $M$, timestamp $t$, ground truth images $\mathcal{I}_{gt}$.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered image $\hat{\mathcal{I}}$, deformed 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ (for deformation methods) or conditional 3D Gaussians (for slicing methods).

### Data Structures
The primitive is a Gaussian. In Native 4DGS, the Gaussian is a 4D entity. In Canonical Deformation methods, the Gaussian is stored in a canonical 3D space with associated feature vectors for deformation prediction.

**1. Native 4D Gaussian (Yang et al. [S7, S14, unofficial])**
*   **Mean:** $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (spatial $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:3}$, temporal $\mu_4$).
*   **Covariance:** $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$, symmetric positive definite.
*   **Rotation:** Represented by two unit quaternions $q_l, q_r \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (left and right isoclinic rotations) forming a 4D rotation matrix $R = L(q_l)R(q_r)$ [S14, unofficial].
*   **Scale:** $S = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z, s_t)$ [S14, unofficial].
*   **Appearance:** 4D Spherindrical Harmonics (4DSH) coefficients. Basis: $Z_{nl}^m(t, \theta, \phi) = \cos(\frac{2\pi n}{T}t) Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$, combining Fourier series for time and 3D SH for space [S14, unofficial].

**2. Canonical 3D Gaussian + Deformation Field (Wu et al. [S5])**
*   **Canonical Gaussians:** $\mathcal{G} = \{\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{R}, \mathbf{S}, \alpha, \mathbf{c}\}$ in 3D space.
*   **Spatial-Temporal Structure Encoder $\mathcal{H}$:** Uses 6 multi-resolution HexPlane/K-Plane modules $R_l(i,j)$ for pairs $(i,j) \in \{(x,y), (x,z), (y,z), (x,t), (y,t), (z,t)\}$ and a tiny MLP $\phi_d$ [S5].
    *   Feature query: $f_h = \bigcup_l \prod_l \text{interp}(R_l(i,j))$ where $\text{interp}$ is bilinear interpolation at grid vertices [S5].
*   **Deformation Decoder:** Predicts offsets $\Delta \boldsymbol{\mu}, \Delta \mathbf{R}, \Delta \mathbf{S}$ based on canonical position and time $t$ [S5].

### Numbered Steps

#### Method A: Native 4D Gaussian Slicing (Yang et al.)
1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Sample points in a 4D bounding box or use COLMAP points from the first frame [S9, S14, unofficial].
    *   Initialize 4D means $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, temporal mean $\mu_4$ (scene duration), and temporal scale $s_t$ (initially half scene duration to leverage anisotropy) [S9, S14, unofficial].
    *   Initialize 4D rotation quaternions $q_l, q_r$ to identity $(1,0,0,0)$ [S9, unofficial].
2.  **Rendering at time $t$:**
    *   Compute marginal temporal probability: $p(t) = \mathcal{N}(t; \mu_4, \Sigma_{4,4})$ [S14, unofficial].
    *   Compute conditional 3D mean: $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3D|t} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:3} + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,4} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,4}^{-1} (t - \mu_4)$ [S7, S14, unofficial].
    *   Compute conditional 3D covariance: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{3D|t} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,1:3} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,4} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,1:3}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,1:3}$ [S7, S14, unofficial].
    *   Project $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3D|t}$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{3D|t}$ to 2D image plane using standard 3DGS projection [S11, unofficial].
    *   Render using the standard alpha-blending equation, weighted by $p(t)$:
        $$ \mathcal{I}(u, v, t) = \sum_{i=1}^N p_i(t) p_i(u, v|t) \alpha_i c_i(d) \prod_{j=1}^N (1 - p_j(t) p_j(u, v|t) \alpha_j) $$
        where $p_i(u,v|t)$ is the 2D Gaussian probability [S14, unofficial].
3.  **Culling:**
    *   Cull Gaussians if outside the view frustum OR if $p_i(t)$ is negligible (not present at timestamp $t$) [S3, S14, unofficial].

#### Method B: Canonical Deformation Field (Wu et al.)
1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize canonical 3D Gaussians using SfM points [S5].
    *   Warm-up: Optimize static 3DGS for 3000 iterations without deformation [S5].
2.  **Deformation Prediction at time $t$:**
    *   Encode spatial-temporal features for each Gaussian using $\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{G}, t)$ via HexPlane queries [S5].
    *   Decode offsets: $(\Delta \boldsymbol{\mu}, \Delta \mathbf{R}, \Delta \mathbf{S}) = \phi_d(f_h)$ [S5].
    *   Apply offsets to canonical Gaussians: $\mathcal{G}' = \{\boldsymbol{\mu} + \Delta \boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{R} \cdot \Delta \mathbf{R}, \mathbf{S} \cdot \Delta \mathbf{S}, \alpha, \mathbf{c}\}$ [S5].
3.  **Rendering:**
    *   Splat the deformed 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ using standard 3DGS rasterizer [S5].
4.  **Optimization:**
    *   Loss: $\mathcal{L} = |\hat{\mathcal{I}} - \mathcal{I}| + \mathcal{L}_{tv}$, where $\mathcal{L}_{tv}$ is grid-based total variation loss [S5].
    *   Train with batch sampling of timestamps to prevent temporal flickering [S14, unofficial].

### Pseudocode (Native 4DGS Slicing)

```python
import numpy as np

def render_4dgs(gaussians, camera, timestamp):
    """
    Render a frame for a specific timestamp using Native 4D Gaussian Slicing.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of Gaussian objects. Each has:
            - mu: (4,) array [x, y, z, t_mean]
            - Sigma: (4, 4) covariance matrix
            - alpha: float
            - sh_coeffs: 4D SH coefficients
            - R_4d: 4x4 rotation matrix (from quaternions)
            - S_4d: (4,) scale vector [sx, sy, sz, st]
        camera: Camera object with projection matrix P and view matrix V.
        timestamp: float, the time t to render.
        
    Returns:
        image: (H, W, 3) rendered image.
    """
    # 1. Compute Marginal Temporal Probability p(t)
    # p(t) = N(t; mu_t, sigma_tt)
    mu_t = gaussians.mu[:, 3]
    sigma_tt = np.diag(gaussians.Sigma)[:, 3] # Assuming diagonal for simplicity or extract Sigma[3,3]
    # More precisely: Sigma_4,4 is the element at index 3,3 of the 4x4 covariance
    sigma_44 = gaussians.Sigma[:, 3, 3] # Shape (N,)
    p_t = gaussian_pdf(timestamp, mu_t, sigma_44)
    
    # 2. Compute Conditional 3D Mean and Covariance
    # mu_3D|t = mu_1:3 + Sigma_1:3,4 * Sigma_4,4^-1 * (t - mu_4)
    mu_13 = gaussians.mu[:, :3] # Shape (N, 3)
    mu_4 = gaussians.mu[:, 3]   # Shape (N,)
    
    # Sigma_1:3,4 is the first 3 rows, 4th column of Sigma
    sigma_134 = gaussians.Sigma[:, :3, 3] # Shape (N, 3)
    
    # Delta t
    delta_t = timestamp - mu_4 # Shape (N,)
    
    # Conditional Mean
    # mu_3D|t = mu_13 + (sigma_134 / sigma_44) * delta_t
    # Note: sigma_44 is scalar per Gaussian
    mu_cond = mu_13 + (sigma_134 / sigma_44[:, np.newaxis]) * delta_t[:, np.newaxis]
    
    # Sigma_3D|t = Sigma_1:3,1:3 - Sigma_1:3,4 * Sigma_4,4^-1 * Sigma_4,1:3
    # Sigma_4,1:3 is the 4th row, first 3 columns (transpose of sigma_134)
    sigma_1313 = gaussians.Sigma[:, :3, :3] # Shape (N, 3, 3)
    
    # Outer product: sigma_134.T @ sigma_134 is (3, N) @ (N, 3) -> (3, 3) per Gaussian?
    # Actually: Sigma_1:3,4 is (N, 3). Sigma_4,1:3 is (N, 3).
    # Term is Sigma_1:3,4 * (1/sigma_44) * Sigma_4,1:3
    # This results in a (N, 3, 3) matrix.
    correction = np.einsum('ni,ni->nij', sigma_134, sigma_134) / sigma_44[:, np.newaxis, np.newaxis]
    sigma_cond = sigma_1313 - correction
    
    # 3. Project to 2D
    # Standard 3DGS projection:
    # mu_2d = P * V * [mu_3D; 1]
    # J = Jacobian of projection
    # Sigma_2D = J * Sigma_3D * J^T
    mu_2d, J, valid_mask = project_to_2d(mu_cond, sigma_cond, camera)
    
    # 4. Rasterize
    # Filter by bounding box and p(t) threshold
    visible = valid_mask & (p_t > 1e-5)
    
    # Sort by depth
    depths = mu_2d[visible, 2] # Assuming z is depth
    indices = np.argsort(depths)
    
    # Alpha blending
    image = rasterize_alpha_blending(
        mu_2d[visible][indices],
        sigma_cond[visible][indices],
        gaussians.alpha[visible][indices],
        p_t[visible][indices],
        gaussians.sh_coeffs[visible][indices],
        camera.resolution
    )
    
    return image

def gaussian_pdf(t, mu, sigma_sq):
    """1D Gaussian PDF."""
    return np.exp(-0.5 * ((t - mu) ** 2) / sigma_sq) / np.sqrt(2 * np.pi * sigma_sq)
```

### Parameters
*   **Densification Threshold:** 5e-5 for D-NeRF, 2e-4 for Plenoptic [S9, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights (Native 4DGS):** $\lambda_1=0.2$ (L1), $\lambda_2=0.01$ (Entropy), $\lambda_3=0.05$ (4D Consistency) [S9, unofficial]. $\lambda_2=0$ for Plenoptic [S9, unofficial].
*   **Initial Time Scale:** Half of total scene duration [S14, unofficial].
*   **Training Steps:** 20,000 (D-NeRF), 30,000 (Plenoptic) [S9, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Temporal Culling:** Points must be culled if they are not present at the requested timestamp, not just outside the frustum [S3, unofficial].
*   **Entropy Loss:** Can cause PSNR degradation on Plenoptic dataset due to dense views and transparent objects; recommended only for opaque surfaces and sparse views [S9, unofficial].
*   **No-4DRot Variant:** Disabling 4D rotation updates reduces temporal variance, impairing motion fitting and causing flicker/blur [S14, unofficial].
*   **Urban Scenes:** Standard indoor bounding boxes fail for infinite-distance backgrounds; requires sky masks and sparse depth supervision [S14, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Spacetime Gaussians (STG)](5997-spacetime-gaussians-stg.md)
Spacetime Gaussians (STG) is a 4D Gaussian Splatting method that models dynamic scenes using native 4D primitives, enabling real-time rendering of any arbitrary time slice without per-frame optimization. It distinguishes itself from MLP-based deformation approaches like DGS by explicitly parameterizing temporal opacity envelopes and polynomial trajectories for each Gaussian, allowing for smooth interpolation of geometry and appearance between discrete keyframes.

### [4D-Rotor Gaussian Splatting](6146-4d-rotor-gaussian-splatting.md)
4D-Rotor Gaussian Splatting (4DRotorGS) is a differentiable rendering method for dynamic scenes that lifts 3D Gaussians into 4D space $(x, y, z, t)$ using a rotor-based rotation representation from Clifford algebra. Unlike sibling methods such as [Real-time 4DGS](7335-real-time-4dgs.md) which use dual-quaternions that entangle spatial and temporal rotations, 4DRotorGS provides a spatial-temporal separable and interpretable rotation representation. It avoids the numerical instability of directly inverting the 3D covariance matrix by leveraging block matrix inversion formulas on the 4D covariance to compute sliced 3D parameters.

### [Real-time 4DGS](7335-real-time-4dgs.md)
Real-time 4DGS is a rendering method that achieves interactive frame rates by fusing a static background with a small, fast-updating set of dynamic foreground Gaussians. It distinguishes itself from general 4D Gaussian Splatting by treating the time dimension equally with spatial dimensions, utilizing a 4D scaling matrix and isotropic rotations to represent 4D rotation, and conditioning the marginal distribution on time.

### [Temporal Axis Resolution Adjustment](17994-temporal-axis-resolution-adjustment.md)
Temporal Axis Resolution Adjustment reduces the temporal axis resolution of K-Planes feature grids by narrowing the time normalization range to $[-0.8, 0.8]$. This technique accommodates camera-specific time offsets in unsynchronized video data without degrading rendering performance. It is distinct from [Initial Time Scaling](6046-initial-time-scaling.md), which adjusts Gaussian variance; this method adjusts the feature grid's temporal domain to prevent offset values from deviating outside the learned feature planes.

### Gaussian-splat timelapses
A time-lapse rendering architecture where each timepoint's splats are fitted independently and stored contiguously, with time acting as a hard ordering barrier to prevent LOD rung blending across timepoints [S15, unofficial]. It fits Gaussian splats per timepoint (e.g., 256K per frame), streams timepoints sequentially, and renders time-dependent elements by clipping polylines and fading splats based on their temporal extent [S15, unofficial].

### Urban Scene Adaptation for 4DGS
Training and rendering modifications for sparse, wide-baseline outdoor scenes to prevent overfitting and model infinite-distance backgrounds [S14, unofficial]. It integrates sparse depth supervision from LiDAR using inverse L1 loss, adopts a cube map for the sky, and penalizes inverse depth in sky-masked regions [S14, unofficial]. The temporal coefficient of 4DSH is deactivated (set to zero) to reduce overfitting [S14, unofficial].

### No-4DRot Variant
A variant that disables 4D rotation updates, resulting in less temporal variance and impaired motion fitting [S14, unofficial]. Disabling 4D rotation causes flicker/blur in rendered videos and poor reconstruction of object rims compared to the Full variant [S14, unofficial].

### RetimeGS
A method that achieves continuous-time 4D reconstruction by modeling the trajectories with a neural ODE solver, allowing frame rates to be arbitrarily adjusted at test time [S16, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering is $O(N)$ per pixel, similar to 3DGS, but requires computing conditional Gaussians or deformation offsets per frame [S5][S14, unofficial]. Training is significantly faster than NeRF-based methods but slower than static 3DGS due to temporal consistency losses and deformation field updates [S7, S9, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:**
    *   Native 4DGS: $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of 4D Gaussians [S14, unofficial].
    *   Canonical Deformation (4D-GS): $O(N + F)$, where $F$ is the size of the deformation field network, avoiding the $O(tN)$ storage of explicit timestamp tables [S5].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **4D-GS (Wu et al.):** 82 FPS at 800x800 on RTX 3090 (synthetic); 30 FPS at 1352x1014 (real) [S5]. PSNR 34.05 dB on synthetic [S5].
    *   **4D-Rotor (Yang et al.):** 277 FPS on RTX 3090 at 1352x1014; 583 FPS on RTX 4090 [S9, unofficial]. PSNR 34.26 dB on D-NeRF [S9, unofficial].
    *   **Optimized 4DGS:** 2.8x training speedup over reference implementation, VRAM reduced from 1.9GiB to 1.2GiB [S7, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering of dynamic scenes; high-quality novel view synthesis; efficient storage compared to per-frame methods.
*   **Limitations:** Struggles with highly complex, non-rigid deformations without sufficient Gaussian density; entropy loss can degrade quality on transparent/dense scenes; urban scenes require specific adaptations for infinite background [S9, S14, unofficial].

## Background
4D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Wu et al. in CVPR'24 [S8, unofficial] and Yang et al. in CVPR'24 [S7, S14, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation of 3D-GS failing to capture the dynamic nature of scenes [S12, unofficial]. Early methods stored explicit tables for each timestamp, leading to linear memory growth $O(tN)$ [S5]. 4DGS methods evolved to use either native 4D primitives (slicing) or canonical deformation fields to maintain efficiency [S5][S14, unofficial]. It is widely used in dynamic scene reconstruction, avatar creation, and video editing [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
