# Human and Articulated Avatars

Also called: Dynamic Gaussian Avatars, Animatable 3DGS

## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct animatable human and non-human avatars from multi-view video or images. It solves the problem of generating high-fidelity, dynamic 3D representations that can be rendered in novel poses and views, addressing the limitations of static reconstruction and the rigidity of traditional mesh-based animation. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Semantic and Language Embeddings](8130-semantic-and-language-embeddings.md) by focusing on geometric and appearance deformation via skeletal or parametric priors rather than semantic feature aggregation.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiable alpha-blending accumulation of feature vectors during the rasterization step. For a pixel $p$, the rendered feature vector $\mathbf{F}(p)$ is computed by sorting overlapping Gaussians $\mathcal{O}_p$ front-to-back and applying the following formula :

$$
\mathbf{F}(p) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}_p} \mathbf{f}_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$

where:
*   $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature vector (semantic, language, or latent) of the $i$-th Gaussian.
*   $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian.
*   $\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance from all preceding Gaussians $j < i$.
*   If $\mathcal{O}_p$ is empty, $\mathbf{F}(p) = \mathbf{0}$.

The feature dimension $d$ varies by method (e.g., 512 for CLIP, 768 for LSeg/CLIP text, or compressed latent dimensions). The rendering pipeline produces a 2D feature map $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times d}$, which is subsequently compared against text embeddings or decoded to produce segmentation masks.

For structural extensions, all methods share a canonical space representation where 3D Gaussians are initialized in a reference pose (e.g., T-pose or neutral expression). Deformation is achieved by mapping these canonical Gaussians to a target pose defined by parameters (e.g., skeleton joints, expression coefficients). The rendering pipeline remains differentiable, allowing optimization of Gaussian attributes and deformation parameters via photometric loss.

## Subtopics

### [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md)
GaussianHead is a method for reconstructing animatable 3D head avatars from video, specifically designed to simulate geometrically changing heads in continuous movements while rendering rich textures, including skin and hair. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [GART](8093-gart.md) and [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md) by utilizing a tri-plane representation combined with motion fields to handle the complex, non-rigid deformations of the human head, rather than relying solely on skeleton-based or mesh-bound deformation priors.

### [GART](8093-gart.md)
GART is a 3D Gaussian Splatting extension designed to generalize articulated avatar reconstruction beyond humans to other articulated models, such as animals. It builds on [Human and Articulated Avatars](8209-human-and-articulated-avatars.md) by adapting the canonical space deformation framework to non-human skeletal structures. Unlike [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md) which focuses on facial details, or [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md) which may target specific dynamic aspects, GART specifically addresses the broader class of articulated non-human meshes.

### [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md)
D3GA is a method for reconstructing animatable human avatars from multi-view videos by combining drivable 3D Gaussians with tetrahedral cage-based deformation. It addresses the challenge of full-body dynamic human modeling, where previous approaches struggled to balance geometric fidelity with appearance quality. Unlike sibling methods that rely on linear blend skinning (e.g., HuGS) or complex SE(3) fields (e.g., SplatArmor), D3GA utilizes tetrahedral cages to provide robust geometry and appearance modeling.

## Background
The field emerged from the need to animate 3D Gaussian Splatting representations, which were initially static. Early works like GaussianAvatar [S1] incorporated optimizable tensors with dynamic appearance networks. Human101 [S1][S2, unofficial] significantly accelerated training to 100 seconds. Subsequent methods like SplattingAvatar and GoMAvatar [S1][S2, unofficial] embedded Gaussians onto canonical meshes. Head-specific modeling advanced with MonoGaussianAvatar [S2, unofficial], GaussianHead [S1][S2, unofficial], and HeadGas [S2, unofficial], while hand modeling was addressed by 3DPSHR and MANUS [S2, unofficial].
