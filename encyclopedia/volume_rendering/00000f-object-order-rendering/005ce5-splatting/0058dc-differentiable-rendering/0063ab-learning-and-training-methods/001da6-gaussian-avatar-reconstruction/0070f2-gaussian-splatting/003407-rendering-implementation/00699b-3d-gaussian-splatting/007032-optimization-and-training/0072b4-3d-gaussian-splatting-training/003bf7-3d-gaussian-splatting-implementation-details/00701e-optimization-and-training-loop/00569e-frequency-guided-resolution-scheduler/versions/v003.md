# Frequency Guided Resolution Scheduler

Also called: Frequency-based resolution scheduling

## Overview
A strategy to schedule rendering resolution throughout optimization by distributing steps based on frequency energy, reducing optimization complexity [S1]. It accelerates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training by starting with low-resolution (LR) images and progressively switching to high-resolution (HR) images as the model learns low-frequency structures, avoiding the high computational cost of optimizing high frequencies from the start [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Total optimization steps $S$, training views $\{I_n\}_{n=1}^N$ at resolution $(H, W)$, downsampling factors $r_1, \dots, r_m$ (where $r_m$ is the max downsampling factor, $r_1=1$ corresponds to HR).
*   **Output:** A schedule mapping each iteration $k \in [1, S]$ to a rendering resolution $r(k)$.

**Data Structures:**
*   Frequency energy $\mathcal{X}(F)$: A scalar value representing the total energy of the frequency domain representation of the training views.

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Frequency Energy:**
    For each training view $I_n$, compute its Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) $F_n$. Calculate the total frequency energy $\mathcal{X}(F)$ of the full-resolution training data:
    $$ \mathcal{X}(F) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{i=1}^{H} \sum_{j=1}^{W} ||F_n(i, j)||^2 $$
    where $N$ is the number of training views, and $H, W$ are the height and width of the original images [S1].

2.  **Determine Max Downsampling Factor ($r_m$):**
    Select the maximum downsampling factor $r_m$ such that the frequency energy of the downsampled image $F_{r_m}$ satisfies:
    $$ \mathcal{X}(F_{r_m}) = \frac{1}{a} \mathcal{X}(F) $$
    where $a$ is a hyperparameter controlling the starting significance level for the lowest resolution. The default value is $a=4$ [S1].

3.  **Sample Intermediate Resolutions:**
    Linearly sample the frequency energies $\mathcal{X}(F_{r_1}), \dots, \mathcal{X}(F_{r_{m-1}})$ between $\mathcal{X}(F)$ (where $r_1=1$) and $\mathcal{X}(F_{r_m})$. Solve for the corresponding resolution factors $r_1, \dots, r_{m-1}$ such that $\mathcal{X}(F_{r_i})$ matches the sampled energy values. This establishes a sequence of downsampling factors $r_1, \dots, r_m$ where $r_1=1$ and $r_m$ is the largest factor [S1].

4.  **Calculate Switch Points:**
    For each downsampling factor $r_i$, calculate the iteration index $s_{r_i}$ where the rendering resolution switches from $r_i$ to $r_{i-1}$ (i.e., from lower to higher resolution). The switch from $r_i$ to $r_{i-1}$ occurs at:
    $$ s_{r_i} = S \cdot \frac{\mathcal{X}(F_{r_i})}{\mathcal{X}(F)} $$

This is consistent.)

5.  **Interpolate Resolution:**
    For iteration $k$ between switch points $s_{r_i}$ and $s_{r_{i-1}}$, the rendering resolution $r(k)$ is interpolated. The text states: "The rendering resolution $r(k)$ for iteration $k$ between $s_{r_i}$ and $s_{r_{i-1}}$ is interpolated with the inverse square of $r_i$ and $r_{i-1}$."
    This implies interpolating $1/r^2$. Let $E(k)$ be the normalized iteration progress within the interval:
    $$ E(k) = \frac{k - s_{r_i}}{s_{r_{i-1}} - s_{r_i}} $$
    Then:
    $$ \frac{1}{r(k)^2} = (1 - E(k)) \frac{1}{r_i^2} + E(k) \frac{1}{r_{i-1}^2} $$
    Solve for $r(k)$:
    $$ r(k) = \left( \frac{1}{(1 - E(k)) \frac{1}{r_i^2} + E(k) \frac{1}{r_{i-1}^2}} \right)^{1/2} $$
    Note: In practice, rendering resolution must be integer dimensions. The interpolated $r(k)$ can be used to determine the dimensions $(H/r(k), W/r(k))$, likely rounded or clamped to valid downsampling factors if discrete steps are preferred, but the formula suggests continuous interpolation of the inverse square of the factor [S1].

import numpy as np

def compute_frequency_energy(images):
    """
    Compute frequency energy X(F) for a set of images.
    images: list of numpy arrays of shape (N, H, W, C) or (N, H, W)
    Returns: scalar float
    """
    N = len(images)
    total_energy = 0.0
    for img in images:
        # Convert to grayscale if necessary, or sum over channels
        if img.ndim == 4:
            img = np.mean(img, axis=-1)
        # Compute 2D FFT
        F = np.fft.fft2(img)
        # Compute energy: sum of squared magnitudes
        energy = np.sum(np.abs(F) ** 2)
        total_energy += energy
    return total_energy / N

**Pitfalls:**
*   Aliasing issues in simple spatial downsampling disturb recoverable low-frequency components. Addressed by using frequency-guided scheduling rather than naive cropping, and applying anti-aliasing (low-pass filter) before downsampling [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduces overall optimization time by distributing steps based on frequency energy, allowing the model to learn low-frequency structures quickly with LR images before switching to HR [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly stated, but using LR images reduces memory bandwidth and computation during early stages.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces optimization time while maintaining or improving quality [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates 3DGS optimization without trading off quality.
*   **Limitations:** Requires computation of frequency energy for scheduling, which adds overhead but is done once.
