# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity by leveraging low-frequency components from low-resolution voxels for coarse geometry early in training, while medium- and high-resolution voxels refine details as training advances [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by avoiding scale mismatches inherent in single-scale depth priors, providing a stronger and more robust constraint that significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$, multi-scale voxel grid depths $D_{il}(p_i)$ for scale $l \in \{0, \dots, L\}$, and RGB maps $C_i, C_j$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxel Grid:** A hierarchical structure where each scale $l$ stores depth values $D_{il}(p_i)$ for every pixel $p_i$ in the training view. The sources do not specify the exact bit-packing or memory layout of the voxel grid, only that it captures varying scene frequencies [S1].
*   **RGB Maps:** 2D arrays $C_i, C_j$ storing color values for frames $i$ and $j$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling:** For each training ray $r_{train} \in \mathcal{R}_{train}$, identify the 2D pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$ [S1].
2.  **Homogeneous Augmentation:** Compute the homogeneous coordinate $\tilde{p}_i$ from $p_i$ [S1].
3.  **3D Point in Camera Coordinates (Frame $i$):** Compute the 3D point $x_{li}$ in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$ using the depth at scale $l$ and camera intrinsics:
    $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    Alternatively, this point can be determined directly from the ray origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
    $$ x_l = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
    [S1]
4.  **Transformation to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]
5.  **Transformation to Camera Coordinates (Frame $j$):** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]
6.  **Reprojection to 2D:** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
7.  **Reprojection Error Calculation:** Compute the reprojection error $e_l(p_i)$ for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]
8.  **Pseudo-Ground Truth Selection:** Select the scale $l$ with the minimum reprojection error as the pseudo-ground truth depth for the ray:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]
9.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth at the selected scale and the pseudo-ground truth depth:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \left( \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \right)^2 $$
    Note: The source formula [S1] sums over $l$, but the logic implies selecting the best $l$. The text states "pseudo-GT depth of the scale with the minimum reprojection error is obtained... utilized to compute the geometric adaptation loss". The formula provided in [S1] appears to sum the squared difference for all scales against the selected pseudo-GT, or potentially implies a weighted sum where the best scale has weight 1. Given the ambiguity, the implementation should calculate $D'(r_{train})$ as the depth from the scale with min error, and then compute the loss against the rendered depths. A common interpretation of such adaptive losses is to penalize the deviation of the *selected* scale's depth from the pseudo-GT, or to use the pseudo-GT to supervise the entire multi-scale representation. The source formula explicitly sums over $l$. We follow the source formula [S1].
    [S1]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geometric_adaptation_loss(
    frames: List[Frame], 
    voxel_grids: List[VoxelGrid], 
    intrinsics: List[Matrix3x3], 
    poses: List[Pose]
) -> float:
    """
    Computes the Lgeo loss for a batch of training frames.
    
    Args:
        frames: List of training frames, each containing RGB map C and ray data.
        voxel_grids: List of voxel grids, one for each scale l.
        intrinsics: List of camera intrinsic matrices K.
        poses: List of camera poses (R, t).
        
    Returns:
        The scalar Lgeo loss value.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # Assume we process pairs of frames (i, j) or use a single frame i and 
    # warp to other views in the batch. The source describes using frame i 
    # and frame j. We assume frames[i] and frames[j] are available.
    # For simplicity, we iterate over rays in frame i and warp to frame j.
    
    for i in range(len(frames)):
        frame_i = frames[i]
        K_i = intrinsics[i]
        R_i, t_i = poses[i].R, poses[i].t
        
        for j in range(len(frames)):
            if i == j:
                continue # Skip self-reprojection
            
            frame_j = frames[j]
            K_j = intrinsics[j]
            R_j, t_j = poses[j].R, poses[j].t
            
            # Get ray data for frame i
            rays_i = frame_i.rays # List of (origin, direction) for each pixel
            
            for pixel_idx, (o_i, v_i) in enumerate(rays_i):
                p_i = frame_i.pixel_coords[pixel_idx]
                
                # Step 1-2: Homogeneous augmentation
                p_i_homogeneous = np.array([p_i[0], p_i[1], 1.0])
                
                # Step 3: 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
                # Using depth from each scale l
                depths_l = []
                for l, voxel_grid in enumerate(voxel_grids):
                    # D_il(p_i) is the depth at scale l for pixel p_i
                    d_il = voxel_grid.get_depth(p_i)
                    depths_l.append(d_il)
                
                # Compute 3D points in world coordinates for each scale
                # x_l = R_i * (D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_hom) + t_i
                # Or x_l = o_i + D_il * v_i
                # We use the second form as it is more direct for rays
                x_l_world = []
                for d_il in depths_l:
                    x_l_cam = d_il * np.linalg.inv(K_i) @ p_i_homogeneous
                    x_l_w = R_i @ x_l_cam + t_i
                    x_l_world.append(x_l_w)
                
                # Step 5-6: Reproject to frame j
                reprojection_errors = []
                projected_pixels_j = []
                
                for l, x_l_w in enumerate(x_l_world):
                    # Transform to camera j
                    x_l_cam_j = R_j.T @ (x_l_w - t_j)
                    
                    # Project to 2D
                    p_j_homogeneous = K_j @ x_l_cam_j
                    p_j_2d = p_j_homogeneous[:2] / p_j_homogeneous[2]
                    
                    # Get RGB value at projected pixel in frame j
                    # C_j(p_j_2d)
                    c_j_val = frame_j.get_rgb_at_pixel(p_j_2d)
                    
                    # Reprojection error: difference in RGB
                    c_i_val = frame_i.get_rgb_at_pixel(p_i)
                    error = np.linalg.norm(c_i_val - c_j_val)
                    
                    reprojection_errors.append(error)
                    projected_pixels_j.append(p_j_2d)
                
                # Step 8: Select scale with minimum reprojection error
                min_error_idx = np.argmin(reprojection_errors)
                pseudo_gt_depth = depths_l[min_error_idx]
                
                # Step 9: Compute Loss
                # L_geo = sum_l (D_hat_l - D')^2
                # D_hat_l is the rendered depth at scale l, which is depths_l[l]
                # D' is the pseudo_gt_depth
                
                scale_loss = 0.0
                for l, d_il in enumerate(depths_l):
                    scale_loss += (d_il - pseudo_gt_depth) ** 2
                
                total_loss += scale_loss

    return total_loss / (num_frames * num_rays)
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Number of Scales ($L$):** Not given in the sources.
*   **Voxel Resolution per Scale:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Weight $\lambda_{geo}$:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Reprojection errors are sensitive to camera pose accuracy. Inaccurate poses can lead to incorrect pseudo-ground truth selection [S1].
*   **Boundaries:** The method assumes valid depth values from volume rendering. If a ray does not intersect any voxel, the depth may be undefined, requiring handling for missing data [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, the method may face challenges in generalization, as accurate camera poses are required [S1].

## Subtopics

### Multi-scale Voxel Grids
A hierarchical representation capturing varying scene frequencies, where low-resolution voxels guide coarse geometry and high-resolution voxels refine details. Chosen when few-shot inputs require robust geometry learning without overfitting. [Link](52-multi-scale-voxel-grids.md)

### Reprojection Error Metric
A measure of geometric consistency between views, used to select pseudo-ground truth depths. Chosen when explicit depth ground truth is unavailable. [Link](52-reprojection-error-metric.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of scales $L$, the number of training rays, and the number of frame pairs used for reprojection. Each ray requires reprojection to $N-1$ other views and error calculation across $L$ scales.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel grids and RGB maps for all training frames.
*   **Measured Performance:** In FrugalNeRF, Lgeo alone achieves lower final depth error than single-scale depth prior (Ld) and significantly reduces floaters [S1]. Combining Lgeo and Ld yields the best results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no complex scheduling required, robust to geometric noise compared to feature warping methods like GeCoNeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses; performance may degrade with sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in FrugalNeRF (2025) by [S1]. It addresses the challenge of few-shot novel view synthesis by regularizing training through multi-scale voxel geometry and reprojection errors. It builds on the idea that low-frequency components can guide coarse geometry early in training, while high-frequency components refine details later.
