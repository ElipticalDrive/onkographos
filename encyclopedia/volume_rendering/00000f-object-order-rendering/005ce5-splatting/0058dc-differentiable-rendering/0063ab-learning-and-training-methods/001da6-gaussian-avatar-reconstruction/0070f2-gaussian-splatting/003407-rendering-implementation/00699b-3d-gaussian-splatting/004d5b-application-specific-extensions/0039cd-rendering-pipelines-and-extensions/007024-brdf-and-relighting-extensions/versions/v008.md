# BRDF and Relighting Extensions

Also called: Physically-based 3D Gaussian Splatting, Inverse Rendering with Gaussians, Relightable 3D Gaussians

## Overview
BRDF and Relighting Extensions extend standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by decoupling material properties from geometry and lighting, enabling physically-based rendering (PBR) and dynamic relighting [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard splatting which bakes lighting into spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients via an emission-absorption model, these methods store per-Gaussian BRDF parameters (e.g., albedo, roughness, metalness) and evaluate them against arbitrary light sources or environment maps [S1, unofficial]. This allows for accurate specular reflections, shadow casting, and indirect illumination reconstruction, addressing the limitations of baked lighting on highly reflective surfaces [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method modifies the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline by replacing the direct color evaluation with a physically-based BRDF evaluation.

**Inputs:**
- 3D Gaussian primitives: Position $\mathbf{p}$, Covariance $\Sigma$, Opacity $\alpha$, and new PBR attributes (Albedo $\mathbf{c}_{diff}$, Roughness $\rho$, Metalness $m$, Normal $\mathbf{n}$, Indirect Lighting SH coefficients).
- Camera parameters: View matrix $W$, Projection matrix.
- Lighting: Environment map $L_e(\omega)$ or point/area lights.

**Outputs:**
- Rendered image with physically accurate reflections and shadows.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian primitive stores:
- $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position.
- $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix (decomposed into rotation $R$ and scale $S$).
- $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity.
- $\mathbf{c}_{diff} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Diffuse albedo color.
- $\rho \in \mathbb{R}$: Roughness parameter.
- $m \in \mathbb{R}$: Metalness parameter.
- $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Surface normal (derived from $\Sigma$ or learned).
- $c_{SH}^{indirect}$: Coefficients for indirect illumination.

**Steps:**

1. **Normal Estimation:**
   The surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ for a Gaussian is derived from its anisotropy. If $\Sigma$ is decomposed into scale $S$ and rotation $R$, the normal is typically the first column of $R$ (or the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of $\Sigma$) [S1, unofficial].

2. **Projection and Rasterization:**
   Follow the standard 3DGS projection:
   $$ \Sigma_c = J W \Sigma W^T J^T $$
   Extract $\Sigma_{2D}$ as the upper-left $2 \times 2$ block. Compute 2D bounding box and assign to tiles [G].

3. **BRDF Evaluation:**
   For each pixel covered by a Gaussian, evaluate the BRDF.
   - **Direct Lighting:** Evaluate the BRDF $f_r(\omega_i, \omega_o, \mathbf{n}, \rho, m)$ using the incident light direction $\omega_i$ and view direction $\omega_o$.
   - **Disney Model:** For datasets with physical attributes, use a simplified Disney principle-based BRDF model. Calculate Normal Distribution Function (NDF) $D$, Fresnel term $F$, and Geometry term $G$ using Neilf's formula [S1, unofficial].
   - **Indirect Lighting:** Add indirect illumination estimated via SH coefficients or environment map sampling [S2, unofficial].

4. **Compositing:**
   Accumulate color using front-to-back blending. The color contribution of a Gaussian $i$ is:
   $$ c_i = \int_{\Omega} f_r(\omega_i, \omega_o, \mathbf{n}_i, \rho_i, m_i) L_i(\omega_i) (\mathbf{n}_i \cdot \omega_i) d\omega_i $$
   The final pixel color is:
   $$ I_f = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \tilde{\sigma}_i \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_i(\mathbf{p}) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \tilde{\sigma}_j \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_j(\mathbf{p})) $$
   where $\tilde{\sigma}_i$ is the effective opacity and $\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_i$ is the 2D Gaussian density [G].

5. **Training Optimization:**
   - **Loss Functions:** Use photometric loss between rendered and ground truth images. Apply bilateral smoothness loss to BRDF parameters to prevent noise [S2, unofficial].
   - **Highlight Preservation:** If training reflection weights directly without physical constraints, apply a brightening operation to training images: multiply pixels with gray values surpassing a threshold by a brightness coefficient to mitigate highlight loss [S1, unofficial].
   - **Parameter Clamping:** Clamp base color and metallic to $[0, 1]$. Scale and shift roughness to lie between $0.09$ and $1$ to prevent exceedingly strong specular reflectance [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_pbr(primitives, camera, lights, env_map):
    H, W = camera['height'], camera['width']
    tiles = [(y, x) for y in range(0, H, 16) for x in range(0, W, 16)]
    tile_prims = {t: [] for t in tiles}
    
    # 1. Project & Filter
    for p in primitives:
        p_cam = transform_to_camera(p, camera['view_matrix'])
        J = perspective_jacobian(p_cam['z'])
        W_mat = camera['view_matrix']
        Sigma_2d = (J @ W_mat) @ p['Sigma'] @ (W_mat.T @ J.T)
        Sigma_2d = Sigma_2d[:2, :2]
        bbox = compute_2d_bbox(p_cam['xy'], Sigma_2d)
        for t in tiles:
            if intersects_bbox(bbox, t, 16):
                tile_prims[t].append({'data': p_cam, 'sigma_2d': Sigma_2d, 'depth': p_cam['z'], 'pbr': p['pbr']})
    
    # 2. Sort & Rasterize
    image = zeros((H, W, 3))
    for t in tiles:
        prims = tile_prims[t]
        prims.sort(key=lambda x: x['depth'])
        for y in range(t[0], min(t[0] + 16, H)):
            for x in range(t[1], min(t[1] + 16, W)):
                acc_color = zeros(3)
                T = 1.0
                for prim in prims:
                    diff = vec2(x, y) - prim['data']['xy']
                    inv_sig = inverse_2x2(prim['sigma_2d'])
                    g_val = exp(-0.5 * dot(diff, inv_sig @ diff))
                    alpha = prim['data']['opacity'] * g_val
                    
                    # BRDF Evaluation
                    normal = get_normal(prim['pbr']['Sigma'])
                    view_dir = get_view_dir(camera, prim['data']['pos'])
                    light_dir = get_light_dir(prim['data']['pos'], lights)
                    
                    # Evaluate Disney BRDF
                    brdf_val = evaluate_disney_brdf(
                        normal=normal, 
                        view_dir=view_dir, 
                        light_dir=light_dir,
                        albedo=prim['pbr']['albedo'],
                        roughness=clamp(prim['pbr']['roughness'], 0.09, 1.0),
                        metalness=clamp(prim['pbr']['metalness'], 0.0, 1.0)
                    )
                    
                    # Add indirect lighting
                    indirect = evaluate_sh_indirect(prim['pbr']['sh_coeffs'], normal)
                    
                    color = brdf_val * light_intensity + indirect
                    
                    weight = alpha * T
                    acc_color += color * weight
                    T *= (1.0 - alpha)
                    if T < 1e-4: break
                image[y, x] = acc_color
    return image
```

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting with BRDF Embedding](1174-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-brdf-embedding.md)
Extends 3DGS by embedding physically based rendering parameters into each Gaussian primitive, enabling per-point BRDF evaluation and relighting. It distinguishes itself by explicitly storing incident lighting and BRDF parameters per Gaussian, facilitating direct lighting reconstruction via environment maps and indirect lighting via spherical harmonics.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting with Reflection and Relighting](5873-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-reflection-and-relighting.md)
Extends 3DGS by incorporating classical PBR concepts to address limitations with highly reflective surfaces and enable relighting under novel illumination. Unlike standard 3DGS, it decomposes geometry, material, and lighting, allowing for dynamic specular shading synthesis. It is distinguished by its specific integration of secondary ray tracing for interreflections and the derivation of normals from the Gaussian kernel's anisotropy.

### [Relightable 3D Gaussian](6001-relightable-3d-gaussian.md)
Extends 3DGS by assigning per-Gaussian BRDF parameters and precomputed visibility to enable dynamic relighting and shadow casting. It distinguishes itself by evaluating material responses to arbitrary light sources rather than relying solely on captured environment maps.

### [Inverse Rendering with Gaussians (GS-IR / GIR)](6153-inverse-rendering-with-gaussians-gs-ir-gir.md)
Employs 3DGS inverse rendering techniques to accurately estimate material properties and lighting conditions. It allows precise control over lighting and material appearance, making results more photorealistic. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on simplified equations or learned light approximations, GIR uses a split-sum approximation to handle indirect light.

### [GaussianShader Material Model](8013-gaussianshader-material-model.md)
Enhances 3DGS by incorporating trainable material parameters—diffuse color, specular intensity, roughness, and surface normal—along with a global environment map to achieve PBR. It distinguishes itself by disentangling geometry from material properties, allowing for accurate specular reflections and relighting capabilities.

### [BRDF-SMAE](12538-brdf-smae.md)
A method for spatially varying BRDF decomposition in neural volumes, where a single BRDF embedding is stored at each point to enable spatially varying material representation. It differs from standard neural volume rendering by explicitly encoding material properties via an auto-encoder structure rather than implicit color or density fields, allowing for disentangled material analysis.

### Deferred Rendering Pipeline for Phys3DGS
A rendering pipeline used to train PBR parameters, involving multiple importance sampling for light rays and post-processing denoising [S2, unofficial].
- **Light Sampling:** Use Fibonacci sampling for random incoming light sampling during training. For relighting, adopt NVDiffrecMC’s multiple importance sampling by casting an equal amount of light importance rays and GGX importance rays [S2, unofficial].
- **Denoising:** Apply a non-parametric bilateral denoiser to the PBR result to cope with low samples per point (spp), enabling real-time rendering [S2, unofficial].
- **Parameters:** 64 spp used for real-time rendering with denoiser, compared to 1,024 spp for voxel grid-based methods [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Rendering is $O(N)$ per pixel where $N$ is the number of overlapping Gaussians. Training is significantly slower than standard 3DGS due to the complexity of BRDF evaluation and the need for multiple importance sampling in inverse rendering setups [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Each Gaussian stores additional attributes (albedo, roughness, metalness, normal, SH coefficients), increasing memory usage per primitive compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Phys3DGS achieves real-time rendering with 64 spp and a bilateral denoiser, outperforming voxel grid-based methods that require 1,024 spp [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Physically accurate reflections, dynamic relighting, and shadow casting. Better handling of highly reflective surfaces compared to baked lighting methods [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Training can be unstable due to large gradients in HDR space, leading to degenerate cases where vertices undergo dramatic position shifts [S2, unofficial]. Direct training of reflection weights without physical constraints may lead to loss of highlight information [S1, unofficial].

## Background
BRDF and Relighting Extensions emerged as a response to the limitations of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which bakes lighting into spherical harmonics coefficients and struggles with dynamic lighting and highly reflective surfaces [S1, unofficial]. The field has seen rapid development with methods like Phys3DGS [S2, unofficial] and Relightable 3D Gaussian [S1, unofficial] introducing physically based material models and inverse rendering techniques to 3DGS. These methods build on the foundational work of 3D Gaussian Splatting [G] and integrate concepts from traditional PBR and inverse rendering [S1, S2, unofficial].
