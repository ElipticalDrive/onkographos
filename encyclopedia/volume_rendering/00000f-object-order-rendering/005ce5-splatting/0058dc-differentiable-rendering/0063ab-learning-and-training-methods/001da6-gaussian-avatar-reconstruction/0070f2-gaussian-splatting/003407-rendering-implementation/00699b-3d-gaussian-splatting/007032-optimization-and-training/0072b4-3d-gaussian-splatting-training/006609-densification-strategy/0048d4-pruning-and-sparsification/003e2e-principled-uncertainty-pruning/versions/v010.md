# Principled Uncertainty Pruning

Also called: Fisher Information-based pruning score, PUP 3D-GS, PUP 3D-GS Pruning Score & Pipeline, Principled Uncertainty Pruning for 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) is a post-hoc method for compressing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing Gaussians with high uncertainty regarding their contribution to the reconstruction error. Unlike heuristic methods that rely on opacity or visibility, PUP computes a sensitivity score based on the Fisher Information matrix of the L2 reconstruction loss, specifically approximating the Hessian with respect to spatial parameters [S1]. It is distinguished by its multi-round prune-refine pipeline, which allows for aggressive pruning (up to 90%) while maintaining higher visual fidelity and rendering speeds than single-round approaches or competing methods like LightGaussian [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A converged 3D-GS model $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, the set of training views $\mathcal{P}_{gt}$ with ground truth images $I_{gt}$, and camera poses $\Phi$.
- **Output:** A pruned set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ and fine-tuned parameters.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian $g_i$ retains its standard 3D-GS parameters: position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (or covariance $\Sigma_i$), rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, opacity $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color. PUP computes a scalar pruning score $u_i$ for each Gaussian.

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Compute the L2 Reconstruction Loss:**
    Calculate the total squared error over all training views:
    $$ L_2 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \| I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt} \|_2^2 $$
    where $I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)$ is the rendered image for pose $\phi$.

2.  **Approximate the Hessian via Fisher Information:**
    The full Hessian of the loss with respect to Gaussian parameters $\mathcal{G}$ is:
    $$ \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 L_2 = \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T + (I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt}) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) $$
    On a converged model, the residual term $(I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt})$ approaches zero, causing the second-order term to vanish. The Hessian is approximated by the Fisher Information matrix:
    $$ \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 L_2 \approx \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T $$
    [S1]

3.  **Compute Block-Wise Sensitivity Score:**
    To manage memory and focus on geometric uncertainty, the Fisher matrix is approximated block-wise for each Gaussian $g_i$. The score is computed exclusively for the mean ($\mu_i$) and scale ($s_i$) parameters, resulting in a $6 \times 6$ matrix per Gaussian. Rotations and RGB color parameters are excluded [S1].
    
    The pruning score $u_i$ for Gaussian $i$ is the log-determinant of this block:
    $$ u_i = \log \det \left( \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\{\mu_i, s_i\}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\{\mu_i, s_i\}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T \right) $$
    This score represents the entropy of the second-order Laplace approximation of the posterior distribution for that Gaussian; higher entropy indicates higher uncertainty and lower sensitivity to parameter changes, making the Gaussian a candidate for removal [S1].

4.  **Patch-Based Computation:**
    The sensitivity score is computed over image patches rather than full images to reduce computational cost. The default patch size is $4 \times 4$ pixels, though $2 \times 2$ and $8 \times 8$ are also viable [S1].

5.  **Multi-Round Prune-Refine Pipeline:**
    The method applies pruning and fine-tuning iteratively:
    - **Round 1:** Prune the top 80% of Gaussians with the highest scores $u_i$. Fine-tune the remaining 20% for 5,000 iterations.
    - **Round 2:** Prune the top 50% of the *remaining* Gaussians. Fine-tune for another 5,000 iterations.
    This two-round process achieves approximately 90% total pruning [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def principled_uncertainty_pruning(model, training_views, num_rounds=2, round1_prune_pct=0.8, round2_prune_pct=0.5, fine_tune_iters=5000, patch_size=(4, 4)):
    """
    model: 3D-GS model with parameters G, converged.
    training_views: List of (pose, gt_image) tuples.
    """
    gaussians = model.gaussians
    n_gaussians = len(gaussians)
    
    # Step 1 & 2 & 3: Compute Fisher Information based pruning scores
    # We compute the 6x6 Fisher block for mean and scale for each Gaussian
    # Memory usage: N * 36 floats (for 6x6 matrix)
    fisher_blocks = compute_fisher_blocks(gaussians, training_views, patch_size)
    
    # Step 3 continued: Compute pruning score as log determinant
    scores = []
    for i in range(n_gaussians):
        F_i = fisher_blocks[i] # 6x6 matrix
        # Guard against singular matrices
        if is_singular(F_i):
            scores.append(0.0)
        else:
            scores.append(log_det(F_i))
            
    scores = array(scores)
    
    # Step 5: Multi-round Prune-Refine
    current_gaussians = gaussians
    current_scores = scores
    
    for round_idx in range(num_rounds):
        if round_idx == 0:
            prune_pct = round1_prune_pct
        else:
            prune_pct = round2_prune_pct
            
        # Determine number of Gaussians to prune
        n_to_prune = int(len(current_gaussians) * prune_pct)
        
        # Get indices of Gaussians with highest uncertainty scores
        indices_to_prune = argsort(current_scores)[-n_to_prune:]
        
        # Remove Gaussians
        mask = ones(len(current_gaussians), dtype=bool)
        mask[indices_to_prune] = False
        current_gaussians = current_gaussians[mask]
        current_scores = current_scores[mask]
        
        # Update model with pruned Gaussians
        model.gaussians = current_gaussians
        
        # Fine-tune remaining Gaussians
        fine_tune_model(model, training_views, fine_tune_iters)
        
        # Recompute scores for remaining Gaussians for next round
        # Note: In practice, one might re-evaluate Fisher blocks on the refined model
        fisher_blocks = compute_fisher_blocks(current_gaussians, training_views, patch_size)
        current_scores = []
        for i in range(len(current_gaussians)):
            F_i = fisher_blocks[i]
            if is_singular(F_i):
                current_scores.append(0.0)
            else:
                current_scores.append(log_det(F_i))
        current_scores = array(current_scores)

    return model

def compute_fisher_blocks(gaussians, training_views, patch_size):
    """
    Computes the 6x6 Fisher Information block for mean and scale parameters
    for each Gaussian over the training views.
    """
    n = len(gaussians)
    fisher_blocks = zeros((n, 6, 6))
    
    for pose, gt_img in training_views:
        # Render gradients for mean and scale only
        # This requires differentiating the splatting rasterizer
        # dI/d_mu and dI/d_s are tensors of shape (H, W, 3) for each Gaussian
        # We aggregate over patches
        
        # For each Gaussian, compute gradient of rendered image w.r.t mean and scale
        # This is typically done via automatic differentiation or custom CUDA kernels
        grads = compute_rendering_gradients(gaussians, pose, gt_img, patch_size)
        
        # grads[i] is a tensor of shape (P, 6, 3) where P is number of pixels in patch
        # Actually, usually we sum outer products of gradients
        
        for i in range(n):
            # grad_i shape: (num_pixels_in_patch, 6)
            # We only care about mean (3) and scale (3)
            grad_i = grads[i][:, :6] 
            
            # Outer product sum
            fisher_blocks[i] += grad_i.T @ grad_i
            
    return fisher_blocks
```

**Parameters:**
- **Pruning Percentages:** 80% in Round 1, 50% in Round 2 [S1].
- **Fine-tuning Iterations:** 5,000 per round [S1].
- **Patch Size:** $4 \times 4$ pixels (default) [S1].
- **Parameter Subset:** Mean ($\mathbb{R}^3$) and Scale ($\mathbb{R}^3$) only. Rotations and RGB are excluded [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Convergence Requirement:** The method assumes the scene is fully converged. The Fisher approximation relies on a small L1 residual; pruning before convergence degrades accuracy [S1].
- **Memory Constraints:** Including rotation parameters expands the Fisher matrix to $10 \times 10$, increasing memory by $2.78\times$. This can cause Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors on 16GB GPUs (e.g., RTX A4000) [S1].
- **RGB Exclusion:** Using RGB color parameters for the sensitivity score degrades image quality because it correlates with visibility rather than reconstruction error sensitivity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The Fisher block computation scales linearly with the number of Gaussians $N$ and the number of training views. The dominant cost is the gradient computation and matrix multiplication for each Gaussian.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$, specifically proportional to $N \times 36$ bytes (for the $6 \times 6$ float32 matrix per Gaussian) [S1].
- **Performance:** On the Deep Blending playroom scene, PUP reduces Gaussians from 2.65M to 0.265M, increasing FPS from 76.65 to 318.06 (4.15x speed-up) [S1]. Across Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending, it achieves an average 3.56x speed-up after 90% pruning [S1].
- **Strengths:** Preserves fine details and foreground information better than LightGaussian. Outperforms single-round pruning.
- **Limitations:** Requires a converged model. High memory usage during the pruning phase compared to simple opacity checks.

## Background
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) was introduced by Alex Hanson, Allen Tu, Vasu Singla, Mayuka Jayawardhana, Matthias Zwicker, and Tom Goldstein from the University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It addresses the high storage and memory requirements of 3D-GS, which can consist of millions of Gaussians for complex scenes. The method builds on the Bayesian interpretation of the Hessian of the reconstruction error, linking Fisher Information to the entropy of the posterior distribution of Gaussian parameters [S1].
