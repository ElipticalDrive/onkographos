# Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting

Also called: GeoGaussian

## Overview
Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting (GeoGaussian) is a method for novel view synthesis that initializes and optimizes 3D Gaussians to explicitly preserve scene geometry, particularly in low-textured or smooth regions where standard differentiable rendering often fails [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by parameterizing Gaussians in smooth areas as "thin ellipsoids" aligned with surface normals and constraining densification operations to the tangent plane of the surface, thereby preventing the generation of floating artifacts and maintaining structural coherence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sparse point cloud $P$ (derived from Structure-from-Motion or SLAM) and camera poses $T_{cw}$ [S1].
**Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$, each defined by position $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$ (parameterized by scale $S$ and rotation $R$), opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics coefficients $C$ for color [S1].

### Data Structures
The method maintains two distinct groups of Gaussians based on local surface geometry:
1.  **$G_{ind}$ (Individual/General):** Gaussians initialized from points in irregular or noisy regions. These behave like standard 3D Gaussians (spherical or arbitrary ellipsoidal).
2.  **$G_{\theta}$ (Thin/Surface-Aligned):** Gaussians initialized from points in smooth, connected areas. These are constrained to be thin ellipsoids aligned with the surface normal [S1].

**Thin Gaussian Parameterization ($G_{\theta}$):**
*   **Position:** $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Scale:** $S = [s_1, s_2, s_3]^T$. The third component is fixed: $s_3 = 0.001$. This creates a thin ellipsoid [S1].
*   **Rotation:** $R = [r_1, r_2, r_3] \in SO(3)$. The third column $r_3$ is explicitly initialized as the surface normal $n$. The first two columns $r_1, r_2$ form an orthonormal basis in the tangent plane, perpendicular to $r_3$ [S1].
*   **Covariance:** $\Sigma_w = R S S^T R^T$ [S1].
*   **Color:** $C$ (Spherical Harmonics).
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha$ [S1].

**General Gaussian Parameterization ($G_{ind}$):**
*   Standard 3DGS parameterization where scale $S$ is learned freely and rotation $R$ is not constrained to a specific normal [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

#### 1. Initialization
1.  **Normal Estimation:** Extract surface normals for all points in the input point cloud $P$ [S1].
2.  **Reliability Check:** Classify points into two groups, $S_{co}$ (smooth/coherent) and $S_{ind}$ (individual/irregular), based on normal reliability:
    *   A point is marked unreliable (assigned to $S_{ind}$) if:
        *   It has a large distance to its neighbors.
        *   Its neighbors within a distance threshold have normals with large angular differences relative to the point's normal [S1].
    *   Points not marked unreliable are assigned to $S_{co}$ [S1].
3.  **Parameter Assignment:**
    *   For $p \in S_{ind}$: Initialize $G_{ind}$ using standard 3DGS methods. Set scale based on the distance to the nearest neighbor [S1].
    *   For $p \in S_{co}$: Initialize $G_{\theta}$.
        *   Set $\mu = p$.
        *   Set $r_3 = n_p$ (the estimated normal).
        *   Compute $r_1, r_2$ as an orthonormal basis perpendicular to $r_3$ [S1].
        *   Set $S = [s_1, s_2, 0.001]^T$. $s_1, s_2$ are determined by local density/nearest neighbor distance similar to standard 3DGS [S1].
        *   Initialize $\alpha$ and $C$ [S1].

#### 2. Rendering
For each camera view with pose $T_{cw}$:
1.  **Projection:** Project 3D Gaussians to 2D image plane.
    *   Mean: $\mu_I = \Pi(T_{cw} \mu_w)$ [S1].
    *   Covariance: $\Sigma_I = J W_{cw} \Sigma_w W_{cw}^T J^T$, where $W_{cw}$ is the rotational part of $T_{cw}$ and $J$ is the Jacobian of the projective transformation [S1].
2.  **Blending:** Synthesize pixel color $C_p$ using alpha blending:
    $$ C_p = \sum_{i \in N} c^i \alpha_w^i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_w^i) $$
    where $N$ is the set of Gaussians overlapping the pixel, $c^i$ is the color, and $\alpha_w^i$ is the opacity [S1].

#### 3. Optimization and Densification
The optimization runs for 30,000 iterations [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
1.  **Photometric Loss ($E_{pho}$):**
    $$ E_{pho}^i = \| c_i - \bar{c}_i \| = \| f(\mathcal{G}, T_{cw}) - \bar{c}_i \| $$
    where $\bar{c}_i$ is the ground truth color [S1].
2.  **Geometric Smoothness Loss ($E_{geo}$):** Applied only to view-dependent thin Gaussians $G_{\theta}$.
    *   Find 8 nearest neighbors using K-NN.
    *   Filter outliers where the angle between normals is large [S1].
    *   Calculate loss:
        $$ E_{geo} = \beta_1 \left\| \mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \bm{\mu} - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j \in m} {\mathbf{r}_3^j}^\intercal \bm{\mu}^j \right\| + \beta_2 \| \mathbf{r}_3^j - \mathbf{r}_3 \|_2 $$
        where $\mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \bm{\mu}$ is the distance from the world origin to the plane of the Gaussian, and the second term aligns normals with neighbors. $\beta_1, \beta_2$ are weights [S1].
3.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = \lambda_1 \sum_{\forall k \in H \times W} E_{pho}^k + \lambda_2 \sum_{\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^i} E_{geo}^i $$
    where $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are hyperparameters [S1].

**Densification Strategy:**
*   **Schedule:**
    *   Iterations 0–2,000: Only photometric constraints ($E_{pho}$) are used. No densification [S1].
    *   Iterations 2,000–10,000: Densification is active.
    *   Iterations >10,000: Densification ceases [S1].
    *   Opacity Culling: Gaussians with $\alpha < 0.05$ are removed [S1].
    *   Neighbor Update: K-NN list updated every 100 iterations until 20,000, then every 1,000 iterations [S1].

*   **Densification for $G_{ind}$ (Standard):**
    *   Split large ellipsoids into two smaller ones located randomly inside the original ellipsoid [S1].

*   **Densification for $G_{\theta}$ (Tangential Constrained):**
    *   **Cloning:**
        *   Accumulate position gradient $\delta \mu_i$ over 10 iterations [S1].
        *   If accumulated gradient magnitude exceeds threshold $\gamma$, clone the Gaussian.
        *   New position $\mu_{i+1}$ is calculated by projecting the gradient onto the tangent plane:
            $$ \mu_{i+1} = \mu_i + \delta \mu_i - \mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \delta \mu_i $$
            *(Note: The source formula $\mu_{i+1} = \mu_i + \delta \mu_i - \mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \delta \mu_i$ implies removing the component along the normal, effectively keeping the movement in the tangent plane defined by $r_1, r_2$. The term $\mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \delta \mu_i$ is a scalar projection; strictly speaking, to remove the vector component, it should be $(\mathbf{r}_3^\intercal \delta \mu_i) \mathbf{r}_3$. However, we follow the source's explicit equation [S1].)*
        *   Clone parameters $R$ (including $r_3$) and $S$ to the new Gaussian [S1].
    *   **Splitting:**
        *   Ensure new split Gaussians are co-planar with the parent.
        *   The new position $\mu_{i+1}$ must satisfy:
            $$ \mu_{i+1}^\intercal \mathbf{r}_3^{i+1} = \mu_i^\intercal \mathbf{r}_3^i $$
            This ensures the new Gaussian lies on the plane established by the parent's position and normal [S1].

import numpy as np

def initialize_geogaussian(point_cloud, normals):
    """
    Initialize Gaussians based on geometric reliability.
    point_cloud: Nx3 array of points.
    normals: Nx3 array of normal vectors.
    Returns: List of Gaussian objects (mu, S, R, alpha, C)
    """
    gaussians = []
    # 1. Detect unreliable normals
    # Rule 1: Long distance to others (handled by distance check in neighbor calc)
    # Rule 2: Large angle difference with neighbors within threshold
    # For simplicity, assume 'is_smooth' is pre-computed boolean array
    # In practice: compute KNN, check angle diffs.
    
    for i, (p, n) in enumerate(zip(point_cloud, normals)):
        if is_smooth_surface(p, n, point_cloud, normals, threshold=0.1):
            # Initialize Thin Gaussian G_theta
            # Scale z-component fixed to 0.001
            s1, s2 = estimate_scale_from_density(p, point_cloud)
            S = np.array([s1, s2, 0.001])
            
            # Rotation: r3 = n, r1, r2 orthonormal basis in tangent plane
            r3 = n / np.linalg.norm(n)
            # Create orthonormal basis (G-Schmidt or similar)
            # [G] Standard method to find two orthogonal vectors to r3
            if abs(r3[2]) < 0.9:
                v = np.array([0, 0, 1])
            else:
                v = np.array([1, 0, 0])
            r1 = np.cross(r3, v)
            r1 = r1 / np.linalg.norm(r1)
            r2 = np.cross(r3, r1)
            R = np.column_stack((r1, r2, r3))
            
            G = Gaussian(mu=p, S=S, R=R, alpha=1.0, C=sample_color(p))
            gaussians.append(G)
        else:

            S = np.array([s, s, s]) # Standard isotropic or learned

def densify_tangential(gaussian, accumulated_gradient, threshold_gamma, r3):
    """
    Clone a thin Gaussian within the tangent space.
    gaussian: The parent Gaussian G_theta
    accumulated_gradient: delta_mu_i
    threshold_gamma: Gradient threshold (0.0002)
    r3: Normal vector of the parent
    """
    if np.linalg.norm(accumulated_gradient) > threshold_gamma:
        # Calculate new position
        # Formula from source: mu_i+1 = mu_i + delta_mu_i - r3^T delta_mu_i
        # Note: r3^T delta_mu_i is a scalar. 
        # To strictly follow source equation structure:
        delta_mu = accumulated_gradient
        proj_scalar = np.dot(r3, delta_mu)
        
        # The source equation is: mu + delta_mu - (r3^T delta_mu)
        # This implies subtracting the scalar from the vector? 
        # Or is it a typo in source for (r3^T delta_mu) * r3?
        # "decoupling the component perpendicular to the normal" suggests removing the normal component.
        # Vector component along normal is (r3 . delta_mu) * r3.
        # Tangent component is delta_mu - (r3 . delta_mu) * r3.
        # New pos = mu + Tangent component.
        
        # Implementing the geometric intent (Tangential Clone) as described in text:
        # "decoupling the component perpendicular to the normal... to guide the position"
        # This usually means keeping the tangent part.
        
        tangent_component = delta_mu - proj_scalar * r3
        new_mu = gaussian.mu + tangent_component
        
        # Clone parameters
        new_G = Gaussian(
            mu=new_mu,
            S=gaussian.S.copy(),
            R=gaussian.R.copy(), # Clones r3
            alpha=gaussian.alpha,
            C=gaussian.C
        )
        return new_G
    return None

def split_tangential(gaussian):
    """
    Split a thin Gaussian ensuring co-planarity.
    """
    # New position must lie on the plane: mu_new^T * r3 = mu_old^T * r3
    # [G] Generate a random offset in the tangent plane
    r1, r2, r3 = gaussian.R[:,0], gaussian.R[:,1], gaussian.R[:,2]
    
    # Random step in tangent plane
    step = np.random.randn(2)
    offset = step[0] * r1 + step[1] * r2
    
    new_mu = gaussian.mu + offset
    # Verify co-planarity constraint: dot(new_mu, r3) == dot(gaussian.mu, r3)
    # Since offset is in tangent plane (orthogonal to r3), this holds.
    
    # Split scale
    new_S = gaussian.S / np.sqrt(2)
    
    new_G = Gaussian(
        mu=new_mu,
        S=new_S,
        R=gaussian.R.copy(),
        alpha=gaussian.alpha / 2,
        C=gaussian.C
    )
    return new_G

def compute_geometric_loss(gaussians, neighbors):
    """
    Compute E_geo for thin Gaussians.
    """
    loss = 0.0
    beta1 = 0.05
    beta2 = 0.01
    
    for G in gaussians:
        if G.is_thin:
            # Get neighbors
            neighbor_list = get_k_neighbors(G, neighbors, k=8)
            
            # Filter outliers by normal angle
            valid_neighbors = [N for N in neighbor_list if angle_diff(N.r3, G.r3) < threshold]
            
            if not valid_neighbors:
                continue
                
            # Term 1: Planar consistency
            # r3^T mu is distance from origin to plane
            dist_self = np.dot(G.r3, G.mu)
            dist_avg = np.mean([np.dot(N.r3, N.mu) for N in valid_neighbors])
            term1 = beta1 * abs(dist_self - dist_avg)
            
            # Term 2: Normal alignment
            term2 = beta2 * np.linalg.norm(G.r3 - np.mean([N.r3 for N in valid_neighbors]))
            
            loss += term1 + term2
            
    return loss

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires 30,000 iterations. The neighbor update schedule (every 100 iterations until 20,000, then every 1,000) adds overhead to K-NN computations. The method requires more GPU resources for training compared to standard 3DGS [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Maintains two sets of parameters and explicit normal vectors for thin Gaussians. Neighbor lists are stored for geometric loss calculation [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the Replica dataset (R1, 10% data), GeoGaussian achieves PSNR 31.65, SSIM 0.937, LPIPS 0.041, outperforming standard 3DGS (PSNR 30.49, SSIM 0.932, LPIPS 0.051) and LightGS (PSNR 30.54, SSIM 0.932, LPIPS 0.051) [S1]. On 100% data, it achieves PSNR 38.24, SSIM 0.979, LPIPS 0.021 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly improves rendering quality in low-textured, smooth regions (walls, ceilings) by preserving geometry and preventing floating artifacts [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot perform real-time optimization; functions as a global refinement module requiring more time than online methods like Vox-Fusion [S1]. Requires reliable initial point clouds and normals [S1].

## Background
GeoGaussian was proposed by Y. Li et al. in the paper "GeoGaussian: Geometry-Aware Gaussian Splatting for Scene Rendering" [S1]. It addresses the degradation of rendering quality in novel views that deviate from training viewpoints, particularly in structured regions with low texture [S1]. The method builds upon the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [S1] but introduces explicit geometric constraints and parameterization for thin ellipsoids. It is evaluated on Replica, TUM RGB-D, and ICL-NUIM datasets [S1].
