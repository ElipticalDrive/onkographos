# H-2DGS (Height-Field 2D Gaussian Splatting)
Also called: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
H-2DGS converts a volumetric 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction into a thin, geometrically sharp, and deformable cortical surface using curved height-field Gaussian primitives initialized on a shared Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) zero level set [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of depth ambiguity and volumetric thickness in standard 3DGS, particularly in medical imaging where limited view coverage creates thick, geometrically ambiguous bands of opacity [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [3D Gaussian Splatting with Gaussian Unpooling](6133-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-gaussian-unpooling.md) which refines density, or [3D Gaussian Splatting with Mesh UV Parameterization](8046-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-mesh-uv-parameterization.md) which relies on explicit mesh topology, H-2DGS uses a three-stage pipeline to bootstrap appearance, concentrate density via a differentiable thickness loss, and finally map the result to a surface-oriented height-field representation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
H-2DGS operates in three distinct stages: appearance bootstrapping, ray-wise density concentration, and TSDF-guided surface conversion [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Multi-view images of the scene (e.g., neurosurgical video frames).
*   Camera poses and intrinsics for the training views.
*   (Optional) Postoperative MRI depth data for deformation [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A set of curved height-field Gaussian primitives, each defined by a position on the TSDF zero level set, a height offset, color, opacity, and orientation [S1, unofficial].
*   A compact representation (e.g., 7.97 MB for neurosurgical cases) supporting interactive rendering [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method transitions from volumetric Gaussians to height-field primitives.

**Stage I/II: Volumetric 3DGS Primitives**
Standard 3DGS parameters [3D Gaussian Splatting MVP](3410-3d-gaussian-splatting-mvp.md):
*   $\mathbf{\mu}_i$: 3D mean position.
*   $\mathbf{q}_i$: Quaternion rotation.
*   $\mathbf{s}_i$: Scaling vector.
*   $\alpha_i$: Opacity.
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$: Spherical harmonics coefficients for color.

**Stage III: Height-Field Gaussian Primitives**
Each primitive $j$ is defined by:
*   $\mathbf{x}_j$: Base position on the TSDF zero level set.
*   $h_j$: Height offset along the local surface normal.
*   $\mathbf{c}_j$: Color basis.
*   $\alpha_j$: Opacity basis.
*   Orientation derived from the TSDF normal at $\mathbf{x}_j$.

### Numbered Steps

**Stage I: Photometric Bootstrap**
1.  Train conventional 3DGS with adaptive densification using the standard photometric objective [S1, unofficial].
2.  Optimize for $N_{warm}$ iterations (default 5,000) to establish appearance and scene support without surface constraints [S1, unofficial].

**Stage II: Ray-wise Density Concentration**
1.  Initialize Stage II with the Stage I model [S1, unofficial].
2.  For each training camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, compute 1D Gaussian contributions from all 3D Gaussians:
    $$ \sigma_i(t) = \beta_i \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}a_i(t - m_i)^2\right] $$
    where $\beta_i$ is the amplitude, $a_i$ is the inverse variance, and $m_i$ is the mean position along the ray [S1, unofficial].
3.  Compute total density $\sigma(t) = \sum_i \sigma_i(t)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  Sample along the ray at positions $\{t_m\}_{m=1}^M$. Compute normalized density $p_m$ and cumulative distribution $F_m$:
    $$ p_m = \frac{\sigma(t_m)}{\sum_n \sigma(t_n) + \epsilon} $$
    $$ F_m = \sum_{n=1}^m p_n $$
    where $\epsilon$ is a small constant for numerical stability [S1, unofficial].
5.  Approximate quantiles $t_q$ using differentiable quantile approximation with temperature $\tau_q$:
    $$ w_m(q) = \frac{\exp(-\tau_q|F_m - q|)}{\sum_n \exp(-\tau_q|F_n - q|)} $$
    $$ t_q = \sum_m w_m(q)t_m $$
    Use $q_{low} = 0.01$ and $q_{high} = 0.99$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  Compute effective thickness $\Delta t = t_{q_{high}} - t_{q_{low}}$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  Compute thickness loss for ray $\mathbf{r}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{thick}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\max(0, \Delta t - \Delta t^*)}{E} $$
    where $\Delta t^*$ is the target thickness (set to 0 for normalized thickness) and $E$ is the scene extent [S1, unofficial].
8.  Compute total loss for Stage II:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{stage2}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{thick}} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{r}}[\mathcal{L}_{\text{thick}}] $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda) \|\hat{I}_v - I_v\|_1 + \lambda(1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}_v, I_v))$ with $\lambda = 0.2$, and $\lambda_{\text{thick}} = 5.0$ [S1, unofficial].
9.  Optimize Stage II parameters for 1,000 iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Stage III: TSDF-Guided Surface Conversion**
1.  Render depth maps $\hat{D}_v$ from all training cameras using the Stage II model [S1, unofficial].
2.  Fuse these multi-view depth observations into a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$. For a voxel center $\mathbf{x}$ and view $v$, compute the signed distance observation:
    $$ \phi_v(\mathbf{x}) = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\hat{D}_v(\pi_v(\mathbf{x})) - d_v(\mathbf{x})}{\mu}, -1, 1\right) $$
    where $d_v(\mathbf{x})$ is the known depth (if available) or derived from the fused field, $\pi_v(\mathbf{x})$ is the projection, and $\mu$ is the truncation threshold [S1, unofficial].
3.  Aggregate observations into the TSDF $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ using view weights $w_v(\mathbf{x})$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  Extract the zero level set of $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ to initialize the base positions $\mathbf{x}_j$ of the height-field primitives [S1, unofficial].
5.  Initialize height-field primitives with height $h_j=0$, color $\mathbf{c}_j$, and opacity $\alpha_j$ derived from the Stage II density/color at $\mathbf{x}_j$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  Apply neighbor-transition optimization to coordinate overlapping patches and ensure locally continuous geometry [S1, unofficial].

**Postoperative Deformation (Optional)**
1.  Render depth from postoperative MRI-derived geometry [S1, unofficial].
2.  Deform the explicit height-field surface toward the MRI depth using tracked-color supervision [S1, unofficial].
3.  Use alternating geometry-appearance updates to preserve photorealistic texture during deformation [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def h2dgs_pipeline(images, poses, intrinsics, num_iterations_stage2=1000, num_iterations_warmup=5000):
    # Stage I: Photometric Bootstrap
    gaussians = initialize_3d_gaussians()
    for i in range(num_iterations_warmup):
        rendered_images = render_3dgs(gaussians, images, poses, intrinsics)
        loss = photometric_loss(rendered_images, images, lambda_param=0.2)
        optimize(gaussians, loss)
    
    # Stage II: Ray-wise Density Concentration
    for i in range(num_iterations_stage2):
        total_loss = 0.0
        for ray in rays_from_images(images, poses, intrinsics):
            # Compute 1D contributions along ray
            densities = []
            for g in gaussians:
                sigma_i = g.amplitude * exp(-0.5 * g.inverse_variance * (ray.t - g.mean_along_ray)**2)
                densities.append(sigma_i)
            
            total_density = sum(densities)
            
            # Compute quantiles
            samples = sample_along_ray(ray, num_samples=100)
            p_m = [d / (sum(total_density) + epsilon) for d in total_density]
            F_m = cumulative_sum(p_m)
            
            # Differentiable quantile approximation
            q_low = 0.01
            q_high = 0.99
            tau_q = 10.0 # Temperature parameter
            
            w_m_low = [exp(-tau_q * abs(F - q_low)) for F in F_m]
            w_m_low = [w / sum(w_m_low) for w in w_m_low]
            t_q_low = sum(w * t for w, t in zip(w_m_low, samples))
            
            w_m_high = [exp(-tau_q * abs(F - q_high)) for F in F_m]
            w_m_high = [w / sum(w_m_high) for w in w_m_high]
            t_q_high = sum(w * t for w, t in zip(w_m_high, samples))
            
            delta_t = t_q_high - t_q_low
            target_thickness = 0.0 # Normalized
            scene_extent = get_scene_extent()
            
            thick_loss = max(0, delta_t - target_thickness) / scene_extent
            photo_loss = photometric_loss_for_ray(ray, rendered_images)
            
            total_loss += photo_loss + 5.0 * thick_loss
        
        optimize(gaussians, total_loss)
        
    # Stage III: TSDF Conversion
    depth_maps = render_depth(gaussians, images, poses, intrinsics)
    tsdf = fuse_tsdf(depth_maps, poses, intrinsics)
    surface_points = extract_zero_level_set(tsdf)
    
    height_field_primitives = []
    for point in surface_points:
        primitive = HeightFieldPrimitive(
            position=point,
            height=0.0,
            color=sample_color_at(point, gaussians),
            opacity=sample_opacity_at(point, gaussians)
        )
        height_field_primitives.append(primitive)
        
    # Neighbor transition optimization
    optimize_neighbor_transitions(height_field_primitives)
    
    return height_field_primitives
```

### Parameters
*   $\lambda$: Photometric SSIM weight, default 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{\text{thick}}$: Thickness loss weight, default 5.0 [S1, unofficial].
*   $q_{low}, q_{high}$: Quantiles for thickness measurement, default 0.01 and 0.99 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Delta t^*$: Target thickness, default 0 (normalized) [S1, unofficial].
*   Warm-up iterations: 5,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   Stage II iterations: 1,000 [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** The differentiable quantile approximation requires careful tuning of the temperature parameter $\tau_q$ to balance smoothness and accuracy [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The TSDF fusion requires accurate depth rendering; errors in Stage II can propagate to the surface initialization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Limited view coverage can lead to ambiguous depth; the thickness loss helps mitigate this but may require sufficient initialization [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### 3D Gaussian Splatting with Gaussian Unpooling
Refines density by unpooling from lower resolutions, focusing on multi-scale representation rather than explicit surface conversion [6133-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-gaussian-unpooling.md].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting with Mesh UV Parameterization
Maps Gaussians to a mesh UV space, relying on explicit topology rather than a height-field on a TSDF [8046-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-mesh-uv-parameterization.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering is interactive, taking 29–42 ms per frame for neurosurgical cases [S1, unofficial]. Training involves three stages, with Stage II adding overhead due to ray-wise quantile computation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Produces a compact representation, e.g., 7.97 MB for five neurosurgical cases [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Sharp geometric boundaries, deformable surface, compact representation, integration with MRI/tractography [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires multi-stage optimization, sensitive to initialization, and relies on TSDF fusion which may be noisy in sparse views [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When a sharp, deformable surface is required from volumetric data, especially in medical imaging with limited views [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When a purely volumetric representation is sufficient or when real-time training is required without multi-stage refinement [S1, unofficial].

## Background
H-2DGS was introduced in "H-2DGS: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting for Sharp and Deformable Neurosurgical Surface Reconstruction" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS in medical visualization, where depth ambiguity and volumetric thickness obscure internal structures [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on [3D Gaussian Splatting MVP](3410-3d-gaussian-splatting-mvp.md) by adding geometric constraints and a surface-oriented representation [S1, unofficial].
