# Densification Strategy

Also called: Adaptive Density Control, Densification and Splitting, Primitive Growth and Pruning

## Overview
Densification strategies dynamically adjust the number and distribution of 3D Gaussian primitives during training to resolve under-reconstructed regions (via cloning/splitting) and remove redundant or low-quality primitives (via pruning) [S1, 16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md]. This family solves the problem of static representation capacity, ensuring that the Gaussian distribution adapts to the scene's geometric and photometric complexity without manual intervention [16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from static initialization methods by using gradient-based metrics, opacity thresholds, and multi-view consistency scores to guide structural changes in real-time [16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, where each $g_k$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$ (parameterized by scale $s_k$ and rotation $q_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k$ [Parent]. The core mechanism involves periodic evaluation of a metric per Gaussian to determine if densification (splitting/cloning) or pruning is required.

Common principles include:
1.  **Gradient Accumulation:** Most methods compute the accumulated 2D positional gradient magnitude per Gaussian:
    $$ \tau_k = \frac{1}{N_{\text{views}}} \sum_{v} \left\| \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu_k} \right\|_2 $$
    [Parent]. High gradients indicate regions where the current representation is insufficient to minimize the loss [16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md, unofficial].
2.  **Scale-Based Decision:** Densification actions are typically conditioned on the Gaussian's scale $s_k$. Large scales (coarse representation) trigger **splitting** into children sampled from $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ with reduced scale, while small scales (fine representation) trigger **cloning** to increase sampling frequency without changing spatial footprint [Parent, 16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md, unofficial].
3.  **Opacity Thresholding:** Pruning is frequently based on opacity $\alpha_k$. Gaussians with $\alpha_k < \alpha_{\min}$ are considered negligible and removed [12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md, unofficial].
4.  **Periodic Execution:** Densification and pruning are executed periodically (e.g., every 100–400 iterations) rather than at every step to allow optimizer stabilization and reduce computational overhead [Parent, 16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md, unofficial].
5.  **Buffer Management:** When primitives are added or removed, the parameter buffer must be compacted or flagged to maintain contiguous memory access for the rasterizer [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [GroupFlow](6351-groupflow.md)
GroupFlow is a motion representation technique for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting that distills per-Gaussian neural deformation fields into shared SE(3) transformations for groups of Gaussians [6351-groupflow.md]. It solves the computational bottleneck of evaluating individual deformation networks for every primitive at every timestep, accelerating rendering and training while maintaining temporal coherence [6351-groupflow.md]. Unlike methods that retain dense neural fields or use global rigid transforms, GroupFlow captures locally coherent motion by clustering Gaussians based on trajectory similarity and fitting a separate SE(3) trajectory to each cluster [6351-groupflow.md].

### [3DGS-MCMC](7852-3dgs-mcmc.md)
3DGS-MCMC replaces the standard heuristic-based densification (gradient magnitude thresholds) of 3D Gaussian Splatting with a probabilistic framework based on Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling [7852-3dgs-mcmc.md]. It treats the set of 3D Gaussians as samples from an underlying probability distribution, using Metropolis–Hastings steps to guide the spawning and pruning of primitives [7852-3dgs-mcmc.md]. This approach allows for precise control over the total number of Gaussians, reduces reliance on high-quality initialization, and yields sharper details and fewer artifacts compared to the original cloning/splitting heuristics [7852-3dgs-mcmc.md].

### [VS-Splat Voxel Selection](9132-vs-splat-voxel-selection.md)
VS-Splat Voxel Selection is a learnable, differentiable mechanism for selecting relevant 3D voxels in feed-forward Gaussian Splatting, replacing static opacity thresholding with a confidence-based selection process [9132-vs-splat-voxel-selection.md]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Opacity-Based Thresholding](6234-opacity-based-thresholding.md) by using a Gumbel-Sigmoid distribution to predict confidence scores, allowing the network to learn which voxels contain object-centric geometry rather than relying on manual hyperparameter tuning [9132-vs-splat-voxel-selection.md].

### [Locality-preserving Densification](9585-locality-preserving-densification.md)
Locality-preserving densification addresses the memory access throttling caused by standard Gaussian densification, where new primitives are appended to the end of parameter buffers, causing spatially close Gaussians to be stored far apart in memory [9585-locality-preserving-densification.md]. By reordering the Gaussian parameter buffers based on 3D spatial proximity using Z-ordering (Morton order), this method ensures that neighboring Gaussians in space are also adjacent in memory, thereby improving memory access coalescing [9585-locality-preserving-densification.md].

### [Progressive and Multi-stage Training](9966-progressive-and-multi-stage-training.md)
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to a family of optimization strategies that decompose the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations into sequential phases, rather than optimizing all parameters simultaneously from initialization [9966-progressive-and-multi-stage-training.md]. This approach solves the instability, slow convergence, and local minima traps inherent in single-stage training, particularly when dealing with complex geometry, sparse inputs, or feed-forward pose estimation [9966-progressive-and-multi-stage-training.md]. By decoupling geometry initialization, appearance refinement, and high-frequency detail capture, these methods ensure that the Gaussian distribution adapts progressively to the scene's complexity [9966-progressive-and-multi-stage-training.md]. Unlike standard single-stage training, which often suffers from error propagation between pose and geometry or insufficient coverage of high-frequency details, multi-stage approaches isolate specific learning objectives (e.g., pose vs. attributes) to stabilize the optimization landscape [9966-progressive-and-multi-stage-training.md].

### [Hyperparameter Settings](11076-hyperparameter-settings.md)
Hyperparameter Settings define the numerical configuration for the optimizer, learning rates, and regularization terms used during the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models [11076-hyperparameter-settings.md]. This method solves the problem of stable convergence and high-fidelity reconstruction by tuning the trade-off between fitting training data and maintaining geometric/photometric consistency [G]. It differs from sibling methods like [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) (which manages primitive count) and [Pruning and Sparsification](12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md) (which removes primitives) by focusing on the gradient descent dynamics and loss weighting rather than structural topology changes [G].

### [Adaptive Gaussian Refinement](11448-adaptive-gaussian-refinement.md)
Adaptive Gaussian Refinement is a technique that dynamically adjusts Gaussian parameters using neural network-predicted residuals to improve the modeling of complex non-rigid deformations and time-varying appearance [11448-adaptive-gaussian-refinement.md]. It addresses the limitation of static covariance matrices in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which cannot adequately represent dynamic behaviors such as motion-induced shape changes [11448-adaptive-gaussian-refinement.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on gradient-based densification metrics (e.g., [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md)) or MCMC sampling (e.g., [3DGS-MCMC](7852-3dgs-mcmc.md)), this method uses a lightweight neural network to predict continuous corrections to the Gaussian's spatial, temporal, directional, and covariance parameters [11448-adaptive-gaussian-refinement.md].

### [Hybrid and Diffusion Representations](11473-hybrid-and-diffusion-representations.md)
This family addresses the problem of generating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations from single images, text, or sparse inputs without relying on iterative per-scene optimization [11473-hybrid-and-diffusion-representations.md]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Densification and Splitting](13466-densification-and-splitting.md) by replacing iterative gradient-based refinement with learned generative priors, either through hybrid feature grids or diffusion models, enabling near-real-time generation [11473-hybrid-and-diffusion-representations.md].

### [Termination entropy criterion](12065-termination-entropy-criterion.md)
The termination entropy criterion is a metric used to calculate information gain in active 3D reconstruction [12065-termination-entropy-criterion.md]. It guides viewpoint selection by quantifying scene uncertainty, distinguishing itself from error-magnitude or density-based strategies by focusing on information saturation rather than reconstruction error [12065-termination-entropy-criterion.md].

### [Pruning and Sparsification](12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md)
Pruning and Sparsification methods remove 3D Gaussian primitives that contribute negligibly to the final rendered image or whose removal would not significantly degrade reconstruction quality, thereby controlling memory usage and computational load during training [12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on static thresholds or uncertainty metrics, this family encompasses strategies that actively manage the density and opacity distribution, including recycling low-opacity components into high-opacity ones to prevent unbounded growth without altering the rendered image [12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md]. It sets itself apart from [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md) by focusing specifically on the removal phase of the feedback loop, and from [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md) by often employing simpler opacity-based heuristics rather than accumulated rendering weights for initial candidate selection.

### [Texel Size Adaptation and Primitive Management](12592-texel-size-adaptation-and-primitive-management.md)
Texel Size Adaptation and Primitive Management is a training-time optimization routine that dynamically adjusts the resolution of per-primitive texture maps and the number of 3D Gaussian primitives to balance model capacity and appearance quality [12592-texel-size-adaptation-and-primitive-management.md]. It differs from sibling methods like [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md) or [GroupFlow](6351-groupflow.md) by explicitly coupling the geometric density of primitives with the textural detail allocated to them, using quantile-based error thresholds to trigger splitting, upscaling, or downscaling [12592-texel-size-adaptation-and-primitive-management.md].

### [Momentum Consistency-Guided Densification](14457-momentum-consistency-guided-densification.md)
Momentum Consistency-Guided Densification is a strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that uses kinematic phase-space trajectories (momentum) to guide primitive growth, aiming to overcome floating artifacts in non-manifold regions caused by single-step gradient snapshots [14457-momentum-consistency-guided-densification.md]. It sets itself apart from standard gradient-based strategies by evaluating the consistency between current gradients and accumulated historical trends, rather than relying solely on instantaneous gradient magnitude [14457-momentum-consistency-guided-densification.md].

### [GSGEN Appearance Refinement](15753-gsgen-appearance-refinement.md)
GSGEN Appearance Refinement is an iterative optimization stage that improves fine-grained visual details in text-to-3D generation while preserving the fundamental geometry established during the preceding geometry optimization phase [15753-gsgen-appearance-refinement.md]. It addresses the limitations of standard gradient-based densification by introducing a compactness-based strategy to fill geometric holes and employs specific regularization losses to prevent overfitting and maintain 3D consistency [15753-gsgen-appearance-refinement.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely primarily on positional gradients or opacity thresholds, GSGEN explicitly incorporates a 3D Stable Diffusion Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss and a mean position penalty to ensure structural integrity [15753-gsgen-appearance-refinement.md].

### [Multi-view Consistency and Scoring](15793-multi-view-consistency-and-scoring.md)
Multi-view Consistency and Scoring is a family of densification strategies for 3D Gaussian Splatting that evaluates Gaussian primitives across multiple camera views to guide densification and pruning operations [15793-multi-view-consistency-and-scoring.md]. It builds on [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) by incorporating multi-view photometric loss and alpha contributions to identify regions requiring refinement or removal. Unlike its siblings [Multi-view Alpha-based Densification and Pruning](15406-multi-view-alpha-based-densification-and-pruning.md) and [Multi-View Consistency Scoring for Densification](15464-multi-view-consistency-scoring-for-densification.md), this family explicitly integrates importance and pruning scores derived from multi-view photometric loss and alpha contributions into the standard gradient masks, ensuring that densification occurs only where geometry is consistently unresolved across views.

### [Guided Sampling](16326-guided-sampling.md)
Guided Sampling is a densification strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that addresses blurring artifacts in regions insufficiently covered by the initial sparse point cloud, such as distant geometry or transparent/translucent objects [16326-guided-sampling.md]. It distinguishes itself from standard gradient-based splitting by explicitly sampling new Gaussian positions along the camera ray depth range rather than relying solely on positional gradients, thereby ensuring coverage in areas where gradient signals are weak or ambiguous [16326-guided-sampling.md].

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Optimization Heuristics](16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md)
This method defines the heuristic-driven densification and pruning strategies used during the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to dynamically adjust the number and distribution of Gaussian primitives [16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md]. It solves the problem of static representation capacity by allowing the model to increase density in under-reconstructed regions (via cloning/splitting) and remove redundant primitives (via pruning) based on gradient magnitude, opacity, and scale [16882-3d-gaussian-splatting-optimization-heuristics.md].

### [Multi-view Consistent Densification](17168-multi-view-consistent-densification.md)
Multi-view Consistent Densification (MCD) is a strategy that reduces Gaussian redundancy by evaluating reconstruction quality across multiple sampled views, rather than relying solely on image-space gradient magnitude [17168-multi-view-consistent-densification.md]. It identifies Gaussians lying in regions of poor reconstruction consistency across views, triggering densification only when errors are persistent [17168-multi-view-consistent-densification.md]. This approach avoids noise from occlusion or view-dependent effects that can mislead single-view gradient-based methods [17168-multi-view-consistent-densification.md].

### [Selective Gaussian Densification](18688-selective-gaussian-densification.md)
Selective Gaussian Densification is a training strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that classifies primitives into 'steady' and 'non-steady' states to determine if densification is needed, replacing the vanilla 3DGS view-space position gradient threshold with a sum-of-norms metric to avoid redundant Gaussians [18688-selective-gaussian-densification.md]. It selectively splits or creates new Gaussians only for non-steady-state Gaussians exhibiting a large magnitude of internal conflicts, while preserving converged steady-state Gaussians to reduce redundant processing and Gaussian count [18688-selective-gaussian-densification.md].

### [PSCD-GS (Perception-Structure Collaborative Densification)](18905-pscd-gs-perception-structure-collaborative-densification.md)
PSCD-GS is a densification strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that replaces single-score heuristics with a continuous perception–structure role representation to guide densification, cloning/splitting selection, and split placement [18905-pscd-gs-perception-structure-collaborative-densification.md]. It augments the baseline 3DGS gradient and scale metrics with complementary perceptual (texture/appearance) and structural (geometry/boundaries) evidence, allowing the renderer to distinguish between regions needing appearance refinement versus geometric reconstruction [18905-pscd-gs-perception-structure-collaborative-densification.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on a single complexity or edge score, PSCD-GS retains the overlapping responses of both cue types to condition candidate selection and split initialization [18905-pscd-gs-perception-structure-collaborative-densification.md].

### [Parallel Grid Sorting Algorithm](19021-parallel-grid-sorting-algorithm.md)
The Parallel Grid Sorting Algorithm is a post-densification optimization step that reorders 3D Gaussian primitives into 2D attribute grids to maximize spatial locality and compressibility [19021-parallel-grid-sorting-algorithm.md]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on geometric refinement or probabilistic sampling, this method specifically targets the data layout for efficient storage and transmission by minimizing the variance of absolute differences between neighboring grid elements [19021-parallel-grid-sorting-algorithm.md]. It is distinct from standard rasterization sorting as it operates on the entire attribute set (position, color, opacity, scale, rotation) simultaneously to prepare the data for subsequent compression codecs [19021-parallel-grid-sorting-algorithm.md].

### Trick-GS
- [S1] A Gaussian Splatting method that combines progressive training with resolution, noise and Gaussian scales, learning to prune and mask primitives and SH bands by their significance, and an accelerated GS training framework to achieve faster run-time, smaller disk size, and faster convergence compared to vanilla GS.
- [S1] 3DGS reconstructs a scene using 3D Gaussian primitives rendered via differentiable volume splatting rasterizer extending EWA volume splatting. Each primitive has 59 parameters: position, covariance (parameterized by scaling matrix S and quaternion rotation q), opacity, diffused color, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients (typically 3 bands).
- [S1] On three datasets, Trick-GS achieves up to 2× faster training, 40× smaller disk size and 2× faster rendering speed compared to vanilla GS, while having comparable accuracy.

### Outlier Removal
- [S2] A process to address noise and missing surface parts in the density space by using perceptual loss to identify problematic geometric aspects and removing outliers.
- [S2] Compute perceptual loss VGG(S_p, S_g) to measure high-level visual similarity between rendered patch S_p and real image patch S_g.
- [S2] Select patches where VGG(S_p, S_g) is lower than half the average value across the training set to prioritize patches with improved visual similarity.
- [S2] Augment the density space by adding a group of points along the pixel ray for selected patches, considering the depth range of neighboring pixels.
- [S2] Remove outliers by examining the number of points within a specified radius around each neural density point; if the count falls below a predefined threshold, the point is removed.

### Joint Optimization
- [S3, unofficial] Joint optimization of newly added view-dependent Gaussians with pre-existing kernels, refining $T$, $\beta$, and opacity orientation using multi-view photometric constraints.
- [S3, unofficial] During image-space optimization, original Gaussians' rendered outputs are treated as background. After projection, original kernels' opacity is made optimizable while freezing other attributes to mitigate over-occlusion.
- [S3, unofficial] Apply full parameter optimization to newly added Gaussians. Set number of iterations proportional to training set size to ensure each additional kernel is sampled approximately the same number of times.
- [S3, unofficial] Optimize parameters with specific learning rates: position (0.000016), diffuse color (0.001), higher-order spherical harmonic coefficients (0.001/20), opacity (0.02), scaling (0.002), rotation (0.0005), opacity lobe orientation `d` (0.001), opacity lobe width `T` (0.0002), and sharpness `β` (0.002).
- [S3, unofficial] Employ SparseAdam to optimize the opacity of original Gaussian primitives visible in the current viewpoint.

### Visibility Loss
- [S4] A regularizer that penalizes NeRF densities at 3D locations not visible from multiple training views to remove floaters outside the convex hull of training views.
- [S4] Approximate visibility $v_{ij}$ of the $i$-th 3D location in the $j$-th training view using a frustum check.
- [S4] Implement by defining a single tight sphere around training images.
- [S4] Render batches of rays shooting from a random location on the sphere surface, through the center of the scene, and far into the distance.
- [S4] Render rays using Nerfacto and apply the visibility loss to the sampled points.
- [S4] $L_{vis} = \sum_i V(q_i) f_\sigma(q_i)$ where $f_\sigma(q_i) = \sigma_i$ is the NeRF density at 3D location $q_i$, and $V(q_i) = 1_{\{\sum_{j=1}^n v_{ij} < 1\}}$ indicates if the location is not visible from any training views.
- [S4] Nerfacto uses a proposal sampler [2] to importance sample around surfaces, making the loss effective in quickly culling floating artifacts with high density outside visible regions.
- [S4] The frustum check approximation does not handle occlusions, instead overestimating the number of views a location is visible from.

### Momentum-based Primitive Budgeting
- [S5] A method to predict the maximum primitive number P_fin for densification, preventing out-of-memory errors and bounding primitive growth.

### Densification Threshold Scaling Rule
- [S8] A rule to maintain consistent Gaussian densification behavior when changing the number of interpolated virtual cameras $n$, by scaling the gradient threshold proportionally.
- [S8] If the number of virtual cameras changes from $n$ to $n'$, the densification gradient threshold must be multiplied by $\frac{n}{n'}$ to match the scaled gradients, because the gradient of every Gaussian during motion-blurred image synthesis is scaled by $\frac{1}{n}$.
- [S8] $n$: Number of interpolated virtual images/cameras used for motion blur synthesis. Default value: 10, chosen to balance rendering performance and training efficiency.

### Noise-Based Densification
- [S10] A densification method that adds controlled spatial noise to Gaussians in under-reconstructed areas to increase spatial coverage and prevent over-clustering/redundancy, instead of cloning or splitting at predetermined points.
- [S10] In under-reconstructed regions, Gaussians are cloned with added noise to their positions to create diversity and avoid duplication.
- [S10] $\mu'_i = \mu_i + \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2 I)$, $\mu_i$ is the original position, and $\sigma$ is a noise factor tuned depending on gradient information and local scene complexity.

## Background
The concept of adaptive density control in 3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in the original 3DGS paper [Parent]. The method uses accumulated view-space position gradients to identify regions requiring higher geometric detail. Over time, various extensions have been proposed to address specific limitations such as memory usage [S5], occlusion handling [S6, unofficial], dynamic scene modeling [6351-groupflow.md], and probabilistic sampling [7852-3dgs-mcmc.md]. The family has become a standard component of 3DGS training pipelines, with numerous variants focusing on efficiency, stability, and specific application domains like SLAM [S7, unofficial] and compression [S9].
