# Anchor3DGS

Also called: Anchor-based 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Anchor3DGS is a pose-free, feed-forward framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that replaces pixel-aligned generation with a compact anchor-based representation [S1, unofficial]. It places a fixed number of 3D anchors on a geometry-aware scaffold, decoupling the total Gaussian count from input resolution and view count to allocate capacity based on scene content [S1, unofficial]. Unlike pixel-aligned methods that produce millions of Gaussians proportional to input views, Anchor3DGS uses an order of magnitude fewer primitives (0.26M) while maintaining comparable quality in dense-view settings and superior quality in sparse-view settings [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md) by introducing a global, resolution-independent anchor structure.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of $V$ input images $\{I_v\}_{v=1}^V$ with known or estimated poses (though the method is designed to be pose-free, it relies on a geometry backbone for depth/pose estimation). Images are resized to a maximum of 448 pixels on the longer side during training, and 448x448 at inference [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A set of $K \times N$ 3D Gaussians defined by mean $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics coefficients for color, which can be rendered to novel views [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Anchor Embeddings ($Z$):** A learnable tensor of shape $(N, D_z)$, where $N$ is the anchor budget and $D_z$ is the embedding dimension.
    *   $N = 32,768$ (default) [S1, unofficial].
    *   $D_z = 128$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Decoded Gaussians:** For each anchor $n$, a decoder network predicts $K$ Gaussians.
    *   $K = 8$ (default) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Total Gaussians per scene: $N \times K \approx 0.26$ million [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Feature Vector ($h$):** A per-Gaussian feature vector of dimension $D_h = 16$ used for rendering refinement [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Geometry Backbone Prediction:**
    Pass the input images through a pretrained geometry backbone (initialized with VGGT weights [S1, unofficial]) to predict depth maps and camera poses. This step is critical; inaccurate poses or noisy depth degrade performance [S1, unofficial]. The patch-embedding layer of the backbone is frozen, and the rest is fine-tuned with a learning rate $0.1\times$ smaller than the rest of the network [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Entropy-Guided Anchor Sampling:**
    To concentrate capacity on visually complex regions, anchors are sampled based on entropy. Uniform grid subsampling is replaced by this strategy, which allocates the limited anchor budget more reasonably [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Occlusion-Aware Multi-View Aggregation:**
    Encode anchor features by aggregating information from multiple input views. Instead of plain mean pooling, an occlusion-aware aggregation mechanism is used to encode anchor features, ensuring that only visible information contributes to the anchor's representation [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Cross-Anchor Self-Attention:**
    Propagate context to poorly-observed areas via cross-anchor self-attention. Each anchor interacts with others, allowing information from well-observed regions to fill gaps in poorly observed ones, mitigating holes prevalent in pixel-aligned methods [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Gaussian Decoding:**
    For each anchor $n$, decode $K$ Gaussians. The decoder outputs the parameters for the Gaussians (mean, covariance, opacity) and a feature vector $h$ of dimension 16 [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Rendering and Refinement:**
    Render the Gaussians using standard differentiable rasterization to get an initial image $\hat{I}_{raw}$. Then, pass $\hat{I}_{raw}$ through an enhancement UNet to recover fine high-frequency details, producing the final refined rendering $\hat{I}$ [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Loss Computation:**
    Compute the total training loss $\mathcal{L}$ as a weighted sum of MSE, LPIPS, and depth consistency losses [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{mse}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{mse}} + \lambda_{\text{lpips}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{lpips}} + \lambda_{\text{depth}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}
    $$
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mse}}$: Mean Squared Error between $\hat{I}$ and ground truth $I$.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{lpips}}$: LPIPS loss between $\hat{I}$ and $I$.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}$: Depth consistency loss encouraging rasterized depth to agree with depth maps from the geometry backbone [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Anchor Budget ($N$):** 32,768 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gaussians per Anchor ($K$):** 8 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Anchor Embedding Dimension ($D_z$):** 128 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Feature Vector Dimension ($D_h$):** 16 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW [S1, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** Peak $2 \times 10^{-4}$ with 1,000 warmup steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Geometry Backbone LR:** $0.1\times$ the main learning rate [S1, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_{\text{mse}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{lpips}} = 0.05$, $\lambda_{\text{depth}} = 0.1$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Context Views:** Randomly sampled between 2 and 16 per scene [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Iterations:** 30,000 (approx. 2-3 days on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs) [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class Anchor3DGS:
    def __init__(self, N=32768, K=8, D_z=128, D_h=16):
        self.N = N
        self.K = K
        self.D_z = D_z
        self.D_h = D_h
        
        # Learnable anchor embeddings
        self.anchors = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(N, D_z))
        
        # Geometry Backbone (VGGT-based)
        self.geometry_backbone = VGGTBackbone()
        # Freeze patch embedding
        self.freeze_patch_embedding()
        
        # Decoder: Maps anchor embedding + context to Gaussians
        self.decoder = GaussianDecoder(D_z, K, D_h)
        
        # Enhancement UNet for refinement
        self.enhancement_unet = EnhancementUNet()
        
        # Loss weights
        self.lambda_mse = 1.0
        self.lambda_lpips = 0.05
        self.lambda_depth = 0.1

    def freeze_patch_embedding(self):
        # Implementation detail: Freeze the patch-embedding layer of VGGT
        pass

    def forward(self, images, gt_images, gt_depths=None):
        """
        images: List of input images [V, C, H, W]
        gt_images: Ground truth images [V, C, H, W]
        gt_depths: Ground truth depths (optional, for depth consistency)
        """
        # 1. Geometry Backbone Prediction
        # Predicts depth and poses. Assumes backbone handles pose estimation internally or returns depth.
        # In pose-free setting, backbone provides depth maps.
        predicted_depths = self.geometry_backbone(images)
        
        # 2. Entropy-Guided Anchor Sampling
        # Calculate entropy from predicted depth or image features to guide anchor selection/weighting
        # This step concentrates capacity on complex regions.
        # Note: Exact entropy calculation depends on backbone output. 
        # We assume a function `compute_entropy` exists based on backbone features.
        entropy_weights = compute_entropy(images, predicted_depths)
        
        # 3. Occlusion-Aware Multi-View Aggregation
        # Aggregate features from multiple views for each anchor
        # This replaces plain mean pooling
        anchor_features = occlusion_aware_aggregation(self.anchors, images, predicted_depths)
        
        # 4. Cross-Anchor Self-Attention
        # Propagate context between anchors
        anchor_features = cross_anchor_self_attention(anchor_features)
        
        # 5. Gaussian Decoding
        # Decode K Gaussians per anchor
        # Returns: means (N, K, 3), covariances (N, K, 6), opacities (N, K, 1), features (N, K, D_h)
        means, covariances, opacities, features = self.decoder(anchor_features)
        
        # Reshape for rendering: (N*K, ...)
        means = means.view(-1, 3)
        covariances = covariances.view(-1, 6)
        opacities = opacities.view(-1, 1)
        features = features.view(-1, self.D_h)
        
        # 6. Rendering
        # Render Gaussians to image
        # Note: Standard 3DGS rasterization is assumed here
        rendered_image = render_gaussians(means, covariances, opacities, features)
        
        # 7. Refinement
        # Enhance high-frequency details
        refined_image = self.enhancement_unet(rendered_image)
        
        # 8. Loss Computation
        loss_mse = F.mse_loss(refined_image, gt_images)
        loss_lpips = compute_lpips(refined_image, gt_images)
        
        # Depth consistency loss
        # Rasterize depth from Gaussians and compare with predicted depth from backbone
        rendered_depth = rasterize_depth(means, covariances, opacities)
        loss_depth = F.mse_loss(rendered_depth, predicted_depths)
        
        total_loss = (self.lambda_mse * loss_mse + 
                      self.lambda_lpips * loss_lpips + 
                      self.lambda_depth * loss_depth)
        
        return refined_image, total_loss

    def train_step(self, images, gt_images, optimizer):
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        _, loss = self.forward(images, gt_images)
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        return loss.item()
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Geometry Backbone Dependency:** Performance relies heavily on the prediction quality of the geometry backbone. Inaccurate poses or noisy depth maps from the backbone degrade anchor placement and final rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Fixed Anchor Budget:** The anchor budget is fixed regardless of scene scale. Very large or unbounded scenes may suffer from insufficient capacity, as the method does not currently support scene-adaptive budgets [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sparse View Holes:** Without entropy-guided sampling and cross-anchor attention, pixel-aligned methods suffer from holes in poorly observed regions. Anchor3DGS mitigates this but still requires sufficient context views for effective attention [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is feed-forward and takes seconds (e.g., 2.9s on a single H800 GPU for 32 views) [S1, unofficial]. Training takes approximately 2-3 days on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs for 30,000 iterations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Storage footprint is 35MB per scene, significantly lower than pixel-aligned methods (>650MB) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gaussian Count:** Fixed at 0.26M Gaussians per scene, independent of input resolution and view count [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Sparse-View (3 views):** PSNR 23.69 on VR-NeRF, outperforming baselines like NoPoSplat and AnySplat [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Dense-View (32 views):** PSNR 24.56 on VR-NeRF, comparable to state-of-the-art pixel-aligned baselines but with far fewer Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Compact representation, fast inference, high quality in sparse-view settings, decoupling of Gaussian count from input views.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on geometry backbone quality, fixed anchor budget may not scale to very large scenes, requires pre-trained VGGT weights [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When fast, feed-forward novel view synthesis is needed, especially in sparse-view settings, or when storage/compute constraints are tight.
*   **When Not to Use:** For very large or unbounded scenes where a fixed anchor budget is insufficient, or when high-quality geometry backbone predictions are not available.

## Background
Anchor3DGS was introduced in the paper "Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based Representation" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of pixel-aligned 3DGS methods, which produce a large number of Gaussians proportional to input resolution and view count, leading to high storage and compute costs. By introducing a compact anchor-based representation, Anchor3DGS achieves state-of-the-art quality in sparse-view settings and comparable quality in dense-view settings with significantly fewer Gaussians and lower storage. It is evaluated on VR-NeRF, MipNeRF360, and DL3DV datasets [S1, unofficial].
