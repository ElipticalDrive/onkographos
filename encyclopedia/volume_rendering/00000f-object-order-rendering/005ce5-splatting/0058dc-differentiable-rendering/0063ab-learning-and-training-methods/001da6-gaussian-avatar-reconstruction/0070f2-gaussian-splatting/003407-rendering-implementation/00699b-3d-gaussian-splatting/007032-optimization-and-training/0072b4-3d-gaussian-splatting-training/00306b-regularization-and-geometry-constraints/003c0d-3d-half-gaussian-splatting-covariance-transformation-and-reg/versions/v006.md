# 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization

Also called: 3D-HGS

## Overview
This method regularizes 3D Gaussian primitives by transforming their covariance matrices to enforce half-Gaussian (half-ellipsoid) geometry, ensuring that the primitives model sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space rather than unstructured density clouds [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) and [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by operating directly on the geometric parameterization of the splats during training, effectively cutting Gaussians in half along their surface normals to prevent "ghosting" or double-sided rendering artifacts common in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1].

## How it works
The method modifies the optimization of the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ of each Gaussian primitive. Instead of optimizing a full ellipsoid, the covariance is transformed into a local basis aligned with the surface normal, regularized to represent a half-ellipsoid, and transformed back to world space. This transformation is applied periodically during training.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** The current covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (symmetric positive definite) and the surface normal vector $n \in \mathbb{R}^3$ associated with the Gaussian.
*   **Output:** A regularized covariance matrix $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ representing a half-Gaussian.

### Data Structures
The covariance matrix is stored as a $3 \times 3$ symmetric matrix. In standard 3D Gaussian Splatting implementations, this is often encoded as a 6-element vector or a $4 \times 4$ matrix with specific constraints. For this method, we assume access to the full $3 \times 3$ matrix $\Sigma$ in world space.

### Parameters
*   **Regularization Interval:** The transformation is performed every 5000 iterations [S1].
*   **Opacity Threshold (Densification/Pruning):** Increased to $0.01$ [S1].
*   **Opacity Reset (Half-Gaussians):** Set to $0.02$ [S1].
*   **Scaling Factor:** The minimum of the modified diagonal elements is divided by 100 to ensure stability [S1].

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Normalize Normal:** Normalize the surface normal vector $n$ to unit length:
    $$ n \leftarrow \frac{n}{\|n\|} $$
2.  **Construct Basis:** Construct an orthonormal basis matrix $B$ using $n$ and two orthogonal vectors $v_1, v_2$ in the tangent plane.
    *   If $n_x = 0$ and $n_y = 0$ (i.e., $n$ is aligned with the Z-axis):
        $$ v_1 = (1, 0, 0) $$
        $$ v_2 = (0, 1, 0) $$
    *   Otherwise:
        $$ v_1 \leftarrow \frac{(n_y, -n_x, 0)}{\|(n_y, -n_x, 0)\|} $$
        $$ v_2 \leftarrow \frac{n \times v_1}{\|n \times v_1\|} $$
    *   Concatenate vectors to form the basis matrix $B$:
        $$ B = [v_1, v_2, n] $$
        where $B$ is a $3 \times 3$ matrix with columns $v_1, v_2, n$.
3.  **Transform to Basis Space:** Project the world-space covariance $\Sigma$ into the local basis coordinate system:
    $$ \text{cov} \leftarrow B^T \Sigma B $$
4.  **Compute Modified Diagonal/Off-Diagonal Elements:** Calculate the Schur complement-like terms for the tangent plane components. Let $\text{cov}_{ij}$ denote the element at row $i$, column $j$ of the matrix $\text{cov}$ (0-indexed).
    $$ a' \leftarrow \text{cov}_{0,0} - \frac{\text{cov}_{0,2}^2}{\text{cov}_{2,2}} $$
    $$ b' \leftarrow \text{cov}_{0,1} - \frac{\text{cov}_{0,2} \cdot \text{cov}_{1,2}}{\text{cov}_{2,2}} $$
    $$ c' \leftarrow \text{cov}_{1,1} - \frac{\text{cov}_{1,2}^2}{\text{cov}_{2,2}} $$
5.  **Construct Regularized Covariance in Basis Space:** Create a new matrix $\sigma_{\text{local}}$ in the basis space. The top-left $2 \times 2$ block represents the tangent plane covariance, and the normal direction is heavily penalized to enforce the "half" geometry.
    $$ \sigma_{\text{local}} = \begin{bmatrix} a' & b' & 0 \\ b' & c' & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{\min(a', c')}{100} \end{bmatrix} $$
    Note: The off-diagonal terms involving the normal ($\text{cov}_{0,2}$ and $\text{cov}_{1,2}$) are set to 0, effectively decoupling the tangent plane from the normal direction in the regularized form. The normal variance is set to a small fraction of the tangent variances.
6.  **Transform Back to World Space:**
    $$ \sigma \leftarrow B \sigma_{\text{local}} B^T $$
7.  **Update Parameters:** Replace the Gaussian's covariance with $\sigma$.

### Pseudocode
```python
import numpy as np

def regularize_half_gaussian_covariance(cov_world: np.ndarray, normal: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Transforms a 3D Gaussian covariance matrix into a half-Gaussian form
    aligned with the given surface normal.
    
    Args:
        cov_world: 3x3 symmetric covariance matrix in world space.
        normal: 3D normal vector (not necessarily normalized).
        
    Returns:
        Regularized 3x3 covariance matrix in world space.
    """
    # 1. Normalize normal
    n = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)
    
    # 2. Construct basis
    # Check if normal is aligned with Z-axis to avoid division by zero or degenerate cross products
    if np.isclose(n[0], 0.0) and np.isclose(n[1], 0.0):
        v1 = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        v2 = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 0.0])
    else:
        # v1 is orthogonal to n and lies in the XY plane projection
        v1 = np.array([n[1], -n[0], 0.0])
        v1 = v1 / np.linalg.norm(v1)
        
        # v2 is orthogonal to both n and v1
        v2 = np.cross(n, v1)
        v2 = v2 / np.linalg.norm(v2)
        
    # Basis matrix B: columns are v1, v2, n
    B = np.column_stack((v1, v2, n))
    
    # 3. Transform covariance to basis space
    cov_local = B.T @ cov_world @ B
    
    # 4. Compute modified elements
    # cov_local is 3x3. Indices are [row, col]
    cov_00 = cov_local[0, 0]
    cov_11 = cov_local[1, 1]
    cov_22 = cov_local[2, 2]
    cov_01 = cov_local[0, 1]
    cov_02 = cov_local[0, 2]
    cov_12 = cov_local[1, 2]
    
    # Avoid division by zero if cov_22 is extremely small
    if cov_22 < 1e-8:
        cov_22 = 1e-8
        
    a_prime = cov_00 - (cov_02 ** 2) / cov_22
    b_prime = cov_01 - (cov_02 * cov_12) / cov_22
    c_prime = cov_11 - (cov_12 ** 2) / cov_22
    
    # 5. Construct regularized local covariance
    # The normal direction variance is scaled down significantly
    min_tangent_var = min(a_prime, c_prime)
    normal_var = min_tangent_var / 100.0
    
    sigma_local = np.array([
        [a_prime, b_prime, 0.0],
        [b_prime, c_prime, 0.0],
        [0.0, 0.0, normal_var]
    ])
    
    # 6. Transform back to world space
    sigma_world = B @ sigma_local @ B.T
    
    return sigma_world
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degenerate Normals:** If the normal vector is zero or near-zero, the basis construction fails. The code must handle this by defaulting to a standard basis or skipping regularization [S1].
*   **Positive Definiteness:** The regularization process may result in a covariance matrix that is not strictly positive definite if $a'$ or $c'$ become negative due to numerical errors or ill-conditioned inputs. A guard should ensure eigenvalues are non-negative [G].
*   **Frequency of Application:** Applying this transformation too frequently may hinder convergence, as it restricts the shape of the Gaussians. The source specifies every 5000 iterations [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The transformation involves matrix multiplications ($3 \times 3$) and vector operations, which are $O(1)$ per Gaussian. However, it is only applied every 5000 iterations, so the overhead per training step is negligible.
*   **Memory Complexity:** No additional memory is required beyond the existing Gaussian parameters.
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, 3D-HGS with an efficient rasterizer achieves 125 FPS and 29.66 PSNR using 3.8M kernels, compared to 3DGS which uses 3.2M kernels [S1]. On Tank & Dengin, it achieves 160 FPS and 24.45 PSNR with 1.8M kernels [S1]. On Tank & Blending, it achieves 126 FPS and 29.76 PSNR with 2.6M kernels [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves PSNR over standard 3DGS across all evaluated datasets. The half-Gaussian geometry better models sharp surface boundaries, reducing artifacts.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate surface normals for the transformation. If normals are noisy or unavailable, the regularization may produce suboptimal results. The method increases the number of kernels required compared to 3DGS in some datasets (e.g., 3.8M vs 3.2M on Mip-NeRF 360) [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in the paper "3D-HGS: 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting" [S1]. It builds upon the principles of [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md), specifically leveraging geometric priors to enforce planarity and surface alignment. The approach was evaluated against Scaffold, mip-splatting, and MCMC architectures, demonstrating consistent improvements in rendering quality [S1].
