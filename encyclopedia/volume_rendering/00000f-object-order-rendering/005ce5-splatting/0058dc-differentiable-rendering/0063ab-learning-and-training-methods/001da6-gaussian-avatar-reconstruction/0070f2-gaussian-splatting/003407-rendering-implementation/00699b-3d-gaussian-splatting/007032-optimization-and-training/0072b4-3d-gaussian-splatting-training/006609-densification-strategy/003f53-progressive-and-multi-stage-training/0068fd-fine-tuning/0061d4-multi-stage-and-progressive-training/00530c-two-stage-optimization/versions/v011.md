# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy that decouples the optimization of 3D Gaussian representations into sequential phases to resolve conflicting objectives, such as stability versus compression or geometry versus appearance. It addresses the instability caused by jointly optimizing complex generative pipelines with quantization bottlenecks [S3, unofficial] or the difficulty of simultaneously fitting geometry and material properties [S2]. By separating these concerns, the method ensures stable initial convergence before refining for specific downstream metrics like rate-distortion performance [S3, unofficial] or inverse rendering fidelity [S2].

## How it works
The method generally follows a sequential dependency where Stage $k$ initializes from the output of Stage $k-1$. The specific implementation varies by application domain, primarily falling into two categories: resolution-based refinement for volumetric reconstruction [S1] and codec-aware refinement for feed-forward generation [S3, unofficial], or geometry-appearance decoupling for inverse rendering [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images and poses), target view images (for supervision), and hyperparameters (learning rates, loss weights, resolution grids).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/SH coefficients) and, in codec variants, latent codes or codec modules.

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Set ($\mathcal{G}$):** A collection of 3D Gaussians. In feed-forward methods, these are predicted from features; in volumetric methods, they are optimized directly.
*   **Feature Tensor ($\mathbf{F}$):** In feed-forward pipelines, a dense tensor (e.g., $N \times C_F \times H \times W$) used to predict Gaussian attributes [S3, unofficial].
*   **Depth Maps:** Encoder-side depth ($D_{enc}$) and decoder-side normalized inverse-depth ($S$) used for supervision [S3, unofficial].

### Steps

#### Variant A: Resolution-Based Refinement (Volumetric/CT)
Used in sparse-view CT reconstruction [S1].

1.  **Stage 1: Low-Resolution Optimization**
    *   Sample a low-resolution grid, specifically $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume [S1].
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters rapidly to place them in approximately correct positions [S1].
    *   Minimize the projection loss using the $L_2$ norm between projected data $\hat{p}$ and actual measurements $p$:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \| \hat{p}[V(\theta)] - p \|_2^2 $$
        where $V(\theta)$ is the discretized volume parameterized by $\theta$ [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Full-Resolution Refinement**
    *   Initialize from the Stage 1 checkpoint.
    *   Sample over all voxels of the original volume [S1].
    *   Refine Gaussian parameters for fine-grained adjustments to capture finer details [S1].
    *   Continue minimizing the same projection loss function with the full-resolution grid [S1].

#### Variant B: Codec-Aware Refinement (Feed-Forward 3DGS)
Used in CodecSplat [S3, unofficial].

1.  **Stage 1: Codec-Free Gaussian Generation**
    *   Train the modified feed-forward Gaussian generation pipeline *without* the feature codec.
    *   Predict the codec-free Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ from the refined feature $\mathbf{F}_c$ and encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Render the target view $\hat{Y}_{tcf}$:
        $$ \hat{Y}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}_{cf}, K_t, T_t) $$
    *   Minimize the rendering reconstruction loss:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage1} = D_{render}(\hat{Y}_{tcf}, Y_t) $$
        where $Y_t$ is the target-view image and $D_{render}$ is the reconstruction loss metric [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Stage 2: Codec-Aware Latent Coding**
    *   Initialize from the Stage 1 checkpoint.
    *   Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner.
    *   Keep remaining backbone modules fixed; optimize only codec-related modules [S3, unofficial].
    *   Decode the Gaussian set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ and render the target view $\hat{Y}_t$:
        $$ \hat{Y}_t = \text{Render}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}, K_t, T_t) $$
    *   Minimize the rate-distortion objective:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} D_{render}(\hat{Y}_t, Y_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} + \beta R $$
        where $R$ is the estimated rate, $\lambda$ is the rate parameter sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training (fixed at test time), $\lambda_{max} = 1024$, $\gamma$ is the depth supervision weight, and $\beta$ is the rate penalty weight [S3, unofficial].
    *   The codec-side depth loss uses normalized inverse depth:
        $$ D_{codec-depth} = \| S - S_{enc} \|_1 $$
        where $S$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $S_{enc}$ is the supervision target derived from encoder depth:
        $$ S_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{D_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        with $n_i$ and $f_i$ being the near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S3, unofficial].

#### Variant C: Geometry-Appearance Decoupling (Inverse Rendering)
Used in GS-IR [S2].

1.  **Stage 1: Initial Optimization**
    *   Optimize geometric parameters $\mathbf{T} = \{p, s, q, n\}$ (position, scale, rotation, normal) and appearance parameters $\mathbf{A}$ (color/SH) [S2].
    *   Run for 30,000 iterations using the Adam optimizer [S2].
    *   Minimize the initial loss $\mathcal{L}_{init}$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{init} = \mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_n $$
        where $\mathcal{L}_c$ is the $L_1$ color reconstruction loss and $\mathcal{L}_n$ is the normal loss:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_n = \mathcal{L}_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} \text{TV}_{normal} $$
        with $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$ [S2].

2.  **Stage 2: Decomposition Optimization**
    *   Fix $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{A}$.
    *   Optimize material parameters $\mathbf{M} = \{a, \rho, m\}$ (albedo, roughness, etc.) [S2].
    *   Run for 10,000 iterations using the Adam optimizer [S2].
    *   Minimize the decomposition loss $\mathcal{L}_d$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_d = \mathcal{L}_{shade} + \lambda_M \text{TV}_{mat} + \lambda_E \text{TV}_{light} $$
        where $\lambda_M = 1.0$ and $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2]. $\mathcal{L}_{shade}$ models the shading difference, and $\text{TV}_{mat}$, $\text{TV}_{light}$ are total variation smoothing terms for material and light fields respectively [S2].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Resolution Grid:** $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ for Stage 1 in volumetric methods [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 30,000 for Stage 1 and 10,000 for Stage 2 in inverse rendering [S2].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S2] or AdamW [S3, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ with weight decay $0.01$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Rate Parameter ($\lambda$):** Sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training [S3, unofficial].
*   **Depth Supervision Weight ($\gamma$):** $0.1$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Rate Penalty Weight ($\beta$):** $10^{-4}$ for DL3DV, $4 \times 10^{-4}$ for RE10K [S3, unofficial].
*   **Smoothing Weights:** $\lambda_{n-TV}=5.0$, $\lambda_M=1.0$, $\lambda_E=0.01$ [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck is unstable due to the need to adapt the generation pipeline and latent representation simultaneously [S3, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Conflict:** Jointly optimizing geometry and appearance can lead to interference; decoupling prevents this [S2].
*   **Hardware Constraints:** Volumetric methods may require custom CUDA kernels for efficient voxelization due to PyTorch's lack of native support for dynamic patch slicing [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two-stage optimization typically doubles the training time compared to single-stage methods, but Stage 1 is often faster due to lower resolution or frozen parameters [S1, S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stage 1 in volumetric methods reduces memory usage by operating on a coarser grid [S1]. Codec-aware methods reduce final storage size significantly (KiB-level) at the cost of encoding/decoding overhead [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves higher quality reconstruction and better rate-distortion trade-offs than single-stage baselines [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes training for complex pipelines; prevents catastrophic forgetting of geometry during material optimization; enables ultra-compact bitstreams [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and loss weights; increased total training time [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

## Background
Two-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address specific limitations of single-stage training. The resolution-based approach was introduced in 3DGR-CT for sparse-view CT reconstruction to handle the computational burden of full-volume optimization [S1]. The codec-aware approach was proposed in CodecSplat to enable ultra-compact latent coding for feed-forward 3DGS, solving the instability of joint quantization training [S3, unofficial]. The geometry-appearance decoupling was introduced in GS-IR for inverse rendering, allowing for the separate optimization of geometry and material properties [S2].
