# Multi-stage and Progressive Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Progressive Training, Stage-wise Prediction

## Overview
Multi-stage and Progressive Training refers to a family of optimization strategies that decouple the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations across distinct phases, either by separating parameter types (geometry vs. attributes) or by accumulating primitives over time. It solves the instability, gradient conflicts, or computational bottlenecks associated with optimizing complex, high-dimensional, or dynamic representations in a single monolithic pass [S1][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from single-stage training by preventing error accumulation and gradient interference through sequential or hierarchical updates, and from standard fine-tuning by explicitly managing the growth or specialization of the Gaussian field [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares the core mechanism of decomposing the optimization problem into multiple, ordered steps. These steps may be:
1.  **Sequential Parameter Optimization:** Fixing a subset of parameters (e.g., geometry) while optimizing others (e.g., materials or latent codes) to prevent gradient conflicts [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Temporal/Incremental Accumulation:** Adding new Gaussians or refining existing ones as new data (images or stages) becomes available, rather than re-optimizing the entire scene from scratch [S1].
3.  **Distillation Targets:** Using intermediate, refined states (e.g., from a short-horizon optimizer) as regression targets for feed-forward predictors to stabilize training [S1].

Common mathematical structures include:
*   **Loss Decomposition:** Total loss $L$ is split into stage-specific losses $L_i$, often weighted by coefficients $\lambda_i$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Freezing/Freeing:** Parameters are either frozen ($\nabla \theta = 0$) or unfrozen for gradient updates in specific stages [S2, unofficial].
*   **Accumulation:** The final representation $G_{final}$ is the union or sum of primitives from all stages, $G_{final} = \bigcup_i G_i$ [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
Two-stage optimization decouples the learning of stable geometric and appearance parameters from the optimization of complex attributes like material properties or compressed latent codes. It solves the instability caused by simultaneously training high-dimensional generative pipelines with compact or physically-based representations, setting itself apart from single-stage training by preventing gradient conflicts and from progressive training by using distinct, non-overlapping parameter sets rather than temporal data splitting. It builds on [Multi-stage and Progressive Training](18485-multi-stage-and-progressive-training.md) and contrasts with [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md) and [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md). See [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md) for details.

### [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)
On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training is a method for incrementally optimizing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) field as new images are acquired in real-time, enabling near real-time reconstruction without retraining the entire scene from scratch. It differs from offline training by decoupling the optimization domain temporally, appending new Gaussians for newly observed geometry and performing localized, weighted gradient updates for each incoming image. Unlike [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md), which separates geometry and appearance into sequential phases, this method integrates densification and appearance refinement continuously through a hierarchical weighting scheme and adaptive learning rates. See [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md) for details.

### [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md)
POD Pretraining is a distillation-based amortized optimization procedure that stabilizes the training of multi-stage Gaussian predictors by supervising them with locally refined Gaussian targets instead of unstable image-space gradients. It solves the problem of training collapse caused by the non-unique inverse mapping from residual images to Gaussian parameters, which leads to degenerate solutions when using direct rendering loss. Unlike direct image-space supervision, which suffers from error accumulation across stages, POD stabilizes stage-wise training by refining predicted Gaussian increments with a short-horizon optimizer and distilling the refined result back into the predictor as a regression target. See [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md) for details.

### Progressive Stage-wise Prediction
- [S2, unofficial] A prediction mode where Gaussian primitives are accumulated progressively across multiple stages rather than predicted in a single shot, allowing for controllable trade-offs between reconstruction quality and representation compactness.
- [S2, unofficial] Patch size (ps): The spatial resolution of the input patches. For the one-shot baseline, ps=7; for the stage-wise model, ps=14.
- [S2, unofficial] On DIV2K, the stage-wise predictor (ps=14) reaches 26.54 dB PSNR with 12,690 Gaussians at Stage 4, compared to the one-shot baseline (ps=7) which reaches 26.58 dB PSNR with 14,528 Gaussians.
- [S2, unofficial] In the stage-wise model on DIV2K, PSNR increases from 21.91 dB (Stage 1) to 26.54 dB (Stage 4), while the number of Gaussians increases from 3,658 to 12,690.
- [S2, unofficial] Compared to one-shot prediction, stage-wise prediction does not clearly improve final reconstruction quality but enables explicit control over the number of activated Gaussians, allowing adjustment of the quality-compactness trade-off.

## Background
Multi-stage and progressive training strategies emerged as 3DGS applications expanded from static scene reconstruction to dynamic, compressed, and physically-based rendering. The need to decouple geometry from appearance arose with the introduction of PBR-based 3DGS [S2, unofficial], while progressive training became essential for real-time SLAM and streaming scenarios [S1]. The concept of stage-wise prediction for feed-forward models was introduced to enable controllable compression and quality trade-offs [S2, unofficial].
