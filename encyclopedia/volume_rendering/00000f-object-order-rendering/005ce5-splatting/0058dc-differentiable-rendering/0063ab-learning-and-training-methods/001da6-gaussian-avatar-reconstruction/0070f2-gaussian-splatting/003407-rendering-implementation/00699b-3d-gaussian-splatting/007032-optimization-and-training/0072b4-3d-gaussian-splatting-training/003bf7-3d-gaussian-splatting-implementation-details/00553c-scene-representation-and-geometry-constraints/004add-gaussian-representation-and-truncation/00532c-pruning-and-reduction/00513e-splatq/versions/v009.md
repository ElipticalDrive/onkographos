# SplatQ

Also called: SplatQ: Post-training Gaussian pruning via QUBO

## Overview
SplatQ is a post-training Gaussian pruning framework that partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent budget concentration, and constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects to jointly select Gaussians under a fixed budget [S1, unofficial]. It differs from global Top-K methods by decomposing the selection into subproblems of specific size and solving a combinatorial optimization problem within each block, rather than ranking all Gaussians globally by independent scores [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model $G$ consisting of $N$ Gaussians, and a target retention count $K$ ($K < N$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A pruned set of $K$ Gaussians with updated attributes after fine-tuning [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The model is represented as $G = \{G_i = (\mu_i, s_i, r_i, c_i, h_i, o_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, where:
*   $\mu_i$: Center position [S1, unofficial].
*   $s_i$: Scale [S1, unofficial].
*   $r_i$: Rotation quaternion [S1, unofficial].
*   $c_i$: Zeroth-order (DC) spherical-harmonic coefficients [S1, unofficial].
*   $h_i$: Higher-order spherical-harmonic coefficients [S1, unofficial].
*   $o_i$: Opacity [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Total number of Gaussians in the pretrained model [S1, unofficial].
*   $K$: Exact number of Gaussians to retain [S1, unofficial].
*   $M$: Maximum size (number of members) of each block $C_b$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $K_b$: Retention quota assigned to block $b$, representing the number of Gaussians to retain within that block [S1, unofficial].
*   $R_b$: Response matrix for block $b$, used to estimate individual deletion effects and pairwise interactions [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Partitioning:** Divide the $N$ Gaussians into several size-limited blocks $\{C_b\}$ of at most $M$ members each, based on spatial proximity and appearance similarity [S1, unofficial]. This is achieved using a 3D grid and Morton ordering to efficiently group related Gaussians without quadratic pairwise comparison costs [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Opacity Response Modeling:** For each block $C_b$, compute a response matrix $R_b$ using sampled pixels from multiple views to estimate individual deletion effects and pairwise interactions [S1, unofficial]. This involves approximating joint rendering effects using the responses of representative pixel colors to changes in Gaussian opacity [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Budget Allocation:** Assign a retention quota $K_b$ to every block such that $\sum_b K_b = K$ [S1, unofficial]. This is done by deriving an initial quota from importance estimates and adjusting the distribution to reduce the risk of concentrating the budget in a few blocks [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Quadratic Selection:** For each block, formulate a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) objective incorporating individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties [S1, unofficial]. Solve the QUBO using Simulated Annealing (SA) or a Coherent Ising Machine (CIM) to determine which $K_b$ block members to retain [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Fine-tuning:** Fine-tune the attributes of the retained Gaussians without changing their count to recover rendering quality [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spatial Quality Imbalance:** Global ranking can lead to spatial quality imbalance where the limited budget concentrates in regions with consistently higher scores, leaving other regions with insufficient representational capacity [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ addresses this by partitioning and smoothing quotas [S1, unofficial].
*   **Joint Deletion Effects:** Independent scoring fails to capture joint rendering effects because multiple Gaussians may jointly support the same pixel or make overlapping contributions, meaning degradation from removing multiple Gaussians is not strictly additive [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ addresses this by modeling pairwise interactions via a quadratic objective [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scalability:** Formulating the optimization problem over the entire model requires $N$ binary variables and an $N \times N$ matrix, which is impractical for millions of Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ mitigates this by partitioning into blocks of size $M$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The partitioning step uses a 3D grid and Morton ordering to avoid quadratic pairwise comparisons [S1, unofficial]. The QUBO solving step is performed per block, with complexity depending on the block size $M$ and the solver (SA or CIM) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory requirements are reduced by solving smaller subproblems within each block rather than handling the full $N \times N$ matrix [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Ensures balanced reconstruction quality across a scene by coordinating regional budget allocation and joint Gaussian selection [S1, unofficial]. Captures joint deletion effects through quadratic approximation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires solving a combinatorial optimization problem (QUBO) for each block, which may be computationally intensive depending on the solver and block size [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When balanced reconstruction quality across different regions of a scene is critical, and when joint deletion effects are significant [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources for QUBO solving are limited, or when a simpler global Top-K pruning is sufficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
SplatQ was proposed by Hongbin Ye, Haixu Liu, Shenwei Kang, Wenxiu Wei, Tinglan Wang, Hongdong Zhu, Fan Tang, Qi Gao, Yin Ma, Kai Wen, Hai Wei, Nuo Qian, Wei-Shi Zheng, Shaobo Chen, Fa-Ting Hong, and Jingke Meng [S1, unofficial]. It was presented as a preprint on arXiv in October 2026 [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses limitations of existing 3DGS pruning methods that rely on global Top-K selection based on independent importance scores, which lack regional budget control and ignore joint deletion effects [S1, unofficial].
