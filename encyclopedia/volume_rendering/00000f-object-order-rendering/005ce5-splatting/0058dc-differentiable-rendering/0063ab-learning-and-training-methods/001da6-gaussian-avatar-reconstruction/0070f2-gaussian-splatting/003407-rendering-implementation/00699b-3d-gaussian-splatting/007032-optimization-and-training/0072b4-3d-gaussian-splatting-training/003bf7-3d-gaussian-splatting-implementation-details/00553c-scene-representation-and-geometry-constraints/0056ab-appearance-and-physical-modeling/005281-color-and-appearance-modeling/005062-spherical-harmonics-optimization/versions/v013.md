# Spherical Harmonics Optimization

Also called: SH Optimization, View-Dependent Color Optimization

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics Optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting refers to the family of techniques that modify the representation, storage, or training dynamics of the view-dependent radiance coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ to improve rendering quality, training stability, or inference efficiency. It solves the problem that standard third-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) are computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and prone to artifacts or instability in specific training regimes. It differs from its siblings by focusing specifically on the SH coefficients themselves (via distillation, quantization, or degree reduction) or the optimization infrastructure surrounding them (via Jacobian derivation or multi-view training), rather than replacing the SH representation entirely with neural fields or affine transformations.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering equation, where the pixel color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is computed via alpha-blending of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
where $T_i$ is the transmittance, $g_i$ is the 2D projected Gaussian density, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, and $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the view-dependent outgoing radiance. The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md).
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md).
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [Compact View-dependent Color].

## Subtopics

### [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md)
Reduces the SH degree from third-order (16 coefficients/channel) to first-order (4 coefficients/channel) to enable real-time mobile rendering. Unlike naive degree reduction, it uses a distillation strategy where a student model approximates the directional radiance of a pretrained third-order teacher, preserving quality better than direct projection. Choose this when target hardware lacks the compute for high-degree SH. See [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md).

### [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md)
Derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian primitives to enable efficient, differentiable camera tracking without automatic differentiation overhead. It provides the geometric gradient backbone required for pose optimization in SLAM systems. Choose this for real-time SLAM applications requiring precise gradient computation. See [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md).

### [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Enables stable optimization by training on multiple views per iteration, addressing the high variance of gradients in standard single-view training. It uses thread-efficient partial rendering and 3D-space loss computation to overcome rasterizer idle threads and Adaptive Density Control (ADC) incompatibilities. Choose this for faster convergence and stability in large-scale datasets. See [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md).

### [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md)
Compresses SH coefficients by replacing per-splat vectors with indices pointing to a shared palette of distinct SH vectors, significantly reducing memory footprint. It targets storage specifically, rather than modifying the rendering equation. Choose this when memory bandwidth or storage is the bottleneck. See [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md).

### SH Degree Zero Optimization
Setting the maximum degree of Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to 0 removes superfluous operations and improves training speed, as higher-degree SH coefficients for color remain unchanged during training [S1, unofficial]. Modifying the model code to delete operations relative to the max degree yields a 16.57% improvement in training speed compared to the default, with no explicit impact on final rendering quality [S1, unofficial]. This is because features represented by SH coefficients with degree > 0 are unchanged during all training in the original 3DGS code [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The use of Spherical Harmonics for view-dependent appearance in 3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the original 3DGS paper [Kerbl et al., 2023]. Subsequent work has identified inefficiencies in high-degree SH, leading to optimizations such as degree reduction [S1, unofficial], quantization [S1, unofficial], and distillation [S2, unofficial]. Analytical Jacobian derivation for camera poses was introduced to facilitate efficient SLAM [S1, S1, unofficial]. Multi-view training strategies were developed to stabilize gradient variance [S1, unofficial].
