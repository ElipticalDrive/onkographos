# Regularization and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevent over-reconstruction, stabilize optimization, and enforce geometric fidelity by modifying the loss function or Gaussian parameters [S1, S2]. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) which relies solely on photometric loss, these methods introduce explicit constraints in the frequency, spatial, or geometric domains to mitigate artifacts like floaters and blurred details [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of improving reconstruction quality beyond raw photometric fitting. All methods modify the training objective or Gaussian state to enforce specific properties: frequency consistency, spatial smoothness, or geometric surface alignment. They typically operate by adding a regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ to the total loss or by directly manipulating Gaussian parameters (covariance, center, opacity) during the optimization loop.

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction. It sets itself apart by operating in the Fourier domain, increasing pixel gradients in over-reconstructed areas to guide densification.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision stabilizes joint optimization of camera poses and scene representation by blurring 2D training images with a scheduled set of 2D Gaussian kernels. It solves noisy gradients in early training stages, unlike [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) which modulates loss weights based on frequency content.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
3D Half-Gaussian Splatting (3D-HGS) modifies the standard 3D Gaussian kernel into a "half-Gaussian" by enforcing a planar cut, effectively modeling sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space. It solves the problem of "floaters" and noise in empty regions by aligning the Gaussian's covariance matrix with local surface geometry, ensuring the ellipsoid is flattened against the surface normal.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization prevents detail loss and blurred appearance by constraining geometry through the movement of Gaussian centers rather than the deformation of their shapes. It solves the conflict that arises when standard depth regularization forces anisotropic Gaussians to conform to smooth monocular depth estimates, which contradicts complex color appearance. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating in spatial parameter space (centers/opacities) rather than image frequency space.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo-labels, ensuring geometric integrity and reducing floaters more robustly than single-scale depth priors.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions by constraining the shape and orientation of the Gaussians, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains their location. It sets itself apart from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by focusing on surface alignment and kernel flattening rather than explicit depth buffer matching.

#### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

#### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images I(i).
- [S2] Create a channel Γ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: Γ[u,v] = γ(u,v) if (u,v) is not in I(i), else 0. Concatenate I_pad and Γ to form I'(i) = concatenate(I_pad, Γ).
- [S2] γ(·) denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

#### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

#### FreGS
- [S4, unofficial] Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training.

## Background
The need for regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the tendency of the method to produce unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces when optimized solely for photometric loss [S3]. Early works like FreGS [S4, unofficial] and Faster-GS [S4, unofficial] addressed overfitting and oscillating gradients. Subsequent methods introduced geometric priors, such as SDFs [S3] and depth regularization [S1, S2], to recover high-quality geometry. The family evolved to include frequency-based constraints [S2] and cross-scale adaptation [S1] to handle complex scenes and transient effects.
