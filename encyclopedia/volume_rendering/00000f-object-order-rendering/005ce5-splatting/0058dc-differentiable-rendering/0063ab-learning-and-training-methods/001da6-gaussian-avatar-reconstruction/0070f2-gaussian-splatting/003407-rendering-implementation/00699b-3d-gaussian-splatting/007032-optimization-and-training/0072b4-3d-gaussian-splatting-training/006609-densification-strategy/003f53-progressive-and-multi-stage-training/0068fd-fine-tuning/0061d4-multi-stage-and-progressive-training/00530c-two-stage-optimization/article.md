# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy that partitions the optimization trajectory of 3D Gaussian Splatting into sequential phases to resolve conflicting gradient updates or stabilize unstable learning processes [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It typically begins with a coarse or codec-free phase to establish stable geometry or initial placement, followed by a fine-grained or codec-aware phase to refine details or optimize rate-distortion performance [S1][S3, unofficial]. This approach builds on [Multi-stage and Progressive Training](18485-multi-stage-and-progressive-training.md) by decoupling parameter optimization into distinct stages, preventing the interference between coarse structural learning and fine-detail refinement [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The method adapts its specific inputs, outputs, and formulas based on the target application (CT reconstruction, inverse rendering, or compression), but consistently follows a two-phase structure: a stabilization/coarse phase and a refinement/detail phase.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images/depth), camera poses, and potentially target views for supervision.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ with optimized parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/material) and, in compression variants, a compact bitstream or latent representation.

### Data Structures
The data structures depend on the variant:
1.  **Grid-based (CT):** A 3D voxel grid where Gaussians are initialized. Stage 1 uses a subsampled grid (e.g., $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of original resolution) [S1]. Stage 2 uses the full-resolution grid [S1].
2.  **Feed-Forward (CodecSplat):** A feature tensor $F_g \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C_F \times H \times W}$ generated by a multi-view encoder [S3, unofficial]. Stage 1 predicts Gaussians $G_{cf}$ directly from $F_g$ and encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$ [S3, unofficial]. Stage 2 introduces a feature codec and post-codec refiner to produce decoded Gaussians $\hat{G}$ [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Inverse Rendering (GS-IR):** Geometric parameters $T = \{p, s, q, n\}$ (position, scale, rotation, normal) and appearance parameters $A$ [S2]. Stage 1 optimizes $T$ and $A$ [S2]. Stage 2 fixes $T$ and $A$ to optimize material parameters $M = \{a, \rho, m\}$ (albedo, roughness, metalness) [S2].

### Numbered Steps

**Variant 1: Low-Resolution to Full-Resolution (CT Reconstruction) [S1]**
1.  **Stage 1 (Low-Resolution Optimization):**
    *   Sample a low-resolution grid, specifically $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume [S1].
    *   Initialize Gaussians on this grid.
    *   Minimize the projection loss using the $L_2$ norm between projected data $\hat{p}$ and actual measurements $p$:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \left\| \hat{p}[V(\theta)] - p \right\|_2^2 $$
        where $V(\theta)$ is the discretized volume defined by Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [S1].
    *   This rapidly places Gaussians in approximately correct positions [S1].
2.  **Stage 2 (Full-Resolution Refinement):**
    *   Sample over all voxels of the original full-resolution volume [S1].
    *   Refine Gaussian parameters to capture fine-grained details [S1].
    *   Continue minimizing the same projection loss to achieve high-quality reconstruction [S1].

**Variant 2: Codec-Free to Codec-Aware (Compression) [S3, unofficial]**
1.  **Stage 1 (Codec-Free Gaussian Generation):**
    *   Train the feed-forward Gaussian generation pipeline *without* the feature codec [S3, unofficial].
    *   Predict the codec-free Gaussian set $G_{cf}$ from the refined feature $F_c$ and encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Render the target view $\hat{Y}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(G_{cf}, K_t, T_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Minimize the rendering reconstruction loss:
        $$ L_{\text{stage1}} = D_{\text{render}}(\hat{Y}_{tcf}, Y_t) $$
        where $Y_t$ is the target-view image and $D_{\text{render}}$ is the reconstruction loss metric [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Stage 2 (Codec-Aware Latent Coding):**
    *   Initialize from the Stage 1 checkpoint [S3, unofficial].
    *   Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner [S3, unofficial].
    *   Optimize codec-related modules while keeping remaining backbone modules fixed [S3, unofficial].
    *   Decode the Gaussian set $\hat{G}$ and render the target view $\hat{Y}_t = \text{Render}(\hat{G}, K_t, T_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Minimize the rate-distortion objective:
        $$ L_{\text{stage2}}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\max}} \left[ D_{\text{render}}(\hat{Y}_t, Y_t) + \gamma D_{\text{codec-depth}} \right] + \beta R $$
        where $R$ is the estimated rate, $\lambda \in [16, 1024]$ is sampled during training (fixed at test time), $\lambda_{\max} = 1024$, $\gamma = 0.1$ is the codec-side depth supervision weight, and $\beta$ is the rate penalty weight ($10^{-4}$ for DL3DV, $4 \times 10^{-4}$ for RE10K) [S3, unofficial].
    *   The codec-side depth loss is defined on normalized inverse depth:
        $$ D_{\text{codec-depth}} = \| S - S_{enc} \|_1 $$
        where $S$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $S_{enc}$ is the supervision target [S3, unofficial].
    *   $S_{enc}$ is computed from encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$:
        $$ S_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{D_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        where $n_i$ and $f_i$ are the near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S3, unofficial].

**Variant 3: Initial Geometry to Material Decomposition (Inverse Rendering) [S2]**
1.  **Stage 1 (Initial Optimization):**
    *   Minimize color reconstruction loss $L_c$ and normal loss $L_n$ to optimize geometric parameters $T$ and appearance $A$ for 30,000 iterations [S2].
    *   The normal loss $L_n$ includes a total variation (TV) smoothing term:
        $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} \text{TV}_{\text{normal}} $$
        where $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$ [S2].
2.  **Stage 2 (Decomposition):**
    *   Fix $T$ and $A$ [S2].
    *   Minimize the decomposition loss $L_d$ to optimize material parameters $M$ for 10,000 iterations [S2].
    *   The decomposition loss is:
        $$ L_d = \underbrace{\| I - \hat{I}_{\text{shade}}(\hat{M}, \hat{E}, V_{\text{illu}}) \|}_{L_{\text{shade}}} + \lambda_M \text{TV}_{\text{mat}} + \lambda_E \text{TV}_{\text{light}} $$
        where $\lambda_M = 1.0$ and $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2].
    *   $\text{TV}_{\text{mat}}$ and $\text{TV}_{\text{light}}$ are total variation smoothing terms for material and light maps, respectively [S2].

### Parameters
*   **Optimizers:** Adam [S2] or AdamW [S3, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ with weight decay $0.01$ (AdamW) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** 30,000 for Stage 1 and 10,000 for Stage 2 in GS-IR [S2]. Not specified for CT or CodecSplat in the provided notes.
*   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA V100 GPU (GS-IR) [S2]; Four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB GPUs (CodecSplat) [S3, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck is unstable; the two-stage separation is required to stabilize Gaussian generation before introducing compression artifacts [S3, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Conflicts:** Fixing parameters in Stage 2 (e.g., geometry in GS-IR) prevents conflicting gradient updates between geometry and material optimization [S2].
*   **Rate Selection:** In CodecSplat, $\lambda$ must be sampled during training to cover the rate-distortion curve, but fixed at test time for a specific bit-rate [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two-stage optimization generally increases total training time compared to single-stage methods due to the sequential nature of the phases [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. However, Stage 1's low-resolution or codec-free setup can accelerate initial convergence [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stage 1 in CT reconstruction uses reduced memory by operating on a subsampled grid ($1/8$ of voxels) [S1]. CodecSplat reduces final storage to KiB-level bitstreams [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **CT:** Achieves accurate sparse-view reconstruction by refining details in Stage 2 [S1].
    *   **Compression:** CodecSplat achieves KiB-level scene bitstreams while preserving dominant scene structure, outperforming MiB-level compressed baselines [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Inverse Rendering:** GS-IR achieves high-quality inverse rendering by decoupling geometry and material optimization [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes training for complex objectives (compression, inverse rendering); prevents gradient conflicts; enables high-fidelity reconstruction [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and hyperparameters (e.g., $\lambda$, $\gamma$, $\beta$); increased training duration [S2][S3, unofficial].

## Background
Two-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address specific challenges in compression and inverse rendering. CodecSplat introduced the codec-free/codec-aware split to stabilize feed-forward Gaussian generation with quantized bottlenecks [S3, unofficial]. GS-IR separated geometry and material optimization to enable inverse rendering with physically based rendering [S2]. The low-resolution to full-resolution approach was used in 3DGR-CT to handle sparse-view CT reconstruction efficiently [S1].
