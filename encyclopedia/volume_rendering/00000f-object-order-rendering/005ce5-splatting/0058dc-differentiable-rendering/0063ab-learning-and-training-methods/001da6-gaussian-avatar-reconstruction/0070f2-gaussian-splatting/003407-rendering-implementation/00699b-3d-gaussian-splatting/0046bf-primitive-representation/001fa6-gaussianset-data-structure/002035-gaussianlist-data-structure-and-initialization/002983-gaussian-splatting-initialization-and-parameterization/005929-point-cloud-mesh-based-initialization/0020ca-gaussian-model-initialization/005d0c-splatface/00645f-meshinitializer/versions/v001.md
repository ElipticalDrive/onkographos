# MeshInitializer

Also called: Mesh-embedded Gaussian Initialization

## Overview
MeshInitializer initializes 3D Gaussian splats by placing them on the surface of a triangle mesh using barycentric coordinates to determine position, normal interpolation, and color interpolation [S1, unofficial]. It differs from VoxelInitializer by adhering strictly to the mesh topology rather than a grid, and from generic point-cloud initialization by leveraging explicit geometric surface data [S1, unofficial]. It is a variant of Gaussian Splatting Initialization and builds on [SplatFace](17294-splatface.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Triangle mesh defined by vertices $V \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times 3}$ and faces $F \in \mathbb{R}^{N_f \times 3}$ (indices into $V$).
- Optional vertex colors $C_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times 3}$.
- Optional vertex normals $N_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times 3}$.
- Parameter $n_{gpst}$ (number of Gaussians per surface triangle), integer $\in \{1, 3, 4, 6\}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Gaussian means $M \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g \times 3}$, where $N_g = N_f \times n_{gpst}$.
- Gaussian rotations $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g \times 4}$ (quaternions).
- Gaussian scales $S \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g \times 3}$ (log-space).
- Gaussian opacities $O \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g}$ (pre-sigmoid).
- Gaussian colors $C \in \mathbb{R}^{N_g \times 3}$ (if vertex colors provided, else None).

**Data Structures:**
- Barycentric patterns are precomputed static arrays based on $n_{gpst}$.
- Edge lengths are computed per face.

**Steps:**

1. **Select Barycentric Pattern:**
   Based on $n_{gpst}$, select the precomputed barycentric coordinates $B \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{gpst} \times 3}$ and circle radius $r_{circ}$:
   - If $n_{gpst} = 1$:
     $$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 & 1/3 & 1/3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad r_{circ} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}} $$
   - If $n_{gpst} = 3$:
     $$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 1/2 & 1/4 & 1/4 \\ 1/4 & 1/2 & 1/4 \\ 1/4 & 1/4 & 1/2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad r_{circ} = \frac{1}{2(\sqrt{3}+1)} $$
   - If $n_{gpst} = 4$:
     $$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 1/3 & 1/3 & 1/3 \\ 2/3 & 1/6 & 1/6 \\ 1/6 & 2/3 & 1/6 \\ 1/6 & 1/6 & 2/3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad r_{circ} = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}} $$
   - If $n_{gpst} = 6$:
     $$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 2/3 & 1/6 & 1/6 \\ 1/6 & 2/3 & 1/6 \\ 1/6 & 1/6 & 2/3 \\ 1/6 & 5/12 & 5/12 \\ 5/12 & 1/6 & 5/12 \\ 5/12 & 5/12 & 1/6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad r_{circ} = \frac{1}{4+2\sqrt{3}} $$
   [S1, unofficial]

2. **Compute Gaussian Positions:**
   For each face $f$ with vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2$, compute positions for each Gaussian $k \in \{0, \dots, n_{gpst}-1\}$:
   $$ M_{f,k} = B_k[0] \cdot v_0 + B_k[1] \cdot v_1 + B_k[2] \cdot v_2 $$
   Vectorized: $M = \sum_{j=0}^{2} F[:, j, :] \otimes B$, where $\otimes$ denotes broadcasting multiplication and summation over the vertex dimension, resulting in shape $(N_f \cdot n_{gpst}, 3)$ [S1, unofficial].

3. **Compute Surface Normals:**
   If vertex normals $N_v$ are available:
   $$ N_{f,k} = B_k[0] \cdot N_{v,0} + B_k[1] \cdot N_{v,1} + B_k[2] \cdot N_{v,2} $$
   Normalize the resulting vector:
   $$ \hat{N}_{f,k} = \frac{N_{f,k}}{\|N_{f,k}\|} $$
   If vertex normals are not available, this step is skipped or defaults to face normals (implementation detail not specified in source, assumed handled by caller or defaulting to None) [S1, unofficial].

4. **Convert Normals to Rotations:**
   Pass $\hat{N}$ to `normal2rotation` to obtain quaternions $Q$ [S1, unofficial].

5. **Compute Colors:**
   If vertex colors $C_v$ exist:
   $$ C_{f,k} = B_k[0] \cdot C_{v,0} + B_k[1] \cdot C_{v,1} + B_k[2] \cdot C_{v,2} $$
   Otherwise, return None [S1, unofficial].

6. **Compute Scales:**
   - Calculate edge lengths for each triangle. Let $L_{f,i}$ be the length of edge $i$ of face $f$.
   - Find the minimum edge length for each face: $L_{min,f} = \min(L_{f,0}, L_{f,1}, L_{f,2})$.
   - Compute base scale: $s_{base,f} = L_{min,f} \cdot r_{circ}$.
   - Clamp to minimum 0: $s'_{base,f} = \max(s_{base,f}, 0)$.
   - Expand to 2D scale $(x, y)$ and append 0 for $z$-scale: $S_{2D,f} = [s'_{base,f}, s'_{base,f}, 0]$.
   - Convert to log space:
     $$ S_{f,k} = \log(S_{2D,f} \cdot 2 + 10^{-7}) $$
     This results in shape $(N_g, 3)$ [S1, unofficial].

7. **Set Opacity:**
   Set all Gaussians to fully opaque:
   $$ O = \text{inverse\_sigmoid}(1.0) $$
   [S1, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def normal2rotation(n):
    """
    Constructs a rotation matrix from the normal and an orthogonal basis, then converts to quaternion.
    Args:
        n: Tensor of shape (..., 3), normals.
    Returns:
        q: Tensor of shape (..., 4), quaternions.
    """
    n = F.normalize(n, dim=-1)
    # Create a reference vector [1, 0, 0]
    w0 = torch.tensor([[1.0, 0.0, 0.0]], device=n.device, dtype=n.dtype).expand(n.shape)
    
    # Project w0 onto the plane orthogonal to n
    # R0 = w0 - (w0 . n) * n
    dot_w0_n = torch.sum(w0 * n, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    R0 = w0 - dot_w0_n * n
    
    # Ensure stability by flipping sign if the first component is negative
    R0 *= torch.sign(R0[:, :1])
    R0 = F.normalize(R0, dim=-1)
    
    # R1 is orthogonal to both n and R0
    R1 = torch.cross(n, R0)
    # Adjust sign for stability
    R1 *= torch.sign(R1[:, 1:2]) * torch.sign(n[:, 2:])
    
    # Construct rotation matrix R = [R0, R1, n]
    R = torch.stack([R0, R1, n], dim=-1)
    
    # Convert rotation matrix to quaternion
    q = rotmat2quaternion(R)
    return q

def rotmat2quaternion(R, normalize=False):
    """
    Converts a rotation matrix to a quaternion.
    Args:
        R: Tensor of shape (..., 3, 3), rotation matrices.
        normalize: Whether to normalize the resulting quaternion.
    Returns:
        q: Tensor of shape (..., 4), quaternions.
    """
    # Trace of the rotation matrix
    tr = R[:, 0, 0] + R[:, 1, 1] + R[:, 2, 2] + 1e-6
    
    # Calculate the scalar part of the quaternion
    r = torch.sqrt(1 + tr) / 2
    
    # Calculate the vector part
    q = torch.stack([
        r,
        (R[:, 2, 1] - R[:, 1, 2]) / (4 * r),
        (R[:, 0, 2] - R[:, 2, 0]) / (4 * r),
        (R[:, 1, 0] - R[:, 0, 1]) / (4 * r)
    ], dim=-1)
    
    if normalize:
        q = F.normalize(q, dim=-1)
    return q

def inverse_sigmoid(x):
    """Computes the inverse sigmoid function."""
    return torch.log(x / (1 - x + 1e-7))

def mesh_initializer(mesh_vertices, mesh_faces, mesh_vertex_normals=None, mesh_vertex_colors=None, n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle=1):
    """
    Initializes 3D Gaussian splats on a mesh surface.
    
    Args:
        mesh_vertices: Tensor of shape (N_v, 3).
        mesh_faces: Tensor of shape (N_f, 3), indices into mesh_vertices.
        mesh_vertex_normals: Tensor of shape (N_v, 3), optional.
        mesh_vertex_colors: Tensor of shape (N_v, 3), optional.
        n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle: int, 1, 3, 4, or 6.
        
    Returns:
        means: Tensor of shape (N_g, 3).
        rotations: Tensor of shape (N_g, 4).
        scales: Tensor of shape (N_g, 3).
        opacities: Tensor of shape (N_g,).
        colors: Tensor of shape (N_g, 3) or None.
    """
    # 1. Setup barycentric coordinates and circle radius
    if n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle == 1:
        barycentric_coords = torch.tensor([[1/3, 1/3, 1/3]], dtype=mesh_vertices.dtype, device=mesh_vertices.device)
        circle_radius = 1.0 / (2.0 * torch.sqrt(torch.tensor(3.0)))
    elif n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle == 3:
        barycentric_coords = torch.tensor([[1/2, 1/4, 1/4], [1/4, 1/2, 1/4], [1/4, 1/4, 1/2]], dtype=mesh_vertices.dtype, device=mesh_vertices.device)
        circle_radius = 1.0 / (2.0 * (torch.sqrt(torch.tensor(3.0)) + 1.0))
    elif n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle == 4:
        barycentric_coords = torch.tensor([[1/3, 1/3, 1/3], [2/3, 1/6, 1/6], [1/6, 2/3, 1/6], [1/6, 1/6, 2/3]], dtype=mesh_vertices.dtype, device=mesh_vertices.device)
        circle_radius = 1.0 / (4.0 * torch.sqrt(torch.tensor(3.0)))
    elif n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle == 6:
        barycentric_coords = torch.tensor([[2/3, 1/6, 1/6], [1/6, 2/3, 1/6], [1/6, 1/6, 2/3], [1/6, 5/12, 5/12], [5/12, 1/6, 5/12], [5/12, 5/12, 1/6]], dtype=mesh_vertices.dtype, device=mesh_vertices.device)
        circle_radius = 1.0 / (4.0 + 2.0 * torch.sqrt(torch.tensor(3.0)))
    else:
        raise ValueError("n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle must be 1, 3, 4, or 6")

    # Gather vertex positions for all faces
    # mesh_faces shape: (N_f, 3)
    # face_vertices shape: (N_f, 3, 3)
    face_vertices = mesh_vertices[mesh_faces]
    
    # Compute positions
    # barycentric_coords shape: (n_gpst, 3)
    # face_vertices shape: (N_f, 3, 3)
    # We need to broadcast multiply and sum over the vertex dimension (dim 1 of face_vertices)
    # Reshape for broadcasting: (N_f, 3, 1) * (1, n_gpst, 3) -> (N_f, n_gpst, 3)
    # But barycentric_coords is (n_gpst, 3). 
    # Let's use einsum or manual expansion.
    # positions shape: (N_f, n_gpst, 3)
    positions = torch.einsum('ijk,kl->ijl', face_vertices, barycentric_coords)
    means = positions.reshape(-1, 3)
    
    # Compute normals
    if mesh_vertex_normals is not None:
        face_normals = mesh_vertex_normals[mesh_faces]
        interpolated_normals = torch.einsum('ijk,kl->ijl', face_normals, barycentric_coords)
        normals = F.normalize(interpolated_normals.reshape(-1, 3), dim=-1)
        rotations = normal2rotation(normals)
    else:
        # Fallback: compute face normals if vertex normals not provided
        # This is a gap filler [G] as source doesn't specify behavior if normals are missing
        v0 = face_vertices[:, 0, :]
        v1 = face_vertices[:, 1, :]
        v2 = face_vertices[:, 2, :]
        n0 = F.normalize(torch.cross(v1 - v0, v2 - v0), dim=-1)
        # Broadcast to all gaussians on the face
        normals = n0.repeat_interleave(n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle, dim=0)
        rotations = normal2rotation(normals)

    # Compute colors
    if mesh_vertex_colors is not None:
        face_colors = mesh_vertex_colors[mesh_faces]
        interpolated_colors = torch.einsum('ijk,kl->ijl', face_colors, barycentric_coords)
        colors = interpolated_colors.reshape(-1, 3)
    else:
        colors = None

    # Compute scales
    # Edge lengths
    v0 = face_vertices[:, 0, :]
    v1 = face_vertices[:, 1, :]
    v2 = face_vertices[:, 2, :]
    e0 = torch.norm(v1 - v0, dim=-1)
    e1 = torch.norm(v2 - v1, dim=-1)
    e2 = torch.norm(v0 - v2, dim=-1)
    
    # Min edge length per face
    min_edge_len = torch.min(torch.stack([e0, e1, e2], dim=-1), dim=-1).values
    
    # Base scale
    base_scale = min_edge_len * circle_radius
    base_scale = torch.clamp(base_scale, min=0.0)
    
    # Expand to 3D scale (x, y, z)
    # Shape (N_f, 3)
    scales_2d = torch.stack([base_scale, base_scale, torch.zeros_like(base_scale)], dim=-1)
    
    # Log space conversion
    # Shape (N_f, 3)
    scales_log = torch.log(scales_2d * 2.0 + 1e-7)
    
    # Repeat for each gaussian on the face
    scales = scales_log.repeat_interleave(n_gaussians_per_surface_triangle, dim=0)
    
    # Opacity
    opacities = inverse_sigmoid(torch.ones_like(means[:, 0]))
    
    return means, rotations, scales, opacities, colors
```

## Subtopics

### VoxelInitializer
VoxelInitializer initializes 3D Gaussian splats by voxelizing a mesh and placing Gaussians at voxel centers that intersect the mesh surface [S1, unofficial]. It differs from MeshInitializer by using a grid-based approach rather than barycentric interpolation on triangles. It is chosen when a uniform grid distribution is preferred over surface-adaptive distribution. Parameter `voxel_size` defaults to $1/256$ [S1, unofficial].

### 3DGS Initialization on CAD Mesh
This variant initializes 600,000 Gaussian primitives on a canonicalized CAD mesh surface [S2, unofficial]. It sets initial log-scales so each splat covers the mean distance to its three nearest neighbors, quaternions to the shortest-arc rotation aligned with the surface normal, and opacities to the post-sigmoid activation 0.5 [S2, unofficial]. A key pitfall is that means and quaternions are frozen entirely; geometric corrections are absorbed by a deformation field $D_\theta$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of triangles $N_f$ and the number of Gaussians per triangle $n_{gpst}$. The dominant operations are barycentric interpolation and edge length calculations, which are vectorizable.
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to $N_g = N_f \times n_{gpst}$. Storage includes means, rotations, scales, opacities, and optionally colors.
- **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Ensures Gaussians are strictly aligned with the mesh geometry, providing accurate surface representation. Leverages existing mesh normals and colors.
- **Limitations:** Dependent on mesh quality (e.g., triangle size variation affects scale distribution). Does not cover volume or off-surface regions.
- **When to use:** When a high-quality triangle mesh is available and surface fidelity is critical.
- **When not to use:** When the input is a point cloud without mesh connectivity, or when volumetric coverage is required.

## Background
MeshInitializer is part of the GauStudio framework, a modular framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting and beyond [S1, unofficial]. It was introduced in the context of supporting mesh-based initialization for 3DGS, allowing for robust reconstruction from CAD models or textured meshes. The method leverages barycentric coordinates to distribute Gaussians evenly across triangle surfaces, a technique common in computer graphics for sampling surfaces.
