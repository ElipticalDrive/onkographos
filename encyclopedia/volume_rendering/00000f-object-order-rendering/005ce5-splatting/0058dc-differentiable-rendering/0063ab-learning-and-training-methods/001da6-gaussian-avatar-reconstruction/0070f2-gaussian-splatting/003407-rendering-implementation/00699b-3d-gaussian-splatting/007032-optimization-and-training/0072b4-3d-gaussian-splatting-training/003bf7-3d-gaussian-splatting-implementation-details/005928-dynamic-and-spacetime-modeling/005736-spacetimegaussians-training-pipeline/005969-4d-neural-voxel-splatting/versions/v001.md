# 4D Neural Voxel Splatting

Also called: 4D-NVS

## Overview
4D Neural Voxel Splatting (4D-NVS) is a dynamic scene rendering method that combines voxel-based representations with neural Gaussian splatting to decouple spatial structure from temporal dynamics [S1]. Unlike static methods like Scaffold-GS or methods that replicate Gaussians across time, 4D-NVS generates time-varying Gaussians on-demand from persistent voxel anchors using a unified deformation field, reducing memory complexity from $O(N \cdot T)$ to $O(fV + F)$ [S1]. This approach enables real-time rendering of dynamic scenes with significantly lower memory consumption and faster training times compared to existing radiance-field optimization techniques [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse point cloud from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]; camera poses and timestamps [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered images for arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Neural Voxels:** A 4D voxel grid. Each voxel $v$ has a center position $x_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a spatial extent (scale factor) $l_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and a learnable feature vector $f_v \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$ [S1].
2.  **Gaussian Offsets:** For each voxel, $k$ learnable offset vectors $O_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ ($i=0 \dots k-1$) define the relative positions of spawned Gaussians [S1].
3.  **HexPlane Grids:** A decomposition of the 4D space into 2D planes (XY, XZ, YZ, XT, YT, ZT) with specific resolutions (e.g., [64, 64, 64, 150] for DyNeRF) and scale multipliers [1, 2] [S1].
4.  **Refinement Stack:** A list of problematic viewpoints with metadata: failure type (quality vs. gradient), severity score, and temporal consistency flags [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $k$: Number of Gaussians per neural voxel. Typical value: 10 [S1].
*   $f_v$: Feature dimension of the neural voxel. Type: $\mathbb{R}^{32}$ [S1].
*   $\gamma$: PSNR threshold decay factor. Starts at 0.05, decays to 0.02 [S1].
*   Momentum for EMA: 0.4 [S1].
*   Densification threshold $\tau_g$: 0.0002 (coarse), 0.0001 (refinement) [S1].
*   Opacity threshold $\tau_\alpha$: 0.05 (coarse), 0.03 (refinement) [S1] [G].
*   Learning rates: Gaussian offsets 0.01, color MLP 0.008, opacity MLP 0.002, rotation/scaling MLPs 0.004, HexPlane grids 0.0016, deformation MLPs 0.00016 [S1]. All decay to 1/10 by iteration 14,000 [S1].
*   Loss weights: Volume regularization $\lambda_{vol} = 0.015$, Total variation $\lambda_{tv} = 0.0002$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Perform COLMAP initialization from the first frame (for Neu3D) or 200 random frames (for HyperNeRF) to yield 5,000–20,000 initial points [S1].
    *   Distribute voxel centers to ensure comprehensive spatial coverage using the sparse point cloud [S1].
    *   Train the scene using Scaffold-GS for 3,000 iterations with mixed timestamp samples to establish a foundational geometric representation without temporal deformation [S1].

2.  **Visibility Culling:**
    *   At each timestamp $t$, identify voxels within the camera frustum [S1].
    *   This reduces computational overhead from $O(N)$ to $O(V_{visible})$ where $V_{visible} \ll N$ [S1].

3.  **Neural Gaussian Generation:**
    *   For each visible voxel $v$, generate $k$ neural Gaussians [S1].
    *   Compute Gaussian positions: $\mu_i = x_v + O_i \cdot l_v$ for $i = 0, \dots, k-1$ [S1].
    *   Decode attributes using dedicated MLPs taking anchor features $f_v$, viewing distance $\delta_{vc}$, and directional vector $d_{vc}$:
        $$ \{\alpha_0 \dots \alpha_{k-1}\} = F_\alpha(f_v, \delta_{vc}, d_{vc}) $$
        $$ \{c_0 \dots c_{k-1}\} = F_c(f_v, \delta_{vc}, d_{vc}) $$
        $$ \{s_0 \dots s_{k-1}\} = F_s(f_v, \delta_{vc}, d_{vc}) $$
        $$ \{r_0 \dots r_{k-1}\} = F_r(f_v, \delta_{vc}, d_{vc}) $$
        where $F_\alpha, F_c, F_s, F_r$ are MLPs for opacity, color, scale, and rotation respectively [S1].

4.  **Deformation (HexPlane Encoder):**
    *   Extract features from HexPlane grids [S1].
    *   Integrate features using a shallow network (MLP) $\phi_d$: $f_d = \phi_d(f_h)$, where $f_h$ are input features and $f_d$ are integrated features [S1].
    *   Predict offsets using single-layer MLPs $\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s$:
        $$ \Delta\mu = \phi_x(f_d) $$
        $$ \Delta r = \phi_r(f_d) $$
        $$ \Delta s = \phi_s(f_d) $$
        where $\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s$ output 3D position offsets, quaternion rotations, and 3D scale factors respectively [S1].
    *   Apply selective deformation: Only geometric attributes are deformed. Color and opacity remain unchanged [S1].
    *   Compute final deformed parameters:
        $$ (\mu', r', s') = (\mu + \Delta\mu, r + \Delta r, s + \Delta s) $$
        Color and opacity remain unchanged [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Minimize loss $L_{color} = L_1 + \lambda_{SSIM} L_{SSIM}$ for pixel accuracy and structural coherence [S1].
    *   Apply volume regularization loss $L_{vol} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_g} sx_i \cdot sy_i \cdot sz_i$ to prevent oversized Gaussians [S1].
    *   Apply total variation loss $L_{tv}$ to enforce spatial smoothness in the HexPlane [S1].
    *   Perform density control: Grow anchors in high-gradient regions and prune those producing consistently low-opacity Gaussians [S1].

6.  **View Refinement (Stage 3):**
    *   **Crude View Selection:** Identify underperforming viewpoints using two strategies [S1]:
        *   PSNR-based: $PSNR_i < (1 + \gamma) \cdot EMA_{PSNR}$, where $EMA_{PSNR} = 0.4 \cdot PSNR_{current} + 0.6 \cdot EMA_{PSNR}$ [S1].
        *   Gradient-based: $\|\nabla L_{view}\| > (1 + \gamma) \cdot EMA_{grad}$, where $\nabla L_{view}$ is the gradient magnitude of viewspace points and $EMA_{grad}$ is the exponential moving average of gradients [S1].
    *   Add identified viewpoints to the refinement stack with metadata [S1].
    *   **Focused Training:** Use only crude viewpoints for training in a dedicated stage (14,000 iterations), with sampling probability weighted by severity scores [S1].
    *   **Adjusted Thresholds:** Use more aggressive densification parameters (lower $\tau_g$ and $\tau_\alpha$) to encourage Gaussian splitting and pruning in challenging regions [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def initialize_voxels(sparse_points):
    # COLMAP initialization yields 5,000-20,000 points
    voxel_centers = distribute_centers(sparse_points)
    voxel_features = init_features(voxel_centers) # R32
    voxel_offsets = init_offsets(k=10) # R3 per voxel
    return voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets

def train_initialization(voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, timestamps, cameras, iterations=3000):
    # Scaffold-GS training with mixed timestamp samples
    for i in range(iterations):
        t = sample_timestamp(timestamps)
        cam = sample_camera(cameras)
        visible_voxels = cull_frustum(voxel_centers, cam)
        gaussians = generate_gaussians(visible_voxels, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, cam)
        image = render(gaussians, cam)
        target = get_ground_truth(t, cam)
        loss = compute_loss(image, target)
        update_parameters(loss)
    return voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets

def train_main(voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, timestamps, cameras, iterations=14000):
    for i in range(iterations):
        t = sample_timestamp(timestamps)
        cam = sample_camera(cameras)
        visible_voxels = cull_frustum(voxel_centers, cam)
        
        # Generate base Gaussians
        gaussians = generate_gaussians(visible_voxels, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, cam)
        
        # Apply deformation
        hex_features = extract_hex_features(t, visible_voxels)
        integrated_features = phi_d(hex_features)
        delta_mu = phi_x(integrated_features)
        delta_r = phi_r(integrated_features)
        delta_s = phi_s(integrated_features)
        
        deformed_gaussians = apply_deformation(gaussians, delta_mu, delta_r, delta_s)
        # Color and opacity remain unchanged
        
        image = render(deformed_gaussians, cam)
        target = get_ground_truth(t, cam)
        
        # Loss computation
        loss_color = L1(image, target) + lambda_ssim * SSIM(image, target)
        loss_vol = volume_regularization(deformed_gaussians)
        loss_tv = total_variation(hex_features)
        loss = loss_color + lambda_vol * loss_vol + lambda_tv * loss_tv
        
        update_parameters(loss)
        
        # Density control
        grow_anchors_in_high_gradient_regions()
        prune_low_opacity_gaussians()
        
    return voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets

def train_refinement(voxel_centers, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, timestamps, cameras, iterations=14000):
    ema_psnr = 0
    ema_grad = 0
    refinement_stack = []
    
    for i in range(iterations):
        # Identify crude views
        crude_views = []
        for cam in cameras:
            t = sample_timestamp(timestamps)
            visible_voxels = cull_frustum(voxel_centers, cam)
            gaussians = generate_gaussians(visible_voxels, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, cam)
            hex_features = extract_hex_features(t, visible_voxels)
            integrated_features = phi_d(hex_features)
            delta_mu = phi_x(integrated_features)
            delta_r = phi_r(integrated_features)
            delta_s = phi_s(integrated_features)
            deformed_gaussians = apply_deformation(gaussians, delta_mu, delta_r, delta_s)
            
            image = render(deformed_gaussians, cam)
            target = get_ground_truth(t, cam)
            
            # Compute metrics
            psnr = compute_psnr(image, target)
            grad_mag = compute_gradient_magnitude(image, target)
            
            # Update EMAs
            ema_psnr = 0.4 * psnr + 0.6 * ema_psnr
            ema_grad = 0.4 * grad_mag + 0.6 * ema_grad
            
            # Check thresholds
            gamma = decay_gamma(i) # 0.05 to 0.02
            if psnr < (1 + gamma) * ema_psnr:
                crude_views.append((cam, 'quality', psnr))
            if grad_mag > (1 + gamma) * ema_grad:
                crude_views.append((cam, 'gradient', grad_mag))
                
        if not crude_views:
            continue
            
        # Focused training on crude views
        for cam, failure_type, severity in crude_views:
            t = sample_timestamp(timestamps)
            visible_voxels = cull_frustum(voxel_centers, cam)
            gaussians = generate_gaussians(visible_voxels, voxel_features, voxel_offsets, cam)
            hex_features = extract_hex_features(t, visible_voxels)
            integrated_features = phi_d(hex_features)
            delta_mu = phi_x(integrated_features)
            delta_r = phi_r(integrated_features)
            delta_s = phi_s(integrated_features)
            deformed_gaussians = apply_deformation(gaussians, delta_mu, delta_r, delta_s)
            
            image = render(deformed_gaussians, cam)
            target = get_ground_truth(t, cam)
            
            loss = compute_loss(image, target)
            update_parameters(loss)
            
            # Aggressive densification
            grow_anchors_in_high_gradient_regions(threshold=0.0001)
            prune_low_opacity_gaussians(threshold=0.03)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(fV + F)$, where $V$ is the number of voxels, $f$ is the feature dimension per voxel, and $F$ represents the deformation network parameters [S1]. This is a significant reduction from the traditional $O(N \cdot T)$ scaling [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   Memory usage: 3,050 MiB (86% reduction vs TiNeuVox-B, 32% vs 4D-GS) [S1].
    *   Training time: 17 minutes with refinement (13 minutes without) (94% speedup vs HyperNeRF, 32% vs 4D-GS, 89% vs GaGS) [S1].
    *   Rendering performance: 43 FPS on Neu3D dataset at 1,352x1,014 resolution (order of magnitude faster than Im4D and MSTH) [S1].
    *   Quality on HyperNeRF: 28.5 PSNR and 0.872 MS-SSIM at 960x540 resolution [S1].
    *   Quality on Neu3D: 33.12 PSNR and 0.021 D-SSIM at 1,352x1,014 resolution [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering, low memory footprint, fast training, stable convergence due to selective deformation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Large motions can be challenging due to absence of initial points and camera pose inaccuracies; popping artifacts are not entirely eliminated [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time dynamic scene visualization on consumer hardware [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with extremely large motions where initial point estimation is poor, or where popping artifacts are unacceptable without further rasterization enhancements [S1].

## Background
4D Neural Voxel Splatting was proposed by the authors of the paper "4D Neural Voxel Splatting: Dynamic Scene Rendering with Voxelized Guassian Splatting" [S1]. It builds on established techniques such as Scaffold-GS for voxel-based generation and HexPlane decomposition for temporal modeling [S1]. The method addresses the substantial memory overhead of extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes by replicating Gaussians across frames [S1].
