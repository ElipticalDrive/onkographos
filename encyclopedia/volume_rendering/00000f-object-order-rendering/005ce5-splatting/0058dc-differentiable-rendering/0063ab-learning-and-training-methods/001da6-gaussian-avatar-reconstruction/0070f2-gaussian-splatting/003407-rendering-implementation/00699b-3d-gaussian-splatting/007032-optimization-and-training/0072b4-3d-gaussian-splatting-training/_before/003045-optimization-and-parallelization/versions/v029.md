# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed 3D Gaussian Splatting Training, Multi-GPU Gaussian Optimization

## Overview
This family encompasses methods that accelerate the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) through algorithmic optimizations, parallel execution strategies, and specialized gradient computations. It solves the computational bottleneck of differentiable rasterization and the memory limits of single-GPU training when handling scenes with billions of primitives. It is distinguished from standard optimization by explicitly addressing hardware constraints (via parallelization and fused kernels) and representation efficiency (via mask-aware gradients and dynamic densification pipelines), building on [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md).

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing the gradient of the loss with respect to probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to 3D Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly accounting for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground Gaussian color accumulation and the background transmittance path, solving the backpropagation problem through masked alpha-blending. Choose this when training with explicit object masks or segmentation priors.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself from standard optimization by adapting the representation density to scene complexity via axis-slice rendering and gradient-based refinement. Choose this as the standard baseline for 3DGS reconstruction.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It distinguishes itself by iterating over Gaussians and updating the gradients of the pixels they cover, addressing the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in the per-pixel approach. Choose this to reduce training time by 4–5× while maintaining numerical equivalence.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs. It distinguishes itself from single-machine streaming methods like [TideGS](7350-tidegs.md) by distributing the parameter table across a GPU cluster, enabling training on scenes with billions of primitives. Choose this when single-GPU memory limits are exceeded.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details the specific algorithmic steps, hyperparameters, and data structures required to implement 3DGS training. It serves as the practical guide for realizing the differentiable rasterization core, covering corrected scene extent calculation, half-precision rasterization, and GEMM-based acceleration. Choose this for implementation reference.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . The initial implementation focused on single-GPU efficiency. Subsequent work addressed scalability and training speed, leading to the development of distributed frameworks like Grendel-GS and optimized backward passes. The method is widely used in novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and dynamic scene modeling.
