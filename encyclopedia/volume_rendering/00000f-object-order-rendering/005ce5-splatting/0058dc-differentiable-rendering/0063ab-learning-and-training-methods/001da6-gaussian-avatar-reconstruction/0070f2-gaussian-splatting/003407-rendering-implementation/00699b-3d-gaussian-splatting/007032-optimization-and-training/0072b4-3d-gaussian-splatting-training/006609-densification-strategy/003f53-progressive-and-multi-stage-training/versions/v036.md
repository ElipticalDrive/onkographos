# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Curriculum Training, Coarse-to-Fine Training

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to a family of optimization strategies that partition the training timeline of 3D Gaussian Splatting into discrete phases, each with distinct objectives, active parameters, or supervision signals. It solves the problem of unstable convergence, local minima, and poor high-frequency detail reconstruction inherent in single-stage, end-to-end optimization [S1, 7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training, these methods decouple initialization from refinement or geometry from appearance, allowing the representation to adapt to scene complexity or pose uncertainty over time [S1, 15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md). The core mechanism involves partitioning the optimization process into sequential stages $S_1, \dots, S_k$. Each stage modifies:
1.  **Trainable Parameters:** Freezing or unfreezing specific branches (e.g., pose estimator vs. Gaussian decoder) or attribute groups (geometry vs. color) [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md].
2.  **Loss Functions:** Activating or deactivating specific loss terms (e.g., pose loss, depth consistency, structural regularization) [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md, 17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md].
3.  **Supervision Signals:** Changing the sampling strategy (e.g., slice vs. voxel, sparse vs. dense views) or input resolution [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md, 15162-free-view-training-fvt.md].

Densification and pruning operations, as defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md), are typically applied within or between stages to manage the primitive count and spatial distribution [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 9138-fine-tuning.md].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
A multi-stage approach that refines the Gaussian point cloud progressively rather than in a single pass. It solves the problem of suboptimal convergence and poor high-frequency detail reconstruction. It sets itself apart by decoupling initialization from refinement, allowing the representation to adapt to scene complexity over time.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the optimization and densification for **MeshGS**, a variant aligning primitives with an explicit mesh. It solves geometric inconsistency by partitioning Gaussians into **tightly-bound** (constrained to mesh) and **loosely-bound** (free-floating) sets. It differs from standard 3DGS by introducing mesh-aware losses (projection, normal consistency) and mesh-aligned densification for tightly-bound primitives, while retaining gradient-based densification for loosely-bound ones.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines an initial Gaussian representation (often from a generative model or sparse input) to achieve high-fidelity reconstruction. It addresses the limitation of static initialization by treating the scene as a single-scene optimization problem. It sets itself apart from [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) by focusing on refining a specific initialized state rather than a curriculum of increasing complexity, and from [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md) by typically operating in image space with full-resolution supervision from the outset.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Optimizes 3D Gaussians for volumetric data by combining sparse voxel sampling with structured slice-based sampling in a curriculum. It solves unstable global approximation and lack of structural continuity by progressively introducing planar constraints and global spatial guidance. It sets itself apart from pure voxel supervision by explicitly aligning training sampling with the shear-warp rendering process, improving local anatomical detail reconstruction.

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
Addresses the instability of jointly optimizing camera poses and 3D Gaussian attributes. It decouples learning into sequential phases: establishing a geometric prior with ground-truth poses, refining the scene representation, and finally enabling end-to-end pose-free inference. This prevents pose errors from corrupting the Gaussian density distribution during early optimization. It differs from pure curriculum learning by explicitly freezing and unfreezing specific model branches (pose estimator vs. Gaussian decoder) rather than just changing input resolution.

### [Free-View Training (FVT)](15162-free-view-training-fvt.md)
Disentangles the performance of generalizable 3DGS from the specific number of input views. It achieves this by adding geometrical constraints on Gaussian localization through supervision on image renderings from broader view interpolations. Unlike methods optimizing for a fixed set of input views, FVT enables robust view synthesis across wider view ranges regardless of input view count.

### [Training Loop with Mixed Losses and Post-Processing](17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md)
Defines the iterative optimization step combining photometric reconstruction, depth consistency, and regularization terms. It distinguishes itself by explicitly handling mixed supervision signals (RGB and depth) and applying post-processing strategies like bilateral grid slicing and opacity/scale regularization within the same backward pass, rather than relying solely on structural densification or curriculum learning.

### [Training Loop with Structure-Aware Densification](22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md)
Accelerates training by integrating a structure-aware densification strategy that estimates local frequency requirements to guide primitive placement. It solves the slow convergence of standard reactive densification by ensuring Gaussians are split early and precisely where high-frequency details exist, rather than relying on opacity thresholds alone. Unlike sibling methods that use uniform or opacity-based splitting, this approach precomputes multi-scale structure tensors and samples frequency violations within Gaussian footprints, achieving significant speedups (e.g., 18x faster than standard 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360) while maintaining competitive perceptual quality.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
- **Definition:** Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].
- **Context:** Used in methods like GaussianPro to manage the growth of the point cloud during training [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
- **Step:** Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].
- **Context:** Applied in adaptive spatio-temporal optimization for scenes with oscillatory motion [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
- **Definition:** A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
- **Step:**
    1.  Construct RGB point clouds for each input view.
    2.  Project the constructed point clouds to the target view using the differentiable point cloud renderer.
    3.  Aggregate all point clouds from the various input views to render RGB images at the target view.
    4.  Optimize the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and the ground truth target images.
    5.  Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) [S3].
- **Parameter:** Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
- **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

### Two-Stage Triplane and MLP Training
- **Step:**
    1.  **Stage 1:** Jointly train triplanes and MLP on a subset of 500 randomly selected shapes. Use a batch size of 1 object per iteration and 500k occupancy values points per object. Train for 200 epochs with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S4].
    2.  **Stage 2:** Freeze the shared MLP. Train triplane features for each object individually using a batch size of 200k occupancy values per object. Train for 30 epochs with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S4].
- **Parameter:** Triplane feature dimension: $128 \times 128 \times 32 \times 3$ [S4]. Triplane initialization: values drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.1 [S4]. MLP architecture: Fourier feature mapping layer (scale factor 1) followed by 3 fully connected layers of dimension 128, each with ReLU activation [S4].
- **Number:** Stage 1 training time: approximately 1 day on a single RTX 2080ti [S4]. Stage 2 training time: 10 minutes per shape on an RTX 2080ti [S4].

### Continual Model Refinement
- **Step:** Augment the initial model with larger sets of closer-up images (64 or 512 images) to simulate prolonged user interaction. Optimize the model for up to 4000 iterations, including 400 iterations of densification to capture finer details [S5].
- **Parameter:** Final Gaussian count grows to between 20k and 50k [S5].
- **Pitfall:** Improvement becomes marginal after 2000 iterations [S5]. Using foveated and subsequently undistorted views for training decreases quality metrics by 5-10% depending on the scene [S5].
- **Complexity:** Training proceeds at over 500 iterations per second [S5].
- **Comparison:** Fixed foveation center and variable foveation points yield less than 1% quality difference [S5].

### Projection-Based Frequency Violation Metric
- **Definition:** A densification trigger applying a three-channel anisotropic criterion where each Gaussian axis is evaluated against its own directional frequency content [S6].
- **Number:** On Mip-NeRF360, $\eta^{(proj)}$ achieves PSNR 27.20 and LPIPS 0.203, compared to the scalar metric $\eta$ (PSNR 27.25, LPIPS 0.197) [S6].
- **Comparison:** Theoretically more precise than the scalar wavelength metric, but yields worse perceptual quality (LPIPS) in practice because it relies on accurate axis-direction alignment that is not yet optimized during training [S6].

## Background
Progressive and multi-stage training strategies have emerged as essential components of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization to address the non-convex nature of the rendering loss landscape. Early implementations of 3DGS [Parent] relied on a single-stage optimization with periodic densification, which often struggled with convergence in complex scenes or when initializing from sparse data. The need for decoupled optimization phases became apparent in tasks involving pose estimation [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md], volumetric data [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md], and generalizable view synthesis [15162-free-view-training-fvt.md]. These methods build on the foundational densification mechanics defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) but introduce temporal or structural segmentation to the training loop to improve stability and final reconstruction quality.
