# 4D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 4D Gaussian Representation, 4D splats, 4DGS, 4DGaussians, Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splattting, Dynamic Scene Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to model dynamic scenes by treating space and time as a unified four-dimensional continuum, allowing each Gaussian primitive to move, rotate, scale, and change appearance over time [S8, S9, unofficial]. Unlike methods that treat space and time independently or rely on rigid bounding boxes, 4DGS uses a coherent 4D Gaussian model to capture underlying scene motion and appearance changes more effectively, enabling real-time rendering of arbitrary time slices from a single optimized representation [S9, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A sequence of images (video) capturing a dynamic scene, optionally with depth/LiDAR for urban scenes. Camera poses and intrinsics for each frame [G].
- **Output:** A set of 4D Gaussian primitives parameterized by mean, covariance, opacity, and 4D spherical harmonics coefficients, capable of rendering novel views at any time $t$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
Each 4D Gaussian $i$ is defined by the following parameters:
- **Mean:** $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i = (\mu_{x}, \mu_{y}, \mu_{z}, \mu_{t}) \in \mathbb{R}^4$ [S12, unofficial].
- **Covariance:** $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$, parameterized via rotation $\mathbf{R}$ and scaling $\mathbf{S}$ such that $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{R}^T$ [S12, unofficial].
    - **Scaling:** $\mathbf{S} = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z, s_t)$ [S12, unofficial].
    - **Rotation:** $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{L}(q_l) \mathbf{R}(q_r)$, constructed from two unit quaternions $q_l = (a, b, c, d)$ and $q_r = (p, q, r, s)$ representing left and right isoclinic rotations [S12, unofficial].
- **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$ [S12, unofficial].
- **Color:** Represented by 4D Spherindrical Harmonics (4DSH) coefficients. The basis function is $Z_{nl}^m(t, \theta, \phi) = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}t\right) Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)$, where $Y_l^m$ are standard 3D spherical harmonics, $n$ is the Fourier order, and $l, m$ are SH degree and order [S12, unofficial].

**Rendering Steps:**
1. **Conditional Gaussian Derivation:** For a target time $t$, compute the conditional 3D Gaussian mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{3D|t}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{3D|t}$ from the 4D parameters using multivariate Gaussian conditioning [S6, S12, unofficial]:
   $$
   \boldsymbol{\mu}_{3D|t} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:3} + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,4} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,4}^{-1} (t - \mu_4)
   $$
   $$
   \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{3D|t} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,1:3} - \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,4} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,4}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,1:3}
   $$
   where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1:3}$ is the spatial mean, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:3,4}$ is the cross-covariance between spatial and temporal dimensions, and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{4,4}$ is the temporal variance [S6, S12, unofficial].

2. **Marginal Probability:** Compute the marginal probability of the Gaussian existing at time $t$:
   $$
   p_i(t) = \mathcal{N}(t; \mu_{4,i}, \Sigma_{4,4,i})
   $$
   This marginal probability weights the Gaussian's contribution at time $t$ [S12, unofficial].

3. **Projection:** Project the conditional 3D Gaussian onto the image plane to obtain the 2D mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2D|t}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2D|t}$ using standard perspective projection and Jacobian approximation as in 3DGS [G].

4. **Rasterization:** Render the frame at time $t$ by accumulating colors from all Gaussians sorted by depth. The contribution of each Gaussian is weighted by its marginal temporal probability $p_i(t)$ [S12, unofficial]:
   $$
   \mathcal{I}(u, v, t) = \sum_{i=1}^N p_i(t) p_i(u, v|t) \alpha_i c_i(\mathbf{d}, t) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(t) p_j(u, v|t) \alpha_j)
   $$
   where $p_i(u, v|t)$ is the conditional 2D Gaussian probability density, $\alpha_i$ is opacity, and $c_i(\mathbf{d}, t)$ is the view-dependent color evaluated using 4DSH at direction $\mathbf{d}$ and time $t$ [S12, unofficial].

**Training:**
- **Initialization:** For Plenoptic Video datasets, initialize Gaussians using COLMAP point clouds from the first frame, with timestamps uniformly distributed across the scene duration. For D-NeRF, initialize 100,000 random points in $[-1.2, 1.2]^3$ with mean time set to the scene duration [S12, unofficial].
- **Optimization:** End-to-end optimization using rendering loss (L1 + D-SSIM). Use batch sampling in time to prevent temporal flickering [S12, unofficial].
- **Densification:** Incorporate average gradients of $\mu_t$ as a density control indicator. Use joint spatial and temporal position sampling during splitting in over-reconstructed regions [S12, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

import math
import numpy as np

def project_3d_to_2d(mu_3d, sigma_3d, K, cam_R, cam_t):
    # Standard perspective projection with Jacobian approximation [G]
    T = K @ np.hstack([cam_R, cam_t])
    mu_2d = T[:2, :] @ np.append(mu_3d, 1.0)
    J = K[:2, :2] @ cam_R[:2, :]
    sigma_2d = J @ sigma_3d @ J.T
    return mu_2d, sigma_2d

def evaluate_4dsh(sh_coeffs, view_dir, time_t, T_scene):
    # 4D Spherindrical Harmonics evaluation [S12, unofficial]
    color = np.zeros(3)
    for n, l, m, coeff in sh_coeffs:
        sh_val = np.cos(2 * math.pi * n * time_t / T_scene) * spherical_harmonic(l, m, view_dir)
        color += coeff * sh_val
    return color

def render_4dgs(gaussians, K, W, H, cam_R, cam_t, time_t, T_scene):
    projected = []
    for g in gaussians:
        mu_4d = g.mu
        sigma_4d = g.sigma
        mu_s, mu_t = mu_4d[:3], mu_4d[3]
        sigma_ss, sigma_st, sigma_tt = sigma_4d[:3, :3], sigma_4d[:3, 3], sigma_4d[3, 3]
        
        delta_t = time_t - mu_t
        mu_3d_cond = mu_s + sigma_st * (1.0 / sigma_tt) * delta_t
        sigma_3d_cond = sigma_ss - sigma_st * (1.0 / sigma_tt) * sigma_st.T
        
        p_t = (1.0 / math.sqrt(2 * math.pi * sigma_tt)) * math.exp(-0.5 * (delta_t ** 2) / sigma_tt)
        
        mu_2d, sigma_2d = project_3d_to_2d(mu_3d_cond, sigma_3d_cond, K, cam_R, cam_t)
        projected.append({'mu_2d': mu_2d, 'sigma_2d': sigma_2d, 'p_t': p_t, 'alpha': g.alpha, 'sh': g.sh_coeffs, 'z': mu_3d_cond[2]})
    
    projected.sort(key=lambda g: g['z'])
    image = np.zeros((H, W, 3))
    coverage = np.zeros((H, W))
    
    for g in projected:
        std_x, std_y = np.sqrt(np.diag(g['sigma_2d']))
        min_x, max_x = max(0, int(g['mu_2d'][0] - 3*std_x)), min(W-1, int(g['mu_2d'][0] + 3*std_x))
        min_y, max_y = max(0, int(g['mu_2d'][1] - 3*std_y)), min(H-1, int(g['mu_2d'][1] + 3*std_y))
        
        inv_sigma_2d = np.linalg.inv(g['sigma_2d'])
        for y in range(min_y, max_y + 1):
            for x in range(min_x, max_x + 1):
                dist = np.array([x - g['mu_2d'][0], y - g['mu_2d'][1]])
                p_2d = math.exp(-0.5 * dist @ inv_sigma_2d @ dist)

                c = evaluate_4dsh(g['sh'], view_dir, time_t, T_scene)
                alpha = g['p_t'] * p_2d * g['alpha']
                image[y, x] += alpha * c * (1.0 - coverage[y, x])
                coverage[y, x] += alpha

## Subtopics

### [Spacetime Gaussians (STG)](5997-spacetime-gaussians-stg.md)
Spacetime Gaussians (STG) is a 4D Gaussian Splatting method that models dynamic scenes using native 4D primitives, enabling real-time rendering of any arbitrary time slice without per-frame optimization. It distinguishes itself from MLP-based deformation approaches like DGS by explicitly parameterizing temporal opacity envelopes and polynomial trajectories for each Gaussian, allowing for smooth interpolation of geometry and appearance between discrete keyframes.

### [Initial Time Scaling](6046-initial-time-scaling.md)
Initial Time Scaling adjusts the temporal variance of initialized 4D Gaussians to match the scene duration, preventing excessive Gaussian proliferation over long videos. It leverages the anisotropic characteristic of Gaussians to maintain tractable Gaussian counts as video length increases, distinguishing it from methods that rely on rigid 3D bounding boxes or dynamic object segmentations.

### [4D-Rotor Gaussian Splatting](6146-4d-rotor-gaussian-splatting.md)
4D-Rotor Gaussian Splatting is a method for modeling dynamic scenes using 4D rotor-based primitives defined via Clifford algebra. It sets itself apart from siblings like Spacetime Gaussians (STG) by explicitly modeling temporal evolution as a rotation component in spacetime, allowing for rigid body motion representation, whereas STG relies on polynomial trajectories and temporal opacity envelopes.

### Gaussian-splat timelapses
A time-lapse rendering architecture where each timepoint's splats are fitted independently and stored contiguously, with time acting as a hard ordering barrier to prevent LOD rung blending across timepoints [S13, unofficial]. It fits Gaussian splats per timepoint (e.g., 256K per frame) and streams timepoints sequentially, fetching chunks as the timeline advances [S13, unofficial].

### Urban Scene Adaptation for 4DGS
Training and rendering modifications for sparse, wide-baseline outdoor scenes to prevent overfitting and model infinite-distance backgrounds [S12, unofficial]. It integrates sparse depth supervision from LiDAR point clouds using an inverse L1 loss, adopts a cube map for the sky, and deactivates the temporal coefficient of 4DSH to reduce overfitting [S12, unofficial].

### No-4DRot Variant
A variant that disables 4D rotation updates, resulting in less temporal variance and impaired motion fitting [S12, unofficial]. Disabling 4D rotation reduces temporal variance, impairing motion fitting capacity and causing flicker/blur in rendered videos [S12, unofficial].

### [Real-time 4DGS](7335-real-time-4dgs.md)
A rendering method that achieves interactive frame rates by fusing a static background with a small, fast-updating set of dynamic foreground Gaussians. It distinguishes itself from general 4D Gaussian Splatting by treating the time dimension equally with spatial dimensions, utilizing a 4D scaling matrix and isotropic rotations to represent 4D rotation, and conditioning the marginal distribution on time.

### RetimeGS
A method that achieves continuous-time 4D reconstruction by modeling the trajectories with a neural ODE solver, allowing frame rates to be arbitrarily adjusted at test time [S14, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Rendering is $O(N)$ per pixel, similar to 3DGS, but requires computing conditional Gaussians and marginal probabilities for each Gaussian at the target time $t$ [S6, unofficial]. Training is end-to-end [S12, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Stores 4D parameters per Gaussian. The number of Gaussians scales with scene complexity but remains stable over video length due to the anisotropic temporal support [S12, unofficial].
- **Performance:** On the D-NeRF dataset (800x800), 4DGS achieves PSNR 31.79, SSIM 0.960, and LPIPS 0.051, with training time reduced to 6m22s (2.8x speedup) and VRAM usage of 1.2GiB compared to the baseline [S6, unofficial]. On the Plenoptic Video dataset, it achieves PSNR 32.01 and 114 FPS [S12, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Real-time rendering of dynamic scenes, coherent spatiotemporal representation, no need for per-frame optimization at test time [S12, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Training is more complex than 3DGS; sensitive to temporal sampling during training to avoid flickering [S12, unofficial].

## Background
4D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Wu et al. in CVPR'24 [S7, unofficial]. It builds on the success of 3D Gaussian Splatting by extending the primitive representation to four dimensions to handle dynamic content [S6, unofficial]. The method addresses the limitations of treating space and time independently, which struggles to capture underlying scene motion [S12, unofficial]. It has been adapted for various applications including urban scenes, timelapses, and real-time rendering [S12, S13, S14, unofficial].
