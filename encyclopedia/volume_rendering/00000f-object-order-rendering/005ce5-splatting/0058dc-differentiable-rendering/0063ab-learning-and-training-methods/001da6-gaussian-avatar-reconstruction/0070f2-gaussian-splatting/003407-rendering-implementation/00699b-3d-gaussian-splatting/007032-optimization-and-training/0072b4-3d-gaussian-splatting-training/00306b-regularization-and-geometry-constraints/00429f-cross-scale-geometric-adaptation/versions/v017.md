# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity by adapting multi-scale voxel geometry [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on frequency domain comparisons  or explicit shape constraints [S1], this method operates in the depth/geometric consistency domain, providing a stronger and more robust constraint than standard depth priors ($L_d$) and significantly reducing floaters [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics ($K_i, K_j$), camera poses (rotation $R$, translation $t$), multi-scale depth maps ($D_l$) obtained from volume rendering, and RGB maps ($C$).
- **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss ($L_{geo}$) and pseudo-ground truth depth ($D'$).

**Data Structures:**
- **Multi-scale Voxels:** The scene is represented by voxels at multiple resolutions (Low, Mid, High). Each scale $l$ produces a depth map $D_l$ and an RGB map $C_l$ (though the loss primarily uses the RGB map of the source frame $i$ and the warped RGB map of frame $j$) [S1].
- **Ray Sampling:** Rays $r_{train}$ are sampled from the training view [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Scale Selection Strategy:** Early in training, low-resolution voxels are used as pseudo-ground truth to guide coarse geometry learning. As training advances, medium- and high-resolution voxels gradually refine details [S1].
2. **3D Point Reconstruction (Frame $i$):** For a 2D pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$ and its homogeneous augmentation $\tilde{p}_i$, compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$ ($x_{li}$) using the depth at scale $l$ ($D_{il}(p_i)$) and camera intrinsics $K_i$:
   $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
   Alternatively, the 3D point $x_l$ in world coordinates can be determined directly from the ray defined by the starting point $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
   $$ x_l = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
   [S1]
3. **World Coordinate Transformation:** Transform the 3D point $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
   $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
   [S1]
4. **Reprojection to Frame $j$:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinates of frame $j$ ($x_{li \to j}$) using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
   $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
   [S1]
5. **2D Projection:** Project the 3D point $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$ ($\tilde{p}_{li \to j}$) using the projection function $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ and camera intrinsics $K_j$:
   $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
   [S1]
6. **Reprojection Error Calculation:** Compute the reprojection error $e_l(p_i)$ for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel $C_i(p_i)$ and the warped pixel $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$:
   $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
   [S1]
7. **Pseudo-Ground Truth Selection:** For each ray, identify the scale $l$ that minimizes the reprojection error. This scale's depth is selected as the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$:
   $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
   [S1]
8. **Loss Computation:** Compute the geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth $D_l$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'$ for all training rays:
   $$ L_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
   [S1]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geometric_adaptation_loss(
    rays, 
    frames, 
    intrinsics, 
    poses, 
    depth_maps, 
    rgb_maps,
    num_scales
):
    """
    Computes the Lgeo loss for cross-scale geometric adaptation.
    
    Args:
        rays: List of rays for training views. Each ray has origin o and direction v.
        frames: List of frame indices (i, j) for reprojection pairs.
        intrinsics: Dictionary of camera intrinsics K for each frame.
        poses: Dictionary of camera poses (R, t) for each frame.
        depth_maps: Dictionary mapping (frame, scale) to depth map D_l.
        rgb_maps: Dictionary mapping frame to RGB map C.
        num_scales: Total number of voxel scales (L).
        
    Returns:
        L_geo: Scalar loss value.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        # Ray parameters
        o_i = ray.origin
        v_i = ray.direction
        
        # Iterate over scales to find the one with minimal reprojection error
        min_error = float('inf')
        best_depth = None
        best_scale_idx = -1
        
        for l in range(num_scales):
            # 1. Get depth at scale l for the current ray in frame i
            # Note: In implementation, depth_maps[frame][scale] is a 2D array.
            # We need to map ray to pixel coordinate p_i first.
            # Assuming ray corresponds to pixel p_i in frame i.
            p_i = ray.pixel_coordinate # Derived from ray and intrinsics
            
            # Get depth D_il(p_i)
            D_il = depth_maps[ray.frame_i][l][p_i.y, p_i.x]
            
            # 2. Compute 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
            # x_li = D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_homogeneous
            p_i_homogeneous = np.array([p_i.x, p_i.y, 1.0])
            K_inv_i = np.linalg.inv(intrinsics[ray.frame_i])
            x_li = D_il * (K_inv_i @ p_i_homogeneous)
            
            # 3. Transform to world coordinates
            # x_l = R_i * x_li + t_i
            R_i = poses[ray.frame_i].R
            t_i = poses[ray.frame_i].t
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            # 4. Transform to camera coordinates of frame j
            # x_li_to_j = R_j^T * (x_l - t_j)
            R_j = poses[ray.frame_j].R
            t_j = poses[ray.frame_j].t
            x_li_to_j = R_j.T @ (x_l - t_j)
            
            # 5. Project to 2D pixel coordinate in frame j
            # p_li_to_j = pi(K_j * x_li_to_j)
            K_j = intrinsics[ray.frame_j]
            p_li_to_j_homogeneous = K_j @ x_li_to_j
            p_li_to_j = np.array([
                p_li_to_j_homogeneous[0] / p_li_to_j_homogeneous[2],
                p_li_to_j_homogeneous[1] / p_li_to_j_homogeneous[2]
            ])
            
            # 6. Compute reprojection error
            # e_l = C_i(p_i) - C_j(p_li_to_j)
            # Check if p_li_to_j is within bounds
            if is_within_bounds(p_li_to_j, rgb_maps[ray.frame_j].shape):
                C_i = rgb_maps[ray.frame_i][p_i.y, p_i.x]
                C_j = rgb_maps[ray.frame_j][p_li_to_j.y, p_li_to_j.x]
                error = np.linalg.norm(C_i - C_j)
            else:
                error = float('inf')
            
            # 7. Track minimum error
            if error < min_error:
                min_error = error
                best_depth = D_il
                best_scale_idx = l
        
        # 8. Compute loss for this ray using the best depth
        # L_geo = sum_l (D_l - D')^2
        # Note: The source formula sums over scales, but the selection step picks one D'.
        # The source states: "pseudo-GT depth of the scale with the minimum reprojection error is obtained... Lgeo = sum (D_l - D')^2"
        # This implies D' is constant for the ray, and we compare all D_l to D'.
        # However, typically only the selected scale's depth is used for supervision, 
        # or the loss is applied to the selected scale. 
        # Re-reading [S1]: "Lgeo(r_train) = sum_{l=0}^L || D_hat_l(r_train) - D'(r_train) ||^2"
        # This suggests a loss for ALL scales against the BEST pseudo-GT.
        
        # For implementation, we need D_hat_l for all scales.
        # Assuming we have access to D_hat_l for the current ray at all scales.
        ray_loss = 0.0
        for l in range(num_scales):
            D_hat_l = depth_maps[ray.frame_i][l][p_i.y, p_i.x] # Get depth for scale l
            ray_loss += (D_hat_l - best_depth)**2
            
        total_loss += ray_loss
        
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters:**
- **Number of Scales ($L$):** Not given in the sources.
- **Training Schedule:** Low-resolution voxels are prioritized early in training, with mid- and high-resolution voxels refining details as training advances [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Boundary Conditions:** If the reprojected pixel $\tilde{p}_{li \to j}$ falls outside the image bounds of frame $j$, the reprojection error is effectively infinite, and that scale is not selected [S1].
- **Sparse Views:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views (e.g., 360° dataset), generalization may be challenging [S1].
- **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry. Novel view regularizations add guidance, reducing overfitting [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** On the FrugalNeRF framework, $L_{geo}$ alone achieves lower final depth error than the depth prior $L_d$ alone, which suffers from higher error later in training due to scale mismatches [S1]. Combining $L_{geo}$ and $L_d$ yields the best results [S1].
- **Strengths:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Provides a stronger and more robust constraint than the depth prior ($L_d$) [S1]. Eliminates the need for complex scheduling and ground truth pose sampling [S1].
- **Limitations:** Few-shot NeRF relies on accurate camera poses. Performance may decline on all metrics without the geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in the FrugalNeRF framework, which synthesizes novel views from extremely few inputs [S1]. It was introduced to accelerate and regularize training without using learned priors [S1]. The method leverages weight-sharing voxels across scales to capture varying scene frequencies and uses reprojection errors to guide multi-scale geometry [S1]. It is compared against methods like GeCoNeRF, which uses warped features as pseudo labels and is sensitive to geometric noise, and ReVoRF, which uses unreliable regions for pseudo-novel view synthesis [S1].
