# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization decomposes the training of 3D Gaussian representations into sequential phases to resolve conflicting objectives, such as stability versus compression or geometry versus material properties. It separates the problem into a coarse or codec-free stage that establishes a stable baseline representation, followed by a fine-grained or codec-aware stage that refines specific attributes or optimizes rate-distortion performance [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. This approach prevents the instability caused by simultaneously learning complex latent codes or decoupled physical properties [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The method adapts its specific parameters and losses based on the application domain (e.g., compression, inverse rendering, or CT reconstruction), but consistently follows a sequential decomposition strategy.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images/poses), target view images (for supervision), and optionally depth maps or SfM points.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ with optimized parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/material) and, in compression contexts, a compressed bitstream or latent code.

### Data Structures
The core data structure is the set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ typically contains:
*   **Position:** $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   **Scale:** $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (or diagonal covariance)
*   **Rotation:** $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$
*   **Appearance:** $\mathbf{c}_i$ (SH coefficients or RGB)
*   **Material/Lighting:** $\mathbf{m}_i$ (albedo, roughness, etc.) or $\mathbf{e}_i$ (environment map) [S2].

In feed-forward variants (CodecSplat), an intermediate feature tensor $\mathbf{F}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C_F \times H \times W}$ is generated from multi-view features before Gaussian prediction [S3, unofficial].

### Steps

#### Stage 1: Baseline Establishment
The first stage focuses on stable geometry and coarse appearance, often using simplified constraints or lower resolution.

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians from SfM points or a coarse grid. In feed-forward models, extract multi-view features $\mathbf{F}_{mv}$ and encoder-side depth $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$ [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Resolution Selection (Optional):** In volumetric approaches, sample a low-resolution grid (e.g., $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume) to place Gaussians in approximately correct positions rapidly [S1].
3.  **Forward Pass:**
    *   *Standard/CT:* Render target view $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t$ from $\mathcal{G}$ using differentiable splatting/projector [S1].
    *   *Feed-forward (CodecSplat):* Predict codec-free Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ from refined feature $\mathbf{F}_c$ and $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$. Render $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}_{cf}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   *Inverse Rendering (GS-IR):* Optimize geometric parameters $\mathbf{T} = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ and appearance $\mathbf{A}$ [S2].
4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   *General/CT:* Minimize projection discrepancy:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage1} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} || \hat{\mathbf{p}}[\mathcal{V}(\cdot|\{\theta_i\}_{i=1}^N)] - \mathbf{p} ||_2^2 $$
        where $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ is projected data and $\mathbf{p}$ is actual measurement [S1].
    *   *CodecSplat:*
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage1} = \mathcal{D}_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf}, \mathbf{Y}_t) $$
        where $\mathcal{D}_{render}$ is the rendering reconstruction loss (e.g., L1/SSIM) [S3, unofficial].
    *   *GS-IR:*
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{init} = \mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_n $$
        where $\mathcal{L}_c$ is color reconstruction loss and $\mathcal{L}_n$ is normal loss (including total variation $\lambda_{n-TV} \text{TV}_{normal}$) [S2].
5.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 30,000 for GS-IR) [S2].

#### Stage 2: Refinement and Specialization
The second stage refines the representation, often enabling complex features like codecs, materials, or full-resolution details.

1.  **Checkpoint Loading:** Initialize from Stage 1 weights. Freeze parameters optimized in Stage 1 (e.g., geometry $\mathbf{T}$ and appearance $\mathbf{A}$ in GS-IR) [S2].
2.  **Feature/Codec Activation:**
    *   *CodecSplat:* Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner. The latent code is quantized/entropy-coded [S3, unofficial].
    *   *Volumetric:* Switch to sampling over all voxels of the original volume for fine-grained adjustments [S1].
3.  **Forward Pass:**
    *   *CodecSplat:* Decode Gaussian set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ from the latent code. Render $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t = \text{Render}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   *GS-IR:* Render with optimized material $\mathbf{M}$ and lighting $\mathbf{E}$ [S2].
4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   *CodecSplat:*
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ \mathcal{D}_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t, \mathbf{Y}_t) + \gamma \mathcal{D}_{codec-depth} + \beta \mathcal{R} \right] $$
        where $\mathcal{R}$ is estimated rate, $\gamma$ weights depth supervision, and $\beta$ controls rate penalty [S3, unofficial].
        The codec-side depth loss uses normalized inverse depth:
        $$ \mathcal{D}_{codec-depth} = || \mathbf{S} - \mathbf{S}_{enc} ||_1 $$
        $$ \mathbf{S}_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{\mathbf{D}_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        where $n_i, f_i$ are near/far bounds [S3, unofficial].
    *   *GS-IR:*
        $$ \mathcal{L}_d = \mathcal{L}_{shade} + \lambda_M \text{TV}_{mat} + \lambda_E \text{TV}_{light} $$
        where $\mathcal{L}_{shade}$ is shading loss, $\text{TV}_{mat}$ is material total variation, and $\text{TV}_{light}$ is light total variation [S2].
5.  **Optimization:** Optimize the remaining parameters (e.g., material $\mathbf{M}$, codec modules) for a fixed duration (e.g., 10,000 iterations for GS-IR) [S2].

### Parameters
*   **Learning Rate:** Typically $10^{-4}$ with Adam/AdamW [S3, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** Stage 1: ~30,000; Stage 2: ~10,000 (GS-IR specific) [S2].
*   **Rate Parameter ($\lambda$):** Sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training for CodecSplat; fixed at test time [S3, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights:**
    *   $\lambda_{n-TV}$ (Normal TV): 5.0 [S2].
    *   $\lambda_M$ (Material TV): 1.0 [S2].
    *   $\lambda_E$ (Light TV): 0.01 [S2].
    *   $\gamma$ (Codec Depth): 0.1 [S3, unofficial].
    *   $\beta$ (Rate Penalty): $10^{-4}$ (DL3DV), $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (RE10K) [S3, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with quantized bottlenecks is unstable; the two-stage separation is required to prevent gradient conflicts between generation and coding [S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Normalized inverse depth calculation requires careful handling of near/far plane bounds ($n_i, f_i$) to avoid division by zero or numerical instability [S3, unofficial].
*   **Resolution Mismatch:** In volumetric approaches, failing to switch from low-res to full-res sampling in Stage 2 results in loss of fine details [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Higher than single-stage training due to sequential optimization phases. Stage 1 is often faster due to low-resolution sampling or simplified losses [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to standard 3DGS, but feed-forward variants (CodecSplat) require memory for the encoder/decoder backbone and latent buffers [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves higher quality reconstructions and better rate-distortion trade-offs than single-stage methods. CodecSplat achieves KiB-level bitstreams with competitive PSNR [S3, unofficial]. GS-IR enables accurate inverse rendering with decoupled material optimization [S2].
*   **Hardware:** Demonstrated on single V100 (GS-IR) and multiple RTX 5880s (CodecSplat) [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and loss weights. Not suitable for real-time training; intended for offline reconstruction or pre-training feed-forward models.

## Background
Two-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address the instability of joint optimization problems. CodecSplat introduced the codec-free/codec-aware split to enable ultra-compact latent coding [S3, unofficial]. GS-IR separated geometry/appearance from material/lighting to facilitate inverse rendering [S2]. Volumetric approaches used low-to-high resolution stages for efficient CT reconstruction [S1].
