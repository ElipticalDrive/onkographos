# Scene Representation and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometry-aware Gaussian Optimization, Surface-constrained 3DGS

## Overview
This family of methods enhances the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation by integrating explicit geometric constraints, such as mesh topology or depth consistency, into the optimization process. While standard 3DGS relies on soft volumetric primitives that may lack precise surface alignment, these methods enforce hard or soft boundaries to ensure Gaussians align with physical surfaces, enabling better mesh extraction, depth estimation, and structural fidelity. They differ from sibling methods like [Color and Appearance Modeling](14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md) by focusing on spatial geometry rather than radiance, and from [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) by performing geometry-aware optimization during training rather than post-processing.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of $N$ 3D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $i$ is defined by learnable parameters describing its geometry, appearance, and position.

**Data Structure:**
Each Gaussian $g_i$ is represented by the following attributes [S1]:
*   **Position ($\mu_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing the centroid in world space.
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):** A unit quaternion $\mathbb{R}^4$ representing orientation.
*   **Scale ($s_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing anisotropic scaling factors along principal axes.
*   **Opacity ($\omega_i$):** A scalar $\mathbb{R} \in [0, 1]$ representing maximum opacity.
*   **Color ($c_i$):** Represented by Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients (typically degree 3, 48 coefficients for RGB) for view-dependent appearance.

**Covariance Matrix:**
The spatial spread is defined by a 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$, constructed from scale and rotation:
$$ \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T $$
where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ is the scaling matrix and $R_i$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q_i$ [S1].

**Projection to 2D:**
During rendering, the 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ is projected to the 2D image plane using the Jacobian $J$ of the affine approximation of the projective transformation:
$$ \Sigma' = J \Sigma_i J^T $$
The relevant part for rasterization is the top-left $2 \times 2$ submatrix $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}$ [S1].

**Spatial Response (Kernel Function):**
The contribution of a Gaussian to a pixel at screen coordinates $x$ is determined by:
$$ R(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu')^T (\Sigma'_{2 \times 2})^{-1} (x - \mu')\right) $$
where $\mu'$ is the projected 2D mean [S1].

**Alpha Compositing:**
The final opacity $\alpha$ contributed by a Gaussian is:
$$ \alpha = \omega_i \cdot R(x) $$
The pixel color is computed via alpha blending with primitives sorted back-to-front:
$$ C_{pixel} = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i C_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j < i} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $C_i$ is evaluated using SH coefficients based on view direction [S1].

**Truncation:**
To optimize speed, fragments are discarded if $\alpha < \tau_\alpha$ (typically $1/255$), limiting rendering to a region of approximately $3.33\sigma$ [S1].

**Density Estimation for Mesh Extraction:**
The scene density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is the superposition of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^T \Sigma_i^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)\right) $$
where $\alpha_i$ is opacity, $\mathbf{p}_i$ is the mean, and $\Sigma_i$ is the covariance [S1].

### [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md)
This family of methods converts the volumetric, implicit representation of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into explicit geometric structures, primarily triangular meshes or point clouds, to enable compatibility with standard 3D graphics pipelines, editing tools, and animation systems. Unlike standard 3DGS, which renders via alpha-composited splats, these methods extract a discrete surface or point set that approximates the scene geometry. [SuGaR Mesh Extraction](12580-sugar-mesh-extraction.md) achieves this by regularizing Gaussians to align with the surface during training, while [3D Gaussian Splatting to Point Cloud](14068-3d-gaussian-splatting-to-point-cloud.md) samples points from the Gaussian distribution post-training. ### Model-based depth estimation for volume parameter update
This variant uses rendered depth to update volumetric medium parameters during optimization.
**Step:** During optimization, use Eq. (6) to extract depth values $z$ from the current scene estimate. These depth values are then used to update the medium-parameter estimates ($B_\infty, B_b$) [S1].
**Formula:** Depth extraction from 3DGS: $z = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^M d_i \alpha_i T_i}{\sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i T_i}$. Here $d_i$ is the depth of Gaussian $i$. This is computed at each pixel during rendering [S1].

### Scale Constrained by Mesh Geometry
A constraint to prevent discrepancies between Gaussian Splat scales and the scales of corresponding triangular mesh faces by bounding the relative scale coefficients.
**Definition:** The scale $s$ is parameterized relative to a predefined mesh-derived scale $s_0$ [S2].
**Formula:** $s = s_{rel} \odot s_0$, where $s$ is the final scale, $s_{rel} = (s_{rel,1}, s_{rel,2}) \in [s_{min}, s_{max}] \times [s_{min}, s_{max}]$ is the coefficient vector, $s_0$ is the predefined scale derived from the mesh faces, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S2].
**Parameter:** $s_{min}, s_{max}$: The lower and upper bounds for the relative scale coefficients $s_{rel}$ [S2].

### Normal Estimation via Back-propagation
A method to solve for the normal attribute $n$ for each 3D Gaussian by optimizing it from an initial random vector via back-propagation, perceiving the depth of all Gaussians along a ray as a distribution.
**Definition:** The normal for each pixel is determined by estimating the expectation of the depth distribution along the ray [S3].
**Step:** Compute the weighted sum of depths and normals from all Gaussians along the ray to render $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{N}$, then enforce consistency with pseudo-normals $\tilde{\mathcal{N}}$ derived from rendered depth [S3].
**Formula:** Normal consistency loss: $\mathcal {L}_{n}=\|\mathcal {N}-\tilde {\mathcal {N}}\|_2$ [S3].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation [G] provides high-fidelity rendering but lacks explicit geometric structure, making mesh extraction and surface-aligned optimization challenging. Recent works have introduced geometry constraints to bridge this gap. [S1] demonstrates using depth estimates from Gaussians to update volumetric parameters, while [S2] introduces mesh-based scale constraints to align Gaussian splats with triangular faces for robust surface reconstruction. [S3] proposes optimizing Gaussian normals via back-propagation to improve surface detail and normal consistency. These methods are primarily used in novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and applications requiring explicit mesh outputs from neural radiance fields.
