# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Gradient Parallelism

## Overview
Optimization and Parallelization refers to the family of techniques that accelerate the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and enable its application to large-scale scenes by optimizing the computational efficiency of the differentiable rasterization pipeline and distributing the workload across hardware resources. It solves the bottleneck of high memory consumption and slow convergence inherent in the standard iterative gradient descent approach [6233-optimization-and-parallelization]. This family is distinguished from its siblings by focusing on the *system-level* execution of the optimization loop—such as parallelizing the backward pass, distributing Gaussian primitives across multiple GPUs, or fusing kernel operations—rather than modifying the mathematical definition of the loss or the specific learning rate schedules for individual parameters.

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing gradients through probabilistic masks applied to Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly accounting for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground color accumulation and the background transmittance path, which standard backpropagation ignores. Choose this when training requires explicit mask supervision or occlusion reasoning.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself by adapting the representation density to scene complexity during optimization, rather than using a fixed set of primitives. Choose this for standard scene reconstruction where geometry density is unknown.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It distinguishes itself by iterating over Gaussians to update pixel gradients, thereby avoiding atomic contention bottlenecks associated with per-pixel accumulation. Choose this to reduce training time by 4–5× while maintaining numerical equivalence.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs. It distinguishes itself from single-machine streaming methods (like TideGS) by distributing the parameter table across a GPU cluster, enabling training on scenes with billions of primitives. Choose this when single-device VRAM limits prevent training on large-scale scenes.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
The practical reference for configuring the training loop, including hyperparameters, data structures, and optimization schedules. It distinguishes itself by providing the concrete values (e.g., learning rates, thresholds) required for stability and convergence, rather than proposing new algorithmic variants. Choose this as the baseline configuration for standard 3DGS training.

## Background
The optimization and parallelization of 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as the method gained popularity for novel view synthesis, revealing that the naive implementation of the differentiable rasterizer and gradient descent was computationally expensive and memory-intensive. The original 3DGS paper [6233-optimization-and-parallelization] introduced the basic training pipeline with densification and pruning. Subsequent works identified specific bottlenecks: the atomic contention in the backward pass [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation], the memory limits of single GPUs for large scenes [7349-grendel-gs], and the need for efficient mask handling [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation]. These methods collectively form the ecosystem of efficient 3DGS training, evolving from a single-GPU implementation to distributed, hardware-aware pipelines.
