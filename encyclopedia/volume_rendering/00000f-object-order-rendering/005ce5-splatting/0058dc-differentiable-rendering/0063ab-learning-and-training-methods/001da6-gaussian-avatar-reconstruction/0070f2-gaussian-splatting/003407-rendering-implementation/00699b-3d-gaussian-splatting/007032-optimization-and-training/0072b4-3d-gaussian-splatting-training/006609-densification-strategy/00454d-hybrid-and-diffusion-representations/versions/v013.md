# Hybrid and Diffusion Representations


## Overview
This family encompasses methods that generate or represent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primitives using hybrid feature grids or diffusion models, rather than relying solely on iterative per-scene optimization [S1, S2, S3]. It addresses single-view and text-to-3D generation, multi-view consistency, and sparse volume representation by predicting Gaussian attributes directly from conditioning signals [S1, S2, S3].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family predict Gaussian attributes ($\mu_k, s_k, q_k, \alpha_k, \mathbf{c}_k$) directly from a conditioning signal (image, text, or noise) using a neural network, bypassing per-scene iterative optimization [S1, S2, S3]. The family builds on [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [TriplaneGaussian](7558-triplanegaussian.md)
TriplaneGaussian is a feed-forward method for generating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations from a single input image. It solves the problem of direct 3D generation by employing a hybrid representation that combines a sparse point cloud with a dense tri-plane feature grid. Unlike sibling methods that rely on iterative optimization or multi-view consistency constraints, TriplaneGaussian directly predicts Gaussian attributes via a transformer-based decoder, leveraging the structural priors of tri-planes to ensure geometric consistency. It is distinguished from its sibling [GaussianDiffusion](9190-gaussiandiffusion.md) by its deterministic, non-diffusion-based architecture that explicitly decouples point cloud generation from attribute encoding via tri-planes. Choose this method when deterministic, single-image feed-forward generation is preferred over iterative optimization or diffusion sampling.

### [GaussianDiffusion](9190-gaussiandiffusion.md)
GaussianDiffusion is a method that trains a 3D diffusion model based on a GaussianCube representation to generate 3D Gaussian primitives directly from conditioning signals. It solves the problem of multi-view inconsistency (the "Janus problem") and spurious geometry ("floaters") common in single-view or text-to-3D generation by incorporating structured noise from multiple viewpoints and using variational 3DGS. Unlike iterative optimization methods, it amortizes generation cost over a trained network, enabling near-real-time sampling. It is distinguished from its sibling [TriplaneGaussian](7558-triplanegaussian.md) by its use of a voxelized GaussianCube representation converted via optimal transport, rather than tri-plane grids. Choose this method when multi-view consistency and near-real-time generation from text/image conditions are prioritized over deterministic decoding.

#### Text2Immersion
Text2Immersion is a scene generation method similar to LucidDreamer but with outlier removal and two-stage optimization [S1]. It removes outliers in the point cloud before performing 3DGS optimization in two stages: training coarse 3DGS and refinement [S1]. Choose this method when text-to-3D scene generation requires explicit outlier removal and coarse-to-fine refinement stages.

#### Atomic-Continuous Prior
Atomic-Continuous Prior is a probabilistic prior for VDB volumes that models the data as a mixture of a discrete atomic component (exact zeros for empty space) and a continuous component (real density values) [S2]. It solves the problem where plain Gaussian diffusion smooths away faint wisps and leaks density into empty regions due to the mix of exact zeros and heavy-tailed densities [S2]. Its steps include amplitude companding, which applies a reversible log mapping to rebalance the training signal and boosts the gradient on faint, low-density structures by a factor of $(1 + \lambda\tilde{x})^{-1}$ to prevent thin wisps from being smoothed away [S2]. It also uses atomic mass supervision to enforce sparsity by using a soft occupancy target to recover coarse shape and applying a leakage penalty that pushes density to exactly zero outside the boundary [S2]. For sequences, it uses temporal conditioning by stacking the previous frame onto the input as an extra channel, allowing the network to learn motion without 4D attention [S2]. The prior is defined as $p(x) = \pi_0\delta_0(x) + (1-\pi_0)p_{ac}(x)$, where $\pi_0$ is the probability mass at zero, $\delta_0(x)$ is the Dirac delta function at zero, and $p_{ac}(x)$ is the continuous probability density function for non-zero values [S2]. Choose this method when generating sparse VDB volumes for visual effects where preserving exact empty space and faint density wisps is critical.

#### 3D Mask Representation
3D Mask Representation uses explicit 3D mask grids with trilinear interpolation instead of MLPs for representing the 3D mask, due to stability and optimization speed [S3]. Its parameter grid resolution was tested at $320^3$, $160^3$, $80^3$, $40^3$, with results showing negligible influence on segmentation outcomes (mIoU ~90%) and a default of $320^3$ [S3]. A pitfall is that MLP representation for 3D mask significantly hampers performance due to unstable optimization and intertwined parameters, which complicates the rapid convergence required for cross-view self-prompting [S3]. Choose this method when stable, rapid convergence for cross-view self-prompting segmentation is required, avoiding implicit MLP parameter entanglement.

## Background
The family emerged to address computational bottlenecks and multi-view inconsistencies inherent in iterative 3DGS optimization pipelines [S1, S2, S3]. TriplaneGaussian and GaussianDiffusion introduce feed-forward and diffusion-based generation architectures for single-view and text-to-3D synthesis, respectively [S1]. Atomic-Continuous Prior and 3D Mask Representation extend the representation framework to handle sparse VDB volumes and stable segmentation masks, respectively, targeting visual effects and 3D segmentation workloads [S2, S3].
