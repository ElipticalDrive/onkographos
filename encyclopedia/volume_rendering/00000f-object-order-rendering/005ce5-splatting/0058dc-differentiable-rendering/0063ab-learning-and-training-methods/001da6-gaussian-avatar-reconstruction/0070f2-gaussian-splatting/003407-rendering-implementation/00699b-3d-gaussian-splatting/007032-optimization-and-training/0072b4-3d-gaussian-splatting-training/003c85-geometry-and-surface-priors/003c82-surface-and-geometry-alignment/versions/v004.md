# Surface and Geometry Alignment


## Overview
This family of methods addresses the fundamental limitation of vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS): the production of "floating" volumetric ellipsoids that lack an explicit, watertight geometric boundary . By aligning the discrete Gaussian representation with continuous geometric priors (such as SDFs, depth maps, or explicit meshes), these methods enforce positional and normal consistency to enable accurate mesh extraction, physically-based rendering, and robust initialization . Unlike sibling methods that may rely solely on photometric loss or implicit constraints, this family explicitly integrates geometric supervision into the training or post-processing pipeline to ensure the Gaussians conform to physical surfaces [G].

## Subtopics

### [SuGaR](9191-sugar.md)
A post-hoc method that converts trained 3DGS into a watertight mesh via self-regularization and Poisson reconstruction. It differs from siblings by operating after training rather than during, solving the "floating" ellipsoid problem for downstream mesh applications.

### [Surface-guided Gaussian Re-initialization](9199-surface-guided-gaussian-re-initialization.md)
A training optimization that re-projects Gaussians onto a canonical surface mesh to resolve unbalanced aggregation from densification. Unlike SuGaR or Geo-Splatting, it explicitly repositions Gaussians to a known geometry rather than relying on loss-driven alignment.

### [Geo-Splatting](9201-geo-splatting.md)
A physically-based inverse rendering method that differentiably constructs surface-aligned Gaussians from an explicit mesh to improve normal estimation and light transport. It distinguishes itself by using ray tracing on the mesh for accurate PBR, avoiding the approximated normals of standard 3DGS.

### [GeoCE Loss](17334-geoce-loss.md)
A geometric contrastive embedding method that aligns token similarity with 3D proximity to ensure clustering by physical surface. Unlike hard positional constraints, it uses soft contrastive targets derived from unprojected depths to shape the latent space.

## Background
The concept of aligning Gaussian representations with geometric priors emerged as a solution to the lack of explicit geometry in 3D Gaussian Splatting [G]. While 3DGS excels at novel view synthesis, its volumetric nature makes mesh extraction difficult [G]. Early works like [SuGaR](9191-sugar.md) introduced post-hoc regularization to flatten Gaussians into surfels. Subsequent methods like [Geo-Splatting](9201-geo-splatting.md) integrated explicit meshes for physically-based rendering, while others like [GeoCE Loss](17334-geoce-loss.md) focused on feature-space geometric consistency. The family continues to evolve with methods addressing initialization and sparse-view constraints [G].
