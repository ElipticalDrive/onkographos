# Compact 3D Gaussian Representation (C3DGS)
Also called: Compact3DGS, Compact 3D Gaussian Representation for Radiance Field

## Overview
Compact 3D Gaussian Representation (C3DGS) is a compression framework that reduces the storage and computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting by pruning redundant Gaussians and compressing their attributes via spatial redundancy exploitation [S1, S2]. It achieves over 25× storage reduction and faster rendering compared to standard 3DGS by replacing per-Gaussian color storage with a grid-based neural field and quantizing geometric attributes into a codebook [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on global significance scoring or 2D grid sorting, C3DGS specifically targets the spatial correlation of color and the geometric similarity of scales/rotations to minimize memory footprint [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$ initialized from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points and densified via standard 3DGS training, including position $\mathbf{p}_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{r}_i$ (quaternion), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients $c_i$ [S1, S2].
*   **Output:** A compact representation consisting of:
    1.  A pruned set of active Gaussians.
    2.  A hash-based grid neural field for view-dependent colors.
    3.  A geometric codebook and per-Gaussian indices.
    4.  (Optional) Quantized and entropy-coded bitstreams [S1, S2].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Volume Mask ($m_i$):** A learnable scalar parameter per Gaussian, initialized to 1.0, used to determine survival [S1, S2].
2.  **Hash Grid ($\mathcal{G}$):** A multi-resolution hash table (Instant-NGP style) mapping 3D coordinates to feature vectors [S1].
3.  **Geometric Codebook ($\mathcal{C}$):** A fixed-size table of learned scale and rotation vectors [S1].
4.  **Gaussian Indices ($idx_i$):** An integer index pointing to an entry in $\mathcal{C}$ for each active Gaussian [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Volume-Based Masking (Pruning):**
    Introduce a learnable mask $m_i$ for each Gaussian $i$. During training, the mask is optimized to regularize the Gaussian volume. Gaussians with low contribution (indicated by low mask values) are pruned [S1, S2]. The volume is implicitly regularized by combining opacity $\alpha_i$ and scale $\mathbf{s}_i$ [S2].
    *   *Pitfall:* Aggressive pruning without recovery may lose high-frequency details. C3DGS relies on the mask learning process to identify non-essential Gaussians iteratively [S2].

2.  **Color Attribute Compression (Grid Neural Field):**
    Instead of storing SH coefficients $c_i$ for each Gaussian, query the hash grid $\mathcal{G}$ at the Gaussian's position $\mathbf{p}_i$ to retrieve a feature vector. This feature vector is decoded (via a small MLP or directly, depending on implementation details not fully specified in sources, but implied to be a compact representation) to generate the view-dependent color [S1, S2].
    *   *Formula:* $Color_i = \text{Decode}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{p}_i), \mathbf{v}_i)$, where $\mathbf{v}_i$ is the view direction [S1].
    *   *Choice:* Hash-based grid (Instant-NGP) is used for compactness and speed [S1].

3.  **Geometric Attribute Compression (Codebook):**
    Quantize the scale $\mathbf{s}_i$ and rotation $\mathbf{r}_i$ of each Gaussian.
    *   Train a codebook $\mathcal{C}$ of size $K$ containing representative scale and rotation vectors.
    *   For each Gaussian, find the closest entry in $\mathcal{C}$ and store only the index $idx_i$ [S1, S2].
    *   *Note:* The sources state that "the majority of Gaussians exhibit similar geometry," justifying this quantization [S1].

4.  **Post-Processing (Quantization and Entropy Coding):**
    Apply lossless compression to the final integer indices (for codebook and grid) and residuals [S1, S2]. This step is optional but recommended to achieve the reported >25× compression [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

m = np.ones(N)  # Learnable masks

    # Render and compute loss (L1, D-SSIM, etc.)
    loss = compute_rendering_loss()

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Type:** Hash-based (Instant-NGP) [S1].
*   **Codebook Size:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Threshold for Masking:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sparse Scenes:** The grid-based color representation may struggle in sparse settings where neighboring Gaussians are far apart, as spatial redundancy is lower [S1, S2].
*   **High-Frequency Details:** Pruning may remove Gaussians contributing to fine details if the mask regularization is too aggressive [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Optimized Minimal 3D Gaussian Splatting (OMG)](13978-optimized-minimal-3d-gaussian-splatting-omg.md)
OMG addresses C3DGS's weakness in sparse settings by retaining per-Gaussian geometry (scale/rotation) while using Sub-Vector Quantization (SVQ) and a small neural field for appearance, achieving >300× compression.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Storage:** Achieves >25× compression compared to standard 3DGS [S1]. On the Deep Blending dataset, it shows over 28× enhancement in storage efficiency [S1].
*   **Speed:** Nearly 40% increase in rendering speed on Deep Blending due to reduced Gaussian count and efficient grid lookup [S1].
*   **Quality:** Maintains or improves PSNR compared to 3DGS on real-world datasets like Deep Blending [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Linear complexity with respect to the number of *active* Gaussians, which is significantly reduced [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance may degrade in highly sparse scenes where the assumption of spatial redundancy for color and geometry breaks down [S1, S2].

## Background
C3DGS was proposed to address the critical memory and storage issues in 3D Gaussian Splatting, which often exceeds 1GB for large scenes [S1]. It was introduced by the authors of the CVPR 2024 paper "Compact 3D Gaussian Representation for Radiance Field" [S1]. The method builds on the observation that standard densification produces redundant Gaussians and that spatial redundancy in color and geometry can be exploited [S1]. It is cited in surveys of 3DGS compression methods as a foundational approach for grid-based and codebook-based compression [S2].
