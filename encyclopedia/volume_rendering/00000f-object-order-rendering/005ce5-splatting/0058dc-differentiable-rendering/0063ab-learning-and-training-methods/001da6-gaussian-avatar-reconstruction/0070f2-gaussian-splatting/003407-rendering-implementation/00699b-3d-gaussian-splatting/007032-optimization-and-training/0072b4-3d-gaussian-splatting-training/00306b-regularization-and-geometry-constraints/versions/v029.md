# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Priors, Surface Regularization, Frequency Constraints

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that address the "floaters" (Gaussians in empty space) and blurred surfaces caused by over-reconstruction and the lack of explicit geometric priors in standard photometric optimization [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5]. Unlike standard training which relies solely on pixel-wise photometric loss, these methods introduce additional constraints in the frequency domain, 2D image space, or 3D geometric space to guide the shape, position, and density of Gaussians [S2, S3]. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on structural integrity and artifact suppression rather than physical accuracy, and from [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) by actively penalizing incorrect geometry rather than just adding primitives.

## Shared principles
The shared mechanism across all regularization and geometry constraint methods is the addition of a regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ to the total training loss $\mathcal{L}_{total}$, typically formulated as:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
where $\lambda_{reg}$ is a weighting factor [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5]. The core problem solved is that photometric loss alone is insufficient to distinguish between valid surface geometry and noise/floaters, leading to overfitting in empty space [S2, S3].

Common mathematical components include:
1.  **Frequency Domain Analysis:** Transforming images to the frequency domain (via FFT) to analyze amplitude and phase discrepancies [S2, S3].
2.  **Geometric Consistency:** Enforcing consistency between rendered depth/normal maps and ground truth or pseudo-ground truth [S1, S3, S4].
3.  **Shape Constraints:** Modifying covariance matrices or scale parameters to enforce surfel-like (flat) or half-Gaussian geometries [S1, S3, S5].
4.  **Smoothing:** Applying spatial blurring to supervision signals to stabilize gradients [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth to alleviate over-reconstruction. It sets itself apart by operating in the 2D image domain, targeting spectral errors (amplitude and phase) rather than 3D structural consistency. It is chosen when high-frequency artifacts and "floaters" are prevalent due to overfitting.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision enhances robustness by blurring 2D training images with scheduled Gaussian kernels to produce smoothed supervision signals. It sets itself apart by operating directly on the pixel supervision signal via explicit spatial blurring, stabilizing camera alignment and restricting the spectrum of the learned scene. It is chosen when gradient signals are weak or noisy during early training or camera alignment.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method regularizes covariance matrices to enforce a "half-Gaussian" geometry, cutting ellipsoids along tangent planes to model sharp surface boundaries. It sets itself apart by operating directly on the primitive's geometric shape in 3D space, penalizing covariance components that extend into empty space. It is chosen to reduce "floaters" and blurred surfaces by enforcing surface-aligned geometry.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization prevents the degradation of high-frequency color details by freezing scale and rotation parameters while optimizing centers and opacities against monocular depth estimates. It sets itself apart by decoupling shape deformation from depth fitting, balancing coarse depth structures with complex color appearance. It is chosen when monocular depth priors are available but standard depth regularization causes geometric distortion.

### [FreGS](9212-fregs.md)
FreGS addresses over-reconstruction by regularizing spectral discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth images to suppress noise while preserving structural integrity. It sets itself apart from geometric constraints by operating in the frequency domain, targeting high-frequency residuals that indicate artifacts. It is chosen for scenes with significant high-frequency noise or floaters.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It sets itself apart by dynamically selecting pseudo-labels from the scale with minimal reprojection error, ensuring geometric integrity in few-shot scenarios. It is chosen when multi-view consistency is available but explicit depth maps are not.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry regularization improves structural integrity by constraining Gaussian shape and orientation using depth normals and scale flattening. It sets itself apart by operating directly on 3D geometric priors (normals and covariance scales) rather than 2D image features. It is chosen to enforce surfel-like geometry and align Gaussians with underlying surfaces.

### Wild-GS
Wild-GS aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry [S1].

### Source Feature Extrapolation
Source Feature Extrapolation improves extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts [S2]. The step involves applying border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$ and creating a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. The concatenated image is $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$, where $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies [S2].

### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
SDF-Regularized 3DGS improves geometric fidelity by imposing physical constraints such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) during optimization [S3]. Steps include introducing a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians, using a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches for mutual guidance, and incorporating geometric regularization like monocular depth priors [S3].

### MVG-Splatting
MVG-Splatting employs a depth map to enforce geometric consistency, specifically applying geometric consistency only in the near and far regions of the scene to mitigate under-reconstruction in these critical regions while avoiding over-reconstruction [S4].

### Shape Energy Constraint
Shape Energy Constraint is an energy term defined as the combination of opacity energy and boundary energy, used to preserve the shape, opacity, and unitized colors (spherical harmonics) of Gaussian radiance fields during deformation [S5]. A pitfall is that relying solely on feature energy is insufficient to preserve opacity and unitized colors; gray Gaussians with SH values close to zero appear and their size becomes uncontrolled because they cannot be constrained by feature energy alone [S5]. Without $E_{shape}$, the optimization process becomes unstable and artifacts appear; with $E_{shape}$, the process stabilizes and these artifacts are mitigated [S5].

## Background
The problem of "floaters" and unstructured density clouds in 3D Gaussian Splatting was identified as a limitation of optimizing solely for photometric loss [S1, S3]. Early methods like Wild-GS [S1] and SWAG [S1] introduced visibility maps and transient Gaussians to handle occlusions. Subsequent work focused on geometric priors: SDF-regularized methods [S3] and depth-guided sampling [S2] were proposed to enforce surface consistency. Frequency-based regularization (FreGS, FR) [S2, S3] emerged to address high-frequency artifacts. Shape constraints like Half-Gaussian [S1] and Shape Energy [S5] were developed to enforce surfel-like geometry. These methods are widely used in high-fidelity reconstruction, dynamic scene modeling, and SLAM applications where geometric accuracy is critical [S1, S3, S4, S5].
