# InfiniSplat v2

Also called: InfiniSplat

## Overview
InfiniSplat v2 is a feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction method for arbitrary input views that decouples geometry and attribute prediction, uses ground-truth (GT) camera poses for alignment during training, and employs large-angle target view supervision to improve rendering quality under large viewpoint changes [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent [Feed-forward Generalizable Reconstruction](23168-feed-forward-generalizable-reconstruction.md) and siblings like [DA3-GS](12847-anysplat-pipeline.md) by freezing the geometry branch to prevent geometric degradation caused by joint optimization, and by using GT poses to avoid pose errors that misguide the network under large-angle supervision [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** $N$ input RGB images $I_C = \{I_i\}_{i \in C}$ and optional pose conditions $Q_C$ (camera intrinsics and poses). In the pose-free setting, $Q_C = \emptyset$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Outputs:** A Gaussian scene $G = \{(\mu_m, \xi_m)\}_{m=1}^M$, where $\mu_m \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the center and $\xi_m = (\alpha_m, s_m, q_m, c_m)$ contains opacity, three-axis scale, rotation, and zeroth-order spherical-harmonic (SH) color [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian Scene:** A list of $M$ Gaussians. Each Gaussian $m$ has center $\mu_m$ and attributes $\xi_m$.
- **Dual-Branch Architecture:**
    - **Geometry Branch ($\Phi_{geo}$):** Frozen. Predicts depths $\hat{C}$ and cameras $\hat{\Pi}_C$ from $I_C$ and $Q_C$. Initialized from DA3 weights [S1, unofficial].
    - **Attribute Branch ($\Phi_{attr}$):** Trainable. Produces multi-layer patch features $F_C$ from $I_C$ and $Q_C$. Initialized from DA3 weights. Only the last $K$ Transformer blocks are fine-tuned [S1, unofficial].
- **Stage II Refiner:**
    - **Hidden State:** 16-dimensional vector per Gaussian.
    - **KNN Graph:** Global graph reused across steps. Core sets of at most 200,000 points expanded with four-hop neighborhoods [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization and Stage I Reconstruction:**
    1.  **Feature Projection:** The frozen geometry branch predicts depths $\hat{C}$ and cameras $\hat{\Pi}_C$. The trainable attribute branch produces features $F_C$. A DPT decoder maps $F_C$ to per-pixel attributes $\xi$ [S1, unofficial].
    2.  **Scale Alignment:** Predicted and GT poses are expressed relative to their respective reference cameras (first input view in sorted order). Predicted depths and translations are normalized by $s_{norm} > 0$. An Umeyama similarity transform is fitted from the GT trajectory to the normalized predicted trajectory using only input-camera center correspondences, retaining its scale $s_C$ [S1, unofficial].
    3.  **Point Construction:** Gaussian centers $\mu_m$ are constructed from normalized predicted $z$-depth, GT intrinsics, and scale-aligned GT poses. The scale alignment applies $s_C$ to translations ($t^\star_v = s_C t^\star_v$) while rotations remain unchanged ($R^\star_v = R^\star_v$) [S1, unofficial].
    4.  **Voxel Merging:** Features are projected and merged using a DPT decoder. Voxel merging uses a side length of 0.004 in the scale-normalized reconstruction frame [S1, unofficial].
    5.  **Pruning:** Gaussians with opacity $\alpha_m < \tau_\alpha = 0.01$ are pruned after merging and before refinement [S1, unofficial].
    6.  **Rendering:** Render initial scene $G_0$ into input and target views $v \in V = C \cup T$: $\hat{I}_{bv} = \mathcal{R}(G_0; \Pi^\star_v)$ [S1, unofficial].
    7.  **Visibility Mask Construction:** Construct visibility masks $M_v$. A target pixel is retained if:
        - It has finite GT depth $> 10^{-6}$.
        - It passes the validity mask.
        - It satisfies $|z_{proj} - d_{src}| \leq \max(\tau_{abs}, \tau_{rel} d_{src})$, where $z_{proj}$ is point depth in input camera, $d_{src}$ is sampled depth, $\tau_{abs}=0.03$, $\tau_{rel}=0.01$ [S1, unofficial].
    8.  **LPIPS Calculation:** Compute LPIPS spatial distance map $d_v = \text{LPIPS}_{spatial}(M_v \odot \hat{I}_v, M_v \odot I_v)$, with invalid regions zeroed before normalization [S1, unofficial].
    9.  **Stage I Loss:** Aggregate losses over valid pixels:
        $$ L_I = \frac{\sum_{v \in V} \sum_p M_v(p) [e_v(p) + d_v(p)]}{\max(1, \sum_{v \in V} \sum_p M_v(p))} $$
        where $e_v(p)$ is the RGB-channel-averaged absolute error [S1, unofficial].
    10. **Optimization:** Train last ten attribute-backbone Transformer blocks and Gaussian head. Learning rates: $10^{-6}$ for backbone, $2 \times 10^{-4}$ for head. 25,000 steps, 2,000 warmup, cosine decay to 0.1 [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Stage II Learned Refinement:**
    1.  **Feature Processing:** DPT features are projected to 8 channels by learnable $1 \times 1$ conv, merged using Gaussian voxel groups/weights, and reused. A linear projection initializes the 16-dim hidden state [S1, unofficial].
    2.  **Input Concatenation:** Image features, current attributes, gradient feedback, neighbor distances, and hidden states are concatenated and projected to 64 dims [S1, unofficial].
    3.  **KNN Transformer:** Processed by four KNN Transformer blocks. Each block contains single-head attention over eight neighbors, LayerNorm, MLP, and residual connections. Output is concatenated with original input, and MLP predicts attribute and hidden-state increments [S1, unofficial].
    4.  **Feedback Loss:** Render current scene into input views. Compute feedback loss:
        $$ \ell_{fb} = 0.8 L_1 + 0.2 L_{DSSIM} $$
        where $L_{DSSIM} = (1 - \text{SSIM})/2$ computed over valid regions [S1, unofficial].
    5.  **Gradient Handling:** Gradients w.r.t Gaussian attributes are normalized and detached before entering the shared updater. Gaussian centers, counts, and correspondence remain fixed [S1, unofficial].
    6.  **Attribute Updates:**
        - Scales: $s_{new} = s_{old} \exp(\Delta \log s)$ without residual clipping.
        - Opacity-logit, local rotation-vector, RGB-logit increments: $\text{tanh}(a \cdot \text{raw})$ with $a = 0.1, 0.25, 1.0$ respectively.
        - Hidden-state increments: $0.1 \cdot \text{tanh}(\text{raw})$.
        - Logit inputs clamped to $[10^{-6}, 1-10^{-6}]$ [S1, unofficial].
    7.  **Multi-step Objective:** Sample $K_r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ updates during training (3 at inference). Let $L^k_{render}$ be the grouped outer loss after update $k$.
        $$ L_{II} = \sum_{k=1}^{K_r} 0.9^{K_r-k} L^k_{render} + 0.1 L_{opacity} $$
        where $L_{opacity} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M [\text{logit}_\epsilon(\alpha^m_{K_r}) - \text{logit}_\epsilon(\alpha^m_0)]^2$, $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ [S1, unofficial].
    8.  **Outer Loss:** For each supervised view:
        $$ \ell_{out} = 0.8 L_1 + 0.2 L_{DSSIM} + 0.5 L_{LPIPS} $$
        Input and target groups receive weight 0.5 each [S1, unofficial].
    9.  **First-Order Surrogate Loss:** Let $x_k$ collect updated parameters. Compute rendering gradients on independent leaf copies $z_k = \text{sg}(x_k)$ to form $r_k = \text{sg}(\nabla_z L_{render}(z_k))$. The surrogate loss is:
        $$ L^k_{proxy} = \text{sg}(L_{render}(z_k)) + \langle x_k - \text{sg}(x_k), r_k \rangle $$
        Gradient w.r.t trainable parameters $\theta$: $\nabla_\theta L^k_{proxy} = (\frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta})^\top r_k$ [S1, unofficial].
    10. **Chunked Execution:** Global KNN graph reused. Sort along longest voxel-coordinate axis. Core sets of at most 200,000 points expanded with four-hop neighborhoods. Chunks > 300,000 points recursively bisected by point count along core sets' longest axes. Only core-point outputs retained. Training uses gradient checkpointing [S1, unofficial].
    11. **Optimization:** Train DPT feature projection and refiner. Learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$, cosine decay to 0.1, 5,000 steps [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

# Constants
TA_ALPHA = 0.01
TA_ABS = 0.03
TA_REL = 0.01
EPSILON = 1e-6
MAX_CORE_SIZE = 200000
MAX_CHUNK_SIZE = 300000
NEIGHBOR_HOPS = 4
NUM_KNN_BLOCKS = 4
HIDDEN_DIM = 16
ATTEND_DIM = 64
NUM_NEIGHBORS = 8

class InfiniSplatV2:
    def __init__(self, num_input_views, num_gaussians, device='cuda'):
        self.num_input_views = num_input_views
        self.num_gaussians = num_gaussians
        self.device = device
        
        # Initialize branches from DA3 weights (simulated)
        self.geo_branch = FrozenGeoBranch()
        self.attr_branch = TrainableAttrBranch()
        
        # Stage II Refiner components
        self.feature_proj = nn.Conv2d(in_channels=..., out_channels=8, kernel_size=1)
        self.hidden_init = nn.Linear(..., HIDDEN_DIM)
        self.input_proj = nn.Linear(..., ATTEND_DIM)
        self.knn_transformers = nn.ModuleList([
            KNNTransformerBlock(num_neighbors=NUM_NEIGHBORS) for _ in range(NUM_KNN_BLOCKS)
        ])
        self.attribute_predictor = nn.Linear(ATTEND_DIM * 2, ...) # Predicts increments
        self.hidden_predictor = nn.Linear(ATTEND_DIM * 2, HIDDEN_DIM)
        
        # AdamW optimizer settings
        self.beta = (0.9, 0.95)
        self.grad_clip = 1.0

    def stage_i_reconstruction(self, images, gt_poses=None):
        """
        Stage I: Initial Gaussian prediction and alignment.
        """
        # 1. Feature Projection
        # Frozen geometry branch predicts depths and cameras
        pred_depths, pred_cameras = self.geo_branch(images)
        
        # Trainable attribute branch produces features
        attr_features = self.attr_branch(images)
        
        # DPT decoder maps features to per-pixel attributes
        # Note: In practice, this involves back-projection and merging
        gaussians = self._predict_gaussians_from_features(attr_features, pred_depths, pred_cameras)
        
        # 2. Scale Alignment
        # Normalize predicted depths and translations
        s_norm = self._get_normalization_scale(pred_depths)
        normalized_pred_depths = pred_depths / s_norm
        normalized_pred_trans = self._normalize_translations(pred_cameras, s_norm)
        
        # Fit Umeyama similarity transform from GT to normalized predicted
        # Using only input-camera center correspondences
        if gt_poses is not None:
            s_c = self._fit_umeyama_scale(gt_poses, normalized_pred_trans)
        else:
            s_c = 1.0 # Fallback or estimated
            
        # 3. Point Construction with GT poses for training
        # Centers from normalized predicted z-depth, GT intrinsics, scale-aligned GT poses
        centers = self._back_project(normalized_pred_depths, gt_poses, s_c)
        
        # 4. Voxel Merging
        merged_gaussians = self._voxel_merge(centers, gaussians.attributes, voxel_side=0.004)
        
        # 5. Pruning
        valid_mask = merged_gaussians.opacity > TA_ALPHA
        final_gaussians = self._filter_gaussians(merged_gaussians, valid_mask)
        
        return final_gaussians

    def stage_ii_refinement(self, gaussians, images, gt_poses, num_steps=3):
        """
        Stage II: Iterative refinement using feedback.
        """
        # Initialize hidden state
        hidden_states = self.hidden_init(self._get_image_features(images))
        
        # Reuse global KNN graph
        knn_graph = self._build_knn_graph(gaussians)
        
        for step in range(num_steps):
            # 1. Render current scene
            rendered_images = self._render(gaussians, images)
            
            # 2. Compute Feedback Loss
            fb_loss = self._compute_feedback_loss(rendered_images, images)
            
            # 3. Compute Gradients
            grads = torch.autograd.grad(fb_loss, gaussians.attributes, retain_graph=True)
            # Normalize and detach gradients
            norm_grads = self._normalize_gradients(grads)
            detached_grads = [g.detach() for g in norm_grads]
            
            # 4. Prepare Input for Refiner
            # Concatenate image features, current attributes, gradient feedback, neighbor distances, hidden states
            input_data = self._prepare_refiner_input(gaussians, images, detached_grads, knn_graph, hidden_states)
            
            # 5. Project to Attention Dim
            projected_input = self.input_proj(input_data)
            
            # 6. Process through KNN Transformer Blocks
            for transformer in self.knn_transformers:
                projected_input = transformer(projected_input, knn_graph)
                
            # 7. Predict Increments
            # Concatenate output with original input
            combined_input = torch.cat([projected_input, input_data], dim=-1)
            
            attr_increments = self.attribute_predictor(combined_input)
            hidden_increments = self.hidden_predictor(combined_input)
            
            # 8. Update Attributes
            gaussians = self._update_attributes(gaussians, attr_increments)
            
            # 9. Update Hidden States
            hidden_states = hidden_states + hidden_increments
            
            # 10. First-Order Surrogate Loss for Multi-step Objective
            # Compute gradients on leaf copies
            leaf_gaussians = gaussians.clone()
            leaf_grads = torch.autograd.grad(self._render_loss(leaf_gaussians, images), leaf_gaussians.attributes, create_graph=True)
            r_k = [g.detach() for g in leaf_grads] # sg(nabla_z L)
            
            # Compute surrogate loss gradient
            # L_proxy = sg(L) + <x - sg(x), r_k>
            # grad_theta L_proxy = (dx/dtheta)^T r_k
            # This is handled by the optimizer step on the refiner parameters
            
        return gaussians

    def _update_attributes(self, gaussians, increments):
        """
        Apply increments to Gaussian attributes.
        """
        # Scales: s_new = s_old * exp(delta_log_s)
        delta_log_s = increments['log_scale']
        gaussians.scales = gaussians.scales * torch.exp(delta_log_s)
        
        # Opacity-logit: tanh(0.1 * raw)
        delta_opacity_logit = torch.tanh(0.1 * increments['opacity_logit'])
        gaussians.opacity_logits = gaussians.opacity_logits + delta_opacity_logit
        gaussians.opacity = torch.sigmoid(gaussians.opacity_logits)
        
        # Local rotation-vector: tanh(0.25 * raw)
        delta_rot_vec = torch.tanh(0.25 * increments['rotation_vec'])
        gaussians.rotations = self._apply_rotation_increment(gaussians.rotations, delta_rot_vec)
        
        # RGB-logit: tanh(1.0 * raw)
        delta_rgb_logit = torch.tanh(1.0 * increments['rgb_logit'])
        gaussians.rgb_logits = gaussians.rgb_logits + delta_rgb_logit
        gaussians.colors = self._logit_to_color(gaussians.rgb_logits)
        
        # Clamp logit inputs
        gaussians.opacity_logits = torch.clamp(gaussians.opacity_logits, EPSILON, 1 - EPSILON)
        
        return gaussians

    def _compute_feedback_loss(self, rendered, target):
        """
        Compute L_fb = 0.8 L1 + 0.2 L_DSSIM
        """
        l1 = F.l1_loss(rendered, target)
        ssim = self._compute_ssim(rendered, target)
        l_dssim = (1 - ssim) / 2
        return 0.8 * l1 + 0.2 * l_dssim

    def _compute_outer_loss(self, rendered, target):
        """
        Compute L_out = 0.8 L1 + 0.2 L_DSSIM + 0.5 L_LPIPS
        """
        l1 = F.l1_loss(rendered, target)
        ssim = self._compute_ssim(rendered, target)
        l_dssim = (1 - ssim) / 2
        lpips = self._compute_lpips(rendered, target)
        return 0.8 * l1 + 0.2 * l_dssim + 0.5 * lpips

    def _build_knn_graph(self, gaussians):
        """
        Build global KNN graph.
        """
        # Sort along longest voxel-coordinate axis
        sorted_indices = self._sort_by_longest_axis(gaussians.centers)
        
        # Expand core sets with four-hop neighborhoods
        core_sets = self._get_core_sets(sorted_indices, MAX_CORE_SIZE)
        neighborhoods = self._get_knn_neighbors(gaussians.centers, core_sets, NUM_NEIGHBORS, hops=NEIGHBOR_HOPS)
        
        return {'core_sets': core_sets, 'neighborhoods': neighborhoods}

    def _chunk_execution(self, gaussians, knn_graph):
        """
        Chunked execution for large scenes.
        """
        core_sets = knn_graph['core_sets']
        neighborhoods = knn_graph['neighborhoods']
        
        chunks = []
        for core_set in core_sets:
            if len(core_set) > MAX_CHUNK_SIZE:
                # Recursively bisect by point count along longest axes
                sub_chunks = self._bisect_chunk(core_set, neighborhoods)
                chunks.extend(sub_chunks)
            else:
                chunks.append({'core': core_set, 'neighbors': neighborhoods[core_set]})
                
        return chunks

    def _bisect_chunk(self, core_set, neighborhoods):
        """
        Recursively bisect a chunk exceeding MAX_CHUNK_SIZE.
        """
        if len(core_set) <= MAX_CHUNK_SIZE:
            return [{'core': core_set, 'neighbors': neighborhoods[core_set]}]
            
        # Find longest axis of core set
        longest_axis = self._find_longest_axis(core_set)
        
        # Bisect by point count along this axis
        mid = len(core_set) // 2
        left_core = core_set[:mid]
        right_core = core_set[mid:]
        
        # Recompute neighborhoods for sub-chunks
        left_neighbors = self._get_knn_neighbors(gaussians.centers, left_core, NUM_NEIGHBORS, hops=NEIGHBOR_HOPS)
        right_neighbors = self._get_knn_neighbors(gaussians.centers, right_core, NUM_NEIGHBORS, hops=NEIGHBOR_HOPS)
        
        left_chunks = self._bisect_chunk(left_core, left_neighbors)
        right_chunks = self._bisect_chunk(right_core, right_neighbors)
        
        return left_chunks + right_chunks

class KNNTransformerBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_neighbors):
        super().__init__()
        self.num_neighbors = num_neighbors
        self.attention = nn.MultiheadAttention(embed_dim=64, num_heads=1)
        self.layernorm1 = nn.LayerNorm(64)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(64, 128),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 64)
        )
        self.layernorm2 = nn.LayerNorm(64)

    def forward(self, x, knn_graph):
        # x: [M, 64]
        # knn_graph: {'core_sets': [...], 'neighborhoods': {...}}
        
        # Single-head attention over eight neighbors
        # Note: Implementation details for KNN attention depend on specific graph structure
        # Here we assume x is already aggregated or processed per core point
        x_normed = self.layernorm1(x)
        # Attention mechanism over neighbors
        # ... (specific KNN attention logic) ...
        x_attn = self.attention(x_normed, x_normed, x_normed)[0]
        
        # MLP and residual
        x_mlp = self.layernorm2(x + x_attn)
        x_out = x_mlp + self.mlp(x_mlp)
        
        return x_out
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Stage I is dominated by the feed-forward pass of the DA3-based backbone and DPT decoder. Stage II involves iterative refinement with KNN graph construction and transformer blocks. Chunked execution ensures memory efficiency for large scenes.
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians $M$ and the KNN graph size. Chunked execution limits peak memory usage by processing subsets of points.
- **Measured Performance:** On six datasets, InfiniSplat v2 achieves state-of-the-art mean LPIPS and DISTS in both pose-conditioned and pose-free settings. In the pose-conditioned setting, mean LPIPS and DISTS decrease by 25.8% and 32.9%, respectively, compared to the strongest evaluated baselines [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** The method scales to large scenes via chunked execution and voxel merging.
- **Strengths:** High-quality rendering under large viewpoint changes, robust to geometric errors due to decoupled geometry and attribute prediction, and effective use of GT poses for alignment.
- **Limitations:** Requires GT camera poses for training alignment (though inference can be pose-free). Joint optimization of geometry and attributes is avoided, which may limit flexibility in some scenarios.
- **When to use:** When high-quality novel view synthesis is required under large viewpoint changes, and ground-truth camera poses are available for training.
- **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and iterative refinement is not feasible, or when ground-truth poses are unavailable and pose-free training is critical without the specific alignment strategy.

## Background
InfiniSplat v2 was introduced by Lin et al. in 2025/2026 as an improvement over DA3-GS and InfiniSplat [S1, unofficial]. It addresses limitations in Gaussian attribute learning, such as flying-point artifacts and pose errors under large-angle supervision. The method builds on the DA3 (Depth Anything 3) backbone and incorporates insights from InfiniSplat regarding large-angle target view supervision [S1, unofficial].
