# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking [S1, unofficial]. It avoids the computational overhead of automatic differentiation and ensures minimal parameterization using Lie algebra, matching the Jacobian dimensionality to the six degrees of freedom [S1, unofficial]. Unlike prior ray-intersection methods that assume fewer Gaussians, this approach provides the first analytical Jacobians for EWA splatting and 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$ and 3D Gaussian parameters (mean $\mu_W$, covariance $\Sigma_W$, opacity $\alpha$, color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Jacobians $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ required for gradient descent optimization of the camera pose [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing the camera-to-world pose [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mu_C$: $3 \times 1$ vector representing the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates [S1, unofficial].
*   $W$: $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix component of $T_{CW}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\tau$: $6 \times 1$ vector in Lie algebra $se(3)$, composed of rotation vector $\theta$ and translation vector $\rho$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\times$: Skew-symmetric matrix operator mapping a 3D vector to a $3 \times 3$ matrix [S1, unofficial].

**Mathematical Definitions:**
The derivative on the manifold is defined using the Lie algebra exponential and logarithmic maps [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau}
$$
where $\mathbf{T} \in SE(3)$, $\tau \in se(3)$, and $\circ$ is group composition [S1, unofficial].

The chain rule decomposes the Jacobians for the projected mean $\mu_I$ and covariance $\Sigma_I$ [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{D\mu_C}{DT_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}
$$
where $J$ is the Jacobian of the perspective projection [S1, unofficial].

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**

1.  **Derive $\frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}$:**
    The Gaussian mean in camera coordinates is $\mu_C = R_{CW} \mu_W + t_{CW}$ [S1, unofficial]. Let $\mathbf{T} = T_{CW}$ and perturb it by $\text{Exp}(\tau)$ where $\tau = [\theta, \rho]^\top$ [S1, unofficial]. Using the approximation $\text{Exp}(\tau) \approx I + \tau^\wedge$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(I + \tau^\wedge) \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Expanding $\tau^\wedge \mu_C = \theta^\times \mu_C + \rho = -\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho$ yields:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    where $I$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix and $\mu_C^\times$ is the skew-symmetric matrix of $\mu_C$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Derive $\frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}$:**
    Let $W$ be the rotation component of $T_{CW}$ [S1, unofficial]. The derivative with respect to the translation part of $\tau$ is zero [S1, unofficial]. The derivative with respect to the rotation vector $\theta$ is derived from $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_i} = e_i^\times W$, where $e_i$ are standard basis vectors [S1, unofficial]. For example:
    $$
    \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_x} = e_1^\times W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -W_{3,1} & -W_{3,2} & -W_{3,3} \\ W_{2,1} & W_{2,2} & W_{2,3} \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Generalizing for all axes and assembling the translation (zero) and rotation blocks results in the $9 \times 6$ Jacobian:
    $$
    \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    where $W_{:,i}$ is the $i$-th column of $W$ and $\times$ denotes the skew-symmetric operator [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Final Jacobians:**
    Substitute the manifold derivatives into the chain rule equations [S1, unofficial]. The terms $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C}$ and $\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C}$ correspond to standard differentiable projection operations, while $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W}$ accounts for the rotation of the 3D covariance into the camera frame [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def skew_symmetric(v):
    """Converts a 3D vector to a 3x3 skew-symmetric matrix."""
    return np.array([
        [0, -v[2], v[1]],
        [v[2], 0, -v[0]],
        [-v[1], v[0], 0]
    ])

def compute_camera_jacobians(mu_C, W):
    """
    Computes the analytical Jacobians of camera pose T_CW (SE(3))
    with respect to the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates mu_C
    and the rotation component W.
    
    Args:
        mu_C: numpy array, shape (3,), Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
        W: numpy array, shape (3,3), Rotation matrix component of T_CW.
        
    Returns:
        d_mu_C_d_T: numpy array, shape (3, 6), Jacobian of mu_C w.r.t T_CW.
        d_W_d_T: numpy array, shape (9, 6), Jacobian of W w.r.t T_CW.
    """
    # 1. Jacobian of mu_C w.r.t T_CW: [I, -mu_C^x]
    I_3 = np.eye(3)
    mu_C_skew = skew_symmetric(mu_C)
    d_mu_C_d_T = np.hstack([I_3, -mu_C_skew])
    
    # 2. Jacobian of W w.r.t T_CW: 9x6 matrix
    # First 3 columns (translation derivatives) are zero.
    # Last 3 columns (rotation derivatives) are constructed from -W_{:,i}^x.
    d_W_d_T = np.zeros((9, 6))
    
    # Extract columns of W
    w1, w2, w3 = W[:, 0], W[:, 1], W[:, 2]
    
    # Compute skew-symmetric matrices for each column
    s1, s2, s3 = skew_symmetric(w1), skew_symmetric(w2), skew_symmetric(w3)
    
    # Fill the 3x3 blocks for the rotation derivatives
    d_W_d_T[0:3, 3:6] = -s1
    d_W_d_T[3:6, 3:6] = -s2
    d_W_d_T[6:9, 3:6] = -s3
    
    return d_mu_C_d_T, d_W_d_T
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Iterations:** At least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame are required for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Photometric and Geometric Tracking](10080-photometric-and-geometric-tracking.md)
Photometric and Geometric Tracking optimizes only the current camera pose without updating the map representation, minimizing photometric and/or geometric residuals to estimate motion. It sets itself apart from full SLAM or mapping methods by decoupling pose estimation from scene reconstruction, allowing for efficient real-time tracking using pre-existing Gaussian splats. Choose this variant when real-time pose estimation is required without rebuilding the scene geometry.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The analytical derivation eliminates the overhead of automatic differentiation, making view synthesis and gradient computation computationally efficient [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The system operates at 3.2 FPS with monocular input and 2.5 FPS with depth on the Replica dataset [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables accurate tracking in SLAM systems using 3DGS as the sole representation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires at least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method originates from the Gaussian Splatting SLAM paper, which introduced the first analytical Jacobians of SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians used in EWA splatting and 3DGS [S1, unofficial]. Prior methods assumed fewer Gaussians and relied on ray-intersection, making them inapplicable to 3DGS [S1, unofficial]. This derivation enables new applications of 3DGS beyond SLAM, such as dynamic tracking and 4D scene representation, by providing efficient gradient computation for camera pose optimization [S1, unofficial].
