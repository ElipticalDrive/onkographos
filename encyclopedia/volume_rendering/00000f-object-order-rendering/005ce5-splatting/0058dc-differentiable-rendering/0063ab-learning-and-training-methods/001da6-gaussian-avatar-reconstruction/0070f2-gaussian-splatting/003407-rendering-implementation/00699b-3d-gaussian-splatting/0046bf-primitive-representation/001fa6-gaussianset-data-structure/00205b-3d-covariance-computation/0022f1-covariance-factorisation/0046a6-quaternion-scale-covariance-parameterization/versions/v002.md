# Quaternion-Scale Covariance Parameterization

Also called: Quaternion-Scale Covariance, Quaternion and Scale Parameterization, Covariance Parameterization via Quaternion and Scale, QS Covariance

## Overview
This method parameterizes the 3D Gaussian covariance matrix $\Sigma$ using a quaternion $q$ and a 3D scale vector $s$ to ensure the matrix remains positive semi-definite during optimization [S1][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem that directly optimizing the entries of $\Sigma$ can result in non-positive semi-definite matrices, violating physical interpretation and causing numerical instability [S1]. Unlike siblings such as [Cholesky Parameterisation for Gaussian Splats](3120-cholesky-parameterisation-for-gaussian-splats.md), which uses a triangular factorization, this approach explicitly decouples rotation and scale, allowing for efficient gradient derivation and intuitive ellipsoid parameterization [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A quaternion $q = [w, x, y, z]^T$ representing rotation, and a 3D scale vector $s = [s_x, s_y, s_z]^T$ representing anisotropic scaling [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ or its inverse $\Sigma^{-1}$, used in the Gaussian density function [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Quaternion ($q$):** A 4-element vector $[w, x, y, z]$ representing orientation [S1]. The quaternion must be normalized to unit length to represent a valid rotation [G].
*   **Scale Vector ($s$):** A 3-element vector $[s_x, s_y, s_z]$ defining the scaling along local axes [S1][S3, unofficial]. The scaling matrix $S = \text{diag}(s)$ guarantees that the eigenvalues of $\Sigma$ remain positive [S3, unofficial].

**Construction Steps**
1.  **Construct Rotation Matrix ($R$):** Convert the quaternion $q$ into a $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix $R$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. The standard conversion from quaternion components $w, x, y, z$ to $R$ is:
    $$
    R = \begin{bmatrix}
    1 - 2(y^2 + z^2) & 2(xy - wz) & 2(xz + wy) \\
    2(xy + wz) & 1 - 2(x^2 + z^2) & 2(yz - wx) \\
    2(xz - wy) & 2(yz + wx) & 1 - 2(x^2 + y^2)
    \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial]. Implementations may vary in convention (e.g., row-major vs column-major), but $R$ must be orthogonal with determinant $+1$ [G].
2.  **Construct Scale Matrix ($S$):** Create a $3 \times 3$ diagonal matrix from the scale vector $s$:
    $$
    S = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z) = \begin{bmatrix}
    s_x & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & s_y & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & s_z
    \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    [S1][S3, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Covariance Matrix ($\Sigma$):** The covariance matrix is reconstructed as:
    $$
    \Sigma = R S S^T R^T
    $$
    [S1][S3, unofficial]. Since $S$ is diagonal, $S S^T = S^2 = \text{diag}(s_x^2, s_y^2, s_z^2)$, yielding:
    $$
    \Sigma = R \begin{bmatrix}
    s_x^2 & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & s_y^2 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & s_z^2
    \end{bmatrix} R^T
    $$
    [S1]. This construction ensures $\Sigma$ is symmetric and positive semi-definite by design [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Inverse Covariance ($\Sigma^{-1}$):** For rendering, the inverse is required [S1]. Using matrix inversion properties:
    $$
    \Sigma^{-1} = (R S S^T R^T)^{-1} = R (S S^T)^{-1} R^T = R S^{-2} R^T
    $$
    where $S^{-2} = \text{diag}(1/s_x^2, 1/s_y^2, 1/s_z^2)$ [G].

**Gradients**
Instead of relying on automatic differentiation through the complex graph $q, s \rightarrow \Sigma \rightarrow \Sigma^{-1} \rightarrow \alpha$ (where $\alpha$ is opacity/density), gradients for $q$ and $s$ are derived analytically to avoid computational cost and potential numerical errors [S1]. The partial derivatives of the intermediate matrix $M=RS$ with respect to the quaternion components and scale components are explicitly computed for efficient backpropagation [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import numpy as np

def quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q):
    """
    Converts a quaternion [w, x, y, z] to a 3x3 rotation matrix R.
    q: array-like of shape (4,)
    Returns: R, shape (3, 3)
    """
    w, x, y, z = q[0], q[1], q[2], q[3]
    xx, yy, zz = x*x, y*y, z*z
    xy, xz, yz = x*y, x*z, y*z
    wx, wy, wz = w*x, w*y, w*z
    R = np.array([
        [1 - 2*(yy + zz), 2*(xy - wz),     2*(xz + wy)],
        [2*(xy + wz),     1 - 2*(xx + zz), 2*(yz - wx)],
        [2*(xz - wy),     2*(yz + wx),     1 - 2*(xx + yy)]
    ])
    return R

def compute_covariance(q, s):
    """
    Computes the covariance matrix Sigma from quaternion q and scale vector s.
    q: array-like of shape (4,), quaternion
    s: array-like of shape (3,), scale factors
    Returns: Sigma, shape (3, 3)
    """
    q = q / np.linalg.norm(q)  # Normalize quaternion [G]
    R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q)
    S_squared = np.diag(s**2)
    Sigma = R @ S_squared @ R.T
    return Sigma

def compute_inverse_covariance(q, s):
    """
    Computes the inverse covariance matrix Sigma_inv from quaternion q and scale vector s.
    q: array-like of shape (4,), quaternion
    s: array-like of shape (3,), scale factors (must be non-zero)
    Returns: Sigma_inv, shape (3, 3)
    """
    q = q / np.linalg.norm(q)  # Normalize quaternion [G]
    R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q)
    s_sq_inv = 1.0 / (s**2 + 1e-8)  # Guard against division by zero [G]
    S_inv_squared = np.diag(s_sq_inv)
    Sigma_inv = R @ S_inv_squared @ R.T
    return Sigma_inv
```

**Parameters**
*   **Learning Rate:** Applied to $q$ and $s$ during optimization. Not given in the sources.
*   **Clamping:** Scale values $s$ are often clamped to a minimum positive value to prevent numerical instability in $\Sigma^{-1}$ [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Non-Positive Semi-Definite Matrix:** Directly optimizing $\Sigma$ entries can lead to invalid matrices [S1]. This parameterization guarantees validity by construction [S3, unofficial].
*   **Quaternion Normalization:** The quaternion must remain unit length during optimization [G].
*   **Scale Zero/Negative:** If any scale component $s_i$ is zero, $\Sigma^{-1}$ becomes singular [G]. Negative scales are mathematically valid since $s^2$ is positive, but the physical interpretation of ellipsoid axis direction becomes ambiguous [G].
*   **Notation Confusion:** In probability theory, $\Sigma$ often denotes standard deviation, while $\Sigma^2$ denotes variance [S2, unofficial]. In this context, $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix (variance), and $s$ represents scale factors analogous to standard deviation [S2, unofficial]. Confusion here leads to errors in constructing $S$ (using $s$ vs $s^2$) [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Constructing $R$ from $q$ is $O(1)$ [G]. Matrix multiplications for $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma^{-1}$ are $O(1)$ for fixed $3 \times 3$ size [G]. Analytical gradient derivation is $O(1)$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stores 4 floats for $q$ and 3 floats for $s$, totaling 7 floats per Gaussian [S1]. This is compared to 6 floats for the upper triangular $\Sigma$ or 9 for full $\Sigma$ [G].
*   **Strengths:** Guarantees positive semi-definiteness; intuitive interpretation as an ellipsoid; efficient gradient computation via analytical derivatives; avoids complex automatic differentiation graphs [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of quaternion normalization; scale parameters may need regularization to prevent degenerate Gaussians [G].
*   **When to Use:** Standard for 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization where physical interpretability and stability are prioritized [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
Not given in the sources. The method draws from computer graphics traditions of parameterizing ellipsoids with scale and rotation, as well as probability theory's generalization of Gaussian PDFs using covariance matrices [S2, unofficial]. It was introduced to address the instability of direct covariance optimization in differentiable rendering pipelines [S1].
