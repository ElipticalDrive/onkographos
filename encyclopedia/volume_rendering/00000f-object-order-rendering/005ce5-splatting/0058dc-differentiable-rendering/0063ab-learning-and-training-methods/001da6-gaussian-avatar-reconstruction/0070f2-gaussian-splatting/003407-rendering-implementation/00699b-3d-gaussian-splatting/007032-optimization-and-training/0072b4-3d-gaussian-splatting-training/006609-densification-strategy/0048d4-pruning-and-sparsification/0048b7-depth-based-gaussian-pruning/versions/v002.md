# Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning

Also called: Depth-adaptive pruning, relative depth pruning

## Overview
Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning is a strategy that varies the pruning threshold based on the relative depth (z-axis value) of 3D Gaussians to preserve points at the far plane in forward-facing scenes [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard pruning methods by preventing the excessive removal of sparse Gaussians located at the scene's periphery, which is a common issue when a single global opacity threshold is applied uniformly across all depths [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 3D Gaussians, each defined by position $\mathbf{p}$, opacity $\alpha$, and other attributes. The base pruning threshold $t_p$ and the depth multiplier $w_p$.
*   **Output:** A filtered set of 3D Gaussians where those with opacity below the depth-dependent threshold are removed.

**Data Structures:**
*   Each 3D Gaussian $i$ has a position vector $\mathbf{p}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)$.
*   The relative depth is derived from the z-axis value $z_i$. In forward-facing scenes, $z_i$ represents the distance from the viewpoint along the viewing direction [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $t_p$: The base 3D Gaussian pruning threshold.
    *   Value: $5 \times 10^{-3}$ for real defocus blur datasets [S1].
    *   Value: $1 \times 10^{-2}$ for real camera motion blur datasets [S1].
*   $w_p$: The pruning threshold multiplier based on depth.
    *   Value: $3$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Determine Relative Depth:** For each 3D Gaussian $i$, identify its z-axis coordinate $z_i$ relative to the camera viewpoint. In forward-facing scenes, this value serves as the relative depth metric [S1].
2.  **Calculate Depth Multiplier:** Determine a scaling factor $w(z_i)$ based on the relative depth. The method applies the lowest threshold to the farthest point. While the exact functional form of $w(z_i)$ is not explicitly defined in the source, the logic implies that $w(z_i)$ increases as $z_i$ increases (farther depth), up to a maximum multiplier of $w_p$ [S1].
3.  **Compute Effective Threshold:** Calculate the effective pruning threshold $t_{eff, i}$ for each Gaussian:
    $$ t_{eff, i} = t_p \times w(z_i) $$
    The threshold is scaled such that the minimum effective threshold corresponds to the base threshold $t_p$ (for near points) and the maximum corresponds to $t_p \times w_p$ (for far points) [S1].
4.  **Prune:** Compare the opacity $\alpha_i$ of each Gaussian against its specific $t_{eff, i}$. If $\alpha_i < t_{eff, i}$, the Gaussian is pruned (removed) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-forward-facing scenes:** The method relies on the z-axis value as a proxy for relative depth. This assumption holds for forward-facing scenes (like the DeblurNeRF dataset) but may fail in general 360-degree or non-forward-facing scenes where z does not correlate monotonically with distance from the camera [S1].
*   **Depth Range:** If the depth range is very small, the distinction between near and far thresholds becomes negligible.
*   **Threshold Selection:** The choice of $t_p$ and $w_p$ is dataset-dependent. Using values from defocus blur datasets ($t_p=5e-3$) on motion blur datasets ($t_p=1e-2$) may lead to suboptimal pruning [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Advance Floater Pruning](17398-advance-floater-pruning.md)
Advance Floater Pruning is an explicit and adaptive operator designed to identify and eliminate undesired floating artifacts (misplaced Gaussians) in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations, particularly in sparse-view synthesis settings.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The pruning step is $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of Gaussians, as it requires a single pass to calculate depths and compare thresholds.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory per Gaussian for calculating the threshold.
*   **Strengths:** Effectively preserves geometric details at the far plane in forward-facing scenes, compensating for the natural sparsity of Gaussians in those regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Restricted to forward-facing scenes due to the reliance on z-axis depth [S1]. May require tuning of $t_p$ and $w_p$ for different datasets [S1].

## Background
Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning was introduced in the context of Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting to address the sparsity of Gaussians at the far end of forward-facing scenes [S1]. It builds on the adaptive density control framework of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) [S1]. The method was evaluated on the DeblurNeRF dataset, which contains real images captured with defocus or camera motion blur [S1].
