# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS addresses the over-reconstruction problem in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by regularizing frequency discrepancies between rendered and ground truth images [S2]. It prevents overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training by applying progressive frequency regularization to the Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on spatial smoothing or geometric constraints, FreGS operates directly in the frequency domain to suppress noise while preserving structural details [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Current 3D Gaussian primitives (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients), camera parameters, and ground truth images.
*   **Outputs:** Updated Gaussian primitives with reduced high-frequency artifacts.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Primitives:** Standard 3DGS representation.
*   **Frequency Domain Data:** FFT coefficients of rendered and ground truth images.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Render:** Render the current scene using standard 3DGS rasterization to obtain a rendered image $I_{render}$.
2.  **Transform:** Apply a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to both the rendered image $I_{render}$ and the ground truth image $I_{gt}$ to obtain their frequency domain representations $F_{render}$ and $F_{gt}$ [S2].
3.  **Compute Discrepancy:** Calculate the frequency discrepancy between $F_{render}$ and $F_{gt}$. This typically involves comparing amplitude or phase spectra to identify over-reconstructed high-frequency components [S2].
4.  **Regularization:** Apply a progressive regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ based on the frequency discrepancy. This term penalizes high-frequency errors that indicate overfitting or noise [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Update:** Back-propagate the combined loss (photometric loss + frequency regularization loss) to update the Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients) [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The total loss includes a frequency regularization term:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda_{freq} \mathcal{L}_{freq} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ measures the discrepancy in the frequency domain [S2]. The exact formulation of $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ is not fully detailed in the provided sources, but it focuses on regularizing frequency discrepancies [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{freq}$: Weighting factor for frequency regularization. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Progressive Strategy:** The regularization is applied progressively, likely starting with lower frequencies and expanding to higher frequencies to ensure smooth convergence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Over-smoothing:** Excessive regularization may remove valid high-frequency details.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds FFT computation overhead to each training step. FFT is $O(N \log N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency domain representations of images.
*   **Strengths:** Effectively reduces over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts [S2].
*   **Limitations:** May require careful tuning of $\lambda_{freq}$ and the progressive schedule.
*   **When to use:** When training results in noisy, over-fitted reconstructions with high-frequency artifacts.

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address the issue of over-reconstruction in 3D Gaussian Splatting [S2]. It applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training [S1, unofficial]. The method is cited in surveys on 3DGS rasterization and compression [S1, unofficial][S2].
