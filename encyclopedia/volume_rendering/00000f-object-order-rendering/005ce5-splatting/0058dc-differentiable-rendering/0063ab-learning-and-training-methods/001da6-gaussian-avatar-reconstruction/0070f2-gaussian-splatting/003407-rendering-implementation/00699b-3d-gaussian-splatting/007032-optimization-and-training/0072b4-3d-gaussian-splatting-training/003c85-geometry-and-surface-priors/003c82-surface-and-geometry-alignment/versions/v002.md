# Surface and Geometry Alignment

Also called: Surface-guided Gaussian Alignment, Geometric Priors for 3DGS

## Overview
Surface and Geometry Alignment methods address the inherent lack of explicit geometric boundaries in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), where "floating" ellipsoids obscure the true surface topology [S1]. By enforcing positional and normal consistency with continuous geometric priors (such as SDFs, depth maps, or explicit meshes), these methods enable the extraction of watertight meshes and accurate material decomposition [S1]. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [NeuSG](9172-neusg.md) or [GSDF](9205-gsdf.md) by their specific approach to aligning discrete Gaussian parameters with external geometric constraints, ranging from post-hoc regularization [SuGaR](9191-sugar.md) to physically-based inverse rendering [Geo-Splatting](9201-geo-splatting.md).

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of aligning the discrete Gaussian representation with a continuous geometric prior. The core mechanism involves adding geometric loss terms to the standard photometric rendering loss $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$. These losses typically enforce two properties:
1.  **Positional Consistency:** Gaussian centers $\mathbf{c}_i$ are encouraged to lie on the zero-level set of a distance function (SDF or UDF) or align with metric depth estimates.
2.  **Normal Consistency:** The orientation of the Gaussians (derived from their covariance matrix or rotation quaternion) is aligned with the surface normal estimated from the geometric prior.

The total loss function generally takes the form:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{geo} \mathcal{L}_{geo} + \lambda_{norm} \mathcal{L}_{norm} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ is the geometric consistency loss and $\mathcal{L}_{norm}$ is the normal consistency loss [S1]. The weights $\lambda_{geo}$ and $\lambda_{norm}$ are hyperparameters that balance photometric fidelity against geometric accuracy [S1].

## Subtopics

### [SuGaR](9191-sugar.md)
SuGaR is a post-hoc surface alignment method that converts the discrete, volumetric 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation into a continuous, watertight triangle mesh. It solves the problem that vanilla 3DGS produces "floating" ellipsoids with no explicit geometric boundary, making it difficult to extract meshes for downstream applications like animation or physics. Unlike sibling methods that enforce surface constraints during training (e.g., [Geo-Splatting](9201-geo-splatting.md)), SuGaR operates after the initial 3DGS optimization, using a self-regularization loss to flatten and align Gaussians to a surface, followed by Poisson reconstruction.

### [Geo-Splatting](9201-geo-splatting.md)
Geo-Splatting is a physically-based inverse rendering method that augments 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with explicit geometric guidance to improve normal estimation and light transport modeling. It addresses the inaccuracies in material decomposition and relighting caused by the approximated normals and semi-transparent nature of standard Gaussian points. Unlike sibling methods like SuGaR which rely on implicit geometric constraints or approximated normals, Geo-Splatting differentiably constructs surface-aligned Gaussian points from an optimizable explicit mesh, leveraging well-defined mesh normals and an opaque mesh surface for accurate light transport.

### Surface-guided Gaussian Re-initialization
**Definition**
A method that proposes a surface-guided Gaussians re-initialization strategy to make the trained Gaussians better fit the input monocular video [S1].

**Steps**
Resolve the unbalanced aggregation of Gaussians caused by densification and splitting operation [S1].

## Background
The concept of embedding Gaussians onto explicit surfaces originated in human avatar modeling. SplattingAvatar [220] and GoMAvatar [221] embed Gaussians onto a canonical human body mesh, where the position of a Gaussian is determined by the barycentric point and the displacement along the normal direction [S1]. To address the unbalanced aggregation of Gaussians caused by densification and splitting operations in these dynamic settings, GVA [222] proposed a surface-guided Gaussians re-initialization strategy [S1]. Similarly, HAHA [223] attaches Gaussians onto the surface of a textured human body mesh to reduce the number of required Gaussians [S1]. In the context of head modeling, MonoGaussianAvatar [214] applies 3DGS to dynamic head reconstruction using canonical space modeling, while PSAvatar [224] introduces the explicit FLAME face model [225] to initialize Gaussians, capturing high-fidelity facial geometry [S1].
