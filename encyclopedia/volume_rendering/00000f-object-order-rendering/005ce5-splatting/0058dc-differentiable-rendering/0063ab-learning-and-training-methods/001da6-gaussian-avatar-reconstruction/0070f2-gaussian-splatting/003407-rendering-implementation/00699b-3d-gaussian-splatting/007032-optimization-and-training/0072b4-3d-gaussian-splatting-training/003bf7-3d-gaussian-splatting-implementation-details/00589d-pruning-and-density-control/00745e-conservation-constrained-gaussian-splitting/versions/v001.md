# Conservation-Constrained Gaussian Splitting

Also called: Closed-form Gaussian Splitting, Gaussian Splitting via Integral Tensor Equations

## Overview
Conservation-Constrained Gaussian Splitting is a method to split an inhomogeneous 3D Gaussian into two new Gaussians using a 3D plane while maintaining 3D visual consistency [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of artifacts (blurring, needle-like bulges) and unstable loss decline associated with direct copy-based splitting strategies in Gaussian Splatting [S1, unofficial]. Unlike direct copy-based splitting, this method minimizes intersection and maximizes similarity to the original distribution by enforcing conservation of zero-, first-, and second-order moments [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Original Gaussian parameters $(\alpha_0, \mu_0, \Sigma_0)$, where $\alpha_0$ is opacity, $\mu_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the mean, and $\Sigma_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is the covariance matrix. Splitting plane defined by unit normal $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and distance $d \in \mathbb{R}$, such that the plane equation is $\hat{P}(\mathbf{n}, d)(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{x} + d = 0$.
*   **Output:** Two new Gaussians $(\alpha_l, \mu_l, \Sigma_l)$ and $(\alpha_r, \mu_r, \Sigma_r)$.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on standard 3D Gaussian parameters. No additional complex data structures are required beyond the plane definition and the Gaussian attributes.

**Steps:**
1.  **Define Half-Spaces:** Partition space into $V_l$ (where $\hat{P}(\mathbf{n}, d)(\mathbf{x}) < 0$) and $V_r$ (where $\hat{P}(\mathbf{n}, d)(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Calculate Intermediate Variables:** Compute $\mathbf{L}_0$, $\tau$, and $d_0$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathbf{L}_0 = \Sigma_0 \mathbf{n} $$
    $$ \tau = \sqrt{\mathbf{n}^T \Sigma_0 \mathbf{n}} $$
    $$ d_0 = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mu_0 + d $$
3.  **Check Split Condition:** Calculate threshold $\eta = 3 \times \max((R \cdot \mathbf{n}) \odot \text{Tri}(S))$, where $R$ is the rotation matrix derived from $\Sigma_0$ (such that $\Sigma_0 = R S S^T R^T$ or similar decomposition, and $\text{Tri}(S)$ is the main diagonal of the scaling matrix $S$) [S1, unofficial]. If $|d_0| \ge \eta$, leave the Gaussian unchanged [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Spatial Weights and Amplitude:** Calculate $C_l$, $C_r$, and $D$ with numerical stability offset $\epsilon = 10^{-20}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ C_l = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \text{erf}\left(\frac{d_0}{\sqrt{2}\tau}\right) \right) + \epsilon $$
    $$ C_r = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \text{erf}\left(\frac{d_0}{\sqrt{2}\tau}\right) \right) + \epsilon $$
    $$ D = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{d_0^2}{2\tau^2}\right) + \epsilon $$
    *Note: The source text contains conflicting formulas for $C_l, C_r, D$. Equations (20-22) do not include $\epsilon$, while (27-29) do. The text explicitly states $\epsilon$ is added to prevent floating point accuracy issues when denominators are small. The implementation should use the versions with $\epsilon$.*
5.  **Compute New Means:**
    $$ \mu_l = \mu_0 - \frac{\mathbf{L}_0 D}{\tau C_l} $$
    $$ \mu_r = \mu_0 + \frac{\mathbf{L}_0 D}{\tau C_r} $$
6.  **Compute New Covariances:**
    $$ \Sigma_l = \Sigma_0 + \frac{\mathbf{L}_0 \mathbf{L}_0^T}{\tau^2} \left( \frac{d_0 D}{\tau C_l} - \frac{D^2}{C_l^2} \right) $$
    $$ \Sigma_r = \Sigma_0 - \frac{\mathbf{L}_0 \mathbf{L}_0^T}{\tau^2} \left( \frac{d_0 D}{\tau C_r} + \frac{D^2}{C_r^2} \right) $$
7.  **Compute New Opacities:**
    $$ \alpha_l = \alpha_0 C_l $$
    $$ \alpha_r = \alpha_0 C_r $$
8.  **Ensure Positive Definiteness:** If the resulting $\Sigma_l$ or $\Sigma_r$ are not positive definite due to floating point errors, correct the eigenvalues to be non-negative [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import erf

def conservation_constrained_splitting(alpha_0, mu_0, Sigma_0, n, d):
    """
    Split a 3D Gaussian into two using a plane while conserving moments.
    
    Args:
        alpha_0: float, original opacity
        mu_0: np.array (3,), original mean
        Sigma_0: np.array (3, 3), original covariance
        n: np.array (3,), unit normal of splitting plane
        d: float, distance of plane from origin
        
    Returns:
        (alpha_l, mu_l, Sigma_l), (alpha_r, mu_r, Sigma_r)
    """
    # 1. Calculate intermediate variables
    L_0 = Sigma_0 @ n
    tau = np.sqrt(n.T @ Sigma_0 @ n)
    d_0 = np.dot(n, mu_0) + d
    
    # 2. Check split condition
    # Note: R and S are not explicitly defined in the source text's formula (26).
    # Typically, Sigma_0 = R @ diag(s^2) @ R.T. Tri(S) likely refers to the standard deviations.
    # Assuming S is the scaling matrix such that Sigma_0 = R @ S @ S.T @ R.T
    # and Tri(S) are the diagonal elements of S (standard deviations).
    # However, the source does not provide the decomposition code.
    # We assume a helper function get_scaling_std(Sigma) exists or use eigenvalues.
    # Eigenvalues of Sigma are squared standard deviations along principal axes.
    # The direction of these axes are the eigenvectors.
    # The term (R @ n) projects the plane normal into the local coordinate frame.
    # Tri(S) is the vector of standard deviations.
    
    # Decompose Sigma_0 to get rotation R and scaling S
    # Sigma_0 = R @ diag(s^2) @ R.T
    eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(Sigma_0)
    # Ensure positive eigenvalues
    eigenvalues = np.maximum(eigenvalues, 1e-6)
    s = np.sqrt(eigenvalues)
    R = eigenvectors
    
    # Project normal into local frame
    n_local = R.T @ n
    # Tri(S) is the diagonal of S, which is s
    tri_S = s
    
    # Threshold calculation
    # max((R @ n) * Tri(S)) -> max element-wise product of n_local and s
    eta = 3.0 * np.max(np.abs(n_local * tri_S))
    
    if np.abs(d_0) >= eta:
        # Leave Gaussian unchanged
        return (alpha_0, mu_0, Sigma_0), (alpha_0, mu_0, Sigma_0)

    # 3. Compute weights and amplitude with epsilon for stability
    epsilon = 1e-20
    sqrt_2_tau = np.sqrt(2) * tau
    
    # Avoid division by zero if tau is 0
    if tau < 1e-10:
        # Degenerate case, return original
        return (alpha_0, mu_0, Sigma_0), (alpha_0, mu_0, Sigma_0)

    erf_arg = d_0 / sqrt_2_tau
    
    C_l = 0.5 * (1.0 - erf(erf_arg)) + epsilon
    C_r = 0.5 * (1.0 + erf(erf_arg)) + epsilon
    
    D = (1.0 / np.sqrt(2 * np.pi)) * np.exp(-(d_0**2) / (2 * tau**2)) + epsilon
    
    # 4. Compute new means
    mu_l = mu_0 - (L_0 * D) / (tau * C_l)
    mu_r = mu_0 + (L_0 * D) / (tau * C_r)
    
    # 5. Compute new covariances
    # Term: L_0 L_0^T / tau^2
    outer_L = np.outer(L_0, L_0)
    factor = outer_L / (tau**2)
    
    term_l = (d_0 * D) / (tau * C_l) - (D**2) / (C_l**2)
    term_r = (d_0 * D) / (tau * C_r) + (D**2) / (C_r**2)
    
    Sigma_l = Sigma_0 + factor * term_l
    Sigma_r = Sigma_0 - factor * term_r
    
    # 6. Ensure positive definiteness
    Sigma_l = ensure_positive_definite(Sigma_l)
    Sigma_r = ensure_positive_definite(Sigma_r)
    
    # 7. Compute new opacities
    alpha_l = alpha_0 * C_l
    alpha_r = alpha_0 * C_r
    
    return (alpha_l, mu_l, Sigma_l), (alpha_r, mu_r, Sigma_r)

def ensure_positive_definite(Sigma):
    """Correct eigenvalues to be non-negative to ensure positive definiteness."""
    eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(Sigma)
    eigenvalues = np.maximum(eigenvalues, 1e-6)
    return eigenvectors @ np.diag(eigenvalues) @ eigenvectors.T
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon = 10^{-20}$: Offset for numerical stability [S1, unofficial].
*   $\eta$: Threshold for splitting, defined as $3 \times \max((R \cdot \mathbf{n}) \odot \text{Tri}(S))$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Plane too far:** If $|d_0| \ge \eta$, the split creates one near-identical Gaussian and one nearly invisible one, causing inefficiency. The Gaussian is left unchanged [S1, unofficial].
*   **Floating point accuracy:** Denominators $C_l, C_r, \tau$ can be very small. An offset $\epsilon = 10^{-20}$ is added to $C_l, C_r, D$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Positive Definiteness:** Floating point precision may cause resulting covariance matrices to deviate from being positive definite. Eigenvalues are clamped to be non-negative [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method involves matrix-vector multiplications and eigenvalue decomposition (for the threshold check and positive definiteness correction). It is more expensive than simple copy-based splitting but avoids the instability of iterative optimization for splitting.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the original Gaussian and two new Gaussians. No additional large data structures.
*   **Strengths:** Maintains 3D visual consistency, avoids needle-like artifacts, and ensures stable loss decline during training [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** More computationally intensive than naive splitting. Requires careful handling of numerical stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When splitting Gaussians during training to increase density in complex regions, especially where visual consistency is critical [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in "A New Split Algorithm for 3D Gaussian Splatting" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses issues in the original Gaussian Splatting [Kerbl et al. 2023] training process where direct copy-based splitting led to unstable loss decline and accumulation of artifacts [S1, unofficial].
