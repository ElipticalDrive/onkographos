# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Constraints, Frequency Regularization

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques applied to 3D Gaussian Splatting to mitigate over-reconstruction, reduce "floaters" (spurious low-opacity primitives in empty space), and enforce geometric fidelity [S1, S2]. While standard photometric loss optimizes for pixel-wise color accuracy, it often fails to distinguish between well-reconstructed regions and over-fitted noise, leading to unstructured density clouds [S2]. These methods introduce additional constraints—operating in the frequency domain, image space, or geometric parameter space—to guide densification and shape optimization toward physically plausible surfaces [S1, S2]. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) or [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md) by explicitly penalizing deviations from structural priors (depth, planarity, or spectral consistency) rather than relying solely on appearance matching [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
The shared mechanism across all regularization and geometry constraint methods is the addition of a penalty term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ to the total training loss $\mathcal{L}_{total}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
where $\lambda$ is a weighting coefficient [G]. The core principle is to constrain the optimization of Gaussian parameters (centers $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors) using external or internal priors that are not directly captured by RGB reconstruction error [S1, S2].

Common prior types include:
1.  **Spectral Consistency:** Enforcing that the frequency spectrum of the rendered image matches the ground truth, preventing high-frequency noise artifacts [S2].
2.  **Geometric Plausibility:** Constraining Gaussian shapes to be planar (surfel-like) or aligned with estimated surface normals, reducing volumetric artifacts [S1, S2].
3.  **Depth Consistency:** Aligning rendered depth maps with monocular or multi-view depth estimates, ensuring correct spatial layout [S1].
4.  **Temporal/View Consistency:** Enforcing reprojection consistency across views to stabilize geometry [S1].

These constraints are typically applied during the gradient descent step, modifying the gradients of the Gaussian parameters to favor solutions that satisfy the geometric prior [S1, S2].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) mitigates over-reconstruction by minimizing the discrepancy between the frequency spectra of rendered and ground-truth images. Unlike spatial losses, FR operates in the Fourier domain to explicitly disentangle frequency components, increasing gradients in over-reconstructed regions where standard L1 loss yields vanishing gradients. It sets itself apart from [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) by operating in the frequency domain rather than blurring images in the spatial domain.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision stabilizes joint optimization of camera poses and geometry by blurring 2D training images with scheduled Gaussian kernels. It addresses instability from high-frequency noise in photometric gradients during early training, which causes camera drift. Unlike [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md), it operates directly in image space to restrict the spectrum of the learned scene, producing smoother gradients.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
3D Half-Gaussian Splatting (3D-HGS) modifies the standard 3D Gaussian into a half-ellipsoid to model sharp boundaries between surfaces and empty space, reducing floaters. It achieves this by projecting the covariance matrix onto a local basis defined by the surface normal, cutting the ellipsoid in half, and regularizing shape parameters. Unlike sibling methods relying on frequency or depth penalties, 3D-HGS enforces geometric regularity through algebraic covariance transformation.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization prevents detail loss by encouraging spatial reshaping (movement of centers) rather than deforming Gaussian shapes to fit smooth monocular depth estimates. It decouples the optimization of center positions and opacities through two distinct rendering passes (Hard and Soft depth maps). This balances complex color appearance fitting with smooth coarse depth supervision, addressing the sensitivity of Gaussian fields to small depth errors.

### [FreGS](9212-fregs.md)
FreGS prevents overfitting and high-frequency artifacts by penalizing frequency discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth images. It operates in the frequency domain to guide optimization, addressing over-reconstruction where the model captures non-generalizing noise. It sets itself apart from spatial or depth-based constraints by focusing on spectral consistency.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive method using reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error as pseudo-labels, ensuring geometric integrity by adapting multi-scale voxel geometry. Unlike frequency or explicit shape constraints, it operates in the depth/geometric consistency domain, providing a stronger constraint than standard depth priors and reducing floaters.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry Regularization improves geometric fidelity and surface smoothness by explicitly constraining the shape and orientation of Gaussian primitives. Unlike depth supervision, which constrains location, this method enforces planarity and normal alignment to reduce floaters and ensure coherent surfaces. It distinguishes itself from frequency-domain methods by operating directly on the spatial covariance structure of the Gaussians.

#### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

#### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.
- [S2] Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.
- [S2] $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

#### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

#### MVG-Splatting
- [S4] A method that employs a depth map to enforce geometric consistency, specifically applying geometric consistency only in the near and far regions of the scene to effectively mitigate under-reconstruction in these critical regions while avoiding the risk of over-reconstruction.

#### Shape Energy Constraint
- [S5] An energy term defined as the combination of opacity energy and boundary energy, used to preserve the shape, opacity, and unitized colors (spherical harmonics) of Gaussian radiance fields during deformation.
- [S5] Relying solely on feature energy is insufficient to preserve opacity and unitized colors; gray Gaussians with SH values close to zero appear and their size becomes uncontrolled because they cannot be constrained by feature energy alone.
- [S5] Without $E_{shape}$, the optimization process becomes unstable and artifacts appear; with $E_{shape}$, the process stabilizes and these artifacts are mitigated.

## Background
The problem of "floaters" and unstructured density clouds in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises because the optimization is driven primarily by photometric loss, which does not inherently enforce surface-like geometry [S1, S2]. Early 3DGS implementations relied on densification heuristics based on gradient magnitude, which could lead to over-reconstruction in textureless or repetitive regions [S2]. To address this, subsequent research introduced various regularization strategies. Frequency-based methods like FR and FreGS were developed to handle high-frequency artifacts by operating in the Fourier domain [S2]. Depth-based methods, including Hard/Soft Depth Regularization and Cross-scale Geometric Adaptation, leveraged monocular or multi-view depth priors to constrain spatial layout [S1]. Geometric regularization techniques, such as 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting and general Geometry Regularization, focused on modifying the covariance structure of Gaussians to enforce planarity and normal alignment [S1, S2]. These methods have become standard components in advanced 3DGS training pipelines to improve both visual quality and geometric accuracy [S1, S2, S3].
