# 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization

Also called: 3D-HGS

## Overview
This method regularizes the covariance matrices of 3D Gaussians to enforce a "half-Gaussian" shape, effectively modeling sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space by cutting ellipsoids with a plane aligned to the surface normal [S1]. It addresses the limitation of full ellipsoids in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which often produce blurry boundaries or "ghosting" artifacts at object edges [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on frequency domain constraints or depth supervision, this approach modifies the geometric structure of the primitives themselves during training [S1].

## How it works
The method transforms the standard 3D Gaussian covariance matrix into a local coordinate system defined by the surface normal, modifies the diagonal elements to zero out components along the normal direction, and transforms it back. This process is applied periodically during training.

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** The current 3D Gaussian covariance matrix $\Sigma$ (3x3 symmetric positive definite), and the surface normal vector $n$ at the Gaussian's center.
*   **Output:** The regularized covariance matrix $\sigma$ (3x3), representing a half-Gaussian.

**Data Structures**
*   **Covariance Matrix:** A 3x3 symmetric matrix. In the local basis, it is stored as a 3x3 matrix where the third dimension corresponds to the normal direction.
*   **Basis Matrix:** A 3x3 orthogonal matrix constructed from two tangent vectors and the normal vector.

**Step-by-Step Procedure**

1.  **Normalize the normal vector:**
    $$ n \leftarrow \frac{n}{\|n\|} $$
    [S1]

2.  **Construct the local basis:**
    Define two orthogonal vectors $v_1$ and $v_2$ that span the plane perpendicular to $n$.
    *   If $n_x = 0$ and $n_y = 0$ (i.e., $n$ is parallel to the z-axis):
        $$ v_1 = (1, 0, 0) $$
        $$ v_2 = (0, 1, 0) $$
    *   Otherwise:
        $$ v_1 \leftarrow \frac{(n_y, -n_x, 0)}{\|(n_y, -n_x, 0)\|} $$
        $$ v_2 \leftarrow \frac{n \times v_1}{\|n \times v_1\|} $$
    [S1]
    
    Construct the basis matrix by concatenating these vectors as columns:
    $$ \text{basis} = [v_1, v_2, n] $$
    [S1]

3.  **Transform covariance to local basis:**
    $$ \text{cov} \leftarrow \text{basis}^T \cdot \Sigma \cdot \text{basis} $$
    [S1]
    Here, $\text{cov}$ is the covariance matrix expressed in the local coordinate system where the z-axis aligns with $n$.

4.  **Compute modified diagonal elements:**
    Calculate the regularized diagonal elements $a'$, $b'$, and $c'$ by subtracting the squared off-diagonal terms divided by the normal-component variance ($\text{cov}[2,2]$). This effectively projects the covariance onto the tangent plane, removing the variance along the normal direction.
    $$ a' \leftarrow \text{cov}[0, 0] - \frac{\text{cov}[0, 2]^2}{\text{cov}[2, 2]} $$
    $$ b' \leftarrow \text{cov}[0, 1] - \frac{\text{cov}[0, 2] \cdot \text{cov}[1, 2]}{\text{cov}[2, 2]} $$
    $$ c' \leftarrow \text{cov}[1, 1] - \frac{\text{cov}[1, 2]^2}{\text{cov}[2, 2]} $$
    [S1]

5.  **Construct the regularized covariance in local basis:**
    Create the matrix $\sigma_{\text{local}}$ using the modified elements. The off-diagonal elements involving the normal direction are set to zero. The normal-direction variance is set to a small fraction of the minimum tangent variance to maintain numerical stability and define the "half" nature.
    $$ \sigma_{\text{local}} = \begin{bmatrix} a' & b' & 0 \\ b' & c' & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \min(a', c') / 100 \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S1]

6.  **Transform back to world space:**
    $$ \sigma \leftarrow \text{basis} \cdot \sigma_{\text{local}} \cdot \text{basis}^T $$
    [S1]

7.  **Application Frequency:**
    This transformation is performed every 5000 training iterations [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def regularize_covariance(cov_world: np.ndarray, normal: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Transforms a 3D Gaussian covariance matrix into a half-Gaussian 
    aligned with the given surface normal.
    
    Args:
        cov_world: 3x3 symmetric covariance matrix in world space.
        normal: 3D normal vector (not necessarily normalized).
        
    Returns:
        3x3 regularized covariance matrix in world space.
    """
    # 1. Normalize normal
    n = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)
    
    # 2. Construct basis
    # Check if normal is parallel to Z axis to avoid division by zero or degenerate cross product
    if n[0] == 0 and n[1] == 0:
        v1 = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        v2 = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 0.0])
    else:
        # v1 is perpendicular to n and lies in the XY plane projection
        v1 = np.array([n[1], -n[0], 0.0])
        v1 = v1 / np.linalg.norm(v1)
        
        # v2 is perpendicular to both n and v1
        v2 = np.cross(n, v1)
        v2 = v2 / np.linalg.norm(v2)
        
    # Basis matrix columns are v1, v2, n
    basis = np.column_stack((v1, v2, n))
    
    # 3. Transform to local basis
    cov_local = basis.T @ cov_world @ basis
    
    # 4. Compute modified elements
    # Extract elements for clarity
    cov_00 = cov_local[0, 0]
    cov_01 = cov_local[0, 1]
    cov_02 = cov_local[0, 2]
    cov_11 = cov_local[1, 1]
    cov_12 = cov_local[1, 2]
    cov_22 = cov_local[2, 2]
    
    # Avoid division by zero if cov_22 is extremely small
    if cov_22 < 1e-8:
        cov_22 = 1e-8
        
    a_prime = cov_00 - (cov_02 ** 2) / cov_22
    b_prime = cov_01 - (cov_02 * cov_12) / cov_22
    c_prime = cov_11 - (cov_12 ** 2) / cov_22
    
    # 5. Construct regularized local covariance
    # The variance along the normal is set to min(a', c') / 100
    min_tangent_var = min(a_prime, c_prime)
    normal_var = min_tangent_var / 100.0
    
    sigma_local = np.array([
        [a_prime, b_prime, 0.0],
        [b_prime, c_prime, 0.0],
        [0.0, 0.0, normal_var]
    ])
    
    # 6. Transform back to world space
    sigma_world = basis @ sigma_local @ basis.T
    
    return sigma_world
```

**Parameters**
*   **Opacity Threshold for Densification/Pruning:** Increased to $0.01$ [S1].
*   **Initial Opacity for Half-Gaussians:** Reset to $0.02$ [S1].
*   **Regularization Interval:** Every 5000 iterations [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Degenerate Normal:** If the normal vector is zero or near-zero, the basis construction fails. The code checks for $n_x=0, n_y=0$ to handle the specific case where the normal is aligned with the Z-axis, but general robustness requires checking for $\|n\| \approx 0$ [S1].
*   **Small Covariance along Normal:** If $\text{cov}[2,2]$ is very small, division in step 4 can cause numerical instability. A small epsilon floor (e.g., $1e-8$) should be applied [G].
*   **Negative Variance:** Due to numerical errors, $a'$ or $c'$ might become slightly negative. In practice, these should be clamped to a small positive value to ensure the resulting matrix remains positive semi-definite [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The transformation involves matrix multiplications ($O(1)$ for fixed 3x3 matrices) and is applied every 5000 iterations, adding negligible overhead to the per-step training cost [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No additional memory structures are required beyond the existing Gaussian parameters [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, 3D-HGS with an efficient rasterizer achieves 125 FPS and 29.66 PSNR using 3.8M kernels, compared to 3DGS which uses 3.2M kernels (FPS/PSNR for 3DGS not explicitly listed in the specific row but implied lower or comparable with fewer kernels) [S1]. On the Tank & Dengin dataset, it achieves 160 FPS and 24.45 PSNR using 1.8M kernels [S1]. On the Tank & Blending dataset, it achieves 126 FPS and 29.76 PSNR using 2.6M kernels [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Consistently outperforms original 3D Gaussian Splatting across all metrics (PSNR, etc.) on evaluated datasets [S1]. The half-Gaussian shape effectively models sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space, reducing artifacts [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate surface normals for the transformation. The method is evaluated against Scaffold, mip-splatting, and MCMC architectures, but specific comparative metrics against these are not detailed in the provided notes beyond the general statement of outperforming 3DGS [S1].

## Background
The method originates from the paper "3D-HGS: 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting" [S1]. It was developed to improve the geometric fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing half-Gaussian kernels that are cut by a plane aligned with the surface normal. This allows for better representation of sharp edges and surfaces compared to full ellipsoids. The approach is part of the broader family of regularization and geometry constraints in differentiable rendering, specifically building on the principles of adding regularization terms to the training loss [Parent].
