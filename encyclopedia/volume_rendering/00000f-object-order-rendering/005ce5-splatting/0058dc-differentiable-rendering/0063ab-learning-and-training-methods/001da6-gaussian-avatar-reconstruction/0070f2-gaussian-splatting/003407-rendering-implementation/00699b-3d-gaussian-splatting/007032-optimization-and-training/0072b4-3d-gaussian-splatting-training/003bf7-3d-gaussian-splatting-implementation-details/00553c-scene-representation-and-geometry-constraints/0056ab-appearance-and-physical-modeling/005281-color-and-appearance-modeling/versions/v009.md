# Color and Appearance Modeling

Also called: Appearance Modeling, View-Dependent Color Optimization

## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting addresses the limitations of the base representation, where view-dependent color is stored as fixed Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients per Gaussian. This approach struggles with high-frequency specularities, complex lighting variations, and transient objects in unstructured datasets. The family solves this by introducing dynamic mechanisms to compute or modulate the outgoing radiance $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and opacity $\alpha_i$, moving beyond static SH storage. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md) by focusing on appearance synthesis and material-light interaction rather than volumetric light transport in participating media.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
A plug-and-post method that mitigates floaters and color distortions from appearance variations (e.g., daylight, ISP) by transforming rendered images using 3D-consistent hash grid features and view embeddings. It decouples global view-dependent appearance from local spatial changes, ensuring 3D consistency and real-time speeds by discarding the module after optimization.

### [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)
Replaces per-Gaussian SH coefficients with a shared grid-based neural field (hash grid + tiny MLP) to represent view-dependent color. This reduces parameter count from 48 coefficients per Gaussian to a compact neural representation, addressing disk space explosion.

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
Models outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field (factorized tensor grid) and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes. It explicitly separates scene lighting from material properties to accurately capture specular effects.

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
Techniques that modify the representation, storage, or training dynamics of SH coefficients (via distillation, quantization, or degree reduction) to improve quality and stability. It differs from siblings by focusing on the SH coefficients themselves rather than replacing them with neural fields or affine transformations.

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
Handles unstructured in-the-wild images by using per-image embeddings and an MLP to modulate Gaussian color and opacity. This disentangles static geometry from dynamic occluders and lighting conditions, enabling transient object removal.

## Background
The base 3D Gaussian Splatting method [1] introduced real-time novel view synthesis using explicit 3D Gaussians with SH-based view-dependent color. However, SH coefficients are limited in representing high-frequency specularities and are sensitive to lighting changes and transient objects in unstructured datasets. Subsequent works like DAVIGS , Compact View-dependent Color , 3iGS , and SWAG  emerged to address these limitations by introducing neural fields, factorized illumination, and appearance conditioning. These methods extend the applicability of 3DGS to complex lighting, specular materials, and wild image collections.
