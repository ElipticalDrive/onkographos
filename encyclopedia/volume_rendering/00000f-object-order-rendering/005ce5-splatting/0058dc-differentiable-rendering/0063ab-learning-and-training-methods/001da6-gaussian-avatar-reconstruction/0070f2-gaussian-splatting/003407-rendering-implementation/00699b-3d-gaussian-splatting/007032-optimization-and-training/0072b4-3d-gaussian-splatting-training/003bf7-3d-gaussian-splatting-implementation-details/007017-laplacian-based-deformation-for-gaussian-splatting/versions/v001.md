# Laplacian-Based Deformation for Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Proxy-Free Gaussian Splats Deformation with Splat-Based Surface Estimation, SpLap

## Overview
SpLap is a proxy-free deformation framework for Gaussian Splatting that enables high-quality, large-scale geometric editing without relying on external meshes or cages. It constructs a surface-aware splat graph based on kernel intersections to estimate local geometry, allowing Laplacian-based deformation techniques to be applied directly to the Gaussian primitives. Unlike proxy-based methods such as [Gaussian Frosting](15863-gaussian-frosting.md) or [SuGaR](15863-gaussian-frosting.md), which depend on explicit geometric templates, SpLap avoids proxy defects and mesh generation overhead. Unlike [LBO-GS](15421-scene-representation-and-geometry-constraints.md), which uses Mahalanobis distance and is sensitive to anisotropic primitives, SpLap uses intersection-based connectivity for robust surface estimation.

## How it works
The method operates on a set of $N$ Gaussian kernels, each representing a primitive in the scene. The pipeline consists of three stages: surface-aware graph construction, Laplacian-based deformation, and surface-preserving kernel adaptation.

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Input**: A trained set of Gaussian splats $\mathcal{G} = \{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$, where each $G_i$ is defined by position $p_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, rotation $R_i$, scale $S_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonics coefficients for color.
- **Output**: Deformed Gaussian splats $\mathcal{G}'$ with updated positions $p'_i$, covariances $\Sigma'_i$, and rotations $R'_i$, preserving visual fidelity under the deformation.

### Data Structures
1.  **Splat Graph $G_{splat} = (V, E)$**:
    -   $V$: Set of vertices, where each vertex $v_i$ corresponds to the mean position $p_i$ of the $i$-th Gaussian kernel.
    -   $E$: Set of edges $(i, j)$ connecting vertices $i$ and $j$ if their Gaussian kernels intersect.
2.  **Geodesic Distance Matrix $D_{geo}$**:
    -   A matrix storing the shortest path distances between all pairs of vertices in $G_{splat}$, computed via Dijkstra's algorithm or similar shortest-path methods on the weighted graph.

### Gaussian Kernel Parametrization
Each Gaussian kernel $G(x)$ is parametrized as [S1]:
$$
G(x) = e^{-\frac{1}{2} (x-p)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x-p)}
$$
where $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the position and $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ is the covariance matrix. The covariance is factorized into a rotation matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ and a diagonal scale matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ as [S1]:
$$
\Sigma = R S S^\top R^\top
$$
For 2D primitive-based methods, the last diagonal element of $S$ is fixed to zero to simplify kernels into flat 2D Gaussian kernels [S1].

### Stage 1: Surface-Aware Splat Graph Construction
The goal is to establish connectivity that reflects the underlying surface manifold, avoiding spurious connections caused by simple Euclidean proximity.

1.  **Intersection Detection**: For every pair of splats $i$ and $j$, determine if they intersect. Two Gaussian kernels intersect if the distance between their means is less than the sum of their effective radii. The effective radius can be derived from the covariance matrix eigenvalues or a fixed threshold based on the scale [S1].
2.  **Graph Construction**: Create an undirected graph where an edge exists between $i$ and $j$ if they intersect.
3.  **Geodesic Estimation**: Compute the geodesic distance $d_{geo}(i, j)$ between all pairs of nodes in the graph. This distance serves as a proxy for the intrinsic surface distance, capturing topology better than Euclidean distance [S1].

### Stage 2: Laplacian-Based Deformation
The means of the Gaussian kernels are treated as a point cloud. A Laplacian matrix $L$ is constructed based on the splat graph to define the deformation operator.

1.  **Laplacian Matrix Construction**: Construct the Laplacian matrix $L$ for the graph $G_{splat}$. The entries $L_{ij}$ are typically defined by the weights of the edges. In the context of surface-aware deformation, weights may be derived from the geodesic distances or intersection overlaps to ensure smoothness along the surface [S1].
2.  **Deformation Application**: Apply a Laplacian-based deformation technique to the point cloud positions $\{p_i\}$. The method supports:
    -   **As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP)**: Minimizes local rotation and scaling deviations from the original configuration [S1].
    -   **Bounded Biharmonic Weights (BBW)**: Provides smooth interpolation of deformations defined by user handles [S1].
3.  **Position Update**: Solve the linear system associated with the chosen deformation technique to obtain new positions $p'_i$. For ARAP, this involves solving for optimal rotations and translations that minimize the energy function subject to handle constraints [S1].

### Stage 3: Surface-Preserving Kernel Adaptation
Displacing the means $p_i$ to $p'_i$ without adjusting the shape and orientation of the kernels causes them to no longer align with the deformed surface, leading to visual artifacts.

1.  **Local Geometry Estimation**: Estimate the local surface normal and tangent plane at each deformed position $p'_i$ using the neighbors in the splat graph.
2.  **Covariance Adaptation**: Update the covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ and rotation $R_i$ to align with the deformed local geometry. The kernel should maintain its original coverage on the surface manifold. Specifically, the scale $S$ may be preserved or adjusted based on the deformation gradient, while $R$ is updated to align the principal axes of the Gaussian with the deformed surface normal and tangents [S1].
3.  **Output Generation**: The final set of splats $\mathcal{G}'$ consists of $(p'_i, \Sigma'_i, R'_i, \alpha_i, \text{color}_i)$.

### Parameters and Typical Values
-   **Deformation Technique**: ARAP or BBW [S1].
-   **Intersection Threshold**: Determined by the sum of effective radii of the Gaussian kernels. The effective radius is derived from the scale matrix $S$ [S1].
-   **Handle Selection**: User-defined vertices on the splat graph [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
-   **Spurious Connections**: Simple Euclidean distance fails to capture valid adjacency on the surface manifold, leading to incorrect connections or fragmentation. This is addressed by using splat intersections for connectivity [S1].
-   **Anisotropic Primitives**: Methods using Mahalanobis distance (e.g., LBO-GS) are sensitive to highly anisotropic primitives. SpLap's intersection-based approach is more robust [S1].
-   **Kernel Misalignment**: If kernels are not adapted after deformation, they will no longer reflect the deformed local surface geometry, causing visual artifacts. The adaptation stage is crucial for preserving visual fidelity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity**: Graph construction is $O(N^2)$ for naive intersection checking, but can be optimized with spatial hashing. Laplacian deformation involves solving a sparse linear system, typically $O(N)$ to $O(N \log N)$ depending on the solver. Kernel adaptation is $O(N \cdot k)$ where $k$ is the average degree of the graph.
-   **Memory Complexity**: $O(N^2)$ for the adjacency matrix if stored densely, but can be sparse $O(E)$ where $E$ is the number of edges.
-   **Strengths**: Proxy-free, avoids mesh generation overhead, robust to anisotropic primitives, supports large-scale deformation.
-   **Limitations**: Requires surface-aligned Gaussian splats for effective graph construction. Performance depends on the density and distribution of splats.
-   **When to Use**: When editing static objects with Gaussian Splatting without access to a high-quality mesh proxy.
-   **When Not to Use**: When real-time performance is critical and $N$ is very large, or when the splats are not surface-aligned.

## Background
SpLap was introduced by Jaeyeong Kim, Seungwoo Yoo, and Minhyuk Sung from KAIST in 2025 [S1]. The paper "Proxy-Free Gaussian Splats Deformation with Splat-Based Surface Estimation" presents the method as a solution to the limitations of proxy-based deformation methods in Gaussian Splatting. It builds on the concept of surface-aligned Gaussian Splatting, where primitives are optimized to align with the underlying surface geometry [S1].
