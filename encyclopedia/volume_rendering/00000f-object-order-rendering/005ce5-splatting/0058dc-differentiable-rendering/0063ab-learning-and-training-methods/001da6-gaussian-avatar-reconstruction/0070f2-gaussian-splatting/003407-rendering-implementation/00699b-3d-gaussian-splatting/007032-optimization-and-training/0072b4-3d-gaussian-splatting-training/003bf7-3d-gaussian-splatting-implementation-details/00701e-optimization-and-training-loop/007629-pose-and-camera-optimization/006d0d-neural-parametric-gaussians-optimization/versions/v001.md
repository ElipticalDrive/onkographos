# Neural Parametric Gaussians Optimization

Also called: two-stage optimization for monocular non-rigid reconstruction

## Overview
Neural Parametric Gaussians (NPGs) optimization is a two-stage training procedure designed for monocular non-rigid object reconstruction, combining a coarse parametric point model regularized by a low-rank deformation basis with 3D Gaussians to model fine geometric and appearance details [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting training by decoupling the learning of the underlying non-rigid template (Stage 1) from the high-frequency detail rendering (Stage 2), thereby preventing the "template collapse" failure mode common in static-camera scenarios [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A monocular video sequence of a non-rigid object; ground-truth images $I_{gt}$ and camera poses; segmentation masks for dynamic objects (if static background exists) [S1].
*   **Output:** Optimized parameters for a coarse parametric point model (positions, deformation basis coefficients) and a set of 3D Gaussians (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Coarse Parametric Model:**
    *   **Points:** A fixed set of $N_p = 1500$ points representing the coarse geometry [S1].
    *   **Deformation Basis:** A low-rank basis of size $K$ (typically 25) [S1].
    *   **MLP:** A 6-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron that maps point indices and time to deformation basis coefficients [S1].
2.  **3D Gaussians:**
    *   Standard 3D Gaussian attributes: position $\mathbf{p}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, rotation $\mathbf{q}$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
    *   **Interpolation Weights:** Weights $\mathbf{w}$ used to blend or interpolate between the coarse model and the Gaussian details [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Stage 1: Coarse Parametric Model Optimization**
    *   Initialize 1500 points to model the coarse object geometry [S1].
    *   Initialize a 6-layer MLP to compute deformation basis coefficients [S1].
    *   Optimize the point positions and MLP parameters using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   **Loss:** Include a rigidity loss to keep the template consistent across timesteps [S1].
    *   **Schedule:** Train for 100,000 iterations with a batch size of 10 [S1].
    *   **Learning Rate:** Use a base learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-4}$ with cosine annealing and warm-up [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Gaussian Refinement**
    *   Initialize 3D Gaussians based on the optimized coarse model from Stage 1 [S1].
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH) and interpolation weights $\mathbf{w}$ [S1].
    *   **Learning Rates:**
        *   Interpolation weights $\mathbf{w}$: Use the default point position learning rate from standard 3DGS [S1].
        *   Gaussian scales: Use a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
    *   **Schedule:** Train for 70,000 iterations [S1].
    *   **Scale Scheduler:** Use an exponential learning rate scheduler for Gaussian scales, starting high and decaying to $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
    *   **Optional Finetuning:** Optionally finetune Stage 1 parameters (MLP and coarse points) during Stage 2 with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ to improve template accuracy [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Rigidity Loss:** Applied in Stage 1 to enforce temporal consistency of the template [S1]. (Exact formula not provided in sources).
*   **Deformation Basis:** The deformation is controlled by the basis size $K$. A smaller $K$ over-regularizes, restricting non-rigid deformations; a larger $K$ overfits, hindering novel view synthesis [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Basis Size ($K$):** Optimal value found to be 25 for the synthetic cactus sequence [S1].
*   **Stage 1 Iterations:** 100,000 [S1].
*   **Stage 2 Iterations:** 70,000 [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 10 (Stage 1) [S1].
*   **MLP Depth:** 6 layers [S1].
*   **Number of Coarse Points:** 1500 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Template Collapse:** If the camera is very static, the optimized template may collapse to a flat surface, leading to incorrect deformation [S1].
*   **Static Background:** For sequences with large static ground planes (e.g., Lego, T-Rex), segmentation masks for dynamic objects only are required to obtain valid results [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** NPGs outperform Nerfies, HyperNeRF, TiNeuVox, and HexPlane on the Unbiased4D dataset, achieving a PSNR of 22.348 compared to 15.92–18.56 for baselines [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Keeps coarse geometry intact over the sequence and successfully fits high-frequency details, whereas baselines fail to capture geometry correctly [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Highly ill-posed problem; performance depends on camera movement, lighting, speed, and extent of deformations [S1]. Fails if the camera is very static due to template collapse [S1].
*   **Complexity:** Two-stage process with significant iteration counts (100k + 70k), implying higher training time compared to single-stage methods [S1].

## Background
Neural Parametric Gaussians were introduced to address the challenges of monocular non-rigid object reconstruction, specifically the ill-posed nature of recovering 3D structure and deformation from a single camera view [S1]. The method was proposed to improve upon existing approaches that struggled with geometry consistency and detail fitting in dynamic scenes [S1].
