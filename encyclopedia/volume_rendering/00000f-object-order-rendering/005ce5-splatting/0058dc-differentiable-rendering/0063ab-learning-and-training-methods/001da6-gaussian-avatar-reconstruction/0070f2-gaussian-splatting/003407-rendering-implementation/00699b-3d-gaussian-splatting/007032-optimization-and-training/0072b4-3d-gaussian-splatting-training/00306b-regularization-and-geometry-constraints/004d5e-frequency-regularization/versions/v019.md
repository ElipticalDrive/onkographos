# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the spectral discrepancy between rendered images and ground truth to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which yields small gradients in over-reconstructed regions and prevents effective densification, FR increases pixel gradients in these areas by explicitly disentangling frequency components (amplitude and phase) [S2]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) or [FreGS](9212-fregs.md) by operating directly in the Fourier domain to guide the spatial distribution of Gaussians toward high-frequency details [S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, and hyperparameter $\lambda_{fr}$ weighting the regularization term.
**Outputs:** Regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{fr}$ added to the total loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Frequency Domain:** Complex-valued matrices $F, \hat{F} \in \mathbb{C}^{H \times W}$ (per channel or luminance).
*   **Amplitude:** Real-valued matrices $A, \hat{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.
*   **Phase:** Real-valued matrices $\Phi, \hat{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Fourier Transform:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for the ground truth $I$ and rendered image $\hat{I}$ to obtain frequency representations $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ [S2].
    $$
    F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}
    $$
    where $(x, y)$ are spatial coordinates, $(u, v)$ are frequency coordinates, and $H, W$ are image height and width [S2].

2.  **Decomposition:** Extract amplitude and phase for both $F$ and $\hat{F}$ [S2]:
    $$
    |F(u, v)| = \sqrt{\text{Re}(u, v)^2 + \text{Im}(u, v)^2}
    $$
    $$
    \angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(u, v)}{\text{Re}(u, v)}\right)
    $$
    where $\text{Re}$ and $\text{Im}$ are the real and imaginary components [S2].

3.  **Discrepancy Calculation:** Compute the total amplitude discrepancy $d_a$ and total phase discrepancy $d_p$ using the Euclidean metric over all frequency components [S2]:
    $$
    d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2}
    $$
    $$
    d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2}
    $$
    Note: The summation indices in the source formulas [S2] use $x, y$ for the spatial domain of the DFT output, which corresponds to frequency coordinates $(u, v)$ in standard notation. We retain the source's notation structure but clarify that the sum is over the frequency domain grid [S2].

4.  **Loss Aggregation:** Combine discrepancies into the final regularization loss:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{fr} = \lambda_{fr} (d_a + d_p)
    $$
    [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{fr}$: Weight for frequency regularization. Not given in the sources.
*   Optimizer: Adam [S2].
*   Framework: PyTorch [S2].
*   Rasterization: Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-reconstruction Bias:** Naively applying FR without distinguishing between low and high frequencies can bias optimization toward undesirable artifacts and restrict Gaussian densification [S2].
*   **Gradient Vanishing:** In well-reconstructed regions, FR may provide less gradient signal than L1 loss, potentially slowing convergence if not balanced [S2].
*   **Implementation:** The arctangent function requires handling quadrants correctly (e.g., using `atan2`) to ensure phase continuity [G].

## Subtopics

### DWTGS
A method that rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds the cost of 2D FFT and inverse FFT operations per training step. FFT complexity is $O(N \log N)$ where $N=HW$ [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing complex frequency matrices for rendered and ground truth images, doubling the memory for image buffers during the loss computation step [G].
*   **Performance:** Significantly increases pixel gradients in over-reconstruction regions compared to L1 loss, enabling better adaptive densification [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Alleviates over-reconstruction, improves Gaussian densification, and pushes Gaussians toward high-frequency regions [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can introduce artifacts if low/high frequency components are not annealed or filtered properly [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene exhibits over-reconstruction artifacts or when standard densification fails to capture high-frequency details.
*   **When not to use:** In very low-resolution scenes where frequency domain analysis may be noisy, or when computational resources are severely constrained due to FFT overhead.

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced to address convergence speed, visual artifacts, and improper Gaussian distributions in 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization [S1]. It was notably implemented in FreGS, which aims to boost 3D Gaussian splatting from a frequency perspective by minimizing the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth [S2]. The method builds on the observation that L1 loss alone cannot differentiate over-reconstructed regions from well-reconstructed ones, leading to redundant Gaussians [S2].
