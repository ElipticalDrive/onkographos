# Zoom-out Adaptation

Also called: Downsample-based adaptation, Zoom-out pseudo-GT generation

## Overview
Zoom-out Adaptation is a post-training optimization technique that adapts a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model to render anti-aliased images at lower resolutions (zoom-out) [S1]. It solves the problem of geometric dilation and aliasing artifacts (e.g., thick spokes on wheels) that occur when a high-resolution model is viewed at a distance or downsampled [S1]. Unlike [Mip-Splatting](15762-mip-splatting.md), which uses filters to mitigate dilation, this method generates pseudo-ground truth (pseudo-GT) by downsampling high-resolution renderings and re-optimizes the Gaussians to fit this multi-scale supervision, achieving a 5dB PSNR improvement over baseline 3DGS on zoom-out tasks [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A pre-trained 3DGS model (positions, covariances, opacities, colors) trained on full-resolution ($\times 1$) images; camera poses for the target scene [S1].
**Outputs:** An adapted 3DGS model optimized for low-resolution rendering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The 3DGS model retains the standard structure: a list of $N$ Gaussians, each defined by mean position $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (or quaternion/scale), opacity $\alpha \in [0,1]$, and spherical harmonics coefficients for color [G].

**Steps:**
1. **Baseline Training:** Train the initial 3DGS model on full-resolution ($\times 1$) training images until convergence [S1].
2. **High-Resolution Rendering:** Render the scene using the trained model at the target lower resolutions (e.g., $\times 1/2$, $\times 1/4$, $\times 1/8$ of the original training resolution) [S1]. Let $I_{HR}$ be the rendered image at the original resolution [S1].
3. **Pseudo-GT Generation:** Downsample the high-resolution renderings to create the pseudo-ground truth $I_{pseudo}$ for the target resolution [S1]. This preserves most essential details of the original scene representation [S1].
    * Formula: $I_{pseudo} = \text{Downsample}(I_{HR}, \text{scale})$, where $\text{scale} \in \{1/2, 1/4, 1/8\}$ [S1].
4. **Adaptation Optimization:** Optimize the 3D Gaussian parameters (positions, scales, opacities, colors) by minimizing the reconstruction loss between the rendered image at the target low resolution and the $I_{pseudo}$ [S1].
5. **Convergence:** Run optimization until convergence, which typically takes 0.5K iterations for zoom-out [S1].

**Parameters:**
* **Target Resolutions:** $\times 1/2$, $\times 1/4$, $\times 1/8$ [S1].
* **Optimization Views:** Test views or synthetic views sampled from the estimated camera trajectory are preferred over training views to retain novel-view performance [S1].
* **Iterations:** 0.5K iterations for zoom-out [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* **Pseudo-GT Quality:** The quality of guidance varies by dataset [S1]. For the Blender dataset (isolated objects), supervision is intensive and high-quality [S1]. For Mip-NeRF 360 (complex backgrounds), the pseudo-GT has limited quality and provides weaker guidance [S1].
* **Needle-like Aliasing:** Without adaptation, 3DGS exhibits needle-like aliasing due to intrinsic shrinkage bias when zoomed in, but for zoom-out, the primary artifact is dilation [S1].

## Subtopics

### Zoom-in Adaptation
A technique to adapt a pre-trained 3DGS model for high-resolution rendering (zoom-in) by using a super-resolution network (SwinIR) to generate high-resolution pseudo-GT from low-resolution training data [S1]. Unlike zoom-out, which downsamples renderings, this method upscales supervision [S1]. Choose it when adapting a model for high-resolution zoom-in rendering [S1]. Builds on [Ground Truth Downsampling](15761-ground-truth-downsampling.md).

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Convergence is very fast [S1]. Zoom-out adaptation converges within 0.5K iterations (on the order of seconds) [S1]. This is faster than the zoom-in adaptation (1K iterations) and significantly faster than Mip-Splatting's filter update, which is not compatible with all 3DGS variations [S1].
* **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead during rendering, as it reuses the standard 3DGS structure [G]. The optimization phase requires storing the pseudo-GT images [S1].
* **Performance:** Improves PSNR by 5dB compared to baseline 3DGS on zoom-out tasks [S1]. Visual results show superior handling of dilation artifacts (e.g., wheel spokes) compared to Mip-Splatting [S1].
* **Scaling:** Decreasing the number of splatted Gaussians in a certain range does not influence rendering performance, allowing up to 25% reduction in primitives without loss [S1].
* **Strengths:** Plug-in compatibility with dynamic 3DGS models; no disturbance to deformation networks [S1].
* **Limitations:** Effectiveness depends on the quality of pseudo-GT, which is lower for complex scenes like Mip-NeRF 360 compared to synthetic datasets like Blender [S1].
* **When to use:** Use for anti-aliased zoom-out rendering of pre-trained models; avoid when training from scratch or when high-frequency detail preservation at extreme zoom-in is required without super-resolution networks [S1].

## Background
Originated in "Mipmap-GS: Let Gaussians Deform with Scale-Specific Mipmap for Anti-Aliasing Rendering" (2025) [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of standard 3DGS, which is optimized for a specific resolution and suffers from aliasing when viewed at different scales [S1]. It builds on the observation that downsampling high-quality renderings preserves essential scene details, providing a robust supervision signal for scale-specific adaptation [S1]. It is used in dynamic 3DGS models for anti-aliasing rendering without disturbing deformation networks [S1].
