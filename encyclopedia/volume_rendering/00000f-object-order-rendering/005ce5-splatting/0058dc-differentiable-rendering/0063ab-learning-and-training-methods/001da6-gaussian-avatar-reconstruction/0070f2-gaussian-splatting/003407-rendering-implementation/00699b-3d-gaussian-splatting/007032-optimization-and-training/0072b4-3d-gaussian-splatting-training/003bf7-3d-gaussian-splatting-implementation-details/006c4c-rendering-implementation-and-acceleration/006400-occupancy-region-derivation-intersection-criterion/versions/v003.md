# Occupancy Region Derivation & ϵ-Intersection Criterion

Also called: Adaptive Threshold Intersection, Elliptic Occupancy Region

## Overview
This method defines a robust geometric intersection test for 3D Gaussian primitives by approximating their density support with an elliptic occupancy region $\Omega_i$. It solves the problem of determining whether two splats $G_i$ and $G_j$ overlap in screen space without relying on strict binary boundary checks, which are sensitive to numerical precision and degenerate cases. It differs from standard bounding-box culling by using a shape-aware, normal-wise offset metric $\delta_{ij}$ combined with an $\epsilon$-tolerance, allowing for robust handling of near-misses and surface-aligned primitives [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Two 3D Gaussian primitives $G_i$ and $G_j$. Each $G_k$ is defined by mean position $\mathbf{p}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (derived from scale $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_k$ and rotation $\mathbf{R}_k$), opacity $\alpha_k \in [0, 1]$, and an adaptive threshold parameter $\bar{V}_{kp}$ [S1].
*   **Outputs**: A boolean indicating intersection: `True` if $\delta_{ij} \le \epsilon$, `False` otherwise.

### Data Structures
The method operates on the parameters of the Gaussians. No additional persistent data structures are required beyond the standard Gaussian attributes. The core geometric object is the occupancy region $\Omega_i$, defined implicitly by the inequality derived from the Gaussian density.

### Steps

1.  **Compute Adaptive Threshold $T_i$**:
    Calculate the density threshold $T_i$ for Gaussian $G_i$ using its opacity $\alpha_i$ and the adaptive threshold $\bar{V}_{ip}$ [S1]:
    $$
    T_i := \max \left( \bar{V}_{ip}, \ln \frac{c}{\alpha_i} \right)
    $$
    where $c = 1/255$ is the minimum rendering contribution threshold. Note that $\bar{V}_{ip}$ is typically passed through a sigmoid function, and entries with $\alpha_i < c$ are discarded, ensuring $T_i$ is well-defined and $\lambda_i > 0$ [S1].

2.  **Derive Occupancy Region Parameter $\lambda_i$**:
    Compute the squared Mahalanobis distance threshold $\lambda_i$:
    $$
    \lambda_i := -2 \ln(T_i)
    $$
    Since $T_i > 0$ and typically $T_i < 1$ for valid splats, $\lambda_i$ is strictly positive [S1].

3.  **Define Elliptic Occupancy Region $\Omega_i$**:
    The region $\Omega_i$ is the set of points $\mathbf{x}$ satisfying the Gaussian density condition $G_i(\mathbf{x}) > T_i$. In the local coordinate system aligned with the Gaussian's principal axes, this forms an ellipse. The region is parametrized as [S1]:
    $$
    \Omega_i = \left\{ \mathbf{p}_i + \sqrt{\lambda_i} \rho (\sigma_1 \cos \theta \, \mathbf{r}_1 + \sigma_2 \sin \theta \, \mathbf{r}_2) \mid \rho \in [0, 1), \theta \in (0, 2\pi] \right\}
    $$
    where $\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2$ are the orthonormal in-plane directions (columns of $\mathbf{R}_i$), and $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ are the semi-axis lengths (square roots of the non-zero eigenvalues of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$). The condition for a point $\mathbf{x}$ to be in $\Omega_i$ is [S1]:
    $$
    (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i^+ (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i) < \lambda_i, \quad \xi_3 = 0
    $$
    where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i^+$ is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the rank-2 covariance matrix, and $\boldsymbol{\xi} = \mathbf{R}_i^\top (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i) = (\xi_1, \xi_2, 0)^\top$ are the local coordinates. In local coordinates, this simplifies to [S1]:
    $$
    \frac{\xi_1^2}{\sigma_1^2} + \frac{\xi_2^2}{\sigma_2^2} < \lambda_i
    $$

4.  **Compute Normal-wise Offset $\delta_{ij}$**:
    To determine intersection between $G_i$ and $G_j$, compute the minimum translation distance $\delta_{ij}$ along the normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ of $\Omega_i$ required for $\Omega_j$ to touch $\Omega_i$ [S1]:
    $$
    \delta_{ij} := \inf_{t \in \mathbb{R}} |t| \quad \text{such that} \quad \Omega_i \cap (\Omega_j - t \mathbf{n}_i) \neq \emptyset
    $$
    By definition, $\delta_{ij} = 0$ if the splats already intersect, and $\delta_{ij} = +\infty$ if no translation along $\mathbf{n}_i$ produces contact [S1].

    **Numerical Approximation of $\delta_{ij}$**:
    In practice, $\delta_{ij}$ is approximated by mapping the problem to a local coordinate system aligned with $\Omega_i$. Let $\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{x}) = \langle \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{r}_1 \rangle$, $\mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{x}) = \langle \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{r}_2 \rangle$, and $\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{x}) = \langle \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{n}_i \rangle$. In this system, $\Omega_i$ becomes the unit disk in the plane $Z=0$:
    $$
    \Omega'_i = \{ (X, Y, Z) \mid X^2 + Y^2 \le 1, Z = 0 \}
    $$
    $\Omega_j$ is mapped to an elliptic patch $\Omega'_j$. The offset $\delta_{ij}$ is the minimum absolute $Z$-value of points in $\Omega'_j$ that lie within the unit disk projection [S1]:
    $$
    \delta_{ij} = \inf \{ |Z(\mathbf{x})| \mid \mathbf{x} \in \Omega_j, X(\mathbf{x})^2 + Y(\mathbf{x})^2 \le 1 \}
    $$
    This infimum is approximated numerically by sampling points on $\Omega'_j$, projecting them to the $(X,Y)$ plane, checking if they fall within the unit disk, and interpolating $Z$-values from nearby samples. The estimate is [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{\delta}_{ij} = \min_k |Z_k|
    $$
    where $\{Z_k\}$ are the candidate heights from valid samples [S1].

5.  **Apply $\epsilon$-Intersection Criterion**:
    Declare that $G_i$ and $G_j$ intersect if [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{\delta}_{ij} \le \epsilon
    $$
    where $\epsilon > 0$ is a small tolerance parameter. This criterion provides robustness against minor misalignments and numerical errors compared to strict binary intersection tests [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\epsilon > 0$: Intersection tolerance. A small positive value (e.g., $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$ depending on scale) that allows for near-misses to be considered intersections [S1].
*   $c = 1/255$: Minimum rendering contribution threshold [S1].
*   $\bar{V}_{ip}$: Adaptive threshold passed through a sigmoid function, specific to the implementation context (e.g., SpLap) [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Rank-2 Covariance**: The covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ is rank-2 (degenerate in the normal direction). The use of the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i^+$ is essential to handle this singularity correctly [S1].
*   **Infinite Offset**: If $\Omega_j$ cannot touch $\Omega_i$ by translation along $\mathbf{n}_i$, $\delta_{ij} = +\infty$. The numerical approximation must handle cases where no samples fall within the unit disk projection, resulting in no valid $Z_k$ candidates [S1].
*   **Precision**: The logarithmic transformation for $T_i$ and $\lambda_i$ requires $\alpha_i > 0$ and $\bar{V}_{ip}$ to be well-behaved. Entries with $\alpha_i < c$ are discarded, avoiding division by zero or invalid log arguments [S1].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity**: The exact computation of $\delta_{ij}$ involves an infimum over a continuous domain, which is approximated numerically. The complexity depends on the number of samples used to approximate $\hat{\delta}_{ij}$. The paper notes that an all-pairs comparison has quadratic complexity, but acceleration via fixed-radius search reduces it to approximately linear for typical scenes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$ additional memory per pair of Gaussians for the intersection test. No persistent data structures are required beyond the Gaussian attributes.
*   **Strengths**: Robust to numerical precision issues and minor misalignments due to the $\epsilon$-tolerance. Shape-aware (elliptic) rather than axis-aligned bounding box, providing tighter culling for oriented primitives.
*   **Limitations**: Numerical approximation of $\delta_{ij}$ can be computationally expensive if high precision is required. The method assumes a specific alignment of $\Omega_i$ with its normal $\mathbf{n}_i$, which may not hold for all Gaussian parameterizations without careful definition of $\mathbf{n}_i$ [S1].
*   **When to Use**: When robust intersection testing is critical for deformation or surface estimation tasks where strict binary culling might fail due to numerical noise or near-tangential contacts [S1].

## Background

*   **Origin**: This method was introduced in the context of "Proxy-Free Gaussian Splats Deformation with Splat-Based Surface Estimation" [S1]. It addresses the need for robust geometric operations on Gaussian splats, particularly for deformation and surface estimation, where standard rasterization-based culling is insufficient.
*   **History**: The derivation of the occupancy region from the Gaussian kernel is a specific adaptation for surface-preserving methods. The $\epsilon$-intersection criterion is a common technique in computational geometry to handle floating-point inaccuracies, adapted here for 3D Gaussian splats [S1].
*   **Usage**: Used in frameworks like SpLap, which is built on SpikingGS, for efficient intersection queries in multi-core CPU implementations [S1].
