# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking. It solves the problem of high computational overhead and redundant parameterization inherent in automatic differentiation when optimizing camera poses in SLAM systems. Unlike prior ray-intersection methods that assume fewer Gaussians, this approach provides the first analytical Jacobians for EWA splatting and 3DGS, ensuring the Jacobian dimensionality matches the six degrees of freedom of the Lie algebra.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$, 3D Gaussian parameters (mean $\mu_W$, covariance $\Sigma_W$, opacity $\alpha$, color $\mathbf{c}$).
*   **Output:** Jacobians $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ required for gradient descent optimization of the camera pose.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing the camera-to-world pose.
*   $\mu_C$: $3 \times 1$ vector representing the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
*   $W$: $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix component of $T_{CW}$.
*   $\tau$: $6 \times 1$ vector in Lie algebra $se(3)$, composed of rotation vector $\theta$ and translation vector $\rho$.
*   $\times$: Skew-symmetric matrix operator mapping a 3D vector to a $3 \times 3$ matrix.

**Mathematical Definitions:**
The derivative on the manifold is defined using the Lie algebra exponential and logarithmic maps [S1, S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau}
$$
where $\mathbf{T} \in SE(3)$, $\tau \in se(3)$, and $\circ$ is group composition.

The chain rule decomposes the Jacobians for the projected mean $\mu_I$ and covariance $\Sigma_I$ [S1, S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{D\mu_C}{DT_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}
$$
where $J$ is the Jacobian of the perspective projection.

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**

1.  **Derive $\frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}$:**
    The Gaussian mean in camera coordinates is $\mu_C = R_{CW} \mu_W + t_{CW}$. Let $\mathbf{T} = T_{CW}$ and perturb it by $\text{Exp}(\tau)$ where $\tau = [\theta, \rho]^T$.
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Exp}(\tau) \cdot \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Using the approximation $\text{Exp}(\tau) \approx I + \tau^\wedge$:
    $$
    = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(I + \tau^\wedge) \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Expanding $\tau^\wedge \mu_C = \theta^\times \mu_C + \rho = -\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho$:
    $$
    = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    where $I$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix and $\mu_C^\times$ is the skew-symmetric matrix of $\mu_C$ [S1, S1, unofficial].

2.  **Derive $\frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}$:**
    Let $W$ be the rotation component of $T_{CW}$. Perturb the rotation part of the Lie algebra by $\theta$.
    $$
    \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_x} & \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_y} & \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_z} & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    The derivative of the rotation matrix $W$ with respect to the rotation vector components is derived from $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_i} = e_i^\times W$, where $e_i$ are basis vectors.
    $$
    \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_x} = e_1^\times W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -W_{3,1} & -W_{3,2} & -W_{3,3} \\ W_{2,1} & W_{2,2} & W_{2,3} \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Generalizing for all columns $W_{:,i}$:
    $$
    \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    where $W_{:,i}$ is the $i$-th column of $W$ and the block structure reflects the $3 \times 6$ output dimension relative to the 6 DOF input [S1, S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Final Jacobians:**
    Substitute the manifold derivatives into the chain rule equations. The term $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C}$ and $\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C}$ are standard differentiable projection operations. The term $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W}$ accounts for the rotation of the 3D covariance into the camera frame.

## Subtopics

### Photometric and Geometric Tracking
This variant optimizes only the current camera pose without updating the map representation. It minimizes photometric residuals $E_{pho} = \| I(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW}) - \bar{I} \|_1$ and/or geometric residuals $E_{geo} = \| D(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW}) - \bar{D} \|_1$, where $I$ is the rendered image and $D$ is the depth rasterization. It optimizes affine brightness parameters for exposure variation and penalizes non-edge or low-opacity pixels. The depth rasterization is computed via alpha-blending: $\mathcal{D}_p = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} z_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$, where $z_i$ is the distance to the Gaussian mean along the camera ray. This approach is chosen for real-time SLAM tracking where map updates are decoupled from pose estimation [S1, S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The analytical derivation eliminates the overhead of automatic differentiation, making view synthesis and gradient computation computationally efficient. This is crucial for SLAM systems requiring at least 50 iterations per frame [S1, S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal parameterization using Lie algebra reduces memory overhead compared to redundant representations [S1, S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The system operates at 3.2 FPS with monocular input and 2.5 FPS with depth on the Replica dataset [S1, S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables accurate tracking in SLAM systems using 3DGS as the sole representation. Unifies tracking, mapping, and novel view synthesis.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful keyframe management to avoid infeasible joint optimization of all poses and Gaussians. Performance may degrade with large baselines if keyframes are not selected based on covisibility [S1, S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method originates from the Gaussian Splatting SLAM paper, which introduced the first analytical Jacobians of SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians used in EWA splatting and 3DGS. Prior methods assumed fewer Gaussians and relied on ray-intersection, making them inapplicable to 3DGS. This derivation enables new applications of 3DGS beyond SLAM, such as dynamic tracking and 4D scene representation, by providing efficient gradient computation for camera pose optimization [S1, S1, unofficial].
