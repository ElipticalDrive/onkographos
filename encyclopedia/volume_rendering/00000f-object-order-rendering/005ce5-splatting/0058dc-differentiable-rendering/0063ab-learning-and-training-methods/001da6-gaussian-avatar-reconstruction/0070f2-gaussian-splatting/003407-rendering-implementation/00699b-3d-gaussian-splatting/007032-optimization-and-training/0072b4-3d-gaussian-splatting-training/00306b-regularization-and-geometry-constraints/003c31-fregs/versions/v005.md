# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS addresses over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts in 3D Gaussian Splatting by applying progressive frequency regularization to the primitives [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on spatial smoothing or depth alignment, FreGS operates in the Fourier domain to penalize frequency discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth images, guiding the optimization toward meaningful details while preventing noise overfitting [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Current 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients), ground-truth images, camera parameters.
- **Outputs:** Updated Gaussian parameters with reduced high-frequency artifacts.

**Data Structures:**
- Standard 3DGS primitive structure.
- Frequency domain representations of rendered and target images (e.g., via FFT).

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Render:** Render the current scene using standard differentiable rasterization to obtain the rendered image $I_{render}$.
2. **Transform to Frequency Domain:** Compute the Fourier transform of the rendered image $F_{render} = \mathcal{F}(I_{render})$ and the ground-truth image $F_{gt} = \mathcal{F}(I_{gt})$.
3. **Compute Frequency Discrepancy:** Calculate the difference between the frequency components of the rendered and ground-truth images. The specific metric is not detailed in the sources, but it focuses on high-frequency discrepancies [S1, unofficial][S2].
4. **Apply Progressive Regularization:** Apply a regularization term based on the frequency discrepancy to the loss function. The regularization is applied progressively, likely increasing in weight or scope during training to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial].
5. **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients with respect to the Gaussian parameters using the combined loss (photometric + frequency regularization) and update the parameters via gradient descent.

**Formulas:**
- Let $L_{total} = L_{photometric} + \lambda \cdot L_{freq}$, where $L_{freq}$ is the frequency regularization loss [S1, unofficial][S2].
- $\lambda$ is a weighting factor for the frequency regularization, which may vary progressively [S1, unofficial].
- $L_{freq} = || F_{render} - F_{gt} ||^2$ or a similar frequency-domain distance metric [S2]. (Exact formula not specified in sources)

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Weight for frequency regularization. Typical values not given in sources.
- Progressive schedule for $\lambda$ or the frequency band considered. Not given in sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** FFT operations may introduce numerical precision issues if not handled carefully.
- **Boundaries:** Boundary effects in FFT may require windowing or padding.
- **Degenerate Input:** If ground-truth images are noisy, frequency regularization might amplify noise if not properly weighted.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def fregs_step(gaussians, gt_image, camera_params, lambda_freq, progress_step):
    # 1. Render the scene
    rendered_image = render(gaussians, camera_params)
    
    # 2. Transform to frequency domain
    F_render = fft(rendered_image)
    F_gt = fft(gt_image)
    
    # 3. Compute frequency discrepancy
    # Note: Exact formula for L_freq is not specified in sources
    # Assuming a simple L2 norm on frequency components
    L_freq = sum(abs(F_render - F_gt)**2)
    
    # 4. Compute photometric loss
    L_photo = sum((rendered_image - gt_image)**2)
    
    # 5. Combine losses with progressive regularization
    # lambda_freq may be adjusted based on progress_step
    total_loss = L_photo + lambda_freq * L_freq
    
    # 6. Backpropagate and update
    gradients = compute_gradients(total_loss, gaussians)
    update_gaussians(gaussians, gradients)
    
    return gaussians
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Additional FFT computations increase per-step time. Not given in sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency domain representations. Not given in sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Reduces over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts. Improves visual quality. Not given in sources.
- **Scaling:** Scales with image resolution due to FFT. Not given in sources.
- **Strengths:** Effective at reducing high-frequency noise and overfitting.
- **Limitations:** May smooth out fine details if not carefully tuned. Computational overhead from FFT.
- **When to use:** When high-frequency artifacts are prominent.
- **When not to use:** When fine detail preservation is critical and FFT overhead is prohibitive.

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address over-reconstruction in 3D Gaussian Splatting by focusing on frequency domain regularization [S1, unofficial][S2]. It builds on the observation that high-frequency discrepancies often lead to artifacts [S1, unofficial][S2].
