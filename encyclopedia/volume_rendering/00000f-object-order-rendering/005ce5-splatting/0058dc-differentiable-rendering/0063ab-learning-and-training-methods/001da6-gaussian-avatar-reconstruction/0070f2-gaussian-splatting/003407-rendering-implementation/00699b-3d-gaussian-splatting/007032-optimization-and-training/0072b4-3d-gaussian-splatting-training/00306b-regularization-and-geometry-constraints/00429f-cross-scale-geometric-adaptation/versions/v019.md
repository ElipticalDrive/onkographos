# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on pre-trained feature extractors or complex scheduling, this method leverages low-frequency components from low-resolution voxels to guide coarse geometry early in training, with mid- and high-resolution voxels refining details as training advances [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K$, camera poses $(R, t)$, multi-scale voxel representations (low, medium, high resolution), and rendered depth maps $D_l$ for each scale $l$.
*   **Outputs:** A geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ used to update the Gaussian/voxel parameters.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxels:** Multi-scale voxel grids. The specific layout is not detailed beyond the existence of low, medium, and high-resolution scales [S1].
*   **Camera Parameters:** Intrinsic matrix $K_i$ and extrinsic parameters (rotation $R_i$, translation $t_i$) for each frame $i$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Select Training Rays:** For each training view $i$, sample rays $r_{train}$ [S1].
2.  **Compute 3D Points per Scale:** For each scale $l$ and pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$:
    *   Compute the homogeneous coordinate $\tilde{p}_i$ [S1].
    *   Retrieve the rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ at scale $l$ [S1].
    *   Compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$:
        $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
        [S1]
    *   Transform to world coordinates:
        $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
        [S1]
3.  **Reproject to Second View:** For a second view $j$ (typically a neighboring training view):
    *   Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$:
        $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
        [S1]
    *   Project back to 2D pixel coordinates in frame $j$:
        $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
        where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the difference between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel from frame $j$:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    where $C_i$ and $C_j$ are the RGB maps of frames $i$ and $j$ [S1].
5.  **Select Pseudo-Depth:** Identify the scale $l'$ that minimizes the reprojection error for the ray:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]
6.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth at the selected optimal scale and the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'$:
    $$ L_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Scales:** Typically three scales are used: low, medium, and high resolution [S1].
*   **Weighting:** The specific weight for $L_{geo}$ in the total loss function is not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Poses:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may be challenging [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Scale Mismatch:** Using a single scale for depth supervision (e.g., only $L_d$) can suffer from higher error later in training due to scale mismatches, whereas this method refines depth progressively [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The method requires reprojection calculations for multiple scales and views, which adds computational overhead compared to single-scale supervision.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Requires storing multi-scale voxel representations and intermediate 3D points.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. Achieves lower final depth error than depth prior ($L_d$) alone [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive; no complex scheduling required; robust to geometric noise compared to methods using warped features directly (e.g., GeCoNeRF) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate camera poses; performance may degrade in few-shot scenarios with sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in **FrugalNeRF** [S1]. The method was introduced to accelerate and regularize training for extreme few-shot novel view synthesis without learned priors. It addresses the issue of floaters and sub-optimal convergence by leveraging multi-scale voxels and reprojection errors. The approach is complementary to depth priors ($L_d$), and combining both yields the best results [S1].
