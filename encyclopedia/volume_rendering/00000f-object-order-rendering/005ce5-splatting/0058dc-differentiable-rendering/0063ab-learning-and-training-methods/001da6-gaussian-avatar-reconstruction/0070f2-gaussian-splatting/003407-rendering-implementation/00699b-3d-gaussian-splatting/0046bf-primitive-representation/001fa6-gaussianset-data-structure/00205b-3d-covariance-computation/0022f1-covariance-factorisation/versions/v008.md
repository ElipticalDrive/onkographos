# Covariance Factorisation

Also called: Covariance Decomposition, RQ Decomposition for Covariance, Rotation and Scale Parameterization

## Overview
Covariance Factorisation is a parameterization technique for 3D Gaussian splats that decomposes the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ into a rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and a scaling matrix $\mathbf{S}$ [S1, unofficial][S2]. This method ensures the covariance matrix remains positive semi-definite (PSD) during unconstrained gradient descent optimization, which is critical for maintaining the physical validity of the Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Unconstrained learnable parameters for rotation and scale.
- **Output:** A valid, positive semi-definite 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on two primary learnable components per Gaussian splat: a $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and a diagonal scaling matrix $\mathbf{S} = \text{diag}(s_1, s_2, s_3)$ [S2, S4]. The scale vector $\mathbf{s}$ is constrained to positive values ($s_i > 0$) to maintain physical meaning [S4].

**Steps:**
1. **Parameterization:** Represent each Gaussian splat with unconstrained learnable rotation and scale parameters [S1, unofficial].
2. **Optimization:** Perform unconstrained gradient descent on these parameters [S1, unofficial].
3. **Reconstruction:** Reconstruct the covariance matrix at each rendering or update step to ensure positive semi-definiteness [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- **Covariance Reconstruction:**
  $$
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{S}^T \mathbf{R}^T
  $$
  Where:
  - $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is the $3 \times 3$ covariance matrix.
  - $\mathbf{R}$ is the $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix.
  - $\mathbf{S}$ is the $3 \times 3$ diagonal scaling matrix.
  - $\mathbf{S}^T$ is the transpose of $\mathbf{S}$ (equal to $\mathbf{S}$ for diagonal matrices).
  All definitions and the reconstruction formula are sourced from [S2].

**Parameters:**
The rotation and scale parameters are optimized unconstrained [S1, unofficial]. The exact representation (e.g., quaternion vs. matrix) is implementation-dependent [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Non-PSD Matrices:** Direct optimization of $\Sigma$ entries can lead to non-PSD matrices, causing numerical instability and invalid Gaussian shapes. Factorisation avoids this by construction [S1, unofficial].
- **Degenerate Scales:** If any scale $s_i$ becomes zero or negative, the Gaussian becomes degenerate. Positive constraints or activation functions are required [G].

## Subtopics

### [Cholesky Parameterisation for Gaussian Splats](3120-cholesky-parameterisation-for-gaussian-splats.md)
Cholesky Parameterisation represents the covariance matrix using a lower triangular Cholesky factor, guaranteeing positive-definiteness by construction. It uses $D(D+1)/2$ elements per splat (6 for 3D) and applies a softplus activation with a minimum standard deviation floor $\sigma_{min} = 1/\sqrt{12}$ voxels to the diagonal, while bounding amplitudes with a configurable maximum $a_{max}$.

### [Quaternion-Scale Covariance Parameterization](11801-quaternion-scale-covariance-parameterization.md)
This method parameterizes the 3D Gaussian covariance matrix $\Sigma$ using a unit quaternion $q$ and a 3D scale vector $s$ to ensure the matrix remains positive semi-definite during optimization. It solves the problem that directly optimizing the entries of $\Sigma$ can result in non-positive semi-definite matrices, violating physical interpretation and causing numerical instability. Unlike siblings such as [Cholesky Parameterisation for Gaussian Splats](3120-cholesky-parameterisation-for-gaussian-splats.md), which uses a triangular factorization, this approach explicitly decouples rotation and scale, allowing for efficient gradient derivation and intuitive ellipsoid parameterization.

### Gamma-Decomposed Covariance Update
A numerical stability technique for computing the new covariance matrix after recycling, replacing the unsupported $\beta()$ function with $\Gamma()$ and $\ln(\Gamma())$ to prevent overflow [S3].

### Covariance Matrix Normalization
A technique to transform a covariance matrix $\Sigma$ into a normalized form $\hat{\Sigma}$ by dividing it by its trace, separating the scaling vector $s$ from the rotation matrix $R$ [S4]. The formula is:
$$
\hat{\Sigma} = \frac{\Sigma}{\text{Tr}(\Sigma)} = \mathbf{R} \frac{\mathbf{S}^2}{\|s\|_2^2} \mathbf{R}^T = \mathbf{R} \hat{\mathbf{S}}^2 \mathbf{R}^T
$$
where $\|s\|_2^2 = \text{Tr}(\Sigma)$ [S4].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The reconstruction of $\Sigma$ from $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ involves a constant number of matrix multiplications per splat [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** Stores rotation and scale parameters instead of the full symmetric covariance matrix, with exact storage sizes not given in the sources [G].
- **Strengths:** Guarantees positive semi-definiteness, enabling stable unconstrained optimization [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires a reconstruction step at each iteration, and parameterization of rotation may introduce singularities or require normalization steps [G].
- **When to Use:** Standard choice for 3D Gaussian Splatting and similar radiance-field optimization methods where stable gradient descent is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Covariance Factorisation is a core component of 3D Gaussian Splatting, introduced to address the instability of directly optimizing covariance matrices in differentiable rendering [S1, unofficial]. It leverages the linear algebra property that any positive semi-definite matrix can be decomposed into rotation and scaling components [G]. The method has been adopted in extensions such as EVolSplat and compressed variants [S2, S4].
