# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization decomposes the training of 3D Gaussian representations into sequential phases to resolve conflicting optimization objectives, such as geometry versus compression or geometry versus material properties [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. By separating the problem, the method stabilizes the initial placement of Gaussians before applying complex constraints like rate-distortion trade-offs or physically based rendering (PBR) decomposition [S3, unofficial]. It differs from single-stage training by preventing the quantization noise or material loss from destabilizing the geometric foundation, and from on-the-fly progressive training by using a fixed, pre-optimized state as the starting point for refinement rather than accumulating primitives dynamically [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The method generally follows a "coarse-to-fine" or "feature-to-codec" progression. While specific implementations vary by application (CT reconstruction, inverse rendering, or compression), the core logic involves an initial stage that establishes a stable geometric and appearance baseline, followed by a second stage that refines parameters under stricter constraints or for specific downstream tasks.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Multi-view images or projection data (CT sinograms) [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   Camera poses and intrinsics ($K_t, T_t$) [S3, unofficial].
    *   Initial Gaussian set or voxel grid (often sparse or low-resolution in Stage 1) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ with parameters $\theta$ (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/material) [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
    *   In compression variants: An entropy-coded bitstream [S3, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The Gaussian representation $\mathcal{G}$ typically consists of a list of primitives. Each primitive $i$ contains:
*   **Position:** $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S2].
*   **Scale:** $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (or diagonal covariance) [S2].
*   **Rotation:** $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion) [S2].
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$ [S2].
*   **Appearance:** Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients or RGB values [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Material (GS-IR):** Albedo $\mathbf{a}$, roughness $\rho$, and metallicity $m$ [S2].
*   **Feature (CodecSplat):** Intermediate feature tensor $\mathbf{F}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C_F \times H \times W}$ before Gaussian prediction [S3, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

#### Stage 1: Stable Initialization
The goal is to place Gaussians in approximately correct positions and optimize basic appearance/geometry without the interference of complex constraints [S1][S3, unofficial].

1.  **Grid Sampling (Optional/Variant):** In CT reconstruction [S1], sample a low-resolution grid (e.g., $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume) to reduce computational burden. In feed-forward methods [S3, unofficial], use the full context views but a codec-free backbone.
2.  **Forward Pass:**
    *   *CT [S1]:* Project discretized volume to generate projected data $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$.
    *   *Feed-Forward [S3, unofficial]:* Extract multi-view features $\mathbf{F}_{mv}$ and encoder-side depth $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$ from context views $\mathcal{C}$ using a multi-view encoder $\mathcal{M}_{enc}$:
        $$ (\mathbf{F}_{mv}, \mathbf{D}_{enc}, \mathbf{P}_{depth}) = \mathcal{M}_{enc}(\mathcal{C}) $$
        Generate Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ from refined feature $\mathbf{F}_c$ and $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$.
3.  **Rendering:** Render target view $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t$ using camera parameters $K_t, T_t$:
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}, K_t, T_t) $$
4.  **Loss Calculation:**
    *   *CT [S1]:* Minimize L2 norm between projected data $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and measurements $\mathbf{p}$:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \sum_{i=1}^{N} || \hat{\mathbf{p}}[\mathcal{V}(\cdot|\{\theta_i\}_{i=1}^N)] - \mathbf{p} ||_2^2 $$
    *   *Inverse Rendering (GS-IR) [S2]:* Minimize color loss $L_c$ and normal loss $L_n$:
        $$ L_{init} = L_c + L_n $$
        Where $L_n$ includes a total variation (TV) smoothing term for normals:
        $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} \text{TV}_{normal} $$
    *   *Compression (CodecSplat) [S3, unofficial]:* Minimize rendering reconstruction loss $D_{render}$:
        $$ L_{stage1} = D_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf}, \mathbf{Y}_t) $$
5.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using an optimizer (e.g., Adam [S2][S3, unofficial]) for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 30,000 for GS-IR [S2]).

#### Stage 2: Refinement and Constraint Application
The goal is to refine parameters for specific properties (material, compression rate) while keeping geometry stable [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

1.  **Freeze/Initialize:**
    *   *GS-IR [S2]:* Fix geometry $\mathbf{T} = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ and appearance $\mathbf{A}$.
    *   *CodecSplat [S3, unofficial]:* Initialize from Stage 1 checkpoint. Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner. Keep backbone modules fixed.
2.  **Forward Pass with Constraints:**
    *   *GS-IR [S2]:* Render with material parameters $\mathbf{M} = \{\mathbf{a}, \rho, m\}$.
    *   *CodecSplat [S3, unofficial]:* Encode features, decode to $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$, and render $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t$. Compute codec-side depth supervision.
3.  **Loss Calculation:**
    *   *GS-IR [S2]:* Minimize decomposition loss $L_d$:
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M \text{TV}_{mat} + \lambda_E \text{TV}_{light} $$
        Where $L_{shade}$ models shading based on material and lighting, and TV terms smooth material and light fields.
    *   *CodecSplat [S3, unofficial]:* Minimize rate-distortion loss $L_{stage2}(\lambda)$:
        $$ L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ D_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t, \mathbf{Y}_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} + \beta R \right] $$
        Where $D_{codec-depth} = ||\mathbf{S} - \mathbf{S}_{enc}||_1$. The normalized inverse depth $\mathbf{S}_{enc}$ is calculated as:
        $$ \mathbf{S}_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{\mathbf{D}_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - \mathbf{f}_i^{-1}}{\mathbf{n}_i^{-1} - \mathbf{f}_i^{-1}} $$
        ($\mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{f}_i$ are near/far bounds).
4.  **Optimization:** Update only the unfrozen parameters (e.g., material $\mathbf{M}$ or codec modules) for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 10,000 for GS-IR [S2]).

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Iterations:** Stage 1: 30,000 [S2]; Stage 2: 10,000 [S2]. (Not specified for S1, S3).
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Weight Decay:** 0.01 [S3, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights (GS-IR):** $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$, $\lambda_M = 1.0$, $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2].
*   **Loss Weights (CodecSplat):** $\gamma = 0.1$ (depth supervision), $\beta = 10^{-4}$ (DL3DV) or $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (RE10K) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Rate Parameter ($\lambda$):** Sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training [S3, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA V100 [S2]; Four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB [S3, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** Normalized inverse depth calculation requires careful handling of near/far plane bounds to avoid division by zero or numerical instability [S3, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Low-resolution grid sampling in Stage 1 (CT) may miss fine details if the grid is too coarse, requiring careful balancing of resolution vs. speed [S1].
*   **Gradient Conflicts:** Freezing parameters in Stage 2 is critical; if geometry is not frozen in inverse rendering, material optimization may distort the shape [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Significantly faster than joint optimization because Stage 1 converges quickly on a simplified problem (low-res or codec-free), and Stage 2 optimizes fewer parameters [S1, S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stage 1 may use less memory if low-resolution grids are employed [S1]. CodecSplat reduces final storage via compression but requires GPU memory for the encoder/decoder pipeline [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves higher PSNR/SSIM than single-stage methods by avoiding local minima caused by conflicting gradients [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. CodecSplat achieves KiB-level bitstreams with minimal quality loss [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Stability, modularity (can swap codecs or material models), high-quality reconstruction.
*   **Limitations:** Requires two distinct training phases; hyperparameter tuning for loss weights ($\lambda, \beta, \gamma$) is necessary [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Two-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address the instability of training complex representations (like compressed or physically based Gaussians) in a single pass.
*   **GS-IR (2024):** Introduced the geometry-appearance-material split to enable inverse rendering [S2].
*   **CodecSplat (2025/2026):** Proposed the codec-free/codec-aware split to enable ultra-compact latent coding for feed-forward 3DGS [S3, unofficial].
*   **3DGR-CT (2025):** Applied low-res to full-res refinement for sparse-view CT reconstruction [S1].
