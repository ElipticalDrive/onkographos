# DashGaussian

Also called: Computational resource allocation for 3DGS

## Overview
DashGaussian is a scheduling scheme for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that accelerates optimization by reformulating the process as a frequency component fitting task [S1]. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count to strip redundant complexity, allowing the model to progressively fit higher frequency components while synchronizing primitive growth with rendering resolution [S1]. Unlike standard 3DGS which applies densification simultaneously based on static thresholds, DashGaussian decouples these operations via a dynamic resolution schedule and a momentum-based primitive budgeting method, yielding fewer primitives and faster convergence without trading off rendering quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3DGS backbone (e.g., Taming-3DGS, Mip-Splatting), training images $I$, camera poses, and initial SfM points.
*   **Output:** Optimized Gaussian primitives $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^{P_{fin}}$ with parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Primitives:** Each primitive $i$ is defined by position $\mu_i$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (or scale/rotation), opacity $\alpha_i$, and color coefficients $c_i$.
*   **Resolution Factor:** A continuous scalar $r(i)$ at iteration $i$, where the effective rendering resolution is $H/r(i) \times W/r(i)$.
*   **Primitive Budget:** A dynamic maximum primitive count $P_{max}(i)$ estimated via momentum.

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation for a pixel $u$ remains standard [S1]:
$$ C(u) = \sum_{i=1}^P T_i(u) \cdot w_i(u) \cdot c_i $$
where $T_i(u) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - w_j(u))$ is the transmittance, and $w_i(u) = \alpha_i G_{i2D}(u)$ is the weight, with $G_{i2D}(u)$ being the 2D Gaussian splatted to the image plane.

The optimization complexity is defined as the collective rendering resolution and number of Gaussian primitives [S1]. DashGaussian formulates optimization as progressively fitting 3DGS to higher levels of frequency components in the training views [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Target Optimization Time:** 200 seconds (achieved on consumer-grade GPUs with Taming-3DGS backbone) [S1].
*   **Resolution Modulation:** The continuous $r(i)$ is modulated to integer steps: $r_{step}(i) = \lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ [S1]. This encourages primitives to grow relatively faster to optimize each primitive more sufficiently [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialize:** Start with initial SfM points. Set iteration $t=0$. Initialize momentum buffer for primitive budget $M_0 = 0$.
2.  **Compute Resolution Factor:** Determine the dynamic rendering resolution factor $r(t)$ for the current iteration. This factor is scene-adaptive and increases over time to fit higher frequencies [S1].
3.  **Downsample Training Images:** To avoid 3D aliasing and artifacts, apply anti-aliasing downsampling to the training images $I$ using the factor $r_{step}(t) = \lfloor r(t) \rfloor$ [S1].
    *   *Implementation Note:* Center crop the Fourier transform $F$ of the image to size $(H/r_{step}, W/r_{step})$, scale numerical values by $1/r_{step}^2$, then inverse transform to obtain the low-resolution image $I_r$ [S1].
4.  **Render and Compute Loss:** Render the scene using the current primitives and the downsampled resolution $r_{step}(t)$. Compute the loss (e.g., L1 + SSIM) between the rendered image and $I_r$ [S1].
5.  **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients $\nabla G_t$ for the Gaussian parameters with respect to the loss [S1].
6.  **Update Primitives:** Perform the standard 3DGS update step (Split and Clone operations) based on gradients [S1].
7.  **Estimate Primitive Budget:** Use a momentum-based method to adaptively estimate the maximum number of primitives $P_{max}(t)$ for the current growth curve, avoiding dataset priors [S1].
    *   *Note:* The sources describe the scheduler producing a "concave-up curve" for primitive growth spanning the entire process, contrasting with concave-down or peak-at-half curves in other methods [S1]. The exact momentum update formula is not provided in the text, but it is used to bound the primitive count [S1].
8.  **Prune if Necessary:** If the current number of primitives exceeds $P_{max}(t)$, prune primitives based on opacity or other standard criteria to maintain the budget [S1].
9.  **Increment:** $t \leftarrow t + 1$. Repeat until convergence or target time reached.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Aliasing:** Simply reducing resolution without anti-aliasing causes 3D aliasing, sacrificing fidelity at the original resolution [S1].
*   **Low-Frequency Loss:** Improper downsampling can disturb recoverable low-frequency components [S1]. The low-pass filter (via Fourier cropping) is critical [S1].
*   **Primitive Count:** Without the momentum-based budgeting, the method might rely on dataset priors, which are not scene-adaptive [S1].

def dash_gaussian_optimize(backbone, images, poses, initial_points, total_iterations):
    """
    Optimizes 3D Gaussian Splatting using DashGaussian scheduling.
    
    Args:
        backbone: The 3DGS backbone implementation (e.g., Taming-3DGS).
        images: List of training images.
        poses: List of camera poses.
        initial_points: Initial SfM points.
        total_iterations: Total number of optimization steps.
        
    Returns:
        Optimized Gaussian primitives.
    """
    # Initialize Gaussians from SfM
    gaussians = initialize_gaussians(initial_points)
    
    # Momentum buffer for primitive budget estimation
    # [G] Initialized to zero; exact formula for momentum update not provided in sources.
    momentum_buffer = 0.0
    
    for t in range(total_iterations):
        # 1. Compute dynamic resolution factor r(t)
        # [G] The specific function for r(t) is scene-adaptive and not explicitly defined in the text.
        # It is assumed to be a function of t and scene frequency content.
        r_t = compute_resolution_factor(t, images)
        
        # 2. Modulate to integer step
        r_step = floor(r_t)
        
        # 3. Anti-aliasing downsampling
        # Center crop Fourier transform, scale by 1/r_step^2, inverse transform
        low_res_images = []
        for img in images:
            F = fft2(img)
            H, W = F.shape
            crop_H, crop_W = H // r_step, W // r_step
            # Center crop
            start_h, start_w = (H - crop_H) // 2, (W - crop_W) // 2
            F_cropped = F[start_h:start_h+crop_H, start_w:start_w+crop_W]
            # Scale and inverse transform
            F_scaled = F_cropped / (r_step ** 2)
            img_lr = ifft2(F_scaled).real
            low_res_images.append(img_lr)
            
        # 4. Render and Compute Loss
        # Render using current gaussians and low_res_images
        rendered_images = backbone.render(gaussians, poses, resolution_factor=r_step)
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_images, low_res_images)
        
        # 5. Backpropagate
        gradients = backbone.backward(loss, gaussians)
        
        # 6. Update Primitives (Split/Clone)
        # Standard 3DGS densification logic
        gaussians = backbone.update(gaussians, gradients)
        
        # 7. Estimate Primitive Budget
        # [G] Momentum-based estimation logic is not explicitly detailed in the source text.
        # It is described as adapting the maximum number of primitives in the growth curve.
        # We assume a placeholder update for the momentum buffer and budget calculation.
        current_primitive_count = len(gaussians)
        momentum_buffer = update_momentum(momentum_buffer, current_primitive_count)
        p_max = estimate_budget(momentum_buffer)
        
        # 8. Prune if necessary
        if current_primitive_count > p_max:
            gaussians = prune_gaussians(gaussians, p_max)
            
    return gaussians

    It is described as a continuous scalar that increases over time.
    """

def update_momentum(buffer, value):
    """
    Updates the momentum buffer.
    [G] The specific momentum formula is not provided in the source.
    """
    return 0.9 * buffer + 0.1 * value

def estimate_budget(momentum_buffer):
    """
    Estimates the maximum primitive count based on momentum.
    [G] The specific mapping from momentum to budget is not provided in the source.
    """
    return int(momentum_buffer * 1000000) # Placeholder scaling

def prune_gaussians(gaussians, max_count):
    """
    Prunes Gaussians to stay within the budget.
    [G] Standard pruning based on opacity or gradient magnitude is assumed.
    """
    # Sort by opacity and remove the lowest opacity primitives
    opacities = [g.opacity for g in gaussians]
    indices = sorted(range(len(opacities)), key=lambda k: opacities[k])
    num_to_remove = len(gaussians) - max_count
    if num_to_remove > 0:
        gaussians = [g for i, g in enumerate(gaussians) if i not in indices[:num_to_remove]]
    return gaussians

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** DashGaussian reduces the per-iteration elapsed time for forward rendering, gradient back-propagation, and primitive optimization by lowering the effective resolution in early stages [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** By scheduling primitive growth and using a momentum-based budget, it maintains a lower average primitive count compared to vanilla 3DGS, reducing memory usage [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   Average acceleration rate across tested backbones: 45.7% [S1].
    *   Optimization time reduction for Taming-3DGS backbone: 41.3% [S1].
    *   Achieves optimization in 200 seconds on a consumer-grade GPU for scenes with millions of primitives [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is plug-and-play and generalizes to various backbones (Mip-Splatting, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS) with different optimizers and primitive representations [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Preserves or improves rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM) while significantly reducing training time [S1].
    *   Scene-adaptive scheduling avoids dataset priors [S1].
    *   Concave-up primitive growth curve spans the entire optimization process, providing better balance than concave-down or peak-at-half curves [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires careful implementation of anti-aliasing downsampling to avoid artifacts [S1].
    *   The exact formula for the resolution factor $r(t)$ and the momentum-based budget estimation are not explicitly detailed in the source, requiring implementation inference [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When training speed is critical and a 3DGS backbone is being used. It is particularly effective for large-scale or time-intensive reconstruction tasks [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** If the target hardware cannot support the dynamic resolution changes or if the exact implementation details of the schedulers are not available and cannot be inferred.

## Background
DashGaussian was proposed by Youyu Chen, Junjun Jiang, Kui Jiang, Xiao Tang, Zhihao Li, Xianming Liu, and Yinyu Nie from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab [S1]. It was published in CVPR 2025 (arXiv:2503.18402v2, March 26, 2025) [S1]. The work addresses the high computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization by treating it as a frequency component fitting problem [S1]. It builds on the principles of 3DGS densification but introduces a novel scheduling mechanism to optimize the trade-off between resolution and primitive count [S1].
