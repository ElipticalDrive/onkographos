# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed 3D Gaussian Splatting Training, Multi-GPU 3DGS Optimization

## Overview
This family encompasses methods for accelerating the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) through parallel computation and distributed memory management. It solves the bottleneck of training on scenes with billions of primitives or high-resolution views, which exceed single-GPU memory and compute limits. It is distinguished from standard optimization by explicitly partitioning data across devices or restructuring the backward pass to minimize atomic contention, building on [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md).

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing gradients through probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly accounting for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground accumulation and the background transmittance path, solving the backpropagation problem for masked alpha-blending.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself by adapting the representation density to scene complexity via axis-slice rendering and gradient-based refinement checks.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It distinguishes itself by iterating over Gaussians to update pixel gradients, addressing the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in the per-pixel approach, yielding a 4–5× reduction in training time.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs. It distinguishes itself from single-machine streaming methods like [TideGS](7350-tidegs.md) by distributing the parameter table across a GPU cluster, enabling training on scenes with billions of primitives via inter-GPU communication.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details the specific hyperparameters, data structures, and optimization schedules for the standard 3DGS training pipeline. It distinguishes itself by focusing on the *configuration* of the standard algorithm (e.g., SH degree, learning rates, densification intervals) rather than novel architectural changes.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . The initial implementation focused on single-GPU efficiency. As applications expanded to large-scale scenes (e.g., New York City, large-scale photogrammetry), memory limits became the primary constraint, leading to the development of distributed frameworks like Grendel-GS and parallelization techniques for the backward pass. The training pipeline's adaptive densification strategy, originally proposed in the foundational paper, remains central to all optimization variants .
