# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Priors, Surface Regularization, Frequency Constraints

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevent over-reconstruction, floating artifacts, and geometric instability by imposing structural, spectral, or physical priors on the optimization process. Unlike standard photometric losses that only minimize pixel-wise color differences, these methods explicitly constrain the shape, position, or frequency content of the Gaussians to ensure the resulting scene represents a coherent surface or stable volume. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on geometric consistency rather than physical plausibility, and from [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) by modifying the loss landscape or parameter constraints rather than the primitive count.

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of improving geometric fidelity and training stability. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Decoupling Shape and Position:** Several methods freeze scale/rotation parameters during depth alignment to prevent shape deformation from conflicting with color appearance [S1, 9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md].
2.  **Surface Alignment:** Techniques encourage Gaussians to flatten into surfels or align with estimated surface normals, reducing volumetric blob artifacts [S1, 9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md][S2, 14222-geometry-regularization.md].
3.  **Frequency/Spectral Control:** Methods operate in the Fourier domain to penalize high-frequency noise or discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth spectra, guiding densification toward meaningful details [S2, 6130-frequency-regularization.md][S1, 9212-fregs.md].
4.  **Multi-Scale Consistency:** Some approaches use reprojection errors across voxel scales to select pseudo-ground truth depths, ensuring geometric integrity without explicit depth sensors [S1, 10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the spectral discrepancy between rendered and ground-truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction. It operates in the Fourier domain, disentangling amplitude and phase to increase gradients in over-reconstructed regions where naive L1 loss fails. It sets itself apart from [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) by targeting spectral components directly rather than blurring the image space.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
This technique enhances robustness by smoothing the 2D supervision signal with a scheduled Gaussian kernel. It solves unstable gradients and poor camera alignment caused by high-frequency noise in raw images. Unlike [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) which works in the spectral domain, Smoothed 2D Supervision explicitly blurs ground-truth images to restrict the spectrum of the learned scene.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method transforms standard 3D Gaussian kernels into "half-Gaussians" to model surface geometry and sharp transitions. It regularizes the covariance matrix to align with local surface normals, effectively cutting the Gaussian ellipsoid in half. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by enforcing geometric consistency through kernel shape parameters rather than image-space frequency spectra.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
This strategy prevents geometric detail loss by decoupling the optimization of Gaussian centers from their shape parameters. It freezes scales and rotations during depth alignment, using "hard" depth for position updates and "soft" depth for opacity updates. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating on spatial primitive distribution rather than image frequency spectra.

### [FreGS](9212-fregs.md)
FreGS prevents overfitting and high-frequency artifacts by penalizing frequency discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth images. It addresses over-reconstruction by explicitly aligning the spectral content of the output with the input. Unlike sibling methods focusing on spatial gradients, FreGS operates directly in the frequency domain.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
This self-adaptive technique uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning in few-shot novel view synthesis. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error as pseudo-labels, ensuring geometric integrity without explicit depth ground truth. It adapts multi-scale voxel geometry by leveraging low-resolution voxels for coarse guidance and high-resolution voxels for detail refinement.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
This technique improves surface smoothness and reduces floating artifacts by constraining the geometric shape of the Gaussians. It focuses on surface orientation and anisotropy, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains location. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating on covariance parameters rather than the image frequency spectrum.

### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.
- [S2] Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.
- [S2] $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

### MVG-Splatting
- [S4] A method that employs a depth map to enforce geometric consistency, specifically applying geometric consistency only in the near and far regions of the scene to effectively mitigate under-reconstruction in these critical regions while avoiding the risk of over-reconstruction.

### Shape Energy Constraint
- [S5] An energy term defined as the combination of opacity energy and boundary energy, used to preserve the shape, opacity, and unitized colors (spherical harmonics) of Gaussian radiance fields during deformation.
- [S5] Relying solely on feature energy is insufficient to preserve opacity and unitized colors; gray Gaussians with SH values close to zero appear and their size becomes uncontrolled because they cannot be constrained by feature energy alone.
- [S5] Without $E_{shape}$, the optimization process becomes unstable and artifacts appear; with $E_{shape}$, the process stabilizes and these artifacts are mitigated.

## Background
The need for regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the method's reliance on photometric loss, which often results in unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces [S3]. Early extensions introduced physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), to recover high-quality geometry [S3]. Subsequent works explored frequency-domain regularization to stabilize optimization and prevent overfitting [S1, 9212-fregs.md]. Techniques like Smoothed 2D Supervision were developed to handle high-frequency noise in training images, while half-Gaussian transformations were proposed to better model surface boundaries [S1, 9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md]. The family has evolved to include multi-scale geometric adaptation and shape energy constraints to address specific issues like transient occlusions and deformation stability [S1, S5].
