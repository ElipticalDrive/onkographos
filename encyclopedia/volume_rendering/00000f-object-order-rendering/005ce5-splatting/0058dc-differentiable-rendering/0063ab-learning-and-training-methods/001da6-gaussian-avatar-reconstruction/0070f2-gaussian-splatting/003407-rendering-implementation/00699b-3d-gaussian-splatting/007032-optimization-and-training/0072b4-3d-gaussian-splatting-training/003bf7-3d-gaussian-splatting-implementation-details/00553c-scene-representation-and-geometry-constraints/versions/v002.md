# Scene Representation and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
This family defines the fundamental data structures and geometric constraints used to represent scenes in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its variants. It addresses the problem of defining learnable primitives and ensuring their geometric consistency, particularly when integrating explicit mesh structures. It differs from sibling methods by focusing on the core mathematical definition of the Gaussian primitives and the constraints applied to their physical parameters, whereas siblings like [Sorting and Rendering Pipeline Optimization](12180-sorting-and-rendering-pipeline-optimization.md) focus on rendering efficiency, and [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) focuses on post-processing or surface alignment strategies.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of $N$ 3D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $i$ is defined by learnable parameters describing its geometry, appearance, and position.

**Data Structure:**
Each Gaussian $g_i$ is represented by the following attributes [S1]:
*   **Position ($\mu_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing the centroid in world space.
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):** A unit quaternion $\mathbb{R}^4$ representing orientation.
*   **Scale ($s_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing anisotropic scaling factors along principal axes.
*   **Opacity ($\omega_i$):** A scalar $\mathbb{R} \in [0, 1]$ representing maximum opacity.
*   **Color ($c_i$):** Represented by Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients (typically degree 3, 48 coefficients for RGB) for view-dependent appearance.

**Covariance Matrix:**
The spatial spread is defined by a 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$, constructed from scale and rotation:
$$ \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T $$
where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ is the scaling matrix and $R_i$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q_i$ [S1].

**Projection to 2D:**
During rendering, the 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ is projected to the 2D image plane using the Jacobian $J$ of the affine approximation of the projective transformation:
$$ \Sigma' = J \Sigma_i J^T $$
The relevant part for rasterization is the top-left $2 \times 2$ submatrix $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}$ [S1].

**Spatial Response (Kernel Function):**
The contribution of a Gaussian to a pixel at screen coordinates $x$ is determined by:
$$ R(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu')^T (\Sigma'_{2 \times 2})^{-1} (x - \mu')\right) $$
where $\mu'$ is the projected 2D mean [S1].

**Alpha Compositing:**
The final opacity $\alpha$ contributed by a Gaussian is:
$$ \alpha = \omega_i \cdot R(x) $$
The pixel color is computed via alpha blending with primitives sorted back-to-front:
$$ C_{pixel} = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i C_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j < i} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $C_i$ is evaluated using SH coefficients based on view direction [S1].

**Truncation:**
To optimize speed, fragments are discarded if $\alpha < \tau_\alpha$ (typically $1/255$), limiting rendering to a region of approximately $3.33\sigma$ [S1].

**Density Estimation for Mesh Extraction:**
The scene density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is the superposition of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^T \Sigma_i^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)\right) $$
where $\alpha_i$ is opacity, $\mathbf{p}_i$ is the mean, and $\Sigma_i$ is the covariance [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md)
Defines the fundamental data structure and rendering primitives used in 3DGS. It specifies how a scene is represented as a set of $N$ learnable 3D Gaussians, detailing the parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color), the construction of the covariance matrix, and the projection to 2D for rasterization. It also defines the standard truncation logic used to optimize rendering speed by discarding fragments with negligible contribution. This representation serves as the foundation for all sibling methods in this family. [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md)

### [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md)
Converts the volumetric, implicit representation of 3DGS into explicit geometric structures, primarily triangular meshes or point clouds, to enable compatibility with standard 3D graphics pipelines. Unlike standard 3DGS, which renders via alpha-composited splats, these methods extract a discrete surface or point set that approximates the scene geometry. [SuGaR Mesh Extraction](12580-sugar-mesh-extraction.md) achieves this by regularizing Gaussians to align with the surface during training, while [3D Gaussian Splatting to Point Cloud](14068-3d-gaussian-splatting-to-point-cloud.md) samples points from the Gaussian distribution post-training. [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md)

### Scale Constrained by Mesh Geometry
A constraint to prevent discrepancies between Gaussian Splat scales and the scales of corresponding triangular mesh faces by bounding the relative scale coefficients. The final scale $s$ is computed as $s = s_{rel} \odot s_0$, where $s_{rel} = (s_{rel,1}, s_{rel,2}) \in [s_{min}, s_{max}] \times [s_{min}, s_{max}]$ is the coefficient vector, $s_0$ is the predefined scale derived from the mesh faces, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication. The bounds $s_{min}$ and $s_{max}$ define the allowable range for the relative scale coefficients [S1].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [G]. The integration of explicit mesh geometry constraints, such as scale bounding relative to mesh faces, was proposed in OMeGa (Joint Optimization of Explicit Meshes and Gaussian Splats for Robust Scene-Level Surface Reconstruction) [S1].
