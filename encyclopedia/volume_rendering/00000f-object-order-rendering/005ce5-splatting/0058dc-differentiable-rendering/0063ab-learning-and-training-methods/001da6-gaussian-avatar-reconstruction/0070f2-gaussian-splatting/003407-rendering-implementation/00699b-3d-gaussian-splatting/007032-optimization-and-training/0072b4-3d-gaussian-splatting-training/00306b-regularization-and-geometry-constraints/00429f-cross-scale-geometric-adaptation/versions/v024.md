# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth or complex scheduling [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo-labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training images (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses (rotation $R$, translation $t$) for frames $i$ and $j$, multi-scale voxel representations (low, mid, high resolution), and current Gaussian/voxel parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$, which is added to the total training loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxels:** The scene is represented by voxels at multiple resolutions (low, mid, high). Low-resolution voxels capture coarse geometry, while high-resolution voxels capture fine details [S1].
*   **RGB Maps:** $C_i$ and $C_j$ denote the rendered RGB maps for frames $i$ and $j$ [S1].
*   **Depth Maps:** $D_{il}(p_i)$ denotes the depth at pixel $p_i$ for scale $l$ obtained from volume rendering [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Select Training Rays:** Sample rays $r_{train}$ from the training views [S1].
2.  **Compute 3D Points in Camera Coordinates:** For a 2D pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$ and its homogeneous augmentation $\tilde{p}_i$, compute the 3D point in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$ for each scale $l$:
    $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    where $D_{il}(p_i)$ is the depth at scale $l$ from volume rendering and $K_i$ are the camera intrinsics [S1].
3.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform the 3D point $x_{li}$ to world coordinates using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]
4.  **Transform to Target Camera Coordinates:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]
5.  **Project to 2D Pixel Coordinates:** Project the 3D point $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]$ [S1].
6.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Compute the reprojection error for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    where $C_i$ and $C_j$ are the RGB maps of frames $i$ and $j$ [S1].
7.  **Select Pseudo-Ground Truth Depth:** Identify the scale $l'$ with the minimum reprojection error for each ray:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]
8.  **Compute Geometric Adaptation Loss:** Calculate the loss as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the current depth estimate $D_l(r_{train})$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ across all training rays:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{r_{train} \in R_{train}} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Scales:** Typically three scales are used: low, mid, and high [S1].
*   **Loss Weighting:** The loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ is weighted by $\lambda$ in the total loss function $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg}$ [S1]. The specific value of $\lambda$ is not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Poses:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may be challenging [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Scale Mismatches:** Using only depth priors ($\mathcal{L}_d$) can suffer from higher error later in training due to scale mismatches, whereas $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ refines depth progressively [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** In FrugalNeRF, $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$-only achieves lower final depth error compared to depth prior $\mathcal{L}_d$ alone [S1]. Combining $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and $\mathcal{L}_d$ yields the best results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly reduces floaters, improves convergence quality across scales, and provides a stronger and more robust constraint than depth priors [S1]. It eliminates the need for complex scheduling and ground truth pose sampling [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Few-shot NeRF methods relying on this may face challenges in generalization with sparse training views or significant viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed in FrugalNeRF [S1].
*   **History:** The method was introduced to accelerate and regularize training for extreme few-shot novel view synthesis without learned priors [S1]. It addresses the issue of geometric inconsistency and floaters common in few-shot scenarios [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in FrugalNeRF for few-shot NeRF training [S1].
