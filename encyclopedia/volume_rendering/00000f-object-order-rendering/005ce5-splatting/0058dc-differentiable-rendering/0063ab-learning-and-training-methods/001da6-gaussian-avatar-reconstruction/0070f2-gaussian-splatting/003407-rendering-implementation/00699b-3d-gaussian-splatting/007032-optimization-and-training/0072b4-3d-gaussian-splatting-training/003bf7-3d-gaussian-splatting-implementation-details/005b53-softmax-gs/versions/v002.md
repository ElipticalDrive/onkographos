# Softmax-GS

Also called: Softmax Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Softmax-GS is a unified rendering and optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that resolves view-inconsistency (popping artifacts) and diffuse boundaries by enforcing a softmax-based competition among overlapping Gaussians [S1]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which assumes non-overlapping primitives, Softmax-GS relaxes this assumption to allow a continuous spectrum of visual behaviors from smooth color blending to sharp, winner-take-all boundaries [S1]. It differs from [DeSplat](12925-desplat.md) and [360 Gaussian Splatting](12929-360-gaussian-splatting.md) by directly modeling overlap via softmax weights rather than re-sorting or stochastic mixing, and from [EVER](12925-desplat.md) by maintaining near-real-time rendering speeds while providing controllable boundary sharpness [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Camera parameters, a set of $K$ 3D Gaussians defined by mean $\mu_k$, covariance $\Sigma_k$, opacity exponent $p_k$ (where opacity $o_k \sim \exp(p_k)$), and color $c_k$ [S1].
**Outputs:** Rendered pixel color $I(\mathbf{x})$ and gradients for optimization [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** For each Gaussian $k$, store:
    *   $p_k$: Exponent (scalar).
    *   $a_k$: Integrated 1D absorbance along the ray (scalar, $a_k = \int o_k dl$).
    *   $d_k$: Depth of the Gaussian center projected onto the ray (scalar).
    *   $\alpha_k$: Boundary sharpness parameter (scalar, learnable).
*   **Intermediate State (per ray/patch):**
    *   $T_k$: Current transmittance (scalar).
    *   $a_{past}$: Accumulated absorbance of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   $d_{past}$: Moving average depth of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   $p_{past}$: Moving average exponent of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   CUDA arrays of length $K$ (typically 128) to cache intermediate values ($a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}, T_k$) for the backward pass [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\beta$: Softmax competition strength. $\beta=0$ yields equal blending; large $\beta$ yields sharp boundaries [S1].
*   $\gamma$: Decay rate for competition based on depth difference [S1].
*   $\alpha$: Learnable parameter for individual Gaussian boundary sharpness via Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) [S1].
*   $K$: Hyperparameter for the number of closest Gaussians processed with Softmax-GS logic. Typical value: 128 [S1].
*   Pruning threshold: Raised to 0.4 to accommodate GEF-shrunk splats [S1].

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) for Absorbance:**
    Replace the standard squared distance $\|\cdot\|^2$ in the Gaussian kernel with $\|\cdot\|^{2\alpha}$ to control individual boundary sharpness [S1]. This modifies the absorbance $a_k$ but does not change the exponent $p_k$ used in softmax competition [S1].
    $$ o_k(\mathbf{x}, l) \propto \exp(-\|\mathbf{x} - \mu_k\|^{2\alpha}) $$
    Compute $a_k = \int_0^L o_k(\mathbf{x}, l) dl$ [S1].

2.  **Sequential Processing (Forward Pass):**
    Process Gaussians sorted from nearest to farthest [S1]. For each Gaussian $k$:
    
    a. **Compute Softmax Weights for Pairwise Competition:**
    Treat all previously processed Gaussians as a single "past" entity with aggregate properties $a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}$ [S1]. Compute the softmax weight $w_k$ between the current Gaussian $k$ and the past entity using their exponents $p_k$ and $p_{past}$:
    $$ w_k = \frac{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k)}{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k) + \exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})} $$
    $$ w_{past} = \frac{\exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})}{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k) + \exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})} $$
    Competition operates on $p_k$ because $o_k \in [0, \infty)$, making $\exp(o_k) \ge 1$, which prevents weights from diminishing [S1].

    b. **Compute Fully Overlapping Absorbances ($\hat{a}$):**
    Apply the softmax weights to the individual absorbances:
    $$ \hat{a}_k = w_k a_k $$
    $$ \hat{a}_{past} = w_{past} a_{past} $$

    c. **Interpolate with Depth Decay:**
    Calculate the exponential decay factor $s$ based on the depth difference $|d_k - d_{past}|$:
    $$ s = \exp(-\gamma |d_k - d_{past}|) $$
    Interpolate between the fully overlapping absorbances ($\hat{a}$) and the original non-softmax absorbances ($a$):
    $$ \bar{a}_k = s \hat{a}_k + (1-s) a_k $$
    $$ \bar{a}_{past} = s \hat{a}_{past} + (1-s) a_{past} $$

    d. **Correction for Order Invariance and Transmittance:**
    The interpolation may violate order invariance (second Gaussian's color discounted by $(1-\bar{a}_{past})$) and transmittance consistency (output transmittance higher than original) [S1]. Solve for corrected absorbances $\tilde{a}_{past}$ and $\tilde{a}_k$ that satisfy:
    $$ (1-\tilde{a}_{past})(1-\tilde{a}_k) = T_k $$
    $$ \tilde{a}_{past} = \frac{\hat{a}_{past}(1-T_k)}{\hat{a}_{past} + \hat{a}_k} $$
    $$ \tilde{a}_k = \frac{\hat{a}_k(1-T_k)}{\hat{a}_k + \tilde{a}_{past}T_k} $$
    Apply the interpolation step to $\tilde{a}_{past}$ and $\tilde{a}_k$ to get $\bar{a}_{past}$ and $\bar{a}_k$ [S1]. Finally, scale them by a factor $m$ to restore exact transmittance $T_k$:
    $$ (1-m \bar{a}_{past})(1-m \bar{a}_k) = T_k $$
    $$ \bar{a}_{past} \leftarrow m \bar{a}_{past}, \quad \bar{a}_k \leftarrow m \bar{a}_k $$

    e. **Update State:**
    Update the moving averages and transmittance:
    $$ T_{k+1} = T_k (1 - \bar{a}_k) $$
    $$ a_{past} \leftarrow a_{past} + \bar{a}_k $$
    $$ d_{past} \leftarrow \text{weighted average of } d_{past} \text{ and } d_k $$
    $$ p_{past} \leftarrow \text{weighted average of } p_{past} \text{ and } p_k $$
    Cache these intermediate values in the CUDA arrays [S1].

3.  **Rendering:**
    The final color is computed using the standard splatting formulation with the corrected absorbances:
    $$ I(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^K c_k \bar{a}_k \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} (1 - \bar{a}_j) $$
    (Note: In the sequential implementation, this is accumulated incrementally as $I \leftarrow I + c_k \bar{a}_k T_k$) [S1].

4.  **Backward Pass:**
    To preserve linear complexity, access the cached intermediate values from the forward pass CUDA arrays directly [S1]. Compute gradients for $p_k, a_k, \alpha_k$ without recomputing the forward pass [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def softmax_gs_forward(gaussians_sorted, beta=2.0, gamma=1.0, K_cache_size=128):
    """
    Forward pass for Softmax-GS.
    gaussians_sorted: List of dicts/objects sorted by depth (nearest first).
    Each element must have: 'p' (exponent), 'a' (absorbance), 'd' (depth), 'c' (color).
    """
    # Initialize state
    T = 1.0
    a_past = 0.0
    d_past = 0.0
    p_past = 0.0
    
    # Cache arrays for backward pass
    cache_T = []
    cache_a_past = []
    cache_d_past = []
    cache_p_past = []
    
    rendered_color = 0.0
    
    for k, g in enumerate(gaussians_sorted):
        p_k = g['p']
        a_k = g['a']
        d_k = g['d']
        c_k = g['c']
        
        if k == 0:
            # First Gaussian: no past entity to compete with
            bar_a_k = a_k
        else:
            # 1. Compute Softmax Weights
            exp_p_k = math.exp(beta * p_k)
            exp_p_past = math.exp(beta * p_past)
            denom = exp_p_k + exp_p_past
            w_k = exp_p_k / denom
            w_past = exp_p_past / denom
            
            # 2. Compute Fully Overlapping Absorbances
            hat_a_k = w_k * a_k
            hat_a_past = w_past * a_past
            
            # 3. Interpolate with Depth Decay
            depth_diff = abs(d_k - d_past)
            s = math.exp(-gamma * depth_diff)
            
            bar_a_k = s * hat_a_k + (1 - s) * a_k
            bar_a_past = s * hat_a_past + (1 - s) * a_past
            
            # 4. Correction for Order Invariance and Transmittance
            tilde_a_past = (hat_a_past * (1 - T)) / (hat_a_past + hat_a_k)
            tilde_a_k = (hat_a_k * (1 - T)) / (hat_a_k + tilde_a_past * T)
            
            # Re-apply interpolation to corrected values
            bar_a_k = s * tilde_a_k + (1 - s) * a_k
            bar_a_past = s * tilde_a_past + (1 - s) * a_past
            
            # 5. Scale to restore exact transmittance T
            # Solve (1 - m*A)(1 - m*B) = T for m, where A=bar_a_past, B=bar_a_k
            A = bar_a_past
            B = bar_a_k
            if A * B > 1e-9:
                disc = (A + B)**2 - 4 * A * B * (1 - T)
                if disc < 0: disc = 0.0
                sqrt_disc = math.sqrt(disc)
                m = ((A + B) - sqrt_disc) / (2 * A * B)
            else:
                # Linear fallback if one absorbance is near zero
                m = (1 - T) / (A + B) if (A + B) > 1e-9 else 1.0
                
            m = max(0.0, min(1.0, m))
            bar_a_k *= m
            bar_a_past *= m
            
        # Accumulate color
        rendered_color += c_k * bar_a_k * T
        
        # Update transmittance
        T *= (1 - bar_a_k)
        
        # Update past entity state for next iteration
        a_past_old = a_past
        a_past += bar_a_k
        if a_past > 1e-9:
            d_past = (a_past_old * d_past + bar_a_k * d_k) / a_past
            p_past = (a_past_old * p_past + bar_a_k * p_k) / a_past
            
        # Cache for backward pass
        if len(cache_T) < K_cache_size:
            cache_T.append(T)
            cache_a_past.append(a_past)
            cache_d_past.append(d_past)
            cache_p_past.append(p_past)
            
    return rendered_color, cache_T, cache_a_past, cache_d_past, cache_p_past
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of Gaussians per ray for both forward and backward passes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires caching intermediate values ($a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}, T_k$) in CUDA arrays of length $K$ (typically 128) per patch for the backward pass [S1].
*   **Performance:** Adds approximately 1.2× increase in training time compared to standard 3D GS [S1]. Rendering speed is roughly 80% of standard 3D GS [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates view-inconsistency (popping) and allows sharp boundaries [S1]. Maintains order invariance and transmittance consistency [S1]. Integrates with GS-MCMC cloning [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Slightly slower than standard 3D GS [S1]. Requires tuning of $\beta, \gamma, \alpha$ [S1]. The approximation assumes Gaussians do not regain dominance after losing it [S1].
*   **When to use:** When view-inconsistency or blurry boundaries are problematic in 3D GS [S1]. When controllable boundary sharpness is needed [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When maximum rendering speed is critical and artifacts are acceptable [S1]. When scene has extreme overlap that violates the dominance assumption (though this is rare in practice) [S1].

## Background
Softmax-GS was proposed in "Softmax-GS: Generalized Gaussians Learning When to Blend or Bound" (2026) [S1]. It addresses limitations in 3D Gaussian Splatting, specifically the non-overlapping assumption which leads to view-inconsistency and diffuse boundaries [S1]. It builds on the Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) from GES and the softmax competition concept, but incorporates the exact GEF within a CUDA kernel and explicitly preserves order invariance while ensuring output transmittance remains unchanged [S1]. It is designed to be a unified solution for both artifacts, unlike methods that address only one [S1].
