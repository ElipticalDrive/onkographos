# GsplatVolumeLighting

Also called: Gaussian Splat Volume Ray Marching, Volume Occlusion for 3DGS

## Overview
GsplatVolumeLighting is a volume lighting and occlusion technique for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that ray-marches through a 3D density texture to compute transmittance for shadow and volume occlusion, combined with per-light attenuation and diffuse shading [S1, unofficial]. It extends the standard 3DGS rendering pipeline by introducing volumetric shadows and ambient occlusion-like effects, allowing splats to cast and receive shadows from a defined volume rather than relying solely on geometric primitives or screen-space techniques. Unlike sibling methods such as [4DGS-Volume-Cloud Two-Stage Relighting](4101-4dgs-volume-cloud-two-stage-relighting.md) which may use separate relighting stages, GsplatVolumeLighting integrates the shadow march directly into the lighting evaluation per splat, making it suitable for real-time interactive applications like those in the Unity engine [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   3D Gaussian splat data: position, covariance (for normal estimation), color, opacity.
    *   Camera parameters: view direction, projection matrix.
    *   Light parameters: up to 4 lights (directional, point, or spot), including position, direction, color, and type.
    *   Volume texture: `_GsplatVolumeDensity`, an R8 3D texture storing density.
    *   Volume parameters: `_GsplatVolumeOcclusionParams` (optical-depth scale, sample steps, start distance), `_GsplatVolumeBoundsMin/Max` (axis-aligned bounds), `_GsplatVolumeBoundsInvSize` (inverse dimensions).
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Final lit color for each splat, including ambient and direct lighting components modulated by volumetric transmittance.

**Data Structures:**
*   `_GsplatVolumeDensity`: `sampler3D` texture. The R8 channel encodes $\sqrt{\text{normalized optical depth}}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   `_GsplatVolumeOcclusionParams`: `float4` packing:
    *   `x`: Optical-depth scale (0 disables volume lighting).
    *   `y`: Number of sample steps (clamped to [1, 24]).
    *   `z`: Start distance for the ray march.
    *   `w`: Unused or reserved.
*   `_GsplatVolumeBoundsMin/Max`: `float4` defining the axis-aligned volume bounds in local space.
*   `_GsplatVolumeBoundsInvSize`: `float4` containing the inverse dimensions of the volume bounds ($1 / (\text{Max} - \text{Min})$).
*   `GSPLAT_VOLUME_OPAQUE_DEPTH`: Constant value $8.0$. Transmittance below $\exp(-8)$ is treated as fully opaque [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Light Direction Transformation:**
    Transform the light direction from world space to the local space of the volume using `_GsplatVolumeWorldToLocal`. Normalize the direction. If the length is $\le 10^{-6}$, return transmittance $1.0$ (no occlusion) [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Ray-Box Intersection:**
    Compute the intersection of the ray (originating from the splat center, direction towards the light) with the volume bounds.
    *   Calculate `directionSign` via `lerp(-1.0, 1.0, step(0.0, direction))` to handle negative directions.
    *   Clamp the ray direction magnitude to $10^{-5}$ to prevent division by zero [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute entry ($t_0$) and exit ($t_1$) distances against `BoundsMin` and `BoundsMax`.
    *   The ray length is `max(0.0, min(farDistance.x, min(farDistance.y, farDistance.z)))` [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Light Type Adjustment:**
    *   For point lights (`lightPosition.w > 0.5`), clamp the ray length to the Euclidean distance from the local center to the light position.
    *   Calculate `usableLength = rayLength - startDistance`. If `usableLength <= 10^{-5}`, return transmittance $1.0$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Sample Count and Step Size:**
    *   Determine `sampleCount = clamp(round(_GsplatVolumeOcclusionParams.y), 1, 24)`.
    *   Compute `stepLength = usableLength / sampleCount` [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Ray Marching Loop:**
    *   Initialize UVW coordinates at the midpoint of the first step:
        $$ \text{uvw} = (\text{localCenter} + \text{localDirection} \cdot (\text{startDistance} + 0.5 \cdot \text{stepLength}) - \text{boundsMin}) \cdot \text{invSize} $$
    *   Compute step delta: $\text{uvwStep} = \text{localDirection} \cdot \text{stepLength} \cdot \text{invSize}$.
    *   Initialize `opticalDepth = 0.0`.
    *   Loop `sampleIndex` from $0$ to `sampleCount - 1`:
        *   If `sampleIndex >= sampleCount` or `opticalDepth >= GSPLAT_VOLUME_OPAQUE_DEPTH`, break.
        *   If `uvw` is outside $[0, 1]$ bounds, skip sampling and advance `uvw += uvwStep`.
        *   Sample the R8 channel of `_GsplatVolumeDensity` at `uvw`. Let $d_i$ be the sampled value.
        *   Add $d_i^2$ to `opticalDepth`.
        *   Advance `uvw += uvwStep` [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Transmittance Calculation:**
    Compute transmittance $T$ using the formula:
    $$ T = \exp(-\text{strength} \cdot \text{opticalDepth}) $$
    where `strength` is `_GsplatVolumeOcclusionParams.x` [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Lighting Evaluation:**
    *   If `_GsplatVolumeEnabled == 0`, return base color with $T=1.0$.
    *   Transform splat center to world space. Transform and normalize the normal, flipping it if $\text{dot}(\text{worldNormal}, \text{viewDirection}) < 0.0$.
    *   Compute ambient: $\text{ambient} = \text{_GsplatVolumeAmbientColor.rgb} \cdot \text{_GsplatVolumeLightingParams.y}$.
    *   Initialize `direct = 0.0`.
    *   For each light (max 4):
        *   Compute vector to light and attenuation.
        *   For directional lights (`lightType < 0.5`), `attenuation = 1.0`.
        *   For point/spot lights, compute `distanceSquared`. Let `normalizedDistanceSquared = distanceSquared * lightDirection.w`. Compute `rangeFade = saturate(1.0 - normalizedDistanceSquared^2)`. Then `attenuation = rangeFade^2 / distanceSquared`.
        *   If `lightType > 1.5` (spot light), compute `spotCosine = dot(lightDirection.xyz, -toLight)`. Compute `spot = saturate((spotCosine - spotParams.x) * spotParams.y)`. Update `attenuation *= spot^2`.
        *   Compute `visibility = T` (from step 6).
        *   Add to direct: $\text{direct} += \text{_GsplatVolumeLightColors[lightIndex].rgb} \cdot (\text{irradiance} \cdot \text{visibility})$.
    *   Scale direct by `_GsplatVolumeLightingParams.z`.
    *   Return $\max(\text{ambient} + \text{direct}, 0.0)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def gsplat_volume_lighting(splat, camera, lights, volume_params, volume_texture):
    # 1. Light Direction Transformation
    light_dir_world = normalize(lights['direction']) # Or compute from position
    light_dir_local = transform_world_to_local(light_dir_world, volume_params['world_to_local'])
    if length(light_dir_local) <= 1e-6:
        return 1.0 # No occlusion

    # 2. Ray-Box Intersection
    # Clamp direction to avoid division by zero
    dir_magnitude = length(light_dir_local)
    if dir_magnitude < 1e-5:
        dir_magnitude = 1e-5
    light_dir_local = light_dir_local / dir_magnitude
    
    # Compute direction sign for t0/t1 calculation
    direction_sign = lerp(-1.0, 1.0, step(0.0, light_dir_local))
    
    # Compute t0 and t1 against bounds
    # Assuming bounds are axis-aligned and stored in volume_params
    bounds_min = volume_params['bounds_min']
    bounds_max = volume_params['bounds_max']
    
    # Ray origin is splat center in local space
    ray_origin_local = transform_world_to_local(splat['position'], volume_params['world_to_local'])
    
    # t0 = (bounds_min - ray_origin) / light_dir_local
    # t1 = (bounds_max - ray_origin) / light_dir_local
    # Handle division by zero if light_dir_local is 0 (already clamped magnitude, but components can be 0)
    t0 = (bounds_min - ray_origin_local) / light_dir_local
    t1 = (bounds_max - ray_origin_local) / light_dir_local
    
    # t_enter = max(min(t0), min(t1))
    # t_exit = min(max(t0), max(t1))
    t_enter = max(min(t0.x, t1.x), min(t0.y, t1.y), min(t0.z, t1.z))
    t_exit = min(max(t0.x, t1.x), max(t0.y, t1.y), max(t0.z, t1.z))
    
    # Ray length is max(0.0, min(farDistance.x, min(farDistance.y, farDistance.z)))
    # Assuming farDistance is effectively t_exit for the box
    ray_length = max(0.0, min(t_exit.x, min(t_exit.y, t_exit.z)))
    
    # 3. Light Type Adjustment
    if lights['type'] == 'point' and lights['position_w'] > 0.5:
        dist_to_light = length(ray_origin_local - lights['position_local'])
        ray_length = min(ray_length, dist_to_light)
        
    start_distance = volume_params['occlusion_params'].z
    usable_length = ray_length - start_distance
    if usable_length <= 1e-5:
        return 1.0

    # 4. Sample Count and Step Size
    sample_count = clamp(round(volume_params['occlusion_params'].y), 1, 24)
    step_length = usable_length / sample_count
    
    # 5. Ray Marching Loop
    inv_size = volume_params['bounds_inv_size']
    uvw = (ray_origin_local + light_dir_local * (start_distance + 0.5 * step_length) - bounds_min) * inv_size
    uvw_step = light_dir_local * step_length * inv_size
    
    optical_depth = 0.0
    opaque_depth_threshold = 8.0 # GSPLAT_VOLUME_OPAQUE_DEPTH
    
    for i in range(sample_count):
        if i >= sample_count or optical_depth >= opaque_depth_threshold:
            break
            
        # Check bounds
        if uvw.x < 0.0 or uvw.x > 1.0 or uvw.y < 0.0 or uvw.y > 1.0 or uvw.z < 0.0 or uvw.z > 1.0:
            uvw += uvw_step
            continue
            
        # Sample R8 channel
        d_i = sample_r8(volume_texture, uvw)
        optical_depth += d_i * d_i
        
        uvw += uvw_step
        
    # 6. Transmittance Calculation
    strength = volume_params['occlusion_params'].x
    transmittance = exp(-strength * optical_depth)
    
    # 7. Lighting Evaluation
    if volume_params['enabled'] == 0:
        return 1.0
        
    # Transform splat data
    splat_world_pos = splat['position']
    splat_normal_world = normalize(transform_normal(splat['normal'], camera['view_matrix']))
    view_dir = normalize(camera['position'] - splat_world_pos)
    
    # Flip normal if back-facing
    if dot(splat_normal_world, view_dir) < 0.0:
        splat_normal_world = -splat_normal_world
        
    ambient = volume_params['ambient_color'].rgb * volume_params['lighting_params'].y
    direct = 0.0
    
    for light in lights:
        to_light = light['position'] - splat_world_pos
        dist_sq = dot(to_light, to_light)
        
        if light['type'] == 'directional':
            attenuation = 1.0
        else:
            # Point/Spot
            norm_dist_sq = dist_sq * light['direction_w']
            range_fade = saturate(1.0 - norm_dist_sq * norm_dist_sq)
            attenuation = (range_fade * range_fade) / dist_sq
            
        if light['type'] == 'spot':
            spot_cosine = dot(light['direction'], -normalize(to_light))
            spot = saturate((spot_cosine - light['spot_params'].x) * light['spot_params'].y)
            attenuation *= spot * spot
            
        visibility = transmittance
        irradiance = light['color'].rgb * attenuation
        direct += irradiance * visibility
        
    direct *= volume_params['lighting_params'].z
    final_color = max(ambient + direct, 0.0)
    
    return final_color
```

**Subtopics:**
*   **Volume Texture Representation:** The density is stored in an R8 texture, encoding the square root of normalized optical depth. This allows for efficient sampling and squaring during the march. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Light Types:** Supports directional, point, and spot lights with specific attenuation models (inverse square with range fade) and spot cone falloff. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Early Exit:** The march terminates early if the accumulated optical depth exceeds the opaque threshold ($8.0$), saving computation. [S1, unofficial]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of splats and $S$ is the number of samples per splat (up to 24). The complexity is linear with respect to the number of lights as well, up to 4.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$, where $V$ is the size of the 3D volume texture. The texture is typically small (e.g., $64^3$ or $128^3$ R8) to fit in cache.
*   **Measured Performance:** Performance depends heavily on the volume density and the number of splats. Early exit on opaque regions helps. The method is designed for real-time performance in Unity 6, leveraging GPU compute or fragment shaders. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Strengths:** Provides volumetric shadows and occlusion for 3DGS, which is otherwise a surface-based representation. Integrates seamlessly with existing lighting pipelines.
*   **Limitations:** Limited to 4 lights. The volume texture must be authored or generated separately. Performance degrades with high sample counts or large volumes.
*   **When to Use:** When volumetric shadows and occlusion are desired for 3DGS content in real-time applications, particularly in Unity. [S1, unofficial]
*   **When Not to Use:** For offline rendering where higher fidelity shadow techniques (e.g., path tracing) are acceptable, or when the scene geometry is complex enough that geometric shadows are preferred.

## Background
GsplatVolumeLighting is part of the UnitySplats package, a cross-platform Unity 6 package for rendering 3D Gaussian Splatting content [S1, unofficial]. It was developed by Arloopa as an extension to the original gsplat-unity project by wuyize25 [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the lack of native volumetric lighting and shadowing in standard 3DGS renderers, which typically rely on screen-space or geometric approximations. It is used in the UnitySplats package for volume occlusion and lighting of 3DGS content in Unity 6 [S1, unofficial].
