# AtomGS

Also called: Atomized Proliferation, Atom Gaussian Densification, Atomizing Gaussian Splatting for High-Fidelity Radiance Field

## Overview
AtomGS (Atomizing Gaussian Splatting for High-Fidelity Radiance Field) is a training optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that enhances geometric accuracy and rendering fidelity by constraining Gaussian densification into uniformly sized "Atom Gaussians" and guiding optimization with edge-aware geometry losses [S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3DGS, which allows Gaussians to freely drift and split based on position gradients, AtomGS provides precise guidance on where to focus Gaussians, balancing visual quality with strict geometric alignment [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Pruning and Reduction](14909-pruning-and-reduction.md) by actively restructuring the Gaussian population into isotropic atoms for fine details while retaining larger anisotropic Gaussians for smooth backgrounds, rather than simply removing low-impact primitives.

## How it works
AtomGS operates on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation [12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md]. It modifies the densification and optimization phases to prioritize geometric precision.

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Pre-optimized 3DGS scene (means $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariances $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, opacities $\alpha$, colors $\mathbf{c}$), ground truth RGB images, camera poses.
- **Outputs**: Optimized set of 3D Gaussians with improved geometric alignment and rendering quality.

### Data Structures
The method retains the standard 3DGS Gaussian structure but categorizes them into two functional groups during training:
1.  **Atom Gaussians**: Isotropic, uniformly sized Gaussians used for fine details.
2.  **Large Anisotropic Gaussians**: Retained for background or smooth surfaces with few features.

### Steps

1.  **Pre-computation of Guidance Maps**:
    -   Compute a **Normal Map** from the pre-optimized 3DGS scene.
    -   Compute an **Edge Map** from the gradient magnitude of the ground truth RGB images.

2.  **Atomized Proliferation (Densification)**:
    -   Apply a progressive split schedule.
    -   **Constraint**: Constrain smaller anisotropic Gaussians into uniformly-sized **Atom Gaussians**. This ensures detailed coverage of complex scenes.
    -   **Retention**: Keep larger anisotropic Gaussians to represent the background or geometric areas with few features.
    -   **Merging**: Merge large Gaussians to cover smooth surfaces.
    -   *Note*: This contrasts with standard 3DGS where splits are driven primarily by position gradients without explicit size uniformity constraints for detail areas [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Geometry-Guided Optimization**:
    -   Optimize Gaussian parameters using a composite loss function.
    -   **Edge-Aware Normal Loss**: Incorporate weights from the Edge Map into the curvature map to compute this loss. It imposes stricter constraints on Gaussian positioning aligned with flat surfaces while allowing flexibility for irregularly shaped areas.
    -   **Multi-scale SSIM**: Use multi-scale Structural Similarity Index Measure to maintain visual fidelity.

### Formulas

**Edge-Aware Normal Loss**:
The loss is calculated as the product of the normal map and the edge map [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{edge\_normal} = \mathbf{N} \odot \mathbf{E} $$
Where:
-   $\mathbf{N}$ is the normal map derived from the pre-optimized 3DGS.
-   $\mathbf{E}$ is the edge map derived from the gradient magnitude of the ground truth RGB image.
-   $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication.

**Total Loss**:
The total loss combines the Edge-Aware Normal Loss and multi-scale SSIM (and implicitly the standard photometric loss, though not explicitly detailed in the provided notes) [S1][S2, unofficial]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{edge\_normal} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{ssim} $$
*Note: The exact weighting coefficients $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are Not given in the sources.*

### Pseudocode

```python
def atomgs_training_step(gaussians, gt_images, camera_poses, pre_opt_gaussians):
    """
    One step of AtomGS optimization.
    """
    # 1. Compute Guidance Maps
    # Normal map from pre-optimized scene
    normal_map = compute_normal_map(pre_opt_gaussians)
    
    # Edge map from ground truth RGB
    edge_map = compute_edge_map(gt_images)
    
    # 2. Compute Edge-Aware Normal Loss
    # Element-wise product of normal and edge maps
    edge_aware_loss = normal_map * edge_map
    
    # 3. Compute Multi-scale SSIM Loss
    # Render current gaussians
    rendered_images = render(gaussians, camera_poses)
    ssim_loss = compute_multiscale_ssim(rendered_images, gt_images)
    
    # 4. Compute Photometric Loss (Standard)
    photometric_loss = compute_l1_loss(rendered_images, gt_images)
    
    # 5. Total Loss
    # Note: Lambda values are not specified in sources [S1][S2, unofficial]
    total_loss = photometric_loss + lambda_edge * edge_aware_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_loss
    
    # 6. Backpropagate and Update
    total_loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    
    # 7. Atomized Proliferation (Densification)
    # This is typically done periodically, not every step
    if should_densify():
        gaussians = atomized_proliferation(gaussians)
        
    return total_loss

def atomized_proliferation(gaussians):
    """
    Constrain small anisotropic Gaussians into Atom Gaussians.
    Merge large Gaussians for smooth surfaces.
    """
    new_gaussians = []
    for g in gaussians:
        if is_small_anisotropic(g):
            # Constrain to uniform size Atom Gaussian
            atom_g = constrain_to_atom(g)
            new_gaussians.append(atom_g)
        elif is_large_smooth(g):
            # Merge with neighbors or keep as large anisotropic
            # Note: Merging logic details are Not given in the sources
            new_gaussians.append(g)
        else:
            # Standard 3DGS split/clone logic based on position gradients
            # [S1] mentions relying on positional gradients for density control
            new_gaussians.extend(standard_split(g))
            
    return new_gaussians
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
-   **Precision**: The reliance on gradient magnitude for the Edge Map may be sensitive to noise in ground truth images [S1].
-   **Boundaries**: The distinction between "small anisotropic" and "large anisotropic" Gaussians requires thresholding parameters that are Not given in the sources.
-   **Degenerate Input**: If the pre-optimized 3DGS has poor geometry, the Normal Map may be inaccurate, leading to misleading Edge-Aware Normal Loss gradients [S1].

## Subtopics

### Atomized Proliferation
A densification strategy that constrains Gaussians into uniform sizes for fine details. Choose when geometric precision in complex areas is prioritized over flexible anisotropic shapes. See [Atomized Proliferation](12924-atomized-proliferation.md).

### Geometry-Guided Optimization
An optimization approach using edge-aware losses. Choose when flat surface smoothing and detail preservation are needed simultaneously. See [Directed Distance Field (DDF) for Gaussian Splatting](12854-directed-distance-field-ddf-for-gaussian-splatting.md) for related geometry constraints.

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity**: Training speed is reported to be significantly improved compared to original 3DGS [S2, unofficial]. However, the exact computational overhead of computing the Edge-Aware Normal Loss is Not given in the sources.
-   **Memory Complexity**: The number of Gaussians is similar to or even reduced compared to original 3DGS due to merging of large Gaussians [S2, unofficial].
-   **Performance**: Outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in rendering quality and achieves competitive accuracy in geometry reconstruction [S2, unofficial].
-   **Strengths**: Precise guidance for Gaussian placement, better 3D geometry optimization, smooth flat surfaces, preserved intricate details.
-   **Limitations**: Requires a pre-optimized 3DGS scene to generate the initial Normal Map [S1]. Relies on specific thresholds for Gaussian sizing which are Not given in the sources.
-   **When to use**: When high-fidelity radiance fields with accurate geometry are required, especially for scenes with both fine details and large smooth surfaces.
-   **When not to use**: When training speed is the only priority and geometric accuracy is secondary, or when ground truth images are noisy.

## Background
AtomGS was introduced by Liu, Xu, Hu, Chen, and Feng [S2, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS, which "freely drifts and splits" [S2, unofficial], by providing structured guidance for densification. The method builds on the principles of 3D Gaussian Splatting [12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md] and integrates concepts from edge-aware optimization and multi-scale loss functions. It is cited in surveys on 3D Gaussian Splatting compression and optimization methods [S1].
