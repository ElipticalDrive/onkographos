# Geo-Splatting


## Overview
Geo-Splatting is a physically-based inverse rendering method that augments 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with explicit geometric priors to improve normal estimation and light transport modeling [S2]. It solves the problem of inaccurate material decomposition and flawed relighting caused by approximated normals and semi-transparent Gaussian points in existing methods [S2]. Unlike siblings that rely on implicit constraints or low-frequency spherical Gaussians, Geo-Splatting differentiably constructs surface-aligned Gaussian points from an optimizable explicit mesh, leveraging well-defined mesh normals and opaque surfaces for superior decomposition and relighting quality [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view RGB images, camera poses, and an initial set of 3D Gaussians.
**Outputs:** Decomposed material properties (Albedo, Roughness, Normal), environment light probe, and rendered images.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **3D Gaussians:** Defined by center $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients for color, and material features (Albedo, Roughness, Normal). The covariance is decomposed into scaling matrix $S$ and rotation matrix $R$ [S2].
2.  **Explicit Mesh:** A triangular mesh extracted from learnable isovalue grids using FlexiCubes [S2].
3.  **BVH:** Bounding Volume Hierarchy built on the explicit mesh for accelerated ray tracing [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Geometry Extraction:** Extract an intermediate triangular mesh from learnable isovalue grids using FlexiCubes [S2]. This mesh serves as the explicit geometric proxy.
2.  **Gaussian Initialization (MGadapter):** Generate 3DGS points on the mesh surface in a differentiable manner using the Mesh-to-Gaussian adaptor (MGadapter) [S2]. This constructs structured Gaussian points grounded on the mesh geometry, ensuring consistency between the mesh and 3DGS [S2].
3.  **Light Transport Evaluation:** Perform BVH-accelerated ray tracing on the extracted mesh to evaluate light transport [S2]. This involves Monte Carlo sampling to evaluate the rendering equation [19], handling self-occlusion and inter-reflection more accurately than split-sum approximations [S2].
4.  **PBR Color Computation:** Compute per-Gaussian physically-based rendering (PBR) colors by evaluating the rendering equation using the results from step 3 and the Gaussian's material properties [S2].
5.  **Rasterization:** Composite the shaded Gaussian points into final images using the standard 3DGS rasterization algorithm [S2].
6.  **Optimization:** Jointly optimize the Gaussian geometry, material properties, environment light, and the explicit mesh parameters. A normal consistency loss is added between the Gaussian normals and a monocular depth estimator to guide the inverse rendering process [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   Gaussian function: $G(\mathbf{x}) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\mu)}$ [S2].
*   Rendering Equation: Evaluated via Monte Carlo sampling on the mesh proxy [S2].
*   Normal Consistency Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{normal}$ between Gaussian normals and monocular depth estimator outputs [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   FlexiCubes: Used for isosurface extraction [S2].
*   MGadapter: Differentiable module for mesh-to-Gaussian conversion [S2].
*   Typical Performance: Training time ~9 minutes; Relighting PSNR 29.5dB [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Existing 3DGS methods suffer from noisy material decomposition due to approximated normals and semi-transparent points [S2].
*   Split-sum approximations lack self-occlusion modeling, leading to inaccuracies under strong shadows [S2].
*   Spherical Gaussian (SG) methods struggle with reflective cases due to low-frequency nature [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is efficient, reported at ~9 minutes on standard datasets, compared to 13 minutes for GS-IR and 56 minutes for R3DG [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the explicit mesh, BVH, and Gaussian attributes. The explicit mesh adds overhead but allows for efficient BVH-accelerated ray tracing [S2].
*   **Performance:** Achieves a Relighting PSNR of 29.5dB, significantly outperforming R3DG (26.6dB) and GS-IR (20.4dB) [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Superior material decomposition and relighting quality; handles self-occlusion and reflective surfaces effectively; differentiable end-to-end optimization [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the explicit mesh extraction; may struggle if the initial isovalue grid fails to capture complex geometry [S2].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity inverse rendering, accurate normal estimation, and realistic relighting are required.
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and approximate results are acceptable; when the scene geometry is too complex for effective isosurface extraction [S2].

## Background
Geo-Splatting was proposed by Wenzheng Chen, Guanbin Li, et al. from Sun Yat-sen University and Peking University [S2]. It was published in ICCV 2025 [S2]. The method addresses limitations in prior 3DGS-based inverse rendering approaches like R3DG and GS-IR, which rely on implicit geometric constraints and approximated normals [S2]. It builds on the principles of Geometry and Surface Priors [9270-geometry-and-surface-priors.md] and integrates techniques from differentiable rendering and explicit mesh representation [S2].
