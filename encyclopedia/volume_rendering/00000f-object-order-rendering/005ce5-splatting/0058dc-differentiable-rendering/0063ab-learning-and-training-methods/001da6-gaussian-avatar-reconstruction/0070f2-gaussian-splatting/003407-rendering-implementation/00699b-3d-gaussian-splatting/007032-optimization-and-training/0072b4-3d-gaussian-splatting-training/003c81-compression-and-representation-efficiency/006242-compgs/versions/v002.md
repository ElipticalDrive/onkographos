# CompGS

Also called: Efficient 3D Scene Representation via Compressed Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
CompGS is a compression method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reduces data volume by introducing a hybrid primitive structure consisting of anchor primitives and coupled primitives [S1]. It solves the redundancy in standard 3DGS representations by using anchors to predict the attributes of coupled primitives, resulting in compact residual representations [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that primarily rely on count reduction or attribute quantization alone, CompGS explicitly models the dependency between primitives to minimize the bit rate while maintaining rendering quality [S1].

## How it works
CompGS operates by optimizing a hybrid representation where the total bit rate is jointly minimized with rendering distortion [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A set of 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}$ with position $\mathbf{p}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{i}$.
- Output: A compressed representation consisting of anchor primitives and coupled primitives with residuals.

**Data Structures:**
The scene is partitioned into two sets of primitives:
1.  **Anchor Primitives ($A$):** A subset of primitives that are stored with higher fidelity or serve as prediction bases.
2.  **Coupled Primitives ($C$):** The remaining primitives whose attributes are predicted from anchors.

For each coupled primitive $j \in C$, the attributes are represented as residuals relative to a predicted value derived from one or more anchor primitives $k \in A$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Hybrid Structure Construction:** Partition the initial set of Gaussians into anchor and coupled sets. The selection criteria for anchors are not explicitly detailed in the provided sources [G: Typically involves clustering or importance sampling, but specific algorithm is not given in [S1]].
2.  **Attribute Prediction:** For each coupled primitive $j$, predict its attributes (position, scale, rotation, SH coefficients) based on the attributes of associated anchor primitives. Let $\hat{\mathbf{a}}_j$ be the predicted attributes.
3.  **Residual Calculation:** Compute the residuals for the coupled primitives: $\mathbf{r}_j = \mathbf{a}_j - \hat{\mathbf{a}}_j$, where $\mathbf{a}_j$ are the original attributes.
4.  **Rate-Constrained Optimization:** Optimize the representation by minimizing a Lagrangian cost function:
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{D} + \lambda \mathcal{R}
    $$
    where $\mathcal{D}$ is the rendering distortion, $\mathcal{R}$ is the total bit rate, and $\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier controlling the rate-distortion trade-off [S1].
5.  **Entropy Estimation:** Model the bit rate $\mathcal{R}$ for both anchor and coupled primitives using entropy estimation. The residuals $\mathbf{r}_j$ are entropy-coded based on their estimated probability distributions [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Rate-distortion trade-off parameter. Typical values are not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The method assumes a valid mapping between coupled and anchor primitives. Degenerate cases where no suitable anchor exists for a coupled primitive are not detailed [S1].
- Precision in entropy estimation is critical for accurate rate modeling [S1].

## Operating characteristics
CompGS achieves compactness by reducing the bit rate through residual coding and entropy estimation [S1]. The method jointly minimizes rendering distortion and bit rate, allowing for flexible compression [S1]. Specific performance metrics (compression ratio, PSNR) are not provided in the sources [S1]. The complexity depends on the entropy estimation and optimization scheme, which are not detailed [S1].
