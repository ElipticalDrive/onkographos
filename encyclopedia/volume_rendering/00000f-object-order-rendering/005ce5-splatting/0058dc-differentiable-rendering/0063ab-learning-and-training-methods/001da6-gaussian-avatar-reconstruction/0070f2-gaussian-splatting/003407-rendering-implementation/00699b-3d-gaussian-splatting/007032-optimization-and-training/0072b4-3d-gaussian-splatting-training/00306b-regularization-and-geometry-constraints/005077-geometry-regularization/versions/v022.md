# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the structural integrity and smoothness of reconstructed surfaces in 3D Gaussian Splatting by constraining the shape of the Gaussian kernels, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains their location [S1, S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by focusing on the anisotropy of the covariance structure rather than just positional alignment with depth buffers [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian scaling factors $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, per-Gaussian rotation matrices $R$, and optionally rendered depth maps and their derived normals.
*   **Outputs:** Gradient updates for the Gaussian parameters (specifically scaling and orientation) to minimize geometric loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Scaling Factor ($s$):** A vector of size 3 for each Gaussian, representing the extent along the principal axes defined by the rotation.
*   **Normals:** Per-Gaussian normals derived from the orientation matrix $R$ (typically the third column or the axis corresponding to the smallest scale).

**Steps:**
1.  **Enable Geometry Regularization:** The feature is controlled by a boolean parameter `use_normal_loss` in the optimization configuration [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Depth Normal Consistency:**
    *   Render the scene to obtain a depth map.
    *   Compute surface normals from the rendered depth map.
    *   Calculate the per-Gaussian normal from its covariance/orientation.
    *   Enforce alignment between the rendered depth normal and the per-Gaussian normal [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Flattening Surface (Scaling Loss):**
    *   For each Gaussian, identify the smallest component of the scaling factor vector $s$.
    *   Minimize this smallest component towards zero during optimization [S2, unofficial].
    *   This forces the Gaussian to flatten into a 3D "surflet" aligned with the local surface [S2, unofficial].

**Formula:**
The scaling loss encourages the smallest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix (related to the smallest scale component $s_{min}$) to approach zero:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{flatten} = s_{min} $$
where $s_{min} = \min(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean flag to enable the regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   Weighting factor $\lambda$: Not explicitly defined in the provided notes for the specific geometry term, but follows the parent family convention $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg}$ [Parent].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Minimizing scale components to zero can lead to numerical instability if not clamped [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If a Gaussian is not aligned with any surface (e.g., in empty space), flattening may produce artifacts unless combined with opacity pruning or entropy loss [S2, unofficial].
*   **Depth Refitting:** Depth maps must be refitted against a prior point cloud to the same scale; those failing refitting are discarded [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A specific implementation of geometry regularization that flattens Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels aligned with the surface by minimizing the smallest component of the scaling factor $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$ towards zero [S2, unofficial]. This aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when explicit normal priors are unavailable or when a simpler, scale-based constraint is preferred over full normal consistency checks [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for depth rendering and normal computation if depth normal consistency is enabled [S1, unofficial]. Scaling loss is computationally cheap as it operates on existing scaling parameters [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal additional memory; requires storing depth maps and normals if used [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces "floaters" (spurious Gaussians in empty space) and improves surface smoothness [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May over-smooth valid high-frequency details if the weight is too high [Parent]. Sensitive to the quality of depth maps if normal consistency is used [Parent].
*   **When to use:** When the reconstruction exhibits noisy geometry or excessive floaters, and depth supervision alone is insufficient [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
Originating from the need to improve the geometric fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting beyond photometric accuracy, this method was highlighted in discussions of optimized implementations in Julia [S1, unofficial] and modular frameworks like GauStudio [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the observation that flattening Gaussians into surflets better represents surface geometry than isotropic kernels [S2, unofficial].
