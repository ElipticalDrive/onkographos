# Lighting, Shadowing & Relighting
## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to support physically based lighting, self-occlusion, and environment reflections . It solves the limitation of standard splatting, which assumes a fixed, uniform lighting environment, by introducing deferred shading pipelines for relighting  and dedicated light-space rasterization passes for self-shadowing . Unlike sibling methods that may use simplified shading functions or voxel-based fallbacks, these approaches provide full PBR material attributes and continuous differentiable gradients for shadows without modifying the primary camera's covariance expansion .

## Shared principles
Builds on [Shading and Lighting Models](11924-shading-and-lighting-models.md) [G].

For relighting, splats must carry physical material attributes: normal, roughness, reflectance (specular strength), and albedo (diffuse color) . These are stored in a parallel Material Buffer aligned with the Splat Buffer .

For self-shadowing, a virtual "sun camera" is constructed to simulate a directional light source . This camera is placed at a large distance with a narrow field of view (FOV) to minimize parallax error to below 2% .

Both relighting (for cache locality) and shadowing (for correct front-to-back accumulation) require sorting. Relighting uses spatial locality algorithms like Morton codes . Shadowing sorts Gaussians by depth relative to the sun camera .

During the light pass for shadowing, the projection covariance is not expanded by $0.3I$ and no amplitude compensation is applied, preserving the main camera pass's sampling expansion . In shadowing calculations, sun camera framing, sorting, coverage, and threshold branches are treated as constants to prevent incorrect gradient propagation .

## Subtopics

### [Ref-Gaussian Deferred Surfel Rendering with Split-Sum IBL](4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md)
A physically based rendering (PBR) method for novel view synthesis and relighting of reflective objects using 2D Gaussian primitives. It solves the problem of blurred reflective details and incorrect lighting in standard Gaussian Splatting by decomposing splats into PBR material attributes (albedo, normal, roughness, reflectance) and computing lighting via a deferred shading pipeline with split-sum environment map integration. It differs from sibling methods like 3DGS-DR by using full PBR with spherical harmonics for diffuse fitting rather than simplified shading functions, and by employing deferred rendering to prevent the blurring of reflective details inherent in per-Gaussian shading. Choose this when high-fidelity reflections and PBR material accuracy are required.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Self-Shadowing (T_light)](4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md)
Calculates the self-shadowing transmittance $T_{light}$ for each Gaussian primitive along the sun direction, representing the 'front occlusion transmittance' to model self-shadowing effects in volume rendering. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by performing a dedicated light-space rasterization pass that accumulates depth-weighted transmittance without modifying the primary camera's covariance expansion or applying amplitude compensation, ensuring consistency with the main rendering pass. Choose this when self-occlusion shadows are needed for directional lighting.

### [Light Space Rasterization for T_light](4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md)
Light Space Rasterization computes $T_{light}$, the front-facing occlusion transmittance for each 3D Gaussian along a directional sun direction. It differs from voxel-based fallbacks by providing full continuous gradients and zero parameter expansion, enabling differentiable relighting without requiring specific training hyperparameters. Choose this when differentiable shadow gradients are required for training or optimization.

## Background
The methods in this family originate from the need to extend 3D Gaussian Splatting beyond simple diffuse rendering . Ref-Gaussian introduces deferred surfel rendering to handle PBR materials . The T_light methods were developed to add self-shadowing capabilities, initially using voxel-based caches and later refined into a differentiable rasterization approach . These techniques are used in high-fidelity novel view synthesis, virtual production, and physically based rendering of 3D Gaussian scenes [G].
