# Sensitivity Calculation and Pruning

Also called: Gradient-based Sensitivity Pruning

## Overview
Sensitivity Calculation and Pruning is a method to eliminate Gaussians that have no impact on training images by calculating sensitivity using the gradient of total image energy with respect to parameters [S1]. It differs from its sibling, [Temporal Sensitivity Pruning](6350-temporal-sensitivity-pruning.md), by using a first-order gradient of total image energy rather than an approximation of second-order sensitivity to the L2 loss [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** The set of 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}$, the scene parameters (position, covariance, opacity, SH coefficients), and the training images.
**Outputs:** A pruned set of Gaussians with zero-sensitivity splats removed.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian $i$ maintains a sensitivity score $S_i$ for its color parameters.

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Sensitivity:** For each Gaussian $i$, calculate the sensitivity $S_i$ using the gradient of the total image energy with respect to the parameter [S1]. The total image energy is defined as the sum of squared differences between the rendered image and the target image, or simply the rendered color energy depending on the specific loss formulation, but the source defines it generally as "total image energy" [S1].
    $$ S_i = \nabla_{\theta_i} E_{total} $$
    where $\theta_i$ represents the parameters of Gaussian $i$ (specifically color/SH coefficients) and $E_{total}$ is the total image energy [S1].

2.  **Avoid Clamping Artifacts:** When calculating the sensitivity, do not clamp negative direction-dependent colors to zero. Kerbl et al. [13] clamp negative colors to zero in the forward pass, setting partial derivatives to zero in the backward pass. This results in a sensitivity of zero for SH coefficients that may still contribute to training images [S1]. Therefore, the sensitivity calculation must use the unclamped color values to ensure non-zero gradients for contributing coefficients [S1].

3.  **Identify Zero-Sensitivity Gaussians:** Observe that a notable number of Gaussians (up to 15%) exhibit zero sensitivity in their color parameters [S1]. These splats have no impact on the training images [S1].

4.  **Eliminate Splats:** Eliminate splats that exhibit zero sensitivity in color parameters from the scene [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Clamping:** Clamping negative colors during sensitivity calculation leads to incorrect zero sensitivity for contributing SH coefficients [S1].
*   **Threshold Selection:** Higher pruning thresholds can remove fine details (e.g., small leaves) from the reconstruction, which is undesirable [S1].

## Subtopics

### Gradient-based Pruning
Gradient-based Pruning removes Gaussians contributing minimally to optimization by evaluating the magnitude of gradients associated with each Gaussian during training [S2]. EfficientGS [27] utilizes cumulative gradient sum analysis to halt unnecessary densification and applies pruning to eliminate redundant Gaussians [S2]. GDGS [91] reduces Gaussian density by modeling scene gradients [S2]. Trimming the Fat [25] and ELMGS [18] apply gradient-based pruning, removing 75% of Gaussians while maintaining high visual quality [S2]. Kim et al. [30] extend this approach by incorporating SH gradients alongside positional gradients [S2]. It is used by EfficientGS [27], GDGS [91], Trimming the Fat [25], ELMGS [18], and Kim et al. [30] [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
**Measured Performance:** Trimming the Fat [25] and ELMGS [18] remove 75% of Gaussians while maintaining high visual quality [S2].
**Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
**Strengths and Limitations:** It effectively removes Gaussians with no impact on training images [S1]. However, higher pruning thresholds can lead to the removal of fine details such as small leaves [S1].
**When to use:** Use when Gaussians have zero sensitivity in color parameters, indicating no impact on training images [S1].

## Background
Origin: Compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting for Accelerated Novel View Synthesis [S1].
History: The method addresses the issue where Kerbl et al. [13] clamp negative direction-dependent colors, causing zero sensitivity for contributing SH coefficients [S1]. It was observed that up to 15% of Gaussians have no impact on training images [S1].
Usage: Used in Compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1].
