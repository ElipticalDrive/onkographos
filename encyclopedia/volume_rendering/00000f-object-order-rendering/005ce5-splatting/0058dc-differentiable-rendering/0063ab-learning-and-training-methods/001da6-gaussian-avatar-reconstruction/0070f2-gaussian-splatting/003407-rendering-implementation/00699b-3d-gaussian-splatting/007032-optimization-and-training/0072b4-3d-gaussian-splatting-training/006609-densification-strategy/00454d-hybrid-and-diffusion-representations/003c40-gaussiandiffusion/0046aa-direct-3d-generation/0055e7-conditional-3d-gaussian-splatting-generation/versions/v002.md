# Conditional 3D Gaussian Splatting Generation

Also called: Conditional Generation (Text/Image/Partial), DiffGS Conditional

## Overview
Conditional 3D Gaussian Splatting Generation extends the DiffGS framework to produce 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations conditioned on external signals such as text prompts, input images, or partial 3DGS inputs containing large occlusions [S1]. It differs from unconditional generation by integrating custom encoders (CLIP or PointNet) to map conditions into embeddings that guide the diffusion process, enabling controllable synthesis and completion [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A condition signal $C$, which is either a text prompt, an input image, or a partial 3DGS point cloud.
*   **Outputs:** A complete set of 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) representing the generated scene.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Condition Embedding:** A vector representation of the input condition, produced by a custom encoder $\gamma$.
*   **DiffGS Model:** A neural network (typically a U-Net or similar diffusion backbone) that takes noisy Gaussian parameters and the condition embedding as input to predict noise or denoised parameters.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Condition Encoding:**
    *   For **Text/Image conditions**: Use frozen CLIP encoders to produce embeddings. Let $\gamma_{\text{text}}$ be the text encoder and $\gamma_{\text{image}}$ be the image encoder [S1].
    *   For **Partial 3DGS conditions**: Use a modified PointNet encoder $\gamma_{\text{partial}}$ to encode the partial 3DGS input into a latent representation [S1].
2.  **Conditional Diffusion Training:**
    *   Train the DiffGS model using the conditional optimization objective defined in Eq. (8) of the source [S1]. This objective incorporates the condition embedding into the standard diffusion loss, allowing the model to learn the mapping from conditions to Gaussian fields.
3.  **Inference:**
    *   Sample from the learned conditional distribution to generate the final 3DGS representation. For completion tasks, the model infers the missing parts of the 3DGS from the partial input [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Dataset:** Experiments are conducted on the ShapeNet chair class with commonly used data splits [S1].
*   **Encoders:** Frozen CLIP models for text/image; modified PointNet for partial 3DGS [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Partial Input Quality:** The quality of completion depends on the extent of occlusion. The method is designed to handle "highly occluded" inputs, but extreme missing data may lead to hallucinated geometry [S1].
*   **Encoder Freezing:** Using frozen CLIP encoders ensures stable embeddings but may limit adaptation to specific domain nuances compared to fine-tuned encoders [S1].

## Subtopics

### Interactive3D
A method operating in two stages for interactive 3D object generation and refinement. In the first stage, it optimizes 3D objects through 3DGS and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), allowing flexible interactions like adding/removing parts, geometry transformation, and semantic editing. In the second stage, the model is transformed into an InstantNGP structure with an interactive hash refinement module to improve specific details [S2].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting (Text-to-3D Implementation)
A reimplementation of 3D Gaussian Splatting adapted for text-to-3D generation, modified to support a learnable MLP background and specific gradient propagation. Key differences from the official implementation include:
*   **Gradient Back-propagation:** Calculates gradients in the forward rendering order (rather than the inverse order used in the official code) to simplify per-pixel background integration [S3].
*   **Depth Rendering:** Renders depth maps by sampling the view-space depth at the centers of the Gaussians, claimed to be accurate due to the tiny scale of Gaussians [S3].
*   **Z-Variance Renderer:** Implements a z-variance renderer to compute z-variance for a z-var loss term, though its impact is limited due to Gaussian sparsity [S3].
*   **Opacity Clamping:** Clamps Gaussian opacity to a fixed range $[0.004, 0.99]$ during rendering and optimization to stabilize gradients and prevent numerical overflow/underflow [S3].
*   **Gaussian Count:** The target number of Gaussians after densification is typically between $10^5$ and $10^6$ [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given in the sources. However, related text-to-3D implementations take about 40 minutes to optimize for one prompt [S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given. The number of Gaussians ranges from $10^5$ to $10^6$ [S3].
*   **Performance:** DiffGS produces significantly more visual-appealing and high-fidelity generations in terms of rendering and geometry qualities compared to DiffTF, which suffers from blurry textures [S1]. It accurately recovers semantics and geometries described in text prompts and images [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Capable of generating high-fidelity 3DGS from text descriptions or vision signals; first to introduce solutions for Gaussian Splatting completion from partial inputs [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Z-var loss has limited impact on generated assets due to Gaussian sparsity [S3].

## Background
Originated from the DiffGS paper, which introduced functional Gaussian Splatting Diffusion [S1]. The method builds on the ability of diffusion models to generate complex 3D structures and extends it to conditional settings. It addresses the need for controllable 3D generation and completion, tasks that were not previously focused on in the context of Gaussian Splatting [S1].
