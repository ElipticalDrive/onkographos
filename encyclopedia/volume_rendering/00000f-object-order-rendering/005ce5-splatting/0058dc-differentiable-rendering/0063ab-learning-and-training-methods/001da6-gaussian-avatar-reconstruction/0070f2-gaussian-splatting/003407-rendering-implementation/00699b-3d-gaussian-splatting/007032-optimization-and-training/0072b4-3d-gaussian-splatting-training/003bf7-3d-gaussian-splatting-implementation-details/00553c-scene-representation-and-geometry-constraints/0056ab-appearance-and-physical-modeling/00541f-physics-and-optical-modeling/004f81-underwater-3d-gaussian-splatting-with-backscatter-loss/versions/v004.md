# Underwater 3D Gaussian Splatting with Backscatter Loss

Also called: 3DGS adapted to aquatic environments

## Overview
This method adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for underwater environments by integrating ocean optics models, specifically learning direct attenuation ($B_d$), infinite backscatter ($B_\infty$), and backscatter coefficient ($B_b$) [S1]. It solves the problem of color distortion and visibility loss in aquatic scenes by modifying the rendering equation to account for light transport in a scattering medium [S1]. Unlike vanilla 3DGS, which fails to handle the predominant bluish palette and distance-dependent degradation, this method maintains real-time inference speeds while significantly improving reconstruction quality at unbounded camera-to-scene distances [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$ initialized from a sparse point cloud (e.g., via COLMAP) [S1]; a set of $K$ input images $\{I_k^{gt}\}_{k=1}^K$ with known camera poses [S1]; ground-truth depth estimates or a mechanism to derive them from the Gaussian representation [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters $\Theta$ (means, covariances, opacities, colors) and medium parameters $B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ [S1]; rendered novel-view images [S1].

**Data Structures**
The scene is represented by the standard 3DGS structure [G]:
*   $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$: Mean position (3 floats).
*   $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$: Covariance matrix, derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$ (6 floats for rotation quaternion, 3 for scale) [G].
*   $\alpha_i$: Opacity (1 float).
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$: View-dependent color, typically stored as spherical harmonic coefficients [G].
*   $B_d$: Direct attenuation parameter (1 float per Gaussian or global, modulating RGB values) [S1].
*   $B_\infty$: Infinite backscatter parameter (1 float, bounded $[0, 1]$) [S1].
*   $B_b$: Backscatter coefficient parameter (1 float, bounded $[0, 5]$) [S1].

**Rendering Equation**
The rendering process evaluates Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions [G]. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$
The specific underwater rendering equation incorporating attenuation and backscatter is referenced as Eq. (5) in the source [S1]. The depth map $z$ is extracted from the 3DGS representation using a customized CUDA implementation, mapping the initial output via a shifted and scaled logistic function: $z = \frac{2}{1 + e^{-0.1 \cdot z}} - 1$ [S1].

**Optimization Steps**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians using COLMAP sparse point cloud [S1]. Initialize medium parameters $B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ [S1].
2.  **Initial Parameter Estimation (Backscatter Guidance):**
    *   Identify pixels in the ground-truth image whose RGB triplets fall below the bottom 1st percentile of all pixel values [S1].
    *   These pixels serve as an overestimate of the backscatter [S1].
    *   Model the backscatter color $B_s$ at a pixel with depth $z$ as:
        $$ B_s = B_\infty (1 - e^{-B_b z}) $$
        where $B_s$ is the color value and $z$ is the depth estimate at such a pixel [S1].
    *   Use nonlinear least squares fitting on the pairs of $(B_s, z)$ values to estimate initial parameters $\hat{B}_\infty$ and $\hat{B}_b$, ensuring they remain within bounds $[0, 1]$ and $[0, 5]$ respectively [S1].
3.  **Alternating Optimization:**
    *   Minimize the total loss $L(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b)$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   The rendered images $I_k^r$ are functions of $\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ [S1].
    *   Every 500 iterations, re-compute the initial estimates $\hat{B}_\infty$ and $\hat{B}_b$ using the current depth map estimates and the nonlinear least squares procedure [S1]. This refines the guidance for the medium parameters as depth improves [S1].
4.  **Loss Function:**
    The total loss is defined as:
    $$ L(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b) = \sum_{k=1}^K (1 - \lambda_1) \|I_k^{gt} - I_k^r(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b)\|_{\ell_1} + \lambda_1 L_{D-SSIM}(I_k^{gt}, I_k^r(\Theta, B_d, B_\infty, B_b)) + \lambda_2 L_{bs}(B_\infty, B_b) $$
    where:
    *   $\| \cdot \|_{\ell_1}$ is the L1 norm [S1].
    *   $L_{D-SSIM}$ is the D-SSIM loss [S1].
    *   $L_{bs}$ is the backscatter guidance loss:
        $$ L_{bs}(B_\infty, B_b) = \|B_\infty - \hat{B}_\infty\|_{\ell_1} + \|B_b - \hat{B}_b\|_{\ell_1} $$
        where $\hat{B}_\infty$ and $\hat{B}_b$ are the initial estimates [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $\lambda_1$: Weight for the D-SSIM loss term. Value: 0.3 (inherited from original 3DGS) [S1].
*   $\lambda_2$: Weight for the backscatter loss term. Value: 0.1 (fixed across all scenes) [S1].
*   Learning Rate: Identical for $B_d, B_\infty, B_b$ and the Gaussian color parameters $c_i$ [S1].
*   Optimization Bounds: $B_\infty \in [0, 1]$, $B_b \in [0, 5]$. $B_d$ follows the normalized RGB image range and physically non-negative attenuation assumption [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes only several minutes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Average memory consumption is ~500 MB per scene over 30,000 steps on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU [S1].
*   **Performance:** Inference speed is ~140 FPS average across multiple datasets, enabling real-time novel-view synthesis [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly improves reconstruction quality in underwater scenes compared to vanilla 3DGS, particularly at larger distances and unbounded camera-to-scene distances [S1]. Much faster than NeRF-based methods (STNeRF, STNeRFacto) in both training and inference [S1].
*   **When to use:** Underwater 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and color-reconstruction applications where real-time rendering is required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with extreme turbidity where the backscatter model assumptions break down, or when high-fidelity color accuracy is required without considering the physical medium (vanilla 3DGS might be preferred for speed if quality is not critical) [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was introduced in "Gaussian splashing enables direct volumetric rendering underwater" [S1].
*   **History:** It builds upon the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [Parent] and adapts it to aquatic environments by incorporating ocean optics models [S1]. It addresses the limitations of vanilla 3DGS in handling underwater light transport [S1].
*   **Usage:** Evaluated on the TableDB dataset (172 images, 1384×918 resolution, unbounded camera-to-scene distances) and public underwater datasets (Red Sea, Curaçao, Panama) [S1].
