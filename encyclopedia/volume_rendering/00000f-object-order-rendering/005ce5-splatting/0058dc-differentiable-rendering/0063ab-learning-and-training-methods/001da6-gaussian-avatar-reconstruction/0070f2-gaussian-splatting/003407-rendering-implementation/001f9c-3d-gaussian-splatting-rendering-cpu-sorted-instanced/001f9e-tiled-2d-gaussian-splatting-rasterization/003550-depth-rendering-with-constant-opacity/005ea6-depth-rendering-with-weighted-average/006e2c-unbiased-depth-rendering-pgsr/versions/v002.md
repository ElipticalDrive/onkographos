# Unbiased Depth Rendering (PGSR)

Also called: Flattened Gaussian Plane Depth, PGSR depth rendering

## Overview
Unbiased Depth Rendering is a technique for estimating precise depth maps and normal maps from 3D Gaussian Splatting by approximating the underlying surface geometry [S1]. It sets itself apart from weighted average depth methods by flattening 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into planes, allowing depth to be calculated via ray-plane intersections rather than simple accumulation [S1]. This approach aims to reduce depth ambiguity at sharp edges and better reflect actual surface geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A set of 3D Gaussians, each defined by mean position $\mu_i$, normal vector $n_i$, and opacity $\alpha_i$ [S1].
*   Camera parameters: intrinsic matrix $K$, camera center $T_C$, and world-to-camera rotation matrix $R_c$ [S1].
*   Image pixel coordinates $\tilde{p}$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Depth map $D(p)$ for each pixel $p$ [S1].
*   Normal map $N(p)$ for each pixel $p$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** For each Gaussian $i$, store $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, normal vector $n_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$ [S1].
*   **Camera Matrices:** $K \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, $R_c \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, $T_C \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Flatten Gaussians to Planes:**
    For each 3D Gaussian $i$, approximate its ellipsoidal shape as a plane and calculate the distance $d_i$ from the camera center to this flattened plane [S1].
    $$ d_i = (R_c^T (\mu_i - T_C))^T R_c^T n_i $$
    Here, $\mu_i - T_C$ is the vector from the camera center to the Gaussian mean, and $R_c^T$ transforms this vector and the normal $n_i$ into the camera coordinate system [S1]. The dot product of the transformed position and the transformed normal yields the signed distance from the camera center to the plane [S1].

2.  **Render Normal Map via Alpha-Blending:**
    Compute the accumulated normal vector $N(p)$ at each pixel $p$ by alpha-blending the rotated normals of all Gaussians $i \in M$ that project onto the pixel [S1].
    $$ N(p) = \sum_{i \in M} R_c n_i \alpha_i T_i $$
    Where $R_c n_i$ is the normal vector of the $i$-th Gaussian rotated into the camera coordinate system, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian, and $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance up to Gaussian $i$ [S1].

3.  **Render Distance Map via Alpha-Blending:**
    Compute the accumulated distance $D(p)$ at each pixel $p$ by alpha-blending the distances $d_i$ of the flattened planes [S1].
    $$ D(p) = \sum_{i \in M} d_i \alpha_i T_i $$
    Where $d_i$ is the distance from the camera center to the $i$-th Gaussian plane as calculated in Step 1 [S1].

4.  **Calculate Final Depth Map:**
    Convert the accumulated distance and normal information into a standard depth map $D(p)$ using ray-plane intersection logic [S1].
    $$ D(p) = \frac{D}{N(p)} K^{-1} \tilde{p} $$
    Where $D$ is the accumulated distance from Step 3, $N(p)$ is the accumulated normal vector, $K^{-1} \tilde{p}$ is the ray direction in camera coordinates, and the source notation implies a dot product in the denominator to yield a scalar depth value consistent with ray-plane intersection [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Normal Ambiguity:** Normal vectors exhibit ambiguity at edges where depth changes are sharp [S1]. The method handles this by using the alpha-blended normal map $N(p)$, which aggregates contributions from all Gaussians passing through the ray [S1].
*   **Precision:** The calculation of $d_i$ involves matrix multiplications and dot products [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the denominator in Step 4 approaches zero (ray parallel to the plane), the depth will explode [G].

## Background
Unbiased Depth Rendering was proposed by Chen et al. (2024) as part of the PGSR framework [S1]. It is inspired by 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024a) and SuGaR (Guédon and Lepetit, 2024) [S1]. The method approximates true surfaces by flattening 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into planes via minimizing aligned scale factors [S1].
