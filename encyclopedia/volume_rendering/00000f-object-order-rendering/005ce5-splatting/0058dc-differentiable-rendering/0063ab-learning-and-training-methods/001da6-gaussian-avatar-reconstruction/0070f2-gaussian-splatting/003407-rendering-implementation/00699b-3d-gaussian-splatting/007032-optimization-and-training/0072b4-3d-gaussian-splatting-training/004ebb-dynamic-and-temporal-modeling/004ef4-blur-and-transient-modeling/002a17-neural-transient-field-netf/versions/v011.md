# Neural Transient Field (NeTF)

## Overview
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1]. It solves the inverse problem of reconstructing hidden 3D scenes from sparse, unbalanced time-resolved measurements captured by Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) arrays [S1]. Unlike NeRF, which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates [S1]. It is distinguished by its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse sampling and its ability to produce sharper edges in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field, DLCT, and F-K methods [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Sparse set of transient measurements $T(x, t)$ captured at detection spots $x$ on a relay wall over time $t$ [S1].
*   **Output:** A 3D density volume implicitly represented by an MLP, which can be converted to a mesh via thresholding and Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron $\Psi$ that maps 5D coordinates $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)$ to transient values or density [S1]. The exact architecture (layer sizes, activation functions) is not specified in the sources [S1].
*   **Spherical Coordinates:** Input positions are parameterized on spherical wavefronts rather than Cartesian rays [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the MLP parameters. [G] Standard random initialization for MLP weights is assumed.
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points in the 5D space (3D spatial location + 2D direction) to query the transient field. To account for sparse and unbalanced sampling in the transient field, NeTF employs Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) [S1].
3.  **Forward Pass:** For each sampled point, the MLP predicts the transient value or density [S1].
4.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the loss between the predicted transients and the measured transients at every measuring spot on the relay wall [S1].
5.  **Optimization:** Update MLP parameters to minimize the loss [S1].
6.  **Reconstruction:** Map the implicit volume to surfaces by thresholding the density and applying Marching Cubes [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   The NLOS process is formulated under spherical coordinates [S1].
*   The loss function is calculated between predicted and measured transients at every measuring spot [S1]. The specific loss function (e.g., L1, L2) is not explicitly defined in the provided text, though photometric loss is common in the parent family [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **MCMC:** Used for sampling [S1]. Specific parameters (chain length, burn-in) are not given [S1].
*   **Threshold:** For Marching Cubes [S1]. Value not given [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion:** Occluded points may be captured from a very small number of viewpoints, leading to strong reconstruction artifacts if not handled properly [S1]. NeTF handles this better than Phasor Field, which is most sensitive to occlusions [S1].
*   **Sampling Bias:** Sparse and unbalanced sampling leads to undersampling in certain regions [S1]. Addressed by Two-stage Training and MCMC [S1].
*   **Confocal vs. Non-Confocal:** NeTF does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups in its current form [S1].
*   **Noise:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].

# Inputs: measurements (sparse transient data on relay wall), num_iterations
# Outputs: MLP model representing the 5D transient field

def train_NeTF(measurements, num_iterations):
    # Initialize MLP
    mlp = initialize_MLP()
    
    # Two-stage training strategy to address sampling bias
    # Stage 1: Initial training
    for i in range(num_iterations_stage_1):
        # Sample points using MCMC to account for sparse/unbalanced sampling
        samples = sample_with_MCMC(mlp, measurements)
        
        # Forward pass
        predictions = mlp.forward(samples)
        
        # Calculate loss
        loss = calculate_loss(predictions, measurements)
        
        # Backpropagation
        mlp.backward(loss)
        
    # Calculate loss at every measuring spot to form PDF for resampling
    spot_losses = []
    for spot in measurements.spots:
        spot_prediction = mlp.forward(spot.coordinates)
        spot_loss = calculate_loss(spot_prediction, spot.measurement)
        spot_losses.append(spot_loss)
        
    # Normalize losses to form Probability Density Function (PDF)
    pdf = normalize(spot_losses)
    
    # Resample detection spots based on PDF (higher loss -> higher PDF -> more samples)
    new_measurements = resample_spots(measurements, pdf)
    
    # Stage 2: Refined training
    for i in range(num_iterations_stage_2):
        # Sample points using MCMC on the new dataset
        samples = sample_with_MCMC(mlp, new_measurements)
        
        # Forward pass
        predictions = mlp.forward(samples)
        
        # Calculate loss
        loss = calculate_loss(predictions, new_measurements)
        
        # Backpropagation
        mlp.backward(loss)
        
    return mlp

def reconstruct_mesh(mlp, threshold):
    # Generate 3D grid points
    grid_points = generate_3D_grid()
    
    # Query MLP for density at each point
    densities = mlp.forward(grid_points)
    
    # Threshold and extract surface
    mesh = marching_cubes(densities, grid_points, threshold)
    
    return mesh

## Subtopics

### Two-stage Training
A training strategy to address sampling bias (undersampling) in NLOS reconstruction by resampling detection spots based on reconstruction loss [S1]. It consists of two stages: Stage 1 trains using all initial samples to obtain an initial reconstruction; Stage 2 calculates the loss at every measuring spot, normalizes it to form a PDF, resamples spots (higher loss = higher PDF = denser sampling), and retrains the network [S1]. This allows recovery of fine details (e.g., bunny ears) largely missing in one-stage training [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].
*   Specific time and memory complexity metrics are not given in the sources [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **Confocal Bunny Dataset:** NeTF achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.65 cm, compared to 2.38 cm for DLCT and 2.43 cm for F-K [S1].
*   **Non-Confocal Bunny Dataset:** NeTF achieved an MAE of 7.45 cm, compared to 3.77 cm for FBP and 7.02 cm for BP [S1].
*   **Non-Confocal Z Dataset:** NeTF achieved an MAE of 0.60 cm, compared to 0.46 cm for FBP [S1].
*   **Robustness:** NeTF produces sharper edges for both front and back planes in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field (most sensitive to occlusions) and DLCT/F-K (can only recover one plane at high accuracy) [S1]. NeTF manages to separate different parts of a mesh, whereas DLCT exhibits adhesion between different parts [S1]. NeTF reconstruction incurs much lower noise than SOTA methods, similar to NeRF [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Robust handling of silhouettes and semi-occlusions; lower noise; ability to separate mesh parts; works with very sparse sampling (e.g., 8x8 spots) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive; does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups; brute-force Marching Cubes may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when high-quality reconstruction of hidden scenes is required, especially in the presence of occlusions or sparse sampling, and computational cost is not the primary constraint [S1].

## Background
NeTF was introduced in the paper "Non-line-of-Sight Imaging via Neural Transient Fields" [S1]. It is inspired by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) but adapts the concept to NLOS imaging by using spherical wavefronts instead of rays [S1]. It has been tested on synthetic and real NLOS data, including the Bunny, Lucy, Indonesian, Bike, Letter Z, and semi-occluded scenes [S1].
