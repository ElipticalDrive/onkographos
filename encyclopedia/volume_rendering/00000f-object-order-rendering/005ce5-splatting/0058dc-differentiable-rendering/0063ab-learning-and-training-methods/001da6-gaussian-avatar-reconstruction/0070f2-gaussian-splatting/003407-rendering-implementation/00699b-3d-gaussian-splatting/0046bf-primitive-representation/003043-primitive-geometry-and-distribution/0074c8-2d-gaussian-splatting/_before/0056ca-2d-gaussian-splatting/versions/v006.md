# 2D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 2D splats, 2D-GS, Perspective accurate Gaussian Splatting, Surfel Splatting

## Overview
2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) is a surface reconstruction technique that replaces the volumetric 3D Gaussian ellipsoids of 3D Gaussian Splatting with oriented, zero-thickness 2D disks (surfels) [S1][S7, S8, unofficial]. It solves the problem of ambiguous geometry and poor mesh extraction inherent in volumetric representations by aligning primitives with local surface tangent planes, thereby improving multi-view depth consistency and enabling high-resolution normal map recovery [S2, unofficial][S4][S6, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Direct Gaussian Splatting](3555-direct-gaussian-splatting.md), which retains volumetric blobs, 2DGS uses planar primitives to provide unambiguous normals and accurate surface intersection [S8, unofficial].

## How it works
2DGS represents a scene as a collection of $N$ independent 2D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive is a planar disk defined by a center position, orientation, scale, opacity, and color. The rendering process projects these 2D primitives into screen space and composites them, using a closed-form ray-splat intersection to evaluate the Gaussian density.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ 2D Gaussian primitives $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
    *   Center position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Scaling factors $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (learnable) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (learnable, activated via sigmoid) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Color $\mathbf{c}_i$ (typically encoded via Spherical Harmonics) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A rendered color image $C(u)$ and depth map for a given camera view [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
Each primitive $i$ stores:
1.  **Position:** $\mathbf{p}_i = (x, y, z)$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Rotation:** $\mathbf{q}_i = (w, x, y, z)$, a unit quaternion [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Scale:** $\mathbf{s}_i = (s_u, s_v)$, two learnable scaling factors [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Opacity:** $\alpha_i$ [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Color:** $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S2, unofficial].

### Projection and Rasterization
Unlike 3DGS, which approximates the projection of a 3D covariance to 2D, 2DGS explicitly models the 2D Gaussian on the local tangent plane of the splat [S2, unofficial].

1.  **Construct the Homography Matrix:**
    For each splat, construct a $3 \times 4$ homography matrix $\mathbf{H}$ that maps local tangent plane coordinates to screen space [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathbf{H} = \begin{bmatrix}
    s_u \mathbf{t}_{u,x} & s_v \mathbf{t}_{v,x} & 0 & \mathbf{p}_x \\
    s_u \mathbf{t}_{u,y} & s_v \mathbf{t}_{v,y} & 0 & \mathbf{p}_y \\
    0 & 0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Here, $\mathbf{t}_u, \mathbf{t}_v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ are tangential vectors derived from the rotation $\mathbf{q}_i$ [S2, unofficial]. $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the splat center [S2, unofficial]. Note: The source [S2, unofficial] presents $\mathbf{H}$ with specific column structures; the implementation must derive $\mathbf{t}_u, \mathbf{t}_v$ from $\mathbf{q}_i$ such that they span the tangent plane orthogonal to the normal.

2.  **Ray-Splat Intersection:**
    For a pixel $x$ in screen space, compute the intersection point on the local tangent plane of the splat. The 2D Gaussian density $G(x)$ is evaluated using the UV coordinates $(u(x), v(x))$ of this intersection [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    G(x) = \exp \left( - \frac{u(x)^2 + v(x)^2}{2} \right)
    $$
    The coordinates $u(x), v(x)$ are obtained via the closed-form ray-splat intersection involving $\mathbf{H}$ [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Opacity Adjustment:**
    The effective opacity $\alpha'_i$ at pixel $x$ is the product of the learnable opacity $\alpha_i$ and the Gaussian density $G(x)$ [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \alpha'_i = \alpha_i G(x)
    $$

4.  **Alpha Compositing:**
    Primitives are sorted by depth. The color $C(x)$ is composited using standard alpha blending [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    C(x) = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha'_i \mathbf{c}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_j)
    $$

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degenerate Line Projection:** 2DGS employs a low-pass filter to avoid degenerated line projections when a splat is viewed edge-on [S3]. Without this, the 2D disk may project to a line, causing numerical instability or artifacts.
*   **Perspective Accuracy:** The method improves upon 3DGS by accurately modeling perspective in splat rendering, as the affine approximation used in 3DGS becomes less accurate away from the primitive center [S2, unofficial].
*   **Surface Alignment:** Primitives must align with the local surface tangent plane. Misalignment leads to incorrect normals and poor geometry [S4].

## Subtopics

### [Flattened Gaussian Ellipsoid Rendering](1667-flattened-gaussian-ellipsoid-rendering.md)
Approximates 2D Gaussian ellipses by flattening 3D Gaussians. Differs from 2DGS by operating within a differentiable volume rendering framework that natively supports anisotropic ellipsoidal kernels and enforces a near-zero determinant covariance.

### [3D Surface Splatting](4524-3d-surface-splatting.md)
A differentiable surface splatting renderer for physically-based inverse rendering. Sets itself apart from 2DGS by using a surface rendering model (surfels carry no learnable opacity) and handling multiple surface layers per pixel through depth-interval merging.

### [Spatially Varying Gaussian Splatting (SVGS)](8729-spatially-varying-gaussian-splatting-svgs.md)
Utilizes spatially varying colors and opacity within a single Gaussian primitive. Sets itself apart from 2DGS by defining color and opacity as functions of the intersection point $p$, rather than constant values per primitive.

### [Learning View-Dependent Splatting Kernels](9548-learning-view-dependent-splatting-kernels.md)
Learns view-dependent 2D splatting kernels. Differs from 2DGS by introducing learned view-dependency to the kernel profile, avoiding line integral computations via neural projection.

### [4D Scale-adaptive Filter](9868-4d-scale-adaptive-filter.md)
A filtering technique for 4D Gaussian Splatting. Distinguishes itself by adaptively adjusting dilation coefficients based on primitive scale and visibility to prevent anisotropy distortion.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering complexity is similar to 3DGS, $O(N)$ per pixel for compositing, but requires closed-form ray-splat intersection calculations which may be slightly more expensive than simple 2D covariance evaluation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stores 2D scaling factors ($\mathbb{R}^2$) instead of full 3D covariance or 3D scaling + rotation. However, it still requires position, rotation, opacity, and color per primitive [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Improves multi-view depth consistency and 3D reconstruction quality compared to 3DGS [S2, unofficial]. It is particularly effective for hard surfaces and sharp edges [S4].
*   **Strengths:** Unambiguous normals, better mesh extraction, accurate surface intersection [S8, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May suffer from lower image quality on fuzzy or non-surface scenes compared to volumetric 3DGS [S8, unofficial]. Requires careful handling of edge-on projections [S3].

## Background
2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) was introduced by Huang et al. in SIGGRAPH 2024 [S2, S6, unofficial]. It was proposed to augment 3D Gaussian Splatting with surface reconstruction capabilities, addressing the limitation that 3D Gaussians are not directly suited for mesh extraction due to their volumetric nature [S7, unofficial]. The method replaces 3D ellipsoids with oriented disks to intersect surfaces accurately [S6, unofficial]. It is widely used in applications requiring high-fidelity geometry reconstruction, such as SLAM and neural rendering [S3][S6, unofficial].
