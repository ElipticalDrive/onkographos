# Splatt3R

Also called: Splatt3R: Zero-Shot Gaussian Splatting from Uncalibrated Image Pairs

## Overview
Splatt3R is a feed-forward, pose-free method for 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis that predicts 3D Gaussian primitives directly from a pair of uncalibrated natural images [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of generalizable reconstruction without requiring camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth maps, avoiding the local minima and per-scene optimization typical of iterative 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that restrict supervision to viewpoints between input stereo views, Splatt3R extends to extrapolated viewpoints using a novel loss masking strategy based on frustum culling and covisibility testing [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Two uncalibrated images $I_1, I_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ (typically $512 \times 512$ pixels) [S1, unofficial].
- **Output:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives defined by mean positions $\mu$, covariance matrices $\Sigma$ (parameterized by rotation $q$ and scale $s$), opacities $\alpha$, and colors $c$ (or spherical harmonics $S$) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The method predicts per-pixel Gaussian parameters. For each pixel $j$ in the input images, the network outputs:
- Base position $x_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (in the coordinate frame of the first image) [S1, unofficial].
- Position offset $\Delta x_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial].
- Rotation quaternion $q_j \in \mathbb{R}^4$ [S1, unofficial].
- Scale $s_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial].
- Opacity $\alpha_j \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1, unofficial].
- Color $c_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (constant view-independent color) or spherical harmonics coefficients [S1, unofficial].

**Architecture:**
The model extends the MASt3R architecture [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Encoder:** A shared Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder processes $I_1$ and $I_2$.
2.  **Cross-Attention:** Cross-attention transformers fuse features from both images. These components are frozen during training [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Heads:**
    -   **Point Cloud Head (Frozen):** Predicts base 3D point locations $\hat{X}_1, \hat{X}_2$ and confidence maps $C_1, C_2$ in the coordinate frame of the first image [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Gaussian Head (Trainable):** A parallel head using the DPT architecture [S1, unofficial] predicts the additional Gaussian attributes: offsets $\Delta$, rotations $q$, scales $s$, opacities $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics $S$ for each pixel [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Encode:** Pass $I_1$ and $I_2$ through the frozen MASt3R backbone to obtain feature maps [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Predict Base Points:** Use the frozen Point Cloud Head to predict base positions $\hat{X}_1, \hat{X}_2$ and confidences $C_1, C_2$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Predict Gaussian Attributes:** Use the Gaussian Head to predict offsets $\Delta$, rotations $q$, scales $s$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors $S$ for each pixel [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Means:** Calculate the final mean position $\mu_j$ for each Gaussian primitive:
    $$ \mu_j = x_j + \Delta x_j $$
    where $x_j$ is the base position predicted by the Point Cloud Head and $\Delta x_j$ is the offset predicted by the Gaussian Head [S1, unofficial][S2].
5.  **Compute Covariance:** Construct the covariance matrix $\Sigma_j$ from the predicted rotation $q_j$ and scale $s_j$:
    $$ \Sigma_j = R(q_j) S(s_j) S(s_j)^T R(q_j)^T $$
    where $R(q_j)$ is the rotation matrix derived from quaternion $q_j$ and $S(s_j)$ is the diagonal scale matrix [S1, unofficial][S2].
6.  **Construct Primitives:** The final set of Gaussian primitives is the union of primitives predicted from both input images $I_1$ and $I_2$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Render:** Render the Gaussian set to generate novel views [S1, unofficial].

**Training Procedure:**
Training is performed in two stages [S1, unofficial]:
1.  **Geometry Stage:** Optimize the 3D point cloud's geometry loss using the frozen MASt3R backbone's point predictions. This ensures accurate 3D structure before appearance learning [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Novel View Synthesis Stage:** Optimize the Gaussian Head parameters using a rendering loss.
    -   **Data Selection:** Context images are selected such that at least $\phi\%$ of pixels in the second image have correspondences in the first. Target images are selected such that at least $\psi\%$ of pixels are present in at least one context image [S1, unofficial]. Typical values for $(\phi, \psi)$ are (0.9, 0.9) for Close, (0.7, 0.7) for Medium, (0.5, 0.5) for Wide, and (0.3, 0.3) for Very Wide overlaps [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Loss Masking:** To prevent destructive updates from unseen parts of the scene (obscured or outside frustums), a loss mask $M$ is calculated [S1, unofficial]. For each target image pixel, unproject the point and reproject it onto context images. The pixel is included in the mask if:
        -   It is inside the frustum of at least one context view.
        -   The rendered depth matches the ground truth depth.
        -   The pixel has valid depth in the dataset [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Loss Function:** The total loss $L$ is a weighted sum of masked MSE and LPIPS losses:
        $$ L = \lambda_{MSE} L_{MSE}(M \odot \hat{I}, M \odot I) + \lambda_{LPIPS} L_{LPIPS}(M \odot \hat{I}, M \odot I) $$
        where $\hat{I}$ is the rendered image, $I$ is the ground truth target image, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S1, unofficial].

**Activation Functions:**
-   Quaternions $q$: Normalization [S1, unofficial].
-   Scales $s$ and Offsets $\Delta$: Exponential [S1, unofficial].
-   Opacities $\alpha$: Sigmoid [S1, unofficial].
-   Colors: Constant view-independent color $S \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is used in experiments, with ablation for view-dependent spherical harmonics [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
-   Supervising rendering loss for pixels not visible in context views (obscured or outside frustums) is counterproductive and can destroy model performance [S1, unofficial]. This is addressed by the loss masking strategy [S1, unofficial].
-   Unlike methods like pixelSplat that use camera intrinsics $K$ to compute location $\mu = K^{-1}ud + \Delta$, Splatt3R directly supervises positions using ground-truth point clouds from uncalibrated images, avoiding the need for explicit camera parameter estimation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** Single forward pass of the network. Construction/export runtime is approximately 1.10 seconds on ScanNet++ with 2 views [S2].
-   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
-   **Performance:** On ScanNet++ test scenes with Close view overlap ($\phi=0.9, \psi=0.9$), Splatt3R achieves PSNR 19.66, SSIM 0.757, LPIPS 0.234 [S1, unofficial]. With 2 views, it achieves PSNR 23.30, SSIM 0.823, LPIPS 0.125 [S2].
-   **Strengths:** Pose-free, generalizable to uncalibrated in-the-wild images, supports extrapolation beyond stereo baseline, avoids per-scene optimization [S1, unofficial].
-   **Limitations:** Performance degrades with wider baselines (lower overlap) [S1, unofficial]. Requires ground truth depth/poses during training for loss masking calculation [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Splatt3R was introduced in 2024 by Brandon, Zheng, Iro, and Victor from the University of Oxford [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the MASt3R foundation model for 3D geometry reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. The method was trained on the ScanNet++ dataset [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of existing generalizable 3D-GS methods that only supervise viewpoints between input stereo views, by introducing a loss masking strategy to enable extrapolation [S1, unofficial].
