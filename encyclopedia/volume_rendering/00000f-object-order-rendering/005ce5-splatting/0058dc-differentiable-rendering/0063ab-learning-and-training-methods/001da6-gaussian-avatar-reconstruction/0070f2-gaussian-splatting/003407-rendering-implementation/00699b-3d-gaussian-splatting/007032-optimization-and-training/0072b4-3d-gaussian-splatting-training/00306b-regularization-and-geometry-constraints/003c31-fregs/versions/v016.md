# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS addresses the over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts common in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training by applying progressive frequency regularization to the Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on spatial geometry or pixel-level gradients, FreGS operates in the frequency domain, penalizing discrepancies between the Fourier spectra of rendered and ground-truth images to guide high-frequency detail recovery while preventing noise [S2]. It builds on [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md) by defining $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ specifically through spectral analysis.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Rendered image $I_{render}$, ground-truth image $I_{gt}$, current Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G}$.
- **Outputs:** Regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$, updated Gaussian parameters.

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian Primitives:** Standard 3DGS representation (position $\mathbf{p}$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients).
- **Frequency Domain:** 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) coefficients of images.

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Render:** Render the current scene using 3DGS rasterization to obtain $I_{render}$.
2. **Transform:** Compute the 2D Fourier Transform of both $I_{render}$ and $I_{gt}$ to obtain their frequency spectra $F_{render}$ and $F_{gt}$.
3. **Compute Discrepancy:** Calculate the frequency discrepancy metric. While the exact formula is not fully detailed in the provided sources, it involves comparing the magnitude or phase of $F_{render}$ and $F_{gt}$ [S2].
4. **Progressive Application:** Apply the regularization progressively during training. This likely involves a schedule that increases the weight or sensitivity of the frequency constraint as training progresses to stabilize convergence [S1, unofficial].
5. **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ with respect to the Gaussian parameters and update them via gradient descent.

**Formulas:**
The total loss is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{freq} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ is derived from the frequency domain discrepancy [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Weight for frequency regularization. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Fourier transforms are sensitive to boundary conditions and image size. Padding or windowing may be required [G].
- **Degenerate Input:** If $I_{gt}$ is missing or corrupted, frequency comparison fails [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_freq_regularization_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, lambda_freq):
    """
    Computes the frequency regularization loss for FreGS.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), rendered image.
        gt_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), ground truth image.
        lambda_freq: Float, weight for frequency loss.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Float, the computed regularization loss.
    """
    # Compute 2D Fourier Transform
    # Note: Sources do not specify if magnitude, phase, or complex coefficients are used.
    # Assuming magnitude spectrum comparison as a common approach.
    F_render = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image)
    F_gt = torch.fft.fft2(gt_image)
    
    # Compute magnitude spectra
    Mag_render = torch.abs(F_render)
    Mag_gt = torch.abs(F_gt)
    
    # Compute frequency discrepancy
    # Exact formula not provided in sources. Placeholder for spectral distance.
    # Common choices: L1, L2, or SSIM in frequency domain.
    freq_discrepancy = torch.mean(torch.abs(Mag_render - Mag_gt))
    
    # Progressive application: lambda_freq may be a function of training step
    # Not specified in sources, so assume constant for now.
    loss = lambda_freq * freq_discrepancy
    
    return loss
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Additional cost of 2D FFT per iteration. FFT is $O(N \log N)$ where $N$ is number of pixels. Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency spectra. Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Improves visual quality by reducing over-reconstruction artifacts. Specific metrics (PSNR, SSIM) not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Effective at preventing high-frequency noise and overfitting.
- **Limitations:** Adds computational overhead per iteration. Sensitive to frequency domain implementation details.
- **When to use:** When training exhibits high-frequency artifacts or over-reconstruction.
- **When not:** When computational resources are extremely limited or artifacts are primarily geometric.

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address over-reconstruction in 3DGS [S2]. It is cited in surveys on 3DGS rasterization and compression [S1, unofficial][S2]. The method focuses on the frequency domain to guide high-frequency detail recovery [S2].
