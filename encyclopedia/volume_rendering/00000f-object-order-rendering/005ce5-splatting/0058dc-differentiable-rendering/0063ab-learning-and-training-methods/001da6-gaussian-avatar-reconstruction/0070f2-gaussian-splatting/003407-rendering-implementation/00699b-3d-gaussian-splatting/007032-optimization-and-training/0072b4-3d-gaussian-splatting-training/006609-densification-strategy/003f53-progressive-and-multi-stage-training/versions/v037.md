# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Coarse-to-Fine Training, Progressive Densification

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to a family of optimization strategies for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that decouple the learning of geometry, appearance, and primitive density into sequential phases rather than performing them simultaneously. It solves the instability, suboptimal convergence, and artifact generation associated with initializing and densifying a point cloud while simultaneously optimizing high-frequency appearance details [S1, 7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training, which often leads to overfitting or poor geometric initialization, these methods transition from a coarse representation to a fine-grained one, ensuring that the spatial distribution of primitives adapts to scene complexity over time [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md). The core mechanism involves partitioning the optimization into sequential stages, modifying the trainable parameters, supervision signals, and densification triggers to transition from a coarse representation to a fine-grained one.

Common principles include:
1.  **Decoupling of Geometry and Appearance:** Early stages focus on establishing coarse geometry and camera poses, often using lower-order spherical harmonics (SH) or simplified loss functions. Later stages refine appearance (higher SH degrees) and fine geometry [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md].
2.  **Progressive Densification:** Densification (splitting and cloning) is either delayed until after a coarse initialization or triggered by specific metrics (e.g., frequency violation, gradient magnitude) that are evaluated differently in each stage [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md].
3.  **Parameter Freezing/Unfreezing:** To stabilize training, parameters learned in earlier stages (e.g., camera poses, initial Gaussian centers) are often frozen during subsequent refinement stages to prevent error propagation [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md].
4.  **Curriculum Learning:** Some methods employ a curriculum where supervision signals change from sparse/global (e.g., voxel or slice supervision) to dense/local (e.g., pixel-level photometric loss) [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
A multi-stage approach that refines the primitive set progressively. It solves the instability of simultaneous initialization and densification by decoupling coarse geometry establishment from fine-grained refinement. It sets itself apart by explicitly transitioning from a sparse point cloud to a densified set, yielding significantly higher rendering quality (e.g., PSNR 30.3 dB vs 26.6 dB on Blender dataset) [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md].

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the optimization for **MeshGS**, a variant aligning primitives with an explicit mesh. It solves the problem of Gaussians drifting from geometry by partitioning $\mathcal{G}$ into **tightly-bound** (constrained to mesh) and **loosely-bound** (standard densification) splats. It differs from standard 3DGS by replacing gradient-based densification for tightly-bound splats with mesh-aware sampling [7448-training-objective-and-densification.md].

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines an initial Gaussian representation (from sparse data, point clouds, or pretrained models) to achieve high-fidelity reconstruction. It addresses initialization from noisy or sparse data by leveraging pretrained priors. It sets itself apart by focusing on refining existing attributes (geometry, color, opacity) rather than solely managing primitive count, though it enables growing/pruning [9138-fine-tuning.md].

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Addresses instability in shear-warp rendering by combining sparse voxel sampling with structured slice-based sampling. It sets itself apart by explicitly leveraging geometric alignment between 2D slice supervision and the rendering pipeline, rather than relying solely on resolution hierarchies [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md].

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
A sequential protocol for feed-forward models predicting geometry and appearance. It solves the ill-posed problem of jointly optimizing camera poses and Gaussian attributes by decoupling coarse geometry/pose learning from fine-grained attribute refinement. It enables pose-free inference by specifying exact parameter freezing and loss activation schedules [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md].

### [Free-View Training (FVT)](15162-free-view-training-fvt.md)
Adds geometrical constraints on Gaussian localization to disentangle model performance from the number of input views. It enables robust novel view synthesis across broader view ranges by supervising image renderings in broader view interpolations and training on long sequences [15162-free-view-training-fvt.md].

### [Training Loop with Mixed Losses and Post-Processing](17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md)
Integrates photometric, structural, depth, and regularization losses. It distinguishes itself by explicitly managing the progressive activation of spherical harmonic degrees and optional bilateral grid post-processing, ensuring high-fidelity color reconstruction while maintaining stability through visibility-aware optimization [17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md].

### [Training Loop with Structure-Aware Densification](22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md)
Accelerates training by replacing reactive, gradient-based densification with a proactive, structure-aware strategy estimating local frequency requirements from image gradients. It solves slow convergence by placing primitives early to match scene complexity, using a single-pass frequency metric $\eta$ computed from precomputed structure tensors [22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md].

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
*   **Step:** Stage 1: Construct RGB point clouds for each input view. Project the constructed point clouds to the target view using the differentiable point cloud renderer. Aggregate all point clouds from the various input views to render RGB images at the target view. Optimize the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and the ground truth target images. Stage 2: Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) [S3].
*   **Parameter:** Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

### Two-Stage Triplane and MLP Training
*   **Step:** Stage 1: Jointly train triplanes and MLP on a subset of 500 randomly selected shapes. Use a batch size of 1 object per iteration and 500k occupancy values points per object. Train for 200 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3. Stage 2: Freeze the shared MLP. Train triplane features for each object individually using a batch size of 200k occupancy values per object. Train for 30 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3 [S4].
*   **Parameter:** Triplane feature dimension: 128 × 128 × 32 × 3. Triplane initialization: values drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.1. MLP architecture: Fourier feature mapping layer (scale factor 1) followed by 3 fully connected layers of dimension 128, each with ReLU activation [S4].
*   **Number:** Stage 1 training time: approximately 1 day on a single RTX 2080ti. Stage 2 training time: 10 minutes per shape on an RTX 2080ti [S4].

### Continual Model Refinement
*   **Step:** Augment the initial model with larger sets of closer-up images (64 or 512 images) to simulate prolonged user interaction. Optimize the model for up to 4000 iterations, including 400 iterations of densification to capture finer details [S5].
*   **Parameter:** Final Gaussian count grows to between 20k and 50k [S5].
*   **Pitfall:** Improvement becomes marginal after 2000 iterations. Using foveated and subsequently undistorted views for training decreases quality metrics by 5-10% depending on the scene [S5].
*   **Complexity:** Training proceeds at over 500 iterations per second [S5].
*   **Comparison:** Fixed foveation center and variable foveation points yield less than 1% quality difference [S5].

### Projection-Based Frequency Violation Metric
A densification trigger applying a three-channel anisotropic criterion where each Gaussian axis is evaluated against its own directional frequency content [S6].
*   **Number:** On Mip-NeRF360, $\eta^{(proj)}$ achieves PSNR 27.20 and LPIPS 0.203, compared to Ours PSNR 27.25 and LPIPS 0.197 [S6].
*   **Comparison:** Theoretically more precise than the scalar wavelength metric, but yields worse perceptual quality (LPIPS) in practice because it relies on accurate axis-direction alignment that is not yet optimized during training [S6].

## Background
The concept of progressive training in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged from the observation that standard 3DGS optimization, which initializes Gaussians from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points and immediately begins densification and high-frequency SH optimization, is prone to local minima and artifacts [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md]. Early works like GaussianPro [S1, unofficial] introduced progressive propagation to ensure smooth expansion. Subsequent methods like MeshGS [7448-training-objective-and-densification.md] and FWD-U [S3] formalized multi-stage approaches to handle specific constraints (mesh alignment, depth network initialization). The family has expanded to include pose-free training [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md], structure-aware densification [22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md], and continual refinement for interactive applications [S5].
