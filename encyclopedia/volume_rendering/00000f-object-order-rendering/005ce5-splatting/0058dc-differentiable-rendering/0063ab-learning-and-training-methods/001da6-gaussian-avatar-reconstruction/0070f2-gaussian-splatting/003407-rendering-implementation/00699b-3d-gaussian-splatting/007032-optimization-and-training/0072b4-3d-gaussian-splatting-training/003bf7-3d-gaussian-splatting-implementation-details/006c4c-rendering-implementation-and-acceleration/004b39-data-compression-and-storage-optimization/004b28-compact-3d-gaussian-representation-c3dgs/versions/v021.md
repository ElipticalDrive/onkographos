# Compact 3D Gaussian Representation (C3DGS)
Also called: Compact3DGS, Compact 3D Gaussian Representation for Radiance Field

## Overview
Compact 3D Gaussian Representation (C3DGS) is a method that reduces the storage footprint and rendering latency of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by eliminating redundant primitives and compressing their attributes via spatial sharing [S1, S2]. Unlike standard 3DGS, which stores full per-Gaussian attributes (color, scale, rotation) independently, C3DGS exploits spatial redundancy by replacing per-primitive color data with a hash-based neural field and geometric attributes with a discrete codebook [S1]. This approach achieves over 25× storage compression and up to 40% faster rendering while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$ trained via standard 3DGS optimization, where each $G_i$ has mean $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$ (derived from scale $\hat{s}_i$ and rotation $r_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $c_i$ [S1, S2].
- **Output:** A compact representation consisting of:
    1. A pruned set of Gaussian means $\{\mu_i\}$ and binary masks $\{m_i\}$.
    2. A hash-based neural field grid for view-dependent colors.
    3. A geometric codebook for scale and rotation indices.
    4. (Optional) Quantized and entropy-coded bitstreams for final storage [S1, S2].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Primitives:** Stores only the mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a binary mask $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$. The mask indicates if the Gaussian is active ($1$) or pruned ($0$) [S1, S2].
2.  **Color Grid:** A multi-resolution hash grid (Instant-NGP style) mapping 3D coordinates to view-dependent color features. The grid consists of $L$ levels, where level $l$ has resolution $R_l \times R_l \times R_l$ and stores feature vectors of dimension $D$ [S1].
3.  **Geometric Codebook:** A lookup table of size $K$ containing representative scale and rotation vectors. Each active Gaussian stores only an index $k_i \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ pointing to this codebook [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Volume-Based Masking (Pruning):**
    During training, a learnable binary mask $m_i$ is introduced for each Gaussian. The mask is regularized to encourage sparsity. Gaussians with low contribution to the scene (indicated by low mask values or low opacity/scale product) are pruned [S1, S2]. The pruning criterion combines opacity $\alpha_i$ and scale to identify redundant Gaussians [S2].
    
2.  **Color Attribute Compression (Neural Field):**
    Instead of storing spherical harmonic coefficients $c_i$ for each Gaussian, the view-dependent color is predicted by querying the hash-based neural field at the Gaussian's mean position $\mu_i$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{v}$ [S1].
    The color $C_i$ is computed as:
    $$ C_i = \text{MLP}(\text{HashGrid}(\mu_i, \mathbf{v})) $$
    where $\text{HashGrid}$ uses the Instant-NGP hashing scheme to map 3D coordinates and view directions to feature vectors, which are then processed by a small MLP to output RGB values [S1]. This exploits spatial redundancy where neighboring Gaussians share similar colors [S1].

3.  **Geometric Attribute Compression (Codebook):**
    Scale $\hat{s}_i$ and rotation $r_i$ are quantized. A codebook of size $K$ is learned to represent common geometric patterns. Each Gaussian is assigned an index $k_i$ corresponding to the closest codebook entry [S1].
    The effective scale and rotation are retrieved as:
    $$ \hat{s}_i = \text{Codebook}_S[k_i], \quad r_i = \text{Codebook}_R[k_i] $$
    This reduces the storage of geometry from continuous floats to a small integer index [S1].

4.  **Post-Processing (Quantization and Entropy Coding):**
    After training, the remaining parameters (mask, codebook indices, grid features) undergo quantization to reduce bit-widths. Entropy coding is then applied to the quantized data for final lossless compression [S2].

**Parameters:**
- **Codebook Size ($K$):** Typically small (e.g., 256 or 512) to ensure high compression [S1].
- **Grid Levels:** Multi-resolution hash grid levels (e.g., 14 levels in Instant-NGP) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision Loss:** Quantization of codebook entries and grid features can introduce artifacts if the codebook size is too small [S1].
- **Boundary Effects:** Hash collisions in the grid may cause color bleeding if not handled with proper hashing schemes [S1].
- **Degenerate Input:** If all Gaussians are pruned, the renderer fails. A minimum number of Gaussians must be maintained [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Optimized Minimal 3D Gaussian Splatting (OMG)](13978-optimized-minimal-3d-gaussian-splatting-omg.md)
OMG retains per-Gaussian parameterization for geometry to handle sparsity, while using a small neural field and Sub-Vector Quantization (SVQ) for appearance features. It achieves higher compression (>300×) than C3DGS by addressing C3DGS's weakness in sparse settings where neural fields struggle with distant neighbors.

## Operating characteristics
- **Storage:** Achieves over 25× compression compared to standard 3DGS [S1]. On the Deep Blending dataset, it achieves over 28× enhancement in storage efficiency [S1].
- **Speed:** Rendering speed increases by nearly 40% due to the reduced number of Gaussians and the efficiency of the hash grid lookup compared to per-Gaussian attribute storage [S1].
- **Quality:** Maintains or improves PSNR compared to 3DGS [S1].
- **Scaling:** The hash grid size is independent of the number of Gaussians, making it scalable to large scenes [S1].
- **Limitations:** The neural field introduces a small computational overhead per Gaussian during color prediction, which is offset by the reduced number of Gaussians [S1].

## Background
C3DGS was introduced in 2024 by the authors of the paper "Compact 3D Gaussian Representation for Radiance Field" [S1]. It addresses the scalability issues of 3DGS in unbounded scenes with large numbers of SfM points [S2]. The method builds on the principles of neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and vector quantization, adapting them to the Gaussian Splatting framework [S1].
