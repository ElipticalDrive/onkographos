# 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization

Also called: 3D-HGS

## Overview
This method modifies the covariance structure of 3D Gaussian primitives to enforce a half-ellipsoid geometry, aligning the Gaussian shape with local surface normals to better model sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing full ellipsoids with truncated shapes that eliminate "dust" and noise artifacts common in unstructured point cloud reconstructions [S1]. Unlike [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) which constrains positions, this method directly regularizes the shape parameters [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $\Sigma$: The $3 \times 3$ covariance matrix of a Gaussian primitive in world space.
*   $n$: The local surface normal vector at the Gaussian center.

**Outputs:**
*   $\sigma$: The regularized covariance matrix in world space, representing a half-ellipsoid.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Basis Matrix ($B$):** A $3 \times 3$ matrix where columns are the orthonormal vectors $v_1, v_2, n$.
*   **Covariance in Basis ($cov$):** A $3 \times 3$ matrix representing $\Sigma$ rotated into the basis defined by $B$.
*   **Regularized Covariance in Basis ($\sigma_{basis}$):** A $3 \times 3$ matrix with specific zeroed entries to enforce the half-Gaussian constraint.

**Parameters:**
*   **Iteration Interval:** The transformation is applied every 5000 iterations [S1].
*   **Opacity Threshold:** Increased to 0.01 for densification and pruning [S1].
*   **Half-Gaussian Opacity Reset:** Set to 0.02 for newly created or transformed half-Gaussians [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The transformation projects the full covariance onto the tangent plane defined by the normal, effectively truncating the Gaussian along the normal direction.

1.  **Normalize Normal:**
    $$ n \leftarrow \frac{n}{\|n\|} $$

2.  **Construct Basis Vectors:**
    Define two vectors $v_1$ and $v_2$ orthogonal to $n$ and each other.
    *   If $n_x = 0$ and $n_y = 0$:
        $$ v_1 = (1, 0, 0) $$
        $$ v_2 = (0, 1, 0) $$
    *   Else:
        $$ v_1 \leftarrow \frac{(n_y, -n_x, 0)}{\|(n_y, -n_x, 0)\|} $$
        $$ v_2 \leftarrow \frac{n \times v_1}{\|n \times v_1\|} $$

3.  **Form Basis Matrix:**
    $$ B = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & | \\ v_1 & v_2 & n \\ | & | & | \end{bmatrix} $$

4.  **Transform Covariance to Basis Space:**
    $$ cov = B^T \Sigma B $$
    Let $cov_{i,j}$ denote the element at row $i$, column $j$ of $cov$.

5.  **Compute Modified Diagonal and Off-Diagonal Elements:**
    Calculate the Schur complement-like terms to remove the influence of the normal direction ($z$-axis in basis space) from the tangent plane ($x,y$ axes).
    $$ a' \leftarrow cov_{0,0} - \frac{cov_{0,2}^2}{cov_{2,2}} $$
    $$ b' \leftarrow cov_{0,1} - \frac{cov_{0,2} \cdot cov_{1,2}}{cov_{2,2}} $$
    $$ c' \leftarrow cov_{1,1} - \frac{cov_{1,2}^2}{cov_{2,2}} $$

6.  **Construct Regularized Covariance in Basis Space ($\sigma_{basis}$):**
    The top-left $2 \times 2$ block contains the modified tangent plane covariance. The entries coupling the tangent plane to the normal are zeroed out. The normal variance is scaled down significantly to enforce the "half" nature (truncation).
    $$ \sigma_{basis} = \begin{bmatrix} a' & b' & 0 \\ b' & c' & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \min(a', c') / 100 \end{bmatrix} $$

7.  **Transform Back to World Space:**
    $$ \sigma = B \sigma_{basis} B^T $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def transform_covariance_to_half_gaussian(cov_matrix: np.ndarray, normal: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Transforms a 3D Gaussian covariance matrix into a half-Gaussian covariance
    aligned with the given surface normal.
    
    Args:
        cov_matrix: 3x3 symmetric positive definite covariance matrix.
        normal: 3D normal vector (not necessarily normalized).
        
    Returns:
        Regularized 3x3 covariance matrix.
    """
    # 1. Normalize normal
    n = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)
    
    # 2. Construct basis vectors v1, v2
    # Check if normal is parallel to Z-axis to avoid division by zero or degenerate cross products
    if np.isclose(n[0], 0.0) and np.isclose(n[1], 0.0):
        v1 = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        v2 = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 0.0])
    else:
        # v1 is orthogonal to n and lies in the XY plane projection
        v1_raw = np.array([n[1], -n[0], 0.0])
        v1 = v1_raw / np.linalg.norm(v1_raw)
        
        # v2 is orthogonal to both n and v1
        v2_raw = np.cross(n, v1)
        v2 = v2_raw / np.linalg.norm(v2_raw)
        
    # 3. Form basis matrix B (columns are v1, v2, n)
    B = np.column_stack((v1, v2, n))
    
    # 4. Transform covariance to basis space: cov = B^T * Sigma * B
    cov_basis = B.T @ cov_matrix @ B
    
    # 5. Compute modified elements a', b', c'
    # Avoid division by zero if cov_basis[2,2] is extremely small
    cov_zz = cov_basis[2, 2]
    if cov_zz < 1e-8:
        cov_zz = 1e-8
        
    a_prime = cov_basis[0, 0] - (cov_basis[0, 2] ** 2) / cov_zz
    b_prime = cov_basis[0, 1] - (cov_basis[0, 2] * cov_basis[1, 2]) / cov_zz
    c_prime = cov_basis[1, 1] - (cov_basis[1, 2] ** 2) / cov_zz
    
    # 6. Construct regularized covariance in basis space
    # The coupling terms (0,2), (1,2), (2,0), (2,1) are set to 0
    # The normal variance (2,2) is scaled by min(a', c') / 100
    min_tangent_var = min(a_prime, c_prime)
    sigma_zz_reg = min_tangent_var / 100.0
    
    sigma_basis_reg = np.array([
        [a_prime, b_prime, 0.0],
        [b_prime, c_prime, 0.0],
        [0.0, 0.0, sigma_zz_reg]
    ])
    
    # 7. Transform back to world space: sigma = B * sigma_basis_reg * B^T
    sigma_reg = B @ sigma_basis_reg @ B.T
    
    return sigma_reg
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Normal:** If the normal vector is zero or near-zero, the basis construction fails. The code handles the specific case where $n_x=0, n_y=0$ (normal aligned with Z-axis). For other near-degenerate normals, standard numerical stability checks (like the `1e-8` threshold in pseudocode) are required [G].
*   **Positive Definiteness:** The resulting $\sigma$ must remain positive semi-definite. The construction ensures the tangent plane block is derived from the Schur complement of the original SPD matrix, which preserves positive definiteness for the tangent components. The scaled normal component is positive if $a', c' > 0$. If the original covariance was ill-conditioned, $a'$ or $c'$ could be negative, requiring clamping [G].
*   **Iteration Frequency:** The transformation is not applied every iteration but every 5000 iterations [S1]. Applying it too frequently might destabilize training if the normal estimates are noisy.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The transformation involves matrix multiplications ($3 \times 3$) and vector operations, which is $O(1)$ per Gaussian. However, it requires computing surface normals, which depends on the neighborhood search or depth estimation method used [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No additional significant memory overhead per Gaussian beyond the normal vector storage if not already present.
*   **Performance:**
    *   On Mip-NeRF 360: 125 FPS, 29.66 PSNR with 3.8M kernels (using Efficient Rasterizer) [S1].
    *   On Tank & Dengin: 160 FPS, 24.45 PSNR with 1.8M kernels (using Efficient Rasterizer) [S1].
    *   On Tank & Blending: 126 FPS, 29.76 PSNR with 2.6M kernels (using Efficient Rasterizer) [S1].
    *   The method consistently outperforms original 3D Gaussian Splatting in PSNR across evaluated datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces noise and "dust" artifacts; better models sharp surface boundaries; improves rendering quality.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate surface normals; increases kernel count slightly compared to baseline for comparable quality; regularization is periodic, not continuous.

## Background
3D Half-Gaussian Splatting (3D-HGS) was introduced to address the limitations of full ellipsoidal Gaussians in representing sharp surface discontinuities. By truncating the Gaussian along the surface normal, it mimics the behavior of a half-ellipsoid, which is more physically plausible for surface reconstruction. The method was evaluated against architectures like Scaffold, mip-splatting, and MCMC [S1].
