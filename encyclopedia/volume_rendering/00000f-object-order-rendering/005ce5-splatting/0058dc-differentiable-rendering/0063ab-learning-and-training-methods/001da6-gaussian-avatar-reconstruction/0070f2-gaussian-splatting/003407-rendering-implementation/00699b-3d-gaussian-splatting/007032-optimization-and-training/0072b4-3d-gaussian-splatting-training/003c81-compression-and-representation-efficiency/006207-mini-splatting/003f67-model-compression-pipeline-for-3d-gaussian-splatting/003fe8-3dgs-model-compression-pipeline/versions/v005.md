# 3DGS Model Compression Pipeline

Also called: Hardware-Friendly Compression, Iterative Pruning & VQ Compression

## Overview
A compression scheme tailored for hardware deployment that reduces model size and bandwidth by iteratively pruning Gaussians, reducing spherical harmonics (SH) degree, and quantizing attributes [S1]. It modifies LightGaussian to achieve higher compression ratios (51.6× vs 15×) by adding iterative pruning, progressive SH reduction, and full VQ on all coefficients/colors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting model containing Gaussians with positions, covariances, opacities, colors, and SH coefficients [S1].
**Outputs:** Compressed model with reduced Gaussian count, lower SH degree, and quantized attributes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Set:** List of $N$ Gaussians, each storing mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (or rotation/scale), opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$, color $c_i$, and SH coefficients [S1].
*   **SH Coefficients:** Vectors of coefficients per RGB channel [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Iterative Gaussian Pruning:** Remove Gaussians contributing least to visual fidelity iteratively until a target compression ratio or sparsity level is reached [S1].
2.  **Progressive Spherical Harmonics (SH) Degree Reduction:** Reduce the SH degree encoding color and directional information to decrease coefficients per Gaussian [S1].
3.  **Vector Quantization (VQ):** Apply VQ to all SH coefficients and colors, clustering them into a codebook and replacing values with indices [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Target Compression Ratio:** 51.6× model-size reduction [S1].
*   **PSNR Loss:** 0.743 dB [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **View-Dependent Culling:** Approximately 42% of Gaussians are unnecessary via near-plane culling, but this benefit is view-dependent and decreases when zooming out [S1].
*   **Alpha-Blending Contribution:** Only about 50% of Gaussians contribute to pixel color during $\alpha$-blending, making culling and pruning critical for performance [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Mini-Splatting-C](10131-mini-splatting-c.md)
A storage-compression variant applying transform coding, quantization, and entropy encoding to pre-trained attributes for minimal footprint. Sets it apart by optimizing for storage efficiency over rendering quality compared to Mini-Splatting-D. Choose when minimal model footprint is required.

### [Hybrid Compression Framework](15040-hybrid-compression-framework.md)
A post-training method combining adaptive voxelization and GPCC to reduce size and bandwidth without generative retraining costs. Sets it apart by achieving higher quality at lower bitrates than siblings like Mini-Splatting-C. Choose for bitrate-efficient deployment.

### [Prune2TargetSize](15817-prune2targetsize.md)
A top-down iterative pruning algorithm guaranteeing a precise target splat count. Sets it apart from bottom-up growth approaches by solving unpredictable model size issues. Choose when exact splat counts are required.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression Ratio:** 51.6× model-size reduction [S1].
*   **Quality Loss:** 0.743 dB PSNR loss [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Modifies LightGaussian (15× reduction) by adding iterative pruning, progressive SH reduction, and full VQ on all coefficients/colors to reach 51.6× [S1].
*   **Hardware Efficiency:** Enables a hardware accelerator delivering 1219 Mpixels/J at 267.5 Mpixels/s, rendering 1080p at 129 FPS [S1].
*   **Memory Usage:** Mitigates the gigabyte-range storage requirements of unbounded 360° scenes containing millions of Gaussians [S1].

## Background
The 3DGS algorithm is not hardware-friendly and faces substantial memory usage challenges due to millions of Gaussians in unbounded scenes [S1]. To enable real-time rendering on portable AR/VR devices at full-HD resolution and 120 FPS per eye, a dedicated hardware accelerator was introduced [S1]. This compression pipeline is a core component, modifying the LightGaussian lightweight model to achieve higher compression ratios for hardware deployment [S1].
