# 3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)


## Overview
3iGS enhances 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by modeling outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes, rather than optimizing outgoing radiance in isolation [S1]. It differs from [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md) and [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md) by explicitly separating scene lighting (via a factorised tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features), enabling accurate specular view-dependent effects while maintaining the rapid rasterization speed of 3DGS [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians (initialized from SfM points or random initialization) and training images.
**Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters (means, covariances, opacities, diffuse colors, BRDF features) and a factorised tensor illumination grid.

### Data Structures
1.  **3D Gaussian $i$:**
    *   Mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Position.
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$: Scalar.
    *   Scaling $\mathbf{S}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and Rotation $\mathbf{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Used to derive covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^T \mathbf{R}_i^T$.
    *   Diffuse Color $\mathbf{c}_{d,i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Constant per Gaussian.
    *   BRDF Features $\boldsymbol{\rho}_i$: Repurposed 16x3 SH coefficients from base 3DGS, plus 4 additional parameters for base color and roughness [S1].
2.  **Illumination Grid $\mathcal{G}_l$:**
    *   Represented using Vector-Matrix (VM) factorisation [S1].
    *   Resolution: Coarse grid of $150^3$ voxels [S1].
    *   Parameters: Feature tensors $\mathbf{A}_{L,r}^X, \mathbf{A}_{L,r}^Y, \mathbf{A}_{L,r}^Z$ and feature vectors $\mathbf{b}_{k}$ for rank $R_L$ [S1].

### Formulas
The outgoing radiance $\mathbf{c}(\omega_o)$ for a viewing direction $\omega_o$ is the sum of diffuse and specular components [S1]:
$$ \mathbf{c}(\omega_o) = \mathbf{c}_d + \mathbf{c}_s(\omega_o) $$

The specular color $\mathbf{c}_s$ is computed by a neural network $\mathcal{F}$ [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{F} : \{ \boldsymbol{\rho}_i, L_i, \omega_o \} \mapsto \mathbf{c}_s $$
where $L_i$ is the incoming illumination at Gaussian mean $\mathbf{x}_i$.

The illumination field $\mathcal{G}_l$ is formulated as [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{G}_l = \sum_{r=1}^{R_L} \mathbf{A}_{L,r}^X \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r-2} + \mathbf{A}_{L,r}^Y \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r-1} + \mathbf{A}_{L,r}^Z \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r} $$
where $\circ$ denotes the outer product or appropriate tensor contraction as defined in TensoRF [S1]. The input to $\mathcal{G}_l$ is the Gaussian mean $\mathbf{x}_i$, yielding $L_i$ via interpolation [S1].

The rendering equation follows standard 3DGS alpha blending [S1]:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
$$ T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - g_j(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_j) $$
where $g_i$ is the 2D projected Gaussian density, $\boldsymbol{\mu}'$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'$ are the mean and covariance in screen space, and $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the radiance computed above [S1].

The covariance matrix in camera space is derived as [S1]:
$$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{W}^T \mathbf{J}^T $$
where $\mathbf{W}$ is the viewing transformation and $\mathbf{J}$ is the affine approximation of the projective transformation [S1].

The loss function combines L1 and D-SSIM terms [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} $$
with $\lambda = 0.2$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Training Iterations:** 30,000 steps [S1].
*   **Illumination Grid Resolution:** $150^3$ voxels [S1].
*   **BRDF Features:** 16x3 SH coefficients + 4 parameters (base color, roughness) [S1].
*   **Loss Weight $\lambda$:** 0.2 [S1].
*   **Rank $R_L$:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Neural Network $\mathcal{F}$:** Architecture details not given in the sources.

### Steps
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize 3D Gaussians with means, opacities, covariances (from S and R), and BRDF features (repurposed SH + 4 params). Initialize the factorised tensor illumination grid $\mathcal{G}_l$ with a boundary encapsulating the scene bounding box [S1].
2.  **Diffuse-Only Training (Iterations 0–3,000):**
    *   For each training step, compute the incoming illumination $L_i$ for each Gaussian by interpolating from $\mathcal{G}_l$ using its mean $\mathbf{x}_i$ [S1].
    *   Compute diffuse radiance $\mathbf{c}_d$ (specular is disabled or zeroed) [S1].
    *   Render the scene using the rasterization pipeline [S1].
    *   Compute loss $\mathcal{L}$ and update Gaussian parameters and $\mathcal{G}_l$ [S1].
3.  **Full Shading Training (Iterations 3,001–30,000):**
    *   Enable specular shading. Compute incoming illumination $L_i$ from $\mathcal{G}_l$ [S1].
    *   Encode viewing direction $\omega_o$ using Integrated Directional Encoding (IDE) [S1].
    *   Compute specular color $\mathbf{c}_s = \mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_i, L_i, \omega_o)$ [S1].
    *   Compute total radiance $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c}_d + \mathbf{c}_s$ [S1].
    *   Render the scene [S1].
    *   Compute loss $\mathcal{L}$ and update all parameters [S1].
4.  **Grid Boundary Management:** Midway through training (approx. 15,000 iterations), shrink the illumination grid boundary to fit the current Gaussian distribution and resample the grid to maintain the same number of voxels [S1].
5.  **Adaptive Control:** Apply the same adaptive control of Gaussians as 3DGS to limit the number of Gaussians and units per volume [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def train_3igs(gaussians, training_images, num_iterations=30000):
    # Initialize Gaussians
    for g in gaussians:
        g.mu = initialize_position()
        g.alpha = initialize_opacity()
        g.S = initialize_scale()
        g.R = initialize_rotation()
        # Repurpose 16x3 SH coefficients as BRDF features
        g.rho = repurpose_sh_coefficients() 
        # Add 4 extra params for IDE (base color, roughness)
        g.ide_params = initialize_ide_params()
        g.c_d = initialize_diffuse_color()

    # Initialize Illumination Grid
    grid = FactorisedTensorGrid(resolution=150, rank=R_L)
    grid.set_boundary(scene_bounding_box)

    for step in range(1, num_iterations + 1):
        # Grid Boundary Management: Shrink midway
        if step == 15000:
            grid.shrink_to_gaussians(gaussians)
            grid.resample()

        # Select random camera and image
        camera, target_image = sample_training_data()

        # Render
        rendered_colors = []
        for g in gaussians:
            # Compute screen-space covariance
            W = camera.view_matrix
            J = camera.affine_approx
            Sigma = g.R @ diag(g.S)**2 @ g.R.T
            Sigma_prime = J @ W @ Sigma @ W.T @ J.T
            
            # Project mean
            mu_prime = project(g.mu, W, J)
            
            # Compute 2D Gaussian density at pixel u
            # (Assuming rasterization handles the loop over pixels)
            # For a specific pixel u, compute contribution
            density = gaussian_2d(u, mu_prime, Sigma_prime)
            
            # Compute Incoming Illumination L_i
            L_i = grid.interpolate(g.mu)
            
            # Compute Radiance
            if step <= 3000:
                # Diffuse only
                c = g.c_d
            else:
                # Diffuse + Specular
                # Encode viewing direction
                omega_o = get_view_direction(camera, g.mu)
                ide_encoding = integrated_directional_encoding(omega_o, g.ide_params)
                
                # Neural network for specular
                c_s = neural_network_F(g.rho, L_i, ide_encoding)
                c = g.c_d + c_s
            
            rendered_colors.append(density * g.alpha * c)

        # Alpha blending
        final_color = alpha_blend(rendered_colors)

        # Loss computation
        loss = compute_loss(final_color, target_image, lambda_param=0.2)
        
        # Optimization step
        optimize(gaussians, grid, loss)

    return gaussians, grid
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Grid Boundary:** If the illumination grid boundary is not shrunk midway, it may cover empty space, wasting memory and potentially causing interpolation artifacts at the edges [S1].
*   **Precision:** The factorised tensor representation relies on interpolation. Ensure interpolation method (e.g., trilinear) is consistent with the grid definition [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Standard 3DGS adaptive control handles degenerate Gaussians (e.g., too small or too large) [S1].

## Subtopics

### DAVIGS
Uses differentiable volume rendering with Gaussian primitives but focuses on differentiable volume rendering techniques. Choose when volumetric accuracy is prioritized over rasterization speed. [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)

### Spherical Harmonics Optimization
Optimizes SH coefficients directly for view-dependent color. Choose for simpler view-dependent effects without explicit lighting models. [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)

### SWAG
Appearance-conditioned Gaussians for in-the-wild images. Choose when handling varying appearance conditions (lighting, weather) in uncontrolled environments. [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering speed is comparable to 3DGS due to rasterization and pre-calculated illumination field [S1]. Training involves neural network evaluation per Gaussian, adding overhead compared to base 3DGS.
*   **Memory Complexity:** The illumination grid uses $150^3$ voxels, which is 87.5% less than TensoRF on synthetic datasets [S1]. Memory footprint is low compared to the number of optimized Gaussians [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the Shiny Blender dataset, 3iGS achieves PSNR 27.51, SSIM 0.930, LPIPS 0.045 [S1]. It surpasses 3DGS in scenes with reflective surfaces and specular highlights [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate specular view-dependent effects, fast rendering, memory-efficient illumination field.
*   **Limitations:** Requires neural network evaluation for specular shading, potentially slower training than base 3DGS. Not given in the sources for specific hardware benchmarks.

## Background
3iGS was introduced by Z.J. Tang and T.J. Cham [S1]. It addresses the limitations of 3DGS in modeling specular effects and view-dependent colors by incorporating a local illumination field and BRDF features [S1]. It builds on the 3DGS rasterization pipeline and TensoRF's factorised tensor representation [S1].
