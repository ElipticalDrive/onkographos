# Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation

Also called: 2DGS/1DGS, Conditional Splat, Factorized 3D Gaussian Splatting, Factorized Density, Unnormalized 4D Gaussian probability distribution

## Overview
The Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation decomposes a 3D Gaussian probability density into a product of a marginal distribution along the depth axis and a conditional distribution over the 2D coordinates given the depth, enabling efficient slice-based rendering [S1, unofficial]. It treats the unnormalized 4D Gaussian as a special probability distribution over spatial coordinates $(u, v)$ and time $t$, enabling factorization into temporal and conditional spatial components [S2, unofficial]. This approach provides a computationally efficient way to model volumetric data for slice-based rendering or tomographic reconstruction compared to processing the full 3D density directly [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D Gaussian primitive defined by mean vector $\mu_{3D}$ and covariance matrix $\Sigma_{3D}$.
*   **Output:** Factorized components: the marginal distribution $p(t)$ and the conditional distribution $p(u, v | t)$, which together reconstruct the joint density $p(u, v, t)$.

**Data Structures:**
The 3D Gaussian is represented by:
*   $\mu_{3D} = [\mu_x, \mu_y, \mu_z]^\top$: The mean vector [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Sigma_{3D}$: The $3 \times 3$ symmetric covariance matrix with elements $\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{xy}, \sigma_{xz}, \sigma_{yy}, \sigma_{yz}, \sigma_{zz}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Mathematical Formulation:**
The joint probability density is defined as:
$$ p(u, v, t) = (2\pi)^{-3/2} \det(\Sigma)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v, t | \mu, \Sigma) $$
where $\mathcal{N}$ denotes the Gaussian kernel [S2, unofficial].

The factorization relies on the identity:
$$ p(u, v, t) = p(t) \cdot p(u, v | t) $$

1.  **Marginal Distribution $p(t)$:**
    The marginal density along the depth axis $t$ is a 1D Gaussian:
    $$ p(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_z, \sigma_{zz}) $$
    where $\mu_z$ and $\sigma_{zz}$ are the mean and variance of the 3D Gaussian along the z-axis [S1, unofficial]. Formally:
    $$ p(t) = (2\pi)^{-1/2} \det(\Sigma_t)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(t | \mu_t, \Sigma_t) $$
    where $\Sigma_t = \sigma_{zz}$ [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Conditional Distribution $p(u, v | t)$:**
    Conditioned on a specific depth $t$, the distribution over the in-plane coordinates $(u, v)$ is a 2D Gaussian:
    $$ p(u, v | t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{uv|t}, \Sigma_{uv|t}) $$
    
    The **conditional mean** $\mu_{uv|t}$ is shifted linearly according to the offset $t - \mu_z$:
    $$ \mu_{uv|t} = \begin{bmatrix} \mu_u \\ \mu_v \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mu_x \\ \mu_y \end{bmatrix} + \frac{t - \mu_z}{\sigma_{zz}} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xz} \\ \sigma_{yz} \end{bmatrix} $$
    This relation reflects how the mean position of the Gaussian footprint in the image plane moves as we slice through different depth levels [S1, unofficial].

    The **conditional covariance** $\Sigma_{uv|t}$ is given by the Schur complement:
    $$ \Sigma_{uv|t} = \Sigma_{uv} - \Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} $$
    Explicitly, using the elements of $\Sigma_{3D}$:
    $$ \Sigma_{uv|t} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} & \sigma_{xy} \\ \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{yy} \end{bmatrix} - \frac{1}{\sigma_{zz}} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xz} \\ \sigma_{yz} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xz} & \sigma_{yz} \end{bmatrix} $$
    $$ \Sigma_{uv|t} = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} - \frac{\sigma_{xz}^2}{\sigma_{zz}} & \sigma_{xy} - \frac{\sigma_{xz}\sigma_{yz}}{\sigma_{zz}} \\ \sigma_{xy} - \frac{\sigma_{xz}\sigma_{yz}}{\sigma_{zz}} & \sigma_{yy} - \frac{\sigma_{yz}^2}{\sigma_{zz}} \end{bmatrix} $$
    This matrix accounts for how the uncertainty in $u$ and $v$ directions reduces once the depth $t$ is fixed, which is critical for accurate slice projection [S1, unofficial].

    Formally, the conditional density is:
    $$ p(u, v | t) = (2\pi)^{-1} \det(\Sigma_{uv|t})^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v | \mu_{uv|t}, \Sigma_{uv|t}) $$
    [S2, unofficial].

**Proof of Factorization:**
The factorization holds because $\det(\Sigma) = \det(\Sigma_t) \det(\Sigma_{uv|t})$. This is derived from the block decomposition of $\Sigma$:
$$ \Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{uv} & \Sigma_{uv,t} \\ \Sigma_{uv,t}^\top & \Sigma_t \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{uv|t} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ -\Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} & I \end{bmatrix} $$
where $\Sigma_{uv,t} = [\sigma_{xz}, \sigma_{yz}]^\top$ and $\Sigma_{t,uv} = [\sigma_{xz}, \sigma_{yz}]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def gaussian_conditional_formulation(mu_3d, sigma_3d, t):
    """
    Computes the conditional 2D Gaussian parameters for a 3D Gaussian
    at a specific depth slice t.
    
    Args:
        mu_3d: Array [mu_x, mu_y, mu_z]
        sigma_3d: 3x3 Covariance matrix [[sxx, sxy, sxz], [sxy, syy, syz], [sxz, syz, szz]]
        t: Scalar depth value
        
    Returns:
        mu_uv_cond: Array [mu_u, mu_v]
        sigma_uv_cond: 2x2 Conditional covariance matrix
        p_t: Scalar marginal probability density at t (unnormalized)
    """
    mu_x, mu_y, mu_z = mu_3d
    sxx, sxy, sxz = sigma_3d[0]
    syy, syz = sigma_3d[1][1], sigma_3d[1][2]
    szz = sigma_3d[2][2]
    
    # 1. Marginal Distribution p(t)
    # p(t) ~ N(mu_z, sigma_zz)
    # Unnormalized density value
    p_t = (2 * 3.141592653589793 * szz)**(-0.5) * exp(-0.5 * ((t - mu_z)**2) / szz)
    
    # 2. Conditional Mean mu_uv|t
    # [mu_u; mu_v] = [mu_x; mu_y] + (t - mu_z)/sigma_zz * [sigma_xz; sigma_yz]
    offset = (t - mu_z) / szz
    mu_u = mu_x + offset * sxz
    mu_v = mu_y + offset * syz
    mu_uv_cond = [mu_u, mu_v]
    
    # 3. Conditional Covariance Sigma_uv|t
    # Sigma_uv|t = Sigma_uv - Sigma_uv,t * Sigma_t^-1 * Sigma_t,uv
    # Sigma_uv = [[sxx, sxy], [sxy, syy]]
    # Sigma_uv,t = [sxz, syz]^T
    # Sigma_t = [szz]
    
    term = (1.0 / szz) * [[sxz * sxz, sxz * syz], [syz * sxz, syz * syz]]
    
    sigma_uv_cond = [
        [sxx - term[0][0], sxy - term[0][1]],
        [sxy - term[1][0], syy - term[1][1]]
    ]
    
    return mu_uv_cond, sigma_uv_cond, p_t
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\mu_{3D}$: Mean vector [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Sigma_{3D}$: Covariance matrix [S1, unofficial].
*   $t$: Depth slice coordinate.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Singular Covariance:** If $\sigma_{zz} \approx 0$, the division in the mean shift and covariance calculation becomes unstable. The sources do not specify a guard, but numerically, $\sigma_{zz}$ must be non-zero for the conditional formulation to be valid [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The conditional covariance accounts for the reduction in uncertainty. Incorrect calculation of the Schur complement leads to inaccurate slice projection [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [N-Dimensional Gaussian (6DGS/7DGS)](9230-n-dimensional-gaussian-6dgs-7dgs.md)
Extends the formulation to model joint distributions over non-spatial dimensions like viewing direction or time, conditioning on queries to derive view-specific or time-specific 3D Gaussians. It differs from standard 3DGS by explicitly encoding dependencies between geometry and observation parameters, allowing for more compact representation of view-dependent effects and dynamic scenes.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The factorized approach is computationally efficient for slice-based rendering or tomographic reconstruction compared to processing the full 3D density directly [S1, unofficial]. The conditional parameters can be computed in $O(1)$ per slice per Gaussian.
*   **Strengths:** Enables efficient volumetric modeling by decoupling depth and in-plane coordinates. Accurately captures the elliptical footprint of a 3D Gaussian on arbitrary depth slices [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires explicit handling of the depth axis (or time axis in 4DGS). The formulation assumes a Gaussian distribution; deviations from Gaussianity in the data may reduce accuracy [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Slice-based rendering, tomographic reconstruction, or dynamic scene representation where factorization along a specific dimension (depth/time) simplifies computation [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Background
The Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation is a mathematical technique applied to 3D Gaussian Splatting to facilitate efficient rendering and reconstruction. It is central to methods like 2DGS/1DGS and 4D Gaussian Splatting. The method leverages standard Gaussian conditional probability properties to decompose higher-dimensional densities into manageable lower-dimensional components.
