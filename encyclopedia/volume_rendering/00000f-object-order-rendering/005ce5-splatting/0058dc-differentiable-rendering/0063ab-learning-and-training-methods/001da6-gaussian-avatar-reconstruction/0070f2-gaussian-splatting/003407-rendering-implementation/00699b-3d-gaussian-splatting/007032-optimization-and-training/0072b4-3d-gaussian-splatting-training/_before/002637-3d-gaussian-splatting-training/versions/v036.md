# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3DGS Learning, G Splats

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting Training is the iterative optimization process that adjusts the parameters of a set of 3D Gaussian primitives to minimize the photometric difference between rendered images and ground truth views [S2][S6, unofficial]. It solves the problem of reconstructing a high-fidelity radiance field from sparse or dense input images by replacing neural network weights with explicit geometric primitives [S5, unofficial]. Unlike NeRF-based methods which optimize implicit MLPs, 3DGS training is non-deep learning and significantly faster, achieving comparable image quality with real-time rendering capabilities [S2][S5, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [GauFRe](7994-gaufre.md) by relying on explicit adaptive density control (splitting/cloning) rather than frequency-domain regularization, and from [3DGS Initialization Policy](1398-3dgs-initialization-policy.md) by covering the full optimization loop including densification and pruning [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images with known camera poses (extrinsics $R_{cw}, t_{cw}$ and intrinsics $K$) and initial 3D Gaussian estimates derived from Structure from Motion (SfM) [S2][S6, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian parameters: positions $\mathbf{p}_i$, rotations $q_i$, scales $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacities $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $h_i$ [S6, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian primitive $i$ is defined by:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (initialized from SfM points) [S2][S6, unofficial].
*   Rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion) [S2].
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (initialized uniformly between 0 and 1) [S2].
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$ (initialized to a constant value, typically low) [S2][S6, unofficial].
*   Appearance: Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients $h_i$ (initialized from SfM colors) [S2][S6, unofficial].

**Rotation Matrix:**
The unit quaternion $q = (q_r, q_i, q_j, q_k)$ is converted to a rotation matrix $R$ to compute the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ [S2]:
$$
R(q) = 2 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} - (q_j^2 + q_k^2) & q_i q_j - q_r q_k & q_i q_k + q_r q_j \\ q_i q_j + q_r q_k & \frac{1}{2} - (q_i^2 + q_k^2) & q_j q_k - q_r q_i \\ q_i q_k - q_r q_j & q_j q_k + q_r q_i & \frac{1}{2} - (q_i^2 + q_j^2) \end{bmatrix}
$$
The covariance is then $\Sigma = R \text{diag}(\mathbf{s}) \text{diag}(\mathbf{s})^T R^T$ [S2].

**Loss Function:**
The optimization minimizes a composite loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered image $\gamma$ and ground truth $\gamma_{gt}$ [S6, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}
$$
where $\lambda$ is the SSIM weight (default 0.2) [S4, S6, unofficial].

**Gradient Computation:**
Gradients are computed via backpropagation through the differentiable rasterization pipeline [S6, unofficial]. The Jacobians for the parameters are derived using the chain rule [S6, unofficial]:
*   **Rotation:** $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i}$ [S6, unofficial].
*   **Scale:** $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i}$ [S6, unofficial].
*   **SH Coefficients:** $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i}$ [S6, unofficial].
*   **Opacity:** $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i}$ [S6, unofficial].
*   **Position:** The position gradient involves three terms accounting for depth sorting, color, and covariance changes:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{w,i}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}}
    $$
    [S6, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `numIters`: Total optimization steps (default 30,000) [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree`: SH degree (default 3) [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree_interval`: Steps to increase SH degree (default 1,000) [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   `resolution_schedule`: Steps between doubling resolution (default 3,000) [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   `densifyFrom`: Step densification begins (default 500) [S3, unofficial].
*   `densifyUntil`: Step densification stops (default $\min(15000, \text{numIters}/2)$) [S3, unofficial].
*   `refineEvery`: Steps between densification/pruning (default 500) [S3, unofficial].
*   `densify_grad_thresh`: Gradient threshold for densification (default 0.0002) [S4, unofficial].
*   `densify_size_thresh`: Size threshold for densification (default 0.01) [S4, unofficial].
*   `split_screen_size`: Screen size threshold for splitting (default 0.05) [S4, unofficial].
*   `stop_screen_size_at`: Step to stop screen-size splitting (default 4,000) [S4, unofficial].
*   `maxGaussians`: Max primitives (default 5,000,000) [S3, unofficial].

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize positions $\mathbf{p}$ from SfM points. Initialize scales $\mathbf{s}$ uniformly in $[0, 1]$. Initialize rotations $q$ as unit quaternions. Initialize opacities $\alpha$ to a small constant. Initialize SH coefficients $h$ from SfM colors [S2][S6, unofficial].
2.  **Iteration Loop:** For step $i$ from 1 to `numIters`:
    a.  **Camera Sampling:** Select a training camera $V$ and ground truth image $\hat{I}$ [S2].
    b.  **Rendering:** Rasterize the Gaussians using camera $V$ to obtain rendered image $I$ [S2].
    c.  **Loss:** Compute $\mathcal{L}(I, \hat{I})$ [S2].
    d.  **Backpropagation:** Compute gradients $\nabla \mathcal{L}$ for all parameters [S2].
    e.  **Update:** Update parameters using the Adam optimizer [S2].
    f.  **Adaptive Density Control:** If $i$ is a refinement iteration (e.g., every `refineEvery` steps):
        i.  **Pruning:** For each Gaussian, if $\alpha < \epsilon$ or the Gaussian is too large (screen size $> \text{split\_screen\_size}$), prune it [S2].
        ii. **Densification:** If the positional gradient magnitude $\|\nabla_p \mathcal{L}\| > \tau_p$:
            *   **Over-reconstruction:** If the Gaussian scale norm $\|\mathbf{s}\| > \tau_s$ (or screen size is large), split the Gaussian into two [S2].
            *   **Under-reconstruction:** Otherwise, clone the Gaussian [S2].
    g.  **Scheduler:** Advance learning rate schedulers [S4, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Initialization
means = initialize_from_sfm(points)
scales = random_uniform(size=(N, 3), low=0, high=1)
quats = random_unit_quaternions(size=N)
opacities = constant_value(size=N, value=0.1) # Typical initial opacity
sh_coeffs = initialize_from_sfm_colors(points)

optimizer = Adam([means, scales, quats, opacities, sh_coeffs], lr=1e-4)

for step in range(1, num_iters + 1):
    # 1. Camera Sampling
    cam_idx = camera_iterator.next()
    cam = cameras[cam_idx]
    gt_image = load_ground_truth(cam, downscale_factor_for_step(step))
    
    # 2. Rendering
    rendered_image = rasterize(means, scales, quats, opacities, sh_coeffs, cam)
    
    # 3. Loss
    loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, ssim_weight=0.2)
    
    # 4. Backpropagation
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    
    # 5. Update
    optimizer.step()
    
    # 6. Adaptive Density Control (every refine_every steps)
    if step % refine_every == 0:
        gradients = get_gradients() # Specifically positional gradients
        
        for i in range(num_gaussians):
            # Pruning
            if opacities[i] < opacity_threshold or screen_size(means[i], scales[i], quats[i], cam) > split_screen_size:
                prune_gaussian(i)
                continue
            
            # Densification
            if norm(gradients[i]) > densify_grad_threshold:
                if norm(scales[i]) > densify_size_threshold or screen_size(means[i], scales[i], quats[i], cam) > split_screen_size:
                    split_gaussian(i) # Over-reconstruction
                else:
                    clone_gaussian(i) # Under-reconstruction

    # 7. Scheduler
    scheduler.step()
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than NeRFs, typically completing in minutes to hours depending on scene complexity and resolution [S2][S5, unofficial]. The C++ implementation with Metal backend achieves training in ~90 seconds on an M4 Max [S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians. Default max is 5,000,000 primitives [S3, unofficial]. Sinc splatting variants consume less memory than standard Gaussians [S7].
*   **Performance:** Achieves real-time rendering speeds (100+ FPS) at 1080p on modern GPUs [S5, unofficial]. PSNR and SSIM are comparable to NeRFs [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Fast training, real-time rendering, explicit representation allows for easy editing and compression [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce "floaters" in empty space or transparent regions if not properly pruned [S1]. Sensitive to initialization quality (SfM points) [S2].
*   **Hardware:** Supports CUDA, MPS, and CPU (CPU is ~100x slower) [S3, S4, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S2]. It builds on the concept of splatting (rasterizing 3D primitives to 2D) and uses adaptive density control to optimize a set of 3D Gaussians. The method was developed to address the slow training and rendering speeds of NeRFs while maintaining high visual fidelity [S2][S5, unofficial]. It has since become a standard baseline for radiance field optimization and has inspired numerous variants for dynamic scenes, SLAM, and physical rendering [S1].
