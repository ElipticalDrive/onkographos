# Semantic and Structural Extensions

Also called: Feature-augmented 3DGS, Dynamic 3DGS, Avatar 3DGS

## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting [2812-3d-gaussian-splatting.md] by attaching high-dimensional feature vectors to Gaussian primitives for semantic understanding or by integrating deformation pipelines for animatable human and articulated avatars. It solves the problem of integrating 2D vision-language models (VLMs) into 3D representations and simulating geometrically changing bodies in continuous movements, addressing the limitation of standard 3DGS which assumes static scenes. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by accumulating feature vectors via differentiable alpha-blending instead of just color, and from sibling methods like [3D Gaussian Splatting Differentiable Rasterizer](2813-3d-gaussian-splatting-differentiable-rasterizer.md) by focusing on the semantic attribute representation and deformation mechanics rather than the rasterization engine itself.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiable alpha-blending accumulation of feature vectors during the rasterization step. For a pixel $p$, the rendered feature vector $\mathbf{F}(p)$ is computed by sorting overlapping Gaussians $\mathcal{O}_p$ front-to-back and applying the following formula :

$$
\mathbf{F}(p) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}_p} \mathbf{f}_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$

where:
*   $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature vector (semantic, language, or latent) of the $i$-th Gaussian.
*   $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian.
*   $\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance from all preceding Gaussians $j < i$.
*   If $\mathcal{O}_p$ is empty, $\mathbf{F}(p) = \mathbf{0}$.

The feature dimension $d$ varies by method (e.g., 512 for CLIP, 768 for LSeg/CLIP text, or compressed latent dimensions). The rendering pipeline produces a 2D feature map $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times d}$, which is subsequently compared against text embeddings or decoded to produce segmentation masks.

For structural extensions, all methods share a canonical space representation where 3D Gaussians are initialized in a reference pose (e.g., T-pose or neutral expression). Deformation is achieved by mapping these canonical Gaussians to a target pose defined by parameters (e.g., skeleton joints, expression coefficients). The rendering pipeline remains differentiable, allowing optimization of Gaussian attributes and deformation parameters via photometric loss.

## Subtopics

### [Semantic and Language Embeddings](8130-semantic-and-language-embeddings.md)
This method extends 3D Gaussian Splatting [2812-3d-gaussian-splatting.md] by attaching high-dimensional feature vectors to Gaussian primitives, enabling the rendering of semantic, language, or latent feature maps alongside color. It solves the problem of integrating 2D vision-language models (VLMs) into 3D representations, allowing for real-time open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, phrase grounding, and 3D visual question answering. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by accumulating feature vectors via differentiable alpha-blending instead of just color, and from sibling methods like [3D Gaussian Splatting Differentiable Rasterizer](2813-3d-gaussian-splatting-differentiable-rasterizer.md) by focusing on the semantic attribute representation rather than the rasterization engine itself. [8130-semantic-and-language-embeddings.md]

### [Human and Articulated Avatars](8209-human-and-articulated-avatars.md)
This method extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct and render animatable avatars of humans and articulated non-human models from multi-view video. It solves the problem of simulating geometrically changing bodies in continuous movements while rendering rich textures, addressing the limitation of standard 3DGS which assumes static scenes. It sets itself apart from static rendering methods by integrating deformation pipelines (such as mesh binding, tetrahedral cages, or neural motion fields) that drive canonical Gaussians into target poses, enabling real-time novel view synthesis of dynamic subjects. [8209-human-and-articulated-avatars.md]

### 3D Gaussian Splatting on Mesh Surfaces
GVA [222] proposes a surface-guided Gaussians re-initialization strategy to make the trained Gaussians better fit the input monocular video, addressing the unbalanced aggregation of Gaussians caused by densification and splitting operations. HAHA [223] attaches Gaussians onto the surface of a mesh and combines rendered results from a textured human body mesh and Gaussians together to reduce the number of Gaussians [S1].

## Background
For human head modeling with 3DGS, MonoGaussianAvatar [214] first applies 3DGS to dynamic head reconstruction using the canonical space modeling and deformation prediction. Further, PSAvatar [224] introduces the explicit FLAME face model [225] to initialize Gaussians, which can capture the high-fidelity facial geometry and even complicated volumetric objects (e.g., glasses). The tri-plane representation and the motion fields are used in GaussianHead [226] to simulate geometrically changing heads in continuous movements and render them [S1].
