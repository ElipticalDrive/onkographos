# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS addresses the over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts common in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by applying progressive frequency regularization to the Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on spatial smoothing or depth constraints, FreGS operates in the frequency domain, penalizing discrepancies between the Fourier transforms of the rendered and ground-truth images to guide densification toward meaningful details while suppressing noise [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Current set of 3D Gaussians (position $\mathbf{p}$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}$), camera parameters, and ground-truth image $I_{gt}$.
*   **Outputs:** Updated Gaussian parameters after one training step.

**Data Structures:**
*   Standard 3DGS Gaussian attributes.
*   Frequency domain buffers for rendered image $I_{render}$ and ground truth $I_{gt}$.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Render:** Render the current Gaussian scene to obtain image $I_{render}$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Transform:** Compute the 2D Fourier transform of the rendered image and the ground truth image:
    $$ F_{render} = \mathcal{F}(I_{render}) $$
    $$ F_{gt} = \mathcal{F}(I_{gt}) $$
    where $\mathcal{F}$ denotes the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) [S2].
3.  **Compute Frequency Loss:** Calculate the discrepancy in the frequency domain. The sources indicate a focus on amplitude and phase discrepancies, though the exact weighted formula is not explicitly detailed in the provided notes [S2]. A common implementation involves the $L_1$ or $L_2$ norm of the difference in magnitude or complex values:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{freq} = \| F_{render} - F_{gt} \| $$
    [G: In practice, this is often applied to the magnitude spectrum $|F|$ to be invariant to phase shifts, or as a complex loss. The specific weighting between amplitude and phase is not given in the sources.]
4.  **Progressive Application:** Apply the regularization progressively during training. This typically involves starting with a low weight for the frequency loss and increasing it, or applying it only after an initial phase of standard photometric optimization to stabilize the Gaussian cloud [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters and update them via stochastic gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Frequency Loss Weight ($\lambda_{freq}$):** Not given in the sources.
*   **Progression Schedule:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **FFT Boundary Effects:** The FFT assumes periodicity, which can introduce artifacts at image boundaries if not handled (e.g., via padding) [G].
*   **Gradient Oscillation:** As noted in related work (Faster-GS), scaling and rotation gradients can oscillate; FreGS focuses on frequency but may benefit from similar stabilization techniques [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Frequency Regularization
The broader category of methods using Fourier domain constraints. FreGS is a specific implementation using progressive regularization for 3DGS [6130-frequency-regularization.md].

### Smoothed 2D Supervision
Uses spatial blurring to stabilize gradients, contrasting with FreGS's frequency-domain approach [7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md].

### 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting
Modifies covariance to enforce planarity, a geometric constraint rather than a frequency one [9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md].

### Hard and Soft Depth Regularization
Uses depth estimates for geometric supervision, distinct from the image-frequency focus of FreGS [9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md].

### Cross-scale geometric adaptation
Adapts geometric constraints across scales, not primarily frequency-based [10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md].

### Geometry Regularization
General term for geometric constraints, of which FreGS is a frequency-specific variant [14222-geometry-regularization.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds the cost of two FFTs per iteration ($O(N \log N)$ for image size $N$) plus the gradient computation. This increases training time per step compared to standard 3DGS [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency domain buffers for the rendered and ground truth images, adding $O(N)$ memory overhead [S2].
*   **Performance:** Reduces over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts, leading to cleaner images [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Strengths:** Effective at suppressing noise and preventing the "fried egg" or over-sharp artifacts common in 3DGS [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May slow down training due to FFT computation; requires careful tuning of the progressive schedule [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene exhibits over-reconstruction or high-frequency noise artifacts in standard 3DGS training [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When not to use:** When training speed is critical and artifacts are minimal, or when the scene is inherently low-frequency [S2].

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address the issue of over-reconstruction in 3D Gaussian Splatting by regularizing frequency discrepancies [S2]. It is part of a broader effort to improve the quality of 3DGS reconstructions by incorporating frequency-domain constraints [S1, unofficial]. The method is cited in surveys on 3DGS rasterization and compression, highlighting its role in artifact reduction [S1, unofficial][S2].
