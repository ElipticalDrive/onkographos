# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Coarse-to-Fine Training, Stage-wise Optimization, Curriculum Learning for 3DGS

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training decomposes the optimization of 3D Gaussian primitives into distinct phases or stages to stabilize convergence, prevent local minima, and decouple conflicting learning objectives (e.g., geometry vs. pose, or coarse structure vs. fine detail) [S1, 7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training, which optimizes all parameters simultaneously from initialization, this family of methods introduces structural constraints, simplified representations, or frozen parameters in early stages to provide a robust prior for subsequent refinement [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [GroupFlow](6351-groupflow.md) or [3DGS-MCMC](7852-3dgs-mcmc.md) by explicitly segmenting the training timeline into sequential, often heterogeneous, optimization regimes rather than relying on dynamic metric-based triggers alone [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian primitive set $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md). The core mechanism involves segmenting the training loop into $K$ stages, where each stage $k$ has specific:
1.  **Active Parameters:** A subset of Gaussian attributes (e.g., position, scale, SH coefficients) or external parameters (e.g., camera poses, neural network weights) are optimized, while others are frozen [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md].
2.  **Supervision Signal:** The loss function $\mathcal{L}$ may change between stages, incorporating different terms (e.g., pose loss, perceptual loss, or simplified photometric loss) [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md, 12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md].
3.  **Representation Complexity:** The level of detail in the Gaussian representation is often increased progressively, such as by enabling higher-degree Spherical Harmonics (SH) or densification only after a coarse geometry is established [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md, 17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
Decouples initialization from geometric refinement by training in a coarse stage (directionally invariant RGB, no SH) followed by a fine stage (full SH, consistency filtering) [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md]. Choose this when initialization is sparse and prone to instability.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines a multi-stage approach for **MeshGS** that classifies Gaussians into tightly-bound ($G^t$) and loosely-bound ($G^l$) sets, applying mesh-aligned constraints and specialized densification only to $G^t$ [7448-training-objective-and-densification.md]. Choose this when integrating explicit mesh geometry to prevent Gaussian drift.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines an initial Gaussian representation (from SfM or feed-forward networks) using iterative optimization with densification [9138-fine-tuning.md]. Choose this for per-scene adaptation or style transfer where a static prior is insufficient.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Stabilizes volumetric optimization by first supervising with sparse planar slices (Stage 1) to establish local continuity, then with dense voxels (Stage 2) for global guidance [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md]. Choose this for volumetric data (e.g., MRI) where random sampling causes instability.

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
Decouples geometry and pose learning in feed-forward rendering via three stages: Pose-Aware Geometry Initialization, Pose-Free Attribute Refinement (using GT poses), and End-to-End Pose-Free Fine-Tuning [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md]. Choose this when camera poses are unknown or noisy.

### [Free-View Training (FVT)](15162-free-view-training-fvt.md)
Disentangles generalizable 3DGS performance from input view count by supervising on broader view interpolations between sampled context views [15162-free-view-training-fvt.md]. Choose this for novel view synthesis across varying view densities.

### [Training Loop with Mixed Losses and Post-Processing](17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md)
Implements a single-stage loop with progressive SH degree activation, mixed photometric/depth losses, and bilateral grid post-processing [17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md]. Choose this for high-fidelity reconstruction with multi-modal (RGB+Depth) supervision.

### [Training Loop with Structure-Aware Densification](22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md)
Accelerates convergence by replacing reactive densification with structure-aware triggers based on precomputed frequency metrics, evaluated periodically [22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md]. Choose this when standard densification is too slow or produces redundant primitives.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud in a fronto-parallel manner, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
Decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability [S3].
**Step:**
1.  Construct RGB point clouds for each input view.
2.  Project point clouds to the target view using differentiable point cloud rendering.
3.  Aggregate point clouds to render RGB images at the target view.
4.  Optimize the depth network using photometric loss between rendered and ground truth target images.
5.  Jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) with the initialized depth network [S3].
**Parameter:** ~0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
**Pitfall:** Jointly training depth and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

### Two-Stage Triplane and MLP Training
Trains triplanes and MLP in two stages for 3D neural field generation [S4].
**Step:**
1.  Jointly train triplanes and MLP on 500 random shapes (batch size 1, 500k points/object, 200 epochs, LR 1e-3).
2.  Freeze shared MLP. Train triplane features for each object individually (batch size 200k points, 30 epochs, LR 1e-3) [S4].
**Parameter:** Triplane dim: $128 \times 128 \times 32 \times 3$; Init: $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.1)$; MLP: Fourier mapping + 3x128 ReLU layers [S4].
**Number:** Stage 1: ~1 day/RTX 2080ti; Stage 2: 10 min/shape [S4].

### Continual Model Refinement
Augments initial models with closer-up images to simulate prolonged interaction [S5].
**Step:** Optimize for up to 4000 iterations, including 400 iterations of densification [S5].
**Parameter:** Final Gaussian count: 20k–50k; Speed: >500 iters/sec [S5].
**Pitfall:** Marginal improvement after 2000 iterations; foveated views decrease quality by 5–10% [S5].
**Comparison:** Fixed vs. variable foveation points yield <1% quality difference [S5].

### Projection-Based Frequency Violation Metric
Applies a three-channel anisotropic criterion where each Gaussian axis is evaluated against its directional frequency content [S6].
**Number:** On Mip-NeRF360, $\eta^{(proj)}$ achieves PSNR 27.20 and LPIPS 0.203 [S6].
**Comparison:** Theoretically more precise than scalar wavelength metrics but yields worse LPIPS in practice due to inaccurate axis-direction alignment during early training [S6].

## Background
The concept of progressive training in neural rendering originated from the need to stabilize the optimization of implicit representations and point-based fields. Early works in NeRF demonstrated that learning high-frequency details and coarse geometry simultaneously leads to local minima [G]. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, which relies on differentiable rasterization of explicit primitives, the sparse initialization from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) often results in "floating" artifacts or insufficient coverage. The **Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy** [7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md] and **Free-View Training** [15162-free-view-training-fvt.md] address these by structuring the training timeline. **Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction** [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md] extends this to feed-forward settings where pose uncertainty is high. The family has evolved to include specialized variants for volumetric data [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md], mesh alignment [7448-training-objective-and-densification.md], and efficiency [22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md].
