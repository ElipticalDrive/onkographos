# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization mechanism that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. It provides a stronger and more robust constraint than standard depth priors, achieving lower final depth error by progressively refining geometry from coarse to fine scales [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$, multi-scale depth maps $D_l(p)$ for scale $l \in \{0, \dots, L\}$, and RGB maps $C_i, C_j$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for each training ray $r_{train}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxels:** The scene is represented by voxels at $L+1$ resolution levels (Low, Mid, High). Low-resolution voxels capture coarse geometry; high-resolution voxels capture details [S1].
*   **Pixel Coordinates:** $p_i$ is a 2D pixel coordinate in frame $i$. $\tilde{p}_i$ is its homogeneous augmentation.
*   **3D Points:** $x_{li}$ is the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$ at scale $l$. $x_l$ is the 3D point in world coordinates. $x_{li \to j}$ is the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $j$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Reproject to 3D (Source Frame):** For a pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$ and scale $l$, compute the depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ from volume rendering. Compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$:
    $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    Alternatively, using the ray origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
    $$ x_l = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
    [S1]

2.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]

3.  **Transform to Target Camera Coordinates:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]

4.  **Project to 2D (Target Frame):** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].

5.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the error between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel from frame $j$:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]

6.  **Select Pseudo-Ground Truth Depth:** Identify the scale $l$ with the minimum reprojection error for the training ray $r_{train}$:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]

7.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the geometric adaptation loss as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth at scale $l$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'$ for all training rays:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Scales:** Typically 3 scales (Low, Mid, High) [S1].
*   **Weighting:** $\lambda_{geo}$ is the weighting factor for $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ in the total loss [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Poses:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may suffer [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Scale Mismatch:** Using only depth priors ($\mathcal{L}_d$) can suffer from scale mismatches later in training, whereas $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ refines depth progressively [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for reprojection and multi-scale depth comparison per training iteration. However, it eliminates the need for complex scheduling or ground truth pose sampling [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale depth maps and RGB maps for reprojection [S1].
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales [S1]. Achieves lower final depth error than depth prior $\mathcal{L}_d$ alone [S1]. Combining $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and $\mathcal{L}_d$ yields the best results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no explicit depth GT needed, robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warped methods like GeCoNeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate camera poses; performance may degrade with very sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in **FrugalNeRF** (2025) by the authors of [S1]. It addresses the challenge of few-shot novel view synthesis by using weight-sharing voxels across scales and guiding geometry via reprojection errors. It is designed to accelerate training and regularize geometry without external priors [S1]. It is distinct from GeCoNeRF, which warps features and is sensitive to geometric noise, and FSGS, which requires a sufficient initial set of Gaussians [S1].
