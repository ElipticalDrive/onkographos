# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by dynamically selecting pseudo-ground truth depths based on minimal reprojection error across scales, rather than relying on a single fixed depth prior or complex scheduling [S1]. This approach significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training images (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics ($K_i, K_j$), camera poses ($R_i, t_i, R_j, t_j$), multi-scale voxel representations, and rendered depth maps $D_{il}(p_i)$ for each scale $l$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ and pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for each ray.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxels:** Multi-scale voxel grid. Low-resolution voxels capture coarse geometry; high-resolution voxels capture fine details [S1].
*   **Ray:** Defined by pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$, origin $o_i(p_i)$, and direction $v_i(p_i)$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Reproject to 3D (Frame $i$):** For a pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$ and its homogeneous augmentation $\tilde{p}_i$, compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$ at scale $l$:
    $$x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i$$
    where $D_{il}(p_i)$ is the depth at scale $l$ obtained from volume rendering [S1].
2.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates $x_l$ using frame $i$'s rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$:
    $$x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i$$
    [S1]
3.  **Reproject to Frame $j$:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using frame $j$'s rotation $R_j$ and translation $t_j$:
    $$x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j)$$
    [S1]
4.  **Project to 2D (Frame $j$):** Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$:
    $$\tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j})$$
    where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
5.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the error for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel from frame $j$:
    $$e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$$
    where $C_i$ and $C_j$ are the RGB maps of frames $i$ and $j$ [S1].
6.  **Select Pseudo-Ground Truth:** Identify the scale $l$ with the minimum reprojection error for the ray $r_{train}$:
    $$D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train}))$$
    [S1]
7.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth $D_{il}(r_{train})$ and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ across all training rays $R_{train}$:
    $$L_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \sum_{r_{train} \in R_{train}} (D_{il}(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}))^2$$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Training Schedule:** Early training uses low-resolution voxels as pseudo-ground truth to guide coarse geometry learning. Medium- and high-resolution voxels gradually refine details as training advances [S1].
*   **Comparison with Depth Prior:** $L_{geo}$ provides a stronger and more robust constraint than the standard depth prior $L_d$, achieving lower final depth error [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Poses:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may be challenging [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Initial Sparsity:** In few-shot scenarios, if the initial set of Gaussians/voxels is extremely sparse, the method may struggle, though it is more robust than methods like GeCoNeRF which are sensitive to geometric noise [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for reprojection calculations across multiple scales and frames. However, it eliminates the need for complex scheduling or ground truth pose sampling [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel representations and intermediate 3D points for reprojection [S1].
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. $L_{geo}$ alone achieves lower final depth error than $L_d$ alone, though combining both yields the best results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no external priors needed, robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warping methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on accurate camera poses; performance may degrade with very sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in **FrugalNeRF** [S1]. It addresses the challenge of few-shot novel view synthesis by using weight-sharing voxels across scales to capture varying scene frequencies and employing reprojection errors to guide multi-scale geometry learning [S1]. The method was designed to accelerate training and regularize geometry without relying on learned priors or explicit depth sensors [S1].
