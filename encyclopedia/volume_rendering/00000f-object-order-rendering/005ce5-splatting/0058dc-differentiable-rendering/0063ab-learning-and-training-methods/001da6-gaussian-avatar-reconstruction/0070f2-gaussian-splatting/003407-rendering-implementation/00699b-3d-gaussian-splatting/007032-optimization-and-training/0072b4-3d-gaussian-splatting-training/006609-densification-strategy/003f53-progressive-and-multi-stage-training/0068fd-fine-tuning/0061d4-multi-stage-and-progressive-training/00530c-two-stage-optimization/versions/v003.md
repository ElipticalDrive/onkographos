# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: GS-IR Training Process

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training protocol that decouples the learning of stable geometric/appearance representations from the optimization of secondary properties (such as material, lighting, or compression efficiency) to prevent training instability [S1][S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem where simultaneous optimization of complex, coupled parameters (e.g., Gaussian primitives and a quantization bottleneck, or geometry and physically based rendering materials) leads to convergence failure or suboptimal rate-distortion trade-offs [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from single-stage training by isolating the "codec-free" or "geometry-only" phase to establish a robust baseline, followed by a "codec-aware" or "decomposition" phase that refines specific attributes while freezing the foundational representation [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of two distinct optimization phases. The specific implementation varies slightly depending on the target application (compression vs. inverse rendering), but the structural logic remains consistent: Stage 1 establishes the base representation, and Stage 2 optimizes the target-specific attributes.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images and poses), target view images (for supervision), and hyperparameters (learning rates, loss weights, rate parameters).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized Gaussian primitives (positions, scales, rotations, opacities, colors/materials) and, if applicable, a trained feature codec or material/lighting parameters.

### Data Structures
The data structures depend on the variant:
1.  **CodecSplat Variant [S2, unofficial]:**
    *   **Gaussian Set ($G$):** Predicted from features $F_g$ and encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$.
    *   **Feature Codec:** Compresses the intermediate feature tensor $F_g$ into a latent representation. Includes a post-codec refiner.
    *   **Depth Maps:** Encoder-side depth $D_{enc} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times H \times W}$ and decoder-side normalized inverse-depth $S$.
2.  **GS-IR Variant [S1]:**
    *   **Geometry/Appearance ($T, A$):** Position, scale, rotation, opacity, and SH coefficients for color.
    *   **Material/Lighting ($M, E$):** Albedo, roughness, and lighting parameters.
    *   **Normals ($n$):** Per-Gaussian normal vectors.

### Numbered Steps

#### Stage 1: Base Representation Learning
This stage stabilizes the core representation without the complexity of the secondary optimization target.

1.  **Initialize:** Load context views $C$. Extract multi-view features $F_{mv}$, encoder-side depth $D_{enc}$, and depth matching features $P_{depth}$ using a backbone network (e.g., MVEnc) [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Generate Features:** Compute dense Gaussian-generation features $F_g$ using a feature head [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ F_g = \text{FeatHead}(I_{1:N}, F_{mv}, P_{depth}) $$
3.  **Predict Primitives (Codec-Free):** Predict the Gaussian set $G_{cf}$ from $F_g$ and $D_{enc}$ *without* passing through the feature codec [S2, unofficial]. Alternatively, in GS-IR, initialize geometry $T$ and appearance $A$ [S1].
4.  **Render:** Render the target view $\hat{Y}_{tcf}$ using the predicted primitives:
    $$ \hat{Y}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(G_{cf}, K_t, T_t) \quad \text{[S2, unofficial]} $$
    Or render color/appearance for GS-IR [S1].
5.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the reconstruction loss.
    *   For CodecSplat: $L_{stage1} = D_{render}(\hat{Y}_{tcf}, Y_t)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   For GS-IR: $L_{init} = L_c + L_n$, where $L_c$ is color L1 loss and $L_n$ is normal loss [S1].
6.  **Optimize:** Update all backbone parameters (encoder, feature head, Gaussian prediction heads) using an optimizer (e.g., Adam/AdamW) for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 30,000 for GS-IR [S1]; unspecified for CodecSplat but implied until convergence) [S1][S2, unofficial].

#### Stage 2: Attribute-Specific Optimization
This stage introduces the secondary constraints (codec or material/lighting) while freezing the Stage 1 learned base.

1.  **Initialize:** Load the checkpoint from Stage 1. Enable the feature codec (CodecSplat) or prepare material/lighting parameters (GS-IR) [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Freeze Base Parameters:**
    *   In CodecSplat, keep the backbone modules (excluding codec-related parts) fixed [S2, unofficial].
    *   In GS-IR, fix geometry $T$ and appearance $A$ [S1].
3.  **Encode/Decode (CodecSplat only):** Pass features through the feature codec and post-codec refiner to obtain decoded features, then predict the decoded Gaussian set $\hat{G}$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Render:** Render the target view $\hat{Y}_t$ using $\hat{G}$ (CodecSplat) or render with material/lighting parameters (GS-IR) [S1][S2, unofficial].
5.  **Compute Loss:**
    *   **CodecSplat:**
        $$ L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ D_{render}(\hat{Y}_t, Y_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} \right] + \beta R $$
        Where $R$ is the estimated rate, $\gamma$ is the depth supervision weight, and $\beta$ is the rate penalty weight [S2, unofficial].
        The codec-side depth loss is:
        $$ D_{codec-depth} = \| S - S_{enc} \|_1 $$
        Where $S_{enc}$ is the normalized inverse-depth of the encoder:
        $$ S_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{D_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        And $S$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth [S2, unofficial].
    *   **GS-IR:**
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
        Where $L_{shade}$ is the shading loss, $TV_{mat}$ is total variation on material, and $TV_{light}$ is total variation on lighting [S1].
6.  **Optimize:** Update only the specific parameters (codec modules for CodecSplat; material $M$ and lighting $E$ for GS-IR) for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 10,000 for GS-IR [S1]).

### Formulas and Symbols
*   $D_{render}$: Rendering reconstruction loss (typically L1 or LPIPS) [S2, unofficial].
*   $Y_t$: Target-view ground truth image [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Rate parameter, sampled from $[16, 1024]$ during training, fixed at test time [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{max}$: Maximum rate parameter value (1024) [S2, unofficial].
*   $\gamma$: Weight for codec-side depth supervision (0.1) [S2, unofficial].
*   $\beta$: Rate penalty weight ($10^{-4}$ for DL3DV, $4 \times 10^{-4}$ for RE10K) [S2, unofficial].
*   $S_{enc}$: Encoder-side normalized inverse-depth map [S2, unofficial].
*   $f_i, n_i$: Near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{n-TV}$: Smoothing weight for normal loss (5.0) [S1].
*   $\lambda_M$: Smoothing weight for material loss (1.0) [S1].
*   $\lambda_E$: Smoothing weight for light loss (0.01) [S1].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1] or AdamW [S2, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ with weight decay 0.01 (AdamW) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** Stage 1: 30,000 (GS-IR) [S1]; Stage 2: 10,000 (GS-IR) [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA V100 (GS-IR) [S1]; Four NVIDIA RTX 5880 (CodecSplat) [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck in Stage 1 causes instability due to the need to adapt Gaussian generation and latent representation simultaneously [S2, unofficial].
*   **Rate Selection:** The rate parameter $\lambda$ must be sampled during training to ensure robustness across different bitrates, but fixed at test time for consistent performance [S2, unofficial].
*   **Depth Normalization:** The normalization of inverse-depth for $S_{enc}$ requires accurate near/far plane bounds ($n_i, f_i$); incorrect bounds lead to misaligned depth supervision [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Stage 1: Codec-Free / Geometry-Appearance Training
def stage_1_training(context_views, target_views, iterations=30000):
    # Initialize backbone and Gaussian prediction heads
    model = initialize_backbone()
    optimizer = AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=1e-4, weight_decay=0.01)
    
    for step in range(iterations):
        # 1. Extract features and encoder-side depth
        F_mv, D_enc, P_depth = model.MVEnc(context_views)
        
        # 2. Generate Gaussian features
        F_g = model.FeatHead(context_views.images, F_mv, P_depth)
        
        # 3. Predict Gaussian set (Codec-Free)
        G_cf = model.predict_gaussians(F_g, D_enc)
        
        # 4. Render target view
        Y_hat = render(G_cf, target_views.cameras)
        
        # 5. Compute Loss (Reconstruction)
        loss = D_render(Y_hat, target_views.images)
        
        # 6. Backpropagate
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
    return model.state_dict()

# Stage 2: Codec-Aware / Material-Lighting Optimization
def stage_2_training(stage_1_checkpoint, context_views, target_views, iterations=10000):
    # Load Stage 1 weights
    model = initialize_backbone()
    model.load_state_dict(stage_1_checkpoint)
    
    # Enable codec modules
    model.enable_codec()
    
    # Freeze backbone modules (except codec parts)
    freeze_backbone(model)
    
    optimizer = AdamW(filter(lambda p: p.requires_grad, model.parameters()), lr=1e-4)
    
    lambda_max = 1024
    gamma = 0.1
    beta = 1e-4 # Example value
    
    for step in range(iterations):
        # Sample lambda for rate-distortion trade-off
        lambda_val = random.randint(16, lambda_max)
        
        # 1. Encode/Decode Features
        F_g = model.get_features(context_views) # From backbone
        F_latent = model.encode(F_g)
        F_decoded = model.decode_and_refine(F_latent)
        
        # 2. Predict Decoded Gaussian Set
        G_hat = model.predict_gaussians(F_decoded, D_enc)
        
        # 3. Render
        Y_hat = render(G_hat, target_views.cameras)
        
        # 4. Compute Depth Loss (CodecSplat specific)
        S_enc = normalize_inverse_depth(D_enc, near_bounds, far_bounds)
        S_dec = model.get_decoder_depth() # From decoded features
        D_codec_depth = L1_loss(S_dec, S_enc)
        
        # 5. Compute Total Loss
        D_render = D_render(Y_hat, target_views.images)
        R = model.estimate_rate(F_latent)
        loss = (lambda_val / lambda_max) * (D_render + gamma * D_codec_depth) + beta * R
        
        # 6. Backpropagate
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
    return model.state_dict()
```

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage training procedure for Relightable 3D Gaussians](15262-two-stage-training-procedure-for-relightable-3d-gaussians.md)
This method defines the optimization protocol for Relightable 3D Gaussians (R3DG), a technique that reconstructs relightable scenes from multi-view images by combining standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering with physically based rendering (PBR) and BRDF decomposition. It solves the problem of recovering geometry, material properties (albedo, roughness), and lighting from static multi-view inputs, enabling novel view synthesis and relighting. It sets itself apart from vanilla 3DGS by introducing normal gradient-based densification in the first stage and PBR-specific loss terms in the second stage, while differing from neural relightable methods by maintaining a discrete point-based representation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two-stage optimization doubles the training time compared to a single-stage approach, as two distinct optimization phases are executed sequentially [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing intermediate features and potentially latent codes. CodecSplat uses four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB GPUs, indicating significant memory requirements for the feature codec and backbone [S2, unofficial]. GS-IR runs on a single V100, suggesting lower memory overhead for the material/lighting variant [S1].
*   **Performance:** CodecSplat achieves KiB-level scene bitstreams while preserving dominant scene structure, outperforming MiB-level compressed baselines [S2, unofficial]. GS-IR enables accurate inverse rendering with stable convergence [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents training instability caused by coupled optimization objectives; allows independent tuning of rate-distortion (CodecSplat) or material fidelity (GS-IR) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Increased training time; requires careful tuning of hyperparameters for the second stage (e.g., $\lambda, \gamma, \beta$) [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Two-stage optimization was introduced in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting to address specific challenges in compression and inverse rendering. The CodecSplat variant was proposed to enable ultra-compact latent coding for feed-forward 3DGS, separating the stable generation of Gaussians from the learning of the quantization bottleneck [S2, S2, unofficial]. The GS-IR variant was introduced to facilitate inverse rendering by decoupling geometry/appearance learning from material/lighting decomposition, allowing for stable recovery of PBR parameters [S1].
