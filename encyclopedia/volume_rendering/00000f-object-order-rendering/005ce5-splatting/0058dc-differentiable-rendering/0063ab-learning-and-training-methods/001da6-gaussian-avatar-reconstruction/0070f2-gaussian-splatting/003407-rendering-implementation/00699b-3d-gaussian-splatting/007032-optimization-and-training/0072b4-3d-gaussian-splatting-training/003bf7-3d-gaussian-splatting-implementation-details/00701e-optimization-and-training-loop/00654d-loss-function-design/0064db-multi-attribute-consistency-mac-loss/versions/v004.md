# Multi-Attribute Consistency (MAC) Loss

Also called: MAC loss, MAC-Splat loss

## Overview
The Multi-Attribute Consistency (MAC) loss is a regularization objective for 3D Gaussian Splatting that enforces geometric and photometric agreement between matched Gaussians across different views [S1]. It solves the ambiguity problem in sparse-view reconstruction by using high-confidence 2D pixel correspondences as anchors to jointly constrain position, shape, and appearance in a common world coordinate frame [S1]. Unlike standard photometric losses that only compare rendered pixels, MAC loss directly supervises the underlying Gaussian parameters, providing robust stability against outliers and preventing geometric degeneracy [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of matched pixel pairs $M = \{p_1^k \leftrightarrow p_2^k\}_{k=1}^K$ derived from a geometric backbone (e.g., MASt3R) [S1]. For each view $v \in \{1, 2\}$, a dense map of predicted Gaussian parameters aligned with the descriptor grid: centroid $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{cam}}^{v}(u,v) \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{cam}}^{v}(u,v) \in \text{Sym}^+(3)$, opacity $\alpha^v(u,v) \in \mathbb{R}$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}^v(u,v)$ [S1]. Camera-to-world transformation matrices $\mathbf{T}_{wc}^v = [\mathbf{R}^v | \mathbf{t}^v]$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAC}}$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Parameter Maps:** Dense grids where each cell $(u,v)$ stores the tuple $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{cam}}, \mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{cam}}, \alpha, \mathbf{c})$ for the Gaussian associated with that pixel [S1].
*   **Match Set $M$:** A list of $K$ tuples, each containing pixel coordinates $(p_1^k, p_2^k)$ and a joint confidence score $w_k$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Sample Gaussian Pairs:** For each matched pixel pair $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, bilinearly sample the parameter maps at coordinates $p_1^k$ and $p_2^k$ to obtain two Gaussians $G_1^k$ and $G_2^k$ [S1].
    $$ G_v^k = \left( \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{cam}}^{v,k}, \mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{cam}}^{v,k}, \alpha^{v,k}, \mathbf{c}^{v,k} \right), \quad v \in \{1, 2\} $$
2.  **Transform to World Space:** Convert camera-space centroids and covariances to the world coordinate system using the camera-to-world rotation $\mathbf{R}^v$ and translation $\mathbf{t}^v$ [S1]:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{world}}^{v,k} = \mathbf{R}^v \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{cam}}^{v,k} + \mathbf{t}^v $$
    $$ \mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{world}}^{v,k} = \mathbf{R}^v \mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{cam}}^{v,k} (\mathbf{R}^v)^\top $$
3.  **Compute Positional Consistency:** Calculate the $L_1$ distance between world-space centroids. Apply Huber loss $\mathcal{H}(\cdot)$ only if both centroids are in front of their respective cameras ($z_{\text{cam}} > 0$) [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}^{(k)} = \mathcal{H}\left( \| \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{world}}^{1,k} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{world}}^{2,k} \|_1 \right) $$
4.  **Compute Shape Consistency:** Extract eigenvalues $\lambda$ from the world-space covariance matrices. Compute the Huber loss of the difference of the log-eigenvalues [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}^{(k)} = \mathcal{H}\left( \| \log(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{1,k}) - \log(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{2,k}) \|_1 \right) $$
    *Note: Euclidean distance between covariance matrices is avoided as it does not respect the geometry of $\text{Sym}^+(3)$ or invariance to rotation/scaling [S1].*
5.  **Compute Appearance Consistency:** Calculate the $L_1$ distance between opacity and spherical harmonic coefficients [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{app}}^{(k)} = \| \alpha^{1,k} - \alpha^{2,k} \|_1 + \| \mathbf{c}^{1,k} - \mathbf{c}^{2,k} \|_1 $$
6.  **Aggregate MAC Loss:** Combine the terms weighted by the joint confidence $w_k$ of the match, normalized by the sum of weights plus a small constant $\epsilon$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{MAC}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \left( \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}^{(k)} + \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}^{(k)} + \lambda_a \mathcal{L}_{\text{app}}^{(k)} \right)}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k + \epsilon} $$

**Formulas and Definitions**
*   $\mathcal{H}(x)$: Huber loss function.
*   $\lambda_p, \lambda_s, \lambda_a$: Scalar weights for position, shape, and appearance terms [S1].
*   $w_k$: Joint confidence score for the $k$-th match [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Small constant to prevent division by zero [S1].
*   $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$: Vector of eigenvalues of the covariance matrix [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $\lambda_p, \lambda_s, \lambda_a$: Not given in the sources.
*   $\epsilon$: Not given in the sources.
*   $w_k$: Derived from the geometric backbone's confidence estimation [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Geometric Degeneracy:** As a Gaussian's smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ approaches zero, the gradient of the shape loss scales inversely with $\lambda$ ($|\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}} / \partial \lambda| \propto 1/\lambda$) [S1]. This creates a strong repulsive effect that pushes $\lambda_{\min}$ away from zero, preventing collapsed artifacts [S1].
*   **Ambiguous Regions:** If no match survives the confidence gating ($w_k > \tau_{\text{conf}}$), the MAC loss for that region falls back to zero, allowing the network to rely solely on photometric supervision [S1].
*   **Coordinate Frame:** Covariance matrices must be transformed to the world frame before eigenvalue extraction to ensure consistency, as they are predicted in the camera frame [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def compute_mac_loss(matched_pairs, param_maps, camera_transforms):
    """
    matched_pairs: List of tuples (p1, p2, w_k)
    param_maps: Dict mapping view_id -> (mu_cam, Sigma_cam, alpha, c)
    camera_transforms: Dict mapping view_id -> (R, t)
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    total_weight = 0.0
    epsilon = 1e-8
    
    for p1, p2, w_k in matched_pairs:
        # 1. Sample parameters
        mu_cam_1, Sigma_cam_1, alpha_1, c_1 = sample_param_map(param_maps[1], p1)
        mu_cam_2, Sigma_cam_2, alpha_2, c_2 = sample_param_map(param_maps[2], p2)
        
        # 2. Transform to world space
        R1, t1 = camera_transforms[1]
        R2, t2 = camera_transforms[2]
        
        mu_world_1 = R1 @ mu_cam_1 + t1
        mu_world_2 = R2 @ mu_cam_2 + t2
        
        Sigma_world_1 = R1 @ Sigma_cam_1 @ R1.T
        Sigma_world_2 = R2 @ Sigma_cam_2 @ R2.T
        
        # Check z_cam > 0 for validity (using camera space z)
        # Note: The source specifies z_cam > 0 check, implying we check the 
        # original camera-space centroid's z-component before or after transform.
        # Assuming mu_cam is in camera frame, we check mu_cam[2] > 0.
        if mu_cam_1[2] > 0 and mu_cam_2[2] > 0:
            # 3. Positional Consistency
            pos_diff = np.linalg.norm(mu_world_1 - mu_world_2, ord=1)
            l_pos = huber_loss(pos_diff)
        else:
            l_pos = 0.0
            
        # 4. Shape Consistency
        eig_1 = np.linalg.eigvalsh(Sigma_world_1)
        eig_2 = np.linalg.eigvalsh(Sigma_world_2)
        # Log-eigenvalues
        log_eig_1 = np.log(eig_1)
        log_eig_2 = np.log(eig_2)
        shape_diff = np.linalg.norm(log_eig_1 - log_eig_2, ord=1)
        l_shape = huber_loss(shape_diff)
        
        # 5. Appearance Consistency
        l_app = np.linalg.norm(alpha_1 - alpha_2, ord=1) + np.linalg.norm(c_1 - c_2, ord=1)
        
        # 6. Aggregate
        # Weights lambda_p, lambda_s, lambda_a are assumed to be global constants
        # defined outside this function or passed as args. 
        # Here we assume they are 1.0 for the sum, but in practice they are scalars.
        # The formula divides by sum(w_k).
        
        term = w_k * (lambda_p * l_pos + lambda_s * l_shape + lambda_a * l_app)
        total_loss += term
        total_weight += w_k
        
    return total_loss / (total_weight + epsilon)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of matched pairs $K$ and the cost of bilinear sampling and eigenvalue decomposition. Eigenvalue decomposition of $3 \times 3$ matrices is constant time.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing dense parameter maps for all views and the set of matched pairs.
*   **Strengths:** Robust to outliers due to Huber loss and confidence weighting. Prevents geometric degeneracy (collapsed Gaussians) via the log-eigenvalue gradient property. Improves PSNR and LPIPS in sparse-view settings [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of 2D correspondences from the geometric backbone. If correspondences are poor, the loss may not provide useful gradients.
*   **When to use:** Sparse-view reconstruction where photometric loss alone is ambiguous.
*   **When not to use:** Dense-view settings where photometric loss is sufficient, or when computational resources are extremely limited.

## Background
The MAC loss was introduced in "MAC-Splat: Multi-Attribute Consistency for High-Fidelity Sparse-View Reconstruction" [S1]. It builds on the MASt3R geometric backbone and a frozen DINOv3 encoder to obtain semantically informed 2D correspondences [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of conventional losses in sparse-view reconstruction by enforcing direct consistency on 3D Gaussian attributes [S1].
