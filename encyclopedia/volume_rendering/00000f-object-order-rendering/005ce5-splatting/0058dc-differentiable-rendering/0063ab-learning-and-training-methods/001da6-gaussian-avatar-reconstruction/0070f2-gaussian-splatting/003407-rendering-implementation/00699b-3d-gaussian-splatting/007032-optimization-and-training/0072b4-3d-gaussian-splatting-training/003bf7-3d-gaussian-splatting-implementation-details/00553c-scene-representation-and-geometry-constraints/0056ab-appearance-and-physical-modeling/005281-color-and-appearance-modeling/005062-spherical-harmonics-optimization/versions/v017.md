# Spherical Harmonics Optimization

Also called: SH Optimization, View-dependent Color Optimization

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Optimization refers to the family of techniques used to estimate, compress, regularize, or replace the SH coefficients that define the view-dependent color of 3D Gaussian primitives in Gaussian Splatting. It solves the problem of accurately modeling complex lighting and specular reflections from limited multi-view observations while managing the high memory and computational costs associated with high-degree SH representations [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings by focusing specifically on the *optimization* and *representation* of the SH coefficients themselves, rather than the camera tracking [10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md] or the multi-view training loop structure [13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves representing the directional radiance $L(\mathbf{d})$ of a Gaussian primitive as a linear combination of spherical harmonic basis functions $Y_l^m(\mathbf{d})$:
$$ L(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} c_l^m Y_l^m(\mathbf{d}) $$
where $L$ is the maximum degree, $c_l^m$ are the coefficients, and $\mathbf{d}$ is the viewing direction. The optimization process typically minimizes a photometric loss $\mathcal{L}$ between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images by updating these coefficients. Key shared constraints include:
1.  **Directional Support:** Coefficients for higher degrees ($l > 0$) require sufficient angular coverage from training views to be well-conditioned; otherwise, they overfit [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md].
2.  **DC Preservation:** The zeroth-order coefficient ($l=0$) represents the average color and is generally preserved or regularized less aggressively than higher-order terms [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md].
3.  **Basis Evaluation:** The SH basis functions are evaluated at the viewing direction vector transformed into the Gaussian's local coordinate frame [16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md].

## Subtopics

### [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md)
First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation reduces the SH degree from third-order to first-order to decrease parameter count and computation cost [9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md]. It differs from standard truncation by using a distillation strategy where a student model approximates the teacher's radiance, minimizing quality loss [9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md]. Choose this when memory/compute is constrained but view-dependent effects must be preserved better than simple color quantization [9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md]. See [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md).

### [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md)
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking [10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md]. It avoids automatic differentiation overhead and ensures minimal parameterization using Lie algebra [10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md]. Choose this for accurate camera pose optimization in SLAM or tracking applications [10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md]. See [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md).

### [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
This method enables stable optimization by training on multiple images per iteration, addressing the high variance of gradients in single-view approaches [13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md]. It uses thread-efficient partial rendering to gather pixels from multiple views without idle thread waste [13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md]. Choose this when convergence stability is critical and hardware allows parallel rasterization [13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md]. See [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md).

### [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md)
Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization (SH VQ) replaces per-splat SH coefficients with indices pointing to a shared palette of distinct SH vectors, reducing memory footprint [14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md]. It differs from distillation by retaining full SH basis functions and from Jacobian derivation by focusing on storage compression [14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md]. Choose this when memory bandwidth or storage size is the primary bottleneck [14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md]. See [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md).

### [Closed-Form Scene Appearance Estimation](16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md)
This method estimates SH coefficients for mesh vertices by solving a global weighted least-squares problem that jointly fits RGB observations from all calibrated views [16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md]. It provides an analytical closed-form solution using adaptive regularization, distinguishing itself from iterative optimization [16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md]. Choose this for mesh-based appearance modeling where a global solution is preferred [16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md]. See [Closed-Form Scene Appearance Estimation](16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md).

### [Adaptive SH Regularization](16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md)
Adaptive SH Regularization stabilizes SH coefficient estimation in sparse scenes by suppressing higher-order components that lack sufficient directional support [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md]. It prevents overfitting to input views and improves generalization, distinguishing itself from methods relying on fixed SH complexity [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md]. Choose this when training data is sparse or unevenly distributed [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md]. See [Adaptive SH Regularization](16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md).

### [Geometry-Guided Appearance Modeling](20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md)
Geometry-Guided Appearance Modeling (GAM) predicts view-dependent colors by conditioning appearance on explicit geometric cues like surface normals and SDF features [20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md]. It solves the limitation of standard SH in capturing fine geometric details, replacing or augmenting SH with a geometry-conditioned MLP [20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md]. Choose this when high-frequency geometric reflections are critical and SH fails to capture them [20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md]. See [Geometry-Guided Appearance Modeling](20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md).

### SH Degree Zero Optimization
#### definition
Setting the maximum degree of Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to 0 to remove superfluous operations and improve training speed, as higher-degree SH coefficients for color remain unchanged during training [S1, unofficial].
#### step
Modify the model code to delete operations relative to the max degree in the model and set the max degree parameter to 0 [S1, unofficial].
#### pitfall
In the original 3D Gaussian Splatting model, features represented by SH coefficients with degree > 0 are unchanged during all training, making them useless [S1, unofficial].
#### number
Directly changing the max degree to 0 yields a 16.25% improvement compared to the default. With specific changing code, it yields a 16.57% improvement but increases CPU-GPU process time [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The use of Spherical Harmonics for view-dependent rendering in 3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the original 3DGS paper [12] (cited in [S1, unofficial]). Subsequent work has focused on optimizing the efficiency and robustness of this representation. I3DGS [S1, unofficial] identified that higher-degree SH coefficients often remain unchanged during training, leading to optimization strategies that reduce the SH degree or remove redundant operations. Other approaches have explored distillation [9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md], quantization [14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md], and regularization [16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md] to handle sparse data and memory constraints.
