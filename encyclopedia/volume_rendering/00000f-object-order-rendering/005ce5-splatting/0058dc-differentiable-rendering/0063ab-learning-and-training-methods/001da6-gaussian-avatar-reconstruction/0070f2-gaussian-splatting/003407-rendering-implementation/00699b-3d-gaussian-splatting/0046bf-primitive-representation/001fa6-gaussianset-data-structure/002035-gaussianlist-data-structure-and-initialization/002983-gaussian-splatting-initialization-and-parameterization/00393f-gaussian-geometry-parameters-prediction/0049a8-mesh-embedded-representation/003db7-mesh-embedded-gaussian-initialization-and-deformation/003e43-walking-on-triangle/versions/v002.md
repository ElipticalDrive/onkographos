# Walking on Triangle

## Overview
Walking on Triangle is a constraint mechanism that binds 3D Gaussians to the surface of a triangle mesh, ensuring they remain within the boundaries of their corresponding triangles during optimization and deformation [S1, unofficial][S2]. It solves the problem of Gaussians drifting off-mesh or piling up at triangle edges when the underlying mesh is manipulated or animated [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that rely heavily on precise mesh accuracy, it defines a local triangle space to ensure robust results even with inaccurate meshes [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A triangle mesh, 3D Gaussian attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color), and barycentric coordinates $(u, v)$ for each Gaussian relative to its host triangle [S1, unofficial][G].
*   **Output:** Updated Gaussian positions and attributes that respect the mesh topology and local triangle constraints [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian State:** Each Gaussian $i$ is associated with a specific triangle $T_k$ of the mesh [G].
*   **Barycentric Coordinates:** For each Gaussian, two scalar values $u_i, v_i$ representing its position within triangle $T_k$ [G]. The third barycentric coordinate is $w_i = 1 - u_i - v_i$ [G].
*   **Local Triangle Space:** Defined by the three vertices of triangle $T_k$: $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3$ [G].

**Steps:**
1.  **Bind Gaussians to Mesh:** Assign each Gaussian to a specific triangle on the mesh during initialization [S2].
2.  **Define Local Triangle Space:** For each triangle, establish a local coordinate space for optimization [S2].
3.  **Optimize Attributes in Local Space:** Optimize Gaussian attributes within the local triangle space during training, ensuring deformations applied to the mesh are directly transferred to the Gaussians [S2].
4.  **Clip UV Values:** To prevent Gaussians from moving beyond the boundaries of their assigned triangle, clip the barycentric coordinates $u_i$ and $v_i$ [S1, unofficial]. The constraints are:
    $$ u_i \ge 0, \quad v_i \ge 0, \quad u_i + v_i \le 1 $$
    If a Gaussian's coordinates violate these bounds, they are clipped to the nearest valid point within the triangle [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Transfer Manipulation:** When the mesh is deformed or manipulated, the new positions of the triangle vertices are used to recompute the 3D position of each Gaussian based on its fixed barycentric coordinates [S2]. This allows for large deformations, soft body simulation, and local manipulation [S2].

**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge cases and pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Artifacts:** Disabling the walking-on-triangle mechanism causes Gaussians to stick and pile up on the boundaries of mesh triangles, leading to visible artifacts when the avatar is animated by novel poses [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Triangles:** Zero-area or highly skewed triangles may cause numerical instability during barycentric interpolation; standard epsilon guards are required [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def clip_barycentric(u, v):
    """Clips barycentric coordinates to the valid triangle region."""
    if u < 0.0:
        u = 0.0
    if v < 0.0:
        v = 0.0
    if u + v > 1.0:
        s = u + v
        u = u / s
        v = v / s
    return u, v

def update_gaussian_position(u, v, v1, v2, v3):
    """Computes 3D position from barycentric coordinates and triangle vertices."""
    u, v = clip_barycentric(u, v)
    w = 1.0 - u - v
    return w * v1 + u * v2 + v * v3
```

## Subtopics

### Mani-GS
A method that defines a local triangle space for each triangle to ensure robust results even with inaccurate meshes, binding Gaussians to the mesh and optimizing attributes within this space [S2]. It transfers mesh manipulation directly to 3DGS, enabling large deformation, soft body simulation, and local manipulation [S2]. Compared with SuGaR, Mani-GS produces fewer artifacts and reduces the blurring effect [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The clipping and position update operations are $O(1)$ per Gaussian per frame [G]. The overall complexity is dominated by the rendering and optimization steps of the 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Adds negligible memory overhead, storing only the barycentric coordinates $(u, v)$ for each Gaussian [G].
*   **Performance:** For the 'bala' head avatar, enabling Walking on Triangle increases PSNR from 29.91 to 30.04, SSIM from 0.933 to 0.938, and decreases LPIPS from 0.070 to 0.062 [S1, unofficial]. For the 'male-3-casual' full-body avatar, it increases PSNR from 32.48 to 33.01, SSIM from 0.979 to 0.982, and decreases LPIPS from 0.024 to 0.020 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents artifacts during animation, robust to mesh inaccuracies, enables direct mesh manipulation [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a valid triangle mesh and initial binding of Gaussians to triangles [G].

## Background
Walking on Triangle was introduced in SplattingAvatar, a method for relightable modeling with explicit shape representations [S1, unofficial]. It was designed to address performance compromises in previous explicit shape methods, particularly in maintaining pixel-wise metrics during animation [S1, unofficial]. The method is also highlighted in surveys of 3D editing based on NeRF and 3DGS as a key technique for mesh-based deformation [S2].
