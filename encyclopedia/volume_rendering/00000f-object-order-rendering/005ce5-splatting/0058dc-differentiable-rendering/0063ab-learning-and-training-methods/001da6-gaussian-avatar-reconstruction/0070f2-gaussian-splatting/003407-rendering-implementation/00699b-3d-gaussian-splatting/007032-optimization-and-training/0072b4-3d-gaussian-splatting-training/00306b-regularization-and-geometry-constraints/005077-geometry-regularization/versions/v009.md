# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian splats by constraining their shape and orientation, distinct from depth supervision which primarily constrains spatial location [S1, S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by focusing on structural alignment (normals and covariance anisotropy) rather than spectral content, and from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by enforcing surface-aligned orientations rather than just depth value consistency [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian parameters (position $\mathbf{p}$, rotation $\mathbf{q}$, scaling $\mathbf{s}$, opacity $\alpha$), rendered depth map $D$, and optionally rendered normal map $N$.
*   **Outputs:** Gradient updates to Gaussian parameters that minimize geometric inconsistency.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian State:** Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, quaternion rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, scaling vector $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and opacity $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$.
*   **Rendered Buffers:** Depth buffer $D(u,v)$ and Normal buffer $N(u,v)$ for each pixel $(u,v)$.

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean flag to enable geometry regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{flat}$: Weight for the flattening loss (Not given in the sources).
*   $\lambda_{norm}$: Weight for the normal consistency loss (Not given in the sources).

**Steps:**
1.  **Depth Refitting:** If using depth supervision, refit all depth maps against a prior point cloud to ensure scale consistency. Discard depth maps that fail refitting [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Normal Estimation:** Compute per-Gaussian normals from their covariance matrices (derived from $\mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{s}$). The normal corresponds to the direction of the smallest axis of the Gaussian ellipsoid [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Flattening Loss:** Minimize the smallest component of the scaling vector $\mathbf{s}$ towards zero, encouraging Gaussians to flatten into surfels aligned with the surface [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Normal Consistency Loss:** Align the rendered normal map $N$ with the per-Gaussian normals projected to the image plane [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
Let $\mathbf{s}_i = [s_{i,x}, s_{i,y}, s_{i,z}]^T$ be the scaling factors. The flattening loss for Gaussian $i$ is:
$$ L_{flat} = \min(s_{i,x}, s_{i,y}, s_{i,z}) $$
The goal is to minimize this value towards zero [S2, unofficial].

For normal consistency, let $\mathbf{n}_i$ be the normal of Gaussian $i$ in world space, and $\mathbf{n}_{rendered}(u,v)$ be the normal from the rendered depth map at pixel $(u,v)$ corresponding to the projection of Gaussian $i$. The loss is typically the dot product or angle difference:
$$ L_{norm} = 1 - \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}_{rendered}(u,v) $$
(Exact formulation for $L_{norm}$ is not specified beyond "align" in sources [S1, S2, unofficial].)

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Refitting Failure:** Depth maps that cannot be refitted to the prior point cloud scale must be discarded to prevent incorrect supervision [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sky/Distant Background:** Geometry regularization may pull geometry onto itself in open scenes. Using a "Sky Dome" (frozen shell of Gaussians) can help disentangle sky from foreground [S1, unofficial].
*   **Floaters:** While geometry regularization helps, floaters may still persist. Combining with entropy loss on opacity and pruning mechanisms is recommended [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A specific implementation of geometry regularization that flattens Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels by minimizing the smallest component of the scaling factor $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ towards zero [S2, unofficial]. This aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when explicit surface alignment is required to reduce anisotropic artifacts.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead for normal estimation and depth/refitting checks. Normal consistency requires additional differentiable projection or rasterization passes [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing rendered depth and normal buffers [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Improves geometric smoothness, reduces floaters, and enforces surface-aligned orientations [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May require careful tuning of loss weights. Depth refitting can fail for dynamic or uncalibrated scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity geometry is required, especially for scenes with planar structures or when floaters are prevalent.
*   **When not to use:** For scenes with significant motion where depth refitting is unstable, or when computational resources are extremely limited.

## Background
Originating from improvements to 3D Gaussian Splatting training, geometry regularization was introduced to address the arbitrary spatial distribution and anisotropic kernels of tiny 3D Gaussians [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of representing surfaces with flattened Gaussians (surfels) [S2, unofficial]. The technique is implemented in frameworks like GaussianSplatting.jl, where it can be enabled via `OptimizationParams` [S1, unofficial].
