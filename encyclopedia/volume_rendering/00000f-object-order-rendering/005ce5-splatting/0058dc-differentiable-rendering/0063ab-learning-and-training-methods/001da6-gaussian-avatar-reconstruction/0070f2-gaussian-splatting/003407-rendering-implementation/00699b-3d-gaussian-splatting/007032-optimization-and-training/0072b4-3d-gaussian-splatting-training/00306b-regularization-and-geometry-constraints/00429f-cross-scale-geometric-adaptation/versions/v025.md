# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth or complex scheduling [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by dynamically selecting pseudo-labels from the scale with the minimal reprojection error, rather than relying on a single fixed depth prior or external depth estimators [S1]. This approach significantly reduces "floaters" (Gaussians in empty space) and improves convergence quality across all scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1].

## How it works
The method operates within a multi-scale voxel framework where low-resolution voxels capture coarse geometry and high-resolution voxels capture fine details [S1]. It computes a geometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ by warping pixels between training views and selecting the depth estimate that yields the most consistent color reprojection [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views $i, j$ with camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$ and extrinsics $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$; rendered RGB images $C_i, C_j$; rendered depth maps $D_{il}$ at multiple scales $l \in \{0, \dots, L\}$; training rays $\mathcal{R}_{train}$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ added to the total loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxel Grid:** Stores depth values $D_{il}(p)$ for each pixel $p$ and scale $l$. The sources do not specify the exact bit-packing or memory layout of the voxel grid, only that it supports low, mid, and high resolutions [S1].
*   **Camera Parameters:** Rotation matrices $R \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ and translation vectors $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ for each frame.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Iterate over training rays:** For each ray $r_{train} \in \mathcal{R}_{train}$ originating from a pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$:
2.  **Compute 3D points at all scales:** For each scale $l$:
    *   Retrieve the rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ from the volume rendering at scale $l$ [S1].
    *   Compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$:
        $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
        where $\tilde{p}_i$ is the homogeneous coordinate of $p_i$ [S1].
    *   Transform to world coordinates:
        $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
        [S1]
3.  **Reproject to second view:**
    *   Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinates of frame $j$:
        $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
        [S1]
    *   Project back to 2D pixel coordinates in frame $j$:
        $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
        where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Reprojection Error:**
    *   Calculate the color difference between the original pixel and the warped pixel:
        $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
        [S1]
5.  **Select Pseudo-Depth:**
    *   Identify the scale $l'$ that minimizes the reprojection error for this ray:
        $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
        [S1]
6.  **Compute Loss:**
    *   Calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the selected pseudo-depth and the rendered depth at the current scale $l$ for all training rays:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(\mathcal{R}_{train}) = \sum_{r_{train} \in \mathcal{R}_{train}} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
        [S1]
    *   Note: The sources indicate $\hat{D}_l$ is the rendered depth at scale $l$ [S1]. The summation implies an aggregate loss over the training set.

**Parameters:**
*   **Scales ($L$):** The number of voxel resolutions (e.g., low, mid, high). Typical values are not explicitly given, but the method relies on at least two scales to compare [S1].
*   **Weight ($\lambda$):** The weight for $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ in the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{geo}$ [S1]. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Pose Accuracy:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may suffer [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays during regularization risks local minima and incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Scale Mismatch:** Using a single depth prior ($L_d$) can suffer from scale mismatches later in training, whereas this method adapts progressively [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_cross_scale_geometric_loss(
    rays: List[Ray],
    frames: List[Frame],
    voxel_grids: List[VoxelGrid],
    intrinsics: List[Matrix3x3],
    extrinsics: List[Tuple[Matrix3x3, Vector3]]
) -> float:
    """
    Computes the cross-scale geometric adaptation loss L_geo.
    
    Args:
        rays: List of training rays, each containing pixel coordinate p_i and frame index i.
        frames: List of frame data, including RGB image C and index.
        voxel_grids: List of voxel grids for each scale l, providing depth D_l(p).
        intrinsics: Camera intrinsic matrices K.
        extrinsics: List of (R, t) tuples for camera poses.
        
    Returns:
        The scalar loss value L_geo.
    """
    loss_sum = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        p_i = ray.pixel_coordinate
        i = ray.frame_index
        
        # Get camera parameters for frame i
        K_i = intrinsics[i]
        R_i, t_i = extrinsics[i]
        C_i = frames[i].rgb_image
        
        min_error = float('inf')
        best_depth = 0.0
        
        # Iterate over all scales to find the one with minimal reprojection error
        for l, voxel_grid in enumerate(voxel_grids):
            # 1. Get depth at scale l for pixel p_i
            D_il = voxel_grid.get_depth(p_i)
            
            # 2. Compute 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
            # Homogeneous coordinate: [p_i.x, p_i.y, 1.0]
            p_i_hom = Vector3(p_i.x, p_i.y, 1.0)
            # x_li = D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_hom
            x_li = D_il * (K_i.inverse() @ p_i_hom)
            
            # 3. Transform to world coordinates
            # x_l = R_i * x_li + t_i
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            # 4. Transform to camera coordinates of a reference frame j
            # Note: The source uses frame j for warping. In implementation, 
            # j is typically another training view. For a single loss term per ray,
            # we assume a specific j is chosen or averaged. 
            # Based on Eq 15-19, it computes error for a specific pair (i, j).
            # We assume 'j' is provided in the ray or we iterate pairs.
            # Here we assume ray contains target frame index j for warping.
            j = ray.target_frame_index 
            K_j = intrinsics[j]
            R_j, t_j = extrinsics[j]
            C_j = frames[j].rgb_image
            
            # x_li_to_j = R_j^T * (x_l - t_j)
            x_li_to_j = R_j.transpose() @ (x_l - t_j)
            
            # 5. Project to 2D
            # p_li_to_j = pi(K_j * x_li_to_j)
            proj = K_j @ x_li_to_j
            p_li_to_j = Vector2(proj.x / proj.z, proj.y / proj.z)
            
            # 6. Compute reprojection error
            # e_l = C_i(p_i) - C_j(p_li_to_j)
            # Assuming bilinear interpolation for C_j at non-integer coordinates
            color_i = C_i.sample(p_i)
            color_j = C_j.sample(p_li_to_j)
            error = (color_i - color_j).norm()
            
            if error < min_error:
                min_error = error
                best_depth = D_il
        
        # 7. Accumulate loss against the rendered depth at the CURRENT scale l_current
        # The source formula sums || D_hat_l - D' ||^2. 
        # D_hat_l is the depth rendered at the scale being optimized.
        # D' is the best_depth found above.
        # We need to know which scale 'l' corresponds to the ray's optimization target.
        # Assuming the ray is associated with a specific scale or we compute loss for all scales.
        # The source implies L_geo is computed for the training rays.
        # Let's assume we are computing the loss for the scale 'l' associated with this ray's current update step.
        # If the ray doesn't specify a scale, we might compute it for the finest scale or all.
        # Based on "D_hat_l(r_train)", l is the scale of the Gaussian/voxel being updated.
        
        # For the purpose of this pseudocode, we assume the ray object has a 'current_scale' attribute
        # or we compute the loss for the scale that generated the ray's depth.
        # However, the source says "selecting render depths with minimal reprojection error... as pseudo labels".
        # And L_geo = sum || D_hat_l - D' ||^2.
        # This implies D_hat_l is the depth from the model at scale l, and D' is the pseudo-GT.
        
        # Let's assume we are computing the loss for the scale 'l' that produced the ray's depth D_il
        # But wait, D' is selected from ALL scales.
        # So for a ray, we find D' (best depth across all scales).
        # Then we compare D_hat_l (depth at scale l) to D'.
        # This creates a loss for scale l.
        
        # To be self-contained, we compute the loss for the scale 'l' that the ray is currently optimizing.
        # If not specified, we compute for the finest scale or iterate.
        # The source Eq 19 sums over r_train. It doesn't sum over l.
        # This implies for each ray, we pick ONE l to compute the loss against D'.
        # Or D_hat_l is the depth at the scale l where the ray was sampled.
        
        # Let's assume the ray was sampled from scale l_ray.
        l_ray = ray.current_scale # [G] Assumption: rays track their source scale
        D_hat_l = voxel_grids[l_ray].get_depth(p_i)
        
        diff = D_hat_l - best_depth
        loss_sum += diff * diff
        
    return loss_sum
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method requires reprojection for every training ray across all voxel scales. If there are $N$ rays and $L$ scales, the complexity is $O(N \cdot L)$. This is higher than single-scale depth regularization but avoids the cost of external depth networks.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing rendered depth maps for all scales simultaneously during training. The voxel grid memory scales with the resolution of the finest scale.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. It achieves lower final depth error than using a depth prior ($L_d$) alone, as it avoids scale mismatches that occur later in training with fixed priors [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive; no complex scheduling required; robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warping methods like GeCoNeRF [S1]; works well with few-shot inputs [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate camera poses; may struggle with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views (e.g., 360° datasets) without novel-view regularization [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in **FrugalNeRF** [S1], a framework for few-shot novel view synthesis. It was introduced to accelerate and regularize training without relying on learned priors or external depth estimators. The method leverages weight-sharing voxels across scales to capture varying scene frequencies and uses reprojection errors to guide multi-scale geometry learning [S1]. It is particularly effective in extreme few-shot scenarios where traditional NeRF methods suffer from overfitting or geometric inconsistencies.
