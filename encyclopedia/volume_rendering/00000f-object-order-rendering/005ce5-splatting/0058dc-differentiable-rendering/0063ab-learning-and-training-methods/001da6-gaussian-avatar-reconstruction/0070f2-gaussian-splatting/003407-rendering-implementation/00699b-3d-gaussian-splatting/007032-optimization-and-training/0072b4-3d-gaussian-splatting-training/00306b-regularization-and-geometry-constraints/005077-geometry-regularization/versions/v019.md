# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the structural integrity of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions by constraining the shape and orientation of the Gaussian primitives, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains their spatial location [S1, S2, unofficial]. It addresses the issue of "floaters" (Gaussians in empty space) and blurred surfaces by enforcing that Gaussians align with the underlying surface geometry [S1, S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating directly on 3D geometric priors (normals and covariance scales) rather than 2D image frequency domains.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian parameters (position $\mathbf{p}$, rotation $\mathbf{q}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, opacity $\alpha$), camera parameters, and optionally rendered depth maps and normals.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar regularization loss value $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ added to the total training loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Properties:** Each Gaussian $i$ has a scale vector $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a rotation quaternion $\mathbf{q}_i$. The covariance matrix is $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T$, where $R_i$ is the rotation matrix derived from $\mathbf{q}_i$ and $S_i = \text{diag}(\mathbf{s}_i)$.
*   **Rendered Auxiliary Data:** Depth map $D$ and normal map $N$ rendered from the current Gaussian scene.

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean flag to enable geometry regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{geo}$: Weighting factor for the geometry loss (part of $\lambda_{reg}$ in the parent's shared principles) [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Depth Normal Consistency:**
    *   Render the depth map $D$ and compute surface normals $N_{depth}$ from $D$ using finite differences or analytical derivatives of the projection [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the per-Gaussian normal $N_{gauss}$ from the Gaussian's orientation. The normal is typically the direction of the smallest axis of the covariance ellipsoid, or derived from the rotation matrix [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute the alignment loss between $N_{depth}$ and $N_{gauss}$ at corresponding pixel locations.
2.  **Flattening Surface (Scaling Loss):**
    *   For each Gaussian, identify the smallest component of the scale vector $\mathbf{s}_i$. Let $s_{min} = \min(\mathbf{s}_i)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Minimize $s_{min}$ towards zero to flatten the Gaussian into a surfel aligned with the surface [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The total geometry regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ is a combination of normal consistency and flattening:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{geo} = \mathcal{L}_{normal} + \mathcal{L}_{flatten} $$

Where:
*   $\mathcal{L}_{normal}$ is the dot product loss between rendered depth normals and Gaussian normals:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{normal} = 1 - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (N_{depth, i} \cdot N_{gauss, i}) $$
    (Note: Exact formulation may vary, but the goal is alignment [S1, unofficial]).
*   $\mathcal{L}_{flatten}$ penalizes the smallest scale component:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{flatten} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} s_{min, i} $$
    or a squared variant [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Refitting:** Depth maps from different frames may vary in scale. They must be refitted against a prior point cloud to the same scale before use. Depth maps that fail refitting are discarded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Minimizing scale components to zero can lead to numerical instability if not clamped.
*   **Trade-offs:** Aggressive flattening may reduce the number of remaining Gaussians during pruning, potentially compromising detail [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A regularization technique to flatten Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels aligned with the surface by minimizing the smallest component of the scaling factor towards zero [S2, unofficial]. This aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when the primary goal is to enforce surface-like geometry and reduce volume-like artifacts. It is a core component of Geometry Regularization [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead proportional to the number of Gaussians for scale computation and normal alignment. Normal consistency requires rendering or computing normals, which is additional to standard color rendering.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing rendered depth and normal maps if not already available.
*   **Strengths:** Significantly reduces "floaters" and improves surface smoothness and geometric accuracy [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May reduce the number of Gaussians during pruning, potentially losing fine details if not balanced with photometric loss [S2, unofficial]. Requires accurate depth maps for normal consistency [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-quality geometry is required, and depth supervision is available or can be estimated.
*   **When not to use:** When depth information is unreliable or unavailable, and only color supervision is present.

## Background
Originated from the need to improve the geometric fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting, which initially suffered from floaters and poor surface reconstruction [S1, S2, unofficial]. The concept of flattening Gaussians into surfels was adopted from prior work [5] in the GauStudio framework [S2, unofficial]. Normal consistency was introduced to align Gaussian orientation with surface normals derived from depth [S1, unofficial]. It is used in modern Gaussian Splatting implementations to enhance reconstruction quality [S1, S2, unofficial].
