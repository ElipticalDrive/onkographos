# Pruning and Sparsification

Also called: Gaussian Pruning, Sparsification, Density Control

## Overview
Pruning and Sparsification methods reduce the number of 3D Gaussian primitives in a scene representation to lower memory consumption, accelerate rendering, and prevent overfitting to noise or redundant data [S1, 12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md, unofficial]. Unlike [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md), which dynamically balances addition and removal during training to maintain optimal density, pruning methods often operate as post-hoc compression or static simplification strategies applied after convergence or during specific phases to achieve a target model size [S1, 9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md, unofficial]. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [GroupFlow](6351-groupflow.md) by focusing on the removal of individual primitives based on significance or uncertainty rather than grouping or hierarchical restructuring [S1, 9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ inherited from [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md). The core mechanism involves evaluating a metric per Gaussian to determine if it contributes negligibly to the final rendered image or if its removal would not significantly degrade reconstruction quality. Common metrics include:
1.  **Opacity:** Low opacity $\alpha_k < \alpha_{\min}$ indicates a primitive contributes negligibly to the alpha-compositing process [S1, 12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md, unofficial].
2.  **Gradient Magnitude:** High accumulated positional gradients indicate a Gaussian is struggling to minimize loss, suggesting it may be redundant or poorly placed, though this is more commonly used for densification [Parent].
3.  **Significance/Weight:** The accumulated alpha blending coefficient $w_k$ across all training views, representing the Gaussian's actual contribution to the pixel colors [S2, 9676-significance-aware-pruning.md].
4.  **Uncertainty/Sensitivity:** A score derived from the Fisher Information matrix or Hessian of the reconstruction error, indicating how sensitive the output is to changes in the Gaussian's parameters [S1, 9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md, unofficial].

Pruning is typically executed either periodically during training (e.g., every 100–1000 iterations) or as a final post-processing step [S1, 9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md, unofficial]. When primitives are removed, buffer compaction or flag-based marking is required to maintain contiguous memory access for the rasterizer [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md)
A dynamic strategy that balances addition and removal during training based on gradient magnitude, scale, and opacity to prevent under- and over-population.

### [Pruning and Opacity Management](9314-pruning-and-opacity-management.md)
Controls population size and transparency via opacity decay and reset mechanisms, serving as the foundation for other pruning methods.

### [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md)
Prevents the removal of geometrically large or structurally important Gaussians with low opacity by evaluating their accumulated alpha blending significance.

### [Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md)
A post-hoc compression method using a Fisher Information matrix-based sensitivity score on spatial parameters to prune up to 90% of Gaussians while preserving fidelity.

### [TangoGS](10180-tangogs.md)
A density control strategy that automatically scales the Gaussian population based on capture size and reconstruction quality feedback without per-capture retuning.

### [Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning](12338-depth-based-gaussian-pruning.md)
Varies the pruning threshold based on the relative depth (z-axis) of Gaussians to preserve sparse points at the far plane in forward-facing scenes.

### [Adaptive Pruning](19972-adaptive-pruning.md)
A sparsification technique for triangle-based splatting that removes primitives based on volume rendering blending weight ($T \cdot o$) to improve generalization.

### GaussianSPA
- **Definition:** Proposes an optimizing-sparsifying loop that interleaves standard training with a sparsification operator [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** Removes redundant primitives while boosting the influence of the remaining collection [S1, unofficial].

### Hierarchy Compaction
- **Definition:** A process to sparsify the generated tree structure to avoid parent-child settings where the parent's size is only marginally larger than the children's, ensuring proper optimization [S2].
- **Step:** Mark all leaf nodes (output of 3DGS optimization) as relevant. Find the union of cuts in the tree over all training views for the minimum target granularity $\tau_{min} = 3$ pixels. Find the bottom-most nodes in this union cut. These are the highest-detail relevant nodes. Remove all nodes between them and already marked nodes. Raise the target granularity by 2x and repeat the process until $\tau_{max}$ (half the image's resolution) is reached [S2].
- **Formula:** If a node has $K$ children, the blended weight $\alpha'$ generalizes to: $\alpha' = \frac{1}{1 - (1 - \alpha_p) \frac{1}{K}}$ [S2].
- **Parameter:** Minimum target granularity $\tau_{min}$: Set to 3 pixels, corresponding to the minimum extent of a primitive 3DGS primitive due to low-pass filtering [S2].

### Simple/MRNF Densification Strategy
- **Step:** Execute strategy if `strategy_name` is 'simple' or 'mrnf', `step > 0`, and `step % 200 == 0`. Prune dead splats using the same logic as adaptive pruning: `alive = torch.sigmoid(splats['opacities'].data) > prune_threshold`. If target splat count `target > 0`, current count `cur_n < target`, and `step < iterations * 3 // 4`, densify. Calculate number of splats to add: `n_add = min(target - cur_n, cur_n)`. Compute selection probabilities based on scale magnitude: `scale_mag = torch.exp(splats['scales'].data).max(dim=-1).values` and `probs = scale_mag / scale_mag.sum()`. Select `n_add` indices using multinomial sampling: `idx_s = torch.multinomial(probs, n_add, replacement=True)`. For 'means', add noise scaled by half the max scale magnitude: `noise = torch.randn_like(new_data)`, normalized, then `new_data = new_data + noise * offset_scale[:, None] * 0.5`. For 'opacities', subtract 0.5 from the new splats. For 'scales', subtract 0.3 from the new splats. Concatenate new data to existing splats and duplicate normals. If `target > 0`, `cur_n > target`, and `step >= iterations * 3 // 4`, trim splats. Remove splats with lowest opacity: `_, remove_idx = torch.topk(opa, n_remove, largest=False)` [S3, unofficial].

### Triangle Pruning
- **Step:** Evaluate pixel coverage for each triangle across the training views. Remove triangles with low pixel coverage. Repeat pruning until the desired primitive budget (target face count) is met [S4, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Pruning frequency: Performed every 100 iterations [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of pruning and sparsification in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a solution to the high memory and computational costs associated with the large number of primitives required for high-fidelity reconstruction. Early implementations relied on simple opacity thresholds to remove "dead" Gaussians [S1, 12354-pruning-and-sparsification.md, unofficial]. Subsequent research introduced more sophisticated metrics, such as significance-aware pruning to preserve structural integrity [S2, 9676-significance-aware-pruning.md] and principled uncertainty pruning using Fisher Information matrices for post-hoc compression [S1, 9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md, unofficial]. Variants like TangoGS and Depth-Based Pruning address specific challenges related to capture size invariance and far-plane preservation, respectively [S1, 12338-depth-based-gaussian-pruning.md, unofficial]. The technique is widely used in real-time rendering pipelines and large-scale scene reconstruction to balance quality and efficiency [S1, unofficial][S2].
