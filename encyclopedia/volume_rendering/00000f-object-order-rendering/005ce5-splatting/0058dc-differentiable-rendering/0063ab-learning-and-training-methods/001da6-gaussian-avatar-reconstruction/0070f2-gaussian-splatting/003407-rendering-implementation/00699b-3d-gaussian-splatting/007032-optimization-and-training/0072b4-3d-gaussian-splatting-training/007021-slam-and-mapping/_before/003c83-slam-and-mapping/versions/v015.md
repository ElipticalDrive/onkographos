# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM, Active Mapping with 3DGS

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) integrates simultaneous localization and mapping with the explicit, differentiable representation of 3D Gaussians. It solves the problem of reconstructing a 3D scene and estimating camera poses simultaneously, leveraging the real-time rendering and explicit geometry of 3DGS to alleviate the misalignment issues common in implicit NeRF-based methods [S1]. Unlike standard offline training, these methods often operate incrementally, updating the Gaussian field as new frames arrive. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by their inclusion of pose estimation and loop closure mechanisms, and from [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md) by their focus on online reconstruction rather than post-hoc scene alignment.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves maintaining a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ that represent the scene geometry and appearance. The system alternates between or jointly optimizes camera poses $\mathbf{T}$ and Gaussian parameters (position $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}$).

1.  **Rendering:** For a given camera pose $\mathbf{T}$, the Gaussians are projected onto the image plane. The 2D covariance $\Sigma_{2D}$ is computed via the Jacobian of the affine approximation of the projection [S2, unofficial]. Pixels are rendered via alpha compositing:
    $$
    C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\vec{\omega})
    $$
    where $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Tracking:** Camera poses are estimated by minimizing a loss between the rendered image $\hat{C}$ and the observed image $C$. This often involves depth consistency and photometric terms [S5].

3.  **Mapping:** The Gaussian parameters are updated via gradient descent to minimize the rendering loss. Densification strategies (cloning, splitting, pruning) are applied based on gradients or uncertainty to adapt the representation density [S1].

4.  **Loop Closure:** To correct drift, the system detects revisited locations and performs global bundle adjustment or elastic deformation of the neural points/Gaussians [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scene representations, typically for tasks such as 3D editing, style transfer, or multi-scene fusion. Unlike standard SLAM mapping which updates a single scene's geometry and appearance, these strategies solve the problem of transferring visual attributes (density, radiance, color) from a source scene to a target scene while maintaining structural fidelity. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md) by their focus on explicit pixel-to-pixel or region-to-region correspondence algorithms rather than high-level semantic label propagation. See [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md).

### CG-SLAM
**Definition:** CG-SLAM is a method that significantly improves reconstruction quality by incorporating uncertainty estimates into the training process [S1].
**Step:** It introduces an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1].

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
**Definition:** This approach combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with these poses and sensor depth of keyframes, effectively performing offline splatting [S2, unofficial].
**Number:** On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), the ATE RMSE (m) for ORB-SLAM3 is 0.052, while the combined approach yields 3.190. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences [S2, unofficial].
**Comparison:** ORB-SLAM achieves the best ATE, but the combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. The unified approach is generally preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S2, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
**Definition:** CP-SLAM is a neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system that enables multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure [S3].
**Step:** It uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization [S3]. It employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly [S3]. It integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S3].

### MNE-SLAM
**Definition:** MNE-SLAM is the first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange [S3].
**Step:** It uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module [S3]. It introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S3].

### HERO-SLAM
**Definition:** HERO-SLAM is a SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings [S3].
**Step:** It uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S3].

### PIN-SLAM
**Definition:** PIN-SLAM is a globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation [S3].
**Step:** It replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure [S3]. Mapping alternates with odometry [S3]. The mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders [S3]. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization [S3]. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates [S3]. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S3].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
**Step:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M(c)$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$ [S4, unofficial].
2.  **Global Policy:** Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$ [S4, unofficial].
3.  **Local Policy:** Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
4.  **Action:** Move agent to $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
5.  **Observation:** Obtain new image observation $I_t$ [S4, unofficial].
6.  **Update Database:** Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$ [S4, unofficial].
7.  **Mapping Optimization:** Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$ [S4, unofficial].
8.  **Stop Check:** Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold [S4, unofficial].
9.  **Fine Stage:** After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M(f)$ with trained coarse model $M_T(c)$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S4, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
**Definition:** A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison [S5].
**Step:**
1.  Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$ [S5].
2.  Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity [S5].
3.  Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$ [S5].
4.  Discard the L channel [S5].
5.  Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences [S5].
**Formula:** $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth [S5].
**Parameter:** $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss [S5]. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S5].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
**Step:** Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM) [S6]. Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration [S6]. Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent [S6]. Compute EIG for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration [S6].
**Formula:** $P_j = \arg\max_{P_j} \text{tr} \left( \sum_{x_{acq_i} \in P_j} H''[y_{i\_acq}|x_{acq_i}, w^*] \right) H''[w^*|D_{train}]^{-1}$ [S6].

## Background
The integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting into SLAM systems emerged as a response to the computational bottlenecks and optimization difficulties of NeRF-based SLAM methods. While NeRF-based methods like NICE-SLAM and ESLAM provided high-quality reconstructions, they suffered from slow training and rendering speeds [S5]. 3DGS, introduced by Kerbl et al. [S2, unofficial], offered real-time rendering and explicit geometry, making it suitable for online applications. Early works like SplaTAM [S5] demonstrated the viability of using 3DGS for joint tracking and mapping. Subsequent research expanded this to multi-agent systems (CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM) [S3], active mapping (ActiveRMAP, FisherRF) [S4, unofficial][S6], and hybrid approaches combining classical SLAM with 3DGS (Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS) [S2, unofficial]. The explicit nature of 3DGS allows for flexible reprojection, alleviating misalignment between viewpoints and leading to better reconstruction quality compared to NeRF-based methods [S1].
