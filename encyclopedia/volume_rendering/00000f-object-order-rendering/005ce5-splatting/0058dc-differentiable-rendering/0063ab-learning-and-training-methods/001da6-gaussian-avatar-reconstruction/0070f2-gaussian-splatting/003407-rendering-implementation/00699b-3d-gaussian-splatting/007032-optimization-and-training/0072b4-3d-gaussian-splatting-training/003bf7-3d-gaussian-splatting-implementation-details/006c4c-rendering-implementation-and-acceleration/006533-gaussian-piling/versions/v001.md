# Gaussian Piling

Also called: Focus Gaussian, GaussianPile, Slice-based Volumetric Reconstruction

## Overview
Gaussian Piling is a slice-based volumetric reconstruction method that unifies 3D Gaussian Splatting with an imaging system-aware focus model to compress aggressively while preserving internal structure [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which discards internal volumetric information and produces incoherent 3D structures with floating artifacts for slice-based modalities, Gaussian Piling models the finite focal zone to preserve high-frequency internal volumetric detail [S1, unofficial]. It achieves this by maintaining Gaussian primitives' form through axial re-parameterization, opacity modulation, and additive rasterization to enforce 3D-to-2D consistency [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $M$ 3D Gaussian primitives defined in world space, each with center $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$, opacity $\alpha$, and scale/orientation parameters. The imaging system's axial resolution $\sigma_z$ (standard deviation of the point spread function in the elevational direction).
*   **Outputs:** Rendered 2D slice images $I(p)$ and a differentiable 3D voxel volume $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian primitive $i$ stores:
*   Position $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (world space).
*   Scale $s_x, s_y, s_z \in \mathbb{R}$ and Rotation quaternion $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (used to construct $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$).
*   Opacity $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.
*   *Note:* Gaussian Piling discards spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients used in standard 3DGS for BRDF fitting, reducing parameters per primitive by ~40% [S1, unofficial].

**Covariance Factorization:**
The covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is factorized as $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^\top\mathbf{R}^\top$, where $\mathbf{S} = \text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ and $\mathbf{R}$ is a rotation matrix derived from unit quaternion $q$ [S1, unofficial].

**Rendering Pipeline Steps:**
The rendering pipeline consists of three stages: Scan, Axial Re-parameterization & Opacity Modulation, and Screen-space Projection & Additive Rasterization [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Scan:** Project Gaussians onto slices at different depths. The analysis is conducted in the camera coordinate system (subscript $c$). The point spread function (PSF) is modeled as a spatially invariant 3D Gaussian with standard deviations $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$ [S1, unofficial]. The sensitivity map $h$ is defined as the system's reversed impulse response along the elevational direction:
    $$h(-z_c) = \exp\left(-\frac{z_c^2}{2\sigma_z^2}\right)$$
    where $z_c$ is the coordinate in the camera system [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Axial Re-parameterization:** To encode finite slice thickness, the axial resolution term is injected into the inverse covariance matrix in camera coordinates. This shrinks the axial support without disturbing the lateral structure, producing an "Effective Gaussian" $\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_e, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e)$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e^{-1} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} + \frac{\mathbf{e}_3 \mathbf{e}_3^\top}{\sigma_z^2}$$
    $$\boldsymbol{\mu}_e = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_c$$
    where $\mathbf{e}_3 = [0, 0, 1]^\top$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Opacity Modulation:** To suppress contributions from out-of-focus primitives and prevent ghosting artifacts, opacity is modulated based on the change in Mahalanobis distance induced by the axial injection [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\text{opacity}_r = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_c^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_c - \boldsymbol{\mu}_e^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_e)\right)$$
    Primitives with opacity below a threshold $\tau$ are culled [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Screen-space Projection:** The 2D Gaussian parameters on the imaging plane are obtained by marginalizing the Effective Gaussian's covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e$ over the $(x_c, y_c)$ plane [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d} = \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_e[0,0] & \Sigma_e[1,0] \\ \Sigma_e[1,0] & \Sigma_e[1,1] \end{bmatrix}$$
    $$\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2d} = [\mu_e[0], \mu_e[1]]^\top$$
    The final rendered intensity $\tilde{\alpha}$ is normalized using the determinant of the 2D covariance to preserve total energy as primitives stretch or shrink [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\tilde{\alpha} = \alpha \cdot \text{opacity}_r / \sqrt{\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})}$$

5.  **Additive Rasterization:** In slice imaging, pixel intensity originates from the integral of Gaussian opacities along the projection line, where contributions superimpose without occlusion. For each pixel $p$, sum the contributions of overlapping Gaussians $i \in \mathcal{N}(p)$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$I(p) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}(p)} \tilde{\alpha}_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}_i^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d,i}^{-1} \mathbf{d}_i\right)$$
    where $\mathbf{d}_i$ is the vector from $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2d}$ to pixel $p$ [S1, unofficial].

**Differentiable Voxelization:**
To enable 3D visualization, a differentiable voxelizer partitions 3D space into uniform $8^3$ tiles. Gaussians are culled per tile if their spatial support (3-$\sigma$ ellipsoid) does not intersect the tile. Voxel intensity $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is computed by summing world-space Gaussians [S1, unofficial]:
$$\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^M g_i^3(\mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i)$$
where $g_i^3$ is the original Gaussian primitive in world coordinates [S1, unofficial].

**Optimization:**
*   **Loss Function:** The framework is optimized using a weighted combination of L1 loss $\mathcal{L}_1$ and D-SSIM loss $\mathcal{L}_{ssim}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{ssim}$$
*   **Gradients:** Full gradient propagation is implemented in CUDA. The gradient w.r.t. opacity $\alpha$ is [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \tilde{\alpha}} \cdot \frac{\text{opacity}_r}{\sqrt{\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})}}$$
    The gradient w.r.t. effective covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e$ couples the determinant term and the conic term [S1, unofficial]:
    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e} \propto \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \tilde{\alpha}} \cdot \frac{\partial \tilde{\alpha}}{\partial \det} + (\text{conic})$$
*   **Densification/Pruning:** An adaptive mechanism based on tile-wise focus radii and Gaussian opacity scores refines the distribution. Adaptive control runs with a gradient threshold [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_z$: Axial resolution term representing slice thickness. Estimated theoretically, numerically, or via experimental calibration. If unknown, assumed equal to scanning step $\delta_z$ satisfying Nyquist criterion ($\sigma_z \approx \delta_z$) [S1, unofficial].
*   $\tau$: Opacity minimal threshold for culling primitives. Default value is 0.02 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Weight balancing perceptual (SSIM) and pixel-wise (L1) fidelity in the loss function. Default value is 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   Learning rates: Position 0.0006, Opacity 0.02, Scale 0.002, Rotation 0.001. Exponentially decay to 0.1 of initial values [S1, unofficial].
*   $M$: Initial number of Gaussians. Default value is 1000k [S1, unofficial].
*   Gradient threshold for adaptive control: 0.00005 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Ghosting or floating artifacts across slices are prevented by downweighting primitives far from the focal vicinity via opacity modulation, which naturally attenuates transparency for out-of-focus primitives [S1, unofficial].
*   Standard 3DGS fails for slice-based modalities because it discards internal volumetric information; Gaussian Piling addresses this by enforcing 3D-to-2D consistency through the focus-aware model [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method delivers high-quality results in as few as 3 minutes, up to 11× faster than NeRF-based approaches [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Quantization and compression achieve a 16× reduction over voxel-based storage while retaining full 2D rendering fidelity and volumetric consistency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Differentiable Voxelizer performance is > 100 FPS for real-time query performance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency internal volumetric detail; achieves 16× compression over voxel grids; fast training and rendering; prevents ghosting artifacts.
*   **Limitations:** Discards spherical harmonics, which may limit view-dependent appearance modeling compared to standard 3DGS (though this reduces parameters by ~40% and improves geometric fidelity) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Slice-based volumetric imaging (e.g., microscopy, ultrasound) where internal structure preservation and compression are critical.
*   **When not to use:** Surface-only rendering where view-dependent BRDF effects are paramount and internal volume consistency is not required.

## Background
Gaussian Piling was introduced as a unified sparse Gaussian splatting framework for slice-based volumetric reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the gap between the high compression ratio of Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) and the fast fitting of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), specifically for scientific and medical imaging where complete internal structure is paramount [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated on datasets including TDSC-ABUS (ultrasound), rDL-LSM (microscopy), and cellular datasets [S1, unofficial].
