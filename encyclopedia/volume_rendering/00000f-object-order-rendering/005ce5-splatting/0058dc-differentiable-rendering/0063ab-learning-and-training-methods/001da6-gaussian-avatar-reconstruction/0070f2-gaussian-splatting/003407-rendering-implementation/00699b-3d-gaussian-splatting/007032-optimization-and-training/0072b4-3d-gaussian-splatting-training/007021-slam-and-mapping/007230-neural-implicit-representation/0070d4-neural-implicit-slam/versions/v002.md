# Neural Implicit SLAM

Also called: NeRF-SLAM, Implicit SLAM, Neural Field SLAM

## Overview
Neural Implicit SLAM is a simultaneous localization and mapping approach that constructs a continuous 3D scene representation using a neural network $F$ that maps spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ to scene properties such as color, density, or signed distance values [S1, S3]. It solves the problem of joint camera pose estimation and dense 3D reconstruction by optimizing the network weights and camera poses $T_t$ to minimize photometric and geometric losses derived from rendered views [S3]. Unlike traditional SLAM systems that rely on discrete feature points or voxel grids, this family leverages differentiable rendering to provide dense, high-fidelity maps, distinguishing itself from explicit representation methods by its ability to model complex geometry and appearance continuously [S1, S3].

## Shared principles
The family relies on a neural network $F$ that maps spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ to scene properties. For appearance, $F$ outputs color $\mathbf{c}$ and volume density $\sigma$ (or occupancy) [S1]. For geometry, $F$ may output a Signed Distance Function (SDF) value [S1]. Rendering is performed via volume rendering or ray marching: points are sampled along camera rays, properties are predicted by $F$, and results are aggregated using alpha compositing or weighted sums [S1]. Optimization jointly updates the network weights and camera poses $T_t$ to minimize photometric ($\ell_{rgb}$) and geometric ($\ell_{depth}$ or SDF) losses [S1].

The problem is formulated as a continuous online learning task where a stream of synchronized input frames is processed to optimize the neural implicit function $F$ and camera pose $T_t$ using cached sampled rays [S3]. For each pixel $\mathbf{x}$ in an input frame, $N$ points are sampled along the camera ray, each at distance $d_i$ with position $\mathbf{p}_i$ [S3]. The network predicts appearance $\mathbf{c}_i$ and geometric properties $\mathbf{g}_i$ for each sample point $\mathbf{p}_i$:
$$
(\mathbf{c}_i, \mathbf{g}_i) = F(\mathbf{p}_i)
$$
The weight $w_i$ for each sample is determined by a geometric rendering function $G$ applied to the geometric properties:
$$
w_i = G(\mathbf{g}_i)
$$
Rendered color $\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{x}}$ and depth $\tilde{d}_{\mathbf{x}}$ are computed as weighted sums:
$$
\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{x}} = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i, \quad \tilde{d}_{\mathbf{x}} = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot d_i
$$
Optimization minimizes photometric and geometric constraints:
$$
e^p_{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{c}_{\mathbf{x}} - c_{\mathbf{x}}, \quad e^g_{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{d}_{\mathbf{x}} - d_{\mathbf{x}}
$$
The weight is formulated as $w_i = G(F_{geo}(\mathbf{p}_i))$, where $F \in \mathcal{F}$ and $G \in \mathcal{G}$ [S3].

## Subtopics

### SLAIM
SLAIM is a SLAM method that introduces a coarse-to-fine tracking pipeline and improves photometric bundle adjustment for NeRF-based mapping [S1]. It addresses the challenge of high-frequency renderings in NeRF hindering image alignment by maintaining NeRF’s original volume density formulation while introducing a KL regularization over the ray termination distribution [S1].

### SNI-SLAM
SNI-SLAM is a dense NeRF-based RGB-D SLAM system designed for accurate real-time 3D semantic mapping [S1]. It introduces a hierarchical semantic encoding and cross-attention mechanism to enable mutual reinforcement between appearance, geometry, and semantics modalities, and proposes a novel one-way decoder design to enhance inter-modal information flow without reverse interference [S1].

### KN-SLAM
KN-SLAM is a NeRF-based SLAM method that integrates local feature correspondences for coarse pose initialization and joint optimization [S1]. It uses global image features and local matches for explicit loop detection, and performs pose graph optimization and global refinement of the neural map to ensure consistency [S1].

### VI-NeRF-SLAM
VI-NeRF-SLAM merges traditional SLAM method benefits with NeRF mapping to facilitate immediate, dense 3D scene reconstruction online without prior training [S2]. It estimates pixel depth and pose based on constraints in reprojecting feature points, refines the depiction of 3D scenes through the use of ray integration and networks, includes a dynamic error correction technique for IMUs, and includes a method for managing closed loops in NeRF mapping [S2]. It surpasses current benchmark techniques in reconstruction and tracking precision on Euroc and Replica datasets, particularly in scenarios limited to grayscale imagery [S2].

### NeRF-SLAM Problem Formulation
NeRF-SLAM Problem Formulation is a continuous online learning task where a stream of synchronized RGB-D input frames is processed to optimize a neural implicit function $F$ and camera pose $T_t$ using cached sampled rays [S3]. For each pixel $\mathbf{x}$ in an RGB-D frame, $N$ points are sampled along the camera ray, each at distance $d_i$ with position $\mathbf{p}_i$. Appearance $\mathbf{c}_i$ and geometric properties $\mathbf{g}_i$ are predicted for each sample point $\mathbf{p}_i$ using the learnable neural implicit function $F$. The weight $w_i$ for each sample is determined by a geometric rendering function $G$ applied to the geometric properties: $w_i = G(\mathbf{g}_i)$. Rendered color and depth are computed as weighted sums: $\tilde{c}_{\mathbf{x}} = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i$ and $\tilde{d}_{\mathbf{x}} = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot d_i$. Optimization minimizes photometric and geometric constraints: $e^p_{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{c}_{\mathbf{x}} - c_{\mathbf{x}}$ and $e^g_{\mathbf{x}} = \tilde{d}_{\mathbf{x}} - d_{\mathbf{x}}$. The weight is formulated as $w_i = G(F_{geo}(\mathbf{p}_i))$, where $F \in \mathcal{F}$ and $G \in \mathcal{G}$ [S3].

### SE3
SE3 is a method that addresses loop closure correction for implicit maps by implementing an equivariant mapping from SE3 to feature space, synchronously adjusting camera poses and implicit map representations [S4]. It leverages the frontend localization module of ORB-SLAM2 and uses a voxel grid as the backend to construct a SLAM system that achieves loop closure correction for implicit maps [S4].

## Background
Neural Implicit SLAM emerged from the convergence of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and traditional SLAM systems. The foundational concept of mapping spatial coordinates to scene properties via neural networks was popularized by NeRF [S1]. Early applications in SLAM focused on RGB-D inputs, formulating the problem as continuous online learning to optimize implicit functions and camera poses simultaneously [S3]. The field has since expanded to include semantic mapping [S1], loop closure handling in implicit spaces [S4], and multi-agent frameworks [S1]. Key developments include the integration of local feature correspondences for initialization [S1], hierarchical semantic encoding [S1], and dynamic error correction for IMUs [S2]. The approach is primarily used in robotics and computer vision for dense 3D reconstruction and localization in environments where traditional feature-based methods may struggle with textureless or repetitive structures [S1, S2].
