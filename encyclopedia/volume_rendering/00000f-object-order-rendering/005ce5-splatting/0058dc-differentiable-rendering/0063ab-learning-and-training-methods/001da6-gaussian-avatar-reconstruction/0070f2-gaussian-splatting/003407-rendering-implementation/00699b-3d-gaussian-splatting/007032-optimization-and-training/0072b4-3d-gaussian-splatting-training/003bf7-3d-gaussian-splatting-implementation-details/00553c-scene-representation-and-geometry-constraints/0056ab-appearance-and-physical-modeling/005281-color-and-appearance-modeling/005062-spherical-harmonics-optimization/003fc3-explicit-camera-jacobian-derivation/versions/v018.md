# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera pose optimization in SLAM systems [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from prior ray-intersection methods by providing the first analytical Jacobian for EWA splatting and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), ensuring minimal parameterization via Lie algebra and avoiding the overhead of automatic differentiation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$, 3D Gaussian mean $\mu_G \in \mathbb{R}^3$, 3D Gaussian covariance $\Sigma_G \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, and projection parameters.
*   **Output:** Jacobians $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$, where $\mu_I$ and $\Sigma_I$ are the projected 2D mean and covariance in image coordinates.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing camera-to-world pose.
*   $\mu_C$: $3 \times 1$ vector representing the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
*   $W$: $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix component of $T_{CW}$.
*   $\tau \in se(3)$: Infinitesimal twist vector (6D).
*   $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Rotation vector component of $\tau$.
*   $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Translation vector component of $\tau$.
*   $\times$: Hat operator mapping a 3D vector to a $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrix.

**Definitions:**
The derivative on the manifold is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau}
$$
where $\mathbf{T} \in SE(3)$, $\tau \in se(3)$, $\circ$ is group composition, and Exp/Log are exponential/logarithmic mappings [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Camera Coordinates:** Transform the Gaussian mean to camera coordinates:
    $$ \mu_C = W \mu_G + t $$
    where $t$ is the translation part of $T_{CW}$.

2.  **Derive Jacobian of $\mu_C$ w.r.t. $T_{CW}$:**
    Using the manifold derivative definition:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Exp}(\tau) \cdot \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Expand $\text{Exp}(\tau) \approx I + \tau^\wedge$:
    $$
    = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(I + \tau^\wedge) \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Decompose $\tau^\wedge \mu_C = \theta^\times \mu_C + \rho = -\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix} \quad (6)
    $$
    where $I$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix and $\mu_C^\times$ is the skew-symmetric matrix of $\mu_C$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Derive Jacobian of Rotation Component $W$ w.r.t. $T_{CW}$:**
    The rotation matrix $W$ is a function of the rotation vector $\theta$. The derivative of the skew-symmetric matrix $\theta^\times$ with respect to $\theta_x$ is $e_1^\times$, where $e_1 = [1, 0, 0]^\top$. Generally:
    $$
    \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_i} = e_i^\times W
    $$
    Constructing the full Jacobian for the rotation part (6D twist):
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{:,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,3}^\times \end{bmatrix} \quad (6)
    $$
    where $W_{:,i}$ is the $i$-th column of $W$, and the top $3 \times 3$ block is zero because $W$ does not depend on translation $\rho$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Chain Rule for Projected Mean $\mu_I$:**
    Apply the chain rule using the Jacobian from Step 2:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} \quad (3)
    $$
    where $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C}$ is the standard projection Jacobian (e.g., from perspective projection) [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Compute Chain Rule for Projected Covariance $\Sigma_I$:**
    The 2D covariance $\Sigma_I$ depends on the projection Jacobian $J$ (which depends on $\mu_C$) and the rotation $W$. Apply the chain rule:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} \quad (4)
    $$
    where $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J}$ and $\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C}$ are derived from the EWA splatting projection equations [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Requires at least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def skew_symmetric(v):
    """Converts a 3D vector to a 3x3 skew-symmetric matrix."""
    return np.array([
        [0, -v[2], v[1]],
        [v[2], 0, -v[0]],
        [-v[1], v[0], 0]
    ])

def compute_camera_jacobians(mu_G, T_CW, projection_jac_mu, projection_jac_sigma_J, projection_jac_sigma_W):
    """
    Computes analytical Jacobians of projected 2D Gaussian parameters w.r.t. SE(3) camera pose.
    
    Args:
        mu_G: 3D Gaussian mean in world coordinates (3,)
        T_CW: Camera-to-world pose (4x4 matrix)
        projection_jac_mu: d(mu_I)/d(mu_C) (2x3 matrix)
        projection_jac_sigma_J: d(Sigma_I)/d(J) (6x9 matrix, flattened J)
        projection_jac_sigma_W: d(Sigma_I)/d(W) (6x9 matrix, flattened W)
        
    Returns:
        d_mu_I_d_T: Jacobian of projected mean w.r.t. camera pose (2x6)
        d_Sigma_I_d_T: Jacobian of projected covariance w.r.t. camera pose (6x6)
    """
    # Extract rotation and translation
    W = T_CW[:3, :3]
    t = T_CW[:3, 3]
    
    # 1. Compute Gaussian mean in camera coordinates
    mu_C = W @ mu_G + t
    
    # 2. Compute D(mu_C)/D(T_CW) = [I, -mu_C^x]
    mu_C_skew = skew_symmetric(mu_C)
    D_mu_C_d_T = np.hstack([np.eye(3), -mu_C_skew])  # 3x6
    
    # 3. Compute D(W)/D(T_CW)
    # Columns of W
    W_col1 = W[:, 0].reshape(3, 1)
    W_col2 = W[:, 1].reshape(3, 1)
    W_col3 = W[:, 2].reshape(3, 1)
    
    # Skew symmetric matrices of columns
    W1_skew = skew_symmetric(W[:, 0])
    W2_skew = skew_symmetric(W[:, 1])
    W3_skew = skew_symmetric(W[:, 2])
    
    # Construct the 9x6 matrix (flattened W derivative)
    # The formula is block column: [0, -W_{:,1}^x; 0, -W_{:,2}^x; 0, -W_{:,3}^x]
    # Each block is 3x6. The first 3 cols are 0, next 3 cols are -W_{:,i}^x
    D_W_d_T = np.zeros((9, 6))
    D_W_d_T[:3, 3:] = -W1_skew
    D_W_d_T[3:6, 3:] = -W2_skew
    D_W_d_T[6:9, 3:] = -W3_skew
    
    # 4. Compute d(mu_I)/d(T_CW)
    # d_mu_I_d_T = d_mu_I/d_mu_C * D_mu_C_d_T
    d_mu_I_d_T = projection_jac_mu @ D_mu_C_d_T  # (2x3) @ (3x6) -> (2x6)
    
    # 5. Compute d(Sigma_I)/d(T_CW)
    # Term 1: d(Sigma_I)/d(J) * d(J)/d(mu_C) * D_mu_C_d_T
    # Note: d(J)/d(mu_C) is typically 9x3. 
    # Let J_jac_mu = d(J)/d(mu_C)
    # We need to pass J_jac_mu or compute it. Assuming it's available or computed from projection geometry.
    # For this pseudocode, we assume projection_jac_sigma_J includes the chain rule part d(Sigma_I)/d(J) * d(J)/d(mu_C)
    # If not, one must compute d(J)/d(mu_C) explicitly.
    # Here we assume projection_jac_sigma_J is pre-computed as d(Sigma_I)/d(mu_C) via J
    term1 = projection_jac_sigma_J @ D_mu_C_d_T  # (6x3) @ (3x6) -> (6x6)
    
    # Term 2: d(Sigma_I)/d(W) * D_W_d_T
    # projection_jac_sigma_W is d(Sigma_I)/d(flattened_W) (6x9)
    term2 = projection_jac_sigma_W @ D_W_d_T  # (6x9) @ (9x6) -> (6x6)
    
    d_Sigma_I_d_T = term1 + term2
    
    return d_mu_I_d_T, d_Sigma_I_d_T
```

## Subtopics

### [Photometric and Geometric Tracking](10080-photometric-and-geometric-tracking.md)
Photometric and Geometric Tracking optimizes only the current camera pose without updating the map representation, minimizing photometric and/or geometric residuals to estimate motion. It sets itself apart from full SLAM or mapping methods by decoupling pose estimation from scene reconstruction, allowing for efficient real-time tracking using pre-existing Gaussian splats.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per frame for $N$ Gaussians, as Jacobians are computed analytically without AD overhead.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing Gaussians and their Jacobians.
*   **Performance:** Enables real-time tracking (e.g., 3.2 FPS monocular, 2.5 FPS RGB-D on Replica) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates AD overhead, ensures minimal parameterization (6 DOF), and is applicable to high-density 3DGS splatting.
*   **Limitations:** Requires at least 50 gradient descent iterations per frame for accurate tracking, which can be computationally expensive if not optimized [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When performing camera pose optimization (tracking) in SLAM systems using 3DGS.
*   **When not to use:** When offline mapping or when camera poses are known and fixed.

## Background
Originated in "Gaussian Splatting SLAM" (2023) by [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the lack of analytical Jacobians for SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians in EWA splatting and 3DGS, enabling efficient SLAM. Prior methods assumed fewer Gaussians or used ray-intersection, which is not applicable to 3DGS.
