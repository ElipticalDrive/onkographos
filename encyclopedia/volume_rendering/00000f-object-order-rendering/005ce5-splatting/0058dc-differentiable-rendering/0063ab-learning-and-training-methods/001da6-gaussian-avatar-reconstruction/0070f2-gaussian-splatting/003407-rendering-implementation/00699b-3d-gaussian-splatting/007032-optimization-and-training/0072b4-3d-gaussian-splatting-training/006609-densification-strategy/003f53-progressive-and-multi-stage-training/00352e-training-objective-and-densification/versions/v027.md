# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the optimization objective and densification strategy for **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting), which reconstructs high-quality 3D scenes by aligning Gaussian primitives with an explicit mesh surface [S1]. It solves the problem of standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) ignoring existing 3D geometry, which can lead to artifacts and inefficient primitive placement [S1]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by explicitly distinguishing between **tightly-bound** splats (constrained to the mesh) and **loosely-bound** splats (free-floating), applying distinct loss functions and densification rules to each group [S1]. Unlike [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) which manages resolution, or [Budget-Centered Training](9682-budget-centered-training.md) which manages count, this method manages the *spatial relationship* between Gaussians and the mesh [S1].

## How it works
The method operates on a set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, partitioned into two subsets: tightly-bound splats $G^t$ and loosely-bound splats $G^l$ [S1]. The partitioning is determined by the distance of each Gaussian center $P_i$ to the mesh surface $M$; if the distance is below a threshold $d_{th}$, the Gaussian is classified as tightly-bound [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$, each defined by mean $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$ (scale $s_i$, rotation $q_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$.
    *   An explicit mesh $M$ consisting of faces $F$ and vertices $V$.
    *   Ground truth images $I_i$ and rendered images $\hat{I}_i$.
    *   Camera poses and intrinsics.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized Gaussian parameters $\mathcal{G}$ and rendered images.

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** Each Gaussian $g_i$ stores:
    *   $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Position.
    *   $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale parameters (log-space or raw, as per 3DGS).
    *   $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation quaternion.
    *   $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$: Opacity.
    *   $\mathbf{c}_i$: Spherical harmonic coefficients.
    *   $type \in \{t, l\}$: Classification as tightly-bound or loosely-bound.
*   **Mesh Data:**
    *   $F$: List of triangular faces.
    *   $V$: List of vertices.
    *   $n_f$: Normal vector for face $f \in F$.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization and Classification:**
    *   Initialize Gaussians.
    *   For each Gaussian $i$, calculate the distance to the mesh surface.
    *   If distance $< d_{th}$, assign $g_i \in G^t$. Otherwise, assign $g_i \in G^l$ [S1].
    *   Default threshold $d_{th} = 0.01$ [S1].

2.  **Rendering:**
    *   Render the scene using standard differentiable splatting to produce $\hat{I}_i$ [S1].

3.  **Loss Calculation:**
    Compute the total loss $L$ as the sum of image loss, normal consistency loss, scale regularization loss, and projection loss [S1]:
    $$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj} $$

    *   **Image Loss ($L_{img}$):**
        Combines L1 loss and D-SSIM [S1]:
        $$ L_{img} = (1 - \lambda_{img}) L_1(\hat{I}_i, I_i) + \lambda_{img} SSIM(\hat{I}_i, I_i) $$
        Where $\lambda_{img}$ is the weight for SSIM [S1].

    *   **Normal Consistency Loss ($L_{nc}$):**
        Aligns tightly-bound splat normals with mesh face normals [S1].
        First, estimate the normal $n_i$ for each tightly-bound Gaussian $g_i \in G^t$:
        1.  Identify the minimum scale axis index $k$ such that $s_k = \min(s_1, s_2, s_3)$.
        2.  Construct the rotation matrix $R_i$ from quaternion $q_i$.
        3.  Transform the local axis vector $e_k = [0,0,0]^T + e_k$ (unit vector along the minimum scale axis) to world coordinates: $n_i = R_i e_k$.
        4.  Compute cosine similarity with the corresponding mesh face normal $n_f$ [S1]:
        $$ L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^t, f \in F} (1 - n_i \cdot n_f) $$
        *Note: The summation implies matching each tightly-bound Gaussian to its closest mesh face $f$ [S1].*

    *   **Scale Regularization Loss ($L_{scale}$):**
        Constrains the scale of tightly-bound splats to align with the mesh surface thickness [S1]:
        $$ L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \left( \lambda_{min} |\min(s_1, s_2, s_3)| + \lambda_{max} |\max(s_1, s_2, s_3) - \rho| \right) $$
        Where $\rho$ is the threshold for the maximum scale [S1].

    *   **Projection Loss ($L_{proj}$):**
        Ensures tightly-bound splat centers remain close to the mesh surface [S1].
        1.  For each $g_i \in G^t$, find the closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$.
        2.  The projection formula given in the source is:
            $$ s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j $$
            *Note: This formula appears to project $P_i$ onto the line defined by $f_j$ passing through $v_i$, or potentially contains a typo in the source regarding the normal vector. Standard projection onto a plane with normal $n_f$ would be $P_i - ((P_i - v_i) \cdot n_f)n_f$. However, the source explicitly writes $f_j$ in the dot product and multiplication. We follow the source's explicit formula [S1].*
        3.  Compute the loss:
            $$ L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \| s_i - P_i \|_2 $$

4.  **Optimization Step:**
    *   Perform gradient descent using the total loss $L$ [S1].

5.  **Densification Strategy:**
    *   **For Loosely-bound Splats ($G^l$):**
        *   Follow the original 3DGS densification strategy (gradient-based splitting and cloning) [S1].
    *   **For Tightly-bound Splats ($G^t$):**
        *   Identify the mesh triangle close to the Gaussian [S1].
        *   **Cloning:**
            *   Conduct point sampling from the corresponding mesh triangle.
            *   Use the sampled position to initialize the new Gaussian.
            *   Copy additional attributes (opacity, SH coefficients, rotation) from the parent Gaussian [S1].
        *   **Splitting:**
            *   Conduct point sampling from the corresponding mesh triangle.
            *   Use the sampled position to initialize the new Gaussian.
            *   Divide the scale of the new Gaussians by a factor of 1.6 [S1].

6.  **Iteration:**
    *   Repeat steps 1-5 for 30,000 iterations [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{img} = 0.001$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{nc} = 0.1$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{min} = 0.1$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{max} = 10$ [S1]
*   $\lambda_{proj} = 50$ [S1]
*   $\rho = 0.1$ (threshold for max scale) [S1]
*   $d_{th} = 0.01$ (distance threshold for tight/loose classification) [S1]
*   Total iterations: 30,000 [S1]

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_loss(G_tight, G_loose, mesh, images, rendered_images, params):
    """
    Computes the total loss for MeshGS training.
    G_tight: List of tightly-bound Gaussians
    G_loose: List of loosely-bound Gaussians
    mesh: Mesh object with faces F and vertices V
    images: Ground truth images
    rendered_images: Rendered images
    params: Dictionary of hyperparameters
    """
    lambda_img = params['lambda_img']
    lambda_nc = params['lambda_nc']
    lambda_min = params['lambda_min']
    lambda_max = params['lambda_max']
    lambda_proj = params['lambda_proj']
    rho = params['rho']
    
    # 1. Image Loss (applied to all, but typically computed per view)
    # Assuming batched images
    l1_loss = L1(rendered_images, images)
    ssim_loss = SSIM(rendered_images, images)
    L_img = (1 - lambda_img) * l1_loss + lambda_img * ssim_loss
    
    # 2. Normal Consistency Loss (only for tight splats)
    L_nc = 0.0
    for g in G_tight:
        # Estimate normal: min scale axis rotated to world
        min_scale_idx = argmin(g.scale)
        local_axis = unit_vector(min_scale_idx) # e.g., [1,0,0] if idx 0
        R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(g.rotation)
        n_i = R @ local_axis
        
        # Find closest face f in mesh
        f = mesh.get_closest_face(g.position)
        n_f = f.normal
        
        L_nc += (1 - dot(n_i, n_f))
        
    # 3. Scale Regularization Loss (only for tight splats)
    L_scale = 0.0
    for g in G_tight:
        s_min = min(g.scale)
        s_max = max(g.scale)
        L_scale += lambda_min * abs(s_min) + lambda_max * abs(s_max - rho)
        
    # 4. Projection Loss (only for tight splats)
    L_proj = 0.0
    for g in G_tight:
        P_i = g.position
        # Find closest face f and vertex v_i
        f = mesh.get_closest_face(P_i)
        v_i = f.vertex # Note: Source formula uses v_i, likely a vertex of the face
        f_j = f.normal # Source uses f_j in formula, likely meaning face normal or vector
        
        # Source formula: s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) . f_j) * f_j
        # Note: This projects P_i onto the line defined by f_j through v_i?
        # Or potentially a typo for plane projection. We follow source exactly.
        diff = P_i - v_i
        projection_component = dot(diff, f_j) * f_j
        s_i = P_i + projection_component
        
        L_proj += norm(s_i - P_i)
        
    L_total = L_img + lambda_nc * L_nc + L_scale + lambda_proj * L_proj
    return L_total

def densify(G_tight, G_loose, mesh, params):
    """
    Performs densification based on Gaussian type.
    """
    # Loosely-bound: Standard 3DGS densification
    G_loose = densify_3dgs(G_loose, params)
    
    # Tightly-bound: Mesh-aligned densification
    G_tight_new = []
    for g in G_tight:
        # Determine if split or clone based on gradient/opacity criteria (standard 3DGS logic)
        if should_split(g):
            # Splitting: Sample from mesh triangle
            f = mesh.get_closest_face(g.position)
            new_pos = sample_point_from_triangle(f)
            
            # Create new Gaussians
            # Scale divided by 1.6
            new_scale = g.scale / 1.6
            new_g1 = Gaussian(new_pos, new_scale, g.rotation, g.opacity, g.sh_coeffs)
            new_g2 = Gaussian(new_pos, new_scale, g.rotation, g.opacity, g.sh_coeffs)
            
            G_tight_new.append(new_g1)
            G_tight_new.append(new_g2)
            
        elif should_clone(g):
            # Cloning: Sample from mesh triangle
            f = mesh.get_closest_face(g.position)
            new_pos = sample_point_from_triangle(f)
            
            # Copy attributes
            new_g = Gaussian(new_pos, g.scale, g.rotation, g.opacity, g.sh_coeffs)
            G_tight_new.append(new_g)
        else:
            G_tight_new.append(g)
            
    return G_tight_new, G_loose
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Projection Formula Ambiguity:** The source formula for $L_{proj}$ uses $f_j$ in both the dot product and the multiplication vector. If $f_j$ is a face normal, this projects onto the line parallel to the normal, not the plane. If $f_j$ is a vector along the face, it projects onto that line. This may be a typo in the source for standard plane projection $P_i - ((P_i - v_i) \cdot n_f)n_f$ [S1]. Implementers should verify the intended geometric constraint.
*   **Normal Estimation:** The normal $n_i$ is derived from the minimum scale axis. If the Gaussian is isotropic (spherical), the minimum axis is arbitrary, leading to unstable normals. This is a known issue in 3DGS but exacerbated here for $L_{nc}$ [S1].
*   **Mesh Quality:** The method relies on a high-quality mesh $M$. If the mesh is noisy or decimated poorly, the projection and normal consistency losses will misguide the optimization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Similar to 3DGS, dominated by rendering and gradient computation. The additional mesh queries (closest face, point sampling) add overhead proportional to the number of tightly-bound splats [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the mesh geometry alongside the Gaussian attributes. The mesh size depends on the scene complexity [S1].
*   **Performance:** Evaluated on Mip-NeRF360 and Deep Blending datasets. Trained for 30k iterations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Aligns Gaussians with explicit geometry, reducing artifacts and improving reconstruction quality on surfaces [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on the quality of the input mesh. The projection loss formula ambiguity may lead to suboptimal convergence if not corrected [S1].

## Background
MeshGS (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting) was introduced to address the limitation of 3DGS in ignoring explicit 3D geometry [S1]. It builds on the original 3DGS densification strategy but adapts it for tightly-bound splats by using mesh-based sampling for cloning and splitting [S1]. The method was evaluated against LTM and SuGaR, demonstrating improved rendering quality [S1].
