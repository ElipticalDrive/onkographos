# DashGaussian

Also called: Computational resource allocation for 3DGS

## Overview
DashGaussian is a scheduling scheme for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) optimization that accelerates training by reformulating the process as a progressive fitting of higher frequency components [S1]. It cooperatively allocates rendering resolution and primitive count to strip redundant computational complexity, distinguishing itself from vanilla 3DGS and other backbones by producing a concave-up primitive growth curve that spans the entire optimization process [S1]. Unlike methods that peak in primitive count at half-iteration or use concave-down curves, DashGaussian synchronizes primitive growth with dynamically increasing rendering resolution to balance computing redundancy and fitting quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of training views, an initial set of 3D Gaussian primitives, and a target optimization time (e.g., 200 seconds) [S1].
*   **Output:** Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) and a final primitive count significantly lower than standard 3DGS [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the standard 3DGS primitive representation. Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ (Spherical Harmonics)

The rendering resolution is tracked as a continuous scalar $r(i)$ modulated to an integer for rasterization [S1].

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation for a pixel $u$ remains the standard volume rendering integral discretized over primitives [S1]:
$$ C(u) = \sum_{i=1}^P T_i(u) \cdot w_i(u) \cdot c_i $$
where:
*   $P$ is the total number of active primitives.
*   $T_i(u) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - w_j(u))$ is the transmittance.
*   $w_i(u) = o_i G_{i2D}(u)$ is the weight, with $o_i$ being the opacity and $G_{i2D}(u)$ the 2D Gaussian projected to the image plane.
*   $c_i$ is the color of the $i$-th primitive.

**Optimization Complexity:**
The method defines "optimization complexity" as the collective product of rendering resolution and the number of Gaussian primitives, which dominate time costs [S1].

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **Frequency Formulation:** Formulate the optimization as progressively fitting the 3DGS to higher levels of frequency components in the training views. This is achieved by gradually increasing the rendering resolution during training [S1].
2.  **Dynamic Rendering Resolution:** Apply a dynamic rendering resolution scheme.
    *   Let $r(i)$ be the continuous resolution downsampling factor at iteration $i$.
    *   In practice, modulate this factor to an integer: $r_{int}(i) = \lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ [S1].
    *   To avoid 3D aliasing artifacts when downsampling, apply a low-pass filter before downsampling the image features or use center-cropping with value scaling ($1/r^2$) if operating in frequency domain, though the text emphasizes anti-aliasing downsampling to preserve low-frequency components [S1].
    *   The resolution increases over time, allowing the model to fit high-frequency details only after low-frequency structures are established [S1].
3.  **Primitive Scheduling (Concave-Up Growth):** Schedule the growth of primitives to synchronize with the rendering resolution.
    *   The primitive count follows a concave-up curve, meaning growth accelerates over time, spanning the entire optimization process [S1].
    *   This contrasts with concave-down curves or peaks at half-iterations found in other methods [S1].
    *   The growth is coupled with the rendering resolution via a numerical constraint to balance computing redundancy and fitting quality [S1].
4.  **Momentum-Based Budgeting:** Estimate the maximum number of primitives ($P_{max}$) adaptively using a momentum-based method.
    *   This avoids relying on dataset priors for the final primitive count [S1].
    *   The budget adapts to the scene's complexity and the current optimization stage [S1].
5.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   Render the scene at the current dynamic resolution $r_{int}(i)$.
    *   Compute loss against the training views.
    *   Backpropagate gradients.
    *   Update primitive parameters.
    *   Apply densification (split/clone) and pruning according to the scheduled primitive budget and resolution [S1].
    *   Repeat until convergence or target time is reached.

**Parameters:**
*   **Target Optimization Time:** Typically 200 seconds on consumer-grade GPUs when applied to Taming-3DGS [S1].
*   **Resolution Modulation:** The continuous resolution factor $r(i)$ is modulated using the floor function $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ to encourage faster primitive growth relative to unmodulated cases, optimizing primitives more sufficiently [S1].
*   **Momentum Decay:** Not explicitly given, but a momentum-based method is used for budgeting [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Aliasing:** Simply reducing resolution causes 3D aliasing, sacrificing fidelity at original resolution. Anti-aliasing downsampling (low-pass filtering) is required before downsampling to avoid disturbing recoverable low-frequency components [S1].
*   **Primitive Count:** The method yields fewer primitives than vanilla 3DGS. If the scene requires high detail, the concave-up growth must be sufficient to capture it by the end of optimization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Significantly reduced. DashGaussian accelerates optimization by 45.7% on average across tested backbones [S1]. Specifically, it reduces optimization time for Taming-3DGS by 41.3% [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced primitive count leads to lower memory usage compared to standard 3DGS [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves SOTA rendering quality among accelerated methods, often improving PSNR/SSIM while using fewer primitives [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Plug-and-play integration with existing backbones (3DGS, Mip-Splatting, Revising-3DGS, Taming-3DGS) [S1]. Scene-adaptive scheduling handles diverse frequency information [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the resolution scheduler and momentum budgeting parameters. Relies on the assumption that low-frequency components are fitted first, which may not hold for all scene types (though it is scene-adaptive) [S1].

## Background
DashGaussian was proposed by Youyu Chen, Junjun Jiang, Kui Jiang, et al. from Harbin Institute of Technology and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab [S1]. It was published in CVPR 2025 (arXiv:2503.18402v2, March 26, 2025) [S1]. The work addresses the high computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization by treating it as a resource allocation problem, building on the observation that optimization complexity is dominated by rendering resolution and primitive count [S1].
