# Progressive and Multi-stage Training
## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to optimization strategies that decompose the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into sequential phases or stages, rather than optimizing all parameters simultaneously from a static initialization [G]. It addresses optimization instability and error propagation by decoupling distinct learning objectives across phases [S1, unofficial][S2]. It sets itself apart from single-stage baselines by stabilizing convergence and improving final rendering fidelity through staged refinement [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, where each $g_k$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$ (parameterized by scale $s_k$ and rotation $q_k$), opacity $\alpha_k$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_k$ [Parent]. The core mechanism, densification operations, and buffer management are detailed in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md).

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
Improves radiance field reconstruction by refining the primitive representation in multiple stages instead of a single static configuration. It sets itself apart by dynamically adjusting primitive density based on error metrics during training. Choose it when single-stage training fails to capture high-frequency details or creates redundant primitives in smooth regions.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the loss function and densification strategy for MeshGS, aligning primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It sets itself apart by splitting Gaussians into tightly-bound and loosely-bound subsets, applying mesh-aware constraints only to the former. Choose it when existing 3D geometry must be strictly respected during optimization.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines a pre-initialized or pre-trained set of Gaussian primitives to improve rendering fidelity and adapt to specific data distributions. It sets itself apart by focusing on the refinement of an established primitive set rather than hierarchical scene construction. Choose it when leveraging existing structural priors for faster convergence or correcting initialization artifacts.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Addresses instability in shear-warp rendering by enforcing a structured training schedule that decouples global spatial guidance (sparse voxels) from local structural continuity (slices). It sets itself apart by preventing unstable global approximations caused by random voxel sampling. Choose it for volumetric data optimization where global coverage and local continuity must be balanced.

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
A progressive strategy for feed-forward 3DGS that decouples geometry learning from pose estimation. It sets itself apart by separating training into pose-aware, pose-free, and end-to-end phases. Choose it for architectures predicting Gaussians from context views without ground-truth camera supervision during inference.

### [Free-View Training (FVT)](15162-free-view-training-fvt.md)
Disentangles model performance from the specific number of input views by training on long sequences with randomly sampled context views. It sets itself apart by ensuring generalizability across broader view ranges regardless of input count. Choose it when robust novel view synthesis across diverse viewpoints is required.

### [Training Loop with Mixed Losses and Post-Processing](17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md)
Defines the iterative optimization step combining multiple loss terms (reconstruction, depth, bilateral grid, regularization) to update parameters. It sets itself apart by integrating auxiliary supervisions and post-backward strategy hooks for densification and learning rate scheduling. Choose it when complex regularization and dynamic densification management are needed within a single iteration.

#### Progressive Propagation Strategy
*   **Definition:** Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial]. It computes a local perceptual error map using a lightweight vision-transformer head and densifies only in regions where SSIM falls below a dynamic threshold, significantly improving detail in visually salient areas [S1, unofficial].
#### Interleaved Densification
*   **Step:** Alternate between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].
#### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
*   **Definition:** A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
*   **Step:**
    1.  Construct RGB point clouds for each input view [S3].
    2.  Project the constructed point clouds to the target view using the differentiable point cloud renderer [S3].
    3.  Aggregate all point clouds from the various input views to render RGB images at the target view [S3].
    4.  Optimize the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and the ground truth target images [S3].
    5.  Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) [S3].
*   **Parameter:** Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].
#### Two-Stage Triplane and MLP Training
*   **Step:**
    1.  Jointly train triplanes and MLP on a subset of 500 randomly selected shapes. Use a batch size of 1 object per iteration and 500k occupancy values points per object. Train for 200 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3 [S4].
    2.  Freeze the shared MLP. Train triplane features for each object individually using a batch size of 200k occupancy values per object. Train for 30 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3 [S4].
*   **Parameter:** Triplane feature dimension: 128 × 128 × 32 × 3 [S4]. Triplane initialization: values drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.1 [S4]. MLP architecture: Fourier feature mapping layer (scale factor 1) followed by 3 fully connected layers of dimension 128, each with ReLU activation [S4].
*   **Number:** Stage 1 training time: approximately 1 day on a single RTX 2080ti [S4]. Stage 2 training time: 10 minutes per shape on an RTX 2080ti [S4].
#### Continual Model Refinement
*   **Step:** Augment the initial model with larger sets of closer-up images (64 or 512 images) to simulate prolonged user interaction [S5]. Optimize the model for up to 4000 iterations, including 400 iterations of densification to capture finer details [S5].
*   **Parameter:** Final Gaussian count grows to between 20k and 50k [S5].
*   **Pitfall:** Improvement becomes marginal after 2000 iterations [S5]. Using foveated and subsequently undistorted views for training decreases quality metrics by 5-10% depending on the scene [S5].
*   **Complexity:** Training proceeds at over 500 iterations per second [S5].
*   **Comparison:** Fixed foveation center and variable foveation points yield less than 1% quality difference [S5].

## Background
Progressive and multi-stage training methods have emerged as essential components of 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines to address the sensitivity of the optimization landscape to initialization and hyperparameters [G]. The original 3DGS paper introduced a coarse-to-fine densification schedule, but subsequent research has expanded this into complex multi-stage frameworks for specific challenges such as pose estimation [15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md], mesh alignment [7448-training-objective-and-densification.md], and volumetric data [12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md] [G]. These methods are widely used in dynamic scene reconstruction and feed-forward 3DGS architectures where stability and convergence speed are critical [S1, unofficial][S2].
