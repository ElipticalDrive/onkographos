# Geometry and Surface Priors

Also called: Geometric Constraints, Surface Alignment, Depth Priors

## Overview
Geometry and Surface Priors address the fundamental limitation of vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS): the production of "floating" volumetric ellipsoids that lack an explicit, watertight geometric boundary [S1, 9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md]. By aligning the discrete Gaussian representation with continuous geometric priors (such as SDFs, depth maps, or explicit meshes), these methods enforce positional and normal consistency to enable accurate mesh extraction, physically-based rendering, and robust initialization [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely solely on photometric loss or implicit constraints, this family explicitly integrates geometric supervision into the training or post-processing pipeline to ensure the Gaussians conform to physical surfaces [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of aligning the discrete Gaussian representation with a continuous geometric prior. The core mechanism involves adding geometric loss terms to the standard photometric rendering loss $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$. These losses typically enforce two properties:
1.  **Positional Consistency:** Gaussian centers $\mathbf{c}_i$ are encouraged to lie on the zero-level set of a distance function (SDF or UDF) or align with metric depth estimates [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md].
2.  **Normal Consistency:** The orientation of the Gaussians (derived from their covariance matrix or rotation quaternion) is aligned with the surface normal estimated from the geometric prior [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md].

The total loss function generally takes the form:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{geo} \mathcal{L}_{geo} + \lambda_{norm} \mathcal{L}_{norm} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ is the geometric consistency loss and $\mathcal{L}_{norm}$ is the normal consistency loss [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md]. The weights $\lambda_{geo}$ and $\lambda_{norm}$ are hyperparameters that balance photometric fidelity against geometric accuracy [9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md].

## Subtopics

### [GaussianPro](9167-gaussianpro.md)
Mitigates reconstruction artifacts and improves geometric fidelity by introducing a progressive propagation strategy. It utilizes propagated depth and normal maps from neighboring views to guide the growth and adjustment of Gaussians, explicitly enforcing normal consistency and planar constraints.

### [NeuSG](9172-neusg.md)
Integrates the NeuS implicit surface reconstruction method into the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. It directly couples a NeuS-style Signed Distance Function (SDF) with a Gaussian rasterizer to transfer explicit surface properties to the discrete Gaussian primitives.

### [Monocular Depth Scale Alignment](9183-monocular-depth-scale-alignment.md)
A post-optimization step that resolves the up-to-scale ambiguity inherent in multi-view reconstruction by aligning the metric scale of optimized 3D Gaussians with metric-scale monocular depth estimates. It operates after standard optimization, fixing Gaussian parameters and optimizing only a global scale factor.

### [Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting with Depth Priors](9185-sparse-view-3d-gaussian-splatting-with-depth-priors.md)
Addresses reconstruction from sparse-view inputs by explicitly integrating depth priors estimated from pre-trained 2D monocular depth estimation networks to supervise the Gaussian distribution, rather than relying solely on implicit neural fields.

### [GSDF](9205-gsdf.md)
Aligns the discrete 3D Gaussian representation with a continuous Signed Distance Function (SDF) prior to enhance geometry reconstruction quality. It explicitly transfers surface properties from a neural SDF to the Gaussians.

### [Surface and Geometry Alignment](9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md)
The overarching family definition focusing on the alignment of discrete Gaussians with continuous priors (SDFs, depth, meshes) to enforce positional and normal consistency for mesh extraction.

### [Joint 2D and 3D Priors](20090-joint-2d-and-3d-priors.md)
Combines 2D diffusion priors (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and 3D diffusion priors (e.g., Zero-1-to-3) to supervise novel view reconstruction. It uses 2D priors for plausible unseen geometry (mitigating the Janus problem) and 3D priors for geometric consistency.

### 3DGSR
- definition: A method that encourages consistency between Signed Distance Function (SDF) and 3DGS to enhance geometry reconstruction quality [S1].

### Gaussian Splatting Surface Refinement
- definition: Using pretrained volume rendering priors (UDF) to push Gaussians toward true surfaces by optimizing along the direction of increasing rendering weights, avoiding off-surface drift and discreteness [S2]. It is a post-training refinement strategy that uses a pretrained volume rendering prior to optimize Gaussian distributions toward true surfaces, particularly effective for open and thin structures [S2].
- step: Integrate the prior into existing Gaussian Splatting variants (e.g., GaussianUDF, QGS) without modifying inference-time resource costs, as the prior network is bypassed during inference [S2].
- comparison: Outperforms handcrafted renderers and closed-surface-tailored 3DGS methods on open and thin structures, where standard Gaussian distributions degrade severely [S2].

## Background
The integration of geometric priors into 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address the "floating" artifact issue inherent in the original 3DGS formulation, where Gaussians represent volume rather than surface [S1, 9267-surface-and-geometry-alignment.md]. Early approaches like GSDF [S1] and 3DGSR [S1] focused on aligning Gaussians with SDFs to improve reconstruction quality. Subsequent methods such as NeuSG [S1] incorporated implicit neural representations (NeuS) to provide explicit surface normals and signed distances. Other lines of work, such as Monocular Depth Scale Alignment [S1, 9183-monocular-depth-scale-alignment.md] and Sparse-view methods [S1], leveraged external monocular depth estimators to resolve scale ambiguities and provide geometric constraints in data-sparse scenarios. More recently, diffusion-based priors (Joint 2D and 3D Priors) have been introduced to leverage large-scale generative models for geometric regularization [20090-joint-2d-and-3d-priors.md]. Volume rendering priors, such as those in Gaussian Splatting Surface Refinement, have also been developed to refine open and thin structures by pushing Gaussians toward true surfaces predicted by unsigned distance functions [S2].
