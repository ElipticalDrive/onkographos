# Gaussian Piling

Also called: Focus Gaussian, GaussianPile, Slice-based Volumetric Reconstruction

## Overview
Gaussian Piling is a slice-based volumetric reconstruction method that unifies 3D Gaussian Splatting with an imaging system-aware focus model to compress data aggressively while preserving internal volumetric structure [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which discards internal volumetric information and produces incoherent 3D structures with floating artifacts for slice-based modalities, Gaussian Piling models the finite focal zone to preserve high-frequency internal volumetric detail [S1, unofficial]. It achieves this by enforcing 3D-to-2D consistency through axial re-parameterization and opacity modulation, preventing ghosting artifacts across slices [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $M$ 3D Gaussian primitives defined by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ (derived from scale $\mathbf{S}$ and rotation $\mathbf{R}$), opacity $\alpha$, and color/radiance parameters. The imaging system's axial resolution $\sigma_z$ is required.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered 2D slice images $I(p)$ and a differentiable 3D voxel volume $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian primitive $i$ stores:
*   Position $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (world space).
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion), combined to form $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^\top \mathbf{R}_i^\top$.
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$.
*   Color/Radiance: Unlike standard 3DGS, Gaussian Piling discards spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients to reduce parameters by ~40% and improve geometric fidelity [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_z$: Axial resolution term (slice thickness). Estimated via scanning step $\delta_z$ satisfying Nyquist criterion ($\sigma_z \approx \delta_z$) if experimental calibration is unavailable [S1, unofficial].
*   $\tau$: Opacity minimal threshold for culling primitives. Default: 0.02 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Weight balancing perceptual (SSIM) and pixel-wise (L1) fidelity. Default: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   Learning Rates: Position 0.0006, Opacity 0.02, Scale 0.002, Rotation 0.001. Exponentially decay to 0.1 of initial values [S1, unofficial].
*   $M$: Initial number of Gaussians. Default: 1,000,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   Gradient threshold for adaptive control: 0.00005 [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The rendering pipeline consists of three stages: Scan, Axial Re-parameterization & Opacity Modulation, and Screen-space Projection & Additive Rasterization [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Scan:** Project 3D Gaussians onto slices at different depths. The analysis is conducted in the camera coordinate system (subscript $c$). The imaging system's Point Spread Function (PSF) is modeled as a spatially invariant 3D Gaussian with standard deviations $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$. The sensitivity map $h$ along the elevational direction is defined as:
    $$ h(-z_c) = \exp\left(-\frac{z_c^2}{2\sigma_z^2}\right) $$
    where $z_c$ is the coordinate in the camera system [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Axial Re-parameterization:** To encode finite slice thickness, the axial resolution term is injected into the inverse covariance matrix in camera coordinates. This shrinks the axial support without disturbing the lateral structure, producing an "Effective Gaussian" $\mathcal{G}_e \sim \mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_e, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e)$:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e^{-1} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} + \frac{\mathbf{e}_3 \mathbf{e}_3^\top}{\sigma_z^2} $$
    $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_e = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_c $$
    where $\mathbf{e}_3 = [0, 0, 1]^\top$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Opacity Modulation:** To suppress contributions from out-of-focus primitives and prevent ghosting artifacts, opacity is modulated based on the change in Mahalanobis distance:
    $$ \text{opacity}_r = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_c^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_c^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_c - \boldsymbol{\mu}_e^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_e)\right) $$
    Primitives with opacity below threshold $\tau$ are culled [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Screen-space Projection:** Compute the 2D marginal distribution of the Effective Gaussian with respect to the $(x_c, y_c)$ plane:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d} = \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_e[0,0] & \Sigma_e[1,0] \\ \Sigma_e[1,0] & \Sigma_e[1,1] \end{bmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol{\mu}_{2d} = [\mu_e[0], \mu_e[1]]^\top $$
    The final rendered intensity $\tilde{\alpha}$ is normalized using the determinant of the 2D covariance to preserve total energy:
    $$ \tilde{\alpha} = \alpha \cdot \text{opacity}_r / \sqrt{\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})} $$
    [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Additive Rasterization:** Pixel intensity is the sum of overlapping Gaussian contributions (no occlusion):
    $$ I(p) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}(p)} \tilde{\alpha}_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}_i^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d,i}^{-1} \mathbf{d}_i\right) $$
    where $\mathcal{N}(p)$ is the set of Gaussians overlapping pixel $p$, and $\mathbf{d}_i = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{2d,i} - p$ [S1, unofficial].

**Differentiable Voxelization:**
To enable 3D visualization, a differentiable voxelizer partitions 3D space into uniform $8^3$ tiles. Gaussians are culled per tile if their 3-$\sigma$ ellipsoid does not intersect the tile. Voxel intensity is computed by summing world-space Gaussians:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^M g_i^3(\mathbf{x}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i) $$
where $g_i^3$ is the original Gaussian primitive in world coordinates [S1, unofficial].

**Optimization:**
The loss function is a weighted combination of L1 and D-SSIM losses:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{ssim} $$
Gradients are propagated through the focus-aware pipeline. The gradient w.r.t. opacity $\alpha$ is:
$$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \tilde{\alpha}} \cdot \frac{\text{opacity}_r}{\sqrt{\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})}} $$
The gradient w.r.t. effective covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_e$ couples the determinant term and the conic term [S1, unofficial].

            intensity = 0.0
            for g in overlapping:
                mu_c = g['mu_c']
                Sigma_c = g['Sigma_c']
                alpha = g['alpha']
                
                # 1. Axial Re-parameterization
                # e3 = [0, 0, 1]
                # Sigma_e_inv = Sigma_c_inv + (e3 @ e3.T) / sigma_z^2
                Sigma_c_inv = np.linalg.inv(Sigma_c)
                e3 = np.array([0, 0, 1])
                Sigma_e_inv = Sigma_c_inv + (np.outer(e3, e3)) / (sigma_z ** 2)
                Sigma_e = np.linalg.inv(Sigma_e_inv)
                
                # mu_e = Sigma_e @ Sigma_c_inv @ mu_c
                mu_e = Sigma_e @ (Sigma_c_inv @ mu_c)
                
                # 2. Opacity Modulation
                # opacity_r = exp(-0.5 * (mu_c^T Sigma_c_inv mu_c - mu_e^T Sigma_e_inv mu_e))
                mahal_c = mu_c @ Sigma_c_inv @ mu_c
                mahal_e = mu_e @ Sigma_e_inv @ mu_e
                opacity_r = np.exp(-0.5 * (mahal_c - mahal_e))
                
                # Cull if below threshold
                if opacity_r < 0.02:
                    continue
                    
                # 3. Screen-space Projection
                # Sigma_2d is top-left 2x2 of Sigma_e
                Sigma_2d = Sigma_e[:2, :2]
                mu_2d = mu_e[:2]
                
                # Det check for stability
                det_Sigma_2d = np.linalg.det(Sigma_2d)
                if det_Sigma_2d <= 1e-8:
                    continue
                    
                # 4. Additive Rasterization
                # d_i = mu_2d - pixel_pos
                d_i = mu_2d - pixel_pos
                Sigma_2d_inv = np.linalg.inv(Sigma_2d)
                
                # Exponent term
                exponent = -0.5 * (d_i @ Sigma_2d_inv @ d_i)
                
                # Normalized intensity
                # alpha_tilde = alpha * opacity_r / sqrt(det(Sigma_2d))
                alpha_tilde = alpha * opacity_r / np.sqrt(det_Sigma_2d)
                
                intensity += alpha_tilde * np.exp(exponent)
                
            image[y, x] = intensity
            
    return image

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Ghosting Artifacts:** Without opacity modulation, primitives far from the focal plane contribute to adjacent slices, causing floating artifacts. The exponential attenuation of $\text{opacity}_r$ prevents this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The determinant term $\sqrt{\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})}$ in the normalization of $\tilde{\alpha}$ ensures brightness adapts to footprint size. Numerical instability occurs if $\det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{2d})$ is near zero; a small epsilon guard is required [S1, unofficial].
*   **SH Discard:** Standard 3DGS uses SH for view-dependent color. Gaussian Piling discards SH, which reduces parameters but assumes isotropic or fixed lighting conditions suitable for slice imaging [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than NeRF-based approaches, delivering high-quality results in as few as 3 minutes (up to 11× faster) [S1, unofficial]. The differentiable voxelizer achieves > 100 FPS for real-time query performance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The method achieves a 16× reduction in storage over voxel-based grids while retaining full 2D rendering fidelity and volumetric consistency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves high-frequency internal volumetric detail; enforces 3D-to-2D consistency; compact encoding via Morton sorting and entropy compression (LZMA) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Discards spherical harmonics, limiting view-dependent color modeling compared to standard 3DGS; relies on accurate $\sigma_z$ estimation (though Nyquist approximation is robust) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Slice-based volumetric imaging (microscopy, ultrasound) where internal structure preservation and compression are critical.
*   **When not to use:** Surface rendering from multi-view images where view-dependent appearance is paramount.

## Background
Gaussian Piling (GaussianPile) was introduced to bridge the gap between the high compression ratio of Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) and the fast fitting of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for slice-based volumetric imaging [S1, unofficial]. Standard 3DGS is designed for surface appearance from multi-view images and discards internal volumetric information, making it unsuitable for scientific and medical imaging [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed by researchers at Tsinghua University and published in 2026 [S1, unofficial]. It utilizes a unified sparse Gaussian splatting framework that incorporates an imaging system-aware focus model [S1, unofficial].
