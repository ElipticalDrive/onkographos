# Spherical Harmonics Optimization

Also called: SH Optimization, View-Dependent Color Optimization

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Optimization encompasses methods that modify, compress, regularize, or replace the standard SH representation of view-dependent color in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to improve rendering quality, training stability, or computational efficiency. It addresses the limitations of fixed-degree SH coefficients, which can lead to overfitting in sparse views, high memory consumption, or inability to capture complex lighting. It differs from its siblings by focusing specifically on the *optimization* and *parameterization* of the directional radiance function $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$, rather than just the geometric representation or the camera tracking mechanism.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation](9467-first-order-spherical-harmonics-distillation.md)
Reduces the SH degree from third-order to first-order to decrease parameter count and computation cost. It differs from standard degree reduction by employing a distillation strategy where a student model with first-order SH approximates the directional radiance of a pretrained third-order teacher model, rather than simply truncating coefficients. This approach minimizes quality loss, achieving a 61% reduction in parameters and 52% reduction in computation with only a 0.48 dB PSNR drop. Choose this when memory and compute are constrained but high-frequency view-dependent details from a high-quality teacher are needed.

### [Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation](10077-explicit-camera-jacobian-derivation.md)
Derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3DGS primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking. It avoids the computational overhead of automatic differentiation and ensures minimal parameterization using Lie algebra. Unlike prior ray-intersection methods, this provides the first analytical Jacobians for EWA splatting and 3DGS. Choose this for applications requiring precise, differentiable camera pose estimation during optimization.

### [Efficient Multi-View Training for 3D Gaussian Splatting](13982-efficient-multi-view-training-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Enables stable optimization by training on multiple images per iteration, addressing the high variance of gradients inherent in the standard single-view approach. It uses thread-efficient partial rendering to gather pixels from multiple views without idle thread waste. Choose this when training stability and convergence speed are critical, and hardware allows for parallel multi-view rasterization.

### [Spherical Harmonics Vector Quantization](14181-spherical-harmonics-vector-quantization.md)
A compression technique that replaces per-splat SH coefficients with indices pointing to a shared palette of distinct SH vectors. It differs from First-order Distillation by retaining full SH basis functions (rather than reducing to first-order) and from Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation by focusing on storage compression. Choose this when memory bandwidth or storage size is the primary bottleneck and view-dependent specular effects must be preserved better than simple color quantization allows.

### [Closed-Form Scene Appearance Estimation](16750-closed-form-scene-appearance-estimation.md)
Estimates SH coefficients for mesh vertices by solving a global weighted least-squares problem that jointly fits RGB observations from all calibrated views. It distinguishes itself by providing an analytical closed-form solution, utilizing adaptive regularization to constrain SH bands based on directional support. Choose this for mesh-based scene representation where a global, non-iterative solution for appearance is preferred.

### [Adaptive SH Regularization](16786-adaptive-sh-regularization.md)
Stabilizes SH coefficient estimation in sparse or unevenly observed scenes by suppressing higher-order SH components that lack sufficient directional support. It prevents overfitting to input views and improves generalization to unseen viewpoints. Choose this when dealing with sparse input views to avoid noisy, overfitted colors.

### [Geometry-Guided Appearance Modeling](20876-geometry-guided-appearance-modeling.md)
Predicts view-dependent colors by conditioning the appearance on explicit geometric cues, such as surface normals and learned geometric features derived from a Signed Distance Field (SDF). It solves the limitation of standard SH, which often fails to capture fine-grained geometric details and high-frequency reflections. Unlike standard SH optimization, GAM replaces or augments SH with a geometry-conditioned Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). Choose this when geometric fidelity is paramount and the scene has well-defined surfaces.

### SH Degree Zero Optimization
**Definition:** Setting the maximum degree of Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients to 0 to remove superfluous operations and improve training speed, as higher-degree SH coefficients for color remain unchanged during training [S1, unofficial].
**Step:** Modify the model code to delete operations relative to the max degree in the model and set the max degree parameter to 0 [S1, unofficial].
**Pitfall:** In the original 3D Gaussian Splatting model, features represented by SH coefficients with degree > 0 are unchanged during all training, making them useless [S1, unofficial].
**Number:** Directly changing the max degree to 0 yields a 16.25% improvement compared to the default. With specific changing code, it yields a 16.57% improvement but increases CPU-GPU process time [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation uses third-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) to encode view-dependent color. While effective, this representation is parameter-heavy and can suffer from overfitting in sparse-view scenarios or artifacts in complex lighting. Recent research has explored various optimization strategies to mitigate these issues, including distillation from higher-order models, analytical gradient derivation for better tracking, multi-view training for stability, and compression via vector quantization. Additionally, methods have emerged to regularize SH coefficients based on directional support or replace them entirely with geometry-guided neural fields for improved geometric consistency.
