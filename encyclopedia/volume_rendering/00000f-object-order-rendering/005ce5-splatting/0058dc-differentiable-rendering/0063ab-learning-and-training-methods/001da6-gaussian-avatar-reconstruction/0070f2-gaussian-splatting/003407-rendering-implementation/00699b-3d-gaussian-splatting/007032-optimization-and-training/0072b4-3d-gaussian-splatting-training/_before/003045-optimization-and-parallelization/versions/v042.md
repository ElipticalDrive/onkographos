# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Per-Splat Backpropagation, Mask Gradient Computation

## Overview
This family encompasses methods for accelerating the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and extending it to large-scale or distributed settings. It addresses the computational bottlenecks of the differentiable rasterization core, specifically the high memory bandwidth required for gradient accumulation and the single-GPU memory limits for scenes with billions of primitives [6233-optimization-and-parallelization]. It is distinguished from standard optimization by focusing on parallel execution strategies (per-splat vs. per-pixel backpropagation) and distributed data partitioning, rather than just hyperparameter tuning.

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
Computes gradients for probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ applied to Gaussians. It distinguishes itself by explicitly backpropagating through the mask-modulated alpha-blending recurrence, accounting for both foreground color accumulation and background transmittance changes.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The core iterative loop for 3DGS. It is distinguished by its nested axis/slice loop structure for gradient accumulation and periodic refinement phases (pruning, cloning, splitting) that dynamically adapt the Gaussian count.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
Replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It accelerates training by 4–5× by eliminating atomic contention in global memory, iterating over Gaussians to update the pixels they cover.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs. Unlike single-machine streaming methods, it enables training on scenes with billions of primitives by leveraging inter-GPU communication for partial rendering aggregation.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Provides concrete implementation parameters, including densification schedules, half-precision rasterization, and specific variant algorithms (e.g., Denoise-GS, LS-Gaussian) to bridge theory and practice.

## Background
The optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting has evolved from the original differentiable rasterization implementation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization] to address scalability and training efficiency. Early work focused on the basic training pipeline [5325-training-pipeline], identifying bottlenecks in gradient computation and memory usage. Subsequent research introduced per-splat backpropagation to reduce atomic contention [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation] and distributed frameworks like Grendel-GS to handle large-scale scenes [7349-grendel-gs]. Implementation details such as half-precision support [9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details] and specialized variants for sparse views or inverse rendering have further refined the training process.
