# Pruning and Sparsification

Also called: Gaussian Sparsification, Primitive Reduction

## Overview
Pruning and Sparsification refers to the family of strategies that reduce the cardinality of the 3D Gaussian primitive set $\mathcal{G}$ by removing redundant, insignificant, or high-uncertainty primitives, thereby improving rendering efficiency and reducing memory footprint without degrading visual fidelity [G]. It sets itself apart from densification methods (e.g., [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md)) by focusing on removal rather than addition, and from static compression techniques by often employing dynamic, training-aware, or uncertainty-based criteria to preserve structural integrity [G].

## Shared principles
This family inherits the shared principles of [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md).

## Subtopics

### [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md)
[Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md) dynamically balances addition and removal to maintain an optimal representation budget, rather than simply removing primitives. It sets itself apart from static pruning methods by using gradient magnitude and opacity thresholds to trigger splitting, cloning, or pruning based on local scene complexity. Choose it when training requires automatic adjustment of primitive density to prevent under-reconstruction or inefficient memory usage.

### [Pruning and Opacity Management](9314-pruning-and-opacity-management.md)
[Pruning and Opacity Management](9314-pruning-and-opacity-management.md) focuses on opacity thresholds, transmittance consistency during cloning, and fixed-schedule resets rather than adaptive uncertainty bounds or significance scores derived from view counts. It sets itself apart by enforcing strict opacity decay and reset schedules. Choose it to solve the problem of unbounded Gaussian growth and the persistence of "floaters" or boundary artifacts.

### [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md)
[Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md) removes primitives based on their accumulated contribution to the rendered image, rather than solely on opacity or scale. It sets itself apart from [Pruning and Opacity Management](9314-pruning-and-opacity-management.md) by sparing Gaussians with low opacity but high alpha-blending significance. Choose it to improve the trade-off between reconstruction quality and scene compactness, especially in scenes with large, low-opacity primitives critical for transmittance.

### [Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md)
[Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md) is a post-hoc method for compressing pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing Gaussians with high uncertainty in their spatial parameters. It sets itself apart from heuristic methods that rely on opacity or gradient magnitude by quantifying the entropy of the posterior distribution over each Gaussian's position and scale using Fisher Information matrices. Choose it when aggressive compression (up to 90%) is required while preserving fine details and foreground information.

### [TangoGS](10180-tangogs.md)
[TangoGS](10180-tangogs.md) is a density control strategy that automatically scales the number of Gaussian primitives to match the complexity of the input capture without per-scene tuning. It sets itself apart from sibling methods that rely on fixed thresholds or per-capture calibration by combining a capture-derived "learning allowance" with training-based quality feedback (PSNR-driven pruning). Choose it to achieve comparable or better PSNR with significantly fewer Gaussians across diverse scene scales.

### [Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning](12338-depth-based-gaussian-pruning.md)
[Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning](12338-depth-based-gaussian-pruning.md) varies the opacity pruning threshold based on the relative depth (z-axis value) of 3D Gaussians to preserve points at the far plane in forward-facing scenes. It sets itself apart from standard fixed-threshold pruning by applying a lower threshold (making pruning harder) to distant primitives. Choose it for forward-facing datasets (e.g., DeblurNeRF) to compensate for the natural sparsity of Gaussians at the back of the scene.

### [Adaptive Pruning](19972-adaptive-pruning.md)
[Adaptive Pruning](19972-adaptive-pruning.md) is a sparsification technique used in triangle-based splatting methods to reduce floaters and prevent overfitting by removing primitives that contribute negligibly to the rendered image. It sets itself apart from Gaussian Splatting methods that rely on opacity thresholds or gradient magnitudes by operating on the volume rendering blending weight ($T \cdot o$) of individual triangles. Choose it to eliminate triangles that explain only small amounts of data within a single view, thereby improving the generalization of the radiance field representation.

### Hierarchy Compaction
- [S2] A process to sparsify the generated tree structure to avoid parent-child settings where the parent's size is only marginally larger than the children's, ensuring proper optimization.
- [S2] Mark all leaf nodes (output of 3DGS optimization) as relevant.
- [S2] Find the union of cuts in the tree over all training views for the minimum target granularity $\tau_{min} = 3$ pixels.
- [S2] Find the bottom-most nodes in this union cut. These are the highest-detail relevant nodes [S2]. Remove all nodes between them and already marked nodes [S2].
- [S2] Raise the target granularity by 2x and repeat the process until $\tau_{max}$ (half the image's resolution) is reached.
- [S2] If a node has $K$ children, the blended weight $\alpha'$ generalizes to: $\alpha' = \frac{1}{1 - (1 - \alpha_p) \frac{1}{K}}$ [S2].
- [S2] Minimum target granularity $\tau_{min}$: Set to 3 pixels, corresponding to the minimum extent of a primitive 3DGS primitive due to low-pass filtering [S2].

## Background
The concept of pruning and sparsification in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged alongside the original 3D-GS paper [Parent], which introduced opacity-based pruning as a basic mechanism to remove "floaters" and insignificant primitives during training [G]. Subsequent research has expanded this into a diverse family of techniques [G]. [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md) addressed the limitations of opacity-only pruning by considering alpha-blending contributions. [Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md) introduced a post-hoc compression approach using Fisher Information matrices to quantify geometric uncertainty, enabling high compression ratios. [TangoGS](10180-tangogs.md) and other methods like GaussianSPA [S1, unofficial] and Hierarchy Compaction [S2] further refined sparsification by incorporating scene complexity metrics, uncertainty-based scores, and hierarchical tree structures to optimize the trade-off between rendering speed, memory usage, and visual quality. These methods are primarily used in the optimization and training phases of 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines to manage the exponential growth of primitives and ensure efficient real-time rendering [G].
