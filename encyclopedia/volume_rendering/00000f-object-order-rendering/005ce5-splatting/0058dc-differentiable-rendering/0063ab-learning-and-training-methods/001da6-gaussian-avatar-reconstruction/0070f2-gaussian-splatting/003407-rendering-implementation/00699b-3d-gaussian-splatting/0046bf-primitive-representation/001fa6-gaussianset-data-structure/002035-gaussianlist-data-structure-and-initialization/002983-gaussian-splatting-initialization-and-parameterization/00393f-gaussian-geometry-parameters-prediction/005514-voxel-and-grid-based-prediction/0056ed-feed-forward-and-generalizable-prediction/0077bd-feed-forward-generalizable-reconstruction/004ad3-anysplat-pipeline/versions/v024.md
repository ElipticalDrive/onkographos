# AnySplat Pipeline

Also called: Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Unconstrained Views

## Overview
AnySplat is a feed-forward method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reconstructs scenes from uncalibrated, unconstrained image collections without per-scene optimization [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods by jointly predicting Gaussian primitives, depth maps, and camera poses in a single forward pass, using a differentiable voxelization module to bridge the gap between pixel-wise predictions and a renderable 3D structure [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of $K$ uncalibrated RGB images $I \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times 3 \times H \times W}$, typically resized to $448 \times 448$ [S1, unofficial]. Camera poses are not required.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ (defined by mean $\mu$, scale $s$, rotation $r$, opacity $\alpha$, and color $c$), predicted depth maps $D$, and predicted camera poses $p$ (intrinsic and extrinsic matrices) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The method constructs "pixel-wise 3D Gaussians" from the decoder outputs. These are then processed by a **Differentiable Voxelization** module to produce "pre-voxel 3D Gaussians" suitable for rasterization [S1, unofficial]. The specific internal layout of the voxelization structure is not detailed in the sources [G].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Feature Encoding:** Input images are passed through a transformer-based geometry encoder to extract multi-scale features [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Parameter Prediction:** Three decoder heads ($F_G, F_D, F_C$) process the encoded features:
    *   $F_G$ predicts Gaussian parameters: mean $\mu$, scale $\sigma$ (interpreted as scale $s$), rotation $r$, and color $c$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   $F_D$ predicts a dense depth map $D$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   $F_C$ predicts camera poses $p$ (intrinsic and extrinsic matrices) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Primitive Construction:** The predicted parameters are assembled into a set of pixel-wise 3D Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Differentiable Voxelization:** The pixel-wise Gaussians are voxelized into a structured set of pre-voxel 3D Gaussians using the proposed Differentiable Voxelization module [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Rendering:** The voxelized Gaussians are rendered to generate novel-view images and depth maps [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **RGB Loss:** The rendered images are compared against ground truth images using an RGB loss (e.g., L1 or L2) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Geometry Loss:** The rendered depth maps, the decoded depth $D$, and the predicted poses $p$ are used to compute geometry losses [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Pseudo-Geometry Supervision:** The geometry losses are supervised by pseudo-geometry priors obtained from a pretrained VGGT model [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Input Resolution:** $448 \times 448$ pixels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size:** 1 (as per example code) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Number of Views ($K$):** Variable, determined by input [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Uncalibrated Input:** The method is designed to handle uncalibrated views, but accuracy depends on the quality of the pseudo-geometry priors from VGGT [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxelization Artifacts:** The quality of the final rendering is heavily dependent on the Differentiable Voxelization module. Issues in voxelization may lead to gaps or artifacts in the reconstructed scene [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pose Ambiguity:** Since poses are predicted, errors in pose estimation can propagate to the Gaussian positions and rendering quality [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [SubSplat](13524-subsplat.md)
A high-resolution variant using a Sub-pixel Gaussian Reparameterizer to increase density without increasing network latency.

### [Deformable Radial Kernel (DRK)](13699-deformable-radial-kernel-drk.md)
A variant replacing fixed radial Gaussians with flexible planar kernels for better geometric fidelity.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is $O(1)$ with respect to scene complexity, as it is a feed-forward process. The dominant cost is the transformer encoder and decoder heads [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of predicted Gaussians and the voxel grid resolution. Not given in the sources [G].
*   **Performance:** The method enables real-time novel-view synthesis from unconstrained images. Specific FPS or memory metrics are not provided in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles uncalibrated inputs; no per-scene optimization required; joint prediction of geometry and appearance.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on pseudo-geometry priors from VGGT, which may introduce biases; voxelization step may limit fidelity compared to direct rasterization [S1, unofficial].

## Background
AnySplat was introduced by InternRobotics as a feed-forward approach to 3D Gaussian Splatting from unconstrained views [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of generalizable reconstruction by predicting Gaussian parameters directly from images, extending this to include pose and depth prediction without ground-truth camera information [S1, unofficial].
