# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Regularization, Frequency Regularization

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevent over-reconstruction, floating artifacts, and geometric instability by imposing structural, frequency, or depth-based priors on the Gaussian primitives [S1, S2]. Unlike standard photometric optimization which only minimizes pixel-wise color differences, these methods explicitly constrain the shape, orientation, or spectral content of the Gaussians to ensure they represent coherent surfaces rather than unstructured density clouds [S1]. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on geometric fidelity and artifact reduction rather than physical plausibility, and from [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md) by adding explicit regularization terms to the loss function or modifying the parameter update rules [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
The shared mechanism across these methods is the introduction of auxiliary constraints during the optimization loop that penalize deviations from desired geometric or spectral properties. These constraints are typically added to the total loss function $\mathcal{L}_{total}$:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg}
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ represents the specific regularization term (e.g., frequency discrepancy, depth error, or shape distortion) and $\lambda_{reg}$ is a weighting factor [S1, S2]. The gradients from $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ are backpropagated to update specific Gaussian parameters (centers, scales, rotations, or opacities) to enforce the constraint [S1, S2]. Common goals include:
1.  **Surface Coherence:** Encouraging Gaussians to align with local surface normals and flatten into surfels [S1, S2].
2.  **Spectral Consistency:** Ensuring the rendered image's frequency spectrum matches the ground truth to avoid high-frequency artifacts or over-smoothing [S2].
3.  **Depth Alignment:** Constraining the 3D positions of Gaussians to be consistent with monocular or multi-view depth estimates [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve densification. It sets itself apart from pixel-wise L1 loss by increasing gradients in over-reconstructed regions, enabling adaptive densification.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision blurs 2D training images with a scheduled set of 2D Gaussian kernels to stabilize camera alignment and restrict the learned scene's spectrum. It operates directly on the pixel supervision signal in the image domain, unlike Frequency Regularization which works in the frequency domain.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method transforms Gaussian covariance matrices to enforce half-ellipsoid geometry, ensuring primitives model sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space. It sets itself apart by operating directly on the geometric parameterization of splats, cutting Gaussians in half along surface normals to prevent "ghosting" artifacts.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
This strategy prevents detail loss by constraining geometry updates to spatial reshaping (movement of centers) rather than deforming Gaussian shapes. It sets itself apart by freezing scale and rotation parameters and rendering two distinct depth maps to independently update center positions and opacities.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It sets itself apart by dynamically selecting the most suitable geometric representation for each scale based on consistency across views, leveraging low-frequency components for coarse geometry and high-resolution voxels for details.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry Regularization improves surface coherence and reduces floating artifacts by constraining the geometric shape of Gaussian primitives. It sets itself apart from depth supervision by enforcing structural alignment (normal consistency and flattening) rather than just depth value matching.

### Wild-GS
A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry [S1].

### Source Feature Extrapolation
A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images [S2].
- **Step:** Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$ [S2].
- **Formula:** Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$ [S2].
- **Parameter:** $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies [S2].

### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization [S3].
- **Step:** Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians [S3].
- **Step:** Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance [S3].
- **Step:** Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization [S3].

### FreGS
Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of regularizing 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as researchers observed that standard photometric optimization often leads to unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces, resulting in floating artifacts and poor geometric fidelity [S1]. Early works like [S1] highlighted the need for physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) or monocular depth priors, to recover high-quality geometry [S1]. Subsequent methods introduced frequency-based constraints to address over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts, such as FreGS which applies progressive frequency regularization [S4, unofficial]. Depth regularization techniques, including Hard and Soft Depth Regularization, were developed to prevent detail loss and blurred appearance by decoupling the optimization of Gaussian shapes from their positions [S1]. The field continues to evolve with methods like Cross-scale geometric adaptation and Smoothed 2D Supervision, which aim to improve geometric integrity and training stability without relying on complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1, S2][S3].
