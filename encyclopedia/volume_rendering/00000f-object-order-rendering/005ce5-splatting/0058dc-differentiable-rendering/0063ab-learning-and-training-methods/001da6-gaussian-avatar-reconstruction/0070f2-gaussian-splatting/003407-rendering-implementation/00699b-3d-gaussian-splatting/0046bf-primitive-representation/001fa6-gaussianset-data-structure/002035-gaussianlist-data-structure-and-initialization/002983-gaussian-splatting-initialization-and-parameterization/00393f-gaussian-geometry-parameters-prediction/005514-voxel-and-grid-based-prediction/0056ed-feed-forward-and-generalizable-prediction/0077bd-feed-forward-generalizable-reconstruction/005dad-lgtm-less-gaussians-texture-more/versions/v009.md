# LGTM (Less Gaussians, Texture More)

Also called: Feed-Forward Prediction of Textured Gaussians, Feed-Forward Textured Splatting

## Overview
LGTM is a feed-forward framework that predicts compact 2D Gaussian primitives coupled with per-primitive texture maps, decoupling geometric complexity from rendering resolution to enable high-fidelity 4K novel view synthesis without per-scene optimization [S1, unofficial]. It solves the quadratic growth in primitive count as resolution increases in existing feed-forward 3DGS methods, which makes high-resolution synthesis (e.g., 4K) intractable due to memory and compute limits [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3DGS which couples appearance and geometry within each primitive, LGTM decouples them, allowing a compact set of geometric primitives to represent rich texture regions via per-primitive textures [S1, unofficial]. Unlike prior textured Gaussian methods that require per-scene optimization, LGTM is a feed-forward network that generalizes across scenes without per-scene optimization [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
The network $f$ takes a set of input images $\{I^v\}_{v=1}^N$ and their low-resolution counterparts $\{I^v_{low}\}_{v=1}^N$ as input. The output is a set of textured 2D Gaussians:
$$ f : (I^v, I^v_{low}) \rightarrow \{\mu_i, s_i, r_i, c_i, T^i_c, T^i_\alpha\} $$
where $\mu_i$ is the mean, $s_i$ is the scale, $r_i$ is the rotation, $c_i$ are the spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients, $T^i_c$ is the color texture map, and $T^i_\alpha$ is the alpha texture map for primitive $i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Primitive Grid:** The primitive network predicts a grid of $h \times w$ 2DGS primitives, where $h \times w$ corresponds to the resolution of the low-resolution input $I^v_{low}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Per-primitive Texture Maps:** Each primitive $i$ has two texture maps:
    *   Color texture: $T^i_c \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T \times 3}$
    *   Alpha texture: $T^i_\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$
    *   $T$ is the texture resolution, a tunable parameter trading off primitive size and texture size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Color Texture Initialization:** $T^i_c$ is zero-initialized [S1, unofficial].

**Rendering Formula:**
For a ray-splat intersection point $u$ on primitive $i$, the alpha value $a_i(u)$ and color $\hat{c}_i(u, d_i)$ are redefined using textures [S1, unofficial]:
$$ a_i(u) = o_i \cdot T^i_\alpha[u] $$
$$ \hat{c}_i(u, d_i) = T^i_c[u] + SH(c_i, d_i) $$
where $o_i$ is the base opacity, $d_i$ is the view direction, and $T[u]$ denotes bilinear sampling from the texture map at local coordinate $u$. The final pixel color $C$ is computed by alpha-blending contributions from all primitives sorted by depth:
$$ C = \sum_i a_i(u_i) \hat{c}_i(d_i) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - a_j(u_j)) $$
[S1, unofficial]

**Architecture and Steps:**
The architecture consists of two submodules: a primitive network $f_{prim}$ and a texture network $f_{texture}$ [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Primitive Network ($f_{prim}$):**
    *   Input: Low-resolution image $I^v_{low} \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times 3}$.
    *   Process: Passes through a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder-decoder.
    *   Output: Predicts primitive grid parameters $\{\mu_i, s_i, r_i, o_i, c_i\}$ and feature maps $F^{v}_{prim}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Note: The network is trained with high-resolution supervision (rendering and supervising at full resolution $H \times W$ despite low-res input) to prevent holes and ensure anti-aliasing [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Texture Network ($f_{texture}$):**
    *   Input: High-resolution image $I^v$ and primitive features $F^{v}_{prim}$.
    *   Process:
        *   **Patch Features ($F^{v}_{patch}$):** Extracted from $I^v$ via image patchification and convolutions [S1, unofficial].
        *   **Projective Features ($F^{v}_{proj}$):** Computed by mapping source image pixels back to primitive local coordinates using the inverse transformation $M^{-1}_{i,v}$. For each primitive $i$, a projective prior texture is computed:
            $$ T^{c,proj}_i[u] = I^v[x] = I^v[M^{-1}_{i,v}(u)] $$
            Features $F^{v}_{proj}$ are extracted from this projective prior to provide high-frequency appearance details [S1, unofficial].
        *   **Aggregation:** Features $F^{v}_{prim}$, $F^{v}_{patch}$, and $F^{v}_{proj}$ are aggregated to predict per-primitive texture maps $T^i_\alpha$ and $T^i_c$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Training Strategy (Staged):**
    *   **Stage 1:** Pre-train the primitive network $f_{prim}$ in isolation to establish a robust geometric foundation. Loss: Standard photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Stage 2:** Jointly train $f_{prim}$ and $f_{texture}$. Freeze or reduce the learning rate of $f_{prim}$ by 0.1x to maintain geometric stability. Loss: Standard photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) [S1, unofficial].

*   $T$: Resolution of per-primitive texture maps ($T \times T$). Tunable trade-off between primitive count and texture memory [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Memory Constraints:** Standard feed-forward methods fail to train at 2K resolution or higher due to memory constraints (e.g., NoPoSplat fails at 2K/4K) [S1, unofficial]. LGTM mitigates this by decoupling geometry and appearance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Holes in Rendering:** Without high-resolution supervision in Stage 1, predicted Gaussians may result in areas with holes as they are not anti-aliased [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory:** LGTM trains successfully at 2K and 4K resolutions using under 30 GB of memory, whereas NoPoSplat fails to train at 2K or higher due to memory constraints (OOM) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scalability:** LGTM scales efficiently at inference; a 64x pixel increase adds only modest memory and time overhead [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Enables high-fidelity 4K novel view synthesis, a capability previously out of reach for feed-forward methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Decouples geometry and appearance, allowing compact primitives to represent rich textures; feed-forward (no per-scene optimization); scalable to 4K.
*   **Limitations:** Requires a dual-network architecture; relies on high-resolution supervision during training to prevent holes [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-resolution (4K) novel view synthesis is required without per-scene optimization, and memory is a constraint for standard feed-forward methods.
*   **When not to use:** When per-scene optimization is acceptable and lower resolutions are sufficient, or when computational resources for training a large feed-forward network are unavailable.

## Background
LGTM was published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026 by Yixing Lao, Xuyang Bai, Xiaoyang Wu, Nuoyuan Yan, Zixin Luo, Jean-Daniel Nahmias, Yanghai Tsin, Shiwei Li, and Hengshuang Zhao from HKU and Apple [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods, which suffer from quadratic growth in primitive count with resolution, and textured Gaussian methods that require per-scene optimization [S1, unofficial].
