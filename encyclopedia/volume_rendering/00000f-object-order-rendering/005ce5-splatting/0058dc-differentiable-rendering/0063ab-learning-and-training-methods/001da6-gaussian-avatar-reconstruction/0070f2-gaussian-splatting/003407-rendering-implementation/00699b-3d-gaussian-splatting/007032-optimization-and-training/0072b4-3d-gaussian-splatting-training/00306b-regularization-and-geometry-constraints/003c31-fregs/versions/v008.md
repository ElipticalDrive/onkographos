# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS is a regularization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevents overfitting and high-frequency artifacts by enforcing spectral consistency between rendered images and ground truth [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on geometric constraints or gradient stabilization, FreGS operates in the frequency domain to penalize frequency discrepancies, addressing the specific issue of over-reconstruction [S2].

## How it works
FreGS integrates a frequency-aware penalty term into the total training loss. The method assumes that valid scene geometry should not produce high-frequency noise artifacts that exceed the signal content of the ground truth.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Rendered image $I_{render}$, Ground truth image $I_{gt}$, Frequency threshold or weighting schedule.
- **Outputs:** Gradient updates for Gaussian parameters ($\mu, \Sigma, \alpha, c$) modified by the regularization term.

**Data Structures:**
- Frequency domain representations of images (e.g., via Fast Fourier Transform).
- Loss accumulator for $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$.

**Steps:**
1. **Render:** Generate the current frame $I_{render}$ from the 3D Gaussian primitives.
2. **Transform:** Compute the frequency spectrum of both $I_{render}$ and $I_{gt}$. Let $\mathcal{F}(I)$ denote the Fourier transform.
3. **Compute Discrepancy:** Calculate the frequency discrepancy. While the exact formula is not fully detailed in the provided notes, it typically involves the difference between the magnitude spectra or a high-pass filtered residual.
4. **Regularize:** Apply progressive frequency regularization. This implies that the regularization strength or the frequency bands considered may evolve during training to guide the optimization from coarse to fine details [S1, unofficial].
5. **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients of the frequency loss with respect to the Gaussian parameters and update them.

**Formula:**
The total loss is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{freq} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ represents the frequency discrepancy penalty [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Weighting coefficient for the frequency regularization term [G].

**Edge Cases:**
- **Over-smoothing:** Excessive regularization may remove valid high-frequency details.
- **Noise Amplification:** If the ground truth contains noise, the method might penalize valid high-frequency components.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def fregs_regularization(rendered_image, ground_truth_image, lambda_freq):
    # Compute frequency spectra
    F_render = fft2(rendered_image)
    F_gt = fft2(ground_truth_image)
    
    # Compute frequency discrepancy (magnitude difference)
    # Note: Exact formula not specified in sources; assuming magnitude difference
    freq_diff = abs(F_render) - abs(F_gt)
    
    # Calculate loss (e.g., L1 or L2 norm of the difference)
    # Progressive aspect implies lambda_freq or the frequency bands may change over time
    L_freq = sum(abs(freq_diff)) 
    
    # Return gradient contribution
    return lambda_freq * L_freq
```

## Subtopics

### Frequency Regularization
A broader category of methods using spectral constraints. FreGS is a specific implementation focused on 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

### Progressive Frequency Regularization
The strategy of gradually adjusting frequency constraints during training to ensure smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead due to FFT computations per training step.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency domain representations.
- **Strengths:** Effectively reduces high-frequency artifacts and over-reconstruction [S2].
- **Limitations:** May require careful tuning of $\lambda$ to avoid under-reconstruction.
- **When to use:** When training results in noisy, high-frequency artifacts or over-fitted geometry.

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address the problem of over-reconstruction in 3D Gaussian Splatting by focusing on the frequency domain [S2]. It is cited in surveys on 3DGS rasterization and compression methods [S1, unofficial][S2].
