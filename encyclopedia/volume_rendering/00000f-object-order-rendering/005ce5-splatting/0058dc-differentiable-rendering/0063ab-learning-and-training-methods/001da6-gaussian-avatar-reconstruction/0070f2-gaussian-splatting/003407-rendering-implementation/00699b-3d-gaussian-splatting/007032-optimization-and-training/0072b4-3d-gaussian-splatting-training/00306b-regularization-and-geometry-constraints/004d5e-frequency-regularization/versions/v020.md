# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which results in small gradients in over-reconstructed regions and prevents effective densification, FR increases pixel gradients in these regions by explicitly disentangling frequency components, thereby guiding the optimization toward meaningful high-frequency details [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Image dimensions $H, W$.
- **Outputs:** Scalar regularization loss values $d_a$ (amplitude discrepancy) and $d_p$ (phase discrepancy), which are added to the total training loss.

**Data Structures:**
- Complex frequency values $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ are computed per pixel coordinate $(u, v)$ in the frequency domain.
- Amplitude $|F(u, v)|$ and Phase $\angle F(u, v)$ are derived from the real and imaginary components of the Fourier transform.

**Steps:**
1. **Fourier Transform:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for both the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I$. For a pixel at spatial coordinates $(x, y)$ and frequency coordinates $(u, v)$:
   $$F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}$$
   $$\hat{F}(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \hat{I}(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}$$
   where $I(x, y)$ and $\hat{I}(x, y)$ are the pixel values, and $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ are the complex frequency values [S2].

2. **Amplitude and Phase Extraction:** For each frequency component, calculate the amplitude and phase for both images:
   $$|F(u, v)| = \sqrt{\text{Re}(u, v)^2 + \text{Im}(u, v)^2}$$
   $$\angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(u, v)}{\text{Re}(u, v)}\right)$$
   where $\text{Re}(u, v)$ and $\text{Im}(u, v)$ are the real and imaginary components of $F(u, v)$ [S2]. The same is computed for $\hat{F}(u, v)$.

3. **Discrepancy Calculation:** Compute the total amplitude discrepancy $d_a$ and total phase discrepancy $d_p$ using the Euclidean metric over all frequency components:
   $$d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2}$$
   $$d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2}$$
   Note: The summation indices in the source formulas use spatial indices $(x, y)$ but the terms inside depend on frequency $(u, v)$. Conceptually, the sum is over all frequency bins $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, H-1]$ and $v \in [0, W-1]$ [S2].

4. **Loss Integration:** The total frequency regularization loss is typically a weighted sum of $d_a$ and $d_p$. This loss is backpropagated to update Gaussian parameters.

**Parameters:**
- **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer [S2].
- **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
- **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS are retained [S2].
- **Frequency Annealing:** Advanced implementations (FreGS) use an annealing technique with a low-pass filter $H_l$ and dynamic high-pass filter $H_h$ to progressively leverage low-to-high frequencies, achieving coarse-to-fine densification [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Over-reconstruction vs. Well-reconstruction:** L1 loss fails to differentiate between these regions, leading to redundant Gaussians in well-reconstructed areas. FR specifically increases gradients in over-reconstructed regions, enabling adaptive densification only where necessary [S2].
- **Artifact Bias:** Naively adopting amplitude and phase discrepancies without distinguishing between low and high frequencies can bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts and restrict densification. Frequency annealing mitigates this [S2].
- **Precision:** The arctangent function for phase calculation requires handling quadrants correctly (e.g., using `atan2`) to avoid discontinuities at the branch cut [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def frequency_regularization_loss(rendered_image: torch.Tensor, ground_truth: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """
    Computes the frequency regularization loss based on amplitude and phase discrepancies.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (C, H, W). Assuming (H, W, C) for spatial indexing.
        ground_truth: Tensor of shape (H, W, C).
        
    Returns:
        Scalar loss value.
    """
    # Ensure inputs are float32
    rendered_image = rendered_image.float()
    ground_truth = ground_truth.float()
    
    H, W, C = rendered_image.shape
    
    # Move to frequency domain using 2D DFT
    # torch.fft.fft2 expects (..., H, W) or (H, W, ...). 
    # We apply it to each channel independently or stack them.
    # Standard approach: FFT along last two dimensions.
    
    # Compute FFT for rendered and ground truth
    # Shape becomes (H, W, C) with complex values
    F_rendered = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image, dim=(0, 1))
    F_gt = torch.fft.fft2(ground_truth, dim=(0, 1))
    
    # Calculate Amplitude and Phase
    # Amplitude: sqrt(Re^2 + Im^2)
    amp_rendered = torch.abs(F_rendered)
    amp_gt = torch.abs(F_gt)
    
    # Phase: atan2(Im, Re)
    phase_rendered = torch.angle(F_rendered)
    phase_gt = torch.angle(F_gt)
    
    # Calculate Discrepancies
    # d_a: Root Mean Square of amplitude differences
    # d_p: Root Mean Square of phase differences
    
    # Note: The source formula uses sum over x,y of (F(u,v) - F_hat(u,v))^2.
    # In implementation, we sum over all frequency bins (u,v) and channels.
    
    # Amplitude discrepancy
    amp_diff = amp_rendered - amp_gt
    # Mean squared error over H, W, C
    mse_amp = torch.mean(amp_diff ** 2)
    d_a = torch.sqrt(mse_amp)
    
    # Phase discrepancy
    phase_diff = phase_rendered - phase_gt
    mse_phase = torch.mean(phase_diff ** 2)
    d_p = torch.sqrt(mse_phase)
    
    # Total loss (weights can be adjusted, e.g., w_a * d_a + w_p * d_p)
    # Default equal weighting if not specified
    loss = d_a + d_p
    
    return loss
```

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The addition of 2D DFT and inverse operations (if used for annealing) per iteration increases computational overhead compared to standard L1 loss. The DFT is $O(HW \log(HW))$ per channel.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing intermediate frequency representations $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$, adding $O(HW)$ memory per channel per iteration.
- **Measured Performance:** In scenes like 'Bicycle', FR significantly increases pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to the baseline method (equivalent to 3D-GS with L1 loss), enabling better densification before the standard termination iteration (e.g., 15,000 iterations) [S2].
- **Strengths:** Effectively mitigates over-reconstruction, reduces redundant Gaussians in well-reconstructed regions, and improves geometric fidelity by guiding densification toward high-frequency details [S2].
- **Limitations:** Naive application without frequency annealing can bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts and restrict densification [S2].
- **When to use:** When the scene exhibits over-reconstruction artifacts or when standard densification fails to capture fine details due to insufficient gradients in complex regions.
- **When not to use:** In scenarios where computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of frequency domain operations is prohibitive, or if the scene is purely low-frequency and does not benefit from high-frequency regularization.

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced as part of the FreGS method to improve 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization. It addresses the limitations of spatial domain losses (like L1) by operating in the Fourier domain. The method was developed to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification by explicitly disentangling frequency components [S2]. It builds on the shared principles of the Regularization and Geometry Constraints family, specifically the mechanism of Frequency/Spectral Control to penalize high-frequency noise or discrepancies [S1, S2].
