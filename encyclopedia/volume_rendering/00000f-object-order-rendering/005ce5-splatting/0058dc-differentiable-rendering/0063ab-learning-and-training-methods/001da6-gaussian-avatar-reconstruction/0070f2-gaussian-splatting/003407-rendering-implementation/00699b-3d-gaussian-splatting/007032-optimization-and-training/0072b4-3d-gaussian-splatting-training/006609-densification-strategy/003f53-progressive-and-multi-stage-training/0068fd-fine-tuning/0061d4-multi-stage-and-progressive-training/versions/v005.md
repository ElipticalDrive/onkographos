# Multi-stage and Progressive Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Progressive Training, Warm-up, POD Pretraining

## Overview
Multi-stage and Progressive Training decomposes the optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into sequential or incremental phases to resolve conflicting objectives such as stability, geometric accuracy, and compression efficiency, or to handle streaming data. It sets itself apart from single-stage training by freezing or decoupling parameter groups (e.g., geometry vs. material) across stages to prevent destabilization [S1, unofficial], and from static training by integrating adaptive weighting and hierarchical updates for dynamic scene expansion [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares a decomposition strategy where the optimization problem is split into distinct temporal or logical phases. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Sequential Decomposition:** Parameters are optimized in a specific order. Early stages establish a stable baseline (geometry, coarse appearance), while later stages refine specific attributes (material, fine details, deformation) often by freezing earlier parameters [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Incremental Expansion:** In streaming contexts, the Gaussian field grows dynamically. New Gaussians are initialized from new data (e.g., SfM points) and integrated with existing fields using hierarchical weighting to prevent catastrophic forgetting [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Stabilization via Decoupling:** Complex objectives (like deformation in 4D-GS or quantization in compression) are introduced only after a stable static representation is achieved, often via a "warm-up" phase .
4.  **Distillation for Feed-Forward Stability:** In amortized optimization, unstable image-space gradients are replaced by locally refined Gaussian targets generated via short-horizon optimization, which are then distilled back into the predictor [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
A method that decomposes training into a baseline establishment phase and a refinement phase. It prevents the destabilization of the Gaussian distribution during refinement by freezing earlier-stage parameters (e.g., geometry) in later stages.
*   **Geometry Reconstruction (Low-Res to Full-Res):** Used in sparse-view CT. Stage 1 optimizes Gaussians on a coarse $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ grid to minimize projection error $|| \hat{p}[V(\theta_i)] - p ||_2^2$. Stage 2 expands the grid and refines parameters.
*   **Inverse Rendering (GS-IR):** Stage 1 (30,000 iters) optimizes Geometry $T=\{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ and Appearance $A$ (color SH) using $L_{init} = L_c + L_n$ ($L_n$ includes normal TV smoothing, $\lambda_{n-TV}=5.0$). Stage 2 (10,000 iters) freezes $T$ and $A$, optimizing Material $M=\{\mathbf{a}, \rho, m\}$ using $L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light}$ ($\lambda_M=1.0, \lambda_E=0.01$).
*   **Compression (CodecSplat):** Stage 1 trains a feed-forward pipeline without the feature codec to generate codec-free Gaussians. Stage 2 initializes from Stage 1, freezes the backbone, and optimizes codec-related modules to minimize $L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} [ D_{render} + \gamma D_{codec-depth} ] + \beta R$ ($\lambda \in [16, 1024], \gamma=0.1, \beta \in \{10^{-4}, 4\times10^{-4}\}$).
*   **When to choose:** Use when specific attributes (material, compression) conflict with geometry stability, or when quantization bottlenecks cause training instability.
*   **Link:** [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)

### [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)
A method for incrementally optimizing a 3DGS field as new images arrive in a streaming/SLAM setting. It solves the problem of maintaining fidelity during dynamic scene expansion by integrating hierarchical image weighting and adaptive learning rates.
*   **Mechanism:** Phase 1 initializes the field with $N_0=30$ images for $T_{init}=2,000$ iters. Phase 2 processes each new image $n$: updates poses via local BA, initializes new Gaussians from sparse points exceeding a distance threshold, and calculates hierarchical weights $\omega_{ki}$ based on feature match counts $M_{i,j}$. Training iterations $T_I=200$ are allocated to top $N_m=10$ images via $T_{im} = \frac{1 - e^{-\omega_i}}{\sum (1 - e^{-\omega_j})} \times T_I$, with remaining iterations distributed semi-globally. Learning rates are adapted based on progress $t_i$ and overlapping image weights. A Load-Balancing-Loss $L_{load} = \text{std}(G_p)$ prevents abnormal accumulation.
*   **When to choose:** Use for streaming video or SLAM applications where the scene geometry expands dynamically and retraining from scratch is infeasible.
*   **Link:** [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)

### [Warm-up stage](18447-warm-up-stage.md)
A preliminary optimization phase that initializes 3D Gaussians before proceeding to complex objectives like 4D-GS or dynamic rendering. It stabilizes convergence when initial SfM points are absent or insufficient, preventing numeric errors in deformation networks.
*   **Mechanism:** Optimizes standard 3DGS parameters ($\mu, s, q, \alpha, c$) using $\mathcal{L} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{L1} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ without optimizing deformation fields. The resulting stable Gaussians $\Gamma$ serve as initialization for subsequent stages.
*   **When to choose:** Use when training dynamic 3DGS variants (e.g., 4D-GS) or when SfM initialization is poor, to avoid convergence difficulties.
*   **Link:** [Warm-up Stage](18447-warm-up-stage.md)

### [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md)
A distillation-based amortized optimization procedure that stabilizes multi-stage feed-forward Gaussian predictors. It solves training collapse in residual Gaussian prediction by replacing unstable image-space gradients with locally refined Gaussian targets.
*   **Mechanism:** For each stage $i$, the predictor outputs increments $\Delta G_i$. A detached copy $\Delta \hat{G}_i^{(0)}$ is optimized for $K_i$ steps using residual rendering loss $L_{render} = 0.7 L_1 + 0.3 (1 - SSIM)$ against ground truth, with previous-stage Gaussians fixed. The refined target $\Delta \hat{G}_i^{(K_i)}$ is then used to compute a Gaussian-space regression loss $L_{gaussian}$, which is distilled back to the predictor. This isolates the optimization of predicted increments.
*   **When to choose:** Use when training feed-forward Gaussian predictors to prevent collapse due to non-unique inverse mappings and error accumulation.
*   **Link:** [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md)

### Progressive Stage-wise Prediction
A prediction mode where Gaussian primitives are accumulated progressively across multiple stages rather than predicted in a single shot, allowing for controllable trade-offs between reconstruction quality and representation compactness [S1, unofficial].
*   **Details:** On DIV2K, the stage-wise model (patch size $ps=14$) reaches 26.54 dB PSNR with 12,690 Gaussians at Stage 4, compared to the one-shot baseline ($ps=7$) which reaches 26.58 dB PSNR with 14,528 Gaussians. PSNR increases from 21.91 dB (Stage 1) to 26.54 dB (Stage 4), with Gaussian count increasing from 3,658 to 12,690. While final quality is comparable, stage-wise prediction enables explicit control over activated Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to choose:** Use when explicit control over the quality-compactness trade-off is required, allowing adjustment of representation size progressively [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Multi-stage and progressive training strategies in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged to address limitations in single-stage optimization, particularly regarding stability in dynamic scenes, compression efficiency, and streaming applications. Two-stage optimization was introduced to handle conflicting objectives in inverse rendering (GS-IR) and compression (CodecSplat) . Progressive training for streaming data (On-the-Fly GS) was developed to enable incremental updates without full retraining [S1, unofficial]. Warm-up stages were identified as critical for stabilizing 4D-GS training . POD pretraining was proposed to stabilize feed-forward predictors by distilling locally optimized targets [S1, unofficial].
