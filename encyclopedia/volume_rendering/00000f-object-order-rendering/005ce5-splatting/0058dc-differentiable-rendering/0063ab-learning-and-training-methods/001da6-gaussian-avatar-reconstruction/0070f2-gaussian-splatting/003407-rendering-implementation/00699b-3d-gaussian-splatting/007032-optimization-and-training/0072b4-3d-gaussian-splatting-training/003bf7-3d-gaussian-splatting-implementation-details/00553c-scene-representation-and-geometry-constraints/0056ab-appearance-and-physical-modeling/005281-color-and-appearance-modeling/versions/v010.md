# Color and Appearance Modeling

Also called: Appearance Modeling, View-Dependent Color Modeling

## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) addresses the limitations of the base representation, which stores view-dependent color as fixed Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients per Gaussian. These fixed coefficients often fail to capture complex lighting, specularities, or appearance variations across different images (e.g., due to ISP processing, daylight changes, or transient objects), leading to artifacts like floaters or color bleeding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md]. This family of methods modifies how $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is defined, stored, or optimized, ranging from post-processing image transformations to neural field substitutions and factorized illumination models.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
is a plug-and-post appearance modeling method that mitigates floaters and color distortions by transforming rendered images at the image level using 3D consistent features. It decouples global view-dependent appearance from local spatial changes, ensuring 3D consistency while maintaining real-time rendering speeds by discarding the appearance module after optimization.

### [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)
replaces per-Gaussian SH coefficients with a shared grid-based neural field to represent view-dependent color. This significantly reduces parameter count and disk space compared to high-order SH storage, addressing the explosion in memory usage.

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
models outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes. It explicitly separates scene lighting from material properties to accurately capture specular effects.

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
focuses on determining, compressing, or regularizing the view-dependent color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ stored per 3D Gaussian primitive. It solves the problem of efficiently representing complex lighting while managing memory bandwidth and training stability [14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md].

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
handles unstructured, in-the-wild image collections by using a per-image embedding vector and an MLP to modulate Gaussian color and opacity. This enables the disentangling of static scene geometry from dynamic occluders and lighting conditions without supervision.

## Background
The base 3D Gaussian Splatting method [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md] uses Spherical Harmonics (SH) to encode view-dependent color. While efficient, SH has limited capacity to represent high-frequency specularities and is static per Gaussian, making it unsuitable for scenes with significant appearance variations (e.g., changing daylight, transient objects) or complex materials. Subsequent works like DAVIGS , Compact View-dependent Color , 3iGS , and SWAG  were developed to address these specific limitations by introducing dynamic, neural, or factorized appearance models.
