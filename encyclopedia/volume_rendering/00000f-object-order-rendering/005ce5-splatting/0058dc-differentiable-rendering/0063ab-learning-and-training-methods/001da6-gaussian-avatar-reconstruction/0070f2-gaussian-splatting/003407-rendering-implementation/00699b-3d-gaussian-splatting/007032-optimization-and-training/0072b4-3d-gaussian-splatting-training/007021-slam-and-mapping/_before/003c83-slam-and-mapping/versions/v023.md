# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM, Implicit and Explicit Mapping

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to the simultaneous estimation of camera poses and the construction of a 3D scene representation using differentiable rendering. It solves the problem of building a consistent, high-fidelity 3D model from a sequence of images or video streams while tracking the camera's trajectory in real-time or near-real-time. Unlike traditional explicit geometry representations (e.g., point clouds or meshes) which may suffer from misalignment or topological errors, modern 3DGS SLAM methods leverage the differentiability of Gaussian splatting to optimize both geometry and appearance jointly. This family distinguishes itself by offering two primary paradigms: **Explicit Gaussian Splatting**, which uses millions of 3D Gaussians for real-time rendering and fast training, and **Implicit/Hybrid Neural Fields** (NeRF-based), which use neural networks to represent continuous volumetric functions, often providing higher geometric consistency at the cost of slower rendering speeds.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of **differentiable rendering** as the bridge between 3D representation and 2D observations. The general optimization loop involves:
1.  **Representation:** Maintaining a scene model $\mathcal{M}$, which is either a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ or a neural field $f_\theta$.
2.  **Rendering:** Given a camera pose $T$, rendering an image $\hat{I}$ or depth map $\hat{D}$ from the representation.
3.  **Loss Computation:** Comparing the rendered output $\hat{I}$ with the ground truth observation $I$ (and optionally depth $D$) using photometric losses (L1, SSIM) and geometric losses.
4.  **Optimization:** Backpropagating gradients to update either the camera poses, the representation parameters, or both.

Key shared mathematical components include:
*   **Alpha Compositing:** For explicit Gaussians, the color $C$ is computed as:
    $$ C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}} \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) c_i $$
    where $\alpha_i$ is the opacity and $c_i$ is the color of the $i$-th Gaussian [Parent](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md).
*   **Volume Rendering:** For implicit NeRFs, the color is computed by integrating along rays:
    $$ C = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
    where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ and $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ [S2].
*   **Pose Parameterization:** Camera poses are typically parameterized by translation $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation quaternion $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ [S4].

## Subtopics

### [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)
CG-SLAM is a 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM method that enhances reconstruction quality by integrating an uncertainty map derived from rendered depth into the training process. It distinguishes itself from standard 3DGS SLAM by explicitly modeling depth uncertainty to guide densification and optimization, addressing misalignment issues common in explicit geometry representations.
*   **When to choose:** Use when high-fidelity depth consistency is required and standard 3DGS SLAM suffers from floating artifacts or misalignment in textureless regions.
*   **Link:** [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md)

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scenes or frames for the purpose of fusion, editing, or SLAM initialization. They solve the problem of establishing spatial and semantic consistency when direct geometric alignment is ambiguous or unavailable. Unlike sibling methods that focus on the continuous representation of a single scene, mapping strategies specifically address the *inter-scene* or *inter-frame* registration step, ranging from naive pixel identity to dense semantic feature matching.
*   **When to choose:** Use when fusing multiple maps, editing scenes, or initializing SLAM from disjointed captures.
*   **Link:** [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)

### [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md)
Semantic-aware mapping extends geometric correspondence by incorporating semantic labels or features to improve alignment robustness in repetitive or textureless regions.
*   **When to choose:** Use in environments with large uniform surfaces (e.g., white walls) where geometric features are insufficient.
*   **Link:** [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md)

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation is a method for modeling 3D scenes using continuous volumetric functions, typically implemented via two distinct but related approaches: implicit neural fields (mapping coordinates to radiance) and explicit differentiable splatting (using 3D Gaussians). Implicit NeRFs solve the problem of novel view synthesis by learning a 6D function that maps spatial position and viewing direction to color and density, enabling photorealistic rendering from sparse inputs. Explicit NeRF representations, specifically 3D Gaussian Splatting, solve the computational bottleneck of implicit methods by representing the scene as millions of semi-transparent 3D Gaussian blobs, allowing real-time rendering speeds (>100 FPS) and significantly faster training times compared to traditional neural field evaluation. While implicit NeRFs store the scene inside a neural network with low memory footprint, explicit Gaussian Splatting stores it as a set of geometric primitives with higher memory usage but superior rendering performance.
*   **When to choose:** Use Implicit NeRFs for high-quality offline reconstruction with limited memory; use Explicit 3DGS for real-time applications and fast training.
*   **Link:** [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
Combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting. On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), ATE RMSE (m): ORB-SLAM3 0.052, Ours 3.190. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences. ORB-SLAM achieves best ATE, but combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. Unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S1, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
A neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure. Uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization. Employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly. Integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S2].

### MNE-SLAM
The first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange. Uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module. Introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S2].

### HERO-SLAM
A SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings. Uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S2].

### PIN-SLAM
A globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation. Replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure. Mapping alternates with odometry. Mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S2].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
An active mapping system that autonomously selects next-best-views to maximize information gain. Initialization: Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M(c)$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$. Global Policy: Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$. Local Policy: Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$. Action: Move agent to $s^*$. Observation: Obtain new image observation $I_t$. Update Database: Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$. Mapping Optimization: Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$. Stop Check: Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold. Fine Stage: After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M(f)$ with trained coarse model $M_T(c)$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S3, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison. Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$. Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity. Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$. Discard the L channel. Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences. Formula: $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth. Parameters: $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S4].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM). Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration. Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent. Compute EIG for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration. Formula: $P_j = \arg \max_{P_j} \text{tr} \left( \sum_{x_{acq_i} \in P_j} H''[y_{i\_acq}|x_{acq_i}, w^*] \right) H''[w^*|D_{train}]^{-1}$ [S5].

## Background
The integration of SLAM with 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a natural extension of the original 3DGS paper [Parent](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md), which demonstrated real-time rendering capabilities. Traditional SLAM systems relied on explicit point clouds or implicit NeRFs. NeRF-based SLAM (e.g., [PIN-SLAM](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md), [CP-SLAM](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)) offered high-quality reconstruction but suffered from slow training and rendering speeds. The advent of 3DGS SLAM (e.g., [CG-SLAM](9196-cg-slam.md), [SplaTAM]) addressed these bottlenecks by leveraging the explicit, differentiable nature of Gaussians. Recent developments have focused on improving robustness through uncertainty estimation [S1, unofficial], handling dynamic scenes [S2], multi-agent collaboration [S2], and active exploration [S3, unofficial][S5]. The field continues to evolve with hybrid approaches combining the geometric consistency of implicit fields with the rendering speed of explicit splatting.
