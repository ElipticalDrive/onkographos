# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Coarse-to-Fine Training, Progressive Densification

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to a family of optimization strategies for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that decompose the reconstruction process into sequential phases rather than performing a single monolithic training run [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of initializing and densifying a point cloud representation uniformly without manual hyperparameter tuning by decoupling the establishment of global topology from high-fidelity local refinement [S1, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training, which often struggles to balance global structure and local detail simultaneously, this approach ensures the primitive distribution adapts to scene complexity progressively [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [GroupFlow](6351-groupflow.md) or [3DGS-MCMC](7852-3dgs-mcmc.md) by explicitly structuring the training timeline into distinct stages with varying objectives, constraints, or data sampling strategies.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian primitive set $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ defined in [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md). The core mechanism involves decoupling optimization objectives or data sampling distributions across time steps (iterations or epochs). Common principles include:
1.  **Stage Segmentation:** The training loop is divided into distinct phases (e.g., Coarse/Fine, Geometry/Pose, Slice/Voxel). Each phase has specific hyperparameters, loss functions, or frozen/unfrozen parameters [S1, unofficial][S2, S3].
2.  **Progressive Complexity:** Methods often start with simpler representations (e.g., lower Spherical Harmonic degree, sparse initialization, or coarse geometry) and increase complexity (densification, higher SH degree, or pose estimation) as training progresses [S1, unofficial][S3].
3.  **Constraint Relaxation:** Early stages may enforce strong constraints (e.g., fixed poses, mesh alignment, or slice-only supervision) to stabilize learning, while later stages relax these constraints to allow full optimization [S1, unofficial][S2, S3].
4.  **Densification Timing:** Densification operations (splitting, cloning) are often triggered at specific intervals or between stages to manage the trade-off between convergence stability and representation capacity [S1, unofficial][S3].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
Decomposes optimization into a Coarse Stage (establishing global geometry/appearance from sparse SfM points) and a Fine Stage (refining details via densification and higher SH degrees). Chosen when uniform initialization fails to capture complex topology.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines a multi-stage approach for **MeshGS** that distinguishes between tightly-bound (mesh-constrained) and loosely-bound (free-floating) Gaussians, applying distinct loss functions and densification rules to each. Chosen when explicit mesh geometry is available and needs to be respected.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines a pre-initialized set of Gaussians (from feed-forward or generative models) via differentiable rendering optimization, often enabling dynamic densification. Chosen for per-scene adaptation of generalizable models or noisy initializations.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Optimizes volumetric data by first applying slice supervision (planar constraints for local structure) and then voxel supervision (sparse sampling for global guidance). Chosen for volumetric datasets (e.g., MRI) where structural continuity is critical.

### [Multi-stage Training with Pose Prediction](15018-multi-stage-training-with-pose-prediction.md)
Decouples geometry reconstruction from pose estimation in feed-forward 3DGS. Stage 1 learns geometry with GT poses; Stage 2 refines attributes with GT poses (frozen pose); Stage 3 unfreezes pose for end-to-end fine-tuning. Chosen for pose-free novel view synthesis to avoid error confounding.

### [Free-View Training (FVT)](15162-free-view-training-fvt.md)
Trains on long sequences with randomly sampled context views to add geometrical constraints that disentangle performance from input view count. Chosen for generalizable 3DGS to improve robustness across broader view ranges.

### [Training Loop with Mixed Losses and Post-Processing](17879-training-loop-with-mixed-losses-and-post-processing.md)
Integrates progressive SH degree activation, optional bilateral grid post-processing, and visibility-aware optimizer stepping within a single iterative loop. Chosen for high-fidelity rendering with view-dependent effects and sparse gradient handling.

### [Training Loop with Structure-Aware Densification](22986-training-loop-with-structure-aware-densification.md)
Replaces reactive densification with structure-aware strategy using precomputed multi-scale structure tensors to detect local frequency violations. Chosen to accelerate training and reduce iteration count while maintaining perceptual quality.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
Decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability [S3].
**Step:** Stage 1: Construct RGB point clouds for each input view, project to target view via differentiable point cloud renderer, aggregate point clouds to render RGB images at target view, and optimize depth network using photometric loss. Stage 2: Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module).
**Parameter:** Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain.
**Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability.

### Two-Stage Triplane and MLP Training
**Step:** Stage 1: Jointly train triplanes and MLP on a subset of 500 randomly selected shapes. Use a batch size of 1 object per iteration and 500k occupancy values points per object. Train for 200 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3. Stage 2: Freeze the shared MLP. Train triplane features for each object individually using a batch size of 200k occupancy values per object. Train for 30 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3.
**Parameter:** Triplane feature dimension: 128 × 128 × 32 × 3. Triplane initialization: values drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.1. MLP architecture: Fourier feature mapping layer (scale factor 1) followed by 3 fully connected layers of dimension 128, each with ReLU activation.
**Number:** Stage 1 training time: approximately 1 day on a single RTX 2080ti. Stage 2 training time: 10 minutes per shape on an RTX 2080ti.

### Continual Model Refinement
**Step:** Augment the initial model with larger sets of closer-up images (64 or 512 images) to simulate prolonged user interaction. Optimize the model for up to 4000 iterations, including 400 iterations of densification to capture finer details.
**Parameter:** Final Gaussian count grows to between 20k and 50k.
**Pitfall:** Improvement becomes marginal after 2000 iterations. Using foveated and subsequently undistorted views for training decreases quality metrics by 5-10% depending on the scene.
**Complexity:** Training proceeds at over 500 iterations per second.
**Comparison:** Fixed foveation center and variable foveation points yield less than 1% quality difference.

### Projection-Based Frequency Violation Metric
A densification trigger applying a three-channel anisotropic criterion where each Gaussian axis is evaluated against its own directional frequency content [S6].
**Number:** On Mip-NeRF360, $\eta^{(proj)}$ achieves PSNR 27.20 and LPIPS 0.203, compared to Ours PSNR 27.25 and LPIPS 0.197.
**Comparison:** Theoretically more precise than the scalar wavelength metric, but yields worse perceptual quality (LPIPS) in practice because it relies on accurate axis-direction alignment that is not yet optimized during training.

## Background
The concept of multi-stage or progressive training in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a response to the instability and inefficiency of single-stage optimization, particularly for complex scenes or generalizable models. The original 3DGS paper [Parent] introduced a single-stage optimization with periodic densification. Subsequent works identified specific failure modes:
*   **Initialization Sensitivity:** Poor initialization from sparse SfM points can lead to local minima [S1, unofficial]. Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine strategies address this by separating global topology establishment from local refinement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pose-Geometry Confounding:** In feed-forward methods, optimizing camera poses and Gaussian attributes simultaneously leads to instability [S1, unofficial][S2]. Multi-stage training with pose prediction decouples these processes [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Volumetric Structure:** Standard 3DGS struggles with volumetric data continuity [S1, unofficial]. Curriculum learning with slice and voxel supervision aligns training with the shear-warp rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial].
*   **Generalization:** Standard 3DGS overfits to input views [S2]. Free-View Training (FVT) uses long sequences and random context sampling to improve generalization [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Efficiency:** Standard densification can be slow or over-densify [S1, unofficial]. Structure-aware densification uses precomputed tensors to accelerate convergence [S1, unofficial].
