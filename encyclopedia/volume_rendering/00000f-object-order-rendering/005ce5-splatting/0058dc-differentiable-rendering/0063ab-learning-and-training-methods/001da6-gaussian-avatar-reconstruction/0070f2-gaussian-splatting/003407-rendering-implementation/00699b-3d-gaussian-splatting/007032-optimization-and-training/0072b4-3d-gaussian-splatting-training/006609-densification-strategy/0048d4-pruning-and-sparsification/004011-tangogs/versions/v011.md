# TangoGS

Also called: Capture-Conditioned Learning Allowance, Quality-Feedback Pruning

## Overview
TangoGS is a density control strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that automatically scales the Gaussian population across different capture sizes without per-capture retuning [S1, unofficial]. It combines a capture-derived learning allowance (model sizing) with training-based adaptation (quality feedback) to determine the final number of Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on fixed defaults or total pixel counts, TangoGS ensures the model budget is invariant to redundant views by using track-adjusted pixels, while using reconstruction quality to dynamically prune or retain capacity [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
TangoGS operates in three phases: allowance calculation, adaptive densification/pruning, and final selection. It uses the LiteGS implementation for scoring and optimization [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Sparse reconstruction model (tracks), capture images (including held-out validation), total training iterations $I_{total}$, initial Gaussian count $N_0$.
- **Outputs**: Final set of Gaussians optimized for the scene.

### Data Structures
- **Gaussian Population**: Unclustered parameters and Adam optimizer buffers.
- **Morton Order**: Parameters are reclustered into Morton-ordered groups of 128 elements to align with batch removal operations [S1, unofficial].
- **Sensitivity Scores**: Accumulated per-Gaussian scores, scatterable via a culling map to original indices [S1, unofficial].

### Constants and Parameters
- $g \approx 0.063362106$: Effective Gaussian count scaling factor [S1, unofficial].
- $T_{ref} = 2,462,476$: Reference count (geometric mean of 13 standard linear allowances) [S1, unofficial].
- $s = 1.7$: Scaling factor for final selection [S1, unofficial].
- $c = 0.0075$: Quality feedback coefficient [S1, unofficial].
- $P^* = 20$ dB: Threshold for no-removal [S1, unofficial].
- $N_{img}$: Total capture image count (including held-out) [S1, unofficial].
- $\rho$: Keep fraction for pruning [S1, unofficial].
- $T_{adapt}$: Adaptive densification target [S1, unofficial].
- $T_{lin}$: Linear allowance [S1, unofficial].
- $T_{cap}$: Capped allowance [S1, unofficial].
- $P$: Mean per-image training PSNR from the preceding calibration epoch [S1, unofficial].
- $N_{pre}$: Population of Gaussians entering the final selection event [S1, unofficial].
- $h$: Number of remaining events in a reference pruning schedule spaced 8% of total training iterations apart [S1, unofficial].
- $N_G$: Retained count in final selection [S1, unofficial].
- $f$: Keep fraction for final selection [S1, unofficial].

### Steps

1. **Calculate Learning Allowance**:
   Compute the linear allowance $T_{lin}$ and capped allowance $T_{cap}$ based on the capture size:
   $$ T_{lin} = \text{round} \left[ 0.90 \cdot \text{round}(0.07040234 \cdot N_{img}) \right] $$
   $$ T_{cap} = \text{round} \left[ T_{ref} \cdot (T_{lin} / T_{ref})^{0.5} \right] $$
   Nested rounding implements the effective scaling factor $g$ [S1, unofficial].

2. **Initialize Training Schedule**:
   - Densification starts at 2% of total training iterations.
   - Densification ends at 80% of total training iterations.
   - Soft pruning begins at approximately 9% of total training iterations.
   - Epoch spacing for growth events is 5 epochs for runs $\ge 150$ epochs; for shorter runs, spacing is reduced proportionally to a minimum of 1 epoch [S1, unofficial].

3. **Adaptive Densification and Pruning Loop** (Iterate from 2% to 80% of iterations):
   a. **Compute Quality Feedback**:
      Calculate $P$, the mean per-image PSNR over the just-completed training epoch [S1, unofficial].
   b. **Compute Keep Fraction**:
      $$ \rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 \cdot P $$
      (Rounding the intercept places the no-removal threshold at $\approx 19.995$ dB) [S1, unofficial].
   c. **Budgeted Densification**:
      If a growth event is scheduled:
      - Add Gaussians up to target $T_{adapt} = \rho \cdot T_{cap}$.
      - Ramp linearly from the current count to $T_{adapt}$ over the growth window [S1, unofficial].
   d. **Soft Pruning**:
      If a pruning event is scheduled:
      - Rank Gaussians present *before* growth using a sensitivity score [S1, unofficial].
      - Newly created Gaussians are exempt until they have trained [S1, unofficial].
      - Keep the top fraction $\rho$ of Gaussians.
      - Guard: If pruning would remove $> 90\%$ of the population, abort the event [S1, unofficial].
      - Removal counts are rounded down to the nearest multiple of 128 [S1, unofficial].

4. **Final Selection** (At 80% of iterations):
   a. **Compute Final Keep Fraction**:
      $$ f = \min(1, s \cdot \rho) $$
   b. **Calculate Retained Count**:
      $$ N_G = N_{pre} \cdot f^h $$
      where $N_{pre}$ is the population entering this event and $h$ is the number of remaining reference pruning events [S1, unofficial].
   c. **Prune**:
      - Rank all Gaussians by sensitivity score.
      - Keep the top $N_G$ Gaussians.
      - Optimize this population for the rest of training [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def tango_gs_training(gaussians, images, sparse_model, total_iterations):
    # Constants
    g = 0.063362106
    T_ref = 2462476
    s = 1.7
    c = 0.0075
    P_star = 20.0
    N_img = len(images) # Including held-out
    
    # 1. Calculate Allowance
    T_lin = round(0.90 * round(0.07040234 * N_img))
    T_cap = round(T_ref * (T_lin / T_ref) ** 0.5)
    
    # Schedule parameters
    densify_start = 0.02 * total_iterations
    densify_end = 0.80 * total_iterations
    prune_start = 0.09 * total_iterations
    
    # Epoch spacing logic (simplified for iteration-based loop)
    # Assume epoch_count is derived from total_iterations and view count
    # For runs >= 150 epochs, spacing = 5 epochs. Else, proportional min 1.
    # This is abstracted as a boolean flag is_growth_event(i) and is_prune_event(i)
    
    for i in range(total_iterations):
        # Standard 3DGS training step
        train_step(gaussians, images, i)
        
        # Quality Feedback
        if i % epoch_size == 0: # End of epoch
            P = compute_mean_psnr(gaussians, images)
            
        # Growth Event
        if is_growth_event(i, total_iterations):
            # Compute rho for this event
            rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 * P
            T_adapt = rho * T_cap
            
            # Budgeted Densification: Ramp to T_adapt
            current_count = len(gaussians)
            if current_count < T_adapt:
                add_gaussians(gaussians, T_adapt - current_count)
                
        # Soft Pruning Event
        if is_prune_event(i, total_iterations) and i > prune_start:
            # Compute rho for this event
            rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 * P
            
            # Sensitivity Scoring (LiteGS kernel)
            scores = compute_sensitivity_scores(gaussians)
            
            # Rank and Prune
            # Only Gaussians present before growth are eligible
            # Newly created are exempt until trained (handled by scoring window)
            
            num_to_keep = max(1, int(len(gaussians) * rho))
            
            # Guard against catastrophic collapse
            if num_to_keep < 0.1 * len(gaussians):
                continue # Abort prune
                
            # Sort by score descending
            sorted_indices = argsort_descending(scores)
            
            # Keep top num_to_keep
            keep_indices = sorted_indices[:num_to_keep]
            
            # Remove others (Morton-ordered groups of 128 alignment)
            remove_indices = sorted_indices[num_to_keep:]
            remove_gaussians(gaussians, remove_indices)
            
        # Final Selection
        if i == densify_end:
            rho = 0.7382 - 0.0075 * P
            f = min(1.0, s * rho)
            
            # h is number of remaining reference pruning events (8% spacing)
            # This is a pre-calculated constant based on total_iterations
            h = calculate_remaining_reference_events(total_iterations, i)
            
            N_pre = len(gaussians)
            N_G = N_pre * (f ** h)
            
            # Final Prune
            scores = compute_sensitivity_scores(gaussians)
            sorted_indices = argsort_descending(scores)
            keep_indices = sorted_indices[:int(N_G)]
            
            # Optimize final population
            optimize_final(gaussians, keep_indices, total_iterations - i)
            break
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Catastrophic Collapse**: If a pruning event would remove more than 90% of the Gaussian population, the event is aborted [S1, unofficial].
- **New Gaussians**: Newly created Gaussians are exempt from pruning until they have trained [S1, unofficial].
- **Alignment**: Removal counts are rounded down to the nearest multiple of 128 to preserve group alignment [S1, unofficial].
- **No-Removal Threshold**: If $P \le 19.995$ dB, $\rho \ge 1$, and no Gaussians are removed [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Capture-Conditioned Learning Allowance](10214-capture-conditioned-learning-allowance.md)
A method to determine the target Gaussian population size for 3D Gaussian Splatting based on capture characteristics, specifically using track-adjusted pixels normalized against standard scenes, to replace fixed defaults. It differs from sibling methods by ensuring the model budget is invariant to redundant views, whereas alternatives like total pixel count scale with view count and SfM point counts scale with matched-observation density rather than capture scale.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Training time is comparable to standard 3DGS, with additional overhead for sensitivity scoring and allowance calculation. Sensitivity scoring uses LiteGS's kernel, which substitutes a unit upstream image multiplier during the photometric-loss backward pass, avoiding additional scoring renders [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity**: Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians, which is automatically adjusted by TangoGS. On 13 standard benchmark scenes, TangoGS uses 48% fewer Gaussians than the best-performing baseline (LeGS) while matching PSNR [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance**: TangoGS matches the mean PSNR of the best-performing evaluated baseline on 13 standard scenes and automatically scales to larger models for 8 large captures without retuning [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Shared configuration across all captures (21 tested), no per-capture tuning required, robust to redundant views, and prevents catastrophic collapse.
- **Limitations**: Requires sparse reconstruction model for track-adjusted pixel calculation. Sensitivity scoring relies on LiteGS implementation details.
- **When to Use**: When training 3D Gaussian Splatting on captures of varying sizes and complexities without wanting to manually tune densification/pruning parameters.
- **When Not to Use**: When strict real-time training constraints prohibit the additional sensitivity scoring overhead, or when the sparse reconstruction model is unavailable or unreliable.

## Background
TangoGS was introduced in "Gauss What You Need: Compact Gaussian Splatting Across Scene Scales" (2026) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the LiteGS implementation and Speedy-Splat's image-sensitivity ranking [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the need for a unified density control strategy that adapts to capture scale and content complexity, overcoming the limitations of fixed-density methods and per-capture tuned baselines.
