# Gaussian Representation and Truncation

Also called: 3D Gaussian Primitives, Gaussian Splats, 3DGS Representation

## Overview
This family defines the fundamental data structure and rendering pipeline for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), representing scenes as a set of learnable 3D Gaussian primitives. It solves the problem of efficient, high-fidelity novel view synthesis by replacing implicit neural fields or explicit polygon meshes with differentiable volumetric splats that can be rasterized in real-time. It differs from [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) by focusing on the raw primitive representation and rendering mechanics rather than post-hoc geometric surface extraction, and from [3D Gaussian Splatting](52-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by detailing the specific parameterization, projection, and truncation logic that enables the optimization.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of $N$ 3D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $i$ is defined by learnable parameters describing its geometry, appearance, and position.

**Data Structure:**
Each Gaussian $g_i$ is represented by the following attributes [S1]:
*   **Position ($\mu_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing the centroid in world space.
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):** A unit quaternion $\mathbb{R}^4$ representing orientation.
*   **Scale ($s_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing anisotropic scaling factors along principal axes.
*   **Opacity ($\omega_i$):** A scalar $\mathbb{R} \in [0, 1]$ representing maximum opacity.
*   **Color ($c_i$):** Represented by Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients (typically degree 3, 48 coefficients for RGB) for view-dependent appearance.

**Covariance Matrix:**
The spatial spread is defined by a 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$, constructed from scale and rotation:
$$ \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T $$
where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ is the scaling matrix and $R_i$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q_i$ [S1].

**Projection to 2D:**
During rendering, the 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ is projected to the 2D image plane using the Jacobian $J$ of the affine approximation of the projective transformation:
$$ \Sigma' = J \Sigma_i J^T $$
The relevant part for rasterization is the top-left $2 \times 2$ submatrix $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}$ [S1].

**Spatial Response (Kernel Function):**
The contribution of a Gaussian to a pixel at screen coordinates $x$ is determined by:
$$ R(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu')^T (\Sigma'_{2 \times 2})^{-1} (x - \mu')\right) $$
where $\mu'$ is the projected 2D mean [S1].

**Alpha Compositing:**
The final opacity $\alpha$ contributed by a Gaussian is:
$$ \alpha = \omega_i \cdot R(x) $$
The pixel color is computed via alpha blending with primitives sorted back-to-front:
$$ C_{pixel} = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i C_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j < i} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $C_i$ is evaluated using SH coefficients based on view direction [S1].

**Truncation:**
To optimize speed, fragments are discarded if $\alpha < \tau_\alpha$ (typically $1/255$), limiting rendering to a region of approximately $3.33\sigma$ [S1].

**Density Estimation for Mesh Extraction:**
The scene density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is the superposition of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^T \Sigma_i^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)\right) $$
where $\alpha_i$ is opacity, $\mathbf{p}_i$ is the mean, and $\Sigma_i$ is the covariance [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Inverse Multiquadric Splatting](9910-inverse-multiquadric-splatting.md)
Replaces the Gaussian kernel with an inverse multiquadric function to reduce artifacts and stabilize gradients in high-opacity regions.

### [Opacity-Independent Gaussian Truncation](10266-opacity-independent-gaussian-truncation.md)
Decouples the spatial truncation threshold from the primitive's opacity, allowing consistent aggressive culling regardless of $\omega_i$.

### [Content-Aware Texturing for Gaussian Splatting](12606-content-aware-texturing-for-gaussian-splatting.md)
Augments primitives with spatially varying texture maps and dynamic resolution management to capture high-frequency details.

### [Directed Distance Field (DDF) for Gaussian Splatting](12854-directed-distance-field-ddf-for-gaussian-splatting.md)
Uses a neural implicit field to provide constant-time ray queries for geometry, bypassing iterative Gaussian traversal.

### [Atomized Proliferation](12924-atomized-proliferation.md)
Constrains fine-detail Gaussians into uniform "Atom Gaussians" and merges smooth surfaces to improve geometric precision and training stability.

### [Voxel-based Gaussian Splatting](13071-voxel-based-gaussian-splatting.md)
Fixes Gaussian positions to a regular grid and removes view-dependent color for stable differentiable X-ray rendering from sparse projections.

### [Gaussian Voxel Kernel Functions (GVKF)](13979-gaussian-voxel-kernel-functions-gvkf.md)
Replaces discrete opacity with Gaussian kernel functions decoded from shared feature vectors in a sparse voxel grid to reduce memory and improve surface continuity.

### [3D Gaussian Particle Approximation of VDB Datasets](14723-3d-gaussian-particle-approximation-of-vdb-datasets.md)
Converts OpenVDB volumes into non-overlapping Gaussian particles with LOD support for memory-efficient rendering of sparse volumetric data.

### [Pruning and Reduction](14909-pruning-and-reduction.md)
Decreases the number of primitives to improve efficiency and mitigate overfitting by evaluating geometric redundancy and spatial importance.

## Background
The 3D Gaussian Splatting representation was introduced by Kerbl et al. in "A Hierarchical 3D Gaussian Representation for Real-Time Rendering of Static Scenes" (2023) [S1]. It emerged as a solution to the computational bottlenecks of neural radiance fields (NeRFs), offering real-time rendering speeds by leveraging differentiable rasterization of Gaussian primitives. The method has since become a standard baseline for novel view synthesis, with numerous extensions focusing on optimization efficiency, geometric accuracy, and specialized applications like medical imaging and inverse rendering.
