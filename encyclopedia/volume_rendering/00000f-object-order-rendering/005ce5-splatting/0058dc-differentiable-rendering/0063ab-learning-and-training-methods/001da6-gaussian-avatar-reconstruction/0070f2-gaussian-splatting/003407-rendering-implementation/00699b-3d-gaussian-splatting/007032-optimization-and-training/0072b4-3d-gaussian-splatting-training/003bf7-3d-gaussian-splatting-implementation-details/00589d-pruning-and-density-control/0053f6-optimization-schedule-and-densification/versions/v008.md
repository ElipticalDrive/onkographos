# Optimization Schedule and Densification

Also called: Training Schedule, Densification Strategy

## Overview
This family defines the temporal scheduling of primitive creation (densification), removal (pruning), and resource allocation (resolution) during the optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It solves the problem of balancing reconstruction fidelity against computational cost by controlling when and how new Gaussians are introduced relative to the rendering resolution and optimization progress. It sets itself apart from static initialization methods by employing dynamic, often concave-up, growth curves that synchronize primitive count with increasing resolution to fit high-frequency details only after low-frequency structures are established [Parent].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3DGS primitive representation:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ (Spherical Harmonics)

The core mechanism involves calculating an importance score for each Gaussian and removing those below a threshold or ranking. Methods typically employ a "concave-up" growth curve for primitives, where growth accelerates over time, synchronized with increasing rendering resolution to fit high-frequency details only after low-frequency structures are established [Parent].

### QuickSplat Optimization Process
*   **Definition:** An optimization framework that uses learned networks for initialization, densification, and parameter updates to accelerate surface reconstruction [S1].
*   **Steps:** Iterate for $t = 0$ to $T - 1$. Reset gradient accumulator $\nabla G_t \leftarrow 0$. For all images, compute rendering loss $L$, compute gradient $\delta G_t$, and accumulate: $\nabla G_t \leftarrow \nabla G_t + \delta G_t$. Generate new Gaussians $\hat{G}_t$ using a densifier network $f_D$ on current Gaussians $G_t$, accumulated gradients $\nabla G_t$, and time step $t$: $\hat{G}_t \leftarrow f_D(G_t, \nabla G_t, t)$ [S1].

### BlitzGS Optimization Schedule
*   **Steps:** Disable the LOD gate during the density-control window (iterations 2,000–32,000). Engage the LOD gate after the first importance-scoring pass ($T_1$). After the importance-scoring pass, reweight density-control gradients using the per-Gaussian visibility factor $\phi_i$ for the remainder of the density-control window [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Density control interval runs every 1,000 steps from iteration 2,000 up to 32,000 (last event at 31,000) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Extending training iterations (e.g., 500k–1000k) does not significantly improve quality on aerial scenes (e.g., Building 19.27→19.50 dB), indicating deficits stem from representation structure rather than insufficient budget [S2, unofficial].

### Normal Gradient Based Densification
*   **Definition:** A densification criterion to improve normal recovery in thin regions by introducing an additional densification criterion on the gradient of normals, beyond the vanilla 3DGS gradient of view space points [S3].
*   **Steps:** Densify Gaussians whose normal gradient exceeds a threshold $T_n$ [S3].
*   **Parameters:** $T_n$: The threshold for normal gradient magnitude used to trigger densification. Typical value: $2 \times 10^{-9}$ [S3].

### Global-to-local densification
*   **Definition:** A densification strategy that decouples split and clone operations across densification phases to accelerate optimization. It uses split for global spread at low resolution and suppresses clone, then reintroduces clone for local refinement at full resolution [S4, unofficial].
*   **Steps:** Calculate the norm of the average position gradient of each Gaussian primitive every 100 iterations. If the gradient norm exceeds a predefined threshold, check the maximum scale of the Gaussian primitive. If the maximum scale exceeds a given scale threshold, split the Gaussian into smaller components. Otherwise, clone the Gaussian with the same parameters. During low-resolution training, promote Gaussian primitives' global spread with the split operation and suppress clone operations to prevent premature local clustering. When transitioning to full-resolution training, reintroduce clone operations to refine high-frequency details [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Gradient calculation frequency: 100 iterations [S4, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Unlike standard 3DGS which applies split and clone simultaneously, this method decouples them across phases (global vs local) to address inefficient spatial spread and redundant primitives [S4, unofficial].

### Resolution Guided Primitive Scheduler
*   **Formula:** $P_{fin}^{(i)} = \max(P_{fin}^{(i-1)}, \gamma \cdot P_{fin}^{(i-1)} + \eta \cdot P_{add})$, where $P_{add}$ is the number of primitives densified in step $i$. $P_{fin}^{(0)}$ is initialized as $5 \cdot P_{init}$ [S5].
*   **Parameters:** Power factor in primitive growth: Starts at 2 and gradually decreases to 1 along the optimization process to suppress early growth and encourage mid-stage growth. Hyperparameter $\gamma$: Momentum decay factor for primitive budget. Default value: 0.98. Hyperparameter $\eta$: Force scaling factor for primitive budget. Default value: 1. Resolution modulation: The continuous scalar $r(i)$ is modulated to $\lfloor r(i) \rfloor$ in practice to encourage faster primitive growth [S5].
*   **Pitfall:** Too much primitive growth at the end of optimization results in poor convergence. Mitigated by the concave-up scheduling function and momentum-based budgeting [S5].

### 3D Refinement View Sampling Strategy
*   **Definition:** A staged sampling schedule for supervising 3D refinement that balances fitting the current refined view with preserving rendering ability in original and previously refined views [S6].
*   **Steps:** Divide the total 3D refinement steps into three equal phases (first third, subsequent third, final third). In the first third, designate every 3 steps as 'current-refine steps' to optimize using the current refined image $\hat{I}^{i,e,f}_{ie}$. In the subsequent third, designate every 5 steps as 'current-refine steps'. In the final third, designate every 8 steps as 'current-refine steps'. For all non-current-refine steps, randomly sample views from the training set $S_{train}$ and the previous refined set $F_{i-1}$ [S6].
*   **Parameters:** Selection weight for $F_{i-1}$ is lower than for $S_{train}$ to prioritize original training data over previously refined views [S6].

## Background
The concept of dynamic densification originated with the introduction of 3D Gaussian Splatting [Parent]. Subsequent works have refined this schedule to address specific bottlenecks: QuickSplat introduced learned densification networks [S1], DashGaussian reformulated the schedule as a frequency-fitting task [S5], and BlitzGS optimized the schedule for city-scale rendering by managing density control windows [S2, unofficial]. Normal gradient densification was introduced to improve geometric fidelity in thin regions [S3], while global-to-local strategies decoupled split and clone operations for faster convergence [S4, unofficial].
