# Duplex-GS

Also called: Proxy-Guided Weighted Blending for Real-Time Order-Independent Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Duplex-GS is a dual-hierarchy rendering framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting that achieves real-time order-independent transparency by combining cell-level alpha-blending with a physically grounded Weighted-Sum Rendering (WSR) strategy [S1]. It solves the transparency artifacts and lack of early termination inherent in vanilla Order-Independent Transparency (OIT) methods, while reducing the sorting overhead of conventional alpha-blending by sorting coarse ellipsoidal cells instead of individual Gaussians [S1]. Unlike siblings such as [LinPrim Differentiable Rendering Pipeline](2195-linprim-differentiable-rendering-pipeline.md) which may use linear corrections, or [GsplatVolumeLighting](2203-gsplatvolumelighting.md) which focuses on lighting, Duplex-GS specifically addresses the physical consistency of blending weights to eliminate popping artifacts and enable early termination [S1].

## How it works
Duplex-GS operates in two layers: Layer I organizes Gaussians into ellipsoidal cells for efficient rasterization and coarse sorting, and Layer II decodes these cells into Gaussians and blends them using a hybrid approach that is ordered across cells but order-independent within cells [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs:** Camera parameters (view matrix, projection matrix, intrinsics), scene representation (initialized from SfM points), and predefined hyperparameters ($K$, $\epsilon$).
- **Outputs:** Final pixel color $\mathcal{C}$.

### Data Structures
**Cell Proxy ($n$):**
- Position $x_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$
- Scales $S_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$
- Quaternion $Q_n \in \mathbb{R}^4$
- Calibration scalar $v_n$ (parameterized by spherical harmonic coefficients for directional variation)
- Latent feature vector $f_n$

**Neural Gaussian ($k$ within cell $n$):**
- Center $\mu_{n,k}$
- Opacity $\sigma_{n,k}$
- Color $\mathcal{C}_{n,k}$
- Scale $s_{n,k}$
- Quaternion $\mathbf{q}_{n,k}$

### Initialization
1. Initialize cells from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points $P \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ [S1].
2. Each cell $n$ is associated with a latent feature $f_n$ and emits $K$ neural Gaussians [S1].

### Gaussian Parameter Decoding
The attributes of the $K$ neural Gaussians emitted by cell $n$ are decoded from $f_n$, viewing distance $\delta_n$, and viewing direction $d_n$ (or $\mathbf{r}_n$) via shared scene-wise Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) [S1]:

1. **Center:**
   $$ \mu_{n,k} = x_n + \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}} \cdot S_n $$
   where $\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}}$ is the learned offset, constrained to $[-1, 1]$ [S1].

2. **Opacity:**
   $$ \{\sigma_{n,1}, \dots, \sigma_{n,K}\} = F_\sigma(\mathbf{f}_n, \delta_n, \mathbf{r}_n) $$
   $F_\sigma$ is activated by Tanh, ensuring $\sigma_{n,k} \in [-1, 1]$ (physically plausible range typically $[0,1]$ after clamping or interpretation) [S1].

3. **Color:**
   $$ \{c^{n,1}, \dots, c^{n,K}\} = F_c(f_n, \delta_n, d_n) $$
   [S1]

4. **Scale Ratio:**
   $$ \{\mathcal{O}_{n,1}^{\text{state}}, \dots, \mathcal{O}_{n,K}^{\text{state}}\} = F_s(f_n, \delta_n, d_n) $$
   $F_s$ is activated by Sigmoid, restricting the ratio to $(0, 1)$ [S1].

5. **Quaternion:**
   $$ \{\mathbf{q}_{n,1}, \dots, \mathbf{q}_{n,K}\} = F_q(f_n, \delta_n, d_n) $$
   [S1]

6. **Gaussian Scale:**
   $$ s_{n,k} = \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}} * S_n $$
   where $\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}}$ is the output from $F_s$ [S1].

7. **Cell Quaternion Update:**
   To align the cell orientation with its Gaussians:
   $$ Q_n = \text{norm}\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{q}_{n,k} * \sigma_{n,k}\right) $$
   [S1]

8. **Scale Clamping:**
   To ensure Gaussian regions stay inside the cell:
   $$ \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}} = \min \left( \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}}, 1 - \|\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}}\| \right) $$
   [S1]

### Rendering Pipeline

**Layer I: Cell Rasterization and Sorting**
1. **Tile Assignment:** Assign visible cells to screen tiles [S1].
2. **Sorting:** Apply radix sort to the sequence of cells based on their depth (e.g., center depth or front-most point) [S1]. This establishes a coarse spatial order for patches of Gaussians.
3. **Cell-Level Transmittance:** Compute the transmittance $T_n^{\text{cell}}$ for each cell $n$ in the sorted order. The transmittance decays based on the accumulated opacity of preceding cells and their contained Gaussians:
   $$ T_n^{\text{cell}} = \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \prod_{k=1}^{K} (1 - v_i \cdot \alpha_{i,k}) $$
   where $v_i$ is the learnable calibration scalar for cell $i$, and $\alpha_{i,k}$ is the opacity $\sigma_{i,k}$ of the $k$-th Gaussian in cell $i$ [S1].
   *Note: The source formula uses $n-1$ in the inner product limit, but logically it iterates over all Gaussians $k=1 \dots K$ of all preceding cells $i=1 \dots n-1$. The provided text says $\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}$ which appears to be a typo for $K$ or the number of Gaussians in cell $i$. Given $K$ is the predefined number of neural Gaussians per anchor, the inner product should iterate over $K$. We assume the standard accumulation over all previous primitives.*
4. **Early Termination:** If $T_n^{\text{cell}} < \epsilon$ (predefined threshold), subsequent cells can be skipped or treated as fully occluded [S1].

**Layer II: Physical Weighted Sum Rendering (WSR)**
1. **Cell Weight:** Compute the weight $w_n$ for cell $n$:
   $$ w_n = v_n \cdot T_n^{\text{cell}} $$
   [S1]
2. **Final Color:** Aggregate the Gaussians inside each cell in an order-independent manner using the cell weights:
   $$ \mathcal{C} = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^N w_n \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_{n,k} \mathcal{C}_{n,k}}{\sum_{n=1}^N w_n \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_{n,k}} $$
   where $\mathcal{C}_{n,k}$ is the color of the $k$-th Gaussian in cell $n$ [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def duplex_gs_render(cells, camera_params, K, epsilon):
    """
    Render a scene using Duplex-GS.
    
    Args:
        cells: List of Cell objects, each containing:
            - x_n: position (3,)
            - S_n: scale (3,)
            - Q_n: quaternion (4,)
            - v_n: calibration scalar (scalar or SH coeffs)
            - f_n: latent feature vector
        camera_params: View and projection matrices, intrinsics
        K: Number of neural Gaussians per cell
        epsilon: Early termination threshold
        
    Returns:
        Image buffer
    """
    # Layer I: Cell Rasterization
    
    # 1. Project cells to screen space to determine visibility and depth
    # Transform cell centers to camera space
    cell_centers_cam = transform_to_camera_space(cells, camera_params)
    
    # Determine visible cells (e.g., via bounding box or projected extent)
    visible_cells = filter_visible_cells(cells, cell_centers_cam, camera_params)
    
    # 2. Sort visible cells by depth (e.g., z-coordinate in camera space)
    sorted_cells = radix_sort(visible_cells, key=lambda c: c.center_z)
    
    # 3. Compute cell-level transmittance and weights
    cell_weights = []
    current_transmittance = 1.0
    
    for cell in sorted_cells:
        # Early termination check
        if current_transmittance < epsilon:
            # Remaining cells are occluded, weight is 0 or negligible
            cell_weights.append(0.0)
            continue
            
        # Decode Gaussians for this cell
        gaussians = decode_neural_gaussians(cell, camera_params, K)
        
        # Compute accumulated opacity of this cell's Gaussians
        # Note: v_n is the calibration scalar for the PRECEDING cells' contribution
        # In the formula T_n^cell = product_{i=1}^{n-1} product_{k=1}^K (1 - v_i * alpha_{i,k})
        # The weight w_n = v_n * T_n^cell. 
        # Here, v_n in the weight formula refers to the calibration scalar of cell n itself?
        # Source says: "w_n = v_n * T_n^cell" and "T_n^cell ... decays based on ... preceding cells".
        # And "v_i is introduced to address ... inter-cell overlaps".
        # It seems v_n is used as a multiplier for the cell's contribution to the final sum,
        # and also potentially in the transmittance of subsequent cells?
        # Let's follow the formula strictly:
        # T_n^cell depends on cells 1 to n-1.
        # w_n depends on v_n and T_n^cell.
        
        # Calculate T_n^cell (transmittance reaching this cell)
        # This requires iterating through all previous cells and their Gaussians.
        # For efficiency, this is often accumulated incrementally.
        # However, the formula implies a global product.
        # Let's assume incremental update for implementation:
        
        # Calculate the "opacity contribution" of the PREVIOUS cells to update transmittance
        # But wait, T_n^cell is the transmittance BEFORE cell n.
        # So for cell n, we use the current_transmittance (which is T_n^cell).
        
        T_n_cell = current_transmittance
        
        # Weight for this cell
        w_n = cell.v_n * T_n_cell
        cell_weights.append(w_n)
        
        # Update transmittance for the NEXT cell (n+1)
        # T_{n+1}^cell = T_n^cell * product_{k=1}^K (1 - v_n * alpha_{n,k})
        # Note: The source formula for T_n^cell uses v_i for cell i.
        # So we use v_n to attenuate the transmittance for subsequent cells.
        
        accumulated_opacity_contribution = 1.0
        for g in gaussians:
            # alpha is opacity sigma
            alpha = g.sigma
            accumulated_opacity_contribution *= (1.0 - cell.v_n * alpha)
            
        current_transmittance *= accumulated_opacity_contribution

    # Layer II: WSR Blending
    numerator = 0.0
    denominator = 0.0
    
    for i, cell in enumerate(sorted_cells):
        w_n = cell_weights[i]
        if w_n == 0:
            continue
            
        gaussians = decode_neural_gaussians(cell, camera_params, K)
        
        cell_sum_color = 0.0
        cell_sum_alpha = 0.0
        
        for g in gaussians:
            # Order-independent sum within the cell
            cell_sum_color += g.sigma * g.color
            cell_sum_alpha += g.sigma
            
        numerator += w_n * cell_sum_color
        denominator += w_n * cell_sum_alpha
        
    if denominator == 0:
        return background_color
        
    final_color = numerator / denominator
    return final_color

def decode_neural_gaussians(cell, camera_params, K):
    """
    Decode K Gaussians from a cell's latent feature and parameters.
    """
    # Extract viewing direction and distance from camera_params
    # This is simplified; in practice, these are per-pixel or per-ray
    # For this high-level pseudocode, we assume they are available or averaged
    viewing_dir = get_viewing_direction(cell, camera_params)
    viewing_dist = get_viewing_distance(cell, camera_params)
    
    # Decode attributes using MLPs
    # F_pos, F_sigma, F_c, F_s, F_q are the shared MLPs
    offsets_pos = F_pos(cell.f_n) # Output shape (K, 3)
    opacities = tanh(F_sigma(cell.f_n, viewing_dist, viewing_dir)) # Output shape (K,)
    colors = F_c(cell.f_n, viewing_dist, viewing_dir) # Output shape (K, 3)
    scale_ratios = sigmoid(F_s(cell.f_n, viewing_dist, viewing_dir)) # Output shape (K,)
    quaternions = F_q(cell.f_n, viewing_dist, viewing_dir) # Output shape (K, 4)
    
    gaussians = []
    for k in range(K):
        # Center
        mu = cell.x_n + offsets_pos[k] * cell.S_n
        
        # Scale
        s_ratio = scale_ratios[k]
        # Clamp scale ratio
        pos_norm = np.linalg.norm(offsets_pos[k])
        s_ratio = min(s_ratio, 1.0 - pos_norm)
        s = s_ratio * cell.S_n
        
        # Quaternion normalization
        q = quaternions[k] / np.linalg.norm(quaternions[k])
        
        gaussians.append({
            'mu': mu,
            'sigma': max(0.0, min(1.0, opacities[k])), # Clamp to [0,1]
            'color': colors[k],
            'scale': s,
            'quaternion': q
        })
        
    return gaussians
```

### Parameters
- $K$: Predefined number of neural Gaussians emitted per anchor/cell [S1].
- $v_n$: Learnable calibration scalar for cell $n$, parameterized using spherical harmonic coefficients to capture directional variation [S1].
- $\epsilon$: Predefined threshold for early termination [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Order Confusion:** Overlap among cells can cause order confusion. This is addressed by the calibration scalar $v_n$ and the physically grounded transmittance model [S1].
- **Transparency Artifacts:** Vanilla OIT-based Gaussian Splatting suffers from transparency artifacts due to lack of physical constraints. Duplex-GS eliminates these by ensuring transmittance decays rapidly for opaque objects [S1].
- **Popping Artifacts:** By making blending order-independent within cells, Duplex-GS eliminates popping artifacts caused by depth sorting changes across frames [S1].
- **Scale Clamping:** The scale ratio $\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}}$ must be clamped to ensure Gaussian regions stay inside the cell, preventing visual leaks or incorrect rasterization bounds [S1].

## Subtopics

### AAA-Gaussians
A method that eliminates sorting analytically by pre-integrating the alpha blending equation to compute an equivalent order-independent transparency through a rational polynomial approximation of the visibility term, suppressing popping artifacts without per-pixel depth sorting [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Reduces sorting overhead significantly compared to global Gaussian sorting because it sorts only the coarser ellipsoidal cells, which are orders of magnitude fewer than individual Gaussians [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Lower memory consumption due to the reduced number of primitives being sorted and managed during rasterization [S1].
- **Performance:** Enables real-time rendering on diverse real-world datasets [S1].
- **Strengths:** Eliminates transparency and popping artifacts; enables early termination for accelerated rendering; physically grounded blending weights [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires the dual-hierarchy structure (cells and Gaussians) and additional MLPs for decoding; calibration scalar $v_n$ adds learnable parameters [S1].
- **When to use:** When real-time performance is required and transparency artifacts from OIT or popping from sorting are unacceptable [S1].
- **When not to use:** If the scene does not benefit from the cell-level organization or if the overhead of managing cells outweighs the sorting benefits for very small scenes [G].

## Background
Duplex-GS was introduced in a paper submitted to the IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems for Video Technology in 2026 [S1]. It builds upon neural Gaussian frameworks like Scaffold-GS, which use anchors to emit neural Gaussians, by replacing point anchors with ellipsoidal cells to provide explicit geometric structure and visible regions [S1]. The method addresses limitations in existing OIT-based 3DGS methods, which lack physical constraints and suffer from transparency artifacts [S1].
