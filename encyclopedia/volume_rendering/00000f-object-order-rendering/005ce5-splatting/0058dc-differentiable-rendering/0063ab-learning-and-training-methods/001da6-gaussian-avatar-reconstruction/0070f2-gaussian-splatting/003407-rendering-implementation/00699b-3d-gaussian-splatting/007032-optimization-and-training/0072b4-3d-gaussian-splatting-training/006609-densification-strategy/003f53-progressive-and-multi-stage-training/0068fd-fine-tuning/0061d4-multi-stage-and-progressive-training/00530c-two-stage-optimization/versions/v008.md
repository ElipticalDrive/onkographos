# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization decomposes the training of 3D Gaussian representations into distinct phases to address conflicting objectives such as stability, geometric accuracy, and compression efficiency. By separating the optimization of geometry/appearance from material properties or codec constraints, it prevents the destabilization of the Gaussian distribution during refinement [S1][S2][S3, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training which optimizes all parameters simultaneously, this method freezes earlier-stage parameters (e.g., geometry) in later stages to ensure that fine-grained adjustments do not corrupt the initial reconstruction [S2].

## How it works
The specific implementation of two-stage optimization varies by application (geometry reconstruction, inverse rendering, or compression), but all variants follow a sequential decomposition where Stage 1 establishes a stable baseline and Stage 2 refines specific attributes [S1][S2][S3, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images (context views $C$), camera poses ($K_t, T_t$), and optionally depth maps or normal maps.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ with optimized parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/material) and, in compression variants, a compressed bitstream [S1][S2][S3, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The core data structure is the set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ typically contains:
*   **Geometry ($T$):** Position $\mathbf{p}$, scale $\mathbf{s}$, rotation $\mathbf{q}$ (quaternion), and normal $\mathbf{n}$ [S2].
*   **Appearance ($A$):** Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients for color [S1][S2].
*   **Material ($M$):** Albedo $\mathbf{a}$, roughness $\rho$, and metallicity $m$ (for inverse rendering) [S2].
*   **Latent Features ($F$):** Intermediate feature tensors used in feed-forward variants [S3, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

#### Variant 1: Low-Resolution to Full-Resolution (Geometry Reconstruction)
Used in sparse-view CT reconstruction [S1].

1.  **Stage 1: Low-Resolution Optimization**
    *   Define a low-resolution 3D grid with dimensions $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume [S1].
    *   Initialize Gaussians within this coarse grid.
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters to minimize the projection error:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \sum_{i=1}^{N} || \hat{p}[V(\theta_i)] - p ||_2^2 $$
        where $\hat{p}$ is the projected data from the differentiable CT projector, $p$ is the actual measurement, and $V$ is the discretized volume [S1].
    *   This stage rapidly places Gaussians in approximately correct positions [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Full-Resolution Refinement**
    *   Expand the sampling grid to cover all voxels of the original volume [S1].
    *   Refine Gaussian parameters (position, scale, etc.) to capture fine-grained details [S1].
    *   Continue minimizing the same $L_2$ projection loss to achieve high-quality reconstruction [S1].

#### Variant 2: Initial Geometry/Appearance to Material Decomposition (Inverse Rendering)
Used in GS-IR [S2].

1.  **Stage 1: Initial Optimization (30,000 iterations)**
    *   Optimize Geometry $T = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ and Appearance $A$ (color SH).
    *   Minimize the initial loss $L_{init}$:
        $$ L_{init} = L_c + L_n $$
        where $L_c$ is the L1 color reconstruction loss and $L_n$ is the normal loss:
        $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} TV_{normal} $$
        $TV_{normal}$ is a total variation smoothing term on normals [S2].
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$ [S2].

2.  **Stage 2: Decomposition Optimization (10,000 iterations)**
    *   **Freeze** Geometry $T$ and Appearance $A$ [S2].
    *   Optimize Material $M = \{\mathbf{a}, \rho, m\}$ (albedo, roughness, metallicity) [S2].
    *   Minimize the decomposition loss $L_d$:
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
        where:
        *   $L_{shade}$ is the shading loss between rendered and target images.
        *   $TV_{mat}$ is the total variation of the predicted material map $\hat{M}$:
            $$ TV_{mat} = \frac{1}{|\hat{M}|} \sum_{i,j} \Delta_{ij} (\hat{M}_{i,j} - \hat{M}_{i,j-1})^2 $$
        *   $TV_{light}$ is the total variation of the estimated light field $\hat{E}$:
            $$ TV_{light} = \frac{1}{|\hat{E}|} \sum_{i,j} \Delta \hat{E}_{ij} $$
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_M = 1.0$, $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2].

#### Variant 3: Codec-Free to Codec-Aware (Compression)
Used in CodecSplat [S3, unofficial].

1.  **Stage 1: Codec-Free Gaussian Generation**
    *   Train the feed-forward Gaussian generation pipeline **without** the feature codec [S3, unofficial].
    *   Input: Context views $C$.
    *   Extract features: $(F_{mv}, D_{enc}, P_{depth}) = MVEnc(C)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Generate refined feature: $F_g = FeatHead(I_{1:N}, F_{mv}, P_{depth})$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Predict codec-free Gaussian set $G_{cf}$ from $F_c$ (refined feature) and $D_{enc}$ (encoder-side depth) [S3, unofficial].
    *   Render target view: $\hat{Y}_{tcf} = Render(G_{cf}, K_t, T_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Minimize Stage 1 loss:
        $$ L_{stage1} = D_{render}(\hat{Y}_{tcf}, Y_t) $$
        where $Y_t$ is the target-view image and $D_{render}$ is the rendering reconstruction loss [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Stage 2: Codec-Aware Latent Coding**
    *   Initialize from Stage 1 checkpoint [S3, unofficial].
    *   Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Freeze** remaining backbone modules; optimize only codec-related modules [S3, unofficial].
    *   Decode Gaussian set $\hat{G}$ from latent codes [S3, unofficial].
    *   Render target view: $\hat{Y}_t = Render(\hat{G}, K_t, T_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Minimize Stage 2 loss:
        $$ L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} [ D_{render}(\hat{Y}_t, Y_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} ] + \beta R $$
        where:
        *   $\lambda \in [16, 1024]$ is sampled during training; fixed at test time [S3, unofficial].
        *   $\lambda_{max} = 1024$ [S3, unofficial].
        *   $R$ is the estimated rate [S3, unofficial].
        *   $\gamma = 0.1$ is the weight for codec-side depth supervision [S3, unofficial].
        *   $\beta = 10^{-4}$ (DL3DV) or $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (RE10K) is the rate penalty weight [S3, unofficial].
    *   Codec-side depth loss:
        $$ D_{codec-depth} = || S - S_{enc} ||_1 $$
        where $S$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $S_{enc}$ is the encoder-side normalized inverse-depth map [S3, unofficial].
    *   Encoder-side normalized inverse-depth calculation:
        $$ S_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{D_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        where $n_i$ and $f_i$ are the near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S3, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Optimizer:** Adam or AdamW [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $10^{-4}$ (CodecSplat) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Weight Decay:** $0.01$ (CodecSplat) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:**
    *   GS-IR Stage 1: 30,000 [S2].
    *   GS-IR Stage 2: 10,000 [S2].
    *   CodecSplat: Not specified in sources [S3, unofficial].
    *   3DGR-CT: Not specified in sources [S1].
*   **Hardware:**
    *   GS-IR: Single NVIDIA V100 [S2].
    *   CodecSplat: Four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB [S3, unofficial].
    *   3DGR-CT: Not specified in sources [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck (CodecSplat) is unstable; the two-stage separation is required to stabilize Gaussian generation [S3, unofficial].
*   **Parameter Freezing:** In GS-IR, failing to freeze geometry and appearance in Stage 2 can lead to suboptimal material decomposition [S2].
*   **Depth Normalization:** In CodecSplat, the normalization of inverse depth bounds ($n_i, f_i$) must be consistent between encoder and decoder to ensure $D_{codec-depth}$ is meaningful [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two-stage optimization generally increases total training time compared to single-stage methods due to the sequential nature of the stages. However, Stage 1 (e.g., low-resolution) can be significantly faster, allowing for rapid convergence of coarse geometry [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is dominated by the Gaussian representation and intermediate features. CodecSplat reduces final storage via compression but requires additional memory for the codec modules during Stage 2 [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **GS-IR:** Achieves high-quality inverse rendering results with accurate material and lighting separation [S2].
    *   **CodecSplat:** Achieves ultra-compact bitstreams (KiB-level) while maintaining competitive PSNR/SSIM compared to MiB-level baselines [S3, unofficial].
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Improves reconstruction quality in sparse-view CT by refining geometry at full resolution [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents optimization conflicts; improves stability for complex objectives (compression, PBR); allows fine-grained control over specific attributes.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and hyperparameters; increased training complexity; potential for suboptimal global minima if Stage 1 is insufficient [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Two-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a solution to the instability and suboptimal convergence of single-stage training for complex tasks.
*   **Geometry Refinement:** The low-to-high resolution approach was introduced in 3DGR-CT to handle sparse-view CT reconstruction, where coarse initialization prevents noise amplification [S1].
*   **Inverse Rendering:** GS-IR adopted a two-stage process to decouple geometry/appearance optimization from material/lighting decomposition, enabling stable inverse rendering [S2].
*   **Compression:** CodecSplat introduced a codec-free to codec-aware two-stage training to stabilize the integration of entropy-coded bottlenecks into feed-forward Gaussian generation pipelines [S3, unofficial].
