# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Priors, Surface Constraints, Frequency Regularization

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints address the tendency of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to over-reconstruct empty space, produce "floaters," and suffer from geometric inaccuracies when optimized solely on photometric loss [S2][S3]. These methods introduce external constraints—such as frequency domain penalties, depth consistency, or shape priors—to guide the optimization of Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity) toward physically plausible surfaces [S2][S3]. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md), which relies on densification heuristics, these techniques explicitly penalize deviations from expected geometric or spectral properties [S2][S3].

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of decoupling photometric fidelity from geometric validity. While [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md) optimizes for pixel color accuracy, these methods add loss terms or modify parameters to enforce:
1.  **Surface Alignment:** Gaussians should align with local surface normals or flatten into surfels [14222-geometry-regularization.md][9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md].
2.  **Depth Consistency:** Rendered depth must match monocular estimates or multi-view reprojections [9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md][10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md].
3.  **Spectral Fidelity:** Rendered images should match the frequency spectrum of ground truth to prevent high-frequency noise [6130-frequency-regularization.md][9212-fregs.md].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum (amplitude and phase) of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction. It addresses the failure of pixel-wise L1 loss to distinguish valid geometry from noise by pushing Gaussians toward high-frequency regions where detail is missing.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Enhances robustness by applying spatial Gaussian blurring to target images, restricting the spectrum of the learned scene and stabilizing gradients. It differs from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating in the spatial domain with a handcrafted schedule rather than adaptive spectral alignment.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
Modifies the covariance matrix to enforce a "half-Gaussian" geometry (half-ellipsoid cut by a plane aligned with the surface normal) [9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md]. It prevents volumetric floaters by ensuring Gaussians do not extend into empty space behind surfaces, distinct from methods relying solely on external loss terms [9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md].

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Prevents detail loss by decoupling the optimization of Gaussian centers from shape parameters. It freezes scale and rotation to enforce "spatial reshaping" (movement of centers) rather than primitive deformation, using two distinct depth renders (hard/center-based and soft/full-Gaussian) to independently update positions and opacities.

### [FreGS](9212-fregs.md)
Addresses over-reconstruction by applying progressive frequency regularization to Gaussian primitives. It operates directly in the frequency domain to suppress noise while preserving structural details, distinct from spatial smoothing methods.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error as pseudo-labels, providing a stronger constraint than standard depth priors by refining geometry from coarse to fine scales.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Improves surface smoothness by constraining the geometric shape of Gaussians (flattening and normal consistency) rather than just their location. It enforces local surface orientation and flatness, distinct from global depth consistency methods.

#### Wild-GS
- [S1] Aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

#### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] Improves extrapolation beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.
- [S2] Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.
- [S2] $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

#### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Improves geometric fidelity by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

#### MVG-Splatting
- [S4] Employs a depth map to enforce geometric consistency, specifically applying geometric consistency only in the near and far regions of the scene to mitigate under-reconstruction while avoiding over-reconstruction.

#### Shape Energy Constraint
- [S5] An energy term defined as the combination of opacity energy and boundary energy, used to preserve the shape, opacity, and unitized colors (spherical harmonics) of Gaussian radiance fields during deformation.
- [S5] Relying solely on feature energy is insufficient to preserve opacity and unitized colors; gray Gaussians with SH values close to zero appear and their size becomes uncontrolled because they cannot be constrained by feature energy alone.
- [S5] Without $E_{shape}$, the optimization process becomes unstable and artifacts appear; with $E_{shape}$, the process stabilizes and these artifacts are mitigated.

## Background
The problem of "floaters" and unstructured density clouds in 3DGS was identified as a consequence of optimizing solely for photometric loss [S3]. Early works like [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md) relied on densification heuristics but did not explicitly constrain geometry [S3]. Subsequent research introduced frequency regularization [6130-frequency-regularization.md][9212-fregs.md] to address spectral discrepancies [S2], and depth-based constraints [9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md][10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md] to enforce surface consistency [S1]. Methods like [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md) and [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md) further refined the primitive shape itself [9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md][14222-geometry-regularization.md].
