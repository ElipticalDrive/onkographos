# H-2DGS (Height-Field 2D Gaussian Splatting)
Also called: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
H-2DGS is a three-stage pipeline that converts a volumetric 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction into a thin, geometrically sharp, and deformable cortical surface using curved height-field Gaussian primitives initialized on a shared Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) zero level set [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of depth ambiguity and thick, geometrically noisy bands in standard 3DGS under limited view coverage by concentrating opacity along rays and then explicitly representing the resulting surface with surface-oriented patches [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [3D Gaussian Splatting MVP](3410-3d-gaussian-splatting-mvp.md), which maintains a volumetric representation, H-2DGS discards the volumetric Gaussians in favor of an explicit height-field surface that supports postoperative deformation and integration with MRI anatomy [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates in three distinct stages: appearance bootstrap, ray-wise density concentration, and TSDF-guided surface conversion, followed by optional postoperative deformation.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- A set of training images $I_v$ from multiple camera views.
- Camera poses and intrinsics for the training views.
- (Optional) Postoperative MRI-derived depth geometry for deformation.

**Outputs:**
- A set of curved height-field Gaussian primitives defining the surface.
- Each primitive contains height, color, and opacity bases.

### Data Structures
The final representation consists of a set of curved height-field primitives. Each primitive is initialized on the zero level set of a TSDF. The primitive stores:
- **Position**: On the TSDF zero level set.
- **Orientation**: Aligned with the TSDF surface normal.
- **Bases**: Separate parameters for height (geometry), color (appearance), and opacity.

### Stage I: Photometric Bootstrap
**Goal:** Establish appearance and scene support without imposing surface constraints.

1.  Train a conventional 3DGS model using adaptive densification.
2.  Optimize using the standard photometric objective:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda) \|\hat{I}_v - I_v\|_1 + \lambda(1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}_v, I_v)) $$
    where $\lambda = 0.2$, $\hat{I}_v$ is the rendered image, and $I_v$ is the input image [S1, unofficial].
3.  This stage produces a volumetric representation that captures texture but may have thick, ambiguous depth distributions [S1, unofficial].

### Stage II: Ray-wise Density Concentration
**Goal:** Remove depth ambiguity by concentrating opacity into a thin band while preserving photometric accuracy.

1.  **Warm Start:** Run Stage I for 5,000 iterations [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Optimization:** Run for 1,000 iterations with the combined loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{stage2}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{thick}} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{r}}[\mathcal{L}_{\text{thick}}] $$
    where $\lambda_{\text{thick}} = 5.0$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Thickness Loss Calculation:**
    For a camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = o + td$, compute the 1D Gaussian contribution along the ray for each candidate 3D Gaussian $i$:
    $$ \sigma_i(t) = \beta_i \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}a_i(t - m_i)^2\right] $$
    where $\beta_i$ is the amplitude, $a_i$ is the inverse variance, and $m_i$ is the mean position along the ray [S1, unofficial].
    Sum contributions: $\sigma(t) = \sum_i \sigma_i(t)$.
4.  **Discretization:**
    At sample points $\{t_m\}_{m=1}^M$ along the ray:
    $$ p_m = \frac{\sigma(t_m)}{\sum_n \sigma(t_n) + \epsilon} $$
    $$ F_m = \sum_{n=1}^m p_n $$
    where $p_m$ is the normalized density and $F_m$ is the cumulative distribution [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Differentiable Quantile Approximation:**
    For a quantile $q$, compute weights and position:
    $$ w_m(q) = \frac{\exp(-\tau_q|F_m - q|)}{\sum_n \exp(-\tau_q|F_n - q|)} $$
    $$ t_q = \sum_m w_m(q)t_m $$
    where $\tau_q$ is the temperature parameter [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Thickness Metric:**
    Compute effective thickness $\Delta t$ using low and high quantiles ($q_{\text{low}} = 0.01, q_{\text{high}} = 0.99$):
    $$ \Delta t = t_{q_{\text{high}}} - t_{q_{\text{low}}} $$
    The target thickness $\Delta t^*$ is set to zero (normalized thickness) [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{thick}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\max(0, \Delta t - \Delta t^*)}{E} $$
    where $E$ is the scene extent [S1, unofficial].

### Stage III: TSDF-Guided Surface Conversion
**Goal:** Convert the concentrated volumetric density into an explicit, sharp surface.

1.  **Depth Rendering:** Render depth maps from all training cameras using the Stage II model.
2.  **TSDF Fusion:** Fuse the multi-view depth observations into a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF).
    For a voxel center $\mathbf{x}$ and view $v$, compute the signed distance observation:
    $$ \phi_v(\mathbf{x}) = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\hat{D}_v(\pi_v(\mathbf{x})) - d_v(\mathbf{x})}{\mu}, -1, 1\right) $$
    where $\hat{D}_v$ is the rendered depth, $d_v(\mathbf{x})$ is the projected depth from the view's geometry, $\pi_v(\mathbf{x})$ is the projection, and $\mu$ is the truncation threshold [S1, unofficial].
    The fused TSDF $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ is the weighted average of $\phi_v(\mathbf{x})$ across views [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Primitive Initialization:**
    -   Discard the volumetric Gaussians from Stage II.
    -   Initialize curved height-field primitives on the TSDF zero level set ($\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = 0$).
    -   Orient primitives using the TSDF surface normals.
4.  **Neighbor-Transition Optimization:**
    Apply an objective to coordinate overlapping patches, ensuring locally continuous geometry [S1, unofficial].

### Postoperative Deformation (Optional)
**Goal:** Align the preoperative surface with postoperative MRI-derived geometry.

1.  Render depth from the MRI-derived mesh.
2.  Deform the explicit height-field surface toward this target geometry.
3.  Use tracked-color supervision and alternating geometry-appearance updates to preserve photorealistic texture during deformation [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
-   **Limited View Coverage:** Standard 3DGS may produce thick, ambiguous bands under limited views; H-2DGS addresses this via density concentration [S1, unofficial].
-   **Floating Density:** Stage II may leave residual floating density; TSDF fusion aggregates evidence to create a clean surface [S1, unofficial].
-   **Postoperative Deformation:** Deformation must preserve photorealistic texture; alternating geometry-appearance updates and tracked-color supervision are used to prevent texture erosion [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Performance:** H-2DGS achieves a compact representation of 7.97 MB across five real neurosurgical cases [S1, unofficial].
-   **Rendering Speed:** Interactive rendering at 29–42 ms per frame [S1, unofficial].
-   **Strengths:** Sharp, geometrically explicit surface; compact storage; supports postoperative deformation; integrates with MRI and tractography [S1, unofficial].
-   **Limitations:** Requires multi-view depth fusion; TSDF initialization depends on accurate depth rendering from Stage II; deformation may require careful tuning to preserve texture [S1, unofficial].
-   **When to Use:** Neurosurgical surface reconstruction where sharp, deformable surfaces are needed for integration with MRI and tractography, especially under limited view coverage [S1, unofficial].
-   **When Not to Use:** When a volumetric representation is required, or when real-time deformation is not needed and volumetric efficiency is prioritized over surface sharpness [S1, unofficial].

## Background
H-2DGS was introduced to address the limitations of conventional 3D Gaussian Splatting in neurosurgical visualization, where limited view coverage leads to geometric ambiguity. It builds on [3D Gaussian Splatting MVP](3410-3d-gaussian-splatting-mvp.md) by converting the volumetric representation into an explicit surface. The method was developed for neurosurgical planning and intraoperative navigation, where integration with MRI and tractography is critical [S1, unofficial].
