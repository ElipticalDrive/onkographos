# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM, Radiance Field SLAM

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) integrates simultaneous localization and mapping by optimizing a radiance field representation—typically explicit 3D Gaussians or implicit neural fields—alongside camera poses to reconstruct a scene and estimate trajectory in real-time. Unlike traditional bundle adjustment which optimizes sparse points, these methods optimize dense volumetric or splat-based representations, leveraging the differentiable rendering of 3DGS for real-time feedback [S1][S2, unofficial]. This family is distinguished from standard offline reconstruction by its incremental, online nature and from NeRF-based SLAM by the superior reconstruction quality and rendering speed enabled by explicit geometric primitives [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS-based SLAM involves a coupled optimization loop between camera pose estimation (tracking) and scene representation (mapping).

1.  **Representation:** The scene is represented as a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}$, where each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by a mean position $\mu_i$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (parameterized by scale $S$ and rotation $R$), an opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent color $c_i$ [S2, unofficial]. Alternatively, implicit neural fields (NeRFs) or hybrid representations (triplanes, neural points) may be used [S3].
2.  **Tracking (Pose Optimization):** Camera poses $T \in SE(3)$ are optimized by minimizing the photometric difference between the rendered image $\hat{C}$ and the input image $C$. This is often done via gradient descent on the pose parameters, using the rendered depth $\hat{D}$ and color $\hat{C}$ [S5].
3.  **Mapping (Scene Optimization):** The Gaussian parameters (or neural field weights) are updated to minimize the rendering loss (e.g., L1 + SSIM) against ground truth images [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Densification:** To maintain reconstruction quality, new Gaussians are added (cloning/splitting) in regions with high reprojection error or low opacity, and redundant Gaussians are pruned [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Loop Closure:** For long-term consistency, loop detection identifies revisited areas, triggering global bundle adjustment or pose graph optimization to correct drift [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct 3D scenes or radiance fields, typically for the purpose of 3D editing, style transfer, or multi-scene fusion. Unlike standard SLAM mapping which aligns a scene to a camera trajectory, these strategies align one static scene representation to another. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Semantic-aware mapping](12053-semantic-aware-mapping.md) by their specific focus on cross-scene pixel-to-pixel or region-to-region correspondence rather than intra-scene semantic labeling, and from [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md) by their role as a post-processing or alignment step rather than the scene representation itself.

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation methods map 3D scene geometry and appearance into a continuous volumetric function, typically implemented as a neural network, to synthesize novel views from sparse input images. Unlike explicit representations that store discrete geometric primitives, NeRF implicitly encodes density and color, solving the problem of view-dependent rendering without requiring explicit mesh extraction. While standard NeRF relies on volumetric ray marching, variants like Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (INGP) optimize training speed via hash-based encoding, and Photon Field Networks replace expensive global illumination tracing with neural inference.

### CG-SLAM
**Definition:** CG-SLAM improves reconstruction quality by introducing an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1].

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
**Definition:** This approach combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting [S2, unofficial].
**Comparison:** On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), ORB-SLAM3 achieves an ATE RMSE of 0.052 m, while the combined approach yields 3.190 m. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences. Although ORB-SLAM achieves the best ATE, there is no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. The unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S2, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
**Definition:** CP-SLAM is a neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure [S3].
**Steps:** It uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization. It employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly. The system integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S3].

### MNE-SLAM
**Definition:** MNE-SLAM is the first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange [S3].
**Steps:** It uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module. It introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S3].

### HERO-SLAM
**Definition:** HERO-SLAM is a SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings [S3].
**Steps:** It uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S3].

### PIN-SLAM
**Definition:** PIN-SLAM is a globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation [S3].
**Steps:** It replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure. Mapping alternates with odometry. The mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders. Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization. A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates. Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S3].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M(c)$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$ [S4, unofficial].
2.  **Global Policy:** Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$ [S4, unofficial].
3.  **Local Policy:** Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
4.  **Action:** Move agent to $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
5.  **Observation:** Obtain new image observation $I_t$ [S4, unofficial].
6.  **Update Database:** Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$ [S4, unofficial].
7.  **Mapping Optimization:** Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$ [S4, unofficial].
8.  **Stop Check:** Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold [S4, unofficial].
9.  **Fine Stage:** After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M(f)$ with trained coarse model $M_T(c)$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S4, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
**Definition:** A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison [S5].
**Steps:**
1.  Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$.
2.  Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity.
3.  Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$.
4.  Discard the L channel.
5.  Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences [S5].
**Formula:** $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth [S5].
**Parameters:** $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss. $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S5].

### Active Mapping with 3D Gaussian Splatting
**Steps:** Reconstruct environment using 3D Gaussians (e.g., via SplaTAM). Propose path candidates using frontier-based exploration. Sample cameras along the path concerning the mobility of the agent. Compute EIG for each path and choose the one with the highest EIG to continue exploration [S6].
**Formula:** $P_j = \arg \max_{P_j} \text{tr} \left( \sum_{x_{acq_i} \in P_j} H''[y_{i\_acq}|x_{acq_i}, w^*] \right) H''[w^*|D_{train}]^{-1}$ [S6].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) was introduced as a real-time explicit scene representation [S2, unofficial]. Its integration into SLAM systems began with methods like SplaTAM, which combined 3DGS with a traditional SLAM pipeline for real-time joint tracking and mapping [S2, unofficial]. Subsequent works explored hybrid neural fields (NeRFs, PointNeRF) for SLAM, such as CP-SLAM and PIN-SLAM, which offer different trade-offs between memory, accuracy, and loop closure capabilities [S3]. The field has expanded to include multi-agent systems (MNE-SLAM), active mapping strategies (ActiveRMAP, FisherRF), and robust tracking under lighting changes (Lightness-Decoupled Tracking) [S4, unofficial][S5][S6].
