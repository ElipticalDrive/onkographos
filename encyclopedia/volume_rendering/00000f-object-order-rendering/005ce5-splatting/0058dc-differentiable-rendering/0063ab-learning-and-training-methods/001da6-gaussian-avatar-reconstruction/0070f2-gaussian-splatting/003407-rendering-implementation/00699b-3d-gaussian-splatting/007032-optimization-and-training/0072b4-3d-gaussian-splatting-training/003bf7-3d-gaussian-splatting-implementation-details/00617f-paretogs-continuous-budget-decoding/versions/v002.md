# ParetoGS Continuous-Budget Decoding

Also called: ParetoGS budget decoding, ParetoGS residual atlas encoding

## Overview
ParetoGS Continuous-Budget Decoding is a runtime procedure that returns parameters for exactly $K$ Gaussians at any valid integer budget by interpolating between nested adapted anchors using a compressed residual atlas and calibrated exponents [S1, unofficial]. It differs from static compression methods like [Data Compression and Storage Optimization](12943-data-compression-and-storage-optimization.md) by enabling smooth, continuous trade-offs between quality and performance without retraining, and from discrete pruning methods like [Pruning and Density Control](16215-pruning-and-density-control.md) by preserving the full geometric fidelity of the original model through residual interpolation rather than hard selection [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of nested adapted anchors (including a Full model), the global nested member ordering, and pre-calibrated interval exponents $(p, q)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Standard $K$-Gaussian parameters (position, log scale, quaternion, opacity logit, DC color, higher-order SH coefficients) for a specified integer budget $K$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Global Nested Ordering:** A permutation of Gaussian IDs derived from concatenating entry sequences of contracted anchors. Full-model parameters are reordered to match this sequence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Compressed Residual Atlas:** Stores orthogonal decompositions of non-full anchor residuals relative to the Full model.
    *   **Layout:** Stacked by member layer and parameter field [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Precision:** Quantization scales, anchor combination coefficients, and SH channel bases are stored in FP16 [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Quantization:** Symmetric INT8 quantization applied independently to each channel within blocks of 64 Gaussian rows [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interval Exponents:** For each interval between neighboring anchors, a pair $(p, q)$ is stored [S1, unofficial].
*   **Member-Order Metadata:** Tracks the entry sequence of Gaussians as budget increases [S1, unofficial].
*   **Runtime Cache:** By default, caches the two most recently used endpoints [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Construct Global Nested Ordering:**
    *   When contracting an upper anchor $U$ to a lower anchor $L$, record the IDs of deleted Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
    *   Reverse each deletion trace to determine the order in which members enter as budget increases [S1, unofficial].
    *   Starting with the smallest anchor, concatenate these entry sequences to form the global nested ordering [S1, unofficial].
    *   Reorder full-model parameters and align all anchors to this ordering by ID [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute and Encode Residuals:**
    *   Compute residuals of non-full anchors relative to the Full model in raw parameter coordinates (position, log scale, quaternion, opacity logit, DC color, higher-order SH coefficients) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Convert non-full anchor residuals to FP16 and stack them by member layer and parameter field [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform orthogonal decomposition along the anchor-state dimension for each layer [S1, unofficial].
    *   Retain all available components along the state dimension (maximum rank 10) [S1, unofficial].
    *   For the 45 higher-order SH channels, use a rank-36 channel subspace, while ensuring decoding outputs all 45 channels [S1, unofficial].
    *   Apply symmetric INT8 quantization independently to each channel within blocks of 64 Gaussian rows using the following formulas [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ s_{bc} = \frac{\max_{i \in b} |x_{ic}|}{127} $$
        $$ q_{ic} = \text{clip}\left(\text{round}\left(\frac{x_{ic}}{s_{bc}}\right), -127, 127\right) $$
        $$ \hat{x}_{bic} = q_{ic} s_{bc} $$
        where $b$ is the block of 64 rows, $c$ is the channel, $x_{ic}$ is the input value, $s_{bc}$ is the scale, and $q_{ic}$ is the quantized value. If a block is all-zero, the scale $s_{bc}$ defaults to 1 [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Calibrate Interval Exponents:**
    *   For each neighboring anchor pair, enumerate shared-state exponent $p$ and entering-member gate exponent $q$ from predefined discrete sets [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ P = \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6\} $$
        $$ Q = \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3\} $$
    *   At rounded budgets corresponding to normalized progress $t \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$, compare all 72 $(p, q)$ pairs on up to ten training views [S1, unofficial].
    *   Views are midpoint selections from equally partitioned intervals of the training-camera list [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate the reconstruction loss $\ell_{v,h}$ for view $v$ at the lower endpoint, three intermediate budgets, and upper endpoint, indexed by $h=0, \dots, 4$, using image loss $\ell = 0.8 L1 + 0.2 (1 - SSIM)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute loss delta, delta variance, and negative delta mean for each view [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ d_{v,h} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{k=1}^{3} \frac{\ell_{v,k} - \ell_{v,k+1}}{t_{k+1} - t_k} $$
        $$ s_v = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{h=0}^{3} |d_{v,h} - (\ell_{v,0} - \ell_{v,4})| $$
        $$ m_v = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{h=0}^{3} \max(-d_{v,h}, 0) $$
    *   Define $Q^+$ as the mean of the largest $\lceil V/4 \rceil$ values in a set [S1, unofficial].
    *   Minimize the calibration objective [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ J(p, q) = \frac{1}{3V} \sum_{v=1}^{V} \sum_{h=1}^{3} \ell_{v,h} + Q^+(\{m_v\}) + 0.25 Q^+(\{s_v\}) $$
    *   Store the minimizing $p$ and $q$ for each interval. Exact ties favor larger $p$, then larger $q$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Pitfall:** Calibration must not update Gaussian parameters or use test views to avoid overfitting and ensure deployment stability [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Decode at Runtime for Budget $K$:**
    *   If $K$ matches an exact anchor, restore that anchor directly [S1, unofficial].
    *   If $K=N$ (full count), use the Full model [S1, unofficial].
    *   For other counts, identify the two neighboring anchors between which $K$ falls [S1, unofficial].
    *   Interpolate shared members in raw parameter coordinates using the shared-state exponent $p$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For entering members, use their upper-anchor state and multiply their activated opacity by $t^q$, where $t$ is the normalized progress within the interval [S1, unofficial].
    *   Clip scaled opacity to $[10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}]$, convert back to logit, and output standard $K$-Gaussian parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math
import numpy as np

def decode_pareto_gs(K, full_model, anchors, ordering, atlas, exponents):
    """
    Decode ParetoGS parameters for budget K.
    
    Args:
        K: Integer budget (number of Gaussians).
        full_model: Array of parameters for the full model, reordered by global_nested_ordering.
        anchors: List of adapted anchors, each containing parameters for a subset of Gaussians.
        ordering: Global nested ordering of Gaussian IDs.
        atlas: Compressed residual atlas containing orthogonal decompositions.
        exponents: Dictionary mapping anchor intervals to (p, q) tuples.
        
    Returns:
        Parameters for exactly K Gaussians.
    """
    N = len(ordering)
    
    # Case 1: Exact anchor match
    for anchor in anchors:
        if anchor.count == K:
            return anchor.parameters[ordering]
            
    # Case 2: Full model
    if K == N:
        return full_model[ordering]
        
    # Case 3: Interpolation between neighboring anchors
    # Find the two anchors between which K falls based on their counts
    # Assuming anchors are sorted by count
    lower_anchor = None
    upper_anchor = None
    for i in range(len(anchors) - 1):
        if anchors[i].count < K < anchors[i+1].count:
            lower_anchor = anchors[i]
            upper_anchor = anchors[i+1]
            break
            
    if lower_anchor is None or upper_anchor is None:
        raise ValueError("K does not fall within any anchor interval")
        
    # Get exponents for this interval
    p, q = exponents[(lower_anchor.id, upper_anchor.id)]
    
    # Calculate normalized progress t
    t = (K - lower_anchor.count) / (upper_anchor.count - lower_anchor.count)
    
    # Identify shared and entering members
    lower_ids = set(lower_anchor.member_ids)
    upper_ids = set(upper_anchor.member_ids)
    
    shared_ids = lower_ids & upper_ids
    entering_ids = upper_ids - lower_ids
    
    # Initialize output parameters
    output_params = {}
    
    # Parameters for shared members: interpolate in raw coordinates
    # Note: The exact interpolation formula for shared members is not provided in the sources [G].
    # Standard linear interpolation is used here as a placeholder for raw coordinate interpolation.
    for gid in shared_ids:
        lower_p = lower_anchor.parameters[gid]
        upper_p = upper_anchor.parameters[gid]
        weight = t ** p
        interpolated_p = lower_p + weight * (upper_p - lower_p)
        output_params[gid] = interpolated_p
        
    # Parameters for entering members: use upper-anchor state, scale opacity by t^q
    for gid in entering_ids:
        upper_p = upper_anchor.parameters[gid].copy()
        
        # Opacity is the 4th component (index 3) in the 59 scalar components
        opacity_logit = upper_p[3]
        opacity = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-opacity_logit))
        
        # Scale opacity
        scaled_opacity = opacity * (t ** q)
        
        # Clip opacity
        scaled_opacity = max(1e-6, min(1 - 1e-6, scaled_opacity))
        
        # Convert back to logit
        scaled_opacity_logit = math.log(scaled_opacity / (1 - scaled_opacity))
        
        # Update the opacity logit in the parameter vector
        new_p = upper_p.copy()
        new_p[3] = scaled_opacity_logit
        
        output_params[gid] = new_p
        
    # Assemble the final parameter array in the global ordering
    final_params = np.zeros((K, 59))
    for i, gid in enumerate(ordering[:K]):
        final_params[i] = output_params[gid]
        
    return final_params
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Scalar components per Gaussian:** 59 at SH degree 3 (position, log scale, quaternion, opacity logit, DC color, higher-order SH coefficients) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Quantization block size:** 64 Gaussian rows per channel [S1, unofficial].
*   **All-zero block scale fallback:** 1 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision for quantization scales, anchor combination coefficients, and SH channel bases:** FP16 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Image loss composition:** $0.8 L1 + 0.2 (1 - SSIM)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Opacity clip range:** $[10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}]$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Calibration Overfitting:** Calibration must not update Gaussian parameters or use test views to avoid overfitting and ensure deployment stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **No Per-Gaussian Progress Correction:** Only interval-level exponents are cached; no per-Gaussian progress correction is stored [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Quantization scales and coefficients are FP16; INT8 quantization is used for the atlas data [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Decoding is $O(K)$ for assembling the $K$ Gaussian parameters, assuming constant-time lookup of anchors and exponents. Calibration is $O(V \cdot |P| \cdot |Q| \cdot H)$, where $V$ is the number of views, $|P|$ and $|Q|$ are the sizes of the exponent sets, and $H$ is the number of intervals [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The compressed residual atlas reduces storage by using orthogonal decomposition (rank 10 for state, rank 36 for SH channels) and INT8 quantization [S1, unofficial]. The exact memory savings depend on the number of anchors and the compression ratio of the atlas [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** The method achieves high-quality continuous-budget rendering, with PSNR results comparable to full models at lower budgets [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables smooth quality-performance trade-offs without retraining; preserves geometric fidelity through residual interpolation; efficient runtime decoding [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires pre-calibration; calibration must be done carefully to avoid overfitting; no per-Gaussian progress correction [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When continuous budget control is needed for deployment on devices with varying capabilities or when dynamic quality adjustment is required [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When static compression is sufficient, or when per-Gaussian adaptive quality is needed [S1, unofficial].

## Background
ParetoGS Continuous-Budget Decoding was introduced in the paper "ParetoGS: High-Quality Continuous-Budget 3D Gaussian Splatting with Sparse Adapted Anchors" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of adapted anchors and residual encoding to provide a flexible rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial].
