# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the optimization objective and densification strategy for **MeshGS** [S1], a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It solves the problem of geometry misalignment and excessive primitive expansion in standard 3DGS by introducing a hybrid representation: **tightly-bound** splats ($G^t$) constrained to the mesh and **loosely-bound** splats ($G^l$) following standard 3DGS rules. It sets itself apart from its siblings by explicitly coupling the training loss and densification logic to a pre-existing mesh geometry, using projection and normal consistency losses to enforce surface adherence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Set of Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G}$, partitioned into tightly-bound $G^t$ and loosely-bound $G^l$.
*   A mesh $M$ defined by faces $F$, where each face $f_j$ has vertices $v_i$ and normal $n_f$.
*   Ground truth images $I_i$ and rendered images $\hat{I}_i$.
*   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{img}=0.001$, $\lambda_{nc}=0.1$, $\lambda_{min}=0.1$, $\lambda_{max}=10$, $\lambda_{proj}=50$, $\rho=0.1$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients) and updated mesh alignment.

**Data Structures:**
*   $G^t$: Set of Gaussians where the distance to the mesh surface is $\le d_{th}$ (default $0.01$) [S1].
*   $G^l$: Set of Gaussians where the distance to the mesh surface is $> d_{th}$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialization and Partitioning:**
    Initialize Gaussians. Classify each Gaussian $i$ into $G^t$ or $G^l$ based on the distance to the nearest point on the mesh surface. If distance $\le 0.01$, it is tightly-bound ($G^t$); otherwise, it is loosely-bound ($G^l$) [S1].

2.  **Loss Computation:**
    Compute the total loss $L$ as the sum of image, normal consistency, scale regularization, and projection losses [S1]:
    $$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj} $$

    *   **Image Loss ($L_{img}$):** Combines L1 and D-SSIM [S1]:
        $$ L_{img} = (1-\lambda_{img})L_{1}(\hat{I}_{i},I_{i}) + \lambda_{img}SSIM(\hat{I}_{i}, I_{i}) $$
        Where $\hat{I}_i$ is the rendered image, $I_i$ is the ground truth, and $\lambda_{img}=0.001$ [S1].

    *   **Normal Consistency Loss ($L_{nc}$):** Aligns Gaussian normals with mesh face normals [S1].
        First, estimate the normal $n_i$ for each Gaussian in $G^t$:
        1.  Identify the minimum scale axis among scale parameters $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$.
        2.  Apply the rotation matrix $R_i$ to transform this axis into world coordinates to obtain $n_i$ [S1].
        
        Then compute:
        $$ L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^{t}, f \in F} (1 - n_{i} \cdot n_{f}) $$
        Where $n_f$ is the normal of the corresponding mesh face $f$ [S1].

    *   **Scale Regularization Loss ($L_{scale}$):** Prevents $G^t$ from expanding excessively [S1]:
        $$ L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^{t}}(\lambda_{min}|\text{min}(s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3})| + \lambda_{max}|\text{max}(s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3}) - \rho|) $$
        Where $\lambda_{min}=0.1$, $\lambda_{max}=10$, and $\rho=0.1$ [S1].

    *   **Projection Loss ($L_{proj}$):** Keeps $G^t$ centers close to the mesh surface [S1].
        Project the Gaussian center $P_i$ to the closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$. The projection is calculated as:
        $$ s_{i} = P_{i} + ((P_{i} - v_{i}) \cdot f_{j}) \cdot f_{j} $$
        *(Note: The source formula uses $f_j$ in the dot product and multiplication, implying $f_j$ is treated as a vector in this context, likely the face normal or a specific geometric vector. Standard projection to a plane uses the normal. Given the text "closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$", and the formula structure, this appears to be a simplified projection onto the plane of the face. We implement exactly as cited [S1].)*
        
        Then:
        $$ L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^{t}}\parallel s_{i} - P_{i} \parallel _{2} $$
        Where $\lambda_{proj}=50$ [S1].

3.  **Densification (Splitting and Cloning):**
    Perform densification every 500 iterations [S1]. The strategy differs for $G^t$ and $G^l$ [S1]:

    *   **For Loosely-bound Splats ($G^l$):**
        Follow the original 3DGS densification strategy (gradient-based splitting and cloning) [S1].

    *   **For Tightly-bound Splats ($G^t$):**
        1.  Identify the mesh triangle $f_j$ closest to the Gaussian.
        2.  **Cloning:** Sample a new point from the triangle $f_j$. Copy the original Gaussian's attributes (position, rotation, opacity, SH) to the new Gaussian, but initialize its position at the sampled point [S1].
        3.  **Splitting:** Sample a new point from the triangle $f_j$. Initialize the new Gaussian's position at the sampled point. Divide the original Gaussian's scale by a factor of 1.6 [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_loss(G_t, G_l, mesh, images, rendered_images, params):
    # 1. Image Loss (applied to all Gaussians)
    l1_loss = L1(rendered_images, images)
    ssim_loss = SSIM(rendered_images, images)
    L_img = (1 - params.lambda_img) * l1_loss + params.lambda_img * ssim_loss
    
    # 2. Normal Consistency Loss (only for G_t)
    L_nc = 0
    for g in G_t:
        # Estimate normal: min scale axis rotated by R
        min_scale_idx = argmin(g.scale)
        n_i = rotate_axis(g.rotation, min_scale_idx)
        
        # Find corresponding mesh face f
        f = mesh.get_closest_face(g.position)
        n_f = f.normal
        
        L_nc += (1 - dot(n_i, n_f))
    L_nc *= params.lambda_nc

    # 3. Scale Regularization Loss (only for G_t)
    L_scale = 0
    for g in G_t:
        s = g.scale
        min_s = min(s)
        max_s = max(s)
        L_scale += params.lambda_min * abs(min_s) + params.lambda_max * abs(max_s - params.rho)

    # 4. Projection Loss (only for G_t)
    L_proj = 0
    for g in G_t:
        P_i = g.position
        f = mesh.get_closest_face(P_i)
        v_i = f.vertices[0] # Assuming v_i is a vertex of the face
        f_j = f.normal # Interpreting f_j as the face normal for projection plane
        
        # Projection formula from source
        s_i = P_i + dot(P_i - v_i, f_j) * f_j
        L_proj += norm(s_i - P_i)
    L_proj *= params.lambda_proj

    return L_img + L_nc + L_scale + L_proj

def densify(G_t, G_l, mesh, params):
    # Densify G_l using standard 3DGS rules
    G_l = densify_standard_3dgs(G_l, params)
    
    # Densify G_t using mesh-aligned sampling
    new_G_t = []
    for g in G_t:
        f = mesh.get_closest_face(g.position)
        
        # Cloning: Copy attributes, new position from sampling
        cloned = copy_gaussian(g)
        cloned.position = sample_point_from_triangle(f)
        new_G_t.append(cloned)
        
        # Splitting: New position from sampling, scale / 1.6
        split = copy_gaussian(g)
        split.position = sample_point_from_triangle(f)
        split.scale = g.scale / 1.6
        new_G_t.append(split)
        
    return G_l, new_G_t
```

## Subtopics

### [Budget-Centered Training](9682-budget-centered-training.md)
Constrains primitive count to a target budget $N^*$ using a redundancy map, eliminating the need for gradient-based pruning.

### [Coarse-to-fine multi-resolution densification](15569-coarse-to-fine-multi-resolution-densification.md)
Allocates optimization iterations across image resolutions based on frequency energy, decoupling global coverage from local refinement.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training runs for 30,000 iterations [S1]. The additional cost comes from mesh proximity queries and normal estimation for $G^t$ at every iteration, and densification every 500 iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the mesh geometry $M$ and maintaining the partition between $G^t$ and $G^l$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Ensures tight alignment with mesh surfaces, preventing excessive expansion of Gaussels into empty space [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the input mesh. The projection formula in the source is geometrically ambiguous (using $f_j$ as both face identifier and vector), which may require careful implementation [S1].
*   **When to use:** When an explicit mesh is available and surface-aligned rendering is required.
*   **When not to use:** For scenes without a reliable mesh or when standard 3DGS performance is sufficient.

## Background
Originated in **MeshGS: Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting for High-Quality Rendering** [S1]. It extends 3DGS [22] by integrating a hybrid densification strategy and a mesh-aware loss function to improve geometry reconstruction quality.
