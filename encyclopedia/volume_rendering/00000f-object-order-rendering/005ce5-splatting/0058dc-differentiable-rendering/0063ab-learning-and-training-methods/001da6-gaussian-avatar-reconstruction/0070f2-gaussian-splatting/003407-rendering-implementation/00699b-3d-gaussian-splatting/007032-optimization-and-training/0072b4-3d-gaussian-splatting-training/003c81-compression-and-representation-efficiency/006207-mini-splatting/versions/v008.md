# Mini-Splatting

Also called: Constrained Gaussian Representation, Hard-constrained 3DGS, Mixed Integer 3DGS, Representing Scenes with a Constrained Number of Gaussians

## Overview
Mini-Splatting is a resource-efficient training and rendering method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that explicitly constrains the total number of Gaussians to a hard limit, addressing the spatial redundancy and inefficient distribution inherent in vanilla 3DGS [S2]. Unlike sibling methods like [C3DGS](9184-c3dgs.md) which focus on voxel-based pruning or [CompGS](18575-compgs.md) which relies on attribute quantization, Mini-Splatting pivots from direct simplification to spatial reorganization, leveraging both screen-space and world-space information to densify around objects and remove redundant overlaps [S2]. It achieves this by stacking densification and simplification passes to maintain a uniform spatial distribution, resulting in significantly reduced memory footprint and faster training times while maintaining comparable or superior visual fidelity [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of initial 3D Gaussians derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points, along with a sequence of training images [G].
*   **Output:** A constrained set of Gaussians with optimized position $\mathbf{p}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{q}_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{i, lm}$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the standard 3DGS attribute set for each Gaussian $i$:
*   Position: $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation: $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (normalized quaternion)
*   Scale: $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Opacity: $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   SH Coefficients: $\mathbf{c}_{i, lm}$ [G]
*   Importance Score: Scalar value used for selection [S3]

**Parameters:**
*   Total Optimization Steps: 30,000 [S2].
*   Densification Stop Iteration: 15,000 [S2].
*   Simplification Iterations: 15,000 and 20,000 [S2].
*   SH Level: Disabled (Degree 0) during densification; increased after simplification [S2].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The optimization process is divided into densification and simplification phases, stacked to reorganize the spatial distribution of Gaussians [S2].

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians from SfM points. Set total optimization steps to 30,000 [S2].
2.  **Densification Phase (Iterations $1$ to $15K$):**
    *   **Blur Split:** Detect blurring areas in the rendered image and split Gaussians located in those regions to increase local density [S2].
        *   *Note:* View-dependent colors (SH coefficients) are disabled during this phase as they barely enhance densification and consume resources [S2].
    *   **Depth Reinitialization:** Use merged depth points to reinitialize scene geometry and Gaussian positions [S2].
        *   Calculate depth points from the mid-point of ray intersections with Gaussian ellipsoids [S4].
        *   Reposition Gaussians based on these newly generated depth points to ensure geometric accuracy and avoid artifacts from alpha blending [S4].
    *   **Constraint:** The total number of Gaussians is kept constant by pruning the least important Gaussians and splitting the most important ones [S1, unofficial]. Importance is determined by an importance score calculation [S3].
3.  **Simplification Phase (Iterations $15K$ and $20K$):**
    *   **Intersection Preserving:** Retain only Gaussians that have the maximal impact on the rendered image, discarding redundant overlaps [S2].
    *   **Sampling:** Sample points to preserve both the underlying geometric structure and the original rendering quality, reducing complexity [S2].
    *   **SH Update:** Increase the SH level (enable view-dependent colors) after simplification (at iteration 15K) [S2].
4.  **Final Optimization:** Continue optimization until the total step count with the constrained set [S2].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def mini_splatting_training(initial_gaussians, images, N_max, T_total=30000):
    """
    Implements the Mini-Splatting optimization pipeline.
    """
    gaussians = initial_gaussians
    gaussians.sh_degree = 0  # Disable SH during densification [S2]

    for t in range(1, T_total + 1):
        # Standard optimization step [G]
        image = sample_random_image(images)
        rendered_image, depth_map, gaussian_indices = render_gaussians(gaussians, image.camera)
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, image.gt)
        gradients = compute_gradients(loss, gaussians)
        update_gaussians(gaussians, gradients)

        if t <= 15000:
            # Densification Phase [S2]
            
            # Blur Split: Detect blurring areas and split Gaussians [S2]
            # [G] Compute blur mask from rendered image gradients
            blur_mask = compute_blur_mask(rendered_image)
            split_candidates = [g for g in gaussians if g.id in blur_mask]
            for g in split_candidates:
                # [S2] Split high-importance Gaussians in blurred regions
                split_gaussian(g)

            # Depth Reinitialization: Reposition based on depth mid-points [S4]
            # [S4] Calculate mid-point of ray intersection with Gaussian ellipsoids
            depth_points = compute_depth_midpoints(gaussians, image.camera)
            # [S4] Reposition Gaussians based on newly generated depth points
            reposition_gaussians(gaussians, depth_points)

            # Maintain N_max: Prune least important, split most important [S1, unofficial]
            # [S1, unofficial] Keep total count constant at N_max
            gaussians = maintain_count(gaussians, N_max)

        elif t in [15000, 20000]:
            # Simplification Phase [S2]
            
            # Intersection Preserving: Retain maximal impact Gaussians [S2]
            # [S2] Compute rendering impact for each Gaussian
            impact_scores = compute_rendering_impact(gaussians, images)
            # [S2] Retain Gaussians with maximal impact
            gaussians = retain_top_impact(gaussians, impact_scores, N_max)

            # Sampling: Preserve geometric structure [S2]
            # [S2] Sample points to preserve geometric structure and rendering quality
            gaussians = sample_gaussians(gaussians, N_max)

            # Enable SH after simplification [S2]
            gaussians.sh_degree = 1

    return gaussians
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Popping Artifacts:** Using fewer Gaussians results in larger Gaussians on average, which increases the prevalence of noticeable popping artifacts [S5].
*   **SH Redundancy:** Incorporating view-dependent colors during densification barely enhances quality but consumes resources; thus, they are disabled until after simplification [S2].
*   **Depth Estimation:** Depth reinitialization relies on initial training to estimate depth, requiring a few iterations to obtain accurate information [S4].

## Subtopics

### Mini-Splatting-D
A quality-prioritized variant of Mini-Splatting that omits the simplification process to maximize rendering quality [S2]. It retains the densification phase (Blur Split and Depth Reinitialization) but skips the Intersection Preserving and Sampling steps [S2]. This results in a higher Gaussian count (~4.69M vs ~0.49M for standard Mini-Splatting) but achieves superior SSIM and LPIPS metrics, surpassing Zip-NeRF on Mip-NeRF360 Outdoor [S2].

### Mini-Splatting-C
A storage-compression variant designed for scenarios requiring reduced file size [S2]. It is based on the Mini-Splatting pipeline but applies transform coding and standard zip compression to attributes (scales, SH coefficients) after training, storing only Gaussian centers as float32 [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Footprint:** Significantly reduced. On Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor), Mini-Splatting uses 0.49M Gaussians compared to 3.36M for vanilla 3DGS, a ~7x reduction [S2]. Peak GPU Memory during training is 2.61GB vs 7.45GB for 3DGS [S2]. Peak Rendering Memory is 0.40GB vs 2.79GB [S2].
*   **Training Time:** Faster. 17m56s vs 30m8s for 3DGS on Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor) [S2]. Mini-Splatting-D takes 31m48s [S2].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Higher FPS. 410 FPS vs 98 FPS for 3DGS on Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor) [S2]. Mini-Splatting-D achieves 83 FPS [S2].
*   **Visual Quality:** Comparable or superior. On Mip-NeRF360 (Outdoor), Mini-Splatting achieves SSIM 0.822 (vs 0.815), PSNR 27.34 (vs 27.21), LPIPS 0.217 (vs 0.214) [S2]. Mini-Splatting-D achieves SSIM 0.831, PSNR 27.51, LPIPS 0.176 [S2]. On MipNeRF360 generally, Mini-Splatting achieves SSIM 0.763, PSNR 25.83, LPIPS 0.273, with a model size of 18.2 MB [S3].
*   **Scaling:** The method scales well to resource-constrained environments, enabling training on consumer-grade GPUs (e.g., GTX 1060 6G) [S2].
*   **Limitations:** The hard constraint on Gaussian count may lead to larger average Gaussian sizes, increasing popping artifacts [S5]. The method requires careful tuning of the densification/simplification schedule [S2].

## Background
Mini-Splatting was introduced by Guangchi Fang and Bing Wang from the Spatial Intelligence Group at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University [S2]. The method addresses the "overlapping" and "under-reconstruction" phenomena observed in vanilla 3DGS, where Gaussians are not uniformly distributed around object surfaces [S2]. It builds on the principles of 3D Gaussian Splatting but introduces a mixed-integer optimization formulation to explicitly bound the Gaussian count [S1, unofficial].
