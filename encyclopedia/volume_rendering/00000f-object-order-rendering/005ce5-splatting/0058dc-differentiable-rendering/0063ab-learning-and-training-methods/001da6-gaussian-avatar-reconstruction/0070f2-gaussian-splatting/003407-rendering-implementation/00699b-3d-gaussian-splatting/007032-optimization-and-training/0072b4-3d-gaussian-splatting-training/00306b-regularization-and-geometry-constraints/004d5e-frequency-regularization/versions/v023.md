# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which results in small gradients in over-reconstructed regions and prevents effective densification, FR increases pixel gradients in these regions by explicitly disentangling frequency components, thereby pushing Gaussians toward high-frequency details [S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, Ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, where $H$ and $W$ are height and width, and $C$ is the number of channels (typically 3 for RGB).
- **Outputs:** A scalar regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{FR}$ used to update Gaussian parameters (centers $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors) via gradient descent.

**Data Structures:**
- Complex frequency values $F(u, v)$ and $\hat{F}(u, v)$ for each frequency coordinate $(u, v)$.
- Amplitude values $|F(u, v)|$ and $|\hat{F}(u, v)|$.
- Phase values $\angle F(u, v)$ and $\angle \hat{F}(u, v)$.

**Steps:**
1. **Compute 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT):** Convert the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and ground truth image $I$ into their frequency representations $\hat{F}$ and $F$. For a single channel, the DFT is computed as:
   $$ F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)} $$
   where $(x, y)$ are image coordinates, $(u, v)$ are frequency coordinates, $I(x, y)$ is the pixel value, and $F(u, v)$ is the complex frequency value [S2]. This is repeated for all channels.

2. **Compute Amplitude:** Calculate the amplitude of the frequency components for both the rendered and ground truth images:
   $$ |F(u, v)| = \sqrt{\text{Re}(u, v)^2 + \text{Im}(u, v)^2} $$
   $$ |\hat{F}(u, v)| = \sqrt{\hat{\text{Re}}(u, v)^2 + \hat{\text{Im}}(u, v)^2} $$
   where $\text{Re}$ and $\text{Im}$ are the real and imaginary components of $F(u, v)$ [S2].

3. **Compute Phase:** Calculate the phase of the frequency components:
   $$ \angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}(u, v)}{\text{Re}(u, v)}\right) $$
   $$ \angle \hat{F}(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{\hat{\text{Im}}(u, v)}{\hat{\text{Re}}(u, v)}\right) $$
   where the arctangent function computes the angle in radians [S2].

4. **Compute Amplitude Discrepancy ($d_a$):** Calculate the root mean square error between the amplitudes of the ground truth and rendered frequency spectra:
   $$ d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2} $$
   Note: The summation indices in the source [S2] use $(x,y)$ but the formula depends on frequency coordinates $(u,v)$. The standard interpretation is summing over all frequency coordinates $(u,v)$ from $0$ to $H-1$ and $0$ to $W-1$ respectively [S2].

5. **Compute Phase Discrepancy ($d_p$):** Calculate the root mean square error between the phases of the ground truth and rendered frequency spectra:
   $$ d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2} $$
   Similarly, this sums over all frequency coordinates [S2].

6. **Combine Discrepancies:** The total frequency regularization loss is typically a weighted sum of amplitude and phase discrepancies. While the exact weighting is not specified in the provided notes, the base loss components are $d_a$ and $d_p$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
- **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer [S2].
- **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
- **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS are used [S2].
- **Typical Values:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Over-reconstruction:** Naive L1 loss fails to differentiate over-reconstructed regions from well-reconstructed ones, leading to redundant Gaussians. FR addresses this by increasing gradients in over-reconstructed regions [S2].
- **Frequency Annealing:** Naively adopting amplitude and phase discrepancies without distinguishing between low and high frequencies can restrict Gaussian densification and bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts. Progressive frequency regularization (frequency annealing) is used to mitigate this by gradually leveraging low-to-high frequencies [S2].
- **Precision:** The arctangent function requires careful handling of quadrants (e.g., using `atan2` in implementation) to ensure correct phase calculation [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import torch
import torch.fft

def frequency_regularization_loss(rendered_image: torch.Tensor, ground_truth_image: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """
    Computes the frequency regularization loss based on amplitude and phase discrepancies.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (B, H, W, C) representing the rendered image.
        ground_truth_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) or (B, H, W, C) representing the ground truth image.
        
    Returns:
        Scalar tensor representing the frequency regularization loss.
    """
    # Ensure inputs are float32 for FFT stability
    rendered_image = rendered_image.float()
    ground_truth_image = ground_truth_image.float()
    
    # Compute 2D FFT. torch.fft.fft2 expects input shape (..., H, W).
    # If input is (B, H, W, C), we need to move C to the last dimension or handle accordingly.
    # Assuming input is (H, W, C) or (B, H, W, C) with C as last dim.
    # torch.fft.fft2 works on the last two dimensions.
    
    # If shape is (H, W, C), we need to transpose to (C, H, W) for fft2 to work on H, W
    if rendered_image.dim() == 3:
        rendered_fft = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image.permute(2, 0, 1))
        gt_fft = torch.fft.fft2(ground_truth_image.permute(2, 0, 1))
    elif rendered_image.dim() == 4:
        rendered_fft = torch.fft.fft2(rendered_image.permute(0, 3, 1, 2))
        gt_fft = torch.fft.fft2(ground_truth_image.permute(0, 3, 1, 2))
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unsupported tensor dimension")
        
    # Compute Amplitude
    rendered_amp = torch.abs(rendered_fft)
    gt_amp = torch.abs(gt_fft)
    
    # Compute Phase
    rendered_phase = torch.angle(rendered_fft)
    gt_phase = torch.angle(gt_fft)
    
    # Compute Amplitude Discrepancy (RMSE)
    # Sum over H, W, and C dimensions
    # Note: The source formula sums over H*W. We assume averaging over all pixels and channels.
    amplitude_diff = rendered_amp - gt_amp
    da = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(amplitude_diff ** 2))
    
    # Compute Phase Discrepancy (RMSE)
    phase_diff = rendered_phase - gt_phase
    dp = torch.sqrt(torch.mean(phase_diff ** 2))
    
    # Combine discrepancies
    # The source does not specify the exact weighting. A simple sum is a common baseline.
    loss = da + dp
    
    return loss
```

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

### DWTGS
DWTGS rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The addition of 2D FFT and inverse FFT operations increases the computational cost per iteration compared to standard L1 loss. The complexity is dominated by the FFT, which is $O(N \log N)$ for an image of $N$ pixels.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the frequency representations of the rendered and ground truth images, adding memory overhead proportional to the image size.
- **Measured Performance:** In the "Bicycle" scene, FR significantly increased pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to the method without FR (equivalent to 3D-GS with L1 loss) [S2].
- **Scaling:** The method is implemented using PyTorch and custom CUDA kernels for rasterization, allowing for efficient computation on GPUs [S2].
- **Strengths:** Alleviates over-reconstruction, improves Gaussian densification, and pushes Gaussians toward high-frequency regions [S2][S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Naive adoption without frequency annealing can restrict Gaussian densification and bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts [S2].
- **When to use:** When over-reconstruction is a significant issue and standard L1 loss fails to trigger densification in those regions.
- **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of FFT is prohibitive, or when the scene does not suffer from over-reconstruction artifacts.

## Background
Frequency Regularization was introduced as part of the FreGS method to improve 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization [S2]. It addresses the problem of over-reconstruction and improper Gaussian distribution by leveraging the frequency domain to guide densification [S1, S2]. The method builds on the shared principles of regularization in 3D GS, which add a penalty term to the total training loss to constrain Gaussian parameters using priors not captured by RGB reconstruction error [S1, S2].
