# Hard and Soft Depth Regularization


## Overview
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization is a geometric constraint method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevents detail loss and blurred appearance by decoupling the optimization of Gaussian centers from their shapes [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard depth regularization (which often deforms Gaussian primitives to fit smooth monocular depth) by freezing scale and rotation parameters, instead using two distinct rendered depth maps to independently update center positions ("hard" depth fitting) and opacities ("soft" color appearance balancing) [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Current set of 3D Gaussians, each defined by center $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$ (parameterized by scale $s$ and rotation quaternion $q$), opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color.
- Monocular depth estimate $D_{mono}$ for the current view.
- Camera parameters for the current view.

**Outputs:**
- Gradient updates for Gaussian centers $\mu$ and opacities $\alpha$.
- Scale $s$ and rotation $q$ remain unchanged during this specific regularization step.

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian State:** Array of objects/structs containing:
  - `mu`: 3D vector $(x, y, z)$
  - `s`: 3D vector (scale along principal axes)
  - `q`: 4D vector (quaternion)
  - `alpha`: scalar
  - `sh`: color coefficients
- **Depth Maps:**
  - $D_{hard}$: Depth map computed using only Gaussian centers (or a specific depth definition that emphasizes position).
  - $D_{soft}$: Depth map computed using the standard alpha-blended depth, which incorporates opacity and shape influence.

**Steps:**
1. **Freeze Shape Parameters:** Temporarily disable gradient updates for scale $s$ and rotation $q$ for all Gaussians. This prevents the primitives from deforming to match the smooth monocular depth, which causes artifacts [S1].
2. **Render Two Depth Maps:**
   - Render $D_{hard}$: A depth map that primarily reflects the spatial position of the Gaussian centers. In the context of the source, this is used to compute gradients for center movement.
   - Render $D_{soft}$: A depth map derived from the standard alpha-blending process, which reflects the combined effect of position, opacity, and shape. This is used to compute gradients for opacity updates.
   *Note: The exact implementation of "rendering two depth maps" implies computing two different depth values per pixel or ray. Typically, $D_{hard}$ might be the weighted average of centers where weights are uniform or based on a simplified projection, while $D_{soft}$ is the standard $\sum \omega_i z_i / \sum \omega_i$ [S1].*
3. **Compute Hard Depth Loss:** Calculate the loss $\mathcal{L}_{hard}$ between $D_{hard}$ and the monocular depth estimate $D_{mono}$. This loss drives the optimization of the Gaussian centers $\mu$.
4. **Compute Soft Depth Loss:** Calculate the loss $\mathcal{L}_{soft}$ between $D_{soft}$ and $D_{mono}$. This loss drives the optimization of the Gaussian opacities $\alpha$.
5. **Apply Gradients:**
   - Update $\mu$ using gradients from $\mathcal{L}_{hard}$.
   - Update $\alpha$ using gradients from $\mathcal{L}_{soft}$.
   - Do not update $s$ or $q$ in this step.

**Formulas:**
Let $D_{mono}$ be the target monocular depth.
Let $D_{hard}(\theta)$ be the rendered hard depth map, dependent on parameters $\theta$ (specifically $\mu$).
Let $D_{soft}(\theta)$ be the rendered soft depth map, dependent on parameters $\theta$ (specifically $\alpha$ and $\mu$).

The total regularization loss is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{HSDR} = \lambda_{hard} \mathcal{L}_{depth}(D_{hard}, D_{mono}) + \lambda_{soft} \mathcal{L}_{depth}(D_{soft}, D_{mono}) $$

Where $\mathcal{L}_{depth}$ is typically a scale-invariant log loss or L1/L2 loss. The gradients are applied selectively:
$$ \nabla_{\mu} \mathcal{L}_{total} \leftarrow \nabla_{\mu} \mathcal{L}_{hard} + \nabla_{\mu} \mathcal{L}_{soft} $$
$$ \nabla_{\alpha} \mathcal{L}_{total} \leftarrow \nabla_{\alpha} \mathcal{L}_{soft} $$
$$ \nabla_{s} \mathcal{L}_{total} \leftarrow 0 $$
$$ \nabla_{q} \mathcal{L}_{total} \leftarrow 0 $$

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_{hard}, \lambda_{soft}$: Weights for the hard and soft depth losses. Typical values are not explicitly given in the source, but they balance the influence of coarse depth fitting vs. fine appearance.
- Freeze $s, q$: Boolean flag, set to `True` during this step.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Detail Loss:** Applying depth regularization directly to NeRF-style MLPs or un-frozen Gaussian shapes forces the primitive geometry to conform to smooth monocular depth, conflicting with complex color appearance and causing detail loss [S1].
- **Noise Sensitivity:** Gaussian radiance fields are more sensitive to small depth errors than NeRFs, potentially resulting in noisy primitive distributions in complex texture regions [S1].
- **Scale Invariance:** Existing scale-invariant depth losses often align depth maps to a fixed scale, which may overlook minor depth variations [S1]. This method mitigates this by decoupling the depth fitting from shape deformation.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def hard_and_soft_depth_regularization(gaussians, camera, mono_depth, lambda_hard, lambda_soft):
    """
    Performs Hard and Soft Depth Regularization on 3D Gaussians.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of Gaussian objects with attributes mu, s, q, alpha, sh.
        camera: Camera object with projection and view matrices.
        mono_depth: 2D array of monocular depth estimates for the current view.
        lambda_hard: Weight for the hard depth loss.
        lambda_soft: Weight for the soft depth loss.
        
    Returns:
        Updated gradients for mu and alpha.
    """
    # 1. Freeze shape parameters
    # In an optimizer, this is done by setting requires_grad=False for s and q
    for g in gaussians:
        g.s.requires_grad = False
        g.q.requires_grad = False
        
    # 2. Render two depth maps
    # D_hard: Depth based primarily on center positions
    # D_soft: Standard alpha-blended depth
    D_hard = render_depth_hard(gaussians, camera)
    D_soft = render_depth_soft(gaussians, camera)
    
    # 3. Compute losses
    # Using a standard depth loss, e.g., Scale-Invariant Log Loss or L1
    loss_hard = compute_depth_loss(D_hard, mono_depth)
    loss_soft = compute_depth_loss(D_soft, mono_depth)
    
    # 4. Compute gradients
    # Gradients are computed w.r.t all parameters initially
    loss_hard.backward(retain_graph=True)
    loss_soft.backward()
    
    # 5. Apply selective gradients
    # Zero out gradients for s and q
    for g in gaussians:
        if g.s.grad is not None:
            g.s.grad.zero_()
        if g.q.grad is not None:
            g.q.grad.zero_()
            
    # Return gradients for mu and alpha (optimizer handles the update)
    return gaussians.mu.grad, gaussians.alpha.grad
```

## Subtopics

### [Global-Local Depth Normalization](9179-global-local-depth-normalization.md)
Global-Local Depth Normalization is a depth regularization technique for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevents scale-invariant losses from overlooking minor depth variations, thereby improving detailed geometry reshaping in texture-rich regions. It sets itself apart from standard scale-invariant losses by combining global scale preservation with local scale amplification, addressing the issue where existing methods align depth maps to a fixed global scale and ignore small local errors. It builds on [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) and differs from siblings [Depth Reinitialization](9233-depth-reinitialization.md), [Position Regularization](11049-position-regularization.md), and [Coherent 3D Gaussian Optimization](14045-coherent-3d-gaussian-optimization.md) by explicitly decoupling absolute scale maintenance from local error amplification during gradient computation.

### [Depth Reinitialization](9233-depth-reinitialization.md)
Depth Reinitialization is a regularization strategy that alleviates 'overlapping' and 'under-reconstruction' artifacts in 3D Gaussian Splatting by leveraging dense depth information to reorganize the spatial distribution of Gaussians. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by rejecting standard alpha-blended depth maps in favor of geometric mid-point depths to generate clean, artifact-free point clouds for reinitialization.

### [Isotropic Regularization](10092-isotropic-regularization.md)
Isotropic Regularization is a geometric constraint used in continuous SLAM-based Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of highly elongated Gaussian primitives along the viewing ray direction. While standard differentiable rendering imposes no constraint on Gaussian scaling along the viewing ray, this lack of constraint causes artifacts and tracking instability in continuous SLAM scenarios where viewpoints are not carefully selected. It sets itself apart from its parent [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by explicitly penalizing anisotropy (stretching) rather than just enforcing depth consistency, and from [Global-Local Depth Normalization](9179-global-local-depth-normalization.md) by operating on the scale parameters directly rather than depth values.

### [Position Regularization](11049-position-regularization.md)
Position Regularization prevents floating artifacts and dramatic geometry shifts in 3D Gaussian Splatting by constraining Gaussian centers to remain close to a reference surface or their initial positions. It operates as a geometric prior constraint, distinct from depth-consistency methods in its parent category [9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md].

### [Coherent 3D Gaussian Optimization](14045-coherent-3d-gaussian-optimization.md)
Coherent 3D Gaussian Optimization is a regularization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that enforces spatial coherence in Gaussian positions and opacities during training, specifically addressing artifacts in sparse-view novel view synthesis. It sets itself apart from standard 3DGS by replacing independent per-pixel residual updates with a learned implicit decoder that produces smooth, coherent residual fields for depth and opacity, supplemented by multiview total variation and optical flow constraints. This prevents the "semi-transparent" artifacts and detail loss common when optimizing Gaussians against sparse input views.

### Geometric Regularization via Bilateral Filter
A regularization loss that enforces coherent motion by grouping Gaussians in image space using a bilateral filter approach, avoiding the dampening of independent motion caused by spatial KNN [S2]. For a training image $I$ at time $t_I$, sample another time $t'$. Compute the 6D screw-axis representation of the transformation between $t_I$ and $t'$ for each Gaussian. Compute the geometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the weighted sum of squared distances between screw-axis representations of neighboring pixels. The formula is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} = \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{N}(p)} k_{c,c'} k_{d,d'} k_{p,p'} \|s_p - s_{p'}\|^2$, where $k_{c,c'}$ is color similarity, $k_{d,d'}$ is depth similarity, $k_{p,p'}$ is spatial distance weight, and $s_p, s_{p'}$ are screw-axis representations. Unlike standard bilateral filters, the loss is not divided by the sum of weights because the goal is comparison, not energy conservation [S2].

### Near-Far Regularization
A regularization term applied alongside depth distortion to constrain Gaussians to aggregate at object surfaces, ensuring robust 3D structure reconstruction [S3]. It narrows the distance between the weighted depth of the nearest and farthest $M$ Gaussians on each ray. Extract subset $N$ (nearest $M$ Gaussians) and $F$ (farthest $M$ Gaussians, where blending weight approaches 1) on each ray. Render depth maps $d_N, d_F$ and blending weight maps $T_N, T_F$ for subsets $N$ and $F$. The formula is $R_{nf} = T_N \cdot T_F \cdot |d_N - d_F|$, where $T$ is the sum of blending weights $\omega_i$. Used in LE3D to improve structural reconstruction quality for downstream tasks [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Adds the cost of rendering two additional depth maps per regularization step. The gradient computation is linear in the number of pixels and Gaussians, similar to standard rendering.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two additional depth maps per view during the regularization step.
- **Strengths:** Prevents detail loss and blurred appearance caused by forcing anisotropic Gaussians to fit smooth monocular depth [S1]. Balances coarse depth fitting with fine color appearance [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of $\lambda_{hard}$ and $\lambda_{soft}$. Sensitive to noise in monocular depth estimates in complex texture regions [S1].
- **When to use:** When using monocular depth priors for 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization, especially in sparse-view or texture-rich scenarios where standard depth regularization causes artifacts.
- **When not to use:** When high-fidelity shape deformation is required and monocular depth is unreliable or not available.

## Background
Originated in the context of optimizing sparse-view 3D Gaussian Radiance Fields, specifically addressing the conflict between smooth monocular depth estimates and complex color appearance in Gaussian fields [S1]. The method was proposed to overcome the limitations of existing depth regularization strategies for NeRFs, which adversely affect quality when applied to Gaussian fields by forcing primitive shapes to fit smooth depth [S1].
