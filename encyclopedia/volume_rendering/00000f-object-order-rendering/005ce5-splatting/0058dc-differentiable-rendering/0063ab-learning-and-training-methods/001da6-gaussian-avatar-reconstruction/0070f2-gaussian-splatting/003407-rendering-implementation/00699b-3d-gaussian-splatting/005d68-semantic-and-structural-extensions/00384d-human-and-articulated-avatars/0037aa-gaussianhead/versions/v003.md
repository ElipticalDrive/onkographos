# GaussianHead

Also called: Gaussian Head Avatar

## Overview
GaussianHead is a method for reconstructing animatable 3D head avatars from video, specifically designed to simulate geometrically changing heads in continuous movements while rendering rich textures, including skin and hair [S1][S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [GART](8093-gart.md) and [D3GA](8094-d3ga.md) by utilizing a tri-plane representation combined with motion fields to handle the complex, non-rigid deformations of the human head, rather than relying solely on skeleton-based or mesh-bound deformation priors [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view video sequences of a human head in various poses and expressions.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians parameterized by position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients, along with learned motion fields that map canonical space to target poses.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-Plane Representation:** A set of three 2D feature planes (XY, YZ, XZ) encoding spatial information. The exact resolution and channel dimensions are not given in the sources [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Motion Fields:** A neural network or implicit function that predicts displacement vectors for Gaussians based on pose parameters.
*   **3D Gaussians:** Standard 3DGS attributes (mean, covariance, opacity, SH coefficients) initialized in a canonical space.

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize 3D Gaussians in a canonical reference pose (e.g., neutral expression), consistent with the shared principles of the family [Parent](8209-human-and-articulated-avatars.md).
2.  **Tri-Plane Encoding:** Encode the spatial features of the head geometry and appearance into a tri-plane representation [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Motion Field Prediction:** Use the motion fields to predict the deformation of the canonical Gaussians to the target pose. This allows for the simulation of continuous geometric changes [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Rendering:** Render the deformed Gaussians using differentiable volume rendering to produce images. The method is optimized to render rich textures, including skin and hair [S1][S2, unofficial].
5.  **Optimization:** Minimize photometric loss between rendered images and ground truth video frames to optimize Gaussian attributes and motion field parameters.

**Formulas:**
Not given in the sources. The specific mathematical formulation of the tri-plane sampling and motion field integration is not provided [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Hair and Volumetric Objects:** The method is noted for handling complicated volumetric objects like hair, which can be challenging for standard mesh-based methods [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Continuous Movements:** The tri-plane and motion field approach is specifically chosen to handle continuous, non-rigid head movements, avoiding artifacts common in rigid or piecewise-linear deformation [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### FlashAvatar
FlashAvatar is a variant that embeds a uniform 3D Gaussian field within a parametric face model and learns additional spatial offsets to capture fine facial details [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished by its high rendering speed, pushing performance to 300 FPS [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when real-time, high-fidelity facial animation is required.

## Background
GaussianHead was introduced in the context of recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting for human avatar reconstruction [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds upon the use of explicit face models (like FLAME) and geometric priors seen in methods like [GaussianAvatars](8209-human-and-articulated-avatars.md) but diverges by using tri-plane representations and motion fields for deformation [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is part of a broader trend towards real-time, high-fidelity head avatars, with variants like FlashAvatar pushing rendering speeds to 300 FPS [S2, unofficial].
