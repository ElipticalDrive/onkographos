# Rendering-aware QP Offset Modelling

Also called: Density-based QP scaling, Inter-Sequence QP Modelling, Intra-Sequence QP Modelling

## Overview
Rendering-aware QP Offset Modelling approximates the optimal Quantization Parameter (QP) distribution for Rate-Distortion Optimized (RDO) RAHT coding of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by linking hierarchical data structure to visual impact [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods requiring per-sequence iterative fitting of layer-specific parameters, this approach uses a generalized heuristic based on spatial density and resolution to eliminate pre-analysis overhead [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Scene primitives: Set of $N_{primitives}$ 3D Gaussians.
*   Training resolution: Width $W$, Height $H$.
*   Hierarchical structure: Average number of primitives per node $\bar{N}_i$ for each RAHT layer $i$.

**Outputs:**
*   QP offset $\Delta q_{p,i}$ for each layer $i$, derived from the model parameters $\beta$ and $\gamma$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Spatial Density ($\rho$):** A scalar value representing the average inverse square nearest-neighbour distance.
*   **Scaling Factor ($s$):** A scalar value modulating QP offset based on $\rho$ and resolution.
*   **Parameters ($\beta, \gamma$):** Sequence-dependent fitting parameters derived analytically from $s$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Calculate Spatial Density ($\rho$):**
    Compute the average inverse square distance to the nearest neighbour for all primitives.
    $$ \rho = \frac{1}{N_{primitives}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{primitives}} \frac{1}{d_j^2} $$
    Where $d_j$ is the Euclidean distance from the $j$-th primitive to its nearest neighbour [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute Global Scaling Factor ($s$):**
    Modulate the QP offset based on scene density and training resolution using the regression-derived formula:
    $$ s = 10 + 0.029 \cdot \rho \cdot (W \cdot H)^{0.2} $$
    Where $W$ and $H$ are the training resolution width and height [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Derive Sequence-Dependent Parameters ($\beta, \gamma$):**
    Calculate the global offset and scaling parameters analytically from $s$:
    $$ \gamma = s^3 $$
    $$ \beta = 0.75 \cdot s $$
    $\beta$ accounts for the global offset of the QP distribution, and $\gamma$ accounts for its scaling [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Determine Layer-wise QP Offsets ($\Delta q_{p,i}$):**
    Model the shape of layer-wise QP offsets using $\bar{N}_i$ (average number of Gaussian primitives per node at layer $i$) as a proxy for hierarchical impact. Solve for $\Delta q_{p,i}$:
    $$ (\Delta q_{p,i} + \beta)^3 = \gamma \cdot \bar{N}_i $$
    $$ \Delta q_{p,i} = \sqrt[3]{\gamma \cdot \bar{N}_i} - \beta $$
    Layers closer to the root (smaller index) have smaller $\bar{N}_i$ and thus smaller QP offsets, reflecting lower error accumulation impact [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The method assumes a fixed-point RAHT precision (e.g., 20 bits) for optimal performance; deviations may affect rate-distortion trade-offs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Density Calculation:** $d_j$ must be computed in 3D space. If primitives are degenerate (co-located), $d_j \to 0$ causes $\rho \to \infty$. A small epsilon guard is required in implementation [G].
*   **Resolution Scaling:** The exponent $0.2$ on $(W \cdot H)$ is empirically derived; using non-standard resolutions may reduce accuracy [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{primitives})$ to compute $\rho$ (nearest neighbour search). Parameter derivation is $O(1)$. Layer-wise offset calculation is $O(L)$ where $L$ is the number of RAHT layers.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_{primitives})$ to store primitive positions for density calculation. $O(1)$ for parameters.
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates per-sequence fitting overhead; generalizes across diverse scenes using global properties; accounts for error accumulation during splatting projection [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on empirical regression coefficients; assumes uniform distribution of primitives for density approximation; performance depends on fixed-point precision settings [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When rapid, generalized QP assignment is needed for 3DGS compression without iterative pre-analysis.
*   **When Not to Use:** When per-sequence optimal fitting is feasible and required for maximum rate-distortion efficiency.

## Background
Originated in the context of Rate-Distortion Optimized RAHT coding for 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the limitation that optimal QP offsets exhibit a linear trend relative to RAHT layer index, but this relationship cannot be directly generalized due to varying total layer counts between sequences [S1, unofficial]. It links the hierarchical structure of 3D data to its final visual impact by accounting for error accumulation during splatting projection, where errors in root layers accumulate constructively, while leaf layer errors tend to cancel out [S1, unofficial].
