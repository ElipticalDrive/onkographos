# Splitting Criteria and Prioritization

Also called: Densification Scoring, Splitting Heuristics

## Overview
Splitting Criteria and Prioritization determines which 3D Gaussian primitives are candidates for densification (splitting or cloning) and in what order they should be processed during training. It solves the problem of inefficient resource allocation by ensuring that new primitives are generated only where reconstruction error is high, rather than uniformly across the scene. This family of methods sets itself apart from sibling approaches by varying the definition of "error" or "importance": some rely on multi-view gradient consistency [5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md], others on spatial complexity [16126-complexity-aware-adaptive-density-control.md], contribution weighting [16892-average-contribution-criterion.md], or frequency analysis [19904-frequency-aware-adaptive-splat-spawning.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the Adaptive Density Control loop, which periodically evaluates a scalar score for each Gaussian $G$ and compares it against a threshold $\tau$. If the score exceeds $\tau$, the Gaussian is marked for densification. The core shared mechanism involves:
1.  **Projection:** Mapping the 3D Gaussian to 2D screen space to identify contributing pixels.
2.  **Aggregation:** Combining per-pixel metrics (gradients, opacities, frequencies) into a single scalar score $S(G)$.
3.  **Thresholding:** Comparing $S(G)$ to $\tau$. The threshold $\tau$ is typically a hyperparameter tuned for the specific scene or dataset.
4.  **Prioritization:** If multiple Gaussians exceed $\tau$, a secondary criterion (e.g., magnitude of score) determines the order of splitting to maximize quality gain per primitive added.

The specific aggregation function and the definition of the input metrics vary by method.

## Subtopics

### [Multi-View Density Control Criterion](5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md)
A densification strategy designed to resolve invalid vector arithmetic in multi-view training. Standard Adaptive Density Control sums 2D positional gradients from pixels covering a Gaussian; however, in multi-view training, a single Gaussian is projected onto multiple image planes, and adding 2D gradient vectors from different view spaces is mathematically invalid, potentially resulting in zero vectors even if the underlying 3D gradients are significant. This method replaces vector addition with the summation of squared norms, ensuring that error metrics remain valid across viewpoints. It yields higher reconstruction quality with fewer primitives compared to standard positional-gradient rules.

### [Complexity-Aware Adaptive Density Control](16126-complexity-aware-adaptive-density-control.md)
A training optimization method that replaces opacity-only heuristics with a composite importance score incorporating image complexity, scale, and opacity. It solves the problem where standard densification concentrates primitives in already-resolved high-opacity regions while neglecting unresolved complex areas, and where low-complexity regions degrade prematurely under budget constraints. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Multi-View Density Control Criterion](5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md) by explicitly modeling spatial complexity as a surrogate for reconstruction error to guide relocation and pruning, rather than relying solely on gradient magnitude or multi-view consistency.

### [Average Contribution Criterion](16892-average-contribution-criterion.md)
A densification strategy that determines whether a Gaussian should be split or cloned based on its mean blending weight per view rather than a binary view count. It solves the problem of occlusion bias inherent in the original 3D Gaussian Splatting criterion, where fully occluded views inflate the denominator and dilute the average gradient signal. By weighting views by their actual contribution, it prevents occluded views from biasing the average downward and produces fewer Gaussians at matched quality compared to pixel-count-based methods.

### [Frequency-Aware Adaptive Splat Spawning](19904-frequency-aware-adaptive-splat-spawning.md)
A densification strategy that spawns new splats in regions of high-frequency content or low opacity (holes) by scoring pixels based on frequency gaps and alpha gaps, then sampling new splats from the nearest existing cloud points. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly incorporating image frequency analysis into the splitting decision, targeting detail loss rather than just gradient magnitude or opacity thresholds.

### Distortion-based Gaussian Splitting Prioritization
- **Definition:** A criterion for prioritizing which Gaussians to split, based on rate-distortion optimization to maximize PSNR gain [S1]. It associates a reconstruction Squared Error $SE_{c,i}$ to each Gaussian $i$ in camera view $c$, defined as:
  $$SE_{c,i} = \sum_{p \in I_c} w_i^{(c)} (R^{(c)}(p) - I^{(c)}(p))^2$$
  where $I^{(c)}$ is the ground-truth image, $R^{(c)}(p)$ is the rendered color, and $w_i^{(c)}$ is the blending weight of Gaussian $i$ at pixel $p$ [S1]. Gaussians inducing the highest gain in PSNR are prioritized for splitting [S1].

### Adaptive Density Control Threshold Tuning
- **Parameter:** The maximum gradient norm threshold for adaptive density control determines the speed of the splitting/pruning process [S2]. Tested values include $10^{-4}$, $10^{-5}$, and $10^{-6}$ [S2].
- **Pitfall:** Smaller thresholds (e.g., $10^{-6}$) do not significantly improve performance over larger thresholds (e.g., $10^{-4}$) but may increase computational cost due to more aggressive splitting [S2].
- **Numbers:** On 40-view Head, Jaw, Vertebrae, Abdomen, Chest, Foot datasets, performance stability across thresholds is observed: $10^{-4}$ yields Avg PSNR 36.54, SSIM 0.954; $10^{-5}$ yields Avg PSNR 37.14, SSIM 0.962; $10^{-6}$ yields Avg PSNR 36.48, SSIM 0.957 [S2].

### Scalar Wavelength Frequency Violation Metric
- **Definition:** A densification trigger that compares all Gaussian axes against a single scalar wavelength, activating splits when any axis exceeds this global frequency limit [S3].
- **Pitfall:** During early optimization, Gaussian rotations are not yet well-optimized, causing inaccurate axis-direction alignment. The scalar wavelength criterion compensates by applying a uniform threshold independent of orientation, giving gradient-based optimization more capacity to fine-tune rotation and position [S3].
- **Numbers:** In ablation tests on Mip-NeRF360, Deep Blending, and Tanks & Temples, the scalar $\eta$ variant achieves LPIPS scores of 0.197, 0.238, and 0.147 respectively, outperforming the projection-based variant $\eta^{(proj)}$ (0.203, 0.242, 0.159) [S3].
- **Comparison:** Outperforms the projection-based variant ($\eta^{(proj)}$) in LPIPS scores despite being theoretically less precise, because the scalar approach does not depend on accurate axis orientation during early training [S3].

### Concave Split Factor Mapping
- **Definition:** A post-processing step that applies a concave power function to compress the raw frequency violation $\eta$, preventing excessive splitting caused by outlier frequency spikes [S3].
- **Parameter:** $p$: exponent controlling concavity. Typical/default value: 0.5 (square root). Higher values increase training time and Gaussian count; image quality saturates at $p=0.5$ [S3].
- **Numbers:** On Mip-NeRF360, varying $p$ yields LPIPS: 0.087 ($p=0.25$), 0.197 ($p=0.5$), 0.819 ($p=0.75$) [S3].

### Coarse Initialization and Chunk Subdivision
- **Step:** Run a very coarse initial optimization on the full dataset using available SfM points and an auxiliary skybox. Disable densification and do not optimize primitive positions, as SfM points are well-placed. This creates a minimal basis for backdrop details [S4].
- **Step:** Split scenes into chunks large enough to establish context for real-world elements (cars, buildings, monuments) [S4].
- **Step:** For each chunk, select cameras that are inside the chunk bounds OR are within 2x the chunk bounds AND have more than 50 SfM points within the chunk bounds [S4].

## Background
The concept of adaptive density control was introduced in the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper [Kerbl et al., 2023], which used opacity and gradient magnitude heuristics. Subsequent research has refined these criteria to address specific limitations such as multi-view inconsistency [5663-multi-view-density-control-criterion.md], computational efficiency [S3], and large-scale scene handling [S4]. The distortion-based prioritization [S1] and frequency-aware methods [19904-frequency-aware-adaptive-splat-spawning.md] represent later developments aimed at improving convergence speed and detail preservation.
