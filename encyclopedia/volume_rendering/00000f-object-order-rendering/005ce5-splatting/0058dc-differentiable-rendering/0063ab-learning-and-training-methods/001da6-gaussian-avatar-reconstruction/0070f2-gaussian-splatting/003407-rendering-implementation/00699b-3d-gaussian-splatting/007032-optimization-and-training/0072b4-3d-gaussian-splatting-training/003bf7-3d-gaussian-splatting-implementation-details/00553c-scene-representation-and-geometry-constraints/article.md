# Scene Representation and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting, Surface-constrained 3DGS

## Overview
This family defines the fundamental geometric and appearance primitives of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and the mechanisms for their optimization, truncation, and structural refinement. It addresses the problem of representing complex scenes with high-fidelity geometry and appearance using a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians, while managing computational cost through truncation and densification strategies. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Discontinuity-aware Gaussian Splatting](13138-discontinuity-aware-gaussian-splatting.md) by focusing on the core primitive definition and training dynamics rather than post-hoc discontinuity handling, and from [Appearance and Physical Modeling](15766-appearance-and-physical-modeling.md) by prioritizing the structural integrity and density of the Gaussian set itself over complex physical light transport models.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [Discontinuity-aware Gaussian Splatting](13138-discontinuity-aware-gaussian-splatting.md)
Discontinuity-aware Gaussian Splatting (DisC-GS) is a framework that enables 3D Gaussian Splatting to represent and render sharp boundaries and discontinuities, addressing the limitation of standard Gaussian Splatting which tends to blur such features. It differs from sibling methods like [Planar Gaussian Splatting](16706-planar-gaussian-splatting.md) by explicitly modeling boundaries as cubic Bézier curves within the Gaussian's local subspace and applying a "pre-scissoring" operation during rendering, rather than relying solely on geometric constraints or mesh extraction. [Discontinuity-aware Gaussian Splatting](13138-discontinuity-aware-gaussian-splatting.md)

### [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md)
This family converts the volumetric 3D Gaussian Splatting representation into explicit geometric structures, primarily triangular meshes or dense point clouds, to enable compatibility with standard 3D graphics pipelines, mesh editing tools, and physics simulations. Unlike standard 3DGS, which renders via alpha-blended splats, these methods extract a discrete surface or point set that approximates the underlying geometry, allowing for operations such as texture mapping and rigging [G]. [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md)

### [Appearance and Physical Modeling](15766-appearance-and-physical-modeling.md)
Appearance and Physical Modeling extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to support physically based rendering (PBR), relighting, and volumetric light transport, solving the limitations of base 3DGS which relies on fixed Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients that cannot accurately represent complex specularities, dynamic lighting, or non-vacuum media [G]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md) by focusing on the radiance and material representation rather than geometric simplification, and distinguishes itself from [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md) by prioritizing surface material decomposition and relighting over volumetric attenuation equations, although both families modify the core Gaussian properties beyond simple color [G]. [Appearance and Physical Modeling](15766-appearance-and-physical-modeling.md)

### [Sparse2DGS](18424-sparse2dgs.md)
Sparse2DGS is a Gaussian Splatting method designed for surface reconstruction from sparse input views, addressing the ill-posed nature of geometric optimization when dense Structure-from-Motion points are unavailable. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting and its sibling [2DGS](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md) by initializing primitives with unsupervised Multi-View Stereo (MVS) points and employing geometric-prioritized enhancements—such as fixed feature splatting and selective updates—to prevent appearance overfitting and improve geometric learning. [Sparse2DGS](18424-sparse2dgs.md)

### [Surface Geometry Constraints](18488-surface-geometry-constraints.md)
Surface Geometry Constraints are optimization and initialization techniques applied to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to enforce geometric fidelity, ensuring that the volumetric Gaussian representation aligns with underlying surface structures such as meshes, planes, or physical boundaries. While standard 3DGS optimizes for photometric accuracy, these methods introduce explicit geometric priors—such as planar alignment, shell confinement, or mesh adherence—to resolve the inherent conflict between volumetric splats and sharp surface representations. They differ from sibling methods like [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) by integrating constraints directly into the training loop rather than post-processing, and from [Appearance and Physical Modeling](15766-appearance-and-physical-modeling.md) by focusing on spatial geometry rather than radiance. [Surface Geometry Constraints](18488-surface-geometry-constraints.md)

### [CADSplat](19767-cadsplat.md)
CADSplat reconstructs photorealistic, geometrically accurate digital twins from sparse (< 15 views), wide-baseline posed images by regularizing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with an explicit Computer-Aided Design (CAD) shape prior. Unlike methods that rely on learned generative priors or large-scale data priors (e.g., InstantSplat, pixelSplat), CADSplat imposes a strict, explicit geometric constraint derived from the object's specific CAD model, providing geometric disambiguation under severe occlusion and view scarcity. It differs from sibling geometry-constrained methods by anchoring Gaussians to a parametric CAD surface and jointly optimizing a non-rigid deformation field, rather than relying solely on surface normal or SDF constraints. [CADSplat](19767-cadsplat.md)

### [Surface-Constrained Gaussian Splatting](20026-surface-constrained-gaussian-splatting.md)
Surface-Constrained Gaussian Splatting is a method for initializing and regularizing 3D Gaussian Splatting representations using a pre-existing mesh surface to eliminate floating artifacts and improve geometric fidelity. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by anchoring Gaussian means to mesh vertices and enforcing planar constraints via specific loss functions, whereas siblings like [Mesh-adsorbed Gaussian](20320-mesh-adsorbed-gaussian.md) may use different adsorption mechanisms and [Surface Reconstruction via SDF](22162-surface-reconstruction-via-sdf.md) relies on signed distance fields rather than explicit mesh anchoring. [Surface-Constrained Gaussian Splatting](20026-surface-constrained-gaussian-splatting.md)

### [Mesh-adsorbed Gaussian](20320-mesh-adsorbed-gaussian.md)
Mesh-adsorbed Gaussian (MaGS) is a hybrid scene representation that combines a triangular mesh with 3D Gaussians, where Gaussians are "adsorbed" to mesh facets rather than being rigidly anchored in 3D space. This allows Gaussians to "roam" on the surface, increasing their degrees of freedom for movement and deformation. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by tying the spatial position of Gaussians to a parametric mesh surface defined by logical coordinates, enabling efficient dynamic reconstruction and simulation. [Mesh-adsorbed Gaussian](20320-mesh-adsorbed-gaussian.md)

### [Surface Reconstruction via SDF](22162-surface-reconstruction-via-sdf.md)
This family of methods reconstructs high-quality, watertight 3D meshes from multi-view RGB images by coupling the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation with an implicit Signed Distance Field (SDF) [17501-3d-gaussian-splatting-for-surface-reconstruction.md]. Standard 3DGS lacks explicit geometric structure, requiring post-processing for mesh extraction; these methods solve this by jointly optimizing Gaussians and an SDF to enforce surface constraints during training [17501-3d-gaussian-splatting-for-surface-reconstruction.md]. They differ from siblings like [Mesh Extraction and Representation](14095-mesh-extraction-and-representation.md) by integrating geometry regularization directly into the differentiable rendering loop rather than relying on separate reconstruction pipelines. [Surface Reconstruction via SDF](22162-surface-reconstruction-via-sdf.md)

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [G]. Subsequent work has focused on addressing the limitations of this representation, particularly regarding geometric fidelity, sharp boundaries, and sparse-view reconstruction. Methods like Discontinuity-aware Gaussian Splatting [S1] and CADSplat [S3, unofficial] have emerged to handle specific geometric challenges such as discontinuities and sparse data, respectively. The integration of CAD models [S3, unofficial] and mesh constraints [S1] represents a shift towards hybrid representations that leverage external geometric priors.
