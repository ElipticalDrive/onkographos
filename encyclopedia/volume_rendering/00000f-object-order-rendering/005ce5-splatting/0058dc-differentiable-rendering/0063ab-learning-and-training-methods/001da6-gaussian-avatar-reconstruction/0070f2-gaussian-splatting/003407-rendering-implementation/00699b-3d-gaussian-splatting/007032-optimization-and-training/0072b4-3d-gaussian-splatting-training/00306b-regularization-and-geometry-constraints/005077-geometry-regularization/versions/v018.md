# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and visual quality of reconstructed scenes by constraining the shape and orientation of Gaussian primitives, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains location [S1, S2, unofficial]. It addresses issues such as floating artifacts and poor surface smoothness by enforcing that Gaussians align with the underlying surface geometry [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by focusing on spatial shape constraints rather than spectral content, and from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by modifying the primitive's anisotropy rather than just its position [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian scaling factors $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, per-Gaussian orientation/normal estimates, and rendered depth maps from training views.
*   **Outputs:** Updated Gaussian parameters (scaling and orientation) that are flattened along the surface normal and aligned with depth-derived normals.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Scaling:** A vector $s = [s_x, s_y, s_z]$ representing the scale along the principal axes of the Gaussian.
*   **Optimization Parameters:** A boolean flag `use_normal_loss` (or similar) to enable the regularization term [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Enable Regularization:** Set the optimization parameter `use_normal_loss = true` [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Depth Normal Consistency:**
    *   Render a depth map from the current camera view.
    *   Compute surface normals from the rendered depth map (e.g., via finite differences or gradient estimation) [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   Retrieve the per-Gaussian normal estimate (often derived from the orientation quaternion or the smallest axis of the covariance) [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute a loss term that minimizes the angular difference between the depth-derived normal and the Gaussian normal, enforcing alignment [S1, S2, unofficial].
3.  **Flattening Surface (Scaling Loss):**
    *   For each Gaussian, identify the smallest component of the scaling vector $s$. Let $s_{min} = \min(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute a loss term that drives $s_{min}$ towards zero, effectively flattening the Gaussian into a 2D surfel aligned with the surface [S2, unofficial].
    *   This flattening aids in determining the Gaussian normal by selecting the shortest scaled edge as the surface normal [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Flattening Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{flatten} = \sum_{i} s_{min, i} $$
    where $s_{min, i}$ is the minimum scaling factor for Gaussian $i$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Consistency Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{normal} = \sum_{i} (1 - \mathbf{n}_{depth, i} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{gauss, i}) $$
    where $\mathbf{n}_{depth, i}$ is the normal derived from the depth map at the projection of Gaussian $i$, and $\mathbf{n}_{gauss, i}$ is the Gaussian's estimated normal [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean flag to enable geometry regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   Weights for $\mathcal{L}_{flatten}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{normal}$ are not specified in the sources [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Refitting:** Depth maps must be refitted against a prior point cloud to the same scale before use. Depth maps that fail this refitting are discarded and not used for supervision [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Minimizing $s_{min}$ to zero can lead to numerical instability if not regularized; however, the sources do not specify a lower bound or epsilon [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Ambiguity:** The Gaussian normal is determined by the shortest scaled edge. If the Gaussian is not sufficiently flattened, the normal estimate may be inaccurate [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A specific implementation of geometry regularization that focuses exclusively on flattening Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels aligned with the surface [S2, unofficial]. It minimizes the smallest component of the scaling factor $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$ towards zero [S2, unofficial]. This approach aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when the primary goal is to enforce surface-like geometry without explicit depth normal alignment [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds a small constant overhead per Gaussian per training step for computing scaling minima and normal alignment [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No additional significant memory requirements beyond storing per-Gaussian scaling and normal estimates [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Improves geometry and reduces the number of floaters [S1, unofficial]. However, aggressive flattening may reduce the number of remaining points, potentially compromising visual quality if not balanced with pruning mechanisms [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances surface smoothness and geometric accuracy [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate depth maps and normal estimation; sensitive to depth refitting failures [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When high-fidelity surface geometry is required and depth supervision alone is insufficient [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the scene contains significant transparency or non-surface structures where flattening is inappropriate [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Originating from the need to improve the geometric quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions, this technique was highlighted in discussions about optimizing Gaussian Splatting in Julia [S1, unofficial] and in the GauStudio framework [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the observation that standard Gaussian kernels are volumetric and do not naturally represent surfaces, necessitating explicit regularization to flatten them into surfels [S1, S2, unofficial].
