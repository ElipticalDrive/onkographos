# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Regularization, Frequency Regularization

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints stabilize the optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting by preventing over-reconstruction, reducing floaters, and enforcing geometric fidelity that photometric loss alone cannot achieve [S2][S4, unofficial][S1]. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md) which relies primarily on pixel-wise L1/L2 photometric losses, this family explicitly constrains the spectral content, covariance shape, or spatial depth consistency of the Gaussians [S2][S1]. It addresses the sensitivity of Gaussian radiance fields to small depth errors and the tendency of naive densification to create redundant structures in already well-reconstructed regions [S2][S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism across all methods in this family is the addition of a regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ to the total training loss $\mathcal{L}_{total}$:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg}
$$
where $\lambda$ is a weighting factor. The regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ is derived from either the frequency domain, the geometric shape of the covariance matrix, or the consistency of rendered depth maps with external priors [S2][S1].

Common mathematical constructs include:
1.  **Frequency Decomposition:** Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) to separate amplitude $|F(u,v)|$ and phase $\angle F(u,v)$ components, allowing independent regularization of spectral content [S2].
2.  **Covariance Transformation:** Projection of the covariance matrix $\Sigma$ into a local basis aligned with a surface normal $n$, followed by modification of the diagonal elements to enforce flatness (half-Gaussian geometry) [S1].
3.  **Depth Consistency:** Rendering depth maps ($D_{hard}, D_{soft}$) and comparing them against monocular estimates or reprojection errors from multi-view geometry [S1].

All methods require differentiable access to the rendered output (image, depth, or normal) to compute gradients with respect to Gaussian parameters (position $\mu$, scale $s$, rotation $q$, opacity $\alpha$) [S2][S1].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) stabilizes optimization by minimizing the spectral discrepancy between rendered and ground truth images, specifically addressing over-reconstruction where redundant Gaussians form in well-reconstructed regions. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which yields small gradients in these areas and stalls densification, FR increases gradients by explicitly disentangling amplitude and phase components, guiding adaptive densification toward high-frequency detail. It is distinct from [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) which modifies the target signal, whereas FR modulates the loss based on frequency domain errors. Choose this method when training stagnates due to lack of gradient signal in high-fidelity regions.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision stabilizes joint optimization by blurring 2D training images with a scheduled set of 2D Gaussian kernels. It solves the problem of noisy gradients and unstable camera alignment during early training by restricting the high-frequency spectral content of the learned scene. Unlike [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) which modulates loss weights, this method explicitly modifies the target supervision signal itself. It is distinct from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) as it operates on photometric color data rather than geometric depth constraints. Choose this method to prevent early-stage noise and improve camera pose convergence.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method regularizes covariance matrices to enforce a "half-Gaussian" geometry, modeling sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space. It transforms the covariance into a local basis aligned with the surface normal, projects the ellipsoid onto the tangent plane, and reconstructs a flattened covariance that penalizes thickness along the normal direction. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting which uses full ellipsoids, this approach explicitly constrains the shape to be thin, improving geometric fidelity and reducing noise artifacts like "dust". Choose this method when high-fidelity surface reconstruction is required and floaters are prevalent.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization prevents detail loss and blurred appearance by constraining geometry optimization to spatial reshaping (movement of centers) rather than deforming Gaussian shapes. It sets itself apart from standard depth regularization methods, which often force anisotropic Gaussian shapes to fit smooth monocular depth estimates, by freezing scale and rotation parameters and using two distinct rendered depth maps to independently update center positions and opacities. This decoupling balances the fitting of complex color appearance with smooth coarse depth supervision, addressing the sensitivity of Gaussian radiance fields to small depth errors. Choose this method when monocular depth priors are available and shape deformation is undesirable.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and significantly reducing floaters compared to training without this adaptation. Choose this method when multi-view consistency is available but explicit depth priors are not, or to refine geometry progressively from coarse to fine scales.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian splats by constraining their shape and orientation, distinct from depth supervision which primarily constrains spatial location. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by focusing on structural alignment (normals and covariance anisotropy) rather than spectral content, and from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by enforcing surface-aligned orientations rather than just depth value consistency. Choose this method to enforce surfel-like properties and normal consistency.

#### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

#### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.
- [S2] Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.
- [S2] Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.
- [S2] $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

#### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- [S3] Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization.
- [S3] Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians.
- [S3] Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance.
- [S3] Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization.

#### FreGS
- [S4, unofficial] Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training.

## Background
The problem of over-reconstruction and floaters in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises because photometric loss alone does not enforce geometric plausibility, leading to unstructured density clouds [S1]. Early methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) and [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) were introduced to stabilize the optimization landscape by managing spectral content [S2][S4, unofficial]. Subsequent work focused on explicit geometric constraints, such as [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) and [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md), to align Gaussians with underlying surfaces [S1]. Recent surveys highlight the integration of SDFs and multi-view consistency as key trends for improving geometric fidelity [S3][S4, unofficial].
