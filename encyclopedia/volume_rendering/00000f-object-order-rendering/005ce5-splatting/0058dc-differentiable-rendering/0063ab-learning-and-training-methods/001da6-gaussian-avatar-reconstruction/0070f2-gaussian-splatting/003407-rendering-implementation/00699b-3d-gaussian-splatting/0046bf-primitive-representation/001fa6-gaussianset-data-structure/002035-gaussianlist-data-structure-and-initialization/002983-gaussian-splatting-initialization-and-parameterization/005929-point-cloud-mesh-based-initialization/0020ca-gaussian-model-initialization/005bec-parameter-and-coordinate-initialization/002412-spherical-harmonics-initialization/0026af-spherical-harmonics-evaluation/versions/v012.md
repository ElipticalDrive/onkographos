# Spherical Harmonics Evaluation

Also called: SH2RGB, Spherical Harmonics to RGB Conversion

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Evaluation computes the view-dependent RGB color of a 3D Gaussian splat by projecting its stored SH coefficients onto a viewing direction vector [S1, S3, S7, unofficial]. It solves the problem of representing directional information (view direction) in a differentiable, basis-function expansion for view-dependent rendering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Spherical Harmonics Initialization](3163-spherical-harmonics-initialization.md), which only sets the zeroth-order (DC) term from RGB colors, this method evaluates the full expansion up to degree 3 [S1, S7, unofficial]. It differs from [Spherical Harmonics Rotation](3985-spherical-harmonics-rotation.md) by performing the final color computation rather than transforming coefficients, and from [Spherical Harmonics Encoder](4390-spherical-harmonics-encoder.md) by outputting linear RGB values rather than basis coefficients [S1, unofficial][S13].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `gaussian_means`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$ containing the 3D positions of $N$ Gaussians [S1, S2, unofficial].
- `camera_position`: Vector of shape $(3)$ representing the camera center in world space [S1, S2, unofficial].
- `sh_coefficients`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3 \times (d_{max}+1)^2)$ containing the SH coefficients, where $d_{max}$ is the active SH degree (typically 2 or 3) [S1, S9, S10, unofficial]. The coefficients are stored per color channel (R, G, B) interleaved or grouped [S9, unofficial].
- `active_sh_degree`: Integer specifying the maximum degree to evaluate (0, 1, 2, or 3) [S4, S7, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `rgb_colors`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$ containing the linear RGB color values for each Gaussian [S1, S5, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The SH coefficients are typically stored in a flat array or tensor [S4, S9, unofficial]. For degree $d$, there are $(d+1)^2$ coefficients per color channel [S3, S9, unofficial]. The standard ordering for degree 2 (9 coefficients per channel) follows the Condon-Shortley phase convention [S1, S12, unofficial]. The coefficients are indexed as follows for each color channel:
- Degree 0: $c_0$ (1 coeff)
- Degree 1: $c_1, c_2, c_3$ (3 coeffs)
- Degree 2: $c_4, c_5, c_6, c_7, c_8$ (5 coeffs) [S7, unofficial]
- Degree 3: $c_9 \dots c_{15}$ (7 coeffs) [S10, unofficial]

**Steps:**

1. **Compute Viewing Direction:** Calculate the direction vector from the camera to the Gaussian center [S2, unofficial].
   $$ \mathbf{v} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{p}_{gaussian} - \mathbf{p}_{camera}) $$
   Let $\mathbf{v} = (x, y, z)$ [S1, S7, unofficial].

2. **Initialize Color with DC Term:** Extract the zeroth-order coefficients ($c_0$) for R, G, B. Compute the base color using the constant $SH\_C_0 = 0.28209479177387814$ [S1, S8, S9, unofficial]:
   $$ \mathbf{C}_{base} = SH\_C_0 \cdot \mathbf{c}_0 $$
   where $\mathbf{c}_0 = [c_{0,R}, c_{0,G}, c_{0,B}]$ [S1, S8, unofficial].

3. **Evaluate Higher-Order Terms:** Depending on `active_sh_degree`, add contributions from degrees 1, 2, and 3 [S3, unofficial]. Precompute squared and cross terms of the direction vector:
   $$ xx = x^2, \quad yy = y^2, \quad zz = z^2 $$
   $$ xy = x \cdot y, \quad yz = y \cdot z, \quad xz = x \cdot z $$
   [S1, S7, S9, unofficial].

   - **Degree 1 (Linear):** Uses coefficients $c_1, c_2, c_3$ and constant $SH\_C_1 = 0.4886025119029199$ [S1, S9, unofficial]:
     $$ \Delta \mathbf{C}_1 = SH\_C_1 \cdot [-y \cdot c_1 + z \cdot c_2 - x \cdot c_3] $$
     [S1, S10, unofficial].

   - **Degree 2 (Quadratic):** Uses coefficients $c_4 \dots c_8$ and constants $SH\_C2 = [1.0925484305920792, -1.0925484305920792, 0.31539156525252005, -1.0925484305920792, 0.5462742152960396]$ [S1, S9, unofficial]:
     $$ \Delta \mathbf{C}_2 = SH\_C2_0 \cdot xy \cdot c_4 + SH\_C2_1 \cdot yz \cdot c_5 + SH\_C2_2 \cdot (2zz - xx - yy) \cdot c_6 + SH\_C2_3 \cdot xz \cdot c_7 + SH\_C2_4 \cdot (xx - yy) \cdot c_8 $$
     [S1, S7, S10, unofficial].

   - **Degree 3 (Cubic):** Uses coefficients $c_9 \dots c_{15}$ and constants $SH\_C3 = [-0.5900435899266435, 2.890611442640554, -0.4570457994644658, 0.3731763325901154, -0.4570457994644658, 1.445305721320277, -0.5900435899266435]$ [S1, S9, unofficial]:
     $$ \begin{aligned} \Delta \mathbf{C}_3 = & SH\_C3_0 \cdot y(3xx - yy) \cdot c_9 + SH\_C3_1 \cdot xyz \cdot c_{10} + SH\_C3_2 \cdot y(4zz - xx - yy) \cdot c_{11} \\ & + SH\_C3_3 \cdot z(2zz - 3xx - 3yy) \cdot c_{12} + SH\_C3_4 \cdot x(4zz - xx - yy) \cdot c_{13} \\ & + SH\_C3_5 \cdot z(xx - yy) \cdot c_{14} + SH\_C3_6 \cdot x(xx - 3yy) \cdot c_{15} \end{aligned} $$
     [S1, S10, unofficial].

4. **Sum and Bias:** Add all contributions and apply a bias of 0.5 to shift the range from $[-1, 1]$ to $[0.5, 1.5]$ (or similar, depending on coefficient scaling) [S1, S5, S9, unofficial]:
   $$ \mathbf{C}_{raw} = \mathbf{C}_{base} + \Delta \mathbf{C}_1 + \Delta \mathbf{C}_2 + \Delta \mathbf{C}_3 + 0.5 $$
   [S1, S5, S9, unofficial].

5. **Clamp:** Clamp the resulting RGB values to the valid range $[0.0, 1.0]$ [S1, S5, S9, S10, unofficial].
   $$ \mathbf{C}_{final} = \text{clamp}(\mathbf{C}_{raw}, 0.0, 1.0) $$
   [S1, S5, S9, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch

def eval_sh(degree: int, sh_coeffs: torch.Tensor, view_dirs: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """
    Evaluate spherical harmonics to compute RGB colors.
    
    Args:
        degree: Active SH degree (0, 1, 2, or 3).
        sh_coeffs: Tensor of shape (N, 3 * (degree+1)**2). 
                   Coefficients are stored as [R_coeffs, G_coeffs, B_coeffs] 
                   where each channel has (degree+1)**2 coefficients.
        view_dirs: Tensor of shape (N, 3). Normalized direction vectors from camera to Gaussian.
        
    Returns:
        rgb_colors: Tensor of shape (N, 3).
    """
    # Constants
    SH_C0 = 0.28209479177387814
    SH_C1 = 0.4886025119029199
    SH_C2 = torch.tensor([1.0925484305920792, -1.0925484305920792, 0.31539156525252005, 
                          -1.0925484305920792, 0.5462742152960396])
    SH_C3 = torch.tensor([-0.5900435899266435, 2.890611442640554, -0.4570457994644658, 
                          0.3731763325901154, -0.4570457994644658, 1.445305721320277, 
                          -0.5900435899266435])

    # Reshape coefficients to (N, 3, num_coeffs)
    num_coeffs = (degree + 1) ** 2
    sh_coeffs = sh_coeffs.view(sh_coeffs.shape[0], 3, num_coeffs)
    
    # Extract x, y, z
    x, y, z = view_dirs[:, 0], view_dirs[:, 1], view_dirs[:, 2]
    
    # Precompute terms
    xx, yy, zz = x*x, y*y, z*z
    xy, yz, xz = x*y, y*z, x*z
    
    # Initialize color with DC term (Degree 0)
    # sh_coeffs[:, :, 0] contains the DC coefficients for R, G, B
    color = SH_C0 * sh_coeffs[:, :, 0]
    
    if degree >= 1:
        # Degree 1: indices 1, 2, 3
        c1, c2, c3 = sh_coeffs[:, :, 1], sh_coeffs[:, :, 2], sh_coeffs[:, :, 3]
        color += SH_C1 * (-y * c1 + z * c2 - x * c3)
        
    if degree >= 2:
        # Degree 2: indices 4, 5, 6, 7, 8
        c4, c5, c6, c7, c8 = sh_coeffs[:, :, 4], sh_coeffs[:, :, 5], sh_coeffs[:, :, 6], sh_coeffs[:, :, 7], sh_coeffs[:, :, 8]
        term1 = SH_C2[0] * xy * c4
        term2 = SH_C2[1] * yz * c5
        term3 = SH_C2[2] * (2.0 * zz - xx - yy) * c6
        term4 = SH_C2[3] * xz * c7
        term5 = SH_C2[4] * (xx - yy) * c8
        color += term1 + term2 + term3 + term4 + term5
        
    if degree >= 3:
        # Degree 3: indices 9 to 15
        c9, c10, c11, c12, c13, c14, c15 = sh_coeffs[:, :, 9], sh_coeffs[:, :, 10], sh_coeffs[:, :, 11], sh_coeffs[:, :, 12], sh_coeffs[:, :, 13], sh_coeffs[:, :, 14], sh_coeffs[:, :, 15]
        term1 = SH_C3[0] * y * (3.0 * xx - yy) * c9
        term2 = SH_C3[1] * xy * z * c10
        term3 = SH_C3[2] * y * (4.0 * zz - xx - yy) * c11
        term4 = SH_C3[3] * z * (2.0 * zz - 3.0 * xx - 3.0 * yy) * c12
        term5 = SH_C3[4] * x * (4.0 * zz - xx - yy) * c13
        term6 = SH_C3[5] * z * (xx - yy) * c14
        term7 = SH_C3[6] * x * (xx - 3.0 * yy) * c15
        color += term1 + term2 + term3 + term4 + term5 + term6 + term7

    # Apply bias and clamp
    color = color + 0.5
    color = torch.clamp(color, 0.0, 1.0)
    
    return color
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Zero Direction:** If the Gaussian is exactly at the camera position, the direction vector is undefined [G].
- **Degree Mismatch:** The number of coefficients in `sh_coefficients` must match the `active_sh_degree` [S3, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Using `float32` is standard [S1, S7, unofficial].
- **Coordinate System:** The direction vector must be computed in the same coordinate system as the SH coefficients are defined [S13, S17].

## Subtopics

### [Spherical Harmonics Rotation](3985-spherical-harmonics-rotation.md)
Spherical Harmonics Rotation computes the new SH coefficients of a function after rotating the viewing coordinate system by $\pm \pi/2$ around the X-axis. It sets itself apart from general rotation methods by using precomputed analytic tables for specific $\pm 90^\circ$ rotations, avoiding expensive matrix multiplications or numerical integration. Choose it when the Gaussian splat's orientation changes relative to the camera, requiring the SH coefficients to be transformed to maintain view-dependent consistency.

### [Spherical Harmonics Encoder](4390-spherical-harmonics-encoder.md)
A neural network module that encodes a 3D direction vector into spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients up to a specified degree. It solves the problem of representing directional information (view direction) in a differentiable, basis-function expansion for view-dependent rendering. Choose it when a differentiable basis-function expansion is required rather than direct RGB evaluation.

### [Spherical Harmonics Data Extraction](6960-spherical-harmonics-data-extraction.md)
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Data Extraction parses SH coefficients from a 3D Gaussian Splatting model file (typically a PLY format) into a structured data layout suitable for GPU rendering. It solves the problem of mapping flat, property-based storage in the source file to the hierarchical texture arrays required by the rendering engine. Choose it when loading model files, as it is distinct from [Spherical Harmonics Evaluation](3848-spherical-harmonics-evaluation.md), which computes color from these coefficients, and [Spherical Harmonics Rotation](3985-spherical-harmonics-rotation.md), which transforms them for view-dependent effects.

### [Spherical Harmonics Decoding](7901-spherical-harmonics-decoding.md)
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Decoding is the process of extracting and converting stored SH coefficients into view-dependent RGB color values for 3D Gaussian splats. It solves the problem of interpreting compressed, packed coefficient data into a format suitable for rendering. Choose it when handling unpacking mechanics, as it differs from [Spherical Harmonics Evaluation](3848-spherical-harmonics-evaluation.md) by focusing on data layout rather than mathematical basis function evaluation, and from [Spherical Harmonics Rotation](3985-spherical-harmonics-rotation.md) by assuming the coefficients are already in the camera space or handling the unpacking prior to rotation.

### [Spherical Harmonics Lighting Calculation](11516-spherical-harmonics-lighting-calculation.md)
Spherical Harmonics Lighting Calculation computes the final RGB color of a 3D Gaussian primitive by evaluating its stored spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients against a viewing direction vector. It solves the problem of representing view-dependent appearance compactly within the Gaussian Splatting framework. Choose it when explicit phase conventions and stability biases are required, as it differs from [Spherical Harmonics Evaluation](3848-spherical-harmonics-evaluation.md) by explicitly applying the Condon–Shortley phase conventions, the specific 0.5 bias for numeric stability, and the hardcoded polynomial basis functions for degrees 0–3 required by the 3D Gaussian Splatting standard.

### Spherical Harmonics Evaluation with Tone Mapping
A module that interprets network output as spherical harmonics coefficients to model view-dependent effects and camera-specific parameters, followed by tone mapping [S14]. The SH coefficients are the output of the last convolution of the neural network [S14]. It applies a tone mapper following Rückert et al. [RFS22] which models exposure time, white balance, sensor response, and vignetting [S14]. It uses spherical harmonics degree 2, corresponding to 27 input coefficients (9 coefficients per color channel) [S14].

### SH Evaluation With Normal
Computing irradiance per-vertex by evaluating spherical harmonic coefficients against a surface normal, avoiding per-pixel cubemap sampling [S15, unofficial]. It computes linear terms by dotting the first 4 coefficients (cAr, cAg, cAb) with the vertex normal (vNormal) [S15, unofficial]. It computes quadratic terms by creating a vector vB from the normal components (xyzz * yzzx) and dotting it with the next 4 coefficients (cBr, cBg, cBb) [S15, unofficial]. It computes the final quadratic term using the constant coefficient cC multiplied by (vNormal.x*vNormal.x - vNormal.y*vNormal.y) [S15, unofficial]. It sums the linear, quadratic, and final quadratic components to produce the output diffuse color [S15, unofficial].

### GPU Constant Store Interpolation
If enough constant store is available, it sends all nearby samples and their gradients to the vertex shader and does the interpolation per-vertex [S15, unofficial]. If constant store is too costly, it interpolates on the CPU and sends a single interpolated sample and interpolated gradient to the vertex shader [S15, unofficial]. A 6th order SH approximation for R, G, and B requires 108 floats, and 6th order SH gradients for R, G, and B require 324 floats, totaling 432 floats per sample [S15, unofficial]. A 3rd order SH approximation for R, G, and B requires 27 floats, and 3rd order SH gradients for R, G, and B require 81 floats, totaling 108 floats per sample [S15, unofficial]. Modern GPUs can typically store 1024 to 2048 floats in VS constant store [S15, unofficial].

### Spherical Harmonics for View-Dependent Colors
SH is parameterized by two integers: l (number of azimuth/nodal lines) and m (number of oscillations/variations) [S16, unofficial]. Each color channel is expressed as a linear combination of SH functions up to a maximum degree l_max [S16, unofficial]. For l_max = 1, there are 3 learned parameters per channel (m=0,1,2) [S16, unofficial]. Learned coefficients are mapped to the [0, 1] interval using a sigmoid function or clipping [S16, unofficial].

### Splat Lighting
It computes each splat's color from its spherical harmonics coefficients and the normalized viewing direction (vector from the camera position to the splat's mean in the global space) [S17]. It transforms the spherical harmonics coefficients by the rotation component of the global transformation matrix of the glTF node that instantiates the mesh containing the splat primitive [S17]. Implementations may ignore higher-degree spherical harmonic coefficients for performance reasons [S17]. This transformation is usually performed using Wigner-D matrices [S17].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Scales linearly with the number of Gaussians $N$ and quadratically with the active SH degree $d$ [S1, S5, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot d^2)$ to store the SH coefficients [S1, S9, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** Evaluation is performed per-splat in the rendering pipeline [S1, S5, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Compact representation of view-dependent effects; differentiable; efficient evaluation using hardcoded constants [S1, S9, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Implementations may ignore higher-degree coefficients for performance reasons [S13, S17].
- **When to Use:** Used when view-dependent appearance is modeled via SH coefficients [S1, S9, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** For purely diffuse, view-independent materials, degree 0 (DC) is sufficient, and higher degrees can be skipped for performance [S13, S17].

## Background
In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, SH evaluation was adapted from radiance field representations to provide efficient, view-dependent coloring for Gaussian primitives [S1, S5, unofficial]. The specific constants and polynomial forms used in Gaussian Splatting are derived from the standard real spherical harmonics with the Condon-Shortley phase [S1, S10, unofficial].
