# Splatt3R

Also called: Splatt3R: Zero-Shot Gaussian Splatting from Uncalibrated Image Pairs

## Overview
Splatt3R is a feed-forward, pose-free method that predicts 3D Gaussian Splat primitives directly from a stereo pair of uncalibrated images, without requiring camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth maps [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike existing generalizable 3D-GS methods that are limited to interpolating viewpoints between input images, Splatt3R supports extrapolation to wider baselines by employing a novel loss masking strategy to prevent training on unseen or obscured geometry [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the MASt3R foundation model, extending its point-cloud prediction capability with a dedicated Gaussian head for appearance and covariance attributes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Two uncalibrated RGB images, $I_1, I_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$.
- Typical input resolution: $512 \times 512$ pixels [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A set of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G}$ representing the scene geometry and appearance. Each primitive $j$ is defined by mean $\mu_j$, covariance $\Sigma_j$, opacity $\alpha_j$, and color $c_j$ [S2].

### Data Structures
The method operates on a per-pixel basis, predicting one Gaussian primitive per pixel for each input image. The final scene is the union of primitives from both images.

**Gaussian Primitive $j$:**
- $\mu_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
- $q_j \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation quaternion.
- $s_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector.
- $\alpha_j \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity.
- $c_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Color (view-independent constant color is used in the primary experiments; view-dependent spherical harmonics are an ablation) [S1, unofficial].

### Architecture and Prediction
The model extends the MASt3R architecture [S1, unofficial]. MASt3R consists of a frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and cross-attention transformers [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Encoding:** The shared ViT encoder processes $I_1$ and $I_2$. Cross-attention transformers fuse features between the two images. These components are frozen during training [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Point Cloud Prediction (MASt3R Backbone):**
    -   Predicts base 3D point locations $\hat{X}_1, \hat{X}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times H \times 3}$ and confidence maps $C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times H}$ for each pixel in the coordinate frame of the first image [S1, unofficial].
    -   This avoids the need for explicit camera pose estimation or intrinsic calibration [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Gaussian Head:**
    -   A third parallel branch, referred to as the "Gaussian head," is added to the MASt3R backbone.
    -   It uses a DPT (Depth Anything) architecture [S1, unofficial].
    -   For each pixel $j$, it predicts:
        -   Position offset $\Delta x_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
        -   Rotation quaternion $q_j \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
        -   Scale $s_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
        -   Opacity $\alpha_j \in \mathbb{R}$.
        -   Color residual or spherical harmonics coefficients [S1, unofficial].
    -   The final mean position is calculated as:
        $$ \mu_j = x_j + \Delta x_j $$
        where $x_j$ is the base position predicted by the MASt3R point cloud head [S2].
    -   The covariance matrix $\Sigma_j$ is derived from the predicted rotation and scale to ensure positive semi-definiteness:
        $$ \Sigma_j = R(q_j) S(s_j) S(s_j)^T R(q_j)^T $$
        where $R(q_j)$ is the rotation matrix constructed from quaternion $q_j$, and $S(s_j)$ is a diagonal matrix with scale factors $s_j$ [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Activation Functions
Specific activations are applied to ensure valid parameter ranges [S1, unofficial]:
-   **Quaternions ($q$):** Normalization (L2-normalization to unit length).
-   **Scales ($s$) and Offsets ($\Delta$):** Exponential activation.
-   **Opacities ($\alpha$):** Sigmoid activation.

### Training Procedure
Training is performed in two stages to avoid local minima and ensure geometric stability [S1, unofficial].

**Stage 1: Geometry Loss**
-   The model is pre-trained or initialized using the MASt3R point cloud geometry loss $L_{pts}$ [S1, unofficial].
-   This loss supervises the base point predictions $x_j$ against ground-truth point clouds derived from high-resolution laser scans (e.g., in ScanNet++) [S1, unofficial].
-   This stage does not train the Gaussian head's appearance/covariance parameters, but establishes the geometric backbone [S1, unofficial].

**Stage 2: Novel View Synthesis (NVS) with Loss Masking**
-   The Gaussian head is trained using a rendering loss.
-   **Data Selection:** Context images ($I_1, I_2$) and target images ($I_{tgt}$) are selected such that:
    -   At least $\phi\%$ of pixels in $I_2$ have correspondences in $I_1$.
    -   At least $\psi\%$ of pixels in $I_{tgt}$ are present in at least one context image [S1, unofficial].
    -   Typical hyperparameter values for $(\phi, \psi)$: Close (0.9, 0.9), Medium (0.7, 0.7), Wide (0.5, 0.5), Very Wide (0.3, 0.3) [S1, unofficial].
-   **Loss Masking ($M$):**
    -   To prevent destructive gradients from unseen geometry (occluded or outside frustum), a binary mask $M \in \{0, 1\}^{W \times H}$ is computed for the target view [S1, unofficial].
    -   For each pixel in $I_{tgt}$:
        1.  Unproject the pixel to 3D using ground-truth depth.
        2.  Reproject onto $I_1$ and $I_2$.
        3.  Check if the reprojected depth matches the ground-truth depth within a tolerance.
        4.  Check if the pixel lies within the frustum of at least one context image.
        5.  If both conditions are met, the pixel is marked as valid (mask value 1) [S1, unofficial].
-   **Rendering Loss:**
    -   The rendered image $\hat{I}$ is compared to the ground truth $I$ using only the masked pixels:
        $$ L = \lambda_{MSE} L_{MSE}(M \odot \hat{I}, M \odot I) + \lambda_{LPIPS} L_{LPIPS}(M \odot \hat{I}, M \odot I) $$
    -   $L_{MSE}$ is Mean Squared Error.
    -   $L_{LPIPS}$ is Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity.
    -   $\lambda_{MSE}$ and $\lambda_{LPIPS}$ are weighting hyperparameters [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class Splatt3R:
    def __init__(self, pretrained_mast3r_path):
        """
        Initialize Splatt3R.
        - Loads the frozen MASt3R backbone (ViT + Cross-Attention + Point Head).
        - Initializes the parallel Gaussian Head (DPT-based).
        """
        self.mast3r_backbone = load_mast3r(pretrained_mast3r_path)
        # Freeze MASt3R parameters
        for param in self.mast3r_backbone.parameters():
            param.requires_grad = False
        
        self.gaussian_head = DPTHead() # Parallel branch to MASt3R's Point Head
        
        # Hyperparameters for activation functions
        self.gamma = 1.0 # For confidence weighting if used in stage 1
        
    def encode_and_predict_points(self, I1, I2):
        """
        Runs the frozen MASt3R backbone.
        Returns base positions X and confidences C in the coordinate frame of I1.
        """
        # Shared ViT Encoder
        features1, features2 = self.mast3r_backbone.encoder(I1, I2)
        
        # Cross-Attention Transformers
        features1, features2 = self.mast3r_backbone.cross_attn(features1, features2)
        
        # Point Cloud Heads (Decoder 1 and 2)
        X_hat_1, C_1 = self.mast3r_backbone.point_head_1(features1)
        X_hat_2, C_2 = self.mast3r_backbone.point_head_2(features2)
        
        return X_hat_1, X_hat_2, C_1, C_2

    def predict_gaussian_params(self, features1, features2):
        """
        Runs the Gaussian Head on the fused features.
        Returns offsets, rotations, scales, opacities, and colors.
        """
        # DPT Head takes the encoded features
        # Note: In implementation, this might share intermediate layers or take concatenated features
        offsets = self.gaussian_head.predict_offsets(features1)
        rotations_raw = self.gaussian_head.predict_rotations(features1)
        scales_raw = self.gaussian_head.predict_scales(features1)
        opacities_raw = self.gaussian_head.predict_opacities(features1)
        colors = self.gaussian_head.predict_colors(features1)
        
        return offsets, rotations_raw, scales_raw, opacities_raw, colors

    def apply_activations(self, offsets, rotations_raw, scales_raw, opacities_raw):
        """
        Applies specific activation functions to ensure valid parameter ranges.
        """
        # Exponential for scales and offsets
        offsets = torch.exp(offsets)
        scales = torch.exp(scales_raw)
        
        # Sigmoid for opacities
        opacities = torch.sigmoid(opacities_raw)
        
        # Normalization for quaternions (L2 norm to 1)
        rotations = F.normalize(rotations_raw, dim=-1)
        
        return offsets, rotations, scales, opacities

    def construct_gaussians(self, X_hat, offsets, rotations, scales, opacities, colors):
        """
        Constructs the final Gaussian primitives.
        Mean: mu = X_hat + offset
        Covariance: Sigma = R(q) S(s) S(s)^T R(q)^T
        """
        # Calculate Means
        mu = X_hat + offsets
        
        # Calculate Covariance Matrices
        # R(q) is rotation matrix from quaternion q
        # S(s) is diagonal matrix of scale s
        R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(rotations)
        S = torch.diag_embed(scales)
        Sigma = torch.bmm(R, torch.bmm(S, torch.transpose(S, 1, 2)))
        # Note: In efficient rasterization, Sigma is often decomposed or stored as R, S
        
        return mu, Sigma, opacities, colors

    def compute_loss_mask(self, target_depth, target_K, context_K, context_T, context_depths):
        """
        Computes the binary loss mask M for a target view.
        M[i,j] = 1 if the point is visible and covisible in context views.
        """
        H, W = target_depth.shape
        M = torch.zeros(H, W, dtype=torch.bool)
        
        # Unproject target pixels to 3D using ground truth depth and intrinsics
        # 3D_target = unproject(target_depth, target_K)
        
        for i in range(H):
            for j in range(W):
                if target_depth[i, j] <= 0:
                    continue
                    
                pt_3d = unproject_pixel(target_depth[i, j], (i, j), target_K)
                
                visible = False
                for k, context_idx in enumerate([0, 1]):
                    # Reproject to context view
                    pt_2d_ctx = project_point(pt_3d, context_K[k], context_T[k])
                    
                    # Check if inside frustum
                    if is_inside_frustum(pt_2d_ctx, context_depths[k].shape):
                        # Check depth consistency
                        ctx_depth = get_depth_at(context_depths[k], pt_2d_ctx)
                        if ctx_depth > 0 and abs(ctx_depth - depth_from_ray(pt_3d, context_T[k])) < tolerance:
                            visible = True
                            break
                
                if visible:
                    M[i, j] = True
                    
        return M

    def forward(self, I1, I2):
        """
        Inference pipeline.
        """
        # 1. Encode and get base points from MASt3R
        X1, X2, C1, C2 = self.encode_and_predict_points(I1, I2)
        
        # 2. Predict Gaussian parameters via Gaussian Head
        # Assuming Gaussian Head takes the same intermediate features as the point heads
        offsets1, rot1_raw, scale1_raw, op1_raw, col1 = self.predict_gaussian_params(X1.features, X2.features)
        offsets2, rot2_raw, scale2_raw, op2_raw, col2 = self.predict_gaussian_params(X2.features, X1.features) # Symmetric or distinct? Source says union from both.
        
        # 3. Apply Activations
        offsets1, rot1, scale1, op1 = self.apply_activations(offsets1, rot1_raw, scale1_raw, op1_raw)
        offsets2, rot2, scale2, op2 = self.apply_activations(offsets2, rot2_raw, scale2_raw, op2_raw)
        
        # 4. Construct Gaussians
        # Note: X1 is in frame of I1. X2 is in frame of I1 (MASt3R property).
        # So we can directly union them.
        mu1 = X1 + offsets1
        mu2 = X2 + offsets2
        
        # Construct full set
        mu = torch.cat([mu1.view(-1, 3), mu2.view(-1, 3)], dim=0)
        # Similarly for Sigma, alpha, color
        # ...
        
        return mu, Sigma, alpha, color

    def training_step(self, I1, I2, I_tgt, tgt_depth, tgt_K, ctx_K, ctx_T, ctx_depths):
        """
        Single training step with loss masking.
        """
        # Forward pass
        X1, X2, _, _ = self.encode_and_predict_points(I1, I2)
        offsets, rots_raw, scales_raw, ops_raw, colors = self.predict_gaussian_params(X1.features, X2.features)
        offsets, rots, scales, ops = self.apply_activations(offsets, rots_raw, scales_raw, ops_raw)
        
        mu = X1 + offsets # Assuming we render from the union, simplified here for loss calc
        # Construct full Gaussians and Render I_hat
        I_hat = render_gaussians(mu, rots, scales, ops, colors)
        
        # Compute Loss Mask
        M = self.compute_loss_mask(tgt_depth, tgt_K, ctx_K, ctx_T, ctx_depths)
        
        # Compute Loss
        masked_I_hat = I_hat * M
        masked_I_tgt = I_tgt * M
        
        loss_mse = F.mse_loss(masked_I_hat, masked_I_tgt)
        loss_lpips = lpips_loss(masked_I_hat, masked_I_tgt)
        
        total_loss = lambda_mse * loss_mse + lambda_lpips * loss_lpips
        
        return total_loss
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
-   **Unseen Geometry:** Supervising rendering loss for pixels not visible in context views (obscured or outside frustums) is counterproductive and destructive to model performance [S1, unofficial]. This is mitigated by the loss masking strategy [S1, unofficial].
-   **Local Minima:** Training 3D Gaussian Splats directly from stereo views without geometric priors can get stuck in local minima due to vanishing gradients for points far from their correct location [S1, unofficial]. The two-stage training (geometry first, then appearance) addresses this [S1, unofficial].
-   **Frame Consistency:** Predicted covariances and spherical harmonics are considered in the first image's camera frame to avoid needing ground-truth transformations for conversion between reference frames [S1, unofficial]. The final set of primitives is the union of those predicted from both images [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** The method is feed-forward, requiring a single forward pass of the network. Construction/export runtime is approximately 1.10 seconds for a scene with 2 views on ScanNet++ [S2].
-   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given in the sources, but depends on the number of pixels ($H \times W$) and the number of input images. Since it predicts per-pixel Gaussians, memory scales linearly with image resolution [S1, unofficial].
-   **Performance:**
    -   On ScanNet++ test scenes with Close view overlap ($\phi=0.9, \psi=0.9$): PSNR 19.66, SSIM 0.757, LPIPS 0.234 [S1, unofficial].
    -   On ScanNet++ with 2 views (reported in [S2]): PSNR 23.30, SSIM 0.823, LPIPS 0.125.
    -   Rendering is real-time once the splats are constructed [S1, unofficial].
-   **Strengths:**
    -   Pose-free: Works with uncalibrated images.
    -   Generalizable: Trained on ScanNet++, generalizes to in-the-wild images.
    -   Extrapolation: Capable of synthesizing views beyond the stereo baseline, unlike many competitors that only interpolate [S1, unofficial].
-   **Limitations:**
    -   Requires pre-training on a large dataset (ScanNet++) [S1, unofficial].
    -   Performance drops as view overlap decreases (e.g., PSNR 18.56 for Very Wide overlap) [S1, unofficial].
    -   Quality is lower than iterative per-scene optimization methods (e.g., 3D-GS) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Splatt3R was introduced in 2024 by Brandon, Zheng, Iro, and Victor from the University of Oxford [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the MASt3R foundation model for 3D reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the limitations of existing generalizable 3D-GS methods (like pixelSplat and MVSplat) which typically require camera intrinsics or are limited to interpolating between input views [S1, unofficial]. Splatt3R demonstrates that adding a Gaussian decoder to a large-scale pretrained geometry model is sufficient for pose-free novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial].
