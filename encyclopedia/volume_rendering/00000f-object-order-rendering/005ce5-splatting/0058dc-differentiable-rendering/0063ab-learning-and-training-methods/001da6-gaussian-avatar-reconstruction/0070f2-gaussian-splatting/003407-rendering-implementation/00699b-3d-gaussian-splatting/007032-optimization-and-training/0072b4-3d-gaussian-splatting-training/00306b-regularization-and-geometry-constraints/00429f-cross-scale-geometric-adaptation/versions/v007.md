# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo-labels, ensuring geometric integrity and reducing floaters more robustly than single-scale depth priors [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on complex scheduling or external feature extractors, this approach adapts multi-scale voxel geometry by leveraging low-resolution voxels for coarse guidance early in training and high-resolution voxels for detail refinement later [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training views (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$, multi-scale voxel representations (low, mid, high resolution), and rendered depth maps $D_l(p)$ and RGB maps $C_i(p)$ for each scale $l \in \{low, mid, high\}$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ used to update voxel parameters.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxels:** Multi-scale grid structures where each scale $l$ stores depth values $D_l$ and color values $C_l$ at pixel coordinates. The specific bit-packing or memory layout of the voxels is not detailed in the sources [G: assumed to be standard dense or sparse voxel grid compatible with the rendering engine].
*   **Pixel Coordinates:** 2D coordinates $p_i$ and their homogeneous augmentation $\tilde{p}_i = [x, y, 1]^T$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Select Training Rays:** Sample rays $r_{train}$ from the training view $i$.
2.  **Compute 3D Points per Scale:** For each scale $l$ and pixel $p_i$:
    *   Retrieve the rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ from volume rendering at scale $l$.
    *   Compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$:
        $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    *   Transform to world coordinates using frame $i$'s pose $(R_i, t_i)$:
        $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    *   *Alternative Definition:* The sources also note that $x_l$ can be determined directly from the ray origin $o_i(p_i)$ and direction $v_i(p_i)$:
        $$ x_l = o_i(p_i) + D_{il}(p_i) v_i(p_i) $$
3.  **Reproject to Second View:** For a second training view $j$:
    *   Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to camera coordinates of frame $j$:
        $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    *   Project back to 2D pixel coordinates in frame $j$:
        $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
        where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$.
4.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the difference between the original RGB pixel and the warped pixel from frame $j$:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    Here, $C_i$ and $C_j$ are the RGB maps of frames $i$ and $j$.
5.  **Select Pseudo-Label:** Identify the scale $l'$ that minimizes the reprojection error for the given ray:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    The depth at this optimal scale $D'(r_{train})$ serves as the pseudo-ground truth.
6.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth at each scale and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    *Note: The summation index $l=0$ to $L$ implies all scales are penalized against the best-matching scale's depth, or specifically that the loss is computed for the training rays.*

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geo_loss(frames, poses, intrinsics, voxels, rays):
    """
    Computes the cross-scale geometric adaptation loss Lgeo.
    
    Args:
        frames: List of training view images (RGB maps C_i, C_j).
        poses: List of camera poses (R_i, t_i).
        intrinsics: Camera intrinsic matrices K_i.
        voxels: Multi-scale voxel structure providing depth D_l and color C_l.
        rays: List of rays sampled from training view i.
        
    Returns:
        L_geo: Scalar loss value.
    """
    L_geo = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        # 1. Get pixel coordinate p_i and homogeneous augmentation
        p_i = ray.pixel_coordinate
        p_i_hom = np.array([p_i[0], p_i[1], 1.0])
        
        # 2. Compute 3D points and reprojection errors for all scales
        errors = []
        depths_per_scale = []
        
        for l in range(num_scales):
            # Get depth from volume rendering at scale l
            D_il = voxels.get_depth(p_i, scale=l)
            
            # Compute 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
            # x_li = D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_hom
            # Note: The source formula x_li = D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_hom assumes 
            # D_il is the depth along the ray direction, effectively scaling the 
            # back-projected vector. Standard back-projection is K^-1 * p_hom.
            # We follow the source formula strictly.
            x_li = D_il * np.linalg.inv(intrinsics[i]) @ p_i_hom
            
            # Transform to world coordinates
            R_i, t_i = poses[i]
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            # Transform to camera coordinates of frame j
            R_j, t_j = poses[j]
            x_li_to_j = R_j.T @ (x_l - t_j)
            
            # Project to 2D pixel coordinates in frame j
            # pi([x,y,z]) = [x/z, y/z]
            if x_li_to_j[2] == 0:
                continue # Avoid division by zero
            p_li_to_j = np.array([x_li_to_j[0] / x_li_to_j[2], 
                                  x_li_to_j[1] / x_li_to_j[2]])
            
            # Compute reprojection error (RGB difference)
            # C_i(p_i) is the color at p_i in frame i
            # C_j(p_li_to_j) is the color at warped coordinate in frame j
            C_i = frames[i][p_i[0], p_i[1]]
            C_j = frames[j][p_li_to_j[0], p_li_to_j[1]]
            
            e_l = np.linalg.norm(C_i - C_j)
            errors.append(e_l)
            depths_per_scale.append(D_il)
            
        # 3. Select scale with minimal reprojection error
        best_scale_idx = np.argmin(errors)
        D_prime = depths_per_scale[best_scale_idx]
        
        # 4. Compute Loss: MSE between rendered depths at all scales and D_prime
        # Sum over all scales l
        scale_loss = 0.0
        for l in range(num_scales):
            D_l = depths_per_scale[l]
            scale_loss += (D_l - D_prime)**2
            
        L_geo += scale_loss
        
    return L_geo
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Number of Scales:** Typically 3 (Low, Mid, High) as indicated by the figures and text [S1].
*   **Loss Weighting:** The method is part of the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photometric} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg}$ [S1]. The specific value for $\lambda_{geo}$ is not given in the sources [G: typical values for such losses are often in the range of 0.1 to 1.0, but must be tuned].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusions:** The method assumes geometric consistency between views. In cases of significant occlusion or non-Lambertian surfaces, reprojection errors may be high for all scales, potentially leading to incorrect pseudo-labels [G: standard multi-view stereo limitation].
*   **Depth Ambiguity:** If multiple scales yield similar reprojection errors, the selection of the "best" scale may be unstable.
*   **Camera Pose Accuracy:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. Inaccurate poses lead to incorrect reprojection and thus incorrect geometric supervision [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method requires computing reprojections for all scales and all pairs of training views (or a subset). This adds significant computational overhead per training iteration compared to photometric loss alone, as it involves matrix inversions and 3D-to-2D projections for each scale [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel representations and intermediate 3D points for all scales during the loss computation.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. It achieves lower final depth error than single-scale depth priors ($\mathcal{L}_d$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive; does not require complex scheduling or external depth priors; robust to geometric noise compared to feature-warping methods like GeCoNeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses; may face challenges in few-shot scenarios with sparse views or significant viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed in the FrugalNeRF framework [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the need for fast convergence and regularization in extreme few-shot novel view synthesis without learned priors [S1]. It builds upon the concept of multi-scale voxels to capture varying scene frequencies [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in FrugalNeRF for few-shot NeRF training [S1].
