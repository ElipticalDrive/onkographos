# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy that decomposes the optimization of 3D Gaussian parameters into sequential phases to address specific stability or complexity challenges. It separates the learning of stable geometric/appearance features from complex constraints such as codec quantization, material decomposition, or high-resolution refinement. Unlike single-stage training, this method prevents gradient conflicts and instability by freezing or constraining parameters optimized in earlier stages during later phases [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The method generally follows a sequential dependency where Stage $k$ initializes from the output of Stage $k-1$. The specific implementation varies by application domain (CT reconstruction, inverse rendering, or compression), but the core logic involves a coarse/stable phase followed by a fine/complex phase.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Context views (images/depth), camera poses, and optionally target views for supervision.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ (positions $\mathbf{p}$, scales $\mathbf{s}$, rotations $\mathbf{q}$, opacities $\alpha$, colors $\mathbf{c}$) and potentially auxiliary parameters (materials $\mathbf{M}$, latent codes, or depth maps).

### Data Structures
The Gaussian representation $\mathcal{G}$ consists of a set of $N$ primitives. Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
*   Position: $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Scale: $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation: $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity: $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color: $\mathbf{c}_i$ (typically SH coefficients or RGB)

In CodecSplat [S3, unofficial], an intermediate feature tensor $\mathbf{F}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C_F \times H \times W}$ is used to predict Gaussians. In GS-IR [S2], material parameters $\mathbf{M} = \{\mathbf{a}, \rho, \mathbf{m}\}$ are added to the geometric set $\mathbf{T} = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$.

### Numbered Steps

#### Variant 1: Low-Resolution to Full-Resolution (3DGR-CT) [S1]
Used for sparse-view CT reconstruction to manage computational burden and place Gaussians correctly.

1.  **Stage 1 (Low-Resolution Optimization):**
    *   Sample a low-resolution grid, specifically $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume.
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters rapidly to place them in approximately correct positions.
    *   Loss: Minimize the $L_2$ norm between projected data $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and actual measurements $\mathbf{p}$:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \| \hat{\mathbf{p}}[\mathcal{V}(\theta)] - \mathbf{p} \|_2^2 $$
        where $\mathcal{V}$ is the discretized volume and $\theta_i$ are the Gaussian parameters [S1].

2.  **Stage 2 (Full-Resolution Refinement):**
    *   Sample over all voxels of the original volume.
    *   Refine Gaussian parameters for fine-grained adjustments and higher-quality reconstruction.
    *   The differentiable CT projector generates projected data $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ from the discretized volume. Discrepancies are quantified using the $L_2$ norm [S1].

#### Variant 2: Initial to Decomposition (GS-IR) [S2]
Used for inverse rendering to separate geometry/appearance from material properties.

1.  **Stage 1 (Initial Optimization):**
    *   Optimize geometric parameters $\mathbf{T} = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ and appearance $\mathbf{A}$ for 30,000 iterations.
    *   Loss: Minimize color reconstruction loss $L_c$ and normal loss $L_n$:
        $$ L_{init} = L_c + L_n $$
    *   $L_n$ includes a total variation smoothing term:
        $$ L_n = L_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} TV_{normal} $$
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$ [S2].

2.  **Stage 2 (Decomposition Optimization):**
    *   Fix $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{A}$.
    *   Optimize material parameters $\mathbf{M}$ for 10,000 iterations.
    *   Loss: Minimize decomposition loss $L_d$:
        $$ L_d = L_{shade} + \lambda_M TV_{mat} + \lambda_E TV_{light} $$
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_M = 1.0$, $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2].

#### Variant 3: Codec-Free to Codec-Aware (CodecSplat) [S3, unofficial]
Used for ultra-compact latent coding in feed-forward 3DGS.

1.  **Stage 1 (Codec-Free Gaussian Generation):**
    *   Train the feed-forward Gaussian generation pipeline *without* the feature codec.
    *   Predict Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ from refined feature $\mathbf{F}_c$ and encoder-side depth $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$.
    *   Render target view $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}_{cf}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$.
    *   Loss:
        $$ L_{stage1} = D_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf}, \mathbf{Y}_t) $$
        where $\mathbf{Y}_t$ is the target-view image and $D_{render}$ is the rendering reconstruction loss [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Stage 2 (Codec-Aware Latent Coding):**
    *   Initialize from Stage 1 checkpoint.
    *   Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner.
    *   Optimize codec-related modules while keeping remaining backbone modules fixed.
    *   Render decoded Gaussian set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ to target view: $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t = \text{Render}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$.
    *   Loss:
        $$ L_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ D_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t, \mathbf{Y}_t) + \gamma D_{codec-depth} + \beta R \right] $$
        where $R$ is the estimated rate, $\gamma$ weights the codec-side depth supervision, and $\beta$ controls the rate penalty [S3, unofficial].
    *   Codec-side depth loss:
        $$ D_{codec-depth} = \| \mathbf{S} - \mathbf{S}_{enc} \|_1 $$
        where $\mathbf{S}$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $\mathbf{S}_{enc}$ is the supervision target [S3, unofficial].
    *   Encoder-side normalized inverse-depth:
        $$ \mathbf{S}_{enc,i}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{\mathbf{D}_{enc,i}(\mathbf{p})^{-1} - \mathbf{f}_i^{-1}}{\mathbf{n}_i^{-1} - \mathbf{f}_i^{-1}} $$
        where $\mathbf{n}_i$ and $\mathbf{f}_i$ are the near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Hyperparameters: $\gamma = 0.1$, $\beta = 10^{-4}$ (DL3DV) or $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (RE10K), $\lambda_{max} = 1024$. During training, $\lambda \in [16, 1024]$; at test time, $\lambda$ is fixed [S3, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def two_stage_optimization(method_variant, context_views, target_views, config):
    """
    Generic two-stage optimization framework.
    method_variant: '3dgr_ct', 'gs_ir', or 'codecsplat'
    """
    if method_variant == '3dgr_ct':
        # Stage 1: Low-Resolution
        volume_grid = create_low_res_grid(scale=0.5)
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians(volume_grid)
        for iteration in range(config.stage1_iters):
            projections = differentiable_ct_projector(gaussians)
            loss = l2_norm(projections, config.measurements)
            update_gaussians(gaussians, loss)
            
        # Stage 2: Full-Resolution
        volume_grid = create_full_res_grid()
        # Re-initialize or refine positions based on Stage 1
        gaussians = refine_gaussians(gaussians, volume_grid)
        for iteration in range(config.stage2_iters):
            projections = differentiable_ct_projector(gaussians)
            loss = l2_norm(projections, config.measurements)
            update_gaussians(gaussians, loss)
            
    elif method_variant == 'gs_ir':
        # Stage 1: Initial
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians()
        for iteration in range(30000):
            rendered_color, rendered_normal = render(gaussians)
            loss_color = l1_loss(rendered_color, target_views)
            loss_normal = normal_loss(rendered_normal, config.lambda_n_tv)
            loss = loss_color + loss_normal
            update_gaussians(gaussians, loss)
            
        # Stage 2: Decomposition
        # Fix geometry T and appearance A
        materials = initialize_materials()
        for iteration in range(10000):
            rendered_shading = render_shading(gaussians, materials)
            loss_shade = l1_loss(rendered_shading, target_views)
            loss_mat = tv_loss(materials, config.lambda_M)
            loss_light = tv_loss(materials, config.lambda_E)
            loss = loss_shade + loss_mat + loss_light
            update_materials(materials, loss)
            
    elif method_variant == 'codecsplat':
        # Stage 1: Codec-Free
        backbone = initialize_backbone()
        for iteration in range(config.stage1_iters):
            features, depth = backbone(context_views)
            gaussians_cf = predict_gaussians(features, depth)
            rendered = render(gaussians_cf)
            loss = rendering_loss(rendered, target_views)
            update_backbone(backbone, loss)
            
        # Stage 2: Codec-Aware
        # Load Stage 1 checkpoint
        codec = initialize_codec()
        refiner = initialize_refiner()
        for iteration in range(config.stage2_iters):
            # Sample lambda for rate-distortion trade-off
            lambda_val = random.uniform(16, 1024)
            
            features, depth = backbone(context_views)
            latent = encode(features, codec)
            features_decoded = decode(latent, codec)
            features_refined = refiner(features_decoded)
            gaussians_decoded = predict_gaussians(features_refined, depth)
            rendered = render(gaussians_decoded)
            
            loss_render = rendering_loss(rendered, target_views)
            loss_depth = l1_loss(normalized_inverse_depth(gaussians_decoded), 
                                 normalized_inverse_depth(context_views))
            rate = estimate_rate(latent)
            
            loss = (lambda_val / 1024) * (loss_render + 
                                          config.gamma * loss_depth + 
                                          config.beta * rate)
            update_codec(codec, refiner, loss)
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Learning Rate:** Adam optimizer is commonly used [S2][S3, unofficial]. CodecSplat uses AdamW with LR $10^{-4}$ and weight decay $0.01$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** GS-IR uses a single NVIDIA V100 [S2]. CodecSplat uses four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB GPUs [S3, unofficial].
*   **Edge Cases:**
    *   **CodecSplat:** Direct training with a quantized bottleneck is unstable [S3, unofficial]. The two-stage approach prevents the model from having to adapt the Gaussian generation pipeline while simultaneously learning a compact latent representation.
    *   **GS-IR:** Fixing parameters in Stage 2 prevents catastrophic forgetting of geometry learned in Stage 1 [S2].
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Low-resolution sampling reduces computational burden without compromising accuracy [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Generally $O(N \cdot I)$ where $N$ is the number of Gaussians and $I$ is the total iterations across both stages. Stage 1 may use fewer voxels or iterations to accelerate convergence.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the resolution of the volume sampled. Stage 1 in 3DGR-CT uses $1/8$ the volume of Stage 2, reducing memory usage during the initial phase [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **CodecSplat:** Achieves KiB-level scene bitstreams while preserving dominant scene structure, outperforming MiB-level compressed baselines [S3, unofficial].
    *   **GS-IR:** Successfully separates geometry, appearance, and material, enabling inverse rendering tasks [S2].
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Provides accurate sparse-view CT reconstruction with reduced computational burden [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes training for complex objectives (codec, material), prevents gradient conflict, allows coarse-to-fine refinement.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and hyperparameters (e.g., $\lambda$, $\beta$). Freezing parameters may lead to suboptimal solutions if the initial stage is poor.

## Background
*   **Origin:** The concept of multi-stage optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting has emerged from various applications requiring stability or complex parameter separation.
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Introduced for sparse-view CT reconstruction to handle computational constraints [S1].
    *   **GS-IR:** Developed for inverse rendering to decouple geometry/appearance from material properties [S2].
    *   **CodecSplat:** Proposed for ultra-compact latent coding to stabilize the training of quantized bottlenecks in feed-forward 3DGS [S3, unofficial].
*   **History:** These methods build upon the foundational 3D Gaussian Splatting technique [Kerbl et al., 2023] and extend it to specialized domains like medical imaging, inverse rendering, and compression.
