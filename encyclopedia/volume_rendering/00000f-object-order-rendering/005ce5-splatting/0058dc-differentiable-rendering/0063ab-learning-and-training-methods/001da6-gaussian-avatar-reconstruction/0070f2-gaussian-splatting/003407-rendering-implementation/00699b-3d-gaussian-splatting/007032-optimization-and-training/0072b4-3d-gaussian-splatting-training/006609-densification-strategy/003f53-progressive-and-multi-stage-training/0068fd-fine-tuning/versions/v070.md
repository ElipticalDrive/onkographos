# Fine-tuning

Also called: 3DGS Fine-tuning, Fine-tuning Strategy, Gaussian Splatting Fine-tuning, Per-scene optimization

## Overview
Fine-tuning in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) refers to a per-scene optimization process that refines an initial Gaussian representation to achieve high-fidelity reconstruction or adaptation to specific constraints [S1, S3]. It addresses the problem of initializing a scene representation from sparse or noisy data (e.g., generated point clouds, low-resolution inputs, or stylized images) by treating the reconstruction as a differentiable optimization problem supervised by rendered images [S1, S3][S4, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training from scratch, fine-tuning leverages pretrained priors or initial estimates to accelerate convergence and improve geometric consistency [S3]. It sets itself apart from general densification strategies by focusing on the refinement of existing attributes (geometry, color, opacity) rather than solely managing primitive count, though it often enables growing and pruning during the process [S3].

## How it works
The fine-tuning process varies depending on the initialization source and the target application (e.g., novel view synthesis, style transfer, or volume visualization) [S1, S3][S4, unofficial][S5]. However, the core mechanism involves initializing a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ and optimizing their parameters using gradient descent on a rendering loss [S3][S4, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Initial Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_0$ (generated via feed-forward prediction, point cloud conversion, or volume sampling) [S1, S3][S4, unofficial].
    *   Supervision data: Scanned color frames, rendered images from predefined poses, or stylized 2D images [S1, S3][S4, unofficial][S5].
    *   Camera poses corresponding to the supervision data [S1, S3].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}^*$ with refined means, covariances, opacities, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients [S3][S4, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ follows the standard 3DGS structure [6234-densification-strategy.md]:
*   **Means ($\mu$):** 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
*   **Covariances ($\Sigma$):** Represented as rotation matrices $R$ and scaling vectors $s$ (or directly as 3D covariance matrices) [G].
*   **Opacities ($\alpha$):** Scalar values per Gaussian.
*   **SH Coefficients ($c$):** Color and view-dependent appearance parameters [G].
*   **Attributes:** Depending on the variant, additional attributes like deformation fields or style latents may be included [S3, S5].

### Steps
1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Generate initial Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_0$ [S1, S3]. This can be done by:
        *   Predicting primitives via a feed-forward network from a point cloud or volume [S1, S3].
        *   Converting a voxel representation or mesh into Gaussians [S1][S4, unofficial].
        *   Using pretrained weights to initialize geometry and color [S3].
    *   Note: Initial feed-forward Gaussians may be redundant; fine-tuning significantly reduces the count via pruning [S3].

2.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   For each iteration $t$:
        *   **Rendering:** Render images $I_{rendered}$ from $\mathcal{G}_t$ using differentiable rasterization [S3][S4, unofficial].
        *   **Loss Computation:** Compute the loss $\mathcal{L}$ between $I_{rendered}$ and ground truth $I_{gt}$ [S1, S3][S4, unofficial][S5].
            *   Common loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{color} + \lambda_{ssim} \mathcal{L}_{SSIM} + \lambda_{entropy} \mathcal{L}_{entropy}$ [S3].
            *   For volume visualization: Image-space loss based on volumetric integration [S4, unofficial].
        *   **Backpropagation:** Update parameters $\theta$ of $\mathcal{G}_t$ using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [S1, S3][S4, unofficial][S5].
        *   **Densification/Pruning:** Enable growing and pruning of primitives during fine-tuning to manage density and remove artifacts [S3].

3.  **Termination:**
    *   Stop when convergence is reached or a fixed number of iterations is completed [G].

### Formulas
*   **Rendering Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{color} = \| I_{rendered} - I_{gt} \|_1 $$
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{SSIM} = 1 - SSIM(I_{rendered}, I_{gt}) $$
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{color} + \lambda_{ssim} \mathcal{L}_{SSIM} + \lambda_{entropy} \mathcal{L}_{entropy} $$ [S3]

*   **Image-space Loss for Volume Visualization:**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{j,o} \sum_{k \in \Omega_{j,o}} \| W^{-1}[e_{j,o,k}(\kappa)] - \text{Target} \|^2 $$ [S4, unofficial]
    Where $W^{-1}$ is the inverse transfer function, $e_{j,o,k}$ are the Gaussian contributions, and $\Omega_{j,o}$ is the set of Gaussians contributing to pixel $(j,o)$ [S4, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ for Adam optimizer [S3].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_{ssim}$ and $\lambda_{entropy}$ are typical hyperparameters [S3].
*   **Transfer Functions:** In volume visualization, 5 transfer functions are typically selected per volume [S4, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** Not given in the sources.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Redundancy:** Initial feed-forward Gaussians may be redundant, requiring effective pruning during fine-tuning to reduce memory consumption [S3].
*   **Style Consistency:** When fine-tuning for style transfer, naive 2D stylization can break multi-view consistency; methods like Ada-GS require training from scratch for each style, limiting usability [S5].
*   **Volume Modes:** In volume visualization, different visual modes (e.g., density ranges) require separate transfer functions and fine-tuning stages [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Image-space Gaussian Fine-tuning
A fine-tuning process for volumetric data that accounts for different visual modes by generating multiple radiance fields using different transfer functions [S4, unofficial]. It optimizes the Gaussian set against rendered images for each mode [S4, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by handling diverse modalities (e.g., medical scans, simulations) with varying dynamic ranges through mode-specific supervision [S4, unofficial].
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Select a series of transfer functions $\{TF_h\}$, where each $TF_h$ is non-zero only within specific value intervals of the physical field $F$ [S4, unofficial].
    2.  Generate radiance fields $RF_h = TF_h(F)$ for each mode $h$ [S4, unofficial].
    3.  Render 64 images through volumetric integration for each radiance field to provide supervision data [S4, unofficial].
    4.  Compute image-space loss and backpropagate to update the Gaussian set $G_h$ [S4, unofficial].
    5.  After fine-tuning, Gaussians $G_h$ for each mode can be concatenated or separately rendered [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** 5 transfer functions are selected for each volume in practice [S4, unofficial].
*   **Link:** [Image-space Gaussian Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md#image-space-gaussian-fine-tuning) (internal reference)

### Ada-GS
A baseline method that trains vanilla 3D Gaussian Splatting directly on 2D stylized images [S5]. It preserves better details than GS-Ada but requires training from scratch for every new style image, limiting practical usability compared to methods like GSS [S5].
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Train vanilla 3DGS using 2D stylized views as ground truth supervision [S5].
*   **Comparison:** Less consistent than GSS; requires full retraining for new styles [S5].
*   **Link:** [Ada-GS](9138-fine-tuning.md#ada-gs) (internal reference)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Fine-tuning typically converges faster than training from scratch due to pretrained priors [S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Fine-tuning can reduce $N$ via pruning, lowering memory usage [S3].
*   **Performance:** Achieves photorealism on par with or surpassing other methods when fine-tuning is applied [S3].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates convergence, improves geometric consistency, handles sparse/noisy initialization [S1, S3].
*   **Limitations:** Requires supervision data (images/poses); style transfer variants may lack multi-view consistency or require retraining [S5].
*   **When to Use:** When high-fidelity reconstruction is needed from limited or noisy data, or when adapting a pretrained model to a specific scene/style [S1, S3, S5].
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time training is required without supervision, or when multi-view consistency is critical and style transfer is needed (prefer GSS over Ada-GS) [S5].

## Background
Fine-tuning in 3DGS is inspired by techniques in NeRF and CNNs [S1, S2]. In NeRF, methods like LOLNeRF and Code-NeRF use per-scene optimization to refine implicit representations [S1]. In CNNs, fine-tuning involves fixing lower layers (e.g., conv1 to conv3) and training upper layers on specific datasets [S2]. The application to 3DGS leverages these principles to refine explicit Gaussian representations, often starting from feed-forward predictions or point clouds [S1, S3].
