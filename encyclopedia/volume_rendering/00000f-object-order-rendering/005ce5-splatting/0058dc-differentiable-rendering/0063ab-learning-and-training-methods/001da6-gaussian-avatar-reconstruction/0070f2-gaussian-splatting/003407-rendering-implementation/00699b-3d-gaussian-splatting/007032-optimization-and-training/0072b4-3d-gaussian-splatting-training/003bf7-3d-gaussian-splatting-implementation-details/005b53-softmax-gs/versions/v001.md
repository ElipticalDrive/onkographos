# Softmax-GS

Also called: Softmax Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Softmax-GS is a unified rendering and optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that resolves view-inconsistency (popping artifacts) and diffuse boundaries by enforcing a softmax-based competition among overlapping Gaussians [S1]. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting, which assumes non-overlapping primitives, Softmax-GS relaxes this assumption to allow a continuous spectrum of visual behaviors from smooth color blending to sharp, winner-take-all boundaries [S1]. It differs from [DeSplat](12925-desplat.md) and [StopThePop](12925-desplat.md) by directly modeling overlap via softmax weights rather than re-sorting or stochastic mixing, and from [EVER](12925-desplat.md) by maintaining near-real-time rendering speeds while providing controllable boundary sharpness [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Camera parameters, a set of $K$ 3D Gaussians defined by mean $\mu_k$, covariance $\Sigma_k$, opacity exponent $p_k$ (where opacity $o_k \sim \exp(p_k)$), and color $c_k$.
**Outputs:** Rendered pixel color $I(\mathbf{x})$ and gradients for optimization.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** For each Gaussian $k$, store:
    *   $p_k$: Exponent (scalar).
    *   $a_k$: Integrated 1D absorbance along the ray (scalar, $a_k = \int o_k dl$).
    *   $d_k$: Depth of the Gaussian center projected onto the ray (scalar).
    *   $\alpha_k$: Boundary sharpness parameter (scalar, learnable).
*   **Intermediate State (per ray/patch):**
    *   $T_k$: Current transmittance (scalar).
    *   $a_{past}$: Accumulated absorbance of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   $d_{past}$: Moving average depth of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   $p_{past}$: Moving average exponent of previous Gaussians (scalar).
    *   CUDA arrays of length $K$ (typically 128) to cache intermediate values ($a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}, T_k$) for the backward pass [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\beta$: Softmax competition strength. $\beta=0$ yields equal blending; large $\beta$ yields sharp boundaries [S1].
*   $\gamma$: Decay rate for competition based on depth difference [S1].
*   $\alpha$: Learnable parameter for individual Gaussian boundary sharpness via Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) [S1].
*   $K$: Hyperparameter for the number of closest Gaussians processed with Softmax-GS logic. Typical value: 128 [S1].
*   Pruning threshold: Raised to 0.4 to accommodate GEF-shrunk splats [S1].

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) for Absorbance:**
    Replace the standard squared distance $\|\cdot\|^2$ in the Gaussian kernel with $\|\cdot\|^{2\alpha}$ to control individual boundary sharpness. This modifies the absorbance $a_k$ but does not change the exponent $p_k$ used in softmax competition [S1].
    $$ o_k(\mathbf{x}, l) \propto \exp(-\|\mathbf{x} - \mu_k\|^{2\alpha}) $$
    Compute $a_k = \int_0^L o_k(\mathbf{x}, l) dl$ [S1].

2.  **Sequential Processing (Forward Pass):**
    Process Gaussians sorted from nearest to farthest. For each Gaussian $k$:
    
    a. **Compute Softmax Weights for Pairwise Competition:**
    Treat all previously processed Gaussians as a single "past" entity with aggregate properties $a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}$. Compute the softmax weight $w_k$ between the current Gaussian $k$ and the past entity using their exponents $p_k$ and $p_{past}$:
    $$ w_k = \frac{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k)}{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k) + \exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})} $$
    $$ w_{past} = \frac{\exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})}{\exp(\beta \cdot p_k) + \exp(\beta \cdot p_{past})} $$
    Note: Competition operates on $p_k$ because $o_k \in [0, \infty)$, making $\exp(o_k) \ge 1$, which prevents weights from diminishing [S1].

    b. **Compute Fully Overlapping Absorbances ($\hat{a}$):**
    Apply the softmax weights to the individual absorbances:
    $$ \hat{a}_k = w_k a_k $$
    $$ \hat{a}_{past} = w_{past} a_{past} $$

    c. **Interpolate with Depth Decay:**
    Calculate the exponential decay factor $s$ based on the depth difference $|d_k - d_{past}|$:
    $$ s = \exp(-\gamma |d_k - d_{past}|) $$
    Interpolate between the fully overlapping absorbances ($\hat{a}$) and the original non-softmax absorbances ($a$):
    $$ \bar{a}_k = s \hat{a}_k + (1-s) a_k $$
    $$ \bar{a}_{past} = s \hat{a}_{past} + (1-s) a_{past} $$

    d. **Correction for Order Invariance and Transmittance:**
    The interpolation may violate order invariance (second Gaussian's color discounted by $(1-\bar{a}_{past})$) and transmittance consistency (output transmittance higher than original). Solve for corrected absorbances $\tilde{a}_{past}$ and $\tilde{a}_k$ that satisfy:
    $$ (1-\tilde{a}_{past})(1-\tilde{a}_k) = T_k $$
    $$ \tilde{a}_{past} = \frac{\hat{a}_{past}(1-T_k)}{\hat{a}_{past} + \hat{a}_k} $$
    $$ \tilde{a}_k = \frac{\hat{a}_k(1-T_k)}{\hat{a}_k + \tilde{a}_{past}T_k} $$
    Apply the interpolation step (Eq. 12) to $\tilde{a}_{past}$ and $\tilde{a}_k$ to get $\bar{a}_{past}$ and $\bar{a}_k$. Finally, scale them by a factor $m$ to restore exact transmittance $T_k$:
    $$ (1-m \bar{a}_{past})(1-m \bar{a}_k) = T_k $$
    $$ \bar{a}_{past} \leftarrow m \bar{a}_{past}, \quad \bar{a}_k \leftarrow m \bar{a}_k $$

    e. **Update State:**
    Update the moving averages and transmittance:
    $$ T_{k+1} = T_k (1 - \bar{a}_k) $$
    $$ a_{past} \leftarrow a_{past} + \bar{a}_k $$
    $$ d_{past} \leftarrow \text{weighted average of } d_{past} \text{ and } d_k $$
    $$ p_{past} \leftarrow \text{weighted average of } p_{past} \text{ and } p_k $$
    Cache these intermediate values in the CUDA arrays [S1].

3.  **Rendering:**
    The final color is computed using the standard splatting formulation with the corrected absorbances:
    $$ I(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{k=1}^K c_k \bar{a}_k \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} (1 - \bar{a}_j) $$
    (Note: In the sequential implementation, this is accumulated incrementally as $I \leftarrow I + c_k \bar{a}_k T_k$) [S1].

4.  **Backward Pass:**
    To preserve linear complexity, access the cached intermediate values from the forward pass CUDA arrays directly. Compute gradients for $p_k, a_k, \alpha_k$ without recomputing the forward pass [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def softmax_gs_forward(gaussians_sorted, camera_params):
    """
    Forward pass for Softmax-GS.
    gaussians_sorted: List of Gaussian objects sorted by depth (nearest first).
    Each Gaussian has: p (exponent), a (absorbance), d (depth), alpha (sharpness).
    """
    # Initialize state
    T = 1.0  # Initial transmittance
    a_past = 0.0
    d_past = 0.0
    p_past = 0.0
    count_past = 0
    
    # Cache arrays for backward pass (size K)
    # In practice, these are CUDA arrays. Here we use Python lists for clarity.
    cache_T = []
    cache_a_past = []
    cache_d_past = []
    cache_p_past = []
    
    # Hyperparameters
    beta = 2.0  # Example value
    gamma = 1.0 # Example value
    K_cache_size = 128
    
    rendered_color = 0.0
    
    for k, g in enumerate(gaussians_sorted):
        # 1. Compute Softmax Weights
        # Avoid overflow by subtracting max if necessary, but for 2 Gaussians direct exp is fine
        exp_p_k = math.exp(beta * g.p)
        exp_p_past = math.exp(beta * p_past) if count_past > 0 else 0.0
        
        denom = exp_p_k + exp_p_past
        w_k = exp_p_k / denom
        w_past = exp_p_past / denom
        
        # 2. Compute Fully Overlapping Absorbances
        # If no past Gaussians, w_past is 0, so hat_a_past is 0
        if count_past > 0:
            hat_a_k = w_k * g.a
            hat_a_past = w_past * a_past
        else:
            hat_a_k = g.a
            hat_a_past = 0.0
            
        # 3. Interpolate with Depth Decay
        if count_past > 0:
            depth_diff = abs(g.d - d_past)
            s = math.exp(-gamma * depth_diff)
            
            bar_a_k = s * hat_a_k + (1 - s) * g.a
            bar_a_past = s * hat_a_past + (1 - s) * a_past
        else:
            bar_a_k = g.a
            bar_a_past = 0.0
            
        # 4. Correction for Order Invariance and Transmittance
        # We need to scale bar_a_k and bar_a_past by m such that:
        # (1 - m * bar_a_past) * (1 - m * bar_a_k) = T
        # Let A = bar_a_past, B = bar_a_k.
        # (1 - m*A)(1 - m*B) = T
        # 1 - m(A+B) + m^2*AB = T
        # m^2*AB - m(A+B) + (1-T) = 0
        # Solve quadratic for m. We want the smaller positive root that keeps absorbance < 1.
        
        A = bar_a_past
        B = bar_a_k
        
        if count_past == 0:
            # No past, just use current
            m = 1.0
        else:
            # Quadratic coefficients
            a_coef = A * B
            b_coef = -(A + B)
            c_coef = 1 - T
            
            discriminant = b_coef**2 - 4 * a_coef * c_coef
            if discriminant < 0:
                discriminant = 0 # Guard against numerical issues
            
            sqrt_disc = math.sqrt(discriminant)
            # Two roots: (-b - sqrt) / 2a and (-b + sqrt) / 2a
            # We want the root that results in smaller absorbance changes, typically the minus branch
            # m = ( (A+B) - sqrt((A+B)^2 - 4AB(1-T)) ) / (2AB)
            if a_coef == 0:
                # Linear case: -m(A+B) + (1-T) = 0 => m = (1-T)/(A+B)
                m = (1 - T) / (A + B) if (A + B) > 1e-6 else 1.0
            else:
                m = ( (A + B) - sqrt_disc ) / (2 * a_coef)
                
            # Clamp m to [0, 1]
            m = max(0.0, min(1.0, m))
            
        # Apply scaling
        final_a_k = m * bar_a_k
        # final_a_past is not used in color accumulation directly, but affects next step's 'past'
        
        # 5. Update Color and State
        # Contribution of current Gaussian
        contribution = g.c * final_a_k * T
        rendered_color += contribution
        
        # Update Transmittance
        T_new = T * (1 - final_a_k)
        
        # Update Moving Averages for Next Step
        # Weighted average based on absorbance contribution
        total_abs = a_past + g.a # Approximate weight for averaging
        if total_abs > 1e-6:
            d_past = (a_past * d_past + g.a * g.d) / total_abs
            p_past = (a_past * p_past + g.a * g.p) / total_abs
            a_past = a_past + g.a
        else:
            d_past = g.d
            p_past = g.p
            a_past = g.a
            
        count_past += 1
        T = T_new
        
        # Cache for backward pass
        if len(cache_T) < K_cache_size:
            cache_T.append(T)
            cache_a_past.append(a_past)
            cache_d_past.append(d_past)
            cache_p_past.append(p_past)
            
    return rendered_color, cache_T, cache_a_past, cache_d_past, cache_p_past
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of Gaussians per ray for both forward and backward passes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires caching intermediate values ($a_{past}, d_{past}, p_{past}, T_k$) in CUDA arrays of length $K$ (typically 128) per patch for the backward pass [S1].
*   **Performance:** Adds approximately 1.2x increase in training time compared to standard 3D GS. Rendering speed is roughly 80% of standard 3D GS [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates view-inconsistency (popping) and allows sharp boundaries. Maintains order invariance and transmittance consistency. Integrates with GS-MCMC cloning [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Slightly slower than standard 3D GS. Requires tuning of $\beta, \gamma, \alpha$. The approximation assumes Gaussians do not regain dominance after losing it [S1].
*   **When to use:** When view-inconsistency or blurry boundaries are problematic in 3D GS. When controllable boundary sharpness is needed.
*   **When not to use:** When maximum rendering speed is critical and artifacts are acceptable. When scene has extreme overlap that violates the dominance assumption (though this is rare in practice) [S1].

## Background
Softmax-GS was proposed in "Softmax-GS: Generalized Gaussians Learning When to Blend or Bound" (2026) [S1]. It addresses limitations in 3D Gaussian Splatting, specifically the non-overlapping assumption which leads to view-inconsistency and diffuse boundaries [S1]. It builds on the Generalized Exponential Function (GEF) from GES [S1] and the softmax competition concept, but provides an exact CUDA implementation with transmittance correction [S1]. It is designed to be a unified solution for both artifacts, unlike methods that address only one [S1].
