# Duplex-GS

Also called: Proxy-Guided Weighted Blending for Real-Time Order-Independent Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Duplex-GS is a dual-hierarchy rendering framework for real-time order-independent Gaussian Splatting that combines alpha-blending with a reformulated Weighted-Sum Rendering (WSR) strategy [S1]. It solves the transparency artifacts and lack of early termination inherent in vanilla OIT-based Gaussian Splatting, while reducing the sorting overhead of conventional alpha-blending [S1]. Unlike vanilla OIT methods which lack physical constraints, Duplex-GS reintroduces them via cell-level transmittance decay, and unlike Sort-free Gaussian Splatting which uses linear depth decay, it uses cell-level sorting to compute a physically grounded transmittance [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A scene encoded as $N$ ellipsoidal cells (proxies), each associated with a latent feature vector $\mathbf{f}_n$ and emitting $K$ neural Gaussians. Camera parameters (view matrix, projection matrix, resolution).
*   **Output:** A 2D image $\mathcal{C}$ of size $H \times W \times 3$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Cell Proxy ($n$):**
    *   Position: $\mathbf{x}_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$
    *   Scale: $S_n \in \mathbb{R}^3$
    *   Quaternion: $Q_n \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (normalized)
    *   Calibration Scalar: $v_n$ (learnable, parameterized by spherical harmonics for directional variation)
    *   Latent Feature: $\mathbf{f}_n$
2.  **Neural Gaussian ($k$ within cell $n$):**
    *   Center: $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k}$
    *   Opacity: $\sigma_{n,k}$
    *   Color: $\mathcal{C}_{n,k}$
    *   Scale: $s_{n,k}$
    *   Quaternion: $\mathbf{q}_{n,k}$

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize cells from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points.
    *   Each cell is parameterized by $\mathbf{x}_n, S_n, Q_n, v_n$.
    *   Each cell is associated with a latent feature vector $\mathbf{f}_n$ that emits $K$ neural Gaussians [S1].

2.  **Layer I: Cell Proxy Rasterization (Coarse Sorting):**
    *   **Projection:** Transform cell centers $\mathbf{x}_n$ to camera space. Compute the 2D bounding box for each cell based on its scale $S_n$ and orientation $Q_n$.
    *   **Tile Assignment:** Assign visible cells to screen tiles.
    *   **Sorting:** Apply radix sort to the sequence of visible cells based on their depth (e.g., center depth or front-most depth). This establishes a coarse spatial order for patches of Gaussians [S1].
    *   **Cell-Level Alpha Blending:** Compute the cell-level transmittance $T_n^{\text{cell}}$ for each sorted cell $n$. The transmittance is computed from preceding cells $i < n$:
        $$ T_n^{\text{cell}} = \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \prod_{k=1}^{K} (1 - v_i \cdot \alpha_{i,k}) $$
        where $\alpha_{i,k}$ is the opacity of the $k$-th Gaussian in cell $i$ (see Step 3 for decoding), and $v_i$ is the calibration scalar [S1].
    *   **Early Termination:** If $T_n^{\text{cell}} < \epsilon$ (predefined threshold), subsequent cells are skipped [S1].
    *   **Cell Weight:** Compute the weight for cell $n$ as $w_n = v_n \cdot T_n^{\text{cell}}$ [S1].

3.  **Layer II: Gaussian Decoding and Blending (Local Order-Independence):**
    *   **Decoding:** For each active cell $n$, decode the attributes of its $K$ neural Gaussians using shared scene-wise MLPs. Inputs to MLPs are $\mathbf{f}_n$, viewing distance $\delta_n$, and viewing direction $\mathbf{r}_n$ (or $d_n$) [S1].
        *   **Centers:** $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k} = \mathbf{x}_n + \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}} \cdot S_n$, where $\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}}$ is the learned offset [S1].
        *   **Opacities:** $\{\sigma_{n,1}, \dots, \sigma_{n,K}\} = F_\sigma(\mathbf{f}_n, \delta_n, \mathbf{r}_n)$, activated by Tanh [S1].
        *   **Colors:** $\{\mathcal{C}^{n,1}, \dots, \mathcal{C}^{n,K}\} = F_c(\mathbf{f}_n, \delta_n, d_n)$ [S1].
        *   **Scales:** $\{\mathcal{O}_{n,1}^{\text{state}}, \dots, \mathcal{O}_{n,K}^{\text{state}}\} = F_s(\mathbf{f}_n, \delta_n, d_n)$, activated by sigmoid. The Gaussian scale is derived as $s_{n,k} = \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}} * S_n$ [S1].
        *   **Quaternions:** $\{\mathbf{q}_{n,1}, \dots, \mathbf{q}_{n,K}\} = F_q(\mathbf{f}_n, \delta_n, d_n)$ [S1].
    *   **Constraints:**
        *   Clamp scale ratio to ensure Gaussians stay inside the cell: $\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}} = \min \left( \mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{scale}}, 1 - \|\mathcal{O}_{n,k}^{\text{pos}}\| \right)$ [S1].
        *   Update cell quaternion as the normalized weighted average of associated Gaussians: $Q_n = \text{norm}\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{q}_{n,k} * \sigma_{n,k}\right)$ [S1].
    *   **Physical OIT Blending (WSR):** Aggregate the $K$ Gaussians within each cell using a Weighted Sum Rendering strategy. The final color $\mathcal{C}$ is:
        $$ \mathcal{C} = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^N w_n \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_{n,k} \mathcal{C}_{n,k}}{\sum_{n=1}^N w_n \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_{n,k}} $$
        where $\alpha_{n,k}$ is the effective opacity of the $k$-th Gaussian in cell $n$ (typically $\sigma_{n,k}$ modulated by the 2D Gaussian kernel value at the pixel) [S1]. Note: The source defines $\alpha_{n,k}$ in the transmittance formula as the opacity, and in the final color formula as the opacity weighted by the Gaussian kernel. For implementation, $\alpha_{n,k}$ in the numerator/denominator sum represents the contribution weight of the Gaussian to the pixel, which includes the spatial falloff [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def duplex_gs_render(cells, camera, K=4, epsilon=1e-4):
    """
    cells: List of Cell objects.
    camera: Camera object with view_matrix, proj_matrix, width, height.
    K: Number of neural Gaussians per cell.
    epsilon: Early termination threshold.
    """
    H, W = camera.height, camera.width
    tiles = get_tiles(H, W, tile_size=16)
    tile_cells = {t: [] for t in tiles}

    # Layer I: Cell Rasterization and Sorting
    for cell in cells:
        # Project cell center to screen space
        pos_cam = transform_to_camera(cell.pos, camera.view_matrix)
        # Compute 2D bounding box of the cell
        bbox = compute_cell_bbox(pos_cam, cell.scale, cell.quaternion, camera.proj_matrix)
        
        # Assign to tiles
        for t in tiles:
            if intersects_bbox(bbox, t, 16):
                tile_cells[t].append(cell)

    # Sort cells within each tile by depth
    for t in tiles:
        tile_cells[t].sort(key=lambda c: get_cell_depth(c, camera))

    image = zeros((H, W, 3))

    # Layer II: Gaussian Decoding and Blending
    for t in tiles:
        sorted_cells = tile_cells[t]
        # Precompute cell-level transmittance and weights for early termination
        # Note: In a real implementation, this might be done per-pixel or per-tile
        # Here we assume a simplified per-tile or per-cell-block approach for clarity
        # The source implies T_n^cell is computed based on preceding cells' opacities.
        # For order-independence within cell, we sum Gaussians.
        
        # To implement the formula correctly per pixel, we need to iterate pixels
        # However, the source describes a hybrid: Cell-level alpha blending for weights,
        # then WSR for Gaussians inside.
        
        # Let's iterate pixels in the tile
        for y in range(t.y, min(t.y + 16, H)):
            for x in range(t.x, min(t.x + 16, W)):
                acc_color_num = zeros(3)
                acc_color_den = 0.0
                T_cell = 1.0
                
                for cell in sorted_cells:
                    # Early termination check
                    if T_cell < epsilon:
                        break
                    
                    # Decode Gaussians for this cell
                    gaussians = decode_gaussians(cell, camera, K)
                    
                    # Compute cell-level transmittance contribution
                    # T_n^cell = product(1 - v_i * alpha_i,k) for i < n
                    # This requires knowing the opacity of previous cells' Gaussians at this pixel.
                    # For simplicity in this pseudocode, we assume T_cell is maintained
                    # and updated by the previous cell's contribution.
                    
                    # Calculate cell weight w_n = v_n * T_n^cell
                    # Note: v_n is the calibration scalar
                    w_n = cell.v * T_cell
                    
                    # Sum Gaussians inside the cell (Order-Independent WSR)
                    cell_sum_num = zeros(3)
                    cell_sum_den = 0.0
                    
                    for g in gaussians:
                        # Compute 2D Gaussian value at pixel (x,y)
                        # g.pos is in camera space, project to screen
                        g_screen = project_to_screen(g.pos, camera)
                        dist_sq = (x - g_screen.x)**2 + (y - g_screen.y)**2
                        # Approximate 2D covariance from scale and orientation
                        inv_sigma_2d = get_inv_2d_cov(g.scale, g.quaternion, camera)
                        exponent = -0.5 * dot([x-g_screen.x, y-g_screen.y], inv_sigma_2d @ [x-g_screen.x, y-g_screen.y])
                        g_val = exp(exponent)
                        
                        # Alpha is opacity * gaussian value
                        alpha = g.opacity * g_val
                        
                        # Accumulate for WSR
                        cell_sum_num += alpha * g.color
                        cell_sum_den += alpha
                    
                    # If cell_sum_den is 0, no contribution
                    if cell_sum_den > 0:
                        # Weighted sum for the cell
                        cell_contrib_num = w_n * cell_sum_num
                        cell_contrib_den = w_n * cell_sum_den
                        
                        acc_color_num += cell_contrib_num
                        acc_color_den += cell_contrib_den
                        
                        # Update T_cell for next cell
                        # T_{n+1}^cell = T_n^cell * product(1 - v_n * alpha_{n,k})
                        # We approximate product(1 - v_n * alpha) as (1 - v_n * sum_alpha) if alphas are small
                        # Or more accurately, we need to track the exact product.
                        # The source formula: T_n^cell = prod_{i=1}^{n-1} prod_{k=1}^{n-1} (1 - v_i * alpha_{i,k})
                        # This implies T_{n+1} = T_n * prod_{k=1}^K (1 - v_n * alpha_{n,k})
                        
                        prod_term = 1.0
                        for g in gaussians:
                            g_screen = project_to_screen(g.pos, camera)
                            dist_sq = (x - g_screen.x)**2 + (y - g_screen.y)**2
                            inv_sigma_2d = get_inv_2d_cov(g.scale, g.quaternion, camera)
                            exponent = -0.5 * dot([x-g_screen.x, y-g_screen.y], inv_sigma_2d @ [x-g_screen.x, y-g_screen.y])
                            g_val = exp(exponent)
                            alpha = g.opacity * g_val
                            prod_term *= (1.0 - cell.v * alpha)
                        
                        T_cell *= prod_term

                if acc_color_den > 0:
                    image[y, x] = acc_color_num / acc_color_den
                else:
                    image[y, x] = zeros(3) # Background

    return image

def decode_gaussians(cell, camera, K):
    """
    Decodes K neural Gaussians from a cell's latent feature.
    """
    gaussians = []
    # Inputs to MLPs
    f_n = cell.latent_feature
    delta_n = get_viewing_distance(cell.pos, camera)
    r_n = get_viewing_direction(cell.pos, camera)
    d_n = r_n # Assuming d_n is viewing direction
    
    # Decode offsets and scales
    # O_pos is learned offset, scaled by S_n
    # We assume MLPs return tensors of size K
    O_pos = MLP_pos(f_n, delta_n, r_n) # Returns K x 3
    O_scale = MLP_scale(f_n, delta_n, d_n) # Returns K (sigmoid activated)
    q = MLP_quat(f_n, delta_n, d_n) # Returns K x 4
    sigma = MLP_opacity(f_n, delta_n, r_n) # Returns K (tanh activated)
    color = MLP_color(f_n, delta_n, d_n) # Returns K x 3
    
    for k in range(K):
        # Clamp scale ratio
        scale_ratio = min(O_scale[k], 1.0 - norm(O_pos[k]))
        
        # Compute Gaussian center
        mu = cell.pos + O_pos[k] * cell.scale
        
        # Compute Gaussian scale
        s = scale_ratio * cell.scale
        
        # Construct Gaussian object
        g = {
            'pos': mu,
            'scale': s,
            'quaternion': q[k],
            'opacity': sigma[k],
            'color': color[k]
        }
        gaussians.append(g)
        
    return gaussians
```

## Subtopics

### AAA-Gaussians
A method that eliminates sorting analytically by pre-integrating the alpha blending equation to compute an equivalent order-independent transparency through a rational polynomial approximation of the visibility term, suppressing popping artifacts without per-pixel depth sorting [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduced sorting overhead compared to standard 3DGS because sorting is performed on $N$ cells rather than $M$ Gaussians ($N \ll M$). Cell-level early termination further accelerates rendering when transmittance drops below $\epsilon$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Lower memory consumption for sorting buffers due to the smaller number of cells [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates transparency artifacts and popping artifacts. Enables early termination. Physically grounded transmittance decay [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Order confusion can occur due to overlap among cells, addressed by the calibration scalar $v_n$ [S1]. Requires decoding MLPs for each cell, adding computational overhead per cell [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time applications requiring high-fidelity view synthesis with transparent objects, where sorting overhead is a bottleneck and transparency artifacts are unacceptable [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with extremely high Gaussian density where cell overhead dominates, or when strict per-Gaussian depth ordering is required for specific physical effects not captured by the cell-level approximation [S1].

## Background
Duplex-GS was introduced in a paper submitted to the IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems for Video Technology in 2026 [S1]. It builds upon Scaffold-GS, which uses anchors to emit neural Gaussians, by replacing point anchors with ellipsoidal cells [S1]. The method addresses limitations of vanilla OIT-based Gaussian Splatting, which suffers from transparency artifacts and lack of early termination, and Sort-free Gaussian Splatting, which uses linear depth decay [S1].
