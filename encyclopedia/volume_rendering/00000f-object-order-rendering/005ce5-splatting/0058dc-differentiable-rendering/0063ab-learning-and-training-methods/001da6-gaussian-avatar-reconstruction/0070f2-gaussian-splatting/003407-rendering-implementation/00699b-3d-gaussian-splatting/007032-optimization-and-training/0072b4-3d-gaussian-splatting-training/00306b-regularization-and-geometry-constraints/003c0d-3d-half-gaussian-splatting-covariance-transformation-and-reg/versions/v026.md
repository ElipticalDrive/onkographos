# 3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization

Also called: 3D-HGS

## Overview
This method regularizes 3D Gaussian kernels to represent half-ellipsoids rather than full ellipsoids, enforcing surface-like geometry by cutting Gaussians along their local normal direction [S1]. It solves the problem of volumetric noise and "dust" in Gaussian Splatting by ensuring that opacity is concentrated on one side of the surface, thereby improving reconstruction fidelity and training stability [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) and [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by operating directly on the covariance structure to enforce a half-space constraint, rather than relying on external depth supervision or frequency-domain penalties [S1].

## How it works
The method modifies the covariance matrix of each Gaussian to represent a half-ellipsoid aligned with the surface normal. This transformation is applied periodically during training.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   Input: Original covariance matrix $\Sigma$ (3x3 symmetric positive definite), surface normal vector $n$ (3x1).
*   Output: Regularized covariance matrix $\sigma$ (3x3) representing a half-ellipsoid.

**Data Structures:**
*   $\Sigma$: $3 \times 3$ matrix.
*   $n$: 3D vector, normalized.
*   $v_1, v_2$: 3D vectors forming an orthonormal basis with $n$.
*   $basis$: $3 \times 3$ matrix where columns are $v_1, v_2, n$.
*   $cov$: $3 \times 3$ matrix in the basis coordinate system.
*   $\sigma$: $3 \times 3$ regularized covariance matrix.

**Parameters:**
*   Regularization interval: Every 5000 iterations [S1].
*   Opacity threshold for densification/pruning: 0.01 [S1].
*   Initial opacity for half-Gaussians: 0.02 [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Normalize the normal vector:**
    $$ n \leftarrow n / \|n\| $$

2.  **Construct the local basis:**
    If $n_x = 0$ and $n_y = 0$:
    $$ v_1 \leftarrow (1, 0, 0) $$
    $$ v_2 \leftarrow (0, 1, 0) $$
    Else:
    $$ v_1 \leftarrow (n_y, -n_x, 0) / \|(n_y, -n_x, 0)\| $$
    $$ v_2 \leftarrow (n \times v_1) / \|n \times v_1\| $$
    $$ basis \leftarrow [v_1, v_2, n] $$
    *(Note: $basis$ is formed by concatenating vectors $v_1, v_2, n$ as columns)* [S1].

3.  **Transform covariance to basis space:**
    $$ cov \leftarrow basis^T \cdot \Sigma \cdot basis $$

4.  **Compute modified diagonal and off-diagonal elements:**
    $$ a' \leftarrow cov[0, 0] - \frac{cov[0, 2]^2}{cov[2, 2]} $$
    $$ b' \leftarrow cov[0, 1] - \frac{cov[0, 2] \cdot cov[1, 2]}{cov[2, 2]} $$
    $$ c' \leftarrow cov[1, 1] - \frac{cov[1, 2]^2}{cov[2, 2]} $$

5.  **Construct the regularized covariance matrix in basis space:**
    $$ \sigma_{basis} = \begin{bmatrix} a' & b' & 0 \\ b' & c' & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \min(a', c') / 100 \end{bmatrix} $$
    *(Note: The element at [0,1] is $b'$, [1,0] is $b'$, [0,2] and [1,2] are 0, [2,2] is $\min(a', c')/100$. All other elements are 0)* [S1].

6.  **Transform back to world space:**
    $$ \sigma \leftarrow basis \cdot \sigma_{basis} \cdot basis^T $$

7.  **Apply regularization:**
    Perform steps 1-6 every 5000 iterations [S1].

8.  **Adjust Opacity Thresholds:**
    *   Increase the opacity threshold for densification and pruning to 0.01 [S1].
    *   Reset the opacity value for half-Gaussians to 0.02 [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_half_gaussian_covariance(cov: np.ndarray, normal: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Transforms a full Gaussian covariance matrix into a half-Gaussian covariance matrix
    aligned with the given normal vector.
    
    Args:
        cov: 3x3 symmetric positive definite covariance matrix.
        normal: 3x1 normal vector.
        
    Returns:
        sigma: 3x3 regularized covariance matrix.
    """
    # Step 1: Normalize normal
    n = normal / np.linalg.norm(normal)
    
    # Step 2: Construct basis
    if n[0] == 0 and n[1] == 0:
        v1 = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0])
        v2 = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 0.0])
    else:
        v1 = np.array([n[1], -n[0], 0.0])
        v1 = v1 / np.linalg.norm(v1)
        v2 = np.cross(n, v1)
        v2 = v2 / np.linalg.norm(v2)
        
    basis = np.column_stack((v1, v2, n))
    
    # Step 3: Transform to basis space
    cov_basis = basis.T @ cov @ basis
    
    # Step 4: Compute modified elements
    a_prime = cov_basis[0, 0] - (cov_basis[0, 2]**2) / cov_basis[2, 2]
    b_prime = cov_basis[0, 1] - (cov_basis[0, 2] * cov_basis[1, 2]) / cov_basis[2, 2]
    c_prime = cov_basis[1, 1] - (cov_basis[1, 2]**2) / cov_basis[2, 2]
    
    # Step 5: Construct regularized covariance in basis space
    # Element [2,2] is min(a', c') / 100
    min_ac = min(a_prime, c_prime)
    sigma_basis = np.array([
        [a_prime, b_prime, 0.0],
        [b_prime, c_prime, 0.0],
        [0.0, 0.0, min_ac / 100.0]
    ])
    
    # Step 6: Transform back to world space
    sigma = basis @ sigma_basis @ basis.T
    
    return sigma

def update_training_parameters():
    """
    Adjusts opacity thresholds for densification and pruning.
    """
    # Opacity threshold for densification and pruning
    opacity_threshold = 0.01
    
    # Opacity value for half-Gaussians
    half_gaussian_opacity = 0.02
    
    return opacity_threshold, half_gaussian_opacity
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Normal:** If the normal vector is zero or near-zero, the basis construction in Step 2 must handle the division by zero. The source specifies a fallback to standard basis vectors $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ if $n_x=0$ and $n_y=0$ [S1].
*   **Precision:** The division by $cov[2, 2]$ in Step 4 requires that the covariance component along the normal direction is non-zero. If it is zero or near-zero, numerical instability may occur [S1].
*   **Regularization Frequency:** The transformation is applied every 5000 iterations. Applying it too frequently may disrupt the optimization landscape, while applying it too infrequently may allow the Gaussians to drift away from the half-ellipsoid constraint [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, 3D-HGS with an Efficient Rasterizer achieves 125 FPS and 29.66 PSNR using 3.8M kernels, compared to 3DGS which uses 3.2M kernels [S1]. On the Tank & Dengin dataset, it achieves 160 FPS and 24.45 PSNR using 1.8M kernels [S1]. On the Tank & Blending dataset, it achieves 126 FPS and 29.76 PSNR using 2.6M kernels [S1].
*   **Comparison:** 3D-HGS consistently outperforms original 3D Gaussian Splatting across all metrics (PSNR, etc.) on the evaluated datasets [S1]. It also outperforms Scaffold, mip-splatting, and MCMC architectures [S1].
*   **Strengths:** The method effectively eliminates dust and noise from the scene, leading to cleaner and more accurate representations [S1]. The splitting planes generally align well with scene surface normals, indicating that half-ellipsoids effectively model sharp transitions between object surfaces and empty space [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The method requires periodic covariance transformation, which adds computational overhead during training. The specific opacity thresholds (0.01 for pruning, 0.02 for half-Gaussians) are tuned for this method and may not be optimal for other Gaussian Splatting variants [S1].

## Background
3D Half-Gaussian Splatting (3D-HGS) was introduced to address the limitations of full Gaussian Splatting in representing surfaces. By cutting Gaussians along their local normal direction, the method enforces a half-space constraint, which is more appropriate for surface reconstruction than volumetric representation [S1]. The method is evaluated against Scaffold, mip-splatting, and MCMC architectures, demonstrating consistent improvements over original 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1].
