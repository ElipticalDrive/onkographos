# Appearance and Physical Modeling


## Overview
Appearance and Physical Modeling extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline to handle complex lighting conditions, transient objects, and non-vacuum media. It solves the problem where static Gaussian attributes (fixed Spherical Harmonics coefficients and constant opacity) fail to capture appearance variations caused by changing illumination, camera ISP processing, or volumetric scattering [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md][15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by introducing learnable medium parameters, neural field substitutions, or physically based rendering (PBR) attributes, rather than relying solely on optimizing base SH coefficients [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md][15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, where the scene is defined by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ (derived from rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$.

The rendering process evaluates the Gaussian density along a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\omega$ and integrates contributions. The Gaussian density function is:
$$ G(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\right) $$

The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md].
5.  **Volumetric Attenuation:** Modifying the alpha-blending equation to incorporate medium properties, such as direct attenuation $B_d$ and backscatter parameters $B_\infty, B_b$, to model light transport in non-vacuum media [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md].

## Subtopics

### [Color and Appearance Modeling](14740-color-and-appearance-modeling.md)
Color and Appearance Modeling addresses the limitations of standard view-dependent radiance representation when handling complex lighting, transient objects, or unstructured data. It solves the problem where static Gaussian attributes cannot capture appearance variations caused by changing illumination, camera ISP processing, or dynamic occluders. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by offering diverse strategies: post-processing image transformations ([DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)), neural field substitution ([Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)), factorized illumination ([3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)), and image-conditioned modulation ([SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)), rather than relying solely on optimizing SH coefficients ([Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)). Choose this when the scene exhibits significant appearance changes due to lighting or transient objects.

### [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md)
Physics and Optical Modeling extends the standard 3DGS image formation pipeline to account for physical light transport phenomena, specifically volumetric attenuation and scattering. It solves the problem of inaccurate rendering in non-vacuum media (such as underwater environments) or the need for geometrically consistent surface extraction by reformulating the discrete alpha-blending rasterization into a continuous volumetric integration framework. It distinguishes itself from standard 3DGS by introducing learnable medium parameters (attenuation and backscatter coefficients) or by deriving continuous attenuation fields from Gaussian densities to ensure unbiased surface extraction, thereby addressing color distortion and depth inconsistency issues inherent in the base method. Choose this for scenes involving fog, water, or smoke.

### Relightable 3D Gaussians
**Definition**
A differentiable point-based rendering framework that enhances vanilla 3D Gaussians by associating extra properties (normal vectors, BRDF parameters, and incident lighting) to achieve photo-realistic relighting and BRDF decomposition from multi-view images [S1].

**Step**
- Assign a normal vector $\mathbf{n}$, BRDF values, and incident light information to each 3D Gaussian point to model per-point light reflectance [S1].
- Apply physically based rendering (PBR) to get a PBR color for each 3D Gaussian point, which is then alpha-composited to obtain the rendered color for the corresponding image pixel [S1].
- Split the incident light into a global environment map and an indirect incident light field for robust material and lighting estimation [S1].
- Introduce proper regularizations to enhance geometry and mitigate material-lighting ambiguity, including constraints on depth distribution, smoothness priors, and a lighting regularization [S1].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting method [26] assumes static appearance and vacuum rendering. Subsequent work recognized that this assumption limits applicability in dynamic lighting or non-vacuum environments. Relightable 3D Gaussians [S1] was introduced to enable photo-realistic relighting by decomposing material and lighting properties. Other approaches like [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md) and [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md) address appearance variability through factorization and modulation, respectively. Physics-based modeling [15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md] extends the rendering equation to handle volumetric media, addressing color distortion and depth inconsistency.
