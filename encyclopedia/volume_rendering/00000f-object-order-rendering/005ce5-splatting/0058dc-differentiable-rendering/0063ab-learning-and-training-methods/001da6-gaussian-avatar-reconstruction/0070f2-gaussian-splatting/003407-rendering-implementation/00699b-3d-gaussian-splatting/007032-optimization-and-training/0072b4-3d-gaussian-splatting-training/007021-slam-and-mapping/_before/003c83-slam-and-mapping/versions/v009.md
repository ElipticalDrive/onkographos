# SLAM and Mapping

Also called: Neural SLAM, Gaussian SLAM, 3DGS-SLAM

## Overview
SLAM and Mapping in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) solves the problem of simultaneously estimating camera poses and reconstructing a high-fidelity 3D scene representation from a stream of images or video. It sets itself apart from offline reconstruction methods by operating incrementally or in real-time, tightly coupling the geometric tracking (odometry/loop closure) with the radiance-field optimization. Unlike traditional NeRF-based SLAM, which suffers from slow training and rendering, 3DGS-based SLAM leverages the explicit, differentiable nature of Gaussian blobs to enable real-time rendering and faster convergence, though it introduces challenges regarding Gaussian degeneracy and memory management [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of 3DGS SLAM involves a coupled loop of tracking and mapping.

1.  **Tracking (Pose Estimation):** Given a current image frame $I_t$ and a current scene representation $\mathcal{G}$ (the set of Gaussians), the system estimates the camera pose $T_t$. This is typically done by rendering a predicted image $\hat{I}_t$ from $\mathcal{G}$ using the current pose estimate and minimizing a photometric or geometric loss between $I_t$ and $\hat{I}_t$.
2.  **Mapping (Scene Update):** Once the pose $T_t$ is estimated, the new frame is used to update the scene representation $\mathcal{G}$. This involves:
    *   **Initialization:** Adding new Gaussians derived from the new frame's depth or feature points.
    *   **Optimization:** Adjusting the parameters (position $\mu$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients $c$) of existing and new Gaussians to minimize the reconstruction error.
    *   **Densification/Pruning:** Managing the density of Gaussians to maintain detail without excessive memory usage, often using criteria based on gradient magnitude or opacity thresholds [S2, unofficial].

**Mathematical Formulation:**
The scene is represented as a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by:
*   Mean position: $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Covariance matrix: $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, parameterized as $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T$ where $R_i$ is a rotation matrix and $S_i$ is a scaling matrix [S2, unofficial].
*   Opacity: $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color: Represented by spherical harmonics coefficients $c_{i,lm}$ [S2, unofficial].

The rendering process projects these 3D Gaussians to 2D screen space, resulting in 2D Gaussians with covariance $\Sigma'_i$. The color $C$ of a pixel is computed via alpha compositing of the sorted Gaussians:
$$ C = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{O}} c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $\mathcal{O}$ is the set of overlapping Gaussians sorted by depth [S2, unofficial].

The tracking loss $L_{track}$ and mapping loss $L_{map}$ are minimized. A common tracking loss combines color and depth terms:
$$ L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D| $$
where $\rho$ converts RGB to LAB space (discarding the L channel to handle lighting changes), $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth [S5].

## Subtopics

### [Mapping Strategies](9321-mapping-strategies.md)
Mapping strategies define the correspondence mechanism used to align pixels or features between two distinct scenes or views for the purpose of alignment, editing, or fusion. In the context of radiance-field optimization, these strategies solve the problem of establishing reliable spatial matching when direct geometric priors are insufficient or when transferring attributes between scenes. This method sets itself apart from sibling optimization techniques by focusing specifically on the *correspondence* phase rather than the gradient-based parameter update, serving as a prerequisite for resampling and fusion.
*   **Identity Mapping**: Assumes direct pixel-to-pixel correspondence based on camera pose or grid alignment. Fails under significant misalignment or viewpoint shifts [9321-mapping-strategies.md].
*   **Semantic Correspondence-based Mapping**: Uses Vision Transformers (ViT) and DINO to generate feature embeddings, finding nearest neighbors in feature space. Robust to appearance changes but struggles in textureless regions [9321-mapping-strategies.md].
*   **Co-segmentation-based Mapping**: Refines alignment by segmenting images into semantically meaningful parts using DINO clustering, enforcing consistency within boundaries [9321-mapping-strategies.md].

### [Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Representation](12096-neural-radiance-field-nerf-representation.md)
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation is a method for implicitly modeling 3D scenes using neural networks to enable novel view synthesis. It solves the problem of reconstructing a continuous volumetric appearance field from a sparse set of 2D images by optimizing a network to predict color and density at any 3D point and viewing direction. Unlike explicit representations such as [3D Gaussian Splatting](12052-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) which store millions of discrete blobs, NeRF stores the scene implicitly within a neural network, resulting in lower memory usage but significantly slower rendering and training times.

### CG-SLAM
*   **Definition**: A method that greatly improves reconstruction quality by introducing an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1].
*   **Step**: Introduce an uncertainty map into the training process based on the rendered depth [S1].

### Combined ORB-SLAM + 3DGS
*   **Definition**: Combines an existing frame-based tracking system (ORB-SLAM) with 3DGS mapping. ORB-SLAM recovers poses, and 3DGS is trained with poses and sensor depth of keyframes, equivalent to offline splatting [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance**: On EuRoC Machine Hall (Stereo Depth), ATE RMSE (m): ORB-SLAM3 0.052, Ours 3.190. Point-SLAM fails on all sequences [S2, unofficial].
*   **Comparison**: ORB-SLAM achieves best ATE, but combined approach shows no significant difference in rendering metrics compared to unified 3DGS SLAM. Unified approach is preferred for exploring 3DGS throughout the entire algorithm [S2, unofficial].

### CP-SLAM
*   **Definition**: A neural point-based hybrid neural field SLAM system enabling multi-agent cooperative localization and mapping with loop closure [S3].
*   **Steps**:
    *   Uses a keyframe-associated neural point representation inspired by PointNeRF, allowing per-point features to be adjusted during pose graph optimization [S3].
    *   Employs a two-stage distributed-to-centralized training scheme: initial decoders are trained separately for each agent, then fused and fine-tuned jointly [S3].
    *   Integrates odometry, loop detection, sub-map fusion, and global refinement into a unified framework [S3].

### MNE-SLAM
*   **Definition**: The first fully distributed multi-agent neural SLAM framework enabling accurate collaborative mapping and robust camera tracking without centralized training or raw data exchange [S3].
*   **Steps**:
    *   Uses a triplane+MLP hybrid neural field representation as a mapping module [S3].
    *   Introduces an intra-to-inter loop closure strategy to reduce pose drift and align submaps across agents through peer-to-peer feature sharing and global consistency loss [S3].

### HERO-SLAM
*   **Definition**: A SLAM method employing a novel multiscale patch-based loss that aligns feature points, maps, and RGB-D pixels through warpings [S3].
*   **Steps**: Uses an INGP-like multiresolution hybrid feature grid+MLP representation for neural SDF learning [S3].

### PIN-SLAM
*   **Definition**: A globally consistent SLAM system using a point-based implicit neural (PIN) representation [S3].
*   **Steps**:
    *   Replaces grid structures with neural feature points, offering spatial flexibility and elastic correction during loop closure [S3].
    *   Mapping alternates with odometry [S3].
    *   Mapping step is based on a hybrid neural SDF representation with explicit neural points and implicit MLP decoders [S3].
    *   Odometry is performed correspondence-free via second-order scan-to-map optimization [S3].
    *   A sliding window replay buffer ensures stable incremental updates [S3].
    *   Loop closures trigger pose graph optimization and elastic deformation of neural points, enabling consistent large-scale mapping [S3].

### Active 3D Reconstruction Algorithm
*   **Steps**:
    *   **Initialization**: Initialize 3D bounding box, uniformly sample goal-state candidates $S_g$ from view space, initialize DVGO coarse model $M^{(c)}$ and agent state $s_0$, append initial image to database $\{I\}$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Global Policy**: Select next-best-view $s^*_g$ from goal candidates $S_g$ using criterion: $s^*_g = \arg\min_{s_g \in S_g} (\lambda_{cp} l_{cp}(x_{s_g}) - \lambda_{ig} E_s(s_g))$, where $x_{s_g}$ is 3D location of $s_g$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Local Policy**: Execute planning optimization to obtain optimized trajectory $S^*$ and local next-best view $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Action**: Move agent to $s^*$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Observation**: Obtain new image observation $I_t$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Update Database**: Add new observation to database $\{I\}_{i=0}^{t+1}$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Mapping Optimization**: Optimize DVGO model $M_t$ to $M_{t+1}$ by sampling pixels from database, including synchronizing entropy volume $V(E,c)$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Stop Check**: Stop if (1) change in entropy volume is below threshold, or (2) maximum information gain of feasible goal candidates is below threshold [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Fine Stage**: After exploration, initialize DVGO fine model $M^{(f)}$ with trained coarse model $M_T^{(c)}$. Perform stochastic training by sampling pixels from database $\{I\}_{i=0}^T$ for $N$ iterations, optimizing every $m$ iterations [S4, unofficial].

### Lightness-Decoupled Camera Tracking
*   **Definition**: A camera tracking method that decouples lightness from the image to handle lighting changes, by converting images to LAB space and discarding the L (lightness) channel during comparison [S5].
*   **Steps**:
    1.  Parameterize camera pose as $T = (t, q)$ with 3D translation $t$ and rotation quaternion $q$ [S5].
    2.  Initialize camera pose assuming constant camera velocity [S5].
    3.  Convert input image $C$ and rendered image $\hat{C}$ to LAB space using $\rho$ [S5].
    4.  Discard the L channel [S5].
    5.  Minimize $L_{tracking}$ using color (A/B channels) and depth differences [S5].
*   **Formula**: $L_{tracking} = \lambda_{color} |\rho(\hat{C}) - \rho(C)| + \lambda_{depth} |\hat{D} - D|$, where $\rho$ is the operation of converting the image from RGB to LAB space, $C$ is the input image, $\hat{C}$ is the rendered image, $D$ is the input depth, and $\hat{D}$ is the rendered depth [S5].
*   **Parameters**:
    *   $\lambda_{color} = 0.5$, weight for the color term in the tracking loss [S5].
    *   $\lambda_{depth} = 1.0$, weight for the depth term in the tracking loss [S5].

## Background
The integration of SLAM with 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a response to the limitations of NeRF-based SLAM systems, which are computationally expensive and slow to render. Traditional SLAM methods (e.g., ORB-SLAM) provide robust pose estimation but lack high-fidelity appearance modeling. NeRF-based SLAM (e.g., NICE-SLAM, ESLAM) offers high-quality rendering but suffers from slow training and rendering speeds [S3]. 3DGS SLAM leverages the explicit, differentiable nature of Gaussian splats to achieve real-time rendering and faster convergence [S1][S2, unofficial]. Early works like CG-SLAM introduced uncertainty maps to improve reconstruction quality [S1]. Combined approaches like ORB-SLAM + 3DGS separate tracking and mapping, while unified approaches like SplaTAM and GS-SLAM integrate them [S2, unofficial]. Recent advancements include multi-agent systems (CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM) and distributed frameworks (MNE-SLAM) [S3], as well as methods handling lighting changes (Lightness-Decoupled Tracking) [S5] and active reconstruction (ActiveRMAP) [S4, unofficial].
