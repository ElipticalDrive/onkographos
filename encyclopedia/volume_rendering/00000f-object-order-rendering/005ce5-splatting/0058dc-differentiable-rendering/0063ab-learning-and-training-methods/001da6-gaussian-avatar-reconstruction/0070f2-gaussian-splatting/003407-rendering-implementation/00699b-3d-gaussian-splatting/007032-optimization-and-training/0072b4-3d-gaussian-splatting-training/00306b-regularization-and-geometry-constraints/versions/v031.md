# Regularization and Geometry Constraints

Also called: Geometric Priors, Frequency Regularization, Depth Constraints

## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that impose physical or spectral priors to improve geometric validity, reduce artifacts, and stabilize optimization. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md), which relies solely on photometric loss and can produce unstructured density clouds or "floaters," these methods constrain the shape, position, or frequency spectrum of the Gaussians. They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on structural integrity (surface alignment, depth consistency) rather than physical plausibility of material properties.

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of modifying the standard photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ by adding a regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
where $\lambda$ is a weighting factor. The core mechanism involves injecting external information (depth, frequency, normals) or internal constraints (covariance structure) into the gradient update step. Common pitfalls include over-smoothing valid details, numerical instability in covariance transformations, and sensitivity to the quality of external priors (e.g., monocular depth estimates).

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization (FR) minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth to alleviate over-reconstruction. It sets itself apart from [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md) by focusing on spectral fidelity rather than surface alignment. It is chosen when naive L1 loss fails to differentiate over-reconstructed regions, leading to redundant Gaussians.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision enhances robustness by blurring ground-truth images with scheduled Gaussian kernels. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by explicitly modifying the supervision signal in the spatial domain to stabilize camera alignment. It is chosen when high-frequency noise in raw pixel supervision causes instability.

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
This method modifies the covariance matrix to enforce a "half-Gaussian" shape, cutting the ellipsoid along the local surface normal. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by directly constraining the spatial support of the kernel to align with surface geometry. It is chosen to reduce "dust" artifacts and prevent full ellipsoids from bleeding into empty space.

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
This strategy prevents detail loss by decoupling depth fitting from shape deformation. It sets itself apart from [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md) by freezing shape parameters (scale/rotation) and optimizing center positions and opacities independently. It is chosen when forcing anisotropic Gaussians to conform to smooth monocular depth conflicts with complex color appearance.

### [FreGS](9212-fregs.md)
FreGS addresses over-reconstruction by regularizing frequency discrepancies in a progressive manner. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by applying a fronto-parallel expansion strategy to ensure smooth coverage. It is chosen to prevent the model from fitting noise in high-frequency regions.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
This self-adaptive technique uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning. It sets itself apart from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by dynamically selecting pseudo-labels from the scale with minimal reprojection error, rather than relying on a single fixed depth prior. It is chosen to reduce floaters and improve convergence without explicit depth ground truth.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
This method improves geometric validity by constraining Gaussian shape and orientation. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by focusing on spatial surface alignment and from [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by enforcing normal consistency and kernel flattening. It is chosen to recover high-quality geometry from unstructured density clouds.

### Wild-GS
- **Definition:** A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry [S1].

### Source Feature Extrapolation
- **Definition:** A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images [S2].
- **Step:** Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$ [S2].
- **Formula:** Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else 0. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$ [S2].
- **Parameter:** $\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies [S2].

### SDF-Regularized 3DGS
- **Definition:** Methods to improve geometric fidelity in 3DGS by imposing physical constraints, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), monocular depth priors, or multi-view geometric constraints, during optimization [S3].
- **Step:** Introduce a regularization term to minimize the difference between an ideal SDF and the SDF calculated from Gaussians [S3]. Use a dual-branch framework with GS- and SDF-branches, jointly optimized to provide mutual guidance [S3]. Incorporate geometric regularization such as monocular depth priors or multi-plane Gaussian optimization [S3].

### MVG-Splatting
- **Definition:** A method that employs a depth map to enforce geometric consistency, specifically applying geometric consistency only in the near and far regions of the scene to effectively mitigate under-reconstruction in these critical regions while avoiding the risk of over-reconstruction [S4].

### Shape Energy Constraint
- **Definition:** An energy term defined as the combination of opacity energy and boundary energy, used to preserve the shape, opacity, and unitized colors (spherical harmonics) of Gaussian radiance fields during deformation [S5].
- **Pitfall:** Relying solely on feature energy is insufficient to preserve opacity and unitized colors; gray Gaussians with SH values close to zero appear and their size becomes uncontrolled because they cannot be constrained by feature energy alone [S5].
- **Comparison:** Without $E_{shape}$, the optimization process becomes unstable and artifacts appear; with $E_{shape}$, the process stabilizes and these artifacts are mitigated [S5].

## Background
The concept of regularizing 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as researchers observed that optimizing solely for photometric loss often results in unstructured density clouds that do not represent valid surfaces [S3]. Early methods introduced geometric priors to recover high-quality geometry, such as integrating Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) or using monocular depth priors [S3]. Subsequent work focused on specific failure modes: frequency regularization addressed over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts [S2, 9212-fregs.md], while covariance transformations aimed to enforce surface-aligned "half-Gaussian" shapes to reduce dust artifacts [9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md]. The field has diversified into depth-based constraints (hard/soft depth, cross-scale adaptation) and spectral constraints (FR, FreGS) to balance geometric fidelity with photometric accuracy.
