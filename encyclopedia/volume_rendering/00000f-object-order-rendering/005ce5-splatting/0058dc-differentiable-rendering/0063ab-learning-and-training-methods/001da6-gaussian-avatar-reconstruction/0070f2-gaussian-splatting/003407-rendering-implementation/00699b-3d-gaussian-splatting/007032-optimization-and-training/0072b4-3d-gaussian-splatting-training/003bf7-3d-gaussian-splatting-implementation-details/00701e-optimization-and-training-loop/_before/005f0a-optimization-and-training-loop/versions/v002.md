# Optimization and Training Loop
Also called: Differentiable Training Loops for Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
This family encompasses the iterative optimization procedures used to adjust 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, and color) to minimize the difference between rendered images and ground-truth photographs. It distinguishes itself from non-differentiable reconstruction methods by leveraging gradient descent through a differentiable rasterizer, allowing simultaneous correction of millions of parameters . While standard approaches optimize single views per iteration, variants introduce multi-view aggregation, subpixel jittering, or boundary-aware gradient approximations to stabilize convergence and mitigate aliasing .

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core objective of minimizing a photometric loss function $\mathcal{L}$ via gradient-based optimization. The general training loop consists of the following shared components:

1.  **Initialization:** Gaussians are initialized from a sparse Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point cloud or template model vertices .
2.  **Sampling:** A training view (image and camera pose) is sampled from the dataset .
3.  **Rendering:** The 3D scene is projected to 2D and alpha-blended using a differentiable rasterizer to produce a rendered image .
4.  **Loss Computation:** The rendered image is compared to the ground-truth image using a composite loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_{L1} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{L1}$ is the L1 loss and $\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ is the differentiable Structural Similarity index loss . The weight $\lambda$ is typically 0.2 .
5.  **Backpropagation:** Gradients $\nabla \mathcal{L}$ are computed with respect to Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, SH coefficients) .
6.  **Update:** Parameters are updated using the Adam optimizer with $\beta_1 = 0.9$ and $\beta_2 = 0.99$ .
7.  **Densification/Pruning:** Periodically, Gaussians are split or pruned based on gradient magnitudes and opacity thresholds to adapt density .

## Subtopics

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training Loop](15139-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-loop.md)
The standard iterative optimization process using single-view sampling and Adam optimization. Choose this for baseline static scene reconstruction.

### [Multi-View Regulated Gaussian Splatting](15783-multi-view-regulated-gaussian-splatting.md)
Aggregates gradients from $M$ views per step to reduce variance and improve multi-view consistency. Choose this when single-view updates cause instability or artifacts.

### [Mip-Splatting Training Loop & Evaluation Pipeline](17975-mip-splatting-training-loop-evaluation-pipeline.md)
Integrates subpixel ray jittering and ground truth resampling to mitigate aliasing. Choose this for high-resolution rendering where aliasing is prominent.

### [Bézier-boundary gradient approximation](13137-b-zier-boundary-gradient-approximation.md)
Approximates gradients for discontinuous indicator functions using cubic Bézier curves. Choose this when handling non-differentiable boundary transitions in alpha-blending.

## Background
The 3D Gaussian Splatting training loop was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . It builds on the differentiable rendering framework established by prior splatting methods, adapting it for explicit 3D Gaussian primitives. The method relies on the differentiability of the Gaussian projection and alpha-blending operations, enabling end-to-end optimization of scene geometry and appearance. Subsequent variations like Mip-Splatting and Multi-View Regulated Splatting address specific limitations such as aliasing and gradient variance in complex scenes .
