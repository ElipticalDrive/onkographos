# Multi-stage and Progressive Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Progressive Training, Stage-wise Training

## Overview
Multi-stage and Progressive Training decomposes the optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations into sequential or incremental phases to resolve conflicting objectives, stabilize convergence, or handle streaming data. It sets itself apart from single-stage optimization by decoupling complex tasks—such as geometry establishment from material refinement, or static initialization from temporal deformation—into distinct stages with specialized losses and parameter updates. This approach prevents instability caused by simultaneous learning of disparate attributes and enables controllable trade-offs between reconstruction quality and representation compactness.

## Shared principles
The family shares a core mechanism of temporal or hierarchical decomposition of the training process. Instead of optimizing all Gaussian parameters ($\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \alpha, \mathbf{c}$) and auxiliary variables (e.g., deformation fields, latent codes) jointly in a single loss landscape, the method splits the optimization into $N$ stages or phases.

1.  **Sequential Dependency:** Stage $k$ typically depends on the output of Stage $k-1$. Parameters optimized in earlier stages are often frozen or constrained in later stages to prevent catastrophic forgetting or gradient conflict [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Specialized Objectives:** Each stage employs a loss function tailored to its specific goal. For example, early stages may prioritize geometric stability and coarse appearance (L1/SSIM), while later stages introduce complex constraints like codec quantization, material total variation, or temporal consistency [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Resource Allocation:** In progressive streaming contexts, training resources (iterations, learning rates) are dynamically allocated based on image overlap or hierarchical weights, ensuring that new data integrates without overwriting existing knowledge [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Distillation/Refinement:** In feed-forward or predictor-based methods, intermediate stages may use short-horizon optimization to generate "ground truth" targets for subsequent stages, stabilizing the learning of residual Gaussian increments [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md)
Decomposes training into a coarse baseline establishment and a fine-grained refinement phase. It is chosen when conflicting objectives (e.g., geometry stability vs. compression efficiency, or appearance vs. material properties) cause instability in joint optimization. Unlike single-stage methods, it prevents gradient conflicts between generation and coding modules by separating them. See [Two-stage Optimization](14857-two-stage-optimization.md).

### [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md)
Optimizes 3DGS fields in a streaming context where images arrive sequentially. It solves catastrophic forgetting by decoupling initialization, incremental updates, and global refinement. It differs from static multi-stage methods by dynamically allocating learning rates and iterations based on hierarchical image overlap weights and introducing a load-balancing loss to prevent Gaussian accumulation. See [On-the-Fly GS Progressive Training](17502-on-the-fly-gs-progressive-training.md).

### [Warm-up stage](18447-warm-up-stage.md)
A preliminary phase used in dynamic variants (e.g., 4D-GS) to stabilize optimization when SfM points are sparse. It optimizes static 3D Gaussians before introducing temporal deformation networks, decoupling spatial initialization from temporal dynamics to prevent convergence difficulties and numeric errors. See [Warm-up stage](18447-warm-up-stage.md).

### [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md)
A distillation-based amortized optimization procedure that stabilizes multi-stage residual Gaussian predictors. It replaces unstable image-space gradients with locally refined Gaussian targets generated by short-horizon optimization. It prevents training collapse in feed-forward splatting where direct image-space supervision leads to degenerate solutions due to non-unique inverse mappings. See [POD Pretraining](19686-pod-pretraining.md).

### Progressive Stage-wise Prediction
A prediction mode where Gaussian primitives are accumulated progressively across multiple stages rather than predicted in a single shot. It enables explicit control over the number of activated Gaussians, allowing adjustment of the quality-compactness trade-off. On DIV2K, the stage-wise model (patch size 14) reaches 26.54 dB PSNR with 12,690 Gaussians at Stage 4, compared to a one-shot baseline (patch size 7) reaching 26.58 dB PSNR with 14,528 Gaussians [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of multi-stage optimization originates from the need to handle complex, non-convex loss landscapes in differentiable rendering. Early works in neural radiance fields (NeRF) and subsequent Gaussian Splatting variants identified that jointly optimizing geometry, appearance, and auxiliary variables (like deformation fields or compression codes) leads to instability. The "Two-stage" approach was formalized in works like GS-IR and CodecSplat to separate geometric initialization from material or codec refinement . Progressive training for streaming data emerged from the requirements of real-time reconstruction, such as in On-the-Fly GS [S1, unofficial]. The POD pretraining method was introduced to stabilize feed-forward Gaussian predictors, addressing the instability of direct image-space supervision [S1, unofficial].
