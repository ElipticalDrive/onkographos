# D3GA
Also called: D3GA, A method that creates animatable human avatars using drivable 3D Gaussians and tetrahedral cages for full-body modeling
## Overview
D3GA is a method for creating animatable human avatars from multi-view video data by combining drivable 3D Gaussians with tetrahedral cages [S1]. It addresses the challenge of full-body dynamic human modeling, aiming to provide promising geometry and appearance reconstruction [S1]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [GaussianHead](8051-gaussianhead.md) and [GART](8093-gart.md) by explicitly utilizing tetrahedral cages to drive the deformation of the Gaussian primitives, enabling full-body articulation [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Multi-view video sequences of a human subject in various poses.
*   **Output:** An animatable 3D Gaussian avatar model capable of rendering novel views and poses.

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Gaussians:** Standard 3D Gaussian splatting primitives (position, covariance, opacity, color/shininess) [S1].
*   **Tetrahedral Cages:** A tetrahedral mesh structure used to define the deformation space [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Initialize drivable 3D Gaussians representing the human body in a canonical space [S1].
2.  Construct tetrahedral cages around the human geometry [S1].
3.  Optimize the Gaussian attributes and cage parameters to reconstruct the input multi-view videos [S1].
4.  Use the tetrahedral cages to drive the deformation of the 3D Gaussians for novel poses [S1].

## Background
D3GA was introduced as part of the recent advances in 3D Gaussian splatting for digital human creation [S1]. It is cited as the first method to create animatable human avatars using drivable 3D Gaussians and tetrahedral cages [S1]. It is referenced in the context of full-body modeling, where it achieves promising geometry and appearance modeling [S1]. Subsequent works like SplatArmor and HuGS have built upon or offered alternatives to D3GA for capturing more dynamic details [S1].
