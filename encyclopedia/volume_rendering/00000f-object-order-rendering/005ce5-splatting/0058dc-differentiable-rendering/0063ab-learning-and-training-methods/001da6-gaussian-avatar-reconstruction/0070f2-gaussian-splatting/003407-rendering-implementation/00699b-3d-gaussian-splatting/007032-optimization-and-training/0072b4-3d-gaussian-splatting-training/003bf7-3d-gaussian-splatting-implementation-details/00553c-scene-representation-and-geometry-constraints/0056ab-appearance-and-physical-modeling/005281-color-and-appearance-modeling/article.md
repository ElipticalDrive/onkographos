# Color and Appearance Modeling
## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling extends the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation by replacing or augmenting the fixed Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients with more flexible mechanisms to represent view-dependent color, lighting, and material properties [G]. It addresses the limitations of base 3DGS, which struggles with complex specularities, dynamic lighting variations, and transient objects in unstructured datasets [G]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Physics and Optical Modeling](15148-physics-and-optical-modeling.md) by focusing on the radiance and material representation rather than volumetric light transport equations [G].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family inherit the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and rendering pipeline from [Scene Representation and Geometry Constraints](15421-scene-representation-and-geometry-constraints.md) [G]. The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients used in base 3DGS [G]. Common strategies include post-processing transformation [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md), factorized illumination [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md), image-conditioned modulation [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md), and neural field substitution [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md) [G].

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
DAVIGS is a plug-and-play appearance modeling method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates floaters and color distortions caused by appearance variations (e.g., daylight changes, ISP processing, local lighting) by transforming rendered images at the image level using 3D consistent features [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md). Unlike coupled methods that manipulate Gaussian properties and slow rendering, or decoupled methods that only handle mild global variations, DAVIGS decouples global view-dependent appearance from local spatial changes, ensuring 3D consistency while maintaining real-time rendering speeds by discarding the appearance module after optimization [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md).

### [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md)
Compact View-dependent Color replaces per-Gaussian Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients with a shared grid-based neural field to represent view-dependent color, reducing parameters from 48 per Gaussian to a compact shared representation [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md). Unlike sibling methods that apply post-processing transformations [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md), factorize illumination [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md), or use image-conditioned modulation [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md), this method substitutes directional encoding with a spatial-hash-based MLP, addressing disk space explosion from high-order SH storage [Compact View-dependent Color](12904-compact-view-dependent-color.md).

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
3iGS enhances 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by modeling outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and per-Gaussian Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) attributes, rather than optimizing outgoing radiance in isolation [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md). It solves the problem of inaccurate specular view-dependent effects in reflective scenes by decoupling scene lighting from material properties, allowing for physically plausible shading while maintaining the rapid training and rendering speeds of rasterization [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md). Unlike vanilla 3DGS, which uses spherical harmonics for view-dependent color without accounting for adjacent Gaussians or scene lighting, 3iGS represents incident illumination locally with grid-based tensors and optimizes Gaussian BRDF features relative to this field [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md).

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Optimization encompasses methods that modify, compress, regularize, or replace the standard SH representation of view-dependent color in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to improve rendering quality, training stability, or computational efficiency [G]. It addresses the limitations of fixed-degree SH coefficients, which can lead to overfitting in sparse views, high memory consumption, or inability to capture complex lighting [G]. It differs from its siblings by focusing specifically on the *optimization* and *parameterization* of the directional radiance function $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$, rather than just the geometric representation or the camera tracking mechanism [G].

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
SWAG is an extension of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) designed to handle unstructured, in-the-wild image collections by modeling appearance variations and handling transient objects (occluders) without supervision [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md). It sets itself apart from base 3DGS and siblings like [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md) and [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md) by using a per-image embedding vector and an MLP to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling the disentangling of static scene geometry from dynamic occluders and lighting conditions [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).

#### GeoSplatting PBR Attribute Modeling
Uses multi-resolution hash grids with MLP headers to model PBR attributes (albedo, roughness, metallic) as continuous fields, avoiding learnable parameters for varying numbers of Gaussian points [S1]. Ed HashEnc uses 16 levels, $2^{19}$ max entries per level, 2 feature dimensions per entry, coarsest resolution 32, and finest resolution 4096 [S1]. Ed MLP uses 32x32x32x6 layers, Kaiming-uniform initialization, and Sigmoid activation [S1]. Es MLP uses 32x32x3 layers, Kaiming-uniform initialization, and no activation [S1]. The attributes are computed as $\text{diffAlbedo} = \mathcal{E}_\text{d}(\text{centerPos})$ and $(\text{roughness}, \text{metalness}) = \mathcal{E}_\text{s}(\text{centerPos})$ [S1].
