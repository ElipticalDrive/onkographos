# FreGS

Also called: Frequency-aware regularization for 3DGS, Progressive frequency regularization

## Overview
FreGS is a regularization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevents over-reconstruction and high-frequency artifacts by penalizing spectral discrepancies between rendered and ground-truth images [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike spatial or depth-based constraints, it operates directly in the frequency domain to stabilize the optimization landscape [S2].

## How it works
FreGS applies a progressive frequency regularization strategy to the Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial]. The method ensures smooth expansion of the Gaussian cloud in a fronto-parallel manner, which improves coverage and reduces noise [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Current set of 3D Gaussians, camera parameters, ground-truth images.
*   **Output:** Regularized gradients for Gaussian parameters.

**Data Structures:**
*   Standard 3DGS Gaussian attributes (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Render the current scene using the standard differentiable rasterizer [S1, unofficial].
2.  Compute the frequency domain representation of the rendered image and the ground-truth image (e.g., via Fourier Transform) [S2].
3.  Calculate the frequency discrepancy between the rendered and ground-truth images [S2].
4.  Apply progressive regularization to the primitives based on this discrepancy [S1, unofficial].
5.  Back-propagate the regularized loss to update Gaussian parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Regularization weight $\lambda$: Not given in the sources.
*   Progression schedule: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases:**
*   Oscillation in scaling and rotation gradients: While FreGS addresses overfitting, related methods like Faster-GS note that gradient updates for scaling and rotation often oscillate due to poorly conditioned learning rate ratios [S1, unofficial]. FreGS does not explicitly solve this; it focuses on frequency regularization [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** Prevents overfitting and high-frequency artifacts [S1, unofficial]. Addresses over-reconstruction by focusing on the frequency domain [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Specific computational overhead or memory complexity is not detailed in the sources.
*   **When to use:** When training leads to noisy, high-frequency artifacts or over-reconstruction of fine details [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Background
FreGS was introduced to address the issue of over-reconstruction in 3D Gaussian Splatting [S2]. It is cited in surveys on 3DGS rasterization and compression [S1, unofficial][S2].
