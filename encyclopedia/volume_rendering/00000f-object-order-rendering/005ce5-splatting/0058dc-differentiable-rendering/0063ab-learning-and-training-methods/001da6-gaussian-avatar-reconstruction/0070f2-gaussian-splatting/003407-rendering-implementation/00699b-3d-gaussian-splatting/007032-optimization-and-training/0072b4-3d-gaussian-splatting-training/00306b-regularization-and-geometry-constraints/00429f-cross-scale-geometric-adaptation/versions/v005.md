# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity by leveraging low-frequency components from low-resolution voxels for coarse geometry early in training, while medium- and high-resolution voxels refine details as training advances [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on pre-trained feature extractors or fixed depth priors, this method adapts to diverse scenes by dynamically choosing the most suitable geometric representation for each scale based on consistency across views [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training image pairs (frames $i$ and $j$), camera intrinsics ($K_i, K_j$), camera poses (rotation $R$, translation $t$) for both frames, multi-scale voxel representations (low, mid, high resolution), and rendered depth maps $D_l(p)$ for each scale $l$ at pixel coordinate $p$.
*   **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$, which is added to the training objective.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-scale Voxels:** The scene is represented by voxels at multiple resolutions (e.g., low, mid, high). Each scale $l$ provides a depth value $D_l(p_i)$ for a given pixel $p_i$ via volume rendering [S1].
*   **Camera Parameters:** Intrinsics $K$ and poses $(R, t)$ are required for coordinate transformations [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Reproject to 3D in Source Camera:** For a pixel coordinate $p_i$ in frame $i$, compute its homogeneous augmentation $\tilde{p}_i = [p_i^x, p_i^y, 1]^T$. Using the depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ at scale $l$ and camera intrinsics $K_i$, compute the 3D point in the camera coordinate system of frame $i$:
    $$ x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i $$
    [S1]

2.  **Transform to World Coordinates:** Transform the 3D point $x_{li}$ to the world coordinate system using the rotation matrix $R_i$ and translation vector $t_i$ of frame $i$:
    $$ x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i $$
    [S1]

3.  **Transform to Target Camera Coordinates:** Transform the world coordinate point $x_l$ to the camera coordinate system of frame $j$ using the rotation matrix $R_j$ and translation vector $t_j$ of frame $j$:
    $$ x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j) $$
    [S1]

4.  **Project to Target Image Plane:** Project the 3D point $x_{li \to j} = [x, y, z]^T$ back to the 2D pixel coordinate system of frame $j$ using the projection function $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$ and intrinsics $K_j$:
    $$ \tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]

5.  **Compute Reprojection Error:** Calculate the reprojection error $e_l(p_i)$ for scale $l$ as the difference between the original RGB pixel value $C_i(p_i)$ from frame $i$ and the warped RGB pixel value $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$ from frame $j$:
    $$ e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j}) $$
    [S1]

6.  **Select Pseudo-Depth:** Identify the scale $l'$ that minimizes the reprojection error for the training ray $r_{train}$:
    $$ D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train})) $$
    [S1]

7.  **Compute Geometric Loss:** Compute the geometric adaptation loss $L_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth $D_l(r_{train})$ at the current scale and the selected pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ across all training rays $R_{train}$:
    $$ L_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{r_{train} \in R_{train}} \| \hat{D}_l(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \|^2 $$
    [S1]

def squared_norm(vec):

def add(v1, v2):

def subtract(v1, v2):

def transpose(M):

**Parameters:**
*   **Voxel Scales:** Typically low, mid, and high resolutions [S1].
*   **Training Schedule:** Early training uses low-resolution voxels as pseudo-ground truth to guide coarse geometry learning. Medium- and high-resolution voxels gradually refine details as training advances [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sparse Views:** The method relies on accurate camera poses. In scenarios with significant viewpoint changes or sparse training views, generalization may be challenging [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Omitting novel view rays risks local minima, causing incorrect geometry [S1].
*   **Depth Mismatches:** Using a fixed depth prior (Ld) can suffer from scale mismatches later in training, whereas Lgeo refines depth progressively [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method requires computing reprojection errors for all scales and all pixels, which increases computational cost during training compared to methods without geometric adaptation. However, it eliminates the need for complex scheduling [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel representations and rendered depth maps for all scales [S1].
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality across scales compared to training without geometric adaptation [S1]. It achieves lower final depth error than the depth prior (Ld) alone [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no complex scheduling, robust to geometric noise, reduces floaters [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate camera poses. Performance may degrade with very sparse views or large viewpoint changes [S1].

## Background
Cross-scale geometric adaptation was proposed in the context of FrugalNeRF, a framework for synthesizing novel views from extremely few inputs [S1]. It was introduced to accelerate and regularize training by using weight-sharing voxels across scales and guiding multi-scale geometry via reprojection errors [S1]. The method addresses the issue of floaters and suboptimal convergence in few-shot NeRF by providing a stronger and more robust geometric constraint than traditional depth priors [S1].
