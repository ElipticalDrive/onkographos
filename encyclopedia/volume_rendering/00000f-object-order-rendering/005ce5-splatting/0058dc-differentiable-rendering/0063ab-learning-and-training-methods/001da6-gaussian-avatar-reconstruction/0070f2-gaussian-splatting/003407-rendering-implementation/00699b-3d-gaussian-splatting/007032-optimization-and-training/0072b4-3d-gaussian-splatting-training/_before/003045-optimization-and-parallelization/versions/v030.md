# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Gradient Parallelism, Warp-Synchronous Backprop

## Overview
Optimization and Parallelization refers to the strategies for accelerating the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its variants through hardware-aware parallelism and distributed computing. It solves the computational bottleneck of differentiable rasterization and gradient accumulation when scaling to large scenes or high-resolution datasets. It is distinguished from standard optimization by focusing on the *mechanism* of computation (e.g., per-splat vs. per-pixel backward passes, multi-GPU partitioning) rather than the *optimization algorithm* (e.g., Adam) or *representation* (e.g., densification) [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation], [7349-grendel-gs.md].

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing gradients through probabilistic masks applied to Gaussians. It explicitly accounts for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground Gaussian color accumulation and the background transmittance path, distinguishing it from standard backpropagation which assumes fixed opacity [4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md].

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop integrating dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training loop. It distinguishes itself by adapting the representation density to scene complexity during optimization, rather than optimizing a static set [5325-training-pipeline.md].

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It addresses the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in the per-pixel approach by iterating over Gaussians and updating the gradients of the pixels they cover, yielding a 4–5× reduction in training time [5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md].

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs. Unlike single-machine streaming methods, it distributes the parameter table across a GPU cluster, enabling training on scenes with billions of primitives by leveraging inter-GPU communication [7349-grendel-gs.md].

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details specific hyperparameters, optimization schedules, and hardware-aware implementation strategies (e.g., half-precision stability, register pressure reduction). It sets itself apart by providing concrete, reproducible configuration values for densification thresholds and numerical stability techniques [9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . The initial implementation focused on real-time rendering and basic differentiable rasterization. Subsequent research addressed scalability and training efficiency, leading to methods like Grendel-GS for distributed training and parallelization techniques for the backward pass to mitigate GPU memory bandwidth and atomic operation bottlenecks .
