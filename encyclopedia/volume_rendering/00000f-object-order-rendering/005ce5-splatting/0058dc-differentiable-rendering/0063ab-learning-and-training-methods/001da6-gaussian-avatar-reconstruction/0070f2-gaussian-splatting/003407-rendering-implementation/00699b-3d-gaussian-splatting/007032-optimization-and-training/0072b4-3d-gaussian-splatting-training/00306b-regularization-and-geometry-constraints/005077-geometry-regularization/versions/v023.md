# Geometry Regularization

Also called: Gaussian Flattening, Normal Consistency and Flattening

## Overview
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions by explicitly constraining the shape and orientation of the Gaussians, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains their spatial location [S1, S2, unofficial]. It addresses geometric inconsistency and floaters by enforcing that Gaussians align with the underlying surface geometry through normal consistency and flattening [S1, S2, unofficial]. This method sets itself apart from frequency-based siblings like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating directly on the Gaussian covariance parameters and rendered normals rather than the image spectrum.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-Gaussian scaling factors $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, per-Gaussian normals $n_g$, rendered depth map $D$, and rendered normal map $N_{render}$ derived from $D$.
*   **Outputs:** Gradient updates to Gaussian parameters (specifically scaling and orientation) to minimize geometric loss.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Scaling ($s$):** A vector of three positive real numbers representing the scale along the principal axes of the Gaussian ellipsoid.
*   **Gaussian Normal ($n_g$):** A unit vector representing the orientation of the Gaussian, typically derived from the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix, or aligned with the shortest scaling axis.
*   **Rendered Depth ($D$):** A 2D array of depth values for each pixel.
*   **Rendered Normal ($N_{render}$):** A 2D array of 3D normal vectors computed from the spatial gradients of the rendered depth map.

**Steps:**
1.  **Enable Geometry Regularization:** The feature is controlled by a boolean parameter `use_normal_loss` in the optimization configuration [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Rendered Normals:** Calculate the surface normals $N_{render}$ from the rendered depth map $D$ using finite differences or analytical derivatives of the projection [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Enforce Depth Normal Consistency:** Align the per-Gaussian normals $n_g$ with the corresponding rendered normals $N_{render}$ at the projected pixel locations. This ensures the Gaussian orientation matches the local surface geometry inferred from depth [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Enforce Flattening Surface:** Minimize the smallest component of the scaling factor $s$ towards zero. This forces the Gaussian to become a flat surflet aligned with the surface, reducing volume leakage and floaters [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Normal Consistency Loss:** Minimizes the angular difference between the Gaussian normal $n_g$ and the rendered normal $n_{render}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{normal} = 1 - |n_g \cdot n_{render}| $$
    (Note: The exact formulation is implied by "align" in [S1, unofficial]; typically this is an L2 or cosine similarity loss).
*   **Flattening Loss:** Minimizes the smallest scale component $s_{min}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{flatten} = s_{min} $$
    where $s_{min} = \min(s_x, s_y, s_z)$ [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `use_normal_loss`: Boolean flag to enable the regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{geo}$: Weighting factor for the geometry regularization term in the total loss. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Refitting:** Depth maps from different frames may vary in scale. They must be refitted against a prior point cloud to a consistent scale before use. Depth maps that fail refitting are discarded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sky/Distant Background:** Geometry regularization may pull geometry onto itself in empty space. Using a "Sky Dome" (a frozen shell of Gaussians) can help disentangle the sky from the scene geometry [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_geometry_regularization_loss(gaussians, rendered_depth, rendered_normals, use_normal_loss):
    """
    Computes geometry regularization loss for 3D Gaussian Splatting.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: Object containing per-Gaussian properties.
            - scaling: Tensor of shape (N, 3), scaling factors s.
            - normals: Tensor of shape (N, 3), per-Gaussian normals n_g.
            - positions: Tensor of shape (N, 3), 3D positions.
        rendered_depth: Tensor of shape (H, W), depth map D.
        rendered_normals: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), normal map N_render.
        use_normal_loss: Boolean, enables normal consistency.
        
    Returns:
        float: Total geometry regularization loss.
    """
    loss = 0.0
    
    # Step 4: Enforce Flattening Surface
    # Minimize the smallest component of the scaling factor towards zero
    # s is shape (N, 3)
    min_scale = torch.min(gaussians.scaling, dim=1).values # Shape (N,)
    loss += torch.mean(min_scale)
    
    if use_normal_loss:
        # Step 2 & 3: Enforce Depth Normal Consistency
        # Note: In a full implementation, one would project Gaussians to 2D,
        # sample the rendered normals at the projected centers, and compute alignment.
        # Here we assume a helper function `get_rendered_normals_at_gaussian_centers`
        # which handles the projection and sampling.
        
        # Get the 2D screen coordinates of the Gaussian centers
        screen_coords = project_to_screen(gaussians.positions, camera_params)
        
        # Sample the rendered normal map at these coordinates
        # Interpolation should be bilinear or nearest neighbor
        sampled_normals = sample_tensor(rendered_normals, screen_coords) # Shape (N, 3)
        
        # Ensure sampled normals are normalized
        sampled_normals = F.normalize(sampled_normals, dim=1)
        
        # Ensure Gaussian normals are normalized
        gaussian_normals = F.normalize(gaussians.normals, dim=1)
        
        # Compute normal consistency loss (cosine similarity)
        # Align n_g with n_render
        dot_product = torch.sum(gaussian_normals * sampled_normals, dim=1)
        # Loss is 1 - |dot| to penalize misalignment
        normal_loss = torch.mean(1.0 - torch.abs(dot_product))
        loss += normal_loss
        
    return loss
```

## Subtopics

### Scaling Loss for Geometry Modeling
A regularization technique to flatten Gaussian kernels into 3D surfels aligned with the surface by minimizing the smallest component of the scaling factor towards zero [S2, unofficial]. It aids in determining Gaussian normals by selecting the shortest scaled edge [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when explicit normal supervision is unavailable or to complement it with shape constraints [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds a constant overhead per Gaussian per training step for computing normals and scaling losses. The complexity is linear with respect to the number of Gaussians $N$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing rendered depth and normal maps, which is $O(H \times W)$ per frame.
*   **Strengths:** Reduces floaters, improves surface smoothness, and enforces geometric consistency [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate depth and normal estimation. Failing depth refitting can lead to discarded data [S1, unofficial]. May require careful tuning of the sky dome to avoid artifacts in empty space [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high geometric fidelity is required and floaters are present.
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited or depth/normal estimation is unreliable.

## Background
Originated from the need to improve the geometric quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions, which can suffer from noise and floaters due to the arbitrary spatial distribution of Gaussians [S1, S2, unofficial]. The technique of flattening Gaussians into surfels was proposed in prior work [5] cited in [S2, unofficial], and implemented in libraries like GaussianSplatting.jl [S1, unofficial].
