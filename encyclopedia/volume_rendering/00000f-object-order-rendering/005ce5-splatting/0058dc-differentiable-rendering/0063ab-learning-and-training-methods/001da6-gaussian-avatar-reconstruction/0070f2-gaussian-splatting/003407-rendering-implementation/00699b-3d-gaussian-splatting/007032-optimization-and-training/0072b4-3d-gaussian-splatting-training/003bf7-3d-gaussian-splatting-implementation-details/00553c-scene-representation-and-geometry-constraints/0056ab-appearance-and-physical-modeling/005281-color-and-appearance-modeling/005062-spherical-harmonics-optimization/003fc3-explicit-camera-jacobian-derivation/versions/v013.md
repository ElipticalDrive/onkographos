# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primitives to enable efficient camera pose optimization in SLAM systems [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of high computational overhead associated with automatic differentiation during gradient descent tracking by providing minimal, closed-form derivatives on the Lie group manifold [S1, unofficial]. Unlike prior ray-intersection methods that assume fewer Gaussians, this approach is specifically designed for the EWA splatting rasterization used in 3DGS, ensuring dimensionality matches the six degrees of freedom of the camera pose [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$, Gaussian mean in world coordinates $\mu_W$, Gaussian covariance in world coordinates $\Sigma_W$, projection matrix $P$, and camera intrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** Jacobians $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ required for gradient-based optimization of the camera pose.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: A $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing the camera-to-world pose.
*   $\mu_C$: A $3 \times 1$ vector representing the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
*   $\Sigma_I$: A $2 \times 2$ matrix representing the projected 2D covariance in image space.
*   $W$: The $3 \times 3$ rotational component of $T_{CW}$.
*   $\tau$: An infinitesimal parameter in the Lie algebra $se(3)$, represented as a $6 \times 1$ vector $[\rho, \theta]^T$, where $\rho$ is translation and $\theta$ is rotation.

**Mathematical Definitions:**
1.  **Lie Algebra Derivative:** The derivative of a function $f(\mathbf{T})$ with respect to $\mathbf{T} \in SE(3)$ is defined on the manifold as [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau} $$
    where $\circ$ is group composition, and $\text{Exp}$ and $\text{Log}$ are the exponential and logarithmic mappings between $se(3)$ and $SE(3)$.

2.  **Skew-Symmetric Matrix:** For a vector $\mathbf{v} = [v_x, v_y, v_z]^T$, the skew-symmetric matrix $\mathbf{v}^\times$ is:
    $$ \mathbf{v}^\times = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -v_z & v_y \\ v_z & 0 & -v_x \\ -v_y & v_x & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Transform Gaussian to Camera Coordinates:**
    Compute the Gaussian mean in camera coordinates $\mu_C$ from the world mean $\mu_W$ and pose $T_{CW}$:
    $$ \mu_C = W \mu_W + t $$
    where $t$ is the translation vector of $T_{CW}$.

2.  **Derive Jacobian of Camera Mean w.r.t. Pose:**
    Using the chain rule and the Lie algebra definition, the derivative of the camera-space mean $\mu_C$ with respect to the camera pose $T_{CW}$ is derived as [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = [I \quad -\mu_C^\times] $$
    *Derivation:*
    $$ \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Exp}(\tau) \cdot \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} $$
    $$ = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(\mathbf{I} + \tau^\wedge) \cdot \boldsymbol{\mu}_C - \boldsymbol{\mu}_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \cdot \boldsymbol{\mu}_C}{\tau} $$
    Decomposing $\tau^\wedge \cdot \boldsymbol{\mu}_C$ into translation $\rho$ and rotation $\theta$:
    $$ = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\theta^\times \boldsymbol{\mu}_C + \rho}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{-\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho}{\tau} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix} $$

3.  **Derive Jacobian of Rotation Component w.r.t. Pose:**
    The derivative of the rotation matrix component $W$ with respect to the camera pose is required for the covariance transformation. The derivative of $W$ with respect to the rotation vector $\theta$ is [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,3}^\times \end{bmatrix} $$
    where $W_{:,i}$ is the $i$-th column of $W$. This is derived from $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_k} = \mathbf{e}_k^\times W$, where $\mathbf{e}_k$ are basis vectors.

4.  **Compute Jacobian of Projected Covariance:**
    The 2D covariance $\Sigma_I$ depends on the 3D covariance $\Sigma_C$ (transformed from $\Sigma_W$) and the projection Jacobian $J$. The total derivative with respect to $T_{CW}$ is [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} $$
    *Note:* $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J}$ and $\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C}$ are standard derivatives from the 3DGS rasterization pipeline. $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W}$ accounts for the rotation of the 3D covariance into camera space.

5.  **Compute Jacobian of Projected Mean:**
    The derivative of the projected mean $\mu_I$ is [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} $$
    where $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C}$ is the standard projection Jacobian from 3DGS.

**Parameters:**
*   **Iterations:** At least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame are required for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hyperparameters:** In SLAM tracking, a balance between photometric and geometric residuals is controlled by $\lambda_{pho}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def skew_symmetric(v):
    """Compute the skew-symmetric matrix of a 3D vector."""
    return np.array([
        [0, -v[2], v[1]],
        [v[2], 0, -v[0]],
        [-v[1], v[0], 0]
    ])

def compute_camera_jacobians(T_CW, mu_W, Sigma_W, P, K):
    """
    Compute analytical Jacobians of 3DGS projection w.r.t. camera pose T_CW.
    
    Args:
        T_CW: 4x4 SE(3) camera-to-world transformation matrix.
        mu_W: 3x1 Gaussian mean in world coordinates.
        Sigma_W: 3x3 Gaussian covariance in world coordinates.
        P: 3x4 projection matrix (P = K [R|t]).
        K: 3x3 camera intrinsic matrix.
        
    Returns:
        d_mu_I_d_T: 2x6 Jacobian of projected mean w.r.t. pose.
        d_Sigma_I_d_T: 2x2x6 Jacobian of projected covariance w.r.t. pose.
    """
    # Extract rotation and translation from T_CW
    R_CW = T_CW[:3, :3]
    t_CW = T_CW[:3, 3]
    
    # 1. Transform Gaussian to camera coordinates
    mu_C = R_CW @ mu_W + t_CW
    
    # 2. Compute Jacobian of mu_C w.r.t. T_CW (6 DOF)
    # d_mu_C/d_T_CW = [I, -mu_C^x]
    I_3 = np.eye(3)
    mu_C_skew = skew_symmetric(mu_C)
    d_mu_C_d_T = np.hstack([I_3, -mu_C_skew])  # Shape: 3x6
    
    # 3. Compute Jacobian of W (R_CW) w.r.t. T_CW
    # d_W/d_T_CW is a 3x3x6 tensor, but we only need the rotation part
    # d_W/d_theta = [e1^x W, e2^x W, e3^x W]
    # Since translation part of T_CW does not affect R_CW, the first 3 cols are 0
    d_W_d_T = np.zeros((3, 3, 6))
    for i in range(3):
        e_i = np.eye(3)[:, i]
        e_i_skew = skew_symmetric(e_i)
        d_W_d_T[:, :, 3+i] = e_i_skew @ R_CW
        
    # 4. Standard 3DGS Projection Jacobians (from rasterization)
    # These are typically computed during the forward pass
    # d_mu_I_d_mu_C: 2x3 Jacobian of projected mean w.r.t. camera mean
    # d_Sigma_I_d_J: 2x2x3x3 Jacobian of covariance w.r.t. projection Jacobian J
    # d_J_d_mu_C: 3x3x3 Jacobian of projection Jacobian w.r.t. camera mean
    # d_Sigma_I_d_W: 2x2x3x3 Jacobian of covariance w.r.t. rotation matrix W
    
    # For the purpose of this article, we assume these standard 3DGS derivatives are available
    # from the existing rasterization kernel.
    
    # 5. Combine using Chain Rule
    
    # Jacobian of projected mean
    # d_mu_I/d_T_CW = (d_mu_I/d_mu_C) @ (d_mu_C/d_T_CW)
    d_mu_I_d_T = d_mu_I_d_mu_C @ d_mu_C_d_T  # Shape: 2x6
    
    # Jacobian of projected covariance
    # d_Sigma_I/d_T_CW = (d_Sigma_I/d_J @ d_J/d_mu_C) @ d_mu_C/d_T_CW + (d_Sigma_I/d_W) @ d_W/d_T_CW
    # Note: The second term involves tensor contraction.
    
    # Term 1: d_Sigma_I/d_J @ d_J/d_mu_C results in a 2x2x3x3 tensor contracted with d_mu_C/d_T_CW (3x6)
    # Result is 2x2x6
    term1 = np.einsum('ijkm,km->ijm', d_Sigma_I_d_J @ d_J_d_mu_C, d_mu_C_d_T)
    
    # Term 2: d_Sigma_I/d_W (2x2x3x3) contracted with d_W/d_T_CW (3x3x6)
    # Result is 2x2x6
    term2 = np.einsum('ijkm,kmn->ijn', d_Sigma_I_d_W, d_W_d_T)
    
    d_Sigma_I_d_T = term1 + term2
    
    return d_mu_I_d_T, d_Sigma_I_d_T
```

## Subtopics

### Photometric and Geometric Tracking
This variant optimizes only the current camera pose without updating the map representation, minimizing photometric and/or geometric residuals [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when the scene geometry is fixed or when real-time tracking speed is prioritized over mapping accuracy.
*   **Photometric Residual:** Minimizes the L1 difference between the rendered image $I(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW})$ and the observed image $\bar{I}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ E_{pho} = \left\| I(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW}) - \bar{I} \right\|_1 $$
*   **Geometric Residual:** Minimizes the L1 difference between the rendered depth $D(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW})$ and the observed depth $\bar{D}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ E_{geo} = \left\| D(\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{T}_{CW}) - \bar{D} \right\|_1 $$
*   **Depth Rasterization:** Per-pixel depth is computed via alpha blending [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathcal{D}_p = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} z_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $z_i$ is the distance to the mean $\mu_W$ of Gaussian $i$ along the camera ray.
*   **Combined Optimization:** The total loss is $\lambda_{pho} E_{pho} + (1-\lambda_{pho}) E_{geo}$, where $\lambda_{pho}$ is a hyperparameter [S1, unofficial]. Affine brightness parameters are optimized to handle exposure changes, and non-edge or low-opacity pixels are penalized [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The explicit derivation eliminates the overhead of automatic differentiation, making the gradient computation comparable to the forward rasterization pass. This enables real-time performance (e.g., 3.2 FPS on monocular, 2.5 FPS on RGB-D) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal, as it only stores the Jacobians for the current frame's optimization step.
*   **Strengths:** Computational efficiency, minimal parameterization (6 DOF), and applicability to large-scale 3DGS scenes with many Gaussians.
*   **Limitations:** Requires at least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame for accurate tracking, which can be computationally expensive if not optimized [S1, unofficial]. Assumes the scene representation (Gaussians) is fixed during tracking.
*   **When to Use:** Real-time SLAM systems where camera pose estimation is critical and the scene geometry is static or slowly changing.
*   **When Not to Use:** Offline mapping where joint optimization of pose and geometry is preferred, or when the number of Gaussians is very small (where ray-intersection methods might suffice).

## Background
The method originates from the "Gaussian Splatting SLAM" paper, which introduced the use of 3D Gaussians as the sole representation for SLAM, unifying tracking, mapping, and novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial]. Prior methods for camera pose optimization with 3D Gaussians relied on ray-intersection approximations that assumed a small number of Gaussians and were not applicable to the EWA splatting rasterization used in 3DGS [S1, unofficial]. This work provides the first analytical Jacobian of SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians in EWA splatting, enabling efficient gradient-based tracking [S1, unofficial].
