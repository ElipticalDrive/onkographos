# Color and Appearance Modeling

Also called: Appearance Modeling, View-Dependent Color Modeling

## Overview
Color and Appearance Modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting addresses the limitations of the base method's fixed Spherical Harmonics (SH) representation, which struggles with complex lighting, transient objects, and high-frequency view-dependent effects. It solves the problem of color distortion and floaters by introducing dynamic mechanisms to modulate Gaussian radiance or opacity based on spatial position, viewing direction, or image context. It sets itself apart from siblings by either decoupling global appearance from local geometry ([DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)), factorizing illumination and material properties ([3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)), or conditioning rendering on per-image embeddings to handle wild images ([SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)), rather than relying solely on the static SH coefficients of base 3DGS.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering equation, where the pixel color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is computed via alpha-blending of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i g_i(\mathbf{u} | \boldsymbol{\mu}', \boldsymbol{\Sigma}') \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i(\omega) $$
where $T_i$ is the transmittance, $g_i$ is the 2D projected Gaussian density, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, and $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ is the view-dependent outgoing radiance. The core variation across methods lies in the definition, storage, and optimization of $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ and potentially $\alpha_i$, moving beyond the fixed SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ used in base 3DGS. Common strategies include:
1.  **Post-Processing Transformation:** Applying a learned affine transformation to the rendered image based on 3D position and view embedding [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md).
2.  **Factorized Illumination:** Separating scene lighting (via a tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to model specular effects [3iGS](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md).
3.  **Image-Conditioned Modulation:** Using an MLP and per-image embeddings to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling transient object removal and appearance synthesis [SWAG](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md).
4.  **Neural Field Substitution:** Replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [Compact View-dependent Color].

## Subtopics

### [DAVIGS](12021-davigs.md)
A plug-and-post appearance modeling method that mitigates floaters and color distortions by transforming rendered images at the image level using 3D consistent features. It decouples global view-dependent appearance from local spatial changes, ensuring 3D consistency while maintaining real-time rendering speeds by discarding the appearance module after optimization.

### [3iGS (Factorised Tensorial Illumination for 3D Gaussian Splatting)](13977-3igs-factorised-tensorial-illumination-for-3d-gaussian-splat.md)
Enhances 3DGS by modeling outgoing radiance as a function of a continuous local illumination field and individual Gaussian BRDF attributes. It explicitly separates scene lighting (via a factorized tensor grid) from material properties (via BRDF features) to accurately capture specular view-dependent effects.

### [Spherical Harmonics Optimization](14206-spherical-harmonics-optimization.md)
Refers to techniques that modify the representation, storage, or training dynamics of the view-dependent radiance coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i(\omega)$ to improve rendering quality, training stability, or inference efficiency. It focuses on the SH coefficients themselves (via distillation, quantization, or degree reduction) or the optimization infrastructure surrounding them, rather than replacing the SH representation entirely.

### [SWAG (Splatting in the Wild images with Appearance-conditioned Gaussians)](14315-swag-splatting-in-the-wild-images-with-appearance-conditione.md)
An extension of 3DGS designed to handle unstructured, in-the-wild image collections by modeling appearance variations and handling transient objects without supervision. It uses a per-image embedding vector and an MLP to modulate Gaussian color and opacity, enabling the disentangling of static scene geometry from dynamic occluders and lighting conditions.

### Compact View-dependent Color
**Definition:** A method to represent view-dependent color using a grid-based neural field instead of storing Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients per Gaussian, reducing parameters from 48 to a shared grid [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Contract unbounded positions $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ to a bounded range using the contraction function from mip-NeRF 360 [S1].
2.  Input positions into hash grids, then feed the resulting feature and view direction into a tiny MLP [S1].
3.  Use 0-degree components of SH (same channels as RGB, not view-dependent) and convert to RGB colors [S1].

**Formulas:**
Contraction function [S1]:
$$ \text{contract}(p_n) = \begin{cases} p_n & \text{if } \|p_n\| \le 1 \\ \frac{p_n}{\|p_n\|} (2 - \|p_n\|) & \text{if } \|p_n\| > 1 \end{cases} $$
View-dependent color $c_n(d)$ is computed as [S1]:
$$ c_n(d) = f(\text{contract}(p_n), d; \theta) $$
where $f$ is a tiny MLP with parameters $\theta$.

**Comparison:** Original 3DGS uses 48 parameters per Gaussian for SH (max 3 degrees) to model view-dependent color; this method uses a shared grid-based neural field [S1].

## Background
The base 3D Gaussian Splatting method uses third-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) to represent view-dependent color, which requires 48 parameters per Gaussian [S1]. This approach is computationally expensive and memory-intensive, and it can produce artifacts such as color bleeding or inability to model complex specularities [S1]. The need for more efficient and accurate appearance modeling led to the development of methods like Compact 3D Gaussian Representation, which proposes replacing per-Gaussian SH with a shared grid-based neural field to reduce parameters and improve generalization [S1]. Other approaches, such as DAVIGS and SWAG, emerged to handle specific challenges like appearance variations in in-the-wild images and transient objects [S1].
