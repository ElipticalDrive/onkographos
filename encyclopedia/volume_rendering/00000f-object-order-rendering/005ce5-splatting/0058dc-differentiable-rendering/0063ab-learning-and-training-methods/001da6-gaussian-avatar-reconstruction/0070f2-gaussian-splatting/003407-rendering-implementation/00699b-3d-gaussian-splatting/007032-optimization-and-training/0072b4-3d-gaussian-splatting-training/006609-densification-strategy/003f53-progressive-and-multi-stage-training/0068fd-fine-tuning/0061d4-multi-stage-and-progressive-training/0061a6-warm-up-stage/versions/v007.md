# Warm-up stage

Also called: 3D Gaussian Initialization (Warm-up)

## Overview
A training adaptation in 3D Gaussian Splatting that improves convergence stability and distractor suppression by optimizing a static 3D Gaussian representation before introducing more complex dynamic or temporal components [S1, S2]. It serves as a prerequisite initialization phase to prevent convergence difficulties when explicit Structure-from-Motion (SfM) points are absent or when transitioning to 4D representations [S2].

## How it works
The warm-up stage acts as a coarse initialization for the scene representation, decoupling the optimization of static geometry from dynamic deformation or complex material properties.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Raw image sequence or static views; optionally absent SfM points.
*   **Output:** Initialized 3D Gaussian parameters $\Gamma$ (position, covariance, opacity, color) that serve as the starting point for subsequent stages (e.g., 4D-GS training) [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Gaussians ($\Gamma$):** A set of $N$ Gaussians. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by mean position $\mu_i$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (or scale/rotation parameters), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color $c_i$.
*   **Deformation Network ($\Delta$):** In dynamic variants, a network that predicts deformation fields. The warm-up stage avoids direct optimization of $\Delta$ initially to prevent numeric errors [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Static Initialization:** If SfM points are available, initialize 3D Gaussians at these points. If SfM points are absent, generate an initial set of 3D Gaussians (e.g., via random sampling or clustering) to cover the scene volume [S2].
2.  **Static Optimization:** Optimize the 3D Gaussian parameters $\Gamma$ using standard 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering and loss functions (e.g., L1 loss, D-SSIM) for a fixed number of iterations or until convergence. This stage establishes a stable geometric prior [S2].
3.  **Transition to Dynamic/Complex Model:** Use the optimized $\Gamma$ as the initialization for the next stage. In 4D-GS, this involves:
    *   (a) Keeping some 3D Gaussians in the dynamic part to release the pressure of learning large deformations directly with 4D Gaussians [S2].
    *   (b) Learning proper 3D Gaussians $\Gamma$ and suggesting deformation fields to pay more attention to the dynamic part [S2].
    *   (c) Avoiding numeric errors in optimizing the Gaussian deformation network $\Delta$ by ensuring the base geometry is stable [S2].

**Formulas:**
*   **Rendering:** The color $C(r)$ of a ray $r$ is computed via alpha compositing:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i \in N} c_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $N$ is the set of sorted Gaussians intersecting the ray.
*   **Loss:** The total loss $L$ typically combines photometric and structural terms:
    $$ L = (1 - \lambda) L_{L1} + \lambda L_{D-SSIM} $$
    where $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter (typically 0.2) [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Warm-up Iterations:** Not explicitly given in the sources. The sources note that training *without* this stage takes 7.5 minutes for the full process, implying the warm-up is a subset of this or a separate phase [S2].
*   **SfM Points:** Optional. Their absence necessitates the warm-up initialization strategy [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Absence of SfM Points:** Directly training 4D-GS without a warm-up stage causes convergence difficulty [S2].
*   **Numeric Errors:** Optimizing the deformation network $\Delta$ directly without a stable geometric prior can lead to numeric instability [S2].
*   **Distractors:** Without adaptations like the warm-up stage (and others like GLO, Bernoulli sampling), transient distractors may leak into the reconstruction [S1].

## Subtopics

### 3D Gaussian Initialization (Warm-up)
A specific variant of the warm-up stage used in 4D Gaussian Splatting (4D-GS) when SfM points initialization is absent. It optimizes 3D Gaussians before training 4D-GS directly to prevent convergence difficulty. It keeps some 3D Gaussians in the dynamic part to release pressure of large deformation learning by 4D Gaussians, learns proper 3D Gaussians $\Gamma$ and suggests deformation fields to pay more attention to the dynamic part, and avoids numeric errors in optimizing the Gaussian deformation network $\Delta$ to keep training stable [S2]. Training without this coarse stage yields PSNR 31.91 dB, SSIM 0.97, LPIPS 0.03, training time 7.5 mins, 79 FPS, and 18 MB storage [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds an initial optimization phase. The sources do not give the specific time cost of the warm-up stage alone, but note that the full process without warm-up takes 7.5 minutes [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the initialized 3D Gaussians. The sources do not give specific memory overhead for the warm-up stage itself, but note the final storage is 18 MB without warm-up [S2].
*   **Performance:** Improves convergence stability and prevents numeric errors in dynamic scenes [S2]. It also contributes to distractor suppression in methods like SpotLessSplats [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents convergence failure in the absence of SfM points; stabilizes deformation network training.
*   **Limitations:** Adds an extra training stage; requires careful tuning of the transition between static and dynamic optimization.
*   **When to use:** When SfM points are absent or when training dynamic 4D-GS models to avoid large deformation learning issues [S2].
*   **When not to use:** When SfM points are abundant and high-quality, and the scene is static, a separate warm-up stage may be redundant [S2].

## Background
The warm-up stage is a training adaptation used in 3D Gaussian Splatting and its dynamic variants (4D-GS). It was introduced to address convergence difficulties in dynamic scene rendering, particularly when explicit SfM points are not available [S2]. It is also cited as one of several adaptations (along with GLO and Bernoulli sampling) that show improvements in distractor suppression in methods like SpotLessSplats [S1].
