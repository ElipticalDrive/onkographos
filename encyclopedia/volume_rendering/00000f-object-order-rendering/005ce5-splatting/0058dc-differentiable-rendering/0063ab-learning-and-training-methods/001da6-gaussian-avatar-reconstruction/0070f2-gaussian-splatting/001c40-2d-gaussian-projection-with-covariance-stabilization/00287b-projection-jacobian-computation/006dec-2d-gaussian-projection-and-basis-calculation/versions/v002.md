# 2D Gaussian Projection and Basis Calculation

Also called: 2D Gaussian Splatting Projection and Basis Calculation, Covariance Projection, Gaussian Splat Basis Computation

## Overview
This method computes the 2D screen-space basis vectors and eigenvalues for a 3D Gaussian splat by projecting its 3D covariance matrix through the view and projection matrices [S1, unofficial]. It transforms the 3D uncertainty representation into a 2D ellipse defined by orthogonal basis vectors and their scales, which are subsequently used to construct screen-aligned quads for rasterization [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Projection Jacobian Computation](4272-projection-jacobian-computation.md), which provides the linearization of the projection function [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `centroid`: Array of `vec3<f32>` containing 3D centers [S1, unofficial].
    *   `covariance`: Array of `vec3<f32>` storing the upper-triangular elements of the 3D covariance matrix [S1, unofficial]. For a Gaussian with index `gid`, the first row is `covariance[gid*2]` and the second row is `covariance[gid*2+1]` [S1, unofficial].
    *   `modelView`: `mat4x4<f32>` model-view matrix [S1, unofficial].
    *   `projection`: `mat4x4<f32>` projection matrix [S1, unofficial].
    *   `screen`: `vec2<f32>` containing the pixel width and height of the viewport [S1, unofficial].
    *   `splatCount`: `u32` number of valid splats [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   `basis`: Array of `vec4<f32>` [S1, unofficial]. Each element stores `basisVector1.x`, `basisVector1.y`, `basisVector2.x`, `basisVector2.y` [S1, unofficial].
    *   `distance`: Array of `u32` storing the sorted depth value for each splat [S1, unofficial].
    *   `id`: Array of `u32` (used for permutation during sorting, though the calculation itself only produces the distance key) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The 3D covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_{3D}$ is symmetric [S1, unofficial]. It is reconstructed from the input arrays as:
$$
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{3D} = \begin{bmatrix}
\sigma_{xx} & \sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{xz} \\
\sigma_{xy} & \sigma_{yy} & \sigma_{yz} \\
\sigma_{xz} & \sigma_{yz} & \sigma_{zz}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Where:
*   $\sigma_{xx} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2].x$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\sigma_{xy} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2].y$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\sigma_{xz} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2].z$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\sigma_{yy} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2 + 1].x$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\sigma_{yz} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2 + 1].y$ [S1, unofficial]
*   $\sigma_{zz} = \text{covariance}[gid \cdot 2 + 1].z$ [S1, unofficial]

**Steps:**

1.  **Construct the 3D Covariance Matrix:**
    Assemble $\mathbf{\Sigma}_{3D}$ from the input arrays as defined above [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute the Jacobian Matrix ($J$):**
    Calculate the $3 \times 3$ Jacobian of the perspective projection with respect to 3D coordinates [S1, unofficial].
    *   Extract camera focal lengths in pixels:
        $$ f_x = \text{projection}[0][0] \cdot \text{screen}.x \cdot 0.5 $$
        $$ f_y = \text{projection}[1][1] \cdot \text{screen}.y \cdot 0.5 $$
    *   Transform the centroid to view space:
        $$ \mathbf{c}_{view} = \text{modelView} \cdot [\text{centroid}[gid], 1]^T $$
        Let $z_c = \mathbf{c}_{view}.z$.
    *   Compute the scaling factor:
        $$ s = \frac{1}{z_c^2} $$
    *   Construct the $3 \times 3$ Jacobian matrix $J$ [S1, unofficial]:
        $$
        J = \begin{bmatrix}
        \frac{f_x}{z_c} & 0 & -f_x \cdot x_c \cdot s \\
        0 & \frac{f_y}{z_c} & -f_y \cdot y_c \cdot s \\
        0 & 0 & 0
        \end{bmatrix}
        $$
        Where $x_c = \mathbf{c}_{view}.x$ and $y_c = \mathbf{c}_{view}.y$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute the Transformation Matrix ($T$):**
    Extract the $3 \times 3$ model-view rotation/translation part (excluding projection) and multiply by $J$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Extract model-view $3 \times 3$ matrix $M_{3x3}$:
        $$
        M_{3x3} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \text{modelView}[0].x & \text{modelView}[0].y & \text{modelView}[0].z \\
        \text{modelView}[1].x & \text{modelView}[1].y & \text{modelView}[1].z \\
        \text{modelView}[2].x & \text{modelView}[2].y & \text{modelView}[2].z
        \end{bmatrix}^T
        $$
        The source code transposes the matrix constructed from the first three columns' xyz components, which effectively extracts the upper-left $3 \times 3$ rotation/affine part correctly for row-major vs column-major conventions depending on the library [S1, unofficial]. In standard column-major GLSL/WebGPU, `modelView[0]` is the first column [S1, unofficial]. The code `transpose(mat3x3(modelView[0].xyz, ...))` creates a matrix where rows are the columns of the original, then transposes it back, effectively just taking the top-left $3 \times 3$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute $T$:
        $$ T = M_{3x3} \cdot J $$ [S1, unofficial]

4.  **Compute the Projected 2D Covariance Matrix ($\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2D}$):**
    $$ \mathbf{\Sigma}_{2D} = T^T \cdot \mathbf{\Sigma}_{3D} \cdot T $$ [S1, unofficial]
    Extract the relevant components into a vector `cov2Dv`:
    $$ \text{cov2Dv} = [\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2D}[0][0], \mathbf{\Sigma}_{2D}[0][1], \mathbf{\Sigma}_{2D}[1][1]] $$ [S1, unofficial]
    Let $a = \text{cov2Dv}.x$, $b = \text{cov2Dv}.y$, $d = \text{cov2Dv}.z$.

5.  **Compute Eigenvalues:**
    *   Determinant: $D = a \cdot d - b^2$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Trace: $\text{tr} = a + d$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Half Trace: $\text{tr}_2 = 0.5 \cdot \text{tr}$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Discriminant term: $\Delta = \sqrt{\frac{\text{tr}^2}{4.0} - D}$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Eigenvalues:
        $$ \lambda_1 = \text{tr}_2 + \Delta $$ [S1, unofficial]
        $$ \lambda_2 = \max(\text{tr}_2 - \Delta, 0.0) $$ [S1, unofficial]
        The $\max$ operation prevents negative eigenvalues due to numerical precision errors [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Compute Eigenvectors:**
    *   First eigenvector (normalized):
        $$ \mathbf{v}_1 = \text{normalize}([b, \lambda_1 - a]) $$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Second eigenvector (orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}_1$):
        $$ \mathbf{v}_2 = [\mathbf{v}_1.y, -\mathbf{v}_1.x] $$ [S1, unofficial]

7.  **Scale Eigenvectors to Form Basis Vectors:**
    *   Define maximum splat size: $S_{max} = 1024.0$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Scale by 4 times the standard deviation (covering ~95% of the probability mass for a 2D Gaussian):
        $$ \mathbf{b}_1 = \mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \min(\sqrt{\lambda_1} \cdot 4, S_{max}) $$ [S1, unofficial]
        $$ \mathbf{b}_2 = \mathbf{v}_2 \cdot \min(\sqrt{\lambda_2} \cdot 4, S_{max}) $$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Store $\mathbf{b}_1$ and $\mathbf{b}_2$ in the `basis` output array [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Compute Distance for Sorting:**
    *   Project the center to clip space:
        $$ \mathbf{c}_{clip} = \text{projection} \cdot \text{modelView} \cdot [\text{centroid}[gid], 1]^T $$ [S1, unofficial]
    *   Convert depth to NDC and then to a normalized integer for radix sorting:
        $$ z_{ndc} = \frac{\mathbf{c}_{clip}.z}{\mathbf{c}_{clip}.w} $$ [S1, unofficial]
        $$ \text{distance}[gid] = \text{u32}\left( \left( \frac{z_{ndc} + 1.0}{2.0} \right) \cdot 2^{24} \right) $$ [S1, unofficial]
        *(Note: $0xFFFFFFFF \gg 8$ is $2^{24}-1$. The source uses `f32(0xFFFFFFFF >> 8)` which is $16777215.0$.)* [S1, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_2d_gaussian_projection(centroid, covariance, model_view, projection, screen, splat_count, gid):
    """
    Computes 2D basis vectors and distance for a single Gaussian splat.
    Assumes row-major matrix multiplication order consistent with the source WebGPU shader logic.
    """
    if gid >= splat_count:
        # Padding dummy splats must sort to the end
        return None, 0xFFFFFFFF

    # 1. Construct 3D Covariance Matrix
    cov_first = covariance[gid * 2]
    cov_second = covariance[gid * 2 + 1]
    
    # Sigma_3D is symmetric
    sigma_xx = cov_first.x
    sigma_xy = cov_first.y
    sigma_xz = cov_first.z
    sigma_yy = cov_second.x
    sigma_yz = cov_second.y
    sigma_zz = cov_second.z
    
    # 2. Compute Jacobian J
    # Extract focal lengths in pixels
    fx = projection[0][0] * screen.x * 0.5
    fy = projection[1][1] * screen.y * 0.5
    
    # Transform centroid to view space
    center_4d = [centroid[gid].x, centroid[gid].y, centroid[gid].z, 1.0]
    view_center = [
        model_view[0][0]*center_4d[0] + model_view[0][1]*center_4d[1] + model_view[0][2]*center_4d[2] + model_view[0][3]*center_4d[3],
        model_view[1][0]*center_4d[0] + model_view[1][1]*center_4d[1] + model_view[1][2]*center_4d[2] + model_view[1][3]*center_4d[3],
        model_view[2][0]*center_4d[0] + model_view[2][1]*center_4d[1] + model_view[2][2]*center_4d[2] + model_view[2][3]*center_4d[3],
        model_view[3][0]*center_4d[0] + model_view[3][1]*center_4d[1] + model_view[3][2]*center_4d[2] + model_view[3][3]*center_4d[3]
    ]
    
    xc = view_center[0]
    yc = view_center[1]
    zc = view_center[2]
    
    s = 1.0 / (zc * zc)
    
    # J is 3x3 in the source implementation (last row is 0)
    J = [
        [fx / zc, 0.0, -fx * xc * s],
        [0.0, fy / zc, -fy * yc * s],
        [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    ]

    # 3. Compute Transformation Matrix T
    # Extract 3x3 model-view matrix (rotation/translation part)
    # Source: transpose(mat3x3(modelView[0].xyz, modelView[1].xyz, modelView[2].xyz))
    # This effectively takes the top-left 3x3 of the 4x4 modelView matrix.
    mv3x3 = [
        [model_view[0][0], model_view[0][1], model_view[0][2]],
        [model_view[1][0], model_view[1][1], model_view[1][2]],
        [model_view[2][0], model_view[2][1], model_view[2][2]]
    ]
    
    # T = mv3x3 * J
    T = [
        [mv3x3[0][0]*J[0][0] + mv3x3[0][1]*J[1][0] + mv3x3[0][2]*J[2][0], mv3x3[0][0]*J[0][1] + mv3x3[0][1]*J[1][1] + mv3x3[0][2]*J[2][1], mv3x3[0][0]*J[0][2] + mv3x3[0][1]*J[1][2] + mv3x3[0][2]*J[2][2]],
        [mv3x3[1][0]*J[0][0] + mv3x3[1][1]*J[1][0] + mv3x3[1][2]*J[2][0], mv3x3[1][0]*J[0][1] + mv3x3[1][1]*J[1][1] + mv3x3[1][2]*J[2][1], mv3x3[1][0]*J[0][2] + mv3x3[1][1]*J[1][2] + mv3x3[1][2]*J[2][2]],
        [mv3x3[2][0]*J[0][0] + mv3x3[2][1]*J[1][0] + mv3x3[2][2]*J[2][0], mv3x3[2][0]*J[0][1] + mv3x3[2][1]*J[1][1] + mv3x3[2][2]*J[2][1], mv3x3[2][0]*J[0][2] + mv3x3[2][1]*J[1][2] + mv3x3[2][2]*J[2][2]]
    ]
    
    # 4. Compute Projected 2D Covariance
    # Sigma_2D = T^T @ Sigma_3D @ T
    # We only need the upper triangle of the resulting 2x2 covariance matrix
    # Let A = Sigma_3D @ T
    t0 = T[0]
    t1 = T[1]
    t2 = T[2]
    
    a0 = [
        sigma_xx*t0[0] + sigma_xy*t0[1] + sigma_xz*t0[2],
        sigma_xy*t0[0] + sigma_yy*t0[1] + sigma_yz*t0[2],
        sigma_xz*t0[0] + sigma_yz*t0[1] + sigma_zz*t0[2]
    ]
    a1 = [
        sigma_xx*t1[0] + sigma_xy*t1[1] + sigma_xz*t1[2],
        sigma_xy*t1[0] + sigma_yy*t1[1] + sigma_yz*t1[2],
        sigma_xz*t1[0] + sigma_yz*t1[1] + sigma_zz*t1[2]
    ]
    
    # new_c[0][0] = t0 . a0
    a = t0[0]*a0[0] + t0[1]*a0[1] + t0[2]*a0[2]
    # new_c[0][1] = t0 . a1
    b = t0[0]*a1[0] + t0[1]*a1[1] + t0[2]*a1[2]
    # new_c[1][1] = t1 . a1
    d = t1[0]*a1[0] + t1[1]*a1[1] + t1[2]*a1[2]
    
    # 5. Compute Eigenvalues
    D_det = a * d - b * b
    trace = a + d
    trace_over_2 = 0.5 * trace
    term2 = (trace * trace / 4.0 - D_det) ** 0.5
    
    eigen_value1 = trace_over_2 + term2
    eigen_value2 = max(trace_over_2 - term2, 0.0)
    
    # 6. Compute Eigenvectors
    # v1 = normalize([b, eigen_value1 - a])
    v1_x = b
    v1_y = eigen_value1 - a
    v1_len = (v1_x*v1_x + v1_y*v1_y) ** 0.5
    v1 = [v1_x / v1_len, v1_y / v1_len]
    
    # v2 = [v1.y, -v1.x]
    v2 = [v1[1], -v1[0]]
    
    # 7. Scale Basis Vectors
    max_splat_size = 1024.0
    scale1 = min((eigen_value1 ** 0.5) * 4, max_splat_size)
    scale2 = min((eigen_value2 ** 0.5) * 4, max_splat_size)
    
    basis_vector1 = [v1[0] * scale1, v1[1] * scale1]
    basis_vector2 = [v2[0] * scale2, v2[1] * scale2]
    
    # 8. Compute Distance
    clip_center = [
        projection[0][0]*view_center[0] + projection[0][1]*view_center[1] + projection[0][2]*view_center[2] + projection[0][3]*view_center[3],
        projection[1][0]*view_center[0] + projection[1][1]*view_center[1] + projection[1][2]*view_center[2] + projection[1][3]*view_center[3],
        projection[2][0]*view_center[0] + projection[2][1]*view_center[1] + projection[2][2]*view_center[2] + projection[2][3]*view_center[3],
        projection[3][0]*view_center[0] + projection[3][1]*view_center[1] + projection[3][2]*view_center[2] + projection[3][3]*view_center[3]
    ]
    
    # NDC z
    z_ndc = clip_center[2] / clip_center[3]
    
    # Map to integer range [0, 2^24)
    distance_val = int(((z_ndc + 1.0) * 0.5) * 16777215.0)
    
    return (basis_vector1, basis_vector2), distance_val
```

## Subtopics

### Screen-Aligned Quad Construction
This variant describes how the computed basis vectors are used in the vertex shader to render the Gaussian [S1, unofficial]. It constructs a screen-aligned quad for each splat [S1, unofficial]. The centroid is transformed to NDC space [S1, unofficial]. The local quad coordinates (`inPos`, typically `[-1, -1]` to `[1, 1]`) are scaled by the basis vectors [S1, unofficial]. A `basisViewport` scaling factor (inverse of half-width/half-height) is applied to convert from pixel units (used in the Jacobian) to NDC units [S1, unofficial]. The final clip position is `ndcCenter.xy + ndcOffset` [S1, unofficial]. This ensures the Gaussian is rendered as a textured quad oriented according to the projected covariance [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per splat for the projection calculation [S1, unofficial]. The dominant cost in the pipeline is typically the subsequent sorting step ($O(N \log N)$ or $O(N)$ with radix sort) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the basis vectors and distances [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently linearizes the perspective projection of Gaussians, allowing for correct 2D ellipse approximation [S1, unofficial]. The use of integer distances enables fast GPU-based radix sorting [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The linearization assumes the Gaussian is small relative to the distance to the camera [S1, unofficial]. For very large or very close Gaussians, the approximation may introduce artifacts [S1, unofficial]. The fixed scaling factor of 4 covers ~95% of the mass; tails are truncated [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Standard Gaussian Splatting rendering pipelines [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** If higher-order projection effects are required (e.g., for extremely wide-angle lenses or very large splats), a full non-linear rasterization or higher-order approximation might be needed [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method is derived from the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper [Kerbl et al., 2023] and its implementation in WebGPU tutorials [S1, unofficial]. It adapts the 3D Gaussian representation for efficient rasterization by projecting the 3D covariance into 2D screen space [S1, unofficial]. The specific implementation details, including the Jacobian calculation and integer distance sorting, are documented in the WebGPU Unleashed tutorial [S1, unofficial].
