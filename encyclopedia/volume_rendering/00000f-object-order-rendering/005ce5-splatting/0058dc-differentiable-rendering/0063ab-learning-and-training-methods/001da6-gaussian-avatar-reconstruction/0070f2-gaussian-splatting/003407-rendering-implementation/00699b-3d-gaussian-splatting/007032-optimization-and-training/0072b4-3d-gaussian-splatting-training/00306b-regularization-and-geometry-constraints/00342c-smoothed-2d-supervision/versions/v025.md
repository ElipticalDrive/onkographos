# Smoothed 2D Supervision

Also called: Adaptive Gaussian blur strategy, Progressive Gaussian Blurring

## Overview
Smoothed 2D Supervision is a regularization technique that enhances the robustness of joint optimization (e.g., camera pose and scene reconstruction) by smoothing the 2D supervision signal [S1][S2, unofficial]. It addresses the instability caused by high-frequency noise and weak gradient signals in raw pixel supervision [S2, unofficial]. Unlike [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) which modifies the positional encoding or loss weights based on frequency analysis, Smoothed 2D Supervision explicitly blurs the ground-truth training images with scheduled Gaussian kernels [S2, unofficial][S3]. This produces smoothed image gradients that stabilize camera alignment and restrict the spectrum of the learned 3D scene [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Ground-truth 2D training images $I_{gt}$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
*   Current iteration $t$ [S4].
*   Hyperparameters: initial kernel size $K_{init}$, initial standard deviation $\sigma_{init}$, decay interval $k_b$, and final iteration $T_{end}$ [S4].

**Outputs:**
*   Smoothed supervision image $I_{smooth}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   The supervision signal is a 2D tensor of the same resolution as the input image [S2, unofficial].
*   The blur kernel is a 2D Gaussian kernel $K_{2D}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Initial kernel size: $9 \times 9$ [S4].
*   Initial standard deviation: $\sigma = 2.4$ [S4].
*   Decay interval: $k_b = 100$ iterations [S4].
*   Final iteration for blurring: $T_{end} = 19500$ [S4].
*   Note: Some sources mention a schedule where $\sigma$ is exponentially reduced to 0 at 10k iterations [S2, unofficial], while others specify the 19.5k schedule [S4]. The 19.5k schedule is used in Trick-GS [S4].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize Kernel:** Define a 2D Gaussian kernel $K_{2D}(x, y)$ with standard deviation $\sigma_t$ and size $S_t \times S_t$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
    $$ K_{2D}(x, y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_t^2} \exp\left(-\frac{x^2 + y^2}{2\sigma_t^2}\right) $$
    where $x, y \in [-\frac{S_t-1}{2}, \frac{S_t-1}{2}]$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Schedule Update:** At every $k_b$ iterations, reduce the blur intensity [S4].
    *   In the Trick-GS variant, the kernel size and $\sigma$ are reduced gradually until iteration 19500 [S4].
    *   If downsampling is active, the kernel size is lowered by the scale of downsampling at the current stage to account for the initial hyperparameter choice based on original image resolutions [S4].
3.  **Convolution:** Apply the 2D Gaussian kernel to the ground-truth image $I_{gt}$ to produce the smoothed supervision image $I_{smooth}$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
    $$ I_{smooth} = I_{gt} * K_{2D} $$
    where $*$ denotes 2D convolution [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the photometric loss (e.g., L1 or L2) between the rendered image $I_{render}$ and the smoothed supervision image $I_{smooth}$ [S2, unofficial][S3].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{smooth} = \| I_{render} - I_{smooth} \|_1 $$
5.  **Decay:** As $t$ increases, $\sigma_t$ and $S_t$ decrease, eventually approaching 0 (or a minimal value) after $T_{end}$, transitioning to raw supervision [S2, unofficial][S4].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Weak Gradients:** Using a large kernel too early can lead to weak gradients because the magnitude of the modulated gradient decreases as the kernel size approaches 0, making the optimization easily influenced by noise [S2, unofficial].
*   **Handcrafted Nature:** The schedule is handcrafted and may not fully align the frequency relationship between positional encodings and pixel supervision compared to adaptive methods [S3].
*   **Downsampling Interaction:** If image downsampling is used, the kernel size must be adjusted by the downsampling scale; otherwise, the blur effect is incorrect relative to the rendered resolution [S4].

## Subtopics

### [Randomly Scaled Kernel Parameter](7875-randomly-scaled-kernel-parameter.md)
Randomly Scaled Kernel Parameter is a regularization technique used during joint optimization to mitigate weak and noisy gradient signals. It addresses the insufficiency of using a single-size kernel for diverse real-world scene structures, where a fixed kernel may be overly aggressive in some contexts and overly gentle in others. By randomly scaling the kernel size, it allows combinations of different-sized kernels to guide the optimization, resulting in a more robust training schedule.

### [Multiscale Pre-convolved Representation](10911-multiscale-pre-convolved-representation.md)
A regularization technique for neural illumination fields that stabilizes the decomposition of object materials from environmental illumination by enforcing consistency with pre-computed, roughness-adapted background reflections. It addresses the ambiguity where diffuse object color is incorrectly absorbed into the estimated environment map, a problem inherent to high-dimensional neural illumination representations. Unlike methods that ignore the background or treat it as static, this method explicitly models the relationship between rough refractive surfaces and the convolved background, allowing for realistic specular reflections even when roughness exceeds the kernel scale. The technique builds on Smoothed 2D Supervision by applying discrete Gaussian blur kernels to the background to stabilize convergence.

### [Soft Rasterization](13687-soft-rasterization.md)
Soft rasterization is a differentiable rendering technique that mitigates the discontinuities inherent in standard rasterization by blending facet contributions for pixels near primitive edges. It solves the problem of sparse and vanishing gradients caused by the non-uniformity of bilinear interpolation operands, which typically have similar values except at object boundaries. Unlike standard hard rasterization, it yields slightly distorted images but allows backpropagating more useful gradients, thereby stabilizing optimization and preventing gradient vanishing at edges.

### Platonic GAN (pGAN)
A learning-based method for 2D supervision per class of reconstructed objects, used here to pre-train a network for per-frame supervision of rising smoke [S5]. It is applied to improve depth artifacts in single-view evaluations but may lead to temporal incoherence if not constrained by temporal self-supervision [S5].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** Achieves 19.72 PSNR on DTU 3-view, improving over the Baseline (19.54) [S3].
*   **Complexity:** Adds computational cost due to the convolution of training images with Gaussian kernels [S2, unofficial]. However, it can reduce the total number of training iterations required (e.g., 50k vs 200k in some MLP-based methods) by stabilizing convergence [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes camera alignment and restricts the spectrum of the learned 3D scene [S2, unofficial]. Experimentally proven crucial for successful pose refinement [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The strategy is handcrafted and cannot fully align frequency relationships compared to adaptive methods [S3]. It may introduce blurring artifacts if the kernel schedule is not properly decayed [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When joint optimization of camera poses and scene geometry is unstable due to high-frequency noise or weak gradients [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
Smoothed 2D Supervision was proposed to improve the robustness of joint optimization in radiance field reconstruction [S1][S2, unofficial]. It was introduced in the context of decomposed low-rank tensorial radiance fields and later adopted in Gaussian Splatting variants like Trick-GS [S1, S4]. The technique is inspired by the analysis of frequency modulation in radiance fields, where blurring the 2D training images helps to stabilize the optimization process [S2, unofficial].
