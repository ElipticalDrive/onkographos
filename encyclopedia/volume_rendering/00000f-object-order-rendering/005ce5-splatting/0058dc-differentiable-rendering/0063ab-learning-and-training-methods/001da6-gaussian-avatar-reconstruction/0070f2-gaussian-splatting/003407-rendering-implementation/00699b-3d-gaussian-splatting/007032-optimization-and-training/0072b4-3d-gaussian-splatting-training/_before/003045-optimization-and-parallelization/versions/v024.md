# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Per-Splat Backprop, Mask Gradient Optimization

## Overview
Optimization and Parallelization encompasses the computational strategies required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) efficiently at scale. It addresses the high memory and compute costs of differentiable rasterization by introducing distributed rendering across multiple GPUs , restructuring the backward pass for per-splat parallelization , and computing specialized gradients for adaptive representation parameters like probabilistic masks . It distinguishes itself from standard optimization by focusing on the infrastructure and algorithmic variants that enable training on large-scale scenes or accelerate convergence, rather than the core loss minimization mechanics .

## Shared principles
The optimization and parallelization methods share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer defined in [Optimization and Training Efficiency](9264-optimization-and-training-efficiency.md). The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ .
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices .
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants (e.g., MaskGaussian, Grendel-GS) modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow .
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation .

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
Computes gradients for per-Gaussian probabilistic masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ that modulate opacity. It explicitly accounts for the interaction between mask-modulated Gaussian contributions and the background color, enabling adaptive pruning and representation.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that integrates dynamic Gaussian management (pruning, densification, cloning) within the training process. It solves the inverse problem by projecting Gaussians onto 2D slice views and adapting density to scene complexity.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
Shifts the computational granularity of the backward pass from per-pixel to per-splat (per-Gaussian). This restructuring provides faster, numerically equivalent gradient computation and attribute updates.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the global set of Gaussian primitives across multiple GPUs. It overcomes single-device memory limits by allowing each GPU to render its local subset and combining results via inter-GPU communication.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Provides concrete hyperparameters, optimization schedules, and low-level kernel optimizations (e.g., half-precision rasterization, GEMM-compatible blending) necessary for a working implementation. It defines the precise mechanics of densification, pruning, and gradient propagation.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 . The initial implementation focused on real-time rendering and basic optimization using Adam . Subsequent research addressed scalability and efficiency, leading to distributed frameworks like Grendel-GS , specialized gradient computations for mask-based methods , and hardware-aware optimizations such as GEMM-compatible blending  and half-precision rasterization . The training pipeline has evolved to include dynamic densification and pruning schedules to manage Gaussian density .
