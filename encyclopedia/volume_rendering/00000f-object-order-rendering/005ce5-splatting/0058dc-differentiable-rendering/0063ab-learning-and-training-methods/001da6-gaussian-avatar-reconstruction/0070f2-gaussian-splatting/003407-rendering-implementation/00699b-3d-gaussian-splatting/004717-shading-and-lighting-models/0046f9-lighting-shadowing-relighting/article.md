# Lighting, Shadowing & Relighting

Also called: Deferred Surfel Rendering, Split-Sum IBL

## Overview
This family extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to support physically based lighting, self-occlusion, and material-aware relighting. It solves the limitation of standard splatting, which renders only baked view-dependent colors, by introducing material attributes (normals, roughness, albedo) and computing lighting via deferred shading or dedicated light-space rasterization passes [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md][4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md][4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md]. It is distinguished from [Analytic-Splatting Forward Shading](3459-analytic-splatting-forward-shading.md) by its focus on physical material properties and deferred pipelines rather than analytic signal response, and from [Transparency and Blending Models](9274-transparency-and-blending-models.md) by its explicit handling of light transport and occlusion transmittance.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on 3D Gaussian primitives defined by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ (or scales and orientation), opacity $o$, and color $\mathbf{c}$ (or spherical harmonic coefficients) [G]. The core challenge is computing the pixel color $C$ and accumulated alpha $\alpha$ given a camera ray [G]. Standard splatting projects 3D Gaussians to 2D ellipses, computes a 2D alpha mask, and blends them in screen space [3D Gaussian Splatting Rasterization](2812-3d-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md). These models modify the blending or shading stage: some refine the depth metric used for sorting, others integrate the 3D density function directly along the ray, and others use weighted averages to bypass sorting [Transparency and Blending Models](9274-transparency-and-blending-models.md). All assume the Gaussians are sorted or processed in a manner that approximates front-to-back accumulation to ensure correct occlusion [G].

For relighting, splats must carry physical material attributes: normal, roughness, reflectance (specular strength), and albedo (diffuse color) [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md]. These are stored in a parallel Material Buffer aligned with the Splat Buffer [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md].

For self-shadowing, a virtual "sun camera" is constructed to simulate a directional light source [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md]. This camera is placed at a large distance with a narrow field of view (FOV) to minimize parallax error to below 2% [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md].

Both relighting (for cache locality) and shadowing (for correct front-to-back accumulation) require sorting [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md][4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md]. Relighting uses spatial locality algorithms like Morton codes [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md]. Shadowing sorts Gaussians by depth relative to the sun camera [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md].

During the light pass for shadowing, the projection covariance is not expanded by $0.3I$ and no amplitude compensation is applied, preserving the main camera pass's sampling expansion [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md]. In shadowing calculations, sun camera framing, sorting, coverage, and threshold branches are treated as constants to prevent incorrect gradient propagation [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md].

## Subtopics

### [Ref-Gaussian Deferred Surfel Rendering with Split-Sum IBL](4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md)
A physically based rendering (PBR) method for novel view synthesis and relighting of reflective objects using 2D Gaussian primitives. It solves the problem of blurred reflective details and inaccurate material representation in standard Gaussian Splatting by decomposing splats into PBR material attributes (albedo, normal, roughness, reflectance) and computing lighting via a deferred shading pipeline with split-sum environment map integration. It is distinguished from its sibling [3D Gaussian Splatting Self-Shadowing (T_light)](4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md) by its focus on material-aware deferred shading and split-sum IBL rather than light-space rasterization for self-shadowing, and from [Light Space Rasterization for T_light](4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md) by its use of a deferred surfel buffer for cache-locality sorting rather than a dedicated light-space depth buffer. Choose this when high-fidelity reflections and material-aware relighting are required.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Self-Shadowing (T_light)](4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md)
Calculates the self-shadowing transmittance $T_{light}$ for each Gaussian along a directional sun direction, representing the 'front occlusion transmittance' to model self-shadowing effects in volume rendering. It differs from standard rasterization by performing a dedicated light-space pass that accumulates front-to-back occlusion without modifying the main camera's sampling expansion or applying amplitude compensation. Builds on [Shading and Lighting Models](11924-shading-and-lighting-models.md) and contrasts with [Ref-Gaussian Deferred Surfel Rendering with Split-Sum IBL](4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md) and [Light Space Rasterization for T_light](4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md) by explicitly tracking front occlusion transmittance per Gaussian footprint rather than deferred surface buffers or alternative light-space projections. Choose this for efficient self-shadowing integration into existing training pipelines.

### [Light Space Rasterization for T_light](4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md)
Light Space Rasterization computes $T_{light}$, the front-facing occlusion transmittance for each Gaussian along a directional sun direction, enabling physically based self-shadowing in 3D Gaussian Splatting. It differs from voxel-based fallbacks by providing full continuous gradients and zero EWA expansion by default, avoiding the inversion artifacts associated with early-termination in deep occlusion. Choose this when continuous gradients and artifact-free deep occlusion handling are prioritized over voxel-based approximations.

## Background
Originating from extensions to 3D Gaussian Splatting for novel view synthesis, these methods address the lack of physical material representation and self-occlusion in the original framework. Ref-Gaussian introduced deferred surfel rendering to handle reflections [4464-ref-gaussian-deferred-surfel-rendering-with-split-sum-ibl.md], while T_light methods were developed to compute self-shadowing transmittance for directional lighting [4487-3d-gaussian-splatting-self-shadowing-t-light.md][4489-light-space-rasterization-for-t-light.md]. These techniques are used in high-fidelity 3D reconstruction, virtual reality, and relighting applications where baked lighting is insufficient.
