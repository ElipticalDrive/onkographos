# Cross-scale geometric adaptation

Also called: Lgeo, Reprojection error supervision, Reprojection-based cross-scale geometric adaptation

## Overview
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization method that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without complex scheduling or explicit depth ground truth [S1]. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity by leveraging low-frequency components from low-resolution voxels for coarse geometry early in training, while medium- and high-resolution voxels refine details as training advances [S1]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md) by avoiding scale mismatches inherent in single-scale depth priors, providing a stronger and more robust constraint that significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Training views $i$ and $j$ (where $j$ is a novel or different training view), camera intrinsics $K_i, K_j$, camera poses $(R_i, t_i), (R_j, t_j)$, multi-scale depth maps $D_{il}(p_i)$ obtained from volume rendering at scale $l$, and RGB maps $C_i, C_j$.
- **Outputs:** Geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ and pseudo-ground truth depth $D'(r_{train})$ for each training ray $r_{train}$.

**Data Structures:**
- Multi-scale voxel grid: Stores depth values $D_{il}(p_i)$ for each scale $l \in \{0, \dots, L\}$, where $l=0$ is low resolution and $l=L$ is high resolution.
- Camera parameters: $K$ (3x3 intrinsic matrix), $R$ (3x3 rotation matrix), $t$ (3x1 translation vector).

**Numbered Steps:**
1. For each training ray $r_{train}$ originating from pixel $p_i$ in frame $i$:
2. Compute the homogeneous pixel coordinate $\tilde{p}_i = [p_i.x, p_i.y, 1]^T$.
3. For each scale $l$:
    a. Retrieve the rendered depth $D_{il}(p_i)$ at scale $l$.
    b. Compute the 3D point in camera coordinates of frame $i$:
       $$x_{li} = D_{il}(p_i) K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i$$
       [G] Note: $K_i^{-1} \tilde{p}_i$ yields the normalized ray direction. Multiplying by depth gives the 3D point in camera space.
    c. Transform $x_{li}$ to world coordinates:
       $$x_l = R_i x_{li} + t_i$$
    d. Transform $x_l$ to camera coordinates of frame $j$:
       $$x_{li \to j} = R_j^T (x_l - t_j)$$
    e. Project $x_{li \to j}$ back to 2D pixel coordinates in frame $j$:
       $$\tilde{p}_{li \to j} = \pi(K_j x_{li \to j})$$
       where $\pi([x, y, z]^T) = [x/z, y/z]^T$.
    f. Compute the reprojection error for scale $l$:
       $$e_l(p_i) = C_i(p_i) - C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$$
       [G] $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$ requires bilinear interpolation if $\tilde{p}_{li \to j}$ is not integer.
4. Select the scale $l^*$ with the minimum reprojection error:
   $$D'(r_{train}) = \arg \min_l (e_l(r_{train}))$$
   [G] In practice, this selects the depth value $D_{il^*}(p_i)$ corresponding to the best scale.
5. Compute the geometric adaptation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered depth at the selected scale and the pseudo-ground truth depth $D'$:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{geo}(r_{train}) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \left( D_{il}(r_{train}) - D'(r_{train}) \right)^2$$
   [G] The summation implies a soft selection or weighting, but the text states $D'$ is the depth of the scale with *minimum* error. The formula (19) in [S1] sums over all $l$, suggesting the loss penalizes deviation from the *best* pseudo-GT across all scales, or effectively acts as a soft-argmin if implemented via differentiable selection. However, the text explicitly says "pseudo-GT depth of the scale with the minimum reprojection error is obtained". The formula (19) is written as a sum over $l$. This implies the loss is computed for all scales against the single best pseudo-GT, or the pseudo-GT is defined per scale. Given the text "selecting render depths with minimal reprojection error... as pseudo labels", it is most likely that $D'$ is a single value derived from the best scale, and the loss is computed against that. However, formula (19) sums over $l$. A strict reading of (19) is: $\mathcal{L}_{geo} = \sum_l (D_l - D')^2$. If $D'$ is fixed to the best scale's depth, this penalizes other scales. This encourages consistency.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_cross_scale_geometric_adaptation_loss(
    rays, 
    camera_intrinsics, 
    camera_poses, 
    voxel_depths, 
    rgb_images
):
    """
    rays: List of rays, each with pixel coordinate p_i and frame index i.
    camera_intrinsics: Dict mapping frame index to K matrix.
    camera_poses: Dict mapping frame index to (R, t).
    voxel_depths: Dict mapping (frame_index, scale) to depth_map.
    rgb_images: Dict mapping frame index to RGB image.
    scales: List of scale indices [0, ..., L].
    """
    L_geo_total = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        p_i = ray.pixel_coordinate
        i = ray.frame_index
        K_i = camera_intrinsics[i]
        R_i, t_i = camera_poses[i]
        
        # Homogeneous pixel coordinate
        p_i_hom = np.array([p_i.x, p_i.y, 1.0])
        
        best_error = float('inf')
        best_scale = 0
        best_depth = 0.0
        
        # Step 1-3: Find scale with minimal reprojection error
        for l in scales:
            # Get depth at scale l
            D_il = voxel_depths[(i, l)][p_i.y, p_i.x] # Assuming 2D indexing
            
            # 3D point in camera coordinates of frame i
            # x_li = D_il * K_i^-1 * p_i_hom
            ray_dir_cam = np.linalg.inv(K_i) @ p_i_hom
            x_li = D_il * ray_dir_cam
            
            # Transform to world coordinates
            x_l = R_i @ x_li + t_i
            
            # Select a reference frame j (e.g., next training view or random other view)
            # For implementation, j must be chosen. Let's assume j is provided or iterated.
            # Here we assume a single j for simplicity, or average over j.
            # The source uses "frame j". Let's assume j is fixed for this ray or averaged.
            # We will compute for one j.
            j = ray.other_frame_index 
            K_j = camera_intrinsics[j]
            R_j, t_j = camera_poses[j]
            
            # Transform to camera coordinates of frame j
            x_li_to_j = R_j.T @ (x_l - t_j)
            
            # Project to 2D in frame j
            # p_li_to_j = pi(K_j * x_li_to_j)
            p_li_to_j_hom = K_j @ x_li_to_j
            p_li_to_j = np.array([p_li_to_j_hom[0]/p_li_to_j_hom[2], 
                                  p_li_to_j_hom[1]/p_li_to_j_hom[2]])
            
            # Get RGB color at projected coordinate in frame j
            # C_j(p_li_to_j) requires interpolation
            C_j_val = interpolate_rgb(rgb_images[j], p_li_to_j)
            
            # Reprojection error
            C_i_val = rgb_images[i][p_i.y, p_i.x]
            e_l = np.linalg.norm(C_i_val - C_j_val)
            
            if e_l < best_error:
                best_error = e_l
                best_scale = l
                best_depth = D_il
        
        # Step 4: Pseudo-GT depth is best_depth
        D_prime = best_depth
        
        # Step 5: Compute Loss
        # L_geo = sum_l (D_il - D_prime)^2
        l_geo_ray = 0.0
        for l in scales:
            D_il = voxel_depths[(i, l)][p_i.y, p_i.x]
            l_geo_ray += (D_il - D_prime)**2
            
        L_geo_total += l_geo_ray
        
    return L_geo_total
```

**Parameters:**
- **Scales:** Multiple resolutions (e.g., Low, Mid, High). Typical values not given, but Figure 8 shows Low, Mid, High.
- **Weighting $\lambda_{reg}$:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Interpolation of $C_j(\tilde{p}_{li \to j})$ must be accurate to avoid noise in reprojection error.
- **Boundaries:** Rays hitting outside the image bounds of frame $j$ must be handled (e.g., ignored or padded).
- **Degenerate Input:** If camera poses are inaccurate, reprojection errors will be high for all scales, leading to incorrect pseudo-GT. The method relies on accurate camera poses [S1].
- **Scale Mismatch:** Without this method, single-scale depth priors suffer from scale mismatches [S1].

## Subtopics

### Low-Resolution Guidance
Uses low-resolution voxels to guide coarse geometry early in training. Less prone to floaters.

### High-Resolution Refinement
Uses high-resolution voxels to refine details as training advances.

### Comparison
Significantly reduces floaters and improves convergence quality compared to training without geometric adaptation. Provides a stronger constraint than depth prior $L_d$, achieving lower final depth error [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Increases training time due to multi-scale reprojection calculations for each ray and each scale. However, it eliminates the need for complex scheduling [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing multi-scale voxel depths.
- **Measured Performance:** On few-shot datasets, Lgeo alone achieves lower final depth error than Ld (depth prior) [S1]. Combining Lgeo and Ld yields the best results [S1].
- **Strengths:** Self-adaptive, no complex scheduling, reduces floaters, robust to scale mismatches.
- **Limitations:** Relies on accurate camera poses. Performance declines without geometric adaptation loss [S1].

## Background
Originated in FrugalNeRF [S1], a framework for extreme few-shot novel view synthesis. It was proposed to accelerate and regularize training without external priors. The method addresses the issue of floaters and sub-optimal convergence in multi-scale voxel-based NeRFs by providing a self-adaptive geometric constraint.
