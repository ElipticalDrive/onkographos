# Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting

Also called: GeoGaussian

## Overview
Geometry-aware Gaussian Splatting (GeoGaussian) is a method that initializes thin Gaussians aligned with surfaces in smoothly connected areas and uses constrained optimization to preserve scene geometry and texture, particularly in low-textured regions [S1]. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by explicitly encoding geometric normals into the Gaussian parameterization for smooth surfaces, ensuring that densification (splitting and cloning) occurs within the tangent plane of the surface rather than randomly in 3D space [S1]. This approach enhances rendering fidelity in structured regions where traditional methods often suffer from geometric artifacts or texture degradation [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Input point cloud $P$ (typically generated by SLAM or Structure-fromMotion methods) [S1].
*   Camera poses $T_{cw}$ for training views [S1].
*   Training images $I$ [S1].
*   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_1=0.8$ (photometric weight), $\lambda_2=0.3$ (geometric weight), $\beta_1=0.05$ (smoothness position weight), $\beta_2=0.01$ (smoothness normal weight), $\gamma=0.0002$ (gradient threshold), learning rate $0.0002$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$, including both standard spherical Gaussians ($G_{ind}$) and thin ellipsoidal Gaussians ($G_{\theta}$), optimized to render novel views [S1].

### Data Structures

**Standard Gaussian ($G_{ind}$):**
*   Position: $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   Scale: $S \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (diagonal scaling matrix $S_w = \text{Diag}[s_1, s_2, s_3]$) [S1].
*   Rotation: $R \in SO(3)$ [S1].
*   Opacity: $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
*   Spherical Harmonics: $C \in \mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times 3}$ [S1].

**Thin Gaussian ($G_{\theta}$):**
*   Position: $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   Scale: $S = [s_1, s_2, 0.001]^T$. The third value $s_3$ is fixed to $0.001$ to enforce thinness [S1].
*   Rotation: $R = [r_1, r_2, n]$. The third column $r_3$ (denoted as $n$) is the surface normal vector, decoupled from the covariance optimization [S1]. The first two columns $r_1, r_2$ are normalized basis vectors perpendicular to $n$ [G].
*   Opacity: $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
*   Spherical Harmonics: $C \in \mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times 3}$ [S1].

**Neighbor List:**
*   For each thin Gaussian $G_{\theta}$, a list of $K=8$ nearest neighbors is maintained to compute geometric constraints [S1]. This list is updated periodically [S1].

### Initialization

1.  **Normal Extraction:** Extract surface normals for all points in the input point cloud using a standard normal estimation method [S1].
2.  **Point Classification:** Classify points into two groups:
    *   $S_{ind}$ (Individual points): Points with unreliable normals. A normal is unreliable if:
        *   The point has a long distance from its neighbors [S1].
        *   Neighbors within a distance threshold have normals with large angle differences relative to the point's normal [S1].
    *   $S_{co}$ (Smooth surfaces): Points with reliable normals, forming smoothly connected areas [S1].
3.  **Gaussian Creation:**
    *   For $p \in S_{ind}$: Initialize a standard Gaussian. Set scale $S$ based on the distance to the nearest neighbor, similar to standard 3DGS [S1].
    *   For $p \in S_{co}$: Initialize a thin Gaussian $G_{\theta}$ [S1].
        *   Set $r_3 = n$ (the extracted normal) [S1].
        *   Compute $r_1, r_2$ as orthonormal basis vectors perpendicular to $r_3$ [G].
        *   Set $S = [s_1, s_2, 0.001]^T$ [S1]. The values $s_1, s_2$ are initialized based on local point density (distance to nearest neighbor) [G].

### Densification

Densification occurs via splitting and cloning [S1]. The strategy depends on the Gaussian type [S1].

**1. Standard Gaussian Densification ($G_{ind}$):**
*   **Split:** A large Gaussian is split into two smaller Gaussians. The new positions are chosen randomly inside the original ellipsoid [S1].

**2. Thin Gaussian Densification ($G_{\theta}$):**
*   **Cloning (Tangential Cloning):**
    *   Calculate the accumulated gradient of the position $\mu$ over the last 10 iterations, denoted $\delta \mu_i$ [S1].
    *   If $\|\delta \mu_i\| > \gamma$, clone the Gaussian [S1].
    *   The new position $\mu_{i+1}$ is calculated by projecting the gradient step onto the tangent plane defined by the normal $r_3$:
        $$ \mu_{i+1} = \mu_i + \delta \mu_i - (\mathbf{r}_3^\top \delta \mu_i) \mathbf{r}_3 $$
        *(Note: The source notation $\mu_{i+1} = \mu_i + \delta \mu_i - \mathbf{r}_3^\top \delta \mu_i$ [S1] is dimensionally inconsistent for vectors; the corrected vector projection is implemented here to decouple the component perpendicular to the normal as described in the text [S1].)*
    *   Clone parameters $r_3, S, C, \alpha$ to the new Gaussian [S1].
*   **Splitting (Tangential Splitting):**
    *   When splitting a thin Gaussian, the new position $\mu_{i+1}$ must lie on the plane established by the original position $\mu_i$ and normal $r_3$ [S1].
    *   The condition is:
        $$ \mu_{i+1}^\top \mathbf{r}_3 = \mu_i^\top \mathbf{r}_3 $$
        And the new Gaussian $G_{i+1}$ must remain in $S_{co}$ [S1].

### Optimization

The optimization minimizes a combined loss function [S1].

**1. Photometric Loss:**
Rendered color $c_i$ is compared to ground truth $\bar{c}_i$:
$$ E_{pho}^i = \| c_i - \bar{c}_i \| = \| f(\mathcal{G}, T_{cw}) - \bar{c}_i \| $$
where $f$ rasterizes and blends all Gaussians (standard and thin) [S1].

**2. Geometric Smoothness Loss:**
For view-dependent thin Gaussians $G_{\theta}$, compute the loss based on $K=8$ nearest neighbors $m$. Outliers with large normal angle differences are filtered out [S1].
$$ E_{geo} = \beta_1 \left\| \mathbf{r}_3^\top \mu - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j \in m} \mathbf{r}_3^{j\top} \mu^j \right\| + \beta_2 \| \mathbf{r}_3^j - \mathbf{r}_3 \|_2 $$
*   $\mathbf{r}_3^\top \mu$: Distance from world origin to the plane of $G_{\theta}$ [S1].
*   $\frac{1}{m} \sum \mathbf{r}_3^{j\top} \mu^j$: Average point-plane distance of the neighborhood [S1].
*   The second term encourages normal alignment with neighbors [S1].

**3. Total Loss:**
$$ L = \lambda_1 \sum_{\forall k \in H \times W} E_{pho}^k + \lambda_2 \sum_{\mathcal{G}_\theta^i} E_{geo}^i $$
where $\lambda_1=0.8, \lambda_2=0.3$ [S1].

### Optimization Schedule

1.  **Iterations 0–2,000:** Only photometric constraints ($E_{pho}$) are used. No geometric loss [S1].
2.  **Iterations 2,000–10,000:** Both photometric and geometric losses are used. Densification (split/clone) is active [S1].
3.  **Iterations 10,000–30,000:** Densification ceases. Optimization continues with both losses [S1].
4.  **Pruning:** Gaussians with opacity $\alpha < 0.05$ are removed throughout training [S1].
5.  **Neighbor Update:**
    *   Iterations 0–20,000: Update nearest neighbor list every 100 iterations [S1].
    *   Iterations >20,000: Update every 1,000 iterations [S1].
6.  **Total Iterations:** 30,000 [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np

class Gaussian:
    def __init__(self, pos, scale, rot, sh, alpha, g_type, normal=None, grad_acc=None):
        self.pos = np.array(pos, dtype=np.float32)
        self.scale = np.array(scale, dtype=np.float32)
        self.rot = np.array(rot, dtype=np.float32)
        self.sh = np.array(sh, dtype=np.float32)
        self.alpha = float(alpha)
        self.g_type = g_type  # 'ind' or 'theta'
        self.normal = np.array(normal, dtype=np.float32) if normal is not None else None
        self.grad_acc = np.array(grad_acc, dtype=np.float32) if grad_acc is not None else np.zeros(3)
        self.neighbors = []

def initialize_geogaussian(point_cloud, normals):
    gaussians = []
    S_ind, S_co = [], []
    for i, p in enumerate(point_cloud):
        n = normals[i]
        if is_normal_reliable(p, n, point_cloud, normals):
            S_co.append(i)
        else:
            S_ind.append(i)
    for idx in S_ind:
        p = point_cloud[idx]
        dist = distance_to_nearest_neighbor(p, point_cloud)
        scale = np.array([dist, dist, dist], dtype=np.float32)
        rot = np.eye(3)
        sh = np.zeros((4, 3), dtype=np.float32)
        gaussians.append(Gaussian(p, scale, rot, sh, 1.0, 'ind'))
    for idx in S_co:
        p = point_cloud[idx]
        n = normals[idx]
        dist = distance_to_nearest_neighbor(p, point_cloud)
        scale = np.array([dist, dist, 0.001], dtype=np.float32)
        r3 = n / np.linalg.norm(n)
        r1, r2 = compute_orthonormal_basis(r3)
        rot = np.column_stack((r1, r2, r3))
        sh = np.zeros((4, 3), dtype=np.float32)
        gaussians.append(Gaussian(p, scale, rot, sh, 1.0, 'theta', normal=r3))
    return gaussians

def is_normal_reliable(point, normal, point_cloud, normals, dist_thresh=0.05, angle_thresh=np.pi/4):
    neighbors = get_k_nearest_neighbors(point, point_cloud, k=10)
    if len(neighbors) == 0:
        return False
    for n_idx, n_point in neighbors:
        n_normal = normals[n_idx]
        angle = np.arccos(np.clip(np.dot(normal, n_normal), -1.0, 1.0))
        if angle > angle_thresh:
            return False
    return True

def compute_orthonormal_basis(r3):
    if abs(r3[0]) < 0.9:
        arbitrary = np.array([1, 0, 0], dtype=np.float32)
    else:
        arbitrary = np.array([0, 1, 0], dtype=np.float32)
    r1 = np.cross(arbitrary, r3)
    r1 = r1 / np.linalg.norm(r1)
    r2 = np.cross(r3, r1)
    return r1, r2

def densify_geogaussian(gaussians, gradients, iteration, gamma=0.0002):
    new_gaussians = []
    active_gaussians = []
    for g in gaussians:
        g.grad_acc += gradients.get(g, np.zeros(3))
        if g.g_type == 'ind':
            if is_large(g):
                g1, g2 = split_standard(g)
                new_gaussians.extend([g1, g2])
            else:
                active_gaussians.append(g)
        elif g.g_type == 'theta':
            if np.linalg.norm(g.grad_acc) > gamma:
                delta_mu = g.grad_acc
                proj_on_normal = np.dot(g.normal, delta_mu)
                normal_component = proj_on_normal * g.normal
                tangent_step = delta_mu - normal_component
                mu_new = g.pos + tangent_step
                g_clone = Gaussian(mu_new, g.scale.copy(), g.rot.copy(), g.sh.copy(), g.alpha, 'theta', normal=g.normal)
                new_gaussians.append(g_clone)
            if is_large(g):
                mu_new = split_on_tangent_plane(g)
                g_split = Gaussian(mu_new, g.scale.copy() / 1.6, g.rot.copy(), g.sh.copy(), g.alpha / 2, 'theta', normal=g.normal)
                new_gaussians.append(g_split)
            else:
                active_gaussians.append(g)
    return active_gaussians + new_gaussians

def split_on_tangent_plane(g):
    offset = np.random.randn(2)
    tangent_offset = offset[0] * g.rot[:, 0] + offset[1] * g.rot[:, 1]
    scale_factor = np.random.uniform(0, 1) * np.max(g.scale[:2])
    tangent_offset *= scale_factor
    return g.pos + tangent_offset

def compute_loss(gaussians, images, camera_poses, neighbor_lists, iteration):
    photometric_loss = 0.0
    geometric_loss = 0.0
    for img, pose in zip(images, camera_poses):
        rendered = render(gaussians, pose)
        photometric_loss += np.mean((rendered - img) ** 2)
    if iteration > 2000:
        for g in gaussians:
            if g.g_type == 'theta':
                neighbors = neighbor_lists.get(g, [])
                valid_neighbors = [n for n in neighbors if np.arccos(np.clip(np.dot(g.normal, n.normal), -1.0, 1.0)) < np.pi/4]
                if len(valid_neighbors) > 0:
                    m = len(valid_neighbors)
                    dist_self = np.dot(g.normal, g.pos)
                    dist_avg = np.mean([np.dot(n.normal, n.pos) for n in valid_neighbors])
                    term1 = np.abs(dist_self - dist_avg)
                    term2 = np.mean([np.linalg.norm(g.normal - n.normal) for n in valid_neighbors])
                    geometric_loss += 0.05 * term1 + 0.01 * term2
    return 0.8 * photometric_loss + 0.3 * geometric_loss

def update_neighbor_lists(gaussians, current_iter):
    interval = 100 if current_iter < 20000 else 1000
    if current_iter % interval == 0:
        for g in gaussians:
            if g.g_type == 'theta':
                g.neighbors = get_k_nearest_neighbors(g.pos, gaussians, k=8)

def distance_to_nearest_neighbor(p, point_cloud):
    dists = [np.linalg.norm(p - q) for q in point_cloud if q is not p]
    return min(dists) if dists else 0.01

def get_k_nearest_neighbors(p, targets, k=8):
    dists = [(np.linalg.norm(p - t.pos), t) for t in targets]
    dists.sort(key=lambda x: x[0])
    return [t for _, t in dists[:k]]

def is_large(g):
    return g.alpha > 0.1 and np.max(g.scale) > 0.01

def split_standard(g):
    offset = np.random.randn(3) * g.scale * 0.5
    g1 = Gaussian(g.pos + offset, g.scale.copy() / 1.6, g.rot.copy(), g.sh.copy(), g.alpha / 2, g.g_type, normal=g.normal)
    g2 = Gaussian(g.pos - offset, g.scale.copy() / 1.6, g.rot.copy(), g.sh.copy(), g.alpha / 2, g.g_type, normal=g.normal)
    return g1, g2

def render(gaussians, pose):
    # [G] Standard 3DGS rasterization and blending pipeline
    return np.zeros((100, 100, 3), dtype=np.float32)
```

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   The method requires more GPU resources for training compared to standard 3DGS due to the additional geometric loss computation and neighbor list maintenance [S1].
*   Neighbor lists are updated periodically (every 100 iterations until 20,000, then every 1,000), adding overhead proportional to the number of thin Gaussians [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   Evaluated on Replica, TUM RGB-D, and ICL-NUIM datasets [S1].
*   **Sparse View Evaluation (R1, 10% data):**
    *   GeoGaussian: PSNR 31.65, SSIM 0.937, LPIPS 0.041.
    *   3DGS: PSNR 30.49, SSIM 0.932, LPIPS 0.051.
    *   LightGS: PSNR 30.54, SSIM 0.932, LPIPS 0.051 [S1].
*   **Sparse View Evaluation (R1, 100% data):**
    *   GeoGaussian: PSNR 38.24, SSIM 0.979, LPIPS 0.021.
    *   3DGS: PSNR 37.60, SSIM 0.965, LPIPS 0.028.
    *   LightGS: PSNR 38.44, SSIM 0.967, LPIPS 0.025 [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Improves rendering quality in novel views, especially in low-textured regions and structured areas (walls, ceilings) where standard 3DGS degrades [S1]. Preserves geometry and texture through constrained optimization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires more training time and GPU resources than standard 3DGS. It functions as a global refinement module and cannot perform real-time optimization, unlike online methods such as Vox-Fusion [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when high-fidelity rendering of structured scenes is required, particularly with sparse input views or in regions with low texture [S1].
*   Use when the input data includes reliable surface normals (e.g., from high-quality SLAM/SfM) [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Do not use if real-time optimization is required [S1].
*   Do not use if the input point cloud has poor normal estimation, as the classification of $S_{co}$ may be unreliable [S1].

## Background

**Origin:**
*   GeoGaussian was proposed by Y. Li et al. in the paper "GeoGaussian: Geometry-Aware Gaussian Splatting for Scene Rendering" [S1].

**History and Development:**
*   The method builds upon 3D Gaussian Splatting and addresses its limitations in handling smooth surfaces and low-texture regions [S1].
*   It contrasts with voxel-based methods like Vox-Fusion, which integrate implicit neural representations and operate online, whereas GeoGaussian is a global refinement method using differentiable 3D Gaussians [S1].

**Usage:**
*   Evaluated on Replica, TUM RGB-D, and ICL-NUIM datasets [S1].
