# DaReNeRF

Also called: DaReNeRF architecture, Direction-Aware Representation, Direction-aware Representation for Dynamic Scenes

## Overview
DaReNeRF is a method for novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes that utilizes a direction-aware representation to capture spatiotemporal dynamics from six distinct orientations [S1]. It addresses the shift variance and direction ambiguity (checkerboard artifacts) inherent in traditional 2D Discrete Wavelet Transforms (DWT) by employing the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) [S1]. Unlike standard plane-based decompositions that blend representations from $\pm 45^\circ$ angles, DaReNeRF models features from six specific directions, eliminating ghosting artifacts around moving objects while maintaining memory efficiency through low-rank plane-based decomposition [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed 2D images with timestamps $t$.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB images for arbitrary camera poses and time steps.

**Data Structures:**
The scene is modeled as a 4D spatiotemporal volume $D$. The core data structure is the **Direction-Aware Representation**, which decomposes $D$ into plane-based components enhanced by wavelet coefficients.

1.  **Plane-based Representations ($M$):** 2D tensors $M^{AB}_r \in \mathbb{R}^{A \times B}$ where $(A, B) \in \{X, Y, Z, T\}^2$ and $A \neq B$. These represent spatial or spatiotemporal planes.
2.  **Learned Vectors ($v$):** Vectors $v_{ri} \in \mathbb{R}^F$ along feature axes, where $F=3$ (RGB).
3.  **Wavelet Coefficients:** For each plane $M^{AB}_r$, the representation is reconstructed from:
    *   Approximation coefficient: $W^{AB}_{a,r} \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2 \times n/2}$.
    *   6 Real direction-aware coefficients: $\{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6$.
    *   6 Imaginary direction-aware coefficients: $\{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6$.
    *   Here, $m, n$ are the spatial dimensions of the plane, and the coefficients are at half resolution ($l-1$ level of DTCWT).
4.  **Masks ($M^{AB}$):** Learned scalar masks used to sparsify the wavelet coefficients.
5.  **Voxel Grid:** A small 3D voxel grid indicating emptiness, storing the maximum opacity across all time steps for each voxel.

**Parameters:**
*   $R_1, R_2, R_3$: Number of low-rank components for the three decomposition terms. Total $R = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 \ll N$ (spatial resolution).
*   Spatial grid size: 512.
*   Temporal grid size: 300.
*   Batch size: 4,096.
*   Coordinate system: Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC).

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Decompose the 4D Volume:**
    The volume $D$ is represented as the sum of three terms, each combining two 2D plane-based representations and a learned vector via the Hadamard product ($\circ$) [S1]:
    $$ D = \sum_{r=1}^{R_1} M_{rXY} \circ M_{rZT} \circ v_{r1} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_2} M_{rXZ} \circ M_{rYT} \circ v_{r2} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_3} M_{rYZ} \circ M_{rXT} \circ v_{r3} $$
    For static scenes, this simplifies to a TensorRF-like decomposition involving vector-based representations $v^r_C$ [S1]:
    $$ D = \sum_{r=1}^{R_1} M^{XY}_r \circ v^r_Z \circ v^{r1} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_2} M^{XZ}_r \circ v^r_Y \circ v^{r2} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_3} M^{YZ}_r \circ v^r_X \circ v^{r3} $$

2.  **Construct Direction-Aware Planes:**
    Each plane $M^{AB}_r$ is reconstructed from its wavelet coefficients using the Inverse Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (IDTCWT) [S1]:
    $$ M^{AB}_r = \text{IDTCWT}\left([W^{AB}_{a,r}, \{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6, \{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6]\right) $$
    The DTCWT uses two complex wavelets (real and imaginary parts) satisfying Hilbert transform properties, providing 6 directional coefficients per part, aligned to specific directions to eliminate the checkerboard effect [S1].

3.  **Apply Sparsity Masks:**
    To handle empty regions and enforce sparsity, wavelet coefficients are masked. The masked coefficients $\tilde{W}^{AB}$ are computed as [S1]:
    $$ \tilde{W}^{AB} = \text{sg}\left(H(M^{AB}) - \text{sigmoid}(M^{AB})\right) \odot W^{AB} $$
    Where $\text{sg}$ is the stop-gradient operator, $H$ is the Heaviside step function, and $M^{AB}$ are learned masks. The stop-gradient is applied to the mask term to allow the mask to learn while keeping the coefficient gradients stable [S1].

4.  **Compute Density and Appearance Features:**
    For a query point in space-time, features are computed by fusing vectors from paired planes (e.g., XY and ZT) [S1]:
    a. Extract feature vectors from the paired planes.
    b. Multiply the feature vectors element-wise.
    c. Concatenate the multiplied results into a single vector.
    d. Multiply the concatenated vector by $V_{RF}$, a 2D tensor stacking all $v_{ri}$ [S1].
    e. Query point opacities directly from the resulting density features [S1].

5.  **Regress Color:**
    Regress RGB color values using a compact Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The inputs to the MLP are the appearance features (derived in Step 4) and the view direction [S1].

6.  **Volumetric Rendering:**
    Synthesize final images via differentiable volumetric rendering along camera rays [S1].

7.  **Optimization:**
    Minimize the total loss $L$ [S1]:
    $$ L = \frac{1}{|R|} \sum_{r \in R} ||C(r) - \hat{C}(r)||^2 + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg} + \lambda_m L_m $$
    Where:
    *   $C(r)$ is the rendered color, $\hat{C}(r)$ is the ground-truth color.
    *   $L_{reg}$ is the Total Variation (TV) loss on the direction-aware representation to enforce spatio-temporal continuity.
    *   $L_m$ is the mask sparsity loss (sum of all masks).
    *   $\lambda_{reg}, \lambda_m$ are weighting hyperparameters.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced from $O(N^3 T F)$ for direct 4D volume modeling to $O(R N^2 T F)$ using the plane-based decomposition, where $R = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 \ll N$ [S1].
*   **Performance:** Trained on a single A100 GPU with a batch size of 4,096 [S1]. Uses a coarse-to-fine training strategy, progressively increasing grid resolution [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates checkerboard artifacts and ghosting caused by direction ambiguity in DWT. Handles shift variance better than DWT due to the shift-invariance properties of DTCWT [S1]. Efficiently handles empty regions via the 3D voxel grid [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The direction-aware representation introduces a 2D redundancy ($d=2$ for plane-based decomposition) compared to DWT, resulting in lower storage efficiency than ideal sparse representations, though still better than dense 4D volumes [S1].
*   **When to use:** Novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes with complex motion, particularly where ghosting artifacts from standard DWT-based methods are problematic. Suitable for both dynamic and static scenes [S1].

## Background
DaReNeRF was introduced in the paper "DaReNeRF: Direction-aware Representation for Dynamic Scenes" [S1]. It addresses limitations of previous methods like D-NeRF and plane-based NeRFs (e.g., TensorRF) which struggle with shift variance and direction selectivity in dynamic scenes [S1]. The method builds on the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) [47] to provide robust direction-aware features [S1]. It is evaluated on the Plenoptic Video and D-NeRF datasets [S1].
