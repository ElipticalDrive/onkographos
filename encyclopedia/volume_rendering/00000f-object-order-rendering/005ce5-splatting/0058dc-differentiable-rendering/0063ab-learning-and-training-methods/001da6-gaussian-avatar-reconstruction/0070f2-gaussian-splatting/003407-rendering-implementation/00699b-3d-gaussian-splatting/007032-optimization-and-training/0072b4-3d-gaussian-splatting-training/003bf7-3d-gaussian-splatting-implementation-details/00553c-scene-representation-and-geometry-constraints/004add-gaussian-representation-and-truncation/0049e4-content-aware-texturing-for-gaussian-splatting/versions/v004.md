# Content-Aware Texturing for Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Content-Aware Texturing, Ours

## Overview
This method augments 2D Gaussian primitives with spatially varying texture maps to capture high-frequency appearance details that spherical harmonics alone cannot represent, while dynamically managing texture resolution to balance quality and memory usage [S1]. Unlike standard Gaussian Splatting which relies on fixed-color primitives, or methods like [BBSplat](9910-bbsplat.md) that use a fixed texel-to-primitive ratio, this approach adapts the texture resolution per primitive based on local image content and error, avoiding the convergence issues associated with cloning-based densification [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of $N$ 2D Gaussian primitives initialized from a point cloud.
*   A set of input views $\Pi$ with corresponding ground truth images.
*   Camera parameters for each view.

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized primitive parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH coefficients).
*   Optimized texture maps $T_i$ for each primitive.
*   Per-primitive texel size ratio $t2pr_i$.

### Data Structures

**Primitive Parameters:**
Each primitive $i$ stores 59 parameters, saved as 4-byte floats [S1]:
*   **Position ($\mu_i$):** $\mathbb{R}^3$ (3 floats).
*   **Scale ($s_i$):** $\mathbb{R}^2$ (2 floats, representing anisotropic scaling in the primitive's local plane) [S1].
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):** $\mathbb{R}^4$ (4 floats, unit quaternion) [S1].
*   **Opacity ($\omega_i$):** $\mathbb{R}$ (1 float) [S1].
*   **Color ($c_i$):** Spherical Harmonic coefficients, Degree 3, 48 floats (RGB) [S1].
*   **Texel Size Ratio ($t2pr_i$):** $\mathbb{R}$ (1 float). This determines the world-space texel size $k_i$ [S1].

**Texture Storage:**
*   **Texels:** Each texel stores 3 floats (RGB offsets) [S1].
*   **Layout:** Textures are stored in a custom "jagged" tensor structure to handle varying resolutions per primitive [S1].
*   **Activation:** Raw texel values $c'_{Ti}$ are passed through a sigmoid activation to constrain them to $[-1, 1]$ before being added to the SH color [S1]:
    $$ c_{Ti} = 2\sigma(c'_{Ti}) - 1 $$

### Coordinate Systems
To facilitate texture sampling and intersection calculations, the method uses four coordinate systems [S1]:
1.  **World Space ($p_w$):** The intersection point in world coordinates.
2.  **World Space Centered ($p_{w0}$):** $p_{w0} = p_w - \mu_i$.
3.  **Local Axis-Aligned ($p_l$):** $p_l = R_i^{-1} p_{w0}$. Since the primitive is 2D, the last component is 0, so $p_l \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
4.  **Local Normalized ($p_c$):** $p_c = S_i^{-1} p_l$, where $S_i = \text{diag}(\sigma_i)$ is the scaling matrix. This is the "canonical" space of the Gaussian.

### Rendering Formula
The color $C(r)$ of a ray $r$ is computed via alpha blending in camera space [S1]:
$$ C(r) = \sum_i w_i(p_i) c_i(d) = \sum_i T_i \omega_i G_i(p_i) c_i(d) $$
$$ T_i = \prod_{j < i} (1 - \omega_j G_j(p_j)) $$
where:
*   $G(x) = e^{-\frac{1}{2} (x^T x)}$ is the Gaussian falloff function.
*   $c_i(d)$ is the color of primitive $i$ for direction $d$.
*   The color is the sum of the SH color and the texture offset:
    $$ c_i = SH(d) + \text{bilerp}(T_i, u) $$
*   $u$ is the UV coordinate in the texture map, calculated as:
    $$ u = \frac{p_l}{k_i} + T_{offset} $$
    where $k_i$ is the texel size in world units and $T_{offset}$ centers the texture map [S1].

### Texel Size Determination
The texel size $k_i$ is derived from the minimum pixel size $k_{min}$ and the primitive-specific ratio $t2pr_i$ [S1]:
$$ k_i = k_{min} \cdot t2pr_i $$
$t2pr_i$ is constrained to powers of two with a lower bound of 1 [S1].

### Training Loss
The total loss $L$ is the sum of RGB loss, texture sparsity loss, and opacity regularization [S1]:
$$ L = L_{RGB} + L_{texture} + L_{opacity} $$

1.  **RGB Loss ($L_{RGB}$):** Weighted sum of L1 and SSIM losses [S1]:
    $$ L_{RGB} = (1 - \lambda_{SSIM}) L_1 + \lambda_{SSIM} L_{SSIM} $$
    Typical value: $\lambda_{SSIM} = 0.2$ [S1].

2.  **Texture Sparsity Loss ($L_{texture}$):** Encourages texels to be zero to allow downsampling [S1]:
    $$ L_{texture} = \lambda_{texture} \sum_i |c_{Ti}| $$

3.  **Opacity Regularization ($L_{opacity}$):** Encourages low-contribution primitives to disappear [S1]:
    $$ L_{opacity} = \lambda_{opacity} \frac{1}{N} \sum_i \omega_i $$

### Primitive Management and Densification
The method avoids cloning to prevent superimposition issues [S1]. Instead, it uses splitting and dynamic texture resolution adjustment [S1].

**Error Calculation:**
For each primitive $i$, the error $E_i$ is a weighted sum of per-view errors [S1]:
$$ E_i = \frac{\sum_{\pi \in \Pi} E_i^\pi w_i^\pi}{\sum_{\pi \in \Pi} w_i^\pi} $$
where $w_i^\pi$ is the total contribution of primitive $i$ in view $\pi$, and $E_i^\pi$ is the weighted error in view $\pi$ [S1]:
$$ E_i^\pi = \frac{\sum_{p \in P_i} G(p) (T_{orig}(p) - T_{lowpass}(p))}{\sum_{p \in P_i} G(p)} $$
Here, $T_{orig}$ is the original texture and $T_{lowpass}$ is a low-pass filtered version. This error $E_d$ measures appearance fidelity [S1].

**Upscaling (Appearance Error):**
If a primitive is in the top 10% of highest error $E_i$, and its $t2pr_i > 2$, it is upscaled [S1]:
*   Action: Halve $t2pr_i$ (texture resolution increases by factor of 4).
*   This allows finer details to be captured.

**Downscaling (Low Frequency):**
If the appearance error $E_d < \tau_{ds}$, the texture is considered redundant [S1]:
*   Action: Double $t2pr_i$ (texture resolution decreases by factor of 4).
*   This reduces memory usage.

**Splitting (Geometric Error):**
If a primitive is in the top 10% of highest error $E_i$, and its texture resolution exceeds a threshold $\tau_{tr}$ (indicating appearance is already well-captured), the error is attributed to geometry [S1]:
*   Action: Replace the primitive with two new primitives.
*   Displacement: Each new primitive is displaced by $\pm 1$ standard deviation of the Gaussian along the axis of greatest error.
*   New Properties:
    *   Scale: Half the scale in the corresponding axis.
    *   Texture Resolution: Half the texture resolution (halving $t2pr_i$).
    *   Opacity: $G(1)$ times the original opacity.
    *   Texture Initialization: Sample the original texture at the new locations.
*   This is performed separately for each axis [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def update_primitives(primitives, views, gt_images, iteration):
    """
    Updates primitive parameters, texture resolutions, and performs splitting.
    Called every 100 iterations.
    """
    # 1. Calculate per-primitive errors
    errors = []
    for prim in primitives:
        # Calculate weighted error across all views
        total_weight = 0
        weighted_error_sum = 0
        
        for view_idx, view in enumerate(views):
            # Render current state to get error map
            error_map = compute_error_map(prim, view, gt_images[view_idx])
            
            # Calculate contribution weight w_i^pi
            weight = compute_contribution_weight(prim, view)
            
            # Calculate appearance error E_d using low-pass filter comparison
            # Note: In practice, this requires rendering the texture with and without filtering
            appearance_error = compute_appearance_error(prim, view)
            
            weighted_error_sum += appearance_error * weight
            total_weight += weight
            
        if total_weight > 0:
            prim.error = weighted_error_sum / total_weight
        else:
            prim.error = 0
        errors.append(prim)

    # 2. Sort primitives by error
    sorted_primitives = sort_by_error(errors, descending=True)
    top_10_percent = sorted_primitives[:len(sorted_primitives) // 10]

    # 3. Process Upscaling and Splitting
    to_split = []
    to_upscale = []
    
    for prim in top_10_percent:
        # Check for Geometric Error (Splitting)
        # If texture resolution is already high (exceeds threshold), error is geometric
        if prim.t2pr > tau_tr:
            to_split.append(prim)
        # Check for Appearance Error (Upscaling)
        # Only upscale if t2pr >= 2 to avoid sub-texel artifacts
        elif prim.t2pr >= 2:
            to_upscale.append(prim)

    # 4. Apply Upscaling
    for prim in to_upscale:
        prim.t2pr = prim.t2pr / 2  # Halve ratio, quadruple resolution

    # 5. Apply Splitting
    for prim in to_split:
        # Determine axis of greatest error (simplified: assume x-axis for example)
        axis = get_greatest_error_axis(prim)
        
        # Create two new primitives
        new_prim_1 = copy(prim)
        new_prim_2 = copy(prim)
        
        # Displace by +/- 1 standard deviation
        std_dev = prim.scale[axis]
        new_prim_1.position[axis] += std_dev
        new_prim_2.position[axis] -= std_dev
        
        # Update properties
        new_prim_1.scale[axis] = prim.scale[axis] / 2
        new_prim_2.scale[axis] = prim.scale[axis] / 2
        
        new_prim_1.t2pr = prim.t2pr / 2
        new_prim_2.t2pr = prim.t2pr / 2
        
        # Opacity: G(1) * original opacity
        # G(1) = exp(-0.5)
        g_val = exp(-0.5)
        new_prim_1.opacity = prim.opacity * g_val
        new_prim_2.opacity = prim.opacity * g_val
        
        # Initialize textures by sampling original
        new_prim_1.texture = sample_texture(prim.texture, new_prim_1.position)
        new_prim_2.texture = sample_texture(prim.texture, new_prim_2.position)
        
        # Remove original and add new ones
        primitives.remove(prim)
        primitives.append(new_prim_1)
        primitives.append(new_prim_2)

    # 6. Apply Downscaling (Low Frequency Regions)
    for prim in primitives:
        if prim.error < tau_ds:
            prim.t2pr = prim.t2pr * 2  # Double ratio, quarter resolution
            # Ensure t2pr stays within bounds if necessary, though logic implies it reduces params

    # 7. Handle Boundary Conditions
    for prim in primitives:
        # If primitive grows beyond allocated texture grid (max 256 resolution)
        if prim.texture_resolution > 256:
            # Zero-pad or clamp. Since texels are offsets, zero-padding reverts to DC color.
            clamp_texture_resolution(prim, 256)
```

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{SSIM}$: 0.2 [S1].
*   $\lambda_{opacity}$: Not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{texture}$: Not given in the sources.
*   $\tau_{ds}$: Threshold for downscaling. Not given in the sources.
*   $\tau_{tr}$: Threshold for texture resolution triggering splitting. Not given in the sources.
*   Max Texture Resolution: 256 [S1].
*   Upscale Trigger: Top 10% of primitives with highest error [S1].
*   Upscale Condition: $t2pr \ge 2$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Texture Stretching:** A naive mapping where UV coordinates are fixed in canonical space causes texture stretching when primitives scale. This method fixes texel size in world space to avoid this [S1].
*   **Cloning Incompatibility:** Cloning-based densification is incompatible because superimposing multiple textured primitives makes convergence difficult and increases parameter count excessively. Splitting is used instead [S1].
*   **Primitive Growth:** If a primitive grows outside its allocated texture grid (due to lack of allocation or hitting the 256 limit), zero-padding is used. This reduces the appearance to the DC color (SH color) since texels are offsets [S1].
*   **Optimization Coupling:** Coupling texture parameters with primitive shape parameters (scale/rotation) can induce local minima. Fixing texel size in world space decouples this [S1].

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Memory:** The number of parameters is 59 per primitive plus 3 per texel. The total number of texels is dynamic. On DeepBlending, it uses 21.6M texels and 78.1M params [S1]. On Mip-Nerf-360, 46.6M texels and 152.6M params [S1]. On Tanks&Temples, 10.4M texels and 41.1M params [S1].
*   **Training Time:** Training is 1.5-2x slower than 2DGS due to per-ray texture querying overhead [S1].
*   **Rendering Time:** Rendering is 25% slower than 2DGS [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **DeepBlending:** SSIM 0.907, PSNR 30.03, LPIPS 0.303, 70 FPS [S1].
*   **Mip-Nerf-360:** SSIM 0.795, PSNR 27.00, LPIPS 0.263, 67 FPS [S1].
*   **Tanks&Temples:** SSIM 0.835, PSNR 23.43, LPIPS 0.225, 121 FPS [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Achieves competitive or superior quality with significantly fewer parameters than methods like GSTex or BBSplat. Dynamically allocates texture capacity to high-frequency regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Slower training and rendering compared to 2DGS. Requires custom jagged tensor storage for textures [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   When high-frequency appearance details are critical and standard SH representations are insufficient.
*   When memory efficiency is important, as the method adapts texture resolution to content.

**When Not to Use:**
*   When real-time rendering speed is the primary constraint and texture overhead is unacceptable.
*   When the scene has very low frequency appearance, as the overhead of texture management may not be justified.

## Background

**Origin:**
The method was introduced by Panagiotis Papantonakis, Georgios Kopanas, Frédo Durand, and George Drettakis in the paper "Content-Aware Texturing for Gaussian Splatting" [S1].

**History and Development:**
The method builds upon 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) [S1]. It addresses limitations in previous texturing approaches like GSTex (which uses a fixed texel budget on pretrained points) and BBSplat (which uses a fixed texel-to-primitive ratio) by introducing dynamic, content-aware texture resolution [S1]. It also improves upon cloning-based densification strategies by using splitting to avoid superimposition issues [S1].

**Usage:**
Implemented using the 3DGS codebase, introducing 2D primitives as in 2DGS, but using direct camera space intersection rather than the $R^2 \to R^2$ transformation [S1].
