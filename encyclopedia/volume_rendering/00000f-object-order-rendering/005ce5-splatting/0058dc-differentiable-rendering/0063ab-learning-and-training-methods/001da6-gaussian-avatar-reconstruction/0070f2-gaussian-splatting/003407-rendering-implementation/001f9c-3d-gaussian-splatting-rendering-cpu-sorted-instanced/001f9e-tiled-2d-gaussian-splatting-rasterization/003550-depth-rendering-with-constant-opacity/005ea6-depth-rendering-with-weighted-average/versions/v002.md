# Depth Rendering with Weighted Average

Also called: Alpha-blending Depth Rendering, Depth Map Rendering, Weighted Depth Accumulation, standard alpha-blending

## Overview
Depth Rendering with Weighted Average computes a depth map for 3D Gaussian Splatting by calculating a weighted average of Gaussian depths along a ray, where the weights are determined by the alpha-compositing process [S1, S2]. It differs from [Depth Rendering with Constant Opacity](7462-depth-rendering-with-constant-opacity.md) by allowing opacity to influence the depth accumulation, which can lead to depth ambiguity at sharp edges or with misplaced "floaters" [S1, S2]. It is distinct from [Normalized Depth Rendering](8298-normalized-depth-rendering.md) and [Depth Rendering with Variable Opacity](12622-depth-rendering-with-variable-opacity.md) in its reliance on standard alpha-blending mechanics rather than normalization or variable opacity constraints [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of 3D Gaussians, each defined by position $\mathbf{c}_i$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color $\mathbf{c}_i^{color}$ [G].
- Camera parameters (view matrix, projection matrix, intrinsics) [G].
- Image resolution $(W, H)$ [G].

**Outputs:**
- A depth map $D$ of size $W \times H$, where each pixel $(x, y)$ contains a single depth value $d_{x,y}$ [G].

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian List:** Array of structs containing fields: `position` (vec3), `scale` (vec3), `rotation` (quat), `opacity` (float), `sh_coeffs` (array) [G].
- **Rasterization Buffer:** For each pixel, store accumulated transmittance $T$ (float, initialized to 1.0) and accumulated depth $D_{acc}$ (float, initialized to 0.0) [G].

**Steps:**
1. **Projection and Sorting:** Project each Gaussian's center to screen space. Sort Gaussians front-to-back relative to the camera for each tile or pixel to ensure correct alpha-compositing order [G].
2. **Rasterization:** For each pixel $(x, y)$, iterate through the sorted Gaussians that overlap the pixel [G].
3. **Weight Calculation:** For the $i$-th Gaussian, calculate the alpha-compositing weight $\alpha_i$. This is typically derived from the Gaussian's 2D covariance and opacity [G].
4. **Transmittance Update:** Update the accumulated transmittance $T_i$ for the current Gaussian:
   $$ T_i = T_{i-1} \cdot (1 - \alpha_i) $$
   where $T_0 = 1.0$ [G].
5. **Depth Contribution:** Calculate the depth contribution of the $i$-th Gaussian. Let $d_i$ be the depth of the Gaussian center (standard alpha-blending uses the center depth projected along the ray) [G]. The weighted depth contribution is $T_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot d_i$ [G].
6. **Accumulation:** Add the contribution to the accumulated depth $D_{acc}$ for the pixel:
   $$ D_{acc} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot d_i $$
   [G].
7. **Final Depth:** The final depth $d_{x,y}$ is the accumulated depth $D_{acc}$. Note: In some implementations, if the accumulated opacity is very low, the depth might be set to a default value (e.g., far plane) to handle background [G].

**Formula:**
The alpha-blending depth at pixel $(x, y)$, denoted as $d_{x,y}^{alpha}$, is calculated as [S2]:
$$ d_{x,y}^{alpha} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i d_i $$
where:
- $N$ is the number of Gaussians overlapping the pixel [G].
- $T_i$ is the accumulated transmittance for the $i$-th Gaussian, defined as $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ [G].
- $\alpha_i$ is the alpha-compositing weight of the $i$-th Gaussian [G].
- $d_i$ is the depth of the $i$-th Gaussian [G].

Alternatively, some implementations normalize the accumulation to compute a true weighted average [S1]:
$$ d = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} z^c_i \omega_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \omega_i} $$
where $\omega_i = \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the blending weight, and $z^c_i$ is the depth of the $i$-th Gaussian in camera coordinates [S1]. The camera coordinates are obtained by transforming the world position $[x_i, y_i, z_i]^T$ using extrinsics $[W, t]$ as $[x^c_i, y^c_i, z^c_i]^T = W[x_i, y_i, z_i]^T + t$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $\alpha_i$: Typically derived from the Gaussian's opacity and the 2D projection. Not given in the sources. [G]
- $d_i$: Depth of the Gaussian center. Not given in the sources. [G]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Floaters:** Misplaced Gaussians (floaters) can significantly influence the depth value, leading to inaccurate depth maps [S1, S2].
- **Precision:** Accumulating many small contributions can lead to floating-point precision issues. Not given in the sources. [G]
- **Boundaries:** Gaussians at the edge of the image or with large covariances may require careful handling to avoid artifacts. Not given in the sources. [G]

## Subtopics

### [Unbiased Depth Rendering (PGSR)](21507-unbiased-depth-rendering-pgsr.md)
Unbiased Depth Rendering is a technique for estimating precise depth maps and normal maps from 3D Gaussian Splatting by approximating the underlying surface geometry. It sets itself apart from weighted average depth methods by flattening 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into planes, allowing depth to be calculated via ray-plane intersections rather than simple accumulation. This approach aims to reduce depth ambiguity at sharp edges and better reflect actual surface geometry.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot R)$, where $N$ is the number of Gaussians and $R$ is the number of rays (pixels). Sorting adds $O(N \log N)$. [G]
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N + W \cdot H)$ for storing Gaussian data and rasterization buffers. [G]
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources. [G]
- **Scaling:** Performance scales linearly with the number of Gaussians and image resolution. [G]
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; widely used in NeRFs and adapted for 3DGS [S1, S2].
- **Limitations:** Susceptible to depth ambiguity caused by floaters and overlapping Gaussians with different opacities [S1, S2].
- **When to Use:** When a quick depth estimate is needed and the scene does not have significant floaters or complex transparency. [G]
- **When Not to Use:** When high-precision depth is required, especially in scenes with sharp edges or many floaters. Consider [Unbiased Depth Rendering (PGSR)](21507-unbiased-depth-rendering-pgsr.md) for better geometric accuracy. [G]

## Background
Alpha-blending is a widely used technique in NeRFs [S1, S2]. It has been adapted for 3D Gaussian Splatting to render depth maps. The method calculates the depth value of a pixel by weighting the depth of each Gaussian by its contribution to the final color, as determined by the alpha-compositing process [S1, S2].
