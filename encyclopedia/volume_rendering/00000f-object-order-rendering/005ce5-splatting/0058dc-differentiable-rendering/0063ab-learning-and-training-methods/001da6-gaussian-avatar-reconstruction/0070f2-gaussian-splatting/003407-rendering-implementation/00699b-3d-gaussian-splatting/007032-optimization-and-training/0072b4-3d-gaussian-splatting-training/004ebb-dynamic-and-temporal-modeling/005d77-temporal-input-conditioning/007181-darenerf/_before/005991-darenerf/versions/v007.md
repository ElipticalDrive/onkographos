# DaReNeRF

Also called: DaReNeRF architecture, Direction-Aware Representation, Direction-aware Representation for Dynamic Scenes

## Overview
DaReNeRF is a method for novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes that employs a direction-aware representation to capture scene dynamics from six distinct orientations [S1]. It addresses the shift variance and direction ambiguity (checkerboard artifacts) inherent in traditional 2D Discrete Wavelet Transforms (DWT) by utilizing the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) [S1]. Unlike standard plane-based decompositions, DaReNeRF models features from six different directions to eliminate ghosting artifacts around moving objects [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of posed 2D images with timestamps representing a dynamic scene [S1].
*   Camera poses and timestamps for query rays [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Rendered RGB images for arbitrary camera poses and time steps [S1].

### Data Structures

**1. Direction-Aware Plane Representation ($M^{AB}_r$)**
Each 2D plane-based representation $M^{AB}_r \in \mathbb{R}^{A \times B}$ (where $(A, B) \in \{X, Y, Z, T\}^2, A \neq B$) is not stored directly but reconstructed from wavelet coefficients [S1].
*   **Approximation Coefficient:** $W^{AB}_{a,r} \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2 \times n/2}$ [S1].
*   **Real Direction-Aware Coefficients:** $\{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6$, where $R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\} \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2 \times n/2}$ [S1].
*   **Imaginary Direction-Aware Coefficients:** $\{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6$, where $I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\} \in \mathbb{R}^{m/2 \times n/2}$ [S1].
*   **Mask:** $M^{AB} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ used to sparsify the representation [S1].

**2. Learned Vectors ($v_{ri}$)**
*   $v_{ri} \in \mathbb{R}^F$ are learned vectors along the feature axis $F$ [S1].
*   $V_{RF}$ is a 2D tensor stacking all $v_{ri}$ [S1].

**3. Emptiness Voxel**
*   A small 3D voxel grid indicating empty regions. Values are aggregated maximum opacities across time steps [S1].

### Parameters
*   $F$: Feature size (typically 3 for RGB) [S1].
*   $R_1, R_2, R_3$: Number of low-rank components for each plane pair combination [S1].
*   $R$: Total low-rank components, $R = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$, where $R \ll N$ ($N$ is spatial resolution) [S1].
*   Spatial grid size: 512 [S1].
*   Temporal grid size: 300 [S1].
*   Batch size: 4,096 [S1].
*   Coordinate system: Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) [S1].

### Formulas

**1. 4D Volume Decomposition**
The 4D spatiotemporal volume $D$ is decomposed into a sum of three terms:
$$
D = \sum_{r=1}^{R_1} M_{rXY} \circ M_{rZT} \circ v_{r1} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_2} M_{rXZ} \circ M_{rYT} \circ v_{r2} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_3} M_{rYZ} \circ M_{rXT} \circ v_{r3}
$$
Where $\circ$ denotes the Hadamard (element-wise) product [S1].

**2. Plane Reconstruction via DTCWT**
Each plane $M^{AB}_r$ is reconstructed from its coefficients using the Inverse Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (IDTCWT):
$$
M^{AB}_r = \text{IDTCWT}([W^{AB}_{a,r}, \{R\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6, \{I\{W^{AB}_{i,r}\}\}_{i=1}^6])
$$
The DTCWT uses two complex wavelets (real and imaginary parts) satisfying Hilbert transform properties, providing 6 directional coefficients per part, eliminating the checkerboard effect of standard DWT [S1].

**3. Masked Wavelet Coefficients**
To encourage sparsity, wavelet coefficients are masked:
$$
\tilde{W}^{AB} = \text{sg}(H(M^{AB}) - \text{sigmoid}(M^{AB})) \odot W^{AB}
$$
Where $\text{sg}$ is the stop-gradient operator, $H$ is the Heaviside step function, and $M^{AB}$ are learned masks [S1].

**4. Feature Computation**
For a space-time point, features are computed by:
1.  Extracting feature vectors from paired planes (e.g., $XY$ and $ZT$).
2.  Multiplying these vectors element-wise.
3.  Concatenating the multiplied results into a single vector.
4.  Multiplying the concatenated vector by $V_{RF}$ (the tensor of all $v_{ri}$) [S1].

**5. Loss Function**
$$
L = \frac{1}{|R|} \sum_{r \in R} ||C(r) - \hat{C}(r)||^2 + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg} + \lambda_m L_m
$$
Where $C(r)$ is rendered color, $\hat{C}(r)$ is ground-truth, $L_{reg}$ is total variation loss, and $L_m$ is mask sparsity loss (sum of all masks) [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def forward_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, time, params):
    """
    Renders a single ray using DaReNeRF.
    ray_origin: (3,) float32
    ray_direction: (3,) float32
    time: float32
    params: Object containing learned weights and structures
    """
    # 1. Sample points along the ray
    # In practice, hierarchical sampling is used [S1]
    points = sample_points_along_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, params.num_samples)
    
    # 2. Query Density and Appearance
    densities = []
    appearances = []
    
    for point in points:
        x, y, z, t = point.xyz, point.t
        
        # 3. Retrieve Plane Representations
        # Reconstruct planes from DTCWT coefficients
        # M_XY, M_ZT, etc. are reconstructed via IDTCWT from stored coefficients
        M_XY = reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['XY'], time)
        M_ZT = reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['ZT'], time)
        
        # 4. Compute Density Feature
        # Extract vectors from paired planes and multiply
        vec_XY = extract_vector(M_XY, x, y)
        vec_ZT = extract_vector(M_ZT, z, t)
        density_vec = elementwise_multiply(vec_XY, vec_ZT)
        
        # Concatenate results from all 3 plane pairs (XY-ZT, XZ-YT, YZ-XT)
        # Note: The formula sums these, but feature extraction typically concatenates 
        # or sums the resulting vectors before projection. 
        # Based on "concatenates the multiplied results into a single vector":
        combined_density_vec = concatenate([
            elementwise_multiply(extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['XY'], time), x, y),
                                 extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['ZT'], time), z, t)),
            elementwise_multiply(extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['XZ'], time), x, z),
                                 extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['YT'], time), y, t)),
            elementwise_multiply(extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['YZ'], time), y, z),
                                 extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_coeffs['XT'], time), x, t))
        ])
        
        # Multiply by V_RF to get density feature
        density_feature = matrix_vector_multiply(combined_density_vec, params.V_RF)
        density = query_opacity(density_feature) # Opacity queried directly from density features
        
        # 5. Compute Appearance Feature
        # Similar process for appearance, using appearance-specific planes/coefficients
        # (Assuming separate coefficients or shared structure for appearance)
        combined_appearance_vec = concatenate([
            elementwise_multiply(extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_appear_coeffs['XY'], time), x, y),
                                 extract_vector(reconstruct_plane(params.M_appear_coeffs['ZT'], time), z, t)),
            # ... other pairs ...
        ])
        appearance_feature = matrix_vector_multiply(combined_appearance_vec, params.V_RF_appear)
        
        densities.append(density)
        appearances.append(appearance_feature)
        
    # 6. Regress Color
    # Compact MLP takes appearance features and view directions
    colors = []
    for i, point in enumerate(points):
        color = compact_mlp(appearances[i], ray_direction)
        colors.append(color)
        
    # 7. Volumetric Rendering
    # Standard alpha compositing
    final_color = volumetric_render(densities, colors, params.depths)
    
    return final_color

def reconstruct_plane(coeffs, time):
    """
    Reconstructs a 2D plane from DTCWT coefficients.
    coeffs: Dictionary containing W_a, R{W_i}, I{W_i}
    time: Used to interpolate temporal coefficients if necessary, 
          though planes are often static in spatial dimensions or 
          interpolated in time.
    """
    # IDTCWT combines approximation and 6 real + 6 imaginary coefficients
    plane = inverse_dual_tree_complex_wavelet_transform(
        coeffs['W_a'], 
        coeffs['R_W'], 
        coeffs['I_W']
    )
    return plane

def extract_vector(plane, coord1, coord2):
    """
    Extracts a feature vector from a 2D plane at specific coordinates.
    """
    # Bilinear interpolation or nearest neighbor sampling
    return plane[coord1, coord2]
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Shift Variance:** Traditional 2D DWT suffers from shift variance, where small shifts in input signal disrupt wavelet oscillation patterns, causing poor results in dynamic regions with motion or camera movement [S1]. DaReNeRF uses DTCWT to ensure shift invariance [S1].
*   **Direction Ambiguity (Checkerboard Effect):** 2D DWT produces a checkered pattern blending representations from $\pm 45^\circ$, leading to ghosting artifacts. DaReNeRF uses DTCWT with 6 real and 6 imaginary coefficients aligned to specific directions to eliminate this [S1].
*   **Empty Regions:** Large empty regions waste computation. DaReNeRF uses a small 3D voxel representation indicating emptiness by aggregating maximum opacities across time steps to skip these points [S1].
*   **Static Scene Decomposition:** For static scenes, the model uses a TensorRF-like decomposition where vector-based representations replace one of the plane pairs (e.g., $M^{XY}_r \circ v^r_Z \circ v^{r1}$) [S1].

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Memory complexity is reduced from $O(N^3 T F)$ for direct 4D volume modeling to $O(R N^2 T F)$ using the plane-based decomposition [S1].
*   The model introduces a 2D redundancy ($d=2$ for plane-based decomposition) compared to DWT, resulting in lower storage efficiency than ideal DWT but higher fidelity [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   Trained on a single A100 GPU with a batch size of 4,096 [S1].
*   Spatial grid size: 512, Temporal grid size: 300 [S1].
*   Achieves state-of-the-art performance on Plenoptic Video and D-NeRF datasets [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Robust handling of complex dynamic scenes, elimination of checkerboard artifacts, shift invariance, efficient memory usage via low-rank decomposition [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The DTCWT approach introduces redundancy compared to standard DWT [S1]. Requires careful tuning of mask sparsity ($\lambda_m$) and regularization ($\lambda_{reg}$) [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes where high fidelity and motion accuracy are required [S1].
*   When ghosting artifacts from standard DWT-based methods are unacceptable [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Static scenes may not require the complexity of DTCWT, though it is applicable [S1].
*   If computational resources are extremely limited and the redundancy of DTCWT is prohibitive [S1].

## Background

**Origin:**
DaReNeRF was introduced in the paper "DaReNeRF: Direction-aware Representation for Dynamic Scenes" [S1]. It builds upon the plane-based 4D volume decomposition [7] and addresses limitations of DWT-based representations [6, 21] by leveraging the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) [47].

**History and Development:**
The method was developed to overcome the shift variance and poor direction selectivity of 2D DWT in dynamic scene modeling. It draws inspiration from DTCWT properties, specifically the Hilbert transform properties of complex wavelets, to provide 6 directional coefficients [S1].

**Usage:**
Used in DaReNeRF and its sparse variant DaReNeRF-S for novel view synthesis on dynamic and static scenes [S1].
