# Frequency Regularization

Also called: Amplitude and Phase Discrepancy Regularization, Improved Optimization via Regularization

## Overview
Frequency Regularization (FR) is a training-time constraint for 3D Gaussian Splatting that minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered images and ground truth to alleviate over-reconstruction and improve Gaussian densification [S2]. Unlike naive pixel-wise L1 loss, which results in small gradients in over-reconstructed regions and prevents effective densification, FR explicitly disentangles frequency components to increase pixel gradients in these regions, enabling adaptive densification [S2]. It builds on [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md) by adding a frequency-domain loss term to the total training loss [S2][S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ and ground truth image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ [S2].
**Outputs:** Amplitude discrepancy $d_a$, phase discrepancy $d_p$, and the total frequency regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{FR}$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Frequency Domain:** Complex-valued matrices $F(u,v)$ and $\hat{F}(u,v)$ of size $H \times W$ [S2].
*   **Amplitude:** Real-valued matrix $|F(u,v)|$ of size $H \times W$ [S2].
*   **Phase:** Real-valued matrix $\angle F(u,v)$ of size $H \times W$ [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Frequency Transformation:** Compute the 2D Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) for both the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth $I$ [S2]. For frequency coordinates $(u, v)$:
    $$F(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} I(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}$$
    $$\hat{F}(u, v) = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1} \sum_{y=0}^{W-1} \hat{I}(x, y) \cdot e^{-i2\pi(\frac{u}{H}x + \frac{v}{W}y)}$$
    where $I(x,y)$ and $\hat{I}(x,y)$ are pixel values, and $F(u,v)$, $\hat{F}(u,v)$ are complex frequency values [S2].
2.  **Amplitude and Phase Decomposition:** For each frequency component $(u, v)$, decompose the complex values into amplitude and phase [S2]. Let $Re(u,v)$ and $Im(u,v)$ be the real and imaginary parts of $F(u,v)$:
    $$|F(u, v)| = \sqrt{Re(u, v)^2 + Im(u, v)^2}$$
    $$\angle F(u, v) = \arctan\left(\frac{Im(u, v)}{Re(u, v)}\right)$$
    Compute $|\hat{F}(u, v)|$ and $\angle \hat{F}(u, v)$ similarly for the rendered image [S2].
3.  **Discrepancy Calculation:** Calculate the total amplitude discrepancy $d_a$ and total phase discrepancy $d_p$ using the Euclidean metric over all frequency components [S2]:
    $$d_a = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{u=0}^{H-1} \sum_{v=0}^{W-1} (|F(u, v)| - |\hat{F}(u, v)|)^2}$$
    $$d_p = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW} \sum_{u=0}^{H-1} \sum_{v=0}^{W-1} (\angle F(u, v) - \angle \hat{F}(u, v))^2}$$
    Note: The source formulas [S2] contain a typographical error using $x, y$ as summation indices for frequency coordinates; the implementation must sum over $u \in [0, H-1]$ and $v \in [0, W-1]$.
4.  **Loss Formulation:** The frequency regularization loss is a weighted sum of amplitude and phase discrepancies [S2]:
    $$\mathcal{L}_{FR} = \lambda_a d_a + \lambda_p d_p$$
    [G] In practice, $\lambda_a$ and $\lambda_p$ are hyperparameters tuned during training, typically combined with the parent method's weighting factor $\lambda$.
5.  **Frequency Annealing (Optional Variant):** To achieve coarse-to-fine densification, a progressive frequency regularization technique can be applied [S2]. This involves incorporating a low-pass filter $H_l$ and a dynamic high-pass filter $H_h$ in Fourier space to gradually leverage low-to-high frequencies [S2]. *Note: The exact mathematical formulation and implementation of the filters $H_l$ and $H_h$ are not provided in the sources.*

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam optimizer [S2].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch [S2].
*   **Rasterization:** Custom CUDA kernels from 3D-GS are used [S2].
*   **Resolution:** Training may start at lower resolution and upsample to the original size after 500 iterations [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-reconstruction:** Naive adoption of amplitude/phase discrepancies without frequency annealing can still suffer from restricted Gaussian densification and bias the splatting towards undesirable artifacts [S2].
*   **Gradient Vanishing:** In well-reconstructed regions, gradients may be small, but FR helps distinguish over-reconstructed regions where gradients are increased [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Scale Regularization](9855-scale-regularization.md)
Scale regularization is a training-time constraint used in 3D Gaussian Splatting to prevent the formation of excessively large Gaussians, which can degrade rendering quality, cause aliasing artifacts, and destabilize optimization. It specifically addresses issues arising from unconstrained Gaussian scale growth by penalizing scales that exceed a predefined maximum or geometric resolution threshold. Unlike general isotropic regularization which merely encourages equal scaling in all directions, this method ties the scale limit directly to the geometric resolution of the underlying mesh or the maximum sampling frequency of the scene.

### DWTGS
DWTGS is a method that rethinks frequency regularization for sparse-view 3DGS by applying a discrete wavelet transform on the rendered image and penalizing the loss in the wavelet domain to encourage sharper edges [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G] The addition of DFT operations increases computational overhead per iteration compared to standard L1 loss, with complexity dominated by the FFT at $O(HW \log(HW))$ per channel.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G] Requires storing frequency domain representations for rendered and ground truth images, adding $O(HW)$ memory overhead.
*   **Measured Performance:** In the 'Bicycle' scene, FR significantly increases pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions compared to the baseline method without FR [S2]. It allows for superior Gaussian densification when combined with frequency annealing [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively alleviates over-reconstruction, improves Gaussian densification by revealing over-reconstructed regions, and handles amplitude and phase information separately for more accurate disparity assessment [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Without frequency annealing, it can still lead to restricted densification and artifacts [S2]. It requires careful tuning of frequency filters if annealing is used [S2].
*   **When to use:** When over-reconstruction is prevalent, or when standard L1 loss fails to drive densification in specific regions [S2].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of FFT is prohibitive, or when the scene is already well-reconstructed without artifacts [G].

## Background
Additional regularization, including frequency regularization, is a primary direction for improving 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization to address convergence speed, visual artifacts, and improper Gaussians [S1]. It is part of the broader direction of "Additional Regularization" in 3D GS improvements, alongside geometry-aware approaches [S1]. The specific formulation using amplitude and phase discrepancy was proposed in FreGS [S2]. It builds on the observation that pixel-wise L1 loss cannot differentiate between over-reconstructed and well-reconstructed regions, leading to redundant Gaussian creation [S2].
