# Principled Uncertainty Pruning

Also called: Fisher Information-based pruning score, PUP 3D-GS, PUP 3D-GS Pruning Score & Pipeline, Principled Uncertainty Pruning for 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) is a post-hoc method for compressing pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing Gaussians that contribute least to the reconstruction error. It solves the problem of high storage and memory requirements in complex scenes by applying a multi-round prune-refine pipeline that preserves visual fidelity and foreground details at high compression ratios (up to 90%). Unlike heuristic-based siblings like [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md) or [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md), PUP uses a mathematically principled sensitivity score derived from the Fisher Information matrix of the spatial parameters, offering a more accurate estimate of Gaussian uncertainty than diagonal approximations or color-based metrics [S1].

## How it works
PUP operates on a converged 3D-GS model. It computes a sensitivity score for each Gaussian based on the Hessian of the L2 reconstruction error, approximated via the Fisher Information matrix. The method excludes rotation and color parameters to reduce memory overhead and improve accuracy, focusing exclusively on spatial mean and scale. The pipeline executes two rounds of pruning and fine-tuning to achieve high compression without quality degradation.

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: A converged 3D-GS model $\mathcal{G}$ (set of Gaussians), training views $\mathcal{I}$ with poses $\Phi$, and target pruning percentage.
- **Outputs**: A pruned set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ and a fine-tuned model.

### Data Structures
Each Gaussian $G_i$ in the 3D-GS representation has parameters:
- Mean position $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
- Scale (covariance diagonal) $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
- Rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
- Color coefficients $c_i$ (spherical harmonics)

The sensitivity score computation focuses on the block of parameters $\theta_i = [\mu_i, s_i] \in \mathbb{R}^6$. The Fisher Information matrix for a single Gaussian is a $6 \times 6$ matrix $F_i$.

### Formulas
The L2 error over input reconstruction images is defined as [S1]:
$$ L_2 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \|I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt}\|_2^2 $$
where $I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)$ is the rendered image for pose $\phi$, and $I_{gt}$ is the ground truth.

The Hessian of the L2 loss with respect to the Gaussian parameters $\mathcal{G}$ is [S1]:
$$ \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 L_2 = \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T + (I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt}) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) $$

On a converged model, the residual term $\|I_{\mathcal{G}} - I_{gt}\|$ approaches zero, causing the second-order term to vanish. The Hessian is approximated by the Fisher Information matrix [S1]:
$$ F \approx \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T $$

The pruning score for a single Gaussian $G_i$ is the log determinant of the block diagonal element of the Fisher matrix corresponding to its spatial parameters $\theta_i$ [S1]:
$$ \text{Score}(G_i) = \log \det(F_i) $$
This score represents the entropy of the second-order Laplace approximation of the posterior distribution over that Gaussian's parameters. A lower score indicates higher uncertainty (less sensitivity to error), making the Gaussian a candidate for pruning.

### Parameters
- **Pruning Percentages**: 80% in Round 1, 50% of the remaining in Round 2 (total ~90% pruning) [S1].
- **Fine-tuning Iterations**: 5,000 steps per round [S1].
- **Patch Size**: 4×4 pixels (default) for computing sensitivity scores [S1].
- **Parameters Included**: Only mean ($\mu$) and scale ($s$). Rotations and RGB are excluded [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
def principled_uncertainty_pruning(model, training_views, rounds=2, 
                                   prune_pct_round1=0.8, prune_pct_round2=0.5, 
                                   fine_tune_iters=5000, patch_size=4):
    """
    model: 3D-GS model with Gaussians G. Each Gaussian has mean, scale, rotation, color.
    training_views: List of (image, pose) tuples.
    """
    current_gaussians = model.gaussians
    
    for round_idx in range(rounds):
        # 1. Compute Fisher Information Matrix blocks for spatial parameters
        # Focus only on mean (3) and scale (3) -> 6x6 matrix per Gaussian
        fisher_blocks = []
        for g in current_gaussians:
            # Initialize 6x6 zero matrix
            F = zeros((6, 6))
            
            # Accumulate gradients over training views
            for image, pose in training_views:
                # Render gradient of image w.r.t spatial params of g
                # dI/d_theta is a vector of size (pixels_in_patch * 6)
                # We approximate the block contribution to the global Hessian
                # by summing outer products of gradients over the patch
                
                # Get indices of pixels covered by this Gaussian in the patch
                patch_indices = get_patch_indices(g, pose, patch_size)
                
                if patch_indices is empty:
                    continue
                
                # Compute gradient of rendered image w.r.t mean and scale
                # Shape: (num_pixels_in_patch, 6)
                grad = compute_spatial_gradient(g, image, pose, patch_indices)
                
                # Add to Fisher block: sum of outer products
                # F += grad^T @ grad
                F += grad.T @ grad
            
            fisher_blocks.append(F)
        
        # 2. Compute Pruning Scores
        scores = []
        for F in fisher_blocks:
            # Log determinant of the 6x6 Fisher block
            # If singular, add small epsilon to diagonal for stability
            det = determinant(F + 1e-6 * identity(6))
            score = log(det)
            scores.append(score)
        
        # 3. Determine Gaussians to Prune
        # Lower score = higher uncertainty = better candidate for pruning
        num_to_prune = int(len(current_gaussians) * prune_pct_round1)
        if round_idx == 1:
            num_to_prune = int(len(current_gaussians) * prune_pct_round2)
        
        # Get indices of Gaussians with lowest scores
        prune_indices = argsort(scores)[:num_to_prune]
        
        # 4. Prune
        remaining_indices = [i for i in range(len(current_gaussians)) if i not in prune_indices]
        current_gaussians = [current_gaussians[i] for i in remaining_indices]
        
        # 5. Fine-tune
        model.gaussians = current_gaussians
        model.fine_tune(training_views, iterations=fine_tune_iters)
        
    return model
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Convergence Requirement**: The Fisher approximation assumes a small L1 residual. Pruning before the model is fully converged degrades accuracy [S1].
- **Memory Constraints**: Including rotation parameters expands the Fisher matrix to $10 \times 10$, increasing memory usage by $2.78\times$. This can cause out-of-memory errors on 16GB GPUs (e.g., RTX A4000) [S1].
- **Color Parameters**: Using RGB color parameters for the sensitivity score degrades image quality because it correlates with visibility rather than reconstruction error sensitivity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: The Fisher matrix computation scales linearly with the number of Gaussians $N$ and the number of training views. Each Gaussian requires computing gradients over all views, which is $O(N \cdot V \cdot P)$ where $V$ is views and $P$ is pixels per patch [S1].
- **Memory Complexity**: Memory scales as $N \times 36$ bytes (for $6 \times 6$ float32 matrices) [S1]. This is manageable on 16GB GPUs but requires more powerful hardware if rotation parameters are included [S1].
- **Performance**: On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, PUP 3D-GS achieves an average 3.56× speedup after 90% pruning, with FPS ranging from 127.85 (bicycle) to 494.76 (train) on an RTX A4000 [S1]. On the Deep Blending playroom scene, it accelerates rendering from 76.65 FPS to 318.06 FPS (4.15×) [S1].
- **Quality**: Retains higher image quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and foreground details compared to LightGaussian and other heuristics [S1].
- **Strengths**: Post-hoc (no training modification), principled uncertainty estimate, high compression ratio (90%).
- **Limitations**: Requires a converged model, memory intensive for large scenes if not optimized, two-round process increases total fine-tuning time [S1].

## Background
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP 3D-GS) was introduced by Alex Hanson, Allen Tu, Vasu Singla, Mayuka Jayawardhana, Matthias Zwicker, and Tom Goldstein from the University of Maryland, College Park. It was published in 2025 at the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) [S1]. The method builds on the observation that uncertainty in 3D-GS can be quantified via the Hessian of the reconstruction error, approximated by the Fisher Information matrix. It addresses the limitations of heuristic-based pruning methods like LightGaussian, which lose fine details at high compression ratios [S1].
