# Scene Representation and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
This family defines the fundamental data structures and geometric constraints used to represent scenes and guide optimization in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its variants. It addresses the problem of converting soft, volumetric Gaussian primitives into structured geometric representations (such as meshes or normals) and enforcing physical or structural consistency during training. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Sorting and Rendering Pipeline Optimization](12180-sorting-and-rendering-pipeline-optimization.md) by focusing on the semantic and geometric validity of the primitives rather than rendering speed, and from [Appearance and Physical Modeling](15766-appearance-and-physical-modeling.md) by prioritizing surface alignment and structural constraints over radiance synthesis.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a set of $N$ 3D Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $i$ is defined by learnable parameters describing its geometry, appearance, and position.

**Data Structure:**
Each Gaussian $g_i$ is represented by the following attributes [S1]:
*   **Position ($\mu_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing the centroid in world space.
*   **Rotation ($q_i$):** A unit quaternion $\mathbb{R}^4$ representing orientation.
*   **Scale ($s_i$):** A 3D vector $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing anisotropic scaling factors along principal axes.
*   **Opacity ($\omega_i$):** A scalar $\mathbb{R} \in [0, 1]$ representing maximum opacity.
*   **Color ($c_i$):** Represented by Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients (typically degree 3, 48 coefficients for RGB) for view-dependent appearance.

**Covariance Matrix:**
The spatial spread is defined by a 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$, constructed from scale and rotation:
$$ \Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^T R_i^T $$
where $S_i = \text{diag}(s_i)$ is the scaling matrix and $R_i$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q_i$ [S1].

**Projection to 2D:**
During rendering, the 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ is projected to the 2D image plane using the Jacobian $J$ of the affine approximation of the projective transformation:
$$ \Sigma' = J \Sigma_i J^T $$
The relevant part for rasterization is the top-left $2 \times 2$ submatrix $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}$ [S1].

**Spatial Response (Kernel Function):**
The contribution of a Gaussian to a pixel at screen coordinates $x$ is determined by:
$$ R(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu')^T (\Sigma'_{2 \times 2})^{-1} (x - \mu')\right) $$
where $\mu'$ is the projected 2D mean [S1].

**Alpha Compositing:**
The final opacity $\alpha$ contributed by a Gaussian is:
$$ \alpha = \omega_i \cdot R(x) $$
The pixel color is computed via alpha blending with primitives sorted back-to-front:
$$ C_{pixel} = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i C_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j < i} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
where $C_i$ is evaluated using SH coefficients based on view direction [S1].

**Truncation:**
To optimize speed, fragments are discarded if $\alpha < \tau_\alpha$ (typically $1/255$), limiting rendering to a region of approximately $3.33\sigma$ [S1].

**Density Estimation for Mesh Extraction:**
The scene density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is the superposition of Gaussian contributions:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^T \Sigma_i^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)\right) $$
where $\alpha_i$ is opacity, $\mathbf{p}_i$ is the mean, and $\Sigma_i$ is the covariance [S1].

#### Model-based depth estimation for volume parameter update
*   **Step:** During optimization, use Eq. (6) to extract depth values $z$ from the current scene estimate. These depth values are then used to update the medium-parameter estimates ($B_\infty, B_b$) [S1].
*   **Formula:** Depth extraction from 3DGS: $z = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^M d_i \alpha_i T_i}{\sum_{i=1}^M \alpha_i T_i}$. Here $d_i$ is the depth of Gaussian $i$. This is computed at each pixel during rendering [S1].

#### Scale Constrained by Mesh Geometry
*   **Definition:** A constraint to prevent discrepancies between Gaussian Splat scales and the scales of corresponding triangular mesh faces by bounding the relative scale coefficients [S2].
*   **Formula:** $s = s_{rel} \odot s_0$, where $s$ is the final scale, $s_{rel} = (s_{rel,1}, s_{rel,2}) \in [s_{min}, s_{max}] \times [s_{min}, s_{max}]$ is the coefficient vector, $s_0$ is the predefined scale derived from the mesh faces, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S2].
*   **Parameter:** $s_{min}, s_{max}$: The lower and upper bounds for the relative scale coefficients $s_{rel}$ [S2].

#### Normal Estimation via Back-propagation
*   **Definition:** A method to solve for the normal attribute $n$ for each 3D Gaussian by optimizing it from an initial random vector via back-propagation, perceiving the depth of all Gaussians along a ray as a distribution [S3].
*   **Step:** Determine the normal for each pixel by estimating the expectation of the depth distribution along the ray [S3].
*   **Formula:** Normal consistency loss: $\mathcal {L}_{n}=\|\mathcal {N}-\tilde {\mathcal {N}}\|_2$ [S3].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [G]. Subsequent work has focused on extracting explicit geometry from these soft primitives. Mesh extraction techniques like SuGaR regularize Gaussians to align with surfaces during training [G]. Normal estimation methods optimize normals via back-propagation to enforce surface consistency [S3]. Depth-based parameter updates for volumetric rendering were explored in underwater reconstruction contexts [S1]. Scale constraints derived from mesh geometry have been proposed to maintain structural fidelity in joint optimization frameworks [S2].
