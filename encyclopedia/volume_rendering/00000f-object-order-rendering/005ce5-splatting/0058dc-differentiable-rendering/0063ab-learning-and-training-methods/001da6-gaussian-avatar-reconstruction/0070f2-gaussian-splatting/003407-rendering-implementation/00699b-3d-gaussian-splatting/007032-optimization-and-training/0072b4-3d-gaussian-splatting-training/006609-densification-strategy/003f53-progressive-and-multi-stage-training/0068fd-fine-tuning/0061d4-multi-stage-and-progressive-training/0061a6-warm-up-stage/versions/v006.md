# Warm-up stage
Also called: 3D Gaussian Initialization (Warm-up)

## Overview
The warm-up stage is a training adaptation designed to stabilize the optimization of dynamic scene representations, specifically 4D Gaussian Splatting, by decoupling the initialization of static geometry from dynamic deformation [S2]. It addresses convergence difficulties that arise when training 4D-GS directly in the absence of Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point initialization [S2]. Additionally, in the context of distractor suppression, a warm-up phase is employed to improve the model's ability to ignore transient artifacts before final refinement [S1]. It sets apart from direct optimization by establishing a robust baseline of 3D Gaussians, thereby preventing the deformation network from being overwhelmed by large deformation learning pressures [S2].

## How it works
The method operates in two primary contexts: stabilizing 4D-GS training via explicit 3D Gaussian initialization [S2], and improving semantic robustness in distractor suppression [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Video frames of a dynamic scene; optionally, no SfM points [S2].
*   **Outputs**: Initialized 3D Gaussian parameters ($\Gamma$) and a stable deformation field ($\Delta$) [S2]; improved semantic feature alignment for masking [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **3D Gaussians ($\Gamma$)**: Set of Gaussians defined by position, scale, rotation, opacity, and color.
*   **Deformation Network ($\Delta$)**: MLPs $\{\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s\}$ that decode spatial-temporal features from encoder $\mathcal{H}$ to predict changes in position, rotation, and scale [S2].

### Steps
1.  **Initialization**: If SfM points are absent, initialize 3D Gaussians randomly or via a sparse heuristic [S2].
2.  **Warm-up Optimization**: Optimize the 3D Gaussian parameters $\Gamma$ while keeping the deformation network $\Delta$ fixed or lightly constrained [S2].
    *   This step allows the model to learn proper static geometry [S2].
    *   It releases the pressure on the 4D Gaussians to learn large deformations immediately [S2].
    *   It suggests deformation fields that pay attention to the dynamic parts of the scene [S2].
    *   It avoids numeric errors in optimizing the Gaussian deformation network $\Delta$ [S2].
3.  **Transition**: Switch to full 4D-GS training, where both $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ are optimized simultaneously [S2].
4.  **Distractor Suppression (Optional)**: In methods like SpotLessSplats, a warm-up stage is used alongside other adaptations (GLO, Bernoulli sampling) to improve semantic feature learning for transient distractor suppression [S1].

### Formulas
*   **Deformation Decoder**: Changes in 3D Gaussians are explained by separate MLPs:
    $$ \mathcal{G}' = \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}(x, t)) $$
    where $\mathcal{D}$ is the Gaussian deformation decoder, $\mathcal{H}$ is the spatial-temporal structure encoder, and $x, t$ are spatial and temporal coordinates [S2].
*   **Deformation Components**:
    $$ \Delta \in \{\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s\} $$
    where $\phi_x, \phi_r, \phi_s$ predict position, rotation, and scale changes respectively [S2].

### Parameters
*   **Warm-up Duration**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Learning Rate**: Not given in the sources.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Absence of SfM Points**: Direct training of 4D-GS without SfM initialization causes convergence difficulty [S2].
*   **Underfitting Details**: Without proper 3D Gaussian motion modeling, excessive stretching may lead to underfitting of details [S2].
*   **Numeric Errors**: Optimizing the deformation network $\Delta$ directly without a warm-up can lead to numeric instability [S2].

### Pseudocode
```python
def warm_up_stage(scene_data, has_sfm=False):
    """
    Performs the warm-up stage for 4D Gaussian Splatting.
    
    Args:
        scene_data: List of video frames.
        has_sfm: Boolean indicating if SfM points are available.
        
    Returns:
        Initialized 3D Gaussians and deformation network parameters.
    """
    if not has_sfm:
        # Initialize 3D Gaussians randomly or via sparse heuristic
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians_randomly(scene_data)
    else:
        # Initialize from SfM points
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians_from_sfm(scene_data)
    
    # Initialize deformation network
    deformation_network = initialize_deformation_network()
    
    # Step 1: Optimize 3D Gaussians only (Warm-up)
    # Keep deformation network fixed or lightly constrained
    for step in range(warm_up_steps):
        loss = compute_rendering_loss(gaussians, scene_data)
        # Backpropagate only to gaussians
        update_parameters(gaussians, loss)
        
    # Step 2: Transition to full 4D-GS training
    # Optimize both gaussians and deformation network
    for step in range(full_training_steps):
        # Decode deformed gaussians
        deformed_gaussians = deformation_network(gaussians, time_steps)
        loss = compute_rendering_loss(deformed_gaussians, scene_data)
        # Backpropagate to both gaussians and deformation network
        update_parameters(gaussians, loss)
        update_parameters(deformation_network, loss)
        
    return gaussians, deformation_network
```

## Subtopics

### 3D Gaussian Initialization (Warm-up)
A specific variant where the warm-up stage optimizes 3D Gaussians before training 4D-GS directly [S2]. It is chosen when SfM points initialization is absent to prevent convergence difficulty [S2]. It keeps some 3D Gaussians in the dynamic part to release pressure of large deformation learning by 4D Gaussians, learns proper 3D Gaussians $\Gamma$, and suggests deformation fields to pay more attention to the dynamic part [S2]. It avoids numeric errors in optimizing the Gaussian deformation network $\Delta$ and keeps training stable [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance Impact**: Training without the warm-up coarse stage yields PSNR 31.91 dB, SSIM 0.97, LPIPS 0.03, training time 7.5 mins, 79 FPS, and 18 MB storage [S2].
*   **Complexity**: The warm-up stage adds an initial optimization phase, increasing total training time but improving convergence stability [S2].
*   **Strengths**: Prevents convergence difficulty in the absence of SfM points; stabilizes deformation network optimization; improves handling of large deformations [S2].
*   **Limitations**: Adds an extra training stage; requires careful tuning of the transition between warm-up and full training [S2].

## Background
The warm-up stage was introduced in the context of 4D Gaussian Splatting for real-time dynamic scene rendering [S2]. It addresses the challenge of training dynamic scenes without reliable SfM initialization [S2]. The method is also used in distractor suppression methods like SpotLessSplats to improve semantic feature learning [S1].
