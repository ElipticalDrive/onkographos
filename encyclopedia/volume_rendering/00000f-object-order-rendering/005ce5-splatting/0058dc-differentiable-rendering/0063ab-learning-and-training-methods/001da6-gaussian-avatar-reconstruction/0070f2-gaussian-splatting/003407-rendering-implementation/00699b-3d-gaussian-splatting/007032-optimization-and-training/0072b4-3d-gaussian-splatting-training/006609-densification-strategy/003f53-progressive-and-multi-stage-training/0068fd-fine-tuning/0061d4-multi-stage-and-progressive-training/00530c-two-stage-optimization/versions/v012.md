# Two-stage Optimization

Also called: Full-Resolution Refinement, GS-IR Training Process, Low-Resolution Optimization

## Overview
Two-stage optimization is a training strategy that partitions the optimization of 3D Gaussian representations into distinct phases to stabilize convergence and improve reconstruction quality. It addresses the difficulty of simultaneously optimizing geometric structure, appearance, and compression or material properties in a single monolithic loop. By decoupling these objectives, the method first establishes a stable coarse representation (often at low resolution or without codec constraints) and then refines it with fine-grained details or specific physical properties [S1, S2][S3, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
The specific implementation of two-stage optimization varies by application domain (CT reconstruction, inverse rendering, or compression), but all variants share the core principle of sequential initialization.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images (or projections), camera poses, and optionally depth maps or lighting conditions.
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ with optimized parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color/material coefficients) and, in compression variants, a compact bitstream or latent code.

### Data Structures
The Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}$ typically consists of primitives defined by:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}$
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}$
*   Rotation $\mathbf{q}$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity $\alpha$
*   Appearance: Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients or material properties (albedo, roughness, etc.) [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].

### Steps and Formulas

#### Variant 1: Low-Resolution to Full-Resolution (CT Reconstruction)
Used in 3DGR-CT for sparse-view CT reconstruction [S1].

1.  **Stage 1: Low-Resolution Optimization**
    *   Sample a low-resolution grid, specifically $1/2 \times 1/2 \times 1/2$ of the original volume.
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters to place them in approximately correct positions.
    *   **Loss:** Minimize the L2 norm between projected data $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and actual measurements $\mathbf{p}$:
        $$ \min_{\theta_i} \sum_{i=1}^{N} || \hat{\mathbf{p}}[\mathcal{V}(\cdot|\{\theta_i\}_{i=1}^N)] - \mathbf{p} ||_2^2 $$
        where $\mathcal{V}$ is the discretized volume generated by the Gaussians [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Full-Resolution Refinement**
    *   Initialize from Stage 1 checkpoint.
    *   Sample over all voxels of the original volume.
    *   Refine Gaussian parameters for fine-grained adjustments.
    *   Apply the same L2 projection loss to capture higher-quality reconstruction [S1].

#### Variant 2: Initial Geometry/Appearance then Material Decomposition (Inverse Rendering)
Used in GS-IR for inverse rendering [S2].

1.  **Stage 1: Initial Optimization (30,000 iterations)**
    *   Optimize geometric parameters $\mathbf{T} = \{\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{n}\}$ (position, scale, rotation, normal) and appearance $\mathbf{A}$ (color).
    *   **Loss:** Combine color reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_c$ and normal loss $\mathcal{L}_n$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{init} = \mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_n $$
    *   **Normal Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_n = \mathcal{L}_{n-p} + \lambda_{n-TV} \text{TV}_{normal} $$
        where $\text{TV}_{normal}$ is a total variation smoothing term for normals [S2].
    *   **Optimizer:** Adam [S2].

2.  **Stage 2: Decomposition (10,000 iterations)**
    *   Fix $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{A}$.
    *   Optimize material parameters $\mathbf{M} = \{\text{albedo}, \text{roughness}, \text{lighting}\}$.
    *   **Loss:** Minimize decomposition loss $\mathcal{L}_d$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_d = \mathcal{L}_{shade} + \lambda_M \text{TV}_{mat} + \lambda_E \text{TV}_{light} $$
    *   **Parameters:** $\lambda_{n-TV} = 5.0$, $\lambda_M = 1.0$, $\lambda_E = 0.01$ [S2].

#### Variant 3: Codec-Free then Codec-Aware (Compression)
Used in CodecSplat for ultra-compact latent coding [S3, S3, unofficial].

1.  **Stage 1: Codec-Free Gaussian Generation**
    *   Train the feed-forward Gaussian generation pipeline *without* the feature codec.
    *   Predict Gaussian set $\mathcal{G}_{cf}$ from refined feature $\mathbf{F}_c$ and encoder-side depth $\mathbf{D}_{enc}$.
    *   Render target view $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf} = \text{Render}(\mathcal{G}_{cf}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$.
    *   **Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage1} = \mathcal{D}_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{tcf}, \mathbf{Y}_t) $$
        where $\mathbf{Y}_t$ is the target-view image and $\mathcal{D}_{render}$ is the rendering reconstruction loss (typically L1+SSIM) [S3, S3, unofficial].

2.  **Stage 2: Codec-Aware Latent Coding**
    *   Initialize from Stage 1 checkpoint.
    *   Enable the feature codec and post-codec refiner.
    *   Optimize codec-related modules while keeping remaining backbone modules fixed.
    *   Render decoded Gaussian set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$ to target view: $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t = \text{Render}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}, \mathbf{K}_t, \mathbf{T}_t)$.
    *   **Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{stage2}(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{max}} \left[ \mathcal{D}_{render}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_t, \mathbf{Y}_t) + \gamma \mathcal{D}_{codec-depth} + \beta R \right] $$
        where $R$ is the estimated rate, $\lambda \in [16, 1024]$ is sampled during training (fixed at test time), $\lambda_{max} = 1024$, $\gamma = 0.1$, and $\beta$ is the rate penalty weight ($10^{-4}$ for DL3DV, $4 \times 10^{-4}$ for RE10K) [S3, S3, unofficial].
    *   **Codec-Side Depth Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{D}_{codec-depth} = || \mathbf{S} - \mathbf{S}_{enc} ||_1 $$
        where $\mathbf{S}$ is the decoder-side normalized inverse-depth map and $\mathbf{S}_{enc}$ is the supervision target:
        $$ \mathbf{S}_{enc,i}(p) = \frac{\mathbf{D}_{enc,i}(p)^{-1} - f_i^{-1}}{n_i^{-1} - f_i^{-1}} $$
        with $n_i, f_i$ being near and far bounds of context view $i$ [S3, S3, unofficial].

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Learning Rate:** Adam optimizer is standard. CodecSplat uses initial LR $10^{-4}$ and weight decay 0.01 [S3, S3, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** GS-IR uses a single NVIDIA V100 [S2]. CodecSplat uses four NVIDIA RTX 5880 48GB GPUs [S3, S3, unofficial].
*   **Pitfalls:** Directly training with a quantized bottleneck is unstable due to the need to adapt the Gaussian generation pipeline and learn a compact latent representation simultaneously [S3, S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two-stage optimization generally increases total training time compared to single-stage methods due to the sequential nature, but Stage 1 (low-res or codec-free) is often faster, allowing rapid convergence to a good initial state.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low-resolution stages (Variant 1) reduce memory footprint during the initial phase. Codec-aware stages (Variant 3) require memory for the codec modules and latent representations.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **GS-IR:** Achieves high-quality inverse rendering results with stable material decomposition [S2].
    *   **CodecSplat:** Achieves KiB-level scene bitstreams while preserving dominant scene structure, outperforming MiB-level compressed baselines [S3, S3, unofficial].
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Provides accurate sparse-view CT reconstruction by avoiding local minima associated with high-resolution initialization [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes training, prevents gradient interference between conflicting objectives (e.g., geometry vs. compression), and allows for specialized optimization strategies in each stage.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage transitions and loss weights. Not given in the sources: specific convergence criteria for switching stages.

## Background
*   **Origin:** The concept of multi-stage optimization in Gaussian Splatting has emerged alongside specific applications requiring complex parameter sets (materials, compression).
    *   **GS-IR:** Introduced by [S2] for inverse rendering, separating geometry/appearance from material/lighting.
    *   **CodecSplat:** Introduced by [S3, S3, unofficial] to handle the instability of end-to-end training with entropy-coded bottlenecks.
    *   **3DGR-CT:** Introduced by [S1] for CT reconstruction, leveraging low-resolution initialization for sparse data.
*   **History:** Builds on the general principle of progressive training in neural rendering, adapted specifically for the discrete and non-differentiable nature of Gaussian primitives and compression codecs.
