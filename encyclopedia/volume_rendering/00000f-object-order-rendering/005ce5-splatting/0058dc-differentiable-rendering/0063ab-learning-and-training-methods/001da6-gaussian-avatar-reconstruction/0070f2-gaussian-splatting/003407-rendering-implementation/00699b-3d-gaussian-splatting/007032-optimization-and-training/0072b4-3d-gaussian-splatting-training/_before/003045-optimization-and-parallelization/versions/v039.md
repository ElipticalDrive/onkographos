# Optimization and Parallelization

Also called: Distributed Training, Per-Splat Backpropagation, Mask Gradient Computation

## Overview
This family encompasses the computational strategies required to train 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models efficiently at scale. It addresses the bottleneck of differentiable rasterization by optimizing gradient computation, managing memory across distributed hardware, and handling specialized loss terms such as probabilistic masks. It is distinguished from standard optimization by its focus on hardware-aware parallelism (per-splat vs. per-pixel) and distributed data partitioning, rather than just hyperparameter tuning.

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the following foundational mechanisms:

1.  **Differentiable Rasterization Core**: All methods rely on the differentiable alpha-blending rasterizer. The forward pass computes pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ via depth-sorted alpha blending, and the backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to Gaussian parameters $\theta$ [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
2.  **Gradient Accumulation**: Gradients are accumulated over multiple training steps or views before an optimizer update. In distributed settings, gradients or intermediate rendering states are synchronized across devices [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
3.  **Adaptive Representation**: Many optimization variants modify the Gaussian representation or its distribution to improve efficiency or fidelity, requiring corresponding adjustments to the gradient computation or data flow [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].
4.  **Hardware Acceleration**: Methods leverage GPU parallelism, often using CUDA kernels, Tensor Cores (via GEMM reformulation), or half-precision arithmetic to accelerate the rasterization and gradient computation [6233-optimization-and-parallelization].

## Subtopics

### [MaskGaussian Gradient Computation](4254-maskgaussian-gradient-computation.md)
A method for computing gradients through masked alpha-blending. It explicitly accounts for the gradient contribution of the mask on both the foreground Gaussian color accumulation and the background transmittance path. Choose this when training with probabilistic masks that modulate Gaussian opacity.

### [Training Pipeline](5325-training-pipeline.md)
The iterative optimization loop that initializes Gaussians and optimizes parameters via gradient descent, coupled with periodic refinement phases (pruning, cloning, splitting) that modify the Gaussian count. Choose this as the standard training structure for 3DGS reconstruction.

### [Parallelization for Backpropagation](5989-parallelization-for-backpropagation.md)
A training optimization that replaces the standard per-pixel backward pass with a per-splat (per-Gaussian) approach. It iterates over Gaussians and updates the gradients of the pixels they cover, addressing the performance bottleneck of atomic contention in the per-pixel approach. Choose this to reduce training time by 4–5× while maintaining numerical equivalence.

### [Grendel-GS](7349-grendel-gs.md)
A distributed optimization framework that partitions the Gaussian primitive list across multiple GPUs to overcome single-device memory limits. Unlike single-machine streaming methods, it distributes the parameter table across a GPU cluster. Choose this for scenes with billions of primitives requiring multi-GPU parallelism.

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Implementation Details](9128-3d-gaussian-splatting-implementation-details.md)
Details the specific hyperparameters, data structures, and optimization routines (densification, pruning, half-precision rasterization) required to implement the training pipeline. Choose this for the practical reference on bridging theoretical formulation and executable code.

## Background
The optimization and parallelization techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged alongside the original 3DGS paper  and subsequent works addressing its scalability and training efficiency. The standard training pipeline relies on the differentiable rasterizer introduced in the original work [6233-optimization-and-parallelization]. Variants like MaskGaussian  and Grendel-GS  were developed to handle specific constraints such as masked supervision and large-scale distributed training. The per-splat backward pass  was introduced to mitigate the atomic contention issues inherent in the original per-pixel gradient accumulation.
