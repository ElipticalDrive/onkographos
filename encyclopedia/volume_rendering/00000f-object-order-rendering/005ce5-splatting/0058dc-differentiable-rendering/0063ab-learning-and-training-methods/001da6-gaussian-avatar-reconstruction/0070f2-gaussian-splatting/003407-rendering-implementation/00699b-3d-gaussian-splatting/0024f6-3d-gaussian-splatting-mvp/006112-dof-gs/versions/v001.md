# DOF-GS

Also called: Adjustable Depth-of-Field 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
DOF-GS is a 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that incorporates a finite-aperture camera model to explicitly model and render depth-of-field (DOF) effects, enabling post-capture control of aperture and focal distance [S1]. Unlike previous NeRF-based techniques that simulate defocus via expensive per-ray blur kernels or recent 3DGS methods that use black-box neural networks for shape offsets, DOF-GS blurs individual Gaussians based on learnable Circle-of-Confusion (CoC) cues before compositing, allowing efficient, differentiable, and intuitive DOF adjustment [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A set of multi-view images with moderate defocus blur.
- **Outputs:** A reconstructed 3D scene representation $\mathcal{S}$ consisting of anisotropic 3D Gaussian points, and optimized camera parameters (aperture and focal distance) per view.

**Scene Representation:**
The scene is represented by a set of $K$ anisotropic 3D Gaussian points $\{G_k\}_{k=1}^K$ [S1]. Each Gaussian $G_k$ is defined by:
- Center position $u_k$
- Covariance matrix $\Sigma_k$, derived from anisotropic scaling $s_k$ and quaternion $q_k$
- Opacity $o_k$
- Spherical harmonics coefficients $h_k$

The 3D Gaussian function is defined as [S1]:
$$ G_k(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(p - u_k)^T \Sigma_k^{-1} (p - u_k)\right) $$

**Projection:**
3D Gaussian points are projected to 2D screen space [S1]. The Jacobian $J$ of the projective transformation is calculated. The 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'_k$ is computed as [S1]:
$$ \Sigma'_k = J W \Sigma_k W^T J^T $$
where $W$ is the view matrix transforming points from world space to camera space. The 2D Gaussian in screen space is [S1]:
$$ G'_k(x) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x - u'_k)^T \Sigma'^{-1}_k (x - u'_k)\right) $$
where $u'_k$ is the 2D center position post-projection.

**Standard Rasterization:**
For each pixel $x$, a small number $N_x$ of Gaussians within a cutoff radius $t$ are associated with it. The image is rendered using alpha blending [S1]:
$$ \tilde{I}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_x} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
where $\alpha_i = G'_i(x)$, $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance, and $c_i$ is the view-dependent color of the $i$-th Gaussian.

**DOF Rendering:**
A differentiable DOF rendering process is integrated into the rasterizer [S1]. Individual Gaussians are blurred based on learnable CoC cues before compositing [S1]. This process is fully differentiable, allowing optimization of camera parameters alongside the 3D scene [S1].

**CoC Calculation:**
The radius of the Circle-of-Confusion (CoC) $R_{coc}$ for an object point at depth $z_o$ is determined by the focal length $F$, aperture diameter $A$, and focal distance $f$ [S1]:
$$ R_{coc} = \frac{1}{2} F A \frac{|z_o - f|}{z_o (f - F)} $$
Since $F$ is often much smaller than $f$ and object depths, this is simplified to [S1]:
$$ R_{coc} = \frac{1}{2} Q \left| \frac{1}{z_o} - \frac{1}{f} \right| $$
where $Q = FA$ is a simplified aperture parameter.

**CoC-Guided Detail Enhancement:**
A lightweight CoC-guided neural network predicts an in-focus mask for each view [S1]. These masks are used to enhance details within All-in-Focus (AiF) renderings during optimization, counteracting reduced sharpness due to defocus blur in multi-view inputs [S1].

**Optimization:**
The scene representation $\mathcal{S}$ and camera parameters are optimized by minimizing the reconstruction loss $L_{rec}$ between rendered views $\tilde{I}_m$ and training views $I_m$ [S1]:
$$ \min_{\mathcal{S}} \sum_{m=1}^M L_{rec}(\tilde{I}_m, I_m) $$
The loss is a weighted combination of MSE and DSSIM [S1]:
$$ L_{rec}(I_a, I_b) = ||I_a - I_b|| + \lambda_{dssim} L_{dssim}(I_a, I_b) $$

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Rendering is efficient, leveraging the standard 3DGS rasterization pipeline with additional CoC-based blurring. This is significantly faster than NeRF-based defocus methods which require hours to days of training and yield <1 FPS [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Stores standard 3DGS attributes plus learnable camera parameters ($A, f$) per view and a lightweight neural network for CoC-guided detail enhancement [S1].
- **Strengths:** Enables post-capture control of aperture and focal distance; differentiable optimization allows learning camera characteristics from defocused images; efficient compared to ray-tracing based defocus methods [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires multi-view defocused images for training; relies on a simplified thin lens model [S1].
- **When to use:** When post-capture DOF control and refocusing from multi-view defocused inputs is required.
- **When not to use:** For scenes without defocus blur where standard pinhole 3DGS is sufficient, or when real-time performance on extremely low-end hardware is critical without the overhead of the CoC-guided network.

## Background
DOF-GS was introduced to address the limitations of the pinhole camera model in existing 3DGS methods, which lack effective modeling of defocus effects [S1]. It builds on the 3DGS framework [8] and surface-splatting techniques [10], extending them with a finite-aperture camera model [S1]. The method was tested on scenes from the multi-view defocus blur dataset [15].
