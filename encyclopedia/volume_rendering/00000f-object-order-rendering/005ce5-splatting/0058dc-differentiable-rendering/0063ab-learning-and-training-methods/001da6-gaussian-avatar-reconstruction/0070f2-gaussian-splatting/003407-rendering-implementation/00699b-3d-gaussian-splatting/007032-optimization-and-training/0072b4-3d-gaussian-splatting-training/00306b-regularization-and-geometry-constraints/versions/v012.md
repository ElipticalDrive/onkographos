# Regularization and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and Geometry Constraints are training-time techniques for 3D Gaussian Splatting that prevent over-reconstruction, reduce floaters, and enforce geometric fidelity by modifying loss functions, covariance structures, or supervision signals. Unlike [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md), which relies on standard photometric losses, these methods introduce structural priors (such as frequency spectra, depth consistency, or surface normals) to guide densification and shape optimization. They set themselves apart from [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on geometric stability and artifact reduction rather than physical light transport accuracy.

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of constraining the optimization trajectory of 3D Gaussians to produce cleaner, more geometrically plausible scenes. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Auxiliary Loss Terms:** Adding terms to the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg}$ that penalize deviations from desired geometric properties (e.g., depth consistency, frequency alignment, or normal alignment) [Parent, S2].
2.  **Parameter Freezing/Constraining:** Restricting updates to specific Gaussian parameters (e.g., freezing scale/rotation during depth regularization) to prevent shape deformation from conflicting with geometric priors [S1].
3.  **Structural Transformation:** Modifying the Gaussian covariance matrix directly to enforce physical constraints, such as flattening into surfels or projecting into half-ellipsoids [S1].
4.  **Frequency/Scale Awareness:** Using multi-scale or frequency-domain information to guide densification, ensuring coarse geometry is learned before fine details [S2][S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md)
Frequency Regularization minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of rendered and ground truth images to alleviate over-reconstruction. It sets itself apart by operating in the frequency domain to increase gradients in over-reconstructed areas, guiding adaptive densification toward high-frequency details.

### [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md)
Smoothed 2D Supervision enhances robustness by applying a scheduled 2D Gaussian blur to training images, stabilizing gradients and restricting the learned scene's spectrum. It sets itself apart by operating directly on the 2D image domain rather than modifying positional encodings or loss weights in the frequency domain [G].

### [3D Half-Gaussian Splatting Covariance Transformation and Regularization](9150-3d-half-gaussian-splatting-covariance-transformation-and-reg.md)
3D Half-Gaussian Splatting modifies the standard 3D Gaussian kernel into a half-ellipsoid to better model sharp surface boundaries and occlusions. It achieves this by transforming the covariance matrix into a local basis aligned with a splitting plane normal, projecting the covariance to enforce near-zero variance along the normal direction, and then transforming it back to world space. This method builds on [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md) by applying a structural constraint to the Gaussian parameters themselves rather than an auxiliary loss term [G].

### [Hard and Soft Depth Regularization](9178-hard-and-soft-depth-regularization.md)
Hard and Soft Depth Regularization prevents detail loss and blurred appearance by constraining geometry updates to spatial reshaping (movement of centers) rather than deforming Gaussian shapes. It sets itself apart from standard depth regularization methods, which often force anisotropic Gaussians to fit smooth monocular depth estimates, by freezing scale and rotation parameters and using two distinct rendered depth maps to independently update center positions and opacities. This approach balances the fitting of complex color appearance with smooth coarse depth constraints, addressing the sensitivity of Gaussian radiance fields to small depth errors.

### [Cross-scale geometric adaptation](10796-cross-scale-geometric-adaptation.md)
Cross-scale geometric adaptation is a self-adaptive regularization technique that uses reprojection errors across multiple voxel scales to guide geometry learning without requiring explicit depth ground truth. It selects render depths with minimal reprojection error across different scales as pseudo labels, ensuring geometric integrity and reducing floaters more effectively than single-scale depth priors. Unlike siblings that rely on fixed schedules or external priors, this method dynamically adapts to scene frequencies by leveraging low-resolution voxels for coarse geometry early in training and high-resolution voxels for detail refinement later.

### [Geometry Regularization](14222-geometry-regularization.md)
Geometry regularization improves the geometric fidelity and smoothness of 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes by constraining the shape and orientation of the Gaussian kernels, distinct from depth supervision which only constrains their spatial location. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Depth Regularization](7616-depth-regularization.md) by enforcing structural constraints (flattening and normal alignment) rather than positional errors, and from [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) by operating in the spatial domain to reduce floaters and improve surface continuity.

#### Wild-GS
- [S1] A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry.

#### Source Feature Extrapolation
- [S2] A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images.

#### FreGS
- [S4, unofficial] Applies progressive frequency regularization to primitives to prevent overfitting and high-frequency artifacts during training.

## Background
The need for regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the tendency of naive optimization to produce "floaters" (unstructured density clouds) and over-reconstruction in well-textured regions [S2][S4, unofficial]. Early works like FreGS [78] and Faster-GS [79] addressed training instability and high-frequency artifacts through frequency regularization and gradient rescaling [S4, unofficial]. Subsequent research introduced geometric priors, such as SDF constraints [S3] and depth-guided sampling [S2], to recover high-quality surfaces. Methods like Wild-GS [S1] and SWAG [S1] further enhanced robustness by using visibility maps and feature alignment to mitigate transient occlusions. The field has evolved from simple photometric losses to complex multi-scale and frequency-aware constraints to balance appearance fidelity with geometric correctness [S1, S2, S3][S4, unofficial].
