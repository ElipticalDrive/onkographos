# Pruning and Density Control

Also called: Gaussian Pruning, Density Control, Adaptive Pruning

## Overview
Pruning and Density Control methods reduce the computational cost and memory footprint of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by selectively removing redundant or low-importance Gaussian primitives during or after training. Unlike densification methods that add primitives to capture detail, this family focuses on compression and efficiency. It differs from [Optimization Schedule and Densification](15107-optimization-schedule-and-densification.md) by prioritizing the reduction of primitive count over the schedule of creation, and from [DropGaussian](16856-dropgaussian.md) by using deterministic importance scores rather than stochastic regularization for compression.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the standard 3DGS primitive representation:
*   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Rotation $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$ (Spherical Harmonics)

The core mechanism involves calculating an importance score for each Gaussian and removing those below a threshold or ranking. The optimization complexity is defined as the product of rendering resolution and primitive count, which dominates time costs . Methods typically employ a "concave-up" growth curve for primitives, where growth accelerates over time, synchronized with increasing rendering resolution to fit high-frequency details only after low-frequency structures are established .

## Subtopics

### [Optimization Schedule and Densification](15107-optimization-schedule-and-densification.md)
This family addresses the inefficiency of standard 3DGS training by controlling the rate of primitive creation and resolution. It differs from siblings like [Pruning and Density Control](16215-pruning-and-density-control.md) by focusing on the *schedule* and *criteria* for adding primitives, and from [3D Gaussian Splatting Training Loop](15139-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-loop.md) by providing specific algorithms for dynamic resolution and adaptive budgeting rather than just the loop structure.

### [Reconstruction-aware Pruning Scheduler](16199-reconstruction-aware-pruning-scheduler.md)
The Reconstruction-aware Pruning Scheduler (RPS) is a dynamic pruning strategy that adaptively determines pruning timing and refining intervals based on reconstruction quality, addressing the instability of fixed-schedule pruning. Unlike previous heuristic pruning methods that perform pruning at fixed iterations and with a preset ratio, this approach performs adaptive, reconstruction-aware pruning, leading to more efficient compression without sacrificing quality.

### [DropGaussian](16856-dropgaussian.md)
DropGaussian is a structural regularization technique for sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting that randomly removes a subset of Gaussians during the training process to alleviate overfitting to the limited number of training views. It sets itself apart from prior-based methods (which use external depth signals or 2D generative models) by being prior-free, requiring no additional computational costs, external data, or complex modules. Unlike standard 3DGS, which suffers from overfitting in sparse-view settings due to low gradient feedback for occluded or distant Gaussians, DropGaussian improves the visibility and gradient magnitude of remaining Gaussians by temporarily excluding closer ones.

### Deformation-Aware Pruning
**Definition**
A pruning technique that identifies and removes redundant Gaussians by gauging their impact on deformation, distinguishing between stable and deformed Gaussians [S1].

### Spatio-spectral Pruning Score
**Definition**
An importance ranking mechanism that incorporates spectral information into the importance ranking of Gaussian primitives to enable stable pruning [S2, unofficial]. It measures the importance of a Gaussian primitive in both spatial and spectral domains [S2, unofficial]. A criterion for evaluating the importance of each Gaussian by measuring its contribution from both spatial and spectral (frequency) perspectives to prevent pruning of primitives essential for sharp structures [S2, unofficial].

**Step**
Iteratively remove low-score points that are relatively unimportant across both spatial and spectral domains [S2, unofficial]. Combine the normalized spatial score and spectral score using weighting coefficients $\lambda_s$ and $\lambda_f$ to obtain the final Spatio-spectral Pruning Score $\tilde{U}_i^*$ [S2, unofficial].

**Formula**
$$ \tilde{U}_i^* = \lambda_s \frac{(\nabla_{g_i} I_G)^2}{\|\tilde{U}_s\|^2} + \lambda_f \frac{(\nabla_{g_i} \text{FFT}(I_G))^2}{\|\tilde{U}_f\|^2} $$
where $\tilde{U}_i^*$ is the Spatio-spectral Pruning Score, $\lambda_s$ and $\lambda_f$ are weighting coefficients balancing spatial and frequency contributions, and $\|\tilde{U}_s\|$ and $\|\tilde{U}_f\|$ are normalization terms for spatial and frequency scores respectively [S2, unofficial].

**Parameter**
$\lambda_s$: Weighting coefficient for the spatial-domain gradient term [S2, unofficial].
$\lambda_f$: Weighting coefficient for the frequency-domain gradient term [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The need for pruning arises from the redundancy inherent in 3D Gaussian Splatting, where thousands of primitives are often required to represent a scene, leading to high memory and computational costs. Early 3DGS implementations used fixed thresholds for splitting and cloning, often leading to redundant primitives or slow convergence . Subsequent research introduced adaptive strategies to manage this density. The Reconstruction-aware Pruning Scheduler was introduced to address the instability of fixed-schedule pruning by linking pruning decisions to reconstruction loss improvements [S1]. Spatio-spectral pruning was proposed to improve the stability of pruning by considering frequency domain information, allowing for significant compression (e.g., 90% fewer primitives) while maintaining quality [S2, unofficial]. Deformation-aware pruning was developed in the context of 4D Gaussian Splatting for surgical scenes to handle dynamic content [S1].
