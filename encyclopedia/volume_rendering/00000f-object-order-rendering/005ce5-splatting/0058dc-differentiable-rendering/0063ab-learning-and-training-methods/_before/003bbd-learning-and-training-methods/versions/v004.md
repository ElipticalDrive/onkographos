# Learning and Training Methods
## Overview
This family covers methods for learning and training 3D Gaussian splatting representations from 2D images [G]. It focuses on optimizing primitive parameters (positions, covariances, opacities, colors) and network weights through differentiable rendering pipelines [G]. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Splatting Without the Blur](1157-splatting-without-the-blur.md) by its reliance on gradient-based learning rather than purely analytical initialization, and from [Rendering Pipelines and Passes](1411-rendering-pipelines-and-passes.md) by its focus on the training loop rather than inference rasterization [G].
## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on differentiable rendering to compute gradients from the rendered image to the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics) and/or network weights [G]. The rendering process remains differentiable with respect to the scene representation [G]. Loss functions typically combine photometric reconstruction error, such as $\mathcal{L}_{L1}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}$, with perceptual or structural metrics like $\mathcal{L}_{MS-SSIM}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{LPIPS}$ [G].
## Subtopics

### [Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction](1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md)
Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction refers to a family of methods that reconstruct animatable, photorealistic 3D human or head avatars by representing them as collections of 3D Gaussians [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md]. Unlike static scene reconstruction, these methods must handle non-rigid deformation, ensuring that the Gaussian primitives move and deform consistently with the underlying subject's pose and expression [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md]. The family is distinguished from static Gaussian Splatting [946-gaussian-splatting.md] by its integration of parametric body models (e.g., SMPL, FLAME) or deformation fields to constrain and drive the Gaussian positions, and from neural radiance field (NeRF) avatars by its explicit, rasterizable representation that enables real-time rendering speeds [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md].

A core principle is the anchoring of Gaussians to a geometric prior. Most methods initialize Gaussians on the surface of a parametric mesh (e.g., SMPL for full body, FLAME for heads) in a canonical rest pose [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md]. During training, these Gaussians are deformed to match the target pose using rigid transformations (e.g., Linear Blend Skinning) and/or non-rigid deformation fields [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md]. This ensures that the avatar can be animated to novel poses while maintaining structural integrity [1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md].

### [Pose-free and Feed-forward Training](6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md)
This family encompasses methods that reconstruct 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes directly from input images without iterative per-scene optimization or reliance on pre-computed Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines [6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md]. Unlike traditional 3DGS which requires minutes to hours of per-scene optimization using known camera poses, these methods use feed-forward neural networks or geometry priors to generate primitives and camera parameters in seconds [6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md]. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Gaussian Avatar Reconstruction](1575-gaussian-avatar-reconstruction.md) by their focus on generalizable scene reconstruction from static, unposed image collections rather than animatable human models [6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md].

### VS-Splat with GenDen Augmentation
VS-Splat integrated with the GenDen add-on model [S1, unofficial]. On CO3D dataset, VS-Splat + GenDen yields PSNR 22.09, SSIM 0.865, LPIPS 0.208 [S1, unofficial].
