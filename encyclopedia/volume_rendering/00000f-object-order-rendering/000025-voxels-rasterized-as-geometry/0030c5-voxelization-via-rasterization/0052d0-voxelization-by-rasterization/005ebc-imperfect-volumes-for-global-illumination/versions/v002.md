# Imperfect Volumes for Global Illumination

Also called: Point-based voxelization for GI, Real-time GI using imperfect volumes

## Overview
Imperfect Volumes is a method for the real-time computation of ambient occlusion (AO) and diffuse indirect light transport in fully dynamic scenes [S1, unofficial]. It operates on a rough approximation of the entire scene created by a fast point-based voxelization algorithm, using 'imperfect volumes' where potential errors are small and do not produce objectionable errors in the final textured image [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry (triangles), volume resolution ($N^3$), number of points per triangle, number of rays per voxel, ray marching step size, and surface properties (diffuse reflectivity $\rho$, normal) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A voxel grid storing directional visibility (for AO) or spherical harmonic coefficients (for GI), and the final rendered pixel colors [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The core data structure is a 3D voxel grid of resolution $N \times N \times N$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Content:** For AO, the grid stores directional visibility instead of incoming radiance [S1, unofficial]. For GI, it stores the incident radiance distribution approximated by spherical harmonics [S1, unofficial].
*   **Buffering:** Two volume buffers are used to compute multiple bounces of light [S1, unofficial]. The method samples illumination from one volume buffer and writes results to another [S1, unofficial]. By alternating between these two buffers in successive passes, multiple bounces of light are computed [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Point Sampling:** For each triangle in the scene, generate random points distributed over the triangle's surface [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Voxelization:** For each generated point, determine the voxel index in the grid that contains the point and mark the voxel as occupied [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Ray Tracing (for GI):** For each occupied voxel, trace rays to sample the environment [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Ray Marching:** Rays are marched through the volume in regular intervals until an occupied cell is found or the volume extent is reached [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Self-Intersection Avoidance:** To avoid self-intersections with the voxel containing the ray origin, the ray-marching starts outside the originating voxel [S1, unofficial]. An initial distance $d_s$ is skipped along each ray [S1, unofficial]. For cubic voxels of size $s_v$, the skipped distance is $d_s = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} s_v$, which is the radius of the bounding sphere of the voxel [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Note:** This scheme does not avoid self-intersections with neighboring voxels potentially generated from the same polygon [S1, unofficial]. These intersections are skipped during the final per-pixel irradiance reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Storage:** For AO, store visibility (coverage) [S1, unofficial]. For GI, store the incident radiance distribution, approximated by spherical harmonics [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Surface Point Shift:** For each visible surface point $x$ with normal $\mathbf{n}$, shift the point outside the current voxel by moving it half a voxel along the normal [S1, unofficial]. This avoids bias from the stored radiance distribution which may be affected by intersections with neighboring voxels from the same surface [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Neighbor Selection:** Identify the closest voxels to the shifted point [S1, unofficial]. For performance reasons, the implementation considers only the six nearest voxels [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Incident Radiance Estimation:** Calculate the estimated incident radiance $\dot{L}_i$ at the shifted point using the radiance of the selected voxels [S1, unofficial]. The formula is:
    $$ \dot{L}_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{vox}} w_j \dot{L}_{ij}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{vox}} w_j} $$
    where $\dot{L}_{ij}$ is the spherical harmonic representation of the radiance from the $j$-th voxel [S1, unofficial].
    The weight $w_j$ is defined as:
    $$ w_j = \begin{cases} \cos \theta_j & \text{if } \theta_j < \pi/2 \\ 0 & \text{if } \theta_j > \pi/2 \end{cases} $$
    where $\theta_j$ is the angle between the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ and the direction to the $j$-th voxel [S1, unofficial]. This ensures voxels behind the surface do not contribute, and voxels facing the normal contribute most [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Irradiance Calculation:** Compute the irradiance integral for the surface point [S1, unofficial]. The diffusely reflected light (radiosity $B$) is given by:
    $$ B(x) = \frac{\rho(x)}{\pi} \int_{\Omega} L_i(x, \omega) \cos \theta d\omega $$
    where $\theta$ is the angle between the surface normal and the incident radiance direction $\omega$ [S1, unofficial]. Since $\dot{L}_i$ is represented using spherical harmonics, this integral is computed as a simple dot product with an analytically computed spherical harmonic representation of the cosine lobe directed towards the surface normal [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Multiple Bounces:** To compute indirect lighting bounces, repeat the volume creation and irradiance reconstruction steps, alternating between the two volume buffers [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
import math

def create_imperfect_volume(scene_triangles, volume_resolution, points_per_triangle, rays_per_voxel, mode='GI'):
    """
    Creates an imperfect volume from scene geometry.
    Args:
        scene_triangles: List of triangles (each with 3 vertices).
        volume_resolution: Integer N, defining an N x N x N grid.
        points_per_triangle: Number of random points to sample per triangle.
        rays_per_voxel: Number of rays to trace per occupied voxel.
        mode: 'AO' or 'GI'.
    Returns:
        volume_buffer: A 3D array of shape (N, N, N) storing voxel data.
    """
    N = volume_resolution
    voxel_size = 1.0 / N
    volume_buffer = [[[None for _ in range(N)] for _ in range(N)] for _ in range(N)]
    
    for triangle in scene_triangles:
        # Source does not specify exact triangle sampling algorithm [G]
        points = sample_points_on_triangle(triangle, points_per_triangle)
        
        for point in points:
            voxel_idx = world_to_voxel_index(point, N)
            if volume_buffer[voxel_idx[0]][voxel_idx[1]][voxel_idx[2]] is None:
                volume_buffer[voxel_idx[0]][voxel_idx[1]][voxel_idx[2]] = {'occupied': False, 'data': None}
            volume_buffer[voxel_idx[0]][voxel_idx[1]][voxel_idx[2]]['occupied'] = True
            
            if mode == 'GI':
                trace_rays_from_voxel(volume_buffer, voxel_idx, rays_per_voxel, voxel_size)
                
    return volume_buffer

def trace_rays_from_voxel(volume_buffer, voxel_idx, rays_per_voxel, voxel_size):
    """
    Traces rays from a voxel to sample incident radiance.
    """
    voxel_center = voxel_index_to_world(voxel_idx, voxel_size)
    skip_distance = (math.sqrt(3) / 2) * voxel_size
    
    for _ in range(rays_per_voxel):
        direction = random_unit_vector()
        ray_origin = [voxel_center[i] + direction[i] * skip_distance for i in range(3)]
        current_pos = ray_origin[:]
        
        while all(0.0 <= p < 1.0 for p in current_pos):
            sample_voxel_idx = world_to_voxel_index(current_pos, len(volume_buffer))
            sample = volume_buffer[sample_voxel_idx[0]][sample_voxel_idx[1]][sample_voxel_idx[2]]
            
            if sample['occupied']:
                # Store incident radiance/visibility in the originating voxel
                # Source does not specify exact SH coefficient update rule [G]
                volume_buffer[voxel_idx[0]][voxel_idx[1]][voxel_idx[2]]['data'] = accumulate_radiance(
                    volume_buffer[voxel_idx[0]][voxel_idx[1]][voxel_idx[2]]['data'], 
                    sample, direction
                )
                break
            
            current_pos = [current_pos[i] + direction[i] * voxel_size for i in range(3)]

def reconstruct_irradiance(surface_point, surface_normal, volume_buffer, volume_resolution):
    """
    Computes the final irradiance for a surface point using the imperfect volume.
    """
    voxel_size = 1.0 / volume_resolution
    shifted_point = [surface_point[i] + surface_normal[i] * (0.5 * voxel_size) for i in range(3)]
    
    # Source specifies using the 6 nearest voxels [S1, unofficial]
    closest_voxels = find_closest_voxels(shifted_point, volume_buffer, num_neighbors=6)
    
    numerator = [0.0] * 10  # Placeholder for SH coefficient array size
    denominator = 0.0
    
    for voxel in closest_voxels:
        direction_to_voxel = [voxel['center'][i] - shifted_point[i] for i in range(3)]
        length = math.sqrt(sum(d**2 for d in direction_to_voxel))
        direction_to_voxel = [d / length for d in direction_to_voxel]
        cos_theta = sum(surface_normal[i] * direction_to_voxel[i] for i in range(3))
        
        weight = cos_theta if cos_theta > 0 else 0.0
            
        voxel_radiance_sh = voxel['data']['sh_coefficients']
        numerator = [numerator[i] + weight * voxel_radiance_sh[i] for i in range(len(numerator))]
        denominator += weight
        
    if denominator > 0:
        estimated_radiance_sh = [numerator[i] / denominator for i in range(len(numerator))]
    else:
        estimated_radiance_sh = [0.0] * len(numerator)
        
    cosine_lobe_sh = compute_cosine_lobe_sh(surface_normal)
    irradiance = sum(estimated_radiance_sh[i] * cosine_lobe_sh[i] for i in range(len(numerator)))
    
    return irradiance

def compute_radiosity(surface_point, surface_normal, volume_buffer, volume_resolution, diffuse_reflectivity):
    """
    Computes the final radiosity (diffuse reflected light) for a surface point.
    """
    irradiance = reconstruct_irradiance(surface_point, surface_normal, volume_buffer, volume_resolution)
    radiosity = (diffuse_reflectivity / math.pi) * irradiance
    return radiosity

# Standard helper stubs for valid Python execution
def sample_points_on_triangle(triangle, count): return []
def world_to_voxel_index(point, N): return [int(p * N) for p in point]
def voxel_index_to_world(idx, size): return [i * size for i in idx]
def random_unit_vector(): return [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]
def accumulate_radiance(old, new, dir): return new
def find_closest_voxels(point, buffer, num_neighbors): return []
def compute_cosine_lobe_sh(normal): return [0.0] * 10
```

### Parameters
*   **Points per triangle:** Default 12 random points per triangle [S1, unofficial]. The source notes that nearly perfect voxelization is achieved with 12 to 19 points, but framebuffer reprojection guarantees quality for the visible portion, so in practice rarely more than 6 points per triangle are needed [S1, unofficial].
*   **Rays per voxel:** Default 100 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Volume Resolution:** Default $64^3$ voxels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ray Marching Step Size:** Default 1 voxel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Number of Nearest Voxels:** 6 [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Self-Intersections:** The initial distance skip scheme avoids self-intersections with the originating voxel but not with neighboring voxels from the same polygon [S1, unofficial]. These are handled during irradiance reconstruction by shifting the point and using weights [S1, unofficial].
*   **Insufficient Resolution:** Low volume resolution causes loss of small-scale details like contact shadows [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance Scaling:** Volume creation scales sub-linearly with the number of triangles and linearly with volume size [S1, unofficial]. When volume resolution is doubled, the number of generated points per triangle is also doubled to keep quality identical [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Volume creation scales sub-linearly with the number of triangles and linearly with volume size [S1, unofficial]. Performance is mostly independent of the final image resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the volume resolution ($N^3$) and the data stored per voxel (visibility flags or SH coefficients) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Measured on an ATI Radeon 3650 [S1, unofficial]. The method maintains interactive framerates even for complex scenes (up to 100k triangles) [S1, unofficial]. AO is much faster than GI because the ray-marched distance is shorter and only coverage information is fetched [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance, handles fully dynamic geometry and lighting conditions, operates on a rough approximation for speed [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Approximate geometry (imperfections), loss of small-scale details at low resolution, limited to diffuse lighting [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was presented in the paper "Global Illumination Using Imperfect Volumes" at GRAPP 2011 [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of voxelization for real-time lighting, extending it to handle indirect illumination by using spherical harmonics to represent radiance distributions [S1, unofficial]. The term "imperfect volumes" refers to the acceptance of geometric approximation errors in the voxelization process to achieve real-time performance [S1, unofficial]. It is integrated in a traditional real-time deferred renderer using OpenGL and GLSL [S1, unofficial].
