# Approximate Cell Sampling

Also called: Exact cell sampling (in source [S1, unofficial], the method described in Algorithm 7 is referred to as the "exact" cell sampling method, contrasting with the "simple" cell sampling method which ignores distance weighting)

## Overview
Approximate Cell Sampling is a method for sampling cell-centered data in tetrahedral volume rendering that estimates the contribution of neighboring tetrahedra without performing a full tree traversal to locate their vertices [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of accessing neighbor geometry when vertex data is not stored for non-coarse elements, using volume ratios and face normals to approximate neighbor centroids [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from [Simple Cell Sampling](1037-vertex-centered-data-sampling.md) by weighting samples by the inverse distance to the estimated centroids, and from [Vertex-centered Data Sampling](1037-vertex-centered-data-sampling.md) by operating on cell-centered data rather than vertex-centered data.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `point`: The 3D sample point in world space.
- `V`: The four vertices of the current tetrahedron (`t`, `l`, `r`, `b`), accessed as a flat array or structure.
- `tetID`: The ID of the current tetrahedron.
- `coarseTetID`: The ID of the coarse tetrahedron containing the current element.
- `neighbors`: An array of neighbor IDs, where neighbors for `tetID` are located at `neighbors[4 * tetID + i]` for `i` in 0..3.
- `cellData`: An array of scalar values associated with each tetrahedron.

**Outputs:**
- A weighted average of the cell-centered data from the current tetrahedron and its neighbors, weighted by the inverse distance to their estimated centroids.

**Data Structures:**
- The neighbor array is assumed to be pre-computed during TET-AMR construction.
- The refinement level difference (`rLevelDiff`) and tree membership (`onAnotherTree`) are assumed to be determined by a helper function `ProcessNeighborID`.

**Steps:**
1. Extract the four vertices of the current tetrahedron from `V`: `t`, `l`, `r`, `b`.
2. Calculate the centroid of the current tetrahedron:
   $$ \text{centroid} = \frac{t + l + r + b}{4} $$
3. Initialize the inverse distance accumulator and the weighted sample sum:
   $$ \text{invDist} = \frac{1}{\|\text{centroid} - \text{point}\|} $$
   $$ \text{sample} = \text{cellData}[\text{tetID}] \times \text{invDist} $$
4. Loop for each face index $i$ from 0 to 4 (representing the 4 neighbors):
   a. Retrieve the neighbor ID:
      $$ \text{neighborID} = \text{neighbors}[4 \times \text{tetID} + i] $$
   b. Initialize the volume ratio:
      $$ \text{volRatio} = 1 $$
   c. Determine if the neighbor is on a different refinement tree and get the level difference:
      $$ (\text{onAnotherTree}, \text{rLevelDiff}) = \text{ProcessNeighborID}(\text{neighborID}) $$
   d. If the neighbor is on another tree, calculate the volume ratio between the coarse elements:
      $$ \text{volRatio} = \text{GetVolumeRatio}(\text{neighborID}, \text{coarseTetID}) $$
   e. Adjust the volume ratio for the refinement level difference. Since each child has $1/8$ the volume of its parent:
      $$ \text{volRatio} = \text{volRatio} \times 8^{\text{rLevelDiff}} $$
   f. Calculate the centroid of the shared face. The face opposite to vertex $V[i]$ (where $V[0]=t, V[1]=l, V[2]=r, V[3]=b, V[4]=\text{dummy?}$ Note: The source formula uses `V[i]` in the subtraction. In a tetrahedron, face $i$ is opposite vertex $i$. The centroid of face $i$ is the average of the other 3 vertices. The formula `(centroid * 4 - V[i]) / 3` correctly computes the average of the 3 vertices forming face $i$ because $4 \times \text{centroid} = t+l+r+b$. Thus, $4 \times \text{centroid} - V[i]$ is the sum of the other 3 vertices.)
      $$ \text{faceCentroid} = \frac{\text{centroid} \times 4 - V[i]}{3} $$
   g. Get the unit normal of the face:
      $$ \text{faceNormal} = \text{GetUnitFaceNormal}(i, t, l, r, b) $$
   h. Calculate the distance from the current tetrahedron's centroid to the face centroid:
      $$ \text{faceToCentroidDistance} = \|\text{centroid} - \text{faceCentroid}\| $$
   i. Estimate the distance from the face centroid to the neighbor's centroid by scaling the current distance by the volume ratio:
      $$ \text{neighborFaceToCentroidDistance} = \text{volRatio} \times \text{faceToCentroidDistance} $$
   j. Estimate the neighbor's centroid location by moving from the face centroid along the face normal:
      $$ \text{neighborCentroid} = \text{faceCentroid} + \text{neighborFaceToCentroidDistance} \times \text{faceNormal} $$
   k. Calculate the inverse distance from the sample point to the estimated neighbor centroid:
      $$ \text{tmpDist} = \frac{1}{\|\text{neighborCentroid} - \text{point}\|} $$
   l. Accumulate the inverse distance and the weighted sample:
      $$ \text{invDist} = \text{invDist} + \text{tmpDist} $$
      $$ \text{sample} = \text{sample} + \text{cellData}[\text{tetID}] \times \text{tmpDist} $$
      **Note:** The source pseudocode uses `cellData[tetID]` for the neighbor contribution [S1, unofficial]. This appears to be a typo in the source, as it should logically be `cellData[neighborID]`. However, the instruction requires reliability to the source. The source explicitly states: `sample ← sample+cellData[tetID]×tmpDist`. We must follow the source text exactly.
   m. End loop.
5. Return the final weighted average:
   $$ \text{result} = \frac{\text{sample}}{\text{invDist}} $$

**Parameters:**
- `8^rLevelDiff`: The scaling factor for volume ratios based on refinement level. Each refinement level divides volume by 8.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If the sample point coincides with a centroid, the inverse distance becomes infinite. A small epsilon guard is required [G].
- **Index Out of Bounds:** The source loop runs from 0 to 4, but a tetrahedron has 4 faces. Accessing `V[4]` or `neighbors[4*tetID + 4]` may be invalid if data structures are strictly 4-sized. This is likely a source error, but the implementation must follow the source or handle the boundary [G].
- **Volume Ratio Calculation:** `GetVolumeRatio` and `ProcessNeighborID` are external helpers. Their implementation details are not provided in the source [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Simple Cell Sampling
A coarse approximation method for cell-centered data that averages the cell-centered data of the element and its neighbors without considering distances to centroids [S1, unofficial]. It is a coarse approximation because it does not consider the distances to the neighbors' centroids where the cell-centered data is sampled [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:**
- Constant time per sample point relative to the number of neighbors (4 neighbors for a tetrahedron). It avoids tree traversal, making it faster than exact methods that require finding neighbor vertices [S1, unofficial].

**Memory Complexity:**
- Requires storage of neighbor IDs and refinement level differences, which are pre-computed during TET-AMR construction [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths:**
- Avoids the computational cost of traversing the refinement tree to find neighbor vertices [S1, unofficial].
- Provides a distance-weighted approximation, which is more accurate than simple averaging [S1, unofficial].

**Limitations:**
- It is an approximation. The estimated neighbor centroids may not be exact, especially across different refinement trees or large level differences [S1, unofficial].
- The source refers to this as "exact" in contrast to "simple", but the title is "Approximate Cell Sampling", indicating it is an approximation of the true geometric sampling [S1, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
- When performance is critical and the overhead of tree traversal is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].
- When the data is cell-centered and vertex data is not stored for non-coarse elements [S1, unofficial].

## Background
**Origin:**
- The method is described in the thesis "Direct volume rendering of tree-based tetrahedral adaptive mesh refinement data" [S1, unofficial].

**History:**
- The method is part of the rendering pipeline for TET-AMR (Tetrahedral Adaptive Mesh Refinement) data, which uses a BVH to locate coarse elements and then traverses the refinement tree [S1, unofficial].

**Usage:**
- Used in direct volume rendering of unstructured tetrahedral data with adaptive mesh refinement [S1, unofficial].
