# TetraSDF

Also called: Analytic Isosurface Extraction with Multi-resolution Tetrahedral Grid

## Overview
TetraSDF is an analytic isosurface extraction framework for neural Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) that enables exact zero-level set extraction by preserving a global Continuous Piecewise Affine (CPWA) structure [S1]. Unlike sampling-based methods like Marching Cubes, which introduce discretization error, or plain ReLU MLPs, which lack high-frequency expressiveness, TetraSDF composes a ReLU MLP with a multi-resolution tetrahedral positional encoder [S1]. This composition ensures the entire network remains CPWA, allowing the zero-level set to be extracted analytically via edge subdivision without marching artifacts [S1]. It is distinguished by its use of barycentric interpolation to maintain affine continuity across tetrahedral cells and an analytic input preconditioner to mitigate directional bias from the encoder's grid geometry [S1].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  A trained neural network $f(\mathbf{x}) = \nu^{(M)}(\tau(\mathbf{x}))$, where $\nu^{(M)}$ is an $M$-layer ReLU MLP and $\tau$ is the multi-resolution tetrahedral positional encoder [S1].
2.  Network parameters: Weights $W^{(i)}$ and biases $b^{(i)}$ for the MLP [S1].
3.  Encoder parameters: Hash table entries $H_i^{(\ell)}$ for each level $\ell$ [S1].
4.  Extraction bounds: A bounding cube $\mathcal{S} = [0, 1]^3$ (or transformed coordinates) [S1].

**Outputs:**
1.  A set of vertices $V$ and edges $E$ defining the polyhedral complex of the zero-level set [S1].
2.  A mesh extracted from $(V, E)$ representing the exact isosurface [S1].

### Data Structures
1.  **Multi-resolution Tetrahedral Grid:**
    *   Defined by $L$ resolution levels, minimum resolution $N_{\min}$, and maximum resolution $N_{\max}$ [S1].
    *   Level resolution $N_\ell$ is calculated via geometric progression [S1].
    *   Each level $\ell$ subdivides the unit cube into $N_\ell^3$ cube cells, each decomposed into 6 congruent tetrahedra [S1].
2.  **Sign Vector $\mathbf{s}_k^{(m)}(\mathbf{x})$:**
    *   A vector of length $K_m$ (number of neurons in layer $m$) [S1].
    *   Entries $\gamma_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ indicate the state of the $j$-th neuron in layer $i$ relative to a threshold $\epsilon_s$ [S1].
3.  **Barycentric Mask $\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{x})$:**
    *   Concatenated binary vector of length $4L$ [S1].
    *   Entries $m_i^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate if the $i$-th barycentric weight at level $\ell$ is above a small threshold $\epsilon_b$ [S1].
4.  **Region Indicator $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})$:**
    *   Concatenated integer vector of length $4L$ [S1].
    *   Entries $\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) = [\mathbf{a}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}), t^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x})]$ identify the containing cube anchor and tetrahedron offset at each level [S1].
5.  **Skeleton $(V, E)$:**
    *   $V$: Set of 3D vertices.
    *   $E$: Set of edges connecting vertices [S1].

### Formulas and Definitions
**1. Multi-resolution Grid Parameters:**
Geometric progression ratio $\gamma$ and level resolution $N_\ell$ [S1]:
$$ \gamma := \exp\left(\frac{\ln N_{\max} - \ln N_{\min}}{L - 1}\right) $$
$$ N_\ell := \lfloor N_{\min} \gamma^\ell \rfloor, \quad \ell \in \{0, 1, \dots, L-1\} $$

**2. Barycentric Weights and Preconditioning:**
Barycentric weights $\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x}) = [w_0, w_1, w_2, w_3]^\top$ are an affine mapping of input $\mathbf{x}$ within a tetrahedron [S1]:
$$ \mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_{\mathcal{T}} $$
where $\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{T}}$ is the Jacobian matrix [S1].
To mitigate directional bias, an input preconditioner $\mathbf{A}^*$ is applied:
$$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{A}^* \mathbf{x} $$
This isotropizes the average local metric $\mathbf{M} = 6 \sum_{\mathcal{T}} \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{T}}^\top \mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{T}}$, reducing the condition number from 16.39 to 5.05 [S1].

**3. Encoder Output:**
The encoder $\tau$ concatenates barycentric interpolations across levels [S1]:
$$ \tau(\mathbf{x}) := \bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{L-1} \left( \sum_{v \in V(\mathcal{T}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}))} w_v^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) H_i^{(\ell)} \right) $$
where $H_i^{(\ell)}$ is the feature vector for vertex $v$ at level $\ell$, and $\oplus$ denotes concatenation [S1].

**4. Sign Vector Entry:**
For neuron preactivation $\nu_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x})$ and threshold $\epsilon_s$ [S1]:
$$ \gamma_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} +1, & \text{if } \nu_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}) > \epsilon_s \\ 0, & \text{if } |\nu_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x})| \leq \epsilon_s \\ -1, & \text{if } \nu_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}) < -\epsilon_s \end{cases} $$
$\gamma = 0$ indicates the point lies on the decision boundary $H_j^{(i)} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \nu_j^{(i)}(\mathbf{x}) = 0\}$ [S1].

**5. Barycentric Mask:**
For threshold $\epsilon_b$ [S1]:
$$ m_i^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} 1, & w_i^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) > \epsilon_b \\ 0, & w_i^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) \leq \epsilon_b \end{cases} $$
Zeros in $\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{x})$ indicate the point lies on the boundary (face, edge, or vertex) of the tetrahedron [S1].

**6. Region Indicator:**
Grid anchor $\mathbf{a}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) = \lfloor N_\ell \mathbf{x} \rfloor$ and tetrahedron offset $t^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) \in \{0, \dots, 5\}$ [S1]:
$$ \mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) = [\mathbf{a}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}), t^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x})] $$
$$ \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}) := \bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{L-1} \mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{x}) $$

**7. Edge Intersection:**
For an edge $(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1)$ crossing a decision boundary where $\gamma(\mathbf{x}_0)\gamma(\mathbf{x}_1) < 0$, let $d_0 = \nu(\mathbf{x}_0)$ and $d_1 = \nu(\mathbf{x}_1)$ [S1]:
$$ w = \frac{d_0}{d_0 - d_1} $$
$$ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0,1} = (1 - w)\mathbf{x}_0 + w\mathbf{x}_1 $$

### Parameters
*   $L$: Number of resolution levels. Typical values not specified in the sources, but implied to be small integers (e.g., 4–8) [S1].
*   $N_{\min}$: Minimum grid resolution [S1].
*   $N_{\max}$: Maximum grid resolution [S1].
*   $\epsilon_s$: Small positive constant for sign vector boundary detection. Value not specified in the sources [S1].
*   $\epsilon_b$: Small threshold for barycentric mask. Value not specified in the sources [S1].

    """

    # Compute average metric M = 6 * sum(J_T^T J_T).
    # Compute preconditioner A* to isotropize M.

    # 2. Initialize Skeleton from Encoder Geometry

def is_sign_change(d0, d1, epsilon_s):
    """
    Checks if the preactivation values d0 and d1 indicate a crossing of the
    decision boundary defined by epsilon_s.
    """
    # Sign function based on epsilon_s
    def sign(val):
        if val > epsilon_s: return 1
        if val < -epsilon_s: return -1
        return 0
        
    s0 = sign(d0)
    s1 = sign(d1)
    
    # A crossing occurs if signs are opposite and non-zero,
    # or if one is zero and the other is non-zero (boundary touch/cross).
    # The source states: gamma(x0)*gamma(x1) < 0 implies crossing.
    # However, gamma=0 means on boundary.
    # Standard edge subdivision logic: if signs differ, edge crosses.
    if s0 * s1 < 0:
        return True
    # Handle boundary cases where one point is exactly on the boundary
    if s0 == 0 or s1 == 0:
        # If one is on boundary, it's a vertex of the isosurface.
        # Depending on implementation, this might trigger subdivision or vertex addition.
        # The source implies subdivision happens when sign changes.
        # If d0=0, x0 is on boundary. If d1!=0, edge connects boundary to interior.
        # We treat this as a crossing for the purpose of adding the vertex x0 to V if not present.
        return True
    return False

def evaluate_neuron_preactivation(network, x, m, k):
    """
    Evaluates the preactivation value of the k-th neuron in the m-th layer
    of the MLP for input x.
    """
    # Forward pass through the MLP up to layer m
    # f(x) = rho(M) o sigma(M-1) o ... o rho(1)
    # We need the preactivation of neuron k in layer m.
    # This requires computing activations of previous layers.
    # For efficiency, this is typically done via tensorized operations on GPU.
    # Here, we assume a helper function exists.
    return network.forward_preactivation(x, m, k)

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
1.  **Precision:** The use of $\epsilon_s$ and $\epsilon_b$ is critical. If $\epsilon_s$ is too large, the boundary region is overestimated; if too small, numerical noise may cause false sign changes. The sources do not specify exact values [S1].
2.  **Boundary Handling:** Points lying exactly on tetrahedron boundaries are handled implicitly by the barycentric interpolation scheme, but the barycentric mask $\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{x})$ explicitly identifies these locations for neighbor identification [S1].
3.  **Degenerate Input:** The method assumes the input domain is a cube $\mathcal{S}$ [S1]. Non-cubic domains require transformation.
4.  **Complexity:** Naively enumerating all polyhedral cells $\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{x}}$ is prohibitive. The method uses plane intersection and tensorized operations to avoid this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given in the sources. The method involves edge subdivision over the bounding volume, with complexity dependent on the number of ReLU neurons and the resolution levels [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given. Depends on the size of the hash table for the encoder and the number of vertices/edges in the extracted mesh [S1].
*   **Performance:** The sources state that TetraSDF matches or surpasses existing grid-based encoders in accuracy across multiple benchmarks [S1]. The preconditioner reduces the condition number from 16.39 to 5.05, improving optimization conditioning [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Exact analytic extraction (no discretization error); preserves high-frequency details via multi-resolution encoder; stable training due to preconditioning [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires the network to be a ReLU MLP composed with the specific tetrahedral encoder; complex implementation of edge subdivision and neighbor identification [S1].
*   **When to use:** When exact isosurface extraction from a neural SDF is required, and high-frequency details are important.
*   **When not to use:** When approximate meshes are acceptable and simpler methods (e.g., Marching Cubes) are preferred for ease of implementation.

## Background
TetraSDF was introduced by Seonghun Oh, Youngjung Uh, and Jin-Hwa Kim from Yonsei University, NAVER AI Lab, and Seoul National University [S1]. The paper "TetraSDF: Analytic Isosurface Extraction with Multi-resolution Tetrahedral Grid" was published in 2025 (arXiv:2511.16273v4) [S1]. It addresses the challenge of extracting explicit surfaces from neural SDFs that exactly match the zero-level set, overcoming the discretization errors of sampling-based methods and the expressiveness limitations of plain ReLU MLPs [S1].
