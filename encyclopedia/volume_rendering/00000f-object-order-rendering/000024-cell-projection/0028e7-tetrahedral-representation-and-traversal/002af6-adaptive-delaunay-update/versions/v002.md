# Adaptive Delaunay Update

Also called: Delaunay Update Frequency

## Overview
Adaptive Delaunay Update is a strategy for maintaining topological consistency in tetrahedral volume rendering during optimization by periodically recomputing the Delaunay triangulation rather than updating it continuously. It solves the problem of geometric invalidity caused by vertex movement during gradient-based optimization, where vertices may cross Delaunay boundaries, violating the empty circumsphere property. It sets itself apart from continuous update methods by decoupling topology changes from attribute updates, balancing GPU acceleration speed with geometric accuracy [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A set of vertices $V$ defining the current tetrahedral mesh, the current Delaunay triangulation $T$, and an optimization iteration counter $k$.
- Output: An updated Delaunay triangulation $T'$ and updated vertex positions $V'$.

**Data Structures:**
- Vertices: A list of 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
- Tetrahedra: A list of indices referencing four vertices per tetrahedron.
- Iteration Counter: An integer tracking optimization steps.

**Parameters:**
- Update Frequency ($F$): The number of optimization iterations between full Delaunay recomputations. Typical value: $F = 10$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Perform standard optimization step (e.g., gradient descent) to update vertex positions $V$ and attributes.
2. Increment iteration counter $k$.
3. If $k \mod F == 0$:
    a. Recompute the Delaunay triangulation $T'$ from the current vertex set $V$ [S1, unofficial].
    b. Reset $k = 0$.
4. Render using the current triangulation $T$ (or $T'$ if just updated).

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Cospherical Degeneracy:** When a vertex moves by a small amount $\epsilon$ and enters the circumsphere of a neighboring tetrahedron, it triggers a topological flip. The vertex was already nearby the circumsphere boundary prior to the move. Consequently, the flips involve sets of points that are nearly cospherical, as the geometric configurations before and after the flip share an almost identical circumsphere [S1, unofficial]. This can lead to numerical instability if precision is insufficient.
- **Stale Topology:** Between updates, the mesh topology may not strictly satisfy the Delaunay condition, potentially affecting rendering quality or sorting correctness if the renderer assumes strict Delaunay properties for visibility sorting [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Radiance Meshes](4929-radiance-meshes.md)
Radiance Meshes represent radiance fields using constant density tetrahedral cells produced via Delaunay tetrahedralization. Unlike Voronoi-based methods (e.g., RadFoam) that require complex polyhedral tessellation, or splatting methods (e.g., 3DGS) that rely on approximate sorting, Radiance Meshes use simple triangles native to hardware rasterizers to enable exact, fast volume rendering. It solves the trade-off between optimization dynamics and rendering speed by decoupling topology (Delaunay mesh) from attribute parameterization (Instant-NGP).

### Delaunay Edge Flipping (Pachner Moves)
A method to form a Delaunay triangulation by flipping edges of triangles that are not Delaunay, specifically those where the circumsphere contains a vertex. In 3D, these edge-flips are called Pachner moves [S1, unofficial]. During optimization, take a valid Delaunay triangulation and move vertices by a small amount $\epsilon$. If a vertex enters the circumsphere of a neighboring tetrahedron, trigger a topological flip [S1, unofficial]. Flips involve sets of points that are nearly cospherical because the vertex was already nearby the circumsphere boundary prior to the move, and the geometric configurations before and after the flip share an almost identical circumsphere [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Recomputing the Delaunay triangulation from scratch is computationally expensive. On a scene with 1.2 million tetrahedra, the Delaunay triangulation computation takes approximately 92 ms [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** On a scene with 1.2 million tetrahedra, rendering takes 19 ms, Instant-NGP query takes 13 ms, and the backwards pass takes 143 ms [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** Updating less frequently (e.g., every 10 iterations) balances GPU acceleration speed with accuracy [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Reduces computational overhead compared to continuous updates; leverages GPU acceleration for rendering while managing expensive topology updates.
- **Limitations:** Stale topology between updates may affect geometric validity; numerical instability due to nearly cospherical points during flips.
- **When to use:** When optimization involves significant vertex movement that could violate Delaunay conditions, but continuous updates are too slow.
- **When not to use:** When real-time strict topological consistency is required, or when vertex movements are negligible.

## Background
The concept of using Delaunay triangulation for volumetric representation and its adaptive update during optimization is discussed in the context of Radiance Meshes for Volumetric Reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the geometric properties of Delaunay triangulations, including the use of circumcircles and radical axes for visibility sorting [S1, unofficial]. The specific update frequency of 10 iterations was chosen to balance performance and accuracy in the GauSTAR framework [S1, unofficial].
