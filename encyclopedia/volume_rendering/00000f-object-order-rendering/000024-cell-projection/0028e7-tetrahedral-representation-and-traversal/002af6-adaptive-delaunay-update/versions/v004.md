# Adaptive Delaunay Update

Also called: Delaunay Update Frequency

## Overview
Adaptive Delaunay Update is a strategy for maintaining the topological validity of a tetrahedral mesh during optimization-based volumetric reconstruction, specifically within the context of Radiance Meshes [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of computational cost versus geometric accuracy when vertex positions change during training [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that recompute the Delaunay triangulation from scratch at every iteration, this method updates the topology only periodically, balancing GPU acceleration speed with rendering accuracy [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Tetrahedral representation and traversal](4396-tetrahedral-representation-and-traversal.md) by ensuring the underlying mesh remains a valid Delaunay triangulation despite vertex perturbations.

## How it works
The method operates within an optimization loop where primitive vertices are updated to minimize a loss function. The core challenge is that moving vertices can violate the Delaunay condition (where a vertex lies inside the circumsphere of a neighboring tetrahedron), requiring topological changes (edge flips/Pachner moves) to restore validity [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Current set of tetrahedral vertices $V$, current Delaunay triangulation $T$, optimization step size $\epsilon$.
- Output: Updated vertices $V'$, updated Delaunay triangulation $T'$ (if an update is triggered).

**Data Structures:**
- The mesh is stored as a set of tetrahedra, each defined by 4 vertex indices.
- A spatial data structure (e.g., Instant-NGP hash grid) is used for fast neighbor queries and circumcenter calculations [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Vertex Update:** During an optimization iteration, update vertex positions by a small displacement $\epsilon$ [S1, unofficial].
2. **Validity Check:** Check if any vertex has entered the circumsphere of a neighboring tetrahedron [S1, unofficial]. This involves calculating the power distance of vertices to the circumcenters of adjacent tetrahedra [S1, unofficial].
3. **Topological Flip (if needed):** If a violation is detected, perform a Pachner move (edge flip) to restore the Delaunay condition [S1, unofficial]. This involves identifying the affected tetrahedra and re-tetrahedralizing the local convex hull [S1, unofficial].
4. **Periodic Full Update:** Regardless of local flips, recompute the global Delaunay triangulation from scratch every $N$ iterations to correct accumulated errors and ensure global consistency [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Update Frequency ($N$): The number of iterations between full Delaunay recomputations. A typical value is 10 iterations [S1, unofficial]. This value balances the cost of triangulation against the risk of topological errors.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Nearly Cospherical Points:** Flips are triggered when a vertex enters a circumsphere. Since the vertex was already nearby the boundary prior to the move, the geometric configurations before and after the flip share an almost identical circumsphere [S1, unofficial]. This near-degeneracy can lead to numerical instability in circumcenter calculations.
- **Performance Bottleneck:** Recomputing the Delaunay triangulation from scratch is computationally expensive. For a scene with 1.2 million tetrahedra, this step takes approximately 92 ms [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Radiance Meshes](4929-radiance-meshes.md)
Radiance Meshes represent radiance fields using constant density tetrahedral cells produced via Delaunay tetrahedralization. Unlike Voronoi-based methods (e.g., RadFoam) that require complex polyhedral tessellation, or splatting methods (e.g., 3DGS) that rely on approximate sorting, Radiance Meshes use simple triangles native to hardware rasterizers to enable exact, fast volume rendering. It solves the trade-off between optimization dynamics and rendering speed by decoupling topology (Delaunay mesh) from attribute parameterization (Instant-NGP).

### Delaunay Edge Flipping (Pachner Moves)
A method to form a Delaunay triangulation by flipping edges of triangles that are not Delaunay, specifically those where the circumsphere contains a vertex. In 3D, these edge-flips are called Pachner moves [S1, unofficial]. During optimization, a valid Delaunay triangulation is taken and vertices are moved by a small amount $\epsilon$. If a vertex enters the circumsphere of a neighboring tetrahedron, a topological flip is triggered [S1, unofficial]. A key pitfall is that flips involve sets of points that are nearly cospherical because the vertex was already nearby the circumsphere boundary prior to the move, and the geometric configurations before and after the flip share an almost identical circumsphere [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The cost of updating the triangulation is dominated by the global recomputation, which occurs every $N$ iterations. For a scene with 1.2 million tetrahedra, the Delaunay triangulation computation takes approximately 92 ms [S1, unofficial]. Local flips are cheaper but depend on the number of violations.
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of tetrahedra, as the mesh structure must be stored.
- **Measured Performance:** On a scene with 1.2 million tetrahedra, the total training time per iteration includes 19 ms for rendering, 13 ms for Instant-NGP query, 143 ms for the backwards pass, and 92 ms for Delaunay triangulation [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Significantly reduces the computational overhead of maintaining a valid Delaunay mesh during optimization compared to full recomputation at every step.
- **Limitations:** The periodic full recomputation remains a bottleneck. The method relies on the assumption that vertex movements are small enough that local flips can correct most violations before the next global update.
- **When to use:** Use when optimizing the geometry of a tetrahedral mesh for volumetric rendering, particularly in methods like Radiance Meshes where topological validity is crucial for correct rasterization.
- **When not to use:** Avoid if the vertex movements are large or erratic, as local flips may not be sufficient to maintain validity, leading to frequent global recomputations or topological errors.

## Background
The concept of adaptive Delaunay updates is integral to the Radiance Meshes method for volumetric reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. Radiance Meshes were introduced in a preprint by Chengwei Zheng, Lixin Xue, Juan Zarate, and Jie Song in 2025 [S1, unofficial]. The method leverages the properties of Delaunay triangulations to enable efficient and exact volume rendering using standard rasterization hardware. The specific technique of updating the Delaunay topology periodically rather than continuously is a practical optimization to handle the computational cost of 3D Delaunay triangulation during gradient-based optimization.
