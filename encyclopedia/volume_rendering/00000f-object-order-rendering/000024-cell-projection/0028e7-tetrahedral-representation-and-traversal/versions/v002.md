# Tetrahedral representation and traversal

Also called: Projected Tetrahedra family, Tetrahedral Volume Rendering, Marching Tetrahedrons

## Overview
Tetrahedral representation and traversal encompasses methods that decompose volumetric data or geometric primitives into tetrahedra to facilitate rendering, isosurface extraction, or optimization. It solves the problem of handling unstructured, non-convex, or irregular grids where regular voxel-based methods fail or introduce artifacts. It is distinguished from [Cell projection](26-cell-projection.md) by its specific reliance on tetrahedral decomposition to resolve "problem faces" (non-planar quadrilaterals) and from [Ray casting](10-ray-casting.md) by its object-order nature (in projection variants) or explicit mesh generation (in marching variants).

## Shared principles
The family relies on the decomposition of complex volumetric cells (typically hexahedra or general polyhedra) into tetrahedra. A cube is commonly decomposed into 5 or 6 tetrahedra [S6, S9, unofficial][G]. To prevent visual cracks between adjacent cells in regular grids, alternating decomposition orientations (checkerboard pattern) are used [S6, S9, unofficial].

The scalar field is assumed to vary linearly within each tetrahedron. For projection-based methods, the tetrahedron is projected onto the image plane, and the viewing ray segment length $l$ varies linearly across the projected polygon for orthogonal projection, or is handled via planar depth interpolation for perspective projection [S1, S2, S4, book]. The integrated opacity $\alpha$ for a segment of length $l$ with average differential opacity $\kappa_{avg}$ is computed as:
$$ \alpha = 1 - e^{-\kappa_{avg} l} $$
[S1][S4, book]

To avoid derivative discontinuities (Mach bands) caused by linear interpolation of $\alpha$, methods typically interpolate the linear parameters $\kappa_{avg}$ and $l$ and use a texture lookup or pre-integration table to compute $\alpha$ per pixel [S1, S2].

## Subtopics

### [Tetrahedral Volume Rendering](820-tetrahedral-volume-rendering.md)
A GPU-based two-pass pipeline (PTINT) for unstructured tetrahedral grids. It distinguishes itself by using a Ternary Truth Table for projection classification and Partial Pre-Integration for color/opacity. It supports both volume rendering and isosurface extraction via gradient-based lighting.

### [Projected Tetrahedra](877-projected-tetrahedra.md)
The foundational object-order technique for unstructured meshes. It projects tetrahedra into screen-space triangles, sorts them back-to-front, and composites them. It sets itself apart by requiring global visibility sorting and handling degenerate cases via specific projection classes.

### [Marching Tetrahedrons Algorithm](940-marching-tetrahedrons-algorithm.md)
An iso-surface extraction method for regular grids. It decomposes voxels into tetrahedra and generates explicit triangular meshes. It differs from projection methods by outputting geometry rather than images, but suffers from directional ambiguities due to non-symmetric cube decomposition.

### [Adaptive Delaunay Update](4923-adaptive-delaunay-update.md)
A technique for differentiable volumetric reconstruction. It maintains a valid Delaunay tetrahedralization during vertex optimization by selectively updating topology (Pachner moves) when the empty circumsphere property is violated, balancing speed and accuracy.

### [GPU-based Projected Tetrahedra](7484-gpu-based-projected-tetrahedra.md)
A hardware-accelerated variant of PT. It uses a fixed "basis graph" (5 vertices: 4 real + 1 phantom) sent as a triangle fan to the GPU. The vertex shader computes the phantom vertex (thickness/depth) and attributes, allowing the fragment shader to handle non-linear opacity integration via texture lookups.

### [Texture-Encoded Isosurfacing](7682-texture-encoded-isosurfacing.md)
A GPU method for isosurfacing that encodes intersection logic into 2D checkerboard textures. It projects smaller tetrahedra (subdivided based on isovalue classification) and uses texture sampling to determine the isosurface intersection, avoiding explicit geometric calculations.

### Point Representation via Tetrahedra
- **Definition**: Representing data points as small tetrahedra to fit them into the triangular mesh framework for rendering [S1].
- **Parameter**: `size`: specifies the size of the point tetrahedra relative to the data ranges [S1].

### LinPrim Tetrahedron Representation
- **Definition**: A scene representation using transparent tetrahedra bounded by triangular faces with homogeneous density [S2].
- **Step**: Define four basis vectors describing directions from the center on which corners are located, equally spaced to represent regular tetrahedra. Optimize the distance between the center and each corner independently [S2].
- **Parameter**: Tetrahedra memory requirement: 12 floats per primitive (omitting color), due to absence of symmetry in the formulation [S2].
- **Pitfall**: Unlike octahedra, the optimized position (center) can differ from the geometric center of the primitive due to lack of symmetry [S2].

### Grid-based Traversal
- **Definition**: A method using a hierarchical data structure for rectilinear grids to quickly determine tetrahedra possibly hit by traversed grid cells, enabling interactive rendering of irregular volume data [S3, unofficial].
- **Step**: Attach all tetrahedra intersecting a given grid cell to that cell. Use minimum and maximum values to cull regions outside of the current iso-value [S3, unofficial].
- **Number**: Rendered a bioelectric field of over one million tetrahedra at interactive rates (512x512 viewport) with 16 processors or more [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of decomposing hexahedral cells into tetrahedra for rendering was pioneered by Parker et al. [PPL* 99], who demonstrated interactive rendering of irregular volume data on supercomputers [S3, unofficial]. The Projected Tetrahedra (PT) algorithm was introduced by Levoy [S1, S4] to handle unstructured grids by projecting volumetric primitives. The Marching Tetrahedrons Algorithm (MTA) was developed to resolve the ambiguities of the original Marching Cubes algorithm by ensuring consistent connectivity through tetrahedral decomposition [S1]. GPU-based implementations emerged to leverage hardware rasterization, moving the classification and decomposition logic into vertex shaders [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
