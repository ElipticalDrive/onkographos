# Delaunay Triangulation for Tetrahedral Representation

Also called: 3D Delaunay Triangulation, gDel3D-based Tetrahedralization

## Overview
This method constructs a tetrahedral mesh from arbitrarily distributed 3D points by enforcing the Delaunay property: no point lies within the circumsphere of any tetrahedron in the mesh [S2]. It serves as a robust alternative to silhouette-based methods like Visual Hull Methods (VMC), which suffer from threshold sensitivity and discontinuous surfaces [S2]. Unlike its sibling [Local Convex Hull (LCH)](11862-local-convex-hull-lch.md), which uses convex hull algorithms for local surface construction, this method relies on global Delaunay triangulation to ensure topological consistency and connectivity [S1]. It is primarily used in surface reconstruction and volumetric tessellation where arbitrary point distributions must be converted into a valid tetrahedral representation [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of 3D points $P = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n\}$ representing Voronoi sites or surface samples.
**Outputs:** A tetrahedral mesh $T$ where each tetrahedron $t \in T$ satisfies the empty circumsphere property.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Points:** Array of 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
*   **Tetrahedra:** List of indices $(i, j, k, l)$ referencing points in $P$.
*   **Circumsphere:** Defined by center $c$ and radius $r$.

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Create a "super tetrahedron" that encloses all input points $P$. This ensures the triangulation starts with a valid convex hull boundary [S2].
2.  **Triangulation:** Generate the initial Delaunay triangulation of the points. This can be performed using specialized libraries such as gDel3D for GPU acceleration [S1].
3.  **Validation and Subdivision:** For each tetrahedron $t$ in the mesh:
    *   Compute the circumsphere of $t$.
    *   Check if any point $p \in P$ lies strictly inside this circumsphere.
    *   If a violating point $p$ is found, subdivide the tetrahedron $t$ to incorporate $p$, ensuring the Delaunay property is restored [S2].
4.  **Iteration:** In dynamic contexts (e.g., evolving Voronoi sites), the triangulation is recomputed from scratch at every iteration rather than using incremental updates [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Circumsphere Check:** A point $p$ is inside the circumsphere of a tetrahedron defined by vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ if the determinant of the following matrix is positive (assuming a right-handed coordinate system and outward normals):
    $$
    \det \begin{pmatrix}
    x_p & y_p & z_p & x_p^2 + y_p^2 + z_p^2 & 1 \\
    x_1 & y_1 & z_1 & x_1^2 + y_1^2 + z_1^2 & 1 \\
    x_2 & y_2 & z_2 & x_2^2 + y_2^2 + z_2^2 & 1 \\
    x_3 & y_3 & z_3 & x_3^2 + y_3^2 + z_3^2 & 1 \\
    x_4 & y_4 & z_4 & x_4^2 + y_4^2 + z_4^2 & 1
    \end{pmatrix} > 0
    $$
*   **Subdivision:** When a point $p$ violates the empty circumsphere property of tetrahedron $t$, $t$ is removed and replaced by new tetrahedra formed by connecting $p$ to the faces of $t$ that are visible from $p$, or by re-triangulating the local convex hull of the affected vertices [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **Super Tetrahedron Size:** Must be large enough to enclose all points $P$. Exact dimensions depend on the bounding box of $P$.
*   **Library:** gDel3D is cited for GPU acceleration [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **NP-Completeness:** Determining whether a given polyhedron can be tetrahedralized is NP-complete [S2]. Constrained Delaunay Triangulation (CDT) may fail on special polyhedra (e.g., twisted prisms) without additional points or surface modification [S2].
*   **Recomputation Cost:** Recomputing the triangulation from scratch at every iteration is computationally expensive [S1]. Incremental updates are noted as a direction for future work but are not implemented in the cited method [S1].
*   **Precision:** Floating-point errors in circumsphere calculations can lead to incorrect subdivision decisions.

## Subtopics

### [Local Convex Hull (LCH)](11862-local-convex-hull-lch.md)
A surface reconstruction method that constructs triangular patches from volumetric data by estimating surfaces from local convexities. It differs from this method by using a convex hull algorithm rather than Delaunay triangulation for local surface construction, offering a trade-off between processing time and shape detail preservation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Recomputing the triangulation from scratch at every iteration is computationally expensive [S1]. The complexity of 3D Delaunay triangulation is generally $O(n \log n)$ on average, but worst-case scenarios can be higher [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of tetrahedra generated, which is proportional to the number of input points $n$.
*   **Performance:** Using gDel3D accelerates computation on the GPU [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles arbitrarily distributed 3D points; ensures topological consistency and connectivity; avoids threshold sensitivity issues of VMC [S2].
*   **Limitations:** NP-complete tetrahedralization for constrained polyhedra; recomputation from scratch is costly; incremental updates are not implemented in the cited method [S1, S2].
*   **When to use:** When arbitrary point distributions need to be converted into a valid tetrahedral mesh for surface reconstruction or volumetric rendering [S2].
*   **When not to use:** When incremental updates are required for dynamic data and performance is critical [S1].

## Background
The 3D Delaunay Triangulation (DT) was introduced as an alternative to silhouette-based methods like Visual Hull Methods (VMC) [S2]. It constructs a surface by defining tetrahedrons from arbitrarily distributed 3D points, characterized by the property that no point lies within the circumsphere of any tetrahedron [S2]. The method has been used in various surface reconstruction applications, including the DCCVT method which uses gDel3D for GPU acceleration [S1]. Constrained Delaunay Triangulation (CDT) has also been evolved to include predescribed boundaries, though it faces limitations with special polyhedra [S2].
