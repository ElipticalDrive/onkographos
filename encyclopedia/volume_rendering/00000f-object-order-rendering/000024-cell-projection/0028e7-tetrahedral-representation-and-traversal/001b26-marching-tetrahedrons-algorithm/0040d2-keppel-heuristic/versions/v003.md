# Keppel Heuristic
Also called: Keppel Tetrahedron Method
## Overview
The Keppel Heuristic is a local decision method used in contour tiling to determine how to connect two parallel contour segments during volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. It resolves the ambiguity of the next vertex to connect by comparing the volumes of two candidate tetrahedra formed with interior reference points [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- Inputs: Contour vertices and two interior reference points $O_p$ and $O_q$ located inside the respective contour boundaries [S1, unofficial].
- Output: A decision to connect to vertex $V_{i,j+1}$ (vertical step) or $V_{i+1,j}$ (horizontal step), and marking the corresponding arc [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
- Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].

**Steps**
1. Form two tetrahedra using the current contour vertices and the interior points $O_p$ or $O_q$ [S1, unofficial].
2. Compute the volume of the vertical tetrahedron $T_{i,j}^v$ and the horizontal tetrahedron $T_{i,j}^h$ [S1, unofficial].
3. Compare the volumes: if $T_{i,j}^v > T_{i,j}^h$, consider the next vertex $V_{i,j+1}$; otherwise, mark the arc and consider the vertex $V_{i+1,j}$ [S1, unofficial].
4. Repeat the process along the contour segments [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**
The volume of a tetrahedron defined by vertices $A, B, C, D$ is computed as the standard scalar triple product formula:
$$ V = \frac{1}{6} | (B-A) \cdot ((C-A) \times (D-A)) | $$

The heuristic uses the following volume functions [S1, unofficial]:
$$ T_{i,j}^v = \text{Volume}(P_j, Q_i, Q_{i+1}, O_p) $$
$$ T_{i,j}^h = \text{Volume}(P_j, Q_{j+1}, Q_i, O_q) $$

Where $P_j$ and $Q$ indices represent contour vertices, and $O_p, O_q$ are interior points [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
- Interior points $O_p, O_q$: Pre-computed points strictly inside the contour regions [S1, unofficial]. Exact computation method is not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Pre-processing Requirement**: The calculation must be performed on a series of convex or concave parts, requiring significant pre-processing to decompose the contours [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def tet_vol(A, B, C, D):
    AB = (B[0]-A[0], B[1]-A[1], B[2]-A[2])
    AC = (C[0]-A[0], C[1]-A[1], C[2]-A[2])
    AD = (D[0]-A[0], D[1]-A[1], D[2]-A[2])
    cross = (AC[1]*AD[2]-AC[2]*AD[1], AC[2]*AD[0]-AC[0]*AD[2], AC[0]*AD[1]-AC[1]*AD[0])
    return abs(AB[0]*cross[0] + AB[1]*cross[1] + AB[2]*cross[2]) / 6.0

def keppel_heuristic(P_j, Q_i, Q_i_plus_1, Q_j_plus_1, O_p, O_q):
    vol_v = tet_vol(P_j, Q_i, Q_i_plus_1, O_p)
    vol_h = tet_vol(P_j, Q_j_plus_1, Q_i, O_q)
    if vol_v > vol_h:
        return 'vertical', 'V_{i,j+1}'
    else:
        return 'horizontal', 'V_{i+1,j}'
```
## Subtopics

### [Planar Contour Tiling](10346-planar-contour-tiling.md)
A global optimization method that uses graph theory to find the optimal surface, contrasting with Keppel's local greedy heuristic. Choose it when a rigorous, globally optimal tiling is required despite higher computational overhead.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Simple local decision logic; computationally inexpensive per step [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: Requires significant pre-processing to decompose contours into convex/concave parts and compute interior points [S1, unofficial]. Does not guarantee global optimality [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use**: When pre-processing is acceptable and a quick, local tiling solution is sufficient [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use**: When global surface optimality is required or when contours are complex and difficult to decompose [S1, unofficial].
## Background
The Keppel Heuristic was described by Keppel, as cited in Ganapathy and DeRose [GD82] [S1, unofficial]. It is one of the major heuristic methods for contour tiling, alongside Fuchs and Christianson/Ganapathy heuristics [S1, unofficial].
