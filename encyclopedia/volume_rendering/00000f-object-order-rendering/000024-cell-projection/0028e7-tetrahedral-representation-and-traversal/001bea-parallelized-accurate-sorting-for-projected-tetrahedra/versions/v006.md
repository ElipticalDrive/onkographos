# Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra

Also called: Logical partition sorting, Quick and accurate sorting algorithm of tetrahedral datasets

## Overview
Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra is a view-dependent sorting algorithm designed to resolve the parallelization bottlenecks inherent in standard projected tetrahedra rendering [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It solves the dependency and sequence limitations of neighboring tetrahedra by using logical partitioning to enable parallel processing [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its siblings, such as [Topological Sort for Cell Projection](999-topological-sort-for-cell-projection.md), by explicitly dividing space into blocks along the viewing direction to isolate independent subsets, thereby allowing accurate sorting via parallel peeling of unobstructed layers [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method processes tetrahedral datasets through four sequential steps: spatial partitioning, parallel sorting, boundary handling, and optical accumulation [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A tetrahedral dataset (set of tetrahedra with geometric and opacity attributes) and a camera view direction.
*   **Output:** A sorted list of tetrahedra ordered front-to-back along the viewing direction, ready for rasterization and compositing.

**Data Structures**
*   **Blocks:** A list of spatial regions (blocks) aligned with the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra.
*   **Layers:** Unobstructed subsets of tetrahedra peeled from the blocks during the sorting phase.
*   **Boundary Tetrahedra:** Tetrahedra that intersect the boundaries between blocks, requiring logical partitioning.

**Steps**
1.  **Spatial Partitioning:** Divide the space containing the tetrahedral datasets into a list of blocks along the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Parallel Sorting:** Sort the tetrahedra accurately by peeling tetrahedral subsets into unobstructed layers in parallel. This step leverages the independence of blocks to process subsets simultaneously [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Boundary Handling and Integration:** Partition tetrahedra on the boundary logically. Compute the optical contribution of each subset using segment integration [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Accumulation:** Accumulate the optical contribution of each subset along the viewing direction to form the final image [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Boundary Tetrahedra:** Tetrahedra intersecting block boundaries require logical partitioning to maintain correct front-to-back order across blocks [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. Failure to handle these correctly leads to visual artifacts.
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision in depth calculations may affect the accuracy of the peeling process, especially for large datasets [G].
*   **Scalability:** The method's performance depends on the efficiency of the parallel peeling step. For very large datasets, distributed or cloud-based architectures may be required, as addressed by [Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra](1173-multi-segment-and-multi-stage-projected-tetrahedra.md) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra](1173-multi-segment-and-multi-stage-projected-tetrahedra.md)
Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra is a technique designed to solve the time-consuming sorting problem inherent in Projected Tetrahedra rendering by partitioning datasets into multiple spatial segments and dividing the visualization procedure into multiple temporal stages. It sets itself apart from standard Projected Tetrahedra by introducing spatial partitioning to enable parallel sorting without write access violations, and temporal staging to satisfy varying user requirements and increase cognition. It builds on [Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra](1128-parallelized-accurate-sorting-for-projected-tetrahedra.md) by addressing the scalability limits of sorting large datasets through distributed or cloud-based architectures.

### GPU-Accelerated Cell Projection for Tetrahedral Datasets
A volume rendering technique for tetrahedral datasets that enhances parallelization by dividing space into blocks along the viewing direction, sorting tetrahedra by peeling unobstructed layers in parallel, partitioning boundary tetrahedra logically, and accumulating optical contributions via segment integration [S2, unofficial]. The algorithm reduces sorting time largely and improves rendering performance greatly compared to previous methods [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The sorting step is parallelized, reducing the overall sorting time significantly compared to sequential topological sorting [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. The exact complexity class is not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for blocks, layers, and boundary tetrahedra. The memory overhead is proportional to the number of tetrahedra and the block partitioning strategy [G].
*   **Measured Performance:** GPU implementation of the parallelized accurate sorting scheme achieves 91% acceleration compared with the CPU implementation [S1, unofficial]. The early tetrahedron termination scheme has a gain of more than 10% in terms of rendering performance for datasets with more than a million tetrahedra [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High parallelization efficiency, reduced sorting time, and improved rendering performance for large datasets [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Complexity in handling boundary tetrahedra and potential scalability issues for extremely large datasets without distributed architectures [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for real-time volume rendering of large tetrahedral datasets where parallel performance is critical [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not ideal for small datasets where the overhead of partitioning and parallel management may outweigh the benefits [G].

## Background
The method was proposed by Xin Li, W. Chen, X. Wu, and W. Zuo in the paper "Fast projected tetrahedra volume rendering based on parallelized accurate sorting" published in August 2013 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of previous topological sorting methods for tetrahedral datasets, which suffered from dependency and sequence limitations that hindered parallel performance [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. The technique is part of the broader cell projection family, which treats volumetric data as geometric primitives transformed and rasterized on the GPU [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial][S4, book].
