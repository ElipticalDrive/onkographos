# Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra

Also called: Logical partition sorting, Quick and accurate sorting algorithm of tetrahedral datasets

## Overview
Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra is a view-dependent sorting algorithm designed to resolve the parallelization bottlenecks inherent in standard projected tetrahedra rendering, where the topological dependency between neighboring tetrahedra prevents efficient concurrent processing [S1, unofficial]. By employing logical partitioning to divide the dataset into blocks along the viewing direction, the method enables the parallel sorting of tetrahedral subsets into unobstructed layers, significantly reducing sorting time and improving rendering performance compared to sequential or CPU-based approaches [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates on tetrahedral datasets projected onto the image plane, building on the principles of [Cell projection](26-cell-projection.md). It replaces the global topological sort with a block-based logical partitioning scheme that allows for parallel execution of the sorting and accumulation phases.

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A tetrahedral dataset (set of tetrahedra $T$), camera parameters (viewing direction), and transfer functions.
*   **Output:** A rendered image with correct front-to-back opacity accumulation.

**Data Structures**
*   **Blocks:** The 3D space containing the tetrahedra is divided into a list of blocks along the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra. The exact spatial partitioning geometry (e.g., uniform grid, octree) is not specified in the sources [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Layers:** Within each block, tetrahedra are organized into "unobstructed layers" during the sorting phase.

**Algorithm Steps**
The algorithm proceeds in four sequential steps [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]:

1.  **Block Division:** Divide the space containing the tetrahedral datasets into a list of blocks along the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra. This step decouples the global dependency, allowing independent processing of blocks.
2.  **Parallel Sorting (Peeling):** Sort tetrahedra accurately within each block by peeling tetrahedral subsets into unobstructed layers in parallel. This "peeling" process identifies the front-most tetrahedra that are not occluded by others within the same block or from previously processed blocks, establishing a valid front-to-back order for rendering.
3.  **Boundary Partitioning and Integration:** Partition tetrahedra located on the boundaries between blocks logically. Compute the optical contribution of each subset using segment integration. This step ensures that the optical properties (color and opacity) are correctly calculated for tetrahedra that may span block boundaries or interact with adjacent blocks' data.
4.  **Accumulation:** Accumulate the optical contribution of each subset along the viewing direction. The results from the parallel blocks are combined to form the final pixel colors.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Boundary Tetrahedra:** Tetrahedra that span block boundaries require logical partitioning to ensure correct optical contribution calculation and sorting order. Failure to handle these correctly can lead to visual artifacts [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The method relies on accurate sorting. Any errors in the "peeling" process can lead to incorrect occlusion and visual artifacts.

## Subtopics

### [Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra](1173-multi-segment-and-multi-stage-projected-tetrahedra.md)
Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra is a technique designed to solve the time-consuming sorting problem inherent in Projected Tetrahedra rendering by partitioning datasets into multiple spatial segments and dividing the visualization procedure into multiple temporal stages. It sets itself apart from standard Projected Tetrahedra by introducing spatial partitioning to enable parallel sorting without write access violations, and temporal staging to satisfy varying user requirements and increase cognition. It builds on [Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra](1128-parallelized-accurate-sorting-for-projected-tetrahedra.md) by addressing the scalability limits of sorting large datasets through distributed or cloud-based architectures.

### GPU-Accelerated Cell Projection for Tetrahedral Datasets
GPU-Accelerated Cell Projection for Tetrahedral Datasets is a volume rendering technique for tetrahedral datasets that enhances parallelization by dividing space into blocks along the viewing direction, sorting tetrahedra by peeling unobstructed layers in parallel, partitioning boundary tetrahedra logically, and accumulating optical contributions via segment integration [S2, unofficial]. It reduces sorting time largely and improves rendering performance greatly compared to previous methods [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method reduces sorting time largely compared to sequential topological sorting [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. The parallel nature of the block-based approach allows for significant speedups.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The block-based approach may require additional memory for storing block structures and intermediate layer data.
*   **Measured Performance:** GPU implementation of the parallelized accurate sorting scheme achieves 91% acceleration compared with the CPU implementation [S1, unofficial]. The early tetrahedron termination scheme has a gain of more than 10% in terms of rendering performance for datasets with more than a million tetrahedra [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High parallelization efficiency, significant performance improvement on GPUs, effective handling of large datasets with early termination.
*   **Limitations:** Complexity in handling boundary tetrahedra, dependency on efficient block partitioning strategies.
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for large-scale tetrahedral datasets where rendering performance is critical and GPU resources are available.
*   **When Not to Use:** May be overkill for small datasets where the overhead of block management and parallel synchronization outweighs the benefits.

## Background
The method was proposed by Xin Li, W. Chen, X. Wu, and W. Zuo in the paper "Fast projected tetrahedra volume rendering based on parallelized accurate sorting" published in August 2013 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of traditional projected tetrahedra rendering, which suffers from sequential dependencies that hinder parallel processing. The work builds on the foundational principles of cell projection and projected tetrahedra, aiming to enhance their parallelization capabilities for modern GPU architectures.
