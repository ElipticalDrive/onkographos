# Power Distance Sort

Also called: Circumsphere radius sort, Delaunay power sort, Power Distance Sort for Delaunay Tetrahedralization, Power Sorting, Radical axis sorting, Visibility sorting via circumcircles

## Overview
Power Distance Sort is a visibility sorting method that orders primitives (triangles or tetrahedra) back-to-front relative to a camera origin by comparing the power of the viewpoint with respect to the circumcircles or circumspheres of the primitives [S2]. It leverages geometric properties of Delaunay triangulations/tetrahedralizations to guarantee correct visibility ordering, whereas it is not guaranteed to produce accurate ordering for non-Delaunay meshes [S1][S4, unofficial]. Unlike topological sort, it avoids storing and accessing an adjacency structure [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of primitives $T_i$ (triangles in 2D or tetrahedra in 3D) forming a Delaunay triangulation/tetrahedralization [S2].
- A camera origin $\mathbf{o}$ [S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A sorted list of primitives in back-to-front order relative to $\mathbf{o}$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
- Each primitive $T$ stores its circumcenter $\mathbb{O}(T)$ and circumradius $\mathbb{R}(T)$ [S3, unofficial]. These are precomputed based on the vertices of $T$ [G].

**Steps:**
1. For each primitive $T$, compute the power of the viewpoint $\mathbf{o}$ relative to the circumcircle/circumsphere of $T$ [S3, unofficial].
2. Sort all primitives in decreasing order of this power value [S2].

**Formula:**
The power $P(T)$ of the viewpoint $\mathbf{o}$ with respect to the circumsphere of primitive $T$ is defined as [S3, unofficial]:

$$
P(T) = \|\mathbb{O}(T) - \mathbf{o}\|_2^2 - \mathbb{R}(T)^2
$$

Where:
- $\mathbb{O}(T)$ is the center of the circumsphere of $T$ [S3, unofficial].
- $\mathbb{R}(T)$ is the radius of the circumsphere of $T$ [S3, unofficial].
- $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin [S3, unofficial].
- $\|\cdot\|_2$ denotes the Euclidean norm [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_power_distance_sort(primitives, camera_origin):
    """
    Sorts primitives by power distance relative to camera_origin.
    Assumes primitives form a Delaunay triangulation/tetrahedralization.
    
    Args:
        primitives: List of primitive objects, each having attributes:
            - circumcenter: tuple (x, y) or (x, y, z)
            - circumradius: float
        camera_origin: tuple (x, y) or (x, y, z)
        
    Returns:
        List of primitives sorted from back to front (decreasing power distance).
    """
    scored_primitives = []
    for prim in primitives:
        # Calculate squared Euclidean distance between circumcenter and camera origin
        dx = prim.circumcenter[0] - camera_origin[0]
        dy = prim.circumcenter[1] - camera_origin[1]
        if len(prim.circumcenter) == 3:
            dz = prim.circumcenter[2] - camera_origin[2]
            dist_sq = dx*dx + dy*dy + dz*dz
        else:
            dist_sq = dx*dx + dy*dy
            
        # Power distance formula: ||O - o||^2 - R^2
        power = dist_sq - (prim.circumradius ** 2)
        scored_primitives.append((power, prim))
        
    # Sort by power distance descending (back-to-front for Delaunay meshes)
    scored_primitives.sort(key=lambda x: x[0], reverse=True)
    
    return [prim for _, prim in scored_primitives]
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Non-Delaunay Meshes:** The method is not guaranteed to produce an accurate visibility ordering unless the mesh is a Delaunay triangulation [S1][S4, unofficial]. There is no way to tell in advance whether any given non-Delaunay mesh will be correctly sorted [S1].
- **Viewpoint Position:** The sort order is relative to the ray origin, making it valid for fisheye lenses and other distorted projections [S3, unofficial]. However, simple schemes like power distance sorts may fail from certain viewpoints or viewing directions [S4, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Computing circumcenters and radii can be numerically unstable for nearly cospherical points, which may lead to incorrect sorting if precision is insufficient [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(n \log n)$ due to sorting, where $n$ is the number of primitives [S2].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(n)$ to store the power values and primitive references [G].
- **Performance:** Fast with very small constant computations and straightforward implementation for certain meshes and viewpoints [S1][S4, unofficial][S5, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Works with distorted projections (e.g., fisheye) because it is based on the camera origin rather than a view direction [S3, unofficial]. It is competitive with topological sort in practice because it avoids storing and accessing the adjacency structure [S2].
- **Limitations:** Not applicable to general non-Delaunay meshes without guarantees of correctness [S1]. Accuracy is not predictable for arbitrary meshes [S1].

## Background
Power Distance Sort was described by Cignoni et al. and Wittenbrink [S1][S4, unofficial]. Edelsbrunner (1989) proved that for a Delaunay tetrahedralization, no visibility cycles are possible and a decreasing sort on the power distance yields a correct visibility sort [S2]. It leverages the geometric property that for two adjacent triangles in a Delaunay triangulation, the shared edge lies along the radical axis of their circumcircles [S3, unofficial]. The radical axis divides the plane into two regions where the viewpoint has a lower power distance to one circumcircle or the other [S3, unofficial]. Since the relative depth order of the two triangles flips only when the viewpoint crosses this axis, sorting by power distance yields the correct visibility order [S3, unofficial]. This property naturally generalizes to 3D, where the shared face of two tetrahedra lies on the radical plane [S3, unofficial].
