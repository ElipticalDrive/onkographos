# Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra

Also called: Logical partition sorting, Quick and accurate sorting algorithm of tetrahedral datasets

## Overview
Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra is a view-dependent sorting algorithm designed to resolve the parallelization bottlenecks inherent in topological sorting for tetrahedral datasets [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It solves the dependency and sequence limitations of neighboring tetrahedra by using logical partitioning to divide the space into blocks along the viewing direction, enabling the parallel peeling of unobstructed layers [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard topological sorts by decoupling the strict global ordering into local block-level parallel operations, and from [Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra](1173-multi-segment-and-multi-stage-projected-tetrahedra.md) by focusing on the single-pass sorting mechanism rather than distributed spatial partitioning or temporal staging.

## How it works
The method processes tetrahedral datasets through four sequential steps: spatial blocking, parallel peeling, logical boundary partitioning, and optical accumulation.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A tetrahedral dataset (vertices, connectivity, scalar values) and a camera view direction.
*   **Output:** A correctly ordered sequence of tetrahedra (or their projected fragments) for back-to-front compositing.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Blocks:** A list of spatial regions (blocks) defined along the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra. The exact geometric definition of a "block" (e.g., axis-aligned bounding boxes, depth-based slices) is not specified in the sources [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Layers:** Unobstructed subsets of tetrahedra identified during the peeling process.

**Steps:**

1.  **Divide Space into Blocks:**
    The space containing the tetrahedral dataset is divided into a list of blocks along the viewing direction. Each block contains a subset of tetrahedra [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

2.  **Parallel Sorting by Peeling:**
    Tetrahedral subsets within each block are sorted accurately by "peeling" them into unobstructed layers in parallel. This step identifies which tetrahedra are visible from the current viewpoint without being occluded by others within the block or previously processed layers [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

3.  **Logical Boundary Partitioning and Integration:**
    Tetrahedra located on the boundaries between blocks or layers are partitioned logically. The optical contribution of each subset is computed using segment integration. This involves calculating the transmittance and color contribution of the tetrahedra within the subset, likely using the pre-integration formulas from [Cell projection](26-cell-projection.md) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

4.  **Accumulation:**
    The optical contribution of each subset is accumulated along the viewing direction to produce the final image [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

    # Step 1: Divide space into blocks along viewing direction

    blocks = divide_space_into_blocks(tetrahedra, view_direction)
    
    sorted_tetrahedra = []
    
    # Step 2: Sort accurately by peeling tetrahedral subsets into unobstructed layers in parallel
    # This implies a parallel reduction or layer-peeling algorithm per block
    # The specific peeling logic (e.g., depth comparison, occlusion culling) is not detailed [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]
    for block in blocks:
        layers = peel_unobstructed_layers(block.tetrahedra)
        for layer in layers:
            sorted_tetrahedra.extend(layer)
            
    # Step 3: Partition tetrahedra on boundary logically and compute optical contribution
    # Boundary tetrahedra are handled to ensure correct ordering across block boundaries
    # Optical contribution is computed via segment integration

    processed_tetrahedra = []
    for tet in sorted_tetrahedra:
        if is_boundary_tetrahedron(tet):

        optical_contribution = compute_segment_integration(tet)
        processed_tetrahedra.append((tet, optical_contribution))
        
    # Step 4: Accumulate optical contribution along viewing direction

    return image

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Tetrahedra:** Tetrahedra crossing block boundaries require logical partitioning to maintain correct sorting order. The specific method for this partitioning is not detailed [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion:** The "peeling" process must correctly identify unobstructed layers. If occlusion is not handled accurately, sorting errors may occur [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra](1173-multi-segment-and-multi-stage-projected-tetrahedra.md)
Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra is a technique designed to solve the time-consuming sorting problem inherent in Projected Tetrahedra rendering by partitioning datasets into multiple spatial segments and dividing the visualization procedure into multiple temporal stages. It sets itself apart from standard Projected Tetrahedra by introducing spatial partitioning to enable parallel sorting without write access violations, and temporal staging to satisfy varying user requirements and increase cognition. It builds on [Parallelized Accurate Sorting for Projected Tetrahedra](1128-parallelized-accurate-sorting-for-projected-tetrahedra.md) by addressing the scalability limits of sorting large datasets through distributed or cloud-based architectures.

### GPU-Accelerated Cell Projection for Tetrahedral Datasets
A volume rendering technique for tetrahedral datasets that enhances parallelization by dividing space into blocks along the viewing direction, sorting tetrahedra by peeling unobstructed layers in parallel, partitioning boundary tetrahedra logically, and accumulating optical contributions via segment integration [S2, unofficial]. The algorithm reduces sorting time largely and improves rendering performance greatly compared to previous methods [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The algorithm reduces sorting time largely compared to previous methods [S2, unofficial]. The GPU implementation achieves 91% acceleration compared with the CPU implementation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** For datasets with more than a million tetrahedra, the early tetrahedron termination scheme (leveraging a summed-area table) has a gain of more than 10% in terms of rendering performance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances the parallelization of topological sorting, reduces sorting time, and improves rendering performance greatly [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The method is view-dependent, requiring sorting by frame [S1, unofficial]. The specific scalability limits of the base algorithm are addressed by its child, Multi-segment and Multi-stage Projected Tetrahedra, implying potential bottlenecks in very large datasets [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering large tetrahedral datasets on GPU and parallelization is critical [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the dataset is small and the overhead of partitioning is not justified, or when a sorting-free method (like [Sorting-free Pre-integrated Projected Tetrahedra](7491-sorting-free-pre-integrated-projected-tetrahedra.md)) is applicable.

## Background
The method was proposed by Xin Li, W. Chen, X. Wu, and W. Zuo in the paper "Fast projected tetrahedra volume rendering based on parallelized accurate sorting" published in August 2013 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of parallel performance in topological sorting for tetrahedral datasets, where the sequence and dependency of neighboring tetrahedra limit parallelization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. The technique is also referenced in the context of GPU-accelerated cell projection [S2, unofficial].
