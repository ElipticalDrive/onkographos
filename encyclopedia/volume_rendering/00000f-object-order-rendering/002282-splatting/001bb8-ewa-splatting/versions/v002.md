# EWA Splatting

Also called: Elliptical Weighted Average Splatting, Surface Splatting, GPU accelerated surface splatting, Point rendering with texture filtering

## Overview
EWA Splatting is an object-order rendering technique that approximates the volumetric integration of 3D Gaussian primitives by projecting them into 2D elliptical disks (splats) and rasterizing them as screen-space filters [S1]. It solves the computational cost of ray-marching by assuming Gaussians do not overlap and are sorted by view-space depth, trading continuous extinction accumulation for efficient, hole-free reconstruction [S1, S2]. It differs from standard point rendering by using anisotropic, band-limited elliptical footprints derived from the Gaussian covariance, whereas simplified variants use screen-aligned spherical impostors with isotropic falloff [S2, S3].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians, each defined by weight $w_i$, mean position $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ (in camera space), and $3 \times 3$ covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ [G]. Camera projection matrix $\mathbf{P}$ and view matrix $\mathbf{W}$ [G].
**Outputs:** A 2D image with accumulated color and depth [G].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Surfel:** Vertex buffer layout (stride 64 bytes) [S2]:
    *   Offset 0: `center` (vec3, 3 floats)
    *   Offset 12: `tangent_u` (vec3, 3 floats)
    *   Offset 24: `tangent_v` (vec3, 3 floats)
    *   Offset 36: `clipping_plane` (vec3, 3 floats)
    *   Offset 48: `color_rgba` (vec4, 4 unsigned bytes)
*   **Uniforms:**
    *   `UniformBufferParameter`: `color` (vec3, offset 0), `shininess` (float, offset 12), `radius_scale` (float, offset 16), `ewa_radius` (float, offset 20), `epsilon` (float, offset 24). Total 8 floats [S2].
    *   `UniformBufferFrustum`: 6 frustum planes (vec4) [S2].
    *   `UniformBufferRaycast`: `projection_matrix_inv` (mat4), `viewport` (vec4) [S2].

**Steps:**

1.  **Frustum Culling:** Extract 6 frustum planes from the projection matrix. A splat is visible if its bounding sphere intersects the view frustum [S2].
    ```python
    # Extract 6 frustum planes from projection matrix P (4x4 list of lists)
    frustum_planes = []
    for i in range(6):
        sign = -1.0 if i % 2 == 0 else 1.0
        row_idx = i // 2
        plane = [P[3][j] + sign * P[row_idx][j] for j in range(4)]
        norm = (plane[0]**2 + plane[1]**2 + plane[2]**2)**0.5
        plane = [x / norm for x in plane]
        frustum_planes.append(plane)
    ```

2.  **2D Gaussian Projection (EWA):** Project the 3D Gaussian to 2D screen space to determine the splat's shape and position [S1].
    *   Compute the Jacobian $\mathbf{J}$ of the perspective projection, approximated by first-order Taylor expansion at the Gaussian mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathbf{J} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/\mu_2 & 0 & -\mu_0/\mu_2^2 \\ 0 & 1/\mu_2 & -\mu_1/\mu_2^2 \\ \mu_0/l & \mu_1/l & \mu_2/l \end{pmatrix} $$
        where $l = \|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (\mu_0, \mu_1, \mu_2)$ is the mean in camera space [S1].
    *   Compute the projected 2D covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'$ [S1]:
        $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}' = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{W} \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{W}^T \mathbf{J}^T $$
        where $\mathbf{W}$ transforms to camera space [S1].
    *   The projected 2D Gaussian is $\mathcal{G}_2^n(\mathbf{p}, w_i, \boldsymbol{\mu}'_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i)$ [S1]. The weight $w$ is preserved across projection [S1].

3.  **Sorting:** Sort all visible Gaussians by their view-space depth (z-coordinate of $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$) from front to back [S1].

4.  **Rasterization (Three-Pass with Soft Z-Buffer):**
    *   **Bandlimiting Prefilter:** Generate a 1D Gaussian filter kernel with $\sigma^2 = 0.1$ (using $\sigma = 0.316228$) to avoid frequencies higher than the Nyquist frequency [S2].
    *   **Pass 1 (Visibility/Depth):**
        *   Bind FBO, clear color and depth buffers [S2].
        *   Render splats as point sprites [S3].
        *   Set `gl_PointSize` based on the projected radius $r$ [S3]:
            $$ s = h \cdot \cot\left(\frac{\theta_{fov}}{2}\right) \cdot \frac{r}{w_{clip}} $$
            where $h$ is viewport height, $\theta_{fov}$ is FOV, and $w_{clip}$ is clip-space w [S3].
        *   Compute analytic front-surface depth of the sphere impostor for each fragment [S3]:
            $$ z_{surface} = z_{center} - \sqrt{r^2 - x^2 - y^2} $$
            where $x, y$ are fragment coordinates relative to splat center [S3].
        *   Push depth back by $\varepsilon$ before writing to define a thin depth slab [S3].
        *   Discard fragments outside the unit disk [S3].
        *   Write only depth; mask off color [S3].
    *   **Pass 2 (Attribute Blending):**
        *   Disable depth writes and depth testing [S3].
        *   Set blending to pure additive (`GL_ONE`, `GL_ONE`) [S3].
        *   Rasterize splats again [S3].
        *   For each fragment, compare its depth against the depth texture from Pass 1. Discard fragments falling behind the $\varepsilon$ slab [S3].
        *   Accumulate attributes with weight $w = w_{radial} \cdot w_{depth}$ [S3]:
            *   Radial falloff: $w_{radial} = 1 - \frac{d}{r}$, where $d$ is distance from splat center [S3].
            *   Depth falloff: $w_{depth} = \frac{z_{surface} - z_{frag}}{\varepsilon}$, where $z_{frag}$ is current fragment depth [S3].
    *   **Pass 3 (Normalization/Shading):**
        *   Render a full-screen quad [S3].
        *   Compute weighted average color and normal [S3]:
            $$ \mathbf{c} = \frac{\sum_i w_i \mathbf{c}_i}{\sum_i w_i}, \quad \mathbf{n} = \frac{\sum_i w_i \mathbf{n}_i}{\lVert \sum_i w_i \mathbf{n}_i \rVert} $$
        *   Apply Phong lighting [S3].

**Parameters:**
*   `m_epsilon`: Epsilon value for numerical stability and soft z-buffer depth blending precision, default $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ [S2]. Defines the thickness of the thin depth slab around the visible surface [S3].
*   `m_radius_scale`: Scale factor for splat radius, default $1.0$ [S2].
*   `m_ewa_radius`: Radius for EWA filter approximation, default $1.0$ [S2].
*   `sigma2`: Gaussian filter kernel variance, $0.316228^2 \approx 0.1$ [S2].
*   `m_shininess`: Shininess value for Phong splatting, default $8.0$ [S2].
*   `m_smooth`: Boolean flag that enables depth and normal texture attachment on the FBO, and enables blending/smoothing in the finalization pass [S2].
*   `m_multisample`: Boolean flag that enables multisampling [S2].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Botsch et al.'s approximation of screen-space extents can underestimate splat size, causing chopping artifacts; a safety factor $>1$ is required [S2].
*   Zwicker et al.'s centralized conics approach is numerically unstable when projection approaches a line [S2].
*   Weyrich et al.'s method yields NDC coordinates, preventing frustum culling, and fails if the projected ellipse becomes a parabola/hyperbola [S2].
*   EWA filter requires soft z-buffer to be active; otherwise, it is disabled [S2].
*   Depth mask is explicitly disabled during splatting when soft z-buffer is enabled to allow alpha blending to accumulate depth correctly [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Hole-free reconstruction, efficient rasterization, avoids expensive volume integration [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes no overlap; continuous extinction is ignored [S1]. Approximation errors in extreme foreshortening [S2].
*   **When to use:** Real-time rendering of point clouds or Gaussian splats where visual quality and speed are prioritized over physical accuracy of attenuation [S1, S2].

## Background
EWA Splatting originated from the need to render point-sampled surfaces without aliasing or holes [S2]. It was formalized by Zwicker et al. (2001) as "Surface Splatting," using Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) filters to project object-space reconstruction filters to screen space [S2]. It builds on [Gaussian Splatting](946-gaussian-splatting.md) by providing the rasterization backend for 3D Gaussian primitives, approximating the volumetric integral via 2D projections [S1].
