# 2D Gaussian Projection with Covariance Stabilization

Also called: 2D Gaussian Projection, 2D Image Generation from 3D Gaussians, 3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering Pipeline, Jacobian Linearization, Perspective Projection of 3D Gaussians, Rasterization of 3D Gaussians, Tsplat projection

## Overview
This method projects a set of 3D Gaussians onto a 2D image plane to generate a rendered image, solving the problem of efficiently rasterizing volumetric scene representations without explicit ray-marching. It differs from sibling methods like [Ray-marching on 3D Gaussians](1036-ray-marching-on-3d-gaussians.md) by using explicit screen-space splatting with alpha compositing, and from [Row-constant hoisting for Gaussian Splatting Rasterization](1195-row-constant-hoisting-for-gaussian-splatting-rasterization.md) by defining the core projection and covariance stabilization logic rather than just the inner-loop optimization. The key distinction is the linearization of the perspective projection via the Jacobian to approximate the resulting 2D Gaussian, combined with a diagonal epsilon stabilization to ensure numerical invertibility.

## How it works
The method computes the 2D screen-space covariance matrix and its inverse for each 3D Gaussian splat, ensuring numerical stability by adding a small epsilon to the diagonal. Since perspective projection is a nonlinear transform, the result is not an exact 2D Gaussian; the method approximates it by locally linearizing the projection function using its Jacobian at the splat center [S1, S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Splats:** A collection of $M$ 3D Gaussians. Each splat $i$ is defined by learnable parameters:
    *   Center $p_w \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (world coordinates).
    *   Rotation $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion).
    *   Scale $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (positive vector).
    *   Opacity $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.
    *   Spherical Harmonics coefficients $h$ (view-dependent color).
*   **Camera:**
    *   Rotation $R_{cw} \in SO(3)$ and translation $t_{cw} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (world-to-camera transform).
    *   Intrinsics $K$: focal lengths $f_x, f_y$ and principal point $c_x, c_y$.
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $\epsilon_{2d}$: Small dilation for covariance stabilization (default 0.3) [S1].
    *   $l_{max}$: Maximum degree of spherical harmonics (typically 3) [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   A 2D image (framebuffer) where each pixel $j$ contains an accumulated RGB color $\gamma_j$.

### Data Structures
The projected splat data structure, stored after projection and sorting, contains:
*   `screen`: `Vec2` (pixel center $sx, sy$).
*   `depth`: `f32` ($z_c$, positive in front of camera).
*   `cov2d_inv`: `Mat2` (inverse 2D covariance matrix).
*   `bbox`: `[i32; 4]` (inclusive bounding box $x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1$).
*   `color`: `Vec3` (RGB color).
*   `opacity`: `f32` (intrinsic opacity $\alpha$).

### Steps

1.  **Transform to Camera Coordinates:**
    Transform the 3D Gaussian center $p_w$ to camera coordinates $p_c$:
    $$ p_c = R_{cw} p_w + t_{cw} $$
    Let $p_c = (x, y, z)^T$. If $z \le 0$, the splat is behind the camera and is culled [S2].

2.  **Compute 3D Covariance Matrix:**
    Construct the 3x3 symmetric positive-definite covariance matrix $\Sigma$ from rotation $q$ and scale $s$:
    $$ \Sigma = R S S^T R^T $$
    Where $R$ is the rotation matrix derived from $q$:
    $$ R = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 2(q_y^2 + q_z^2) & 2(q_xq_y - q_zq_w) & 2(q_xq_z + q_yq_w) \\ 2(q_xq_y + q_zq_w) & 1 - 2(q_x^2 + q_z^2) & 2(q_yq_z - q_xq_w) \\ 2(q_xq_z - q_yq_w) & 2(q_yq_z + q_xq_w) & 1 - 2(q_x^2 + q_y^2) \end{bmatrix} $$
    And $S$ is the diagonal scale matrix:
    $$ S = \begin{bmatrix} s_0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & s_1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & s_2 \end{bmatrix} $$
    $\Sigma$ is stored as a 6-element upper triangular vector $\sigma$ for efficiency [S2].

3.  **Compute Jacobian of Projection:**
    The projection mapping from view space $(x, y, z)$ to pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ is:
    $$ u = f_x \frac{x}{z} + c_x, \quad v = f_y \frac{y}{z} + c_y $$
    The Jacobian $J$ of this function with respect to $(x, y, z)$ is:
    $$ J = \begin{bmatrix} f_x/z & 0 & -f_x x/z^2 \\ 0 & f_y/z & -f_y y/z^2 \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S2].

4.  **Compute 2D Covariance Matrix:**
    Project the 3D covariance using the Jacobian and the camera rotation part of the view transform. The 2D covariance $\Sigma'$ is:
    $$ \Sigma' = J R_{cw} \Sigma R_{cw}^T J^T $$
    Note: $R_{cw}$ here refers to the upper-left 3x3 rotation component. The result $\Sigma'$ is a 2x2 symmetric matrix. To ensure numerical stability and positive definiteness, a small epsilon $\epsilon_{2d}$ is added to the diagonal entries:
    $$ cov2d_{00} = \Sigma'_{00} + \epsilon_{2d} $$
    $$ cov2d_{11} = \Sigma'_{11} + \epsilon_{2d} $$
    $$ cov2d_{01} = \Sigma'_{01} $$
    [S1]. This addition guarantees that $x^T (A^T A + \lambda I) x = \|Ax\|^2 + \lambda \|x\|^2 > 0$, making the matrix strictly positive definite and invertible [S1].

5.  **Compute Inverse 2D Covariance:**
    Calculate the determinant:
    $$ det = cov2d_{00} cov2d_{11} - cov2d_{01}^2 $$
    If $det \le 0.0$, the splat is degenerate or invalid; return None (cull splat) [S1].
    Compute the inverse determinant:
    $$ inv\_det = 1.0 / det $$
    Construct the inverse matrix:
    $$ cov2d\_inv = \frac{1}{det} \begin{bmatrix} cov2d_{11} & -cov2d_{01} \\ -cov2d_{01} & cov2d_{00} \end{bmatrix} $$
    Explicitly:
    $$ cov2d\_inv = \begin{bmatrix} cov2d_{11} \cdot inv\_det & -cov2d_{01} \cdot inv\_det \\ -cov2d_{01} \cdot inv\_det & cov2d_{00} \cdot inv\_det \end{bmatrix} $$
    [S1].

6.  **Compute Screen Position:**
    Perform the perspective divide to get screen coordinates $(sx, sy)$:
    $$ sx = f_x \frac{x}{z} + c_x $$
    $$ sy = f_y \frac{y}{z} + c_y $$
    [S1, S2].

7.  **Compute View-Dependent Color:**
    Calculate the direction vector $r$ from the camera center to the Gaussian center in world coordinates:
    $$ r = \frac{p_w - t_{wc}}{\lVert p_w - t_{wc} \rVert} $$
    where $t_{wc}$ is the camera center in world coordinates (inverse of $t_{cw}$ relative to world) [S2].
    Evaluate the color $c$ using spherical harmonics:
    $$ c(r, h) = \sum_{l=0}^{l_{max}} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} h_{lm} Y_l^m(r) $$
    [S2].

8.  **Compute Bounding Box:**
    Compute a tight bounding box based on an opacity threshold $\tau$ (e.g., 0.05). Calculate $k^2$:
    $$ k^2 = \min(2.0 \cdot \ln(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}), max\_k2) $$
    If $k^2 \le 0.0$, return None [S1].
    Compute half-widths:
    $$ rx_f = \sqrt{k^2 \cdot cov2d_{00}} $$
    $$ ry_f = \sqrt{k^2 \cdot cov2d_{11}} $$
    If not finite or $< 1.0$, return None [S1].
    Compute integer bounding box:
    $$ x_0 = \lfloor sx - rx_f \rfloor, \quad y_0 = \lfloor sy - ry_f \rfloor $$
    $$ x_1 = \lceil sx + rx_f \rceil, \quad y_1 = \lceil sy + ry_f \rceil $$
    Clip to framebuffer dimensions $[0, w-1] \times [0, h-1]$. If $x_0 > x_1$ or $y_0 > y_1$, return None [S1].

9.  **Sort and Composite:**
    Sort all projected splats by depth $z$ (ascending, closest first) [S2].
    Initialize framebuffer with $(0, 0)$ for $(RGB, \alpha)$.
    For each pixel $j$, iterate through splats $i$ that project to it (sorted front-to-back):
    $$ \alpha_{ij}' = \exp(-0.5 (u_i - x_j) \Sigma_i'^{-1} (u_i - x_j)^T) \alpha_i $$
    $$ \tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_{kj}') $$
    $$ \gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha_{ij}' c_i \tau_{ij} $$
    where $N$ is the set of Gaussians projecting to pixel $j$, $u_i$ is the projected center, and $x_j$ is the pixel coordinate [S2].

```python
import math
from typing import List, Tuple, Optional

# Vector and Matrix helpers (standard math operations)
def vec2(x, y): return (x, y)
def vec3(x, y, z): return (x, y, z)
def mat2_from_cols(c0, c1): return ((c0[0], c1[0]), (c0[1], c1[1]))
def mat2_mul(a, b):
    return (
        (a[0][0]*b[0][0] + a[0][1]*b[1][0], a[0][0]*b[0][1] + a[0][1]*b[1][1]),
        (a[1][0]*b[0][0] + a[1][1]*b[1][0], a[1][0]*b[0][1] + a[1][1]*b[1][1])
    )
def mat2_transpose(m):
    return ((m[0][0], m[1][0]), (m[0][1], m[1][1]))
def mat2_mul_vec(m, v):
    return (m[0][0]*v[0] + m[0][1]*v[1], m[1][0]*v[0] + m[1][1]*v[1])
def vec3_mul_mat(v, m):
    return (v[0]*m[0][0] + v[1]*m[1][0] + v[2]*m[2][0],
            v[0]*m[0][1] + v[1]*m[1][1] + v[2]*m[2][1],
            v[0]*m[0][2] + v[1]*m[1][2] + v[2]*m[2][2])

def quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q):
    qw, qx, qy, qz = q
    return (
        (1 - 2*(qy**2 + qz**2), 2*(qx*qy - qz*qw), 2*(qx*qz + qy*qw)),
        (2*(qx*qy + qz*qw), 1 - 2*(qx**2 + qz**2), 2*(qy*qz - qx*qw)),
        (2*(qx*qz - qy*qw), 2*(qy*qz + qx*qw), 1 - 2*(qx**2 + qy**2))
    )

def project_splat(splat, camera, epsilon_2d=0.3):
    """
    Projects a single 3D Gaussian splat to 2D screen space.
    Returns a ProjectedSplat struct or None if culled.
    """
    # 1. Transform to camera coordinates
    # p_c = R_cw * p_w + t_cw
    # Note: camera.R_cw is 3x3, camera.t_cw is 3x1
    p_w = splat.p_w
    R_cw = camera.R_cw
    t_cw = camera.t_cw
    
    # Rotation part
    rx = R_cw[0][0]*p_w[0] + R_cw[0][1]*p_w[1] + R_cw[0][2]*p_w[2]
    ry = R_cw[1][0]*p_w[0] + R_cw[1][1]*p_w[1] + R_cw[1][2]*p_w[2]
    rz = R_cw[2][0]*p_w[0] + R_cw[2][1]*p_w[1] + R_cw[2][2]*p_w[2]
    
    x = rx + t_cw[0]
    y = ry + t_cw[1]
    z = rz + t_cw[2]
    
    if z <= 0.0:
        return None # Behind camera

    # 2. Compute 3D Covariance Sigma
    R_mat = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(splat.q)
    S_diag = splat.s # (s0, s1, s2)
    
    # Sigma = R * S * S^T * R^T
    # S*S^T is diagonal with s_i^2
    ss = (S_diag[0]**2, S_diag[1]**2, S_diag[2]**2)
    
    # Compute R * diag(ss)
    # Col 0 of result is R_col0 * ss[0]
    r_diag = (
        (R_mat[0][0]*ss[0], R_mat[0][1]*ss[1], R_mat[0][2]*ss[2]),
        (R_mat[1][0]*ss[0], R_mat[1][1]*ss[1], R_mat[1][2]*ss[2]),
        (R_mat[2][0]*ss[0], R_mat[2][1]*ss[1], R_mat[2][2]*ss[2])
    )
    
    # Sigma = r_diag * R^T
    # Sigma is symmetric, we only need upper triangular or full for Jacobian mult
    # Sigma_ij = sum_k r_diag[i][k] * R_mat[j][k]
    sigma = (
        (r_diag[0][0]*R_mat[0][0] + r_diag[0][1]*R_mat[1][0] + r_diag[0][2]*R_mat[2][0],
         r_diag[0][0]*R_mat[0][1] + r_diag[0][1]*R_mat[1][1] + r_diag[0][2]*R_mat[2][1],
         r_diag[0][0]*R_mat[0][2] + r_diag[0][1]*R_mat[1][2] + r_diag[0][2]*R_mat[2][2]),
        (r_diag[1][0]*R_mat[0][0] + r_diag[1][1]*R_mat[1][0] + r_diag[1][2]*R_mat[2][0],
         r_diag[1][0]*R_mat[0][1] + r_diag[1][1]*R_mat[1][1] + r_diag[1][2]*R_mat[2][1],
         r_diag[1][0]*R_mat[0][2] + r_diag[1][1]*R_mat[1][2] + r_diag[1][2]*R_mat[2][2]),
        (r_diag[2][0]*R_mat[0][0] + r_diag[2][1]*R_mat[1][0] + r_diag[2][2]*R_mat[2][0],
         r_diag[2][0]*R_mat[0][1] + r_diag[2][1]*R_mat[1][1] + r_diag[2][2]*R_mat[2][1],
         r_diag[2][0]*R_mat[0][2] + r_diag[2][1]*R_mat[1][2] + r_diag[2][2]*R_mat[2][2])
    )

    # 3. Compute Jacobian J
    fx = camera.fx
    fy = camera.fy
    inv_z = 1.0 / z
    inv_z2 = inv_z * inv_z
    
    J = (
        (fx * inv_z, 0.0, -fx * x * inv_z2),
        (0.0, fy * inv_z, -fy * y * inv_z2)
    )

    # 4. Compute 2D Covariance Sigma' = J * R_cw * Sigma * R_cw^T * J^T
    # First: T = R_cw * Sigma
    # T_ij = sum_k R_cw[i][k] * Sigma[k][j]
    T = (
        (R_cw[0][0]*sigma[0][0] + R_cw[0][1]*sigma[1][0] + R_cw[0][2]*sigma[2][0],
         R_cw[0][0]*sigma[0][1] + R_cw[0][1]*sigma[1][1] + R_cw[0][2]*sigma[2][1],
         R_cw[0][0]*sigma[0][2] + R_cw[0][1]*sigma[1][2] + R_cw[0][2]*sigma[2][2]),
        (R_cw[1][0]*sigma[0][0] + R_cw[1][1]*sigma[1][0] + R_cw[1][2]*sigma[2][0],
         R_cw[1][0]*sigma[0][1] + R_cw[1][1]*sigma[1][1] + R_cw[1][2]*sigma[2][1],
         R_cw[1][0]*sigma[0][2] + R_cw[1][1]*sigma[1][2] + R_cw[1][2]*sigma[2][2]),
        (R_cw[2][0]*sigma[0][0] + R_cw[2][1]*sigma[1][0] + R_cw[2][2]*sigma[2][0],
         R_cw[2][0]*sigma[0][1] + R_cw[2][1]*sigma[1][1] + R_cw[2][2]*sigma[2][1],
         R_cw[2][0]*sigma[0][2] + R_cw[2][1]*sigma[1][2] + R_cw[2][2]*sigma[2][2])
    )
    
    # Second: U = T * R_cw^T
    # U_ij = sum_k T[i][k] * R_cw[j][k]
    U = (
        (T[0][0]*R_cw[0][0] + T[0][1]*R_cw[0][1] + T[0][2]*R_cw[0][2],
         T[0][0]*R_cw[1][0] + T[0][1]*R_cw[1][1] + T[0][2]*R_cw[1][2],
         T[0][0]*R_cw[2][0] + T[0][1]*R_cw[2][1] + T[0][2]*R_cw[2][2]),
        (T[1][0]*R_cw[0][0] + T[1][1]*R_cw[0][1] + T[1][2]*R_cw[0][2],
         T[1][0]*R_cw[1][0] + T[1][1]*R_cw[1][1] + T[1][2]*R_cw[1][2],
         T[1][0]*R_cw[2][0] + T[1][1]*R_cw[2][1] + T[1][2]*R_cw[2][2]),
        (T[2][0]*R_cw[0][0] + T[2][1]*R_cw[0][1] + T[2][2]*R_cw[0][2],
         T[2][0]*R_cw[1][0] + T[2][1]*R_cw[1][1] + T[2][2]*R_cw[1][2],
         T[2][0]*R_cw[2][0] + T[2][1]*R_cw[2][1] + T[2][2]*R_cw[2][2])
    )
    
    # Third: Sigma_prime = J * U
    # Sigma_prime is 2x2
    cov2d_00 = J[0][0]*U[0][0] + J[0][1]*U[1][0] + J[0][2]*U[2][0]
    cov2d_01 = J[0][0]*U[0][1] + J[0][1]*U[1][1] + J[0][2]*U[2][1]
    cov2d_11 = J[1][0]*U[0][1] + J[1][1]*U[1][1] + J[1][2]*U[2][1] # Symmetric: U[1][0] == U[0][1] usually, but let's be safe
    
    # Actually Sigma_prime is symmetric. 
    # Sigma_prime[0][1] = J[0][0]*U[0][1] + J[0][1]*U[1][1] + J[0][2]*U[2][1]
    # Sigma_prime[1][0] = J[1][0]*U[0][0] + J[1][1]*U[1][0] + J[1][2]*U[2][0]
    # Due to symmetry of Sigma and R, these should be equal. We use cov2d_01 for both.
    
    # 5. Stabilize Covariance
    cov2d_00 += epsilon_2d
    cov2d_11 += epsilon_2d
    
    # 6. Compute Inverse Covariance
    det = cov2d_00 * cov2d_11 - cov2d_01 * cov2d_01
    if det <= 0.0:
        return None
        
    inv_det = 1.0 / det
    cov2d_inv = mat2_from_cols(
        (cov2d_11 * inv_det, -cov2d_01 * inv_det),
        (-cov2d_01 * inv_det, cov2d_00 * inv_det)
    )
    
    # 7. Screen Position
    sx = fx * x * inv_z + camera.cx
    sy = fy * y * inv_z + camera.cy
    
    # 8. Bounding Box
    # k^2 = min(2 * ln(alpha / threshold), max_k2)
    # Assuming threshold and max_k2 are passed or global defaults
    threshold = 0.05
    max_k2 = 4.0 # Example default
    alpha = splat.alpha
    if alpha <= threshold:
        return None
        
    k2_val = 2.0 * math.log(alpha / threshold)
    k2 = min(k2_val, max_k2)
    
    if k2 <= 0.0:
        return None
        
    rx_f = math.sqrt(k2 * cov2d_00)
    ry_f = math.sqrt(k2 * cov2d_11)
    
    if not math.isfinite(rx_f) or rx_f < 1.0:
        return None
    if not math.isfinite(ry_f) or ry_f < 1.0:
        return None
        
    x0 = math.floor(sx - rx_f)
    y0 = math.floor(sy - ry_f)
    x1 = math.ceil(sx + rx_f)
    y1 = math.ceil(sy + ry_f)
    
    # Clip to image bounds (w, h)
    # w, h are image dimensions
    # x0 = max(0, x0) ... etc.
    
    # 9. Color
    # Direction r
    t_wc = camera.t_wc # Camera center in world
    dx = p_w[0] - t_wc[0]
    dy = p_w[1] - t_wc[1]
    dz = p_w[2] - t_wc[2]
    norm = math.sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy + dz*dz)
    if norm == 0:
        r = (0, 0, 1) # Default
    else:
        r = (dx/norm, dy/norm, dz/norm)
        
    color = evaluate_spherical_harmonics(splat.h, r, splat.l_max)
    
    return ProjectedSplat(
        screen=vec2(sx, sy),
        depth=z,
        cov2d_inv=cov2d_inv,
        bbox=(x0, y0, x1, y1),
        color=color,
        opacity=alpha
    )

def render(splats, camera, width, height):
    projected_splats = []
    for s in splats:
        p = project_splat(s, camera)
        if p is not None:
            projected_splats.append(p)
            
    # Sort by depth (ascending)
    projected_splats.sort(key=lambda p: p.depth)

    # Framebuffer: list of (rgb, alpha)
    fb = [((0.0, 0.0, 0.0), 0.0) for _ in range(width * height)]
    
    for p in projected_splats:
        x0, y0, x1, y1 = p.bbox
        # Clip to image bounds
        x0 = max(0, x0)
        y0 = max(0, y0)
        x1 = min(width - 1, x1)
        y1 = min(height - 1, y1)

            continue
            
        sx, sy = p.screen
        cov_inv = p.cov2d_inv
        color = p.color
        alpha = p.opacity
        
        for py in range(y0, y1 + 1):
            for px in range(x0, x1 + 1):
                # Displacement
                dx = px - sx
                dy = py - sy
                
                # Power = -0.5 * d^T * Sigma_inv * d
                # d = (dx, dy)
                # Sigma_inv * d
                sd = mat2_mul_vec(cov_inv, (dx, dy))
                power = -0.5 * (dx * sd[0] + dy * sd[1])
                
                # Alpha at pixel
                alpha_pixel = alpha * math.exp(power)
                
                # Composite
                idx = py * width + px
                fb_rgb, fb_alpha = fb[idx]
                
                # Transmittance from previous splats is implicit in the loop order
                # We need to track transmittance per pixel or accumulate
                # Standard 3DGS compositing:
                # C_out = C_in + alpha_pixel * c * (1 - alpha_accum)
                # alpha_accum_new = alpha_accum + alpha_pixel * (1 - alpha_accum)
                
                # However, the formula in S2 uses tau_ij which is product of (1-alpha_k)
                # This is equivalent to standard alpha blending if processed front-to-back
                
                # Let's use standard alpha blending accumulation
                # Note: alpha_pixel is already modulated by intrinsic alpha
                
                # Transmittance reaching this splat
                # tau = 1 - alpha_accum
                tau = 1.0 - fb_alpha
                
                # Contribution
                contrib_r = alpha_pixel * color[0] * tau
                contrib_g = alpha_pixel * color[1] * tau
                contrib_b = alpha_pixel * color[2] * tau
                
                new_r = fb_rgb[0] + contrib_r
                new_g = fb_rgb[1] + contrib_g
                new_b = fb_rgb[2] + contrib_b
                new_alpha = fb_alpha + alpha_pixel * tau
                
                fb[idx] = ((new_r, new_g, new_b), new_alpha)
                
    return fb
```

## Subtopics

### Adaptive Bounding Box Computation
Computes a tight bounding box for a 2D Gaussian splat based on an opacity threshold, avoiding the wasteful circular bounding box of the original 3DGS method. It calculates $k^2 = \min(2.0 \cdot \ln(\frac{\alpha}{\tau}), max\_k2)$ and derives half-widths $rx_f = \sqrt{k^2 \cdot cov2d_{00}}$ and $ry_f = \sqrt{k^2 \cdot cov2d_{11}}$. If the splat opacity is below the threshold or the resulting box is invalid, the splat is culled. This reduces the number of pixels processed during compositing [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(M \cdot N)$ in the worst case where $M$ is the number of splats and $N$ is the number of pixels, but effectively $O(M \cdot A)$ where $A$ is the average area of the bounding boxes, due to the tight bounding box culling.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(M + W \cdot H)$ for storing projected splats and the framebuffer.
*   **Performance:** Highly optimized for rasterization on GPUs and CPUs using tile-based rendering and fast approximate exponentials. The stabilization epsilon ensures robustness against degenerate Gaussians (e.g., when projected very far away or edge-on).
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering capability, view-dependent color via SH, efficient culling.
*   **Limitations:** Approximation error from Jacobian linearization (perspective distortion is not perfectly Gaussian), requires careful tuning of $\epsilon_{2d}$ and opacity thresholds.

## Background
Originated from the "3D Gaussian Splatting" paper by Kerbl et al. (2023), which introduced the representation of scenes as 3D Gaussians for efficient novel view synthesis. The projection method described here is the core rasterization technique used in implementations like the unofficial "tsplat" library and the LichtFeld-Studio reference implementation [S1, S2]. It builds on the mathematical foundation of projecting ellipsoids via Jacobian linearization, a technique common in computer graphics for handling perspective transforms of quadratic surfaces.
