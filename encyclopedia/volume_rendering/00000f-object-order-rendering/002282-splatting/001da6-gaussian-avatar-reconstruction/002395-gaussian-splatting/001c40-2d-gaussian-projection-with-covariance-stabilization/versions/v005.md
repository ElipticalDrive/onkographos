# 2D Gaussian Projection with Covariance Stabilization

Also called: 2D Gaussian Projection, EWA Projection, Elliptical Weighted Average projection, 2D Covariance Projection, Covariance Projection, Screen-space Gaussian Projection, EWA Splatting Projection, Perspective Projection of 3D Gaussians, Perspective-correct covariance projection

## Overview
This method projects a 3D Gaussian primitive, defined by its mean and covariance in world space, onto the 2D image plane to produce a screen-space Gaussian ellipse (splat) for rasterization [S3]. It solves the problem of rendering anisotropic, perspective-distorted 3D distributions efficiently by approximating the non-linear perspective projection with a local affine transformation, thereby preserving the Gaussian form in 2D [S1, unofficial, S3].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- 3D Gaussian parameters: mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (world space), rotation quaternion $q$, scale vector $s$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic coefficients $h$ for color [S6, unofficial].
- Camera parameters: View matrix $V$, Projection matrix $P$, image width $W$, height $H$, focal lengths $f_x, f_y$, principal point $(c_x, c_y)$ [S1, unofficial, S6, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Screen-space mean $\mu_{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ [S1, unofficial].
- Screen-space covariance matrix $\Sigma_{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ [S2, unofficial].
- Inverse covariance (conic) $\Sigma_{2D}^{-1}$ [S2, unofficial].
- Bounding box for rasterization.

**Data Structures:**
The projected Gaussian is stored with the following fields [S2, unofficial]:
- `screen`: `Vec2` (pixel center $s_x, s_y$).
- `depth`: `f32` (camera-space $z_c$, positive in front).
- `cov2d_inv`: `Mat2` (inverse 2D covariance).
- `bbox`: `[i32; 4]` (inclusive $x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1$).
- `color`: `Vec3` (RGB).
- `opacity`: `f32` (in $[0, 1]$).

**Steps:**

1. **Reconstruct 3D Covariance:**
   Compute the 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ from the quaternion $q$ and scale $s$ [S6, unofficial]. The rotation matrix $R$ is derived from $q$, and the scale matrix $S$ is diagonal with entries $s_i$ [S6, unofficial]:
   $$ \Sigma = R S S^\top R^\top $$
   Where $R$ is:
   $$ R = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 2(q_y^2 + q_z^2) & 2(q_xq_y - q_zq_w) & 2(q_xq_z + q_yq_w) \\ 2(q_xq_y + q_zq_w) & 1 - 2(q_x^2 + q_z^2) & 2(q_yq_z - q_xq_w) \\ 2(q_xq_z - q_yq_w) & 2(q_yq_z + q_xq_w) & 1 - 2(q_x^2 + q_y^2) \end{bmatrix} $$
   Implementations typically exponentiate the scale parameters before forming $S$ to ensure positive scales [S1, unofficial].

2. **Transform to Camera Space:**
   Transform the mean to camera space: $\mu_c = V_{3\times3} \mu + t_{cw}$ [S6, unofficial].
   Transform the covariance to eye space using the rotation block $M = V_{3\times3}$ of the view matrix. Translation is ignored for covariance [S1, unofficial]:
   $$ \Sigma_{cam} = M \Sigma M^\top $$
   This relies on the property that if $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$, then $AX \sim \mathcal{N}(A\mu, A\Sigma A^\top)$ [S1, unofficial].

3. **Compute Jacobian of Perspective Projection:**
   Linearize the perspective projection locally at $\mu_c = (x_c, y_c, z_c)$ using the Jacobian $J$ [S1, unofficial]:
   $$ J = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{f_x}{z_c} & 0 & \frac{f_x x_c}{z_c^2} \\ 0 & -\frac{f_y}{z_c} & \frac{f_y y_c}{z_c^2} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} $$
   Focal lengths are derived from the projection matrix entries $p_{00}, p_{11}$: $f_x = p_{00} \frac{W}{2}$, $f_y = p_{11} \frac{H}{2}$ [S1, unofficial].

4. **Project Covariance to 2D:**
   Compute the unnormalized 2D covariance:
   $$ \Sigma'_{2D} = J_{2\times3} \Sigma_{cam} J_{2\times3}^\top $$
   where $J_{2\times3}$ is the top two rows of $J$ [S1, unofficial, S3].

5. **Covariance Stabilization (Low-Pass Filter):**
   Add a small epsilon $\epsilon_{2d}$ to the diagonal elements to ensure positive definiteness and prevent singularities or infinitely small Gaussians [S2, unofficial, S4, unofficial]:
   $$ \Sigma_{2D} = \Sigma'_{2D} + \begin{bmatrix} \epsilon_{2d} & 0 \\ 0 & \epsilon_{2d} \end{bmatrix} $$
   Typical value: $\epsilon_{2d} = 0.3$ [S2, unofficial]. This guarantees invertibility because $x^T (A^T A + \lambda I) x = \|Ax\|^2 + \lambda \|x\|^2 > 0$ for $\lambda > 0$ [S2, unofficial].

6. **Compute Screen Mean:**
   Apply the full MVP transform and perspective divide to get the screen coordinates [S1, unofficial]:
   $$ \text{clip} = P \cdot V \cdot \mu = (x_c, y_c, z_c, w_c)^\top $$
   $$ \mu_{2D} = \left( \frac{x_c}{w_c} \frac{W}{2} + c_x, \frac{y_c}{w_c} \frac{H}{2} + c_y \right) $$
   Note: Some implementations compute NDC first: $\text{ndc} = (x_c/w_c, y_c/w_c, z_c/w_c)$, then map to pixels [S1, unofficial].

7. **Compute Inverse Covariance (Conic):**
   For a symmetric matrix $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ b & d \end{bmatrix}$, the inverse is:
   $$ \det = ad - b^2 $$
   If $\det \le 0$, cull the splat [S2, unofficial].
   $$ \Sigma_{2D}^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det} \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -b & a \end{bmatrix} $$
   Store as upper triangular vector $(a, b, d)$ for efficiency [S6, unofficial].

8. **Compute Bounding Box:**
   Compute eigenvalues $\lambda_{1,2}$ and eigenvectors $e_1, e_2$ of $\Sigma_{2D}$ [S1, unofficial]:
   $$ \lambda_{1,2} = \frac{\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_{2D})}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_{2D})^2}{4} - \det(\Sigma_{2D})} $$
   $$ v_1 = (\Sigma_{12}, \lambda_1 - \Sigma_{11}), \quad e_1 = \frac{v_1}{\|v_1\|}, \quad e_2 = (e_{1,2}, -e_{1,1}) $$
   Define half-axes $b_1 = 3\sqrt{\lambda_1}e_1$ and $b_2 = 3\sqrt{\lambda_2}e_2$ (using $3\sigma$ cutoff) [S1, unofficial].
   The bounding box is the axis-aligned box containing the parallelogram spanned by $b_1$ and $b_2$ centered at $\mu_{2D}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(q):
    """Convert quaternion (w, x, y, z) to 3x3 rotation matrix."""
    w, x, y, z = q
    return np.array([
        [1 - 2*(y*y + z*z), 2*(x*y - z*w),     2*(x*z + y*w)],
        [2*(x*y + z*w),     1 - 2*(x*x + z*z), 2*(y*z - x*w)],
        [2*(x*z - y*w),     2*(y*z + x*w),     1 - 2*(x*x + y*y)]
    ])

def project_gaussian(gaussian, camera):
    """
    gaussian: dict with 'mean' (3,), 'q' (4,), 'scale' (3,), 'opacity' (float)
    camera: dict with 'view' (4x4), 'proj' (4x4), 'W', 'H', 'fx', 'fy', 'cx', 'cy'
    """
    # 1. Reconstruct 3D Covariance
    R = quaternion_to_rotation_matrix(gaussian['q'])
    # S is diagonal with scale entries; implementations typically exponentiate scales first [S1, unofficial]
    S = np.diag(np.exp(gaussian['scale']))
    Sigma_3d = R @ S @ S.T @ R.T
    
    # 2. Transform to Camera Space
    # Extract 3x3 rotation from view matrix
    M = camera['view'][:3, :3]
    Sigma_cam = M @ Sigma_3d @ M.T
    
    # Transform mean to camera space
    mu_cam = M @ gaussian['mean'] + camera['view'][:3, 3]
    x_c, y_c, z_c = mu_cam
    
    # 3. Compute Jacobian
    # J = [[-fx/z, 0, fx*x/z^2], [0, -fy/z, fy*y/z^2]]
    inv_z = 1.0 / z_c
    inv_z2 = inv_z * inv_z
    J = np.array([
        [-camera['fx'] * inv_z, 0, camera['fx'] * x_c * inv_z2],
        [0, -camera['fy'] * inv_z, camera['fy'] * y_c * inv_z2]
    ])
    
    # 4. Project Covariance
    Sigma_2d_unstable = J @ Sigma_cam @ J.T
    
    # 5. Stabilization
    eps_2d = 0.3
    Sigma_2d = Sigma_2d_unstable.copy()
    Sigma_2d[0, 0] += eps_2d
    Sigma_2d[1, 1] += eps_2d
    
    # 6. Compute Screen Mean
    # Apply full MVP
    mu_homogeneous = np.array([*gaussian['mean'], 1.0])
    clip = camera['proj'] @ camera['view'] @ mu_homogeneous
    w_c = clip[3]
    ndc_x = clip[0] / w_c
    ndc_y = clip[1] / w_c
    
    screen_x = ndc_x * camera['W'] / 2 + camera['cx']
    screen_y = ndc_y * camera['H'] / 2 + camera['cy']
    
    # 7. Inverse Covariance
    a, b = Sigma_2d[0, 0], Sigma_2d[0, 1]
    d = Sigma_2d[1, 1]
    det = a * d - b * b
    
    if det <= 0.0:
        return None # Cull splat
        
    inv_det = 1.0 / det
    cov2d_inv = np.array([
        [d * inv_det, -b * inv_det],
        [-b * inv_det, a * inv_det]
    ])
    
    # 8. Bounding Box (Simplified AABB of 3-sigma ellipse)
    # Eigen decomposition for bounding box
    eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eigh(Sigma_2d)
    # eigenvalues are sorted ascending, so lambda1 is eigenvalues[0]
    lambda1 = eigenvalues[0]
    lambda2 = eigenvalues[1]
    e1 = eigenvectors[:, 0]
    e2 = eigenvectors[:, 1]
    
    b1 = 3.0 * math.sqrt(lambda1) * e1
    b2 = 3.0 * math.sqrt(lambda2) * e2
    
    # Compute corners of the parallelogram
    corners = [
        screen_x + b1[0] + b2[0], screen_y + b1[1] + b2[1],
        screen_x + b1[0] - b2[0], screen_y + b1[1] - b2[1],
        screen_x - b1[0] + b2[0], screen_y - b1[1] + b2[1],
        screen_x - b1[0] - b2[0], screen_y - b1[1] - b2[1]
    ]
    
    x_min = min(corners[0::2])
    x_max = max(corners[0::2])
    y_min = min(corners[1::2])
    y_max = max(corners[1::2])
    
    # Convert to integer pixel coordinates (inclusive)
    bbox = [
        int(math.floor(x_min)),
        int(math.floor(y_min)),
        int(math.ceil(x_max)),
        int(math.ceil(y_max))
    ]
    
    return {
        'screen': (screen_x, screen_y),
        'depth': z_c,
        'cov2d_inv': cov2d_inv,
        'bbox': bbox,
        'opacity': gaussian['opacity']
    }
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Performance:** Enables real-time rendering of millions of Gaussians (e.g., 134 fps on Mip-NeRF360) by avoiding ray marching [S3].
- **Strengths:** Closed-form projection allows differentiability and fast GPU/CPU rasterization. The method sacrifices negligible global geometric accuracy to enable real-time rendering [S3].
- **Limitations:** The linearization error increases for large splats or extreme perspective, potentially causing geometric inaccuracies [S3]. Singularities are handled by stabilization but may introduce slight blurring if $\epsilon_{2d}$ is too large [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) splatting, described by Zwicker et al. in 2002 for anti-aliased volume rendering [S3]. It was adapted by Kerbl et al. (2023) for 3D Gaussian Splatting, where it projects 3D Gaussians to 2D ellipses for real-time rasterization [S3]. The stabilization term $\epsilon_{2d}$ is a practical addition to ensure numerical robustness in the inverse covariance computation [S2, unofficial].
