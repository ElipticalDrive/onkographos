# Gabor Noise Surface Filtering

Also called: Analytical filtering of Gabor noise, Gabor Noise Filtering

## Overview
Gabor Noise Surface Filtering is an analytical method for generating anisotropically filtered procedural noise on surfaces by exploiting the spectral structure of Gabor noise. Unlike methods that discretize the spectrum into bands or apply post-hoc blurring, this approach computes the exact filtered spectrum per pixel by multiplying the Gabor kernel's frequency-domain Gaussian with the view-dependent filter Gaussian, then generating noise from the resulting parameters [S1, S2]. It is distinct from [Fourier Spectral Synthesis](16451-fourier-spectral-synthesis.md) because it operates on oriented subbands (Gabor kernels) rather than a global isotropic spectrum, allowing it to adapt to non-uniform perspective distortions and anisotropic filtering requirements without aliasing or over-blurring [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- The set of Gabor kernels defining the unfiltered noise. Each kernel $k$ is defined by its center frequency $\mathbf{u}_k$, orientation, and bandwidth parameters.
- The view-dependent filter function $H(\mathbf{u})$, typically a Gaussian low-pass filter determined by the screen-space footprint of the surface patch.

**Outputs:**
- A noise value $N(\mathbf{x})$ at surface point $\mathbf{x}$, already filtered according to the local view-dependent frequency response.

**Data Structures:**
- **Gabor Kernel:** Defined by parameters $\{\mathbf{u}_k, \sigma_{k,u}, \sigma_{k,v}, \theta_k\}$, where $\mathbf{u}_k$ is the center frequency vector, $\sigma$ represents bandwidths in the frequency domain, and $\theta$ is the orientation.
- **Filter Gaussian:** Defined by $\{\mathbf{u}_f, \Sigma_f\}$, where $\Sigma_f$ is the covariance matrix of the filter in frequency space, derived from the screen-space derivatives of the surface parameterization.

**Steps:**
1. **Decompose Noise:** Represent the Gabor noise as a sum of individual Gabor kernels. Each kernel $k$ has a frequency-domain representation that is a Gaussian centered at $\mathbf{u}_k$ with covariance $\Sigma_k$ [S1, S2].
2. **Determine Filter:** For the current screen pixel, compute the view-dependent filter Gaussian $H(\mathbf{u})$ in texture space. This filter accounts for the anisotropic projection of the surface onto the screen [S2].
3. **Multiply in Frequency Domain:** Compute the product of the Gabor kernel's Gaussian spectrum $G_k(\mathbf{u})$ and the filter Gaussian $H(\mathbf{u})$. Since Gaussians are closed under multiplication, the result is a new Gaussian $G'_k(\mathbf{u})$ [S1, S2].
   $$ G'_k(\mathbf{u}) = G_k(\mathbf{u}) \cdot H(\mathbf{u}) $$
4. **Compute New Parameters:** Extract the parameters of the new Gaussian $G'_k(\mathbf{u})$. The new center frequency $\mathbf{u}'_k$ and covariance $\Sigma'_k$ are calculated from the product of the two Gaussians. For two Gaussians with covariances $\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2$ and means $\mathbf{m}_1, \mathbf{m}_2$, the product is a Gaussian with:
   $$ \Sigma' = (\Sigma_1^{-1} + \Sigma_2^{-1})^{-1} $$
   $$ \mathbf{m}' = \Sigma' (\Sigma_1^{-1}\mathbf{m}_1 + \Sigma_2^{-1}\mathbf{m}_2) $$
   [G: This is the standard formula for the product of two unnormalized Gaussian functions in the frequency domain. The amplitude scaling factor is also updated but does not affect the zero-crossings or shape if normalized later.]
5. **Generate Filtered Noise:** Use the new parameters $\{\mathbf{u}'_k, \Sigma'_k, \dots\}$ to generate the noise value for kernel $k$ at the surface point. Sum the contributions of all kernels to produce the final filtered noise value [S1, S2].

**Pitfalls:**
- The filter in texture space varies per screen pixel due to view-dependence. Failing to recompute the filter Gaussian for each pixel or using a static approximation leads to incorrect anisotropic filtering [S2].
- Precision issues may arise when inverting covariance matrices if the filter bandwidth is extremely narrow or wide relative to the kernel bandwidth [G].

## Subtopics

### NPR Gabor Noise
A variant of Gabor Noise for coherent stylization in non-photorealistic rendering (NPR) [S1]. It defines noise parameters in 2D screen space while maintaining point distribution in 3D object space to preserve the 2D aspect of the noise pattern despite 3D scene motion [S1]. Presented by Benard et al. [BLV∗ 10] [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of Gabor kernels. The per-pixel cost involves computing the filter Gaussian, multiplying it with each kernel's Gaussian parameters, and evaluating the modified kernels.
- **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it is a procedural method requiring no storage of pre-filtered textures.
- **Strengths:** Provides exact analytical filtering that adapts to non-uniform perspective distortions and anisotropy. Avoids the compromise between over-blurring and residual aliasing inherent in frequency clamping or band-discretization methods [S2].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful computation of the view-dependent filter Gaussian for each pixel. More complex to implement than isotropic filtering methods [S2].
- **When to use:** When high-quality anisotropic filtering is required for procedural textures on surfaces, especially under perspective projection.
- **When not to use:** For simple isotropic filtering where standard mipmapping or post-processing suffices, or if computational resources are extremely limited and approximate filtering is acceptable.

## Background
Gabor noise was introduced by Goldberg et al. [GZD08] in 2008 as a noise function supporting high-quality anisotropic filtering by tiling the frequency domain into oriented subbands [Parent]. The analytical filtering method for Gabor noise was described by Lagae et al. [LLDD09a] in 2009, exploiting the property that Gabor kernels correspond to Gaussians in the frequency domain, allowing direct generation of filtered noise by modifying kernel parameters [S1, S2]. The NPR Gabor Noise variant was presented by Benard et al. [BLV∗ 10] in 2010 [S1].
