# Fourier Spectral Synthesis
Also called: Fourier Noise Generation, Spectral Synthesis

## Overview
Fourier Spectral Synthesis generates noise with a specific power spectrum by filtering white noise in the frequency domain [S1]. It is distinguished from spatial-domain noise methods by its direct control over the spectral density, making it ideal for generating random fractals and simulating natural phenomena with precise statistical properties [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Target power spectrum $P(\mathbf{k})$, where $\mathbf{k}$ is the frequency vector.
- Resolution $N$ (grid dimensions).

**Outputs:**
- A noise field $f(\mathbf{x})$ with the desired spectral characteristics.

**Data Structures:**
- Complex-valued frequency domain array $F[\mathbf{k}]$ of size $N \times N \times N$ (or 2D/1D as needed).
- Real-valued spatial domain array $f[\mathbf{x}]$ of size $N \times N \times N$.

**Steps:**
1. **Generate White Noise:** Create a complex-valued array $W[\mathbf{k}]$ in the frequency domain. For each frequency bin $\mathbf{k}$, assign a complex value with random phase uniformly distributed in $[0, 2\pi)$ and magnitude 1. Ensure Hermitian symmetry ($W[-\mathbf{k}] = \overline{W[\mathbf{k}]}$) to guarantee a real-valued output [S1, G].
2. **Apply Power Spectrum:** Scale the white noise by the square root of the target power spectrum:
   $$ F[\mathbf{k}] = W[\mathbf{k}] \cdot \sqrt{P(\mathbf{k})} $$
   Note: $P(\mathbf{k})$ must be non-negative. For fractal noise, $P(\mathbf{k})$ typically follows a power law $1/|\mathbf{k}|^{2\beta}$ [S1].
3. **Inverse Fourier Transform:** Compute the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT) of $F[\mathbf{k}]$ to obtain the spatial noise field:
   $$ f[\mathbf{x}] = \text{IFFT}(F[\mathbf{k}]) $$
   The result should be real-valued; discard imaginary parts due to numerical precision errors [G].

**Parameters:**
- $\beta$: Fractal dimension parameter (typically $0.5 < \beta < 1.5$ for natural textures) [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
- **DC Component:** $P(\mathbf{0})$ should be handled carefully to avoid infinite mean or variance. Often set to 0 or a small constant [G].
- **Symmetry:** Failure to enforce Hermitian symmetry results in complex-valued noise [G].

## Subtopics

### [Surface Noise via Slice-Projection Theorem](16460-surface-noise-via-slice-projection-theorem.md)
A method to obtain band-pass noise on a surface from band-pass solid noise by exploiting the slice-projection theorem, which states that slicing in one domain corresponds to projection or integration in the other.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N^3 \log N)$ for 3D noise due to FFT/IFFT [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N^3)$ for storing frequency and spatial arrays [S1].
- **Strengths:** Exact control over power spectrum; efficient generation of large-scale fractal noise [S1].
- **Limitations:** Global nature of FFT makes local edits difficult; boundary effects if not using periodic assumptions [S1].
- **Use Cases:** Terrain generation, cloud simulation, reference solutions for noise validation [S1].

## Background
Introduced in computer graphics by Anjyo [Anj88], Saupe [Sau88], and Voss [Vos88] to generate random fractals for simulating natural phenomena like terrain [S1]. The mathematical texturing function of Gardner [Gar84] is also considered a form of Fourier spectral synthesis [S1]. It is used in explicit noise methods such as spot noise [vW91] and anisotropic noise [GZD08].
