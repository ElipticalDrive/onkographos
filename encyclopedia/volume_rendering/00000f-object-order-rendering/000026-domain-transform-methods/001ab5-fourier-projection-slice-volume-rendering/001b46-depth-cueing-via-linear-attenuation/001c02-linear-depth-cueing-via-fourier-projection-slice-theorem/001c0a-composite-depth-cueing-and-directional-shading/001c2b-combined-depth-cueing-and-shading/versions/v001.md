# Combined Depth Cueing and Shading

Also called: Directional Shading in Frequency Domain, Frequency Domain Shading and Depth Cueing, Linear Depth Cueing, Reversed order shading and cueing

## Overview
This method combines linear depth cueing and directional shading within the frequency domain to replace $O(n^3)$ spatial domain computations with $O(n^2)$ frequency domain operations [S1, S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently applying both attenuation and illumination in Fourier Projection-Slice Volume Rendering [Parent]. It sets itself apart from naive frequency domain combinations by reversing the standard order of operations (shading then cueing) to utilize a single composite filter, thereby avoiding the computational cost of multiple convolutions [S1, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   Input: 3D volume spectrum $\hat{f}(\mathbf{s})$, view direction $\mathbf{V}$, light direction $\mathbf{L}$, depth cueing function $d(x)$, and interpolation filter $H(\mathbf{s})$ [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   Output: Rendered image with combined depth cueing and directional shading [S1, S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   Spectrum: 3D complex array $\hat{f}(\mathbf{s})$ where $\mathbf{s} = (s_x, s_y, s_z)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Composite Filter: $H'(\mathbf{s})$ combines interpolation and depth cueing in the frequency domain [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   Light Coefficients: Constants $C_{dif}$, $L_{dif}$ for shading magnitude [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   Depth Cueing Coefficients: $C_{cue}$ for linear depth cueing [S1, S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Reverse Order Strategy:** Instead of applying shading first (which requires two convolutions: one to recover the pure spectrum for shading, and one to apply depth cueing), reverse the order: apply depth cueing and interpolation simultaneously using the composite filter $H'$, then apply shading [S1, S3, unofficial].
2.  **Composite Filtering:** Use $H'(\mathbf{s})$ to perform interpolation and linear depth cueing at once during slice extraction [S1, S3, unofficial].
3.  **Shading Application:** Apply directional shading to the depth-cued and interpolated data [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Adjustment for Non-Perpendicular Light:** If the light direction $\mathbf{L}$ is not perpendicular to the view direction $\mathbf{V}$, an adjustment term must be computed to correct for the distortion of the gradient vector caused by the reversed order [S1, S3, unofficial].
5.  **Linear Depth Cueing Adjustment:** For linear depth cueing, compute the difference term adjustment during slice extraction without convolution if $\mathbf{L}$ is not perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$ [S1, S3, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The shading equation in the spatial domain includes an ambient term and a shading term involving the gradient $\nabla f$ [S3, unofficial]:
$$ I = C_{amb} + C_{dif} (\nabla f \cdot \mathbf{L}) $$
When depth cueing $d(x)$ is applied, the gradient term becomes $\nabla [f(x) d(x)] \cdot \mathbf{L}$ [S1, S3, unofficial]. Using the product rule:
$$ \nabla [f(x) d(x)] \cdot \mathbf{L} = (\nabla f(x) \cdot \mathbf{L}) d(x) + f(x) (\nabla d(x) \cdot \mathbf{L}) $$
Since $d(x)$ is a function of depth $t = \mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{x}$, $\nabla d(x) = d'_{1D}(\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{x}) \mathbf{V}$ [S1, S3, unofficial]. The difference term (error introduced by reversed order) is:
$$ f(x) d'_{1D}(\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{x}) (\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{L}) $$
For linear depth cueing, $d_{1D}(t)$ is linear, so $d'_{1D}(t)$ is constant [S1, S3, unofficial]. The full adjustment term including coefficients is:
$$ \frac{1}{2} C_{cue} C_{dif} L_{dif} f(x) (\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{L}) $$
This term can be computed during slice extraction without convolution [S1, S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Light Direction $\mathbf{L}$: Often set perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$ to maximize shading effect (side lighting) [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   Depth Cueing Function: Linear depth cueing is specified [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   Filter Size: $3 \times 3 \times 3$ filter used in experiments, consuming 27 input samples per output sample [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def render_combined_depth_cueing_shading(spectrum, view_dir, light_dir, filter_coeffs):
    """
    Renders volume with combined depth cueing and shading using reversed order.
    
    Args:
        spectrum: 3D complex array of volume spectrum.
        view_dir: 3D vector, view direction V.
        light_dir: 3D vector, light direction L.
        filter_coeffs: Dictionary containing C_cue, C_dif, L_dif, C_amb.
        
    Returns:
        Rendered image array.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute dot product V . L
    v_dot_l = dot(view_dir, light_dir)
    
    # Step 2: Extract slices using composite filter H' (interpolation + depth cueing)
    # This step applies depth cueing and interpolation simultaneously.
    # The exact implementation of H' depends on the specific Fourier Projection-Slice method.
    # For linear depth cueing, this is done in frequency domain.
    depth_cued_slices = extract_slices_with_composite_filter(spectrum, view_dir, filter_coeffs)
    
    # Step 3: Apply shading to the depth-cued slices
    # Compute gradient of depth-cued data in frequency domain or spatial domain as appropriate
    # For frequency domain shading, use the filtered spectrum.
    shaded_slices = apply_directional_shading(depth_cued_slices, light_dir, filter_coeffs)
    
    # Step 4: Apply adjustment if V is not perpendicular to L
    if v_dot_l != 0:
        # Compute adjustment term: 0.5 * C_cue * C_dif * L_dif * f(x) * (V . L)
        # This term is added to the shaded result.
        # For linear depth cueing, this can be computed during slice extraction.
        adjustment = compute_linear_adjustment(depth_cued_slices, v_dot_l, filter_coeffs)
        shaded_slices = shaded_slices + adjustment
        
    # Step 5: Composite slices into final image
    image = composite_slices(shaded_slices)
    
    return image

def compute_linear_adjustment(depth_cued_slices, v_dot_l, filter_coeffs):
    """
    Computes the adjustment term for non-perpendicular light.
    Adjustment = 0.5 * C_cue * C_dif * L_dif * f(x) * (V . L)
    """
    C_cue = filter_coeffs['C_cue']
    C_dif = filter_coeffs['C_dif']
    L_dif = filter_coeffs['L_dif']
    
    # f(x) is the depth-cued data (which includes the linear depth cueing factor)
    # Note: In the reversed order, depth_cued_slices already contains the depth cueing effect.
    # The adjustment term corrects the shading gradient distortion.
    # The formula from sources: 1/2 * C_cue * C_dif * L_dif * f(x) * (V . L)
    
    adjustment_factor = 0.5 * C_cue * C_dif * L_dif * v_dot_l
    
    # Apply adjustment to the spatial domain data of the slices
    # This is a scalar multiplication per voxel
    adjusted_slices = depth_cued_slices * adjustment_factor
    
    return adjusted_slices
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Perpendicular Light:** If $\mathbf{L}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$, $\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{L} = 0$, and the adjustment term is zero. No correction is needed [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Non-Linear Depth Cueing:** For complex depth cueing functions, a convolution is necessary for the adjustment term, increasing computational cost [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Distortion:** Applying depth cueing before shading distorts the gradient vector, leading to incorrect shading. Reversing the order avoids this but requires the adjustment term [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Combined Depth Cueing and Shading (Reversed Order)
A technique to combine depth cueing and directional shading by reversing the order of operations (shading first, then depth cueing) to use a single composite filter $H'$ and avoid two convolutions [S3, unofficial]. It is chosen when computational efficiency is prioritized and the light direction is not perpendicular to the view direction, requiring an adjustment term [S3, unofficial]. The adjustment term is $f(x) \nabla d(\mathbf{V} \cdot \mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{L}$, which is zero if $d(x)$ is linear and $\mathbf{L}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$ [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(n^2 \log n)$ for the frequency domain computation, replacing $O(n^3)$ spatial domain computations [S1, unofficial]. The interpolation step is $O(n^2)$ and consumes most of the running time [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the 3D spectrum and intermediate slices [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** A $128^3$ volume can be rendered in a fraction of a second on a conventional workstation [S1, unofficial]. The adaptive filtering scheme reduced the cost of interpolation to about 15% of the non-relative case [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Significant speed advantage over conventional volume rendering; no visible difference compared to conventional spatial domain rendering with identical shading and depth cueing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The frequency domain projection operator is non-occluding, resulting in a loss of realism [S1, unofficial]. The reversed order requires an adjustment term if $\mathbf{L}$ is not perpendicular to $\mathbf{V}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When real-time or near-real-time rendering is required for large volumes and shading/depth cueing are needed [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When occlusion is critical for realism or when the light direction is complex and non-perpendicular, requiring expensive convolutions for adjustment [S1, S3, unofficial].

## Background
Originated by Takashi Totsuka and Marc Levoy in 1993 [S1, S3, unofficial]. The paper "Frequency Domain Volume Rendering" was presented at SIGGRAPH 1993 [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to reduce the computational cost of volume rendering by leveraging the Fourier Projection-Slice Theorem [S1, unofficial]. It is used in medical imaging and scientific visualization where large volumes need to be rendered efficiently [S1, unofficial].
