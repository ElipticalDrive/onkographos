# Variational Isotropic Meshing

Also called: CVT for Implicit Surfaces, Delaunay refinement with CVT optimization

## Overview
Variational Isotropic Meshing is a two-phase method for computing high-quality triangular meshes from a given implicit surface $S$ (a 2-manifold) within a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$. It solves the problem of generating meshes that are both topologically correct (homeomorphic to $S$) and geometrically accurate (isotropic sampling and optimal approximation). It sets itself apart from standard Delaunay refinement by incorporating a variational relaxation phase that optimizes vertex positions via Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation (CVT) principles and Quadric Error Metrics (QEM), ensuring uniform normal variation and improved shape approximation [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in two distinct phases: an initial **Delaunay Refinement** phase to establish a homeomorphic mesh satisfying geometric and topological constraints, followed by a **Relaxation** phase to optimize vertex distribution and approximation quality.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input**: An implicit surface $S$ defined by a function $f: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$, where $S = \{x \in \Omega \mid f(x) = 0\}$. The function $f$ must allow evaluation of $f(x)$ and its gradient $\nabla f(x)$ for any $x \in \Omega$. The domain $\Omega$ is a finite box.
*   **Output**: A triangular mesh $M$ that is homeomorphic to $S$, locally Delaunay, and optimized for isotropic sampling and geometric approximation.

### Data Structures
*   **Mesh $M$**: A set of vertices $V$, edges $E$, and triangles $T$.
*   **Voronoi Diagram $VorP|S$**: The restriction of the 3D Voronoi diagram of the vertex set $P$ to the surface $S$.
*   **Priority Queue**: A min-heap or max-heap storing triangles $t \in T$ with associated priority values $P_t$, used in Geometrical Refinement.
*   **Vertex Attributes**: Each vertex $p$ stores its position, estimated normal $n_p$, and incident facets.

### Parameters
*   $\lambda$: User-defined coefficient for the face distance bound, $0 < \lambda < 1$. Typical values range from 0.01 to 0.2 [S1].
*   $d$: The shortest edge length of the bounding box of the domain $\Omega$.
*   $L$: The bound for the face distance condition, $L = \lambda \cdot d$.
*   $\gamma$: Gradation parameter for curvature-adaptive sampling. $\gamma = 0$ for uniform sampling; $\gamma > 0$ for curvature-adaptive sampling [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Small offset to handle perfectly flat regions. Typical value: $10^{-50}$ [S1].

### Phase 1: Delaunay Refinement
This phase builds an initial mesh that is homeomorphic to $S$ and approximates its geometry. It ensures two conditions are met for all facets $\sigma$ in the current mesh restricted to the surface, $DelP|S$.

#### 1. Face Distance Condition
For any facet $\sigma \in DelP|S$, calculate the face distance $DF(\sigma)$.
$$ DF(\sigma) = \| c_\sigma - q_{max} \| $$
where $c_\sigma$ is the circumcenter of $\sigma$, and $q_{max}$ is the furthest intersection point between the dual Voronoi edge $V_\sigma^*$ and the implicit surface $S$.

If $DF(\sigma) \ge L$, the condition is violated. The algorithm inserts the intersection point $q$ (between the dual of $\sigma$ and the surface) into $VorP|S$ and updates the triangulation.

#### 2. Topological Disk Condition
For every vertex $p$, let $N_p$ be the set of facets incident to $p$. The mesh must satisfy the topological disk condition: all facets in $N_p$ must form a topological disk around $p$.

If this condition is not fulfilled (i.e., the link of $p$ is not a simple cycle), find the facet $\sigma \in N_p$ that maximizes $DF(\sigma)$. Insert the corresponding intersection $q$ (between the dual of $\sigma$ and the surface) into $VorP|S$ and update the triangulation.

#### 3. Geometrical Refinement (Vertex Insertion)
If the mesh does not have enough vertices to meet user requirements, new vertices are added using a priority queue based on curvature.

**Priority Calculation**:
For each triangle $t$ with vertices $p_1, p_2, p_3$, calculate the priority $P_t$:
$$ P_t = \frac{|t| \cdot (9 - \| n_1 + n_2 + n_3 \|^2)}{\gamma + \epsilon} $$
where:
*   $|t|$ is the area of triangle $t$.
*   $n_k$ is the estimated normal at vertex $p_k$, computed as $n_k = -\frac{\nabla f(p_k)}{\|\nabla f(p_k)\|}$.
*   $\gamma$ and $\epsilon$ are the gradation and offset parameters.

**Refinement Loop**:
1.  Pop the triangle $t$ with the highest priority $P_t$ from the queue.
2.  Calculate the circumcenter $c_t$ of $t$.
    *   **Pitfall Handling (Obtuse Triangles)**: If $t$ contains an obtuse angle, $c_t$ lies outside $t$. Constrain $c_t$ to lie within the boundary of $t$ to maintain consistency between neighboring Voronoi cells [S1].
3.  Add a new vertex $q_t$ at the constrained circumcenter.
4.  Split $t$ into three new triangles using the original vertices and $q_t$.
5.  Update the triangulation locally via edge flips to restore the local Delaunay property.
    *   **Edge Flip Condition**: An edge $e$ shared by triangles $t_1$ and $t_2$ is flipped if the sum of the angles opposite to $e$ is greater than $\pi$ ($\alpha + \beta > \pi$) [S1].
6.  Calculate priorities for the three new triangles and push them into the queue.
7.  Repeat until the desired number of vertices is reached.

### Phase 2: Relaxation
This phase optimizes the mesh by moving vertices to improve isotropic sampling and approximation quality. It iterates until convergence or a fixed number of iterations.

#### 1. Isotropic Sampling (CVT Relaxation)
For each vertex $p_i$, compute its geodesic Voronoi cell $V_i$ approximated by piecewise linear cells. The cell boundary consists of midpoints of edges incident to $p_i$ and circumcenters of adjacent triangles.
*   **Pitfall Handling**: If an adjacent triangle is obtuse, its circumcenter is constrained to the triangle boundary. This may result in fewer than $2n$ triangles in the Voronoi cell approximation for a vertex with $n$ neighbors [S1].

Compute the weighted barycenter $\bar{p}_i$ of the Voronoi cell $V_i$:
$$ \bar{p}_i = \frac{\sum_{t \in V_i} \chi_t \cdot c_t \cdot A_t}{\sum_{t \in V_i} \chi_t \cdot A_t} $$
where $c_t$ is the circumcenter, $A_t$ is the area, and $\chi_t$ is a weight associated with triangle $t$ in the Voronoi cell.

**Weight Calculation**:
For a triangle $t$ adjacent to vertex $p$, the weight $\chi$ is:
$$ \chi = \frac{9 - \| n_p + n_1 + n_2 \|^2}{\gamma + \epsilon} $$
where:
*   $n_p$ is the estimated normal at $p$ ($n_p = -\frac{\nabla f(p)}{\|\nabla f(p)\|}$).
*   $n_1, n_2$ are the estimated normals at the barycenters of the two triangles sharing the edge opposite to $p$ in the Voronoi cell approximation, projected onto the surface using Newton's method [S1].

**Vertex Update**:
Move vertex $p_i$ to the barycenter $\bar{p}_i$, constrained to the local tangent plane at $p_i$:
$$ p_i^{t+1} = p_i^t - \langle p_i^t - \bar{p}_i^t, n_i^t \rangle \cdot n_i^t $$
where $n_i^t$ is the normal at $p_i$ at iteration $t$ [S1].

#### 2. Approximation Quality Enhancement (QEM Relocation)
After isotropic sampling, vertices are relocated to minimize the Quadric Error Metric (QEM) to improve geometric approximation. This step moves vertices in the normal direction to better fit the implicit surface.

Minimize the functional:
$$ E_i = \int_{x \in V_i} \chi(x) \cdot \| x - p_i \|^2 dx $$
In practice, this is approximated by moving $p_i$ towards the implicit surface along its normal, minimizing the distance error weighted by $\chi$ [S1].

#### 3. Triangulation Update
After vertex relocation, check for non-local Delaunay edges in the mesh. Flip any edge $e$ where the sum of opposite angles $\alpha + \beta > \pi$. This maintains the local Delaunay property required for the next iteration's Voronoi cell computation.

### Pseudocode

```python
def variational_isotropic_meshing(f, grad_f, domain_box, lambda_param, gamma, epsilon, target_vertex_count):
    """
    f: function returning scalar value at point x
    grad_f: function returning gradient vector at point x
    domain_box: bounding box of domain Omega
    lambda_param: coefficient for face distance bound L
    gamma: gradation parameter
    epsilon: small offset
    target_vertex_count: desired number of vertices
    """
    # Initialize mesh with a coarse homeomorphic triangulation
    mesh = initialize_coarse_mesh(f, domain_box)
    
    # Phase 1: Delaunay Refinement
    while True:
        refined = False
        # Check Face Distance Condition
        for facet in mesh.facets:
            circumcenter = compute_circumcenter(facet.vertices)
            # Find furthest intersection of dual Voronoi edge with surface
            dual_edge = compute_dual_voronoi_edge(facet)
            intersections = find_surface_intersections(dual_edge, f)
            if not intersections:
                continue
            q_max = max(intersections, key=lambda q: distance(circumcenter, q))
            df = distance(circumcenter, q_max)
            
            if df >= lambda_param * min_edge_length(domain_box):
                insert_vertex(mesh, q_max)
                refined = True
                break # Restart loop to maintain consistency
        
        # Check Topological Disk Condition
        if not refined:
            for vertex in mesh.vertices:
                if not is_topological_disk(vertex.neighbors):
                    # Find facet incident to vertex with max DF
                    incident_facets = vertex.incident_facets
                    max_df_facet = max(incident_facets, key=lambda f: compute_df(f, f))
                    dual_edge = compute_dual_voronoi_edge(max_df_facet)
                    intersections = find_surface_intersections(dual_edge, f)
                    if intersections:
                        q = intersections[0] # Insert intersection
                        insert_vertex(mesh, q)
                        refined = True
                        break
        
        if not refined:
            break

    # Geometrical Refinement if vertex count is insufficient
    if len(mesh.vertices) < target_vertex_count:
        priority_queue = PriorityQueue()
        for triangle in mesh.triangles:
            priority = compute_priority(triangle, f, gamma, epsilon)
            priority_queue.push(triangle, priority)
        
        while len(mesh.vertices) < target_vertex_count and not priority_queue.empty():
            t, _ = priority_queue.pop()
            circumcenter = compute_circumcenter(t.vertices)
            
            # Handle obtuse triangles
            if is_obtuse(t):
                circumcenter = constrain_to_triangle_boundary(circumcenter, t)
            
            new_vertex = insert_vertex(mesh, circumcenter)
            split_triangle(mesh, t, new_vertex)
            
            # Update local Delaunay via edge flips
            update_delaunay_local(mesh, t.neighbors)
            
            # Add new triangles to queue
            for new_t in t.new_triangles:
                priority = compute_priority(new_t, f, gamma, epsilon)
                priority_queue.push(new_t, priority)

    # Phase 2: Relaxation
    for iteration in range(max_iterations):
        # Step 1: Isotropic Sampling (CVT)
        for vertex in mesh.vertices:
            voronoi_cell = compute_geodesic_voronoi_cell(vertex, mesh)
            barycenter = compute_weighted_barycenter(voronoi_cell, f, gamma, epsilon)
            
            # Constrain to tangent plane
            normal = -grad_f(vertex.pos) / norm(grad_f(vertex.pos))
            vertex.pos = vertex.pos - dot(vertex.pos - barycenter, normal) * normal
            
        # Step 2: QEM Relocation
        for vertex in mesh.vertices:
            # Move vertex along normal to minimize distance to surface
            # Using Newton's method or similar root finding for f(x)=0
            vertex.pos = project_to_surface_qem(vertex, f, grad_f)
            
        # Step 3: Triangulation Update
        for edge in mesh.edges:
            if is_non_local_delaunay(edge, mesh):
                flip_edge(mesh, edge)
                
        # Check convergence (e.g., max displacement < threshold)
        if check_convergence(mesh):
            break

    return mesh

def compute_priority(triangle, f, gamma, epsilon):
    area = triangle_area(triangle)
    normals = [-grad_f(v.pos) / norm(grad_f(v.pos)) for v in triangle.vertices]
    sum_normals = normals[0] + normals[1] + normals[2]
    numerator = area * (9 - norm(sum_normals)**2)
    denominator = gamma + epsilon
    return numerator / denominator

def compute_weighted_barycenter(voronoi_cell, f, gamma, epsilon):
    weighted_sum = Vector3(0, 0, 0)
    weight_sum = 0
    for triangle in voronoi_cell.triangles:
        # Compute weight chi
        p = voronoi_cell.center_vertex
        n_p = -grad_f(p.pos) / norm(grad_f(p.pos))
        # Normals at adjacent triangle barycenters projected on surface
        bary1 = triangle.barycenter
        n1 = project_normal_to_surface(grad_f(bary1), f)
        bary2 = triangle.barycenter # Simplified: use adjacent triangles in cell
        n2 = project_normal_to_surface(grad_f(bary2), f)
        
        chi = (9 - norm(n_p + n1 + n2)**2) / (gamma + epsilon)
        weighted_sum += chi * triangle.circumcenter * triangle.area
        weight_sum += chi * triangle.area
        
    return weighted_sum / weight_sum
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The Delaunay refinement phase is computationally expensive due to the need to maintain a 3D Delaunay triangulation and compute surface intersections for dual Voronoi edges. The relaxation phase involves iterative vertex moves and local triangulation updates, with complexity proportional to the number of vertices and iterations.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Linear in the number of vertices and triangles, storing the mesh, Voronoi cells, and priority queue.
*   **Strengths**: Produces high-quality, isotropic meshes with excellent geometric approximation. The variational approach ensures uniform normal variation and handles complex implicit surfaces well.
*   **Limitations**: Computationally intensive, especially the initial refinement phase. Requires efficient computation of surface intersections and gradient evaluations.
*   **When to use**: When high-quality meshing of implicit surfaces is required, and computational resources allow for iterative optimization.
*   **When not to use**: For real-time applications requiring immediate mesh generation, or when the implicit function is expensive to evaluate.

## Background
Variational Isotropic Meshing was proposed in the paper "Variational implicit surface meshing" [S1]. It builds on the concept of Delaunay refinement for implicit surfaces and incorporates Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation (CVT) and Quadric Error Metrics (QEM) to optimize the resulting mesh. The method addresses the limitations of standard refinement by ensuring both topological correctness and geometric optimality.
