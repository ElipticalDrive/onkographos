# Sorting-free Projected Tetrahedra

Also called: Stochastic Projected Tetrahedral

## Overview
Sorting-free Projected Tetrahedra (SPT) is a volume rendering technique for irregular tetrahedral datasets that approximates back-to-front accumulation using stochastic color composition, thereby eliminating the need for explicit visibility sorting [S1]. Unlike its sibling [Projected Tetrahedra](877-projected-tetrahedra.md), which requires sorting tetrahedra before rendering, SPT controls particle rendering via opacity as a probability in the rasterization process, avoiding the sorting artifacts inherent in k-buffer methods like [Hardware-Assisted Projection](5625-hardware-assisted-projection.md) (HAVS) [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A tetrahedral mesh (irregular volume data), a transfer function, and a repetition level $N$ (or $L_r$).
*   **Output:** A rendered image where brightness is the ensemble average of $N$ stochastic passes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Pre-integration Lookup Table:** A 3D texture mapping three integration parameters to pre-integrated color $C$ and opacity $\alpha$ [S1].
    *   **Fields:** Scalar value at the front ($s_f$), scalar value at the back ($s_b$), and segment length ($d$) [S1].
    *   **Mapping:** $C = C(s_f, s_b, d)$ and $\alpha = \alpha(s_f, s_b, d)$ [S1].
    *   **Note:** The pre-integrated color is opacity-weighted (divided by the corresponding opacity) [S1].
2.  **Geometry Shader Outputs:** For each split triangle, the "thickest" vertex passes:
    *   Front scalar ($s_f$)
    *   Back scalar ($s_b$)
    *   Distance value ($d$) [S1]

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Classification and Decomposition (Geometry Shader):**
    *   Input: A single tetrahedron (4 vertices) [S1].
    *   Classify the tetrahedron into 4 classes of projection patterns based on its intersection with the view frustum/image plane [S1].
    *   Decompose the tetrahedron into triangles.
    *   Emit one or two triangle strips (triangle fans are not supported by geometry shaders) [S1].
    *   Calculate parameters ($s_f, s_b, d$) for vertices on each triangle. Pass these through the "thickest" vertex of the split triangle [S1].

2.  **Stochastic Color Composition (Fragment Shader) - Repeated $N$ times:**
    *   For each fragment generated by the rasterized triangles:
        *   Look up the pre-integrated transfer function in the 3D texture using the interpolated $(s_f, s_b, d)$ to obtain color $C$ and opacity $\alpha$ [S1].
        *   **Stochastic Discard:** Generate a random number $r \in [0, 1]$. If $r > \alpha$, discard the fragment. Otherwise, proceed [S1].
        *   **Brightness Assignment:** If the fragment is not discarded, it represents a particle. The luminosity of the particle nearest to the viewpoint is assigned to the brightness value for this pass [S1].
            *   *Note:* In the basic SPT algorithm, particle depth is determined as that of a pre-defined location (front, back, or middle point on the ray segment) [S2, abstract only].

3.  **Accumulation:**
    *   Repeat Steps 1-2 for $N$ independent ensembles (repetitions) [S1].
    *   Calculate the average of the brightness values from all $N$ repetitions to obtain the final pixel color [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Probability of Brightness:** The probability that the $k$-th luminosity equals the brightness is defined as the event where there is no particle from the first to the $(k-1)$-th ray-segments and more than one particle in the $k$-th ray segment [S1].
*   **Particle Distribution:** The number of particles follows a Poisson distribution along the viewing ray segment intersecting the tetrahedral cell [S2, abstract only]. The particle spacing follows an exponential distribution [S2, abstract only].
*   **Variance of Total Brightness:** Since variances are identical and covariance is zero (independent occurrences):
    $$ Var(B_{total}) = \frac{Var(B^{(i)})}{N} $$
    where $B^{(i)}$ is the brightness value of the $i$-th ensemble [S1].
*   **Deviation:**
    $$ \sigma_{total} = \sqrt{\frac{Var(B^{(i)})}{N}} $$
    [S1]
*   **Complexity:** The computational complexity of the standard stochastic approach is $O(n^2)$, but SPT's complexity is proportional to the repetition level $N$ and the number of cells [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Repetition Level ($N$ or $L_r$):** The number of times Steps 1-2 are repeated. Controls the level of detail and noise. Larger deviation allows smaller $N$ [S1]. Typical values tested: 1, 25, 100, 144 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Triangle Fans:** Geometry shaders do not support triangle fans. Workaround: Emit one or two triangle strips instead [S1].
*   **Multiple Volumes:** The basic SPT algorithm causes prominent artifacts when treating multiple volumes because it controls particles stochastically in the image plane but not in the depth direction [S2, abstract only].
*   **Sorting Errors:** Unlike HAVS, SPT avoids rendering artifacts caused by visibility sorting errors [S1].

## Subtopics

### Nearest particle calculation technique
A technique applied to multiple volumes to resolve the depth-direction artifact of the SPT algorithm by accurately determining particle positions along the ray [S2, abstract only]. It constructs a cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the particle spacing and develops a technique for calculating the nearest particle along the interval using the CDF [S2, abstract only].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Proportional to the repetition level $N$ and the number of cells [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on VRAM. On an NVIDIA GeForce 9600GT (512MB VRAM), it supports up to ~24.5 million tetrahedral cells at 1024x1024 resolution [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   Computing time: ~1.4 seconds per repetition for 24.5 million cells at 1024x1024 [S1].
    *   Construction time: ~100 milliseconds to construct the 3D texture for pre-integrated classification [S1].
    *   Generation time: ~17.4 seconds to generate particles (if using particle-based comparison models like PBVR) [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   No visibility sorting required, avoiding k-buffer artifacts [S1].
    *   No extra buffer space for candidate fragments [S1].
    *   Can visualize continuous iso-surfaces of precipitous transfer functions better than PBVR [S1].
    *   Suitable for high-resolution displays (e.g., tiled-display walls) [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Noise decreases with $N$, requiring higher $N$ for high quality, which increases compute time [S1].
    *   Basic SPT has artifacts with multiple volumes [S2, abstract only].
    *   Construction/generation times are significant when transfer functions change or for time-varying datasets [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When the number of tetrahedral cells is smaller than 24.5 million and particle count is larger than 24 million, or when sorting artifacts are unacceptable [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** For datasets with >24.5 million cells (PBVR is preferred) or when real-time performance is critical and high $N$ is required [S1].

## Background
Sorting-free Projected Tetrahedra was introduced as a sorting-free volume rendering technique for irregular tetrahedral datasets [S1]. It builds on the concept of Projected Tetrahedra but replaces the sorting step with stochastic color composition [S1]. The method was developed to overcome drawbacks of visibility sorting (like in HAVS) and particle-based methods (like PBVR) [S1]. It was tested on datasets with up to 1.25 million cells and later scaled to 24.5 million cells [S1]. A later extension addressed the multiple volume artifact by introducing nearest particle calculation techniques [S2, abstract only].
