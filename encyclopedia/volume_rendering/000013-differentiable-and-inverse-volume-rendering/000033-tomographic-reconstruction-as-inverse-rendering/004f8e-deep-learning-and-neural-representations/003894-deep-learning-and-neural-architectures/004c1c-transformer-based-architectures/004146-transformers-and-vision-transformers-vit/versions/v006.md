# Transformers and Vision Transformers (ViT)

Also called: Vision Transformer

## Overview
Transformers compute pairwise attention over input tokens to capture long-range dependencies that convolutions miss [S1, unofficial]. The Vision Transformer (ViT) adapts this architecture for images by treating fixed-size patches as tokens [S1, unofficial]. In volume rendering, they are used for super-resolution and denoising, where their ability to attend across the full spatial extent of a subvolume helps recover large-scale structures that local convolutions miss [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sequence of $N$ tokens derived from image patches. Each token $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ represents a flattened patch of the input volume slice or feature map.
*   **Output:** A sequence of transformed tokens $z'_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with the same dimensionality, preserving the spatial arrangement for reconstruction.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tokens:** A matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, where $N$ is the number of patches and $d$ is the embedding dimension.
*   **Learnable Parameters:**
    *   Query, Key, Value projection matrices: $W_Q, W_K, W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.
    *   Output projection matrix: $W_O \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.
    *   Layer normalization parameters (scale $\gamma$, shift $\beta$).
    *   Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) weights: $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 4d}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{4d \times d}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Patch Embedding:** The input image (or volume slice) is divided into non-overlapping patches of size $P \times P$. Each patch is flattened and linearly projected to dimension $d$. A learnable class token or positional embeddings are added to preserve spatial information [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Self-Attention:** For each token $i$, compute attention scores against all other tokens $j$:
    $$
    \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V
    $$
    where $Q = X W_Q$, $K = X W_K$, and $V = X W_V$. The scaling factor $\sqrt{d}$ prevents vanishing gradients in the softmax [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Residual Connection and Normalization:** The output of the attention layer is added to the input (residual connection) and passed through Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Feed-Forward Network (FFN):** The normalized tokens are passed through a position-wise FFN, typically consisting of two linear layers with a non-linear activation (e.g., GELU) in between:
    $$
    \text{FFN}(x) = x W_1 + b_1) \text{GELU}(\cdot) W_2 + b_2
    $$
5.  **Second Residual and Normalization:** Another residual connection and LayerNorm are applied after the FFN [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Reconstruction:** The final token sequence is reshaped back to spatial dimensions and upsampled or decoded to form the output volume or image.

**Parameters:**
*   **Patch Size ($P$):** Typically $16 \times 16$ or $32 \times 32$ pixels for images; analogous voxel block sizes for volumes.
*   **Embedding Dimension ($d$):** Commonly 768 or 1024.
*   **Number of Heads:** Typically 12 or 16.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Positional Information:** Without explicit positional embeddings, the permutation-invariant nature of attention loses spatial structure [S1, unofficial].
*   **Computational Cost:** Self-attention scales quadratically with the number of tokens ($O(N^2)$), which can be prohibitive for high-resolution volume data [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Fusion ViT](13080-fusion-vit.md)
Fusion ViT is a neural module designed to fuse multi-view features by performing all-to-all self-attention over encoded image patches or rendered feature vectors from multiple input views. It solves the problem of multi-view inconsistency and incomplete context in neural rendering and tomographic reconstruction by providing each view's features with full spatial and semantic context from all other views. It distinguishes itself from standard fusion methods by leveraging the Transformer's ability to model long-range dependencies across views, either at the patch level for global reconstruction or at the pixel level for lightweight synthesis.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N^2 d)$ per layer due to the attention matrix computation, where $N$ is the number of patches/tokens and $d$ is the embedding dimension. This is significantly higher than convolutional $O(k^2 d)$ operations for small kernels $k$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N^2)$ to store the attention matrix.
*   **Strengths:** Captures global context and long-range dependencies effectively, which is critical for recovering large-scale structures in volume super-resolution and denoising [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High computational and memory requirements for large volumes; requires large amounts of data to train effectively compared to CNNs [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When global context is more important than local texture details, or when dealing with artifacts that span large distances in the volume.
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are limited or the volume resolution is extremely high without optimization (e.g., sparse attention).

## Background
The Transformer architecture was originally introduced for sequence modeling tasks [S1, unofficial]. The Vision Transformer (ViT) adapted this for computer vision by treating image patches as tokens [S1, unofficial]. In the context of scientific volume visualization and rendering, Transformers have been applied to super-resolution and denoising tasks to leverage their global receptive field [S1, unofficial].
