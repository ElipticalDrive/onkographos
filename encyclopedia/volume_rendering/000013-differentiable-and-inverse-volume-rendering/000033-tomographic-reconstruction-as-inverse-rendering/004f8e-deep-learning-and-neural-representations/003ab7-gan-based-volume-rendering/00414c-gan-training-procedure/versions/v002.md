# GAN Training Procedure
Also called: Min-Max Game Training, Adversarial Training
## Overview
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) train a generator and a discriminator in a competitive framework to produce perceptually realistic outputs, avoiding the oversmoothing typical of pixelwise error minimization [S2, unofficial][S3]. This method is particularly suited for volume super-resolution and inverse rendering tasks where structural fidelity is critical [S2, unofficial][S3].
## How it works
**Inputs:** Real images (ground truth), visualization parameters (e.g., viewpoint, transfer function coefficients), and optionally auxiliary data (e.g., opacity images for translation tasks) [S1, S5].
**Outputs:** Updated network parameters for the generator ($\theta_G$) and discriminator ($\theta_D$) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Generator ($G$):** Neural network mapping visualization parameters to rendered images [S1, S3].
*   **Discriminator ($D$):** Neural network mapping images to scalar probabilities of being real [S1, S3].
*   **Optimizer:** ADAM optimizer with parameters $\epsilon$ (learning rate denominator), $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$ [S1, S5].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Discriminator Update (Real):** Pass a batch of real images and their corresponding visualization parameters into the discriminator. Compute the binary cross-entropy loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these images as real [S1].
2.  **Image Synthesis:** Pass the visualization parameters (and opacity image, if applicable) through the generator to synthesize fake images [S1].
3.  **Discriminator Update (Fake):** Pass the synthesized images into the discriminator. Compute the binary cross-entropy loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these images as false (fake) [S1].
4.  **Generator Update:** Pass the synthesized images into the discriminator. Compute the loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these images as real (tricking the discriminator). Update the generator's parameters to minimize this loss [S1].
5.  **Parameter Update:** Update all network parameters using minibatch stochastic gradient descent with the ADAM optimizer [S1].

**Formulas:**
The core training objective is a min-max game [S1]:
$$ \min_G \max_D V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}(x)}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z(z)}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))] $$
Where $x$ is a real image, $z$ represents the visualization parameters/noise, and $D(\cdot)$ is the discriminator's output probability [S1, S3].

For specific applications like ocean geometry reconstruction, a total loss function may be used [S5]:
$$ L_{Total} = \alpha L_{Image} + \beta_1 L_{Dn} + \beta_2 L_{Dr} + \gamma_1 L_{Dtn} + \gamma_2 L_{Dtr} $$
Where $\alpha, \beta_1, \beta_2, \gamma_1, \gamma_2$ are weights, and $L_{Dn}, L_{Dr}, L_{Dtn}, L_{Dtr}$ are the losses from spatial discriminators for normal/roughness maps and temporal discriminators [S5].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** ADAM [S1, S5].
*   **ADAM Settings:** $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$, $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$ [S5].
*   **Loss Weighting:** In volume rendering GANs, $\lambda = 150$ is used to weight the $L_1$ loss relative to adversarial loss to preserve color and stabilize training [S1].
*   **Training Duration:** 200 epochs for initial sub-networks, followed by 100 epochs for subsequent networks [S5].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Oversmoothing:** Standard MSE-optimized outputs tend toward oversmoothing; GANs mitigate this but require careful balancing of adversarial and reconstruction losses [S2, unofficial].
*   **Training Stability:** Progressive growing strategies help stabilize training by starting at low resolutions [S4].
*   **Data Generation:** Training data for volume rendering GANs often requires generating viewpoints and transfer functions (e.g., via Gaussian Mixture Models) to cover the parameter space [S1].

## Subtopics

### Progressive GAN Discriminator
A convolutional discriminator that grows progressively in resolution to stabilize training and allow larger batch sizes at lower resolutions [S4]. It begins training at 32×32 resolution and doubles the resolution twice during training, up to 128 × 128, adding new layers to handle higher resolutions [S4]. Unlike standard ProgressiveGAN, the generator architecture does not grow; instead, resolution is increased by sampling rays more densely from the same implicit representation [S4]. Choose this when training stability is a concern or when starting with limited computational resources for high-resolution batches.

### Adversarial Loss for Ocean Geometry
Uses spatial discriminators for normal and roughness maps, and temporal discriminators for temporal coherence, to enforce that inferred data spatial distribution is similar to real data [S5]. It trains sub-networks together using a total loss function combining image, spatial, and temporal discriminator losses for 200 epochs, alternating with auxiliary discriminators [S5]. Choose this when reconstructing dynamic scenes requiring both spatial detail and temporal consistency, such as ocean surfaces [S5].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Time complexity is dominated by the alternating gradient descent steps for the generator and discriminator, requiring multiple forward and backward passes per iteration [S1].
*   **Performance:** GANs produce perceptually superior results compared to MSE-optimized methods, which tend to oversmooth [S2, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** Demonstrated on single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 SUPER GPUs [S5].
*   **Strengths:** High perceptual fidelity, ability to generate realistic textures and structures [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Limitations:** Training can be unstable; requires careful balancing of loss functions and potentially progressive growing strategies [S1, S4].
*   **When to use:** When perceptual quality is more important than pixel-wise accuracy, or when dealing with tasks like super-resolution where MSE fails [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When strict pixel-wise accuracy is required, or when training stability is difficult to achieve without extensive tuning [S2, unofficial].

## Background
GANs were introduced as a technique consisting of two neural networks, a generator and a discriminator, trained in a competitive framework [S3]. They have demonstrated effectiveness for super-resolution, style transfer, and image-to-image translation [S3]. In the context of volume rendering, they were applied to generate realistic volume visualizations by training against real rendered images, addressing the limitations of traditional optimization methods [S1]. The concept of progressive growing for discriminators was introduced to stabilize training at high resolutions [S4].
