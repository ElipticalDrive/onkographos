# Proximal Operator
Also called: Prox, Proximal Mapping
## Overview
The proximal operator is a mathematical tool used in proximal algorithms to solve non-smooth, constrained, or large-scale optimization problems by iteratively getting closer to the optimal solution while staying close to the current estimate [S1, unofficial]. It serves as a generalization of projections onto convex sets, allowing complex optimization problems to be split into smaller, simpler sub-problems [S1, unofficial]. In the context of tomographic reconstruction, it enables the handling of regularization terms (such as sparsity or smoothing norms) that often lack closed-form solutions in standard gradient-based methods [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A vector $u \in \mathbb{R}^n$ (the current estimate or intermediate variable), a scalar $\lambda > 0$ (regularization parameter), and a proper, lower-semicontinuous, convex function $h: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$.
- Output: A vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ that minimizes the objective function.

**Data Structures:**
- The input $u$ and output $x$ are typically dense vectors or grids (e.g., voxel arrays in tomography).
- The function $h$ is defined analytically or via its subgradient.

**Formula:**
The proximal operator of $h$ at $u$ with parameter $\lambda$ is defined as:
$$ \text{prox}_{\lambda h}(u) = \operatorname{argmin}_x \left( h(x) + \frac{1}{2\lambda} \|x - u\|_2^2 \right) $$
where:
- $x$ is the variable to optimize.
- $u$ is the current estimate.
- $\lambda$ is the regularization parameter controlling the trade-off between minimizing $h(x)$ and staying close to $u$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\| \cdot \|_2$ denotes the Euclidean norm [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Define the Objective:** For a given $u$ and $\lambda$, construct the composite objective function $J(x) = h(x) + \frac{1}{2\lambda} \|x - u\|_2^2$.
2. **Minimize:** Solve for $x$ that minimizes $J(x)$.
   - If $h(x)$ is differentiable and strongly convex, this can be solved via gradient descent or Newton's method.
   - If $h(x)$ is non-smooth (e.g., L1 norm) or has a known closed-form solution, use the specific analytical formula.
3. **Return:** The resulting $x$ is $\text{prox}_{\lambda h}(u)$.

**Pseudocode (General Case):**
```python

    # This step varies significantly depending on h.
    # For many common h, a closed-form exists (see Subtopics).

    if h_subgradient is not None:
```

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Regularization parameter. Typical values depend on the scale of $h(x)$ and the data fidelity term. In tomography, it balances noise suppression against resolution loss [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Non-convex $h$:** The proximal operator is well-defined and single-valued only if $h$ is convex. For non-convex functions, the operator may be set-valued or undefined [S1, unofficial].
- **Large $\lambda$:** If $\lambda$ is too large, the output $x$ will be heavily biased towards minimizing $h(x)$, potentially ignoring the data fidelity term in the larger algorithm.
- **Small $\lambda$:** If $\lambda$ is too small, the output $x$ will be close to $u$, providing little regularization effect.
- **Precision:** Numerical stability depends on the implementation of the inner minimization. For closed-form solutions (like L1), precision issues arise near the threshold boundary [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### L1 Norm Proximal Operator
The proximal operator for the L1 norm, used for sparsity regularization. It applies soft thresholding to the input vector [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when the solution is expected to be sparse (e.g., in compressed sensing or edge-preserving reconstruction) [S1, unofficial].
**Formula:**
$$ \text{prox}_{\lambda g}(u) = (u - \lambda)_+ - (-u - \lambda)_+ $$
where $(u)_+ = \max(u, 0)$ is the positive part of $u$ [S1, unofficial].
**Element-wise:**
$$ (\text{prox}_{\lambda g}(u))_i = \begin{cases} u_i - \lambda & \text{if } u_i \geq \lambda \\ 0 & \text{if } |u_i| \leq \lambda \\ u_i + \lambda & \text{if } u_i \leq -\lambda \end{cases} $$
where $(\text{prox}_{\lambda g}(u))_i$ is the $i$-th element of the output [S1, unofficial].

### Anisotropic Total Variation (ATV) Prior
A regularization prior defined as $h(x) = \sum_{i,j,k} |x_{i+1,j,k} - x_{i,j,k}| + |x_{i,j+1,k} - x_{i,j,k}| + |x_{i,j,k+1} - x_{i,j,k}|$, where $x_{i,j,k}$ is the voxel value [S1, unofficial]. It can be represented as $g(Dx)$ where $D$ is the forward difference matrix [S1, unofficial]. The proximal operator for ATV is more complex than L1 and typically requires iterative methods or splitting techniques (like Chambolle-Pock) to solve, as it does not have a simple closed-form element-wise solution [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when piecewise constant solutions with sharp edges are desired [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the function $h$. For L1, it is $O(n)$ where $n$ is the number of elements. For general $h$, it depends on the inner optimization solver.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(n)$ to store the input and output vectors.
- **Strengths:** Modular; allows handling of non-smooth regularization terms. Often admits closed-form solutions for common norms (L1, L2, etc.).
- **Limitations:** Requires convexity for well-definedness. For complex priors (like Total Variation), the proximal operator may not have a closed form, requiring nested optimization.
- **When to use:** When the optimization problem involves non-smooth regularization terms (sparsity, total variation) that are difficult to handle with standard gradient descent.
- **When not to use:** When the function $h$ is smooth and strongly convex, standard gradient-based methods may be simpler and faster.

## Background
The proximal operator was introduced as a generalization of projections onto convex sets [S1, unofficial]. It has been widely used in optimization theory and applied to various fields, including image processing and tomography. In industrial computed tomography, proximal algorithms utilizing these operators have been employed to reconstruct high-quality images from limited or noisy projection data [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the Chambolle-Pock algorithm, which splits the optimization problem into sub-problems solvable via proximal operators [3282-chambolle-pock-algorithm.md].
