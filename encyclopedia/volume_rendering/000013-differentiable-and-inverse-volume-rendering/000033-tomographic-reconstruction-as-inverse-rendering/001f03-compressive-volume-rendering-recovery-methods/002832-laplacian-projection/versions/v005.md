# Laplacian Projection

Also called: Laplacian Regularization for Sparse View Reconstruction

## Overview
Laplacian Projection is a physics-based tomographic reconstruction method employing Laplacian regularization for sparse view reconstruction [S1]. It represents the state-of-the-art in physics-based smoke reconstruction [S1]. It is evaluated against methods such as 3D Gaussian Splatting to validate its effectiveness across different input configurations [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A set of 2D projection images (views). The method supports single-view ($N=1$) and multi-view ($N=16$) configurations [S1].
- Output: A 3D volume grid representing the reconstructed physical property [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- The volume is represented as a 3D grid with a resolution of $128 \times 196 \times 128$ [S1]. Not given in the sources for other layout details [G].

**Method Steps:**
1. **Initialization:** Initialize the 3D volume field $V$ (e.g., with zeros or a prior estimate) [G].
2. **Forward Projection:** Render the current volume estimate $V$ to generate synthetic 2D projections $P_{\text{syn}}$ for each input view angle [G]. This involves integrating the volume along rays, potentially using directional samples [G]. The source recommends 30 directional samples as the optimal balance between accuracy and performance [S1].
3. **Error Calculation:** Compute the difference between the synthetic projections $P_{\text{syn}}$ and the observed input views $P_{\text{obs}}$ [G].
4. **Regularization Term Calculation:** Compute the Laplacian of the volume field, $\nabla^2 V$ [G]. This term penalizes curvature, enforcing smoothness [G].
5. **Optimization Step:** Update the volume field $V$ by minimizing a combined cost function comprising a data fidelity term and a Laplacian regularization term [G]. The update can be performed using gradient-based optimization or projection-based iterative solvers [G]. Not given in the sources for the exact mathematical formulation or solver specifics [G].
6. **Iteration:** Repeat steps 2-5 until convergence or a maximum number of iterations is reached [G]. The source investigates iteration limits of 250, 1000, and 5000 to analyze the quality-efficiency trade-off [S1].

**Parameters:**
- Number of views: 1 (single-view) or 16 (multi-view) [S1].
- Directional samples: 30 (recommended for optimal balance) [S1].
- Volume resolution: $128 \times 196 \times 128$ (as used in experiments) [S1].
- Iteration limit: Variable (250, 1000, 5000 tested) [S1].
- Regularization weight $\lambda$: Not given in the sources [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Sparse views can lead to significant artifacts if regularization is too weak [G].
- Precision issues may arise in the calculation of second-order derivatives on discrete grids [G].
- Over-regularization may blur sharp features [G].

## Subtopics

### [Laplacian Regularization](10293-laplacian-regularization.md)
Enforces spatial smoothness on volume properties by penalizing their Laplacian, addressing the ill-posed nature of sparse-view tomographic reconstruction [G]. It distinguishes itself from standard Tikhonov regularization by directly penalizing second-order spatial derivatives (curvature) rather than first-order gradients, which better preserves sharp features while suppressing high-frequency noise [G]. Choose it when second-order smoothness constraints are preferred over first-order gradient penalties [G].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of iterations and the cost of forward projection and Laplacian computation [G]. The source notes that 30 directional samples are used for pre-computation [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the volume grid size ($128 \times 196 \times 128$) and the number of views [S1].
- **Performance:** Evaluated on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 Ti GPU using the ScalarFlow dataset (300×531 pixels) [S1]. It is described as state-of-the-art for physics-based smoke reconstruction [S1].
- **Strengths:** Effective for sparse-view reconstruction; suitable for smoke and spray imaging [S1].
- **Limitations:** Performance depends on the choice of regularization parameter and iteration count; may be computationally intensive for high-resolution volumes or large numbers of views [G].
- **When to use:** When reconstructing volumetric media like smoke from limited angular data [S1].
- **When not to use:** When high computational resources are unavailable or when the input data is dense and standard methods suffice [G].

## Background
The method has been evaluated against 3D Gaussian Splatting using the ScalarFlow dataset to demonstrate its effectiveness for smoke reconstruction [S1].
