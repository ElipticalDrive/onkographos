# Differentiable Detector Panel Model

Also called: TomOpt panel model

## Overview
The Differentiable Detector Panel Model is a hit-recording method used in muon tomography optimization (specifically within the TomOpt framework) to make detector performance metrics—spatial resolution and hit efficiency—continuous functions of the muon's position relative to the panel [S1]. It solves the problem of non-differentiability in standard binary detector models (where hits are either recorded or not, and resolution is constant or zero) by replacing step functions with smooth sigmoid transitions [S1]. This allows gradients to flow with respect to all panel parameters (position, size, and z-depth) during inverse rendering or optimization, unlike its nearest alternative, the binary rectangle model, which is only suitable for validation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Muon trajectory parameters (specifically the true intersection point $xy_{true}$ on the detector plane), panel configuration parameters (position $xyz$, width $xy$-span), and physical limits ($\sigma_{max}$, $\epsilon_{max}$).
*   **Outputs:** Effective spatial resolution $\sigma_{xy,eff}$ and effective detection efficiency $\epsilon_{eff}$ for the hit.

**Data Structures:**
Each panel is defined by 5 learnable parameters:
1.  $x_{pos}$: Center x-coordinate.
2.  $y_{pos}$: Center y-coordinate.
3.  $z_{pos}$: Z-depth (position along beam axis).
4.  $w_{span}$: Width in x-dimension (assumed square or defined by separate x/y spans, though the text specifies "width in xy" generally, the model uses symmetric sigmoids).
5.  $h_{span}$: Height in y-dimension (implied by "width in xy" and symmetric treatment, or treated as a single span parameter if square). *Note: The source specifies "width in xy" as a single learnable parameter, implying a square panel or identical scaling.*

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_{max}$: Maximum spatial resolution of the panel (fixed constant).
*   $\epsilon_{max}$: Maximum detection efficiency of the panel (fixed constant).
*   $xy_{true}$: The true coordinates of the muon's intersection with the detector plane.

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Unphysical Hit Recording:** During optimization, every muon is assumed to "hit" the panel at its current $z$ position, regardless of whether the $xy_{true}$ coordinates fall within the physical boundaries of the panel [S1]. This ensures the hit count is differentiable with respect to panel position and size [S1].
2.  **Panel Model Evaluation ($w$):** Compute a weighting factor $w(xy_{true})$ that scales between 0 and 1. This factor represents the "quality" of the hit based on its distance from the panel center. The model uses a pair of double-sigmoid functions, one for the x-dimension and one for the y-dimension [S1].
3.  **Compute Effective Resolution:** Calculate the effective spatial resolution using the inverse of the weighting factor:
    $$ \sigma_{xy,eff} = \frac{\sigma_{max}}{w(xy_{true})} $$
    As the muon moves away from the center, $w$ decreases, causing $\sigma_{xy,eff}$ to increase (worsen) [S1].
4.  **Compute Effective Efficiency:** Calculate the effective detection efficiency using the direct weighting factor:
    $$ \epsilon_{eff} = \epsilon_{max} \cdot w(xy_{true}) $$
    As the muon moves away from the center, $w$ decreases, causing $\epsilon_{eff}$ to decrease [S1].

**Formulas:**
The core weighting function $w(xy_{true})$ is constructed from double-sigmoid functions. Let $x_{rel} = x_{true} - x_{pos}$ and $y_{rel} = y_{true} - y_{pos}$ be the relative coordinates. Let $S_{span}$ be the panel span (width/height). The double-sigmoid function $D(u, S)$ for a dimension $u$ with span $S$ is typically defined to be 1 at $u=0$ and decay to 0 as $|u|$ increases. While the exact mathematical form of the "double-sigmoid" is not explicitly expanded in the provided text, it is described as a pair of functions in x and y [S1].

Let $w_x(x_{rel})$ and $w_y(y_{rel})$ be the sigmoid values for x and y respectively.
$$ w(xy_{true}) = w_x(x_{rel}) \cdot w_y(y_{rel}) $$

The effective metrics are then:
$$ \sigma_{xy,eff} = \sigma_{max} \cdot \frac{1}{w(xy_{true})} $$
$$ \epsilon_{eff} = \epsilon_{max} \cdot w(xy_{true}) $$

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-trivial Derivatives:** In a binary model, derivatives for $xy$-position and $xy$-span are zero or undefined because they only affect whether a hit is recorded, not the hit's properties. The differentiable model solves this by making resolution and efficiency continuous functions of position [S1].
*   **Unphysical Hits:** The model records hits for muons outside the panel. This is unphysical but necessary for gradient flow. Validation should revert to a binary model [S1].
*   **Division by Zero:** If $w(xy_{true})$ approaches zero (muon far from panel), $\sigma_{xy,eff}$ approaches infinity. Implementation should handle this numerically (e.g., clamping or large value) [S1].

### [Sigmoid Panel Modeling](21394-sigmoid-panel-modeling.md)
Sigmoid Panel Modeling is a differentiable detector hit-recording method used in muon tomography optimization (specifically within the TomOpt framework) to make detector performance metrics—spatial resolution and hit efficiency—continuous functions of the muon's position relative to the panel. It solves the problem of non-differentiability in standard binary detector models (where hits are either recorded or not, and resolution is constant or zero) by replacing step functions with smooth sigmoid transitions. This allows gradients to flow with respect to all panel parameters (position, size, and z-depth) during inverse rendering or optimization, unlike its nearest alternative, the binary rectangle model, which is only suitable for validation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per muon per panel interaction, as it involves simple arithmetic operations and sigmoid evaluations.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per panel, storing 5 learnable parameters plus fixed constants.
*   **Strengths:** Enables gradient-based optimization of detector geometry (position and size) which is impossible with binary models. Provides smooth transitions for resolution and efficiency.
*   **Limitations:** Unphysical (records hits outside panel). The number of panels per layer is fixed during optimization because gradients for integer variables (number of panels) are difficult to compute [S1].
*   **When to use:** During the optimization phase of detector design in TomOpt.
*   **When not to use:** For final validation or simulation where physical accuracy is required; revert to binary model [S1].

## Background
The Differentiable Detector Panel Model was introduced in the TomOpt framework for differential optimization of particle detectors in muon tomography [S1]. It addresses the challenge of optimizing detector geometry (position and size) using gradient-based methods, which require differentiable objective functions. Traditional binary detector models are non-differentiable with respect to geometric parameters, preventing their use in such optimization [S1]. The method was developed to allow flexible parameterization of detectors without a priori assumptions about optimal configuration [S1].
