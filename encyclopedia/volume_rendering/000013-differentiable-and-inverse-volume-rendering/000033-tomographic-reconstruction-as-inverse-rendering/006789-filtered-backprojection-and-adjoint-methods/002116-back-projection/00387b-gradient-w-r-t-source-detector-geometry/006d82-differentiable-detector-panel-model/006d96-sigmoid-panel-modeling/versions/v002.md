# Sigmoid Panel Modeling

Also called: Double-sigmoid hit recording model

## Overview
Sigmoid Panel Modeling is a differentiable detector hit-recording method used in muon tomography optimization to make detector performance metrics—spatial resolution and hit efficiency—continuous functions of the muon's position relative to the panel [S1]. It solves the problem of non-differentiability in standard binary detector models by replacing step functions with smooth sigmoid transitions [S1]. This allows gradients to flow with respect to all panel parameters during inverse rendering or optimization, unlike its nearest alternative, the binary rectangle model, which is only suitable for validation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Muon trajectory parameters (position and direction) [S1].
*   Detector panel parameters: maximum spatial resolution $\sigma_{max}$, maximum hit efficiency $\epsilon_{max}$, panel dimensions, and panel position $(x_p, y_p, z_p)$ [S1].
*   Sigmoid smoothness parameter controlling the transition width at panel edges [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Reconstructed hit position $(x_{rec}, y_{rec}, z_j)$ [S1].
*   Effective hit efficiency $\epsilon_{eff}$ [S1].
*   Effective spatial resolutions $\sigma_{x,eff}, \sigma_{y,eff}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   The panel model evaluates a weight function $w(u)$ for a coordinate $u$ (where $u$ is either $x$ or $y$ relative to the panel center) [S1].
*   The weight function $w(u)$ is a double-sigmoid function that maps the normalized distance from the panel center to a value in $[0, 1]$ [S1].
*   The specific mathematical form of the double-sigmoid is not explicitly defined in the sources, but it is described as a pair of functions in $x$ and $y$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Propagation:** For each muon and each detector panel $j$ at depth $z_j$, propagate the muon to determine the true intersection point $(x_{true}, y_{true})$ on the panel plane [S1].
2.  **Weight Evaluation:** Compute the panel model weights $w(x_{true})$ and $w(y_{true})$ based on the true hit position relative to the panel center [S1].
3.  **Resolution Calculation:** Compute the effective spatial resolutions for the x and y axes using the maximum resolution $\sigma_{max}$ and the respective weights:
    $$ \sigma_{x,eff} = \frac{\sigma_{max}}{w(x_{true})} $$
    $$ \sigma_{y,eff} = \frac{\sigma_{max}}{w(y_{true})} $$
    As $w \to 0$ (far from center), $\sigma_{eff} \to \infty$, effectively discarding the hit's positional accuracy [S1].
4.  **Hit Sampling:** Generate the reconstructed hit position by sampling from a Gaussian distribution centered at the true position with the effective resolutions:
    $$ x_{rec} = x_{true} + \delta_x $$
    $$ y_{rec} = y_{true} + \delta_y $$
    where $\delta_x, \delta_y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{x,y,eff})$ [S1].
5.  **Efficiency Calculation:** Compute the effective hit efficiency as the product of the maximum efficiency $\epsilon_{max}$ and the weights for both axes:
    $$ \epsilon_{eff} = \epsilon_{max} \cdot w(x_{true}) \cdot w(y_{true}) $$
    This value represents the probabilistic likelihood of the hit being recorded [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def double_sigmoid(u, center, half_width, smoothness):
    # [G] Standard double-sigmoid implementation: plateau in center, decay at edges
    # Returns a value in [0, 1]
    edge1 = center - half_width
    edge2 = center + half_width
    # Using a standard logistic sigmoid for the transition
    def sigmoid(x):
        return 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-smoothness * x))
    w = sigmoid(u - edge1) - sigmoid(u - edge2)
    # Normalize to [0, 1] range assuming smoothness is calibrated
    return max(0.0, min(1.0, w))

def sigmoid_panel_model(muon, panel, rng):
    """
    muon: object with method propagate_to_z(z) returning (x_true, y_true)
    panel: object with attributes sigma_max, epsilon_max, center_x, center_y, width, height, smoothness
    rng: random number generator
    """
    # Step 1: Propagate muon to panel z-position
    z_j = panel.z_position
    x_true, y_true = muon.propagate_to_z(z_j)
    
    # Step 2: Compute weights using double-sigmoid function
    w_x = double_sigmoid(x_true, panel.center_x, panel.width / 2, panel.smoothness)
    w_y = double_sigmoid(y_true, panel.center_y, panel.height / 2, panel.smoothness)
    
    # Step 3: Compute effective resolutions
    sigma_x_eff = panel.sigma_max / w_x
    sigma_y_eff = panel.sigma_max / w_y
    
    # Step 4: Sample reconstructed hit
    delta_x = rng.gauss(0, sigma_x_eff)
    delta_y = rng.gauss(0, sigma_y_eff)
    
    x_rec = x_true + delta_x
    y_rec = y_true + delta_y
    
    # Step 5: Compute hit efficiency
    epsilon_eff = panel.epsilon_max * w_x * w_y
    
    return {
        'x_rec': x_rec,
        'y_rec': y_rec,
        'z_rec': z_j,
        'epsilon_eff': epsilon_eff,
        'sigma_x_eff': sigma_x_eff,
        'sigma_y_eff': sigma_y_eff
    }
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma_{max}$: Maximum spatial resolution of the panel (e.g., in mm) [S1].
*   $\epsilon_{max}$: Maximum hit efficiency of the panel (e.g., 1.0 for 100%) [S1].
*   Sigmoid smoothness: Controls the steepness of the transition at the panel edges; higher smoothness leads to broader transitions [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Division by Zero:** If $w(x_{true})$ or $w(y_{true})$ is exactly zero, $\sigma_{eff}$ becomes infinite, meaning the hit has no positional information [S1].
*   **Inference Sensitivity:** The smoothness of the sigmoid affects optimization stability [S1]. Smoother panels make inference sensitive to muons far from the panel (affecting xy position/span optimization) [S1]. Sharper transitions make inference sensitive to z-position, as muons far from the panel receive diminished weights (efficiency/resolution drop to near zero) [S1].

## Subtopics

### Probabilistic Hit Recording with Efficiency
This variant interprets the binary nature of hit recording probabilistically [S1]. Instead of a hard "hit/no-hit" decision, every muon passing through a panel records a hit with an associated efficiency factor $\epsilon_{hit}$ [S1]. The total muon efficiency $\epsilon_\mu$ is computed as the probability of recording at least two hits (one before and one after the passive volume), which is required to reconstruct a trajectory [S1]. More hits better constrain the uncertainty on trajectory parameters, but at least two are strictly necessary [S1]. This approach allows the optimization process to account for partial detection probabilities rather than binary outcomes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of muons and detector panels [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low; requires storing panel parameters and temporary variables for each hit [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fully differentiable with respect to all panel parameters (x, y, z, width, height), enabling gradient-based optimization of detector geometry [S1]. Provides a smooth transition between detected and undetected regions, avoiding discontinuities that hinder optimization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally more expensive than binary models due to the evaluation of sigmoid functions and Gaussian sampling [S1]. The choice of smoothness parameter requires tuning; too smooth leads to noise from distant muons, too sharp leads to vanishing gradients for xy parameters [S1].
*   **When to use:** During the optimization phase of detector design or alignment where gradients are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** During validation or inference where physical realism is prioritized over differentiability; in these cases, a binary rectangle model is used [S1].

## Background
Sigmoid Panel Modeling was introduced in the TomOpt framework for differential optimization of particle detectors in muon tomography [S1]. It was developed to address the non-differentiability of standard detector models, allowing for task- and constraint-aware design of detectors [S1]. The method uses a pair of double-sigmoid functions to model spatial resolution and efficiency as continuous functions of position, enabling the use of gradient-based optimization techniques for detector geometry [S1].
