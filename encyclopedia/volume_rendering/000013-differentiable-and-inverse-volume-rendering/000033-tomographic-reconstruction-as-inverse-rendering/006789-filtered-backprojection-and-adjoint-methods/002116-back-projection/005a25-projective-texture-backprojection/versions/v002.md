# Projective Texture Backprojection

Also called: Projective Texture Method

## Overview
Projective Texture Backprojection is a GPU-based tomographic reconstruction method that simulates projecting a volume slice onto the image plane using projective textures to guide reverse texture mapping [S1, unofficial]. It computes the transformation matrix between the projection and the volume slice to be backprojected, and uses it as the texture matrix to guide the reverse texture mapping [S1, unofficial]. The method requires maintaining two volume slice stacks in GPU memory simultaneously, one for each major direction (X and Z), and merging them at the end of reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 2D projection images and a target 3D volume grid of size $N \times N \times N$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A reconstructed 3D volume density field [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Slice Stacks:** Two stacks of 2D textures (or render targets) representing slices of the volume [S1, unofficial].
    *   **X-Set Stack:** Slices aligned with the X-axis [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Z-Set Stack:** Slices aligned with the Z-axis [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* The Y-axis is the axis about which the projection rotates [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Allocate two stacks of volume slices in GPU memory, one for the X-axis and one for the Z-axis [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Projection Classification:** Divide the input projection images into two sets (X-set and Z-set) based on which axis they are more perpendicular to [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Transformation Matrix Computation:** For each projection, compute the transformation matrix between the projection plane and the volume slice to be backprojected [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Reverse Texture Mapping:** Use the computed transformation matrix as the texture matrix to guide the reverse texture mapping [S1, unofficial]. Render the projection image onto the corresponding volume slice texture [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Accumulation:** Repeat the reverse texture mapping for all projections in the X-set against the X-stack, and all projections in the Z-set against the Z-stack [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Merging:** Merge the two stacks of volume slices at the end of reconstruction to produce the final result [S1, unofficial].

            projections_x.append(proj)

    stack_z = [create_2d_texture(volume_size, volume_size) for _ in range(volume_size)]

            render_quad(proj.texture, stack_x[slice_idx])

            set_texture_matrix(T)
            render_quad(proj.texture, stack_z[slice_idx])

    # 5. Merge stacks to produce final volume
    return merge_stacks(stack_x, stack_z)

**Parameters:**
*   **Volume Resolution ($N$):** The grid size of the reconstructed volume [S1, unofficial].
*   **Number of Projections:** Must provide two orthogonal half-orbits of projections, equally spaced in angle, to satisfy Tuy’s condition for exact 3D reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

*   **Memory Usage:** The method requires two copies of the volume slices to remain in GPU memory simultaneously, occupying precious video memory [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Projective Texture Shuffling
A data transfer operation on the GPU to swap partially reconstructed volume data between viewing (V) and support (S) stacks [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Rotation:** Rotate volume proxy polygons so one slice set is perpendicular (V-stack) and the other is orthogonal (S-stack) to the viewing vector [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Classification:** Divide projections into V-set and S-set based on which stack they are more perpendicular to [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Initial Reconstruction:** Reconstruct the S-stack from projections from the S-set [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Shuffling:** Shuffle the partially reconstructed volume into the V-stack by repeatedly rendering different columns of a source texture from one stack to the same column on all target textures from the other stack [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Final Reconstruction:** Use the V-stack to fully accumulate/reconstruct the volume, then shade and composite [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Proportional to the number of projections and volume slices, as each projection is rendered onto a subset of slices [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High, requiring two full stacks of volume slices in GPU memory simultaneously [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Leverages GPU texture hardware for efficient coordinate transformation and sampling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High video memory consumption; requires merging two stacks at the end [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When GPU texture units are available and video memory is sufficient [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When video memory is extremely limited, as the method requires maintaining two full stacks [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of GPU-accelerated D2VR (Direct 3D Volume Rendering) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of projective textures [SKv*92] and was extended to cone-beam geometry [MX06]. It is related to the work by [CM03] on parallel-beam projection using texture spreading [S1, unofficial]. The method adheres to Tuy’s condition for exact 3D reconstruction [Tuy83] by requiring two orthogonal half-orbits of projections [S1, unofficial].
