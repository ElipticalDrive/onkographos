# Forward Projection
Also called: 3D → 2D Forward Projection, Central Slice Theorem Projection, Fourier Slice Projection

## Overview
Forward projection generates 2D projections from a 3D Fourier-space reconstruction by sampling central slices through the 3D volume, implementing the Central Slice Theorem [S1, unofficial]. It serves as the differentiable forward operator in tomographic reconstruction pipelines, acting as the mathematical adjoint to back-projection [S1, unofficial]. Unlike spatial-domain projection methods that integrate ray paths through density fields, this method operates directly in the frequency domain, enabling efficient gradient computation for rotation and translation parameters without explicit ray casting [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 3D reconstruction tensor in Fourier space with shape `[B, D, H, W/2+1]`, where `B` is batch size, `D` is depth, `H` is height, and `W` is width (real-valued FFT output) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Inputs:** Rotation matrices with shape `[B, P, 3, 3]`, where `P` is the number of projection poses [S1, unofficial].
*   **Inputs (Optional):** 2D shift vectors with shape `[B, P, 2]` [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** 2D projection tensor with shape `[B, P, Hout, Wout/2 + 1]` [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The 3D reconstruction is stored as a complex-valued tensor (or real-valued tensor with conjugate symmetry for the last dimension) representing the 3D Fourier transform of the volume. Coordinates are defined in frequency space $(k_r, k_c, k_d)$.

**Parameters:**
*   **Interpolation:** Trilinear or tricubic (Catmull-Rom) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Oversampling:** The output dimensions $H_{out}, W_{out}$ may differ from input $H, W$ depending on the desired resolution of the projection [S1, unofficial].

**Forward Pass Steps:**
1.  **Coordinate Extension:** For each output projection coordinate $\mathbf{k}_{proj} = (k_r, k_c)$, extend it to 3D by setting the depth coordinate to zero: $\mathbf{k}_{3D} = (k_c, k_r, 0)$. This selects the central slice through the origin [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform the 3D coordinate into the reconstruction frame using the inverse of the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$:
    $$ \mathbf{k}_{rec} = \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{k}_{3D} $$
    where $\mathbf{R}$ is the $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Interpolation:** Sample the 3D reconstruction tensor $F_{rec}$ at $\mathbf{k}_{rec}$ using trilinear or tricubic interpolation.
    *   For **trilinear interpolation**, the value $F_{rec}(\mathbf{k}_{rec})$ is computed from the 8 surrounding voxels using linear kernel $l(s) = 1 - |s|$ for $|s| \le 1$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For **tricubic interpolation**, the value is computed from the 64 surrounding voxels using the Catmull-Rom kernel $w(s)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Phase Modulation:** Apply the translation shift $\mathbf{s}$ to the interpolated value:
    $$ P(\mathbf{k}_{proj}) = F_{rec}(\mathbf{k}_{rec}) \cdot e^{-i2\pi\mathbf{k}_{proj}\cdot\mathbf{s}} $$
    where $\mathbf{s}$ is the 2D shift vector [S1, unofficial].

**Backward Pass Steps:**
1.  **Shift Gradient:** Compute the gradient with respect to the shift vector $\mathbf{s}$:
    $$ \frac{\partial P}{\partial \mathbf{s}} = -i2\pi\mathbf{k}_{proj}P(\mathbf{k}_{proj}) $$
    [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Rotation Gradient:** Compute the gradient with respect to the rotation matrix elements $R_{ij}$ using the chain rule:
    $$ \frac{\partial P}{\partial R_{ij}} = \frac{\partial F_{rec}(\mathbf{k}_{rec})}{\partial \mathbf{k}_{rec}} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{k}_{rec}}{\partial R_{ij}} $$
    The term $\frac{\partial \mathbf{k}_{rec}}{\partial R_{ij}}$ is derived from the analytical derivative of the matrix inverse $\mathbf{R}^{-1}$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Spatial Gradients:** Compute the gradient of the interpolated value with respect to the coordinates $\mathbf{k}_{rec} = (d, r, c)$.
    *   For **trilinear interpolation**, the spatial derivatives are computed using the derivatives of the linear kernel $l'(s) = \text{sign}(s)$ for $|s| < 1$:
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial d} = \sum_{i=0}^1 \sum_{j=0}^1 \sum_{k=0}^1 p_{ijk} \cdot l'(d_f - i) \cdot l(r_f - j) \cdot l(c_f - k) $$
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial r} = \sum_{i=0}^1 \sum_{j=0}^1 \sum_{k=0}^1 p_{ijk} \cdot l(d_f - i) \cdot l'(r_f - j) \cdot l(c_f - k) $$
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial c} = \sum_{i=0}^1 \sum_{j=0}^1 \sum_{k=0}^1 p_{ijk} \cdot l(d_f - i) \cdot l(r_f - j) \cdot l'(c_f - k) $$
        where $p_{ijk}$ are the sample weights and $d_f, r_f, c_f$ are the fractional coordinates [S1, unofficial].
    *   For **tricubic interpolation**, the spatial derivatives use the derivative of the cubic kernel $w'(s)$:
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial d} = \sum_{i=-1}^2 \sum_{j=-1}^2 \sum_{k=-1}^2 p_{ijk} \cdot w'(d_f - i) \cdot w(r_f - j) \cdot w(c_f - k) $$
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial r} = \sum_{i=-1}^2 \sum_{j=-1}^2 \sum_{k=-1}^2 p_{ijk} \cdot w(d_f - i) \cdot w'(r_f - j) \cdot w(c_f - k) $$
        $$ \frac{\partial F}{\partial c} = \sum_{i=-1}^2 \sum_{j=-1}^2 \sum_{k=-1}^2 p_{ijk} \cdot w(d_f - i) \cdot w(r_f - j) \cdot w'(c_f - k) $$
        [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Accumulation:** Accumulate the reconstruction gradients into the 3D volume tensor at the transformed coordinates $\mathbf{k}_{rec}$ using transposed interpolation operations [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the interpolation step, which is $O(P \cdot H_{out} \cdot W_{out})$ per batch element, where $P$ is the number of projections. The matrix inverse and coordinate transformation are $O(P)$ or $O(P \cdot H_{out} \cdot W_{out})$ depending on implementation.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(B \cdot D \cdot H \cdot W)$ for the input volume and $O(B \cdot P \cdot H_{out} \cdot W_{out})$ for the output projections. Intermediate gradients require storing the interpolation weights and coordinates.
*   **Strengths:** Efficient for differentiable tomography where the reconstruction is in Fourier space. Avoids the computational cost of ray casting. Analytical gradients for rotation and translation are available.
*   **Limitations:** Requires the reconstruction to be in Fourier space. Interpolation artifacts (aliasing, blurring) can affect accuracy, especially with tricubic interpolation if not properly handled. The method assumes a specific coordinate system and central slice geometry.
*   **When to use:** When performing differentiable tomographic reconstruction where the forward model is defined in the frequency domain.
*   **When not to use:** When the reconstruction is in spatial domain and requires spatial-domain projection (e.g., for non-Fourier-based regularizers).

## Background
The method is based on the Central Slice Theorem, which states that the 1D Fourier transform of a projection of a 2D object is a slice through the 2D Fourier transform of the object. In 3D, the 2D Fourier transform of a projection is a central slice through the 3D Fourier transform of the volume [S1, unofficial]. This operator is implemented in the `torch-projectors` library, a high-performance differentiable projection library for PyTorch [S1, unofficial]. It is used in differentiable tomographic reconstruction pipelines, such as those in [S1, unofficial].
