# Stochastic Tomography

Also called: Random walk tomography, Stochastic solver for tomography, random walk algorithm for tomographic reconstruction

## Overview
Stochastic Tomography is a matrix-free, grid-free iterative solver for tomographic reconstruction that represents the 3D volume as a set of discrete samples explored via random walks [S1]. It minimizes a convex objective function composed of squared residuals against observed projections and a convex regularizer, avoiding the fixed spatial discretization required by methods like [SIRT](3101-sirt-simultaneous-iterative-reconstruction-technique.md) or [SART](3101-sirt-simultaneous-iterative-reconstruction-technique.md) [S1]. Unlike its siblings in the [General Numerical Optimization Framework for Tomography](3251-general-numerical-optimization-framework-for-tomography.md), it does not rely on a global matrix $W$ or explicit proximal operators for the data fidelity term; instead, it uses on-the-fly kernel density estimation (splatting) to track residuals and accepts samples based on local improvements to the objective function [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $M$ camera views with observations $o_i$ for $i=1 \dots M$.
*   Camera projection functions $proj_i(\mathbf{x})$ mapping 3D coordinates to 2D image coordinates.
*   A visual hull mask $H(\mathbf{x})$ (binary volume or per-camera masks) defining the region of interest.
*   Parameters: Sample budget $N_{total}$, number of parallel walks $K$, Gaussian mutation width $\sigma$, initial emissivity step $e_d$ (optional, often adaptive), and regularizer type $\Gamma$.

**Outputs:**
*   A set of samples $\mathcal{S} = \{ (\mathbf{x}_k, e_k) \}$ representing the volume estimate, where $\mathbf{x}_k$ is the 3D position and $e_k$ is the deposited emissivity (density).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Residual Images:** $M$ 2D images $\mathbf{r}_j$ of size $W_{img} \times H_{img}$, initialized to the observed images $o_j$. These store the current squared error residuals.
*   **Sample List:** A dynamic list of accepted samples. Each entry stores $\mathbf{x}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $e_k \in \mathbb{R}$.
*   **Current Objective Value:** $F_{curr}$, the sum of squared residuals plus regularization energy.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize residual images $\mathbf{r}_j = o_j$ for all $j=1 \dots M$.
    *   Initialize total objective $F_{curr} = \sum_j \| \mathbf{r}_j \|_2^2 + \Gamma(\emptyset)$.
    *   Initialize empty sample list $\mathcal{S}$.
    *   Spawn $K$ random walks. For each walk $k$, pick a starting position $\mathbf{x}_{start}^{(k)}$ uniformly within the visual hull.

2.  **Random Walk Loop:**
    *   Repeat until the total number of mutations across all walks exceeds $N_{total}$.
    *   For each active walk $k$ with current position $\mathbf{x}_{prev}$:
        a.  **Mutation:** Generate candidate $\mathbf{x}_{cand}$ by adding Gaussian noise to $\mathbf{x}_{prev}$:
            $$ \mathbf{x}_{cand} = \mathbf{x}_{prev} + \mathbf{\epsilon}, \quad \mathbf{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I) $$
            where $\sigma$ is typically 5-10% of the diagonal of the reconstruction bounding box [S1].
        b.  **Visual Hull Check:** If $\mathbf{x}_{cand}$ is outside the visual hull (i.e., not visible in all cameras or outside the bounding volume), reject immediately and continue to next mutation [S1].
        c.  **Objective Change Calculation:** Compute $\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand})$, the change in the objective function if a sample were placed at $\mathbf{x}_{cand}$.
            *   Calculate tentative residual updates for both positive ($+e_d$) and negative ($-e_d$) emissivity.
            *   For each camera $j$, project $\mathbf{x}_{cand}$ to 2D: $\mathbf{u}_j = proj_j(\mathbf{x}_{cand})$.
            *   Compute the change in squared residual at the projected pixel location. Let $r_j(\mathbf{u})$ be the value in residual image $\mathbf{r}_j$ at pixel $\mathbf{u}$.
            *   The change in data fidelity for camera $j$ with emissivity sign $s \in \{+1, -1\}$ is:
                $$ \Delta_j^s F = r_j(\mathbf{u}_j)^2 - (r_j(\mathbf{u}_j) - s \cdot e_d \cdot K(\mathbf{u}_j))^2 $$
                where $K(\mathbf{u}_j)$ is the kernel value (e.g., Gaussian or box filter) at the center of the splat. For simplicity, if using a delta-like splat or assuming the residual is updated by subtracting the contribution, the formula simplifies to checking the reduction in squared error. The source defines:
                $$ \tilde{\mathbf{r}}_j^\pm = splat(\mathbf{r}_j, proj_j(\mathbf{x}_{cand}), \pm e_d) $$
                $$ \Delta_j^\pm F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) = \mathbf{r}_j(proj_j(\mathbf{x}_{cand}))^2 - \tilde{\mathbf{r}}_j^\pm(proj_j(\mathbf{x}_{cand}))^2 $$
                $$ \Delta^\pm F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) = \sum_{j=1}^M \Delta_j^\pm F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) $$
            *   Select the best sign: $\Delta_{data} F = \max(\Delta^+ F, \Delta^- F)$.
            *   Calculate regularization change $\Delta_{reg} F$. This depends on the specific regularizer (see [Stochastic Tomography Regularization](#stochastic-tomography-regularization)).
            *   Total change: $\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) = \Delta_{data} F + \Delta_{reg} F$.
        d.  **Acceptance:**
            *   If $\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) \le 0$, the sample does not improve the objective. Reject: $\mathbf{x}_{prev} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{cand}$ (continue walk from new location, but do not record sample) [S1].
            *   If $\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand}) > 0$:
                *   Calculate acceptance probability $a$. Since the transition PDF is symmetric ($t(\mathbf{x}_{cand}|\mathbf{x}_{prev}) = t(\mathbf{x}_{prev}|\mathbf{x}_{cand})$), the Metropolis-Hastings ratio simplifies. However, the source notes this is not strictly MCMC because the target PDF is unknown and $\Delta F$ depends on history. The algorithm uses a simplified acceptance rule derived from the ratio of improvements:
                    $$ a = \min\left(1, \frac{\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand})}{\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{prev})}\right) $$
                    *Note: The source pseudocode uses `random() <= a` where `a` is the ratio. If $\Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{prev})$ is not defined for the first step, it is treated as infinite or the sample is accepted if $\Delta F > 0$.*
                *   If `random() <= a`:
                    *   Accept sample: Add $(\mathbf{x}_{cand}, s \cdot e_d)$ to $\mathcal{S}$, where $s$ is the sign that yielded $\Delta_{data} F$.
                    *   Update residual images: $\mathbf{r}_j \leftarrow splat(\mathbf{r}_j, proj_j(\mathbf{x}_{cand}), -s \cdot e_d)$ for all $j$. (Subtract the contribution from the residual).
                    *   Update $F_{curr} \leftarrow F_{curr} + \Delta F(\mathbf{x}_{cand})$.
                    *   $\mathbf{x}_{prev} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{cand}$.
                *   Else:
                    *   Reject sample.
                    *   $\mathbf{x}_{prev} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{cand}$ (continue walk from new location).

3.  **Post-processing:**
    *   The final volume is represented by the set $\mathcal{S}$. Rendering is performed by splatting these samples into images.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import random
import math

def stochastic_tomography(observations, cameras, visual_hull, N_total, K_walks, sigma, e_d, regularizer):
    """
    observations: list of M 2D arrays (images)
    cameras: list of M projection functions proj(x) -> (u, v)
    visual_hull: function is_inside(x) -> bool
    N_total: total number of mutations allowed
    K_walks: number of parallel random walks
    sigma: Gaussian mutation width
    e_d: emissivity step size
    regularizer: object with method delta_energy(samples, x_new)
    """
    M = len(observations)
    # Initialize residual images with observations
    residuals = [obs.copy() for obs in observations]
    
    # Current objective value (sum of squared residuals)
    # Note: Regularizer energy of empty set is 0
    current_F = sum(sum(r**2 for r in row) for r in residuals)
    
    samples = [] # List of (x, emissivity)
    
    # Initialize K walks with random positions inside visual hull
    # Helper to get random point in hull
    current_positions = [get_random_point_in_hull() for _ in range(K_walks)]
    
    mutations_count = 0
    
    while mutations_count < N_total:
        for k in range(K_walks):
            if mutations_count >= N_total:
                break
                
            x_prev = current_positions[k]
            
            # 1. Mutation
            # Add Gaussian noise to each coordinate
            dx = random.gauss(0, sigma)
            dy = random.gauss(0, sigma)
            dz = random.gauss(0, sigma)
            x_cand = (x_prev[0] + dx, x_prev[1] + dy, x_prev[2] + dz)
            
            # 2. Visual Hull Check
            if not visual_hull.is_inside(x_cand):
                current_positions[k] = x_cand
                mutations_count += 1
                continue
            
            # 3. Calculate Delta F
            # Calculate data fidelity change for positive and negative emissivity
            delta_data_pos = 0.0
            delta_data_neg = 0.0
            
            for j in range(M):
                # Project to 2D
                u, v = cameras[j].project(x_cand)
                
                # Get current residual value at this pixel
                # Assume bilinear interpolation or nearest neighbor for residual access
                r_val = residuals[j].get_value(u, v)
                
                # Tentative new residual if we add +e_d
                # The residual is o - v_proj. If we add emissivity e_d to v_proj,
                # the new residual is r_val - e_d.
                # Change in squared residual: r^2 - (r - e_d)^2 = 2*r*e_d - e_d^2
                # Source uses splatting which might blur, but for single pixel update:
                # delta = r^2 - (r - e_d)^2
                delta_pos_j = r_val**2 - (r_val - e_d)**2
                delta_neg_j = r_val**2 - (r_val + e_d)**2
                
                delta_data_pos += delta_pos_j
                delta_data_neg += delta_neg_j
            
            # Select best sign
            if delta_data_pos > delta_data_neg:
                best_sign = 1
                delta_data = delta_data_pos
            else:
                best_sign = -1
                delta_data = delta_data_neg
            
            # Calculate regularization change
            # Regularizer is evaluated on stencils around x_cand
            delta_reg = regularizer.delta_energy(samples, x_cand)
            
            delta_F = delta_data + delta_reg
            
            # 4. Acceptance
            # Only accept if it reduces the objective (delta_F > 0 means reduction in error)
            # Note: Source defines delta_F as reduction. If delta_F <= 0, no improvement.
            if delta_F <= 0:
                current_positions[k] = x_cand
                mutations_count += 1
                continue
            
            # Acceptance probability
            # If first step or previous delta_F was not stored/defined, accept if delta_F > 0
            # The source pseudocode: a = delta_F(x_k) / delta_F(x_{k-1})
            # We need to track the delta_F of the *previous accepted step* or the *current walk step*?
            # The source says: "a = Delta F(x_k) / Delta F(x_{k-1})". 
            # In the context of the loop, x_{k-1} is the previous sample in the walk.
            # However, the walk always moves to x_cand. The "previous" delta_F refers to the 
            # improvement achieved by the step that led to x_prev? 
            # The source pseudocode implies we compare the current improvement to the previous improvement.
            # If we don't have a previous improvement (start of walk), we accept.
            
            # To implement strictly from source:
            # We need to store the delta_F of the last step taken in this walk.
            # Let's assume we track last_delta_F for each walk.
            
            # For simplicity in this self-contained code, if we can't retrieve last_delta_F,
            # we treat it as infinity (acceptance prob 1) or use a heuristic.
            # The source says: "if Delta F(x_{k-1}) < 0 or random() <= a".
            # This implies if the previous step was bad (which shouldn't happen if we only record good steps?),
            # we accept? No, the walk continues regardless.
            
            # Let's assume we store the delta_F of the transition x_prev -> x_cand? 
            # No, x_prev was accepted. We need the delta_F of the transition that created x_prev.
            
            # Simplified logic matching source structure:
            # a = min(1, delta_F / last_delta_F)
            # If last_delta_F is None (start), a = 1.
            
            # Note: The source pseudocode has `if Delta F(x_{k-1}) < 0`. 
            # This likely refers to the case where the previous step didn't improve the objective 
            # in a standard MCMC sense, but here we only move if we improve? 
            # Actually, the walk moves to x_cand even if rejected. 
            # The variable `Delta F(x_{k-1})` in the ratio likely refers to the improvement 
            # of the step that landed on x_{k-1}.
            
            # Implementation detail: Store `walk_improvements[k]`
            
            # ... (Assuming walk_improvements is maintained) ...
            
            if k in walk_improvements and walk_improvements[k] > 0:
                a = min(1.0, delta_F / walk_improvements[k])
            else:
                a = 1.0
            
            if random.random() <= a:
                # Accept
                samples.append((x_cand, best_sign * e_d))
                
                # Update residuals: subtract the contribution
                for j in range(M):
                    u, v = cameras[j].project(x_cand)
                    # Splat negative emissivity to reduce residual
                    residuals[j].splat(u, v, -best_sign * e_d)
                
                current_F += delta_F
                walk_improvements[k] = delta_F
                current_positions[k] = x_cand
            else:
                # Reject
                current_positions[k] = x_cand
                # Do not update walk_improvements? Or keep old? 
                # The walk continues from x_cand. The next step will compare to this one?
                # The source doesn't specify updating the denominator on rejection.
                # It implies the denominator is the improvement of the *previous accepted sample's creation*.
                # So we do NOT update walk_improvements on rejection.
            
            mutations_count += 1
            
    return samples

    # Placeholder for sampling uniformly within the visual hull bounding box
    # and checking visibility.
    return (random.random(), random.random(), random.random())
```

## Subtopics

### Stochastic Tomography Regularization
This variant extends the core algorithm by incorporating convex regularizers $\Gamma(\mathbf{v})$ into the objective function. It distinguishes itself by evaluating the regularizer change $\Delta \Gamma$ on local stencils centered at the candidate sample $\mathbf{x}_k$, allowing for efficient per-sample updates without global recomputation [S1]. It supports 3D grid-based regularizers (L2, TV, SAD) and 2D image-space regularizers. Image-space regularization applies 2D variants of 3D regularizers directly to the rendered output images, biasing the solution to satisfy measurements while producing smooth outputs, which is superior to post-processing smoothing that breaks measurement consistency [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of samples and cameras per mutation. Each mutation requires projecting the sample to $M$ cameras and updating $M$ residual images. Total time is proportional to $N_{total} \times M$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(M \cdot W_{img} \cdot H_{img} + N_{samples})$. Stores $M$ residual images and the list of accepted samples. No large projection matrix $W$ is stored.
*   **Measured Performance:** On a single core of an Intel i7 desktop, 200 million mutations took 14 minutes per frame, and 400 million mutations took 31 minutes per frame [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Grid-free representation avoids fixed resolution tradeoffs; easy to swap regularizers without re-implementing the solver; handles sparse views well; allows positive and negative emissivity for rough-in and refinement phases [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive for high sample budgets; sensitive to camera misalignment (limits resolution); reflections in capture setup can introduce artifacts if not separated from volume data [S1].
*   **When to use:** When grid-free reconstruction is desired, when regularizer flexibility is needed, or when dealing with sparse views.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and sample budgets must be low; when camera alignment is poor.

## Background
Stochastic Tomography was introduced in the paper "Stochastic tomography and its applications in 3D imaging of mixing fluids" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of spatial discretization in traditional CT methods like SIRT and SART, which require fixed grids and complex re-implementation for different regularizers. The method was applied to 3D imaging of mixing fluids, including fluorescein-sodium dye in water, alcohol-fluorescein-sodium mixtures, and dyepowder [S1].
