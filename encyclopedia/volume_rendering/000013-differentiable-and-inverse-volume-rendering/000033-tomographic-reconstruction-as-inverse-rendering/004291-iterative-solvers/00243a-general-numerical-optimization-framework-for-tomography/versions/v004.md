# General Numerical Optimization Framework for Tomography

Also called: Regularized Inverse Problem Solver for Tomography, Regularized Optimization for Cryo-ET, Tomographic Reconstruction as Optimization, Variational Tomography Framework

## Overview
This framework formulates tomographic reconstruction as a regularized inverse problem, minimizing a composite objective function consisting of a data fidelity term and a regularization term [S1][S2, unofficial]. It solves the ill-posed nature of tomography (e.g., from noisy, angle-limited, or sparse projections) by decomposing the optimization into modular proximal operators [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished from direct algebraic methods like [SIRT](3101-sirt-simultaneous-iterative-reconstruction-technique.md) and [ART](6944-algebraic-reconstruction-technique-art.md) by its ability to incorporate complex, non-smooth regularizers (such as Total Variation) and its reliance on first-order primal-dual or alternating direction methods rather than direct matrix inversion or simple gradient descent [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The framework addresses the linear inverse problem defined by the discretized image formation model. In log space, the relationship between projections and the 3D volume is linear:
$$
W \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{p}
$$
where $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the unknown volume, $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^M$ is the set of known ray integrals (tilt-series or sinogram), and $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ is the weight matrix representing the contribution of each voxel to each ray integral [S1].

The reconstruction is formulated as the following optimization problem:
$$
\arg \min_{\mathbf{v}} \frac{1}{2} \| W \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{p} \|_2^2 + \Gamma(\mathbf{v})
$$
where the first term is the data fidelity (least-squares error) and the second term $\Gamma(\mathbf{v})$ is a non-linear regularizer [S1].

To apply efficient proximal algorithms, the problem is rewritten in a composite convex form:
$$
\min_{\mathbf{f}} f(\mathbf{f}) + g(\mathbf{Kf})
$$
where:
*   $f(\mathbf{f}) = \frac{1}{2} \| W \mathbf{f} - \mathbf{p} \|_2^2$ is the data fidelity term [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   $g(\cdot)$ is the loss function associated with the regularizer [S2, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{K}$ is a linear operator (e.g., the gradient operator for Total Variation) that transforms the volume into a sparse domain [S1][S2, unofficial].

The optimization is solved by decomposing it into independent proximal operators. The proximal operator for a function $h$ and parameter $\zeta$ is defined as:
$$
\text{prox}_{\zeta h}(\mathbf{q}) \triangleq \operatorname*{argmin}_{\mathbf{f}} h(\mathbf{f}) + \frac{1}{2\zeta} \| \mathbf{f} - \mathbf{q} \|_2^2
$$
where $\mathbf{q}$ is the input vector [S1][S2, unofficial].

The framework requires two specific proximal operators:
1.  **Data Fidelity Proximal Operator**: $\text{prox}_{\tau f}(\mathbf{q})$. This solves the least-squares problem involving the forward projection matrix $W$. Due to the large size and sparsity of $W$, this is typically solved using a matrix-free iterative solver such as [SIRT](3101-sirt-simultaneous-iterative-reconstruction-technique.md), which yields better results than Conjugate Gradients for sparse projection matrices [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Regularization Proximal Operator**: $\text{prox}_{\mu g}(\mathbf{q})$. This handles the non-smooth regularizer (e.g., soft-thresholding for L1 norms in Total Variation) [S1][S2, unofficial].

For algorithms using the convex conjugate $g^*$, Moreau's Decomposition is used to compute the proximal operator of the conjugate:
$$
\text{prox}_{\mu g^*}(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{u} - \mu \, \text{prox}_{\frac{1}{\mu}g}\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}}{\mu}\right)
$$
A scaling property is also utilized:
$$
\text{prox}_{\mu g^*}(\mathbf{u}) = \sigma \, \text{prox}_{(\mu/\sigma)g^*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}}{\sigma}\right)
$$
This allows the dual step to be computed using the proximal operator of the original regularization function $g$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Chambolle-Pock Algorithm](3282-chambolle-pock-algorithm.md)
A first-order primal-dual optimization method. It is distinguished by its deterministic nature and specific reliance on Moreau decomposition to handle non-smooth regularization terms efficiently within a primal-dual framework.

### [Coupled Density and Velocity Estimation](4622-coupled-density-and-velocity-estimation.md)
A strategy for fluid reconstruction that couples density and velocity updates within a physics-based model (transport and incompressibility constraints). It differs by applying the optimization to physical priors rather than generic regularizers.

### [Stochastic Tomography](8886-stochastic-tomography.md)
A matrix-free, grid-free solver that represents the volume as discrete samples. It avoids spatial discretization artifacts and allows easy replacement of regularizers by treating proximal operators via a stochastic random walk.

### Generative Tomography Reconstruction via Optimization
This variant solves the linear inverse problem $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$ by optimizing over a latent variable $\mathbf{z}$ of a generative model $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{z})$, rather than directly over the image $\mathbf{x}$ [S3, unofficial]. It uses a weighted data fidelity term to account for non-uniform confidence in estimated sinogram values and a generator prior [S3, unofficial]. The optimization objective is:
$$
\arg\min_{\mathbf{z} \in S^m} \| A\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{z}) - \boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathbf{r}) \|_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})}^2 + \lambda D(\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{z}))
$$
where $\mathbf{z}$ lies on an $m+1$ dimensional sphere, $\boldsymbol{\mu}(\mathbf{r})$ is the estimated sinogram, and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})$ represents the confidence weights [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Tomographic reconstruction is formulated as a regularized optimization problem to handle noisy and angle-limited tilt-series, particularly in cryogenic electron tomography (Cryo-ET) [S1]. The image formation model is derived from the Beer-Lambert Law, where the measured intensity is converted to log space to linearize the relationship between projections and volume density [S1].

The general numerical optimization framework was introduced as a core component for various inverse problem applications in tomography, including 3D super-resolution, sparse view tomography, space-time reconstruction of deforming objects, and dynamic fluid capture [S2, unofficial]. It allows novel regularizers to be incorporated flexibly depending on the application [S2, unofficial]. The use of proximal algorithms provides a modular nature, splitting optimization problems into smaller sub-problems (proximal operators) that can be solved efficiently [S1].
