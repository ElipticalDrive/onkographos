# Coupled Density and Velocity Estimation

Also called: Coupled Estimation, Coupled Estimation Strategy, Physics-Based Fluid Reconstruction

## Overview
Coupled Density and Velocity Estimation is a physics-based strategy for solving the ill-posed tomography problem in fluid reconstruction when the number of cameras (projections) is reduced [S1][S2, unofficial]. It addresses the instability of standard tomographic reconstruction by coupling the estimation of density and velocity fields, using physical priors such as transport equations and incompressibility to constrain the solution [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike the original method by Eckert et al. which required 50–60 minutes per frame, this simplified strategy is highly efficient, enabling interactive reconstruction [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds on the [General Numerical Optimization Framework for Tomography](3251-general-numerical-optimization-framework-for-tomography.md) by applying proximal operators to solve the coupled optimization problem [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates on a sequence of time steps $t$. It predicts the density field $\tilde{\rho}_t$ for the current time step $t$ based on the reconstructed states from time steps $t-1$ and $t-2$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. The process involves two main phases: Velocity Reconstruction and Density Prediction/Advection [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   $\rho_{t-1}$: Reconstructed density field at time $t-1$.
    *   $\rho_{t-2}$: Reconstructed density field at time $t-2$.
    *   $\Delta t$: Time step duration.
    *   $\alpha, \beta$: Regularization weights for smoothness and kinetic energy [S2, unofficial].
    *   $\sigma, \gamma, \theta$: Convergence parameters for the primal-dual solver [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs**:
    *   $\tilde{\rho}_t$: Predicted density field at time $t$.
    *   $\tilde{u}_t$: Predicted velocity field at time $t$ (used for the next step).

### Data Structures
*   **Density Field**: A 3D grid $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ representing density values at voxel positions $v_p$.
*   **Velocity Field**: A 3D vector field $u \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ representing velocity vectors at voxel positions.
*   **Gradient Field**: $\nabla \rho$, computed via finite differences.

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Velocity Reconstruction**: Estimate the velocity field $u_{t-1}$ from $\rho_{t-1}$ and $\rho_{t-2}$ by solving the optimization problem defined in Equation (11) subject to Equation (12) [S1][S2, unofficial]. This is done using a fast primal-dual method [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Velocity Prediction**: Advect the reconstructed velocity $u_{t-1}$ with itself to create a velocity guess $\tilde{u}_t$. Project this guess onto the space of divergence-free velocities $\Pi_{DIV}$ to ensure incompressibility [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Density Prediction**: Advect the density field $\rho_{t-1}$ forward using the predicted velocity guess $\tilde{u}_t$ (or $\tilde{u}_{t-1}$ as per Algorithm 4 lines 3-4 in [S1], though [S2, unofficial] implies using the predicted velocity for the current step) to create the density guess $\tilde{\rho}_t$ [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Velocity Reconstruction Optimization
The velocity $u_{t-1}$ is estimated by minimizing the objective function $f(u_{t-1})$ subject to the incompressibility constraint $\nabla \cdot u_{t-1} = 0$ [S2, unofficial]:

$$
\min_{u_{t-1}} f(u_{t-1}) = \left\| \frac{\rho_{t-1} - \rho_{t-2}}{\Delta t} + u_{t-1} \cdot \nabla \rho_{t-1} \right\|^2 + E_{smooth}(u_{t-1}) + E_{kinetic}(u_{t-1})
$$

where:
*   The first term is the optical flow constraint derived from $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho u) = 0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $E_{smooth}(u_{t-1})$ is a smoothness regularizer (Tikhonov) [S2, unofficial].
*   $E_{kinetic}(u_{t-1})$ is a kinetic energy regularizer (Tikhonov) [S2, unofficial].
*   The relative weights $\alpha$ and $\beta$ balance the smoothing and kinetic energy constraints [S2, unofficial].

Note: Depth regularization is explicitly **not** applied because it breaks the sparse property of the matrix, making the optimization inefficient [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Primal-Dual Solver
The optimization problem is solved using the fast primal-dual method (PD) [S2, unofficial]. The problem is split into two components solved by proximal operators [S2, unofficial]. The iterative updates are:

$$
X_{k+1} = X_k + \sigma Y_k - \sigma \text{prox}_{f, \sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma} X_k + Y_k\right)
$$
$$
u_{k+1}^{t-1} = \text{prox}_{g, \frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(u_{k+1}^{t-1} - \gamma X_{k+1}\right)
$$
$$
Y_{k+1} = u_{k+1}^{t-1} + \theta \left(u_{k+1}^{t-1} - u_k^{t-1}\right)
$$

where:
*   $k$ is the iteration number [S2, unofficial].
*   $X, Y$ are helper variables [S2, unofficial].
*   $\sigma, \gamma, \theta$ are convergence parameters [S2, unofficial].
*   $\text{prox}_{f, \sigma}(\xi) = \arg \min_{u_{t-1}} f(u_{t-1}) + \frac{\sigma}{2} \| u_{t-1} - \xi \|^2$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\text{prox}_{g, \frac{1}{\gamma}}$ handles the constraint/proximal step for the regularization [S2, unofficial].

### Advection and Projection
*   **Advection**: The velocity field is advected using a semi-Lagrangian or similar advection scheme. The density $\rho_{t-1}$ is advected by the velocity field to predict $\tilde{\rho}_t$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Projection**: To ensure incompressibility, the predicted velocity $\tilde{u}_t$ is projected onto the divergence-free space $\Pi_{DIV}$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. This typically involves solving a Poisson equation for pressure and subtracting the gradient of the pressure from the velocity field.

### Pseudocode

```python
def coupled_density_velocity_estimation(rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, alpha, beta, sigma, gamma, theta, num_iterations):
    """
    Performs coupled density and velocity estimation for time step t.
    
    Args:
        rho_t_minus_1: Density field at t-1 (3D array).
        rho_t_minus_2: Density field at t-2 (3D array).
        dt: Time step duration.
        alpha: Weight for smoothness regularization.
        beta: Weight for kinetic energy regularization.
        sigma, gamma, theta: Primal-dual solver parameters.
        num_iterations: Number of PD solver iterations.
        
    Returns:
        rho_t: Predicted density field at t.
        u_t: Predicted velocity field at t.
    """
    
    # Step 1: Velocity Reconstruction
    # Solve for u_{t-1} using Primal-Dual method
    u_t_minus_1 = velocity_reconstruction_primal_dual(
        rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, alpha, beta, 
        sigma, gamma, theta, num_iterations
    )
    
    # Step 2: Velocity Prediction
    # Advect velocity with itself
    u_t_raw = advect_velocity(u_t_minus_1, u_t_minus_1, dt)
    
    # Project onto divergence-free space
    u_t = project_divergence_free(u_t_raw)
    
    # Step 3: Density Prediction
    # Advect density with predicted velocity
    rho_t = advect_density(rho_t_minus_1, u_t, dt)
    
    return rho_t, u_t

def velocity_reconstruction_primal_dual(rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, alpha, beta, sigma, gamma, theta, num_iterations):
    """
    Solves the velocity reconstruction optimization problem using Primal-Dual method.
    """
    # Initialize variables
    u = zeros_like(rho_t_minus_1) # Velocity field
    X = zeros_like(rho_t_minus_1) # Helper variable X
    Y = zeros_like(rho_t_minus_1) # Helper variable Y
    
    # Precompute gradient of rho_{t-1}
    grad_rho = compute_gradient(rho_t_minus_1)
    
    for k in range(num_iterations):
        # Compute the data fidelity term gradient part for prox_f
        # f(u) = || (rho_{t-1} - rho_{t-2})/dt + u . grad_rho ||^2 + alpha * E_smooth(u) + beta * E_kinetic(u)
        # The proximal operator prox_{f, sigma} handles the minimization of f(u) + sigma/2 ||u - xi||^2
        
        # Compute intermediate variable for prox_f
        xi = (1/sigma) * X + Y
        
        # Compute prox_f(xi)
        # This involves solving: min_u || (rho_{t-1} - rho_{t-2})/dt + u . grad_rho ||^2 + alpha * E_smooth(u) + beta * E_kinetic(u) + sigma/2 ||u - xi||^2
        # This is a regularized least squares problem.
        u_prox = solve_regularized_least_squares(
            rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, grad_rho, alpha, beta, sigma, xi
        )
        
        # Update X
        X = X + sigma * Y - sigma * u_prox
        
        # Update u (prox_g step)
        # g handles the incompressibility constraint or other proximal terms. 
        # In the context of the paper, g is associated with the regularizer/constraint.
        # The update is u = prox_{g, 1/gamma}(u - gamma * X)
        u = proximal_operator_g(u - gamma * X, gamma)
        
        # Update Y
        Y = u + theta * (u - Y) # Note: Y_{k+1} = u_{k+1} + theta(u_{k+1} - u_k)
        
    return u

def solve_regularized_least_squares(rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, grad_rho, alpha, beta, sigma, xi):
    """
    Solves the subproblem for prox_f.
    min_u || (rho_{t-1} - rho_{t-2})/dt + u . grad_rho ||^2 + alpha * E_smooth(u) + beta * E_kinetic(u) + sigma/2 ||u - xi||^2
    """
    # This is a quadratic optimization problem.
    # Let R = (rho_{t-1} - rho_{t-2})/dt
    # Let G = grad_rho
    # The term is || R + u . G ||^2.
    # E_smooth(u) is typically || grad u ||^2.
    # E_kinetic(u) is typically || u ||^2.
    # The problem can be solved using conjugate gradients or a direct solver if small.
    # For implementation, one would set up the linear system A u = b.
    
    # Placeholder for the actual linear system setup and solve.
    # A = (G^T G) + alpha * Laplacian + beta * I + sigma * I
    # b = -G^T R + sigma * xi
    # u = solve(A, b)
    
    # Since the exact form of E_smooth and E_kinetic is not fully detailed in the notes,
    # we assume standard Tikhonov regularization.
    
    # Compute residual
    residual = (rho_t_minus_1 - rho_t_minus_2) / dt
    
    # Compute gradient of the data fidelity term w.r.t u
    # d/du || R + u . G ||^2 = 2 * G * (R + u . G) (simplified)
    
    # Use an iterative solver like Conjugate Gradients to find u
    u = conjugate_gradients_solve(
        lambda u: compute_residual_and_gradient(u, residual, grad_rho, alpha, beta, sigma, xi),
        initial_guess=xi,
        tolerance=1e-6
    )
    
    return u

def compute_residual_and_gradient(u, residual, grad_rho, alpha, beta, sigma, xi):
    """
    Computes the residual and gradient for the least squares problem.
    """
    # Data fidelity term: || residual + u . grad_rho ||^2
    # Gradient: 2 * grad_rho * (residual + u . grad_rho)
    
    # Smoothness term: alpha * || grad u ||^2
    # Gradient: -2 * alpha * Laplacian(u)
    
    # Kinetic term: beta * || u ||^2
    # Gradient: 2 * beta * u
    
    # Proximal term: sigma/2 || u - xi ||^2
    # Gradient: sigma * (u - xi)
    
    # Combine gradients
    grad_data = 2 * grad_rho * (residual + u * grad_rho) # Element-wise multiplication for dot product approximation
    grad_smooth = -2 * alpha * laplacian(u)
    grad_kinetic = 2 * beta * u
    grad_prox = sigma * (u - xi)
    
    gradient = grad_data + grad_smooth + grad_kinetic + grad_prox
    
    # Residual for CG
    residual_val = residual + u * grad_rho
    
    return residual_val, gradient

def project_divergence_free(u):
    """
    Projects velocity field u onto the divergence-free space.
    Solves Poisson equation: Laplacian(p) = div(u)
    u_new = u - grad(p)
    """
    div_u = divergence(u)
    p = solve_poisson(div_u)
    grad_p = compute_gradient(p)
    u_new = u - grad_p
    return u_new

def advect_velocity(u, u_source, dt):
    """
    Advects velocity field u_source by u over time dt.
    """
    # Semi-Lagrangian advection
    # For each voxel, trace back along the velocity field
    # Interpolate the value from u_source
    u_new = zeros_like(u_source)
    for voxel in voxels:
        pos = voxel.position
        vel = u[voxel] * dt
        back_pos = pos - vel
        u_new[voxel] = interpolate(u_source, back_pos)
    return u_new

def advect_density(rho, u, dt):
    """
    Advects density field rho by velocity u over time dt.
    """
    rho_new = zeros_like(rho)
    for voxel in voxels:
        pos = voxel.position
        vel = u[voxel] * dt
        back_pos = pos - vel
        rho_new[voxel] = interpolate(rho, back_pos)
    return rho_new
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The method is significantly faster than the original coupled estimation by Eckert et al., which took 50–60 minutes per frame [S1][S2, unofficial]. The simplified strategy enables interactive reconstruction [S1][S2, unofficial]. The complexity is dominated by the primal-dual solver iterations and the advection/projection steps [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storage for density and velocity fields at multiple time steps, as well as intermediate variables for the primal-dual solver [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**:
    *   Handles ill-posed tomography problems with few cameras [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Produces realistic and smooth fluid flow reconstructions [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Interactive speed [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**:
    *   Does not apply depth regularization, which may limit control over motion in the depth direction [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Relies on the accuracy of the physical priors (incompressibility, transport) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use**: When reconstructing fluid flows from limited camera views (few projections) and interactive speed is required [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use**: When depth regularization is critical and the computational cost of breaking sparsity is acceptable [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method was developed to address the limitations of standard tomographic reconstruction in fluid modeling, particularly when the number of cameras is reduced [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds upon the coupled density and velocity updating strategy introduced by Eckert et al. [4], but simplifies it for efficiency [S1][S2, unofficial]. The velocity reconstruction uses the fast primal-dual method introduced by Chambolle and Pock [35] [S2, unofficial]. The approach is part of a physics-based differentiable rendering framework for fluid modeling [S1][S2, unofficial].
