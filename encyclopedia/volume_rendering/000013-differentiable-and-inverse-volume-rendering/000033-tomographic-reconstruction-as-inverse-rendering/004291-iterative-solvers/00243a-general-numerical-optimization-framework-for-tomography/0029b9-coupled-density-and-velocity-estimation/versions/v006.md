# Coupled Density and Velocity Estimation

Also called: Coupled Estimation, Coupled Estimation Strategy, Physics-Based Fluid Reconstruction

## Overview
Coupled Density and Velocity Estimation is a physics-based strategy for reconstructing fluid flow fields from sparse multi-view video data, designed to solve the ill-posed tomography problem when the number of cameras is reduced [S1][S2, unofficial]. It differs from standard tomographic reconstruction by coupling the estimation of density and velocity fields through physical priors (transport and incompressibility), enabling efficient interactive reconstruction where previous methods (e.g., Eckert et al.) were too slow (50–60 min per frame) [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds on [General Numerical Optimization Framework for Tomography](3251-general-numerical-optimization-framework-for-tomography.md) by applying specific proximal operators to a coupled optimization problem [G].

## How it works
The method operates on a sequence of time steps $t$ [G]. It predicts the density field $\tilde{\rho}_t$ at time $t$ based on the reconstructed states at $t-1$ and $t-2$ [S1]. The core innovation is the **Velocity Reconstruction** step, which estimates the velocity field $\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$ from the density difference between $t-1$ and $t-2$, followed by an advection step to predict $\tilde{\rho}_t$ [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   $\rho_{t-1}$: Reconstructed density field at time $t-1$.
    *   $\rho_{t-2}$: Reconstructed density field at time $t-2$.
    *   $\Delta t$: Time step duration.
    *   Parameters: $\alpha, \beta$ (regularization weights), $\sigma, \gamma, \theta$ (primal-dual convergence parameters).
*   **Outputs**:
    *   $\tilde{\rho}_t$: Predicted density field at time $t$.
    *   $\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$: Estimated velocity field at time $t-1$.

### Data Structures
*   **Density Field**: A 3D grid $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ representing scalar density values [G].
*   **Velocity Field**: A 3D grid $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ representing vector velocity components $(u_x, u_y, u_z)$ [G].
*   **Gradient Fields**: $\nabla \rho$ computed via finite differences [G].

### Optimization Problem (Velocity Reconstruction)
The velocity $\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$ is estimated by minimizing the following objective function subject to incompressibility [S2, unofficial]:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{u}_{t-1}} f(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}) = \left\| \frac{\rho_{t-1} - \rho_{t-2}}{\Delta t} + \mathbf{u}_{t-1} \cdot \nabla \rho_{t-1} \right\|^2 + E_{smooth}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}) + E_{kinetic}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1})
$$

Subject to:
$$
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}_{t-1} = 0
$$

Where:
*   The first term enforces the optical flow constraint (continuity equation linearized): $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) \approx 0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $E_{smooth}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1})$ is a smoothness regularizer (e.g., Tikhonov regularization on gradients of $\mathbf{u}$) [S2, unofficial].
*   $E_{kinetic}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1})$ is a kinetic energy regularizer (penalizing large velocities) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Note**: Depth regularization is explicitly **not** applied because it breaks the sparsity of the matrix, making the optimization inefficient [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Primal-Dual Solver
The problem is solved using a fast primal-dual algorithm [S2, unofficial]. The optimization is split into two components solved by proximal operators [S2, unofficial]. The iterative updates for iteration $k$ are [S2, unofficial]:

$$
\mathbf{X}_{k+1} = \mathbf{X}_k + \sigma \mathbf{Y}_k - \sigma \, \text{prox}_{f, \sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma} \mathbf{X}_k + \mathbf{Y}_k\right)
$$

$$
\mathbf{u}_{k+1}^{t-1} = \text{prox}_{g, \frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{k+1}^{t-1} - \gamma \mathbf{X}_{k+1}\right)
$$

$$
\mathbf{Y}_{k+1} = \mathbf{u}_{k+1}^{t-1} + \theta \left(\mathbf{u}_{k+1}^{t-1} - \mathbf{u}_k^{t-1}\right)
$$

Where:
*   $\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}$ are auxiliary dual/primal variables [G].
*   $\sigma, \gamma, \theta$ are step-size/convergence parameters [S2, unofficial].
*   $\text{prox}_{f, \sigma}(\xi) = \operatorname*{argmin}_{\mathbf{u}_{t-1}} f(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}) + \frac{\sigma}{2} \| \mathbf{u}_{t-1} - \xi \|^2$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\text{prox}_{g, \frac{1}{\gamma}}$ handles the incompressibility constraint $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$ [S2, unofficial]. This is implemented by projecting the velocity field onto the space of divergence-free velocities, denoted $\Pi_{DIV}$ [G].

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Velocity Estimation**: Call Velocity Reconstruction (Algorithm 5) to estimate $\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$ using $\rho_{t-1}$ and $\rho_{t-2}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Initialize $\mathbf{u}_0^{t-1} = \mathbf{0}$ (or previous estimate) [G].
    *   Iterate the primal-dual updates until convergence [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Velocity Prediction**: Advect the estimated velocity $\mathbf{u}_{t-1}$ with itself to predict the velocity at time $t$, denoted $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t$. Ensure incompressibility by projecting $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t$ onto $\Pi_{DIV}$ [S1].
    *   $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t = \text{Advection}(\mathbf{u}_{t-1}, \mathbf{u}_{t-1})$ [S1].
    *   $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t \leftarrow \Pi_{DIV}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t)$ [S1].
3.  **Density Prediction**: Advect the density $\rho_{t-1}$ forward using the velocity guess $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t-1}$ to create the density guess $\tilde{\rho}_t$ [S1].
    *   $\tilde{\rho}_t = \text{Advection}(\rho_{t-1}, \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t-1})$ [S1].

```python
def coupled_density_velocity_estimation(rho_t_minus_1, rho_t_minus_2, dt, params):
    """
    Estimates velocity u_{t-1} and predicts density rho_t.
    
    Args:
        rho_t_minus_1: 3D array, density at t-1
        rho_t_minus_2: 3D array, density at t-2
        dt: float, time step
        params: dict containing alpha, beta, sigma, gamma, theta, max_iter
        
    Returns:
        u_t_minus_1: 3D vector field, estimated velocity at t-1
        rho_t_pred: 3D array, predicted density at t
    """
    # Step 1: Velocity Reconstruction (Algorithm 5)
    # Minimize f(u) = || (rho_{t-1} - rho_{t-2})/dt + u . grad(rho_{t-1}) ||^2 
    #                + alpha * E_smooth(u) + beta * E_kinetic(u)
    # Subject to div(u) = 0
    
    # Compute density gradient using central finite differences [G]
    grad_rho = compute_gradient(rho_t_minus_1)
    
    # Initialize primal-dual variables
    u = zeros_like_vector_field(rho_t_minus_1)
    X = zeros_like_vector_field(rho_t_minus_1)
    Y = zeros_like_vector_field(rho_t_minus_1)
    
    # Precompute velocity residual term v
    v = (rho_t_minus_1 - rho_t_minus_2) / dt
    
    for k in range(params['max_iter']):
        # Compute gradient of data fidelity term f w.r.t u
        residual = v + dot_product(u, grad_rho)
        
        # Smoothness and Kinetic Energy gradients [G]
        grad_smooth = compute_smoothness_gradient(u, params['alpha'])
        grad_kinetic = compute_kinetic_gradient(u, params['beta'])
        
        # Combine gradients for f
        grad_f = 2 * residual * grad_rho + grad_smooth + grad_kinetic
        
        # Primal-Dual Update (Eq 14-16)
        temp = (1.0 / params['sigma']) * X + Y
        # Solve prox_{f, sigma}(temp) = argmin_u f(u) + sigma/2 ||u - temp||^2 [G]
        prox_f = solve_prox_f(temp, grad_rho, v, params['alpha'], params['beta'], params['sigma'])
        
        X = X + params['sigma'] * Y - params['sigma'] * prox_f
        
        # u_{k+1} = prox_{g, 1/gamma}(u_{k+1} - gamma * X_{k+1})
        u_new = u - params['gamma'] * X
        u_old = u
        # Project onto divergence-free space Pi_DIV [G]
        u = project_divergence_free(u_new)
        
        # Y_{k+1} = u_{k+1} + theta * (u_{k+1} - u_k)
        Y = u + params['theta'] * (u - u_old)
        
        if convergence_check(u, X, Y, k):
            break
            
    u_t_minus_1 = u
    
    # Step 2: Velocity Prediction
    # Advect u_{t-1} with itself [S1]
    u_t_pred = advect_vector_field(u_t_minus_1, u_t_minus_1)
    # Project to divergence-free [S1]
    u_t_pred = project_divergence_free(u_t_pred)
    
    # Step 3: Density Prediction
    # Advect rho_{t-1} with velocity guess u_{t-1} [S1]
    rho_t_pred = advect_scalar_field(rho_t_minus_1, u_t_minus_1)
    
    return u_t_minus_1, rho_t_pred
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Depth Regularization**: Do not apply depth regularization. It breaks the sparsity of the matrix, making the optimization problem inefficient to solve [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Ill-Posedness**: The method is specifically designed for scenarios with few cameras where standard tomography fails [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision**: Advection steps require careful interpolation to avoid numerical diffusion or instability [G].
*   **Incompressibility**: The projection $\Pi_{DIV}$ must be accurate to maintain physical plausibility [G].

## Subtopics

### [Global Transport Formulation](20592-global-transport-formulation.md)
Global Transport Formulation is a tomographic reconstruction method that optimizes for a single initial density state $\rho_0$ and a velocity sequence $\{u_0, \dots, u_{F-1}\}$, rather than optimizing density $\rho_t$ at every time step individually. This approach enforces correct transport over the entire fluid flow trajectory, addressing the ill-posed nature of sparse-view tomography by leveraging physical priors. It sets itself apart from methods that treat density frames independently or use local temporal constraints by propagating gradients through the full advection chain, ensuring global consistency in the reconstructed motion. Choose it when global temporal consistency is prioritized over per-frame optimization. [Global Transport Formulation](20592-global-transport-formulation.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Significantly faster than previous coupled methods (e.g., Eckert et al. took 50–60 min per frame; this method enables interactive reconstruction) [S1][S2, unofficial]. The efficiency comes from avoiding depth regularization and using a fast primal-dual solver [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing density and velocity fields for at least two time steps, plus auxiliary variables for the primal-dual solver [G].
*   **Strengths**: Handles sparse camera setups; enforces physical constraints (incompressibility, smoothness); enables interactive reconstruction [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Relies on the accuracy of the optical flow constraint; assumes incompressibility; performance depends on the convergence of the primal-dual solver [G].
*   **When to Use**: Fluid reconstruction from sparse multi-view video where physical plausibility is important and computational efficiency is required [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: When high-precision depth information is available and can be used without breaking sparsity; when the fluid is compressible [G].

## Background
The method was developed to address the limitations of previous coupled density and velocity estimation strategies, such as those by Eckert et al., which were too slow for practical use [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of differentiable rendering and tomographic reconstruction, applying physical priors to regularize the inverse problem [S1]. The primal-dual algorithm used is based on the work of Chambolle and Pock [S2, unofficial].
