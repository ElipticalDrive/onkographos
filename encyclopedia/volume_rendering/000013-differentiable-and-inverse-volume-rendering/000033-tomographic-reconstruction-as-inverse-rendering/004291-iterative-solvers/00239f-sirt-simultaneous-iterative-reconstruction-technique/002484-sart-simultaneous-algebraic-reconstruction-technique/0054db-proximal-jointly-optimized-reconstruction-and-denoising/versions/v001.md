# Proximal Jointly-Optimized Reconstruction and Denoising

Also called: Iterative Reconstruction with Integrated Denoising, Proximal Optimization Framework

## Overview
This method solves the tomographic reconstruction problem for noisy electron microscopy tilt-series by integrating denoising directly into the iterative reconstruction process [S1]. Unlike traditional pipelines that apply denoising as separate pre- or post-processing steps, this approach alternates between minimizing re-projection error and decreasing noise within a single optimization loop [S1]. It is distinguished from its parent [SART](3277-sart-simultaneous-algebraic-reconstruction-technique.md) by its use of a proximal algorithm framework that allows the substitution of various regularizers and denoising methods alongside the algebraic solver [S1].

## How it works
The method formulates tomographic reconstruction as an optimization problem where the goal is to find a 3D volume that minimizes the discrepancy with measured projections while adhering to noise constraints [S1]. It relies on the flexibility of proximal algorithms to decouple the data fidelity term (handled by algebraic reconstruction) from the regularization term (handled by denoising) [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** Aligned tilt-series from cryogenic electron microscopy (noisy 2D projection images) [S1].
*   **Output:** A denoised 3D tomogram [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Tilt-series:** A set of 2D projection images corresponding to specific tilt angles. The exact bit-depth and resolution are not specified in the sources, but they are treated as raw input data [S1].
*   **Tomogram:** A 3D volume grid representing the reconstructed object. The sources do not specify the voxel size or grid dimensions, noting only that the method is GPU-accelerated [S1].
*   **Regularizer/Denoiser:** A module or function applied during the denoising step. The specific implementation is flexible and not fixed by the method definition [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Reconstruction Algorithm:** The method supports SART or SIRT as the solver for the data fidelity term [S1].
*   **Regularizer/Denoising Method:** Various regularization and denoising methods can be integrated [S1]. The specific choice is not fixed in the general description [S1].
*   **Iteration Count:** The number of iterations is not specified in the sources [S1].
*   **Typical Values:** Not given in the sources.

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Initialize:** Start with an initial estimate of the 3D tomogram (e.g., zero-filled or back-projected). [G] (Standard initialization for iterative reconstruction).
2.  **Reconstruction Step (Data Fidelity):** Minimize the re-projection error using an algebraic reconstruction technique (SART or SIRT) [S1]. This step updates the tomogram to better match the measured tilt-series [S1].
3.  **Denoising Step (Regularization):** Apply a regularization or denoising method to the current tomogram estimate [S1]. This step decreases noise and enforces prior constraints on the volume [S1].
4.  **Iterate:** Repeat steps 2 and 3 until convergence or a maximum number of iterations is reached [S1].
5.  **Output:** Return the final denoised tomogram [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Noise Levels:** The method is specifically designed for low signal-to-noise ratio data typical of cryo-EM, where high electron doses would damage the sample [S1].
*   **Consistency Check:** A key advantage is that the method maintains a direct relationship between the original tilt-series and the reconstructed tomogram, allowing for direct consistency checks between the reconstruction and raw input projections [S1]. This is difficult in pipelines with separate denoising steps [S1].
*   **Implementation:** The method is implemented for GPU execution to accelerate the iterative process [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method is iterative, so the time complexity depends on the number of iterations and the cost of the forward/back-projection steps. The sources state that results can be achieved in minutes due to GPU acceleration [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method achieves excellent denoising results and sharp details in a matter of minutes on GPUs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Integrates denoising into reconstruction, removing the need for separate pre- or post-processing steps; maintains direct consistency with raw input data; flexible framework allowing various regularizers; GPU-accelerated [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires aligned tilt-series; performance depends on the choice of regularizer and solver; specific convergence criteria and iteration counts are not detailed in the sources [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for 3D tomographic reconstruction from noisy electron microscopy tilt-series where integrated denoising is desired [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not suitable for non-tomographic reconstruction or when high-dose, low-noise projections are available and separate denoising is preferred [S1].

## Background
The method was presented by J. Strnad, Z. Zhang, S. Li, I. Viola, and W. Heidrich in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the challenges of cryogenic electron tomography (cryo-ET), where specimens are sensitive to electron radiation, necessitating low-dose imaging and resulting in noisy tilt-series [S1]. Traditional pipelines apply denoising before or after reconstruction, which can lead to loss of information or inconsistency with the raw data [S1]. This approach leverages the flexibility of proximal algorithms to combine reconstruction and denoising into a single iterative process [S1]. The framework is open-source and allows for easy extension with additional reconstruction and denoising methods [S1].
