# Conjugate Gradient (CG)

Also called: GC (Conjugate Gradient)

## Overview
Conjugate Gradient (CG) is an iterative descent method used to solve the linear system $R^T R f = R^T \text{proj}$ arising from the minimization of the quadratic error functional $J(f) = ||\text{proj} - Rf||^2$ in overdetermined tomographic reconstruction problems [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from sibling methods like Expectation-Maximization (EM) by its significantly faster convergence speed, though it lacks the inherent positivity guarantee of EM and may exhibit non-convergence issues more rapidly [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Projection data vector $\text{proj}$, forward projection matrix $R$ (or its action), initial estimate $f^{(0)}$ (typically zero or a filtered backprojection result).
- Output: Reconstructed volume $f$.

**Data Structures:**
- Vectors $f, r, p$ of size $N$ (number of voxels).
- Scalars $\alpha_n, \beta_n$.
- Matrix-vector products $R v$ and $R^T v$ are computed using the specific geometry of the tomographic setup (e.g., ray-driven or voxel-driven projection/backprojection).

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. Initialize $f^{(0)} = 0$ (or another initial guess).
2. Compute initial residual: $r_0 = R^T (\text{proj} - R f^{(0)})$.
3. Set initial search direction: $p_1 = r_0$.
4. For iteration $n = 1, 2, \dots$ until convergence:
   a. Compute step size:
      $$ \alpha_n = \frac{p_n^T p_n}{(R p_n)^T (R p_n)} $$
   b. Update solution:
      $$ f^{(n+1)} = f^{(n)} + \alpha_n p_n $$
   c. Compute new residual:
      $$ r_n = R^T (\text{proj} - R f^{(n+1)}) $$
      *Note: The source [S1, unofficial] defines $r_n = R^T (\text{proj} - R f^{(n)})$ in the context of the orthogonality conditions, but standard CG updates the residual based on the new solution. The formula for $\beta_n$ in [S1, unofficial] uses $R r_{n-1}$, implying $r$ is the residual of the normal equations or the original system. Given the minimization of $J(f)$, the residual of the cost function is $g_n = R^T R f^{(n)} - R^T \text{proj} = -r_n$. The update $f^{(n+1)} = f^{(n)} + \alpha_n p_n$ is standard.*
   d. If $n > 1$, compute coefficient:
      $$ \beta_n = \frac{(R r_{n-1})^T (R p_n)}{(R p_{n-1})^T (R p_{n-1})} $$
      *Note: [S1, unofficial] provides this specific formula for $\beta_n$. Standard CG often uses $\frac{r_n^T r_n}{r_{n-1}^T r_{n-1}}$. The source's formula involves $R$, suggesting it might be derived for the normal equations or a specific preconditioned variant. We follow the source exactly.*
   e. Update search direction:
      $$ p_{n+1} = r_n - \beta_n p_n $$
      *Conditions: $<p_{n+1}, r_n> = 0$ and $<p_{n+1}, R^T R p_n> = 0$ [S1, unofficial].*

**Parameters:**
- `max_iter`: Maximum number of iterations. Not given in the sources. [G] Typical values range from 10 to 100 for tomography, depending on convergence criteria.
- `tol`: Convergence tolerance. Not given in the sources. [G] Typical values are $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Positivity:** The additive correction term does not guarantee that the estimated values remain positive [S2, unofficial]. This is a significant limitation for tomographic reconstruction where voxel values (e.g., density, attenuation) must be non-negative.
- **Convergence:** Non-convergence problems appear faster with CG compared to EM [S2, unofficial].
- **Conditioning:** The method assumes the system $R^T R$ is positive definite. If the system is ill-conditioned, convergence may be slow or unstable.

## Subtopics

### Filtered Conjugate Gradient with Surface Projection
A variant used to solve linear equations with surface constraints without degrading system conditioning or losing positive definiteness [S3, unofficial]. It projects residuals and solutions along the surface tangent plane during the conjugate gradient resolution and corrects particle positions by projecting them directly to the surface after resolution [S3, unofficial]. This avoids the numerical instabilities associated with augmenting the system with Lagrange multipliers, which can degrade conditioning and destroy positive definiteness [S3, unofficial]. The surface projection is performed using an iterative Newton-Raphson method [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Each iteration requires two matrix-vector multiplications ($R p$ and $R^T r$). The total time is $O(k \cdot (N_{voxels} + N_{rays}))$ where $k$ is the number of iterations.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_{voxels} + N_{rays})$ to store the volume, projections, and intermediate vectors.
- **Performance:** CG converges significantly faster than EM [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Rapid convergence for overdetermined systems [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Does not guarantee positivity [S2, unofficial]. More prone to non-convergence issues than EM [S2, unofficial]. Sensitive to the conditioning of $R^T R$.

## Background
Conjugate Gradient is a classic descent method used for tomographic reconstruction [S2, unofficial]. It is based on the minimization of a quadratic functional, commonly the quadratic error [S1, unofficial]. The method is equivalent to the EM algorithm if a Gaussian distribution is chosen [S2, unofficial]. It is widely used in tomographic reconstruction due to its speed [S2, unofficial].
