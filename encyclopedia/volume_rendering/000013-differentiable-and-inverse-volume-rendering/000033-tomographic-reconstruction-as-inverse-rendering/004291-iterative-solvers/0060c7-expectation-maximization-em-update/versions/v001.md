# Expectation-Maximization (EM) Update

Also called: EM Update, Expectation Maximization, Expectation Maximization reconstruction, Maximum Likelihood Expectation Maximization

## Overview
The Expectation-Maximization (EM) Update is an iterative statistical reconstruction algorithm that finds the maximum likelihood estimate of a volume distribution from incomplete or missing projection data, specifically accounting for Poisson noise inherent in X-ray or emission tomography [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike algebraic methods such as [ART](6944-algebraic-reconstruction-technique-art.md) or [SIRT](3101-sirt-simultaneous-iterative-reconstruction-technique.md) which minimize least-squares errors, EM maximizes the likelihood function representing the probability that the reconstructed image generated the measured projections [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished by its multiplicative update rule, which guarantees non-negativity and conservation of total events, but suffers from slow convergence and high-frequency noise amplification (over-fitting) after many iterations [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `projections`: 1D or 2D array of measured projection data (sinogram). Must be non-negative ($proj_i \ge 0$) [S3, unofficial].
*   `volume`: Initial guess of the reconstruction volume. Must be strictly positive ($vol_j > 0$) for all voxels to avoid division by zero or log(0) errors [S3, unofficial].
*   `geometry`: Projection geometry object defining the forward projector $R$ and backprojector $R^*$ [S3, unofficial].
*   `iterations`: Integer number of EM updates [S3, unofficial].
*   `bounds` (optional): Tuple `[min_val, max_val]` for clipping the volume after each iteration [S3, unofficial].
*   `subsets` (optional): Integer $B$ for Ordered Subsets EM (OSEM) acceleration [S1, S2, unofficial]. If 1, standard EM is used.

**Outputs:**
*   `volume`: The reconstructed volume array updated after `iterations` steps.

**Data Structures:**
*   `volume`: Array of shape matching the initial guess, dtype float.
*   `residual`: Temporary array matching projection shape, used to store the ratio of measured to estimated projections.

**Formulas:**
The goal is to minimize the log-likelihood function for Poisson data [S1, unofficial]:
$$ l(f) = \sum_i (proj_i \log (Rf)_i - (Rf)_i) $$
where $R$ is the forward projection operator and $f$ is the image vector [S1, unofficial].

The standard EM multiplicative update for iteration $k+1$ is [S1, S2, unofficial]:
$$ f_j^{(k+1)} = f_j^{(k)} \frac{1}{s_j} \sum_i R_{ij} \frac{proj_i}{(R f^{(k)})_i} $$
where $s_j = \sum_i R_{ij}$ is the normalization factor (sum of the backprojected unity image) [S2, unofficial].

In matrix-vector notation, this is often written as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ f^{(k+1)} = f^{(k)} \odot \left( R^* \left( \frac{proj}{R f^{(k)}} \right) \right) / s $$
where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication, and division is element-wise [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `iterations`: Typical values range from 10 to 50 for standard EM, though convergence is slow [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   `subsets`: For OSEM, $B$ is typically 8-16 [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
1.  **Negative Data:** If `projections.min() < 0`, the algorithm fails because the ratio $proj_i / (Rf)_i$ becomes negative or undefined in log-likelihood contexts. Check and reject or clip negative projections [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Zero Initial Guess:** If `volume.min() <= 0`, the forward projection $(Rf)_i$ may be zero, causing division by zero in the ratio step. Initialize with `numpy.ones_like(vol)` or a positive constant [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Noise Amplification:** EM produces images with excessive high-frequency details ("too many details") [S1, unofficial]. It is unstable after a large number of iterations. Solutions include early stopping (e.g., after 12 steps) or adding a penalty term $B(f)$ to minimize $l(f) - B(f)$, where $B(f) = (f - \bar{f})^T B (f - \bar{f})$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Conservation:** If initialized positively, all subsequent estimates remain positive. Voxels initialized to zero remain zero. Total number of events is conserved at each iteration [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def em_update(projections, volume, geometry, iterations, bounds=None, subsets=1):
    """
    Perform Expectation-Maximization reconstruction.
    
    Args:
        projections: 1D or 2D array of measured projections.
        volume: Initial positive guess of the volume.
        geometry: Object with forwardproject and backproject methods.
        iterations: Number of EM steps.
        bounds: Optional tuple (min, max) to clip volume.
        subsets: Number of subsets for OSEM. 1 for standard EM.
        
    Returns:
        volume: Reconstructed volume.
    """
    # Validation
    if np.any(projections < 0):
        raise ValueError("Projections must be non-negative.")
    if np.any(volume <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Initial volume guess must be strictly positive.")

    # Precompute normalization factor s_j = sum_i R_ij
    # This is the backprojection of a unity image (ones)
    unity_proj = np.ones_like(projections)
    s = geometry.backproject(unity_proj, volume.shape, geometry)
    
    # Handle potential zeros in s to avoid division by zero
    # [G] In practice, s should be positive everywhere for valid geometry.
    # If s has zeros, those voxels are unobservable and remain at initial value.
    s_safe = np.where(s > 0, s, 1.0)
    
    # Initialize residual norm tracking if needed
    rnorms = []

    for ii in range(iterations):
        # Forward project current estimate
        estimated_proj = geometry.forwardproject(volume, geometry)
        
        # Compute ratio: measured / estimated
        # Guard against division by zero in estimated_proj
        # [G] If estimated_proj is 0, the ratio is undefined. 
        # Standard practice is to treat 0/0 as 0 or ignore those pixels.
        # Here we use a small epsilon for stability.
        epsilon = 1e-10
        ratio = projections / (estimated_proj + epsilon)
        
        # Clip ratio to avoid extreme values causing instability
        # [G] Source [S3, unofficial] clips ratio based on max, but standard EM uses raw ratio.
        # We follow the standard formula derived from likelihood maximization.
        
        # Backproject the ratio
        backprojected_ratio = geometry.backproject(ratio, volume.shape, geometry)
        
        # Multiplicative update: f_new = f_old * (backprojected_ratio / s)
        volume = volume * (backprojected_ratio / s_safe)
        
        # Apply bounds if specified
        if bounds is not None:
            volume = np.clip(volume, bounds[0], bounds[1])
            
        # Compute residual norm for monitoring (optional)
        # [G] Source [S3, unofficial] computes L2 norm statistic of positive residuals.
        res_pos = ratio[ratio > 0]
        if len(res_pos) > 0:
            rnorm = np.std(res_pos) / np.mean(res_pos)
            rnorms.append(rnorm)

    return volume, rnorms
```

## Subtopics

### [Ordered Subsets EM (OSEM)](20046-ordered-subsets-em-osem.md)
Accelerated variant of EM that decomposes each iteration into $B$ sub-iterations using subsets of projection data, speeding up convergence but potentially leading to earlier divergence.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(I \cdot N \cdot M)$ per iteration, where $I$ is iterations, $N$ is number of voxels, and $M$ is number of projections. Each iteration requires one forward projection and one backprojection [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N + M)$ for storing the volume and projections, plus temporary buffers for forward/backprojection [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Convergence is slow compared to algebraic methods like SIRT or CG. It typically requires many iterations to reach a stable solution [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Statistically optimal for Poisson noise; guarantees non-negativity; conserves total activity/events [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow convergence; prone to noise amplification and artifacts after many iterations; sensitive to initial guess [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When data follows Poisson statistics (e.g., low-count X-ray, emission tomography) and non-negativity is critical.
*   **When not to use:** For high-count data where Gaussian noise approximation holds (use SIRT/CG); when speed is paramount (use OSEM or algebraic methods).

## Background
The EM algorithm was introduced as a general method for finding maximum likelihood estimates in the presence of incomplete data [S1, unofficial]. In tomography, it was adapted to handle Poisson noise in projection data [S2, unofficial]. The Ordered Subsets EM (OSEM) variant was introduced by Hudson and Larkin in 1994 to accelerate convergence [S1, unofficial]. It is widely used in medical imaging, particularly in SPECT and PET reconstruction, and in X-ray CT for low-dose scenarios [S1, S2, unofficial].
