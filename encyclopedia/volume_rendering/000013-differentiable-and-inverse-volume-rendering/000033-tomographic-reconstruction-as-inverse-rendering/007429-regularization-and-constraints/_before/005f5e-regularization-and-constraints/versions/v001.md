# Regularization and Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and Constraints are techniques applied during the optimization of neural radiance fields to prevent overfitting, suppress artifacts, and enforce geometric or photometric priors. This family addresses the instability of implicit representations in sparse-view or noisy settings by modifying the loss function or network behavior. It differs from sibling methods like [Depth and Geometry Priors](18223-depth-and-geometry-priors.md) by focusing on implicit constraints (entropy, frequency, smoothness) rather than explicit supervision from external depth sensors or meshes.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves augmenting the standard photometric reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{render}$ with additional penalty terms $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{render} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)} $$
where $\lambda_k$ are weighting coefficients. These terms typically operate on:
1.  **Ray-space distributions**: Constraining the probability mass distribution of density along rays to encourage sparsity or consistency [S1].
2.  **Frequency domain**: Limiting the high-frequency components of the positional encoding to improve generalization [S1].
3.  **Spatial/Geometric continuity**: Enforcing smoothness of derived quantities (normals, density) while preserving edges [S1].

## Subtopics

### [FreeNeRF](17644-freenerf.md)
[FreeNeRF](17644-freenerf.md) improves few-shot NeRF training by penalizing high-frequency signal reliance and near-camera density. It is chosen when training views are sparse (2–10) to reduce floating artifacts and overfitting.

### [InfoNeRF](17849-infonerf.md)
[InfoNeRF](17849-infonerf.md) uses information-theoretic entropy minimization and KL-divergence between neighboring rays to enforce scene compactness and view consistency. It is chosen for few-shot novel view synthesis to mitigate noise and degenerate overfitting without external data structures.

### Normal Smoothness Constraint
An adaptive constraint ensuring continuity of estimated surface normals in flat regions while preserving sharp features by weighting the normal difference based on albedo similarity [S1].

**Definition**
The constraint minimizes the discrepancy between the surface normal at a point and a randomly perturbed neighboring point, using an edge-aware weight mask derived from albedo differences [S1].

**Step**
1.  Sample a perturbed point by adding uniform noise in the normalized 3D space with magnitude 0.01 to generate the neighbor point for $N_{near}$ [S1].
2.  Compute the edge-aware weight mask $M_i$ from the albedo color difference $\tau = \| c_i - c_j \|_2$. If $\tau \le \tau_n = 0.15$, set $M_i = 1.0$; otherwise, set $M_i = 0.05$ [S1].

**Formula**
$$ L_{normalSM} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} M_i \cdot \| N_{pi} - N_{near} \| $$
where $N_{pi}$ is the surface normal at point $p$, $N_{near}$ is the normal at a randomly perturbed neighboring point, and $M_i$ is the edge-aware weight mask [S1].

**Parameter**
$\gamma_{sm}$: Weight for the normal smoothness term in the final objective function. Selected from {0.001, 0.005, 0.01} [S1].

**Pitfall**
Over-smoothing across edges: The mask $M_i$ weakens the constraint when local albedo differences indicate an edge (e.g., roofs, walls), preventing undesired smoothing across structural breaks [S1].

## Background
Regularization in NeRFs originated from the need to stabilize training in sparse-view scenarios. FreeNeRF [S1] and InfoNeRF [S1] introduced frequency and entropy-based constraints respectively. The Normal Smoothness Constraint [S1] was developed for DSM reconstruction from multi-view satellite images to handle the specific challenges of implicit geometry in remote sensing, such as noise and flat-region ambiguity.
