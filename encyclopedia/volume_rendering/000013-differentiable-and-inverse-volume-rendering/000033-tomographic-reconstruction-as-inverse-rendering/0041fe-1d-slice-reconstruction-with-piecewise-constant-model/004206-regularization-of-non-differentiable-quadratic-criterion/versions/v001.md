# Regularization of Non-Differentiable Quadratic Criterion

Also called: Regularization for Piecewise Constant Model

## Overview
This method addresses the non-differentiability of the quadratic criterion used in 1D slice reconstruction with a piecewise constant model [S1, unofficial]. It applies regularization techniques to handle the optimization problem where the standard quadratic criterion cannot be directly differentiated [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like Noise Sensitivity Analysis via Covariance Matrix by focusing on the stabilization of the inversion process itself rather than the post-hoc analysis of parameter uncertainty [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** The measured projection data vector $y$, the forward operator matrix $F$ (mapping the unknown radial field to projections), and the regularization weight $\mu_2$.
*   **Outputs:** The reconstructed radial field vector $x$.

**Data Structures:**
*   $x$: Vector of size $n$ representing the piecewise constant parameters of the 1D slice.
*   $F$: Matrix of size $m \times n$, where $m$ is the number of projections and $n$ is the number of parameters.
*   $y$: Vector of size $m$ containing measured data.
*   $L_2$: Second-order finite-difference operator matrix of size $n \times n$.

**Formulas:**
The objective function $\Phi(x)$ combines data fidelity and Tikhonov regularization [S2, unofficial]:
$$ \Phi(x) = \|Fx - y\|_2^2 + \mu_2^2 \|L_2 x\|_2^2 $$
where:
*   $\|Fx - y\|_2^2$ is the data fidelity term.
*   $\mu_2^2 \|L_2 x\|_2^2$ is the Tikhonov regularization term [S2, unofficial].
*   $L_2$ is defined as [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ L_2 = \frac{1}{\Delta r^2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & & & \ddots & & \vdots \\ 0 & \cdots & 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    Note: The exact boundary conditions for the first and last rows of $L_2$ are not fully specified in the provided text, but the interior structure is a standard second-order difference [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Expand the Objective Function:**
    Expand the terms of $\Phi(x)$ to prepare for differentiation [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \|Fx - y\|_2^2 = x^T F^T Fx - 2x^T F^T y + y^T y $$
    $$ \mu_2^2 \|L_2 x\|_2^2 = \mu_2^2 x^T L_2^T L_2 x $$
    Combining these, the full objective function is [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \Phi(x) = x^T F^T Fx - 2x^T F^T y + y^T y + \mu_2^2 x^T L_2^T L_2 x $$

2.  **Derive the Normal Equation:**
    Compute the derivative of $\Phi(x)$ with respect to $x$ and set it to zero [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial x} = 2F^T Fx - 2F^T y + 2\mu_2^2 L_2^T L_2 x = 0 $$
    Rearranging yields the regularized normal equation [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ (F^T F + \mu_2^2 L_2^T L_2) x = F^T y $$
    Let $B^T = F^T F + \mu_2^2 L_2^T L_2$. The system becomes [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ B^T x = F^T y $$

3.  **Solve the Linear System:**
    Solve for $x$ using Cholesky factorization of $B^T$ [S2, unofficial]. Note: $B^T$ is symmetric positive definite if $F^T F$ and $L_2^T L_2$ are positive semi-definite and $\mu_2^2 > 0$.

4.  **Determine Optimal Regularization Weight:**
    Use the L-curve method to find the optimal $\mu_2$ [S2, unofficial]:
    *   Scan $\mu_2$ from $10^{-10}$ to $10^{-2}$ with $N_k = 201$ steps [S2, unofficial].
    *   For each $\mu_{2,k}$, solve for $x_k$.
    *   Compute the residual norm $R(\mu_{2,k}) = \|Fx_k - y\|_2$ and the regularization norm $S(\mu_{2,k}) = \|L_2 x_k\|_2$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Plot $\log_{10} R(\mu_{2,k})$ vs $\log_{10} S(\mu_{2,k})$ to form the L-curve [S2, unofficial].
    *   Identify the "corner" of the L-curve as the optimal point [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\mu_2$: Regularization weight. Scanned from $10^{-10}$ to $10^{-2}$ with 201 steps for L-curve analysis [S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   The second-order operator $L_2$ acts globally and does not explicitly enforce the physical center-axis condition ($\partial x / \partial r |_{r=0} = 0$) [S2, unofficial]. This leads to inaccuracies near the symmetry axis in axisymmetric tomography [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Single-Parameter Tikhonov Regularization
A regularization technique to mitigate noise amplification in direct and iterative inversions by adding a second-order smoothness penalty to the objective function [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from multi-regularized approaches by using only one regularization parameter $\mu_2$ [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when noise amplification is the primary concern and the global smoothness assumption is acceptable [S2, unofficial]. It is limited by its failure to enforce the physical center-axis condition in axisymmetric problems [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Solving the linear system using Cholesky factorization is $O(n^3)$ for dense matrices, but $F^T F$ and $L_2^T L_2$ may have structure that allows for faster solvers. The L-curve method requires solving the system $N_k$ times, where $N_k = 201$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Storing $F^T F$ and $L_2^T L_2$ requires $O(n^2)$ memory.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method was tested with 0.3% noise [S2, unofficial]. It stably reconstructs the true extinction coefficient and radiative source term [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates noise amplification [S2, unofficial]. Improves stability of inversion [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Does not explicitly enforce the physical center-axis condition ($\partial x / \partial r |_{r=0} = 0$) [S2, unofficial]. Leads to inaccuracies near the symmetry axis [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When noise amplification is a significant issue in direct or iterative inversions [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the physical center-axis condition is critical and cannot be approximated by global smoothness [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the work of Daun et al. (2006) and Tikhonov (1975) [S2, unofficial]. It was applied to tomographic reconstruction of axisymmetric flames [S2, unofficial]. The specific application in the source is tomographic reconstruction of infrared radiance from axisymmetric sprays in a hot environment [S2, unofficial]. The source [S1, unofficial] is a thesis by an unnamed author on tomographic reconstruction from a small number of views, proposing three regularization techniques for a non-differentiable quadratic criterion [S1, unofficial].
