# ViiNeuS

Also called: ViiNeuS: Volumetric Initialization for Implicit Neural Surface reconstruction of urban scenes with limited image overlap

## Overview
ViiNeuS is a hybrid implicit surface learning method designed to efficiently initialize the signed distance field (SDF) for reconstructing large urban driving scenes from 2D street-view images with limited overlap [S1]. It distinguishes itself from its sibling [NeRF Model Initialization](1868-nerf-model-initialization.md) and related methods like StreetSurf by modeling volumetric density and signed distance in two separate implicit fields, utilizing a self-supervised probabilistic density estimation strategy to sample points near the surface, and transitioning progressively from volumetric to surface representation without relying on geometric priors or LiDAR [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sequence of 2D street-view images with known camera poses (or estimated poses, e.g., via MOISST for Waymo [S1]), masking out dynamic objects (vehicles, pedestrians) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A signed distance field (SDF) and a volumetric density field, from which a final mesh is extracted using Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Hash Grid:** A 3D hierarchical hash grid for position encoding, using default parameters from InstantNGP [26] [S1].
2.  **Spherical Harmonics:** Used for viewing direction encoding [S1].
3.  **MLPs:** Two separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons, denoted as $F_\Theta$ (for density) and $F_\Theta$ (for SDF, though the source uses the same symbol, they represent distinct networks) [S1].
    *   **Architecture:** Each MLP has 2 hidden layers with 64 hidden units [S1].
    *   **Inputs:** Concatenated hash-encoded position and spherical harmonic-encoded viewing direction.
    *   **Outputs:**
        *   Density network outputs volumetric density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$.
        *   SDF network outputs signed distance $s \in \mathbb{R}$ and potentially other features (source implies separate fields, but specific output dimensions beyond $s$ and $\sigma$ are not detailed beyond the rendering formula) [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Encoding:**
    *   Encode the 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ using the 3D hierarchical hash grid [S1].
    *   Encode the viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (or $\mathbb{R}^3$ normalized) using spherical harmonics [S1].

2.  **Network Forward Pass:**
    *   Pass the encoded position and direction through the density network to predict volumetric density $\sigma$ [S1].
    *   Pass the encoded position and direction through the SDF network to predict the signed distance $s$ [S1].

3.  **Progressive Training Stages:**
    The training is divided into two stages based on the total number of iterations $T_{total}$ (default 14,000) [S1]:
    *   **Pure Volumetric Stage:** Lasts for the first 100 steps [S1]. During this stage, the model relies primarily on the volumetric density field for rendering [S1].
    *   **Hybrid Stage:** Begins after step 100 and ends at 35% of the total training time ($0.35 \times T_{total}$) [S1]. In this stage, the method transitions from volumetric to surface representation using the SDF [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering Strategy:**
    *   The method employs a novel volume-rendering strategy relying on self-supervised probabilistic density estimation to sample points near the surface [S1].
    *   The final pixel color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is rendered using alpha compositing [S1]:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i c_i $$
        where $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ is the weight for sample $i$, $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity derived from the density, and $c_i$ is the color [S1].
    *   *Note:* The source does not explicitly detail how the SDF $s$ is converted to opacity $\alpha$ or color $c$ in the hybrid stage, only that the transition occurs and normals are supervised [S1].

5.  **Supervision:**
    *   **Color Loss:** Standard photometric loss between rendered and ground truth colors [S1].
    *   **Normal Loss:** Surface normals are supervised using cues predicted from a pre-trained monocular estimator [S1].
    *   **SDF Regularization:** A regularization loss is applied to the SDF parameter $s$ to stabilize convergence:
        $$ L_s = \frac{1}{s + \epsilon} $$
        where $\epsilon$ is a small constant [S1].

6.  **Optimization:**
    *   Use the Adam optimizer [S1].
    *   Learning rate: Cosine decay from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-4}$ [S1].
    *   Weight decay for parameter $s$: Decay from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$ [S1].
    *   Total training iterations: 14,000 [S1].

7.  **Mesh Extraction:**
    *   Generate the final mesh using the Marching Cubes algorithm on the learned SDF [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Road Height Assumptions:** Unlike StreetSurf, ViiNeuS does not assume the road and sidewalk are at the same height, making it robust to non-flat roads (e.g., downhills) where StreetSurf fails [S1].
*   **Scene Boundedness:** ViiNeuS does not require a bounded scene initialization (unlike Neuralangelo, which requires a sphere shape), making it suitable for open driving sequences [S1].
*   **Wide/Open Sequences:** ViiNeuS may fail to reconstruct fine details in very wide and open sequences [S1].
*   **Parameter Convergence:** The parameter $s$ (SDF) may not always converge to a satisfactory level without the regularization term $L_s = 1/(s + \epsilon)$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** ViiNeuS requires half the training time of state-of-the-art solutions like StreetSurf (20 minutes vs. 40 minutes for a single scene on an RTX 4090) [S1]. It is two times faster to train than previous solutions [S1].
*   **Memory and Parameters:** ViiNeuS uses a single 3D hierarchical hash grid, whereas StreetSurf uses two grids (3D and 4D). This results in ViiNeuS having one-third the parameters and half the GPU memory of StreetSurf [S1].
*   **Accuracy:**
    *   Mean Point-to-Mesh (P→M) error on KITTI-360: 12 cm [S1].
    *   Mean P→M error averaged over 4 datasets (KITTI-360, nuScenes, Waymo, Pandaset): 0.24 m [S1].
    *   Mean Precision (percentage of LiDAR points within 0.15m of mesh) averaged over 4 datasets: 0.46% [S1].
    *   Comparison: StreetSurf has a mean P→M error of 0.47 m and precision of 0.42% over the same datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient initialization of SDF without geometric priors or LiDAR; adaptable to short and curvy driving scenarios; faster training and lower memory usage than StreetSurf; suitable for open scenes unlike Neuralangelo [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Fails to reconstruct fine details in very wide and open sequences [S1].

## Background
ViiNeuS was introduced by Bennehar, Roldão, Tsishkou, et al. in the paper "ViiNeuS: Volumetric Initialization for Implicit Neural Surface reconstruction of urban scenes with limited image overlap," published at CVPR 2025 [S1]. It addresses the challenges of reconstructing large urban driving scenes from 2D images with limited overlap, where traditional methods require LiDAR, strong geometric priors, or long training times [S1]. It builds on the hybrid implicit surface learning approach, contrasting with StreetSurf's road-surface initialization and Neuralangelo's bounded scene requirements [S1].
