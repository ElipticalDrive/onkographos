# Reparameterized Volume Sampling

Also called: Differentiable Volume Sampling, Inverse CDF Sampling

## Overview
Reparameterized Volume Sampling is a differentiable volume rendering technique that computes color estimates by sampling points on a ray using the inverse of the cumulative density function (CDF), allowing gradients to back-propagate through the sampling locations with respect to the density field [S1, unofficial]. It differs from NeRF's hierarchical sampling, which relies on an auxiliary coarse model trained separately, by learning sampling points end-to-end without auxiliary networks [S1, unofficial]. Compared to standard importance sampling with a uniform distribution, this stratified reparameterized sampling exhibits lower variance [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $o$ and direction $d$ [G].
- Ray segment bounds $[t_{r_1}, t_{r_2}]$ (near and far planes) [G].
- Density field $\sigma_r(t)$, typically represented by a neural network or grid [S1, unofficial].
- Radiance field $c_r(t)$ [S1, unofficial].
- Number of samples per ray $k$ (typically 32) [S1, unofficial].
- Number of knots $m$ for piecewise linear approximation (typically 256) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Estimated color $C(r)$ for the ray [S1, unofficial].
- Gradients $\frac{\partial C}{\partial \theta}$ with respect to density field parameters $\theta$ [G].

**Data Structures:**
- **Knots:** A set of $m+1$ positions $t_0, t_1, \dots, t_m$ along the ray. In experiments, these are formed by slightly perturbing a uniform grid [S1, unofficial].
- **Density Approximation:** Piecewise linear interpolation of $\sigma_r(t)$ between knots [S1, unofficial]. Let $\sigma_i = \sigma_r(t_i)$. The density at any $t \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$ is:
  $$ \sigma_r(t) = \sigma_i \frac{t_{i+1} - t}{t_{i+1} - t_i} + \sigma_{i+1} \frac{t - t_i}{t_{i+1} - t_i} $$

**Steps:**
1. **Approximate Density:** Construct the piecewise linear approximation of $\sigma_r(t)$ using $m$ knots [S1, unofficial].
2. **Compute CDF Integral Approximation:** Define the cumulative integral approximation $I(t) = \int_{t_{r_1}}^t \sigma_r(s) ds$ [S1, unofficial]. Since $\sigma_r$ is piecewise linear, $I(t)$ is piecewise quadratic [S1, unofficial]. Specifically, within bin $i$ ($t \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]$), letting $x = t - t_i$ and $\Delta t = t_{i+1} - t_i$:
   $$ I(t) = I(t_i) + \sigma_i x + \frac{\sigma_{i+1} - \sigma_i}{2 \Delta t} x^2 $$
3. **Generate Random Sample:** Draw $u \sim \text{Uniform}[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
4. **Compute Target Opacity:** Calculate the target cumulative opacity value $y_f u$ [S1, unofficial]. The source uses $y_f$ as a scaling factor, typically $1.0$ for full opacity range [G]. The equation is:
   $$ y_f u = F_r(t) = 1 - \exp\left(-\int_{t_{r_1}}^t \sigma_r(s) ds\right) $$
5. **Rewrite Inversion Equation:** Isolate the integral:
   $$ -\log(1 - y_f u) = \int_{t_{r_1}}^t \sigma_r(s) ds = I(t) $$
   Let $Y = -\log(1 - y_f u)$. We need to solve $I(t) = Y$ for $t$ [S1, unofficial].
6. **Find Bin:** Evaluate $I(t)$ at the knots $t_i$ to find the interval $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$ such that $I(t_i) \le Y < I(t_{i+1})$ [S1, unofficial]. Since $I(t)$ is monotonic, this bin is unique [G].
7. **Solve for $t$:** Within the identified bin, solve the quadratic equation $I(t) = Y$ for $t$ [S1, unofficial]. The explicit formula depends on the coefficients of the quadratic polynomial representing $I(t)$ in that bin [S1, unofficial]. The source notes that explicit formulae are provided in supplementary materials and that numerical stability is crucial [S1, unofficial].
8. **Evaluate Radiance:** Evaluate $c_r(t)$ at the computed sample position $t$ [S1, unofficial].
9. **Accumulate Color:** Repeat steps 3-8 for $k$ samples (using stratified sampling for lower variance) and accumulate the color estimate [S1, unofficial]. The color estimate is:
   $$ C(r) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=1}^k c_r(t_j) $$
   where weights are uniform because sampling is performed directly from the approximate CDF [G]. The source states the estimate is unbiased [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $k = 32$ samples per ray [S1, unofficial].
- $m = 256$ knots [S1, unofficial].
- Activation function for density field: Softplus with $\beta = 10$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Zero Gradients:** Using ReLU activation for the density field can result in zero gradients, hindering training [S1, unofficial].
- **Numerical Stability:** Solving the quadratic equation requires careful handling to ensure numerical stability, especially when $Y$ is close to bin boundaries or when coefficients are small [S1, unofficial].
- **Degenerate Distributions:** Importance sampling requires only a few points for degenerate distributions, but the method is designed to work generally [S1, unofficial].
- **Approximation Error:** Piecewise constant approximation was faster but delivered worse rendering quality compared to piecewise linear [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def reparameterized_volume_sampling(ray_origin, ray_direction, t_near, t_far, 
                                    density_net, radiance_net, k=32, m=256):
    """
    Performs reparameterized volume sampling for a single ray.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: (3,) array, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: (3,) array, direction of the ray.
        t_near: float, near plane distance.
        t_far: float, far plane distance.
        density_net: callable, returns density sigma at 3D points.
        radiance_net: callable, returns radiance c at 3D points.
        k: int, number of samples per ray.
        m: int, number of knots for piecewise linear density approximation.
        
    Returns:
        color: (3,) array, estimated color.
    """
    # 1. Normalize ray direction to ensure t represents physical distance
    d = ray_direction / np.linalg.norm(ray_direction)
    
    # 2. Define knots
    # Slightly perturb a uniform grid
    t_uniform = np.linspace(t_near, t_far, m + 1)
    # Perturbation [G]: Add small random noise to avoid degenerate cases
    noise = np.random.uniform(-1e-6, 1e-6, m + 1)
    t_knots = t_uniform + noise
    
    # 3. Evaluate density at knots
    # Sample points along the ray at knots
    points_knots = ray_origin + t_knots[:, None] * d
    sigma_knots = density_net(points_knots).squeeze() # Shape (m+1,)
    
    # 4. Precompute integral values I(t) at knots
    # I(t) = integral from t_near to t of sigma(s) ds
    # Since sigma is piecewise linear, I(t) is piecewise quadratic.
    I_knots = np.zeros(m + 1)
    for i in range(m):
        t_i = t_knots[i]
        t_ip1 = t_knots[i+1]
        sigma_i = sigma_knots[i]
        sigma_ip1 = sigma_knots[i+1]
        
        # Integral of linear function from t_i to t_ip1
        delta_t = t_ip1 - t_i
        integral_bin = 0.5 * (sigma_i + sigma_ip1) * delta_t
        I_knots[i+1] = I_knots[i] + integral_bin
        
    # 5. Stratified sampling
    # Generate k stratified samples
    u_stratified = (np.arange(1, k + 1) - 0.5) / k
    
    # 6. Compute target Y values
    # y_f is 1.0 for full opacity range [G]
    y_f = 1.0
    Y = -np.log(1.0 - y_f * u_stratified)
    
    # Clamp Y to the maximum accumulated optical depth to handle boundary cases
    Y = np.minimum(Y, I_knots[-1])
    
    # 7. Find bins and solve for t
    t_samples = np.zeros(k)
    for j in range(k):
        Y_val = Y[j]
        
        # Find bin i such that I_knots[i] <= Y_val < I_knots[i+1]
        i = np.searchsorted(I_knots, Y_val, side='right') - 1
        i = np.clip(i, 0, m - 1)
        
        t_i = t_knots[i]
        t_ip1 = t_knots[i+1]
        sigma_i = sigma_knots[i]
        sigma_ip1 = sigma_knots[i+1]
        I_i = I_knots[i]
        
        # Solve I(t) = Y_val for t in [t_i, t_ip1]
        # I(t) = I_i + sigma_i * x + 0.5 * slope * x^2, where x = t - t_i
        # 0.5 * slope * x^2 + sigma_i * x + (I_i - Y_val) = 0
        
        delta_t = t_ip1 - t_i
        slope = (sigma_ip1 - sigma_i) / delta_t
        
        a = 0.5 * slope
        b = sigma_i
        c = I_i - Y_val
        
        # Solve quadratic equation a*x^2 + b*x + c = 0
        if np.abs(a) < 1e-9:
            # Linear case (constant density)
            if np.abs(b) < 1e-9:
                x = 0.0
            else:
                x = -c / b
        else:
            discriminant = b**2 - 4*a*c
            if discriminant < 0:
                discriminant = 0.0
            sqrt_disc = np.sqrt(discriminant)
            # Use stable quadratic formula
            if b >= 0:
                x1 = (-b - sqrt_disc) / (2*a)
                x2 = (2*c) / (-b - sqrt_disc)
            else:
                x1 = (2*c) / (-b + sqrt_disc)
                x2 = (-b + sqrt_disc) / (2*a)
            
            # Select root in [0, delta_t]
            if 0 <= x1 <= delta_t:
                x = x1
            elif 0 <= x2 <= delta_t:
                x = x2
            else:
                x = 0.0
                    
        t_samples[j] = t_i + x
        
    # 8. Evaluate radiance and accumulate
    points_samples = ray_origin + t_samples[:, None] * d
    radiance = radiance_net(points_samples) # Shape (k, 3)
    
    # Uniform weights because sampling is from the approximate CDF [G]
    color = np.mean(radiance, axis=0)
    
    return color
```

## Subtopics

### Defensive Sampling
A sampling strategy modifying the density to include a constant background density $\epsilon$ to prevent gradient outliers when $\sigma_t$ is near zero [S2]. The defensive sampling density is $p_{\text{def}}(t) = (\sigma_t(t) + \epsilon) T_\epsilon(t)$ [S2]. The problematic term is bounded by $\frac{1}{\sigma_t(t) + \epsilon} \leq \frac{1}{\epsilon}$ [S2]. However, with $p_{\text{def}}$, non-unit sampling weights compound over the length of the path, which is problematic in volumetric rendering where long paths are common [S2]. When using path replay backpropagation, defensive sampling must either be used for incident radiance terms (injecting variance) or additional samples must be drawn (increasing cost) [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the density and radiance network evaluations at $k$ sample points and $m$ knots [G]. The root-finding step is $O(k \log m)$ or $O(k)$ with precomputed bins [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(m)$ to store knot positions and integral values [G].
- **Measured Performance:** In the 'foggy' density field test, 32 samples matched the baseline performance of 128 samples [S1, unofficial]. In 'Glass and wall' and 'Wall' density fields, 4 samples matched the baseline performance [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** The method scales with the number of knots $m$ and samples $k$ [G].
- **Strengths:** Lower variance compared to standard importance sampling with uniform distribution. End-to-end differentiable without auxiliary networks [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful numerical implementation for stability. Piecewise linear approximation is more complex than piecewise constant [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When differentiable sampling is required and lower variance is desired compared to uniform importance sampling [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and piecewise constant approximation is acceptable (though quality suffers) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Reparameterized Volume Sampling was introduced in the paper "Differentiable Rendering With Reparameterized Volume Sampling" (DVGO) [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the reparameterization estimator principles [S1, unofficial]. The method was applied to 3D scene reconstruction based on a set of image projections, using the synthetic data from NeRF [S1, unofficial]. It was compared against NeRF and DVGO baselines [S1, unofficial].
