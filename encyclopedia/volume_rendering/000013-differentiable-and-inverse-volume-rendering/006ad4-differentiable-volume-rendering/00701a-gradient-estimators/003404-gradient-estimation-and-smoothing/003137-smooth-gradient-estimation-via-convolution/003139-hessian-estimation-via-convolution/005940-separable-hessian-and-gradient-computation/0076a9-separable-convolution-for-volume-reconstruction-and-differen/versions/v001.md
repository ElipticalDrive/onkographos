# Separable Convolution for Volume Reconstruction and Differentiation

Also called: Separable Reconstruction and Differentiation via 1D Kernel Product

## Overview
This method computes 3D volume reconstruction and its spatial derivatives by decomposing the 3D convolution into a product of three 1D kernels, treating each dimension in isolation [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from direct 3D convolution by reducing computational complexity through separability, allowing efficient calculation of gradients and higher-order derivatives required for differentiable volume rendering [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Discrete sampled volume data $v[i, j, k]$ defined on a 3D grid.
- Input: A 1D reconstruction kernel $h(x)$.
- Output: Reconstructed continuous field value $(v * h)(x, y, z)$.
- Output: Partial derivatives $\frac{\partial(v * h)}{\partial x}$, $\frac{\partial(v * h)}{\partial y}$, $\frac{\partial(v * h)}{\partial z}$.

**Data Structures:**
- The 3D kernel is defined as the tensor product of three identical 1D kernels: $h(x, y, z) = h(x)h(y)h(z)$ [S1, unofficial].
- The derivative kernel $h'(x)$ is the analytic derivative of $h(x)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Kernel choice: Cubic kernels, specifically the Mitchell and Netravali "BC" family, are recommended as a compromise between frequency response and cost [S1, unofficial].
- Parameters $B$ and $C$ define the specific cubic spline:
  - Catmull-Rom: $B=0, C=0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
  - Uniform cubic B-spline: $B=1, C=0$ [S1, unofficial].
- The B-spline is preferred for derivative computation because it is a non-interpolating smoothing kernel that attenuates high-frequency noise [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Define the 1D reconstruction kernel $h(x)$ and its derivative $h'(x)$.
2. For any point $(x, y, z)$, compute the reconstructed value by convolving the data with the separable 3D kernel:
   $$ (v * h)(x, y, z) = \sum_{i,j,k} v[i, j, k]h(x-i)h(y-j)h(z-k) $$
   [S1, unofficial]
3. To compute the partial derivative with respect to $x$, convolve the data using $h'(x)$ in the x-dimension and $h(y), h(z)$ in the other dimensions:
   $$ \frac{\partial(v * h)(x, y, z)}{\partial x} = \sum_{i,j,k} v[i, j, k]h'(x - i)h(y - j)h(z - k) $$
   [S1, unofficial]
4. To compute the partial derivative with respect to $y$, use $h(x)$, $h'(y)$, and $h(z)$:
   $$ \frac{\partial(v * h)(x, y, z)}{\partial y} = \sum_{i,j,k} v[i, j, k]h(x - i)h'(y - j)h(z - k) $$
   [S1, unofficial]
5. To compute the partial derivative with respect to $z$, use $h(x)$, $h(y)$, and $h'(z)$:
   $$ \frac{\partial(v * h)(x, y, z)}{\partial z} = \sum_{i,j,k} v[i, j, k]h(x - i)h(y - j)h'(z - k) $$
   [S1, unofficial]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Noise sensitivity: Analytic differentiation has a linear frequency response, amplifying high-frequency noise [S1, unofficial]. Using the derivative of a smoothing kernel (like the cubic B-spline) is preferable to mitigate this [S1, unofficial].
- Kernel Support: Cubic kernels have a larger support than simpler kernels, increasing computational cost [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of voxels and $K$ is the kernel support size. Separability reduces the complexity from $O(N \cdot K^3)$ for direct 3D convolution to $O(N \cdot 3K)$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for the input and output volumes, plus $O(K)$ for the kernel storage.
- **Strengths:** Efficient computation of gradients and reconstruction; flexible kernel choice allows balancing between smoothing and detail preservation [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Noise amplification in derivatives if non-smoothing kernels are used; larger kernel support increases computation time compared to nearest-neighbor or linear interpolation [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When differentiable volume rendering requires accurate gradient estimation and computational efficiency is critical [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When real-time performance is required with very large kernels or when noise is extremely high and cannot be pre-filtered [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method is based on the approach adopted in visualization for using separable convolution to treat each dimension of the image in isolation [S1, unofficial]. It relies on the mathematical property that a 3D kernel can be generated as the product of three 1D kernels, simplifying the evaluation of partial derivatives [S1, unofficial]. The technique is used throughout research in volume rendering, particularly in the context of rendering continuous field structures and their attributes [S1, unofficial]. The specific kernel recommendations (Mitchell-Netravali BC family) are drawn from standard image processing literature [S1, unofficial].
