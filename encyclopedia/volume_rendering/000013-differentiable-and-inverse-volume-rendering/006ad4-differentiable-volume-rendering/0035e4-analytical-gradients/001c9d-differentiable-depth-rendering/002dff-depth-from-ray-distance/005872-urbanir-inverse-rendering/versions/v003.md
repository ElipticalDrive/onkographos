# UrbanIR Inverse Rendering

Also called: Inverse Rendering with Visibility Guidance

## Overview
UrbanIR Inverse Rendering jointly optimizes a neural scene representation and lighting parameters from monocular video to recover physically plausible albedo, normals, semantics, and shadows. [S1] It solves the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering in urban environments by computing visibility via volume rendering transmittance rather than explicit geometry, ensuring consistency between the learned density field and shadow casting. [S1] Unlike explicit geometry shadow casting which may be inconsistent with neural fields, this method computes visibility via volume rendering transmittance, ensuring consistency with the learned density field. [S1]

## How it works
The method trains a neural field $F(\cdot)$ representing density $\sigma$ and color components (albedo $\mathbf{a}$, normal $\mathbf{n}$, semantic $\mathbf{s}$) and a lighting model $\mathcal{L}$ jointly. [S1]

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera rays $\mathcal{R}$, ground-truth RGB images $C_{gt}$, shadow masks $M$ (from external detector [MTMT]), semantic priors, normal priors. [S1]
*   **Outputs:** Rendered color $C(r)$, albedo $A$, normal $N$, semantic $S$, visibility $V$, lighting parameters (ambient $L_{amb}$, sky color $C_{sky}$). [S1]

**Data Structures:**
The neural field $F$ maps 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color attributes. [S1]
*   Density: $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$
*   Albedo: $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Normal: $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Semantic: $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^k$ (where $k$ is the number of classes) [S1]

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Volume Rendering of Intrinsics:** For each camera ray $r$, sample points $x_i$ along the ray. [S1] Compute density $\sigma_i$ and attributes $\mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{s}_i$ using $F$. [S1] Compute alpha-composition weights:
    $$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$. [S1] Render the final color, normal, and semantic maps:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad N(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad S(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i $$
    [S1]

2.  **Depth Estimation for Shading Point:** Compute the expected depth $\hat{t}$ for the ray to determine the 3D point $x(r)$ being shaded:
    $$ \hat{t} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i t_i $$
    [S1] Substitute $\hat{t}$ into the ray equation to get the 3D position $x(r)$. [S1]

3.  **Visibility Estimation via Volume Rendering:** To estimate visibility $V(x, \text{sun})$ of point $x$ to the sun, cast a ray from $x$ in the direction of the sun $\mathbf{l}_{\text{sun}}$. [S1] Compute transmittance along this segment using the same neural field:
    $$ V(x, \text{sun}) = \exp\left(-\sum_i \sigma_i(x_i) \delta_i\right) \quad \text{where } x_i = x + t_i \mathbf{l}_{\text{sun}} $$
    [S1]

4.  **Guidance Visibility Head:** To avoid instability from direct 2D shadow mask supervision (which is inconsistent across frames), train an intermediate MLP head $\hat{V}(r)$ to reproduce the 2D shadow masks $M(r)$. [S1] This provides a consistent guidance signal. [S1]

5.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{render}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{visibility}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{normal}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{semantics}} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} $$
    [S1]

**Formulas:**
*   **Rendering Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{render}} = \sum_{r} \| C_{gt}(r) - C(r) \|_2^2 $$
    [S1]
*   **Visibility Loss:** The loss encourages $V(x, \text{sun})$ to be low where the guidance head $\hat{V}(r)$ predicts shadow, and high otherwise. [S1] Specifically, it constrains geometry along the ray from the pixel to the sun based on shadow masks. [S1]
*   **Normal Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{normal}}$ supervises the rendered normal against priors from Omnidata. [S1]
*   **Semantic Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{semantics}}$ supervises the rendered semantic against priors. [S1]
*   **Regularization Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{albedo}} + \| L_{\text{amb}} \|_2^2 $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{albedo}}$ regularizes ground albedo to be homogeneous:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{albedo}} = \sum_{r \in \text{ground}} \| A(r) - \bar{A}_g \|_2^2 $$
    and $\bar{A}_g$ is the average ground albedo. [S1] $\| L_{\text{amb}} \|_2^2$ prevents unnatural color shifts from large ambient light intensity. [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \lambda_4$: Weights for visibility, normal, semantics, and regularization losses. Typical values are not specified in the sources. [S1]

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Instability from 2D Masks:** Direct supervision of visibility with 2D shadow masks is unstable due to inconsistency across frames. [S1] Solved by using the intermediate MLP head $\hat{V}$ for guidance. [S1]
*   **Ill-posed Optimization:** Large ambient light intensity causes unnatural color shifts in recovered albedo. [S1] Solved by regularizing $\| L_{\text{amb}} \|_2^2$. [S1]
*   **Inconsistency with Explicit Geometry:** Explicit geometry shadow casting may be inconsistent with neural fields. [S1] This method avoids this by computing visibility via volume rendering transmittance. [S1]

## Subtopics

### UrbanIR Training Pipeline
The training pipeline retrieves scene intrinsics (albedo, normal, semantic) via volume rendering from camera rays, guided by semantic and normal priors. [S1] Transmittance along tracing rays is supervised with shadow masks. [S1] Inputs include the input image $I$, Instruct-Pix2Pix output $I_{p2p}$, shadow mask $M$ from MTMT, RGB ground truth $C_{gt}$, albedo $A$, and sky color $C_{sky}$. [S1] The pipeline uses losses $\mathcal{L}_{render}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{visibility}$ for volume rendering (Eq. 4 and 5), and $\mathcal{L}_{seman/c}$, $\mathcal{L}_{normal}$, $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ for intrinsic decomposition. [S1] Priors come from Normal [Omnidata] and Semantic models. [S1]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1]
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on KITTI-360 and Waymo Open Dataset. [S1] The method successfully removes dark shadows with sharp boundaries and enables realistic nighttime rendering by introducing new light sources. [S1]
*   **Scaling:** Evaluated on large-scale urban video sequences. [S1]
*   **Strengths:** Consistency between learned density field and shadow casting; effective shadow removal in albedo; handles relighting. [S1]
*   **Limitations:** Ill-posed nature requires strong regularization; dependency on external shadow masks and priors. [S1]
*   **When to use:** When recovering physically plausible inverse rendering results (albedo, normals, shadows) from monocular urban video is required. [S1]
*   **When not:** Not given in the sources. [S1]

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced in the paper "UrbanIR: Large-Scale Urban Scene Inverse Rendering from a Single Video" published in 3DV 2025. [S1] It addresses the challenges of inverse rendering in urban driving sequences, where shadows and lighting variations complicate the recovery of intrinsic scene properties. [S1] The method builds on [Depth from Ray Distance](5665-depth-from-ray-distance.md) and differentiable volume rendering, and integrates semantic and normal priors to guide the optimization. [S1]
