# Material-Form Differential Path Integral

Also called: Differentiable Path Integral with BSSRDF, Differential path integral, Material Space Differentiation, Reynolds Transport Theorem for Path Integrals, Spatial-form Differential Path Integral, Spatial-form path integral derivative

## Overview
The Material-Form Differential Path Integral is a method for computing the gradient of a radiometric measurement with respect to scene parameters $\pi$ by transforming the integration domain from a deforming spatial path space to a fixed "material space" [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem that standard differentiation of path integrals fails when the integration domain or the integrand's discontinuities depend on the parameters being differentiated [S1][S3, unofficial]. Unlike the Spatial-Form approach, which integrates over the moving spatial domain and requires handling all parameter-dependent discontinuities (including static mesh edges), the material form maps the domain to a reference geometry, reducing the boundary term to only include visibility-related discontinuities (silhouettes) [S2][S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Differentiable volume rendering](43-differentiable-volume-rendering.md) by extending differentiable path tracing to subsurface scattering and implicit surfaces where domain deformation is significant [S1, S2].

## How it works
The method relies on the Reynolds Transport Theorem applied to path integrals. It decomposes the derivative of the measurement $I$ with respect to a parameter $\pi$ into an interior integral (derivative of the integrand over the fixed material space) and a boundary integral (integral over visibility boundaries weighted by their velocity) [S2][S3, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Scene parameter $\pi$ (e.g., vertex positions, implicit surface parameters) [S3, unofficial].
    *   A global parameterization (motion) $X(\cdot, \pi)$ mapping material points to spatial points [S3, unofficial].
    *   The material path space $\hat{\Omega}$, which is independent of $\pi$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   The material boundary path space $\partial\hat{\Omega}$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   The gradient $\frac{dI}{d\pi}$ [S2].

### Data Structures
*   **Material Path $\bar{\mathbf{p}}$:** A sequence of vertices $\mathbf{p}_0, \dots, \mathbf{p}_n$ in the fixed material space.
*   **Spatial Path $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$:** The corresponding path in world space, where $\mathbf{x}_i = X(\mathbf{p}_i, \pi)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Jacobian Determinant $J(\mathbf{p})$:** The determinant of the Jacobian of the map $\chi$ (inverse of $X$), defined as $J(\mathbf{p}) = |dA(\mathbf{x})/dA(\mathbf{p})|$ [S1, S2].
*   **Visibility Boundary Normal $\mathbf{n}_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K)$:** The unit normal of the visibility boundary at vertex $\mathbf{p}_K$, perpendicular to both the surface normal and the tangent of the discontinuity curve [S2].

### Formulas
The derivative of the radiometric measurement $I$ is given by [S2][S3, unofficial]:

$$
\frac{dI}{d\pi} = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \hat{f}'(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) d\mu(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) + \int_{\partial\hat{\Omega}} \hat{f}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) V_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K) d\mu_{\partial}
$$

Where:
*   $\hat{\Omega}$ is the material path space [S3, unofficial].
*   $\partial\hat{\Omega}$ is the material boundary path space containing paths with exactly one vertex $\mathbf{p}_K$ constrained to a set of visibility boundaries $\Delta B(\mathbf{p}_{K-1})$ [S2]. $\mathbf{p}_K$ is a jump discontinuity point of mutual visibility with $\mathbf{p}_{K-1}$ fixed [S2].
*   $\hat{f}(\bar{\mathbf{p}})$ is the material measurement contribution [S2]:
    $$
    \hat{f}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) = \hat{W}_e(\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1) C(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) G(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \hat{L}_e(\mathbf{p}_n, \mathbf{p}_{n-1})
    $$
    where $C(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) = \prod \hat{\rho}$ and $G(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) = \prod \hat{G}$ are products of scattering and geometry terms in material space [S1].
*   The scattering term in material space is transformed as [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{\rho}(\mathbf{p}_{i-1}, \mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{p}_{i+1}) = \rho(\mathbf{y}_{i-1}, \mathbf{y}_i, \mathbf{y}_{i+1}) J(\mathbf{p}_i)
    $$
    $$
    \hat{G}(\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{p}_{i+1}) = G(\mathbf{y}_i, \mathbf{y}_{i+1})
    $$
    where $\mathbf{y}$ are the spatial coordinates and $J(\mathbf{p}) = |dA(\mathbf{y})/dA(\mathbf{p})|$ is the Jacobian of the map [S1].
*   $\hat{f}'$ is the derivative of the material measurement contribution with respect to $\pi$ [S2].
*   $V_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K)$ is the boundary velocity [S2]:
    $$
    V_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K) = \mathbf{n}_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K) \cdot \frac{d\mathbf{p}_K}{d\pi}
    $$
    where $\mathbf{n}_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K)$ is the unit normal of the visibility boundary (perpendicular to both the surface normal and the tangent of the curve), assumed to point toward the occluded side [S2].
*   The boundary contribution term $\Delta \hat{f}_K$ is defined as [S3, unofficial]:
    $$
    \Delta \hat{f}_K(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) = \hat{f}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \frac{\Delta \hat{g}(\mathbf{p}_K; \mathbf{p}_{K-2}, \mathbf{p}_{K-1})}{\hat{g}(\mathbf{p}_K; \mathbf{p}_{K-2}, \mathbf{p}_{K-1})}
    $$
    where $\Delta \hat{g}$ represents the discontinuity in the visibility function [S3, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Define Material Space:** Establish a fixed reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$ and a parameterization map $X(\mathbf{p}, \pi)$ that maps material points $\mathbf{p}$ to spatial points $\mathbf{x}$ [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Interior Term:** Evaluate the integral over $\hat{\Omega}$ by differentiating the material integrand $\hat{f}$ with respect to $\pi$. This involves computing the derivative of the Jacobian $J$ and the transformed scattering terms $\hat{\rho}$ [S1, S2].
3.  **Identify Boundary Discontinuities:** Determine the set of visibility boundaries $\Delta B(\mathbf{p}_{K-1})$ in material space. These are the curves where visibility jumps occur for fixed $\mathbf{p}_{K-2}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{K-1}$ [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Boundary Term:** Evaluate the integral over $\partial\hat{\Omega}$. For each boundary path, calculate the boundary velocity $V_{\partial}$ using the normal $\mathbf{n}_{\partial}$ and the parameter derivative of the vertex position [S2].
5.  **Sum Terms:** Add the interior and boundary integrals to obtain the total gradient [S3, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def material_form_gradient(path, pi, motion_X, reference_map):
    """
    Computes the gradient of the path integral with respect to pi
    using the material-form differential path integral.
    
    path: list of material vertices [p0, p1, ..., pn]
    pi: scene parameter
    motion_X: function X(p, pi) mapping material to spatial points
    reference_map: function P(p, pi0) mapping material to reference
    """
    
    # 1. Map material path to spatial path
    spatial_path = [motion_X(p, pi) for p in path]
    
    # 2. Compute Jacobian J for each vertex
    # J(p) = |dA(spatial)/dA(material)|
    # In practice, this is computed via the determinant of the Jacobian matrix
    # of the map X with respect to p.
    Js = []
    for p in path:
        J = compute_jacobian_determinant(motion_X, p, pi)
        Js.append(J)
        
    # 3. Compute Interior Term
    # Integral over Omega_hat of d(f_hat)/d(pi)
    # f_hat involves transformed rho and G terms
    interior_integral = 0.0
    for sample in sample_material_space():
        f_hat = compute_material_measurement(sample, Js)
        df_hat_dpi = differentiate_material_measurement(sample, Js, pi)
        interior_integral += df_hat_dpi / pdf_sample_material_space(sample)
        
    # 4. Compute Boundary Term
    # Integral over dOmega_hat of f_hat * V_boundary
    boundary_integral = 0.0
    
    # Identify visibility boundaries (silhouettes) in material space
    # These are discontinuities of g_hat with respect to p_K
    boundary_curves = find_visibility_discontinuities(path, pi)
    
    for curve in boundary_curves:
        # Sample points on the boundary curve
        for p_K in sample_boundary_curve(curve):
            # Compute boundary velocity V_partial
            # V_partial = n_partial . (d p_K / d pi)
            n_partial = compute_visibility_boundary_normal(p_K, path)
            dp_K_dpi = differentiate_vertex_position(p_K, pi)
            V_partial = dot(n_partial, dp_K_dpi)
            
            # Compute f_hat at this boundary path
            # Note: path must be completed to form a valid boundary path
            boundary_path = construct_boundary_path(path, p_K)
            f_hat_boundary = compute_material_measurement(boundary_path, Js)
            
            # Add to integral
            # Measure mu' is differential area-product measure
            boundary_integral += f_hat_boundary * V_partial / pdf_sample_boundary(curve)
            
    return interior_integral + boundary_integral

def compute_jacobian_determinant(motion_X, p, pi):
    """
    Computes J(p) = |dA(x)/dA(p)|
    """
    # Compute the Jacobian matrix of X with respect to p
    J_matrix = compute_jacobian_matrix(motion_X, p, pi)
    return abs(det(J_matrix))

def compute_visibility_boundary_normal(p_K, path):
    """
    Computes n_partial, the unit normal of the visibility boundary.
    Perpendicular to both surface normal and tangent of the curve.
    Points toward the occluded side.
    """
    surface_normal = compute_surface_normal(p_K)
    tangent = compute_tangent_of_discontinuity_curve(p_K)
    n_partial = cross(surface_normal, tangent)
    n_partial = normalize(n_partial)
    
    # Ensure it points toward the occluded side
    # This requires checking visibility from the previous vertex
    if is_visible(path[-2], p_K):
        n_partial = -n_partial
    return n_partial
```

### Parameters
*   $\pi$: The scene parameter (e.g., vertex positions) [S3, unofficial].
*   $X(\cdot, \pi)$: The global parameterization induced by motion [S3, unofficial].
*   $\hat{\Omega}$: The material path space [S3, unofficial].
*   $\partial \hat{\Omega}$: The material boundary path space [S3, unofficial].
*   $\Delta B_K(\pi)$: Discontinuity curves of $\hat{g}$ with respect to $\mathbf{p}_K$ [S3, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{n}_{\partial}(\mathbf{p}_K)$: Unit normal of the visibility boundary, pointing toward the occluded side [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Reciprocity:** The boundary term is not reciprocal due to the unidirectional definition of $\partial M^n(\pi)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Implicit Surfaces:** Obtaining a global parameterization $X$ for implicit surfaces may be difficult, requiring a resort to the spatial form [S3, unofficial].
*   **Static Discontinuities:** If discontinuity curves $\Delta B[J]$ are independent of $\pi$, the boundary term for those discontinuities vanishes [S3, unofficial].
*   **Polygonal Meshes:** Face edges in the reference $B$ are $\pi$-independent and do not need to be included in $\Delta B[\hat{f}]$ [S3, unofficial]. Only visibility-related discontinuities (silhouettes) need boundary integrals [S3, unofficial].
*   **Jacobian Derivatives:** Even when using a local parameterization where $J=1$, the scene derivatives $\dot{J}$ generally remain nonzero [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Material-form reparameterization](8849-material-form-reparameterization.md)
Material-form reparameterization is a technique for differentiating the path integral with respect to scene parameters by mapping evolving geometry to a fixed reference domain, thereby avoiding the direct differentiation of path space boundaries. It sets itself apart from standard path integral differentiation by converting light paths into material paths via a differentiable mapping, allowing the derivative to be computed as an integral over a static material path space plus a boundary term. This method builds on [Material-Form Differential Path Integral](8831-material-form-differential-path-integral.md).

### Local Parameterization via Reference Map
A technique to define a local motion $\hat{X}$ at a fixed parameter $\pi_0$ such that the Jacobian determinant $J$ becomes one, simplifying estimation [S3, unofficial]. The local motion is defined as $\hat{X}(\cdot, \pi) = X(\cdot, \pi) \circ P(\cdot, \pi_0)$, where $P$ is the reference map of motion $X$ [S3, unofficial]. This reduces $\hat{X}(\cdot, \pi)$ to the identity map when $\pi = \pi_0$ [S3, unofficial]. Using $\hat{X}$ allows efficient estimation using previously developed path sampling methods due to the unit Jacobian, whereas setting the reference $\hat{B}$ to $[0,1)^2$ makes the path space equivalent to the primary sample space in MCMC [S3, unofficial]. A pitfall is that scene derivatives $\dot{J}$ generally remain nonzero even when $J=1$ [S3, unofficial].

### Discontinuity Handling in Material Form
Analysis of discontinuity sources in the material-form differential path integral, noting that many jump discontinuities of $g$ with respect to $\mathbf{p}_{n+1}$ do not move with $\pi$, causing normal velocity to vanish [S3, unofficial]. For polygonal meshes, face edges in the reference $B$ are $\pi$-independent and do not need to be included in $\Delta B[\hat{f}]$ [S3, unofficial]. The Jacobian determinant $J$ is constant within faces but discontinuous across boundaries; however, face boundaries are $\pi$-independent and excluded from discontinuity curves [S3, unofficial]. Consequently, only visibility-related discontinuities (silhouette edges) need to be handled with boundary integrals in the material form for polygonal meshes [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method requires sampling both the interior path space and the boundary path space. The boundary term requires silhouette detection, which is computationally expensive [S2].
*   **Memory:** Memory usage depends on the number of samples required for the boundary term. Unlike reverse-mode AD, it does not require storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states [S5].
*   **Strengths:** It handles domain deformation explicitly and reduces the number of discontinuities that need to be handled in the boundary term compared to the spatial form [S3, unofficial]. It is particularly useful for subsurface scattering and implicit surfaces [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** It requires a global parameterization, which may be difficult to obtain for implicit surfaces [S3, unofficial]. The boundary term is not reciprocal [S3, unofficial]. Silhouette detection is challenging [S2].
*   **When to use:** When the scene geometry deforms significantly with respect to parameters, or when differentiating with respect to subsurface scattering parameters [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When a global parameterization is difficult to obtain and the spatial form is more suitable [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the application of the Reynolds Transport Theorem to path integrals in differentiable rendering [S1][S3, unofficial]. It was introduced to handle differentiable translucent rendering with BSSRDF, where the integration domain depends on scene parameters [S1]. The material-form approach was developed to simplify the boundary term by leveraging a fixed reference domain [S1][S3, unofficial]. It builds on earlier work in differentiable path tracing [S1, S2].
