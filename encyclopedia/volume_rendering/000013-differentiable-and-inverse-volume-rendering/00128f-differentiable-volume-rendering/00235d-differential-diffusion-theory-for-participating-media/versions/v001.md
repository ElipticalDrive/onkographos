# Differential Diffusion Theory for Participating Media

Also called: Differential Diffusion Approximation, Differential Diffusion Equation, Differential Diffusion Method, Differential Diffusion Theory, Diffusion Approximation Derivative, Diffusion-based Differentiable Rendering

## Overview
Differential Diffusion Theory is a differentiable rendering framework for participating media that computes arbitrary scene parameter derivatives (density, absorption/scattering coefficients, anisotropic parameters) by solving the differential form of the diffusion equation [S1]. It replaces expensive Monte Carlo path tracing for multiply-scattered light with a PDE solver, enabling real-time optimization of dense media [S1]. Unlike [Differentiable Volumetric Rendering (DVR)](775-differentiable-volumetric-rendering-dvr.md) or [Adjoint Method](789-adjoint-method.md) which rely on path-based transport, this method bypasses path complexity entirely by approximating multiple scattering via diffusion [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Scene parameters $\pi$ (density $\rho$, scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$, absorption coefficient $\sigma_a$, anisotropy $\bar{\mu}$), initial radiance fields $L_d^0, \vec{L}_d^1$, and their previous derivatives $\partial_\pi L_d^0, \partial_\pi \vec{L}_d^1$.
**Outputs:** Derivatives of output radiance $\partial_\pi L_{out}$ with respect to $\pi$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** Uniform 3D grid with integer coordinates $(i, j, k)$, edge length $h$, and position $v_p$.
*   **Voxel Content:** Stores $L_0^d$ (scalar average radiance), $\vec{L}_1^d$ (vector first moment), and their derivatives $\partial_\pi L_0^d, \partial_\pi \vec{L}_1^d$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Pre-computation (Diffusion Solve):**
    Solve the classical diffusion equation for $L_d^0$ and $\vec{L}_d^1$ using the current parameters $\pi$. This provides the base radiance fields required for the derivative calculation [S1].

2.  **Compute Parameter Derivatives:**
    Calculate the derivatives of optical coefficients with respect to $\pi$:
    *   Transport cross section derivative:
        $$ \partial_\pi \sigma_{tr} = \partial_\pi \sigma_s \left(1 - \frac{\bar{\mu}}{3}\right) - \frac{\partial_\pi \bar{\mu} \sigma_s}{3} + \partial_\pi \sigma_a \quad (13) $$
    *   Diffusion coefficient derivative:
        $$ \partial_{\pi} \kappa(x_u) = -\frac{\partial_{\pi} \rho(x_u)}{\sigma_{tr} \rho^2(x_u)} - \frac{\partial_{\pi} \sigma_{tr}}{\sigma_{tr}^2 \rho(x_u)} \quad (12) $$
    *   Albedo derivative:
        $$ \partial_{\pi}\Omega = \frac{\sigma_t\partial_{\pi}\sigma_s - \sigma_s\partial_{\pi}\sigma_t}{(\sigma_t)^2} \quad (9) $$
    *   Anisotropy derivative (for phase function $p(u)$):
        $$ \partial_\pi \bar{\mu} = \frac{3}{2} \int_{-1}^1 \partial_\pi \mu \mu(u) + \mu \partial_\pi p(u) du \quad (10) $$

3.  **Compute Source Derivatives:**
    Calculate the derivative of the source term $S(x_u)$, which depends on the incident radiance $L_{ri}$ and its moments $Q_{ri}^0, \vec{Q}_{ri}^1$:
    $$ \partial_\pi S(x_u) = \partial_\pi \kappa_S(x_u) Q_{ri}^0(x_u) + \kappa_S(x_u) \partial_\pi Q_{ri}^0(x_u) - \frac{\sigma_S}{\sigma_{tr}} \nabla \cdot \partial_\pi \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u) - \frac{\partial_\pi \sigma_S \sigma_{tr} - \partial_\pi \sigma_{tr} \sigma_S}{\sigma_{tr}^2} \nabla \cdot \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u) \quad (14) $$
    Where the moment derivatives are:
    $$ \partial_\pi \mathcal{Q}_{ri}^0(x_u) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \int_{\mathbb{S}^2} \partial_\pi L_{ri}(x_u, \omega') d\omega' \quad (15) $$
    $$ \partial_\pi \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u) = \frac{\partial_\pi \bar{\mu}}{4\pi} \int_{\mathbb{S}^2} L_{ri}(x_u, \omega') \cdot \omega' \, d\omega' + \frac{\bar{\mu}}{4\pi} \int_{\mathbb{S}^2} \partial_\pi L_{ri}(x_u, \omega') \cdot \omega' \, d\omega' \quad (16) $$
    *Note: The method assumes no surface models, neglecting interface integral derivatives and assuming $L_{ri}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{S}^2$ [S1].*

4.  **Solve Differential Diffusion Equation:**
    Solve for $\partial_\pi L_d^0(x_t)$ using the differential diffusion equation (PDE):
    $$ \begin{aligned} & \nabla \partial \kappa(x_t) \cdot \nabla L_d^0(x_t) + \nabla \kappa(x_t) \cdot \nabla \partial L_d^0(x_t) \\ & + \partial_{\kappa} \kappa(x_t) \nabla^2 L_d^0(x_t) + \kappa(x_t) \nabla^2 \partial_{\kappa} L_d^0(x_t) \\ & - \partial_{\kappa} \kappa_d(x_t) L_d^0(x_t) - \kappa_d(x_t) \partial_{\kappa} L_d^0(x_t) + \partial_{\kappa} S(x_t) = 0 \quad (11) \end{aligned} $$
    This is solved iteratively using the **Jacobi method** or **Gauss-Seidel** [S1].
    *   **Initial Condition:** Use the results from the previous iteration ($L_0^d$ and $\partial_\pi L_0^d$) to reduce convergence time [S1].
    *   **Iteration Count:** Typically 500 iterations for convergence in reported experiments [S1].

5.  **Compute First-Order Radiance Derivative:**
    Calculate $\partial_\pi \vec{L}_d^1(x_u)$ using the solved $\partial_\pi L_d^0$:
    $$ \begin{aligned} \partial_\pi \vec{L}_d^1(x_u) &= \partial_\pi \kappa(x_u) (-\nabla L_d^0(x_u) + \kappa_s(x_u) \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u)) \\ &+ \kappa(x_u) (-\nabla \partial_\pi L_d^0(x_u) + \kappa_s(x_u) \partial_\pi \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u)) \\ &+ \partial_\pi \kappa_s(x_u) \vec{Q}_{ri}^1(x_u) \quad (17) \end{aligned} $$

6.  **Compute Multiple Scattering Derivative:**
    Calculate $\partial_\pi J_{ms}$ using the Taylor expansion derivatives:
    $$ \begin{aligned} \partial_{\pi} J_{ms}(x_u, \mathbf{\omega}) &= \partial_{\pi} \Omega(L_d^0(x_u) + \frac{\bar{\mu}}{3} \vec{L}_d^1(x_u) \cdot \mathbf{\omega}) \\ &+ \Omega(\partial_{\pi} L_d^0(x_u) + \frac{\bar{\mu}}{3} \partial_{\pi} \vec{L}_d^1(x_u) \cdot \mathbf{\omega} + \frac{\partial_{\pi} \bar{\mu}}{3} \vec{L}_d^1(x_u) \cdot \mathbf{\omega}) \quad (8) \end{aligned} $$
    Then compute the final multiple scattering radiance derivative $\partial_\pi L_{ms}$:
    $$ \begin{aligned} \partial_{\pi} L_{ms}(x, \omega) &= \int_{D_b}^{D_a} \tau(x_u, x_b) \kappa_I(x_u) \partial_{\pi} J_{ms}(x_u, \omega) du \\ &+ \int_{D_b}^{D_a} (\partial_{\pi} \tau(x_u, x_b) \kappa_I(x_u) + \tau(x_u, x_b) \partial_{\pi} \kappa_I(x_u)) J_{ms}(x_u, \omega) du \quad (7) \end{aligned} $$

7.  **Ray Marching:**
    Use ray marching to estimate the final output radiance $L_{out}$ and its total derivative $\partial_\pi L_{out}$ by combining single scattering and multiple scattering derivatives [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def solve_differential_diffusion(params, prev_L0, prev_dL0, prev_L1, prev_dL1):
    """
    params: dict containing rho, sigma_s, sigma_a, mu_bar, etc.
    prev_L0, prev_dL0: scalar fields from previous iteration
    prev_L1, prev_dL1: vector fields from previous iteration
    """
    # 1. Compute coefficient derivatives
    d_sigma_tr = compute_d_sigma_tr(params) # Eq 13
    d_kappa = compute_d_kappa(params, d_sigma_tr) # Eq 12
    d_Omega = compute_d_Omega(params) # Eq 9
    d_mu_bar = compute_d_mu_bar(params) # Eq 10

    # 2. Compute source derivatives
    # Requires incident radiance Lri and its derivative dLri (from single scattering or boundary)
    d_Q0 = compute_d_Q0(params, dLri) # Eq 15
    d_Q1 = compute_d_Q1(params, Lri, dLri, d_mu_bar) # Eq 16
    d_S = compute_d_S(params, d_kappa, d_Q0, d_Q1, prev_Q1) # Eq 14

    # 3. Solve PDE for dL0 using Jacobi iteration
    # Initialize with previous result to speed up convergence
    dL0 = prev_dL0.copy()
    L0 = prev_L0.copy()
    
    for iteration in range(500):
        # Discretize Eq 11:
        # nabla(d_kappa) . nabla(L0) + nabla(kappa) . nabla(dL0) + ... + d_S = 0
        # Rearrange to solve for dL0 at each voxel
        dL0_new = jacobi_step(dL0, L0, kappa, d_kappa, d_S, h)
        
        # Check convergence or just run fixed iterations
        dL0 = dL0_new
        
    # 4. Compute dL1 using Eq 17
    dL1 = compute_dL1(d_kappa, L0, dL0, kappa, dQ1, d_kappa_s, Q1)

    # 5. Compute dJms using Eq 8
    dJms = compute_dJms(d_Omega, L0, L1, dL0, dL1, d_mu_bar, mu_bar)

    # 6. Return fields for ray marching
    return dL0, dL1, dJms

def compute_d_sigma_tr(params):
    d_sigma_s = params.d_sigma_s
    d_sigma_a = params.d_sigma_a
    d_mu_bar = params.d_mu_bar
    sigma_s = params.sigma_s
    mu_bar = params.mu_bar
    return d_sigma_s * (1 - mu_bar/3) - (d_mu_bar * sigma_s)/3 + d_sigma_a

def compute_d_kappa(params, d_sigma_tr):
    rho = params.rho
    sigma_tr = params.sigma_tr
    d_rho = params.d_rho
    # Eq 12
    return - (d_rho / (sigma_tr * rho**2)) - (d_sigma_tr / (sigma_tr**2 * rho))
```

## Subtopics

### [Volume Rendering with Diffusion](5412-volume-rendering-with-diffusion.md)
Uses diffusion approximation for rendering but does not explicitly derive differentiable gradients for optimization; focuses on forward rendering speed.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of voxels per iteration, but depends on the number of Jacobi iterations required for convergence.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stores scalar and vector radiance fields and their derivatives for all voxels.
*   **Performance:**
    *   GPU implementation is ~140x faster than CPU [S1].
    *   512x512 resolution: 300 iterations in 13.3s (22.5 FPS) [S1].
    *   1024x1024 resolution: 300 iterations in 18.2s (16.4 FPS) [S1].
    *   256x256 resolution: 300 iterations in 10.2s (29.4 FPS) [S1].
    *   ~100x faster than Radiative Backpropagation (RB) for density optimization [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance for dense media; handles >49 million parameters efficiently; avoids Monte Carlo noise [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Approximate gradients; cumulative errors in vacuum/thin regions due to ray marching bias and diffusion approximation limits [S1]. Fails in transparent regions without flux-limited diffusion [S1].
*   **When to use:** Optimization of dense participating media where multiple scattering dominates and speed is critical.
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with significant surface interactions (method omits Rendering Equation) or highly transparent/vacuum regions without flux limiting [S1].

## Background
Originated from the work of Stam [Sta95] who introduced diffusion theory to graphics, and Koerner et al. [KPS*14] who introduced flux-limited diffusion. The specific differentiable framework was proposed by Cen et al. [S1] (2024/2025), implementing it in Taichi [HLY*21]. It addresses the computational bottleneck of Monte Carlo methods for multiply-scattered light derivatives [S1].
