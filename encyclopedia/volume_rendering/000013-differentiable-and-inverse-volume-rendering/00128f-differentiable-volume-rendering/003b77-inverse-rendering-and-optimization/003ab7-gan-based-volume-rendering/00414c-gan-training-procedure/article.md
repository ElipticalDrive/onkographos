# GAN Training Procedure
Also called: Generative Adversarial Networks, Min-Max Game Training

## Overview
A GAN training procedure trains a generator network against a discriminator network to produce perceptually realistic outputs rather than merely minimizing pixelwise error [S2, unofficial]. This perceptual advantage makes GANs well-suited for volume super-resolution, where MSE-optimized outputs tend toward oversmoothing [S2, unofficial]. It builds on [GAN-based Volume Rendering](8825-gan-based-volume-rendering.md) by treating the rendering process as a differentiable mapping from viewpoint and transfer function parameters directly to images, bypassing explicit volume data storage.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A batch of real rendered images, corresponding visualization parameters (viewpoint, transfer function), and synthetic images generated by the generator.
*   **Outputs:** Updated parameters for the generator ($\theta_G$) and discriminator ($\theta_D$).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Generator ($G$):** A neural network mapping visualization parameters to image pixels.
*   **Discriminator ($D$):** A neural network (typically convolutional) mapping images and parameters to a scalar probability of being "real".
*   **Visualization Parameters:** Includes camera pose $\xi$ and transfer function coefficients. In translation GAN variants, an opacity image is also included as input to the generator [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Sample Data:** Randomly sample a batch of visualization parameters $\xi$ from a distribution $p_\xi$ (e.g., Gaussian or uniform on a unit sphere) and retrieve corresponding real images [S3, S1].
2.  **Discriminator Update (Real):** Pass real images and their visualization parameters into the discriminator. Compute the binary cross-entropy loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these images as real (label 1) [S1].
3.  **Synthesize Images:** Pass the visualization parameters (and opacity image, if applicable) through the generator $G$ to synthesize fake images [S1].
4.  **Discriminator Update (Fake):** Pass the synthesized images and visualization parameters into the discriminator. Compute the binary cross-entropy loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these images as fake (label 0) [S1].
5.  **Generator Update:** Pass the visualization parameters through the generator to produce synthetic images. Pass these synthetic images into the discriminator. Compute the loss encouraging the discriminator to predict these synthetic images as real (label 1). This "tricks" the discriminator [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Update parameters $\theta_G$ and $\theta_D$ using minibatch stochastic gradient descent with the ADAM optimizer [S1].

**Formulas:**
The training optimizes the min-max game [S1]:
$$ \min_G \max_D V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))] $$
Where $x$ is a real image, $z$ represents the visualization parameters, and $D(\cdot)$ outputs the probability of being real [S1].

In practice, an L1 loss term may be added to stabilize training and preserve color, weighted by $\lambda$ [S1]:
$$ L_{total} = L_{GAN} + \lambda L_{L1} $$
Typical value: $\lambda = 150$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** ADAM [S1].
*   **Loss Function:** Binary cross-entropy for discriminator; combined GAN/L1 for generator [S1].
*   **$\lambda$:** 150 (for L1 weighting) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Oversmoothing:** Without perceptual losses (GAN objective), outputs tend to be oversmoothed [S2, unofficial].
*   **Training Stability:** Progressive growing strategies help stabilize training by starting at low resolutions [S3].
*   **Data Distribution:** Camera poses must be sampled from a known distribution (e.g., Gaussian for CelebA/Cats, uniform for CARLA) [S3].

## Subtopics

### Progressive GAN Discriminator
A convolutional discriminator that grows progressively in resolution to stabilize training and allow larger batch sizes at lower resolutions [S3]. Training begins at 32×32 resolution. The resolution is doubled twice during training, up to 128 × 128, adding new layers to the discriminator to handle higher resolutions [S3]. Unlike standard ProgressiveGAN where the generator also grows, in this volume rendering context, the generator architecture does not grow; instead, resolution is increased by sampling rays more densely from the same implicit representation [S3]. Final results are rendered at 512 × 512 pixels [S3].

**Scaling:**
Progressive growing allows for larger batch sizes at the beginning of training, which helps to stabilize and speed up training [S3].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Produces perceptually realistic outputs; avoids oversmoothing common in MSE-optimized methods; stabilizes training via progressive growing [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters (e.g., $\lambda$); training can be unstable without progressive strategies [S1][S3].

**When to Use:**
Use when perceptual quality is prioritized over pixel-wise accuracy, particularly in volume super-resolution tasks [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Generative Adversarial Networks were introduced by Goodfellow et al. [S2, unofficial]. In volume rendering, GANs are used to train a generator against a discriminator to produce perceptually realistic outputs [S2, unofficial]. The specific training procedure described here, including the use of ADAM and binary cross-entropy, follows standard GAN training protocols adapted for volume rendering [S1]. Progressive growing was introduced by Karras et al. [S3].
