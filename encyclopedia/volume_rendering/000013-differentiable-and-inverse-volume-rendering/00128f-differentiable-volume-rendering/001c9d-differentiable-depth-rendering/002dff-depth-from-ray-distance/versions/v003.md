# Depth from Ray Distance

Also called: Weighted Median Depth Rendering

## Overview
Depth from Ray Distance computes the depth of a ray by calculating the median of sample depths weighted by their opacity contributions along the ray [S2, unofficial]. It differs from standard depth rendering methods that simply pick the first opaque surface or the mean of all samples by providing a robust estimator that is less sensitive to outliers in the volume density distribution [S2, unofficial]. This method builds on [Differentiable Depth Rendering](1332-differentiable-depth-rendering.md) by providing the specific accumulation logic for depth estimation.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Ray origins and directions (or pixel coordinates and camera intrinsics).
    *   Volume density ($\sigma$) and color ($c$) samples along the ray.
    *   Weights ($w_i$) derived from transmittance and absorption, representing the opacity contribution of each sample.
    *   Sample depths ($d_i$) relative to the camera.
    *   Camera intrinsics matrix $K$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   A depth map where each pixel contains the weighted median depth value [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Samples:** A list or tensor of tuples/structs containing $(d_i, \sigma_i, c_i)$ for $i=0 \dots N-1$, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Weights Tensor:** A tensor of shape $(N)$ containing the computed weights $w_i$.
*   **Depths Tensor:** A tensor of shape $(N)$ containing the distances $d_i$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel $(x, y)$, compute the normalized homogeneous coordinate $(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y})$ using the inverse camera intrinsics $K^{-1}$ [S1]:
    $$
    \begin{bmatrix} \tilde{x} \\ \tilde{y} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
    $$
2.  **Sample Traversal:** Trace the ray through the volume to obtain $N$ samples. For each sample $i$, compute the weight $w_i$ based on the volume rendering equation components (transmittance $T_i$ and density $\sigma_i$) [S2, unofficial]. The weight represents the probability that the ray terminates at sample $i$.
3.  **Depth Calculation:** Compute the depth $z_c$ for the ray. If using the weighted median strategy:
    *   Sort the samples based on their depths $d_i$.
    *   Accumulate the weights $w_i$ in sorted order.
    *   Find the sample index $k$ where the cumulative weight first exceeds 0.5 (the median).
    *   The depth is $d_k$.
    *   Alternatively, if the source implies a direct conversion from ray distance $d$ (total length) to camera-space depth $z_c$:
        $$
        z_c = \frac{d}{\sqrt{\tilde{x}^2 + \tilde{y}^2 + 1}}
        $$
        where $d$ is the ray distance [S1]. Note: [S2, unofficial] specifies the output is the *median* of sample depths weighted by opacity, while [S1] provides a formula for converting a scalar ray distance $d$ to camera depth $z_c$. The weighted median approach is the primary definition for this method's alias [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Execution:** The depth output is computed by calling the renderer with the weights and ray samples. This execution is performed without gradient tracking during the forward pass if used for debugging or visualization, but gradients are handled by the parent Differentiable Depth Rendering framework [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Iteration:** In multi-stage rendering (e.g., proposal networks), iterate over each proposal network iteration. Apply a depth colormap to the depth output from the $i$-th proposal network, using the accumulation for normalization [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `method`: The strategy for computing depth. Value `'median'` uses the weighted median depth [S2, unofficial].
*   `sigma_thresh`: Not used in the standard weighted median method, but relevant for variants like Sigma-Threshold Depth Rendering [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Zero Weights:** If all weights are zero (empty volume), the median is undefined. Handle by returning a default value or NaN [G].
*   **Precision:** Sorting operations for the median can be computationally expensive for large $N$. Use efficient sorting algorithms or approximations if performance is critical [G].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Ensure ray bounds are correctly defined to avoid sampling outside the volume of interest [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Expected Term Rendering](5952-expected-term-rendering.md)
Expected Term Rendering computes the expected termination distance (depth) for each ray traversing a volume, providing a differentiable depth map output. It differs from standard depth rendering by calculating the weighted mean of sample depths along the ray, where weights are derived from the volume's transmittance and absorption properties, rather than simply picking the first opaque surface or the mean of all samples. This method builds on [Depth from Ray Distance](5665-depth-from-ray-distance.md) by specifically implementing the "expected" strategy for depth estimation.

### Sigma-Threshold Depth Rendering
Sigma-Threshold Depth Rendering renders a binary or thresholded output based on whether the volume density ($\sigma$) exceeds a given threshold along the ray [S3, unofficial]. It computes depth based on a sigma threshold, referencing the method used in Dex-NeRF [S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Get `ray_id`, create `SingleRaySpec`, and find bounds [S3, unofficial].
2.  Call `trace_ray_sigma_thresh` with `sigma_thresh` [S3, unofficial].
3.  Create a result tensor of dtype and device matching `rays.origins`, size `{rays.origins.size(0)}`, with `requires_grad=false` [S3, unofficial].
4.  Launch `device::render_ray_sigma_thresh_kernel` with blocks calculated by `CUDA_N_BLOCKS_NEEDED(Q, TRACE_RAY_CUDA_THREADS)`. Pass `grid`, `rays`, `opt`, `sigma_thresh`, and `results.data_ptr<float>()` [S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `sigma_thresh`: A float threshold value for density comparison [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The weighted median calculation requires sorting the samples, which is $O(N \log N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of samples [G]. The sigma-threshold method is $O(N)$ as it involves a simple comparison [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray to store samples and weights [G].
*   **Strengths:** The weighted median is robust to outliers in the volume density, providing cleaner depth maps in noisy or sparse volumes [S2, unofficial]. The sigma-threshold method is simple and fast, suitable for binary segmentation tasks [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The weighted median is less smooth than the expected value (mean) method, potentially leading to discontinuities in the depth map [G]. The sigma-threshold method is sensitive to the choice of threshold and may produce noisy results if the threshold is not well-calibrated [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Use weighted median when robustness to outliers is required. Use sigma-threshold for binary depth estimation or when a clear density boundary exists.

## Background
The concept of differentiable depth rendering has been explored in various forms, including sphere tracing for signed distance fields [S1]. The weighted median approach is specifically highlighted in the context of generative neural radiance fields (GANeRF) for debugging and analysis [S2, unofficial]. The sigma-threshold method is associated with Dex-NeRF and implemented in frameworks like Svox2 [S3, unofficial].
