# Scene Derivatives for Evolving Surfaces

Also called: Material-Form Parameterization for Implicit Surfaces, Normal Scene Derivative, Reynolds transport relation for evolving surfaces, Scene Derivative, Surface Evolution and Scene Derivatives, Transport Relation for Differentiating Surface Integrals

## Overview
Scene Derivatives for Evolving Surfaces is a mathematical framework for differentiating integrals over implicit surfaces that change shape with respect to scene parameters $\pi$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of computing the gradient of rendering path integrals where the geometry is defined as a zero-level set $\phi(\mathbf{x}; \pi) = 0$, by decomposing the derivative into an interior term (accounting for field changes and surface curvature) and a boundary term (accounting for discontinuities) [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard Reynolds transport formulations by explicitly handling the parameterization dependence of tangential velocity and providing a parameterization-independent "normal scene derivative" for implicit surfaces, building on [Reynolds Transport Theorem for Differentiable Rendering](6839-reynolds-transport-theorem-for-differentiable-rendering.md).

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   $\pi$: An abstract parameter controlling global scene geometry (e.g., shape deformation, time) [S2, unofficial].
*   $\phi(\mathbf{x}; \pi)$: A scalar-valued function defining the evolving implicit surface $M(\pi) = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \phi(\mathbf{x}; \pi) = 0\}$ [S1].
*   $\varphi(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: A scalar spatial field defined on the trajectory $T$ of the surface [S2, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: The unit normal field of $M(\pi)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\kappa(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: The total curvature (sum of principal curvatures) of $M(\pi)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\Delta_M$: The set of discontinuity curves on $M(\pi)$ where $\varphi$ is discontinuous [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   $\dot{\varphi}(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: The scene derivative (parameterization-dependent) [S2, unofficial].
*   $\square \varphi(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: The normal scene derivative (parameterization-independent) [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{d}{d\pi} \int_{M(\pi)} \varphi \, dA$: The derivative of the surface integral [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method relies on the following geometric and kinematic quantities:
1.  **Reference Surface $B$**: A fixed surface $B = M(0)$ [S1].
2.  **Deformation Map $X(\mathbf{p}, \pi)$**: Maps a material point $\mathbf{p} \in B$ to a spatial point $\mathbf{x} \in M(\pi)$ [S2, unofficial]. For implicit surfaces, this is often approximated as $X(\mathbf{p}, \pi) = \mathbf{p} + t(\mathbf{p}, \pi)\mathbf{n}_B(\mathbf{p})$, where $t$ is the distance along the normal $\mathbf{n}_B$ to the intersection with $M(\pi)$ [S1].
3.  **Local Parameterization $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi')$**: A local coordinate map from an open set $\mathcal{O} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ to $M(\pi')$ near a point $\mathbf{x}$ [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Velocity Fields**:
    *   $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$: Local velocity of the surface point [S2, unofficial].
    *   $V = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{n}$: Scalar normal velocity (parameterization-independent) [S2, unofficial].
    *   $\mathbf{v}_{tan} = \mathbf{v} - V\mathbf{n}$: Local tangential velocity (parameterization-dependent) [S2, unofficial].
    *   $V_{\Delta_M}$: Scalar normal velocity of the discontinuity curve $\Delta_M$ [S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Define the Reference Configuration**:
    Establish the reference surface $B = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \phi(\mathbf{x}; 0) = 0\}$ [S1].

2.  **Compute Local Velocity**:
    For a point $\mathbf{x} \in M(\pi)$, determine the local velocity $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, \pi)$ using the local parameterization $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi')$:
    $$
    \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, \pi) = \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi')}{\partial \pi'} \bigg|_{\pi'=\pi}
    $$
    where $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ are the local coordinates of $\mathbf{x}$ [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Decompose Velocity**:
    Calculate the scalar normal velocity $V$ and tangential velocity $\mathbf{v}_{tan}$:
    $$
    V = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{n}
    $$
    $$
    \mathbf{v}_{tan} = \mathbf{v} - V\mathbf{n}
    $$
    Note that $V$ is independent of the parameterization, while $\mathbf{v}_{tan}$ depends on it [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Scene Derivative**:
    Calculate the parameterization-dependent scene derivative $\dot{\varphi}$:
    $$
    \dot{\varphi}(\mathbf{x}, \pi) = \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi'} \varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi'), \pi') \bigg|_{\pi'=\pi}
    $$
    This represents the rate of change of the field $\varphi$ following the parameterization [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Compute Normal Scene Derivative**:
    Calculate the parameterization-independent normal scene derivative $\square \varphi$:
    $$
    \square \varphi = \dot{\varphi} - \mathbf{v}_{tan} \cdot \text{grad}_M(\varphi)
    $$
    where $\text{grad}_M(\varphi)$ is the surface gradient of $\varphi$ [S2, unofficial].

6.  **Evaluate Discontinuity Jump**:
    For points on the discontinuity curve $\Delta_M$, compute the jump $\Delta \varphi$:
    $$
    \Delta \varphi(\mathbf{x}, \pi) = \varphi^-(\mathbf{x}, \pi) - \varphi^+(\mathbf{x}, \pi)
    $$
    where $\varphi^-$ and $\varphi^+$ are the one-sided limits approaching $\mathbf{x}$ from directions $-\mathbf{n}_{\Delta_M}$ and $\mathbf{n}_{\Delta_M}$ respectively [S2, unofficial].

7.  **Compute Integral Derivative**:
    Apply the transport relation to find the derivative of the integral:
    $$
    \frac{d}{d\pi} \int_{M(\pi)} \varphi \, dA = \int_{M(\pi)} (\square \varphi - \varphi \kappa V) \, dA + \int_{\partial M(\pi)} \Delta \varphi V_{\partial M} \, d\ell
    $$
    where $\partial M(\pi)$ denotes the boundary of the surface or discontinuity curves, $dA$ is the area measure, and $d\ell$ is the curve length measure [S2, unofficial].

### Formulas
*   **Evolving Surface**: $M(\pi) = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3 : \phi(\mathbf{x}; \pi) = 0\}$ [S1].
*   **Reference Surface**: $B = M(0)$ [S1].
*   **Deformation (Implicit Approximation)**: $X(\mathbf{p}, \pi) = \mathbf{p} + t(\mathbf{p}, \pi)\mathbf{n}_B(\mathbf{p})$ [S1].
*   **Local Velocity**: $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, \pi) = \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi')}{\partial \pi'} \big|_{\pi'=\pi}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Velocity**: $V = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{n}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Tangential Velocity**: $\mathbf{v}_{tan} = \mathbf{v} - V\mathbf{n}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Scene Derivative**: $\dot{\varphi}(\mathbf{x}, \pi) = \frac{\partial}{\partial \pi'} \varphi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}, \pi'), \pi') \big|_{\pi'=\pi}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Normal Scene Derivative**: $\square \varphi = \dot{\varphi} - \mathbf{v}_{tan} \cdot \text{grad}_M(\varphi)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Transport Relation**: $\frac{d}{d\pi} \int_{M} \varphi dA = \int_{M} (\square \varphi - \varphi \kappa V) dA + \int_{\partial M} \Delta \varphi V_{\partial M} d\ell$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Static Surface Simplification**: If $V=0$, $\frac{d}{d\pi} \int_{M} \varphi dA = \int_{M} \dot{\varphi} dA + \int_{\Delta_M} \Delta \varphi V_{\Delta_M} d\ell$ [S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   $\pi$: Abstract scene parameter. Typical values depend on the specific application (e.g., time steps, shape coefficients) [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Zero Tangential Velocity**: If the parameterization results in $\mathbf{v}_{tan} = 0$, then $\square \varphi = \dot{\varphi}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Static Surface with Moving Discontinuities**: Even if the surface is static ($V=0$), the velocity of discontinuity curves $V_{\Delta_M}$ may be nonzero because the discontinuity curves themselves can depend on $\pi$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameterization Dependence**: The scene derivative $\dot{\varphi}$ depends on the choice of local parameterization $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, whereas the normal scene derivative $\square \varphi$ does not [S2, unofficial].
*   **Implicit Mapping Limitations**: Defining a global one-to-one mapping $X(\cdot, \pi)$ for all $\pi$ for implicit surfaces is hard or impossible; the method often relies on local parameterizations or the specific normal-offset approximation $X(\mathbf{p}, \pi) = \mathbf{p} + t(\mathbf{p}, \pi)\mathbf{n}_B(\mathbf{p})$ which is only one-to-one at $\pi=0$ [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_scene_derivatives(phi, x, pi, local_param_func, grad_M_func):
    """
    Computes the scene derivative and normal scene derivative for a scalar field
    on an evolving implicit surface.

    Args:
        phi: Function phi(x, pi) returning the scalar field value.
        x: Spatial point on M(pi).
        pi: Current parameter value.
        local_param_func: Function xi_to_x(xi, pi_prime) returning the spatial point
                          for local coordinates xi at parameter pi_prime.
                          Inverse: x_to_xi(x, pi) returns local coordinates.
        grad_M_func: Function grad_M_phi(x, pi) returning the surface gradient of phi.

    Returns:
        dot_phi: Scene derivative (parameterization-dependent).
        square_phi: Normal scene derivative (parameterization-independent).
        v: Local velocity vector.
        v_tan: Tangential velocity vector.
        V: Normal velocity.
    """
    # 1. Get local coordinates of x at current pi
    xi = x_to_xi(x, pi)

    # 2. Compute local velocity v by differentiating the parameterization
    # v = d/d(pi') x_hat(xi, pi') |_{pi'=pi}
    # Approximated using finite differences or automatic differentiation
    epsilon = 1e-5
    x_plus = local_param_func(xi, pi + epsilon)
    x_minus = local_param_func(xi, pi - epsilon)
    v = (x_plus - x_minus) / (2 * epsilon)

    # 3. Compute normal n and curvature kappa at x, pi
    # n = grad(phi) / |grad(phi)| evaluated at x, pi
    grad_phi = compute_gradient_phi(x, pi)
    n = grad_phi / norm(grad_phi)
    # kappa is the sum of principal curvatures, requires Hessian of phi
    kappa = compute_total_curvature(phi, x, pi)

    # 4. Decompose velocity
    V = dot(v, n)
    v_tan = v - V * n

    # 5. Compute scene derivative dot_phi
    # dot_phi = d/d(pi') phi(x_hat(xi, pi'), pi') |_{pi'=pi}
    phi_plus = phi(x_plus, pi + epsilon)
    phi_minus = phi(x_minus, pi - epsilon)
    dot_phi = (phi_plus - phi_minus) / (2 * epsilon)

    # 6. Compute normal scene derivative square_phi
    grad_M_phi = grad_M_func(x, pi)
    square_phi = dot_phi - dot(v_tan, grad_M_phi)

    return dot_phi, square_phi, v, v_tan, V

def compute_integral_derivative(phi, M, pi, dot_phi_func, square_phi_func,
                                kappa_func, V_func, discontinuity_curves,
                                delta_phi_func, V_delta_func):
    """
    Computes the derivative of the integral of phi over M(pi) using the
    transport relation.

    Args:
        M: The surface M(pi).
        pi: Current parameter value.
        dot_phi_func: Function to compute dot_phi(x, pi).
        square_phi_func: Function to compute square_phi(x, pi).
        kappa_func: Function to compute kappa(x, pi).
        V_func: Function to compute V(x, pi).
        discontinuity_curves: List of discontinuity curves on M(pi).
        delta_phi_func: Function to compute delta_phi(x, pi) on discontinuities.
        V_delta_func: Function to compute V_delta(x, pi) on discontinuities.

    Returns:
        d_integral_d_pi: The derivative of the integral.
    """
    # Interior term: integral over M of (square_phi - phi * kappa * V) dA
    interior_integral = 0.0
    for point, area in M.sample_points_and_areas():
        phi_val = phi(point, pi)
        sq_phi = square_phi_func(point, pi)
        k = kappa_func(point, pi)
        V = V_func(point, pi)
        integrand = sq_phi - phi_val * k * V
        interior_integral += integrand * area

    # Boundary term: integral over discontinuity curves of delta_phi * V_delta dL
    boundary_integral = 0.0
    for curve in discontinuity_curves:
        for point, length in curve.sample_points_and_lengths():
            delta_phi = delta_phi_func(point, pi)
            V_delta = V_delta_func(point, pi)
            boundary_integral += delta_phi * V_delta * length

    d_integral_d_pi = interior_integral + boundary_integral
    return d_integral_d_pi
```

## Subtopics

### Reynolds Transport Theorem for Differentiable Rendering
The parent method that decomposes the derivative of a rendering integral into interior and boundary terms. Scene Derivatives provide the specific kinematic definitions for implicit surfaces within this framework. [Reynolds Transport Theorem for Differentiable Rendering](6839-reynolds-transport-theorem-for-differentiable-rendering.md)

### Path-Space Differentiable Rendering
The broader context of differentiating path integrals directly. Scene Derivatives are a key component for handling geometry changes in this approach. [Path-Space Differentiable Rendering](52-path-space-differentiable-rendering.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Computing the scene derivatives requires evaluating the local parameterization and its derivative with respect to $\pi$, as well as the surface gradient of $\varphi$. This is typically $O(1)$ per sample point, but depends on the cost of evaluating $\phi$ and its derivatives. The integral evaluation is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of samples.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per sample, plus storage for the surface representation and field data.
*   **Strengths**: Provides a rigorous, parameterization-independent normal scene derivative for implicit surfaces. Handles discontinuities explicitly. Integrates naturally with path-space differentiable rendering.
*   **Limitations**: Requires a local parameterization or deformation map, which can be difficult to define globally for complex implicit surfaces. The tangential velocity component is parameterization-dependent, which can introduce artifacts if the parameterization is poor. Computing curvature and surface gradients can be numerically unstable.
*   **When to Use**: When differentiating rendering integrals over implicit surfaces that evolve with parameters, especially when discontinuities are present.
*   **When Not to Use**: For explicit meshes where standard mesh deformation derivatives are easier to compute. For static scenes where $\pi$ does not affect geometry.

## Background
The method originates from the application of continuum mechanics concepts, specifically the Reynolds transport theorem for evolving surfaces, to differentiable rendering [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the work of Cermelli et al. [2005] in fluid mechanics and Zhang et al. [2020] in differentiable rendering of implicit surfaces [S1][S2, unofficial]. The parameter $\pi$ is analogous to time in physics but represents abstract scene parameters in rendering [S2, unofficial]. The approach was formalized in the context of path-space differentiable rendering to handle the differentiation of surface integrals over evolving implicit geometries [S1][S2, unofficial].
