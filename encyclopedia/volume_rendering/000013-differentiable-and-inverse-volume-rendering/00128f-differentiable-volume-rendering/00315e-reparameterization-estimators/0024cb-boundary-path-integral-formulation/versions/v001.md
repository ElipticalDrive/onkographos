# Boundary Path Integral Formulation

Also called: Reparameterized Boundary Path Integral, Interior-to-Boundary Reparameterization, Volume Boundary Path Integral Formulation, Boundary Path Integral Formulation for Differentiable Rendering, Zhang et al. 2020 formulation, Zhang et al. 2020 reparameterization

## Overview
The Boundary Path Integral Formulation is a method for computing the derivative of differentiable volume rendering with respect to object shape parameters by converting volume integrals into boundary area integrals using the divergence theorem [S1]. It solves the problem of expensive interior mapping evaluations by eliminating the need to specify change rates in the interior of objects, relying instead on boundary sampling [S1]. Unlike [Reparameterized Volume Sampling](3309-reparameterized-volume-sampling.md) which samples the volume directly, this method moves the differentiation burden to the material boundary path space [S1].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Scene parameter $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ controlling the shape of translucent objects [S1].
*   Reference configuration $B$ (typically $B = M(\theta_0)$ when estimating gradient at $\theta_0$) [S2, unofficial].
*   Mapping $X(\cdot, \theta)$: A differentiable one-to-one mapping from the reference configuration $B$ to the scene geometry $M(\theta)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   Assumptions:
    *   S.1: Translucent objects contain no refractive or reflective boundaries (null BSDFs) and there are no visibility boundaries (shadows) dependent on $\theta$ [S1].
    *   S.2: $\theta$ controls only shapes, not material properties (extinction/scattering coefficients) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Gradient $\frac{dI}{d\theta}$ decomposed into interior and boundary components [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Reference Configuration ($B$):** Defined as $B_V = V(0)$ and $B_M = M(0)$ [S1]. The mapping $X(\cdot, 0)$ is the identity map, and the Jacobian $J(p, 0) = 1$ [S1].
*   **Material Path Space ($\hat{\Omega}$):** $\hat{\Omega} = \cup_{N \ge 1} B^{N+1}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Volume Boundary Path Space ($\partial \hat{\Omega}$):** Comprises paths $\vec{p} = (p_0, \dots, p_N)$ containing exactly one volume boundary vertex $p_K$ residing on the boundary $\partial B_V$ of the reference volume for some $0 < K < N$ [S1]. Paths with two or more vertices on the boundary have zero measure [S1].

### Formulas
The forward rendering intensity is:
$$ I = \int_{\Omega} f(\vec{x}) \, d\mu(\vec{x}) $$
where $\Omega := \cup_{N \ge 1} M^{*N+1}$ is the path space of all light transport paths $\vec{x} = (x_0, \dots, x_N)$ [S2, unofficial].

Reparameterization maps ordinary light path $\vec{x}$ to material path $\vec{p}$ via $x_n = X(p_n, \theta)$ [S2, unofficial]. The integral becomes:
$$ I = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \hat{f}(\vec{p}) \, d\mu(\vec{p}) $$
where $\hat{f}$ captures the ordinary measurement contribution and the Jacobian from reparameterization [S2, unofficial].

The gradient is the sum of an interior term and a boundary term [S2, unofficial]:
$$ \frac{dI}{d\theta} = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \frac{d\hat{f}(\vec{p})}{d\theta} d\mu(\vec{p}) + \int_{\partial\hat{\Omega}} \hat{f}^B \hat{f}^S \hat{f}^D d\mu $$
The boundary term is unique to differentiable rendering [S2, unofficial].

The core interior-to-boundary conversion for a function $h$ (where $h$ does not directly depend on $\theta$) is derived as follows. First, the chain rule expansion for the derivative of $h$ with respect to $\theta$ is [S1]:
$$ d_{\theta} h(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \sum_{n=0}^N \left\{ \frac{\partial h}{\partial \mathbf{x}_n} \right\}_{\mathbf{x}_n=\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{p}_n, 0)} \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{p}_n) $$
where $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{p}) := d_{\theta} \mathbf{X}(\mathbf{p}, \theta)$ is the velocity vector field [S1].

The product rule expansion for the Jacobian product $J = \prod_{n=0}^N J(\mathbf{p}_n, \theta)$ is [S1]:
$$ \partial_{\theta} \prod_{n=0}^N J(\mathbf{p}_n, \theta) = \sum_{n=0}^N \partial_{\theta} J(\mathbf{p}_n, \theta) $$
Thus, the combined derivative of the path weight is [S1]:
$$ d_{\Theta} \left( h(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \prod_{n=0}^N J(\mathbf{p}_n, \Theta) \right) = \sum_{n=0}^N ((\nabla_n h)(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{p}_n) + h(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \partial_{\Theta} J(\mathbf{p}_n, \Theta)) $$

Using the divergence theorem, the interior integral over volume $\mathcal{B}_V$ is converted to a boundary integral [S1]:
$$ \int_{\mathcal{B}_V} ((\nabla h)(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{p}_n) + h(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) \partial_\theta J(\mathbf{p}_n, \theta)) d\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{p}_n) = \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_V} h(\bar{\mathbf{p}}) v_\perp(\mathbf{p}_n) d\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{p}_n) $$
where $v_\perp$ is the velocity component perpendicular to the boundary [S1].

This yields the main result, converting the differential path integral into interior and boundary components [S1]:
$$ \int_{\hat{\Omega}} d_{\theta} \hat{f}(\vec{p}) d\mu(\vec{p}) = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \text{interior} f(\vec{p}) d\mu(\vec{p}) + \int_{\partial \hat{\Omega}} f(\vec{p}) v_{\perp}(\mathbf{p}_K) d\dot{\mu}(\vec{p}) $$
where $\partial \hat{\Omega}$ is the volume boundary path space [S1].

The measure for the boundary integral is defined as [S1]:
$$ d\dot{\mu}(\vec{p}) = dA(\mathbf{p}_K) \prod_{n \neq K} \begin{cases} dV(\mathbf{p}_n), & (\mathbf{p}_n \in \mathcal{B}_Y) \\ dA(\mathbf{p}_n), & (\mathbf{p}_n \in \mathcal{B}_M) \end{cases} $$
where $\mathcal{B}_Y$ is volume boundary and $\mathcal{B}_M$ is surface boundary [S1].

If the integrand $h$ directly depends on $\theta$, an extra interior integral term containing the partial derivative $\partial_\theta h$ is introduced [S1].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode implements the evaluation of the boundary path integral component for a single path $\vec{p}$ with boundary vertex index $K$.

```python
def compute_boundary_contribution(p, K, theta, X, v_perp, f):
    """
    Computes the contribution of the boundary path integral for a specific path.
    
    Args:
        p: Material path (p_0, ..., p_N).
        K: Index of the volume boundary vertex (0 < K < N).
        theta: Scene parameter.
        X: Mapping function X(p, theta).
        v_perp: Scalar field for normal velocity at p_K.
        f: Measurement contribution function evaluated at the path.
        
    Returns:
        The boundary integral contribution for this path.
    """
    # Check if path has exactly one boundary vertex at K
    # In practice, this is handled by the sampler generating paths in partial Omega
    if not (0 < K < len(p) - 1):
        return 0.0
        
    # Evaluate the measurement contribution f(p)
    # Note: f(p) may vary with theta even if p is fixed, due to transmittance
    # crossing null interfaces.
    contribution = f(p, theta)
    
    # Get the normal velocity at the boundary vertex
    # v_perp is precomputed or computed on the fly based on X and theta
    vp = v_perp(p[K], theta)
    
    # The integrand is f(p) * v_perp(p_K)
    # The measure dA(p_K) is handled by the sampling density
    return contribution * vp

def compute_gradient_boundary_term(paths, K, theta, X, v_perp, f, density):
    """
    Estimates the boundary integral using Monte Carlo sampling.
    
    Args:
        paths: List of sampled material paths in partial Omega.
        K: Index of the boundary vertex for all paths.
        theta: Scene parameter.
        X: Mapping function.
        v_perp: Normal velocity field.
        f: Measurement contribution function.
        density: Probability density function for sampling the boundary paths.
        
    Returns:
        Estimated gradient component from the boundary term.
    """
    total = 0.0
    for p in paths:
        # Compute the weight for the boundary integral
        # Weight = (f(p) * v_perp(p_K)) / density(p)
        # Note: The measure d(mu_dot) includes dA(p_K) and volume/surface measures for other vertices.
        # The density must account for the full measure d(mu_dot).
        
        # Evaluate f(p)
        fp = f(p, theta)
        
        # Evaluate normal velocity
        vp = v_perp(p[K], theta)
        
        # Evaluate density for the specific path configuration
        # This density must correspond to the measure d(mu_dot) defined in Eq 35
        dp = density(p, K)
        
        if dp > 0:
            weight = (fp * vp) / dp
            total += weight
            
    return total / len(paths)
```

### Parameters
*   $\theta$: Scene parameter. Assumed scalar $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ for simplicity, generalizable to $\mathbb{R}^m$ [S1]. Derivatives are evaluated at $\theta = 0$ [S1].
*   $K$: Index of the volume boundary vertex. Must satisfy $0 < K < N$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Multiple Boundary Vertices:** Paths with two or more vertices constrained on the boundary have zero measure and make no contribution to the first-order derivatives [S1].
*   **Visibility Boundaries:** The derivation assumes no reflective/refractive or volume shadow boundaries (Assumption S.1) to ensure material measurement contribution is continuous in the interior of $B_V$, required for the divergence theorem [S1]. If violated, the velocity of visibility boundaries is nontrivial to compute without tetrahedralization [S1].
*   **Interior Mapping:** Previously, tetrahedral meshes were used to interpolate the mapping $X(\cdot, \theta)$ into the interior, introducing high computational overhead due to frequent tetrahedralizations and point-in-tetrahedron lookups [S1]. This method avoids that [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the Monte Carlo estimator used (unidirectional, bidirectional, MIS). Avoids the $O(N)$ interior mapping evaluations per vertex, reducing overhead compared to tetrahedralization-based methods [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the reference configuration and the mapping $X$. Does not require tetrahedral meshes for the interior [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Validated against finite-difference gradients and compared against Zhang et al.'s Path-Space Differentiable Rendering (PSDR) and Differential Theory of Radiative Transfer (DTRT) [S1]. Inverse rendering compared against Nimier-David et al.'s voxel-based approach [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates the need for expensive interior mapping evaluations and tetrahedralizations [S1]. Enables the use of sophisticated Monte Carlo estimators beyond unidirectional path tracing [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes no refractive/reflective boundaries or $\theta$-dependent visibility boundaries (Assumption S.1) [S1]. Cannot handle visibility discontinuities without significant modification [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Differentiable volume rendering of translucent objects with respect to shape parameters, where objects have null BSDFs and no $\theta$-dependent shadows [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with refractive/reflective boundaries or complex visibility changes dependent on $\theta$ [S1].

## Background
The Boundary Path Integral Formulation was introduced by Zhang et al. in 2020 [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is part of the differential path integral framework that formulates both interior and boundary components as full path integrals [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the reparameterization technique using a reference configuration independent of $\theta$ [S2, unofficial]. The method was developed to address the computational overhead of tetrahedralization-based interior mapping in previous works like Zhang et al. 2021 (PSDR) [S1]. It is used in differentiable rendering and inverse rendering of translucent objects [S1].
