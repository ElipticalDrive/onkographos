# Plenoxels Optimization Configuration

Also called: Plenoxels optimizer settings, RMSProp vs SGD for Plenoxels

## Overview
Plenoxels Optimization Configuration defines the hyperparameter settings and training schedules required to optimize the voxel opacity ($\sigma$) and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients of a Plenoxels radiance field. It solves the problem of converging to a high-fidelity appearance and geometry representation from multi-view images without using neural networks. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Coarse-to-Fine Optimization with Pruning](1153-coarse-to-fine-optimization-with-pruning.md) by focusing on the specific optimizer choice and learning rate dynamics for a fixed-resolution grid, and from [HDR-Plenoxels](12123-hdr-plenoxels.md) by targeting standard dynamic range scenes.

## How it works
The configuration specifies the gradient-based optimization routine applied to the voxel grid parameters.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images, camera poses, initial voxel grid (typically initialized via space carving or density estimation), and hyperparameters (optimizer type, learning rates, schedules).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized voxel opacity values ($\sigma$) and spherical harmonic coefficients for each active voxel.

**Data Structures:**
The optimization targets two primary sets of parameters stored in the voxel grid:
1.  **Opacity ($\sigma$):** A scalar value per voxel representing density.
2.  **Spherical Harmonic Coefficients:** A vector of coefficients per voxel representing directional color/appearance. For degree $L$, this requires $(L+1)^2$ coefficients per voxel.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the voxel grid with opacity and SH coefficients. Common initialization involves space carving to determine active voxels and setting initial SH coefficients to zero or mean color [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Sampling:** For each training iteration, sample rays through the voxel grid. Compute transmittance and accumulated color using the current $\sigma$ and SH coefficients [S1].
3.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the photometric loss (e.g., MSE) between rendered images and ground truth images. Regularization terms (TV loss, sparsity) may be added [S1].
4.  **Backpropagation:** Compute gradients of the loss with respect to $\sigma$ and SH coefficients.
5.  **Parameter Update:** Update $\sigma$ and SH coefficients using the specified optimizer and learning rate schedule.

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is:
$$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance, $\delta_i$ is the step length, $\sigma_i$ is the opacity, and $c_i$ is the color computed from SH coefficients at the sample point.

**Parameters and Typical Values:**
*   **Optimizer:** RMSProp is preferred over SGD, particularly for optimizing SH coefficients [S1].
*   **Learning Rate Schedule:** A delayed schedule for $\sigma$ and a constant schedule for SH yields the best results [S1].
*   **Resolution:** Experiments often use $256^3$ resolution [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Optimizer Choice:** Using SGD for both $\sigma$ and SH results in significantly lower quality (PSNR 29.35 vs 30.57) [S1].
*   **Schedule Mismatch:** Using a constant schedule for both parameters yields lower quality than the delayed/constant combination (PSNR 30.13 vs 30.57) [S1].
*   **Robustness:** The method is noted to be robust to variations in optimizer and schedule, but optimal performance requires specific tuning [S1].

## Subtopics

### Space Carving and Opaque Voxels Optimization with Spherical Harmonics
A technique composed of two steps: space carving and opaque voxels optimization, used to generate neural radiance fields based on plenoxels [S2, unofficial]. Spherical harmonics of degree 9 are used to render the shading of each voxel [S2, unofficial]. Running the implementation `main_carve_sph.py` takes approximately 4 minutes on a single GPU [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of voxels, samples per ray, and SH degree. RMSProp is generally more computationally expensive per step than SGD due to momentum tracking, but converges faster [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the number of voxels times the size of SH coefficients. For degree 9, this is significant [S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On 8 synthetic NeRF scenes at $256^3$ resolution, RMSProp for both SH and $\sigma$ achieves PSNR 30.57, SSIM 0.950, LPIPS 0.065 [S1]. SGD for both drops to PSNR 29.35, SSIM 0.932, LPIPS 0.087 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** RMSProp provides better convergence for SH coefficients. The method is robust to variations in optimizer and schedule [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of learning rate schedules for optimal performance. High SH degrees increase memory and compute costs [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When optimizing Plenoxels for standard dynamic range scenes and high fidelity is required.
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and lower fidelity is acceptable (SGD may be faster per step, though convergence is slower).

## Background
Plenoxels Optimization Configuration originates from the Plenoxels paper, which introduced radiance fields without neural networks using voxel grids and spherical harmonics [S1]. The specific optimization settings were derived from empirical evaluation on synthetic NeRF scenes [S1].
