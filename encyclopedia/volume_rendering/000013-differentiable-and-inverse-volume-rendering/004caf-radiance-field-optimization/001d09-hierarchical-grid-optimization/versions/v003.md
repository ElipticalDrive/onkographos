# Hierarchical Grid Optimization

Also called: Sparse Voxel Grid Optimization, Coarse-to-Fine Optimization, PlenOctree Optimization

## Overview
Hierarchical Grid Optimization refers to a family of training strategies for 3D radiance fields that utilize structured, multi-resolution data structures (such as sparse voxel grids or octrees) rather than dense neural networks or unstructured point clouds. It solves the memory and computational bottlenecks associated with high-resolution scene representations by dynamically adapting the grid density to the scene's complexity [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Neural Radiance Fields](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) by replacing MLPs with explicit grid lookups, and from [3D Gaussian Splatting](1795-3d-gaussian-splatting.md) by using axis-aligned, spatially disjoint voxels with explicit continuity enforcement [S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses).

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [S1, unofficial][S5]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [S1, unofficial][S5].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories:
1.  **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [S5].
2.  **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
3.  **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [S5].

## Subtopics

### [Plenoxels Optimization Configuration](929-plenoxels-optimization-configuration.md)
Defines hyperparameter settings for static voxel grids with spherical harmonics. Distinguished by its use of a fixed-resolution dense or sparse voxel grid and RMSProp optimizer.

### [Coarse-to-Fine Optimization with Pruning](1153-coarse-to-fine-optimization-with-pruning.md)
A hierarchical training strategy that starts with a coarse dense grid, prunes unoccupied regions, and iteratively subdivides. Distinguished by dynamic voxel structure adaptation and trilinear interpolation for initialization.

### [PlenOctree Optimization](1291-plenoctree-optimization.md)
Post-training fine-tuning of a pre-converted PlenOctree using SGD. Distinguished by operating on a hierarchical octree structure with fixed topology and specific learning rates for different datasets.

### [Sparse Voxel Grid Optimization](2568-sparse-voxel-grid-optimization.md)
Optimizes a dynamically managed sparse octree for real-time synthesis and mesh reconstruction. Distinguished by Morton code indexing, explicit sparsity management (pruning/subdivision), and support for SDF-based reconstruction.

## Background
The concept of hierarchical optimization for radiance fields emerged as a response to the memory limitations of dense neural representations. PlenOctree [S1, unofficial] introduced the idea of converting a trained NeRF into a sparse octree for efficient rendering, followed by fine-tuning. Coarse-to-fine strategies [S1] and sparse voxel grids [S2, unofficial] further refined this by allowing the resolution to adapt to the scene content during training, enabling high-fidelity novel-view synthesis and surface reconstruction at interactive frame rates.
