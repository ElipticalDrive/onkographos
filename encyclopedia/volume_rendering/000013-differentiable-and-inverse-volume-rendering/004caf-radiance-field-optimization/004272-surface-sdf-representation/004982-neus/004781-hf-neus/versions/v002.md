# HF-NeuS

Also called: High-Frequency NeuS

## Overview
HF-NeuS is an improvement on NeuS that separates low-frequency details into a base SDF and high-frequency details into a displacement function to increase reconstruction quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:** The method takes multi-view images as input and outputs a reconstructed 3D surface and appearance [S1].

**Data Structures:** Geometry is decoupled into a base SDF for low-frequency details and a displacement function for high-frequency details [S1]. The specific network architecture for the displacement function is not given in the sources [S1].

**Numbered Steps:** The optimization procedure follows the NeuS volume rendering framework [S1]. The specific steps for HF-NeuS are not detailed in the sources [S1].

**Formulas:** Not given in the sources [S1].

**Parameters:** Not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:** Not given in the sources [S1].

## Subtopics

### Neural Multi-view Image Reconstruction
A framework that decouples geometry (SDF) and appearance (light field) from calibrated multi-view images, using volume rendering to generate images and minimizing the difference between rendered and input images [S2]. It utilizes the NeuS volume rendering framework to render 2D images based on the neural implicit SDF and light field [S2].

**Steps:** Define a ray $r(s) = o + sv$ for $s \ge 0$, where $o$ is the camera center and $v$ is the unit direction vector [S2]. Uniformly sample 500 points per iteration in the bounding volume $\Omega$ for the $L_{off}$ calculation [S2].

**Formulas:** 
$$ T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{s_n}^{t} \sigma(r(u)) du\right) $$
where $T(t)$ is the accumulated transmittance along the ray [S2].

$$ L = L_{color} + \alpha L_{eikonal} + \beta L_{mask} + \gamma L_{off} $$
where $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ are weights [S2].

$$ L_{eikonal} = \frac{1}{\#X} \sum_{p \in X} (\|\nabla_x \Phi(p)\|^2 - 1)^2 $$
where $X$ is the sample point set on rays [S2].

$$ L_{off} = \frac{1}{\#\Omega} \sum_{x \in \Omega} \psi(\Phi(x), \beta_0) $$
where $\Omega$ is the bounding volume and $\beta_0$ is 100 [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].

**Measured Performance:** HF-NeuS outperformed the baseline NeRF model and provided theoretical and experimental justifications for its SDF-based scene density implementation [S1]. It greatly increased reconstruction quality compared to standard NeuS [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:** The primary strength is improved geometric reconstruction quality by separating low-frequency and high-frequency details [S1]. The specific computational overhead of the displacement function is not quantified in the sources [S1].

**When to use it:** Use when high-frequency geometric details are required and standard NeuS reconstruction quality is insufficient [S1].

## Background
HF-NeuS was introduced in June 2022 [S1]. It is an improvement on NeuS [S1]. Concurrently, other methods like Geo-NeuS and SparseNeus were introduced to address multi-view constraints and sparse-view reconstruction, respectively [S1].
