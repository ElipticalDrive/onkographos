# NeuS2

Also called: NeuS2 Dynamic Reconstruction, NeuS2 Static Reconstruction

## Overview
NeuS2 is a fast neural surface reconstruction approach for multi-view RGB input that achieves two orders of magnitude improvement in training speed over NeuS without compromising reconstruction quality, applicable to both static and dynamic scenes [S1]. It sets itself apart by parameterizing the neural network-encoded Signed Distance Field (SDF) using multi-resolution hash tables of learnable feature vectors, as introduced in Instant-NGP, and by calculating second-order derivatives using a novel lightweight formula tailored to ReLU-based MLPs to leverage CUDA parallelism and reduce memory footprint [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view RGB images [S1]. For dynamic scenes, a sequence of such views [S1].
**Outputs:** A neural implicit surface representation (SDF and color) parameterized by multi-resolution hash encodings [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Multi-resolution Hash Table:** A set of hash tables, one for each resolution level $l \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$, mapping 3D coordinates to learnable feature vectors [S1].
*   **MLP:** A ReLU-based Multi-Layer Perceptron that takes the hashed features as input and outputs the SDF value and color [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Training time for dynamic scenes: up to 20 seconds per frame [S1].
*   Typical training time for static scenes: minutes [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Hash Encoding:** For a given 3D point $\mathbf{x}$, query the multi-resolution hash tables to retrieve feature vectors at different resolutions and combine them [S1].
2.  **MLP Forward Pass:** Pass the combined features through the ReLU-based MLP to predict the SDF value $d(\mathbf{x})$ and color $c(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
3.  **Second-Order Derivative Calculation:** Compute the second-order derivatives of the SDF required for the surface constraint using a novel lightweight formula tailored to ReLU-based MLPs to enable efficient CUDA implementation with a small memory footprint [S1].
4.  **Progressive Learning (Static):** Optimize the multi-resolution hash encodings from coarse to fine to stabilize and expedite training convergence [S1].
5.  **Incremental Learning (Dynamic):**
    *   Train the first frame of the sequence [S1].
    *   For subsequent frames, sequentially fine-tune the representation, exploiting similarity in shape and appearance [S1].
    *   If the movement between consecutive frames is large, predict a global transformation to roughly align the frames before learning the representation of the new frame, addressing issues where occluded regions get stuck in the previous frame's SDF [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Large Movements in Dynamic Scenes:** If the movement between consecutive frames is large, occluded regions may get stuck in the previous frame's SDF [S1]. The method addresses this by predicting a global transformation to align frames [S1].
*   **Precision:** The method aims to reduce memory footprint and computational cost, but specific precision details are not provided [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two orders of magnitude faster than NeuS [S1]. Over 3x faster than Voxurf [S1]. 100x faster than Neuralangelo [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced memory footprint due to the lightweight calculation of second-order derivatives [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Static scenes reconstructed in minutes; dynamic scenes up to 20 seconds per frame [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fast training, high-quality geometry, applicable to both static and dynamic scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Not explicitly stated in the sources.
*   **When to Use:** When fast training of neural implicit surfaces is required for multi-view reconstruction, especially for dynamic scenes [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When extremely dense geometry is required and training time is not a constraint (Neuralangelo might be better) [S1].

## Background
NeuS2 was proposed as a fast neural surface reconstruction approach to address the long training time of NeuS, which takes an extremely long time (8 hours) and makes it almost impossible to apply to dynamic scenes with thousands of frames [S1]. It builds on the multi-resolution hash encodings introduced in Instant-NGP [S1]. The method was presented in the paper "NeuS2: Fast Learning of Neural Implicit Surfaces for Multi-view Reconstruction" [S1].
