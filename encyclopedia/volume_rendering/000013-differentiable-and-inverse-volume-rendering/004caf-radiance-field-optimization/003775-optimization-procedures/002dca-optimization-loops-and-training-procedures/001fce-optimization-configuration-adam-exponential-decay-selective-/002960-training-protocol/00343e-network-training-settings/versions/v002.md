# Network Training Settings

Also called: Activation and Dropout, General Network Configuration

## Overview
This article specifies the activation functions, dropout regularization, and latent space dimensionality validation for neural networks in radiance-field optimization [S1, unofficial][S2]. It addresses stabilizing training and preventing overfitting in differentiable rendering pipelines by defining architectural hyperparameters [S1, unofficial][S2]. It builds on [Training Protocol](4528-training-protocol.md).

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Network architecture definition (layer types, residual blocks) and training dataset [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Outputs**: A trained network with optimized weights and a validated latent space dimensionality [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Steps
1. **Initialize Network**: Construct the network using convolutional layers and residual blocks [S1, unofficial].
2. **Apply Activation**: Use Parametric ReLU (PReLU) for all hidden layers and a sigmoid function for the final output layer [S1, unofficial].
3. **Apply Dropout**: During training, apply dropout with probability $p=0.5$ after every convolutional layer, excluding layers within residual blocks [S1, unofficial].
4. **Validate Latent Dimensionality**: Test latent space dimensions $d \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$ [S2]. Modify the architecture to produce $64 \times 64$ resolution images by removing the last few upsampling layers to reduce computational cost [S2]. Set the loss weight $\lambda = 0$ in the loss function to remove the L1 loss influence, as the latent space primarily impacts shape rather than color [S2]. Evaluate performance on the Combustion dataset without illumination [S2]. Select the dimension that minimizes color image RMSE, as latent space dimensionality has minimal impact on opacity image quality [S2].
5. **Finalize Configuration**: Use the validated dimension (typically 8) for full-resolution training [S2].

### Formulas
- **Modified Loss Function**: $L = L_{\text{adv}} + \lambda L_{\text{L1}}$, where $\lambda = 0$ during latent dimension validation to isolate shape impact from color [S2].
- **Dropout**: Applied with probability $p=0.5$ [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
- **Activation**: PReLU for hidden layers, Sigmoid for output [S1, unofficial].
- **Dropout Probability**: 0.5 [S1, unofficial].
- **Latent Space Dimensions Tested**: 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 [S2].
- **Optimal Latent Dimension**: 8 [S2].
- **Validation Resolution**: $64 \times 64$ [S2].
- **Loss Weight ($\lambda$)**: 0 during validation [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Overestimating Dimensionality**: Using a latent space dimension larger than necessary results in poorer generalization and makes the TF latent space more difficult to sample [S2].
- **Color vs. Shape**: Latent space dimensionality has minimal impact on opacity image quality but significant impact on color image quality [S2].
- **Computational Burden**: Full-resolution training for all dimensions is computationally expensive; validation uses reduced resolution [S2].

        is_last = (i == len(layer_definitions) - 1)

        if is_last:
            activation = lambda x: 1.0 / (1.0 + exp(-x))

        network.append({'op': conv_op, 'act': activation, 'is_residual': layer.get('residual', False)})

## Subtopics

### Latent Space Dimensionality Validation
A validation process comparing networks with different latent space dimensionalities (4, 8, 16, 32, 64) to determine the optimal dimension for capturing shape variations [S2]. It sets $\lambda=0$ in the loss function to isolate shape impact from color [S2]. The optimal dimension found was 8, as higher dimensions lead to poorer generalization and sampling difficulties [S2]. Use this when determining the capacity of the latent space for a new dataset [S2].

### Strengths and Limitations
- **Strengths**: PReLU and Dropout help stabilize training and prevent overfitting [S1, unofficial]. Validating latent dimensionality ensures a balance between detail capture and generalization [S2].
- **Limitations**: High-dimensional latent spaces are difficult to sample and may generalize poorly [S2]. Validation requires reduced resolution, which may not fully reflect full-resolution performance [S2].

### When to Use It and When Not
- **Use**: When training deep convolutional networks for differentiable rendering, especially when dealing with 3D shape and color reconstruction [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Do Not Use**: When computational resources are extremely limited and latent space validation is not feasible; consider using a fixed, smaller dimension (e.g., 4) with caution [S2].
