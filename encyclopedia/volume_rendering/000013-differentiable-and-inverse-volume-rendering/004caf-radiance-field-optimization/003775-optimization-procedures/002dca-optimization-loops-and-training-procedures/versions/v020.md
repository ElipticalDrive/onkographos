# Optimization Loops and Training Procedures

Also called: Inverse Rendering Loop

## Overview
This family defines the iterative optimization procedures used to estimate scene parameters (geometry, materials, illumination, or camera poses) from observed images or measurements by minimizing a photometric or physical loss [S1, unofficial]. It solves the inverse problem of differentiable rendering, where the forward rendering operator is embedded within an optimizer to propagate gradients from the loss back to the scene representation [S1, unofficial]. What sets this family apart from its siblings is its role as the foundational control flow for training; while [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) specifies the standard SGD loop for implicit fields, and [Optimization Configuration (Adam + Exponential Decay + Selective L2)](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md) details specific hyperparameter settings, this family encompasses all strategies including black-box optimization [Optimization Baselines](6675-optimization-baselines.md), GPU-native autograd [DressiAD Optimization Loop](10728-dressiad-optimization-loop.md), and specialized protocols for dynamic or holographic scenes [Training & Optimization Protocol](12939-training-optimization-protocol.md), [Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)](12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md).

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md)
The standard stochastic gradient descent loop for implicit neural representations, minimizing photometric error between rendered images and ground-truth observations. It serves as the foundational baseline for training coordinate-based neural networks.

### [Optimization Configuration (Adam + Exponential Decay + Selective L2)](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md)
A specific configuration combining Adam with staircase exponential learning rate decay and selective L2 weight decay to stabilize training of coordinate-based neural fields. It integrates domain-specific augmentations and regularization into the core loop.

### [Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)](3429-neural-volume-rendering-training-mse.md)
Focuses on the core MSE loss metric and explicit strategies to mitigate depth-supervision bias, such as Hierarchical Geometric Guidance (HGG) and Hierarchical Photometric Guidance (HPG).

### [Optimization Baselines](6675-optimization-baselines.md)
Black-box optimization strategies (e.g., CMA-ES) that treat the renderer as an opaque function, using population-based search distributions to adapt to the local loss landscape without explicit gradients.

### [DressiAD Optimization Loop](10728-dressiad-optimization-loop.md)
A define-and-run differentiable rendering loop that packs the backward pass into Vulkan command buffers, executing optimization directly on the GPU to avoid CPU-side backpropagation overhead.

### [Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)](12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md)
An optimization algorithm for encoding complex wave fields into phase-only holograms, leveraging phase information to reduce ringing artifacts and capture directional light field information.

### [Training & Optimization Protocol](12939-training-optimization-protocol.md)
A protocol for decomposed Gaussian splatting (DeSplat) that separates static geometry from dynamic distractor points, using specialized learning rate schedules and densification control.

### [Staged and Alternating Optimization](12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md)
Decouples optimization into sequential phases (e.g., geometry then materials) to stabilize gradient descent and prevent bias from approximation thresholds [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md].

### [Genetic Algorithm Optimization](13247-genetic-algorithm-optimization.md)
A stochastic search method for Direct Volume Rendering Images (DVRIs) that evolves a population of opacity seed values to guarantee visibility of specific structures, without relying on gradients.

### [Temporal Optimization Heuristics](13688-temporal-optimization-heuristics.md)
Heuristics for differentiable bitmap primitive (DiffBMP) rendering that address "stuck" primitives and parameter drift in sequential frame optimization via warm-starting, freezing, and decay.

### 4DGS Baseline Implementation
A specific training schedule for 4D Gaussian Splatting that optimizes the first frame for 3,000 iterations, then optimizes the full multi-view video sequence for 14,000 iterations [S2].

### Geometry Focused Model Optimization
An initial stage strategy where the rendering equation is applied directly to each Gaussian primitive to compute outgoing radiance using its material and geometry properties, then alpha-blended during rasterization. This stabilizes gradient transfer to geometry in the initial phase by avoiding pixel-level shading disturbances [S3, unofficial].

### Continuous Coarse-To-Fine Optimization
A progressive optimization strategy that avoids shape-radiance ambiguity by restricting the Level of Detail (LOD) to coarse levels initially, then relaxing this regularization to recover scene details continuously in LOD space [S4, unofficial]. During cone casting, samples' LOD are clamped up to a maximal LOD $L$, and grids of level $L+1$ and above are set to zero and not optimized [S4, unofficial]. The maximal LOD $L$ is updated per epoch using the linear scheduler: $L = (L_0 + n / n_{ctf}) \in \mathbb{R}^+$, where $n$ is the current epoch, $n_{ctf}$ is a hyperparameter describing the speed of the process, and $L_0$ defines the number of used grids at initialization [S4, unofficial]. Unlike I-NGP-based architectures, this method keeps supervising all decoder latent space dimensions, adding degrees of freedom without modifying decoder weights or previously trained grids [S4, unofficial].

### Shape-from-Projections Optimization Pipeline
An iterative pipeline that initializes a mesh, refines it through subdivision and mesh cleaning, runs differentiable rendering optimization, and saves results [S5, unofficial]. It loads the dataset, computes physical dimensions, and initializes the mesh via `save_init_mesh`. If `args.niter0 > 0`, it runs a coarse refinement loop: creates a `Model`, estimates/sets initial `mu`, runs `optimize.run`, extracts vertices/faces/labels, updates `mus`, saves a subdivided mesh, and cleans it with `pymeshfix` and `manifold` tools. After refinement, it runs final optimization with `args.niter` iterations and saves results to HDF5 [S5, unofficial]. Key parameters include `args.niter0` (coarse refinement iterations), `args.niter` (final optimization iterations), `args.nmu0` (initial attenuation values), `args.wlap` and `args.wflat` (regularization weights), and `args.subdiv` (subdivision level) [S5, unofficial]. A pitfall is that mesh cleaning uses external `pymeshfix` and `manifold` executables, where `manifold` requires `outresolution` from the first refinement pass, and `pymeshfix` cleans with `max_iters=10, inner_loops=3` [S5, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of using differentiable renderers within optimizers to solve inverse problems was formalized in the mid-2010s, with early work on differentiable ray tracing and volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. The "inverse rendering loop" became a standard paradigm as automatic differentiation libraries (like PyTorch and JAX) made it feasible to backpropagate through complex rendering pipelines [S1, unofficial]. The field has since expanded to include specialized loops for neural radiance fields (NeRF) [970-nerf-training-loop.md], Gaussian splatting [12939-training-optimization-protocol.md], and holography [12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md].
