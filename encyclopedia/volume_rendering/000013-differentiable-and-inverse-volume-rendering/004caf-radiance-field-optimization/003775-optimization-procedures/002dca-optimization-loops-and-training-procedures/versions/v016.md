# Optimization Loops and Training Procedures

Also called: Inverse Rendering Loop

## Overview
Optimization Loops and Training Procedures define the iterative computational framework used to solve inverse rendering problems by minimizing the discrepancy between rendered images and ground-truth observations [S1, unofficial]. This family of methods leverages the differentiability of the forward rendering operator to propagate gradients from a photometric loss back to scene parameters (geometry, materials, illumination, or camera poses) [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from evaluation procedures by employing stochastic sampling, adaptive strategies, and specific regularization techniques to manage memory and stabilize convergence in ill-conditioned optimization landscapes [S2, S4, S9, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md)
The NeRF Training Loop is the iterative optimization procedure that minimizes the photometric reconstruction error between rendered images and ground-truth observations by updating the parameters of a continuous volumetric radiance field. It solves the inverse rendering problem by leveraging the differentiability of the volume rendering equation, computing gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, color, and potentially camera poses) via backpropagation [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. It sets itself apart from evaluation by using stochastic ray sampling, adaptive strategies, and chunked processing to manage memory, whereas evaluation disables these for deterministic results. See [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).

### [Optimization Configuration (Adam + Exponential Decay + Selective L2)](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md)
This configuration defines the standard hyperparameter setup for training neural radiance fields and implicit volume representations, combining the Adam optimizer with a staircase exponential learning rate decay and selective L2 weight decay. It addresses the problem of stabilizing gradient descent and preventing divergence in high-dimensional, ill-conditioned optimization landscapes typical of differentiable volume rendering. It sets itself apart from generic optimizers by explicitly disabling regularization on positional encodings and appearance embeddings while applying it to network weights, and by using a delayed decay schedule to allow initial convergence. See [Optimization Configuration (Adam + Exponential Decay + Selective L2)](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md).

### [Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)](3429-neural-volume-rendering-training-mse.md)
This method defines the core optimization loop for training Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and similar implicit neural representations by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered images and ground truth observations. It addresses the problem of converging to geometrically accurate and photometrically correct scene representations from sparse or noisy input data. It is distinguished from its siblings by its foundational reliance on the standard differentiable volume rendering equation and explicit strategies to mitigate depth-supervision bias, such as Hierarchical Geometric Guidance (HGG) and Hierarchical Photometric Guidance (HPG). See [Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)](3429-neural-volume-rendering-training-mse.md).

### [Photometric Optimization](5790-photometric-optimization.md)
Photometric optimization is a training procedure for differentiable volume rendering that minimizes the pixel-wise difference between rendered images and ground-truth observations. It serves as the primary loss-driven mechanism for refining scene representations (geometry, appearance, lighting) and camera parameters by backpropagating gradients through the volume rendering equation. It sets itself apart from [Optimization Configuration](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md) by defining the objective function rather than the solver dynamics, and from [Stage-based Differentiable Volume/Radiance Field Training](4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md) by focusing on the specific loss formulation (photometric vs. multi-stage constraints) rather than the temporal scheduling of training phases. See [Photometric Optimization](5790-photometric-optimization.md).

### [Optimization Baselines](6675-optimization-baselines.md)
This family provides black-box optimization strategies for inverse rendering problems where the forward rendering operator $f$ is non-differentiable or gradient computation is prohibitively expensive. Unlike gradient-based siblings that rely on automatic differentiation or adjoint methods, these methods treat the renderer as an opaque function, minimizing a photometric loss $\mathcal{L}$ by iteratively sampling and evaluating parameter candidates. The family is distinguished by its reliance on population-based search distributions (typically Gaussian) that adapt to the local loss landscape without explicit gradient information, offering robustness against local minima and Monte Carlo noise. See [Optimization Baselines](6675-optimization-baselines.md).

### [DressiAD Optimization Loop](10728-dressiad-optimization-loop.md)
The DressiAD Optimization Loop is a define-and-run differentiable rendering optimization procedure that constructs a computational graph, marks variables for gradient tracking, and executes forward/backward passes using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD). It distinguishes itself from standard AD libraries by packing the backward pass into Vulkan command buffers, allowing the optimization loop to execute directly on the GPU without CPU-side backpropagation overhead. See [DressiAD Optimization Loop](10728-dressiad-optimization-loop.md).

### [Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)](12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md)
Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD) is an optimization algorithm designed to encode complex wave fields into phase-only holograms for 3D holographic displays with continuous depth cues. It solves the problem of ringing artifacts and unnatural blur spreading inherent in amplitude-only or RGB-D holographic synthesis methods by leveraging phase information for supervision. Unlike amplitude-based approaches that rely on discrete multi-plane supervision, PAGD uses a camera-calibrated learned forward model to propagate the phase-only hologram, enabling accurate reconstruction of tilted wave fields and view-dependent occlusion. See [Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)](12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md).

### [Training & Optimization Protocol](12939-training-optimization-protocol.md)
This protocol defines the iterative optimization procedure for decomposed Gaussian splatting methods, specifically targeting distractor-free rendering by jointly optimizing static scene geometry and learned distractor points. It differs from standard NeRF training [970-nerf-training-loop.md] by explicitly modeling and optimizing distractor elements (e.g., transient objects, people) alongside the static scene, using a specialized densification schedule and per-parameter learning rates. It extends the base Splatfacto configuration [12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md] with specific regularization and distractor initialization strategies. See [Training & Optimization Protocol](12939-training-optimization-protocol.md).

### [Staged and Alternating Optimization](12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md)
Staged and Alternating Optimization decomposes complex inverse rendering problems into sequential sub-problems to stabilize gradient descent and manage computational resources [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]. Unlike joint optimization methods that update all parameters simultaneously, this family alternates between optimizing distinct parameter groups (e.g., geometry vs. material, or shape vs. blur) or progressively refines resolution [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]. It distinguishes itself from single-stage radiance field methods by explicitly decoupling ill-posed variables, such as separating geometric proxy recovery from physically-based material refinement [7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]. See [Staged and Alternating Optimization](12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md).

### [Genetic Algorithm Optimization](13247-genetic-algorithm-optimization.md)
Genetic Algorithm (GA) Optimization is a stochastic search method used to automatically generate Direct Volume Rendering Images (DVRIs) that guarantee the visibility of specific structures of interest within a Region of Interest (ROI). Unlike gradient-based methods that optimize continuous parameters via backpropagation, this approach treats the selection of opacity seed values for atomic structures as a discrete optimization problem, using an energy function to balance visibility, coherence, and context. It is distinct from standard differentiable optimization loops because it does not rely on gradients of the rendering equation; instead, it iteratively evolves a population of candidate transfer function configurations until user-defined quality thresholds are met. See [Genetic Algorithm Optimization](13247-genetic-algorithm-optimization.md).

### Geometry Focused Model Optimization
Initial Stage: Apply the rendering equation directly to each Gaussian primitive to compute outgoing radiance using its material and geometry properties, then alpha-blend during rasterization. This stabilizes gradient transfer to geometry in the initial phase [S3, unofficial].

### Continuous Coarse-To-Fine Optimization
A progressive optimization strategy that avoids shape-radiance ambiguity by restricting the Level of Detail (LOD) to coarse levels initially, then relaxing this regularization to recover scene details. It operates continuously in LOD space rather than discretely [S4, unofficial]. During cone casting, clamp the samples' LOD up to a maximal LOD $L$. Set grids of level $L + 1$ and above to zero and do not optimize them [S4, unofficial]. The maximal LOD $L$ is updated per epoch using the linear scheduler: $L = (L_0 + n / n_{ctf}) \in \mathbb{R}^+$, where $n$ is the current epoch, $n_{ctf}$ is a hyperparameter describing the speed of the process, and $L_0$ defines the number of used grids at initialization [S4, unofficial]. Unlike I-NGP-based architectures where keeping grid values to zero leaves some decoder latent space dimensions unsupervised (causing global weight refinement issues when new grids are optimized), this method keeps supervising all decoder latent space dimensions. Adding new grid levels only adds degrees of freedom for the mapping function without modifying decoder weights or previously trained grids [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of using differentiable renderers within an optimization loop for inverse problems was formalized in the context of differentiable ray tracing and volume rendering. The loop structure is simple: initialize parameters, trace rays, compute loss, backpropagate, and update [S1, unofficial]. However, choosing a faithful model, identifiable parameters, a stable estimator, and a good initialization remains difficult [S1, unofficial]. The field has evolved from basic neural radiance fields (NeRF) [S5, S6] to more complex representations like Gaussian Splatting [S2, S12939] and holographic synthesis [S12876], requiring specialized training protocols to handle the specific inductive biases and computational constraints of each representation.
