# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting to incorrect geometries when given insufficient training images by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution matches surface priors derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires only the existence of 3D keypoints, making it flexible and independent of training data distribution assumptions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $I_j$ with known camera poses $P_j$ (estimated via SfM), and a sparse 3D point cloud $X$ generated by the SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) that synthesizes novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ representing 3D locations [S1].
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that camera [S1].
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input training images to estimate camera parameters and generate a sparse 3D point cloud $X$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Calculation:**
    *   For each image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$ [S1].
    *   Compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ for each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
    *   Model the true depth of the first surface encountered by a ray as a random variable $D_{ij}$ normally distributed around the estimated depth with variance $\hat{\sigma}_i$:
        $$ D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i) $$
        [S1][S3, unofficial]
3.  **Ray Termination Distribution:**
    *   Define the ray termination distribution $h(t)$, which describes the likelihood of a ray terminating at distance $t$:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right)\sigma(t) $$
        where $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density [S1].
    *   The rendered color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}$ is the expectation of color $c(t)$ under this distribution:
        $$ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} = \int_0^\infty h(t)c(t)dt = \mathbb{E}_{h(t)}[c(t)] $$
        [S1]
    *   Note: $h(t)$ is a valid probability distribution ($\int_0^\infty h(t)dt = 1$) assuming $\sigma(s)$ is non-negative and the scene is bounded by a near $t_n$ and far $t_f$ plane, where $t_f$ is treated as an opaque wall [S1].
4.  **Depth Loss Formulation:**
    *   Minimize the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $\mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1].
    *   The depth loss $L_{Depth}$ is computed as:
        $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\log h(t) \exp \left( -\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \right] dt $$
    *   In discrete form, using sample points $t_k$ and step size $\Delta t_k$:
        $$ L_{Depth} \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
        where $h_k = h(t_k)$ [S1].
    *   Alternative: Simple MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ can be used, but KL divergence yields slightly better results with fewer artifacts [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Training Loop:**
    *   For each training iteration, allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   **Keypoint Rays:**
        *   Calculate ray origins in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of detected keypoints, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
        *   Shoot these rays into the scene and render depth using the standard NeRF sampling procedure [S1].
        *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for these rays from the ground truth image $I_j$ [S1].
        *   Compute both color loss and depth loss for these rays in a single ray marching step [S1].
    *   **Standard Rays:** Render remaining rays using standard color supervision [S1].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ L = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth} $$
        where $L_{Color}$ is the standard MSE color reconstruction loss and $\lambda_D$ is a hyperparameter balancing the losses [S1].
    *   Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update NeRF parameters [S1].

    """

        target_depth (float): The SfM-estimated depth D_ij.
        uncertainty (float): The depth uncertainty sigma_hat_i.
        dt (float): The step size Delta t_k (assumed constant for simplicity or vectorized).
        
    Returns:
        float: The computed depth loss value.
    """
    # Term 1: -log(h(t))
    safe_h = np.maximum(rendered_h, 1e-10)
    log_h = np.log(safe_h)
    
    # Term 2: exp(-(t - D)^2 / (2 * sigma^2))
    exponent = -((t_samples - target_depth) ** 2) / (2 * uncertainty ** 2)
    exp_term = np.exp(exponent)
    
    # Summation: sum_k [ -log(h_k) * exp_term_k * dt_k ]
    # Assuming uniform dt for simplicity, or dt is a scalar step size
    loss = np.sum(-log_h * exp_term * dt)
    
    return loss

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   Near/Far bounds ($t_n, t_f$): Scene bounds. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   Training Iterations: Typically 10k–100k [S3, unofficial].
*   Image Resolution: 400 × 300 in experiments [S1].
*   Ray Allocation: A portion of rays is dedicated to keypoint rays [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different datasets (e.g., NeRF Real) if combined with other priors, though DS-NeRF itself is domain-agnostic [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are 2–3x fewer than standard NeRF to reach the same PSNR [S1]. Wall time per iteration is comparable (~362.4 ms vs ~359.8 ms on RTX A5000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** On a single RTX A5000, DS-NeRF achieves target PSNR significantly faster. In a 5-view setup, it reaches NeRF's peak PSNR ~13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified with fewer training views. With 2 views, NeRF often fails completely, while DS-NeRF produces usable results [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with sparse views, compatible with other NeRF methods, no need for category-specific pre-training [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of SfM keypoints; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in 2022 [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the difficulty of training NeRFs with insufficient input images by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM pipelines [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1]. Compatible with depth from scanned sensors and RGB-D outputs [S1].
