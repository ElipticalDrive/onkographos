# Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training

Also called: DDM Regularization, DDM-regularized NeRF, DiffusioNeRF Regularization, Diffusion-regularized NeRF

## Overview
Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training addresses the severe under-constraint problem in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), where limited input views lead to implausible geometry artifacts such as "floaters" (semi-transparent material floating in mid-air) [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like RegNeRF by directly regularizing geometry via a Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM) trained on RGBD (color and depth) patches, rather than indirectly via RGB patches and normalizing flows [S1]. The method backpropagates the DDM's score function (gradient of the log-probability of the RGBD distribution) to the NeRF's density and color fields during optimization [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images $\mathcal{I}$ with known camera poses and intrinsics; a pre-trained DDM $\epsilon_\theta$ trained on synthetic RGBD patches (e.g., from Hypersim) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters (density field $\sigma$ and color field $\mathbf{c}$) [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **NeRF Representation:** Uses Instant NGP, which stores feature encodings of all 3D coordinates in multi-scale hash tables within a fixed memory block, allowing varying spatial resolutions and reducing MLP size [S1].
*   **DDM Model:** A neural network $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau)$ that predicts noise $\epsilon$ given a noised input $\mathbf{x}_\tau$ and diffusion time step $\tau$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **DDM Training (Pre-computation):**
    *   Extract RGBD patches from a synthetic dataset (e.g., Hypersim). Patch sizes used are $24 \times 24$ or $48 \times 48$ pixels [S1].
    *   Train the DDM to predict the noise $\epsilon$ added during the forward diffusion process. The forward process adds Gaussian noise to a data sample $\mathbf{x}_0$ (an RGBD patch) to produce $\mathbf{x}_\tau$:
        $$ \mathbf{x}_\tau = \sqrt{\alpha_\tau} \mathbf{x}_{\tau-1} + \sqrt{\beta_\tau} \epsilon_{\tau-1} $$
        where $\epsilon_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, $\alpha_\tau = 1 - \beta_\tau$, and $\bar{\alpha}_\tau = \prod_{s=0}^\tau \alpha_s$ [S1].
    *   The DDM is trained by minimizing the mean squared error between the true noise $\epsilon$ and the predicted noise $\epsilon_\theta$:
        $$ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \epsilon} \left[ \frac{\beta_\tau}{2\alpha_\tau (1-\bar{\alpha}_\tau)} || \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta \left( \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_\tau} \mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_\tau} \epsilon, \tau \right) ||^2 \right] $$
        [S1]. Training is performed for 650,000 steps with a batch size of 32 [S1].

2.  **NeRF Optimization Loop:**
    *   For each optimization step, render random RGBD patches from the current NeRF model. A patch consists of rays passing through a $48 \times 48$ (or $24 \times 24$) pixel region [S1].
    *   **Early Training Guard:** During the first 2500 steps, 25% of the time, use a training pose for patch rendering and sample the RGB component directly from the corresponding training image, while rendering depth from the NeRF. This mitigates inaccuracy in early NeRF renderings [S1].
    *   Compute the expected color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ for each ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ in the patch using discrete samples $t_{0:N}$:
        $$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) \approx \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t_i), \mathbf{d}) + \left( 1 - \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \right) \mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}} $$
        where $\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}$ is white [S1].
    *   Calculate weights $w_i = T(t_i)\rho(t_i)$, opacity $\rho(t_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_i))(t_{i+1} - t_i))$, and accumulated transmittance $T(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \rho(t_j))$ [S1].
    *   Calculate expected depth:
        $$ \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i} $$
        [S1].
    *   Pass the rendered RGBD patch $\mathbf{x} = \{\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}), \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \text{Patch}\}$ into the trained DDM to obtain the noise prediction $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau)$ [S1].

3.  **Gradient Computation and Update:**
    *   The DDM noise prediction is proportional to the negative score function (gradient of the log-probability) of the RGBD distribution:
        $$ \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau) \propto -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x}) $$
        [S1].
    *   Compute the total loss gradient. The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ combines photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$, geometric regularizers ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$), and the DDM regularizer:
        $$ \nabla \mathcal{L} = \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{fg}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} + \lambda_{\text{fr}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} + \lambda_{\text{dist}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} - \lambda_{\text{DDM}} \epsilon_\theta $$
        where $\lambda_{\text{DDM}}$ is the weight for the DDM regularizer [S1].
    *   Backpropagate $\nabla \mathcal{L}$ through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF's density and color MLP parameters [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Patch Size:** $48 \times 48$ pixels is standard for Instant NGP due to fast rendering; $24 \times 24$ may perform better on LLFF but worse on DTU [S1].
*   **Diffusion Time $\tau$:** Schedules the noise level. Interpolates smoothly from 0.1 to 0 during the first 2500 optimization steps. $\bar{\alpha}_\tau = \cos(0.5\pi(\tau + 0.008)/1.008)$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{DDM}}$:** Controls the strength of the DDM regularizer. Trades off reconstruction accuracy around thin structures against overall depth smoothness [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{dist}}$:** Distance regularizer weight. Linearly increases from 0 to $1 \times 10^{-4}$ (DTU) or $1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ (LLFF) between steps 3000 and 8000 [S1].
*   **NeRF Optimization Steps:** 12,000 steps total [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

# Assume InstantNGP model and DDM model are defined elsewhere
# instant_ngp: NeRF model returning sigma and color
# ddm_model: DDM model returning noise prediction epsilon_theta

def render_patch(instant_ngp, rays, num_samples=128):
    """
    Renders a patch of rays.
    Returns: color C, depth D, weights w
    """
    # Sample points along rays
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, num_samples)
    t_samples = t_vals.unsqueeze(0) # Shape: [1, num_samples]
    
    # Get 3D points from rays
    points = rays.origins.unsqueeze(1) + rays.directions.unsqueeze(1) * t_samples.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # Query NeRF for sigma and color
    # sigma: [num_rays, num_samples, 1]
    # color: [num_rays, num_samples, 3]
    sigma, color = instant_ngp(points)
    
    # Compute opacity and transmittance
    # dt = t[i+1] - t[i]
    dt = t_samples[:, 1:] - t_samples[:, :-1]
    dt = torch.cat([dt, dt[:, -1:]], dim=1) # Extend for last sample
    
    rho = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * dt)
    T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - rho, dim=1)
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=1)
    
    weights = T * rho
    color_bg = torch.tensor([1.0, 1.0, 1.0], device=color.device) # White background
    
    # Expected Color
    C = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=1) + (1.0 - torch.sum(weights, dim=1)).unsqueeze(-1) * color_bg
    
    # Expected Depth
    D = torch.sum(weights * t_samples, dim=1) / torch.sum(weights, dim=1)
    
    return C, D, weights

def train_step(instant_ngp, ddm_model, optimizer, rays, training_image_rgb, step, total_steps=12000):
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    
    # 1. Render RGBD patch
    C, D, _ = render_patch(instant_ngp, rays)
    
    # 2. Early training guard: 25% of time, use ground truth RGB
    use_gt_rgb = (step < 2500) and (torch.rand(1).item() < 0.25)
    if use_gt_rgb:
        # Assume training_image_rgb is the RGB patch corresponding to the rays
        C_gt = training_image_rgb
        # D is still rendered from NeRF
        x_patch = torch.cat([C_gt, D.unsqueeze(-1)], dim=-1)
    else:
        x_patch = torch.cat([C, D.unsqueeze(-1)], dim=-1)
        
    # 3. Schedule diffusion time tau
    # tau interpolates from 0.1 to 0 in first 2500 steps
    if step < 2500:
        tau = 0.1 - 0.1 * (step / 2500.0)
    else:
        tau = 0.0
        
    # Compute alpha_bar_tau
    # alpha_bar_tau = cos(0.5 * pi * (tau + 0.008) / 1.008)
    alpha_bar_tau = torch.cos(0.5 * torch.pi * (tau + 0.008) / 1.008)
    
    # 4. Get DDM noise prediction
    # ddm_model expects noised input. In training, we pass the rendered patch directly
    # as the DDM acts as a score estimator for the data distribution.
    # Note: The source implies passing the rendered patch to DDM to get epsilon_theta.
    # The DDM is trained to predict noise, but here it is used as a score function.
    # The gradient is -lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta.
    epsilon_theta = ddm_model(x_patch, tau)
    
    # 5. Compute Losses
    # Photometric Loss (L2 between rendered C and GT C)
    # If using GT RGB guard, compare rendered C (if not guarded) or just use GT?
    # Standard practice: compare rendered color to GT color.
    # If use_gt_rgb, we might still compute photo loss against GT, but the DDM input is GT RGB.
    # Let's assume standard photo loss against GT image for the rays.
    # For simplicity, assume we have GT RGB for these rays in 'gt_rgb'
    # (In implementation, 'training_image_rgb' passed to function is the GT patch)
    L_photo = F.mse_loss(C, training_image_rgb)
    
    # Geometric Regularizers (Placeholder for L_fg, L_fr, L_dist)
    # These are computed from sigma and depth D.
    L_fg = torch.tensor(0.0) # Placeholder
    L_fr = torch.tensor(0.0) # Placeholder
    L_dist = torch.tensor(0.0) # Placeholder
    
    # DDM Regularizer
    # lambda_DDM schedule: 0 until 3000, linear to max at 8000
    if step < 3000:
        lambda_ddm = 0.0
    elif step < 8000:
        lambda_ddm = 1.5e-5 * ((step - 3000) / 5000.0) # Example for LLFF
    else:
        lambda_ddm = 1.5e-5
        
    # The gradient contribution is -lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta
    # We need to backprop through epsilon_theta to instant_ngp parameters
    # L_ddm_term = lambda_ddm * (epsilon_theta ** 2) ? 
    # No, the source says: grad L = ... - lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta
    # This implies we treat epsilon_theta as the gradient direction.
    # To backprop, we minimize a loss that yields this gradient.
    # If grad L = - lambda * eps, then L = lambda * <eps, x>? 
    # Actually, in practice, one often minimizes the norm of the score or uses the score as a gradient.
    # The source Eq 19: grad L = ... - lambda_DDM epsilon_theta.
    # This means we add the term -lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta to the gradient buffer.
    
    # To implement this in PyTorch:
    # We can compute a loss L_ddm such that dL_ddm/dx = -lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta.
    # However, epsilon_theta is a function of x (rendered RGBD).
    # The source states: "backpropagate as usual".
    # This implies we compute a loss term. 
    # If we define L_ddm = lambda_DDM * ||epsilon_theta||^2 / 2, 
    # grad L_ddm = lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta * d(epsilon_theta)/dx. This is not -lambda * epsilon.
    # The source says epsilon_theta is proportional to -grad log p.
    # So -lambda * epsilon_theta is proportional to lambda * grad log p.
    # This is a gradient ascent on log p.
    # In SGD, we do theta = theta - lr * grad L.
    # If grad L contains -lambda * epsilon, then theta = theta + lr * lambda * epsilon.
    # This moves theta in the direction of epsilon, which is the negative score direction?
    # Wait: epsilon ~ -grad log p. So -epsilon ~ grad log p.
    # grad L = ... - lambda * epsilon.
    # Update: theta -= lr * grad L => theta += lr * lambda * epsilon.
    # So we move in direction of epsilon.
    # Since epsilon ~ -grad log p, we move in direction -grad log p.
    # This is gradient descent on log p? No, gradient ascent on log p is +grad log p.
    # Gradient descent on log p is -grad log p.
    # So we are doing gradient descent on the negative log probability (i.e., maximizing likelihood)?
    # Yes, moving towards modes.
    
    # Implementation:
    # We need to backprop the gradient -lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta.
    # We can do this by computing a dummy loss or using torch.autograd.grad.
    # Easier: L_ddm = lambda_DDM * torch.sum(epsilon_theta * x_patch) ? No.
    # The source says "backpropagate as usual".
    # Let's assume we construct a loss that produces this gradient.
    # If we define L_ddm = lambda_DDM * torch.dot(epsilon_theta, x_patch), 
    # grad is complex.
    # Actually, the standard way to apply a score-based regularizer is:
    # grad_theta = grad_theta - lambda_DDM * epsilon_theta
    # But epsilon_theta depends on theta.
    # So we compute epsilon_theta, then compute its gradient w.r.t theta?
    # No, the source says the gradient OF THE LOSS is ... - lambda_DDM epsilon_theta.
    # This means the term in the loss function is such that its derivative is -lambda_DDM epsilon_theta.
    # This is non-standard. Usually, you minimize a loss.
    # If we minimize L = lambda_DDM * ||epsilon_theta||^2, grad is lambda * J^T epsilon.
    # The source might mean that the DDM provides a gradient vector that is ADDED to the gradient.
    # "backpropagate the DDM gradients directly"
    # This suggests:
    # 1. Compute epsilon_theta.
    # 2. Compute gradient of epsilon_theta w.r.t NeRF params? No.
    # 3. The DDM output IS the gradient direction.
    # In PyTorch, to add a custom gradient:
    # epsilon_theta.retain_grad()
    # loss_ddm = torch.zeros(1, device=device)
    # torch.autograd.grad(epsilon_theta, instant_ngp.parameters(), grad_outputs=-lambda_DDM, retain_graph=True)
    # This adds -lambda_DDM * d(epsilon_theta)/d(params) to the gradients.
    # This matches "backpropagate the DDM gradients".
    
    # Let's implement the gradient addition manually.
    ddm_grads = torch.autograd.grad(
        epsilon_theta, 
        list(instant_ngp.parameters()), 
        grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(epsilon_theta) * (-lambda_ddm),
        retain_graph=True,
        create_graph=False
    )
    
    # Add these gradients to the existing gradients (from photo loss)
    for p, g in zip(instant_ngp.parameters(), ddm_grads):
        if p.grad is None:
            p.grad = g
        else:
            p.grad = p.grad + g
            
    # Add geometric regularizer gradients (placeholders)

    optimizer.step()
    
    return L_photo
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** NeRF training takes approximately 30 minutes per scene on a single Nvidia A100 GPU [S1]. The use of Instant NGP allows for fast rendering of large patches ($48 \times 48$), which is critical as patch rendering cost can extend training time [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Instant NGP uses multi-scale hash tables in a fixed memory block, reducing the MLP size compared to standard MLP-based NeRFs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves geometry quality and generalization to novel views on LLFF and DTU datasets compared to geometric baselines and RegNeRF [S1]. Directly regularizes geometry via RGBD, avoiding structural constraints of normalizing flows [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Can cause over-smoothing of thin structures and hallucinate details in occluded regions not visible in training views, impacting reconstruction scores [S1]. Struggles with shiny objects compared to diffuse/textured surfaces [S1]. Requires careful scheduling of $\tau$ and $\lambda_{\text{DDM}}$ [S1].
*   **When to use:** When training NeRFs with few input views or when geometric artifacts like floaters are prevalent. Suitable for diffuse/textured surfaces.
*   **When not to use:** For scenes with shiny objects or where preserving thin structures is critical without post-processing.

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed in "DiffusioNeRF: Regularizing Neural Radiance Fields with Denoising Diffusion Models" (CVPR 2023) [S1].
*   **History:** Builds on the observation that NeRFs are under-constrained with few views [S1]. Leverages Denoising Diffusion Models (DDMs) trained on synthetic data (Hypersim) to provide a learned prior over RGBD patches [S1]. Contrasts with Dreamfusion, which uses DDMs for text-to-3D, and RegNeRF, which uses normalizing flows [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented using torch-ngp and tiny-cuda-nn. Applied to novel-view synthesis and 3D reconstruction on LLFF and DTU datasets [S1].
