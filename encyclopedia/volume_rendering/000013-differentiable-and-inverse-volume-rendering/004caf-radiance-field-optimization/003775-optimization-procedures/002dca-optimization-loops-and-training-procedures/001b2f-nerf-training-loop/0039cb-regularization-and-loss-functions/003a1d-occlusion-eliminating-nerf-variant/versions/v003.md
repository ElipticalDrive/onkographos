# Occlusion-Eliminating NeRF Variant

Also called: Occlusion-Eliminating NeRF, Zhu et al. NeRF

## Overview
This method addresses the degradation of novel view synthesis caused by foreground occlusions that obscure background features during training [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF by explicitly modeling the scene and the occluding background as separate components, allowing for the recovery of occlusion-free background information [S1]. Unlike siblings that rely on generic regularization or diffusion priors, this variant uses a dual-MLP architecture with specific cost volume and depth inconsistency losses to disentangle foreground objects from background artifacts [S1].

## How it works
The method employs a neural network architecture composed of two distinct Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a Scene MLP and a Background MLP [S1]. The Scene MLP models the primary physical entities, while the Background MLP models the occluding elements and the environment behind them [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera parameters (position, orientation), 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ for both the scene and background components, which are combined to render the final image [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Scene MLP:** A standard NeRF-style MLP mapping $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma_s, \mathbf{c}_s)$.
*   **Background MLP:** A separate MLP mapping $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma_b, \mathbf{c}_b)$.
*   **Cost Volume:** A 3D structure used to guide the joint optimization of scene parameters and camera poses [S1].

**Training Procedure:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the Scene MLP and Background MLP with random weights.
2.  **Cost Volume Loss:** Construct a cost volume to guide the joint optimization of the Scene MLP parameters and the camera parameters. This ensures that the scene geometry is consistent across multiple views despite occlusions [S1].
3.  **Depth Bidirectional Inconsistency Prior:** Apply a depth bidirectional inconsistency prior within the Background MLP. This involves computing depth maps from the rendered outputs and enforcing a distance supervision constraint that penalizes inconsistencies between forward and backward depth predictions [S1]. This step specifically aims to restore occlusion-free backgrounds by learning the structure behind the occluders [S1].
4.  **Photometric Loss:** Compute the reconstruction loss between the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I_{gt}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = || \hat{I} - I_{gt} ||_2^2 $$
    where $\hat{I}$ is the composite image generated by combining the scene and background renderings [S1].
5.  **Total Loss:** Minimize the sum of the photometric loss and the regularization terms derived from the cost volume and depth priors [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Transparent Occluders:** When the occluding object is a light-loss transparent glass body, reflection artifacts introduce noise pollution into the training images. This prevents the model from generalizing effectively, as the background MLP struggles to distinguish between true background geometry and reflection artifacts [S1].

## Subtopics

### NeRFReN
A model proposed by Guo et al. to address the limitations of the basic occlusion-eliminating variant [S1]. It utilizes two types of geometric priors to establish depth smoothness and bidirectional depth consistency losses, specifically designed to separate reflection artifacts from physical entities [S1]. It is chosen when the scene contains significant reflection artifacts from transparent or reflective surfaces [S1]. However, it fails to distinguish separation when entities and artifacts are complexly interlaced [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** Capable of recovering background information that is occluded in training views, improving synthesis quality in cluttered scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance degrades significantly in the presence of transparent or reflective occluders (e.g., glass) due to reflection artifacts [S1].
*   **Complexity:** Requires maintaining and optimizing two separate MLPs and additional depth-based loss computations, increasing memory and computational overhead compared to single-MLP NeRF [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed by Zhu et al. in 2023 [S1]. It was developed to solve the problem of feature damage in NeRF synthesis caused by foreground occlusions [S1]. It is part of the broader effort to improve NeRF robustness in real-world scenarios where perfect unobstructed views are unavailable [S1].
