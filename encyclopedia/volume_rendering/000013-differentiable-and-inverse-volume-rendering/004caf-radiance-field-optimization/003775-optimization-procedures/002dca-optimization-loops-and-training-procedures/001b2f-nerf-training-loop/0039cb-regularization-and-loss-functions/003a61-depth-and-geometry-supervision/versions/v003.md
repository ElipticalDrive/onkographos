# Depth and Geometry Supervision
Also called: Geometric regularization, Depth-supervised NeRF, Geometry-constrained optimization

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of training procedures for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that incorporate explicit geometric constraints—typically derived from monocular depth estimators, Structure-from-Motion (SfM) keypoints, or multi-view consistency errors—to resolve the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in volume rendering [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which relies solely on photometric consistency (RGB loss) and often produces plausible colors with incorrect geometry, these methods enforce geometric plausibility by either guiding the sampling intervals along rays, supervising the rendered depth distribution against a prior, or initializing the scene with estimated depth maps [S1]. This family is distinct from sibling methods like [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) (which defines the integration mechanics) and [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md) (which defines general penalties) by specifically targeting the geometric fidelity of the implicit surface representation using external or derived depth information [S1].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of constraining the optimization of the density field $\sigma$ to recover correct geometry. They generally operate by modifying the training loop of a standard NeRF [S1]. The core shared mechanism involves:
1.  **Geometric Prior Acquisition:** Obtaining depth information from an external source (monocular estimator, SfM) or internal consistency metrics [S1].
2.  **Geometric Constraint Application:** Integrating this prior into the optimization via one of three primary strategies:
    *   **Sampling Guidance:** Adjusting the near ($t_n$) and far ($t_f$) bounds of ray sampling to focus computation on regions likely to contain geometry [S1].
    *   **Distribution Supervision:** Minimizing the divergence (e.g., KL divergence) between the rendered ray termination distribution and a probabilistic depth prior [S1].
    *   **Initialization and Alignment:** Using depth maps to initialize the scene or align multi-view estimates to a canonical space [S1].
3.  **Loss Combination:** Combining the standard photometric loss (RGB reconstruction) with a geometric loss term (depth error, consistency error, or alignment loss) [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured environments by adapting sampling intervals $[t_n, t_f]$ using monocular depth priors and multi-view consistency error maps, rather than relying on explicit depth loss terms. Choose this when multi-view data is available but texture is sparse.

### [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md)
Addresses single-view panoramic reconstruction by initializing and supervising geometry using 360-degree depth estimation, resolving scale ambiguity via deformable alignment of perspective projections. Choose this when only a single panorama is available.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Accelerates optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and a probabilistic depth prior derived from sparse SfM keypoints. Choose this when sparse SfM data is available and multi-view consistency is not the primary constraint.

## Background
The concept of using depth supervision for NeRF was introduced to address the "shape-radiance ambiguity" where different geometry-radiance combinations can produce the same RGB image [S1]. Early work demonstrated that standard NeRF often fails to recover correct geometry in textureless regions [S1]. The specific methods in this family emerged from the need to handle sparse inputs, single-view scenarios, and the limitations of monocular depth estimators in 360-degree or unstructured settings [S1].
