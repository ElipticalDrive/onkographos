# Regularization and Loss Functions

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Diffusion Priors, Patch-based Regularization

## Overview
This family defines the objective functions and penalty terms applied during the optimization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) to ensure geometric consistency, prevent overfitting, and enforce photorealistic priors. While the [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) manages the iterative update process, these methods specifically address the ill-posed nature of inverse volume rendering, particularly in sparse-view or few-shot scenarios where standard photometric loss leads to degenerate solutions such as "floaters" or image-plane collapse.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family augment the standard photometric reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ with additional terms that constrain the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields. The total loss is generally formulated as:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}, k} $$
where $\lambda_k$ are hyperparameters balancing the regularization strength. The core mechanism involves computing gradients from these auxiliary losses and backpropagating them through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters. Common constraints include enforcing weight compactness along rays, penalizing density unsupported by multiple views, or aligning rendered patches with learned data distributions.

## Subtopics

### [Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md)
Uses a pre-trained Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM) as a prior on RGBD patches. It backpropagates the DDM's score function (gradient of log-likelihood) to the NeRF, enforcing photorealistic geometry and color distributions. It differs from text-to-3D methods by using the DDM on input training images rather than text prompts.

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss combining photometric loss with three geometric terms: Foreground (encouraging full ray absorption), Frustum (penalizing density in single-view frustums to prevent image-plane collapse), and Distribution (encouraging compact weight distribution along rays). It explicitly targets degenerate solutions in few-view settings.

### [RegNeRF](8582-regnerf.md)
A framework for sparse-view synthesis using a Normalizing Flow prior on radiance/density values and a Smoothness prior. It approximates gradients via finite differences on patch-based dataloaders, distinguishing it from methods using automatic differentiation on ray-based representations.

## Background
The problem of overfitting and geometric inconsistency in NeRFs, especially with limited input views, was identified early in the field. Standard NeRF training relies heavily on photometric loss, which can lead to "floaters" (semi-transparent geometry in empty space) and view-dependent artifacts. Geometric regularization techniques, such as those penalizing density outside multi-view consistent regions, were introduced to enforce 3D consistency [S1]. More recently, generative priors, including diffusion models and normalizing flows, have been integrated to constrain the solution space to photorealistic manifolds, addressing the ambiguity of sparse data [S1].
