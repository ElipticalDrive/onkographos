# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for indoor scenes by restricting the sampling of points along camera rays to adaptive ranges derived from monocular depth priors [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which samples uniformly between sparse reconstruction bounds, this method uses multi-view consistency errors to dynamically adjust the near and far sampling limits, ensuring that the neural network focuses on geometrically plausible regions [S1]. It further refines the output depth using confidence scores derived from RGB reconstruction errors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $N$ input views with RGB images and camera parameters (intrinsics $K$, relative poses $T^{i \to j}$).
- Adapted monocular depth priors $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$ for each view, obtained by fine-tuning a pre-trained monocular depth network on the target scene [S1].
- Hyperparameters: $K=4$ (number of cross-view errors used for error map calculation), $\alpha_l = 0.05$ (lower bound for range expansion), $\alpha_h = 0.15$ (upper bound for range expansion), $M$ (number of samples per ray) [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Optimized NeRF model parameters $\theta$ representing the implicit radiance field $F_\theta: (x, d) \to (c, \sigma)$ [S1].
- Refined depth map for each view.

**Data Structures:**
- **Depth Priors:** Dense 2D arrays $D^i$ of size $H \times W$ for each view $i$.
- **Error Maps:** Dense 2D arrays $e^i$ of size $H \times W$ representing the reprojection error for each view.
- **Confidence Scores:** Dense 2D arrays $S^i$ of size $H \times W$ representing pixel-wise confidence.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j \neq i$.
    Calculate the 2D projection coordinates $p^{i \to j}$ and projected depth $D^{i \to j}$ using camera intrinsics $K$ and relative pose $T^{i \to j}$ [S1]:
    $$ p^{i \to j}, D^{i \to j} = \text{proj}(K, T^{i \to j}, D^i) $$
    Sample the depth from the target view's prior at these coordinates:
    $$ D^{j'} = D^j(p^{i \to j}) $$
    Compute the relative reprojection error. The error map value $e^i$ for view $i$ is the average of the top $K$ minimum cross-view depth projection errors [S1].

2.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ corresponding to pixel $(u,v)$ in view $i$, let $D$ be the adapted depth prior value $D^i(u,v)$ and $e$ be the error map value $e^i(u,v)$.
    Compute the near ($t_n$) and far ($t_f$) bounds for sampling:
    $$ t_n = D(1 - \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D(1 + \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    where $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ restricts $x$ to the interval $[a, b]$ [S1].

3.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Query the NeRF network $F_\theta$ at each sample location $t_i$ along the ray to obtain color $c_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute the accumulated transmittance $T_i$ and rendering weights:
    $$ T_i = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) $$
    $$ w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1].
    Render the RGB color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and expected depth $D(\mathbf{r})$:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i c_i $$
    $$ D(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i t_i $$
    [S1].

5.  **Compute Confidence Scores:**
    Calculate the confidence score $S^i_j$ for pixel $j$ in view $i$ based on the $L_1$ difference between the ground truth RGB $C_{gt}^i(j)$ and the rendered RGB $C_{render}^i(j)$, normalized by 255:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} \| C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) \|_1 $$
    The norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

6.  **Depth Refinement (Post-Processing):**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the rendered depth map, weighted by the confidence scores $S^i$ [S1]. This step is optional for training but used for final output refinement [S1].

7.  **Optimization Loop:**
    Backpropagate gradients through the volume rendering equations to update the NeRF parameters $\theta$ using the photometric loss between $C(\mathbf{r})$ and $C_{gt}$ [S1].

## Subtopics

### ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training
Integrates pixel and depth-guided ray sampling into the Efficient NeRF (ENeRF) framework [S2]. It samples fewer rays per iteration than standard ENeRF, reducing GPU memory usage while improving performance [S2]. It uses a combined loss of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Perceptual Loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{mse} + \lambda' \mathcal{L}_{perc}$, with $\lambda' = 0.01$ [S2]. It is used for generalization tasks on datasets like DTU, Real Forward-facing, and NeRF Synthetic [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- The method adds overhead for multi-view depth projection and error map calculation during initialization [S1].
- ENeRF variant reduces memory usage by sampling fewer rays per iteration [S2].
- Standard NeRF sampling is $O(M)$ per ray; guided sampling maintains this complexity but with a tighter, adaptive range $[t_n, t_f]$ [S1].

**Performance:**
- Outperforms state-of-the-art depth estimation methods on ScanNet in terms of accuracy metrics [S1].
- Significantly outperforms original NeRF in view synthesis quality for indoor scenes [S1].
- Applying existing correspondence-based neural optimization (like CVD) to adapted depth priors degrades quality due to unreliable flow correspondences in poorly textured regions [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths:** Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor areas (e.g., walls) where standard NeRF fails [S1]. Balances diversity and precision of confidence distribution [S1].
- **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. Performance may degrade if the monocular prior is inaccurate [S1].

**When to Use:**
- Indoor scenes with poor texture where standard NeRF exhibits shape-radiance ambiguity [S1].
- When accurate depth estimation is required alongside view synthesis [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
- Richly textured outdoor scenes where standard NeRF assumptions hold [S1].
- Scenes where monocular depth priors are unreliable [S1].

## Background
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) were initially proposed in [33] (Mildenhall et al., 2020) [S1]. The Guided Optimization method was introduced to address the limitations of NeRF in indoor environments, specifically the shape-radiance ambiguity [S1]. The method builds upon adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. It is cited in *Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo* (2023) [S1].
