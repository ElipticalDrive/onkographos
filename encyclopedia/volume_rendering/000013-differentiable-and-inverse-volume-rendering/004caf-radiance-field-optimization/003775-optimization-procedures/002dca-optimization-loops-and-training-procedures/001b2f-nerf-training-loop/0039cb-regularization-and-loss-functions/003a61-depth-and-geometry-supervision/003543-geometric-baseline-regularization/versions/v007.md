# Geometric Baseline Regularization

Also called: Geometric Baseline, Geometric Loss

## Overview
Geometric Baseline Regularization is a composite loss function designed to prevent degenerate solutions in Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) optimization, particularly in few-view settings where the network might collapse to reproducing training images directly [S1]. It combines photometric reconstruction with three geometric priors: foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness. It serves as a strong baseline for differentiable volume rendering, distinguishing itself from sibling methods like [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md) by enforcing geometric priors without requiring external depth supervision [S1].

## How it works
The method minimizes a total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ composed of the photometric loss and three regularization terms [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Training images $\{I_i\}$, camera poses, ray origins $\mathbf{o}$ and directions $\mathbf{d}$, sampled points along rays $t_i$, density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields.
- **Outputs:** Gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ with respect to $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ for optimization [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- Ray sampling: $N$ points per ray, sorted by distance $t_i$.
- Rendering weights: $w_i = \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_j)$, where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ and $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1].
- Expected depth: $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i}$ [S1].
- Frustum count: $n_i$ is the number of training view frustums containing the point at $t_i$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{fg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} + \lambda_{\text{fr}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} + \lambda_{\text{dist}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} $$

1. **Photometric Loss** [S1]:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}(\sigma, \mathbf{c}) = \sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}} ||I_i - \mathbf{C}_i||_2 $$
   where $\mathcal{I}$ is the number of training images and $\mathbf{C}_i$ is the rendered color.

2. **Foreground Loss** [S1]:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} = \left( 1 - \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \right)^2 $$
   Encourages the sum of weights along a ray to equal 1, ensuring the ray is fully absorbed by scene geometry.

3. **Frustum Loss** [S1]:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} = \sum_i w_i \mathbf{1}(n_i \le 1) $$
   Penalizes density placed in only one view frustum ($n_i \le 1$), preventing the "copying" of training images in few-view scenarios. $\mathbf{1}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function.

4. **Distribution Loss** [S1]:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} = \frac{1}{\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})} \left( \sum_{i,j} w_i w_k \left| \frac{t_i + t_{i+1}}{2} - \frac{t_j + t_{j+1}}{2} \right| + \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i=1}^N w_i^2 (t_{i+1} - t_i) \right) $$
   Encourages compact weight distribution. The term is divided by expected depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ to increase strength for geometry close to the camera [S1]. Note: The source uses indices $i,j$ for the first sum and $i,k$ in the absolute difference term; standard interpretation implies summing over all pairs of samples or adjacent samples. The source text explicitly writes $\sum_{i,j} w_i w_k \dots$, which likely implies a typo for $\sum_{i,j} w_i w_j$ or similar, but we retain the source's variable names $w_k$ as written in the formula block [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_{\text{fg}}, \lambda_{\text{fr}}$: Not given in the sources.
- $\lambda_{\text{dist}}$: 0 for the first 2500 steps. Linearly increases to $1 \times 10^{-4}$ (DTU) or $1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ (LLFF) at 8000 steps [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ in $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ can be zero if no density is present. Guard against this [S1].
- **Missing Parameters:** $\lambda_{\text{fg}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{fr}}$ values are not specified in the sources [S1].
- **Ambiguous Formula:** The distribution loss formula uses $w_k$ in the summation $\sum_{i,j} w_i w_k$. This is likely a typo for $w_j$ [S1].
- **Ray Sampling Dependency:** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ requires midpoints $(t_i + t_{i+1})/2$, which depend on the specific ray sampling strategy [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead proportional to the number of rays and samples for computing the regularization terms. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ involves pairwise comparisons ($O(N^2)$) if implemented naively as written, though optimizations may exist.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frustum counts $n_i$ for each sample point, increasing memory usage per ray [S1].
- **Strengths:** Effective at preventing degenerate "copying" solutions in few-view settings [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of $\lambda_{\text{dist}}$ schedule. Performance varies by dataset (DTU vs LLFF) [S1].

## Background
Originated in the DiffusioNeRF paper (CVPR 2023) as a strong baseline for regularizing NeRFs using denoising diffusion models [S1]. It builds on the idea that geometric priors can stabilize optimization without explicit depth supervision.
