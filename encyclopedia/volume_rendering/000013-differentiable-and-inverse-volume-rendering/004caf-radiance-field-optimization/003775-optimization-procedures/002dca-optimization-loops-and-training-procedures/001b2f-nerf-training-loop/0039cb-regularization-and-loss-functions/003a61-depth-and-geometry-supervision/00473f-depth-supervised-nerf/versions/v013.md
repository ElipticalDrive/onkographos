# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints generated during Structure-from-Motion (SfM) preprocessing [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF imposes a loss that forces the rendered ray termination distribution to match the surface priors provided by these keypoints, effectively regularizing the density field [S1]. This approach is distinct from [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md) which enforces smoothness, and [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md) which typically uses dense depth maps; DS-NeRF specifically leverages the "free" sparse geometry already available from standard SfM pipelines like COLMAP [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $\{I_j\}$ with known camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM), and the corresponding sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ with visibility flags $\{X_j\}$ and reprojection errors $\{\hat{\sigma}_i\}$ from the SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLPs predicting density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) and novel view renderings [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoint Set $X$:** A list of 3D points $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Visibility $X_j$:** A subset of $X$ visible from camera $j$.
*   **Depth Estimate $D_{ij}$:** For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, the depth is the re-projected z-value of $x_i$ using camera $P_j$ [S1].
*   **Uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$:** The average reprojection error of keypoint $x_i$ across all views where it was detected [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses and generate a sparse 3D point cloud $X$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Calculation:** For each training image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$:
    *   Identify visible keypoints $X_j \subset X$ [S1].
    *   For each $x_i \in X_j$, compute the estimated depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the z-component in camera coordinates [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ as the average reprojection error of $x_i$ across all views [S1].
3.  **Ray Generation:**
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For each keypoint ray, determine the ray origin and direction from the image coordinates of the detected keypoint $x_i$ in camera $j$. In local camera coordinates, the direction is defined by the image coordinates and $-1$ in the camera axis [S1].
    *   Interpolate the ground truth RGB color $\mathbf{C}(r)$ from the input image $I_j$ at the keypoint's continuous image coordinates [S1].
4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Shoot the ray $r$ into the scene using the standard NeRF sampling procedure (sample points $t_k$ along the ray between near $t_n$ and far $t_f$ bounds) [S1].
    *   Compute the density $\sigma(t_k)$ and color $\mathbf{c}(t_k)$ at each sample point [S1].
    *   Compute the ray termination distribution $h(t)$, which represents the probability that a ray terminates at distance $t$:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right)\sigma(t) $$
        where $T(t)$ is the transmittance [S1].
    *   Discretize $h(t)$ at sample points $t_k$ with step size $\Delta t_k$:
        $$ h_k \approx h(t_k)\Delta t_k $$
        Note: $h(t)$ is a valid probability distribution assuming $\sigma(s) \ge 0$ and the scene is bounded by an opaque wall at $t_f$ such that $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$ [S1].
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Color Loss:** Compute the photometric loss for the keypoint ray:
        $$ L_{Color} = || \widehat{\mathcal{C}}(r) - \mathbf{C}(r) ||_2^2 $$
        where $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(r) = \sum_k h_k \mathbf{c}(t_k)$ [S1].
    *   **Depth Loss:** Model the true depth of the first surface as a random variable $D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i^2)$ [S1]. Minimize the KL divergence between the rendered distribution $h(t)$ and this noisy depth distribution. The resulting loss term is:
        $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\log h(t) \exp \left( -\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \right] dt \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
        [S1]. An alternative is Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$, but KL divergence yields fewer artifacts [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ L = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth} $$
        where $\lambda_D$ is a hyperparameter balancing the losses [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def compute_depth_loss(h_k, t_k, D_ij, sigma_hat_i, delta_t_k):
    """
    Computes the KL-divergence-based depth loss for a single ray/keypoint.
    
    Args:
        h_k: Tensor of shape (num_samples,), discretized ray termination distribution.
        t_k: Tensor of shape (num_samples,), sample distances along the ray.
        D_ij: Float, COLMAP-estimated depth for the keypoint.
        sigma_hat_i: Float, average reprojection error (uncertainty) for the keypoint.
        delta_t_k: Tensor of shape (num_samples,), step size between samples.
        
    Returns:
        Float, the depth loss value.
    """
    # Gaussian kernel for the noisy depth distribution
    # exp(-(t - D)^2 / (2 * sigma^2))
    gaussian_kernel = torch.exp(-((t_k - D_ij) ** 2) / (2 * sigma_hat_i ** 2))
    
    # Log-probability of the rendered distribution
    # -log(h_k)
    log_h = torch.log(h_k + 1e-8) # Add epsilon for numerical stability
    
    # Integrate: sum(-log(h_k) * gaussian_kernel * delta_t_k)
    # This approximates E[-log h(t) * exp(...)]
    loss = -torch.sum(log_h * gaussian_kernel * delta_t_k)
    
    return loss

def train_step_ds_nerf(images, cameras, keypoints, nerf_model, lambda_D, num_samples):
    """
    Single training iteration for DS-NeRF.
    
    Args:
        images: Tensor of shape (batch_size, H, W, 3), input images.
        cameras: List of camera parameters for the batch.
        keypoints: List of keypoints visible in each image. 
                   Each element is a dict with 'x_i' (3D point), 'D_ij' (depth), 
                   'sigma_hat_i' (uncertainty), 'u, v' (image coordinates).
        nerf_model: The NeRF MLP model.
        lambda_D: Weight for the depth loss.
        num_samples: Number of samples per ray.
        
    Returns:
        Total loss value.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Generate standard rays for color supervision (omitted for brevity, standard NeRF)
    # ... compute standard rays, render, compute L_color ...
    
    # 2. Generate keypoint rays for depth supervision
    for i, camera in enumerate(cameras):
        visible_keypoints = keypoints[i]
        
        for kp in visible_keypoints:
            # Extract keypoint data
            D_ij = kp['D_ij']
            sigma_hat_i = kp['sigma_hat_i']
            u, v = kp['u'], kp['v']
            
            # Interpolate RGB from image for color supervision
            # Assuming images are normalized to [0, 1] or similar
            rgb_gt = F.grid_sample(
                images[i].unsqueeze(0), 
                torch.tensor([[[v, u]]]).unsqueeze(0).float(), 
                align_corners=False
            ).squeeze()
            
            # Construct ray from keypoint image coordinates
            # Direction in local camera coordinates: image coords + -1 in camera axis
            ray_origin, ray_direction = construct_ray_from_keypoint(camera, u, v)
            
            # Sample points along the ray
            t_vals = sample_along_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, num_samples)
            
            # Query NeRF for density and color
            sigma, color = nerf_model.sample_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, t_vals)
            
            # Compute transmittance and termination distribution h(t)
            # T(t) = exp(-int_0^t sigma(s) ds)
            # h(t) = T(t) * sigma(t)
            # Discretized: h_k = T_k * sigma_k * delta_t_k
            # Note: Standard NeRF alpha computation:
            # alpha_k = 1 - exp(-sigma_k * delta_t_k)
            # T_k = prod_{j<k} (1 - alpha_j)
            # h_k = alpha_k * T_k
            
            delta_t = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
            # Append a small delta_t for the last sample if needed, or handle boundaries
            # Assuming standard NeRF sampling where t_vals includes near/far bounds
            
            # Compute alpha and T
            alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta_t)
            T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha + 1e-10, dim=0) # Cumulative product of transmittance
            # Shift T to align with samples: T_k is transmittance BEFORE sample k
            T_shifted = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:1]), T[:-1]], dim=0)
            
            # h_k is the probability mass at sample k
            h_k = alpha * T_shifted
            
            # Normalize h_k to ensure it sums to 1 (optional but good for stability)
            # h_k = h_k / (torch.sum(h_k) + 1e-8)
            
            # Compute Color Loss
            rendered_color = torch.sum(h_k.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=0)
            l_color = F.mse_loss(rendered_color, rgb_gt)
            
            # Compute Depth Loss
            # h_k must be associated with t_k. 
            # Note: h_k corresponds to the interval around t_k or the sample point t_k.
            # In the formula, delta_t_k is part of the integral approximation.
            # We use the delta_t computed above.
            l_depth = compute_depth_loss(h_k, t_vals[:-1], D_ij, sigma_hat_i, delta_t)
            
            # Accumulate losses
            # Allocate portion of rays to keypoint rays, so we might scale loss or just add
            total_loss += l_color + lambda_D * l_depth
            
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   Training iterations: $O(10k - 100k)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   Image resolution: $400 \times 300$ in experiments [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Poor SfM:** If the SfM solver produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate keypoints, optimization may fail [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes keypoint uncertainty is Gaussian. This may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** While DS-NeRF itself generalizes, combining it with methods like pixelNeRF can suffer from domain gaps if pre-trained on different datasets (e.g., DTU vs. NeRF Real) [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a strong geometry prior from its Plane Sweep Volume initialization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time per iteration is comparable to standard NeRF. On an RTX A5000, DS-NeRF takes $\sim 362.4$ ms/iter vs. $\sim 359.8$ ms/iter for standard NeRF [S1].
*   **Speedup:** DS-NeRF achieves target test PSNR in 2-3x fewer training iterations than standard NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, this translates to $\sim 13$ hours faster convergence [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as it reuses the same ray marching structure and only adds supervision for a subset of rays [S1].
*   **Performance:** Improves PSNR significantly with few views. For example, on NeRF Real with 5 views, DS-NeRF achieves 22.6 PSNR (100% keypoints) vs. 18.2 for standard NeRF [S1]. With 2 views, standard NeRF fails to converge to useful quality, while DS-NeRF succeeds [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with sparse views, requires no additional data collection (uses "free" SfM output), and is compatible with other NeRF variants [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the success of SfM; fails if SfM fails. Assumes Gaussian uncertainty. Dense MVS depth is not used in training, only for evaluation [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in "Depth-supervised NeRF: Fewer Views and Faster Training for Free" (CVPR 2022) [S1].
*   **History:** The method leverages the fact that NeRF pipelines already require SfM for camera pose estimation, which produces sparse 3D points. DS-NeRF formalizes the use of these points as depth supervision to enforce that the ray termination distribution matches surface priors [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1]. It is compatible with other NeRF methods and can use depth from scanned sensors or RGB-D outputs in addition to SfM keypoints [S1].
