# RePaint-NeRF Limitations

Also called: NeRF editing constraints

## Overview
This article documents the specific failure modes and resource constraints of the RePaint-NeRF method [S1], which edits NeRF content using 2D diffusion model guidance. It addresses the limitations that prevent the method from scaling to large scenes or handling drastic geometric transformations, distinguishing it from siblings that may use different priors or optimization strategies.

## Subtopics

### Performance and Scalability Constraints
The RePaint-NeRF framework is computationally intensive, requiring more than 30 minutes to generate a single high-quality 3D model at the object level [S1]. This high time and memory cost prevents the method from scaling to larger resolutions or entire scenes [S1]. While other works have addressed time issues in related diffusion-based methods like DreamFusion, RePaint-NeRF remains limited to object-level editing due to these resource demands [S1].

### Geometric Consistency and Shape Fidelity
The method struggles when the target object shape differs significantly from the source object [S1]. In cases of drastic geometric changes, the training process becomes difficult, often resulting in incomplete edits where the new object fails to fully replace the old one [S1]. This suggests that the diffusion guidance may not provide sufficient structural constraints for topological changes.

### View-Dependent Artifacts
The method is sensitive to camera angles, leading to geometric artifacts at image edges [S1]. Objects generated at the periphery of the view may incorrectly combine features from multiple source objects, whereas central objects are rendered more normally [S1]. This indicates that the semantic mask acquisition or diffusion guidance lacks robustness in handling occluded or partially visible regions from extreme viewpoints.

## Background
The limitations described herein originate from the RePaint-NeRF paper, which proposed a NeRF editing framework based on pre-trained 2D diffusion model guidance [S1]. The method uses gradient guidance from the diffusion model to regenerate masked parts of the scene, supplemented by background prompts and CLIP loss to handle invisible backgrounds, and depth information for semantic mask acquisition [S1]. The cited limitations highlight the trade-offs between using 2D priors for 3D editing without explicit 3D structural constraints.
