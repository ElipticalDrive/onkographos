# Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline

Also called: Deceptive-3DGS, Deceptive-NeRF

## Overview
Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS is a sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training [S1]. Unlike methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations for uncertainty estimation, this approach acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, thereby enforcing epipolar constraints with minimal additional computation [S1]. It addresses the problem of artifacts and missing geometry in sparse-view settings by using a "deceptive" diffusion model conditioned on rendered RGB, depth, and uncertainty maps to generate photorealistic pseudo-observations that guide the optimization of the 3D representation [S1].

## How it works
The method operates in an iterative loop that alternates between generating pseudo-observations via a diffusion model and optimizing the 3D representation using both real and synthetic data.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $W$ input images $I = \{I_1, \dots, I_W\}$ with calibrated camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** An optimized NeRF or 3DGS representation $F_\Theta$ and a set of accumulated pseudo-observations.

### Data Structures
1.  **NeRF/3DGS Representation ($F_\Theta$):** Maps 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $c$ (or Gaussian attributes for 3DGS).
2.  **Deceptive Diffusion Model ($G$):** A latent diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion with ControlNet) conditioned on coarse RGB, depth, uncertainty, and text embeddings.
    *   **ControlNet Channels:** 5 channels total (3 for coarse RGB, 1 for depth, 1 for uncertainty) [S1].
3.  **Uncertainty Map ($U_A$):** A 2D map where each pixel value is the squared difference between a warped real image and the rendered image at a novel view.
4.  **Pseudo-Observation ($P_A$):** A synthetic RGB image generated by $G$.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initial Training:** Train the NeRF/3DGS representation $F_\Theta$ using the few-view input images $I$ for a set number of epochs to obtain a coarse reconstruction [S1].
2.  **Novel View Sampling:** Randomly sample a novel viewpoint $A$ within the bounding box defined by the outermost input views [S1].
3.  **Rendering:** At viewpoint $A$, render:
    *   RGB image $\hat{I}_A$.
    *   Depth map $D_A$.
    *   Uncertainty map $U_A$ (see *Uncertainty Map Generation* below).
4.  **Text Embedding Derivation:**
    *   Generate an initial text prompt $s_0$ using a pre-trained image captioning network on $\hat{I}_A$ [S1].
    *   Optimize a shared latent text embedding $s^*$ using textual inversion, shared across all input observations and coarse images [S1].
    *   Form the composite feature $s = [s_0, s^*]$ by concatenating the embeddings [S1].
5.  **Pseudo-Observation Generation:** Generate the pseudo-observation $P_A$ using the deceptive diffusion model:
    $$ P_A = G(\hat{I}_A, D_A, U_A, s) $$
    where $G$ is the fine-tuned diffusion model [S1].
6.  **Accumulation:** Add $P_A$ to the set of pseudo-observations.
7.  **Filtering:** Discard 20% of the accumulated pseudo-observations with the lowest perceptual similarity to the input images, quantified via the LPIPS metric [S1].
8.  **Optimization:** Fine-tune the NeRF/3DGS parameters $\Theta$ using a composite loss function that includes photometric loss (between rendered and target images, including pseudo-observations), depth loss, and regularization [S1].
9.  **Iteration:** Repeat steps 2–8 until a sufficient number of observations are accumulated [S1].

### Uncertainty Map Generation
To enforce view consistency, the method computes an uncertainty map $U_A$ for a novel view $A$ by warping the nearest input view $B$ to $A$ and comparing it to the rendered image $\hat{I}_A$ [S1].

1.  Identify $B$ as the nearest input viewpoint to $A$ [S1].
2.  Compute the warped image $I_{B \rightarrow A}$:
    $$ I_{B \rightarrow A} = \psi(I_B; D_A, R_{B \rightarrow A}) $$
    where $R_{B \rightarrow A}$ is the viewpoint difference [S1].
3.  For each pixel $p$ in $\hat{I}_A$, compute the transformed pixel location $q$ in $I_B$:
    $$ q = K R_{B \rightarrow A} D_A(p) K^{-1} p $$
    where $K$ is the camera parameter matrix [S1].
4.  Compute the uncertainty map:
    $$ U_A = (I_{B \rightarrow A} - \hat{I}_A)^2 $$
    defined as the squares of the distance between the warped image and the rendered image [S1].

### Depth Rendering
The depth map $D_A$ is rendered as a weighted composition of distances traveled from the origin:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{D}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{T}(t_k) \alpha (\sigma(t_k) \delta_k) $$
where $\hat{T}(t_k)$ is transmittance, $\alpha$ is opacity, $\sigma$ is density, and $\delta_k$ is the distance traveled [S1].

### Diffusion Model Fine-Tuning
The deceptive diffusion model $G$ is fine-tuned using a dataset of quartets $(I_{fine}, I_{coarse}, D, U)$ [S1]. The objective is:
$$ \min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{E}, t, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)} \| \epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(z_t, t, c(I_{coarse}, D, U, s)) \|_2^2 $$
where:
*   $z \sim \mathcal{E}$ is the latent code from encoder $\mathcal{E}$.
*   $t \sim [1, 1000]$ is the diffusion time step.
*   $\epsilon$ is the noise.
*   $\epsilon_{\theta}$ is the denoiser U-Net.
*   $c$ is the embedding of the coarse RGB image, depth, uncertainty, and text embedding $s$ [S1].

### Data Augmentation for Diffusion Training
To mitigate computational costs of training NeRF duos for large datasets, data augmentation is used:
1.  Add random Gaussian noise with standard deviation $\sigma = 0.3$ to 60,000 images from Hypersim [S1].
2.  Use these noisy images and their depth maps as training inputs, and the original RGB images as targets [S1].
3.  **Pitfall:** When training with this noisy RGB-depth data augmentation, uncertainty maps are **not** fed into the diffusion model [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Discard Rate:** 20% of pseudo-observations with the lowest perceptual similarity are discarded [S1].
*   **LPIPS Metric:** Used to quantify perceptual similarity for filtering [S1].
*   **ControlNet:** Used to implement the training paradigm while preserving pre-trained 2D diffusion model weights [S1].
*   **Gaussian Noise Standard Deviation:** 0.3, added to 60,000 images from Hypersim for data augmentation [S1].
*   **Control Map Channels:** 5 channels (3 for coarse RGB, 1 for depth, 1 for uncertainty) [S1].
*   **Image Resolution:** All images are randomly cropped to $512 \times 512$ resolution [S1].
*   **Training Dataset Size:** 200 scenes and approximately 20,000 images for the major section of the training dataset, with Hypersim and CO3D each contributing half [S1].
*   **Coarse NeRF Training Data:** Coarse NeRFs are trained only with one-fifth of input images [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Image Captioning Efficacy:** Image captioning efficacy diminishes for images of lower quality or those with pronounced artifacts. Textual inversion is used to counteract this by optimizing a shared latent text embedding [S1].
*   **Data Augmentation Condition:** When training the diffusion model with noisy RGB-depth data augmentation (Gaussian noise), uncertainty maps are **not** fed into the diffusion model [S1].
*   **Precision:** The uncertainty map calculation involves pixel-wise warping and squaring differences; ensure floating-point precision is maintained to avoid artifacts in $U_A$ [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds significant computational overhead due to the iterative generation of pseudo-observations via a diffusion model and the fine-tuning of the 3D representation. Each iteration requires rendering, warping, diffusion inference, and optimization steps [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the NeRF/3DGS representation, the diffusion model (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet), and the accumulated pseudo-observations. The dataset for training the diffusion model (200 scenes, ~20,000 images) also requires significant storage [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the LLFF dataset, Deceptive-NeRF achieves a PSNR of 19.87 (3-view), 21.75 (6-view), and 23.22 (9-view), outperforming PixelNeRF, MVSNeRF, DietNeRF, RegNeRF, and FreeNeRF [S1]. Deceptive-3DGS achieves a PSNR of 19.95 (3-view), 23.73 (6-view), and 24.15 (9-view) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enforces 3D consistency through uncertainty maps derived from epipolar constraints; generates high-quality photorealistic pseudo-observations; effective for few-shot novel view synthesis and super-resolution [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to diffusion model usage; relies on the quality of the initial coarse reconstruction; performance may degrade if the initial NeRF/3DGS is too poor to provide meaningful depth/uncertainty guidance [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Sparse-view reconstruction where standard NeRF/3DGS fails due to insufficient input views; tasks requiring high-fidelity novel view synthesis from limited data [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Real-time applications due to high computational cost; scenarios with very low-quality input images where captioning and warping may fail [S1].

## Background
Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS was introduced by X. Liu et al. in the paper "Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS: Diffusion-Generated Pseudo-Observations for High-Quality Sparse-View Reconstruction" [S1]. The method leverages latent diffusion models (specifically Stable Diffusion with ControlNet) to generate pseudo-observations that densify sparse input data. It builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and inverse rendering, integrating semantic and geometric guidance to improve reconstruction quality [S1]. The approach is particularly relevant in the context of few-shot 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis, addressing the limitations of traditional methods that struggle with sparse inputs [S1].
