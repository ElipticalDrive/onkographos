# Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline

Also called: Deceptive-3DGS, Deceptive-NeRF

## Overview
Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS is a sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training [S1]. Unlike methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations to estimate uncertainty, this approach acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, thereby enforcing epipolar constraints with minimal additional computation [S1]. It operates by alternating between generating view-consistent pseudo-observations and optimizing the 3D representation, effectively "deceiving" the renderer with high-quality synthetic data to fill sparse input gaps [S1].

## How it works
The method enhances a pre-trained or partially trained NeRF/3DGS representation $F_\Theta$ mapping $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ (or Gaussian parameters) by iteratively adding synthetic views. The core mechanism involves generating pseudo-observations conditioned on rendered RGB, depth, and an uncertainty map derived from view consistency checks.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: A set of $W$ input images $I = \{I_1, \dots, I_W\}$ with calibrated camera poses. A pre-trained diffusion model $G$ (specifically a Stable Diffusion model with ControlNet).
*   **Outputs**: An optimized NeRF/3DGS representation and a set of accumulated pseudo-observations $P$.

### Data Structures
1.  **NeRF/3DGS Representation**: Standard parameters for density/color or Gaussian splats.
2.  **ControlNet Input Tensor**: A 5-channel tensor per image:
    *   Channels 0-2: Coarse RGB image $\hat{I}_{coarse}$ (resolution $512 \times 512$).
    *   Channel 3: Depth map $D$.
    *   Channel 4: Uncertainty map $U$.
3.  **Text Embedding**: A composite vector $s = [s_0, s^*]$, where $s_0$ is a text prompt and $s^*$ is a shared latent text embedding.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initial Training**: Train the NeRF/3DGS representation using the few input images $I$.
2.  **Coarse Reconstruction**: After a set number of epochs, obtain a coarse reconstruction. [S1]
3.  **Novel View Sampling**: Randomly sample a novel viewpoint $A$ within a bounding box defined by the outermost input views. [S1]
4.  **Rendering**: At viewpoint $A$, render:
    *   RGB image $\hat{I}_A$.
    *   Depth map $\hat{D}_A$.
    *   Uncertainty map $U_A$.
5.  **Uncertainty Map Generation**:
    *   Identify the nearest input viewpoint $B$ to $A$.
    *   Compute the warped image $I_{B \rightarrow A}$ from $I_B$ using $\hat{D}_A$ and the viewpoint difference $R_{B \rightarrow A}$.
    *   Calculate $U_A$ as the squared difference between the warped image and the rendered image.
6.  **Text Embedding Derivation**:
    *   Generate a text prompt $s_0$ using a pre-trained image captioning network on $\hat{I}_A$.
    *   Optimize a shared latent text embedding $s^*$ using textual inversion on all input observations and coarse images.
    *   Form the composite feature $s = [s_0, s^*]$.
7.  **Pseudo-Observation Generation**: Generate the pseudo-observation $P_A$ using the deceptive diffusion model:
    $$ P_A = \operatorname{G}(\hat{I}_A, \hat{D}_A, U_A, s) $$
    where $G$ is the fine-tuned diffusion model conditioned on the coarse image, depth, uncertainty, and text embedding.
8.  **Filtering**: Discard 20% of the generated pseudo-observations with the lowest perceptual similarity to input images, quantified via the LPIPS metric. [S1]
9.  **Optimization**: Continue training the NeRF/3DGS representation using the combination of real input images and the accumulated pseudo-observations.
10. **Iteration**: Repeat steps 3-9 until a sufficient number of observations are accumulated.

### Formulas

**Depth Rendering**
The depth value for each ray is computed as a weighted composition of distances traveled:
$$ \hat{\mathbf{D}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{T}(t_k) \alpha (\sigma(t_k) \delta_k) $$
where $\hat{T}(t_k)$ is the transmittance, $\alpha$ is the opacity, $\sigma$ is the density, and $\delta_k$ is the distance traveled at sample point $t_k$ [S1].

**Warped Image**
The ground truth image $I_B$ from the nearest input view $B$ is warped to the novel view $A$:
$$ I_{B \rightarrow A} = \psi(I_B; \hat{D}_A, R_{B \rightarrow A}) $$
where $R_{B \rightarrow A}$ is the viewpoint difference [S1].

**Pixel Transformation**
Each pixel location $p$ in the rendered image $\hat{I}_A$ is transformed to location $q$ in $I_B$:
$$ q = K R_{B \rightarrow A} \hat{D}_A(p) K^{-1} p $$
where $K$ is the camera parameter matrix [S1].

**Uncertainty Map**
The uncertainty map $U_A$ at viewpoint $A$ is defined as the squares of the distance between the warped image and the rendered image:
$$ U_A = (I_{B \rightarrow A} - \hat{I}_A)^2 $$
This measure enforces view consistency by leveraging epipolar constraints [S1].

**Pseudo-Observation Generation**
The deceptive diffusion model $G$ generates the pseudo-observation:
$$ P_A = \operatorname{G}(\hat{I}_A, \hat{D}_A, U_A) $$
The model conditions on the coarse RGB, depth, and uncertainty to rectify NeRF/3DGS artifacts [S1].

**Diffusion Fine-Tuning Objective**
The diffusion model is fine-tuned by solving:
$$ \min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{E}, t, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)} \| \epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}(z_t, t, c(I_{\text{coarse}}, D, U, s)) \|_2^2 $$
where $z \sim \mathcal{E}$ is the latent code, $t \sim [1, 1000]$ is the diffusion time step, $\epsilon$ is noise, $\epsilon_{\theta}$ is the denoiser U-Net, and $c$ is the embedding of the coarse image, depth, uncertainty, and text embedding $s$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Discard Rate**: 20% of pseudo-observations are discarded based on LPIPS similarity [S1].
*   **Image Resolution**: All images are randomly cropped to $512 \times 512$ [S1].
*   **ControlNet Channels**: 5 channels (3 RGB, 1 Depth, 1 Uncertainty) [S1].
*   **Gaussian Noise**: Standard deviation of 0.3, added to 60,000 images for data augmentation [S1].
*   **Coarse NeRF Data**: Trained on one-fifth of input images [S1].
*   **Training Dataset**: 200 scenes, ~20,000 images (Hypersim and CO3D) [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Captioning Efficacy**: Image captioning efficacy diminishes for low-quality images or those with pronounced artifacts. Textual inversion is used to optimize a shared latent text embedding $s^*$ to counteract this [S1].
*   **Data Augmentation Limitation**: When training with noisy RGB-depth data augmentation (adding Gaussian noise to RGB images), uncertainty maps are **not** fed into the diffusion model [S1].
*   **Precision**: The uncertainty map calculation relies on accurate depth rendering and camera calibration; errors in $K$ or depth estimation will propagate into $U_A$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The method adds significant computational overhead due to the iterative generation of pseudo-observations via a diffusion model and the subsequent optimization of the 3D representation. Each iteration involves rendering, warping, diffusion sampling, and training. [S1]
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the accumulated pseudo-observations and the diffusion model weights. The ControlNet architecture adds memory overhead for the control maps. [S1]
*   **Measured Performance**: On the LLFF dataset, Deceptive-NeRF achieved a PSNR of 19.87 (3-view), 21.75 (6-view), and 23.22 (9-view), outperforming SRF, PixelNeRF, MVSNeRF, DietNeRF, RegNeRF, and FreeNeRF. Deceptive-3DGS achieved 19.95, 21.75, and 23.22 respectively. [S1]
*   **Strengths**: Enforces view consistency through uncertainty maps derived from epipolar constraints, minimizing additional computation compared to ensemble or variational methods. Generates high-quality photorealistic pseudo-observations that rectify NeRF/3DGS artifacts. [S1]
*   **Limitations**: Relies on the quality of the diffusion model and the accuracy of the initial coarse reconstruction. Text captioning may fail on low-quality images, requiring textual inversion. [S1]
*   **When to Use**: Sparse-view reconstruction where input views are insufficient for high-quality NeRF/3DGS rendering. [S1]
*   **When Not to Use**: When computational resources are extremely limited, as the diffusion-based augmentation is expensive. [S1]

## Background
Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS was introduced by X. Liu et al. in the paper "Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS: Diffusion-Generated Pseudo-Observations for High-Quality Sparse-View Reconstruction" [S1]. The method leverages latent diffusion models (specifically Stable Diffusion with ControlNet) to generate pseudo-observations that guide the training of NeRF and 3DGS representations. It addresses the challenge of sparse-view reconstruction by "deceiving" the renderer with synthetic data that is consistent with the input views. The method was evaluated on datasets including Hypersim, ScanNet, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].
