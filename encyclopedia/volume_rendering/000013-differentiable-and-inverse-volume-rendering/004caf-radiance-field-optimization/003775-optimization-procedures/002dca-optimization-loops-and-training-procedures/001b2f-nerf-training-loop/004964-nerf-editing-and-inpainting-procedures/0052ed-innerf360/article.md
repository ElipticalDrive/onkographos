# InNeRF360

Also called: InNeRF360

## Overview
InNeRF360 is a method for 3D scene inpainting and editing that combines multiview-consistent 2D priors with 3D diffusion guidance to reconstruct missing or edited regions in a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF). It addresses the problem of generating geometrically and photometrically consistent content for occluded or removed objects by first establishing a coherent 2D inpainting across all views and then refining the 3D representation. It differs from direct 3D optimization methods by decoupling the generation of plausible content (via 2D inpainting) from the 3D reconstruction, using 3D diffusion priors primarily to eliminate artifacts and enforce consistency rather than generating the primary geometry.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A pre-trained NeRF representation $f_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, a set of input images $\{I_i\}$, and binary masks $\{M_i\}$ indicating regions to be inpainted or edited.
*   **Outputs:** An updated NeRF parameters $\theta'$ representing the scene with inpainted content.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Segmentation Mask:** A binary mask $M_{2D}$ derived from 2D segmentation, refined for view consistency.
*   **Inpainted Image:** A 2D image $I_{inpaint}$ generated by a 2D inpainting model.
*   **NeRF Parameters:** $\theta$, typically including MLP weights for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Multiview Consistent Segmentation Initialization:**
    *   Initialize segmentations for the target regions using the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [S1].
    *   Refine these segmentations using depth-warping to ensure they are consistent across multiple views. This produces a set of view-consistent 2D masks $\{M_i^{consistent}\}$ [S1].

2.  **Multiview 2D Inpainting:**
    *   Apply a 2D image inpainting model (e.g., a diffusion-based inpainter) to each view $i$ using the consistent masks $M_i^{consistent}$ and the original images $I_i$.
    *   Generate inpainted images $\{I_i^{inpaint}\}$ that fill the masked regions with plausible content [S1].

3.  **NeRF Training from Inpainted Views:**
    *   Train a new NeRF (or fine-tune the existing one) using the inpainted images $\{I_i^{inpaint}\}$ as supervision.
    *   The loss function includes the standard photometric reconstruction loss on the inpainted regions. This step establishes the initial geometry and appearance of the new content [S1].

4.  **3D Diffusion Prior Fine-tuning:**
    *   To eliminate artifacts and ensure 3D consistency, fine-tune the NeRF using 3D diffusion priors.
    *   This involves computing gradients from a 3D diffusion model (likely via Score Distillation Sampling or similar) to guide the NeRF parameters $\theta$ towards distributions consistent with 3D generative priors [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total loss $L$ during the fine-tuning phase can be conceptualized as:
$$
L = L_{render} + \lambda_{diff} L_{diff}
$$
where $L_{render}$ is the photometric loss between rendered images and inpainted images, and $L_{diff}$ is the 3D diffusion prior loss [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{diff}$: Weight for the diffusion prior loss. Not given in the sources.
*   Learning rate for NeRF optimization. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Inconsistency in 2D Inpainting:** If the 2D inpainter generates inconsistent content across views, the initial NeRF training may produce geometric artifacts. The depth-warping refinement step mitigates this but may not fully resolve severe inconsistencies.
*   **Over-smoothing:** Heavy reliance on diffusion priors may lead to over-smoothed or hallucinated details that deviate from the original scene's texture.
*   **Mask Accuracy:** The quality of the final inpainting is heavily dependent on the accuracy of the initial SAM segmentation and the subsequent depth-warping refinement.

## Subtopics

### ViCA-NeRF
ViCA-NeRF is a method for 3D editing that enhances efficiency and consistency by operating in the latent domain [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF editing by focusing on local editing via latent domain refinement rather than direct field optimization [S1]. It builds upon IN2N with improvements, initializing and refining a NeRF model within the latent domain using a refinement adapter with residual and self-attention mechanisms. It iteratively updates the training set with edited latents and employs a delta module for targeted editing guided by prompts [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method involves multiple stages: SAM segmentation, depth-warping, 2D inpainting (potentially expensive diffusion-based), NeRF training, and 3D diffusion fine-tuning. The overall process is computationally intensive, primarily due to the iterative nature of NeRF training and diffusion sampling.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the NeRF parameters, intermediate rendered images, and potentially latent representations if ViCA-NeRF-like optimizations are used.
*   **Strengths:** Produces visually plausible and consistent inpainted content by leveraging strong 2D priors and refining with 3D consistency checks. The decoupled approach allows for the use of state-of-the-art 2D inpainting models.
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive. Relies on the quality of 2D inpainting, which may struggle with complex occlusions or lack of texture. The 3D diffusion prior may introduce artifacts if not tuned correctly.
*   **When to use:** When high-quality inpainting of missing regions in a NeRF is required, and computational resources are available for multi-stage optimization.
*   **When not to use:** For real-time applications or when strict geometric fidelity to the original scene (without generative hallucination) is critical.

## Background
InNeRF360 was introduced in a survey on 3D editing based on NeRF and 3DGS [S1]. It represents a trend in 3D editing that combines 2D generative models with 3D scene representations to achieve high-quality results. The method builds on earlier works like IN2N and incorporates advancements in multiview consistency and diffusion priors [S1].
