# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Content Editing

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field ($F_\Theta$) to remove, add, or alter content while maintaining multi-view consistency. Unlike standard training which optimizes $\Theta$ to fit input images, these methods intercept the rendering pipeline or optimize parameters using external guidance (2D priors, diffusion models, or semantic features) to achieve specific edits. They set themselves apart from sibling methods by focusing on the *modification* of an existing scene representation rather than initial reconstruction, utilizing techniques ranging from post-hoc inference-time interception to iterative optimization with synthetic pseudo-observations.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a pre-trained NeRF representation $F_\Theta$ mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. The core mechanism involves either:
1.  **Optimization-based Editing:** Updating parameters $\Theta$ by minimizing a loss function that combines photometric fidelity to target images (real or synthetic) with geometric consistency constraints (e.g., depth, multi-view warping).
2.  **Inference-time Interception:** Modifying the query logic during volume rendering by injecting external guidance (semantic probabilities, masks, or transformed coordinates) to selectively apply transformations (deletion, colorization, translation) without retraining the field [S1].

The volume rendering equation remains the foundation:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T(t_i) (1 - \exp(-\sigma(t_i)\delta_i)) c(t_i) $$
where $T(t_i) = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(t_j)\delta_j)$ is the transmittance, $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between samples, and $c(t_i), \sigma(t_i)$ are produced by $F_\Theta$ [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
*   **What it is:** A training procedure for generating a panoramic NeRF from a single input panorama.
*   **Distinction:** Solves geometry conflict in single-view synthesis by iteratively expanding the training set via inpainting and erasing conflicting regions, unlike standard multi-view training.
*   **When to choose:** When only a single panorama is available and novel views from arbitrary poses are required.
*   **Link:** [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
*   **What it is:** A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3DGS by generating synthetic pseudo-observations via a diffusion model during training.
*   **Distinction:** Acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching (reprojecting real observations) rather than ensemble learning, using a "deceptive" diffusion model conditioned on RGB, depth, and uncertainty maps.
*   **When to choose:** When reconstructing scenes from very few input views (sparse-view) and artifacts/missing geometry are prevalent.
*   **Link:** [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)

### [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)
*   **What it is:** Enables 3D semantic segmentation and editable novel-view synthesis by distilling features from a pre-trained 2D teacher network (LSeg or DINO) into a NeRF.
*   **Distinction:** Targets semantic decomposition via feature distillation, allowing multi-view consistent editing (deletion/extraction) without relying on diffusion priors or geometric consistency alone.
*   **When to choose:** When semantic-level editing (e.g., "remove the chair") is required with multi-view consistency.
*   **Link:** [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
*   **What it is:** A framework for editing specific Regions of Interest (ROI) in an existing NeRF using text prompts and a user-defined 3D bounding box.
*   **Distinction:** Initializes a generator NeRF with original weights and blends content volumetrically, preserving the scene outside the ROI without requiring semantic masks or learning a new feature space.
*   **When to choose:** When local 3D editing (e.g., "add a vase here") is needed without global retraining or semantic segmentation.
*   **Link:** [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
*   **What it is:** A framework for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained NeRF using user-provided masks and RGB-D priors from 2D inpainting/depth completion.
*   **Distinction:** Explicitly enforces geometric consistency via a depth-guiding loss alongside photometric color guidance, ensuring multi-view consistency without category-specific training.
*   **When to choose:** When removing specific objects defined by a mask, requiring both color and geometric correctness in the inpainted region.
*   **Link:** [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
*   **What it is:** Addresses inconsistency artifacts in applying 2D inpainters (e.g., LaMa) to NeRFs by adaptively switching between 2D priors and multi-view geometric consistency based on regional gradients.
*   **Distinction:** Evaluates texture richness to decide whether to trust the 2D inpainter or the NeRF's multi-view geometry, unlike methods that uniformly blend priors.
*   **When to choose:** When inpainting occluded regions where some views have rich texture (reliable geometry) and others do not.
*   **Link:** [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
*   **What it is:** Enables semantic and geometric manipulation of a pre-trained NeRF by intercepting the volume rendering pipeline with external guidance (2D teacher networks or masks).
*   **Distinction:** Performs edits as a post-hoc inference-time operation, allowing immediate visual feedback without retraining, unlike optimization-based methods like DFF or Blended-NeRF.
*   **When to choose:** When rapid, interactive editing (deletion, colorization, translation) is needed without the computational cost of retraining.
*   **Link:** [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)

### Semantic Inpainting for Neural Fields
*   **Definition:** A 3D content editing capability allowing editing of neural fields through simple interfaces like free-form sketching or language instructions [S1].
*   **Steps:** Given a user-supplied edit in a single reference view, the encoding of a neural field automatically propagates this edit to all views. This propagation promotes consistency between all views thanks to the implicit geometrical and photometric priors embodied by the neural network, preserving the structure of the whole scene [S1].
*   **History:** Described in reference [19] [S1].

### Post-Refinement Strategy
*   **Definition:** A strategy employed after the NeRF has been trained on updated data to enhance consistency by generating a more accurate target for each view using the modified Instruct-Pix2Pix with averaging architecture [S2].
*   **Steps:** Use the rendered image as input while continuing to use the mixup image as a condition [S2].

## Background
The concept of 3D content editing in neural fields emerged as NeRF technology matured, moving from static reconstruction to dynamic manipulation. Early developments highlighted the ability to propagate edits from a single view to all views via the implicit priors of the neural network [S1]. Recent advancements integrate 2D generative models (like Stable Diffusion and Instruct-Pix2Pix) as parametric priors to ensure photorealism and semantic fidelity, effectively "distilling" 2D knowledge into 3D geometry without explicit 3D supervision [S1]. This has led to a variety of specialized procedures, from diffusion-guided sparse-view reconstruction to semantic feature distillation and post-hoc inference editing.
