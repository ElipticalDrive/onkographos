# Blended-NeRF

Also called: Zero-Shot Object Generation and Blending in Existing Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene using text prompts and a user-defined 3D bounding box [S1]. It solves the problem of local scene manipulation without requiring semantic masks or feature decomposition by initializing a generator NeRF with the original scene's weights and blending the synthesized content with the original scene using a novel volumetric blending technique [S1]. Unlike methods that synthesize objects from scratch or require 2D masks, Blended-NeRF operates on any region of a real-world scene, preserving the geometry outside the ROI while allowing for object insertion, replacement, or blending [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** An existing NeRF model $F_{\theta_O}$ representing scene $x_o$, a 3D Region of Interest (ROI) box $B$, and a text prompt $y$.
*   **Output:** An edited NeRF model $F_{\theta_E}$ (implicitly defined by the blending of $F_{\theta_O}$ and the trained generator $F_{\theta_G}$) that renders the modified scene $x_e$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model ($F_\theta$):** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. Inputs are embedded using high-frequency sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\cos(2^l \mathbf{x}), \sin(2^l \mathbf{x})]_{l=0}^{L-1}$ [S1].
*   **ROI Box ($B$):** A 3D axis-aligned or oriented box defined by its center and dimensions, localized within the scene using a GUI that leverages the original NeRF's depth map for 3D understanding [S1].
*   **Language-Image Model:** A pretrained model (e.g., CLIP) with image encoder $E_{img}$ and text encoder $E_{txt}$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a generator NeRF model $F_{\theta_G}$ with the weights of the original scene model $F_{\theta_O}$ [S1].
2.  **ROI Localization:** Define the 3D box $B$ inside the scene. The method modifies the scene inside $B$ while preserving the rest ($x_e \odot (1 - B) = x_o \odot (1 - B)$) [S1].
3.  **Training Loop:** Optimize $F_{\theta_G}$ by minimizing the total loss $L_{total}$ for a set number of iterations. In each iteration:
    a.  **Pose Sampling:** Sample a random camera pose $P$ around the scene. The initial distance $d$ from the ROI is calculated as $d = \frac{e_{max}}{2 \tan(\gamma/2)}$, where $e_{max}$ is the maximum dimension of box $B$ and $\gamma$ is the camera FOV. A random radius $r$ is sampled around $d$ [S1].
    b.  **Ray Casting:** Cast rays $r$ from the sampled pose. Sample points $\mathbf{x}_i$ along these rays.
    c.  **Conditional Rendering (Inside ROI):** For points $\mathbf{x}_i$ inside $B$, query $F_{\theta_G}$ for density $\sigma_G$ and color $c_G$. For points outside $B$, set density to zero. Render the generated content $I_{ROI}$ using only points inside $B$ [S1]:
        $$ C(r) = \begin{cases} \frac{\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in B} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i}{\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in B} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i})} & \text{if } \exists \mathbf{x}_i \in r \text{ s.t. } \mathbf{x}_i \in B \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j < i} \sigma_j \delta_i)$ [S1].
    d.  **Background Augmentation:** To prevent scene diffusion, render with random backgrounds (Gaussian noise, checkerboard, or random Fourier textures) during optimization [S1].
    e.  **Directional Dependent Prompts:** Concatenate a view-dependent text prompt to the original caption $y$ to constrain object description across views [S1].
    f.  **Loss Computation:**
        *   **Similarity Loss:** Minimize cosine distance between rendered image features and text prompt: $L_{sim} = -E_{img}(I_{ROI})^T E_{txt}(y)$ [S1].
        *   **Transmittance Loss:** Encourage sparse geometry: $L_T = -\min(\tau, \text{mean}(T(P)))$, where $\tau$ is max transmittance [S1].
        *   **Depth Loss:** Encourage volumetric shape (especially for forward-facing scenes): $L_D = -\min(\rho, \sigma^2(D(P)))$, where $\sigma^2(D(P))$ is the variance of the disparity map and $\rho$ is max variance [S1].
        *   **Total Loss:** $L_{total} = L_{sim} + \lambda_T L_T + \lambda_D L_D$. $L_T$ and $L_D$ are introduced gradually via annealing [S1].
4.  **Blending (Inference):** After training, render the final edited scene $x_e$ by blending $F_{\theta_O}$ and $F_{\theta_G}$ along each view ray [S1].
    a.  **Outside ROI:** Use $F_{\theta_O}$ directly.
    b.  **Inside ROI:** Blend based on the chosen mode:
        *   **Object Insertion/Replacement:** Consider only $F_{\theta_G}$ during training. At inference, blend densities and colors using distance smoothing:
            $$ f(\mathbf{x}) = 1 - \exp\left(-\alpha \frac{d(\mathbf{x})}{diag}\right) $$
            $$ \sigma_{blend}(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \sigma_O(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - f(\mathbf{x})) \cdot \sigma_G(\mathbf{x}) $$
            $$ c_{blend}(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) \cdot c_O(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - f(\mathbf{x})) \cdot c_G(\mathbf{x}) $$
            where $d(\mathbf{x})$ is Euclidean distance from ROI center, $diag$ is box diagonal, and $\alpha$ controls blending strength [S1].
        *   **Object Blending:** Query both $F_{\theta_O}$ and $F_{\theta_G}$ inside the box. Compute alpha values: $\alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \exp(\phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i)) \cdot \delta_i)$, $\alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \exp(\phi(\sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i)) \cdot \delta_i)$, where $\phi$ is a non-negative activation [S1].
            *   **Density Options:**
                1.  Sum in activation: $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) = \phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + \sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i))$ (allows removal of original density) [S1].
                2.  Sum out activation: $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) = \phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i)) + \phi(\sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i))$ (allows only addition) [S1].
            *   **Color Blending:**
                $$ c(\mathbf{x}_i) = S\left(\frac{c_O(\mathbf{x}_i) \cdot \alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + c_G(\mathbf{x}_i) \cdot \alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i)}{\epsilon + \alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + \alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i)}\right) $$
                where $S$ is sigmoid and $\epsilon$ is a small constant [S1].
    c.  **Final Render:** Combine the blended ROI content with the original scene content outside the ROI using standard volume rendering [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def blended_nerf_edit(F_theta_O, box_B, text_prompt_y, num_iterations):
    # Initialize generator with original weights
    F_theta_G = copy_weights(F_theta_O)
    
    # Precompute box properties
    center = box_B.center
    diag = box_B.diagonal_length
    e_max = box_B.max_dimension
    gamma = camera_FOV
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Pose Sampling
        theta = random_angle()
        phi = random_angle()
        d_init = e_max / (2 * tan(gamma / 2))
        r = random_radius_around(d_init)
        pose_P = get_pose_from_angles_and_distance(theta, phi, r)
        
        # 2. Ray Casting
        rays = cast_rays(pose_P)
        samples = sample_along_rays(rays, num_samples)
        
        # 3. Conditional Rendering & Loss Calculation
        # Only render inside ROI for similarity loss
        I_ROI = render_roi_only(F_theta_G, samples, box_B)
        
        # Similarity Loss
        img_features = E_img(I_ROI)
        txt_features = E_txt(text_prompt_y)
        L_sim = -dot_product(img_features, txt_features)
        
        # Priors (Annealed)
        L_T = compute_transmittance_loss(pose_P, max_transmittance=tau)
        L_D = compute_depth_variance_loss(pose_P, max_variance=rho)
        
        # Total Loss
        L_total = L_sim + lambda_T * L_T + lambda_D * L_D
        
        # 4. Optimization Step
        gradients = compute_gradients(L_total, F_theta_G)
        update_weights(F_theta_G, gradients)
        
    return F_theta_G

def render_edited_scene(F_theta_O, F_theta_G, box_B, alpha_blend):
    rays = cast_rays(camera_poses)
    samples = sample_along_rays(rays, num_samples)
    final_colors = []
    
    for ray, samples_on_ray in rays, samples:
        # Split samples into inside and outside ROI
        samples_inside = [s for s in samples_on_ray if is_inside(s, box_B)]
        samples_outside = [s for s in samples_on_ray if not is_inside(s, box_B)]
        
        # Render Outside (Original)
        sigma_out, c_out = F_theta_O(samples_outside)
        color_out = volume_render(sigma_out, c_out)
        
        # Render Inside (Blended)
        if samples_inside:
            sigma_O, c_O = F_theta_O(samples_inside)
            sigma_G, c_G = F_theta_G(samples_inside)
            
            # Distance Smoothing Factor
            d_x = distance_to_center(samples_inside, box_B.center)
            f_x = 1 - exp(-alpha_blend * d_x / box_B.diagonal_length)
            
            # Blending Mode: Insertion/Replacement
            sigma_blend = f_x * sigma_O + (1 - f_x) * sigma_G
            c_blend = f_x * c_O + (1 - f_x) * c_G
            
            color_inside = volume_render(sigma_blend, c_blend)
        else:
            color_inside = 0
            
        # Combine (Simplified: assuming ROI is distinct or alpha compositing handles it)
        # In practice, standard volume rendering equation is applied to the combined 
        # density/color field along the ray.
        final_color = combine_outside_inside(color_out, color_inside)
        final_colors.append(final_color)
        
    return final_colors
```

**Parameters:**
*   **$\alpha$ (Distance Smoothing):** Controls blending strength. $\alpha=0$ gives all weight to the edited scene; higher values give more attention to original scene points closer to the ROI center. Typical values: 0, 0.5, 2, 4, 10 [S1].
*   **$\lambda_T, \lambda_D$:** Weights for transmittance and depth losses [S1].
*   **$\tau, \rho$:** Max transmittance and max depth variance thresholds [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scene Diffusion:** Without background augmentation, the scene may populate the background with noise. Use random backgrounds (Gaussian, checkerboard, Fourier textures) [S1].
*   **Flat Geometry:** In forward-facing scenes, the edit may appear as a flat billboard. Use depth loss ($L_D$) to encourage volumetric shape [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Without transmittance loss ($L_T$), the scene may become completely transparent or amorphous. Use annealing to gradually introduce $L_T$ and $L_D$ [S1].
*   **Density Removal:** In Object Blending mode, using sum-in-activation density blending allows the generator to remove original densities, while sum-out-activation only allows addition [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time depends on the number of iterations and the cost of rendering rays through the MLPs. The method requires training a new generator network, which is comparable to training a standard NeRF but constrained to the ROI.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two NeRF models ($F_{\theta_O}$ and $F_{\theta_G}$) during inference for blending.
*   **Strengths:** Does not require semantic masks or feature decomposition; works on any region; preserves the original scene outside the ROI; enables text-guided editing.
*   **Limitations:** Quality depends on the pretrained language-image model (CLIP); may struggle with complex geometric interactions if priors are not well-tuned; blending artifacts may occur at ROI boundaries if $\alpha$ is not chosen carefully.
*   **When to use:** When you need to insert, replace, or blend objects into an existing NeRF scene using text prompts without retraining the entire scene or having semantic masks.
*   **When not to use:** When global scene editing is required, or when high-fidelity geometric consistency outside the ROI is critical and the ROI blending parameters are difficult to tune.

## Background
Blended-NeRF was introduced by Ori Gordon, Omri Avrahami, and Dani Lischinski from The Hebrew University in 2023 [S1]. It builds upon the principles of Dream Fields [S1], which used CLIP to guide NeRF generation, and extends them to the context of editing existing scenes. The method addresses the limitations of prior editing techniques that required semantic decomposition or 2D masks [S1].
