# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Scene Editing

## Overview
This family of procedures modifies a pre-trained or training Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to alter its content, such as removing objects, changing colors, or filling occluded regions, without requiring full retraining from scratch. Unlike standard training loops that optimize for photometric consistency on input views, these methods introduce external priors (2D inpainting, diffusion models, or semantic features) and specialized loss functions or rendering pipelines to enforce 3D consistency while applying edits. They differ from sibling methods like [Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth](8869-progressive-and-dynamic-scene-growth.md) by focusing on content modification rather than scene expansion, and from [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md) by integrating specific editing objectives (e.g., semantic segmentation, text prompts) into the optimization or rendering process.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a NeRF representation $F_\Theta$ mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $c$. The core mechanism involves either modifying the optimization objective or altering the rendering pipeline to incorporate external guidance.

1.  **External Guidance Integration**: Methods utilize 2D priors (inpainting, diffusion) or semantic features from pre-trained networks (LSeg, DINO, CLIP) to guide the 3D optimization. This guidance is typically projected into 3D via ray casting or used to compute losses against rendered outputs [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6].
2.  **Consistency Enforcement**: To prevent view-dependent artifacts, methods enforce multi-view consistency. This is achieved through:
    *   **Depth/Geometry Constraints**: Using depth maps from the NeRF or external estimators to warp masks or features between views [S2, S5, S6].
    *   **Uncertainty Maps**: Computing uncertainty based on epipolar reprojection errors to weight pseudo-observations [S2].
    *   **Adaptive Weighting**: Dynamically selecting between 2D priors and 3D consistency based on local texture gradients [S6].
3.  **Optimization Strategy**: Most methods fine-tune the pre-trained NeRF parameters $\Theta$ using Adam, minimizing a composite loss function $L = L_{photometric} + L_{guidance} + L_{regularization}$. Some methods, like PERF, iteratively update the training dataset by adding inpainted views [S1].
4.  **Masking and Region Selection**: Edits are localized using user-defined masks, 3D bounding boxes, or semantic segmentation probabilities. Rays or points falling outside the edit region are either preserved from the original model or masked out of the loss calculation [S3, S4, S5, S6].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A training procedure for expanding a NeRF from a single input panorama to a complete 3D representation by progressively incorporating novel views. It solves the problem of geometry conflicts that arise when inpainting invisible regions with generative models, which may violate the consistency constraints of the already-learned visible geometry. Unlike methods that assume multi-view consistency from the start, PERF iteratively detects and erases these conflicted regions from the supervision signal, ensuring the learned geometry remains consistent with the original reference views.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly enforcing 3D view consistency through an uncertainty map derived from epipolar reprojection of nearest input views, rather than relying on ensemble learning or variational inference. This approach allows the diffusion model to condition on geometric and consistency cues to rectify artifacts inherent in sparse-view renderings.

### [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)
DFF enables 3D semantic segmentation and editable novel-view synthesis by distilling high-level semantic features from a pre-trained 2D teacher network (such as LSeg or DINO) into a neural radiance field. Unlike standard NeRFs, whose internal feature spaces are entangled with low-level factors like color and spatial adjacency, DFF decomposes scenes into distinct semantic regions, allowing for multi-view consistent editing, deletion, and extraction of objects. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by relying on knowledge distillation from external 2D models rather than explicit 3D supervision or iterative inpainting, achieving segmentation performance competitive with supervised 3D convolutional models.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene based on text prompts and a user-defined 3D ROI box. It solves the problem of local scene modification while preserving the unedited background, avoiding the need for semantic decomposition or 2D masks required by prior methods. Unlike methods that synthesize objects from scratch (e.g., Dream Fields) or require complex feature distillation, Blended-NeRF initializes a generator NeRF with the original scene's weights and blends the synthesized content with the original scene using a novel volumetric blending technique.

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
NeRF-In is a framework for removing unwanted objects from a 3D scene represented by a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) without requiring category-specific training data or retraining the entire model from scratch. It solves the problem of visual inconsistency and geometric artifacts that arise when simply masking out regions in a NeRF, by jointly optimizing the NeRF parameters against explicit color and depth guidance derived from 2D inpainting priors. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on separate per-view updates or random re-initialization, NeRF-In maintains view consistency by using a single user-drawn mask to generate guidance for multiple sampled views, optimizing the original pre-trained parameters to inpaint the masked region across all views simultaneously.

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting is a method for 3D scene de-occlusion and inpainting that combines a pretrained 2D image inpainter with multi-view NeRF optimization. It solves the problem of inconsistent texture synthesis in occluded regions by dynamically selecting whether to rely on 2D inpainting priors or supplementary information from other views based on local texture richness. Unlike methods that apply uniform inpainting strategies or rely solely on 3D consistency without explicit view selection, this approach uses regional average gradients to adaptively weight the reconstruction loss, ensuring that high-texture areas leverage multi-view correlations while low-texture areas rely on 2D priors.

### NeRF Editing Procedure
A specific editing pipeline within the DFF framework that modifies scene content at the ray-sampling level [S1].
- Sample points $[\dots, x_i, \dots]$ on a ray as usual in NeRF [S1].
- Query the DFF for each point to calculate probability $p$ of coordinate matching a set of queries [S1].
- Define 'coordinate selected by query' if $p$ is above user-defined threshold, otherwise 'not selected' [S1].
- If not selected, calculate density $\sigma(x)$ and color $c(x)$ at $x$ via vanilla NeRF [S1].
- If selected, apply transforms: Deletion sets density $\sigma(x)$ to zero [S1].
- If selected, apply transforms: Color editing queries NeRF for density $\sigma(x)$ and color $c(x)$, then transforms color to $b(c(x))$ via colorization function $b$ [S1].
- If selected, apply transforms: Translation/rescaling computes deformed point coordinate $x_0 = g^{-1}(x)$ where $g$ is inverse editing transformation [S1].
- If $x_0$ is selected by query, use $x_0$ instead of $x$ for calculating color and density [S1].
- If both $x$ and $x_0$ are selected and have non-zero density, mix colors $c(x)$ and $c(x_0)$ in ratio of their alphas [S1].
- Perform volume rendering with series of (density, color) tuples [S1].

## Background
The field of NeRF editing emerged as NeRFs became popular for novel view synthesis, with researchers seeking to manipulate the generated 3D content. Early work focused on semantic segmentation to enable object removal [S1]. Subsequent methods introduced text-guided editing [S4] and inpainting for sparse views or occluded regions [S2, S5, S6]. The progression has moved from simple mask-based removal to complex pipelines involving diffusion models, feature distillation, and adaptive consistency checks.
