# NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization

Also called: Free-Form NeRF Inpainting with RGB-D Priors, NeRF object removal, NeRF-In

## Overview
NeRF-In is a method for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by optimizing the model parameters $\Theta$ using user-provided masks and external RGB-D priors [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods requiring category-specific training data or retraining from random initialization, NeRF-In updates an existing model to inpaint masked regions across multiple views while maintaining 3D consistency [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself by jointly optimizing color and depth guidance losses, preventing the visual inconsistencies and incorrect geometry often seen in color-only or mask-only baselines [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A pre-trained NeRF model $F_\Theta$, a user-drawn binary mask $M_u$ on a user-chosen view $I_u = F_{\Theta_{image}}(o_u)$, and a set of $K$ sampled camera positions $O = \{o_s \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ along a pre-set trajectory [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Updated NeRF parameters $\tilde{\Theta}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model $F_\Theta$:** A multi-layer perceptron mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [Parent].
*   **Masks:** $M_u$ is the user mask on view $u$. $M = \{M_s \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ are transferred masks for sampled views, where $M_s=1$ indicates pixels outside the masked region (background/valid) and $M_s=0$ inside the object to remove [S1, unofficial].
*   **Guidance Images:** $I^G = \{I_s^G \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ are color guidance images generated by an inpainting method $\rho$. $D^G = \{D_s^G \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ are depth guidance images generated by a depth completion method $\tau$ [S1, unofficial]. Note: In the main framework, $O_{all} = \{o_u\}$ and $O_{out} = O$ (all other views) [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize NeRF parameters $\Theta$ (using a pre-trained model) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample $K$ camera positions $O$ along a trajectory (e.g., LLFF test-set trajectory) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Rendering:** Render RGB images $I_s$ and depth images $D_s$ from $F_\Theta$ for all $o_s \in O$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Mask Transfer:** Transfer the user-drawn mask $M_u$ to sampled views to generate $M = \{M_s\}$ using a video object segmentation method (e.g., STCN) [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Guidance Generation:**
    *   Generate color guidance $I_s^G = \rho(I_s, M_s)$ for each sampled view [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generate depth guidance $D_s^G = \tau(D_s, M_s, I_s^G)$ for each sampled view [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Optimization:** Optimize $\Theta$ to $\tilde{\Theta}$ by minimizing the total loss $L = L_{color}(\Theta) + L_{depth}(\Theta)$ for 50,000 steps using the Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The expected color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ of a camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is computed via volume rendering [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T(t_i) (1 - \exp(-\sigma(t_i)\delta_i)) \mathbf{c}(t_i)
$$
where $T(t_i) = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(t_j)\delta_j)$, $N$ is the number of quadrature points, $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$, and $\mathbf{c}(t_i), \sigma(t_i)$ are color and density at point $t_i$ [S1, unofficial].

The total color-guiding loss $L_{color}(\Theta)$ is the sum of all-view and outside-view losses [S1, unofficial]:
$$
L_{color}(\Theta) = L_{all\_color}(\Theta) + L_{out\_color}(\Theta)
$$
where [S1, unofficial]:
$$
L_{all\_color}(\Theta) = \sum_{o \in O_{all}} \| F_{\Theta_{image}}(o) - I_o^G \|_2^2
$$
$$
L_{out\_color}(\Theta) = \sum_{o \in O_{out}} \| (F_{\Theta_{image}}(o) - I_o^G) \odot M_o \|_2^2
$$
Here, $O_{all}$ is the set of all views used for color guidance, and $O_{out}$ is the set of outside views. In the main framework, $O_{all} = \{o_u\}$ and $O_{out} = O$ [S1, unofficial].

The depth-guiding loss $L_{depth}(\Theta)$ measures the L2 distance between rendered fine/coarse depth images and guiding depth [S1, unofficial]:
$$
L_{depth}(\Theta) = \sum_{o_s \in O} \left( \| D_f(o_s) - D^G(o_s) \|_2^2 + \| D_c(o_s) - D^G(o_s) \|_2^2 \right)
$$
where $D_f(o_s)$ and $D_c(o_s)$ are the fine and coarse volume predicted depth images for camera position $o_s$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: Number of sampled camera positions [S1, unofficial].
*   Batch size: 4,096 [S1, unofficial].
*   Sample points (fine): 128 [S1, unofficial].
*   Sample points (coarse): 64 [S1, unofficial].
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1, unofficial].
*   Training steps for inpainting: 50,000 [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Optimizing only with masked MSE (retraining from random initialization or per-view updating without depth) causes the unwanted object to remain or introduces visual inconsistencies [S1, unofficial].
*   Using only depth-guiding loss introduces color noise outside the masked region and loses color information in flat regions [S1, unofficial].
*   Using multiple inpainted images as color guidance introduces visual inconsistency between views; the method recommends using only the user-chosen view for color guidance [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Optimization takes approximately 5 hours per scene on an Intel i7-7800X CPU and GTX-1080 GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** No category-specific training data required; maintains 3D consistency across views; corrects geometry via depth guidance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Performance depends on the quality of the initial guidance images generated by $\rho$ and $\tau$; artifacts in guidance propagate to the NeRF [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Removing unwanted objects from pre-trained NeRFs without access to the original training data or category-specific models [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality depth completion is unavailable or when the object to remove is complex and requires semantic understanding beyond geometric priors [S1, unofficial].

## Background
NeRF-In was introduced by Hao-Kang Liu, I-Chao Shen, and Bing-Yu Chen in 2022 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the unintuitive nature of editing pre-trained NeRFs by providing a free-form inpainting framework that updates model parameters based on user masks and RGB-D priors [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and external guidance integration [Parent].
