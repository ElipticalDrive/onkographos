# NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization

Also called: Free-Form NeRF Inpainting with RGB-D Priors, NeRF object removal, NeRF-In

## Overview
NeRF-In is a framework for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by optimizing its parameters $\Theta$ using color and depth guidance derived from 2D images, without requiring category-specific training data [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by explicitly combining a color-guiding loss with a depth-guiding loss to ensure both visual plausibility and geometric consistency across novel views, addressing the common failure modes of color-only optimization (incorrect depth) and depth-only optimization (color noise) [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A pre-trained NeRF model $F_\Theta$; a user-drawn free-form mask $M_u$ on a user-chosen rendered view $I_u$; a pre-set trajectory of camera positions.
*   **Output:** Updated NeRF parameters $\tilde{\Theta}$ that remove the masked object in all rendered views.

**Data Structures**
*   **NeRF Model $F_\Theta$:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{v}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. The specific architecture (layer sizes, activation functions) is not detailed in the source, but it follows the standard NeRF formulation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Masks:** Binary masks $M$ where $M=1$ indicates pixels outside the masked region (background/valid) and $M=0$ indicates pixels inside the masked region (object to remove) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Guidance Images:**
    *   $I^G$: Guiding color images generated by an inpainting network $\rho$.
    *   $D^G$: Guiding depth images generated by a depth completion method $\tau$.

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **User Input:** The user selects a camera position $o_u$ and draws a mask $M_u$ on the rendered view $I_u = F_{\Theta_{image}}(o_u)$, where $M_u=1$ for pixels outside the masked region [S1, unofficial].
2.  **View Sampling:** Sample $K$ camera positions $O = \{o_s \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ along a pre-set trajectory (e.g., LLFF test-set trajectory) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Initial Rendering:** Render RGB images $I_s$ and depth images $D_s$ for each sampled view $o_s \in O$ using the current NeRF model $F_\Theta$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Mask Transfer:** Transfer the user-drawn mask $M_u$ to the sampled views to generate transferred masks $M = \{M_s \mid s = 1 \dots K\}$ using a video object segmentation method (e.g., STCN) [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Guidance Generation:**
    *   Generate guiding color images $I_s^G = \rho(I_s, M_s)$ for each sampled view using an image inpainting method $\rho$ (e.g., MST inpainting network) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generate guiding depth images $D_s^G = \tau(D_s, M_s, I_s^G)$ for each sampled view using a depth completion method $\tau$ (e.g., Fast Bilateral Solver) [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Optimization Setup:** Define the set of all views $O_{all}$ and outside views $O_{out}$. In the main framework, set $O_{all} = \{o_u\}$ (the user-chosen view) and $O_{out} = O$ (all other sampled views) [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $L(\Theta) = L_{color}(\Theta) + L_{depth}(\Theta)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Color-Guiding Loss:**
        $$L_{color}(\Theta) = L_{all\_color}(\Theta) + L_{out\_color}(\Theta)$$
        where:
        $$L_{all\_color}(\Theta) = \sum_{o \in O_{all}} \| F_{\Theta_{image}}(o) - I_u^G \|_2^2$$
        $$L_{out\_color}(\Theta) = \sum_{o \in O_{out}} \| (F_{\Theta_{image}}(o) - I_s^G) \odot M_s \|_2^2$$
        Here, $F_{\Theta_{image}}(o)$ is the rendered RGB image at camera position $o$, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Depth-Guiding Loss:**
        $$L_{depth}(\Theta) = \sum_{o_s \in O} \left( \| D_f(o_s) - D_s^G \|_2^2 + \| D_c(o_s) - D_s^G \|_2^2 \right)$$
        where $D_f(o_s)$ and $D_c(o_s)$ are the fine and coarse volume predicted depth images, respectively, computed by accumulating density $\sigma$ from ray batches during volume rendering [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Parameter Update:** Optimize the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ to obtain $\tilde{\Theta}$ using the Adam optimizer for 50,000 steps [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**

*   **Volume Rendering (Color):**
    The expected color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ of a camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is computed via numerical quadrature:
    $$\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T(t_i) (1 - \exp(-\sigma(t_i)\delta_i)) c(t_i)$$
    where $T(t_i) = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(t_j)\delta_j)$, $N$ is the number of quadrature points, $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$, and $c(t_i), \sigma(t_i)$ are the color and density at point $t_i$ produced by $F_\Theta$ [S1, unofficial].

*   **Volume Rendering (Depth):**
    Depth images $D_f$ and $D_c$ are computed by accumulating density $\sigma$ from ray batches. The specific depth weighting formula is not explicitly provided, but it is derived from the same ray marching process as color [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $K$: Number of sampled camera positions/views. Not given in the sources.
*   Batch size: 4,096 [S1, unofficial].
*   Sample points (fine model): 128 [S1, unofficial].
*   Sample points (coarse model): 64 [S1, unofficial].
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1, unofficial].
*   Training steps for inpainting: 50,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   Hardware: Intel i7-7800X CPU and GTX-1080 GPU [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases**
*   **Masked MSE Only:** Optimizing only with masked MSE on transferred masks without explicit guidance causes the unwanted object to remain in the result [S1, unofficial].
*   **Depth-Only Loss:** Using only $L_{depth}$ introduces color noise outside the masked region and loses color information in flat regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Multiple Color Guidance:** Using multiple inpainted images as color guidance introduces visual inconsistency between views. The method restricts color guidance to the user-chosen view to maintain consistency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Guidance Artifacts:** If the initial guidance materials (generated by $\rho$ and $\tau$) contain artifacts, the optimized NeRF may output undesired results [S1, unofficial].

import torch
import torch.nn as torch.nn
import torch.optim as torch.optim

def volume_render_color(ray_origins, ray_directions, nerf_model, num_samples):
    """
    Renders color for a batch of rays using the NeRF model.
    Returns the expected color C(r).
    """
    # Sample points along rays
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, num_samples).to(ray_origins.device)
    t_vals = t_vals.unsqueeze(0).expand(ray_origins.shape[0], num_samples)
    
    # Interpolate points
    t_points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_directions.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    
    # Query NeRF for density and color
    # sigma: [batch, num_samples, 1], color: [batch, num_samples, 3]
    sigma, color = nerf_model(t_points, ray_directions)
    
    # Compute transmittance
    deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
    # Add a small epsilon to avoid division by zero or log(0) issues
    deltas = torch.cat([deltas, torch.tensor([1e-10]).to(deltas.device)], dim=1)
    
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    T = torch.cumprod(torch.exp(-sigma * deltas), dim=1)
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=1)
    
    # Weighted sum
    weights = alpha * T
    color_rendered = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=1)
    
    return color_rendered

def volume_render_depth(ray_origins, ray_directions, nerf_model, num_samples):
    """
    Renders depth for a batch of rays.
    Returns the expected depth D(r).
    """
    # Sample points along rays (same as color)
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, num_samples).to(ray_origins.device)
    t_vals = t_vals.unsqueeze(0).expand(ray_origins.shape[0], num_samples)
    
    # Interpolate points
    t_points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_directions.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    
    # Query NeRF for density
    sigma = nerf_model(t_points, ray_directions)[0]
    
    # Compute transmittance (same as color)
    deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
    deltas = torch.cat([deltas, torch.tensor([1e-10]).to(deltas.device)], dim=1)
    
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    T = torch.cumprod(torch.exp(-sigma * deltas), dim=1)
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=1)
    
    # Weighted sum of t_vals for depth
    weights = alpha * T
    depth_rendered = torch.sum(weights * t_vals, dim=1)
    
    return depth_rendered

            # L2 distance between rendered and guiding depth
            loss_depth += torch.mean((fine_depth - target_depth) ** 2)
            loss_depth += torch.mean((coarse_depth - target_depth) ** 2)
            
        # C. Total Loss and Update
        total_loss = loss_color + loss_depth
        total_loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
    return nerf_model

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The optimization takes approximately 5 hours per scene on an Intel i7-7800X CPU and GTX-1080 GPU for 50,000 steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on the LLFF dataset and a custom dataset with 24 camera positions per scene [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Does not require category-specific training data or procedures [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generates visually plausible inpainted results with correct geometry and color consistency across views [S1, unofficial].
    *   Jointly optimizes color and depth, avoiding the pitfalls of using either loss alone [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the quality of the initial guidance materials (inpainting and depth completion); artifacts in guidance can propagate to the NeRF [S1, unofficial].
    *   Using multiple inpainted images as color guidance can introduce visual inconsistency; the method restricts this to the user-chosen view [S1, unofficial].
    *   Baseline methods (per-view color updating, masked MSE retraining) show incorrect depth or visual inconsistencies, highlighting the necessity of the joint optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained NeRF without access to category-specific training data, and when geometric consistency is as important as visual appearance.
*   **When not to use:** When the initial guidance materials are likely to be poor, or when real-time editing is required (5 hours per scene is not real-time).

## Background
NeRF-In was introduced by Hao-Kang Liu, I-Chao Shen, and Bing-Yu Chen in 2022 [S1, unofficial]. It is the first framework to enable free-form inpainting on NeRFs without category-specific data or training. The method builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and 2D image inpainting techniques [S1, unofficial].
