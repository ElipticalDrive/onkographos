# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Content Editing

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to remove, insert, or alter objects and textures while maintaining multi-view consistency. These methods solve the problem of 3D editing by either optimizing the radiance field parameters against 2D guidance (inpainting) or intercepting ray samples with semantic/feature fields (editing) [S1, S2]. They set themselves apart from standard training loops by incorporating external priors (diffusion models, 2D segmenters) or geometric constraints (ROI boxes, depth warping) to guide the reconstruction of specific regions without retraining the entire scene from scratch.

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of modifying the radiance field $F_\Theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ to achieve a target appearance or geometry defined by user input (masks, text, or 3D boxes). Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Guidance Generation:** Using 2D priors (diffusion models, inpainting networks, or segmentation models) to generate target images, depth maps, or feature maps for novel or existing views [S1, S2].
2.  **Consistency Enforcement:** Ensuring edits are consistent across views. This is achieved via:
    *   **Optimization-based:** Minimizing loss between rendered NeRF outputs and 2D guidance (RGB, depth, features) [S1, S2].
    *   **Inference-based:** Using semantic probability fields to select points for transformation [S1].
    *   **Geometric Warping:** Warping depths or images between views to detect conflicts or transfer masks [S1].
3.  **Loss Functions:** Utilizing photometric losses (MSE), depth consistency losses, and semantic similarity losses (CLIP, LSeg) [S1, S2].
4.  **Region Isolation:** Defining regions of interest (ROI) using 3D boxes, masks, or semantic probabilities to limit the scope of editing [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A training procedure for generating a panoramic NeRF from a single input panorama. It solves the problem of geometry conflict and incomplete geometry that arises when expanding a NeRF from one view to multiple novel views by progressively inpainting invisible regions and erasing conflicted regions from the training supervision. It sets itself apart from other NeRF editing methods by explicitly detecting and removing geometric inconsistencies between the learned model and the inpainted guidance data, rather than relying solely on generative priors or multi-view consistency losses.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training. Unlike methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations to quantify uncertainty, this approach acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, thereby enforcing epipolar constraints and minimizing additional computation. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md) by integrating a "deceptive" diffusion model conditioned on rendered RGB, depth, and uncertainty maps to rectify artifacts and fill geometric gaps, rather than solely relying on 2D guidance images or textual prompts for optimization.

### [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)
DFF enables 3D semantic segmentation and editable novel-view synthesis by distilling features from a pre-trained 2D teacher network (such as LSeg or DINO) into a neural radiance field. Unlike methods that rely on explicit 3D labels or naive reuse of NeRF's internal feature spaces—which are entangled with low-level factors like color and geometry—DFF learns a dedicated feature field that captures semantic similarity, allowing for multi-view consistent decomposition of scenes into semantic regions. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md) by providing a semantic decomposition mechanism that can be combined with optimization-based editors (e.g., [CLIPNeRF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00000)) to update only desired objects without breaking unintended areas.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene based on text prompts and a user-defined 3D ROI box. It solves the problem of local 3D editing by initializing a generator NeRF with the weights of the original scene and blending the synthesized content with the original scene using volumetric blending. Unlike methods that synthesize new objects from scratch (e.g., Dream Fields) or require semantic decomposition/2D masks for localization (e.g., Kobayashi, Benaim), Blended-NeRF operates on any region using a 3D box without learning a new feature space or requiring 2D masks.

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
NeRF-In is an optimization-based method for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) without requiring category-specific training data or retraining the model from scratch. It solves the problem of multi-view consistent inpainting by updating the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ to minimize a joint loss function derived from 2D color and depth guidance generated from a single user-drawn mask. Unlike inference-time interception methods that simply zero out density, NeRF-In actively reconstructs the geometry and appearance of the occluded region by leveraging RGB-D priors, ensuring that the edited scene renders consistently across novel views.

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
This method addresses the inconsistency of single-view 2D inpainting in 3D scenes by integrating a 2D appearance prior with multi-view geometric consistency. It distinguishes itself from pure optimization-based inpainting [12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md] by explicitly selecting training views based on texture richness (regional average gradients) to decide whether to rely on the 2D inpainter or supplementary information from other views.

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
This method modifies the radiance field $F_\Theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ during the rendering pass by intercepting ray samples and applying transformations based on semantic or geometric queries, rather than retraining the network parameters. It solves the problem of achieving multi-view consistent edits (deletion, colorization, translation) without the computational cost of iterative optimization. It sets itself apart from optimization-based siblings like [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md) by performing edits at inference time via an external feature field, and from direct blending methods by using probability-weighted or thresholded selection of points.

### Semantic Inpainting for Neural Fields
- **Definition:** A 3D content editing capability allowing editing of neural fields through simple interfaces like free-form sketching or language instructions [S1].
- **Step:** Given a user-supplied edit in a single reference view, the encoding of a neural field automatically propagates this edit to all views [S1]. The propagation promotes consistency between all views thanks to the implicit geometrical and photometric priors embodied by the neural network, preserving the structure of the whole scene [S1].
- **History:** Described in reference [19] [S1].

### Post-Refinement Strategy
- **Definition:** A strategy employed after the NeRF has been trained on updated data to enhance consistency by generating a more accurate target for each view using the modified Instruct-Pix2Pix with averaging architecture [S2].
- **Step:** Use the rendered image as input while continuing to use the mixup image as a condition [S2].

## Background
NeRF editing emerged as a natural extension of the original NeRF paper [G], addressing the limitation that NeRFs are static representations. Early work focused on optimization-based inpainting, where 2D inpainting tools (e.g., LaMa, MST) provide guidance for removing objects [S1, S2]. The field rapidly expanded to include semantic editing via feature distillation (DFF) [S1] and generative editing using diffusion models (DreamFusion, NeRF-Art) [S1, S2]. Recent developments emphasize multi-view consistency, often achieved through uncertainty estimation (Deceptive-NeRF) or progressive inpainting with conflict erasing [S1]. The integration of 2D foundation models (LSeg, DINO, Stable Diffusion) has become a standard paradigm for providing semantic and generative priors in 3D editing [S1, S2].
