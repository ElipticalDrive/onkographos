# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Scene Editing

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field ($f_\theta$) to remove objects, insert new content, or alter appearance based on user inputs such as masks, text prompts, or reference images. Unlike standard training which optimizes for reconstruction of observed data, these procedures optimize for a target state that may contradict the original observations, requiring specialized loss functions and guidance mechanisms to maintain multi-view consistency. They are distinguished from [Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth](8869-progressive-and-dynamic-scene-growth.md) by their focus on semantic or geometric modification of an existing representation rather than expanding the spatial coverage of the scene, and from [Sparse View and Initialization Methods](9394-sparse-view-and-initialization-methods.md) by their reliance on dense supervision or external priors to fill occluded or removed regions.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a radiance field representation $f_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. The rendering process follows the standard volume rendering equation [S1, 12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i
$$

where $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is a camera ray, $N$ is the number of sample points, $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between samples, and $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance.

Optimization typically involves minimizing a loss function $L$ with respect to $\theta$ using gradient descent (e.g., Adam). The core challenge is that user inputs (masks, text, reference images) are often 2D or semantic, while the NeRF is a 3D continuous field. Methods resolve this by:
1.  **Projection/Back-projection:** Projecting 3D points to 2D image space to compare with 2D guidance, or back-projecting 2D guidance to 3D points to supervise density/color.
2.  **Masking:** Applying binary or soft masks to restrict loss computation to specific regions (e.g., only occluded areas or only regions outside an object to be removed).
3.  **External Priors:** Integrating outputs from external models (diffusion models, depth estimators, segmentation networks) as additional supervision signals or constraints.

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A method for reconstructing a panoramic NeRF from a single input panorama by iteratively expanding the field of view. It solves geometry conflicts between visible and inpainted regions by detecting and erasing inconsistent geometry from training supervision. Unlike methods requiring multiple views, it alternates between 2D inpainting and 3D geometry erasure.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3DGS by densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model. It acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching (warping nearest views) rather than ensemble learning. It conditions a 2D diffusion model on coarse RGB, depth, and view-consistency uncertainty maps to rectify artifacts.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
A framework for editing a specific Region of Interest (ROI) in an existing NeRF using text prompts and a 3D ROI box. It initializes a generator NeRF with original weights and blends synthesized content with the original scene using volumetric blending. It operates on any region using a 3D box without requiring 2D masks or learning a new feature space, unlike Dream Fields or Volumetric Disentanglement.

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
A framework for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained NeRF using user-provided masks and RGB-D priors. It differs from siblings by explicitly combining color guidance from 2D inpainting models with depth guidance from depth completion methods. This ensures both visual plausibility and geometric consistency across multiple views without category-specific training data.

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
This method addresses the inconsistency of 2D inpainting priors by introducing a training view selection mechanism leveraging multi-view correlation. It distinguishes itself by dynamically weighting the reconstruction loss between ground-truth visible pixels and 2D inpainted priors based on the texture richness (average gradient) of occluded sub-regions.

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
A method for modifying a trained NeRF by integrating external semantic or geometric guidance directly into the volume rendering pipeline. It uses a Distilled Feature Field (DFF) to determine *where* edits occur in 3D space. It solves localized 3D editing (deletion, colorization, translation) by applying transformations to density and color values based on semantic probability, operating on continuous parameters rather than discrete pixel buffers.

### [Reference-guided Controllable Inpainting of NeRFs](13952-reference-guided-controllable-inpainting-of-nerfs.md)
This method performs 3D inpainting on a NeRF using a single inpainted reference view to guide geometry and appearance. It distinguishes itself by relying on only one user-provided inpainted image rather than multi-view consistency or complex diffusion pipelines, utilizing monocular depth estimation and bilateral solvers to propagate this single view into a coherent 3D structure.

### [RePaint-NeRF Limitations](14340-repaint-nerf-limitations.md)
This article documents the specific failure modes and resource constraints of the RePaint-NeRF method, which performs NeRF editing via semantic masks and diffusion model guidance. It addresses limitations where the target object shape drastically differs from the source, restricted view angles cause geometric artifacts at image edges, and the process is computationally prohibitive for large scenes.

## Background
The field of NeRF editing emerged as a natural extension of the original NeRF paper [Kerbl et al., 2023] and subsequent work on differentiable rendering. Early methods focused on simple object removal or addition using mask-based supervision. The introduction of diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) enabled text-guided editing, leading to methods like [NeRF-Art](13438-nerf-art.md) and [ViCA-NeRF](S1). The challenge of maintaining multi-view consistency while altering 3D structure has driven the development of specialized techniques such as progressive inpainting, reference-guided warping, and adaptive view selection. These methods are primarily used in virtual production, augmented reality, and content creation pipelines where dynamic scene modification is required.
