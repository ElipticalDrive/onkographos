# Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting

Also called: 3D Inpainting Component, 3D Inpainting Component with Adaptive View Selection, Adaptive Weight Inpainting, Gradient-Based View Selection

## Overview
This method addresses the inconsistency artifacts inherent in applying 2D image inpainters directly to NeRFs by introducing a training view selection mechanism that leverages multi-view correlation [S1]. It distinguishes itself from pure 2D-inpainting-guided methods (like [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)) by dynamically deciding, per sub-region of the occluded area, whether to rely on the 2D inpainter's appearance prior or on supplementary geometric/color information from other training views, based on local texture richness [S1].

## How it works
The method operates within a NeRF training loop where the scene is represented by a coarse MLP $F_c$ and a fine MLP $F_f$, both mapping $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ [S1]. It requires a binary mask $M$ identifying occluded regions in the input views, typically generated by an object segmentation network [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **NeRF Models**: Two MLPs, $F_c$ (coarse) and $F_f$ (fine).
*   **2D Inpainter**: A pretrained model (e.g., LaMa [25]) that takes an input image and a mask, outputting an inpainted image $I_l$.
*   **Mask**: $\hat{P}(r) \in \{0, 1\}$, the predicted probability that ray $r$ passes through an occluded region.
*   **Sub-regions**: The occluded area in each view is divided into $K$ sub-regions (patches) for gradient evaluation.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Training views $\{I_s\}$, corresponding masks $\{M_s\}$, 2D inpainted images $\{I_{l,s}\}$, and camera poses.
*   **Outputs**: Updated NeRF parameters $\Theta$, resulting in a de-occluded scene representation.

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **2D Inpainting Prior Generation**: For each training view $s$, apply the 2D inpainter to the original image $I_s$ using the occlusion mask $M_s$ to generate the inpainted image $I_{l,s}$. This provides the color prior $C_l(r)$ for rays $r$ in occluded regions [S1].

2.  **Sub-region Division**: Divide the occluded region in each view into multiple sub-regions (patches). This avoids applying a uniform strategy to the entire occluded area, allowing for detailed recovery [S1].

3.  **Gradient Evaluation**: For each sub-region in the current training step, calculate the average gradient of the rendered image $\nabla \hat{g}$ and the average gradient of the 2D inpainted image $\nabla g$ [S1]. The gradient serves as an indicator of texture richness [S1].

4.  **Adaptive Weight Calculation**: Determine the adaptive weight $\lambda$ for the reconstruction loss based on the gradient comparison. Let $\tau$ be a threshold parameter.
    $$
    \lambda = \begin{cases} 
    1 & \text{if } \nabla \hat{g} > \tau \cdot \nabla g \\
    0 & \text{if } \nabla \hat{g} \le \tau \cdot \nabla g 
    \end{cases}
    $$
    If the rendered gradient is sufficiently rich (exceeding the threshold relative to the inpainter's output), $\lambda=1$, prioritizing the NeRF's own reconstruction (multi-view consistency). If the rendered gradient is weak, $\lambda=0$, prioritizing the 2D inpainting prior [S1].

5.  **Loss Computation**: Compute the total reconstruction loss $L$ as a weighted sum of non-occluded loss $L_{NonOcc}$ and occluded loss $L_{Occ}$ [S1]:
    $$
    L = \lambda L_{NonOcc} + (1 - \lambda) L_{Occ}
    $$
    
    *   **Non-occluded Loss** ($L_{NonOcc}$): Applies to rays where $\hat{P}(r) \approx 0$. It minimizes the L2 distance between the predicted colors (from coarse and fine networks) and the ground truth image $C(r)$ [S1]:
        $$
        L_{NonOcc} = \sum_{r \in R} (1 - \hat{P}(r)) \left[ \| \hat{C}_c(r) - C(r) \|_2^2 + \| \hat{C}_f(r) - C(r) \|_2^2 \right]
        $$
        where $\hat{C}_c(r)$ and $\hat{C}_f(r)$ are the colors predicted by the coarse and fine networks, respectively [S1].

    *   **Occluded Loss** ($L_{Occ}$): Applies to rays where $\hat{P}(r) \approx 1$. It minimizes the L2 distance between the predicted colors and the 2D inpainting prior $C_l(r)$ [S1]:
        $$
        L_{Occ} = \sum_{r \in R} \hat{P}(r) \left[ \| \hat{C}_c(r) - C_l(r) \|_2^2 + \| \hat{C}_f(r) - C_l(r) \|_2^2 \right]
        $$
        where $C_l(r)$ is the color of the corresponding pixel in the 2D inpainted image $I_{l,s}$ [S1].

6.  **Optimization**: Update the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) to minimize $L$. The method also typically includes auxiliary losses for the segmentation network (region encoding, RGB, depth) to ensure accurate mask propagation across views, though the core inpainting logic relies on the adaptive loss above [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def adaptive_view_selection_inpainting_step(
    nerf_coarse, 
    nerf_fine, 
    rays, 
    gt_colors, 
    masks, 
    inpainted_images, 
    tau, 
    optimizer
):
    """
    Performs one optimization step for Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting.
    
    Args:
        nerf_coarse: Coarse NeRF MLP.
        nerf_fine: Fine NeRF MLP.
        rays: List of Ray objects, each with origin, direction, and pixel coordinates.
        gt_colors: Ground truth colors for the rays.
        masks: Binary masks indicating occluded regions (1 if occluded, 0 otherwise).
        inpainted_images: Pre-computed 2D inpainted images for the current views.
        tau: Threshold for adaptive weight calculation.
        optimizer: Optimizer for NeRF parameters.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar loss value.
    """
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    
    total_loss = 0.0
    non_occ_loss = 0.0
    occ_loss = 0.0
    
    # Group rays by view to calculate sub-region gradients
    # Note: In practice, rays are batched. Gradient calculation here assumes
    # access to rendered images per sub-region.
    
    for view_idx, view_rays in enumerate(group_rays_by_view(rays)):
        # 1. Render current NeRF prediction
        pred_colors_c = nerf_coarse.render(view_rays)
        pred_colors_f = nerf_fine.render(view_rays)
        
        # 2. Get 2D inpainting prior for these rays
        # C_l(r) is the color from the inpainted image at the corresponding pixel
        inpainted_colors = get_colors_from_image(inpainted_images[view_idx], view_rays)
        
        # 3. Calculate gradients for sub-regions
        # Divide view_rays into sub-regions (patches)
        sub_regions = divide_into_sub_regions(view_rays)
        
        lambda_weight = 0.0
        active_sub_regions = 0
        
        for sub_region in sub_regions:
            # Get rays in this sub-region
            sr_rays = sub_region.rays
            
            # Render for sub-region
            sr_pred_c = nerf_coarse.render(sr_rays)
            sr_pred_f = nerf_fine.render(sr_rays)
            sr_inpainted = get_colors_from_image(inpainted_images[view_idx], sr_rays)
            sr_gt = get_gt_colors(gt_colors, sr_rays)
            sr_mask = get_masks(masks, sr_rays)
            
            # Calculate average gradient of rendered image
            # Gradient is typically computed on the rendered image plane
            grad_rendered = compute_average_gradient(render_image_from_rays(sr_rays, sr_pred_c))
            
            # Calculate average gradient of inpainted image
            grad_inpainted = compute_average_gradient(render_image_from_rays(sr_rays, sr_inpainted))
            
            # Determine lambda for this sub-region
            if grad_rendered > tau * grad_inpainted:
                lambda_sr = 1.0
            else:
                lambda_sr = 0.0
            
            # Compute losses for this sub-region
            # Non-occluded part (1 - mask)
            non_occ_term = (1 - sr_mask) * (
                (sr_pred_c - sr_gt)**2 + 
                (sr_pred_f - sr_gt)**2
            )
            non_occ_loss += torch.sum(non_occ_term)
            
            # Occluded part (mask)
            occ_term = sr_mask * (
                (sr_pred_c - sr_inpainted)**2 + 
                (sr_pred_f - sr_inpainted)**2
            )
            occ_loss += torch.sum(occ_term)
            
            # Accumulate weighted loss
            # Note: The paper defines L = lambda * L_NonOcc + (1-lambda) * L_Occ
            # This implies lambda is global or per-sub-region. 
            # The pseudocode aggregates L_NonOcc and L_Occ separately, then applies lambda.
            # However, the paper says "adaptive weight adjustment based on texture richness".
            # If lambda is per-sub-region, we should weight the terms locally.
            # Re-reading [S1]: "L = lambda * L_NonOcc + (1-lambda) * L_Occ"
            # And lambda is determined by gradient comparison.
            # It is most consistent to apply lambda globally per view or per batch if gradients are averaged.
            # Given "sub-regions", let's assume lambda is computed per sub-region and applied to the loss contribution of that sub-region.
            
            sub_region_loss = lambda_sr * torch.sum(non_occ_term) + (1 - lambda_sr) * torch.sum(occ_term)
            total_loss += sub_region_loss
            
            active_sub_regions += 1
            
    # Normalize losses if necessary (e.g., by number of rays)
    total_loss = total_loss / len(rays)
    
    total_loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    
    return total_loss

def compute_average_gradient(image):
    """
    Computes the average magnitude of the image gradient.
    """
    # Convert to tensor if not already
    # Compute Sobel or simple difference gradients
    grad_x = image[:, 1:] - image[:, :-1]
    grad_y = image[1:, :] - image[:-1, :]
    # Average magnitude
    avg_grad = torch.mean(torch.abs(grad_x)) + torch.mean(torch.abs(grad_y))
    return avg_grad
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Uniform Strategy Misjudgment**: Applying a single global weight to the entire occluded area can lead to poor detail recovery. The method mitigates this by dividing the occluded area into sub-regions and evaluating gradients individually [S1].
*   **2D Inpainter Inconsistency**: Single 2D inpainting does not consider 3D consistency. The method addresses this by selecting views based on texture richness (gradient) to ensure multi-view consistency [S1].
*   **Gradient Noise**: Computing gradients on low-resolution or noisy rendered images may lead to incorrect $\lambda$ selection. [G] It is recommended to smooth the rendered images or use a larger patch size for gradient calculation to stabilize the weight selection.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Adds overhead for 2D inpainting (LaMa) and gradient calculation per sub-region. The core NeRF rendering complexity remains $O(N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the 2D inpainted images for all training views. No significant additional memory for NeRF parameters.
*   **Strengths**: Improves fidelity of de-occluded results by leveraging multi-view correlation when texture is rich, and falls back to 2D priors when texture is weak. Avoids the inconsistency of pure 2D inpainting [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Relies on the quality of the 2D inpainter and the accuracy of the segmentation masks. Performance depends on the availability of multi-view information for occluded areas [S1].
*   **When to use**: When inpainting occluded regions in NeRFs and multi-view consistency is critical. Particularly effective in scenes with wide baseline multi-view data [S1].
*   **When not to use**: When single-view inpainting is sufficient or when multi-view data is sparse/insufficient to provide reliable supplementary information [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed in the context of 3D scene de-occlusion, aiming to remove obstacles and realistically inpaint the occluded areas in Neural Radiance Fields [S1]. It builds upon the observation that standard 2D inpainters fail to maintain 3D consistency, and introduces a mechanism to dynamically select between 2D priors and multi-view NeRF reconstruction based on local texture information [S1].
