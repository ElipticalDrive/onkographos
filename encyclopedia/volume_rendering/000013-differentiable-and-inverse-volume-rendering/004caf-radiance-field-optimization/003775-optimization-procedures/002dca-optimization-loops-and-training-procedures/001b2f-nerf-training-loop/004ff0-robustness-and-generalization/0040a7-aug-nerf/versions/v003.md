# Aug-NeRF

Also called: Aug-NeRF Training Pipeline, Augmented NeRF, Triple-Level Physically-Grounded Augmentations

## Overview
Aug-NeRF is a training framework for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that injects worst-case adversarial perturbations at three distinct levels of the rendering pipeline—input coordinates, intermediate MLP features, and pre-rendering output—to implicitly regularize the model with physical grounds [S1]. Unlike explicit smoothness regularizers (e.g., Laplacian or Total Variation) that aggressively constrain MLP flexibility, Aug-NeRF uses data-adaptive perturbations to enhance generalization to unseen views and improve geometry smoothness without sacrificing representation capacity [S1]. It addresses the generalization gap caused by inconsistent and non-smooth geometries in standard NeRF by treating the search for worst-case perturbations as a regularization term in a min-max optimization game [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images $I = \{I_i \in \mathbb{R}^M\}_{i=1}^N$ and their corresponding camera poses $P = \{\phi_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}\}_{i=1}^N$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized neural radiance field parameters $\Theta$.

**Data Structures and Layout**
*   **Ray Representation:** A ray is defined as $r = (o, d, \theta)$, where $o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the optical center, $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the direction, and $\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]^2$ is the angular view direction [S1].
*   **Radiance Field:** The field is parameterized as $F_\Theta(p, \theta) = (g \circ f(p, \theta), h \circ f(p))$, mapping spatial coordinate $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and view direction $\theta$ to color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^+$ [S1]. The function $f$ maps coordinates to a $D$-dimensional feature vector using positional encoding, while $g$ and $h$ project features to color and density, respectively [S1].
*   **Perturbations:** The perturbation vector is $\delta = (\delta_p, \delta_f, \delta_r)$, where $\delta_p$ affects coordinates, $\delta_f$ affects features, and $\delta_r$ affects the pre-rendering output [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $K$: Total number of depth intervals sampled per ray (sum of coarse and fine samples).
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far bounds of the ray segment [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Regularization weight, set to $0.1$ [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Radius constraining the magnitude of perturbations [S1].
*   Training iterations: $500,000$ [S1].
*   PGD Configuration: Coordinate level uses 3 steps with step size $10^{-2}$; Feature level uses 1 step with step size $10^{-3}$; Pre-rendering level uses 1 step with step size $10^{-5}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Ray Casting:** For each pixel in each training image $I_i$, cast a ray $r_i = (o_i, d_i, \theta_i)$ using the inverse projection of camera pose $\phi_i$. Collect the set of rays $\mathcal{R} = \{(r_i, \hat{C}_i)\}_{i=1}^{NM}$, where $\hat{C}_i$ is the ground-truth color [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** For each ray, generate $K$ depth intervals. First, sample $K/2$ intervals uniformly between $t_n$ and $t_f$. Second, sample $K/2$ intervals proportional to the densities from the coarse sampling [S1].
3.  **Perturbation Search (Inner Loop):** For the current batch of rays, compute worst-case perturbations $\delta$ by maximizing the loss with respect to $\delta$ using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) [S1].
    *   The PGD update rule for iteration $t$ is:
        $$ \delta^{(t+1)} = \text{Proj}_{\|\delta\|_\infty \le \epsilon} \left[ \delta^{(t)} + \alpha \cdot \text{sgn}(\nabla_\delta \mathcal{L}(\Theta | \mathcal{R}, \delta)) \right] $$
        where $\alpha$ is the step size and $\text{Proj}$ clamps the perturbation to the valid range [S1].
    *   **Coordinate Perturbation ($\delta_p$):** Compute perturbed depth $t^\dag_k = t_k + \delta_t$, perturbed view direction $\theta^\dag = \theta + \delta_\theta$, and perturbed point $p^\dag_k = o + t^\dag_k d + \delta_{xyz}$ [S1].
        *   $\delta_t$ is constrained by $|\delta_t| \le \alpha_t (t_{k+1} - t_k)$.
        *   $\delta_{xyz}$ is constrained to a ball $B(0, \epsilon_p)$.
        *   $\delta_\theta$ is constrained to the conical frustum $[-\epsilon_p/2f, \epsilon_p/2f]^2$, where $f$ is focal length and $\epsilon_p$ is pixel size [S1].
    *   **Feature Perturbation ($\delta_f$):** Inject noise into the intermediate features of the MLP. The feature input becomes $f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f$. The search space is $S_f = [-\epsilon_f, \epsilon_f]^D$ [S1].
    *   **Pre-rendering Perturbation ($\delta_r$):** Perturb the output color and density. $\delta_r = (\delta_c, \delta_\sigma)$. The search space is $[-\epsilon_c, \epsilon_c]^3 \times [-\epsilon_\sigma, \epsilon_\sigma]$. The resulting color and density are clamped to $[0, 1]$ [S1].
4.  **Forward Pass (Perturbed):**
    *   Compute perturbed color and density:
        $$ c^\dag_k = g(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f, \theta^\dag) + \delta_c $$
        $$ \sigma^\dag_k = h(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f) + \delta_\sigma $$
    *   Compute perturbed depth intervals $\Delta t^\dag_k = t^\dag_{k+1} - t^\dag_k$ [S1].
    *   Compute perturbed transmittance:
        $$ T^\dag(k) = \exp\left(-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1} \sigma^\dag_l \Delta t^\dag_l\right) $$
    *   Render perturbed color:
        $$ C^\dag(r | \Theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T^\dag(k) (1 - \exp(-\sigma^\dag_k \Delta t^\dag_k)) c^\dag_k $$
    *   Compute unperturbed color $C(r | \Theta)$ using standard NeRF rendering with original coordinates and no feature/output perturbations [S1].
5.  **Loss Calculation:**
    The total loss combines the photometric loss and the adversarial regularization term:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \| C(r | \Theta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 + \lambda \| C^\dag(r | \Theta, \delta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 $$
    where the first term is the standard MSE loss and the second term penalizes the deviation caused by worst-case perturbations [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Update network parameters $\Theta$ via gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}$ [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def aug_nerf_training(images, poses, F_theta, K, lambda_reg, pgd_config):
    """
    images: List of N images, each M pixels.
    poses: List of N camera poses (3x4).
    F_theta: Neural Radiance Field function returning (color, density).
    K: Total number of samples per ray.
    lambda_reg: Regularization weight (0.1).
    pgd_config: Dict with 'steps' and 'step_sizes' for [coord, feat, out].
    """
    # 1. Cast Rays
    rays = []
    for i in range(len(images)):
        for j in range(images[i].shape[0] * images[i].shape[1]):
            # Inverse projection to get o, d, theta
            o, d, theta = cast_ray(poses[i], j, images[i].shape)
            gt_color = images[i].flatten()[j]
            rays.append((o, d, theta, gt_color))
    
    # 2. Training Loop
    for iteration in range(500000):
        # Sample a batch of rays (assuming batch size 1 for simplicity as per source)
        o, d, theta, gt_color = random_sample(rays)
        
        # 3. Generate Adversarial Perturbations via PGD
        # Initialize perturbations to zero
        delta_t = 0.0
        delta_xyz = np.zeros(3)
        delta_theta = np.zeros(2)
        delta_f = np.zeros(D) # D is hidden dimension
        delta_c = np.zeros(3)
        delta_sigma = 0.0
        
        # Coordinate PGD
        for step in range(pgd_config['steps'][0]):
            # Compute loss w.r.t coordinate perturbations
            loss_coord = compute_coord_loss(o, d, theta, gt_color, F_theta, 
                                            delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, K)
            grad = gradient(loss_coord, [delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta])
            # Update and Project
            delta_t += pgd_config['step_sizes'][0] * np.sign(grad[0])
            delta_xyz += pgd_config['step_sizes'][0] * np.sign(grad[1])
            delta_theta += pgd_config['step_sizes'][0] * np.sign(grad[2])
            
            # Project constraints
            delta_t = project_coord_t(delta_t)
            delta_xyz = project_coord_xyz(delta_xyz)
            delta_theta = project_coord_theta(delta_theta)

        # Feature PGD
        for step in range(pgd_config['steps'][1]):
            loss_feat = compute_feat_loss(o, d, theta, gt_color, F_theta, 
                                          delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, K)
            grad = gradient(loss_feat, delta_f)
            delta_f += pgd_config['step_sizes'][1] * np.sign(grad)
            delta_f = project_feat(delta_f)

        # Output PGD
        for step in range(pgd_config['steps'][2]):
            loss_out = compute_out_loss(o, d, theta, gt_color, F_theta, 
                                        delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, 
                                        delta_c, delta_sigma, K)
            grad_c = gradient(loss_out, delta_c)
            grad_s = gradient(loss_out, delta_sigma)
            delta_c += pgd_config['step_sizes'][2] * np.sign(grad_c)
            delta_sigma += pgd_config['step_sizes'][2] * np.sign(grad_s)
            
            delta_c = project_output_c(delta_c)
            delta_sigma = project_output_sigma(delta_sigma)

        # 4. Forward Pass with Perturbations
        # Sample depths
        t_coarse = uniform_sample(K // 2)
        t_fine = proportional_sample(K // 2, t_coarse)
        t_all = np.concatenate([t_coarse, t_fine])
        
        # Perturbed coordinates
        t_dag = t_all + delta_t
        theta_dag = theta + delta_theta
        p_dag = o + t_dag * d + delta_xyz
        
        # Query Network
        # Note: F_theta returns (c, sigma)
        # Unperturbed query for baseline loss
        c_clean, sigma_clean = query_nerf(o + t_all * d, theta, F_theta)
        
        # Perturbed query
        features = query_feature(p_dag, F_theta) + delta_f
        c_pert, sigma_pert = query_output(features, theta_dag, F_theta)
        c_pert = c_pert + delta_c
        sigma_pert = sigma_pert + delta_sigma
        
        # Clamp outputs
        c_pert = np.clip(c_pert, 0, 1)
        sigma_pert = np.clip(sigma_pert, 0, 1)
        
        # Volumetric Rendering
        dt_dag = np.diff(t_dag)
        dt_clean = np.diff(t_all)
        
        # Transmittance
        T_clean = np.exp(-np.cumsum(sigma_clean[:-1] * dt_clean))
        T_pert = np.exp(-np.cumsum(sigma_pert[:-1] * dt_dag))
        
        # Render Colors
        C_clean = np.sum(T_clean * (1 - np.exp(-sigma_clean[:-1] * dt_clean)) * c_clean[:-1], axis=0)
        C_pert = np.sum(T_pert * (1 - np.exp(-sigma_pert[:-1] * dt_dag)) * c_pert[:-1], axis=0)
        
        # 5. Loss Calculation
        loss_photo = np.linalg.norm(C_clean - gt_color) ** 2
        loss_adv = np.linalg.norm(C_pert - gt_color) ** 2
        total_loss = loss_photo + lambda_reg * loss_adv
        
        # 6. Update Theta
        grad_theta = gradient(total_loss, F_theta.parameters)
        F_theta.update(grad_theta)

def query_nerf(p, theta, F_theta):
    # Standard NeRF query
    return F_theta(p, theta)

def query_feature(p, F_theta):
    # Extract intermediate features from MLP
    return F_theta.get_features(p)

def query_output(features, theta, F_theta):
    # Project features to color and density
    c = F_theta.color_head(features, theta)
    sigma = F_theta.density_head(features)
    return c, sigma
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Perturbation Strength:** Using excessively strong worst-case perturbations (e.g., PGD-10) can deteriorate training performance instead of improving it [S1].
*   **Feature Injection Point:** Injecting perturbation into the middle layer of the NeRF MLP contributes most significantly to performance gains [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** The algorithm description assumes a batch size of 1 for simplicity [S1].
*   **Clamping:** Perturbed color and density values must be clamped to $[0, 1]$ to maintain physical validity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds an inner optimization loop (PGD) to each training step. The complexity increases by a factor proportional to the number of PGD steps. The optimal configuration uses 3 steps for coordinates, 1 for features, and 1 for output [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing gradients for perturbations $\delta$ in addition to network parameters $\Theta$. The perturbation search spaces are defined as $\ell_\infty$ norm balls [S1].
*   **Performance:** Aug-NeRF reduces average error by 14.3%–26.0% on LLFF and 12.5%–44.7% on 360° NeRF-Synthetic datasets compared to baseline NeRF [S1]. It achieves up to 1.5dB PSNR gain in novel view synthesis [S1]. Specific PSNR improvements on LLFF include fern (+1.34), orchids (+1.24), and trex (+1.37) [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU with 48 GB memory [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Implicitly regularizes the model with physical grounds, improving robustness to noisy supervision (6.5%–26.9% error reduction under Gaussian/Shot noise) and geometry smoothness [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of PGD steps and step sizes; too strong perturbations hurt performance [S1].

## Background
Aug-NeRF was proposed by Tianlong Chen, Peihao Wang, Zhiwen Fan, and Zhangyang Wang from The University of Texas at Austin [S1]. It was published in 2022 [S1]. The method addresses the generalization gap in NeRF where interpolated novel views often yield inconsistent and non-smooth geometries [S1]. It draws inspiration from robust data augmentations in convolutional neural networks, applying worst-case perturbations to the NeRF pipeline for the first time [S1].
