# Nerfbusters

Also called: Density Score Distillation Sampling, Nerfbusters method

## Overview
Nerfbusters is a 3D diffusion-based regularization method that improves scene geometry and removes ghostly artifacts (floaters) from Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) trained on casually captured images [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by employing a **local 3D geometric prior** learned via a diffusion model on synthetic surface patches, rather than global scene priors or 2.5D RGB-D priors [S1]. This local approach is category-agnostic, simpler to model, and explicitly 3D-consistent, making it suitable for arbitrary scenes without requiring large-scale global networks [S1].

## How it works
The method consists of two phases: training a local 3D diffusion prior and applying it to a NeRF via Density Score Distillation Sampling (DSDS).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Training images and camera poses.
    *   Synthetic 3D meshes (ShapeNet) for prior training.
    *   NeRF parameters $\theta$ (initialized from a standard NeRF training run or jointly).
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ with reduced floaters and crisper geometry.

### Data Structures

**1. Diffusion Model**
*   **Architecture:** 3D U-Net [S1].
*   **Parameters:** 7.2 million parameters (28 MB) [S1].
*   **Input/Output:** Voxelized 3D cube of occupancy $x$ with resolution $32 \times 32 \times 32$ [S1].
*   **Values:** Occupancy values are discretized to $\{-1, 1\}$, where $-1$ represents empty space and $1$ represents occupied space [S1].

**2. Importance Sampling Grid**
*   **Layout:** Low-resolution 3D grid storing density values.
*   **Resolution:** $20 \times 20 \times 20$ [S1].
*   **Values:** Densities clamped to $[0, 1]$ [S1].
*   **Update:** Updated with Exponential Moving Average (EMA) decay during NeRF training to reflect current density distribution [S1].

**3. Sampled Cube**
*   **Layout:** $32 \times 32 \times 32$ grid of NeRF densities $\sigma$ [S1].
*   **Center:** Selected based on probability distribution defined by the Importance Sampling Grid [S1].
*   **Size:** Randomly chosen between 1% and 10% of the scene bounding volume [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Diffusion Timestep $t$:** Determines the amount of noise removed; interpreted as a learning rate. Typical range: $t \in [10, 50]$ [S1].
*   **Density Threshold $\tau$:** Threshold for binarizing densities. Typical value: $\tau = 0.01$ [S1].
*   **Density Weight $w$:** Target density for occupied voxels. Typical value: $w = 0.01$ [S1].
*   **Visibility Depth Threshold:** Two times the largest distance between any two camera origins in training/evaluation splits. In Nerfstudio (where bounds are $[-1, 1]$), this value is 2 [S1].

### Numbered Steps

**Phase 1: Training the Local 3D Prior**
1.  **Data Extraction:** From ShapeNet meshes, extract $N$ local cube-bounded patches along the object surface. Cube sizes vary from 1% to 10% of the object's bounding volume [S1].
2.  **Voxelization:** Voxelize these patches at resolution $32 \times 32 \times 32$ [S1].
3.  **Augmentation:** Apply random rotations and random dilation to the cubes online during training to increase diversity [S1].
4.  **Diffusion Training:** Train a 3D U-Net ($\epsilon_\theta$) using the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) loss [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{Diff} = \| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta (\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon, t) \|_2^2 $$
    where $t \sim U(0, 1000)$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, and $\bar{\alpha}$ follows a linear schedule [S1].

**Phase 2: NeRF Optimization with DSDS**
1.  **Query Densities:** Sample a $32 \times 32 \times 32$ cube of NeRF densities $\sigma$ from the scene using importance sampling guided by the low-resolution density grid [S1].
2.  **Discretize:** Convert densities $\sigma$ to binary occupancy $x_t$:
    $$ x_t = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \sigma_i > \tau \\ -1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    [S1].
3.  **Denoise:** Pass the discretized cube $x_t$ through the pre-trained diffusion model $\epsilon_\theta$ at timestep $t$ to obtain the denoised cube $x_0$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Mask:** Create a mask $m$ indicating predicted empty voxels:
    $$ m_i = \mathbb{I}(x_{0,i} < 0) $$
    [S1].
5.  **Compute DSDS Loss:** Calculate the regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{DSDS}$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{DSDS} = \sum_i \left( m_i \sigma_i + (1 - m_i) \max(w - \sigma_i, 0) \right) $$
    [S1].
    *   If $m_i=1$ (predicted empty), penalize density $\sigma_i$.
    *   If $m_i=0$ (predicted occupied), increase density if $\sigma_i < w$.
6.  **Compute Visibility Loss:** (Optional but recommended) Penalize densities at locations not seen by training views:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{vis} = \sum_i V(q_i) \sigma_i $$
    where $V(q_i) = 1$ if the point $q_i$ is not visible from any training view (approximated via frustum check), else 0 [S1].
7.  **Update NeRF:** Backpropagate the total loss (typically $\mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_{diff}\mathcal{L}_{DSDS} + \lambda_{vis}\mathcal{L}_{vis}$) to update NeRF parameters $\theta$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Differentiable Conversion Issues:** Using a differentiable function (e.g., sigmoid) to map densities to the diffusion range and backpropagating through it causes optimization issues due to steep or zero gradients [S1]. DSDS avoids this by using a hard threshold $\tau$ and a single denoising step [S1].
*   **Accumulated Weights:** Using accumulated weights for sampling is more expensive (requires ray shooting) and limits regularization to visible regions from a specific view, potentially missing occluded floaters [S1].
*   **Visibility Approximation:** The visibility loss approximation using frustum checks overestimates visibility by ignoring occlusions, which is a conservative but safe approach [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The DSDS loss requires one forward pass through the diffusion model per sampled cube. The diffusion model is small (7.2M params), making it relatively fast compared to large 2D diffusion models [S1]. Importance sampling adds negligible overhead as it reuses ray data [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The diffusion model requires 28 MB [S1]. The importance sampling grid is small ($20^3$ floats) [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance for casual captures compared to other geometry regularizers on the proposed Nerfbusters dataset [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Removes floaters effectively; local prior is category-agnostic; 3D-consistent; computationally efficient due to small model size [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires decent starting densities as the local prior needs contextual information [S1]. Performance depends on the quality of the synthetic training data (ShapeNet) [S1].

## Background
Nerfbusters was introduced in 2023 by Weber et al. [S1]. It addresses the issue of floaters and flawed geometry in NeRFs trained on casually captured images, which are often not detected by standard evaluation protocols that only assess views near the training trajectory [S1]. The method builds on the concept of Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) but adapts it to 3D density values using a locally trained diffusion prior [S1]. It is implemented in the Nerfstudio repository [S1].
