# DreamFusion

Also called: Diffusion-guided NeRF Optimization, Score Distillation Sampling, Text-to-3D NeRF Optimization, Text-to-3D diffusion NeRF

## Overview
DreamFusion is a text-to-3D generation method that optimizes a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation to match a given textual description or image without explicit 3D supervision [S2][S5]. It solves the problem of 3D data scarcity by distilling priors from a pretrained 2D diffusion model into a 3D scene representation [S4]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by introducing Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to backpropagate gradients from a frozen 2D diffusion model through a differentiable renderer, effectively "distilling" 2D generative knowledge to 3D geometry [S2]. Unlike [Single-Stage Diffusion NeRF Training](12803-single-stage-diffusion-nerf-training.md) which may use different distillation objectives, DreamFusion specifically relies on the noise-prediction objective of the diffusion model [S4].

## How it works
DreamFusion optimizes a parametric 3D scene representation (specifically a mipNeRF 360-based NeRF) by iteratively rendering images from random viewpoints, perturbing them with noise, and computing gradients based on a pretrained 2D diffusion model's noise prediction [S1][S3, unofficial]. The core mechanism is Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), which approximates the gradient of the log-probability of the rendered image under the diffusion prior [S4].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Text prompt $y$ (e.g., "a red apple") [S1][S4].
    *   Pretrained 2D diffusion model $\phi$ (e.g., Imagen) [S1][S2].
    *   Initial NeRF parameters $\theta$ (initialized randomly or from a coarse prior) [S1].
    *   Camera poses for rendering (randomized viewpoints) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ that render images consistent with $y$ [S2].

### Data Structures
*   **NeRF Representation:** A mipNeRF 360-based NeRF [S1].
    *   **Geometry:** Parameterized by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) $\mathcal{F}_\theta$ mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density $\sigma$ and color.
    *   **Color Parameterization:** A key modification in DreamFusion is that surface color is parameterized by an MLP instead of standard radiance [S1].
    *   **Fields:** The MLP takes $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ as input and outputs $(\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x}$ is the 3D position and $\mathbf{d}$ is the viewing direction.
*   **Diffusion Model:** A pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Imagen) [S1].
    *   **Noise Predictor:** $\hat{\epsilon}_\phi(z_t; y, t)$ predicts the noise $\epsilon$ added to a latent/image $z_t$ at timestep $t$, conditioned on text $y$ [S4].
*   **Rendering:** Differentiable volume rendering [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Output:** A rendered image $x$ (or latent $z_t$ if operating in latent space, but DreamFusion originally operated in pixel space with Imagen) [S4]. Note: While later methods like Latent-NeRF operate in latent space [S1], DreamFusion's original formulation with Imagen rendered images at 64x64 resolution [S1].

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Initialize:** Initialize NeRF parameters $\theta$ randomly [S1].
2.  **Sample Viewpoint:** Randomly sample a camera pose for rendering [S1].
3.  **Render:** Use the differentiable renderer to render an image $x$ from the current NeRF $\theta$ at the sampled viewpoint [S3, unofficial].
4.  **Sample Timestep:** Sample a diffusion timestep $t$ uniformly from $[1, T]$ [S4].
5.  **Sample Noise:** Sample Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ [S4].
6.  **Perturb:** Add noise to the rendered image to create a noisy image $z_t$:
    $$ z_t = \alpha_t x + \sigma_t \epsilon $$
    where $\alpha_t$ and $\sigma_t$ are timestep-dependent coefficients [S4].
7.  **Predict Noise:** Pass the noisy image $z_t$, the text prompt $y$, and timestep $t$ into the pretrained diffusion model $\phi$ to predict the noise:
    $$ \hat{\epsilon} = \hat{\epsilon}_\phi(z_t; y, t) $$
    [S4].
8.  **Compute SDS Gradient:** Calculate the gradient of the SDS loss with respect to the NeRF parameters $\theta$:
    $$ \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{SDS}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon}\left[ w(t)\,(\hat{\epsilon} - \epsilon)\,\frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta} \right] $$
    where $w(t)$ is a timestep weighting function (e.g., $1/t$ or similar, though exact form not specified in sources, typically $w(t) = 1/t$ or constant in basic SDS) [S3, unofficial][S4]. The term $\frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta}$ is the gradient of the rendered image with respect to the NeRF parameters, computed via backpropagation through the differentiable renderer [S3, unofficial][S4].
9.  **Update:** Update NeRF parameters $\theta$ using the computed gradient (e.g., via Adam optimizer) [S3, unofficial].
10. **Repeat:** Repeat steps 2-9 for a fixed number of iterations or until convergence [S1].

### Formulas
*   **SDS Loss Gradient:**
    $$ \nabla_\theta L_{SDS}(\phi, x) \triangleq \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon} w(t) \left[ \hat{\epsilon}_\phi(z_t; y, t) - \epsilon \right] \frac{\partial z_t}{\partial \theta} $$
    [S4]. Note: Since $z_t$ depends on $x$ and $\epsilon$, and $\epsilon$ is independent of $\theta$, $\frac{\partial z_t}{\partial \theta} = \alpha_t \frac{\partial x}{\partial \theta}$ [S4]. Thus, the gradient is proportional to the gradient of the clean rendered image.
*   **Noisy Image:**
    $$ z_t = \alpha_t x + \sigma_t \epsilon $$
    [S4].

### Parameters
*   **Diffusion Model:** Pretrained 2D diffusion model (e.g., Imagen) [S1][S2].
*   **Text Prompt:** $y$, controls the semantic content of the generated 3D object [S1].
*   **Viewpoint Control:** Keywords in the prompt (e.g., "overhead view", "front view") can guide the diffusion model to generate images from specific viewpoints, aiding in consistent 3D generation [S1].
*   **Timestep Weighting:** $w(t)$, affects the contribution of different diffusion timesteps to the gradient [S3, unofficial][S4].
*   **Resolution:** Original DreamFusion used 64x64 resolution images from Imagen [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Resolution Limitation:** The use of 64x64 resolution images from Imagen resulted in NeRF models lacking the capability to produce finer details [S1].
*   **Janus Problem:** The method suffers from the Janus problem, where the generated 3D object may have multiple faces or inconsistent features when viewed from different angles [S3, unofficial].
*   **Oversaturation and Oversmoothing:** SDS tends to produce saturated colors and oversmoothed textures [S3, unofficial][S4].
*   **Insufficient Detail:** The method may fail to generate sufficient fine-grained details in the 3D geometry [S4].
*   **Slow Optimization:** The optimization process is slow, taking hours per scene [S3, unofficial].
*   **Color Saturation:** The resulting images often have saturated colors [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Instruct 3D-to-3D](14866-instruct-3d-to-3d.md)
Instruct 3D-to-3D is a method for performing 3D-to-3D conversions guided by text instructions. It solves the problem of editing an existing 3D scene according to natural language prompts, building on [DreamFusion](12087-dreamfusion.md) by conditioning the optimization on a source scene rather than generating from noise. It sets itself apart by explicitly integrating the source 3D scene as a conditional input and employing dynamic scaling to modify geometry, whereas standard text-to-3D methods lack this conditional grounding.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Optimization is slow, taking hours per scene [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Hardware:** Not given in the sources.
    *   **Resolution:** Original implementation used 64x64 resolution images [S1].
    *   **Scene:** Text-to-3D generation of objects/scenes [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Requires no 3D training data [S5].
    *   Can generate photorealistic and semantically faithful 3D objects from text prompts [S2].
    *   Allows for viewpoint control via text prompts [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Suffers from the Janus problem (multi-face artifacts) [S3, unofficial].
    *   Produces saturated colors [S3, unofficial].
    *   Lacks fine details due to low-resolution diffusion images [S1].
    *   Prone to oversaturation and oversmoothing [S4].
    *   Slow optimization speed [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When generating 3D objects from text descriptions without access to 3D training data, and high photorealism is desired despite potential artifacts [S2][S5].
*   **When not to use:** When fine details are critical, when the Janus problem is unacceptable, or when fast optimization is required [S1][S3, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** DreamFusion was introduced in September 2022 [S1][S5].
*   **Paper:** The method was proposed by Poole et al. [S3, unofficial] (referenced as [18] in [S2] and [85] in [S1]).
*   **History:** It was a groundbreaking approach for text-to-3D synthesis that addressed the limitations of 3D data scarcity by introducing Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) [S4]. It built upon the NeRF framework and leveraged pretrained 2D diffusion models like Imagen [S1].
*   **Usage:** It is used for text-to-3D generation and has inspired subsequent methods like Magic3D and Latent-NeRF [S1].
