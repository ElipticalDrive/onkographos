# Method-Specific Training Configurations


## Overview
This family defines the hyperparameter sets, optimization loops, and architectural constraints required to train specific Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) variants from multi-view images. It addresses the problem of converging implicit volumetric representations to match observed photometric data while managing computational costs and stability. What sets these configurations apart from generic volume rendering is the specific interplay between sampling strategies (e.g., coarse-to-fine, importance sampling), activation functions (e.g., shifted softplus, widened sigmoid), and loss formulations tailored to the high-frequency nature of radiance fields [S1][S3, S6].

## Shared principles
The training process generally optimizes the parameters of MLPs (and potentially auxiliary structures like hash tables or caches) by minimizing the difference between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images.

*   **Inputs:** A dataset of images with known camera poses (extrinsics) and intrinsics (focal length, principal point). Pixel values are typically normalized to $[0, 1]$ by dividing by 255.0 [S7]. Camera centers are often normalized to the range $[-1, 1]$ to ensure reliable training of positional encodings [S11].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$), and optionally a learned proposal distribution or cache.
*   **Data Structures:**
    *   **Ray Batch:** A tensor of shape $[B, 5]$ or $[B, 6]$, where $B$ is the batch size (number of rays). Each row contains ray origin $(x, y, z)$, ray direction $(d_x, d_y, d_z)$, and optionally near/far bounds or viewing direction for view-dependent effects.
    *   **Sample Points:** For each ray, $N$ query points are sampled along the ray segment $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$.
    *   **MLP State:** Parameters for the position MLP (density/feature) and view-direction MLP (color).
*   **General Optimization Loop:**
    1.  **Data Loading:** Load images and camera transforms. Reserve one image for validation/testing [S7]. Normalize pixel values [S7].
    2.  **Ray Sampling:** For each training iteration, sample $B$ random rays from the training images.
    3.  **Point Sampling:** Sample points along the ray. Strategies vary by method (uniform, stratified, importance sampling).
    4.  **Feature Encoding:** Apply positional encoding (PE) or Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) to the 3D coordinates and viewing directions.
    5.  **Network Forward Pass:** Query the network(s) with encoded positions to get volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.
    6.  **Volume Rendering:** Compute the rendered color $\hat{C}$ for each ray using the standard differentiable volume rendering integral:
        $$ \hat{C} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
        where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1][S6].
    7.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the photometric loss (MSE or Charbonnier) between $\hat{C}$ and ground truth $C$. Add regularization terms if applicable (e.g., distortion, normal consistency) [S1, S8, S9].
    8.  **Optimization Step:** Compute gradients and update model parameters using the Adam optimizer [S1][S3, S6].
    9.  **Learning Rate Update:** Adjust the learning rate according to the schedule (exponential decay with optional warmup/delay) [S6, S12, unofficial].
    10. **Iteration:** Repeat until convergence.

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md)
NeRF hyperparameters define the configuration of the optimization loop, sampling strategies, and loss functions required to train a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) from multi-view images. They address the problem of converging an implicit volumetric representation to match observed photometric data while managing the computational cost of ray sampling and the stability of gradient descent. What sets this configuration apart from generic volume rendering is the specific interplay between coarse and fine sampling networks, the use of positional encoding frequencies, and the exponential learning rate decay schedules tailored to the high-frequency nature of radiance fields.

### [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md)
This article details the specific optimization hyperparameters, activation functions, and architectural modifications used in the original Mip-NeRF and JaxNeRF implementations to ensure training stability and robustness. It solves the problem of optimization failure modes (such as zero gradients from negative density predictions) and aliasing artifacts in multiscale radiance fields. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF training by replacing the identity encoding with Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) inputs, using shifted softplus and widened sigmoid activations, and employing a specific warmup learning rate schedule.

### [DONeRF](4461-donerf.md)
DONeRF is a training configuration for neural radiance fields that replaces standard volumetric raymarching with a two-network architecture: a depth oracle network and a shading network. It solves the prohibitive computational cost of NeRF by using the oracle to predict a small set of high-importance sample locations, allowing the shading network to render high-quality images with only 2–16 samples per ray instead of hundreds. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF by decoupling geometry estimation from color/density prediction, and from other acceleration methods like NSVF by avoiding explicit voxel structures or large memory caches.

### [AR-NeRF Training Configuration](12407-ar-nerf-training-configuration.md)
AR-NeRF (Aperture Rendering Neural Radiance Fields) is a method for unsupervised learning of depth and defocus effects from natural images by integrating aperture rendering into a generative NeRF framework. It solves the problem of synthesizing photorealistic images with controllable depth-of-field effects without requiring explicit depth or camera aperture annotations. Unlike standard NeRF variants that optimize for static scene reconstruction, AR-NeRF distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling the camera aperture as a learnable parameter, allowing for the generation of images with varying defocus strengths and focus distances. It builds on [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) but introduces specific architectural modifications to support FiLM-based conditioning and distinct foreground/background synthesis networks.

#### Blender Dataset Simplified Configuration
*   **Parameters:** For the Blender dataset, use one round of sampling, 128 samples for the proposal MLP, and 32 samples for the NeRF MLP. The proposal MLP has 4 layers and 256 hidden units. The NeRF MLP has 8 layers and 256 (or 512) hidden units. Use axis-aligned IPE, MSE loss, and no distortion regularizer [S1].

#### LLFF Training Configuration
*   **Parameters:** The distortion regularizer is not disabled. Charbonnier loss is used instead of MSE [S1].
*   **Performance Metrics:**
    *   On LLFF dataset with 256 hidden units: mip-NeRF achieves PSNR 26.93, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.177, Time 2.48 hrs, Params 0.61M. Our Model achieves PSNR 26.68, SSIM 0.847, LPIPS 0.150, Time 2.39 hrs, Params 0.84M [S1].
    *   On LLFF dataset with 512 hidden units: mip-NeRF achieves PSNR 27.01, SSIM 0.845, LPIPS 0.148, Time 6.15 hrs, Params 2.27M. Our Model achieves PSNR 26.86, SSIM 0.858, LPIPS 0.128, Time 3.84 hrs, Params 3.23M [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Our model does not outperform mip-NeRF in PSNR, but yields improved SSIM and LPIPS metrics and a significant speedup when the NeRF MLP is large [S1].

#### NeUDF Training Configuration
*   **Parameters:** Optimizer: ADAM. Learning rate warms up to $2 \times 10^{-4}$ in the first 5k iterations, decreasing to $1 \times 10^{-5}$ by the end. Batch size: 512 random rays sampled from 8 randomly selected camera poses per iteration [S2].
*   **Performance:** Training duration is 400k iterations total. It takes 9 hours with mask and 11 hours without mask on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU [S2].

#### NeRF-SDP Training Configuration
*   **Parameters:** Optimizer: Adam. Learning rate: 1e-3 for Res-UNet, 5e-4 for other parts. Batch Size: 500. Hardware: Nvidia GeForce RTX 3090. Total Steps: 250,000. First 170,000 steps optimize auxiliary and rendering losses. Last 80,000 steps optimize only rendering loss. Feature Dimensions: 2D features 24, 3D features 8. Datasets: LLFF, IBRNet, Google Scanned [S3].

#### Neuralangelo Training Configuration
*   **Steps:** Sample pixels from images randomly without tracking their statistics and errors. Use long training iterations to reduce stochastics and ensure sufficient sampling of details [S4].
*   **Parameters:** ROI: Assumed to be inside a unit sphere. Total training iterations: 500k. Inactive hash resolution handling: Feature vectors are set to zero when a given hash resolution is not active. Learning rate: 1×10⁻³ with a linear warmup of 5k iterations. Decays by a factor of 10 at 300k and 400k iterations. Optimizer: AdamW with weight decay of 10⁻². $w_{iek}$ is set to 0.1. MLP architecture: SDF MLP has one layer; color MLP has four layers. Batch size: 1 for DTU benchmark, 16 for Tanks and Temples dataset [S4].

#### GO-NeRF Training Configuration
*   **Steps:** Optimize the generation model on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU for a total of 20,000 iterations. Train 30% of the iterations with background augmentation [S5].
*   **Parameters:** The scheduler values range from 30 to 300. $\lambda_R$ is set to 500 [S5].

#### Tri-MipRF Training Configuration
*   **Parameters:** Base mipmap shape $M_{l0}$: H=512, W=512, C=16. Optimizer: AdamW with weight decay 1e-5 and learning rate 2e-3. Learning rate schedule: MultiStepLR. Tri-Mip encoding learning rate is scaled up 10x relative to the MLP. Loss function: Area loss $L_{area}$, scaling pixel loss by the area of its footprint on the image plane. Implementation framework: PyTorch with tiny-cuda-nn extension and nvdiffrast library for mipmap query [S6].

#### NeRF† Implementation Configuration
*   **Definition:** A variant of nerfacto that uses a standard MLP density field instead of HashMLPDensityField, with specific hyperparameter settings per dataset [S7].
*   **Parameters:**
    *   Blender dataset: $t_n = 2.0$, $t_f = 6.0$. Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, distortion loss, and scene contraction are disabled. Background color is manually set to white. Batch size = 2048 rays. Initial sampler = uniform. Training epochs = 16.5 × 10^3 [S7].
    *   LLFF dataset: Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, and distortion loss are disabled. Scene contraction uses $\ell_\infty$ norm. Batch size = 1024 rays during training, 2048 during evaluation. Training epochs = 30 × 10^3. Leaves uses uniform initial proposal sampler [S7].
*   **Performance:** On Mip-NeRF 360, nerfacto† achieves PSNR 24.20, SSIM 0.726, LPIPS 0.247 [S7].
*   **Comparison:** Compared to nerfacto (HashMLPDensityField), nerfacto† achieves comparable PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360 but is significantly slower [S7].

#### DVGO Reference Architecture
*   **Parameters:** Compression method: Re:NeRF. Hardware: NVIDIA A40 GPU. Software: PyTorch 1.12 [S8].

## Background
The configuration of training loops for Neural Radiance Fields originated with the seminal NeRF paper [S1], which established the coarse-to-fine sampling and exponential learning rate decay as standard practices. Subsequent works like Mip-NeRF [S1] introduced activation function modifications (shifted softplus, widened sigmoid) to handle multiscale rendering and optimization stability. DONeRF [S3] proposed a decoupled architecture for efficiency, while methods like NeUDF [S2] and NeRF-SDP [S3] adapted these configurations for specific tasks like unsigned distance fields and generalizable reconstruction. More recent approaches like Neuralangelo [S4] and Tri-MipRF [S6] have refined these settings for hash-based encodings and anti-aliasing, respectively.
