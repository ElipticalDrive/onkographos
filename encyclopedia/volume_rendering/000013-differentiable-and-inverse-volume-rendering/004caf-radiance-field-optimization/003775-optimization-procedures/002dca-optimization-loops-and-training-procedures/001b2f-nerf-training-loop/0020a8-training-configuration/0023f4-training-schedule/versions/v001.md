# Training Schedule

Also called: Optimization schedule

## Overview
A training schedule defines the temporal progression of hyperparameters, loss functions, and structural updates during the optimization of differentiable volume rendering models. It solves the problem of unstable convergence and inefficient resource usage by separating the optimization into distinct phases (e.g., warm-up, densification, refinement) [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike static configurations, it dynamically alters the active loss terms and geometric primitives to handle sparse initialization and high-frequency detail recovery [S2, unofficial]. It differs from [Learning Rate Scheduling](2701-learning-rate-scheduling.md) by controlling structural topology changes and loss activation, rather than just the step size of the optimizer.

## How it works
The schedule is defined by a sequence of phases, each with specific active losses, structural operations, and duration. The total training duration $E_{total}$ is scaled by volume size: 2800 epochs for small-medium volumes, 1600 for large, and 800 for extremely large volumes [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** Scene data (images, masks), initial primitive set (e.g., surfels or photon fields), total epoch count $E_{total}$.
*   **Output:** Optimized primitive parameters (position, scale, orientation, color, opacity) and final scene representation.

### Data Structures
*   **Surfel Set:** A collection of $N$ primitives. Each primitive $i$ contains position $\mathbf{p}_i$, tangent scales $\mathbf{s}_i$, orientation $\mathbf{q}_i$, color $\mathbf{c}_i$, and opacity $\alpha_i$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights:** A vector of active weights for photometric, silhouette, and geometric regularizers ($L_{cons}, L_{nc}$) [S1, S2, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Warm-up Phase (Epochs $0$ to $0.25 E_{total}$ approx, or first 16 epochs):**
    *   Activate only photometric loss ($L_{photo}$) and silhouette loss ($L_{sil}$) [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   Disable structural updates (splitting/pruning) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Goal: Allow the initial sparse surfel set to deform and align with observed images [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Densification Phase (Epochs $0.25 E_{total}$ to $0.5 E_{total}$ approx, or until 14–25% of training):**
    *   Enable adaptive density control: split and prune surfels at regular intervals (e.g., every 4 epochs) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Disable silhouette loss [S2, unofficial].
    *   Activate geometric regularizers: consistency loss ($L_{cons}$) and normal consistency loss ($L_{nc}$) [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   **Optional Mask-based Pruning:** If ground-truth masks are available, periodically remove surfels that consistently project outside the foreground mask across a majority of training views [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Structure-aware Importance Pruning:** Apply pruning based on importance scores at 42–50% of the training process [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Refinement Phase (Epochs $0.5 E_{total}$ to $E_{total}$):**
    *   Freeze densification (no more splitting/pruning) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Fine-tune with all remaining losses until convergence [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Special Case (BigBrain):** Use multi-stage pruning due to heterogeneous regions. Prune three times: twice during densification (epochs 200, 300, ratio 0.8) and once after densification (epoch 800, ratio 0.85) [S1, unofficial]. Other datasets use a single pruning iteration [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def train_schedule(scene_data, initial_primitives, E_total):
    primitives = initial_primitives
    current_epoch = 0
    
    # Phase 1: Warm-up
    # Duration: First 16 epochs or until densification starts
    while current_epoch < 16:
        # Only photometric and silhouette losses active
        loss = compute_photometric_loss(primitives, scene_data) + \
               compute_silhouette_loss(primitives, scene_data)
        optimize(primitives, loss)
        current_epoch += 1

    # Phase 2: Densification
    # Duration: Until 14-25% of training or specific epoch thresholds
    while current_epoch < 0.25 * E_total: # Approximate boundary
        # Enable adaptive density control
        primitives = adaptive_density_control(primitives, scene_data)
        
        # Disable silhouette, enable geometric regularizers
        loss = compute_photometric_loss(primitives, scene_data) + \
               compute_consistency_loss(primitives) + \
               compute_normal_consistency_loss(primitives)
        
        # Optional mask-based pruning
        if scene_data.has_masks:
            primitives = mask_based_pruning(primitives, scene_data.masks)
            
        # Structure-aware pruning at specific intervals (e.g., 42-50% mark)
        if is_pruning_interval(current_epoch, E_total):
            primitives = structure_aware_pruning(primitives)
            
        optimize(primitives, loss)
        current_epoch += 1

    # Phase 3: Refinement
    # Duration: Remaining epochs
    while current_epoch < E_total:
        # Freeze densification
        loss = compute_all_remaining_losses(primitives, scene_data)
        optimize(primitives, loss)
        current_epoch += 1
        
    return primitives
```

### Parameters
*   **Densification Interval:** Every 4 epochs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pruning Ratios:** 0.8 for intermediate stages, 0.85 for final stage in BigBrain [S1, unofficial].
*   **Initial Surfel Count:** ~50k surfels per scene [S2, unofficial].
*   **Final Surfel Count:** ~100k surfels on average [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** Early warm-up is critical; skipping it leads to poor alignment under sparse coverage [S2, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Mask-based pruning requires accurate ground-truth masks; incorrect masks may remove valid geometry [S2, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Heterogeneous volumes (e.g., BigBrain) require multi-stage pruning to handle both smooth and detailed regions [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Staggered KNN Training Schedule
A training schedule for photon field networks that uses a sequence of increasing KNN query radii $r_i$ to improve convergence time [S3]. It addresses the high cost of large-K range queries by starting with small radii for quick adaptation and increasing them for detail refinement [S3].
*   **Mechanism:** During training, the current step's sampling radius is determined from a predefined sequence. If the radius is small, $k < K$ samples are returned; as the radius increases, $k$ approaches $K$ [S3].
*   **Loss:** Relative Mean Square Error (rMSE) applied to each set of outputs individually [S3].
*   **Parameters:** Adam optimizer ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.99$, no L2 regularization); Learning rate $9 \times 10^{-4}$ with exponential decay of 0.92 starting at 70% of total steps, triggering every 25 steps [S3].
*   **Duration:** 2000–3000 steps [S3].
*   **Example Schedule (1M photons):** 0–36% steps radius 25, 37–63% radius 50, 64–90% radius 50, 91–100% radius 0 (target) [S3].
*   **Performance:** Provides 2–3× improvement in training speed compared to naïve training using the target radius directly [S3].
*   **Note:** The exact design is empirical and depends on the dataset, lighting, and transfer function [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The staggered KNN schedule reduces training time by 2–3× compared to using the target radius from the start [S3]. The densification phase adds overhead due to splitting/pruning but improves final quality [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of primitives. Initial ~50k, converging to ~100k for surface splatting [S2, unofficial]. Photon fields scale with the number of photons $K$ [S3].
*   **Strengths:** Handles sparse initialization effectively; adapts to heterogeneous data; reduces training time for KNN-based methods.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of phase boundaries and pruning ratios; mask-based pruning requires ground-truth masks [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When optimizing differentiable volume rendering with adaptive primitives (surfels, photon fields) and sparse initial coverage.
*   **When Not to Use:** For fixed-grid or fixed-topology methods that do not support densification or structural updates.

## Background
The concept of phased training schedules in volume rendering emerged with the advent of differentiable rendering and adaptive primitive representations. The warm-up/densification/refinement structure is prominent in 3D Surface Splatting (3DSS) [S2, unofficial] and Ellipsoid-based Sparse Volume Rendering (ESVR) [S1, unofficial]. The staggered KNN schedule was introduced for Photon Field Networks to mitigate the computational cost of large-K nearest neighbor queries during training [S3]. These methods build on earlier work in [Radiance-field optimization](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) and [NeRF Training Loop](2332-training-configuration.md).
