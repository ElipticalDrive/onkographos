# Mip-NeRF 360 Optimization Setup

Also called: Mip-NeRF 360 Training Procedure

## Overview
This article defines the specific hyperparameters, network architectures, and loss functions used to train the Mip-NeRF 360 model. It solves the problem of stabilizing the optimization of unbounded, anti-aliased radiance fields by combining a multi-stage proposal sampling strategy with a robust reconstruction loss. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF training by using a two-stage proposal network to guide coarse sampling and a Charbonnier loss for reconstruction, while building on the anti-aliasing foundations of [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md).

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Camera poses, intrinsics, and images for a scene.
*   **Outputs:** Trained proposal MLP parameters $\Theta_{\text{prop}}$ and NeRF MLP parameters $\Theta_{\text{NeRF}}$.

### Data Structures
The method relies on two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs):
1.  **Proposal MLP:** 4 layers, 256 hidden units.
2.  **NeRF MLP:** 8 layers, 1024 hidden units.

Both networks use ReLU for internal activations and softplus for the output density $\tau$. The proposal network produces histograms $(\hat{s}, \hat{w})$ representing interval midpoints and weights. The NeRF network produces histograms $(s, w)$.

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the proposal MLP and NeRF MLP with the specified architectures and activation functions [S1].
2.  **Proposal Sampling (Stage 0):** Evaluate the proposal MLP using 64 samples to produce the first histogram $(\hat{s}_0, \hat{w}_0)$ [S1].
3.  **Proposal Resampling:** Resample the proposal based on $(\hat{s}_0, \hat{w}_0)$ to generate new intervals [S1].
4.  **Proposal Sampling (Stage 1):** Evaluate the proposal MLP on the resampled intervals using 64 samples to produce the second histogram $(\hat{s}_1, \hat{w}_1)$ [S1].
5.  **NeRF Sampling:** Resample based on $(\hat{s}_1, \hat{w}_1)$ and evaluate the NeRF MLP using 32 samples to produce the final histogram $(s, w)$ [S1].
6.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}$ as defined in the formula section [S1].
7.  **Backpropagation:** Update parameters using Adam optimizer. Apply stop-gradient to the proposal loss terms to decouple proposal optimization from NeRF optimization [S1].

### Formulas
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is averaged over all rays in a batch:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{t}), \mathbf{C}^*) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{w}) + \sum_{k=0}^1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{w}, \hat{\mathbf{s}}^k, \hat{\mathbf{w}}^k)
$$

Where:
*   $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{t})$ is the rendered color and $\mathbf{C}^*$ is the ground truth color.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is the Charbonnier loss: $\sqrt{(x - x^*)^2 + \epsilon^2}$ with $\epsilon = 0.001$ [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$ is a regularizer minimizing the weighted size of individual intervals [S1].
*   $\lambda = 0.01$ balances the reconstruction and regularization terms [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}$ is the interlevel loss supervising the proposal network. A stop-gradient is applied to this term to make $\Theta_{\text{prop}}$ optimization independent of $\Theta_{\text{NeRF}}$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Beta 1 ($\beta_1$):** 0.9 [S1].
*   **Beta 2 ($\beta_2$):** 0.999 [S1].
*   **Epsilon ($\epsilon_{\text{opt}}$):** $10^{-6}$ [S1].
*   **Learning Rate Schedule:** Annealed log-linearly from $2 \times 10^{-3}$ to $2 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   **Warm-up:** 512 iterations [S1].
*   **Gradient Clipping:** Norm of $10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 250,000 [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** $2^{14}$ rays [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Z-aliasing:** The standard interlevel loss can cause the proposal MLP to learn non-smooth mappings, leading to rays "skipping" scene content. This is particularly visible when using high-capacity backends like iNGP [S2].
*   **Overlap Penalty:** The original Mip-NeRF 360 interlevel loss penalizes proposal bins only when they completely fail to overlap with NeRF bins, ignoring partial overlaps. This contributes to the non-smooth density maps [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Anti-Aliased Interlevel Loss](3457-anti-aliased-interlevel-loss.md)
The Anti-Aliased Interlevel Loss is a proposal supervision loss function that measures the distance between a NeRF histogram and a proposal histogram by blurring the NeRF histogram into a piecewise-linear spline and resampling it into the proposal's interval endpoints. Unlike the piecewise constant loss used in Mip-NeRF 360, which suffers from aliasing when NeRF histogram bins are narrow or translate relative to the proposal, this method produces a smooth loss surface that prevents aliasing artifacts during training.

### [Isotropic Gaussian Scale Contraction](5407-isotropic-gaussian-scale-contraction.md)
Isotropic Gaussian Scale Contraction is a technique for efficiently adapting the scale of isotropic Gaussians when a scene contraction function $C(\mathbf{x})$ is applied. It solves the problem of maintaining correct anti-aliasing properties in contracted coordinate spaces without the computational overhead of general multivariate Gaussian transformations. It sets itself apart from the standard Mip-NeRF 360 approach by linearizing the contraction around each Gaussian center to derive an isotropic scale from the Jacobian determinant, rather than contracting a full covariance matrix.

### Smooth Proposal Supervision
Smooth Proposal Supervision is a method to resolve 'z-aliasing' artifacts in proposal networks by replacing the discrete interlevel loss with a continuous, smooth loss function that penalizes partial overlaps between proposal and NeRF histograms [S2]. It identifies z-aliasing artifacts where the proposal MLP learns a non-smooth mapping, causing rays to 'skip' scene content, especially when using iNGP backends for rapid optimization [S2]. The original Mip-NeRF 360 interlevel loss assigns equivalent loss to NeRF and proposal histogram bins regardless of overlap extent, penalizing only complete failures to overlap, leading to non-smooth density maps [S2]. The new loss prevents the proposal network from learning non-smooth mappings that occur with the previous loss, particularly when the proposal network has high capacity (e.g., iNGP backend) [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method uses a multi-stage sampling process (2 stages for proposal, 1 for NeRF), increasing the number of forward passes per ray compared to single-stage NeRF. However, the proposal network guides sampling to more efficient regions, potentially reducing the total number of samples needed for convergence.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing parameters for two MLPs (proposal and NeRF). The proposal MLP is smaller (4x256) than the NeRF MLP (8x1024).
*   **Performance:** The setup is designed for stability and robustness in unbounded scenes. The Charbonnier loss provides slightly more stable optimization than MSE [S1]. The log-linear learning rate schedule with warm-up helps in early training stability [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to unbounded scenes, anti-aliased rendering, stable optimization with Charbonnier loss.
*   **Limitations:** The standard interlevel loss can cause z-aliasing artifacts, especially with high-capacity backends like iNGP [S2]. The multi-stage sampling adds computational overhead per iteration.
*   **When to use:** Training Mip-NeRF 360 models for unbounded scenes where stability and anti-aliasing are priorities.
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and single-stage sampling is required, or when using backends prone to z-aliasing without the Smooth Proposal Supervision variant.

## Background
Mip-NeRF 360 was introduced in 2022 by Barron et al. [S1]. It extends the original Mip-NeRF approach to handle "in-the-wild" images with unbounded scenes and complex lighting. The optimization setup described here was designed to improve training stability and robustness without significantly changing accuracy or speed, building on the activation and scheduling details from [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md). The method was evaluated on 9 scenes (5 outdoors, 4 indoors) with complex central objects and detailed backgrounds [S1]. Camera poses were estimated using COLMAP [S1]. The issue of z-aliasing in proposal networks was later identified and addressed in subsequent work like Zip-NeRF [S2].
