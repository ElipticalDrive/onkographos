# Training Setup and Baselines


## Overview
This family defines the specific hyperparameter configurations, optimization loops, and baseline protocols required to train differentiable radiance field representations. It addresses the problem of stabilizing convergence and achieving high-fidelity reconstruction across diverse architectural choices, ranging from standard MLP-based NeRFs to sparse VDB-based networks and GAN-based 3D generators. It differs from sibling articles by focusing on the *configuration* of the training process (learning rates, batch sizes, regularization strengths) rather than the architectural components (encodings, samplers) or the loss functions themselves.

## Shared principles
The training setup for radiance fields generally involves minimizing a photometric loss between rendered pixels and ground truth images using gradient-based optimization. Common principles include:
1.  **Ray Sampling:** Training data is typically generated by sampling rays through the camera frustum. The density and distribution of these samples (e.g., uniform vs. opacity-weighted) significantly impact convergence [13271-nerf-training-setup.md].
2.  **Optimization:** Adam optimizer is the standard choice, often with specific learning rate schedules (decay or step-wise reduction) to handle the non-convex nature of the loss landscape [12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md][13271-nerf-training-setup.md].
3.  **Precision:** Mixed-precision training (FP16/FP32) is frequently employed to accelerate computation and reduce memory footprint, particularly in GAN-based or large-scale volume rendering [12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md].
4.  **Regularization:** Techniques such as R1 gradient penalties (for GANs) or specific priors are used to prevent overfitting and ensure smooth field properties [12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md][6281-regularization-and-priors.md].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup](11349-nerf-gt-baseline-training-setup.md)
A baseline protocol for training NeRFs on High Dynamic Range (HDR) data. It differs from standard LDR training by optimizing the radiance field directly against HDR ground truth, mapping predictions to LDR space via tone-mapping for loss computation. It is chosen when HDR novel view synthesis is required. A key pitfall is that direct HDR training may fail to encode all scene areas uniformly due to intensity variance [11349-nerf-gt-baseline-training-setup.md].

### [StyleGAN2 Training Configuration](12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md)
A configuration adapted from StyleGAN2 for 3D-aware radiance fields. It distinguishes itself by using equalized learning rates, minibatch standard deviation in the discriminator, EMA of generator weights, and sparse R1 regularization. It omits path regularization and style-mixing to reduce computational overhead. It is chosen for GAN-based volume rendering where high visual fidelity and training stability are prioritized [12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md].

### [NeRF Training Setup](13271-nerf-training-setup.md)
The training setup for Control-NeRF, which replaces standard positional encoding of 3D coordinates with sampled feature vectors from a volume representation. It solves the problem of enabling direct scene content manipulation. It differs from standard NeRF by omitting positional encoding for the input feature vector and using a fixed 64-dimensional feature vector instead of a 60-parameter positionally encoded position [13271-nerf-training-setup.md].

### NeuralVDB Hyperparameter Configuration
*   **Step:** Batch sampling strategy is either drawing $2^{16}$ random samples per epoch, or resampling a subset of input voxels at fixed intervals (100 or 500) and drawing $2^{12}$ batches per epoch. The resampled subset size equals the resampling intervals multiplied by the batch size. Resampling is used when fine details with thin structures are critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Subdomain size must be a multiple of 512. Too large wastes compute on empty subdomains; too small increases dispatch overhead and causes dominant overlapping halo regions leading to redundant computation [S1, unofficial]. Network width: Level-1 networks use half the width of level-0 networks. Default starting point: level-0 width 128, depth 3. Use 4 layers for volumes requiring more detail capture [S1, unofficial]. Optimizer: Adam. Default learning rate: 0.001. Decay: 0.975 every 100 epochs (no resampling) or 0.75 every 1,000 epochs (with resampling). Max epochs: 2,500 for non-resampled; more for resampled cases [S1, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Dense grid training produces the highest noise. Block grid (dense leaf nodes without active masks) reduces noise but still shows visible fog artifacts. Sparse VDB with active voxel masks yields the best visual quality and low noise reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

### Neural Codebook Training Configuration
*   **Step:** Multiply learning rate by 0.3 for every 5,000 iterations. Cast the trained Neural Codebook (NCB) to float16 after training [S2].
*   **Parameter:** Optimizer: Adam with $\beta_1 = 0.9$ and $\beta_2 = 0.999$. Weight decay: $1e-5$. Initial learning rate: $5e-3$. Total training iterations: 20,000. Batch size: 100,000 voxels [S2].

## Background
The concept of training radiance fields originated with the original NeRF paper [Mildenhall et al., 2020], which established the basic volume rendering integral and optimization loop. Subsequent works introduced various training configurations to address specific challenges: HDR rendering [11349-nerf-gt-baseline-training-setup.md], GAN-based synthesis [12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md], and sparse volume representations like NeuralVDB [S1, unofficial] and Neural Codebooks [S2]. These configurations have become standard practices in differentiable rendering research.
