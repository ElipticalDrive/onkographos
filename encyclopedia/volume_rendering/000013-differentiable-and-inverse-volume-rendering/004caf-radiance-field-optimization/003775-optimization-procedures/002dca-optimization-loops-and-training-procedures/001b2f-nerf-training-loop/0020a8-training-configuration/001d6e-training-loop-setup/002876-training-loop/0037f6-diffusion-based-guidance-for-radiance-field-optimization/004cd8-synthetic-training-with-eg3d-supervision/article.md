# Synthetic Training with EG3D Supervision

Also called: Alternating supervision training, Distillation Training, On-the-fly Augmentation, Training Procedure, Two-stage training

## Overview
This method trains an image-to-triplane encoder to reconstruct a 3D radiance field representation from a single input image by leveraging synthetic data generated by a frozen, pretrained EG3D generator [S1]. It solves the problem of generalizing single-image portrait synthesis by ensuring the encoder produces triplanes that are consistent not only with the input reference view but also with novel multiview renderings, thereby enforcing geometric and photometric coherence [S1]. It differs from standard supervised training by using a frozen generative model as a ground-truth oracle and alternating between reference-view and multiview supervision to prevent overfitting to the specific distribution of the input image [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A single high-resolution reference image $I_{512}$ rendered from a reference camera $P_{ref}$ (or real input image in inference, but synthetic in training).
*   **Output:** A predicted triplane $\hat{T}$ and associated rendered images ($I_f, \hat{I}_{128}, \hat{I}_{512}, \hat{I}_D$) from the encoder's triplane.
*   **Frozen Components:** EG3D generator (produces ground-truth triplanes $T$ and images), EG3D MLP decoder, EG3D super-resolution network, EG3D dual discriminator.
*   **Trainable Components:** Image-to-triplane encoder $E$, MLP decoder, super-resolution network, dual discriminator (fine-tuned).

**Data Structures**
*   **Trplane $T$:** A 3-channel feature map per plane (front, side, top) intersecting at the head center. Dimensions are not explicitly stated in the notes, but follow EG3D conventions [S1].
*   **Camera Parameters $P$:** Focal length, principal point, orientation, and position [S1].
*   **Rendered Images:** Four resolutions/types per camera: feature image $I_f$, 128x128 image $I_{128}$, 512x512 image $I_{512}$, and depth map $I_D$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Synthesize Ground Truth:** Sample a latent vector $z$ and pass it through the frozen EG3D generator to obtain a ground-truth triplane $T$ [S1].
2.  **Encode Reference View:** Render the reference image $I_{512}$ from camera $P_{ref}$ using $T$. Pass $I_{512}$ through the encoder $E$ to predict a triplane $\hat{T} = E(I_{512})$ [S1].
3.  **Render Predicted Views:** Use $\hat{T}$ to condition volume rendering from both the reference camera $P_{ref}$ and a multiview camera $P_{mv}$ to generate predicted images $\hat{I}_f, \hat{I}_{128}, \hat{I}_{512}, \hat{I}_D$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Losses:** Calculate the total loss $L$ using the formulas below, comparing ground-truth quantities (from $T$) with predicted quantities (from $\hat{T}$) [S1].
5.  **Optimize:** Perform end-to-end gradient descent with respect to all trainable parameters (encoder, decoder, upsampler, discriminator) [S1].

**Formulas**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ L = L_{tri} + L_{col} + L_{LPIPS} + L_{feat} + \lambda_1 L_{adv} + \lambda_2 L_{cate} $$

Where:
*   $L_{tri}$: L1 loss between predicted triplane $\hat{T}$ and ground-truth triplane $T$.
*   $L_{col}$: Mean L1 loss between pairs $(I_{128}, \hat{I}_{128})$ and $(I_{512}, \hat{I}_{512})$.
*   $L_{LPIPS}$: LPIPS perceptual loss [Zhang et al. 2018] between pairs $(I_{128}, \hat{I}_{128})$ and $(I_{512}, \hat{I}_{512})$.
*   $L_{feat}$: Mean L1 loss between feature images $(I_f, \hat{I}_f)$.
*   $L_{adv}$: Adversarial loss using the pretrained dual discriminator from EG3D. The discriminator is fine-tuned to differentiate between images rendered from the encoder and images rendered from the frozen EG3D generator. It does not use real data [S1].
*   $L_{cate}$: Optional category-specific loss. For human faces, this is the ArcFace identity loss [Deng et al. 2019a] with $\lambda_2 = 1$. For cat faces, $\lambda_2 = 0$ [S1].
*   $\lambda_1$: Adversarial weight. Set to 0.1 for reference images and 0.025 for multiview images [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ for encoder parameters; $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for transformer parameters [S1].
*   **Depth Samples:** 48 depth samples for coarse and fine passes in volume rendering [S1].
*   **Discriminator:** Identical settings to EG3D (2D dual discriminator) [S1].
*   **Hardware/Duration:** ~10 days on 8 A100 or 8 A40 GPUs for ~220k iterations [S1].

def train_step(encoder, decoder, upsampler, discriminator, eg3d_generator,

    Performs one training step.
    stage: 1 for first 30k iterations, 2 for remaining.
    """
    # 1. Sample cameras and latent
    # Sample 32 reference cameras P_ref and 32 multiview cameras P_mv

    # Sample latent vectors for EG3D

    # EG3D yields triplanes T and renders images
    T = eg3d_generator(z)
    I_gt_ref = render(T, P_ref) # Returns I_f, I_128, I_512, I_D
    I_gt_mv = render(T, P_mv)
    
    # 3. Encode Reference Views
    # Input is the high-res reference image I_512 from ground truth
    I_512_gt_ref = I_gt_ref['I_512']
    T_hat = encoder(I_512_gt_ref)
    
    # 4. Render Predicted Views
    I_pred_ref = render(T_hat, P_ref)
    I_pred_mv = render(T_hat, P_mv)
    
    # 5. Compute Losses
    L_tri = l1_loss(T_hat, T)
    L_col = mean_l1_loss([I_gt_ref['I_128'], I_gt_ref['I_512']], 
                         [I_pred_ref['I_128'], I_pred_ref['I_512']])
    L_lpips = lpips_loss([I_gt_ref['I_128'], I_gt_ref['I_512']], 
                         [I_pred_ref['I_128'], I_pred_ref['I_512']])
    
    # Feature loss is removed in stage 2
    if stage == 1:
        L_feat = mean_l1_loss([I_gt_ref['I_f']], [I_pred_ref['I_f']])
    else:
        L_feat = 0.0
        
    # Adversarial loss
    # Discriminator differentiates between encoder outputs and EG3D outputs
    # Note: Lambda1 varies by view type in the paper, but here we compute a combined adv loss
    # The paper specifies lambda1=0.1 for ref, 0.025 for mv. 
    # We compute adv loss on both sets of images.
    D_real_ref = discriminator(I_gt_ref['I_512'])
    D_fake_ref = discriminator(I_pred_ref['I_512'])
    D_real_mv = discriminator(I_gt_mv['I_512'])
    D_fake_mv = discriminator(I_pred_mv['I_512'])
    
    # Standard GAN loss (e.g., hinge or BCE)
    L_adv_ref = adv_loss(D_real_ref, D_fake_ref)
    L_adv_mv = adv_loss(D_real_mv, D_fake_mv)
    
    # Weighted adversarial loss
    lambda1_ref = 0.1
    lambda1_mv = 0.025
    L_adv = lambda1_ref * L_adv_ref + lambda1_mv * L_adv_mv

    # lambda2 = 1 for human faces, 0 for cats
    if category == 'human':
        L_cate = arcface_loss(I_gt_ref['I_512'], I_pred_ref['I_512'])
        lambda2 = 1.0
    else:
        L_cate = 0.0
        lambda2 = 0.0
        
    # Stage-specific adjustments
    if stage == 1:
        # First stage: 30k iterations
        # No adversarial or category loss
        L_adv = 0.0
        L_cate = 0.0
        
        # Reference loss computed once every 10 syntheses
        # Multiview supervision every step
        # Freeze decoder and upsampler parameters
        # For implementation, we assume gradients are zeroed for decoder/upsampler

        # And "compute losses for the reference set... once every 10".

        # Let's implement the dynamic loss based on iteration count
        if iteration % 10 == 0:
            # Compute full reference loss
            L_ref = L_tri + L_col + L_lpips + L_feat
            # Also need multiview loss for the "every step" part?
            # "perform gradient steps with respect to multiview supervision at every gradient step"
            # This implies multiview loss is always part of the gradient.
            L_mv = mean_l1_loss([I_gt_mv['I_128'], I_gt_mv['I_512']], 
                                [I_pred_mv['I_128'], I_pred_mv['I_512']])
            L = L_ref + L_mv
        else:
            # Only multiview supervision
            L_mv = mean_l1_loss([I_gt_mv['I_128'], I_gt_mv['I_512']],

            L = L_mv
            
        # Freeze decoder and upsampler
        # In code, this is done by not passing their gradients or zeroing them
        # We assume the optimizer handles this via parameter groups or masking
        
    else:
        # Second stage: 37.5k to 220k iterations
        # Add adversarial and category losses
        # Backpropagate to all parameters
        # Compute reference losses every 2 gradient passes
        # Remove feature loss (L_feat = 0)
        # Set triplane loss weight to 0.01
        
        L_tri_weight = 0.01
        
        if iteration % 2 == 0:
            L_ref = L_tri_weight * L_tri + L_col + L_lpips + L_cate
        else:
            L_ref = L_col + L_lpips + L_cate # No L_tri, no L_feat
            
        L_mv = mean_l1_loss([I_gt_mv['I_128'], I_gt_mv['I_512']],

            L = L_ref + L_adv + L_cate
        else:
            L = L_ref + L_adv

    # 6. Backpropagate
    L.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    optimizer.zero_grad()

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Overfitting to Synthetic Data:** Naive optimization leads to models that perform well on synthetic data but fail on real images. The alternating supervision and adversarial training mitigate this [S1].
*   **Discriminator Training:** The dual discriminator is fine-tuned but does not use real data; it only distinguishes between encoder-rendered and EG3D-rendered images [S1].
*   **Category Specificity:** For cat faces, the category loss weight $\lambda_2$ is set to 0, and transfer learning from a human face model is used [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes approximately 10 days on 8 A100 or 8 A40 GPUs for ~220k iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** The method relies on the capacity of the EG3D generator and the encoder. The use of a frozen generator allows for efficient gradient computation without backpropagating through the entire GAN generator [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enforces multiview consistency, which is critical for 3D reconstruction. The adversarial component improves texture quality [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on the quality and diversity of the EG3D training data (e.g., FFHQ, AFHQ). Performance may degrade for categories not well-represented in the EG3D checkpoint [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When synthesizing 3D portraits from single images where multiview consistency is required.
*   **When Not to Use:** When real data supervision is preferred or when the target domain is significantly different from the EG3D training distribution.

## Background
The method is described in "Real-Time Radiance Fields for Single-Image Portrait View Synthesis" by Alex Trevithick et al. [S1]. It builds upon the EG3D architecture [Karras et al. 2020] and uses techniques from 2D GAN inversion [Richardson et al. 2021; Tov et al. 2021] for category-specific losses. The approach was developed to enable real-time rendering of high-quality portraits from a single image by learning an encoder that maps images to triplanes [S1].
