# NeRV Training
Also called: NeRV loss minimization, NeRV optimization

## Overview
NeRV training optimizes a continuous volumetric representation that decouples scene geometry (shape) from material appearance (reflectance) and lighting interactions (visibility) to enable efficient relighting and view synthesis [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by introducing a separate "visibility MLP" that predicts ray visibility and termination depth, supervised by consistency terms with the primary shape MLP rather than ground-truth labels, allowing the simulation of direct and one-bounce indirect illumination within the training loop [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed RGB images of a scene and corresponding lighting environment maps.
*   **Outputs:** Weights for three MLPs: Shape/Reflectance MLP ($\theta$), Visibility MLP ($\phi$).

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Positional Encoding:** Inputs are mapped using NeRF's positional encoding [S1].
    *   3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$: Maximum frequency $L_{coord} = 7$ (frequencies $2^0$ to $2^7$).
    *   3D direction vectors $\boldsymbol{\omega}$: Maximum frequency $L_{dir} = 4$ (frequencies $2^0$ to $2^4$).
2.  **Shape and Reflectance MLP ($\theta$):**
    *   Architecture: 8 fully-connected ReLU layers, 256 channels each.
    *   Input: Encoded $\mathbf{x}$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.
    *   Output: Volume density $\sigma$ and reflectance properties.
3.  **Visibility MLP ($\phi$):**
    *   Stage 1: 8 fully-connected ReLU layers, 256 channels each. Maps encoded $\mathbf{x}$ to an 8-dimensional feature vector.
    *   Stage 2: Concatenation with encoded $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.
    *   Stage 3: 4 fully-connected ReLU layers, 128 channels each.
    *   Output: Predicted visibility $\tilde{V}_\phi$ and expected termination depth $\tilde{D}_\phi$.

**Training Procedure:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize $\theta$ and $\phi$ from scratch.
2.  **Ray Sampling:** At each iteration:
    *   Sample a batch of $\mathcal{R}$ pixel rays from input images. Size: 512 rays.
    *   Sample a set of random rays $\mathcal{R}'$ intersecting the volume. Size: 256 rays.
3.  **Forward Pass (Pixel Rays $\mathcal{R}$):**
    *   Render pixels using the current NeRV model (Shape/Reflectance MLPs) to obtain rendered radiance $\tilde{L}(\mathbf{r})$.
    *   Compute pixel loss against ground truth image pixels $L(\mathbf{r})$.
4.  **Forward Pass (Random Rays $\mathcal{R}'$):**
    *   For each ray $\mathbf{r}' \in \mathcal{R}'$, compute visibility $V_\theta(\mathbf{r}'(t))$ and expected termination depth $D_\theta(\mathbf{r}'(t))$ implied by the Shape MLP's density $\sigma$ [S1].
    *   Query the Visibility MLP to get predictions $\tilde{V}_\phi(\mathbf{r}'(t))$ and $\tilde{D}_\phi(\mathbf{r}'(t))$.
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Define tone-mapping operator: $\tau(x) = \frac{x}{1+x}$.
    *   Compute total loss $\mathcal{L}$:
        $$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \tau(\tilde{L}(\mathbf{r})) - \tau(L(\mathbf{r})) \right\|_2^2 + \lambda \sum_{\mathbf{r}' \in \mathcal{R} \setminus \mathcal{R}_{\text{ret}}} \left( \left\| \tilde{V}_\phi(\mathbf{r}'(t)) - V_\theta(\mathbf{r}'(t)) \right\|_2^2 + \left\| \tilde{D}_\phi(\mathbf{r}'(t)) - D_\theta(\mathbf{r}'(t)) \right\|_2^2 \right) $$
    *   Where $\lambda = 20$ [S1].
    *   $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ret}}$ denotes rays excluded from the consistency term (likely those not interacting with reflective surfaces, though exact definition is not provided in the source text beyond the set difference notation) [S1].
    *   Apply a **stop gradient** to $V_\theta$ and $D_\theta$ in the consistency terms to prevent the shape MLP from degrading to match the visibility MLP [S1].
6.  **Optimization:**
    *   Update $\theta$ and $\phi$ using Adam optimizer.
    *   Total iterations: 1,000,000.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Stop Gradient:** The shape MLP must not be updated via the consistency loss terms to prevent it from degrading its rendering quality to match the visibility MLP [S1].
*   **R_ret:** The set $\mathcal{R}_{\text{ret}}$ is subtracted from the random rays in the consistency loss. The source does not define what constitutes a "ret" ray (likely "retro-reflected" or similar, but exact criteria are not given) [S1].
*   **Visibility Supervision:** The visibility MLP is not supervised by ground-truth visibility/depth. It is only optimized for consistency with the shape MLP's current geometry estimate [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering involves 256 stratified samples per ray [S1]. The visibility MLP adds computational overhead but reduces the complexity of computing direct and indirect illumination from quadratic/cubic to linear, enabling real-time-like training updates for relighting [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Hardware:** Implemented in JAX and trained on 128 TPU cores [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables simulation of direct illumination from environment lighting and one-bounce indirect illumination within the training loop [S1]. Decouples geometry and appearance for relighting.
*   **Limitations:** Requires posed RGB images and corresponding lighting environments for each scene [S1]. The visibility MLP is not supervised by ground truth, relying solely on consistency with the shape MLP [S1].

## Background
NeRV (Neural Reflectance and Visibility Fields) was introduced in the paper "NeRV: Neural Reflectance and Visibility Fields for Relighting and View Synthesis" [S1]. The method was developed to address the limitations of standard NeRF in handling relighting and complex lighting interactions by explicitly modeling visibility and reflectance fields. It builds on the NeRF training loop but adds the visibility MLP and consistency losses [S1].
