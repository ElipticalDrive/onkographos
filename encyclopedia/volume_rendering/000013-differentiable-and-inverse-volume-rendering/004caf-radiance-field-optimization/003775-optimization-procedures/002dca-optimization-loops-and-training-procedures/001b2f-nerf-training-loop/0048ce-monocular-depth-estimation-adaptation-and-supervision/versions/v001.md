# Monocular Depth Estimation Adaptation and Supervision

Also called: Monocular Depth Regularization, MDE Adaptation, Depth-Guided NeRF

## Overview
This family of methods integrates pre-trained Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) networks into NeRF training to resolve geometric ambiguities inherent in sparse-view or single-view reconstruction. By supervising the NeRF's rendered depth against pseudo-depth maps generated by MDE, these methods enforce multi-view consistency and absolute scale, which photometric loss alone cannot guarantee. Unlike standard photometric training, this approach leverages external geometric priors to guide ray termination distributions and regularize depth maps, distinguishing it from sibling methods that rely solely on internal NeRF optimization or differentiable rendering without external priors.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of aligning NeRF-rendered depth with monocular depth estimates. The general workflow involves:
1.  **Depth Rendering:** The NeRF model renders a depth map $\bar{D}$ for a given camera pose. This is typically computed as the expected distance of ray termination: $\bar{D}(r) = \sum_i w_i y_i$, where $w_i$ is the weight of sample $i$ and $y_i$ is its distance from the camera [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Monocular Prior Extraction:** A pre-trained MDE network $G_\phi$ predicts a pseudo-depth map $D^*$ from an input image $I$ (either a training view or a rendered novel view) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Alignment:** Since MDE outputs are scale- and shift-ambiguous, a linear alignment is computed between $D^*$ and $\bar{D}$. This is typically done via least squares to find scale $w$ and shift $q$ such that $w \cdot D^* + q \approx \bar{D}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Loss Computation:** A consistency loss is minimized. To prevent gradient flow into the MDE network during NeRF updates, the stop-gradient operator $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is applied to the monocular depth $D^*$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance](11911-depth-guided-nerf-training-via-earth-mover-s-distance.md)
This method improves geometric understanding in sparse-view settings by supervising the *distribution* of ray termination distances rather than direct pixel-wise depth matching. It uses Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) to align the NeRF's predicted ray termination distribution with monocular depth priors from diffusion models (DiffDP). Unlike methods that enforce direct L2 depth matching, this approach guides the ray termination distribution to avoid wrong geometry while maintaining photometric quality. It utilizes uncertainty maps derived from the diffusion process to weight the loss, down-weighting uncertain pixels for depth supervision.

### [Monocular Depth Regularization on Seen Views](12330-monocular-depth-regularization-on-seen-views.md)
This method applies a depth consistency loss to align pseudo-depth maps from a pre-trained MDE network with NeRF-rendered depth for *seen* input views. It differs from global scale/shift fitting by computing alignment parameters ($w_i, q_i$) per image patch. This patch-wise approach prevents dominant objects from skewing the depth alignment and alleviates local geometric inconsistencies. It is computationally efficient compared to full-image rendering losses.

### [NeRF-guided MDE Adaptation](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md)
This method adapts the weights of the pre-trained MDE network itself, using the NeRF as a geometric prior. It solves the ambiguity of monocular depth (e.g., convexity/concavity) by leveraging the NeRF's absolute geometry. Unlike sibling methods that only use depth to regularize NeRF training, this creates a bidirectional knowledge transfer: the MDE provides priors to the NeRF, and the NeRF provides absolute scale/orientation to the MDE. It minimizes a composite loss including absolute alignment and patch-wise fitting.

#### Monocular Depth Regularization on Unseen Views
- **Definition:** A loss function that supervises NeRF geometry at unseen/novel viewpoints by aligning NeRF-rendered depth with monocular depth estimated from the rendered novel view image [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** For each unseen view $l$ in the set of unseen views $U$: 1. Sample a patch of the novel viewpoint. 2. Render color $\bar{I}_l$ and depth $\bar{D}_l$ from NeRF. 3. Extract monocular depth $D_l^* = G_\phi(\bar{I}_l)$ from the rendered color image. 4. Compute scale $w_l$ and shift $q_l$ via least squares between $D_l^*$ and $\bar{D}_l$. 5. Calculate loss $L_{unseen}$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** $U$: Set of unseen view images; $P$: Randomly sampled patch of pixels [S1, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Monocular depth from noisy NeRF rendering may be affected by fine-grained artifacts. However, the MDE model's strong geometric prior is robust against these artifacts, filtering them out [S1, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Unlike previous methods that only impose priors on input viewpoints (limited by self-occlusion/sparsity), this method applies priors to arbitrary viewpoints, reaching every location [S1, unofficial].

#### MDE Adaptation Loss
- **Definition:** A composite loss function for adapting a monocular depth estimator to predict scene-specific absolute geometry by enforcing multi-view consistency and local patch-wise scale-shift fitting [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** Compute the first addend term (scale-shift loss) to force the MDE to adapt towards multiview consistency, reducing its ill-posed nature and aligning its initial global depth prediction with the absolute geometry captured by NeRF. Compute the second addend term (L1 loss with patch-wise scale-shift fitting) to aid the modeling of fine, detailed, local geometry. Optimize the MDE network by minimizing the sum of both terms simultaneously; using only one term performs worse on all metrics than using both in conjunction [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Scale-shift loss weight and L1 loss weight are required for optimization, though specific values are not detailed in the source section [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The integration of monocular depth priors into NeRF optimization emerged to address the "flatness" and geometric ambiguity problems in sparse-view NeRF training. Standard NeRF relies on photometric consistency, which can be satisfied by many incorrect geometric configurations (e.g., a flat surface with varying texture). Early works like [14, 41, 61, 50] in the source literature applied depth priors only to input viewpoints, limiting their effectiveness due to sparsity and occlusion [S1, unofficial]. The DaRF method [S1, unofficial] introduced the concept of adapting MDE networks to the specific scene geometry via NeRF, enabling supervision on unseen views and bidirectional knowledge transfer. This approach leverages the robustness of modern MDE models (including diffusion-based ones like DiffDP) to provide absolute scale and orientation cues that NeRF lacks from images alone.
