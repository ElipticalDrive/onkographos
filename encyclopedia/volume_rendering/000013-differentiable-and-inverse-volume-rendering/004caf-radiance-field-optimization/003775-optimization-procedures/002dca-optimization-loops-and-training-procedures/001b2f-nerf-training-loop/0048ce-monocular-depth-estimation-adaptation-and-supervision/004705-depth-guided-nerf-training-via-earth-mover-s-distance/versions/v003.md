# Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance

Also called: Depth-guided NeRF via Earth Mover’s Distance, EM D-Guided Sampling, EMD-Guided Depth Supervision for NeRF, EMD-NeRF, EMD-based objective

## Overview
This method improves the geometric accuracy of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) trained on sparse views by supervising the distribution of ray termination distances using Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) against a monocular depth prior, rather than enforcing a direct L2 match on rendered depth [S1]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [DäRF](12331-d-rf.md) and [SCADE](9518-nerfingmvs.md) by utilizing a single depth prior from a diffusion model (DiffDP) and weighting the supervision by pixel-wise uncertainty, thereby avoiding the propagation of prior inaccuracies and the instability associated with multiple ambiguous hypotheses [S1].

## How it works
The method integrates a pre-trained diffusion-based depth estimator with the NeRF optimization loop. It constructs a probability distribution from NeRF samples, aligns it with a depth prior via EMD, and balances the loss using uncertainty maps derived from the diffusion process.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Set of $N$ input images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^N$ with known SfM camera poses (position $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ and viewing direction $(\theta_i, \phi_i)$) [S1].
    *   Pre-trained DiffDP network $G_\phi$ for monocular depth estimation and uncertainty quantification [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Trained NeRF network $F_\Theta$ mapping $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi) \to (c, \sigma)$ [S1].
    *   Rendered images and depth maps with improved geometric consistency [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **NeRF Network $F_\Theta$:** Multilayer perceptron.
    *   Input: 5D vector $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)$.
    *   Output: Color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$.
    *   Regularization: Dropout ($p=0.1$) and Weight Decay ($\lambda_{wd}=10^{-6}$) [S1].
*   **Depth Prior $z_0$:** 2D tensor representing the predicted depth map for an input image $I$, output by DiffDP [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Map $u$:** 2D tensor with values in $[0, 1]$, representing pixel-wise reliability of the depth prior [S1].
*   **Ray Samples:**
    *   Coarse network: 64 samples per ray [S1].
    *   Fine network: 128 samples per ray [S1].
    *   EMD Sampling: Additional 128 samples drawn from the NeRF's termination distribution for loss calculation [S1].

### Step-by-Step Method

1.  **Depth Prior and Uncertainty Extraction:**
    For each input image $I$, compute the depth prior $z_0$ and uncertainty map $u$ using the DiffDP network [S1].
    *   DiffDP formulates denoising as $p_\theta(z_{0:T}|I) = p_\theta(z_T) \prod_{t=1}^T p_\theta(z_{t-1}|z_t, I)$, where $z_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and $z_0$ is the final depth estimate [S1].
    *   Compute temporal uncertainty count $c(z_{0:T})$ by comparing consecutive estimates $z_t$ and $z_{t-1}$ against a threshold $\tau$:
        $$ c(z_{0:T}) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=T,\dots,1} \mathbb{I}(|z_t - z_{t-1}| \ge \tau) $$
        where $\mathbb{I}$ is the indicator function [S1].
    *   Compute spatial consistency uncertainty by mirroring the image $M(I)$ and comparing predictions:
        $$ U(z_{0:T}|I) = \frac{c(z_{0:T}|I) + M(c(z_{0:T}|M(I)))}{2} $$
    *   Augment with pixel-wise difference between original and mirrored predictions:
        $$ u(z_{0:T}|I) = U(z_{0:T}|I) \cdot \| z_0|_I - M(z_0|_{M(I)}) \|_1 $$
    *   Normalize $u$ to $[0, 1]$ [S1].

2.  **NeRF Forward Pass and Weight Calculation:**
    For a ray $r$ with samples at distances $y_i$ (where $y_i$ is the distance from the camera):
    *   Query $F_\Theta$ to get density $\sigma_i$ at each sample $y_i$ [S1].
    *   Compute the distance between adjacent samples $\delta_i = y_{i+1} - y_i$ [S1].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i$ and weight $w_i$ for sample $i$:
        $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
        $$ w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    *   Compute the rendered depth $\bar{D}(r)$ as the weighted average:
        $$ \bar{D}(r) = \sum_i w_i y_i $$
        [S1].

3.  **EMD-Guided Sampling:**
    Instead of using $\bar{D}(r)$ directly for loss, construct a probability distribution from the NeRF samples to guide the optimization [S1].
    *   Normalize weights to form a piece-wise constant probability density function:
        $$ \hat{w}_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j} $$
    *   Construct the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) from $\hat{w}_i$ [S1].
    *   Sample $N_{EMD}$ new ray termination distances $y = \{y_1, \dots, y_{N_{EMD}}\}$ using inverse transform sampling from the CDF [S1].
    *   *Note:* The source specifies sampling 128 additional samples for this step [S1].

4.  **Loss Calculation:**
    Compute the total loss $L$ for the ray, combining photometric and depth components weighted by uncertainty $u$ [S1].
    *   **Photometric Loss:**
        $$ L_{photo} = \| \hat{C}(r) - C(r) \|_2^2 $$
        where $\hat{C}(r) = \sum_i w_i c_i$ is the rendered color and $C(r)$ is the observed color [S1].
    *   **EMD Depth Loss:**
        $$ L_{EMD} = EMD(y, z_0) $$
        where $y$ are the sampled termination distances and $z_0$ is the depth prior [S1].
        *   Since EMD is non-differentiable, approximate it using Sinkhorn Divergence during training [S1].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ L = (1 + u)^\gamma L_{photo} + \lambda (1 - u)^\gamma L_{EMD} $$
        where $\lambda$ is a balancing weight and $\gamma$ controls the impact of uncertainty $u$ [S1].
        *   *Note:* The source uses $\gamma$ in the exponent but does not specify its value. The balancing weight $\lambda$ is also not explicitly numerically defined in the provided text, though standard hyperparameters for Sinkhorn are mentioned [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Initialize the scale of the depth priors to 1 and learn the scale with a small learning rate [S1].
    *   Perform gradient descent on $F_\Theta$ using the computed loss $L$ [S1].
    *   Batch size: 1024 rays per batch [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Photometric vs. Geometric Discrepancy:** High photometric quality (PSNR/SSIM) does not guarantee correct geometry. NeRFs can render plausible images while misinterpreting structure (e.g., misinterpreting a cabinet as a carpet pattern) [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Handling:** Unlike Focal Loss which increases weight for uncertain examples, this method *decreases* the weight of the depth loss for uncertain pixels, relying more on photometric loss [S1].
*   **Prior Noise:** Using multiple depth hypotheses (as in SCADE) can introduce noise and training instability. This method uses a single prior to avoid this [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Map Sensitivity:** The construction of the uncertainty map can be sensitive to hyperparameters (e.g., threshold $\tau$) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of NeRF samples (64 coarse, 128 fine) and EMD samples (128). The Sinkhorn approximation adds overhead proportional to the number of samples and iterations.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Standard NeRF memory plus storage for depth prior and uncertainty maps.
*   **Performance:** On ScanNet+, reduces baseline RMSE by at least 56% while maintaining comparable photometric results [S1]. With DiffDP prior, EMD objective reduces initial prior RMSE by 31.4%; with DepthAnything prior, reduces it by 18.8% [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to noisy priors; handles multimodal ray termination distributions better than mean-depth supervision; avoids instability from multiple hypotheses.
*   **Limitations:** Uncertainty measure is specific to diffusion-based models (DiffDP); sensitive to hyperparameters in uncertainty map construction [S1].
*   **When to use:** Sparse view reconstruction (N < 20), challenging indoor scenes (e.g., ScanNet, ScanNet+), where geometric accuracy is critical [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Dense view settings where NeRF geometry is already well-constrained; scenes where diffusion-based uncertainty is not available or reliable [S1].
