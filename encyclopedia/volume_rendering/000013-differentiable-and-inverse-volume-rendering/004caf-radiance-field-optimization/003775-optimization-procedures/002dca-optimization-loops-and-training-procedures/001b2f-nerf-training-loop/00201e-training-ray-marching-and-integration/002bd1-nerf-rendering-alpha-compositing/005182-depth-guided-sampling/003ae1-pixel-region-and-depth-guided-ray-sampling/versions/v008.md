# Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling


## Overview
Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling is a ray sampling strategy for neural radiance fields that increases sampling probability in pixel areas exhibiting significant color and depth variation, reducing redundant rays in flat regions to accelerate network convergence and improve geometry perception [S1]. Unlike conventional NeRF which uses uniform distribution, this method samples varying ray densities based on depth boundaries and pixel regions to simultaneously accelerate convergence and improve geometry perception [S1]. It builds on [Depth-Guided Sampling](14489-depth-guided-sampling.md) by explicitly incorporating color variation alongside depth variation to guide ray density.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ source training images $I_i$ (where $i=1, \dots, N$), each with height $H_i$ and width $W_i$. A neural network $\Theta$ parameterizing the radiance field. Depth estimation data derived from the ENeRF cost volume [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of sampled rays $\mathbb{R}_i$ for each image $I_i$, where the number of rays $M$ per image is determined by the sampling probabilities. The final rendered colors are produced via volume rendering along these rays.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Image Grid:** Each image $I_i$ is treated as a grid of pixels $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, H_i]$ and $v \in [0, W_i]$ [S1].
*   **Depth Map:** A rough depth estimate $D(u, v)$ for each pixel, obtained from the ENeRF depth estimation module [S1].
*   **Color Value:** The RGB color value $C(u, v)$ for each pixel from the source view images [S1].
*   **Sampling Probability Map:** A scalar probability $P(u, v)$ for each pixel, derived from color and depth variations [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Obtain Depth Information:** Use the depth estimation module from the ENeRF framework to obtain rough depth information for the scene. Specifically, leverage the cost volume from ENeRF to obtain depth information $D(u, v)$ for each pixel $(u, v)$ [S1].
2.  **Calculate Sampling Probability:** Calculate the sampling probability $P(u, v)$ for each pixel based on its color value $C(u, v)$ and the estimated depth information $D(u, v)$ [S1]. The probability is higher in areas with substantial color variation and marked geometric depth variation [S1].
3.  **Ray Sampling:** During ray sampling, generate rays for image $I_i$. For each pixel $(u, v)$, sample additional rays with a frequency proportional to $P(u, v)$. This results in varying ray densities: more rays are sampled in regions with significant color variation from the source view images and regions where geometric depth varies markedly [S1].
4.  **Volume Rendering:** Perform volume rendering using the network $\Theta$ to output colors and densities along the sampled rays. The network maps 5D coordinate points $(p, d)$ to volume density $\sigma$ and directional emitted color $c$ [S1].
5.  **Optimization:** Optimize the network parameters $\Theta$ by minimizing the volume rendering loss [S1].

**Formulas:**
The optimization goal minimizes the volume rendering loss [S1]:
$$
\min_{\Theta} \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{NM} \sum_{r_i^k \in \mathbb{R}_i} \|V_{\Theta}(r_i^k) - c_i^k\|_2^2 \quad (1)
$$
where:
*   $c_i^k$ is the true color of pixels [S1].
*   $M$ is the number of sampled rays per image [S1].
*   $N$ is the number of trained images [S1].
*   $r_i^k$ is the $k$-th ray from the $i$-th training image [S1].
*   $V_{\Theta}$ is the volume rendering function [S1].
*   $\mathbb{R}_i$ is the set of rays generated from image $I_i$ [S1].

Uniform ray sampling assumes rays follow a uniform distribution [S1]:
$$
r_i(u, v) \sim U(I_i), \quad u \in [0, H_i], \quad v \in [0, W_i] \quad (2)
$$
where $H_i$ and $W_i$ are the height and width of image $I_i$ [S1]. The proposed method deviates from this by making the distribution non-uniform based on $P(u, v)$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: The number of trained images (source views) [S1].
*   $M$: The number of sampled rays per image [S1].
*   $\Theta$: The network parameters optimized by minimizing the color error [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The method relies on the accuracy of the rough depth estimation from ENeRF. Inaccurate depth estimates may lead to incorrect sampling probabilities in depth-variant regions [S1].
*   **Boundaries:** The method assumes that significant color and depth variations correlate with important geometric features. In regions with high-frequency noise that does not correspond to geometry, this may lead to unnecessary sampling [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the scene is entirely uniform in color and depth, the sampling probability becomes uniform, degrading to standard uniform sampling [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

def pixel_region_depth_guided_sampling(images, depth_maps, network, num_rays_per_image):
    """
    Implements Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling.
    
    Args:
        images: List of source view images I_i, each with shape (H_i, W_i, 3).
        depth_maps: List of depth maps D_i corresponding to images, shape (H_i, W_i).
        network: The neural radiance field model Theta.
        num_rays_per_image: M, the target number of rays to sample per image.
        
    Returns:
        sampled_rays: List of rays for each image.
        rendered_colors: Rendered colors for each image.
    """
    sampled_rays = []
    rendered_colors = []
    
    for i, image in enumerate(images):
        H, W, _ = image.shape
        depth_map = depth_maps[i]
        
        # Step 1 & 2: Calculate sampling probability based on color and depth variation
        # Note: The exact formula for P(u,v) combining color and depth is not explicitly 
        # provided in the sources, only that it increases in areas of significant variation.
        # We assume a normalized probability map is generated.
        color_variation = calculate_color_variation(image) # [G] Placeholder for specific variation metric
        depth_variation = calculate_depth_variation(depth_map) # [G] Placeholder for specific variation metric
        
        # Combine variations to get probability. Sources state probability increases with variation.
        # A common implementation would be a weighted sum or product, normalized.
        raw_probability = color_variation + depth_variation
        total_prob = np.sum(raw_probability)
        if total_prob > 0:
            sampling_prob = raw_probability / total_prob
        else:
            sampling_prob = np.ones((H, W)) / (H * W)
            
        # Step 3: Sample rays based on probability
        # Flatten probability map
        flat_prob = sampling_prob.flatten()
        
        # Sample M indices from the flattened grid according to flat_prob
        # This ensures pixels with higher variation are sampled more frequently
        pixel_indices = np.random.choice(H * W, size=num_rays_per_image, p=flat_prob)
        
        # Convert flat indices to (u, v) coordinates
        u_coords = pixel_indices // W
        v_coords = pixel_indices % W
        
        # Generate rays for these pixels
        # Ray direction is determined by the viewing direction of image I_i
        rays = generate_rays_from_pixels(u_coords, v_coords, image, i)
        sampled_rays.append(rays)
        
        # Step 4 & 5: Volume Rendering and Optimization
        # Render colors along the sampled rays
        rendered_color = volume_render(rays, network)
        rendered_colors.append(rendered_color)
        
    return sampled_rays, rendered_colors

def calculate_color_variation(image):
    """
    [G] Helper to calculate local color variation.
    Typically computed using gradient magnitude or variance in local windows.
    """
    # Example: Sobel gradient magnitude
    gray = np.mean(image, axis=2)
    gx = np.abs(np.gradient(gray, axis=1))
    gy = np.abs(np.gradient(gray, axis=0))
    return np.sqrt(gx**2 + gy**2)

def calculate_depth_variation(depth_map):
    """
    [G] Helper to calculate local depth variation.
    Typically computed using gradient magnitude of the depth map.
    """
    dz = np.abs(np.gradient(depth_map))
    return dz

## Subtopics

### Superpixel-based Efficient Sampling
A sampling method for learning neural fields from large input images that optimizes pixel-level ray sampling by segmenting the error map into superpixels and dynamically updating their errors to increase ray sampling in areas with higher rendering errors [S2]. Unlike quadtree or fixed-shape patch sampling methods which may cause fragmented image information, superpixels reduce redundancy while considering local information [S2]. Unlike color-guided ray sampling which does not simultaneously consider rendering errors, SES uses rendering errors to guide sampling [S2]. It segments the input image into various superpixels using the SLIC algorithm, where each superpixel represents unique local information [S2]. It treats superpixels as the smallest processing unit for sampling decisions [S2]. It sets different sampling probabilities for various superpixels based on their rendering errors [S2]. It continuously updates the superpixel errors during the training process [S2]. It integrates the rendering errors into the loss function to speed up convergence in areas with significant errors [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method accelerates network convergence, achieving the same convergence effect in minutes through fine-tuning reconstructions from given views [S1]. It reduces redundant rays, thereby reducing the number of forward passes required for training [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Applied to the ENeRF framework, it achieves state-of-the-art performance on both view synthesis and training time [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is applicable to static or dynamic scenes [S1]. It is designed to handle high-resolution inputs by reducing redundancy [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates per-scene optimization, improves efficiency, better perceives spatial geometry, and improves rendering quality especially for rich-texture regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the depth estimation module [S1]. May sample unnecessarily in noisy regions if color/depth variation does not correlate with geometry [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When fast convergence and improved geometry perception are required, particularly for scenes with rich textures and significant depth variations [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the scene is uniform in color and depth, or when accurate depth estimation is not available [S1].

## Background
Originated in the context of accelerating neural radiance field (NeRF) training. The method was introduced in the paper "Efficient ray sampling for radiance fields reconstruction" [S1]. It builds on the ENeRF framework [S1]. The key hypothesis is that uniform ray sampling fails to match the real-world surface density distribution, leading to redundant sampling in flat regions [S1]. The method was applied to achieve state-of-the-art performance on view synthesis and training time [S1].
