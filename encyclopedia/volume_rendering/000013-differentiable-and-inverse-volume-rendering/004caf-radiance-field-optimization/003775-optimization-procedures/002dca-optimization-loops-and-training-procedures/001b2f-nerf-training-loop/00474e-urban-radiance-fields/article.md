# Urban Radiance Fields

Also called: URFs

## Overview
Urban Radiance Fields is a method for 3D reconstruction and view synthesis in large-scale urban environments using sparse multi-view images supplemented by LiDAR data [S1]. It addresses the challenges of unbounded outdoor scenes, limited camera pose variety, and large-scale geometry by incorporating specific depth, sight, and sky segmentation losses into the standard NeRF training loop [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which relies solely on photometric consistency, this method enforces geometric constraints via LiDAR and semantic constraints via sky segmentation to stabilize training in open environments [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Sparse multi-view images.
*   LiDAR depth measurements ($z$).
*   Sky segmentation masks for input images (generated by a pretrained model) [S1].
*   Camera poses and intrinsics for input images [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A trained neural radiance field model (implicitly defined by the network parameters).
*   A 3D point cloud extracted from the model.
*   A 3D mesh reconstructed from the point cloud [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   The method uses the standard NeRF representation: a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that maps 3D coordinates and viewing directions to volume density ($\sigma$) and view-dependent color ($c$) [S1].
*   Ray sampling follows the standard volumetric rendering pipeline: rays are cast from virtual camera pixels, and samples are taken along the ray segment $[t_1, t_2]$ [S1].
*   Weight function $w(t)$ is defined as $T(t)\sigma(t)$, where $T(t)$ is the accumulated transmittance and $\sigma(t)$ is the volume density at sample point $t$ [S1].

**Training Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** For each pixel in the training images, cast a ray through the scene [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points along the ray within the bounding volume of interest [S1].
3.  **Network Query:** Query the neural network for density $\sigma(t)$ and color $c(t)$ at each sample point $t$ [S1].
4.  **Rendering:** Compute the rendered color $C$ and estimated depth $\hat{z}$ using volumetric integration [S1].
    *   Estimated depth $\hat{z}$ is computed as the expected depth along the ray [S1].
5.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the total loss as a weighted sum of the following components [S1]:
    *   **Photometric Loss:** Standard $L_2$ loss between rendered color and ground truth image color [S1].
    *   **Depth Loss ($L_{depth}$):** Forces the estimated depth $\hat{z}$ to match the LiDAR-measured depth $z$ [S1].
    *   **Sight Loss ($L_{sight}$):** Forces the radiance weight $w(t)$ to concentrate at the surface defined by the LiDAR depth $z$ [S1].
    *   **Segmentation Loss ($L_{seg}$):** Forces density to be zero for rays passing through sky pixels [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Update network parameters using gradient descent [S1].
7.  **3D Reconstruction (Post-Training):**
    *   Cast rays for each pixel in a virtual camera grid [S1].
    *   Extract 3D points using the estimated depth $\hat{z}$ [S1].
    *   Apply Poisson Surface Reconstruction to the resulting point cloud to generate a mesh [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total loss is composed of the following terms [S1]:

$$ L_{depth} = \mathbb{E}[(z - \hat{z})^2] $$

$$ L_{sight} = \mathbb{E}\left[ \int_{t_1}^{t_2} (w(t) - \delta(z))^2 dt \right] $$

$$ L_{seg} = \mathbb{E}\left[ \int_{t_1}^{t_2} S_i(r) (w(t) - \delta(z))^2 dt \right] $$

Where:
*   $z$: LiDAR measured depth [S1].
*   $\hat{z}$: Estimated depth from the NeRF model [S1].
*   $w(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$: The weight of the sample at $t$, where $T(t)$ is transmittance and $\sigma(t)$ is density [S1].
*   $\delta(z)$: The Dirac delta function centered at the LiDAR depth $z$ [S1].
*   $S_i(r)$: A binary indicator function. $S_i(r) = 1$ if ray $r$ passes through a sky pixel in the $i$-th image, and $0$ otherwise. Sky pixels are identified using a pretrained segmentation model [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Weights for the loss terms ($L_{depth}, L_{sight}, L_{seg}$): Not given in the sources.
*   Sky segmentation model: Pretrained model referenced as [198] in the source [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **LiDAR Sparsity:** The method relies on LiDAR data. In areas with no LiDAR coverage, the depth and sight losses may not provide sufficient geometric constraint, potentially leading to artifacts [S1].
*   **Sky Detection:** The segmentation loss depends on accurate sky pixel identification. Errors in the pretrained sky segmentation model [S1] may lead to incorrect density suppression in non-sky regions or failure to suppress density in actual sky regions.
*   **Unbounded Scenes:** Urban environments are unbounded. The method uses a bounding volume for rendering, but the sky loss helps handle the background, preventing the network from learning arbitrary geometry in empty space [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** The method is designed for large-scale urban scenes and sparse views, where standard NeRF often fails due to lack of geometric constraints [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Incorporates explicit geometric priors (LiDAR) and semantic priors (sky segmentation) to stabilize training in challenging outdoor environments [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires LiDAR data and sky segmentation masks. Performance depends on the quality of these auxiliary inputs [S1].
*   **When to use:** Large-scale urban reconstruction with sparse multi-view images and available LiDAR data [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Indoor scenes or scenes without LiDAR data where the specific losses cannot be applied [S1].

## Background
Urban Radiance Fields was proposed in November 2021 [S1]. It is part of the broader effort to adapt NeRF-based methods to outdoor, large-scale environments, which present challenges such as unbounded space and limited camera pose diversity [S1]. The method builds on the standard NeRF training loop but extends it with domain-specific losses [S1].
