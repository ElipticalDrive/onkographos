# AltNeRF

Also called: Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization

## Overview
AltNeRF is a framework for creating resilient Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations from monocular videos without relying on known camera poses [S1]. It solves the problem of suboptimal NeRF reconstruction caused by the lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise initial camera poses by integrating self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SMDE) [S1]. Unlike methods that strictly align rendered depth with priors, AltNeRF uses an alternating algorithm to harmonize NeRF outputs with SMDE through error-tolerant regularization, allowing it to progressively refine both the scene geometry and camera poses [S1].

## How it works
AltNeRF comprises two components: the Scene Prior Module (SPM) and the Scene Representation Module (SRM) [S1]. The SPM provides robust depth and pose priors, while the SRM refines these poses and learns the continuous volumetric field, regularized by the depth priors [S1]. The modules interact via an alternating optimization loop [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** Monocular video data (sequence of images $I$) and known camera intrinsics $K$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Refined camera poses $P$, depth maps $D$, and the NeRF representation $f_n$ [S1].

### Data Structures and Layouts

**Scene Prior Module (SPM)**
The SPM consists of two networks, $f_d$ (depth) and $f_p$ (pose), pretrained on a large dataset [S1].
*   **Depth Network ($f_d$):**
    *   **Encoder:** ResNet-50 with the fully-connected layer removed [S1].
    *   **Decoder:** Ten $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (two per scale) with bilinear up-sampling [S1].
    *   **Output:** Depth map $D$ [S1].
*   **Pose Network ($f_p$):**
    *   **Structure:** ResNet-34 [S1].
    *   **Output:** Vector of 9 elements [S1].
        *   First 6 elements: Continuous rotation representation [S1].
        *   Last 3 elements: Translation vector $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

**Scene Representation Module (SRM)**
The SRM learns the NeRF and refines poses [S1].
*   **NeRF Network ($f_n$):**
    *   **Structure:** Eight fully-connected layers with skip connections for density output, one linear layer for color [S1].
    *   **Function:** $f_n: (p, d) \rightarrow (\sigma, c)$, where $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a 3D point and $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a unit viewing direction [S1].
*   **Pose Representation:**
    *   Poses are represented as rigid transformations in $SE(3)$ [S1].
    *   **Initialization:** The world coordinate system aligns with the first frame $I_0$ (identity matrix) [S1].
    *   **Chain Rule:** Relative transformations $P_{i-1 \rightarrow i}$ predicted by SPM are combined to calculate absolute poses: $P_i = P_{i-1 \rightarrow i} \times P_{i-1}$ [S1].
    *   **Refinement:** SRM optimizes a residual pose $\Delta P$ against the prior $P$. The refined pose is $\hat{P} = \Delta P \times P$ [S1].

### Formulas

**Volume Rendering**
NeRF renders color $\hat{C}(r)$ for a ray $r = o + td$ [S1]:
$$ \hat{C}(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\sigma(t)c(t)dt $$
where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(s)ds)$ handles occlusions, and $t_n, t_f$ are near and far depth bounds [S1].

**Scene Depth**
The rendered depth $\hat{D}(r)$ is the mean terminating distance [S1]:
$$ \hat{D}(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\sigma(t)t dt $$

**Loss Functions**
1.  **Photometric Loss ($L_p$):** Used for SMDE reconstruction [S1]:
    $$ L_p(I_t, \hat{I}_t) = (1 - \text{SSIM}(I_t, \hat{I}_t)) + \frac{\alpha}{2}(1 - \alpha)\|I_t - \hat{I}_t\|_1 $$
    where $\alpha = 0.85$ [S1].
2.  **Edge-aware Smoothness Loss ($L_s$):** Ensures smoothness in depth maps [S1]:
    $$ L_s = |\partial_x D|e^{-|\partial_x I|} + |\partial_y D|e^{-|\partial_y I|} $$
    where $\partial_x, \partial_y$ are image gradients [S1].
3.  **Distilling Loss ($L_r$):** Used in SPM fine-tuning [S1]:
    $$ L_r = 1 - \text{SSIM}(D, D_r) + 0.1 \times (E_r \oplus E / \text{size}(E)) $$
    where $D_r$ is a reference depth map, $\oplus$ is XOR, and $E_r, E$ are occluding boundary maps [S1].
4.  **SRM Total Loss ($L_{pt}$):** For SPM pretraining/fine-tuning [S1]:
    $$ L_{pt} = L_p + L_r + 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times L_s $$
5.  **Error-Tolerant Depth Regularization ($L_e$):** SRM uses Huber loss with tolerance $\epsilon$ [S1]:
    $$ L_e = H\left(\max\left(\frac{\|\hat{D} - D\|_1}{\hat{D} + D} - \epsilon, 0\right)\right) $$
    where $H(\cdot)$ is the Huber loss [S1].
6.  **SRM Optimization Objective ($L_{sr}$):** Combines reconstruction and depth regularization [S1]:
    $$ L_{sr} = L_c + \gamma \cdot L_e $$
    where $L_c = \|\hat{C}(r) - C(r)\|_2$ is the reconstruction loss, and $\gamma$ is a balancing hyper-parameter [S1].

**Multi-view Consistency**
Uncertainty $U_t$ for a target depth map $D_t$ is computed against warped source depths $D_{s \rightarrow t}$ [S1]:
$$ U_t = \|D_t - D_{s \rightarrow t}\|_1 $$
This uncertainty weights the loss functions via Softmin to mitigate unreliable estimates [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\gamma$: Balances reconstruction and depth regularization. Values: 0.08 (LLFF, CO3D); 0.15 (ScanNet, Captures) [S1].
*   $S_r$: SRM iterations per alternation. Value: 50K [S1].
*   $S_p$: SPM fine-tuning iterations per alternation. Value: 500 [S1].
*   Alternations: Total alternating cycles. Value: 2 [S1].
*   SPM Pretrain LR: $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SPM Fine-tune LR: $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   SRM NeRF LR: Initial $10^{-3}$, exponentially decays to $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SRM Pose LR: Initial $10^{-5}$, linearly increases to $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ after 1K iterations, then exponentially decays to $10^{-5}$ [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def altnerf_train(video_frames, intrinsics_K):
    """
    Main training loop for AltNeRF.
    video_frames: List of images [I_0, I_1, ..., I_N]
    intrinsics_K: Camera intrinsic matrix
    """
    
    # 1. Initialize SPM (Scene Prior Module)
    # SPM consists of depth network f_d and pose network f_p
    # Pretrained on large dataset (NYUv2, VOID)
    spm = SPM()
    
    # Test-time adaptation: Fine-tune SPM on target video to close domain gap
    # Use distilling loss L_r and photometric loss L_p
    # Learning rate: 5.0e-5
    spm.fine_tune(video_frames, intrinsics_K, iterations=1000) # [G] Iterations not specified, assumed small
    
    # 2. Initialize SRM (Scene Representation Module)
    # SRM contains NeRF f_n and residual pose optimizer
    srm = SRM()
    
    # 3. Initial Pose and Depth Estimation
    # SPM predicts relative poses P_{i-1->i}
    # Compute absolute poses P_i using chain rule: P_i = P_{i-1->i} * P_{i-1}
    # P_0 is identity matrix
    initial_poses = []
    current_pose = IdentityMatrix()
    for i in range(1, len(video_frames)):
        relative_pose = spm.f_p(video_frames[i-1], video_frames[i])
        current_pose = relative_pose * current_pose
        initial_poses.append(current_pose)
    initial_poses.insert(0, IdentityMatrix())
    
    # SPM predicts initial depth maps D_0
    initial_depths = [spm.f_d(frame) for frame in video_frames]
    
    # 4. Alternating Optimization Loop
    # Number of alternations: 2
    for i in range(2):
        
        # --- Step A: SRM Optimization (Scene Representation & Pose Refinement) ---
        # Inputs: Initial poses P_i, Depth priors D_i
        # Outputs: Refined poses P_hat_{i+1}, Refined depth D_hat_{i+1}
        
        # Warmup Learning: Set pose LR to low value initially
        # Linearly increase pose LR to 2.0e-3 after 1K iterations
        # NeRF LR: 1.0e-3, exponentially decays to 1.0e-4
        # Pose LR: 1.0e-5, linearly increases to 2.0e-3 after 1K, then decays to 1.0e-5
        
        for iteration in range(50000): # S_r = 50K
            # Sample rays based on current poses
            rays = sample_rays(video_frames, initial_poses)
            
            # Render NeRF
            rendered_colors, rendered_depths = srm.f_n.render(rays)
            
            # Compute Losses
            # L_c: Reconstruction loss (MSE between rendered and GT colors)
            loss_c = mse(rendered_colors, ground_truth_colors)
            
            # L_e: Error-tolerant depth regularization
            # Use Huber loss with tolerance epsilon
            # Weight by Softmin of multi-view consistency uncertainty
            uncertainty = compute_multi_view_uncertainty(rendered_depths, initial_poses)
            loss_e = huber_loss(rendered_depths, initial_depths, uncertainty)
            
            # Total SRM Loss
            loss_sr = loss_c + gamma * loss_e
            
            # Backpropagate and Update SRM (NeRF weights and Residual Pose Delta P)
            srm.update(loss_sr)
            
            # Update current poses for next iteration within SRM loop
            # P_hat = Delta_P * P_initial
            current_poses = srm.get_refined_poses()
            
        # End of SRM Optimization
        refined_poses = current_poses
        refined_depths = rendered_depths
        
        # --- Step B: SPM Fine-tuning (Scene Prior Update) ---
        # Inputs: Refined poses P_hat_{i+1}, Refined depth D_hat_{i+1}
        # Outputs: Updated Depth D_{i+1}, Updated Poses P_{i+1}
        
        # Replace reference depth D_r with rendered depth D_hat_{i+1} in Distilling Loss
        # Use Distilling Loss L_r and Photometric Loss L_p
        # Learning rate: 5.0e-5
        # Iterations: 500 (S_p)
        for iteration in range(500):
            # Render depth from SPM's current state or use refined depth as target
            # The paper states: "fine-tune SPM... using distillation loss Eq. (6) 
            # replacing reference depth Dr with rendered depth D_hat_{i+1}"
            
            # Predict depth from SPM
            spm_depths = [spm.f_d(frame) for frame in video_frames]
            
            # Compute Distilling Loss against refined depth
            loss_r = distilling_loss(spm_depths, refined_depths)
            
            # Compute Photometric Loss for consistency
            # Reconstruct target frames from source frames using SPM depth and pose
            # L_p = (1 - SSIM) + alpha/2 * (1 - alpha) * L1
            loss_p = photometric_loss(video_frames, spm_depths, refined_poses)
            
            # Total SPM Loss
            loss_pt = loss_p + loss_r + 1e-3 * smoothness_loss(spm_depths, video_frames)
            
            # Backpropagate and Update SPM (f_d and f_p weights)
            spm.update(loss_pt)
            
        # Update initial states for next alternation
        initial_poses = refined_poses
        initial_depths = refined_depths
        
    return srm.f_n, refined_poses, refined_depths
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Dynamic Objects:** SMDE is susceptible to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances. AltNeRF mitigates this via distilling strategy and multi-view consistency checks [S1].
*   **Imprecise Priors:** The depth prior from SPM is not precise. Strict alignment can mislead optimization. AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization (Huber loss with tolerance) to fit a possible depth range rather than exact values [S1].
*   **Non-Convex Optimization:** Joint optimization of pose and NeRF is highly non-convex. Poor initialization can lead to local minima. AltNeRF uses SPM priors for initialization and warmup learning for pose refinement to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Occlusions:** Multi-view consistency check accounts for occlusions by computing uncertainty and weighting losses with Softmin [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 150K-200K total iterations, costing 4.0-6.4 hours on a single RTX 3090 [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Resilient to imprecise camera poses; leverages self-supervised depth priors; error-tolerant regularization handles noisy priors [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the pre-trained SPM; alternating algorithm increases training complexity compared to single-stage optimization [S1].
*   **When to use:** Monocular video input without known camera poses; scenes with dynamic objects or view-dependent appearances where standard SMDE might fail [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When high-precision ground truth poses are available; when computational resources are extremely limited due to alternating nature [S1].

## Background
AltNeRF was proposed in 2024 by authors from the Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence [S1]. It builds upon Self-supervised Monocular Depth Estimation (SMDE) and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [S1]. The method addresses limitations of existing NeRF approaches that suffer from suboptimal outcomes due to lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise camera poses [S1]. It extends the BARF approach by introducing depth regularization, improved pose initialization, and warmup learning [S1].
