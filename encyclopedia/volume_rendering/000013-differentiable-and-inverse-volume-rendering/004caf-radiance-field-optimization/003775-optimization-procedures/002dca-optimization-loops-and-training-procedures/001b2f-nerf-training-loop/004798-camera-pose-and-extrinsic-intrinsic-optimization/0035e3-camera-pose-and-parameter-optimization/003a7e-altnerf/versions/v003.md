# AltNeRF

Also called: Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization

## Overview
AltNeRF is a framework for creating resilient Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations from monocular videos without relying on known camera poses [S1]. It solves the problem of suboptimal NeRF reconstruction caused by imprecise initial camera poses and lack of explicit 3D supervision by using an alternating algorithm to harmonize NeRF outputs with self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SMDE) priors [S1]. Unlike methods that strictly align rendered depth with priors, AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization because the depth priors themselves are not precise, allowing it to progressively refine both the scene geometry and camera poses [S1].

## How it works
AltNeRF comprises two components: the Scene Prior Module (SPM) and the Scene Representation Module (SRM) [S1]. The SPM estimates initial depth and pose priors, while the SRM refines these poses and learns the 3D scene representation, regularized by the depth priors [S1]. The refined outputs are fed back to the SPM to improve its performance in subsequent iterations [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A monocular video sequence $V$ consisting of frames $I_t$. Known camera intrinsics $K$ are required for the SMDE component [S1].
*   **Output:** A trained NeRF field $f_n$ (density $\sigma$ and color $c$) and refined camera poses $P_i$ for all frames [S1].

### Data Structures and Layouts

**Scene Prior Module (SPM)**
The SPM consists of two networks:
1.  **Depth Network ($f_d$):**
    *   **Encoder:** ResNet-50 with the fully-connected layer removed [S1].
    *   **Decoder:** Ten $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (two per scale) with bilinear up-sampling [S1].
    *   **Output:** Depth map $D$ [S1].
2.  **Pose Network ($f_p$):**
    *   **Architecture:** ResNet-34 [S1].
    *   **Output:** A vector of 9 elements. The first 6 elements represent a continuous rotation representation (axis-angle or similar Lie algebra element), and the last 3 elements represent the translation vector [S1].
    *   **Relative Pose:** Predicts relative transformation $P_{t \to s}$ between a target frame $I_t$ and a source frame $I_s$ [S1].

**Scene Representation Module (SRM)**
1.  **NeRF Network ($f_n$):**
    *   **Architecture:** Eight fully-connected layers with skip connections for density output, and one linear layer for color [S1].
    *   **Input:** 3D point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
    *   **Output:** Density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\gamma$: Scalar hyper-parameter balancing reconstruction and depth regularization losses. Value: 0.08 for LLFF and CO3D; 0.15 for ScanNet and Captures [S1].
*   $S_r$: Number of SRM iterations per alternation. Value: 50,000 [S1].
*   $S_p$: Number of SPM fine-tuning iterations per alternation. Value: 500 [S1].
*   Alternations: Number of full alternating cycles. Value: 2 [S1].
*   SPM Pretrain LR: Learning rate for pretraining SPM. Value: $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SPM Fine-tune LR: Learning rate for fine-tuning SPM. Value: $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   SRM NeRF LR: Initial learning rate for NeRF learning in SRM. Value: $10^{-3}$, exponentially decays to $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SRM Pose LR: Initial learning rate for pose refinement. Value: $10^{-5}$, linearly increases to $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ after 1,000 iterations, then exponentially decays to $10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   $\alpha$: Weight for L1 term in photometric loss. Value: 0.85 [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Tolerance coefficient for depth regularization. Value: Not given in the sources.

### Step-by-Step Method

**1. SPM Pretraining**
The SPM is pretrained on a large dataset (e.g., NYUv2, VOID) to accumulate prior knowledge for 3D recovery [S1]. It is built on SMDE but employs a distilling strategy to mitigate susceptibility to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances [S1].
*   **SMDE Objective:** The SMDE uses $f_d$ and $f_p$ to predict depth $D$ and relative pose $P_{t \to s}$ [S1]. The training objective is to reconstruct the target frame $I_t$ from nearby views $I_s$ by warping pixels $x_s$ from the source image to the target image $x_t$ based on predicted depth and camera pose: $x_s \sim K P_{t \to s} D(x_t) K^{-1} x_t$ [S1].
*   **Photometric Loss ($L_p$):**
    $$L_p(I_t, \hat{I}_t) = (1 - \text{SSIM}(I_t, \hat{I}_t)) + \frac{\alpha}{2} (1 - \alpha) \|I_t - \hat{I}_t\|_1$$
    where $\alpha = 0.85$ [S1].
*   **Edge-aware Smoothness Loss ($L_s$):**
    $$L_s = |\partial_x D| e^{-|\partial_x I|} + |\partial_y D| e^{-|\partial_y I|}$$
    where $\partial_x$ and $\partial_y$ are image gradients along horizontal and vertical axes [S1].
*   **Distilling Loss ($L_r$):**
    $$L_r = 1 - \text{SSIM}(D, D_r) + 0.1 \times (E_r \oplus E / \text{size}(E))$$
    where $D_r$ is a reference depth map from an off-the-shelf estimator (e.g., DPT), $\oplus$ denotes XOR operation, $\text{size}(\cdot)$ returns the size of a set, and $E_r, E$ are occluding boundary maps of $D_r$ and $D$ respectively [S1].
*   **Total SPM Loss:**
    $$L_{pt} = L_p + L_r + 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times L_s$$
    [S1].

**2. Pose Initialization**
*   Establish a world coordinate system aligning with the first frame $I_0$ (identity matrix) [S1].
*   Calculate initial camera poses $P_i$ using the chain rule: $P_i = P_{i-1 \to i} \times P_{i-1}$, where $P_{i-1 \to i}$ is the relative transformation predicted by SPM [S1].
*   Use the pose prior $P_0$ from SPM as initialization for SRM [S1].
*   Refine by optimizing a residual pose $\Delta P$. The refined pose $\hat{P}$ is calculated as:
    $$\hat{P} = \Delta P \times P$$
    [S1].

**3. Alternating Algorithm Workflow**
The framework alternates between SRM and SPM. Let cycle $i$ involve SRM $\Psi_i$ and SPM $\Phi_i$ [S1].
$$\Psi_i: (D_i, P_i) \to (\hat{D}_{i+1}, \hat{P}_{i+1})$$
$$\Phi_i: (\hat{D}_{i+1}, \hat{P}_{i+1}) \to (D_{i+1}, P_{i+1})$$
[ S1 ].

**3.1. SRM Iteration (Scene Representation Module)**
SRM runs for $S_r$ iterations [S1].
*   **NeRF Rendering:** Render color $\hat{C}(r)$ for ray $r = o + td$:
    $$\hat{C}(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(t) c(t) dt$$
    where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(s) ds)$ handles occlusions, and $t_n, t_f$ are near and far depth bounds [S1].
*   **Rendered Depth:** Compute mean terminating distance:
    $$\hat{D}(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(t) t dt$$
    [S1].
*   **Error-Tolerant Depth Regularization:** Instead of strict alignment, enforce that rendered depth fits a possible range defined by the prior $D$:
    $$L_e = H\left(\max\left(\frac{\|\hat{D} - D\|_1}{\hat{D} + D} - \epsilon, 0\right)\right)$$
    where $H(\cdot)$ is the Huber loss and $\epsilon$ is a tolerance coefficient [S1].
*   **SRM Loss:**
    $$L_{sr} = L_c + \gamma \cdot L_e$$
    where $L_c = \|\hat{C}(r) - C(r)\|_2$ is the reconstruction loss [S1].
*   **Warmup Learning:** Set the learning rate of $\Delta P$ to a low value initially, linearly increasing to the original rate after 1,000 iterations to allow SRM to learn a scene prototype before updating the pose [S1].
*   **Multi-view Consistency Check:** Compute uncertainty $U_t = \|D_t - D_{s \to t}\|_1$, where $D_{s \to t}$ are depth maps warped from nearby source views using poses from SPM or SRM [S1]. Incorporate this uncertainty into loss functions ($L_r$ and $L_e$) by weighting them with $\text{Softmin}(\cdot)$ to mitigate unreliable depth estimates [S1].
*   **Output:** Estimated scene depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ and improved camera pose $\hat{P}_{i+1}$ [S1].

**3.2. SPM Fine-tuning (Scene Prior Module)**
Fine-tune SPM for $S_p$ iterations [S1].
*   Use the distilling loss $L_r$ (Eq. 6 in source) replacing the reference depth $D_r$ with the rendered depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ from SRM [S1].
*   Supervise with the total loss $L_{pt}$ (Eq. 7 in source) [S1].
*   **Output:** Improved depth $D_{i+1}$ and pose $P_{i+1}$ [S1].

**4. Iteration**
Repeat steps 3.1 and 3.2 for the specified number of alternations (typically 2) [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def altnerf_train(video_frames, intrinsics_K, num_alternations=2, Sr=50000, Sp=500):
    """
    Trains AltNeRF using alternating optimization between SPM and SRM.
    
    Args:
        video_frames: List of image frames I_t.
        intrinsics_K: Camera intrinsic matrix K.
        num_alternations: Number of alternating cycles.
        Sr: Iterations for SRM per cycle.
        Sp: Iterations for SPM fine-tuning per cycle.
        
    Returns:
        trained_nerf: The final NeRF network f_n.
        refined_poses: List of refined camera poses P_i.
    """
    
    # 1. Pretrain SPM on large dataset (not shown in detail here)
    spm_depth_net = PretrainSPMDepthNet()
    spm_pose_net = PretrainSPMPoseNet()
    
    # 2. Initialize Poses using SPM
    # Set first frame pose to Identity
    poses = [IdentityMatrix()]
    
    # Predict relative poses and chain them
    for i in range(1, len(video_frames)):
        prev_frame = video_frames[i-1]
        curr_frame = video_frames[i]
        rel_pose = spm_pose_net(prev_frame, curr_frame) # Returns 9-element vector
        # Convert 9-element vector to SE(3) matrix
        rel_pose_mat = VectorToSE3(rel_pose)
        prev_pose = poses[-1]
        curr_pose = rel_pose_mat @ prev_pose
        poses.append(curr_pose)
        
    # Get initial depth priors from SPM
    depths = []
    for frame in video_frames:
        d = spm_depth_net(frame)
        depths.append(d)
        
    # Initialize SRM (NeRF)
    nerf = InitializeNeRF()
    
    # 3. Alternating Loop
    for i in range(num_alternations):
        # --- SRM Step ---
        # Warmup learning for pose refinement
        pose_lr_schedule = WarmupSchedule(initial_lr=1e-5, peak_lr=2e-3, warmup_iters=1000)
        
        # Optimize NeRF and Residual Pose Delta_P
        # Note: In implementation, Delta_P is optimized, and final pose is Delta_P @ P_i
        for step in range(Sr):
            # Sample rays from current poses
            rays = SampleRays(poses, intrinsics_K)
            
            # Render colors and depths
            sigma, color = nerf(rays.points, rays.directions)
            rendered_color = VolumeRenderColor(rays, sigma, color)
            rendered_depth = VolumeRenderDepth(rays, sigma)
            
            # Compute Losses
            # Reconstruction Loss
            gt_colors = GetGroundTruthColors(rays)
            L_c = SquaredError(rendered_color, gt_colors)
            
            # Depth Regularization (Error-Tolerant)
            # Use depth prior from previous step (or current if updated)
            depth_prior = depths[i] # Or depths from previous iteration if updated
            L_e = HuberLoss(ComputeRelativeDepthError(rendered_depth, depth_prior))
            
            # Multi-view consistency weighting (simplified)
            uncertainty = ComputeMultiViewUncertainty(rendered_depth, poses, intrinsics_K)
            weight = Softmin(uncertainty)
            L_e_weighted = weight * L_e
            
            # Total SRM Loss
            gamma = GetGamma(dataset_name)
            L_sr = L_c + gamma * L_e_weighted
            
            # Backpropagate to Nerf and Delta_P
            # Note: Delta_P is updated via gradient descent
            # Final pose for next ray sampling in this step would be (Delta_P @ P_i)
            # But typically Delta_P is accumulated or optimized directly
            UpdateParameters(nerf, Delta_P, L_sr)
            
            # Adjust Pose LR based on warmup
            current_pose_lr = pose_lr_schedule(step)
            
        # Output of SRM
        refined_poses_step = [Delta_P @ P for P in poses]
        refined_depths_step = [VolumeRenderDepth(SampleRays(P, K), ...) for P in refined_poses_step]
        
        # --- SPM Fine-tuning Step ---
        # Fine-tune SPM depth network using distillation loss
        # Replace reference depth D_r with refined_depths_step
        for step in range(Sp):
            for frame_idx in range(len(video_frames)):
                frame = video_frames[frame_idx]
                refined_depth = refined_depths_step[frame_idx]
                
                # Predict depth from SPM
                spm_depth = spm_depth_net(frame)
                
                # Distilling Loss L_r
                # L_r = 1 - SSIM(spm_depth, refined_depth) + 0.1 * XOR_Occlusion(spm_depth, refined_depth)
                L_r = DistillingLoss(spm_depth, refined_depth)
                
                # Photometric Loss L_p (using SMDE warping)
                # Predict relative pose for warping
                # Reconstruct target frame from source views using SPM depth and pose
                # L_p = (1 - SSIM) + alpha/2 * (1-alpha) * L1
                L_p = PhotometricLoss(frame, spm_depth, spm_pose_net)
                
                # Smoothness Loss L_s
                L_s = SmoothnessLoss(spm_depth, frame)
                
                L_pt = L_p + L_r + 1e-3 * L_s
                
                UpdateParameters(spm_depth_net, L_pt)
                
        # Update Poses and Depths for next iteration
        # The SPM fine-tuning produces new depth estimates D_{i+1}
        # And potentially refined relative poses which are chained to get P_{i+1}
        # For simplicity, we assume the refined_poses_step from SRM are the P_{i+1}
        # and the SPM fine-tuning improves the depth priors D_{i+1} used in next SRM step
        poses = refined_poses_step
        depths = refined_depths_step # Or re-estimate from SPM if needed
        
    return nerf, poses
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Non-Convexity:** Joint optimization of camera pose and NeRF representation is highly non-convex and prone to local minima [S1]. Poor initialization can lead to divergence [S1].
*   **Imprecise Priors:** The depth prior from SPM is not precise. Strict alignment can mislead NeRF optimization to degenerate solutions [S1]. AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Dynamic Objects:** SMDE is susceptible to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances [S1]. The distilling strategy in SPM helps mitigate this [S1].
*   **Warmup:** Without warmup learning for pose refinement, the asynchrony between NeRF learning (from scratch) and pose refinement (using prior) can result in incorrect update directions for the camera pose [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 150K-200K total iterations, costing approximately 4.0-6.4 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Resilient to imprecise camera poses and lack of explicit 3D supervision [S1].
    *   Alternating algorithm synergistically boosts SPM and SRM, tolerating imprecise priors [S1].
    *   Error-tolerant depth regularization prevents degenerate solutions [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires monocular video input [S1].
    *   Susceptible to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances without distillation [S1].
    *   Performance depends on the quality of the initial SPM priors [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera poses are unknown and only monocular video is available, and robustness to initial pose errors is required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When known camera poses are available, or when real-time inference is required (training time is significant) [S1].

## Background
AltNeRF was proposed in the paper "AltNeRF: Learning Robust Neural Radiance Field via Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization" presented at the Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1]. It builds upon the BARF (Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields) approach by introducing depth regularization, improved pose initialization, and warmup learning [S1]. The method addresses the challenges of existing NeRF approaches that encounter suboptimal outcomes due to the lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise camera poses [S1].
