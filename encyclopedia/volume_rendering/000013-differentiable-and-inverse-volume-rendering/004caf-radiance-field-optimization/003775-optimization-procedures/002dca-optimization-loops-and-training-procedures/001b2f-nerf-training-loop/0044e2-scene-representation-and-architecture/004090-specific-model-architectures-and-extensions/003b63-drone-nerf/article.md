# Drone-NeRF


## Overview
Drone-NeRF is a specialized volume rendering method for reconstructing large-scale 3D scenes from aerial drone imagery, addressing the challenges of unbounded space representation and varying camera exposure [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF training by integrating view frustum sampling for anti-aliasing, a pose optimizer for joint pose refinement, and appearance embeddings to handle exposure discrepancies [S1]. It builds on [Specific Model Architectures and Extensions](10278-specific-model-architectures-and-extensions.md) by modifying the input encoding and training loop to handle the specific geometric and photometric properties of drone-captured data [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ images with known camera poses (initially estimated, later refined), intrinsics, and extrinsics [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB images, depth maps, and optimized camera poses for the entire scene [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sub-bounding Boxes:** The scene is divided into a grid of sub-bounding boxes [S1].
*   **Neural Network Models:** Each box contains a trained neural network model [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** A learnable vector per image to account for exposure variations [S1].

**Network Topology:**
The method uses a fully connected ReLU MLP [S1]. The architecture consists of 8 hidden layers with 256 hidden units each, followed by a final stage of 2 layers with 128 channels [S1].
*   **Input Encoding:** Raw coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ are encoded using positional encoding [Parent].
    *   Position Encoding Highest Frequency: 64 [S1].
    *   Direction Encoding Highest Frequency: 4 [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** To resolve discrepancies in images arising due to variations in camera exposure, appearance embeddings are seamlessly integrated into the MLP [S1]. These embeddings are concatenated with the encoded position and direction inputs before being processed by the network [S1].
*   **Output:** The network produces RGB information (color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1]. The density $\sigma$ is typically derived from an intermediate layer or a separate head, consistent with the parent architecture [Parent].

**Rendering Integration:**
During training, rays are sampled along the camera frustum [S1]. The method employs hierarchical sampling:
*   **Coarse Samples:** 128 per ray [S1].
*   **Fine Samples:** 256 per ray [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays [S1].

The pixel color $C(\mathbf{r})$ is computed via volumetric rendering:
$$
C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad \text{where } \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i), \quad T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$
where $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [Parent].

**Training Procedure:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large-scale scene is divided into sub-bounding boxes [S1].
2.  **Block Parallel Training:** Each sub-block is trained independently [S1].
3.  **Initial Stage Optimization:** To facilitate rapid query scenarios where high-frequency information is not initially required, hash coding is leveraged in conjunction with a small fusion MLP [S1].
4.  **Pose Optimization:** Camera poses are optimized by back-propagating the loss gradient into the input pose computation [S1]. The learning rate for camera optimization is set to 0.0005 [S1].
5.  **Main Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.005 [S1].
6.  **Iteration Count:** Blocks undergo training for 5,000 and 30,000 iterations [S1]. Over-fitting is avoided by limiting iterations due to the model's rapid convergence [S1].

**Merging and Shadow Mitigation:**
After training individual sub-scenes, they are merged to render the complete scene:
1.  **Essential Box Selection:** The number of sub-bounding boxes required for merging is determined by calculating intersections between the rays of the rendering area and the sub-bounding boxes. Only essential sub-bounding boxes are used to reduce computation [S1].
2.  **Shadow Mitigation:** Shadows caused by the vertical faces of sub-bounding boxes are addressed by creating an expanded sub-bounding box larger than the original [S1].
    *   RGB values corresponding to projection areas generated by vertical surface ray irradiation from the smaller bounding box are captured using the enlarged box [S1].
    *   Shadow occlusion regions are replaced with these captured RGB values [S1].
3.  **Color Uniformization:** In overlapping areas of enlarged sub-bounding boxes, scene color is uniformized [S1].
4.  **Depth Merging:**
    *   Depths falling within the scope of the previous larger sub-bounding box are employed for merging [S1].
    *   In overlapping regions, depths pertaining to each sub-bounding box are averaged [S1].
    *   Depth value averages are compared across overlapping regions to adjust the overall depth for smoother continuity [S1].

import numpy as np

class AdamOptimizer:
    def __init__(self, params, lr=0.005, beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, eps=1e-8):
        self.params = params
        self.lr = lr
        self.beta1, self.beta2 = beta1, beta2
        self.eps = eps
        self.m = {p: np.zeros_like(p) for p in params}
        self.v = {p: np.zeros_like(p) for p in params}
        self.t = 0

    def step(self):
        self.t += 1
        for p in self.params:
            grad = p.grad
            self.m[p] = self.beta1 * self.m[p] + (1 - self.beta1) * grad
            self.v[p] = self.beta2 * self.v[p] + (1 - self.beta2) * (grad ** 2)
            m_hat = self.m[p] / (1 - self.beta1 ** self.t)
            v_hat = self.v[p] / (1 - self.beta2 ** self.t)
            p -= self.lr * m_hat / (np.sqrt(v_hat) + self.eps)

def positional_encoding(x, max_freq=64):
    freq_bands = 2.0 ** np.linspace(0, np.log2(max_freq), num=10)
    encoded = []
    for f in freq_bands:
        encoded.append(np.sin(f * np.pi * x))
        encoded.append(np.cos(f * np.pi * x))
    return np.concatenate(encoded, axis=-1)

def mlp_forward(mlp, x):
    for layer in mlp.layers[:-2]:
        x = np.maximum(0, x @ layer.weights + layer.bias)
    density = x @ mlp.layers[-2].weights + mlp.layers[-2].bias
    color = x @ mlp.layers[-1].weights + mlp.layers[-1].bias
    return density, color

def volumetric_render(density, color, deltas):
    alphas = 1.0 - np.exp(-density * deltas)
    transmittance = np.cumprod(1.0 - alphas, axis=-1)
    transmittance = np.concatenate([np.ones_like(transmittance[:, :1]), transmittance[:, :-1]], axis=-1)
    return np.sum(transmittance * alphas * color, axis=-1)

def compute_loss(rendered, ground_truth):
    return np.mean((rendered - ground_truth) ** 2)

def compute_gradients(loss, params):
    # Placeholder for automatic differentiation engine
    # In practice, this is handled by PyTorch/TensorFlow
    return [np.zeros_like(p) for p in params]

def compute_pose_gradients(loss, rays, poses):
    # Back-propagate loss gradient into input pose computation
    return np.zeros_like(poses)

def get_gt_color(rays, images):
    return images[rays.image_indices]

def sample_rays(images, poses, batch_size=1024):
    indices = np.random.choice(len(images), batch_size)
    return {'image_indices': indices, 'directions': poses[indices][:, :3]}

def sample_hierarchical(rays, num_samples, mlp, hash_grid=None, coarse_samples=None):
    # Hierarchical sampling: 128 coarse, 256 fine
    positions = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, num_samples)
    deltas = np.diff(np.concatenate([np.zeros(1), positions]))
    return {'positions': positions, 'distances': deltas}

def get_embeddings(images, rays, embeddings):
    return embeddings[rays.image_indices]

def concatenate(*arrays):
    return np.concatenate(arrays, axis=-1)

def partition_scene(scene_bounds):
    # Returns dict of sub-bounding boxes
    return {0: {'min': np.array([-10, -10, -10]), 'max': np.array([10, 10, 10])}}

def get_images_for_box(images, box, poses):
    return images

def get_poses_for_box(poses, box):
    return poses

def initialize_mlp(hidden_layers=8, hidden_units=256, final_layers=2, final_units=128):
    layers = []
    for _ in range(hidden_layers):
        layers.append(type('Layer', (), {'weights': np.random.randn(256, 256), 'bias': np.zeros(256)})())
    for _ in range(final_layers):
        layers.append(type('Layer', (), {'weights': np.random.randn(256, 128), 'bias': np.zeros(128)})())
    return type('MLP', (), {'layers': layers})()

def initialize_embeddings(num_images):
    return np.random.randn(num_images, 16)

def initialize_hash_grid():
    return {}

def update_global_poses(global_poses, local_poses):
    return local_poses

def get_essential_boxes(box, sub_boxes):
    return [box]

def expand_box(box, margin=0.1):
    return {'min': box['min'] - margin, 'max': box['max'] + margin}

def render_with_box(model, box, poses, intrinsics):
    return np.zeros((100, 100, 3))

def detect_shadow_regions(box, expanded_box):
    return np.ones((100, 100))

def replace_regions(main_rgb, shadow_rgb, shadow_mask):
    return shadow_rgb

def uniformize_color(rgb, neighbors, models):
    return rgb

def get_overlapping_neighbors(box, sub_boxes):
    return []

def average_depths(main_depth, expanded_depth, depth_mask):
    return (main_depth + expanded_depth) / 2.0

def adjust_depth_continuity(depth, neighbors, models):
    return depth

def drone_nerf_training(images, initial_poses, intrinsics, scene_bounds, num_iterations=30000):
    sub_boxes = partition_scene(scene_bounds)
    trained_models = {}
    optimized_poses = initial_poses.copy()

    for box_id, box in sub_boxes.items():
        relevant_images = get_images_for_box(images, box, initial_poses)
        relevant_poses = get_poses_for_box(initial_poses, box)

        mlp = initialize_mlp(hidden_layers=8, hidden_units=256, final_layers=2, final_units=128)
        appearance_embeddings = initialize_embeddings(len(relevant_images))
        hash_grid = initialize_hash_grid()

        adam_optimizer = AdamOptimizer(params=[p for layer in mlp.layers for p in [layer.weights, layer.bias]], lr=0.005)
        pose_optimizer = AdamOptimizer(params=[optimized_poses], lr=0.0005)

        for iteration in range(num_iterations):
            rays = sample_rays(relevant_images, relevant_poses, batch_size=1024)
            coarse_samples = sample_hierarchical(rays, num_samples=128, mlp=mlp, hash_grid=hash_grid)
            fine_samples = sample_hierarchical(rays, num_samples=256, mlp=mlp, coarse_samples=coarse_samples)

            encoded_positions = positional_encoding(coarse_samples['positions'], max_freq=64)
            encoded_directions = positional_encoding(coarse_samples['directions'], max_freq=4)
            ray_embeddings = get_embeddings(relevant_images, rays, appearance_embeddings)

            inputs = concatenate(encoded_positions, encoded_directions, ray_embeddings)
            density, color = mlp_forward(mlp, inputs)

            rendered_color = volumetric_render(density, color, coarse_samples['distances'])
            gt_color = get_gt_color(rays, relevant_images)
            loss = compute_loss(rendered_color, gt_color)

            mlp_grads = compute_gradients(loss, [p for layer in mlp.layers for p in [layer.weights, layer.bias]])
            adam_optimizer.step()

            emb_grads = compute_gradients(loss, [appearance_embeddings])
            adam_optimizer.step()

            pose_grads = compute_pose_gradients(loss, rays, optimized_poses)
            pose_optimizer.step()

            if iteration < 1000:
                update_hash_grid(hash_grid, coarse_samples)

        trained_models[box_id] = {'mlp': mlp, 'appearance_embeddings': appearance_embeddings, 'hash_grid': hash_grid}
        optimized_poses = update_global_poses(optimized_poses, relevant_poses)

    return trained_models, optimized_poses

def merge_sub_boxes(trained_models, sub_boxes, optimized_poses, intrinsics):
    final_scene = {}
    for box_id, box in sub_boxes.items():
        model = trained_models[box_id]
        essential_boxes = get_essential_boxes(box, sub_boxes)
        expanded_box = expand_box(box, margin=0.1)
        shadow_rgb = render_with_box(model, expanded_box, optimized_poses, intrinsics)
        main_rgb = render_with_box(model, box, optimized_poses, intrinsics)
        shadow_mask = detect_shadow_regions(box, expanded_box)
        merged_rgb = replace_regions(main_rgb, shadow_rgb, shadow_mask)
        neighbors = get_overlapping_neighbors(box, sub_boxes)
        merged_rgb = uniformize_color(merged_rgb, neighbors, trained_models)
        main_depth = render_with_box(model, box, optimized_poses, intrinsics)
        expanded_depth = render_with_box(model, expanded_box, optimized_poses, intrinsics)
        depth_mask = get_overlap_mask(box, expanded_box)
        merged_depth = average_depths(main_depth, expanded_depth, depth_mask)
        merged_depth = adjust_depth_continuity(merged_depth, neighbors, trained_models)
        final_scene[box_id] = {'rgb': merged_rgb, 'depth': merged_depth}
    return final_scene

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is performed in parallel for each sub-block, reducing total wall-clock time. The use of hash coding in the initial stage facilitates rapid queries [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of sub-bounding boxes and the MLP size. The MLP has 8 layers of 256 units and 2 layers of 128 units [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on the Mill 19 dataset (Building and Rubble scenes) [S1].
    *   **Hardware:** Ubuntu 20.04, Nvidia 4070ti GPU (12GB VRAM), Intel i5-13600KF CPU, 32GB RAM [S1].
    *   **Building Scene (5,000 iterations):**
        *   Drone-NeRF Average PSNR: 17.401 [S1].
        *   MipNeRF Average PSNR: 16.637 [S1].
        *   Instant-NGP Average PSNR: 17.179 [S1].
    *   Drone-NeRF outperforms MipNeRF and Instant-NGP in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics for both 5,000 and 30,000 iterations on the Building Scene [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates shadow artifacts from vertical faces, and corrects exposure discrepancies using appearance embeddings [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful partitioning of the scene into sub-bounding boxes. Over-fitting can occur if iterations are too high, necessitating careful tuning of iteration counts (5,000 or 30,000) [S1].

## Background
Drone-NeRF was introduced to address the specific challenges of 3D reconstruction from aerial drone imagery, such as large-scale unbounded scenes and varying camera exposure [S1]. It builds upon the NeRF framework [Parent] by incorporating techniques for efficient large-scale rendering and photometric consistency [S1]. The method was evaluated on the Mill 19 dataset, demonstrating superior performance compared to MipNeRF and Instant-NGP [S1].
