# Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction

Also called: Hybrid Scene Representation Network, Neural RGB-D

## Overview
Neural RGB-D is a method for high-quality 3D reconstruction of room-scale scenes from an RGB-D input sequence [S1]. It solves the problem of generating metric, clean triangle meshes from consumer-grade depth sensors by training a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to represent a hybrid scene consisting of a truncated signed distance field (TSDF) for geometry and a volumetric radiance field for appearance [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which uses a density-based volumetric representation that can introduce semitransparent artifacts, this method uses an implicit surface representation (TSDF) leading to high-quality geometry estimates with hard boundaries between occupied and free space [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** $N$ color frames $I_i$, corresponding aligned depth frames $D_i$, and initial camera poses $T_i$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized network parameters $\Theta$, camera poses $T_i$, per-frame latent codes, and a mesh extracted via Marching Cubes.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Shape MLP:** Input is the sinusoidal positional encoding of a 3D point $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$. Output is the truncated signed distance $D_i$.
*   **Radiance MLP:** Inputs are the sinusoidal positional encoding of the view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, an intermediate feature vector from the Shape MLP, and a per-frame latent corrective code vector. Output is the surface radiance $\mathbf{c}_i$.
*   **Deformation Field:** A 6-layer ReLU MLP acting as a residual 2D vector to pixel locations before unprojection.
*   **Camera Poses:** Represented with Euler angles and a translation vector for every frame.

**Parameters:**
*   Truncation distance $tr = 5$ cm [S1].
*   Loss weights: $\lambda_1 = 0.1$ (RGB), $\lambda_2 = 10$ (Free-space), $\lambda_3 = 6 \times 10^3$ (Truncation) [S1].
*   Fine samples $S'_f = 16$ [S1].
*   Coarse sampling rate: One sample for every 1.5 cm of ray length on average [S1].
*   Ray length: Must be greater than the largest distance in the scene (typical range 4 to 8 meters) [S1].
*   Optimizer: ADAM with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Training iterations: $2 \times 10^5$ iterations, with $|P_b| = 1024$ randomly chosen rays per iteration [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize camera poses $T_i$ using BundleFusion [S1].
2.  **Ray Generation & Deformation:** For a pixel in frame $i$, retrieve its 2D location. Shift this location by the 2D vector output from the deformation field MLP. Transform the shifted ray into world space using the camera pose $T_i$ [S1].
3.  **Coarse Sampling:** Generate $S'_c$ sample points on the ray using stratified sampling. The sampling density ensures an average of one sample per 1.5 cm of ray length [S1].
4.  **Coarse Evaluation:** Evaluate the Shape MLP on the $S'_c$ points to obtain signed distances. Search for the zero-crossing (where signed distance changes sign) to estimate the ray depth [S1].
5.  **Fine Sampling:** Generate $S'_f$ sample points around the identified zero-crossing [S1].
6.  **Fine Evaluation:** Evaluate both the Shape MLP and Radiance MLP on the $S'_f$ points. Concatenate the outputs from the coarse step ($S'_c$) and fine step ($S'_f$) to form the full set of $K = S'_c + S'_f$ samples [S1].
7.  **Weight Calculation:** For each sample $i$ with signed distance $D_i$, calculate the weight $w_i$ using the product of two sigmoid functions:
    $$ w_i = \sigma \left( \frac{D_i}{tr} \right) \cdot \sigma \left( -\frac{D_i}{tr} \right) $$
    where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function and $tr$ is the truncation distance [S1].
    *   *Edge Case:* If multiple intersections occur, set weights of samples beyond the first truncation region to zero [S1].
8.  **Color Integration:** Compute the final pixel color $\mathbf{C}$ as a weighted sum:
    $$ \mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i} \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the radiance output from the Radiance MLP for sample $i$ [S1].
9.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P})$ for the batch, where $\mathcal{P}$ includes network parameters $\Theta$ and camera poses $T_i$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{b=0}^{B-1} \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P}) $$
    *   **RGB Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} (\mathbf{C}_p - \hat{\mathbf{C}}_p)^2$, where $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_p$ is the observed color [S1].
    *   **Free-Space Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p^{fs}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p^{fs}} (D_s - tr)^2$. This forces samples $s$ between the camera origin and the truncation region to predict a signed distance of $tr$ [S1].
    *   **Truncation Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|S_p^{tr}|} \sum_{s \in S_p^{tr}} (D_s - \hat{D}_s)^2$. This constrains samples $s$ within the truncation region to match the observed depth $\hat{D}_s$ from the sensor [S1].
10. **Optimization:** Update $\Theta$, $T_i$, latent codes, and deformation field parameters using gradients of $\mathcal{L}$ [S1].
11. **Mesh Extraction:** At evaluation, use Marching Cubes with a spatial resolution of 1 cm to extract a triangle mesh from the optimized signed distance function [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   If the coarse sampling rate $S'_c$ is too low, samples may miss the truncation region entirely, causing the zero-crossing search to fail [S1].
*   The scene must be scaled such that the truncation region maps to $[-1, 1]$ (positive in front of the surface, negative behind) to ensure consistent weight calculation [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Determined by the number of MLP forward passes per iteration. Each ray requires two forward passes (coarse and fine) for $S'_c + S'_f$ samples. With $2 \times 10^5$ iterations and 1024 rays per iteration, the total compute is significant but feasible for room-scale scenes [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Dominated by the MLP weights and the storage of per-frame latent codes and camera poses. The deformation field is shared across frames [S1].
*   **Performance:** On 10 synthetic scenes, the method achieved a Chamfer $\ell_1$ distance of 0.044, IoU of 0.747, Normal Consistency of 0.918, and F-score of 0.924 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces high-quality, metric 3D reconstructions with hard boundaries, avoiding the noisy artifacts and semitransparent matter common in density-based NeRF models [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires RGB-D input (depth sensors). Performance may degrade with very noisy depth data or thin structures missed by the sensor, though the color loss helps compensate [S1].

## Background
Neural RGB-D was proposed by Dejan Azinovic, Ricardo Martin-Brualla, Dan B Goldman, Matthias Nießner, and Justus Thies [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2022 [S1]. The method builds on the neural radiance field (NeRF) framework [48] but replaces the density-based volume rendering with a differentiable formulation based on signed distance fields (SDF) [S1]. It is designed specifically for geometry reconstruction from commodity RGB-D sensors like the Kinect, addressing the limitations of prior NeRF variants that focused primarily on novel view synthesis rather than metric mesh generation [S1].
