# BlockNeRF

Also called: Block-based NeRF, Divided Scene NeRF

## Overview
BlockNeRF is a method for scalable neural view synthesis in large-scale urban environments that decomposes a complex scene into spatial blocks, training a separate NeRF model for each block [S2]. It solves the problem of high-frequency information loss, slow training/inference, and inability to capture main subjects in large scenes by decoupling rendering time from scene size [S2]. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF by using a "total-part-total" approach to enable seamless integration of adjacent NeRFs [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Large-scale scene data (e.g., satellite and ground images with varying scales), spatial partitioning scheme.
*   **Outputs:** A set of MLP networks, one per block, aligned for seamless rendering.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Blocks:** The scene is divided into a set of regions (blocks) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **NeRF Models:** Each region is represented by an independent MLP network [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Decomposition:** Divide the large scene into a set of spatial regions (blocks) [S1, unofficial][S2].
2.  **Local Training:** Train a separate NeRF model (MLP) for each block independently [S1, unofficial][S2].
3.  **Alignment:** Design a program to align the appearance between adjacent NeRFs to ensure seamless integration [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Artifacts:** Misalignment between adjacent NeRFs can cause visible seams. The method explicitly addresses this with an alignment program [S2].
*   **Scale Variation:** Large scenes may contain multi-scale data (e.g., satellite vs. ground level). The method aims to handle this by decomposing the scene [S2].

## Subtopics

### MegaNeRF
Also divides large scenes into regions with MLPs, but focuses on virtual fly-throughs [S1, unofficial].

### Grid-NeRF
Uses planar grid features with small MLPs for large urban scenes, addressing issues with plane-based methods [S1, unofficial].

### CityNeRF
Uses a progressive learning paradigm, growing the NeRF model and training set synchronously [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Scalability:** Decouples rendering time from scene size, allowing rendering to scale to arbitrarily large environments [S2].
*   **Performance:** Solves the issue of slow training and inference processes in large scenes [S2].
*   **Quality:** Addresses high-frequency information loss in large-scale city buildings and natural environments [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires complex alignment procedures to ensure seamless integration between blocks [S2].

## Background
BlockNeRF was proposed by Tancik et al. in 2022 to solve the complex rendering problems of large-scale city buildings [S2]. It was introduced as an improvement over standard NeRF, which struggles with large-scale scenes due to high-frequency information loss and slow processing [S2]. The method is part of the broader effort to scale NeRF for urban environments [S2].
