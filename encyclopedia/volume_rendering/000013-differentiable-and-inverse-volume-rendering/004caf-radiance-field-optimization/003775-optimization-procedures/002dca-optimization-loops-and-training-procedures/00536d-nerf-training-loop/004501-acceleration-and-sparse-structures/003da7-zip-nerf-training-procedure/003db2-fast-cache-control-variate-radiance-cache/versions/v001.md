# Fast Cache (Control Variate Radiance Cache)
Also called: Fast Cache architecture, Flash Cache fast cache

## Overview
Fast Cache is a computationally efficient approximation of the incoming radiance used in radiance caching for inverse rendering, designed to replace or augment the expensive NeRF-based cache. It solves the bottleneck of evaluating high-fidelity neural radiance fields for every importance sampling direction by using a lightweight, low-resolution hash grid and a small MLP to predict incident radiance. It sets itself apart from the standard NeRF-based cache by using a fixed, small number of samples (8) and direct distance prediction, while maintaining accuracy by serving as a control variate to correct the bias of the NeRF cache.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Ray origin $\mathbf{x}$, outgoing direction $\omega_o$, incoming direction $\omega_i$ (for cache evaluation), material parameters, and scene geometry (implicit via density field).
- **Output:** Estimated outgoing radiance $L_o$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Low-resolution NGP Grid:** A multi-resolution hash grid (Neural Graphics Primitive) storing features $\mathbf{f}_k$ for sample points. Lower resolution than the main Zip-NeRF grid.
2.  **Sampling MLP ($g_{\text{sample}}$):** A small MLP that takes ray origin and direction, outputting sample distances $t'_k$ and unnormalized weights $w'_k$.
3.  **Color MLP ($\text{MLP}_{\text{color}}$):** A small MLP that takes the aggregated ray feature vector and outputs incident radiance $\hat{L}_i^{\text{fast}}$.
4.  **Environment MLP ($\text{MLP}_{\text{env}}$):** A small MLP taking direction $\omega_i$ to output far-field illumination.

**Parameters:**
-   $S$: Number of samples for the fast cache ray integration. Value: $S=8$ [S1].
-   $M'$: Number of directions sampled for the fast cache estimate.
-   $M$: Number of additional directions sampled for the error correction term ($M \ll M'$) [S1].
-   $p(\omega)$: Importance sampling distribution for incoming directions [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Fast Cache Ray Integration:**
    For a given ray origin $\mathbf{x}$ and direction $\omega_i$:
    a.  Compute sample distances and weights using the sampling function:
        $$ \{t'_k, w'_k\}_{k=1}^{S} = g_{\text{sample}}(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i) $$
    b.  Compute sample positions:
        $$ \mathbf{x}'_k = \mathbf{x} + t'_k \omega_i $$
    c.  Query features from the low-resolution NGP grid at each $\mathbf{x}'_k$ to get $\mathbf{f}_k$.
    d.  Normalize weights using softmax:
        $$ \tilde{w}'_k = \frac{\exp(w'_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^{S} \exp(w'_j)} $$
    e.  Compute the ray feature vector:
        $$ \mathbf{F}_{\text{ray}} = \sum_{k=1}^{S} \tilde{w}'_k \mathbf{f}_k $$
    f.  Decode incident radiance:
        $$ \hat{L}_i^{\text{fast}}(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i) = \text{MLP}_{\text{color}}(\mathbf{F}_{\text{ray}}) $$

2.  **Control Variate Estimation:**
    To estimate outgoing radiance $L_o$, sample $M'$ directions $\{\omega'_i\}_{j=1}^{M'}$ and $M$ additional independent directions $\{\omega_i\}_{j=1}^{M}$ from the importance sampling distribution $p(\omega)$ [S1].
    a.  Evaluate the fast cache $\hat{L}_i^{\text{fast}}$ for all $M' + M$ directions.
    b.  Evaluate the accurate NeRF-based cache $\hat{L}_i^{\text{NeRF}}$ for the $M$ additional directions only.
    c.  Compute the fast outgoing radiance estimate:
        $$ \hat{L}_o^{\text{fast}} = \frac{1}{M'} \sum_{j=1}^{M'} f(\mathbf{x}, \omega'_i, \omega_o) \hat{L}_i^{\text{fast}}(\mathbf{x}, \omega'_i) \frac{(\mathbf{n} \cdot \omega'_i)}{p(\omega'_i)} $$
    d.  Compute the delta (error) outgoing radiance:
        $$ \Delta \hat{L}_o = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} f(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i, \omega_o) \left( \hat{L}_i^{\text{NeRF}}(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i) - \hat{L}_i^{\text{fast}}(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i) \right) \frac{(\mathbf{n} \cdot \omega_i)}{p(\omega_i)} $$
    e.  Combine estimates:
        $$ \hat{L}_o = \hat{L}_o^{\text{fast}} + \Delta \hat{L}_o $$

3.  **Far-Field Illumination:**
    Add environment lighting to the cache radiance. Compute transparency $T = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{S} w_k$ (where $w_k$ are the final normalized weights from the NeRF-based cache or equivalent integration) [S1].
    $$ L_{\text{env}} = \text{MLP}_{\text{env}}(\omega_i) \cdot T $$
    Add $L_{\text{env}}$ to the cache radiance contribution [S1].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** Requires $M$ evaluations of the expensive NeRF-based cache and $M + M'$ evaluations of the cheap fast cache. This achieves the quality of $M'$ rays from the accurate cache with a fraction of the computation [S1].
-   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a low-resolution NGP grid and small MLPs, significantly less memory than the full Zip-NeRF grid [S1].
-   **Strengths:** High efficiency due to low sample count ($S=8$) and lightweight network [S1]. Unbiased estimator when combined with the NeRF cache [S1].
-   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the fast cache; if the fast cache is inaccurate, the control variate correction may not fully compensate. Requires careful tuning of $M$ and $M'$ [S1].
-   **When to use:** When real-time or near-real-time inverse rendering is required, and the full NeRF-based cache is too slow [S1].

## Background
Fast Cache is part of the Flash Cache method, introduced in "Flash Cache: Reducing Bias in Radiance Cache Based Inverse Rendering" [S1]. It builds on the Zip-NeRF architecture [S1] and Ref-NeRF [S1]. The method addresses the bias and computational cost of radiance caching in inverse rendering by using a control variate approach [S1].
