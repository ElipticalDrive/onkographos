# Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance

Also called: Depth-guided NeRF via Earth Mover’s Distance, EM D-Guided Sampling, EMD-Guided Depth Supervision for NeRF, EMD-NeRF, EMD-based objective

## Overview
This method improves the geometric understanding of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in sparse-view settings by supervising the distribution of ray termination distances using Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) against monocular depth priors, rather than enforcing direct depth matching [S1]. It differs from siblings like [Monocular Depth Regularization on Seen Views](12330-monocular-depth-regularization-on-seen-views.md) and [NeRF-guided MDE Adaptation](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md) by utilizing a diffusion-based depth prior (DiffDP) to generate uncertainty maps, which dynamically weight the EMD loss to prevent the NeRF from learning incorrect geometry from noisy priors [S1]. Unlike [DäRF](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md) which minimizes the norm between rendered and prior depth, this approach guides the probability distribution of ray terminations, avoiding the collapse of multimodal distributions and reducing interference with photometric learning [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of $N$ training images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^N$ with known camera poses (position $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ and viewing direction $(\theta_i, \phi_i)$) [S1].
*   A pre-trained monocular depth estimation network, specifically DiffDP [11], used to generate depth priors and uncertainty maps [S1].
*   NeRF hyperparameters: number of coarse samples ($N_c=64$), fine samples ($N_f=128$), and additional EMD samples ($N_{em}=128$) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A trained NeRF network $F_\Theta$ that maps 3D coordinates and viewing directions to color $c$ and volume density $\sigma$ [S1].
*   Rendered novel views with improved geometric consistency [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **NeRF Network $F_\Theta$:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) taking 5D input $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)$ and outputting color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
*   **Depth Prior $z_0$:** A 2D tensor of the same resolution as the input image, representing the estimated depth map from DiffDP [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Map $u$:** A 2D tensor of the same resolution as the input image, with values normalized to $[0, 1]$, representing the reliability of the depth prior at each pixel [S1].
*   **Ray Samples:** For each ray, a list of termination distances $y = \{y_1, ..., y_N\}$ sampled from the NeRF's predicted distribution [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Depth Prior and Uncertainty Extraction:**
    For each training image $I$, use the DiffDP network to generate a depth prior $z_0$ and an uncertainty map $u$. DiffDP formulates denoising as $p_\theta(z_{0:T}|I)$. The uncertainty is derived by comparing estimates at consecutive denoising steps $t$ and $t-1$.
    *   Calculate step-wise count $c(z_{0:T})$:
        $$ c(z_{0:T}) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=T,...,1} \mathbb{I}(|z_t - z_{t-1}| \ge \tau) $$
        where $\tau$ is a threshold [S1].
    *   Compute spatial consistency using mirrored image $M(I)$:
        $$ U(z_{0:T}|I) = \frac{c(z_{0:T}|I) + M(c(z_{0:T}|M(I)))}{2} $$
    *   Augment with pixel-wise difference between original and mirrored predictions:
        $$ u(z_{0:T}|I) = U(z_{0:T}|I) \cdot \|z_0|I - M(z_0|M(I))\|_1 $$
    *   Normalize $u$ to $[0, 1]$ [S1].

2.  **NeRF Forward Pass and Weight Calculation:**
    For a ray $r$ with samples at distances $y_l$ (where $l=1...L$):
    *   Query $F_\Theta$ to get density $\sigma_l$ and color $c_l$ for each sample [S1].
    *   Calculate the distance between adjacent samples $\delta_l = y_{l+1} - y_l$ (with $\delta_L$ being the distance to the far plane) [S1].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_l$:
        $$ T_l = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{l-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
        with $T_1 = 1$ [S1].
    *   Compute sample weights $w_l$ (probability of ray traversal):
        $$ w_l = T_l (1 - \exp(-\sigma_l \delta_l)) $$
    *   Normalize weights to form a piece-wise constant probability density function:
        $$ \hat{w}_l = \frac{w_l}{\sum_{j=1}^L w_j} $$
    *   Construct the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) from $\hat{w}_l$ [S1].

3.  **EMD-Guided Sampling:**
    *   Sample $N_{em}$ new ray termination distances $y = \{y_1, ..., y_{N_{em}}\}$ from the NeRF's CDF using inverse transform sampling [S1].
    *   These samples represent the NeRF's predicted distribution of surface locations [S1].

4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Photometric Loss:** Standard L2 loss between rendered color $\hat{C}(r)$ and observed color $C(r)$:
        $$ L_{photo} = \|\hat{C}(r) - C(r)\|_2^2 $$
        where $\hat{C}(r) = \sum_{l=1}^L w_l c_l$ [S1].
    *   **EMD Loss:** Compute the Earth Mover’s Distance between the sampled NeRF distances $y$ and the depth prior $z_0$. Since EMD is non-differentiable, approximate it using Sinkhorn Divergence [S1]:
        $$ L_{EMD} = \text{EMD}(y, z_0) \approx \text{Sinkhorn}(y, z_0) $$
    *   **Total Loss:** Combine losses weighted by uncertainty $u$ and balancing weight $\lambda$. The exponent $\gamma$ controls the impact of uncertainty (typically $\gamma=1$ or similar, though the source formula uses $\gamma$ explicitly):
        $$ L = (1 + u)^\gamma L_{photo} + \lambda (1 - u)^\gamma L_{EMD} $$
        Note: The source formula in Eq. 8 is $L = (1 + u)^\gamma L_{photo} + \lambda (1 - u)^\gamma L_{EMD}$. The text states $\lambda$ is a balancing weight. 5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Update NeRF parameters $\Theta$ using gradient descent.
    *   The depth prior $z_0$ and uncertainty $u$ are fixed during NeRF training (stop-gradient on the prior network) [S1].
    *   Initialize the scale of the depth priors to 1 and learn the scale with a small learning rate [S1].

    # So we shift the cumsum result.
    cum_sigma_delta = torch.cumsum(sigma_delta, dim=0)

def sinkhorn_divergence(x, y, epsilon=0.01, num_iterations=10):
    """
    Approximates EMD using Sinkhorn Divergence.
    x: Tensor of shape (N,) NeRF samples
    y: Tensor of shape (M,) Depth prior samples (or flattened depth map)

    # Here we assume a simple implementation for clarity.
    # x: (N,), y: (M,) -> C: (N, M)
    dist_matrix = torch.cdist(x.unsqueeze(1), y.unsqueeze(1)).squeeze()
    
    # Sinkhorn algorithm
    # Initialize potentials
    a = torch.ones(x.shape[0], device=x.device) / x.shape[0]
    b = torch.ones(y.shape[0], device=y.device) / y.shape[0]
    
    K = torch.exp(-dist_matrix / epsilon)
    
    for _ in range(num_iterations):
        a = a / (K @ b)
        b = b / (K.T @ a)
        
    # Optimal transport plan
    P = torch.diag(a) @ K @ torch.diag(b)
    
    # EMD approximation
    emd = torch.sum(P * dist_matrix)
    return emd

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Photometric Quality vs. Geometry:** High PSNR/SSIM does not guarantee correct geometry. NeRFs can render plausible images while misinterpreting structure (e.g., misinterpreting a cabinet as a pattern on the floor) [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Handling:** Unlike Focal Loss which increases weight for uncertain examples, this method *decreases* the weight of the depth loss for uncertain pixels to rely more on photometric loss [S1].
*   **Prior Noise:** Using multiple depth hypotheses (like SCADE) can introduce noise and training instability. This method uses a single prior, reducing this risk [S1].
*   **Scale Ambiguity:** Monocular depth priors are scale-ambiguous. The method initializes the scale of the depth priors to 1 and learns the scale during training [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Map Sensitivity:** The construction of the uncertainty map can be sensitive to hyperparameters (e.g., threshold $\tau$) [S1].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is increased compared to standard NeRF due to the additional forward pass through the DiffDP network (once per image to generate priors) and the computation of Sinkhorn Divergence for each ray batch. Sinkhorn Divergence is iterative, adding overhead to the loss computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the depth prior and uncertainty maps for all training images. The NeRF model size is standard. The additional sampling for EMD increases memory usage for ray samples [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On ScanNet+, the method reduces baseline RMSE by at least 56% while maintaining comparable photometric results (PSNR/SSIM) [S1]. With DiffDP prior, EMD objective reduces initial prior RMSE by 31.4%; with DepthAnything prior, it reduces it by 18.8% [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for sparse-view settings ($N < 20$). It scales to larger datasets but the benefit of depth supervision is most critical when views are sparse [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to noisy depth priors due to uncertainty weighting. Improves geometric accuracy significantly without degrading photometric quality. Avoids the collapse of multimodal distributions by supervising the distribution rather than the mean [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the monocular depth prior. The uncertainty measure is specific to diffusion-based models (DiffDP) and may not be easily transferable to other MDE networks without modification [S1]. Training can be slower due to Sinkhorn computation [S1].

## Background

*   **Origin:** The method was proposed by A. Rau et al. in the paper "Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance" [S1].
*   **History:** It builds upon the observation that NeRFs often fail to learn accurate geometry in sparse-view settings, despite good photometric reconstruction [S1]. Previous works like DäRF supervised rendered depth directly with L2 loss, and SCADE used multiple depth hypotheses with a space-carving loss. This method introduces EMD to compare distributions and uses diffusion-based uncertainty to weight the supervision [S1].
*   **Usage:** It is used as a drop-in replacement for existing depth-supervised NeRFs, particularly on challenging indoor scenes like ScanNet and ScanNet+ [S1].
