# Surrogate Objective-based Trajectory Planning

Also called: Ensemble Objective E, SOARNet, Surrogate Objectives for Active Radiance Fields Network, Trajectory Planning with SOARNet

## Overview
Surrogate Objective-based Trajectory Planning is a method for selecting informative camera poses for training radiance fields (such as NeRF) without requiring the training of a radiance field model or access to ground-truth meshes during inference [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficient active view planning by using a deep neural network, SOARNet, to predict surrogate objective scores (Surface Coverage, Geometric Complexity, Textural Complexity, Ray Diversity) for candidate poses given monocular images and camera parameters [S1, unofficial]. Unlike in-flight or post-hoc methods that rely on uncertainty estimation from a trained model, this approach is prescriptive and model-agnostic, enabling trajectory generation in seconds rather than hours [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by decoupling view selection from radiance field optimization, allowing for rapid, training-free planning [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of monocular 2D images $\hat{M} = \{m_k : k \le n\}$ taken from visited camera poses $\hat{C} = \{\hat{c}_k : k \le n\}$, the camera parameters (extrinsics) for each image, and a set of candidate unvisited poses $C^-$. The method does not require the image of the unvisited pose or the ground-truth mesh [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A sequence of selected camera poses $\hat{C}$ up to a budget $B$, and the predicted surrogate objective scores for visited and candidate poses [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **SOARNet Architecture:**
    *   **Encoder $\mu(\cdot)$:** A ResNet-18 encoder processing the input images $\hat{M}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Camera Extrinsics Encoder $o(\cdot)$:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) taking positionally encoded camera extrinsics [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Prediction Heads $G(\cdot)$ and $H(\cdot)$:** Simple MLPs for generating objective scores via regression [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Output Format:** The network outputs a set of scores $F_g$ for $g \in \{n, n+1\}$, where $F_g = \{f_{Cg}, f_{Qg}, f_{Dg}, f_{Tg}\}$ [S1, unofficial]. $F_n$ contains normalized cumulative scores of all poses seen so far. $F_{n+1}$ returns normalized cumulative scores for an unseen camera pose $c_{n+1}$ conditioned upon visited poses [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The surrogate objectives are defined as follows [S1, unofficial]:

1.  **Surface Coverage ($f_C$):** Measures the ratio of seen faces to total faces.
    $$ f_C = \frac{1}{J} \sum_{j}^{J} \sum_{k}^{n} U(V_j^k) $$
    Where $J$ is the total number of faces, $n$ is the number of visited poses, $U(\cdot)$ is the Heaviside step function, and $V_j^k$ is an indicator of face $j$ visibility from pose $k$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Geometric Complexity ($f_Q$):** Quantifies variability in mesh surface normals [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Textural Complexity ($f_T$):** Measures photometric variation [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Ray Diversity ($f_D$):** Measures the diversity of viewing rays [S1, unofficial].

The SOARNet prediction is formalized as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \text{SOARNet}(\hat{C}, \hat{M}, c_{n+1}) = \{F_g : g \in \{n, n+1\}\} $$

The training loss $L$ for SOARNet is the sum of Huber losses on the predictions of each head [S1, unofficial]:
$$ L = L_G(\hat{F}_n, F_n) + L_H(\hat{F}_{n+1}, F_{n+1}) $$
Where $L_G$ and $L_H$ are Huber losses [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Budget $B$:** The maximum number of camera poses to select [S1, unofficial].
*   **Initialization Steps $n_{init}$:** Set to 3 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ensemble Objective Sequence $E$:** $[C, Q, Q, D, D, T]$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX8000 GPU, 64GB DRAM, 8 CPUs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with AMSGrad enabled, no weight decay [S1, unofficial].
*   **Learning Rate:** $3 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Beta1:** 0.9 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Beta2:** 0.999 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Epsilon:** $1 \times 10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scheduler:** Step size 0.6, $\gamma$ 0.5 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Epochs:** 12 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size:** 8 [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** For the first $n_{init}$ steps (3 steps), select poses at the extremum along each principal spatial axis (PseudoCoverage) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Loop:** While the number of selected steps is less than the budget $B$:
    a.  Determine the current objective index: $idx = \text{step} \pmod{\text{len}(E)}$ [S1, unofficial].
    b.  Select the objective function $f$ from the ensemble sequence $E$ at index $idx$ [S1, unofficial].
    c.  For each candidate unvisited pose $c_z \in C^-$:
        i.  Compute the score using SOARNet: $S_z = \text{SOARNet}(\hat{C}, \hat{M}, c_z)$ [S1, unofficial].
        ii. Extract the score corresponding to the selected objective $f$ [S1, unofficial].
    d.  Select the candidate pose $c_z$ with the highest score as the next pose $\hat{c}_{n+1}$ [S1, unofficial].
    e.  Add $\hat{c}_{n+1}$ to $\hat{C}$ and its image to $\hat{M}$ [S1, unofficial].
    f.  Remove $\hat{c}_{n+1}$ from $C^-$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def generate_trajectory(images, camera_extrinsics, candidate_poses, budget, ensemble_sequence, n_init=3):
    """
    Generates a trajectory using Surrogate Objective-based Planning.
    
    Args:
        images: List of monocular images for visited poses.
        camera_extrinsics: List of camera extrinsics for visited poses.
        candidate_poses: Set of unvisited candidate poses.
        budget: Maximum number of poses to select.
        ensemble_sequence: List of objective names, e.g., ['C', 'Q', 'Q', 'D', 'D', 'T'].
        n_init: Number of initialization steps.
        
    Returns:
        selected_poses: List of selected camera poses.
    """
    selected_poses = []
    visited_images = list(images)
    visited_extrinsics = list(camera_extrinsics)
    
    # Initialization phase
    for step in range(n_init):
        # PseudoCoverage: Pick poses at extremum along principal spatial axes
        # [G] Implementation detail: This requires access to the scene geometry 
        # or bounding box to determine principal axes and extremities.
        # Assuming a function get_extremum_poses exists based on scene bounds.
        next_pose = get_extremum_pose(candidate_poses, step)
        selected_poses.append(next_pose)
        candidate_poses.remove(next_pose)
        
        # [G] In a real scenario, the image for next_pose would be captured 
        # and added to visited_images. Here we assume the input 'images' 
        # contains the initial set, and we append as we go.
        # Note: The source implies images are inputs for the network. 
        # For the first n_init steps, the network might not be used, 
        # or the images are pre-captured. We assume the images list is updated.
        # Since the source says "given monocular 2D images... without requiring 
        # the image of the unvisited pose", the initialization likely uses 
        # pre-captured images or a different mechanism. 
        # We assume the input 'images' list is extended by the system capturing 
        # the pseudo-coverage images.
        # For the sake of the algorithm structure from the source:
        # We assume the images for the selected poses are available or captured.
        
    # Greedy selection phase
    while len(selected_poses) < budget:
        step = len(selected_poses)
        idx = step % len(ensemble_sequence)
        objective_name = ensemble_sequence[idx]
        
        best_score = -float('inf')
        best_pose = None
        
        for candidate in candidate_poses:
            # SOARNet predicts scores for visited poses and the candidate
            # Input: visited images, visited extrinsics, candidate pose
            # Output: F_n (cumulative scores for visited), F_{n+1} (cumulative for candidate)
            scores = soar_net(visited_images, visited_extrinsics, candidate)
            
            # Extract the specific objective score for the candidate
            # scores['F_{n+1}'] contains {f_C, f_Q, f_D, f_T}
            # We need to map objective_name to the key
            if objective_name == 'C':
                score = scores['F_{n+1}']['f_C']
            elif objective_name == 'Q':
                score = scores['F_{n+1}']['f_Q']
            elif objective_name == 'D':
                score = scores['F_{n+1}']['f_D']
            elif objective_name == 'T':
                score = scores['F_{n+1}']['f_T']
                
            if score > best_score:
                best_score = score
                best_pose = candidate
                
        selected_poses.append(best_pose)
        candidate_poses.remove(best_pose)
        
        # [G] The image for best_pose must be captured and added to visited_images
        # and its extrinsics to visited_extrinsics for the next iteration.
        # This is an external action not detailed in the neural network inference.
        new_image, new_extrinsics = capture_pose(best_pose)
        visited_images.append(new_image)
        visited_extrinsics.append(new_extrinsics)
        
    return selected_poses
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The use of Huber loss suggests robustness to outliers in objective score predictions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The method relies on the availability of monocular images and camera extrinsics. If extrinsics are inaccurate, the positionally encoded input to the MLP may be misleading [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The method assumes a set of candidate poses exists. If the candidate set is empty or poorly distributed, the greedy selection may fail to cover the object [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Surface Coverage Objective
A surrogate objective function measuring the ratio of seen faces to total faces to characterize informative samples for training radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. It optimizes for maximizing the number of unique faces seen across camera poses, given the mesh of an object [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when surface completeness is the primary goal for reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method provides a significant speed-up, generating trajectories in seconds instead of hours, offering an ~80x speed-up compared to baselines [S1, unofficial]. This is due to the fast inference of SOARNet compared to iterative radiance field training or uncertainty estimation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** SOARNet outperforms baselines with comparable or better reconstruction qualities [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** The method is model-agnostic, generalizing across fully neural-implicit (e.g., NeRF) to fully explicit approaches (e.g., Plenoxels) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Training-free, fast, interpretable objectives, model-agnostic [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a set of candidate poses and monocular images; does not account for radiance field-specific uncertainties during planning [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rapid view selection is needed without the computational cost of training a radiance field first [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the scene geometry is unknown and cannot be approximated by candidate poses, or when high-precision uncertainty quantification is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the paper "SO-NeRF: Active View Planning for NeRF using Surrogate Objectives" by Chen Feng et al. [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of existing active perception methods for radiance fields, which often require in-flight training or post-hoc uncertainty estimation [S1, unofficial]. The work is supported by NSF Awards 2238968 and 2322242 [S1, unofficial].
