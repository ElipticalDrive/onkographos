# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF, Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) Training with Keypoint Supervision, Depth-Supervised NeRF Training, NeRF with Keypoint Depth Supervision

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating geometric constraints from sparse 3D keypoints [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which relies solely on photometric consistency, DS-NeRF minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and a probabilistic depth prior derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]. This approach is distinct from [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md), which adapts sampling intervals, and [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md), which typically uses dense depth maps; DS-NeRF specifically leverages sparse, noisy keypoints and their uncertainty estimates [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_j\}$ with known camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM); sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ and their visibility flags $\{X_j\}$ from the SfM solver; ground truth RGB colors $C(r)$ for rays $r$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF network parameters $\theta$ defining the radiance field $F_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (c, \sigma)$; rendered novel views.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoint Data:** A list of 3D points $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$. For each keypoint $x_i$, store the reprojection error $\hat{\sigma}_i$ (variance) computed as the average reprojection error across all views where $x_i$ was detected [S1].
*   **Camera Data:** For each view $j$, the projection matrix $P_j$ and the subset of visible keypoints $X_j \subset X$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Near/Far Bounds:** $t_n, t_f$. The scene is bounded by these planes. $t_f$ is treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$, making the termination distribution a valid probability distribution [S1].
*   **Ray Allocation:** A fraction of training rays per iteration are designated as "keypoint rays" for simultaneous RGB and depth supervision [S1].
*   **Activation:** $\sigma(t)$ must be non-negative, typically implemented via ReLU or Softplus [S1].
*   **Iterations:** Typical training ranges from 10k to 100k iterations [S2, unofficial].

**Method Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses $P_j$ and generate sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1].
2.  **Depth Estimation:** For each image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each $x_i \in X_j$, compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
3.  **Uncertainty Calculation:** Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where it was detected [S1].
4.  **Ray Generation:**
    *   For a subset of training rays, generate rays originating from the camera center with directions corresponding to the image coordinates of detected keypoints. In local camera coordinates, the ray direction is derived from the keypoint's 2D image coordinates and $-1$ in the camera axis [S1].
    *   For the remaining rays, generate standard random rays through the image grid [S1].
5.  **Rendering:**
    *   For each ray, sample points along the segment $[t_n, t_f]$.
    *   Query the NeRF network $F_\theta$ to obtain density $\sigma(t)$ and color $c(t)$ at each sample point [S1].
    *   Compute the ray termination distribution $h_{ij}(t)$:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left[-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right]\sigma(t) $$
        where $T(t)$ is the transmittance [S1].
    *   Render the color $\hat{C}$ as the expectation under $h(t)$:
        $$ \hat{C} = \int_0^\infty h(t)c(t)dt \approx \sum_k h(t_k)c(t_k)\Delta t_k $$
    *   Render the depth $\hat{D}$ as the expected termination distance:
        $$ \hat{D} = \int_0^\infty t h(t)dt \approx \sum_k t_k h(t_k)\Delta t_k $$
6.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Color Loss:** Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered color $\hat{C}$ and ground truth $C(r)$ for all rays [S1].
    *   **Depth Loss:** For rays corresponding to keypoints, compute the KL divergence between the rendered distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $N(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$. The loss is approximated as:
        $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\log h(t) \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2}\right) \right] \approx \sum_k -\log h(t_k) \exp\left(-\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2}\right)\Delta t_k $$
        Alternatively, a simple MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ can be used, though KL divergence yields slightly better results [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Interpolation:** Since keypoints are discrete, interpolate RGB supervision values for the rays corresponding to detected keypoints to allow simultaneous supervision in a single ray marching step [S1].
7.  **Optimization:** Minimize the combined loss $L = L_{Color} + \lambda L_{Depth}$ with respect to $\theta$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_transmittance_and_density(sigma_samples, deltas):
    """
    Compute transmittance T(t) and termination distribution h(t) from density samples.
    sigma_samples: array of shape (N,) representing sigma(t_k)
    deltas: array of shape (N,) representing delta_t_k
    Returns:
        h: array of shape (N,) representing h(t_k)
        T: array of shape (N,) representing T(t_k) (optional, for debugging)
    """
    # Compute accumulated sigma
    # alpha_k = 1 - exp(-sigma_k * delta_k)
    # T_k = product_{j<k} (1 - alpha_j)
    # h_k = T_k * alpha_k
    
    # Using log-space for stability if needed, but direct computation is standard for small N
    alphas = 1.0 - np.exp(-sigma_samples * deltas)
    
    # Cumulative product of (1 - alpha)
    # T_0 = 1.0
    # T_k = T_{k-1} * (1 - alpha_{k-1})
    one_minus_alphas = 1.0 - alphas
    T_cumprod = np.ones_like(deltas)
    T_cumprod[1:] = np.cumprod(one_minus_alphas[:-1])
    
    h = T_cumprod * alphas
    return h

def depth_supervised_nerf_step(rays, keypoints, camera_poses, nerf_model, t_n, t_f, num_samples, lambda_depth):
    """
    Single training step for DS-NeRF.
    rays: list of ray origins and directions for all training rays
    keypoints: dict mapping view_index -> list of (keypoint_3d, reprojection_error)
    camera_poses: dict mapping view_index -> projection matrix P_j
    nerf_model: function F_theta(x, d) -> (sigma, color)
    t_n, t_f: near and far clipping planes
    num_samples: number of samples per ray
    lambda_depth: weight for depth loss
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    color_loss = 0.0
    depth_loss = 0.0
    
    # Separate rays into keypoint rays and standard rays
    keypoint_rays = []
    standard_rays = []
    
    for view_idx, ray in enumerate(rays):
        # Check if this ray corresponds to a keypoint
        # In practice, rays are generated based on keypoint image coordinates
        if view_idx in keypoints:
            keypoint_rays.append((view_idx, ray))
        else:
            standard_rays.append(ray)
            
    # Process standard rays (Color Loss only)
    for ray in standard_rays:
        origins, directions = ray
        # Sample points along ray
        t_vals = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, num_samples)
        points = origins + t_vals[:, np.newaxis] * directions
        
        # Query NeRF
        sigma, color = nerf_model(points, directions)
        
        # Compute weights
        deltas = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        deltas = np.append(deltas, 1e-10) # Handle last delta
        
        # Compute h(t)
        h = compute_transmittance_and_density(sigma, deltas)
        
        # Render color
        rendered_color = np.sum(h[:, np.newaxis] * color[:-1], axis=0) # Use color at sample points
        
        # Ground truth color (interpolated from image)
        gt_color = get_gt_color(ray) # Helper to get GT color from image
        
        color_loss += np.mean((rendered_color - gt_color) ** 2)
        
    # Process keypoint rays (Color + Depth Loss)
    for view_idx, ray in keypoint_rays:
        origins, directions = ray
        t_vals = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, num_samples)
        points = origins + t_vals[:, np.newaxis] * directions
        
        sigma, color = nerf_model(points, directions)
        deltas = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        deltas = np.append(deltas, 1e-10)
        
        h = compute_transmittance_and_density(sigma, deltas)
        
        # Render color
        rendered_color = np.sum(h[:, np.newaxis] * color[:-1], axis=0)
        gt_color = get_gt_color(ray)
        color_loss += np.mean((rendered_color - gt_color) ** 2)
        
        # Depth Loss
        # Get keypoint data for this view
        kp_data = keypoints[view_idx]
        for kp_3d, sigma_hat in kp_data:
            # Project keypoint to get depth D_ij
            # D_ij is the z-coordinate of the keypoint in camera frame
            # Assuming ray direction points towards keypoint
            D_ij = get_keypoint_depth(kp_3d, view_idx, camera_poses)
            
            # Compute KL divergence approximation
            # L_depth = -sum(h_k * exp(-(t_k - D_ij)^2 / (2 * sigma_hat^2)))
            exponent = -((t_vals[:-1] - D_ij) ** 2) / (2 * sigma_hat ** 2)
            depth_term = np.exp(exponent)
            l_depth = -np.sum(h * depth_term)
            depth_loss += l_depth
            
    total_loss = color_loss + lambda_depth * depth_loss
    return total_loss, color_loss, depth_loss
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are comparable in wall-clock time to standard NeRF. On an RTX A5000, a DS-NeRF iteration takes ~362.4 ms vs ~359.8 ms for standard NeRF [S1].
*   **Convergence Speed:** DS-NeRF reaches target test PSNR in 2-3x fewer training iterations than standard NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, it achieves peak PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing sparse 3D keypoints and their uncertainties, which is negligible compared to the NeRF network weights [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly reduces the number of input views required for high-quality synthesis (e.g., improving PSNR from 13.5 to 20.2 with very few views) [S1]. It is compatible with other NeRF methods (e.g., PixelNeRF, metaNeRF) and does not rely on domain-specific priors [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance is dependent on the quality of the SfM reconstruction. Poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements can cause optimization failure [S1]. The method assumes Gaussian uncertainty for keypoints, which may not hold for non-SfM depth sources [S1]. Dense MVS depth is not used during training [S1].

## Background
DS-NeRF was introduced to address the failure mode of NeRF when given insufficient input views, where the model fits incorrect geometries due to lack of constraints on empty space and opaque surfaces [S1]. The method leverages the "free" depth supervision provided by sparse 3D point clouds generated during standard SfM preprocessing (e.g., COLMAP) [S1]. It was demonstrated on datasets including DTU, Redwood, and NeRF Real with 2, 5, or 10 input views [S1]. The approach is distinct from methods using data-driven priors (like PixelNeRF) as it requires no additional training on other scenes and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds [S1].
