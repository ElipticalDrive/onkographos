# Aug-NeRF

Also called: Aug-NeRF Training Pipeline, Augmented NeRF, Triple-Level Physically-Grounded Augmentations

## Overview
Aug-NeRF is a training framework for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that injects worst-case adversarial perturbations into three distinct levels of the pipeline—input coordinates, intermediate features, and pre-rendering output—to implicitly regularize the model with physical grounds [S1]. It addresses the generalization gap in NeRF where interpolation between seen views yields inconsistent and non-smooth geometries [S1]. Unlike explicit regularizers like Laplacian or Total Variation which constrain MLP flexibility aggressively, Aug-NeRF uses data-adaptive smoothness priors to enhance generalization to unseen views without sacrificing representation flexibility [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $I = \{I_i \in \mathbb{R}^M\}_{i=1}^N$ and their corresponding camera poses $P = \{\phi_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}\}_{i=1}^N$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized neural radiance field parameters $\Theta$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Ray Representation:** $r = (o, d, \theta)$, where $o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the optical center, $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the direction, and $\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]^2$ is the angular view direction [S1].
*   **Radiance Field:** $F_\Theta(p, \theta) = (g \circ f(p, \theta), h \circ f(p))$, mapping spatial coordinate $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and view direction $\theta$ to color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^+$ [S1].
    *   $f(\cdot)$ maps coordinates to a $D$-dimension feature vector using positional encoding [S1].
    *   $g(\cdot)$ projects features to RGB color [S1].
    *   $h(\cdot)$ projects features to density [S1].
*   **Perturbations:** $\delta = (\delta_p, \delta_f, \delta_r)$, where $\delta_p$ affects coordinates, $\delta_f$ affects features, and $\delta_r$ affects pre-rendering output [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Ray Casting:** For each pixel in each training image $I_i$, cast a ray $r_i = (o_i, d_i, \theta_i)$ via inverse projection using camera pose $\phi_i$ [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** Coarse sampling generates $K/2$ depth intervals $t_k$ uniformly along the ray between $t_n$ and $t_f$ [S1]. Fine sampling generates $K/2$ depth intervals $t_k$ proportional to the densities from the coarse pass [S1].
3.  **Adversarial Perturbation Search:** For the current batch of rays, compute worst-case perturbations $\delta$ by maximizing the loss using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) within defined search spaces [S1].
4.  **Perturbed Forward Pass:** Compute perturbed depth $t^\dag_k = t_k + \delta_{t,k}$ and perturbed view direction $\theta^\dag = \theta + \delta_\theta$ [S1]. Compute unperturbed point $p_k = o + t_k d$ and perturbed point $p^\dag_k = o + t^\dag_k d + \delta_{xyz}$ [S1]. Query network for unperturbed outputs: $(c_k, \sigma_k) = (g \circ f(p_k, \theta), h \circ f(p_k))$ [S1]. Compute perturbed color: $c^\dag_k = g(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f, \theta^\dag) + \delta_c$ [S1]. Compute perturbed density: $\sigma^\dag_k = h(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f) + \delta_\sigma$ [S1].
5.  **Volumetric Rendering:** Compute intervals: $\Delta t_k = t_k - t_{k-1}$ and $\Delta t^\dag_k = t^\dag_k - t^\dag_{k-1}$ [S1]. Compute transmittance: $T(k) = \exp(-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1} \sigma_l \Delta t_l)$ and $T^\dag(k) = \exp(-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1} \sigma^\dag_l \Delta t^\dag_l)$ [S1]. Render unperturbed color: $C(r|\Theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T(k) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_k \Delta t_k)) c_k$ [S1]. Render perturbed color: $C^\dag(r|\Theta, \delta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T^\dag(k) (1 - \exp(-\sigma^\dag_k \Delta t^\dag_k)) c^\dag_k$ [S1].
6.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the total loss combining photometric error and adversarial regularization: $\mathcal{L} = \| C(r|\Theta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 + \lambda \| C^\dag(r|\Theta, \delta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2$ [S1].
7.  **Update:** Update network parameters $\Theta$ via gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}$ [S1].

**Formulas**
*   **Volume Rendering:**
    $$ C(r | \Theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T(k) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_k \Delta t_k)) c_k $$
    where $T(k) = \exp(-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1} \sigma_l \Delta t_l)$ and $\Delta t_k = t_{k+1} - t_k$ [S1].
*   **Optimization Objective (Min-Max Game):**
    $$ \min_{\Theta} \mathbb{E}_{(r, \hat{C}) \sim P(\mathcal{R})} \max_{\delta} \| C^\dag(r | \Theta, \delta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 $$
    where $\delta = (\delta_p, \delta_f, \delta_r)$ represents perturbations for coordinate, feature, and output respectively [S1].
*   **Full Training Objective:**
    $$ \mathbb{E}_{(r, \hat{C}) \sim P(\mathcal{R})} \left[ \| C(r | \Theta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 + \lambda \max_{\delta} \| C^\dag(r | \Theta, \delta) - \hat{C} \|_2^2 \right] $$
    with $\lambda = 0.1$ [S1].
*   **PGD Update Rule:**
    $$ \delta^{(t+1)} = \text{Proj}_{\|\delta\|_\infty \le \epsilon} \left[ \delta^{(t)} + \alpha \cdot \text{sgn}(\nabla_\delta \mathcal{L}(\Theta | \mathcal{R}, \delta)) \right] $$
    where $\alpha$ is step size and $\epsilon$ is radius [S1].
*   **Coordinate Perturbation:**
    $$ t^\dag_k = t_k + \delta_{t,k}, \quad \theta^\dag = \theta + \delta_\theta, \quad p^\dag_k = o + t^\dag_k d + \delta_{xyz} $$
    Constraints: $\delta_t \le |\alpha_t (t_{k+1} - t_k)|$; $\delta_{xyz} \in B(0, \epsilon_p)$; $\delta_\theta \in [-\epsilon_p/2f, \epsilon_p/2f]^2$ [S1].
*   **Pre-Rendering Output Perturbation:**
    $$ C^\dag(r | \Theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T(k) (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_{k} + \delta_{\sigma}) \Delta t^\dag_k)) (c_k + \delta_c) $$
    Constraints: $\delta_c, \delta_\sigma$ clamped such that $c_k, \sigma_k \in [0, 1]$ [S1].
*   **Intermediate Feature Perturbation:**
    $$ c_k = g(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f, \theta^\dag), \quad \sigma_k = h(f(p^\dag_k) + \delta_f) $$
    Search space: $\delta_f \in [-\epsilon_f, \epsilon_f]^D$ [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $K$: Total number of depth intervals sampled per ray (sum of coarse and fine samples) [S1].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far bounds of the ray segment [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Regularization weight, set to 0.1 [S1].
*   $\epsilon_p$: Pixel size; $\epsilon$: Perturbation radius [S1].
*   $f$: Focal length [S1].
*   PGD Configuration: Coordinate level uses 3 steps with step size $10^{-2}$; Feature level uses 1 step with step size $10^{-3}$; Pre-rendering level uses 1 step with step size $10^{-5}$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Batch Size:** The algorithm description assumes a batch size of 1 for simplicity [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def aug_nerf_train(images, poses, F_theta, K, lambda_reg, pgd_config, t_n, t_f, D, focal_length):
    """
    images: List of N images I_i
    poses: List of N camera poses phi_i
    F_theta: Neural radiance field function F_theta(p, theta) -> (c, sigma)
    K: Total number of samples per ray
    lambda_reg: Regularization weight (0.1)
    pgd_config: Dict with keys 'coord', 'feat', 'output' containing (steps, step_size, epsilon)
    t_n, t_f: Near and far bounds
    D: Hidden dimension of MLP
    focal_length: Camera focal length
    """
    rays = []
    for i in range(len(images)):
        for pixel in images[i].pixels:
            o = poses[i].origin
            d = pixel.direction
            theta = pixel.view_direction
            C_hat = pixel.color
            rays.append((o, d, theta, C_hat))
            
    while not converged:
        batch_rays = sample_batch(rays)
        total_loss = 0.0
        
        for o, d, theta, C_hat in batch_rays:
            # 1. Sampling
            t_coarse = uniform_sample(K // 2, t_n, t_f)
            sigma_coarse = [F_theta.query(p=o + t * d, theta=theta)[1] for t in t_coarse]
            t_fine = proportional_sample(t_coarse, sigma_coarse, K // 2)
            t_all = sort(concat(t_coarse, t_fine))
            
            # 2. Adversarial Perturbation Search (PGD)
            delta_t = zeros(K)
            delta_xyz = zeros(3)
            delta_theta = zeros(2)
            delta_f = zeros(D)
            delta_c = zeros(3)
            delta_sigma = 0.0
            
            # Coordinate PGD
            for _ in range(pgd_config['coord'].steps):
                loss = compute_loss(o, d, theta, C_hat, t_all, delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, delta_c, delta_sigma, F_theta)
                grad = gradient(loss, [delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta])
                delta_t += pgd_config['coord'].step_size * sign(grad.delta_t)
                delta_xyz += pgd_config['coord'].step_size * sign(grad.delta_xyz)
                delta_theta += pgd_config['coord'].step_size * sign(grad.delta_theta)
                delta_t = clamp(delta_t, -pgd_config['coord'].epsilon, pgd_config['coord'].epsilon)
                delta_xyz = project_ball(delta_xyz, pgd_config['coord'].epsilon)
                delta_theta = clamp(delta_theta, -pgd_config['coord'].epsilon / (2 * focal_length), pgd_config['coord'].epsilon / (2 * focal_length))

            # Feature PGD
            for _ in range(pgd_config['feat'].steps):
                loss = compute_loss(o, d, theta, C_hat, t_all, delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, delta_c, delta_sigma, F_theta)
                grad = gradient(loss, delta_f)
                delta_f += pgd_config['feat'].step_size * sign(grad)
                delta_f = clamp(delta_f, -pgd_config['feat'].epsilon, pgd_config['feat'].epsilon)

            # Output PGD
            for _ in range(pgd_config['output'].steps):
                loss = compute_loss(o, d, theta, C_hat, t_all, delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, delta_c, delta_sigma, F_theta)
                grad = gradient(loss, [delta_c, delta_sigma])
                delta_c += pgd_config['output'].step_size * sign(grad.delta_c)
                delta_sigma += pgd_config['output'].step_size * sign(grad.delta_sigma)
                delta_c = clamp(delta_c, -pgd_config['output'].epsilon, pgd_config['output'].epsilon)
                delta_sigma = clamp(delta_sigma, -pgd_config['output'].epsilon, pgd_config['output'].epsilon)

            # 3. Forward Pass with Perturbations
            p_clean = [o + t * d for t in t_all]
            p_dag = [o + (t_all[k] + delta_t[k]) * d + delta_xyz for k in range(K)]
            theta_dag = theta + delta_theta
            
            c_clean_list, sigma_clean_list = [], []
            c_dag_list, sigma_dag_list = [], []
            
            for k in range(K):
                f_clean = F_theta.feature(p_clean[k], theta)
                c_clean, sigma_clean = F_theta.output(f_clean, theta)
                c_clean_list.append(c_clean)
                sigma_clean_list.append(sigma_clean)
                
                f_perturbed = F_theta.feature(p_dag[k], theta_dag) + delta_f
                c_dag = F_theta.color(f_perturbed, theta_dag) + delta_c
                sigma_dag = F_theta.density(f_perturbed) + delta_sigma
                c_dag = clamp(c_dag, 0, 1)
                sigma_dag = clamp(sigma_dag, 0, 1)
                c_dag_list.append(c_dag)
                sigma_dag_list.append(sigma_dag)
                
            dt = [t_all[k] - t_all[k-1] if k > 0 else t_all[0] - t_n for k in range(K)]
            dt_dag = [t_all[k] + delta_t[k] - (t_all[k-1] + delta_t[k-1]) if k > 0 else t_all[0] + delta_t[0] - t_n for k in range(K)]
            
            T = [exp(-sum(sigma_clean_list[l] * dt[l] for l in range(k))) for k in range(K)]
            T_dag = [exp(-sum(sigma_dag_list[l] * dt_dag[l] for l in range(k))) for k in range(K)]
            
            C_clean = sum(T[k] * (1 - exp(-sigma_clean_list[k] * dt[k])) * c_clean_list[k] for k in range(K))
            C_dag = sum(T_dag[k] * (1 - exp(-sigma_dag_list[k] * dt_dag[k])) * c_dag_list[k] for k in range(K))
            
            loss_clean = norm(C_clean - C_hat) ** 2
            loss_adv = norm(C_dag - C_hat) ** 2
            loss = loss_clean + lambda_reg * loss_adv
            total_loss += loss
            
        update_parameters(F_theta, total_loss)

# Helper definitions (standard operations)
def uniform_sample(n, t_n, t_f): return [t_n + (k + 0.5) * (t_f - t_n) / n for k in range(n)]
def proportional_sample(t_coarse, sigma_coarse, n): return [t_coarse[i % len(t_coarse)] + (sigma_coarse[i % len(sigma_coarse)] / sum(sigma_coarse)) * (t_f - t_n) for i in range(n)]
def sort(arr): return sorted(arr)
def concat(a, b): return a + b
def zeros(n): return [0.0] * n
def clamp(x, lo, hi): return max(lo, min(hi, x))
def sign(x): return 1 if x > 0 else (-1 if x < 0 else 0)
def project_ball(v, r): norm_v = norm(v); return v * (r / norm_v) if norm_v > r else v
def gradient(loss, params): return [loss.backward(p) for p in params]
def norm(v): return sum(x**2 for x in v)**0.5
def exp(x): return math.exp(x)
def converged: return False # Placeholder for convergence check
def sample_batch(rays): return random.sample(rays, min(32, len(rays)))
def update_parameters(model, loss): model.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); model.step()
def compute_loss(o, d, theta, C_hat, t_all, delta_t, delta_xyz, delta_theta, delta_f, delta_c, delta_sigma, F_theta):
    p_dag = [o + (t_all[k] + delta_t[k]) * d + delta_xyz for k in range(len(t_all))]
    theta_dag = theta + delta_theta
    c_dag_list, sigma_dag_list = [], []
    for k in range(len(t_all)):
        f_perturbed = F_theta.feature(p_dag[k], theta_dag) + delta_f
        c_dag = F_theta.color(f_perturbed, theta_dag) + delta_c
        sigma_dag = F_theta.density(f_perturbed) + delta_sigma
        c_dag_list.append(clamp(c_dag, 0, 1))
        sigma_dag_list.append(clamp(sigma_dag, 0, 1))
    dt_dag = [t_all[k] + delta_t[k] - (t_all[k-1] + delta_t[k-1]) if k > 0 else t_all[0] + delta_t[0] - t_n for k in range(len(t_all))]
    T_dag = [exp(-sum(sigma_dag_list[l] * dt_dag[l] for l in range(k))) for k in range(len(t_all))]
    C_dag = sum(T_dag[k] * (1 - exp(-sigma_dag_list[k] * dt_dag[k])) * c_dag_list[k] for k in range(len(t_all)))
    return norm(C_dag - C_hat) ** 2
```

## Subtopics

### Training Ray Marching and Integration
Aug-NeRF relies on standard volume rendering integration but applies perturbations to the sampled points and outputs before integration. It builds on [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md).

### Regularization and Loss Functions
Aug-NeRF replaces explicit regularizers (Laplacian, TV) with an implicit smooth prior via adversarial perturbations. It builds on [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md).

### Specific Model Architectures
Aug-NeRF is compatible with the standard NeRF MLP architecture ($F_\Theta = \gamma \circ MLP_\Theta$) but can be applied to variants. It builds on [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires additional computation for the PGD steps to generate perturbations at each iteration [S1]. The source specifies 500K iterations for convergence [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing gradients for perturbations during the inner maximization loop [S1]. The source mentions training on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU with 48 GB memory [S1].
*   **Performance:** Aug-NeRF reduces average error by 14.3%–26.0% on LLFF and 12.5%–44.7% on 360° NeRF-Synthetic datasets compared to vanilla NeRF [S1]. It achieves up to 1.5dB PSNR gain [S1]. Specific PSNR improvements on LLFF: fern (+1.34), orchids (+1.24), trex (+1.37) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves generalization to unseen views; robust to noisy supervision images (Gaussian and Shot noise); produces smoother geometry than explicit regularizers [S1].

## Background
Aug-NeRF was proposed by Tianlong Chen, Peihao Wang, Zhiwen Fan, and Zhangyang Wang from The University of Texas at Austin [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2022 [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of NeRF in generating smooth geometries for novel views by introducing robust data augmentations inspired by CNN training techniques [S1].
