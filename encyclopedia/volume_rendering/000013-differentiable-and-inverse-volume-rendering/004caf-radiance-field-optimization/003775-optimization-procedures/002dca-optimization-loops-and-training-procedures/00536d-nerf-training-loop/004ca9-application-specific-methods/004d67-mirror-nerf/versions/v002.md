# Mirror-NeRF

Also called: Mirror-NeRF: Learning Neural Radiance Fields for Mirrors with Whitted-Style Ray Tracing

## Overview
Mirror-NeRF is a neural rendering framework that learns accurate mirror geometry and reflection by unifying the radiance field and tracing rays according to Whitted-style light transport, enabling novel view synthesis and scene manipulation [S1]. Unlike NeRF and NeRFReN, which treat mirror reflections as separate virtual scenes or interpolate memorized reflections, Mirror-NeRF uses a unified radiance field with explicit reflection probability and Whitted-style ray tracing to synthesize physically consistent reflections from novel viewpoints [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of input images with known camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** A rendered image for a target camera pose, including the direct view and physically accurate reflections.

**Data Structures:**
The method uses a unified set of Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to represent scene properties at any 3D spatial location $\mathbf{x}$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}$. The fields are:
1.  **Geometry MLP ($F_{geo}$):** Takes positional encoded position $\gamma_x(\mathbf{x})$ to output a geometry feature vector $f_{geo}$.
2.  **Density MLP ($F_\sigma$):** Takes $f_{geo}$ to output volume density $\sigma$.
3.  **Radiance MLP ($F_c$):** Takes $f_{geo}$ and positional encoded view direction $\gamma_d(\mathbf{d})$ to output radiance color $c$.
4.  **Normal MLP ($F_n$):** Takes $f_{geo}$ to output a smoothed surface normal $\tilde{\mathbf{n}}$. This is used to avoid noise from analytical gradients of volume density [S1].
5.  **Reflection Probability MLP ($F_m$):** Takes $f_{geo}$ to output a scalar reflection probability $m \in [0, 1]$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel in the target image, generate a camera ray $\mathbf{r}$ originating from the camera center with direction $\mathbf{d}$.
2.  **Sampling:** Sample $N$ points along the ray $\mathbf{r}$ at positions $\mathbf{x}_i$ and distances $\delta_i$.
3.  **Field Query:** For each sample point $\mathbf{x}_i$:
    *   Compute geometry feature: $f_{geo} = F_{geo}(\gamma_x(\mathbf{x}_i))$.
    *   Compute density: $\sigma_i = F_\sigma(f_{geo})$.
    *   Compute radiance: $c_i = F_c(f_{geo}, \gamma_d(\mathbf{d}))$.
    *   Compute reflection probability: $m_i = F_m(f_{geo})$.
    *   Compute normal: $\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_i = F_n(f_{geo})$.
4.  **Ray Tracing and Reflection:**
    *   Determine if the ray hits a mirror surface. The method uses the reflection probability $m_i$ and the density $\sigma_i$ to identify the mirror surface.
    *   When a ray intersects a surface point with high reflection probability (indicating a mirror), trigger physical reflection.
    *   Calculate the reflected ray direction $\mathbf{r}'$ using the surface normal $\tilde{\mathbf{n}}$ and the incident ray direction, following Whitted-style ray tracing [S1].
    *   Trace the reflected ray $\mathbf{r}'$ through the scene to accumulate radiance from the reflection. Let the accumulated color of the reflected ray be $C_{refl}$.
5.  **Volume Rendering Accumulation:**
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$.
    *   Compute opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$.
    *   The final color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ is a blend of the direct view color and the reflected color, weighted by the reflection probability. The standard volume rendering equation is modified to include the reflection component [S1]:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i c_i + T_{hit} m_{hit} C_{refl} $$
        Where $T_{hit}$ is the transmittance up to the mirror surface, and $m_{hit}$ is the reflection probability at the hit point. Note: The source formula contains typos ($\mathbf{u}_{Ray_r}$, etc.), but the text describes blending camera ray color and reflected ray color weighted by reflection probability [S1]. A more precise interpretation based on the text "blend the final image color from the camera ray color and the reflected ray color weighted by the reflection probability" suggests:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = (1 - m_{hit}) C_{direct} + m_{hit} C_{refl} $$
        where $C_{direct}$ is the volume-rendered color of the direct ray (including occlusions) and $C_{refl}$ is the color of the reflected ray. The unified field ensures that $C_{refl}$ is consistent with the scene geometry learned by the same MLPs.
6.  **Optimization:**
    *   Optimize the parameters of all MLPs ($F_{geo}, F_\sigma, F_c, F_n, F_m$) using a loss function.
    *   The loss includes photometric reconstruction error ($L_{photo}$) between rendered and ground truth images.
    *   **Plane Consistency Constraint:** Enforce that the mirror surface lies on a plane. This reduces ambiguity in mirror geometry reconstruction [S1].
    *   **Forward-Facing Normal Constraint:** Enforce that the surface normals of the mirror face towards the camera (or consistent with the expected mirror orientation) to reduce reflection ambiguity [S1].
    *   **Progressive Training:** Apply a progressive training strategy to stabilize the optimization of the mirror geometry, likely by gradually increasing the complexity of the scene or the resolution of the training [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Mirror Ambiguity:** The "appearance" of a mirror comes from other objects and is not consistent from different viewpoints, making reconstruction ambiguous [S1]. This is mitigated by plane consistency and forward-facing normal constraints.
*   **Unobserved Reflections:** Standard NeRF cannot infer reflections unobserved in training views (e.g., missing ceiling). Mirror-NeRF handles this by using accurate depth and ray tracing [S1].
*   **Noise in Normals:** Analytical gradients of volume density can be noisy. A separate MLP is used to parameterize the surface normal distribution to ensure smoothness [S1].

*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   Physically consistent reflections from novel viewpoints.
    *   Accurate mirror geometry reconstruction.
    *   Supports scene manipulation (adding objects/mirrors, controlling roughness) [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the assumption that mirrors are planar.
    *   Requires careful tuning of regularization constraints to stabilize training.
*   **When to Use:** When accurate mirror geometry and physically correct reflections are required for novel view synthesis or scene manipulation.
*   **When Not to Use:** When mirrors are non-planar or when computational resources are extremely limited and approximate reflections are acceptable.

## Background
Mirror-NeRF was introduced by Junyi Zeng, Chong Bao, et al. from Zhejiang University and the State Key Lab of CAD&CG [S1]. The paper was presented at MM '23, October 29-November 3, 2023, Ottawa, ON, Canada [S1]. It addresses the limitations of NeRF and NeRFReN in handling mirrors by introducing a unified radiance field with explicit reflection probability and Whitted-style ray tracing [S1].
