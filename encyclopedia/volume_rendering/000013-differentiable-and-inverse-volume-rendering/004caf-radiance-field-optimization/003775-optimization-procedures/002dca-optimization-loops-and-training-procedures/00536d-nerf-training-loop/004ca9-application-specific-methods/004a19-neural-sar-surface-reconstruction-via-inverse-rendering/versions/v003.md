# Neural SAR Surface Reconstruction via Inverse Rendering

Also called: Differentiable SAR Rendering, Neural DSM Reconstruction

## Overview
This method reconstructs a Digital Surface Model (DSM) and a backscattering coefficient map from Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) images by minimizing the difference between observed SAR intensities and synthesized intensities using a differentiable rendering model [S1]. It models elevation $Z$ and backscattering $B$ within a bounding box using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), reducing sampling degrees of freedom from a 3D volume to a 2D surface [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which optimizes a 3D density field, this approach enforces surface constraints by mapping 2D ground coordinates directly to elevation and appearance, leading to faster and easier reconstruction of surfaces [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** $N$ SAR images with known acquisition parameters (azimuth, range, viewing geometry). Each image has dimensions $N_A \times N_R$ (azimuth $\times$ range).
*   **Outputs:** A reconstructed DSM (elevation map $Z(x,y)$) and a backscattering coefficient map $B(x,y)$.

**Data Structures**
*   **MLP:** A fully-connected network.
    *   **Input:** 2D position $(x, y)$ on the ground, encoded using Instant NGP’s positional encoding $\gamma_P$ [S1].
    *   **Output:** Two values: Elevation $Z(x, y)$ and Backscattering coefficient $B(x, y)$ [S1].
*   **Bounding Box:** A 3D volume of side length $L$ defines the spatial extent for the reconstruction [S1].

**Rendering Model**
The method uses a simplified physically-based SAR intensity rendering model. For a sample $k$ at ground range $X_k$, the scattering coefficient $\sigma_k^0$ is computed based on the surface normal $\vec{n}_k$ and the local incidence unit vector $\vec{u}_k$ pointing from the sensor to the midpoint $P_{k+1/2}$ of the sample interval [S1]:

$$ \sigma_k^0(\vec{u}_k) = B(P_{k+1/2}) \cdot |\vec{u}_k \cdot \vec{n}_k| $$

where $B(P_{k+1/2})$ is the backscattering coefficient at the midpoint [S1]. The synthesized intensity $\hat{I}$ is derived from these scattering coefficients.

**Optimization Steps**
1.  **Sampling:** For each minibatch, select a random subset of $M$ lines across all $N$ views. $M > 80$ to ensure adequate sample distribution while meeting memory constraints [S1].
2.  **Ground Range Shifting:** Shift the ground ranges sampled by a random value along their line of sight to avoid over-fitting at specific coordinates [S1].
3.  **Positional Encoding:** Encode the 2D position $(x, y)$ using Instant NGP’s positional encoding $\gamma_P$ [S1].
4.  **Frequency Windowing:** Apply Nerfies’ windowing on frequency bands on the output of $\gamma_P$ to regulate frequency bias during early training phases [S1]. Dynamically determine the target maximum frequency level $l$ for each sample $k$ of ground range $X_k$ such that $l$ corresponds to $|X_{k+1} - X_{k-1}| / 4$ [S1].
5.  **MLP Forward Pass:**
    *   Compute $B$ by applying an exponential activation to the MLP output to ensure positive values [S1].
    *   Compute $Z$ by applying the activation function $L_8 \cdot \arctan$ to the MLP output to bound its values [S1].
6.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the total loss $L = L_D + \lambda L_{TV}$ [S1].
    *   $L_D$ is the negative log-likelihood loss:
        $$ L_D(\hat{I}) = \frac{1}{M \cdot N_R} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \sum_{n=1}^{N_R} \left( \frac{I_{m,n}}{\hat{I}_{m,n}} + \log\left(\frac{\hat{I}_{m,n}}{I_{m,n}}\right) \right) $$
        where $I_{m,n}$ is observed intensity and $\hat{I}_{m,n}$ is synthesized intensity [S1].
    *   $L_{TV}$ is total variation regularization on $B$ [S1].
7.  **Update:** Minimize $L$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].

**Coarse-to-Fine Strategy**
To efficiently reconstruct multiple scales and speed up initial training, gradually increase the number of samples $K$ by annealing a subsampling factor $\beta$ from $\beta_0$ to 1 [S1]. The parameters are updated as:
$$ K = K_f / \beta $$
$$ \mu = \mu_f \cdot \beta $$
where $K_f$ is the target number of samples per line and $\mu_f$ is the target accuracy [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim

class NeuralSARReconstruction:
    def __init__(self, L, K_f, mu_f, beta_0, lambda_tv=0.1):
        """
        Initialize the reconstruction model.
        
        Args:
            L: Side length of the bounding box.
            K_f: Target number of samples per line.
            mu_f: Target accuracy.
            beta_0: Initial subsampling factor.
            lambda_tv: Weight for Total Variation regularization.
        """
        self.L = L
        self.K_f = K_f
        self.mu_f = mu_f
        self.beta = beta_0
        self.lambda_tv = lambda_tv
        
        # MLP for Elevation Z and Backscattering B
        # Input: 2D position (x, y)
        # Output: 2 values (Z, B_raw)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(2, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 2) # Output: [Z_raw, B_raw]
        )
        
        # Instant NGP positional encoding (simplified placeholder for implementation)
        # In practice, this uses the official Instant-NGP implementation
        self.pos_encoding = get_instant_ngp_encoding() 
        
        self.optimizer = optim.Adam(self.mlp.parameters(), lr=1e-3)

    def activation_z(self, z_raw):
        """
        Bounding activation for elevation.
        Uses L_8 * arctan. L_8 is a constant related to the bounding box scale.
        """
        # Assuming L_8 is a scaling factor derived from L
        return self.L * torch.arctan(z_raw)

    def activation_b(self, b_raw):
        """
        Exponential activation for backscattering to ensure positive values.
        """
        return torch.exp(b_raw)

    def nerfies_windowing(self, pos_encoded, x_k, x_k_minus_1, x_k_plus_1):
        """
        Apply frequency windowing to regulate frequency bias.
        """
        # Determine target max frequency level l
        # l corresponds to |X_{k+1} - X_{k-1}| / 4
        freq_limit = torch.abs(x_k_plus_1 - x_k_minus_1) / 4.0
        
        # Apply windowing to pos_encoded based on freq_limit
        # This typically involves masking high frequencies above the limit
        # Implementation depends on specific Instant-NGP windowing function
        return apply_frequency_window(pos_encoded, freq_limit)

    def compute_scattering_coefficient(self, B_mid, u_k, n_k):
        """
        Compute scattering coefficient sigma_k^0.
        sigma_k^0 = B(P_{k+1/2}) * |u_k . n_k|
        """
        dot_product = torch.sum(u_k * n_k, dim=-1)
        return B_mid * torch.abs(dot_product)

    def render_line(self, line_indices, view_params, images):
        """
        Render a subset of lines for a minibatch.
        
        Args:
            line_indices: List of line indices to render.
            view_params: Acquisition parameters for the views.
            images: Observed SAR intensities.
            
        Returns:
            synthesized_intensities, observed_intensities
        """
        # Select random subset of M lines across all N views
        # M > 80
        M = len(line_indices)
        N_R = view_params['range_cells']
        
        synthesized = []
        observed = []
        
        for m in line_indices:
            # Get view parameters for this line
            # Shift ground ranges by random value along line of sight
            # to avoid over-fitting
            shifted_ranges = shift_ground_ranges(view_params['ranges'], view_params['view_direction'])
            
            # Sample points along the line
            # Number of samples K depends on current beta
            K = int(self.K_f / self.beta)
            mu = self.mu_f * self.beta
            
            # Generate sample positions (x, y) on the ground
            # These are derived from the shifted ranges and view geometry
            sample_positions = generate_sample_positions(shifted_ranges, view_params, K)
            
            # Encode positions
            pos_encoded = self.pos_encoding(sample_positions)
            
            # Apply Nerfies windowing
            # Need x_{k-1}, x_k, x_{k+1} for each sample
            pos_encoded_windowed = self.nerfies_windowing(
                pos_encoded, 
                sample_positions[:, 0], 
                sample_positions[:, 0].roll(1), 
                sample_positions[:, 0].roll(-1)
            )
            
            # MLP Forward Pass
            mlp_output = self.mlp(pos_encoded_windowed)
            z_raw, b_raw = mlp_output[:, 0], mlp_output[:, 1]
            
            Z = self.activation_z(z_raw)
            B = self.activation_b(b_raw)
            
            # Compute surface normals from Z
            # n_k is deduced from the quadrilateral patch
            n_k = compute_normals(Z, sample_positions)
            
            # View direction u_k
            u_k = view_params['view_direction']
            
            # Compute scattering coefficients
            sigma_k0 = self.compute_scattering_coefficient(B, u_k, n_k)
            
            # Render intensity (simplified volume rendering along the ray)
            # Since it's a surface model, integration is along the ray intersecting the surface
            # This step depends on the specific differentiable SAR renderer implementation
            # Assuming a function render_sar_intensity that integrates sigma_k0 along the ray
            intensity = render_sar_intensity(sigma_k0, view_params['ray_length'])
            
            synthesized.append(intensity)
            observed.append(images[m])
            
        return torch.stack(synthesized), torch.stack(observed)

    def total_variation(self, B):
        """
        Total variation regularization on B.
        """
        # Compute gradient of B in x and y directions
        # B is a 2D map
        B_grad_x = B[:, 1:] - B[:, :-1]
        B_grad_y = B[1:, :] - B[:-1, :]
        return torch.mean(torch.abs(B_grad_x)) + torch.mean(torch.abs(B_grad_y))

    def train_step(self, images, view_params):
        """
        Perform one training step.
        """
        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        
        # Render a minibatch
        synthesized, observed = self.render_line(
            select_random_lines(images, M=80), 
            view_params, 
            images
        )
        
        # Compute Negative Log-Likelihood Loss
        # L_D = (1/(M*NR)) * sum( I/I_hat + log(I_hat/I) )
        ld_loss = torch.mean(observed / synthesized + torch.log(synthesized / observed))
        
        # Compute Total Variation Loss on B
        # Need to extract B from the MLP output for the current batch
        # This requires re-running the forward pass or storing B
        # For simplicity, assume we have access to B from the render step
        # In practice, B is computed inside render_line
        # We need to pass B back or recompute it
        # Let's assume we recompute B for the loss calculation
        # (In a real implementation, B would be returned from render_line)
        B = get_backscattering_from_render_step() # Placeholder for actual retrieval
        ltv_loss = self.total_variation(B)
        
        # Total Loss
        loss = ld_loss + self.lambda_tv * ltv_loss
        
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()
        
        # Anneal beta
        if self.beta < 1.0:
            self.beta += 0.01 # Example annealing schedule
            
        return loss.item()

    def get_dsm_and_backscattering(self, grid_resolution):
        """
        Extract the final DSM and backscattering map.
        """
        # Create a grid of (x, y) coordinates
        x = torch.linspace(-self.L/2, self.L/2, grid_resolution)
        y = torch.linspace(-self.L/2, self.L/2, grid_resolution)
        X, Y = torch.meshgrid(x, y)
        positions = torch.stack([X.flatten(), Y.flatten()], dim=-1)
        
        # Encode and predict
        pos_encoded = self.pos_encoding(positions)
        pos_encoded_windowed = pos_encoded # Assuming final training has beta=1 and no windowing needed for inference
        mlp_output = self.mlp(pos_encoded_windowed)
        
        Z = self.activation_z(mlp_output[:, 0])
        B = self.activation_b(mlp_output[:, 1])
        
        return Z.view(grid_resolution, grid_resolution), B.view(grid_resolution, grid_resolution)
```

*   $\lambda$: Weight for Total Variation regularization, set to 0.1 [S1].
*   $M$: Number of lines per minibatch, $M > 80$ [S1].
*   $K_f$: Target number of samples per line. Not given in the sources.
*   $\mu_f$: Target accuracy. Not given in the sources.
*   $\beta_0$: Initial subsampling factor. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Limited Views:** Reconstruction from only ascending/descending passes (2 views) shows higher RMSE due to a grainier heightmap from slight over-fitting to speckle, caused by reduced speckle realizations per spatial position [S1].
*   **Shadows vs. Submerged Areas:** Cliffs create large shadow areas similar to submerged areas, but the model does not confuse them: submerged areas are reconstructed with low backscattering coefficients, while cliff shadows are handled correctly [S1].
*   **Speckle Noise:** The negative log-likelihood loss accounts for the distribution of speckle, allowing training on noisy images [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method reduces sampling degrees of freedom from 3 (volume) to 2 (surface), leading to faster and easier reconstruction compared to volume rendering methods [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   Forez scene (5 views, 75m resolution, uniform material): RMSE 36.7m [S1].
    *   Forez scene (2 views, 75m resolution, uniform material): RMSE 52.9m [S1].
    *   Island scene (5 views, 0.75m resolution, multiple materials): RMSE 4.72m [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Successfully retrieves neural elevation models from only a few images and correctly separates geometry from texture [S1]. Handles multiple materials and complex scenes (e.g., cliffs, water) effectively [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance degrades with fewer views (e.g., 2 views) [S1]. Does not retrieve a precise map of materials, though demarcation can be observed on flatter terrain [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced in a paper by [S1] to demonstrate the ability of a simplified differentiable SAR intensity rendering model to retrieve DSMs from only a few images. It builds on the principles of inverse rendering and differentiable volume rendering, adapting them to the specific constraints and noise characteristics of SAR data. The method establishes results beyond self-inversion through experiments on EMPRISE® simulations, a step towards reconstructions from real-world SAR data [S1].
