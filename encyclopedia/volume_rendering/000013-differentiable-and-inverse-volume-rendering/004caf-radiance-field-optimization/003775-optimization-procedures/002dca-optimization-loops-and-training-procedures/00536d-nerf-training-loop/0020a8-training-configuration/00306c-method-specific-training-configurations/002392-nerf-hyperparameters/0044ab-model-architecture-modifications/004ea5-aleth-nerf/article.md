# Aleth-NeRF

Also called: Aleth-NeRF with Concealing Field Assumption

## Overview
Aleth-NeRF is a NeRF variant that trains on under- and over-exposed images to render normal-light novel views by introducing a 'Concealing Field' assumption, where air particles attenuate emission rather than objects lacking emission [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF by modeling illumination adaptive volume rendering through local and global concealing fields to suppress light transport, enabling synthesis of normal-light views from low-light or over-exposed inputs without paired training data [S1]. Unlike RAW-NeRF which requires 16-bit HDR RAW images, Aleth-NeRF operates on standard 8-bit LDR images and trains end-to-end without pre-processing or post-processing enhancement networks [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images captured under adverse lighting conditions (low-light or over-exposed). The implementation uses 8-bit LDR images [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Novel views rendered in normal-light conditions [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method extends the standard two-MLP NeRF architecture [S6, unofficial] with specific concealing field parameters:
1.  **Density MLP ($F_\sigma$):** Takes 3D position $\mathbf{r}(i)$ and outputs density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ and a hidden feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ [S1].
2.  **Color MLP ($F_c$):** Takes $\mathbf{h}$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}$ to output color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
3.  **Local Concealing Field ($\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i))$):** A learnable field derived from the output of $F_\sigma$. It is generated by applying a large kernel convolution layer of size 7 to the output of $F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ [S1].
    *   Layout: Same spatial resolution as the density output per sample point.
    *   Activation: Implicitly regularized via the convolution and subsequent multiplication in transmittance.
4.  **Global Concealing Field ($\Theta_G(i)$):** A set of learnable scalar parameters corresponding to the camera distance index $i$ for all camera rays. It remains constant within a scene and is independent of voxels [S1].
    *   Layout: Array of size $N_{samples}$ (or max depth), initialized to 0.5 [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Positional Encoding:** Encode 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}$ using standard positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ [S1, S6, unofficial].
2.  **Density and Feature Prediction:** Pass encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ through $F_\sigma$ to obtain density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ and hidden feature $\mathbf{h}$ [S1].
    $$ \sigma(\mathbf{r}(i)), \mathbf{h} = F_\sigma(\gamma(\mathbf{r}(i))) $$
3.  **Local Concealing Field Generation:** Apply a convolution with kernel size 7 to the density output to generate the local concealing field $\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i))$ [S1].
    $$ \Omega(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \text{Conv}_{7}(\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))) $$
    *Note: The source describes this as `convsize:7 (Fσ (r(i))) → Ω(r(i))` [S1].*
4.  **Color Prediction:** Pass $\mathbf{h}$, $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ through $F_c$ to obtain color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    $$ \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d}) = F_c(\mathbf{h}, \gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d})) $$
5.  **Transmittance Calculation with Concealing Fields:** Calculate the accumulated transmittance $T_{conceal}(\mathbf{r}(i))$ along the ray. This modifies the standard NeRF transmittance by multiplying the local $\Omega$ and global $\Theta_G$ fields [S1].
    $$ T_{conceal}(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(j)) \cdot \delta\right) \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} \Omega(\mathbf{r}(j)) \Theta_G(j) $$
    Where $\delta$ is the constant distance between adjacent sample points under uniform sampling [S1].
6.  **Rendering Mode Selection:**
    *   **For Low-Light Training:**
        *   Generate adverse-light prediction $\hat{C}_{adv}$ using $T_{conceal}$ [S1].
        *   Generate normal-light prediction $\hat{C}_{nor}$ using standard transmittance $T$ (i.e., set $\Omega=1, \Theta_G=1$) [S1].
    *   **For Over-Exposed Training:**
        *   Generate very-bright prediction $\hat{C}_{adv}$ using standard transmittance $T$ [S1].
        *   Generate normal-light prediction $\hat{C}_{nor}$ using $T_{conceal}$ [S1].
7.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $L$ (see Formulas below) and backpropagate to update $F_\sigma$, $F_c$, $\Omega$, and $\Theta_G$ [S1].

**Formulas:**

*   **Inverse Tone Curve $\Phi(x)$:** Used to re-balance pixel weights in the regression loss to handle the imbalance between dark and bright pixels [S1].
    $$ \Phi(x) = \frac{1}{3} \left( \sin^{-1}(1 - 2x) - \sin\left(\frac{1 - 2x}{2}\right) \right) $$
    *   $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$ is added to inputs to facilitate accurate estimations [S1].

*   **Inverse Tone MSE Loss ($L_{it-mse}$):**
    $$ L_{it-mse} = \sum_{r} \| \Phi(\hat{C}(r) + \epsilon) - \Phi(C(r) + \epsilon) \|^2 $$
    Where $\hat{C}$ is the predicted image and $C$ is the ground truth image [S1].

*   **Degree Loss ($L_{de}$):** Controls the enhancement degree by comparing the global average of the predicted normal-light image to a target degree $e$ [S1].
    $$ L_{de} = \| \text{avgpool}(\hat{C}_{nor}) - e \|^2 $$
    *   $e = 0.45$ is the hyperparameter signifying the enhancement degree [S1].

*   **Contrast Degree Loss ($L_{co}$):** Maintains structural similarity and controls contrast between adjacent sampling pixels of predicted normal-light and ground truth adverse-light images [S1].
    $$ L_{co} = \sum_{r} \sum_{k \in \{+1, -1\}} \| \hat{C}_{nor}(r) - \hat{C}_{adv}(r+k) \|^2 $$
    *   In over-exposure scenes, the product $e \cdot \eta$ is fixed to 0.5, where $\eta$ controls contrast [S1].

*   **Color Constancy Loss ($L_{cc}$):** Regularizes color based on the gray-world assumption to prevent color imbalance when removing/adding concealing fields [S1].
    $$ L_{cc} = \sum_{r} \sum_{p,q} (\hat{C}_{nor}(r)_p - \hat{C}_{nor}(r)_q)^2 $$
    Where $p, q$ denote different color channels [S1].

*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{it-mse} + \lambda_1 L_{de} + \lambda_2 L_{co} + \lambda_3 L_{cc} $$
    *   $\lambda_1 = 10^{-3}$ [S1]
    *   $\lambda_2 = 10^{-3}$ [S1]
    *   $\lambda_3 = 10^{-8}$ [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Learning Rate:** $5 \times 10^{-4}$ with Adam optimizer and cosine decay every 2500 iterations [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 4096 [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 62,500 total [S1].
*   **Global Field Initialization:** $\Theta_G(i)$ initialized to 0.5 for each camera distance $i$ [S1].
*   **Enhancement Degree ($e$):** 0.45 [S1].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
*   **MSE Imbalance:** Standard MSE loss causes imbalance where dark pixels contribute minimally and bright pixels dominate. This is solved by using the inverse tone curve $\Phi$ [S1].
*   **Post-processing Failure:** Applying enhancement methods to NeRF results ('NeRF + *') underperforms pre-processing ('* + NeRF') because NeRF produces poor-quality dark images that struggle enhancement methods [S1].
*   **Color Imbalance:** Directly removing/adding concealing fields may cause color imbalance, necessitating the Color Constancy Loss [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Similar to standard NeRF, dominated by the two MLP evaluations and ray marching. The additional convolution for the local concealing field adds a small constant overhead per sample or per feature map, depending on implementation. The global field adds negligible memory [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on the LOM dataset (5 scenes: 'buu', 'chair', 'sofa', 'bike', 'shrub') with images down-sampled to 375x500. Trained for 62,500 iterations with batch size 4096 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for standard NeRF scales. The convolution kernel size (7) is fixed [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles low-light and over-exposed inputs without paired training data or pre/post-processing. Outperforms "NeRF + Enhancement" and "Enhancement + NeRF" pipelines in perceptual similarity (LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption that darkness is caused by concealing fields (air particles) rather than object emission. May not generalize to scenes where the physical assumption of "concealing" does not hold [S1].

## Background
Aleth-NeRF was introduced by Ziteng Cui, Lin Gu, Xiao Sun, Xianzheng Ma, Yu Qiao, and Tatsuya Harada at The University of Tokyo, Shanghai AI Laboratory, RIKEN AIP, and University of Oxford [S1]. It was published in the Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1]. The method is motivated by the ancient Greek emission theory, which posits that visual perception is formed by visual rays emitted from the eye, and darkness is caused by particles between the object and the eye concealing the emission [S1]. The implementation uses the open-source PyTorch toolbox NeRF-Factory [S1].
