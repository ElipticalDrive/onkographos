# NeRF Hyperparameters

Also called: NeRF Training Hyperparameters, ViCA-NeRF Training

## Overview
NeRF hyperparameters define the configuration of the optimization loop, network architecture, and sampling strategies required to train a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) from multi-view images. This method specifies the precise numerical values, data structures, and procedural steps for initializing, training, and evaluating standard and variant NeRF models. It sets itself apart from general optimization procedures by providing the concrete "recipe" for convergence, including specific learning rates, sample counts, and architectural dimensions that distinguish implementations like Mip-NeRF, FastNeRF, and Strata-NeRF.

## How it works
The training process minimizes the difference between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images by optimizing neural network parameters. The core loop involves ray sampling, point sampling, feature encoding, network forward pass, volume rendering, loss computation, and optimization.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training images, camera intrinsics (focal length), camera extrinsics (poses), and dataset metadata (near/far clipping planes).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$), and optionally a cached density field or mesh for acceleration.

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Bundle:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ for origins and $[B, 3]$ for directions, where $B$ is the batch size in rays.
*   **Query Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, N, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates along rays, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Depth Values:** A tensor of shape $[B, N]$ containing the distance from the camera origin to each query point.
*   **Render Kwargs:** A dictionary containing:
    *   `network_fn`: The coarse NeRF MLP.
    *   `network_fine`: The fine NeRF MLP (if using coarse-to-fine).
    *   `N_samples`: Number of coarse samples per ray.
    *   `N_importance`: Number of additional fine samples per ray.
    *   `perturb`: Jitter factor for sampling (float).
    *   `raw_noise_std`: Standard deviation of noise added to $\sigma$ output (float).
    *   `white_bkgd`: Boolean for background color assumption.
    *   `use_viewdirs`: Boolean for view-dependent color.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Data Pre-processing:**
    *   Normalize camera center coordinates to the range $[-1, 1]$ to ensure reliable training with positional encoding [S10].
    *   Normalize image pixel values by dividing by 255.0 [S8, unofficial].
    *   Reserve one image for validation/testing (e.g., index 99 or 101) and slice the remaining images for training [S8, unofficial].
    *   For large-scale scenes, use K-Means clustering of camera centers to partition into sub-modules (e.g., for Mega-NeRF) [S10].

2.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize the Adam optimizer with model parameters. Typical defaults: $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, $\epsilon=10^{-7}$ [S4, S7, S8, unofficial].
    *   Initialize MLP weights. Standard NeRF uses an 8-layer MLP with 256 or 512 hidden units for position and a 4-layer MLP with 128 hidden units for direction [S7, unofficial].
    *   For Mip-NeRF 360 variants, initialize with the "360.gin" configuration which includes a contract function mapping coordinates to a ball of radius 2 [S12].

3.  **Ray and Point Sampling:**
    *   Generate rays from training images using camera intrinsics and extrinsics [S8, unofficial].
    *   Sample points along rays.
        *   **Uniform/Stratified:** Sample $N$ points linearly or stratified between near and far clipping planes.
        *   **Coarse-to-Fine:** Sample $N_{coarse}$ points (e.g., 64) uniformly, query the coarse network, then sample $N_{fine}$ points (e.g., 128) using importance sampling based on the coarse density [S7, S11, unofficial].
        *   **Mip-NeRF 360:** Use 64 coarse and 128 fine samples for NeRF 360 Synthetic; 64 coarse and 64 fine for LLFF [S7, unofficial].
        *   **Proposal Networks:** Use a proposal MLP (4 layers, 256 hidden units) to generate 8 samples for importance weighting, followed by 32 samples for the main NeRF MLP [S1].

4.  **Feature Encoding:**
    *   Apply Positional Encoding (PE) to 3D coordinates and viewing directions.
    *   **Standard NeRF:** 10 frequencies for position, 4 for direction [S6, S7, unofficial].
    *   **FastNeRF Variant:** Reduce direction encoding frequency to $L=1$ to mitigate view-dependent flickering artifacts [S7, unofficial].
    *   **Mip-NeRF 360:** Use axis-aligned IPE (Interval Positional Encoding) [S1].

5.  **Network Forward Pass:**
    *   Query the MLP(s) with encoded coordinates and directions.
    *   Output volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ (RGB).
    *   **Noise Regularization:** Add Gaussian noise with standard deviation `raw_noise_std` to $\sigma$ during training to prevent overfitting [S11, unofficial].

6.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute accumulated color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using the differentiable volume rendering integral:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
        where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1][S6, unofficial].
    *   **Tone Mapping:** For motion-blurred scenes (DP-NeRF), apply a tone mapping function (gamma function) to predicted radiance before blur operation [S3].

7.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Photometric Loss:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) or L1 loss between $\hat{C}$ and ground truth $C_{gt}$ [S1, S9].
    *   **Perceptual Loss:** LPIPS or VGG-19 feature distance for enhanced NeRFs (RoGUENeRF) [S9].
    *   **Coarse-to-Fine Weighting:** Apply weights $\lambda_s = 0.9$ and $\lambda_e = 0.1$ to coarse and fine losses respectively [S3].
    *   **Regularization:** Distortion loss (disabled for Blender datasets in Strata-NeRF) [S12].

8.  **Optimization:**
    *   Update parameters using Adam optimizer.
    *   **Learning Rate Schedule:**
        *   **Standard:** Exponential decay from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S4, unofficial] or $8 \times 10^{-5}$ [S3].
        *   **Strata-NeRF:** Log-linear interpolation from 0.002 to 0.00002 with 512 warmup steps [S12].
        *   **Pix2NeRF:** Progressive training. Start at 32x32 resolution with LR $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (generator), then increase resolution to 64x64 with LR $2 \times 10^{-4}$ [S14].
    *   **Iterations:**
        *   Standard NeRF: 200k–400k iterations [S3][S6, S7, unofficial].
        *   FastNeRF: 300k iterations [S7, unofficial].
        *   RoGUENeRF: 3000 epochs pre-training, 1 hour fine-tuning per scene [S9].
        *   ViCA-NeRF: 30,000 iterations pre-training, 35,000 post-refinement [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
def train_nerf(dataset, config):
    # 1. Initialization
    model = NeRFMLP(
        net_depth=config.net_depth,      # Default: 8
        net_width=config.net_width,      # Default: 256
        dir_enc_freqs=config.dir_enc_freqs, # Default: 4
        pos_enc_freqs=config.pos_enc_freqs  # Default: 10
    )
    optimizer = Adam(model.parameters(), lr=config.lr, beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, eps=1e-7)
    
    # 2. Data Loading
    images = load_dataset(dataset.path, mode='train')
    images = images / 255.0  # Normalize
    val_image = images[99]   # Reserve for validation
    train_images = images[:98]
    
    # 3. Training Loop
    for iteration in range(config.num_iters): # Default: 4096 (example) or 200k
        # Ray Sampling
        ray_origins, ray_directions = get_ray_bundle(
            height=dataset.height, 
            width=dataset.width, 
            focal_length=dataset.focal_length,
            tform_cam2world=dataset.poses[iteration % len(dataset.poses)]
        )
        
        # Point Sampling (Coarse)
        query_points, depth_values = compute_query_points_from_rays(
            ray_origins, ray_directions, 
            near_thresh=config.near_thresh, # Default: 2.0
            far_thresh=config.far_thresh,   # Default: 6.0
            depth_samples_per_ray=config.n_samples_coarse # Default: 64
        )
        
        # Chunked Forward Pass (to avoid OOM)
        chunksize = config.chunksize # Default: 4096
        rgb_coarse, sigma_coarse = render_rays(
            query_points, ray_directions, 
            network_fn=model, 
            N_samples=config.n_samples_coarse,
            perturb=config.perturb, # Default: 1.0
            raw_noise_std=config.raw_noise_std # Default: 0.0
        )
        
        # Optional: Fine Sampling (if config.n_samples_fine > 0)
        if config.n_samples_fine > 0:
            # Importance sampling based on sigma_coarse
            query_points_fine, depth_values_fine = importance_sample(
                sigma_coarse, depth_values, config.n_samples_fine
            )
            rgb_fine, sigma_fine = render_rays(
                query_points_fine, ray_directions,
                network_fn=model,
                N_samples=config.n_samples_fine,
                perturb=0.0, # No perturbation for fine pass usually
                raw_noise_std=0.0
            )
            # Combine coarse and fine losses
            loss = compute_loss(rgb_coarse, rgb_fine, gt_image)
        else:
            loss = compute_loss(rgb_coarse, gt_image)
            
        # Optimization Step
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # Learning Rate Decay
        current_lr = decay_lr(config.lr, iteration, config.decay_start)
        for param_group in optimizer.param_groups:
            param_group['lr'] = current_lr
            
        # Logging
        if iteration % config.display_every == 0:
            log_stats(loss, iteration)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **View-Dependent Artifacts:** FastNeRF exhibits flickering on synthetic datasets due to overfitting of the view-dependent MLP. Mitigate by reducing direction Fourier encoding dimensionality to $L=1$ or using a smaller direction-dependent cache ($16^3$ to $64^3$) [S7, unofficial].
*   **Scene Scale:** The magnitude of optimized density $\sigma$ is tied to the domain of integration. Scaling the scene requires inversely scaling density (Alpha Invariance) [S10127-alpha-invariance-inverse-scaling.md].
*   **Background Omission:** In nadir camera perspectives (aerial imagery), background NeRF can be omitted as sky pixels are not visible [S10].
*   **Test Rendering:** Disable perturbation and raw noise for test rendering to ensure deterministic output [S11, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md)
NeRF Initialization Strategies address the slow convergence and instability inherent in training classical Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) from random weight initialization. By leveraging pre-computed weights from related models or geometric priors, these methods significantly reduce the number of training epochs required to achieve high-quality rendering. Unlike standard random initialization, which often leads to local minima or requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, initialization strategies provide a geometrically or structurally informed starting point, trading off initial setup time for faster convergence. See [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md).

### [Mega-NeRF Training Configuration](8766-mega-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Mega-NeRF is a training configuration for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) designed to accelerate training and reduce model query costs for large-scale scenes. It differs from standard NeRF by using a single shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for both coarse and fine sampling stages, which reduces model size and saves 25% of model queries per ray by reusing coarse outputs during the fine rendering stage. See [Mega-NeRF Training Configuration](8766-mega-nerf-training-configuration.md).

### [Alpha Invariance / Inverse Scaling](10127-alpha-invariance-inverse-scaling.md)
Alpha invariance is a property in neural volume rendering where the opacity (alpha value) of a discretized segment remains constant despite changes in the scene's physical scale. It solves the problem that the magnitude of the optimized volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is intrinsically tied to the domain of integration (distance along the ray), meaning that scaling the scene requires inversely scaling the density to preserve the visual appearance of local geometry. Unlike Multi-Plane Images (MPIs) which fix discretization in advance, NeRF's continuous density parameterization allows flexible resampling but requires this specific handling to maintain robust view synthesis across arbitrary scene scales. See [Alpha Invariance / Inverse Scaling](10127-alpha-invariance-inverse-scaling.md).

See [Batch size scaling for computational parity](10942-batch-size-scaling-for-computational-parity.md).

### [Model Architecture Modifications](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md)
Model Architecture Modifications alter the fundamental structure of the Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation—specifically the Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), input conditioning, or output heads—to address limitations in standard NeRF regarding geometry, efficiency, dynamic scenes, or specific physical phenomena. Unlike standard NeRF, which maps 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color via a static MLP, these modifications introduce auxiliary inputs (e.g., time, appearance embeddings), auxiliary outputs (e.g., semantics, uncertainty), or substitute the continuous function with discrete structures (e.g., grids, SDFs). See [Model Architecture Modifications](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md).

### [Regularization Techniques](11314-regularization-techniques.md)
Regularization techniques in NeRF optimization are strategies designed to mitigate artifacts such as floaters, blur, and geometric instability, particularly in sparse-view or few-shot settings where the inverse problem is ill-posed. Unlike standard hyperparameters that control convergence speed, these techniques modify the loss landscape or the representation capacity during training to enforce smoother geometry, consistency between coarse and fine sampling, or physical plausibility. They set themselves apart from sibling techniques like initialization strategies or architecture modifications by actively constraining the optimization trajectory or the gradient flow rather than just setting the starting point or the model capacity. See [Regularization Techniques](11314-regularization-techniques.md).

### [Exponential Learning Rate Decay with Delay](12969-exponential-learning-rate-decay-with-delay.md)
A continuous learning rate decay function that interpolates between initial and final learning rates, with an optional delay period at the start. See [Exponential Learning Rate Decay with Delay](12969-exponential-learning-rate-decay-with-delay.md).

### [Adaptive Time-step Training Framework](13342-adaptive-time-step-training-framework.md)
Adaptive Time-step Training (PATA) is a training strategy for spike-based Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that reduces inference time steps by learning a scene-wise target time step $t^*$ while maintaining rendering quality through distillation and loss functions. Unlike sample-level or layer-level dynamic inference strategies which cause irregular execution, PATA uses a scene-wise temporal budget to preserve the parallel rendering pattern required by dense ray sampling. It converts time-step selection from a manually tuned hyperparameter into a learned scene-wise budget, reducing temporal computation while preserving rendering behavior. See [Adaptive Time-step Training Framework](13342-adaptive-time-step-training-framework.md).

### [BAD-NeRF](13523-bad-nerf.md)
BAD-NeRF is a method for 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis from motion-blurred images that jointly optimizes the Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) parameters and the camera motion trajectories during the shutter exposure interval. Unlike standard NeRF which assumes sharp images with infinitesimal exposure times, BAD-NeRF models the physical image formation process by rendering a sequence of sharp intermediate views along a continuous SE(3) trajectory and averaging them to synthesize the observed blur. It distinguishes itself from methods like DeblurNeRF, which rely on fixed camera poses (often inaccurate due to blur), by estimating the start and end poses of each image's exposure jointly with the scene representation, making it robust to severe motion blur and inaccurate initial pose estimates. See [BAD-NeRF](13523-bad-nerf.md).

### [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md)
This family defines the structural components and embedding mechanisms used within NeRF training configurations to map spatial coordinates and viewing directions to radiance field properties. It addresses the problem of how to represent the continuous scene function efficiently and accurately, distinguishing between standard MLP-based encodings, hash-based sparse representations, and architectural modifications for specific priors like depth or semantic style. See [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md).

### NeRF Training Configuration for View-Dependent Artifacts
Mitigates direction-dependent flickering artifacts on synthetic datasets caused by overfitting of the view-dependent MLP. Choose a direction-dependent cache resolution between $16^3$ and $64^3$, which uses interpolation at lookup by default. Alternatively, adjust the Fourier feature encoding of view directions at training time by setting $L=1$ (instead of $L=4$ used in the baseline). Using an 8-layer 256 hidden unit MLP for position and a 4-layer 128 MLP for direction, setting $L=1$ yields an average PSNR of 29.748dB on synthetic data, compared to 29.646dB for $L=2$ and 29.449dB for $L=3$. Smaller caches have negligible impact on metrics, occasionally improving them (e.g., Drums $23.745 \rightarrow 23.836$ dB) [S7, unofficial].

### RoGUENeRF Training and Loss
Pre-train on LLFF dataset using NeRF renders for 3000 epochs (~5 days), then fine-tune on new scenes/models for 1 hour per scene. Use random crops of size 512x512, batch size 4, learning rate $1 \times 10^{-4}$, and Adam optimizer on 4x Nvidia V100 GPUs. The loss function is $L = |\hat{H}_i - H_i|_1 + 10^{-3}|\omega(\hat{H}_i) - \omega(H_i)|_1$, where $\hat{H}_i$ is the predicted enhanced image, $H_i$ is the ground truth, and $\omega()$ is a pre-trained VGG-19 network [S9].

### NeRF Pre-processing
Normalize all camera center coordinates to the range $[-1, 1]$ for reliable training of the NeRF MLP based on positional encoding. Use K-Means clustering of camera centers to partition into sub-modules for Mega-NeRF and Block-NeRF. A nadir camera perspective allows the omission of background NeRF because sky pixels are not visible [S10].

### Coarse/Fine Rendering Pipeline Setup
Configures the rendering kwargs to support multi-resolution sampling (coarse and fine networks) and noise regularization for sigma. Create `render_kwargs_train` dict with `network_query_fn`, `perturb`, `N_importance`, `network_fine`, `N_samples`, `network_fn`, `use_viewdirs`, `white_bkgd`, `raw_noise_std`. If `args.dataset_type != 'llff' or args.no_ndc`, set `ndc=False` and `lindisp=args.lindisp`. Copy train kwargs to `render_kwargs_test`, overriding `perturb=False` and `raw_noise_std=0.`. Test rendering disables perturbation and raw noise to ensure deterministic output. Default `N_samples` is 64, `N_importance` is 0, `perturb` is 1.0, `raw_noise_std` is 0.0 [S11, unofficial].

### Strata-NeRF Training Configuration
Uses Adam optimizer with $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, $\epsilon=10^{-6}$, and initial learning rate 0.002. Learning rate schedule is log-linear interpolation from 0.002 down to 0.00002 at maximum steps, with 512 warmup steps. Loss weighting for the Latent Generator is $\lambda_2=0.1$. Codebook sizes tested: 512, 1024, 4096. Size 1024 yields the best overall PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across Coffee Shop, Bhutanese House, and Buddhist Temple datasets. Distortion loss from Mip-NeRF 360 is disabled for Blender datasets to prevent artifacts. Uses the "360.gin" configuration instead of "blender 256.gin" because the contract function handles large scenes better, yielding higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS [S12].

### Learned Exposure Scaling
A mechanism to learn exposure scaling offsets for RawNeRF, parameterized as an offset from 1, constrained to be zero for exposure index 0. If `learned_exposure_scaling` is True, create embedding `exposure_scaling_offsets = nn.Embed(max_num_exposures, features=3, embedding_init=jax.nn.initializers.zeros, name='exposure_scaling_offsets')`. During rendering, if `rays.exposure_idx is not None`, compute `mask = exposure_idx > 0`. Compute `scaling = 1 + mask[..., None] * exposure_scaling_offsets(exposure_idx)`. Multiply RGB by scaling. Default `learned_exposure_scaling` is False [S13, unofficial].

### Pix2NeRF Training Configuration
Progressive training starts at 32x32 resolution with 96 sampled points per ray. At 32x32, learning rates are set to $4 \times 10^{-5}$ (discriminator), $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (generator), and $4 \times 10^{-4}$ (encoder). Resolution is increased to 64x64, updating learning rates to $2 \times 10^{-5}$ (discriminator), $2 \times 10^{-4}$ (generator), and $2 \times 10^{-4}$ (encoder). Trained on 64x64 but rendered at 128x128, yielding a super-resolution effect compared to models trained directly on 128x128. Uses Adam optimizer for 300k iterations, batch size 48 [S14].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time scales with the number of iterations, batch size (in rays), and scene complexity. Standard NeRF takes ~12–15 hours on a single GPU for 200k iterations on synthetic scenes [S6, unofficial]. Mip-NeRF 360 variants are significantly faster to train than standard Mip-NeRF, with relative speedup increasing as model capacity rises (e.g., 1.86 hrs vs 2.89 hrs for 256 hidden units) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is dominated by the MLP weights and the batch of rays processed. Chunking (e.g., 4096 points per batch) is used to avoid out-of-memory errors during the forward pass [S8, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On the Blender dataset with 256 hidden units, Mip-NeRF achieves PSNR 33.09, SSIM 0.961, LPIPS 0.043 in 2.89 hours [S1]. On the Lego scene, PSNR is ~31–32 dB after 200k iterations [S6, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** The hyperparameter configurations provided allow for reproducible training of high-quality NeRFs. Variants like FastNeRF and Strata-NeRF offer specific optimizations for artifacts and large scenes.
*   **Limitations:** Training is computationally expensive and sensitive to hyperparameters like learning rate and sample counts. Overfitting can occur with high-capacity models without regularization [S7, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Use standard NeRF hyperparameters for small, bounded scenes (Blender). Use Mip-NeRF 360 configurations for unbounded, large-scale scenes. Use FastNeRF configurations if view-dependent flickering is observed.

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1]. The method uses a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to represent a continuous 5D function mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to volume density and color. The training process involves differentiable volume rendering and gradient-based optimization. Subsequent works like Mip-NeRF [S1], FastNeRF [S7, unofficial], and Strata-NeRF [S12] have refined the hyperparameters and training procedures to address issues such as anti-aliasing, training speed, and view-dependent artifacts.
