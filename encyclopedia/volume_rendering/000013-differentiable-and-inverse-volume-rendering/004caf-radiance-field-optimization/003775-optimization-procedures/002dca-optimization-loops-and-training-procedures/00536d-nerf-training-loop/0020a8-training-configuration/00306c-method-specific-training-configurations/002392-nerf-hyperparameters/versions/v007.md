# NeRF Hyperparameters

Also called: Blender Training Configuration, DP-NeRF Training Configuration, FastNeRF Training Configuration, NeRF Model and Sampling Configuration, NeRF Training Hyperparameters, NeTF Training Configuration, TinyNeRF Training Setup, Training Loop Configuration

## Overview
NeRF Hyperparameters define the configuration of the training loop, model architecture, and optimization strategy for Neural Radiance Fields. They solve the problem of converging an implicit volumetric representation to a high-fidelity 3D scene from 2D images. What sets this configuration apart from its siblings is the specific combination of hierarchical sampling (coarse/fine networks), positional encoding frequencies, and learning rate schedules that balance rendering quality with computational cost [S1, S2][S4, S6, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A dataset of images with known camera poses (extrinsics) and intrinsics (focal length, principal point). Ground truth pixel colors.
*   **Outputs:** A trained MLP (or hash grid) mapping 3D coordinates and view directions to volume density ($\sigma$) and view-dependent color ($\mathbf{c}$).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Bundle:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ for origins and $[B, 3]$ for directions, where $B$ is the batch size (number of rays).
*   **Query Points:** For each ray, a tensor of shape $[B, N]$ containing 3D coordinates, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Model Parameters:** Weights and biases for the MLP layers.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Data Loading and Preprocessing:**
    *   Load images and camera parameters [S6, unofficial].
    *   Normalize image pixel values to $[0, 1]$ by dividing by 255.0 [S6, unofficial].
    *   Normalize camera center coordinates to the range $[-1, 1]$ to ensure reliable training of positional encoding [S10].
    *   Reserve one image for validation/testing [S6, unofficial].

2.  **Ray Sampling:**
    *   For each training iteration, sample $B$ rays from the training images [S2][S4, unofficial].
    *   Sample $N_{coarse}$ points along each ray between near and far clipping planes [S4, S12, unofficial].
    *   Sample $N_{fine}$ additional points using importance sampling based on the coarse network's density [S1][S4, unofficial].

3.  **Network Query (Forward Pass):**
    *   Apply Positional Encoding (PE) to the 3D coordinates and view directions [S4, unofficial].
        *   Standard NeRF uses $L_p$ frequencies for position and $L_d$ for direction [S4, unofficial].
        *   FastNeRF may use $L=1$ for direction to mitigate view-dependent artifacts [S5, unofficial].
    *   Pass encoded coordinates through the Coarse MLP to get $\sigma_c$ and $\mathbf{c}_c$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   Pass encoded coordinates through the Fine MLP to get $\sigma_f$ and $\mathbf{c}_f$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* In some variants (e.g., Mega-NeRF), a single shared MLP is used for both stages [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ and opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   Render color: $\mathbf{C} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i$ [S4, unofficial].
    *   Apply tone mapping (gamma function) if the blur operation is applied to scene irradiance [S2].

5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute photometric loss (MSE or Charbonnier) between rendered color and ground truth [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   Apply coarse-to-fine optimization weights: $\lambda_s = 0.9$ for coarse loss, $\lambda_e = 0.1$ for fine loss [S2].
    *   Apply regularization terms if specified (e.g., Simple-RF, Asymmetric Normal Loss) [S7, S8].

6.  **Optimization:**
    *   Initialize Adam optimizer [S6, unofficial].
    *   Compute gradients and update weights [S2].
    *   Apply learning rate schedule [S2][S11, unofficial].

**Formulas:**

*   **Volume Rendering Equation:**
    $$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ and $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S4, unofficial].

*   **Simple-RF Loss Function:**
    $$ L = \lambda_m L_m + \lambda_a L_a + \lambda_{sd} L_{sd} + \lambda_{aug} L_{aug} + \lambda_{cfc} L_{cfc} + \lambda_{mc} L_{mc} $$
    where $L_m$ is the main photometric loss, $L_a$ is augmentation loss, $L_{sd}$ is smoothness/depth loss, $L_{aug}$ is augmentation loss, $L_{cfc}$ is consistency/contrastive loss, and $L_{mc}$ is multi-cycle consistency loss [S7].

*   **Asymmetric Predicted Normal Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} = \lambda_n \mathcal{L}_p(w, \mathbf{n}, \text{sg}(\tilde{\mathbf{n}})) + \lambda_{\tilde{n}} \mathcal{L}_p(\text{sg}(w), \text{sg}(\mathbf{n}), \tilde{\mathbf{n}}) $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_p(w, \mathbf{n}, \tilde{\mathbf{n}}) = \sum_{i=1}^N w^{(i)} \|\mathbf{n}^{(i)} - \tilde{\mathbf{n}}^{(i)}\|^2$, $\mathbf{n}$ are geometry normals, $\tilde{\mathbf{n}}$ are predicted normals, $w$ are rendering weights, and $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator [S8].

*   **Exponential Learning Rate Decay with Delay:**
    $$ t = \text{clip}\left(\frac{\text{step}}{\text{max\_steps}}, 0, 1\right) $$
    $$ \text{log\_lerp} = \exp(\log(\text{lr\_init}) \cdot (1 - t) + \log(\text{lr\_final}) \cdot t) $$
    $$ \text{delay\_rate} = \text{lr\_delay\_mult} + (1 - \text{lr\_delay\_mult}) \cdot \sin\left(0.5 \cdot \pi \cdot \text{clip}\left(\frac{\text{step}}{\text{lr\_delay\_steps}}, 0, 1\right)\right) $$
    $$ \text{lr} = \text{delay\_rate} \cdot \text{log\_lerp} $$
    [S11, unofficial].

**Parameters with Typical Values:**

*   **Samples per Ray:**
    *   Coarse ($N_{coarse}$): 64 [S2][S4, S5, S12, unofficial].
    *   Fine ($N_{fine}$): 64 (LLFF) or 128 (NeRF 360 Synthetic) [S5, unofficial].
    *   Mip-NeRF 360 (Blender): 8 proposal, 32 NeRF [S1].
*   **MLP Architecture:**
    *   Depth: 8 layers [S1][S4, S5, unofficial].
    *   Width: 256 hidden units (standard), 512 (larger capacity) [S1][S4, S5, unofficial].
    *   Proposal MLP: 4 layers, 256 hidden units [S1].
*   **Positional Encoding:**
    *   Position frequencies ($L_p$): 10 [S4, unofficial].
    *   Direction frequencies ($L_d$): 4 (standard), 1 (FastNeRF for synthetic data) [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   **Optimizer:**
    *   Adam [S2][S6, unofficial][S8].
    *   $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$ [S2][S5, unofficial].
    *   $\epsilon = 1 \times 10^{-7}$ [S3].
*   **Learning Rate:**
    *   Initial: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S2][S4, S5, S12, unofficial].
    *   Decay: Exponential to $8 \times 10^{-5}$ [S2] or $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S3].
*   **Batch Size:**
    *   Rays per batch: 1024 [S2][S4, unofficial].
    *   Transients: 1 to 4 [S3].
*   **Training Iterations:**
    *   Standard NeRF: 200k to 300k [S2][S5, unofficial].
    *   NeTF: 5 epochs (approx. 10 hours on RTX 3090) [S3].
    *   RoGUENeRF: 3000 epochs pre-training, 1 hour fine-tuning [S9].
*   **Loss Weights (Simple-RF):**
    *   $\lambda_{sd} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{aug} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{cfc} = 0.1$ [S7].
*   **Loss Weights (Asymmetric Normal):**
    *   $\lambda_n = 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_{\tilde{n}} = 0.3$ [S8].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **View-Dependent Artifacts:** Overfitting of the view-dependent MLP can cause flickering in motion. Mitigated by reducing direction encoding frequency ($L=1$) or using a smaller direction-dependent cache ($16^3$ to $64^3$) [S5, unofficial].
*   **Normal Convergence:** Using standard symmetric predicted normal loss tends to oversmooth or underconstrain geometry. Asymmetric loss is required [S8].
*   **Test Rendering:** Test rendering must disable perturbation and raw noise to ensure deterministic output [S12, unofficial].
*   **Background Omission:** Nadir camera perspective allows omission of background NeRF as sky pixels are not visible [S10].
*   **Loss Scheduling:** In Simple-RF, $L_{aug}$ and $L_{cfc}$ should only be imposed after 10k iterations [S7].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim
import numpy as np

class NeRFModel(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, pos_enc_freqs=10, dir_enc_freqs=4, net_depth=8, net_width=256):
        super().__init__()
        self.pos_enc_freqs = pos_enc_freqs
        self.dir_enc_freqs = dir_enc_freqs
        # Simplified MLP structure
        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Linear(3 * 2 * pos_enc_freqs + 3, net_width),
            nn.ReLU()
        ] + [nn.Linear(net_width, net_width) for _ in range(net_depth - 2)] + [
            nn.Linear(net_width, 4) # 3 for color, 1 for density
        ])

    def positional_encoding(self, x, freqs):
        # Standard NeRF PE: sin(2^i * pi * x)
        x = x.unsqueeze(-1)
        scales = torch.tensor([2**i for i in range(freqs)], dtype=x.dtype, device=x.device)
        emb = torch.sin(torch.cat([x * s for s in scales], dim=-1))
        return torch.cat([x, emb], dim=-1)

    def forward(self, xyz, viewdirs):
        # Encode position
        xyz_enc = self.positional_encoding(xyz, self.pos_enc_freqs)
        # Encode direction
        dir_enc = self.positional_encoding(viewdirs, self.dir_enc_freqs)
        
        # Concatenate
        input_vec = torch.cat([xyz_enc, dir_enc], dim=-1)
        
        # Pass through MLP
        h = input_vec
        for i, layer in enumerate(self.layers):
            h = layer(h)
            if i < len(self.layers) - 1:
                h = torch.relu(h)
        
        # Split output
        sigma = h[..., 3:4]
        color = h[..., :3]
        return sigma, color

def render_rays(ray_origins, ray_directions, near, far, model, n_samples, perturb=True):
    # Stratified sampling
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., n_samples, device=ray_origins.device)
    if perturb:
        mids = .5 * (t_vals[1:] + t_vals[:-1])
        t_vals = torch.cat([t_vals[0:1], mids, t_vals[-1:]], dim=0)
        t_vals = t_vals + (torch.rand_like(t_vals) * 1./n_samples)
    
    # Map to depth
    t_vals = near + t_vals * (far - near)
    xyz = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_directions.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # Query model
    sigma, color = model(xyz, ray_directions.unsqueeze(1).expand_as(xyz))
    
    # Volume rendering
    deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
    delta_inf = 1e10 * torch.ones_like(deltas[:, :1])
    deltas = torch.cat([deltas, delta_inf], dim=-1)
    
    alpha = 1. - torch.exp(-sigma.squeeze(-1) * deltas)
    T = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([torch.ones_like(alpha[:, :1]), 1. - alpha + 1e-10], dim=-1), dim=-1)[:, :-1]
    
    weights = alpha * T
    color_rendered = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=1)
    return color_rendered

def train_step(model, optimizer, ray_origins, ray_directions, gt_colors, near, far, n_samples):
    model.train()
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    
    pred_colors = render_rays(ray_origins, ray_directions, near, far, model, n_samples, perturb=True)
    
    # MSE Loss
    loss = torch.mean((pred_colors - gt_colors) ** 2)
    
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    
    return loss.item()

def get_learning_rate(step, max_steps, lr_init=5e-4, lr_final=8e-5, lr_delay_steps=0, lr_delay_mult=1.0):
    t = np.clip(step / max_steps, 0, 1)
    log_lerp = np.exp(np.log(lr_init) * (1 - t) + np.log(lr_final) * t)
    
    if lr_delay_steps > 0:
        delay_rate = lr_delay_mult + (1 - lr_delay_mult) * np.sin(0.5 * np.pi * np.clip(step / lr_delay_steps, 0, 1))
    else:
        delay_rate = 1.0
        
    return delay_rate * log_lerp
```

## Subtopics

### [HashNeRF Parameters](3068-hashnerf-parameters.md)
HashNeRF parameters configure the training loop and model architecture for the HashNeRF method, which builds on [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md). Unlike standard NeRF which relies on positional encoding or fixed-resolution grids, HashNeRF uses a multi-resolution hashed embedding table indexed by voxel coordinates, requiring specific settings for the hash map size, loss regularization, and dataset-specific loading behaviors.

### [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md)
NeRF Initialization Strategies address the slow convergence and instability inherent in training classical Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) from random weight initialization. By leveraging pre-computed weights from related models or geometric priors, these methods significantly reduce the number of training epochs required to achieve high-quality rendering. Unlike standard random initialization, which often leads to local minima or requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, initialization strategies provide a geometrically or structurally informed starting point, trading off initial setup time for faster convergence.

### [Mega-NeRF Training Configuration](8766-mega-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Mega-NeRF is a training configuration for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) designed to accelerate training and reduce model query costs for large-scale scenes. It differs from standard NeRF by using a single shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for both coarse and fine sampling stages, which reduces model size and saves 25% of model queries per ray by reusing coarse outputs during the fine rendering stage.

### [Alpha Invariance / Inverse Scaling](10127-alpha-invariance-inverse-scaling.md)
Alpha invariance is a property in neural volume rendering where the opacity (alpha value) of a discretized segment remains constant despite changes in the scene's physical scale. It solves the problem that the magnitude of the optimized volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is intrinsically tied to the domain of integration (distance along the ray), meaning that scaling the scene requires inversely scaling the density to preserve the visual appearance of local geometry. Unlike Multi-Plane Images (MPIs) which fix discretization in advance, NeRF's continuous density parameterization allows flexible resampling but requires this specific handling to maintain robust view synthesis across arbitrary scene scales.

### [Batch size scaling for computational parity](10942-batch-size-scaling-for-computational-parity.md)

### [Model Architecture Modifications](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md)
Model Architecture Modifications refer to structural changes in the neural network components of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to address specific limitations such as overfitting in sparse views, lack of semantic understanding, dynamic scene representation, or computational efficiency. Unlike standard NeRF which maps 3D coordinates and view directions to density and color via a single Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), these modifications introduce auxiliary branches, conditional latent codes, or alternative geometric representations (e.g., SDFs, Voxels) to decouple or enhance specific scene properties. They are chosen when the standard volume rendering pipeline fails to capture complex phenomena like depth-of-field blur, semantic segmentation, or topological changes.

### [Regularization Techniques](11314-regularization-techniques.md)
Regularization techniques in NeRF training stabilize optimization, suppress artifacts (such as floaters and blur), and improve generalization in few-shot or sparse input scenarios. Unlike [HashNeRF Parameters](3068-hashnerf-parameters.md) which focus on architectural capacity, or [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md) which set initial states, regularization modifies the loss function or gradient updates during training to enforce physical priors or consistency.

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Training configuration for a NeRF model using depth priors, including architecture modifications, loss weights, and optimizer settings.

### [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md)

### [Adaptive Time-step Training Framework](13342-adaptive-time-step-training-framework.md)
A two-stage training framework that learns a scene-wise temporal budget and applies a shared target time step to all sampling points within a scene, preserving parallel inference while adapting computation to scene complexity.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time scales with the number of iterations, samples per ray, and MLP depth/width. Quadrupling sampling resolution (e.g., 32x32 to 64x64) quadruples training time [S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is dominated by the MLP weights and the batch of query points. Chunking (e.g., 4096 points per minibatch) is used to avoid out-of-memory errors [S6, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Blender dataset with 256 hidden units, a model achieves PSNR 32.96, SSIM 0.960, LPIPS 0.043 in 1.86 hours [S1]. With 512 hidden units, PSNR 33.25, SSIM 0.962, LPIPS 0.039 in 3.42 hours [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Relative speedup increases as model capacity rises [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality view synthesis; flexible scene representation.
*   **Limitations:** Slow training; prone to floaters and overfitting in sparse views; sensitive to hyperparameters like encoding frequencies and learning rate schedules [S5, unofficial][S7, S8].
*   **When to Use:** High-quality novel view synthesis from dense input views.
*   **When Not to Use:** Real-time rendering (without acceleration structures); sparse input views (without regularization/initialization strategies); large-scale unbounded scenes (without partitioning like Mega-NeRF).

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1][S4, unofficial][S9]. The hyperparameters described here are based on the original NeRF implementation and its immediate variants (Mip-NeRF, FastNeRF, DP-NeRF) [S1, S2][S4, S5, unofficial]. The use of Adam optimizer, positional encoding, and hierarchical sampling has become the standard baseline for subsequent NeRF variants [S1, S2, S3, S7, S8, S9].
