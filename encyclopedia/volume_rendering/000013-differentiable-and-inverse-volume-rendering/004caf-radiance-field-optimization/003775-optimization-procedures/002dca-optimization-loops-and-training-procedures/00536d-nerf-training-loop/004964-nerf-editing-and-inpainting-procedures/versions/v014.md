# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Content Editing

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to remove, insert, or alter objects and regions without retraining the entire scene from scratch. These methods solve the problem of local scene manipulation, object removal, and sparse-view reconstruction by optimizing network parameters or intercepting the rendering pipeline. They are distinguished from standard training by their focus on specific regions (via masks, bounding boxes, or semantic queries) and their use of external priors (2D inpainting, diffusion models, or feature fields) to fill missing data or guide edits, rather than relying solely on multi-view photometric consistency of the original input set.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a NeRF representation $f_\theta$ mapping 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. The core volume rendering equation used for supervision or inference is:
$$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$ and $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ [S1, 12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md].

Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Masking/Selection:** Defining a region of interest (ROI) or target object using 2D masks $M$, 3D bounding boxes $B$, or semantic probabilities $p$ [S1, 12273-blended-nerf.md, 12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md].
2.  **External Priors:** Using 2D image inpainters (e.g., LaMa, Stable Diffusion), depth estimators, or feature distillation networks to provide target colors, depths, or semantic features for regions that are occluded, removed, or edited [S1, 12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md, 12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md].
3.  **Optimization or Interception:** Either optimizing the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ to fit new constraints derived from these priors (e.g., minimizing loss between rendered and inpainted views) [S1, 12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md], or intercepting the ray marching loop to modify $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ on-the-fly based on selection queries [S1, 12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A method for reconstructing a panoramic NeRF from a single input panorama by iteratively expanding the training viewpoint coverage. It solves the problem of geometry conflict between visible regions (observed in the initial panorama) and invisible regions (occluded or unseen) by generating pseudo-observations for the invisible areas and erasing conflicting geometry from the training supervision. Unlike methods that rely on multiple input views or explicit 3D priors, PERF uses a generative prior (Stable Diffusion) to inpaint missing RGB and depth data, then progressively refines the NeRF while masking out regions where the generated geometry contradicts the learned structure.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training. Unlike methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations to estimate uncertainty, this approach acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, thereby enforcing epipolar constraints with minimal additional computation. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md) by integrating a "deceptive" diffusion model conditioned on rendered depth and view-consistency uncertainty maps to rectify artifacts and fill unobserved regions, rather than relying solely on generative priors for inpainting or feature distillation.

### [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)
DFF enables 3D semantic segmentation and editable novel-view synthesis by distilling features from a 2D teacher network (such as LSeg or DINO) into a neural radiance field, allowing the decomposition of scenes into semantic regions. Unlike methods that rely on explicit semantic labels or on-the-fly geometric masking, DFF learns a dedicated feature field that disentangles semantic information from appearance, enabling editing via semantic queries without retraining the underlying geometry. It sets itself apart by achieving segmentation performance competitive with supervised 3D models while maintaining multi-view consistency for editing tasks, overcoming the entanglement of low-level factors (color, spatial adjacency) found in standard NeRF hidden layers.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene using text prompts and a user-defined 3D bounding box. It solves the problem of local scene manipulation without requiring semantic masks or feature decomposition by initializing a generator NeRF with the original scene's weights and blending the synthesized content with the original scene using a novel volumetric blending technique. Unlike methods that synthesize objects from scratch or require 2D masks, Blended-NeRF operates on any region of a real-world scene, preserving the geometry outside the ROI while allowing for object insertion, replacement, or blending.

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
NeRF-In is a method for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by optimizing the network parameters $\Theta$ to fit new constraints derived from 2D RGB-D priors. Unlike methods requiring category-specific training data or on-the-fly masking, NeRF-In updates the global model parameters to jointly inpaint color and geometry across multiple views, ensuring view consistency without retraining from scratch. It sets itself apart by combining a color-guiding loss with a depth-guiding loss to prevent the "ghosting" of unwanted objects and color noise in flat regions.

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting is a method for 3D scene de-occlusion and object removal that combines a pretrained 2D image inpainter with multi-view NeRF optimization. It solves the inconsistency problem of applying 2D inpainting directly to 3D scenes by selectively using 2D inpainting priors only when multi-view geometric consistency cannot be established. Unlike sibling methods that rely on generative diffusion priors or feature distillation, this approach uses a gradient-based view selection mechanism to dynamically weight the reconstruction loss between standard photometric error and 2D inpainting priors.

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
The NeRF Editing Procedure modifies the output of a trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) without retraining the underlying network parameters $\Theta$. It achieves this by intercepting the volume rendering pipeline to alter density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ values on-the-fly based on semantic or geometric queries. Unlike optimization-based methods that minimize a loss function over many iterations [12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md], this approach applies immediate transformations during ray marching, enabling real-time or near-real-time editing such as deletion, colorization, and geometric transformation.

### [Reference-guided Controllable Inpainting of NeRFs](13952-reference-guided-controllable-inpainting-of-nerfs.md)
A 3D inpainting algorithm that uses a single inpainted reference view to guide the geometry and appearance of a NeRF, avoiding view inconsistencies and generating view-dependent effects. It sets itself apart from multi-view inpainting methods by requiring only one user-provided inpainted image to control the entire 3D scene, and from purely generative methods by explicitly modeling view-dependent effects (VDEs) and disocclusions through optimization rather than implicit diffusion priors.

### [RePaint-NeRF Limitations](14340-repaint-nerf-limitations.md)
This article documents the limitations of the RePaint-NeRF framework, which performs NeRF editing via semantic masks and diffusion models. It addresses the constraints regarding computational efficiency, geometric fidelity during drastic shape changes, and view-angle-dependent artifacts. These limitations distinguish the method from sibling approaches that may prioritize speed or geometric consistency over the specific text-guided semantic editing pipeline.

### Semantic Inpainting for Neural Fields
- **Definition:** A 3D content editing capability allowing editing of neural fields through simple interfaces like free-form sketching or language instructions [S1].
- **Step:** Given a user-supplied edit in a single reference view, the encoding of a neural field automatically propagates this edit to all views [S1]. The propagation promotes consistency between all views thanks to the implicit geometrical and photometric priors embodied by the neural network, preserving the structure of the whole scene [S1].
- **History:** Described in reference [19] [S1].

### Post-Refinement Strategy
- **Definition:** A strategy employed after the NeRF has been trained on updated data to enhance consistency by generating a more accurate target for each view using the modified Instruct-Pix2Pix with averaging architecture [S2].
- **Step:** Use the rendered image as input while continuing to use the mixup image as a condition [S2].

### Mask+CLIP
- **Pitfall:** CLIP provides weak guidance for NeRF optimization; after several epochs, the masked region develops unreasonable textures instead of converging to the target prompt [S3].

## Background
Neural rendering has evolved from explicit physical modeling to data-driven inference, enabling high photorealism and real-time rates [S1]. The concept of editing neural fields via simple interfaces, such as semantic inpainting where a single-view edit propagates to all views, was highlighted in early reviews of the field [S1]. Recent developments integrate 2D generative models (like DreamFusion's Imagen or Stable Diffusion) as parametric priors to distill 2D knowledge into 3D geometry without explicit 3D supervision [S1]. Methods like ViCA-NeRF [S2] and RePaint-NeRF [S3] have further refined these techniques using view-consistency awareness and semantic masks, respectively, addressing issues like texture artifacts and weak guidance from models like CLIP [S2, S3].
