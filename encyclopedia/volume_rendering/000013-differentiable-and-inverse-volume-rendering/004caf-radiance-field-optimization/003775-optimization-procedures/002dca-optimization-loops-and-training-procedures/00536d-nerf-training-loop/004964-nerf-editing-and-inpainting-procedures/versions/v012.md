# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: 3D Content Editing, Neural Field Editing, Inpainting Optimization

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a radiance field $F_\Theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ to remove unwanted objects, insert new content, or alter semantic regions without retraining the underlying network from scratch. Unlike global optimization methods that require full scene re-convergence, these procedures often operate as differentiable rendering-time transforms or targeted parameter updates [S1, 12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md]. They solve the problem of maintaining multi-view consistency when introducing geometric or appearance changes that were not present in the original training data, leveraging 2D priors (such as diffusion models or inpainters) to guide 3D consistency [S1, 12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of modifying the radiance field representation while preserving the integrity of the unedited scene regions. The core mechanism involves either:
1.  **Optimization-based Inpainting/Editing:** Updating the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ using a loss function that combines photometric reconstruction of unmasked regions with guidance from 2D priors (e.g., inpainted images, depth maps, or diffusion outputs) for masked/edited regions [12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md, 12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md].
2.  **Rendering-time Transformation:** Modifying the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ values on-the-fly during volume rendering based on semantic probabilities or geometric masks, without changing the stored weights [12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md].
3.  **Feature Distillation:** Injecting semantic information into the radiance field via a teacher network (e.g., LSeg, DINO) to enable semantic-aware editing [12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md].

Common mathematical components include the standard volume rendering equation for color and depth, and the use of binary or probabilistic masks $M$ to distinguish between valid, masked, or edited regions [12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md, 12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A training procedure for generating a panoramic NeRF from a single input panorama. It solves the problem of geometry conflict between visible regions and invisible regions (synthesized via inpainting) by iteratively detecting and erasing inconsistent geometry from the supervision signal. Unlike methods that rely on multi-view consistency from the start, PERF progressively expands the viewpoint range by inpainting novel views and erasing regions that violate geometric consistency with existing reference views.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
A sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training. Unlike sibling methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations for uncertainty, this method acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, minimizing additional computation while enforcing epipolar constraints. It builds on [NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures](12494-nerf-editing-and-inpainting-procedures.md) by using 2D diffusion priors to rectify artifacts and fill geometric gaps in sparse inputs.

### [DFF (Decomposing NeRF for Editing Via Feature Field Distillation)](12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md)
DFF enables 3D semantic segmentation and editable novel-view synthesis by distilling features from a 2D teacher network (such as LSeg or DINO) into a neural radiance field, allowing the decomposition of scenes into semantic regions. It addresses the challenge that original NeRF feature spaces are entangled with low-level factors like color or spatial adjacency rather than semantic similarity, which makes naive decomposition using hidden layer features robust. Unlike sibling methods that rely on optimization-based consistency enforcement or geometric warping, DFF achieves decomposition through feature distillation, enabling zero-shot text-based or patch-based region selection without requiring 3D semantic labels during training.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene based on text prompts and a 3D ROI box. It modifies the scene inside the box while preserving the scene outside, using a generator NeRF initialized with the original scene's weights and guided by a language-image model (CLIP). Unlike methods that synthesize objects from scratch or require semantic masks, Blended-NeRF blends the synthesized content with the original scene using a novel volumetric blending technique that accounts for density and distance from the ROI center.

### [NeRF-In Inpainting Optimization](12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md)
NeRF-In is a framework for removing unwanted objects from a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by optimizing its parameters $\Theta$ using color and depth guidance derived from 2D images, without requiring category-specific training data. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by explicitly combining a color-guiding loss with a depth-guiding loss to ensure both visual plausibility and geometric consistency across novel views, addressing the common failure modes of color-only optimization (incorrect depth) and depth-only optimization (color noise).

### [Adaptive View Selection for Inpainting](12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md)
This method is a component for 3D scene de-occlusion and inpainting that recovers missing geometry and appearance in occluded regions of a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF). It addresses the inconsistency of single-view 2D inpainting by introducing a training view selection mechanism based on regional average gradients. Unlike siblings that may use uniform masking or semantic priors alone, this method adaptively switches between relying on a 2D inpainter's appearance prior and leveraging multi-view geometric consistency based on texture richness.

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
The NeRF Editing Procedure modifies a radiance field $F_\Theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ by selectively transforming density and color values during volume rendering based on semantic or geometric selection criteria. It distinguishes itself from global optimization methods by operating as a differentiable rendering-time transform, allowing edits (deletion, colorization, translation) without retraining the underlying NeRF weights. It builds on [NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures](12494-nerf-editing-and-inpainting-procedures.md) by utilizing a Distilled Feature Field (DFF) to define regions of interest (ROI) via coordinate probability.

### [Reference-guided Controllable Inpainting of NeRFs](13952-reference-guided-controllable-inpainting-of-nerfs.md)
This method performs 3D inpainting on a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) using a single inpainted reference view to guide geometry and appearance, thereby avoiding view inconsistencies and generating view-dependent effects (VDEs). It differs from multi-view inpainting approaches by relying on one user-provided image to define the target content, propagating this information to other viewpoints via depth supervision and bilateral rendering, while handling disocclusions via image inpainters.

### Semantic Inpainting for Neural Fields
- **Definition:** A 3D content editing capability allowing editing of neural fields through simple interfaces like free-form sketching or language instructions [S1].
- **Step:** Given a user-supplied edit in a single reference view, the encoding of a neural field automatically propagates this edit to all views [S1]. The propagation promotes consistency between all views thanks to the implicit geometrical and photometric priors embodied by the neural network, preserving the structure of the whole scene [S1].
- **History:** Described in reference [19] [S1].

### Post-Refinement Strategy
- **Definition:** A strategy employed after the NeRF has been trained on updated data to enhance consistency by generating a more accurate target for each view using the modified Instruct-Pix2Pix with averaging architecture [S2].
- **Step:** Use the rendered image as input while continuing to use the mixup image as a condition [S2].

## Background
The concept of editing neural radiance fields emerged as NeRF technology matured, moving from static reconstruction to dynamic content manipulation. Early works focused on semantic segmentation within NeRFs to enable object removal [12068-dff-decomposing-nerf-for-editing-via-feature-field-distillat.md]. Subsequent research integrated 2D generative models (like Stable Diffusion) and inpainting networks to guide 3D optimization, addressing the "ghosting" and inconsistency issues inherent in sparse-view or masked editing [S1, 12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md]. The field has since expanded to include text-guided editing [12273-blended-nerf.md], single-view inpainting propagation [13952-reference-guided-controllable-inpainting-of-nerfs.md], and adaptive view selection for occluded regions [12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md].
