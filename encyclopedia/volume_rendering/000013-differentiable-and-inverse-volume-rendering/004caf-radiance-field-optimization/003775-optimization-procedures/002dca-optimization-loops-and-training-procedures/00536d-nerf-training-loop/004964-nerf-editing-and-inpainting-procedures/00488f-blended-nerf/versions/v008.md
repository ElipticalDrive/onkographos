# Blended-NeRF

Also called: Zero-Shot Object Generation and Blending in Existing Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing a specific region of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene based on text prompts and a user-defined 3D ROI box [S1]. It solves the problem of local 3D editing by initializing a generator NeRF with the original scene's weights and blending the synthesized content with the original scene using volumetric blending [S1]. Unlike methods that synthesize new NeRF objects from scratch (e.g., Dream Fields) or require semantic decomposition/2D masks for localization (e.g., Kobayashi, Benaim), Blended-NeRF operates on any region using a 3D box without learning a new feature space or requiring 2D masks [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** An existing NeRF model $F_{\theta_O}$ representing scene $x_o$, a 3D ROI box $B$ localized inside the scene, and a user-provided text prompt $y$ [S1].
*   **Output:** An edited scene $x_e$ such that $x_e \odot B$ is consistent with $y$ and $x_e \odot (1 - B) = x_o \odot (1 - B)$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model ($F_{\theta}$):** A 3D Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
*   **Positional Encoding:** Inputs are embedded using high-frequency sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ [S1]:
    $$ \gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\cos(2^l \mathbf{x}), \sin(2^l \mathbf{x})]_{l=0}^{L-1} $$
*   **ROI Box ($B$):** Defined by a center and dimensions, localized via a GUI using the original NeRF's depth map [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\alpha$: Hyperparameter for distance smoothing blending strength. $\alpha=0$ gives all weight to the edited scene; higher values give more attention to original scene points closer to the ROI center. Typical values demonstrated: 0, 0.5, 2, 4, 10 [S1].
*   $\lambda_T, \lambda_D$: Weights for transmittance and depth losses [S1].
*   $\tau$: Max transmittance for transmittance loss [S1].
*   $\rho$: Max variance allowed for depth loss [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Small constant for numerical stability in blending [S1].
*   $\gamma$: Camera Field of View (FOV) [S1].
*   $e_{max}$: Maximum dimension of the ROI box [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a generator NeRF model $F_{\theta_G}$ with the weights of the original scene model $F_{\theta_O}$ [S1].
2.  **ROI Localization:** Localize a 3D box $B$ inside the scene using a GUI that renders the original NeRF and uses the output depth map for 3D understanding [S1].
3.  **Pose Sampling:** For each training iteration, sample a random camera pose around the scene with azimuth/elevation angles $(\theta, \phi)$. The initial distance $d$ from the ROI is calculated as:
    $$ d = \frac{e_{max}}{2 \tan(\gamma/2)} $$
    Randomly sample radius $r$ around $d$ [S1].
4.  **Ray Casting and Sampling:** Cast rays $\mathbf{r}$ that pass through the box $B$. Sample points $\mathbf{x}_i$ along these rays. For sample points outside $B$, set density to zero [S1].
5.  **Background Augmentation:** Use random backgrounds (Gaussian noise, checkerboard, random Fourier textures) during optimization to prevent scene diffusion [S1].
6.  **Directional Prompts:** Concatenate a view-dependent text prompt to the original caption $y$ to constrain object description across views [S1].
7.  **Optimization:** Optimize $F_{\theta_G}$ by minimizing the total loss $L_{total}$ (defined below). The similarity loss $L_{sim}$ is computed by rendering the generated content inside the ROI box $I_{ROI}$ using only points $\mathbf{x}_i \in B$ [S1].
8.  **Blending (Inference):** After training, render the edited scene by blending sample points generated by $F_{\theta_O}$ and $F_{\theta_G}$ along each view ray [S1].

**Formulas:**

*   **Classical Volume Rendering:**
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between adjacent samples, $\sigma_i$ is density, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is color, and $T_i$ is transmittance [S1].

*   **ROI Rendering (Training):**
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \begin{cases} \frac{\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in B} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) \mathbf{c}_i}{\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in B} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i})} & \text{if } \exists \mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbf{r} \text{ s.t. } \mathbf{x}_i \in B \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j < i} \sigma_j \delta_i)$ [S1].

*   **Similarity Loss:**
    $$ L_{sim} = -E_{img}(I_{ROI})^T E_{txt}(y) $$
    where $E_{img}$ and $E_{txt}$ are image and text encoders of a language-image model (e.g., CLIP), and $y$ is the text prompt [S1].

*   **Total Objective:**
    $$ L_{total} = L_{sim} + \lambda_T L_T + \lambda_D L_D $$
    where $L_T$ and $L_D$ are transmittance and depth losses, respectively [S1].

*   **Transmittance Loss:**
    $$ L_T = -\min(\tau, \text{mean}(T(P))) $$
    where $T(P)$ is transmittance of rendered view from pose $P$ [S1].

*   **Depth Loss:**
    $$ L_D = -\min(\rho, \sigma^2(D(P))) $$
    where $\sigma^2(D(P))$ is variance of disparity map of rendered view from pose $P$ [S1].

*   **Distance Smoothing Blending (Object Insertion/Replacement):**
    $$ f(\mathbf{x}) = 1 - \exp\left(-\alpha \frac{d(\mathbf{x})}{diag}\right) $$
    $$ \sigma_{blend}(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \sigma_O(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - f(\mathbf{x})) \cdot \sigma_G(\mathbf{x}) $$
    $$ \mathbf{c}_{blend}(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{c}_O(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - f(\mathbf{x})) \cdot \mathbf{c}_G(\mathbf{x}) $$
    where $d(\mathbf{x})$ is Euclidean distance from ROI center, $diag$ is box diagonal, and $\alpha$ is the hyperparameter [S1].

*   **Object Blending Mode:**
    Compute alpha values for each point $\mathbf{x}_i$ on the ray separately from each model:
    $$ \alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \exp(\phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i)) \cdot \delta_i) $$
    $$ \alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \exp(\phi(\sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i)) \cdot \delta_i) $$
    where $\phi$ is a non-negative activation function [S1].
    
    Blend colors:
    $$ \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_i) = S\left(\frac{\mathbf{c}_O(\mathbf{x}_i) \cdot \alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + \mathbf{c}_G(\mathbf{x}_i) \cdot \alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i)}{\epsilon + \alpha_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + \alpha_G(\mathbf{x}_i)}\right) $$
    where $S$ is sigmoid and $\epsilon$ is a small constant [S1].
    
    Blend densities (two options):
    Option 1 (Sum in activation): $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) = \phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i) + \sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i))$ [S1].
    Option 2 (Sum out activation): $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) = \phi(\sigma_O(\mathbf{x}_i)) + \phi(\sigma_G(\mathbf{x}_i))$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def blended_nerf_editing(F_theta_O, B, text_prompt_y, num_iterations):
    """
    F_theta_O: Original NeRF model (MLP)
    B: 3D ROI Box (center, dimensions)
    text_prompt_y: User text prompt
    num_iterations: Training steps
    """
    # 1. Initialize Generator
    F_theta_G = initialize_mlp_with_weights(F_theta_O)
    
    # Precompute box diagonal and center
    diag = compute_box_diagonal(B)
    center = compute_box_center(B)
    
    # 2. Training Loop
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # Pose Sampling
        theta, phi = sample_random_angles()
        d_init = B.e_max / (2 * tan(camera_fov / 2))
        r = random_sample_radius(d_init)
        pose = sample_pose_from_angles_and_radius(theta, phi, r)
        
        # Ray Casting
        rays = cast_rays_through_box(pose, B)
        
        # Background Augmentation
        background = random_background() # Gaussian, checkerboard, or Fourier
        
        # Optimization Step
        loss_sim = 0.0
        loss_T = 0.0
        loss_D = 0.0
        
        for ray in rays:
            # Sample points along ray
            points, deltas = sample_points_along_ray(ray, B)
            
            # Filter points inside B
            points_inside = [p for p in points if is_inside_box(p, B)]
            deltas_inside = [d for p, d in zip(points, deltas) if is_inside_box(p, B)]
            
            if not points_inside:
                continue
                
            # Query Generator for points inside B
            # Points outside B have density 0 for generator rendering
            sigmas_G, colors_G = F_theta_G.query(points_inside)
            
            # Compute Transmittance and Color for ROI rendering
            # Using Eq. 4 logic: normalize by sum of alpha if points exist in B
            alphas_G = 1 - exp(-sigmas_G * deltas_inside)
            Ts = exp(-cumsum(sigmas_G * deltas_inside)) # T_i
            # Note: Eq 4 uses normalized sum for ROI rendering
            numerator = sum(T[i] * alphas_G[i] * colors_G[i] for i in range(len(points_inside)))
            denominator = sum(T[i] * alphas_G[i] for i in range(len(points_inside)))
            
            if denominator > 0:
                I_ROI = numerator / denominator
            else:
                I_ROI = background # Fallback or black
                
            # Similarity Loss (Eq 3)
            # E_img and E_txt are CLIP encoders
            img_features = E_img(I_ROI)
            txt_features = E_txt(text_prompt_y)
            loss_sim += -dot(img_features, txt_features)
            
            # Transmittance Loss (Eq 11)
            # T(P) is average transmittance of the view
            # For simplicity, use mean of Ts from this ray as proxy or accumulate
            avg_T = mean(Ts)
            loss_T += -min(max_transmittance, avg_T)
            
            # Depth Loss (Eq 12)
            # Requires disparity map D(P). Assuming we can compute disparity from points
            # This is a simplification; in practice, render depth map and compute variance
            disparity_map = compute_disparity_map_from_points(points_inside, pose)
            var_D = variance(disparity_map)
            loss_D += -min(max_variance, var_D)
            
        # Total Loss
        # Annealing strategy for lambda_T and lambda_D should be applied here
        L_total = loss_sim + lambda_T * loss_T + lambda_D * loss_D
        
        # Backpropagate and update F_theta_G
        update_weights(F_theta_G, L_total)

    # 3. Blending (Inference)
    def render_edited_view(pose):
        rays = cast_rays(pose)
        edited_image = zeros_like(image)
        
        for ray in rays:
            points, deltas = sample_points_along_ray(ray)
            
            # Query Original NeRF
            sigmas_O, colors_O = F_theta_O.query(points)
            
            # Query Generator NeRF
            sigmas_G, colors_G = F_theta_G.query(points)
            
            # Determine if point is in ROI
            in_roi = [is_inside_box(p, B) for p in points]
            
            # Blend densities and colors based on mode
            # Example: Object Blending Mode (Eq 6, 7, 8/9)
            # Apply activation phi to ensure non-negative density
            sigmas_O_pos = phi(sigmas_O)
            sigmas_G_pos = phi(sigmas_G)
            
            # Compute alphas
            alphas_O = 1 - exp(-sigmas_O_pos * deltas)
            alphas_G = 1 - exp(-sigmas_G_pos * deltas)
            
            # Blend Colors (Eq 7)
            # S is sigmoid, epsilon is small constant
            blended_colors = sigmoid(
                (colors_O * alphas_O + colors_G * alphas_G) / (epsilon + alphas_O + alphas_G)
            )
            
            # Blend Densities (Eq 8 or 9)
            # Option 1: Sum in activation
            blended_sigmas = phi(sigmas_O + sigmas_G)
            
            # Compute final transmittance and color for the ray
            # Using standard volume rendering with blended values
            # Note: This is a simplified blending along the ray. 
            # The paper describes blending sample points, then rendering.
            # For strict implementation, one might blend the final alpha/composite.
            # However, Eq 5 suggests blending densities/colors per point before 
            # or during the ray integration.
            
            # Re-calculate T and C with blended values
            T_blend = exp(-cumsum(blended_sigmas * deltas))
            alpha_blend = 1 - exp(-blended_sigmas * deltas)
            
            # Standard Volume Rendering
            color = sum(T_blend[i] * alpha_blend[i] * blended_colors[i] for i in range(len(points)))
            
            # Apply Distance Smoothing if in ROI (Eq 5)
            # This step is typically applied to the final result or intermediate densities.
            # The paper says: "resulted raw densities and RGB values inside and outside 
            # the ROI are then blended along each ray using eq. (1)".
            # And Eq 5 blends sigma/c per point.
            # Implementation detail: Eq 5 is applied to sigma/c before volume rendering 
            # if using the "blending" mode described in section 3.1.
            
            edited_image[ray.id] = color
            
        return edited_image

    return render_edited_view
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scene Diffusion:** Without background augmentation, the scene may populate the background with noise [S1].
*   **Flat Geometry:** In forward-facing scenes, limited viewing intervals can cause a "billboard" effect. Depth loss is required to enforce volumetric shape [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Without transmittance loss, the generated object may be completely transparent or amorphous [S1].
*   **Blending Artifacts:** The choice of density blending (sum in vs. sum out activation) affects whether the generator can remove original densities or only add new ones [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires optimizing a new NeRF model $F_{\theta_G}$ initialized with $F_{\theta_O}$, guided by CLIP similarity. This is comparable to text-to-NeRF methods like Dream Fields but restricted to the ROI [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two NeRF models ($F_{\theta_O}$ and $F_{\theta_G}$) and their weights during training [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Can operate on any region of a real-world scene without learning a new feature space or requiring 2D masks. Generates natural-looking and view-consistent results [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the pretrained language-image model (CLIP). May struggle with complex text-guided manipulations if the prompt is ambiguous [S1].
*   **When to use:** When editing a specific object or region in an existing NeRF scene using text prompts, especially when 2D masks are unavailable or semantic decomposition is not desired [S1].

## Background
Blended-NeRF was introduced by Ori Gordon, Omri Avrahami, and Dani Lischinski from The Hebrew University in 2023 [S1]. It builds on previous works like Dream Fields [27] and CLIP-NeRF [67], adapting their priors and augmentations for local editing of existing NeRF scenes. It addresses the limitations of methods that synthesize new NeRF objects from scratch or require semantic decomposition [S1].
