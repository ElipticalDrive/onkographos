# Neural Light Field Representations

Also called: NeLF, R2L (Ray-to-Light)

## Overview
Neural Light Field Representations replace the volumetric ray-marching paradigm of NeRF with a direct mapping from camera rays to pixel colors, enabling single-pass inference without iterative sampling [S1]. This approach solves the computational bottleneck of volume rendering by treating the scene as a 4D light field, where the input is a ray (origin and direction) and the output is RGB, bypassing the need to query density and radiance at intermediate points along the ray [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism is the distillation of a volumetric teacher (NeRF) into a deep residual student network that predicts color directly from ray parameters.

1.  **Input Representation**: The network input is a 4D oriented ray, parameterized by origin $(x_o, y_o, z_o)$ and normalized direction $(x_d, y_d, z_d)$ [S1]. To capture local geometry, the input is expanded by sampling $K$ points along the ray and concatenating their 3D coordinates, forming a $3K$-dimensional vector (typically $K=16$) [S1].
2.  **Architecture**: The student $G_\phi$ is a deep Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with residual connections to support significant depth (e.g., 88 layers) without vanishing gradients [S1].
3.  **Distillation**: The student is trained to mimic a pre-trained NeRF teacher $F_{\Theta^*}$ [S1]. The teacher maps 5D coordinates (position + direction) to density and radiance [S1].
4.  **Pseudo Data Generation**: Training data is generated by sampling rays uniformly within a viewing bounding box and querying the teacher for target RGB values [S1].
5.  **Loss**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the student's prediction and the teacher's RGB output [S1].
6.  **Hard Example Mining**: A pool of high-loss rays is maintained to augment training batches, preventing overfitting to easy samples [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Light Field (NeLF)](9257-neural-light-field-nelf.md)
Uses R2L distillation with an 88-layer residual MLP. Sets itself apart by eliminating ray marching entirely and using hard-example mining with pseudo-data. Choose when inference speed is critical and a pre-trained NeRF is available.

## Background
Originating from the R2L (Ray-to-Light) distillation method, this approach addresses the latency of standard NeRF training and inference [S1]. It leverages the fact that while NeRF accurately models light transport, its sampling-based rendering is slow. By distilling this knowledge into a direct function approximator, it enables real-time novel view synthesis [S1].
