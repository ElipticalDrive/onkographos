# SfMNeRF
Also called: SfM-NeRF

## Overview
SfMNeRF is a neural radiance field optimization method designed to improve novel view synthesis and 3D geometry reconstruction under sparse input conditions by integrating Structure-from-Motion (SfM) constraints into the training loop [S1]. It addresses the geometric ambiguity and local minima issues common in standard NeRF by explicitly enforcing epipolar geometry, multi-view photometric consistency, depth smoothness, and feature position constraints [S1]. Unlike vanilla NeRF which relies solely on photometric rendering loss, SfMNeRF leverages self-supervised depth estimation priors and cross-view feature matching to regularize the implicit scene representation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $m$ training images with known camera intrinsics $K$ and global poses $[\mathbf{r}_t, \mathbf{t}_t]$. Matched Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) feature correspondences between image pairs.
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF MLP parameterized by $\theta$ that maps 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$, producing rendered novel views and depth maps.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Model:** Intrinsic matrix $K \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$. Global pose for target image $t$: rotation $\mathbf{r}_t \in SO(3)$, translation $\mathbf{t}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Relative Pose:** $T_{r \to t} = [\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}]$, where $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_t^{-1}$ and $\mathbf{t} = -\mathbf{r}_t^{-1}\mathbf{t}_t$ [S1].
*   **Mask Map:** Binary mask $M(p)$ indicating if a pixel $p$ lies within the minimum bounding rectangle of matched SIFT features in the reference image [S1].
*   **Depth Map:** $D(p)$ representing the estimated depth at pixel $p$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Pyramidal Initialization:** Construct an $L$-level image pyramid for the reference image $I_r$ and target image $I_t$. Level $n$ images $I^n_r, I^n_t$ are half-resolution versions of level $n-1$. Start optimization at the lowest resolution level $L-1$ [S1].
2.  **Sub-pixel Sampling:** For a reference image patch $\Omega_p$ (size $32 \times 32$), generate sub-pixel coordinates $p = (x + x_{off}, y + y_{off})$ where $x_{off}, y_{off} \in (0, 1)$ [S1].
3.  **Ground Truth Interpolation:** Compute ground-truth color $\hat{I}(p)$ for sub-pixels via bilinear interpolation of the four nearest integer-pixel neighbors $p^{ij}$ in the reference image:
    $$ \hat{I}(p) = \sum_{i \in \{top, bottom\}, j \in \{left, right\}} w^{ij} I(p^{ij}) $$
    where weights $w^{ij}$ are proportional to the inverse Euclidean distance between $p$ and $p^{ij}$ [S1].
4.  **NeRF Forward Pass:** Query the NeRF with sub-pixel 3D coordinates (derived from ray casting through $p$) and viewing directions to obtain density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. Compute rendered color $\tilde{I}(p)$ using standard volume rendering integration [S1].
5.  **Feature Coordinate Estimation:** For each matched SIFT feature $k$, compute its estimated 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}_s^k$ as a weighted sum of samples along the ray:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_s^k = \sum_{j=1}^{N_c} w_j (\mathbf{o} + t_j \mathbf{d}), \quad w_j = T_j (1 - \exp(-\sigma_j \delta_j)) $$
    where $\delta_j = t_{j-1} - t_j$, $T_j = \exp(-\sum_{l=1}^{j-1} \sigma_l \delta_l)$, and $N_c$ is the number of samples [S1].
6.  **Constraint Computation:** Calculate the following loss terms:
    *   **Rendering Loss ($L_{ren}$):** Mean squared error between interpolated ground truth $\hat{I}(p)$ and rendered color $\tilde{I}(p)$ over the patch [S1].
    *   **Position-of-Matches Loss ($L_{3D}$):** Squared Euclidean distance between 3D coordinates of matched SIFT features in reference and target views [S1].
    *   **Epipolar Loss ($L_{epi}$):** Minimum squared distance between the 3D coordinate of a reference point and points on the epipolar line in the target view, filtered by color similarity [S1].
    *   **Photometric Consistency Loss ($L_{pr}$):** L1 difference between reference patch colors and warped target patch colors, masked by $M$ [S1].
    *   **SSIM Loss ($L_{SSIM}$):** Structural Similarity index between reference and warped target patches, masked by $M$ [S1].
    *   **Depth Smoothness Loss ($L_{ds}$):** Weighted L1 norm of depth gradients, where weights are determined by image intensity [S1].
    *   **Depth Prior Loss ($L_{depth}$):** (Used in levels $n < L-1$) L1 difference between current depth estimate and upsampled depth from the previous lower-resolution level [S1].
7.  **Optimization:** Minimize the total loss $L_{total}$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].
8.  **Pyramidal Progression:** After converging at level $n$, upsample the depth map to level $n-1$ and repeat the optimization with the added depth prior loss, until reaching level 0 [S1].

**Formulas:**

*   **Rendering Loss:**
    $$ L_{ren} = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{p_i \in \Omega_p} \left\| \hat{I}(p_i) - \tilde{I}(p_i) \right\|_2^2 $$
    where $\hat{I}(p_i)$ is the ground-truth color and $\tilde{I}(p_i)$ is the synthesized pixel [S1].

*   **Position-of-Matched Features Loss:**
    $$ L_{3D} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \|\mathbf{x}_s^k - \mathbf{x}_{s'}^k\|_2^2 $$
    where $\mathbf{x}_s^k$ and $\mathbf{x}_{s'}^k$ are the 3D coordinates of matched feature $k$ in the reference and target images, respectively [S1].

*   **Epipolar Constraint Loss:**
    $$ L_{epi} = \min_t \left\{ \left\| \mathbf{d}_{p_r} - \mathbf{d}_{p_t} \right\|_2^2 \right\} $$
    where $\mathbf{d}_{p_r}$ is the 3D coordinate of the point in the reference image, and $\mathbf{d}_{p_t}$ are 3D coordinates of points on the epipolar line in the target image. Implausible points (large color difference from reference) are filtered out [S1].

*   **Warped Pixel Coordinate:**
    $$ \hat{p}_t = K T_{r \to t} \mathbf{x}_p $$
    where $\mathbf{x}_p$ is the 3D coordinate of pixel $p$ in the reference patch, $K$ is the intrinsic matrix, and $T_{r \to t}$ is the relative pose [S1].

*   **Photometric Reconstruction Loss:**
    $$ L_{pr} = \sum_{p(k) \in \Omega_p} M(p(k)) \left| \hat{I}_r(p(k)) - \hat{I}_t(\hat{p}_t(k)) \right| $$
    where $\hat{I}_r$ is the reference image color, $\hat{I}_t$ is the interpolated color from the target image at the warped coordinate, and $M$ is the binary mask [S1].

*   **SSIM Loss:**
    $$ L_{SSIM} = M \left( \frac{1 - SSIM_{r \to t}}{2} \right) $$
    where $SSIM_{r \to t}$ is the structural similarity between the reference patch and the warped target patch [S1].

*   **Depth Smoothness Loss:**
    $$ L_{ds} = \sum_{p(k) \in \Omega_p} \left| \nabla D ( p(k) ) \cdot \left( e^{-|\hat{I}(p(k))|} \right) \right| $$
    where $\nabla$ is the 2D differential operator and $D(p(k))$ is the estimated depth [S1].

*   **Depth Prior Loss (Pyramidal):**
    $$ L_{depth} = \sum_{p(k) \in \Omega_p} |D(p(k)) - D_r^{L-1}(p(k)/2)| $$
    where $D_r^{L-1}$ is the depth map from the lower-resolution level, sampled via bilinear interpolation at half coordinates [S1].

*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ L_{total} = \lambda_{ren} L_{ren} + \lambda_{3D} L_{3D} + \lambda_{pr} L_{pr} + \lambda_{epi} L_{epi} + \lambda_{SSIM} L_{SSIM} + \lambda_{ds} L_{ds} + \lambda_{depth} L_{depth} $$
    Note: $L_{depth}$ is only active in pyramidal levels $n < L-1$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Loss weights: $\lambda_{ren} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{3D} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{pr} = 0.001$, $\lambda_{epi} = 0.0001$, $\lambda_{SSIM} = 0.008$, $\lambda_{ds} = 0.01$ [S1].
*   Patch size: $32 \times 32$ [S1].
*   Sampling: Hierarchical sampling with 64 coarse samples and 64 importance samples [S1].
*   Optimizer: Adam with $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$, learning rate $10^{-3}$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Epipolar Points:** The number of points on an epipolar line is large, causing computational inefficiency. Occluded points may not have corresponding points on the epipolar line. Solution: Filter epipolar points by color similarity; only keep points where the color difference between the reference point and the epipolar point is below a threshold [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Large pixel motions can cause the photometric consistency loss to get stuck in local minima. Solution: Use the pyramidal strategy starting from low resolution [S1].
*   **Masking:** Pixels outside the minimum bounding rectangle of matched SIFT features are masked out ($M=0$) to avoid unreliable photometric constraints [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Higher than vanilla NeRF due to additional loss computations (SIFT matching, epipolar geometry, warping) and the pyramidal training strategy which requires multiple passes over the data at different resolutions [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to vanilla NeRF for the MLP, but requires storing SIFT features, masks, and potentially intermediate depth maps for the pyramidal strategy [S1].
*   **Performance:** Evaluated on LLFF-NeRF, ScanNet, and DTU MVS datasets. It surpasses state-of-the-art approaches given sparse inputs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves geometric accuracy and avoids local minima in sparse view scenarios. Explicit constraints reduce shape-radiance ambiguity [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computational inefficiency due to large number of epipolar points (mitigated by filtering). Relies on accurate SIFT matching and camera poses [S1].

## Background
SfMNeRF was proposed to address the failure of existing NeRF-based methods to obtain moderate 3D structures under sparse input conditions, where novel view synthesis quality drops dramatically due to implicitly reconstructed inaccurate 3D-scene structures [S1]. It leverages knowledge from self-supervised depth estimation methods to constrain the 3D-scene geometry during view synthesis training [S1]. The method was evaluated on LLFF-NeRF, ScanNet, and DTU MVS datasets [S1].
