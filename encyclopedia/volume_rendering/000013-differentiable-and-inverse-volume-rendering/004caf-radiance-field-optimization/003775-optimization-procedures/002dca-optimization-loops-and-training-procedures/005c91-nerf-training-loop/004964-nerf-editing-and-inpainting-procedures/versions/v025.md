# NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures

Also called: NeRF Editing, NeRF Inpainting, 3D Scene Editing

## Overview
NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures modify a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to alter its appearance, geometry, or content while maintaining multi-view consistency. This family solves the problem of localized 3D content manipulation—such as object removal, style transfer, or filling occluded regions—by optimizing NeRF parameters or querying auxiliary fields based on external 2D priors (e.g., diffusion models, semantic segmentations) or geometric constraints. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) is the explicit integration of external guidance or deterministic transformation pipelines into the rendering or optimization loop, rather than merely reconstructing the scene from raw input images.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a pre-trained NeRF representation $f_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x}$ is a 3D coordinate, $\mathbf{d}$ is the viewing direction, $\sigma$ is volume density, and $\mathbf{c}$ is color [Parent]. The core mechanism involves an optimization loop that minimizes a loss function $L$ to update the NeRF parameters $\theta$ (or a subset thereof).

The general optimization objective is:
$$
L = L_{render} + \lambda_{guidance} L_{guidance} + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg}
$$
where $L_{render}$ is the standard photometric reconstruction loss on visible pixels, $L_{guidance}$ incorporates external priors (e.g., 2D inpainted images, diffusion model gradients, or semantic features), and $L_{reg}$ prevents artifacts or overfitting [S1, 12372-nerf-in-inpainting-optimization.md, 13438-nerf-art.md].

Key shared components include:
1.  **Masking/Selection:** A binary or probabilistic mask $M$ or selection field identifies the region of interest (ROI) or occluded areas. This can be user-defined, derived from semantic segmentation (e.g., LSeg, CLIP), or computed via geometric conflict detection [S1, 14365-repaint-nerf.md, 8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md].
2.  **External Priors:** Most methods rely on 2D priors to guide 3D changes. Common priors include:
    *   **2D Inpainting:** Using models like LaMa or Stable Diffusion to generate color/depth priors for occluded regions [S1, 12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md].
    *   **Diffusion Guidance:** Using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) to guide appearance and geometry [S1, 14365-repaint-nerf.md].
    *   **Semantic/Text Features:** Using CLIP or LSeg to align rendered features with text prompts or semantic labels [S1, 13438-nerf-art.md, 14365-repaint-nerf.md].
3.  **View Consistency Enforcement:** To prevent flickering or inconsistencies across views, methods enforce multi-view constraints. This is achieved through:
    *   **Geometric Consistency:** Using depth maps or epipolar constraints to ensure warped views align [S1, 11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md].
    *   **Gradient-Based Selection:** Adapting the reliance on 2D priors vs. multi-view geometry based on local texture gradients [S1, 12405-adaptive-view-selection-for-inpainting.md].
    *   **Reference Views:** Using a single or multiple inpainted reference views to propagate consistent content [S1, 13952-reference-guided-controllable-inpainting-of-nerfs.md].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing (PERF) is a method for reconstructing a panoramic Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) from a single input panorama by iteratively expanding the viewpoint coverage. It solves the problem of geometry conflict between visible regions (observed in reference views) and invisible regions (occluded or unseen) by computing a mask of conflicted areas and eliminating them from the NeRF training supervision. Unlike methods that rely on multiple input views or semantic priors, PERF generates novel views via inpainting and progressively integrates them while erasing geometrically inconsistent pixels to ensure 3D consistency.

### [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS Pipeline](11860-deceptive-nerf-3dgs-pipeline.md)
Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS is a sparse-view reconstruction method that enhances NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations by continuously densifying input observations with synthetic pseudo-observations generated by a diffusion model during training. Unlike methods that rely on ensemble learning, variational inference, or spatial perturbations to estimate uncertainty, this approach acquires uncertainty through correspondence matching by reprojecting real input observations to novel views, thereby enforcing epipolar constraints with minimal additional computation. It sets itself apart by conditioning a 2D diffusion model on rendered RGB images, depth maps, and view-consistency uncertainty maps to rectify artifacts and generate photorealistic pseudo-observations that are filtered by perceptual similarity before being used to retrain the 3D representation.

### [Blended-NeRF](12273-blended-nerf.md)
Blended-NeRF is a framework for editing specific regions of interest (ROI) in an existing Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) scene using text prompts and a user-defined 3D bounding box. It differs from generative methods that synthesize objects from scratch (e.g., Dream Fields) by initializing a generator NeRF with the weights of the original scene and blending the synthesized content with the original scene using a novel volumetric blending technique. Unlike methods requiring semantic masks or feature decomposition, it operates on any region defined by a 3D box without learning a new feature space.

### [NeRF Editing Procedure](12491-nerf-editing-procedure.md)
This procedure enables direct 3D editing of a pre-trained NeRF by decoupling semantic or geometric selection from appearance rendering using a Distilled Feature Field (DFF). It solves the problem of inconsistent multi-view edits by defining a selection probability $p$ for each 3D point based on external feature queries, allowing for precise operations like deletion, colorization, and geometric transformation that respect the underlying scene structure. It sets itself apart from standard optimization-based methods (e.g., [RePaint-NeRF](14365-repaint-nerf.md)) by avoiding iterative gradient descent for the edit itself, instead relying on a deterministic query-and-transform pipeline during rendering.

### [NeRF-Art](13438-nerf-art.md)
NeRF-Art is a text-guided method for stylizing pre-trained Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) that simultaneously alters both the appearance and the 3D geometry of a scene without requiring mesh guidance. It distinguishes itself from prior approaches by introducing a global-local contrastive learning strategy combined with directional constraints, addressing the insufficiency of direct CLIP similarity supervision in ensuring strong style adherence and cross-view consistency.

### [RePaint-NeRF Limitations](14340-repaint-nerf-limitations.md)
This article documents the empirical limitations of the RePaint-NeRF method, specifically regarding computational efficiency, geometric fidelity under significant shape changes, and view-angle consistency. It serves as a critical reference for implementers to understand the failure modes and resource constraints inherent in diffusion-guided NeRF editing, distinguishing these issues from the algorithmic mechanics described in [RePaint-NeRF](14365-repaint-nerf.md).

### [Semantic and Language-Guided Editing](14393-semantic-and-language-guided-editing.md)
Semantic and Language-Guided Editing enables the modification of neural radiance fields (NeRFs) using high-level semantic cues, such as text prompts, language instructions, or semantic masks, rather than relying solely on low-level geometric or photometric constraints. It solves the problem of localized 3D content manipulation—such as object deletion, appearance transformation, or semantic inpainting—while maintaining multi-view consistency and scene integrity. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [NeRF Editing and Inpainting Procedures](12494-nerf-editing-and-inpainting-procedures.md) is the explicit integration of external semantic priors (e.g., CLIP, LSeg, or diffusion models) into the optimization loop to guide the 3D structure and appearance, rather than merely filling in missing geometry or color.

### [InNeRF360](14845-innerf360.md)
InNeRF360 is a method for 3D scene inpainting and editing that combines multiview-consistent 2D priors with 3D diffusion guidance to reconstruct missing or edited regions in a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF). It addresses the problem of generating geometrically and photometrically consistent content for occluded or removed objects by first establishing a coherent 2D inpainting across all views and then refining the 3D representation. It differs from direct 3D optimization methods by decoupling the generation of plausible content (via 2D inpainting) from the 3D reconstruction, using 3D diffusion priors primarily to eliminate artifacts and enforce consistency rather than generating the primary geometry.

### [Diffusion-guided editing](15110-diffusion-guided-editing.md)
Diffusion-guided editing methods modify pre-trained Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) using text prompts or image instructions by leveraging 2D text-to-image diffusion models as external priors. Unlike sibling methods that rely on semantic segmentation masks or geometric constraints for inpainting, this family uses diffusion gradients or iterative image synthesis to guide 3D changes. It is distinct from direct gradient-based guidance (like SDS) in variants that use iterative dataset updates, and from single-view guidance in variants that enforce multi-view consistency through iterative retraining [14365-repaint-nerf.md, 15089-instruct-nerf2nerf-optimization.md].

### [Inpainting and Object Removal](17712-inpainting-and-object-removal.md)
This family addresses the removal of unwanted objects or the filling of occluded regions in a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by optimizing its parameters to match external 2D priors while maintaining multi-view consistency [Parent]. It differs from sibling methods like [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md) by focusing on optimization-based refinement rather than iterative erasing, and from [Semantic and Language-Guided Editing](14393-semantic-and-language-guided-editing.md) by primarily relying on geometric or appearance priors derived from inpainted images rather than text prompts .

## Background
The field of NeRF editing emerged as NeRFs gained popularity for novel view synthesis, with early works focusing on simple object removal or colorization. The integration of 2D diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) as priors for 3D editing became prominent around 2023, leading to methods like Instruct-NeRF2NeRF and RePaint-NeRF. Recent developments include semantic-guided editing, sparse-view inpainting, and hybrid approaches combining 3D Gaussian Splatting with diffusion priors. These methods are widely used in virtual production, augmented reality, and 3D content creation.
