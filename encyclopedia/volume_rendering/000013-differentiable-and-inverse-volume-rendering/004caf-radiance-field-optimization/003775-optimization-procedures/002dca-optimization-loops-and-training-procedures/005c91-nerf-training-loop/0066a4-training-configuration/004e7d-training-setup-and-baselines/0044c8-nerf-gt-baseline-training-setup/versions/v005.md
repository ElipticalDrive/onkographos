# NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup

Also called: NeRF-GT

## Overview
The NeRF-GT baseline establishes a reference performance standard for training Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by optimizing the model directly on High Dynamic Range (HDR) views, serving as an upper-bound for methods that synthesize HDR from Low Dynamic Range (LDR) inputs [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by explicitly handling the high dynamic range of scene radiance, though it is noted to be difficult to converge for all scene areas due to the extreme range of values [S1]. This setup builds on [NeRF Training Setup](13271-nerf-training-setup.md) but modifies the input data and loss computation to accommodate HDR characteristics.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A dataset of HDR views (or consistently exposed LDR views) with known camera poses.
*   **Output:** A trained NeRF model (MLP and/or voxel grid) representing the scene's radiance field.

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model:** Standard MLP architecture as defined in [NeRF Training Setup](13271-nerf-training-setup.md).
*   **Ray Batch:** A batch of 2,048 rays [S2, unofficial].
*   **Samples:** 64 samples per ray in the coarse phase and 128 samples in the fine phase [S2, unofficial].

**Training Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling:** For each training step, sample 2,048 rays [S2, unofficial]. Along each ray, evaluate the NeRF at 64 positions for the coarse phase and 128 positions for the fine phase [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Volume Rendering:** Compute the predicted HDR color $C$ and opacity $\alpha$ for each ray using the standard volume rendering integral [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Tone Mapping:** Apply a tone-mapping operator to the predicted HDR pixel values to convert them into LDR pixel values [S1]. Let $T(\cdot)$ denote this tone-mapping function.
4.  **Ground Truth Preparation:** Apply the same tone-mapping operator $T(\cdot)$ to the ground truth LDR views [S1].
5.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the loss by comparing the tone-mapped predictions to the tone-mapped ground truth LDR views [S1]. The loss is typically the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between $T(C_{pred})$ and $T(C_{gt})$ [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Update the NeRF parameters using gradient descent. Training is stopped after 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Batch Size:** 2,048 rays [S2, unofficial].
*   **Samples (Coarse):** 64 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Samples (Fine):** 128 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Resolution:** 800 × 600 pixels [S2, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Training directly on HDR views can fail to encode all scene areas well due to the high dynamic range, even when using tone-mapped colors for loss calculation [S1]. This is because the vast range of HDR values can make it difficult for the network to learn fine details in darker or brighter regions simultaneously [S1].

## Subtopics

### NeRF Baseline
The standard NeRF baseline uses the same training configuration (64 coarse samples, 128 fine samples, 2,048 rays per batch, 800x600 resolution, 200,000 steps) but typically operates on LDR data without the specific HDR tone-mapping loss strategy described in the NeRF-GT setup [S2, unofficial]. It serves as the primary comparison point for the NeRF-GT baseline to demonstrate the impact of HDR training [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is approximately 0.40 seconds per iteration [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Rendering time is approximately 32 seconds per frame [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides an upper-bound reference for HDR synthesis methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Difficult to ensure all areas of a scene are encoded well due to the high dynamic range [S1].
*   **When to use:** When establishing a reproducible baseline for HDR NeRF variants or when evaluating the performance limits of NeRF on HDR data [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are limited and LDR training is sufficient, or when the scene has extreme dynamic range that causes convergence issues [S1].

## Background
The NeRF-GT baseline originates from the work on HDR-NeRF, which introduced the concept of training NeRF models directly on HDR views to serve as a ground truth or upper bound for novel HDR view synthesis [S1]. The specific training parameters (64/128 samples, 2048 rays, 200k steps) are adapted from the original NeRF implementation's recommended configuration for scene fitting [S2, unofficial]. The setup is used to evaluate the performance of HDR-NeRF against other methods like IBRNet and NLR [S2, unofficial].
