# Lighting and Appearance Models


## Overview
Lighting and Appearance Models modify the standard NeRF architecture to explicitly represent, decompose, or stylize illumination and material properties, addressing the standard formulation's lack of semantic lighting control and its inability to handle varying lighting conditions or artistic styles [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]. This family distinguishes itself by replacing or augmenting the view-dependent color MLP with explicit lighting bases, pre-integrated neural queries, or decoupled stylization networks, rather than relying solely on appearance embeddings [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) serves as the baseline for most modifications. It consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a coarse network and a fine network. The coarse network takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs a latent feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and volume density $\sigma$. The fine network takes $\mathbf{h}$, positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and positional encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$ [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
$$

Modifications generally fall into three categories [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
    [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs) [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md)
Replaces numerical light integration with an MLP query for pre-integrated light, capturing fine illumination details better than SH or SG [13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md].

### [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md)
Replaces the view-dependent color MLP with an explicit 2nd-order SH model, enabling direct relighting via learnable coefficients [14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md].

### [StylizedNeRF](14882-stylizednerf.md)
Jointly optimizes a 3D radiance field and a 2D stylization network with latent codes to maintain geometric consistency while applying artistic styles [14882-stylizednerf.md].

## Background
The standard NeRF formulation implicitly encodes lighting in the view-dependent color output, limiting explicit control [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]. These methods emerged to address specific limitations: SH NeRF for explicit relighting [14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md], Neural-PIL for efficient, detailed illumination representation [13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md], and StylizedNeRF for artistic style transfer [14882-stylizednerf.md].
