# Rip-NeRF

Also called: Rip-NeRF25k

## Overview
Rip-NeRF is a hybrid radiance field method that renders high-fidelity, anti-aliased images by combining cone casting with anisotropic 3D Gaussian characterization [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like Zip-NeRF by using Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids for efficient anisotropic area-sampling, whereas Zip-NeRF relies on a multi-sampling mechanism that is effective but computationally inefficient [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated multi-view 2D images.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB images and optimized scene parameters (Ripmap features, Gaussian parameters).

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ripmap Encoding:** A hierarchical feature grid.
    *   **Base Level ($R_{0,0}$):** Learnable tensor of shape $H \times W \times C$. Default: $512 \times 512 \times 16$ [S1].
    *   **Derived Levels:** Generated from the base level using average pooling, not learnable [S1].
2.  **Platonic Solid:** A geometric proxy for the view space. Default: Icosahedron with 10 unparalleled faces [S1].
3.  **3D Gaussian:** Characterizes a conical frustum. Defined by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].
4.  **Tiny MLP ($F_\theta$):** Takes aggregated features and view direction to output density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Cone Casting and Frustum Division:**
    For each pixel, cast a cone into the scene. Divide the cone into multiple conical frustums [S1].

2.  **3D Gaussian Characterization:**
    Characterize each conical frustum as an anisotropic 3D Gaussian parameterized by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].

3.  **Platonic Solid Projection:**
    Project the 3D Gaussian onto the $n$ faces $\{P_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ of the Platonic solid (default $n=10$ for icosahedron) [S1].
    For each face $P_i$, compute the projected 2D Gaussian parameters $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj})$ using the projection matrix $M_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj} = M_i^T \boldsymbol{\mu} $$
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj} = M_i^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} M_i $$
    [S1]

4.  **Ripmap Encoding Query:**
    For each face $P_i$, query a feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i$ from the Ripmap Encoding using tetra-linear interpolation [S1].
    The interpolation position $(p_x, p_y)$ and level $(l_x, l_y)$ are determined by $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj}$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj}$ respectively [S1].
    The level indices are scaled by a hyper-parameter $w$ (default $w=2.0$) to adjust the probability mass coverage of the Gaussian footprint [S1].
    The Ripmap levels are derived from the base level $R_{0,0}$ via average pooling:
    $$ R_{i,j} = \begin{cases} \text{Avg}_{2\times1} R_{i, j-1} & \text{if } j \neq 0 \\ \text{Avg}_{1\times2} R_{i-1, j} & \text{if } i \neq 0 \text{ \& } j = 0 \\ R_{0,0} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $i, j$ are level indices in $x$ and $y$ directions [S1].

5.  **Feature Aggregation:**
    Concatenate all feature vectors $\{\mathbf{f}_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ from the Platonic solid faces and the encoded view direction $\mathbf{d}$ to form the final feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ [S1].

6.  **Density and Color Estimation:**
    Pass $\mathbf{f}$ through the tiny MLP $F_\theta$ to estimate density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

7.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Render the pixel color using the estimated $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ via standard volume rendering [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Optimize the system end-to-end using a photometric loss scaled by the area of the pixel footprint (area loss $L_{area}$) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** 2.6 hours on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU for 120k iterations [S1]. Rip-NeRF25k takes 32 minutes (11.76% of Zip-NeRF's 4.5h) [S1].
*   **Model Size:** 160 MB [S1].
*   **GPU Memory:** 20 GB during training [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** 3 FPS [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Outperforms Zip-NeRF in training time, model size, and rendering speed. Zip-NeRF has slightly better SSIM but requires 4.5h training, 592 MB model size, 80 GB GPU memory, and 0.25 FPS rendering [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-fidelity anti-aliasing, efficient anisotropic area-sampling, compact representation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires calibrated multi-view images [S1].

## Background
Rip-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Rip-NeRF: Anti-aliasing Radiance Fields with Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids" [S1]. It builds on the hybrid representation approach of NeRF [Mildenhall et al. 2020] and the area-sampling techniques of Mip-NeRF [Barron et al. 2021] and Tri-MipRF [Hu et al. 2023]. The method was developed to address the inefficiency of multi-sampling mechanisms in methods like Zip-NeRF while maintaining high rendering quality [S1].
