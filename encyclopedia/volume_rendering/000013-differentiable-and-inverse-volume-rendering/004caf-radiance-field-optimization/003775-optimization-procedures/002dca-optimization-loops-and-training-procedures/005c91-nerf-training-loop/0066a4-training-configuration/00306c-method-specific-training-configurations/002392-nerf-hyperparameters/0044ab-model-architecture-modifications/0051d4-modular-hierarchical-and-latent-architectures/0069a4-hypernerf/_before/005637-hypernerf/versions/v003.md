# HyperNeRF

Also called: Hyper-Space Template, Hyper-space Neural Radiance Fields, HyperNeRF (2021), HyperNeRF Template

## Overview
HyperNeRF is a method for modeling non-rigidly deforming scenes with topological variations (e.g., mouth opening/closing, peeling) by extending the canonical NeRF input space to include higher-dimensional ambient coordinates [S2][S3, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation of continuous deformation fields, which cannot model discontinuities required for topological changes, by representing each observation frame as a non-planar slice through a hyperdimensional radiance field [S2]. Unlike strictly deformation-based approaches like Nerfies, HyperNeRF uses these additional dimensions to capture topology changes while retaining a deformation field to fuse information across observations [S2].

## How it works
HyperNeRF builds on [Modular, Hierarchical, and Latent Architectures](14567-modular-hierarchical-and-latent-architectures.md) by embedding the template NeRF in a higher-dimensional space. The method combines deformation-based and modulation-based approaches: it uses deformations to model motion for well-behaved optimization and extends the 3D input coordinate space to take additional higher-dimension coordinates as input [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Set of $N$ images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^N$ with known camera poses $P_i$ and view directions $d$.
*   Spatial positions $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and view directions $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   Per-observation latent deformation codes $\omega_i$ and latent appearance codes $\psi_i$.

**Outputs:**
*   Density $\sigma$ and color $c$ for volume rendering.

### Data Structures
The method relies on three primary neural networks (MLPs):

1.  **Deformation Field ($W$):**
    *   Input: $(x, \omega_i)$ where $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $\omega_i$ is the latent deformation code.
    *   Output: $(r, v) \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$, encoding rotation and translation parameters for the spatial deformation.
2.  **Slicing Surface Field ($H$):**
    *   Input: $(x, \omega_i)$ where $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $\omega_i$ is the latent deformation code.
    *   Output: $w \in \mathbb{R}^W$, the position along the ambient axes.
3.  **Template NeRF ($F$):**
    *   Input: $(x', w, d, \psi_i)$ where $x'$ is the deformed spatial coordinate, $w$ is the ambient coordinate, $d$ is view direction, and $\psi_i$ is the latent appearance code.
    *   Output: $(c, \sigma)$.

### Parameters
*   $W$: Number of higher (ambient) dimensions. Typical value: 2 [S2].
*   $m$: Hyper-parameter for positional encoding bandwidth. $m=1$ for hyper-coordinate input $w$ to the template; $m=6$ for spatial position input $x$ to the slicing surface field $H$ [S2].
*   $\alpha$: Window function parameter for coarse-to-fine optimization, linearly increased from 0 to 1 over 10k iterations [S2].

### Steps

1.  **Positional Encoding:**
    Apply sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma_\alpha(x)$ to inputs. For the spatial position $x$ and view direction $d$, use standard encoding with bandwidth controlled by $\alpha$. For the ambient coordinate $w$, use $m=1$ [S2].
    $$ \gamma(x) = [\sin(x), \cos(x), \dots, \sin(2^{m-1}x), \cos(2^{m-1}x)]^T $$
    The windowed encoding $\gamma_\alpha(x)$ weights frequency bands $j \in \{0, \dots, m-1\}$ with:
    $$ w_j(\alpha) = \frac{1 - \cos(\pi \text{clamp}(\alpha - j, 0, 1))}{2} $$
    $$ \gamma_\alpha(x) = [w_0(\alpha)\sin(x), \dots, w_{m-1}(\alpha)\cos(2^{m-1}x)]^T $$

2.  **Deformation:**
    Compute the spatial deformation field $W(x, \omega_i)$ to obtain rotation $r$ and translation $v$. Transform the observation-space coordinate $x$ to the canonical space $x'$ using the rigid transformation defined by $(r, v)$ [S2].

3.  **Slicing Surface Query:**
    Compute the deformable slicing surface $H(x, \omega_i)$ to obtain the ambient coordinate $w$. This allows different spatial locations to reference different parts of the ambient coordinate space, creating a non-planar slice [S2].

4.  **Radiance Field Evaluation:**
    Query the template NeRF $F$ with the concatenated canonical coordinate $(x', w)$, view direction $d$, and appearance code $\psi_i$:
    $$ F: (x', w, d, \psi_i) \rightarrow (c, \sigma) $$
    The SDF is effectively queried at the coordinate obtained by concatenating $x'$ and $H(x, \omega_i)$ [S2].

5.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Perform standard volume rendering along the ray using the sampled $(c, \sigma)$ pairs [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Topological Changes:** Continuous deformation fields (e.g., Nerfies) struggle with topological changes because they require discontinuities in the deformation field, which coordinate-based MLPs with positional encoding cannot model easily due to band-limited kernel interpolation [S2]. HyperNeRF avoids this by using higher dimensions [S2].
*   **Regularization:** The elastic regularization loss from Nerfies, which penalizes non-unit singular values of the Jacobian of the deformation field, is ill-suited for scenes with topological variations because these variations directly violate its assumptions [S2].
*   **Camera Registration:** HyperNeRF is dependent on camera registration; poor registration leads to artifacts or degraded performance [S3, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion and Rapid Motion:** The method is limited to observed data and cannot faithfully reconstruct rapid motion or occluded regions [S3, unofficial].
*   **Real-world Photos:** HyperNeRF fails on real-world photos with varying illumination and transient occluders [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Strata-NeRF
A sibling method that uses stratified sampling for efficient volume rendering. It differs from HyperNeRF by focusing on sampling efficiency rather than topological variation modeling. Choose Strata-NeRF for static scenes requiring faster rendering or better sampling; choose HyperNeRF for dynamic scenes with topological changes. [Strata-NeRF](14015-strata-nerf.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is computationally intensive. Training at half 1080p resolution (960x540) for 250k iterations takes roughly 8 hours on 4 TPU v4s. Full-HD training for 1M iterations takes roughly 64 hours [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** HyperNeRF reduces average error rates by 4.1% for interpolation and 8.6% for novel-view synthesis compared to Nerfies, as measured by LPIPS [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Capable of synthesizing views in scenes with topological changes (e.g., human mouth opening, banana peeling) [S1]. Uses an optimization strategy that encourages smooth behavior in higher dimensions, enabling high-quality geometry reconstruction even with limited angle views [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on accurate camera registration; limited to observed data; fails with varying illumination and transient occluders; computational overhead [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When modeling dynamic scenes with topological variations (e.g., facial expressions, deformable objects) where continuous deformation fields fail.
*   **When not to use:** For static scenes, scenes with significant illumination changes, or scenes with transient occluders [S3, unofficial].

## Background
HyperNeRF was introduced by Keunhong Park et al. in June 2021 [S1]. It is inspired by level set methods pioneered in the late 1980s by Osher and Sethian, which model moving scenes as static objects in a higher-dimensional ambient space [S2]. The method builds on Nerfies by extending the canonical space to a higher dimension and using a slicing MLP to describe how to return to the 3D representation using ambient space coordinates [S1]. It is used for reconstructing human avatars and dynamic scenes, particularly in virtual/augmented reality and digital entertainment, where facial expressions and deformable objects require handling topological changes [S1].
