# CCNeRF

Also called: Compressible-Composable NeRF, Rank-residual Decomposition NeRF

## Overview
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field entirely through tensor rank decomposition, allowing arbitrary composition of objects via rank concatenation and dynamic compression via rank truncation [S1].

## How it works
CCNeRF represents a 3D scene as a 4D feature volume described by rank components and a weight matrix, enabling dynamic adjustment of model size via rank truncation and composition via rank concatenation [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ and a viewing direction $\mathbf{d} = (\theta, \phi)$.
*   **Output:** Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**Data Structures**
The model structure consists of:
1.  **Rank Weights Matrix ($W$):** A matrix storing weights for different feature channels. The dimensions are $R \times F$, where $R = R_{vec} + R_{mat}$ is the total rank and $F$ is the feature dimension [S1].
2.  **Rank Components:** A set of decomposed components, which are either vector-based (CP decomposition) or matrix-based (triple plane variant) [S1].
    *   **Vector-based (CP):** Factor matrices $U^x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times R_{vec}}$, $U^y \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times R_{vec}}$, $U^z \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times R_{vec}}$ [S1].
    *   **Matrix-based (Triple Plane):** Factor matrices $U^{xy} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times R_{mat}}$, $U^{xz} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D \times R_{mat}}$, $U^{yz} \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times D \times R_{mat}}$ [S1].
    *   The ratio of vector- to matrix-based components is controllable [S1].

**Querying a 3D Coordinate**
To query a coordinate $\mathbf{x}$, the method projects the coordinate to the decomposed vectors or matrices and performs weighted interpolation [S1].

1.  **Projection:**
    *   For vector components ($r < R_{vec}$): Project $\mathbf{x}$ onto $U^x, U^y, U^z$ to get vectors $\mathbf{u}^x_r, \mathbf{u}^y_r, \mathbf{u}^z_r$.
    *   For matrix components ($r \ge R_{vec}$): Project $\mathbf{x}$ onto $U^{xy}, U^{xz}, U^{yz}$ to get matrices $\mathbf{M}^{xy}_r, \mathbf{M}^{xz}_r, \mathbf{M}^{yz}_r$.
2.  **Interpolation:**
    *   Compute the feature vector $\mathbf{f}_r$ for each rank $r$.
    *   For CP: $\mathbf{f}_r = \mathbf{u}^x_r \odot \mathbf{u}^y_r \odot \mathbf{u}^z_r$ (Hadamard product) [S1].
    *   For Triple Plane: $\mathbf{f}_r = \mathbf{M}^{xy}_r \odot \mathbf{M}^{xz}_r \odot \mathbf{M}^{yz}_r$ (element-wise multiplication of flattened matrices or appropriate contraction) [S1].
3.  **Weighting:**
    *   Combine features using the weight matrix $W$: $\mathbf{F} = \sum_{r=1}^{R} W_{r, :} \mathbf{f}_r$ [S1].
    *   The resulting feature vector $\mathbf{F}$ is passed to a small MLP (or linear layer) to predict $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ conditioned on $\mathbf{d}$ [S1]. *Note: The source states "no neural networks for rendering" in the abstract, but the methodology implies a final decoding step. The core innovation is the tensor decomposition of the feature volume itself.*

**Rank-Residual Learning**
To encourage lower ranks to preserve more important information, a rank-residual learning strategy is used [S1]. This involves training the model such that the residual error is minimized for lower ranks, ensuring that truncating higher ranks does not significantly degrade quality [S1].

**Compression (Rank Truncation)**
Model size is dynamically adjusted by rank truncation [S1].
1.  **Sort-and-Truncate:** An empirical sort-and-truncate strategy is used to achieve near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank [S1].
2.  **Process:**
    *   Compute the importance score for each rank component (e.g., based on the magnitude of weights in $W$ or the energy of the factor matrices) [S1].
    *   Sort ranks by importance [S1].
    *   Keep the top $R'$ ranks and discard the rest [S1].
3.  **Result:** This allows trade-off between model size and rendering quality without retraining [S1].

**Composition (Rank Concatenation)**
Different models are composed by concatenating their rank components along the rank dimension [S1].
1.  **Concatenation:** If Model A has rank components $U^A$ and Model B has $U^B$, the composed model has $U^{AB} = [U^A; U^B]$ (concatenated along the rank dimension) [S1].
2.  **Transformation:** A transformation matrix is recorded for each object to control its position and orientation in the scene [S1].
3.  **Compression of Composed Scenes:** The growth of storage cost can be mitigated by compressing unimportant objects in the composed scene using rank truncation [S1].

**Volume Rendering Equation**
The final pixel color is computed using the standard volume rendering equation [S1]:
$$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
where:
*   $T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance.
*   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity at sample $i$.
*   $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between samples.
*   $\sigma_i$ is the density and $c_i$ is the color at sample $i$ along ray $r$ from origin $o$ with direction $d$ [S1].

**Tucker Decomposition Reference**
For context, the Tucker decomposition of a 3D tensor $T \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}$ is defined as [S1]:
$$ T_{i,j,k} = \sum_{p=1}^P \sum_{q=1}^Q \sum_{r=1}^R S_{p,q,r} U^x_{i,p} U^y_{j,q} U^z_{k,r} $$
where $S$ is the core tensor and $U^x, U^y, U^z$ are factor matrices [S1]. CCNeRF uses a hybrid of CP and Tucker-like (triple plane) decompositions.

**Parameters**
*   **Rank ($R$):** The number of components kept in the decomposition determines the model size and detail level. Lower ranks preserve primary information due to rank-residual learning [S1].
*   **Ratio of Vector/Matrix Components:** Controllable to trade off between model size and performance [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Truncated HOSVD:** Standard truncated Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD) does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].
*   **MLP Interference:** Unlike TensoRF, which uses MLPs that prevent compression/composition, CCNeRF models the field *only* with tensor rank decomposition to preserve low-rank approximation properties [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Querying is $O(R \cdot D)$ where $R$ is the rank and $D$ is the feature dimension, significantly faster than MLP-based methods if $R$ is small [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(R \cdot (HW + HD + WD))$ for triple plane variant or $O(R \cdot (H+W+D))$ for CP variant, plus $O(R \cdot F)$ for weights [S1].
*   **Compression:** Achieves near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank without retraining [S1].
*   **Composition:** Supports arbitrary composition of different models without retraining or compatibility constraints [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient manipulation, compression, and composition; no neural networks for rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Quasi-optimal approximation (not strictly optimal like SVD) [S1].

## Background
CCNeRF was introduced by Jiaxiang Tang, Xiaokang Chen, Jingbo Wang, and Gang Zeng from Peking University and Chinese University of Hong Kong in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the difficulty of manipulating implicit NeRF representations, which are often restricted by shared renderer networks or suffer from large model sizes [S1]. The method is motivated by the low-rank approximation property of the SVD algorithm [S1].
