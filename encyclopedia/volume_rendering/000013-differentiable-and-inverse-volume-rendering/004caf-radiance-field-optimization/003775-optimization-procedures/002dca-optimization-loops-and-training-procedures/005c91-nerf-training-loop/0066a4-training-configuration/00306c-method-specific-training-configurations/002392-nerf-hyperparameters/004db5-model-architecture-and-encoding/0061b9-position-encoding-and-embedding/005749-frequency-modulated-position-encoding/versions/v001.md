# Frequency-Modulated Position Encoding

Also called: FM-NeRF Encoding, Frequency-Modulated Point Cloud Rendering with Easy Editing

## Overview
Frequency-Modulated Position Encoding is a coordinate encoding scheme used in point-based differentiable volume rendering to map 3D spatial coordinates and viewing directions to feature vectors. It addresses the problem of representing high-frequency geometric details and view-dependent appearance in sparse point clouds by applying frequency modulation using sine and cosine basis functions with exponentially spaced frequencies [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF positional encoding by narrowing the encoding interval and using specific frequency bands ($L=10$ for space, $L=2$ for direction) tailored for point cloud rasterization, which compensates for sparsity and enables efficient editing [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A 3D spatial coordinate $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ or a viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in S^2$.
*   **Output:** A feature vector $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ or $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ of dimension $2L$, where $L$ is the number of frequency bands.

**Data Structures**
The encoding produces a fixed-length vector. For spatial coordinates, the output dimension is $2 \times 10 = 20$. For view directions, the output dimension is $2 \times 2 = 4$. These vectors are concatenated with other features (e.g., point attributes) and fed into the subsequent network (e.g., AFNet MLP) [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $L$: Number of frequency bands. Set to $10$ for spatial coordinates and $2$ for view directions [S1].
*   The base frequency scaling is implicit in the exponent $2^k$ where $k$ ranges from $0$ to $L-1$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Bleeding Problem:** If the rasterization radius (used to expand points to disks) is too small, points from occluded surfaces and the background can be seen through the front surface [S1].
*   **Edge Expansion:** If the rasterization radius is too large, it leads to inaccurate depth estimation and object edges expand [S1].
*   **Low Opacity Points:** Points with low opacity should be removed after training convergence to clean the representation [S1].
*   **Point Cloud Sparsity:** The method relies on expanding points to disks to compensate for sparsity; the radius must be tuned based on point cloud density [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity**
*   **Training Time:** The method requires approximately 10 minutes to achieve realistic rendering and about 10 hours to almost converge. One epoch consumes approximately 12.8 seconds on the NeRF-Synthetic dataset [S1].
*   **Preprocessing:** Requires 4-20 loops of point cloud denoising and completion, with 300 epochs of training per denoising step [S1].

**Measured Performance**
*   Achieves higher PSNR and SSIM on NeRF-Synthetic and Tanks and Temples datasets compared to NeRF, CCNeRF, NPBG, NPBG++, and Huang et al. [S1].
*   Comparable or better results on ScanNet and DTU datasets [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations**
*   **Strengths:** Fast convergence to realistic rendering; supports easy editing via point cloud masking; handles sparse point clouds effectively via frequency modulation and disk expansion [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of rasterization radius to avoid bleeding or edge expansion; sensitive to initial point cloud quality (requires denoising/completion loops) [S1].

## Background
Frequency-Modulated Position Encoding was introduced in the paper "Frequency-Modulated Point Cloud Rendering with Easy Editing" [S1]. It builds upon the positional encoding form of NeRF [S1] but adapts it for point-based rendering by narrowing the encoding interval. The method is designed to facilitate easy editing of 3D scenes by leveraging the properties of frequency-modulated point clouds [S1].
