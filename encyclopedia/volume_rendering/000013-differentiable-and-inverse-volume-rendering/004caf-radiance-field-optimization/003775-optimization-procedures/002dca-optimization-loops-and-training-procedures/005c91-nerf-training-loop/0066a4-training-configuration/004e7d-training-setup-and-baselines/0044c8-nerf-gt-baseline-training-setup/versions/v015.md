# NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup
Also called: NeRF-GT, HDR-NeRF Baseline, Vanilla NeRF with HDR
## Overview
The NeRF-GT baseline establishes a reference for training Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) using High Dynamic Range (HDR) input views. It solves the problem of establishing a performance upper bound for novel view synthesis by training directly on HDR data, where predicted HDR pixel values are tone-mapped to Low Dynamic Range (LDR) for loss computation against tone-mapped ground truth [S1]. It differs from standard LDR training by attempting to encode the full dynamic range of the scene, though this approach is noted to be difficult due to the high dynamic range causing poor encoding of scene areas [S1].
## How it works
### Inputs and outputs
- **Inputs**: HDR views of the scene (or consistently exposed LDR views considered as HDR proxies), camera poses, and intrinsics.
- **Outputs**: A trained NeRF model capable of rendering HDR views, and the associated training metrics.

### Data structures
The method relies on the standard NeRF data structures defined in [52-volume-rendering.md], specifically the volume representation (MLP mapping $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi) \to (\sigma, c)$) and the ray sampling strategy.

### Steps
1. **Train the NeRF model** using HDR views as input [S1].
2. **Render and Tone-map**: During training, render the predicted HDR pixel values for each ray. Apply a tone-mapping operator to convert these HDR predictions into LDR pixel values [S1].
3. **Compute Loss**: Apply the same tone-mapping operator to the ground truth LDR views. Compute the photometric loss (typically Mean Squared Error) between the tone-mapped predicted LDR values and the tone-mapped ground truth LDR values [S1].

### Parameters
- **Sampling**: 64 samples along each ray in the coarse phase and 128 samples in the fine phase [S2, unofficial].
- **Batch Size**: 2,048 rays processed in each batch [S2, unofficial].
- **Resolution**: 800 × 600 pixels [S2, unofficial].
- **Iterations**: Training stopped after 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

### Pitfalls
- **HDR Training Failure**: Training directly on HDR views can fail to encode all areas of a scene well, even when using tone-mapped colors for loss calculation. This is attributed to the high dynamic range of the scenes [S1].

## Subtopics

### NeRF Baseline
The standard NeRF baseline typically refers to training on LDR data. In the context of the NeRF-GT comparison, the "NeRF Baseline" serves as the standard LDR training configuration, whereas NeRF-GT specifically utilizes HDR inputs to test the limits of dynamic range encoding [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: The training time is approximately 0.40 seconds per iteration [S2, unofficial].
- **Rendering Time**: Rendering time is approximately 32 seconds per frame [S2, unofficial].
- **Scaling**: The method scales with the number of solver steps (200,000 steps recommended for convergence) [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Provides an upper bound for performance when HDR data is available [S1].
- **Limitations**: Difficult to ensure all areas of a scene are encoded well due to high dynamic range issues [S1].

## Background
The NeRF-GT baseline originates from the work on HDR-NeRF, which introduced the concept of training NeRF models directly from HDR views to evaluate the potential of high dynamic range in neural radiance fields [S1]. The specific training parameters (64/128 samples, 2048 rays, 200k steps) are derived from the original NeRF implementation's configuration (config_fern.txt) as used in comparative studies [S2, unofficial].
