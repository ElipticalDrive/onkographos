# Human and Specific Subject Modeling

Also called: Human NeRF, Dynamic Human NeRF, Avatar NeRF

## Overview
This family of methods adapts the standard NeRF architecture [Parent](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md) to model dynamic human subjects, enabling novel view synthesis and novel pose rendering from monocular or sparse multi-view inputs. Unlike static scene modeling, these methods must handle non-rigid deformation, occlusion, and the specific topology of the human body. They distinguish themselves from siblings by explicitly integrating parametric human priors (such as SMPL) or structured latent codes anchored to deformable meshes, rather than relying solely on implicit MLP representations or UV-mapping [S1].

## Shared principles
The core challenge in human-specific modeling is bridging the gap between the continuous, unstructured nature of NeRF and the structured, articulated nature of the human body. All methods in this family share the following mechanisms:

1.  **Canonical Space Transformation:** To decouple geometry from pose, query points $\mathbf{x}_t$ in the target (observation) space are transformed into a canonical (T-pose) space $\mathbf{x}_c$. This allows the radiance field to learn a static representation of the subject's geometry and appearance, which is then warped by the current pose.
    $$ \mathbf{x}_c = \mathcal{T}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_t, \theta_t) $$
    where $\mathcal{T}^{-1}$ is the inverse deformation field and $\theta_t$ represents the pose parameters.

2.  **Parametric Priors:** Most methods incorporate a parametric human body model (e.g., SMPL) to provide initial rigid deformation estimates. The deformation field $\mathcal{T}$ is typically composed of a rigid component (Linear Blending Skinning, LBS) based on SMPL joints and weights, and a non-rigid residual component predicted by a neural network.
    $$ \mathbf{x}_c = \text{LBS}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{w}_{smpl}, \theta_t) + \Delta \phi(\mathbf{x}_t) $$
    where $\mathbf{w}_{smpl}$ are the SMPL blending weights and $\Delta \phi$ is the learned non-rigid deformation.

3.  **Pose-Conditioned Appearance:** The color output is modulated by the pose to account for view-dependent effects and clothing folds that change with articulation. This is often achieved by conditioning the appearance MLP on pose parameters or by using pose-dependent shading factors.

4.  **Volume Rendering Integration:** The final pixel color is computed via standard volume rendering integration along the ray, using the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ predicted at the canonical points:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$

## Subtopics

### [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md)
UVA is a neural representation for dynamic human avatars that enables local and independent editing of both geometry and texture while supporting novel view synthesis and novel pose rendering. It distinguishes itself from UV-mapping-based methods by anchoring structured latent codes to a deformable mesh in canonical space, allowing direct 3D-to-3D manipulation, and from two-stage distillation methods by being end-to-end trainable from scratch without manual parameter tuning. It uses a deformable canonical mesh with anchor nodes storing geometry and appearance latent codes, interpolated via Top-K nearest neighbors. A signed height indicator $\tilde{h} = [u, v, \bar{d}]$ provides local geometric context to resolve spatial ambiguity in latent interpolation.

### [HumanNeRF](13842-humannerf.md)
HumanNeRF is a neural representation for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans that generalizes to unseen subjects and camera settings using sparse RGB inputs, avoiding per-scene training through aggregated pixel-alignment features and a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field. It differs from methods like [37] which generate blur texture results due to reliance on implicit texture representation, by combining image-based rendering with NeRF-based volume rendering into a novel neural blending scheme. It employs a Feature Extractor Network (U-Net), a Feature Blending Network, a Deformation Network using SMPL joint distances/directions, and a Generalizable Dynamic NeRF. An optional Appearance Blending Network refines high-frequency details.

### [HumanNeRF-SE](15640-humannerf-se.md)
HumanNeRF-SE synthesizes novel pose images of humans from monocular video inputs by combining explicit SMPL shape priors with an implicit NeRF representation. It avoids the overfitting and high computational complexity of previous methods by using frozen SMPL blending weights for rigid deformation and a sparse voxel volume to filter irrelevant sampling points. Unlike siblings [HumanNeRF](13842-humannerf.md) and [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md) which often rely on learnable weight volumes or frame-level features, HumanNeRF-SE uses only point-level spatial-aware features to ensure pose generalization. It utilizes a Conv-Filter to zero out density for non-human voxels, preventing color diffusion into the background.

#### LISA
LISA is a method targeting the modeling of hands by approximating human hands with a collection of rigid parts [S1]. Query points are input into MLPs, which are used to predict geometry (via SDFs) and color [S1].

#### NeuMan Network Architecture
NeuMan is a NeRF architecture consisting of a coarse sub-model and a fine sub-model for the scene, and a single sub-model for the human NeRF model, leveraging SMPL geometry priors [S2]. Unlike NeuralBody which overfits to training observations and produces poor rendering on the back of the subject, NeuMan generalizes better and faithfully renders the back [S2].

## Background
The modeling of dynamic humans in NeRF emerged as a distinct sub-area following the success of static NeRF . Early works like NeuralBody [33] demonstrated the potential but suffered from overfitting and poor generalization to unseen poses [S2]. The field evolved to incorporate parametric models like SMPL to constrain the deformation space, leading to methods such as NeuMan [S2], HumanNeRF [S1], and UVA [S1]. Recent trends include diffusion-based generation (DreamAvatar, DreamHuman) and face-specific avatars (Neural Head Avatar, IMAvatar) [S1].
