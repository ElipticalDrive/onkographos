# Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details

Also called: JaxNeRF Training Procedure, Mip-NeRF Misc. Changes

## Overview
This article defines the specific implementation details for training Mip-NeRF and JaxNeRF, focusing on activation functions, input encoding strategies, and learning rate scheduling [S1]. These modifications are designed to improve training stability and robustness, particularly against catastrophic failure modes, without significantly altering the method's accuracy or speed [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF implementations, Mip-NeRF removes the identity concatenation from the MLP input and employs shifted softplus and widened sigmoid activations to ensure smoother optimization landscapes [S1].

## How it works
The training process minimizes the difference between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images using the Adam optimizer [S1]. The core loop involves ray sampling, point sampling, feature encoding, network forward pass, volume rendering, loss computation, and optimization, building on the shared principles of the parent configuration [Parent].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training images, camera intrinsics, camera extrinsics, and dataset metadata (near/far clipping planes) [Parent].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights for density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Bundle:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ for origins and $[B, 3]$ for directions, where $B$ is the batch size in rays [Parent].
*   **Query Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, N, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates along rays, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray [Parent].
*   **Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE):** The input to the MLP is the IPE only, derived from the mean and covariance of the conical frustums, without concatenation with the original position and view direction [S1].

### Network Architecture and Forward Pass
The MLP predicts density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

1.  **Input Encoding:** The input to the MLP consists solely of the Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) features [S1]. The identity encoding (concatenation of position and view direction with their positional encodings) is removed [S1].
2.  **Density Prediction:** The MLP outputs a raw value $x_\tau$. The density $\tau$ is computed using a shifted softplus activation:
    $$ \tau = \log(1 + \exp(x_\tau - 1)) $$
    This shift is equivalent to initializing the biases for $\tau$ to $-1$, resulting in small initial density values which accelerates early optimization by preventing dense scene content from suppressing gradients from content behind it [S1].
3.  **View Direction Handling:** The ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ is normalized. A positional encoding is applied with $L=4$ frequencies [S1]. This encoded view direction is injected into the last layer of the MLP after $\tau$ is predicted but before $\mathbf{c}$ is predicted [S1].
4.  **Color Prediction:** The MLP outputs a raw value $x_c$. The color $\mathbf{c}$ is computed using a "widened" sigmoid activation:
    $$ \mathbf{c} = \frac{1 + 2\epsilon}{1 + \exp(-x_c)} - \epsilon $$
    where $\epsilon = 0.001$ [S1]. This saturation slightly outside $[0, 1]$ avoids optimization failure when explaining black or white pixels [S1].

### Optimization Loop
The optimization uses the Adam optimizer [S1].

1.  **Batch Size:** 4096 rays per batch [S1].
2.  **Total Iterations:** 1,000,000 iterations [S1].
3.  **Learning Rate Schedule:** The learning rate $\eta_i$ at iteration $i$ is determined by a combination of a warmup schedule and logarithmic annealing [S1]:
    $$ \eta_i = \left( \lambda_w + (1 - \lambda_w) \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} \operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{i}{n_w}, 0, 1\right)\right) \right) \times \left( \exp\left( (1 - \frac{i}{n}) \log(\eta_0) + \frac{i}{n} \log(\eta_n) \right) \right) $$
    Where:
    *   $n = 1,000,000$ is the total number of iterations [S1].
    *   $n_w = 2,500$ is the number of warmup iterations [S1].
    *   $\eta_0 = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ is the initial learning rate [S1].
    *   $\eta_n = 5 \cdot 10^{-6}$ is the final learning rate [S1].
    *   $\lambda_w$ is a scale factor annealed from $0.01$ to $1$ during the warmup period [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Catastrophic Failure:** Using standard ReLU for density can cause the MLP to emit negative values everywhere, leading to zero gradients and optimization failure. The shifted softplus prevents this [S1].
*   **Black/White Pixel Saturation:** Standard sigmoid saturation can cause optimization failure when trying to explain extreme pixel values. The widened sigmoid mitigates this [S1].
*   **Identity Concatenation:** Including the identity encoding makes the formalization of IPE features challenging and does not meaningfully improve performance [S1].

This configuration defines the specific optimization setup for Mip-NeRF 360, combining a multi-stage proposal sampling strategy with a robust reconstruction loss. ### [Scale-Dependent Downweighting for Grid Interpolation](8571-scale-dependent-downweighting-for-grid-interpolation.md)
This technique anti-aliases trilinear interpolation in neural radiance fields by downweighting features interpolated from grid cells that are small relative to the sampling Gaussian's spread. Unlike naive trilinear interpolation, which causes aliasing at high frequencies, this method uses the Gaussian CDF to weight samples, similar to Mip-NeRF's IPE features but applied to grid-based representations.

### [S-NeRF Training Configuration](15165-s-nerf-training-configuration.md)
S-NeRF training configuration defines the specific hyperparameters and confidence-based loss weighting for optimizing NeRFs for dynamic street scenes using LiDAR and RGB data. It distinguishes itself by employing a dual-phase training loop (foreground vehicles vs. background street views) and integrating geometry/projection confidence maps to mitigate depth outliers from moving objects. This configuration builds on Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details but adds specific LiDAR-guided confidence mechanisms and distinct MLP topologies for foreground/background separation.

### Alternative Gaussian Positional Encoding
An alternative approach explored for featurizing the mean and covariance matrix of the multivariate Gaussians used by Mip-NeRF involves applying positional encoding to the mean and the (signed) square root of the elements of the covariance matrix, then concatenating them as input to the MLP [S1]. Specifically, positional encoding with $L=12$ is applied to the mean $\mu$, and positional encoding with $L=2$ is applied to $\operatorname{vec}(\operatorname{triu}(\operatorname{sign}(\Sigma) \circ \sqrt{|\Sigma|}))$ [S1]. This alternative performs comparably to IPE features but is sensitive to hyperparameters $L$ and covariance parameterization, whereas IPE is hyperparameter-free and compact [S1]. Additionally, while IPE features are insensitive to off-diagonal elements of $\Sigma$ (correlation), experiments show that modeling this correlation does not improve accuracy [S1].

### Reflected Cone Derivation
A geometric derivation to shift the origin of a reflected ray so that its cone radius matches the incident ray's cone radius at the intersection point, ensuring consistent view-dependent appearance for reflections [S2]. The condition for equating radii is $\dot{r}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{o}\| = \dot{r}'\|\bar{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{o}'\|$, where $\dot{r}$ is the incident ray radius parameter, $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$ is the intersection point, $\mathbf{o}$ is the incident ray origin, $\dot{r}'$ is the reflected ray radius parameter, and $\mathbf{o}'$ is the reflected ray origin [S2]. Solving this equation along with the constraint that $\mathbf{o}'$ lies on the reflected ray yields the shifted origin [S2].

### RawNeRF
A method for High Dynamic Range (HDR) image view synthesis and denoising that adapts Mip-NeRF to use raw linear images as training data, allowing post-processing after rendering [S3]. Unlike HDR-NeRF which uses LDR images, RawNeRF uses raw linear images, allowing varying exposure and tone-mapping curves to be applied after NeRF rendering [S3]. It is supervised with variable exposure images, scaling the NeRF models’ “exposure” by the training image’s shutter speed, as well as a per-channel learned scaling factor [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** Mip-NeRF is 13x faster to train than a brute-force supersampling baseline (16 rays/pixel) [S1].
*   **Evaluation Speed:** Mip-NeRF is 16x faster to evaluate than the supersampling baseline [S1].
*   **Quality:** Mip-NeRF outperforms the supersampling baseline in quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Stability:** The activation functions and learning rate schedule improve training stability, particularly with large learning rates, though the specific results in the paper use lower learning rates for fair comparison [S1].
*   **Scalability:** The method uses a single unified multiscale model, reducing the complexity compared to NeRF's separate per-scale models [S1].

## Background
Mip-NeRF was introduced by Barron et al. in 2021 [S1]. The optimization details described here are from the JaxNeRF codebase, which was used to train Mip-NeRF and JaxNeRF [S1]. The method builds on the original NeRF paper by Mildenhall et al. [S1]. RawNeRF, an adaptation of Mip-NeRF for HDR, was created by Mildenhall et al. in November 2021 [S3]. NeRF-Casting, which uses the reflected cone derivation, was introduced in 2024 [S2].
