# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Dynamic Neural Radiance Fields](15537-dynamic-neural-radiance-fields.md) by replacing time-conditioned feature interpolation with a deformation field guided by a Kalman filter, leveraging the inductive smoothness bias of MLPs to fit motion more effectively than high-frequency feature signals [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamps $t_i$, and image observations.
*   **Outputs:** Deformation field $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ (warping points to canonical space), volume density $\sigma$, and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-plane Representation:** Three sets of mutually orthogonal 2D planes encode the canonical space [S1]. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
*   **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP that takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ as input [S1].
*   **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that takes noise terms and timestamps to compute the Kalman gain [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$
    y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i)
    $$

2.  **Prediction Step:** Calculate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using deformations from the previous two frames ($t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$) [S1].
    $$
    v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i}
    $$
    where $d\mathbf{x}_{t}$ is the deformation at time $t$, and $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    
    Compute the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using local linear motion assumption:
    $$
    \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}
    $$
    [S1].

3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ [S1].
    $$
    K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1})
    $$
    [S1].

4.  **Deformation Estimation (Update):** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$
    \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-})
    $$
    [S1].

5.  **Warping and Rendering:** Warp all points in real space to a time-independent canonical space using $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]. Render the radiance field using the standard volume rendering equation with the canonical space features [S1].

**Training Strategy:**
*   **Chronological Release:** Training images are gradually released in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Weighting Balance:** During initial training stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. In later stages, observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv}
$$
where:
*   $\mathcal{L}_{image}$ is the standard image reconstruction loss [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{kf}$ is the Kalman loss, minimizing the error between observation and estimated deformation:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2
    $$
    [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{co}$ is the Canonical Observation Loss, a soft regularization for frame $t_0$:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x}
    $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ is the proposal sampling loss for density field consistency [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{tv}$ is the Total Variation Loss applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2)
    $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{tv}$ (Total Variation Weight): $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Observer MLP depth: 2 layers [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, 
                              d_x_prev_1, d_x_prev_2, eps_prev_1):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter guidance.
    
    Args:
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP taking (x, t) -> (y, eps)
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer taking (eps_curr, eps_prev, t_curr, t_prev) -> K
        x: 3D point coordinates
        t_i: Current timestamp
        t_prev_1: Previous timestamp (t_{i-1})
        t_prev_2: Timestamp before previous (t_{i-2})
        d_x_prev_1: Deformation at t_{i-1}
        d_x_prev_2: Deformation at t_{i-2}
        eps_prev_1: Noise terms at t_{i-1}
        
    Returns:
        d_x_hat: Estimated deformation at t_i
    """
    # 1. Observer Step for current frame
    y_t_i, eps_t_i = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # 2. Prediction Step
    # Calculate velocity using previous two frames
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev_1
    v_t_i = (d_x_prev_1 - d_x_prev_2) / delta_t
    
    # Predict deformation based on local linear motion
    d_x_pred_minus = 2 * d_x_prev_1 - d_x_prev_2
    
    # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Note: eps_prev_2 is not passed directly here but would be needed if strictly following 
    # the formula K = f(eps_t_i, eps_t_{i-1}, t_i, t_{i-1}). 
    # The source specifies inputs as eps_t_i, eps_t_{i-1}, t_i, t_{i-1}.
    # We assume eps_prev_1 corresponds to eps_t_{i-1}.
    K_t_i = kalman_gain_net(eps_t_i, eps_prev_1, t_i, t_prev_1)
    
    # 4. Update Step
    d_x_hat = d_x_pred_minus + K_t_i * (y_t_i - d_x_pred_minus)
    
    return d_x_hat, y_t_i, eps_t_i

def kfd_nerf_training_loop(dataset, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, tri_plane, renderer):
    """
    Simplified training loop for KFD-NeRF.
    """
    # Initialize deformation history for each point
    # In practice, this is handled by the network's internal state or explicit storage
    # for the specific points being sampled.
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs):
        # Chronological release strategy: 
        # In early epochs, only use frames 0..k. In later epochs, expand k.
        current_frames = get_chronological_subset(epoch, dataset)
        
        for frame in current_frames:
            x, t_i = sample_rays(frame)
            
            # Get previous deformations from history
            # Note: In a real implementation, d_x_prev_1 and d_x_prev_2 
            # are outputs from previous forward passes or stored states.
            d_x_prev_1, eps_prev_1 = get_history_state(t_i - 1)
            d_x_prev_2, eps_prev_2 = get_history_state(t_i - 2)
            
            # Compute deformation
            d_x_hat, y_t_i, eps_t_i = kfd_nerf_deformation_step(
                observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, x, t_i, 
                t_i - 1, t_i - 2, 
                d_x_prev_1, d_x_prev_2, eps_prev_1
            )
            
            # Warp points to canonical space
            x_canonical = x - d_x_hat
            
            # Render using tri-plane and canonical points
            sigma, color = renderer(tri_plane, x_canonical)
            
            # Compute losses
            loss_image = compute_image_loss(color, frame.image)
            loss_kf = compute_kalman_loss(y_t_i, d_x_hat)
            
            # Canonical observation loss (only for t=0)
            loss_co = 0
            if t_i == 0:
                loss_co = compute_canonical_observation_loss(d_x_hat)
                
            # Total variation loss on tri-planes
            loss_tv = compute_total_variation_loss(tri_plane)
            
            # Proposal loss (requires proposal network)
            loss_prop = compute_proposal_loss(renderer, tri_plane)
            
            # Total loss
            lambda_tv = 1e-4
            total_loss = loss_image + loss_kf + loss_co + loss_prop + lambda_tv * loss_tv
            
            # Backpropagate
            total_loss.backward()
            
            # Update history for next step
            update_history(t_i, d_x_hat, eps_t_i)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The use of a tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations, allowing for high-resolution canonical space reconstruction [S1]. The shallow observer MLP (2 layers) further reduces computational cost [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method demonstrates superior view synthesis performance with thorough training, achieving state-of-the-art results on synthetic and real data within comparable computational time to other dynamic NeRF methods [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate deformation estimation from scene observations and predictions; efficient tri-plane representation allows for rapid convergence and high quality; Kalman filter balances early-stage convergence (via prediction) and fine-detail recovery (via observation) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of local linear motion, which may not capture highly non-linear dynamics perfectly; requires careful tuning of the chronological release strategy [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for dynamic scenes where motion can be approximated as locally linear and where high-quality deformation fields are preferred over feature interpolation [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with abrupt, non-linear motion changes that violate the local linear assumption may suffer from prediction errors [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Y. Zhan, Z. Li, et al. in 2024 [S1]. It builds on the concept of modeling 4D radiance fields as dynamic systems, drawing inspiration from control theory and the Kalman filter [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of previous dynamic NeRF approaches that either disregard motion priors or use feature interpolation which is hard to fit due to high-frequency signals [S1]. It replaces the MLP-based backbone of D-NeRF with a tri-plane representation (TiNeuVox) to enable a shallower observation MLP [S1].
