# RoseNeRF

Also called: Refracting Once is Enough, Refracting once is enough for NeRF

## Overview
RoseNeRF is a method for high-fidelity novel view synthesis of real refractive objects that simplifies complex ray interactions inside objects to a single refraction event [S1]. It models refraction by predicting a single refracted ray using a 4D light field representation and a refraction network, avoiding the need for Index of Refraction (IoR) fields or detailed surface reconstructions [S1]. Unlike deformation field methods that predict position offsets for each sampling point, RoseNeRF predicts the refracted ray and uses hierarchical sampling, avoiding the need for point-by-point deformation and additional priors like IoR [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera rays defined by origin $o$ and direction $d$; ground truth images for supervision.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel colors $\hat{C}(r)$ for novel viewpoints.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ray Representation:** Rays are parameterized as a 4D light field coordinate $(u, v, s, t)$ by mapping intersections with two planes at $Z_0=0$ and $Z_1=1$ [S1].
2.  **NeRF Spatial MLP:** Standard NeRF architecture with 256 hidden units, taking positional encoding $\gamma(x)$ as input [S1].
3.  **Refraction Network (RefNet):**
    *   **Student Network ($\Theta_s$):** 2-layer MLP with 64 hidden units. Input: 4D light field $(u, v, s, t)$. Output: 4D refracted ray coordinates $(u_{nr}, v_{nr}, s_{nr}, t_{nr})$ [S1].
    *   **Teacher Network ($\Theta_t$):** 3-layer MLP with 128 hidden units. Input: 5D vector $(u, v, s, t, N)$ where $N$ is the surface normal. Output: 4D refracted ray coordinates $(u_{nr}, v_{nr}, s_{nr}, t_{nr})$ [S1].
4.  **Decoder MLP ($\Theta_D$):** 2-layer MLP with 256 hidden units and ReLU activations. Input: Densities from original and refracted rays. Output: Combined density [S1].
5.  **Directional MLP:** Standard NeRF directional MLP. Input: Combined density and direction. Output: View-dependent color [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Positional Encoding:** Project sampled points $x$ along rays $r(t) = o + td$ using sinusoidal positional encoding:
    $$ \gamma(x) = [\sin(x), \cos(x), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1}x), \cos(2^{L-1}x)] $$
    where $L$ is the highest frequency hyperparameter [S1].

2.  **Ray Parameterization:** Convert incident ray direction $d$ to 4D light field coordinates $(u, v, s, t)$ by intersecting with planes $Z_0=0$ and $Z_1=1$. The Cartesian direction is recovered via:
    $$ d = (Z_1 - Z_0, u - s, v - t) $$
    where $Z_0=0$ and $Z_1=1$ [S1].

3.  **Initial Sampling (Warm-up):** In the first 5k warm-up iterations, sample only along the original ray. Estimate the surface normal $N$ using the NeRF spatial MLP [S1]. During this phase, only density $\sigma$ is queried; the decoder is not used [S1].

4.  **Refracted Ray Prediction:**
    *   Use the Student RefNet ($\Theta_s$) to predict the refracted ray coordinates $(u_{nr}, v_{nr}, s_{nr}, t_{nr})$ from the incident 4D light field $(u, v, s, t)$ [S1].
    *   Convert predicted 4D coordinates back to Cartesian direction $d_r$ using the same conversion as Step 2 [S1].
    *   Determine the origin of the refracted ray $o_r$ at its intersection with the camera plane $Z = z_c$ (where $z_c$ is the camera's Z-coordinate). The coordinates satisfy:
        $$ \frac{u - x_r}{s - x_r} = \frac{v - y_r}{t - y_r} = \frac{Z_0 - z_r}{Z_1 - z_r} $$
        where $z_r = z_c$ [S1]. The specific choice of starting point does not affect the final outcome [S1].

5.  **Hierarchical Sampling:**
    *   **Phase 1:** Sample along the original ray.
    *   **Phase 2:** Sample along the predicted refracted ray $r_r = o_r + t d_r$. Query density $\sigma_i$ (original) and $\sigma_{ir}$ (refracted) at all sampling points using only the NeRF Spatial MLP [S1].
    *   **Importance Sampling:** Apply importance sampling based on Phase 1 results to the original ray, while uniformly sampling the refracted ray. In subsequent stages, both rays undergo independent importance sampling [S1].

6.  **Density Decoding:** Decode the densities of both rays into the complete ray density $\sigma_{ic}$ using the Decoder MLP $\Theta_D$:
    $$ \sigma_{ic} = \Theta_D(\{\sigma_i, \sigma_{ir}\}) $$
    [S1].

7.  **Color Prediction:** Feed the combined density $\sigma_{ic}$ and direction $d$ into the Directional MLP. Input the direction last to better output view-dependent RGB color $c_c$ [S1].

8.  **Volume Rendering:** Compute the final pixel color $\hat{C}(r)$ via alpha compositing:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ and $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].

9.  **Loss Calculation and Optimization:**
    *   **Refraction Loss ($L_{ref}$):** Combines reversible and irreversibility terms to ensure optical path reversibility:
        $$ L_{ref} = \sum_i (1 - d_i \cdot \hat{d}_i) + \sum_i (1 - d_i \cdot \hat{d}_{r_i}) $$
        where $d_i$ is the ground truth direction (if available) or reference, and $\hat{d}_i, \hat{d}_{r_i}$ are predicted directions [S1].
    *   **Distillation Loss ($L_{dist}$):** Encourages the student network (without normal input) to match the teacher network (with normal input):
        $$ L_{dist} = \sum_i (1 - \hat{d}_{ri} \cdot \hat{d}_{rni}) $$
        where $\hat{d}_{ri}$ is the student output and $\hat{d}_{rni}$ is the teacher output [S1].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ L = \lambda_1 L_{dist} + \lambda_2 L_{ref} + L_{NeRF} $$
        where $L_{NeRF}$ is the baseline photometric loss [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$: Weighting coefficients for distillation and refraction losses. Set to 0.003 [S1].
*   $L$: Highest frequency in positional encoding [S1].
*   Batch size: 1024 rays [S1].
*   Iterations: 200k [S1].
*   Warm-up: First 5k iterations use only density query without decoder [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def positional_encoding(x, L):
    """
    Applies sinusoidal positional encoding.
    x: (..., 3) coordinates
    L: highest frequency
    Returns: (..., 2*L*3) encoded vector
    """
    # Implementation of gamma(x) = [sin(x), cos(x), ..., sin(2^(L-1)x), cos(2^(L-1)x)]
    # Note: Standard NeRF encoding usually applies to each dimension separately.
    # Simplified for clarity:
    scales = [2**i for i in range(L)]
    encoded = []
    for scale in scales:
        encoded.append(np.sin(x * scale))
        encoded.append(np.cos(x * scale))
    return np.concatenate(encoded, axis=-1)

def ray_to_4d(o, d, Z0=0, Z1=1):
    """
    Converts ray origin o and direction d to 4D light field (u, v, s, t).
    Intersects ray with planes Z=Z0 and Z=Z1.
    """
    # Ray: P(t) = o + t*d
    # Intersection with Z=Z0: o_z + t0*d_z = Z0 => t0 = (Z0 - o_z) / d_z
    # Intersection with Z=Z1: o_z + t1*d_z = Z1 => t1 = (Z1 - o_z) / d_z
    
    # Handle division by zero if d_z is 0 (parallel to planes)
    # Assuming general position for now.
    t0 = (Z0 - o[2]) / d[2]
    t1 = (Z1 - o[2]) / d[2]
    
    p0 = o + t0 * d
    p1 = o + t1 * d
    
    # (u, v) at Z0, (s, t) at Z1
    u, v = p0[0], p0[1]
    s, t = p1[0], p1[1]
    return np.array([u, v, s, t])

def _4d_to_direction(u, v, s, t, Z0=0, Z1=1):
    """
    Converts 4D light field back to Cartesian direction.
    """
    d_x = u - s
    d_y = v - t
    d_z = Z1 - Z0
    d = np.array([d_z, d_x, d_y])
    return d / np.linalg.norm(d)

def train_step(batch_rays, gt_images, nerf_spatial, nerf_directional, 
               refnet_student, refnet_teacher, decoder, optimizer, 
               L, lambda1, lambda2, iteration):
    """
    Single training step for RoseNeRF.
    """
    # 1. Positional Encoding
    # batch_rays contains origins and directions for 1024 rays
    # Sample points along rays
    t_vals = np.linspace(0, 1, 128) # Example sampling
    points = batch_rays.origins[:, None, :] + t_vals[None, :, None] * batch_rays.directions[:, None, :]
    points_enc = positional_encoding(points, L)
    
    # 2. Initial Normal Estimation (if in warm-up or for teacher)
    # In warm-up (first 5k iters), skip decoder and complex refraction logic
    if iteration < 5000:
        # Standard NeRF forward pass
        sigma = nerf_spatial(points_enc)
        # ... standard rendering ...
        loss = photometric_loss(rendered, gt_images)
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        return

    # 3. Refraction Prediction
    # Convert incident rays to 4D
    incident_4d = ray_to_4d(batch_rays.origins, batch_rays.directions)
    
    # Student RefNet: Input 4D, Output 4D refracted ray
    refracted_4d_student = refnet_student(incident_4d)
    
    # Teacher RefNet: Requires Normal N
    # Estimate N from spatial MLP gradients or previous step
    # N = normalize(grad(sigma)) # Simplified
    refracted_4d_teacher = refnet_teacher(np.concatenate([incident_4d, N], axis=-1))
    
    # Convert 4D to directions
    d_r_student = _4d_to_direction(*refracted_4d_student.T)
    d_r_teacher = _4d_to_direction(*refracted_4d_teacher.T)
    
    # 4. Sampling along Original and Refracted Rays
    # Sample along original ray
    points_orig = sample_along_ray(batch_rays.origins, batch_rays.directions)
    # Sample along refracted ray (origin at Z=zc)
    # Determine o_r based on intersection with Z=zc
    zc = batch_rays.origins[:, 2] # Camera Z
    # Calculate t_r such that (o_r + t_r * d_r)_z = zc
    # o_r_z = zc. So t_r starts from 0 relative to o_r?
    # Actually, o_r is the intersection point.
    # Points on refracted ray: P_r(t) = o_r + t * d_r
    points_refr = sample_along_ray(o_r, d_r_student)
    
    # Query densities
    points_orig_enc = positional_encoding(points_orig, L)
    points_refr_enc = positional_encoding(points_refr, L)
    
    sigma_orig = nerf_spatial(points_orig_enc)
    sigma_refr = nerf_spatial(points_refr_enc)
    
    # 5. Density Decoding
    # Decoder takes {sigma_orig, sigma_refr} -> sigma_combined
    sigma_combined = decoder(np.stack([sigma_orig, sigma_refr], axis=-1))
    
    # 6. Color Prediction
    # Feed sigma_combined and direction d (original view direction) to directional MLP
    # Input direction last
    color = nerf_directional(np.concatenate([sigma_combined, batch_rays.directions[:, None, :]], axis=-1))
    
    # 7. Rendering
    rendered = volume_rendering(sigma_combined, color, batch_rays.t_vals)
    
    # 8. Loss Calculation
    # Photometric Loss
    l_neurf = np.mean((rendered - gt_images) ** 2)
    
    # Refraction Loss
    # L_ref = sum(1 - d_i . d_hat_i) + sum(1 - d_i . d_hat_r_i)
    # Assuming ground truth directions d_i are available or approximated
    # For simplicity, using teacher output as reference for reversibility
    l_ref = np.mean(1 - np.sum(batch_rays.directions * d_r_student, axis=1)) + \
            np.mean(1 - np.sum(batch_rays.directions * d_r_teacher, axis=1))
            
    # Distillation Loss
    # L_dist = sum(1 - d_hat_r_i . d_hat_rn_i)
    l_dist = np.mean(1 - np.sum(d_r_student * d_r_teacher, axis=1))
    
    total_loss = lambda1 * l_dist + lambda2 * l_ref + l_neurf
    
    total_loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Floater Artifacts:** Without refraction modeling, NeRF suffers from semi-transparent floaters because sampling points are not aligned with the bent ray path [S1].
*   **Loss Importance:** Removing the refraction loss ($L_{ref}$) disrupts the fitting process because the absence of the correct refracted ray path leads to erroneous sampling points [S1].
*   **Decoder Necessity:** Substituting the specialized decoder with a simple computed average leads to a significant decrease in performance [S1].
*   **Parameterization:** Using the origin and direction of the ray for prediction instead of a 4D light field parameterization worsens accuracy, especially for LPIPS [S1].
*   **Normal Dependency:** While the teacher uses normals, the student does not. Predicting refracted rays without normals (relying solely on angular information) does not significantly degrade performance and may even enhance it in some scenes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method adds a student RefNet (2-layer MLP, 64 units) and a teacher RefNet (3-layer MLP, 128 units) to the base NeRF. The decoder is a 2-layer MLP (256 units). This adds minimal computational overhead compared to deformation field methods [S1].
*   **Performance:** RoseNeRF demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on the Eikonal Fields’ dataset and Shiny dataset without additional information [S1]. It achieves better results on CD and Lab scenes than Ref-NeRF [S1]. It surpasses NeX (which has 500 times more parameters) in detail and achieves competitive results in specular highlights [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for seamless integration into most NeRF-like frameworks [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Simplifies complex ray interactions to a single event; no need for IoR fields or masks; reduces degrees of freedom compared to point-by-point deformation; eliminates need for Collinearity Regularization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes a single refraction event, which may not model complex multi-refraction scenarios perfectly, though it ignores intermediate bending by assuming zero density for intermediate particles [S1].

## Background
RoseNeRF (Refracting Once is Enough for NeRF) was introduced in 2024 by Xiaoqian Liang et al. in the paper "Refracting Once is Enough: Neural Radiance Fields for Novel-View Synthesis of Real Refractive Objects" presented at ICMR ’24 [S1]. It addresses the challenge of applying NeRF to real-world scenes with refractive elements, where acquiring Index of Refraction (IoR) information is often impractical [S1]. The method is heavily inspired by Han et al. [20], who verified the feasibility of reconstructing transparent objects by immersing them in liquid, simplifying modeling by not requiring detailed simulations of light interactions [S1]. It is implemented on top of MS-NeRF [S1].
