# CLIPNeRF Training Configuration

Also called: Absolute Directional CLIP Loss, CLIPNeRF Hyperparameters, CLIPNeRF Implementation, Directional CLIP Loss, Relative Directional CLIP Loss, StyleGAN-NADA CLIP Loss

## Overview
CLIPNeRF training configuration defines the optimization procedure for text-guided stylization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) by minimizing the negative cosine similarity between rendered image embeddings and text prompt embeddings in the CLIP latent space [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF reconstruction by freezing density-related parameters and utilizing a directional loss that enforces semantic trajectory rather than pixel-wise photometric accuracy [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A pre-trained NeRF representation $F_\theta$, a source image $\mathbf{I}_{src}$ (optional, for relative loss), a target text prompt $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$, and a source text prompt $\mathbf{t}_{src}$ (optional).
*   **Outputs:** Updated NeRF parameters $\theta$ that produce stylized renderings consistent with $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **CLIP Model:** Uses the Vision Transformer variant ViT-B/32 [S1]. The image encoder $\mathcal{E}_i$ and text encoder $\mathcal{E}_t$ map inputs to normalized embedding vectors $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ [S2].
*   **NeRF Representation:** Standard MLP-based radiance field. Parameters are split into density-related sub-parameters $\theta_\sigma$ and appearance-related sub-parameters $\theta_c$ [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
Two variants of the directional CLIP loss are defined based on the embedding vectors $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I})$ and $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t})$ [S2]:

1.  **Absolute Directional CLIP Loss:** Measures cosine similarity between the rendered image $\mathbf{I}_{tgt}$ and the target text $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$ [S2]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{dir} = 1 - \langle \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{tgt}), \hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt}) \rangle $$
    This guides the rendering with a global direction independent of a reference starting point [S2].

2.  **Relative Directional CLIP Loss:** Transfers style from a source domain to a target domain using a pair of text prompts $(\mathbf{t}_{src}, \mathbf{t}_{tgt})$ [S2]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{dir} = 1 - \left\langle \frac{\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{src})}{\|\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{src})\|}, \frac{\hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{src})}{\|\hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{src})\|} \right\rangle $$
    This enforces the correct stylization trajectory while ignoring magnitude differences in the embedding space [S2].

**Optimization Steps:**
1.  **Freeze Density:** Freeze sub-parameters $\theta_\sigma$ related to density [S1].
2.  **Sample View:** Randomly sample a camera pose from the training dataset [S1].
3.  **Render Patch:** Render a $128 \times 128$ pixel patch $\mathbf{I}_{patch}$ from the sampled view [S1].
    *   **Ray Sampling:** To cover a wide receptive field, rays within the patch are sampled with a stride interval of 5, effectively covering a $640 \times 640$ region of the original image [S1].
    *   **Sampling Points:** Use 16 importance sampling points per ray for memory efficiency [S1].
    *   **Fine MLP:** The fine MLP uses a total of 80 samples (64 coarse + 16 fine) [S1].
4.  **Resize:** Apply bilinear interpolation to resize $\mathbf{I}_{patch}$ to the input resolution required by the CLIP encoder [S1].
5.  **Encode:** Compute embeddings $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_i = \mathcal{E}_i(\mathbf{I}_{patch})$ and $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_t = \mathcal{E}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt})$ [S1].
6.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{dir}$ using the absolute or relative formula [S2].
7.  **Update:** Minimize $\mathcal{L}_{dir}$ with respect to $\theta_c$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Iterations:** 500 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** $0.0001$ [S1].
*   **Patch Size:** $128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   **Ray Stride:** 5 [S1].
*   **Importance Samples:** 16 [S1].
*   **Fine Samples:** 80 total (64 coarse, 16 fine) [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Memory Constraints:** Naive differentiable rendering of high-resolution images is intractable due to memory limits, necessitating the patch-based approach [S1].
*   **Mode Collapse:** The absolute directional CLIP loss can cause mode collapse of the generator and reduce generation diversity [S2].
*   **Insufficient Magnitude:** Directional loss alone struggles to reach sufficient stylization magnitude, resulting in weak style transfer because it ignores the distance between embedded vectors [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Proportional to the number of iterations (500) and the cost of rendering patches and CLIP inference [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced by rendering small patches ($128 \times 128$) with limited sampling points (16) instead of full-resolution images [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables text-driven stylization of 3D scenes without requiring 3D ground truth labels [S2].
*   **Limitations:** May produce insufficient stylization magnitude or mode collapse if absolute loss is used without magnitude constraints [S2].

## Background
The method originates from CLIP-NeRF [Wang et al. 2021a], which extended the absolute directional CLIP loss from StyleCLIP [Patashnik et al. 2021] to generative NeRF editing [S2]. The relative directional loss was proposed in StyleGAN-NADA [Gal et al. 2021] to mitigate mode collapse and is adapted here for NeRF stylization [S2]. The specific training configuration (patch rendering, freezing density) is detailed in the implementation notes of [S1].
