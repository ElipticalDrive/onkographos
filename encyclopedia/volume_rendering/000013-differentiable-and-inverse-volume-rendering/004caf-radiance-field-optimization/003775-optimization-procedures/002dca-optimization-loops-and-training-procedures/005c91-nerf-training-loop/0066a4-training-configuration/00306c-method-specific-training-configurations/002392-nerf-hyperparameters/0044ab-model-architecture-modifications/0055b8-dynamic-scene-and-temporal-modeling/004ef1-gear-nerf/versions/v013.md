# Gear-NeRF

Also called: Gear-NeRF: Free-Viewpoint Rendering and Tracking with Motion-aware Spatio-Temporal Sampling

## Overview
Gear-NeRF is a method for dynamic novel view synthesis and free-viewpoint tracking that replaces the unified 4D radiance field representation with a stratified series of 4D feature volumes called "gears" [S1]. It leverages semantic embeddings from image segmentation models to assign each spatial region to a gear level based on its motion scale, allowing higher-resolution spatio-temporal sampling for rapidly moving objects while maintaining lower resolution for static or slow-moving regions [S1]. This approach improves rendering quality for dynamic scenes and enables object tracking as a by-product of the semantic representation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $W$ input videos $V = \{V_1, V_2, \dots, V_W\}$ of a dynamic scene with calibrated camera poses [S1].

**Outputs:** A 4D radiance field representation stratified into $N_{gear}$ gear levels, enabling the rendering of novel views at arbitrary times $t$ and viewpoints, and the tracking of semantic objects [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The representation consists of $N_{gear}$ separate 4D feature volumes, one for each gear level $G \in \{1, \dots, N_{gear}\}$ [S1].
Each gear volume is defined by planar factorized embeddings. For a specific gear $G$, the feature vector $\mathbf{f}^G(\mathbf{x}, t)$ at space-time coordinate $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is computed using three terms [S1]:
1.  Spatial planes $h_1(\mathbf{x}, y)$, $h_2(\mathbf{x}, z)$, $h_3(\mathbf{y}, z)$ shared across all gears.
2.  Gear-specific spatio-temporal planes $k^G_1(z, t)$, $k^G_2(y, t)$, $k^G_3(x, t)$, each in $M$-dimensional space.
3.  Linear transforms $B_1, B_2, B_3$ mapping the element-wise products to feature vectors [S1].

The gear level $g(\mathbf{x}, t)$ for any coordinate is also computed from a planar-factorized 4D feature volume using similar spatial planes $h'_j$ and gear-specific temporal planes $k'_j$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Semantic Embedding Optimization:** Optimize a 4D semantic embedding based on powerful image segmentation models (specifically SAM) to profile the scene into semantic regions [S1].
2.  **Gear Determination:** Assign a gear level $G$ to each spatio-temporal coordinate $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ based on the local motion scale. Regions with larger motion scales are assigned higher gear levels [S1].
3.  **Feature Vector Computation:** For a given coordinate $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and assigned gear $G$, compute the feature vector $\mathbf{f}^G(\mathbf{x}, t)$ using the formula below [S1].
4.  **Radiance Field Query:** Pass the feature vector $\mathbf{f}^G(\mathbf{x}, t)$ through a tiny MLP to predict volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ given viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].
5.  **Spatio-Temporal Sampling:** Perform sampling along the ray. Higher gear levels (assigned to high-motion regions) use higher-resolution spatio-temporal sampling to capture rapid changes [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Compute the final color $C(\mathbf{r}, t)$ using the standard volume rendering integral [S1].

**Formula:**
The feature vector for gear $G$ at coordinate $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is calculated as [S1]:
$$
\mathbf{f}^G(\mathbf{x}, t) = B_1 (h_1(x, y) \bowtie k^G_1(z, t)) + B_2 (h_2(x, z) \bowtie k^G_2(y, t)) + B_3 (h_3(y, z) \bowtie k^G_3(x, t))
$$
Where:
*   $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ is the 3D spatial coordinate.
*   $t$ is the time coordinate.
*   $h_j(\cdot, \cdot)$ are functions evaluated by bilinear interpolation on regularly spaced 2D feature grids embedding coordinate tuples to features.
*   $k^G_j(\cdot, \cdot)$ are gear-specific functions evaluated by bilinear interpolation on regularly spaced 2D feature grids.
*   $\bowtie$ denotes element-wise product.
*   $B_j$ denotes a linear transform mapping products to feature vectors of dimension $M$.
*   The vector-valued functions $h_j$ and linear transforms $B_j$ are shared by all gears.

**Parameters:**
*   $N_{gear}$: The total number of gear levels used to stratify the scene representation based on motion scales [S1].
*   $M$: The dimensionality of the feature vectors in the spatio-temporal embeddings [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Motion Scale Ambiguity:** The method relies on a "gear determination scheme" to map motion scales to gear levels. The exact thresholding or continuous mapping function for this scheme is not detailed in the provided sources [S1].
*   **Semantic Dependency:** The initial stratification depends on the quality of the 4D semantic embedding derived from segmentation models like SAM. Errors in semantic segmentation may lead to incorrect gear assignments [S1].

def gear_nerf_render(ray_origin, ray_direction, time_t, scene_params):
    """
    Renders a single ray for Gear-NeRF.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: 3D vector, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: 3D vector, direction of the ray.
        time_t: float, time coordinate.
        scene_params: Object containing N_gear, feature grids, MLP, etc.
        
    Returns:
        color: 3D vector, rendered color.
        depth: float, accumulated depth.
    """
    # Sample points along the ray
    # Note: Sampling density depends on the gear level of the region
    # Higher gear levels (high motion) require denser sampling
    num_samples = get_sampling_density(ray_origin, ray_direction, time_t, scene_params)
    sample_points, sample_alphas = sample_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, num_samples, scene_params)
    
    accumulated_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    accumulated_depth = 0.0
    accumulated_weight = 0.0
    transmittance = 1.0
    
    for i in range(num_samples):
        x, y, z = sample_points[i]
        
        # Determine gear level for this point
        gear_level = get_gear_level(x, y, z, time_t, scene_params)
        
        # Compute feature vector for this gear
        f_g = compute_feature_vector(x, y, z, time_t, gear_level, scene_params)
        
        # Query MLP for density and color
        sigma, color = query_mlp(f_g, ray_direction, scene_params.mlp)
        
        # Volume rendering step
        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * scene_params.delta_t)
        weight = transmittance * alpha
        accumulated_color += weight * color
        accumulated_depth += weight * (sample_points[i].z) # Or depth along ray
        accumulated_weight += weight
        transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha)
        
        if transmittance < 1e-5:
            break
            
    return accumulated_color, accumulated_depth

def compute_feature_vector(x, y, z, t, gear, params):
    """
    Computes f^G(x, t) as per Equation 4.
    """
    # Shared spatial planes
    h1_xy = interpolate_2d_grid(params.h1, x, y)
    h2_xz = interpolate_2d_grid(params.h2, x, z)
    h3_yz = interpolate_2d_grid(params.h3, y, z)
    
    # Gear-specific spatio-temporal planes
    k1_zt = interpolate_2d_grid(params.k[gear][1], z, t)
    k2_yt = interpolate_2d_grid(params.k[gear][2], y, t)
    k3_xt = interpolate_2d_grid(params.k[gear][3], x, t)
    
    # Element-wise products
    prod1 = h1_xy * k1_zt
    prod2 = h2_xz * k2_yt
    prod3 = h3_yz * k3_xt
    
    # Linear transforms
    f_g = params.B1 @ prod1 + params.B2 @ prod2 + params.B3 @ prod3
    
    return f_g

def get_gear_level(x, y, z, t, params):

## Subtopics

### Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling
The parent framework that establishes the canonical space and deformation field or time-conditioned radiance field. Gear-NeRF builds on this by stratifying the representation based on motion [15546-dynamic-scene-and-temporal-modeling.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Not given in the sources. However, the method involves querying multiple gear-specific feature grids and a semantic embedding optimization, which may increase training time compared to single-volume methods [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources. The method stores $N_{gear}$ separate feature volumes, which may consume more memory than a single unified volume, but allows for lower resolution in static regions [S1].
*   **Measured Performance**: The method achieves state-of-the-art rendering and tracking performance on multiple challenging datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths**:
    *   Improved dynamic novel view synthesis through motion-aware spatio-temporal sampling [S1].
    *   Enables free-viewpoint tracking of objects of interest given user prompts [S1].
    *   Efficient resource allocation by assigning higher resolution to high-motion regions [S1].
*   **Limitations**:
    *   Relies on external semantic segmentation models (SAM) for initial profiling [S1].
    *   Requires careful tuning of the gear determination scheme [S1].
*   **When to use**: When high-quality dynamic novel view synthesis is required for scenes with varying motion scales, and when free-viewpoint tracking is desired [S1].
*   **When not**: When computational resources are extremely limited and the scene is mostly static, as the overhead of multiple gear volumes may not be justified [S1].

## Background
Gear-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Gear-NeRF: Free-Viewpoint Rendering and Tracking with Motion-aware Spatio-Temporal Sampling" [S1]. The method addresses the challenge of representing dynamic scenes efficiently by stratifying the 4D radiance field into motion-based "gears" [S1]. It leverages semantic information from image segmentation models to guide this stratification [S1]. The project page is available at the URL provided in the sources [S1].
