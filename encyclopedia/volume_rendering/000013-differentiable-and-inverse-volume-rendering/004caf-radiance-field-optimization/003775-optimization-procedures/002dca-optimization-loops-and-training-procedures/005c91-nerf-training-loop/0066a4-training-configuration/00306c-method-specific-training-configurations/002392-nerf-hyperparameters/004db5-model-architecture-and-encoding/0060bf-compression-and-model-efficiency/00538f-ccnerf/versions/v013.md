# CCNeRF

Also called: Compressible-Composable NeRF, Rank-residual Decomposition NeRF

## Overview
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field entirely via tensor rank decomposition, allowing arbitrary composition of objects and dynamic level-of-detail (LOD) control via rank truncation without retraining [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 2D images with known camera poses $(o, d)$; target rank $R$ for compression; ratio of vector- to matrix-based components.
*   **Output:** A compressed radiance field representation consisting of rank components and a weight matrix, capable of rendering images via volume rendering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The model represents a 3D scene as a 4D feature volume described by rank components and a weight matrix [S1]. The structure consists of:
1.  **Rank Weights Matrix ($W$):** A matrix storing weights for different feature channels [S1].
2.  **Rank Components:** A set of decomposed components, which are either:
    *   **Vector-based (CP):** Corresponding to CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) decomposition [S1].
    *   **Matrix-based (Triple Plane):** A less compact variant using factor matrices [S1].
    The ratio of vector- to matrix-based components is controllable to trade off between model size and performance [S1].

**Querying a 3D Coordinate:**
To query a 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$:
1.  Project the coordinate to the decomposed vectors or matrices [S1].
2.  Perform weighted interpolation using the stored rank weights [S1].

**Volume Rendering:**
The rendered color $\hat{C}(r)$ for a ray $r$ is computed using the standard volume rendering equation [S1]:
$$
\hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i
$$
where:
*   $T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_i)$ is the transmittance.
*   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity.
*   $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between samples.
*   $\sigma_i$ is the density and $c_i$ is the color at sample $i$ along ray $r$ from origin $o$ with direction $d$ [S1].

**Composition:**
Different models are composed by concatenating their rank components along the rank dimension [S1]. A transformation matrix is recorded for each object to control its position and orientation in the scene [S1].

**Compression (Rank Truncation):**
Model size is dynamically adjusted by rank truncation [S1]. An empirical sort-and-truncate strategy is used to achieve near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank, allowing trade-off between model size and rendering quality without retraining [S1]. Lower ranks preserve primary information due to rank-residual learning [S1].

**Tucker Decomposition:**
The method utilizes Tucker decomposition for 3D tensors $T \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}$ [S1]:
$$
T_{i,j,k} = \sum_{p=1}^P \sum_{q=1}^Q \sum_{r=1}^R S_{p,q,r} U^x_{i,p} U^y_{j,q} U^z_{k,r}
$$
where $S$ is the core tensor and $U^x, U^y, U^z$ are factor matrices [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Standard truncated Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD) does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression:** Achieves near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank without extra optimization [S1].
*   **Composition:** Supports arbitrary transformation and composition of different models without retraining or compatibility constraints [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Unlike TensoRF, which uses MLPs that prevent compression/composition, CCNeRF models the field *only* with tensor rank decomposition to preserve low-rank approximation properties [S1].
*   **Performance:** Comparable rendering quality to state-of-the-art methods while enabling extra capability of compression and composition [S1].

## Background
CCNeRF was introduced by Jiaxiang Tang, Xiaokang Chen, Jingbo Wang, and Gang Zeng from Peking University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong [S1]. The paper "Compressible-Composable NeRF via Rank-residual Decomposition" was published on arXiv in May 2022 (v2 in October 2022) [S1]. The method addresses the difficulty of manipulating implicit NeRF representations, which are often restricted by shared renderer networks or suffer from large model sizes [S1]. It is motivated by the low-rank approximation property of the SVD algorithm [S1].
