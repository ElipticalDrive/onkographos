# Model Architecture and Encoding


## Overview
Model Architecture and Encoding defines the neural network structures and input feature representations used to map 3D spatial coordinates and viewing directions to radiance field properties (density, color, semantics). It solves the problem of how to efficiently and accurately represent complex 3D scenes within a differentiable rendering pipeline. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md) or [Regularization Techniques](11314-regularization-techniques.md) is its focus on the structural definition of the radiance field itself—whether it uses MLPs, hash grids, tensor decompositions, or hybrid meshes—rather than the optimization dynamics or loss functions applied to it.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of all radiance field architectures is the query function $f: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the 3D position, $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the viewing direction, $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ is the volume density, and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the RGB color. This function is typically composed of two stages:
1.  **Feature Encoding:** A mechanism to convert the continuous input coordinates into a discrete or latent feature representation. Common encodings include sinusoidal positional encoding [Parent], hash-based multi-resolution grids [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6][S7, unofficial], or tensor decompositions [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6][S7, unofficial].
2.  **MLP/Network Decoding:** A neural network (usually a Multi-Layer Perceptron) that takes the encoded features and the viewing direction to predict density and color.

The rendering process integrates these predictions along rays using volume rendering equations:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity, $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between sample points [Parent, S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7].

## Subtopics

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md)
This configuration defines the training protocol for a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) model that incorporates depth priors to improve reconstruction quality, specifically addressing the ambiguity of scale and structure in sparse-view or noisy-pose scenarios. It builds on the standard NeRF architecture [Parent](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by modifying the optimization loop to include a depth consistency loss. What sets this apart from standard NeRF training is the explicit weighting of depth supervision and the specific regularization (dropout) applied to the positional encoding layers to prevent overfitting when depth constraints are active [11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md].

### [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md)
CLIPNeRF training configuration defines the optimization procedure for text-guided stylization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) by minimizing the negative cosine similarity between rendered images and text prompts in the CLIP embedding space. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF reconstruction by freezing density-related parameters and utilizing directional losses (absolute or relative) to enforce semantic style trajectories rather than photometric fidelity.

### [Binary Radiance Fields](13622-binary-radiance-fields.md)
Binary Radiance Fields (BiRF) is a storage-efficient radiance field representation that constrains feature grid parameters to binary values (+1 or -1) to achieve compact storage. Unlike post-processing compression methods such as PlenOctrees or Re:NeRF, which require additional optimization steps and are bounded by pre-trained model performance, BiRF performs binarization during training without post-optimization. It utilizes a 2D-3D hybrid multi-resolution architecture to maintain reconstruction quality while significantly reducing storage overhead compared to real-valued floating-point grids.

### [Mip-NeRF 360 MLP Architecture](14098-mip-nerf-360-mlp-architecture.md)
The Mip-NeRF 360 MLP architecture is a specific configuration of the radiance field neural network that replaces standard positional encoding with **Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE)** to analytically integrate the radiance field over cone-shaped volumes, thereby reducing aliasing artifacts in novel view synthesis. It distinguishes itself from the standard NeRF architecture [Parent](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by treating input coordinates as Gaussian distributions (defined by mean and covariance) rather than single points, allowing the MLP to approximate the average color and density over the viewing cone.

### SUDS
SUDS is a 4D radiance field representation designed for large-scale dynamic urban scenes that factorizes the scene into three separate hash table data structures to efficiently encode static, dynamic, and far-field radiance fields [S1]. It enables free-viewpoint rendering, semantic decomposition, and 3D scene flow estimation at arbitrary poses and time steps using multiple video inputs without requiring supervised 3D annotations like bounding boxes or segmentation masks [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF architectures that use a single MLP, SUDS employs a multi-resolution hash table encoding with a three-branch decomposition to handle scale and dynamism simultaneously [S1].

### CCNeRF
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition of the scene without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field *only* with tensor rank decomposition, preserving low-rank approximation properties to allow dynamic model size adjustment via rank truncation and arbitrary composition via rank concatenation [S1].

### [Rip-NeRF](15532-rip-nerf.md)
Rip-NeRF is a hybrid radiance field method that renders high-fidelity, anti-aliased images by combining cone casting with an anisotropic 3D Gaussian characterization. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Zip-NeRF by using Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids to enable efficient anisotropic area-sampling, avoiding the discrete point-based sampling of continuous signals and the inefficiency of multi-sampling mechanisms.

### [UE4-NeRF](15539-ue4-nerf.md)
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline. It addresses the computational limitations of traditional NeRFs and existing real-time variants (like Mobile-NeRF and Mega-NeRF) by partitioning large environments into sub-NeRFs, representing them via optimized textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and leveraging multi-resolution hash encoding for rapid training. Unlike its siblings which often rely on grid structures or suffer from slow convergence on tilted surfaces, UE4-NeRF supports various scene types (including mountainous terrain) and achieves real-time performance at high resolutions by deploying the neural network as an HLSL fragment shader within the engine.

### [Generative Occupancy Fields (GOF)](15773-generative-occupancy-fields-gof.md)
Generative Occupancy Fields (GOF) is a generative model for 3D-aware image synthesis that combines radiance fields and occupancy representations to learn compact object surfaces without impeding training convergence. It solves the problem of diffused surfaces in standard generative radiance fields (like pi-GAN) and the sparse gradient convergence issues in direct occupancy networks by transitioning from cumulative volume rendering to surface-point rendering. Unlike its siblings, GOF predicts alpha values (interpreted as occupancy) instead of volume densities, enabling surface-aware image synthesis via root-finding.

### [Panoptic Radiance Field](15849-panoptic-radiance-field.md)
Panoptic Lifting learns a panoptic 3D volumetric representation from posed RGB images and machine-generated 2D panoptic segmentation masks, lifting noisy 2D labels into a consistent 3D representation of color, density, semantics, and instances. It differs from sibling methods like [Panoptic NeRF](11916-panoptic-nerf.md) and [DM-NeRF](11916-panoptic-nerf.md) by requiring only machine-generated 2D labels rather than manual ground truth or coarse meshes, using linear assignment to resolve instance inconsistencies across views.

#### Triplane NeRF with Diffusion Prior
- **Definition:** The framework uses a triplane NeRF representation where scene codes are optimized jointly with a diffusion prior to handle sparse-view data and ensure end-to-end coherence [S1].
- **Parameters:** Prediction Format uses v-parameterization $\hat{v}_\phi(x(t), t)$ from [43], such that $\hat{x} = \alpha(t)x(t) - \sigma(t)\hat{v}$. Weighting Function uses SNR-based weighting $w(t) = (\alpha(t)/\sigma(t))^2\omega$ from Eq. (5), instead of LSGM mechanisms [S1].
- **Layout:** Scene code $\xi_i$ has shape $\mathbb{R}^{3 \times 6 \times 128 \times 128}$, representing a triplane NeRF representation. Denoising Network Input/Output involves noisy and denoised triplane features, with channels of all three planes stacked together. Denoising Network Architecture is a U-Net with 122M parameters [S1].

#### EG3D Triplane Representation
- **Steps:** Render feature image $I_f \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 128 \times 128}$ and raw RGB image $I_{128} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$ by accumulating points along rays and performing volume rendering. Extract dense depth map $I_D \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 128}$ from the volume rendering. Feed $I_f$ and $I_{128}$ into a 2D super-resolution network to yield final output $I_{512} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 512 \times 512}$ [S2].
- **Formula:** $\text{SuperRes}(I_f, I_{128}) = I_{512}$ [S2].
- **Parameters:** Image resolution is 512x512 for input images and final output [S2].
- **Layout:** Triplane representation $T$ is a tensor in $\mathbb{R}^{256 \times 256 \times 96}$, corresponding to 3 axis-aligned planes (xy, xz, yz), each with 32 channels and resolution 256x256 [S2].
- **Number:** Rendering speed is 42 fps on RTX 3090 [S2].

#### NeRF-W Hyperparameter Configuration
- **Parameters:** Base MLP: 8 layers of 512 hidden units. Static and Transient/Uncertainty MLPs: 4 layers of 128 hidden units each. Positional encoding frequencies: 15 for position, 4 for viewing direction. Sampling points: 512 per ray per model (coarse and fine) during training (1024 total), doubled to 2048 during evaluation. Appearance embedding dimension $n(a) = 48$. Transient object embedding dimension $n(\tau) = 16$. L1 regularizer multiplier $\lambda_u = 0.01$. Minimum importance threshold $\beta_{min} = 0.03$. Optimizer: Adam, batch size 2048, initial learning rate 0.001, decaying ten-fold every 150,000 iterations, over 300,000 iterations total [S3].
- **Number:** Hyperparameters were optimized for the Brandenburg Gate scene of the Phototourism dataset and reused for remaining scenes [S3].

#### Explicit Mesh Reconstruction from Neural Fields
- **Definition:** An algorithm to extract an explicit 3D mesh representation from the implicit neural radiance field learned by CodeNeRF, allowing for 3D reconstruction from a single input image [S4].
- **Steps:** Feed the center coordinates of a $256^3$ voxel grid to the CodeNeRF MLP to obtain volume density values $\sigma$. Apply the Marching Cubes algorithm to the volume density values $\sigma$ to find mesh vertices. Cast rays along the vertex normals of the generated mesh to associate colors to the vertices [S4].

#### Efficient EndoNeRF scene representation
- **Definition:** A voxel-grids-based scene representation that replaces coordinate-based MLPs with explicit density and feature volume grids to accelerate optimization, combined with a canonical field and time-dependent deformation field for dynamic surgical scenes [S5].
- **Steps:** The dynamic scene at time $t$ is modeled by warping a canonical radiance field (represented by $\mathbf{V}_\sigma$ and $\mathbf{V}_a$) with a time-dependent deformation field. The deformation field is modeled using decomposed 4D motion fields and a 3-layer MLP that maps a spatial-temporal coordinate to a deformation offset [S5].

#### NeRF-Liif
- **Definition:** A baseline method that super-resolves LR images using a pretrained LIIF (Local Implicit Image Function) model, which requires LR-HR pairs for training and introduces external data priors [S6].
- **Parameters:** Training data requirement: LR-HR pairs [S6].
- **Pitfalls:** On synthetic Blender dataset, LIIF may produce false predictions due to domain mismatch with its external training set. On real-world LLFF dataset, LIIF tends to be oversmooth and may predict false color or geometry [S6].
- **Numbers:** Blender ×2 (100x100): PSNR 27.07, SSIM 0.919, LPIPS 0.067. Blender ×4 (100x100): PSNR 25.36, SSIM 0.885, LPIPS 0.125. Blender ×2 (200x200): PSNR 28.81, SSIM 0.934, LPIPS 0.058. Blender ×4 (200x200): PSNR 27.34, SSIM 0.912, LPIPS 0.096. LLFF ×2: PSNR 26.81, SSIM 0.823, LPIPS 0.145. LLFF ×4: PSNR 24.76, SSIM 0.723, LPIPS 0.292 [S6].

#### UniSurf
- **Definition:** A method unifying volume rendering (radiance fields) and surface rendering (implicit surfaces) to achieve high-quality geometry and sharp textures without mask supervision [S7, unofficial].
- **Steps:** Uses an occupancy field where the occupancy at the $i^{th}$ sample corresponds to an indicator of surface presence. Uses volume rendering in the early stage for optimization without mask supervision. Uses surface rendering in the later stage to extract level-set surfaces [S7, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Unlike pure surface rendering which requires mask supervision and has blurry texture mapping, this method provides high-quality novel views and sharp textures/colors without mask supervision [S7, unofficial].

## Background
The foundational NeRF architecture [Parent] introduced the use of fully connected MLPs to map 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color, using sinusoidal positional encoding for inputs. Subsequent developments focused on efficiency (Instant-NGP hash encoding [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6][S7, unofficial]), generative capabilities (Diffusion NeRF [S1], EG3D [S2]), dynamic scenes (SUDS [S1]), and integration with real-time engines (UE4-NeRF [S1]). The field has evolved from pure MLP-based representations to hybrid approaches involving grids, tensors, and explicit meshes to balance quality, speed, and storage.
