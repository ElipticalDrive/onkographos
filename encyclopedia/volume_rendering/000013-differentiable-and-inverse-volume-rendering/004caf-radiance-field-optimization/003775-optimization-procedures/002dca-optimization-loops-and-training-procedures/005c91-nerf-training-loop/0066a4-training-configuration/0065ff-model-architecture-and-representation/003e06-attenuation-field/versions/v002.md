# Attenuation Field

Also called: Beer-Lambert NeRF, X-ray NeRF

## Overview
The Attenuation Field method maps 3D coordinates $\mathbf{X}$ to attenuation coefficients $\mu$ rather than color values, modeling X-ray beam decay through diverse structures to a flat-panel detector [S1]. It solves transmission-based imaging reconstruction by adhering to the Beer–Lambert Law, differing from natural-image NeRFs that model emitted radiance [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{X}$, initial beam intensity $I_0$, and sampling step size $\delta$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Attenuated intensity $I$ at the detector plane [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Neural Network:** A coordinate-based network (e.g., MLP) that takes $\mathbf{X}$ as input and outputs a scalar attenuation coefficient $\mu$ [S1].
*   **Sampling:** Uniform sampling is used along the ray path [S1]. Stratified or importance sampling is explicitly avoided because, in medical volume reconstruction, every region contributes equally, and uniform distribution ensures no regions are overlooked or over-represented [S1].

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation follows the Beer–Lambert Law [S1]:
$$I = I_0 e^{-\sum_i \mu_i \delta_i}$$
Where:
*   $I$: Resultant attenuated intensity at the detector [S1].
*   $I_0$: Initial intensity of the X-ray source [S1].
*   $\mu_i$: Attenuation coefficient at the $i$-th sample point [S1].
*   $\delta_i$: Sampling step size (distance between samples) [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** Cast a ray from the X-ray source through the volume to the flat-panel detector [S1].
2.  **Uniform Sampling:** Sample points $\mathbf{X}_i$ uniformly along the ray path within the volume bounds [S1].
3.  **Query Attenuation:** Pass each $\mathbf{X}_i$ through the neural network to obtain the local attenuation coefficient $\mu_i$ [S1].
4.  **Integration:** Compute the total optical depth $\tau = \sum_i \mu_i \delta_i$ [S1].
5.  **Attenuation:** Calculate the final intensity $I = I_0 e^{-\tau}$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def render_attenuation_field(ray_origin, ray_direction, network, I0, num_samples, step_size):
    # 1. Ray Casting & 2. Uniform Sampling
    points = ray_origin + ray_direction * (step_size * torch.arange(num_samples))
    # 3. Query Attenuation
    mu_values = network(points)  # Returns scalar attenuation coefficient μ for each point
    # 4. Integration
    optical_depth = torch.sum(mu_values * step_size)
    # 5. Attenuation
    intensity = I0 * torch.exp(-optical_depth)
    return intensity
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Strategy:** Uniform sampling is the standard configuration for this method [S1].
*   **Initial Intensity ($I_0$):** Treated as a known constant or calibrated parameter of the X-ray source [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Negative Attenuation:** Attenuation coefficients must be non-negative to maintain physical interpretation [S1]. If the network outputs negative values, truncation or non-negative activation functions are required (see *Trainable Flat Field*) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Empty Space:** Rays passing through air (empty space) should ideally not contribute to attenuation [S2, unofficial]. Standard networks may predict false density in empty regions if background attenuation is not decoupled [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Trainable Flat Field
A technique to decouple the attenuation path into material and air components using a trainable parameter for background attenuation, preventing false density in empty space [S2, unofficial]. It addresses the issue where rays passing through empty space (air) get incorrectly attenuated, leading to inaccurate density fields [S2, unofficial].

**Definition:**
The method introduces a trainable scalar parameter $f_f$ representing the flat field (background) attenuation [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  Define a trainable parameter $f_f$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  Derive the air attenuation coefficient $\mu_A$ using truncation to ensure non-negativity: $\mu_A = \max(0, f_f)$ [S2, unofficial].
3.  Assume the path through air is approximately constant for all rays [S2, unofficial].
4.  Compute intensity by separating the integral into material ($M$) and air ($A$) components [S2, unofficial]:
$$I = I_0 \exp \left( - \int_M \mu(s) ds - \int_A \mu(s) ds \right) = I_0 \exp(-\mu_A) \exp \left( - \int_M \mu(s) ds \right)$$

**When to choose:**
Use this variant when training on data with significant empty space or when a "white background" assumption (no air attenuation) leads to inaccurate density representations [S2, unofficial]. It allows the network to learn the true material density by decoupling it from the background air attenuation [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The Attenuation Field formulation was introduced to adapt Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for high-fidelity volume reconstruction in medical images, specifically X-ray modalities [S1]. It maps 3D coordinates to attenuation coefficients $\mu$ rather than color values, based on the Beer–Lambert Law [S1]. The approach recognizes that X-ray sources emit cone-shaped beams that undergo attenuation as they pass through diverse structures before being received by a flat-panel detector [S1]. The use of uniform sampling was specifically justified for medical volumes where every region equally contributes to reconstruction, unlike natural images where surface regions are prioritized [S1].
