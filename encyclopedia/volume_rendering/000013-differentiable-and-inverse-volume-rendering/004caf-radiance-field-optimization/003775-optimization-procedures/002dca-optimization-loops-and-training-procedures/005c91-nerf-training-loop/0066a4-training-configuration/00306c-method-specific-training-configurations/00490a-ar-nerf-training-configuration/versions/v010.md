# AR-NeRF Training Configuration

Also called: AR-NeRF Implementation Details, AR-NeRF architecture and training configuration

## Overview
AR-NeRF is a method for unsupervised learning of depth and defocus effects from natural images using aperture rendering neural radiance fields [S1]. It extends the generative NeRF framework (pi-GAN) by introducing a differentiable aperture rendering mechanism that simulates camera optics, allowing the model to learn scene geometry and optical properties simultaneously [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF variants that optimize for photometric consistency alone, AR-NeRF explicitly models the interaction between light rays and camera apertures, enabling continuous control over defocus strength and focus distance [S1]. It is distinguished from its sibling [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) by its specific use of FiLM-SIREN networks for radiance field synthesis and its integration of GAN-based training objectives with identity regularization [S1].

## How it works
AR-NeRF builds on the pi-GAN architecture [S1]. The method involves training a generative radiance field that maps latent codes to images, while simultaneously optimizing for geometric consistency across viewpoints and photometric consistency with input images [S1]. The core innovation is the aperture rendering module, which modifies the standard volume rendering integral to account for the finite size of the camera aperture [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A dataset of unposed natural images (e.g., flowers, birds, faces); a random latent code $\mathbf{z}$ sampled from a standard normal distribution; camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) for training images.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered images at various viewpoints; depth maps; defocused images (via the AR-NeRF-R variant).

### Data Structures and Network Layouts
The network consists of three main components: a Mapping Network, a Synthesis Network (Foreground and Background), and a Discriminator [S1].

1.  **Mapping Network:**
    *   Architecture: MLP with 3 hidden layers.
    *   Units per layer: 256.
    *   Activation: Leaky ReLU (LReLU) with negative slope 0.2.
    *   Latent code dimension: 256.
    *   Function: Maps the input latent code $\mathbf{z}$ to an intermediate latent space $\mathbf{w}$, propagating information to each layer of the synthesis network [S1].

2.  **Synthesis Network:**
    *   **Foreground Synthesis Network:**
        *   Architecture: MLP with 8 FiLM-SIREN hidden layers.
        *   Units per layer: 128.
        *   Activation: SIREN (Sinusoidal Representation Networks) with FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) conditioning [S1].
        *   Purpose: Represents the complex foreground geometry and appearance. The unit count was reduced from 256 (original pi-GAN) to 128 to allow more points per ray, improving performance without significantly affecting output quality [S1].
    *   **Background Synthesis Network:**
        *   Architecture: MLP with 8 FiLM-SIREN hidden layers.
        *   Units per layer: 64.
        *   Purpose: Represents the simpler background. Fewer parameters are used under the assumption that the background is less complex than the foreground [S1].

3.  **Discriminator:**
    *   **For FFHQ (Faces):** Uses CoordConv layers followed by residual blocks [S1]. CoordConv is used because faces are aligned based on facial landmarks [S1].
    *   **For Oxford Flowers and CUB-200-2011:** Same as FFHQ but **without** CoordConv layers. CoordConv was found to have negative effects on these datasets because flowers and birds are not strictly aligned [S1].

### Training Procedure
The training minimizes a combined loss function involving GAN loss, identity regularization, and R1 regularization [S1].

1.  **Dataset Preparation:**
    *   Oxford Flowers: 8,189 images, 102 categories [S1].
    *   CUB-200-2011: 11,788 images, 200 bird species [S1].
    *   FFHQ: 70,000 face images [S1].

2.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
    *   **Learning Rate:** 0.00006 [S1].
    *   **Iterations:** 200,000 [S1].
    *   **Loss Components:**
        *   **Non-saturating GAN loss:** Standard GAN objective [S1].
        *   **R1 Regularization:** Weight $\lambda_{R1} = 0.2$ [S1].
        *   **Identity Regularization:** Weight $\lambda_{id} = 15$ [S1]. This term ensures that the generated image maintains the same identity (semantic content) across different viewpoints, preventing the model from generating different objects for different views [S1].

3.  **Ray Sampling and Rendering:**
    *   Rays are sampled from the camera frustum.
    *   Points are sampled along the rays. The number of points is determined by the network capacity and memory constraints. The reduction in synthesis network units (128 vs 256) allows for a larger number of points per ray, which is critical for performance [S1].
    *   The aperture rendering mechanism simulates the effect of the camera aperture by integrating light over a range of angles, controlled by the aperture size parameter [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
# AR-NeRF Training Loop
# Based on [S1]

def train_arnrf(dataset, num_iterations=200000, lr=0.00006):
    # Initialize networks
    mapping_net = MappingNetwork(layers=3, units=256, activation='LReLU', slope=0.2, latent_dim=256)
    foreground_net = SynthesisNetworkFiLM_SIREN(layers=8, units=128)
    background_net = SynthesisNetworkFiLM_SIREN(layers=8, units=64)
    
    # Discriminator depends on dataset
    if dataset == 'FFHQ':
        discriminator = DiscriminatorCoordConvResidual()
    else: # Oxford Flowers, CUB-200-2011
        discriminator = DiscriminatorResidual() # No CoordConv
        
    optimizer = Adam([mapping_net.parameters(), foreground_net.parameters(), 
                      background_net.parameters(), discriminator.parameters()], lr=lr)
    
    lambda_r1 = 0.2
    lambda_id = 15
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # Sample batch of images
        real_images = dataset.next_batch()
        
        # Sample latent codes
        z = sample_latent_code(batch_size)
        
        # Generate images
        w = mapping_net(z)
        generated_images = synthesis_network(w, foreground_net, background_net)
        
        # Compute GAN loss
        d_real = discriminator(real_images)
        d_fake = discriminator(generated_images.detach())
        gan_loss = non_saturating_gan_loss(d_real, d_fake)
        
        # Compute R1 regularization
        r1_grad = compute_gradient_penalty(discriminator, real_images)
        r1_loss = lambda_r1 * r1_grad
        
        # Compute Identity regularization
        # Render same scene from different viewpoints
        viewpoint_1 = get_viewpoint_1()
        viewpoint_2 = get_viewpoint_2()
        img_1 = render_with_viewpoint(w, viewpoint_1, foreground_net, background_net)
        img_2 = render_with_viewpoint(w, viewpoint_2, foreground_net, background_net)
        id_loss = lambda_id * identity_loss(img_1, img_2)
        
        # Total loss
        loss = gan_loss + r1_loss + id_loss
        
        # Update parameters
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **CoordConv Misuse:** Using CoordConv layers in the discriminator for non-aligned objects (flowers, birds) leads to negative effects. It should be removed for such datasets [S1].
*   **Ray Density:** The number of rays and points per ray must be tuned. Using too few rays with stratified sampling can lead to worse performance than unstratified sampling with more rays [S1]. Processing time and memory increase with the number of rays [S1].
*   **Resolution Scaling:** For higher-resolution images, more rays and points along the ray may be needed [S1].

## Subtopics

### AR-NeRF-R
A defocus renderer trained on AR-NeRF-generated images that manipulates defocus strength and focus distance continuously and intuitively given an input image [S1]. Unlike AR-GAN-DR, where defocus parameters are uniquely determined by the input image, AR-NeRF-R allows continuous interactive adjustment of aperture size $s$ and focus distance $f$ via auxiliary inputs [S1].
*   **Definition:** A conditional U-Net that takes an image and auxiliary parameters $s$ (aperture size) and $f$ (focus distance) to output a defocused image [S1].
*   **Training Steps:**
    1.  Generate training images at 128×128 resolution using AR-NeRF by increasing input point density [S1].
    2.  Train a conditional U-Net on these images [S1].
    3.  Feed aperture size $s$ and focus distance $f$ as auxiliary information to the U-Net [S1].
    4.  Output defocused images by varying $s$ and $f$ continuously [S1].
*   **Parameters:** Aperture size $s$ and focus distance $f$ are continuous control parameters [S1].
*   **Architecture:** U-Net with two auxiliary scalar inputs ($s, f$) concatenated or injected as conditioning signals [S1].
*   **Usage:** Trained on Oxford Flowers dataset using 128×128 images generated by AR-NeRF; evaluated on iPhone2DSLR Flower dataset [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Processing time and memory increase as the number of rays increases [S1]. The reduction in synthesis network units (128 vs 256) allows for a larger number of points per ray, which improves performance but increases computational cost per ray [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** AR-NeRF achieves lower FID and KID scores compared to baseline GANs (AR-GAN, AR-GAN++, RGBD-GAN) on Oxford Flowers, CUB-200-2011, and FFHQ datasets [S1]. For example, on FFHQ, AR-NeRF achieves an FID of 7.8 and KID of 3.67 (×10⁻³), outperforming AR-GAN (FID 10.4, KID 5.75) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables unsupervised learning of depth and defocus effects; allows continuous control over defocus parameters; maintains identity across viewpoints.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of ray density and network units; CoordConv is not suitable for all datasets; higher resolutions may require more computational resources [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When the goal is to learn geometric and optical properties of a scene from unposed natural images without depth labels, and when continuous defocus control is desired.
*   **When Not to Use:** When strict alignment of objects is not present and CoordConv is inadvertently used; when computational resources are limited and high ray density is required for quality.

## Background
AR-NeRF was introduced in the paper "AR-NeRF: Unsupervised Learning of Depth and Defocus Effects from Natural Images with Aperture Rendering Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It builds upon the pi-GAN framework [S1], which itself is a generative variant of NeRF. The method was developed to address the challenge of learning 3D structure and optical effects (like defocus) from unposed 2D images without explicit supervision. It was applied to three datasets: Oxford Flowers, CUB-200-2011, and FFHQ [S1]. The AR-NeRF-R variant was developed to provide interactive defocus control, trained on images generated by AR-NeRF [S1].
