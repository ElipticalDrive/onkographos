# CCNeRF
Also called: Compressible-Composable NeRF, Rank-residual Decomposition NeRF

## Overview
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition of the scene without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field *only* with tensor rank decomposition, preserving low-rank approximation properties to allow dynamic model size adjustment via rank truncation and arbitrary composition via rank concatenation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 2D images with known camera poses $(o, d)$ and a target rank configuration (ratio of vector-based to matrix-based components).
*   **Output:** A compressed radiance field representation consisting of rank components (vectors or matrices) and a weight matrix, capable of rendering colors $\hat{C}(r)$ for any ray $r$ and supporting composition of multiple scenes.

**Data Structures:**
The model structure consists of two main parts [S1]:
1.  **Rank Weights Matrix ($W$):** A matrix storing weights for different feature channels. If the total rank is $R$, and the rank is split into $R_{vec}$ vector components and $R_{mat}$ matrix components, this matrix maps the decomposed features to the final output dimensions (density and color).
2.  **Decomposed Rank Components:** A set of components that are either vector-based (CANDECOMP/PARAFAC or CP decomposition) or matrix-based (a triple plane variant).
    *   **Vector-based (CP):** For a 3D grid of size $H \times W \times D$, the feature tensor is decomposed into three factor matrices $U^x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times R}$, $U^y \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times R}$, and $U^z \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times R}$, and a core tensor $S \in \mathbb{R}^{R \times R \times R}$ (often simplified or absorbed into weights).
    *   **Matrix-based (Triple Plane):** Uses pairwise 2D planes (e.g., $U^{xy}, U^{xz}, U^{yz}$) to represent the 3D volume.
    *   The ratio of vector- to matrix-based components is controllable to trade off between model size and performance [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Representation (Rank-Residual Learning):**
    The 3D scene is modeled as a 4D feature volume described by rank components and a weight matrix. To encourage the preservation of primary information in lower ranks, a rank-residual learning strategy is employed. This ensures that the lower-rank components capture the most significant features of the scene, enabling near-optimal low-rank approximation [S1].

2.  **Querying a 3D Coordinate:**
    To evaluate the radiance field at a 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$:
    a. Project the coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ to the decomposed vectors or matrices. For CP decomposition, this involves selecting the appropriate rows from $U^x, U^y, U^z$. For the triple plane variant, it involves selecting elements from the 2D planes.
    b. Perform weighted interpolation using the stored rank weights $W$. The projected features are combined (e.g., via dot products or element-wise multiplication depending on the specific decomposition variant) and passed through the weight matrix to predict density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

3.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Given a ray $r$ originating at $o$ with direction $d$, sample points $x_i = o + t_i d$ along the ray. Compute the pixel color $\hat{C}(r)$ using the standard volume rendering equation [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i
    $$
    where:
    *   $T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance.
    *   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity of the $i$-th sample.
    *   $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between samples.
    *   $\sigma_i$ is the density and $c_i$ is the color predicted by the CCNeRF representation at $x_i$ [S1].

4.  **Compression (Rank Truncation):**
    Model size is dynamically adjusted by rank truncation. An empirical sort-and-truncate strategy is used to achieve near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank.
    a. Compute the importance of each rank component (e.g., based on the magnitude of weights or contribution to the field).
    b. Sort the components by importance.
    c. Truncate the components below a threshold or keep only the top $R'$ components.
    This allows trade-off between model size and rendering quality without retraining [S1]. Note that standard truncated Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD) does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].

5.  **Composition:**
    Different models are composed by concatenating their rank components along the rank dimension.
    a. For each object/model, a transformation matrix is recorded to control its position and orientation in the scene.
    b. To compose models, their rank components (vectors or matrices) are concatenated along the rank dimension.
    c. The weight matrix is expanded or adjusted to handle the concatenated features.
    This allows arbitrary composition without retraining or compatibility constraints, as there are no MLP renderers involved [S1].

## Subtopics

### Rank-Residual Learning
A strategy to encourage lower ranks to preserve more important information of the whole scene, enabling near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank [S1]. Choose this when dynamic level-of-detail (LOD) control is required without retraining.

### Hybrid Tensor Decomposition
Combines CP (vector-based) and Triple Plane (matrix-based) decompositions. Choose this when a balance between compactness (CP) and expressive power (Matrix) is needed.

### Composition via Concatenation
Composing models by concatenating rank components. Choose this when arbitrary composition of multiple 3D objects is required without retraining or compatibility constraints.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Querying a 3D coordinate involves projecting to decomposed vectors/matrices and weighted interpolation. This is generally faster than MLP-based rendering as it avoids non-linear activations and multiple layers. Complexity depends on the rank $R$ and grid size.
*   **Memory Complexity:** The model size is determined by the rank components and weight matrix. Compression via rank truncation allows dynamic reduction of memory footprint. The memory usage is $O(H \cdot W \cdot D \cdot R)$ for CP or $O((HW + HD + WD) \cdot R)$ for Triple Plane, where $R$ is the rank.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method achieves comparable rendering quality to state-of-the-art methods while enabling extra capability of compression and composition [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Model size can be dynamically adjusted by rank truncation. Composition scales by concatenating ranks, with storage cost mitigated by compressing unimportant objects in the composed scene [S1].
*   **Strengths:** No neural networks for rendering, allowing arbitrary composition without retraining. Dynamic compression without retraining. Near-optimal low-rank approximation.
*   **Limitations:** Quasi-optimal solution for truncated HOSVD. Requires careful tuning of rank and component ratios.
*   **When to use:** When compression and composition of 3D scenes are critical, and dynamic LOD control is needed.
*   **When not to use:** When maximum rendering fidelity is required without any compression, or when the scene has very high-frequency details that cannot be captured by low-rank approximations.

## Background
CCNeRF was introduced by Jiaxiang Tang, Xiaokang Chen, Jingbo Wang, and Gang Zeng from Peking University and Chinese University of Hong Kong in 2022 [S1]. The paper addresses the difficulty of manipulating implicit NeRF representations, which are often restricted by shared renderer networks or suffer from large model sizes. CCNeRF builds on the low-rank approximation property of SVD and tensor decomposition techniques like CP and Tucker decomposition [S1]. It is used for photo-realistic rendering and manipulation of 3D scenes, enabling efficient compression and composition [S1].
