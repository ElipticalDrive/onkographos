# UE4-NeRF

Also called: UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene

## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes, such as industrial parks and farmlands, by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. It partitions large environments into sub-NeRFs represented by textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, enabling real-time performance at 4K resolution [S1]. Unlike Block-NeRF, which is limited to grid structures, UE4-NeRF supports diverse terrains including mountainous areas, and unlike Mobile-NeRF, it handles tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects more effectively [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed camera images (often from drones) with GPS metadata; a large-scale scene to be partitioned.
*   **Outputs:** Real-time rendered images at high resolution (up to 4K) and 3D scene data compatible with UE4, including textured polygonal meshes and neural shaders.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large scene is divided into blocks (sub-NeRFs). Each training block is slightly larger than the corresponding physical area to create overlapping borders with neighbors for smooth transitions [S1].
2.  **Grid and Mesh:**
    *   A $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid is initialized for each block [S1].
    *   Polygonal meshes are created by selecting the center point of each grid cell and its six neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) [S1].
    *   The mesh geometry is based on a regular octahedron with 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
    *   Vertex positions are continuously optimized during training [S1].
3.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity information for the mesh vertices [S1].
4.  **Neural Networks:**
    *   **Encoder:** A 4-layer MLP with dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$. Inputs are positional information encoded via multi-resolution hash encoding. Outputs are opacity $\alpha_i$ and an 8-dimensional feature vector $M_i$ [S1].
    *   **Decoder:** A 3-layer MLP with dimensions $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$. Inputs are the feature vector $M_i$ and the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ encoded via spherical harmonics $SH(\mathbf{d})$. Outputs the final color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
5.  **Level of Detail (LOD) Hierarchy:** Meshes are trained at varying levels of detail. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights to maintain real-time performance [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform GPS-captured imagery coordinates to the UE4 coordinate system where the X-axis points east, Y-axis points south, and Z-axis points up, with a world scale of 100:1 [S1].
2.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the large scene into multiple blocks. Train a coarse model for sub-region segmentation to capture overall terrain and building outlines. Filter images associated with sub-regions and use a mask to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].
3.  **Mesh Initialization:** Initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. Select the center point of each grid along with its six neighboring grids to create polygonal meshes based on regular octahedra (20 faces) [S1].
4.  **Training:**
    *   For each block, train the Encoder-Decoder network for 80,000 epochs on a single Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
    *   The encoder $E$ takes positional information $p_i$ encoded by multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) and outputs opacity $\alpha_i$ and feature vector $M_i$:
        $$ E(\text{hash}(p_i); \theta_E) \rightarrow M_i, \alpha_i $$
    *   The decoder $D$ takes the feature vector $M_i$ and the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ encoded by spherical harmonics $SH(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}_i$:
        $$ D(M_i, SH(\mathbf{d}); \theta_D) \rightarrow \mathbf{c}_i $$
    *   Continuously optimize vertex positions of the meshes during training to address convergence issues on tilted surfaces [S1].
    *   Implement computationally intensive portions using CUDA to utilize GPU parallel computing [S1].
5.  **Shading Mesh Extraction:** Extract coarse polygonal meshes without textures during training. Apply vertex optimization. Encode texture maps in network outputs [S1].
6.  **LOD Generation:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail for different observation heights. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights [S1].
7.  **Rendering Integration:**
    *   Implement the Decoder network as a High-Level Shading Language (HLSL) fragment shader in UE4. The shader takes feature vectors as input and outputs colors [S1].
    *   Extract pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different levels of detail.
    *   Combine these meshes with the UE4 rasterization pipeline for real-time interactive rendering [S1].
8.  **Scene Editing (Optional):** Import traditional 3D models (.obj, .fbx) into the scene. UE4 automatically handles occlusion between added objects and the NeRF-rendered background, skipping neural shader computation for occluded regions [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Grid size: $128 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   Octahedron faces: 20 (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   Encoder MLP layers: 4 ($32 \to 64 \to 64 \to 64 \to 8$) [S1].
*   Decoder MLP layers: 3 ($17 \to 16 \to 16 \to 3$) [S1].
*   Training iterations: 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
*   Hardware: 1 Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   Coordinate System: X=East, Y=South, Z=Up [S1].
*   World Scale: 100:1 [S1].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces; UE4-NeRF addresses this by using regular octahedra [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass, plastic) due to opacity binarization; UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Performance:** Semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, potentially resulting in gaps or holes in the final rendered scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead, which increases with accuracy [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** Approximately 40 minutes per block on a single Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1]. For a full scene with $n$ blocks, the time is $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ [S1]. This is ~1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF and ~54× faster per block than Mega-NeRF [S1].
*   **Rendering Performance:** Real-time rendering at 4K resolution with up to 43 FPS on level-1 mesh hierarchy [S1]. 50 FPS on full scene at 2K resolution; $\ge 70$ FPS on individual blocks at 2K resolution [S1].
*   **Memory:** Significant memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers); increasing accuracy amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes; handles tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects better than Mobile-NeRF; supports scene editing via UE4 integration; avoids blurriness on natural scenes compared to Mip-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs and CUDA; pre-rendering may cause gaps/holes; exposure matching issues affect metric evaluation; semi-transparent objects reduce frame rates [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time visualization of large-scale outdoor scenes (farmland, construction sites, industrial parks) where interactivity and high resolution are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring precise ray-tracing from all angles without pre-rendering artifacts; environments where NVIDIA hardware is unavailable; scenes where exposure consistency is critical for metric-based evaluation [S1].

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of existing NeRF methods in real-time rendering of large-scale scenes, such as high training costs, inability to handle tilted surfaces, and lack of integration with game engines for scene editing [S1]. The method builds on [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by using multi-resolution hash encoding and integrates with [Optimization Procedures](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) via block-wise training [S1]. It is used in Unreal Engine 4 for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
