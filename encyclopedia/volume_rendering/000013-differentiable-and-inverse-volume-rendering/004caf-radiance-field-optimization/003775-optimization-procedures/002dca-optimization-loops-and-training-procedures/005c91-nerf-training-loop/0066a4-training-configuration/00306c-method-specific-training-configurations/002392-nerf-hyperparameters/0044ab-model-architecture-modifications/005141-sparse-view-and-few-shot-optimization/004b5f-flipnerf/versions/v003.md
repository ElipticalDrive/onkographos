# FlipNeRF

Also called: Flipped Reflection Rays for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis

## Overview
FlipNeRF is a regularization method for few-shot novel view synthesis that generates auxiliary "flipped reflection rays" from original input rays and estimated surface normals to improve rendering quality and surface normal accuracy with sparse inputs [S1]. Unlike existing regularization methods that focus on accurate depth estimation or pre-training methods requiring large datasets, FlipNeRF is optimized per scene without pre-training and derives rays from estimated normals without heuristic factors [S1]. It sets itself apart from Ref-NeRF by generating additional training rays rather than reparameterizing the radiance field's input viewing direction [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse set of input images with known camera poses; original input rays $\mathbf{r}$ defined by origins $\mathbf{o}$ and directions $\mathbf{d}$.
*   **Outputs:** A trained radiance field $F_\theta$ (built upon MixNeRF [S1]) capable of rendering novel views; estimated surface normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the MixNeRF architecture [S1], which models the color distribution along a ray using a mixture of density functions. For each sample $i$ along a ray:
*   Blending weights: $\pi_i$
*   Mean color: $\mu_i^c$
*   Scale parameter (uncertainty proxy): $\beta_i^c$
*   Density: $\sigma_i$
*   Normal vector estimate: $\mathbf{n}_i$ (derived from density gradient)

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Estimate Surface Normals:**
    Compute the estimated surface normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ for a ray as the weighted sum of blending weights and estimated normal vectors along the ray [S1]:
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \mathbf{n}_i $$
    where $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the gradient of volume density (following [3, 34, 36]) and $w_i$ are the blending weights. Note that $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is not explicitly normalized during training; it is trained to approximate a unit vector naturally [S1].

2.  **Derive Flipped Reflection Rays:**
    Calculate the flipped reflection direction $\mathbf{d}'$ using the original direction $\mathbf{d}$ and estimated normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{d}' = 2(\mathbf{d} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}} - \mathbf{d} $$
    Identify the position of the peak blending weight sample $p_s$ along the original ray, where the $s$-th sample has the highest blending weight:
    $$ p_s = \mathbf{o} + t_s \mathbf{d} $$
    Calculate the new origin $\mathbf{o}'$ for the flipped ray such that the peak sample of the flipped ray aligns with $p_s$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{o}' = p_s - t_s \mathbf{d}' $$
    Define the flipped reflection ray $\mathbf{r}'(t) = \mathbf{o}' + t \mathbf{d}'$.

3.  **Apply Masking Strategy:**
    Filter out ineffective flipped rays using a masking function $M(\mathbf{r}')$ based on the angle $\theta$ between the normal and the negative original direction [S1]:
    $$ M(\mathbf{r}') = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \arccos(-(\hat{\mathbf{d}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})) < \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $\tau$ is the threshold (typically $90^\circ$, or $30^\circ$ for LLFF) [S1]. Only rays with $M(\mathbf{r}')=1$ are used for training.

4.  **Compute Losses:**
    *   **MSE Loss:** Standard pixel-wise reconstruction error [S1]:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} || \hat{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{c}^{\text{GT}}(\mathbf{r}) ||_2^2 $$
    *   **NLL Loss:** Negative log-likelihood for original rays $\mathbf{r}$ and flipped rays $\mathbf{r}'$ (modeled by mixture density) [S1]:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}} = -\sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \log p(\mathbf{c}|\mathbf{r}) $$
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}' = -\sum_{\mathbf{r}' \in \mathcal{R}'} \log p(\mathbf{c}|\mathbf{r}') $$
    *   **Uncertainty-aware Emptiness Loss (UE Loss):** Reduces floating artifacts by considering output uncertainty $\rho$ [S1]:
        $$ l_{\text{UE}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \log (1 + \rho \cdot \eta \cdot w_i) $$
        where $\rho = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{c}^{\{r,g,b\}} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \beta_i^c$ is the uncertainty, and $\eta$ is a scaling factor [S1]. Apply similarly for flipped rays $\mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}'$.
    *   **Bottleneck Feature Consistency Loss (BFC Loss):** Encourages consistency between bottleneck features $\mathbf{b}$ (outputs of spatial MLP) of original and flipped rays using Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) [S1]:
        $$ l_{\text{BFC}}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}') = JSD(\psi(\mathbf{b}), \psi(\mathbf{b}')) $$
        where $\psi$ is the softmax function [S1].
    *   **Orientation Loss:** Penalizes backward-facing normal vectors using the underlying density gradient normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ [S1]:
        $$ l_{\text{Ori.}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \max(0, \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}})^2 $$

5.  **Optimize:**
    Minimize the total loss [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}' + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}' + \lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{BFC}} + \lambda_6 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Ori.}} $$
    Use the Adam optimizer with gradient clipping by value at 0.1 and norm at 0.1 [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Masking Threshold $\tau$:** $90^\circ$ for Realistic Synthetic 360° and DTU; $30^\circ$ for LLFF [S1].
*   **Training Duration:** 500 pixel epochs [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 4,096 [S1].
*   **Hardware:** 4 NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs [S1].
*   **Learning Rate Annealing:**
    *   Real.Syn. 360°: $[10^{-3}, 10^{-5}]$
    *   DTU: $[2 \times 10^{-3}, 2 \times 10^{-5}]$
    *   LLFF: $[2 \times 10^{-3}, 2 \times 10^{-5}]$
*   **Warm-up Step:** 512 (Real.Syn. 360°, LLFF); 1024 (DTU) [S1].
*   **Delay Multiplier:** $10^{-2}$ (Real.Syn. 360°); $10^{-3}$ (DTU, LLFF) [S1].
*   **Loss Balancing Weights ($\lambda$):**
    *   $\lambda_1$ (NLL): Annealed from $[4.0, 10^{-3}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[4 \times 10^{-1}, 10^{-4}]$ (DTU), $[4 \times 10^{-2}, 10^{-5}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_2$ (NLL'): Annealed from $[4 \times 10^{-1}, 10^{-4}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[4 \times 10^{-2}, 10^{-5}]$ (DTU), $[4 \times 10^{-3}, 10^{-6}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_3$ (UE): Annealed from $[10^{-4}, 10^{-1}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[10^{-5}, 10^{-2}]$ (DTU), $[10^{-4}, 10^{-1}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_4$ (UE'): Annealed from $[10^{-5}, 10^{-2}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$ (DTU), $[10^{-6}, 10^{-3}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_5$ (BFC): $10^{-2}$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $10^{-3}$ (DTU), $10^{-5}$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_6$ (Ori.): $10^{-1}$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $10^{-2}$ (DTU), $10^{-3}$ (LLFF) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Explicit Normalization:** Using explicitly normalized surface normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ causes training instability and degenerate results (PSNR 16.72) compared to not using explicit normalization (PSNR 22.24) [S1]. The normals should not be normalized in the forward pass; regularization ensures they approximate unit vectors [S1].
*   **Initial Training Stage:** Without explicit normalization, estimated surface normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ are not unit vectors initially, with most $||\hat{\mathbf{n}}||^2$ far from 1. The training process naturally concentrates $||\hat{\mathbf{n}}||^2$ close to 1 [S1].
*   **Ineffective Rays:** Flipped rays that do not satisfy the photo-consistency condition (angle $\theta > \tau$) provide misleading training cues and must be masked [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Achieves SOTA performance on Realistic Synthetic 360°, DTU, and LLFF benchmarks, outperforming baselines by a large margin in 3/4-view settings [S1]. On the evaluation dataset, masking with $\tau=90^\circ$ achieved PSNR 19.55, SSIM 0.767, LPIPS 0.180, and Average Error 0.101, outperforming no masking ($\tau=180^\circ$) which had PSNR 18.76 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is optimized per scene and does not require pre-training on large datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces floating artifacts effectively; improves surface normal accuracy; does not require heuristic factors for unseen view generation; works with sparse inputs (3-4 views) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of estimated surface normals; performance degrades if ineffective rays are not properly masked [S1].
*   **When to use:** Few-shot novel view synthesis where accurate surface normals and reduced floating artifacts are critical, and dense input data is unavailable [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When dense input views are available (standard NeRF suffices); when pre-training on large datasets is feasible and preferred [S1].

## Background
FlipNeRF was proposed in 2023 by researchers supported by NRF, IITP, and Samsung Electronics [S1]. It is built upon MixNeRF [S1], which leverages a mixture model framework to represent input rays. The method addresses the limitation of NeRF requiring dense multi-view images for practical application [S1]. It differs from pre-training methods [5, 6, 12, 19, 29, 35, 39, 43] and other regularization methods that focus on depth estimation [31, 25, 15, 30, 8] or unseen viewpoint sampling with heuristic factors [25, 15, 17] [S1]. It also differs from Ref-NeRF [36] by generating additional training rays rather than reparameterizing the radiance field [S1].
