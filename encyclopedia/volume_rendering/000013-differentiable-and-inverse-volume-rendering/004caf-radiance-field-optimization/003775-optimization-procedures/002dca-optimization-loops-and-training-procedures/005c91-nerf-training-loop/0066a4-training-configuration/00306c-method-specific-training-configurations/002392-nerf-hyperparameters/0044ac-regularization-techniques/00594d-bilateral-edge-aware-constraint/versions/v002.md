# Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint

Also called: Lbilateral

## Overview
The Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint is a geometric regularization technique for NeRF-based volume rendering that suppresses high-frequency noise while preserving sharp geometric discontinuities such as building roofs and shadow boundaries [S1]. Unlike standard smoothness constraints that blur distinct edges, this method dynamically weights geometric penalties using photometric differences between neighboring rays, ensuring that significant color changes are not penalized as geometric errors [S1].

## How it works
The method enforces local coplanarity of sampled 3D points by minimizing their projected distance onto the tangent plane of a central point, weighted by bilateral coefficients derived from spatial proximity and photometric similarity [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training rays. For each ray $i$, the method requires the rendered 3D position $P_i$, the albedo feature $c_i$, and the surface normal $n_i$ at the sampled point.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}_{bilateral}$ added to the total optimization objective.

**Data Structures**
*   **Ray Sample:** Contains 3D coordinates $P \in \mathbb{R}^3$, albedo feature $c \in \mathbb{R}^C$ (where $C$ is the feature dimension), and normal vector $n \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Neighbor Set:** For each ray $i$, a set $N_i$ of $K$ neighbor rays.

**Parameters**
*   $K$: Number of neighbors sampled per ray. Value: 4 [S1].
*   $\gamma_{bi}$: Weight for the bilateral edge-aware term. Selected from $\{0.1, 0.01, 0.001\}$ based on validation performance [S1].
*   $\sigma_d$: Adaptive spatial scale.
*   $\sigma_w$: Adaptive photometric scale.

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Neighbor Sampling:** For each training ray $i$, draw $K=4$ integer image-plane offsets uniformly from the range $[-2, 2] \times [-2, 2]$, excluding the zero offset $(0,0)$ [S1].
2.  **Neighbor Rendering:** Render the corresponding neighbor rays to recover their 3D positions $P_j$, albedo features $c_j$, and normals $n_j$ for all $j \in N_i$ [S1].
3.  **Compute Adaptive Scales:**
    *   Calculate the adaptive spatial scale $\sigma_d$ as the root mean square of the Euclidean distances between the central point $P_i$ and its neighbors $P_j$:
        $$ \sigma_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| P_i - P_j \|^2} $$
    *   Calculate the adaptive photometric scale $\sigma_w$ as the root mean square of the Euclidean distances between the central albedo $c_i$ and its neighbors $c_j$:
        $$ \sigma_w = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| c_i - c_j \|^2} $$
    *   *Note:* If $K=0$ or distances are zero, $\sigma$ values may require a small epsilon guard to avoid division by zero or undefined weights [G].
4.  **Calculate Bilateral Weights:** For each neighbor $j \in N_i$, compute the weight $w_{i,j}$ using the exponential terms for spatial and photometric distances:
    $$ w_{i,j} = \exp\left(-\frac{\| P_i - P_j \|^2}{2\sigma_d^2}\right) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\| c_i - c_j \|^2}{2\sigma_w^2}\right) $$
    Points close in both geometry and albedo receive strong weights; points with inconsistent photometric cues receive weak weights, preserving potential discontinuities [S1].
5.  **Compute Normalization Factor:** Calculate $l_i$, the average edge length for the central point, defined as the mean distance to its neighbors:
    $$ l_i = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| P_i - P_j \| $$
    This ensures the loss is scale-invariant [S1].
6.  **Calculate Loss:** Minimize the projected distance of neighboring points onto the tangent plane of the central point. The bilateral geometric loss is:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{bilateral} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{1}{l_i} \sum_{j \in N_i, j \neq i} w_{i,j} \left( (P_i - P_j) \cdot n_i \right)^2 $$
    where $n_i$ is the unit normal vector at point $i$, and $N$ is the total number of rays [S1].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def bilateral_edge_aware_constraint(rays, neighbors, normals, albedos, gamma_bi):
    """
    Computes the Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint loss.
    
    Args:
        rays: List of ray objects, each containing:
            - P: 3D position (tensor, shape [3])
            - c: Albedo feature (tensor, shape [C])
            - n: Surface normal (tensor, shape [3])
        neighbors: List of lists. neighbors[i] contains indices of K neighbor rays for ray i.
        normals: List of normal tensors for all rays.
        albedos: List of albedo feature tensors for all rays.
        gamma_bi: Weight for the bilateral term.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar tensor representing the bilateral loss.
    """
    K = 4
    total_loss = 0.0
    N = len(rays)
    
    for i in range(N):
        P_i = rays[i].P
        c_i = rays[i].c
        n_i = normals[i]
        
        neighbor_indices = neighbors[i]
        if len(neighbor_indices) != K:
            # Fallback or error handling if sampling fails
            continue
            
        # Step 3: Compute Adaptive Scales
        spatial_diffs_sq = []
        photometric_diffs_sq = []
        
        for j_idx in neighbor_indices:
            P_j = rays[j_idx].P
            c_j = rays[j_idx].c
            
            dist_sq = torch.sum((P_i - P_j) ** 2)
            spatial_diffs_sq.append(dist_sq)
            
            photometric_dist_sq = torch.sum((c_i - c_j) ** 2)
            photometric_diffs_sq.append(photometric_dist_sq)
            
        # Calculate sigma_d and sigma_w
        # Using mean of squared distances inside sqrt
        sigma_d_sq = sum(spatial_diffs_sq) / K
        sigma_w_sq = sum(photometric_diffs_sq) / K
        
        # Guard against zero variance
        sigma_d_sq = max(sigma_d_sq, 1e-8)
        sigma_w_sq = max(sigma_w_sq, 1e-8)
        
        # Step 5: Compute Normalization Factor l_i
        l_i = sum(torch.sqrt(torch.tensor(d)) for d in spatial_diffs_sq) / K
        l_i = max(l_i, 1e-8) # Guard against zero length
        
        # Step 4 & 6: Calculate Weights and Accumulate Loss
        ray_loss = 0.0
        for idx, j_idx in enumerate(neighbor_indices):
            P_j = rays[j_idx].P
            c_j = rays[j_idx].c
            
            # Spatial weight
            w_spatial = torch.exp(-spatial_diffs_sq[idx] / (2 * sigma_d_sq))
            # Photometric weight
            w_photometric = torch.exp(-photometric_diffs_sq[idx] / (2 * sigma_w_sq))
            
            w_ij = w_spatial * w_photometric
            
            # Projected distance onto tangent plane
            diff_vec = P_i - P_j
            projected_dist = torch.dot(diff_vec, n_i)
            
            ray_loss += w_ij * (projected_dist ** 2)
            
        total_loss += ray_loss / l_i
        
    return (gamma_bi * total_loss) / N
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $K$ is the number of neighbors (typically 4). Each neighbor requires a forward pass to render $P_j$ and $c_j$, making it computationally more expensive than simple smoothness terms that rely on pre-computed or cached data [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing neighbor indices and temporary tensors for distances and weights. No significant additional persistent memory compared to standard NeRF training [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively preserves sharp geometric features (roofs, walls, shadow transitions) that are often blurred by standard L2 smoothness constraints [S1]. It is adaptive to local scene complexity via $\sigma_d$ and $\sigma_w$ [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate normal estimation and albedo features; errors in these inputs propagate to the weights. The requirement to render neighbor rays adds overhead to each training step [S1].
*   **When to use:** Recommended for 3D reconstruction tasks requiring high geometric fidelity, such as Digital Surface Model (DSM) generation from satellite imagery, where building boundaries are critical [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Not suitable for scenes with uniform albedo and no sharp edges, where simpler smoothness constraints suffice, or in resource-constrained environments where the extra rendering cost is prohibitive [S1].

## Background
The Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint was introduced in the context of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for Digital Surface Model (DSM) reconstruction from multi-view satellite images [S1]. It addresses the limitation of implicit NeRF geometry, which lacks fixed surface connectivity, by using image-plane offsets to define neighborhoods [S1]. The method was proposed alongside a Normal Smoothness Constraint and a Physical Shading Loss to enhance geometric accuracy and suppress noise while maintaining structural integrity [S1].
