# Lighting and Appearance Models

Also called: View-Dependent Appearance, Illumination Modeling

## Overview
Lighting and Appearance Models modify the standard NeRF architecture to explicitly represent or control the illumination and material properties of a scene, rather than relying solely on view-dependent color predictions from a single MLP [Parent]. These models solve the problem of semantic lighting control, relighting, and stylization by decoupling geometry, albedo, and illumination . They set themselves apart from sibling methods like [NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W)](11995-nerf-in-the-wild-nerf-w.md) by using explicit mathematical bases or neural modules for light, rather than appearance embeddings , and from [StylizedNeRF](14882-stylizednerf.md) by focusing on physical or explicit lighting representations rather than mutual learning with 2D style transfer .

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture  consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a coarse network and a fine network. The coarse network takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs a latent feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and volume density $\sigma$. The fine network takes $\mathbf{h}$, positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and positional encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$.

$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
$$

Modifications generally fall into three categories:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation.
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs).

## Subtopics

### [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md)
Neural-PIL replaces costly numerical light integration with a neural query, predicting pre-integrated light directly from an illumination embedding, direction, and roughness. It captures finer illumination details than Spherical Harmonics or Spherical Gaussians.

### [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md)
This method replaces the view-dependent color MLP with an explicit 2nd-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) illumination model. It allows for explicit relighting by varying learnable SH coefficients, addressing the lack of semantic lighting control in standard NeRF.

### [StylizedNeRF](14882-stylizednerf.md)
StylizedNeRF generates view-consistent stylized renderings by decoupling style transfer from geometry optimization through mutual learning with a 2D stylization network. It differs from lighting-specific models by focusing on artistic style transfer rather than physical illumination representation.

## Background
The standard NeRF formulation  predicts color directly as a function of position and view direction, implicitly encoding lighting but lacking explicit control. Subsequent works introduced explicit lighting models to enable relighting and better generalization to varying illumination conditions. Neural-PIL  and Spherical Harmonics NeRF  represent early efforts to separate material/albedo from illumination. StylizedNeRF  extends this by integrating 2D style transfer techniques into the 3D optimization loop.
