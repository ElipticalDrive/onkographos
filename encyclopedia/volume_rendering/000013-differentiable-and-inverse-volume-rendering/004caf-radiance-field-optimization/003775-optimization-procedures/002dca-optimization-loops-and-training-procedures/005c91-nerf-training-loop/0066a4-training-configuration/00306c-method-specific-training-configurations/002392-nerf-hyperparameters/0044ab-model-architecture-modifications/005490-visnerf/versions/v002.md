# ViSNeRF

Also called: ViSNeRF: Efficient Multidimensional Neural Radiance Field Representation for Visualization Synthesis of Dynamic Volumetric Scenes

## Overview
ViSNeRF is a hybrid neural radiance field method that combines explicit feature grids with implicit MLP decoders to synthesize visualizations of dynamic volumetric scenes [S1]. It solves the problem of efficiently representing and rendering scenes with arbitrary adjustable parameters (e.g., time, isovalue, transfer functions) by factorizing the scene into a 3D spatial tensor and a K-dimensional parameter tensor [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that use spherical harmonics for view-dependent color or fully implicit networks, ViSNeRF uses vector-matrix decomposition for spatial features and CP decomposition for parameters, decoded by small MLPs [S1]. This approach reduces memory complexity from $O(N^3 M^K)$ to $O(N^2 M)$ while maintaining high visualization quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Training images (DVR or IR) with camera poses, volume resolution, and parameter values defining scene frames.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered color images $\hat{C}(r)$ for arbitrary viewpoints and parameter combinations.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Spatial Feature Grid:** A 3D tensor $T^3$ of size $N \times N \times N$ (initially $128^3$, up to $300^3$). It is not stored as a dense volume but factorized into:
    *   Three spatial matrices: $M^X_{XY}, M^Y_{XZ}, M^Z_{YZ}$ representing XY, XZ, and YZ planes.
    *   Three spatial vectors: $v^X, v^Y, v^Z$ representing axes X, Y, Z.
    *   Density features use $R_\sigma=16$ components; appearance features use $R_c=48$ components [S1].
2.  **Parameter Feature Grid:** A K-D tensor $T^K$ of size $M^K$, where $M$ is the number of distinct values for each parameter. Factorized via CP decomposition into $R_p$ sets of K vectors $\{v^1_r, \dots, v^K_r\}$ [S1].
3.  **MLP Decoders:** Two small MLPs ($g_\sigma$ for density, $g_c$ for color), each with one hidden layer of 128 channels [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** Cast rays from screen pixels through camera locations into the scene [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** For each ray, sample $n$ points along the ray. For each point, obtain spatial coordinates $(x,y,z)$ and scene parameters $(p_0, p_1, \dots)$ [S1].
3.  **Feature Retrieval:**
    *   Query the spatial feature grid at $(x,y,z)$ to retrieve spatial features.
    *   Query the parameter feature grid at $(p_0, p_1, \dots)$ to retrieve parameter features [S1].
4.  **Feature Combination:**
    *   Compute element-wise products between each spatial matrix-vector pair ($M^X_{XY} \circ v^Z$, etc.) [S1].
    *   Compute element-wise product of the parameter vectors [S1].
    *   Concatenate these products to form the final feature vector $f(x)$ [S1].
5.  **Decoding:**
    *   Encode view direction $d$ using Spherical Harmonics ($E_{SH}$) and position $x$ using Positional Encoding ($E_P$) [S1].
    *   Decode density: $\sigma(x) = g_\sigma(f_\sigma(x), E_P(x))$ [S1].
    *   Decode color: $c(x) = g_c(f_c(x), E_{SH}(d))$ [S1].
6.  **Volume Rendering:** Estimate the ray color $\hat{C}(r)$ using stratified sampling [S1]:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ and $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1].
7.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate total loss $L = L_{REC} + \lambda_1 L_{L1} + \lambda_2 L_{TV}$ [S1].
8.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using Adam optimizer [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Spatial Factorization (Block-Term):**
    $$ V = \sum_{r=1}^{R_1} M^X_{XY,r} \circ v^Z_r + \sum_{r=1}^{R_2} M^Y_{XZ,r} \circ v^Y_r + \sum_{r=1}^{R_3} M^Z_{YZ,r} \circ v^X_r $$
    where $R_1=R_2=R_3=R$ for simplicity [S1].
*   **Parameter Factorization (CP):**
    $$ T^K = \sum_{r=1}^{R_p} v^1_r \circ v^2_r \circ \dots \circ v^K_r $$
    where $R_p$ is the number of one-rank components [S1].
*   **L1 Norm Loss:**
    $$ L_{L1} = \begin{cases} \sum ||1 - w|| & \text{if } W \in v_p \text{ (parameter vectors)} \\ \sum_{w \in W} ||w||_1 / |W| & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $W$ are weights in vectors/matrices [S1].
*   **Total Variation Loss:**
    $$ L_{TV} = L_{TV1} + L_{TV2} $$
    $$ L_{TV1} = \frac{1}{|V||Q|l} \sum_{v,q,i} ||v_{i,q} - v_{i-1,q}||_2 $$
    $$ L_{TV2} = \frac{1}{|M||Q|hw} \sum_{M,q,i,j} (||M_{q,i,j} - M_{q,i-1,j}||_2^2 + ||M_{q,i,j} - M_{q,i,j-1}||_2^2) $$
    where $v \in V$ (vectors), $M \in M$ (matrices), $q \in Q$ (channels), $l$ is vector length, $h,w$ are matrix dimensions [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Resolution:** Initial $N=128$; increases to $300^3$ for complex scenes [S1].
*   **Feature Depths:** Density $R_\sigma=16$, Appearance $R_c=48$, Parameter $R_p=4$ [S1].
*   **Samples per Ray:** 192 (standard), 512 (if grid is $300^3$) [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 4096 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.99$) [S1].
*   **Learning Rates:** Matrices/Vectors: 0.02; MLPs: 0.001 [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_1$ (L1): $10^{-4}$ initially, $10^{-5}$ after 2,000 iterations; $\lambda_2$ (TV): 1.0 [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 30,000 for static; 90,000 for dynamic [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Noise:** Without L1 and TV regularization, excessive noise and film grains appear in spatial matrices and images [S1].
*   **Memory:** High-resolution datasets (e.g., 1024x1024) require significant RAM (~29 GB for ray storage initially). ViSNeRF filters rays not intersecting the bounding box to reduce runtime RAM [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Minimum 32 GB RAM / 10 GB VRAM; 64 GB RAM / 16 GB VRAM recommended for high-res [S1].
*   **Bounding Box:** Empty voxels must be handled. A mask volume is created at iteration 2,000 to shrink the bounding box and enable voxel skipping [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is faster than baselines like InSituNet and CoordNet [S1]. Inference is slower than InSituNet and StyleGAN2, but faster than traditional DVR/IR for low-resolution (256x256) datasets [S1]. Frame rate is at least 1.86 FPS for 256x256 images, dropping for 1024x1024 [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduces spatial memory from $O(N^3)$ to $O(N^2)$ and parameter space from $O(M^K)$ to $O(M)$ [S1]. Overall complexity $O(N^2 M)$ [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the five jets dataset, ViSNeRF achieves PSNR 27.51, SSIM 0.945, LPIPS 0.050, outperforming EG3D, CoordNet, InSituNet, and StyleGAN2 [S1]. Model size is 15.43 MB for five jets, 69.91 MB for Tangaroa [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High quality with minimal training data (95% less than InSituNet), efficient parameter-space exploration, super-resolution capability [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Slow inference for high resolutions, high VRAM/RAM requirements for large datasets [S1].
*   **When to use:** When synthesizing visualizations for dynamic volumetric scenes with adjustable parameters, especially when training data is limited [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When real-time inference at high resolutions is critical and VRAM is limited [S1].

## Background
ViSNeRF was introduced in the paper "ViSNeRF: Efficient Multidimensional Neural Radiance Field Representation for Visualization Synthesis of Dynamic Volumetric Scenes" [S1]. It builds on the NeRF framework [S1] and TensoRF [S1], adapting tensor factorization techniques (block-term and CP decomposition) to handle dynamic scenes with arbitrary parameters [S1]. It is used in scientific visualization for datasets like Nyx, Tangaroa, and five jets [S1].
