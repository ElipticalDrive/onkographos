# UE4-NeRF

Also called: UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene

## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes, such as industrial parks and farmlands, by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. Unlike traditional NeRF methods that rely on volumetric ray-marching or grid-based lookups, UE4-NeRF partitions the scene into sub-regions (blocks), represents each block using textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and computes colors via a neural shader within the engine [S1]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by enabling real-time frame rates at high resolutions (up to 4K) and supporting scene editing with traditional 3D models, addressing the slow convergence and opacity binarization limitations of Mobile-NeRF and the grid-structure constraints of Block-NeRF [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Posed camera images (typically drone-captured, ~6000×4000 pixels) with GPS metadata; coarse segmentation masks for block boundaries.
*   **Outputs:** Real-time rendered images at user-defined viewpoints; pre-rendered polygonal meshes with texture atlases for UE4 import.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Block Partition:** The scene is divided into overlapping blocks. Each block has a training area slightly larger than the visible scene area to ensure smooth transitions at borders [S1].
2.  **Mesh Geometry:**
    *   Initialized from a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid.
    *   Vertices are selected from the center of each grid cell and its six immediate neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) [S1].
    *   Faces are formed into regular octahedra. Each octahedron consists of 20 faces: 8 exterior and 12 interior [S1].
    *   Vertex positions are optimized during training [S1].
3.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity values for each vertex/face, replacing the binary opacity mask used in Mobile-NeRF to support semi-transparent objects [S1].
4.  **Neural Shader (HLSL):** Implemented as a fragment shader in UE4. It takes feature vectors as input and outputs final colors [S1].
5.  **LOD Hierarchy:** Multiple levels of mesh detail are trained. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights to maintain performance [S1].

**Network Architecture:**
The method uses an Encoder-Decoder MLP structure, implemented in CUDA for training acceleration [S1].

*   **Encoder Network ($E$):**
    *   Input: Positional information $p_i$ encoded via multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) [S1].
    *   Structure: 4-layer MLP.
    *   Dimensions: $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
    *   Output: Opacity $\alpha_i$ and an 8-dimensional feature vector $M_i$ [S1].
    *   Formula:
        $$ M_i, \alpha_i = E(\text{hash}(p_i); \theta_E) $$
        where $\theta_E$ are the encoder parameters [S1].

*   **Decoder Network ($D$):**
    *   Input: Feature vector $M_i$ and view direction $d_i$.
    *   View Direction Encoding: Spherical Harmonics (SH) of degree $k$ (implied by $SH(d_i)$) [S1].
    *   Structure: 3-layer MLP.
    *   Dimensions: $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1]. (Note: Input dimension 17 combines the 8-dim feature vector and 9-dim SH coefficients for degree 2, or similar configuration) [S1].
    *   Output: Final color $c_i$ [S1].
    *   Formula:
        $$ c_i = D(M_i, SH(d_i); \theta_D) $$
        where $\theta_D$ are the decoder parameters [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Convert GPS-captured drone imagery coordinates to the UE4 coordinate system. In this system, the X-axis points East, the Y-axis points South, and the Z-axis points Up. Apply a 100:1 scale conversion (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].
2.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the large-scale scene into multiple blocks. Train a coarse segmentation model to capture overall terrain and building outlines. Filter images associated with each sub-region and apply masks to exclude pixels outside the specific block's area [S1].
3.  **Mesh Initialization:** For each block, initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. Select center points and six neighboring grids to form polygonal meshes based on regular octahedra (20 faces: 8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
4.  **Training Loop:**
    *   For each ray $r(t) = o + td$ (where $o$ is camera origin, $d$ is viewing direction $(\theta, \phi)$), calculate intersections with the polygonal meshes to determine sampling points [S1].
    *   Compute opacity and feature vectors using the Encoder: $M_i, \alpha_i = E(\text{hash}(p_i); \theta_E)$.
    *   Compute color using the Decoder: $c_i = D(M_i, SH(d_i); \theta_D)$.
    *   Optimize vertex positions of the meshes continuously during training [S1].
    *   Train for 80,000 epochs per block to achieve convergence [S1].
5.  **Shading Mesh Extraction:** Extract coarse polygonal meshes without textures during training. Apply vertex optimization. Encode texture maps (feature vectors and opacity) in the network outputs [S1].
6.  **LOD Generation:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail. Extract pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different LODs for the final rendering module [S1].
7.  **Rendering Integration:** Deploy the neural shader (HLSL fragment shader) in UE4. The shader takes the feature vectors from the texture atlas as input and outputs colors. Combine these meshes with the UE4 rasterization pipeline for real-time interactive rendering [S1].
8.  **Scene Editing (Optional):** Import traditional 3D models (.obj, .fbx). UE4 handles occlusion automatically, skipping neural shader computation for occluded regions [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
# UE4-NeRF Training and Rendering Logic
# Note: Multi-resolution hash encoding is assumed to be provided by Instant-NGP implementation.

def train_block(block_data, coarse_mask):
    """
    Trains a single block of the scene.
    block_data: List of camera poses, images, and GPS coordinates.
    coarse_mask: Mask defining the valid region of the block.
    """
    # 1. Coordinate Transformation
    # Convert GPS to UE4 coords: X=East, Y=South, Z=Up. Scale 100:1.
    cameras = transform_gps_to_ue4(block_data.gps, scale_factor=100.0)
    
    # 2. Mesh Initialization
    # Initialize 128x128x128 grid. Create octahedra from center + 6 neighbors.
    mesh = initialize_octahedral_mesh(grid_size=128)
    
    # 3. Network Initialization
    # Encoder: 4-layer MLP (32->64->64->64->8)
    encoder = MLP(layers=[32, 64, 64, 64, 8])
    # Decoder: 3-layer MLP (17->16->16->3)
    # Input 17 = 8 (features) + 9 (SH degree 2)
    decoder = MLP(layers=[17, 16, 16, 3])
    
    # 4. Optimization Loop
    for epoch in range(80000):
        for camera in cameras:
            # Filter images using coarse_mask to exclude pixels outside scene area
            valid_pixels = apply_mask(camera.image, coarse_mask)
            
            for pixel in valid_pixels:
                # Ray definition: r(t) = o + t*d
                ray_origin = camera.position
                ray_dir = pixel.direction # (theta, phi)
                
                # Find intersection with mesh to get sampling point p_i
                p_i = intersect_mesh(mesh, ray_origin, ray_dir)
                
                # Encode position using multi-resolution hash
                hash_features = hash_encode(p_i)
                
                # Encoder: Output opacity and feature vector
                # E(hash(p_i); theta_E) -> M_i, alpha_i
                M_i, alpha_i = encoder(hash_features)
                
                # View direction encoding (Spherical Harmonics)
                # Assuming SH degree 2 for 9 coefficients
                sh_coeffs = spherical_harmonics(ray_dir, degree=2)
                
                # Concatenate feature vector and SH coefficients
                # 8 (M_i) + 9 (SH) = 17
                decoder_input = concatenate(M_i, sh_coeffs)
                
                # Decoder: Output color
                # D(M_i, SH(d_i); theta_D) -> c_i
                c_i = decoder(decoder_input)
                
                # Compute loss against ground truth pixel color
                loss = mse(c_i, pixel.color)
                
                # Backpropagate to update encoder, decoder, and mesh vertices
                update_gradients(loss, [encoder, decoder, mesh.vertices])
                
    return mesh, encoder, decoder

def render_block(mesh, encoder, decoder, camera, lod_level):
    """
    Renders a block using the UE4 rasterization pipeline.
    """
    # Select LOD mesh
    current_mesh = get_lod_mesh(mesh, lod_level)
    
    # Extract texture atlas (feature vectors and opacity)
    texture_atlas = extract_texture_atlas(current_mesh, encoder)
    
    # Deploy HLSL Fragment Shader in UE4
    # The shader takes feature vectors from texture atlas and outputs colors
    # This is handled by the engine, but logically:
    for pixel in camera.framebuffer:
        # Interpolate feature vector from texture atlas based on barycentric coords
        M_i = interpolate_texture(texture_atlas, pixel.uv)
        
        # Compute SH coefficients from view direction
        sh_coeffs = spherical_harmonics(pixel.view_direction, degree=2)
        decoder_input = concatenate(M_i, sh_coeffs)
        
        # Neural shader computes color
        color = decoder(decoder_input)
        
        # Apply opacity blending if necessary (though rasterization handles depth)
        pixel.color = color
        
    return camera.framebuffer
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Size:** $128 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   **Octahedron Faces:** 20 total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   **Encoder Layers:** $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
*   **Decoder Layers:** $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1].
*   **Training Hardware:** 1 Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
*   **Coordinate System:** X=East, Y=South, Z=Up [S1].
*   **World Scale:** 100:1 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces. UE4-NeRF mitigates this by using regular octahedra and continuous vertex optimization [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Mobile-NeRF uses opacity binarization, failing on transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass). UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps, supporting these materials [S1].
*   **Performance:** Semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, potentially resulting in gaps or holes in the final scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs. Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead, which increases with accuracy [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than predecessors. UE4-NeRF trains ~54× faster per block than Mega-NeRF and 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers). Increasing rendering accuracy amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Training Time:** ~40 minutes per block on 1× RTX 3090. Full scene time: $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ for $n$ blocks [S1].
    *   **Rendering FPS:** Up to 43 FPS at 4K resolution (Level-1 mesh). 50 FPS on full scene at 2K resolution. $\ge$70 FPS on individual blocks at 2K resolution [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes; supports scene editing with traditional 3D models; handles semi-transparent objects; high fidelity on small objects and dense textures compared to Mip-NeRF and Mega-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs and CUDA; pre-rendering artifacts (gaps/holes) at extreme angles; exposure matching issues in UE4 affect metric evaluation; semi-transparent vegetation reduces FPS [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time visualization of large-scale outdoor scenes (farmland, construction sites, industrial parks) where interactivity and scene editing are required.
*   **When Not to Use:** Small-scale indoor scenes (overkill), scenes requiring perfect geometric completeness without pre-rendering gaps, or environments without NVIDIA hardware.

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of NeRF in real-time rendering and large-scale scene handling. It builds on [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by utilizing multi-resolution hash encoding from Instant-NGP [S1]. It is designed to integrate with the Unreal Engine 4 rasterization pipeline, enabling real-time interactive rendering and scene editing, which distinguishes it from offline NeRF methods and other real-time variants like Mobile-NeRF and Mega-NeRF [S1]. It is used for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
