# NeRF2Points

Also called: Layered Perception and Integrated Modeling

## Overview
NeRF2Points is a method for generating large-scale, geometrically consistent RGBD point clouds from street-view radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of "pavement collapse" and floating artifacts in point clouds generated by standard NeRF variants when applied to sparse, large-scale street-view data with weak textures (e.g., road surfaces) [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by separating the scene into road and street components via a ray gate, modeling them with separate radiance fields, and merging them using rigid transformation derived from shared rays [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** $N$ RGB images $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ from a street-view sequence; coarse camera poses from WIGO; refined poses from COLMAP; depth and normal estimations from Omnidata; road/scene masks from Mask2Former; SIFT features for temporal correspondence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A merged RGBD point cloud $PC \in \mathbb{R}^{(N_1+N_2) \times 3}$; rendered RGB images [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Gate Model $M(\cdot)$:** A segmentation model (trained on Cityscapes) outputting a binary mask $M(I) \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ indicating road pixels [S1, unofficial]. Includes a morphological dilation operator to create an intersection zone [S1, unofficial].
*   **Radiance Fields:** Two separate MLP-based radiance fields (based on NeRFacto architecture [S1, unofficial]):
    *   $\mathcal{F}_r$: Models road surface [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathcal{F}_s$: Models street scene (buildings, sky, etc.) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Each field maps $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (\mathbf{c}, \sigma, \mathbf{n})$, where $\mathbf{c}$ is color, $\sigma$ is density, and $\mathbf{n}$ is normal [S1, unofficial]. Depth is predicted directly by the network [G].
*   **Point Clouds:**
    *   $PC_r \in \mathbb{R}^{N_1 \times 3}$: Points from $\mathcal{F}_r$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   $PC_s \in \mathbb{R}^{N_2 \times 3}$: Points from $\mathcal{F}_s$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   $P \subset PC_r, Q \subset PC_s$: Intersection points from shared rays (size $N' \ll N_1, N_2$) [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Data Preparation:**
    *   Obtain initial 6-DoF poses using Wheel-IMU-GNSS Odometry (WIGO) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Refine poses and estimate sparse point cloud using COLMAP with Structure from Motion (SfM) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Predict depth and normal maps using Omnidata [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generate road/scene masks $M(I)$ using Mask2Former [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Ray Partitioning (Layered Perception):**
    *   For each image $I$ and pixel $i$, define ray $Ray_i = \{\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{d}_i\}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute road rays $Ray_{i_1}^r = Ray_i(M(I))$ and scene rays $Ray_{i_2}^s = Ray_i(1-M(I))$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute intersection rays $Ray_{i_3} = Ray_{i_1}^r \cap Ray_{i_2}^s$ (pixels where dilation causes overlap) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Reconstruct original ray set: $Ray_i = (Ray_{i_1}^r - Ray_{i_3}) \cup (Ray_{i_2}^s - Ray_{i_3}) \cup Ray_{i_3}$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Separate Radiance Field Training:**
    *   Train $\mathcal{F}_r$ using supervision from $Ray_{i_1}^r$ (color, depth, normal) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Train $\mathcal{F}_s$ using supervision from $Ray_{i_2}^s$ (color, depth, normal) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use **Geometric-Aware Consistency Regularization (GAC)** during training:
        *   **Spatial Dynamic Consistency ($\ell_{sdc}$):**
            *   Calculate matching cost for each ray bundle $Ray_i'$ (road or scene):
                $$ \mathbf{cos}_i = \lambda_1 \ell_{depth} + \lambda_2 \ell_{rgb} + \lambda_3 \ell_{normal} $$
                where:
                $$ \ell_{depth}(\mathbf{t}_1', \mathbf{t}_2') = \sum \| \textit{norm}(\mathbf{t}_1') - \textit{norm}(\mathbf{t}_2') \|_2^2 $$
                $$ \ell_{rgb}(\mathbf{c}_1', \mathbf{c}_2') = \sum \| \mathbf{c}_1' - \mathbf{c}_2' \|_2^2 $$
                $$ \ell_{normal}(\mathbf{n}_1', \mathbf{n}_2') = \sum \| \mathbf{n}_1' - \mathbf{n}_2' \|_1 + \| 1 - \mathbf{n}_1'^\top \mathbf{n}_2' \|_1 $$
                $\textit{norm}(\cdot)$ is max-min normalization [S1, unofficial].
            *   Select top-$n$ rays with highest cost as hard samples $Ray_{i,n}'$ [S1, unofficial].
            *   Construct proposal regions $R$ of size $s \times s$ centered on pixel indices of $Ray_{i,n}'$ [S1, unofficial].
            *   Randomly select consistent matching samples from $R$ for each ray in $Ray_{i,n}'$ (samples change per optimization step) [S1, unofficial].
            *   Compute $\ell_{sdc}$:
                $$ \ell_{sdc} = \lambda_1' \ell_{jsd}(\phi(\mathbf{r}_1'), \phi(\mathbf{r}_2')) + \lambda_2' \ell_{rgb} + \lambda_3' \ell_{depth} $$
                where $\mathbf{r}_1', \mathbf{r}_2'$ are MLP features of the hard sample and its neighbors in $R$, $\phi(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{e^{x_i}}{\sum_{c=1}^C e^{x_c}}$ is softmax, and $\ell_{jsd}$ is Jensen-Shannon Divergence:
                $$ \ell_{jsd}(\phi(\mathbf{r}_1'), \phi(\mathbf{r}_2')) = 0.5 \frac{\sum \phi(\mathbf{r}_1') \ln(\phi(\mathbf{r}_2')) + \sum \phi(\mathbf{r}_2') \ln(\phi(\mathbf{r}_1'))}{(\phi(\mathbf{r}_1') + \phi(\mathbf{r}_2'))/2} $$
        *   **Temporal Invariant Consistency ($\ell_{tic}$):**
            *   Extract SIFT features from all images [S1, unofficial].
            *   Identify correspondences between current frame $I_{cur}$ and next frame $I_{next}$ [S1, unofficial].
            *   Identify corresponding ray bundles $Ray_{cur}, Ray_{next}$ [S1, unofficial].
            *   Compute $\ell_{tic}$ using JSD on features of $Ray_{cur}$ and $Ray_{next}$:
                $$ \ell_{tic} = \ell_{jsd}(\phi(\mathbf{r}_{cur}'), \phi(\mathbf{r}_{next}')) $$
        *   **Total Loss:**
            $$ \ell_{total} = \ell_{rec} + \beta \ell_{sdc} + \gamma \ell_{tic} $$
            $$ \ell_{rec} = \theta_1 \ell_{rgb} + \theta_2 \ell_{depth} + \theta_3 \ell_{normal} $$
            Parameters: $\lambda_1=0.1, \lambda_2=1.0, \lambda_3=0.05$; $\theta_1=1.0, \theta_2=0.1, \theta_3=0.005, \beta=0.01, \gamma=0.01$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Point Cloud Generation:**
    *   For each pixel, obtain ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Predict depth $z$ using trained $\mathcal{F}_r$ and $\mathcal{F}_s$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generate $PC_r = \mathbf{o} + \mathbf{d} \cdot z_r$ and $PC_s = \mathbf{o} + \mathbf{d} \cdot z_s$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Integrated Modeling (Merging):**
    *   Identify intersection points $P \subset PC_r$ and $Q \subset PC_s$ from shared rays $Ray_{i_3}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Find rigid transformation $(\mathbf{R}, \mathbf{t})$ aligning $P$ to $Q$ by minimizing:
        $$ \min_{\mathbf{R}, \mathbf{t}} \sum_{i=1}^{N'} (\textit{Dis}_i(\mathbf{R}, \mathbf{t}))^2 + I_{SO}(\mathbf{R}) $$
        where $\textit{Dis}_i(\mathbf{R}, \mathbf{t}) = \min_{\mathbf{q}_j \in Q, \mathbf{p}_k \in P} \|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{p}_i + \mathbf{t} - \mathbf{q}_j\|$ and $I_{SO}(\mathbf{R})$ is an indicator function:
        $$ I_{SO}(\mathbf{R}) = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } \mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I} \text{ and } \det(\mathbf{R}) = 1 \\ +\infty, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    *   Merge point clouds: $PC = PC_r \mathbf{R} + \mathbf{t} + PC_s$ [S1, unofficial].

    def forward(self, x, d):
        # Returns color, density, normal vectors
        return np.zeros_like(x), np.zeros(x.shape[0]), np.zeros_like(x)

def softmax(x):
    e_x = np.exp(x - np.max(x, axis=-1, keepdims=True))
    return e_x / np.sum(e_x, axis=-1, keepdims=True)

def jsd(p, q):
    m = 0.5 * (p + q)
    m = np.clip(m, 1e-10, 1.0)
    p = np.clip(p, 1e-10, 1.0)
    q = np.clip(q, 1e-10, 1.0)
    # Formula from source [S1, unofficial]
    numerator = np.sum(p * np.log(q), axis=-1) + np.sum(q * np.log(p), axis=-1)
    denominator = np.sum(m, axis=-1)
    return 0.5 * numerator / denominator

def max_min_norm(x):
    x_min, x_max = np.min(x), np.max(x)
    if x_max == x_min: return np.zeros_like(x)
    return (x - x_min) / (x_max - x_min)

def compute_losses(pred, gt):
    l_rgb = np.sum((pred['c'] - gt['c']) ** 2)
    l_depth = np.sum((max_min_norm(pred['t']) - max_min_norm(gt['t'])) ** 2)
    l_normal = np.sum(np.abs(pred['n'] - gt['n'])) + np.sum(np.abs(1 - np.sum(pred['n'] * gt['n'], axis=-1)))
    return l_rgb, l_depth, l_normal

def solve_rigid_transformation(P, Q):
    # Kabsch algorithm [G]
    centroid_P = np.mean(P, axis=0)
    centroid_Q = np.mean(Q, axis=0)
    P_centered = P - centroid_P
    Q_centered = Q - centroid_Q
    H = P_centered.T @ Q_centered
    U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(H)
    R = Vt.T @ U.T
    if np.linalg.det(R) < 0:
        Vt[-1, :] *= -1
        R = Vt.T @ U.T
    t = centroid_Q - R @ centroid_P
    return R, t

    return origins + directions * depths[:, np.newaxis]

    return np.random.rand(batch_size, 3), np.random.rand(batch_size, 3)

def select_top_n(rays, costs, n):
    idx = np.argsort(costs)[-n:]
    return [rays[i] for i in idx]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Pavement Collapse:** Standard NeRF methods fail to optimize road surfaces due to weak textures, leading to poor depth estimation [S1, unofficial]. LPiM addresses this by separating road and scene modeling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Floaters:** Sparse views and viewing angle shifts cause inconsistent geometry and floaters above the road surface [S1, unofficial]. GAC addresses this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Matching Overhead:** Erroneous optimization of hard samples leads to artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Intersection Handling:** The ray gate $M(\cdot)$ uses morphological dilation to ensure an intersection zone $Ray_{i_3}$ exists for merging [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training involves 20,000 iterations with a batch size of 2048 on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU [S1, unofficial]. The complexity is dominated by the NeRFacto backbone and the additional consistency loss calculations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two radiance fields and intermediate features for consistency losses [S1, unofficial]. Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On a proprietary street-view dataset (180k images, 20km span): PSNR 29.139, SSIM 0.831, Chamfer Distance 1.876 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Designed for large-scale street-view data (20km span) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Outperforms NeRFacto, Instant-NPG, NeuS, Depth-NeRFacto, and Mip-NeRF in PSNR, SSIM, and Chamfer Distance on street-view data [S1, unofficial]. Generates clear RGBD point clouds with distinct features [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires additional supervision (depth, normal, masks) from external models (Omnidata, Mask2Former) [S1, unofficial]. Relies on SIFT for temporal consistency, which may fail in textureless regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Large-scale street-view reconstruction where geometric accuracy (point cloud) is as important as visual fidelity [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Small-scale, object-centered scenes where standard NeRF variants suffice; scenes without road/scene distinction [S1, unofficial].

## Background
NeRF2Points was introduced by Peng Tu et al. in 2024 [S1, unofficial]. It extends the NeRFacto framework within the NeRFSTUDIO ecosystem [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the limitations of existing NeRF-based methods (NeRFacto, Instant-NPG, NeuS, Depth-NeRFacto, Mip-NeRF) in generating high-quality point clouds from large-scale street-view data [S1, unofficial]. The core innovation is the Layered Perception and Integrated Modeling (LPiM) strategy, which separates road and scene modeling to prevent pavement collapse, and Geometric-Aware Consistency Regularization (GAC) to mitigate floaters and artifacts [S1, unofficial].
