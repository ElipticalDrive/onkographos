# Training Setup and Baselines


## Overview
This family defines the hyperparameter configurations, data handling strategies, and optimization protocols required to train differentiable volume rendering models. It establishes the reference conditions for standard Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and adapts training loops for specific architectures such as StyleGAN2-based generators, feature-volume-based NeRFs, and Neural VDBs. It differs from sibling articles by focusing on the static configuration of the training environment (batch sizes, sampling rates, loss functions, and optimizer settings) rather than the dynamic scheduling of learning rates or the specific regularization priors applied during optimization.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on stochastic gradient descent to minimize a photometric loss, typically Mean Squared Error (MSE), between rendered pixels and ground truth images. The core loop involves:
1.  **Ray Sampling:** Generating rays from camera poses through image pixels.
2.  **Point Sampling:** Sampling 3D coordinates along these rays.
3.  **Network Query:** Evaluating a neural network (MLP or grid-based) at these points to predict density ($\sigma$) and color ($\mathbf{c}$).
4.  **Volume Rendering:** Computing the final pixel color using the differentiable volume rendering integral [S2].
5.  **Backpropagation:** Updating network weights based on the gradient of the loss.

The specific implementation of ray marching, density-to-opacity conversion ($\alpha = 1 - \exp(-\sigma \delta)$), and transmittance accumulation ($T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$) is assumed to be handled by the underlying differentiable renderer, as detailed in [Volume Rendering](52-volume-rendering.md).

## Subtopics

### [NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup](11349-nerf-gt-baseline-training-setup.md)
Establishes the reference configuration for standard NeRF using Low Dynamic Range (LDR) inputs. It strictly adheres to the original NeRF optimization protocol to avoid encoding difficulties associated with high dynamic range data.
*   **Inputs:** LDR images with consistent exposure and camera poses.
*   **Sampling:** $N_c = 64$ coarse samples and $N_f = 128$ fine samples per ray.
*   **Batch Size:** 2,048 rays.
*   **Resolution:** 800 × 600 pixels.
*   **Optimization:** Adam optimizer for 200,000 steps.
*   **Pitfall:** Training directly on HDR views can fail to encode all scene areas well, even with tone-mapped loss.

### [StyleGAN2 Training Configuration](12972-stylegan2-training-configuration.md)
Adapts StyleGAN2 for 3D-aware image synthesis, leveraging its generator/discriminator structures for differentiable volume rendering. It distinguishes itself by using equalized learning rates, minibatch standard deviation, and R1 regularization, excluding path regularization and style-mixing.
*   **Regularization:** R1 penalty $\gamma \cdot \frac{1}{2} \|\nabla_{x_r} D(x_r)\|^2$ applied once every 4 minibatches with $\gamma = 1.0$.
*   **Precision:** Mixed-precision training.
*   **EMA:** Exponential Moving Average applied to generator weights.
*   **Fine-tuning:** For small datasets (e.g., AFHQ), fine-tune from a FFHQ-pretrained generator with input resolution $R_I = 128$ and generator resolution $R_G = 32$ before pruning.
*   **Pitfall:** R1 regularization is computationally expensive; applying it every 4 minibatches mitigates this.

### [NeRF Training Setup](13271-nerf-training-setup.md)
Defines the configuration for NeRF using the Nerfstudio library, leveraging NerfAcc and Instant-NGP for fast convergence on static scenes. It differs from standard NeRF by replacing positional encoding of 3D positions with pre-sampled 64-dimensional feature vectors from a volume $V_s$, while retaining positional encoding for viewing directions.
*   **Input:** Multi-view images, camera poses, and pre-computed feature volume $V_s$.
*   **Sampling:** 1024 rays per batch; images scaled to $504 \times 378$.
*   **Network Input:** Tuple $(\mathbf{v}_s, \gamma(\mathbf{d}))$, where $\mathbf{v}_s$ is the trilinearly sampled feature vector and $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ is the encoded direction.
*   **Encoding:** Directional positional encoding with depth $L=4$.
*   **Points:** 64 coarse and 64 fine points per ray.
*   **Hardware:** Single RTX A6000 GPU, 128 GB RAM.
*   **Pitfall:** Assumes static scenes; dynamic content (e.g., surgical tools) causes artifacts if not masked. Feature volume $V_s$ must be accurately aligned with camera poses.

### [NLR Baseline](15254-nlr-baseline.md)
Represents a scene as a collection of neural radiance fields associated with specific camera viewpoints, rather than a single global field. It solves efficient rendering by leveraging spatial coherence of light fields, learning separate color and shape networks for each input view.
*   **Architecture:** Per-view Shape Network ($\Phi_i$) and Color Network ($\Psi_i$).
*   **Sampling:** Points sampled along rays based on the shape network of the source view.
*   **Rendering:** Colors predicted by the color network of the source view, conditioned on position and direction.
*   **Advantage:** Handles occlusion implicitly through per-view shape networks.

### NeuralVDB Hyperparameter Configuration
Configures training for sparse volume representations using hierarchical neural networks [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sampling:** Either $2^{16}$ random samples per epoch, or resample a subset of input voxels at fixed intervals (100 or 500) and draw $2^{12}$ batches per epoch. Resampling is used for fine details with thin structures [S1, unofficial].
*   **Subdomain Size:** Must be a multiple of 512. Too large wastes compute on empty subdomains; too small increases dispatch overhead and causes dominant overlapping halo regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Network Width:** Level-1 networks use half the width of level-0 networks. Default: level-0 width 128, depth 3. Use 4 layers for volumes requiring more detail [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam, LR 0.001. Decay: 0.975 every 100 epochs (no resampling) or 0.75 every 1,000 epochs (with resampling). Max epochs: 2,500 for non-resampled [S1, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Sparse VDB with active voxel masks yields the best visual quality and low noise, whereas dense grid training produces the highest noise and block grid shows visible fog artifacts [S1, unofficial].

### Neural Codebook Training Configuration
Configures training for Neural Codebooks (NCB) for compact real-time radiance fields [S2].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$, weight decay $1e-5$ [S2].
*   **Learning Rate:** Initial $5e-3$, multiplied by 0.3 every 5,000 iterations [S2].
*   **Iterations:** 20,000 total iterations [S2].
*   **Batch Size:** 100,000 voxels [S2].
*   **Post-Training:** Cast NCB to float16 after training [S2].

## Background
The NeRF-GT baseline follows the original NeRF protocol [S2]. The StyleGAN2 configuration adapts GAN training principles to volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. The NeRF Training Setup using Nerfstudio and Instant-NGP represents a modern optimization for static scenes [S2]. The NLR baseline originates from light-field-based novel view synthesis . NeuralVDB and Neural Codebook configurations address sparse and compact volume representations, respectively [S1, unofficial][S2].
