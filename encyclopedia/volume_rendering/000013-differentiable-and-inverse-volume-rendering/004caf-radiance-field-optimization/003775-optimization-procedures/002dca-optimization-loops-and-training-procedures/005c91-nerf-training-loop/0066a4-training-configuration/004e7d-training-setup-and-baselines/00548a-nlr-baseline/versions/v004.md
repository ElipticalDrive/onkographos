# NLR Baseline

Also called: Neural Lumigraph Representation, NLR

## Overview
NLR is a neural implicit representation method for novel view synthesis that models the light field using a neural network to predict density and color along rays [S1, unofficial]. It serves as a baseline for comparing training efficiency and rendering speed against other neural rendering approaches like NeRF and IDR [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that optimize geometry explicitly, NLR learns a continuous volumetric representation directly from images, optimized via gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A set of calibrated images (views) with known camera poses.
- **Outputs:** A neural network mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density ($\sigma$) and color ($c$).

**Data Structures:**
- **Neural Network:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) taking 3D position and viewing direction as input. The specific architecture details (layer sizes, activation functions) are not explicitly defined in the provided notes, but it is referenced as the "NLR implementation" from the original authors [S1, unofficial].
- **Ray Sampling:** Rays are sampled through the volume. The specific sampling strategy (e.g., uniform, stratified) is not detailed in the notes, but standard volume rendering integration is implied [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Ray Casting:** For each pixel in a training image, cast a ray through the scene.
2. **Sampling:** Sample points along the ray.
3. **Network Forward Pass:** Query the NLR network at each sample point to get density $\sigma$ and color $c$.
4. **Volume Rendering:** Compute the final color $C(r)$ for the ray $r$ using standard differentiable volume rendering integration:
   $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
   where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [G].
5. **Loss Computation:** Compute the photometric loss (e.g., Mean Squared Error) between the rendered image and the ground-truth image.
6. **Optimization:** Update network parameters using gradient-based optimization (typically Adam) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- **Training Time:** 2.21 seconds per iteration [S1, unofficial].
- **Rendering Time:** 0.025 seconds per frame [S1, unofficial].
- **Resolution:** Training and testing views are at 800x600 resolution [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Convergence:** NLR requires more iterations to converge compared to some other methods, leading to longer total training times despite reasonable per-iteration costs [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Standard floating-point precision is assumed for ray integration and network queries [G].

## Subtopics

### MetaNLR Baseline
MetaNLR is a variant of NLR that wraps the NLR implementation with an outer meta-learning loop using Reptile [S1, unofficial]. It learns the initialization of the shape and color networks to enable fast adaptation to new scenes [S1, unofficial]. Meta-training and meta-testing are performed on the same scenes, with specialization to test scenes using the same training and testing views at 800x600 resolution [S1, unofficial]. The meta-learning rate is set to $5 \times 10^{-2}$ [S1, unofficial]. It is noted that MetaNLR++ (an extension) takes 2.19 sec/iteration without shape optimization and 7.69 sec/iteration with shape optimization, requiring fewer iterations to converge than standard NLR due to optimizing over entire images rather than random rays [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** 2.21 seconds per iteration [S1, unofficial]. This is significantly slower than NeRF (0.40 sec/iteration) and IDR (0.2 sec/iteration) [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Rendering time is 0.025 seconds per frame [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** MetaNLR++ iterations are longer than NLR because they optimize the representation for multiple entire images instead of randomly selected rays, resulting in a significantly larger ray-batch size [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Fast rendering time once trained [S1, unofficial]. MetaNLR allows for fast specialization to new scenes [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Slow per-iteration training time compared to other neural rendering methods [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When fast rendering is prioritized over training speed, or when meta-learning capabilities are required for few-shot adaptation [S1, unofficial].
- **When not:** When training time is a critical constraint and methods like IDR or NeRF are acceptable [S1, unofficial].

## Background
NLR (Neural Lumigraph Representation) was introduced by [S1, unofficial] as a method for novel view synthesis. The baseline implementation was provided by the original authors [S1, unofficial]. It is used as a reference point in comparative studies of neural rendering methods, particularly in the context of meta-learning for fast adaptation [S1, unofficial].
