# Signed Distance Function (SDF) Representations

Also called: SDF-based Neural Fields, Truncated SDF NeRF, Signed Ray Distance Function (SRDF)

## Overview
Signed Distance Function (SDF) Representations replace the implicit density field of standard NeRFs with a continuous scalar field representing the distance to the closest surface, providing a more explicit and smooth formulation of geometry [S1, 12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. This approach addresses the geometric inaccuracy and surface holes typical of density-based NeRFs by leveraging the zero-level set of the SDF to define geometry [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [ProbeSDF](11363-probesdf.md) or [SDF-based Neural Volume Rendering with PBR](15009-sdf-based-neural-volume-rendering-with-pbr.md) by focusing primarily on the geometric representation, whereas those siblings integrate specific appearance models (light field probes or PBR) or architectural modifications (SRDF) on top of the SDF foundation.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves predicting a continuous scalar field $s: \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ instead of a density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. For any 3D point $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the function value $s(\mathbf{x})$ represents the distance to the closest surface, with the sign conventionally negative inside the object and positive outside [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. The surface is defined as the zero-level set $\{\mathbf{x} \mid s(\mathbf{x}) = 0\}$ [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md].

To enable volumetric rendering, the predicted SDF value $s(\mathbf{x})$ is converted into a density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. The specific conversion function is not detailed in the provided sources for all variants [G]. Standard differentiable rendering pipelines then integrate this density along rays to produce images, while the SDF structure encourages sharper, more coherent surface reconstructions compared to soft volumetric boundaries [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md].

The surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ is derived from the SDF gradient $\nabla s(\mathbf{x})$ [15009-sdf-based-neural-volume-rendering-with-pbr.md]:
$$ \mathbf{n} = \frac{\nabla s(\mathbf{x})}{\|\nabla s(\mathbf{x})\|} $$
[11363-probesdf.md]

## Subtopics

### [ProbeSDF](11363-probesdf.md)
ProbeSDF is a neural surface reconstruction method that decouples spatial and angular light field components using two volumetric grids of different resolutions to achieve real-time rendering and accelerated training [11363-probesdf.md]. It addresses the computational bottleneck of standard SDF-based neural fields by replacing the core appearance MLP with a lightweight model that uses spherical harmonic light field probes for angular variation and planar factorized features for spatial texture [11363-probesdf.md]. Unlike Plenoxels or 3D Gaussian Splatting which require significantly more parameters per voxel (27 and 48 respectively), ProbeSDF uses as few as four parameters per voxel while maintaining high expressiveness [11363-probesdf.md]. Choose this method when real-time rendering and high expressiveness with low parameter count are required.

### [SDF-based Neural Fields](12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md)
SDF-based Neural Fields replace the implicit density field of standard NeRFs with a Signed Distance Function (SDF) to provide a more explicit and smooth formulation of surfaces [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. This approach addresses the geometric inaccuracy and surface holes typical of density-based NeRFs by leveraging the zero-level set of the SDF to define geometry [12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md]. Choose this method when the primary goal is improving geometric accuracy and surface coherence over standard density-based NeRFs.

### [Generalizable Implicit Reconstruction with SRDF](14760-generalizable-implicit-reconstruction-with-srdf.md)
VolRecon is a generalizable implicit reconstruction method that predicts a Signed Ray Distance Function (SRDF) to reconstruct scenes with fine details and minimal noise from multi-view images [14760-generalizable-implicit-reconstruction-with-srdf.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [SDF-based Neural Fields](12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md) by combining local projection features aggregated via a view transformer with global shape priors from a coarse feature volume, processed by a ray transformer to resolve surface ambiguity [14760-generalizable-implicit-reconstruction-with-srdf.md]. Unlike standard SDF approaches that define distance in any direction, SRDF constrains the distance measurement to the specific ray direction, enabling more robust surface localization along the viewing ray [14760-generalizable-implicit-reconstruction-with-srdf.md]. Choose this method for generalizable reconstruction from multi-view images where ray-specific surface ambiguity is a concern.

### [SDF-based Neural Volume Rendering with PBR](15009-sdf-based-neural-volume-rendering-with-pbr.md)
This method integrates Signed Distance Function (SDF) representations for geometry with Physically Based Rendering (PBR) material models to achieve smoother geometric structures and accurate specular reflections, overcoming the coarse detail and foggy artifacts typical of standard NeRF [15009-sdf-based-neural-volume-rendering-with-pbr.md]. Unlike sibling methods such as [ProbeSDF](11363-probesdf.md) or [SDF-based Neural Fields](12947-sdf-based-neural-fields.md) which may focus primarily on geometry or general neural fields, this approach specifically decouples geometry (via SDF) from material appearance (via PBR), providing interpretable parameters for reflection and diffuse components [15009-sdf-based-neural-volume-rendering-with-pbr.md]. It builds on [Signed Distance Function (SDF) Representations](12495-signed-distance-function-sdf-representations.md) by converting the SDF into a density field for volumetric integration while using the SDF gradient for surface normals required by the PBR model. Choose this method when physically accurate specular reflections and interpretable material parameters are required.

### [RS-Recon](15321-rs-recon.md)
RS-Recon is a neural scene reconstruction method that replaces the standard Signed Distance Function (SDF) with a Signed Ray Distance Function (SRDF) to model volume density, specifically addressing noise and inaccuracies in multi-object indoor scenes [15321-rs-recon.md]. Unlike SDF-based methods which consider surface points across the entire scene and may exhibit fluctuations leading to local minimum values, SRDF measures the shortest distance to the surface along a specific camera ray, eliminating the influence of surrounding surfaces not located on that ray [15321-rs-recon.md]. Choose this method for multi-object indoor scenes where standard SDFs suffer from noise due to surrounding surfaces.

### Truncated SDF NeRF
A method that replaces the density MLP with a truncated SDF MLP and computes pixel color as a weighted sum of sampled colors [S1]. Reconstruct the triangular mesh of the scene by using Marching Cubes on the truncated SDF MLP [S1]. The pixel color is computed as:
$$ C(r) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i c_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i} $$
where $c_i$ is the sampled color and $w_i$ is the weight [S1]. The weight is given by:
$$ w_i = \Phi\left(\frac{D_i}{t_r}\right) \cdot \Phi\left(-\frac{D_i}{t_r}\right) $$
where $D_i$ is the SDF value, $t_r$ is the truncation distance, and $\Phi$ is the sigmoid function [S1]. The truncation distance cuts off any SDF value too far from individual surfaces [S1]. Per-frame appearance latent code from NeRF-W is used to account for white-balance and exposure changes [S1]. Multiple ray-surface intersections are handled by assigning a weight of zero to subsequent truncation regions so they do not contribute to the pixel color [S1].

## Background
The concept of replacing density MLPs with SDFs emerged concurrently with early NeRF developments. Azinovic et al. introduced a truncated SDF MLP approach in April 2021, computing pixel color as a weighted sum of sampled colors and using Marching Cubes for mesh reconstruction [S1]. Park et al. introduced Nerfies in November 2020, which used a deformation field for non-rigid transformations, laying groundwork for dynamic SDF-based methods [S1]. SparseNeuS, published in June 2022, improved on NeuS by focusing on sparse-view SDF reconstruction using a geometry encoding volume with learnable image features as a hybrid representation method [S1]. These methods collectively shifted the focus from soft volumetric boundaries to explicit surface definitions via zero-level sets.
