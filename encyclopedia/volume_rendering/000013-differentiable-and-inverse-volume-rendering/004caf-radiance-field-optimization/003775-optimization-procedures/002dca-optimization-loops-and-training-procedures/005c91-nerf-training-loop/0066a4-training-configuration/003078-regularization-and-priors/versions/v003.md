# Regularization and Priors


## Overview
Regularization and Priors in NeRF training apply constraints to the optimization process to enforce physical plausibility, structural stability, or uncertainty awareness, addressing issues such as scale ambiguity, overfitting to noise, and instability in dynamically growing networks. This family is distinct from standard photometric reconstruction by modifying the loss landscape to guide the neural field toward specific semantic or geometric properties (e.g., material reflectance, shading smoothness) rather than merely minimizing pixel-wise error.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves augmenting the primary photometric loss $\mathcal{L}$ with additional regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$, resulting in a total training loss:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{train} = \mathcal{L} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg}^k
$$
where $\lambda_k$ are hyperparameters balancing the influence of each prior. These priors typically operate on intermediate network outputs (e.g., reflectance, shading, uncertainty maps) rather than just the final rendered color, enforcing constraints on the internal representation of the scene. Common goals include enforcing spatial smoothness, maintaining physical scale consistency, or guiding the exploration of the hypothesis space during early training stages.

## Subtopics

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Parameters](2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters.md)
This method addresses the instability of training deep, adaptive networks by jointly optimizing coarse and fine representations in a dynamically growing framework [2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters]. It couples loss coefficients with a multi-stage optimization loop where the number of active stages $D$ increases dynamically. It is distinct from static loss weighting by ensuring that uncertainty estimates guide the structural growth of the model while maintaining reconstruction fidelity. Choose this when using recursive or dynamically growing NeRF architectures that require stability across multiple resolution scales. See [Multi-scale Joint Training Parameters](2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters.md).

### [Reflectance and Shading Decomposition Priors](4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors.md)
This method enforces physical plausibility by decoupling rendered color into material reflectance and shading components, solving the problem of unstable or physically inconsistent material properties in novel view synthesis [4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors]. It distinguishes itself from standard photometric priors by explicitly regularizing intermediate decomposition components, thereby mitigating scale ambiguity and ensuring consistent brightness levels. Choose this when inserting objects into scenes or handling complex lighting where intrinsic material properties must be preserved. See [Reflectance and Shading Decomposition Priors](4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors.md).

### Weight Annealing
A technique to anneal proposal weights by raising them to a power that increases from 0 to 1 over training steps, encouraging exploration by presenting a wider range of proposal intervals early in training [S1]. The annealed weights $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_n$ at step $n$ (out of $N$ total steps) are computed as:
$$
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_n \propto \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\frac{bn/N}{(b-1)n/N+1}}
$$
where $b$ is the bias hyperparameter [S1]. The exponent follows Schlick's bias function, starting at 0 (flat distribution) and saturating at 1 (proposal distribution) [S1]. The bias hyperparameter is set to $b = 10$ in experiments [S1]. Ablating this annealing causes SSIM on the bicycle scene to decrease from 0.687 to 0.679 [S1].

### Laplace Approximation for Neural Networks
This method approximates the posterior distribution of network parameters as a multivariate Gaussian centered at the optimal weights $\omega^*$, using the inverse Hessian of the negative log-posterior as the covariance matrix [S2]. The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is derived as $\Sigma = -H(\omega^*)^{-1}$ [S2]. However, directly applying Laplace approximation to NeRF parameters is impracticable due to high correlation between parameters, making accurate covariance estimation challenging, and the expensive sampling overhead required for geometrically meaningful distributions [S2].

## Background
Regularization techniques in neural radiance fields have evolved from simple $L_2$ penalties on weights to complex, task-specific priors. Weight annealing was introduced in mip-NeRF 360 to improve the robustness of proposal networks during training [S1]. Reflectance and shading decomposition priors were developed to handle intrinsic image decomposition within the NeRF framework, addressing scale ambiguity between albedo and illumination [4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors]. Laplace approximation for uncertainty estimation in NeRFs has been explored in contexts such as underwater 3D reconstruction, though practical limitations regarding parameter correlation and computational cost remain significant [S2].
