# WaH-NeRF

Also called: Where and How NeRF, Where and How: Mitigating Confusion in Neural Radiance Fields from Sparse Inputs

## Overview
WaH-NeRF is a training framework for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) from sparse inputs that mitigates rendering confusion by addressing two root causes: sample-position confusion ("WHERE to Sample?") and prediction confusion ("HOW to Predict?") [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Simple-NeRF](7371-simple-nerf.md) and [Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS](11859-deceptive-nerf-3dgs.md) by analyzing the sample-render process and mitigating confusion before volume rendering, rather than relying on regularization for rendered color and depth images after volume rendering [S1]. Unlike [FlipNeRF](12967-flipnerf.md) which uses geometric consistency via flipped rays, WaH-NeRF uses a Deformable Sampling strategy for seen viewpoints and a Semi-Supervised NeRF paradigm based on pose perturbation for unseen viewpoints [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ seen viewpoints with known camera poses and images (typically $N=3$), and a set of unseen viewpoints with known poses but no ground truth images [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP parameters $\theta$ for the radiance field $F_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Radiance Field Model:** Based on mip-NeRF [S1]. The model consists of MLPs $f_\theta$ and $f_\psi$ mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $\mathbf{d} \in S^2$ [S1].
    *   $\sigma \in [0, \infty)$, $\mathbf{c} \in [0, 1]^3$ [S1].
    *   Positional encoding $\psi(\mathbf{x})$ and $\kappa(\mathbf{d})$ are used [S1].
*   **Sampling:** Conical frustum sampling is used to enhance receptive field [S1].
    *   $M$ sampling points per ray/cone [S1].
    *   $\delta_i$ is the distance between the $i$-th point and its adjacent point [S1].
*   **Offset MLP:** An additional MLP layer predicts an offset $O_{offset}$ for each conical frustum [S1].
    *   Input: Intermediate features $F_\psi(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ from $f_\psi$ [S1].
    *   Output: $O_{offset} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 1}$ [S1].
    *   Parameters: Weights $w_0$ and bias $b_0$ [S1].
*   **Patch:** Training uses a patch-based strategy [S1].
    *   Patch size $PS \times PS$ (typically $8 \times 8$) [S1].
    *   Batch size 16, resulting in 1024 input rays ($|R| = 1024$) [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Baseline Setup:** Initialize the mip-NeRF model. mip-NeRF shares training parameters in coarse-sampling and fine-resampling stages [S1].
2.  **Seen Viewpoints Processing (Deformable Sampling):**
    a. For each ray/cone $r$ in the seen viewpoints, sample points using conical frustums [S1].
    b. Compute intermediate features $F_\psi(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
    c. Predict offset $O_{offset} = w_0 \times F_\psi(\mathbf{x}) + b_0$ [S1].
    d. Deform the original sampling positions by shifting them along the cone by $O_{offset}$ [S1].
    e. Render the image $\hat{C}(r)$ using the deformed positions [S1].
3.  **Unseen Viewpoints Processing (Semi-Supervised Paradigm):**
    a. For each unseen viewpoint, perturb the camera pose (radius, azimuth, polar angle) to generate perturbed rays [S1].
    b. Render the unseen ray $\hat{C}_{unseen}(r)$ and the perturbed rays $\hat{C}_{perturbation}(r)$ [S1].
    c. Compute the Pixel-Patch Correspondence Loss (PPC) between the unseen ray and the patch of perturbed rays [S1].
4.  **Loss Calculation:**
    a. Compute MSE Loss between rendered seen images and ground truth [S1].
    b. Compute Weight-based Mutual Information Loss ($Loss_{MI}$) for the offset [S1].
    c. Compute Offset Loss ($Loss_{Offset}$) combining $Loss_{MI}$ and Distortion Loss [S1].
    d. Compute Pixel-Patch Correspondence Loss ($Loss_{PPC}$) for unseen viewpoints [S1].
    e. Combine all losses: $Loss = Loss_{MSE} + \mu Loss_{Offset} + \nu Loss_{PPC} + Loss_{smooth}$ [S1].
5.  **Optimization:** Iteratively optimize parameters $\theta$ using Adam optimizer [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Volume Rendering:**
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i $$
    where $M$ is sampling points, $\delta_i$ is distance between $i$-th and adjacent point, $r$ is ray/cone [S1].
*   **Deformable Sampling Offset:**
    $$ O_{offset} = w_0 \times F_\psi(\mathbf{x}) + b_0 $$
    where $F_\psi(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ are intermediate features, $d$ is feature dimension, $w_0$ and $b_0$ are MLP weights [S1].
*   **Weight (Surface Probability):**
    $$ w_i = \left( \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) $$
    where $w_i$ approximates the probability of the surface [S1].
*   **Weight-based Mutual Information Loss:**
    $$ Loss_{MI} = - \sum_{r \in R} I\left(\frac{1}{w+\epsilon}; O_{offset} \mid mask\right) $$
    where $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-4}$, $mask$ removes positions where $w$ is close to zero, $I$ is mutual information, $H$ is entropy [S1].
    $$ I\left(\frac{1}{w+\epsilon}; O_{offset}\right) = H(O_{offset}) - H\left(O_{offset} \mid \frac{1}{w+\epsilon}\right) $$
    [S1]
*   **Offset Loss:**
    $$ Loss_{Offset} = \lambda Loss_{MI} + Loss_{dist} $$
    where $\lambda = 5 \times 10^{-3}$, $Loss_{dist}$ is distortion loss [S1].
*   **Pixel-Patch Correspondence Loss (PPC):**
    $$ Loss_{PPC} = \sum_{p=0}^{P} \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{i=0}^{PS \times PS} \frac{1}{PS \times PS} \left( \hat{C}_{unseen}(r) - \hat{C}_{perturbation(i)}(r) \right)^2 $$
    where $PS$ is patch size, $\hat{C}_{perturbation(i)}(r)$ is the $i$-th pixel in the perturbed patch [S1].
    Note: $Loss_{PPC}$ includes both RGB and depth losses [S1].
*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ Loss = Loss_{MSE} + \mu Loss_{Offset} + \nu Loss_{PPC} + Loss_{smooth} $$
    where $\mu = 0.5$, $\nu = 0.5$, $Loss_{smooth}$ improves depth smoothness [S1].
    The coefficient of the coarse-sampling loss is set to 0.1 [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   $\lambda = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   $w_0, b_0$ initialized to $10^{-7}$ [S1].
*   $PS = 8$ (patch size) [S1].
*   Batch size = 16, $|R| = 1024$ [S1].
*   Learning rate: Exponential decay from $10^{-3}$ to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ by a factor of 10 every 2500 iterations [S1].
*   $\mu = 0.5$, $\nu = 0.5$ [S1].
*   Coarse-sampling loss coefficient = 0.1 [S1].
*   Training iterations: 20,000 for Realistic Synthetic 360° [S1].
*   Model saving: Every 5,000 iterations [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Training $w_0$ and $b_0$ from scratch is sub-optimal because low-weight sample conical frustums struggle to offset towards the surface; addressed by Weight-based Mutual Information Loss [S1].
*   Vanilla MSE loss struggles with pixel-wise correspondence due to mismatches from minor camera pose perturbations; addressed by Pixel-Patch Correspondence Loss [S1].
*   The Deformable Sampling strategy is not applied to unseen perspectives to ensure training stability [S1].
*   The method assumes the baseline is mip-NeRF, which shares parameters in two stages, unlike vanilla NeRF which uses non-sharing parameters [S1].

from torch.utils.data import DataLoader

        self.num_views = num_views

        self.b0 = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1) * 1e-7)

        self.lambda_mi = 5e-3
        self.mu = 0.5
        self.nu = 0.5
        self.coarse_coeff = 0.1
        
        self.optimizer = optim.Adam(list(self.model.parameters()) + [self.w0, self.b0], lr=1e-3)
        self.lr_scheduler = optim.lr_scheduler.ExponentialLR(self.optimizer, gamma=0.1) # Decay factor 10 every 2500 iters handled manually or via custom scheduler

    def compute_offset(self, x_features):

        Returns: O_offset, shape (M, 1)
        """
        return self.w0 * x_features + self.b0

    def deform_sample(self, original_points, offset):

        Deform sampling positions along the ray/cone by offset.
        original_points: shape (M, 3) or (M, 1) depending on representation
        offset: shape (M, 1)
        Returns: deformed_points

        Compute weight w_i approximating surface probability.

        delta: shape (M,)
        Returns: w, shape (M,)
        """
        # w_i = (sum_{j=1}^{i-1} sigma_j delta_j) * (1 - exp(-sigma_i delta_i))
        # Cumulative sum of sigma * delta
        cumulative = torch.cumsum(sigma * delta, dim=0)

        transmittance_term = 1 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)

    def mutual_information_loss(self, offset, w):

        Compute Weight-based Mutual Information Loss.
        offset: shape (M,) or (batch, M)
        w: shape (M,) or (batch, M)
        Returns: scalar loss
        """

        mask = w > self.epsilon

        return loss_mi

    def pixel_patch_correspondence_loss(self, unseen_render, perturbed_patches):
        """
        Compute Pixel-Patch Correspondence Loss.
        unseen_render: shape (batch, H, W, 3) or (batch, M, 3)

        """
        rays: seen rays, shape (batch, M, 3)
        gt_images: ground truth images, shape (batch, H, W, 3)

        loss_mse = torch.mean((rendered_seen - gt_images) ** 2)

        loss_dist = self.compute_distortion_loss(sigma, deformed_rays) # Placeholder for distortion loss
        loss_offset = self.lambda_mi * loss_mi + loss_dist
        
        # Unseen Viewpoints
        # 1. Perturb camera pose
        perturbed_rays = self.perturb_pose(unseen_rays) # Shape (batch, P, M, 3)
        
        # 2. Render unseen and perturbed
        sigma_unseen, color_unseen = self.model(unseen_rays)
        rendered_unseen = self.render_volume(sigma_unseen, color_unseen, unseen_rays)
        
        perturbed_colors = []
        for i in range(len(perturbed_rays)):
            sigma_p, color_p = self.model(perturbed_rays[i])
            rendered_p = self.render_volume(sigma_p, color_p, perturbed_rays[i])
            perturbed_colors.append(rendered_p)
        
        # 3. Compute PPC Loss
        loss_ppc = self.pixel_patch_correspondence_loss(rendered_unseen, perturbed_colors)
        
        # 4. Total Loss
        loss = loss_mse + self.mu * loss_offset + self.nu * loss_ppc
        
        # Add smoothness loss if depth is available
        if unseen_gt_depths is not None:
            depth_unseen = self.compute_depth(sigma_unseen, unseen_rays)
            loss_smooth = torch.mean((depth_unseen - unseen_gt_depths) ** 2)
            loss += loss_smooth
            
        return loss, rendered_seen

    def render_volume(self, sigma, color, rays):
        """
        Render volume using quadrature.
        sigma: shape (batch, M, 1)
        color: shape (batch, M, 3)
        rays: object containing delta
        Returns: rendered image, shape (batch, H, W, 3)
        """
        delta = rays.delta # Shape (batch, M, 1)
        # C_hat(r) = sum_{i=1}^{M} (sum_{j=1}^{i-1} sigma_j delta_j) * (1 - exp(-sigma_i delta_i)) * c_i
        cumulative = torch.cumsum(sigma * delta, dim=1)
        cumulative_shifted = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(cumulative[:, :1, :]), cumulative[:, :-1, :]], dim=1)
        transmittance_term = 1 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)
        alpha = transmittance_term
        weight = cumulative_shifted * alpha
        rendered = torch.sum(weight * color, dim=1)
        return rendered

    def perturb_pose(self, rays):
        """
        Perturb camera pose for unseen rays.
        Returns: list of perturbed rays
        """

            # Add small random perturbation to ray origins and directions
            perturbed = rays + torch.randn_like(rays) * 0.01

        return perturbed_rays

        Compute distortion loss.
        Returns: scalar loss
        """
        # Placeholder for distortion loss calculation
        return torch.tensor(0.0)

    def compute_depth(self, sigma, rays):
        """
        Compute depth from sigma and rays.
        Returns: depth map
        """
        # Placeholder for depth calculation
        return torch.zeros_like(sigma[:, 0, 0])

    def train_step(self, batch):
        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss, _ = self.forward(batch['rays'], batch['gt_images'], batch['unseen_rays'], batch.get('unseen_gt_depths'))
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()
        return loss.item()

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   On Realistic Synthetic 360° dataset with 3 inputs: mean PSNR 17.90, SSIM 0.776, LPIPS 0.230 [S1].
    *   On LLFF dataset with 3 inputs: mean PSNR 19.23, SSIM 0.457, LPIPS 0.312 [S1].
    *   Surpasses RegNeRF by 0.87 PSNR, 0.046 SSIM, and 0.048 LPIPS on Realistic Synthetic 360° [S1].
    *   Surpasses baseline (mip-NeRF with patch-based training) by 1.18 PSNR, 0.065 SSIM, and 0.060 LPIPS [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths and Limitations:**
    *   **Strengths:** Mitigates confusion before volume rendering, leading to better performance than post-rendering regularization methods like RegNeRF [S1]. Uses pose perturbation instead of time-consuming image warping [S1].
    *   **Limitations:** Relies on the baseline mip-NeRF architecture [S1]. The Deformable Sampling strategy is not applied to unseen perspectives, which may limit its effectiveness in those regions [S1].
*   **When to use it:** When optimizing NeRF from sparse inputs (e.g., 3 views) and seeking to mitigate sample-position and prediction confusion [S1].
*   **When not to use it:** When dense inputs are available, as the method is designed for sparse settings [S1]. When computational resources are limited, as it adds complexity with offset MLP and additional loss terms [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** WaH-NeRF was proposed by Yanqi Bao, Yuxin Li, Jing Huo, Tianyu Ding, Xinyue Liang, Wenbin Li, and Yang Gao in 2023 [S1].
*   **History and Development:** The method addresses the "CONFUSION" phenomenon in NeRF-S (NeRF from Sparse inputs), where performance degrades due to lack of geometric constraints [S1]. It builds on mip-NeRF, which uses conical frustum sampling and integrated positional encoding to enhance receptive field [S1].
*   **Where it is used:** Evaluated on Realistic Synthetic 360° and LLFF datasets [S1]. Implemented on top of Pytorch mip-NeRF [S1].
