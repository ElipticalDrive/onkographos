# CLIPNeRF Training Configuration

Also called: Absolute Directional CLIP Loss, CLIPNeRF Hyperparameters, CLIPNeRF Implementation, Directional CLIP Loss, Relative Directional CLIP Loss, StyleGAN-NADA CLIP Loss

## Overview
CLIPNeRF training configuration defines the optimization procedure for text-guided stylization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) by minimizing the negative cosine similarity between rendered images and text prompts in the CLIP embedding space [S1, S2]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF reconstruction by freezing density-related parameters and utilizing a directional loss to enforce stylistic trajectories, often employing patch-based rendering to mitigate memory constraints [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A pre-trained NeRF model (specifically the color MLP parameters), a source image $\mathbf{I}_{src}$ (optional, for relative loss), a target text prompt $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$, and a source text prompt $\mathbf{t}_{src}$ (optional, for relative loss).
*   **Outputs:** Stylized NeRF model parameters.

**Data Structures:**
*   **CLIP Encoder:** Uses CLIP ViT-B/32 [S1].
*   **Patch Data:** A $128 \times 128$ pixel patch is rendered from a randomly sampled view [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Iterations:** 500 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with learning rate $0.0001$ [S1].
*   **Ray Sampling:** Stride of 5 within the $128 \times 128$ patch, resulting in a $640 \times 640$ receptive field on the original image scale [S1].
*   **Sampling Points:** 16 importance sampling points per ray during patch rendering [S1].
*   **MLP Samples:** The fine MLP uses a total of 80 samples (64 coarse + 16 fine) [S1].
*   **Freezing:** Sub-parameters related to density ($\sigma$) are frozen; only color parameters are optimized [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
Two variants of the directional CLIP loss are defined, measuring cosine similarity $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ between normalized embeddings $\hat{\mathcal{E}}_i$ (image) and $\hat{\mathcal{E}}_t$ (text) [S2]:

1.  **Absolute Directional CLIP Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{dir} = 1 - \langle \hat{\mathcal{E}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{tgt}), \hat{\mathcal{E}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt}) \rangle $$
    This guides the rendering $\mathbf{I}_{tgt}$ toward the global direction of the target text $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$ without a reference starting point [S2].

2.  **Relative Directional CLIP Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{dir} = 1 - \langle \hat{\mathcal{E}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathcal{E}}_i(\mathbf{I}_{src}), \hat{\mathcal{E}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{tgt}) - \hat{\mathcal{E}}_t(\mathbf{t}_{src}) \rangle $$
    This transfers the style from $\mathbf{I}_{src}$ to $\mathbf{I}_{tgt}$ guided by the trajectory between text embeddings $\mathbf{t}_{src}$ and $\mathbf{t}_{tgt}$ [S2].

**Algorithm:**
1.  Initialize NeRF model with pre-trained weights.
2.  Freeze density MLP weights.
3.  For iteration $i$ from 1 to 500:
    a. Sample a random camera pose from the training dataset.
    b. Render a $128 \times 128$ patch $\mathbf{I}_{patch}$ using ray stride 5 and 16 importance samples per ray.
    c. Compute CLIP embeddings for $\mathbf{I}_{patch}$ and text prompt(s).
    d. Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{dir}$ (Absolute or Relative).
    e. Backpropagate $\mathcal{L}_{dir}$ to update color MLP parameters using Adam ($lr=0.0001$).
4.  Return updated NeRF model.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Memory Constraints:** Naive differentiable rendering of high-resolution images is intractable due to memory limits; patch-based rendering is required [S1].
*   **Mode Collapse:** Absolute directional loss can cause mode collapse of the generator and reduce generation diversity [S2].
*   **Insufficient Magnitude:** Directional loss alone struggles to reach sufficient stylization magnitude, resulting in weak style transfer, as it ignores the distance between embedded vectors [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Limited to 500 iterations with patch-based rendering, making it significantly faster than full-resolution iterative optimization [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced by rendering $128 \times 128$ patches instead of full images and using fewer sampling points (16) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables text-driven stylization of existing NeRFs; computationally efficient via patch rendering [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** May produce insufficient stylization magnitude; absolute loss risks mode collapse; requires careful tuning of patch size and sampling [S1, S2].
*   **When to use:** When text-guided stylization of a pre-existing NeRF is required and computational resources are limited.
*   **When not to use:** When high-fidelity magnitude control is critical or when full-resolution consistency is required without post-processing.

## Background
CLIPNeRF was introduced by Wang et al. in 2021, extending the StyleCLIP and StyleGAN-NADA approaches to 3D neural radiance fields [S2]. It was designed to enable text-driven editing of 3D scenes by leveraging the semantic alignment capabilities of the CLIP model [S1, S2]. The method addresses the challenge of applying 2D generative editing techniques to 3D representations by using directional losses in the CLIP embedding space [S2].
