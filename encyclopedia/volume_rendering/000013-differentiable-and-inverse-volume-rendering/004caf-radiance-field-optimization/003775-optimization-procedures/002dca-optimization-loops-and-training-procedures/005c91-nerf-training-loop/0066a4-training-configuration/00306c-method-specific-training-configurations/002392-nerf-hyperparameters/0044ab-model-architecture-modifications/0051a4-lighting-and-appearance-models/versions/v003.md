# Lighting and Appearance Models

Also called: Appearance Conditioning, Lighting-Aware NeRF, Transient Modeling

## Overview
Lighting and Appearance Models modify the standard NeRF architecture to handle per-image photometric variations (such as changing illumination or auto-exposure) and transient objects (such as moving people or vehicles) that violate the static, consistently lit scene assumption [S1]. They set themselves apart from standard NeRF by decoupling static geometry from image-dependent appearance or transience, and from analytical lighting models like [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md) by learning these variations implicitly through embeddings or neural networks rather than explicit basis functions [S1].

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture  serves as the baseline for these modifications. It consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a coarse network and a fine network. The coarse network takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs a latent feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and volume density $\sigma$. The fine network takes $\mathbf{h}$, positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and positional encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$.

$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
$$

Modifications generally fall into three categories:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation.
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs).

## Subtopics

### [NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W)](11995-nerf-in-the-wild-nerf-w.md)
NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W) addresses the failure of standard NeRF models when applied to real-world image collections containing per-image appearance variations (due to lighting changes) and transient objects (such as moving people or vehicles). It sets itself apart from standard NeRF by introducing per-image appearance embeddings to condition the color prediction and a separate transient network to model occluders that do not belong to the static scene geometry. Unlike [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md), which models lighting analytically, NeRF-W learns appearance variations implicitly through embeddings.

### [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md)
Neural-PIL is a neural network module that replaces the costly numerical integration of incoming light with a simple network query, predicting pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$ directly from an illumination embedding, direction, and roughness. Unlike spherical Gaussian (SG) or spherical harmonic (SH) light representations which suffer from lack of fine details or high memory costs, Neural-PIL is an MLP that directly produces pre-integrated light, representing finer details in illumination.

### [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md)
Spherical Harmonics NeRF replaces the view-dependent color Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) of the standard NeRF architecture with an explicit 2nd-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) illumination model. This modification allows for explicit relighting by varying learnable SH coefficients, addressing the lack of semantic meaning and direct lighting control in the standard formulation. It differs from siblings like [NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W)](11995-nerf-in-the-wild-nerf-w.md) by explicitly modeling illumination rather than relying on appearance embeddings to handle varying lighting conditions, and from [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md) by using a global SH basis rather than pre-integrated local lighting.

### [NeRF-W](14630-nerf-w.md)
NeRF-W extends the standard NeRF framework to handle unconstrained, "in-the-wild" photo collections by relaxing the assumption of a static, consistently lit scene. It disentangles the scene into shared static geometry and image-dependent transient elements (such as pedestrians) and photometric variations (such as lighting and auto-exposure) using learned appearance embeddings and a secondary transient radiance field with uncertainty modeling. Unlike NeRF-A, which only models appearance, or NeRF-U, which only models uncertainty, NeRF-W combines both to reconstruct high-frequency details and occluders without artifacts.

#### StylizedNeRF
StylizedNeRF utilizes a mutual learning strategy to optimize both the stylized NeRF and the 2D stylization network [S1]. It handles ambiguities of the 2D stylized results via learnable latent codes, ensuring consistency in geometry [S1].

## Background
The standard NeRF formulation assumes a static scene with consistent lighting, which fails on "in-the-wild" image collections [S1]. Early extensions like NeRF-W introduced appearance embeddings and transient fields to handle these variations [S1]. Concurrently, methods like Spherical Harmonics NeRF explored explicit lighting models for relighting capabilities [S1]. Neural-PIL introduced neural approximations of pre-integrated lighting to handle complex illumination efficiently [S1]. StylizedNeRF extended these concepts to artistic style transfer [S1].
