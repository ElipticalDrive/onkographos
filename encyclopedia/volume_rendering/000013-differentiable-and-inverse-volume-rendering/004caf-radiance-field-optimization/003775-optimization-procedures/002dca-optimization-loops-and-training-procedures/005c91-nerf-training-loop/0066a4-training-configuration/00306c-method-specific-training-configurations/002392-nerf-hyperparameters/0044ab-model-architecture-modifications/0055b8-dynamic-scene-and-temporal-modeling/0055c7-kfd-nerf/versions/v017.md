# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (direct observation via a shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (history-based prediction), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain [S1]. Unlike [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) which may rely on feature interpolation, KFD-NeRF uses deformation fields which are smoother and easier for MLPs to fit due to inductive smoothness bias [S1]. It further distinguishes itself by employing a tri-plane spatial representation for the canonical space, enabling a shallower observation network and faster convergence compared to MLP-only backbones like [D-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, timestamp $t_i$, and camera pose.
*   **Outputs:** Density $\sigma$, color $\mathbf{c}$, and deformation field $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ (or state $d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_i}$) for the current frame.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes encoding the canonical space. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ as input [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that takes noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and timestamps $t_i, t_{i-1}$ to output the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
2.  **Prediction Step:**
    *   Retrieve previous frame states $d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-1}}$ and $d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-2}}$ and their noise terms $\varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-2}}$ [S1].
    *   Calculate velocity $v_{t_i}$ using deformation from the previous two frames:
        $$ v_{t_i} = (d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-1}} - d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-2}}) / \Delta t_i $$
        where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    *   Compute the predicted deformation state $\hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_i}$ using local linear motion:
        $$ \hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_i} = d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-1}} - d_{\mathbf{x}}^{t_{i-2}} $$
        [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ with inputs $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
4.  **State Update:** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and prediction $\hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_i}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation $\hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_i} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-}_{t_i}) $$
5.  **Warping:** Warp all points $\mathbf{x}$ in real space to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$ using the estimated deformation $\hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Query the radiance field (using the tri-plane representation and raw coordinates) at $\mathbf{x}'$ and $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, then render using the standard volume rendering equation [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of five components [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{image}$):** Standard photometric reconstruction loss [S1].
2.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d}_{\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
3.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and real-world radiance field [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d_{\mathbf{x}} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
4.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Ensures consistency between the proposal network (used for occupancy estimation via distillation from Mip-NeRF 360 [2]) and the learned model [S1].
5.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges in the canonical space [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Training Strategy:** Gradually release training images in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Training Dynamics:** In initial stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. In later stages, observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(x, t_i, t_prev, t_prev_prev, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter logic.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D point coordinates (tensor)
        t_i: Current timestamp (float)
        t_prev: Previous timestamp (float)
        t_prev_prev: Timestamp before previous (float)
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP returning (observation, noise_terms)
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer returning Kalman gain
        
    Returns:
        estimated_deformation: Deformation vector for x at t_i
    """
    # 1. Observer Step for current frame
    y_t, eps_t = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # 2. Retrieve previous states (assumed to be computed in previous iterations)
    # d_x_prev is deformation at t_prev
    # d_x_prev_prev is deformation at t_prev_prev
    # eps_prev is noise terms at t_prev
    d_x_prev = get_previous_deformation(x, t_prev)
    d_x_prev_prev = get_previous_deformation(x, t_prev_prev)
    eps_prev = get_previous_noise(x, t_prev)
    
    # 3. Prediction Step
    # Calculate velocity based on local linear motion
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev
    v_t = (d_x_prev - d_x_prev_prev) / delta_t
    
    # Predicted deformation state
    d_x_pred = d_x_prev + delta_t * v_t
    # Simplified: d_x_pred = 2 * d_x_prev - d_x_prev_prev
    
    # 4. Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Input: noise terms from current and previous frame, and timestamps
    K_t = kalman_gain_net(eps_t, eps_prev, t_i, t_prev)
    
    # 5. Update Step
    # Combine prediction and observation
    estimated_deformation = d_x_pred + K_t * (y_t - d_x_pred)
    
    return estimated_deformation

def render_kfd_nerf(x, d, t_i, deformation_field, tri_plane_rep):
    """
    Renders a ray using KFD-NeRF.
    """
    # Warp point to canonical space
    x_canonical = x + deformation_field(x, t_i)
    
    # Query radiance field
    # Concatenate tri-plane features with raw coordinates
    features = query_tri_plane(x_canonical, tri_plane_rep)
    sigma, color = radiance_network(features, x_canonical, d)
    
    return sigma, color
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Mitigated by concatenating encoded tri-plane features with raw coordinate inputs [S1].
*   **Hard Masking:** Unlike D-NeRF which hard-masks deformation at $t_0$ to 0, KFD-NeRF uses a soft regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$) to avoid making the canonical space too complex for warping [S1].
*   **Prediction Confidence:** In later training stages, the predictor's confidence drops due to inherent errors in the modeled motion dynamics. The model automatically balances this by relying more on the observation MLP [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to voxel-based methods. On synthetic data, KFD-NeRF-L takes 3 hours, while KFD-NeRF-S takes 30 minutes [S1]. This is significantly faster than D-NeRF (48 hours) and comparable to TiNeuVox-B (40 minutes) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations, allowing for high-resolution canonical space reconstruction [S1]. Parameter size is 175 MB for KFD-NeRF-L and 175 MB for KFD-NeRF-S (Note: Source lists 175 MB for both, likely a typo in source or specific configuration, but explicitly states "reducing memory footprint" [S1]).
*   **Performance (Synthetic Data):** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
*   **Performance (Real Data):** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality motion reconstruction, efficient convergence due to tri-plane representation, robust handling of substantial motion via Kalman prediction [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion for prediction, which may not hold for highly non-linear dynamics [S1]. Requires chronological training strategy [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Y. Zhan, Z. Li, M. Niu, Z. Zhong, S. Nobuhara, K. Nishino, and Y. Zheng from The University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory [S1]. It was published in July 2024 (arXiv:2407.13185v1) [S1]. The method draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states estimated via Kalman filtering [S1]. It addresses limitations of previous dynamic NeRF methods that either disregard motion priors or rely on feature interpolation which exhibits high-frequency signals during abrupt radiance changes [S1].
