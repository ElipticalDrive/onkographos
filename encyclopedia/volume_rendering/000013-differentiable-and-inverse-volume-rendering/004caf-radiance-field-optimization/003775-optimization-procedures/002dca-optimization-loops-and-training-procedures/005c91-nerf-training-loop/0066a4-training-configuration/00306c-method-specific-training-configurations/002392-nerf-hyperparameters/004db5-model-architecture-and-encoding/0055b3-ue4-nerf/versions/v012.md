# UE4-NeRF


## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes by partitioning the environment into sub-NeRFs represented as textured polygonal meshes [S1]. It solves the limitations of traditional NeRFs regarding training time and real-time performance by integrating a learned neural shader into the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. Unlike grid-based large-scale methods like Block-NeRF, UE4-NeRF uses regular octahedra to represent scene geometry, enabling it to handle tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects more effectively while achieving real-time frame rates at 4K resolution [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed images (typically from drones) with GPS coordinates; camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** A textured polygonal mesh hierarchy (LOD levels) and a neural shader (HLSL fragment shader) deployed in UE4, producing real-time rendered RGB images [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large scene is divided into blocks. Each block's training area is slightly larger than the corresponding scene area to create overlapping borders for smooth transitions [S1].
2.  **Grid Initialization:** A $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid is initialized for each block [S1].
3.  **Polygonal Mesh:**
    *   Vertices are derived from the center point of each grid cell and its six neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) [S1].
    *   The mesh topology is based on a regular octahedron with 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
    *   Vertex positions are optimized during training [S1].
4.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity information for the mesh vertices/faces [S1].
5.  **Network Architectures:**
    *   **Encoder Network ($E$):** A 4-layer MLP with dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$. It takes hash-encoded position $\mathbf{p}_i$ and outputs opacity $\alpha_i$ and an 8-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ [S1].
    *   **Decoder Network ($D$):** A 3-layer MLP with dimensions $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$. It takes the feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ and encoded direction $\mathbf{SH}(\mathbf{d}_i)$ to output the final color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
6.  **Coordinate System:** X-axis points east, Y-axis points south, Z-axis points up. World scale is 100:1 (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the large-scale scene into multiple blocks. Train a coarse model for sub-region segmentation to capture overall terrain and building outlines. Filter images associated with sub-regions and use a mask to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].
2.  **Mesh Initialization:** Initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. Select the center point of each grid along with its six neighboring grids to create polygonal meshes. Use a regular octahedron with 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) to address slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces [S1].
3.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Apply the UE4 coordinate system and 100:1 scale conversion to GPS-captured drone imagery prior to modeling [S1].
4.  **Training:**
    *   Employ multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) for training acceleration [S1].
    *   Implement computationally intensive portions using CUDA [S1].
    *   Optimize vertex positions continuously during training [S1].
    *   Train the Encoder-Decoder network for 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
5.  **Shading Mesh Extraction:** Extract coarse polygonal meshes without textures during training. Apply vertex optimization. Encode texture maps in network outputs [S1].
6.  **LOD Generation:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail for different observation heights. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights to ensure real-time performance [S1].
7.  **Shader Deployment:** Implement the Decoder network as a High-Level Shading Language (HLSL) fragment shader in UE4. The shader takes feature vectors as input and outputs colors [S1].
8.  **Rendering:** Combine pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different levels of detail with the UE4 rasterization pipeline for real-time interactive rendering [S1].

**Formulas:**
The ray emitted from the camera origin to the pixel is defined as:
$$ \mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d} $$
where $\mathbf{d} = (\theta, \phi)$ is the viewing direction of the ray [S1].

For each ray, the intersection with the polygonal meshes determines the sampling point. The networks are defined as:
$$ E(\text{hash}(\mathbf{p}_i); \theta_E) \rightarrow \mathbf{M}_i, \alpha_i $$
$$ D(\mathbf{M}_i, \text{SH}(\mathbf{d}_i); \theta_D) \rightarrow \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $\mathbf{p}_i$ is the positional information encoded by multi-resolution hash encoding, $\mathbf{M}_i$ is the feature vector, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, $\mathbf{d}_i$ is the viewing direction, $\text{SH}(\cdot)$ is the direction encoding, and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the final color [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Grid size: $128 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   Octahedron faces: 20 total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   Encoder MLP layers: 4 layers, dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
*   Decoder MLP layers: 3 layers, dimensions $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1].
*   Training iterations: 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
*   Hardware: 1 Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   Training time: ~40 minutes per block [S1].
*   Total training time for $n$ blocks: $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow and unstable convergence on tilted surfaces; UE4-NeRF addresses this by using regular octahedra [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass, plastic) due to opacity binarization. UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Performance:** Semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, resulting in gaps or holes in the final rendered scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead, and increasing accuracy further amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Speed:** 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 40 minutes per block on 1×RTX 3090. Full scene training time is $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ for $n$ blocks [S1].
*   **Rendering Performance:** Real-time rendering at 4K resolution with up to 43 FPS [S1]. 50 FPS on full scene at 2K resolution; ≥70 FPS on individual blocks at 2K resolution [S1].
*   **Memory:** Significant memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time interactive rendering, scene editing capabilities (importing .obj/.fbx), handling of tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects, high fidelity on natural scenes and small objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs/CUDA, pre-rendering gaps/holes, exposure matching issues in UE4, performance degradation with semi-transparent objects [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes (e.g., farmland, construction sites, industrial parks) in UE4 [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring precise exposure matching without post-processing, scenes with extreme semi-transparency where frame rate is critical, or environments without NVIDIA hardware [S1].

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the significant limitations of NeRF in real-time rendering of large-scale scenes, particularly the high training costs and lack of interactivity [S1]. The method builds on concepts from Mobile-NeRF and Block-NeRF but introduces regular octahedra and integration with the UE4 rasterization pipeline to achieve real-time performance [S1]. It is used in Unreal Engine 4 for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
