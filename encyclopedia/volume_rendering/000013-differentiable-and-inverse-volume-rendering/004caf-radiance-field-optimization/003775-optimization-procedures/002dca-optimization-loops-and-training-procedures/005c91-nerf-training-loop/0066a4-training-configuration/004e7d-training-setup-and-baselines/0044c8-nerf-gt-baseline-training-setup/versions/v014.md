# NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup
Also called: NeRF-GT
## Overview
The NeRF-GT baseline establishes a standard reference for training Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) using standard Low Dynamic Range (LDR) or High Dynamic Range (HDR) inputs. It serves as the ground-truth comparison for evaluating novel view synthesis methods, particularly those addressing HDR synthesis or generalized rendering. It differs from variants like DBARF [S1] by relying on accurate initial camera poses and standard supervised learning, rather than self-supervised pose optimization.
## How it works
The method follows the standard differentiable volume rendering pipeline [Parent](13740-training-setup-and-baselines.md), with specific hyperparameters and HDR handling protocols.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ calibrated images with known camera poses $\{I_i, \mathbf{P}_i\}_{i=1}^N$. Images may be LDR (consistent exposure) or HDR.
*   **Outputs:** A trained neural radiance field (MLP) parameterized by weights $\theta$, capable of rendering novel views.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Batch:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ containing the origin and direction for $B$ rays.
*   **Sample Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, S, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates sampled along each ray, where $S$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Network Input:** Concatenation of position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, potentially passed through positional encoding functions (implied by standard NeRF implementation [S2, unofficial]).

**Parameters:**
*   **Coarse Samples ($S_c$):** 64 samples per ray [S2, unofficial].
*   **Fine Samples ($S_f$):** 128 samples per ray [S2, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size ($B$):** 2,048 rays per batch [S2, unofficial].
*   **Resolution:** 800 × 600 pixels [S2, unofficial].
*   **Max Steps:** 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Ray Generation:** For each training iteration, generate $B$ rays by sampling pixel coordinates from the training images and unprojecting them using the known camera poses.
2.  **Coarse Sampling:** Sample $S_c = 64$ points along each ray. The sampling distribution is typically uniform or stratified between near and far clipping planes [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Coarse Network Query:** Evaluate the coarse MLP at the sampled points to predict density $\sigma_c$ and color $\mathbf{c}_c$.
4.  **Coarse Volume Rendering:** Compute the coarse pixel color $C_c$ using the differentiable volume rendering integral [Parent](13740-training-setup-and-baselines.md).
5.  **Fine Sampling:** Use the coarse density estimates to guide importance sampling for $S_f = 128$ points along each ray, concentrating samples in high-density regions [S2, unofficial].
6.  **Fine Network Query:** Evaluate the fine MLP at the sampled points to predict density $\sigma_f$ and color $\mathbf{c}_f$.
7.  **Fine Volume Rendering:** Compute the fine pixel color $C_f$.
8.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   If inputs are **LDR**: Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered color $C_f$ and the ground truth LDR image pixel $I_{gt}$.
    *   If inputs are **HDR**: Tone-map the predicted HDR pixel values $C_f$ into LDR space using a tone-mapping operator $T(\cdot)$. Compute the MSE between $T(C_f)$ and the tone-mapped ground truth LDR image $T(I_{gt})$ [S1].
9.  **Backpropagation:** Update network weights $\theta$ using stochastic gradient descent to minimize the loss.
10. **Iteration:** Repeat steps 1-9 for 200,000 steps [S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **HDR Training Failure:** Training directly on HDR views can fail to encode all scene areas well due to the high dynamic range, even when using tone-mapped colors for loss calculation [S1]. The model may struggle to represent extreme brightness variations accurately [S1].
*   **Convergence:** The method requires a fixed number of iterations (200,000) to converge, as recommended by the original authors [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### NeRF Baseline
This is the standard implementation of the NeRF method used as a reference in comparative studies. It utilizes the specific hyperparameters detailed in the main article (64 coarse, 128 fine samples; 2048 batch size) and is trained for 200,000 steps on 800x600 resolution images [S2, unofficial]. It serves as the primary baseline for evaluating performance against faster or more generalized methods like IBRNet or NLR [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The training time is approximately 0.40 seconds per iteration [S2, unofficial]. This is measured by averaging 100 iteration times and extrapolating [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Rendering a single frame takes approximately 32 seconds [S2, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** The method processes 2,048 rays per batch [S2, unofficial]. The training time per iteration is significantly lower than methods like NLR (2.21 sec/iteration) or SVS (1.38 sec/iteration after mesh computation) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides a high-quality ground-truth reference for novel view synthesis. The use of coarse and fine sampling stages improves rendering quality [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow rendering time (32 sec/frame) [S2, unofficial]. Training directly on HDR data can lead to poor encoding of scene areas due to high dynamic range [S1].
*   **When to use:** When a high-quality baseline is needed for comparison, or when training on standard LDR data.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time rendering is required, or when HDR data is used without careful handling of dynamic range issues [S1].

## Background
The NeRF-GT baseline is based on the original Neural Radiance Fields paper by Mildenhall et al. [S2, unofficial]. The specific hyperparameters (64/128 samples, 2048 batch size, 200,000 steps) are derived from the configuration provided by the authors in the original NeRF repository (config_fern.txt) [S2, unofficial]. The method is widely used as a reference in subsequent research on neural rendering, including studies on HDR synthesis [S1] and meta-learning for fast training [S2, unofficial].
