# Model Architecture and Encoding


## Overview
Model Architecture and Encoding defines the neural network structures and feature representation schemes used to map 3D spatial coordinates and viewing directions to volume density and color in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). It solves the problem of how to efficiently and accurately represent continuous 3D scenes using differentiable functions. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [NeRF Initialization Strategies](8674-nerf-initialization-strategies.md) by focusing on the structural definition of the encoder-decoder pipeline rather than parameter initialization, and from [Model Architecture Modifications](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md) by covering the foundational encoding hierarchies (hash grids, triplanes, Gaussian lifting) that serve as the basis for those modifications.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves a coordinate-based neural network that takes a 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ as input and outputs volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$. The architecture typically consists of two components: an **encoding scheme** that transforms raw coordinates into a higher-dimensional feature space, and a **multi-layer perceptron (MLP)** that decodes these features into radiance field properties.

1.  **Positional Encoding:** Raw coordinates are often mapped to a higher-dimensional space using sinusoidal positional encoding to help the network learn high-frequency details. For a coordinate $\mathbf{x}$, the encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ includes the original coordinate and its sine/cosine transformations at multiple frequencies [S1, S3].
2.  **Feature Grids:** To improve efficiency and capacity, many architectures replace or augment MLPs with multi-resolution hash grids or tensor decompositions. These grids store feature vectors at discrete voxel corners or planes, which are interpolated to obtain features for continuous coordinates [S1, S3, S4].
3.  **View-Dependent Rendering:** The viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ is encoded separately (often using spherical harmonics or positional encoding) and concatenated with the spatial features before being passed to the color prediction network [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md)
This configuration defines the hyperparameters, architecture modifications, and optimization settings for training a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) model that incorporates depth priors to improve reconstruction quality, particularly in sparse-view or noisy-pose scenarios. It builds on [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by adding specific regularization and loss terms. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md) by its explicit integration of depth supervision via Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) and uncertainty weighting, rather than semantic or geometric consistency losses alone.
[Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md)

### [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md)
CLIPNeRF training configuration defines the optimization procedure for text-guided stylization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) by minimizing the negative cosine similarity between rendered image features and text prompt embeddings in the CLIP embedding space. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF reconstruction by freezing density-related parameters and utilizing a patch-based rendering strategy to manage memory constraints while enforcing stylistic directionality via directional CLIP losses.
[CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md)

### [Binary Radiance Fields](13622-binary-radiance-fields.md)
Binary Radiance Fields (BiRF) is a storage-efficient radiance field representation that constrains feature encoding parameters to binary values (+1 or -1) to achieve compact storage without post-processing compression. Unlike methods that compress pre-trained models or use real-valued embeddings, BiRF performs binarization during training, allowing it to outperform state-of-the-art efficient radiance field models with significantly lower storage overhead (e.g., < 0.5 MB) while maintaining high reconstruction quality. It builds on [Model Architecture and Encoding](13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md) by replacing real-valued hash grid embeddings with binary ones and extending the architecture to a 2D-3D hybrid structure to mitigate hash collisions inherent in high-resolution 3D grids.
[Binary Radiance Fields](13622-binary-radiance-fields.md)

### [Mip-NeRF 360 MLP Architecture](14098-mip-nerf-360-mlp-architecture.md)
The Mip-NeRF 360 MLP architecture is a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that maps 3D spatial coordinates and view directions to volume density and color, utilizing integrated positional encoding to handle aliasing in continuous radiance fields. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF by lifting Gaussian covariance matrices into the positional encoding space, allowing the network to explicitly model the frequency content of the signal along a ray. The architecture supports advanced features such as specular tinting, roughness prediction, and integrated directional encoding (IDE) for view-dependent effects.
[Mip-NeRF 360 MLP Architecture](14098-mip-nerf-360-mlp-architecture.md)

### [SUDS](14964-suds.md)
SUDS is a 4D neural representation for large-scale dynamic urban scenes that factorizes the scene into three separate hash table data structures to efficiently encode static, dynamic, and far-field radiance fields. It solves the problem of scaling neural reconstruction to city-level environments with tens of thousands of objects and ego-motion across multiple videos by using unlabeled target signals (RGB, sparse LiDAR, 2D descriptors, 2D optical flow) for supervision. Unlike sibling methods that rely on supervised 3D bounding boxes or panoptic labels, SUDS disentangles dynamic objects from the static background and models shadow effects without manual annotation, enabling free-viewpoint rendering, semantic decomposition, and 3D scene flow estimation.
[SUDS](14964-suds.md)

### [CCNeRF](15008-ccnerf.md)
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition of the scene without using neural networks for rendering. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or TensoRF which uses MLPs that prevent direct compression/composition, CCNeRF models the field *only* with tensor rank decomposition, allowing arbitrary composition without retraining and dynamic model size adjustment via rank truncation.
[CCNeRF](15008-ccnerf.md)

### [Rip-NeRF](15532-rip-nerf.md)
Rip-NeRF is a radiance field method that renders high-fidelity, anti-aliased images by combining cone casting with an anisotropic 3D Gaussian characterization. It differs from sibling methods like Zip-NeRF by using Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids to enable efficient anisotropic area-sampling, whereas Zip-NeRF relies on a multi-sampling mechanism that is effective but computationally inefficient.
[Rip-NeRF](15532-rip-nerf.md)

### [UE4-NeRF](15539-ue4-nerf.md)
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline. It partitions large environments into sub-NeRFs, representing each partition using textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and employs a Level of Detail (LOD) hierarchy to maintain high frame rates. Unlike sibling methods such as [Mobile-NeRF](13622-mobile-nerf.md) which rely on binary opacity and grid structures, UE4-NeRF supports semi-transparent materials and arbitrary terrain types by storing opacity in texture maps and optimizing mesh vertices.
[UE4-NeRF](15539-ue4-nerf.md)

### Triplane NeRF with Diffusion Prior
- **Definition:** The framework uses a triplane NeRF representation where scene codes are optimized jointly with a diffusion prior to handle sparse-view data and ensure end-to-end coherence [S1].
- **Parameters:** Prediction Format uses v-parameterization $\hat{v}_\phi(x(t), t)$ from [43], such that $\hat{x} = \alpha(t)x(t) - \sigma(t)\hat{v}$ [S1]. Weighting Function $w(t)$ uses SNR-based weighting $w(t) = (\alpha(t)/\sigma(t))^2\omega$ from Eq. (5), instead of LSGM mechanisms [S1].
- **Layout:** Scene code $x_i$ has shape $\mathbb{R}^{3 \times 6 \times 128 \times 128}$, representing a triplane NeRF representation [S1]. Denoising Network Input/Output involves noisy and denoised triplane features, with channels of all three planes stacked together [S1]. Denoising Network Architecture is a U-Net with 122M parameters [S1].

### EG3D Triplane Representation
- **Steps:** Render feature image $I_f \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 128 \times 128}$ and raw RGB image $I_{128} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 128 \times 128}$ by accumulating points along rays and performing volume rendering [S2]. Extract dense depth map $I_D \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 128}$ from the volume rendering [S2]. Feed $I_f$ and $I_{128}$ into a 2D super-resolution network to yield final output $I_{512} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 512 \times 512}$ [S2].
- **Formula:** $\text{SuperRes}(I_f, I_{128}) = I_{512}$ [S2].
- **Parameters:** Image resolution is 512x512 for input images and final output [S2].
- **Layout:** Triplane representation $T$ is a tensor in $\mathbb{R}^{256 \times 256 \times 96}$, corresponding to 3 axis-aligned planes (xy, xz, yz), each with 32 channels and resolution 256x256 [S2].
- **Number:** Rendering speed is 42 fps on RTX 3090 [S2].

### NeRF-W Hyperparameter Configuration
- **Parameters:** Base MLP has 8 layers of 512 hidden units [S3]. Static and Transient/Uncertainty MLPs have 4 layers of 128 hidden units each [S3]. Positional encoding frequencies are 15 for position and 4 for viewing direction [S3]. Sampling points are 512 per ray per model (coarse and fine) during training (1024 total), doubled to 2048 during evaluation [S3]. Appearance embedding dimension $n(a) = 48$ [S3]. Transient object embedding dimension $n(\tau) = 16$ [S3]. L1 regularizer multiplier $\lambda_u = 0.01$ [S3]. Minimum importance threshold $\beta_{min} = 0.03$ [S3]. Optimizer is Adam, batch size 2048, initial learning rate 0.001, decaying ten-fold every 150,000 iterations, over 300,000 iterations total [S3].
- **Number:** Hyperparameters were optimized for the Brandenburg Gate scene of the Phototourism dataset and reused for remaining scenes [S3].

### Explicit Mesh Reconstruction from Neural Fields
- **Definition:** An algorithm to extract an explicit 3D mesh representation from the implicit neural radiance field learned by CodeNeRF, allowing for 3D reconstruction from a single input image [S4].
- **Steps:** Feed the center coordinates of a $256^3$ voxel grid to the CodeNeRF MLP to obtain volume density values $\sigma$ [S4]. Apply the Marching Cubes algorithm to the volume density values $\sigma$ to find mesh vertices [S4]. Cast rays along the vertex normals of the generated mesh to associate colors to the vertices [S4].

### Efficient EndoNeRF scene representation
- **Definition:** A voxel-grids-based scene representation that replaces coordinate-based MLPs with explicit density and feature volume grids to accelerate optimization, combined with a canonical field and time-dependent deformation field for dynamic surgical scenes [S5].
- **Steps:** The dynamic scene at time $t$ is modeled by warping a canonical radiance field (represented by $\mathbf{V}_\sigma$ and $\mathbf{V}_a$) with a time-dependent deformation field [S5]. The deformation field is modeled using decomposed 4D motion fields and a 3-layer MLP that maps a spatial-temporal coordinate to a deformation offset [S5].

### NeRF-Liif
- **Definition:** A baseline method that super-resolves LR images using a pretrained LIIF (Local Implicit Image Function) model, which requires LR-HR pairs for training and introduces external data priors [S6].
- **Parameters:** Training data requirement is LR-HR pairs [S6].
- **Pitfalls:** On synthetic Blender dataset, LIIF may produce false predictions due to domain mismatch with its external training set [S6]. On real-world LLFF dataset, LIIF tends to be oversmooth and may predict false color or geometry [S6].
- **Numbers:** Blender ×2 (100x100): PSNR 27.07, SSIM 0.919, LPIPS 0.067. Blender ×4 (100x100): PSNR 25.36, SSIM 0.885, LPIPS 0.125. Blender ×2 (200x200): PSNR 28.81, SSIM 0.934, LPIPS 0.058. Blender ×4 (200x200): PSNR 27.34, SSIM 0.912, LPIPS 0.096. LLFF ×2: PSNR 26.81, SSIM 0.823, LPIPS 0.145. LLFF ×4: PSNR 24.76, SSIM 0.723, LPIPS 0.292 [S6].

### UniSurf
- **Definition:** A method unifying volume rendering (radiance fields) and surface rendering (implicit surfaces) to achieve high-quality geometry and sharp textures without mask supervision [S7, unofficial].
- **Steps:** Uses an occupancy field where the occupancy at the $i^{th}$ sample corresponds to an indicator of surface presence [S7, unofficial]. Uses volume rendering in the early stage for optimization without mask supervision [S7, unofficial]. Uses surface rendering in the later stage to extract level-set surfaces [S7, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Unlike pure surface rendering which requires mask supervision and has blurry texture mapping, this method provides high-quality novel views and sharp textures/colors without mask supervision [S7, unofficial].

## Background
The foundational NeRF architecture introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 used a simple MLP with positional encoding [S3]. Subsequent works have diversified the encoding and architecture to address limitations in storage, training speed, and representation capacity. Instant-NGP introduced multi-resolution hash grids for real-time training [S3]. EG3D utilized triplane representations for efficient 3D-aware generation [S2]. NeRF-W extended the architecture to handle unconstrained photo collections with appearance embeddings [S3]. CodeNeRF disentangled shape and texture using separate latent embeddings [S4]. These developments form the basis for the specific configurations and variants detailed in the subtopics.
