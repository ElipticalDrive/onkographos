# UE4-NeRF


## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes, such as industrial parks and farmlands, by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. It partitions large environments into sub-NeRFs, represents each partition using textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and utilizes a Level of Detail (LOD) hierarchy to maintain high frame rates [S1]. Unlike standard NeRFs that rely on volumetric ray-marching or grid-based hashing for inference, UE4-NeRF extracts explicit geometry and encodes neural features into texture atlases, allowing the scene to be rendered as standard rasterized meshes within a game engine [S1]. This approach distinguishes it from [Mobile-NeRF](13622-mobile-nerf.md) by handling tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects more effectively, and from [Block-NeRF](13622-block-nerf.md) by supporting non-grid scene structures like mountainous terrain [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed images (typically from drone capture) with GPS metadata; camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** A real-time renderable scene in UE4 consisting of LOD polygonal meshes with associated texture atlases containing feature vectors and opacity; the final pixel colors $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large scene is divided into $N$ blocks. Each block has a training area slightly larger than the physical scene area to create overlapping borders for smooth transitions [S1].
2.  **Mesh Geometry:** Each block is represented by a polygonal mesh. The mesh is initialized from a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. For each grid cell, the center point and its six neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) are selected to form polygonal faces [S1]. Specifically, regular octahedra are used, consisting of 20 faces total (8 exterior, 12 interior) to address convergence issues on tilted surfaces [S1]. The vertex positions of these meshes are optimized during training [S1].
3.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores two types of data per vertex/face:
    *   An 8-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$.
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i$.
    *   These are encoded into the network outputs and stored in texture maps [S1].
4.  **Neural Network (Encoder-Decoder):**
    *   **Encoder ($E$):** A 4-layer MLP with dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$. Input: Positional information $\mathbf{p}_i$ encoded via multi-resolution hash encoding. Output: Opacity $\alpha_i$ and feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ [S1].
    *   **Decoder ($D$):** A 3-layer MLP with dimensions $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$. Input: Feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ and spherical harmonics coefficients of the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}_i$ (denoted $SH(\mathbf{d}_i)$). Output: Final color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
5.  **LOD Hierarchy:** Multiple levels of mesh detail are trained. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights to ensure real-time performance [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform GPS-captured drone imagery coordinates to the UE4 coordinate system. In this system, the X-axis points east, the Y-axis points south, and the Z-axis points up. Apply a 100:1 scale conversion (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].
2.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the large-scale scene into multiple blocks. Train a coarse model for sub-region segmentation to capture overall terrain and building outlines. Filter images associated with sub-regions and use a mask to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].
3.  **Mesh Initialization:** For each block, initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. Select the center point of each grid along with its six neighboring grids to create polygonal meshes. Use regular octahedra (20 faces: 8 exterior, 12 interior) instead of the Mobile-NeRF approach to handle tilted surfaces [S1].
4.  **Network Training:**
    *   Implement the computationally intensive portions of the multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) using CUDA for GPU parallel computing [S1].
    *   Define the ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin and $\mathbf{d} = (\theta, \phi)$ is the viewing direction [S1].
    *   Calculate the intersection of rays with the polygonal meshes. The intersection point serves as the sampling point [S1].
    *   Optimize the Encoder-Decoder network parameters and the vertex positions of the meshes simultaneously.
    *   The Encoder takes the hash-encoded position $\mathbf{p}_i$ to output $\mathbf{M}_i$ and $\alpha_i$:
        $$ E(\text{hash}(\mathbf{p}_i); \theta_E) \rightarrow \mathbf{M}_i, \alpha_i $$
    *   The Decoder takes $\mathbf{M}_i$ and the spherical harmonics of the viewing direction $SH(\mathbf{d}_i)$ to output color $\mathbf{c}_i$:
        $$ D(\mathbf{M}_i, SH(\mathbf{d}_i); \theta_D) \rightarrow \mathbf{c}_i $$
    *   Train for 80,000 epochs per block to achieve convergence [S1].
5.  **Shading Mesh Extraction:** Extract coarse polygonal meshes without textures during training. Apply vertex optimization. Encode texture maps (feature vectors and opacity) in network outputs [S1].
6.  **LOD Generation:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail for different observation heights. Extract pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different levels of detail for the final rendering module [S1].
7.  **UE4 Integration:** Implement the Decoder network as a High-Level Shading Language (HLSL) fragment shader in UE4. The shader takes the feature vectors from the texture atlas as input and outputs colors [S1]. Combine the extracted meshes with the UE4 rasterization pipeline [S1].
8.  **Rendering:** Render the scene using the UE4 rasterization pipeline. The engine handles occlusion between added traditional 3D models (e.g., .obj, .fbx) and the NeRF-rendered background, skipping neural shader computation for occluded regions [S1].

**Parameters:**

*   **Octahedron Faces:** 20 faces total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].

*   **Evaluation Metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow and unstable convergence on tilted surfaces. UE4-NeRF addresses this by using regular octahedra [S1].
*   **Semi-transparent Objects:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass, plastic) due to opacity binarization. UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps to mitigate this, though semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead and reduce frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, resulting in gaps or holes in the final rendered scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory Overhead:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead. Increasing accuracy further amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** 40 minutes per block on 1×RTX 3090. For a full scene with $n$ blocks, the time is $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ [S1]. This is approximately 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1].
*   **Rendering Performance:** Real-time rendering at 4K resolution with up to 43 FPS on level-1 mesh hierarchy. 50 FPS on full scene at 2K resolution; $\ge 70$ FPS on individual blocks at 2K resolution [S1].
*   **Memory:** Significant memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers). Memory consumption increases with accuracy [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes; handles tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects better than Mobile-NeRF; supports scene editing in UE4; faster training than Mega-NeRF and Mobile-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs; pre-rendering gaps/holes; exposure matching issues in UE4; semi-transparent objects reduce frame rates; limited to scenes that can be partitioned into blocks [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Large-scale outdoor scenes (farmland, construction sites, industrial parks) requiring real-time interaction and high fidelity.
*   **When Not to Use:** Small-scale scenes where standard NeRF is sufficient; scenes requiring perfect geometric completeness without pre-rendering artifacts; hardware without CUDA support.

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of NeRF in real-time rendering of large-scale scenes, specifically the high computational overhead and lack of interactivity. The method builds on [Instant-NGP](13622-instant-ngp.md) for training acceleration via multi-resolution hash encoding and [Mobile-NeRF](13622-mobile-nerf.md) for mesh-based representation, but improves upon them by using regular octahedra and integrating with UE4 for real-time rasterization [S1]. It is used in Unreal Engine 4 for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
