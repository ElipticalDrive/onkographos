# UE4-NeRF


## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes, such as industrial parks and farmlands, by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. It partitions large environments into sub-NeRFs, representing each block using textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and employs a Level of Detail (LOD) hierarchy to maintain high frame rates at varying observation heights [S1]. Unlike Block-NeRF, which is limited to grid structures, UE4-NeRF supports diverse terrains including mountainous areas, and unlike Mobile-NeRF, it handles tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects more effectively through continuous vertex optimization and texture-based opacity storage [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed images (e.g., drone-captured) with GPS metadata; camera poses; scene bounding box.
*   **Outputs:** A UE4 scene containing LOD polygonal meshes with embedded neural shaders, enabling real-time rendering of colors $\mathbf{c}$ for any viewpoint.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large scene is divided into $N$ blocks. Each block's training area is slightly larger than the physical scene area to create overlapping borders for smooth transitions [S1].
2.  **Grid Initialization:** For each block, a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ voxel grid is initialized [S1].
3.  **Mesh Representation:** Polygonal meshes are constructed by selecting the center point of each grid cell and its six immediate neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) [S1]. To handle tilted surfaces, a regular octahedron with 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) is used instead of the standard cube-based approach [S1]. The vertices of these meshes are optimized during training [S1].
4.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity information for each mesh vertex/face [S1].
5.  **LOD Hierarchy:** Multiple mesh resolutions are trained for different observation heights. Coarser meshes are used for higher viewpoints to ensure performance [S1].

**Network Architecture:**
The method uses an Encoder-Decoder MLP structure implemented as an HLSL fragment shader in UE4 [S1].

*   **Encoder ($E$):**
    *   Input: Positional information $\mathbf{p}_i$ encoded via multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) [S1].
    *   Structure: 4-layer MLP.
    *   Dimensions: $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
    *   Output: Opacity $\alpha_i$ and an 8-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ [S1].
    *   Formula:
        $$ \mathbf{M}_i, \alpha_i = E(\text{hash}(\mathbf{p}_i); \theta_E) $$
        where $\theta_E$ are the encoder parameters [S1].

*   **Decoder ($D$):**
    *   Input: Feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ encoded via Spherical Harmonics (SH) [S1].
    *   Structure: 3-layer MLP.
    *   Dimensions: $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1]. Note: The input dimension is 17, implying the SH encoding of the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ results in a 9-dimensional vector (combined with $\mathbf{M}_i$'s 8 dimensions to make 17) [S1].
    *   Output: Final color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
    *   Formula:
        $$ \mathbf{c}_i = D(\mathbf{M}_i, \text{SH}(\mathbf{d}_i); \theta_D) $$
        where $\theta_D$ are the decoder parameters [S1].

**Training Procedure:**
1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform GPS-captured imagery coordinates to the UE4 coordinate system where X points East, Y points South, and Z points Up, with a 100:1 scale conversion (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].
2.  **Coarse Model Training:** Train a coarse model to capture overall terrain and building outlines for sub-region segmentation [S1]. Filter images associated with sub-regions and use a mask to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].
3.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the scene into blocks. Ensure overlapping borders between neighboring blocks [S1].
4.  **Mesh Initialization:** Initialize the $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid and create polygonal meshes using the center and 6 neighbors for each cell, forming regular octahedra [S1].
5.  **Optimization Loop:**
    *   For each ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ from camera origin $\mathbf{o}$ to pixel with direction $\mathbf{d}=(\theta, \phi)$, calculate intersection with the polygonal meshes [S1].
    *   The intersection point serves as the sampling point $\mathbf{p}_i$ [S1].
    *   Compute $\mathbf{M}_i, \alpha_i$ using the Encoder.
    *   Compute $\mathbf{c}_i$ using the Decoder.
    *   Optimize vertex positions of the meshes, $\theta_E$, and $\theta_D$ simultaneously [S1].
    *   Repeat for 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
6.  **LOD Training:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail for different observation heights [S1].
7.  **Extraction:** Extract pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different LODs for the final rendering module [S1].

**Rendering Procedure:**
1.  **Integration:** Deploy the neural shader (Encoder-Decoder MLP) into the UE4 rasterization pipeline as an HLSL fragment shader [S1].
2.  **LOD Selection:** Select the appropriate mesh LOD based on the camera's observation height [S1].
3.  **Rasterization:** Render the textured polygonal meshes using UE4's rasterization pipeline. The shader computes color based on the stored feature vectors and viewing direction [S1].
4.  **Occlusion Handling:** When traditional 3D models (e.g., .obj, .fbx) are imported, UE4 automatically handles occlusion, skipping neural shader computation for occluded regions [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Size:** $128 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   **Octahedron Faces:** 20 total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   **Encoder Layers:** 4 layers, dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
*   **Decoder Layers:** 3 layers, dimensions $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1].
*   **Training Hardware:** 1 Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
*   **Coordinate System:** X=East, Y=South, Z=Up [S1].
*   **World Scale:** 100:1 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces; UE4-NeRF addresses this with regular octahedra [S1].
*   **Semi-transparent Objects:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass) due to opacity binarization; UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps [S1]. However, semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, potentially resulting in gaps or holes in the final scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory Overhead:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead, which increases with accuracy [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Speed:** 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1].
    *   Time per block: ~40 minutes on 1×RTX 3090 [S1].
    *   Time for full scene ($n$ blocks): $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ on 1×RTX 3090 [S1].
    *   Comparison: Mobile-NeRF takes 48h/block on 4×3090; Mega-NeRF takes 36h/block on 1×3090 [S1].
*   **Rendering Performance:**
    *   Real-time rendering at 4K resolution with up to 43 FPS [S1].
    *   50 FPS on full scene at 2K resolution [S1].
    *   ≥70 FPS on individual blocks at 2K resolution [S1].
    *   Comparison: Mobile-NeRF max 35 FPS at 2K; Mega-NeRF max 60 FPS at 800×800 [S1].
*   **Memory:** Significant memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers); increasing accuracy amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time performance, handles large-scale scenes, supports scene editing (importing .obj/.fbx), handles tilted surfaces and semi-transparent objects better than Mobile-NeRF, avoids blurriness on natural scenes compared to Mip-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs/CUDA, pre-rendering gaps, exposure matching issues in UE4, semi-transparent objects reduce FPS [S1].
*   **When to use:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale outdoor scenes (farmland, construction sites) where high fidelity and editability are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes requiring precise exposure matching with original images without post-processing, or on hardware without CUDA support [S1].

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of existing NeRF methods in real-time rendering of large-scale scenes, such as high training costs, slow rendering speeds, and lack of editability [S1]. The method builds on techniques from Mobile-NeRF, Block-NeRF, and Instant-NGP, integrating them with the Unreal Engine 4 rasterization pipeline [S1]. It is used for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
