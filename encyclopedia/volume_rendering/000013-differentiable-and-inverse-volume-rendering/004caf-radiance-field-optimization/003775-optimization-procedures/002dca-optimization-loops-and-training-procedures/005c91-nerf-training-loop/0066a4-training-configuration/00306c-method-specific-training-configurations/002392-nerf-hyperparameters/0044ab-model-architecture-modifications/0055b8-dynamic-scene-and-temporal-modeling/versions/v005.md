# Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, Time-conditioned NeRF, 4D NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling extends the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) assumption to handle time-varying geometry and appearance, enabling novel view synthesis for moving objects and scenes . Unlike static variants, these methods introduce temporal coordinates or deformation fields to map 3D space to radiance properties that evolve over time . This family is distinguished by its approach to modeling motion: some methods directly condition the neural network on time , others use explicit deformation fields or warping to align canonical frames , and some employ hybrid representations like particles or semantic stratification to handle complex dynamics .

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) serves as the baseline. Modifications for dynamic scenes generally fall into three categories [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding a temporal coordinate $t$ or motion embeddings to the input concatenation .
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict deformation fields, velocities, or semantic labels, rendered using standard transmittance weights [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or explicit motion models (particles, warping fields) [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

The rendering equation is adapted to include time $t$:
$$
C(\mathbf{r}, t) = \int_{s_n}^{s_f} T(s, t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s), t) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(s), \mathbf{d}, t) ds
$$
where $\mathbf{r}(s) = \mathbf{o} + s\mathbf{d}$ is the ray equation, and $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ are functions of space, view direction, and time .

## Subtopics

### [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)
Directly extends the MLP input domain with a time coordinate $t$ encoded via sinusoidal positional encoding. It serves as a baseline for time-aware rendering by modeling density and color as explicit functions of time.

### [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md)
Models a static canonical scene and forward warps it to target times using learned deformation trajectories and average splatting. It addresses many-to-one mapping issues inherent in dynamic reconstruction by leveraging smooth trajectory fields.

### [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md)
Combines a static grid-based field with a particle-based dynamic component. It solves the problem of reconstructing time-varying scenes from monocular video without multi-view stereo data by applying appearance particles only to dynamic elements.

### [KFD-NeRF](15551-kfd-nerf.md)
Models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system estimated using a Kalman filter. It explicitly separates motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), weighted by a learnable Kalman gain, leveraging inductive smoothness bias for faster convergence.

### [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md)
Stratifies the scene into motion-based 'gears' using semantic embeddings. It solves the inefficiency of uniform 4D sampling by assigning higher resolution to regions with larger motion scales, enabling enhanced dynamic novel view synthesis and free-viewpoint tracking.

## Background
The standard NeRF architecture assumes static scenes [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md). Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling emerged to address the limitation that standard NeRF cannot capture time-varying geometry and appearance . Early adaptations introduced time as an additional input to the implicit function . Subsequent methods explored deformation fields, warping techniques, and hybrid representations to handle complex non-rigid motions and occlusions more effectively . These methods are used in applications requiring free-viewpoint rendering of dynamic events, such as sports, human performance, and urban dynamics .
