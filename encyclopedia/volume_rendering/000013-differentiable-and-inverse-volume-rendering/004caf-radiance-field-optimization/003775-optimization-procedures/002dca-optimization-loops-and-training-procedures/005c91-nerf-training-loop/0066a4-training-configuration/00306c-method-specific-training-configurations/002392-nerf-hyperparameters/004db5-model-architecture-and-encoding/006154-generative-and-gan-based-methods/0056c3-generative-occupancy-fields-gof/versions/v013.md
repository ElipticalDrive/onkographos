# Generative Occupancy Fields (GOF)

## Overview
Generative Occupancy Fields (GOF) is a 3D-aware image synthesis method that combines radiance fields with occupancy representations to learn compact object surfaces without impeding training convergence [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of diffused surfaces in standard generative radiance fields (like GRAFs) and the sparse gradient convergence issues in direct occupancy networks by transitioning from cumulative volume rendering to surface-point rendering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike siblings that predict volume density $\sigma$ and rely on cumulative alpha composition, GOF predicts alpha values $\alpha$ directly, reinterpreting them as occupancy to enable root-finding for precise surface location [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A latent code $z \sim p_z$ (typically truncated for inference), a camera pose $\xi \sim p_\xi$, and a viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in S^2$.
*   **Output:** A synthesized 2D image color $\hat{C}(r)$ for each ray $r$ defined by the camera pose.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Generator $G_\theta$:** A neural network (specifically a FiLMed-SIREN backbone) that takes position $\mathbf{x}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$, and latent code $z$ as input. It outputs a scalar alpha value $\alpha_\theta(\mathbf{x}, z) \in [0, 1]$ and a color vector $\mathbf{c}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Discriminator $D_\phi$:** A standard GAN discriminator evaluating real vs. generated images [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\tau$: Threshold for root-finding. Typical value: $0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $N$: Number of rendering samples. Typical value: $12$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $M$: Number of root-finding bins. Typical value: $12$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $m_s$: Number of secant iterations for surface refinement. Typical value: $3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Delta_{init}$: Initial sampling interval radius. Defined as $(t_f - t_n)/2$, where $[t_n, t_f]$ is the near/far bound of the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Delta_{min}$: Minimum sampling interval radius. Not given in the sources.
*   $\gamma$: Decay rate for shrinking interval. Not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{normal}, \lambda_{opacity}$: Loss balancing coefficients. Not given in the sources.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Surface Location via Root-Finding:**
    To locate the surface $S$ along a ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$:
    1.  Sample $M$ evenly-spaced points $x_k$ partitioning the volume interval $[t_n, t_f]$. These points are used only for root-finding and do not require gradient computation [S1, unofficial].
    2.  Query the generator for alpha values $\alpha_\theta(x_k, z)$ at each point.
    3.  Find the bin $k_S$ where the alpha value crosses the threshold $\tau$:
        $$ k_S = \arg\min_k (\alpha_\theta(x_k, z) < \tau \le \alpha_\theta(x_{k+1}, z)) $$
    4.  Apply the secant method iteratively $m_s$ times within the bin $[x_{k_S}, x_{k_S+1}]$ to find the precise surface point $x_s$ and its distance $t_s$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Shrinking Sampling Region (Training Only):**
    To encourage surface compactness during training, the sampling region shrinks over time:
    1.  Initialize $\Delta_{init} = (t_f - t_n)/2$.
    2.  At training step $n$, compute the current interval radius:
        $$ \Delta_n = \max(\Delta_{init} \exp(-\gamma n), \Delta_{min}) $$
    3.  Sample $N$ points uniformly in the interval $[t_s - \Delta_n, t_s + \Delta_n]$. The $i$-th point's distance $t_i$ is sampled from:
        $$ t_i \sim U\left[t_s - \Delta_n + \frac{2i-2}{N}\Delta_n, t_s - \Delta_n + \frac{2i}{N}\Delta_n\right] $$
    4.  If the sampling interval exceeds the global bounds $[t_n, t_f]$, shift it back to fit within bounds [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Rendering:**
    Compute the integrated color $\tilde{C}(r)$ using alpha composition. Since GOF predicts $\alpha$ directly, the formula simplifies from the standard density-based rendering:
    $$ \tilde{C}(r, z) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_\theta(x_i, z) \left( \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_\theta(x_j, z)) \right) c_\theta(x_i, \mathbf{d}, z) $$
    where $x_i$ are the sampled points from Step 2 [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Inference Rendering:**
    Fetch a truncated latent code $\hat{z}$.
    *   **Standard Mode:** Sample $N$ points in the minimal region $[t_s - \Delta_{min}, t_s + \Delta_{min}]$ and render using the formula above [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Surface-Only Mode:** Set $N=1$ and sample only the surface point $x_s$. Render using the single point's color $c_\theta(x_s, \mathbf{d}, \hat{z})$ weighted by $\alpha_\theta(x_s, \hat{z})$ (which should be close to 0.5 or 1 depending on implementation, but effectively the color is taken directly) [S1, unofficial].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss is a combination of GAN loss and regularizations:
$$ L(\theta, \phi) = L_{origin}(\theta, \phi) + \lambda_{normal} L_{normal} + \lambda_{opacity} L_{opacity} $$

1.  **GAN Loss ($L_{origin}$):**
    $$ L_{origin}(\theta_D, \theta_G) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z, \xi \sim p_\xi} f(D_{\theta_D}(G_{\theta_G}(z, \xi))) + \mathbb{E}_{I \sim p_D} f(-D_{\theta_D}(I)) + \lambda ||\nabla_{D_{\theta_D}}(I)||_2^2 $$
    where $f(u) = -\log(1 + \exp(-u))$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Opacity Regularization ($L_{opacity}$):**
    Reduces the entropy of predicted alpha values to push them towards 0 or 1:
    $$ L_{opacity} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[ \log(\alpha_\theta(x_i, z)) + \log(1 - \alpha_\theta(x_i, z)) \right] $$
    [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Normal Regularization ($L_{normal}$):**
    Encourages surface normal smoothness at the located surface points $x_s \in S$:
    $$ L_{normal} = \sum_{x_s \in S} ||\mathbf{n}_\theta(x_s, z) - \mathbf{n}_\theta(x_s + \epsilon, z)||_2^2 $$
    where $\mathbf{n}_\theta$ is the estimated surface normal (computed via finite differences or Jacobian of the alpha field) and $\epsilon$ is a small perturbation [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
*   **Shape-Color Ambiguity:** Direct training of generative radiance fields fails to maintain surface compactness because small perturbations on surfaces still lead to realistic RGB images, fooling the discriminator [S1, unofficial]. GOF mitigates this by shrinking the sampling region.
*   **Gradient Sparsity:** Directly applying occupancy representations causes severe convergence problems due to sparse gradients located only on surfaces. GOF avoids this by starting with cumulative rendering (radiance field) and transitioning to surface rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Density Thresholding:** Predicted densities in generative radiance fields can range from 0 to 50, making thresholding difficult. GOF uses alpha values (bounded [0,1]) instead, allowing a fixed threshold $\tau=0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Furry/Translucent Objects:** Surface rendering mode may make furry cat images over-smooth and less realistic. GOF is best suited for solid objects with a single clear surface [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Query Complexity:** GOF requires $M + m_s + N$ queries for standard rendering, or $M + m_s + 1$ for single-point surface rendering. Baselines (like pi-GAN) typically require $2N$ queries due to hierarchical sampling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** GOF significantly outperforms baselines on MAD (Mean Angle Deviation, measuring compactness) and is comparable on SIDE (Scale-Invariant Depth Error, measuring depth accuracy) on datasets like CelebA, BFM, and Cats [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Produces compact and smooth surfaces; alleviates the burden of sampling many points during inference (surface-only mode); combines the training stability of radiance fields with the geometric precision of occupancy networks.
*   **Limitations:** Less effective for translucent or furry objects where a single surface is not well-defined; requires careful tuning of $\Delta_{min}$ and $\gamma$ [S1, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed by Xudong Xu, Xingang Pan, Dahua Lin, and Bo Dai from CUHK-SenseTime Joint Lab, MPI Informatics, and Nanyang Technological University [S1, unofficial].
*   **Publication:** Published on arXiv in November 2021 (arXiv:2111.00969) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Context:** GOF addresses limitations in prior generative radiance fields (like GRAFs and pi-GAN) which suffered from diffused surfaces, and occupancy networks which suffered from training instability due to sparse gradients. It builds on the concept of reinterpreting alpha values in volume rendering as occupancy values [S1, unofficial].
