# SCARF
Also called: Scalable Continual Learning Framework, Scalable Continal Learning Framework for Memory-efficient Multiple Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
SCARF is a method for memory-efficient continual learning of multiple 3D scenes using a single neural network, addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem in NeRF when training on a sequence of scenes with access to only one scene at a time and training data accessible only once [S1]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [DoF-NeRF](9432-dof-nerf.md) and [NeRF-VPT](11770-nerf-vpt.md) by factorizing the NeRF MLP into scene-specific and cross-scene shared components generated by a hypernetwork, rather than using prompt tuning or separate density fields [S1]. Unlike [Uncertainty and Reliability](14487-uncertainty-and-reliability.md) which focuses on error estimation, SCARF focuses on parameter efficiency and knowledge retention across domain gaps [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A sequence of scenes $\{S_i\}_{i=1}^N$. For the $t$-th scene $S_t$, the input is the training images and camera poses for $S_t$. The model also requires pre-extracted explicit density occupancy grids for all previously learned scenes $S_i$ ($i < t$) to guide sampling [S1].
- **Output:** A single unified NeRF model $M_t$ capable of rendering all scenes $\{S_i\}_{i=1}^t$ with minimal catastrophic forgetting [S1].

**Data Structures and Layout:**
The NeRF MLP is divided into an encoder and a decoder [S1].
- **Encoder:** Consists of $L=9$ layers (in the experimental setup, though $L$ is a parameter). The first 8 layers are fully connected with 256 channels each. A skip connection is applied at the 5th layer. The 9th layer outputs density $\sigma$ and a 256-dimensional feature vector [S1].
- **Decoder:** Consists of 2 layers. It takes the 256-dim feature vector concatenated with the 4-degree positional encoding of the viewing direction. The first decoder layer has 128 channels, the second has 3 channels (RGB) [S1].
- **Positional Encoding:** Position $x$ uses 10-degree positional encoding. Viewing direction uses 4-degree positional encoding [S1].
- **Weight Factorization:** Each encoder layer $l$ weight matrix $E_l \in \mathbb{R}^{c_{in} \times c_{out}}$ is factorized into a Scene-Specific Weight Matrix (SSWM) $S_{li} \in \mathbb{R}^{c_{in} \times K}$ and a Cross-Scene Weight Matrix (CSWM) $C_l \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times c_{out}}$ [S1].
- **Coefficient Matrix:** A learnable coefficient matrix $C_{li} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ is maintained for each scene $i$ and layer $l$ [S1].
- **Hypernetwork:** A generator network $G_l$ generates the SSWM from random noise $z_{il}$ [S1].
- **Occupancy Grid:** An explicit density occupancy grid of resolution $50 \times 50 \times 50$ is pre-extracted for each learned scene. During distillation, each grid cell is subdivided into a $5 \times 5 \times 5$ subgrid; a cell is considered "surface" if any subgrid point has density $\sigma > \tau$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $K$: Intermediate latent dimension for factorized weights. Smaller $K$ reduces parameters but requires constraints [S1].
- $z_{il}$: Random noise sampled from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, input to hypernetwork $G_l$ for scene $i$, layer $l$ [S1].
- $\alpha$: Hyperparameter balancing radiance and density regularization in $L_{ic\sigma}$ [S1].
- $\beta_1, \beta_2$: Learnable self-balancing parameters for uncertainty in distillation loss [S1].
- $\tau$: Density threshold for surface distillation (default 3) [S1].
- $\gamma$: Hyperparameter balancing distillation loss for previous scenes and learning loss for the new scene [S1].
- Learning Rates: Parameter generator $G_l$ starts at $1e-4$, decays to $5e-5$. Learnable matrices (SSWM, CSWM, coefficient) start at $5e-4$, decays to $5e-5$ [S1].
- Batch Sizes: New scene training uses 4096 pixel rays. Distillation uses 1024 pixel rays and 8192 random sampled points [S1].
- Warm-up: 2000 steps of training only on the new scene's RGB loss before joint training [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization for Scene $t$:**
    - Initialize model $M_t$ with parameters from $M_{t-1}$: shared CSWM $\{C_l\}_{l=1}^L$, shared hypernetwork $\{G_l\}_{l=1}^L$, and existing scene-specific coefficient matrices $\{C_{li}\}_{i=1}^{t-1, l=1}^L$ and random noises $\{z_{il}\}_{i=1}^{t-1, l=1}^L$ [S1].
    - For the new scene $S_t$, add only new parameters: $L$ layers of random noise $\{z_{tl}\}_{l=1}^L$ and $L$ layers of coefficient matrices $\{C_{tl}\}_{l=1}^L$ [S1].

2.  **Weight Generation:**
    - For each layer $l$ and scene $i$, generate the SSWM using the hypernetwork:
      $$ S_{li} = G_l(z_{il}) $$
      where $G_l$ maps noise $z_{il}$ (dim 256) to a latent feature, resized to $256 \times 21$ (assuming $c_{in}=256, K=21$ in the specific implementation described in Appendix B, though general dimensions are $c_{in} \times K$) [S1].
    - Compute the transformed CSWM:
      $$ C'_{li} = C_{li} * C_l $$
      where $*$ denotes matrix multiplication [S1].
    - Compute the final encoder layer weights:
      $$ E_{li} = S_{li} * C'_{li} = G_l(z_{il}) * C_{li} * C_l $$
      [S1].
    - The decoder $D$ is shared globally across all scenes [S1].

3.  **Sampling:**
    - For the new scene $S_t$, sample rays from the training images [S1].
    - For distillation of previous scenes $S_i$ ($i < t$):
        - Use the pre-extracted occupancy grid for $S_i$.
        - Sample points such that density $\sigma_i > \tau$ to ensure sampling on the surface, avoiding blank regions where $\sigma \to 0$ [S1].
        - Sample 1024 pixel rays and 8192 random points for distillation [S1].

4.  **Loss Computation:**
    - **Distillation Loss for Scene $i$ ($i < t$):**
        - Compute density and radiance distillation loss $L_{ic\sigma}^i$:
          $$ L_{ic\sigma}^i = ||c_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t} - c_{i}^{M_{t-1}}||^2 + \alpha ||\sigma_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t} - \sigma_{i}^{M_{t-1}}||^2 $$
          where $c_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t}$ and $\sigma_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t}$ are density/radiance sampled from $M_t$ in scene $S_i$, and $c_{i}^{M_{t-1}}$ and $\sigma_{i}^{M_{t-1}}$ are from $M_{t-1}$ in scene $S_i$ [S1].
        - Compute rendered pixel distillation loss $L_{C(r)}^i$:
          $$ L_{C(r)}^i = ||C(r)_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t} - C(r)_{i}^{M_{t-1}}||^2 $$
          where $C(r)_{i}^{\hat{}}_{M_t}$ is the rendered pixel from $M_t$ in scene $S_i$, and $C(r)_{i}^{M_{t-1}}$ is from $M_{t-1}$ [S1].
        - Combine using uncertain loss formulation:
          $$ L_{idis}^i = \beta_1 \mathbb{I}(\sigma_i > \tau) L_{ic\sigma}^i + \beta_2 L_{C(r)}^i + \log(\beta_1) + \log(\beta_2) $$
          where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function, $\tau$ is the density threshold, and $\beta_1, \beta_2$ are learnable self-balancing parameters [S1].
    - **New Scene Loss for Scene $t$:**
        - Compute standard RGB pixel loss $L_{C(r)}^t$:
          $$ L_{C(r)}^t = ||C(r)_{t}^{\hat{}}_{M_t} - C(r)_{t}^{GT}||^2 $$
          where $C(r)_{t}^{\hat{}}_{M_t}$ is the predicted pixel from $M_t$ and $C(r)_{t}^{GT}$ is the ground truth [S1].
    - **Total Loss:**
      $$ L = \sum_{i=1}^{t-1} L_{idis}^i + \gamma L_{C(r)}^t $$
      [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    - Perform warm-up training for 2000 steps optimizing only $L_{C(r)}^t$ for the new scene [S1].
    - Jointly optimize all parameters (including $z_{tl}, C_{tl}$ for new scene and $\beta_1, \beta_2$) using the total loss $L$ [S1].
    - Use Adam optimizer [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class SCARF:
    def __init__(self, c_in=256, c_out=256, decoder_dims=[128, 3], K=21, L=9):
        """
        Initialize SCARF model.
        c_in: Input dimension for encoder layers (after positional encoding).
        c_out: Output dimension for encoder layers.
        decoder_dims: Dimensions of decoder layers.
        K: Latent dimension for factorization.
        L: Number of encoder layers.
        """
        self.L = L
        self.K = K
        self.c_in = c_in
        self.c_out = c_out
        
        # Shared Cross-Scene Weight Matrices (CSWM)
        # Shape: [L, K, c_out]
        self.C_swm = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(L, K, c_out) * 0.02)
        
        # Shared Hypernetworks (Parameter Generators)
        # Each G_l generates a matrix of shape [c_in, K]
        # Simplified hypernetwork: Linear(256, 256*K) -> reshape
        self.G = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Sequential(
                nn.Linear(256, 256),
                nn.ReLU(),
                nn.Linear(256, c_in * K)
            ) for _ in range(L)
        ])
        
        # Global Decoder
        self.decoder = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.Linear(c_out + 24, decoder_dims[0]), # 24 = 4 * 6 (view dir PE)
            nn.Linear(decoder_dims[0], decoder_dims[1])
        ])
        
        # Scene-specific parameters (initialized when a new scene arrives)
        self.scene_params = {} # Key: scene_id, Value: {'z': [], 'C_coeff': []}

    def get_encoder_weights(self, scene_id, layer_idx):
        """
        Generate encoder weights for a specific scene and layer.
        Returns E_li = G_l(z_li) * C_li * C_l
        """
        if scene_id not in self.scene_params:
            raise ValueError("Scene parameters not initialized.")
        
        params = self.scene_params[scene_id]
        z = params['z'][layer_idx] # Shape: [256]
        C_coeff = params['C_coeff'][layer_idx] # Shape: [K, K]
        
        # 1. Generate SSWM from Hypernetwork
        # G_l output is [c_in * K], reshape to [c_in, K]
        sswm_flat = self.G[layer_idx](z)
        S_li = sswm_flat.view(self.c_in, self.K)
        
        # 2. Get CSWM
        C_l = self.C_swm[layer_idx] # Shape: [K, c_out]
        
        # 3. Apply Coefficient Matrix
        # C'_li = C_li * C_l
        C_prime_li = torch.matmul(C_coeff, C_l) # Shape: [K, c_out]
        
        # 4. Final Weight
        # E_li = S_li * C'_li
        E_li = torch.matmul(S_li, C_prime_li) # Shape: [c_in, c_out]
        
        return E_li

    def forward_encoder(self, x_pos, scene_id, layer_idx, skip_input=None):
        """
        Forward pass for one encoder layer.
        x_pos: Input tensor [batch, c_in]
        """
        E_li = self.get_encoder_weights(scene_id, layer_idx)
        x = torch.matmul(x_pos, E_li)
        
        # ReLU activation
        x = F.relu(x)
        
        # Skip connection at 5th layer (index 4)
        if layer_idx == 4 and skip_input is not None:
            x = torch.cat([x, skip_input], dim=-1)
            
        return x

    def forward_decoder(self, feature_vec, x_view):
        """
        Forward pass for decoder.
        feature_vec: [batch, c_out]
        x_view: View direction [batch, 3]
        """
        # View direction positional encoding (4-degree)
        # Simplified: assume x_view is already encoded or handled externally
        # Concatenate feature and view
        x = torch.cat([feature_vec, x_view], dim=-1)
        
        for i, layer in enumerate(self.decoder):
            x = layer(x)
            if i < len(self.decoder) - 1:
                x = F.relu(x)
        return x

    def initialize_scene(self, scene_id):
        """
        Initialize parameters for a new scene.
        """
        z_list = []
        C_coeff_list = []
        
        for l in range(self.L):
            # Random noise z ~ N(0, 1)
            z = torch.randn(256)
            z_list.append(z)
            
            # Learnable coefficient matrix C_li
            C_coeff = nn.Parameter(torch.eye(self.K))
            C_coeff_list.append(C_coeff)
            
        self.scene_params[scene_id] = {
            'z': z_list,
            'C_coeff': C_coeff_list
        }

    def compute_loss(self, model_t, model_t_minus_1, scene_id, rays_t, occupancy_grid, tau=3.0, alpha=1.0, gamma=1.0):
        """
        Compute the total loss for continual learning.
        """
        loss_distill = 0.0
        loss_new = 0.0
        
        # 1. Distillation for previous scenes (if scene_id < t, we iterate over all i < t)
        # Here we assume we are computing loss for a specific previous scene i
        # In practice, this loop runs over all i < t
        
        # Sample points on surface for scene i using occupancy grid
        # This is a placeholder for the specific sampling logic described
        points_i, rays_i = sample_on_surface(occupancy_grid, tau)
        
        # Forward pass through M_t and M_{t-1}
        # Note: In implementation, M_t and M_{t-1} share structure but have different params
        # For simplicity, we assume we can switch params or have two instances
        
        # Get density and radiance from M_t and M_{t-1} for scene i
        sigma_t, c_t = model_t.forward_rays(rays_i, scene_id) # Returns density and feature
        sigma_tm1, c_tm1 = model_t_minus_1.forward_rays(rays_i, scene_id)
        
        # Render pixels
        C_r_t = model_t.render(rays_i, sigma_t, c_t)
        C_r_tm1 = model_t_minus_1.render(rays_i, sigma_tm1, c_tm1)
        
        # L_ic_sigma
        L_ic_sigma = torch.mean((c_t - c_tm1)**2) + alpha * torch.mean((sigma_t - sigma_tm1)**2)
        
        # L_C(r)
        L_Cr = torch.mean((C_r_t - C_r_tm1)**2)
        
        # L_idis
        # Indicator function I(sigma > tau)
        indicator = (sigma_t > tau).float()
        
        # Beta1, Beta2 are learnable parameters in the model
        beta1 = model_t.beta1
        beta2 = model_t.beta2
        
        L_idis = beta1 * indicator * L_ic_sigma + beta2 * L_Cr + torch.log(beta1) + torch.log(beta2)
        loss_distill += torch.mean(L_idis)
        
        # 2. Loss for new scene t
        # rays_t are from the new scene
        sigma_t_new, c_t_new = model_t.forward_rays(rays_t, scene_id)
        C_r_t_new = model_t.render(rays_t, sigma_t_new, c_t_new)
        
        # Ground truth rays_t
        C_r_gt = rays_t.color
        
        L_Cr_new = torch.mean((C_r_t_new - C_r_gt)**2)
        loss_new = gamma * L_Cr_new
        
        return loss_distill + loss_new
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Overfitting with Small K:** Setting a smaller $K$ reduces parameters but can lead to overfitting of SSWMs if not constrained by the hypernetwork and coefficient matrices [S1].
- **Blank Region Sampling:** Random sampling for distillation can hit blank regions (density $\approx 0$), leading to inefficient knowledge distillation. The occupancy grid and threshold $\tau$ are critical to avoid this [S1].
- **Domain Gaps:** Large domain gaps between scenes limit the linear combination of CSWM and SSWM. The coefficient matrix $C_{li}$ is introduced to mitigate this [S1].
- **Warm-up:** Skipping warm-up for new scenes may result in suboptimal performance; 2000 steps of single-scene training are recommended [S1].

## Subtopics

### Uncertain Surface radiance field Distillation (USD)
A single-stage distillation method suitable for continual learning of multiple 3D scenes, unlike Progressive Volume Distillation (PVD) which is multi-stage and overfits to single scenes [S1].

### Factorized NeRF MLP
The core architectural modification where the MLP is split into scene-specific and cross-scene shared weights, enabling memory-efficient scaling [S1].

### Hypernetwork-based Parameter Generation
Using a hypernetwork to generate scene-specific weights from random noise, allowing for compact representation and easy addition of new scenes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Training is approximately 1.2x slower than vanilla NeRF due to the USD strategy [S1]. Rendering speed is equivalent to vanilla NeRF as weights are pre-generated [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Parameters depend most on the number of layers $L$ rather than the number of scenes $N$. Vanilla NeRF parameters grow linearly with $N$. SCARF achieves a 16.4% compression rate for 8 scenes and 0.03% for 50 scenes compared to vanilla NeRF [S1].
- **Performance:** On NeRF-Synthetic dataset, PSNR degradation for previous scenes is minor (e.g., Lego drops from 32.80 to 32.49 after training on Ship) [S1].
- **Strengths:** Memory-efficient, handles vast domain gaps between scenes, single-stage distillation, orthogonal to other NeRF extensions [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires pre-extraction of occupancy grids, slightly slower training, sensitive to hyperparameters like $K$ and $\tau$ [S1].
- **When to use:** When learning multiple 3D scenes sequentially with limited memory and no access to previous training data.
- **When not to use:** When only a single scene is needed, or when high-frequency details within a single scene are the primary focus (consider [BungeeNeRF](15547-nerf-casting.md) for multi-scale single scene) [S1].

## Background
SCARF was proposed by Y. Wang et al. in a preprint submitted in 2024, titled "SCARF: Scalable Continual Learning Framework for Memory-efficient Multiple Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of previous continual learning works like MEIL-NeRF and CL-NeRF, which focused on single scenes with multiple sequences, by focusing on continual learning across different 3D scenes with vast domain gaps [S1]. It builds on the concept of HyperNetworks [HDL16] and addresses issues found in Progressive Volume Distillation (PVD) [FXW*23] by introducing a single-stage distillation method [S1].
