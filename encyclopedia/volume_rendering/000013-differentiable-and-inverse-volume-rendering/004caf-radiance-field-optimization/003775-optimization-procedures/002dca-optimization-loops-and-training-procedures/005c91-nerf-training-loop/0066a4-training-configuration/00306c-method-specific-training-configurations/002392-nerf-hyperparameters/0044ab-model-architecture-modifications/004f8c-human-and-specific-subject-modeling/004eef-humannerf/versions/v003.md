# HumanNeRF

Also called: HumanNeRF

## Overview
HumanNeRF is a generalizable neural radiance field method for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans from sparse multi-view inputs, designed to generalize to unseen subjects and camera settings without per-scene training [S1]. It distinguishes itself from prior methods by combining a generalizable neural radiance field with an aggregated pixel-alignment feature extraction and a novel neural appearance blending scheme, addressing the blur artifacts and geometric discontinuities common in implicit texture representations [S1]. Unlike methods requiring dense inputs or costly per-frame optimization, HumanNeRF leverages a pre-trained network on large human datasets to enable efficient generalization, with an optional fast per-scene fine-tuning stage to refine details [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $K$ synchronized videos of a performer captured at different viewpoints (preferably 360°) with $T$ frames, denoted as $I = \{I_{k,t}\}$, where each image $I_k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 4}$ includes the mask as the last channel [S1].
*   **Output:** Synthesized RGB images and depth/volume density for novel viewpoints and arbitrary poses [S1].

**Data Structures and Networks:**
The method employs five primary neural components:

1.  **Feature Extractor Network ($U$):** A U-Net architecture with 8 layers using ConvBnReLU2D (CRB2D) blocks (kernel $k=3$, stride $s=1$, dilation $d=1$) [S1].
    *   Layers: Down0-Down3, Up1-Up3 [S1].
    *   Channel mappings: $4/32, 32/64, 64/128, 128/256, 256/256, 256/128, 128/64, 64/32$ [S1].
    *   Output: 2D feature map $f^k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ [S1].

2.  **Feature Blending Network ($MLP_B$):**
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR6 [S1].
    *   PE0: Positional Encoding mapping $6/54$ (view direction) and $3/27$ (angle) [S1].
    *   LR0: Input $54+27+32*6$ channels, output 256 [S1].
    *   LR1-4: $256/256$ [S1].
    *   LR5: $256/128$ [S1].
    *   LR6: $128/6$ [S1].
    *   Input: View direction $\vec{v}$, angle $\theta$, PE0 outputs, features $f$ [S1].

3.  **Deformation Network ($MLP_d$):**
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR5 [S1].
    *   PE0: Positional Encoding mapping $24/216$ (distances $R_d$) [S1].
    *   LR0: Input $216+72+32$ channels, output 256 [S1].
    *   LR1-4: $256/256$ [S1].
    *   LR5: $128/3$ [S1].
    *   Input: $R_d$ (distances to 24 SMPL joints), $R_v$ (directions to 24 SMPL joints, size 72), $F$ (blended feature) [S1].

4.  **Generalizable Dynamic Neural Radiance Field ($\Phi$):**
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR8, Density $\sigma$, PE1, LR7, LR8, Color $c$ [S1].
    *   PE0: $3/63$ (position $x$) [S1].
    *   LR0: $63/256$ [S1].
    *   LR1-4: $256/256$ [S1].
    *   LR5: Input $27+256$, output 256 [S1].
    *   LR6: $256/256$ [S1].
    *   Density $\sigma$: $256/1$ [S1].
    *   PE1: $3/27$ (view direction $d$) [S1].
    *   LR7: Input $256+27+32$, output 256 [S1].
    *   LR8: $256/128$ [S1].
    *   Color $c$: $128/3$ [S1].
    *   Input: Position $x$, view direction $d$, PE0, LR2, PE0, LR2, PE1, $F$, LR7, LR8 [S1].

5.  **Appearance Blending Network ($MLP_A$):**
    *   Layers: LR0-LR6, Blending weights $W$ [S1].
    *   LR0: Input $(32+3)*2$, output 256 [S1].
    *   LR1-5: $256/256$ [S1].
    *   LR6: $128/3$ [S1].
    *   Blending weights $W$: $128/3$ [S1].
    *   Input: $f_{r,0}^r, f_{l,0}^l$ (two adjacent image features and occlusion maps) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** For each input view $k$, extract 2D feature maps using the U-Net network $U$:
    $$ f^k = U(I^k) $$
    [S1]

2.  **Aggregated Pixel Alignment Feature:** For a 3D spatial point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, project it into view $k$ at $q_k \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and fetch the feature vector $f_{q_k}^k$. Compute the aggregated feature $F_p$ as a weighted summation:
    $$ F_p = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_q^k f_{q_k}^k $$
    where the blending weights $w_q^k$ are estimated by $MLP_B$:
    $$ w_q^k = MLP_B(\vec{v}, \theta_q^k, f_q^k) $$
    Here, $\vec{v}$ is the viewing direction and $\theta_q^k$ is the angle of the viewing direction relative to the query ray of the source view [S1].

3.  **Deformation to Canonical Space:** Compute the canonical position $p'$ using the SMPL model $\mathcal{M}$ and skinning weights $w^s$ for rigid deformation, plus a non-rigid displacement from $MLP_d$:
    $$ p' = \mathcal{S}(p, \mathcal{M}, w^s) + MLP_d(R_d, R_v, F_p) $$
    $R_d \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ and $R_v \in \mathbb{R}^{72}$ model the distances and directions between $p$ and the 24 joints of the SMPL skeleton [S1].

4.  **Radiance Field Query:** Query the generalizable neural radiance field $\Phi$ at $p'$ with view direction $d$ and feature $F$ to obtain volume density $\sigma$ and color $c$:
    $$ (\sigma, c) = \Phi(p', d, F) $$
    [S1]

5.  **Volume Rendering:** Render the final color $C(r)$ along a ray $r$ using standard differentiable volume rendering:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ and $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S1].

6.  **Appearance Blending (Optional Refinement):** To refine texture details and address artifacts, aggregate colors from neighboring views using $MLP_A$. The network takes two adjacent image features and occlusion maps as input to predict blending weights $W$, which are applied to combine the rendered color with neighboring view colors [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **SMPL Joints:** 24 joints used for computing $R_d$ and $R_v$ [S1].
*   **Fine-tuning Duration:** 300 frames for a specific performer [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Multi-person/Interaction:** The method relies on a regressed parametric human model (SMPL), limiting it to single-person setups. It fails on multi-person or human-object interactions [S1].
*   **Lighting Variation:** No explicit lighting model is used. Significant brightness or color changes between views cause severe artifacts. Switching nearest views during appearance blending causes jumping artifacts, especially with high brightness variance [S1].
*   **Generalization Limits:** Capability is limited because human dataset distributions only cover a small portion of human dynamics and appearances [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method is significantly more efficient than per-scene methods. On the 'batman' video (300 frames), full training takes 1.2 hours, compared to Neural Body (6.7h), Neural Volumes (8.4h), and ST-NeRF (9.5h) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** On six input views, the method achieves PSNR = 32.59, SSIM = 0.9817, and LPIPS = 0.0304 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance degrades as the number of input views decreases; average error increases rapidly with fewer cameras (e.g., 2 views vs 6 views) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient generalization to unseen identities and poses; requires only ~1 hour of fine-tuning for improved results on unseen identities; handles large pose deformations [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to single-person setups; sensitive to lighting variations; generalization is constrained by the diversity of training data [S1].
*   **When to use:** Dynamic human rendering from sparse multi-view inputs, specifically targeting unseen identities and poses with efficient generalization [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Multi-person scenes, human-object interactions, or scenes with significant lighting variations between views [S1].

## Background
HumanNeRF was introduced in the paper "HumanNeRF: Efficiently Generated Human Radiance Field from Sparse Inputs" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous neural human representations that required dense multi-view inputs and costly per-scene training, which restricted them to static models or infeasible per-frame training for dynamic humans [S1]. The method builds on the idea of generalizable neural radiance fields, analogous to how IBRNet assists NeRF, by employing aggregated pixel-alignment features and a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field [S1].
