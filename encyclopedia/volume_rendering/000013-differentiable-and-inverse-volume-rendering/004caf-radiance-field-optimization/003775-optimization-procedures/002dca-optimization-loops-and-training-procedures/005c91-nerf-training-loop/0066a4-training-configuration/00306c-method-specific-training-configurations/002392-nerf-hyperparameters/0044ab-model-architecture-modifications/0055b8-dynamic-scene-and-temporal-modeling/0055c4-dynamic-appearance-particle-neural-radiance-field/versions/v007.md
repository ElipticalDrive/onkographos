# Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field

Also called: Dynamic Appearance Particle NeRF, Lagrangian dynamic model in NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field is a method for modeling dynamic scenes from monocular video data by superimposing a static grid-based radiance field with a particle-based dynamic field [S1]. It solves the problem of reconstructing time-varying scenes without requiring multi-view stereo data or prior geometric knowledge of dynamic objects [S1]. Unlike ParticleNeRF, which encodes the entire scene with particles and requires multiple views per frame, this method applies appearance particles only to dynamic elements and uses a grid-based field for efficient linear interpolation querying [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Monocular video data (a sequence of images from a single camera at different times $t$).
- **Outputs:** A dynamic radiance field $V(x, t)$ capable of rendering novel views at arbitrary times $t$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Static Field ($V_s$):** A time-independent grid-based model (e.g., a canonical feature field) storing static scene information [S1].
2.  **Dynamic Field ($V_{td}$):** A particle-based representation consisting of an ensemble of particles, each carrying appearance features and motion parameters [S1].
3.  **Weight Map ($w_t$):** A binary indicator field where $w_t(x) = 1$ if the dynamic field is effective at location $x$ and time $t$, and $0$ otherwise [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total radiance field is defined as a superposition of static and dynamic components [S1]:
$$
V(x, t) = (1 - w_t(x))V_s(x) + w_t(x)V_{td}(x)
$$
where $V_s(x)$ is the static field and $V_{td}(x)$ is the dynamic field [S1].

The dynamic field $V_{td}$ is formulated as a convolution with a Dirac function to integrate over finite volumes [S1]:
$$
V_{td}(x) = (V_{td} * \delta)(x) = \int V_{td}(x')\delta(x - x')d\nu(x')
$$
where $\delta(\cdot)$ is the Dirac function ($\delta(r) = 0$ for $r \neq 0$ and $\int \delta(r)d\nu = 1$) and $\nu(\cdot)$ is the volume integration variable [S1].

For implementation, the Dirac function is replaced by a kernel $W$ with finite support to quantize the field [S1]:
$$
W(x) = 0 \quad \text{when } \|x\| \text{ exceeds a small radius}
$$
$$
\int W(x)d\nu(x) = 1
$$

Rendering a pixel color $C(r, t)$ at time $t$ involves casting a ray $r(s) = o + sd$ from camera center $o$ and integrating along the ray from near bound $s_n$ to far bound $s_f$ [S1]:
$$
C(r, t) = \int_{s_n}^{s_f} T(s)\sigma(r(s), t)c(r(s), d, t)ds
$$
where $T(s) = \exp\left(-\int_{s_n}^{s} \sigma(r(s'), t)ds'\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance, $\sigma$ is volume density, and $c$ is emitted radiance [S1].

**Notation Convention:**
Time $t$ is denoted in subscripts on the right-hand side of the superposition equation (e.g., $V_{td}$) to clarify that the dynamic component consists of an ensemble of particles, where the time argument is fixed for the instantaneous status construction [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialize Static Field:** Construct a time-independent grid-based model $V_s(x)$ using the static parts of the scene [S1].
2.  **Initialize Dynamic Particles:** Create an ensemble of particles representing dynamic elements. Each particle stores appearance features and motion parameters [S1].
3.  **Define Weight Map:** Initialize $w_t(x)$ to indicate regions where dynamic particles are active [S1].
4.  **Particle Motion Network:** Train a network to predict particle motion across time frames, allowing the dynamic field $V_{td}$ to evolve continuously [S1].
5.  **Field Query:** For a given query point $x$ and time $t$:
    a. Query the static field $V_s(x)$ [S1].
    b. Query the dynamic field $V_{td}(x)$ using the kernel $W$ for efficient linear interpolation from the particle ensemble [S1].
    c. Combine using the superposition formula: $V(x, t) = (1 - w_t(x))V_s(x) + w_t(x)V_{td}(x)$ [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Cast rays and integrate the combined field to render images [S1].

**Parameters:**
-   **Kernel Radius:** The small radius for the kernel $W$ where $W(x) \neq 0$. Not given in the sources.
-   **Weight $w_t(x)$:** Binary value (0 or 1) determining the effectiveness of the dynamic field at $(x, t)$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
-   **Boundary Handling:** The kernel $W$ has finite support; queries outside the particle radius return zero contribution from the dynamic field [S1].
-   **Monocular Ambiguity:** Since the method uses monocular video, depth and motion disambiguation rely on the particle motion network's ability to model dynamics without prior geometric knowledge [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed in "Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field" [S1]. It builds on the Lagrangian dynamic model, embedding it into the radiance field representation. It differs from ParticleNeRF [1] by focusing on dynamic elements only and using a grid-based field for efficient querying, whereas ParticleNeRF encodes the entire scene with particles and requires 20 camera views per time frame [S1]. The technique is not tightly coupled to a specific architecture and can be integrated into frameworks like InstantNGP [S1].
