# Surface Smoothness Regularization

Also called: Depth Smoothness Regularization, Surface Smoothness on Integrated Unobserved Rays

## Overview
Surface Smoothness Regularization imposes piece-wise smoothness constraints on rendered depth from unobserved rays to alleviate spatial ambiguity and stabilize the joint optimization of blur kernels and radiance fields in sparse-view DeRF [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF regularization by integrating "hidden rays" derived from the estimated blur kernel alongside standard "unseen rays," specifically targeting the coupled uncertainty of defocus and geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of target camera poses $\{P_i^{target}\}$, an estimated blur kernel $h$, and the current radiance field parameters.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}_{ss}$ used in the total training loss.

**Data Structures**
*   **Target Poses:** $P_i^{target} = [R_i^{target} | t_i^{target}] \in SE(3)$.
*   **Sampled Poses:** $S_P$, a set of camera poses $[R|t]$.
*   **Integrated Unobserved Rays:** $r_{iu}$, a union of hidden rays $r_q$ and unseen rays $r_{us}$.
*   **Depth Map:** $\hat{d}$, computed per ray.
*   **Color Map:** $\hat{C}$, computed per ray.

**Step-by-Step Method**

1.  **Define Sample Space for Unseen Rays:**
    Determine the bounding box for camera translation $t$ based on target poses:
    $$ t_{min} = \min(\{t_i^{target}\}), \quad t_{max} = \max(\{t_i^{target}\}) $$
    Sample translation $t$ uniformly within $[t_{min}, t_{max}]$ [S1].

2.  **Compute Mean Focus Point:**
    Calculate the mean focus point $\bar{p}_f$ by solving a least squares problem to find the central point of the scene [S1].

3.  **Sample Camera Rotation:**
    Calculate the normalized mean over the up axes of all target poses, denoted $\bar{p}_u$. Sample a rotation matrix $R$ using:
    $$ S_R|t = \{R(\bar{p}_u, \bar{p}_f + \epsilon, t) \mid \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.125)\} $$
    where $\epsilon$ is a small jitter value added to the focus point to ensure the camera roughly focuses on the scene center [S1].

4.  **Generate Unseen Rays ($r_{us}$):**
    Form the set of sampled camera poses:
    $$ S_P = \{[R|t] \mid R \sim S_R|t, t \sim S_t\} $$
    Generate rays $r_{us}$ from these poses [S1].

5.  **Generate Hidden Rays ($r_q$):**
    Derive rays $r_q$ from the estimated blur kernel $h$. The kernel transforms rays to approximate the color composition process of the blur. These rays are defined as:
    $$ r_q \sim h(r) $$
    where $r$ represents the main rays. These hidden rays provide supplementary information exclusively present in blurry inputs [S1].

6.  **Form Integrated Unobserved Rays:**
    Combine the two sets:
    $$ r_{iu} = \{r_q, r_{us}\} $$
    Note that $r_{us}$ remains unchanged during training, while $r_q$ changes as the blur kernel $h$ is optimized [S1].

7.  **Compute Expected Depth and Color:**
    For each ray $r \in r_{iu}$, compute the expected depth $\hat{d}$ and color $\hat{C}$:
    $$ \hat{d} = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma(r(t))t \, dt $$
    $$ \hat{C} = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma(r(t))c(r(t)) \, dt $$
    where $\sigma$ is density, $c$ is color, and $t_n, t_f$ are near and far bounds [S1].

8.  **Calculate Weighted Depth Differences:**
    For a patch of size $S_{ptc} \times S_{ptc}$ centered at ray $r_{i,j}$, compute horizontal and vertical weighted depth differences:
    $$ \Delta \hat{d}_x(r_{i,j}) = \omega_{i+1,j} (\hat{d}(r_{i+1,j}) - \hat{d}(r_{i,j}))^2 $$
    $$ \Delta \hat{d}_y(r_{i,j}) = \omega_{i,j+1} (\hat{d}(r_{i,j+1}) - \hat{d}(r_{i,j}))^2 $$
    The weights $\omega$ are based on pixel-wise color difference:
    $$ \omega_{i+k,j+l} = \exp(-|\hat{C}(r_{i,j}) - \hat{C}(r_{i+k,j+l})|) $$
    This ensures smoothness is enforced where colors are similar, preserving edges [S1].

9.  **Compute Loss:**
    Sum the differences over all rays in $r_{iu}$ and all positions in the patch:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{ss}\{r_{iu}\} = \sum_{r \in r_{iu}} \sum_{i,j=1}^{S_{ptc}-1} (\Delta \hat{d}_x(r_{i,j}) + \Delta \hat{d}_y(r_{i,j})) $$

```python
def compute_surface_smoothness_loss(target_poses, blur_kernel, radiance_field, patch_size=5):
    """
    Computes the Surface Smoothness Regularization loss.
    
    Args:
        target_poses: List of SE(3) poses [R|t] used for training.
        blur_kernel: The current estimated blur kernel h.
        radiance_field: The radiance field model (NeRF/DeRF).
        patch_size: Integer size of the depth patch for smoothness calculation.
        
    Returns:
        Scalar loss value.
    """
    # 1. Define Sample Space
    t_min = min(t for _, t in target_poses)
    t_max = max(t for _, t in target_poses)
    
    # 2. Compute Mean Focus Point (Least Squares)
    # [G] Placeholder for specific least squares implementation details not provided in source.
    # Assuming a function solve_least_squares_focus(target_poses) exists.
    p_f = solve_least_squares_focus(target_poses)
    
    # 3. Compute Mean Up Axis
    # [G] Assuming normalized up axes are extracted from R matrices.
    up_axes = [get_up_axis(R) for R, _ in target_poses]
    p_u = normalize(mean(up_axes))
    
    # 4. Generate Unseen Rays (r_us)
    unseen_rays = []
    num_samples = 100 # [G] Arbitrary sample count for implementation completeness; source does not specify N.
    for _ in range(num_samples):
        # Sample translation
        t = random_uniform_vector(t_min, t_max)
        
        # Sample rotation with jitter
        epsilon = random_normal(0, 0.125)
        R = generate_rotation(p_u, p_f + epsilon, t)
        
        pose = [R, t]
        # Generate ray from pose
        ray = generate_ray_from_pose(pose)
        unseen_rays.append(ray)
        
    # 5. Generate Hidden Rays (r_q)
    # [G] Implementation of ray transformation via blur kernel h.
    # Source states r_q ~ h(r). We sample main rays r and transform them.
    main_rays = [generate_ray_from_pose(p) for p in target_poses]
    hidden_rays = []
    for r in main_rays:
        r_q = transform_ray_by_blur_kernel(r, blur_kernel)
        hidden_rays.append(r_q)
        
    # 6. Integrated Unobserved Rays
    integrated_rays = hidden_rays + unseen_rays
    
    # 7 & 8 & 9. Compute Loss
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # [G] Iterate over rays in batches or patches. 
    # Source sums over r in r_iu and i,j in patch.
    # We assume a grid structure or spatial hashing to find neighbors (i+1,j) and (i,j+1).
    # For simplicity, we assume rays are sorted/spatially indexed.
    
    # Create a spatial map for neighbor lookup
    ray_map = {ray.id: ray for ray in integrated_rays}
    
    for ray in integrated_rays:
        # [G] Placeholder for patch extraction logic.
        # In practice, rays are projected to image plane or voxel grid.
        # Here we assume we can access neighbors r_{i+1,j} and r_{i,j+1}.
        neighbors = get_spatial_neighbors(ray, integrated_rays, patch_size)
        
        if not neighbors:
            continue
            
        # Compute depth and color for current ray
        d_curr, c_curr = radiance_field.integrate(ray)
        
        # Horizontal neighbor (i+1, j)
        r_h = neighbors.get('right')
        if r_h:
            d_h, c_h = radiance_field.integrate(r_h)
            weight_h = exp(-abs(c_curr - c_h))
            diff_x = weight_h * (d_h - d_curr)**2
            total_loss += diff_x
            
        # Vertical neighbor (i, j+1)
        r_v = neighbors.get('down')
        if r_v:
            d_v, c_v = radiance_field.integrate(r_v)
            weight_v = exp(-abs(c_curr - c_v))
            diff_y = weight_v * (d_v - d_curr)**2
            total_loss += diff_y
            
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters**
*   **Jitter Standard Deviation:** $0.125$ for the normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.125)$ [S1].
*   **Patch Size ($S_{ptc}$):** Not given in the sources.
*   **Number of Unseen Ray Samples:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Blur Kernel Convergence:** Hidden rays $r_q$ depend on the blur kernel $h$. If $h$ is poorly estimated, hidden rays may provide misleading smoothness constraints [S1].
*   **Spatial Ambiguity:** The method specifically addresses ambiguity in sparse views; it may be redundant or computationally wasteful in dense-view settings where geometry is well-constrained [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The summation limits $i,j=1$ to $S_{ptc}-1$ imply that boundary rays in the patch do not contribute to the loss in the same way, or require padding. Not given in the sources.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead proportional to the number of sampled unseen and hidden rays and the patch size. Requires additional ray marching for unobserved rays.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the set of integrated unobserved rays and their associated depth/color values during the backward pass.
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes joint optimization of blur and geometry in sparse-view DeRF; alleviates spatial ambiguity by leveraging unobserved rays [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate estimation of the blur kernel for hidden rays; requires careful sampling of the camera pose space [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Sparse-view DeRF training where spatial ambiguity is high and blur kernel optimization is coupled with radiance field optimization [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Dense-view settings where geometry is well-constrained by observed rays; non-blurry scenes where hidden rays provide no additional information [S1].

## Background
Originated in the context of Deblurred Neural Radiance Fields (DeRF) from sparse views. The method was introduced in "Sparse-DeRF: Deblurred Neural Radiance Fields From Sparse View" (2025) to address the difficulty of regularizing complex joint optimization in DeRF, where standard NeRF regularization fails [S1]. It builds on the concept of using unseen rays for regularization as seen in RegNeRF [6], but extends it with blur-kernel-derived hidden rays [S1].
