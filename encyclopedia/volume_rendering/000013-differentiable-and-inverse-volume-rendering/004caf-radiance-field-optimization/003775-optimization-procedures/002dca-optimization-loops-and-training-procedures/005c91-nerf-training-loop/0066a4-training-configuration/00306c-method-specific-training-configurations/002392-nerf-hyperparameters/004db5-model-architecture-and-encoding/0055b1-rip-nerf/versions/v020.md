# Rip-NeRF

Also called: Rip-NeRF25k

## Overview
Rip-NeRF is a hybrid radiance field method that renders high-fidelity, anti-aliased images by combining cone casting with anisotropic 3D Gaussian characterization [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Zip-NeRF by using Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids to enable efficient anisotropic area-sampling, whereas Zip-NeRF relies on a multi-sampling mechanism that is effective but computationally inefficient [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated multi-view 2D images.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel colors $\hat{C}(r)$ for new viewpoints.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ripmap Encoding:** A hierarchical feature grid.
    *   **Base Level ($R_{0,0}$):** Learnable tensor with shape $H \times W \times C$. Default: $512 \times 512 \times 16$ [S1].
    *   **Derived Levels:** Generated from the base level using average pooling to ensure compactness and consistency [S1].
2.  **Platonic Solid Projection:** A geometric structure used to project 3D Gaussians. Default: Icosahedron with 10 unparalleled faces [S1].
3.  **Anisotropic 3D Gaussian:** Characterizes each conical frustum, parameterized by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].
4.  **Tiny MLP ($F_\theta$):** Maps aggregated features to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
5.  **Binary Occupancy Grid:** Used to skip samples in empty areas [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Cone Casting and Frustum Division:**
    For each pixel, cast a cone and divide it into multiple conical frustums [S1]. This avoids discrete point-based sampling of continuous signals [S1].

2.  **Gaussian Characterization:**
    Characterize each conical frustum as an anisotropic 3D Gaussian with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].

3.  **Platonic Solid Projection:**
    Project the 3D Gaussian onto the $n$ faces $\{P_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ of a Platonic solid (default: icosahedron, $n=10$) [S1].
    For each face $P_i$, compute the projected 2D Gaussian parameters $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj}^i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj}^i)$ using the projection matrix $M_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj}^i = M_i^T \boldsymbol{\mu} $$
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj}^i = M_i^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} M_i $$
    [S1].

4.  **Ripmap Encoding Query:**
    For each projected 2D Gaussian on face $P_i$, query a feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i$ from the Ripmap Encoding using tetra-linear interpolation [S1].
    The interpolation position $(p_x, p_y)$ is determined by $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{proj}^i$ [S1].
    The interpolation levels $(l_x, l_y)$ are determined by $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{proj}^i$ [S1].
    The hyper-parameter $w$ in the level calculation is set to 2.0 to adjust the probability mass coverage [S1].
    *Note: The specific interpolation formula (Eq. 2 and Eq. 3 in [S1]) is not fully detailed in the provided text, but the inputs are defined.* [S1].

5.  **Feature Aggregation:**
    Concatenate all feature vectors $\{\mathbf{f}_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ from the Platonic solid faces and the encoded view direction $\mathbf{d}$ to form the final feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ [S1].

6.  **Density and Color Estimation:**
    Pass the final feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ through the tiny MLP $F_\theta$ to estimate density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

7.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Render the pixel color using the standard volume rendering equation:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i, \quad T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_i), \quad \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i) $$
    where $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Optimize the system end-to-end using a photometric loss between rendered and observed images [S1].
    The loss is scaled by the area of the pixel footprint on the image plane (area loss $L_{area}$) [S1].

*   **Hyper-parameter $w$:** 2.0 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 120k (standard Rip-NeRF), 25k (Rip-NeRF25k variant) [S1].
*   **Weight Decay:** $1 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   **Initial Learning Rate:** $2 \times 10^{-3}$, modulated by MultiStepLR [S1].
*   **Ripmap Learning Rate:** Scaled up by 10.0 times [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Empty Space:** Use a binary occupancy grid (via NerfAcc library) to skip samples in empty areas [S1].
*   **Precision:** The method relies on tetra-linear interpolation, which may introduce artifacts if the Ripmap levels are not properly derived or if the interpolation coordinates are out of bounds [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The method assumes calibrated multi-view images. Uncalibrated inputs may lead to incorrect Gaussian parameterization [S1].

## Background
Rip-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Rip-NeRF: Anti-aliasing Radiance Fields with Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids" [S1]. It builds on the hybrid representation approach, combining the efficiency of explicit representations (3D Gaussians) with the flexibility of implicit representations (NeRF) [S1]. The method was developed to address the limitations of discrete point-based sampling and the inefficiency of multi-sampling mechanisms in existing methods like Zip-NeRF [S1].
