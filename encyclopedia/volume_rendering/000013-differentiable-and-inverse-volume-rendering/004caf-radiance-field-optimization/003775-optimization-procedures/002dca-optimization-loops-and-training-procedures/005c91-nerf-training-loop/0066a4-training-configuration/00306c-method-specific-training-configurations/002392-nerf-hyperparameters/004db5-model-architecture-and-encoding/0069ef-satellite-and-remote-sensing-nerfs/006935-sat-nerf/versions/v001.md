# Sat-NeRF

Also called: Sat-NeRF model, Satellite Neural Radiance Field

## Overview
Sat-NeRF is a neural rendering method adapted for multi-view satellite photogrammetry that simultaneously learns the geometry and appearance of permanent structures while explicitly modeling shadows and transient objects (e.g., vehicles, vegetation) [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by assuming a Lambertian surface, omitting viewing-angle dependence, and using native Rational Polynomial Camera (RPC) models for ray casting instead of pinhole approximations [S1]. Unlike indoor NeRFs, it incorporates a shadow-aware irradiance model and uncertainty weighting to handle multi-date imagery where lighting and transient occlusions vary significantly [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
The model takes a set of multi-date satellite images as input. For each training iteration, rays are cast into the scene volume. The network function $F$ takes three inputs:
1.  $\mathbf{x}$: A 3-valued vector of spatial coordinates of a point on the ray [S1].
2.  $\boldsymbol{\omega}$: A 3-valued direction vector encoding the direction of solar rays, extracted from the azimuth and elevation angles in the image metadata [S1].
3.  $\mathbf{t}_j$: An $N(t)$-valued learned embedding vector specific to image index $j$, used to featurize transient elements not explained by the sun's position. In the source, $N(t)=4$ [S1].

The network outputs a tuple $(\sigma, c_a, s, a, \beta)$:
*   $\sigma$: Scalar volume density at $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
*   $c_a$: Albedo RGB color, dependent only on geometry ($\mathbf{x}$) [S1].
*   $s$: Shadow-aware shading scalar, dependent on $\mathbf{x}$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$, taking values in $[0, 1]$ [S1].
*   $a$: Ambient RGB color, independent of geometry, dependent on $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ [S1].
*   $\beta$: Uncertainty coefficient related to the probability that the color is explained by a transient object [S1].

**Data Structures and Network Layout**
The architecture consists of three parts [S1]:
1.  **Main Block**: A fully-connected block with $h$ channels per layer. It predicts $\sigma$ and $c_a$. It uses SIREN layers with the initialization from [46] [S1].
2.  **Secondary Head**: Fewer layers with $h/2$ channels per layer. It estimates $s$ based on $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and the $h$-dimensional geometry features from the main block [S1].
3.  **Single-Layer Heads**:
    *   One head predicts $\beta$ from $\mathbf{t}_j$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ [S1].
    *   One head predicts $a$ from $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ [S1].

Typical value for $h$ is 512, adjusted based on resolution and area size [S1].

**Activation Functions**
*   $\sigma$: Softplus [S1].
*   $\beta$: Softplus (yields smoother optimization than ReLU) [S1].
*   $c_a, s, a$: Sigmoid (outputs are normalized RGB values in $[0, 1]$) [S1].

**Ray Casting and Sampling**
Rays are cast using the Rational Polynomial Camera (RPC) model of each input image directly in object space, providing independence from the specific satellite system [S1]. Prior to training, a bundle adjustment is applied to the RPC models to boost performance and optionally extract a sparse point cloud for depth supervision [S1]. Each ray $\mathbf{r}$ is discretized into 64 uniformly distributed 3D points [S1].

**Color Rendering**
The color $c(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}, \mathbf{t}_j)$ at point $\mathbf{x}$ is computed using a shadow-aware irradiance model [S1]:
$$ c(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}, \mathbf{t}_j) = c_a(\mathbf{x}) \cdot s(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}) + (1 - s(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega})) \cdot a(\boldsymbol{\omega}) $$
where $s \approx 1$ for sun-lit points and $s \approx 0$ for shadowed points [S1].

**Loss Function**
The total loss combines image reconstruction, solar correction, and optional depth supervision [S1].

1.  **Main Image Loss with Uncertainty**:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{img} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in R} \sum_{i=1}^{N_r} \beta_i (\mathbf{c}_i - \hat{\mathbf{c}}_i)^2 $$
    where $R$ is the batch of viewing rays, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the ground truth color, $\hat{\mathbf{c}}_i$ is the rendered color, and $\beta_i$ is the uncertainty coefficient [S1]. Note: The source text describes $\beta$ as weighting the impact of transient objects to minimize their contribution to the loss, effectively down-weighting inconsistent pixels [S1]. The specific formula in the source notes refers to equation (8) incorporating $\beta$.

2.  **Solar Correction Term** (Optional):
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{SC}(\mathcal{R}_{SC}) = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}_{SC}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{SC}} (T_i - s_i)^2 + \sum_{i=1}^{N_{SC}} \left(1 - \sum_{i=1}^{N_{SC}} T_i \alpha_i s_i \right)^2 $$
    where $\mathcal{R}_{SC}$ is a secondary batch of rays following the solar direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}$, $T_i$ is transmittance, $\alpha_i$ is opacity, and $s_i$ is shading [S1].

3.  **Depth Supervision Term** (Optional):
    Uses sparse 3D points from the bundle adjustment [S1]. Weight $\lambda_{DS}$ [S1].

Total Loss:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{img} + \lambda_{SC} \mathcal{L}_{SC} + \lambda_{DS} \mathcal{L}_{DS} $$

**Parameters**

*   $\lambda_{SC}$: Weight for solar correction. Value: $0.05/3$ (if only SC added to S-NeRF baseline) or $0.1/3$ (otherwise) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Sparse Point Cloud Outliers**: The sparse point cloud from bundle adjustment may contain outliers, especially in multi-date collections, leading to slightly worse altitude Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in some areas compared to plain Sat-NeRF loss [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Limitations**: The uncertainty coefficient $\beta$ can only absorb occasional inconsistencies. It does not account for roofs under construction or roofs unusually free of parked cars, which may cause holes in the Digital Surface Model (DSM) [S1].
*   **Solar Direction Extrapolation**: The shading scalar $s$ can produce unrealistic results for solar ray directions not seen in training data. This is mitigated by the solar correction term [S1].

## Subtopics

### [SpS-NeRF](18130-sps-nerf.md)
A variant for sparse single-date satellite imagery using Semi-Global Matching (SGM) depth supervision. Choose when only 2-3 images are available and standard Sat-NeRF fails due to insufficient geometric constraints.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Training takes approximately 10 hours for 300k iterations with a single batch of rays, or ~20 hours if solar correction or depth supervision terms are added [S1].
*   **Memory**: Requires a GPU with 16 GB RAM [S1].
*   **Performance**: Sat-NeRF provides higher PSNR/SSIM and similar or smaller altitude MAE compared to S-NeRF without requiring solar correction. Adding solar correction decreases altitude MAE further [S1].
*   **Geometry Quality**: Sat-NeRF DSMs have more detailed structures and sharper edges than traditional stereo pipelines (S2P), but S2P provides more regular surfaces. Sat-NeRF produces more local irregularities (less flat roofs/roads) but is free of single-point outliers [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Robust to shadows and transient objects; uses native RPC models; high-quality novel view synthesis and 3D modeling [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Local irregularities in flat surfaces; potential holes in DSM for dynamic structures not captured by $\beta$; sensitive to outliers in bundle adjustment point clouds [S1].

## Background
Sat-NeRF was introduced by Roger Marí, Gabriele Facciolo, and Thibaud Ehret from Université Paris-Saclay and Centre Borelli in 2022 [S1]. It extends the S-NeRF method by incorporating modeling of transient objects similar to NeRF-W and using RPC camera models more adapted to satellite data [S1]. It was evaluated on the 2019 IEEE GRSS Data Fusion Contest data, using Maxar WorldView-3 images over Jacksonville, Florida [S1].
