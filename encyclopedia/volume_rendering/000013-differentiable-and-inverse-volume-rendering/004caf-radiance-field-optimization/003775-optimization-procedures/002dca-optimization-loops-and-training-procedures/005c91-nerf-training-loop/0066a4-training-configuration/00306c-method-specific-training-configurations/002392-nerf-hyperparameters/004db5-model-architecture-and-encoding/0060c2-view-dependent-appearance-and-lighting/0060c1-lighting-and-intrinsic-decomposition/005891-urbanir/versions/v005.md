# UrbanIR

Also called: Urban Scene Inverse Rendering

## Overview
UrbanIR is a method for the inverse rendering of large-scale urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination, inferring shape, albedo, visibility, and sun/sky illumination to enable relighting and night simulation [S1]. It differs from standard NeRFs by learning intrinsic attributes (albedo, normal) independent of lighting, and from other relightable methods like NeRF-OSR or FEGR by requiring only single-illumination RGB video without LiDAR or multiple cameras [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multi-frame video of a scene under single illumination with known camera motion, defined as $\{I_i, E_i, K_i\}$, where $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is the RGB image, $E_i \in SE(3)$ is the camera pose, and $K_i$ is the camera intrinsic matrix [S1].
*   **Output:** A neural scene model capable of rendering novel viewpoints under novel lighting conditions, including intrinsic maps (albedo, normal, semantics) and density [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene representation is built on Instant-NGP, a spatial hash-based voxel NeRF representation [S1].
*   **Feature Hash Table:** A dense feature hash table representing the scene, providing compactness and consistency for expansive outdoor scenes [S1].
*   **MLP Headers:** Individual Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) headers are used to decode each attribute (albedo, normal, semantics, density) given a queried feature at point $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
*   **Scene Function:** The model learns an intrinsic scene attributes field $F_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$, where $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is diffuse albedo, $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is surface normal, $\mathbf{s}$ is a semantic vector, and $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ is density [S1].
*   **Lighting Model:** The illumination is encoded as $\mathcal{L} = \{(\mathbf{L}_{\text{sun}}, \psi_{\text{sun}}, \phi_{\text{sun}}), \mathbf{L}_{\text{amb}}, \mathbf{L}_{\text{sky}}\}$, where $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sun}}$ is sun color, $\psi_{\text{sun}}, \phi_{\text{sun}}$ are azimuth and zenith, $\mathbf{L}_{\text{amb}}$ is ambient light, and $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sky}}$ is the sky texture inferred from viewing direction [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Query Features:** For a query point $\mathbf{x}$, retrieve features from the dense feature hash table [S1].
2.  **Decode Intrinsics:** Pass the retrieved features through individual MLP headers to predict albedo $\mathbf{a}$, normal $\mathbf{n}$, semantics $\mathbf{s}$, and density $\sigma$ [S1].
3.  **Volume Render Intrinsics:** Sample points along a camera ray $\mathbf{r}$. Compute alpha-composition weights $w_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$, where $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1]. Accumulate intrinsic attributes:
    $$
    \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i
    $$
    [S1].
4.  **Estimate Depth:** Compute an estimate $\hat{x}(\mathbf{r})$ of the 3D point being shaded by volume rendering depth (substituting $\hat{t} = \sum w_i t_i$) [S1].
5.  **Visibility Estimation:** To model shadows, compute the transmittance along the ray segment between the estimated point $\hat{x}(\mathbf{r})$ and the light source (sun) using volume rendering to determine visibility $V(\hat{x}, \text{sun})$ [S1]. This avoids per-scene optimized MLP heads for visibility, which are impractical because the sun position changes during relighting while training data has only one sun position [S1].
6.  **Render Color:** Combine intrinsic attributes, visibility, and the lighting model $\mathcal{L}$ using a shading function to produce the final RGB color $\mathbf{C}$:
    $$
    \mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathcal{L}), \mathcal{L})
    $$
    [S1].
7.  **Optimize:** Jointly optimize scene parameters $\theta$ and lighting parameters $\mathcal{L}$ using a loss function comprising rendering loss and other regularization terms [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Standard NeRFs bake shading, shadow, and specularity into observed color, making them non-relightable [S1].
*   Modeling visibility with a per-scene optimized MLP head is impractical because the sun position changes during relighting, but training data only has one sun position [S1].

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced by Chih-Hao Lin, Bohan Liu, Yi-Ting Chen, et al. from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It addresses the limitation of object-centric relightable methods and indoor-focused NeRFs, which fail to reconstruct large-scale urban scenes [S1]. Unlike NeRF-OSR which assumes multiple lighting sources, or FEGR/LightSim which require LiDAR or multiple cameras, UrbanIR uses only single illumination RGB video without LiDAR [S1].
