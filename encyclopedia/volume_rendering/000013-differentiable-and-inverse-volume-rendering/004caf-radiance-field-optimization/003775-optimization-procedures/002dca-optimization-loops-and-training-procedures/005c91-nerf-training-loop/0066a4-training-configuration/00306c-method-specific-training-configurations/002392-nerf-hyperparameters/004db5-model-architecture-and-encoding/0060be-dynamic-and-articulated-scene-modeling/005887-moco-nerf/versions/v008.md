# MoCo-NeRF

Also called: Motion-Oriented Compositional Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
MoCo-NeRF is a framework for free-viewpoint rendering of monocular human videos that models non-rigid body motions as radiance residual fields rather than spatial offsets, using rigid skeletal transformations as a prior [S1]. It decomposes the representation into a rigid branch learning a canonical T-pose radiance field and a non-rigid branch optimizing pose-driven radiance residuals, conditioned by pose-embedded implicit features [S1]. Unlike offset-based strategies that suffer from high computational complexity and misaligned learning objectives, MoCo-NeRF reduces prediction range to the radiance space and establishes direct supervision from ground-truth pixel colors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Monocular video frames of a dynamic human subject; camera poses; skeletal joint positions $J$ for each frame; camera intrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB images for arbitrary viewpoints and poses.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Skeletal Weights Volume Decoder:** An explicit volume decoder optimizing canonical skinning weights $w^c_k$ for $K$ joints [S1].
2.  **Multi-Hash Encoding (MHE):** A hash-based encoding $\psi_{hash}$ with 16 levels and feature dimension $H=2$ per level [S1].
    *   **Global MHE ($\psi^G_{hash}$):** Used in multi-subject mode; 4 levels, $H=2$ [S1].
    *   **Local MHEs ($\psi^i_{hash}$):** One per subject $i$; 16 levels, $H=2$ [S1].
3.  **Learnable Base Code ($S$):** A tensor $S \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$, randomly initialized, jointly optimized [S1].
4.  **Identity Codes ($S_i$):** Learnable base codes for each subject $i$ in multi-subject mode [S1].
5.  **MLP Decoders:**
    *   $M_{rigid}$: Predicts rigid color and density.
    *   $M_{nr}$: Predicts non-rigid residuals.
6.  **Projection Matrices:** $W_p, W_Q, W_K, W_V$ for pose-embedded feature generation [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Canonical Transformation:**
    For a query point $\mathbf{x}$ in observation space, transform it to canonical space $\mathbf{x}_c$ using inverse linear blend skinning [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_c = \sum_{k=1}^K w^o_k (R_k \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_k) $$
    where $w^o_k$ are observation skinning weights derived from canonical weights $w^c_k$ [S1]:
    $$ w^o_k = \frac{w^c_k(R_k \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K w^c_j(R_j \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_j)} $$
    $R_k, \mathbf{t}_k$ are the rotation and translation of bone $k$ [S1].

2.  **Rigid Branch Feature Query:**
    Query multiresolution volumetric features $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ from the MHE $\psi_{hash}$ (or global/local MHEs in multi-subject mode) [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c} = \psi_{hash}(\mathbf{x}_c) $$
    Slice the first half ($H$ dimensions) of $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ at each resolution level to compute $\mathbf{f}^{:H}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ [S1].

3.  **Rigid Radiance Prediction:**
    Predict rigid color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ using MLP $M_{rigid}$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{c}, \sigma = M_{rigid}(\mathbf{f}^{:H}_{\mathbf{x}_c}) $$
    *Multi-subject extension:* Concatenate global features, sliced local features, and subject ID code $\mathbf{S}_i$:
    $$ \mathbf{c}, \sigma = M_{rigid}(\psi^G_{hash}(\mathbf{x}_c) \oplus (\psi^i_{hash}(\mathbf{x}_c))^{:H} \oplus \mathbf{S}_i) $$ [S1].

4.  **Non-Rigid Feature Preparation:**
    Use the full feature $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ (not sliced). Freeze the first half representing canonical radiance. Denote the remaining optimized half as $\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ [S1].

5.  **Pose-Embedded Implicit Feature Generation:**
    *   **Joint Encoding:** Apply sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma$ to 23 joint positions $J=\{j_i\}_{i=1}^{23}$ [S1]:
        $$ \gamma(j_i) = [j_i, \sin(2^0 \pi j_i), \cos(2^0 \pi j_i), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi j_i), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi j_i)] $$
        where $L=10$ frequency bands [S1]. This results in $\gamma(J) \in \mathbb{R}^{23 \times 63}$ [S1].
    *   **Raw Pose Code:** Project encoded joints using matrix $W_p$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_{raw} = W_p \cdot \gamma(J) $$ [S1].
    *   **Attention Projections:** Generate query $\mathbf{P}_q$ from base code $S$, and key $\mathbf{P}_k$, value $\mathbf{P}_v$ from $\mathbf{P}_{raw}$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_q = W_Q \cdot S, \quad \mathbf{P}_k = W_K \cdot \mathbf{P}_{raw}, \quad \mathbf{P}_v = W_V \cdot \mathbf{P}_{raw} $$ [S1].
    *   **Implicit Feature:** Compute final pose-embedded implicit feature $\mathbf{P}_{nr}$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_{nr} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{P}_q \cdot \mathbf{P}_k^\top) \cdot \mathbf{P}_v $$ [S1].

6.  **Non-Rigid Residual Prediction:**
    Predict color and density residuals $\Delta \mathbf{c}, \Delta \sigma$ using MLP $M_{nr}$ conditioned on $\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{nr}$ [S1]:
    $$ \Delta \mathbf{c}, \Delta \sigma = M_{nr}(\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}; \mathbf{P}_{nr}) $$ [S1].

7.  **Compositional Rendering:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$, query $M$ samples. Compute final RGB $\mathbf{C}_{final}$ using stratified sampling and volume rendering with compositional density $(\sigma + \Delta \sigma)$ and color $(\mathbf{c} + \Delta \mathbf{c})$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{final}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{m=1}^M T_m (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_m + \Delta \sigma_m)\delta_m)) (\mathbf{c}_m + \Delta \mathbf{c}_m) $$ [S1].
    Transmittance $T_m$ is defined as [S1]:
    $$ T_m = \exp\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{m-1} \sigma_n \delta_n\right) $$
    where $\delta_m$ is the adjacent distance from the $m$-th to the $(m+1)$-th sample [S1].
    Also compute $\mathbf{C}_{rigid}$ from $(\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ for loss calculation [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: Total number of joints (23) [S1].
*   $L$: Number of frequency bands in sinusoidal PE ($L=10$) [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Hyperparameter for balancing rigid and final loss terms ($\lambda=0.2$) [S1].
*   Optimizer: Adam with betas $(0.9, 0.99)$ [S1].
*   Learning Rate: $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for skeletal weights volume decoder; $5 \times 10^{-3}$ for all other components [S1].
*   MHE Levels: 16 levels for local MHEs, 4 levels for global MHE [S1].
*   Feature Dimension: $H=2$ per level [S1].
*   Sampling: 256 samples per ray [S1].
*   Patch Sampling: 6 patches of size $32 \times 32$ per iteration [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class MoCoNeRF:
    def __init__(self, num_joints=23, num_freq_bands=10, num_mhe_levels=16, 
                 mhe_feature_dim=2, num_subjects=1, use_multi_subject=False):
        self.num_joints = num_joints
        self.num_freq_bands = num_freq_bands
        self.num_mhe_levels = num_mhe_levels
        self.mhe_feature_dim = mhe_feature_dim
        self.use_multi_subject = use_multi_subject
        
        # Skeletal weights decoder (explicit volume decoder)
        self.skeletal_weights_decoder = nn.Linear(3, num_joints) # [G] Input dim assumed 3 for x_c
        
        # MHE Encoders
        if use_multi_subject:
            self.global_mhe = MultiHashEncoding(num_levels=4, feature_dim=mhe_feature_dim)
            self.local_mhes = nn.ModuleList([
                MultiHashEncoding(num_levels=num_mhe_levels, feature_dim=mhe_feature_dim) 
                for _ in range(num_subjects)
            ])
        else:
            self.local_mhes = nn.ModuleList([
                MultiHashEncoding(num_levels=num_mhe_levels, feature_dim=mhe_feature_dim)
            ])
            
        # Rigid Branch MLP
        self.m_rigid = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(mhe_feature_dim, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 4) # 3 for color, 1 for density
        )
        
        # Non-Rigid Branch MLP
        self.m_nr = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(mhe_feature_dim, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 256),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(256, 4) # 3 for color residual, 1 for density residual
        )
        
        # Pose-Embedded Implicit Feature Generation
        self.base_code = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(64)) # S in R^64
        self.joint_projection = nn.Linear(num_joints * (1 + 2 * num_freq_bands), 64) # W_p
        self.q_proj = nn.Linear(64, 64) # W_Q
        self.k_proj = nn.Linear(64, 64) # W_K
        self.v_proj = nn.Linear(64, 64) # W_V
        
        # Identity codes for multi-subject
        if use_multi_subject:
            self.identity_codes = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(num_subjects, 64))
            
        # Hyperparameters
        self.lambda_loss = 0.2
        self.num_samples_per_ray = 256
        self.num_patches = 6
        self.patch_size = 32

    def get_canonical_point(self, x, joints):
        """
        Transform observation point x to canonical point xc using inverse linear blend skinning.
        x: (B, 3)
        joints: (B, num_joints, 3)
        """
        # [G] Skeletal weights decoder takes canonical point coordinates to predict weights
        # Note: The paper says "optimize an explicit volume decoder to optimize {w^c_k}".
        # Here we assume the decoder outputs weights for the current canonical point.
        # In practice, this might be a grid or another MLP. We use a simple MLP for [G].
        w_c = self.skeletal_weights_decoder(x) # (B, K)
        
        # Extract rotations and translations from joints? 
        # The paper uses R_k, t_k from the skeletal model. 
        # For implementation, we assume these are provided or derived from joints.
        # Here we assume a helper function `get_skinning_transforms` exists or is passed.
        # To keep it self-contained, we assume R_k, t_k are available or computed from joints.
        # Let's assume `joints` contains the necessary info or we have a separate transform module.
        # For the sake of the pseudocode, we assume `compute_obs_weights` is implemented.
        
        # w_o_k = w^c_k(R_k x + t_k) / sum_j w^c_j(R_j x + t_j)
        # This requires R_k, t_k. Let's assume they are passed or computed.
        # We will skip the detailed R/t computation and assume `compute_obs_weights` handles it.
        w_o = compute_obs_weights(w_c, x, joints) # (B, K)
        
        # x_c = sum_k w^o_k (R_k x + t_k)
        # This requires R_k, t_k.
        x_c = compute_canonical_point(w_o, x, joints) # (B, 3)
        return x_c

    def encode(self, x_c, subject_id=None):
        """
        Query features from MHE.
        """
        if self.use_multi_subject and subject_id is not None:
            global_feat = self.global_mhe(x_c) # (B, num_levels * H)
            local_feat = self.local_mhes[subject_id](x_c) # (B, num_levels * H)
            # Slice first half H dimensions at each level
            # local_feat shape: (B, num_levels * H)
            # Reshape to (B, num_levels, H)
            local_feat_reshaped = local_feat.view(local_feat.size(0), self.num_mhe_levels, self.mhe_feature_dim)
            sliced_local = local_feat_reshaped[:, :, :self.mhe_feature_dim//2] # (B, num_levels, H/2)
            sliced_local_flat = sliced_local.view(local_feat.size(0), -1)
            
            # Concatenate global, sliced local, and identity code
            id_code = self.identity_codes[subject_id].unsqueeze(0).expand(local_feat.size(0), -1)
            feat = torch.cat([global_feat, sliced_local_flat, id_code], dim=-1)
        else:
            local_feat = self.local_mhes[0](x_c)
            # Slice first half
            local_feat_reshaped = local_feat.view(local_feat.size(0), self.num_mhe_levels, self.mhe_feature_dim)
            sliced_local = local_feat_reshaped[:, :, :self.mhe_feature_dim//2]
            sliced_local_flat = sliced_local.view(local_feat.size(0), -1)
            feat = sliced_local_flat
            
        return feat

    def encode_full(self, x_c, subject_id=None):
        """
        Query full features for non-rigid branch.
        """
        if self.use_multi_subject and subject_id is not None:
            global_feat = self.global_mhe(x_c)
            local_feat = self.local_mhes[subject_id](x_c)
            # Concatenate global and full local
            id_code = self.identity_codes[subject_id].unsqueeze(0).expand(local_feat.size(0), -1)
            feat = torch.cat([global_feat, local_feat, id_code], dim=-1)
        else:
            feat = self.local_mhes[0](x_c)
        return feat

    def generate_pose_feature(self, joints):
        """
        Generate pose-embedded implicit feature P_nr.
        joints: (B, num_joints, 3)
        """
        # Sinusoidal PE
        # gamma(j_i) = [j_i, sin(2^0 pi j_i), cos(2^0 pi j_i), ..., sin(2^{L-1} pi j_i), cos(2^{L-1} pi j_i)]
        # This is applied to each joint.
        # We can use a standard positional encoding function.
        # Input: (B, num_joints, 3) -> Output: (B, num_joints, 1 + 2*L)
        # Note: The paper says gamma(J) in R^{23x63}. 1 + 2*10 = 21? 
        # Wait, 23 joints * 21 = 483. But the paper says R^{23x63}. 
        # 63 / 23 is not integer. 
        # Let's re-read: "gamma(j_i) = [j_i, sin(...), cos(...)]". 
        # If j_i is 3D, then 1 + 2*L = 21. 23 * 21 = 483.
        # The paper says "gamma(J) in R^{23x63}". 
        # Maybe L is different or the encoding is different. 
        # "L=10 frequency bands". 
        # Perhaps the input is not 3D? Or maybe the projection is different.
        # Let's stick to the formula: gamma(j_i) has 1 + 2*L components.
        # If the paper says 63, maybe L=31? Or maybe the joint is not 3D?
        # "23 joint positions J". 
        # Let's assume the paper's dimension 63 is correct and L might be derived or there's a specific encoding.
        # However, the formula explicitly shows L=10. 
        # 1 + 2*10 = 21. 
        # 23 * 21 = 483. 
        # 63 is 3 * 21. 
        # Maybe the encoding is applied to each coordinate separately? 
        # If j_i = (x,y,z), and we encode each coordinate with L bands? 
        # Then for one joint: 3 * (1 + 2*L) = 3 * 21 = 63. 
        # This matches! So gamma is applied to each coordinate of the joint.
        
        B, K, _ = joints.shape
        # joints: (B, K, 3)
        # Reshape to (B*K, 3)
        joints_flat = joints.view(-1, 3)
        
        # Apply sinusoidal PE to each coordinate
        # Standard PE: [x, sin(x), cos(x), ..., sin(Lx), cos(Lx)]
        # We need to do this for x, y, z separately.
        # Let's assume a function `sinusoidal_pe` that takes (N, 1) and returns (N, 1+2L)
        
        x_coords = joints_flat[:, 0].unsqueeze(-1)
        y_coords = joints_flat[:, 1].unsqueeze(-1)
        z_coords = joints_flat[:, 2].unsqueeze(-1)
        
        pe_x = sinusoidal_pe(x_coords, self.num_freq_bands)
        pe_y = sinusoidal_pe(y_coords, self.num_freq_bands)
        pe_z = sinusoidal_pe(z_coords, self.num_freq_bands)
        
        pe_joint = torch.cat([pe_x, pe_y, pe_z], dim=-1) # (B*K, 63)
        pe_joints = pe_joint.view(B, K, -1) # (B, K, 63)
        
        # Project to raw pose-embedded code
        P_raw = self.joint_projection(pe_joints) # (B, K, 64)
        
        # Attention
        P_q = self.q_proj(self.base_code.unsqueeze(0).expand(B, -1)) # (B, 64)
        P_k = self.k_proj(P_raw) # (B, K, 64)
        P_v = self.v_proj(P_raw) # (B, K, 64)
        
        # Softmax attention
        # P_q: (B, 64), P_k: (B, K, 64)
        # P_q @ P_k^T -> (B, K)
        attn_weights = F.softmax(torch.matmul(P_q, P_k.transpose(1, 2)), dim=-1) # (B, K)
        
        # P_nr = attn_weights @ P_v
        P_nr = torch.matmul(attn_weights, P_v) # (B, 64)
        
        return P_nr

    def forward(self, x, joints, subject_id=None):
        """
        Forward pass for a single point.
        """
        # 1. Canonical Transformation
        x_c = self.get_canonical_point(x, joints)
        
        # 2. Rigid Branch
        feat_rigid = self.encode(x_c, subject_id)
        out_rigid = self.m_rigid(feat_rigid)
        c_rigid = out_rigid[:, :3]
        sigma_rigid = out_rigid[:, 3:]
        
        # 3. Non-Rigid Branch
        feat_nr = self.encode_full(x_c, subject_id)
        # Freeze first half of feat_nr? 
        # The paper says "freeze the first half of fxc that represents the canonical radiance field".
        # In training, we can detach the first half or mask gradients.
        # For simplicity, we assume the network is designed such that the first half is not updated in M_nr.
        # Here we just pass the full feature.
        
        # Slice the first half for the non-rigid branch input? 
        # "Denote the remaining optimized half as f*_xc".
        # So we pass f*_xc to M_nr.
        # feat_nr shape: (B, num_levels * H)
        # Reshape to (B, num_levels, H)
        feat_nr_reshaped = feat_nr.view(feat_nr.size(0), self.num_mhe_levels, self.mhe_feature_dim)
        # Slice first half (H/2)
        feat_nr_frozen = feat_nr_reshaped[:, :, :self.mhe_feature_dim//2]
        feat_nr_opt = feat_nr_reshaped[:, :, self.mhe_feature_dim//2:]
        feat_nr_opt_flat = feat_nr_opt.view(feat_nr.size(0), -1)
        
        # Generate pose feature
        P_nr = self.generate_pose_feature(joints)
        
        # Concatenate P_nr to the first intermediate output of M_nr?
        # "concatenating Pnr to the first intermediate output of the MLP decoder Mnr"
        # This implies M_nr has an internal structure. 
        # For simplicity, we concatenate P_nr to the input of M_nr.
        # Or we modify M_nr to accept P_nr.
        # Let's assume M_nr takes (feat, P_nr) as input.
        
        out_nr = self.m_nr(torch.cat([feat_nr_opt_flat, P_nr], dim=-1))
        delta_c = out_nr[:, :3]
        delta_sigma = out_nr[:, 3:]
        
        # 4. Compositional Radiance
        c_final = c_rigid + delta_c
        sigma_final = sigma_rigid + delta_sigma
        
        return c_final, sigma_final, c_rigid, sigma_rigid

    def render_ray(self, ray_origins, ray_directions, joints, subject_id=None):
        """
        Render a ray using stratified sampling.
        """
        # Sample points along the ray
        t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, self.num_samples_per_ray)
        # Stratified sampling
        t_vals = t_vals + torch.rand_like(t_vals) / self.num_samples_per_ray
        # Map to near/far bounds (assuming fixed bounds for simplicity)
        # [G] Near and far bounds should be defined.
        near, far = 0.1, 10.0
        z_vals = near * (1.0 - t_vals) + far * t_vals
        
        # Points
        points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_directions.unsqueeze(1) * z_vals.unsqueeze(2)
        points = points.view(-1, 3)
        
        # Forward pass
        c_final, sigma_final, c_rigid, sigma_rigid = self.forward(points, joints, subject_id)
        
        # Volume Rendering
        # delta_z = z_vals[i+1] - z_vals[i]
        delta_z = torch.diff(z_vals, dim=-1, append=torch.tensor([0.0]).to(z_vals.device))
        delta_z = delta_z.unsqueeze(0).expand(points.size(0), -1) # (B, M)
        
        # Transmittance
        # T_m = exp(-sum_{n=1}^{m-1} sigma_n delta_n)
        # Accumulate sigma * delta
        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma_final * delta_z)
        transmittance = torch.cumprod(torch.exp(-sigma_final * delta_z), dim=-1)
        transmittance = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(transmittance[:, :1]), transmittance[:, :-1]], dim=-1)
        
        # Color
        # C = sum T_m (1 - exp(-sigma_m delta_m)) c_m
        # C = sum T_m alpha_m c_m
        color = torch.sum(transmittance.unsqueeze(-1) * alpha.unsqueeze(-1) * c_final, dim=1)
        
        return color

    def compute_loss(self, rendered_patches, gt_patches, rigid_patches, joints, subject_id=None):
        """
        Compute loss.
        """
        # LPIPS
        lpips_r = lpips_loss(rigid_patches, gt_patches)
        lpips_f = lpips_loss(rendered_patches, gt_patches)
        
        # MSE
        mse_r = F.mse_loss(rigid_patches, gt_patches)
        mse_f = F.mse_loss(rendered_patches, gt_patches)
        
        # SSIM
        ssim_f = ssim_loss(rendered_patches, gt_patches)
        
        L_r = lpips_r + mse_r
        L_f = lpips_f + mse_f + ssim_f
        
        L_o = self.lambda_loss * L_r + (1 - self.lambda_loss) * L_f
        
        return L_o
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spatial Offset Misalignment:** Traditional methods model non-rigid motions as spatial offsets, which span an unbounded range and are unique for every 3D point, leading to high computational complexity and misaligned learning objectives with RGB supervision [S1]. MoCo-NeRF avoids this by modeling residuals in radiance space [S1].
*   **Precision:** The freezing of the first half of the MHE features for the non-rigid branch requires careful gradient management to ensure only the optimized half is updated [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The inverse linear blend skinning transformation requires valid skinning weights and joint transforms. Degenerate poses or missing joints may cause undefined behavior [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is significantly reduced compared to offset-based methods. Single-subject training takes 1–2 hours on an RTX 3090 GPU [S1]. Multi-subject training (6 subjects) takes ~2 hours on one RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a single MHE for single-subject, and global + local MHEs for multi-subject. MHE is memory-efficient compared to dense voxel grids [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves state-of-the-art PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS* on ZJU-MoCap and MonoCap datasets [S1].
    *   Single-subject (ZJU-MoCap): PSNR 31.06, SSIM 0.9734, LPIPS* 28.83 [S1].
    *   Multi-subject (ZJU-MoCap): PSNR 31.97, SSIM 0.9781, LPIPS* 24.54 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Direct color supervision for non-rigid residuals; efficient training; scalable to multi-subject; handles complex cloth dynamics [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires skeletal joint positions as input; relies on the quality of the skeletal model [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Free-viewpoint rendering of dynamic humans from monocular videos where training time and non-rigid motion fidelity are critical [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Static scenes; scenes without skeletal structure; multi-view setups where other methods might be more efficient [S1].

## Background
MoCo-NeRF was proposed by J. Kim et al. in 2024 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of spatial offset-based methods for modeling non-rigid motions in dynamic human rendering [S1]. The method builds on the principles of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and linear blend skinning [S1]. It is evaluated on the ZJU-MoCap and MonoCap datasets [S1].
