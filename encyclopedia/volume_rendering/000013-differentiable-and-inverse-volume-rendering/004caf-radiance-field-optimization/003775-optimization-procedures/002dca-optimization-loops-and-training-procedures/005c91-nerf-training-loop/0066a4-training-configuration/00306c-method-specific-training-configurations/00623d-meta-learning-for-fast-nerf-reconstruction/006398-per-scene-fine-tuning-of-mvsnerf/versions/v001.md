# Per-scene fine-tuning of MVSNeRF

Also called: per-scene optimization

## Overview
Per-scene fine-tuning is a post-processing optimization step applied to the neural encoding volume and MLP decoder predicted by MVSNeRF to achieve high-quality final renderings [S1]. It solves the problem of residual reconstruction errors and view-dependent shading artifacts that remain after the initial fast generalizable inference [S1]. Unlike from-scratch optimization methods like NeRF, which require hundreds of thousands of iterations, this method leverages a strong initial reconstruction to converge to comparable or superior quality in approximately 15 minutes [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  **Neural Encoding Volume ($V$):** A 3D grid of feature vectors predicted by the MVSNeRF 3D CNN for the reference view's frustrum [S1].
2.  **MLP Decoder ($f_{\theta}$):** A multi-layer perceptron with weights $\theta$ predicted by the MVSNeRF network [S1].
3.  **Reference Image ($I$):** The input image used to initialize the volume [S1].
4.  **Camera Poses:** Intrinsics and extrinsics for the reference view and target novel views [S1].

**Outputs:**
1.  **Optimized Neural Encoding Volume ($V'$):** The updated 3D feature grid after fine-tuning [S1].
2.  **Optimized MLP Weights ($\theta'$):** The updated decoder weights [S1].
3.  **High-Quality Renderings:** Novel views synthesized using $V'$ and $\theta'$ [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize the neural encoding volume $V$ within the frustrum of the reference view using the MVSNeRF inference [S1].
    *   Initialize the MLP decoder weights $\theta$ using the MVSNeRF inference [S1].
    *   **Padding (Optional):** For large scenes, pad the boundary voxels of $V$ to extend beyond the reference frustrum. This prevents out-of-frustrum artifacts in novel views [S1]. Note that these padded voxels are not well-reconstructed initially and may require longer fine-tuning [S1].

2.  **Ray Sampling:**
    *   For each pixel in the target view, cast a ray through the camera center and the pixel coordinates [S1].
    *   **Do not use two-stage (coarse and fine) ray sampling** [S1].
    *   Uniformly sample $N$ points along the marching ray within the volume bounds [S1].
    *   Add small per-step randomness to the sample positions to break symmetry and improve gradient flow [S1].
    *   **Parameter $N$:**
        *   Standard scenes: $N = 128$ [S1].
        *   Challenging scenes (high gloss/specularity): $N = 256$ [S1].

3.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   For each sampled point $x_i$ on the ray:
        *   Retrieve the 3D feature vector $c_i$ from the neural encoding volume $V$ at $x_i$ via trilinear interpolation [S1].
        *   Compute the positional encoding $PE(x_i)$ [S1].
        *   Pass $[PE(x_i), c_i]$ through the MLP decoder $f_{\theta}$ to predict density $\sigma_i$ and color $c_i^{color}$ [S1].
    *   Compute the rendered color $C(r)$ for ray $r$ using standard volume rendering integration:
        $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i c_i^{color} $$
        where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between adjacent samples [S1]. (Standard NeRF formula, implied by context).

4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute the photometric loss between the rendered image and the ground truth target view image [S1].
    *   Common losses include $L_1$ or $L_2$ pixel-wise error, and potentially SSIM [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Perform gradient descent to update $V$ and $\theta$ [S1].
    *   **Iterations:** 10,000 iterations [S1].
    *   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU [S1].
    *   **Duration:** Approximately 15 minutes [S1].

6.  **Iteration:**
    *   Repeat steps 2-5 for all pixels in all target views (or a subset for speed) until the iteration count is reached [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def per_scene_finetuning(volume, mlp_weights, reference_view, target_views, num_iterations=10000, points_per_ray=128):
    """
    Fine-tunes the neural encoding volume and MLP decoder for a specific scene.
    
    Args:
        volume: 3D tensor of shape (D, H, W, C_feat), initialized from MVSNeRF inference.
        mlp_weights: Dictionary of MLP weights, initialized from MVSNeRF inference.
        reference_view: Camera pose and intrinsics of the reference view used for initialization.
        target_views: List of camera poses and intrinsics for novel views to render.
        num_iterations: Number of optimization steps (default 10000).
        points_per_ray: Number of points sampled per ray (default 128, 256 for challenging scenes).
        
    Returns:
        volume: Optimized volume.
        mlp_weights: Optimized MLP weights.
    """
    
    # Initialize optimizer for volume and MLP weights
    optimizer = Adam([volume, mlp_weights.parameters()], lr=0.001) # Learning rate not specified, standard NeRF is 5e-5 or similar. [G]
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        total_loss = 0
        
        # Sample a batch of target views or all views
        for view in target_views:
            # Get image dimensions
            height, width = view.image_height, view.image_width
            
            # Initialize rendered image
            rendered_image = torch.zeros((height, width, 3))
            
            for y in range(height):
                for x in range(width):
                    # Cast ray
                    ray_origin, ray_direction = get_ray(view, x, y)
                    
                    # Sample points along the ray
                    # Uniform sampling with small per-step randomness
                    t_vals = uniform_sample(ray_origin, ray_direction, points_per_ray)
                    points = ray_origin + t_vals * ray_direction
                    
                    # Volume Rendering
                    alphas = []
                    colors = []
                    ts = []
                    
                    for i, point in enumerate(points):
                        # 1. Retrieve feature from volume
                        # Trilinear interpolation
                        feat = trilinear_interpolate(volume, point)
                        
                        # 2. Positional Encoding
                        pe = positional_encoding(point) # Standard NeRF PE [S1]
                        
                        # 3. MLP Forward Pass
                        # Concatenate PE and feature
                        # Note: Source Table 3 suggests complex input dimensions.
                        # We assume the MLP handles the concatenation internally or via a linear layer.
                        input_to_mlp = concatenate(pe, feat)
                        
                        sigma, color = mlp_forward(mlp_weights, input_to_mlp)
                        
                        # 4. Compute alpha
                        # Delta is distance between samples
                        delta = distance(points[i], points[i+1]) if i < len(points)-1 else 0.01 # [G]
                        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)
                        
                        alphas.append(alpha)
                        colors.append(color)
                        ts.append(t_vals[i])
                    
                    # Composite color
                    # T_i = exp(-sum_{j=1}^{i-1} sigma_j * delta_j)
                    # C(r) = sum T_i * alpha_i * c_i
                    rendered_color = composite_color(alphas, colors)
                    
                    rendered_image[y, x] = rendered_color
            
            # Compute Loss
            # Assuming target image is available for the view
            target_image = view.image
            loss = l1_loss(rendered_image, target_image)
            total_loss += loss
        
        # Backpropagate
        total_loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        
        if iteration % 1000 == 0:
            print(f"Iteration {iteration}, Loss: {total_loss.item()}")
            
    return volume, mlp_weights
```

### Parameters
*   **Training Iterations:** 10,000 [S1].
*   **Points per Ray:** 128 for standard scenes, 256 for challenging scenes (high gloss/specularity) [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU [S1].
*   **Training Duration:** ~15 minutes [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
1.  **Out-of-Frustum Artifacts:** If the scene is large, parts visible in novel views may lie outside the reference view's frustum. This causes artifacts because the volume is only initialized within the frustum [S1].
    *   **Solution:** Pad the boundary voxels of the neural encoding volume to extend beyond the frustum [S1].
    *   **Pitfall:** Padded voxels are not well-reconstructed initially and may require longer fine-tuning to achieve high quality [S1].
2.  **Limited Viewing Range:** The representation is initialized only for content seen by the reference view. Unseen parts (occluded or outside frustum) are not directly recovered, making 360-degree rendering challenging with a single volume [S1].
    *   **Solution:** Combine multiple neural encoding volumes at multiple views for larger viewing ranges [S1].
3.  **View-Dependent Shading:** Highly challenging scenes with high glossiness or specularities are hard to recover via network inference alone [S1].
    *   **Solution:** Use more points per ray (256) and potentially longer fine-tuning [S1].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** $O(I \cdot N \cdot P)$, where $I$ is the number of iterations (10,000), $N$ is the number of points per ray (128-256), and $P$ is the number of pixels [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V \cdot C + M)$, where $V$ is the volume size, $C$ is the feature dimension, and $M$ is the MLP size [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the DTU and LLFF datasets, MVSNeRF's 15-minute fine-tuning (10k iterations) achieves quality comparable to or better than NeRF's 10.2-hour optimization (200k iterations) [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance scales with the number of iterations. The 12k-iteration result (18 min) is already better than NeRF's 48k-iteration result (10.2h) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly faster than from-scratch optimization (15 min vs 10.2 hours) [S1]. High quality results quickly [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Limited viewing range with a single volume [S1]. Struggles with highly specular scenes without extended fine-tuning [S1]. Padding voxels require longer convergence [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality novel view synthesis is required for a specific scene and a generalizable initial reconstruction is available. Ideal for scenarios where training time is constrained but quality is critical.
*   **When Not to Use:** When 360-degree rendering is required and multiple volumes cannot be combined. When scenes have extreme specularities and very short fine-tuning time is available.

## Background

*   **Origin:** MVSNeRF was introduced by Sun et al. in 2021 [S1].
*   **Development:** The method builds on the idea of generalizable radiance field reconstruction, using a CNN to predict a neural encoding volume and an MLP decoder [S1]. The per-scene fine-tuning step was introduced to refine the initial prediction, addressing the limitations of pure inference-based reconstruction [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in view synthesis tasks on datasets like DTU and LLFF [S1].
