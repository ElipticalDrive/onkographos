# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain, whereas siblings typically rely on direct time-conditioned feature interpolation or rigid body assumptions [S1]. It uses a tri-plane spatial representation for the canonical space to enable a shallower observation network and faster convergence compared to the MLP-based backbones used in [D-NeRF](14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamp $t_i$, and previous frame deformation states $d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}}, d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}$ and noise terms $\varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$.
*   **Outputs:** Estimated deformation $d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}$ for the current frame, which warps points to a canonical space. The canonical space features are then used to render color and density.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes ($C = \{P_{xy}, P_{yz}, P_{xz}\}$) encode the canonical space. Each plane has resolution $R \times R$. Features are sampled via bilinear interpolation. Raw coordinate inputs are concatenated with encoded features to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP. Input: $[\mathbf{x}, t_i]$. Output: Mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer (single dense layer). Input: $[\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}]$. Output: Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Observer Query (Current Frame):**
    Query the observer MLP with current position $\mathbf{x}$ and timestamp $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$
    y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{ObserverMLP}(\mathbf{x}, t_i)
    $$

2.  **Velocity Calculation (Prediction Branch):**
    Approximate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using the deformation from the previous two frames. Let $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    $$
    v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i}
    $$
    *Note: For the first frame ($t_1$), this step may require initialization or skipping, as $t_{-1}$ is undefined. The sources do not specify the initialization for $t_1$ [S1].*

3.  **Deformation Prediction:**
    Calculate the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the locally linear motion model. The prediction assumes the deformation at $t_i$ is the deformation at $t_{i-1}$ plus the displacement due to velocity over the time step [S1].
    $$
    \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i}
    $$
    Substituting the velocity equation:
    $$
    \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + (d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}) = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}
    $$

4.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:**
    Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ that takes the current and previous noise-related terms and timestamps [S1].
    $$
    K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1})
    $$

5.  **Deformation Estimation (Update):**
    Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final estimated deformation $d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$
    d\mathbf{x}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-})
    $$

6.  **Warping to Canonical Space:**
    Warp the 3D point $\mathbf{x}$ from the real space to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$ using the estimated deformation [S1].
    $$
    \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}
    $$

7.  **Rendering:**
    Sample the tri-plane representation at $\mathbf{x}'$ to obtain features. Concatenate with raw coordinates $\mathbf{x}'$. Pass through the final MLP (not detailed in the Kalman section, but implied by the NeRF baseline) to output density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. Render using standard volume rendering [S1].

**Formulas:**

*   **Kalman Loss:** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation [S1].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_i} \|_2^2
    $$
    *Note: The source defines this as minimizing error between observation $y_{t_i}$ and estimated deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ (which is $d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}$ in our notation) [S1].*

*   **Canonical Observation Loss:** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ (canonical frame) to normalize the difference between the canonical space and real world [S1].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x}
    $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise.

*   **Total Variation Loss:** Encourages sparse edges in the tri-plane representation [S1].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2)
    $$
    where $c$ is a plane from collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution.

*   **Total Loss:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv}
    $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ is the proposal sampling loss from Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **First Frame Initialization:** The velocity calculation requires $t_{i-2}$. For the first frame ($t_1$), $t_{-1}$ is undefined. The sources do not specify the initialization strategy for the first frame's deformation or velocity [S1]. [G] A common approach is to assume zero deformation or use the observer output directly with zero Kalman gain.
*   **Temporal Order:** The training strategy releases images in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors [S1]. Random sampling may break the Kalman filter's dependency on history.
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Tri-plane representations suffer from coordinate shift errors due to finite resolution. Concatenating raw coordinates with encoded features mitigates this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The observer MLP is shallow (2 layers), and the Kalman gain is a linear layer, making the per-point deformation estimation computationally efficient compared to deep MLPs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a tri-plane representation, which significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations (like D-NeRF) or dense voxel grids [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 30 minutes (small model) to 3 hours (large model) on real data, comparable to or faster than baselines like D-NeRF (48 hrs) and TiNeuVox-B (40 mins) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate deformation estimation for scenes with substantial motion due to the Kalman filter's ability to balance prediction and observation. Fast convergence due to tri-plane representation.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion, which may not hold for highly non-rigid or complex motions. Performance degrades if the temporal context is insufficient (e.g., sparse timestamps).

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system. It addresses the limitations of previous dynamic NeRF methods that either disregard motion priors (feature interpolation) or struggle with significant motion (vanilla deformation fields) [S1]. The method builds on the tri-plane representation from TiNeuVox [S1] and integrates a Kalman filter module to guide the deformation field estimation.
