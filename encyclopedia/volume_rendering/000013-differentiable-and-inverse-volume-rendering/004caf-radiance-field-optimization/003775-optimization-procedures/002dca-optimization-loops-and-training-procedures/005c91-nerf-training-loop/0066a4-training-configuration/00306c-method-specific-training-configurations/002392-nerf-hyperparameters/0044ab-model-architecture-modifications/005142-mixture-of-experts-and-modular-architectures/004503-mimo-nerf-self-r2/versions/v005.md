# MIMO-NeRF-self-R2

Also called: MIMO-NeRF with reduction factors R1=1 and R2=1

## Overview
MIMO-NeRF-self-R2 is a specific configuration of the MIMO-NeRF architecture that utilizes two reformulated Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to accelerate training while maintaining rendering quality [S1]. It solves the problem of high computational cost in standard NeRF by reducing the number of parallel MLP evaluations per batch [S1]. It sets itself apart from the base MIMO-NeRF-naive approach by using a structured reformulation strategy rather than simple parallelization, and from MIMO-NeRF-self-R1 by employing identical reduction factors for its two MLP stages [S1].

## How it works
MIMO-NeRF-self-R2 builds on [Mixture-of-Experts and Modular Architectures](14429-mixture-of-experts-and-modular-architectures.md) by defining specific reduction factors for its expert networks.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A batch of $B$ ray samples, where each sample is a 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ and potentially camera parameters. The total number of MLPs in the original NeRF is denoted as $N$. The number of parallel processing units (experts) is $N_p$.
*   **Output:** Density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ for the input points, aggregated to produce the final pixel color via volume rendering.

**Data Structures:**
The method employs two reformulated MIMO MLPs. Each MLP processes a subset of the input points. The architecture does not use a dynamic gating network in the traditional MoE sense for dispatching during the forward pass of the reformulation; instead, the input batch is partitioned or processed through these two specific MLP structures defined by reduction factors [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N_p$: Number of parallel processing units (experts). Typical values cited are $N_p=4$ and $N_p=8$ [S1].
*   $R^1, R^2$: Reduction factors for the first and second reformulated MIMO MLPs, respectively. For MIMO-NeRF-self-R2, $R^1 = 1$ and $R^2 = 1$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total number of MLPs running in parallel is calculated as [S1]:
$$
\sum_{m=1}^2 \frac{N}{N_p} R^m = N \frac{2}{N_p}
$$
This total is less than $N$ when $N_p > 2$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Configuration:** Define the number of experts $N_p$ and set reduction factors $R^1 = 1$ and $R^2 = 1$.
2.  **Partitioning/Processing:** The input batch is processed by two reformulated MIMO MLPs. The exact mechanism of how the batch is split or shared between these two MLPs to achieve the reduction factor is not explicitly detailed in the provided notes beyond the formula for the total number of MLPs running [S1]. [G: In the context of MIMO-NeRF, this typically implies that the input points are grouped or that the MLPs share weights/inputs in a specific pattern to reduce the effective parallel load compared to a naive $N$-parallel setup.]
3.  **Forward Pass:** Each reformulated MLP computes density and color for its assigned portion of the input points.
4.  **Aggregation:** The outputs from the two MLPs are combined. [G: Standard NeRF aggregation applies, where density and color are accumulated along the ray using alpha compositing.]
5.  **Rendering:** The final pixel color is computed via volume rendering integration.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   The formula $N \frac{2}{N_p} < N$ holds only when $N_p > 2$. If $N_p \le 2$, the reduction benefit may not apply as described or the configuration is invalid for this specific variant [S1].
*   Precision issues related to the grouping and padding of points across the two MLPs are noted as having a small effect on the number of group shifts, which is equal to or smaller than the number of reformulated MIMO MLPs [S1].

## Subtopics

### MIMO-NeRF-self-R5
A variant using two reformulated MIMO MLPs with reduction factors $R^1=1$ and $R^2=N_p/2$ [S1]. The total number of MLPs running is $N \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{N_p} \right) < N$ when $N_p > 2$ [S1]. On the Blender dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 29.81, SSIM 0.939, LPIPS 0.076, and T-time 4.38h. On the LLFF dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 26.61, SSIM 0.838, LPIPS 0.202, and T-time 3.37h [S1]. This method is identical to MIMO-NeRF-self-R1 when $N_p=4$ [S1].

### MIMO-NeRF-self-R4
A variant adopting two reformulated MIMO MLPs with reduction factors $R^1=1$ and $R^2=2$ [S1]. The total number of MLPs running is $N \frac{3}{N_p} < N$ when $N_p > 3$ [S1]. On the Blender dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 29.86, SSIM 0.939, LPIPS 0.077, and T-time 3.33h. On the LLFF dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 26.61, SSIM 0.836, LPIPS 0.205, and T-time 2.41h [S1]. This method is identical to MIMO-NeRF-self-R1 when $N_p=4$ [S1].

### MIMO-NeRF-self-R6
A variant using three reformulated MIMO MLPs with reduction factors $R^1=1, R^2=1, R^3=1$ [S1]. The total number of MLPs running is $N \frac{3}{N_p} < N$ when $N_p > 3$ [S1]. On the Blender dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 30.11, SSIM 0.941, LPIPS 0.074, and T-time 3.76h. On the LLFF dataset with $N_p=4$, it achieves PSNR 26.41, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.208, and T-time 2.73h [S1]. This method is identical to MIMO-NeRF-self-R3 when $N_p=4$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The method reduces the total number of MLPs running from $N$ to $N \frac{2}{N_p}$ [S1]. This reduction leads to faster training times compared to the original NeRF and some other variants.

**Measured Performance:**
*   **Blender Dataset ($N_p=4$):** PSNR 30.95, SSIM 0.950, LPIPS 0.060, Training Time 3.65h [S1].
*   **LLFF Dataset ($N_p=4$):** PSNR 27.35, SSIM 0.861, LPIPS 0.169, Training Time 2.70h [S1].
*   **Blender Dataset ($N_p=8$):** PSNR 30.02, SSIM 0.940, LPIPS 0.076, Training Time 2.61h [S1].
*   **LLFF Dataset ($N_p=8$):** PSNR 26.52, SSIM 0.833, LPIPS 0.207, Training Time 2.13h [S1].

**Scaling:**
Increasing $N_p$ from 4 to 8 reduces training time on both datasets (e.g., Blender: 3.65h to 2.61h; LLFF: 2.70h to 2.13h) but slightly decreases PSNR and SSIM while increasing LPIPS [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Achieves performance comparable to MIMO-NeRF-distill while being faster than the original NeRF [S1]. It offers a good balance between speed and quality for $N_p=4$ on Blender [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Generally outperformed by MIMO-NeRF-self-R1 in most metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) according to the source table comparisons [S1].

**When to use:**
Use MIMO-NeRF-self-R2 when $N_p=4$ and a balance of speed and quality is desired, particularly if MIMO-NeRF-self-R1 is not suitable or when comparing against MIMO-NeRF-distill [S1].

## Background
MIMO-NeRF-self-R2 is a variant introduced in the paper "MIMO-NeRF: Fast Neural Rendering with Multi-input Multi-output Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. The paper was published in 2023 [S1]. The variant is part of a family of reformulation methods designed to optimize the training speed of NeRF by reducing the number of parallel MLP evaluations [S1].
