# Vanilla RS-NeRF

Also called: RS-NeRF (basic model)

## Overview
Vanilla RS-NeRF is a method that reconstructs a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) from rolling shutter (RS) images by explicitly modeling the camera's motion trajectory during exposure and jointly optimizing NeRF parameters and camera extrinsics for each image row [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by replacing the single static camera pose per image with a row-wise continuous pose trajectory, allowing it to handle severe motion blur and geometric distortions inherent to RS sensors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $K$ rolling shutter images $\mathbf{I}^R \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, where $H$ is the image height and $W$ is the width.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta_n$ and optimized camera pose parameters $\theta_p$ defining the trajectory for each RS image.

**Data Structures**
*   **Camera Pose ($\mathbf{T}$):** Represented as elements of the Lie algebra of SE(3). The pose for row $i$ is denoted $\mathbf{T}_i$.
*   **Ray ($\hat{\mathbf{r}}$):** Defined by origin and direction, parameterized by learnable pose parameters $\theta_p$ and pixel coordinates $(u, v)$.
*   **NeRF Network ($\mathbf{C}$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that takes a 3D point and viewing direction to predict color and density.

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Initialize Poses:** For each of the $K$ RS images, define two learnable camera poses: $\mathbf{T}_0$ for the first row (top) and $\mathbf{T}_{H-1}$ for the last row (bottom) [S1].
2.  **Interpolate Trajectory:** For any intermediate row index $i \in [0, H-1]$, compute the pose $\mathbf{T}_i$ by linearly interpolating between $\mathbf{T}_0$ and $\mathbf{T}_{H-1}$ in the Lie algebra of SE(3) [S1].
3.  **Sample Rays:** For each iteration, select $M$ rows per image and $N$ points per row. This yields $K \times M \times N$ distinct camera rays [S1].
4.  **Compute Ray Parameters:** For each sampled pixel $(u, v)$ on row $i$, determine the camera ray $\hat{\mathbf{r}}(\theta_p; u, v)$ using the interpolated pose $\mathbf{T}_i$ derived from $\theta_p$ [S1].
5.  **Render and Loss:** Render the predicted RGB $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ for the ray and compute the photometric loss against the ground truth row $[\mathbf{I}^R]_i$ [S1].
6.  **Optimize:** Update $\theta_n$ and $\theta_p$ to minimize the loss [S1].

**Formulas**
The image formation process assumes an infinitesimal exposure time for each row. The relationship between the RS image and a virtual global shutter image is defined as [S1]:
$$
[\mathbf{I}^R(\mathbf{x})]_i = [\mathbf{I}^G_i(\mathbf{x})]_i
$$
where $[\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{x})]_i$ extracts the $i$-th row from image $\mathbf{I}$, $\mathbf{I}^R$ is the rolling shutter image, and $\mathbf{I}^G_i$ is the virtual global shutter image captured at the same pose as the $i$-th row of $\mathbf{I}^R$ [S1].

The camera motion trajectory for row $i$ is computed via linear interpolation in the Lie algebra of SE(3) [S1]:
$$
\mathbf{T}_i = \mathbf{T}_0 \cdot (1 - \rho) + \mathbf{T}_{H-1} \cdot \rho
$$
where $\rho = \frac{i}{H-1}$ is the interpolation coefficient based on the row index $i$ [S1].

The optimization objective is the photometric loss [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = ||\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) - \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\theta_n; \hat{\mathbf{r}}(\theta_p; (u,v)))||_2
$$
where $\theta_n$ are NeRF parameters, $\theta_p$ are learnable pose parameters, $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is the predicted RGB value, and $\mathbf{C}$ is the ground truth RGB value for the pixel at $(u, v)$ [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Exposure Time:** Assumed infinitesimal for each row [S1].
*   **Sampling:** $M$ rows per image and $N$ points per row are selected per iteration [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Lie Algebra Interpolation:** The interpolation must occur in the Lie algebra of SE(3), not in Euclidean space, to ensure valid rigid body transformations [S1].
*   **Readout Direction:** The model assumes a top-to-bottom readout direction [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method introduces $K \times H$ additional pose parameters (or $2K$ if only endpoints are optimized and interpolated) compared to standard NeRF. The ray sampling complexity scales with $K \times M \times N$ per iteration, similar to standard NeRF but with per-ray pose lookups.
*   **Strengths:** Explicitly handles rolling shutter distortions and motion blur by modeling continuous camera motion during exposure [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of linear motion in the Lie algebra, which may not hold for complex camera movements. Requires accurate initialization of boundary poses [S1].
*   **When to use:** When input images are captured with a rolling shutter sensor and exhibit significant motion blur or geometric distortion due to camera movement during readout [S1].

## Background
RS-NeRF was introduced to address the limitations of standard NeRFs when applied to images captured with rolling shutter cameras, which capture each row at distinct timestamps [S1]. The method builds upon the physical model of RS image formation and integrates it into the differentiable rendering pipeline [S1].
