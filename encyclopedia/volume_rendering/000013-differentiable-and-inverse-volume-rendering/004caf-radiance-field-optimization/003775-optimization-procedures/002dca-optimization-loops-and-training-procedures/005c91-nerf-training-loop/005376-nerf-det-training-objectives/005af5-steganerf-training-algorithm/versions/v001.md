# StegaNeRF Training Algorithm

Also called: Steganographic NeRF Training

## Overview
StegaNeRF is a training procedure that embeds invisible, recoverable information (such as images or identity markers) into a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by jointly optimizing the radiance field and a detector network [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by adding steganographic loss terms that force the rendered images to carry hidden data while maintaining photometric fidelity to the original scene [S1]. It builds on [NeRF-Det Training Objectives](14994-nerf-det-training-objectives.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Training images $\{Y_i\}$ with corresponding camera poses $\{P_i\}$ [S1].
*   Hidden information $I$ to be embedded (e.g., an image or identity vector) [S1].
*   Learning rates $\eta = [\eta_0, \eta_1]$ for the NeRF and detector, respectively [S1].
*   Hyperparameters: $\alpha=3$ for importance sampling [S1], $\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ for loss weighting [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Steganographic NeRF model $\theta$ [S1].
*   Detector network $F_\psi$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $\theta$: The NeRF model parameters (typically a multi-layer perceptron mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to color and density) [S1].
*   $F_\psi$: The detector network, which takes a rendered image $x$ and the classifier output $F_{\psi c}(x)$ as input to recover hidden information [S1].
*   $F_{\psi c}$: The classifier network, which predicts the presence/class of hidden information in the rendered image [S1].
*   $m$: Importance mask for sampling, computed from the initial NeRF $\theta_0$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
1.  **Importance Mask:**
    $$ w = \frac{|\theta_0|^\alpha}{\sum |\theta_0|^\alpha}, \quad \alpha=3 $$
    $$ m \propto w $$
    [S1]

2.  **Steganographic Losses:**
    Let $F_{\psi c}(\theta(P))$ be the classifier output for the steganographic NeRF rendering, and $F_{\psi c}(\theta_0(P))$ be the classifier output for the initial (unmodified) NeRF rendering.
    *   **Classifier Loss ($L_{cdec}$):**
        $$ L_{cdec} = -\log F_{\psi c}(\theta(P)) - \log (1 - F_{\psi c}(\theta_0(P))) $$
        [S1]
    *   **Positive Detection Loss ($L_{+dec}$):**
        Defined in Equation (1) of [S1]. (Specific formula not provided in notes, but implied to drive detection of the hidden signal).
    *   **Negative Detection Loss ($L_{dec}$):**
        Defined in Equation (2) of [S1]. (Specific formula not provided in notes, but implied to suppress detection where no signal should exist).
    *   **Photometric Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rgb}$):**
        $$ L_{rgb} = \| \theta(P) - \theta_0(P) \| $$
        [S1]

3.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = \lambda_0 L_{cdec} + \lambda_1 L_{+dec} + \lambda_2 L_{dec} + \lambda_3 L_{rgb} $$
    [S1]

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  Initialize the NeRF model $\theta_0$ and the detector network $F_\psi$ [S1].
2.  Optimize $\theta_0$ on the training images $\{Y_i\}$ with poses $\{P_i\}$ using standard NeRF training to obtain the initial model [S1].
3.  Compute the importance mask $m$ for $\theta_0$ using the formula above [S1].
4.  For each training iteration $t$:
    a. Randomly sample a training pose $P_i$ and render the current NeRF $\theta(P_i)$ [S1].
    b. Compute the steganographic losses $L_{cdec}$, $L_{+dec}$, and $L_{dec}$ [S1].
    c. Compute the standard photometric reconstruction loss $L_{rgb}$ [S1].
    d. Combine the total loss $L$ [S1].
    e. Update the NeRF parameters $\theta$ using gradient descent with learning rate $\eta_0$:
       $$ \theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta_0 \cdot \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta} \cdot m \right) $$
       [S1]
    f. Update the detector parameters $\psi$ using gradient descent with learning rate $\eta_1$:
       $$ \psi \leftarrow \psi - \eta_1 \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial \psi} $$
       [S1]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_steganerf(training_images, poses, hidden_info, eta_0, eta_1, lambda_0, lambda_1, lambda_2, lambda_3, alpha=3):
    # 1. Initialize models
    theta = initialize_nerf()
    theta_0 = initialize_nerf() # Copy for reference
    F_psi = initialize_detector()
    F_psi_c = initialize_classifier()

    # 2. Standard NeRF training for initialization
    optimize_nerf(theta_0, training_images, poses)

    # 3. Compute importance mask
    # Note: Implementation detail for |theta_0|^alpha depends on density representation
    density = theta_0.density_field()
    w = density ** alpha
    m = w / sum(w)

    # 4. Training Loop
    for t in range(num_iterations):
        # Sample pose
        P = random_sample(poses)
        
        # Render
        y_stega = theta.render(P)
        y_init = theta_0.render(P)
        
        # Classifier outputs
        pred_stega = F_psi_c(y_stega)
        pred_init = F_psi_c(y_init)
        
        # Compute Steganographic Losses
        # L_cdec: Encourage detection in stega, non-detection in init
        L_cdec = -log(pred_stega) - log(1 - pred_init)
        
        # L_plus_dec and L_dec: Specific detection losses (formulas from [S1] Eq 1, 2)
        # Assuming these are defined based on detector output F_psi
        L_plus_dec = compute_positive_detection_loss(F_psi, y_stega, hidden_info)
        L_dec = compute_negative_detection_loss(F_psi, y_init)
        
        # Compute Photometric Loss
        L_rgb = norm(y_stega - y_init)
        
        # Total Loss
        L = lambda_0 * L_cdec + lambda_1 * L_plus_dec + lambda_2 * L_dec + lambda_3 * L_rgb
        
        # Update NeRF
        grad_theta = gradient(L, theta)
        # Apply importance mask to gradient
        grad_theta_masked = grad_theta * m
        theta = theta - eta_0 * grad_theta_masked
        
        # Update Detector
        grad_psi = gradient(L, F_psi)
        F_psi = F_psi - eta_1 * grad_psi

    return theta, F_psi
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\alpha=3$: Exponent for importance sampling [S1].
*   $\eta_0, \eta_1$: Learning rates for NeRF and Detector [S1].
*   $\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$: Loss weights [S1]. Typical values not specified in notes.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The use of logarithms in $L_{cdec}$ requires that classifier outputs are bounded away from 0 and 1 to avoid numerical instability [S1].
*   **Boundaries:** The importance mask $m$ guides sampling; if $\theta_0$ is poorly initialized, sampling may be inefficient [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the hidden information $I$ is not representable by the detector architecture, convergence may fail [S1].

## Subtopics

### Multi-Identity Embedding
A method to identify contributing users in collaborative NeRFs by embedding customized hidden information about specific clusters of training views [S1]. It differs from single-identity embedding by clustering views and assigning identities to clusters [S1]. Choose this when identifying multiple contributors in a large-scale collaborative scene [S1]. Links to [StegaNeRF Training Algorithm](14995-steganerf-training-algorithm.md).
*   **Definition:** Identify contributing users by embedding customized hidden information about specific clusters of training views [S1].
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Select $M$ anchor views with different viewing positions [S1].
    2.  Find the $K$ nearest neighbour views to each anchor to form clusters [S1].
    3.  Modify the classifier network $F_{\phi c}$ to output $M$-class cluster predictions plus one class for identities outside the $M$ clusters [S1].
    4.  Compute $L_{-dec}$ and $L_{dec}$ for poses outside the $M$ clusters to ensure no information is recovered for unmarked identities [S1].
*   **Parameters:** $M=3$ (Number of anchor views/clusters), $K=20$ (Number of nearest neighbours per anchor) [S1].

### Multi-Scene Embedding Settings
Embedding hidden information across multiple scenes using a single detector and classifier [S1]. It differs from single-scene embedding by handling multiple distinct scenes within one training loop [S1]. Choose this when embedding watermarks or identities across a dataset of scenes [S1]. Links to [StegaNeRF Training Algorithm](14995-steganerf-training-algorithm.md).
*   **Definition:**
    *   **One-for-All:** Embedding a common hidden image across multiple scenes using a single detector $F_\psi$ and classifier $F_{\psi c}$ [S1].
    *   **One-for-One:** Embedding scene-specific hidden images across multiple scenes using a single detector $F_\psi$ and classifier $F_{\psi c}$ [S1].
*   **Steps:** Sample one scene for every training epoch to handle multiple scenes [S1].
*   **Parameters:** Number of scenes varies (e.g., 3 LLFF scenes: flower, fern, fortress) [S1]. Training epochs increased due to increased data amount [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of training iterations and the cost of rendering NeRF views and detector forward passes. Multi-scene settings require more epochs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the NeRF model, detector, classifier, and training images [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** In multi-scene settings, performance drop compared to single-scene training is noticeable but expected due to per-scene fitting requirements [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Can embed various modalities (images, strings, text, audio) by modifying detector architecture [S1]. Effective for collaborative NeRF identity tracking [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Performance drop in multi-scene settings [S1]. Requires careful tuning of loss weights [S1].
*   **When to use:** When invisible watermarking or identity tracking is required in NeRFs [S1].
*   **When not:** When high photometric fidelity is the sole priority without steganographic needs [S1].

## Background
StegaNeRF was introduced in "StegaNeRF: Embedding Invisible Information within Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It extends NeRF capabilities to include steganography, allowing for the embedding of hidden images or identities [S1]. It is used in scenarios such as collaborative NeRF construction and copyright protection [S1].
