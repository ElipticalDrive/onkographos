# Geometric Priors and Depth Integration

Also called: Geometric regularization, Depth-guided NeRF, Signed Distance Field NeRF

## Overview
Geometric Priors and Depth Integration refers to a family of neural rendering methods that enhance the reconstruction quality of implicit radiance fields by incorporating explicit geometric constraints or external depth estimates. While standard NeRF relies solely on photometric consistency to infer scene structure, this family addresses the inherent ambiguity of inverse volume rendering—particularly in sparse-view settings or untextured regions—by enforcing structural priors. This family is distinct from sibling methods like [AdaNeRF](6195-adanerf.md) or [SinNeRF](6199-sinnerf.md), which focus on frequency-domain or adaptive encoding, by explicitly modifying the loss landscape or sampling strategy to align with known geometric structures, such as depth maps, surface normals, or multi-view stereo cost volumes.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family build on [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md) but diverge in how the geometric information is integrated into the optimization loop. The shared mechanism involves augmenting the standard photometric loss with geometric terms or modifying the ray sampling process to respect geometric boundaries.

**Geometric Loss Functions:**
Many methods introduce explicit loss terms that penalize deviations from geometric priors. Common forms include:
1.  **Depth Consistency Loss:** Minimizes the difference between the rendered depth (computed as the expected depth along the ray) and a prior depth map $D_{prior}$:
    $$ L_{depth} = \| D_{rendered} - D_{prior} \|_1 $$
2.  **Normal Smoothness:** Encourages surface normals derived from the density field to be smooth or aligned with estimated normals .
3.  **Epipolar Constraints:** Enforces that 3D points reconstructed from matched features in different views lie on the corresponding epipolar lines .

**Depth-Guided Sampling:**
Instead of uniform or hierarchical sampling along the entire ray, these methods use depth priors to concentrate samples near the estimated surface. This reduces computational cost and mitigates the "transparent" artifacts common in standard NeRF when geometry is ambiguous. For example, samples are clustered around the depth value predicted by a monocular depth estimator .

**Signed Distance Fields (SDF):**
Some variants replace the volumetric density $\sigma$ with a Signed Distance Field (SDF). The rendering integral is modified to integrate weights based on the SDF value relative to a truncation distance $tr$, effectively creating a thin shell around the surface rather than a volumetric blob. The weight for a sample at distance $d$ is often computed as:
$$ w = \sigma\left(\frac{d}{tr}\right) \cdot \sigma\left(-\frac{d}{tr}\right) $$
This allows for direct mesh extraction via Marching Cubes .

## Subtopics

### [SfMNeRF](8664-sfmnerf.md)
Integrates Structure-from-Motion (SfM) constraints, including matched feature positions and epipolar geometry, into the NeRF loss. It uses a pyramidal training strategy to handle large pixel motions. Choose this when sparse feature matches are available and multi-view consistency is critical.

### [DASNeRF](8668-dasnerf.md)
Uses monocular depth priors (e.g., DepthAnythingV2) to enforce relative depth ordering and structural consistency. It employs a dynamic sampling mechanism to reduce lighting artifacts. Choose this for sparse-view reconstruction where monocular depth estimators are accurate.

### [GeoNeRF](8710-geonerf.md)
Replaces the implicit MLP with an explicit 3D cost volume constructed via plane sweeping and refined by a Transformer. It extracts 2D features from all views and aggregates them in 3D. Choose this when generalizing to new scenes with multiple calibrated views is required.

### [Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction](11352-neural-rgb-d-surface-reconstruction.md)
Uses RGB-D input sequences to train a hybrid model of a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) for geometry and a radiance field for appearance. It outputs clean triangle meshes. Choose this when high-quality mesh extraction from noisy depth data is the primary goal.

### [DINER](11354-diner.md)
Leverages estimated dense depth maps to guide volumetric sampling for novel view synthesis from very sparse inputs (e.g., 4 images). It conditions the NeRF on depth deviations from source views. Choose this for extreme sparse-view scenarios where implicit correspondence fails.

### [Volume Rendering Prior (VRPrior)](14662-volume-rendering-prior-vrprior.md)
Replaces handcrafted UDF-to-density mappings with a learnable neural network that aggregates features from multi-scale neighborhoods along a ray. Choose this when robust density estimation from unsigned distance fields is needed without sensitivity to outliers.

### [Geometric Structure Constraint](16347-geometric-structure-constraint.md)
Applies Normal Smoothness and Bilateral Edge-Aware constraints to guide density field learning. Choose this when elevation estimates need improvement through structural regularization.

## Background
The problem of geometric ambiguity in NeRF was first highlighted in the original NeRF paper . Early attempts to resolve this included using multi-view stereo (MVS) cost volumes, as seen in [GeoNeRF](8710-geonerf.md), which predates the widespread use of deep learning-based depth estimation. The integration of monocular depth priors became popular with the advent of robust depth estimators like MiDaS and later DepthAnything, leading to methods like [DASNeRF](8668-dasnerf.md) and [DINER](11354-diner.md). The use of SDFs for surface reconstruction, pioneered by methods like [Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction](11352-neural-rgb-d-surface-reconstruction.md), shifted the focus from volumetric rendering to surface-based rendering, enabling direct mesh extraction.
