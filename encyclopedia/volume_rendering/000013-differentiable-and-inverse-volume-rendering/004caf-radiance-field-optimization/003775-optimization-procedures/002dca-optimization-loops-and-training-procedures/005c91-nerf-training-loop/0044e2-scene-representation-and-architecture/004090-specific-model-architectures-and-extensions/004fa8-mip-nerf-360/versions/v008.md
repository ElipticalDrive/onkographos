# Mip-NeRF 360

Also called: Unbounded NeRF, mip360

## Overview
Mip-NeRF 360 extends the quality of neural radiance fields to unbounded, 360-degree real-world scenes where the camera may point in any direction and content exists at arbitrary distances [S2][S5, unofficial]. It solves the problems of aliasing from multi-scale details and the ambiguity of infinite space by combining anti-aliased cone-casting with a non-linear scene contraction parameterization [S1, S2]. Unlike its parent [Mip-NeRF](10277-mip-nerf.md), which handles bounded scenes, Mip-NeRF 360 maps infinite 3D space into a bounded domain to allocate MLP capacity effectively, and unlike [NeRF](10276-nerf.md), it uses online distillation to train a large, high-capacity model efficiently [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated images with known camera poses (position and orientation) and intrinsics (focal length, pixel size).
*   **Outputs:** A differentiable neural network model (Proposal MLP and NeRF MLP) that renders novel views by computing pixel colors $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for any ray $\mathbf{r}$.

**Data Structures**
The method uses two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs):
1.  **Proposal MLP:** A smaller network predicting only volumetric density $\tau$. It is used to guide sampling.
2.  **NeRF MLP:** A larger network (approx. 15$\times$ the capacity of mip-NeRF) predicting density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Scene Parameterization (Contraction):**
    To handle unbounded scenes, 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ are mapped to a bounded domain using a contraction function [S5, unofficial]:
    $$
    \text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} 
    \mathbf{x} & \|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1 \\
    \left(2 - \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|}\right) \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \|\mathbf{x}\| > 1
    \end{cases}
    $$
    This maps the infinite exterior of the unit sphere to the shell between radius 1 and 2, allocating more MLP capacity to nearby content and compressing distant content [S2][S5, unofficial].

2.  **Ray Parameterization (Cone-Casting):**
    Instead of sampling points, the ray is divided into intervals $T_i = [t_i, t_{i+1})$ representing conical frustums [S4]. For each interval, the mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ of the frustum are computed based on the ray origin, direction, focal length, and pixel size [S2].

3.  **Integrated Positional Encoding:**
    The frustum statistics are encoded using integrated positional encoding to provide anti-aliasing [S2, S4]:
    $$
    \gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 
    \left[ \sin(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \right] \\
    \left[ \cos(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \right]
    \end{bmatrix} \right\}_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
    $$
    where $L$ is the number of frequency bands. High-frequency components are attenuated where the frustum variance is large [S5, unofficial].

4.  **Network Forward Pass:**
    The encoded features are passed to the MLPs. The Proposal MLP outputs density $\tau^p$. The NeRF MLP outputs density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S2]:
    $$
    (\tau_i, \mathbf{c}_i) = \text{MLP}_{\text{NeRF}}(\gamma(\mathbf{r}(T_i)); \Theta_{\text{NeRF}})
    $$
    $$
    \tau^p_i = \text{MLP}_{\text{Prop}}(\gamma(\mathbf{r}(T_i)); \Theta_{\text{Prop}})
    $$

5.  **Volume Rendering:**
    The pixel color is computed via alpha compositing [S2]:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_i w_i \mathbf{c}_i
    $$
    $$
    w_i = \left(1 - e^{-\tau_i(t_{i+1}-t_i)}\right) e^{-\sum_{i' < i} \tau_{i'}(t_{i'+1}-t_{i'})}
    $$

6.  **Hierarchical Sampling (Online Distillation):**
    *   **Coarse Pass:** Sample $N_c$ distances $t^c$ uniformly from $[t_n, t_f]$ (near/far planes) [S2]. Render coarse image $\mathbf{C}^c$ and compute coarse weights $\mathbf{w}^c$.
    *   **Fine Pass:** Sample $N_f$ distances $t^f$ from the histogram defined by $\mathbf{t}^c$ and $\mathbf{w}^c$ using inverse transform sampling [S2]. Render fine image $\mathbf{C}^f$.
    *   **Proposal Supervision:** The Proposal MLP is not supervised by image loss. Instead, it is supervised by the histogram weights $\mathbf{w}^c$ generated by the NeRF MLP. This "online distillation" allows the Proposal MLP to learn the density structure efficiently [S2].

7.  **Loss Function:**
    The total loss combines reconstruction error and a distortion regularizer [S2]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left( \frac{1}{10} \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}^c, \mathbf{C}^*) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}^f, \mathbf{C}^*) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{distortion}} \right)
    $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is mean squared error and $\mathbf{C}^*$ is the ground truth color [S2]. The distortion regularizer penalizes floaters and background collapse by encouraging weights to be concentrated [S2][S5, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def contract(x):
    """
    Non-linear scene contraction mapping infinite space to bounded domain.
    """
    norm = np.linalg.norm(x, axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    # Avoid division by zero
    norm = np.maximum(norm, 1e-8)
    mask = norm <= 1.0
    result = np.where(mask, x, (2.0 - 1.0 / norm) * (x / norm))
    return result

def integrated_positional_encoding(mu, sigma, num_bands):
    """
    Computes integrated positional encoding for conical frustums.
    mu: mean of frustum (shape: [..., 3])
    sigma: covariance of frustum (shape: [..., 3])
    num_bands: L, number of frequency bands
    """
    freq_bands = 2.0 ** np.arange(num_bands)
    # diag(sigma) extracts diagonal elements
    diag_sigma = np.diag(sigma, axis1=-2, axis2=-1) if sigma.ndim > 1 else sigma
    
    # Compute attenuation factor: exp(-2^(2l-1) * diag(sigma))
    # Note: 2^(2l-1) = 0.5 * 4^l
    attenuation = np.exp(-0.5 * (freq_bands ** 2) * diag_sigma[..., np.newaxis])
    
    # Compute sin/cos terms
    sin_term = np.sin(freq_bands * mu) * attenuation
    cos_term = np.cos(freq_bands * mu) * attenuation
    
    # Concatenate sin and cos for each band
    # Shape: [..., 2 * num_bands * 3]
    features = np.concatenate([sin_term, cos_term], axis=-2)
    return features

def volume_rendering(densities, colors, t_intervals):
    """
    Computes alpha compositing weights and final color.
    densities: tau (shape: [..., N])
    colors: c (shape: [..., N, 3])
    t_intervals: [t_0, t_1, ..., t_N] (shape: [..., N+1])
    """
    # Compute delta (interval lengths)
    deltas = t_intervals[..., 1:] - t_intervals[..., :-1]
    
    # Compute transmittance
    # alpha = 1 - exp(-tau * delta)
    alpha = 1.0 - np.exp(-densities * deltas)
    
    # T_i = product of (1 - alpha_j) for j < i
    # Using cumulative product for efficiency
    # (1 - alpha) is the transmittance through the interval
    transmittance_through = 1.0 - alpha
    # Cumulative product from left, shifted
    # T_0 = 1
    # T_i = T_{i-1} * (1 - alpha_{i-1})
    cum_prod = np.concatenate([np.ones_like(transmittance_through[..., :1]), 
                               np.cumprod(transmittance_through[..., :-1], axis=-1)], axis=-1)
    
    weights = alpha * cum_prod
    color = np.sum(weights[..., np.newaxis] * colors, axis=-2)
    return color, weights

def mipnerf360_train_step(images, poses, intrinsics, proposal_mlp, nerf_mlp, 
                          num_coarse, num_fine, near_plane, far_plane, num_bands):
    """
    Single training step for Mip-NeRF 360.
    """
    # 1. Generate rays from images
    rays = generate_rays(images, poses, intrinsics) # Returns origins, directions, pixel coords
    
    # 2. Coarse Sampling
    t_coarse = np.sort(np.random.uniform(near_plane, far_plane, (rays.origin.shape[0], num_coarse)), axis=-1)
    
    # 3. Coarse Rendering
    # Compute frustum stats for t_coarse intervals
    mu_coarse, sigma_coarse = compute_frustum_stats(rays, t_coarse)
    # Contract coordinates
    mu_coarse_contracted = contract(mu_coarse)
    
    # Encode
    features_coarse = integrated_positional_encoding(mu_coarse_contracted, sigma_coarse, num_bands)
    
    # Proposal MLP (density only)
    tau_prop_coarse = proposal_mlp(features_coarse)
    
    # NeRF MLP (density and color)
    nerf_output_coarse = nerf_mlp(features_coarse)
    tau_coarse = nerf_output_coarse.density
    color_coarse = nerf_output_coarse.color
    
    # Render
    color_rendered_coarse, weights_coarse = volume_rendering(tau_coarse, color_coarse, t_coarse)
    
    # 4. Fine Sampling (Histogram sampling based on coarse weights)
    t_fine = sample_from_histogram(t_coarse, weights_coarse, num_fine)
    t_fine = np.sort(t_fine, axis=-1)
    
    # 5. Fine Rendering
    mu_fine, sigma_fine = compute_frustum_stats(rays, t_fine)
    mu_fine_contracted = contract(mu_fine)
    features_fine = integrated_positional_encoding(mu_fine_contracted, sigma_fine, num_bands)
    
    nerf_output_fine = nerf_mlp(features_fine)
    tau_fine = nerf_output_fine.density
    color_fine = nerf_output_fine.color
    
    color_rendered_fine, weights_fine = volume_rendering(tau_fine, color_fine, t_fine)
    
    # 6. Loss Calculation
    # Reconstruction Loss
    loss_recon_coarse = np.mean((color_rendered_coarse - images) ** 2)
    loss_recon_fine = np.mean((color_rendered_fine - images) ** 2)
    
    # Distortion Loss (Regularizer)
    # Encourages weights to be concentrated, reducing floaters
    # Formula involves weighted average of interval centers and distances
    loss_distortion = compute_distortion_loss(t_fine, weights_fine, tau_fine)
    
    total_loss = 0.1 * loss_recon_coarse + loss_recon_fine + lambda_dist * loss_distortion
    
    # 7. Proposal MLP Update (Online Distillation)
    # Supervise proposal density to match coarse weights distribution
    # This is typically done by minimizing the KL divergence or MSE between 
    # proposal density and the normalized coarse weights
    loss_proposal = compute_proposal_loss(tau_prop_coarse, weights_coarse)
    
    return total_loss, loss_proposal
```

*   **Number of fine samples ($N_f$):** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Precision:** The contraction function maps infinity to a finite radius; care must be taken with numerical stability near $\|\mathbf{x}\|=1$ [S5, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** The method assumes the scene is unbounded; if the scene is strictly bounded, the contraction may waste capacity on empty space outside the scene [S2].
*   **Floaters:** Without the distortion regularizer, unbounded scenes suffer from "floaters" (spurious geometry) and background collapse [S2][S5, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is approximately 2$\times$ that of mip-NeRF, taking 24-48 hours on the Blender dataset [S2, S1]. It accelerates training by 300% compared to NeRF in Detail's learned proposer approach [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The total model capacity is approximately 15$\times$ larger than mip-NeRF due to the separate Proposal and NeRF MLPs [S2].
*   **Performance:**
    *   NeRF Synthetic (Blender): PSNR = 32.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1].
    *   Mip-NeRF 360 Unbounded Dataset: PSNR = 29.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1].
    *   LLFF Forward-Facing: PSNR = 26.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles unbounded scenes effectively; reduces mean squared error by 57% compared to mip-NeRF; produces realistic views and detailed depth maps for intricate real-world scenes [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Poor surface accuracy because it uses a density representation without explicit surface geometry [S1]. Slower rendering speed (30+ sec/image at 1080p) compared to grid-based methods like Instant-NGP [S1].

## Background
Mip-NeRF 360 was introduced in November 2021 as an extension of Mip-NeRF to handle unbounded, 360-degree real-world scenes [S2, S3]. It was developed to overcome the limitations of NeRF and mip-NeRF, which struggled with large, unbounded scenes due to aliasing and the ambiguity of infinite space [S2]. The method builds on the cone-casting anti-aliasing technique from Mip-NeRF and introduces scene contraction, online distillation, and a distortion regularizer [S2][S5, unofficial]. It is widely used as a baseline for unbounded neural rendering tasks [S4].
