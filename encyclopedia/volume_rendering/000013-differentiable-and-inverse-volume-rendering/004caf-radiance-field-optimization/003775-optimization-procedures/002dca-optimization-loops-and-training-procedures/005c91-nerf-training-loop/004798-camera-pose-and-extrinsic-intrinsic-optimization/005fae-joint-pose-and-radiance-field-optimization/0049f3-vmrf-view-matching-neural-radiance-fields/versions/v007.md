# VMRF (View Matching Neural Radiance Fields)
Also called: View Matching NeRF

## Overview
VMRF is a method for novel view synthesis that learns Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations without prior knowledge of camera poses or hand-crafted camera pose distributions [S1]. It solves the problem of pose estimation in texture-poor or repetitive scenes where classical Structure-from-Motion (SfM) fails, and where direct absolute pose estimation methods (like GNeRF) are sensitive to out-of-dataset views [S1]. VMRF distinguishes itself by estimating **relative** pose transformations via a view matching scheme using unbalanced optimal transport (UOT), rather than predicting absolute poses directly from images [S1]. It builds on [Joint Pose and Radiance Field Optimization](17928-joint-pose-and-radiance-field-optimization.md) by introducing a differentiable feature-matching step to guide pose calibration.

## How it works
VMRF operates in an iterative, end-to-end optimization loop, initially following the hybrid training scheme of GNeRF [S1]. It begins with randomly initialized camera poses and a coarse NeRF, then refines both the NeRF parameters and the camera poses jointly.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $n$ RGB images $I = \{I_1, I_2, ..., I_n\}$ of a static scene. No camera poses are provided.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and estimated camera poses $\hat{\Phi} = \{\hat{\phi}_1, ..., \hat{\phi}_n\}$.

### Data Structures
1.  **NeRF Model ($F_\Theta$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) parameterizing the 5D scene function $F_\Theta(x, b) \rightarrow (c, \sigma)$, mapping a 3D point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $b \in S^2$ to RGB color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
2.  **Camera Pose ($\phi$):** Represented as a $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix containing rotation and translation. Rotation is encoded as a continuous 6D embedding, and position as a 3D Euclidean embedding [S1].
3.  **Feature Sets:**
    *   Source feature set $\mathbf{f}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$ extracted from the rendered image.
    *   Target feature set $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$ extracted from the real image.
    *   Parameters: $l = 64$ (number of feature vectors), $d = 256$ (dimension of feature vectors) [S1].
4.  **Transport Plan ($T$):** A matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ where $T_{ij}$ represents the amount of mass moved from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].
5.  **Relative Transformation Predictor ($RTP$):** A Vision Transformer-based network that outputs a 6-dimensional vector $[t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z]$ representing relative translation and rotation [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization:**
    Initialize the set of randomly sampled camera poses $\Phi' = \{\phi'_i, i \in [1, n]\}$ [S1]. Initialize the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and a discriminator $D$ [S1].

2.  **Coarse NeRF Training (Adversarial Phase):**
    Train the coarse NeRF $F$ and discriminator $D$ using adversarial loss to ensure generated images have a similar distribution to real images [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{adv}(F, D) = \mathbb{E}_{I \sim P_{data}} [\log(D(I))] + \mathbb{E}_{F(\Phi') \sim P_g} [\log(1 - D(F(\Phi')))] $$
    where $P_{data}$ is the distribution of real images and $P_g$ is the distribution of generated images [S1].

3.  **Feature Extraction:**
    For each image $I_i$ and its corresponding rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ (generated using current pose $\phi'_i$ and NeRF $F$):
    *   Extract source features $\mathbf{f}_s$ from $\hat{I}_i$ using a pre-trained VGG-19 network [S1].
    *   Extract target features $\mathbf{f}_t$ from $I_i$ using a pre-trained VGG-19 network [S1].

4.  **View Matching via Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT):**
    Compute the cost matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ based on feature similarity:
    $$ M = 1 - \frac{\mathbf{f}_s \cdot \mathbf{f}_t^T}{\|\mathbf{f}_s\| \|\mathbf{f}_t\|} $$
    where $M_{ij}$ denotes the moving cost from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].
    
    Solve for the optimal transport plan $T^*$ by minimizing the entropic regularized UOT objective:
    $$ T^* = \arg \min_T \left[ \sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} M_{ij} - \eta H(T) + \epsilon KL(T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_s) + \epsilon KL(T^T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_t) \right] $$
    where:
    *   $\eta = 0.005$ is the entropic regularization parameter [S1].
    *   $H(T) = -\sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} \log(T_{ij} + 1)$ is the entropic regularization term [S1].
    *   $\epsilon > 0$ is the KL divergence regularization parameter [S1].
    *   $\mu_s$ and $\mu_t$ are the discrete source and target distributions defined by the feature sets [S1].
    *   The solution is computed using the Sinkhorn algorithm on the Fenchel-Legendre dual form [S1].
    *   *Note:* UOT is used instead of standard OT because occlusions cause inconsistent total mass between views, leading to false matches with standard OT [S1].

5.  **Pose Calibration (Relative Transformation Prediction):**
    Predict the relative pose transformation between the real image and the rendered image using the transport plan $T^*$:
    $$ [t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z] = RTP(T^*) $$
    where $RTP$ is the relative transformation predictor [S1].
    
    Construct the relative rotation matrix $\Delta R$ and translation vector $\Delta t$:
    $$ \Delta R = R_x(\theta_x) R_y(\theta_y) R_z(\theta_z) $$
    $$ \Delta t = [t_x, t_y, t_z]^T $$
    where $R_x, R_y, R_z$ are standard rotation matrices [S1].
    
    Calibrate the randomized pose $\phi'_i$ to estimate the real pose $\hat{\phi}_i$:
    $$ \hat{\phi}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta R & \Delta t \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \phi'_i $$
    [S1]

6.  **NeRF and Pose Optimization:**
    Optimize the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and the estimated poses $\hat{\Phi}$ using the photometric loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_P(F, \hat{\Phi}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \| I_i - F(\hat{\phi}_i) \|^2 $$
    [S1]
    
    Additionally, train the relative transformation predictor using a calibration loss $\mathcal{L}_{ca}$ in an unsupervised manner using pairs of randomly initialized poses [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{ca}(RTP) = \mathbb{E} \| F_{ca}(RTP(T^*_{ab}), \phi'_a) - \phi'_b \|^2 $$
    where $T^*_{ab}$ is the transport plan between renders from $\phi'_a$ and $\phi'_b$, and $F_{ca}$ is a coarse NeRF [S1].

7.  **Iteration:**
    Repeat steps 3-6 end-to-end. The method uses a hybrid and iterative optimization scheme similar to GNeRF [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Low Texture/Repetitive Patterns:** Classical SfM fails here. VMRF avoids this by not relying on SfM [S1].
*   **Out-of-Dataset Views:** Direct absolute pose estimation (e.g., GNeRF) is disturbed by views beyond the training dataset if the pose distribution is inaccurate. VMRF mitigates this by using relative pose estimation via view matching [S1].
*   **Occlusion:** Causes inconsistent total mass between source and target feature distributions. Standard OT fails by matching outliers. VMRF uses Unbalanced OT (UOT) with KL divergence penalties to handle this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The dominant cost is the volume rendering and the UOT computation. UOT via Sinkhorn iterations is $O(l^2 \cdot k)$ where $k$ is the number of iterations [S1]. Rendering is $O(N \cdot W \cdot H)$ per image [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the NeRF parameters, the transport plan $T$ ($l \times l$), and feature sets ($l \times d$) [S1].
*   **Performance:** VMRF outperforms state-of-the-art methods like GNeRF qualitatively and quantitatively on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Does not require prior knowledge of camera poses or hand-crafted pose distributions. Robust to out-of-dataset views and low-texture scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on a pre-trained VGG-19 for feature extraction, which may not capture all semantic details. The UOT optimization adds computational overhead compared to standard OT [S1].

## Background
VMRF was proposed by Jiahui Zhang, Fangneng Zhan, Rongliang Wu, Yingchen Yu, Wenqing Zhang, Bai Song, Xiaoqin Zhang, and Shijian Lu in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous pose-free NeRF methods like GNeRF, which required accurate, hand-crafted camera pose distributions for training their inversion networks [S1]. VMRF introduces the concept of view matching via unbalanced optimal transport to estimate relative poses, enabling effective NeRF training without prior pose knowledge [S1]. It is used in novel view synthesis tasks on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets [S1].
