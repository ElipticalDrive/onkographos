# Volume Rendering Integration and Forward Pass

Also called: Volume Rendering Integration, Alpha Compositing Forward Pass, NeRF Rendering Forward Pass

## Overview
This family defines the differentiable forward pass that synthesizes a 2D image from a continuous 5D radiance field by numerically approximating the volume rendering integral along camera rays [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of mapping implicit scene representations (neural networks or dense grids) to pixel colors by integrating predicted volume density and view-dependent color, using probabilistic density functions to determine sample contributions rather than deterministic surface intersections . It sets itself apart from standard ray tracing by relying on alpha compositing to handle transparency and occlusion within a medium, and from [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) by focusing specifically on the mathematical integration and compositing mechanics rather than ray generation or adaptive pruning strategies.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family compute the final pixel color $\hat{C}(r)$ and optionally depth $\hat{D}(r)$ for a camera ray $r(t) = o + td$ by integrating samples along the ray segment $[t_n, t_f]$ [S1, unofficial]. The core mechanism involves:
1.  **Sampling:** Discretizing the ray interval into $N$ samples with positions $t_i$ and 3D coordinates $x_i = o + t_i d$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Querying:** Obtaining volume density $\sigma_i$ and color $c_i$ (or emission $L_e$) at each sample point [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Alpha Compositing:** Converting density and inter-sample distance $\delta_i$ into opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Transmittance:** Computing accumulated transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, representing the probability of the ray reaching sample $i$ without interaction [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Weighting:** Calculating sample weights $w_i = \alpha_i T_i$ .
6.  **Compositing:** Summing weighted contributions: $\hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i c_i$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Background Handling:** Adding the contribution of rays that exit the scene without being fully absorbed, typically assuming a white background: $\hat{C}_{final}(r) = \hat{C}(r) + (1 - \sum w_i) \cdot c_{bg}$ .

Numerical stability is critical; transmittance is often computed via `cumprod` of $(1 - \alpha_i + \epsilon)$ with `exclusive=True` to prevent gradient NaNs when $\sigma_i \delta_i$ is large or small .

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing)](5124-nerf-rendering-alpha-compositing.md)
The standard differentiable volume rendering technique introduced by Mildenhall et al., mapping a continuous 5D implicit radiance field to a 2D image using neural network predictions. It uses stratified sampling for coarse networks and potentially density-weighted sampling for fine networks. It is chosen when implementing the original NeRF formulation with MLP-based density/color prediction. It differs from the grid-based integrator by relying on neural network queries for $\sigma$ and $c$.

### [NeRF-style Volume Rendering Integrator](5964-nerf-style-volume-rendering-integrator.md)
A simplified integrator that accumulates emission along a ray through a medium backed by a dense grid rather than a neural network, with no direction-dependent emission. It explicitly handles primal/dual modes for gradient computation in inverse rendering contexts. It is chosen for grid-based inverse rendering or when avoiding the overhead of neural network queries. It differs from standard NeRF by using dense grid storage and explicit mode handling for gradients.

### NeRF Forward Pass with Alpha Masking and Two-Stage Shading
A variant that incorporates alpha masking for empty space culling and NDC (Normalized Device Coordinates) ray simulation [S1, unofficial]. It adjusts distances by ray norm, normalizes view directions, and combines ray validity with an alpha mask to mark rays invalid if they hit empty space [S1, unofficial]. It computes sigma using feature-to-density conversion and applies raw2alpha with distance scaling [S1, unofficial]. Depth is computed as a weighted sum of z_vals, adjusted by background weight and ray direction Z component, and clamped if NDC Euclidean depth simulation is enabled [S1, unofficial]. This variant is chosen when working with NDC coordinate systems or when explicit alpha masks are available to prune empty rays.

## Background
The core alpha compositing formulation was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in the original NeRF paper [S1, unofficial]. The technique approximates the volume rendering integral, a standard concept in computer graphics . The specific implementation details regarding stratified sampling, transmittance computation via cumprod, and background handling are widely adopted in subsequent NeRF implementations [S1, unofficial]. The grid-based integrator variant appears in unofficial implementations and tools like Joint-TensoRF [S1, unofficial].
