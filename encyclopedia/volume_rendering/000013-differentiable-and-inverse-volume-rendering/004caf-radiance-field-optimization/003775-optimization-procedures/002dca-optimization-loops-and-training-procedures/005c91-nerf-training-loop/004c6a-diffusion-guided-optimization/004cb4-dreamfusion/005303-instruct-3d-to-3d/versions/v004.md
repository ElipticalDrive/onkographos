# Instruct 3D-to-3D

Also called: Instruct 3D-to-3D conversion

## Overview
Instruct 3D-to-3D is a method for performing 3D scene editing or conversion guided by natural language text instructions [S1]. It solves the problem of modifying an existing 3D scene's geometry and appearance according to specific textual commands, such as "make the car red" or "add a roof" [S1]. It distinguishes itself from text-to-3D generation methods like [DreamFusion](12087-dreamfusion.md) by conditioning the optimization on a source 3D scene rather than generating from noise, and from other 3D editing methods like [SKED](12087-dreamfusion.md#sked) by leveraging InstructPix2Pix for instruction following [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
1. A source 3D scene representation (e.g., a NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting model) [S1].
2. A text instruction describing the desired modification [S1].

**Outputs:**
1. An optimized target 3D scene representation that satisfies the text instruction while preserving unmodified parts of the source scene [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the latent representation of the 3D scene (e.g., neural radiance field parameters or Gaussian attributes). The specific internal data structures depend on the underlying 3D representation (NeRF or 3DGS) but generally involve continuous fields or discrete points with associated attributes [S1].

**Steps:**
1. **Conditional Integration:** The source 3D scene is integrated as a conditional input to the optimization process [S1]. This ensures that the optimization is anchored to the existing geometry and appearance, allowing for targeted edits rather than wholesale regeneration.
2. **SDS Loss Optimization:** The target scene parameters are optimized using the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss [S1]. The SDS loss leverages a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (specifically InstructPix2Pix in the original implementation) to provide gradient signals that guide the 3D scene towards the visual characteristics described by the text instruction [S1]. The diffusion model is conditioned on the text instruction and the current rendered image of the 3D scene.
3. **Dynamic Scaling:** Dynamic scaling is integrated into the optimization loop to effectively modify scene geometry [S1]. This mechanism allows the method to handle geometric changes, such as expansion or contraction of objects, which are common in text-guided edits [S1].

**Formulas:**
The core optimization objective minimizes the SDS loss:
$$ \nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}_{SDS} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon} \left[ w(t) ( \epsilon_{\theta_t}(v_t; y, c) - \epsilon ) \frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta} \right] $$
Where:
*   $\theta$: Parameters of the 3D scene representation.
*   $v_t$: Noisy latent image at timestep $t$.
*   $\epsilon$: Predicted noise from the diffusion model.
*   $\epsilon_{\theta_t}$: The diffusion model's noise prediction.
*   $y$: The text instruction.
*   $c$: The conditional input, which is the source 3D scene (or its rendered views) [S1].
*   $w(t)$: Weighting function for the diffusion timestep.
*   $v$: The clean latent image corresponding to the current 3D scene parameters.

**Parameters:**
*   **Diffusion Model:** InstructPix2Pix is used as the base diffusion model [S1].
*   **Optimization Steps:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Learning Rate:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Geometric Consistency:** Without proper conditioning, edits may distort the underlying geometry. Dynamic scaling helps mitigate this [S1].
*   **Instruction Adherence:** The quality of the edit depends on the clarity of the text instruction and the capability of the InstructPix2Pix model to interpret it [S1].
*   **Source Scene Quality:** The method assumes the source 3D scene is a reasonable starting point. Poor initial geometry may lead to suboptimal edits [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Focaldreamer](14868-focaldreamer.md)
Focaldreamer is a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) editing method that performs localized geometric and textural modifications guided by text prompts [14868-focaldreamer.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [I3DS](16419-i3ds.md) by explicitly utilizing rendered normal maps as shape encodings for the text-to-image (T2I) model and employing a pixelwise discriminative mask to unify base and editable textures, thereby ensuring geometric consistency during optimization [14868-focaldreamer.md].

### I3DS
I3DS is an iterative framework for super-resolving a Low-Resolution (LR) Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) into a Super-Resolution (SR) NeRF by alternating between 2D image upscaling via diffusion models and 3D NeRF synchronization to ensure view consistency [S1]. It distinguishes itself from methods that jointly optimize 2D and 3D parameters in a single loop by segregating the process into distinct upscaling and synchronization stages, allowing it to achieve higher quality than prior-based methods while being significantly faster than diffusion-guided methods like DreamFusion [S1].

### Pix2Vex Reconstructor
Pix2Vex Reconstructor is a network translating images to a 3D model, based on a pix2pix convolutional and deconvolutional ResNet, outputting vertex offsets from a base model [S2, unofficial]. It predicts offsets from a base model (uniform sphere with predefined topology) rather than absolute coordinates to improve convergence smoothness [S2, unofficial]. It optionally predicts symmetry by splitting the model into four quarters, predicting shifts for one quarter, mirroring it, and predicting shifts for the mirrored quarter until all four are predicted [S2, unofficial]. It also predicts uniform translation and scaling values to reduce offset magnitude and improve stability [S2, unofficial]. Regularization on the 3D mesh considers angles between normals, edge lengths, and curvature [S2, unofficial]. The input is images, with an internal state of a single representative image from the pix2pix component. The output is $3 \times \#vertices$ values from two fully connected layers, passed through a sigmoid layer, then linearly transformed to range $[-1, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Allows for precise, text-guided editing of existing 3D scenes without requiring 3D training data [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the underlying diffusion model (InstructPix2Pix) and the source 3D scene [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When you have an existing 3D scene and want to modify it based on text instructions.
*   **When Not to Use:** When generating a 3D scene from scratch (use [DreamFusion](12087-dreamfusion.md)) or when high-fidelity super-resolution is needed without text guidance (use [I3DS](16419-i3ds.md)).

## Background
Instruct 3D-to-3D was introduced as a method for 3D editing following text instructions, utilizing InstructPix2Pix [S1]. It builds on the SDS loss framework popularized by [DreamFusion](12087-dreamfusion.md) but adapts it for conditional editing rather than generation [S1]. The method is part of a broader trend in 3D editing that leverages 2D diffusion models for 3D optimization [S1].
