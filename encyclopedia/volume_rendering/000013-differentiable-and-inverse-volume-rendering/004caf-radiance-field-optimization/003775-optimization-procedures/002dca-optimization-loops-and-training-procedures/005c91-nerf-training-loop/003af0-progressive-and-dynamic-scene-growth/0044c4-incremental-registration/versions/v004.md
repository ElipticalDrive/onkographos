# Incremental Registration

Also called: Incremental CONfidence (ICON) registration, Incremental Image Registration

## Overview
Incremental Registration is a training strategy that registers camera poses sequentially following the temporal order of input images, leveraging the intuition that adjacent camera motions in videos are smooth [S2]. It solves the problem of non-convex pose optimization and local minima in joint pose and radiance field optimization by providing good initializations for new frames based on previously registered ones [S2]. Unlike global registration methods that optimize all poses simultaneously, this method builds the trajectory incrementally, freezing older parameters to prevent catastrophic forgetting and reduce computational cost [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sequence of unposed images $\{I_1, \dots, I_N\}$; a scene regressor $\Phi$ (e.g., a neural network predicting 3D coordinates or features); a batch size $B$ for incremental addition.
**Outputs:** Optimized NeRF/radiance field parameters $\Theta$; optimized camera poses $\{P_1, \dots, P_N\}$; registered image set.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Training Buffer $\mathcal{D}_{buf}$:** A set of images currently used for training. Initially contains $\{I_1, I_2\}$.
*   **Pose History $\mathcal{P}$:** Stores optimized poses $P_i$.
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $\Theta$: Radiance field parameters.
    *   $P_i$: Camera pose (extrinsics) for image $I_i$.
    *   $k$: Iteration interval for adding new frames.
    *   $T_{freeze}$: Number of iterations to freeze learning rates after registration.
    *   $T_{finetune}$: Number of iterations to finetune the scene regressor during incremental steps.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Jointly optimize NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and poses $\{P_1, P_2\}$ using the first two frames $\{I_1, I_2\}$ [S2].
2.  **Iterative Registration Loop:** Repeat until all $N$ images are registered:
    a.  **Add Frame:** After every $k$ iterations, add a new frame $I_i$ to the training buffer $\mathcal{D}_{buf}$ [S2].
    b.  **Pose Initialization:** Initialize the pose $P_i$ of the new frame using the previous pose $P_{i-1}$ [S2]. This leverages the smooth motion prior [S2].
    c.  **Freeze:** Freeze the learning rates for poses $\{P_j\}_{j < i}$ and NeRF parameters $\Theta$ for $T_{freeze}$ iterations [S2]. This prevents the new frame from disrupting previously learned geometry and poses [S1, unofficial][S2].
    d.  **Optimize:** Optimize the new pose $P_i$ and potentially refine $\Theta$ (if not fully frozen) or the scene regressor $\Phi$ [S1, unofficial].
    e.  **Reference Selection:** In subsequent batches, select a reference image from the registered images that connects to the most unregistered images [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Convergence & Expansion:** Upon training convergence for the current batch, expand the training buffer by selecting the next batch of images [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Finalization:** After all $N$ images are registered, apply a learning rate decay schedule to $\Theta$ and $\{P_i\}$ [S2]. Optionally, perform a final global optimization of all camera poses using photometric loss to correct accumulated errors [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Pose Initialization:** $P_i^{(0)} = P_{i-1}$ [S2].
*   **Photometric Loss (for pose refinement):**
    $$ L = \sum_{i,k} \rho(\|d_{i,k} \cdot \nu_{i,k} - (X_i - C_k)\|_2) $$
    Where $C_k$ is the camera center for image $I_k$, $d_{i,k}$ is a scaling factor, $\nu_{i,k}$ is the ray direction, and $X_i$ is the 3D point [S1, unofficial].
*   **Inlier Threshold:** A new image $I_k$ is added only if the number of inliers $s_{inlier}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., 5,000) [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def incremental_registration(images, scene_regressor, nerf_params, k, T_freeze, T_finetune):
    """
    images: List of unposed images [I1, ..., IN]
    scene_regressor: Neural network predicting 3D coords/features
    nerf_params: Radiance field parameters Theta
    k: Iteration interval for adding new frames
    T_freeze: Iterations to freeze old poses/Theta
    T_finetune: Iterations to finetune scene regressor
    """
    N = len(images)
    # Step 1: Initialize with first two frames
    current_buffer = [images[0], images[1]]
    poses = optimize_poses_and_nerf(current_buffer, scene_regressor, nerf_params) # Joint optimization
    
    # Initialize pose history
    pose_history = {1: poses[0], 2: poses[1]}
    
    i = 3 # Next frame to register
    
    while i <= N:
        # Step 2a: Add new frame after k iterations
        # Note: In practice, this loop runs for 'k' iterations of training on current_buffer
        for step in range(k):
            train_step(current_buffer, scene_regressor, nerf_params, poses)
            
        # Step 2b: Add new frame and initialize pose
        new_image = images[i-1] # 0-indexed
        current_buffer.append(new_image)
        
        # Initialize pose P_i based on P_{i-1}
        prev_pose = pose_history[i-1]
        new_pose = init_pose(prev_pose) # P_i = P_{i-1}
        poses[i] = new_pose
        pose_history[i] = new_pose
        
        # Step 2c: Freeze old parameters
        freeze_params(poses[1:i], nerf_params, duration=T_freeze)
        
        # Step 2d: Optimize new pose and/or refine regressor
        # Finetune scene regressor for T_finetune iterations
        finetune_scene_regressor(current_buffer, scene_regressor, T_finetune)
        
        # Optimize the new pose P_i
        optimize_pose(poses[i], current_buffer, scene_regressor, nerf_params)
        
        # Step 2e: Select reference image for next batches
        # Select reference from registered images connecting to most unregistered
        ref_idx = select_reference(pose_history, i, N)
        
        i += 1
        
    # Step 4: Finalization
    apply_lr_decay(nerf_params, poses)
    global_optimize_poses(poses, scene_regressor, nerf_params)
    
    return poses, nerf_params
```

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   $k$: 1,000 iterations for CO3D/HO3D datasets; 500 iterations for LLFF dataset [S2].
*   $T_{freeze}$: 100,000 iterations for HO3D and CO3D; 30,000 iterations for LLFF [S2].
*   $T_{finetune}$: 1,000 iterations for LLFF; 1,500 iterations for Mip-NeRF360 [S1, unofficial].
*   Inlier threshold $s_{inlier}$: 5,000 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Local Minima:** Optimizing poses and NeRF using photometric losses is highly non-convex. Incorrectly optimized poses can mislead the NeRF, causing re-registration errors [S2].
*   **Accumulated Error:** Errors accumulate during incremental registration. A final global optimization stage is recommended to correct this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion:** Avoid sampling rays from occluded regions to prevent incorrect pose estimation [S2].
*   **Break Point:** Without incremental registration, a rotation difference of 20 degrees in the trajectory can cause optimization failure [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of frames $N$, as each frame is registered once. However, the total training time is significant due to the iterative freezing and finetuning steps [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the scene regressor and NeRF parameters. The training buffer grows incrementally [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles large sequences efficiently by avoiding global optimization of all poses at once. Leverages temporal smoothness for robust initialization [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to the initial pair $\{I_1, I_2\}$. Errors in early frames propagate to later frames [S1, unofficial]. Requires careful tuning of $k$ and $T_{freeze}$ [S2].
*   **When to Use:** Video sequences or image collections with smooth camera motion where global registration is unstable or computationally expensive [S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** Static scenes with no temporal order or highly discontinuous camera motion [S2].

## Background
Incremental Registration was proposed in the ICON (Incremental CONfidence) paper by Wang et al. (2024) [S2]. It builds on the intuition that camera motions in videos are smooth, addressing the breaking point of 20-degree rotation differences observed in global optimization methods [S2]. The method is part of a broader trend in differentiable volume rendering to handle unposed images by integrating pose optimization into the training loop [S2].
