# Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth


## Overview
This family of methods addresses the limitations of static NeRF training by dynamically expanding the scene representation or the training dataset during optimization. Unlike static approaches that optimize a fixed architecture on a fixed dataset, these methods adaptively increase model capacity or view coverage to handle complex geometries, large-scale environments, or sequential data streams. They distinguish themselves from sibling methods like [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md) and [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md) by focusing on the structural or data-driven evolution of the training process itself, rather than just the loss functions or acceleration structures.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves a loop where the scene representation $\Phi$ and/or the training dataset $\mathcal{D}$ are modified during optimization. Common principles include:
1.  **Adaptive Expansion:** The model or data is not fixed at initialization. Capacity is added (e.g., new network branches, new blocks) or data is added (e.g., new views, new frames) based on specific criteria such as reconstruction error, uncertainty, or temporal order.
2.  **Iterative Refinement:** After expansion, the entire system (or relevant parts) is re-optimized to integrate the new components. This often involves resetting or adjusting learning rates to accommodate the new degrees of freedom without catastrophic forgetting.
3.  **Criteria-Driven Growth:** Growth is triggered by measurable signals, such as high uncertainty in query points, the arrival of new sensor data, or the completion of a training interval.

## Subtopics

### [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md)
A procedure that adaptively increases model capacity by adding network branches in regions of high reconstruction uncertainty. It clusters high-error query points and spawns child networks to refine predictions locally. Choose this when the scene has complex local details that a single global network cannot capture efficiently.

### [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md)
A strategy for Panoramic NeRF that synthesizes novel views from a single input panorama by iteratively generating synthetic reference views to resolve occluded regions. It uses collaborative RGBD inpainting to fill missing content. Choose this when dealing with panoramic inputs where occluded regions need semantic and geometric coherence.

### [Incremental Registration](11341-incremental-registration.md)
A training strategy that registers camera poses sequentially following the temporal order of video frames. It solves non-convex pose optimization by leveraging smooth adjacent camera motions and freezes previously registered parameters. Choose this for unposed video sequences where joint optimization is unstable.

### [Online Incremental NeRF Optimization](12066-online-incremental-nerf-optimization.md)
Addresses real-time or near-real-time reconstruction from sequentially captured data. It updates the radiance field incrementally as new observations arrive and includes active planning for next-best views. Choose this for dynamic environments or robotic applications requiring continuous updates.

### CityNeRF
**Definition:** A progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the NeRF model and training set [S1].
**Steps:**
1.  Starts by fitting distant views with shallow basic blocks [S1].
2.  New blocks are continuously attached as training iterates to adapt to details in far-near views [S1].
3.  Effectively activates high-frequency channels in position encoding and unfolds more complex details as training progresses [S1].

## Background
The concept of progressive and dynamic growth in NeRF stems from the need to handle large-scale scenes and complex details that static architectures struggle with. Block-NeRF (Tancik et al., 2022) introduced a "total-part-total" approach to decompose large urban environments into separately trained blocks [S1]. Building on this, CityNeRF (Xiangli et al., 2021) proposed a progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the model and training set, starting with shallow blocks for distant views and adding complexity as training progresses [S1]. This approach helps activate high-frequency channels in position encoding, allowing for the unfolding of complex details [S1]. Other methods like Dynamic Growth Parameters (2022) focus on uncertainty-driven branch addition, while Progressive View Synthesis Training addresses the specific challenges of panoramic inputs by iteratively expanding the view set [S1]. Incremental Registration methods address the challenge of unposed video data by registering poses sequentially .
