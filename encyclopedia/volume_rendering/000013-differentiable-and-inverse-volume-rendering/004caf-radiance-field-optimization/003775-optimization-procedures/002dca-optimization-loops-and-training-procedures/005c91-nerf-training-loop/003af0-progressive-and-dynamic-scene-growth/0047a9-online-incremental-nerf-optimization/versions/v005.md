# Online Incremental NeRF Optimization
Also called: Incremental NeRF Training, Online NeRF

## Overview
Online Incremental NeRF Optimization addresses the problem of reconstructing 3D scenes from a continuous stream of incoming camera views, rather than a fixed, pre-collected dataset. It differs from offline methods like DVGO [S1, unofficial] by updating the scene representation $\Phi$ dynamically as new observations arrive, enabling active exploration and real-time adaptation. This approach is essential for robotics and large-scale mapping where data volume is unbounded and memory constraints require discarding older frames [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates as a closed-loop system where the agent plans the next best view, captures new observations, and incrementally updates the radiance field.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Current scene representation $\Phi$ (e.g., feature grids and decoder), current camera pose $P_t$, and new observation (image $I_{new}$ and pose $P_{new}$).
*   **Output:** Updated scene representation $\Phi'$ and potentially the next camera pose $P_{t+1}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Scene Representation $\Phi$:** Typically consists of a spatial feature storage (e.g., hash grid, voxel grid) and a decoding network (MLP). The feature storage maps 3D coordinates to feature vectors.
*   **Training Set $\mathcal{D}$:** A buffer of recent observations $(I_i, P_i)$. In online settings, this may be a sliding window or subject to eviction policies to manage memory [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Perception:** Render images from the current representation $\Phi$ using the current camera pose $P_t$ to assess reconstruction quality or uncertainty.
2.  **Planning:** Determine the next best view $P_{t+1}$ based on criteria such as entropy or reconstruction error [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Acquisition:** Capture new observation $(I_{new}, P_{new})$.
4.  **Incremental Update:**
    *   Add $(I_{new}, P_{new})$ to the training set $\mathcal{D}$.
    *   Perform gradient descent on $\Phi$ using the new observation.
    *   If the representation is grid-based, ensure new spatial regions are initialized or expanded to accommodate the new view [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Iteration:** Repeat steps 1-4 for subsequent frames.

**Formulas:**
The optimization minimizes the photometric loss between rendered and observed images:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}} \| I_i - \mathcal{R}(\Phi, P_i) \|^2 $$
where $\mathcal{R}$ is the volume rendering function. In online settings, $\mathcal{D}$ is updated incrementally [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Learning Rate:** Often requires adjustment or resetting when new spatial regions are added to prevent catastrophic forgetting [S2, unofficial].
*   **Entropy Threshold:** Used in active planning to determine when to stop or move [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Updating shared MLP weights globally causes previously reconstructed regions to degrade if they are no longer supervised [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Management:** Large environments generate high memory usage; older frames may need discarding, which exacerbates forgetting [S2, unofficial].
*   **Initialization:** New grid cells or regions must be properly initialized to represent unseen areas without disrupting existing knowledge [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Incremental Reconstruction with Frozen Decoder
A strategy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in online incremental reconstruction. It employs pre-trained, scene-agnostic decoders that remain frozen during the optimization of feature grids [S2, unofficial]. This allows new grid cells to represent unseen areas while preserving the integrity of already reconstructed regions, as the decoder weights are not updated to overwrite previously learned information [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of new views processed, but each update requires full gradient computation over the current training set or a batch thereof.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the scene representation size. Grid-based methods scale with the occupied volume. Online methods must manage memory by evicting old data or limiting grid expansion [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables real-time reconstruction and active exploration; suitable for large-scale, dynamic environments [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Susceptible to catastrophic forgetting if not carefully managed (e.g., via frozen decoders or regularization); performance may lag behind offline methods that optimize globally on all data [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Robotics navigation, real-time mapping, scenarios with streaming data and limited storage.
*   **When Not to Use:** Offline processing where all data is available and maximum reconstruction quality is prioritized over speed/adaptability.

## Background
The concept emerged from the need to adapt NeRF for real-world robotic applications where data is acquired sequentially. ActiveRMAP introduced the online incremental framework, contrasting with offline methods like DVGO [S1, unofficial]. Subsequent work highlighted the challenge of catastrophic forgetting in grid-based representations and proposed frozen decoders as a mitigation strategy [S2, unofficial].
