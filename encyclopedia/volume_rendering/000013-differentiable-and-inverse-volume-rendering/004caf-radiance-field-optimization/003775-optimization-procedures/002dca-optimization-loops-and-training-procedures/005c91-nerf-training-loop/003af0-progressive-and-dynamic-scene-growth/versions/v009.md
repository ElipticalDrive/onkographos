# Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth


## Overview
Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth refers to a family of training procedures that adaptively expand the capacity of a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) or its associated training data during optimization, rather than optimizing a fixed architecture on a static dataset. This approach solves the problem of efficiently modeling large-scale, complex, or dynamically acquired scenes where a single static model may suffer from underfitting in complex regions or where data arrives sequentially. It sets itself apart from static training loops by introducing a feedback mechanism—based on uncertainty, view synthesis needs, or temporal sequence—that triggers structural growth (new network branches) or data expansion (new views/poses) mid-training.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism shared by all methods in this family is the iterative expansion of the optimization state. Unlike standard NeRF training which runs for a fixed number of iterations on a fixed set of parameters and data, these methods introduce a "growth" phase. This phase is triggered by specific criteria:
1.  **Uncertainty/Error:** High reconstruction error or uncertainty in query points triggers the addition of new network branches or sub-networks to refine local details [S1].
2.  **View Synthesis Needs:** Missing geometric or semantic information in novel views triggers the generation of synthetic data (inpainting) and the addition of new camera poses to the training set [S1].
3.  **Temporal/Sequential Acquisition:** The arrival of new frames triggers the registration of new poses and the freezing of previously optimized parameters to prevent catastrophic forgetting .

Mathematically, the base rendering equation remains standard:
$$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \alpha_i) c_i $$
However, the parameters $\Phi$ (network weights) and the dataset $\mathcal{D}$ (poses/images) are functions of the training step $t$: $\Phi_t, \mathcal{D}_t$. The growth condition $G(\Phi_t, \mathcal{D}_t)$ determines when to transition to $t+1$ with expanded capacity $\Phi_{t+1} \supset \Phi_t$ or data $\mathcal{D}_{t+1} \supset \mathcal{D}_t$.

## Subtopics

### [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md)
A training procedure that adaptively increases model capacity by adding network branches in regions of high reconstruction uncertainty. Unlike static architectures, it clusters high-error query points and spawns child networks to refine predictions locally, enabling efficient coarse-to-fine scene representation. Choose this when the scene has localized complex details that a single global MLP cannot capture efficiently. See [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md).

### [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md)
A training strategy for Panoramic NeRF that addresses the challenge of synthesizing high-quality novel views from a single input panorama, specifically targeting occluded regions where geometric and semantic information is missing. Unlike methods that rely solely on semantic regularization, it iteratively expands the training set by progressively sampling new camera positions and filling missing data via collaborative RGBD inpainting, thereby improving geometry and semantic coherence in unseen areas. Choose this for single-image panorama synthesis. See [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md).

### [Incremental Registration](11341-incremental-registration.md)
A training strategy for joint pose and radiance field optimization that registers camera frames sequentially following their temporal order, leveraging the intuition that adjacent camera poses in video sequences are spatially close. It solves the non-convex optimization landscape and local minima inherent in simultaneous pose and NeRF optimization by providing a robust initialization for new frames based on previously registered ones, thereby preventing "overlapping registration" errors where incorrect poses mislead the scene regressor. Unlike global registration methods that optimize all poses simultaneously, this method builds the trajectory incrementally, freezing previously registered parameters to stabilize the optimization. Choose this for unposed video sequences or SLAM-like scenarios. See [Incremental Registration](11341-incremental-registration.md).

### [Online Incremental NeRF Optimization](12066-online-incremental-nerf-optimization.md)
A training paradigm where the neural radiance field representation is updated continuously as new camera viewpoints and observations arrive, rather than optimizing on a fixed, pre-collected dataset. It solves the problem of building 3D models in dynamic or large-scale environments where data is acquired sequentially. It differs from offline methods like DVGO by integrating new observations into the training set incrementally, requiring the system to plan next-best views and perceive observations based on the current scene representation. Choose this for interactive or real-time reconstruction systems. See [Online Incremental NeRF Optimization](12066-online-incremental-nerf-optimization.md).

### CityNeRF
A progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the NeRF model and training set [S1]. It starts by fitting distant views with shallow basic blocks. New blocks are continuously attached as training iterates to adapt to details in far-near views. This effectively activates high-frequency channels in position encoding and unfolds more complex details as training progresses [S1].

## Background
The concept of progressive training in NeRF emerged to address scalability and complexity issues. In 2021, Xiangli et al. proposed CityNeRF, a progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the NeRF model and training set, starting with shallow basic blocks for distant views and attaching new blocks to adapt to details [S1]. In 2022, Tancik et al. proposed Block-NeRF, which decomposes large urban environments into blocks trained separately, using a "total-part-total" approach to decouple rendering time from scene size [S1]. These methods laid the groundwork for dynamic growth, where model capacity is not fixed at initialization but expanded based on data complexity or uncertainty.
