# SparseGS

Also called: Sparse View Synthesis Using 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
SparseGS is a training optimization method for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) designed to solve the problem of sparse-view 3D reconstruction, specifically addressing issues such as misplaced Gaussians ("floaters"), geometry collapse, and texture noise that arise when input view coverage is insufficient [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by combining a novel depth rendering bias (softmax-scaling) with external priors: monocular depth estimation and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from a diffusion model, whereas siblings like DNGaussian rely on depth-normal regularizers and FSGS uses Gaussian unpooling [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
SparseGS extends the standard 3DGS training loop by introducing an auxiliary regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ and a post-processing pruning step. The method operates on a set of 3D Gaussians defined by centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for colors $c$ [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A sparse set of $N$ input images (typically $\sim 3$ to 12 views) with known camera poses [S1].
*   A pre-trained monocular depth estimation model to generate reference depth maps for input views [S1].
*   A pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v2.1) for SDS priors [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters $\theta$ representing the scene geometry and appearance [S1].

### Data Structures
The method maintains the standard 3DGS Gaussian attributes. Additionally, it computes rendered depth maps $D_{rendered}$ and reference depth maps $D_{ref}$ for loss calculation [S1].

### Steps
1.  **Depth Rendering with Softmax-Scaling:**
    Standard 3DGS renders depth via alpha blending, which averages depth values weighted by opacity, leading to "floaters" (misplaced low-opacity Gaussians) in sparse settings. SparseGS replaces this with a softmax-scaling depth rendering technique [S1].
    The depth $D_{rendered}(x)$ at pixel $x$ is computed as:
    $$ D_{rendered}(x) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}(x)} \beta_i \cdot z_i $$
    where $\mathcal{N}(x)$ is the set of Gaussians splatting to pixel $x$, $z_i$ is the depth of Gaussian $i$, and $\beta_i$ is the softmax-weighted contribution:
    $$ \beta_i = \frac{\exp(\alpha_i \cdot \tau)}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(x)} \exp(\alpha_j \cdot \tau)} $$
    Here, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of Gaussian $i$, and $\tau$ is a temperature parameter [G: The source does not specify the exact value of $\tau$ or the precise formulation of the mode-selection bias mentioned in the abstract, but implies this softmax weighting reduces the averaging effect of alpha blending] [S1].

2.  **Depth Correlation Loss (Pearson Loss):**
    To constrain the geometry, a Pearson correlation loss is applied between the rendered depth $D_{rendered}$ and the reference depth $D_{ref}$ from the monocular estimator. This is computed locally and globally [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{depth} = \mathcal{L}_{Pearson}(D_{rendered}, D_{ref}) $$
    The weights for local and global Pearson losses are both set to 0.15 [S1].

3.  **Unseen Viewpoints Regularization (UVR):**
    To prevent overfitting to the sparse input views and reduce high-frequency artifacts, regularization is applied at viewpoints away from the training set. This involves rendering at random or held-out viewpoints and comparing against diffusion priors or depth consistency [S1]. This component improves PSNR by an average of 0.33dB [S1].

4.  **Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and Depth Warping:**
    In regions with insufficient input coverage, geometry collapse and texture noise occur. SparseGS leverages SDS from a diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2.1) to guide the appearance and structure. SDS is enabled after two-thirds of the total training iterations, once the scene structure has stabilized [S1].
    Additionally, a depth warping loss is applied to ensure consistency between rendered depth and reference depth across views. The weight for the depth warping loss is 0.05 [S1]. The SDS loss weight is set to $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].

5.  **Floater Pruning:**
    After training, a heuristic process identifies and removes low-opacity Gaussians that are identified as "floaters" (misplaced Gaussians not contributing to coherent geometry). This step sharpens both the image and the depth map, boosting PSNR by 0.28dB [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
def train_sparsegs(input_images, camera_poses, num_iterations):
    # Initialize 3D Gaussians from SfM points
    gaussians = initialize_gaussians_from_sfm(input_images, camera_poses)
    
    # Load pre-trained models
    depth_estimator = load_monocular_depth_model()
    diffusion_model = load_stable_diffusion_v2_1()
    
    # Hyperparameters
    lambda_depth_pearson = 0.15
    lambda_depth_warping = 0.05
    lambda_sds = 5e-4
    sds_start_iter = int(num_iterations * 2/3)
    
    for iter in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Sample a random camera pose from input views
        cam_idx = random_sample(input_images)
        image = input_images[cam_idx]
        pose = camera_poses[cam_idx]
        
        # 2. Render RGB and Depth
        # Use softmax-scaling for depth rendering
        rgb_rendered, depth_rendered = render_gaussians_softmax(gaussians, pose)
        
        # 3. Get reference depth from monocular estimator
        depth_ref = depth_estimator(image)
        
        # 4. Compute Depth Correlation Loss (Pearson)
        # Compute local and global Pearson correlation
        loss_depth = lambda_depth_pearson * (pearson_loss(depth_rendered, depth_ref) + 
                                             pearson_loss_global(depth_rendered, depth_ref))
        
        # 5. Compute RGB Loss (standard 3DGS)
        loss_rgb = compute_rgb_loss(rgb_rendered, image)
        
        # 6. Compute Unseen Viewpoints Regularization (UVR)
        # Render at a random unseen viewpoint and apply regularization
        unseen_pose = random_unseen_pose()
        rgb_unseen, depth_unseen = render_gaussians_softmax(gaussians, unseen_pose)
        loss_uvr = compute_uvr_loss(rgb_unseen, depth_unseen, diffusion_model)
        
        # 7. Compute SDS and Depth Warping (after 2/3 iterations)
        loss_sds = 0
        loss_warping = 0
        if iter > sds_start_iter:
            # SDS Loss using diffusion model
            loss_sds = lambda_sds * compute_sds_loss(gaussians, diffusion_model, unseen_pose)
            
            # Depth Warping Loss
            # Warp reference depth from input view to unseen view and compare
            depth_warped = warp_depth(depth_ref, pose, unseen_pose)
            loss_warping = lambda_depth_warping * mse_loss(depth_warped, depth_unseen)
        
        # 8. Total Loss and Backpropagation
        loss_total = loss_rgb + loss_depth + loss_uvr + loss_sds + loss_warping
        loss_total.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
    # 9. Floater Pruning
    gaussians = prune_floaters(gaussians, threshold=low_opacity_threshold)
    
    return gaussians
```

### Parameters
*   **Depth Warping Loss Weight:** 0.05 [S1].
*   **SDS Loss Weight:** $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Local/Global Pearson Loss Weight:** 0.15 [S1].
*   **SDS Activation:** Enabled after 2/3 of total iterations [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Monocular Depth Accuracy:** The method relies on the quality of the monocular depth estimator. Inaccurate reference depths can mislead the Pearson loss [S1].
*   **SDS Timing:** Enabling SDS too early may destabilize training; it is specifically enabled after 2/3 of iterations when structure is stable [S1].
*   **Floaters:** Without softmax-scaling and pruning, alpha-blended depth rendering produces misplaced low-opacity Gaussians [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF360 dataset (12-view setting), SparseGS improves PSNR by 1.37dB over standard 3DGS (using softmax-scaling), by an average of 0.33dB with UVR, and by 0.28dB with floater pruning [S1].
*   **Comparison:** SparseGS outperforms NeRF-based methods and concurrent works FSGS and DNGaussian in both 12-view and 24-view settings [S1]. FSGS preserves fine details but fails on background geometry; DNGaussian renders coherent backgrounds but prunes foreground objects incorrectly. SparseGS reconstructs high-frequency geometry and preserves sharp boundaries [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Effective in sparse-view settings (~3 to 12 views); reduces floaters and geometry collapse; produces cleaner, more coherent scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires pre-trained monocular depth and diffusion models; training time may increase due to SDS and UVR computations [S1].

## Background
SparseGS was proposed to tackle the challenges of sparse-view 3D reconstruction using 3D Gaussian Splatting. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS, which suffers from floaters and geometry collapse when input views are sparse [S1]. The method builds on the principles of Depth Regularization, using differentiable auxiliary losses to guide Gaussian parameters [Parent]. It was evaluated on forward-facing datasets (LLFF, DTU) and the Mip-NeRF360 dataset [S1].
