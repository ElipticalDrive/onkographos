# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Priors, Geometric Losses

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision methods enforce geometric consistency in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by incorporating external depth priors, structural constraints, or probabilistic regularization into the training loss. They solve the shape-radiance ambiguity and overfitting issues inherent in sparse-view or textureless scenarios where RGB data alone is insufficient to constrain the density field [S1, S2]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on diffusion priors [7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md] or semantic consistency [11955-semantic-consistency-loss-clip-based.md], this family explicitly targets the geometric structure of the scene, either by aligning rendered depth with priors, regularizing the density distribution, or constraining sampling ranges [S1, S2].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the differentiable volume rendering framework where gradients flow from the loss $\mathcal{L}$ back through the rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\hat{\mathbf{D}}(\mathbf{r})$ to the network parameters $\theta$ (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md].

The rendering process computes expected color and depth along rays $r(t) = o + td$ using sample points $t_i$ with weights $w_i = T(t_i)\rho(t_i)$, where $\rho(t_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_i))(t_{i+1} - t_i))$ and $T(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \rho(t_j))$ [S1]. The rendering weights $w_i$ serve as the probability distribution of absorption along the ray, often denoted as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, which is used in distributional geometric losses [S1, S2].

The rendered depth is typically the expected distance along the ray:
$$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i $$
where $w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$ are the rendering weights, $t_i$ are sample depths, and $T_i$ is the accumulated transmittance [S1, S2].

Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the density and color field parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss function designed to prevent degenerate solutions in NeRF optimization, particularly in few-view settings where the network might collapse to copying training images. It combines photometric reconstruction with geometric priors that enforce compact depth distributions, full absorption, and multi-view consistency. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on external depth supervision, this method derives regularization directly from the probabilistic interpretation of the volume rendering weights.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in indoor scenes by constraining the sampling range of NeRF using adapted monocular depth priors. It calculates adaptive bounds based on multi-view consistency errors of the depth priors, ensuring samples concentrate around plausible geometry even in textureless regions. It sets itself apart from Depth and Geometry Supervision methods that apply explicit geometric losses by instead guiding the *sampling distribution* itself, thereby preventing the network from exploring degenerate geometric solutions that still fit the RGB data.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Incorporates sparse 3D depth supervision from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds to accelerate convergence and improve reconstruction quality with fewer input views. It treats sparse keypoints generated during standard SfM preprocessing as "free" geometric constraints, enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution aligns with these surface priors. This approach reduces overfitting in sparse-view settings and typically achieves target quality in 2–3× fewer training iterations than standard NeRF.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A geometry supervision function designed to mitigate the degradation of extrapolation performance caused by inaccurate or noisy pseudo-depth maps in differentiable volume rendering. By using a loss function that is less sensitive to large errors than L2, it encourages the network to terminate rays near the pseudo-depth while retaining freedom to ignore inaccurate geometry when the error is very large or very small. It is distinct from Geometric Baseline Regularization which focuses on baseline consistency, and Depth-Supervised NeRF which typically uses standard L1/L2 losses without this specific robust formulation.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
A geometric regularization technique applied during NeRF training to enforce continuity constraints on rendered depth maps, leveraging external depth priors such as those from DepthAnythingV2. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Patch-Based Depth Regularization by explicitly modeling structural consistency through k-nearest neighbor (KNN) relationships in depth space rather than spatial proximity alone, ensuring that neighboring pixels in the prior map exhibit similar continuity in the rendered output.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates the shape-radiance ambiguity in NeRF by incorporating rays from unobserved viewpoints into the training loop. Unlike methods that rely solely on observed image supervision, this approach adds geometric guidance to prevent overfitting to sparse input views. It sets itself apart by explicitly sampling and rendering novel views during optimization to enforce geometric consistency, whereas siblings like Depth and Geometry Supervision may rely on external depth priors or structural constraints without necessarily synthesizing new viewpoints.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit penalties to the opacity parameters of a volume rendering model to enforce geometric compactness and binary opacity states. It solves the problem of "floaters" (spurious geometry in empty space) and translucent artifacts that arise when neural fields learn to distribute density across many samples rather than concentrating it on actual surfaces. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like Geometric Baseline Regularization by operating directly on the opacity values $O(i)$ or $\alpha_i$ rather than relying on depth consistency or gradient penalties on the density field $\sigma$.

### [Direct Depth Supervision](11396-direct-depth-supervision.md)
A training objective that minimizes the pixel-wise difference between the depth rendered by a NeRF and a ground truth depth prior. It differs from indirect methods by applying the loss directly to the expected depth value $\hat{\mathbf{D}}(\mathbf{r})$ rather than using geometric constraints on density or surface normals. It builds on Depth and Geometry Supervision.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the Euclidean distance between 3D points backprojected from rendered depth and ground-truth 3D points to enforce global geometric consistency during NeRF optimization. Unlike direct depth regression, which often fails to register camera poses in sparse-view regimes by favoring per-image local geometry, this loss explicitly connects training images to the underlying scene structure, enabling robust pose refinement.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
A framework for optimizing neural scene representations by treating arbitrary rays in a scene as "ray sensors" that record incoming radiance, extending the standard NeRF approach which only supervises camera rays. It solves the problem of limited geometric and photometric constraints in standard multi-view optimization by allowing supervision signals from non-camera perspectives, such as shadow rays or rays from unseen viewpoints, to guide the learning of density and color fields. Unlike standard NeRF which assumes light emission from scene particles, this generalization allows for physically correct modeling where the scene absorbs light from known sources or enforces semantic consistency across views.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting mechanism for differentiable volume rendering that adapts the reliability of supervision signals based on scene content, specifically distinguishing between static background environments and dynamic foreground objects like vehicles. It sets itself apart from standard geometric regularization by decoupling confidence metrics per object class: it employs geometry and reprojection measurements for background learning, while restricting foreground vehicle learning to RGB and SSIM confidences, leveraging the higher quality of LiDAR depth maps available for foreground objects.

### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
- **Definition**: A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
- **Formula**: $L_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{m_f=1} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}_{m_c=1} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2$, where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
- **Definition**: A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S2].
- **Formula**: $L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}\right) \Delta t_k$, where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2].
- **Parameter**: $\epsilon(x)$: A kernel that integrates to one (a distribution) with a bounded domain parameterized by $\epsilon$ [S2].
- **Pitfall**: The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S2].

### GeoAug
- **Definition**: An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The concept of using depth priors to regularize NeRF training emerged as a solution to the ambiguity of sparse-view reconstruction. Early works like DS-NeRF demonstrated that sparse SfM points could serve as effective geometric constraints [S1, S3]. Subsequent research expanded this to include monocular depth priors [S1], robust losses for noisy pseudo-depth [S1], and structural consistency constraints [S1]. The field has evolved from simple L2/L1 depth matching to more sophisticated probabilistic formulations (KL divergence) [S2] and confidence-weighted multi-task learning [S1]. Generalized ray supervision further extended these concepts beyond camera rays to shadow and virtual rays [S1, S2].
