# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure for Neural Radiance Fields that incorporates sparse 3D keypoints from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) as depth supervision to enforce that the ray termination distribution matches surface priors, thereby reducing overfitting and accelerating training [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of slow convergence and poor quality in NeRF when trained on a limited number of input views by adding a geometric loss term to the standard photometric loss [S1][S3, unofficial]. Unlike methods that rely on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires no additional training and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds, making it a flexible, "free" supervisory signal [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_j\}$ with known camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM); sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ and their visibility flags $\{X_j\}$ from the SfM pipeline (e.g., COLMAP); near and far clipping bounds $t_n, t_f$.
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$); rendered novel views with improved PSNR and faster convergence.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set of 3D points $X = \{x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\}$.
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that camera.
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each visible keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$.
*   **Ray Termination Distribution:** Discretized over sample points $t_k$ along a ray, represented as weights $h_k$.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses $P_j$ and reconstruct sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Estimation:** For each image $I_j$ and camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each $x_i \in X_j$:
    *   Compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ as the average reprojection error of keypoint $x_i$ across all views where it was detected [S1].
    *   Model the true depth of the first surface as a random variable $D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1, S2].
3.  **Ray Generation and Allocation:**
    *   Generate standard training rays from random pixel coordinates for color supervision [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Generate "keypoint rays" by projecting rays from the camera center through the image coordinates of detected keypoints $x_i \in X_j$. The ray direction in local camera coordinates is defined by the keypoint's 2D image coordinates and $-1$ in the camera axis [S1].
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to these keypoint rays to enable simultaneous RGB and depth supervision in a single ray marching step [S1].
    *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for the rays corresponding to detected keypoints, exploiting the continuity of image coordinates [S1].
4.  **Volume Rendering:** For each ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample points $\mathbf{x}_k$ along the ray between $t_n$ and $t_f$. Compute density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_k)$ and color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_k)$ using the NeRF MLP [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Compute the ray termination distribution weight $h(t)$:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right)\sigma(t) $$
        where $T(t)$ is the transmittance [S1].
    *   Discretize $h(t)$ into $h_k$ for sample points $t_k$ with step size $\Delta t_k$ [S1].
    *   Render the color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and expected depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ [S3, unofficial][S4]:
        $$ \widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_k w_k \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_k), \quad \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_k w_k t_k $$
        where $w_k = h_k$ (normalized weights summing to 1) [S1, S4].
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{Color}$):** Standard MSE between rendered color and ground truth image color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1][S3, unofficial]:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{Color} = \|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2 $$
    *   **Depth Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{Depth}$):** Minimize the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $\mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1]. This is approximated as:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k \right] $$
        Alternatively, an MSE loss between rendered depth and sparse depth can be used, though KL divergence yields fewer artifacts [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{Color} + \lambda_D \mathcal{L}_{Depth} $$
        where $\lambda_D$ is a hyper-parameter balancing the losses [S1, S4].
6.  **Optimization:** Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update NeRF parameters [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_ray_termination_distribution(sigma_samples, delta_t):
    """
    Computes the discrete ray termination distribution h(t) from density samples.
    sigma_samples: array of density values sigma(t_k)
    delta_t: array of step sizes delta_t_k
    Returns: h_k, the probability mass at each sample point.
    """
    # Compute transmittance T(t_k) = exp(-integral_0^t_k sigma(s) ds)
    # Cumulative sum of sigma * delta_t gives the integral up to t_k
    # Note: integral_0^t_k is sum_{j=0}^{k-1} sigma_j * delta_t_j
    cumulative_sigma_dt = np.cumsum(sigma_samples * delta_t)
    # Transmittance at sample k (before absorbing at k)
    # T(t_k) corresponds to the integral from 0 to t_k. 
    # However, standard NeRF formulation for weight w_k uses T(t_k) where t_k is start of interval.
    # Let's align with h(t) = T(t)sigma(t).
    # T(t_k) = exp(-integral_0^t_k sigma(s) ds).
    # integral_0^t_k is sum_{j=0}^{k-1} sigma_j * delta_t_j.
    
    # Shift cumulative sum to align with start of intervals
    # integral_0^t_0 = 0
    # integral_0^t_1 = sigma_0 * delta_t_0
    integrals = np.zeros_like(cumulative_sigma_dt)
    integrals[1:] = cumulative_sigma_dt[:-1]
    
    transmittance = np.exp(-integrals)
    
    # h_k = T(t_k) * sigma(t_k)
    h = transmittance * sigma_samples
    
    # Normalize to ensure sum(h) = 1 (approximate, assuming opaque wall at end)
    # In practice, NeRF often normalizes weights w_k = h_k / sum(h)
    # But for KL loss, we treat h as the distribution.
    # The source defines h(t) as a probability distribution.
    # We return normalized h to be safe for log(h) in KL loss.
    h_sum = np.sum(h)
    if h_sum > 0:
        h = h / h_sum
    return h

def depth_supervised_nerf_step(rays, gt_colors, keypoints, camera_poses, nerf_model, lambda_D, t_n, t_f, num_samples):
    """
    Single training step for DS-NeRF.
    rays: list of ray origins and directions
    gt_colors: ground truth colors for rays
    keypoints: list of (keypoint_3d, keypoint_2d_image_coords, keypoint_uncertainty)
    camera_poses: camera parameters P_j
    nerf_model: function to query (sigma, color) at 3D points
    lambda_D: weight for depth loss
    t_n, t_f: near and far bounds
    num_samples: number of samples per ray
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    color_loss = 0.0
    depth_loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Process standard rays for color supervision
    for ray, gt_color in zip(rays, gt_colors):
        # Sample points along ray
        t_vals = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, num_samples)
        points = ray.origin + ray.direction * t_vals
        
        # Query NeRF
        sigma, color = nerf_model(points)
        
        # Compute weights (standard NeRF)
        delta_t = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        delta_t = np.append(delta_t, delta_t[-1]) # Handle last interval or assume opaque wall
        
        # Transmittance
        alpha = 1 - np.exp(-sigma * delta_t)
        T = np.cumprod(1 - alpha)
        T = np.concatenate(([1.0], T[:-1]))
        
        weights = alpha * T
        
        # Rendered color
        rendered_color = np.sum(weights * color, axis=0)
        
        # Color Loss
        color_loss += np.mean((rendered_color - gt_color) ** 2)
        
    # 2. Process keypoint rays for depth supervision
    for kp_3d, kp_2d, sigma_hat in keypoints:
        # Construct ray from camera through keypoint 2D projection
        # Ray origin is camera center, direction is from camera center to kp_3d
        # Or as per source: direction given by image coords and -1 in camera axis
        # Assuming we have a function to get ray from camera and 2D coord
        ray = get_ray_from_camera_and_2d(camera_poses, kp_2d)
        
        # Sample points
        t_vals = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, num_samples)
        points = ray.origin + ray.direction * t_vals
        
        # Query NeRF
        sigma, _ = nerf_model(points)
        
        # Compute termination distribution h(t)
        h = compute_ray_termination_distribution(sigma, t_vals[1]-t_vals[0])
        
        # Depth Loss: KL divergence between h(t) and N(D_ij, sigma_hat)
        # D_ij is the depth of the keypoint along this ray
        D_ij = np.dot(kp_3d - ray.origin, ray.direction) # Project 3D point onto ray direction
        
        # Approximate KL loss term: -log(h_k) * exp(-(t_k - D_ij)^2 / (2 * sigma_hat^2))
        # Note: Source uses expectation over keypoints.
        # We sum over samples k.
        gaussian_term = np.exp(-((t_vals - D_ij) ** 2) / (2 * sigma_hat ** 2))
        
        # Avoid log(0)
        h_safe = np.clip(h, 1e-8, 1.0)
        
        loss_term = -np.log(h_safe) * gaussian_term * (t_vals[1] - t_vals[0])
        depth_loss += np.sum(loss_term)
        
    # Combine losses
    num_rays = len(rays)
    num_kps = len(keypoints)
    
    # Normalize losses by number of samples/rays
    color_loss /= num_rays
    depth_loss /= num_kps
    
    total_loss = color_loss + lambda_D * depth_loss
    
    return total_loss, color_loss, depth_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   Training iterations: O(10k - 100k) [S3, unofficial].
*   Image resolution: 400 × 300 [S1].
*   Ray allocation: A portion of total training rays is dedicated to keypoint rays [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **SfM Quality:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint location uncertainty, which may not hold for depth derived from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different datasets (e.g., NeRF Real) due to domain differences, though DS-NeRF itself is less susceptible than data-driven priors [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has an existing geometry prior handled by its PSV-initialization [S1].
*   **Evaluation vs Training:** Dense MVS depth is used only for evaluation; it is not used during training [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are reduced by 2-3x compared to standard NeRF [S1]. Wall time per iteration is comparable: ~362.4 ms/iter for DS-NeRF vs ~359.8 ms/iter for standard NeRF on a single RTX A5000 [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On a single RTX A5000, DS-NeRF reaches a target test PSNR in 2-3x fewer training iterations than NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified at lower view counts. For example, with 2 views, NeRF fails to match DS-NeRF's performance [S1]. With 5 views, PSNR improves from 13.5 (NeRF) to 20.2 (DS-NeRF) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with fewer views, requires no additional training data or priors, compatible with other NeRF methods [S1, S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the success of SfM; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].
*   **When to use:** When training data is sparse (few views) or training time is a constraint. Suitable for real-world applications where SfM succeeds.
*   **When not to use:** When SfM fails or produces unreliable keypoints; when using methods like MVSNeRF that already have strong geometry priors [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in "Depth-supervised NeRF: Fewer Views and Faster Training for Free" (CVPR 2022) [S1, S2].
*   **History:** Developed to address the limitation of NeRF requiring many images for high-quality reconstruction and the slow convergence of volumetric rendering [S1]. It leverages the "free" depth supervision already available from the SfM preprocessing step (e.g., COLMAP) commonly used in NeRF pipelines [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1]. Compatible with depth from scanned sensors and RGB-D outputs [S1].
