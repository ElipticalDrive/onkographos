# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes by constraining the sampling range of rays using adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which samples uniformly across a fixed near/far bound and often converges to geometrically incorrect but photometrically accurate solutions on texture-less surfaces, this method dynamically adjusts the sampling interval $[t_n, t_f]$ based on multi-view consistency errors of the depth priors [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md) by using depth priors primarily to guide *sampling locations* rather than as a direct regularization term in the loss function, thereby preventing the optimizer from exploring implausible geometry regions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $N$ input views with RGB images $I^i$, camera intrinsics $K$, and relative poses $T^{i \to j}$.
- Adapted monocular depth priors $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$ for each view, obtained from a finetuned monocular depth network [S1].
- Hyperparameters: $K=4$ (number of cross-view errors used for error map calculation), $\alpha_l = 0.05$ (lower bound for range expansion), $\alpha_h = 0.15$ (upper bound for range expansion), $M$ (number of samples per ray) [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ defining the implicit radiance field $F_\theta: (x, d) \to (c, \sigma)$ [S1].
- Refined depth maps for input and novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- **Error Map $e^i$:** A 2D array of size $H \times W$ for view $i$, storing the average of the $K$ minimum cross-view reprojection errors for each pixel [S1].
- **Sample Range $[t_n, t_f]$:** Scalar values per ray, derived from the depth prior $D$ and error map $e$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Acquire Adapted Depth Priors:** Obtain initial depth estimates $D^i$ for all $N$ input views using a monocular depth network finetuned on the target scene [S1].

2.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j \neq i$.
    Calculate the 2D projection coordinates $p^{i \to j}$ and projected depth $D^{i \to j}$:
    $$ p^{i \to j}, D^{i \to j} = \text{proj}(K, T^{i \to j}, D^i) $$
    $$ D^{j'} = D^j(p^{i \to j}) $$
    Compute the relative reprojection error between $D^{j'}$ and $D^{i \to j}$ for all overlapping pixels [S1].
    For each pixel in view $i$, calculate the error map value $e^i$ as the average of the $K$ minimum cross-view depth projection errors [S1].

3.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ corresponding to a pixel in view $i$, let $D$ be the depth prior value at that pixel and $e$ be the error map value $e^i$.
    Compute the near and far bounds $t_n$ and $t_f$:
    $$ t_n = D(1 - \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D(1 + \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    where $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ restricts $x$ to the interval $[a, b]$ [S1].
    *Note:* If the error $e$ is small, the range is tight around $D$. If $e$ is large, the range expands up to the limits defined by $\alpha_h$, approximating the original NeRF uniform sampling behavior [S1].

4.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Query the network $F_\theta$ at positions $x_i$ along the ray at depths $t_i$ to obtain color $c_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S1].

5.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute accumulated transmittance $T_i$:
    $$ T_i = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1].
    Render the color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $D(\mathbf{r})$:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i $$
    $$ D(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) t_i $$
    [S1]

6.  **Loss Computation and Optimization:**
    Compute the photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ between the rendered color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground truth image $I$ (typically MSE) [S1, 8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md].
    Backpropagate through the differentiable volume rendering process to update NeRF parameters $\theta$ [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

7.  **Confidence Score Calculation (Post-Processing/Refinement):**
    After training, compute confidence scores $S^i_j$ for each pixel $j$ in view $i$ based on the RGB reconstruction error:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} || C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) ||_1 $$
    where $C_{gt}$ and $C_{render}$ are normalized by 255, and the $L_1$ norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

8.  **Depth Refinement:**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the rendered depth map, weighted by the confidence scores $S^i_j$, to produce the final refined depth output [S1].

## Subtopics

### [ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training](7580-enerf-with-guided-sampling-training.md)
Integrates pixel-region and depth-boundary-guided ray sampling into the ENeRF framework to reduce GPU memory consumption and improve sampling efficiency in generalizable NeRF models by using adapted monocular depth priors.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The method adds a pre-processing step for error map calculation ($O(N^2 \cdot HW)$ for projections) and requires computing adaptive ranges per ray. The training loop itself is similar to standard NeRF but with potentially fewer effective samples if the error is low, though $M$ is fixed.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing error maps for all views ($O(N \cdot HW)$) and the adapted depth priors. No significant additional memory overhead during training compared to standard NeRF.
- **Performance:** Outperforms original NeRF and other state-of-the-art methods on indoor datasets like ScanNet in terms of depth estimation accuracy and view synthesis quality, particularly in texture-less regions [S1].
- **Strengths:** Effectively mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in indoor scenes; does not require ground truth depth for training; leverages multi-view consistency to refine priors.
- **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the initial monocular depth priors; performance may degrade if priors are poor or if multi-view consistency checks fail (e.g., in highly dynamic scenes or with unreliable flow correspondences) [S1]. Applying existing correspondence-based optimization directly to adapted priors can degrade quality [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in "Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF [33] which, while successful in outdoor scenes, struggles with shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor environments (e.g., walls) [S1]. The authors observed that NeRF can synthesize high-quality RGB images with incorrect geometry in these areas because the optimizer favors smoothed surface light fields, and incorrect geometry is not sufficiently penalized if the radiance field can still explain the input images [S1]. By guiding the sampling process with adapted monocular depth priors, the method constrains the search space to plausible geometric regions [S1].
