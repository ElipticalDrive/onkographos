# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting to incorrect geometries when insufficient views are available by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution aligns with surface priors derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires only the existence of 3D keypoints, making it flexible and independent of training data distribution assumptions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $I_j$ with known camera poses $P_j$ (estimated via SfM, e.g., COLMAP); sparse 3D keypoints $X$ and their visibility flags $X_j \subset X$ from the SfM pipeline [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoint Depth ($D_{ij}$):** For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$ visible in image $I_j$, the depth is computed by projecting $x_i$ with camera $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
*   **Depth Uncertainty ($\hat{\sigma}_i$):** The average reprojection error of keypoint $x_i$ across all views where it was detected [S1].
*   **Ray Termination Distribution ($h(t)$):** Defined as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds)$ is the transmittance [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses and generate sparse 3D keypoints $X$ with visibility information [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Calculation:** For each training image $I_j$ and camera $P_j$:
    *   Identify visible keypoints $X_j$.
    *   Compute the estimated depth $D_{ij}$ for each $x_i \in X_j$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and extracting the z-coordinate [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ as the average reprojection error of $x_i$ across all views [S1].
3.  **Ray Generation for Supervision:**
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, compute the ray origin in local camera coordinates using the image coordinates of $x_i$ and setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
    *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for these rays from the ground truth image $I_j$, exploiting the continuity of image coordinates [S1].
4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Shoot the keypoint rays into the scene using the standard NeRF sampling procedure [S1].
    *   Compute the rendered color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ray termination distribution samples $h_k$ at sample points $t_k$ along the ray [S1, S4].
    *   The rendered depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ is computed as the expected depth: $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} w_j t_j$, where $w_j$ are the rendering weights [S4].
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute the photometric loss $L_{Color}$ between rendered color and ground truth [S1].
    *   Compute the depth loss $L_{Depth}$ using the KL divergence between the rendered distribution $h(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $N(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1].
    *   The total loss is $\mathcal{L} = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth}$ [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update NeRF parameters [S1].

**Formulas:**

The ray termination distribution is:
$$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left[-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right]\sigma(t) $$
where $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density, assumed non-negative (e.g., via ReLU or softplus) [S1].

The noisy depth distribution for keypoint $x_i$ is modeled as:
$$ D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i) $$
where $D_{ij}$ is the COLMAP-estimated depth and $\hat{\sigma}_i$ is the average reprojection error [S1].

The depth loss is the KL divergence between the noisy depth distribution and the rendered distribution:
$$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ \text{KL}\left( \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i) \parallel h_{ij}(t) \right) \right] $$
Expanding this into an integral over the ray distance $t$:
$$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\int_0^\infty \log h(t) \exp \left( -\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) dt \right] $$
Approximated discretely over sample points $t_k$ with step size $\Delta t_k$:
$$ L_{Depth} \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
where $h_k = h(t_k)$ [S1].

The total loss is:
$$ \mathcal{L} = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth} $$
where $\lambda_D$ is a hyper-parameter balancing color and depth supervision [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **$\lambda_D$:** Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 in some implementations [S4], though the original paper treats it as a hyper-parameter [S1].
*   **Near/Far Bounds ($t_n, t_f$):** The scene is bounded by near and far clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$, making $h(t)$ a valid probability distribution [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** Typically $O(10k - 100k)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Ray Allocation:** A portion of training rays is dedicated to keypoint rays for simultaneous RGB and depth supervision [S1].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
*   **SfM Quality:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on specific domains (like DTU) may struggle on others (like NeRF Real) if combined with DS-NeRF, though DS-NeRF itself generalizes better than data-driven priors [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].
*   **Evaluation vs. Training:** Dense MVS depth is used only for evaluation, not during training [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import numpy as np

def compute_depth_loss(rendered_h, t_samples, keypoint_depth, keypoint_uncertainty):
    """
    Computes the KL divergence-based depth loss.
    
    Args:
        rendered_h: Tensor of shape (num_rays, num_samples), the probability mass 
                    h(t_k) = T(t_k) * sigma(t_k) for each sample along each ray.
        t_samples: Tensor of shape (num_samples,), the distances t_k along the ray.
        keypoint_depth: Tensor of shape (num_rays,), D_ij for each ray.
        keypoint_uncertainty: Tensor of shape (num_rays,), sigma_hat_i for each ray.
        
    Returns:
        Scalar loss value.
    """
    # Ensure h is normalized or treated as probability mass
    # Note: In practice, h is computed during volume rendering.
    
    # Gaussian PDF for the noisy depth distribution
    # N(t; D_ij, sigma_i^2)
    gaussian_pdf = torch.exp(-0.5 * ((t_samples.unsqueeze(0) - keypoint_depth.unsqueeze(1)) / keypoint_uncertainty.unsqueeze(1))**2)
    gaussian_pdf = gaussian_pdf / (keypoint_uncertainty.unsqueeze(1) * torch.sqrt(2 * torch.pi))
    
    # KL divergence approximation: sum over samples of -log(h(t)) * p_gaussian(t) * delta_t
    # delta_t is assumed to be uniform or passed in. Here we assume uniform step for simplicity 
    # or that h is already scaled by delta_t if it represents probability mass.
    # The formula in [S1] uses log(h_k) * exp(...) * delta_t_k.
    # We assume rendered_h contains h(t_k) values.
    
    # Avoid log(0)
    epsilon = 1e-10
    log_h = torch.log(torch.clamp(rendered_h, min=epsilon))
    
    # Weighted sum
    # The term exp(-(t - D)^2 / 2sigma^2) is part of the Gaussian PDF numerator.
    # The full Gaussian PDF includes the normalization factor 1/(sigma * sqrt(2pi)).
    # The loss in [S1] is E[-log h(t) * exp(...)].
    # Let's implement the exact integral approximation from [S1]:
    # sum_k -log(h_k) * exp(-(t_k - D_ij)^2 / (2*sigma_i^2)) * delta_t_k
    
    # Assuming uniform delta_t = 1 for relative scaling, or passed as argument.
    # For standard NeRF sampling, delta_t varies. We'll assume delta_t is handled 
    # in the rendering step or is uniform here for the loss calculation structure.
    # To be precise to [S1], we multiply by the exponential term directly.
    
    exp_term = torch.exp(-0.5 * ((t_samples.unsqueeze(0) - keypoint_depth.unsqueeze(1)) / keypoint_uncertainty.unsqueeze(1))**2)
    
    # Loss per ray
    loss_per_ray = -torch.sum(log_h * exp_term, dim=1)
    
    # Average over rays
    loss = torch.mean(loss_per_ray)
    
    return loss

def train_step(images, cameras, keypoints, nerf_model, lambda_D=1.0):
    """
    Single training step for DS-NeRF.
    """
    # 1. Generate rays
    # Standard rays for color supervision
    standard_rays = generate_standard_rays(images)
    
    # Keypoint rays for depth supervision
    keypoint_rays = []
    keypoint_depths = []
    keypoint_uncertainties = []
    
    for img_idx, (image, camera) in enumerate(zip(images, cameras)):
        # Get visible keypoints for this image
        visible_kps = keypoints[img_idx] # List of (x, y, z, uncertainty)
        
        for kp in visible_kps:
            # Compute ray origin and direction from keypoint image coordinates
            # Direction: from camera center through keypoint projection
            # Origin: Camera center
            ray_o, ray_d = ray_from_keypoint(camera, kp)
            
            keypoint_rays.append((ray_o, ray_d))
            keypoint_depths.append(kp.z) # D_ij
            keypoint_uncertainties.append(kp.uncertainty) # sigma_hat_i
            
    # 2. Render Standard Rays (Color Loss)
    colors_std, _ = nerf_model.render(standard_rays)
    gt_colors_std = get_gt_colors(standard_rays, images)
    l_color_std = torch.mean((colors_std - gt_colors_std)**2)
    
    # 3. Render Keypoint Rays (Depth + Color Loss)
    # We render these rays to get h(t) for depth loss and color for color loss
    colors_kp, h_samples, t_samples = nerf_model.render_with_distribution(keypoint_rays)
    
    # Color loss for keypoint rays (interpolated from GT)
    gt_colors_kp = interpolate_gt_colors(keypoint_rays, images)
    l_color_kp = torch.mean((colors_kp - gt_colors_kp)**2)
    
    # Depth Loss
    keypoint_depths_tensor = torch.tensor(keypoint_depths, device=colors_kp.device)
    keypoint_uncertainties_tensor = torch.tensor(keypoint_uncertainties, device=colors_kp.device)
    
    # h_samples shape: (num_keypoint_rays, num_samples)
    # t_samples shape: (num_samples,)
    l_depth = compute_depth_loss(h_samples, t_samples, keypoint_depths_tensor, keypoint_uncertainties_tensor)
    
    # 4. Total Loss
    l_color = l_color_std + l_color_kp
    l_total = l_color + lambda_D * l_depth
    
    # 5. Backprop
    l_total.backward()
    
    return l_total
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations take approximately 362.4 ms on an RTX A5000, compared to 359.8 ms for standard NeRF, a negligible overhead [S1].
*   **Speedup:** DS-NeRF achieves target test PSNR in 2-3x fewer training iterations than standard NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, it reaches peak PSNR ~13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Data Efficiency:** Significantly improves PSNR with fewer views. For example, on NeRF Real with 5 views, PSNR improves from 13.5 (NeRF) to 20.2 (DS-NeRF) [S1]. With 2 views, NeRF fails to match DS-NeRF's performance [S1].
*   **Memory:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as it reuses SfM outputs and renders additional rays in the same pass [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with sparse views, compatible with other NeRF methods (e.g., pixelNeRF, IBRNet), and does not require domain-specific pre-training [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on the quality of SfM; fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates [S1]. Assumes Gaussian uncertainty, which may be inaccurate for non-SfM depth [S1].

## Background
DS-NeRF was introduced by Deng et al. in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the limitation of NeRF requiring many views for high-quality reconstruction by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM keypoints [S1]. The method was applied to datasets including LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU [S1]. It is compatible with depth from scanned sensors and RGB-D outputs [S1].
