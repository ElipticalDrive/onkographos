# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes by constraining the sampling range of rays using adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which samples uniformly across a fixed frustum, this method adapts the near and far bounds of sampling based on multi-view geometric consistency errors, ensuring points are concentrated around plausible geometry [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md) by using depth priors primarily to guide *sampling locations* rather than as a direct regularization term in the loss function, although it employs confidence-based filtering on the output [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $N$ input views with RGB images and camera parameters (intrinsics $K$, poses $T$).
- Adapted monocular depth priors $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$ for each view, obtained from a fine-tuned monocular depth network [S1].
- Hyperparameters: $K=4$ (number of cross-view errors for averaging), $\alpha_l = 0.05$ (lower bound), $\alpha_h = 0.15$ (upper bound), $M$ (number of samples per ray) [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ (implicitly defined by the training process).
- Final depth map $D_{final}$ after filtering.

**Data Structures:**
- **Adapted Depth Priors:** $D^i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, where $H, W$ are image dimensions.
- **Error Map:** $e^i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, representing the reprojection error for view $i$.
- **Sample Ranges:** $t_n, t_f \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, the near and far bounds for each pixel.
- **Confidence Map:** $S^i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, derived from RGB reconstruction error.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Acquire Adapted Depth Priors:** Obtain initial depth estimates $D^i$ for all $N$ input views using a monocular depth network fine-tuned on the target scene [S1].

2.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j \neq i$.
    Calculate the 2D projection coordinates $p^{i \rightarrow j}$ and projected depth $D^{i \rightarrow j}$ using camera intrinsics $K$ and relative pose $T^{i \rightarrow j}$:
    $$ p^{i \rightarrow j}, D^{i \rightarrow j} = \text{proj}(K, T^{i \rightarrow j}, D^i) $$
    Sample the depth from the target view $j$ at these coordinates:
    $$ D^{j'} = D^j(p^{i \rightarrow j}) $$
    Compute the relative reprojection error between $D^{j'}$ and $D^{i \rightarrow j}$.
    For each pixel in view $i$, calculate the error map value $e^i$ as the average of the $K$ minimum cross-view reprojection errors [S1].

3.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ corresponding to pixel $(u,v)$ in view $i$, determine the near bound $t_n$ and far bound $t_f$ using the depth prior $D$ at that pixel and the error map value $e$:
    $$ t_n = D \cdot (1 - \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D \cdot (1 + \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    Here, $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ restricts $x$ to the interval $[a, b]$. This ensures that for low-error pixels, sampling is tightly concentrated around the prior $D$, while for high-error pixels, the range expands towards the original NeRF bounds [S1].

4.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Query the NeRF network $F_\theta$ at spatial location $\mathbf{x}_i$ (derived from $t_i$ and ray direction) and direction $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain color $c_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S1].

5.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute the accumulated transmittance $T_i$:
    $$ T_i = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$.
    Render the RGB color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and expected depth $D(\mathbf{r})$:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i $$
    $$ D(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) t_i $$
    [S1]

6.  **Compute Confidence Scores:**
    Calculate the confidence score $S^i_j$ for pixel $j$ in view $i$ based on the $L_1$ difference between the ground truth RGB $C_{gt}^i(j)$ and the rendered RGB $C_{render}^i(j)$, normalized by 255:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} \| C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) \|_1 $$
    The norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

7.  **Depth Refinement (Post-Processing):**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the rendered depth map $D(\mathbf{r})$, using the confidence scores $S^i$ as weights. This step improves depth quality in regions where the RGB reconstruction was inaccurate [S1].

## Subtopics

### [ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training](7580-enerf-with-guided-sampling-training.md)
Integrates guided sampling into the Efficient NeRF framework to reduce GPU memory usage and improve sampling efficiency by using adapted monocular depth priors to guide ray sampling, addressing shape-radiance ambiguity more effectively than uniform sampling.

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- The method adds overhead for multi-view depth projection and error map calculation during the initialization or early training phases. The core training loop complexity is similar to standard NeRF but with potentially fewer effective samples per ray due to the constrained range, which can lead to faster convergence or lower memory usage if $M$ is reduced [S1].
- Memory usage is dominated by the NeRF network and the storage of adapted depth priors and error maps [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
- On the ScanNet dataset, the method outperforms state-of-the-art depth estimation methods in all metrics (Table 1 in [S1]).
- It significantly outperforms original NeRF in view synthesis quality (Table 5 in [S1]).
- Applying existing correspondence-based neural optimization (like CVD) to adapted depth priors degrades quality, highlighting the necessity of this specific guided optimization [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths:** Effectively mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor areas (e.g., walls) where standard NeRF fails [S1]. Produces accurate depth maps and high-quality novel views [S1].
- **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of adapted monocular depth priors. Performance may degrade if the priors are inaccurate or if the scene has significant motion blur [S1].

**When to Use:**
- Use for indoor multi-view stereo and depth estimation where texture is sparse and standard NeRF produces geometrically incorrect but visually plausible results [S1].
- Not recommended for scenes with large motion blur or where correspondence-based optimization is attempted directly on priors without this guided sampling [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed in "Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF, which assumes that the correct shape is favored due to smoother surface light fields, an assumption that fails in poorly textured indoor environments [S1]. The authors observed that NeRF can synthesize high-quality RGB images with incorrect geometry in such areas, leading to depth deviation from ground truth [S1]. The method builds on the concept of using monocular depth priors but introduces a guided optimization strategy that uses multi-view consistency to refine these priors and guide the sampling process [S1].
