# Few-View Reconstruction via Regularization


## Overview
Few-View Reconstruction via Regularization is a family of approaches designed to mitigate the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) when supervision from multiple viewpoints is sparse [S1, unofficial]. By applying geometric or semantic priors during optimization, these methods prevent overfitting to observed views and improve generalization to unseen ones [S1, unofficial]. Unlike progressive reconstruction methods that restrain model capacity early in training [S1, unofficial], this family explicitly guides the optimization toward geometrically plausible solutions through added regularization terms [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The mechanism applies regularization terms to the optimization process to guide solutions toward geometrically plausible outcomes [S1, unofficial]. Two primary types of priors are employed:
1. Geometric Priors: These include smoothness constraints on the density field and normal consistency constraints, which help stabilize the learned density field and reduce overfitting [S1, unofficial].
2. Semantic Regularization: This approach leverages high-level scene structure to enforce cross-view consistency, promoting reconstructions that align with semantic expectations [S1, unofficial].
These terms counteract the tendency to memorize noise or artifacts in limited input data [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The challenge of reconstructing 3D scenes from sparse viewpoints is critical in real-world settings where dense multi-view captures are impractical or impossible [S1, unofficial]. Accurate camera parameters are a strong requirement for NeRF training [S1, unofficial]. This approach emerged to address shape-radiance ambiguity, distinguishing itself from progressive reconstruction techniques (e.g., FreeNeRF [193], Nerfies [135]) that rely on curriculum learning rather than explicit regularization [S1, unofficial]. Key references for geometric priors include works [129, 199, 185] for smoothness/normal consistency and [65] for semantic regularization [S1, unofficial].
