# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure for Neural Radiance Fields that incorporates sparse 3D depth supervision from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds to enforce geometric consistency [S1][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting and slow convergence when trained on a limited number of input views by adding a depth loss term to the standard photometric reconstruction loss [S1][S3, unofficial]. Unlike methods that rely on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires no additional training and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds, making it particularly effective for sparse-view synthesis [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $\{I_j\}$ with known camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (typically obtained via COLMAP); sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ and their visibility flags from the SfM process; near and far clipping bounds $t_n$ and $t_f$ for the scene.
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) capable of rendering novel views.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set of 3D points $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Visibility:** For each image $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from camera $P_j$.
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each visible keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and an uncertainty variance $\hat{\sigma}_i^2$.
*   **Ray Termination Distribution:** A discrete approximation $h_k$ of the continuous function $h(t)$ along sampled points $t_k$ on a ray.

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses $P_j$ and reconstruct sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Calculation:**
    *   For each image $I_j$ and its camera $P_j$, identify the visible keypoints $X_j$ [S1].
    *   For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
    *   Model the true depth of the first surface encountered by a ray as a random variable $D_{ij}$ normally distributed around the estimated depth:
        $$ D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i) $$
        [S1][S3, unofficial]
3.  **Ray Termination Distribution:**
    *   Define the ray termination distribution $h(t)$ as:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right)\sigma(t) $$
        where $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density at distance $t$ [S1].
    *   The distribution is valid because $\int_0^\infty h(t)dt = 1$, assuming $\sigma(s) \ge 0$ and the scene is bounded by an opaque wall at $t_f$ [S1].
    *   During rendering, sample points $t_k$ along the ray and compute the discrete weights $h_k = h(t_k) \Delta t_k$ [S1].
4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Color Loss:** Standard MSE between rendered color $\widehat{\mathbf{C}}$ and ground truth $I$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Color}} = || \widehat{\mathbf{C}} - I ||_2^2 $$
        [S1][S3, unofficial]
    *   **Depth Loss:** Minimize the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $\mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$. This is approximated as:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Depth}} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k \right] $$
        [S1]
        *Note: An MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ is also possible but yields slightly lower quality than KL divergence [S3, unofficial].*
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Color}} + \lambda_D \mathcal{L}_{\text{Depth}} $$
        where $\lambda_D$ is a hyperparameter (typically 1) [S1, S4].
5.  **Training Loop Optimization:**
    *   To avoid the overhead of separate ray marching passes, exploit the continuity of image coordinates [S1].
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For these rays, compute origins in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of detected keypoints, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
    *   Shoot these rays into the scene and render depth using the standard NeRF sampling procedure [S1].
    *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for these rays from the ground truth image $I_j$ [S1].
    *   Simultaneously supervise RGB and depth for these rays within a single ray marching procedure [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

    """
    images: List of ground truth images I_j
    poses: List of camera poses P_j
    keypoints: List of 3D points X
    uncertainties: Dictionary mapping keypoint index to sigma_hat_i
    near: t_n
    far: t_f
    num_iters: Number of training iterations
    lambda_d: Weight for depth loss
    """
    nerf_model = initialize_nerf()
    
    for iteration in range(num_iters):
        # 1. Sample rays
        # Allocate a portion of rays to keypoint rays, rest to random rays
        random_rays = sample_random_rays(images, poses)
        keypoint_rays = []
        
        for j, (img, pose) in enumerate(zip(images, poses)):
            # Identify visible keypoints for this view
            visible_keys = get_visible_keypoints(keypoints, pose)
            
            for key in visible_keys:
                # Compute ray direction from keypoint image coordinates
                # In local camera coords, direction is (u, v, -1) normalized
                ray_dir = compute_ray_direction_from_keypoint(key, pose)
                ray_origin = pose.origin
                
                # Interpolate RGB supervision from image at keypoint location
                rgb_supervision = interpolate_rgb(img, key.image_coords)
                
                keypoint_rays.append({
                    'origin': ray_origin,
                    'direction': ray_dir,
                    'rgb_target': rgb_supervision,
                    'depth_target': key.projected_depth,
                    'depth_sigma': uncertainties[key.index]
                })
        
        # 2. Forward Pass: Render Random Rays
        rendered_colors_random, _, _ = render_rays(nerf_model, random_rays, near, far)
        loss_color_random = mse(rendered_colors_random, [r['target'] for r in random_rays])
        
        # 3. Forward Pass: Render Keypoint Rays (Single Pass for both RGB and Depth)
        # We render these rays to get the termination distribution h(t)
        rendered_colors_kp, termination_dist, sample_points = render_rays(nerf_model, keypoint_rays, near, far)
        
        loss_color_kp = 0
        loss_depth = 0
        
        for i, ray in enumerate(keypoint_rays):
            # Color Loss
            loss_color_kp += mse(rendered_colors_kp[i], ray['rgb_target'])
            
            # Depth Loss (KL Divergence)
            # h_k is the weight at sample point t_k
            # D_ij is ray['depth_target'], sigma_i is ray['depth_sigma']
            kl_term = 0
            for k, (t_k, h_k) in enumerate(zip(sample_points, termination_dist[i])):
                # Gaussian likelihood of depth target given sample point
                gaussian_prob = exp(-(t_k - ray['depth_target'])**2 / (2 * ray['depth_sigma']**2))
                # KL term: -log(h_k) * P(D_ij | t_k)
                # Note: The source formula is E[-log h(t) * exp(...)]
                # In discrete sum: sum_k -log(h_k) * exp(...) * delta_t_k
                # However, standard KL is sum p log(p/q). 
                # The source defines L_Depth = E[-log h(t) * exp(...)].
                # This effectively minimizes the cross-entropy between the Gaussian prior and the NeRF distribution.
                kl_term += -log(h_k + epsilon) * gaussian_prob * (sample_points[1] - sample_points[0])
            
            loss_depth += kl_term
            
        loss_color_kp /= len(keypoint_rays)
        loss_depth /= len(keypoint_rays)
        
        # 4. Total Loss
        loss = loss_color_random + loss_color_kp + lambda_d * loss_depth
        
        # 5. Backpropagate
        gradient = compute_gradients(loss, nerf_model)
        update_parameters(nerf_model, gradient)

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   Training Iterations: $O(10k - 100k)$ [S3, unofficial].
*   Image Resolution: $400 \times 300$ [S1].
*   Keypoint Sparseness: Performance degrades with fewer keypoints but remains superior to baseline NeRF [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different datasets (e.g., NeRF Real) if fine-tuned without care, though DS-NeRF itself is domain-agnostic [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are reduced by 2-3x compared to standard NeRF [S1]. Wall-clock time per iteration is comparable ($\sim 362.4$ ms vs $\sim 359.8$ ms on RTX A5000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as depth supervision is integrated into the existing ray marching loop [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the NeRF Real dataset with 5 input views, DS-NeRF achieves a test PSNR of 22.6 (100% keypoints) compared to NeRF's 18.2 [S1]. It reaches target PSNR significantly faster (e.g., 13 hours faster in 5-view setup) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with few views, compatible with other NeRF methods, requires no pre-training on other scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the success of SfM; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].

## Background
DS-NeRF was introduced by Deng et al. in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the limitation of NeRF requiring many views for high-quality reconstruction by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM pipelines (like COLMAP) which are already required for NeRF training [S1]. The method was applied to datasets including LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU [S1].
