# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth-Supervised NeRF, Geometric Priors

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of regularization techniques that incorporate explicit geometric constraints—typically derived from depth priors, 3D keypoints, or multi-view consistency—into the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). These methods solve the ambiguity between shape and radiance, particularly in sparse-view settings where photometric loss alone leads to degenerate solutions such as "floaters" (translucent artifacts in empty space) or incorrect geometry on texture-less surfaces [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on semantic consistency or diffusion priors, this family explicitly constrains the density field or ray termination distributions to align with known geometric structures [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the augmented loss structure defined in [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md), where the total loss is a weighted sum of photometric reconstruction and geometric regularization terms [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

The geometric terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ generally fall into two categories:
1.  **Direct Depth Supervision:** Comparing the rendered expected depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ with a ground truth or pseudo-depth prior $D(\mathbf{r})$ using pixel-wise losses (e.g., L1, L2, or robust variants) [S2, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md]. The rendered depth is computed as the expectation of the ray termination distribution:
    $$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i $$
    where $w_i$ are the rendering weights and $t_i$ are sample depths [S2, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md].
2.  **Indirect/Structural Supervision:** Constraining the probability distribution of the rendering weights $w_i$ or the density field $\sigma$ to match a prior distribution (e.g., via KL divergence) or enforcing structural consistency (e.g., smoothness, multi-view consistency) without direct pixel-wise depth matching [S2, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md].

Gradients from these terms are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss designed to prevent degenerate solutions in few-view settings by combining photometric loss with three geometric priors: foreground completeness (forcing $\sum w_i = 1$), distribution compactness, and multi-view consistency (penalizing density visible in only one frustum). It is chosen when avoiding "image copying" artifacts is critical.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured scenes by dynamically adjusting the ray sampling range $[t_n, t_f]$ based on monocular depth priors and cross-view consistency errors, rather than adding a loss term. It is chosen when sampling efficiency and avoiding implausible geometry regions are priorities.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Accelerates optimization and improves quality with sparse views by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution $h(t)$ aligns with sparse 3D keypoints from SfM using KL divergence. It is chosen when sparse, accurate 3D keypoints are available and data-driven priors are undesirable.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A geometric regularization term using dense but potentially inaccurate pseudo-depth maps. It uses a piecewise loss that is quadratic for small errors and logarithmic for large errors, making it robust to outliers. It is chosen when using noisy pseudo-depth from monocular estimators.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity constraints on rendered depth maps by comparing depth differences between neighboring pixels against a reference depth map (e.g., DepthAnythingV2), tolerating minor variations via a boundary value. It is chosen to reduce noise and discontinuities in sparse-view reconstruction.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity by injecting synthetic "novel view" rays into the training loop to provide additional geometric supervision, reducing overfitting to input views. It is chosen when generalization to unseen viewpoints is the primary goal.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit constraints to opacity parameters to enforce geometric compactness and binary opacity values, solving the "floater" problem. It is chosen when translucent artifacts in empty space are a major issue.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the Euclidean distance between 3D points backprojected from rendered depth and ground-truth 3D keypoints, enabling pose refinement in sparse-view regimes. It is chosen when global geometric registration is required.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Extends NeRF optimization to arbitrary rays (e.g., shadow rays) by treating them as sensors, leveraging known light source positions to supervise density fields. It is chosen when non-camera rays (like shadows) are available and useful.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
Adaptive weighting mechanisms that modulate geometric and photometric supervision based on scene segmentation (background vs. foreground), solving conflicts between static geometry and moving objects. It is chosen for dynamic street scene synthesis.

### [Depth Priors and Initialization](15188-depth-priors-and-initialization.md)
Uses depth priors to initialize the NeRF geometry or guide the initial sampling distribution, improving convergence speed and final quality. It is chosen when initialization from a blank field leads to slow convergence or local minima.

### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
$$ \mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbf{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbf{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2 $$
where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S2].
$$ L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}\right) \Delta t_k $$
where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2]. The kernel $\epsilon(x)$ integrates to one [S2].

### Direct Depth Supervision
A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S2].
$$ L_{MSE} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \|D(r_i) - \hat{D}(r_i)\|^2, \quad L_{L1} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} |D(r_i) - \hat{D}(r_i)| $$
where $D(r_i)$ and $\hat{D}(r_i)$ are the predicted and ground truth depth of ray $r_i$ [S2].

### GeoAug
An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The problem of geometric ambiguity in NeRF was first addressed by [DS-NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md), which introduced KL-divergence-based depth supervision using sparse SfM keypoints [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. Subsequent work explored direct depth losses with pseudo-depth from monocular estimators [S2, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md] and composite geometric baselines to prevent degenerate solutions in sparse views [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md]. The field has expanded to include robust losses for noisy priors [9368-robust-depth-loss.md], structural consistency [9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md], and dynamic scene handling [15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md].
