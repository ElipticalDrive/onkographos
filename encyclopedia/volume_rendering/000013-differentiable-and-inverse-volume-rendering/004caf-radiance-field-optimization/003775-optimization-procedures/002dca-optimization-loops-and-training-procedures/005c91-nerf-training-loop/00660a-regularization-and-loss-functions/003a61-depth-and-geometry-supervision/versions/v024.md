# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Supervised NeRF

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of regularization techniques that incorporate geometric constraints—such as depth maps, 3D point clouds, or structural priors—into the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity and prevent degenerate solutions [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. Unlike pure photometric methods that rely solely on RGB reconstruction, these methods enforce geometric consistency by adding terms to the total loss that penalize deviations from expected depth distributions, sparse 3D keypoints, or structural smoothness [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md]. This family is distinct from [Diffusion-regularized NeRF](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md) (which uses generative priors) and [Semantic Consistency Loss](11955-semantic-consistency-loss-clip-based.md) (which uses high-level semantic features), focusing instead on explicit metric or structural geometric signals [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the augmented loss structure defined by [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md), where the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ is a weighted sum of the photometric reconstruction loss and one or more geometric regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

The photometric loss is typically the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered color $\mathbf{C}$ and the ground truth image $I$:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = || \mathbf{C} - I ||_2^2 $$

Geometric regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ operate on the density $\sigma$, color $\mathbf{c}$, or the rendered weights $w_i$ to constrain the solution space. Common strategies include:
1.  **Depth Matching:** Aligning the expected depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ with ground truth or pseudo-depth values $D(\mathbf{r})$ using L1, L2, or robust loss functions [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md].
2.  **Distribution Alignment:** Minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution $h(t)$ and a target depth distribution [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md].
3.  **Structural Consistency:** Enforcing smoothness or relative depth ordering between neighboring pixels [S1, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md].
4.  **3D Point Constraints:** Minimizing the Euclidean distance between backprojected rendered depths and sparse 3D keypoints [S1, 12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md].
5.  **Opacity/Sparsity Priors:** Encouraging binary opacity values to eliminate floaters [S1, 11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md].

The gradients of these terms are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss function designed to prevent degenerate solutions in few-view settings by combining foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness. It derives constraints solely from the differentiable volume rendering process and training camera frustums, without requiring external depth sensors. Choose this method when training on sparse views where the network might collapse to reproducing training images directly.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes by adapting the sampling range of rays using monocular depth priors and multi-view geometric consistency errors. It sets itself apart by using depth priors to guide *sampling locations* (near/far bounds) rather than as a direct loss term, although it employs confidence-based filtering on the output. Choose this for indoor scenes with limited texture where standard uniform sampling fails to converge to the correct geometry.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Incorporates sparse 3D depth supervision from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and the noisy depth distribution. Unlike methods relying on dense pseudo-depth or external priors, DS-NeRF requires no additional training and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds. Choose this for sparse-view synthesis where SfM provides reliable sparse keypoints.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A regularization term designed to mitigate geometry error degradation when using dense but inaccurate pseudo-depth maps. It uses a piecewise function (quadratic for small errors, logarithmic for large errors) to reduce sensitivity to outliers compared to standard L2 loss. Choose this when pseudo-depth maps (e.g., from monocular estimators) contain significant noise or outliers.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity in rendered depth maps by aligning the relative depth relationships of neighboring pixels with those in a reference depth map (e.g., from DepthAnythingV2). It distinguishes itself by focusing on structural consistency (relative ordering) rather than absolute depth matching. Choose this when absolute depth accuracy is less critical than preserving local geometric structure and smoothness.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity by injecting synthetic rays from unobserved viewpoints into the training loop to provide additional geometric supervision. It sets itself apart by actively sampling unobserved viewpoints rather than relying on static geometric priors. Choose this when overfitting to training views is a primary concern and synthetic viewpoints can be generated.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit constraints to opacity parameters to enforce geometric compactness and binary opacity values, solving the problem of "floaters" and translucent artifacts. Unlike sibling methods that rely on external depth priors, this method operates directly on the internal opacity distribution. Choose this to clean up reconstructed geometry and remove translucent artifacts in empty space.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the Euclidean distance between 3D points backprojected from rendered depth and ground-truth 3D points, enabling robust camera pose registration in sparse-view regimes. Unlike depth-only losses, it enforces a global geometric connection in the world coordinate frame. Choose this for sparse-view pose registration where photometric or depth-only losses fail to provide global consistency.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Treats arbitrary rays (e.g., shadow rays) as "ray sensors" to constrain the neural field, extending standard camera ray supervision. It enables the use of non-camera rays to leverage additional geometric constraints. Choose this when additional geometric information (like light source directions) is available and can be modeled as rays.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting strategy for dynamic street scenes, distinguishing between static background and dynamic foreground (vehicles). It solves the problem of unreliable depth supervision in autonomous driving datasets by applying geometry-aware confidence metrics to backgrounds and RGB/SSIM-only metrics to vehicles. Choose this for autonomous driving datasets with dynamic objects and unreliable LiDAR depth for vehicles.

#### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
- **Definition:** A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
- **Formula:** $\mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot ||z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)||^2 + \mathbb{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot ||\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f||^2$, where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

#### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
- **Definition:** A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S2].
- **Formula:** $L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}) \Delta t_k$, where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2].
- **Parameter:** $\epsilon(x)$: A kernel that integrates to one (a distribution) with a bounded domain parameterized by $\epsilon$ [S2].
- **Pitfall:** The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S2].

#### Direct Depth Supervision
- **Definition:** A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S2].
- **Step:** First sample a batch of $N_r$ rays, then compare the rendered depth and ground truth depth with different loss functions (MSE or L1) [S2].

#### GeoAug
- **Definition:** An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The concept of depth supervision in NeRF originated with [Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF)](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md), which introduced the use of sparse SfM keypoints to regularize the density field via KL divergence [S1, S3]. Subsequent works expanded this to include dense pseudo-depth from monocular estimators [S1, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md], robust loss functions to handle noise [S1, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md], and structural consistency constraints [S1, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md]. The family also includes methods that use depth priors to guide sampling ranges [S1, 7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md] or to regularize opacity distributions [S1, 11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md]. Recent developments include confidence-weighted supervision for dynamic scenes [S1, 15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md] and generalized ray supervision for non-camera rays [S1, 14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md]. These methods are widely used in sparse-view synthesis, indoor reconstruction, and autonomous driving applications where geometric fidelity is critical [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md].
