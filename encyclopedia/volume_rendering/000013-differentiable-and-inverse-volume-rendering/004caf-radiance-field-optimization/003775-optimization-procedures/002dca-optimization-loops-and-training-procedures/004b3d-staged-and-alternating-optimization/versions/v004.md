# Staged and Alternating Optimization

Also called: Multi-stage optimization, Alternating minimization, Progressive training

## Overview
Staged and Alternating Optimization addresses the instability and local minima inherent in jointly optimizing complex scene parameters (geometry, materials, lighting) in differentiable volume rendering and inverse rendering [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md]. By decoupling the optimization problem into sequential phases or alternating sub-problems, this family of methods stabilizes gradient descent, manages computational cost, and prevents bias from approximation thresholds [5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]. It distinguishes itself from simultaneous optimization approaches by explicitly separating the recovery of coarse geometric structure from fine-scale material details or specific physical properties [7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Stage-based Differentiable Volume/Radiance Field Training](4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md)
A stage-based optimization loop for differentiable volume or radiance field reconstruction that alternates between image-based and ray-based sampling modes, applies stochastic preconditioning with exponential alpha decay, and optionally upsamples parameters between stages. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by explicitly managing memory via ray batching and using a progressive upsampling schedule to stabilize training of high-resolution radiance fields.

### [Staggered Optimization Scheme](5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md)
The Staggered Optimization Scheme addresses the computational bottleneck and bias inherent in jointly optimizing geometric shape parameters and Point Spread Function (PSF) parameters in differentiable volume rendering. By decomposing the coupled optimization problem into two sequential sub-problems—shape optimization and PSF optimization—it manages computational cost and prevents bias from approximation thresholds. Unlike joint optimization methods that update all parameters simultaneously, this method alternates between optimizing vertex positions (with fixed PSF) and optimizing PSF covariance (with fixed shape), stabilizing the gradient descent process.

### [Two-Stage Inverse Rendering Optimization](7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md)
Two-Stage Inverse Rendering Optimization decouples the recovery of scene geometry from material reflectance and illumination by alternating between an approximate differentiable renderer and a physically-based ray tracer. It solves the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by first establishing a robust geometric proxy using silhouette constraints, then refining physically accurate albedo and environment maps while holding geometry fixed. This approach differs from single-stage radiance field methods (e.g., [NeRF](44-radiance-field-optimization.md), [PhySG](44-radiance-field-optimization.md)) by explicitly modeling triangle meshes or Gaussian splats with PBR materials, enabling direct deployment in graphics engines and superior specular handling.

### [Optimization Schedule](14063-optimization-schedule.md)
Optimization Schedule refers to the structured progression of training parameters in differentiable volume and radiance field reconstruction, designed to mitigate local minima and ensure stable convergence. It addresses the problem of ambiguous gradients in inverse rendering by decoupling the optimization of geometric structure from material properties or fine-scale details. Unlike simultaneous optimization of all parameters, which often leads to instability in sparse-view settings, this method separates the process into distinct phases or scales, allowing the solver to first establish a coarse global alignment before refining local details.

## Background
The concept of staged optimization in computer graphics originates from the need to handle ill-posed inverse problems where simultaneous optimization of geometry and appearance leads to ambiguity and local minima [7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md]. Early inverse rendering methods often used hierarchical approaches to refine geometry before appearance [7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]. The specific application of staged optimization to differentiable volume rendering and radiance fields has emerged with the rise of NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting, where training stability at high resolutions requires progressive upsampling [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md]. Alternating optimization for coupled physical parameters (like shape and blur) has been used in computational microscopy and medical imaging to reduce computational cost and bias [5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md].
