# Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)
Also called: Phase-Only Hologram Synthesis via PAGD

## Overview
Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD) is an optimization algorithm designed to encode complex wave fields into phase-only holograms for 3D holographic displays with continuous depth cues [S1]. It solves the problem of unnatural blur spreading and ringing artifacts inherent in amplitude-only or RGB-D holographic synthesis methods by incorporating phase information into the supervision signal [S1]. Unlike amplitude-based optimization, which is constrained to single viewpoints and discrete axial depth cues, PAGD captures directional light field information and enables accurate reconstruction of tilted wave fields [S1]. It builds on [Optimization Loops and Training Procedures](5622-optimization-loops-and-training-procedures.md) by utilizing a differentiable forward model (either analytical or learned) to propagate the phase hologram and compute gradients with respect to the phase values [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Mesh-represented scene $M$ [S1].
*   Camera settings $C$ (including Model-View-Projection matrices) [S1].
*   Lighting $L$ [S1].
*   Number of optimization iterations $T$ [S1].
*   Initial phase $\Phi_{init}$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized phase-only hologram $\Phi_{OPT}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $\Phi$: Complex-valued or real-valued phase map of the Spatial Light Modulator (SLM) resolution. Initialized to $\Phi_{init}$ [S1].
*   $u_{WR}$: Target complex wave field generated by the Phase-Only Wave Field Rendering (POS-WRP) model [S1].
*   $u_{ti}$: Target complex wave field at the $i$-th propagation plane ($z_i$) [S1].
*   $u_{ri}$: Reconstructed complex wave field at the $i$-th propagation plane resulting from the current phase hologram $\Phi$ [S1].
*   $s$: Complex-valued scale factor used to align the reconstructed field with the target field, accounting for intensity deviations and phase shift invariances [S1].

**Propagation Model:**
The method requires a forward model $P(\cdot)$ that propagates a wave field from the SLM plane to a target plane at distance $z$. This can be an analytical model like the Angular Spectrum Method (ASM) or a learned surrogate model trained on a "Flying Planes" dataset to capture optical imperfections (aberrations, SLM non-linearities) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialize Phase:** Set $\Phi \leftarrow \Phi_{init}$ [S1].
2.  **Generate Target Wave Field:** Compute the target complex wave field $u_{WR}$ using the POS-WRP model with inputs $M, C, L$ [S1].
3.  **Define Target Planes:** Identify three propagation distances $z_i$ (near, medium, far) [S1].
4.  **Compute Target Fields:** Propagate $u_{WR}$ to each target plane $z_i$ to obtain the target fields $u_{ti}$:
    $$ u_{ti} = P(u_{WR}, z_i) $$
    [S1]
5.  **Iterative Optimization:** For iteration $n$ from 1 to $T$:
    a.  **Reconstruct Fields:** For each of the 3 planes $i$:
        Propagate the current phase hologram $e^{j\Phi}$ shifted by the wave field recording distance $z_{WR}$ and the target plane distance $z_i$ to obtain the reconstructed field $u_{ri}$:
        $$ u_{ri} = P(e^{j\Phi}, z_{WR} + z_i) $$
        [S1]
    b.  **Compute Scale Factor:** Calculate the complex-valued scale factor $s$ that minimizes the difference between $s \odot u_{ri}$ and $u_{ti}$. The explicit expression for $s$ is derived by minimizing the MSE between the real and imaginary parts:
        $$ \text{Re}(s) = \frac{\text{Re}(u_r) \odot \text{Re}(u_t) + \text{Im}(u_r) \odot \text{Im}(u_t)}{\text{Re}(u_r)^2 + \text{Im}(u_r)^2} $$
        $$ \text{Im}(s) = \frac{-\text{Im}(u_r) \odot \text{Re}(u_t) + \text{Re}(u_r) \odot \text{Im}(u_t)}{\text{Re}(u_r)^2 + \text{Im}(u_r)^2} $$
        where $u_r$ and $u_t$ represent the reconstructed field $u_{ri}$ and target field $u_{ti}$ respectively, and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication [S1].
    c.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate the total loss $E(\Phi)$ by summing the scaled losses for all 3 planes:
        $$ E(\Phi) = \sum_{i=1}^3 L(s_i \odot u_{ri}, u_{ti}) $$
        where the loss function $L$ is defined as:
        $$ L(s \odot u_r, u_t) = \text{MSE}(\text{Re}(s u_r), \text{Re}(u_t)) + \text{MSE}(\text{Im}(s u_r), \text{Im}(u_t)) $$
        [S1]
    d.  **Backpropagation:** Compute the gradient $\nabla E(\Phi)$ and update $\Phi$ using gradient descent (or an optimizer like Adam) [S1].
6.  **Finalize:** Set $\Phi_{OPT} = \Phi$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $T$: Number of optimization iterations. Typical training time for the PAGD component is 30 seconds per hologram [S1].
*   $z_{WR}$: Distance associated with the wave field recording step in POS-WRP [S1].
*   $z_i$: Distances for the near, medium, and far target planes [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Phase Shift Invariance:** Phase supervision faces challenges due to non-uniqueness. Two inherent shift invariances exist: 1) Global shift in phase does not alter the wave field. 2) Phase value on each pixel can be shifted by $2\pi$ without changing the target field [S1]. The complex-valued scale factor $s$ accounts for these deviations and light source intensity variations [S1].
*   **Tilted Wave Fields:** Amplitude-only supervision fails to reconstruct correct defocus cues on unsupervised planes for tilted wave fields (e.g., 1° tilt). Phase-aware supervision is required for accurate reconstruction in such cases [S1].
*   **Optical Imperfections:** Numerical models like ASM struggle to replicate real-world optical systems due to aberrations, SLM non-linearities, and stray light. Using a learned forward model mitigates this [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Camera-Calibrated Wave Propagation Model Training](12905-camera-calibrated-wave-propagation-model-training.md)
This method trains a data-driven surrogate model to predict the intensity distribution of light propagating from a Spatial Light Modulator (SLM) to multiple target planes, capturing real-world optical imperfections such as non-linearities, aberrations, and stray light. It solves the model mismatch problem inherent in analytical propagation methods (like the Angular Spectrum Method) by replacing idealized physics with empirical measurements, thereby providing accurate gradient signals for phase-aware gradient descent (PAGD). It is distinct from analytical models by mapping input phase patterns directly to captured multi-plane intensity images rather than computing them via wave optics equations.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The training time for the PAGD optimizer component is approximately 30 seconds per hologram [S1]. The total time for generating one phase-only hologram from a mesh scene is on average 54.96 s (24.96 s for POS-WRP and 30 s for PAGD) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Phase-aware supervision offers more accurate reconstruction than amplitude-only supervision, particularly for tilted wave fields and continuous depth cues [S1]. It mitigates ringing artifacts and unnatural blur spreading found in RGB-D methods [S1]. It adheres to the physical two-step hologram process (recording and reconstruction) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires training the forward model (if learned) [S1]. Real-time hologram generation remains a goal for future exploration [S1].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity 3D holographic displays with continuous depth cues and accurate defocus are required, especially for scenes with tilted wave fields or when amplitude-only methods produce unnatural blur [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and the computational cost of iterative optimization is prohibitive [S1].

## Background
PAGD was proposed in a 2026 paper published in *Nature Communications* by researchers investigating mesh-represented and learning-empowered hologram synthesis for full 3D holographic displays [S1]. The method addresses limitations in existing end-to-end optimization techniques, which often rely on amplitude-only supervision or implicit models that fail to capture the physical two-step process of holography [S1]. It builds upon the Phase-Only Wave Field Rendering (POS-WRP) technique [S1].
