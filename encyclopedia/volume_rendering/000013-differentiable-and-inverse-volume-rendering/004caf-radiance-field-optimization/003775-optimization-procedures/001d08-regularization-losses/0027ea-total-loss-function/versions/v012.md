# Total Loss Function

Also called: Combined Loss, Full Training Objective, Loss function, Total Loss

## Overview
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective used to optimize radiance field parameters (density, color, geometry, or Gaussian attributes) by minimizing the discrepancy between rendered images and ground-truth observations. It aggregates multiple loss terms—such as photometric reconstruction, geometric regularization, and latent space constraints—into a single scalar value [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. Unlike individual regularization methods that enforce specific properties (e.g., smoothness or sparsity), the Total Loss Function orchestrates the trade-off between data fidelity and structural priors to stabilize training and prevent overfitting [S2, S8]. It differs from [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md) by encompassing the entire optimization objective, including the primary reconstruction error, rather than serving as a supplementary constraint [S2].

## How it works
The method combines distinct loss components $\mathcal{L}_k$ weighted by hyperparameters $\lambda_k$. The specific terms depend on the radiance field representation (volumetric, implicit, or Gaussian) and the available supervision signals (RGB, depth, masks) [S1, S2][S3, unofficial][S8].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Rendered image $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$, auxiliary supervision (depth $\mathbf{D}^*$, masks $\mathbf{m}$, normals $\mathbf{n}^*$), model parameters $\theta$ (e.g., densities $\sigma$, colors $\mathbf{c}$, SDF values $f$, latent codes $\mathbf{z}$), and hyperparameters $\lambda$.
- **Outputs**: Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ and gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ for the optimizer.

### Data Structures
The loss computation typically operates on tensors of shape $(B, H, W, C)$ for images or $(N)$ for rays/samples, where $B$ is batch size, $H, W$ are spatial dimensions, and $C$ is channels (usually 3 for RGB).

### Formulas and Steps
The general form is:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k
$$

#### 1. Photometric Reconstruction Loss
The primary term aligns rendered colors with ground truth. Common variants include L1, L2, and SSIM [S1][S3, unofficial].

**L1 Loss** [S3, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |C_i - \hat{C}_i|
$$
where $N$ is the number of batch rays, $C_i$ is ground truth color, and $\hat{C}_i$ is rendered color.

**L2 Loss** [S2]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*(\mathbf{p}) \right\|^2
$$
where $P$ is the number of sampled pixels.

**SSIM Loss** [S1]: Often combined with L2/L1. Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) is computed over local windows.

#### 2. Geometric and Regularization Losses
These terms enforce physical plausibility or smoothness.

**Eikonal Loss** [S3, unofficial]: Regularizes the gradient magnitude of an SDF field $f$ to 1.
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{i,j=1}^{MN} \|\nabla f(p_{i,j}) - 1\|^2
$$
where $M$ is sampled locations per ray, $N$ is batch rays.

**Total Variation (TV) Loss** [S2][S6, unofficial]: Encourages spatial smoothness in opacity or bilateral grid parameters.
For a tensor $x$ with batch size $B$ and dimensions $D$:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{TV}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=2}^{D} \frac{\sum_{j} (x_{..., j+1, ...} - x_{..., j, ...})^2}{N_i}
$$
where $N_i$ is the number of elements in dimension $i$ excluding batch and channel.

**KL Divergence** [S2]: Used in variational autoencoder-based radiance fields to constrain latent encoding $\mathbf{z}$ to a standard normal distribution.
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} = D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1))
$$

#### 3. Auxiliary and Specific Losses
- **Optical Flow Loss** [S1]: $\mathcal{L}_f$ for dynamic scenes.
- **Depth Consistency** [S1, S8]: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}$ penalizing differences between rendered and sparse depth.
- **Opacity/Throughput Losses** [S4, unofficial]:
  - **Empty Space Loss**: Encourages throughput $T_i \to 0$ where mask $m_i=0$.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{empty}} = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|
    $$
  - **Opacity Loss**: Encourages throughput $T_i \to 1$ where mask $m_i=1$.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{opacity}} = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|
    $$
- **Discriminator Loss** [S7, unofficial]: For GAN-based training, including R1 gradient penalty.
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_D = \text{mean}(\text{softplus}(-\text{pred}_{\text{real}})) + \text{mean}(\text{softplus}(\text{pred}_{\text{fake}})) + \text{gp\_loss\_mult} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \text{mean}(\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} D(\hat{x})\|^2)
  $$
  where $\hat{x}$ is interpolated between real and fake patches.

#### 4. Curriculum Learning [S8]
In some methods, the loss weights shift during training.
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}
$$
where $\beta = -10^5$ and $t$ is the iteration number.

### Parameters and Typical Values
- **$\lambda_{\text{KL}}$**: 0.001 [S2].
- **$\lambda_{\text{tv}}$**: 0.01 [S2].
- **$\lambda_B$**: 0.1 [S2].
- **$\alpha$**: 5 for real-world, 1 for synthetic [S1].
- **$\beta$**: 0.0008 [S1].
- **$\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$**: 1, 1, 0.25 respectively for uncertain SDF, bias, and point cloud losses [S3, unofficial].
- **Weight for AOV losses**: 1.0 [S4, unofficial].
- **Batch Size**: 16 [S2].
- **Learning Rate**: $10^{-4}$ for encoder-decoder weights [S2].

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_total_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image, depth=None, mask=None, 
                       sdf_field=None, latent_z=None, bilateral_grid=None, 
                       discriminator=None, real_patches=None, fake_patches=None,
                       iteration=0, config=None):
    """
    Computes the total loss for radiance field optimization.
    """
    loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Photometric Loss (L1 or L2 + SSIM)
    # Using L1 as default example from [S3, unofficial]
    l_rgb = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered_image - ground_truth_image))
    loss += l_rgb
    
    # Optional: Add SSIM if supported by config
    if config.use_ssim:
        l_ssim = compute_ssim_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image)
        loss += l_ssim

    # 2. Geometric Regularization (Eikonal)
    if sdf_field is not None:
        # Sample points on rays for gradient computation
        # Assuming get_eikonal_loss handles sampling and gradient calculation [S3, unofficial]
        l_eik = compute_eikonal_loss(sdf_field)
        loss += 0.1 * l_eik

    # 3. Latent Regularization (KL Divergence)
    if latent_z is not None:
        # KL(z || N(0,1)) [S2]
        l_kl = compute_kl_divergence(latent_z)
        loss += config.lambda_kl * l_kl

    # 4. Total Variation (Bilateral Grid or Opacity)
    if bilateral_grid is not None:
        l_tv = compute_total_variation(bilateral_grid)
        loss += config.lambda_tv * l_tv

    # 5. Auxiliary Losses (Depth, Flow, Masks)
    if depth is not None:
        # Example: Depth consistency [S1]
        l_depth = compute_depth_loss(rendered_depth, depth)
        loss += config.lambda_depth * l_depth
        
    if mask is not None:
        # Empty Space and Opacity Losses [S4, unofficial]
        # Throughput T is assumed to be available from renderer
        throughput = rendered_image[:, :, :, 3] # Assuming alpha channel or separate output
        l_empty = torch.mean((1 - mask) * torch.abs(throughput))
        l_opacity = torch.mean(mask * torch.abs(throughput - 1.0))
        loss += config.weight_empty * l_empty
        loss += config.weight_opacity * l_opacity

    # 6. GAN Discriminator Loss [S7, unofficial]
    if discriminator is not None and real_patches is not None:
        pred_real = discriminator(real_patches)
        pred_fake = discriminator(fake_patches.detach())
        
        l_d_real = torch.mean(torch.nn.functional.softplus(-pred_real))
        l_d_fake = torch.mean(torch.nn.functional.softplus(pred_fake))
        
        # Gradient Penalty
        real_patches.requires_grad_(True)
        pred_real_sum = torch.sum(discriminator(real_patches))
        gradients = torch.autograd.grad(pred_real_sum, real_patches, create_graph=True)[0]
        gradients = gradients.view(gradients.size(0), -1)
        gradient_penalty = torch.mean(torch.sum(gradients**2, dim=1)) / 2.0
        
        l_d = l_d_real + l_d_fake + config.gp_loss_mult * gradient_penalty
        loss += l_d

    # 7. Curriculum Learning Schedule [S8]
    if config.use_curriculum:
        beta = -1e5
        t = iteration
        weight_photo = 2 ** (beta * t)
        weight_rgb = 1 - weight_photo
        # Note: This modifies the weighting of existing terms, not adding new ones.
        # Implementation depends on how terms are accumulated.
        # Simplified: Re-weight the final scalar if terms are stored separately.
        pass

    return loss
```

### Empty Space Loss
An AOV loss encouraging rendering throughput to equal 0.0 in regions where a binary object mask is inactive [S4, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|$ [S4, unofficial]. Parameters: $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$, weight $w=1.0$ [S4, unofficial].

### Opacity Loss
An AOV loss encouraging rendering throughput to equal 1.0 in regions where a binary object mask is active [S4, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|$ [S4, unofficial]. Parameters: $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$, weight $w=1.0$ [S4, unofficial].

### Auxiliary Losses
Includes per-point RGB loss for color emission supervision and background entropy loss to concentrate background probability [S5]. Adding both yields PSNR 35.54 vs 35.37 without [S5].

### Total Variation Loss
Regularization loss computing total variation of bilateral grid parameters to encourage smoothness in color transformations [S6, unofficial]. Formula: $L_{TV} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=2}^{D} \frac{\sum_{j} (x_{..., j+1, ...} - x_{..., j, ...})^2}{N_i}$ [S6, unofficial].

### Discriminator Loss with Gradient Penalty
Loss for training the discriminator, including R1 gradient penalty for Lipschitz continuity [S7, unofficial]. Formula: $\text{loss}_D = \text{mean}(\text{softplus}(-\text{pred}_{\text{real}})) + \text{mean}(\text{softplus}(\text{pred}_{\text{fake}})) + \text{gp\_loss\_mult} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \text{mean}(\sum \text{gradients}^2)$ [S7, unofficial].

### Background Blending for Loss Computation
Blends predicted RGB with ground truth background color for accurate loss computation when background is not black [S7, unofficial].

### Curriculum Learning Loss
Transitions from depth/photometric supervision to RGB supervision using an exponential schedule [S8]. Formula: $\mathcal {L}_{\text {final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal {L}_{\text {depth}} + \mathcal {L}_{\text {photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal {L}_{\text {rgb}}$ with $\beta = -1e5$ [S8].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Dominated by the forward rendering pass and the backward pass through the loss terms. The number of loss terms adds linear overhead to the gradient computation.
- **Memory Complexity**: High, as intermediate rendering states (transmittance, colors) must be stored for backpropagation [S5]. Sampling strategies (e.g., 128x128 pixels) reduce memory footprint [S2].
- **Performance**: Training can take significant time; e.g., 500,000 iterations on a single NVIDIA Tesla V100 takes 10 days [S2].
- **Strengths**: Combines multiple signals for robust reconstruction; handles ill-posed inverse problems by adding priors [S2, S8].
- **Limitations**: Sensitive to hyperparameter weights; improper balancing leads to artifacts (e.g., floaters, blurry details) [S1, S2].

## Background
The concept of combining multiple loss terms for radiance field optimization emerged with early neural volume rendering works. [S2] (Neural Volumes, 2019) introduced a comprehensive training objective combining photometric error, KL divergence for latent encoding, and total variation priors, setting a precedent for composite loss functions in differentiable rendering. Subsequent works like [S1] (Dynamic Gaussians, 2025) and [S3, unofficial] (Point-NeuS, 2023) refined these objectives by adding geometric constraints (Eikonal, depth consistency) and auxiliary losses to address specific challenges like dynamic scenes and surface reconstruction.
