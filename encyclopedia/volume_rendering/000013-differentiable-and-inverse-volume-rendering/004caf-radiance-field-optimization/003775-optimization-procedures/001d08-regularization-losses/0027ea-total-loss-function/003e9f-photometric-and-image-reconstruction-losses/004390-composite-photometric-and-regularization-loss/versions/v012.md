# Composite Photometric and Regularization Loss

Also called: Combined Loss Function, L_recon + λ_TV L_TV + λ_smooth L_smooth, Model Optimization with Loss Functions, Optimization Loss, Optimization Loss Functions, Training Losses, Training Objective and Regularization

## Overview
This method defines the total training objective for differentiable volume rendering and inverse rendering pipelines by aggregating a primary photometric reconstruction term with multiple regularization constraints. It solves the ill-posed nature of recovering 3D structure and appearance from 2D images by penalizing spatial noise, enforcing physical priors (such as SDF gradient norms or Gaussian compactness), and stabilizing optimization. It differs from its sibling [Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting](4262-composite-image-loss-with-visibility-weighting.md) by explicitly incorporating diverse geometric and structural regularizers (e.g., total variation, eikonal, size penalties) rather than focusing solely on visibility-weighted photometric fidelity.

## How it works
The method computes a scalar loss $\mathcal{L}$ by summing weighted components. The specific components depend on the scene representation (voxels, Gaussians, SDFs) and the rendering setup (static, dynamic, inverse rendering).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image/patch $\tilde{I}$ (or $\mathbf{C}_{\text{Inp}}, \mathbf{C}_{\text{Up}}$), ground-truth image/patch $I$ (or $\mathbf{C}_{\text{gt}}$), scene parameters (opacity $\sigma$, SH coefficients, Gaussian scales $s$, SDF $S$, roughness $\alpha$), and sampled rays $\mathcal{R}$.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ and gradients for backpropagation.

### Data Structures
*   **Rays:** Set $\mathcal{R}$ of sampled rays.
*   **Voxels/Grid:** Voxel grid containing opacity $\sigma$ and color features (e.g., SH coefficients).
*   **Gaussians:** Set $\mathcal{G}$ of primitives, each with position, rotation, scale $s_{i,j}$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color.
*   **SDF:** Implicit function $S(\mathbf{x})$ and its gradient $\nabla_x S$.

### Formulas and Steps
The total loss is generally structured as:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}, k} $$

#### 1. Photometric Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$)
Computes the error between rendered and ground-truth images. Variants include:

*   **L1/L2 + Perceptual:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \| \tilde{I}_P - I_P \|_2^2 + \| F_{\text{vgg}} (\tilde{I}_P) - F_{\text{vgg}} (I_P) \|_2^2 $$
    where $F_{\text{vgg}}$ is a VGG feature extractor [S4].

*   **Multi-Grid Photometric:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \frac {1}{|\mathcal {R}|} \sum _{r\in \mathcal {R}} \left \| \mathbf {C_{\text {Inp}}}(\mathbf {r}) - \mathbf {C}_{\text {gt}}(\mathbf {r}) \right \|_2^2 + \frac {1}{|\mathcal {R}|} \sum _{r\in \mathcal {R}} \left \| \mathbf {C_{\text {Up}}}(\mathbf {r}) - \mathbf {C}_{\text {gt}}(\mathbf {r}) \right \|_2^2 $$
    where $\mathbf{C}_{\text{Inp}}$ is rendered from an inpainted grid and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{Up}}$ from an up-sampled grid [S2].

*   **Saturation-Masked LDR:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}=\frac {1}{|\mathcal {R}|} \sum _{\mathbf {r} \in \mathcal {R}} M_i(\mathbf {r}) \|I_i\left (\mathrm {\Pi }_i(\mathbf {r})\right ) -\mathcal{T} \left (\hat {C}(\mathbf {r})\right )\|_{2}^{2} $$
    where $\Pi_i$ is camera projection, $M_i$ is a saturation mask, and $\mathcal{T}$ is tone mapping [S1].

*   **Pyramid + SSIM:**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = L_2(\text{pyramid}(\hat{I}), \text{pyramid}(I)) + (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)) $$
    where $\text{pyramid}$ denotes a Gaussian pyramid [S6].

*   **LD-SSIM:**
    Used in combination with L1: $\mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}}$ [S3, S7].

#### 2. Regularization Terms
Specific regularizers are applied based on the representation:

*   **Total Variation (TV) Loss:**
    Encourages spatial smoothness. For voxels:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{TV}}=\frac {1}{|\mathcal {V}|} \sum _{\mathbf {v} \in \mathcal {V}, d \in [D]} \sqrt {\Delta _{x}^{2}(\mathbf {v}, d)+\Delta _{y}^{2}(\mathbf {v}, d)+\Delta _{z}^{2}(\mathbf {v}, d) +\epsilon } $$
    where $\Delta$ is the difference between successive voxels along axes [S1]. Applied to opacity $\sigma$ and SH coefficients separately [S1]. Also used for density $\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_{\sigma}^{\text{Can}})$, flow $\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_{\text{flow}}^t)$, and depth $\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{D})$ [S2].

*   **Smoothness Loss (CRF):**
    Ensures physically appropriate Camera Response Functions (CRF):
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}=\sum _{i=1}^N \sum _{e \in [0,1]} g_i^{\prime \prime }(e)^{2} $$
    where $g_i''(e)$ is the second-order derivative of the CRF $g_i$ [S1].

*   **Eikonal Loss (SDF):**
    Enforces unit gradient magnitude for valid SDF:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eikonal}} = \| \nabla_x S - 1 \|_2^2 $$
    [S6].

*   **Size/Compactness Regularization (Gaussians):**
    *   **Volume Term:** Penalizes oversized primitives by the product of scale axes:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{vol}} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_i \prod_{j=1}^3 s_{i,j} $$
        [S7].
    *   **Opacity Term:** Drives redundant Gaussians to zero:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{op}} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_i \alpha_i $$
        [S7].
    *   **Scaling Regularization:** Prevents long, thin Gaussians:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{scaling}}(i) = \begin{cases} |\hat{s}_i|, & \hat{s}_i > \max(T_s, T_r \tilde{s}_i) \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        where $\hat{s}_i$ is max scaling, $\tilde{s}_i$ is min scaling, $T_s$ is length threshold, $T_r$ is thinness ratio [S5, unofficial].
    *   **Triangle Size:** Encourages larger triangles in triangle splatting:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_s = 2 \| (v_1 - v_0) \times (v_2 - v_0) \|^{-1} $$
        [S3].

*   **Other Regularizers:**
    *   **Opacity Loss ($\mathcal{L}_0$):** [S3].
    *   **Distortion Loss ($\mathcal{L}_d$):** [S3].
    *   **Normal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_n$):** [S3].
    *   **Point Color Supervision ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}}$):** Directly supervises sampled point colors [S2].
    *   **Background Entropy ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}$):** Encourages density concentration on foreground/background [S2].
    *   **Flow Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}$):** L1 norm of motion vectors in canonical space [S2].
    *   **Inpaint Diff ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{vdiff}}$):** L1 norm of difference between inpainted and upsampled grids [S2].
    *   **Roughness Range Loss:** Encourages roughness $\alpha < 0.5$ [S6].

### Parameters
*   **$\lambda_{\text{TV, SH}}$:** TV weight for SH coefficients. Typical: $1 \times 10^{-2}$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{TV, } \sigma}$:** TV weight for opacity. Typical: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{smooth}}$:** Smoothness weight. Typical: $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_D$:** D-SSIM weight. Typical: $0.2$ [S7].
*   **$\lambda_s, \lambda_o$:** Volume and opacity regularization weights. Typical: $0.01$ [S7].
*   **$\lambda_1, \lambda_2$:** Weights for Eikonal and Roughness losses [S6].
*   **$w_1 \dots w_7$:** Balancing weights for dynamic scene components [S2].
*   **$\beta_1 \dots \beta_4$:** Weights for opacity, distortion, normal, and size regularization [S3].
*   **$T_s, T_r$:** Thresholds for scaling regularization [S5, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_composite_loss(rendered_img, gt_img, scene_params, config):
    """
    Computes the total composite loss.
    rendered_img: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3) or (B, H, W, 3)
    gt_img: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3)
    scene_params: Dict containing relevant fields (sigma, sh, scales, opacity, sdf, etc.)
    config: Dict containing hyperparameters (lambda_tv, lambda_smooth, etc.)
    """
    loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Photometric Loss
    # Select variant based on config or scene type
    if config.get('use_perceptual'):
        # L2 + VGG Perceptual
        l_rgb = mse(rendered_img, gt_img) + perceptual_loss(rendered_img, gt_img)
    elif config.get('use_ssim'):
        # L2 on Pyramids + SSIM
        l_rgb = l2_pyramid(rendered_img, gt_img) + (1.0 - ssim(rendered_img, gt_img))
    elif config.get('use_ld_ss'):
        # L1 + D-SSIM
        l_rgb = l1_loss(rendered_img, gt_img) + config['lambda_d'] * d_ssir_loss(rendered_img, gt_img)
    else:
        # Standard MSE
        l_rgb = mse(rendered_img, gt_img)
        
    loss += l_rgb
    
    # 2. Regularization Terms (Conditional on scene representation)
    
    # Total Variation on Opacity/Features
    if 'sigma' in scene_params:
        tv_sigma = total_variation(scene_params['sigma'])
        loss += config['lambda_tv_sigma'] * tv_sigma
        
    if 'sh_coeffs' in scene_params:
        tv_sh = total_variation(scene_params['sh_coeffs'])
        loss += config['lambda_tv_sh'] * tv_sh
        
    # Smoothness for CRF (if applicable)
    if 'crf' in scene_params:
        crf_smooth = sum(second_derivative(crf) ** 2 for crf in scene_params['crf'])
        loss += config['lambda_smooth'] * crf_smooth
        
    # SDF Specific Regularizers
    if 'sdf' in scene_params:
        grad_sdf = gradient(scene_params['sdf'])
        eikonal = l2_norm(grad_sdf - 1.0)
        loss += config['lambda_1'] * eikonal
        
        # Roughness constraint (if material params exist)
        if 'roughness' in scene_params:
            roughness = scene_params['roughness']
            # Encourage roughness < 0.5 (specific formula not provided, assumed hinge or soft constraint)
            roughness_loss = max(0, roughness - 0.5) 
            loss += config['lambda_2'] * roughness_loss

    # Gaussian Specific Regularizers
    if 'gaussians' in scene_params:
        scales = scene_params['scales'] # Shape (N, 3)
        opacities = scene_params['opacity'] # Shape (N,)
        
        # Volume term (product of axes)
        vol_term = mean(product(scales, axis=1))
        loss += config['lambda_s'] * vol_term
        
        # Opacity term
        op_term = mean(opacities)
        loss += config['lambda_o'] * op_term
        
        # Scaling regularization (prevent long/thin)
        max_scales = max(scales, axis=1)
        min_scales = min(scales, axis=1)
        scaling_mask = max_scales > max(config['Ts'], config['Tr'] * min_scales)
        scaling_loss = mean(max_scales * scaling_mask)
        loss += config['lambda_scaling'] * scaling_loss

    # Dynamic Scene Regularizers
    if 'flow' in scene_params:
        flow_loss = l1_norm(scene_params['flow'])
        loss += config['w_flow'] * flow_loss
        
    if 'depth' in scene_params:
        depth_tv = total_variation(scene_params['depth'])
        loss += config['w_7'] * depth_tv

    return loss

def total_variation(grid):
    """Computes L1 total variation over a 3D grid."""
    # Differences along x, y, z axes
    dx = grid[1:, :, :] - grid[:-1, :, :]
    dy = grid[:, 1:, :] - grid[:, :-1, :]
    dz = grid[:, :, 1:] - grid[:, :, :-1]
    # Sum of absolute differences (or sqrt of sum of squares for L2-TV)
    # Note: Source [S1] uses sqrt(sum of squares) which is L2-TV magnitude
    return mean(sqrt(dx**2 + dy**2 + dz**2 + epsilon))
```

## Subtopics

### [MPF Training Objective](10247-mpf-training-objective.md)
The MPF Training Objective is a composite loss function designed to optimize the parameters of a Metric-Phase Field (MPF) network for multi-view image reconstruction. It combines standard photometric supervision with geometric regularizers that exploit rendering weights as soft indicators for near-surface and free-space regions, addressing the lack of ground-truth 3D geometry in this setting. Unlike the original MPF formulation which relies on explicit point cloud supervision, this method adapts the metric-phase decomposition constraints to be implicit and differentiable through the rendering process.

### Scaling and Background Mixing Regularization
This variant augments the photometric loss with a scaling regularization term to prevent Gaussians from growing long and thin, which is critical for monocular video reconstruction with limited viewing angles [S5, unofficial]. It also employs background mixing, where a random background color is generated every iteration to mix with the rendered image and ground truth to provide silhouette cues [S5, unofficial]. The scaling loss is applied only when the max scaling is both long (larger than $T_s$) and thin (larger than $T_r$ times the min scaling) [S5, unofficial].

### Inverse Rendering Loss Function
This variant is used to optimize neural SDF and material parameters from multi-view photometric images. It combines image fidelity (L2 on Gaussian pyramids + SSIM), SDF validity (Eikonal loss), and material constraints (Roughness Range Loss) [S6]. It differs from standard volume rendering losses by explicitly enforcing the gradient magnitude of the SDF to be 1 and constraining material roughness [S6].

### Compactness-Regularized Objective
This variant augments the standard 3DGS photometric loss with two lightweight compactness terms: a volume term (product of scale axes) and an opacity term (L1 on opacity) [S7]. It replaces the L1 penalty on scale axes (used in 3D-GS-MCMC) with a volume term, which penalizes oversized primitives more sharply than their sum [S7]. The volume term discourages oversized Gaussians, and the opacity term drives redundant Gaussians below the prune threshold [S7].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of sampled rays $|\mathcal{R}|$ and the complexity of the regularizers. TV loss requires neighborhood access ($O(|\mathcal{V}|)$ for voxels). Perceptual loss adds VGG inference cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing gradients for all regularized parameters (opacity, SH, scales, SDF, etc.).
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes training in ill-posed scenarios (e.g., dynamic scenes, monocular inputs). Prevents artifacts like noise, floating geometry, and overly large primitives.
*   **Limitations:** Hyperparameter tuning ($\lambda$ values) is critical and scene-dependent. Over-regularization can blur details or suppress valid high-frequency features.
*   **When to use:** When photometric loss alone leads to unstable optimization, noisy reconstructions, or physically implausible structures. Essential for dynamic scenes, inverse rendering, and compact representations.

## Background
The concept of combining photometric and regularization losses is fundamental to differentiable rendering. Early works like NeRF used simple L2 losses. Subsequent works introduced SSIM [S6], perceptual losses [S4], and various regularizers. Total variation regularization for radiance fields was popularized by RegNeRF [S2, citing RegNeRF]. Compactness regularization for 3DGS was introduced in 3D-GS-MCMC [S7, citing 3D-GS-MCMC] and refined in DART-3DGS [S7]. Inverse rendering losses combining SDF and material constraints were developed in IRON [S6].
