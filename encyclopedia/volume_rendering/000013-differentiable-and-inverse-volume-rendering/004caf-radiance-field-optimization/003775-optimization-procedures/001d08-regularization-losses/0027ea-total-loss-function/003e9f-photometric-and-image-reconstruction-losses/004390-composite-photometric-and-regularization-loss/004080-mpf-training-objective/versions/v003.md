# MPF Training Objective


## Overview
The MPF Training Objective is a composite loss function designed to optimize the parameters of a Metric-Phase Field (MPF) network for multi-view image reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. It combines standard photometric supervision with geometric regularizers that exploit rendering weights as soft indicators for near-surface and free-space regions, addressing the lack of ground-truth 3D geometry in this setting [S1, unofficial]. Unlike the original MPF formulation which relies on explicit point cloud supervision, this method adapts the metric-phase decomposition constraints to be implicit and differentiable through the rendering process [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of camera rays $\mathcal{R}$ sampled from training images; ground truth colors $C^*(\mathbf{r})$; optional foreground masks $A^*(\mathbf{r})$; the MPF network parameters (defining composite field $\varphi$, phase field $\theta$, and metric field $r$); the sharpness parameter $s$; and the phase scale $\beta$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A scalar total loss value $\mathcal{L}$ used for gradient-based optimization [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
*   **Rays:** Each ray $\mathbf{r}$ consists of an origin and direction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Samples:** Along each ray, $n$ samples are taken at positions $\mathbf{r}(t_i)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Fields:** The network outputs scalar values for $\varphi(\mathbf{x})$, $\theta(\mathbf{x})$, and $r(\mathbf{x})$ at any 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gradients:** Spatial gradients $\nabla \varphi$, $\nabla \theta$, and $\nabla r$ are computed via automatic differentiation [G].
*   **Rendering Weights:** $\{w_i\}$ are the standard volumetric rendering weights indicating the contribution of sample $i$ to the final color [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $\alpha_f = 50$: Sharpness parameter for the far loss barrier [S1, unofficial].
*   $T_{\text{warm}} = 50,000$: Number of warm-up iterations [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{\text{anchor}}, \lambda_{\text{surf}}, \lambda_{\text{align}}, \lambda_{\text{eik}}, \lambda_{\text{far}}, \lambda_{\text{mask}}$: Loss weights [S1, unofficial]. The source notes that these are chosen such that the four groups of terms contribute comparable gradient magnitudes [S1, unofficial]. Specific target values for $\lambda$ are not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].

**Initialization [G]**
1.  Initialize the phase branch $\theta$ (and thus the composite field $\varphi$) to approximate the signed distance of a coarse sphere bounding the scene [S1, unofficial].
2.  Initialize the metric branch $r$ as a nonnegative, distance-like field, e.g., proportional to $|\theta|$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  If coarse geometric priors (visual hull, SfM points, or depth) are available, initialize $\theta$ from the corresponding coarse implicit surface instead of a sphere [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Compute Rendering Weights:** For each ray $\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}$, compute the rendering weights $\{w_i\}_{i=1}^n$ using the standard volume rendering equation and the sharpness parameter $s$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Free-Space Indicators:** For each sample $i$ on ray $\mathbf{r}$, compute the free-space weight:
    $$ w_{\text{free}, i} = 1 - \exp(-100 |r(\mathbf{r}(t_i))|) $$
    This accounts for occluded surfaces by identifying regions where the metric field $r$ is large [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Photometric Loss:** Calculate the $\ell_1$ difference between rendered color $\widehat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth $C^*(\mathbf{r})$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \|\widehat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C^*(\mathbf{r})\|_1 $$
4.  **Compute Near-Surface Regularization:** Apply losses weighted by $w_i$ to samples near the surface [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Near-surface Eikonal Loss:** Encourages unit gradients for $\varphi$ and $\theta$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{surf}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}| n} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \left( (\|\nabla \varphi(\mathbf{r}(t_i))\|_2 - 1)^2 + (\|\nabla \theta(\mathbf{r}(t_i))\|_2 - 1)^2 \right) $$
    *   **Gradient Alignment Loss:** Aligns normalized gradients of $r$ and $\theta$ to prevent degenerate decompositions:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}| n} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \left( 1 - \langle \widehat{\nabla} r_i, \widehat{\nabla} \theta_i \rangle^2 \right) $$
        where $\widehat{\nabla} r_i = \frac{\nabla r(\mathbf{r}(t_i))}{\|\nabla r(\mathbf{r}(t_i))\|_2 + \epsilon}$ and $\widehat{\nabla} \theta_i$ is defined analogously [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Anchor Loss:** Encourages $\varphi$ and $r$ to be small at near-surface samples:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{anchor}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}| n} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \left( |\varphi(\mathbf{r}(t_i))| + r(\mathbf{r}(t_i)) \right) $$
5.  **Compute Free-Space Regularization:** Apply losses weighted by $w_{\text{free}, i}$ to samples away from the surface [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Free-Space Eikonal Loss:** Encourages $r$ to behave like an unsigned distance field:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}| n} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{i=1}^n w_{\text{free}, i} (\|\nabla r(\mathbf{r}(t_i))\|_2 - 1)^2 $$
    *   **Free-Space Far Loss:** Prevents large regions with near-zero UDF values:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{far}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \sum_{i=1}^n w_{\text{free}, i} \exp(-\alpha_f (r(\mathbf{r}(t_i)) + |\varphi(\mathbf{r}(t_i))|)) $$
6.  **Compute Optional Silhouette Loss:** If foreground masks $A^*(\mathbf{r})$ are available, compute the $\ell_1$ loss on accumulated opacity $\widehat{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i$:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \|\widehat{A}(\mathbf{r}) - A^*(\mathbf{r})\|_1 $$
7.  **Compute Total Loss:** Combine all terms:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + \lambda_{\text{anchor}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{anchor}} + \lambda_{\text{surf}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{surf}} + \lambda_{\text{align}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_{\text{eik}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} + \lambda_{\text{far}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{far}} + \lambda_{\text{mask}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} $$

**Pseudocode**
```python
import math

def mpf_training_loss(rays, gt_colors, gt_masks, network, s, beta, alpha_f=50, Twarm=50000, iteration=0):
    """
    Computes the MPF training loss.
    rays: list of Ray objects with origin and direction
    gt_colors: list of ground truth color vectors
    gt_masks: list of ground truth mask scalars or None
    network: object with query(x) -> (phi, theta, r)
    s: sharpness parameter, beta: phase scale
    alpha_f: far loss sharpness (default 50)
    Twarm: warm-up iterations (default 50000)
    iteration: current training step
    """
    n_samples = 128  # [G] Standard sampling count; exact value not specified in sources
    l_rgb = 0.0
    l_anchor = 0.0
    l_surf = 0.0
    l_align = 0.0
    l_eik = 0.0
    l_far = 0.0
    l_mask = 0.0

    for ray in rays:
        # [G] Standard ray sampling (e.g., uniform or stratified)
        positions = [ray.origin + ray.direction * t for t in sample_t_values(n_samples)]
        
        # Query network
        phi_vals = []
        theta_vals = []
        r_vals = []
        for pos in positions:
            phi, theta, r = network.query(pos)
            phi_vals.append(phi)
            theta_vals.append(theta)
            r_vals.append(r)
        
        # [G] Compute gradients via automatic differentiation
        grad_phi = compute_autodiff_gradient(phi_vals, positions)
        grad_theta = compute_autodiff_gradient(theta_vals, positions)
        grad_r = compute_autodiff_gradient(r_vals, positions)
        
        # [G] Compute rendering weights w_i using standard volume rendering equation
        weights = compute_rendering_weights(phi_vals, theta_vals, r_vals, s, beta)
        
        # Compute accumulated opacity for mask loss
        opacity = sum(weights)
        
        # Photometric loss
        rendered_color = volume_render_color(weights, positions, network, s, beta)
        l_rgb += abs(rendered_color - gt_colors[ray.idx])
        
        # Free-space indicators
        w_free = [1.0 - math.exp(-100.0 * abs(r_val)) for r_val in r_vals]
        
        # Near-surface regularization
        for i in range(n_samples):
            w = weights[i]
            # Stop gradients through w_i when used inside regularization losses [S1, unofficial]
            w_reg = w  # [G] In practice, detach w to stop gradients
            
            # L_surf
            norm_g_phi = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad_phi[i]))
            norm_g_theta = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad_theta[i]))
            l_surf += w_reg * ((norm_g_phi - 1.0)**2 + (norm_g_theta - 1.0)**2)
            
            # L_align
            norm_g_r = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad_r[i]))
            norm_g_theta_val = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad_theta[i]))
            eps = 1e-8
            grad_r_hat = [g / (norm_g_r + eps) for g in grad_r[i]]
            grad_theta_hat = [g / (norm_g_theta_val + eps) for g in grad_theta[i]]
            dot_prod = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(grad_r_hat, grad_theta_hat))
            l_align += w_reg * (1.0 - dot_prod**2)
            
            # L_anchor
            l_anchor += w_reg * (abs(phi_vals[i]) + r_vals[i])
            
        # Free-space regularization
        for i in range(n_samples):
            w_f = w_free[i]
            w_f_reg = w_f  # [G] In practice, detach w_f to stop gradients
            
            # L_eik
            norm_g_r = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad_r[i]))
            l_eik += w_f_reg * (norm_g_r - 1.0)**2
            
            # L_far
            l_far += w_f_reg * math.exp(-alpha_f * (r_vals[i] + abs(phi_vals[i])))
        
        # Optional silhouette loss
        if gt_masks is not None:
            l_mask += abs(opacity - gt_masks[ray.idx])

    # Normalize losses
    num_rays = len(rays)
    l_rgb /= num_rays
    l_surf /= (num_rays * n_samples)
    l_align /= (num_rays * n_samples)
    l_anchor /= (num_rays * n_samples)
    l_eik /= (num_rays * n_samples)
    l_far /= num_rays
    if gt_masks is not None:
        l_mask /= num_rays

    # Warm-up schedule
    lambda_align = 0.0
    lambda_far = 0.0
    if iteration >= Twarm:
        # Progressive ramp from 0 to target values [S1, unofficial]
        # [G] Exact ramp function and targets not specified in sources; set to 1.0
        lambda_align = 1.0
        lambda_far = 1.0

    lambda_anchor = 1.0
    lambda_surf = 1.0
    lambda_eik = 1.0
    lambda_mask = 1.0

    total_loss = (l_rgb + 
                  lambda_anchor * l_anchor + 
                  lambda_surf * l_surf + 
                  lambda_align * l_align + 
                  lambda_eik * l_eik + 
                  lambda_far * l_far + 
                  lambda_mask * l_mask)
    return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Trivial Solutions:** The network may reshape the weight distribution to reduce regularization penalties. Mitigation: Stop gradients through the rendering weights $w_i$ when they are used inside the regularization losses [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Decompositions:** Without alignment, the metric and phase fields may collapse or dominate each other. The gradient alignment loss prevents this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sign Ambiguity:** The squared inner product in $\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$ makes the constraint orientation-invariant, which is desirable for sign-ambiguous regions where a consistent global orientation may not exist [S1, unofficial].
*   **Surface Interference:** The far loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}$ is not applied exactly on the surface to prevent it from disrupting the actual surface reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides robust geometric regularization without requiring ground-truth 3D data [S1, unofficial]. The use of rendering weights as soft indicators allows the regularizers to adapt to the learned surface location dynamically [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption that rendering weights concentrate near the zero level set, which may be noisy early in training [S1, unofficial]. The warm-up schedule is critical for stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Multi-view image reconstruction where ground-truth 3D geometry is unavailable, and thin structures or sign-ambiguous geometry are present [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When explicit ground-truth point clouds are available, the original MPF formulation may be more direct [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The MPF Training Objective was introduced in the context of the "Volume Rendering with Metric--Phase Fields for Thin Structures and Sign-Ambiguous Geometry" paper [S1, unofficial]. It adapts the original MPF method, which was designed for surface reconstruction from known point clouds, to the more challenging setting of multi-view image reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. In the original MPF, near-surface and far-away points were easily separated using ground-truth point clouds, and losses like $\mathcal{L}_{\text{anchor}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{surf}}$ acted as explicit supervision [S1, unofficial]. In the multi-view setting, the surface location is dynamic and unknown, necessitating the use of rendering weights as soft selectors [S1, unofficial]. The method follows the standard NeuS training pipeline for optimization and ray sampling [S1, unofficial].
