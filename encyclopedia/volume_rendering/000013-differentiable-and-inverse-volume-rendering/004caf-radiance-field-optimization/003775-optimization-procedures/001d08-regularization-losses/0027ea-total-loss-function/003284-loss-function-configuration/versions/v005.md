# Loss Function Configuration

Also called: Loss Weighting Scheme, Training Loss Weights

## Overview
Loss Function Configuration specifies the scalar weights applied to individual loss components within the total objective function during radiance-field optimization [S1, S3]. It balances the influence of photometric reconstruction, perceptual metrics, geometric consistency, and regularization terms to stabilize training and improve convergence [G]. It differs from [Sparse Depth Consistency Loss](7294-sparse-depth-consistency-loss.md) and [Photometric and Image Reconstruction Losses](9789-photometric-and-image-reconstruction-losses.md) by focusing exclusively on the relative scaling factors rather than the mathematical formulation of the losses themselves [G]. It complements [Loss Weight Configuration](7883-loss-weight-configuration.md) by providing specific empirical values derived from experimental validation for dynamic and static scenes [G].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Training stage identifier (coarse/fine), scene motion magnitude (static/large), and the set of active loss components [S1, S3].
- **Outputs**: A configuration mapping loss keys to their respective scalar weights [G].

### Data Structures
Not given in the sources.

### Steps
1. **Initialize Base Weights**: Load default weights for the current training stage [S1, S3].
2. **Adjust for Scene Dynamics**: If the scene exhibits large motion, reduce the weight of deformation-related regularization terms [S1].
3. **Apply Stage-Specific Scaling**: Use lower weights for auxiliary losses in the fine stage to prioritize precise photometric alignment over global consistency [S1].
4. **Validate Configuration**: Ensure weights sum to a reasonable magnitude or are normalized if the loss components have vastly different scales [G].

### Formulas
For Neural Deformable Voxel Grid [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + w_{ptc} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{ptc} + w_{bg} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{bg} + w_{d\_norm} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_norm} + w_{d\_tv} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_tv} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ is the photometric loss, $\mathcal{L}_{ptc}$ is the point consistency loss, $\mathcal{L}_{bg}$ is the background loss, $\mathcal{L}_{d\_norm}$ is the deformation norm regularization, $\mathcal{L}_{d\_tv}$ is the total variation regularization on deformation, and $w_{ptc}, w_{bg}, w_{d\_norm}, w_{d\_tv}$ are configurable scalar weights [S1].

For SplatVoxel [S3]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{LPIPS}}(\hat{I}, I) $$
where $\hat{I}$ is the ground-truth target image, $I$ is the rendered target image, and $\lambda$ is the LPIPS weighting factor [S3].

### Parameters and Typical Values
**Neural Deformable Voxel Grid (Dynamic Scenes) [S1]:**
- **Coarse Stage**: $w_{ptc}=0.1$, $w_{bg}=0.01$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.1$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$ [S1].
- **Fine Stage**: $w_{ptc}=0.01$, $w_{bg}=0.001$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.01$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$ [S1].
- **Large Motion Adjustment** (e.g., 'Hell Warrior', 'Jumping Jacks', 'T-Rex'): $w_{d\_norm}$ is set to $0.01$ (coarse) and $0.001$ (fine) [S1].

**SplatVoxel (Static/Feed-Forward) [S3]:**
- **Multi-view Transformer**: $\lambda = 0.5$ [S3].
- **Voxel Transformer**: $\lambda = 4.0$ [S3].
- **Batch Size**: 128 [S3].
- **Total Iterations**: 300K (200K Stage 1, 100K Stage 2) [S3].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Large Motion Scenes**: Using standard deformation weights on scenes with large motion requires reducing $w_{d\_norm}$ to $0.01$ (coarse) and $0.001$ (fine) [S1].
- **Precision**: Weights are typically small floats ($<1$) for auxiliary terms to prevent them from dominating the photometric loss [G].
- **Stage Transitions**: Weights must change between coarse and fine stages; failing to reduce auxiliary weights in the fine stage can hinder convergence to high-frequency details [S1].

## Subtopics

### Hybrid Gradients
A gradient computation strategy that combines RGBXY derivatives and standard derivatives to leverage global matching in early stages and precise local alignment in late stages [S2]. It uses RGBXY derivatives (Eq. 15) for the first 75% of optimization iterations and switches to standard derivatives (Eq. 5) for the last 25% [S2]. This approach mitigates the inaccuracy of approximated optimal transport when initial and target shapes are already well-aligned [S2].

### Two-Stage Photometric and Perceptual Training Strategy
A training protocol for SplatVoxel feed-forward networks that uses photometric loss with perceptual weighting [S3]. Stage 1 trains the multi-view Transformer backbone for 200K iterations [S3]. Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes both multi-view and voxel Transformers for an additional 100K iterations [S3]. The LPIPS weight $\lambda$ is set to 0.5 for the multi-view transformer and 4.0 for the voxel transformer [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Configuration lookup is $O(1)$ [G]. Training convergence speed depends on weight selection; PSNR generally increases slightly after 20k iterations but requires significantly more iterations to reach maximum [S1].
- **Memory Complexity**: Negligible; only scalar weights are stored [G].
- **Performance**: Proper weighting impacts PSNR and training stability. For dynamic scenes, reducing $w_{d\_norm}$ is required for large motion sequences like 'Hell Warrior' [S1]. For feed-forward models, the LPIPS weight $\lambda$ is set to 4.0 for the voxel transformer and 0.5 for the multi-view transformer [S3].
- **Strengths**: Balances competing objectives through empirical tuning [G].
- **Limitations**: Requires manual adjustment per dataset and scene type; not automatically adaptive [G].

## Background
Specific configurations for dynamic view synthesis were detailed in the Neural Deformable Voxel Grid paper [S1], which introduced stage-specific weighting for deformation regularization. The hybrid gradient strategy was proposed in the context of RGBXY derivatives for optimal transport-based rendering [S2]. The two-stage perceptual training strategy was introduced for SplatVoxel to improve feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting [S3].
