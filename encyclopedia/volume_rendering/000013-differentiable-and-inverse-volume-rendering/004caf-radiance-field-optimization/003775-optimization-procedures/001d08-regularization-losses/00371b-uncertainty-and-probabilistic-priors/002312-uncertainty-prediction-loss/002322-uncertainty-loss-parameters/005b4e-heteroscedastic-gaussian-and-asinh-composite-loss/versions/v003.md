# Heteroscedastic Gaussian and Asinh Composite Loss

Also called: Joint Gaussian-Asinh Objective, Ltrain

## Overview
The Heteroscedastic Gaussian and Asinh Composite Loss is a training objective for neural tomography that combines a heteroscedastic Gaussian image loss with a fixed-scale asinh image loss to optimize density and temperature fields under observational noise [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation of pure Gaussian losses in physical field reconstruction by incorporating a task-aware asinh term, which yields lower log-field mean absolute error (MAE) than Gaussian-only objectives [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes a composite loss $L_{\text{train}}$ by weighting a Gaussian term $L_G$ and an asinh term $L_A$ [S1, unofficial]. The Gaussian term uses the known observation noise standard deviation to normalize residuals, while the asinh term uses a fixed characteristic intensity scale to normalize inputs before applying the inverse hyperbolic sine transformation [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
- $\theta$: Model parameters [S1, unofficial].
- $q$: Ray index [S1, unofficial].
- $c$: Spectral channel index [S1, unofficial].
- $\hat{I}_{\theta,q,c}$: Predicted intensity for ray $q$ and channel $c$ [S1, unofficial].
- $y_{q,c}$: Ground truth observation intensity for ray $q$ and channel $c$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\sigma_{q,c}$: Target-σ, the known per-pixel, per-channel observation noise standard deviation from the generative model [S1, unofficial].
- $s_c$: Channel-dependent fixed characteristic intensity scale [S1, unofficial].
- $\lambda_G$: Weight for the Gaussian loss term [S1, unofficial].
- $\lambda_A$: Weight for the asinh loss term [S1, unofficial].
- $\eta$: Noise multiplier used for calibrating $s_c$ during training [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- $L_{\text{train}}$: The scalar composite loss value to be minimized [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- Intensity values $\hat{I}$ and $y$ are floating-point numbers [G].
- $\sigma_{q,c}$ is a pre-computed tensor of noise standard deviations matching the shape of the observations [G].
- $s_c$ is a scalar or vector of scales, one per channel [G].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. Compute per-pixel, per-channel predicted intensity $\hat{I}_{\theta,q,c}$ and ground truth observation $y_{q,c}$ [S1, unofficial].
2. Calculate the Gaussian loss term $L_G$ as the squared difference normalized by $\sigma_{q,c}^2$ [S1, unofficial].
3. Calculate the asinh loss term $L_A$ as the absolute difference of asinh-transformed normalized intensities [S1, unofficial].
4. Combine losses using weights: $L_{\text{train}} = \lambda_G L_G + \lambda_A L_A$ [S1, unofficial].
5. Optimize $\theta$ via gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The composite loss is defined as:
$$ L_{\text{train}} = \lambda_G L_G + \lambda_A L_A $$
[S1, unofficial].

The Gaussian loss term $L_G$ is the inverse-variance-weighted squared image residual:
$$ L_G = \left( \frac{\hat{I}_{\theta,q,c} - y_{q,c}}{\sigma_{q,c}} \right)^2 $$
[S1, unofficial]. This is equivalent to the Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) up to a positive constant factor and additive terms independent of $\theta$ [S1, unofficial].

The asinh loss term $L_A$ is the absolute difference of asinh-transformed normalized intensities:
$$ L_A = \left| \text{asinh}\left(\frac{\hat{I}_{\theta,q,c}}{s_c}\right) - \text{asinh}\left(\frac{y_{q,c}}{s_c}\right) \right| $$
[S1, unofficial]. In the "target-σ" oracle variant, $s_c$ is replaced by $\sigma_{q,c}$, but the standard method uses the fixed $s_c$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_G$: Typical values tested are $1, 10^{-1}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-5}, 0$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\lambda_A$: Fixed at $1$ in hybrid experiments; $0$ for pure Gaussian [S1, unofficial].
- $s_c$: For noise multipliers $\eta > 1$, $s_c$ is scaled by $\sqrt{\eta}$ [S1, unofficial]. For $\eta \in \{0, 1\}$, $s_c$ remains fixed at its baseline value [S1, unofficial]. The scale remains constant within a single training run [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def compute_heteroscedastic_asinh_loss(theta, rays, channels, sigma, ground_truth, s_base, lambda_G, lambda_A, eta):
    # Calibrate asinh scale based on noise multiplier [S1, unofficial]
    if eta > 1:
        s_c = s_base * math.sqrt(eta)
    else:
        s_c = s_base

    # Initialize loss accumulator [G]
    L_train = 0.0

    for q in rays:
        for c in channels:
            # Compute predicted intensity via volume rendering [G]
            I_hat = ...  # Implementation depends on parent volume rendering method
            y = ground_truth[q, c]
            sigma_qc = sigma[q, c]

            # Compute Gaussian loss term [S1, unofficial]
            L_G = ((I_hat - y) / sigma_qc) ** 2

            # Compute asinh loss term [S1, unofficial]
            L_A = abs(math.asinh(I_hat / s_c) - math.asinh(y / s_c))

            # Accumulate composite loss [S1, unofficial]
            L_train += lambda_G * L_G + lambda_A * L_A

    # Average or sum over batch before backpropagation [G]
    return L_train
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If $\sigma_{q,c} = 0$, the Gaussian loss term becomes undefined [G]. The sources define $\sigma_{q,c}$ as the known per-pixel, per-channel observation noise standard deviation from the generative model [S1, unofficial]. If $\sigma_{q,c}$ is zero (noise-free), the Gaussian term may need a small epsilon or the loss should default to MSE [G].
- **Sparse Views + High Noise:** The combination of sparse views (e.g., $V=20$) and high noise ($\eta=25$) leads to a failure mode in latent physical-field recovery, with density MAE increasing to 0.336 and temperature MAE to 0.380 [S1, unofficial].
- **Scale Calibration:** The asinh scale $s_c$ must be calibrated to the noise level $\eta$ for $\eta > 1$ to track the noise standard deviation [S1, unofficial]. Using a fixed $s_c$ without scaling for high noise may degrade performance [S1, unofficial].

### Subtopics
**Pure Gaussian Loss** uses only $L_G$ with $\lambda_A=0$ and performs worst among tested objectives [S1, unofficial]. Choose it only when task-aware robustness is unnecessary [G].
**Pure Asinh Loss** uses only $L_A$ with $\lambda_G=0$, yields the lowest log-field MAE, and is the preferred task-aware objective for reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
**Target-σ Asinh Loss** replaces $s_c$ with $\sigma_{q,c}$ in $L_A$ and is used only for diagnostic ablation, not standard training [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Training a single reconstruction takes approximately 3 GPU-hours on an 80 GB NVIDIA A100 [S1, unofficial]. A $K=10$ ensemble takes approximately 30 aggregate GPU-hours [S1, unofficial]. Rendering a 300-view set takes 10-100 minutes on a local NVIDIA RTX 4090, depending on resolution and integration step size [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** The multiresolution hash grid uses approximately 40M parameters (2M per hash table, 20 levels) [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** The pure asinh objective yields lower log-field MAE than the pure Gaussian objective [S1, unofficial]. Hybrid losses land between the two regimes [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** The method is robust to strong heteroscedastic observation noise ($\eta=25$) when sufficient angular coverage (300 views) is provided [S1, unofficial]. However, it is unstable under sparse views and high noise [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Robust to noise; better physical field reconstruction than Gaussian-only loss; outperforms the classical grid by about 4× [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Fails under sparse views + high noise; requires careful calibration of $s_c$ for different noise levels [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Heteroscedastic Gaussian and Asinh Composite Loss was introduced in the context of joint thermodynamic reconstruction and error localization in neural tomography [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address the limitations of standard Gaussian losses in reconstructing physical fields (density and temperature) from noisy observational data [S1, unofficial].
