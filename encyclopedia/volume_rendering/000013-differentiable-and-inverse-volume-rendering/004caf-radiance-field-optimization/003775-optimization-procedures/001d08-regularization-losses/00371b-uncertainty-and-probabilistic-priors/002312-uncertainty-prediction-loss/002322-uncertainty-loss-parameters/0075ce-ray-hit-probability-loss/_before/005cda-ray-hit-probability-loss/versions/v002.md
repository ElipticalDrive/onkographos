# Ray Hit Probability Loss

Also called: Binary Cross-Entropy Ray Hit Loss, Ray Hit Loss

## Overview
The Ray Hit Probability Loss supervises a DDF model's ray hit probability predictions against ground truth ray hits using binary cross-entropy [S1]. It focuses solely on the existence of an intersection rather than geometric depth, distinguishing it from distance-based losses like $\mathcal{L}_d$ [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   Input: The model's predicted ray hit probability $\sigma$ (scalar, typically from a sigmoid activation) [S1].
*   Input: The ground truth ray hit label $\sigma_{GT}$ (binary: 1 if the ray hits the object, 0 otherwise) [S1].
*   Output: The scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $\sigma$: A single floating-point value representing the probability of a hit [S1].
*   $\sigma_{GT}$: A single integer or boolean value representing the ground truth hit status [S1].

**Steps:**
1. Obtain the predicted hit probability $\sigma$ from the neural network's output layer [S1].
2. Determine the ground truth hit status $\sigma_{GT}$ for the specific ray (1 if the ray intersects the target geometry, 0 otherwise) [S1].
3. Compute the BCE loss using the formula above [S1].

**Formula:**
The loss is defined as the Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) between $\sigma$ and $\sigma_{GT}$ [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_\sigma = - \left[ \sigma_{GT} \log(\sigma) + (1 - \sigma_{GT}) \log(1 - \sigma) \right]
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$ is the loss, $\sigma$ is the predicted probability, and $\sigma_{GT}$ is the ground truth label [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma$: Must be clamped to a small epsilon range $[\epsilon, 1-\epsilon]$ during log computation to prevent numerical instability, where $\epsilon$ is typically $10^{-7}$ [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Stability:** Direct computation of $\log(0)$ results in NaN; clamping $\sigma$ is required [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The sources do not specify handling for partial hits, implying a hard binary classification [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def ray_hit_probability_loss(sigma: float, sigma_gt: int, epsilon: float = 1e-7) -> float:
    """
    Computes the Binary Cross-Entropy loss for ray hit prediction.
    
    Args:
        sigma: Predicted probability of ray hit (float in [0, 1]).
        sigma_gt: Ground truth ray hit label (int 0 or 1).
        epsilon: Small constant for numerical stability.
        
    Returns:
        The computed BCE loss.
    """
    sigma_clamped = max(epsilon, min(1.0 - epsilon, sigma))
    loss = -(sigma_gt * math.log(sigma_clamped) + 
             (1 - sigma_gt) * math.log(1.0 - sigma_clamped))
    return loss
```

## Subtopics

### NeuralODF
**Definition:** A method for predicting ray hit and DDF, restricted to input on the unit sphere for fair comparison [S1].
**Distinction:** Unlike general DDF models that predict directional distances everywhere, NeuralODF explicitly optimizes ray hit prediction as a distinct output [S1].
**Usage:** Choose this variant when comparing against baseline methods like DIST or IF-NET that also predict surface distance and hit status, ensuring consistent input domains (unit sphere) [S1]. Predicting ray hit from the final layer results in higher accuracy [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Time: Not given in the sources.
*   Memory: Not given in the sources.

**Measured Performance:**
*   The method achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.55 on the "Car" shape, compared to 0.60 for DeepSDF and 0.61 for DIST [S1].
*   Inference speed is 17 ms/iteration versus 282 ms/iteration for DeepSDF on the "Car" shape [S1].
*   Frame rendering speed (256×256) is 62 ms versus 132 ms for DeepSDF [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Predicting ray hit from the final layer results in higher accuracy [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate ground truth ray hit labels, which may be difficult to compute for complex scenes without explicit geometry [G].

**History:** It is used in the context of inverse rendering to predict directional distances (DDF) and ray hits, offering a faster alternative to traditional Signed Distance Function (SDF) models [S1]. The approach is compared against DIST and IF-NET [S1].
