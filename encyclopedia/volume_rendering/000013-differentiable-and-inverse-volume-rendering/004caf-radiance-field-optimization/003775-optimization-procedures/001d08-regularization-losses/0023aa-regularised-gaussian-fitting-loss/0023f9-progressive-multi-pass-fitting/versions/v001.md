# Progressive Multi-Pass Fitting

Also called: Progressive fitting, Residual fitting, Single-Pass and Multi-Pass Residual Fitting

## Overview
Progressive multi-pass fitting is a strategy for optimizing Gaussian splats where a nominal splat budget is distributed across sequential passes, with each pass fitting the residual left by frozen earlier passes [S1, unofficial]. It serves as an alternative to single-pass fitting within the Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss framework [3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md]. While single-pass fitting generally achieves higher training PSNR, progressive fitting is evaluated for its ability to improve structural similarity (SSIM) and manage error distribution, though it typically incurs higher wall time for marginal or negative gains in PSNR on most volumes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Input**: A 3D volume dataset, a nominal initial splat count ($N_{seed}$), a total iteration budget ($I_{total}$), and the number of passes ($P$).
- **Output**: A set of optimized Gaussian splats.

**Data Structures**
- **Splat State**: Each splat maintains position, covariance, opacity, and color attributes. During multi-pass fitting, the state of splats from previous passes is frozen (not updated) in subsequent passes.
- **Residual Volume**: Implicitly computed as the difference between the target volume intensity and the current rendered image from frozen splats.

**Parameters**
- $N_{seed} = 32,000$: Nominal initial splat count [S1, unofficial].
- $I_{total} = 10,000$: Total optimization steps [S1, unofficial].
- $P \in \{1, 2, \dots, 8\}$: Number of passes. Single-pass is $P=1$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Iteration Split**: Iterations are distributed equally across passes. For $P$ passes, iterations per pass $I_{pass} = I_{total} / P$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm**
1. **Initialization**: Generate $N_{seed}$ initial splats. The default seeder draws 60% of seeds from edges (using Sobel gradients with normalized magnitude > 0.1, sampled via gradient-weighted Poisson-disk sampling with 2-voxel exclusion radius) and 40% from a uniform grid [S1, unofficial].
2. **Loop Passes** ($p = 1$ to $P$):
   a. **Freeze Previous**: If $p > 1$, freeze the parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) of all splats optimized in passes $1$ to $p-1$.
   b. **Optimize**: Run optimization for $I_{pass}$ iterations. The loss function is the Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss [3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md], which combines L1 reconstruction loss with regularization terms for splat amplitude and extent. The optimization targets the residual error relative to the current frozen state.
   c. **Cull**: Apply post-fit culling to remove low-contribution splats [S1, unofficial].
3. **Finalize**: Return the union of all splats from all passes.

**Pseudocode**
```python
def progressive_multi_pass_fitting(volume, seed_count=32000, total_iterations=10000, num_passes=2):
    # Step 1: Initialization
    splats = initialize_splats(volume, seed_count)
    # Default seeder: 60% edge seeds (Sobel gradient > 0.1, Poisson-disk), 40% uniform grid
    
    iterations_per_pass = total_iterations // num_passes
    
    for p in range(1, num_passes + 1):
        # Step 2a: Freeze previous splats
        if p > 1:
            freeze_splats(splats[:get_splat_count_from_previous_pass(p-1)])
            
        # Step 2b: Optimize current pass
        # Only update parameters for splats introduced in this pass or not yet frozen
        # Note: In residual fitting, earlier splats are frozen, so only new or active splats are updated
        optimize_splats(splats, volume, iterations_per_pass, loss_function="regularised_gaussian_fitting_loss")
        
        # Step 2c: Cull
        splats = cull_splats(splats)
        
    return splats
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Precision**: The smallest PSNR margin (Fly brain neurons, 0.07 dB) is below the largest run-to-run spread (0.093 dB), indicating that differences between single-pass and 2-pass may be statistically insignificant for some volumes [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundaries**: Four or more passes trail single-pass on every volume, suggesting diminishing returns or overfitting with excessive passes [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Single-Pass Fitting
Optimizes all splats in one go. Yields higher training PSNR on most volumes. Choose when PSNR is the primary metric and wall time is comparable. [Single-Pass Fitting](link-to-single-pass.md)

### Multi-Pass Fitting
General term for $P > 1$. Used when SSIM is prioritized or when residual fitting helps in specific complex structures (e.g., Mouse heart nuclei). [Multi-Pass Fitting](link-to-multi-pass.md)

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Wall time for 1-pass vs 2-pass is comparable, with a ratio of 0.70–1.75× (median 1.07×) on an otherwise idle GPU [S1, unofficial]. Multi-pass overhead increases with $P$.
- **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance**:
  - **PSNR**: Single-pass wins on 15 of 17 volumes by 0.07–6.02 dB over 2-pass. 2-pass wins on Mouse heart nuclei (+0.40 dB) and Zebrafish embryo nuclei (+0.85 dB) [S1, unofficial].
  - **SSIM**: 2-pass yields higher SSIM on 12 of 17 volumes [S1, unofficial].
  - **Error**: Single-pass has lower maximum absolute error on 12 of 17 volumes [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling**: Four or more passes trail single-pass on every volume in PSNR [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Can improve SSIM and handle specific complex structures better than single-pass.
- **Limitations**: Generally lower PSNR than single-pass. Higher wall time for marginal gains.
- **When to Use**: When SSIM is more important than PSNR, or for specific volumes like Mouse heart nuclei.
- **When Not to Use**: When PSNR is the primary metric or when computational efficiency is critical.

## Background
Progressive multi-pass fitting was evaluated in the context of Luxar, a system for Gaussian splatting for microscopy and scalable interactive web visualization of multidimensional scientific data [S1, unofficial]. The method was compared against single-pass fitting across 17 volumes, including spinning-disk, confocal, light-sheet, and iSIM modalities [S1, unofficial]. The study found that while single-pass is generally superior in PSNR, multi-pass (specifically 2-pass) offers advantages in SSIM and specific structural fidelity.
