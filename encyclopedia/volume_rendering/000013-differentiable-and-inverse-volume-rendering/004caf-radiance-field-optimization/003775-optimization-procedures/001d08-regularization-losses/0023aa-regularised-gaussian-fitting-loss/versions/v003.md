# Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss

Also called: L1+Regularization Loss for Gaussians, Regularised Loss Function for Gaussian Fitting

## Overview
The Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss is a composite objective function used to optimize 3D Gaussian splats for volumetric data reconstruction. It augments the base L1 reconstruction error with regularization penalties on splat amplitude and spatial extent to prevent overfitting and enforce physical plausibility. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) by operating directly on the parametric attributes of the Gaussian primitives (amplitude and covariance diagonal) rather than on the rendered volume grid or field values.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of $K$ Gaussian splats. Each splat $k$ is defined by parameters including amplitude $a_k$ and a diagonal covariance matrix with entries $L_{k,ii}$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$ (where $D$ is the spatial dimension, typically 3).
- Target volumetric data.
- Hyperparameters: learning rate $lr$, amplitude regularization weight $\lambda_a$, extent regularization weight $\lambda_d$, and optional boundary weight $\lambda_b$.
- Constants: minimum covariance floor $\sigma_{min}$, maximum amplitude clamp $a_{max}$, truncation threshold $\tau$, and volume extent $S_i$ for each dimension.

**Outputs:**
- Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ used for gradient-based optimization.

**Data Structures:**
- Splat parameters are stored as tensors. The covariance diagonal entries $L_{k,ii}$ are typically stored as softplus-activated parameters to ensure positivity. The amplitude $a_k$ is subject to a clamp $a_{max}$.

**Steps:**
1. **Compute Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{recon}$):** Calculate the L1 loss between the volume rendered from the current Gaussian splats and the target volume.
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{recon} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{p} | V_{rendered}(p) - V_{target}(p) | $$
   where $N$ is the number of voxels or samples $p$.

2. **Compute Amplitude Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{amp}$):** Calculate the mean deviation of the splat amplitudes from a minimum threshold (or zero, depending on implementation specifics, but the source implies a mean over softplus-activated parameters for extent, while amplitude is clamped). The source specifies the penalty is on the softplus-activated parameters for the diagonal, but for amplitude, it is a mean over $K$ splats. The formula provided in the source groups amplitude and extent:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{amp} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} | a_k | $$
   *Note: The source formula (2) shows $|L_{k,ii} - \sigma_{min}|$ for extent and $|a_k|$ for amplitude. The amplitude term is a mean over $K$.*

3. **Compute Extent Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{ext}$):** Calculate the mean deviation of the diagonal covariance entries from the minimum floor $\sigma_{min}$. This is evaluated on the softplus-activated parameters before the amplitude clamp is applied.
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{ext} = \frac{1}{K \cdot D} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{D} | L_{k,ii} - \sigma_{min} | $$

4. **Compute Boundary Containment (Optional, $\mathcal{L}_{boundary}$):** If enabled, calculate the penalty for splats extending beyond the volume boundaries.
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{boundary} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{D} \left[ \max(0, r_{k,i} - \mu_{k,i})^2 + \max(0, r_{k,i} - (S_i - 1 - \mu_{k,i}))^2 \right] $$
   where:
   - $\mu_{k,i}$ is the center coordinate of splat $k$ along dimension $i$.
   - $S_i$ is the extent (size) of the volume along dimension $i$.
   - $r_{k,i}$ is the effective radius of splat $k$ along dimension $i$ at truncation threshold $\tau$:
     $$ r_{k,i} = \tau \sqrt{\sum_{j} L_{k,ij}} $$
     *Note: The source formula uses $\sqrt{\sum_j L_{k,ij}}$. In a diagonal covariance context, this sum is just the diagonal entry $L_{k,ii}$ if $j=i$, or potentially the trace if off-diagonals exist. Given the context of "diagonal covariance", $L_{k,ij}$ is non-zero only for $i=j$. Thus $r_{k,i} = \tau \sqrt{L_{k,ii}}$.*

5. **Combine Losses:**
   $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_a \mathcal{L}_{amp} + \lambda_d \mathcal{L}_{ext} + \lambda_b \mathcal{L}_{boundary} $$

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_a = 10^{-3}$ (derived from $0.1 \times lr$ with $lr=0.01$).
- $\lambda_d = 10^{-4}$ (derived from $0.01 \times lr$ with $lr=0.01$).
- $\lambda_b = 0$ (disabled in reported benchmarks).
- $lr = 0.01$.
- $\sigma_{min}$: Minimum floor for covariance softplus activation.
- $a_{max}$: Maximum clamp for amplitude.
- $\tau$: Truncation threshold for effective radius calculation.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def regularised_gaussian_fitting_loss(
    splats, # Object containing attributes: amplitudes (K,), cov_diagonals (K, D), centers (K, D)
    target_volume, # Tensor of shape (D1, D2, ..., DD)
    rendered_volume, # Tensor of shape (D1, D2, ..., DD)
    lr=0.01,
    sigma_min=1e-6, # Example value, not explicitly given but implied by "floor"
    tau=3.0, # Example value, not explicitly given but standard for Gaussian truncation
    volume_extents=None # Tuple of sizes (S1, S2, S3)
):
    # 1. L1 Reconstruction Loss
    l_recon = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered_volume - target_volume))
    
    K = splats.amplitudes.shape[0]
    D = splats.cov_diagonals.shape[1]
    
    # 2. Amplitude Regularization
    # Mean over K splats of absolute amplitude
    l_amp = torch.mean(torch.abs(splats.amplitudes))
    
    # 3. Extent Regularization
    # Mean over K splats and D dimensions of |L_ii - sigma_min|
    # Note: splats.cov_diagonals are the softplus-activated parameters
    l_ext = torch.mean(torch.abs(splats.cov_diagonals - sigma_min))
    
    # 4. Boundary Containment (Optional)
    # Disabled by default in benchmarks
    l_boundary = 0.0
    # if lambda_b > 0:
    #     for k in range(K):
    #         for i in range(D):
    #             mu_ki = splats.centers[k, i]
    #             Si = volume_extents[i]
    #             # Effective radius r_ki = tau * sqrt(L_kii)
    #             r_ki = tau * torch.sqrt(splats.cov_diagonals[k, i])
    #             
    #             term1 = torch.clamp(r_ki - mu_ki, min=0.0) ** 2
    #             term2 = torch.clamp(r_ki - (Si - 1 - mu_ki), min=0.0) ** 2
    #             l_boundary += term1 + term2
    
    # 5. Combine
    lambda_a = 0.1 * lr
    lambda_d = 0.01 * lr
    lambda_b = 0.0 # Disabled
    
    total_loss = l_recon + lambda_a * l_amp + lambda_d * l_ext + lambda_b * l_boundary
    
    return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** The softplus activation for covariance ensures positivity, but values close to $\sigma_{min}$ may cause gradient vanishing if not handled carefully.
- **Boundary Terms:** The boundary term is quadratic and can dominate if splats are far outside the volume. It is disabled in standard benchmarks.
- **Amplitude Clamp:** The regularization is evaluated *before* the amplitude clamp $a_{max}$ is applied, meaning the gradient flows through the unclamped value.

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Multi-Pass Fitting](3185-progressive-multi-pass-fitting.md)
A strategy where a nominal splat budget is distributed across sequential passes, fitting residuals left by frozen earlier passes. Evaluated as an alternative to single-pass fitting within this framework.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of splats $K$ and dimensions $D$ for the regularization terms. The reconstruction loss depends on the rendering cost (typically $O(K \cdot N)$ or optimized via rasterization).
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in $K$ and $D$ for storing splat parameters.
- **Performance:** L1 reconstruction loss outperforms MSE on held-out PSNR for 11 of 17 tested volumes (gains of +0.20 to +7.78 dB) and never trails by more than 0.28 dB. MSE is not the best training-PSNR loss for these regularised, early-stopped fits [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Prevents overfitting by penalizing large amplitudes and excessive spread. Simple to implement.
- **Limitations:** Requires tuning of $\lambda_a$ and $\lambda_d$. Does not enforce geometric smoothness directly (unlike Total Variation).

## Background
The method originates from the Luxar project, which applies Gaussian splatting to microscopy and scientific data visualization. It was developed to handle scalable interactive web visualization of multidimensional scientific data. The specific loss formulation was used for single-pass benchmarks in the Luxar paper, with ablations detailed in supplementary documents [S1, unofficial].
