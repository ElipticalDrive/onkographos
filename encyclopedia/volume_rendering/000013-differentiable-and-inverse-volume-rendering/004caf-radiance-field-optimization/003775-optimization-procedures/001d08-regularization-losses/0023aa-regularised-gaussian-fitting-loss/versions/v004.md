# Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss

Also called: L1+Regularization Loss for Gaussians, Regularised Loss Function for Gaussian Fitting

## Overview
The Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss is a composite objective function used to optimize Gaussian splat representations for volume rendering. It solves the problem of fitting a discrete set of Gaussian primitives to continuous volumetric data by combining a data fidelity term with structural regularization penalties. It is distinguished from standard photometric losses by the explicit inclusion of amplitude and extent regularization terms to control the physical properties of the splats, and it adopts L1 reconstruction loss over MSE to improve held-out PSNR performance in early-stopped optimization regimes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A target volume $V$, a set of $K$ Gaussian splats, each defined by center $\mu_k$, diagonal covariance parameters $L_{k,ii}$, and amplitude $a_k$. Hyperparameters: learning rate $lr$, regularization weights $\lambda_a, \lambda_d, \lambda_b$, minimum covariance floor $\sigma_{min}$, truncation threshold $\tau$, and volume extent $S_i$.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ and its gradients with respect to the splat parameters for use in an optimizer (e.g., Adam).

**Data Structures**
*   **Splat Parameters:** Stored as tensors. $L_{k,ii}$ are softplus-activated parameters representing the diagonal covariance entries. $a_k$ is the amplitude.
*   **Volume:** Discretized grid with extent $S_i$ along dimension $i$.

**Formulas**
The total loss is defined as:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_a \sum_{k=1}^{K} |a_k| + \lambda_d \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{D} |L_{k,ii} - \sigma_{min}|
$$
Where:
*   $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$ is the L1 reconstruction loss between the predicted volume (rendered from the splats) and the target volume.
*   The second term regularizes splat amplitude.
*   The third term regularizes the extent (diagonal covariance).
*   $D$ is the number of dimensions (typically 3).
*   $\sigma_{min}$ is a floor value for the covariance parameters.

**Optional Boundary Term**
An optional boundary-containment term can be added:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{boundary} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{i=1}^{D} \left[ \max(0, r_{k,i} - \mu_{k,i})^2 + \max(0, r_{k,i} - (S_i - 1 - \mu_{k,i}))^2 \right]
$$
Where $r_{k,i} = \tau \sqrt{\sum_j L_{k,ij}}$ is the effective radius of splat $k$ along dimension $i$ at truncation threshold $\tau$. This term is disabled ($\lambda_b = 0$) in standard benchmarks [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $\lambda_a = 0.1 \times lr$. With $lr = 0.01$, $\lambda_a = 10^{-3}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_d = 0.01 \times lr$. With $lr = 0.01$, $\lambda_d = 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_b = 0$ (disabled) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Multi-Pass Fitting](3185-progressive-multi-pass-fitting.md)
A strategy where a nominal splat budget is distributed across sequential passes, fitting residuals left by frozen earlier passes. Evaluated as an alternative to single-pass fitting within this framework.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of splats $K$ and the cost of volume rendering. The regularization terms are $O(K \cdot D)$, which is negligible compared to rendering.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(K \cdot D)$ to store splat parameters.
*   **Performance:** L1 reconstruction loss outperforms MSE on held-out PSNR for 11 of 17 tested volumes (by +0.20 to +7.78 dB) and never trails by more than 0.28 dB. MSE is not the best training-PSNR loss for these regularised, early-stopped fits [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Improves structural quality and PSNR. Prevents splat amplitudes and extents from becoming unbounded or degenerate.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of $\lambda_a$ and $\lambda_d$. The optional boundary term is not used in standard benchmarks, potentially allowing splats to extend outside the volume bounds if not constrained by other means.

## Background
The method originates from the Luxar project, which applies Gaussian splatting to microscopy and scientific data visualization. It was developed to handle scalable interactive web visualization of multidimensional scientific data. The specific loss formulation was used for single-pass benchmarks in the Luxar paper, with ablations and progressive fitting variants documented in supplementary materials [S1, unofficial].
