# Ambiguity-Aware Inverse Rendering Evaluation Metrics

Also called: Posterior Variance Metrics

## Overview
Ambiguity-Aware Inverse Rendering Evaluation Metrics quantify the uncertainty inherent in ill-posed inverse rendering problems by measuring the variance of reconstructions across multiple stochastic samples or input perturbations [S1]. Unlike standard fidelity metrics that assume a single deterministic ground truth, these metrics evaluate the stability of albedo/illumination estimates under varying conditions and the diversity of solutions when the input is fixed, providing a more robust assessment of reconstruction quality in ambiguous scenarios [S1].

## How it works
The method evaluates reconstruction quality by computing statistical moments (variance) of outputs generated under specific perturbations, alongside standard fidelity and naturalness metrics [S1].

**Inputs:**
- Input image(s) for the inverse rendering model [S1].
- Trained inverse rendering model capable of stochastic sampling [S1].
- Ground truth data (albedo, ORM, HDR environment maps) if available for fidelity metrics [S1, S3].
- Unconditional distribution of environment maps from the training data corpus for FID computation [S1].

**Outputs:**
- $\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$: Variance of albedo reconstruction across multiple runs with identical input [S1].
- $\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}$: Variance of albedo reconstruction under different illumination conditions [S1].
- FID: Fréchet Inception Distance score for illumination naturalness [S1].
- Standard fidelity metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM, L1, LPIPS) [S1, S3].

**Data Structures:**
- List of $K$ reconstructed albedo maps $\{A_1, A_2, \dots, A_K\}$ for identical input [S1].
- List of $M$ reconstructed albedo maps $\{B_1, B_2, \dots, B_M\}$ for varied illumination inputs [S1].
- List of reconstructed environment maps $\{E_i\}$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1. **Compute Sample Diversity ($\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$):**
   Run the inverse rendering model $K$ times (typically $K=5$) on the exact same input image to obtain albedo estimates $A_k$ [S1]. Compute the pixel-wise variance across these runs:
   $$
   \sigma^2_{\text{sample}}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K (A_k(\mathbf{p}) - \bar{A}(\mathbf{p}))^2
   $$
   where $\bar{A}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K A_k(\mathbf{p})$ is the mean albedo at pixel $\mathbf{p}$ [S1]. Report the mean $\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$ over all pixels [S1]. A high value indicates diversity [S1].

2. **Compute Illumination Invariance Variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}$):**
   Generate albedo estimates under different illumination conditions (e.g., varying lighting priors or inputs) [S1]. Let these be $B_m$. Compute the pixel-wise variance:
   $$
   \sigma^2_{\text{invar}}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M (B_m(\mathbf{p}) - \bar{B}(\mathbf{p}))^2
   $$
   where $\bar{B}(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M B_m(\mathbf{p})$ [S1]. A reasonably low value indicates stability, meaning the albedo estimate is robust to changes in illumination assumptions [S1].

3. **Compute Illumination Naturalness (FID):**
   Compare the distribution of reconstructed environment maps $\{E_i\}$ to the unconditional distribution of environment maps in the training data corpus [S1]. Compute the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) [S1]. FID scores may be relatively high due to distributional dissimilarity by construction and low sample counts, but correlate well with perceived naturalness [S1].

4. **Compute Fidelity Metrics:**
   - For albedo: Compute Mean Squared Error (MSE), PSNR, SSIM, and L1 against ground truth [S1, S3].
   - For ORM (Occclusion, Roughness, Metallicness): Compute PSNR and L1 [S3].
   - For environment maps: Compute PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS [S1].

5. **Interpretation:**
   - Do not blindly minimize $\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}$ or maximize $\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$ [S1]. A deterministic method converging to the same (potentially incorrect) albedo will have $\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}=0$ but is sub-optimal [S1].
   - $\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$ and $\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}$ should reflect the variance of the true posterior distribution, which is unknown [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
- Blindly optimizing for low $\sigma^2_{\text{invar}}$ or high $\sigma^2_{\text{sample}}$ without considering fidelity leads to sub-optimal solutions [S1].
- FID scores are inherently high for inverse rendering reconstructions due to distribution shifts; they should be used as relative indicators of naturalness rather than absolute thresholds [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_ambiguity_metrics(input_image, model, albedo_gt, env_gt, env_training_samples):
    # 1. Sample Diversity (σ²_sample)
    K = 5  # [S1]
    albedo_samples = [model(input_image) for _ in range(K)]
    mean_albedo = sum(albedo_samples) / K
    # Pixel-wise variance, then averaged over all pixels [S1]
    sigma2_sample = mean(mean((a - mean_albedo)**2 for a in albedo_samples) for pixel in albedo_samples[0].shape)

    # 2. Invariance Variance (σ²_invar)
    # Generate estimates under different illumination conditions [S1]
    # M is not given in the sources.
    albedo_invar = [model(input_image, lighting_condition) for lighting_condition in conditions]
    M = len(albedo_invar)
    mean_invar = sum(albedo_invar) / M
    # Pixel-wise variance, then averaged over all pixels [S1]
    sigma2_invar = mean(mean((b - mean_invar)**2 for b in albedo_invar) for pixel in albedo_invar[0].shape)

    mse_albedo = mean((albedo_gt - mean_albedo)**2)  # [S1]
    psnr_env = compute_psnr(env_gt, env_recon)  # [S1]
    ssim_env = compute_ssim(env_gt, env_recon)  # [S1]
    lpips_env = compute_lpips(env_gt, env_recon)  # [S1]

    # 4. Naturalness Metrics

    return sigma2_sample, sigma2_invar, fid, mse_albedo, psnr_env, ssim_env, lpips_env
```

## Subtopics

### Auxiliary Quantitative Evaluation of Lighting Estimation
An evaluation metric that inserts a diffuse or specular sphere into a scene using estimated lighting and measures the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the insertion result and the ground truth HDR panorama [S2]. It serves as an auxiliary result for spatially-varying lighting estimation [S2]. Pitfall: This metric ignores spatially-varying effects and cannot be used as a training loss because ground truth lighting for specific 3D locations of inserted objects is unavailable [S2].

### StableMaterial Evaluation Protocol
A standardized procedure to evaluate 2D material predictions by mitigating output variance and scale ambiguity [S3]. Steps: (1) Generate 10 separate estimates for albedo and ORM images from the model; (2) Average the 10 estimates to form a single prediction per image; (3) Rescale albedo predictions to match the ground truth albedo scale to account for scale ambiguity; (4) Compute PSNR, SSIM, and L1 for albedo; PSNR and L1 for ORM; (5) Compute final results as the mean metric value across all views and objects [S3]. Parameter: 10 stochastic estimates per image [S3]. Pitfall: Failing to rescale albedo to ground truth scale yields misleading results [S3]. Performance on BlenderVault: StableMaterialMV achieves PSNR 24.70, SSIM 0.907, ORM L1 0.018, ORM PSNR 26.34 [S3]. Comparison: Derender3D does not produce ORM (N/A); IIR and IID underperform on albedo/ORM metrics compared to StableMaterial variants [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- Time and memory complexity: Not given in the sources.
- Strengths: Provides a nuanced view of reconstruction quality by accounting for the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering; captures both stability and diversity [S1].
- Limitations: Computationally expensive due to multiple samples; interpretation of variance metrics requires care to avoid penalizing stable but incorrect solutions [S1].

## Background
The metrics were introduced in [S1] to evaluate reconstruction quality and naturalness in inverse rendering, specifically addressing the ill-posed nature of the problem and the stochastic nature of diffusion-based priors. The metrics were designed to measure albedo reconstruction accuracy, diversity across runs, and invariance under illumination changes [S1]. The StableMaterial protocol extends evaluation principles to 2D material prediction, addressing scale ambiguity and output variance in material estimation [S3].
