# Joint Optimization with Loss Functions

Also called: Joint Optimization of Surfel Parameters and HDR Environment Map

## Overview
This method defines a composite objective function for the joint optimization of 3D surface splatting (surfel) parameters and a learnable HDR environment map in inverse rendering tasks [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of unstable geometry and lighting estimation by combining photometric fidelity with geometric priors, specifically depth consolidation and normal consistency [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from sibling regularization methods by its explicit coupling of surfel primitives (positions, orientations, scales, material properties) with global lighting parameters, and its use of layer-wise depth consolidation to prevent multi-layer compositing artifacts [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$, ground-truth silhouette mask $\mathbf{M}^*$ (optional), surfel parameters (positions $\mathbf{c}$, quaternions $\mathbf{q}$, log-scales $\log \mathbf{s}$, albedo $\rho$, metallic $\mu$, roughness $\eta$), and environment map coefficients.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar total loss $\mathcal{L}$ and gradients for all learnable parameters.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Surfel Parameters:** Stored in contiguous arrays or tensors. Positions $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, quaternions $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (normalized), log-scales $\log \mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (for 2D splat extents), material properties $\rho, \mu, \eta \in \mathbb{R}$.
*   **Layer Data:** For each pixel $p$, a list of $K$ composited layers. Each layer $i$ stores compositing weight $w_i$, mean depth $z_i$, and depth variance $\sigma_i^2$.
*   **Neighbor Index:** A spatial data structure (e.g., KD-Tree or Grid) mapping surfel indices to their nearest neighbors $\mathcal{N}_i$ with compatible normal orientation. This structure is rebuilt periodically and cached [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The total loss is a weighted sum of four terms [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{sil}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{nc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}
$$

1.  **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$):** Combines L1 reconstruction and SSIM. For HDR targets, both $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and $\mathbf{I}^*$ are transformed to log-space and sRGB before loss computation to compress dynamic range [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{ssim}}) \|\hat{\mathbf{I}} - \mathbf{I}^*\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{ssim}} (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{I}}, \mathbf{I}^*))
    $$
    where $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$ is the weighting factor.

2.  **Silhouette Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$):** Supervises total accumulated coverage using soft IoU. It is disabled once adaptive density control begins [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} = \text{IoU}(1 - \bar{T}_{K+1}, \mathbf{M}^*)
    $$
    where $\bar{T}_{K+1}$ is the residual transmittance (silhouette mask) and $\mathbf{M}^*$ is the ground-truth mask.

3.  **Depth Consolidation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$):** Enforces geometric thinness by penalizing weight allocation across distant layers (inter-layer) and depth spread within layers (intra-layer) [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}})
    $$
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} = \sum_{i < j} w_i w_j |z_i - z_j|
    $$
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}} = \sum_i w_i^2 \sigma_i^2
    $$
    where sums run over $K$ layers at pixel $p$, $w_i$ is the compositing weight, $z_i$ is mean depth, and $\sigma_i^2$ is depth variance of layer $i$.

4.  **Normal Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}$):** Penalizes angular deviation between a surfel's geometric normal and its orientation-aware nearest neighbors in 3D [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}} = \frac{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij} (1 - \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}_j)}{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij}}
    $$
    where $\mathcal{N}_i$ are nearest neighbors with compatible normal orientation, $\mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{n}_j$ are normals, and $w_{ij} = \exp(-\|\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ are distance-dependent weights with $\sigma$ derived from local intersample spacing.

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$: SSIM weight. Not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{\text{sil}}$: Silhouette loss weight. Not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{\text{cons}}$: Consolidation loss weight. Not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{\text{nc}}$: Normal consistency loss weight. Not given in the sources.
*   $\sigma$: Bandwidth for normal consistency weight decay. Derived from local intersample spacing [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_joint_loss(surfels, env_map, gt_image, gt_mask, 
                       lambda_ssim, lambda_sil, lambda_cons, lambda_nc, 
                       use_silhouette, rebuild_knn_interval, current_step):
    """
    Computes the total joint optimization loss for 3D surface splatting.
    
    Args:
        surfels: Object containing positions (c), quaternions (q), log-scales, 
                 material properties (rho, mu, eta), and layer data (w, z, sigma2) per pixel.
        env_map: Learnable HDR environment map parameters.
        gt_image: Ground-truth image (HDR).
        gt_mask: Ground-truth silhouette mask (optional).
        lambda_ssim: Weight for SSIM component.
        lambda_sil: Weight for silhouette loss.
        lambda_cons: Weight for depth consolidation loss.
        lambda_nc: Weight for normal consistency loss.
        use_silhouette: Boolean flag to enable silhouette loss.
        rebuild_knn_interval: Frequency (in steps) to rebuild the KNN structure.
        current_step: Current optimization step index.
        
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar tensor.
    """
    
    # 1. Render Image
    # Render current surfels and env_map to get predicted image I_hat
    # Apply tone mapping and gamma correction to I_hat
    I_hat = render_and_tone_map(surfels, env_map)
    
    # 2. Photometric Loss
    # For HDR, transform both images to log-space and sRGB
    I_hat_log_srgb = transform_hdr_to_log_srgb(I_hat)
    I_gt_log_srgb = transform_hdr_to_log_srgb(gt_image)
    
    l1_loss = l1_norm(I_hat_log_srgb - I_gt_log_srgb)
    ssim_loss = 1.0 - structural_similarity(I_hat_log_srgb, I_gt_log_srgb)
    l_photo = (1.0 - lambda_ssim) * l1_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_loss
    
    total_loss = l_photo
    
    # 3. Silhouette Loss (Conditional)
    if use_silhouette and gt_mask is not None:
        # Get residual transmittance (silhouette mask) from rendering
        # T_bar_K_plus_1 is the accumulated transmittance after K layers
        T_residual = get_residual_transmittance(surfels)
        silhouette_pred = 1.0 - T_residual
        
        # Soft IoU loss
        l_sil = soft_iou_loss(silhouette_pred, gt_mask)
        total_loss += lambda_sil * l_sil
        
    # 4. Depth Consolidation Loss
    # Compute inter-layer and intra-layer penalties for all pixels
    l_inter = 0.0
    l_intra = 0.0
    pixel_count = 0
    
    for pixel in surfels.pixels:
        layers = pixel.layers # List of (w_i, z_i, sigma2_i)
        if len(layers) == 0:
            continue
            
        # Inter-layer: sum over all pairs i < j
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(layers)):
                w_i, z_i, _ = layers[i]
                w_j, z_j, _ = layers[j]
                l_inter += w_i * w_j * abs(z_i - z_j)
                
        # Intra-layer: sum over all layers
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            w_i, _, sigma2_i = layers[i]
            l_intra += (w_i ** 2) * sigma2_i
            
        pixel_count += 1
        
    if pixel_count > 0:
        l_cons = (l_inter + l_intra) / pixel_count
        total_loss += lambda_cons * l_cons
    else:
        l_cons = torch.tensor(0.0, device=surfels.positions.device)
        
    # 5. Normal Consistency Loss
    # Rebuild KNN structure periodically
    if current_step % rebuild_knn_interval == 0:
        rebuild_knn_structure(surfels)
        
    # Get neighbors for each surfel
    l_nc_num = 0.0
    l_nc_den = 0.0
    
    for surfel in surfels:
        neighbors = get_neighbors(surfel) # Returns list of neighbor surfels with compatible normals
        if not neighbors:
            continue
            
        for neighbor in neighbors:
            # Distance-dependent weight
            dist_sq = squared_distance(surfel.c, neighbor.c)
            sigma = get_local_spacing(surfel) # Derived from local intersample spacing
            w_ij = exp(-dist_sq / (2.0 * sigma ** 2))
            
            # Normal dot product
            n_i = get_normal(surfel)
            n_j = get_normal(neighbor)
            dot_product = dot(n_i, n_j)
            
            l_nc_num += w_ij * (1.0 - dot_product)
            l_nc_den += w_ij
            
    if l_nc_den > 0:
        l_nc = l_nc_num / l_nc_den
        total_loss += lambda_nc * l_nc
    else:
        l_nc = torch.tensor(0.0, device=surfels.positions.device)
        
    return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **HDR Gradient Dominance:** Without log-space/sRGB transformation, the brightest pixels dominate gradients. This is handled by the transformation step in $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Multi-layer Artifacts:** Without $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$, the optimizer may distribute surfels across spurious depth layers. The inter-layer term drives consolidation into a single surface layer [S1, unofficial].
*   **Oversmoothing:** Screen-space normal regularizers can oversmooth geometry. This method uses 3D geometric normals and KNN consistency to avoid this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Silhouette Loss Timing:** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$ should be disabled once adaptive density control begins, as the photometric loss and densification mechanism take over coverage enforcement [S1, unofficial].
*   **KNN Cost:** Computing nearest neighbors is expensive. The spatial data structure is rebuilt periodically and cached to amortize cost [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by rendering and KNN neighbor queries. KNN structure rebuild is periodic, not per-step.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing surfel parameters, layer data per pixel, and the KNN spatial structure.
*   **Strengths:** Joint optimization of geometry and lighting improves inverse rendering stability. Depth consolidation prevents multi-layer artifacts. Normal consistency enforces geometric smoothness in 3D.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of multiple loss weights ($\lambda$). Silhouette loss is only useful when masks are available and early in training.
*   **When to use:** Object-level inverse rendering where geometry and lighting need to be recovered simultaneously from multi-view images.
*   **When not to use:** When only novel view synthesis is needed without lighting estimation, or when computational resources are extremely limited and periodic KNN rebuilds are prohibitive.

## Background
This method originates from the 3DSS (3D Surface Splatting for Inverse Rendering) framework [S1, unofficial]. It builds on scattered-data approximation frameworks [Wendland 2004] and integrates concepts from depth consolidation [Barron et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024] and HDR training techniques [Munkberg et al. 2022]. It is used for object-level inverse rendering, assessing geometry reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and novel scene relighting.
