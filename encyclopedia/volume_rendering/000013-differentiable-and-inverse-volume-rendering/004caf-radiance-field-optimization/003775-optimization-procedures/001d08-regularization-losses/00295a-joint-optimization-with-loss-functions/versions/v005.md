# Joint Optimization with Loss Functions

Also called: Joint Optimization of Surfel Parameters and HDR Environment Map

## Overview
This method defines a composite loss function for the joint optimization of 3D scene parameters (specifically surfel primitives and environment lighting) in inverse rendering. It addresses the ambiguity of multi-layer compositing and geometric smoothness by combining photometric fidelity, silhouette coverage, depth consolidation, and normal consistency. It is distinguished from sibling regularization methods by its specific application to surfel-based inverse rendering, where it jointly optimizes geometric primitives (positions, normals, scales) and physical material properties (albedo, metallic, roughness) alongside a learnable HDR environment map.

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Rendered image $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ (after tone mapping and gamma correction).
*   Ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$.
*   Ground-truth foreground mask $\mathbf{M}^*$ (optional).
*   Surfel parameters: positions $\mathbf{c}$, tangents/quaternions $\mathbf{q}$, log-space scales, albedo $\rho$, metallic $\eta$, roughness $\xi$.
*   Depth statistics per layer $i$ at pixel $p$: compositing weight $w_i$, mean depth $z_i$, depth variance $\sigma_i^2$.
*   Surfel normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ and neighbor indices $\mathcal{N}_i$.
*   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$, $\lambda_{\text{sil}}$, $\lambda_{\text{cons}}$, $\lambda_{\text{nc}}$, and kernel width $\sigma$ for normal consistency.

**Outputs:**
*   Scalar total loss $\mathcal{L}$ to be minimized by an optimizer (e.g., Adam).

### Data Structures
*   **Surfel Attributes:** Each surfel $i$ stores position $\mathbf{c}_i$, normal $\mathbf{n}_i$, orientation $\mathbf{q}_i$, scales, and material properties.
*   **Layer Compositing Data:** For each pixel, a list of $K$ layers, each containing weight $w_i$, mean depth $z_i$, and depth variance $\sigma_i^2$.
*   **Spatial Index:** A spatial data structure (e.g., KD-tree or grid) mapping surfel indices to their nearest neighbors $\mathcal{N}_i$ with compatible normal orientation. This structure is rebuilt periodically and cached.

### Formulas
The total loss is a weighted sum of four components:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{sil}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{nc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}
$$

1.  **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$):** Combines L1 reconstruction and Structural Similarity (SSIM). For HDR targets, a log-space and sRGB transfer is applied to both $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and $\mathbf{I}^*$ before computation to compress dynamic range [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{ssim}}) \|\hat{\mathbf{I}} - \mathbf{I}^*\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{ssim}} (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{I}}, \mathbf{I}^*))
    $$

2.  **Silhouette Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$):** Supervises total accumulated coverage using soft Intersection-over-Union (IoU). It is disabled once adaptive density control begins [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} = \text{IoU}(1 - \bar{T}_{K+1}, \mathbf{M}^*)
    $$
    where $\bar{T}_{K+1}$ is the residual transmittance.

3.  **Depth Consolidation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$):** Penalizes spurious depth layers. It is the average over all pixels $\mathcal{P}$ of the sum of inter-layer and intra-layer terms [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}})
    $$
    *   **Inter-layer term:** Penalizes weight allocation across distant layers.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} = \sum_{i < j} w_i w_j |z_i - z_j|
        $$
    *   **Intra-layer term:** Penalizes depth spread within a single layer.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}} = \sum_i w_i^2 \sigma_i^2
        $$

4.  **Normal Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}$):** Penalizes angular deviation between a surfel's normal and its orientation-aware nearest neighbors in 3D [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}} = \frac{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij} (1 - \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}_j)}{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij}}
    $$
    where $w_{ij} = \exp(-\|\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ are distance-dependent weights, and $\sigma$ is derived from local intersample spacing.

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_joint_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, gt_mask, 
                       surfels, layers_per_pixel, 
                       lambda_ssim, lambda_sil, lambda_cons, lambda_nc,
                       use_mask=True, training_stage='early'):
    """
    Computes the total joint optimization loss for surfel inverse rendering.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), tone-mapped and gamma-corrected.
        gt_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), ground truth.
        gt_mask: Tensor of shape (H, W), binary or soft foreground mask.
        surfels: Object containing positions, normals, and other attributes.
        layers_per_pixel: List of lists, where layers_per_pixel[p] is a list of 
                          layer dicts for pixel p, each with keys 'w', 'z', 'sigma_sq'.
        lambda_ssim: Weight for SSIM term in photometric loss.
        lambda_sil: Weight for silhouette loss.
        lambda_cons: Weight for depth consolidation loss.
        lambda_nc: Weight for normal consistency loss.
        use_mask: Boolean, whether to compute silhouette loss.
        training_stage: String, 'early' or 'late'. Silhouette loss is disabled in 'late'.
        
    Returns:
        Total loss scalar.
    """
    # 1. Photometric Loss
    # Apply log-space and sRGB transfer if HDR training is required
    img_processed = apply_hdr_transfer(rendered_image)
    gt_processed = apply_hdr_transfer(gt_image)
    
    l1_term = torch.mean(torch.abs(img_processed - gt_processed))
    ssim_term = 1.0 - compute_ssim(img_processed, gt_processed)
    loss_photo = (1.0 - lambda_ssim) * l1_term + lambda_ssim * ssim_term
    
    total_loss = loss_photo
    
    # 2. Silhouette Loss
    if use_mask and training_stage == 'early':
        # Residual transmittance is 1 - accumulated coverage
        # Assuming layers_per_pixel provides accumulated coverage or we compute it
        # Here we assume a function to get residual transmittance from layers
        residual_transmittance = compute_residual_transmittance(layers_per_pixel)
        # Soft IoU between (1 - T) and Mask
        loss_sil = compute_soft_iou(1.0 - residual_transmittance, gt_mask)
        total_loss += lambda_sil * loss_sil
        
    # 3. Depth Consolidation Loss
    loss_cons_total = 0.0
    pixel_count = 0
    for p in range(num_pixels):
        layers = layers_per_pixel[p]
        if not layers:
            continue
            
        l_inter = 0.0
        l_intra = 0.0
        
        # Inter-layer: sum over all pairs i < j
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(layers)):
                w_i = layers[i]['w']
                w_j = layers[j]['w']
                z_i = layers[i]['z']
                z_j = layers[j]['z']
                l_inter += w_i * w_j * torch.abs(z_i - z_j)
                
        # Intra-layer: sum over all layers
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            w_i = layers[i]['w']
            sigma_sq_i = layers[i]['sigma_sq']
            l_intra += (w_i ** 2) * sigma_sq_i
            
        loss_cons_total += (l_inter + l_intra)
        pixel_count += 1
        
    if pixel_count > 0:
        loss_cons = loss_cons_total / pixel_count
        total_loss += lambda_cons * loss_cons
        
    # 4. Normal Consistency Loss
    # Requires spatial index of surfels
    loss_nc_total = 0.0
    weight_sum_total = 0.0
    
    # Iterate over surfels
    for i in range(num_surfels):
        neighbors = get_neighbors(surfels, i) # Returns list of neighbor indices j
        if not neighbors:
            continue
            
        n_i = surfels.normals[i]
        c_i = surfels.positions[i]
        
        numerator = 0.0
        denominator = 0.0
        
        for j in neighbors:
            n_j = surfels.normals[j]
            c_j = surfels.positions[j]
            
            # Distance-dependent weight
            dist_sq = torch.sum((c_i - c_j) ** 2)
            w_ij = torch.exp(-dist_sq / (2.0 * sigma_kernel ** 2))
            
            # Dot product
            dot_prod = torch.dot(n_i, n_j)
            # Clamp dot product to [-1, 1] for numerical stability
            dot_prod = torch.clamp(dot_prod, -1.0, 1.0)
            
            numerator += w_ij * (1.0 - dot_prod)
            denominator += w_ij
            
        if denominator > 0:
            loss_nc_total += numerator / denominator
            weight_sum_total += denominator # Not strictly needed for mean if normalized, but good for debugging
            
    if weight_sum_total > 0:
        loss_nc = loss_nc_total / num_surfels # Or normalized by valid surfels
        total_loss += lambda_nc * loss_nc
        
    return total_loss
```

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$: Weight for SSIM in photometric loss. Typical value: $0.2$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{\text{sil}}$: Weight for silhouette loss.
*   $\lambda_{\text{cons}}$: Weight for depth consolidation loss.
*   $\lambda_{\text{nc}}$: Weight for normal consistency loss.
*   $\sigma$: Kernel width for normal consistency distance weights, derived from local intersample spacing [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Silhouette Loss Timing:** The silhouette loss must be disabled once adaptive density control begins, as the photometric loss and densification mechanism take over coverage enforcement [S1, unofficial].
*   **HDR Gradient Dominance:** Without log-space and sRGB transfer, gradients may be dominated by the brightest pixels in HDR training targets [S1, unofficial].
*   **Spatial Index Cost:** The neighbor index computation for normal consistency is expensive. The spatial data structure must be rebuilt periodically and cached between updates to amortize the cost [S1, unofficial].
*   **Depth Ambiguity:** Without depth consolidation, multi-layer compositing allows the optimizer to distribute surfels across spurious depth layers [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The photometric and silhouette losses are $O(N)$ with respect to pixels. The depth consolidation loss is $O(N \cdot K^2)$ where $K$ is the number of layers per pixel. The normal consistency loss is $O(N_s \cdot K_{nn})$ where $N_s$ is the number of surfels and $K_{nn}$ is the number of neighbors, but amortized by spatial indexing.
*   **Strengths:** Enforces geometric thinness (consolidation) and smoothness (normal consistency) directly on surfel primitives, which is critical for inverse rendering stability. Jointly optimizes lighting and geometry.
*   **Limitations:** Computationally heavier than photometric-only losses due to depth and normal terms. Requires careful scheduling of loss terms (e.g., disabling silhouette loss later).
*   **When to use:** When optimizing surfel-based inverse rendering models where geometric accuracy and material/lighting separation are required.

## Background
This method originates from the **3DSS: 3D Surface Splatting for Inverse Rendering** paper [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the differentiable volume rendering equation and extends it to surfel-based representations. The depth consolidation term is inspired by [Barron et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024], but adapted to operate at the level of surface layers rather than samples. The normal consistency term regularizes geometric normals in 3D, avoiding the oversmoothing issues of screen-space normal regularizers [S1, unofficial].
