# Joint Optimization with Loss Functions

Also called: Joint Optimization of Surfel Parameters and HDR Environment Map

## Overview
This method defines a composite loss function for the joint optimization of 3D surface splat (surfel) parameters and a learnable HDR environment map in inverse rendering [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the ambiguity of multi-layer compositing and geometric smoothness by combining photometric reconstruction, silhouette supervision, depth consolidation, and normal consistency terms [S1, unofficial]. It is distinct from sibling regularization methods by explicitly coupling the optimization of material properties (albedo, metallic, roughness) and lighting (HDR map) with geometric parameters (positions, normals, scales) within a single differentiable framework [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$, ground-truth silhouette mask $\mathbf{M}^*$, surfel parameters (positions $\mathbf{c}$, normals $\mathbf{n}$, scales, albedo $\rho$, metallic $\rho_m$, roughness $\alpha$), and compositing weights $w_i$, mean depths $z_i$, and depth variances $\sigma_i^2$ for $K$ layers per pixel.
*   **Outputs:** Total loss scalar $\mathcal{L}$ and gradients for all surfel parameters and environment map coefficients.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Surfel Parameters:** Stored in contiguous arrays or structures. Positions $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, normals $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (unit vector), tangents/quaternions $\mathbf{q}$, log-space scales $(\log s_x, \log s_y)$, albedo $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^3$, metallic $\rho_m \in \mathbb{R}$, roughness $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$.
*   **Compositing Data:** For each pixel, an array of $K$ layers containing weights $w_i$, mean depths $z_i$, and variances $\sigma_i^2$.
*   **Spatial Index:** A KNN (K-Nearest Neighbors) data structure (e.g., KD-Tree or Ball Tree) mapping surfel indices to their nearest neighbors $\mathcal{N}_i$ with compatible normal orientations. This structure is rebuilt periodically and cached between optimization steps [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The total loss is a weighted sum:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{sil}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{nc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}
$$
where $\lambda_{\text{sil}}, \lambda_{\text{cons}}, \lambda_{\text{nc}}$ are scalar hyperparameters [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$):** Combines L1 and SSIM. For HDR targets, $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and $\mathbf{I}^*$ are transformed to log-space and sRGB before computation to prevent gradient dominance by bright pixels [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{ssim}}) \|\hat{\mathbf{I}} - \mathbf{I}^*\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{ssim}} (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{I}}, \mathbf{I}^*))
    $$
    where $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$ is the SSIM weighting factor [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Silhouette Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$):** Uses soft IoU on the residual transmittance $\bar{T}_{K+1}$ (coverage $1 - \bar{T}_{K+1}$) against the ground-truth mask $\mathbf{M}^*$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} = \text{IoU}(1 - \bar{T}_{K+1}, \mathbf{M}^*)
    $$
    This term is disabled once adaptive density control begins [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Depth Consolidation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$):** Enforces geometric thinness by penalizing spread across and within layers [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}})
    $$
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} = \sum_{i < j} w_i w_j |z_i - z_j|
    $$
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}} = \sum_i w_i^2 \sigma_i^2
    $$
    where $i, j$ index the $K$ layers at pixel $p$, $w_i$ is the compositing weight, $z_i$ is the mean depth, and $\sigma_i^2$ is the depth variance of layer $i$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Normal Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}$):** Penalizes angular deviation between a surfel's normal and its nearest neighbors' normals [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}} = \frac{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij} (1 - \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}_j)}{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij}}
    $$
    where $\mathcal{N}_i$ are nearest neighbors with compatible normal orientation, and $w_{ij} = \exp(-\|\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ are distance-dependent weights derived from local intersample spacing $\sigma$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_joint_loss(surfels, environment_map, gt_image, gt_mask, 
                       lambda_ssim, lambda_sil, lambda_cons, lambda_nc, 
                       training_step, adaptive_density_active):
    """
    Computes the total loss for joint optimization of surfels and HDR map.
    
    Args:
        surfels: Object containing positions (c), normals (n), scales, 
                 albedo (rho), metallic (rho_m), roughness (alpha).
        environment_map: Learnable HDR environment map parameters.
        gt_image: Ground-truth image (HDR).
        gt_mask: Ground-truth silhouette mask.
        lambda_ssim: Weight for SSIM term in photometric loss.
        lambda_sil, lambda_cons, lambda_nc: Scalar weights for respective losses.
        training_step: Current optimization step.
        adaptive_density_active: Boolean flag for adaptive density control.
        
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar tensor.
    """
    
    # 1. Render image from current surfels and environment map
    # This step involves differentiable volume/splat rendering
    rendered_image_raw = render(surfels, environment_map)
    
    # 2. Photometric Loss
    # Apply log-space and sRGB transfer for HDR targets
    rendered_image_log = log_srgb_transfer(rendered_image_raw)
    gt_image_log = log_srgb_transfer(gt_image)
    
    l1_loss = mean_absolute_error(rendered_image_log, gt_image_log)
    ssim_loss = 1 - structural_similarity_index(rendered_image_log, gt_image_log)
    
    loss_photo = (1 - lambda_ssim) * l1_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_loss
    
    # 3. Silhouette Loss
    # Compute residual transmittance (coverage) from compositing
    # Assume render() also returns compositing weights and transmittance
    coverage = 1 - residual_transmittance # 1 - T_{K+1}
    
    if not adaptive_density_active:
        loss_sil = soft_iou(coverage, gt_mask)
    else:
        loss_sil = 0.0
        
    # 4. Depth Consolidation Loss
    # Aggregate per-pixel inter and intra layer costs
    # Assume render() returns layer-wise weights (w), mean depths (z), 
    # and variances (sigma_sq) for K layers per pixel
    inter_costs = []
    intra_costs = []
    
    for pixel in pixels:
        layers = get_pixel_layers(pixel) # List of (w, z, sigma_sq) for K layers
        
        # Inter-layer: sum over all pairs i < j
        inter_cost = 0.0
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(layers)):
                w_i, z_i, _ = layers[i]
                w_j, z_j, _ = layers[j]
                inter_cost += w_i * w_j * abs(z_i - z_j)
        inter_costs.append(inter_cost)
        
        # Intra-layer: sum over all layers
        intra_cost = 0.0
        for i in range(len(layers)):
            w_i, _, sigma_sq_i = layers[i]
            intra_cost += (w_i ** 2) * sigma_sq_i
        intra_costs.append(intra_cost)
        
    loss_cons = mean(inter_costs) + mean(intra_costs)
    
    # 5. Normal Consistency Loss
    # Requires KNN structure of surfels
    # Assume surfels has a method to get neighbors and their normals
    total_weighted_angle_diff = 0.0
    total_weight_sum = 0.0
    
    for surfel_i in surfels:
        neighbors = get_knn_neighbors(surfel_i, compatible_normals=True)
        
        for surfel_j in neighbors:
            # Distance-dependent weight
            dist_sq = squared_distance(surfel_i.c, surfel_j.c)
            sigma_local = get_local_spacing_sigma() # Derived from intersample spacing
            w_ij = exp(-dist_sq / (2 * sigma_local ** 2))
            
            # Angular difference
            dot_product = dot(surfel_i.n, surfel_j.n)
            angle_diff = 1 - dot_product
            
            total_weighted_angle_diff += w_ij * angle_diff
            total_weight_sum += w_ij
            
    if total_weight_sum > 0:
        loss_nc = total_weighted_angle_diff / total_weight_sum
    else:
        loss_nc = 0.0
        
    # 6. Total Loss
    total_loss = loss_photo + \
                 lambda_sil * loss_sil + \
                 lambda_cons * loss_cons + \
                 lambda_nc * loss_nc
                 
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$: SSIM weighting factor. Value not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{\text{sil}}, \lambda_{\text{cons}}, \lambda_{\text{nc}}$: Scalar weights for silhouette, consolidation, and normal consistency losses. Values not given in the sources.
*   $\sigma$: Parameter for distance-dependent weights in normal consistency, derived from local intersample spacing. Value not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **HDR Gradient Dominance:** Without log-space and sRGB transfer, gradients may be dominated by the brightest pixels in HDR training targets [S1, unofficial].
*   **Silhouette Activation:** The silhouette loss must be disabled when adaptive density control begins, as the densification mechanism takes over coverage enforcement [S1, unofficial].
*   **KNN Cost:** The neighbor index computation is expensive. The spatial data structure must be rebuilt periodically and cached between updates to amortize cost [S1, unofficial].
*   **Normal Orientation:** Neighbors must share a compatible normal orientation to avoid penalizing correct geometric variations (e.g., concave corners) [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The photometric and silhouette losses are $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels. The depth consolidation loss is $O(N \cdot K^2)$ due to pairwise layer comparisons, where $K$ is the number of layers per pixel. The normal consistency loss is $O(N \cdot K_{nn})$ where $K_{nn}$ is the number of nearest neighbors, dominated by the KNN query cost.
*   **Memory:** Requires storing per-pixel compositing data (weights, depths, variances) for $K$ layers and the KNN spatial index for all surfels.
*   **Strengths:** Jointly optimizes geometry, material, and lighting, reducing ambiguity in inverse rendering. Depth consolidation prevents spurious multi-layer artifacts. Normal consistency enforces geometric smoothness in 3D space.
*   **Limitations:** High computational cost due to KNN queries and pairwise layer comparisons. Requires careful tuning of multiple loss weights.
*   **When to use:** Object-level inverse rendering where geometry, material, and lighting reconstruction are required simultaneously.
*   **When not to use:** Real-time applications due to optimization cost; scenes without reliable ground-truth masks or where adaptive density control is preferred for silhouette enforcement.

## Background
The method originates from the 3DSS (3D Surface Splatting for Inverse Rendering) framework [S1, unofficial]. It builds on scattered-data approximation frameworks [Wendland 2004] and integrates concepts from depth consolidation regularization inspired by [Barron et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024] [S1, unofficial]. It uses the Adam optimizer [Kingma and Ba 2014] for joint parameter updates [S1, unofficial].
