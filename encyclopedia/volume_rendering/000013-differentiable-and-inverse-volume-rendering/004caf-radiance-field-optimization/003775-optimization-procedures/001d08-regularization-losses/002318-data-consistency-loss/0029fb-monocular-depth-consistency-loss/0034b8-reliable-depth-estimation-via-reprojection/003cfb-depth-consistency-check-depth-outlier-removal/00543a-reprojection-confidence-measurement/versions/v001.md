# Reprojection Confidence Measurement

Also called: Warping-based confidence

## Overview
Reprojection Confidence Measurement is a method to measure depth accuracy and locate outliers by warping pixels from source images to target images and comparing similarities [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of wrong depth supervisions caused by moving objects, ill-posed views, and occlusions in street view scenarios [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Probabilistic Depth Testing](11488-probabilistic-depth-testing.md) by using deterministic geometric warping and multi-level similarity metrics (RGB, SSIM, VGG, flow) rather than probabilistic distributions.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Source image $I_s$, source depth map $D_s$, source camera parameters $P_s$, target image $I_t$, target depth map $D_t$, target camera parameters $P_t$.
- Output: Confidence maps $C_{rgb}$, $C_{ssim}$, $C_{vgg}$, $C_{depth}$, $C_{flow}$, and a combined confidence map $\hat{C}$.

**Data Structures:**
- Pixel coordinates: $X = (x, y)$.
- 3D point: $X_{3D} = (x, y, d)$.
- Camera parameters: $P$ (intrinsics and extrinsics).
- Threshold: $\tau$ for geometry consistency.
- Learnable weights: $\omega_i$ for each confidence metric $i \in \{rgb, ssim, vgg, depth, flow\}$.

**Steps:**

1. **Warping Operation**: Reproject pixels from the source view to the target view.
   For each pixel $X_s = (x_s, y_s)$ in the source image with depth $d_s \in D_s$:
   $$ X_t = \psi(\psi^{-1}(X_s, P_s), P_t) $$
   where $\psi$ maps 3D points to the camera plane and $\psi^{-1}$ maps 2D points to 3D points using the depth [S1, unofficial].
   Let $X_t = (x_t, y_t, d_t)$ be the projected coordinates in the target view, where $d_t$ is the depth derived from the source depth map.

2. **RGB Confidence**:
   Compute the warped RGB image $\hat{I}_s$ by sampling the target image $I_t$ at the projected coordinates:
   $$ \hat{I}_s = I_t(X_t) $$
   Calculate the confidence:
   $$ C_{rgb} = 1 - |I_s - \hat{I}_s| $$
   [S1, unofficial]

3. **SSIM Confidence**:
   Calculate the Structural Similarity Index between the source image and the warped image:
   $$ C_{ssim} = \text{SSIM}(I_s, \hat{I}_s) $$
   [S1, unofficial]

4. **VGG Confidence**:
   Extract features from the source image $F_s$ and the warped image $\hat{F}_s$ using a pre-trained VGG network:
   $$ \hat{F}_s = F_t(X_t) $$
   Calculate the confidence:
   $$ C_{vgg} = 1 - ||F_s - \hat{F}_s|| $$
   [S1, unofficial]

5. **Geometry Confidence (Depth)**:
   Compare the projected depth $d_t$ with the original target view's depth $\hat{d}_t = D_t(x_t, y_t)$:
   $$ C_{depth} = \gamma\left(\frac{|d_t - \hat{d}_t|}{d_s}\right) $$
   where $\gamma(x) = 0$ if $x \ge \tau$, else $1 - x/\tau$. $\tau$ is a threshold to identify outliers [S1, unofficial].

6. **Geometry Confidence (Flow)**:
   Compute pixel motions $f_{s \to t}$ from the source image to adjacent target views using an optical flow method [S1, unofficial].
   Calculate the flow consistency:
   $$ C_{flow} = \gamma\left(\frac{||\Delta_{x,y} - f_{s \to t}(x_s, y_s)||}{||\Delta_{x,y}||}\right) $$
   where $\Delta_{x,y} = (x_t - x_s, y_t - y_s)$ [S1, unofficial].

7. **Learnable Confidence Combination**:
   Compute the final robust confidence map by weighting the individual metrics:
   $$ \hat{C} = \sum_i \omega_i C_i $$
   subject to $\sum_i \omega_i = 1$. The weights $\omega_i$ are jointly optimized during training [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $\tau$: Threshold for outlier identification in geometry consistency. Value not given in the sources.
- $\omega_i$: Learnable weights for confidence metrics. Initialized and optimized during training.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Occlusions**: Warping may fail in occluded regions, leading to incorrect depth comparisons. The confidence metrics help identify these as outliers [S1, unofficial].
- **Moving Objects**: Dynamic objects cause inconsistencies between source and target views. The method is designed to handle this by locating outliers [S1, unofficial].
- **Ill-posed Views**: Views with insufficient overlap or large baselines may produce unreliable warping. The multi-level confidence (pixel, patch, feature) helps construct robust measurements [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Geometry Confidence Measurement
A method to measure geometry consistency of depths and flows across different views to identify outliers [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by explicitly checking depth consistency ($C_{depth}$) and flow consistency ($C_{flow}$) against geometric projections, complementing the appearance-based metrics (RGB, SSIM, VGG). It is chosen when geometric plausibility is critical for filtering outliers in dynamic scenes. Link: [Reprojection Confidence Measurement](9364-reprojection-confidence-measurement.md).

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Depends on the warping operation and the computation of SSIM and VGG features. VGG feature extraction is computationally expensive. Optical flow computation is also costly.
- **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing source and target images, depth maps, and intermediate feature maps.
- **Measured Performance**: Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling**: The method scales with the number of target views and the resolution of the images.
- **Strengths**: Robust to moving objects, ill-posed views, and occlusions by using multiple confidence metrics. Learnable weights allow adaptation to different scene characteristics.
- **Limitations**: Computationally intensive due to VGG and optical flow. Relies on accurate depth maps for warping.
- **When to use**: In street view scenarios with dynamic objects and occlusions where robust depth supervision is needed.
- **When not to use**: In static scenes with high-quality depth data where simpler consistency checks suffice.

## Background
Originated in S-NeRF: Neural Radiance Fields for Street Views [S1, unofficial]. The method was designed to address the challenge of wrong depth supervisions in street view neural radiance fields. It builds on the idea of using reprojection errors to identify outliers in depth maps.
