# MLP Output Regularization

Also called: Translation, Rotation, and AO Regularization

## Overview
MLP Output Regularization applies specific loss terms to the outputs of a neural network (typically a scene manipulator or deformation field) to enforce physical plausibility or stability [S1]. It solves the problem of unrealistic deformations or artifacts by constraining translation residuals, rotation residuals, and ambient occlusion factors [S1]. It differs from general model regularization by targeting the semantic meaning of the output vectors rather than the network weights directly [G].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** The raw output vectors from the MLP: translation residual $t_{mlp}$, rotation residual quaternion $q_{mlp}$, and ambient occlusion factor $s$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Scalar loss values $L_{trans}$, $L_{rot}$, and $L_s$ added to the total optimization objective [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $t_{mlp}$: A 3D vector representing translation residuals [S1].
*   $q_{mlp}$: A 4D vector representing a quaternion rotation residual [S1].
*   $q_{id}$: The identity quaternion [S1].
*   $s$: A scalar value representing the ambient occlusion factor [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. Extract $t_{mlp}$, $q_{mlp}$, and $s$ from the MLP forward pass [S1].
2. Compute the translation loss $L_{trans} = \|t_{mlp}\|_2^2$ [S1].
3. Compute the rotation loss $L_{rot} = \|q_{mlp} - q_{id}\|_1$ [S1].
4. Compute the ambient occlusion loss $L_s = \|s - 1\|_2^2$ [S1].
5. Sum the three terms into the total training objective [S1].

**Formulas:**
The regularization consists of three terms [S1]:
1. **Translation Regularization:** $L_{trans} = \|t_{mlp}\|_2^2$
2. **Rotation Regularization:** $L_{rot} = \|q_{mlp} - q_{id}\|_1$
3. **Ambient Occlusion (AO) Regularization:** $L_s = \|s - 1\|_2^2$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_output_regularization(t_mlp, q_mlp, s, q_id):
    # L_trans: L2 norm squared of translation residual
    l_trans = sum(t ** 2 for t in t_mlp)
    # L_rot: L1 norm of difference between rotation residual and identity
    l_rot = sum(abs(q - q_i) for q, q_i in zip(q_mlp, q_id))
    # L_s: L2 norm squared of difference between AO factor and 1
    l_s = sum((val - 1.0) ** 2 for val in s)
    # Total regularization loss
    return l_trans + l_rot + l_s
```

**Parameters:**
Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Quaternion Normalization:** Quaternions must be normalized before computing $L_{rot}$ to ensure they represent valid rotations [G].
*   **Identity Quaternion Convention:** The definition of $q_{id}$ depends on the quaternion convention (scalar-first vs. scalar-last) [G].
*   **AO Factor Bounds:** The term $L_s$ assumes $s$ should be near 1 [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Lipschitz MLP Regularization](17403-lipschitz-mlp-regularization.md)
A technique applied to the scene manipulator MLP to ensure that interpolated latent codes yield plausible, visually natural deformations by bounding the Lipschitz constant of the network. It sets itself apart from standard weight decay or gradient penalty methods by explicitly parameterizing the layer-wise Lipschitz bounds as trainable constants, allowing the network to adapt its smoothness during optimization.

### Explicit Density Regularization
A regularization term added to the training objective to remove floating artifacts in the volume by penalizing differences between the neural field output at a point and its offset neighbor [S2]. The procedure samples a set of random points from the volume, offsets them by a random vector $\omega$, feeds both sets through the MLP, and calculates the mean squared error between the outputs [S2]. The formula is $E_{DR}(N_F(x), \omega) = \|N_F(x) - N_F(x + \omega)\|_2^2$, where $N_F$ is the neural field output, $x$ is a sampled point, and $\omega$ is a random offset vector [S2]. Without this regularization, the learned occupancy field contains significant 'floater' artifacts due to overfitting to the limited number of pre-computed coordinate-occupancy pairs [S2].

### Domain Regularization
A technique to prevent networks from learning incorrect visibility due to floating-point precision issues when original geometry lands precisely on voxel boundaries [S3]. The method extrudes the voxel boundary outwards to define a slightly larger learning domain for the network, and during inference queries the original voxel boundary rather than the extended one [S3]. The domain extension amount is 0.5% of the original voxel extent [S3].

## Background
Originated in the context of differentiable volume rendering and neural radiance fields [S1, S2, S3].
