# DN-Splatter

Also called: DN-Splatter Regularization and Initialization, Depth and Normal Priors for Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
DN-Splatter is a method that enhances 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction quality in challenging indoor scenes by incorporating depth and normal priors [S1, S2]. It addresses the lack of geometric constraints in standard 3DGS, which often leads to poor geometry reconstruction and artifacts in textureless regions [S2]. Unlike its siblings, which may rely on SDF gradients or photometric stereo, DN-Splatter explicitly regularizes the optimization with sensor or monocular depth and normal cues, enabling direct mesh extraction via Poisson surface reconstruction [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Training images (RGB) and their camera poses.
*   Optional: Sensor depth maps (e.g., from LiDAR or structured light) or monocular depth/normal predictions from off-the-shelf networks (e.g., Omnidata, ZoeDepth) [S2].
*   Initial Gaussian points: Either sparse COLMAP SfM points or dense points back-projected from sensor depth [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives (means $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$, opacities $\alpha$, colors $c$).
*   Rendered RGB, depth, and normal maps.
*   Extracted triangular mesh [S2].

**Data Structures:**
The method extends the standard 3DGS primitive. Each Gaussian $i$ retains standard parameters: mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (parameterized by scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$), opacity $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color [S2].
*   **Depth Parameter:** The z-depth coordinate in view space, $d_i$, is derived from the Gaussian mean $\mu_i$ and the view transformation matrix. It is not a separate learnable parameter but a function of $\mu_i$ [S2].
*   **Normal Parameter:** The normal vector $\mathbf{n}_i$ is not a direct parameter. It is derived from the Gaussian's orientation. In the context of DN-Splatter, normals are extracted from the Gaussians to align with surface boundaries. The specific derivation of $\mathbf{n}_i$ from the Gaussian parameters (e.g., using the gradient of the implicit surface defined by the Gaussian or the principal axis of the covariance) is implied by the need to render normal maps [S2]. *Note: The source [S2] states "extract normal directions from Gaussians" but does not provide the explicit mathematical formula for $\mathbf{n}_i$ from $\Sigma_i$ or $\mu_i$. Standard practice in related works (like GSDF or 3DGSR) often uses the gradient of the SDF or the principal component of the covariance. For implementation, one must assume a derivation, e.g., $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of $\Sigma_i$ or the direction of the Gaussian's principal axis in view space [G].*

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize $N$ Gaussians.
    *   If sensor depth is available, back-project depth pixels to 3D to create $N$ points. The source recommends $N=1,000,000$ (1M) points for sensor depth initialization [S2].
    *   If using COLMAP, use sparse SfM points [S2].
    *   Set initial opacities and colors from the first frame or average color [S2].

2.  **Forward Rendering (CUDA Kernel):**
    *   Rasterize color, depth, and normals simultaneously in a single pass for efficiency [S2].
    *   For each pixel $(u,v)$, sort contributing Gaussians $i=1 \dots N$ by their z-depth $d_i$ in view space. *Note: 3DGS uses a global sort, not per-pixel sort, which is an approximation [S2].*
    *   Compute accumulated transmittance $T_i$ and opacity contribution $\alpha_i$ for each Gaussian [S2].
    *   **Rendered Depth ($\hat{D}_{uv}$):**
        $$ \hat{D}_{uv} = \sum_{i \in N} d_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_j) $$
        where $d_i$ is the z-depth of the $i$-th Gaussian in view space [S2].
    *   **Normalization:** Normalize $\hat{D}_{uv}$ by the final accumulated transmittance $T_{final} = \prod_{j=1}^{N} (1-\alpha_j)$ to handle pixels where $T_{final} \neq 1$ [S2].
        $$ \hat{D}_{uv}^{norm} = \frac{\hat{D}_{uv}}{1 - T_{final}} $$
    *   **Rendered Normal ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv}$):**
        $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv} = \sum_{i \in N} \mathbf{n}_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_j) $$
        where $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the normal vector of the $i$-th Gaussian [S2].

3.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$):** Standard MSE or L1 between rendered RGB and ground truth [S2].
    *   **Gradient-Aware Depth Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\hat{D}}$):**
        *   Compute the gradient magnitude of the current rendered RGB image, $|\nabla I_{uv}|$ [S2].
        *   Define a weight $w_{uv}$ that lowers the loss in high-gradient regions (edges):
            $$ w_{uv} = \exp(-k |\nabla I_{uv}|) $$
            where $k$ is a constant [G]. *Note: The source [S2] describes the concept ("lowered in regions with large image gradients") but does not provide the exact exponential formula. A standard implementation uses an exponential decay or inverse relationship [G].*
        *   Compute the depth error. The source specifies a **logarithmic penalty** for smoother reconstructions:
            $$ \mathcal{L}_{\hat{D}} = \sum_{uv} w_{uv} | \log(\hat{D}_{uv}^{norm}) - \log(D_{gt}) | $$
            where $D_{gt}$ is the ground truth sensor depth or monocular depth prediction [S2].
    *   **Normal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\hat{N}}$):**
        *   Compute cosine distance between rendered normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv}$ and target normal $\mathbf{n}_{gt}$ (from monocular network or sensor-derived normals):
            $$ \mathcal{L}_{\hat{N}} = \sum_{uv} (1 - \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{gt}) $$
            [S2].
    *   **Normal Smoothing Prior ($\mathcal{L}_{smooth}$):**
        *   Apply a smoothing prior on the rendered normal maps to align nearby Gaussians. The source cites Eq. (11) but does not provide the formula. Typically, this involves a Laplacian smoothing or neighbor consistency term on $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv}$ [S2]. *Note: The exact formula is not given in the sources [G].*
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_D \mathcal{L}_{\hat{D}} + \lambda_N \mathcal{L}_{\hat{N}} + \lambda_{smooth} \mathcal{L}_{smooth} $$
        where $\lambda_D, \lambda_N, \lambda_{smooth}$ are hyperparameters [S2].

4.  **Optimization:**
    *   Optimize Gaussian parameters using Adam or SGD for 30,000 iterations [S2].
    *   Hardware: Nvidia RTX 4090 GPU [S2].

5.  **Mesh Extraction:**
    *   Extract the mesh from the optimized Gaussian scene using Poisson Surface Reconstruction [S2].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def dn_splatter_forward(Gaussians, CameraPose, ImageWidth, ImageHeight):
    """
    Renders RGB, Depth, and Normal maps for a single view.
    Gaussians: List of Gaussian primitives with attributes:
        - mu: (N, 3) Mean in world space
        - cov: (N, 3, 3) Covariance matrix in world space
        - alpha: (N,) Opacity
        - sh: (N, K) Spherical Harmonic coefficients
    CameraPose: (4, 4) World-to-View matrix
    """
    # Transform Gaussians to view space
    # This involves rotating and translating mu, and transforming cov
    view_mu = transform_to_view(Gaussians.mu, CameraPose)
    view_cov = transform_covariance(Gaussians.cov, CameraPose)
    
    # Global sort of Gaussians by z-depth (approximation of per-pixel sort)
    sorted_indices = sort_by_z_depth(view_mu)
    
    # Initialize buffers
    rgb_map = zeros((ImageHeight, ImageHeight, 3))
    depth_map = zeros((ImageHeight, ImageHeight))
    normal_map = zeros((ImageHeight, ImageHeight, 3))
    transmittance_map = ones((ImageHeight, ImageHeight))
    
    # Rasterization loop (conceptually, implemented in CUDA)
    for i in sorted_indices:
        g = Gaussians[i]
        # Project Gaussian center to screen
        screen_pos = project(g.mu, CameraPose)
        
        # Compute 2D covariance in screen space
        cov_2d = compute_2d_cov(g.cov, CameraPose, screen_pos)
        
        # Determine bounding box for this Gaussian
        bbox = get_bounding_box(screen_pos, cov_2d)
        
        # Iterate over pixels in bbox
        for (u, v) in pixels_in_bbox:
            if not in_bounds(u, v): continue
            
            # Compute 2D Gaussian value at (u, v)
            dist = distance(screen_pos, (u, v))
            sigma = exp(-0.5 * dist.T @ inv(cov_2d) @ dist)
            
            # Alpha contribution
            alpha_contrib = g.alpha * sigma
            
            # Accumulate
            T_prev = transmittance_map[u, v]
            alpha_final = alpha_contrib * T_prev
            
            # Color
            color = evaluate_sh(g.sh, view_ray_direction)
            rgb_map[u, v] += color * alpha_final
            
            # Depth (z-depth in view space)
            d_i = view_mu[i, 2] # Z component in view space
            depth_map[u, v] += d_i * alpha_final
            
            # Normal
            # Derive normal from Gaussian orientation. 
            # Assumption: Normal is the principal axis of the covariance in view space.
            # Eigenvector corresponding to smallest eigenvalue of cov_2d or 3D cov.
            n_i = get_gaussian_normal(g, CameraPose) 
            normal_map[u, v] += n_i * alpha_final
            
            # Update transmittance
            transmittance_map[u, v] *= (1 - alpha_contrib)
            
    # Normalize Depth and Normal
    # Avoid division by zero
    valid_pixels = transmittance_map < 0.99 
    
    # Normalized Depth
    depth_map[valid_pixels] = depth_map[valid_pixels] / (1 - transmittance_map[valid_pixels])
    
    # Normalized Normal (already weighted by alpha, but needs normalization if we want unit vector)
    # The source formula sums n_i * alpha_i * product(1-alpha_j). 
    # This is effectively the weighted average. 
    # To get the final normal vector, we might need to normalize the result vector.
    normal_map = normalize(normal_map)
    
    return rgb_map, depth_map, normal_map

def compute_gradient_aware_depth_loss(rendered_rgb, rendered_depth, gt_depth):
    """
    Computes the DN-Splatter depth loss.
    """
    # Compute gradient magnitude of RGB image
    grad_rgb = compute_gradient_magnitude(rendered_rgb)
    
    # Weight: Lower loss in high-gradient regions (edges)
    # k is a hyperparameter, e.g., 1.0
    k = 1.0
    weight = exp(-k * grad_rgb)
    
    # Logarithmic depth error
    # Handle invalid depth values (e.g., 0 or infinity)
    valid_mask = (gt_depth > 0) & (rendered_depth > 0)
    
    log_diff = log(rendered_depth[valid_mask]) - log(gt_depth[valid_mask])
    depth_error = abs(log_diff)
    
    # Apply weight
    weighted_error = weight[valid_mask] * depth_error
    
    loss = sum(weighted_error) / sum(valid_mask)
    return loss

def compute_normal_loss(rendered_normal, gt_normal):
    """
    Computes cosine distance between rendered and ground truth normals.
    """
    # Normalize inputs to ensure unit length
    rendered_normal = normalize(rendered_normal)
    gt_normal = normalize(gt_normal)
    
    # Cosine similarity
    dot_product = sum(rendered_normal * gt_normal, axis=-1)
    # Clip to [-1, 1] for numerical stability
    dot_product = clip(dot_product, -1.0, 1.0)
    
    # Cosine distance: 1 - similarity
    loss = sum(1 - dot_product)
    return loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Training Iterations:** 30,000 [S2].
*   **Number of Gaussians:** 1,000,000 (1M) when using sensor depth initialization [S2].
*   **Depth Loss Penalty:** Logarithmic ($|\log(\hat{D}) - \log(D_{gt})|$) [S2].
*   **Hardware:** Nvidia RTX 4090 [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Global Sort Approximation:** 3DGS uses a single global sort for efficiency, not per-pixel sorting. This makes depth rendering an approximation. DN-Splatter handles this by normalizing depth estimates with the final accumulated transmittance $T_i$ to ensure correct depth values where $T_i \neq 1$ [S2].
*   **Sensor Depth Inaccuracy:** Sensor depths (e.g., from iPhones) suffer from low resolution and inaccuracies, particularly at object edges. DN-Splatter mitigates this by using a gradient-aware loss that lowers regularization at edges [S2].
*   **Monocular Depth Inferiority:** Monocular depth predictions and patch-based Pearson correlation losses perform worse than direct sensor depth supervision [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is reported as 17.9 minutes for 30k iterations on a Nvidia 4090 GPU for the MuSHRoom dataset [S2]. The simultaneous rasterization of color, depth, and normals improves inference and training speed compared to separate passes [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses 1M Gaussians for sensor depth initialization, which increases memory usage compared to sparse SfM initialization (~54.8K points) [S2].
*   **Performance:**
    *   On MuSHRoom (left test set): DN-Splatter achieves Abs Rel 0.0228, Sq Rel 0.0089, RMSE 0.1280, $\delta<1.25$ 0.9854, PSNR 24.58, SSIM 0.8558 [S2].
    *   On ScanNet++: DN-Splatter achieves C-L1 0.0940, NC 0.8316, F1 0.7658 [S2].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Significantly improves geometry reconstruction and mesh quality (normal-completeness and F-scores) compared to standard 3DGS [S2].
    *   Sensor depth initialization outperforms COLMAP SfM initialization [S2].
    *   Gradient-aware depth loss mitigates uncertainties at edges from raw depth captures [S2].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the availability of sensor depth or accurate monocular priors.
    *   Sensor depths are low-resolution and inaccurate at edges, though the method mitigates this [S2].
    *   Monocular depth supervision is inferior to sensor depth [S2].

## Background
DN-Splatter was introduced by Matias Turkulainen, Xuqian Ren, Iaroslav Melekhov, Otto Seiskari, Esa Rahtu, and Juho Kannala [S2]. It was published in WACV 2025 (arXiv:2403.17822v3, Nov 2024) [S2]. The method builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [S2] and addresses the challenges of high-fidelity 3D reconstruction of common indoor scenes for VR/AR applications, where standard 3DGS suffers from poor geometry reconstruction in textureless regions [S2]. It is evaluated on the MuSHRoom and ScanNet++ datasets [S2].
