# 3D Gaussian Splatting with Normal Information for Mesh Extraction

Also called: GS with Normal Regularization, SDF Gradient Normal Supervision

## Overview
This method enhances 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by incorporating normal supervision derived from the gradients of an estimated Signed Distance Function (SDF) to improve geometric accuracy and enable high-quality mesh extraction [S1]. While standard 3DGS relies solely on photometric losses, which often result in imprecise geometry and noisy normals, this approach uses monocular normal estimates to regularize the SDF gradients, facilitating the reconstruction of surface-aligned Gaussians suitable for Poisson meshing [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by explicitly linking the SDF gradient direction to monocular normal priors rather than relying on depth consistency or photometric stereo alone.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of calibrated camera poses and images obtained via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1].
*   A sparse point cloud from SfM for Gaussian initialization [S1].
*   Pretrained monocular normal estimation network (e.g., Omnidata or MiDaS) to generate target normal maps [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized 3D Gaussians (means $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$, opacities $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients) [S1].
*   A surface mesh extracted via Poisson reconstruction from the optimized Gaussians [S1].

### Data Structures
The scene is represented by a set of $N$ 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian $g$ is defined by:
*   **Mean ($\mu_g$):** 3D position vector $\mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Covariance ($\Sigma_g$):** $3 \times 3$ symmetric positive semi-definite matrix, parametrized by rotation $R_g$ and scaling $S_g$ such that $\Sigma_g = R_g S_g S_g^T R_g^T$ [S1].
*   **Opacity ($\alpha_g$):** Scalar in $[0, 1]$.
*   **Color:** Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients [S1].

The SDF is not stored as a grid but estimated implicitly per point $p$ during optimization [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians using the sparse SfM point cloud [S1].
2.  **Standard 3DGS Optimization:** Perform standard differentiable rendering optimization using tile-based rasterization and $\alpha$-blending to minimize photometric loss ($L_1$ and D-SSIM) [S1].
3.  **SDF Estimation:** For a point $p$ visible from a training viewpoint, estimate the SDF value $\hat{f}(p)$ as the difference between the depth of $p$ and the depth value in the rendered depth map at the projection of $p$ [S1].
4.  **Gradient Computation:** Compute the gradient of the estimated SDF, $\nabla \hat{f}_p$, at point $p$ [S1].
5.  **Normal Target Generation:** Obtain the target monocular normal $\mathbf{n}_p$ for the corresponding pixel using a pretrained network (e.g., Omnidata or MiDaS) [S1].
6.  **Projection and Rasterization:** Project the SDF gradient $\nabla \hat{f}_p$ into camera space using the world-to-view transform. Rasterize these gradients to obtain the value at the corresponding pixel position, denoted as $\nabla \hat{f}_p$ in pixel space [S1].
7.  **Normal Regularization Loss:** Calculate the cosine similarity between the rasterized SDF gradient and the monocular normal [S1]:
    $$ R(\hat{f}) = \sum_{p \in P} \frac{|\nabla \hat{f}_p \cdot \mathbf{n}_p|}{\|\nabla \hat{f}_p\| \|\mathbf{n}_p\|} $$
    where $P$ is the set of pixel points in the training images [S1].
8.  **Total Loss Optimization:** Minimize the total loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda_{DSSIM} \mathcal{L}_{DSSIM} + \lambda_r R(\hat{f}) $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_1$ is the L1 norm loss, $\mathcal{L}_{DSSIM}$ is the D-SSIM loss, and $\lambda_r$ is the weight for the normal regularization term [S1].
9.  **Entropy Regularization:** Apply entropy regularization for a short number of iterations to encourage opacities to become binary (0 or 1), avoiding semi-transparent Gaussians to better represent a surface [S1].
10. **Adaptive Density Control:** Interleave optimization with adaptive density control: add Gaussians to empty areas or areas where a single Gaussian covers large parts of the scene; prune transparent Gaussians with opacities close to 0 [S1].
11. **Mesh Extraction:** Sample points on the optimized Gaussians and run Poisson reconstruction to extract the mesh [S1].

### Formulas

**Gaussian Function:**
$$ G(x, y) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu)\right) $$
where $\mu$ is the mean and $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix [S1].

**Density Function:**
$$ d(p) = \sum_{g} \alpha_g \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (p - \mu_g)^T \Sigma_g^{-1} (p - \mu_g)\right) $$
where $d(p)$ is the density at location $p$, $\alpha_g$ is opacity, $\mu_g$ is mean, and $\Sigma_g$ is covariance of Gaussian $g$ [S1].

**Normal Regularization Term:**
$$ R(\hat{f}) = \sum_{p \in P} \frac{|\nabla \hat{f}_p \cdot \mathbf{n}_p|}{\|\nabla \hat{f}_p\| \|\mathbf{n}_p\|} $$
where $\nabla \hat{f}_p$ is the rasterized SDF gradient and $\mathbf{n}_p$ is the monocular normal [S1].

**Total Loss:**
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda_{DSSIM} \mathcal{L}_{DSSIM} + \lambda_r R(\hat{f}) $$
where $\mathcal{L}_1$ is L1 loss, $\mathcal{L}_{DSSIM}$ is D-SSIM loss, and $R(\hat{f})$ is the normal regularization term [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{DSSIM}$: Weight for the D-SSIM loss term, typically set to 0.2 [S1].
*   $\lambda_r$: Weight for the normal regularization term $R(\hat{f})$, tuned per scene, typically between 0.1 and 0.4 [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Semi-transparency:** Without entropy regularization, Gaussians may remain semi-transparent, leading to poor surface representation. The method explicitly applies entropy regularization for a short duration to force binary opacities [S1].
*   **Monocular Normal Accuracy:** The quality of the normal supervision depends on the pretrained network (Omnidata/MiDaS). Inaccurate monocular normals may lead to incorrect gradient alignment [S1].
*   **SDF Estimation:** The SDF is estimated based on depth differences. In regions with occlusions or large depth discontinuities, the SDF estimate may be noisy, affecting the gradient direction [S1].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Time:** The method adds computational overhead for SDF estimation, gradient computation, and projection into camera space during each optimization step. The rasterization of gradients is efficient as it leverages the existing tile-based rasterizer [S1].
*   **Memory:** Memory usage is similar to standard 3DGS, storing Gaussians and their parameters. The SDF is not stored explicitly, reducing memory overhead compared to grid-based SDF methods [S1].

### Measured Performance
*   **Datasets:** Evaluated on Mip-NeRF360, Tanks and Temples, and Deep-Blending [S1].
*   **Metrics:** Scores higher on photorealism metrics (PSNR, SSIM) compared to other mesh-extracting rendering methods (e.g., SuGaR, Plenoxels, INGP) without compromising mesh quality [S1].
    *   *Mip-NeRF360 Indoor:* PSNR 29.87, SSIM 0.921 (vs. SuGaR PSNR 29.43, SSIM 0.910) [S1].
    *   *Mip-NeRF360 Outdoor:* PSNR 24.61, SSIM 0.719 (vs. SuGaR PSNR 24.40, SSIM 0.699) [S1].

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:** Improves geometric accuracy and enables high-quality mesh extraction. Facilitates better rendering in regions with high curvature or fine detail. Real-time rendering capability is maintained due to efficient rasterization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of monocular normal estimation networks. May struggle in textureless regions where monocular normals are unreliable. Requires careful tuning of $\lambda_r$ per scene [S1].

### When to Use
*   Use when high-quality surface mesh extraction is required from 3DGS.
*   Use when photometric-only optimization leads to imprecise geometry.
*   Use in applications like video generation, animation, AR-VR, and gaming where surface representation is crucial [S1].

### When Not to Use
*   Use standard 3DGS if only novel-view synthesis is needed and mesh extraction is not required.
*   Use if computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of SDF gradient computation is prohibitive.

## Background

### Origin
The method was proposed by Meenakshi Krishnan, Liam Fowl, and Ramani Duraiswami from the University of Maryland, College Park, and Google, New York [S1]. It was published in January 2025 [S1].

### History and Development
The method builds upon the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper [1] and addresses its limitations in geometric reconstruction. It incorporates ideas from density function regularization [8] and adapts them for normal supervision. The use of monocular normal estimation networks (Omnidata, MiDaS) leverages recent advances in single-image normal estimation [S1].

### Applications
The method is designed for downstream applications in video generation, animation, AR-VR, and gaming, where high-quality surface meshes are essential [S1].
