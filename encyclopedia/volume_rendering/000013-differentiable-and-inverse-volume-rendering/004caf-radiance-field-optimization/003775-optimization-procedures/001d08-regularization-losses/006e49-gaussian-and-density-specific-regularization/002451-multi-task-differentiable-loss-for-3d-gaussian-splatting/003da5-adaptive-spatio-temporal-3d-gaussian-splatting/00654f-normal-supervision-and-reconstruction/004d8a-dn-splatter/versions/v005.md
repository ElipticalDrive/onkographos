# DN-Splatter

Also called: DN-Splatter Regularization and Initialization, Depth and Normal Priors for Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
DN-Splatter is a method that enhances 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction quality by incorporating depth and normal priors captured from common devices or predicted from general-purpose networks [S1, S2]. It addresses the challenge of achieving both photorealism and geometric accuracy in challenging, ill-posed, and textureless indoor scenes where standard photometric optimization often fails [S2]. Unlike standard 3DGS which lacks explicit geometric constraints, DN-Splatter regularizes the optimization with depth information, enforces local smoothness of nearby Gaussians, and utilizes off-the-shelf monocular networks to align Gaussians with true scene geometry [S2]. It enables direct mesh extraction from the optimized Gaussian representation, yielding more physically accurate reconstructions [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of input images (RGB), corresponding camera poses, and optionally sensor depth maps (e.g., from LiDAR or structured light) or monocular depth/normal predictions.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives (means $\mu$, covariances $\Sigma$, opacities $\alpha$, colors $c$) and a rendered normal map.

**Data Structures:**
The method extends the standard 3DGS primitive. While standard 3DGS does not explicitly store normals, DN-Splatter derives them.
*   **Gaussian Primitive:** Parameterized by mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (decomposed into scaling $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$), opacity $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, and color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S2].
*   **Implicit Normal:** The normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ for the $i$-th Gaussian is derived from the gradient of the Gaussian density or by assuming the Gaussian aligns with the surface normal estimated from the depth/normal priors. The rendering pipeline computes a rendered normal map $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv}$ by alpha-blending these normals [S2].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize the Gaussian scene using either sparse COLMAP SfM points or dense sensor depth readings back-projected into 3D [S2].
    *   Sensor depth initialization is preferred over COLMAP SfM as it enhances both mesh and novel-view synthesis metrics [S2].
    *   Typical initialization uses 1 million Gaussians when using sensor depth [S2].

2.  **Depth Rendering:**
    *   Render per-pixel z-depth estimates $\hat{D}$ using a discrete volume rendering approximation similar to color values [S2]:
        $$ \hat{D}_{uv} = \sum_{i \in N} d_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_j) $$
        where $d_i$ is the $i$-th Gaussian's z-depth coordinate in view space, $\alpha_i$ is its opacity, and the product term represents the transmittance of preceding Gaussians [S2].
    *   Since 3DGS uses a single global sort for efficiency rather than per-pixel sorting, this depth rendering is an approximation [S2].
    *   To handle pixels where accumulated transmittance $T_i \neq 1$, normalize depth estimates with the final accumulated transmittance per pixel to ensure correct depth values [S2].
    *   Rasterize color and depths simultaneously per pixel in a single CUDA kernel forward pass to improve inference and training speed [S2].

3.  **Normal Rendering:**
    *   Extract normal directions from Gaussians.
    *   Compute the rendered normal map $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv}$ for each pixel by alpha-blending the normals of contributing Gaussians in depth order [S2]:
        $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv} = \sum_{i}^{N} \mathbf{n}_{i} \alpha_{i} \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1-\alpha_{j}) $$
        where $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the normal of the $i$-th Gaussian [S2].

4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Gradient-Aware Depth Loss ($L_{\hat{D}}$):** Apply an adaptive depth regularization based on the current RGB image. The loss is lowered in regions with large image gradients (edges) to enforce regularization more on smoother texture-less regions [S2].
        *   Use a logarithmic penalty for the depth loss instead of linear or quadratic penalties to result in smoother reconstructions [S2].
        *   Formula (implied from context of "gradient-aware" and "logarithmic penalty"):
            $$ L_{\hat{D}} = \sum_{uv} w_{uv} \log(1 + |\hat{D}_{uv} - D_{target, uv}|) $$
            where $w_{uv}$ is a weight inversely proportional to the image gradient magnitude at $(u,v)$ [S2].
    *   **Normal Loss ($L_{\hat{N}}$):** Align Gaussians along the scene geometry using normal cues from monocular networks (e.g., Omnidata) or depth gradients [S2].
        *   Formula:
            $$ L_{\hat{N}} = \sum_{uv} (1 - \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{target, uv}) $$
            where $\mathbf{n}_{target, uv}$ is the target normal [S2].
    *   **Normal Smoothing Prior ($L_{smooth}$):** Add a smoothing prior on rendered normal maps to better align nearby Gaussians during optimization [S2].
        *   Formula:
            $$ L_{smooth} = \sum_{uv} \|\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{uv} - \text{mean}(\{\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{neighbor}\})\|^2 $$
            where the mean is taken over neighboring pixels [S2].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photometric}} + \lambda_{\text{depth}} L_{\hat{D}} + \lambda_{\text{normal}} L_{\hat{N}} + \lambda_{\text{smooth}} L_{smooth} $$
        where $\lambda$ are hyperparameters [S2].

5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Optimize the Gaussian scene parameters using the total loss [S2].
    *   Training iterations: 30k iterations for all reported experiments [S2].

6.  **Mesh Extraction:**
    *   Extract meshes directly from the optimized Gaussian scene using Poisson surface reconstruction [S2].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def dn_splatter_optimize(input_images, camera_poses, sensor_depths=None, monocular_normals=None):
    """
    Optimizes 3D Gaussians using DN-Splatter regularization.
    
    Args:
        input_images: List of RGB images.
        camera_poses: List of camera pose matrices.
        sensor_depths: Optional list of sensor depth maps.
        monocular_normals: Optional list of monocular normal maps.
        
    Returns:
        Optimized Gaussian primitives.
    """
    # 1. Initialization
    if sensor_depths is not None:
        # Back-project sensor depth to 3D points
        init_points = back_project_depth(sensor_depths, camera_poses)
        # Initialize 1M Gaussians at these points
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians(init_points, num_gaussians=1000000)
    else:
        # Fallback to COLMAP SfM points
        init_points = get_colmap_sfm_points()
        gaussians = initialize_gaussians(init_points)

    # 2. Training Loop
    for iteration in range(30000):
        # Sample a random view
        view_idx = random_sample(input_images)
        image = input_images[view_idx]
        pose = camera_poses[view_idx]
        
        # Render Color, Depth, and Normal
        # Rasterize color and depths simultaneously in a single CUDA kernel
        rendered_color, rendered_depth, rendered_normal = render_gaussians(gaussians, pose)
        
        # Get target depth and normal
        if sensor_depths is not None:
            target_depth = sensor_depths[view_idx]
            # Compute target normal from sensor depth or use monocular if available
            target_normal = compute_normal_from_depth(target_depth) if monocular_normals is None else monocular_normals[view_idx]
        else:
            # Use monocular predictions if no sensor depth
            target_depth = monocular_depth_predictions[view_idx]
            target_normal = monocular_normals[view_idx]

        # Compute Photometric Loss
        loss_rgb = mse_loss(rendered_color, image)
        
        # Compute Gradient-Aware Depth Loss
        # Calculate image gradient magnitude
        img_grad = compute_gradient_magnitude(image)
        # Weight inversely proportional to gradient (lower weight at edges)
        depth_weight = 1.0 / (1.0 + img_grad) 
        # Logarithmic penalty
        depth_diff = log(1.0 + abs(rendered_depth - target_depth))
        loss_depth = sum(depth_weight * depth_diff)
        
        # Compute Normal Loss
        # Ensure normals are normalized
        rendered_normal = normalize(rendered_normal)
        target_normal = normalize(target_normal)
        loss_normal = sum(1.0 - dot(rendered_normal, target_normal))
        
        # Compute Normal Smoothing Prior
        # Average normals of neighboring pixels
        smoothed_normal = average_neighbors(rendered_normal)
        loss_smooth = sum(squared_norm(rendered_normal - smoothed_normal))
        
        # Total Loss
        total_loss = loss_rgb + lambda_depth * loss_depth + lambda_normal * loss_normal + lambda_smooth * loss_smooth
        
        # Backpropagate and update Gaussians
        update_gaussians(gaussians, total_loss)

    return gaussians
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Logarithmic penalty:** Used for depth loss to ensure smoother reconstructions [S2].
*   **Number of initialized Gaussians:** 1M points when using sensor depth initialization [S2].
*   **Training iterations:** 30k iterations [S2].
*   **Hardware:** Nvidia 4090 GPU [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Approximation:** 3DGS uses a single global sort for efficiency, making depth rendering an approximation. This is handled by normalizing depth estimates with the final accumulated transmittance $T_i$ per pixel [S2].
*   **Sensor Depth Inaccuracies:** Sensor depths suffer from low resolution and inaccuracies, particularly at object edges. The gradient-aware loss mitigates this by lowering regularization at edges [S2].
*   **Textureless Regions:** Standard photometric regularization fails in textureless regions. Depth and normal priors are crucial here [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires 30k iterations. Rasterization is optimized by rendering color and depth simultaneously in a single CUDA kernel [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians. 1M Gaussians are used for sensor depth initialization [S2].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Depth Estimation (MuSHRoom left test set):** Abs Rel 0.0228, Sq Rel 0.0089, RMSE 0.1280, $\delta<1.25$ 0.9854, PSNR 24.58, SSIM 0.8558 [S2].
    *   **Mesh Quality (ScanNet++):** C-L1 0.0940, NC 0.8316, F1 0.7658 [S2].
*   **Scaling:** Sensor depth initialization outperforms COLMAP SfM initialization [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Improves photorealism and geometric accuracy in challenging indoor scenes; enables direct mesh extraction; robust to textureless regions [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of sensor depth or monocular predictions; sensor depths can be inaccurate at edges [S2].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity 3D reconstruction of indoor scenes is required, especially with textureless regions, and mesh extraction is desired [S2].
*   **When not to use:** If sensor depth is unavailable and monocular predictions are unreliable; if real-time rendering speed is critical and 30k iterations are too slow [S2].

## Background
*   **Origin:** DN-Splatter was introduced by Matias Turkulainen, Xuqian Ren, Iaroslav Melekhov, Otto Seiskari, Esa Rahtu, and Juho Kannala in the paper "DN-Splatter: Depth and Normal Priors for Gaussian Splatting and Meshing" [S2].
*   **History:** It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [19] and addresses its limitations in geometric reconstruction [S2].
*   **Usage:** Evaluated on MuSHRoom (6 scenes) and ScanNet++ datasets [S2].
