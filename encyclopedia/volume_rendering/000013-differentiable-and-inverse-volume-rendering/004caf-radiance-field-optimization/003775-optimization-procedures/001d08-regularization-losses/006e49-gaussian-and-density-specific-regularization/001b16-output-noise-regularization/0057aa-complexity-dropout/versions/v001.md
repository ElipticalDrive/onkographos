# Complexity Dropout


## Overview
Complexity Dropout is a training regularization technique that probabilistically excludes the complexity term from the importance score calculation during Gaussian primitive densification [S1, unofficial]. By occasionally ignoring geometric complexity, the method prevents the densification process from repeatedly targeting the same high-complexity regions, thereby allowing opacity sampling to cover remaining areas [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Smoothness Priors](11408-smoothness-priors.md) by directly manipulating the selection bias of the densification algorithm rather than penalizing attribute values, and from [Background Regularization](13413-background-regularization.md) by focusing on spatial distribution of primitives rather than background modeling.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Current set of Gaussian primitives, their importance scores (used for densification), and the dropout rate parameter $\delta$.
*   **Output:** Modified importance scores or a binary mask determining which primitives are eligible for densification in the current step.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Importance Score ($I_i$):** A scalar value for each primitive $i$, typically derived from gradient magnitude and/or accumulated opacity contribution.
*   **Dropout Mask ($M_i$):** A binary value for each primitive $i$, sampled from a Bernoulli distribution.

**Parameters:**
*   **$\delta$ (Dropout Rate):** The probability of excluding the complexity term.
    *   $\delta = 0$: Always applies the complexity term (no dropout).
    *   $\delta = 1$: Reduces the method to pure opacity sampling.
    *   **Default:** $\delta = 0.2$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm:**
1.  For each primitive $i$ in the scene:
    a. Calculate the base importance score $I_i$ based on gradient and opacity [S1, unofficial].
    b. Sample a random number $r$ from a uniform distribution $U[0, 1]$.
    c. If $r < \delta$:
        i. Set the effective importance score $I'_i = I_{opacity, i}$ (or simply exclude the complexity component from $I_i$). [G: In the context of KoalaGS, this implies the complexity term in the importance metric is zeroed out or ignored for this step].
    d. Else:
        i. Set the effective importance score $I'_i = I_i$ (complexity term included).
2.  Use $I'_i$ to select primitives for densification (splitting/copying).

**Pitfalls:**
*   Using $\delta = 0$ (no dropout) is not beneficial and performs worse than opacity sampling on Deep Blending (29.00 vs. 29.28 AUC PSNR) [S1, unofficial].
*   Large intermediate rates ($\delta \in \{0.6, 0.8\}$) perform worst on both tested datasets [S1, unofficial].
*   Without dropout, relocation repeatedly targets the same high-complexity regions, starving other areas of primitives [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Adds negligible overhead: one random number generation and one comparison per primitive per densification step.
*   No additional memory storage required beyond the primitive attributes.

**Measured Performance:**
*   **TandT Dataset [S1, unofficial]:** $\delta=0.2$ yields PSNR 24.65, SSIM 0.868, LPIPS 0.150.
*   **Deep Blending (DB) Dataset [S1, unofficial]:** $\delta=0.2$ yields PSNR 29.92, SSIM 0.911, LPIPS 0.240.
*   These results are the best or near-best compared to other $\delta$ values (0, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1) [S1, unofficial].
*   Training is more stable, especially on indoor scenes of Deep Blending [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Prevents over-concentration of primitives in complex regions; improves coverage of sparse or under-represented areas; robust default performance across datasets.
*   **Limitations:** Requires tuning of $\delta$; extreme values ($\delta \ge 0.6$) degrade performance significantly.

**When to Use:**
*   Use when densification is biased towards high-gradient/complex regions, leading to poor coverage elsewhere.
*   Use when training instability is observed in indoor scenes.

**When Not to Use:**
*   Avoid $\delta = 0$ as it is inferior to opacity sampling in some metrics.
*   Avoid $\delta \in \{0.6, 0.8\}$ due to worst-case performance.

## Background
Complexity Dropout was introduced in the KoalaGS paper [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the issue of Gaussian Splatting densification algorithms repeatedly selecting the same high-complexity regions, which limits the ability of opacity sampling to fill in gaps. The method was developed to improve the robustness and coverage of Gaussian primitives in novel view synthesis, particularly in sparse-view tomography and indoor scenes.
