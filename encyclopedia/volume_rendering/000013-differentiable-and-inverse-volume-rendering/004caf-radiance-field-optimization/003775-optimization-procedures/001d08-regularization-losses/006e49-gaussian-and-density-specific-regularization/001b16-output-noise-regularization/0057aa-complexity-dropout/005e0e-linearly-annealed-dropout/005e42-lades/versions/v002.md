# LADES

Also called: Linearly Annealed Dropout and Structure-Aware Early Stopping, Linearly Annealed Dropout with Structure-Aware Early Stopping

## Overview
LADES is a ground-truth-free optimization controller for sparse-view 3D Gaussian tomography that mitigates Projection-Volume Fidelity Divergence (PVFD) by combining Linearly Annealed Dropout (LAD) and Structure-Aware Early Stopping (SAES) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem where improved projection fitting coincides with deteriorating volumetric fidelity due to structural degeneration, such as needle-like anisotropic primitives and fragile co-adaptation [S1, unofficial]. Unlike fixed dropout or validation-based stopping, LADES disrupts premature co-adaptation via time-decaying stochastic masking and terminates densification based on intrinsic Gaussian population dynamics rather than external metrics [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse-view X-ray projections $P_v$, a set of Gaussian primitives $G_t$ (initialized via R2-GS or similar), and hyperparameters $p_0, T_{anneal}, t_{min}, \tau, W, \gamma_{cool}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A final set of Gaussian primitives $G_{final}$ and their parameters (position, scale, opacity, color) optimized for volumetric fidelity [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Primitive $i$:** Contains position $\mathbf{x}_i$, scale $\mathbf{s}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color coefficients [G].
*   **Mask $m^{(t)}$:** A binary vector where $m_i^{(t)} \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates if primitive $i$ is active at iteration $t$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Population Counter $N_j$:** The number of active Gaussians at monitoring step $j$ [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussian primitives $G_0$ [G]. Set iteration counter $t=0$. Set monitoring step counter $j=0$. Initialize Adam optimizer moments for position and scaling parameters [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Dropout Probability Calculation:** Compute the current dropout probability $p_t$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    p_t = \begin{cases} p_0 \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{t}{T_{anneal}}\right), & t < t_s \\ 0, & t \ge t_s \end{cases}
    $$
    where $p_0$ is the initial dropout probability, $T_{anneal}$ is the annealing horizon, and $t_s$ is the SAES trigger iteration (initially undefined or $\infty$) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Mask Sampling:** For each Gaussian primitive $i$, sample a Bernoulli mask $m_i^{(t)}$ with retention probability $1 - p_t$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    m_i^{(t)} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1 - p_t)
    $$
    $m_i^{(t)} = 1$ means the primitive is retained for the forward and backward pass [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the projection loss using the masked Gaussian set $G_t \odot m^{(t)}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    L_{LAD}^{(t)} = \sum_{v \in V} \left\| \hat{P}_v(G_t \odot m^{(t)}) - P_v \right\|^2 + \lambda_{SSIM} L_{SSIM}(\hat{P}_v(G_t \odot m^{(t)}), P_v)
    $$
    where $\hat{P}_v$ is the rendered projection, $P_v$ is the ground truth projection, and $L_{SSIM}$ is the SSIM loss [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Optimization Step:** Perform gradient descent using the Adam optimizer to update Gaussian parameters based on $L_{LAD}^{(t)}$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Densification (Conditional):** If $t < t_s$, perform standard densification operations (clone, split, prune) according to the R2-GS densification rule [S1, unofficial]. If $t \ge t_s$, disable all topology-changing operations [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Monitoring (SAES):** Every $\Delta$ iterations, increment $j$ and monitor the Gaussian population [S1, unofficial]:
    *   Let $N_j$ be the number of active Gaussians at step $j$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the normalized population growth rate $g_j$:
        $$
        g_j = \frac{N_j - N_{j-1}}{\max(N_{j-1}, 1)}
        $$
    *   Compute the averaged growth rate $\bar{g}_j$ over a sliding window of size $W$:
        $$
        \bar{g}_j = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{q=j-W+1}^{j} g_q
        $$
    *   Check for SAES trigger: If $t_j \ge t_{min}$ and $\bar{g}_j < \tau$, set $t_s = t_j$ [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Transition to Fixed-Topology Refinement:** Once $t_s$ is triggered (or if $t_s$ was never triggered, after $T_{anneal}$ or a max iteration limit):
    *   Set final training iteration $T_{final} = 2t_s$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Disable dropout ($p_t = 0$).
    *   Cool down learning rates for geometry ($\eta_{xyz}$) and scaling ($\eta_{scale}$) parameters by factor $\gamma_{cool}$:
        $$
        \eta_{xyz} \leftarrow \gamma_{cool} \eta_{xyz}, \quad \eta_{scale} \leftarrow \gamma_{cool} \eta_{scale}
        $$
    *   Reset the first- and second-order Adam moments for position and scaling parameters to remove optimization inertia [S1, unofficial].
    *   Continue optimization until $T_{final}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $p_0$: Initial dropout probability. Default: 0.9 [S1, unofficial].
*   $T_{anneal}$: Annealing horizon. Default: 30,000 iterations [S1, unofficial].
*   $t_{min}$: Minimum iteration count before SAES can trigger. Default: 2,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\tau$: Growth threshold for SAES. Default: $10^{-3}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $W$: Sliding window size for SAES. Default: 5 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\gamma_{cool}$: Learning rate cooling factor. Default: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Delta$: Monitoring interval. Default: 100 iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-regularization:** If $p_0$ is too large, the model may underfit volumetrically, dropping 3D PSNR significantly (e.g., from 35.45 dB to 30.62 dB) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Under-suppression:** If $p_0$ is too small, early co-adaptation is not sufficiently disrupted [S1, unofficial].
*   **Premature Stopping:** If $t_{min}$ is too low, SAES may trigger before structural saturation, leading to incomplete reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ground-Truth Dependency:** SAES must not access ground-truth volumes or 3D PSNR during training; doing so violates the ground-truth-free design and may lead to overfitting to validation metrics [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Linearly Annealed Dropout (LAD)
A regularization technique that applies strong stochastic masking in early training to disrupt premature primitive co-adaptation, then linearly decays the dropout probability to zero to restore full capacity for structural consolidation [S1, unofficial]. Choose when early view-specific co-adaptation is a primary concern [S1, unofficial]. See [Linearly Annealed Dropout](17550-linearly-annealed-dropout.md).

### Structure-Aware Early Stopping (SAES)
A stopping criterion that monitors Gaussian population growth momentum to detect structural saturation and terminates densification without ground-truth metrics [S1, unofficial]. Choose when volumetric fidelity degradation due to redundant late-stage densification is observed [S1, unofficial]. See [Structure-Aware Early Stopping](17551-structure-aware-early-stopping.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is reduced compared to fixed-budget training because SAES terminates densification early, though the final refinement phase extends training to $2t_s$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is determined by the number of active Gaussians, which is controlled by SAES to prevent excessive growth [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On sparse-view CT reconstruction (e.g., 25-view FIPS dataset), LADES improves volumetric fidelity, suppresses structural degeneration, and maintains competitive projection accuracy (PSNR ~36.33 dB, SSIM ~0.853) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Ground-truth-free; mitigates PVFD; reduces training time; robust to hyperparameter variations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Evaluated primarily on sparse-view CT with Gaussian-based backbones; generalization to other scanner geometries, noise levels, and clinical protocols requires further validation [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Sparse-view 3D Gaussian tomography where volumetric fidelity is critical and ground-truth volumes are unavailable during training [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Dense-view reconstruction where PVFD is less severe; non-Gaussian representations; scenarios where ground-truth validation metrics are available and preferred [S1, unofficial].

## Background
LADES was introduced in the paper "Projection-Volume Fidelity Divergence: Diagnosing and Controlling Optimization Drift in Sparse-View 3D Gaussian Tomography" [S1, unofficial]. The authors identified PVFD as a failure mode in sparse-view 3DGS-CT where projection fitting improves while volumetric fidelity deteriorates due to structural degeneration (needle-like primitives, fragile co-adaptation) [S1, unofficial]. LADES combines LAD and SAES to address this, using diagnostics like Geometric Anisotropy Index (GAI) and Volumetric Co-adaptation Score (VCS) to characterize the problem [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated on real X-ray CT data from the Finnish Inverse Problems Society (FIPS) dataset [S1, unofficial].
