# Regularization losses


## Overview
Regularization losses are penalty terms added to the total optimization objective of differentiable volume rendering to constrain learned parameters, prevent overfitting, and enforce physical or statistical priors [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike photometric losses that measure pixel-wise error, regularization targets the internal state of the model (e.g., density, geometry, or uncertainty) [S1][S2, unofficial]. This family distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) or [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md) by serving as the umbrella category for all such constraints, integrating them into a single scalar value $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ minimized by the optimizer [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family augment the base photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ (typically MSE or L1) with a weighted sum of regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)}$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)}
$$

where $\lambda_k$ are scalar hyperparameters balancing the influence of each regularizer [S1][S2, unofficial]. The optimization loop computes gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{total}$ with respect to model parameters $\theta$ (network weights, voxel grid values, or Gaussian attributes) and updates them via an optimizer (typically Adam) [S1][S2, unofficial]. Common goals include enforcing spatial smoothness, geometric compactness, temporal consistency, or uncertainty calibration [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md)
Total Variation (TV) regularization suppresses visual artifacts like "floaters" and "holes" in explicit radiance fields by penalizing large gradients between neighboring voxels. It operates directly on the 3D spatial structure, distinguishing it from distortion losses that focus on ray-wise geometric compactness. Choose this method when training explicit voxel-based or Gaussian splatting models prone to high-frequency noise.

### [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md)
This loss prevents "floaters" and "background collapse" by minimizing the integral of distances between all pairs of points along the 1D step function parameterized by ray distances and weights. It is set apart by its focus on geometric compactness along rays rather than spatial smoothness, and its $O(N)$ complexity enables high sample counts without excessive memory usage. Choose this for implicit MLP-based NeRFs to improve depth accuracy and reduce ghosting.

### [MSE Loss and PSNR Conversion](1651-mse-loss-and-psnr-conversion.md)
Computes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and target RGB values as the primary photometric loss, converting it to Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) for evaluation. It differs from structural metrics like SSIM by treating all pixel errors equally based on intensity magnitude. Use this as the baseline reconstruction loss for standard neural volume rendering.

### [Perceptual Loss Training](2000-perceptual-loss-training.md)
Incorporates a perceptual loss term using high-level CNN feature representations (e.g., VGG) to improve perceptual quality, addressing the blurriness of standard pixel-wise losses. It sets itself apart from TV or L1+SSIM by leveraging semantic features rather than low-level statistics. Choose this when visual sharpness and texture fidelity are prioritized over strict pixel accuracy.

### [Second-Order Chamfer Distance](2170-second-order-chamfer-distance.md)
A supervised geometric loss measuring discrepancy between predicted mesh vertices and ground truth surfaces by incorporating local curvature. Unlike standard Chamfer Distance, it approximates the distance function quadratically to account for local geometry. Use this as a baseline for meshing implicit functions when ground truth meshes are available.

### [Color-Corrected Error Metrics](2737-color-corrected-error-metrics.md)
An evaluation procedure mitigating photometric variations (autoexposure, white balance) in "in the wild" datasets by fitting a quadratic polynomial to rendered RGB values. It neutralizes global shifts, making view synthesis models quantitatively comparable. Use this for evaluation on datasets with inconsistent lighting, not as a training loss.

### [L1+SSIM Loss](2890-l1-ssim-loss.md)
A composite objective combining pixel-wise L1 error with Structural Similarity (SSIM) to balance color accuracy with perceptual sharpness. It is distinguished from pure MSE by its robustness to outliers and explicit reward for matching local structural details. Choose this for 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF training to achieve sharper results.

### [Model Regularization](2912-model-regularization.md)
Applies penalty terms to constrain learned parameters, preventing overfitting and enforcing priors. It differs from photometric losses by targeting the internal state (e.g., density, uncertainty) rather than final pixel values. Use this as a general category for any non-photometric constraint.

### [Visibility-Based Photometric Loss](2917-visibility-based-photometric-loss.md)
Computes photometric error only on pixels identified as visible across views by comparing reprojected source depth with rendered target depth. It differs from standard losses by filtering out occluded or transient geometry. Choose this for multi-view stereo or dynamic scene reconstruction to avoid fitting to occlusions.

### [Uncertainty Prediction Loss](2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md)
Predicts and penalizes aleatoric uncertainty of rendered outputs, allowing the optimizer to down-weight noisy data. It differs from standard losses by explicitly modeling confidence, unlike noise models that only visualize distributions. Use this when dealing with noisy inputs or ambiguous geometry.

### [Data Consistency Loss](2976-data-consistency-loss.md)
Enforces that rendered output matches measured input projection data by minimizing discrepancy in the measurement domain (e.g., sinogram space). It differs from photometric losses by operating on raw measurements rather than final 2D images. Use this for inverse rendering tasks with known physical projection models (e.g., CT).

### [Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss](3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md)
Augments L1 reconstruction loss with penalties on Gaussian splat amplitude and extent to prevent overfitting and enforce physical plausibility. It is distinguished by explicit constraints on Gaussian parameters, unlike distortion losses. Choose this for optimizing 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters.

### [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md)
The aggregate optimization objective combining photometric errors with regularization terms to ensure physically plausible or structurally smooth field properties. It differs from individual terms by integrating all active penalties into a single scalar $\mathcal{L}_{total}$. Use this as the final loss passed to the optimizer.

### [Joint Optimization with Loss Functions](4506-joint-optimization-with-loss-functions.md)
Defines a composite objective for joint optimization of 3D surface splatting parameters and learnable HDR environment maps, combining photometric fidelity with geometric priors (depth consolidation, normal consistency). It is distinguished by coupling surfel primitives with global lighting parameters. Choose this for inverse rendering with complex lighting.

### [Forplane Optimization Losses](4672-forplane-optimization-losses.md)
Regularization terms for neural radiance fields using orthogonal neural planes (Forplanes), enforcing spatial smoothness, temporal consistency, and disentanglement of static/dynamic features. Unlike standard spatial regularization, these operate on 2D orthogonal projections. Choose this for spatiotemporal neural field optimization.

### Output Noise Regularization
Adds random Gaussian noise (mean 0, variance 1) to output density $\sigma$ values before ReLU activation during optimization [S1]. This slightly improves visual performance for novel view synthesis in real scenes [S1]. Use this for implicit MLP-based NeRFs trained on real-world data [S1].

### Temporal Instability Metric
Measures temporal variation in reconstruction error (flickering) based on frame-to-frame changes in error [S2, unofficial]. Defined as $TI = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} \frac{1}{T} \sum_t (E_i(t) - E_i(t-1))^2}$ [S2, unofficial]. Use this for evaluating temporal stability in dynamic volume rendering [S2, unofficial].

### Temporal Reconstruction Error Metric
Measures constant reconstruction error (ghosting) by comparing froxel data directly, rather than final image pixels [S2, unofficial]. Defined as $RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} \frac{1}{T} \sum_t E_i(t)^2}$ [S2, unofficial]. Use this for evaluating estimator bias in dynamic scenes [S2, unofficial].

### Beta Distribution Prior on Exit Opacity
Regularization term using the negative log-likelihood of a Beta(0.5, 0.5) distribution on final image opacities to reduce entropy [S3]. Formula: $J_B(\mathbf{I}_\alpha) = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \lambda_B [\log(\mathbf{I}_\alpha(\mathbf{p})) + \log(1 - \mathbf{I}_\alpha(\mathbf{p}))]$ [S3]. Use this to encourage rays to strike objects or background rather than grazing surfaces [S3].

### Leak Metric
Measures the fraction of a method's near-plane foreground energy that incorrectly falls on the culled side where the reference is background [S4, unofficial]. Defined as $\text{Leak} = \frac{\sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{L}} \max_{\mathbf{x}} R_c(\mathbf{x})}{\sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \max_{\mathbf{x}} R_c(\mathbf{x})}$ [S4, unofficial]. Use this for evaluating clipping artifacts in Gaussian Splatting [S4, unofficial].

### Band Fidelity Metric (Sb)
Measures structural similarity between a method's render and a reference render over the exposed cut face (reference foreground) [S4, unofficial]. Computes SSIM restricted to foreground mask $F(G)$ [S4, unofficial]. Use this for evaluating band fidelity in clipped volumes [S4, unofficial].

### Distortion Loss
Computes distortion loss for background rays if `learned_background` is enabled, using background-specific output keys [S5, unofficial]. Scaled by `lambda_distortion_bg` [S5, unofficial]. Use this for NeRFs with explicit background modeling [S5, unofficial].

### Proposal Supervision Loss
Supervises the proposal MLP by minimizing excess histogram mass that violates a bound established between histograms of 1D data, encouraging consistency between proposal and NeRF weights [S6]. Use this for two-stage NeRF optimization (e.g., Mip-NeRF 360) [S6].

### Uncertainty-aware Squared Error Loss
Punishes predicted uncertainty $\delta_i$ less than the squared error $E(r)$, defined as $\sigma_{SE} = \sum_{r \in \sigma} \sum_{i=1}^N \max(E(r) - \delta_{c_i}, 0)$ [S7]. Use this for uncertainty-aware NeRF training [S7].

### Multi-Task Differentiable Loss for 3D Gaussian Splatting
Combines segmentation mask loss (cross-entropy), color loss (L1), and optical flow loss (L1) for optimizing 3D Gaussians from video [S8, unofficial]. Total loss: $L = L_M + \lambda_C L_C + \lambda_F L_F$ [S8, unofficial]. Use this for multi-task Gaussian Splatting optimization [S8, unofficial].

### TensoRF Reconstruction Optimization
Optimizes tensorial radiance fields via gradient descent, minimizing L2 rendering loss with L1 sparsity and TV regularization on factors [S9, unofficial]. Use this for tensor-based NeRF optimization [S9, unofficial].

### Variance Loss
Adds a variance-based loss term, either total variance loss or voxel disparity loss, to scene optimization [S10, unofficial]. Use this for Plenoxels or sparse volume rendering [S10, unofficial].

## Background
Regularization losses originated with the introduction of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by Mildenhall et al. (2020), which introduced output noise regularization for real scene data [S1]. Subsequent works expanded this family: Neural Volumes (Mildenhall et al., 2019) introduced Beta distribution priors on opacity [S3]; TensoRF (Gu et al., 2022) applied L1 and TV regularization to tensor factors [S9, unofficial]; and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its variants introduced distortion losses and regularized fitting losses [S1][S2, unofficial][S5, unofficial][S8, unofficial]. The family has grown to include uncertainty prediction [S7], perceptual losses [S1], and geometric constraints [S1], reflecting the diverse needs of differentiable volume rendering in static, dynamic, and inverse rendering contexts.
