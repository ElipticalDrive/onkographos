# Regularization losses


## Overview
Regularization losses are penalty terms added to the optimization objective of differentiable volume rendering to constrain the solution space, prevent overfitting, and enforce physical or structural priors that photometric data alone cannot provide. They solve the ill-posed nature of inverse volume rendering by suppressing artifacts such as "floaters" (spurious geometry), transparency noise, and geometric inconsistencies. This family is distinguished from photometric reconstruction losses (e.g., [MSE Loss and PSNR Conversion](1651-mse-loss-and-psnr-conversion.md)) by operating on derived quantities (gradients, opacities, normals) or latent parameters rather than direct pixel-wise color errors, and from general [Optimization Loops and Training Procedures](5622-optimization-loops-and-training-procedures.md) by focusing specifically on the mathematical form of the penalty rather than the scheduling or convergence criteria.

## Shared principles
All regularization losses in this family share the mechanism of augmenting the total loss function $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ with a weighted penalty term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg, k}
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_{data}$ is the primary photometric or geometric reconstruction loss, $\lambda_k$ is a scalar hyperparameter balancing the influence of the $k$-th regularizer, and $\mathcal{L}_{reg, k}$ is the regularization term. The gradients of these terms are backpropagated through the differentiable rendering equation to update scene parameters (density, color, geometry, latent codes). Common structural goals include enforcing spatial smoothness (via Total Variation), encouraging binary opacity distributions (via priors), or ensuring geometric consistency (via normal alignment).

## Subtopics

### [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md)
Total Variation (TV) regularization suppresses visual artifacts like "floaters" and "holes" in explicit radiance field representations (voxel grids) by penalizing large gradients between neighboring voxels. It distinguishes itself from [Output Noise Regularization](928-output-noise-regularization.md) (which targets noise distribution) and [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md) (which targets depth consistency). It is chosen when sparse or dense grids exhibit high-frequency noise or disconnected geometry. Efficient implementations often use Huber loss or fused CUDA kernels to balance artifact suppression with computational efficiency.

### [Output Noise Regularization](928-output-noise-regularization.md)

### [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md)
The Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss prevents "floaters" and "background collapse" by minimizing the integral of distances between all pairs of points along the 1D step function parameterized by ray distances and weights. It sets itself apart by reducing computational complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N)$ using prefix and suffix sums, enabling handling of large numbers of sampled points (>256) without excessive GPU memory consumption.

### [MSE Loss and PSNR Conversion](1651-mse-loss-and-psnr-conversion.md)
This method computes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and target RGB values to serve as the primary photometric loss, converting it to PSNR for evaluation. It differs from structural metrics like SSIM by treating all pixel errors equally and from perceptual losses by operating on raw intensities. It is the standard baseline loss for optimizing radiance fields.

### [Perceptual Loss Training](2000-perceptual-loss-training.md)
Perceptual Loss Training incorporates a loss term based on high-level feature representations from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to improve perceptual quality, addressing the blurriness of standard pixel-wise losses. It sets itself apart from [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) and [L1+SSIM Loss](2890-l1-ssim-loss.md) by leveraging CNN features rather than low-level statistics or structural similarity.

### [Second-Order Chamfer Distance](2170-second-order-chamfer-distance.md)
Second-Order Chamfer Distance is a supervised geometric loss that measures discrepancy between a predicted mesh vertex and ground truth surface by incorporating local surface curvature. Unlike standard Chamfer Distance, it approximates the distance function quadratically to account for local geometry. It serves as a baseline for meshing implicit functions where ground truth meshes are available.

### [Beta Distribution Prior on Exit Opacity](2274-beta-distribution-prior-on-exit-opacity.md)
The Beta Distribution Prior on Exit Opacity encourages the scene to contain solid, opaque materials by penalizing exit opacities that are neither fully transparent nor fully opaque. Unlike the Entropy prior, which can cause material loss, the Beta prior consistently produces images with more solid material and reduced noise.

### [Color-Corrected Error Metrics](2737-color-corrected-error-metrics.md)
Color-Corrected Error Metrics mitigate photometric variations (autoexposure, white balance) in "in the wild" datasets by fitting a quadratic polynomial to rendered RGB values to neutralize global shifts. This makes view synthesis models quantitatively comparable.

### [L1+SSIM Loss](2890-l1-ssim-loss.md)
L1+SSIM Loss combines pixel-wise L1 error with Structural Similarity (SSIM) to balance color accuracy with perceptual sharpness. Unlike pure MSE losses which produce blurry results, it explicitly rewards matching local structures such as edges and textures.

### [Model Regularization](2912-model-regularization.md)
Model regularization applies explicit penalty terms to neural network parameters or outputs to enforce properties like smoothness or uncertainty bounds. It complements photometric data losses by preventing overfitting. Unlike sibling methods focusing on image-space metrics, it operates directly on the latent representation or auxiliary outputs.

### [Visibility-Based Photometric Loss](2917-visibility-based-photometric-loss.md)
Visibility-Based Photometric Loss computes photometric error only on pixels identified as visible across source and target views, filtering out occluded or transient geometry. It differs from standard photometric losses by using depth consistency thresholds to define visibility.

### [Uncertainty Prediction Loss](2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md)
Uncertainty Prediction Loss predicts and penalizes aleatoric uncertainty of rendered outputs, allowing the optimizer to down-weight noisy data points. Unlike [Uniform and Gaussian Noise Models](5127-uniform-and-gaussian-noise-models.md) which visualize uncertainty, this method incorporates uncertainty parameters into the training objective to improve reconstruction.

### [Data Consistency Loss](2976-data-consistency-loss.md)
Data Consistency Loss minimizes the discrepancy between forward-projected scene representation and measured input data, ensuring fidelity to observations. It operates on the raw projection domain (sinogram/ray-space) rather than the rendered image domain, making it essential for inverse problems where the forward operator is explicitly differentiable.

### [Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss](3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md)
The Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss balances reconstruction accuracy against geometric priors for Gaussian splats, combining L1 reconstruction loss with regularization for splat amplitude and extent. It ensures stable optimization of splat geometry during early-stopped training.

### [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md)
The Total Loss Function aggregates heterogeneous loss components—photometric fidelity, geometric priors, and latent space constraints—into a single scalar value. It differs from siblings like [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) by combining multiple specific terms rather than focusing on one aspect.

### [Joint Optimization with Loss Functions](4506-joint-optimization-with-loss-functions.md)
This method defines a composite loss for joint optimization of 3D scene parameters (surfel primitives and environment lighting) in inverse rendering. It addresses ambiguity of multi-layer compositing by combining photometric fidelity, silhouette coverage, depth consolidation, and normal consistency. It is distinguished by its application to surfel-based inverse rendering.

### [Forplane Optimization Losses](4672-forplane-optimization-losses.md)
Forplane Optimization Losses constrain the optimization of orthogonal neural planes in spatiotemporal volume rendering for deformable tissue. They solve entanglement between static and dynamic components by enforcing structural priors on specific orthogonal projections (space-space and space-time planes).

### [Geometric and Normal Alignment Losses](4970-geometric-and-normal-alignment-losses.md)
Geometric and Normal Alignment Losses enforce physical plausibility by penalizing deviations from expected geometric properties (smoothness, normal consistency). This family is distinct from photometric losses because it operates on derived geometric quantities (normals, curvature) rather than pixel intensities.

### Temporal Reconstruction Error Metric
#### definition
- [S1, unofficial] A metric measuring constant reconstruction error (ghosting) by comparing froxel data directly, rather than final image pixels.
#### formula
- [S1, unofficial] RMSE = sqrt( (1/|Ω|) * sum_{i in Ω} (1/T) * sum_t E_i(t)^2 ), where Ω is the region of interest, T is the total number of frames, and color channels are averaged at the end.

### Proposal Supervision Loss
#### definition
- [S3] A loss function that supervises the proposal MLP by minimizing excess histogram mass that violates a bound established between histograms of 1D data, encouraging consistency between proposal weights $(\hat{t}, \hat{w})$ and NeRF weights $(t, w)$ despite different bin locations.
#### step
- [S3] 1. Construct histograms $(t, w)$ from points $\bar{x}$ and $(\hat{t}, \hat{w})$ from points $\bar{\bar{x}}$. 2. Establish a bound implied by $(\hat{t}, \hat{w})$ on $(t, w)$. 3. If $\bar{x} = \bar{\bar{x}}$, the bound is an upper bound and loss is zero. 4. If $\bar{x} \neq \bar{\bar{x}}$, compute loss for excess mass exceeding the bound.

### Uncertainty-aware Squared Error Loss
#### formula
- [S4] $σ_{SE} = Σ_{r ∈ σ} Σ_{i=1}^N \max(E(r) - δ_{c_i}, 0)$ where $E(r) = ||σ(r) - C(r)||_2^2$ is the squared error of ray $r$, $c_i$ is the sample point of ray $r$, and $δ_{c_i}$ is the predicted uncertainty for query coordinates $c_i$. This punishes $δ_i$ less than the squared error.

### Multi-Task Differentiable Loss for 3D Gaussian Splatting
#### definition
- [S5, unofficial] A set of loss functions used to optimize 3D Gaussians from video input, combining segmentation mask loss, color loss, and optical flow loss.
#### formula
- [S5, unofficial] Mask loss (cross-entropy): $L_M = \alpha \cdot \log(\hat{\alpha}) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \log(1 - \hat{\alpha})$, where $\alpha$ is estimated alpha and $\hat{\alpha}$ is the target mask.
- [S5, unofficial] Color loss (L1): $L_C = \alpha \cdot \|c - \hat{c}\|_1$, where $c$ is rendered color and $\hat{c}$ is target color.
- [S5, unofficial] Flow loss (L1): $L_F = \alpha \cdot \|f - \hat{f}\|_1$, where $f$ is rendered optical flow and $\hat{f}$ is target flow.
- [S5, unofficial] Total loss: $L = L_M + \lambda_C L_C + \lambda_F L_F$, where $\lambda_C$ and $\lambda_F$ are weighting coefficients.
#### parameter
- [S5, unofficial] Input resolution: Experiments use image sequences of roughly $250 \times 130$ on GPU (Nvidia GTX 1080) and $125 \times 65$ on CPU.
- [S5, unofficial] Number of Gaussians: 40 Gaussians are used in the experiments for ease of comparison with prior work.
#### pitfall
- [S5, unofficial] Estimated alpha must be clipped to the range $[10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}]$ to prevent numerical instability in the log terms of the mask loss.

### TensoRF Reconstruction Optimization
#### definition
- [S6, unofficial] Optimizing the tensorial radiance field per scene via gradient descent to minimize L2 rendering loss, using ground truth pixel colors as supervision.
#### step
- [S6, unofficial] Initialize tensor factors (global vectors and matrices).
- [S6, unofficial] Compute rendering loss (L2) between rendered pixel colors and ground truth pixel colors.
- [S6, unofficial] Apply regularization terms to vector and matrix factors to address overfitting and local minima.
- [S6, unofficial] Apply coarse-to-fine reconstruction by linearly and bilinearly upsampling XYZ-mode vector and matrix factors at specific optimization steps.
#### parameter
- [S6, unofficial] L1 norm loss: Sparsity loss applied to factors. Used for most datasets.
- [S6, unofficial] TV (Total Variation) loss: Applied for real datasets with few input images (e.g., LLFF) or imperfect capture conditions (e.g., Tanks and Temples with varying exposure/inconsistent masks).
- [S6, unofficial] Upsampling Steps: 2000, 3000, 4000, 5500, 7000.
- [S6, unofficial] Initial Resolution: N0^3 voxels with N0 = 128.
- [S6, unofficial] Final Resolution: N^3 voxels. Interpolation between N0^3 and N^3 is linear in logarithmic space.

## Background
Regularization in volume rendering originated from the need to stabilize the optimization of neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and explicit voxel grids, which are prone to overfitting and artifacts due to the ill-posed nature of the inverse problem. Early works introduced Total Variation (TV) loss to enforce smoothness in voxel grids [S6, unofficial]. The concept of distortion loss was formalized to handle unbounded scenes and background collapse [S2, unofficial]. Perceptual losses were adapted from image synthesis to improve the visual quality of rendered views [S1, unofficial]. More recent developments include uncertainty-aware losses [S4] and priors on opacity distributions [S1, unofficial] to handle transparency noise. The family has evolved to include specialized losses for Gaussian Splatting [S5, unofficial], tensorial fields [S6, unofficial], and joint optimization of geometry and lighting [S1, unofficial].
