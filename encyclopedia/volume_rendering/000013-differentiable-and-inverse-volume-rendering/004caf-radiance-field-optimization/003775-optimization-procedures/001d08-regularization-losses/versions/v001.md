# Regularization losses

Also called: Auxiliary losses, Regularization terms, Training objectives

## Overview
Regularization losses are auxiliary terms added to the primary photometric reconstruction loss to stabilize training, enforce geometric priors, or improve perceptual quality in differentiable volume rendering. While the parent method [Radiance-field optimization](44-radiance-field-optimization.md) minimizes pixel-wise error, this family addresses specific failure modes such as "floaters" (spurious geometry), overfitting to noise, or temporal flickering. They set themselves apart from photometric losses by constraining the latent field representation or intermediate predictions rather than comparing rendered images to ground truth directly.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the mechanism of augmenting the total optimization objective $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ with additional penalty terms $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)}
$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ is the photometric loss (e.g., MSE, L1, SSIM) and $\lambda_k$ are scalar weights. The regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)}$ typically operate on one of three domains:
1.  **Spatial/Structural:** Penalizing gradients or variance in the field parameters (e.g., density, opacity, Gaussian extents) to enforce smoothness or sparsity [S1][S9, unofficial].
2.  **Geometric:** Constraining the predicted geometry to be consistent with physical priors (e.g., Eikonal equation, normal consistency, depth consolidation) [S2, S4, unofficial].
3.  **Statistical/Uncertainty:** Modeling the confidence of predictions or enforcing distributional priors on output values (e.g., uncertainty regularization, Beta priors on opacity) [S1, S3, S7].

The optimization loop integrates these terms by computing their gradients and adding them to the gradient buffer of the relevant model parameters before the optimizer step [S1][S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md)
Total Variation (TV) regularization penalizes large spatial gradients between neighboring voxels or splats to suppress visual artifacts like floaters and holes. It is particularly effective for explicit representations (voxel grids, Gaussian splats) which are prone to non-convex optimization artifacts compared to implicit MLPs. It operates directly on the 3D spatial structure, distinguishing it from ray-based distortion losses.

### [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md)
This loss minimizes the integral of distances between all pairs of points along the 1D step function parameterized by ray distances and weights. It prevents "floaters" and background collapse by encouraging weights to be concentrated at a single depth per ray. It sets itself apart by reducing computational complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N)$ using prefix/suffix sums, enabling efficient handling of large numbers of sampled points.

### [MSE Loss and PSNR Conversion](1651-mse-loss-and-psnr-conversion.md)
Computes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and target RGB values as the primary photometric loss and converts it to Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) for evaluation. It serves as the baseline fidelity metric against which regularization improvements are measured.

### [Perceptual Loss Training](2000-perceptual-loss-training.md)
Incorporates a perceptual loss term using high-level feature representations from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to improve perceptual quality and reduce blurriness associated with standard pixel-wise losses. It differs from TV or L1+SSIM by leveraging semantic features rather than low-level statistics.

### [Second-Order Chamfer Distance](2170-second-order-chamfer-distance.md)
A supervised geometric loss that measures discrepancy between predicted mesh vertices and ground truth surfaces by incorporating local surface curvature. Unlike standard Chamfer Distance, it approximates the distance function quadratically to account for local geometry, serving as a baseline for meshing implicit functions where ground truth meshes are available.

### [Color-Corrected Error Metrics](2737-color-corrected-error-metrics.md)
An evaluation procedure that mitigates photometric variations (autoexposure, white balance) by fitting a quadratic polynomial to rendered RGB values to match ground truth. It neutralizes global brightness shifts, making view synthesis models quantitatively comparable despite uncontrolled camera settings.

### [L1+SSIM Loss](2890-l1-ssim-loss.md)
A composite objective combining pixel-wise L1 error with Structural Similarity (SSIM) to balance color accuracy with perceptual sharpness. It differs from pure MSE by being less sensitive to outliers and from pure SSIM by retaining strict pixel-level fidelity, addressing blurring artifacts in differentiable volume rendering.

### [Model Regularization](2912-model-regularization.md)
Applies auxiliary loss terms directly to the neural network's internal predictions (e.g., uncertainty, density) to stabilize training and prevent pathological behaviors like uncertainty explosion. It constrains the latent space or intermediate outputs based on prior assumptions, distinguishing it from geometric or perceptual sibling losses.

### [Visibility-Based Photometric Loss](2917-visibility-based-photometric-loss.md)
Restricts photometric error computation to pixels that are geometrically consistent across views, excluding occluded regions and transient objects. It differs from standard losses by explicitly filtering pixels where reprojection depth disagrees with rendered depth, preventing fitting to non-static geometry.

### [Uncertainty Prediction Loss](2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md)
Predicts and penalizes aleatoric uncertainty of rendered outputs or geometric fields, allowing the optimizer to down-weight noisy or ambiguous data points. Unlike noise models that visualize uncertainty, this method incorporates uncertainty parameters directly into the training objective to improve reconstruction.

### [Data Consistency Loss](2976-data-consistency-loss.md)
Enforces geometric and photometric fidelity by minimizing the discrepancy between measured projection data and the forward-projected output of the learned representation. It directly anchors the latent representation to raw sensor measurements, solving scale ambiguity and noise sensitivity in inverse rendering and tomography.

### [Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss](3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md)
A composite objective for Gaussian splats that combines L1 reconstruction error with explicit regularization terms for splat amplitude and spatial extent. It distinguishes itself by penalizing the magnitude and spread of Gaussian parameters directly, stabilizing optimization in capacity-constrained scenarios.

### [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md)
The composite objective aggregating multiple terms—reconstruction error, regularization priors (TV, Eikonal), and latent penalties—into a single scalar value. It defines the complete training signal and weighting strategy, differing from individual component losses by managing the balance between fidelity and priors.

### [Joint Optimization with Loss Functions](4506-joint-optimization-with-loss-functions.md)
Defines the composite objective for jointly optimizing 3D surface splatting parameters and a learnable HDR environment map in inverse rendering. It is distinguished by its focus on surfel-level geometric thinness (depth consolidation) and 3D normal alignment, rather than general field smoothness.

### [Forplane Optimization Losses](4672-forplane-optimization-losses.md)
A set of regularization terms for neural radiance fields using orthogonal neural planes (Forplanes). They address static/dynamic ambiguity by enforcing structural smoothness in spatial planes, temporal consistency in space-time planes, and feature separation via a disentangle loss.

### Output Noise Regularization
Adds random Gaussian noise (mean 0, variance 1) to the output density $\sigma$ values before the ReLU activation during optimization for real scene data [S1]. This slightly improves visual performance for rendering novel views by regularizing the network [S1].

### Temporal Instability Metric
Measures temporal variation in reconstruction error (flickering) based on frame-to-frame changes in error [S2, unofficial]. It is defined as the root mean square of the difference in error between consecutive frames [S2, unofficial]. High values indicate quick changes perceived as flicker, though it does not distinguish between ground truth changes and slow reconstruction responses [S2, unofficial].

### Temporal Reconstruction Error Metric
Measures constant reconstruction error (ghosting) by comparing froxel data directly across frames [S2, unofficial]. It calculates the RMSE of the froxel values over time, capturing estimator bias [S2, unofficial].

### Beta Distribution Prior on Exit Opacity
Regularization term using the negative log-likelihood of a Beta(0.5, 0.5) distribution on final image opacities to reduce entropy [S3]. It enforces the intuition that rays should strike the object or background rather than grazing surfaces, reducing artifacts from semi-transparent floaters [S3].

### Leak Metric
Measures the fraction of a method's near-plane foreground energy that incorrectly falls on the culled side where the reference is background [S4, unofficial]. It combines proxy reconstruction error, renderer mismatch, and soft tails to evaluate clipping accuracy in Gaussian Splatting [S4, unofficial].

### Band Fidelity Metric (Sb)
A fidelity metric measuring structural similarity between a method's render and a reference render over the exposed cut face (reference foreground) [S4, unofficial]. It computes SSIM restricted to the foreground mask, averaged across perpendicular views [S4, unofficial].

### Distortion Loss
A regularization term for differentiable volume rendering that prevents floaters and background collapse by minimizing the integral of distances between all pairs of points along the ray [S1]. The efficient O(N) implementation uses prefix/suffix sums to compute this loss without $O(N^2)$ complexity [S2, unofficial].

### Proposal Supervision Loss
Supervises the proposal MLP by minimizing excess histogram mass that violates a bound established between histograms of 1D data [S6]. It encourages consistency between proposal weights and NeRF weights despite different bin locations [S6].

### Uncertainty-aware Squared Error Loss
A loss function that punishes predicted uncertainty $\delta_i$ less than the squared error $E(r)$, ensuring that high error regions are associated with high uncertainty [S7]. It is defined as $\sum \max(E(r) - \delta_{c_i}, 0)$ [S7].

### Multi-Task Differentiable Loss for 3D Gaussian Splatting
Combines segmentation mask loss (cross-entropy), color loss (L1), and optical flow loss (L1) for optimizing 3D Gaussians from video input [S8, unofficial]. It weights these losses by the estimated alpha mask to focus optimization on the object [S8, unofficial].

### TensoRF Reconstruction Optimization
Optimizes tensorial radiance fields via gradient descent to minimize L2 rendering loss, using L1 norm sparsity loss and TV loss on vector/matrix factors to address overfitting and local minima [S9, unofficial]. It employs coarse-to-fine reconstruction by upsampling factors at specific steps [S9, unofficial].

### Variance Loss
Adds a variance-based loss term to scene optimization, either total variance loss or voxel disparity loss [S10, unofficial]. It is applied periodically during training to stabilize the field representation [S10, unofficial].

## Background
Regularization in neural radiance fields and volume rendering emerged alongside the original NeRF paper [S1], which introduced output noise regularization for real scenes. Subsequent works identified specific artifacts like "floaters" and "background collapse," leading to specialized losses such as Distortion Loss [S1] and Total Variation for explicit representations [S1]. The field has expanded to include perceptual losses [S1], uncertainty modeling [S7], and geometric constraints for inverse rendering [S4, unofficial]. Metrics for evaluating these regularizations, such as Temporal Instability [S2, unofficial] and Leak [S4, unofficial], have also been developed to quantify improvements in visual quality and geometric fidelity.
