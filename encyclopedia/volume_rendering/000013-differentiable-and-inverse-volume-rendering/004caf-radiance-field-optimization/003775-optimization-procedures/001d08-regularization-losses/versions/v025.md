# Regularization losses
## Overview
Regularization losses are auxiliary objective terms added to the training of differentiable volume rendering to constrain the solution space, prevent overfitting, and enforce physical or structural plausibility beyond simple pixel-wise reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. They solve the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by suppressing artifacts such as "floaters," transparent holes, or erratic geometry that standard photometric losses (e.g., L1/L2) fail to penalize [S1, unofficial]. This family is distinguished from [Photometric and Color Consistency Losses](8132-photometric-and-color-consistency-losses.md) by targeting the latent parameters or spatial distributions of the scene representation rather than the final rendered pixel values, and from [Evaluation and Reconstruction Metrics](7149-evaluation-and-reconstruction-metrics.md) by being differentiable and used for gradient-based optimization rather than post-hoc assessment [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md)
Total Variation (TV) regularization suppresses visual artifacts like "floaters" and "holes" in explicit radiance fields by penalizing large spatial gradients between neighboring voxels. It encourages spatial smoothness, distinguishing itself from [Output Noise Regularization](928-output-noise-regularization.md) which targets noise distribution, or [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md) which targets depth consistency.

### [Output Noise Regularization](928-output-noise-regularization.md)
Output Noise Regularization improves novel view synthesis by injecting Gaussian noise into predicted density ($\sigma$) or opacity values during training. It encourages the network to learn a robust density field less sensitive to small perturbations, operating directly on the radiance field's scalar output before activation, unlike methods targeting geometric consistency.

### [Perceptual Loss Training](2000-perceptual-loss-training.md)
Perceptual Loss Training incorporates features from pre-trained convolutional neural networks (typically VGG) to enforce high-level structural consistency, addressing the smoothness and texture distortion issues inherent in pixel-wise losses. It sets itself apart from [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) by leveraging high-level features rather than operating directly on spatial gradients of the output or scene parameters.

### [Second-Order Chamfer Distance](2170-second-order-chamfer-distance.md)
Second-Order Chamfer Distance is a supervised geometric loss that measures the discrepancy between a predicted mesh vertex and a ground truth surface by incorporating local surface curvature. It applies a quadratic approximation of the point-to-point distance to penalize deviations along principal curvature directions, serving as a supervised baseline for meshing implicit functions.

### [Color-Corrected Error Metrics](2737-color-corrected-error-metrics.md)
Color-Corrected Error Metrics mitigate the impact of photometric variations (autoexposure, white balance) in "in the wild" datasets by aligning rendered images to ground truth via per-image least-squares fitting. Unlike standard metrics that penalize brightness shifts as errors, these allow quantitative comparison of geometry and texture independent of lighting conditions.

### [Distortion Loss](2755-distortion-loss.md)
Distortion Loss prevents "floaters" and "background collapse" by encouraging rendering weights to be compact along the ray, minimizing the expected distance between two independent samples drawn from the weight distribution. It operates on the 1D depth distribution along rays, setting itself apart from [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) which smooths spatial gradients, and from [Variance Loss](5449-variance-loss.md) by explicitly modeling pairwise distances between all samples.

### [Proposal Supervision Loss](2889-proposal-supervision-loss.md)
Proposal Supervision Loss ($L_{prop}$) supervises a proposal MLP by minimizing excess histogram mass that violates a bound established between histograms of 1D data. It encourages consistency between proposal weights and NeRF weights despite differing bin locations, operating directly on volume rendering histogram weights rather than rendered pixel colors.

### [Model Regularization](2912-model-regularization.md)
Model Regularization applies explicit penalty terms to the raw outputs or internal parameters of a neural radiance field or Gaussian splatting decoder to constrain the solution space and prevent overfitting. Unlike sibling methods that regularize the output image or final rendering noise, Model Regularization targets the *latent* or *parameter* space of the scene representation itself.

### [Data Consistency Loss](2976-data-consistency-loss.md)
Data Consistency Loss enforces fidelity between a predicted volumetric representation and measured data by minimizing the discrepancy between the forward-projected prediction and ground truth observations. It serves as the primary supervisory signal in differentiable volume rendering, directly constraining the physical consistency of the rendering process.

### [Regularised Gaussian Fitting Loss](3104-regularised-gaussian-fitting-loss.md)
This method defines the objective for optimizing Gaussian splats by combining data fidelity with structural regularization, explicitly penalizing extreme splat amplitudes and extents to prevent overfitting. It differs from standard reconstruction losses by ensuring stable optimization in single-pass benchmarks.

### [Multi-Task Differentiable Loss for 3D Gaussian Splatting](3263-multi-task-differentiable-loss-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
This method defines a composite differentiable loss for optimizing 3D Gaussians from video, combining segmentation mask supervision, photometric color fidelity, and optical flow consistency. It distinguishes itself from [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) by explicitly leveraging multi-modal targets weighted by the estimated alpha channel.

### [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md)
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective used to optimize radiance fields by aggregating multiple distinct error terms into a single scalar value. It solves the problem of balancing competing objectives (photometric fidelity, geometric consistency, structural smoothness) within a unified training loop, distinguishing it from simple photometric losses by incorporating auxiliary geometric or semantic priors. It builds on [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md) by providing the structural framework for combining these terms.

### [Joint Optimization with Loss Functions](4506-joint-optimization-with-loss-functions.md)
Joint optimization with loss functions simultaneously updates scene geometry, material properties, and illumination parameters by minimizing a composite objective function. It solves the ill-posed inverse rendering problem by regularizing the solution space through geometric and photometric constraints, distinguishing itself from single-parameter optimization by enforcing consistency across depth layers, surface normals, and image appearance. Builds on [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md).

### [Forplane Optimization Losses](4672-forplane-optimization-losses.md)
Forplane Optimization Losses are regularization terms designed to stabilize the training of neural radiance fields using orthogonal neural planes (Forplanes) for spatiotemporal volume representation. They solve the problem of entanglement between static and dynamic scene components by enforcing spatial smoothness, temporal consistency, and static-field isolation, specifically targeting the structural integrity of the orthogonal plane representations.

### [Geometric and Normal Alignment Losses](4970-geometric-and-normal-alignment-losses.md)
Geometric and Normal Alignment Losses enforce physical plausibility and structural coherence in reconstructed scenes by penalizing geometric inconsistencies, noisy surface normals, and high-frequency oscillations in scalar fields [13295-normal-consistency-and-alignment.md][2911-curvature-loss.md]. This family sets itself apart from [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) or [Distortion Loss](2755-distortion-loss.md) by explicitly targeting the orientation, curvature, and spatial consistency of the geometry and material fields [13295-normal-consistency-and-alignment.md][2911-curvature-loss.md].

### [Variance Loss](5449-variance-loss.md)
Variance Loss is a regularization technique that adds a variance-based loss term to scene optimization, selectable as either total variance loss or voxel disparity loss. It builds on [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md) and sets itself apart from standard photometric losses by explicitly penalizing spatial variance in the rendered output or intermediate volume representations.

### [Evaluation and Reconstruction Metrics](7149-evaluation-and-reconstruction-metrics.md)
This family comprises metrics and objectives used to evaluate the quality, stability, and robustness of radiance field optimizations, distinct from the regularization losses that drive optimization [7149-evaluation-and-reconstruction-metrics.md]. While regularization losses shape scene parameters during training, evaluation metrics quantify the final output's fidelity to ground truth, temporal coherence, or robustness to perturbations [7149-evaluation-and-reconstruction-metrics.md].

### [Uncertainty and Probabilistic Priors](7917-uncertainty-and-probabilistic-priors.md)
This family incorporates probabilistic models into differentiable volume rendering to quantify aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, or to enforce structural priors [7917-uncertainty-and-probabilistic-priors.md]. Unlike deterministic regularization losses, these methods model the observation or scene distribution as a random variable, allowing the optimizer to downweight noisy observations or enforce sharp material boundaries [2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md][2964-uncertainty-aware-squared-error-loss.md][2274-beta-distribution-prior-on-exit-opacity.md].

### [Photometric and Color Consistency Losses](8132-photometric-and-color-consistency-losses.md)
This family of losses enforces pixel-level color fidelity and structural consistency between rendered images and ground-truth observations, serving as the primary supervisory signal for differentiable volume rendering [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. Unlike structural or geometric regularizers, these methods directly minimize the discrepancy in radiance values [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

### [Masked Opacity Regularization](11046-masked-opacity-regularization.md)
Masked opacity regularization prevents large primitives (such as Gaussians) from saturating visibility tests and causing gradient explosion by only penalizing opacity outside a specified binary mask. It differs from conventional mask regularization by leaving opacity inside the mask unconstrained, thereby avoiding the degradation of BRDF parameter training and visibility accuracy.

#### Temporal Reconstruction Error Metric

### definition
- [S1, unofficial] A metric measuring constant reconstruction error (ghosting) by comparing froxel data directly, rather than final image pixels.

### formula
- [S1, unofficial] RMSE = $\sqrt{ \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} \frac{1}{T} \sum_t E_i(t)^2 }$, where $\Omega$ is the region of interest, $T$ is the total number of frames, and color channels are averaged at the end.

## Background
Regularization losses in volume rendering emerged alongside the rise of differentiable rendering and neural radiance fields (NeRFs) to address the ill-posed nature of inverse problems, where photometric data alone is insufficient to uniquely determine complex 3D structures [S1, unofficial]. Early methods focused on Total Variation (TV) to smooth sparse voxel grids [S1, unofficial], while later developments introduced distortion losses to handle the ambiguity of depth in continuous volumetric representations [S1, unofficial]. The field has since expanded to include perceptual losses for texture fidelity [S1, unofficial], geometric constraints for mesh extraction [S1, unofficial], and probabilistic priors for uncertainty quantification [2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md][2964-uncertainty-aware-squared-error-loss.md]. These techniques are now standard in optimizing various radiance field representations, including implicit MLPs, explicit grids, and 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1, unofficial].
