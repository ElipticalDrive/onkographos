# Loss Combination

Also called: Combined Training Loss, Composite Loss Function

## Overview
Loss Combination aggregates multiple distinct error metrics—specifically photometric, depth, and coverage errors—into a single scalar value for backpropagation during the optimization of radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods like [SRF-Loss](6978-srf-loss.md) or [NTS Training Objective](18042-nts-training-objective.md) by explicitly weighting depth and coverage terms alongside standard color reconstruction, addressing the ambiguity of volumetric alignment and floating artifacts in open regions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `color_loss`: Scalar representing the average photometric error (L1 + SSIM) across all training views.
*   `d_loss`: Scalar representing the average depth estimation error across all training views.
*   `coverage_loss`: Scalar representing the coverage or visibility error.
*   `depth_lambda`: Hyperparameter weight for the depth loss.
*   `coverage_lambda`: Hyperparameter weight for the coverage loss.

**Outputs:**
*   `loss`: A single scalar float representing the total training loss.

**Data Structures:**
No specific complex data structures are required for the combination step itself; it operates on scalar values derived from previous loss computation stages.

**Steps:**
1.  Retrieve the pre-computed scalar values for `color_loss`, `d_loss`, and `coverage_loss` from their respective modules.
2.  Apply the hyperparameter weights `depth_lambda` and `coverage_lambda` to their respective loss terms.
3.  Sum the weighted terms to produce the final loss.

**Formula:**
$$
\text{loss} = \text{color\_loss} + \lambda_d \cdot \text{d\_loss} + \lambda_c \cdot \text{coverage\_loss}
$$

Where:
*   $\text{loss}$ is the final scalar loss.
*   $\text{color\_loss}$ is the sum of L1 and SSIM losses averaged over views.
*   $\lambda_d$ is `depth_lambda`.
*   $\text{d\_loss}$ is the depth loss averaged over views.
*   $\lambda_c$ is `coverage_lambda`.
*   $\text{coverage\_loss}$ is the coverage loss term.

**Parameters:**
*   `depth_lambda`: Weight for depth loss. Typical values are not specified in the sources.
*   `coverage_lambda`: Weight for coverage loss. Typical values are not specified in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scale Mismatch:** If `d_loss` or `coverage_loss` have significantly different magnitudes than `color_loss`, the hyperparameters must be tuned carefully to prevent one term from dominating the gradient.
*   **Missing Data:** If depth or coverage data is unavailable for a specific view, the corresponding loss term should be excluded or masked before averaging, though the specific masking logic is not detailed in the sources [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_total_loss(color_loss, d_loss, coverage_loss, depth_lambda, coverage_lambda):
    """
    Computes the combined training loss.
    
    Args:
        color_loss (float): Average photometric loss (L1 + SSIM).
        d_loss (float): Average depth loss.
        coverage_loss (float): Coverage/visibility loss.
        depth_lambda (float): Weight for depth loss.
        coverage_lambda (float): Weight for coverage loss.
        
    Returns:
        float: Total scalar loss.
    """
    # Compute weighted depth loss
    weighted_depth = depth_lambda * d_loss
    
    # Compute weighted coverage loss
    weighted_coverage = coverage_lambda * coverage_loss
    
    # Sum all components
    total_loss = color_loss + weighted_depth + weighted_coverage
    
    return total_loss
```

Extends the standard combination by replacing generic coverage with modality-specific terms for normals and sky, providing stricter geometric regularization.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The combination step is $O(1)$ relative to the number of pixels, as it operates on pre-aggregated scalar losses. The cost is dominated by the preceding loss computations.
*   **Strengths:** Simplifies the optimization landscape by providing a single gradient signal that balances multiple objectives.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters (`depth_lambda`, `coverage_lambda`) to balance competing objectives.
*   **When to use:** When training radiance fields where depth consistency and coverage are critical for reducing floating artifacts.

## Background
This loss combination strategy is derived from implementations in the SpaceCollectors/dust3r-3dgs repository [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the [Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting](4262-composite-image-loss-with-visibility-weighting.md) framework by integrating depth and coverage constraints to improve volumetric alignment.
