# Inverse Rendering Loss Function

Also called: Composite Loss for Neural SDF and Material Optimization, GeoSplatting Loss Functions, Training loss composition

## Overview
This method defines the composite objective function used to optimize neural Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) and physically-based rendering (PBR) material parameters from multi-view photometric images [S1, S2]. It addresses the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by combining image fidelity, structural similarity, geometric validity constraints, and material smoothness priors [S1, S2]. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly integrating SDF entropy regularization for geometry cleanup and light regularization for shadow disentanglement, often in conjunction with Gaussian Splatting or neural SDF representations [S2].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:** Ground truth multi-view images $I_{gt}$ and masks $M_{gt}$ [S2]. Predicted images $\hat{I}$ and masks $\hat{M}$ from the differentiable renderer [S2]. Neural SDF field $S(\mathbf{x})$ or scalar field $\zeta(\mathbf{x})$ [S1, S2]. Material attributes: diffuse albedo $\beta$, roughness $\alpha$, metalness $\kappa$ [S1, S2]. Lighting components: diffuse lighting $L_d$, specular lighting $L_s$ [S2]. Camera parameters and light positions (often co-located with camera) [S1].
**Outputs:** Scalar loss value $L$ for backpropagation [S1, S2].

### Data Structures
The method operates on standard tensor structures: Images are $H \times W \times 3$ (RGB) or $H \times W \times 4$ (RGBA) [S2]. The SDF is a scalar field, typically stored in a grid or queried via a neural network MLP [S1, S2]. Material attributes are stored per-vertex or per-Gaussian [S2].

### Formulas and Steps
The total loss is a weighted sum of photometric, geometric, and material/lighting terms [S1, S2]. Two primary formulations exist in the literature, detailed below [S1, S2].

#### Formulation 1: IRON-style (Neural SDF)
Used in [S1], this formulation focuses on neural SDF validity and roughness constraints.
$$ L = L_2(\text{pyramid}(\hat{I}), \text{pyramid}(I)) + (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)) + \lambda_1 \|\nabla_x S - 1\|_2^2 + \lambda_2 L_{roughness} $$
1. **Image Loss:** Combines multi-scale pixel error and structural similarity [S1].
   - $\text{pyramid}(\cdot)$ denotes a Gaussian pyramid [S1].
   - $L_2$ is the mean squared error between the predicted and ground truth pyramid levels [S1].
   - $\text{SSIM}$ is the Structural Similarity Index [S1].
2. **Eikonal Loss:** Enforces the SDF property that the gradient magnitude is 1 [S1].
   - $S$ is the neural SDF value at position $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
   - $\nabla_x S$ is the gradient of the SDF with respect to spatial coordinates [S1].
3. **Roughness Range Loss:** Encourages estimated roughness to stay below 0.5 [S1]. Specific formula not provided in the sources [S1]. [G] Typically implemented as a penalty if $\alpha > 0.5$.

#### Formulation 2: GeoSplatting-style (Gaussian Splatting/Tetrahedral)
Used in [S2], this formulation includes entropy regularization for geometry and light regularization for appearance.
$$ L = L_{photo} + \lambda_{sdf} L_{sdf} + \lambda_{smooth} L_{smooth} + \lambda_{light} L_{light} $$
1. **Photometric Loss:** [S2]
   $$ L_{photo} = \|I_{gt} - \hat{I}\|_1 + \lambda_{ssim} \text{SSIM}(I_{gt}, \hat{I}) + \lambda_{mask} \|\hat{M} - M_{gt}\|_2^2 $$
   - $\lambda_{ssim} = 0.2$ [S2].
   - $\lambda_{mask} = 5.0$ [S2].
2. **SDF Entropy Regularization:** Penalizes internal geometry and floaters by encouraging consistent signs of the SDF across edges [S2].
   $$ L_{sdf} = \sum_{(v_i, v_j) \in \mathcal{E}, \text{sgn}(S(v_i)) \neq \text{sgn}(S(v_j))} \left[ \mathcal{H}(S(v_i), \text{sgn}(S(v_j))) + \mathcal{H}(S(v_j), \text{sgn}(S(v_i))) \right] $$
   - $\mathcal{E}$ is the set of edges in the underlying mesh/tetrahedral structure [S2].
   - $\mathcal{H}(a, b)$ is the binary cross-entropy loss [S2].
   - $\text{sgn}(\cdot)$ returns the sign of the SDF value [S2].
3. **Smoothness Regularization:** Prevents high-frequency variations in material attributes [S2].
   $$ L_{smooth} = \|\beta - \beta'\|_1 + \|\alpha - \alpha'\|_1 + \|\kappa - \kappa'\|_1 $$
   - $\beta, \alpha, \kappa$ are albedo, roughness, and metalness at position $\mathbf{p}$ [S2].
   - $\beta', \alpha', \kappa'$ are the same attributes sampled at $\mathbf{p} + \Delta \mathbf{p}$, where $\Delta \mathbf{p} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and $\sigma = 0.01$ [S2].
4. **Light Regularization:** Disentangles shadows by assuming demodulated lighting is monochrome [S2].
   $$ L_{light} = \|Y(L_d + L_s) - V(I_{gt})\| $$
   - $Y(\mathbf{x}) = (x_r + x_g + x_b)/3$ is the luminance operator [S2].
   - $V(\mathbf{x}) = \max(x_r, x_g, x_b)$ is the HSV value component [S2].

### Parameters
- **IRON [S1]:** $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are loss weights. Values not explicitly given in the provided text [S1].
- **GeoSplatting [S2]:**
  - $\lambda_{ssim} = 0.2$
  - $\lambda_{mask} = 5.0$
  - $\lambda_{smooth} = 0.03$
  - $\lambda_{light} = 0.15$
  - $\lambda_{sdf}$: Starts at 0.2, linearly decreases to 0.01 by the midpoint of training [S2].
  - $\sigma = 0.01$ for smoothness sampling [S2].

### Pseudocode
```python
import math
import numpy as np

def compute_inverse_rendering_loss(I_gt, M_gt, I_pred, M_pred, sdf_field, material_field, L_d, L_s, config):
    lambda_ssim = config.get('lambda_ssim', 0.2)
    lambda_mask = config.get('lambda_mask', 5.0)
    lambda_smooth = config.get('lambda_smooth', 0.03)
    lambda_light = config.get('lambda_light', 0.15)
    lambda_sdf = config.get('lambda_sdf', 0.2)
    sigma = config.get('sigma', 0.01)
    edges = config.get('edges', [])

    # 1. Photometric Loss
    l1_loss = np.mean(np.abs(I_gt - I_pred))
    ssim_val = compute_ssim(I_gt, I_pred)
    mask_loss = np.mean((M_gt - M_pred) ** 2)
    L_photo = l1_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_val + lambda_mask * mask_loss

    # 2. SDF Entropy Regularization
    L_sdf = 0.0
    for v_i, v_j in edges:
        s_i = sdf_field.query(v_i)
        s_j = sdf_field.query(v_j)
        if math.copysign(1, s_i) != math.copysign(1, s_j):
            # Convert SDF values to probabilities for BCE [G]
            p_i = 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-s_i))
            p_j = 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-s_j))
            t_i = 1.0 if s_i > 0 else 0.0
            t_j = 1.0 if s_j > 0 else 0.0
            eps = 1e-7
            h_ij = -(t_i * math.log(p_j + eps) + (1 - t_i) * math.log(1 - p_j + eps))
            h_ji = -(t_j * math.log(p_i + eps) + (1 - t_j) * math.log(1 - p_i + eps))
            L_sdf += h_ij + h_ji

    # 3. Smoothness Regularization
    positions = material_field.get_positions()
    # Sample noise per iteration for differentiable training [G]
    deltas = np.random.normal(0, sigma, positions.shape)
    pos_noisy = positions + deltas
    beta, alpha, kappa = material_field.query(positions)
    beta_n, alpha_n, kappa_n = material_field.query(pos_noisy)
    L_smooth = np.mean(np.abs(beta - beta_n)) + np.mean(np.abs(alpha - alpha_n)) + np.mean(np.abs(kappa - kappa_n))

    # 4. Light Regularization
    lighting_sum = L_d + L_s
    luminance_light = np.mean(lighting_sum, axis=-1)
    value_gt = np.max(I_gt, axis=-1)
    L_light = np.mean(np.abs(luminance_light - value_gt))

    # 5. Total Loss
    L_total = L_photo + lambda_sdf * L_sdf + lambda_smooth * L_smooth + lambda_light * L_light
    return L_total

def compute_ssim(I1, I2, window_size=11):

    Returns a scalar in [0, 1]."""

    # Returns 0.95 for demonstration; replace with actual differentiable kernel.
    return 0.95
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **SDF Sign Ambiguity:** The entropy loss requires consistent sign interpretation [S2]. If the SDF is not properly initialized or regularized, signs may flip arbitrarily, leading to unstable gradients [S2].
- **Light Regularization Assumption:** The light regularization assumes demodulated lighting is monochrome [S2]. This may fail for scenes with strong colored shadows or non-Lambertian lighting effects [S2].
- **Smoothness Noise:** The smoothness term relies on random perturbations [S2]. If $\sigma$ is too large, it may oversmooth fine details; if too small, it may not regularize high-frequency noise effectively [S2].
- **Mask Loss:** The mask loss weight $\lambda_{mask}=5.0$ is high, indicating that accurate alpha blending is critical for the photometric term [S2].

## Subtopics

### Composite RGB, Eikonal, and Curvature Loss
Used in [S3], this variant optimizes spatial mask and SDF networks jointly in an end-to-end manner for surface reconstruction [S3]. It combines RGB reconstruction, eikonal regularization, and mean curvature smoothing [S3].
$$ \mathcal{L} = L_{rgb} + w_{eik} L_{eik} + w_{curv} L_{curv} $$
- $L_{rgb} = \|\hat{\mathbf{I}} - \mathbf{I}\|_1$ [S3].
- $L_{eik} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\|\nabla SDF(\mathbf{x}_i)\|_2 - 1)^2$ [S3].
- $L_{curv} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |\nabla^2 SDF(\mathbf{x}_i)|$, calculated using a discrete Laplacian [S3].
- Weights: $w_{eik} = 0.1$, $w_{curv} = 5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S3].

### Strengths and Limitations
- **Strengths:** Combines multiple constraints to stabilize the ill-posed inverse rendering problem [S1, S2]. Entropy regularization effectively removes floaters and internal geometry [S2]. Light regularization helps disentangle shadows from albedo [S2].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of loss weights ($\lambda$ values) [S1, S2]. The SDF entropy term requires access to geometric connectivity (edges), which may not be available in all neural SDF implementations (e.g., pure grid-based without mesh extraction) [S2]. The roughness range loss formula is not explicitly defined in [S1], potentially leading to implementation ambiguity [S1].

### When to Use
- Use when optimizing neural SDFs or Gaussian Splatting attributes for physically-based inverse rendering [S1, S2].
- Use when floaters or internal geometry artifacts are prevalent [S2].
- Use when shadow disentanglement is required [S2].

### When Not to Use
- Use [MPF Training Objective](10247-mpf-training-objective.md) if the focus is purely on multi-view photometric consistency without explicit SDF/material regularization.
- Use simpler L2/SSIM losses if computational resources are extremely limited and geometry/material priors are not needed.

## Background

### Origin
- **IRON [S1]:** Introduced in "IRON: Inverse Rendering by Optimizing Neural SDFs and Materials from Photometric Images" (CVPR 2022). It combines neural SDFs with PBR materials and uses Eikonal and roughness losses [S1].
- **GeoSplatting [S2]:** Introduced in "GeoSplatting: Towards Geometry Guided Gaussian Splatting for Physically-Based Inverse Rendering" (ICCV 2025). It extends Gaussian Splatting with geometry guidance and introduces entropy, smoothness, and light regularization [S2].

### History and Development
- Inverse rendering has evolved from mesh-based methods to neural representations (NeRF, SDFs) and now to Gaussian Splatting [S1, S2].
- The composite loss function reflects the trend of combining multiple regularization terms to handle the ambiguities of inverse rendering, such as the separation of albedo, shading, and geometry [S1, S2].
