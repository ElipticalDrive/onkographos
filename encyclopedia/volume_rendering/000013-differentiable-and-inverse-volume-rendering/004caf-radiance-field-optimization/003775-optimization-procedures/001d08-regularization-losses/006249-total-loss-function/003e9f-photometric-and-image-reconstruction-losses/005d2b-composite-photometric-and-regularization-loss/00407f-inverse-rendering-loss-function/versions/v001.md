# Inverse Rendering Loss Function

Also called: Composite Loss for Neural SDF and Material Optimization, GeoSplatting Loss Functions, Training loss composition

## Overview
This method defines the composite objective function used to optimize neural Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) and physically-based rendering (PBR) material parameters from multi-view photometric images. It addresses the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by combining image fidelity, structural similarity, geometric validity constraints, and material smoothness priors. It differs from sibling methods by explicitly integrating SDF entropy regularization for geometry cleanup and light regularization for shadow disentanglement, often in conjunction with Gaussian Splatting or neural SDF representations.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Ground truth multi-view images $I_{gt}$ and masks $M_{gt}$.
- Predicted images $\hat{I}$ and masks $\hat{M}$ from the differentiable renderer.
- Neural SDF field $S(\mathbf{x})$ or scalar field $\zeta(\mathbf{x})$.
- Material attributes: diffuse albedo $\beta$, roughness $\alpha$, metalness $\kappa$ (or similar parameterizations).
- Lighting components: diffuse lighting $L_d$, specular lighting $L_s$.
- Camera parameters and light positions (often co-located with camera).

**Outputs:**
- Scalar loss value $L$ for backpropagation.

### Data Structures
The method operates on standard tensor structures:
- Images: $H \times W \times 3$ (RGB) or $H \times W \times 4$ (RGBA).
- SDF: Scalar field, typically stored in a grid (e.g., VDB, Tetrahedral mesh) or queried via a neural network MLP.
- Materials: Per-vertex or per-Gaussian attributes.

### Formulas and Steps

The total loss is a weighted sum of photometric, geometric, and material/lighting terms. Two primary formulations exist in the literature, detailed below.

#### Formulation 1: IRON-style (Neural SDF)
Used in [S1], this formulation focuses on neural SDF validity and roughness constraints.

$$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_1 L_{eikonal} + \lambda_2 L_{roughness} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($L_{img}$):** Combines multi-scale pixel error and structural similarity.
    $$ L_{img} = L_2(\text{pyramid}(\hat{I}), \text{pyramid}(I)) + (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)) $$
    - $\text{pyramid}(\cdot)$ denotes a Gaussian pyramid.
    - $L_2$ is the mean squared error between the predicted and ground truth pyramid levels.
    - $\text{SSIM}$ is the Structural Similarity Index.

2.  **Eikonal Loss ($L_{eikonal}$):** Enforces the SDF property that the gradient magnitude is 1.
    $$ L_{eikonal} = \|\nabla_x S - 1\|_2^2 $$
    - $S$ is the neural SDF value at position $\mathbf{x}$.
    - $\nabla_x S$ is the gradient of the SDF with respect to spatial coordinates.

3.  **Roughness Range Loss ($L_{roughness}$):** Encourages estimated roughness to stay below 0.5.
    - Specific formula not provided in [S1], referred to as Eq. 13. [G] Typically implemented as a clamp or penalty if $\alpha > 0.5$.

#### Formulation 2: GeoSplatting-style (Gaussian Splatting/Tetrahedral)
Used in [S2], this formulation includes entropy regularization for geometry and light regularization for appearance.

$$ L = L_{photo} + \lambda_{sdf} L_{sdf} + \lambda_{smooth} L_{smooth} + \lambda_{light} L_{light} $$

1.  **Photometric Loss ($L_{photo}$):**
    $$ L_{photo} = \|I_{gt} - \hat{I}\|_1 + \lambda_{ssim} \text{SSIM}(I_{gt}, \hat{I}) + \lambda_{mask} \|\hat{M} - M_{gt}\|_2^2 $$
    - $\lambda_{ssim} = 0.2$.
    - $\lambda_{mask} = 5.0$.

2.  **SDF Entropy Regularization ($L_{sdf}$):** Penalizes internal geometry and floaters by encouraging consistent signs of the SDF across edges.
    $$ L_{sdf} = \sum_{(v_i, v_j) \in \mathcal{E}, \text{sgn}(S(v_i)) \neq \text{sgn}(S(v_j))} \left[ \mathcal{H}(S(v_i), \text{sgn}(S(v_j))) + \mathcal{H}(S(v_j), \text{sgn}(S(v_i))) \right] $$
    - $\mathcal{E}$ is the set of edges in the underlying mesh/tetrahedral structure.
    - $\mathcal{H}(a, b)$ is the binary cross-entropy loss.
    - $\text{sgn}(\cdot)$ returns the sign of the SDF value.

3.  **Smoothness Regularization ($L_{smooth}$):** Prevents high-frequency variations in material attributes.
    $$ L_{smooth} = \|\beta - \beta'\|_1 + \|\alpha - \alpha'\|_1 + \|\kappa - \kappa'\|_1 $$
    - $\beta, \alpha, \kappa$ are albedo, roughness, and metalness at position $\mathbf{p}$.
    - $\beta', \alpha', \kappa'$ are the same attributes sampled at $\mathbf{p} + \Delta \mathbf{p}$, where $\Delta \mathbf{p} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and $\sigma = 0.01$.

4.  **Light Regularization ($L_{light}$):** Disentangles shadows by assuming demodulated lighting is monochrome.
    $$ L_{light} = \|Y(L_d + L_s) - V(I_{gt})\| $$
    - $Y(\mathbf{x}) = (x_r + x_g + x_b)/3$ is the luminance operator.
    - $V(\mathbf{x}) = \max(x_r, x_g, x_b)$ is the HSV value component.

### Parameters
- **IRON [S1]:** $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are loss weights. Values not explicitly given in the provided text.
- **GeoSplatting [S2]:**
  - $\lambda_{ssim} = 0.2$
  - $\lambda_{mask} = 5.0$
  - $\lambda_{smooth} = 0.03$
  - $\lambda_{light} = 0.15$
  - $\lambda_{sdf}$: Starts at 0.2, linearly decreases to 0.01 by the midpoint of training.
  - $\sigma = 0.01$ for smoothness sampling.

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_inverse_rendering_loss(
    I_gt, M_gt, I_pred, M_pred, 
    sdf_field, material_field, 
    L_d, L_s, 
    config
):
    """
    Computes the composite inverse rendering loss.
    Supports both IRON-style and GeoSplatting-style formulations.
    """
    lambda_ssim = config.get('lambda_ssim', 0.2)
    lambda_mask = config.get('lambda_mask', 5.0)
    lambda_smooth = config.get('lambda_smooth', 0.03)
    lambda_light = config.get('lambda_light', 0.15)
    lambda_sdf = config.get('lambda_sdf', 0.2) # Should be decayed by epoch
    sigma = config.get('sigma', 0.01)
    
    # 1. Photometric Loss
    l1_loss = mean_absolute_error(I_gt, I_pred)
    ssim_loss = 1.0 - structural_similarity(I_gt, I_pred)
    mask_loss = mean_squared_error(M_gt, M_pred)
    
    L_photo = l1_loss + lambda_ssim * ssim_loss + lambda_mask * mask_loss
    
    # 2. SDF Entropy Regularization (GeoSplatting style)
    # Requires access to mesh edges or tetrahedral connectivity
    L_sdf = 0.0
    edges = get_edges(sdf_field) # e.g., from FlexiCubes or mesh
    for v_i, v_j in edges:
        s_i = sdf_field.query(v_i)
        s_j = sdf_field.query(v_j)
        
        # Only compute if signs differ
        if sign(s_i) != sign(s_j):
            # Binary cross entropy approximation for SDF values
            # H(p, q) = - [q * log(p) + (1-q) * log(1-p)]
            # Here, target is sign(s_j) which is 1 or -1. 
            # Map to [0,1] for BCE: target = (sign + 1) / 2
            target_j = (sign(s_j) + 1) / 2
            target_i = (sign(s_i) + 1) / 2
            
            # Clamp SDF values to [0,1] for log stability if treating as probability
            # Or use sigmoid output if SDF is normalized. 
            # Note: Standard SDF is not probability. 
            # [S2] implies using H(S(v), sgn(S(v))). 
            # Implementation detail: Often S(v) is passed through sigmoid or 
            # treated as logit. Assuming S(v) is raw SDF, we might need 
            # a sigmoid activation for the BCE input.
            prob_i = sigmoid(s_i)
            prob_j = sigmoid(s_j)
            
            L_sdf += binary_cross_entropy(prob_i, target_j)
            L_sdf += binary_cross_entropy(prob_j, target_i)
            
    # 3. Smoothness Regularization
    # Sample neighbors with Gaussian noise
    positions = get_material_positions(material_field)
    deltas = random_gaussian(positions.shape, sigma)
    pos_noisy = positions + deltas
    
    beta, alpha, kappa = material_field.query(positions)
    beta_n, alpha_n, kappa_n = material_field.query(pos_noisy)
    
    L_smooth = mean_absolute_error(beta, beta_n) + \
               mean_absolute_error(alpha, alpha_n) + \
               mean_absolute_error(kappa, kappa_n)
               
    # 4. Light Regularization
    # Y(x) = mean(x), V(x) = max(x)
    lighting_sum = L_d + L_s
    luminance_light = mean(lighting_sum, axis=-1) # (H, W)
    value_gt = max(I_gt, axis=-1) # (H, W)
    
    # Reshape for broadcasting if necessary
    L_light = mean_absolute_error(luminance_light, value_gt)
    
    # 5. Total Loss
    L_total = L_photo + lambda_sdf * L_sdf + lambda_smooth * L_smooth + lambda_light * L_light
    
    return L_total
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **SDF Sign Ambiguity:** The entropy loss requires consistent sign interpretation. If the SDF is not properly initialized or regularized, signs may flip arbitrarily, leading to unstable gradients.
- **Light Regularization Assumption:** The light regularization assumes demodulated lighting is monochrome. This may fail for scenes with strong colored shadows or non-Lambertian lighting effects.
- **Smoothness Noise:** The smoothness term relies on random perturbations. If $\sigma$ is too large, it may oversmooth fine details; if too small, it may not regularize high-frequency noise effectively.
- **Mask Loss:** The mask loss weight $\lambda_{mask}=5.0$ is high, indicating that accurate alpha blending is critical for the photometric term.

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
- **Photometric Loss:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels. SSIM adds overhead due to local window computations.
- **SDF Entropy:** $O(E)$ where $E$ is the number of edges in the geometric representation.
- **Smoothness:** $O(V)$ where $V$ is the number of material points (Gaussians or vertices). Requires two additional field queries per point.
- **Light Regularization:** $O(N)$ for luminance/value computation.

### Measured Performance
- Specific performance metrics (FPS, convergence time) are not provided in the sources.
- The method is designed for offline optimization or slow training stages, as it involves multiple differentiable passes (SDF gradient, material sampling, light computation).

### Strengths and Limitations
- **Strengths:**
  - Combines multiple constraints to stabilize the ill-posed inverse rendering problem.
  - Entropy regularization effectively removes floaters and internal geometry.
  - Light regularization helps disentangle shadows from albedo.
- **Limitations:**
  - Requires careful tuning of loss weights ($\lambda$ values).
  - The SDF entropy term requires access to geometric connectivity (edges), which may not be available in all neural SDF implementations (e.g., pure grid-based without mesh extraction).
  - The roughness range loss formula is not explicitly defined in [S1], potentially leading to implementation ambiguity.

### When to Use
- Use when optimizing neural SDFs or Gaussian Splatting attributes for physically-based inverse rendering.
- Use when floaters or internal geometry artifacts are prevalent.
- Use when shadow disentanglement is required.

### When Not to Use
- Use [MPF Training Objective](10247-mpf-training-objective.md) if the focus is purely on multi-view photometric consistency without explicit SDF/material regularization.
- Use simpler L2/SSIM losses if computational resources are extremely limited and geometry/material priors are not needed.

## Background

### Origin
- **IRON [S1]:** Introduced in "IRON: Inverse Rendering by Optimizing Neural SDFs and Materials from Photometric Images" (CVPR 2022). It combines neural SDFs with PBR materials and uses Eikonal and roughness losses.
- **GeoSplatting [S2]:** Introduced in "GeoSplatting: Towards Geometry Guided Gaussian Splatting for Physically-Based Inverse Rendering" (ICCV 2025). It extends Gaussian Splatting with geometry guidance and introduces entropy, smoothness, and light regularization.

### History and Development
- Inverse rendering has evolved from mesh-based methods to neural representations (NeRF, SDFs) and now to Gaussian Splatting.
- The composite loss function reflects the trend of combining multiple regularization terms to handle the ambiguities of inverse rendering, such as the separation of albedo, shading, and geometry.
