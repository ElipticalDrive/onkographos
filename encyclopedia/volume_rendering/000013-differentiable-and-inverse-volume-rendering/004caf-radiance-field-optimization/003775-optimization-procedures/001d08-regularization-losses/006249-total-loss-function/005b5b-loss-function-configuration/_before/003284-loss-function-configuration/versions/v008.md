# Loss Function Configuration

Also called: Loss Weighting Scheme, Loss function parameter configuration, Removing Repulsion Loss, Total Training Loss, Training Loss Weights, w/o Lrep

## Overview
Loss function configuration defines the composite objective and hyperparameter schedule used to train differentiable radiance fields, balancing geometric reconstruction fidelity with perceptual and semantic constraints. It differs from simple reconstruction objectives by integrating high-level perceptual metrics (SSIM, LPIPS) and specific regularization terms (e.g., repulsion, sky, palette sparsity) to guide the network toward desired visual outputs, often varying weights across training stages or scene characteristics.

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Rendered images $\hat{I}$ and ground-truth images $I$.
- Predicted geometric fields (density $\sigma$, occupancy, normals $\mathbf{n}$, depth $d$).
- Optional masks (transparent mask, sky label, foreground/background masks).
- Training iteration count $t$ and total iterations $T$.
- Scene-specific flags (e.g., large motion, static vs. dynamic).

**Outputs:**
- Scalar total loss $\mathcal{L}$ and its components for backpropagation.
- Updated model parameters via gradient descent.

### Data Structures
The loss configuration is typically stored as a dictionary or object containing:
- `weights`: A mapping of loss term names to scalar coefficients (e.g., `{"w_ptc": 0.1}`).
- `schedules`: Optional step functions or thresholds for dynamic weight adjustment (e.g., hybrid gradient switching).
- `flags`: Boolean or categorical flags for conditional logic (e.g., `is_large_motion`, `use_nerfacto`).

### Formulas and Steps
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of multiple components. The specific formulation depends on the variant (see Subtopics). Below are the primary configurations cited.

#### 1. Joint Reflection-Transmission Model (RT-Splatting)
The total loss combines appearance, normal consistency, and transparent mask regularization [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{img}} + \lambda_{\text{n}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{n}} + \lambda_{\text{mask}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}}
$$
Where:
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{img}}$ is the appearance loss:
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{img}} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_{1} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} + \lambda_{\text{perc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{perc}}
  $$
  - $\mathcal{L}_{1}$: L1 loss between rendered and ground-truth images.
  - $\mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}}$: D-SSIM loss [S1, unofficial].
  - $\mathcal{L}_{\text{perc}}$: Perceptual loss from EnvGS [S1, unofficial].
  - $\lambda$: D-SSIM weight.
  - $\lambda_{\text{perc}}$: Perceptual loss weight.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{n}}$: Normal consistency loss.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}}$: Transparent mask regularization loss.

**Parameters [S1, unofficial]:**
- $\lambda = 0.2$
- $\lambda_{\text{perc}} = 0.01$
- $\lambda_{\text{n}} = 0.05$
- $\lambda_{\text{mask}} = 0.01$

**Density Control Strategy [S1, unofficial]:**
- Interleaved reset schedule: Pruning and resetting occur every 1,500 iterations, alternating between geometric occupancy $\sigma$ and optical opacity $\alpha$.
- Pruning is based on geometric occupancy $\sigma$ to prevent removal of semi-transparent structures.

#### 2. Neural Deformable Voxel Grid (Coarse-to-Fine)
The overall loss for dynamic view synthesis [S2]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_{\text{ptc}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}} + w_{\text{bg}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}} + w_{d\_norm} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_norm} + w_{d\_tv} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_tv}
$$
Where:
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$: Photometric loss.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}}$: Point consistency loss.
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}$: Background loss.
- $\mathcal{L}_{d\_norm}$: Deformation norm regularization.
- $\mathcal{L}_{d\_tv}$: Total variation loss on deformation.

**Parameters [S2]:**
- **Coarse Stage:** $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.1$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.01$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.1$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$.
- **Fine Stage:** $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.01$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.001$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.01$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$.
- **Large Motion Scenes** ('Hell Warrior', 'Jumping Jacks', 'T-Rex'): $w_{d\_norm}$ is reduced to $0.01$ (coarse) and $0.001$ (fine).

#### 3. SplatVoxel Feed-Forward Networks
The loss function for training multi-view and voxel transformers [S6]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{LPIPS}}(\hat{I}, I)
$$
**Parameters [S6]:**
- **Multi-view Transformer:** $\lambda = 0.5$.
- **Voxel Transformer:** $\lambda = 4.0$.
- **Batch Size:** 128.
- **Total Iterations:** 300K.

#### 4. Hybrid Gradients (Differentiable Rendering with RGBXY)
A strategy combining RGBXY derivatives and standard derivatives [S5]:
- **Step 1:** Use RGBXY derivatives (Eq. 15 in [S5]) for the first 75% of optimization iterations.
- **Step 2:** Switch to standard derivatives (Eq. 5 in [S5]) for the last 25% of optimization iterations.

#### 5. Sky Loss (SDFStudio/NeRF)
Conditional loss for sky regions [S7, unofficial]:
- Compute `sky_label` as $1.0 - \text{batch}['\text{sky}']$ (converted to float, moved to device).
- If `use_nerfacto`:
  - Compute `sky_loss` = BCE(sum of `density_field_weights` over dim=1, clipped to $[10^{-3}, 1-10^{-3}]$, `sky_label`) $\times 0.01$.
- If occupancy field:
  - Compute `osky_loss` = BCE(sum of `occupancy_field_weights` over dim=1, clipped to $[10^{-3}, 1-10^{-3}]$, `sky_label`) $\times 0.01$.

#### 6. Color Palette Sparsity Loss (LAENeRF)
Measures sparsity of learned color palette $\hat{P}$ [S8]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sp}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\hat{P}}} \hat{w}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\hat{P}}} \hat{w}_i^2} - 1
$$
Where $\hat{w}_i$ is the per-palette weight and $N_{\hat{P}}$ is the number of palettes.

#### 7. Shape Part Addition Loss (Editing Conditional Radiance Fields)
Loss for adding a local part to a shape instance [S9]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{add}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}
$$
Where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}$ is the reconstruction loss on the composite image (foreground from new instance, background from original) and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$ is a regularization term (e.g., weight difference penalty).
**Parameters [S9]:**
- $\lambda_{\text{reg}} = 10$.
- Optimizer: Adam, learning rate $10^{-2}$.

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_total_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, geometry, masks, iteration, total_iterations, config):
    """
    Computes the total loss based on the configured variant.
    """
    loss = 0.0
    
    # Determine variant based on config flags or context
    if config.variant == "rt_splatting":
        # 1. Appearance Loss
        l1 = l1_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        d_ssim = d_ssim_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        perc = perceptual_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        
        lambda_d_ssim = 0.2
        lambda_perc = 0.01
        l_img = (1 - lambda_d_ssim) * l1 + lambda_d_ssim * d_ssim + lambda_perc * perc
        
        # 2. Normal Consistency
        l_n = normal_consistency_loss(geometry.normals, geometry.gt_normals)
        lambda_n = 0.05
        
        # 3. Transparent Mask Regularization
        l_mask = transparent_mask_loss(geometry.opacity, masks.transparent)
        lambda_mask = 0.01
        
        loss = l_img + lambda_n * l_n + lambda_mask * l_mask
        
    elif config.variant == "deformable_voxel":
        # Photometric and auxiliary losses
        l_photo = mse_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        l_ptc = point_consistency_loss(geometry.points, geometry.gt_points)
        l_bg = background_loss(rendered_image, masks.background)
        l_d_norm = deformation_norm_loss(geometry.deformation)
        l_d_tv = total_variation_loss(geometry.deformation)
        
        # Stage-specific weights
        if config.stage == "coarse":
            w_ptc = 0.1
            w_bg = 0.01
            w_d_norm = 0.1
            w_d_tv = 1.0
        else: # fine
            w_ptc = 0.01
            w_bg = 0.001
            w_d_norm = 0.01
            w_d_tv = 1.0
            
        # Large motion adjustment
        if config.is_large_motion:
            w_d_norm = 0.01 if config.stage == "coarse" else 0.001
            
        loss = l_photo + w_ptc * l_ptc + w_bg * l_bg + w_d_norm * l_d_norm + w_d_tv * l_d_tv
        
    elif config.variant == "splatvoxel":
        l_mse = mse_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        l_lpips = lpips_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        
        if config.transformer_type == "multi_view":
            lambda_lpips = 0.5
        else: # voxel
            lambda_lpips = 4.0
            
        loss = l_mse + lambda_lpips * l_lpips
        
    elif config.variant == "hybrid_gradients":
        # This variant affects the gradient computation, not just the loss value.
        # The loss value itself is typically standard photometric/perceptual.
        # However, the derivative calculation changes based on iteration.
        if iteration < 0.75 * total_iterations:
            gradients = compute_rgbxy_gradients(rendered_image, gt_image)
        else:
            gradients = compute_standard_gradients(rendered_image, gt_image)
        # Loss value is standard, but gradients are hybrid
        loss = mse_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        
    elif config.variant == "sky_loss":
        # Add sky loss to existing loss
        sky_label = 1.0 - masks.sky.float()
        if config.use_nerfacto:
            density_weights = geometry.density_field_weights.sum(dim=1)
            density_weights = torch.clamp(density_weights, 1e-3, 1-1e-3)
            sky_loss = bce_loss(density_weights, sky_label) * 0.01
        else:
            occ_weights = geometry.occupancy_field_weights.sum(dim=1)
            occ_weights = torch.clamp(occ_weights, 1e-3, 1-1e-3)
            sky_loss = bce_loss(occ_weights, sky_label) * 0.01
        loss += sky_loss
        
    elif config.variant == "palette_sparsity":
        # Add palette sparsity loss
        l_sp = palette_sparsity_loss(geometry.palette_weights)
        loss += l_sp
        
    elif config.variant == "shape_addition":
        # Composite image rendering
        composite_image = render_composite(geometry, masks, config.edit_params)
        l_rec = reconstruction_loss(composite_image, gt_image, masks.foreground, masks.background)
        l_reg = regularization_loss(geometry.weights)
        lambda_reg = 10.0
        loss = l_rec + lambda_reg * l_reg
        
    return loss
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Repulsion Loss Removal:** In sphere-cloud guided training, removing the repulsion loss ($L_{rep}$) causes spheres to clump together instead of covering the object, leading to poor reconstructions [S3].
- **Hybrid Gradient Alignment:** When initial and target shapes are already well-aligned, the approximated optimal transport method for RGBXY derivatives may lead to inaccurate matching, making them less effective in late stages [S5].
- **MS-SSIM Defects:** Using MS-SSIM loss instead of MSE for defocus-engineered holography optimization resulted in unclear effects and defects in the synthesized images [S4].
- **Defocus Blur Weighting:** In defocus-blur considered depth maps, out-of-focus images (20) significantly outnumber in-focus images (1). The weight $\beta$ must be set to 20 to compensate and ensure in-focus images have similar or higher influence [S4].
- **Gamma Parameter:** $\gamma$ should be fixed to 40 to avoid simultaneous focusing of an object at two different focal planes [S4].

## Subtopics

### [Overall Training Objective](13444-overall-training-objective.md)
The Overall Training Objective defines the composite loss function and optimization schedule used to train differentiable radiance fields, specifically in the context of text-driven stylization and multi-baseline reconstruction. It combines geometric reconstruction fidelity with perceptual and semantic constraints to guide the neural network toward the desired visual output. It differs from simple reconstruction objectives by integrating high-level perceptual metrics (SSIM, LPIPS) and, in stylization variants, contrastive text-image alignment losses.

## Operating characteristics
- **Complexity:** The computational overhead of loss configuration is generally low compared to the rendering and gradient computation steps, as it involves simple weighted sums and element-wise operations on tensors.
- **Performance Impact:** Proper weighting is critical for convergence. Incorrect weights (e.g., too high perceptual loss) can lead to artifacts or slow convergence. The two-stage strategy (SplatVoxel) allows for faster initial convergence with the multi-view transformer before fine-tuning [S6].
- **Scalability:** The hybrid gradient strategy [S5] improves optimization quality without increasing memory footprint, though it requires tracking iteration counts.
- **Strengths:** Flexibility to incorporate diverse constraints (normal, mask, sky, palette) allows for specialized training regimes (e.g., relighting, stylization, editing).
- **Limitations:** Hyperparameter sensitivity is high. For example, the gating strength $k$ in RT-Splatting peaks at $k=4$ [S1, unofficial], and large motion scenes require specific weight adjustments [S2].

## Background
Loss function configuration has evolved alongside differentiable rendering techniques. Early methods relied primarily on photometric (L1/MSE) losses. As radiance fields became more complex (incorporating normals, depth, and semantic information), auxiliary losses were introduced. The RT-Splatting method [S1, unofficial] (2025) explicitly combines appearance, normal, and mask losses for joint reflection-transmission modeling. The Neural Deformable Voxel Grid [S2] (2023) introduced stage-specific weighting for dynamic scenes. The SplatVoxel method [S6] (2025) utilizes a two-stage photometric and perceptual training strategy for feed-forward networks. The Hybrid Gradients approach [S5] (2022) addresses the limitations of standard derivatives in optimal transport-based rendering.
