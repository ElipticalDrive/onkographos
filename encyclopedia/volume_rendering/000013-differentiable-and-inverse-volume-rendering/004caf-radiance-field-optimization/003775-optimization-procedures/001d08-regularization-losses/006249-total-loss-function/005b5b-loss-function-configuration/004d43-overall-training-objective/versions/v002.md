# Overall Training Objective

Also called: Total Loss Function for Radiance Field Stylization

## Overview
The overall training objective defines the composite loss function used to optimize neural radiance fields for text-driven stylization and conditional image translation. It addresses the problem of aligning rendered images with target style descriptions while preserving geometric structure. It differs from standard reconstruction objectives by incorporating adversarial, perceptual, and contrastive terms to guide the latent space, and from pure inverse rendering by focusing on style transfer rather than physical property recovery.

## How it works
The optimization minimizes a weighted sum of reconstruction, adversarial, perceptual, and contrastive losses. The process is divided into two stages: reconstruction and stylization.

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Source images (or videos), text descriptions (positive and negative samples), camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized radiance field parameters (density $\sigma$, color $c$).

**Data Structures**
*   **Radiance Field:** Implicit representation (e.g., VolSDF) mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color.
*   **Text Embeddings:** Vector representations of positive and negative text prompts.

**Parameters**
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_g = 0.2$, $\lambda_l = 0.1$, $\lambda_p = 2.0$, $\lambda_r = 0.1$ [S1].
*   **Sampling:** 192 points per ray [S1].
*   **Patch Size:** 1/10 of the original input image for local contrastive loss [S1].
*   **Negative Samples:** ~200 manually collected text descriptions (e.g., "Zombie", "Tolkien elf") [S1].

**Steps**
1.  **Reconstruction Stage:**
    *   Initialize the base NeRF model (e.g., VolSDF) [S1].
    *   Sample 192 points along each ray [S1].
    *   Optimize the model for 6 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0005 [S1].
    *   Minimize the reconstruction loss to ensure geometric fidelity.

2.  **Stylization Stage:**
    *   Optimize the model for 4 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 [S1].
    *   Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{total}$:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \lambda_g \mathcal{L}_g + \lambda_l \mathcal{L}_l + \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_p + \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_r $$
        where $\mathcal{L}_g$ is the adversarial loss, $\mathcal{L}_l$ is the local contrastive loss, $\mathcal{L}_p$ is the perceptual loss, and $\mathcal{L}_r$ is the reconstruction loss [S1].
    *   Update radiance field parameters to minimize $\mathcal{L}_{total}$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Incomplete Renderings:** Deactivating the conditional adversarial loss leads to incomplete renderings and loss of style coherence [S2].
*   **Memory Consumption:** Rendering high-resolution images requires significant memory; patch-level processing is used to mitigate this [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Composite Training Loss](13833-composite-training-loss.md)
Composite Training Loss combines multiple perceptual and pixel-wise metrics to supervise the optimization of radiance fields. It addresses the problem of generating high-fidelity, visually consistent rendered images by penalizing errors in color accuracy, structural similarity, and high-level perceptual features. It differs from single-metric approaches (like pure L1 or MSE) by balancing local pixel fidelity with global structural integrity and perceptual quality, and from complex inverse rendering objectives by excluding explicit physical priors like visibility or normal consistency unless specified by a variant.

### No Conditional Adversarial Objective
This variant involves deactivating the conditional adversarial loss and retraining the model. The absence of this loss results in renderings that are incomplete and lack clear style definition, demonstrating the necessity of the adversarial component for effective stylization [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dependent on the number of epochs (6 for reconstruction, 4 for stylization) and points per ray (192) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High due to 3D grid or MLP storage; patch-level rendering reduces memory consumption [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Effective text-driven stylization; preserves geometry while transferring style.
*   **Limitations:** Requires manual collection of negative samples; sensitive to loss weight tuning.
*   **When to Use:** Text-guided neural radiance field stylization.
*   **When Not to Use:** When adversarial stability is critical or when negative samples are unavailable.

## Background
The method originates from NeRF-Art, which introduces text-driven neural radiance fields stylization [S1]. It builds on VolSDF as the base NeRF model [S1]. The approach is also related to Pix2NeRF, which explores conditional GANs for single-image to NeRF translation, highlighting the importance of adversarial objectives [S2].
