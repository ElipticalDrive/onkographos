# DMF Training Losses

Also called: DMF Losses, DMF Regularization Terms

## Overview
The DMF training losses constitute the regularization and supervision terms used to optimize the neural implicit representation of Medial Fields, specifically the Signed Distance Function (SDF) $\Phi$ and the medial field. While the parent [Total Loss Function for Neural Scene Reconstruction](15309-total-loss-function-for-neural-scene-reconstruction.md) combines seven components for general scene reconstruction, the DMF losses focus on stabilizing the implicit surface representation, enforcing Eikonal constraints, and preventing interference from Fourier feature embeddings. They are distinct from standard NeRF losses by their heavy reliance on surface-augmented volume sampling and specific regularizers for medial field consistency.

## How it works
The method optimizes a neural network predicting $\Phi(x)$ and associated heads by minimizing a weighted sum of six loss terms. The optimization uses the Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A ground-truth surface mesh $\partial O$ (providing triangle normals), a bounding box defining the volume $\mathbb{R}^d$, and a neural network parameterized by weights $\theta$.
*   **Output:** Optimized network weights $\theta$ that minimize the total loss.

**Data Structures and Sampling:**
Two distinct sampling strategies are required because some losses depend on surface points ($x \sim \partial O$) and others on volume points ($x \sim \mathbb{R}^d$) [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Surface Samples:** Points are drawn uniformly from the input mesh $\partial O$. The ground-truth normal $\nabla\Phi_{GT}(x)$ is obtained directly from the triangle normals of the mesh at the projected sample location [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Volume Samples:** To generate points in the volume $\mathbb{R}^d$, surface samples are augmented with an isotropic Gaussian offset. The standard deviation $\sigma$ is set to half the bounding box diagonal [S1, unofficial].

**Loss Formulas:**
The total loss is a weighted summation. The individual components are:

1.  **Surface Reconstruction Loss ($L_{surface}$):** Encourages the SDF to be zero at the ground-truth surface [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{surface} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \partial O} [\Phi(x)^2] $$

2.  **Normal Reconstruction Loss ($L_{normal}$):** Aligns the predicted gradient with the mesh normals [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{normal} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \partial O} [(\nabla\Phi(x) - \nabla\Phi_{GT}(x))^2] $$

3.  **Eikonal Loss ($L_{Eikonal}$):** Enforces the Eikonal equation $||\nabla\Phi(x)|| = 1$ in the volume, ensuring $\Phi$ behaves as a proper SDF [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{Eikonal} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{R}^d} [(||\nabla\Phi(x)|| - 1)^2] $$

4.  **Min-Surface Regularizer ($L_{minsurface}$):** Prevents Fourier features from interfering with implicit SDF training by penalizing non-zero SDF values in the volume [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{minsurface} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{R}^d} [e^{-100 |\Phi(x)|}] $$

5.  **Curvature Regularizer ($L_{curvature}$):** A regularizer for curvature, scheduled during training [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{curvature} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{R}^d} \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \nabla\Phi(x + t\nabla\Phi(x)) \right] $$
    *Note: The exact implementation of the derivative with respect to $t$ is not explicitly detailed in the source beyond the formula notation [S1, unofficial].*

6.  **Gradient Consistency Loss ($L_{gradient}$):** Speeds up medial sphere tracing by predicting the gradient directly in a network head and constraining it to match the analytic gradient from back-propagation [S1, unofficial].
    $$ L_{gradient} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathbb{R}^d} [||\nabla\Phi(x) - \nabla_{analytic}\Phi(x)||^2] $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_dmf(mesh, bounding_box, network, optimizer, num_iterations=900000):
    # Hyperparameters
    sigma = bounding_box.diagonal() / 2.0
    batch_size = 2**13
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Sample surface points uniformly from the mesh
        surface_points = sample_uniform_from_mesh(mesh, count=batch_size // 2)
        
        # 2. Sample volume points by augmenting surface points with Gaussian noise
        # [G] Assuming 'sample_uniform_from_mesh' returns points in R^d.
        # Noise is drawn from N(0, sigma^2 * I).
        noise = torch.randn_like(surface_points) * sigma
        volume_points = surface_points + noise
        
        # 3. Forward pass to get SDF and gradients
        # [G] network.forward(x) returns phi(x). 
        # network.get_gradient_head(x) returns the predicted gradient head.
        # torch.autograd.grad is used for analytic gradient.
        
        phi_surface = network.forward(surface_points)
        grad_surface = torch.autograd.grad(
            phi_surface, surface_points, 
            grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(phi_surface), 
            create_graph=True, retain_graph=True
        )[0]
        
        phi_volume = network.forward(volume_points)
        grad_volume = torch.autograd.grad(
            phi_volume, volume_points, 
            grad_outputs=torch.ones_like(phi_volume), 
            create_graph=True, retain_graph=True
        )[0]
        
        # 4. Compute Losses
        
        # L_surface: Phi(x)^2 for surface points
        loss_surface = torch.mean(phi_surface ** 2)
        
        # L_normal: (grad - GT_normal)^2 for surface points
        # [G] mesh.get_normals(points) returns the triangle normal for each point
        gt_normals = mesh.get_normals(surface_points)
        loss_normal = torch.mean((grad_surface - gt_normals) ** 2)
        
        # L_Eikonal: (||grad|| - 1)^2 for volume points
        grad_norm = torch.norm(grad_volume, dim=1, keepdim=True)
        loss_eikonal = torch.mean((grad_norm - 1.0) ** 2)
        
        # L_minsurface: exp(-100 * |Phi|) for volume points
        loss_minsurface = torch.mean(torch.exp(-100.0 * torch.abs(phi_volume)))
        
        # L_curvature: d/dt grad(x + t*grad(x))
        # [G] The source formula is ambiguous regarding 't'. 
        # Typically this implies a finite difference or directional derivative.
        # Without explicit implementation details, this step is marked as requiring source clarification.
        # Placeholder for curvature loss calculation based on source formula (19).
        loss_curvature = 0.0 # Not given in the sources: exact numerical implementation of derivative.
        
        # L_gradient: ||grad_predicted_head - grad_analytic||^2 for volume points
        grad_head = network.get_gradient_head(volume_points)
        loss_gradient = torch.mean((grad_head - grad_volume) ** 2)
        
        # 5. Combine losses
        # [G] Weights are empirically determined (Table 2 in source), but exact values 
        # are not provided in the text snippets. We assume equal weighting or standard 
        # practice if weights are missing.
        total_loss = (loss_surface + loss_normal + loss_eikonal + 
                      loss_minsurface + loss_curvature + loss_gradient)
        
        # 6. Optimize
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        total_loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with default parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size:** $2^{13}$ (8192) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Iterations:** 500k–900k per run [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Time:** Average 6 hours per model [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gaussian Offset $\sigma$:** Half the bounding box diagonal [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Fourier Feature Interference:** The $L_{minsurface}$ term is critical to prevent high-frequency Fourier features from creating spurious zero-crossings in the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Prediction:** The $L_{gradient}$ term is specifically introduced to speed up medial sphere tracing, which requires $\nabla\Phi(x)$ at every query point. Relying solely on back-propagation for every query is computationally expensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **Curvature Implementation:** The formula for $L_{curvature}$ involves a derivative with respect to $t$ along the gradient direction. The exact numerical discretization or implementation of this term is not provided in the source text [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is computationally intensive, taking approximately 6 hours per model for 500k–900k iterations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Batch size is fixed at $2^{13}$ points. Memory usage depends on the network architecture and the storage of intermediate gradients for back-propagation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** The combination of surface and volume supervision stabilizes the SDF. The gradient head prediction significantly accelerates inference tasks like medial sphere tracing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The method relies on a ground-truth mesh for surface and normal supervision, limiting its applicability to unstructured point clouds without prior meshing [S1, unofficial]. The curvature loss implementation details are sparse [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The DMF training losses were introduced in the paper "Deep Medial Fields" [S1, unofficial]. The work presents medial fields as an implicit representation of local thickness, stored within neural network parameters. This allows for O(1) access and efficient computation of medial properties. The losses were designed to support applications such as improved convergence for sphere tracing, efficient ambient occlusion, and physics proxies [S1, unofficial].
