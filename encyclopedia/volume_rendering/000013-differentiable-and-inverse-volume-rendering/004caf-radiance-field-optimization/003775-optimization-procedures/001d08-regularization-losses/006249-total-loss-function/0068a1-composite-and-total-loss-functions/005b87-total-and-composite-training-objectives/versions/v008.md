# Total and Composite Training Objectives

Also called: Composite Loss, Multi-term Objective

## Overview
Total and Composite Training Objectives define the aggregation of multiple distinct loss terms into a single scalar value to guide the gradient-based optimization of differentiable volume rendering and radiance field models. This approach solves the problem of balancing competing objectives—such as photometric fidelity, geometric consistency, semantic coherence, and structural smoothness—within a unified training loop. What sets this family apart from single-term objectives is the explicit weighting and combination of heterogeneous constraints (e.g., image reconstruction vs. depth consistency) to prevent local minima and improve generalization, distinguishing it from simple photometric losses by incorporating auxiliary geometric or semantic priors.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of total and composite training objectives is the linear combination of individual loss components $\mathcal{L}_k$, each addressing a specific aspect of the reconstruction or understanding task. The general form is:

$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{k} \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

where $\lambda_k$ are scalar hyperparameters that balance the contribution of each term. The specific composition of $\mathcal{L}_k$ varies by the underlying radiance field representation (e.g., Gaussian Splatting, NeRF, Voxel Grid) and the training stage. Common components include photometric errors (L1, L2, SSIM), geometric constraints (depth, normals, Eikonal), and semantic regularizers. The optimization process minimizes this scalar value, computing gradients through all active terms simultaneously.

## Subtopics

### [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md)
The Overall Training Loss is the composite objective function used to optimize differentiable volume rendering and radiance field models. It aggregates multiple component losses—such as photometric rendering errors, geometric constraints, semantic regularization, and structural smoothness penalties—into a single scalar value to guide gradient-based optimization. Unlike single-term objectives, it balances competing goals like image fidelity against geometric consistency or semantic coherence. It builds on [Total and Composite Training Objectives](16924-total-and-composite-training-objectives.md) by defining the specific terms and weights used in various state-of-the-art radiance field training pipelines. See [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md).

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md)
Multi-scale Joint Training Loss is a composite objective function used in differentiable volume rendering to balance low-frequency structural consistency with high-frequency detail fidelity during optimization. It addresses the problem of refining rendered images by preventing global appearance damage while enhancing texture sharpness. It differs from single-scale losses by explicitly weighting contributions from multiple network stages or resolution levels, and from simple photometric losses by incorporating uncertainty and multi-scale aggregation. See [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md).

### Total Loss Function for Neural Scene Reconstruction
- **Definition**: The total loss function combines seven components to train the neural scene reconstruction model: RGB loss ($L_c$), normal loss ($L_n$), depth loss ($L_d$), Eikonal loss ($L_e$), smooth loss ($L_s$), SRDF-SDF consistency loss ($L_{con}$), and visibility loss ($L_{vis}$) [S1].
- **Step**: Compute 2.5D depth and normal maps using volume rendering with the density function $\sigma_{SRDF}$ to exploit geometric cues and support training [S1].
- **Formula**: $\mathcal {L} = \mathcal {L}_c + \lambda _n \mathcal {L}_n + \lambda _d \mathcal {L}_d + \lambda _e \mathcal {L}_e + \lambda _s \mathcal {L}_s + \lambda _{con} \mathcal {L}_{con} + \lambda _{vis} \mathcal {L}_{vis}$, where $\lambda_{(\cdot)}$ are the loss weights [S1].
- **Parameters**: $\lambda_n, \lambda_d, \lambda_e, \lambda_s, \lambda_{con}, \lambda_{vis}$ are loss weights for normal, depth, Eikonal, smooth, consistency, and visibility losses respectively. Typical values are not specified in this text [S1].

### Joint Training Losses
- **Formula**:
  - Reconstruction loss: $L_{rgb} = \sum_{r \in R} ||\hat{C}(r) - C(r)||$ [S2].
  - Skinning weight loss: $L_W = ||G_{x_c->w}^W(J_{p0}) - \hat{w}||_2^2$ [S2].
  - Cycle consistency loss (INN): $L_{INN} = ||H(H^{-1}(x_v, J_{p0}), J_p) - x_v||_2^2$ [S2].
  - Consistency loss: $L_{Consis} = ||x'_c - x_c||_2^2$ [S2].
- **Parameters**:
  - $L_{rgb}$: Reconstruction loss between rendered RGB $\hat{C}(r)$ and input pixel RGB $C(r)$ along ray $r$ [S2].
  - $L_{pose}$: Pose loss ensuring INN correctly transforms point set $X_{J_p}$ (around deformed bones) to $X_{J_{p0}}$ (around canonical bones). $P$ is total points sampled around bones [S2].
  - $L_W$: Skinning weight loss ensuring predicted skinning weight for canonical joints is a one-hot vector $\hat{w}$ (1 for respective joint, 0 for others) [S2].
  - $L_{INN}$: Cycle consistency loss for INN, where $H$ transforms canonical to deformed space conditioned by posed skeleton, and $H^{-1}$ transforms view to canonical [S2].
  - $L_{Consis}$: Consistency loss minimizing L2 distance between INN-transformed point $x'_c$ and skinning-weight-conditioned canonical point $x_c$ [S2].

## Background
The concept of composite training objectives in neural rendering evolved from early NeRF implementations that relied primarily on photometric L1/L2 losses. As representations became more complex (e.g., 3D Gaussians, explicit volumes), researchers introduced auxiliary losses to enforce geometric plausibility and semantic consistency. Key developments include the use of total variation for smoothness [S1], multi-stage uncertainty weighting for dynamic growth [S1], and joint training of pose and skinning networks for dynamic scenes [S2]. These methods are widely used in state-of-the-art radiance field pipelines, including Gaussian Splatting variants (ReSplat, Compact 3DGS), semantic NeRFs (OV-NeRF), and deblurring methods (PDRF).
