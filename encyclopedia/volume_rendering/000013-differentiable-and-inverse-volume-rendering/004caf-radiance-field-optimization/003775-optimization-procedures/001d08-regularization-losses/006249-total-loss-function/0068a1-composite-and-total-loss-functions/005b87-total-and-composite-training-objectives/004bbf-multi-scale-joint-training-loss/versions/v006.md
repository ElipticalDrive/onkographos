# Multi-scale Joint Training Loss

Also called: Coarse-Fine Joint Loss, Joint Loss, Multi-scale Joint Loss

## Overview
Multi-scale Joint Training Loss is a composite objective function used in radiance-field optimization to balance low-frequency structural consistency with high-frequency detail fidelity across multiple network stages or resolution levels [S1, S2]. It addresses the problem of training instability and detail loss in dynamic or multi-stage rendering pipelines by jointly optimizing coarse and fine representations [S2]. Unlike single-scale losses, it explicitly weights contributions from different resolution stages or coarse/fine branches to ensure global appearance is preserved while local textures are refined [S1, S2].

## How it works
The method constructs a total loss $L_{tot}$ as a weighted sum of component losses derived from multiple output stages or resolution levels. Two primary formulations exist in the literature, depending on whether the architecture uses dynamic stage growth or coarse-fine refinement.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Network parameters $\theta$; ground truth images $I_{gt}$; rendered images $I_{render}^i$ from stage $i$ (where $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$); smoothing filters for low-frequency extraction; masks $M$ for background exclusion [S1, S2].
*   **Outputs**: Scalar loss value $L_{tot}$; gradients $\nabla_\theta L_{tot}$ [S1, S2].

### Data Structures
*   **Stage Outputs**: A list or tensor of rendered images $I_{render}^i$ for each stage $i$. In dynamic growth models, $D$ represents the current number of active stages [S1].
*   **Smoothing Filters**: Low-pass filters (e.g., Gaussian blur) applied to $I_{render}$ and $I_{gt}$ to extract low-frequency components [S2].

### Formulas and Parameters

#### Formulation 1: Multi-Stage Dynamic Growth (Recursive-NeRF)
Used when the network grows dynamically through stages [S1]. The total loss sums the MSE and uncertainty losses from all active stages $i=1$ to $D$:

$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{i=1}^D \left( \beta_1 \mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i + \beta_2 \mathcal{L}_{unct}^i \right) $$

Where:
*   $D$: Current number of stages (equal to $1 + \text{growth\_iterations}$) [S1].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$: Mean Squared Error loss between rendered and ground truth images at stage $i$.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$: Uncertainty loss at stage $i$, often derived from per-pixel variance estimates [S1].
*   $\beta_1$: Weight for MSE loss. Typical value: $1.0$ [S1].
*   $\beta_2$: Weight for uncertainty loss. Typical value: $0.1$ [S1].

#### Formulation 2: Coarse-Fine Refinement (Denoise-GS)
Used when refining a coarse output with a fine branch [S2]. The loss balances low-frequency and high-frequency terms:

$$ L(\theta) = \alpha L_{coarse}(\theta) + (1-\alpha) L_{fine}(\theta), \quad \alpha \in [0, 1] $$

Where:
*   $L_{coarse}(\theta)$: Low-frequency consistency term. Computed by comparing smoothed versions of input and output images. It focuses on tone and large-scale structure [S2].
*   $L_{fine}(\theta)$: Detail consistency term. Computed as a sum of:
    *   Pixel-level robust distance (suppresses outlier noise) [S2].
    *   Structural Similarity (SSIM) term (aligns textures/contours) [S2].
    *   Gradient consistency term (matches edge direction/strength) [S2].
    *   Chroma consistency term (reduces color shifts) [S2].
*   $\alpha$: Balance factor between coarse and fine losses. Typical value: Not explicitly given in sources, but $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Stage Growth**: In dynamic growth models, $D$ increases during training. Loss weights must be applied to all active stages, not just the final one [S1].
*   **Masking**: Background regions must be masked out to prevent the network from optimizing for default background colors. Masks using $-1$ or $0$ for background must be converted to $0$ weight multipliers [S1, S2].
*   **Uncertainty Loss**: The exact formulation of $\mathcal{L}_{unct}$ is not fully detailed in the provided sources, but it is critical for balancing confidence in predictions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Linear in the number of stages $D$ and the number of pixels. Each stage requires forward pass and loss computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Proportional to the number of stages $D$ and resolution. Storing intermediate rendered images for all stages increases memory usage [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Improves detail fidelity while preserving global structure; robust to noise via uncertainty weighting or robust distance terms [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters ($\alpha, \beta_1, \beta_2$); dynamic growth adds complexity to training loop [S1, S2].
*   **When to Use**: Multi-stage or dynamic growth architectures; denoising/refinement pipelines [S1, S2].
*   **When Not to Use**: Single-stage, fixed-resolution models without uncertainty estimation [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin**: The multi-stage formulation originates from Recursive-NeRF, which introduces dynamic network growth [S1]. The coarse-fine joint loss formulation is used in Denoise-GS for self-supervised denoising in 3D Gaussian Splatting [S2].
*   **History**: Recursive-NeRF (2022) proposed growing networks based on uncertainty clustering [S1]. Denoise-GS (2026) applied joint coarse-fine loss to refine sparse-view Gaussian Splatting outputs [S2].
*   **Usage**: Used in dynamic NeRF variants and denoising pipelines for sparse-view reconstruction [S1, S2].
