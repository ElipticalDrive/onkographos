# Total and Composite Training Objectives

Also called: Composite Loss Functions, Multi-Objective Training Objectives

## Overview
Total and Composite Training Objectives define the scalar loss functions used to optimize radiance-field and Gaussian splatting parameters by aggregating multiple distinct error signals. They solve the problem of balancing competing training goals—such as photometric accuracy, geometric consistency, and structural regularization—into a single differentiable quantity for backpropagation. This family is distinguished from sibling articles by its focus on the **composition** and **aggregation** of these terms (e.g., weighted sums, multi-stage balancing) rather than the definition of individual loss primitives like opacity or total variation.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of this family is the linear combination of heterogeneous loss terms. The total loss $L_{tot}$ is generally formulated as:
$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{k} w_k \mathcal{L}_k $$
where $\mathcal{L}_k$ represents a specific loss component (e.g., photometric, geometric, regularization) and $w_k$ is its corresponding weight [S1].

Key shared characteristics include:
1.  **Heterogeneity:** The terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ often measure different modalities (RGB color, depth, normals, point clouds) or properties (smoothness, sparsity) [S1, S2].
2.  **Masking:** Foreground/background masks $M$ are frequently applied to loss terms to prevent background pixels from contributing to the gradient, as empty regions lack ground truth or have arbitrary default values .
3.  **Multi-Stage/Scale Aggregation:** In methods with dynamic growth or coarse-to-fine refinement, losses are aggregated across multiple network stages or resolution levels, often with stage-specific weights [S1, S2].
4.  **Regularization:** Terms such as Total Variation (TV), Eikonal, or codebook constraints are included to enforce physical priors or model compactness [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md)
Combines rendering, masking, and geometry codebook losses for end-to-end training of radiance fields and Gaussian splatting models. It aggregates photometric reconstruction, geometric consistency, and regularization terms. Choose this when training a standard single-stage or recurrent radiance field/Gaussian model that requires a unified objective for all parameters.

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md)
A composite objective for methods employing dynamic network growth or multi-stage refinement. It balances low-frequency structural agreement with high-frequency detail fidelity by weighting contributions from multiple network stages or resolution levels. Choose this when the architecture explicitly produces outputs at multiple scales or stages simultaneously.

### Total Loss Function for Neural Scene Reconstruction
A specific composite formulation for neural scene reconstruction that combines seven components: RGB loss ($L_c$), normal loss ($L_n$), depth loss ($L_d$), Eikonal loss ($L_e$), smooth loss ($L_s$), SRDF-SDF consistency loss ($L_{con}$), and visibility loss ($L_{vis}$) [S1].
**Formula:**
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_c + \lambda_n \mathcal{L}_n + \lambda_d \mathcal{L}_d + \lambda_e \mathcal{L}_e + \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_s + \lambda_{con} \mathcal{L}_{con} + \lambda_{vis} \mathcal{L}_{vis} $$
where $\lambda_{(\cdot)}$ are loss weights [S1].
**Implementation Note:** 2.5D depth and normal maps are volume rendered using the density function $\sigma_{SRDF}$ to exploit geometric cues [S1].

### Joint Training Losses
A set of losses used for jointly training NeRF and an Implicit Neural Network (INN) for dynamic scene reconstruction, focusing on skeleton and skinning constraints [S2].
**Formulas:**
-   **Reconstruction Loss:** $L_{rgb} = \sum_{r \in R} \|\hat{C}(r) - C(r)\|$ [S2].
-   **Skinning Weight Loss:** $L_W = \|G_{x_c \to w}^W(J_{p0}) - \hat{w}\|_2^2$, ensuring predicted skinning weights are one-hot vectors [S2].
-   **Cycle Consistency Loss:** $L_{INN} = \|H(H^{-1}(x_v, J_{p0}), J_p) - x_v\|_2^2$, ensuring invertibility between view and canonical spaces [S2].
-   **Consistency Loss:** $L_{Consis} = \|x'_c - x_c\|_2^2$, minimizing distance between INN-transformed points and skinning-weight-conditioned points [S2].
**Parameters:** $L_{pose}$ ensures INN correctly transforms point sets around deformed bones to canonical bones [S2].

## Background
The concept of composite training objectives in volume rendering and neural radiance fields emerged alongside the first differentiable rendering frameworks. Early works like NeRF [G] primarily used simple L1 or L2 photometric losses. As the field advanced, researchers introduced geometric priors (depth, normals) and regularization terms (Eikonal, TV) to improve convergence and geometry quality [S1]. The specific formulations cited here reflect the diversification of training protocols:
-   **Neural Scene Reconstruction:** The 7-component loss in [S1] represents a comprehensive approach to single-view or multi-view reconstruction using SRDF-based volume rendering.
-   **Dynamic Scenes:** The joint training losses in [S2] address the specific challenges of dynamic scenes, requiring consistency between canonical and deformed spaces via INN constraints.
-   **Gaussian Splatting & Compactness:** The inclusion of codebook losses in  reflects recent efforts to compress Gaussian attributes (rotation, scale) for efficient storage and rendering.
-   **Multi-Stage Refinement:** The multi-scale losses in [S1, S2] address the stability issues in growing networks, ensuring that early coarse stages do not hinder the learning of fine details in later stages.
