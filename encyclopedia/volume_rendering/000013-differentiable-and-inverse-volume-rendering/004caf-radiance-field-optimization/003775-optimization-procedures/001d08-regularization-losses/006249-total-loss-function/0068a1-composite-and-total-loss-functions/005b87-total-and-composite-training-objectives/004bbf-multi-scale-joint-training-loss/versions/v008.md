# Multi-scale Joint Training Loss

Also called: Coarse-Fine Joint Loss, Joint Loss, Multi-scale Joint Loss

## Overview
Multi-scale Joint Training Loss is a composite objective function used in radiance-field optimization to balance low-frequency structural consistency with high-frequency detail fidelity across multiple network stages or resolution levels [S1, S2]. It sets itself apart from single-scale losses by explicitly weighting contributions from coarse and fine outputs (or different network stages) to prevent the optimization from overfitting to noise while preserving global appearance [S1, S2].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: Rendered images or volumes from multiple stages/resolutions ($\hat{I}_i$ or $\hat{V}_i$), ground truth images ($I$), and model parameters ($\theta$).
*   **Outputs**: A scalar total loss value $L_{tot}$ for backpropagation.

### Data Structures
*   **Stage Outputs**: In recursive or growing networks, intermediate outputs are stored for each stage $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$ [S1].
*   **Coarse/Fine Outputs**: In joint coarse-fine schemes, two distinct image representations are maintained [S2].

### Formulas
The specific formulation depends on the underlying architecture:

1.  **Multi-Stage Recursive Loss (Recursive-NeRF)**:
    For a network with $D$ stages, the loss sums the Mean Squared Error (MSE) and uncertainty losses from all stages [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^D \left( \beta_1 \mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i + \beta_2 \mathcal{L}_{unct}^i \right) $$
    Where $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ is the MSE loss at stage $i$, $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ is the uncertainty loss at stage $i$, and $\beta_1, \beta_2$ are weights [S1].

2.  **Coarse-Fine Joint Loss (Denoise-GS)**:
    For a two-stage refinement process, the loss balances low-frequency agreement and high-frequency fidelity [S2]:
    $$ L(\theta) = \alpha L_{coarse}(\theta) + (1-\alpha) L_{fine}(\theta) $$
    Where $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ controls the trade-off [S2].
    *   $L_{coarse}$: Low-frequency consistency term comparing smoothed input and output to preserve tone and large-scale structure [S2].
    *   $L_{fine}$: Detail consistency term comprising pixel-level robust distance, structural similarity (SSIM), gradient consistency, and chroma consistency [S2].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **$\beta_1$**: Weight for MSE loss in multi-stage training. Value: $1.0$ [S1].
*   **$\beta_2$**: Weight for uncertainty loss in multi-stage training. Value: $0.1$ [S1].
*   **$\alpha$**: Weight for coarse loss in joint coarse-fine training. Range: $[0, 1]$ [S2].
*   **$D$**: Number of stages or network growth iterations [S1].

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Render Multi-Scale Outputs**: Compute rendered images/volumes for all active stages or resolutions ($\hat{I}_1, \dots, \hat{I}_D$) [S1].
2.  **Compute Stage Losses**: For each stage $i$, calculate $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ and $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ [S1].
3.  **Aggregate Multi-Stage Loss**: Sum the weighted stage losses using $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Coarse/Fine Terms**: If using joint coarse-fine, calculate $L_{coarse}$ (low-frequency) and $L_{fine}$ (high-frequency components) [S2].
5.  **Combine**: Apply the joint weighting $\alpha$ to combine coarse and fine losses [S2].
6.  **Backpropagate**: Update parameters using the total loss [S1, S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Uncertainty Loss Calculation**: $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ requires proper modeling of prediction uncertainty; incorrect variance estimation can destabilize training [S1].
*   **Stage Imbalance**: If $D$ is large, later stages may dominate if weights are not normalized, though equal weighting is suggested in [S1].
*   **Alpha Sensitivity**: In joint coarse-fine, $\alpha$ must be tuned to prevent the coarse term from suppressing high-frequency details or the fine term from introducing noise [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Increases linearly with the number of stages $D$ in recursive methods [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing intermediate outputs for all stages, increasing memory usage with $D$ [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Improves convergence stability by guiding optimization with low-frequency cues while refining details [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations**: Higher computational overhead due to multiple forward passes or stage evaluations [S1].

## Background
Originated in Recursive-NeRF [S1] and Denoise-GS [S2] to address the challenges of optimizing radiance fields with dynamic growth or sparse-view inputs. It enables stable training by leveraging multi-scale supervision.
