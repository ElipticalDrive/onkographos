# Overall Training Loss

Also called: Total Training Loss, End-to-End Loss, End-to-End Training Objective

## Overview
The overall training loss aggregates multiple supervision signals into a single scalar value to optimize differentiable volume rendering representations [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6][S7, S8, S9, unofficial]. It solves the problem of balancing photometric fidelity, geometric consistency, semantic alignment, and regularization constraints during end-to-end training [G]. It builds on the parent's weighted summation principle [16924-total-and-composite-training-objectives.md].

## How it works
The method constructs the total loss $L_{tot}$ via the weighted summation of individual loss components $\mathcal{L}_k$, as defined in the parent [16924-total-and-composite-training-objectives.md]:
$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{k} \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$
The specific instantiation varies based on the representation, training stage, and scale of supervision [G].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Rendered images/volumes ($\hat{I}, \hat{V}$), ground truth images/volumes ($I, V$), camera poses ($P$), depth maps ($D$), semantic features/labels, and masks ($M$) [G].
*   **Outputs:** Scalar loss value $L_{tot}$ used for gradient backpropagation [G].

### Data Structures
*   **Loss Components:** Individual loss terms ($\mathcal{L}_k$) are computed per-pixel or per-voxel and then aggregated (summed or averaged) [G].
*   **Weights:** Scalar hyperparameters $\lambda_k$ control the contribution of each term [G].

### Formulas and Steps
1. **PDRF (Progressively Deblurring Radiance Field) [S2]**
Used for deblurring scene reconstruction from blurry images [S2].
$$ L_{tot} = L_{CRR\_photo} + L_{FVR\_photo} + \lambda (L_{TV\_coar} + L_{TV\_fine}) $$
*   $L_{CRR\_photo}$: Photometric loss for Coarse Radiance Field (CRR).
*   $L_{FVR\_photo}$: Photometric loss for Fine Voxel Renderer (FVR).
*   $L_{TV\_coar}, L_{TV\_fine}$: Total variation constraints on explicit feature volumes to enforce smoothness [S2].
*   $\lambda$: Weight for total variation.
*   **Parameters:** Batch size: 1024 rays. Gamma correction: 2.2 [S2].

2. **TD-NeRF (Truncated Depth Prior) [S3]**
Used for joint camera pose and neural radiance field optimization [S3].
$$ L = L_{rgb} + \lambda_1 L_{self-depth} + \lambda_2 L_{GPC} + \lambda_3 L_{reproj} $$
*   $L_{rgb}$: RGB photometric loss.
*   $L_{self-depth}$: Self-supervised depth loss.
*   $L_{GPC}$: Gaussian Point Constraint loss (based on Gaussian kernel function) [S3].
*   $L_{reproj}$: Reprojection loss.
*   **Parameters:** $\lambda_1 = 0.04, \lambda_2 = 1.0, \lambda_3 = 1.0$ [S3].

3. **Compact 3D Gaussian Representation [S4]**
Used for end-to-end training of Gaussian splatting with codebooks [S4].
$$ L = L_{ren} + \lambda_m L_m + L_r + L_s $$
*   $L_{ren}$: Rendering loss (weighted sum of L1 and SSIM loss between GT and rendered images) [S4].
*   $L_m$: Masking loss.
*   $L_r$: Rotation codebook loss.
*   $L_s$: Scale codebook loss.
*   $\lambda_m$: Hyperparameter to regularize the number of Gaussians [S4].
*   **Note:** $L_r$ and $L_s$ are applied only for the last 1K training iterations to avoid heavy computation [S4].

4. **ZipSplat [S7, unofficial]**
Used for refining cameras and scene tokens with rendering, geometric, and camera supervision [S7, unofficial].
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{render}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{geo}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geo}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{cone}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cone}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{pose}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pose}} $$
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{render}}$: Combines $\ell_1$ and perceptual (LPIPS) terms [S7, unofficial].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geo}}$: Geometric loss.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cone}}$: Cone loss.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pose}}$: Pose supervision loss.
*   **Parameters:** LPIPS weight: 0.1. $\lambda_{geo} = 0.05, \lambda_{cone} = 0.01, \lambda_{pose} = 0.05$ [S7, unofficial].

5. **ReSplat (Recurrent Gaussian Splatting) [S5]**
Uses a two-stage training process [S5].
*   **Stage 1 (Initialization):**
    $$ L_{1st} = \sum_{v=1}^V \ell_{\text{render}}(\hat{I}_v, I_v) + \alpha \cdot \sum_{i=1}^N \ell_{\text{smooth}}(I_i, \hat{D}_i) $$
    *   $\ell_{\text{render}}(\hat{I}, I) = \ell_1(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda \cdot \ell_{\text{percept}}(\hat{I}, I)$ [S5].
    *   $\ell_{\text{smooth}}(I, \hat{D}) = |\partial_x \hat{D}| e^{-|\partial_x I|} + |\partial_y \hat{D}| e^{-|\partial_y I|}$ [S5].
    *   **Parameters:** $\alpha = 0.01, \lambda = 0.5$ [S5].
*   **Stage 2 (Recurrent Training):**
    $$ L_{2nd} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \gamma^{T-1-t} \sum_{v=1}^V \ell_{\text{render}}(\hat{I}_v^t, I_v) $$
    *   Supervises all Gaussian predictions during the recurrent process with exponentially increasing weights [S5].
    *   **Parameters:** $\gamma = 0.9$ [S5].

6. **OV-NeRF (Open-Vocabulary NeRF) [S6]**
Uses a two-stage semantic field training process [S6].
*   **Stage I (Initial Training):**
    $$ L_{Total} = L_{color} + L_{feat} + L_{dino} + L_{RSR} $$
    *   $L_{RSR}$: Region semantic ranking regularization loss.
*   **Stage II (Cross-View Enhancement):**
    $$ L_{Total} = L_{color} + L_{feat} + L_{dino} + L_{CSE} $$
    *   $L_{CSE}$: Cross-view self-enhancement loss.
    *   **Parameters:** $T = 10,000$ iterations before switching to Stage II [S6].

7. **X-GRM (X-ray Gaussian Reconstruction Model) [S8, unofficial]**
Uses volume and rendering constraints [S8, unofficial].
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{volume}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{render}} $$
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{volume}}(\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{V}_{gt}) = \sum_{x,y,z} (\mathbf{V}(x, y, z) - \mathbf{V}_{gt}(x, y, z))^2$ [S8, unofficial].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{render}}(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{I}_{gt}) = \lambda_{L1}\mathcal{L}_1(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{I}_{gt}) + \lambda_{ssim}\mathcal{L}_{ssim}(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{I}_{gt})$ [S8, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Volume resolution: $256^3$. Sub-volume sampling factor: 1/4 in each dimension [S8, unofficial].

8. **VoxelTTO [S9, unofficial]**
Combines rendered-depth and rendered-RGB losses [S9, unofficial].
$$ L = L_{depth} + L_{rgb} = \lambda_{\ell1}^d L_{\ell1}^{depth} + \lambda_{align} L_{align}^{depth} + \lambda_{\ell1} L_{\ell1}^{rgb} + \lambda_{ssim} L_{ssim}^{rgb} $$
*   $L_{\ell1}^{depth}$: Supervises rendered depth with ground-truth depth.
*   $L_{align}^{depth}$: Enforces alignment with VFM-predicted depth.
*   $L_{\ell1}^{rgb}, L_{ssim}^{rgb}$: $\ell_1$ and SSIM losses between rendered and ground-truth images.
*   **Parameters:** $\lambda_{\ell1}^d = 0.25, \lambda_{align} = 0.25, \lambda_{\ell1} = 0.9, \lambda_{ssim} = 1.0$ [S9, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Masking:** Without loss masking, the network must match default ground truth values in empty regions (background), which complicates the task [S2]. Loss masking simplifies this by ignoring these areas [S2].
*   **Codebook Training:** Applying codebook losses ($L_r, L_s$) for the entire training duration causes heavy computation. It is recommended to apply them only for the last 1K iterations [S4].
*   **Memory Usage:** Rendering with full volume resolution ($256^3$) requires exhaustive GPU memory to preserve gradients. Randomly sampling sub-volumes (e.g., 1/4 in each dimension) reduces memory usage during training [S8, unofficial].
*   **Depth Sensitivity:** Chamfer distance for point cloud constraints can be sensitive to noise in estimated depth maps. Gaussian Point Constraint (GPC) is proposed as a more robust alternative [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Loss Masking](12742-loss-masking.md)
A technique to modulate loss terms for normals, ambient occlusion, and colors with the output mask, so that masked-out pixels (background) do not contribute to the loss function. It simplifies the network's task by ignoring areas where the ground truth images are filled with default values.

### X-GRM Training Objectives
A training objective for X-ray Gaussian Reconstruction Models that uses volume constraints (MSE) and rendering constraints (L1 + SSIM) [S8, unofficial]. It employs random sub-volume sampling to reduce GPU memory usage during training [S8, unofficial].

### VoxelTTO Training Loss
A training loss for Voxel-Aligned Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting that combines rendered-depth loss and rendered-RGB loss [S9, unofficial]. It supervises depth alignment with VFM predictions and RGB appearance with ground truth [S9, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of rays/samples per iteration and the complexity of the loss terms (e.g., LPIPS requires feature extraction) [G]. PDRF uses importance sampling to reduce samples [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High for voxel-based methods due to gradient storage. Sub-volume sampling reduces GPU memory usage for gradients during training [S8, unofficial]. Gaussian Splatting methods generally have lower memory overhead than dense voxel grids [G].
*   **Strengths:** Flexible composition of multiple supervision signals; allows for end-to-end optimization of complex representations; handles various tasks (rendering, depth, semantics) jointly [G].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters ($\lambda_k$); some loss terms (e.g., perceptual, codebook) are computationally expensive; sensitive to initialization and training stages [G].
*   **When to Use:** When optimizing differentiable volume rendering representations with multiple supervision signals (photometric, geometric, semantic) [G].
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are severely limited and simple photometric loss is sufficient; when ground truth for specific loss terms (e.g., depth, semantics) is unavailable [G].

## Background
The concept of combining multiple loss terms for training differentiable rendering models has evolved alongside the development of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting [G]. Early NeRF methods primarily used photometric loss [S1]. As the field advanced, additional constraints such as depth, pose, and semantic information were incorporated to improve robustness and generalization [S2, S3, S5, S6]. The specific formulations of the overall training loss vary significantly depending on the representation (Gaussian, Voxel, Implicit) and the specific task (deblurring, sparse-view reconstruction, open-vocabulary understanding) [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6][S7, S8, S9, unofficial].
