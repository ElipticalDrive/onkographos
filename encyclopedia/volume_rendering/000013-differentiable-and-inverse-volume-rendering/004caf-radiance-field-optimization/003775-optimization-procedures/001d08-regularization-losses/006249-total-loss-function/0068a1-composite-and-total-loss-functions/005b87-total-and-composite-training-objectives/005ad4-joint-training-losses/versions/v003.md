# Joint Training Losses

Also called: Composite Loss with Pose, Skin, and INN Constraints

## Overview
This method defines a composite objective function for the joint optimization of a pose estimation network (specifically an Implicit Neural Network, INN) and a generalized 3D Gaussian or NeRF rendering network in dynamic scene reconstruction [S1]. It solves the ambiguity in skinning weight prediction and canonical-to-deformed mapping by enforcing geometric consistency in the skeleton space alongside photometric rendering accuracy [S1]. It distinguishes itself from static multi-task learning by employing a dynamic weighting strategy that shifts focus from pose-related constraints to appearance reconstruction as training progresses [S1].

## How it works
The method optimizes a total loss $L_{tot}$ composed of five distinct terms: photometric reconstruction, pose consistency, skinning weight regularization, INN cycle consistency, and geometric consistency. The optimization targets the parameters of the INN (mapping between view and canonical spaces) and the skinning weight prediction network, while simultaneously updating the radiance field or Gaussian splatting parameters.

**Inputs:**
*   Input images $I$ and corresponding camera poses $P$.
*   Canonical skeleton joints $J_{p0}$ and posed skeleton joints $J_p$.
*   Points sampled around bones in canonical space $X_{J_{p0}}$ and deformed space $X_{J_p}$.
*   View space points $x_v$.

**Outputs:**
*   Scalar total loss $L_{tot}$.
*   Gradients with respect to INN parameters, skinning weight network parameters, and rendering network parameters.

**Loss Components:**

1.  **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rgb}$):** Minimizes the difference between rendered RGB $\hat{C}(r)$ and input pixel RGB $C(r)$ along rays $r \in R$ [S1].
    $$ L_{rgb} = \sum_{r \in R} ||\hat{C}(r) - C(r)|| $$

2.  **Pose Loss ($L_{pose}$):** Ensures the INN correctly transforms points from the deformed skeleton space to the canonical skeleton space. Let $G_{v \to c}$ be the INN transformation [S1].
    $$ L_{pose} = \sum_{p \in P} ||X_{J_{p0}} - G_{v \to c}(X_{J_p})|| $$
    Where $P$ is the total number of points sampled around bones from each skeleton [S1].

3.  **Skinning Weight Loss ($L_W$):** Constrains the predicted skinning weight $\hat{w}$ for canonical joints to be a one-hot vector (1 for the respective joint, 0 for others) [S1].
    $$ L_W = ||G_{x_c \to w}(J_{p0}) - \hat{w}||_2^2 $$

4.  **Cycle Consistency Loss ($L_{INN}$):** Enforces that transforming a view point to canonical space and back to deformed space (conditioned on the pose) recovers the original view point [S1]. Let $H$ be the INN transformation from canonical to deformed space conditioned by $J_p$, and $H^{-1}$ transform view to canonical conditioned by $J_{p0}$ [S1].
    $$ L_{INN} = ||H(H^{-1}(x_v, J_{p0}), J_p) - x_v||_2^2 $$

5.  **Consistency Loss ($L_{Consis}$):** Minimizes the L2 distance between the INN-transformed point $x'_c$ and the skinning-weight-conditioned canonical point $x_c$ [S1].
    $$ L_{Consis} = ||x'_c - x_c||_2^2 $$

**Total Loss:**
The total loss is the weighted sum of these components. The weights $\lambda_k$ are dynamic, exponentially shifting focus from pose/consistency losses to $L_{rgb}$ as training progresses [S1].
$$ L_{tot} = \lambda_{rgb} L_{rgb} + \lambda_{pose} L_{pose} + \lambda_{W} L_{W} + \lambda_{INN} L_{INN} + \lambda_{Consis} L_{Consis} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_joint_training_loss(rendered_rgb, input_rgb, 
                                inn_transform, skinning_net, 
                                canonical_joints, posed_joints,
                                points_deformed, points_canonical,
                                view_points, skinning_weights_target,
                                weights_rgb, weights_pose, weights_w, 
                                weights_inn, weights_cons):
    """
    Computes the joint training loss for dynamic NeRF/Gaussian optimization.
    """
    # 1. Photometric Reconstruction Loss
    # Sum of L2 distances between rendered and input RGB along rays
    loss_rgb = sum(norm(rendered_rgb[r] - input_rgb[r]) for r in rays)
    
    # 2. Pose Loss
    # Transform deformed points to canonical space using INN and compare to ground truth canonical points
    transformed_deformed = inn_transform.transform_deformed_to_canonical(points_deformed, posed_joints)
    loss_pose = sum(norm(points_canonical[p] - transformed_deformed[p]) for p in points_deformed)
    
    # 3. Skinning Weight Loss
    # Ensure predicted weights are one-hot vectors for canonical joints
    predicted_weights = skinning_net.predict_weights(canonical_joints)
    loss_w = norm(predicted_weights - skinning_weights_target) ** 2
    
    # 4. INN Cycle Consistency Loss
    # View -> Canonical -> Deformed should equal View
    canonical_points = inn_transform.transform_view_to_canonical(view_points, canonical_joints)
    deformed_points_back = inn_transform.transform_canonical_to_deformed(canonical_points, posed_joints)
    loss_inn = norm(deformed_points_back - view_points) ** 2
    
    # 5. Consistency Loss
    # Compare INN-transformed point with skinning-weight-conditioned canonical point
    # Note: x'_c is INN transformed, x_c is skinning-weight conditioned
    # Implementation detail: x_c is derived from skinning weights and canonical geometry
    loss_cons = norm(inn_transformed_point - skinning_conditioned_canonical_point) ** 2
    
    # Aggregate with dynamic weights
    total_loss = (weights_rgb * loss_rgb + 
                  weights_pose * loss_pose + 
                  weights_w * loss_w + 
                  weights_inn * loss_inn + 
                  weights_cons * loss_cons)
    
    return total_loss
```

## Subtopics

### [Joint Training Strategy](17557-joint-training-strategy.md)
Enables simultaneous optimization of pose estimation and rendering by combining photometric, depth, and RGB losses. Distinguishes itself by using dynamic weight coefficients that exponentially shift focus from pose constraints to appearance reconstruction as training progresses.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The method requires sampling points around bones and performing multiple INN transformations per ray/point, increasing computational overhead compared to static NeRF [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Resolves ambiguities in skinning weight prediction and canonical mapping that arise from lack of direct supervision [S1]. Accelerates learning via shape initialization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of dynamic weight schedules to balance geometric and photometric objectives [S1].
*   **When to use:** Dynamic scene reconstruction where explicit skeleton or bone structure is available or estimable [S1].

## Background
Originated in TFS-NeRF (Template-Free NeRF for Semantic 3D Reconstruction of Dynamic Scene) [S1]. The method addresses the issue that incorrect combinations of canonical points and skinning weights can satisfy forward Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) equations without direct supervision [S1]. It builds on shape loss principles from Guo et al. [S1].
