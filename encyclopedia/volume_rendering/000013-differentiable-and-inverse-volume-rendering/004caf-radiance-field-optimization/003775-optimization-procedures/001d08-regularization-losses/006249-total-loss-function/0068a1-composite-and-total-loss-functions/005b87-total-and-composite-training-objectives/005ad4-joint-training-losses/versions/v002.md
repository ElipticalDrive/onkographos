# Joint Training Losses

Also called: Composite Loss with Pose, Skin, and INN Constraints

## Overview
This method defines the composite objective function for jointly optimizing a Template-Free NeRF (TFS-NeRF), an Implicit Neural Network (INN) for spatial transformation, and a skinning weight prediction network. It solves the ambiguity in dynamic scene reconstruction where multiple combinations of canonical points and skinning weights can satisfy forward Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) equations [S1]. It distinguishes itself by enforcing constraints directly in the skeleton space (pose, skinning weights, and consistency) alongside standard photometric reconstruction, ensuring the INN learns a correct mapping between view and canonical spaces [S1].

## How it works
The method optimizes three coupled components simultaneously: the NeRF for appearance, the INN for coordinate transformation, and a skinning weight network for deformation blending. The total loss $L_{tot}$ is the sum of five weighted terms:

$$ L_{tot} = \lambda_{rgb} L_{rgb} + \lambda_{pose} L_{pose} + \lambda_{W} L_{W} + \lambda_{INN} L_{INN} + \lambda_{Consis} L_{Consis} $$

**Inputs:**
*   Input video frames with known camera poses.
*   Canonical skeleton joints $J_{p0}$ and posed skeleton joints $J_p$.
*   Point clouds initialized around the canonical skeleton for shape regularization (optional, referenced as $L_{shape}$ in source [S1]).

**Outputs:**
*   Rendered RGB images $\hat{C}(r)$.
*   Transformed canonical points $x'_c$.
*   Predicted skinning weights $\hat{w}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **INN ($H$):** A neural network mapping view space coordinates $x_v$ to canonical space $x_c$, conditioned on skeleton pose $J_p$.
*   **Inverse INN ($H^{-1}$):** Maps canonical space back to view space, conditioned on canonical skeleton $J_{p0}$.
*   **Skinning Weight Network ($G_{x_c \to w}$):** Maps canonical point $x_c$ and joint $J_{p0}$ to a one-hot weight vector $\hat{w}$.
*   **LBS Module:** Applies skinning weights to transform canonical points to deformed space.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling & Rendering:** For each ray $r$, sample points. Use the INN to transform view-space points to canonical space. Render RGB $\hat{C}(r)$ using NeRF densities and colors.
2.  **Photometric Loss:** Compute $L_{rgb}$ between rendered $\hat{C}(r)$ and input pixel RGB $C(r)$.
3.  **Pose Loss:** Sample points $X_{J_p}$ around deformed bones and $X_{J_{p0}}$ around canonical bones. Transform $X_{J_p}$ to canonical space using INN ($G_{v \to c}$). Compute $L_{pose}$ as the L2 distance between transformed points and $X_{J_{p0}}$.
4.  **Skinning Weight Loss:** For each canonical joint $J_{p0}$, predict skinning weights $\hat{w}$ for points $x_c$. Enforce $\hat{w}$ to be a one-hot vector (1 for the respective joint, 0 for others). Compute $L_W$.
5.  **Cycle Consistency Loss:** Transform a view point $x_v$ to canonical space via $H^{-1}(x_v, J_{p0})$, then back to deformed space via $H(x_c, J_p)$. Compute $L_{INN}$ as the L2 distance between the result and original $x_v$.
6.  **Consistency Loss:** Compute the L2 distance between the INN-transformed point $x'_c$ and the skinning-weight-conditioned canonical point $x_c$. Compute $L_{Consis}$.
7.  **Aggregation:** Sum the losses with weights $\lambda_k$.
8.  **Backpropagation:** Update parameters for NeRF, INN, and skinning weight network.

**Formulas:**
*   **Reconstruction Loss:**
    $$ L_{rgb} = \sum_{r \in R} ||\hat{C}(r) - C(r)|| $$
    Where $R$ is the set of rays, $\hat{C}(r)$ is rendered RGB, $C(r)$ is input RGB [S1].
*   **Pose Loss:**
    $$ L_{pose} = \sum_{p \in P} ||X_{J_{p0}} - G_{v \to c}(X_{J_p})|| $$
    Where $P$ is total points sampled around bones, $G_{v \to c}$ is the INN transformation [S1].
*   **Skinning Weight Loss:**
    $$ L_W = ||G_{x_c \to w}(J_{p0}) - \hat{w}||_2^2 $$
    Where $\hat{w}$ is the target one-hot vector [S1].
*   **Cycle Consistency Loss (INN):**
    $$ L_{INN} = ||H(H^{-1}(x_v, J_{p0}), J_p) - x_v||_2^2 $$
    Where $H$ is the INN forward transform and $H^{-1}$ is the inverse [S1].
*   **Consistency Loss:**
    $$ L_{Consis} = ||x'_c - x_c||_2^2 $$
    Where $x'_c$ is the INN-transformed point and $x_c$ is the skinning-weight-conditioned canonical point [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_k$: Scalar hyperparameters for each loss term. Values not given in the sources.
*   $P$: Total number of points sampled around bones. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Ambiguity:** Without $L_{pose}$, $L_W$, and $L_{Consis}$, incorrect combinations of canonical points and skinning weights may satisfy forward LBS [S1].
*   **Initialization:** Shape loss $L_{shape}$ is used to accelerate learning by initializing point clouds around the canonical skeleton [S1].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Requires simultaneous backpropagation through NeRF, INN, and skinning weight networks. Complexity depends on the depth of the INN and the number of sampled points $P$.
*   **Performance:** Enables template-free reconstruction of dynamic scenes without explicit mesh templates [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Resolves ambiguities in deformation learning; jointly optimizes appearance and geometry [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of multiple loss weights; sensitive to initialization of point clouds [S1].

## Background
Originated in TFS-NeRF (Template-Free NeRF for Semantic 3D Reconstruction of Dynamic Scene) [S1]. The method addresses the lack of direct supervision for skinning weight prediction and INN by introducing skeleton-space constraints [S1]. It builds on prior work in dynamic NeRFs and LBS [S1].
