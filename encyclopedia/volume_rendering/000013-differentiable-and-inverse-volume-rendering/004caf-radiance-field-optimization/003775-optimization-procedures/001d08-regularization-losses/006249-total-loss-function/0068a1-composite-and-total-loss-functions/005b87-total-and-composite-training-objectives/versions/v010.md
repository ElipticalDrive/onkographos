# Total and Composite Training Objectives

Also called: Composite Loss, Multi-Task Loss, Joint Objective

## Overview
Total and Composite Training Objectives define the scalar objective function minimized during the optimization of radiance fields and related 3D representations. They aggregate multiple loss components—such as photometric rendering errors, geometric constraints, semantic regularizers, and structural priors—into a single value to guide network parameters toward high-fidelity reconstruction. Unlike single-term objectives, this composite structure allows simultaneous optimization of appearance, geometry, and semantic consistency, addressing the ambiguities inherent in inverse volume rendering. It differs from its siblings by providing the overarching mathematical framework ($L_{tot} = \sum \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k$) that unifies specific implementations like [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md), [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md), and [Joint Training Losses](16752-joint-training-losses.md).

## Shared principles
The core mechanism is the linear combination of heterogeneous loss terms, each addressing a specific aspect of the reconstruction (appearance, geometry, topology, or semantics).

The total loss $L_{tot}$ is defined as:
$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{k} \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

Where:
*   $\mathcal{L}_k$ is the $k$-th individual loss component (e.g., photometric, geometric, semantic).
*   $\lambda_k$ is the scalar hyperparameter weight balancing the contribution of $\mathcal{L}_k$.

The components are generally categorized into:
1.  **Photometric Losses:** Measure pixel-wise differences between rendered and ground truth images (e.g., L1, SSIM, Perceptual).
2.  **Geometric/Regularization Losses:** Enforce structural consistency, smoothness, or physical constraints (e.g., Depth Smoothness, Total Variation, Eikonal, Cycle Consistency).
3.  **Semantic/Structural Losses:** Align high-level features or enforce topological constraints (e.g., Codebook Loss, DINO features, Skinning Weight constraints).

The optimization process follows a standard loop:
1.  Sample rays or sub-volumes.
2.  Perform forward rendering to obtain predictions ($\hat{I}, \hat{V}, \hat{D}$).
3.  Compute individual losses $\mathcal{L}_k$ using predictions and ground truth/auxiliary data.
4.  Aggregate into $L_{tot}$.
5.  Backpropagate gradients to update parameters.

## Subtopics

### [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md)
[Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md) details the standard composition of the objective function for general radiance field representations (Gaussian Splatting, NeRF, Voxel Grids). It distinguishes itself by categorizing losses into photometric, geometric, and semantic components, and handling specific implementation details like background masking and codebook quantization. It is the default choice for static scene reconstruction where a single resolution or stage is optimized.

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md)
[Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md) addresses the problem of refining coarse reconstructions by balancing low-frequency structural consistency with high-frequency detail fidelity. It differs from single-scale losses by explicitly weighting contributions from different resolution stages or coarse/fine branches. It is chosen when using multi-stage or dynamically growing networks to prevent the sacrifice of global appearance for local texture.

### [Joint Training Losses](16752-joint-training-losses.md)
[Joint Training Losses](16752-joint-training-losses.md) defines a composite objective for the joint optimization of a Template-Free NeRF (TFS-NeRF) and an Implicit Neural Network (INN) for dynamic scene reconstruction. It distinguishes itself by explicitly constraining the INN and skinning weight networks through pose, cycle consistency, and skinning weight losses, which are absent in standard NeRF training. It is chosen for dynamic scene reconstruction where canonical-to-view mapping and skinning weights must be learned simultaneously.

#### Total Loss Function for Neural Scene Reconstruction
This variant combines seven specific components to train a neural scene reconstruction model using SRDF-SDF consistency [S1].
*   **Definition:** The loss aggregates RGB, normal, depth, Eikonal, smooth, SRDF-SDF consistency, and visibility losses [S1].
*   **Formula:**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_c + \lambda_n \mathcal{L}_n + \lambda_d \mathcal{L}_d + \lambda_e \mathcal{L}_e + \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_s + \lambda_{con} \mathcal{L}_{con} + \lambda_{vis} \mathcal{L}_{vis} $$
    where $\lambda_{(\cdot)}$ are the loss weights [S1].
*   **Step:** 2.5D depth and normal maps are computed using volume rendering with the density function $\sigma_{SRDF}$ to exploit geometric cues [S1].
*   **Parameters:** $\lambda_n, \lambda_d, \lambda_e, \lambda_s, \lambda_{con}, \lambda_{vis}$ are the weights for normal, depth, Eikonal, smooth, consistency, and visibility losses respectively. Typical values are not specified in the source [S1].

## Background
The concept of composite training objectives in neural rendering emerged alongside the first Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) papers, which introduced the combination of photometric loss with regularization terms to handle sparse views and occlusions. Early works like [S1] (Ray-Distance Volume Rendering for Neural Scene Reconstruction) formalized the inclusion of geometric priors (normal, depth, Eikonal) alongside photometric terms to improve 3D geometry reconstruction. Subsequent developments introduced multi-scale strategies [S1] and dynamic joint training for dynamic scenes [S1] to address specific limitations in static and single-resolution optimization.
