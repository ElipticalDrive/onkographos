# Total and Composite Training Objectives

Also called: Composite Loss Functions, Multi-Objective Training Objectives

## Overview
Total and Composite Training Objectives define the scalar objective functions minimized during the optimization of differentiable volume rendering models. They aggregate heterogeneous loss terms—photometric, geometric, semantic, and regularization—into a single value to guide the update of scene parameters and camera poses. This family sets itself apart from individual component losses by enforcing a multi-objective trade-off, ensuring rendered outputs match ground truth while maintaining geometric consistency, topological validity, or semantic alignment. It builds on [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md).

## Shared principles
The core mechanism is the weighted summation of distinct loss components $\mathcal{L}_k$, each targeting a specific property of the rendered output or the underlying scene representation. The general form is:

$$ L_{tot} = \sum_{k} \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

Where:
*   $L_{tot}$ is the total scalar loss.
*   $\mathcal{L}_k$ is the $k$-th loss term (e.g., photometric, depth, regularization).
*   $\lambda_k$ is the scalar hyperparameter balancing the contribution of $\mathcal{L}_k$.

The optimization process follows an iterative loop: sample rays or sub-volumes, perform forward rendering to obtain predictions, compute individual loss components, aggregate them into $L_{tot}$, and backpropagate gradients. Inputs typically include ground-truth images ($I$), optional depth maps ($D$), and camera poses ($P$). Outputs are the scalar loss and its gradients with respect to model parameters.

## Subtopics

### [Overall Training Loss](10621-overall-training-loss.md)
The overall training loss is the scalar objective function minimized during the optimization of differentiable volume rendering models. It aggregates heterogeneous loss terms—photometric, geometric, semantic, and regularization—into a single value to guide the update of scene parameters (e.g., Gaussian attributes, neural field weights, or voxel densities) and camera poses. It sets itself apart from individual component losses by enforcing a multi-objective trade-off, ensuring that the rendered output matches ground-truth images while maintaining geometric consistency, topological validity, or semantic alignment. It builds on [Total and Composite Training Objectives](16924-total-and-composite-training-objectives.md).

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Loss](13064-multi-scale-joint-training-loss.md)
Multi-scale Joint Training Loss is a composite objective function used in the second round of training for radiance-field optimization, designed to balance low-frequency structural agreement with high-frequency detail fidelity. It sets itself apart from standard single-scale losses by simultaneously optimizing across multiple network stages or resolution levels, ensuring that global appearance is preserved while local textures are refined.

### [Joint Training Losses](16752-joint-training-losses.md)
This method defines the composite objective function for jointly optimizing a Template-Free NeRF (TFS-NeRF), an Implicit Neural Network (INN) for spatial transformation, and a skinning weight prediction network. It solves the ambiguity in dynamic scene reconstruction where multiple combinations of canonical points and skinning weights can satisfy forward Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) equations. It distinguishes itself by enforcing constraints directly in the skeleton space (pose, skinning weights, and consistency) alongside standard photometric reconstruction, ensuring the INN learns a correct mapping between view and canonical spaces.

#### Total Loss Function for Neural Scene Reconstruction
**Definition**
The total loss function combines seven components to train the neural scene reconstruction model: RGB loss ($L_c$), normal loss ($L_n$), depth loss ($L_d$), Eikonal loss ($L_e$), smooth loss ($L_s$), SRDF-SDF consistency loss ($L_{con}$), and visibility loss ($L_{vis}$) [S1].

**Formula**
$$ \mathcal {L} = \mathcal {L}_c + \lambda _n \mathcal {L}_n + \lambda _d \mathcal {L}_d + \lambda _e \mathcal {L}_e + \lambda _s \mathcal {L}_s + \lambda _{con} \mathcal {L}_{con} + \lambda _{vis} \mathcal {L}_{vis} $$
where $\lambda_{(\cdot)}$ are the loss weights [S1].

**Steps**
Compute 2.5D depth and normal maps using volume rendering with the density function $\sigma_{SRDF}$ to exploit geometric cues and support training [S1].

**Parameters**
$\lambda_n, \lambda_d, \lambda_e, \lambda_s, \lambda_{con}, \lambda_{vis}$: Loss weights for normal, depth, Eikonal, smooth, consistency, and visibility losses respectively. Typical values are not specified in this text [S1].

## Background
The concept of composite training objectives in volume rendering evolved alongside differentiable rendering techniques. Early methods relied primarily on photometric reconstruction losses. As representations became more complex (e.g., Neural Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussian Splatting), the need for geometric and semantic regularization grew. The specific formulation combining RGB, normal, depth, Eikonal, smooth, consistency, and visibility losses is detailed in Ray-Distance Volume Rendering for Neural Scene Reconstruction [S1].
