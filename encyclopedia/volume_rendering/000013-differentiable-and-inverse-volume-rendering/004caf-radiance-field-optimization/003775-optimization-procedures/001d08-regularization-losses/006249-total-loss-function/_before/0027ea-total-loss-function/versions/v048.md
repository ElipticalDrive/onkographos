# Total Loss Function

Also called: Total Loss

## Overview
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective function minimized during the optimization of differentiable volume rendering models. It aggregates heterogeneous loss terms—photometric, geometric, semantic, and regularization—into a single scalar value to guide the gradient-based update of scene parameters (such as density, color, geometry, and camera poses) [S1, S2, S6, S8, S9]. Unlike individual component losses that measure specific discrepancies (e.g., pixel color error), the Total Loss enforces a multi-objective trade-off, ensuring rendered outputs match ground truth observations while maintaining geometric consistency, topological validity, and physical plausibility [S1, S2, S6, S8, S9, S16]. It builds on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator [44-radiance-field-optimization.md] by providing the scalar signal for backpropagation.

## How it works
The Total Loss Function is computed as a weighted sum of multiple loss components. The specific components and weights depend on the rendering representation (e.g., neural implicit fields, Gaussian splatting, voxel grids) and the available supervision signals (e.g., RGB images, depth maps, masks, normals) [S1, S2, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**:
  - Rendered image $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ or volume properties (density $\sigma$, color $\mathbf{c}$, SDF $f$, etc.) for a batch of rays $\mathbf{r}$ [S1, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S19].
  - Ground truth observations: RGB image $\mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*$, depth $D^*$, normal $\mathbf{N}$, mask $m$, transient data $H$, etc. [S1, S3, S5, S8, S9][S11, S12, unofficial][S19].
  - Scene parameters $\Theta$ (e.g., MLP weights, voxel embeddings, Gaussian attributes) [S3, S5, S6].
- **Outputs**:
  - Scalar loss value $L$ [S1, S2, S3, S5, S6, S8, S9][S11, S12, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].
  - Gradients $\nabla_\Theta L$ for optimizer update [S3, S5, S6].

### Data Structures
The loss function itself does not define a unique data structure but operates on tensors representing:
- **Rendered quantities**: $\hat{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ (RGB), $\hat{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ (depth), $\hat{\mathbf{N}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ (normals), $\hat{\sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{samples}}}$ (density), $\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{samples}}}$ (opacity) [S1, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S19].
- **Ground Truth**: $\mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*$, $D^*$, $\mathbf{N}$, $m$, $H$ [S1, S3, S5, S8, S9][S12, unofficial][S19].
- **Parameters**: $\Theta$ (MLP weights, voxel grids, Gaussian parameters) [S3, S5, S6].

### Formulas
The general form of the Total Loss is:
$$
L = \sum_{k} w_k L_k
$$
where $L_k$ are individual loss terms and $w_k$ are their respective weights [S1, S2, S3, S5, S6, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].

Common components include:

1.  **Photometric/Reconstruction Loss ($L_{\text{RGB}}$)**: Measures discrepancy between rendered and ground truth colors.
    $$
    L_{\text{RGB}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \| \hat{C}_i - C_i^* \|^p
    $$
    where $p=1$ (L1) [S11, unofficial][S19] or $p=2$ (L2/Charbonnier) [S2, S5, S7, S9]. SSIM may also be included [S2, S4].

2.  **Geometric Losses**:
    -   **Depth Loss ($L_{\text{depth}}$)**:
        $$
        L_{\text{depth}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \| \hat{D}_i - D_i^* \|
        $$
        [S8, S19].
    -   **Normal Loss ($L_{\text{normal}}$)**:
        $$
        L_{\text{normal}} = \| 1 - \hat{\mathbf{N}} \cdot \mathbf{N}^* \|_1
        $$
        [S8].
    -   **Eikonal Loss ($L_{\text{eik}}$)**: Enforces $\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_2 = 1$ for SDFs.
        $$
        L_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{M \cdot N} \sum_{i,j=1}^{M \cdot N} \| \nabla f(\mathbf{p}_{i,j}) - 1 \|^2
        $$
        [S6][S11, unofficial].
    -   **Curvature Loss ($L_{\text{curv}}$)**: [S6].
    -   **Smoothness Loss ($L_{\text{smooth}}$)**: Penalizes high-frequency variations in depth or color.
        $$
        L_{\text{smooth}} = |\partial_x d^*_t| e^{-|\partial_x I_t|} + |\partial_y d^*_t| e^{-|\partial_y I_t|}
        $$
        [S7].

3.  **Regularization Losses**:
    -   **Sparsity Loss ($L_{\text{sparse}}$)**: Encourages sparse occupancy.
        $$
        L_{\text{sparse}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p_i \in \mathcal{P}} |\alpha_i|
        $$
        [S2].
    -   **Opacity Loss ($L_{\text{opacity}}$)**: Encourages opacity to be 0 or 1.
        $$
        L_{\text{opacity}} = -\sum_k (\alpha^k \log(\alpha^k) + (1-\alpha^k) \log(1-\alpha^k))
        $$
        [S2].
    -   **Total Variation ($J_{\text{tv}}$)**: [S5].
    -   **KL Divergence ($D_{\text{KL}}$)**: Regularizes latent encodings.
        $$
        D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1))
        $$
        [S5].
    -   **Transient Data Loss ($E_H$)**: For NLOS rendering.
        $$
        E_H = \frac{1}{N_H} \sum_i (H_i - H_{R,i})^2
        $$
        [S3].
    -   **Volumetric Intensity Regularization ($E_{I_{pf}}$)**: [S3].
    -   **Surface Reflectance Regularization ($E_\rho$)**: [S3].

4.  **Advanced/Specific Losses**:
    -   **Dual-Buffer Loss ($L_{\text{dual}}$)**: For unbiased path tracing.
        $$
        L_{\text{dual}}(\hat{y}, \hat{y}', y) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^N \frac{(\hat{y}_i - y) \cdot (\hat{y}'_i - y)}{2}
        $$
        [S17, unofficial].
    -   **Empty Space Loss ($L_{\text{empty}}$)**:
        $$
        L_{\text{empty}} = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|
        $$
        [S12, unofficial].
    -   **Curriculum Loss ($L_{\text{final}}$)**:
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}
        $$
        [S13].
    -   **DeSplat Loss**:
        $$
        L = L_{\text{GS}} + \lambda_d \alpha_d + \lambda_{\text{bg}} L_{\text{bg}}
        $$
        [S15].
    -   **Vox-Fusion Free-Space/SDF Loss**:
        $$
        L_{\text{fs}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{|S_p|} \sum_{s \in S_p} (D_s - tr)^2
        $$
        [S19].

### Parameters
-   **Weights ($\lambda_k, w_k$)**: Balance the contribution of each term. Typical values vary by method:
    -   $\lambda_{\text{KL}} = 0.001$ [S5].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{eik}} = 0.1$ [S8][S11, unofficial].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{depth}} = 0.1$ [S8].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{normal}} = 0.05$ [S8].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{smooth}} = 0.01$ [S8].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{bubble}} = 0.5$ [S8].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{L2}} = 5.0$ [S9].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{photo}} = 1.0$ [S9].
    -   $\alpha = 5$ (real-world) or $1$ (synthetic) [S4].
    -   $\beta = -1e5$ [S13].
    -   $\lambda_d = 0.01$ [S15].
    -   $\lambda_{\text{bg}} = 0.15$ [S15].
    -   $w = 1.0$ [S12, unofficial].
-   **Optimizer**: Adam is commonly used [S5, S9].
-   **Learning Rate**: $10^{-4}$ for network weights [S5, S9].
-   **Batch Size**: 16 [S5].
-   **Training Iterations**: ~500,000 [S5].

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_total_loss(rendered, ground_truth, params, masks=None, normals=None, depths=None, transients=None):
    """
    Computes the total loss for differentiable volume rendering.
    
    Args:
        rendered: Dict containing rendered RGB, depth, normal, opacity, density, etc.
        ground_truth: Dict containing ground truth RGB, depth, normal, mask, transients, etc.
        params: Dict containing loss weights and hyperparameters.
        masks: Binary mask tensor (optional).
        normals: Ground truth normal tensor (optional).
        depths: Ground truth depth tensor (optional).
        transients: Ground truth transient data (optional).
        
    Returns:
        scalar_loss: Total loss value.
    """
    scalar_loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Photometric Loss (RGB)
    # Use L2 or Charbonnier if specified, else L1
    if params.get('loss_type') == 'l2':
        l_rgb = torch.mean((rendered['rgb'] - ground_truth['rgb']) ** 2)
    elif params.get('loss_type') == 'charbonnier':
        l_rgb = torch.mean(torch.sqrt((rendered['rgb'] - ground_truth['rgb']) ** 2 + params.get('eps', 1e-3)))
    else:
        l_rgb = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered['rgb'] - ground_truth['rgb']))
    
    scalar_loss += params.get('w_rgb', 1.0) * l_rgb
    
    # 2. Depth Loss
    if 'depth' in ground_truth:
        l_depth = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered['depth'] - ground_truth['depth']))
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_depth', 0.1) * l_depth
        
    # 3. Normal Loss
    if 'normal' in ground_truth:
        # Dot product between rendered and GT normal
        dot_prod = torch.sum(rendered['normal'] * ground_truth['normal'], dim=-1)
        l_normal = torch.mean(torch.abs(1.0 - dot_prod))
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_normal', 0.05) * l_normal
        
    # 4. Eikonal Loss (for SDFs)
    if 'sdf_grad' in rendered:
        # ||grad(f) - 1||^2
        grad_mag = torch.norm(rendered['sdf_grad'], dim=-1)
        l_eik = torch.mean((grad_mag - 1.0) ** 2)
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_eik', 0.1) * l_eik
        
    # 5. Regularization: Sparsity
    if 'opacity' in rendered:
        l_sparse = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered['opacity']))
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_sparse', 0.0) * l_sparse
        
    # 6. Regularization: Opacity (Binary Cross Entropy-like)
    if 'opacity' in rendered:
        alpha = rendered['opacity']
        # Clamp to avoid log(0)
        alpha_clamped = torch.clamp(alpha, 1e-6, 1 - 1e-6)
        l_opacity = -torch.mean(alpha_clamped * torch.log(alpha_clamped) + 
                                (1 - alpha_clamped) * torch.log(1 - alpha_clamped))
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_opacity', 0.0) * l_opacity
        
    # 7. Curriculum Learning (if enabled)
    if params.get('curriculum', False):
        t = params.get('current_iteration', 0)
        beta = params.get('beta', -1e5)
        # Exponential schedule
        weight_photo = 2 ** (beta * t)
        weight_rgb = 1 - weight_photo
        # Note: L_photo and L_depth are combined in the source formula
        l_photo = l_rgb # Assuming L_photo is similar to L_rgb or includes SSIM
        l_final = weight_photo * (l_depth + l_photo) + weight_rgb * l_rgb
        # Replace standard RGB loss with curriculum version if active
        scalar_loss = scalar_loss - params.get('w_rgb', 1.0) * l_rgb + l_final
        
    # 8. Empty Space Loss (if mask provided)
    if masks is not None and 'transmittance' in rendered:
        # T is transmittance/throughput
        t = rendered['transmittance']
        # Loss is applied where mask is 0 (background)
        mask_float = masks.float()
        l_empty = torch.mean((1.0 - mask_float) * torch.abs(t - 0.0))
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_empty', 1.0) * l_empty
        
    # 9. Dual-Buffer Loss (if two renders provided)
    if 'rgb2' in rendered:
        y = rendered['rgb']
        y_prime = rendered['rgb2']
        y_gt = ground_truth['rgb']
        # Correlate errors
        error1 = y - y_gt
        error2 = y_prime - y_gt
        l_dual = torch.mean(error1 * error2) / 2.0
        scalar_loss += params.get('w_dual', 1.0) * l_dual
        
    return scalar_loss
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
-   **Biased Gradients**: Using L2/MSE loss against a single noisy render (e.g., from path tracing) yields biased gradients because the squared noise does not average to zero [S17, unofficial]. Use Dual-Buffer loss or unbiased estimators [S17, unofficial].
-   **Weight Sensitivity**: Using 10x or 0.1x weights on surface constraints (e.g., $L_{DC}, L_{NCC}, L_{Fit}$) degrades reconstruction accuracy by not balancing well with volume rendering constraints [S1].
-   **Precision**: Step sizes in normalized voxel space $[-1, 1]^3$ are used to make the end of the ray the expected saturation point at network initialization [S5].
-   **Memory**: Randomly sampling pixels (e.g., 128x128) reduces memory usage during training [S5].

## Subtopics

### [Opacity Loss](4203-opacity-loss.md)
The Opacity Loss is a regularization term used in differentiable volume rendering to enforce geometric consistency when a binary object mask is available. It forces the rendered opacity of rays passing through the object region to align with the mask distribution, typically resulting in an opaque surface. It is distinct from photometric losses by operating directly on the alpha compositing output rather than pixel color, and from depth losses by using binary segmentation data rather than continuous depth estimates.

### [Auxiliary Losses](4678-auxiliary-losses.md)
Auxiliary losses are regularization terms added to the total training objective in differentiable volume rendering to improve rendering quality, prevent overfitting, and enforce physical plausibility in particle or voxel trajectories. They distinguish themselves from the primary photometric reconstruction loss by supervising intermediate representations (such as per-point color or opacity distributions) rather than just the final rendered image. This method builds on [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md) by providing specific components for stability and structure.

### [Loss Function Configuration](6782-loss-function-configuration.md)
Loss Function Configuration defines the composite objective function and its hyperparameters used to optimize radiance fields, balancing pixel-wise fidelity, structural similarity, perceptual quality, and various regularization constraints. It differs from single-metric approaches by combining multiple perceptual and pixel-wise metrics to supervise optimization, addressing the problem of generating high-fidelity, visually consistent rendered images. It excludes explicit physical priors like visibility or normal consistency unless specified by a variant.

### [Total Variation Loss](7047-total-variation-loss.md)
Total Variation (TV) Loss is a regularization constraint applied to explicit feature volumes or bilateral grid parameters to enforce spatial smoothness in learned color transformations. It differs from photometric reconstruction losses by penalizing high-frequency variations in the latent representation rather than matching pixel intensities. It is typically combined with other regularization terms, such as Eikonal loss, within the total training objective.

### [Sparse Depth Consistency Loss](7294-sparse-depth-consistency-loss.md)
The Sparse Depth Consistency Loss enforces geometric consistency between rendered depth maps and sparse, structure-from-motion (SfM) derived depth estimates in radiance field optimization. It distinguishes itself from photometric losses by directly penalizing depth discrepancies, thereby mitigating floaters and depth discontinuities common in sparse-input scenarios.

### [Discriminator Loss with Gradient Penalty](7695-discriminator-loss-with-gradient-penalty.md)
A regularization loss for training the discriminator in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) that enforces Lipschitz continuity by penalizing the gradient norm of the discriminator's output with respect to its input. It distinguishes itself from weight clipping or spectral normalization methods by using an explicit penalty term on the gradients of real samples, specifically stabilizing training in radiance-field optimization contexts like GANeRF.

### [Background Blending for Loss Computation](7719-background-blending-for-loss-computation.md)
This method addresses the discrepancy between the rendered image and the ground truth when the background is not black, ensuring accurate loss computation in volume rendering optimization. It blends the predicted RGB image with the ground truth background color based on the accumulated opacity. It differs from [Photometric and Image Reconstruction Losses](9789-photometric-and-image-reconstruction-losses.md) by explicitly handling the background contribution before calculating the error, and from [Luminance Background Calculation](#luminance-background-calculation) by using the ground truth background rather than a style-derived luminance value.

### [Loss Weight Configuration](7883-loss-weight-configuration.md)
Loss weight configuration determines the scalar coefficients that balance distinct error terms within a composite differentiable rendering loss function. It solves the problem of optimizing heterogeneous objectives by preventing one term from dominating the gradient updates. Unlike [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md), which defines the mathematical structure of the error terms, this method focuses exclusively on the hyperparameter selection that dictates their relative influence during training.

### [Photometric and Image Reconstruction Losses](9789-photometric-and-image-reconstruction-losses.md)
This family defines the primary objective functions for differentiable volume rendering, measuring the discrepancy between rendered images and ground-truth observations to drive gradient-based optimization. It sets itself apart from sibling regularization losses (e.g., Total Variation, Eikonal) by focusing exclusively on pixel-level or perceptual fidelity rather than geometric constraints or smoothness priors.

### [Specific Method Implementations](9794-specific-method-implementations.md)
This family details concrete loss function formulations and training routines used in specific radiance-field optimization methods [G]. It covers implementations ranging from neural implicit fields to Gaussian splatting and sparse voxel representations [G]. These implementations differ in their component terms (e.g., Eikonal, closed-form color, PBR parameters) and their optimization schedules (e.g., delayed updates, early stopping) [G].

### [Numerical Gradient Eikonal Loss](9954-numerical-gradient-eikonal-loss.md)
Numerical Gradient Eikonal Loss is a regularization technique used in differentiable volume rendering with hash-encoded Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) to enforce the eikonal equation $\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_2 = 1$. It addresses the locality problem inherent in analytical gradients of hash encodings, where non-differentiable rounding operations restrict back-propagation updates to only the local hash grid cells involved in trilinear interpolation. By computing surface normals via numerical finite differences, the method distributes optimization updates beyond the immediate local grid cell, acting as a smoothed gradient that ensures non-local smoothness and consistent surface reconstruction without requiring auxiliary networks.

### [Total and Composite Training Objectives](16924-total-and-composite-training-objectives.md)
Total and Composite Training Objectives define the scalar objective functions minimized during the optimization of differentiable volume rendering models. They aggregate heterogeneous loss terms—photometric, geometric, semantic, and regularization—into a single value to guide the update of scene parameters and camera poses. This family sets itself apart from individual component losses by enforcing a multi-objective trade-off, ensuring rendered outputs match ground truth while maintaining geometric consistency, topological validity, or semantic alignment. It builds on [Total Loss Function](4177-total-loss-function.md).

### Empty Space Loss
An AOV loss that encourages the rendering throughput to equal 0.0 in regions where a binary object mask is inactive [S12, unofficial]. It is computed as $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|$, where $m_i$ is the binary mask and $T_i$ is the throughput [S12, unofficial]. The weight $w$ is typically 1.0 [S12, unofficial].

### Curriculum Learning Loss
A curriculum learning loss that transitions from depth/photometric supervision to RGB supervision during training, controlled by an exponential schedule based on iteration count [S13]. The formula is $\mathcal {L}_{\text {final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal {L}_{\text {depth}} + \mathcal {L}_{\text {photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal {L}_{\text {rgb}}$, with $\beta = -1e5$ [S13].

### Softmax Weighted Volume Rendering
A rendering equation that accumulates colors along a ray weighted by densities after applying softmax, used to speed up convergence [S14]. The total loss is the sum of contrastive loss and color loss: $L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{contrast}} + L_{\text{color}}$ [S14].

### DeSplat Loss Function
The total loss combines the standard Gaussian Splatting loss, a distractor regularization term, and a background regularization term: $L = L_{\text{GS}} + \lambda_d \alpha_d + \lambda_{\text{bg}} L_{\text{bg}}$ [S15]. Typical weights are $\lambda_d = 0.01$ and $\lambda_{\text{bg}} = 0.15$ [S15].

### Energy Function for Optimization
An energy function $E(c) = H(c) + C(c)$ where $H(c)$ is the coherence metric and $C(c)$ is the context metric [S16]. Optimization ends successfully if $E(c) < 1$ [S16].

### Dual-Buffer Loss
A loss function for material reconstruction using unbiased path tracing that cancels Monte Carlo noise by correlating errors from two independent noisy estimates against the ground truth, avoiding biased gradients from L2/MSE on single noisy renders [S17, unofficial]. Formula: $L_{\text{dual}}(\hat{y}, \hat{y}', y) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N} ((\hat{y}_i - y) \cdot (\hat{y}'_i - y)) / 2$ [S17, unofficial].

### Subset Consistency Objective
When subset consistency is enabled, the final objective is $L = 0.5 (L_{\text{subset}}^a + L_{\text{subset}}^b) + L_{\text{con}}$, where $L_{\text{subset}}^k = L_{\text{ren}}^k + L_{\text{reg}}^k$ for $k \in \{a,b\}$ [S18].

### Vox-Fusion Optimization Losses
A set of four loss functions used to supervise the implicit network and voxel embeddings: RGB loss, depth loss, free-space loss, and SDF loss [S19]. RGB Loss: $L_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{i=0}^{|P|} \|C_i - C^{gt}_i\|$ [S19]. Free-Space Loss: $L_{\text{fs}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{|S_p|} \sum_{s \in S_p} (D_s - tr)^2$ [S19]. SDF Loss: $L_{\text{sdf}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{|S_p|} \sum_{s \in S_p} (D_s - D^{gt}_s)^2$ [S19].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity**: Depends on the number of loss terms and the complexity of the rendering operator. Each term requires a forward pass or additional computation (e.g., gradient computation for Eikonal loss) [S1, S5, S8].
-   **Memory Complexity**: High memory usage can occur if intermediate states are stored for backpropagation, especially with reverse-mode AD [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. Techniques like random pixel sampling [S5] or adjoint methods [44-radiance-field-optimization.md] can mitigate this.
-   **Performance**: Training can take significant time (e.g., 10 days on a single NVIDIA Tesla V100 for 500,000 iterations) [S5].
-   **Strengths**: Flexible, allows multi-objective optimization, can incorporate various forms of supervision (RGB, depth, normals, masks) [S1, S2, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].
-   **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of weights to balance competing objectives [S1, S2, S8]. Poorly chosen weights can lead to suboptimal convergence or artifacts [S1].
-   **When to Use**: When optimizing radiance fields with multiple types of supervision or when specific geometric/physical constraints need to be enforced [S1, S2, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].
-   **When Not to Use**: When a simple photometric reconstruction is sufficient and computational resources are limited [S9].

## Background
The concept of a composite loss function for optimizing differentiable rendering has evolved alongside the development of neural radiance fields (NeRF) and other implicit representations. Early works like Neural Volumes [S5] introduced variational autoencoder components and TV regularization. Subsequent methods incorporated geometric constraints like Eikonal loss [S6][S11, unofficial], depth consistency [S7, S8, S19], and normal alignment [S8]. The use of curriculum learning [S13], contrastive learning [S14], and GAN-based discriminators [S17, unofficial] has further diversified the components of the total loss. The specific formulation of the total loss is highly dependent on the underlying representation (e.g., MLP, Gaussian Splatting, Voxel Grid) and the available data [S1, S2, S5, S8, S9][S11, unofficial][S14, S15, S19].
