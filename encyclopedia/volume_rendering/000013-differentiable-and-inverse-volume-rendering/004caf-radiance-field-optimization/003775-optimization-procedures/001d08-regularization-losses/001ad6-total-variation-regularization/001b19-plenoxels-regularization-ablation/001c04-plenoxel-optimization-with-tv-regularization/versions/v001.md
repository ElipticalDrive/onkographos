# Plenoxel Optimization with TV Regularization

Also called: Chambolle-Pock TV, Plenoxels TV and sparsity regularization, TV regularization, Total Variation Reconstruction

## Overview
Plenoxel Optimization with TV Regularization is a method for training sparse voxel-based radiance fields by combining mean squared error (MSE) reconstruction loss with Total Variation (TV) regularization on voxel opacities and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients [S1]. It solves the problem of visual artifacts and memory bloat in high-dimensional, non-convex optimization by enforcing spatial smoothness and sparsity [S1]. Unlike standard differentiable rendering which relies solely on pixel-wise MSE, this method explicitly penalizes high-frequency variations in the voxel grid, allowing for faster convergence and cleaner geometry [S1]. It builds on [Plenoxels](52-plenoxels.md) and [Plenoxels regularization ablation](901-plenoxels-regularization-ablation.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ground truth images and corresponding camera poses.
- A sparse voxel grid structure containing opacity ($\sigma$) and SH coefficients for each active voxel [S1].
- Optimization hyperparameters: $\lambda_{TV}$ (TV weight), learning rates for $\sigma$ and SH coefficients, and batch sizes for rays $\mathcal{R}$ and voxels $\mathcal{V}$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Optimized voxel opacities and SH coefficients [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- **Voxel Grid:** A sparse 3D grid where each active voxel stores a scalar opacity $\sigma$ and a vector of SH coefficients. The dimensions of the SH vector depend on the chosen SH degree [S1].
- **Ray Sample $\mathcal{R}$:** A stochastic subset of training rays used for the reconstruction loss [S1].
- **Voxel Sample $\mathcal{V}$:** A stochastic subset of active voxels used for the TV loss [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_{TV} \mathcal{L}_{TV}
$$

The reconstruction loss is the MSE between rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth $C(\mathbf{r})$ [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{recon} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \|C(\mathbf{r}) - \hat{C}(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2
$$

The TV regularization loss is the sum of isotropic gradients over sampled voxels and SH dimensions [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{TV} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|} \sum_{\substack{\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{V} \\ d \in [D]}} \sqrt{\Delta_x^2(\mathbf{v}, d) + \Delta_y^2(\mathbf{v}, d) + \Delta_z^2(\mathbf{v}, d)}
$$
where $D$ is the dimensionality of the SH coefficients, and $\Delta^2_x(\mathbf{v}, d)$ is the squared difference between the $d$-th value in voxel $\mathbf{v}=(i,j,k)$ and its neighbor $(i+1,j,k)$, normalized by the voxel resolution. Analogous definitions apply for $\Delta^2_y$ and $\Delta^2_z$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_{TV}$: Fixed per scene type (bounded, forward-facing, 360°). Different weights are used for SH coefficients and $\sigma$ [S1].
- Optimizer: RMSProp, chosen to handle ill-conditioning without the cost of second-order methods [S1].
- Learning Rate: Fixed for all scenes in main experiments, but tuned separately for opacity and harmonic coefficients [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Sample Rays and Voxels:** For each optimization step, draw a stochastic batch of rays $\mathcal{R}$ for reconstruction and a stochastic batch of voxels $\mathcal{V}$ for TV regularization [S1].
2. **Render:** Compute rendered colors $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for all $\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}$ using differentiable volume rendering [S1].
3. **Compute Reconstruction Loss:** Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{recon}$ using the formula above [S1].
4. **Compute TV Loss:** For each voxel $\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{V}$ and each SH dimension $d \in [D]$, compute the finite differences in x, y, and z directions. Normalize by resolution, square them, sum them, and take the square root to get the isotropic gradient magnitude. Sum over all sampled voxels and dimensions to get $\mathcal{L}_{TV}$ [S1].
5. **Combine Losses:** Compute $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{recon} + \lambda_{TV} \mathcal{L}_{TV}$ [S1].
6. **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to voxel opacities and SH coefficients [S1].
7. **Update:** Update parameters using RMSProp [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def optimize_plenoxels_tv(voxel_grid, gt_images, cameras, lambda_tv, lr_sigma, lr_sh, n_iters, batch_size_rays, batch_size_voxels):
    """
    Optimizes Plenoxel parameters using TV regularization.
    voxel_grid: Sparse voxel grid with opacity and SH coefficients.
    gt_images: List of ground truth images.
    cameras: List of camera poses.
    lambda_tv: TV regularization weight.
    lr_sigma: Learning rate for opacity.
    lr_sh: Learning rate for SH coefficients.
    n_iters: Number of optimization iterations.
    batch_size_rays: Number of rays per batch.
    batch_size_voxels: Number of voxels per batch.
    """
    optimizer_sigma = RMSProp(voxel_grid.opacities, lr=lr_sigma)
    optimizer_sh = RMSProp(voxel_grid.sh_coeffs, lr=lr_sh)
    
    for step in range(n_iters):
        # 1. Sample rays and voxels
        rays = sample_rays(cameras, batch_size_rays)
        voxels = sample_voxels(voxel_grid, batch_size_voxels)
        
        # 2. Render colors for sampled rays
        rendered_colors = render_rays(voxel_grid, rays)
        
        # 3. Compute reconstruction loss
        gt_colors = get_gt_colors(rays, gt_images)
        l_recon = mean_squared_error(rendered_colors, gt_colors)
        
        # 4. Compute TV loss
        l_tv = compute_tv_loss(voxel_grid, voxels)
        
        # 5. Combine losses
        loss = l_recon + lambda_tv * l_tv
        
        # 6. Backpropagate
        loss.backward()
        
        # 7. Update parameters
        optimizer_sigma.step()
        optimizer_sh.step()
        
        # Clear gradients
        optimizer_sigma.zero_grad()
        optimizer_sh.zero_grad()

def compute_tv_loss(voxel_grid, voxels):
    """
    Computes the Total Variation loss for a batch of voxels.
    """
    tv_sum = 0.0
    count = 0
    for v in voxels:
        # Get SH coefficients and opacity for voxel v
        sh_coeffs_v = voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v)
        sigma_v = voxel_grid.get_opacity(v)
        
        # Get neighbors
        v_x = get_neighbor(v, direction='x')
        v_y = get_neighbor(v, direction='y')
        v_z = get_neighbor(v, direction='z')
        
        # Check if neighbors exist (boundary check)
        if v_x is not None:
            sh_coeffs_x = voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_x)
            sigma_x = voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_x)
            # Compute differences for SH coefficients
            for d in range(len(sh_coeffs_v)):
                diff_x = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - sh_coeffs_x[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                tv_sum += diff_x ** 2
            # Compute difference for opacity
            diff_x_sigma = (sigma_v - sigma_x) / voxel_grid.resolution
            tv_sum += diff_x_sigma ** 2
            
        if v_y is not None:
            sh_coeffs_y = voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_y)
            sigma_y = voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_y)
            for d in range(len(sh_coeffs_v)):
                diff_y = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - sh_coeffs_y[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                tv_sum += diff_y ** 2
            diff_y_sigma = (sigma_v - sigma_y) / voxel_grid.resolution
            tv_sum += diff_y_sigma ** 2
            
        if v_z is not None:
            sh_coeffs_z = voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_z)
            sigma_z = voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_z)
            for d in range(len(sh_coeffs_v)):
                diff_z = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - sh_coeffs_z[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                tv_sum += diff_z ** 2
            diff_z_sigma = (sigma_v - sigma_z) / voxel_grid.resolution
            tv_sum += diff_z_sigma ** 2
            
        count += 1
        
    # Take square root of sum of squares (isotropic TV)
    # Note: The formula in [S1] sums the square roots of the squared differences.
    # The implementation above sums the squared differences first. 
    # To match [S1] exactly, we should compute the norm per voxel/dimension.
    # However, [S1] defines L_TV as sum over v,d of sqrt(sum of squared diffs).
    # So we need to restructure the loop.
    
    # Corrected implementation matching [S1] formula:
    tv_sum = 0.0
    count = 0
    for v in voxels:
        sh_coeffs_v = voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v)
        sigma_v = voxel_grid.get_opacity(v)
        
        for d in range(len(sh_coeffs_v)):
            grad_sq_sum = 0.0
            # X direction
            v_x = get_neighbor(v, 'x')
            if v_x is not None:
                diff = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_x)[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                grad_sq_sum += diff ** 2
            # Y direction
            v_y = get_neighbor(v, 'y')
            if v_y is not None:
                diff = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_y)[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                grad_sq_sum += diff ** 2
            # Z direction
            v_z = get_neighbor(v, 'z')
            if v_z is not None:
                diff = (sh_coeffs_v[d] - voxel_grid.get_sh_coeffs(v_z)[d]) / voxel_grid.resolution
                grad_sq_sum += diff ** 2
            tv_sum += sqrt(grad_sq_sum)
            
        # Opacity TV
        grad_sq_sum_sigma = 0.0
        v_x = get_neighbor(v, 'x')
        if v_x is not None:
            diff = (sigma_v - voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_x)) / voxel_grid.resolution
            grad_sq_sum_sigma += diff ** 2
        v_y = get_neighbor(v, 'y')
        if v_y is not None:
            diff = (sigma_v - voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_y)) / voxel_grid.resolution
            grad_sq_sum_sigma += diff ** 2
        v_z = get_neighbor(v, 'z')
        if v_z is not None:
            diff = (sigma_v - voxel_grid.get_opacity(v_z)) / voxel_grid.resolution
            grad_sq_sum_sigma += diff ** 2
        tv_sum += sqrt(grad_sq_sum_sigma)
        
        count += 1
        
    return tv_sum / count
```

## Subtopics

### Sparse Gradient Optimization
This variant leverages the observation that during the first 1-2 minutes of optimization (or first 12,800 batches for synthetic scenes), fewer than 10% of voxels have non-zero gradients, with as few as 1% updating per batch in synthetic cases [S1]. This sparsity allows for significant computational savings by only updating active voxels [S1]. Implementation requires a custom PyTorch CUDA extension for fast differentiable volume rendering to exploit this sparsity efficiently [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The method uses stochastic sampling for both rays and voxels, making each iteration faster than full-batch methods [S1]. The use of RMSProp avoids the high cost of second-order methods [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the sparse voxel grid size and the number of SH coefficients per voxel [S1].
- **Performance:** Achieves high-quality results after less than 1.5 minutes (first epoch) on synthetic scenes [S1]. Dramatic speedup in training compared to prior methods [S1].
- **Strengths:** Prevents visual artifacts, controls memory usage, handles ill-conditioning, and enables fast training [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of $\lambda_{TV}$ and learning rates for different parameters [S1]. The non-convex nature of the rendering formula makes optimization challenging [S1].
- **When to use:** When training sparse voxel radiance fields and needing to prevent artifacts and control memory [S1].
- **When not to use:** When real-time training speed is not critical and simpler methods suffice, or when the scene is too complex for the sparse grid to capture efficiently.

## Background
The method originates from the Plenoxels paper, which introduced radiance fields without neural networks using sparse voxel grids and SH coefficients [S1]. The TV regularization was introduced to address artifacts and improve optimization stability [S1]. The implementation details, including the use of ODL for TV regularization, are described in an unofficial GitHub repository [S2, unofficial].
