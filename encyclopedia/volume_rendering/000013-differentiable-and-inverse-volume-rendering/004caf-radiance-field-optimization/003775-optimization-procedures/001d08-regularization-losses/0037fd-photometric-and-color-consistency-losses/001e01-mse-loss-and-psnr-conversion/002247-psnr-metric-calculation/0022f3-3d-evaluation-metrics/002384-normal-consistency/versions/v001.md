# Normal Consistency


## Overview
Normal Consistency (NC) is a metric for 3D surface reconstruction that measures the angular deviation between estimated surface normals and ground truth normals [S1]. It quantifies how well the orientation of the reconstructed surface aligns with the underlying geometry, serving as a complementary measure to distance-based metrics like Chamfer Distance [S1]. Unlike distance metrics that assess positional accuracy, NC specifically evaluates surface smoothness and orientation fidelity [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A set of $N$ points on the estimated surface, their corresponding estimated surface normals $\{N_i\}_{i=1}^N$, and the ground truth surface normals $\{N_i^*\}_{i=1}^N$ at the corresponding locations.
- **Output:** A scalar percentage value representing the average normal consistency error.

**Data Structures:**
- $N_i$: Estimated surface normal vector for the $i$-th point.
- $N_i^*$: Ground truth surface normal vector for the $i$-th point.
- $N$: Total number of points on the surface.

**Steps:**
1. For each point $i$ from $1$ to $N$:
   a. Compute the cosine similarity between the estimated normal $N_i$ and the ground truth normal $N_i^*$:
      $$ \cos(N_i, N_i^*) = N_i \cdot N_i^* $$
      (Assuming $N_i$ and $N_i^*$ are unit vectors).
   b. Compute the term $(1 - \cos(N_i, N_i^*))$.
2. Sum these terms for all $N$ points.
3. Divide the sum by $N$ to get the mean error.
4. Multiply by $100\%$ to express the result as a percentage.

**Formula:**
$$ \text{NC} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (1 - \cos(N_i, N_i^*)) \times 100\% $$

**Parameters:**
- $N_i, N_i^*$: Estimated and ground truth surface normals for the $i$-th point [S1].
- $N$: Number of points on the surface [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The normalization term $\frac{1}{N}$ ensures the metric is independent of the number of points on the surface [S1].
- If normals are not normalized, the cosine similarity calculation must explicitly divide by the magnitudes of the vectors.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def normal_consistency(estimated_normals, ground_truth_normals):
    """
    Calculate Normal Consistency metric.
    
    Args:
        estimated_normals: Array of shape (N, 3) containing estimated surface normals.
        ground_truth_normals: Array of shape (N, 3) containing ground truth surface normals.
        
    Returns:
        float: NC value in percentage.
    """
    # Ensure inputs are numpy arrays
    import numpy as np
    
    estimated_normals = np.asarray(estimated_normals)
    ground_truth_normals = np.asarray(ground_truth_normals)
    
    # Check shapes
    if estimated_normals.shape != ground_truth_normals.shape:
        raise ValueError("Estimated and ground truth normals must have the same shape.")
    
    # Normalize vectors to unit length if they are not already
    # This handles cases where normals might not be pre-normalized
    est_norms = estimated_normals / (np.linalg.norm(estimated_normals, axis=1, keepdims=True) + 1e-8)
    gt_norms = ground_truth_normals / (np.linalg.norm(ground_truth_normals, axis=1, keepdims=True) + 1e-8)
    
    # Compute dot products (cosine similarity for unit vectors)
    # Shape: (N,)
    dot_products = np.sum(est_norms * gt_norms, axis=1)
    
    # Clamp to [-1, 1] to avoid numerical errors in acos if used later, 
    # though here we just use dot product directly in the formula
    # The formula uses cos(Ni, Ni*), which is the dot product for unit vectors.
    
    # Compute (1 - cos(Ni, Ni*))
    errors = 1.0 - dot_products
    
    # Average error
    mean_error = np.mean(errors)
    
    # Convert to percentage
    nc_value = mean_error * 100.0
    
    return nc_value
```

## Subtopics

### Angular Normal Error Metric
Measures surface orientation accuracy by comparing ground truth mesh normals with network-computed SDF gradients (normalized) at sampled surface points [S2, unofficial]. It computes the angular difference in degrees [S2, unofficial].
- **Formula:** $\theta = \arccos( | \mathbf{n}_{mesh} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{net} | ) \times \frac{180}{\pi}$ where $\mathbf{n}_{mesh}$ and $\mathbf{n}_{net}$ are normalized mesh and network normals [S2, unofficial].
- **Parameters:** $n\_samples$: 2097152 (default number of surface points); $batch\_size$: 16384 (processing chunk size) [S2, unofficial].
- **Steps:** Sample points and get face normals. Scale points by `training_data.R`. Batch process with `batch_size = 16384`: enable gradients, compute SDF, compute normals via `net.normal(batch_points)`, normalize gradients. Concatenate normals. Normalize mesh normals. Compute dot products, clamp to [-1, 1], apply `acos(abs(dot))`, convert to degrees. Compute MAE, Std, Max, Median [S2, unofficial].
- **Code:**
  ```python
  dot_products = torch.sum(mesh_normals_normalized * net_normals_normalized, dim=1)
  dot_products = torch.clamp(dot_products, -1.0, 1.0)
  angular_differences_rad = torch.acos(torch.abs(dot_products))
  angular_differences_deg = torch.rad2deg(angular_differences_rad)
  ```
- **Pitfall:** Dot products may exceed [-1, 1] due to floating point precision. Clamped to [-1.0, 1.0] before `acos` to avoid NaNs [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of points, as it requires a single pass over all points to compute dot products and averages [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the normals for both estimated and ground truth surfaces [S1].
- **Strengths:** Provides a direct measure of surface orientation accuracy, which is crucial for applications requiring smooth surfaces or accurate lighting calculations [S1]. It is independent of the number of points on the surface [S1].
- **Limitations:** Does not account for positional errors; a surface can have perfect normal consistency but be displaced from the ground truth [S1]. It is sensitive to the quality of normal estimation, especially for noisy or complex geometries [S1].
- **When to use:** When surface orientation and smoothness are critical, such as in rendering or physical simulation [S1].
- **When not to use:** When positional accuracy is the primary concern, or when the ground truth normals are not available [S1].

## Background
Normal Consistency was introduced as a standard metric for evaluating 3D surface reconstruction tasks [S1]. It is often used in conjunction with Chamfer Distance to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of reconstruction quality [S1]. The metric has been widely adopted in recent literature on 3D shape reconstruction, particularly in methods involving implicit surfaces and neural networks [S1].
