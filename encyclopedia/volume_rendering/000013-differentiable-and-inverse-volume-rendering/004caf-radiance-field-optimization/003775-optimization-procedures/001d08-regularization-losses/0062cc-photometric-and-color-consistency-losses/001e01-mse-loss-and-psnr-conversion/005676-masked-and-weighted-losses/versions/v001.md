# Masked and Weighted Losses


## Overview
Masked and Weighted Losses modify standard photometric error metrics (such as MSE or Huber loss) by excluding irrelevant regions from the error calculation or adjusting the contribution of individual pixels based on geometric properties. This solves the problem of optimization bias caused by empty background, invalid rays, or multiscale resolution discrepancies, ensuring the model focuses only on meaningful signal [S1, S1, S2, S2]. It sets itself apart from standard MSE by dynamically defining a Region of Interest (ROI) based on active radiance or ray validity, and by weighting losses to account for pixel footprint area [S1, S1, S2, S2].

## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of applying a binary mask $M$ or a scalar weight $W$ to the per-pixel or per-ray loss term $L_{pixel}$ before aggregation. The total loss $L$ is computed as:
$$ L = \frac{1}{N_{valid}} \sum_{i} M_i \cdot W_i \cdot L_{pixel}(y_i, \hat{y}_i) $$
where $y_i$ is the ground truth, $\hat{y}_i$ is the prediction, and $N_{valid}$ is the count of valid elements (either valid rays or non-masked pixels) to prevent division by zero or bias from empty regions [S1, S1, S2, S2].

Key shared terms:
- **Mask ($M$):** A binary indicator (0 or 1) determining if a sample contributes to the loss. Sources define masks based on ray validity (intersection with scene geometry) [S1, S1] or spatial ROI (union of active radiance and ground truth data) [S1, S1].
- **Weight ($W$):** A scalar multiplier adjusting the influence of a sample. In multiscale contexts, this is the area scaling factor [S1]. In density editing, this is implicit in the cross-entropy formulation over specific ray sets [S2].
- **Valid Ray/Region:** A sample is considered valid if it carries meaningful signal. For training, this excludes background-only rays [S1, S1]. For evaluation, it excludes empty froxels [S1, S1].

## Subtopics

### [Masked Volume Metrics](1927-masked-volume-metrics.md)
Computes evaluation metrics (MSE, PSNR) by excluding empty or irrelevant froxels. It sets itself apart by defining the Region of Interest (ROI) as the union of the rendered scenario's active radiance and the ground truth's active data, ensuring only froxels participating in light transport are evaluated. Choose this for volumetric evaluation to prevent skew from empty space.

### [Training Loss Computation with Huber and Masking](4765-training-loss-computation-with-huber-and-masking.md)
Computes photometric training loss using Huber loss, masked by ray validity flags. It differs from standard MSE by being more robust to outliers while maintaining differentiability [G]. Choose this for differentiable volume rendering training to handle outliers and exclude background rays.

### Area Loss Scaling
**Definition:** Scaling the loss of each pixel by the area of that pixel's footprint in the original image to ensure low-resolution pixels have comparable influence to high-resolution pixels in multiscale datasets [S1].
**Step:** For multiscale datasets, calculate the area scaling factor based on the downsampling factor (e.g., factor of 2 -> area factor 4, factor of 4 -> area factor 16). Multiply the pixel loss by this factor [S1].

### Density-based Loss for Shape Addition
**Definition:** A method for shape addition using a density-based loss which is faster but less effective than composite image reconstruction [S2]. The loss encourages predicted densities to match target densities for edited regions and be unchanged for unedited regions [S2].
**Step:** Let $y_f = \{(r, \sigma_f)\}$ be the set of rays and densities in the foreground mask, and $y_b = \{(r, \sigma_b)\}$ be the set of rays and densities in the background mask. $\sigma_f$ are density values of the rays copied from the new object instance, and $\sigma_b$ represent density values of the rays from the original instance. Let $\sigma_r$ be the densities predicted by the model for ray $r$. Densities are normalized to sum to one [S2].
**Formula:**
$$ L_{dens} = \sum_{(r, \sigma_f) \in y_f} (-\sigma_f^T \log \sigma_r) + \sum_{(r, \sigma_b) \in y_b} (-\sigma_b^T \log \sigma_r) $$
[S2]

## Background
Originating from the analysis of multiscale rendering and neural radiance field editing, these methods address specific failure modes of standard L2 losses. Area loss scaling was introduced in Mip-NeRF to handle aliasing and scale-space reasoning in multiscale datasets, ensuring that downscaled images do not disproportionately affect the gradient updates [S1]. Masked volume metrics and ray-validity masking were developed to handle the sparsity of volumetric data and the prevalence of background rays in training, preventing the model from optimizing for static background colors [S1, S1, S2, S2]. Density-based losses were proposed in Editing Conditional Radiance Fields to facilitate faster shape editing by directly optimizing the density field rather than composite image reconstruction [S2].
