# Bjøntegaard Delta-Rate (BD-Rate)

Also called: BD-R, BD-Rate, Piecewise cubic Bjøntegaard Delta-Rate (BD-Rate) metric

## Overview
BD-Rate is a metric for fair comparison of overall rate-distortion performance that calculates the average bit-rate saving at a given quality level between two methods [S1]. It sets itself apart from point-wise comparisons by integrating the area between two continuous RD curves, providing a single percentage value representing efficiency gains [S1]. It builds on the concept of BD-PSNR but inverts the perspective to measure bitrate reduction [7176-bj-ntegaard-delta-psnr-bd-psnr-and-bd-br.md][S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Two sets of rate-distortion points for each method, where rate is bitrate (e.g., bits per pixel) and distortion is quality (e.g., PSNR in dB) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A single percentage value representing the average bit-rate saving [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Interpolation:** Employ piecewise cubic interpolation to fit a continuous rate-distortion curve through the discrete points for each method [S1].
2.  **Integration:** Calculate the bit saving at the same quality level by integrating the area between the two interpolated curves over their overlapping distortion range [S1].

## Subtopics

### Rate-Distortion Performance Evaluation
This is the primary application context for BD-Rate. It is used to compare compression algorithms (e.g., SPC-NeRF vs VQRF) by evaluating their overall efficiency across multiple quality levels [S1]. It is chosen when a single scalar metric is needed to summarize the trade-off between model size/bitrate and rendering quality (PSNR) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Not given in the sources.

**Measured Performance:**
*   In comparisons of SPC-NeRF against VQRF on the Synthetic-NeRF dataset, BD-Rate showed a 28% bit saving for the base configuration and 32% for the HR configuration [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Provides a single, comparable metric for overall RD performance [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes a smooth, continuous RD curve estimated via piecewise cubic interpolation [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when comparing two compression methods across a wide range of bitrates and quality levels [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Do not use if the RD curves do not overlap significantly in distortion range [S1].

## Background
BD-Rate was introduced by Bjøntegaard in the context of video compression standards [S1]. It has been adapted for evaluating neural radiance field compression methods, such as SPC-NeRF, to provide a fair comparison against prior works like VQRF [S1]. The metric relies on piecewise cubic interpolation to estimate the continuous RD curve from discrete experimental points [S1].
