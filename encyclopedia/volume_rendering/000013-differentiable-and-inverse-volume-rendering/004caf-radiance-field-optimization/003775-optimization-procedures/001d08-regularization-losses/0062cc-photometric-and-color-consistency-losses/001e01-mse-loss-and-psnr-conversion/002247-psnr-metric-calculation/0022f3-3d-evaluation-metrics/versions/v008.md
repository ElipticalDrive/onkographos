# 3D Evaluation Metrics


## Overview
3D Evaluation Metrics quantify the fidelity of reconstructed volumetric or radiance-field representations against ground-truth references, addressing the problem of assessing both geometric accuracy and photometric consistency in 3D reconstruction [S1, unofficial][S3]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on specific aspects like mesh topology [Mesh Reconstruction Evaluation](2909-mesh-reconstruction-evaluation.md) or surface orientation [Normal Consistency](3050-normal-consistency.md), this category encompasses a suite of metrics including PSNR, SSIM, and mIoU to provide a comprehensive assessment of reconstruction quality [S1, unofficial][S3][S4, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from [PSNR Metric Calculation](2742-psnr-metric-calculation.md) by extending the evaluation to 3D volumes and multi-view consistency, and from [Benchmark Performance Numbers](3471-benchmark-performance-numbers.md) by providing the specific algorithmic definitions of the scores rather than just reporting aggregated results.

## How it works
The evaluation process varies depending on the specific metric (photometric vs. geometric) and the representation type (voxel volume vs. splat-based radiance field). The following steps cover the standard procedures for computing PSNR/SSIM on volumes and mIoU for occupancy grids.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   Reconstructed volume $V_{pred}$ (normalized to $[0, 1]$ or binarized occupancy).
    *   Ground-truth volume $V_{gt}$ (normalized to $[0, 1]$ or binary occupancy).
    *   Camera poses (for novel view synthesis evaluation).
    *   Rendering engine (differentiable renderer for splats or direct volume access for voxels).
*   **Outputs**:
    *   PSNR (dB), SSIM (0–1), and/or mIoU (0–100%).

### Data Structures
*   **Volume**: A 3D tensor of shape $(D, H, W)$ or $(N, D, H, W)$ for multiple views/channels.
*   **Occupancy Grid**: A binary 3D tensor where $1$ indicates occupied space and $0$ indicates empty space.
*   **Splats**: A list of Gaussian parameters $(\mu, \Sigma, c, \alpha)$ used for rendering.

### Numbered Steps

#### 1. Photometric Evaluation (PSNR and SSIM)
1.  **Normalization**: Ensure $V_{pred}$ and $V_{gt}$ are normalized to the range $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **View Selection**: Iterate over axial, coronal, and sagittal slices, or render novel views from held-out camera poses [S2, S4, unofficial].
3.  **MSE Calculation**: Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) averaged over all voxels, including background [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (V_{pred}[i] - V_{gt}[i])^2 $$
    where $N$ is the total number of voxels.
4.  **PSNR Calculation**: Compute PSNR using the normalized maximum value $MAX=1$:
    $$ PSNR = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{1}{MSE} \right) $$
    [S1, unofficial].
5.  **SSIM Calculation**: Compute Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) using a window size of 11 and Gaussian weighting with $\sigma = 1.5$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Aggregation**: Print or return the mean PSNR and SSIM across all views or slices [S2, unofficial].

#### 2. Geometric Evaluation (mIoU for Occupancy)
1.  **Alpha Extraction**: Extract the predicted $\alpha$ volume, which corresponds to the last channel of the predicted volume $V_s$ [S3].
2.  **Binarization**: Binarize the predicted $\alpha$ volume using a fixed threshold $\tau = 0.05$ [S3]:
    $$ V_{pred\_bin}[i] = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } V_{pred}[i] \ge \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
3.  **Ground Truth Preparation**: Ensure $V_{gt}$ is a binary occupancy volume (upsampled to the target resolution, e.g., $64^3$) [S3].
4.  **IoU Calculation**: For each semantic category $c$, compute the Intersection-over-Union:
    $$ IoU_c = \frac{|V_{pred\_bin}^c \cap V_{gt}^c|}{|V_{pred\_bin}^c \cup V_{gt}^c|} $$
5.  **mIoU Aggregation**: Average the per-category IoU scores to obtain the final mIoU metric [S3].

### Parameters
*   **SSIM Window Size**: 11 [S1, unofficial].
*   **SSIM Sigma ($\sigma$)**: 1.5 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Binarization Threshold ($\tau$)**: 0.05 [S3].
*   **MSE Scope**: Includes all voxels (background included) [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Overfitting**: Low error on training views but high error on held-out views indicates overfitting, usually caused by insufficient scene coverage [S4, unofficial].
*   **Background Inclusion**: MSE must be averaged over all voxels including background to be comparable with standard definitions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Resolution Mismatch**: Ground truth volumes may need upsampling (e.g., nearest-neighbor interpolation from $32^3$ to $64^3$) to match the prediction resolution [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Mesh Reconstruction Evaluation](2909-mesh-reconstruction-evaluation.md)
Mesh Reconstruction Evaluation quantifies the geometric fidelity of a reconstructed triangular mesh against a ground-truth reference mesh. It solves the problem of assessing how well a surface reconstruction method captures the true shape, topology, and detail of the underlying scene. Unlike radiance-field metrics that evaluate visual appearance, this method focuses strictly on spatial accuracy, distinguishing it from [PSNR Metric Calculation](2903-psnr-metric-calculation.md) which measures pixel-level photometric error.

### [Normal Consistency](3050-normal-consistency.md)
Normal Consistency (NC) is a metric for 3D surface reconstruction that measures the angular deviation between estimated surface normals and ground truth normals. It quantifies how well the orientation of the reconstructed surface aligns with the underlying geometry, serving as a complementary measure to distance-based metrics like Chamfer Distance. Unlike distance metrics that assess positional accuracy, NC specifically evaluates surface smoothness and orientation fidelity.

### [Standard Rendering Metrics](3473-standard-rendering-metrics.md)
Standard Rendering Metrics provide an objective, numerical assessment of image fidelity for 3D reconstructions and novel view synthesis. The primary metric is the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), which quantifies pixel-wise reconstruction error in decibels by comparing rendered images against ground-truth references. While PSNR is the standard baseline due to its computational cheapness, it is widely recognized as insensitive to perceptual quality, often necessitating companion metrics like SSIM or LPIPS for a complete evaluation.

### 3D Reconstruction Evaluation (mIoU)
Evaluates 3D geometry learned in a self-supervised manner by minimizing a novel view synthesis objective, using mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) between predicted and ground truth volumes [S3]. The predicted $\alpha$ volume (last channel of $V_s$) is binarized using a threshold $\tau = 0.05$ and compared against the ground truth occupancy volume [S3]. The mIoU is averaged across categories [S3]. In benchmarks, this method achieved 63.25% mIoU, outperforming supervised baselines like 3D-R2N2 (55.1%) [S3].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting Evaluation Metrics
Evaluation requires holding out photos never trained on, rendering the trained splat from those poses, and comparing against real photos [S4, unofficial]. Standard scores are PSNR (higher is better), SSIM (closer to 1 is better), and LPIPS (lower is better, correlates with human perception) [S4, unofficial]. A key pitfall is that low training-view error but high held-out error indicates overfitting, usually caused by insufficient scene coverage [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: PSNR/SSIM computation is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels or pixels. SSIM requires convolution, adding a factor related to window size. mIoU is $O(N)$ for voxel-wise comparison.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(N)$ to store the volumes and intermediate buffers for SSIM.
*   **Strengths**: PSNR and SSIM are computationally cheap and provide a standard baseline . mIoU provides a direct geometric measure for occupancy grids [S3].
*   **Limitations**: PSNR is insensitive to perceptual quality . SSIM parameters (window size, sigma) must be chosen carefully [S1, unofficial]. mIoU requires a threshold, which can be sensitive to the choice of $\tau$ [S3].
*   **When to Use**: Use PSNR/SSIM for photometric evaluation of novel views or volume slices [S2, S4, unofficial]. Use mIoU for evaluating self-supervised 3D geometry reconstruction [S3]. Use LPIPS for perceptual quality assessment [S4, unofficial].

## Background
3D evaluation metrics have evolved alongside 3D reconstruction techniques. PSNR and SSIM were originally developed for 2D image compression and quality assessment but have been adapted for 3D volumes and radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. The use of mIoU for 3D reconstruction evaluation in self-supervised settings was introduced in works like Fast and Explicit Neural View Synthesis [S3]. The evaluation of 3D Gaussian Splatting has popularized the use of held-out view metrics including LPIPS to assess generalization [S4, unofficial].
