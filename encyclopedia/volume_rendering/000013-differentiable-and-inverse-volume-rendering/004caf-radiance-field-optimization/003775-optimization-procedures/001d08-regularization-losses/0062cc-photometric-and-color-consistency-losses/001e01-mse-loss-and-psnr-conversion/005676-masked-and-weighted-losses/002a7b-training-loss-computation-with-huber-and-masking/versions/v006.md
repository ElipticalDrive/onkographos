# Training Loss Computation with Huber and Masking

Also called: NeRF training loss, RGB and TV loss calculation

## Overview
This method defines the standard photometric loss computation pipeline for differentiable volume rendering, specifically combining Huber loss for robustness against outliers with binary masking to exclude invalid background rays. It solves the problem of biased gradient updates caused by saturated pixels or empty background regions during radiance-field optimization. It differs from its sibling [Masked Volume Metrics](1927-masked-volume-metrics.md) by focusing on the training-time loss aggregation rather than evaluation-time metric calculation, and from [Masked and Weighted Losses](15713-masked-and-weighted-losses.md) by specifying the exact Huber loss function and the `ray_is_valid` masking logic used in modern JAX-based implementations [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes the total training loss by rendering predictions, blending ground truth to match rendering conditions, and aggregating masked Huber losses.

**Inputs:**
- `pred_rgbds`: Tensor of shape $(N_{rays}, 4)$ containing predicted RGB and depth values [S1, unofficial].
- `gt_rgba_f32`: Ground truth RGBA image array [S1, unofficial].
- `bg`: Background color tensor [S1, unofficial].
- `batch_metrics`: Dictionary containing `ray_is_valid` (boolean mask of shape $(N_{rays})$) and `n_valid_rays` (scalar count) [S1, unofficial].
- `loss`: Accumulator for other loss components (e.g., TV, regularization) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `loss`: Scalar total loss value [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `ray_is_valid`: Binary mask where $1$ indicates the ray intersects valid scene geometry or foreground, and $0$ indicates background-only rays [S1, unofficial].
- `pred_rgbs`: Tensor of shape $(N_{rays}, 3)$ extracted from `pred_rgbds` [S1, unofficial].
- `pred_depths`: Tensor of shape $(N_{rays}, 1)$ extracted from `pred_rgbds` [S1, unofficial].
- `gt_rgbs`: Tensor of shape $(N_{rays}, 3)$ representing ground truth RGB blended with the background [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Render Predictions:** Call the training renderer with worldspace rays, appearance embeddings, background color, and total sample count to obtain `pred_rgbds` [S1, unofficial].
2. **Split Predictions:** Separate `pred_rgbds` into `pred_rgbs` (first 3 channels) and `pred_depths` (last channel) [S1, unofficial].
3. **Blend Ground Truth:** Compute `gt_rgbs` by blending the ground truth RGBA image with the background color to ensure consistency with the renderer's background handling [S1, unofficial].
4. **Compute RGB Huber Loss:** Calculate the per-ray Huber loss between `pred_rgbs` and `gt_rgbs` using a delta parameter $\delta = 0.1$. Apply the `ray_is_valid` mask to zero out losses for invalid rays. Average the loss over the valid rays [S1, unofficial].
   $$ L_{rgb} = \frac{1}{N_{valid}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{rays}} \mathbb{I}(valid_i) \cdot \text{Huber}(pred\_rgbs_i, gt\_rgbs_i, \delta=0.1) $$
   where $\mathbb{I}(valid_i)$ is $1$ if `ray_is_valid[i]` is true, else $0$, and $N_{valid}$ is the count of valid rays [S1, unofficial].
5. **Aggregate Loss:** Sum the computed RGB loss with other components in the `loss` accumulator (e.g., total variation, other regularization terms) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Huber Delta ($\delta$): $0.1$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If `n_valid_rays` is zero, the division in step 4 must be handled to prevent NaN gradients. The source implementation divides by `n_valid_rays` [S1, unofficial].
- **Background Consistency:** Ground truth must be blended with the same background color used during rendering; otherwise, background pixels will contribute large errors [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_training_loss(pred_rgbds, gt_rgba_f32, bg, batch_metrics):
    # Step 1 & 2: Split predictions
    pred_rgbs, pred_depths = jnp.split(pred_rgbds, [3], axis=-1)
    
    # Step 3: Blend ground truth to match rendering conditions
    gt_rgbs = blend_rgba_image_array(gt_rgba_f32, bg)
    
    # Step 4: Compute masked Huber loss for RGB
    # Huber loss with delta=0.1
    huber_rgb = optax.huber_loss(pred_rgbs, gt_rgbs, delta=0.1)
    
    # Mask by ray validity
    ray_is_valid = batch_metrics["ray_is_valid"]
    masked_loss = jnp.where(ray_is_valid, huber_rgb.mean(axis=-1), 0.0)
    
    # Average over valid rays
    n_valid_rays = batch_metrics["n_valid_rays"]
    loss_rgb = masked_loss.sum() / n_valid_rays
    
    # Step 5: Aggregate with other losses
    # Assuming batch_metrics["loss"] contains other components like TV or regularization
    total_loss = jax.tree_util.tree_reduce(lambda x, y: x + y, batch_metrics["loss"]) + loss_rgb
    
    return total_loss
```

## Subtopics

### Convergence Hinge Loss
A regularization term used in sparse voxel grid generators (e.g., VoxGRAF) to ensure both foreground and background generators cover a minimum fraction of the image, preventing one generator from dominating [S2]. It uses a hinge loss on the mean mask value [S2].

**Formula:**
Foreground coverage loss:
$$ L_{cvg}^{fg} = \lambda_{cvg}^{fg} \max\left(0, \eta_{fg} - \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \hat{m}_{mask}^{fg}[i]\right) $$
Background coverage loss:
$$ L_{cvg}^{bg} = \lambda_{cvg}^{bg} \max\left(0, \eta_{bg} - \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} (1 - \hat{m}_{mask}^{bg}[i])\right) $$
where $\hat{m}_{mask}$ is the predicted mask, $S$ is the set of samples, and $\eta$ is the minimum coverage fraction [S2].

**Parameters:**
- Foreground minimum coverage ($\eta_{fg}$): $0.4$ for AFHQ and FFHQ; $0.1$ for Carla [S2].
- Background minimum coverage ($\eta_{bg}$): $0.1$ [S2].
- Convergence loss weight ($\lambda_{cvg}^{fg}$): $0.1$ [S2].
- Convergence loss weight ($\lambda_{cvg}^{bg}$): $0.1$ [S2] [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of rays $N_{rays}$ for the loss computation [S1, unofficial]. The rendering step dominates the cost.
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in $N_{rays}$ to store intermediate loss tensors [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Huber loss is robust to outliers (e.g., saturated pixels) compared to MSE [S1, unofficial]. Masking prevents background rays from biasing the optimization [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires accurate `ray_is_valid` masks. The choice of $\delta=0.1$ is specific to the implementation in [S1, unofficial] and may need tuning for other datasets.
- **When to use:** Standard training for differentiable volume rendering where background exclusion and outlier robustness are needed [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method is derived from the training pipeline of JAX-based neural rendering frameworks, specifically `jaxngp` [S1, unofficial], which adapts techniques from Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (NGP) and other differentiable rendering works. The use of Huber loss and ray masking is standard in modern neural rendering optimization to stabilize training [S1, unofficial]. The Convergence Hinge Loss variant originates from VoxGRAF, which uses sparse voxel grids and mask-based generators [S2].
