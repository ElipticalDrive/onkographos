# BARF (Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields)
Also called: Bundle-Adjusting NeRF
## Overview
BARF reconstructs a Neural Radiance Field from multi-view images when the input camera poses contain errors [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the sensitivity of standard NeRF to inaccurate camera poses, which typically leads to poor reconstruction quality [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which assumes known poses, BARF jointly optimizes the scene representation and camera parameters using a coarse-to-fine strategy that gradually introduces high-frequency positional encoding details [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and outputs:**
- Inputs: A set of multi-view images and their corresponding initial camera poses, where the poses may contain errors [S1, unofficial].
- Outputs: A reconstructed NeRF model and refined camera poses [S1, unofficial].

**Data structures with exact layouts:**
Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Initialization:** Standard NeRF weight initialization is used, with network biases initialized to negative values to enforce low initial opacity [G].
2. **Coarse-to-Fine Refinement:** Gradually increase the high-frequency part of the positional encoding over the course of training [S1, unofficial].
3. **Joint Optimization:** Optimize the NeRF parameters and camera poses jointly by minimizing the photometric loss between rendered and ground truth images [S1, unofficial].
4. **Final Output:** Return the optimized NeRF and refined camera poses [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Iterative Alignment Strategy](6957-iterative-alignment-strategy.md)
A post-processing and data-generation technique that mitigates reconstruction artifacts from inaccurate poses and non-rigid motion by iteratively warping ground truth images with optical flow to refine alignment, rather than optimizing a loss during training.

## Background
BARF was introduced in "Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields" (ICCV 2021) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation that standard NeRF is not robust to inaccurate camera poses, a common issue in real-world scenarios where poses are estimated from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines [S1, unofficial]. The method leverages the observation that high-frequency positional encoding can hinder optimization when poses are inaccurate, leading to a coarse-to-fine refinement strategy [S1, unofficial].
