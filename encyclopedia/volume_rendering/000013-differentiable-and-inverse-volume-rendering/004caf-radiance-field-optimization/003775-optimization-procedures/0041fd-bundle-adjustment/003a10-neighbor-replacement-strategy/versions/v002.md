# Neighbor-Replacement Strategy

Also called: Neighbor Replacement

## Overview
The Neighbor-Replacement Strategy is a post-processing step applied within cascaded Bundle Adjustment (BA) modules to correct poorly estimated camera poses [S1]. It identifies inferior views based on rendered image quality and replaces their poses with those of the most similar neighboring cameras [S1]. Unlike standard BA which refines poses iteratively, this strategy explicitly swaps poses to eliminate the detrimental impact of inaccurate initial estimates on scene representation [S1]. It is distinct from sibling methods like [Best Viewpoint Selection via Opacity Entropy](1121-best-viewpoint-selection-via-opacity-entropy.md) by focusing on pose correction via replacement rather than viewpoint selection, and differs from [BARF (Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields)](6872-barf-bundle-adjusting-neural-radiance-fields.md) by using a discrete replacement mechanism rather than continuous joint optimization of poses and fields [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of all camera views $Q = \{q_k | k = 1, 2, ..., N\}$, where each $q_k$ includes a camera pose estimate and an associated image [S1]. A classification of each view as either "superior" ($Q_s$) or "inferior" ($Q_i$) based on a prior quality criterion [S1].
*   **Output:** An updated set of camera pose estimates $\tilde{Q} = \{\tilde{q}_k | k = 1, 2, ..., N\}$ where inferior poses have been replaced [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $Q$: List of $N$ views.
*   $Q_s, Q_i$: Subsets of superior and inferior views.
*   $\tilde{Q}_s, \tilde{Q}_i$: Updated subsets after non-maxima suppression.
*   `used_poses`: A set tracking IDs of poses already utilized for replacement to prevent redundancy [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Identify Inferior Views:**
    Apply the quality criterion from the preceding "Erroneous Pose Detection" step to label views in $Q$ as superior ($Q_s$) or inferior ($Q_i$) [S1].

2.  **Non-Maxima Suppression:**
    Inaccurate poses affect neighboring views, causing them to also appear low-quality [S1]. To prevent mislabeling accurate neighbors as inferior, when multiple neighboring camera poses are assigned low scores, assume only the one with the lowest score is inaccurate [S1]. Discard misidentified low-scoring poses to update classifications to $\tilde{Q}_s$ and $\tilde{Q}_i$ [S1].

3.  **Neighbor Search:**
    For each inferior view $q^e_i \in \tilde{Q}_i$, search for the nearest neighbor view $q^e_{sim}$ in the full set $Q$ [S1]. Measure similarity using the combined criterion from Erroneous Pose Detection [S1]. Perform matching on rotated images with equivalently rotating camera poses to expand the search space and improve matching accuracy [S1].

4.  **Pose Replacement:**
    Replace camera poses in $\tilde{Q}_i$ with their nearest neighbors in $Q$ [S1]. Obtain the updated set $\tilde{Q} = \{\tilde{q}_k | k = 1, 2, ..., N\}$ [S1].

5.  **Replacement Memory:**
    Keep track of the pose used for replacement for each erroneous viewpoint and skip poses that have already been utilized to prevent redundant erroneous replacements and optimization stagnation [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def neighbor_replacement(views, scores, angles, threshold, radius):
    """
    views: list of dicts {'id': int, 'pose': list, 'image': array}
    scores: dict mapping view id to float (lower is worse)
    angles: list of rotation angles in radians
    threshold: quality threshold from prior step
    radius: spatial radius for neighbor grouping in NMS
    Returns: updated_views: list of view dicts with replaced poses
    """
    # Step 1: Initial Classification
    superior = [v for v in views if scores[v['id']] > threshold]
    inferior = [v for v in views if scores[v['id']] <= threshold]

    # Step 2: Non-Maxima Suppression
    final_inferior = []
    final_superior = list(superior)
    remaining = list(inferior)

    while remaining:
        # Find view with lowest score
        min_view = min(remaining, key=lambda v: scores[v['id']])
        final_inferior.append(min_view)

        # Identify neighbors in remaining set within spatial radius
        neighbors = [v for v in remaining if v is not min_view and pose_distance(min_view['pose'], v['pose']) < radius]
        for n in neighbors:
            remaining.remove(n)
            final_superior.append(n)

    # Step 3 & 4: Replacement with Memory
    used_poses = set()
    updated_views = []

    for inf_view in final_inferior:
        best_sim_view = None
        best_score = -float('inf')

        for cand in views:
            if cand['id'] in used_poses:
                continue

            current_score = -float('inf')
            for angle in angles:
                rot_img = rotate_image(cand['image'], angle)
                rot_pose = rotate_pose(cand['pose'], angle)
                sim = compute_similarity(inf_view['image'], rot_img, inf_view['pose'], rot_pose)
                if sim > current_score:
                    current_score = sim

            if current_score > best_score:
                best_score = current_score
                best_sim_view = cand

        if best_sim_view is not None:
            new_view = dict(inf_view)
            new_view['pose'] = best_sim_view['pose']
            updated_views.append(new_view)
            used_poses.add(best_sim_view['id'])
        else:
            updated_views.append(inf_view)

    for v in final_superior:
        updated_views.append(v)

    return updated_views
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Threshold:** Quality score threshold for initial classification. Defined in the Erroneous Pose Detection step [S1].
*   **Rotation Angles:** Discrete angles tested for rotational matching. Not given in the sources.
*   **Radius:** Spatial radius for neighbor grouping in non-maxima suppression. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Misidentification:** Inaccurate poses can cause neighboring accurate poses to be labeled as inferior. Non-maxima suppression mitigates this [S1].
*   **Redundant Replacement:** Incorrect matching can lead to cycles or redundant swaps. The replacement-memory technique prevents this [S1].
*   **No Ground Truth:** Since ground-truth poses are unknown, identification relies entirely on rendered image quality [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** Significantly improves pose optimization in the presence of large camera pose noise [S1]. Enhances the initialization for subsequent BA stages in a cascade [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the initial quality criterion [S1]. May fail if the nearest neighbor is also inaccurate and not caught by memory/guards [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When initial camera poses are noisy or insufficient, and ground-truth poses are unavailable [S1].

## Background
The Neighbor-Replacement Strategy was introduced in the CBARF (Cascaded Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields) model [S1]. It addresses the problem that poor initialization of camera poses significantly impacts BA performance [S1]. The method is part of a cascade of BA modules, where replacement is applied between each pair of modules [S1]. It was developed to handle large camera pose noise in 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis [S1].
