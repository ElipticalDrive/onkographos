# Token-level Bundle Adjustment

Also called: TokenBA

## Overview
Token-level Bundle Adjustment (TokenBA) is a per-scene refinement procedure that fits a frozen ZipSplat decoder to input views by jointly optimizing camera poses and scene tokens, recovering fine detail and correcting initial pose errors in a few seconds [S1, unofficial]. It exploits the persistent 2D–3D correspondences established by the cone loss to refine cameras via robust Perspective-n-Point (PnP) and update scene tokens through the frozen decoder using photometric error [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that optimize raw Gaussians or require pose supervision, TokenBA achieves faster convergence and higher quality by leveraging the built-in correspondence of scene tokens [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ unposed, uncalibrated input images $I = \{I_n\}_{n=1}^N$, a pre-trained ZipSplat encoder producing scene tokens $Z$, and an initial estimate of camera poses. The decoder MLP is frozen.
*   **Outputs:** Refined camera poses and refined scene tokens $Z$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Scene Tokens ($Z$):** Each token represents a surface region and contains a set of 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian is parameterized by center $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scales $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$, rotation quaternion $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$, opacity $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, and color coefficients $c \in \mathbb{R}^C$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Correspondences:** Derived from cluster assignments. Every Gaussian of a scene token is paired with the patch center of each member patch [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Learning Rates:** Tokens use Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$ during alternation and $2 \times 10^{-3}$ in the joint tail [S1, unofficial].
*   **Joint Tail Increment:** The joint tail refines tokens and poses using an $se(3)$ increment with learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Compression Ratio ($r$):** Controls Gaussian budget. $r=1.0$ keeps full capacity; $r=0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05$ progressively reduce the budget [S1, unofficial].
*   **Resolution:** Feed-forward encoder runs at $252 \times 252$. TokenBA rendering loss is resolution-agnostic; higher resolutions (e.g., $448 \times 448$ or $540 \times 540$) can be used to recover fine features [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Decoder MLP body runs in half precision (bfloat16); readout stays in full precision [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
TokenBA executes alternating rounds of optimization, each comprising a PnP snap, photometric polish, and token update, followed by a joint tail refinement [S1, unofficial].

1.  **PnP Snap (Camera Update):**
    *   Construct 2D–3D correspondences: Pair every Gaussian of a scene token with the patch center of each member patch based on cluster assignments [S1, unofficial].
    *   Solve for each pose using robust PnP with graduated non-convexity.
    *   Sample 2048 correspondences [S1, unofficial].
    *   Anneal robust scale from 64 to 12 to 4 pixels over 4 iterations per level [S1, unofficial].
    *   Generate seven rotation hypotheses per view: the current rotation and $\pm 40^\circ$ about each camera axis [S1, unofficial].
    *   Solve hypotheses in one batched call; select the hypothesis with the most inliers within 2 patches [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Photometric Polish:**
    *   Remove patch-center quantization error using a direct Levenberg–Marquardt solver on the photometric residual [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Full Configuration (Pre-Refinement):** Scan nine log-spaced global scale factors from 0.5 to 2.0. Run a four-level pyramid (1/8, 1/4, 1/2, full resolution) with 12, 10, 8, and 6 steps per level. Apply Gaussian blur at the two coarse levels. Use accept-reject steps with a trust-region clamp on the pose twist. Keep the best full-resolution pose across levels [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Inside TokenBA:** Only the two fine levels (1/2, full) run, without verification or scale scan [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Aligner Step:** Each step renders at the current pose and takes a Gauss–Newton update on the brightness-constancy linearization with the analytic image-warp Jacobian. Uses one render per step and no autograd [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Execution:** Run two steps at half resolution and one at full resolution [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Token Update:**
    *   Refine scene tokens by minimizing the $\ell_1$ rendering loss on the input views [S1, unofficial].
    *   Gradients propagate through the renderer and frozen decoder to the tokens [S1, unofficial].
    *   Optimizing tokens instead of raw Gaussians retains the decoder as a learned scene prior [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Joint Tail Refinement:**
    *   Jointly refine tokens and poses for at most 96 steps, reusing the same Adam state [S1, unofficial].
    *   Exits early once a block of 8 steps improves the loss by less than 2% [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Termination:**
    *   Alternating rounds stop once the PnP snap moves every pose by less than $0.3^\circ$, or after four rounds [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def token_level_bundle_adjustment(input_images, initial_poses, scene_tokens, decoder, renderer):
    """
    Performs TokenBA refinement.
    input_images: List of N images.
    initial_poses: Initial camera poses.
    scene_tokens: Initial scene tokens Z.
    decoder: Frozen ZipSplat decoder.
    renderer: Differentiable rasterizer.
    """
    poses = initial_poses
    tokens = scene_tokens
    
    # Alternating Rounds
    for round_idx in range(4):
        # 1. PnP Snap
        poses = pnp_snap(poses, tokens, input_images)
        
        # Check termination condition
        max_pose_move = compute_max_pose_movement(poses, initial_poses) # Implementation detail [G]
        if max_pose_move < 0.3: # degrees
            break
            
        # 2. Photometric Polish
        poses = photometric_polish(poses, tokens, input_images, decoder, renderer)
        
        # 3. Token Update
        tokens = token_refit(tokens, poses, input_images, decoder, renderer)
        
        # Reset initial poses for next round comparison if needed, 
        # or track cumulative movement [G]
        initial_poses = poses 

    # Joint Tail
    tokens, poses = joint_tail_refinement(tokens, poses, input_images, decoder, renderer)
    
    return tokens, poses

def pnp_snap(poses, tokens, input_images):
    """
    Refines camera poses using robust PnP on patch-Gaussian correspondences.
    """
    refined_poses = []
    for i, pose in enumerate(poses):
        # Get correspondences for view i
        # Pair every Gaussian of a scene token with the patch center of each member patch
        correspondences = get_correspondences(tokens, input_images[i]) 
        
        # Sample 2048 correspondences
        sampled_corr = sample(correspondences, 2048)
        
        # Generate 7 rotation hypotheses: current + +/- 40 deg about each axis
        hypotheses = generate_rotation_hypotheses(pose.rotation, 40.0)
        
        best_hypothesis = None
        max_inliers = -1
        
        for rot in hypotheses:
            # Solve PnP with graduated non-convexity
            # Anneal robust scale from 64 -> 12 -> 4 over 4 iterations per level
            est_pose = solve_pnp_graduated(sampled_corr, rot, pose.translation, 
                                           scale_schedule=[64, 12, 4], iters_per_level=4)
            # Count inliers within 2 patches
            inliers = count_inliers(est_pose, sampled_corr, threshold=2.0)
            if inliers > max_inliers:
                max_inliers = inliers
                best_hypothesis = est_pose
                
        refined_poses.append(best_hypothesis)
        
    return refined_poses

def photometric_polish(poses, tokens, input_images, decoder, renderer):
    """
    Removes patch-center quantization error using direct Levenberg-Marquardt.
    """
    refined_poses = []
    for i, pose in enumerate(poses):
        # Inside TokenBA: only two fine levels (1/2, full)
        levels = [0.5, 1.0]
        steps_per_level = [2, 1] # 2 steps at half, 1 at full
        
        current_pose = pose
        
        for level_idx, level in enumerate(levels):
            for step in range(steps_per_level[level_idx]):
                # Render at current pose
                rendered_img = renderer.render(tokens, current_pose, decoder, resolution_scale=level)
                
                # Compute photometric residual against input image
                # Note: Input images might need resizing to match resolution_scale [G]
                input_img_resized = resize(input_images[i], scale=level)
                residual = rendered_img - input_img_resized
                
                # Gauss-Newton update on brightness-constancy linearization
                # Analytic image-warp Jacobian
                # One render per step, no autograd
                jacobian = compute_analytic_jacobian(current_pose, rendered_img)
                
                # Levenberg-Marquardt update
                # (J^T J + lambda I) delta = -J^T residual
                delta = solve_lm_step(jacobian, residual)
                current_pose = apply_pose_twist(current_pose, delta)
                
        refined_poses.append(current_pose)
        
    return refined_poses

def token_refit(tokens, poses, input_images, decoder, renderer):
    """
    Refines scene tokens by minimizing rendering loss.
    """
    # Gradients propagate through renderer and frozen decoder to tokens
    # Optimizer: Adam, lr=1e-3
    tokens = optimize_tokens(tokens, poses, input_images, decoder, renderer, 
                             optimizer='Adam', lr=1e-3, loss_fn='L1')
    return tokens

def joint_tail_refinement(tokens, poses, input_images, decoder, renderer):
    """
    Jointly refines tokens and poses for up to 96 steps.
    """
    # Optimizer: Adam, lr=3e-4 for se(3) increment
    # Reuse Adam state from alternation [G]
    
    for step in range(96):
        # Render and compute loss
        rendered_imgs = [renderer.render(tokens, p, decoder) for p in poses]
        loss = compute_l1_loss(rendered_imgs, input_images)
        
        # Backward pass
        loss.backward()
        
        # Update tokens and poses
        tokens, poses = optimizer_step(tokens, poses)
        
        # Early exit check: block of 8 steps improves loss by < 2%
        if step > 0 and step % 8 == 0:
            if check_loss_improvement(loss, previous_loss_block, threshold=0.02):
                break
                
    return tokens, poses
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Early Stopping Variability:** Early stopping makes TokenBA's per-scene cost highly variable; reported averages mask scene-dependent convergence behavior [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pose Accuracy Trade-off:** Freezing poses during the joint refinement costs only 0.1 dB in-domain and 0.3 dB out-of-domain, while keeping pose accuracy fixed at the level reached by the alternating rounds [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### TokenBA: Scene Token Update
Refines scene tokens by minimizing the rendering loss on the input views, with gradients propagating through the renderer and frozen decoder to the tokens [S1, unofficial]. Optimizing tokens instead of raw Gaussians retains the decoder as a learned scene prior [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   TokenBA cost scales nearly linearly with both the number of input views and the Gaussian budget [S1, unofficial].
*   At 128 views, doubling the budget from 65K multiplies cost by 1.6–2.0 [S1, unofficial].
*   At 262K Gaussians, doubling views multiplies cost by 1.7 [S1, unofficial].
*   Without TokenBA, the three largest budgets at 128 views are up to 1.7 s slower than full capacity [S1, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
*   Hardware: RTX 4090 [S1, unofficial].
*   Resolution: $252 \times 252$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Batch size: 1 [S1, unofficial].
*   One alternation round takes 35.5 ms [S1, unofficial].
*   One joint tail step takes 18.1 ms [S1, unofficial].
*   Breakdown of one alternation round (16 views, full capacity): PnP snap (2.1 ms), photometric polish (15.0 ms), token update (18.3 ms), rasterizer backward (10.8 ms), rasterizer forward (4.3 ms), decoder forward/backward (2.4 ms), Adam update (0.4 ms) [S1, unofficial].
*   Breakdown of one joint tail step: Rasterizer forward/backward passes consume 83% of the time [S1, unofficial].
*   At 128 views, reducing the Gaussian budget from 1M to 262K cuts TokenBA time from 31 s to 9.0 s, with a quality drop of 0.8 dB [S1, unofficial].
*   The joint tail exits after 94 of its 96 steps on average at 16 views [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Recovers fine detail (text, textures, foliage) and corrects initial pose errors without pose supervision [S1, unofficial]. Faster convergence than optimizing raw Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Cost is scene-dependent due to early stopping [S1, unofficial]. Freezing poses in the joint tail incurs a small quality penalty (0.1–0.3 dB) [S1, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
*   When high-quality reconstruction is needed from unposed, uncalibrated images and the feed-forward prediction has residual camera and geometry error [S1, unofficial].
*   When fine detail recovery is critical, potentially at higher resolutions than the encoder's training resolution ($252 \times 252$) [S1, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
*   When computational resources are extremely constrained and the feed-forward prediction is already sufficient.
*   When ground-truth camera poses are available and highly accurate, as the refinement may offer diminishing returns (though it still adds detail) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Token-level Bundle Adjustment was introduced in the ZipSplat paper (An et al., 2026) as a refinement step for the feed-forward ZipSplat model [S1, unofficial]. ZipSplat predicts a Gaussian scene from a collection of unposed, uncalibrated images without requiring camera poses or intrinsics [S1, unofficial]. TokenBA exploits the persistent 2D–3D correspondences established by the cone loss and GeoCE to refine the scene representation [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the observation that scene tokens, grounded in specific surface regions, provide a compact and stable basis for bundle adjustment [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated on DL3DV, RealEstate10K, Mip-NeRF360, and ScanNet++ [S1, unofficial].
