# BARF (Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields)
Also called: Bundle-Adjusting NeRF
## Overview
BARF reconstructs a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) and refines inaccurate camera poses given multi-view images with pose errors [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard NeRF optimization by decoupling the optimization of high-frequency scene details from camera pose updates, preventing the optimizer from getting trapped in local minima caused by the high-frequency positional encoding [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and outputs:**
- Inputs: Multi-view images $\{I_i\}$, initial camera poses $\{P_i\}$ (with errors), and a NeRF model $\mathcal{F}_\theta$.
- Outputs: Refined camera poses $\{\hat{P}_i\}$ and optimized NeRF parameters $\hat{\theta}$.

**Data structures:**
- **NeRF Model $\mathcal{F}_\theta$**: A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.
- **Positional Encoding $\Gamma$**: Standard Fourier feature mapping used to embed $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$. The encoding uses $L$ frequency bands.
- **Optimization State**: Parameters $\theta$ for the MLP and pose parameters (rotation $\mathbf{R}$, translation $\mathbf{t}$) for each camera.

**Method:**
The core innovation is a coarse-to-fine refinement strategy that gradually introduces high-frequency information into the positional encoding. This allows the optimizer to first converge on low-frequency structures (which are robust to pose errors) before refining high-frequency details (which require accurate poses).

1.  **Initialization**: Initialize the NeRF parameters $\theta$ and camera poses $P_i$ using the provided (potentially inaccurate) initial poses.
2.  **Coarse-to-Fine Schedule**: Define a schedule for the number of frequency bands $L(t)$ used in the positional encoding $\Gamma$ at training step $t$. The schedule starts with a small $L$ (e.g., $L=0$ or $1$) and increases to the target $L_{max}$ (e.g., $6$ or $10$) over the course of training [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Optimization Loop**: For each training step $t$:
    a. Set the positional encoding frequency limit $L(t)$ based on the schedule.
    b. Sample rays through the scene using the current camera poses $P_i$.
    c. Render colors $\hat{C}$ using the NeRF $\mathcal{F}_\theta$ with positional encoding $\Gamma_{L(t)}$.
    d. Compute the loss $\mathcal{L}$ between rendered colors $\hat{C}$ and ground truth images $I_i$ (typically Mean Squared Error).
    e. Perform gradient descent to update both NeRF parameters $\theta$ and camera pose parameters. The gradients flow through the differentiable rendering process and the positional encoding [S1, unofficial].
    f. Increment $t$ and update $L(t)$ if the schedule dictates.

**Formulas:**
- Positional Encoding with frequency limit $L$:
  $$ \Gamma_L(\mathbf{x}) = [\sin(2^0 \pi \mathbf{x}), \cos(2^0 \pi \mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{x})] $$
- Rendering equation (standard volume rendering):
  $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \left( 1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i} \right) \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j} $$
- Loss function:
  $$ \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \| \hat{C}_i - I_i \|^2 $$

**Parameters:**
- Initial frequency bands: Not given in the sources.
- Final frequency bands: Not given in the sources.
- Learning rate: Not given in the sources.
- Optimization algorithm: Not given in the sources (typically Adam).

**Edge cases and pitfalls:**
- **Precision**: The gradual introduction of frequencies prevents the optimizer from being misled by high-frequency noise in the presence of pose errors [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundaries**: The schedule must be smooth enough to allow convergence at each frequency level.
- **Degenerate input**: If initial poses are extremely poor, even the coarse stages may fail to converge.

## Subtopics

### [Iterative Alignment Strategy](6957-iterative-alignment-strategy.md)
The Iterative Alignment Strategy is a post-processing and data-generation technique used to mitigate reconstruction artifacts in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) caused by inaccurate camera poses and non-rigid scene motion. It addresses the "domain gap" where NeRF-rendered views are blurry due to misalignment, violating the sharp-pair assumption of standard optical flow estimators. Unlike direct alignment methods that optimize a loss function during training, this strategy iteratively warps ground truth images using optical flow to create a better-aligned training set, leveraging the sharpening effect of improved alignment to refine flow estimates in subsequent rounds.

### Global Pose and Scene Refinement
Final refinement step using BARF to achieve accurate poses and scene representation for novel view synthesis, leveraging the good initial poses from synchronization [S2]. Apply BARF optimization using the global poses estimated from the synchronization stage as initialization [S2].

## Background
BARF (Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced in ICCV 2021 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the issue of NeRF's sensitivity to camera pose accuracy, which is a common problem in real-world scenarios where poses are estimated from SfM pipelines and may contain errors [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to improve the robustness of NeRF reconstruction under pose uncertainty [S1, unofficial]. It is also used as a refinement step in more complex pipelines like LU-NeRF+Sync, which synchronizes local unposed NeRFs [S2].
