# Photometric Reconstruction Loss

Also called: Geometric Photometric Loss, Lrec, Multi-view Photometric Loss, Reprojection Loss

## Overview
Photometric Reconstruction Loss minimizes the error between rendered pixel colors and input training images during joint optimization of scene representation and camera parameters [S2, S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses by explicitly mapping 3D sample coordinates from a canonical ray to camera-specific rays via rigid transformations, ensuring geometric consistency across views [S2, S2, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely solely on pixel-wise intensity matching without explicit 3D warping, this method enforces that the radiance field evaluated at the transformed 3D position matches the observed image color [S2, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   $L$: Number of input 2D images.
    *   $I = \{I_1, \dots, I_L\}$: Set of input images, where $I_{iu} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the RGB color of pixel $u$ in image $i$.
    *   $P = \{P_1, \dots, P_L\}$: Camera poses, parameterized as elements of the $se(3)$ Lie algebra, representing rigid 3D transformations [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $F_\sigma, F_c$: Radiance field functions representing density and color, respectively [S2, S2, unofficial].
    *   $U$: Set of all possible 2D coordinates in the input images.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Scalar loss value $L_{rec}$ representing the photometric error.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Pose ($P_i$):** Represented as a vector in $se(3)$ Lie algebra. This parameterization is standard in robotics for rigid transformations [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Sample Coordinate ($s(\vec{0}, \vec{d_u})$):** A 3D point on the canonical ray (origin $\vec{0}$, direction $\vec{d_u}$) corresponding to pixel $u$.
*   **Warping Function ($W_{3d}$):** A function $W_{3d}: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ that performs rigid 3D transformation parameterized by $P \in se(3)$ [S2, S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  For each image $i$ from $1$ to $L$:
2.  For each pixel coordinate $u$ in the set $U$:
3.  Determine the 3D sample coordinate in the canonical ray space: $s_{canon} = s(\vec{0}, \vec{d_u})$, where $\vec{d_u}$ is the ray direction for pixel $u$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
4.  Warp the canonical sample coordinate to the camera ray space using the camera pose $P_i$:
    $$ s_{cam} = W_{3d}(P_i, s_{canon}) $$
    This maps the sample 3D coordinate from the canonical ray into the 3D sample coordinate of the camera ray with pose $P_i$ [S2, S2, unofficial].
5.  Evaluate the radiance field at the warped coordinate to get the rendered color:
    $$ \hat{C}_{iu} = V(F_\sigma, F_c, s_{cam}) $$
    where $V$ is the volume rendering operator [S2, S2, unofficial].
6.  Compute the difference between the rendered color and the ground truth image color $I_{iu}$:
    $$ \text{error}_{iu} = \| \hat{C}_{iu} - I_{iu} \| $$
7.  Sum the errors over all pixels and images to get the total loss:
    $$ L_{rec}(F_\sigma, F_c) = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \sum_{u \in U} \| V(F_\sigma, F_c, W_{3d}(P_i, s(\vec{0}, \vec{d_u}))) - I_{iu} \| $$
    [S2, S2, unofficial]

**Formulas:**
The core photometric reconstruction loss is defined as [S2, S2, unofficial]:
$$ L_{rec}(F_\sigma, F_c) = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \sum_{u \in U} \| V(F_\sigma, F_c, W_{3d}(P_i, s(\vec{0}, \vec{d_u}))) - I_{iu} \| $$

In joint optimization scenarios where camera poses $P$ are also unknown and optimized, the objective extends to [S2, S2, unofficial]:
$$ L_{joint}(F_\sigma, F_c, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \sum_{u \in U} \| V(F_\sigma, F_c, W_{3d}(P_i, s(\vec{0}, \vec{d_u}))) - I_{iu} \| $$

**Parameters:**
*   $P_i$: Camera pose parameterized in $se(3)$ Lie algebra [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   Typical values: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Ill-posedness:** The reconstruction problem is ill-posed and suffers from shape-radiance ambiguity [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Joint Optimization:** When jointly estimating camera poses and scene representation, the problem becomes even more ill-defined [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The warping function $W_{3d}$ must correctly handle the $se(3)$ parameterization to avoid geometric misalignment [S2, S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def photometric_reconstruction_loss(F_sigma, F_c, images, poses):
    """
    Computes the photometric reconstruction loss.
    
    Args:
        F_sigma: Density field function.
        F_c: Color field function.
        images: List of L images, where images[i][u] is the RGB value of pixel u in image i.
        poses: List of L camera poses, where poses[i] is the se(3) parameter for image i.
        
    Returns:
        Total photometric loss.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    L = len(images)
    
    for i in range(L):
        P_i = poses[i]
        image_i = images[i]
        
        # Iterate over all pixels in the image
        # Note: In practice, U is the set of valid pixel coordinates
        for u in image_i.coordinates():
            # Get ground truth color
            I_iu = image_i[u]
            
            # Get ray direction for pixel u
            d_u = get_ray_direction(u)
            
            # Sample point on canonical ray (origin 0, direction d_u)
            s_canon = sample_on_canonical_ray(d_u)
            
            # Warp canonical sample to camera ray space using pose P_i
            s_cam = warp_3d(P_i, s_canon)
            
            # Evaluate radiance field at warped position
            # V(F_sigma, F_c, s_cam) typically involves volume rendering integration
            # For simplicity, assuming V returns the final color for the ray
            rendered_color = volume_render(F_sigma, F_c, s_cam)
            
            # Compute L2 norm difference
            error = norm(rendered_color - I_iu)
            
            total_loss += error
            
    return total_loss
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of images $L$ and the number of pixels $|U|$ per image. The evaluation of $V(F_\sigma, F_c, \cdot)$ dominates the cost per pixel.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the storage of the radiance field $F_\sigma, F_c$ and the intermediate gradients during backpropagation.
*   **Strengths:** Enforces geometric consistency across views by explicitly warping 3D points. Effective for joint optimization of camera poses and scene representation [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The problem is ill-posed and suffers from shape-radiance ambiguity [S2, S2, unofficial]. Joint optimization is highly ill-defined without additional constraints [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When optimizing both camera poses and radiance fields simultaneously, or when geometric consistency across views is critical [S2, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When camera poses are fixed and known, or when computational resources are extremely limited due to the cost of 3D warping and volume rendering per pixel.

## Background
The method originates from the need to jointly optimize camera poses and neural radiance fields. The use of $se(3)$ Lie algebra for camera pose parameterization is a common technique in robotics [S2, S2, unofficial]. The formulation addresses the ill-posed nature of shape-radiance ambiguity [S2, S2, unofficial].
