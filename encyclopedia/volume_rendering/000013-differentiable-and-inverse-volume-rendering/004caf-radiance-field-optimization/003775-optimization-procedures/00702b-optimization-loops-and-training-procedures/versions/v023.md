# Optimization Loops and Training Procedures

Also called: Inverse Rendering Loop

## Overview
This family defines the iterative optimization procedure that minimizes a photometric or physical loss between rendered images and ground-truth observations by updating the parameters of a scene representation (e.g., neural radiance fields, meshes, or transfer functions) [S1, unofficial]. It solves the inverse rendering problem by differentiating the volume rendering equation with respect to scene parameters, enabling gradient-based learning of geometry and appearance [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md). It sets itself apart from [NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation](3079-nerf-evaluation-and-metric-calculation.md) by using stochastic ray sampling and adaptive integration strategies to facilitate gradient propagation, rather than deterministic rendering for metric assessment [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md). The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md):

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [S1, unofficial]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph [S1, unofficial]. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input [S1, unofficial]. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md).

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md)
The iterative optimization procedure that minimizes photometric error between rendered images and ground-truth observations by updating neural radiance field parameters. It sets itself apart by using stochastic ray sampling and adaptive integration to facilitate gradient propagation, rather than deterministic rendering for metric assessment. Choose it for standard neural radiance field training.

### [Optimization Configuration (Adam + Exponential Decay + Selective L2)](2133-optimization-configuration-adam-exponential-decay-selective-.md)
Defines the standard training loop for neural radiance fields and implicit surfaces, combining Adam with a staircase exponential learning rate schedule and selective L2 weight decay. It sets itself apart by explicitly integrating domain-specific augmentations and regularization into the core loop. Choose it for training coordinate-based neural fields on noisy real-world data.

### [Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)](3429-neural-volume-rendering-training-mse.md)
Optimizes neural radiance fields by minimizing photometric error while using sparse depth priors solely to schedule sampling region expansion, avoiding direct depth supervision bias. It sets itself apart by replacing global initialization with a local-to-global sampling schedule. Choose it when sparse depth priors are available but direct depth supervision is undesirable.

### [Optimization Baselines](6675-optimization-baselines.md)
Provides black-box optimization strategies for inverse rendering where the forward operator is non-differentiable or gradients are expensive. It sets itself apart by relying on population-based search distributions (typically Gaussian) that adapt to the loss landscape without explicit gradients. Choose it for non-differentiable renderers or when gradient computation is prohibitive.

### [Activation-Informed Coarse-to-Fine Rendering](12277-activation-informed-coarse-to-fine-rendering.md)
Accelerates NeRF rendering by skipping the expensive fine-pass MLP evaluation for rays determined to be empty or background, using intermediate activation values from the coarse pass. It sets itself apart by introducing a data-dependent mask derived from hidden layer statistics rather than a fixed threshold. Choose it for synthetic scenes with transparent backgrounds to speed up inference.

### [Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)](12876-phase-aware-gradient-descent-pagd.md)
Encodes complex wave fields into phase-only holograms for 3D holographic displays by supervising the phase of the reconstructed wave field. It sets itself apart from amplitude-only methods by adhering to the physical two-step hologram process and using gradient-based loss minimization with a learned forward model. Choose it for holographic display optimization.

### [Staged and Alternating Optimization](12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md)
Decouples the optimization of coupled parameters (geometry, material, lighting, camera poses) into sequential phases or alternating sub-problems. It sets itself apart from simultaneous joint optimization by preventing gradient interference and local minima. Choose it when optimizing multiple interdependent scene parameters.

### [Continuous Coarse-To-Fine Optimization](13025-continuous-coarse-to-fine-optimization.md)
Progressively restricts the Level of Detail (LOD) of the scene representation to coarse levels initially, then gradually relaxes this regularization. It sets itself apart by operating continuously in LOD space without modifying previously trained parameters. Choose it to mitigate shape-radiance ambiguity and converge to higher-quality optima.

### [Last Layer Re-training](13532-last-layer-re-training.md)
Propagates a color edit from a single view to the entire NeRF by selectively retraining only the last layer of the color MLP. It sets itself apart from full retraining by avoiding the loss of pre-trained information and preventing color bleeding at boundaries. Choose it for efficient NeRF editing.

### [Temporal Optimization Heuristics](13688-temporal-optimization-heuristics.md)
Models dynamic content in differentiable bitmap primitive rendering by optimizing video frames sequentially to achieve per-frame fidelity and temporal anti-flicker. It sets itself apart by applying lightweight heuristics (warm-starting, removing stuck primitives, freezing unchanged regions) to balance competing goals. Choose it for dynamic scene optimization.

### [Framework and Baseline Implementations](16220-framework-and-baseline-implementations.md)
Defines the software architectures and training protocols for executing differentiable volume rendering optimization. It sets itself apart by specifying the low-level execution model and high-level training schedules rather than mathematical loss functions. Choose it for implementing the computational infrastructure for differentiable rendering.

### [Multi-scale L2 Loss Optimization with Random Variable Perturbation](20413-multi-scale-l2-loss-optimization-with-random-variable-pertur.md)
Optimizes shader parameters to match a target image by minimizing a multi-scale L2 loss while using random variable perturbation to escape local minima. It sets itself apart by alternating between low-resolution and full-resolution objectives and perturbing non-ray-marching parameters. Choose it for differentiable shader parameter optimization with sparse gradient signals.

### [TomOpt Optimisation Callbacks](21619-tomopt-optimisation-callbacks.md)
A modular system of hooks that augment the basic differentiable optimization loop for muon tomography detector design, stabilizing training and ensuring physically meaningful updates. It sets itself apart by integrating physical unit scaling and in-place de-biasing directly into the gradient update cycle. Choose it for physics-constrained detector optimization.

### [Submap Optimization](21622-submap-optimization.md)
Manages memory and accuracy in Gaussian Splatting-based SLAM by dividing the scene into local submaps and refining them iteratively. It sets itself apart from global optimization by limiting the scope to a local region around the camera. Choose it for large-scale scene optimization.

### [Differentiable Optimisation via Back-Propagation](21689-differentiable-optimisation-via-back-propagation.md)
Optimizes scene or system parameters by computing gradients of a task-specific loss function with respect to those parameters using automatic differentiation. It sets itself apart by focusing on end-to-end task performance optimization rather than proxy metrics like image fidelity. Choose it for optimizing parameters in differentiable forward models.

### [Domain-Specific Optimization Pipelines](22012-domain-specific-optimization-pipelines.md)
Specialized training procedures that adapt the general differentiable volume rendering framework to specific physical or geometric constraints. It sets itself apart by explicit integration of external domain tools or structured data partitioning. Choose it when optimizing for specific physical or geometric constraints.

### Rip-NeRF Optimization
Optimization process for Rip-NeRF involving tiny MLP weights and ripmaps $R$ [S2]. Learning rate is scaled by 10x for $R$ due to its direct scene representation role, with a base learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-3}$ reduced by 0.6x at steps 60K, 90K, 100K, and 108K over 120K iterations [S2]. Batch size is dynamically adjusted to maintain ~256K spheres per batch [S2]. MLP weights are initialized via Glorot and Bengio (2010), and ripmaps $R$ via uniform distribution over $[-0.01, 0.01]$ [S2]. Optimizer is AdamW [S2].

### [Geometry Focused Model Optimization](12346-geometry-focused-model-optimization.md)
Initial stage applies the rendering equation directly to each Gaussian primitive to compute outgoing radiance using material and geometry properties, then alpha-blends during rasterization. This stabilizes gradient transfer to geometry in the initial phase.

### Polyak-Ruppert Averaging
Reduces wandering around the true solution due to Monte Carlo noise by maintaining a running average of parameter values over the last 10% of the optimization steps [S3].

### Primal Rendering for Many-Worlds Inverse Rendering
Computes primal radiance by averaging over all perturbations, scaling the sum by $1/N$ to prevent unbounded values [S4]. If $k == k_{mw}$, samples a point $t$ and surface $S_{surf}$ from distribution $D$ within segment $s$, then returns final radiance by linearly interpolating between background and foreground radiance using occupancy $O(x')$ at the sampled point, multiplied by the weight [S4]. Rendering only the surface $\bar{S}$ and discarding other surfaces causes the extended parameter space to disappear, leading to random optimization behavior in distant regions because occupancy values do not decay to zero [S4].

### Batch View-Independent Rendering Optimization
Optimization strategy that propagates radiance once to compute light transport, then renders multiple training images simultaneously for supervision to accelerate training [S5]. It sets itself apart by using a batch of training images at each step to compute light transport once and render multiple images for supervision [S5]. Choose it to accelerate training with global illumination. Batch size 4 reduces training time to slightly over one-fourth of batch size 1, improves view synthesis quality (PSNR 40.05, SSIM 0.9908, LPIPS 0.0218, CD 0.190), but slightly compromises geometry reconstruction quality (CD 0.190 vs 0.173) [S5].

## Background
The concept of using a differentiable renderer inside an optimizer to estimate pose, geometry, materials, or illumination from images or other measured responses relies on the renderer propagating relevant derivatives [S1, unofficial]. The loop is simple; choosing a faithful model, identifiable parameters, a stable estimator, and a good initialization is difficult [S1, unofficial].
