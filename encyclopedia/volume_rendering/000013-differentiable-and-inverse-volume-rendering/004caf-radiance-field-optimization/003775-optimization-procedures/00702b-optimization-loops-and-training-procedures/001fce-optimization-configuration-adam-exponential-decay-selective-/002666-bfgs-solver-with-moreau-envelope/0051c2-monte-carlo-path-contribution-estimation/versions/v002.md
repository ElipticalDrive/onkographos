# Monte Carlo Path Contribution Estimation

Also called: Caustic contribution estimation, RRTE path estimator

## Overview
This method estimates the radiance contribution of a stationary light path found by the BFGS solver, based on the path formulation of the Radiative Transfer Equation (RRTE) [S1]. It differs from standard path tracing by evaluating contributions for specific, optimized stationary paths rather than sampling paths stochastically from the camera, and it differs from the parent BFGS solver by handling the final radiance accumulation and variance reduction [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Stationary path $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k = (\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_n)$ found by the BFGS solver, where $\mathbf{x}_0$ is the receiver (camera) and $\mathbf{x}_n$ is the light source [S1].
*   Refractive Index Field (RIF) $\eta(\mathbf{x})$ describing the media [S1].
*   Extinction coefficient $\sigma_t(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
*   Final inverse Hessian matrix from the BFGS solver [S1].
*   Scene geometry for occlusion testing [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Estimated radiance contribution $I$ for the path [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   Path points $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   Generalized geometric factor $G$ [S1].
*   Transmittance $T$ [S1].
*   Exact layouts, fields, sizes, and bit packing: Not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1. Numerically integrate the transmittance along each ray section $\Gamma_k$ between $\mathbf{x}_k$ and $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}$ [S1].
2. Trace a shadow ray from $\mathbf{x}_0$ in the direction of $\mathbf{x}_1$ to check for intersections with the rest of the scene or bubbles inside the panel [S1].
3. Compute the generalized geometric factor $G(\mathbf{x}_1 \leftrightarrow \dots \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_{n-1})$ for the specular interfaces using the implicit function theorem and the final inverse Hessian matrix from the BFGS solver [S1].
4. Regularize $G$ by clamping it to a minimum $\epsilon$ value to prevent instability [S1].
5. Estimate the inverse probability $1/p(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)$ of the BFGS solver converging to the path using the unbiased iterative procedure described in [21] [S1].
6. Compute the contribution function $f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)$ using the integrated transmittance, regularized geometric factors, and estimated probability [S1].
7. Sum the weighted contributions over $K$ samples to obtain the final radiance estimate [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   Transmittance: $T(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1}) = \exp \left( - \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \sigma_t(\mathbf{x}(\tau)) \eta(\mathbf{x}(\tau)) d\tau \right)$ [S1].
*   Integration method: Composite Simpson rule, assuming $\sigma_t$ varies slowly within a single tetrahedral cell [S1].
*   Regularized geometric factor: $\bar{G}(\mathbf{x}_1 \leftrightarrow \dots \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_{n-1}) = \max(G(\mathbf{x}_1 \leftrightarrow \dots \leftrightarrow \mathbf{x}_{n-1}), \epsilon)$ [S1].
*   Contribution function: $f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k) = W_e(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1) \eta^2(\mathbf{x}_n) L_e(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{x}_{n-1}) G(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1) T(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1) \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} G(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1}) T(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1}) f_c(\mathbf{x}_{i-1}, \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_{i+1})$ [S1].
*   Final estimator: $I \simeq \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)}{p(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k)}$ [S1].
*   Symbol definitions: $W_e$ is the exitance weight, $\eta(\mathbf{x}_n)$ is the refractive index at the source, $L_e$ is a radiance term, $f_c$ is a scattering term, $G$ is the geometric factor, $T$ is transmittance, $p$ is the sampling probability, and $i$ is the product index running from $1$ to $n-1$ [S1]. The source formula explicitly places $G(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1)$ and $T(\mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1)$ outside the product despite $\mathbf{x}_0$ being the camera [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon$: Regularization constant for $G$. Typical values: $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   The computation of $G$ can be unstable, leading to high variance, necessitating regularization [S1].
*   The BFGS solver initialization does not guarantee finding all possible paths in regions with strong RIF variations, which may cause local underestimation of transmitted lighting [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** The method is used in conjunction with BFGS optimization. The text mentions 1M samples per pixel at 1024x1024 resolution for the final images, but this refers to the overall rendering pipeline, not just this estimator [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Provides an unbiased estimate of the contribution of stationary paths. Regularization helps eliminate highly contributing outliers [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Can locally underestimate the brightest parts of the transmitted caustic due to regularization. May miss paths in regions with strong RIF variations [S1].

## Background
This method is part of a pipeline for rendering old glass windows, where ancient windows were composed of several smaller fragments of glass assembled with lead [S1]. The method builds on the path formulation of the RRTE [S1].
