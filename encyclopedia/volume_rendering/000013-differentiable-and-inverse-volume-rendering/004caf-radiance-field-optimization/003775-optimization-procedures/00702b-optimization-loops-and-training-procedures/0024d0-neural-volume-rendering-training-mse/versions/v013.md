# Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)

Also called: Hierarchical Geometric Guidance (HGG)

## Overview
Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE) using Hierarchical Geometric Guidance (HGG) is a training procedure for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that optimizes scene density and color representations by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered and ground-truth images, while using sparse depth priors solely to guide the sampling region initialization [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard NeRF training by avoiding direct depth supervision, which introduces geometric bias due to keypoint mismatching and translation errors, and instead uses depth to define a local-to-global sampling schedule that expands from the depth prior to full scene bounds [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Optimization Loops and Training Procedures](5622-optimization-loops-and-training-procedures.md) by implementing a specific initialization and expansion strategy for the volume rendering integration bounds.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Sparse depth priors $t_{depth}$ from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1, unofficial]; ground-truth images $C_{gt}$; camera poses; total training iterations $N_{total}$; HGG iteration count $N_{hgg}$; minimum adjusting rate $\epsilon_{hgg}$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Outputs:** Optimized neural radiance field parameters $\Theta$ (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Scene Representation:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) $f_\Theta$ mapping 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Sampling Points:** A set of $N$ points $t_i$ along each ray $\mathbf{r}$, defined by near plane $t_n$ and far plane $t_f$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Iteration State:** Current iteration $i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Initialization:** For each ray, initialize the near plane $t_n$ and far plane $t_f$ close to the sparse depth prior $t_{depth}$ to define a local sampling region [S1, unofficial].
2. **Region Expansion Schedule:** For iterations $i = 0$ to $N_{hgg}$, compute the region adjustment rate $\gamma(i)$ and update $t_n(i)$ and $t_f(i)$ using the formulas below [S1, unofficial]. For iterations $i > N_{hgg}$, keep $t_n$ and $t_f$ at the full scene bounds [S1, unofficial].
3. **Volume Rendering:** Sample $N$ points within the current bounds $[t_n(i), t_f(i)]$. Query $f_\Theta$ for $\sigma_i$ and $\mathbf{c}_i$. Compute the rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using the standard emission-absorption model [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
   $$
   \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
   $$
   where $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ and $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1, unofficial].
4. **Loss Computation:** Compute the MSE loss between the rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground-truth color $C_{gt}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1, unofficial].
5. **Optimization:** Update $\Theta$ using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- **Region Adjustment Rate:**
  $$
  \gamma(i) = \frac{1 - \cos\left( (\min(\max(i/N_{hgg}, \epsilon_{hgg}), 1)) \pi \right)}{2}
  $$
  where $i$ is the current iteration, $N_{hgg}$ is the number of iterations until full bounds are reached, and $\epsilon_{hgg}$ is the minimum adjusting rate [S1, unofficial].
- **Near/Far Plane Update:**
  $$
  t_n(i) = t_{depth} + (t_n - t_{depth}) \gamma(i)
  $$
  $$
  t_f(i) = t_{depth} + (t_f - t_{depth}) \gamma(i)
  $$
  where $t_n, t_f$ are the initial near/far planes, and $t_{depth}$ is the depth prior [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $N_{hgg}$: Number of iterations for region expansion. Typical value: 10% of total training iterations [S1, unofficial].
- $\epsilon_{hgg}$: Minimum adjusting rate. Typical value: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
- Learning Rate: $1 \times 10^{-3}$, exponentially decaying to $1 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1, unofficial].
- Samples: 64 volume points for coarse model, 128 for fine model (if using Hierarchical Photometric Guidance variant) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Depth Bias:** Direct supervision of sparse depth prior introduces bias $\Delta P$ due to keypoint mismatching ($\Delta d$) and small translation between frames, causing geometric misalignment [S1, unofficial]. HGG avoids this by using depth only for sampling region initialization [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Ensure $\gamma(i)$ is clamped to $[0, 1]$ to prevent invalid bounds [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def compute_gamma(i, N_hgg, epsilon_hgg):
    """Compute the region adjustment rate gamma(i)."""
    ratio = i / N_hgg
    clamped_ratio = max(epsilon_hgg, min(ratio, 1.0))
    return (1 - math.cos(clamped_ratio * math.pi)) / 2

def update_bounds(t_n_init, t_f_init, t_depth, i, N_hgg, epsilon_hgg):
    """Update near and far planes based on iteration i."""
    if i <= N_hgg:
        gamma = compute_gamma(i, N_hgg, epsilon_hgg)
        t_n = t_depth + (t_n_init - t_depth) * gamma
        t_f = t_depth + (t_f_init - t_depth) * gamma
    else:
        # Full scene bounds reached
        t_n = t_n_init
        t_f = t_f_init
    return t_n, t_f

def train_neural_volume_rendering_mse_hgg(
    depth_prior, 
    initial_t_n, 
    initial_t_f, 
    total_iterations, 
    epsilon_hgg=0.2,
    samples_per_ray=64
):
    """
    Train a neural volume rendering model using MSE loss and HGG.
    
    Args:
        depth_prior: Sparse depth prior from SfM.
        initial_t_n: Initial near plane.
        initial_t_f: Initial far plane.
        total_iterations: Total number of training iterations.
        epsilon_hgg: Minimum adjusting rate.
        samples_per_ray: Number of samples per ray.
        
    Returns:
        Optimized model parameters.
    """
    N_hgg = int(0.1 * total_iterations)  # Typical value: 10% of total iterations
    
    # Initialize model parameters Theta
    Theta = initialize_model()
    
    for i in range(total_iterations):
        # 1. Update sampling bounds
        t_n, t_f = update_bounds(initial_t_n, initial_t_f, depth_prior, i, N_hgg, epsilon_hgg)
        
        # 2. Sample points along rays within [t_n, t_f]
        # This step depends on the specific sampling strategy (e.g., uniform, stratified)
        # and is not fully detailed in the source for the basic HGG method.
        # [G] Assume a standard uniform or stratified sampling within [t_n, t_f]
        samples = sample_points(t_n, t_f, samples_per_ray)
        
        # 3. Query model for density and color
        # [G] Assume model query returns sigma and c for each sample
        sigma, c = query_model(Theta, samples)
        
        # 4. Compute transmittance and rendered color
        # Standard volume rendering equation
        deltas = samples[1:] - samples[:-1]
        deltas = np.append(deltas, 1e-10) # Handle last delta
        transmittance = np.exp(-np.cumsum(sigma * deltas, axis=0))
        weights = (1 - np.exp(-sigma * deltas)) * transmittance
        rendered_color = np.sum(weights * c, axis=0)
        
        # 5. Compute MSE loss
        # [G] Assume ground_truth_color is available for the current batch
        loss = np.mean((rendered_color - ground_truth_color) ** 2)
        
        # 6. Update parameters
        # [G] Assume optimizer step is performed
        update_parameters(Theta, loss)
        
    return Theta
```

## Subtopics

### Hierarchical Photometric Guidance
Trains a coarse model and a fine model to improve novel view synthesis performance by combining their predicted colors in the loss function [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard HGG by using a two-stage hierarchical loss rather than just geometric guidance [S1, unofficial]. Choose it when higher fidelity is needed at the cost of increased memory and computation [S1, unofficial].
- **Definition:** Combines coarse and fine model predictions in the loss [S1, unofficial].
- **Steps:** 1. Query coarse model $f_\Theta$ with stratified volume samples $t_c$ to get $\hat{C}_{crecon}$ [S1, unofficial]. 2. Query fine model with union of coarse and fine samples $t_c \cup t_f$ (produced by inverse transform sampling) to get $\hat{C}_{frecon}$ [S1, unofficial]. 3. Compute MSE loss against ground truth $C_{gt}$ for both predictions [S1, unofficial].
- **Formula:** $L_{hpg} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{r \in R} \left( C_{gt}(r) - \hat{C}_{crecon}(r; t_c) \right)^2 + \left( C_{gt}(r) - \hat{C}_{frecon}(r; t_c \cup t_f) \right)^2$, where $R$ is the set of all rays, $t_c$ are coarse stratified samples, and $t_f$ are fine samples produced by inverse transform sampling [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of samples and rays, similar to standard NeRF, but with additional overhead for computing the expansion schedule and potentially querying two models if using Hierarchical Photometric Guidance [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(n)$ for storing intermediate rendering states, similar to standard NeRF [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
- **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on DTU dataset for 3, 6, and 9 views, showing more fine details and distinct edges in depth maps compared to SOTA baselines [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Avoids geometric bias from sparse depth supervision; improves novel view synthesis in sparse view scenarios [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires sparse depth priors from SfM; performance may degrade if depth priors are inaccurate [S1, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** Sparse view reconstruction where direct depth supervision is unreliable [S1, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** Dense view scenarios where standard NeRF suffices; scenarios without reliable depth priors [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated in the HG3-NeRF paper (2024) by authors of the arXiv preprint "HG3-NeRF: Hierarchical Geometric, Semantic, and Photometric Guided Neural Radiance Fields for Sparse View Inputs" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of geometric misalignment in sparse view NeRF reconstruction caused by bias in sparse depth supervision [S1, unofficial].
