# Staged and Alternating Optimization

Also called: Multi-stage optimization, Alternating minimization, Progressive training

## Overview
Staged and Alternating Optimization addresses the ill-posed nature and high dimensionality of inverse volume rendering and radiance field reconstruction by decoupling the optimization of distinct parameter groups (e.g., geometry vs. material, shape vs. blur, or coarse vs. fine scales) into sequential phases [S1]. Unlike simultaneous joint optimization, which often suffers from gradient interference and local minima, this family of methods alternates between optimizing subsets of parameters while holding others fixed, or progressively refines the representation resolution [S1, S2, 4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md, 14063-optimization-schedule.md, 16123-differentiable-uncalibrated-imaging-optimization.md]. It sets itself apart from single-stage methods by explicitly managing the coupling between variables—such as separating silhouette alignment from material reflectance or shape from Point Spread Function (PSF) parameters—to stabilize convergence and improve physical accuracy [S1, 5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md, 7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Stage-based Differentiable Volume/Radiance Field Training](4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md)
A stage-based optimization loop for differentiable volume or radiance field reconstruction that alternates between image-based and ray-based sampling modes, applies stochastic preconditioning with exponential alpha decay, and optionally upsamples parameters between stages [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by explicitly managing memory via ray batching and using a progressive upsampling schedule to stabilize training of high-resolution radiance fields [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md]. Choose this method when training large-scale radiance fields where memory constraints or resolution scaling are critical bottlenecks [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md]. [4241-stage-based-differentiable-volume-radiance-field-training.md]

### [Staggered Optimization Scheme](5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md)
The Staggered Optimization Scheme addresses the computational bottleneck and bias inherent in jointly optimizing geometric shape parameters and Point Spread Function (PSF) parameters in differentiable volume rendering. By decomposing the coupled optimization problem into two sequential sub-problems—shape optimization and PSF optimization—it manages computational cost and prevents bias from approximation thresholds. Unlike joint optimization methods that update all parameters simultaneously, this method alternates between optimizing vertex positions (with fixed PSF) and optimizing PSF covariance (with fixed shape), stabilizing the gradient descent process. Choose this method when reconstructing scenes with significant blur or when the PSF is unknown and coupled with geometry. [5832-staggered-optimization-scheme.md]

### [Two-Stage Inverse Rendering Optimization](7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md)
Two-Stage Inverse Rendering Optimization decouples the recovery of scene geometry from material reflectance and illumination by alternating between an approximate differentiable renderer and a physically-based ray tracer. It solves the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by first establishing a robust geometric proxy using silhouette constraints, then refining physically accurate albedo and environment maps while holding geometry fixed. This approach differs from single-stage radiance field methods (e.g., [NeRF](44-radiance-field-optimization.md), [PhySG](44-radiance-field-optimization.md)) by explicitly modeling triangle meshes or Gaussian splats with PBR materials, enabling direct deployment in graphics engines and superior specular handling. Choose this method when physically accurate relighting and material separation are required. [7387-two-stage-inverse-rendering-optimization.md]

### [Optimization Schedule](14063-optimization-schedule.md)
Optimization Schedule refers to the structured progression of training parameters in differentiable volume and radiance field reconstruction, designed to mitigate local minima and ensure stable convergence. It addresses the problem of ambiguous gradients in inverse rendering by decoupling the optimization of geometric structure from material properties or fine-scale details. Unlike simultaneous optimization of all parameters, which often leads to instability in sparse-view settings, this method separates the process into distinct phases or scales, allowing the solver to first establish a coarse global alignment before refining local details. Choose this method when dealing with sparse views or when high-frequency details (textures) are prone to compensating for geometric errors. [14063-optimization-schedule.md]

### [Differentiable uncalibrated imaging optimization](16123-differentiable-uncalibrated-imaging-optimization.md)
This method jointly optimizes the volume reconstruction (radiance field) and the measurement coordinates (e.g., view angles, detector positions) by minimizing a differentiable objective function using automatic differentiation. It addresses the problem of uncalibrated imaging where the geometric parameters of the acquisition system are unknown or uncertain. Unlike methods that assume fixed camera poses, this approach treats measurement coordinates as trainable parameters alongside the scene representation, allowing for simultaneous refinement of geometry and appearance. It builds on [Staged and Alternating Optimization](12945-staged-and-alternating-optimization.md) by integrating coordinate optimization into the primary training loop rather than separating it into distinct phases. Choose this method when camera poses or detector positions are inaccurate or unknown. [16123-differentiable-uncalibrated-imaging-optimization.md]

### Multi-Stage Albedo Optimization
A variant of staged optimization specifically for material recovery. It optimizes albedo first as a 1024x1024 RGB texture, then refines in two stages to reach the final 4096x4096 resolution [S1]. After the first stage, emitters contributing a small fraction of the total scene radiance are rounded to zero [S1]. This prevents low-energy noise from interfering with the high-resolution material refinement [S1].

## Background
The concept of staged optimization in inverse rendering and volume reconstruction has evolved to address the non-convexity and ill-posedness of the underlying problems. Early works in differentiable rendering demonstrated that joint optimization of geometry and appearance often leads to local minima [S1]. The Staggered Optimization Scheme [S1] was developed to handle the specific coupling between shape and blur in microscopy or medical imaging contexts. Two-Stage Inverse Rendering [S1] emerged from the need to produce physically plausible materials and lighting for graphics applications, moving beyond simple color fitting to PBR parameter recovery. Optimization Schedules [S1] became prominent with the rise of 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRFs to handle the scale disparity between coarse geometry and fine textures. Uncalibrated optimization [S1] addresses practical acquisition scenarios where calibration data is imperfect.
