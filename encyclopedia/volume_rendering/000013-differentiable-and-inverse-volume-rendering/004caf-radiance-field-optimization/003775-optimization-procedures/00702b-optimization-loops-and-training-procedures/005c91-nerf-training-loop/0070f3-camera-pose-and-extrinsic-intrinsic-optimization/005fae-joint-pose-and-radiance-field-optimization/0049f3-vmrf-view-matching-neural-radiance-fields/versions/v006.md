# VMRF (View Matching Neural Radiance Fields)

Also called: View Matching NeRF

## Overview
VMRF is a method for novel view synthesis that learns Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations without prior knowledge of camera poses or hand-crafted camera pose distributions [S1]. It employs a view matching scheme using unbalanced optimal transport to map features from a rendered image (with a random pose) to a real image, generating a feature transport plan [S1]. This plan guides a pose calibration technique that predicts relative pose transformations to rectify randomized poses into estimated real-image poses, which are then used to jointly refine the NeRF and camera parameters [S1]. Unlike GNeRF, which estimates absolute poses directly and requires accurate pose distributions, VMRF estimates relative poses, making it robust to out-of-dataset views [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $n$ real RGB images $I = \{I_1, I_2, ..., I_n\}$ of a static scene. No prior camera poses are provided.
**Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and estimated camera poses $\hat{\Phi} = \{\hat{\phi}_i, i \in [1, n]\}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF ($F_\Theta$):** A 5D function mapping 3D point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $d \in S^2$ to color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$. Parameterized by an MLP with parameters $\Theta$ [S1].
*   **Camera Pose ($\phi$):** Represented as a $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix. Rotation is encoded as a continuous 6D embedding; position as a 3D Euclidean embedding [S1].
*   **Feature Sets:** Source feature set $f_s \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$ and target feature set $f_t \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$, where $l=64$ is the number of feature vectors and $d=256$ is the dimension [S1].
*   **Transport Plan ($T$):** A matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ where $T_{ij}$ represents the mass moved from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].
*   **Relative Transformation Predictor ($RTP$):** A Vision Transformer-based network that outputs a 6-value vector $[t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z]$ representing relative translation and rotation [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of random samples along a camera ray for volume rendering [S1].
*   $\eta$: Regularization parameter for entropic UOT smoothness. Typical value: $0.005$ [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Regularization parameter for KL divergence penalties in UOT [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a coarse NeRF with random network parameters $\Theta$ and randomly initialized camera poses $\Phi' = \{\phi'_i, i \in [1, n]\}$ [S1].

2.  **Coarse Training (Adversarial):** Train the coarse NeRF using an adversarial loss to ensure generated images match the distribution of real images. This stabilizes the initial representation [S1].
    *   Loss: $L_{adv}(F, D) = \mathbb{E}_{I \sim P_{data}}[\log(D(I))] + \mathbb{E}_{F(\Phi') \sim P_g}[\log(1 - D(F(\Phi')))]$, where $D$ is a discriminator [S1].

3.  **Rendering:** For each real image $I_i$, render a corresponding image using the current NeRF $F$ and the randomized pose $\phi'_i$ [S1].

4.  **Feature Extraction:** Extract feature sets from the rendered image (source) and the real image (target) using pre-trained VGG-19 networks [S1].
    *   Source: $f_s \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$
    *   Target: $f_t \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$

5.  **Cost Matrix Calculation:** Compute the cost matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ based on feature similarities [S1].
    $$ M = 1 - \frac{f_s \cdot f_t^T}{\|f_s\| \|f_t\|} $$
    Here, $M_{ij}$ denotes the moving cost from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].

6.  **Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT):** Solve for the optimal transport plan $T^*$ that minimizes the transport cost while allowing for unequal mass distributions (due to occlusion/view differences) via KL divergence penalties and entropic regularization [S1].
    $$ T^* = \arg \min_T \left[ \sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} M_{ij} - \eta H(T) + \epsilon KL(T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_s) + \epsilon KL(T^T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_t) \right] $$
    Where:
    *   $H(T) = -\sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} \log(T_{ij} + 1)$ is the entropic regularization term [S1].
    *   $\mu_s$ and $\mu_t$ are the discrete source and target distributions derived from $f_s$ and $f_t$ [S1].
    *   The solution is computed using the Sinkhorn algorithm on the Fenchel-Legendre dual form [S1].

7.  **Relative Pose Prediction:** Predict the relative transformation between the real image and the rendered image using the transport plan $T^*$ [S1].
    $$ [t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z] = RTP(T^*) $$
    The output is a 6-dimensional vector representing relative translation and rotation [S1].

8.  **Pose Calibration:** Construct the relative rotation matrix $\Delta R$ and translation vector $\Delta t$ from the predicted values [S1].
    $$ \Delta R = R_x(\theta_x) R_y(\theta_y) R_z(\theta_z) $$
    Where $R_x, R_y, R_z$ are standard rotation matrices:
    $$ R_x(\theta_x) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\theta_x & \sin\theta_x \\ 0 & -\sin\theta_x & \cos\theta_x \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_y(\theta_y) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_y & 0 & -\sin\theta_y \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ \sin\theta_y & 0 & \cos\theta_y \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_z(\theta_z) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_z & \sin\theta_z & 0 \\ -\sin\theta_z & \cos\theta_z & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    $$ \Delta t = [t_x, t_y, t_z]^T $$
    Calibrate the randomized pose $\phi'_i$ to obtain the estimated pose $\hat{\phi}_i$ [S1]:
    $$ \hat{\phi}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta R & \Delta t \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \phi'_i $$

9.  **NeRF Optimization:** Optimize the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ using the calibrated poses $\hat{\Phi}$ and a photometric loss [S1].
    $$ L_P(F, \hat{\Phi}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \| I_i - F(\hat{\phi}_i) \|^2 $$

10. **Iterative Refinement:** The process iterates, refining both the NeRF and the poses. The relative transformation predictor is also trained with a calibration loss $L_{ca}$ using pairs of random poses to ensure consistency [S1].

def volume_render(nerf, ray_origins, ray_directions, N):
    """
    Standard NeRF volume rendering.
    """
    # Sample points along ray
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, N)
    z_vals = 0.1 + t_vals * 0.9 # Near/Far bounds assumed
    points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_directions.unsqueeze(1) * z_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    
    # Query NeRF
    colors, densities = nerf(points, ray_directions)
    
    # Compute alpha and transmittance
    deltas = z_vals[:, 1:] - z_vals[:, :-1]
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-densities * deltas)
    
    T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha, dim=1)
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T], dim=1)
    
    weights = alpha * T[:, :-1]
    color = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * colors, dim=1)
    
    return color

## Subtopics

### GNeRF
A related method that estimates absolute camera poses directly from images using an inversion network. It requires hand-crafted camera pose distributions for training, which can be disturbed by out-of-dataset views. VMRF improves upon this by using relative pose estimation via view matching. [GNeRF](11988-gnerf.md)

### RoDynRF
A sibling method in the family of joint pose and radiance field optimization. While VMRF focuses on view matching for pose calibration, RoDynRF likely addresses dynamic scenes or different optimization constraints. [RoDynRF](11988-rodynrf.md)

### NeRF Pose and Appearance Optimization
A broader category of methods that optimize camera poses and appearance factors. VMRF is a specific instance within this category that uses optimal transport for pose estimation. [NeRF Pose and Appearance Optimization](13113-nerf-pose-and-appearance-optimization.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The dominant cost is the Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) computation via the Sinkhorn algorithm, which is $O(l^2 \cdot k)$ where $l$ is the number of feature vectors (64) and $k$ is the number of Sinkhorn iterations. This is performed per image pair per training step.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the feature sets ($l \times d$), the cost matrix ($l \times l$), and the transport plan ($l \times l$). With $l=64, d=256$, this is negligible compared to the NeRF MLP and image buffers.
*   **Strengths**:
    *   Does not require prior camera poses or hand-crafted pose distributions [S1].
    *   Robust to out-of-dataset views because it estimates relative poses rather than absolute ones [S1].
    *   Handles low-texture and repetitive pattern scenes where classical SfM fails [S1].
    *   Uses Unbalanced OT to handle occlusions and non-surjective feature matching [S1].
*   **Limitations**:
    *   Relies on a pre-trained VGG-19 for feature extraction, which may not capture all semantic details relevant to pose estimation.
    *   Requires careful tuning of regularization parameters $\eta$ and $\epsilon$ [S1].
    *   Training is iterative and joint, which may be slower to converge than methods with known poses.
*   **When to use**: When camera poses are unknown or unreliable, and the scene contains low-texture regions or repetitive patterns that break SfM. Suitable for synthetic and real-world datasets like Synthetic-NeRF and DTU [S1].
*   **When not to use**: When high-precision absolute poses are available and SfM works reliably, as standard NeRF training is faster and simpler.

## Background
VMRF was proposed by Jiahui Zhang, Fangneng Zhan, Rongliang Wu, Yingchen Yu, Wenqing Zhang, Bai Song, Xiaoqin Zhang, and Shijian Lu from Nanyang Technological University, Max Planck Institute for Informatics, and ByteDance [S1]. It was published in 2022 [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of previous pose-free NeRF methods like GNeRF, which struggled with out-of-dataset views due to their reliance on absolute pose estimation and hand-crafted pose distributions [S1]. VMRF introduces the concept of view matching via optimal transport to estimate relative poses, enabling effective NeRF training without prior pose knowledge [S1]. It has been evaluated on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets, showing superior performance over GNeRF [S1].
