# RoDynRF
Also called: Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields

## Overview
RoDynRF enables dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular videos without known camera poses by jointly optimizing intrinsics, extrinsics, and both static and dynamic radiance fields [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [SPARF](12705-sparf.md) by explicitly separating static and dynamic components via a two-branch architecture and using motion masks to exclude dynamic pixels during pose estimation, thereby stabilizing the joint optimization of camera parameters and scene geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A sequence of monocular images (video frames) with unknown camera poses.
- **Outputs:** Optimized camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics), a static radiance field, and a dynamic radiance field with time-dependent deformation.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Static Radiance Field:** Standard NeRF representation (MLP or voxel grid) encoding density $\sigma$ and color $c$ for static background regions.
2.  **Dynamic Radiance Field:** A separate representation for moving objects, coupled with a deformation network.
3.  **Motion Mask:** A binary mask $M$ identifying dynamic pixels in each frame, used to exclude them from pose optimization losses.
4.  **Deformation Network:** A time-conditioned network that maps spatial coordinates and time $t$ to deformation vectors.

**Steps:**
1.  **Static-to-Dynamic Learning Paradigm:**
    -   First, learn the static scene representation from multi-view stills or the static portions of the video. This provides a stable baseline for geometry and appearance [S1].
    -   Second, learn the deformation from the dynamic sequence. This two-stage approach allows for much faster convergence than fully implicit networks [S1].
2.  **Two-Branch Architecture:**
    -   The method introduces a two-branch architecture that separates the background (static) and moving regions (dynamic) [S1].
    -   For a given ray, the rendering process queries both branches. The final color is a composite of the static and dynamic contributions, weighted by the occupancy or mask.
3.  **Pose Estimation with Motion Masks:**
    -   During the optimization of camera poses (extrinsics and intrinsics), motion masks are used to exclude dynamic pixels from the loss calculation [S1].
    -   This prevents the optimizer from being misled by moving objects, ensuring that pose updates are driven by the static background structure.
4.  **Coarse-to-Fine Voxel Optimization:**
    -   A coarse-to-fine voxel optimization scheme is employed to ensure stability during the joint optimization of poses and fields [S1].
    -   Optimization begins at a lower resolution and progressively refines the voxel grid or sampling density.
5.  **Time-Conditioned Deformation:**
    -   A time-conditioned deformation network models non-rigid motion [S1].
    -   For a point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t$, the deformed position $\mathbf{x}'$ is computed as:
        $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \Delta \mathbf{x}(\mathbf{x}, t) $$
        where $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ is the output of the deformation network. This allows the recovery of dynamic geometry and appearance from unposed, in-the-wild video data [S1].

**Parameters:**
-   Learning rates for static field, dynamic field, and camera poses (typically different to stabilize convergence, as per shared principles).
-   Time encoding parameters for the deformation network.
-   Coarse-to-fine resolution levels.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
-   **Dynamic Background:** If the background is also dynamic, the separation assumption fails. The method relies on a significant static component for pose estimation [S1].
-   **Precision:** Joint optimization of poses and fields is sensitive to initialization. The coarse-to-fine scheme mitigates this but requires careful scheduling [S1].

## Subtopics

### SceneRF
SceneRF is a self-supervised monocular 3D scene reconstruction method trained using only image sequences with poses [S2]. It handles large scenes through explicit depth optimization and a novel probabilistic sampling strategy [S2]. It achieves the best performance in all metrics for new depth view synthesis, including absolute relative error, root mean square error, and IoU [S2].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** The two-stage learning paradigm (static then dynamic) and coarse-to-fine optimization reduce convergence time compared to fully implicit dynamic NeRFs [S1].
-   **Memory Complexity:** Maintaining two separate radiance fields (static and dynamic) increases memory usage compared to single-field methods.
-   **Strengths:** Robust recovery of dynamic geometry and appearance from unposed, in-the-wild video data; stable pose estimation via motion masking [S1].
-   **Limitations:** Requires a sufficient amount of static background for pose estimation; performance may degrade in fully dynamic scenes [S1].
-   **When to use:** When reconstructing dynamic scenes from monocular video without known camera poses.
-   **When not to use:** When the scene is entirely dynamic or when multi-view static calibration is available and preferred.

## Background
RoDynRF (Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields) was introduced in January 2023 [S1]. It addresses the challenge of dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular videos where camera poses are unknown. The method builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and joint optimization of camera parameters and scene representation [S1]. It is cited in comprehensive reviews of Neural Radiance Fields in 3D vision [S1].
