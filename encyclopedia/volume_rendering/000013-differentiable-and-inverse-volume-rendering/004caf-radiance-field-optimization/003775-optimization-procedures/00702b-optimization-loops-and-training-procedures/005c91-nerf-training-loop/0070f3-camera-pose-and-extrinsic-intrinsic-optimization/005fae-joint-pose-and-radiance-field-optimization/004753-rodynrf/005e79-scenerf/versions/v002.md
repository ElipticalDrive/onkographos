# SceneRF

Also called: Behind the Scenes

## Overview
SceneRF is a self-supervised monocular 3D scene reconstruction method trained using only image sequences with known poses [S2]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF approaches by reducing scene representation complexity through monocular depth estimation, enabling efficient handling of large scenes via explicit depth optimization and a novel probabilistic sampling strategy [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Inputs:** A sequence of monocular images with known camera poses [S2].
- **Outputs:** A radiance field representation optimized for new view synthesis and depth estimation [S2].

**Data Structures**
The method redefines feature extraction and ray/color sampling to account for the self-supervised depth network [S1]. Specific data structure layouts (e.g., voxel grid dimensions, feature vector sizes, or depth map resolutions) are not given in the sources.

**Algorithm Steps**
1. **Monocular Depth Estimation:** Generate depth priors using a self-supervised depth estimation network [S1].
2. **Feature Extraction Redefinition:** Redefine feature extraction to incorporate geometric information from the monocular depth estimator [S1].
3. **Ray and Color Sampling:** Redefine ray marching and color sampling strategies to explicitly account for the self-supervised depth network's output [S1].
4. **Explicit Depth Optimization:** Optimize the scene representation through explicit depth optimization to efficiently handle large scenes [S2].
5. **Probabilistic Sampling:** Apply a novel probabilistic sampling strategy to handle large scenes efficiently [S2].
6. **Self-Supervised Training:** Train the model using only image sequences and poses, relying on the scene’s geometric and lighting details as training cues [S2].

**Formulas and Parameters**
The specific mathematical formulations for the probabilistic sampling strategy, the loss functions combining photometric and depth consistency, and the architecture of the self-supervised depth network are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Large Scenes:** Standard NeRF methods may struggle with large scenes; SceneRF addresses this via explicit depth optimization and probabilistic sampling [S2].

## Operating characteristics
SceneRF achieves the best performance in all metrics for new depth view synthesis, including absolute relative error, root mean square error, and IoU, outperforming existing methods for both new view synthesis and scene reconstruction tasks [S2]. Specific time and memory complexity metrics, hardware performance data, or resolution limits are not given in the sources.

## Background
SceneRF was proposed in 2023 as a NeRF-based autonomous learning technique that employs the scene’s geometric and lighting details as training cues, bypassing direct labeling [S2]. It belongs to a lineage of methods, including MINE and Behind the Scenes, that reduce scene representation complexity by leveraging monocular depth estimation [S1].
