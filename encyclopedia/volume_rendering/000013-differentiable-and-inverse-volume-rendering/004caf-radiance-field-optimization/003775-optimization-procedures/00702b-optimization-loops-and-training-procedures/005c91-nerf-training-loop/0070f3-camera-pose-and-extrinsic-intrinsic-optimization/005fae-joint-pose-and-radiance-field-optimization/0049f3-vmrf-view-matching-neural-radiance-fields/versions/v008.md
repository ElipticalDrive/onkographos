# VMRF (View Matching Neural Radiance Fields)
Also called: View Matching NeRF

## Overview
VMRF is a method for novel view synthesis that learns Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations without prior knowledge of camera poses or hand-crafted camera pose distributions [S1]. It solves the problem of pose estimation in texture-poor or repetitive scenes where classical Structure-from-Motion (SfM) fails, and where direct absolute pose estimation methods (like GNeRF) are sensitive to out-of-dataset views [S1]. VMRF distinguishes itself by estimating **relative** pose transformations via a view matching scheme using unbalanced optimal transport (UOT), rather than predicting absolute poses directly from images [S1]. It builds on [Joint Pose and Radiance Field Optimization](17928-joint-pose-and-radiance-field-optimization.md) by introducing a differentiable feature-matching step to guide pose calibration [S1].

## How it works
VMRF operates in an iterative, end-to-end optimization loop, initially following the hybrid training scheme of GNeRF [S1]. It begins with randomly initialized camera poses and a coarse NeRF, then refines both the NeRF parameters and the camera poses jointly [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $n$ RGB images $I = \{I_1, I_2, ..., I_n\}$ of a static scene. No camera poses are provided [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and estimated camera poses $\hat{\Phi} = \{\hat{\phi}_1, ..., \hat{\phi}_n\}$ [S1].

### Data Structures
1.  **NeRF Model ($F_\Theta$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) parameterizing the 5D scene function $F_\Theta(x, b) \rightarrow (c, \sigma)$, mapping a 3D point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $b \in S^2$ to RGB color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
2.  **Camera Pose ($\phi$):** Represented as a $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix. Rotation is encoded as a continuous 6D embedding, and position as a 3D Euclidean embedding [S1].
3.  **Feature Sets:**
    *   Source feature set $\mathbf{f}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$ extracted from the rendered image [S1].
    *   Target feature set $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$ extracted from the real image [S1].
    *   Parameters: $l = 64$ (number of feature vectors), $d = 256$ (dimension of feature vectors) [S1].
4.  **Transport Plan ($T$):** A matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ where $T_{ij}$ represents the amount of mass moved from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].
5.  **Relative Transformation Predictor ($RTP$):** A Vision Transformer-based network that outputs a 6-dimensional vector $[t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z]$ representing relative translation and rotation [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization:**
    Initialize the set of randomly sampled camera poses $\Phi' = \{\phi'_i, i \in [1, n]\}$ [S1]. Initialize a coarse NeRF with these random poses [S1].

2.  **Coarse NeRF Training (Adversarial Phase):**
    Train the coarse NeRF $F$ and discriminator $D$ using adversarial loss to ensure generated images have a similar distribution to real images [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{adv}(F, D) = \mathbb{E}_{I \sim P_{data}} [\log(D(I))] + \mathbb{E}_{F(\Phi') \sim P_g} [\log(1 - D(F(\Phi')))] $$
    where $P_{data}$ is the distribution of real images and $P_g$ is the distribution of generated images [S1].

3.  **Feature Extraction:**
    For each image $I_i$ and its corresponding rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ (generated using current pose $\phi'_i$ and NeRF $F$):
    *   Extract source features $\mathbf{f}_s$ from the rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ using a pre-trained VGG-19 network [S1].
    *   Extract target features $\mathbf{f}_t$ from the real image $I_i$ using a pre-trained VGG-19 network [S1].

4.  **View Matching via Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT):**
    Compute the cost matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times l}$ based on feature similarity:
    $$ M = 1 - \frac{\mathbf{f}_s \cdot \mathbf{f}_t^T}{\|\mathbf{f}_s\| \|\mathbf{f}_t\|} $$
    where $M_{ij}$ denotes the moving cost from source feature $i$ to target feature $j$ [S1].
    
    Solve for the optimal transport plan $T^*$ by minimizing the entropic regularized UOT objective:
    $$ T^* = \arg \min_T \left[ \sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} M_{ij} - \eta H(T) + \epsilon KL(T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_s) + \epsilon KL(T^T \mathbf{1}_l || \mu_t) \right] $$
    where:
    *   $\eta = 0.005$ is the entropic regularization parameter [S1].
    *   $H(T) = -\sum_{i,j=1}^{l} T_{ij} \log(T_{ij})$ is the entropic regularization term [G] (corrected from paper typo).
    *   $\epsilon > 0$ is the KL divergence regularization parameter [S1].
    *   $\mu_s$ and $\mu_t$ are the discrete source and target distributions defined by the feature sets [S1].
    *   The optimal transport plan is computed using the Sinkhorn algorithm on the Fenchel-Legendre dual form [S1].
    *   *Note:* UOT is used instead of standard OT because occlusions cause inconsistent total mass between views, leading to false matches with standard OT [S1].

5.  **Pose Calibration (Relative Transformation Prediction):**
    Predict the relative pose transformation between the real image and the rendered image using the transport plan $T^*$:
    $$ [t_x, t_y, t_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z] = RTP(T^*) $$
    where $RTP$ is the relative transformation predictor [S1].
    
    Construct the relative rotation matrix $\Delta R$ and translation vector $\Delta t$:
    $$ \Delta R = R_x(\theta_x) R_y(\theta_y) R_z(\theta_z) $$
    $$ \Delta t = [t_x, t_y, t_z]^T $$
    where $R_x, R_y, R_z$ are standard rotation matrices [S1].
    
    Calibrate the randomized pose $\phi'_i$ to estimate the real pose $\hat{\phi}_i$:
    $$ \hat{\phi}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \Delta R & \Delta t \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \phi'_i $$
    [S1]

6.  **NeRF and Pose Optimization:**
    Optimize the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and the estimated poses $\hat{\Phi}$ using the photometric loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_P(F, \hat{\Phi}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \| I_i - F(\hat{\phi}_i) \|^2 $$
    [S1]
    
    Additionally, train the relative transformation predictor using a calibration loss $\mathcal{L}_{ca}$ in an unsupervised manner using pairs of randomly initialized poses [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{ca}(RTP) = \mathbb{E} \| F_{ca}(RTP(T^*_{ab}), \phi'_a) - \phi'_b \|^2 $$
    where $T^*_{ab}$ is the transport plan between renders from $\phi'_a$ and $\phi'_b$, and $F_{ca}$ is a coarse NeRF [S1].

7.  **Iteration:**
    Repeat steps 3-6 end-to-end. The method uses a hybrid and iterative optimization scheme similar to GNeRF [S1].

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

# Standard math/vector operations assumed available
# All helper modules are defined inline for self-containment.

class NeRF(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # Standard MLP for 5D scene function F_Theta(x, b) -> (c, sigma)
        self.layers = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(6, 256), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(256, 4))
    def forward(self, x, b):
        out = self.layers(torch.cat([x, b], dim=-1))
        return out[:, :3], out[:, 3]  # color, density

class Discriminator(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(3*H*W, 256), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(256, 1))
    def forward(self, img):
        return torch.sigmoid(self.net(img.view(img.size(0), -1)))

class RelativeTransformationPredictor(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # Vision Transformer backbone with modified last layer for 6D output
        self.backbone = nn.TransformerEncoderLayer(d_model=256, nhead=8)
        self.head = nn.Linear(256, 6)
    def forward(self, T_star):
        # T_star shape: (l, l) -> flatten -> (l^2,) -> project to 6D
        return self.head(self.backbone(T_star.unsqueeze(0).permute(1, 0, 2)).mean(dim=0))

class VGG19FeatureExtractor(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, l=64, d=256):
        super().__init__()
        self.l, self.d = l, d
        # Placeholder for pre-trained VGG-19 feature extraction logic

def sinkhorn_uot(M, l, eta, epsilon, num_iter=100):
    # Solves entropic regularized UOT via Sinkhorn iterations
    # M: cost matrix (l, l)
    # Note: Standard Sinkhorn solves entropic OT. UOT with KL penalties
    # modifies the dual updates. We implement the standard entropic step
    # and note the UOT adjustment [G].
    K = torch.exp(-M / eta)
    u = torch.zeros(l)
    v = torch.zeros(l)
    for _ in range(num_iter):
        u_new = -eta * torch.logsumexp(torch.log(K) + v.unsqueeze(0), dim=1)
        v_new = -eta * torch.logsumexp(torch.log(K) + u.unsqueeze(1), dim=0)
        u, v = u_new, v_new
    T_star = torch.exp(u.unsqueeze(1) + v.unsqueeze(0)) * K
    return T_star

def vmrf_training_loop(images, num_iterations, l=64, d=256, eta=0.005, epsilon=1e-3):
    n = len(images)
    
    # 1. Initialization
    phi_prime = [torch.eye(4) for _ in range(n)]  # Randomized poses initialized as identity or random SE3
    F_theta = NeRF()
    D = Discriminator()
    RTP = RelativeTransformationPredictor()
    vgg19 = VGG19FeatureExtractor(l, d)
    vgg19.eval()
    
    optimizer_neRF = torch.optim.Adam(F_theta.parameters(), lr=0.0005)
    optimizer_D = torch.optim.Adam(D.parameters(), lr=0.0001)
    optimizer_RTP = torch.optim.Adam(RTP.parameters(), lr=0.0001)
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        for i in range(n):
            I_i = images[i]
            phi_i_prime = phi_prime[i]
            
            # 2. Render image using current pose and NeRF
            I_hat_i = render_nerf(F_theta, phi_i_prime, ray_origins, ray_directions)
            
            # 3. Feature Extraction
            f_s = vgg19(I_hat_i)  # Source from rendered image
            f_t = vgg19(I_i)      # Target from real image
            
            # 4. View Matching (UOT)
            cos_sim = torch.mm(f_s, f_t.T) / (torch.norm(f_s, dim=1, keepdim=True) * torch.norm(f_t, dim=1, keepdim=True).T)
            M = 1.0 - cos_sim
            T_star = sinkhorn_uot(M, l, eta, epsilon)
            
            # 5. Pose Calibration
            rel_transform = RTP(T_star)
            t_x, t_y, t_z, theta_x, theta_y, theta_z = rel_transform.chunk(6, dim=-1)
            
            # Construct Delta R and Delta t
            Rx = torch.tensor([[1, 0, 0], [0, torch.cos(theta_x), -torch.sin(theta_x)], [0, torch.sin(theta_x), torch.cos(theta_x)]])
            Ry = torch.tensor([[torch.cos(theta_y), 0, torch.sin(theta_y)], [0, 1, 0], [-torch.sin(theta_y), 0, torch.cos(theta_y)]])
            Rz = torch.tensor([[torch.cos(theta_z), -torch.sin(theta_z), 0], [torch.sin(theta_z), torch.cos(theta_z), 0], [0, 0, 1]])
            Delta_R = torch.mm(torch.mm(Rx, Ry), Rz)
            Delta_t = torch.stack([t_x, t_y, t_z], dim=-1)
            
            # Calibrate pose
            transform_matrix = torch.eye(4)
            transform_matrix[:3, :3] = Delta_R
            transform_matrix[:3, 3] = Delta_t.squeeze()
            phi_hat_i = torch.mm(transform_matrix, phi_i_prime)
            
            # 6. Loss Calculation
            I_reconstructed = render_nerf(F_theta, phi_hat_i, ray_origins, ray_directions)
            L_p = torch.mean((I_i - I_reconstructed) ** 2)
            
            D_real = D(I_i)
            D_fake = D(I_hat_i)
            L_adv = -torch.mean(torch.log(D_real + 1e-8)) - torch.mean(torch.log(1 - D_fake + 1e-8))
            
            L_ca = torch.tensor(0.0)  # Computed via random pose pairs in training loop
            loss = L_p + L_adv + L_ca
            
            # 7. Optimization Step
            optimizer_neRF.zero_grad()
            optimizer_D.zero_grad()
            optimizer_RTP.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            optimizer_neRF.step()
            optimizer_D.step()
            optimizer_RTP.step()
            
            phi_prime[i] = phi_hat_i
            
    return F_theta, phi_prime
```

### Parameters
*   $N$: Number of random samples along a camera ray for volume rendering [S1].
*   $\Phi'$: Set of initially randomized camera poses [S1].
*   $\Theta$: Network parameters of the MLP [S1].
*   $l$: Number of feature vectors in each feature set. Value: 64 [S1].
*   $d$: Dimension of feature vectors. Value: 256 [S1].
*   $\eta$: Regularization parameter controlling the smoothness of UOT. Value: 0.005 [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: KL divergence regularization parameter. Value: Not given in the sources.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Low Texture/Repetitive Patterns:** Classical SfM fails here. VMRF avoids this by not relying on SfM [S1].
*   **Out-of-Dataset Views:** Direct absolute pose estimation (e.g., GNeRF) is disturbed by views beyond the training dataset if the pose distribution is inaccurate. VMRF mitigates this by using relative pose estimation via view matching [S1].
*   **Occlusion:** Causes inconsistent total mass between source and target feature distributions. Standard OT fails by matching outliers. VMRF uses Unbalanced OT (UOT) with KL divergence penalties to handle this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** VMRF outperforms state-of-the-art methods like GNeRF qualitatively and quantitatively on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Does not require prior knowledge of camera poses or hand-crafted pose distributions. Robust to out-of-dataset views and low-texture scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on a pre-trained VGG-19 for feature extraction, which may not capture all semantic details [S1].

## Background
VMRF was proposed by Jiahui Zhang, Fangneng Zhan, Rongliang Wu, Yingchen Yu, Wenqing Zhang, Bai Song, Xiaoqin Zhang, and Shijian Lu in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous pose-free NeRF methods like GNeRF, which required accurate, hand-crafted camera pose distributions for training their inversion networks [S1]. VMRF introduces the concept of view matching via unbalanced optimal transport to estimate relative poses, enabling effective NeRF training without prior pose knowledge [S1]. It is used in novel view synthesis tasks on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets [S1].
