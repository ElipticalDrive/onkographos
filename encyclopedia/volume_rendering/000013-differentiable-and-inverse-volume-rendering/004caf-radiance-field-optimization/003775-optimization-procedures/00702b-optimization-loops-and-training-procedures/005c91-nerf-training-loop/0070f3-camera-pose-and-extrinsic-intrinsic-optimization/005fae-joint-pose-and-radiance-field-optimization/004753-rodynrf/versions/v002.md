# RoDynRF

Also called: Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields

## Overview
RoDynRF enables dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular videos without known camera poses by jointly optimizing intrinsics, extrinsics, and both static and dynamic radiance fields [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like VMRF and NeRF Pose and Appearance Optimization by explicitly separating static and dynamic components via a two-branch architecture and using motion masks to isolate static geometry for robust pose estimation, thereby handling unposed, in-the-wild video data [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sequence of monocular images (video) with unknown camera poses and intrinsics.
*   **Output:** Optimized camera poses, intrinsics, a static radiance field, and a dynamic radiance field with non-rigid motion.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Two-Branch Architecture:** The scene representation is split into a static branch and a dynamic branch [S1].
*   **Static Radiance Field:** Represents the background or stationary objects.
*   **Dynamic Radiance Field:** Represents moving objects, coupled with a deformation network.
*   **Motion Masks:** Binary masks identifying dynamic pixels in each frame, used to exclude them from pose optimization [S1].
*   **Time-Conditioned Deformation Network:** A network that takes spatial coordinates and time as input to output displacement vectors for non-rigid motion [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the static radiance field, dynamic radiance field, camera poses, and intrinsics. [G] Typically, initial poses may be estimated via Structure-from-Motion (SfM) or random initialization, though the method assumes no prior known poses.
2.  **Static-to-Dynamic Learning Paradigm:**
    *   First, learn the static scene representation from multi-view stills or the static portions of the video [S1].
    *   Then, learn the deformation from the dynamic sequence [S1]. This staged approach allows for much faster convergence than fully implicit networks [S1].
3.  **Coarse-to-Fine Voxel Optimization:** Employ a coarse-to-fine voxel optimization scheme to ensure stability during the joint optimization of geometry and appearance [S1].
4.  **Pose Estimation with Motion Masks:**
    *   Generate motion masks to identify dynamic pixels in the current frame [S1].
    *   Exclude dynamic pixels from the pose estimation loss calculation, relying only on static pixels to update camera extrinsics and intrinsics [S1]. This prevents moving objects from corrupting the camera trajectory.
5.  **Non-Rigid Motion Modeling:**
    *   Use the time-conditioned deformation network to model non-rigid motion for dynamic points [S1].
    *   The network takes a 3D point and time $t$ as input and outputs a deformation offset.
6.  **Volume Rendering and Loss:**
    *   Render images from the combined static and dynamic fields.
    *   Compute photometric loss between rendered and input images.
    *   Backpropagate to update poses, intrinsics, radiance fields, and deformation network parameters.

**Formulas:**
*   Let $x$ be a 3D point, $t$ be time, and $d(x, t)$ be the deformation offset from the time-conditioned deformation network.
*   The deformed point is $x' = x + d(x, t)$.
*   The density $\sigma$ and color $c$ are queried from the dynamic field at $(x', t)$ and the static field at $(x, t)$ (or similar combination depending on the mask).
*   The pose optimization loss $L_{pose}$ is computed only on pixels where the motion mask $M=0$ (static):
    $$ L_{pose} = \sum_{i \in \text{static}} \| I_{rendered}(i) - I_{input}(i) \|^2 $$
*   The total loss includes photometric loss on all pixels and potentially regularization terms for the deformation field. [G] Specific regularization weights are not given in the sources.

**Parameters:**
*   Coarse-to-fine voxel grid resolutions: Not given in the sources.
*   Learning rates for poses, fields, and deformation network: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Dynamic Pixels in Pose Estimation:** If motion masks are inaccurate, dynamic pixels may be included in pose optimization, leading to incorrect camera trajectories [S1].
*   **Convergence:** Fully implicit networks may converge slowly; the static-to-dynamic paradigm is used to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Precision:** Coarse-to-fine optimization helps with stability but requires careful scheduling of grid resolutions [S1].

## Subtopics

### SceneRF
SceneRF is a self-supervised monocular 3D scene reconstruction method trained using only image sequences with poses [S2]. It handles large scenes through explicit depth optimization and a novel probabilistic sampling strategy [S2]. It achieves the best performance in all metrics for new depth view synthesis, including absolute relative error, root mean square error, and IoU [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources for RoDynRF specifically. SceneRF (a sibling variant) achieves best performance in depth view synthesis metrics [S2].
*   **Scaling:** The coarse-to-fine voxel optimization scheme aids stability, suggesting scalability to higher resolutions with increased computational cost [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust recovery of dynamic geometry and appearance from unposed, in-the-wild video data; faster convergence due to static-to-dynamic learning paradigm [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate motion masks for pose estimation; requires joint optimization of many parameters (poses, intrinsics, fields, deformation) which can be complex [S1].
*   **When to use:** When reconstructing dynamic scenes from monocular video without known camera poses.
*   **When not to use:** When camera poses are known, or when the scene is static (simpler methods like standard NeRF are more efficient).

## Background
RoDynRF (Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields) was introduced in January 2023 [S1]. It addresses the challenge of dynamic scene reconstruction from monocular videos without known camera poses. It builds on the concept of differentiable volume rendering and extends it to handle non-rigid motion and unknown camera parameters simultaneously. The method is part of the broader family of radiance-field optimization techniques for dynamic scenes, alongside HexPlane and K-Planes, which use hybrid 4D representations [S1].
