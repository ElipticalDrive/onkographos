# Unified Metric Aggregation

Also called: Evaluation & Metric Computation, Evaluation Reporting, Global and Per-view Metric Aggregation

## Overview
Unified Metric Aggregation computes standard photorealistic and perceptual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for rendered images against ground truth references, then aggregates these scores into global averages and per-view dictionaries for evaluation reporting [S1, S2, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of quantifying novel-view synthesis fidelity by combining pixel-level precision, structural integrity, and perceptual similarity [S1, unofficial][S3]. What sets it apart from raw error metrics is the inclusion of perceptual models (LPIPS) and structural models (SSIM) that correlate better with human judgment, while providing a standardized reporting structure for both individual views and the entire dataset [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Paired lists of rendered and ground truth (GT) images, typically in tensor format (Channel, Height, Width) normalized to $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. Configuration flags for extra metrics (SSIM, LPIPS) and evaluation intervals [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Global metric averages (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and per-view metric dictionaries, often saved as JSON or text files [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Per-view metrics:** A dictionary mapping image names to metric scores.
    *   Layout: `{"image_name": {"PSNR": float, "SSIM": float, "LPIPS": float}}` [S1, unofficial].
*   **Global metrics:** A dictionary containing arithmetic means of all per-view scores.
    *   Layout: `{"ours_{iteration}": {"PSNR": float, "SSIM": float, "LPIPS": float}}` [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize:** Create empty lists for per-view metric scores (e.g., `PSNRs`, `SSIMs`, `LPIPSs`) and a dictionary for per-view results [S1, S2, unofficial].
2.  **Determine Evaluation Set:** Calculate the evaluation interval to select a subset of rays/views if necessary: `img_eval_interval = 1 if N_vis < 0 else max(test_dataset.all_rays.shape[0] // N_vis, 1)` [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Iterate:** Loop over the selected rays or image pairs [S2, unofficial].
    a.  **Load and Normalize:** Load RGB images, normalize to $[0, 1]$, and convert to CHW tensor format [S1, unofficial].
    b.  **Validate Shape:** Check that rendered and GT images have matching shapes (H, W, C) [S1, unofficial].
    c.  **Render (if ray-based):** Reshape rays to HxW, render with a chunked renderer, and clamp RGB to $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
    d.  **Compute MSE:** Calculate Mean Squared Error: $\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (R_i - G_i)^2$, where $R$ is rendered and $G$ is ground truth [S2, unofficial].
    e.  **Compute PSNR:** Calculate PSNR using the formula:
        $$ \text{PSNR} = -10 \cdot \frac{\log(\text{MSE})}{\log(10)} $$
        [S2, unofficial].
    f.  **Compute Extra Metrics:** If `compute_extra_metrics` is true, compute SSIM and LPIPS (using AlexNet or VGG backbone) [S2, unofficial].
    g.  **Validate Finiteness:** Ensure computed metrics are finite (not NaN or Inf). Handle edge cases such as zero MSE (which yields infinite PSNR) by capping or excluding [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
    h.  **Store Per-View:** Append scores to per-view lists and store in the per-view dictionary [S1, S2, unofficial].
    i.  **Save Images:** Optionally save RGB and depth images for visual inspection [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Aggregate:** After the loop, compute the arithmetic mean of each metric list to obtain global averages [S1, S2, unofficial].
5.  **Report:** Write global metrics and per-view JSON to disk [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `compute_extra_metrics`: Boolean flag to enable SSIM and LPIPS computation [S2, unofficial].
*   `N_vis`: Number of views to visualize/evaluate. If $< 0$, evaluate all [S2, unofficial].
*   `img_eval_interval`: Step size for iterating through test rays [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Zero MSE:** Results in infinite PSNR. Must be handled by capping or replacing with a maximum finite value [S1, unofficial].
*   **Shape Mismatch:** Rendered and GT images must have identical dimensions; otherwise, computation fails [S1, unofficial].
*   **NaN/Inf:** Numerical instability in SSIM or LPIPS computation can produce non-finite values. Validation step is required [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [LPIPS Evaluation Protocol](4376-lpips-evaluation-protocol.md)
LPIPS is a full-reference perceptual quality metric that measures image similarity by comparing deep feature representations extracted from a pretrained convolutional neural network. Unlike pixel-wise metrics such as PSNR or MSE, which often correlate poorly with human perception, LPIPS aligns closely with human judgments of image similarity by leveraging semantic features. It is primarily used in volume rendering and NeRF optimization to evaluate reconstruction fidelity or as a perceptual loss function to guide training, capturing nuances like texture and structure that standard metrics miss.

### [Metric Computation (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS)](9928-metric-computation-psnr-ssim-lpips.md)
This method computes standard photorealistic metrics—Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS)—to quantify the fidelity of rendered images against ground truth references. It addresses the problem of evaluating novel-view synthesis quality by balancing pixel-level precision (PSNR), structural integrity (SSIM), and perceptual similarity (LPIPS). What sets this apart from raw error metrics is the inclusion of perceptual models (LPIPS) and structural models (SSIM) that correlate better with human judgment than simple pixel differences, while remaining computationally efficient enough for per-view evaluation during training loops.

### Evaluation Reporting
This variant extends standard metric computation to include system-level and domain-specific reporting. It evaluates approaches by comparing them to previous methods in specific domains like anatomy visualization and thermal scene representation [S3]. It reports PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, but also includes the size of the resulting splats in MB and rendering speeds on various platforms (standalone PC, PC-VR, mobile VR) [S3].

### Quantitative Evaluation Metrics
This variant includes temporal metrics for dynamic scene evaluation. It reports PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, along with temporal Optical Flow (tOF) and temporal Perceptual Loss (tLP) [S4]. tOF examines errors in motion by comparing optical flow to ground truth, while tLP measures perceptual distance as the change in LPIPS between consecutive frames [S4]. Lower values are better for LPIPS, tOF, and tLP, while higher is better for PSNR and SSIM [S4].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of evaluated views and pixels. SSIM and LPIPS add significant overhead due to sliding windows and deep network inference, respectively [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of views for storing per-view results. LPIPS requires loading a pretrained CNN model into memory [S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On KITTI reconstruction, NSG achieved PSNR 26.66, SSIM 0.806, LPIPS 0.186, tOF 0.765×10^6, tLP 0.246×100 [S4]. On KITTI novel composition, NSG achieved PSNR 25.11, SSIM 0.789, LPIPS 0.204 [S4].
*   **Strengths:** Provides a comprehensive view of quality, combining pixel accuracy, structural fidelity, and perceptual similarity. Standardized reporting facilitates comparison across methods [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** LPIPS is computationally expensive and requires a pretrained model. PSNR and SSIM may not correlate well with human perception for complex textures [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Standard evaluation of novel-view synthesis quality, especially when perceptual fidelity is important [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Real-time training loops where computational overhead is prohibitive; use PSNR only for speed [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Unified Metric Aggregation originated from the need to standardize evaluation in the NeRF and radiance field community. It builds on the principles of standard metric computation [13741-standard-metric-computation.md]. The specific aggregation of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS into global and per-view reports was popularized by implementations in repositories like TensoRF [S2, unofficial] and Advanced3DGS [S1, unofficial]. The inclusion of temporal metrics (tOF, tLP) was introduced for dynamic scene evaluation in Neural Scene Graphs [S4]. The method is widely used in academic papers and open-source projects for reporting reconstruction quality [S3, S4].
