# NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing)

Also called: Alpha Compositing in NeRF, NeRF Integration, NeRF Rendering Equation, NeRF Rendering Process, Neural Radiance Field Volume Rendering

## Overview
NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing) is the differentiable forward pass that synthesizes a pixel's color and depth by integrating the volume density and emitted radiance predicted by a neural network along a camera ray [S1, S2, S3]. It solves the problem of mapping a continuous 5D implicit function (mapping spatial coordinates and view directions to density and color) to a 2D image by discretizing the ray integral [S12, S13]. Unlike deterministic ray tracing which follows specific light paths, NeRF leverages a probabilistic density function to compute the expected color, handling view-dependent effects (non-Lambertian materials) that standard photogrammetry often assumes as diffuse [S13]. It sets itself apart from its sibling [NeRF-style Volume Rendering Integrator](5964-nerf-style-volume-rendering-integrator.md) by explicitly defining the standard alpha-compositing formulation with stratified sampling and background handling as the baseline for training and inference [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Camera ray $r(t) = o + td$, where $o$ is the camera origin and $d$ is the unit viewing direction [S1, S13].
    *   Near bound $t_n$ (camera center) and far bound $t_f$ (scene boundary) [S2, S12].
    *   Number of samples $N$ (typical value: 64 for both coarse and fine networks) [S11].
    *   Neural network model $F_\Theta$ (MLP) taking positional encoding of $(x, d)$ and outputting density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1, S12].
    *   Optional background color $c_{bg}$ [S7, S8, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Rendered RGB color $\hat{C}(r)$ [S1][S7, unofficial].
    *   Expected depth $\hat{D}(r)$ [S2][S7, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sample Points:** Array of $N$ distances $t_i$ along the ray [S2, S12].
*   **Predictions:** Tensor of shape $(N, 4)$ containing $N$ samples, each with 3 RGB channels and 1 density channel.
*   **Deltas:** Array of interval lengths $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ for $i=1 \dots N-1$, plus a large value (e.g., $10^{10}$) for the final segment to infinity [S7, unofficial][S9].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel in the target image, generate a ray $r(t) = o + td$ using the camera's intrinsic and extrinsic parameters [S1, S4, S13].
2.  **Sampling:** Divide the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $N$ equally spaced bins. Draw one sample $t_i$ uniformly at random from each bin (non-deterministic stratified sampling) [S2, S12, S13]:
    $$ t_i \sim U\left[t_n + \frac{i-1}{N}(t_f - t_n), t_n + \frac{i}{N}(t_f - t_n)\right] $$
    [S12, S13].
3.  **Querying:** For each sample point $t_i$, compute the 3D position $x_i = o + t_i d$ [S1, S12]. Input $x_i$ and the ray direction $d$ (often with positional encoding $\gamma$) into the MLP $F_\Theta$ to predict volume density $\sigma_i$ and color $c_i$ [S1, S2, S12, S13]:
    $$ [\sigma_i, c_i] = F_\Theta(\gamma(x_i), \gamma(d)) $$
    [S1, S17]. Apply ReLU to $\sigma_i$ and Sigmoid to $c_i$ to constrain ranges [S9].
4.  **Delta Calculation:** Compute the distance between adjacent samples:
    $$ \delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i \quad \text{for } i=1 \dots N-1 $$
    [S7, unofficial][S9]. Append a large value $\delta_\infty = 10^{10}$ (or $t_f - t_N$ if bounded) for the last segment to handle rays that do not terminate within the scene [S7, unofficial][S9].
5.  **Opacity Calculation:** Compute the opacity $\alpha_i$ for each sample using the Beer-Lambert law:
    $$ \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i) $$
    [S2, S9].
6.  **Transmittance Calculation:** Compute the accumulated transmittance $T_i$, representing the probability that the ray travels from $t_n$ to $t_i$ without being intercepted [S2, S16]. Use a cumulative product with a small epsilon $\epsilon$ (typically $10^{-10}$) added to $(1 - \alpha_j)$ for numerical stability to prevent NaNs during gradient computation [S2, S9]:
    $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j + \epsilon) $$
    [S2, S9]. Note: $T_1 = 1$ [S9].
7.  **Weight Calculation:** Compute the weight $w_i$ for each sample, representing the probability that the ray terminates at sample $i$:
    $$ w_i = \alpha_i T_i $$
    [S2, S9].
8.  **Color Integration:** Compute the final rendered color $\hat{C}(r)$ as the weighted sum of colors:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i c_i $$
    [S2][S7, unofficial][S9].
9.  **Background Handling (Optional):** If a background color $c_{bg}$ is assumed, add the contribution of rays that pass through the scene without terminating [S7, S8, unofficial]. Let $W = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i$ be the total accumulated opacity [S7, S8, unofficial]. The final color is:
    $$ \hat{C}_{final}(r) = \hat{C}(r) + (1 - W) c_{bg} $$
    [S7, S8, unofficial].
10. **Depth Estimation:** Compute the expected depth $\hat{D}(r)$ analogously to color, using the sample distances $t_i$ as the value:
    $$ \hat{D}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i t_i $$
    [S2][S7, unofficial][S9].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def nerf_rendering(rays_o, rays_d, t_vals, model, white_background=True, c_bg=np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0]), eps=1e-10):
    """
    Render RGB and Depth for a batch of rays using NeRF alpha compositing.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: (B, 3) Camera origins.
        rays_d: (B, 3) Ray directions (unit vectors).
        t_vals: (B, N) Sample distances along rays.
        model: Function that takes (points, directions) and returns (sigma, rgb).
        white_background: Boolean, whether to add background color.
        c_bg: (3,) Background color.
        eps: Float, epsilon for numerical stability.
        
    Returns:
        rgb: (B, 3) Rendered colors.
        depth: (B,) Expected depths.
    """
    B, N = t_vals.shape
    # 1. Compute 3D points
    # points shape: (B, N, 3)
    points = rays_o[:, np.newaxis, :] + rays_d[:, np.newaxis, :] * t_vals[:, :, np.newaxis]
    
    # 2. Query model
    # model returns sigma (B, N) and rgb (B, N, 3)
    # Note: In practice, positional encoding is applied inside or before model call.
    # Here we assume model handles input or we pass raw coords.
    # For strict adherence to NeRF [S1], positional encoding is applied to x and d.
    # We assume 'model' expects encoded inputs or handles them. 
    # To be self-contained, we denote the query as:
    sigma, rgb = model(points, rays_d)
    
    # 3. Apply activation functions if not done in model
    # sigma = np.maximum(sigma, 0) # ReLU
    # rgb = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-rgb)) # Sigmoid (if raw logits)
    # Assuming model outputs valid sigma >= 0 and rgb in [0,1] or logits.
    # Standard implementation [S9] applies sigmoid to rgb and relu to sigma.
    sigma = np.maximum(sigma, 0)
    rgb = 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-rgb))
    
    # 4. Compute deltas
    # deltas shape: (B, N-1)
    deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
    
    # Append infinity for the last segment
    # delta_inf shape: (B, 1)
    delta_inf = np.ones((B, 1)) * 1e10
    
    # Concatenate to get (B, N)
    deltas = np.concatenate([deltas, delta_inf], axis=1)
    
    # 5. Compute opacity alpha
    # alpha shape: (B, N)
    alpha = 1.0 - np.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    
    # 6. Compute transmittance T
    # T_i = product_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - alpha_j)
    # Use cumulative product with exclusive=True
    # (1 - alpha + eps) shape: (B, N)
    term = 1.0 - alpha + eps
    # cumprod along axis=1 (samples)
    # T shape: (B, N), T[:,0] = 1.0
    T = np.cumprod(term, axis=1, exclusive=True)
    
    # 7. Compute weights
    # w_i = alpha_i * T_i
    # weights shape: (B, N)
    weights = alpha * T
    
    # 8. Compute RGB
    # rgb_final = sum(weights * rgb)
    # weights needs to be broadcasted to (B, N, 1)
    rgb_final = np.sum(weights[:, :, np.newaxis] * rgb, axis=1)
    
    # 9. Background handling
    if white_background:
        # Total opacity W = sum(weights)
        W = np.sum(weights, axis=1) # (B,)
        # Add (1 - W) * c_bg
        rgb_final = rgb_final + (1.0 - W[:, np.newaxis]) * c_bg[np.newaxis, :]
        
    # 10. Compute Depth
    # depth = sum(weights * t_vals)
    depth = np.sum(weights * t_vals, axis=1)
    
    return rgb_final, depth
```

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of samples per ray. Typical value: 64 [S11].
*   $\epsilon$: Epsilon for stability. Typical value: $10^{-10}$ [S2, S9].
*   $c_{bg}$: Background color [S7, S8, unofficial].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Integration bounds. $t_n$ is camera center, $t_f$ is scene boundary [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Stability:** Without $\epsilon$, the cumulative product of $(1-\alpha)$ can underflow to 0, causing NaNs in gradients. $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ is standard [S2, S9].
*   **Infinity Handling:** The last segment $\delta_N$ must be large enough to capture rays that exit the scene [S7, unofficial][S9]. Using $10^{10}$ ensures $\alpha_N \approx 1$ if $\sigma_N > 0$, effectively terminating the ray.
*   **View-Dependent Effects:** NeRF handles non-Lambertian materials by conditioning color on view direction $d$ [S13].
*   **Computational Cost:** Original NeRF requires high computing power and long training times due to dense sampling and MLP queries [S13].

### NeRF-XL Multi-GPU Volume Rendering
A method for scaling NeRF rendering across multiple GPUs [S18]. It performs global composition by aggregating transmittance and color contributions from segments processed on different GPUs [S18].
*   **Step:** Initialize global transmittance $T_{1 \to i} = 1$ and global color $C_{1 \to N+1} = 0$. For each segment $s$ in the GPU order list $I$, if the segment's GPU $i \neq k$ (current GPU), gather $T_{i \to i+1}$ and $C_{i \to i+1}$ [S18]. Update global color: $C_{1 \to N+1} += T_{1 \to i} \times C_{i \to i+1}$ [S18]. Update global transmittance: $T_{1 \to i^*} *= T_{i \to i+1}$ [S18].
*   **Pitfall:** During inference, data gathering is only required on a single GPU (e.g., GPU 0) to render the final image, reducing data transfer compared to training [S18].

### NeRF Volume Rendering via Numerical Quadrature
Approximates the volume rendering integral by summing contributions from sampled spheres inside the cone using numerical quadrature [S19].
*   **Formula:** $\text{Color} = \sum_i T_i (1 - \exp(-\text{density}_i (t_{i+1} - t_i))) \text{color}_i$, with $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{k<i} \text{density}_k (t_{k+1} - t_k))$ [S19].

### Raymarcher
A rendering procedure using RM-LSTM to predict a single intersection point and output RGB directly, skipping volume rendering [S20].
*   **Step:** Extract latent features from PixelNeRF CNN encoder, feed into RM-LSTM. RM-LSTM predicts step length and updates depth for 10 steps with 512 feature channels [S20]. Pass final depth point through PixelNeRF network to obtain RGB [S20].
*   **Comparison:** ~80 times faster than baseline but slightly lower quality (PSNR 23.02 vs 23.34) [S20].

### 2D Neural Rendering Module
Maps low-resolution feature maps to final images using upsampling and 2D convolution to alleviate multi-view inconsistent artifacts [S21].
*   **Formula:** $\text{Upsample}(X) = \text{Conv2D}(Y, K)$, where $Y = \text{Pixelshuffle}(\text{repeat}(X, 4) + \beta_\zeta(X), 2)$ [S21].
*   **Parameter:** Convolution kernel size is 1x1 to avoid multi-view inconsistencies [S21].

### HeadNeRF Inference
Real-time NeRF-based parametric head model inference [S21].
*   **Step:** Sample 64 points per ray, compute volume rendering to get feature map IF, pass IF through 2D neural rendering module (upsampling + 1x1 Conv) to get final image I [S21].
*   **Performance:** >40 fps on NVIDIA 3090 GPU [S21].

### Depth Estimation from NeRF
Extracts 3D point-cloud data by estimating depth where transmittance changes significantly [S22, unofficial].
*   **Formula:** $t_{surface} \approx \arg \min_t \left| \frac{dT(t)}{dt} \right|$ [S22, unofficial].

### PixelNeRF Rendering Module
Produces approximate rendering (RGB and features) at a target pose using input images [S23].
*   **Step:** Pass N input images through 2D U-Net to create feature images. Cast rays, sample 128 points (uniform up to distance 1, then linear in disparity) [S23]. Reproject 3D points into input cameras, gather features [S23]. Append positionally-encoded 3D locations and statistics [S23].
*   **Layout:** Output tensor 64x64x131 (3 RGB + 128 features) [S23].

### NeRF Rendering with Stratified Sampling
Standard NeRF rendering using coarse and fine sampling [S24].
*   **Step:** Discretize integral using stratified sampling. Coarse sampling uses uniform distribution [S24]. Fine sampling uses volume density weights [S24].
*   **Loss:** Sum of squared errors for coarse and fine samples against ground truth [S24].

### Motion Blur Simulation via Pose Averaging
Models image motion blur by discretizing exposure time into $p$ poses, rendering sharp images, and averaging them [S11].
*   **Parameter:** $p$ (number of poses), typical value 5 [S11].
*   **Pitfall:** Assumes uniform motion or linear interpolation for intermediate poses [S11].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Measured Performance:** Original NeRF is slow; Instant NGP reduces processing time significantly using multi-resolution hash encoding [S13]. HeadNeRF achieves >40 fps on NVIDIA 3090 with 2D neural rendering module [S21].
*   **Strengths:** Handles view-dependent effects, high-quality novel view synthesis, fine details [S13].
*   **Limitations:** High computational cost, neither a point cloud nor a mesh (continuous voxels) [S13].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality novel view synthesis is required, especially for complex scenes with view-dependent effects [S13].
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time performance is critical without acceleration techniques, or when explicit geometry (mesh/point cloud) is required [S13].

## Background
NeRF was first presented by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S13]. It represents a static scene as a continuous 5D function outputting volume density and view-dependent emitted radiance [S13]. The method was initially presented at CVPR 2020 [S13]. Subsequent developments include Instant NGP by Müller et al. (2022) which introduced multi-resolution hash encoding [S13]. It is used in Cultural Heritage for reconstruction of metallic, translucent, and transparent surfaces [S13]. Implemented in platforms like Instant NGP, Nerfstudio, and Luma AI [S13].
