# MipNerfModel

Also called: Mip-NeRF 360 Model

## Overview
The MipNerfModel is a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) architecture that approximates integrated positional encoding by treating rays as cones or cylinders rather than infinitesimally thin lines, thereby solving aliasing artifacts caused by high-frequency scene details [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF implementations by using multi-level sampling (coarse and fine) and integrated positional encoding to model the frequency content of the signal along the ray, which is essential for training on unbounded, outdoor scenes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The model implements a differentiable rendering pipeline that processes rays defined by origins, directions, radii, and viewing parameters [S1, unofficial]. It utilizes a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict density and color, conditioned on integrated positional encodings of the sampled points [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
* `rays`: A structure containing:
  * `origins`: 3D coordinates of ray origins [S1, unofficial].
  * `directions`: Normalized ray direction vectors [S1, unofficial].
  * `viewdirs`: View direction vectors, explicitly passed for conditioning [S1, unofficial].
  * `radii`: The radius of the ray at the sample points (used for integrated encoding) [S1, unofficial].
  * `near`: Near plane distance for each ray [S1, unofficial].
  * `far`: Far plane distance for each ray [S1, unofficial].
  * `temperature`: Camera temperature parameter for ISP [S1, unofficial].
  * `exposure`: Camera exposure parameter for ISP [S1, unofficial].
  * `coc`: Circle of Confusion parameter (used to scale ray radii during training) [S1, unofficial].
* `rng`: Random number generator state [S1, unofficial].
* `white_bkgd`: Boolean indicating if the background is white (affects alpha compositing) [S1, unofficial].
* `train_coc`: Circle of Confusion multiplier for ray radii during training [S1, unofficial].
* `randomized`: Boolean indicating whether to use randomized sampling [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
* `comp_rgb`: Computed RGB color per ray [S1, unofficial].
* `distance`: Expected distance along the ray [S1, unofficial].
* `acc`: Accumulated alpha (opacity) along the ray [S1, unofficial].
* `weights`: Per-sample weights used for resampling in subsequent levels [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The `rays` object holds batched arrays:
* `origins`: Shape `(B, 3)` [G].
* `directions`: Shape `(B, 3)` [G].
* `viewdirs`: Shape `(B, 3)` [G].
* `radii`: Shape `(B,)` initially, representing the radius at the pixel, scaled by `coc` [S1, unofficial].
* `near`: Shape `(B,)` [G].
* `far`: Shape `(B,)` [G].

### Parameters
* `num_samples`: int, number of samples per level. Default: 128 [S1, unofficial].
* `num_levels`: int, number of sampling levels (coarse and fine). Default: 2 [S1, unofficial].
* `resample_padding`: float, Dirichlet/alpha padding added to weights before normalization during resampling. Default: 0.01 [S1, unofficial].
* `stop_level_grad`: bool, if True, gradients do not backpropagate across levels. Default: True [S1, unofficial].
* `use_viewdirs`: bool, if True, view directions are used as a condition for the MLP. Default: True [S1, unofficial].
* `lindisp`: bool, if True, sampling is linear in disparity ($1/z$) rather than depth ($z$). Default: False [S1, unofficial].
* `ray_shape`: str, shape of cast rays, must be 'cone' or 'cylinder'. Default: 'cone' [S1, unofficial].
* `min_deg_point`: int, minimum degree of positional encoding for 3D points. Default: 0 [S1, unofficial].
* `max_deg_point`: int, maximum degree of positional encoding for 3D points. Default: 16 [S1, unofficial].
* `deg_view`: int, degree of positional encoding for view directions. Default: 4 [S1, unofficial].
* `density_activation`: Callable, density activation function. Default: `softplus` [S1, unofficial].
* `density_noise`: float, standard deviation of noise added to raw density. Default: 0.0 [S1, unofficial].
* `density_bias`: float, shift added to raw densities pre-activation. Default: -1.0 [S1, unofficial].
* `rgb_activation`: Callable, RGB activation function. Default: `sigmoid` [S1, unofficial].
* `rgb_padding`: float, padding added to RGB outputs. Default: 0.001 [S1, unofficial].
* `disable_integration`: bool, if True, use Point-wise Positional Encoding instead of Integrated Positional Encoding. Default: False [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1. **Initialization**: Initialize the MLP and Image Signal Processing (ISP) modules [S1, unofficial]. Create an empty result list `ret` [S1, unofficial].
2. **Level Loop**: Iterate `i_level` from 0 to `num_levels - 1` [S1, unofficial].
3. **Randomness**: Split the random number generator `rng` into `key` and a new `rng` [S1, unofficial].
4. **Sampling**:
   * If `i_level == 0` (Coarse): Perform stratified sampling along rays [S1, unofficial].
     * Calculate effective radius: `effective_radius = rays.radii * train_coc` [S1, unofficial].
     * Call `sample_along_rays(key, rays.origins, rays.directions, effective_radius, num_samples, rays.near, rays.far, randomized, lindisp, ray_shape)` [S1, unofficial].
     * This returns `t_vals` (sample positions along the ray) and `samples` (3D coordinates and radii at those positions) [S1, unofficial].
   * If `i_level > 0` (Fine): Resample along rays based on previous weights [S1, unofficial].
     * Call `resample_along_rays(key, rays.origins, rays.directions, effective_radius, t_vals, weights, randomized, ray_shape, stop_level_grad, resample_padding)` [S1, unofficial].
     * This returns updated `t_vals` and `samples` [S1, unofficial].
5. **Integration Check**: If `disable_integration` is True, replace `samples` with `(samples[0], jnp.zeros_like(samples[1]))` to disable integrated encoding (effectively reverting to point-wise PE) [S1, unofficial].
6. **Positional Encoding**:
   * Compute integrated positional encoding for the 3D samples using `integrated_pos_enc(samples, min_deg_point, max_deg_point)` [S1, unofficial]. This function accounts for the cone/cylinder shape of the ray segment by integrating the standard positional encoding over the interval defined by the sample's radius [G].
   * If `use_viewdirs` is True, compute view direction encoding using `pos_enc(rays.viewdirs, min_deg=0, max_deg=deg_view, append_identity=True)` [S1, unofficial].
7. **MLP Forward Pass**:
   * Concatenate point encoding and view direction encoding (if used) to form the input to the MLP [S1, unofficial].
   * The MLP outputs `raw_density` and `raw_rgb` [S1, unofficial].
   * If `randomized` is True and `density_noise > 0`, add Gaussian noise: `raw_density += density_noise * random.normal(key, raw_density.shape, dtype=raw_density.dtype)` [S1, unofficial].
8. **Activations**:
   * Apply RGB activation: `rgb = rgb_activation(raw_rgb)` [S1, unofficial].
   * Apply RGB padding: `rgb = rgb * (1 + 2 * rgb_padding) - rgb_padding` [S1, unofficial].
   * Apply density activation: `density = density_activation(raw_density + density_bias)` [S1, unofficial].
9. **Volume Rendering**:
   * Perform volumetric rendering using `volumetric_rendering(rgb, density, t_vals, rays.directions, white_bkgd)` [S1, unofficial].
   * This step computes the final color `comp_rgb`, expected distance, accumulated alpha `acc`, and per-sample weights `weights` using alpha compositing [S1, unofficial].
10. **ISP**: Apply the learned Image Signal Processing pipeline to `comp_rgb` using `isp(comp_rgb, rays.temperature, rays.exposure)` to get `rgb_out` [S1, unofficial].
11. **Result Storage**: Append `(rgb_out, distance, acc)` to `ret` [S1, unofficial].
12. **Return**: Return the list `ret` [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
* **Precision**: The integrated positional encoding involves integration over the cone/cylinder. Numerical stability depends on the radius size. Very small radii may lead to precision issues in the integration [G].
* **Boundaries**: The `near` and `far` planes must be correctly set for each ray. Incorrect bounds can lead to sampling outside the scene or missing geometry [G].
* **Degenerate Input**: If `radii` are zero (e.g., at the camera origin), the cone shape degenerates to a line. The `integrated_pos_enc` function should handle this gracefully, typically by falling back to point-wise encoding or ensuring the integration interval is non-zero [G].
* **Gradient Flow**: If `stop_level_grad` is True, gradients do not flow from the fine level to the coarse level. This is a design choice to stabilize training but must be respected in the implementation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity**: $O(L \cdot N)$, where $L$ is the number of levels and $N$ is the number of samples per level. The integrated positional encoding adds a constant factor overhead per sample [G].
* **Memory Complexity**: $O(L \cdot N)$ for storing samples, encodings, and intermediate rendering results [G].
* **Measured Performance**: The model is designed for unbounded, outdoor scenes (Mip-NeRF 360 dataset). It provides higher quality images with fewer artifacts compared to standard NeRF, especially at high frequencies [S1, unofficial].
* **Scaling**: Scales linearly with the number of samples and levels [G].
* **Strengths**: Reduces aliasing, handles high-frequency details better, suitable for unbounded scenes [S1, unofficial].
* **Limitations**: More complex implementation, slightly higher computational cost per sample due to integration [G].
* **When to Use**: When training on unbounded scenes or scenes with high-frequency details where standard NeRF exhibits aliasing [S1, unofficial].
* **When Not to Use**: For simple, bounded scenes with low-frequency content, standard NeRF may be sufficient and faster [G].

## Background
The MipNerfModel was introduced in the Mip-NeRF paper by Barron et al. (2021) and extended in Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al., 2022). It addresses the issue of aliasing in NeRF by modeling rays as cones/cylinders and integrating positional encodings over these volumes [G].
