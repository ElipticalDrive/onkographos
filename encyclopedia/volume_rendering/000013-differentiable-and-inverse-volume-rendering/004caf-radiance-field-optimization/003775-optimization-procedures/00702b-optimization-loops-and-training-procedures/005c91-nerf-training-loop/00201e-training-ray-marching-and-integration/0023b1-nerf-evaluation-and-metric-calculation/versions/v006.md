# NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation

Also called: Evaluation and Metric Calculation, NeRF Evaluation, NeRF Validation, Single Image Rendering, Validation Rendering

## Overview
NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation is the procedure for rendering single images from a trained Radiance Field at specified camera poses and quantifying the quality of the reconstruction against ground-truth data [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of validating that the learned volumetric representation generalizes to novel viewpoints by generating RGB, depth, and disparity maps, then computing photometric and perceptual metrics [S1, S3, unofficial]. It differs from training procedures by disabling stochastic perturbations to ensure deterministic, reproducible results and by focusing on post-processing aggregation of metrics rather than gradient-based optimization [S2, S13741-standard-metric-computation.md, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `coarse_model`: Trained coarse NeRF model [S1, unofficial].
*   `fine_model`: Trained fine NeRF model [S1, unofficial].
*   `pose`: 4x4 camera-to-world matrix (numpy array or torch tensor) [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   `K`: 3x3 camera intrinsic matrix (numpy array or torch tensor) [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   `H`, `W`: Image height and width in pixels [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   `near`, `far`: Near and far plane distances for ray marching [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   `args`: Configuration object containing `chunk` (default 1024), `n_samples_coarse`, `n_samples_fine`, `white_bkgd`, `lindisp`, `netchunk`, `use_ndc` [S1, unofficial].
*   `gt_rgb`: Ground-truth RGB image for metric calculation [S3, unofficial].
*   `device`: Torch device (CPU/GPU) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `rgb`: Rendered RGB image tensor/numpy array of shape `[H, W, 3]` [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   `depth`: Rendered depth map of shape `[H, W]` [S1, unofficial].
*   `disp`: Rendered disparity map of shape `[H, W]` [S1, unofficial].
*   `metrics`: Dictionary or list containing PSNR and SSIM values [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Rays:** Represented as two tensors `rays_o` (origins) and `rays_d` (directions), each of shape `[-1, 3]` after flattening [S1, unofficial].
*   **Chunks:** Rays are processed in batches of size `chunk` to manage GPU memory [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Results:** Intermediate rendering results (RGB, depth, disparity) are stored in dictionaries keyed by `_coarse` or `_fine` suffixes [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Set `coarse_model` and `fine_model` to evaluation mode (`model.eval()`) [S2, S3, unofficial]. Disable gradients using `torch.no_grad()` [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Input Conversion:** Convert `pose` and `K` from numpy arrays to `torch.float32` tensors on the specified `device` if they are not already [S1, S2, unofficial].
3.  **Ray Generation:** Generate ray origins `rays_o` and directions `rays_d` using the intrinsic matrix `K`, image dimensions `H, W`, and camera pose `pose` [S1, S3, unofficial].
    *   Formula for `K` (if not provided):
        $$ K = \begin{bmatrix} f & 0 & 0.5 W \\ 0 & f & 0.5 H \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
        where $f$ is the focal length [S1, unofficial].
4.  **NDC Conversion (Optional):** If `args.use_ndc` is true, convert `rays_o` and `rays_d` to Normalized Device Coordinates using the focal length and near plane [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Flattening:** Reshape `rays_o` and `rays_d` to shape `[-1, 3]` [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Chunked Rendering:** Iterate over the flattened rays in chunks of size `chunk`:
    *   Extract chunk of `rays_o` and `rays_d`.
    *   Call the rendering function (e.g., `batchify_render` or `render_rays`) with `perturb=False` [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   The rendering function queries the `coarse_model` and `fine_model` to compute volume rendering integrals [S1, unofficial].
    *   Concatenate results from all chunks [S2, unofficial].
7.  **Reshaping:** Reshape the concatenated RGB result to `[H, W, 3]`, move to CPU, and convert to numpy array [S1, unofficial]. Similarly reshape and convert depth and disparity results to `[H, W]` numpy arrays [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Metric Calculation:**
    *   **MSE:** Calculate Mean Squared Error between predicted RGB and `gt_rgb` [S3, unofficial].
        $$ MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (RGB_{pred}[i] - RGB_{gt}[i])^2 $$
    *   **PSNR:** Calculate Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio [S3, unofficial].
        $$ PSNR = -10 \cdot \log_{10}(MSE) $$
    *   **SSIM:** Calculate Structural Similarity Index using `compare_ssim` with `full=True` and `multichannel=True` [S3, unofficial].
9.  **Visualization (Optional):** Create a side-by-side image of Ground Truth and Prediction [S2, unofficial]. Save predicted RGB as PNG [S3, unofficial].
10. **Aggregation:** Append PSNR and SSIM to lists for all test images [S3, unofficial]. Calculate mean PSNR and mean SSIM across the dataset [S3, unofficial].

                white_bkgd=args.white_bkgd,
                lindisp=args.lindisp,
                netchunk=args.netchunk

            # Extract keys based on presence of '_fine'

            depth_key = 'depth_fine' if 'depth_fine' in result else 'depth_coarse'

            all_depth.append(result[depth_key])

## Subtopics

### [Masked Evaluation](7531-masked-evaluation.md)
Masked Evaluation restricts NeRF metric calculation (PSNR, SSIM) to pixels visible in at least two training views, preventing metric degradation from predicting unseen geometry or depth of single-view objects. It differs from unmasked evaluation by excluding regions where radiance fields memorize rather than predict scene content, which artificially inflates error metrics. It differs from feature-level occlusion handling by operating directly on rendered depth maps to guide RGBD inpainting or consistency losses.

### [MIMO Objective](10931-mimo-objective.md)
The MIMO objective is the total training loss function for MIMO-NeRF, combining pixel-wise reconstruction errors with a 3D consistency regularization term across multiple reformulated Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs). It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF training by explicitly enforcing consistency between different "views" of the same scene generated by parallel MLPs with varying sampling densities, thereby enabling faster rendering without sacrificing quality. Unlike sibling methods that focus on evaluation metrics like PSNR or SSIM, this objective drives the optimization loop during training.

### [Text-Image Direction Score](13695-text-image-direction-score.md)
The Text-Image Direction Score (TIDS) is a quantitative metric used in differentiable volume rendering to estimate the semantic alignment between text-driven scene edits and the resulting visual changes in rendered images. It measures the cosine similarity between the direction of change in the CLIP image embedding space and the direction of change in the CLIP text embedding space. Unlike standard pixel-wise metrics such as PSNR or SSIM, TIDS evaluates whether the *semantic intent* of a text prompt was correctly translated into the 3D scene's appearance. It is primarily used in text-guided NeRF editing pipelines to verify that modifications (e.g., "add fireworks") affect the correct regions and semantics without distorting unrelated content.

### [Standard Metric Computation](13741-standard-metric-computation.md)
Standard Metric Computation quantifies the photorealistic and perceptual quality of rendered NeRF images by comparing them against ground-truth data using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. It differs from sibling methods by focusing on the post-processing stage: aggregating per-view scores into global statistics and structured reports, rather than performing the ray marching or the individual metric calculations themselves. It serves as the final quantitative step in the NeRF evaluation pipeline, ensuring that results from [PSNR Evaluation Pipeline](4315-psnr-evaluation-pipeline.md) and [Masked Evaluation](7531-masked-evaluation.md) are standardized for comparison.

### [Depth Supervision and Pseudo-Depth](13742-depth-supervision-and-pseudo-depth.md)
This family of techniques incorporates geometric constraints into the radiance field optimization loop to improve reconstruction quality, particularly in low-data regimes or when explicit geometry is required for downstream tasks. While standard NeRF optimization relies solely on photometric RGB losses, these methods introduce depth-related penalties or sampling strategies. Depth Supervision explicitly minimizes the difference between rendered depth and ground-truth depth (derived from SfM keypoints or stereo), whereas Pseudo-Depth approximates surface geometry via weighted averaging of sampled points to guide feature distillation or segmentation without requiring explicit depth labels.

### [Depth-Supervised EndoNeRF Loss](15276-depth-supervised-endonerf-loss.md)
A training objective that combines photometric color loss with a depth-supervised loss using estimated stereo depth to accelerate convergence and remove the need for a depth refinement module.

#### LiDAR Semantic Filtering for Evaluation
Identifies LiDAR points that fall within the camera's field of view, selects rays terminating on desired semantic classes (e.g., roads, sidewalks) using segmentation predictions from SegFormer, and feeds the filtered LiDAR rays into the NeRF model to perform depth rendering and predict the corresponding point cloud [S4]. Both filtered LiDAR ground truth and NeRF-predicted point clouds are accumulated for each test frame to evaluate geometry reconstruction quality [S4].

#### Novel View Selection Policy
Generates initial synthetic training data by rendering RGB-D images with uncertainty estimation from various viewpoints using U-NeRF [S5]. Prunes views based on rendering quality (depth too close, large depth/color uncertainty), applies a pre-trained E-SCRNet to get epistemic uncertainty maps, filters out unreliable pixels, and calculates mean epistemic uncertainty as a novel view score to select top-k images [S5].

#### Nerfbusters Evaluation Protocol
An evaluation procedure for measuring NeRF quality at novel viewpoints by using two separate camera trajectories: one for training and one for evaluation [S6]. It involves recording two videos (10-20 seconds for training, different viewpoints for evaluation), extracting poses, training a "pseudo ground truth" model on both splits to save depth/normal/visibility maps, and optimizing a NeRF on the training split to evaluate at novel viewpoints using the captured evaluation images and pseudo ground truth maps [S6]. Standard benchmarks evaluate only on held-out frames from the training sequence, which does not reflect quality at extreme novel viewpoints [S6].

#### Expected Termination Depth Synthesis
Synthesizes depth maps from a trained NeRF's density field by computing the expected termination depth of a ray [S7]. A ray is cast towards the camera, and the density field determines how deep into the volumetric object the ray is expected to penetrate, using that distance as the depth map value [S7]. NeRF does not directly estimate object boundaries nor directly produce depth maps; hard surfaces are simulated using a very dense but not infinitely dense field [S7]. It differs from direct depth sensors (e.g., RealSense D415) which fail on thin/reflective objects, and from MVS (e.g., COLMAP) which assumes Lambertian surfaces and fails on specularities [S7].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of pixels ($H \times W$) and the number of samples per ray ($N$). Chunking adds overhead but prevents memory overflow.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to the chunk size. The total memory footprint is dominated by the model weights and the intermediate tensors for the current chunk.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Scales linearly with resolution. Chunking allows processing of arbitrarily large images given sufficient time.
*   **Strengths:** Deterministic results (with perturb=False); provides standard industry metrics (PSNR, SSIM); supports various metric types (photometric, perceptual, semantic).
*   **Limitations:** Standard metrics (PSNR/SSIM) may not correlate perfectly with perceptual quality; unmasked evaluation can be misleading for novel views; depth estimation is indirect.
*   **When to use:** Validating trained NeRF models; comparing different NeRF architectures; generating datasets for downstream tasks.
*   **When not to use:** During training (use loss functions instead); when real-time rendering is required (evaluation is typically offline).

## Background
NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation originated with the introduction of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1, S3, unofficial]. The standard evaluation protocol involves rendering images at held-out camera poses and comparing them to ground truth using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS [S1, S3, unofficial]. Subsequent work has refined this process, introducing masked evaluation to account for novel view synthesis [S1, S2, unofficial], specialized protocols for casual captures like Nerfbusters [S6], and semantic/depth-aware metrics [S4, S7]. The method is widely used in computer vision research to benchmark novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction quality.
