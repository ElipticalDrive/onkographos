# Depth-Guided Sampling

Also called: Local Sampling Strategies

## Overview
Depth-Guided Sampling is a ray sampling strategy for neural radiance fields that focuses scene sampling on estimated surface regions using depth priors from input views to increase sample density in relevant areas, thereby improving synthesis quality and allowing a reduction in the number of samples per ray [S2, S3]. It sets itself apart from uniform or hierarchical stratified sampling by explicitly leveraging predicted depth maps (and their uncertainties) to bias the placement of query points toward likely object surfaces, reducing redundant sampling in free space or occluded regions [S2].

## How it works
The method operates by estimating depth and uncertainty for each pixel in the input views, projecting candidate points along camera rays onto these depth maps to compute surface likelihoods, and then selecting or generating sample points based on these likelihoods [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Camera rays $r(t)$ defined by near plane $t_{near}$ and far plane $t_{far}$ [S2].
*   For each input view $i$:
    *   Input image $I^{(i)}$ [S2].
    *   Estimated depth map $D^{(i)}$ (mean depth) [S2].
    *   Estimated standard deviation map $D_{std}^{(i)}$ (depth uncertainty) [S2].
    *   Camera projection matrix $K^{(i)}$ [S2].
    *   Depth map normals (derived from $D^{(i)}$) [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   A set of 3D sample points $\{x_j\}$ along the ray $r(t)$, optimized for surface presence [S2].

### Data Structures
*   **Candidate Samples:** A list of $N_{cand}$ points uniformly distributed along the ray interval $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ [S2].
*   **Likelihood Fields:** Per-view probability density values $p^{(i)}(x)$ for each candidate point $x$ [S2].
*   **Occlusion-Aware Likelihoods:** Values $p_{oa}(x)$ representing the probability that $x$ is the first surface point encountered along the ray [S2].

### Steps

1.  **Candidate Generation:**
    Uniformly sample $N_{cand}$ candidate points $\{x\}$ along the ray $r(t)$ within the interval $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ [S2].
    $$ \{x\} = \{r(t) \mid t \sim [t_{near}, t_{far}]\} $$
    The step size $\delta$ for these candidates is defined as:
    $$ \delta = (t_{far} - t_{near}) / N_{cand} $$
    [S2]

2.  **Backface Culling:**
    Calculate normals from the depth map $D^{(i)}$ [S2]. For each candidate point $x$, project it into the $i$-th camera space to get $x^{(i)}$ [S2]. Discard $x$ if the angle between the ray direction and the projected normal is smaller than 90 degrees [S2].

3.  **Per-View Surface Likelihood Calculation:**
    For each input view $i$ and each candidate point $x$:
    a. Calculate the difference $\Delta z$ between the sample's z-component in the $i$-th camera space and its corresponding projected depth value:
    $$ \Delta z = D^{(i)}(K^{(i)} \circ x^{(i)}) - x^{[z]} $$
    where $x^{[z]}$ is the z-component of $x$ in camera space [S2].
    
    b. Determine the likelihood $p^{(i)}(x)$ of $x$ being a surface point for view $i$ by integrating a Gaussian PDF over the sample interval $\delta$:
    $$ p^{(i)}(x) = \int_{z-\delta/2}^{z+\delta/2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma} e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{z'-\mu}{\sigma})^2} dz' $$
    where:
    *   $\mu = D^{(i)}(K^{(i)} \circ x^{(i)})$
    *   $\sigma = D_{std}^{(i)}(K^{(i)} \circ x^{(i)})$
    *   $z = x^{[z]}$ [S2]

4.  **Multi-View Likelihood Aggregation:**
    Determine the multi-view likelihood $p(x)$ by performing view-wise max-pooling over all input views:
    $$ p(x) = \max_i p^{(i)}(x) $$
    [S2]

5.  **Shortlisting:**
    Shortlist $N_{samples}$ candidates with the highest likelihoods $p(x)$ for sampling the NeRF [S2].

6.  **Gaussian Boosting:**
    a. Calculate the occlusion-aware likelihood $p_{oa}(x)$ for each candidate $x$ along the ray $r(t)$:
    $$ p_{oa}(x) = p(x) \cdot \prod_{\tilde{x} < x} (1 - p(\tilde{x})) $$
    where $\tilde{x}$ denotes candidates with a smaller ray parameter $t$ (closer to the camera) than $x$ [S2].
    
    b. Fit a Gaussian distribution against the occlusion-aware likelihoods $p_{oa}(x)$ along the ray [S2].
    
    c. Sample $N_{gauss}$ points from this fitted Gaussian distribution and add them to the shortlisted candidates [S2].

### Alternative: Uniform Interval Sampling [S3]
Some implementations simplify the process by skipping coarse sampling and blank areas, directly sampling $M$ points uniformly from the confidence interval $[d-s, d+s]$ of the estimated depth $d$ with confidence interval $s$ [S3]. The depth $t_j$ of the $j$-th query point ($j=1..M$) is calculated as:
$$ t_j = d - s + \frac{2s}{M - 1} (j - 1) $$
[S3]

### Alternative: Stereo Depth-Cueing [S4]
In stereo settings, points are drawn from a normalized impulse distribution centered at the stereo depth prior $D_i[u, v]$ [S4]. The transfer function is:
$$ \delta(s; u, v, i) = \exp \left( -(s - D_i[u, v])^2 / 2\xi^2 \right) $$
[S4]
Steps $s_j$ are drawn from $\frac{1}{\xi\sqrt{2\pi}} \delta(s; u, v, i)$ [S4].

### Parameters
*   $N_{cand}$: Number of initial candidate samples. Not given in the sources.
*   $N_{samples}$: Number of shortlisted candidates. Not given in the sources.
*   $N_{gauss}$: Number of points sampled during Gaussian boosting. Not given in the sources.
*   $M$: Number of points in uniform interval sampling. Not given in the sources.
*   $d$: Estimated depth. Not given in the sources.
*   $s$: Confidence interval. Not given in the sources.
*   $\xi$: Hyperparameter controlling the width of the Gaussian impulse in stereo depth-cueing. Set to a small value to mimic a Dirac delta impulse. Not given in the sources.
*   $\delta$: Step size for candidate samples, $(t_{far} - t_{near})/N_{cand}$. [S2]

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Large Confidence Interval:** If the confidence interval $s$ is large, sampling might still cover blank areas, although this method reduces this occurrence compared to uniform camera-space sampling [S3].
*   **Occlusion Ordering:** The occlusion-aware likelihood calculation assumes a specific ordering of samples along the ray; incorrect ordering leads to incorrect $p_{oa}$ values [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def depth_guided_sampling(ray_origin, ray_direction, t_near, t_far, 
                          depth_maps, depth_std_maps, camera_matrices, 
                          N_cand=100, N_samples=20, N_gauss=10):
    """
    Implements Depth-Guided Sampling as described in [S2].
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: (3,) array, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: (3,) array, direction of the ray.
        t_near: float, near plane distance.
        t_far: float, far plane distance.
        depth_maps: list of (H, W) arrays, depth maps D^(i).
        depth_std_maps: list of (H, W) arrays, depth std maps D_std^(i).
        camera_matrices: list of (3, 4) or (4, 4) arrays, projection matrices K^(i).
        N_cand: int, number of initial candidates.
        N_samples: int, number of shortlisted samples.
        N_gauss: int, number of Gaussian boosted samples.
        
    Returns:
        samples: (N_total, 3) array of 3D points.
    """
    # 1. Candidate Generation
    t_candidates = np.linspace(t_near, t_far, N_cand)
    candidates = ray_origin + t_candidates[:, np.newaxis] * ray_direction
    
    # Helper to project point to camera space and get depth/std
    def get_projected_depth_and_std(point, K, D, D_std):
        # Transform point to camera coordinates
        point_h = np.append(point, 1.0)
        cam_coords = (K @ point_h)[:3]
        u, v, z_cam = cam_coords[0], cam_coords[1], cam_coords[2]
        
        # Check if point is within image bounds
        if not (0 <= u < D.shape[1] and 0 <= v < D.shape[0]):
            return None, None, None
            
        # Interpolate depth and std at (u, v)
        # Using nearest neighbor for simplicity; bilinear is better [G]
        mu = D[int(v), int(u)]
        sigma = D_std[int(v), int(u)]
        
        return mu, sigma, z_cam

    # 2. Per-View Likelihood Calculation
    # Initialize likelihood array
    likelihoods = np.zeros(N_cand)
    
    for i in range(len(depth_maps)):
        D = depth_maps[i]
        D_std = depth_std_maps[i]
        K = camera_matrices[i]
        
        view_likelihoods = np.zeros(N_cand)
        
        for j in range(N_cand):
            x = candidates[j]
            mu, sigma, z_cam = get_projected_depth_and_std(x, K, D, D_std)
            
            if mu is None:
                view_likelihoods[j] = 0.0
                continue
                
            # Step size delta
            delta = (t_far - t_near) / N_cand
            
            # Calculate likelihood by integrating Gaussian PDF over interval [z-delta/2, z+delta/2]
            # PDF: 1/(sqrt(2*pi)*sigma) * exp(-0.5 * ((z' - mu)/sigma)^2)
            # We integrate this from z_cam - delta/2 to z_cam + delta/2
            
            lower = z_cam - delta / 2.0
            upper = z_cam + delta / 2.0
            
            # Using scipy norm.cdf for efficiency
            p_i = norm.cdf(upper, loc=mu, scale=sigma) - norm.cdf(lower, loc=mu, scale=sigma)
            view_likelihoods[j] = p_i
            
        # Max-pooling across views
        if i == 0:
            likelihoods = view_likelihoods
        else:
            likelihoods = np.maximum(likelihoods, view_likelihoods)
            
    # 3. Shortlisting
    # Get indices of top N_samples likelihoods
    top_indices = np.argsort(likelihoods)[-N_samples:][::-1]
    shortlisted_samples = candidates[top_indices]
    
    # 4. Gaussian Boosting
    # Calculate occlusion-aware likelihoods
    # Sort candidates by distance (t value)
    t_sorted_indices = np.argsort(t_candidates)
    t_sorted = t_candidates[t_sorted_indices]
    candidates_sorted = candidates[t_sorted_indices]
    likelihoods_sorted = likelihoods[t_sorted_indices]
    
    # Calculate product of (1 - p(x_tilde)) for x_tilde < x
    one_minus_p = 1.0 - likelihoods_sorted
    # Handle cases where p=1 to avoid log(0) or negative probs
    one_minus_p = np.clip(one_minus_p, 1e-7, 1.0)
    
    cum_prod = np.cumprod(one_minus_p)
    # Shift cum_prod to align with current point (exclude current point's contribution)
    # For the first point, product is 1.0
    occlusion_aware = np.zeros(N_cand)
    occlusion_aware[0] = likelihoods_sorted[0]
    for k in range(1, N_cand):
        occlusion_aware[k] = likelihoods_sorted[k] * cum_prod[k-1]
        
    # Fit Gaussian to occlusion_aware likelihoods along the ray
    # We treat t_sorted as the domain and occlusion_aware as the PDF
    # Normalize occlusion_aware to be a valid PDF
    occlusion_aware_pdf = occlusion_aware / np.sum(occlusion_aware)
    
    # Calculate mean and std of this distribution
    mean_t = np.sum(t_sorted * occlusion_aware_pdf)
    std_t = np.sqrt(np.sum((t_sorted - mean_t)**2 * occlusion_aware_pdf))
    
    # Sample N_gauss points from this Gaussian
    if N_gauss > 0:
        gaussian_t = np.random.normal(mean_t, std_t, N_gauss)
        # Clip to [t_near, t_far]
        gaussian_t = np.clip(gaussian_t, t_near, t_far)
        gaussian_samples = ray_origin + gaussian_t[:, np.newaxis] * ray_direction
        final_samples = np.vstack([shortlisted_samples, gaussian_samples])
    else:
        final_samples = shortlisted_samples
        
    return final_samples
```

## Subtopics

### [Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling](8866-pixel-region-and-depth-guided-ray-sampling.md)
Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling is a ray sampling strategy that increases sampling probability in pixel areas exhibiting significant color and depth variation, reducing redundant rays in flat regions to accelerate network convergence and improve geometry perception. Unlike conventional NeRF which uses uniform distribution, this method samples varying ray densities based on depth boundaries and pixel regions to simultaneously accelerate convergence and improve geometry perception. It builds on Depth-Guided Sampling by explicitly incorporating color variation alongside depth estimates.

### [Truncated Depth-Based Sampling](10613-truncated-depth-based-sampling.md)
Truncated Depth-Based Sampling (TDBS) is a ray sampling strategy that utilizes monocular depth priors informed by the truncated normal distribution to optimize ray point sampling, improving convergence speed and accuracy of pose estimation. It differs from siblings like NoPe-NeRF by explicitly using the truncated normal distribution for sampling to fully exploit depth priors, whereas NoPe-NeRF considers overall depth distortion and does not fully exploit the potential of monocular depth maps. It builds on Depth-Guided Sampling by replacing uniform or heuristic sampling with a probabilistic model centered on the estimated depth.

### [Surface-Based Sampling](11356-surface-based-sampling.md)
Surface-Based Sampling is a strategy that restricts 3D sample point generation to a narrow band around the estimated surface of an object, rather than sampling throughout the entire volume or free space. It solves the problem of quality degradation caused by standard NeRF sampling in irrelevant regions (free space or occluded areas) by concentrating computational resources where the radiance field actually varies. It differs from its siblings by explicitly using depth priors to define this sampling band: Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling uses pixel-region constraints, Truncated Depth-Based Sampling truncates rays based on depth, while Surface-Based Sampling defines a symmetric range around the depth value.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** [S2] reports improved synthesis quality and allows reduction of samples per ray on the FaceScape dataset. [S3] reports skipping coarse sampling and reducing sampling in blank areas. [S4] reports concentrating points near tissue surfaces for robotic surgery scenes.
*   **Scaling:** Scales linearly with the number of input views and candidate points.
*   **Strengths:** Reduces redundant sampling in free space; improves geometry perception; leverages multi-view consistency.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the depth estimator; backface culling may remove valid samples if normals are noisy; Gaussian fitting assumes unimodal likelihood distribution.
*   **When to use:** When depth priors are available and accurate; when computational budget for sampling is limited; when scene geometry is sparse or surface-dominated.
*   **When not to use:** When depth priors are unreliable or unavailable; when the scene has large transparent or volumetric regions not well-captured by surface depth.

## Background
Depth-Guided Sampling was introduced in the DINER paper (2023) [S2] as part of a depth-aware image-based neural radiance field framework. It builds on pixelNeRF and uses TransMVSNet for depth estimation. The method was developed to address the inefficiency of uniform sampling in NeRF by focusing computation on relevant surface regions. Variants like NeRF-SDP [S3] and Stereo Depth-Cueing [S4] have adapted the core idea for generalizable NeRFs and stereo surgical reconstruction, respectively.
