# Space-time Neural Irradiance Fields

Also called: ST-NIF, Space-time Neural Irradiance Fields for Free-Viewpoint Video

## Overview
Space-time Neural Irradiance Fields (ST-NIF) is a method for rendering free-viewpoint videos from a stream of RGB-D images by modeling the scene as a 4D function that maps spatiotemporal coordinates to color and volume density [S2]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Dynamic NeRF and Neural Scene Flow Fields by directly conditioning the radiance field on time rather than predicting explicit deformation fields or scene flow [S1, unofficial][S2]. This approach requires carefully selected auxiliary losses to resolve the motion-appearance ambiguity inherent in single-video dynamic scene reconstruction [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A stream of $N_f$ RGB-D images $I_t: u \rightarrow (c, d)$ at discrete time steps $t \in \mathcal{T} = \{1, 2, \dots, N_f\}$, where $u=(u,v)$ are 2D pixel coordinates, along with their associated camera calibrations $P_t$ [S2].
*   **Output:** A rendered color $\hat{C}(r,t)$ and depth $\hat{D}(r,t)$ for a ray $r$ at time $t$, and the implicit function $F$ representing the scene [S2].

**Data Structures**
*   **Implicit Function $F$:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) mapping $(x, t) \rightarrow (c, \sigma)$, where $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the spatial coordinate and $t \in [-1, 1]$ is the normalized time [S2].
*   **MLP Architecture:** Identical to the original NeRF architecture, except the first 8 hidden layers use 1024 activations instead of 256 [S2].
*   **Positional Encoding:** Applied to both spatial coordinates and time, using 4 frequency bands [S2].
*   **Sampling Pool $\mathcal{X}$:** A set of 3D points derived from the union of all sampling locations along all rays of all frames, excluding points closer than $\epsilon$ to observed surfaces [S2].

**Ray Parameterization**
A ray $r$ at time $t$ is defined by a pixel location $u$ and camera calibration $P_t$. The ray is parameterized such that the parameter $s$ denotes scene depth. This is achieved by setting the directional vector $\mathbf{d}$ such that its projection onto the principal ray has a unit norm in the camera space [S2]. The ray is defined as $r(s) = \mathbf{o} + s\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera center [S2].

**Sampling Strategy**
1.  **Hierarchical Sampling:** Uses hierarchical volume sampling as in the original NeRF, simultaneously training coarse and fine networks [S2].
2.  **Static Scene Sampling:** To compute the static scene loss, a sample pool $\mathcal{X}$ is formed by taking the union of all sampling locations along all rays of all frames. Points closer than $\epsilon$ to any observed surface are excluded. A fixed number of locations are randomly drawn from $\mathcal{X}$ at each iteration, with small random jitters added. A random time $t'$ is chosen for each sample location $x$, ensuring $(x, t')$ is not close to any scene surfaces [S2].

**Rendering**
The color $\hat{C}(r,t)$ is computed via numerical quadrature (volume rendering):
$$ \hat{C}(r,t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i(t) \sigma(r(s_i), t) c(r(s_i), t) \Delta s_i $$
where $T_i(t) = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(r(s_j), t) \Delta s_j\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance [S2].

**Loss Functions**
The total loss is a linear combination of four terms:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{color} + \alpha \mathcal{L}_{depth} + \beta \mathcal{L}_{empty} + \gamma \mathcal{L}_{static} $$
where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are weighting coefficients [S2].

1.  **Color Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{color}$):** Penalizes the difference between rendered and ground truth colors for the input viewpoints:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{color} = \sum_{(r,t) \in \mathcal{R}} \| \hat{C}(r,t) - C(r,t) \|^2 $$
    where $\mathcal{R}$ is a batch of rays [S2].

2.  **Depth Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{depth}$):** Constrains the predicted depth using input depth maps:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{depth} = \sum_{(r,t) \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \frac{1}{\hat{D}(r,t)} - \frac{1}{D(r,t)} \right\|^2 $$
    where $\hat{D}(r,t) = \int_{s_n}^{s_f} T(s,t) \sigma(r(s),t) s \, ds$ is the integrated sample depth, and $D(r,t)$ is the input depth [S2].

3.  **Empty-Space Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{empty}$):** Encourages zero density in the region between the camera and the first visible surface to prevent haze-like artifacts. Let $d_t(u)$ be the scene depth for pixel $u$ at time $t$. The margin is $\epsilon = 0.05 \cdot (s_f - s_n)$ [S2]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{empty} = \sum_{(r,t) \in \mathcal{R}} \int_{s_n}^{d_t(u) - \epsilon} \sigma(r(s), t) \, ds $$
    [S2].

4.  **Static Scene Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{static}$):** Constrains the MLP on points from the static sample pool $\mathcal{X}$ at random times $t'$ to prevent disocclusion artifacts in hidden regions. The specific formulation is not provided in the sources, but it involves querying $F(x, t')$ for $x \in \mathcal{X}$ [S2].

**Training Parameters**
*   **Batch Size:** $N_r = 1024$ rays randomly drawn from an input frame $I_t$ without replacement [S2].
*   **Time Normalization:** Time $t$ is normalized to $[-1, 1]$ [S2].
*   **Iterations:** 800,000 iterations [S2].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def train_st_nif(frames, camera_poses, depths, num_iterations=800000):
    # Initialize MLP F(x, t) -> (c, sigma) with NeRF architecture (1024 units, first 8 layers)
    # Initialize optimizer
    # Define positional encoding with 4 bands
    # Define ray parameterization: d such that projection onto principal ray has unit norm
    
    # Precompute static sample pool X
    X = []
    for t in frames:
        rays = generate_rays(frames[t], camera_poses[t])
        samples = hierarchical_sample(rays)
        # Exclude points closer than epsilon to observed surfaces
        for s in samples:
            if distance_to_surface(s, depth=depths[t]) > epsilon:
                X.append(s)
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # Sample batch of rays from a random frame t
        t = random_choice(frames)
        rays = random_sample(frames[t], camera_poses[t], count=1024)
        
        # Forward pass for color and depth losses
        colors_pred = []
        depths_pred = []
        for r in rays:
            # Sample points along ray r at time t
            points, weights = hierarchical_volume_sample(r, t)
            # Query MLP
            c, sigma = query_mlp(points, t)
            # Render color
            c_render = integrate(c, sigma, weights)
            # Render depth
            d_render = integrate_depth(sigma, points, weights)
            colors_pred.append(c_render)
            depths_pred.append(d_render)
            
        # Compute L_color
        l_color = mean_squared_error(colors_pred, ground_truth_colors(t))
        
        # Compute L_depth
        l_depth = mean_squared_error(
            inverse(depths_pred), 
            inverse(ground_truth_depths(t))
        )
        
        # Compute L_empty
        l_empty = 0
        for r in rays:
            # Integrate sigma from s_n to d_t(u) - epsilon
            depth_u = ground_truth_depth(r.pixel, t)
            empty_sigma = integrate_sigma_along_ray(r, t, s_n, depth_u - epsilon)
            l_empty += empty_sigma
        l_empty = mean(l_empty)
        
        # Compute L_static
        # Sample random points from X and random times t'
        static_samples = random_sample(X, count=1024)
        static_times = random_uniform(-1, 1, count=1024)
        static_preds = []
        for x, t_prime in zip(static_samples, static_times):
            # Ensure (x, t_prime) is not close to surface
            if distance_to_surface(x, depth=depths_at_time(t_prime)) > epsilon:
                c, sigma = query_mlp(x, t_prime)
                static_preds.append(sigma) # Or color, depending on specific implementation not fully detailed
        l_static = mean_squared_error(static_preds, 0) # Assuming target is zero density/color for empty space
        
        # Total Loss
        loss = l_color + alpha * l_depth + beta * l_empty + gamma * l_static
        
        # Backpropagate and update
        optimizer.step(loss)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes approximately 48 hours for a network with about 100 video frames [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Capable of rendering free-viewpoint videos from a single RGB-D video stream without explicit deformation fields [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Limitations:** The method suffers from motion-appearance ambiguity, leading to under-constrained geometry estimation [S2]. It is prone to haze-like artifacts due to the nature of weighted depth integration and disocclusion artifacts in hidden regions [S2].
*   **When to use:** When RGB-D data is available and explicit scene flow or deformation fields are undesirable or difficult to estimate [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When not to use:** For scenes with significant disocclusions or when high-fidelity novel view synthesis outside the original camera trajectory is required without the auxiliary losses [S2].

## Background
Space-time Neural Irradiance Fields was introduced by Xian et al. in 2020 [S2]. It is cited as a method that simply uses time as an additional input to render freeviewpoint videos from RGBD data [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the limitations of static NeRF in dynamic scenes by extending the implicit representation to 4D [S2].
