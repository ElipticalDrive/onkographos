# Training Ray Marching and Integration

Also called: Training Ray Marching, NeRF Forward Pass, Standard NeRF forward

## Overview
Training Ray Marching and Integration is the differentiable forward pass that synthesizes a 2D image from a continuous 5D radiance field by numerically approximating the volume rendering integral along camera rays [S1, S3, S5, S10, unofficial]. It solves the problem of mapping implicit scene representations (neural networks or dense grids) to pixel colors by integrating predicted volume density and view-dependent color, using probabilistic density functions to determine sample contributions rather than deterministic surface intersections [S1, S5, S10, unofficial]. Unlike [NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation](3079-nerf-evaluation-and-metric-calculation.md), which uses deterministic rendering for reproducible metrics, this method employs stochastic sampling with perturbation to facilitate gradient-based optimization [S2, S5, S10, unofficial]. It differs from [Volume Rendering Integration and Forward Pass](15462-volume-rendering-integration-and-forward-pass.md) by encompassing the entire training-specific pipeline—including ray generation, adaptive pruning via occupancy grids, and network querying—rather than focusing solely on the mathematical compositing mechanics [S1, S2, S3, S5, S10, unofficial].

## How it works
The method takes camera parameters and training images as input and outputs rendered RGB images, depth maps, and accumulated transmittance (opacity) maps, which are used to compute loss gradients [S3, S5, S10, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   `rays_o`: Ray origins in world space, shape `[N, 3]` (float) [S1, S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `rays_d`: Ray directions in world space, shape `[N, 3]` (float, unit vectors) [S1, S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `nears`, `fars`: Near and far clipping distances for each ray, shape `[N]` (float) [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `bg_color`: Background color tensor, shape `[3]` (float) [S7, S10, unofficial].
    *   `perturb`: Boolean flag enabling noise addition to sample positions [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `max_steps`: Maximum number of sampled points along each ray, integer [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `bound`: Spatial bounding box size scalar [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `density_bitfield`: Packed density bitfield, `uint8` array of shape `[C * H^3 / 8]` [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `dt_gamma`: Time delta gamma parameter (cone angle) for adaptive step sizing, float [S2, S10, unofficial].
    *   `T_thresh`: Transmittance threshold for early termination, float [S10, unofficial].
*   **Outputs**:
    *   `image`: Rendered RGB image, shape `[N, 3]` or `[H, W, 3]` (float) [S5, S10, unofficial].
    *   `depth`: Rendered depth map, shape `[N]` or `[H, W]` (float) [S5, S10, unofficial].
    *   `weights_sum`: Accumulated transmittance/opacity map, shape `[N]` or `[H, W]` (float) [S8, S10, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Bundle**: Contains `rays_o` and `rays_d` [S3, S5, unofficial].
*   **Sample Points (`xyzs`)**: Generated 3D coordinates, shape `[M, 3]` (float), where $M$ is the total number of samples across all rays [S2, S10, unofficial].
*   **Directions (`dirs`)**: View directions corresponding to samples, shape `[M, 3]` (float) [S2, S10, unofficial].
*   **Deltas (`deltas`)**: Step sizes (segment lengths), shape `[M, 2]` or `[M]`. The first value is the distance $\delta_t$, and the second is the ray parameter $t$ [S2, S10, unofficial].
*   **Ray Metadata (`rays`)**: Integer array, shape `[N, 3]`, storing `(index, point_offset, point_count)` for each ray to map samples back to rays [S2, S10, unofficial].
*   **Density Bitfield**: A packed `uint8` grid representing coarse density estimates for pruning [S2, S10, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Ray Generation**: Compute ray origins and directions in world space using camera intrinsics and extrinsics [S1, S3, S5, S9, unofficial].
2.  **Chunking**: Partition rays into chunks of size `chunk_size` (default $64 \times 1024$) to manage GPU memory [S1, S3, S6, unofficial].
3.  **Ray Marching (Sampling)**:
    *   Calculate near and far bounds for each ray using the scene bounding box [S9, S10, unofficial].
    *   Generate initial uniform depth values (z-values) between `nears` and `fars` [S10, unofficial].
    *   If `perturb` is true, add uniform noise in range $[-0.5, 0.5] \times \text{sample\_dist}$ to z-values, where $\text{sample\_dist} = (\text{fars} - \text{nears}) / \text{num\_steps}$ [S10, unofficial].
    *   Use an occupancy grid or density bitfield to prune empty space. If `grid_prune` is enabled, an `OccupancyGrid` is used to skip rays passing through empty voxels [S7, S9, S11, unofficial].
    *   Call the CUDA kernel `march_rays_train` with inputs `rays_o`, `rays_d`, `bound`, `density_bitfield`, `nears`, `fars`, `perturb`, `dt_gamma`, and `max_steps` to generate `xyzs`, `dirs`, `deltas`, and `rays` [S2, S8, S10, unofficial].
    *   If `mean_count` is set and active, trim outputs to the actual count $m$ from the step counter [S2, S10, unofficial].
4.  **Neural Network Query**:
    *   Query the neural network (MLP or hash grid) at `xyzs` and `dirs` to obtain volume density $\sigma$ (sigmas) and RGB color [S3, S8, S10, unofficial].
    *   Scale densities by `density_scale` if applicable [S10, unofficial].
5.  **Integration (Alpha Compositing)**:
    *   Compute segment distances `dists` by taking the difference between consecutive depth values, appending a large constant ($10^{10}$) for the last segment to ensure full contribution if opaque [S1, S3, S5, S10, unofficial].
    *   Compute opacity $\alpha$ for each sample:
        $$ \alpha = 1 - \exp(-\sigma \cdot d) $$
        where $\sigma$ is density and $d$ is the segment length [S5, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance $T$ as the cumulative product of $(1 - \alpha)$, with a small epsilon ($10^{-10}$) added for numerical stability:
        $$ T_i = \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} \exp(-\sigma_j \delta_j) $$
        Implemented as `T = torch.cumprod(torch.cat((torch.ones_like(rays_density[:1,...]), torch.exp(-rays_density * deltas)), dim=0), dim=0)` [S3, unofficial].
    *   Compute weights $w_i$ for each sample:
        $$ w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
        Implemented as `weights = T[:-1,...]*(1 - torch.exp(-rays_density * deltas))` [S3, unofficial].
    *   Composite the final RGB color:
        $$ C = \sum w_i c_i $$
        where $c_i$ is the color of sample $i$ [S3, S5, unofficial].
    *   Composite the final depth:
        $$ D = \sum w_i d_i $$
        where $d_i$ is the depth of sample $i$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Compute accumulated transmittance (opacity map):
        $$ A = \sum w_i $$
        [S5, unofficial].
    *   Add background color contribution: $C_{final} = C + (1 - A) \times \text{bg\_color}$ [S7, S10, unofficial].
    *   Normalize depth: $D_{norm} = \max(0, D - \text{nears}) / (\text{fars} - \text{nears})$ [S10, unofficial].
6.  **Early Termination**: If `T_thresh` is set, terminate ray marching early when transmittance drops below the threshold [S10, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def training_ray_marching_and_integration(
    rays_o: torch.Tensor,  # [N, 3]
    rays_d: torch.Tensor,  # [N, 3]
    nears: torch.Tensor,   # [N]
    fars: torch.Tensor,    # [N]
    bg_color: torch.Tensor, # [3]
    density_bitfield: torch.Tensor, # [C*H^3//8]
    bound: float,
    dt_gamma: float,
    max_steps: int,
    perturb: bool,
    T_thresh: float,
    density_scale: float,
    chunk_size: int = 64 * 1024
):
    """
    Performs one forward pass of training ray marching and integration.
    Returns: image [N, 3], depth [N], weights_sum [N]
    """
    N = rays_o.shape[0]
    all_images = []
    all_depths = []
    all_weights_sum = []

    # Chunking to manage memory
    for i in range(0, N, chunk_size):
        rays_o_chunk = rays_o[i:i+chunk_size]
        rays_d_chunk = rays_d[i:i+chunk_size]
        nears_chunk = nears[i:i+chunk_size]
        fars_chunk = fars[i:i+chunk_size]

        # 1. Ray Marching (Sampling)
        # This step typically calls a CUDA kernel. Here we simulate the logic.
        # In practice, this returns xyzs, dirs, deltas, rays_indices
        xyzs, dirs, deltas, rays_indices = march_rays_train_cuda(
            rays_o_chunk, rays_d_chunk, bound, density_bitfield,
            nears_chunk, fars_chunk, perturb=perturb, dt_gamma=dt_gamma,
            max_steps=max_steps, T_thresh=T_thresh
        )

        if xyzs.numel() == 0:
            continue

        # 2. Neural Network Query
        # Query density and color at sampled points
        sigmas, rgbs = query_neural_network(xyzs, dirs)
        
        # Scale density
        sigmas = sigmas * density_scale

        # 3. Integration
        # Compute distances between samples
        # deltas shape: [M, 2] where delta[0] is distance, delta[1] is ray param
        dists = deltas[:, 0] # [M]

        # Compute opacity alpha
        # alpha = 1 - exp(-sigma * dist)
        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigmas * dists)

        # Compute transmittance T
        # T[i] = product_{j=0}^{i-1} (1 - alpha[j])
        # Use cumprod for efficiency
        # Add epsilon for stability
        one_minus_alpha = 1.0 - alpha + 1e-10
        T = torch.cumprod(one_minus_alpha, dim=0)
        # Shift T by 1 position to get T[i] for sample i
        T_shifted = F.pad(T, (1, 0), value=1.0)[:-1]

        # Compute weights
        # w[i] = T[i] * alpha[i]
        weights = T_shifted * alpha

        # Composite RGB
        # image = sum(weights * color)
        # weights shape [M], rgbs shape [M, 3]
        image = torch.sum(weights[:, None] * rgbs, dim=0)

        # Composite Depth
        # We need depth values for each sample. 
        # In a real implementation, z_vals are returned from march_rays_train.
        # Here we assume we have z_vals corresponding to xyzs.
        # z_vals = rays_o + rays_d * t, where t is derived from deltas or returned.
        # For simplicity, assume z_vals are available from the marching step.
        # z_vals shape [M]
        # depth = sum(weights * z_vals)
        # Note: In the CUDA implementation, depth is often computed directly.
        # We will simulate it here assuming z_vals are passed or computed.
        # Since z_vals are not explicitly in the input/output of the CUDA call in the notes,
        # we assume they are part of the marching output or derived from deltas.
        # Let's assume z_vals are returned by march_rays_train for this pseudocode completeness.
        # However, the notes say march_rays_train returns xyzs, dirs, deltas, rays.
        # So we must compute z_vals from xyzs and rays_o/d.
        # z_vals = dot(xyzs - rays_o[rays_indices], rays_d[rays_indices])
        # This is complex to write without helper functions. 
        # We will use the depth computation from the notes which implies z_vals are available.
        
        # Let's assume z_vals are computed from xyzs and ray origins/directions
        # rays_indices maps each sample to its ray
        ray_indices = rays_indices[:, 0] # [M]
        ray_origins = rays_o_chunk[ray_indices] # [M, 3]
        ray_directions = rays_d_chunk[ray_indices] # [M, 3]
        
        # Project xyzs onto ray directions to get depth
        # z_vals = dot(xyzs - ray_origins, ray_directions)
        # This assumes ray_directions are unit vectors
        z_vals = torch.sum((xyzs - ray_origins) * ray_directions, dim=1)

        depth = torch.sum(weights * z_vals, dim=0)

        # Accumulated Transmittance (Opacity)
        weights_sum = torch.sum(weights, dim=0)

        # Add Background
        image = image + (1.0 - weights_sum[:, None]) * bg_color

        # Normalize Depth
        # Clamp to min 0, then normalize by (fars - nears)
        depth = torch.clamp(depth - nears_chunk[ray_indices], min=0.0)
        depth = depth / (fars_chunk[ray_indices] - nears_chunk[ray_indices])

        all_images.append(image)
        all_depths.append(depth)
        all_weights_sum.append(weights_sum)

    # Concatenate results
    if all_images:
        image = torch.cat(all_images, dim=0)
        depth = torch.cat(all_depths, dim=0)
        weights_sum = torch.cat(all_weights_sum, dim=0)
    else:
        # Fallback for empty chunks (e.g., all rays pruned)
        image = torch.zeros((N, 3), device=rays_o.device)
        depth = torch.zeros((N,), device=rays_o.device)
        weights_sum = torch.zeros((N,), device=rays_o.device)

    return image, depth, weights_sum
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   `chunk_size`: 65536 ($64 \times 1024$) [S6, unofficial].
*   `max_steps`: 1024 [S10, unofficial].
*   `dt_gamma`: 0.0 for NeRF Synthetic, $10^{-3}$ for MIPNeRF360 [S11, unofficial].
*   `T_thresh`: Not specified in sources, but used for early termination [S10, unofficial].
*   `density_scale`: Not specified, but applied to network output [S10, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: True for training, False for evaluation [S2, S10, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision**: Add epsilon ($10^{-10}$) to $1-\alpha$ in transmittance calculation to avoid NaN gradients [S3, S5, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries**: Use a large constant ($10^{10}$) for the last segment's delta to ensure full contribution if the ray is opaque [S1, S5, unofficial].
*   **Empty Rays**: If all rays are pruned by the occupancy grid, return zero tensors or handle gracefully [S10, unofficial].
*   **Memory**: Use chunking to avoid OOM errors during the neural network query step [S1, S3, S6, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Training Ray Marching and Integration — CUDA Ray Marching (Training), NeRF Forward Pass, Standard NeRF forward, Training Ray Marching, Training Ray Marching and Integration — NeRF Forward Pass, march_rays_train, run_cuda
A rendering function that uses an occupancy grid estimator to skip empty space during volume rendering, optimizing performance [S11, unofficial]. It is the core CUDA kernel (`march_rays_train`) called during the forward pass [S2, S8, S10, unofficial]. Key parameters include `near_plane` (0.2 for MIPNeRF360, 0.0 for NeRF Synthetic), `render_step_size` (1e-3 for MIPNeRF360, 5e-3 for NeRF Synthetic), `cone_angle` (0.004 for MIPNeRF360, 0.0 for NeRF Synthetic), and `alpha_thre` (1e-2 for MIPNeRF360, 0.0 for NeRF Synthetic) [S11, unofficial].

### Progressive Strided Ray Sampling
A progressive sampling strategy to stabilize GAN training, starting with large stride ray generation and progressively reducing stride size to cover larger regions initially and focus on local regions later [S12]. In each iteration, it randomly samples two patches: one from the reference view and one from a random unseen view [S12]. It obtains geometry pseudo labels via image warping and adds regularization on each patch’s intersection with the corresponding patch’s warped result [S12]. As training proceeds, the stride $s$ is decreased to focus on more local regions [S12]. The $K \times K$ patch $P$ of stride $s$ containing point $(u, v)$ is defined as: $\mathcal{P}(u, v, s)=\left \{(u +sx, v +sy) \mid x, y \in \left \{0, \ldots , {K}\right \}\right \}$ [S12].

### Coarse-to-Fine Optimization Scheme
An optimization scheme that uses multi-view stereo normal maps for supervision to rapidly converge mesh geometry in the initial phase [S13, unofficial]. The loss function is $L = L_{rgb} + \lambda_1 L_h + \lambda_2 (L_{mask} + L_{normal})$ [S13, unofficial]. After approximately 100 iterations, remeshing (subdivision) is performed, and the loss shifts to $L = L_{rgb} + \lambda'_1 L_h + \lambda_2 L_{mask}$, removing normal supervision to restore fine-grained textures [S13, unofficial]. Remeshing operations are performed at the 100th, 200th, and 400th iterations [S13, unofficial]. The mask loss is $L_{mask} = 1 - IOU(\hat{M}, M)$, where $\hat{M}$ is predicted mask and $M$ is ground truth mask [S13, unofficial]. The learning rate is $lr = 1e-3$ using Adam optimizer, decayed at the 100th, 200th, and 400th iterations, with $\lambda_2 = 50$ for mask and normal losses [S13, unofficial].

### Real-time Novel View Rendering during Training
A procedure to render novel views during training to enable interactive previews by querying the pre-trained NeRF [S14]. It queries $\Theta_{NeRF}$ to obtain the estimated ray termination $x_{term}$ and accumulated alpha $T_\alpha$ [S14]. If $T_\alpha > 0.5$, it uses $x_{term}$ as input to LAENeRF and outputs the rendered image [S14].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of rays and samples per ray, $O(N \times M)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $M$ is the number of samples [S1, S5, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Dominated by the storage of intermediate tensors (`xyzs`, `dirs`, `deltas`, `sigmas`, `rgbs`) during the forward pass, $O(N \times M)$ [S1, S3, S6, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance**: On NeRF-synthetic dataset, the method achieves an average PSNR of 33.09 at 33.7k steps [S9, unofficial].
*   **Scaling**: Scales well with GPU parallelism due to the CUDA implementation of ray marching and compositing [S2, S9, S10, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Differentiable, allowing end-to-end optimization; efficient pruning via occupancy grids; supports adaptive sampling [S1, S2, S7, S9, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Memory-intensive for high-resolution scenes; sensitive to hyperparameters like `dt_gamma` and `T_thresh` [S1, S2, S10, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: During the training phase of NeRF and its variants to compute gradients for network optimization [S1, S3, S5, S10, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: During evaluation or inference where deterministic, high-quality rendering is required without stochastic noise [S2, S5, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the NeRF paper (Mildenhall et al. 2020) [S3, unofficial]. It has been widely adopted in subsequent works such as Instant-NGP [S2, S7, S10, unofficial], MIPNeRF360 [S11, unofficial], and various other NeRF variants [S1, S3, S5, S9, S10, unofficial]. It is a core component of the differentiable volume rendering pipeline [S1, S5, S10, unofficial].
