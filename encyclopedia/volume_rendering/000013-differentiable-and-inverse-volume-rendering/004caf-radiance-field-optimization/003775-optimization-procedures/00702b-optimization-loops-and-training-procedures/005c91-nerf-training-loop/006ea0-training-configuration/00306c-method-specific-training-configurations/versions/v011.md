# Method-Specific Training Configurations


## Overview
This family encompasses the specific hyperparameter settings, architectural tweaks, and optimization routines required to train distinct Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) variants and their extensions. While standard NeRF training follows a general recipe, these methods introduce specific configurations—such as modified activation functions, two-stage training pipelines, or dataset-specific loss adjustments—to address challenges like view-dependent artifacts, computational efficiency, or super-resolution. They set themselves apart from general optimization procedures by providing concrete "recipes" for convergence that distinguish implementations like Mip-NeRF, DONeRF, and NeRF-SR from the baseline.

## Shared principles
The training process for all methods in this family minimizes the difference between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images by optimizing neural network parameters. The core loop involves ray sampling, point sampling, feature encoding, network forward pass, volume rendering, loss computation, and optimization.

*   **Inputs:** Training images, camera intrinsics (focal length), camera extrinsics (poses), and dataset metadata (near/far clipping planes).
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$), and optionally a cached density field or mesh for acceleration.
*   **Ray Bundle:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ for origins and $[B, 3]$ for directions, where $B$ is the batch size in rays.
*   **Query Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, N, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates along rays, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray.
*   **Volume Rendering:** The differentiable volume rendering integral is standard across most methods:
    $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1][S6].
*   **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is the standard choice, typically with $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, and $\epsilon=10^{-7}$ [S4, S7, S8].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md)
Defines the precise numerical values, data structures, and procedural steps for initializing, training, and evaluating standard and variant NeRF models. It sets itself apart by providing the concrete "recipe" for convergence, including specific learning rates, sample counts, and architectural dimensions.

### [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md)
Defines specific implementation details for training Mip-NeRF and JaxNeRF, focusing on activation functions, input encoding strategies, and learning rate schedules. It sets itself apart by replacing ReLU with shifted softplus for density, using a widened sigmoid for color, removing identity concatenation from inputs, and employing a specific warmup learning rate schedule.

### [DONeRF](4461-donerf.md)
A compact neural rendering method that replaces the standard NeRF raymarching scheme with a two-network architecture: a **depth oracle network** to predict sample locations and a **shading network** to compute color and density. It solves the prohibitive computational cost of dense sampling by reducing samples per ray to 2–4. Unlike standard NeRF, it uses a classification-based oracle to guide sampling.

### [AR-NeRF Training Configuration](12407-ar-nerf-training-configuration.md)
An unsupervised method that learns depth and defocus effects from natural images by integrating aperture rendering into a NeRF framework. It differs by explicitly modeling camera aperture size and focus distance as continuous control parameters, enabling variable depth of field. Unlike GAN-based approaches, it relies on photometric constraints and differentiable volume rendering.

### [NeRF-SR Training Configuration](15560-nerf-sr-training-configuration.md)
Designed to synthesize high-resolution novel views from low-resolution input images by exploiting 3D consistency. It differs by supersampling the input resolution during training and optionally applying a post-refinement network to restore high-frequency details, addressing the blurriness of standard NeRF trained on low-res inputs.

### Blender Dataset Simplified Configuration
For the Blender dataset, a simplified configuration is used to prioritize speed over accuracy, as the task involves small synthetic objects on a white background [S1].
*   **Sampling:** One round of sampling only [S1].
*   **Proposal Network:** 128 samples, 4 layers, 256 hidden units [S1].
*   **NeRF MLP:** 32 samples, 8 layers, 256 (or 512) hidden units [S1].
*   **Encoding:** Axis-aligned Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) [S1].
*   **Loss:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) [S1].
*   **Regularization:** No distortion regularizer [S1].

### LLFF Training Configuration
Configurations for the LLFF dataset differ from the Blender setup primarily in loss function and regularization [S1].
*   **Loss:** Charbonnier loss is used instead of MSE [S1].
*   **Regularization:** The distortion regularizer is **not** disabled [S1].
*   **Performance Metrics (256 hidden units):**
    *   mip-NeRF: PSNR 26.93, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.177, Time 2.48 hrs, Params 0.61M [S1].
    *   Our Model: PSNR 26.68, SSIM 0.847, LPIPS 0.150, Time 2.39 hrs, Params 0.84M [S1].
*   **Performance Metrics (512 hidden units):**
    *   mip-NeRF: PSNR 27.01, SSIM 0.845, LPIPS 0.148, Time 6.15 hrs, Params 2.27M [S1].
    *   Our Model: PSNR 26.86, SSIM 0.858, LPIPS 0.128, Time 3.84 hrs, Params 3.23M [S1].
*   **Comparison:** The model does not outperform mip-NeRF in PSNR but yields improved SSIM and LPIPS metrics and a significant speedup when the NeRF MLP is large [S1].

### NeUDF Training Configuration
*   **Optimizer:** ADAM [S2].
*   **Learning Rate:** Warms up to $2 \times 10^{-4}$ in the first 5k iterations, decreases to $1 \times 10^{-5}$ by the end [S2].
*   **Batch Size:** 512 random rays sampled from 8 randomly selected camera poses per iteration [S2].
*   **Training Duration:** 400k iterations total. Takes 9 hours with mask, 11 hours without mask, on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU [S2].

### NeRF-SDP Training Configuration
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S3].
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ for Res-UNet, $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for other parts [S3].
*   **Batch Size:** 500 [S3].
*   **Hardware:** Nvidia GeForce RTX 3090 [S3].
*   **Total Steps:** 250,000. First 170,000 steps optimize auxiliary and rendering losses. Last 80,000 steps optimize only rendering loss [S3].
*   **Feature Dimensions:** 2D features 24, 3D features 8 [S3].
*   **Datasets:** LLFF, IBRNet, Google Scanned [S3].

### Neuralangelo Training Configuration
*   **Sampling:** Samples pixels from images randomly without tracking statistics/errors. Uses long training iterations to reduce stochastics [S4].
*   **ROI:** Assumed to be inside a unit sphere [S4].
*   **Total Iterations:** 500k [S4].
*   **Inactive Hash Resolution:** Feature vectors are set to zero when a given hash resolution is not active [S4].
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ with a linear warmup of 5k iterations. Decays by a factor of 10 at 300k and 400k iterations [S4].
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay of $10^{-2}$. $w_{iek}$ is set to 0.1 [S4].
*   **MLP Architecture:** SDF MLP has one layer; color MLP has four layers [S4].
*   **Batch Size:** 1 for DTU benchmark, 16 for Tanks and Temples dataset [S4].

### GO-NeRF Training Configuration
*   **Optimization:** Optimize the generation model on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU for a total of 20,000 iterations [S5].
*   **Augmentation:** Train 30% of the iterations with background augmentation [S5].
*   **Scheduler:** Values range from 30 to 300 [S5].
*   **Lambda R:** Set to 500 [S5].

### Tri-MipRF Training Configuration
*   **Base Mipmap Shape:** $M_{l0}$: H=512, W=512, C=16 [S6].
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW with weight decay $1 \times 10^{-5}$ and learning rate $2 \times 10^{-3}$ [S6].
*   **Schedule:** MultiStepLR [S6].
*   **LR Scaling:** Tri-Mip encoding learning rate is scaled up 10x relative to the MLP [S6].
*   **Loss:** Area loss $L_{area}$, scaling pixel loss by the area of its footprint on the image plane [S6].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch with tiny-cuda-nn extension and nvdiffrast library [S6].

### NeRF† Implementation Configuration
A variant of nerfacto that uses a standard MLP density field instead of HashMLPDensityField [S7].
*   **Blender Dataset:** $t_n = 2.0$, $t_f = 6.0$. Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, distortion loss, and scene contraction are disabled. Background color is manually set to white. Batch size = 2048 rays. Initial sampler = uniform. Training epochs = $16.5 \times 10^3$ [S7].
*   **LLFF Dataset:** Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, and distortion loss are disabled. Scene contraction uses $\ell_\infty$ norm. Batch size = 1024 rays during training, 2048 during evaluation. Training epochs = $30 \times 10^3$. Leaves scenes use uniform initial proposal sampler [S7].
*   **Performance (Mip-NeRF 360):** PSNR 24.20, SSIM 0.726, LPIPS 0.247 [S7].
*   **Comparison:** Achieves comparable PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS to nerfacto (HashMLPDensityField) but is significantly slower [S7].

## Background
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) were introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1, S7][S9, unofficial]. The field has since evolved with numerous variants optimizing for speed, quality, or specific capabilities like super-resolution or defocus modeling. Mip-NeRF [S1] introduced anti-aliasing via integrated positional encoding. DONeRF [S3] proposed a two-stage oracle-based sampling approach. NeRF-SR [S1] addressed low-resolution inputs. Neuralangelo [S4] focused on high-fidelity surface reconstruction using hash encoding. These methods have become standard baselines and extensions in the differentiable volume rendering community.
