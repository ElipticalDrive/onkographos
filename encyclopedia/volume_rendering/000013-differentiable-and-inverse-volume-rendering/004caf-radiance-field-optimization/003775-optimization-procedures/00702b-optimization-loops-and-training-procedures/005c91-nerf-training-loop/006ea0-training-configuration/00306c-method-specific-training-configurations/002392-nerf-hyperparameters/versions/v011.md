# NeRF Hyperparameters

Also called: NeRF Training Hyperparameters, ViCA-NeRF Training

## Overview
NeRF hyperparameters define the configuration of the training loop, including network architecture dimensions, sampling strategies, optimization schedules, and loss weighting, which collectively determine the convergence speed, rendering quality, and computational cost of Neural Radiance Fields. Unlike generic volume rendering methods, NeRF optimization is highly sensitive to the balance between coarse and fine sampling densities and the specific learning rate decay profiles required to stabilize the implicit surface representation [S1][S7, unofficial].

## How it works
The training process minimizes the photometric difference between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images by optimizing neural network parameters [S1, S9]. The core loop involves ray sampling, point sampling, feature encoding, network forward pass, volume rendering, loss computation, and optimization [Parent].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training images, camera intrinsics (focal length), camera extrinsics (poses), and dataset metadata (near/far clipping planes) [Parent].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$), and optionally a cached density field or mesh for acceleration [Parent].

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Bundle:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ for origins and $[B, 3]$ for directions, where $B$ is the batch size in rays [Parent].
*   **Query Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, N, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates along rays, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray [Parent].
*   **Depth Values:** A tensor of shape $[B, N]$ containing the distance from the camera origin to each query point [Parent].

### Volume Rendering
Compute accumulated color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using the differentiable volume rendering integral:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [Parent].

### Loss Computation
Typically involves photometric loss (MSE or L1) between $\hat{C}$ and ground truth $C_{gt}$ [Parent]. Variants may include perceptual losses (LPIPS) [S2] or regularization terms for occlusion [S12] and geometry [S12].

### Parameters and Typical Values
Standard configurations vary by dataset and variant. Key parameters include:

*   **Network Architecture:**
    *   **NeRF MLP:** Typically 8 layers with 256 or 512 hidden units [S1].
    *   **Proposal MLP:** 4 layers with 256 hidden units [S1].
    *   **Positional Encoding:** 10 frequencies for position, 4 for direction [S6, unofficial].
*   **Sampling:**
    *   **Coarse Samples ($N_c$):** 64 samples per ray is standard for synthetic datasets (Blender/NeRF 360) [S3][S7, unofficial]. Some variants use 128 for proposal networks [S1].
    *   **Fine Samples ($N_f$):** 128 samples per ray for synthetic datasets [S7, unofficial], or 64 for LLFF datasets [S3][S7, unofficial].
    *   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays is common [S3][S6, unofficial], though larger batches (3072 rays) are used to maximize GPU utilization [S4, unofficial].
*   **Optimization:**
    *   **Optimizer:** Adam with $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999, \epsilon=10^{-7}$ [S4, unofficial][S5][S7, unofficial].
    *   **Learning Rate:** Initial rate of $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S4, S7, unofficial], decaying exponentially to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S4, unofficial] or $8 \times 10^{-5}$ [S3].
    *   **Iterations:** 200k to 300k iterations for convergence [S3][S4, S7, unofficial].
*   **Clipping Planes:**
    *   **Near:** 2.0 [S8, unofficial].
    *   **Far:** 6.0 [S8, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode outlines a standard NeRF training step, incorporating hierarchical sampling and Adam optimization.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim

def train_step(model, coarse_model, optimizer, images, poses, intrinsics, args):
    """
    Performs one training step for a NeRF model.
    
    Args:
        model: Fine NeRF MLP.
        coarse_model: Coarse NeRF MLP (used for importance sampling).
        optimizer: Adam optimizer.
        images: Ground truth images tensor [B, H, W, 3].
        poses: Camera pose matrices [B, 4, 4].
        intrinsics: Camera intrinsic parameters.
        args: Configuration object containing hyperparameters.
    """
    # 1. Ray Generation
    # Generate ray origins and directions from camera poses and intrinsics
    ray_origins, ray_directions = generate_rays(poses, intrinsics, args.image_shape)
    
    # 2. Stratified Sampling (Coarse)
    # Sample N_c points along each ray between near and far planes
    # Perturbation is added to avoid aliasing
    coarse_depths = stratified_sample(
        ray_origins, ray_directions, 
        args.near, args.far, 
        args.n_samples_coarse, 
        perturb=True
    )
    
    # 3. Coarse Network Forward Pass
    # Query coarse model to get density and color
    coarse_points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + coarse_depths.unsqueeze(2) * ray_directions.unsqueeze(1)
    coarse_sigma, coarse_color = coarse_model(coarse_points, ray_directions)
    
    # 4. Importance Sampling (Fine)
    # Use coarse weights to sample more points in high-density regions
    coarse_weights = coarse_sigma * torch.exp(-torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(coarse_sigma[:, :1]), 
                                                           torch.cumsum(coarse_sigma[:, :-1], dim=1)]) * 
                                             torch.diff(torch.cat([args.near.unsqueeze(1), coarse_depths], dim=1), dim=1))
    fine_depths = importance_sample(coarse_depths, coarse_weights, args.n_samples_fine)
    
    # 5. Fine Network Forward Pass
    fine_points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + fine_depths.unsqueeze(2) * ray_directions.unsqueeze(1)
    fine_sigma, fine_color = model(fine_points, ray_directions)
    
    # 6. Volume Rendering
    # Compute transmittance and accumulated color
    deltas = torch.cat([fine_depths[:, 1:] - fine_depths[:, :-1], 
                        torch.tensor([args.far - fine_depths[:, -1]])], dim=1)
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-fine_sigma * deltas)
    T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha + 1e-10, dim=1)[:, :-1]
    weights = alpha * T
    rendered_color = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * fine_color, dim=1)
    
    # 7. Loss Computation
    # L1 loss between rendered and ground truth colors
    loss = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered_color - images))
    
    # Optional: Add regularization terms (e.g., occlusion, geometry)
    # loss += args.lambda_occ * compute_occlusion_regularization(fine_sigma, ray_origins)
    
    # 8. Optimization Step
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    
    return loss.item()

def stratified_sample(origins, directions, near, far, n_samples, perturb=True):
    """
    Divides the ray segment [near, far] into N bins and samples uniformly.
    """
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., steps=n_samples)
    t_vals = torch.stack([near * (1. - t_vals) + far * t_vals], dim=-1)
    
    if perturb:
        # Add uniform noise to each sample
        mids = 0.5 * (t_vals[..., 1:] + t_vals[..., :-1])
        upper = torch.cat([mids, t_vals[..., -1:]], dim=-1)
        lower = torch.cat([t_vals[..., :1], mids], dim=-1)
        t_rand = torch.rand_like(t_vals)
        t_vals = lower * (1. - t_rand) + upper * t_rand
        
    return t_vals

def importance_sample(depths, weights, n_samples):
    """
    Resamples points based on the density weights from the coarse pass.
    """
    # Compute CDF from weights
    cdf = torch.cumsum(weights, dim=-1)
    cdf = cdf / cdf[..., -1:]
    
    # Sample uniformly in [0, 1] and invert CDF
    u = torch.rand_like(cdf)
    # Find indices where u falls in the CDF bins
    indices = torch.searchsorted(cdf, u, side='left')
    
    # Interpolate depths at these indices
    # Note: Simplified interpolation; full implementation requires bin edge handling
    sampled_depths = interpolate_depths(depths, indices)
    return sampled_depths
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **View-Dependent Artifacts:** Overfitting of the view-dependent MLP can cause direction-dependent flickering in motion [S7, unofficial]. Mitigation includes reducing the Fourier encoding dimensionality for directions ($L=1$ instead of $L=4$) or using a smaller direction-dependent cache resolution ($16^3$ to $64^3$) [S7, unofficial].
*   **Scene Scale:** The magnitude of optimized density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ is tied to the domain of integration. Scaling the scene requires inversely scaling the density to preserve appearance (Alpha Invariance) [10127-alpha-invariance-inverse-scaling.md].
*   **Nadir Perspective:** For aerial imagery, nadir camera perspectives allow the omission of background NeRFs as sky pixels are not visible [S10].
*   **Exposure:** RawNeRF variants may require learned exposure scaling offsets to handle varying illumination [S11, unofficial].

### NeRF Training Configuration for View-Dependent Artifacts
Mitigates direction-dependent flickering artifacts on synthetic datasets caused by overfitting of the view-dependent MLP [S7, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself by adjusting the Fourier feature encoding of view directions ($L=1$) or using a smaller direction-dependent cache resolution ($16^3$ to $64^3$) [S7, unofficial]. This is chosen when motion artifacts are observed in the rendered video [S7, unofficial].

### RoGUENeRF Training and Loss
A training configuration for a robust geometry-consistent universal enhancer for NeRF [S9]. It differs by pre-training on LLFF dataset renders for 3000 epochs and fine-tuning on new scenes for 1 hour per scene [S9]. The loss function combines L1 and perceptual losses: $L = |\hat {H}_{i} - H_i|_{1} + 10^{-3}|\omega (\hat {H}_{i}) - \omega (H_i)|_{1}$, where $\omega$ is a pre-trained VGG-19 [S9]. It is chosen for enhancing NeRF outputs with geometric consistency [S9].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training typically requires 200–300k iterations [S3][S4, S7, unofficial]. On a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, this takes approximately 17–25 hours for standard scenes [S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on batch size and chunk size. A batch size of 1024 rays with 128 samples requires significant VRAM, often necessitating chunking (e.g., 4096 points per chunk) to avoid out-of-memory errors [S8, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Blender dataset with 256 hidden units, a model with proposal networks achieves PSNR 32.96, SSIM 0.960, and LPIPS 0.043 in 1.86 hours, compared to mip-NeRF's 2.89 hours [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Training time quadruples when increasing sampling resolution from 32x32 to 64x64 samples [S5]. The relative speedup of proposal-based methods increases as model capacity rises [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Hierarchical sampling improves rendering quality with minimal overhead [S1].
*   **Limitations:** High computational cost for large scenes; sensitive to hyperparameter tuning for learning rate and sampling densities [S1][S7, unofficial].

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1][S7, S8, unofficial]. The method uses a continuous volumetric scene function parameterized by a non-linear MLP to render novel views from unstructured input images. The training hyperparameters described here are derived from the original NeRF implementation and subsequent optimizations such as mip-NeRF [S1], FastNeRF [S7, unofficial], and various extensions for large-scale scenes (Mega-NeRF [S10]) and editing (ViCA-NeRF [S2]).
