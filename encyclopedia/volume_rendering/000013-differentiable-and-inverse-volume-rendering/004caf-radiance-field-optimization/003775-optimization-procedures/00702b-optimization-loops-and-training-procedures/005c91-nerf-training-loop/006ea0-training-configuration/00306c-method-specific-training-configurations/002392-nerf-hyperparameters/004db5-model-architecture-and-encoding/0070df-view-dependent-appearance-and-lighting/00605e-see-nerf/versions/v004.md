# See-NeRF

Also called: See-NeRF framework, Sensor-Physics Grounded Deblurring HDR NeRF

## Overview
See-NeRF is a unified sensor-physics grounded NeRF framework for sharp High Dynamic Range (HDR) novel view synthesis from single-exposure blurry Low Dynamic Range (LDR) images and corresponding event data [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of recovering sharp 3D radiance from degraded inputs by explicitly modeling the physical imaging process of both RGB and event sensors, rather than applying tone mapping directly to 3D points [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods like [REF2-NeRF](18124-ref2-nerf.md) or EvHDR-NeRF which ignore sensor-physics mismatches, See-NeRF bridges the gap between physical scene dynamics and sensor output using differentiable Camera Response Function (CRF) models for both RGB and event streams [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A single blurry LDR RGB image $\mathcal{I}_{LDR}$, corresponding event data $B$, camera poses $p(t)$, and exposure time $\Delta t_{exp}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Sharp HDR novel view images and LDR images at arbitrary exposures [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Network ($F_\theta$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with parameters $\theta$ [S1, unofficial]. It takes positional encodings of 3D position $\mathbf{o}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ as input [S1, unofficial].
    *   Input: $\gamma_o(\mathbf{o}), \gamma_d(\mathbf{d})$.
    *   Output: Raw HDR radiance $\mathbf{e}$ and density $\sigma$.
*   **RGB CRF Model:** Three separate MLPs ($f_{crf}$), one for each color channel (R, G, B) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Event CRF Model:**
    *   Delay Coefficient MLP ($f_{ev}$): Outputs scalar $\epsilon_i$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Offset Estimator ($h$): Estimates temporal delay offset from input events [S1, unofficial].
*   **Positional Encoding ($\gamma_M$):**
    $$ \gamma_M(x) = \{\sin(2^m\pi x), \cos(2^m\pi x)\}_{m=0}^{M} $$ [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Representation:** For a 3D point $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$, compute raw HDR radiance $\mathbf{e}$ and density $\sigma$ using the NeRF network:
    $$ (\mathbf{e}, \sigma) = F_{\theta}(\gamma_o(\mathbf{o}), \gamma_d(\mathbf{d})) $$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Time Discretization:** Discretely sample $b+1$ time points $\{t_i\}_{i=0}^b$ within the exposure interval $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$ [S1, unofficial]. The parameter $b$ is typically set to 4 [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Volume Rendering (Latent HDR):** For each time point $t_i$, render the latent sharp HDR radiance $E(x,y,p(t_i))$ at pixel $(x,y)$ using standard volume rendering:
    $$ E(x,y,p(t)) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1-\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{e}_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance, $\delta_i$ is the distance between adjacent sampled 3D points, and $N$ is the number of samples per ray [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Raw HDR Pixel Calculation:** Calculate the predicted raw HDR pixel value by summing the weighted radiance values at the sampled time points:
    $$ \hat{\mathcal{I}}_{HDR}(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^{b} w_i E_i(x,y,p(t_i)) $$
    where $w_i$ are weights calculated based on the time points $\{t_i\}$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **RGB CRF Application:** Convert the raw HDR pixel value to LDR using the learned RGB CRF models in the log domain [S1, unofficial]. For each color channel $c \in \{R, G, B\}$:
    $$ \hat{\mathcal{I}}_{LDR}^c(x,y) = f_{crf}^c(\ln(\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{HDR}^c(x,y) \Delta t_{exp})) $$
    where $\Delta t_{exp} = t_{end} - t_{start}$ is the exposure time [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Event CRF - Bayer Adaptation:** Input the $b+1$ scene radiance values $\{E(x,y,p(t_i))\}_{i=0}^b$ into an RGGB Bayer pattern to obtain event sensor pixel radiance values $E_{ev}(x,y,p(t_i))$ [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Event CRF - Delay Coefficient:** Calculate the delay coefficient $\epsilon_i$ for each time step using the event CRF MLP:
    $$ \epsilon_i = f_{ev}(E_{ev}(x,y,p(t_i))) $$ [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Event CRF - Latency Synthesis:** Synthesize event latency using a second-order low-pass filter [S1, unofficial]. Initialize $L^0_{lp} = E_{ev}(p(t_0))$. For $i > 0$:
    $$ L^i_{lp} = (1-\epsilon_i)L^{i-1}_{lp} + \epsilon_i E_{ev}(p(t_i)) $$ [S1, unofficial].

9.  **Event CRF - Ideal Event Count:** Calculate the estimated number of events $\hat{B}'(t_i, t_{i+1})$ between time steps $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$ using the contrast threshold $\Theta$:
    $$ \hat{B}'(t_i, t_{i+1}) = \text{floor}\left(\frac{\ln(L^{i+1}_{lp}) - \ln(L^{i}_{lp})}{\Theta}\right) $$ [S1, unofficial].

10. **Event CRF - Delay Compensation:** Compensate for temporal delay by estimating an offset $h(B(t_i, t_{i+1}))$ from the input events and subtracting it to get the final estimated event count:
    $$ \hat{B}(t_i, t_{i+1}) = \hat{B}'(t_i, t_{i+1}) - h(B(t_i, t_{i+1})) $$ [S1, unofficial].

11. **Optimization:** Jointly optimize the NeRF network parameters $\theta$ and the CRF model parameters by minimizing the combined loss function:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \lambda \mathcal{L}_{evs} + \mathcal{L}_{ldr} $$
    where:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{ldr} = \sum_{(x,y)\in \mathcal{X}} ||\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{LDR}(x,y) - \mathcal{I}_{LDR}(x,y)||^2_2 $$
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{evs} = \sum_{(x,y)\in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{i=0}^{b-1} ||\hat{B}(t_i, t_{i+1}) - B(t_i, t_{i+1})||^2_2 $$
    $\mathcal{X}$ denotes all pixels of the camera sensor [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $b$: Number of time intervals minus one. Value: 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Weight for event loss. Value: 0.005 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Theta$: Contrast threshold in event generation. Definition: Minimum detectable brightness change lower bound [S1, unofficial].
*   $\Delta t_{exp}$: Exposure time ($t_{end} - t_{start}$) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sensor-Physics Mismatch:** Traditional methods apply tone mapping directly to 3D points, ignoring that CRFs apply to pixel-level integrals. This leads to suboptimal HDR and deblurring results [S1, unofficial].
*   **Low-Light Delay:** Event generation exhibits significant delay and uncertainty in low-light regions where brightness changes are below the threshold $\Theta$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pose Estimation:** Camera poses $p(t)$ change during exposure in handheld scenarios. The method relies on event-guided COLMAP for pose estimation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Achieves state-of-the-art HDR and deblurring novel view synthesis results on synthetic and real datasets compared to EvHDR-NeRF and HDR-NeRF [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Physically consistent 3D representation; handles single-exposure blurry LDR images and events; explicitly models sensor physics for both RGB and event cameras [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires corresponding event data; relies on accurate pose estimation (event-guided COLMAP); joint optimization of multiple networks may be computationally intensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When reconstructing sharp HDR scenes from single-exposure blurry images and event streams, particularly in extreme lighting conditions [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When only standard LDR images are available without events, or when real-time performance is critical and the overhead of joint CRF optimization is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].

## Background
See-NeRF was proposed by researchers from iCVTEAM [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of prior works like EvHDR-NeRF, which extended HDR-NeRF by applying tone mapping to 3D points without considering event generation characteristics [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the physical imaging principles of RGB and event sensors, leveraging spatial and temporal dynamic information in events to enhance sharp HDR 3D representation learning [S1, unofficial].
