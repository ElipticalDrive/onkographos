# Controllable Radiance Fields (CoRF)
## Overview
CoRF is a method for generalizable, 3D-aware, and motion-controllable synthesis of face dynamics, generating never-before-seen faces with controlled dynamic motions and viewpoints [S1]. It extends the NeRF paradigm by integrating a style-based generator that decouples identity and motion, allowing for intuitive control over facial expressions while maintaining 3D consistency [S1]. Unlike standard NeRFs that reconstruct a single static scene, CoRF learns a latent space for motion representations that can be interpolated or sampled to synthesize novel dynamic behaviors [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
The generator $G$ takes a noise vector $z$, a motion representation $m$, a camera pose $\xi$, and 3D position $x$ as inputs, and outputs a rendered RGB image $I_0$ [S1].
**Data Structures:**
Exact layouts, field sizes, and bit packing are not given in the sources. The method uses the following vectors:
*   **Motion Representation ($m$):** A vector representing facial expression or dynamic motion state [S1].
*   **Noise Vector ($z$):** A latent vector sampled from a prior distribution to control identity and fine details [S1].
*   **Style Vector ($w$):** The output of the noise mapping module, defined as $w = f_z(z)$ [S1].
*   **Camera Pose ($\xi$):** A condition vector for the discriminator [S1].
**Numbered Steps:**
1. The generator $G$ applies two mapping modules, $f_z$ and $f_m$, before rendering [S1].
2. The noise mapping module $f_z$ maps the noise vector $z$ to a style vector $w$ [S1].
3. The motion mapping module $f_m$ embeds the motion representation $m$ into a layered latent motion space [S1].
4. The embedded motion representation is combined with the style vector $w$ via summation ($\oplus$) [S1].
5. The combined features are passed to the style-based synthesis module $G_s$ along with position $x$ and camera pose $\xi$ to render the image $I_0$ [S1].
6. The discriminator $D$ compares real and generated images, conditioned on $\xi$ and $m$, using an adversarial loss $\mathcal{L}_{adv}$ to ensure photo-realism [S1].
7. The regressor $R$ estimates motion from images to supervise motion control [S1].
8. To preserve consistency over views and poses, $R$ and an identity encoder $E$ extract features from paired images with the same identity but different motions [S1].
9. A consistency loss $\mathcal{L}_{consist}$ and an identity loss $\mathcal{L}_{id}$ encourage these extracted features to be similar [S1].
10. A motion reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{motion}$ minimizes the distance between the estimated motion from $R$ and the conditioned motion $m$ [S1].
**Formulas:**
Generator composition:
$$ G = \{f_z, f_m, G_s\} $$
where $f_z$ maps noise to style, $f_m$ maps motion, and $G_s$ is the synthesis module [S1].
Style vector definition:
$$ w = f_z(z) $$
where $w$ is combined with the motion embedding via summation [S1].
**Parameters:**
*   **Motion representation $m$:** Input to the generator and discriminator, representing facial expression/motion [S1].
*   **Noise vector $z$:** Input to the generator, processed by $f_z$ to produce style vector $w$ [S1].
*   **Style vector $w$:** Output of $f_z(z)$, combined with motion embedding [S1].
*   **Camera pose $\xi$:** Condition for the discriminator $D$ [S1].
Typical values are not given in the sources.
**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Motion Control:** Effective motion control requires the embedding of $m$ into a layered latent space via $f_m$ [S1].
*   **Consistency:** Spatio-temporal consistency is ensured by the regressor $R$ and encoder $E$ [S1].

## Background
CoRF was proposed to address limitations in existing 3D-aware generative models. Prior methods such as GRAF and π-GAN operate on randomly sampled camera poses and latent vectors to produce radiance fields via MLPs, while StyleNeRF and CIPS-3D combine shallow NeRFs with 2D rendering networks for high-resolution details, and LolNeRF optimizes foreground and background NeRFs for 3D objects [S1]. CoRF specifically targets the generalizable, 3D-aware, and motion-controllable synthesis of face dynamics [S1]. It enables the generation of never-before-seen faces with controlled dynamic motions and viewpoints [S1].
