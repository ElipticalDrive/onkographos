# DDIM Sampling with Classifier-Free Guidance

Also called: Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models, DDIM Sampling, DDIM Sampling Step (with CFG & v-parameterization)

## Overview
DDIM Sampling with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is a deterministic (or stochastic) reverse diffusion algorithm that generates samples or refines existing representations by iteratively denoising a latent variable $x_t$ to estimate the clean latent $x_0$ [S1, S2, unofficial]. It builds on [DDIM Sampling and Denoising Mechanisms](14430-diffusion-sampling-and-denoising-mechanisms.md) by skipping intermediate noise steps to allow for fewer sampling iterations than standard DDPM while maintaining sample quality [S2, unofficial]. It differs from its sibling [DDPM Denoise Function Network](11102-ddpm-denoise-function-network.md) by using a fixed noise schedule and deterministic transition equations, and it incorporates CFG to steer generation towards conditional inputs (e.g., text or image features) by combining unconditional and conditional model predictions [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Current latent $x_t$ at timestep $t$.
- Conditioning information $c$ (e.g., embeddings).
- Unconditional conditioning $c_{uncond}$ (e.g., empty string or null vector).
- Timestep $t$ (integer).
- Schedule parameters: $\alpha_t, \bar{\alpha}_t$ (cumulative alphas), $\sigma_t$ (noise scale).
- Guidance scale $s$ (scalar, typically $>1$).
- Stochasticity parameter $\eta$ (scalar, typically $0$ for deterministic).

**Outputs:**
- Previous latent $x_{t-1}$.
- Predicted clean latent $\hat{x}_0$.

**Data Structures:**
The scheduler maintains buffers on the device (e.g., CUDA) for the diffusion schedule [S2, unofficial]:
- `alphas`: Tensor of shape $(T)$, $\alpha_t$.
- `alphas_prev`: Tensor of shape $(T)$, $\alpha_{t-1}$ (or $\alpha_{t-1}$ mapped to the subsampled timestep).
- `ddim_sigmas`: Tensor of shape $(T)$, $\sigma_t$.
- Derived buffers: `sqrt_alphas_cumprod`, `sqrt_one_minus_alphas_cumprod`, etc., all `torch.float32` [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- `num_inference_steps`: Number of sampling steps. Default: 50 [S1, unofficial].
- `unconditional_guidance_scale` (or `guidance_scale`): CFG multiplier. Default: 1.0 (disables guidance), typical values 7.5–100 [S1, S2, unofficial].
- `ddim_eta`: Controls stochasticity. Default: 0.0 [S2, unofficial].
- `temperature`: Scales injected noise. Default: 1.0 [S2, unofficial].
- `noise_dropout`: Dropout probability for noise. Default: 0.0 [S2, unofficial].
- `parameterization`: Either `"eps"` (noise prediction) or `"v"` (v-prediction) [S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1. **Initialization:**
   If $x_T$ is not provided, sample $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
   Set timesteps $T$ using a discretization strategy (e.g., uniform or linear) [S2, unofficial].
   Calculate schedule buffers $\alpha_t, \sigma_t$ for the selected timesteps [S2, unofficial].

2. **Iterate backwards through timesteps:**
   For each timestep $t$ in reversed order from $T$ to $1$:
   
   a. **Prepare Model Input:**
      Concatenate the current latent $x_t$ for both conditional and unconditional branches:
      $$ x_{in} = [x_t, x_t] $$
      Concatenate conditioning embeddings:
      $$ c_{in} = [c, c_{uncond}] $$
      (If CFG is disabled ($s=1$), skip concatenation and use only $c$) [S2, unofficial].

   b. **Predict Noise/Output:**
      Pass $x_{in}$ and $c_{in}$ through the denoiser network $f_\theta$:
      $$ \text{output} = f_\theta(x_{in}, t, c_{in}) $$
      Split output into conditional prediction $e_t^{cond}$ and unconditional prediction $e_t^{uncond}$ [S2, unofficial].

   c. **Apply Classifier-Free Guidance:**
      Compute the guided prediction $e_t$:
      $$ e_t = e_t^{uncond} + s \cdot (e_t^{cond} - e_t^{uncond}) $$
      If `parameterization == "v"`, convert the v-prediction output to noise estimate $\epsilon_t$ using the model's helper function (e.g., `predict_eps_from_z_and_v`) [S2, unofficial].

   d. **Compute Predicted Clean Latent $\hat{x}_0$:**
      - If `parameterization == "eps"`:
        $$ \hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{1 - \alpha_t} \cdot e_t}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} $$
      - If `parameterization == "v"`:
        Use model-specific function `predict_start_from_z_and_v` [S2, unofficial].

   e. **Quantize (Optional):**
      If `quantize_denoised` is true, quantize $\hat{x}_0$ via the first-stage model's quantizer [S2, unofficial].

   f. **Compute Direction to $x_t$:**
      $$ \text{dir\_xt} = \sqrt{1 - \alpha_{t-1} - \sigma_t^2} \cdot e_t $$
      (Note: $\alpha_{t-1}$ corresponds to the previous timestep in the subsampled schedule) [S2, unofficial].

   g. **Sample Noise:**
      Generate standard normal noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
      Apply dropout if `noise_dropout > 0` [S2, unofficial].
      $$ \text{noise} = \sigma_t \cdot \epsilon \cdot \text{temperature} $$
      (If $\eta > 0$, $\sigma_t$ is scaled by $\eta$; if $\eta=0$, $\sigma_t=0$ for deterministic DDIM) [S2, unofficial].

   h. **Update Latent:**
      $$ x_{t-1} = \sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}} \cdot \hat{x}_0 + \text{dir\_xt} + \text{noise} $$

3. **Final Output:**
   Return $x_0$ after the final step [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Masking:** If inpainting is used, the original image $x_0$ must be provided to compute the masked noisy image $x_{orig} = q(x_0, t)$, and the result is blended: $x = x_{orig} \cdot \text{mask} + x_{prev} \cdot (1 - \text{mask})$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Conditioning Shape:** If conditioning is a dict/list, ensure batch sizes match; otherwise, warnings or errors may occur [S2, unofficial].
- **Dynamic Thresholding:** Not implemented in standard DDIM; raises `NotImplementedError` if enabled [S2, unofficial].
- **Score Corrector:** Only supports `"eps"` parameterization; raises assertion error otherwise [S2, unofficial].
- **UCG Schedule:** If using a dynamic guidance schedule, its length must exactly match the number of timesteps [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### NeRF Interpolation via DDIM and SLERP
A procedure to generate smooth transitions between 3D samples by interpolating in the diffusion latent space before deterministic decoding [S3]. It samples two initial diffusion latents $x^{(T)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, interpolates them using spherical linear interpolation (SLERP), and passes the result through a deterministic solver [S3]. Standard Gaussian diffusion models often produce non-smooth interpolation; however, models trained with early stopping for sparse-view reconstruction yield smoother transitions than those trained with long unconditional schedules [S3]. Used in SSDNeRF [S3].

### DDIM Stochastic Encode
Adds noise to an initial image $x_0$ according to the diffusion process at timestep $t$, using either DDPM or DDIM schedule parameters [S2, unofficial]. It selects schedule parameters based on `use_original_steps` (DDPM vs DDIM alphas), generates noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, and computes $x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon$ [S2, unofficial].

### DDIM Decode
Decodes a latent representation $x_{latent}$ into a sample by running the DDIM sampling process from a starting timestep $t_{start}$ down to 0 [S2, unofficial]. It selects timesteps (either original DDPM steps or subsampled DDIM steps), reverses the order, and iteratively calls `p_sample_ddim` to update the latent [S2, unofficial].

### DDIM Sampling for Interpolation
Uses DDIM to sample shapes for interpolation [S4]. Typical settings include 25 steps for cars and planes, and 250 steps for chairs [S4]. Note: DDIM setting may produce visually worse-quality meshes compared to DDPM setting in some contexts [S4].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot C)$, where $N$ is the number of sampling steps and $C$ is the cost of one denoiser forward pass. DDIM allows $N$ to be significantly smaller than DDPM (e.g., 50 vs 1000) while maintaining quality [S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(H \cdot W \cdot C)$ for the latent tensor, plus $O(T)$ for schedule buffers [S2, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Deterministic sampling (eta=0) is faster and reproducible. Stochastic sampling (eta>0) adds variance but may improve diversity [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Fewer steps than DDPM; supports CFG for conditional generation; deterministic option for stable training/refinement [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Quality may degrade with very few steps compared to DDPM; interpolation can be discontinuous if the model is over-trained [S3, S4].
- **When to Use:** When fast sampling is required, or when conditional guidance is needed for 3D generation/refinement [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** When maximum sample diversity is required (use DDPM with eta>0) or when high-fidelity mesh generation is critical and DDPM interpolation is preferred [S4].

## Background
DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) was introduced as a deterministic sampling method for diffusion models [S2, unofficial]. It allows for fewer sampling steps by skipping intermediate timesteps and using a fixed noise schedule [S2, unofficial]. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) was later integrated to allow conditional generation without a separate classifier [S1, S2, unofficial]. In the context of volume rendering and NeRFs, DDIM with CFG is used for 3D generation, interpolation, and refinement of radiance fields [S1, unofficial][S3].
