# Renoised Score Distillation (RSD)


## Overview
Renoised Score Distillation (RSD) is a score-distillation objective for 2D image resolution and super-resolution, specifically designed to refine latent vectors during the upscaling stage of iterative 3D synchronization [S1]. It combines features from ancestral sampling and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to generate sharp, high-resolution images that remain consistent with low-resolution (LR) conditioning [S1]. Unlike standard SDS which optimizes in the $z_0$ space, RSD formulates the loss in the latent space of intermediate timesteps, guiding the image towards the predicted denoised latent $\hat{z}_{t-1}$ over multiple noise predictions [S1]. This approach produces sharper details than SDS while maintaining better LR consistency than default ancestral sampling [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Latent $z_0$: The encoded latent of the interpolated low-resolution image [S1].
    *   Conditioning $y$: A concatenation of text prompt embeddings ($y_{text}$), noise level embeddings ($y_{noise\_level}$), and the LR image embedding ($I_{lr}$) [S1].
    *   Timestep bounds: $t_{min}$ and $t_{max}$ [S1].
    *   Hyperparameters: Learning rate ($1 \times 10^{-2}$) and number of super-resolution iterations ($max\_sr\_iter$) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Refined latent residuals $h_\theta$: A zero-initialized tensor of the same shape as $z_0$, containing the high-resolution details [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $h_\theta$: Learnable latent residuals, initialized to zero [S1].
*   $z'_0$: Refined latent, computed as $z_0 + h_\theta$ [S1].
*   $\epsilon_\phi$: The noise prediction network (UNet backbone of SD$\times$4), parameterized by $\phi$ [S1].
*   $\bar{\alpha}_t$: The cumulative noise schedule coefficient at timestep $t$ [S1].
*   $\sigma_t$: The standard deviation of Gaussian noise samples at timestep $t$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  Initialize learnable latent residuals $h_\theta = 0$ with the same shape as $z_0$ [S1].
2.  Loop for $sr\_iter$ from $0$ to $max\_sr\_iter$:
    a.  Sample noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ [S1].
    b.  Construct conditioning $y = y_{text} + y_{noise\_level} + I_{lr}$ [S1].
    c.  Calculate current timestep $t$ using a linearly decreasing schedule:
        $$ t = t_{max} - (t_{max} - t_{min}) \cdot \frac{sr\_iter}{max\_sr\_iter} $$
        [S1]
    d.  Compute the refined latent $z'_0$:
        $$ z'_0 = z_0 + h_\theta $$
        [S1]
    e.  Apply the forward noising process to $z'_0$ at timestep $t$ to obtain $z'_t$:
        $$ z'_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} z'_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon $$
        [S1]
    f.  Apply the forward noising process to $z'_0$ at timestep $t-1$ to obtain $z'_{t-1}$:
        $$ z'_{t-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} z'_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} \epsilon $$
        [S1]
    g.  Pass $z'_t$ and conditioning $y$ to the noise prediction network $\epsilon_\phi$ to predict the noise residual:
        $$ \hat{\epsilon}_\phi = \epsilon_\phi(z'_t, y, t) $$
        [S1]
    h.  Construct the predicted denoised latent $\hat{z}_{t-1}$ using the DDPM reverse process equation:
        $$ \hat{z}_{t-1} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}_t}} z'_t - \sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \hat{\epsilon}_\phi + \sigma_t \epsilon $$
        [S1]
    i.  Compute the L1 loss between the current noised latent $z'_{t-1}$ and the predicted denoised latent $\hat{z}_{t-1}$:
        $$ L_{RSD} = \| z'_{t-1} - \hat{z}_{t-1} \| $$
        [S1]
    j.  Backpropagate gradients through $z'_{t-1}$ towards $h_\theta$ and take a gradient descent step:
        $$ h_\theta \leftarrow h_\theta - \eta \nabla_{h_\theta} L_{RSD} $$
        [S1]
3.  Return the refined latent residuals $h_\theta$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **RSD Loss:**
    $$ L_{RSD} = \| z_{t-1} - \hat{z}_{t-1} \| $$
    where $z_{t-1}$ is the current noised latent at $t-1$ and $\hat{z}_{t-1}$ is the predicted denoised latent from $z_t$ [S1].
*   **Gradient:**
    $$ \nabla_\theta L_{RSD} = \frac{\partial \| z_{t-1} - \hat{z}_{t-1} \|}{\partial z_{t-1}} \frac{\partial z_{t-1}}{\partial \theta} $$
    [S1]
*   **Relation to SDS:**
    RSD can be interpreted as a renoised variant of SDS [S1]. While SDS optimizes in $z_0$ space as $\nabla_\theta L_{SDS} = \gamma(t) \sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} (z_0 - \hat{z}_0) \frac{\partial z_0}{\partial \theta}$, RSD applies the forward noising process to both $z_0$ and $\hat{z}_0$ towards time $t-1$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Scheduler:** DDPM scheduler [S1].
*   **Time Schedule:** Linearly decreasing time schedule following ancestral sampling, from $t_{max}$ to $t_{min}$ [S1].
*   **Patch Sampling:** Uniform random crops of $128 \times 128$ resolution in latent space (corresponding to $512 \times 512$ image patches) are used for optimization [S1].
*   **Text Prompt:** Fixed prompt: `<subject>, high resolution, 4K, photo`, where `<subject>` is the scene name [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** Constant $1 \times 10^{-2}$ for all learnable parameters [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Inconsistency:** Default ancestral sampling can generate details structurally inconsistent with the conditioned LR image, aggravating multi-view inconsistency [S1].
*   **Detail Limitation:** SDS produces LR-consistent features but with limited details [S1].
*   **Implementation Note:** In practice, $\hat{z}_{t-1}$ can be obtained directly from $\hat{\epsilon}_\phi(z_t, y, t)$ using the DDPM denoising equation instead of renoising $\hat{z}_0$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def RSD(z0, y_text, y_noise_level, I_lr, t_min, t_max, max_sr_iter, lr, alpha_bar, sigma_schedule):
    """
    Renoised Score Distillation optimization loop.
    
    Args:
        z0: Initial latent (H, W, C)
        y_text: Text prompt embeddings
        y_noise_level: Noise level embeddings
        I_lr: Low-resolution image embedding
        t_min: Minimum timestep
        t_max: Maximum timestep
        max_sr_iter: Number of super-resolution iterations
        lr: Learning rate
        alpha_bar: Array of cumulative noise schedule coefficients [G]
        sigma_schedule: Array of Gaussian noise standard deviations [G]
        
    Returns:
        h_theta: Refined latent residuals (H, W, C)
    """
    # Initialize learnable latent residuals
    h_theta = zeros_like(z0)

    for sr_iter in range(max_sr_iter):
        # 1. Sample noise
        epsilon = randn_like(z0)

        # 2. Construct conditioning
        y = concatenate([y_text, y_noise_level, I_lr])

        # 3. Calculate timestep t (linearly decreasing)
        t = t_max - (t_max - t_min) * (sr_iter / max_sr_iter)

        # 4. Compute refined latent z'_0
        z0_prime = z0 + h_theta

        # 5. Forward noising at t
        alpha_t = alpha_bar[t]
        zt_prime = sqrt(alpha_t) * z0_prime + sqrt(1 - alpha_t) * epsilon

        # 6. Forward noising at t-1
        alpha_t_minus_1 = alpha_bar[t - 1]
        zt_minus_1_prime = sqrt(alpha_t_minus_1) * z0_prime + sqrt(1 - alpha_t_minus_1) * epsilon

        # 7. Predict noise using the diffusion model backbone
        epsilon_phi = noise_predictor(zt_prime, y, t)

        # 8. Construct predicted denoised latent z_hat_{t-1}
        sigma_t = sigma_schedule[t]
        z_hat_t_minus_1 = sqrt(1 / alpha_t) * zt_prime - sqrt(1 / (1 - alpha_t)) * epsilon_phi + sigma_t * epsilon

        # 9. Compute L1 loss
        loss = mean(abs(zt_minus_1_prime - z_hat_t_minus_1))

        # 10. Backpropagate and update
        loss.backward()
        with no_grad():
            h_theta -= lr * h_theta.grad
            h_theta.grad.zero_()

    return h_theta
```

## Subtopics

### Iterative 3D Synchronization (I3DS)
A framework that alternates between upscaling LR rendered images with diffusion models (using RSD) and updating the underlying 3D representation (NeRF) with standard training [S1].

### DiSR-NeRF
The overall method that utilizes I3DS and RSD to achieve high-quality super-resolution NeRF using only LR training images and a pretrained 2D diffusion upscaler (SD$\times$4) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The optimization time for DiSR-NeRF (which includes RSD) is longer than standard NGP or NeRF-SR due to the RSD optimization steps, but it is faster than DreamFusion-SDS because I3DS segregates upscaling and synchronization stages [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses dynamic ray sampling to increase ray count when the occupancy grid is pruned, optimizing GPU memory usage [S1]. Patch sampling ($128 \times 128$ latent space) is used to further optimize speed and memory compared to full image optimization [S1].
*   **Performance:** RSD produces sharper details compared to SDS and better LR consistency compared to ancestral sampling [S1]. Quantitative results on synthetic and real-world datasets show DiSR-NeRF outperforms existing baselines (NGP, SD$\times$4, NeRF-SR, DreamFusion, IN2N) in PSNR and SSIM [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Generates sharp, high-resolution images consistent with LR conditioning; alleviates the need for high-resolution reference images or large-scale multi-view HR datasets [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a pretrained 2D diffusion upscaler (SD$\times$4); optimization time is increased compared to non-diffusion methods [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced in the paper "DiSR-NeRF: Diffusion-Guided View-Consistent Super-Resolution NeRF" [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the limitations of standard NeRF super-resolution methods, which either lack detail (SDS) or suffer from multi-view inconsistency (ancestral sampling) [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in the upscaling stage of the I3DS framework for super-resolving NeRFs from low-resolution inputs [S1].
