# HumanNeRF

Also called: HumanNeRF (Generalized)

## Overview
HumanNeRF is a neural representation for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans that generalizes to unseen subjects and camera settings using sparse RGB inputs, avoiding per-scene training through aggregated pixel-alignment features and a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field [S2]. It differs from per-scene methods like [LISA](12036-lisa.md) by learning a generalized field from a dataset, and from other generalizable methods like [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md) by explicitly combining image-based rendering features with a neural radiance field via a novel appearance blending scheme to preserve high-frequency texture details [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $K$ synchronized videos of a performer captured at different viewpoints (preferably 360°) with $T$ frames, denoted as $I = \{I_{k,t}\}$. Each image $I_k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 4}$ includes the RGB channels and a mask as the last channel [S2].
*   **Output:** Free-viewpoint video synthesis for arbitrary camera poses and unseen identities [S2].

**Data Structures and Network Architectures:**
The method consists of four main neural components:

1.  **Feature Extractor Network $U$:** A U-Net architecture with 8 layers using ConvBnReLU2D (CRB2D) blocks (kernel $k=3$, stride $s=1$, dilation $d=1$) [S2].
    *   Layers: Down0-Down3, Up1-Up3.
    *   Channel mappings: $4/32, 32/64, 64/128, 128/256, 256/256, 256/128, 128/64, 64/32$ [S2].
    *   Output: 2D feature map $f_k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ [S2].

2.  **Feature Blending Network $MLP_B$:** Estimates blending weights for aggregating features from $K$ views.
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR6.
    *   PE0 channels: $6/54, 3/27$.
    *   LR0: $54+27+32*6/256$.
    *   LR1-4: $256/256$.
    *   LR5: $256/128$.
    *   LR6: $128/6$.
    *   Input: View direction $\vec{v}$, angle $\theta$, PE0, features $f$ [S2].

3.  **Deformation Network $MLP_d$:** Learns non-rigid displacements based on SMPL skeleton distances and directions.
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR5.
    *   PE0 channels: $24/216$.
    *   LR0: $216+72+32/256$.
    *   LR1-4: $256/256$.
    *   LR5: $128/3$.
    *   Input: $R_d$ (distances to 24 SMPL joints), PE0, $R_v$ (directions to 24 SMPL joints), $F$ (blended feature) [S2].

4.  **Generalizable Dynamic Neural Radiance Field $\Phi$:** Regresses density and color in canonical space.
    *   Layers: PE0, LR0-LR8, Density $\sigma$, PE1, LR7, LR8, Color $c$.
    *   PE0: $3/63$. LR0: $63/256$. LR1-4: $256/256$. LR5: $27+256/256$. LR6: $256/256$.
    *   Density $\sigma$: $256/1$.
    *   PE1: $3/27$. LR7: $256+27+32/256$. LR8: $256/128$.
    *   Color $c$: $128/3$.
    *   Input: Position $x$, view direction $d$, PE0, LR2, PE0, LR2, PE1, $F$, LR7, LR8 [S2].

5.  **Appearance Blending Network $MLP_A$:** Refines texture by aggregating colors from neighboring views.
    *   Layers: LR0-LR6, Blending weights $W$.
    *   LR0: $(32+3)*2/256$. LR1-5: $256/256$. LR6: $256/128$.
    *   $W$: $128/3$.
    *   Input: $f_{r,0}^r, f_{l,0}^l$ (two adjacent image features and occlusion maps) [S2].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** For each input image $I^k$, extract local appearance features using the U-Net network $U$:
    $$f^k = U(I^k) \quad [S2]$$

2.  **Aggregated Pixel Alignment Feature:** For a spatial query point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, project it into view $k$ at $q_k \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and fetch the feature vector $f_{q}^k$. Aggregate features from $K$ views using blending weights $w_q^k$:
    $$F_p = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_q^k f_{q}^k \quad [S2]$$
    The weights are estimated by $MLP_B$:
    $$w_q^k = MLP_B(\vec{v}, \theta_q^k, f_q^k) \quad [S2]$$
    where $\vec{v}$ is the viewing direction and $\theta_q^k$ is the angle of the viewing direction relative to the query ray of the source view [S2].

3.  **Deformation to Canonical Space:** Use the SMPL parametric model $\mathcal{M}$ with skinning weights $w^s$ to estimate a rigid deformation. Compute $R_d \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ and $R_v \in \mathbb{R}^{72}$ representing distances and directions between $p$ and the 24 SMPL joints. Apply the non-rigid deformation network $MLP_d$ to learn subtle displacements:
    $$p' = \mathcal{S}(p, \mathcal{M}, w^s) + MLP_d(R_d, R_v, F_p) \quad [S2]$$
    where $p'$ is the point in canonical space [S2].

4.  **Radiance Field Evaluation:** Feed $p'$, view direction $d$, and the aggregated feature $F$ into the neural radiance field $\Phi$ to regress volume density $\sigma$ and RGB radiance $c$ [S2].

5.  **Appearance Blending (Optional Fine-tuning):** To refine high-frequency details and reduce artifacts, use the appearance blending network $MLP_A$ to aggregate colors from neighboring views $f_{r,0}^r$ and $f_{l,0}^l$, producing blending weights $W$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   SMPL skeleton joints: 24 joints [S2].
*   Fine-tuning duration: 300 frames for a specific performer [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Multi-person/Interaction:** The method relies on a regressed parametric human model, limiting it to single-person setups. It fails on multi-person or human-object interactions [S2].
*   **Lighting Variation:** No explicit lighting model is used. Significant brightness or color changes between views cause severe artifacts. Switching nearest views during appearance blending causes jumping artifacts, especially with high brightness variance [S2].
*   **Generalization Limits:** Capability is limited because human dataset distributions only cover a small portion of human dynamics and appearances [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method is significantly more efficient than per-scene methods. On the 'batman' video (300 frames), full training takes 1.2 hours, compared to Neural Body (6.7h), Neural Volumes (8.4h), and ST-NeRF (9.5h) [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** On six input views, the method achieves PSNR = 32.59, SSIM = 0.9817, and LPIPS = 0.0304 [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient generalization to unseen identities without per-scene training; high-fidelity texture via neural appearance blending; handles large pose deformations [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires consistent lighting across views; limited to single-person subjects; generalization is bounded by the diversity of the training dataset [S2].
*   **When to use:** Dynamic human rendering from sparse multi-view inputs, specifically targeting unseen identities and poses with efficient generalization [S2].
*   **When not to use:** Multi-person scenes, scenes with drastic lighting changes, or when high-fidelity geometry is required without texture blending [S2].

## Background
HumanNeRF was introduced by Zhang et al. in 2021/2022 [S2]. It builds on the concept of generalizable neural radiance fields, drawing inspiration from IBRNet's ability to assist NeRF by avoiding per-scene training [S2]. The method addresses the limitations of previous approaches that either required dense multi-view inputs and costly training or suffered from blur artifacts and geometric discontinuities when generalizing to unseen identities [S2].
