# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md) by explicitly integrating control-theoretic state estimation (Kalman filtering) into the deformation field estimation, rather than relying solely on time-conditioned feature interpolation or implicit temporal MLPs [S1]. It builds on [Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling](15546-dynamic-scene-and-temporal-modeling.md) by using a deformation field $\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ to warp points to a canonical space, but enhances this with a two-branch observer-predictor architecture [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamps $t_i$, and a sequence of training images with known camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Deformation fields $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ for each frame, which warp real-space points to a time-independent canonical space. The radiance field (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) is then queried in this canonical space.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** The canonical space is encoded using three mutually orthogonal 2D planes (a tri-plane). This replaces the deep MLP backbone used in methods like D-NeRF [S1]. The tri-plane features are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors due to finite resolution [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A shallow two-layer MLP that takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ as input. It outputs the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that takes noise terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and timestamps $t_i, t_{i-1}$ to compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with current coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and timestamp $t_i$ to obtain the observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise term $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
2.  **Prediction Step (History):**
    *   Retrieve deformation and noise from previous two frames: $d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and $d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-2}}$ [S1].
    *   Calculate velocity $v_{t_i}$ using the deformation difference between the previous two frames and the time step $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$:
        $$ v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i} $$
    *   Compute the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the local linear motion model:
        $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}} $$
3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ with inputs $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
4.  **State Update (Deformation Estimation):** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimate $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
5.  **Warping:** Warp all points $\mathbf{x}$ in real space to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$ using the estimated deformation:
    $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} $$
6.  **Rendering:** Query the tri-plane representation and MLP for density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}')$ and color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}')$ in the canonical space. Render the image using the standard volume rendering integral extended for time [Parent](15546-dynamic-scene-and-temporal-modeling.md).

**Formulas:**
*   **Kalman Loss:** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation to supervise the observer:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
*   **Canonical Observation Loss:** A soft regularization for the first frame ($t=0$) to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real-world radiance field:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise.
*   **Total Variation Loss:** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution.
*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ is the proposal consistency loss from Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane (three orthogonal 2D planes) [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(x, t_i, t_prev, t_prev2, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, prev_deformations):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter logic.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D spatial coordinates (batch, 3)
        t_i: Current timestamp (scalar)
        t_prev: Previous timestamp (scalar)
        t_prev2: Timestamp before previous (scalar)
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP returning (observation, noise_term)
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer returning Kalman gain
        prev_deformations: Dictionary mapping timestamp to deformation d_x and noise epsilon
        
    Returns:
        final_deformation: Estimated deformation d_x_t_i
    """
    # 1. Observer Step: Get current observation and noise
    y_t_i, epsilon_t_i = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # 2. Prediction Step: Use history
    # Retrieve previous states
    d_x_prev, epsilon_prev = prev_deformations[t_prev]
    d_x_prev2, epsilon_prev2 = prev_deformations[t_prev2]
    
    # Calculate velocity based on local linear motion
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev
    v_t_i = (d_x_prev - d_x_prev2) / delta_t
    
    # Predict deformation
    d_x_pred = d_x_prev + delta_t * v_t_i
    
    # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Input: current noise, previous noise, current time, previous time
    K_t_i = kalman_gain_net(epsilon_t_i, epsilon_prev, t_i, t_prev)
    
    # 4. Update Step: Combine prediction and observation
    # final_deformation = prediction + K * (observation - prediction)
    final_deformation = d_x_pred + K_t_i * (y_t_i - d_x_pred)
    
    return final_deformation

def train_kfd_nerf(images, poses, timestamps, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, tri_plane, proposal_network):
    """
    Training loop for KFD-NeRF.
    """
    # Training Strategy: Gradually release images in chronological order
    sorted_timestamps = sorted(timestamps)
    
    for t_i in sorted_timestamps:
        # Get batch of points and rays for current timestamp
        rays, target_colors = sample_rays(images[t_i], poses[t_i])
        
        # Get points x in real space
        x_real = rays.origin + rays.direction * depths # Simplified sampling
        
        # Compute deformation using history
        # Note: In practice, prev_deformations are accumulated from previous steps
        deformation = kfd_nerf_deformation_step(
            x_real, t_i, 
            t_prev=get_prev_timestamp(t_i), 
            t_prev2=get_prev_prev_timestamp(t_i),
            observer_mlp=observer_mlp,
            kalman_gain_net=kalman_gain_net,
            prev_deformations=history_buffer
        )
        
        # Warp to canonical space
        x_canonical = x_real + deformation
        
        # Query radiance field in canonical space
        sigma, color = query_radiance_field(x_canonical, tri_plane)
        
        # Render image
        rendered_color = volume_render(rays, sigma, color)
        
        # Compute Losses
        loss_image = mse_loss(rendered_color, target_colors)
        loss_kf = kalman_loss(observer_mlp(x_real, t_i)[0], deformation) # y_t_i vs d_x_hat
        loss_co = canonical_observation_loss(deformation, t_i) # Only active if t_i == 0
        loss_prop = proposal_consistency_loss(proposal_network, x_canonical, sigma)
        loss_tv = total_variation_loss(tri_plane)
        
        total_loss = loss_image + loss_kf + loss_co + loss_prop + 1e-4 * loss_tv
        
        # Backpropagate and update
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        total_loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # Update history buffer
        history_buffer[t_i] = (deformation, observer_mlp(x_real, t_i)[1])
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Training Stages:** Predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion, as observations may lack confidence [S1].
*   **Later Training Stages:** Observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details as the observer MLP learns better [S1].
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Concatenating raw coordinate inputs with encoded tri-plane features mitigates this [S1].
*   **Canonical Space Complexity:** Unlike D-NeRF which hard-masks deformation at $t=0$ to zero, KFD-NeRF uses a soft regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$) to avoid making the canonical space too complex for warping [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to other dynamic NeRF methods. On synthetic data, KFD-NeRF takes 30 minutes (small) to 3 hours (large), compared to 48 hours for D-NeRF and 60 minutes for TiNeuVox-B [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based backbones, allowing for higher resolution in the canonical space [S1]. Parameter size is 175 MB for the large variant, comparable to TiNeuVox-B (50 MB) and Mixvoxels-L (140 MB) [S1].
*   **Performance (Synthetic Data):** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
*   **Performance (Real Data):** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality motion reconstruction due to Kalman filtering; efficient convergence due to tri-plane representation; smooth deformation fields leveraging MLP inductive bias [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on local linear motion assumption, which may struggle with highly non-linear or chaotic motion; requires chronological training order [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng from The University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Shanghai Artificial Laboratory in 2024 [S1]. It is based on the paper "KFD-NeRF: Rethinking Dynamic NeRF with Kalman Filter" published in ECCV 2024 [S1]. The method draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system. It addresses the limitations of previous methods like D-NeRF (which uses deep MLPs for deformation) and DyNeRF/KPlanes (which use time-conditioned feature interpolation) by combining the smoothness of deformation fields with the temporal context of Kalman filtering [S1].
