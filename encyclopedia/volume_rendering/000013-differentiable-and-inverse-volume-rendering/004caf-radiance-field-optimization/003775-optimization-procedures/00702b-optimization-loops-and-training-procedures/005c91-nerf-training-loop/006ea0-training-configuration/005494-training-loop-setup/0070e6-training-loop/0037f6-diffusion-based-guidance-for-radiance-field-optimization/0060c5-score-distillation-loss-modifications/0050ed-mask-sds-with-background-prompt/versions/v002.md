# Mask+SDS with Background Prompt

Also called: Mask+SDS+BGT

## Overview
Mask+SDS with Background Prompt is a Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) variant that mitigates the "black hole" artifact in masked NeRF editing by explicitly conditioning the diffusion model on the surrounding scene content [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard masked SDS by appending a description of the background to the object prompt, ensuring the diffusion prior generates plausible geometry for occluded regions rather than defaulting to empty space [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A radiance field $\Theta$, a semantic mask $M$ (where $M=1$ indicates the region to edit, $M=0$ the background), an object text prompt $P_{obj}$, and a background text prompt $P_{bg}$.
*   **Output:** Updated radiance field parameters $\Theta$ after gradient descent.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Prompt:** A single concatenated string $P_{total} = P_{obj} + \text{" in "} + P_{bg}$ (e.g., "a lego man head in leaves") [S1].
*   **Mask:** Binary or soft mask $M$ defining the editable region.

**Steps:**
1.  **Render:** Render an image $I$ from the current radiance field $\Theta$ at a random camera viewpoint.
2.  **Masking:** Apply the mask $M$ to the rendered image. The method typically relies on the standard SDS formulation where the loss is computed on the noisy image $x_t$. The source notes that the Stable Diffusion model receives full-resolution images without explicit mask injection into the diffusion process itself; instead, the prompt guides the generation [S1].
3.  **Noise Addition:** Transform $I$ into a noisy image $x_t$ at a random time step $t$:
    $$ x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} I + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon $$
    where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t$ is the cumulative noise schedule coefficient [S1, Parent].
4.  **Diffusion Prediction:** Pass $x_t$ and the combined prompt $P_{total}$ to the frozen diffusion model $\epsilon_\theta$ to predict the noise $\hat{\epsilon}$:
    $$ \hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, P_{total}, t) $$
5.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the SDS loss based on the noise prediction error. The source indicates that a specific loss function (Eq. 10 in [S1]) is used to strongly control the unmasked part to avoid unwanted content changes, implying a weighted or masked loss term $\lambda_{unmask}$ is applied to the gradient [S1].
    $$ L = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon} [||\hat{\epsilon} - \epsilon||^2_2] $$
    *Note: The exact weighting formula for $\lambda_{unmask}$ is referenced as Eq. 14 in [S1] but not provided in the text. The implementation must apply a mask-weighted gradient to the unmasked regions to preserve background integrity [S1].*
6.  **Gradient Backpropagation:** Compute the gradient with respect to $\Theta$:
    $$ \nabla_\Theta L = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon,C} [\omega(t) \cdot (\hat{\epsilon} - \epsilon) \frac{\partial x_t}{\partial \Theta}] $$
    where $\omega(t)$ is the weighting function [Parent].

**Parameters:**
*   **Prompt Construction:** The background prompt $P_{bg}$ must semantically describe the surrounding environment (e.g., "leaves", "sky") [S1].
*   **Mask Weight ($\lambda_{unmask}$):** Controls the strength of supervision on unmasked regions. The source compares different effects of this weight but does not provide specific values [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Black Holes:** If the background prompt is omitted (standard Mask+SDS), previously occluded or covered regions develop into "black holes" due to a lack of explicit supervision for those areas [S1].
*   **Precision:** The diffusion model receives full-resolution images without mask injection, so the prompt must be precise enough to guide the generation of the background content within the masked region [S1].

## Subtopics

### Mask+SDS+CLIP
A variant that removes the background prompt from the diffusion model's text prompt and instead uses CLIP with a background prompt to fill occluded regions [S1]. It provides a view of the unmasked region to CLIP along with a background prompt to supervise and fill the black hole [S1]. While CLIP fills the black hole with reasonable texture, the generated shapes retain artifacts from the original occluded geometry (e.g., petal shapes) [S1].

### Mask+SDS+BGT+CLIP
The final combined editing configuration that uses SDS with a full prompt (object + background) and CLIP with a background prompt on unmasked views [S1]. It sets the diffusion model text prompt to include both object and background (e.g., "object in background") and simultaneously feeds unmasked views to CLIP with the background prompt to supervise the surroundings [S1]. This produces normal shapes and fills black holes with appropriate background material (e.g., green leaves around a blue rose) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** Explicitly addresses the "black hole" artifact common in masked NeRF editing by providing semantic context for occluded regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful construction of the background prompt; incorrect prompts may lead to semantically inconsistent background generation [S1].
*   **Performance:** The source does not provide specific timing or memory metrics. It notes that the method uses the full-resolution Stable Diffusion model without mask injection into the diffusion process, which may impact computational cost compared to masked diffusion variants [S1].

## Background
The method originates from "RePaint-NeRF: NeRF Editing via Semantic Masks and Diffusion Models" [S1]. It was developed to improve upon basic masked SDS editing, which fails to generate plausible content for occluded regions. The approach leverages the ability of text-to-image diffusion models to understand spatial relationships (e.g., "object in background") to guide the synthesis of missing geometry [S1].
