# Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)
Also called: Neural-PIL

## Overview
Neural-PIL replaces the costly numerical integration of incoming light with a simple neural network query, predicting pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i$ directly from illumination embedding, direction, and roughness [S1, unofficial]. Unlike Spherical Harmonics NeRF or Spherical Gaussian representations which suffer from lack of fine details or high memory costs, Neural-PIL is an MLP that directly produces pre-integrated light, representing finer details in illumination [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Illumination embedding $\mathbf{z}_l \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$, incoming light direction $\omega_r \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and roughness $b_r$ (scalar) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The Neural-PIL architecture leverages a pi-GAN-like structure with FiLM-SIREN layers [S1, unofficial].
*   **FiLM-SIREN Layer:** Takes modulating parameters (scalar $\lambda_0$ and vectors $\beta, \gamma$) to modulate the output $y$ of an earlier linear layer followed by a sine computation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Mapping Networks:** Two mapping networks predict the modulating parameters $(\gamma, \beta)$ for the FiLM-SIREN layers [S1, unofficial].
    *   The first mapping network generates parameters for the first few layers from the illumination embedding $\mathbf{z}_l$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   The second mapping network generates parameters for the penultimate layer from the roughness $b_r$ [S1, unofficial].

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Encode Illumination:** The illumination embedding $\mathbf{z}_l$ is passed through the first mapping network to generate modulating parameters $(\gamma, \beta)$ for the initial layers of the Neural-PIL network [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Decode Direction:** The incoming direction $\omega_r$ is processed through the initial layers of the Neural-PIL network, conditioned by the parameters from Step 1, to decode illumination information for the given direction [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Condition Roughness:** The roughness $b_r$ is passed through the second mapping network to generate modulating parameters for the penultimate layer of the Neural-PIL network [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Integrate Light:** The network continues processing through the remaining layers, conditioned by the roughness parameters from Step 3, to perform light integration [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Query Output:** The final output of the network is the pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation using Neural-PIL approximates the outgoing radiance $L_o(x, \omega_o)$ as [S1, unofficial]:

$$
L_o(x, \omega_o) \approx (b_d / \pi)\tilde{L}_i(n, 1) + b_s (F_0(\omega_o, n)B_0(\omega_o \cdot n, b_r) + B_1(\omega_o \cdot n, b_r))\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)
$$

where:
*   $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r) = \int_{\Omega} D(b_r, \omega_i, \omega_r)L_i(x, \omega_i)d\omega_i$ is the pre-integrated light [S1, unofficial].
*   $F_0(\omega_o, n) = (1 - \omega_o \cdot h)^5$ with $h = (\omega_i + \omega_o)/||\omega_i + \omega_o||$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $B_0, B_1$ are 2D lookup textures indexed by $(\omega_o \cdot n)$ and roughness $b_r$ [S1, unofficial].
*   The FiLM-SIREN activation function is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \phi(y) = \sin(\lambda_0 \gamma \odot y + \beta)
    $$
    where $\odot$ denotes the Hadamard product [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Illumination embedding dimension: 128 [S1, unofficial].
*   Direction dimension: 3 [S1, unofficial].
*   Roughness: Scalar [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   The pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i$ is queried twice: once for the diffuse part (parameterized by surface normal $n \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and diffuse roughness 1) and once for the specular part (parameterized by mirrored view direction $\omega_r$ and roughness $b_r$) [S1, unofficial].
*   Training the Neural-PIL jointly with other networks can cause high memory requirements; it is recommended to train the Light-SMAE (Smooth Manifold Auto-encoder) first with a frozen encoder, then train the Neural-PIL using the manifold created by the frozen Light-SMAE encoder [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Split-Sum and PIL Approximation
A baseline approximation method that splits the specular term into two factors: one involving the light integral and one involving the BRDF terms (D, G, F), allowing the light integral to be pre-integrated into a neural network [S2, unofficial]. The specular term is split as [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\int_{\Omega_+} L(\hat{x}, l)(\hat{n} \cdot l) dl \int_{\Omega_+} \frac{DGF}{4(\hat{n} \cdot v)(\hat{n} \cdot l)} (\hat{n} \cdot l) dl
$$
The rendering equation becomes [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\tilde{L}\hat{\mathbf{c}}_b + (\pi\tilde{L}) \int_{\Omega_+} \frac{DGF}{4(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{v})(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l})} (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l}) d\mathbf{l}
$$
where $\tilde{L}$ is the pre-integrated neural network representing the light integral factor divided by $\pi$ [S2, unofficial]. The forward rendering model with split-sum + PIL approximation is [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\widehat{Lc_b} + (\pi\widehat{L}) \int_{\Omega_+} \frac{DGF}{4(\hat{n} \cdot \mathbf{v})(\hat{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})} (\hat{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}) d\mathbf{l}
$$

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Replaces costly numerical accumulation or convolution on the complete environment map with a simple network query [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Avoids the high memory costs associated with representing fine illumination details using a large number of SG or SH bands [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Can represent finer details in illumination compared to SG representation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient query time; captures fine illumination details better than SG/SH; differentiable.
*   **Limitations:** Requires training of the Neural-PIL network; joint training with other networks can be memory-intensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When fine illumination details are required and numerical integration is too slow or non-differentiable.
*   **When not to use:** When memory is extremely constrained and training overhead is prohibitive.

## Background
Neural-PIL was introduced in the paper "Neural-PIL: Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting for Reflectance Decomposition" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the impracticality of costly numerical accumulation or convolution on the complete environment map within a differential rendering engine [S1, unofficial]. The method leverages a pi-GAN-like architecture with FiLM-SIREN layers [S1, unofficial]. It is trained using a Light-SMAE (Smooth Manifold Auto-encoder) with a CNN encoder and decoder on a dataset of 320 environment maps to map 2D environment maps onto a 128-dimensional smooth latent space [S1, unofficial].
