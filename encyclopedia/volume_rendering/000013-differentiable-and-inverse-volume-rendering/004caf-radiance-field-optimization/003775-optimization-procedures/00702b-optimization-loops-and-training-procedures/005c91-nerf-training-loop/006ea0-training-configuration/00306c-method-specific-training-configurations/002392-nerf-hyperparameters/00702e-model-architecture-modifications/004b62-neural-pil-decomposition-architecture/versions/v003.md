# Neural-PIL Decomposition Architecture

Also called: Coarse and Decomposition Networks, Neural-PIL Decomposition

## Overview
The Neural-PIL Decomposition Architecture separates scene appearance into geometry, material, and illumination to enable reflectance decomposition under varying lighting conditions. Unlike standard NeRF which predicts view-dependent colors directly, this method predicts density and material parameters (BRDF) in a decomposition network, using a neural pre-integrated lighting (Neural-PIL) model to compute shading at ray termination points. It is distinguished by its two-stage training protocol (pre-training manifolds, then joint optimization) and its shift from volumetric integration to single-point shading at expected ray depths.

## How it works
The method consists of two primary networks: a **Coarse Network** and a **Decomposition Network**, along with two pre-trained manifold auto-encoders (SMAEs).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Coarse Network Input:** 3D position $x$, view direction $\omega_o$, illumination embedding $z_l$.
*   **Coarse Network Output:** Point density $\sigma$ and color $c$.
*   **Decomposition Network Input:** 3D position $x$.
*   **Decomposition Network Output:** Density $\sigma$ and BRDF embedding $z_b \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
*   **Rendering Output:** Shaded color $c$ at the ray termination point.

### Data Structures
1.  **Light-SMAE:** Encoder/Decoder pair for environment maps. The encoder maps an environment map to a latent manifold. [S1, unofficial]
2.  **BRDF-SMAE:** Encoder/Decoder pair for BRDF parameters. The decoder maps $z_b$ to physical BRDF parameters $b$. [S1, unofficial]
3.  **Neural-PIL:** A neural network that approximates the pre-integrated illumination $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$, replacing costly Monte Carlo integration or environment map convolution. [S1, unofficial]
4.  **Coarse Network:** A standard MLP (architecture details in supplement) taking $(x, \omega_o, z_l)$ and outputting $(\sigma, c)$. [S1, unofficial]
5.  **Decomposition Network:** An MLP taking $x$ and outputting $(\sigma, z_b)$. [S1, unofficial]

### Rendering Process
1.  **Ray Sampling:** Cast a camera ray. Sample points along the ray.
2.  **Density Estimation:** Use the **Decomposition Network** to estimate density $\sigma$ at each sample point. [S1, unofficial]
3.  **Expected Termination:** Compute the expected termination depth of the ray, similar to a depth map, using the sample densities along the ray. [S1, unofficial]
4.  **Termination Point Selection:** Perform rendering only at the estimated termination position $x_{term}$ along the ray. [S1, unofficial]
5.  **Normal Estimation:** Compute the surface normal $n$ at $x_{term}$ by calculating the gradient of the density with respect to position:
    $$ n = \frac{\nabla_x \sigma}{\|\nabla_x \sigma\|} $$
    This gradient is computed by passing gradients through the Decomposition Network. [S1, unofficial]
6.  **BRDF Parameter Extraction:** Convert the BRDF embedding $z_b$ (output of Decomposition Network at $x_{term}$) into BRDF parameters $b$ using the **BRDF-SMAE** decoder. [S1, unofficial]
7.  **Illumination Computation:** Compute the pre-integrated illumination $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$ at $x_{term}$ using the **Neural-PIL** network. This network takes the view direction and BRDF parameters as input. [S1, unofficial]
8.  **Shading:** Compute the final color $c$ using the estimated BRDF $b$, normal $n$, and illumination $\tilde{L}_i$. The specific shading equation is referenced as Equation 2 in the source. [S1, unofficial]

### Training Protocol
The training occurs in three distinct phases:

1.  **Pre-training Manifolds:**
    *   Train **Light-SMAE** on a dataset of environment maps. [S1, unofficial]
    *   Train **BRDF-SMAE** on a dataset of BRDFs. [S1, unofficial]
2.  **Training Neural-PIL:**
    *   Train the **Neural-PIL** network using the latent manifold created by the **frozen** Light-SMAE encoder. This separation reduces memory requirements during joint optimization. [S1, unofficial]
3.  **Joint Scene Optimization:**
    *   Initialize **Coarse Network** and **Decomposition Network**.
    *   Freeze the **BRDF-SMAE** decoder and **Neural-PIL** network.
    *   Optimize the Coarse and Decomposition networks jointly on scene images.
    *   **Constraint:** Only optimize the illumination embedding $z_l$ via the Decomposition Network. Do **not** backpropagate the loss signal onto the illumination in the Coarse Network. [S1, unofficial]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Rendering is computationally cheaper per ray than full volumetric integration because it samples only at the expected termination depth rather than integrating along the entire ray. However, it requires computing gradients for normal estimation.
*   **Memory:** The separation of training (pre-training manifolds separately) reduces memory requirements during the joint optimization phase. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Strengths:** Handles varying illumination conditions by explicitly modeling illumination embeddings and using Neural-PIL for efficient shading. Provides explicit material (BRDF) and geometry (density/normal) estimates.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the expected termination depth; errors in depth estimation can lead to incorrect shading. Requires pre-training of SMAEs and Neural-PIL before scene optimization.
*   **When to use:** When the scene has varying illumination and explicit reflectance decomposition (separating albedo, normal, roughness, etc.) is desired.
*   **When not to use:** For static illumination scenes where standard NeRF is sufficient, or when real-time rendering is required without pre-computed manifolds.

## Background
The Neural-PIL Decomposition Architecture was introduced in the paper "Neural-PIL: Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting for Reflectance Decomposition" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the challenge of decomposing scene appearance into geometry, material, and illumination when images are captured under varying lighting conditions. Traditional NeRF methods estimate view-dependent colors, which conflates lighting and material effects. This method introduces a decomposition network that estimates density and BRDF embeddings, and uses a neural network to approximate pre-integrated lighting, enabling efficient and differentiable rendering under complex illumination.
