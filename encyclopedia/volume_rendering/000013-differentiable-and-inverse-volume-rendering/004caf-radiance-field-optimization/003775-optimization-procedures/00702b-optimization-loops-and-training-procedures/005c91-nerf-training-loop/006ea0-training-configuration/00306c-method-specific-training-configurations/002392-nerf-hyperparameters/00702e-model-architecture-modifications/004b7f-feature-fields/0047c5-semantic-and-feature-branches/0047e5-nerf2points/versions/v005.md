# NeRF2Points

Also called: Layered Perception and Integrated Modeling

## Overview
NeRF2Points is a method for generating large-scale, geometrically consistent RGBD point clouds from street-view radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the "pavement collapse" and matching overhead artifacts common in standard NeRF methods when applied to sparse, large-scale street data by separating the scene into road and street components, modeling them independently, and merging them via rigid transformation [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [PI-NeRF](12124-pi-nerf.md) and [GazeNeRF](12127-gazenerf.md) by its specific focus on large-scale outdoor street-view geometry through layered perception and geometric-aware consistency regularization [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sequence of RGB images $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ from a street-view dataset; coarse initial camera poses from Wheel-IMU-GNSS Odometry (WIGO); refined camera poses and sparse point clouds from COLMAP Structure from Motion (SfM); depth and normal estimations from Omnidata; road/scene masks from Mask2Former; and SIFT features for temporal correspondence.
*   **Outputs:** A merged RGBD point cloud $PC \in \mathbb{R}^{(N_1+N_2) \times 3}$ and a trained radiance field capable of rendering RGB, depth, and normals.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Gate $M(\cdot)$:** A segmentation model (trained on Cityscapes) with an embedded morphological dilation operator. It outputs a binary mask for each pixel indicating "road" or "street".
*   **Ray Bundles:**
    *   $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{1}}^{r}$: Rays classified as road.
    *   $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{2}}^{s}$: Rays classified as street.
    *   $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}}$: Intersection of road and street rays (shared rays).
    *   $\mathbf{Ray}_{i}$: Reconstructed original ray bundle.
*   **Point Clouds:**
    *   $PC_r \in \mathbb{R}^{N_1 \times 3}$: Point cloud from road radiance field.
    *   $PC_s \in \mathbb{R}^{N_2 \times 3}$: Point cloud from street radiance field.
    *   $P \subset PC_r, Q \subset PC_s$: Intersection points for rigid alignment.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Data Preparation:**
    *   Collect street-view images (e.g., 180k images, 20km span) from multiple cameras.
    *   Obtain initial 6-DoF camera poses using WIGO (GNSS + Pose Graph Optimization).
    *   Refine poses and estimate sparse point clouds using COLMAP SfM.
    *   Generate depth and normal maps using Omnidata.
    *   Generate road/scene masks using Mask2Former.
    *   Extract SIFT features from all images to identify correspondences between adjacent frames.

2.  **Layered Perception (Ray Splitting):**
    *   For each image $I$ and ray $Ray_i = \{x_i, d_i\}$, apply the ray gate $M(I)$:
        $$ \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{1}}^{r} = \mathbf{Ray}_{i}(M(\mathbf{I})) $$
        $$ \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{2}}^{s} = \mathbf{Ray}_{i}(1-M(\mathbf{I})) $$
        $$ \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}} = \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{1}}^{r} \cap \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{2}}^{s} $$
    *   Reconstruct the original ray set:
        $$ \mathbf{Ray}_{i}=(\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{1}}^{r} - \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}}) \cup (\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{2}}^{s} - \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}}) \cup \mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}} $$
    *   Train two separate radiance fields (MLPs): one for road rays $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{1}}^{r}$ and one for street rays $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{2}}^{s}$. The intersection rays $\mathbf{Ray}_{i_{3}}$ are used for both or handled via shared supervision to ensure continuity.

3.  **Geometric-Aware Consistency Regularization (GAC):**
    *   **Spatial Dynamic Consistency ($\ell_{sdc}$):**
        *   Calculate matching cost for each ray bundle $\mathbf{Ray}_{i}$ (road or street):
            $$ \mathbf{cos}_{i} = \lambda _{1} \ell _{depth} + \lambda _{2} \ell _{rgb} + \lambda _{3} \ell _{normal} $$
        *   Define losses:
            $$ \ell _{depth}(\mathbf {t}_{1}^{'}, \mathbf {t}_{2}^{'}) = \sum _{} \left \| \textit {norm}(\mathbf {t}_{1}^{'}) - \textit {norm}(\mathbf {t}_{2}^{'}) \right \|_2^2 $$
            $$ \ell _{rgb}(\mathbf {c}_{1}^{'}, \mathbf {c}_{2}^{'}) = \sum _{} \left \| \mathbf {c}_{1}^{'} - \mathbf {c}_{2}^{'} \right \|_{2}^{2} $$
            $$ \ell _{normal}(\mathbf {n}_{1}^{'}, \mathbf {n}_{2}^{'}) = \sum _{} {\lVert \mathbf {n}_{1}^{'} - \mathbf {n}_{2}^{'} \rVert }_1 + {\lVert 1 - \mathbf {n}_{1}^{'\top } \mathbf {n}_{2}^{'} \rVert }_1 $$
            where $\textit {norm}(\cdot)$ is max-min normalization.
        *   Rank costs $\mathbf{cos}_i$ and select top-$n$ rays $\mathbf{Ray}_{i,n}^{'}$ as hard samples.
        *   Construct proposal regions $R$ of size $s \times s$ centered on pixel indices of hard samples.
        *   Randomly select consistent matching samples from $R$ for each hard ray.
        *   Compute $\ell_{sdc}$:
            $$ \ell _{sdc} = \lambda _{1}^{'} \ell _{jsd} (\phi (\mathbf {r}_{1}^{'}) ,\phi (\mathbf {r}_{2}^{'})) + \lambda _{2}^{'} \ell _{rgb} + \lambda _{3}^{'} \ell _{depth} $$
            where $\phi(\mathbf{x})$ is the softmax function and $\ell_{jsd}$ is Jensen-Shannon Divergence:
            $$ \ell _{jsd} (\phi (\mathbf {r}_{1}^{'}) ,\phi (\mathbf {r}_{2}^{'}) ) = 0.5 * \frac {\sum \phi (\mathbf {r}_{1}^{'}) * \ln(\phi (\mathbf {r}_{2}^{'}) ) + \sum \phi (\mathbf {r}_{2}^{'}) * \ln(\phi (\mathbf {r}_{1}^{'}) )}{(\phi (\mathbf {r}_{1}^{'}) + \phi (\mathbf {r}_{2}^{'}) )/2} $$
    *   **Temporal Invariant Consistency ($\ell_{tic}$):**
        *   Identify SIFT correspondences between current frame $I_{cur}$ and next frame $I_{next}$, yielding ray bundles $\mathbf{Ray}_{cur}$ and $\mathbf{Ray}_{next}$.
        *   Compute $\ell_{tic}$ using the same JSD formula on features from $\mathbf{Ray}_{cur}$ and $\mathbf{Ray}_{next}$.

4.  **Training:**
    *   Minimize total loss:
        $$ \ell _{total} = \ell _{rec} + \beta * \ell _{sdc} + \gamma * \ell _{tic} $$
        $$ \ell _{rec} = \theta _{1} * \ell _{rgb} + \theta _{2} * \ell _{depth} + \theta _{3} * \ell _{normal} $$
    *   Use Adam optimizer, gradient clipping (threshold/norm 0.1), scene space annealing, and exponential learning rate decay with warm-up.

5.  **Point Cloud Generation and Merging:**
    *   Generate separate point clouds $PC_r$ and $PC_s$ by sampling the trained radiance fields:
        $$ PC = origin + direction * depth $$
    *   Identify intersection points $P \subset PC_r$ and $Q \subset PC_s$.
    *   Compute rigid transformation $(R, t)$ by minimizing:
        $$ \min _{\mathbf {R}, \mathbf {t}} ~~ \sum _{i=1}^{N^{'}} \bigl (\textit {Dis}_i(\mathbf {R}, \mathbf {t}) \bigr )^2 + \textit {I}_{SO} (\mathbf {R}) $$
        where $\textit {Dis}_i(\mathbf {R}, \mathbf {t}) = \min_{q_j \in Q, p_k \in P} \|\mathbf {R} p_i + \mathbf {t} - q_j \|$ and $\textit {I}_{SO}(\mathbf {R})$ is an indicator function for $SO(3)$ (0 if $\mathbf {R}^T \mathbf {R} = \mathbf {I}$ and $\det (\mathbf {R}) = 1$, else $+\infty$).
    *   Merge point clouds:
        $$ \mathbf {PC} = \mathbf {PC}_r \mathbf {R} + \mathbf {t} + \mathbf {PC}_s $$

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_1 = 0.1, \lambda_2 = 1.0, \lambda_3 = 0.05$ (matching cost weights).
*   $\theta_1 = 1.0, \theta_2 = 0.1, \theta_3 = 0.005$ (reconstruction loss weights).
*   $\beta = 0.01, \gamma = 0.01$ (consistency loss weights).
*   Training: 20,000 iterations, batch size 2048, single NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Pavement Collapse:** Standard NeRF methods fail to optimize weak textures like road surfaces, leading to poor depth estimation. Layered perception prevents this by isolating road rays.
*   **Floaters:** Sparse views and viewing angle shifts cause geometric inconsistencies and floaters. GAC mitigates this.
*   **Matching Overhead:** High-frequency details can overshadow weak texture optimization. The ray gate ensures balanced gradient optimization.

import torch.nn.functional as F

def softmax(x):
    return F.softmax(x, dim=-1)

def jsd_loss(p, q):
    # p, q are logits or probabilities? Source implies phi(r) are probabilities via softmax.
    # Assuming p, q are already softmaxed probabilities.
    m = 0.5 * (p + q)
    # Avoid log(0)
    eps = 1e-10
    p = torch.clamp(p, min=eps)
    q = torch.clamp(q, min=eps)
    m = torch.clamp(m, min=eps)
    kl_pm = F.kl_div(m.log(), p, reduction='sum') # KL(P || M)
    kl_qm = F.kl_div(m.log(), q, reduction='sum') # KL(Q || M)
    # JSD = 0.5 * (KL(P||M) + KL(Q||M))
    # Note: F.kl_div expects log target. Source formula: sum(p * ln(q) + q * ln(p)) / ((p+q)/2)
    # This is equivalent to 0.5 * (KL(P||M) + KL(Q||M)) if M is average.
    # Let's implement the source formula directly for clarity.
    term1 = torch.sum(p * torch.log(q + eps))
    term2 = torch.sum(q * torch.log(p + eps))
    denom = torch.sum((p + q) / 2.0)
    return 0.5 * (term1 + term2) / denom

def max_min_norm(x):
    x_min = x.min()
    x_max = x.max()
    return (x - x_min) / (x_max - x_min + 1e-10)

def compute_depth_loss(t1, t2):
    t1_norm = max_min_norm(t1)
    t2_norm = max_min_norm(t2)
    return torch.sum((t1_norm - t2_norm) ** 2)

def compute_rgb_loss(c1, c2):
    return torch.sum((c1 - c2) ** 2)

def compute_normal_loss(n1, n2):
    l1_norm = torch.sum(torch.abs(n1 - n2))
    dot_prod = torch.sum(n1 * n2)
    l2_norm = torch.sum(torch.abs(1.0 - dot_prod))
    return l1_norm + l2_norm

def compute_matching_cost(ray_bundle, gt_rgb, gt_depth, gt_normal):
    # ray_bundle contains predicted values from NeRF
    pred_rgb, pred_depth, pred_normal = ray_bundle
    lambda1, lambda2, lambda3 = 0.1, 1.0, 0.05
    cost = (lambda1 * compute_depth_loss(pred_depth, gt_depth) +
            lambda2 * compute_rgb_loss(pred_rgb, gt_rgb) +
            lambda3 * compute_normal_loss(pred_normal, gt_normal))
    return cost

def compute_sdc_loss(hard_rays, neighbor_rays, lambda1_prime, lambda2_prime, lambda3_prime):
    # hard_rays, neighbor_rays are MLP outputs (features)
    r1 = hard_rays
    r2 = neighbor_rays
    phi_r1 = softmax(r1)
    phi_r2 = softmax(r2)
    
    jsd = jsd_loss(phi_r1, phi_r2)
    # Assuming r1, r2 also contain RGB/Depth info or we extract them. 
    # Source says r1, r2 are MLP output features. 
    # For simplicity, assuming we have access to rendered RGB/Depth from these rays.
    # In practice, this requires rendering.
    pred_rgb_1, pred_depth_1 = render_rgb_depth(r1) 
    pred_rgb_2, pred_depth_2 = render_rgb_depth(r2)
    
    rgb_loss = compute_rgb_loss(pred_rgb_1, pred_rgb_2)
    depth_loss = compute_depth_loss(pred_depth_1, pred_depth_2)
    
    return lambda1_prime * jsd + lambda2_prime * rgb_loss + lambda3_prime * depth_loss

def compute_tic_loss(r_cur, r_next):
    phi_cur = softmax(r_cur)
    phi_next = softmax(r_next)
    return jsd_loss(phi_cur, phi_next)

def rigid_alignment(P, Q):
    # P, Q are sets of points (N x 3)
    # Find closest pairs
    # This is a simplified representation of the min-min distance assignment
    # In practice, use KNN or similar
    dists = torch.cdist(P, Q) # (N, M)
    # Find min for each p in P
    min_dists, min_indices = torch.min(dists, dim=1)
    
    # Corresponding points
    matched_Q = Q[min_indices]
    
    # Solve for R, t using SVD (Procrustes analysis)
    # Center points
    P_centered = P - P.mean(dim=0)
    Q_centered = matched_Q - matched_Q.mean(dim=0)
    
    H = P_centered.T @ Q_centered
    U, S, Vt = torch.linalg.svd(H)
    R = Vt.T @ U.T
    
    # Check determinant
    if torch.det(R) < 0:
        Vt[-1, :] *= -1
        R = Vt.T @ U.T
        
    t = matched_Q.mean(dim=0) - (P @ R).mean(dim=0)
    return R, t

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Training is intensive, requiring 20,000 iterations with a batch size of 2048 on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU [S1, unofficial]. The method involves two separate NeRF models and additional consistency loss calculations, increasing memory usage compared to a single NeRF.
*   **Measured Performance:** On a proprietary 20km street-view dataset (180k images), NeRF2Points achieves PSNR 29.139, SSIM 0.831, and Chamfer Distance 1.876 [S1, unofficial]. This outperforms NeRFacto (PSNR 19.788, CD 5.617), Instant-NPG (PSNR 24.169, CD 5.035), Depth-NeRFacto (PSNR 19.653, CD 5.881), Mip-NeRF (PSNR 17.723, CD 6.690), and NeuS (PSNR 21.071, CD 5.327) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively handles large-scale street-view data, prevents pavement collapse, and generates clear RGBD point clouds with distinct features.
*   **Limitations:** Requires significant computational resources and high-quality initial poses (WIGO + COLMAP). Performance on small-scale or object-centered scenes may not be optimal compared to methods like Mip-NeRF.
*   **When to use:** Large-scale outdoor street-view reconstruction where geometric accuracy and point cloud quality are prioritized over pure image rendering speed.
*   **When not to use:** Small-scale indoor scenes or scenarios with limited computational resources.

## Background
NeRF2Points was introduced by Peng Tu et al. in 2024 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of existing NeRF-based methods (like NeRFacto and Instant-NPG) in generating geometrically accurate point clouds from large-scale street-view data. The method builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and extends them with layered perception and geometric consistency regularization.
