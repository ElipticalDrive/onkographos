# Neural Density-Distance Fields

Also called: NeDDF (Neural Density-Distance Fields), Neural Density-Distance Fields (NeDDF) Architecture

## Overview
Neural Density-Distance Fields (NeDDF) is a neural network architecture that represents a scene using a reciprocally constrained distance and density field, extending the distance field formulation to adapt to arbitrary density distributions derived from volume rendering depth [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which predicts density directly, NeDDF predicts a distance field $D$ and derives density $\sigma$ from its first spatial derivative, allowing for boundaryless scenes and complex shapes while providing stable mesh extraction [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Signed Distance Function (SDF) Representations](12495-signed-distance-function-sdf-representations.md) by handling translucent and thin surfaces through volumetric depth rather than hard boundaries, and from [DoF-NeRF](9432-dof-nerf.md) by explicitly linking the distance gradient to the viewing direction for reprojection-based localization [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 3D position $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Distance field value $D(\mathbf{p})$, auxiliary gradient component $\frac{\partial D}{\partial w}$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{v})$ [S1, unofficial]. Density $\sigma(\mathbf{p})$ is derived from $D$ and $\nabla D$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures and Layout:**
The network consists of two parallel MLP branches with a width of 256 neurons per hidden layer [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Distance Branch:**
    *   Input: Concatenated positional encodings (size 144).
    *   Layers: Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation $\rightarrow$ Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation $\rightarrow$ Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation.
    *   Output: $D$ (scalar) and $\frac{\partial D}{\partial w}$ (scalar) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Color Branch:**
    *   Input: Concatenated positional encodings (size 144).
    *   Layers: Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation $\rightarrow$ Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation $\rightarrow$ Dense(256) $\rightarrow$ Activation $\rightarrow$ Dense(128) $\rightarrow$ Activation.
    *   Output: $\mathbf{c}$ (vector, size 3) [S1, unofficial].

**Positional Encoding (PE):**
Standard PE $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ and damped PE $\gamma'(\mathbf{p})$ are used [S1, unofficial]. Let $L$ be the number of frequency bands.
*   Standard PE $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ (size $6L$):
    $$ \gamma(\mathbf{p}) = [\sin(\mathbf{p}), \cos(\mathbf{p}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1}\mathbf{p}), \cos(2^{L-1}\mathbf{p})]^T $$
*   Damped PE $\gamma'(\mathbf{p})$ (size $6L$):
    $$ \gamma'(\mathbf{p}) = [\sin(\mathbf{p}), \cos(\mathbf{p}), \dots, \frac{1}{2^{L-1}}\sin(2^{L-1}\mathbf{p}), \frac{1}{2^{L-1}}\cos(2^{L-1}\mathbf{p})]^T $$
    High-frequency terms are dampened by $1/2^{L-1}$ to maintain scale consistency in the differentiation hierarchy [S1, unofficial].
*   Directional PE $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ (size $6L$) is concatenated for the color branch [S1, unofficial].
*   The input to the MLPs is the concatenation: $[\gamma(\mathbf{p}), \gamma(\mathbf{d}), \gamma'(\mathbf{p})]$ [S1, unofficial]. The source diagram specifies sizes $\gamma(\mathbf{p})=60$, $\gamma(\mathbf{d})=24$, $\gamma'(\mathbf{p})=60$, implying $L=10$ for position and $L=4$ for direction [S1, unofficial].

**Activation Function:**
The network uses `tanhExp` activation, chosen because its second derivative is continuous, which is required for the objective function involving density field gradients [S1, unofficial].

**Density Calculation:**
Density $\sigma(\mathbf{p})$ is derived from the distance field $D(\mathbf{p})$ and its gradient $\nabla D(\mathbf{p})$ [S1, unofficial].
1.  Compute the gradient $\nabla D(\mathbf{p}) = [\frac{\partial D}{\partial p_x}, \frac{\partial D}{\partial p_y}, \frac{\partial D}{\partial p_z}]^T$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  Calculate density using the approximation (assuming near field $t_n \approx 0$):
    $$ \sigma(\mathbf{p}) \simeq \frac{1 - \|\nabla D(\mathbf{p})\|_2}{D(\mathbf{p})} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

**Auxiliary Gradient for Cusp Handling:**
To handle discontinuities in the distance field gradient at cusps, the domain is extended to 4D hyperspace $[x, y, z, w]$ with an auxiliary gradient axis $w$ [S1, unofficial]. The slice at $w=0$ is the distance field [S1, unofficial]. The network outputs $\frac{\partial D}{\partial w}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   The optimal view direction $\mathbf{v}_n$ is recovered from the gradient:
    $$ \mathbf{v}_n = -\frac{\nabla D(\mathbf{p})}{\|\nabla D(\mathbf{p})\|_2} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

**Pseudo-Correspondence and Reprojection:**
For camera pose optimization, pseudo-correspondence points are calculated [S1, unofficial].
1.  For each sampling point $\mathbf{p}_i$, compute the near-surface point $\mathbf{q}_i = \mathbf{p}_i - D_i \frac{\nabla D_i}{\|\nabla D_i\|_2}$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  Calculate weight $g_i$ for combining points:
    $$ g_i = \mathrm{softmax}\left(-\lambda_D \frac{D_i \|\nabla D_i \times \mathbf{v}\|_2}{t_i} - \lambda_c \|\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}) - \mathbf{c}_i\|_2\right) $$
    where $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q})$ is the observed color, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the predicted color, $t_i$ is the camera depth, and $\lambda_D, \lambda_c$ are hyperparameters [S1, unofficial].
3.  The reprojection error is the distance between the pixel coordinate $\mathbf{q}$ and the projected pseudo-correspondence point $\hat{\mathbf{q}}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Training Objectives:**
1.  **Photometric Loss:** Standard color difference between rendered and observed images [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Auxiliary Gradient Constraint ($L_{const}$):**
    $$ L_{\mathrm{const}} = \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{const}}}{M} \sum_{\mathbf{p} \in \mathcal{P}} \beta \left[\frac{\partial^2 D}{\partial t \partial w} - \frac{\alpha}{D(\mathbf{p})} \frac{\partial D}{\partial w}\right]^2 $$
    where $\beta = D \left(\frac{\partial D}{\partial t}\right)^2 \frac{\partial D}{\partial w}$ is separated from the calculation graph (stop-gradient) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Blank Region Penalty ($L_{blank}$):**
    $$ L_{blank} = \sum_i \|\nabla c_i (\nabla D_i)^T \|_2^2 $$
    This penalizes regions outside the object where $\|\nabla D_i\|_2$ is small [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def positional_encoding(p, L):
    scales = np.array([2**i for i in range(L)])
    freqs = np.einsum('...c,d->...cd', p, scales)
    feats = np.stack([np.sin(freqs), np.cos(freqs)], axis=-1)
    return feats.reshape(*p.shape[:-1], -1)

def damped_positional_encoding(p, L):
    scales = np.array([2**i for i in range(L)])
    freqs = np.einsum('...c,d->...cd', p, scales)
    feats = np.stack([np.sin(freqs), np.cos(freqs)], axis=-1)
    dampening = np.zeros_like(scales)
    dampening[-1] = 1.0 / (2**(L-1))
    feats_damped = feats * np.broadcast_to(dampening, feats.shape)
    return feats_damped.reshape(*p.shape[:-1], -1)

def tanh_exp(x):
    return np.tanh(x)

def compute_density(D, grad_D):
    norm_grad = np.linalg.norm(grad_D, axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    sigma = np.where((D > 1e-6) & (norm_grad < 1.0), (1.0 - norm_grad) / D, 0.0)
    return sigma

def numerical_gradient(func, x, eps=1e-4):
    grad = np.zeros_like(x)
    for i in range(x.shape[-1]):
        x_plus = x.copy(); x_plus[..., i] += eps
        x_minus = x.copy(); x_minus[..., i] -= eps
        grad[..., i] = (func(x_plus) - func(x_minus)) / (2 * eps)
    return grad

def volume_render(ox, oy, oz, dx, dy, dz, t_n, t_f, num_samples, network_forward):
    ts = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, num_samples)
    xs = ox + ts * dx
    ys = oy + ts * dy
    zs = oz + ts * dz
    positions = np.stack([xs, ys, zs], axis=-1)
    D, dD_dw, colors = network_forward(positions)
    grad_D = numerical_gradient(lambda p: network_forward(p)[0], positions)
    sigma = compute_density(D, grad_D)
    dt = (t_f - t_n) / (num_samples - 1)
    T = np.ones_like(sigma)
    C = np.zeros_like(colors)
    for i in range(num_samples - 2, -1, -1):
        T[i] = T[i+1] * np.exp(-sigma[i] * dt)
        alpha = 1.0 - np.exp(-sigma[i] * dt)
        C[i] = C[i+1] * T[i] + colors[i] * alpha
    return C[0]

def train_step(positions, directions, observed_colors, camera_poses, 
             lambda_const, alpha, lambda_D, lambda_c):
    pe = positional_encoding(positions, L=10)
    pe_damp = damped_positional_encoding(positions, L=10)
    pe_dir = positional_encoding(directions, L=4)
    input_vec = np.concatenate([pe, pe_dir, pe_damp], axis=-1)
    D, dD_dw, colors = network_forward(input_vec)
    grad_D = numerical_gradient(lambda p: network_forward(p)[0], positions)
    sigma = compute_density(D, grad_D)
    rendered_colors = volume_render(0,0,0, 1,0,0, 0, 10, 128, lambda p: network_forward(p))
    photometric_loss = np.mean((rendered_colors - observed_colors)**2)
    norm_grad = np.linalg.norm(grad_D, axis=-1)
    dD_dt = -norm_grad
    beta = D * (dD_dt**2) * dD_dw
    constraint_term = (d2D_dt_dw - (alpha / D) * dD_dw)**2
    const_loss = lambda_const * np.mean(beta * constraint_term)
    grad_c = numerical_gradient(lambda p: network_forward(p)[2], positions)
    blank_loss = np.mean(np.sum(grad_c * grad_D, axis=-1)**2)
    total_loss = photometric_loss + const_loss + blank_loss
    return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Cusps:** Distance fields are non-differentiable at cusps where the minimum distance direction switches, causing false densities. Mitigated by the auxiliary gradient $\frac{\partial D}{\partial w}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Zero Distance:** Density is undefined for $D \sim 0$ due to numerical instability. The approximation assumes $t_n$ is small [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interior Regions:** As a UDF-based method, it lacks distance field information inside objects, providing no helpful gradient directions in the interior [S1, unofficial].
*   **High-Frequency PE:** Standard PE amplifies high-frequency elements in the density derivative hierarchy by $2^{L-1}$, causing instability. Damped PE is required [S1, unofficial].
*   **Color Reconstruction:** Color field reconstruction is worse than NeRF for the same network size because damped PE neglects high-frequency components in the non-derivative hierarchy [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Provides competitive novel view synthesis quality (PSNR slightly inferior to SOTA NeRF variants but better than NeuS on some metrics) and more stable meshes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles transparency and thin surfaces better than NeuS; supports reprojection-based camera pose localization; boundaryless scene representation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Poor color reconstruction for high frequencies compared to NeRF; undefined density at $D=0$; lack of interior gradient information; computationally expensive optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When stable mesh extraction and camera pose estimation are required, or when representing thin/translucent structures is critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When maximum photometric accuracy (PSNR) is the sole priority, or when interior volume details are needed [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Neural Density-Distance Fields (NeDDF) was proposed in 2022 [S1, unofficial]. It extends the distance field formulation to correspond to various density distributions from depth values derived from volume rendering, addressing the limitation of traditional distance fields that assume the existence of a boundary surface [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the NeRF architecture [S1, unofficial] and NeuS [S1, unofficial], aiming to combine the benefits of density fields (view-dependent color) and distance fields (stable geometry) [S1, unofficial].
