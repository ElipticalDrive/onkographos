# Coarse-to-Fine Model Initialization

Also called: Coarse-to-Fine Initialization, Progressive Scale Training

## Overview
Coarse-to-Fine Model Initialization is a training strategy for differentiable volume rendering that initializes a high-resolution (fine) radiance field model using the optimized parameters of a lower-resolution (coarse) model. It solves the problem of geometric artifacts and slow convergence that occur when training directly at high resolution by establishing a coherent global structure before fitting high-frequency features [S2, unofficial][S3]. It differs from standard full-resolution training by deferring the fitting of fine features until the global geometry is established [S2, unofficial], and differs from simple two-stage pipelines by potentially using continuous or multi-stage resolution scaling rather than a single abrupt switch [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Optimized parameters (weights and feature grids) from a trained coarse model; hyperparameters for resolution scaling and learning rate decay.
*   **Outputs:** Initialized parameters for the fine model, ready for continued optimization.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Coarse Model:** A neural radiance field (e.g., MLP or voxel grid) trained at a base resolution.
*   **Fine Model:** A neural radiance field with the same architecture as the coarse model but at a higher resolution (e.g., doubled voxel grid dimensions).
*   **Feature Grid:** A 3D tensor storing deformation or density features. In voxel-based methods, this is a dense grid; in MLP-based methods, this may refer to the network weights themselves if architectures are identical [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Train Coarse Model:** Optimize the coarse model for a specified number of iterations or until convergence on global geometry [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Interpolate/Transfer Weights:**
    *   If using a voxel grid: Interpolate the optimized feature grid from the coarse model to initialize the fine model's feature grid [S1].
    *   If using an MLP with identical architecture: Directly copy the trained network weights from the coarse deformation module to the fine model [S1].
3.  **Progressive Scale Training (Optional/Variant):** Instead of a single jump, gradually increase the resolution. For example, double the voxel resolution after a set number of training iterations [S1]. This can be viewed as a sequence of coarse-to-fine initializations.
4.  **Resume Optimization:** Continue training the fine model using the initialized parameters. The learning rate is typically decayed from the coarse model's final rate [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Feature Grid Interpolation:** Let $G_{coarse}$ be the coarse feature grid and $G_{fine}$ be the fine feature grid. If the fine grid has resolution $2N \times 2N \times 2N$ and the coarse has $N \times N \times N$, then $G_{fine}[i,j,k]$ is initialized by interpolating values from $G_{coarse}$ [S1].
*   **Loss Function (in context of UP-NeRF):**
    $$ L = w_{u,v} L_{rgb} + (1 - w_{u,v})(L_{feat} + \lambda_d L_{depth}) $$
    where $w_{u,v}$ is a scheduling factor that decays over time, $L_{rgb}$ is the RGB reconstruction loss, $L_{feat}$ is a feature loss, and $L_{depth}$ is a depth loss [S2, unofficial].
*   **Coarse RGB Loss (detached):**
    $$ L_{coarse\_rgb} = \frac{1}{2} \| \hat{C}_i(r) - C_i(r) \|_2^2 $$
    where $\hat{C}_i(r)$ is the rendered color using detached transient outputs and $C_i(r)$ is the target color [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Learning Rate:** Typically starts at $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and decays to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for NeRF and transient networks [S2, unofficial].
*   **Pose/Depth Factor Learning Rate:** Typically starts at $2 \times 10^{-3}$ and decays to $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Sampling Points:** 128 points per ray for both coarse and fine models [S2, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** 600K iterations is a common duration for the full pipeline [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Architecture Mismatch:** If the fine model's architecture differs from the coarse model (e.g., different MLP widths), direct weight copying is not possible, and interpolation must be used for grid-based features [S1].
*   **Overfitting:** Initializing with coarse weights may cause the fine model to overfit to the coarse geometry if not regularized or if the resolution jump is too large [S2, unofficial].
*   **Local Minima:** Without proper initialization (e.g., LiDAR Initialization), the model may struggle to represent sparse space, leading to infinite training times or failure to converge [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Coarse-to-Fine Training Strategy](8900-progressive-coarse-to-fine-training-strategy.md)
Progressive Coarse-to-Fine Training is a strategy that gradually introduces finer levels of detail during optimization to ensure stable convergence and prevent overfitting. It solves the problem of geometric artifacts and slow convergence that occur when training directly at high resolution by establishing a coherent global structure before fitting high-frequency features. It differs from standard full-resolution training by deferring the fitting of fine features until the global geometry is established, and differs from simple two-stage pipelines by potentially using continuous or multi-stage resolution scaling rather than a single abrupt switch.

### [NeRF Restarting Approach](12980-nerf-restarting-approach.md)
The NeRF Restarting Approach is a training schedule for joint pose and radiance field optimization that eliminates artifacts learned during the pose refinement phase by discarding the neural network weights and retraining from scratch. Unlike staged training, which freezes poses and continues training the existing networks, this method re-initializes both coarse and fine Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) after pose convergence. It is chosen when the initial joint optimization introduces persistent artifacts into the scene representation that persist even after pose stabilization.

### Overall Training Pipeline
This variant integrates the coarse-to-fine initialization into a broader pipeline involving transient object handling and depth supervision.
*   **Training Duration:** 600K iterations with 2048 randomly sampled pixel rays per step [S2, unofficial].
*   **Transient Handling:** The TransientNet is optimized with the fine model alone; the coarse model uses detached $\alpha(\tau)$ and $\hat{C}_i(\tau)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Sampling:** The sample set for the fine model uses both shared density $\sigma$ and candidate density $\sigma_i$ until a scheduling factor eliminates the candidate head [S2, unofficial].
*   **Loss:** $L = w_{u,v} L_{rgb} + (1 - w_{u,v})(L_{feat} + \lambda_d L_{depth})$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Learning rate for NeRF/TransientNet: $5e-4$ decaying to $5e-5$. Learning rate for pose/depth: $2e-3$ decaying to $1e-3$. Optimizer: Adam (AdamW for test-time appearance) [S2, unofficial].

### LiDAR Initialization
An initialization technique using LiDAR point clouds to provide prior information for the scene representation, helping to avoid local minima in optimization [S3].
*   **Performance:** On Argoverse2, the combination of Hybrid Scene Representation (HSR) and LiDAR Initialization (LI) yields more than 4 times boost in convergence speed compared with NGP [S3].
*   **Necessity:** Without LI, the model cannot converge to NGP's PSNR, and training time is infinite ('Inf.') because it struggles to represent space sparsely [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduces total training time compared to training from scratch at high resolution. LiDAR Initialization can provide a 4x speedup in convergence [S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing both coarse and fine model parameters during the transition phase, or re-initializing the fine model. Voxel-based methods scale memory with resolution cubed.
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes convergence, prevents geometric artifacts, allows for higher final resolution quality.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful scheduling of resolution jumps and learning rates. May not be suitable for scenes with extreme dynamic changes if the coarse model fails to capture global motion [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When training high-resolution NeRFs or dynamic scenes where direct high-res training is unstable or slow.
*   **When Not to Use:** For low-resolution scenes where the overhead of multi-stage training is unjustified, or when computational resources are extremely limited and a single coarse model suffices.

## Background
Coarse-to-Fine initialization is a common practice in neural rendering to handle the optimization landscape of high-dimensional radiance fields. It was notably employed in methods like BARF (Blending Artificially Realistic Fusion) for unconstrained pose estimation [S2, unofficial] and in dynamic view synthesis methods like Neural Deformable Voxel Grid [S1]. The concept of progressive scale training has also been used in 3D Gaussian Splatting and other grid-based representations. LiDAR Initialization was introduced in Lightning NeRF to address convergence issues in autonomous driving scenes [S3].
