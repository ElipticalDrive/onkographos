# UrbanIR

Also called: Urban Scene Inverse Rendering

## Overview
UrbanIR is a method for the inverse rendering of large-scale, unbounded urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination [S1]. It infers intrinsic scene properties—shape (via density), albedo, surface normals, semantics, and visibility—along with sun and sky illumination parameters, enabling realistic relighting, night simulation, and novel view synthesis [S1]. Unlike standard NeRFs that bake lighting effects into color, or methods requiring LiDAR/multiple cameras (e.g., FEGR, LightSim) or multiple light sources (e.g., NeRF-OSR), UrbanIR relies solely on RGB video and monocular cues like shadows and normals to achieve relightability [S1]. It builds on the spatial hash-based voxel representation of Instant-NGP to handle the scale and memory constraints of outdoor environments [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multi-frame video of a scene under single illumination, defined as a set $\{I_i, E_i, K_i\}$, where $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is the RGB image, $E_i \in SE(3)$ is the camera pose, and $K_i$ is the camera intrinsic matrix [S1].
*   **Output:** A relightable neural scene model capable of rendering novel views under arbitrary lighting conditions, including sun position, ambient light, and sky texture [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene representation is based on Instant-NGP [S1]. It uses a dense feature hash table to map 3D positions to feature vectors. The hash table provides compactness and consistency for expansive outdoor scenes [S1].
*   **Scene Field:** $F_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the 3D position [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Diffuse albedo (intrinsic color/texture) [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Surface normal (intrinsic geometry) [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{s}$: Semantic vector (used to query surface reflectance) [S1].
    *   $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$: Volume density (encodes spatial opacity) [S1].
*   **Lighting Model:** $\mathbf{L} = \{(\mathbf{L}_{\text{sun}}, \psi_{\text{sun}}, \phi_{\text{sun}}), \mathbf{L}_{\text{amb}}, \mathbf{L}_{\text{sky}}\}$ [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sun}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Sun color [S1].
    *   $\psi_{\text{sun}}, \phi_{\text{sun}}$: Sun azimuth and zenith angles (5-DoF sun model) [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{L}_{\text{amb}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Ambient light (Lambertian model, 3-DoF) [S1].
    *   $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sky}}$: Sky texture inferred from viewing direction [S1].
*   **Decoding:** Individual MLP headers are used to decode each attribute ($\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma$) given the queried feature at point $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].

**Rendering Equation:**
The final color $\mathbf{C}$ is computed by combining intrinsic properties with lighting and visibility [S1]:
$$ \mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{L}), \mathbf{L}) $$
where $\mathbf{r}$ is the camera ray and $\mathbf{L}$ is the lighting model [S1].

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **Intrinsic Decomposition via Volume Rendering:**
    For each camera ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample $N$ points along the ray. Let $t_i$ be the distance of the $i$-th sample from the camera origin, and $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ be the segment length [S1]. Compute the alpha-composition weight $w_i$ [S1]:
    $$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    Accumulate the intrinsic attributes using these weights [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i $$
    where $\mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{s}_i$ are the albedo, normal, and semantic vectors predicted by $F_\theta$ at the $i$-th sample point [S1].

2.  **Visibility Estimation (Shadowing):**
    To avoid impractical per-scene optimized MLP heads for visibility (which cannot generalize to changing sun positions during relighting) or explicit geometry models (which may be inconsistent with neural fields), UrbanIR estimates visibility using volume rendering [S1].
    *   Compute the estimated 3D point $\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{r})$ being shaded by volume rendering the depth:
        $$ \hat{t} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i t_i $$
        The point is $\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{o} + \hat{t}\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the ray direction [S1].
    *   Estimate the visibility $V(\mathbf{x}, \text{sun})$ by computing the transmittance along the ray segment between $\mathbf{x}$ and the light source (sun) using volume rendering [S1]. This assumes shadows are primarily due to the visibility of a single directional sunlight [S1].

3.  **Shading:**
    Combine the intrinsic attributes $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}), \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r})$, the visibility $V$, and the lighting model $\mathbf{L}$ (sun color, ambient, sky) using a shading function to produce the final RGB color $\mathbf{C}$ [S1].

4.  **Optimization:**
    Jointly optimize the scene parameters $\theta$ and lighting parameters $\mathbf{L}$ by minimizing a loss function [S1]:
    $$ \min_{\theta, \mathbf{L}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{render}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\dots} + \dots $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{render}}$ is the reconstruction loss between rendered and input images, and other terms (not fully detailed in the provided text) likely enforce consistency or regularization [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Hash Table:** Details of the hash table size and resolution are not given in the sources, but it is based on Instant-NGP [S1].
*   **Lighting:** Sun position is optimized as part of the training [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Standard NeRF Limitations:** Standard NeRFs bake shading, shadow, and specularity into observed color, making them non-relightable. UrbanIR solves this by learning intrinsic attributes independent of lighting [S1].
*   **Visibility Modeling:** Modeling visibility with a per-scene optimized MLP head is impractical because the sun position changes during relighting, but training data only has one sun position [S1].
*   **Scale:** The method is designed for large-scale, unbounded urban scenes, which require the compactness of hash-based representations to manage memory [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The use of Instant-NGP's spatial hash-based voxel representation offers low memory consumption and high efficiency in training and rendering compared to dense voxel grids or unbounded NeRFs [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** The method is specifically designed to scale to large outdoor scenes, unlike object-centric or small bounded indoor methods [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Requires only single monocular video input (no LiDAR, no multi-camera setups, no multiple light sources) [S1].
    *   Enables realistic relighting, night simulation, and object insertion [S1].
    *   Handles large-scale, unbounded scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Assumes single illumination during training (though it can relight to other times of day) [S1].
    *   Relies on monocular cues (shadows, normals) which may be ambiguous in textureless regions or uniform lighting [S1].
*   **When to use:** When relightable inverse rendering of large urban scenes is needed from a single video, and LiDAR/multi-camera data is unavailable.
*   **When not to use:** When multiple light sources are present during capture, or when high-fidelity specular reflections are critical and cannot be approximated by the intrinsic model [S1].

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced by Chih-Hao Lin, Bohan Liu, Yi-Ting Chen, Kuan-Sheng Chen, David Forsyth, Jia-Bin Huang, Anand Bhattad, and Shenlong Wang from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and the University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. The paper was published in 3D Vision 2025 (arXiv:2306.09349v4, Jan 14, 2025) [S1]. It addresses the gap in inverse rendering methods for large-scale urban scenes, which object-centric methods fail to reconstruct, and overcomes the limitations of existing outdoor scene models that require LiDAR or multiple cameras (e.g., FEGR, LightSim) or multiple lighting sources (e.g., NeRF-OSR) [S1].
