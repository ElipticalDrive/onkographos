# Trainable Camera Parameters

Also called: Camera Parameter Optimization, Camera Parameters Joint Learning, Joint Camera-ndf Optimization, NeRF Initialization with Trainable Camera Parameters

## Overview
This method optimizes camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters jointly with the neural radiance field (NeRF) during training to handle scenarios where Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines like COLMAP fail, such as small camera motion videos or narrow-baseline structured light fields [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike methods that train camera parameters from scratch, this approach initializes residuals from a prior estimate (e.g., RCVD) and optimizes additive or multiplicative corrections [S1]. It builds on [Camera and Pose Optimization](21918-camera-and-pose-optimization.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Initial camera extrinsics $P_{init}$ and focal lengths $f_{init}$ for each frame, obtained from a prior estimation method (e.g., RCVD [S1] or COLMAP [S2, unofficial]).
- Ground-truth images $I_{gt}$ for each frame.
- NeRF model parameters (MLP weights and biases).

**Outputs:**
- Optimized camera extrinsics $P$ and focal lengths $f$.
- Optimized NeRF model parameters.

**Data Structures:**
- For each frame $i$:
  - $P_{init}^{(i)}$: Initial $4 \times 4$ camera extrinsic matrix (or equivalent representation).
  - $f_{init}^{(i)}$: Initial scalar focal length.
  - $\Delta P^{(i)}$: Residual extrinsic variable, same size and type as $P_{init}^{(i)}$.
  - $\Delta f^{(i)}$: Residual focal length variable, same size and type as $f_{init}^{(i)}$.
  - $P^{(i)}$: Final extrinsic, computed as $P_{init}^{(i)} \cdot \Delta P^{(i)}$.
  - $f^{(i)}$: Final focal length, computed as $f_{init}^{(i)} + \Delta f^{(i)}$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Initialization:**
   - Obtain $P_{init}$ and $f_{init}$ for all frames using a prior method (e.g., RCVD [S1] or COLMAP [S2, unofficial]).
   - Initialize residual variables $\Delta P^{(i)}$ and $\Delta f^{(i)}$ to zero for all frames $i$ [S1].
   - Initialize NeRF model parameters (weights $W$, biases $b$) [S2, unofficial].

2. **Ray Generation:**
   - For each training image $i$, generate rays using the current camera parameters $P^{(i)}$ and $f^{(i)}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
   - For structured light fields, rays may be defined using only focal length $f$ and a parallax offset $\Delta$ (proportional to angular distance) [S2, unofficial].

3. **Rendering:**
   - Render the image $I'$ by querying the NeRF model along the generated rays [S2, unofficial].

4. **Loss Computation:**
   - Compute the rendering loss, typically the mean squared error between the rendered image $I'$ and the ground-truth image $I_{gt}$ [S2, unofficial]:
     $$ \mathcal{L} = || I' - I_{gt} ||_2^2 $$
   - Additional constraints (e.g., depth smoothing gradient loss) may be added [S1].

5. **Optimization:**
   - Update all trainable parameters jointly using gradient descent:
     - NeRF parameters: $W, b$
     - Camera residuals: $\Delta P^{(i)}, \Delta f^{(i)}$ for all frames $i$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
   - Repeat until convergence.

**Formulas:**
- Final focal length:
  $$ f = f_{init} + \Delta f \quad (1) $$
  where $f$ is the final focal length, $f_{init}$ is the initial focal length, and $\Delta f$ is the learned residual [S1].
- Final camera extrinsic:
  $$ P = P_{init} \cdot \Delta P \quad (2) $$
  where $P$ is the final camera extrinsic, $P_{init}$ is the initial extrinsic, and $\Delta P$ is the learned residual [S1].
- Optimization objective (for structured light fields):
  $$ \arg\min_{W, b, f, \Delta} || c' - c_{gt} ||_2^2 \quad (3) $$
  where $c'$ is the rendered pixel value and $c_{gt}$ is the ground-truth pixel value [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Learning rate for camera residuals and NeRF parameters: Not given in the sources.
- Initialization method for $P_{init}$ and $f_{init}$: RCVD [S1] or COLMAP [S2, unofficial].
- For structured light fields, only focal length $f$ and parallax offset $\Delta$ are required for ray tracing [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Small Camera Motion:** COLMAP may fail to predict camera parameters; this method addresses this by optimizing residuals [S1].
- **Narrow-Baseline Light Fields:** COLMAP may struggle; treating camera pose parameters as trainable variables helps [S2, unofficial].
- **Initialization:** Residuals are initialized to zero, ensuring the initial camera parameters are close to the prior estimate [S1].
- **Depth Noise:** Depth maps may display noise; gradient loss can be added to alleviate this [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Initialization
P_init = get_initial_extrinsics_from_rcvd() # or COLMAP
f_init = get_initial_focal_lengths_from_rcvd() # or COLMAP
Delta_P = zeros_like(P_init)
Delta_f = zeros_like(f_init)
nerf_params = initialize_nerf()

# Training Loop
for epoch in range(num_epochs):
    for image, gt_image in training_data:
        # Compute final camera parameters
        P = P_init * Delta_P # Element-wise or matrix multiplication as defined
        f = f_init + Delta_f
        
        # Generate rays using P and f
        rays = generate_rays(P, f)
        
        # Render image
        rendered_image = nerf_render(nerf_params, rays)
        
        # Compute loss
        loss = mse(rendered_image, gt_image)
        
        # Backpropagate and update
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        optimizer.zero_grad()
```

## Subtopics

### [Joint Optimization of Scene and Calibration Parameters](21724-joint-optimization-of-scene-and-calibration-parameters.md)
Treats camera pose parameters as trainable variables, particularly for narrow-baseline light fields. Choose when dealing with structured light fields where COLMAP struggles.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of training iterations and the size of the NeRF model. Joint optimization may increase training time compared to fixed-camera NeRF.
- **Memory Complexity:** Additional memory for storing residual variables $\Delta P$ and $\Delta f$ for each frame.
- **Strengths:** Handles cases where SfM fails (small motion, narrow baseline). Improves reconstruction quality in challenging scenarios.
- **Limitations:** Requires a reasonable initial estimate (e.g., from RCVD or COLMAP). May be sensitive to initialization if the prior is poor.
- **When to Use:** Small camera motion videos, narrow-baseline structured light fields.
- **When Not to Use:** Well-calibrated datasets with sufficient camera motion where COLMAP performs reliably.

## Background
- **Origin:** Proposed in "Dynamic View Synthesis From Small Camera Motion Videos" [S1] to address COLMAP failures in small motion videos. Also adopted in "Distilled Low Rank Neural Radiance Field with Quantization for Light Field Compression" [S2, unofficial] for narrow-baseline light fields.
- **History:** Builds on the idea of treating camera parameters as trainable variables, as seen in earlier works [20] cited in [S2, unofficial].
- **Usage:** Used in dynamic NeRF methods and light field compression pipelines.
