# A-NeRF

Also called: Articulated Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
A-NeRF is a method for learning a user-specific neural 3D body model and underlying skeleton pose from unlabelled monocular videos by extending Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) with a skeleton to apply to time-varying and articulated motion [S1, unofficial]. The core technical novelty is defining spatial latent variables relative to the pose of body parts to overcome ill-posed inverse operations with an overparameterization, embedding domain knowledge of how humans move [S1, unofficial]. Unlike explicit models like SMPL that deform a surface via forwards kinematics, A-NeRF uses implicit networks requiring inverse transformation from 3D world coordinates to reference skeleton [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sequence of $N$ images $[I_k]_{k=1}^N$ from one or several videos of the same person. Initial estimates of camera intrinsics, bone lengths, and skeleton pose $\hat{\theta}_k$ are obtained from an off-the-shelf estimator (e.g., SPIN) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Refined skeleton poses $\theta_k$ for each frame, neural network parameters $\phi$ defining volumetric shape and color, and rendered images $C_\phi(\theta_k)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Skeleton Representation:**
    *   Rest pose: 3D joint locations $\tilde{a}_m$ for $m=1..24$, root at 0, connected via $B$ bones [S1, unofficial].
    *   Per-frame pose $\theta_k$: Includes relative rotation of 24 joints $\tilde{\theta}_{k,m}$ to parents and global position $\tilde{\theta}_{k,0}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Network Architecture:**
    *   Follows original NeRF [42].
    *   Inputs: Position, direction, appearance code, and Relative Ray (Rel. Ray.) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Intermediate features: 10 layers of size 256, followed by 1 layer of size 128 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Outputs: Density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Activations: ReLU for intermediate layers, sigmoid for output, no activation for specific connections [S1, unofficial].
    *   Appearance code and Rel. Ray. are fed into the network in the second last fully-connected layer [S1, unofficial].

**Positional Encoding (PE):**
*   $\Gamma$ maps input scene point $q$ and view direction $d$ to a higher dimensional space [S1, unofficial].
*   Frequencies: $L_{input} = 7$ for position, $L_{view} = 4$ for view direction [S1, unofficial].

**Skeleton-Relative Encoding:**
*   To resolve the ill-posed inverse problem, a single 3D query location maps to an overcomplete reparametrization represented relative to each skeleton bone [S1, unofficial].
*   **Bone-relative Position (Rel. Pos.):** Distance from query point to joint $m$.
*   **Bone-relative Direction (Rel. Dir.):** Angle between ray direction and vector from query point to joint $m$.
*   **Rel. Ray:** Combination of Bone-relative Position and Bone-relative Direction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Cutoff:** A cutoff distance $t$ and temperature $\tau$ are applied to limit the influence of distant bones. $t$ starts at 500mm and decays exponentially over 250k iterations. $\tau$ starts at -20 and decays exponentially to -200 over 500k iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Training Objective:**
The objective function $L_{SV}(\theta, \phi)$ consists of three terms [S1, unofficial]:
$$ L_{SV}(\theta, \phi) = \sum_k \|C_\phi(\theta_k) - I_k\|_1 + \lambda_\theta \sum_k d(\theta_k - \hat{\theta}_k) + \lambda_t \sum_k \|\frac{\partial^2 \theta_k}{\partial t^2}\|^2 $$
Where:
*   Data term: L1 distance between generated images and input images [S1, unofficial].
*   Pose regularizer: Encourages solution to stay close to initial pose estimate $\hat{\theta}_k$. $d(x) = \min(\|x\|_2 - \epsilon, 0)$ where $\epsilon = 0.01$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Smoothness prior: Penalizes acceleration $\frac{\partial^2 \theta_k}{\partial t^2}$ between poses of consecutive frames [S1, unofficial].
*   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_\theta$ balances pose regularizer, $\lambda_t$ balances smoothness prior [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize camera intrinsics, bone lengths $a_m$, and pose $\theta_k$ using SPIN [26] for every frame $k$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Joint Optimization:** Optimize poses $\theta_k$ and neural parameters $\phi$ (shape/appearance) for 500k iterations on objective Eq. 2 [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Fine-tuning:** Stop optimizing pose and continue training $\phi$ with only the data term for additional 200k iterations [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Batch Sampling:** Form a training batch by randomly sampling $N_{batch} = 3072$ rays from all available images [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Gradient Accumulation:** Accumulate gradient update $\nabla \theta$ for $G \times (30 \times N_{batch}) / N$ iterations so that each $\theta_k$ is expected to be sampled 30 times before update [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Importance Sampling:** Use coarse-to-fine importance sampling strategy. Restrict samples to be within a coarse human silhouette estimated with [10] unless the background is monochrome [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Rendering:** Render images via classical ray-marching from computer graphics using the MLP $F_\phi$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes approximately 60 hours on 2 Nvidia Tesla V100 32GB GPUs [S1, unofficial]. Rendering time is 1-4 seconds per 512x512x3 image on a single Nvidia Tesla V100 GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Encoding positions relative to the skeleton has high dimensionality compared to world coordinates [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Learns volumetric body shape and appearance from scratch while jointly refining the articulated pose without ground truth labels [S1, unofficial].
    *   Works better than NeuralBody for longer sequences and retargeting to diverse poses [S1, unofficial].
    *   Can be trained with only a single view; adding more views does not help if poses are not diverse enough [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Using World Ray direction causes overfitting in static/estimated camera settings, leading to flickering results for novel view rendering [S1, unofficial].
    *   Encoding world coordinates directly fails on motions and has large memory consumption [S1, unofficial].
    *   Straightforward baselines conditioning directly on pose perform worst [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When learning a neural body model from monocular video is required, especially for novel-view-synthesis and motion capture [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When surface supervision or initialization is available, or when the scene is static [S1, unofficial].

## Background
A-NeRF builds on NeRF [42] and uses appearance code design from [36, 49] [S1, unofficial]. It was proposed to learn a generative neural body model from unlabelled monocular videos by extending Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) with a skeleton to apply to time-varying and articulated motion [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the challenge of implicit models requiring the inverse of the forward kinematics used in explicit surface models [S1, unofficial].
