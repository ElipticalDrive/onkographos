# NeRF Restarting Approach

Also called: Joint Pose-NeRF Refinement with Restart, Pose-Constrained NeRF Restart

## Overview
The NeRF Restarting Approach is a training schedule for joint pose and radiance field optimization that eliminates artifacts learned during the pose refinement phase by discarding the neural network weights and retraining from scratch [S1]. Unlike staged training, which freezes poses and continues training the existing networks, this method re-initializes both coarse and fine Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) after pose convergence [S1]. It is chosen when the initial joint optimization introduces persistent artifacts into the scene representation that persist even after pose stabilization [S1].

## How it works
The method consists of three distinct phases [S1]. It takes as input a set of $N$ images with noisy initial camera poses and a target radiance field representation defined by a coarse MLP $F_{\theta_c}$ and a fine MLP $F_{\theta_f}$ [G]. The output is the optimized camera poses and the final radiance field parameters [G].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Poses:** A set of $N$ transformation matrices $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ or corresponding rotation/translation vectors [G].
*   **Coarse MLP ($F_{\theta_c}$):** A neural network mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color [G]. Parameters $\theta_c$ are initialized from scratch in Phase 3 [S1].
*   **Fine MLP ($F_{\theta_f}$):** A neural network mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to color (and optionally density) [G]. Parameters $\theta_f$ are initialized from scratch in Phase 3 [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Phase 1: Joint Pose and Network Training**
    *   Initialize $F_{\theta_c}$ and $F_{\theta_f}$ from scratch [G].
    *   Initialize camera poses $P_i$ with the provided noisy estimates [G].
    *   Perform gradient descent to jointly optimize the network parameters $\theta_c, \theta_f$ and the camera poses $P_i$ [S1].
    *   Continue until the camera pose estimates converge [S1].

2.  **Phase 2: Pose Freezing and Network Re-initialization**
    *   Freeze the optimized camera poses $P_i^{*}$ obtained from Phase 1 [S1].
    *   Discard the current weights $\theta_c$ and $\theta_f$ learned in Phase 1 [S1].
    *   Re-initialize the parameters for both the coarse MLP $F_{\theta_c}$ and the fine MLP $F_{\theta_f}$ from scratch [S1].

3.  **Phase 3: Fixed-Pose Radiance Field Training**
    *   Train the re-initialized $F_{\theta_c}$ and $F_{\theta_f}$ using the fixed, optimized poses $P_i^{*}$ [S1].
    *   The optimization minimizes the rendering loss with respect to $\theta_c$ and $\theta_f$ only [G].
    *   This phase allows the networks to learn sharp geometry without the interference of pose uncertainty [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_nerf_with_restarting(images, initial_poses, coarse_mlp_init, fine_mlp_init, learning_rate, phase1_max_epochs, phase3_max_epochs):
    # Phase 1: Jointly train the NeRF with camera poses until pose convergence [S1]
    poses = initial_poses.clone()
    theta_c = coarse_mlp_init.clone()
    theta_f = fine_mlp_init.clone()
    
    for epoch in range(phase1_max_epochs):
        rays = sample_rays_from_images(images)
        rendered_colors = render_rays(poses, theta_c, theta_f, rays)
        loss = compute_mse_loss(rendered_colors, images[rays.pixel_indices])
        
        theta_c = gradient_descent_step(theta_c, loss, learning_rate)
        theta_f = gradient_descent_step(theta_f, loss, learning_rate)
        poses = gradient_descent_step(poses, loss, learning_rate)
        
        if pose_convergence_check(poses, threshold=1e-4):
            break
            
    # Phase 2: Freeze the optimized pose estimates and re-initialize both coarse and fine MLPs from scratch [S1]
    optimized_poses = poses.detach()
    theta_c = coarse_mlp_init.clone()
    theta_f = fine_mlp_init.clone()
    
    # Phase 3: Train both MLPs from scratch using the fixed optimized poses [S1]
    for epoch in range(phase3_max_epochs):
        rays = sample_rays_from_images(images)
        rendered_colors = render_rays(optimized_poses, theta_c, theta_f, rays)
        loss = compute_mse_loss(rendered_colors, images[rays.pixel_indices])
        
        theta_c = gradient_descent_step(theta_c, loss, learning_rate)
        theta_f = gradient_descent_step(theta_f, loss, learning_rate)
        
    return optimized_poses, theta_c, theta_f
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Pose Convergence Criteria:** If Phase 1 stops too early, the frozen poses in Phase 2 will be inaccurate, leading to poor reconstruction [G].
*   **Re-initialization Strategy:** The method relies on the assumption that the artifacts are tied to the specific weight configuration of Phase 1 [S1].

## Subtopics

### Staged Training
Staged training is a variant where the camera poses are refined while training the coarse network, and then the poses are frozen while training both coarse and fine networks, without re-initializing the weights [S1]. It prevents blurry surfaces compared to joint training of all parameters because the fine network learns sharp geometry from frozen, registered poses and a pre-trained coarse network [S1]. On DTU with 3 views and 15% initial additive Gaussian noise, staged training achieves PSNR 17.58 (18.62 masked), SSIM 0.71 (0.82 masked), LPIPS 0.26 (0.13 masked), and DE 1.81 [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On DTU with 3 views and 15% initial additive Gaussian noise, the restarting approach achieves PSNR 17.80 (19.07 masked), SSIM 0.72 (0.83 masked), LPIPS 0.25 (0.12 masked), and DE 1.84 [S1]. This outperforms staged training (PSNR 17.58) and joint training of poses with both coarse and fine MLPs, which often results in slightly blurry surfaces [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Removes artifacts learned during pose optimization that may persist in staged training [S1]. Provides the best rendering quality among the compared schedules [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires more training time due to the restart [G]. Sensitive to the convergence criterion of Phase 1 [G].

## Background
The NeRF Restarting Approach was introduced in the context of optimizing Neural Radiance Fields from sparse and noisy poses [S1]. It was identified as the best alternative for joint pose-NeRF training in ablation studies comparing various training schedules [S1]. The method addresses the issue of artifacts persisting in the scene representation when poses and networks are optimized jointly or in a staged manner without weight reset [S1].
