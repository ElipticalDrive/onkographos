# Uncertainty and Reliability

Also called: Uncertainty-aware NeRF, UA-PlantNeRF, Recursive-NeRF, NeuMan Error Correction

## Overview
Uncertainty and Reliability methods extend standard NeRF architectures to quantify prediction confidence, addressing the limitations of deterministic outputs in sparse-view, occluded, or dynamic scenes. Unlike standard NeRF which minimizes mean squared error (MSE) or standard negative log-likelihood (NLL) without explicit reliability metrics, these methods output probabilistic distributions or calibrated uncertainty bounds, enabling the filtering of artifacts, weighting of training samples, and coverage guarantees [S1, 12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md, 13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md].

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) serves as the baseline for most modifications. It consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a coarse network and a fine network. The coarse network takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs a latent feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and volume density $\sigma$. The fine network takes $\mathbf{h}$, positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and positional encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$ [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
$$

Modifications generally fall into three categories [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs).

## Subtopics

### [Uncertainty-aware NeRF](12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md)
Uncertainty-aware NeRF (U-NeRF) explicitly models aleatoric uncertainty for rendered color and depth, outputting Gaussian distributions rather than deterministic values [12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md]. It addresses Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) limitations by providing pixel-level reliability metrics to filter synthetic artifacts and weight training samples [12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md]. Unlike standard NeRF, it optimizes the negative log-likelihood (NLL) augmented by a variance-weighting term to prevent premature convergence in hard-to-fit regions [12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md].

### [UA-PlantNeRF Architecture](13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md)
UA-PlantNeRF is a unified framework for reconstructing volumetric radiance fields from sparse, unstructured video footage of plants, addressing degraded geometry and the absence of calibrated uncertainty estimates [13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md]. It distinguishes itself by explicitly decomposing uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic components via a dual-head architecture and Monte Carlo Dropout, while employing conformal prediction for distribution-free coverage guarantees [13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md]. Unlike [Uncertainty-aware NeRF](12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md), which focuses on general scene uncertainty, UA-PlantNeRF is specialized for botanical structures, incorporating semantic consistency losses to prevent geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions [13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md].

### Recursive-NeRF
Recursive-NeRF (2022) dynamically adjusts to scene intricacies by continuously implementing the NeRF framework and expanding the network architecture based on uncertainty forecasts at every phase [S1]. It determines the transfer of query coordinates to a more advanced neural network depending on the uncertainty degree, possessing the capability to dynamically modify the network’s architecture across various image resolutions to cater to tasks of diverse complexity [S1]. Experimental findings indicate it surpasses conventional NeRF in performance across public and extensive scene datasets [S1].

### Error Correction and Scene Conditioning
This variant uses an error correction network and conditioning on a scene NeRF to prevent the canonical NeRF from hallucinating clothing dynamics or background colors [S2]. Training only the human NeRF in isolation leads to worse performance because the human NeRF model may encode background pixels into its radiance fields due to segmentation errors [S2]. Without error correction or scene conditioning, the canonical NeRF creates fogs around the human to hallucinate the clothing dynamics or the background colors [S2].

## Background
Uncertainty quantification in NeRFs emerged to address the brittleness of deterministic radiance fields in sparse-view and unstructured settings. Early work like U-NeRF introduced probabilistic rendering to handle synthetic data artifacts in Scene Coordinate Regression [12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md]. Subsequent developments like UA-PlantNeRF targeted specific domains (botany) requiring calibrated uncertainty for sparse video inputs [13768-ua-plantnerf-architecture.md]. Recursive-NeRF (2022) introduced dynamic network expansion based on uncertainty [S1], while NeuMan addressed human-specific artifacts through error correction and scene conditioning [S2].
