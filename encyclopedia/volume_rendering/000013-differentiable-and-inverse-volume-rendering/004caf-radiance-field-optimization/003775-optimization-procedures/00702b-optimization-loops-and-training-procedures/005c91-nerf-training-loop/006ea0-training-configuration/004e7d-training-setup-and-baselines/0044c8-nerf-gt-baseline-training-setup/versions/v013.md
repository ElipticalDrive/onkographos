# NeRF-GT Baseline Training Setup

Also called: NeRF-GT

## Overview
The NeRF-GT baseline establishes a reference training configuration for standard Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) using Low Dynamic Range (LDR) inputs, serving as a ground-truth comparison for high-dynamic-range variants [S1]. It differs from HDR-specific baselines by strictly adhering to the original NeRF optimization protocol on consistent LDR views, avoiding the encoding difficulties associated with high dynamic range data [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of LDR images with consistent exposure levels and their corresponding camera poses [S1].
*   **Output:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters) and associated data structures [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The implementation relies on the standard NeRF coordinate-based MLP architecture. The specific data layout for the training loop involves:
*   **Ray Batch:** A tensor of shape $[B, 3]$ containing the origin and direction for $B$ rays [S2, unofficial].
*   **Sample Points:** A tensor of shape $[B, N, 3]$ containing 3D coordinates sampled along the rays, where $N$ is the total number of samples per ray (coarse + fine) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Network Input:** The coordinates are processed through positional encoding (not detailed in the baseline notes, but implied by the standard NeRF architecture referenced in [S2, unofficial]).

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling:** Generate rays from the camera poses. Sample $N_c = 64$ points in the coarse phase and $N_f = 128$ points in the fine phase along each ray [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Batching:** Process a batch of $B = 2,048$ rays simultaneously [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Forward Pass:** Query the NeRF network to predict volume density ($\sigma$) and view-dependent color ($c$) for all sampled points [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Volume Rendering:** Compute the rendered color $C(r)$ for each ray $r$ using the differentiable volume rendering integral [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered LDR colors and the ground truth LDR pixel values [S1][S2, unofficial].
6.  **Optimization:** Update the network weights using stochastic gradient descent (Adam optimizer, as per shared principles) [S2, unofficial].
7.  **Iteration:** Repeat steps 1-6 for a total of 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Coarse Samples ($N_c$):** 64 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Fine Samples ($N_f$):** 128 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size ($B$):** 2,048 rays [S2, unofficial].
*   **Resolution:** 800 × 600 pixels [S2, unofficial].
*   **Total Iterations:** 200,000 solver steps [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **HDR Failure Mode:** Training directly on HDR views (as a variant of this setup) can fail to encode all scene areas well due to the high dynamic range, even if tone-mapped colors are used for loss calculation [S1]. The NeRF-GT baseline avoids this by using consistent LDR views [S1].

## Subtopics

### [DBARF](11583-dbarf.md)
DBARF jointly optimizes Generalizable Neural Radiance Fields (GeNeRFs) and relative camera poses in a self-supervised manner without requiring accurate initial poses. It differs from BARF, which relies on coarse-to-fine training with positional encodings absent in GeNeRFs, by using a RAFT-like recurrent network to implicitly minimize feature consistency errors, bypassing direct gradient descent on the cost function. See [DBARF](11583-dbarf.md).

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Training Time:** Approximately 0.40 seconds per iteration [S2, unofficial].
*   **Rendering Time:** Approximately 32 seconds per frame [S2, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** The method scales linearly with the number of samples and batch size. The timing results were derived by sampling 100 iteration times and averaging them, then extrapolating [S2, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Provides a stable baseline for LDR scenes with consistent exposure [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Not suitable for scenes with high dynamic range without modification, as direct HDR training leads to poor encoding of scene areas [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Use for standard LDR novel view synthesis where camera poses are known and exposure is consistent [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Use as a baseline for comparison against HDR-NeRF methods [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Do not use for HDR novel view synthesis without the specific tone-mapping and HDR-aware modifications described in HDR-NeRF [S1].

## Background
The NeRF-GT baseline is derived from the original NeRF paper [S2, unofficial]. It was used as a reference point in the evaluation of HDR-NeRF to demonstrate the limitations of standard NeRF when applied to high dynamic range content [S1]. The specific hyperparameters (64/128 samples, 2048 batch size, 200k steps) were taken from the official NeRF configuration (`config_fern.txt`) provided by the authors [S2, unofficial].
