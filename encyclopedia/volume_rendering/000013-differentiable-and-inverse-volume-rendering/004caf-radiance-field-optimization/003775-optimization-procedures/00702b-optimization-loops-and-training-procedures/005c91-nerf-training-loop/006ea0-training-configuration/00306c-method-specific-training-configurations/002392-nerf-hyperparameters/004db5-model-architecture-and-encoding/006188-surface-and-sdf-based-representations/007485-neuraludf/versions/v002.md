# NeuralUDF

Also called: Learning Unsigned Distance Fields for Multi-View Reconstruction, Neural Unsigned Distance Fields, NeuralUDF Training with Probabilistic Volume Rendering

## Overview
NeuralUDF is a method for reconstructing surfaces with arbitrary topologies (including open surfaces) from multi-view 2D images using volume rendering, representing the surface as the zero level set of an Unsigned Distance Function (UDF) rather than a Signed Distance Function (SDF) [S1]. Unlike SDF-based methods such as [NeuS](18458-explicit-mesh-and-surface-reconstruction.md) or [VolSDF](18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md) which are limited to closed surfaces, NeuralUDF avoids the inside/outside distinction, enabling the reconstruction of open geometries like garments [S1]. It solves the non-monotonicity and non-injectivity of UDF values along a ray by introducing a differentiable visibility indicator based on probabilistic surface existence and gradient-aware masking [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A set of $M$ images with known camera poses (view directions $\mathbf{v}$ and origins $\mathbf{o}$). Optional binary object masks $\mathbf{M}_k$.
- **Outputs:** A learned neural implicit scene representation consisting of an Unsigned Distance Field $f_u(\mathbf{x})$ and a view-dependent color field $c(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})$, from which an explicit mesh is extracted.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **UDF Network ($f_u$):** An MLP mapping $\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$.
    -   Architecture: 8 hidden layers, size 256.
    -   Input: Positional encoding of spatial location $\mathbf{r}(t)$ (6 frequencies).
    -   Skip connection: Output of the 4th layer concatenated with the input.
    -   Output: Scalar UDF value $f_u(\mathbf{x})$.
2.  **Color Network ($c$):** An MLP mapping $\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^3$.
    -   Architecture: 8 hidden layers, size 256.
    -   Input: Concatenation of positional encoding of view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (4 frequencies), positional encoding of spatial location $\mathbf{r}(t)$, and a 256-dimensional feature vector extracted from the UDF network (typically the output of the 4th hidden layer before the skip connection is applied to the final output, or a specific intermediate feature; the source states "256-dim UDF feature" [S1]).
    -   Output: RGB color $\mathbf{C}$.
3.  **Probabilistic Parameters:** Two trainable scalars $\kappa$ and $\beta$, and two fixed constants $\alpha=20$ and $\tau=25000$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Representation:**
    The scene is represented by two functions:
    -   $f_u(\mathbf{x})$: Unsigned distance from point $\mathbf{x}$ to the nearest surface.
    -   $c(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})$: View-dependent color at $\mathbf{x}$ for viewing direction $\mathbf{v}$.

2.  **Ray Sampling:**
    For a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{v}$, sample $N$ points.
    -   **Hierarchical Sampling:**
        1.  Uniformly sample 64 points along the ray.
        2.  Iteratively perform importance sampling 5 times.
        3.  In iteration $i$ (1 to 5), estimate coarse probability using fixed parameters $\kappa_i = 32 \times 2^i$ and $\beta_i = 32 \times 2^{i+1}$.
        4.  Sample 16 additional points per iteration.
        5.  Total points per ray: $64 + 5 \times 16 = 144$.
    -   Scale the scene to fit within a unit sphere. Space outside the unit sphere is modeled using the NeRF++ approach [S1].

3.  **Surface Existence Probability:**
    Compute the probability $h(t_i)$ that a sample point $\mathbf{r}(t_i)$ lies on the surface. This uses a logistic distribution centered at 0:
    $$
    \phi_\beta(x) = \frac{\beta e^{-\beta x}}{(1 + e^{-\beta x})^2}
    $$
    $$
    h(t_i) = 1 - \exp\left(-\alpha \cdot \phi_\beta(f_u(\mathbf{r}(t_i))) \cdot \delta_i\right)
    $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between adjacent samples, $\alpha=20$ is a constant, and $\beta$ is a trainable parameter [S1].

4.  **Differentiable Visibility Indicator:**
    Standard UDF values are non-negative and non-monotonic across a surface, making it difficult to distinguish the true intersection $t^*$ from other points with small UDF values. NeuralUDF constructs a differentiable visibility indicator $\hat{\Psi}(t_i)$ to accumulate surface existence probability only up to the first intersection.
    
    The indicator is defined as:
    $$
    \hat{\Psi}(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - h(t_j) \cdot m(t_j))
    $$
    where $m(t_j)$ is a masking function that prevents early occlusion by masking points ahead of the intersection where the UDF gradient flips.
    
    The masking function $m(t_j)$ is:
    $$
    m(t_j) = \begin{cases} 
    0 & \text{if } \cos(\theta_{j+1}) < 0 \\
    1 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    where $\theta_{j+1}$ is the angle between the ray direction $\mathbf{v}$ and the UDF gradient $\nabla f_u(\mathbf{r}(t_{j+1}))$. If $\cos(\theta_{j+1}) < 0$, the gradient points against the ray direction, indicating the point is "ahead" of the surface intersection (in the free space before hitting the surface from the camera side), so it is masked out ($m=0$) to prevent it from contributing to the occlusion of subsequent points [S1].
    
    *Note: The source also mentions a term involving $\tau$ in Eq. 11: $\frac{1}{M} \sum \exp(-\tau \cdot f_u(\dots))$. This appears to be part of the probability estimation or regularization context, but the primary visibility formulation relies on the product of $(1-h \cdot m)$ [S1].*

5.  **Density Function:**
    The density $\sigma(t)$ is derived from the UDF to enable volume rendering. To ensure the density is monotonically increasing (unbiased), the method leverages the visibility indicator. The density is modeled such that it peaks at the surface. The specific mapping $\Omega$ transforms $f_u$ into $\sigma$. While the exact closed-form $\Omega$ is not explicitly isolated in the provided text snippets, it relies on the probabilistic model where the effective density contribution is gated by $\hat{\Psi}(t)$ and modulated by the surface existence probability $h(t)$ and the trainable parameter $\kappa$. The rendering equation uses $\sigma(t)$ which is effectively constructed to be monotonic via the visibility indicator mechanism [S1].

6.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute the rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ for the ray:
    $$
    \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \sigma_i \delta_i c(\mathbf{r}(t_i), \mathbf{v})
    $$
    where $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \sigma_j \delta_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance. The density $\sigma_i$ is derived from the UDF field using the probabilistic model described above [S1].

7.  **Loss Function:**
    The total loss $L$ is a weighted sum:
    $$
    L = L_{color} + \lambda_0 L_{patch} + \lambda_1 L_{eik} + \lambda_2 L_{reg} + \gamma L_{mask}
    $$
    -   **Color Loss ($L_{color}$):** L1 loss between rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_k$ and ground truth $\mathbf{C}_k$:
        $$
        L_{color} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=1}^{M} |\hat{\mathbf{C}}_k - \mathbf{C}_k|
        $$
    -   **Patch Loss ($L_{patch}$):** Structural Similarity (SSIM) between rendered patch $\hat{\mathbf{P}}_k$ and ground truth patch $\mathbf{P}_k$:
        $$
        L_{patch} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=1}^{M} (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_k, \mathbf{P}_k))
        $$
    -   **Eikonal Loss ($L_{eik}$):** Regularizes the gradient norm of the UDF to 1:
        $$
        L_{eik} = \frac{1}{NM} \sum_{k,i} (\|\nabla f_u(\mathbf{r}_k(t_{k,i}))\|^2 - 1)^2
        $$
    -   **Regularization ($L_{reg}$):** Iso-surface regularizer (details not fully specified in snippets, but mentioned as helping with UDF optimization) [S1].
    -   **Mask Loss ($L_{mask}$):** Optional, if binary masks $\mathbf{M}_k$ are provided:
        $$
        L_{mask} = \text{BCE}(\mathbf{M}_k, \hat{\mathbf{O}}_k)
        $$
        where $\hat{\mathbf{O}}_k = \sum_{i} T_{k,i} \sigma_{k,i} \delta_{k,i}$ is the sum of rendering weights (opacity) along the ray [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    -   Optimizer: Adam.
    -   Learning Rate: $1 \times 10^{-4}$ for the UDF MLP; $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for the color network and other parameters.
    -   Schedule: Cosine decay.
    -   Batch Size: 512 rays.
    -   Iterations: 300,000.
    -   Parameters $\kappa$ and $\beta$ are trained jointly with the networks [S1].

9.  **Mesh Extraction:**
    Extract the mesh from the learned $f_u$ field using **MeshUDF**, an extended marching cubes algorithm designed for UDFs [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class NeuralUDF:
    def __init__(self, num_rays=512, num_samples_uniform=64, num_importance_iter=5, 
                 num_samples_importance=16, alpha=20, tau=25000, 
                 lr_udf=1e-4, lr_other=5e-4, lambda_patch=0.1, lambda_eik=0.1, 
                 lambda_reg=0.0, lambda_mask=0.1):
        self.num_rays = num_rays
        self.num_samples_uniform = num_samples_uniform
        self.num_importance_iter = num_importance_iter
        self.num_samples_importance = num_samples_importance
        self.alpha = alpha
        self.tau = tau
        self.lambda_patch = lambda_patch
        self.lambda_eik = lambda_eik
        self.lambda_reg = lambda_reg
        self.lambda_mask = lambda_mask
        
        # UDF Network: 8 hidden layers, size 256, skip connection from 4th layer
        self.udf_net = UDFNetwork(hidden_dim=256, num_layers=8, skip_layer=4)
        
        # Color Network: 8 hidden layers, size 256
        self.color_net = ColorNetwork(hidden_dim=256, num_layers=8)
        
        # Trainable parameters
        self.beta = nn.Parameter(torch.tensor(1.0))
        self.kappa = nn.Parameter(torch.tensor(1.0))
        
        # Optimizer
        optimizer_params = [
            {'params': self.udf_net.parameters(), 'lr': lr_udf},
            {'params': list(self.color_net.parameters()) + [self.beta, self.kappa], 'lr': lr_other}
        ]
        self.optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(optimizer_params)
        self.scheduler = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(self.optimizer, T_max=300000)

    def positional_encoding(self, x, num_frequencies):
        # Standard NeRF positional encoding
        # x: (..., 3)
        # Returns: (..., 3 * 2 * num_frequencies)
        pe = []
        for i in range(num_frequencies):
            freq = 2**i * torch.pi
            pe.append(torch.sin(x * freq))
            pe.append(torch.cos(x * freq))
        return torch.cat(pe, dim=-1)

    def forward(self, rays_o, rays_d, gt_colors, gt_masks=None):
        # rays_o: (B, 3), rays_d: (B, 3)
        # gt_colors: (B, 3)
        
        # 1. Hierarchical Sampling
        # Uniform sampling
        t_uniform = torch.linspace(0, 1, self.num_samples_uniform).to(rays_o.device)
        # Scale to ray length (assuming unit sphere, max t approx 1.5 or similar, 
        # but source says scale scene to unit sphere and model outside with NeRF++. 
        # For simplicity, assume t in [0, 1] covers the sphere, 
        # actual implementation needs ray length calculation)
        # Note: Source says "scale scene to unit sphere". 
        # Let's assume t is normalized or we compute actual t.
        # For pseudocode clarity, we assume t is sampled in a range covering the object.
        
        # Coarse points
        t_coarse = t_uniform
        pts_coarse = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_coarse.unsqueeze(0) # (B, N, 3)
        
        # Importance sampling iterations
        t_fine = t_coarse
        for i in range(self.num_importance_iter):
            # Fixed kappa and beta for coarse estimation in iteration i
            kappa_i = 32 * (2 ** i)
            beta_i = 32 * (2 ** (i + 1))
            
            # Compute density for importance sampling weights
            # Density depends on UDF. 
            # We need UDF values for coarse points first
            udf_coarse = self.udf_net(pts_coarse) # (B, N)
            
            # Compute surface existence probability h(t)
            # phi_beta(x) = beta * exp(-beta*x) / (1 + exp(-beta*x))^2
            # h(t) = 1 - exp(-alpha * phi_beta(udf) * delta)
            # delta is distance between samples. For uniform, delta is constant.
            # For importance, delta varies.
            
            # Simplified: Compute weights based on h(t) and visibility
            # Visibility indicator calculation requires gradient
            grad_udf = torch.autograd.grad(udf_coarse.sum(), pts_coarse, create_graph=True)[0] # (B, N, 3)
            
            # Masking function m(t_j)
            # cos(theta) = dot(rays_d, grad_udf) / (|rays_d| * |grad_udf|)
            # rays_d is unit vector.
            cos_theta = torch.sum(rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * grad_udf, dim=-1) / (torch.norm(grad_udf, dim=-1) + 1e-6)
            m = (cos_theta >= 0).float()
            
            # Compute h(t)
            # phi_beta
            exp_neg_beta_x = torch.exp(-beta_i * udf_coarse)
            phi_beta = beta_i * exp_neg_beta_x / (1 + exp_neg_beta_x)**2
            
            # Delta t
            # For uniform sampling, delta is constant. For importance, it's t[i+1]-t[i]
            # Assuming uniform for coarse step
            delta_t = t_coarse[1] - t_coarse[0]
            
            h = 1 - torch.exp(-self.alpha * phi_beta * delta_t)
            
            # Visibility indicator Psi(t)
            # Psi(t_i) = product_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - h(t_j) * m(t_j))
            # Cumulative product
            mask_h_m = h * m
            one_minus_mask = 1 - mask_h_m
            # Cumulative product along ray dimension
            # Add 1 at start for the first point (Psi=1)
            cum_prod = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(one_minus_mask[:, :1]), 
                                  torch.cumprod(one_minus_mask[:, :-1], dim=1)], dim=1)
            
            # Density sigma(t)
            # Source implies sigma is derived from UDF and visibility.
            # A common formulation for unbiased density with visibility is:
            # sigma(t) = kappa * h(t) * Psi(t) / delta_t ? 
            # Or sigma(t) = kappa * phi_beta(udf) * Psi(t)
            # The source says "density function for UDF can be defined as ... with Psi(t)"
            # And "unbiased density function sigma(t) = Omega_s(f(r(t))) that is monotonically increasing"
            # Let's use a standard form consistent with the probabilistic model:
            # sigma(t) = kappa * h(t) * Psi(t) / delta_t is not quite right because h includes delta.
            # Let's use: sigma(t) = kappa * phi_beta(udf) * Psi(t)
            # Note: The source Eq 7 defines h(t) with delta. 
            # The rendering equation uses sigma * delta.
            # So effective weight is sigma * delta.
            # If sigma = kappa * phi_beta * Psi, then weight = kappa * phi_beta * Psi * delta.
            # But h = 1 - exp(-alpha * phi * delta).
            # Let's stick to the visibility indicator logic.
            
            # Compute weights for importance sampling
            # Weight w_i = sigma_i * delta_i * Psi_i
            # Using the probabilistic interpretation:
            # w_i = kappa_i * phi_beta(udf_i) * cum_prod_i * delta_t
            
            weights = kappa_i * phi_beta * cum_prod * delta_t
            weights = weights + 1e-6 # Avoid zero weights
            
            # Sample new t_fine based on weights
            # (Implementation of inverse CDF sampling omitted for brevity, 
            # standard NeRF importance sampling)
            t_fine = sample_from_weights(t_coarse, weights)
            
            # Update coarse for next iteration
            t_coarse = t_fine
            
        # Final fine points
        pts_fine = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_fine.unsqueeze(0)
        
        # 2. Forward Pass Networks
        # UDF
        udf_fine = self.udf_net(pts_fine) # (B, N)
        
        # Color
        # Get 256-dim feature from UDF net (e.g., output of 4th layer)
        # This requires modifying UDFNetwork to return intermediate features
        udf_feature = self.udf_net.get_feature(pts_fine) # (B, N, 256)
        color_input = torch.cat([
            self.positional_encoding(rays_d.unsqueeze(1), 4), # (B, 1, 12)
            self.positional_encoding(pts_fine, 6),            # (B, N, 36)
            udf_feature                                        # (B, N, 256)
        ], dim=-1)
        colors = self.color_net(color_input) # (B, N, 3)
        
        # 3. Compute Gradients for Eikonal and Masking
        grad_udf = torch.autograd.grad(udf_fine.sum(), pts_fine, create_graph=True)[0] # (B, N, 3)
        grad_norm = torch.norm(grad_udf, dim=-1) # (B, N)
        
        # 4. Compute Visibility and Density for Rendering
        # Recompute h, m, Psi for fine points with learned beta
        exp_neg_beta_x = torch.exp(-self.beta * udf_fine)
        phi_beta = self.beta * exp_neg_beta_x / (1 + exp_neg_beta_x)**2
        
        cos_theta = torch.sum(rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * grad_udf, dim=-1) / (grad_norm + 1e-6)
        m = (cos_theta >= 0).float()
        
        delta_t = t_fine[1] - t_fine[0]
        h = 1 - torch.exp(-self.alpha * phi_beta * delta_t)
        
        mask_h_m = h * m
        one_minus_mask = 1 - mask_h_m
        cum_prod = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(one_minus_mask[:, :1]), 
                              torch.cumprod(one_minus_mask[:, :-1], dim=1)], dim=1)
        
        # Density
        # Using kappa (learned) and phi_beta
        sigma = self.kappa * phi_beta * cum_prod
        
        # 5. Volume Rendering
        T = torch.cumprod(1 - sigma * delta_t, dim=1)
        T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T], dim=1)
        weights = T[:, :-1] * sigma * delta_t
        rendered_colors = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * colors, dim=1) # (B, 3)
        
        # 6. Loss Calculation
        L_color = torch.mean(torch.abs(rendered_colors - gt_colors))
        
        L_eik = torch.mean((grad_norm - 1)**2)
        
        L_patch = 0 # SSIM loss calculation omitted
        if self.lambda_patch > 0:
            L_patch = compute_ssim_loss(rendered_colors, gt_colors) # Placeholder
            
        L_reg = 0 # Placeholder
        if self.lambda_reg > 0:
            L_reg = compute_iso_surface_reg(udf_fine)
            
        L_mask = 0
        if gt_masks is not None and self.lambda_mask > 0:
            # Opacity sum
            opacity_sum = torch.sum(weights, dim=1)
            L_mask = F.binary_cross_entropy(opacity_sum, gt_masks.float())
            
        L = L_color + self.lambda_patch * L_patch + self.lambda_eik * L_eik + \
            self.lambda_reg * L_reg + self.lambda_mask * L_mask
            
        return rendered_colors, L

class UDFNetwork(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim, num_layers, skip_layer):
        super().__init__()
        self.layers = nn.ModuleList()
        for i in range(num_layers):
            if i == 0:
                self.layers.append(nn.Linear(36, hidden_dim)) # 3*6=18? No, PE is 3*2*6=36
            else:
                self.layers.append(nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim))
            self.layers.append(nn.ReLU())
        self.skip_layer = skip_layer
        self.final_layer = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 1)
        
    def get_feature(self, x):
        # x: (B, N, 3)
        pe_x = positional_encoding(x, 6) # (B, N, 36)
        h = pe_x
        features = []
        for i, layer in enumerate(self.layers):
            h = layer(h)
            if i == self.skip_layer:
                features.append(h)
            if i < self.skip_layer:
                h = h + pe_x # Skip connection from 4th layer (index 3? or 4th layer output?)
                # Source: "skip connection from 4th layer output to input"
                # Usually means concat.
        # Simplified:
        return features[0] # Return the feature from the skip layer

class ColorNetwork(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim, num_layers):
        super().__init__()
        self.layers = nn.ModuleList()
        for i in range(num_layers):
            self.layers.append(nn.Linear(hidden_dim + 36 + 256, hidden_dim)) # Input dim varies
            self.layers.append(nn.ReLU())
        self.final_layer = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 3)
        
    def forward(self, x):
        h = x
        for layer in self.layers:
            h = layer(h)
        return self.final_layer(h)

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the forward pass of two MLPs and the volume rendering integration for each ray. Hierarchical sampling increases the number of points per ray (144 points) compared to standard NeRF, increasing compute per iteration.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the UDF and Color MLPs. The UDF MLP has 8 layers of size 256, similar to NeuS.
- **Performance:** On the DTU dataset, NeuralUDF achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.68 (compared to 0.87 for NeuS and 0.86 for VolSDF) [S1]. On the DeepFashion3D dataset, it achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.97 (compared to 4.36 for NeuS and 6.72 for VolSDF) [S1].
- **Strengths:** Capable of reconstructing open surfaces and arbitrary topologies, which SDF-based methods fail to do accurately. Robust to the non-differentiability of the UDF zero-level set via probabilistic modeling.
- **Limitations:** The UDF is not occlusion-aware by default, requiring the complex visibility indicator mechanism. Training requires careful tuning of hyperparameters $\alpha, \beta, \kappa$. The method assumes the scene can be scaled to a unit sphere.
- **Hardware:** Evaluated on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU [S1].

## Background
NeuralUDF was introduced by Xiaoxiao Long et al. in 2023 [S1]. It addresses the limitation of previous neural implicit surface reconstruction methods (like NeuS, VolSDF, IDR) that rely on Signed Distance Functions (SDF). SDFs require the surface to be closed to define an inside and outside, making them unsuitable for open surfaces like clothing or plants. NeuralUDF extends the representation to Unsigned Distance Functions (UDF), allowing for arbitrary topologies. It builds on the volume rendering framework of NeRF and the implicit surface representation of NeuS, but introduces a novel density modeling scheme to handle the non-monotonicity of UDFs along rays.
