# Neural Multi-View Image Reconstruction with Hash Encoding

Also called: Ours (multi-view)

## Overview
This method performs neural multi-view image reconstruction and novel view synthesis by combining a hash-encoded geometry network with a separate light field MLP [S1]. It solves the problem of reconstructing high-frequency geometric details and accurate textures from multi-view images, addressing the noise and roughness issues inherent in density-based methods like NeRF and the poor performance in textureless areas seen in SDF-based baselines like NeuS and IDR [S1]. It is distinguished by its use of a hierarchical hash grid for spatial features and a specific initialization strategy that enables faster convergence and lower memory usage compared to voxel-based or octree-based alternatives [S1].

## How it works
The method consists of two primary neural networks: a **Geometry Network** that predicts the Signed Distance Function (SDF) and spatial features, and a **Light Field MLP** that predicts view-dependent color [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view RGB images with known camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics).
*   **Outputs:** Reconstructed 3D geometry (extracted as a mesh) and novel view images.

### Data Structures
**Hash Encoding Grid:**
*   Structure: A set of $L$ hash tables (layers).
*   Layer Count ($L$): 16 [S1].
*   Feature Dimension ($D$): 2 per grid feature in each layer [S1].
*   Hashing: Spatial coordinates are hashed to indices in these tables. The specific hash function and table size are not detailed in the source, but the feature retrieval is standard for hash encoding [S1].

**Network Architectures:**
1.  **Geometry Network:**
    *   Type: MLP with 6 layers [S1].
    *   Activation: Softplus with $\beta = 100$ [S1].
    *   Input: Fourier positional encoding of spatial location $\mathbf{x}$ (dimension 6) [S1].
    *   Hidden Layers: Layers 1-2 process the input. Layer 3 concatenates the output of Layer 2 with the hash encoding of $\mathbf{x}$. Layers 4-6 process the concatenated features.
    *   Output: SDF value $S(\mathbf{x})$ and a geometry feature vector $\mathbf{f}_{geo}$ [S1].
2.  **Light Field MLP:**
    *   Type: MLP with 4 hidden layers [S1].
    *   Hidden Units: 256 per layer [S1].
    *   Input: Concatenation of:
        *   Fourier positional encoding of view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (dimension 6) [S1].
        *   Surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ (gradient of SDF) [S1].
        *   Geometry feature vector $\mathbf{f}_{geo}$ from the Geometry Network [S1].
    *   Output: View-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

### Training Procedure
The training minimizes a loss function comprising photometric, SDF consistency, and regularization terms. The specific weights for the loss function (Eq. 8 in source) are:
*   $\alpha = 0.1$ (Photometric loss weight)
*   $\beta = 0.1$ (SDF consistency weight)
*   $\gamma = 5 \times 10^{-4}$ (Regularization weight) [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the Geometry Network using a modified strategy from [12] (referenced as [S1] in context of NeuS/IGR comparisons) [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** For each training iteration, sample 512 rays [S1]. Use a hierarchical sampling strategy to sample points along these rays within the bounding volume $\Omega$ [S1].
3.  **Feature Retrieval:** For each sampled point $\mathbf{x}$:
    *   Compute Fourier positional encoding $\text{enc}_{ff}(\mathbf{x})$ with dimension 6 [S1].
    *   Compute Hash encoding $\text{enc}(\theta_h, \mathbf{x})$ using the 16-layer hash grid [S1].
    *   Pass through the Geometry Network to obtain SDF $S(\mathbf{x})$ and feature vector $\mathbf{f}_{geo}$ [S1].
4.  **Invalid Point Elimination:** Calculate the mean SDF of the sampled points on each ray. Use this mean as a threshold to eliminate invalid sampled points (points far from the surface) [S1].
5.  **Color Prediction:** For valid points, compute the surface normal $\mathbf{n} = \nabla S(\mathbf{x})$. Compute Fourier positional encoding of the view direction $\mathbf{v}$ [S1]. Pass $\mathbf{v}$, $\mathbf{n}$, and $\mathbf{f}_{geo}$ through the Light Field MLP to get color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Render the image using volume rendering or surface rendering equations based on the predicted SDF and color [S1].
7.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the total loss using weights $\alpha=0.1, \beta=0.1, \gamma=5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
8.  **Optimization:** Update network parameters using gradient descent on a GeForce RTX 3090 GPU [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Invalid Points:** The method relies on eliminating points based on the mean SDF. If the scene has large empty spaces or complex topology, the mean SDF might not be a robust threshold. [S1]
*   **Textureless Areas:** While the method performs better than baselines, extreme texturelessness can still challenge the photometric loss. [S1]
*   **Initialization:** The specific initialization strategy from [12] is critical for convergence. Using standard initialization may lead to poor results. [S1]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is 1.8 hours for the tested scenes on a GeForce RTX 3090 [S1]. This is significantly faster than IDR (5.2 hours), NeuS (7.2 hours), UNISURF (21 hours), VolSDF (9 hours), and NeRF (9.1 hours) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is 5 GB [S1]. This is lower than IDR (6.5 GB), NeuS (7 GB), UNISURF (6 GB), VolSDF (13.8 GB), and NeRF (8 GB) [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **PSNR:** Mean PSNR of 30.85 dB on the DTU dataset [S1]. This is higher than IDR (23.20 dB), NeuS (29.66 dB), UNISURF (28.53 dB), VolSDF (27.11 dB), and NeRF (29.80 dB) [S1].
    *   **Chamfer Distance:** Mean Chamfer-L2 distance of $0.72 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1]. This is lower (better) than IDR ($0.90 \times 10^{-6}$), NeuS ($0.77 \times 10^{-6}$), UNISURF ($1.02 \times 10^{-6}$), VolSDF ($0.86 \times 10^{-6}$), and NeRF ($1.49 \times 10^{-6}$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reconstructs more geometric detail, especially high-frequency details and in textureless areas, compared to NeuS, IDR, VolSDF, and UNISURF. Outperforms NeRF in geometry quality and texture detail. Faster training and lower memory usage. [S1]
*   **Limitations:** Requires multi-view images with known camera parameters. Performance depends on the quality of the hash encoding and initialization. [S1]
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity geometry and texture are required from multi-view images, and computational resources (time/memory) are a constraint. [S1]
*   **When not to use:** For single-view reconstruction or when camera parameters are unknown. [S1]

## Background
The method was proposed by Z. Deng, H. Xiao, Y. Lang, et al. in the paper "Multi-scale hash encoding based neural geometry representation" [S1]. It builds upon previous work in neural implicit representations like NeuS and IDR, aiming to improve geometric detail and training efficiency using hash encoding [S1]. The method was evaluated on the DTU and BlendedMVS datasets [S1].
