# Pix2NeRF Training Configuration

Also called: Pix2NeRF Training Pipeline, Unsupervised conditional $\pi$-GAN training

## Overview
Pix2NeRF is an unsupervised method for translating a single image into a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by leveraging a pre-trained $\pi$-GAN [S1]. Unlike supervised methods that require multi-view data, Pix2NeRF optimizes an encoder to map the input image to a latent code, which is then decoded into a NeRF representation [S1]. It is distinguished from its sibling [BAD-NeRF](13523-bad-nerf.md) by its reliance on a generative adversarial framework with a discriminator and encoder, rather than explicit geometric constraints or multi-view consistency losses alone [S1]. The method achieves a super-resolution effect by training at 64×64 resolution while rendering at 128×128 [S1].

## How it works
The training process optimizes three components: the Generator (the pre-trained $\pi$-GAN decoder mapping latent codes to images), the Encoder (mapping input images to latent codes), and a Discriminator (distinguishing real from generated views). The NeRF is implicitly represented by the $\pi$-GAN's latent space and decoder architecture, where the generator outputs rendered views [S1].

**Inputs:**
- Single input image $I_{in}$.
- Pre-trained $\pi$-GAN generator weights.

**Outputs:**
- Optimized Encoder weights.
- A NeRF representation capable of synthesizing novel views from the latent code produced by the Encoder.

**Data Structures:**
- **Generator ($G$):** A neural network mapping latent vector $z$ to image $I$. Architecture details are inherited from the base $\pi$-GAN [S1].
- **Encoder ($E$):** A neural network mapping image $I$ to latent vector $z$. Uses the discriminator architecture as a backbone with a tanh activation at the latent code head [S1].
- **Discriminator ($D$):** A neural network distinguishing real images from generated images [S1].

**Parameters:**
- **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
- **Total Iterations:** 300,000 [S1].
- **Batch Size:** 48 [S1].
- **Progressive Training:** Starts at 32×32, moves to 64×64 [S1].
- **Rendering Resolution:** 128×128 [S1].

**Steps:**

1. **Initialize** the Generator $G$ with pre-trained $\pi$-GAN weights [S1]. Initialize Encoder $E$ and Discriminator $D$ weights randomly or via standard initialization [G].
2. **Set Resolution Phase 1:** Set training resolution to 32×32 [S1].
3. **Set Learning Rates Phase 1:**
   - Discriminator: $\eta_D = 4 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
   - Generator: $\eta_G = 4 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
   - Encoder: $\eta_E = 4 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
4. **Set Ray Sampling Phase 1:** Sample 96 points per ray [S1].
5. **Optimize Phase 1:** Run for a portion of the 300k iterations (duration not specified in sources, but implied to be until resolution switch) [S1].
   - For each batch:
     - Sample input images.
     - Encode images to latent codes $z = E(I_{in})$ [S1].
     - Render images from $z$ using $G$ (via NeRF volume rendering) [S1].
     - Compute adversarial losses for $D$, $G$, and $E$ [S1].
     - Update parameters using Adam [S1].
6. **Set Resolution Phase 2:** Increase resolution to 64×64 [S1].
7. **Set Learning Rates Phase 2:**
   - Discriminator: $\eta_D = 2 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
   - Generator: $\eta_G = 2 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
   - Encoder: $\eta_E = 2 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
8. **Set Ray Sampling Phase 2:** Sample 24 points per ray [S1].
9. **Optimize Phase 2:** Continue optimization for the remaining iterations to reach 300k total [S1].
   - For each batch:
     - Sample input images.
     - Encode images to latent codes $z = E(I_{in})$ [S1].
     - Render images from $z$ using $G$ at 128×128 resolution [S1].
     - Compute adversarial losses [S1].
     - Update parameters using Adam [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The sources do not specify the exact iteration count for the resolution switch from 32×32 to 64×64 [S1].
- The specific loss function weights for the adversarial components are not detailed in the provided notes, only that the $\pi$-GAN strategy is used [S1].

## Subtopics

### Hybrid Inversion Optimization
A variant used in related work (e.g., [S2]) that replaces the encoder-based approach with a direct inversion of the latent code. It uses a dual discriminator (unconditional + conditional) instead of a single unconditional discriminator, which yields better results and avoids balancing multiple loss terms [S2]. The objective minimizes LPIPS distance between predicted and ground-truth images over 16 augmentations [S2]. Pose parameters are reprojected onto valid constraints (unit length quaternions) after each iteration [S2]. It uses Adam with a base learning rate of 0.02 and $\beta_2 = 0.95$ [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Training runs for 300,000 iterations [S1]. The computational cost is approximately equivalent to obtaining a standard $\pi$-GAN model [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Performance:** The model synthesizes novel views with good quality, even under extreme poses and artifacts, where few-shot NeRF methods fail due to lack of multi-view supervision [S1].
- **Super-Resolution Effect:** Training at 64×64 but rendering at 128×128 yields a super-resolution effect compared to models trained directly on 128×128 [S1].
- **Strengths:** Unsupervised; does not require multi-view training data or test-time optimization [S1].
- **Limitations:** Performance does not drop significantly below competition, but specific quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, PSNR) are not provided in the training configuration notes [S1].

## Background
Pix2NeRF was introduced in 2022 [S1]. It builds on the $\pi$-GAN framework [S1] and addresses the problem of single-image to NeRF translation without requiring multi-view supervision, which is a limitation for "in the wild" datasets like CelebA and CARLA [S1]. The method utilizes a progressive training strategy and an encoder-based architecture to map images to the latent space of a generative model, which is then interpreted as a radiance field [S1].
