# UE4-NeRF

Also called: UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene

## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes by partitioning the environment into sub-NeRFs represented by textured polygonal meshes [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF variants that render volumetrically or rely on grid-based acceleration structures like [Block-NeRF](13622-block-nerf.md) or [Mega-NeRF](15532-mega-nerf.md), UE4-NeRF integrates a neural shader into the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. It sets itself apart by using regular octahedra for mesh initialization to handle tilted surfaces and multi-resolution hash encoding for training acceleration, achieving real-time frame rates at 4K resolution [S1].

## How it works
UE4-NeRF operates in three phases: block partitioning and coarse segmentation, mesh-based training with neural encoding, and LOD-based rasterization in UE4.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed images (e.g., drone-captured) with GPS metadata; camera poses; scene bounding box.
*   **Outputs:** A set of textured polygonal meshes (LOD hierarchy) and a neural shader (HLSL fragment shader) deployed in UE4 for real-time rendering.

### Data Structures
**1. Scene Partitioning**
The large scene is divided into $N$ blocks. Each block's training area is slightly larger than the physical scene area to create overlapping borders for smooth transitions [S1].

**2. Mesh Representation**
Each block is represented by a textured polygonal mesh.
*   **Geometry:** Initialized from a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. For each grid cell, the center point and its six neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) are used to form polygonal faces [S1].
*   **Topology:** Regular octahedra are used instead of the cube-based approach of Mobile-NeRF. Each octahedron has 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity information for each vertex/face [S1].

**3. Neural Network Architecture**
The method uses an Encoder-Decoder network implemented as an HLSL fragment shader in UE4 [S1].
*   **Encoder ($E$):** A 4-layer MLP.
    *   Input: Positional information $p_i$ encoded via multi-resolution hash encoding [S1].
    *   Layers: $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$.
    *   Output: Opacity $\alpha_i$ and an 8-dimensional feature vector $M_i$ [S1].
*   **Decoder ($D$):** A 3-layer MLP.
    *   Input: Feature vector $M_i$ and view direction $d_i$ encoded via spherical harmonics $SH(d_i)$ [S1].
    *   Input dimension: $8 (\text{features}) + 9 (\text{SH coefficients}) = 17$ [S1].
    *   Layers: $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$.
    *   Output: Final color $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Transform GPS-captured imagery coordinates to the UE4 coordinate system.
    *   X-axis: East.
    *   Y-axis: South.
    *   Z-axis: Up.
    *   Scale: 100:1 (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].

2.  **Block Partitioning:**
    *   Partition the scene into blocks.
    *   Train a coarse model to capture overall terrain and building outlines for sub-region segmentation [S1].
    *   Filter images associated with each sub-region and apply a mask to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].

3.  **Mesh Initialization:**
    *   Initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid for each block [S1].
    *   Select the center point of each grid and its six neighbors to create polygonal meshes [S1].
    *   Construct regular octahedra (20 faces: 8 exterior, 12 interior) to address slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces [S1].

4.  **Training (Optimization):**
    *   For each ray $r(t) = o + td$ from the camera origin, calculate intersections with the polygonal meshes to determine sampling points [S1].
    *   Optimize vertex positions of the meshes continuously during training [S1].
    *   Optimize network parameters $\theta_E$ (Encoder) and $\theta_D$ (Decoder) [S1].
    *   The encoding function uses multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) [S1].
    *   The rendering equation for a pixel is approximated by the shader output at the intersection point:
        $$ c_i = D(M_i, SH(d_i); \theta_D) $$
        $$ M_i, \alpha_i = E(hash(p_i); \theta_E) $$
        where $p_i$ is the position, $d_i$ is the view direction, and $SH$ denotes spherical harmonics encoding [S1].
    *   Training runs for 80,000 epochs per block on a single Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].

5.  **Pre-rendering (LOD Extraction):**
    *   Extract pre-rendered polygonal meshes at different levels of detail (LOD) [S1].
    *   Coarser meshes are trained/used for higher observation heights to ensure real-time performance [S1].
    *   The texture atlas stores the final feature vectors and opacity [S1].

6.  **Real-Time Rendering (UE4 Integration):**
    *   Import the extracted meshes and the neural shader (HLSL fragment shader) into UE4 [S1].
    *   The rasterization pipeline renders the meshes. The fragment shader computes color based on the stored features and view direction [S1].
    *   UE4 handles occlusion: if traditional 3D models (.obj, .fbx) are added, occluded NeRF regions skip neural shader computation [S1].

### Parameters

*   **Octahedron Faces:** 20 total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   **Encoder MLP:** 4 layers, dimensions $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces. UE4-NeRF addresses this by using regular octahedra instead of cubes [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects (water, glass) due to opacity binarization. UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps to mitigate this, though semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead and reduce frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, potentially resulting in gaps or holes in the final rendered scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory Overhead:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead. Increasing accuracy further amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Speed:** 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1].
    *   Time per block: ~40 minutes on 1×RTX 3090 [S1].
    *   Time for full scene ($n$ blocks): $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ on 1×RTX 3090 [S1].
    *   Comparison: Mobile-NeRF takes 48h/block (4×3090); Mega-NeRF takes 36h/block (1×3090) [S1].
*   **Rendering Performance:**
    *   Real-time at 4K resolution: Up to 43 FPS (Level-1 mesh hierarchy) [S1].
    *   Full scene at 2K resolution: 50 FPS [S1].
    *   Individual blocks at 2K resolution: $\ge 70$ FPS [S1].
    *   Comparison: Mobile-NeRF max 35 FPS at 2K (block only); Mega-NeRF max 60 FPS at 800×800 [S1].
*   **Memory:** Significant memory overhead for large scenes (square kilometers). Memory consumption increases with accuracy [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes; handles tilted surfaces well; supports scene editing (importing .obj/.fbx); handles semi-transparent materials better than Mobile-NeRF; high fidelity on small objects and dense textures compared to Mip-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires NVIDIA GPUs/CUDA; pre-rendering artifacts (gaps/holes); exposure matching issues in UE4; semi-transparent objects reduce FPS; limited to UE4 environment [S1].
*   **When to use:** Large-scale outdoor scenes (farmland, construction sites, industrial parks) requiring real-time interaction and high resolution.
*   **When not to use:** Small-scale scenes where standard NeRF suffices; scenes requiring precise exposure control without post-processing; non-NVIDIA hardware environments.

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of existing NeRF methods in real-time rendering of large-scale scenes, such as high training costs (Mobile-NeRF, Mega-NeRF) and lack of editability. The method combines NeRF with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline, leveraging Level of Detail (LOD) techniques and multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) [S1]. It is used for drone-captured large-scale scenes and evaluated on the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
