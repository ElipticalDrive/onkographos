# Aleth-NeRF

Also called: Aleth-NeRF with Concealing Field Assumption

## Overview
Aleth-NeRF is a NeRF variant that trains on under- and over-exposed images to render normal-light novel views by introducing a 'Concealing Field' assumption, where air particles attenuate emission rather than objects lacking emission [S1]. It differs from RAW-NeRF by taking 8-bit LDR images as inputs, whereas RAW-NeRF requires 16-bit HDR RAW images [S1]. Unlike methods that apply enhancement networks before or after NeRF training, Aleth-NeRF trains end-to-end without pre-processing or post-processing [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Low-light or over-exposed multi-view images (8-bit LDR) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Novel views rendered in normal-light conditions [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Density MLP ($F_\sigma$):** Maps 3D location $\mathbf{r}(i)$ to density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ and a hidden feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ [S1].
2.  **Color MLP ($F_c$):** Maps hidden feature $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
3.  **Local Concealing Field ($\Omega$):** Defines an extra light concealing capacity at each 3D position, individually learned [S1].
    *   Layout: $\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \text{Conv}_{\text{size}:7} (F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i)))$ [S1].
4.  **Global Concealing Field ($\Theta_G$):** A set of learnable parameters corresponding to camera distance $i$ for all rays, constant within a scene and independent of voxels [S1].
    *   Layout: $\Theta_G(i) \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
    *   Initialization: $\Theta_G(i) = 0.5$ for each camera distance $i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Standard NeRF Rendering:**
    The network uses two MLPs: density MLP $F_\sigma$ maps 3D location $\mathbf{r}(i)$ to density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$, and color MLP $F_c$ maps hidden feature $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    The discrete form of standard volume rendering is:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T(\mathbf{r}(i))(1-\exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))\cdot\delta))\cdot\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d}) $$
    $$ T(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(j)) \cdot \delta \right) $$
    where $\delta$ is a constant distance value between adjacent sample points under uniform sampling [S1].

2.  **Compute Local Concealing Field:**
    Calculate local concealing field $\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i))$ by applying a large kernel convolution (size 7) on the density MLP output $F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ [S1]:
    $$ \Omega(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \text{Conv}_{\text{size}:7} (F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))) $$
    This establishes spatial relationships and enriches the field with light-related information rather than structural information [S1].

3.  **Compute Concealed Transmittance:**
    Calculate accumulated transmittance $T_{\text{conceal}}(\mathbf{r}(i))$ by multiplying standard transmittance terms with the product of local and global concealing fields along the ray [S1]:
    $$ T_{\text{conceal}}(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(j)) \cdot \delta \right) \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} \Omega(\mathbf{r}(j))\Theta_G(j) $$
    This mimics the process of light suppression [S1].

4.  **Rendering for Training (Low-Light Scenario):**
    *   Generate low-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{adv}}$ by incorporating $T_{\text{conceal}}$ during volume rendering [S1].
    *   Generate normal-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ by removing concealing fields (using original $T$) [S1].

5.  **Rendering for Training (Over-Exposure Scenario):**
    *   Generate very-bright images $\hat{C}_{\text{adv}}$ using original $T$ [S1].
    *   Generate normal-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ by incorporating concealing fields along with $T_{\text{conceal}}$ [S1].

6.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Inverse Tone MSE Loss ($L_{\text{it-mse}}$):** To rectify imbalance where dark pixels contribute minimally and bright pixels dominate, use an inverse tone curve $\Phi$ to re-balance pixel weights [S1].
        $$ \Phi(x) = \frac{1}{3} \left( \sin^{-1}(1 - 2x) - \sin\left(\frac{1 - 2x}{2}\right) \right) $$
        $$ L_{\text{it-mse}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \| \Phi(\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon) - \Phi(C(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon) \|^2 $$
        where $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$ [S1].
    *   **Degree Loss ($L_{\text{de}}$):** Separates the predicted normal-light image from density, controlling enhancement degree [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{de}} = \| \text{avgpool}(\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})) - e \|^2 $$
        where $e = 0.45$ is the enhancement degree hyperparameter [S1].
    *   **Contrast Degree Loss ($L_{\text{co}}$):** Maintains structural similarity and controls contrast between adjacent sampling pixels of predicted normal-light and ground truth adverse-light images [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{co}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \sum_{k \in \{+1, -1\}} \| \hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r}) - \hat{C}_{\text{adv}}(\mathbf{r}+k) \|^2 $$
    *   **Color Constancy Loss ($L_{\text{cc}}$):** Regularizes color based on the gray-world assumption to prevent color imbalance from removing/adding concealing fields [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{cc}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \sum_{p,q} (\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})_p - \hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})_q)^2 $$
        where $p, q$ are color channels [S1].

7.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{\text{it-mse}} + \lambda_1 \cdot L_{\text{de}} + \lambda_2 \cdot L_{\text{co}} + \lambda_3 \cdot L_{\text{cc}} $$
    where $\lambda_1 = 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_2 = 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_3 = 10^{-8}$ [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Use Adam optimizer with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class DensityMLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim=256, num_layers=8):
        super().__init__()
        layers = []
        for _ in range(num_layers):
            layers.extend([nn.Linear(3, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU()])
        self.net = nn.Sequential(*layers)
        self.sigma_head = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 1)
        self.h_head = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim)

    def forward(self, x):
        h = self.net(x)
        # Density is activated by ReLU to [0, ∞) [S1]
        return torch.relu(self.sigma_head(h)).squeeze(-1), self.h_head(h)

class ColorMLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim=256, num_layers=4):
        super().__init__()
        layers = []
        for _ in range(num_layers):
            layers.extend([nn.Linear(hidden_dim + 3, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU()])
        self.net = nn.Sequential(*layers)
        self.color_head = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, 3)

    def forward(self, h, d):
        hd = torch.cat([h, d], dim=-1)
        h = self.net(hd)
        # Color is activated by Sigmoid to [0, 1) [S1]
        return torch.sigmoid(self.color_head(h))

class AlethNeRF:
    def __init__(self, num_rays, num_samples, hidden_dim=256):
        self.F_sigma = DensityMLP(hidden_dim)
        self.F_c = ColorMLP(hidden_dim)
        # Local concealing field uses a 7x7 convolution on spatial sigma [S1]
        self.conv_local = nn.Conv1d(1, 1, kernel_size=7, padding=3)
        # Global concealing field initialized to 0.5 [S1]
        self.theta_G = nn.Parameter(torch.full((num_rays,), 0.5))
        self.delta = 0.01  # Not given in the sources. [G]
        self.e = 0.45
        self.epsilon = 1e-3
        self.lambda1, self.lambda2, self.lambda3 = 1e-3, 1e-3, 1e-8

    def inverse_tone_curve(self, x):
        x = torch.clamp(x, 0.0, 1.0)
        # Inverse tone curve Φ(x) [S1]
        return (1/3) * (torch.asin(1 - 2 * x) - torch.sin((1 - 2 * x) / 2))

    def compute_omega(self, sigma_1d):
        # Applies 7x7 convolution to spatial sigma representation [S1]
        # sigma_1d shape: (num_samples,)
        return self.conv_local(sigma_1d.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)).squeeze(0)

    def render_ray(self, ray_o, ray_d, ray_idx, num_samples):
        t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, num_samples)
        points = ray_o + ray_d * t_vals.unsqueeze(0)
        sigma, h = self.F_sigma(points)
        color = self.F_c(h, ray_d)

        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * self.delta)
        cum_sigma_delta = torch.cumsum(sigma * self.delta, dim=0)
        T_std = torch.exp(torch.cat([torch.zeros(1, device=sigma.device), cum_sigma_delta[:-1]]))

        theta_g = self.theta_G[ray_idx]
        omega = self.compute_omega(sigma)

        prod_input = omega * theta_g
        cum_prod = torch.cumprod(prod_input, dim=0)
        prod_term = torch.cat([torch.ones(1, device=sigma.device), cum_prod[:-1]])

        T_conceal = T_std * prod_term

        weights_nor = T_std * alpha
        weights_adv = T_conceal * alpha

        C_nor = torch.sum(weights_nor.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=0)
        C_adv = torch.sum(weights_adv.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=0)
        return C_nor, C_adv

    def compute_loss(self, C_nor, C_adv, C_gt):
        # C_nor, C_adv, C_gt are batched tensors of shape (B, 3)
        phi_C_nor = self.inverse_tone_curve(C_nor + self.epsilon)
        phi_C_gt = self.inverse_tone_curve(C_gt + self.epsilon)
        L_it_mse = torch.mean((phi_C_nor - phi_C_gt) ** 2)

        L_de = (torch.mean(C_nor) - self.e) ** 2

        # Contrast degree loss over k ∈ {+1, -1} [S1]
        # Shifts over batch/spatial dimension to approximate adjacent pixels
        C_adv_shift_p = torch.roll(C_adv, shifts=1, dims=0)
        C_adv_shift_m = torch.roll(C_adv, shifts=-1, dims=0)
        L_co = torch.mean((C_nor - C_adv_shift_p) ** 2 + (C_nor - C_adv_shift_m) ** 2)

        # Color constancy loss over channels p, q [S1]
        L_cc = torch.mean((C_nor[:, 0] - C_nor[:, 1]) ** 2 + 
                          (C_nor[:, 0] - C_nor[:, 2]) ** 2 + 
                          (C_nor[:, 1] - C_nor[:, 2]) ** 2)

        return L_it_mse + self.lambda1 * L_de + self.lambda2 * L_co + self.lambda3 * L_cc
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\delta$: Constant distance between adjacent sample points. Not given in the sources. [G]
*   $e$: 0.45, hyperparameter signifying the enhancement degree [S1].
*   $\eta$: Hyperparameter controlling contrast degree [S1]. In over-exposure scenes, $e \cdot \eta$ is fixed to 0.5 [S1].
*   $\lambda_1$: $10^{-3}$, weight for degree loss $L_{\text{de}}$ [S1].
*   $\lambda_2$: $10^{-3}$, weight for contrast loss $L_{\text{co}}$ [S1].
*   $\lambda_3$: $10^{-8}$, weight for color constancy loss $L_{\text{cc}}$ [S1].
*   Learning rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Batch size: 4096 [S1].
*   Iterations: 62,500 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **MSE Imbalance:** Standard MSE loss causes imbalance where dark pixels contribute minimally and bright pixels dominate. This is solved by using the inverse tone curve $\Phi$ to re-balance pixel weights [S1].
*   **Post-processing vs Pre-processing:** Post-processing NeRF results with enhancement methods ('NeRF + *') underperforms pre-processing ('* + NeRF') because NeRF produces poor-quality dark images that struggle enhancement methods [S1].
*   **Color Imbalance:** Directly removing/adding concealing fields may cause color imbalance, addressed by the color constancy loss [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1]
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on the LOM dataset (5 scenes: 'buu', 'chair', 'sofa', 'bike', 'shrub') with paired low-light, normal-light, and over-exposure images. Images collected at $3000 \times 4000$, down-sampled to $375 \times 500$ [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources. [S1]
*   **Strengths:** End-to-end training without pre-processing or post-processing; handles 8-bit LDR inputs unlike RAW-NeRF; effective in low-light and over-exposure conditions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires paired ground truth images for evaluation metrics, though training uses unsupervised lightness correction losses alongside a regression loss [S1].
*   **When to use:** When synthesizing normal-light novel views from under- or over-exposed 8-bit LDR inputs [S1].
*   **When not:** When 16-bit HDR RAW images are available (RAW-NeRF may be preferred) or when pre/post-processing pipelines are acceptable [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Aleth-NeRF was introduced by Ziteng Cui, Lin Gu, Xiao Sun, Xianzheng Ma, Yu Qiao, and Tatsuya Harada at The University of Tokyo, Shanghai AI Laboratory, RIKEN AIP, and University of Oxford [S1].
*   **Paper:** "Aleth-NeRF: Illumination Adaptive NeRF with Concealing Field Assumption", published in The Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1].
*   **History:** The method is motivated by the ancient Greek emission theory, which posits that visual perception is formed by visual rays emitted from the eye, and darkness is caused by particles between the object and the eye. This contrasts with the standard NeRF setting where air density is zero [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented using the open-source PyTorch toolbox NeRF-Factory [S1].
