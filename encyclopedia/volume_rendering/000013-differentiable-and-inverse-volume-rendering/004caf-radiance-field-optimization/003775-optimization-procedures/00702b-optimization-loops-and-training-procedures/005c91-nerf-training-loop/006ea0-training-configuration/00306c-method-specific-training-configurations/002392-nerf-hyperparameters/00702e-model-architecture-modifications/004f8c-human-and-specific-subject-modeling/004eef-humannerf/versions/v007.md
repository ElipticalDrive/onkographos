# HumanNeRF

Also called: HumanNeRF (Generalized)

## Overview
HumanNeRF is a generalizable neural radiance field method for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans from sparse multi-view inputs, designed to generalize to unseen subjects and camera settings without per-scene training [S2]. It distinguishes itself from prior generalizable methods by combining aggregated pixel-alignment features with a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field and a neural appearance blending scheme, addressing the blur artifacts and geometric discontinuities common in implicit texture representations [S2]. Unlike per-scene methods that require hours of optimization per subject, HumanNeRF provides reasonable results immediately and achieves state-of-the-art quality with only ~1 hour of fine-tuning [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $K$ synchronized videos of a performer captured at different viewpoints (preferably 360°) with $T$ frames, denoted as $I = \{I_{k,t}\}$, where $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ and $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$. Each image $I_k$ has dimensions $H \times W \times 4$, with the last channel being a mask [S2].
*   **Output:** Synthesized RGB images and volume density for novel viewpoints and time steps [S2].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **SMPL Model:** A parametric human body model with 24 joints is used to estimate a basis rigid deformation [S2].
2.  **Feature Maps:** 2D feature maps $f_k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ extracted from input images [S2].
3.  **Deformation Field:** An MLP $\mathbf{MLP}_d$ that outputs displacement vectors [S2].
4.  **Neural Radiance Field (NeRF):** An MLP $\Phi$ that outputs volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ in canonical space [S2].
5.  **Appearance Blending Field:** An MLP $\mathbf{MLP}_A$ that outputs blending weights [S2].

**Network Architectures:**
*   **Feature Extractor ($U$):** A U-Net with 8 layers of ConvBnReLU2D (CRB2D). Kernel $k=3$, stride $s=1$, dilation $d=1$.
    *   Down0: 4/32
    *   Down1: 32/64
    *   Down2: 64/128
    *   Down3: 128/256
    *   Up1: 256/256
    *   Up2: 256/128
    *   Up3: 128/64
    *   Up4 (implied by structure, listed as Up3 in source but channels suggest final output): 64/32 [S2].
*   **Feature Blending Network ($\mathbf{MLP}_B$):**
    *   PE0: 6/54, 3/27 (Positional Encoding for view direction and angle)
    *   LR0: 54+27+32*6/256
    *   LR1-4: 256/256
    *   LR5: 256/128
    *   LR6: 128/6 [S2].
*   **Deformation Network ($\mathbf{MLP}_d$):**
    *   PE0: 24/216 (Positional Encoding for distances)
    *   LR0: 216+72+32/256
    *   LR1-4: 256/256
    *   LR5: 128/3 [S2].
*   **Generalizable Dynamic NeRF ($\Phi$):**
    *   PE0: 3/63 (Positional Encoding for position)
    *   LR0: 63/256
    *   LR1-4: 256/256
    *   LR5: 27+256/256
    *   LR6: 256/256
    *   Density $\sigma$: 256/1
    *   PE1: 3/27 (Positional Encoding for view direction)
    *   LR7: 256+27+32/256
    *   LR8: 256/128
    *   Color $\mathbf{c}$: 128/3 [S2].
*   **Appearance Blending Network ($\mathbf{MLP}_A$):**
    *   LR0: (32+3)*2/256
    *   LR1-5: 256/256
    *   LR6: 256/128
    *   Blending weights $\mathbf{W}$: 128/3 [S2].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Feature Extraction:** For each input image $I^k$, extract a 2D feature map $f^k$ using the U-Net network $U$:
    $$ f^k = U(I^k) $$
    [S2]

2.  **Aggregated Pixel Alignment Feature:** For a 3D spatial point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, project it into view $k$ at $q^k \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and fetch the feature vector $f^k_{q}$. Compute the aggregated feature $F_p$ as a weighted summation:
    $$ F_p = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w^k_q f^k_q $$
    where the blending weights $w^k_q$ are estimated by $\mathbf{MLP}_B$:
    $$ w^k_q = \mathbf{MLP}_B(\vec{v}, \theta^k_q, f^k_q) $$
    Here, $\vec{v}$ is the viewing direction, and $\theta^k_q$ is the angle of the viewing direction relative to the query ray of the source view [S2].

3.  **Deformation to Canonical Space:** Estimate the rigid deformation using the SMPL model $\mathcal{M}$ with skinning weights $w^s$. Compute the distances $R_d \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ and directions $R_v \in \mathbb{R}^{72}$ between the sample point $p$ and the 24 joints of the SMPL skeleton. The non-rigid displacement is predicted by $\mathbf{MLP}_d$. The point $p$ is deformed to canonical space $p'$:
    $$ p' = \mathcal{S}(p, \mathcal{M}, w^s) + \mathbf{MLP}_d(R_d, R_v, F_p) $$
    [S2]

4.  **NeRF Rendering:** Feed the canonical position $p'$, viewing direction $\vec{v}$, and aggregated feature $F_p$ into the generalizable dynamic neural radiance field $\Phi$ to regress volume density $\sigma$ and RGB radiance $\mathbf{c}$ [S2].

5.  **Neural Appearance Blending (Optional Fine-tuning/Refinement):** To refine texture details and address artifacts, aggregate colors from neighboring views. Input two adjacent image features and occlusion maps ($f_{r,0}^r, f_{l,0}^l$) into $\mathbf{MLP}_A$ to obtain blending weights $\mathbf{W}$, which are used to blend the synthesized colors [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   **SMPL Joints:** 24 joints [S2].
*   **Fine-tuning Duration:** 300 frames for a specific performer [S2].
*   **CRB2D Layer:** ConvBnReLU2D with kernel size 3, stride 1, dilation 1 [S2].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Multi-person/Interaction:** The method relies on a regressed parametric human model, limiting it to single-person setups and failing on multi-person or human-object interactions [S2].
*   **Lighting Variation:** No explicit lighting model; significant brightness or color changes between views cause severe artifacts. Switching nearest views during appearance blending causes jumping artifacts, especially with high brightness variance [S2].
*   **Generalization Limits:** Capability is limited because human dataset distributions only cover a small portion of human dynamics and appearances [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The generalizable NeRF module provides immediate results without per-scene training. Fine-tuning for a specific performer takes approximately 1 hour for 300 frames [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On six input views, the method achieves PSNR = 32.59, SSIM = 0.9817, and LPIPS = 0.0304 [S2].
*   **Scaling:** Performance degrades as the number of input views decreases; average error increases rapidly with fewer cameras [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient generalization to unseen identities and poses; significantly less fine-tuning time than per-scene methods (Neural Body 6.7h, Neural Volumes 8.4h, ST-NeRF 9.5h) [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to single-person setups; sensitive to lighting variations; generalization is bounded by the diversity of training data [S2].
*   **When to use:** Dynamic human rendering from sparse multi-view inputs, specifically targeting unseen identities and poses with efficient generalization [S2].
*   **When not to use:** Multi-person scenarios, human-object interactions, or scenes with significant lighting variations across views [S2].

## Background
HumanNeRF was introduced by Zhang et al. in 2021/2022 [S2]. It builds on the principles of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [S1, unofficial] and generalizable image-based rendering methods like IBRNet [S2]. The method addresses the limitations of previous neural human representations that required dense multi-view inputs and costly per-scene training, which restricted them to static models or specific subjects [S2]. It was developed to enable high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans from sparse inputs, leveraging aggregated pixel-alignment features and pose-embedded non-rigid deformation fields [S2].
