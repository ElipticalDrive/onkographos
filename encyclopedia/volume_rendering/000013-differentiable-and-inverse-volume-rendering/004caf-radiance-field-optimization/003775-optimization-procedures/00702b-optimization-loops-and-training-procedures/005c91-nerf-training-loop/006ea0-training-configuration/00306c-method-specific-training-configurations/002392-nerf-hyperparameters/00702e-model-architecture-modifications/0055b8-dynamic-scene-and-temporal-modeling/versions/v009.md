# Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, 4D NeRF, Temporal NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling extends static Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to reconstruct and render time-varying scenes from video data . It solves the limitation of static representations by allowing density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ to vary with time $t$, enabling novel view synthesis for moving objects and cameras . Unlike static methods, these approaches explicitly model temporal dynamics, either by treating time as a direct input coordinate, decomposing the scene into static and dynamic components, or warping a canonical representation to target time steps .

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) serves as the baseline for most modifications. It consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a coarse network and a fine network. The coarse network takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs a latent feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and volume density $\sigma$. The fine network takes $\mathbf{h}$, positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and positional encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$ [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
$$

Modifications generally fall into three categories [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs).

## Subtopics

### [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md)
Uses semantic segmentation to stratify a scene into motion-based 'gears', assigning region-specific spatio-temporal sampling resolutions to handle varying motion scales efficiently.

### [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)
Treats time $t$ as a direct coordinate input to the MLP alongside position and view direction, serving as a baseline dynamic method that often suffers from blurriness.

### [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md)
Models a static canonical radiance field and differentially warps it to target times using average splatting and an inpaint network to resolve occlusions.

### [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md)
Decomposes the field into a static grid and dynamic particles, allowing monocular video reconstruction without multi-view stereo data.

### [KFD-NeRF](15551-kfd-nerf.md)
Models the 4D field as a dynamic system estimated via a Kalman filter, separating motion estimation into observer and predictor branches fused by a learnable gain.

## Background
The extension of NeRF to dynamic scenes originated with the introduction of time as an explicit input coordinate, establishing the foundational approach for temporal modeling . Subsequent developments introduced more complex mechanisms such as semantic stratification , canonical space warping , particle-based decomposition , and Kalman filtering for deformation estimation . These methods address specific challenges in dynamic reconstruction, including motion blur, occlusion handling, and computational efficiency.
