# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain, whereas alternatives often rely on direct time-conditioned feature interpolation or rigid body assumptions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamps $t_i$, and previous frame deformation states $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}}, \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}$ along with their associated noise terms $\varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-2}}$.
*   **Outputs:** Estimated deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ for the current frame, which warps real-space points to a time-independent canonical space. The canonical space is encoded using a tri-plane representation.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-plane Representation:** Three sets of mutually orthogonal 2D planes encoding the canonical space [S1]. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
*   **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1].
*   **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that computes the Kalman gain [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Observer Step (Prediction Branch Inputs):** Query the observer MLP with coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and timestamps $t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$ to obtain previous deformation states and noise-related terms:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_{i-1}) $$
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-2}} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_{i-2}) $$
    [S1]

2.  **Velocity Calculation:** Approximate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using the deformation from the previous two frames:
    $$ v_{t_i} = (\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}) / \Delta t_i $$
    where $\Delta t_i$ is the time difference between frames [S1].

3.  **Prediction Step:** Calculate the predicted deformation state $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the locally linear motion model:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = 2\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}} $$
    [S1]

4.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with current coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and timestamp $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$:
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
    [S1]

5.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ taking noise-related terms and timestamps as inputs:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
    [S1]

6.  **Update Step:** Combine the observation and prediction using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
    [S1]

7.  **Warping:** Warp all points in real space to the time-independent canonical space according to the estimated deformations $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].

8.  **Rendering:** Render the radiance field from the canonical space using the tri-plane representation [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Kalman Loss:** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
    where $\mathcal{N}$ is the batch of points [S1].

*   **Canonical Observation Loss:** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real-world radiance field [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].

*   **Total Variation Loss:** Encourages sparse edges in the tri-plane representation [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].

*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ is the proposal loss from Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter guidance.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D point coordinates (tensor)
        t_i: Current timestamp (float)
        t_prev_1: Previous timestamp (float)
        t_prev_2: Timestamp before previous (float)
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP returning (deformation, noise_terms)
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer returning Kalman gain
        
    Returns:
        estimated_deformation: Tensor of shape (3,)
    """
    # Step 1: Get previous states
    dx_prev_1, eps_prev_1 = observer_mlp(x, t_prev_1)
    dx_prev_2, eps_prev_2 = observer_mlp(x, t_prev_2)
    
    # Step 2: Calculate velocity
    # Note: Delta t is assumed to be known or constant between frames
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev_1
    velocity = (dx_prev_1 - dx_prev_2) / delta_t
    
    # Step 3: Predict deformation (Local linear motion)
    # dx_pred = 2 * dx_prev_1 - dx_prev_2
    dx_pred = 2 * dx_prev_1 - dx_prev_2
    
    # Step 4: Get current observation
    y_curr, eps_curr = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # Step 5: Calculate Kalman Gain
    # Input to linear layer: noise terms and timestamps
    kalman_gain = kalman_gain_net(eps_curr, eps_prev_1, t_i, t_prev_1)
    
    # Step 6: Update deformation
    estimated_deformation = dx_pred + kalman_gain * (y_curr - dx_pred)
    
    return estimated_deformation
```
[G] The pseudocode assumes `observer_mlp` and `kalman_gain_net` are standard PyTorch/TensorFlow modules. The `kalman_gain_net` is a linear layer as specified in [S1]. The velocity calculation uses $\Delta t_i$ which is the time difference between $t_i$ and $t_{i-1}$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Training Stages:** Predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion [S1].
*   **Later Training Stages:** Observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details [S1].
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Inevitable errors due to coordinate shifts when points are encoded by finite resolution tri-planes are mitigated by concatenating encoded tri-plane features with raw coordinate inputs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation allows for rapid convergence and significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 30 minutes for KFD-NeRF-S and 3 hours for KFD-NeRF-L on the tested datasets, compared to 48 hours for D-NeRF [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Deformation fields are smoother and easier to fit with MLPs due to inductive smoothness bias compared to feature interpolation which exhibits high-frequency signals during abrupt radiance changes [S1]. The Kalman filter automatically balances leveraging predictions in early stages and observations in later stages [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Deformation fields may fail to find correspondences in frames with significant motion if not properly guided [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality motion reconstruction is required and computational resources allow for the training of the Kalman filter guided deformation field [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally-varying states [S1]. The method integrates a plug-and-play Kalman filter module to optimize state estimations from both observations and predictions [S1]. It builds on the observation that deformation fields have an inductive smoothness bias suitable for MLPs, unlike feature interpolation [S1].
