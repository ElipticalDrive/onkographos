# SelfNeRF

Also called: SelfNeRF: Fast Training NeRF for Human from Monocular Self-rotating Video

## Overview
SelfNeRF is a novel view synthesis method for human performance that trains from scratch using monocular self-rotating video inputs, achieving high-fidelity results in approximately twenty minutes [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [MoCo-NeRF](16197-moco-nerf.md) by utilizing a surface-relative hash encoding to aggregate inter-frame information, enabling rapid convergence without multi-view inputs [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A monocular self-rotating video of a human performer consisting of $N_t$ frames.
*   **Output:** A trained neural radiance field capable of rendering novel views of the dynamic human performance.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Surface Shape $\{T_t\}$:** A point cloud representing the surface geometry for each frame $t$, where $t = 0, \dots, N_t - 1$. These are recovered using external reconstruction methods (e.g., VideoAvatar or SelfRecon) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Body Mask $\{M_t\}$:** Binary masks for each frame $t$, used to distinguish the human from the background [S1, unofficial].
*   **Canonical Space:** Defined as Frame 0 ($t=0$), serving as the static reference frame [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hash Grid:** A multi-resolution hash encoding structure mapping low-dimensional coordinates to high-dimensional feature vectors [S1, unofficial].
*   **MLP ($F_\omega$):** A multi-layer perceptron with learnable weights $\omega$ that takes the encoded feature vector as input and outputs density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Preprocessing:** For the input video, recover the surface shape point clouds $\{T_t\}$ and body masks $\{M_t\}$ for all frames $t \in [0, N_t - 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Surface-Relative Representation:** For a query point $\mathbf{x}$ at frame $t$:
    *   Find the $K$-nearest neighbor points in the current frame's point cloud $T_t$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate the signed distances from $\mathbf{x}$ to these neighbors [S1, unofficial].
    *   Map these neighbors and their signed distances to their corresponding points and signed distances in the canonical space (Frame 0) [S1, unofficial].
    *   This relative representation captures the local geometric context relative to the surface [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Hash Encoding:** Encode the surface-relative representation using multi-resolution hash encoding to produce a high-dimensional feature vector [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Radiance Field Regression:** Feed the encoded feature vector into the MLP $F_\omega$ to regress the density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and color $c(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Render the image by calculating the pixel color $C(\mathbf{r})$ for each ray $\mathbf{r}$ using the volume rendering equation [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Optimization:** Train the model by minimizing photometric loss and geometric guidance loss [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The volume rendering equation for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is [S1, unofficial]:
$$
C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha(\mathbf{x}_i) \left( \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha(\mathbf{x}_j)) \right) c(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{v})
$$
where:
*   $\alpha(\mathbf{x}_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) \delta_i)$ is the transmittance-weighted opacity [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \mathbf{v}$ are the sample points on the ray, with $\mathbf{o}$ being the ray origin and $\mathbf{v}$ the view direction [S1, unofficial].
*   $\delta_i = u_{i+1} - u_i$ is the distance between adjacent sample points [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $N_t$: Total number of frames in the input monocular video [S1, unofficial].
*   $K$: Number of nearest neighbors used to calculate the surface-relative representation [S1, unofficial].
*   Typical training time: Approximately 20 minutes [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Reconstruction Quality:** The method relies on external tools (VideoAvatar/SelfRecon) for surface shape recovery. Errors in these initial reconstructions propagate to the relative representation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Monocular Ambiguity:** Without multi-view constraints, the depth ambiguity is resolved primarily through the self-rotation motion and the geometric guidance loss [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than multi-view NeRF methods, converging in approximately 20 minutes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses multi-resolution hash encoding, which is more memory-efficient than dense voxel grids or pure MLPs for high-frequency details [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-fidelity results from monocular input; fast training time; no need for multi-view camera calibration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires the subject to self-rotate; performance depends on the quality of the initial surface reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When only monocular video is available and rapid training is required.
*   **When not to use:** When multi-view inputs are available and higher geometric accuracy is needed, or when the subject does not self-rotate.

## Background
SelfNeRF was proposed by Bo Peng, Jun Hu, Jingtao Zhou, and Juyong Zhang from the University of Science and Technology of China [S1, unofficial]. It was published on arXiv in October 2022 [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the challenge of novel view synthesis for human performance, which is important for applications like sports broadcasts, video conferences, and VR/AR [S1, unofficial]. Previous methods often required multi-view inputs and hours of training, making them impractical for public users [S1, unofficial]. SelfNeRF introduces a surface-relative representation based on multi-resolution hash encoding to aggregate inter-frame information, significantly speeding up training [S1, unofficial].
