# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) or [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observation branch (shallow MLP) and a prediction branch (linear motion model), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain [S1]. This approach leverages the inductive smoothness bias of deformation fields over feature interpolation, allowing for faster convergence and higher quality reconstruction of non-rigid motions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sequence of images with known camera poses and timestamps $t_i$. 3D points $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ sampled along rays.
*   **Output:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ in a canonical space, derived from warped real-space points.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes (collection $\mathcal{C}$) encoding the canonical space. Features are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP mapping $(\mathbf{x}, t_i) \rightarrow (y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i})$, where $y_{t_i}$ is the mean observation (deformation) and $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ are noise-related terms [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ mapping $(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) \rightarrow K_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The deformation estimation for a point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t_i$ follows these steps:

1.  **Observer Query (Current Frame):**
    Compute the observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ by passing $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ through the Observer MLP:
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
    [S1]

2.  **Prediction (History-Based):**
    Calculate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using deformations from the previous two frames ($t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$):
    $$ v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i} $$
    where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    
    Compute the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the local linear motion model:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}} $$
    [S1]

3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:**
    Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
    [S1]

4.  **State Update (Fusion):**
    Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ to produce the final estimated deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
    [S1]

5.  **Warping and Rendering:**
    Warp the real-space point $\mathbf{x}$ to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$ using the estimated deformation:
    $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} $$
    [S1]
    Query the Tri-Plane representation at $\mathbf{x}'$ to obtain features, which are processed to yield density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. Render using standard volume rendering [G].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of five components [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{image}$):** Standard photometric reconstruction loss [S1].
2.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation and the final estimated deformation, acting as a supervision for the observation MLP:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
    [S1]
3.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** Soft regularization for the initial frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real-world radiance field:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
4.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Ensures consistency between the proposal network (used for density field distillation) and the learned model [S1].
5.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Encourages sparse edges in the tri-plane representation:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from collection $\mathcal{C}$, and $i, j$ are spatial indices [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, 
                              d_x_prev_1, d_x_prev_2, eps_prev_1):
    """
    Computes the final deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman filtering.
    
    Args:
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP returning (observation, noise_terms)
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer returning Kalman gain
        x: 3D point coordinates
        t_i: Current timestamp
        t_prev_1: Timestamp of previous frame (t_{i-1})
        t_prev_2: Timestamp of frame before previous (t_{i-2})
        d_x_prev_1: Deformation of x at t_{i-1}
        d_x_prev_2: Deformation of x at t_{i-2}
        eps_prev_1: Noise terms from observer at t_{i-1}
        
    Returns:
        final_deformation: Estimated deformation d_x_t_i
    """
    # 1. Observer Query for current frame
    y_t_i, eps_t_i = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # 2. Prediction Branch
    # Calculate velocity based on previous two frames
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev_1
    v_t_i = (d_x_prev_1 - d_x_prev_2) / delta_t
    
    # Predict deformation using local linear motion
    # d_x_t_i^- = d_x_t_{i-1} + delta_t * v_t_i
    # Simplified: 2 * d_x_t_{i-1} - d_x_t_{i-2}
    d_x_pred = 2 * d_x_prev_1 - d_x_prev_2
    
    # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Input: current noise terms, previous noise terms, current time, previous time
    K_t_i = kalman_gain_net(eps_t_i, eps_prev_1, t_i, t_prev_1)
    
    # 4. Update Step
    # final_deformation = prediction + K * (observation - prediction)
    final_deformation = d_x_pred + K_t_i * (y_t_i - d_x_pred)
    
    return final_deformation, y_t_i, eps_t_i
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Frames:** For $t_0$ and $t_1$, previous frame data is unavailable. The training strategy mitigates this by gradually releasing images in chronological order, ensuring previous frames offer ample priors [S1].
*   **Large Motion:** In early training stages, predictions influence the process more to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. In later stages, observations take precedence to recover fine-grained details [S1].
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Concatenating raw coordinate inputs with encoded tri-plane features helps mitigate this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to other dynamic NeRF methods. On synthetic data, it takes approximately 30 minutes (KFD-NeRF-S) to 3 hours (KFD-NeRF-L), significantly faster than D-NeRF (48 hours) but slower than some voxel-based methods like TiNeuVox-B (40 mins) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations, allowing for high-resolution canonical space reconstruction [S1]. Parameter size is 175 MB for the large variant [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Faster convergence due to the tri-plane backbone and Kalman filter guidance; handles non-rigid motion effectively by leveraging temporal context; smoother deformation fields compared to feature interpolation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion, which may not hold for highly complex or non-linear dynamics; performance depends on the quality of the initial frames for prediction [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It builds upon the concept of modeling 4D radiance fields as dynamic systems, drawing inspiration from control theory and the Kalman filter [S1]. Previous methods like D-NeRF used MLP-based deformation fields, while others like DyNeRF used time-conditioned feature interpolation. KFD-NeRF combines the smoothness benefits of deformation fields with the temporal context utilization of Kalman filtering, using a tri-plane representation for efficiency [S1].
