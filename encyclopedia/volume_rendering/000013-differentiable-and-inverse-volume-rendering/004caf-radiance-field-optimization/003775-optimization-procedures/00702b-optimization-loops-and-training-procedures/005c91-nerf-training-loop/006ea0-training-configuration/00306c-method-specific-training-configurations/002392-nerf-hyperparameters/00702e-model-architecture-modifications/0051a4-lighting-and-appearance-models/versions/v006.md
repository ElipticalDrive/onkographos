# Lighting and Appearance Models


## Overview
Lighting and Appearance Models modify the standard NeRF architecture to explicitly represent illumination and material properties, enabling relighting and handling varying lighting conditions. Unlike standard NeRF, which learns view-dependent color implicitly via an MLP, these models separate albedo from illumination. They differ from siblings like [NeRF in the Wild (NeRF-W)](11995-nerf-in-the-wild-nerf-w.md) by explicitly modeling illumination rather than relying on appearance embeddings, and from [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md) by using global bases rather than pre-integrated local lighting.

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) serves as the baseline. Modifications generally fall into three categories [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]:
1.  **Input Expansion:** Adding new conditioning vectors (e.g., appearance embeddings $\mathbf{z}_i$, fluid volume $v$, or previous stage RGB) to the input concatenation [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
2.  **Output Expansion:** Adding auxiliary heads to predict values other than $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$, such as semantic labels $y_{aux}$, object codes $o$, or area encoding probabilities $p$. These are rendered using the same transmittance weights $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ as color:
    $$
    \hat{Y}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i y_{aux}(x_i)
    $$
    [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
3.  **Representation Substitution:** Replacing the continuous MLP function with discrete structures (voxel grids, hash tables) or different implicit surfaces (SDFs) [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md)
Neural-PIL replaces costly numerical integration of incoming light with a neural network query, predicting pre-integrated light $\tilde{L}_i(\omega_r, b_r)$ directly from an illumination embedding, direction, and roughness [13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md]. It captures finer illumination details than Spherical Harmonics (SH) or Spherical Gaussians (SG) [13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md]. Choose this when high-fidelity specular details are required and memory allows for separate encoder training. See [Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting (Neural-PIL)](13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md).

### [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md)
Spherical Harmonics NeRF replaces the view-dependent color MLP with an explicit 2nd-order SH illumination model [14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md]. This allows explicit relighting by varying learnable SH coefficients, addressing the lack of semantic meaning in standard NeRF [14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md]. Choose this for efficient global illumination control. See [Spherical Harmonics NeRF](14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md).

### StylizedNeRF
StylizedNeRF utilizes a mutual learning strategy to optimize both the stylized NeRF and the 2D stylization network [S1]. It handles ambiguities of 2D stylized results via learnable latent codes, ensuring consistency in geometry [S1].

## Background
The standard NeRF formulation [Parent](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md) implicitly encodes lighting in view-dependent colors. Explicit lighting models emerged to enable relighting and handle dynamic illumination. Neural-PIL was introduced to overcome the limitations of SH/SG representations [13783-neural-pre-integrated-lighting-neural-pil.md]. Spherical Harmonics NeRF explicitly models illumination [14480-spherical-harmonics-nerf.md]. StylizedNeRF addresses style transfer consistency [S1].
