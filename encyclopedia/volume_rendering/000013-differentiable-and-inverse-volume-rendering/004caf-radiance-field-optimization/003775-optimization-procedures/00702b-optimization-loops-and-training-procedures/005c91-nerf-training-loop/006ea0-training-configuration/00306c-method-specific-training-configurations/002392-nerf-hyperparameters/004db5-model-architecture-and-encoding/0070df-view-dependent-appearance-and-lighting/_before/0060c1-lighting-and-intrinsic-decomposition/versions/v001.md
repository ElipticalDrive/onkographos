# Lighting and Intrinsic Decomposition

Also called: Intrinsic NeRF, Inverse Rendering in NeRF, Unsupervised Decomposition and Enhancement in NeRF

## Overview
Lighting and Intrinsic Decomposition methods extend standard Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by decoupling the observed radiance into view-independent material properties (albedo, normals) and view-dependent lighting components, enabling relighting and intrinsic analysis [S1]. Unlike standard NeRFs that bake shading and shadows into the color output, these methods explicitly model the lighting environment and surface reflectance, allowing for novel viewpoint rendering under arbitrary lighting conditions [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves modifying the standard NeRF volume rendering pipeline to predict intrinsic attributes alongside density.

1.  **Intrinsic Prediction:** For a query point $\mathbf{x}$ along a ray, the network predicts intrinsic attributes $\mathbf{a}$ (albedo), $\mathbf{n}$ (normal), and potentially semantics $\mathbf{s}$ or lighting components $v$, in addition to density $\sigma$.
2.  **Volume Rendering of Intrinsics:** These attributes are accumulated along the ray using alpha-composition weights $w_i$ derived from density $\sigma$ and step size $\delta_i$:
    $$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    The final intrinsic value $\mathbf{I}$ (e.g., albedo $\mathbf{A}$ or lighting $V$) is:
    $$ \mathbf{I}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{I}_i $$
3.  **Lighting Model:** A separate parametric or learned lighting model $\mathcal{L}$ (e.g., sun direction, ambient color, sky texture) is defined.
4.  **Shading:** The final color $\mathbf{C}$ is computed by combining the rendered intrinsic attributes with the lighting model $\mathcal{L}$ and visibility/shadow estimates $V_{\text{shadow}}$:
    $$ \mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsics}, \mathcal{L}, V_{\text{shadow}}) $$
5.  **Optimization:** The scene parameters and lighting parameters are jointly optimized to minimize the difference between rendered images and input observations, often using additional priors (e.g., gray-world, smoothness) to constrain the ill-posed decomposition [S1].

## Subtopics

### UrbanIR
UrbanIR is a method for inverse rendering of large-scale urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination, inferring shape, albedo, visibility, and sun/sky illumination to enable relighting and night simulation [S1]. It differs from standard NeRFs by learning intrinsic attributes (albedo, normal) independent of lighting, and from other relightable methods like NeRF-OSR or FEGR by requiring only single-illumination RGB video without LiDAR or multiple cameras [S1].
*   **Representation:** Built on Instant-NGP spatial hash-based voxel NeRF.
*   **Attributes:** Dense Feature Hash Table decodes albedo $\mathbf{a}$, normal $\mathbf{n}$, semantics $\mathbf{s}$, and density $\sigma$ via MLP headers [S1].
*   **Lighting:** Modeled as $\mathcal{L} = \{ (\mathbf{L}_{\text{sun}}, \psi_{\text{sun}}, \phi_{\text{sun}}), \mathbf{L}_{\text{amb}}, \mathbf{C}_{\text{sky}} \}$, where sun is 5-DoF, ambient is 3-DoF Lambert, and sky is a directional texture [S1].
*   **Visibility:** Shadows are estimated by volume rendering depth to find the shaded point $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, then computing transmittance to the light source, avoiding per-scene optimized MLPs that fail with changing sun positions [S1].
*   **Rendering:** $\mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathcal{L}), \mathcal{L})$ [S1].

### [Unsupervised Decomposition and Enhancement in NeRF](18127-unsupervised-decomposition-and-enhancement-in-nerf.md)
Unsupervised Decomposition and Enhancement in NeRF (LLNeRF) decomposes the view-dependent color of a neural radiance field into a view-dependent lighting component and a view-independent reciprocal color basis, then enhances the lighting component using a neural network to produce brightened images without ground truth supervision. It differs from 2D Retinex-based methods by leveraging 3D geometry and multi-view consistency to constrain the decomposition, avoiding the need for paired low-light/high-light training data.
*   **Decomposition:** Uses two MLPs: $F_1(\tau, d)$ for lighting component $v$ and $F_2(\tau)$ for reciprocal color basis $r$. Color is $c = v \circ r$.
*   **Enhancement:** A network $\phi$ processes $v$ to output coefficients $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ for dynamic gamma correction: $\hat{v}_i = (v_i / \gamma_0)^{\alpha / (1+\gamma)}$.
*   **Losses:**
    *   $L_c$: Gray-world prior to correct color bias in RAW images.
    *   $L_s$: Smoothness prior on integrated coefficients $\alpha, \gamma$ with respect to integrated lighting $v$ across neighboring rays.
    *   $L_d$: Data supervision using tone-mapped RAW values.
*   **Rendering:** Enhanced color $\hat{c}_r = \sum w_i \hat{v}_i \circ r_i$.

### IntrinsicNeRF
IntrinsicNeRF is a method capable of breaking down a static scene’s multi-view image into consistent elements like reflectance, shading, and residual layers, achieved through intrinsic decomposition in a NeRF-based neural rendering technique [S1]. It facilitates web-based applications like changing scenes, altering lighting, and creating editable new perspectives, while also allowing IntrinsicNeRF to undergo autonomous training for consistent multi-viewpoint intrinsic decomposition, introducing an innovative distance-conscious point-sampling and iterative clustering optimization technique for adaptive reflectance [S1]. While IntrinsicNeRF excels in indoor settings (like Replica scenes), its application to outdoor environments with extensive scenes and limited images might result in diminished detail in the rendering outcomes [S1].

## Background
The concept of intrinsic decomposition in computer vision dates back to separating reflectance from illumination. In the context of NeRF, standard models [G] bake all lighting effects into the color field, making them non-relightable [S1]. Recent works like UrbanIR [S1] and IntrinsicNeRF [S1] explicitly model lighting and materials. UrbanIR targets large-scale urban scenes from monocular video [S1], while IntrinsicNeRF focuses on consistent multi-view decomposition for indoor scenes [S1]. Unsupervised Decomposition and Enhancement in NeRF addresses low-light scenarios by decomposing lighting and color to enable enhancement without paired data [S1].
