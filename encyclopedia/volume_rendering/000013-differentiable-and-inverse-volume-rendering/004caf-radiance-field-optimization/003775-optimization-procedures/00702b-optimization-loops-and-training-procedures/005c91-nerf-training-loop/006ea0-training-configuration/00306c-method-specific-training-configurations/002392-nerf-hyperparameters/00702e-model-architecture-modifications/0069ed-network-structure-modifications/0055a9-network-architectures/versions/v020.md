# Network Architectures
Also called: NeRF Network Architectures

## Overview
Network architectures in NeRF-based methods define the specific neural network structures used to map spatial coordinates and viewing directions to radiance field properties (density and color). While the baseline NeRF [Parent](20497-network-structure-modifications.md) employs two standard MLPs, various modifications alter input dimensions, output channels, network depth, or the number of parallel networks to address specific challenges such as dynamic scenes, transparency, mobile efficiency, or semantic consistency. These architectures set themselves apart by introducing specialized components like blend weight fields, residual networks, or multi-input multi-output (MIMO) structures, optimizing for trade-offs between training speed, inference latency, and reconstruction fidelity.

## How it works
The core mechanism involves defining the input-output mapping of one or more Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). The specific architecture depends on the variant, but general implementation details for notable variants are provided below.

### Baseline NeRF Architecture (Reference)
The baseline consists of two MLPs:
1.  **Position MLP ($\Phi_{pos}$)**:
    *   **Input**: Spatial position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and its positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$.
    *   **Output**: Feature vector $\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_h}$.
2.  **Shading MLP ($\Phi_{shade}$)**:
    *   **Input**: Feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ encoded as $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$.
    *   **Output**: Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and RGB color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

### MobileNeRF Architecture
MobileNeRF modifies the baseline to exploit the polygon rasterization pipeline for mobile efficiency [S1].
*   **Structure**: Uses three specific MLPs: A, F, and H.
*   **MLP A and F**:
    *   **Design**: Based on the standard NeRF MLP design.
    *   **Parameters**: Hidden layer sizes are increased from 256 to 384 neurons [S1].
    *   **Usage**: Used during training to compute features but are **not** used during inference, allowing for larger capacity without inference latency penalties [S1].
*   **MLP H**:
    *   **Design**: A small MLP identical to the one used in SNeRG.
    *   **Parameters**: Two hidden layers, each consisting of 16 neurons [S1].
*   **Data Storage**: Features are stored in texture images. Since features are 8-dimensional and PNGs have 4 channels, two PNG images are used. The alpha channel is squeezed into the first feature channel to store opacity implicitly (alpha is 1 if the first feature channel is non-zero, 0 otherwise) [S1].

### Dynamic Scene Network Architecture
This architecture divides dynamic scenes into static and dynamic components modeled by two separate MLPs [S5].
*   **Dynamic Network ($F_{\Theta}^{dy}$)**:
    *   **Inputs**: Positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and time $\gamma(t)$.
    *   **Outputs**: Color $\mathbf{c}_t$, density $\sigma_t$, scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$, and disocclusion weights $\mathcal{W}_t$.
    *   **Formula**:
        $$ (\mathbf{c}_t, \sigma_t, \mathcal{F}_t, \mathcal{W}_t) = F_{\Theta}^{dy}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d}), \gamma(t)) \quad (22) $$
*   **Static Network ($F_{\Theta}^{st}$)**:
    *   **Inputs**: Positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$.
    *   **Outputs**: Color $\mathbf{c}$, density $\sigma$, and blending weight $v$.
    *   **Formula**:
        $$ (\mathbf{c}, \sigma, v) = F_{\Theta}^{st}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d})) \quad (23) $$
*   **Sampling**: Uses equidistant sampling along rays instead of hierarchical sampling [S5].

### NeRF Network Architecture with Blend Weight Field
This architecture extends the radiance field with a dedicated blend weight field, often used for animatable human bodies [S6].
*   **Radiance Field Networks ($F_\sigma, F_c$)**:
    *   **Design**: Closely follow the original NeRF architecture.
    *   **Sampling**: Single-level NeRF, sampling 64 points along each camera ray [S6].
*   **Blend Weight Field Network ($F_{\Delta w}$)**:
    *   **Design**: Almost identical to $F_\sigma$.
    *   **Modification**: The final output layer has 24 channels.
    *   **Activation**: Applies $\exp(\cdot)$ to the output [S6].
*   **Codes**: Appearance code $\ell_i$ and blend weight field code $\psi_i$ both have dimensions of 128 [S6].

### Residual-NeRF-S Architecture
An ablation variant of Residual-NeRF that merges background and residual NeRFs [S3].
*   **Structure**: Two MLPs, $\Phi_{bg}$ and $\Phi_{res}$.
*   **Merging Strategy**: Outputs are added before activation, unlike the standard Residual-NeRF which uses a Mixnet.
*   **Formulas**:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_{bg} + \sigma_{res})_i \delta_i)) (c_{bg+res})_i $$
    $$ c_{bg+res} = S(c'_{bg} + c'_{res}) $$
    $$ (c'_{bg}, \sigma_{bg}) = \Phi_{bg}(h(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)) $$
    $$ (c'_{res}, \sigma_{res}) = \Phi_{res}(h(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)) $$
    $$ S(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}} $$
    where $S(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid activation [S3].

### OccNet (Single-Level NeRF)
A single-level version of NeRF that does not leverage positional encodings [S4, unofficial].
*   **Structure**: Single transformation level.
*   **Performance**:
    *   2D Images: 13.90 PSNR / 0.437 SSIM.
    *   Radiance Field: 20.60 PSNR / 0.849 SSIM.
    *   Training Time: 02:17 / 100:23.
    *   Model Size: 0.38 / 0.43 [S4, unofficial].

### Pyramid NeRF
Accomplishes rapid training and inference through an image pyramid and frequency optimization [S2].
*   **Strategy**: "Low-frequency-first, high-frequency-second" approach.
*   **Structure**: Substitutes large MLPs with thousands of smaller ones via progressive segmentation.
*   **Sampling**: Introduces depth-aware sampling to enhance rendering quality and hasten convergence [S2].
*   **Performance**: Enhances training speed by 15x and inference speed by 805x relative to original NeRF [S2].

## Subtopics

### [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md)
MIMO-DONeRF applies the Multi-Input Multi-Output concept to the DONeRF architecture. It modifies the shading network to accept multiple spatial inputs and view directions simultaneously, producing multiple color and density outputs per forward pass. This improves inference speed compared to increasing samples in standard DONeRF while maintaining quality by accumulating neighbor information. It differs from standard DONeRF by processing $N_p$ samples in parallel rather than sequentially, and from MIMO-NeRF by being adapted to the two-network structure (depth oracle and shading) of DONeRF.

### [Point-NeRF Adaptation for Object Manipulation](16395-point-nerf-adaptation-for-object-manipulation.md)
This method adapts the Point-NeRF architecture to enable 3D scene geometry decomposition and manipulation from 2D images. It replaces standard MVS point cloud generation with ground-truth dense data during training and uses a diffusion model to infer object codes. It differs from standard Point-NeRF by integrating a DM-NeRF module to map spatial locations to object codes and modifying the rendering pipeline to support point transformation.

### [Sparse Latent Space for 4D Novel View Synthesis (SLS4D)](18112-sparse-latent-space-for-4d-novel-view-synthesis-sls4d.md)
SLS4D represents dynamic radiance fields using a learnable sparse latent space, reducing parameters compared to dense grid discretizations while maintaining high-frequency detail via attention-based interpolation. It differs from dense grid methods (like TiNeuVox) by avoiding $O(N^3TF)$ space complexity, and from per-image latent code methods by offering better generalization across time through continuous temporal interpolation.

### [Neural Feature Fusion Fields (N3F)](18114-neural-feature-fusion-fields-n3f.md)
N3F distills dense 2D image features from a pre-trained teacher network into a 3D neural field (student) via differentiable volumetric rendering. Unlike Semantic NeRF or Panoptic NeRF which fuse discrete semantic labels, N3F fuses continuous dense feature vectors, allowing for open-world applications without manual labels. It differs from standard feature distillation because the student operates in 3D world space (ensuring spatial consistency) while the teacher operates in 2D image space.

### [CLNeRF Architecture with Embeddings](18159-clnerf-architecture-with-embeddings.md)
CLNeRF enables continual learning by incorporating scene changes (appearance and geometry) over time within a single model, preventing model size growth. It sets itself apart from siblings like MIMO-DONeRF and SLS4D by using explicit, trainable per-scan embedding vectors rather than latent space compression or multi-instance networks, allowing for efficient updates and low overhead for generative replay.

### [Mutual Information Gradient Shaping](18669-mutual-information-gradient-shaping.md)
Implemented as JacobiNeRF, this technique shapes the tangent space of a NeRF to encode semantic correlations between scene entities. Unlike first-order approaches that distill 2D features into 3D radiance channels, MIGS aligns the Jacobians of correlated entities to maximize their mutual information under random weight perturbations, enabling efficient sparse label propagation and semantic consensus.

### [Multi-Level Neural Scene Graph](19889-multi-level-neural-scene-graph.md)
Multi-Level Neural Scene Graph (NSG) decomposes an environment into a graph of static, dynamic, and temporal nodes to model urban environments. It distinguishes itself by explicitly separating the radiance field into a static component $\phi$ and a dynamic component $\psi$, conditioned on sequence-level and object-level latent vectors respectively, enabling the disentanglement of scene-dependent appearance from object texture.

### [NAN (Noise-Aware NeRFs)](20279-nan-noise-aware-nerfs.md)
NAN extends the IBRNet architecture to handle degraded noisy images in low-light conditions by modifying feature extraction, aggregation, and color blending to be noise-aware. Unlike standard NeRFs that synthesize novel views, NAN fixes an existing view image by leveraging multiple noisy inputs. It distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling spatial noise statistics through 3D blending kernels and cross-view variance features, rather than relying solely on single-pixel cross-view consistency.

### [Multiplicative Interaction Module](21736-multiplicative-interaction-module.md)
The Multiplicative Interaction Module (MIM) generates conditional inputs for a NeRF by computing multiplicative interactions between expression, identity, and optional latent codes. It sets itself apart by explicitly modeling higher-order (second and third) multiplicative dependencies between semantic codes rather than relying on linear concatenation or simple additive fusion.

### [Part-Wise NARF (NARFP)](23078-part-wise-narf-narfp.md)
Part-Wise NARF models an articulated object by training a separate Ray-Traced NeRF (RT-NeRF) for each rigid part, combining their outputs to estimate global density and color. It differs from holistic approaches by explicitly computing rigid transformations via forward kinematics, thereby avoiding implicit transformation issues and part dependency problems inherent in other methods.

## Operating characteristics
*   **MobileNeRF**: Optimizes for mobile inference by moving heavy computation (MLPs A and F) to training time only. Inference relies on rasterization of stored features, significantly reducing runtime memory and compute on device [S1].
*   **Dynamic Scene Network**: Uses equidistant sampling which may be less efficient than hierarchical sampling but simplifies the training pipeline for dynamic scenes [S5].
*   **Residual-NeRF-S**: Simpler to implement than Residual-NeRF with Mixnet but produces worse depth and view reconstruction, especially for partially opaque objects [S3].
*   **OccNet**: Significantly lower performance (approx. 10dB PSNR drop) compared to multi-level NeRF due to the lack of positional encodings, but offers a baseline for single-level models [S4, unofficial].
*   **Pyramid NeRF**: Offers massive speedups (15x training, 805x inference) but relies on progressive segmentation and depth-aware sampling which may complicate implementation [S2].
*   **Blend Weight Field**: Adds computational overhead due to the additional $F_{\Delta w}$ network and 24-channel output, but enables robust handling of dynamic human poses [S6].

## Background
The baseline NeRF architecture [Parent](20497-network-structure-modifications.md) introduced the use of two MLPs to represent continuous volumetric scenes [S1]. Subsequent research has modified this architecture to address specific limitations:
*   **MobileNeRF** [S1] was developed to enable neural field rendering on mobile architectures by exploiting the rasterization pipeline.
*   **Dynamic Scene Networks** [S5] were introduced to handle time-variant scenes by separating static and dynamic components.
*   **Residual-NeRF** [S3] was proposed to improve depth perception in scenes with transparent objects by learning residuals against a background NeRF.
*   **Pyramid NeRF** [S2] was introduced to accelerate training and inference through frequency optimization.
*   **OccNet** [S4, unofficial] serves as a baseline comparison for the importance of positional encodings in coordinate-based networks.
*   **Blend Weight Fields** [S6] were developed to model animatable human bodies by incorporating explicit blend weights.
