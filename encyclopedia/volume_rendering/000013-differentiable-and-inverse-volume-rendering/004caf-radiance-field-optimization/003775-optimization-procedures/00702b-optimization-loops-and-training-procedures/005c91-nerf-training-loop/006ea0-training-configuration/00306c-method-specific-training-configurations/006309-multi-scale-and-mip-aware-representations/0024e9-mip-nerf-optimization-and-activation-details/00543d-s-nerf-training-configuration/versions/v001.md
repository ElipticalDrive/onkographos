# S-NeRF Training Configuration

Also called: S-NeRF Optimization Setup, S-NeRF Hyperparameters

## Overview
S-NeRF training configuration defines the specific hyperparameters, network architectures, and confidence-based loss weighting used to optimize Neural Radiance Fields for dynamic street scenes using LiDAR and RGB data [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by employing a dual-phase training loop (foreground vehicles vs. background street views) and integrating geometry/projection confidence maps to mitigate depth outliers from moving objects [S1, unofficial]. This configuration builds on [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md) but adds specific LiDAR-guided confidence mechanisms and distinct MLP topologies for foreground/background separation.

## How it works
The method optimizes two distinct scene components—foreground vehicles and background street views—using separate network architectures, sampling strategies, and loss functions.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Sparse LiDAR points (consecutive frames $t-1, t, t+1$), RGB images, camera poses, and displacement $\Delta P$ between frames [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP weights for density and color fields, and confidence maps for geometry and projection consistency [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method uses two primary MLP structures, sharing the same encoding scheme as mip-NeRF [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Foreground Vehicle MLP:**
    *   Structure: 4 layers.
    *   Hidden units: 256 per layer.
    *   Total parameters: Part of the global 8.76M count [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Background Street View MLPs:**
    *   **Density MLP:** 8 layers, 1024 hidden units per layer.
    *   **Color MLP:** 3 layers, 128 hidden units per layer.
    *   These share parameters with mip-NeRF for fair comparison [S1, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps
1.  **Scene Parameterization:** Define the scene radius $r = 3$ for the background scene parameterization function [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Confidence Map Computation:** Compute geometry and projection confidence maps to reduce the influence of depth outliers [S1, unofficial].
    *   Project 3D LiDAR points from time $t-1$ and $t+1$ to time $t$ using displacement $\Delta P$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform consistency checks using optical flow and depth projection to remove moving object outliers [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute RGB, SSIM, and VGG confidence maps to measure photometric and feature consistency [S1, unofficial].
    *   Combine depth, flow, RGB, SSIM, and VGG components via learnable aggregation to produce final confidence maps [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Foreground Training (Vehicles):**
    *   Sample 64 times along each ray [S1, unofficial].
    *   Train for 30,000 iterations using Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Batch size: 1024 [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Background Training (Street Views):**
    *   Sample 128 times along each ray in a log scale [S1, unofficial].
    *   Train for 100,000 iterations using Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate schedule: Log-linear decay from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ to $5 \times 10^{-6}$ with a warm-up phase of 2,500 iterations [S1, unofficial].
    *   Batch size: 2048 [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Loss Calculation:** Apply weighted loss functions using confidence maps [S1, unofficial].
    *   Depth loss weight $\lambda_1$: 1 (foreground), 0.2 (background) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Smooth loss weight $\lambda_2$: 0.15 (foreground), 0.01 (background) [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
*   **Reprojection:** 3D points at $t-1$ and $t+1$ are projected to $t$ using displacement $\Delta P$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Confidence Threshold:** A threshold $\tau = 20\%$ is used for confidence measurement in background scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights:** The total loss incorporates $\lambda_1$ for depth consistency and $\lambda_2$ for smoothness, scaled by the computed confidence maps [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Foreground:**
    *   Samples per ray: 64 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Iterations: 30,000 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Batch size: 1024 [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\lambda_1$ (depth): 1 [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\lambda_2$ (smooth): 0.15 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Background:**
    *   Samples per ray: 128 (log scale) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Iterations: 100,000 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate: Log-linear decay $5 \times 10^{-4} \to 5 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Warm-up: 2,500 iterations [S1, unofficial].
    *   Batch size: 2048 [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\lambda_1$ (depth): 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\lambda_2$ (smooth): 0.01 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Confidence threshold $\tau$: 20% [S1, unofficial].
    *   Scene radius $r$: 3 [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Moving Objects:** Outliers from moving objects (e.g., other vehicles, pedestrians) are handled by the confidence-guided learning and instance segmentation/optical flow removal [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hardware Scaling:** Training time varies significantly by component; vehicles are faster (~2 hours/vehicle) than background scenes (~17 hours/scene) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Confidence Map Computation
Computes geometry and projection confidence maps to reduce the influence of depth outliers during training [S1, unofficial]. It projects 3D LiDAR points from time $t-1$ and $t+1$ to time $t$ using displacement $\Delta P$ between frames [S1, unofficial]. Consistency checks using optical flow and depth projection remove moving object outliers [S1, unofficial]. RGB, SSIM, and VGG confidence maps measure photometric and feature consistency [S1, unofficial]. These components are combined via learnable aggregation to produce final confidence maps [S1, unofficial]. The confidence threshold $\tau$ is set to 20% for background scenes [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes approximately 2 hours per vehicle on a single RTX 3090 GPU and ~17 hours per background scene (250 images, 1280×1920) on two RTX 3090 GPUs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Total learnable parameters are 8.76M, similar to other SOTA methods (8.7–9.9M) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The method achieves a PSNR of 23.60 and SSIM of 0.743 on the Waymo dataset, outperforming Mip-NeRF 360 (PSNR 22.10, SSIM 0.724) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effective handling of dynamic street scenes using LiDAR guidance; robust against depth outliers via confidence maps.
*   **Limitations:** Requires LiDAR data; training background scenes is computationally expensive compared to methods without coarse rendering supervision (e.g., Mip-NeRF 360 is faster as it skips coarse rendering) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
S-NeRF was introduced in the paper "S-NeRF: Neural Radiance Fields for Street Views" published at ICLR 2023 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the challenge of reconstructing large-scale dynamic street scenes by combining LiDAR depth information with RGB imagery, leveraging confidence maps to handle moving objects and depth outliers [S1, unofficial].
