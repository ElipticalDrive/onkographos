# NeRF-Casting

Also called: NeRF-Casting: Improved View-Dependent Appearance with Consistent Reflections

## Overview
NeRF-Casting is a method for synthesizing photorealistic specular appearance and reflections in real-world scenes by integrating ray tracing into the Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) rendering pipeline [S1]. It improves upon standard NeRF and prior inverse rendering approaches by casting reflection rays from points along camera rays into the NeRF geometry to render feature vectors, which are then decoded into color using a small MLP, rather than querying a large MLP for outgoing radiance [S1]. Unlike Ref-NeRF, which reparameterizes outgoing radiance as a function of reflected view direction and requires a large MLP to encode the entire environment, NeRF-Casting uses ray tracing to naturally synthesize consistent reflections of nearby and distant content without requiring the MLP to represent the full environment at every point [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera pose (origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$), NeRF geometry representation (density field $\tau$), and a small color decoder MLP.
*   **Outputs:** Volume-rendered color $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ for the camera ray.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the standard NeRF volume rendering data structure:
*   **Samples:** A set of points $\{ \mathbf{x}^{(i)} \}$ along the camera ray, where $\mathbf{x}^{(i)} = \mathbf{o} + t^{(i)}\mathbf{d}$.
*   **Density:** Volume density $\tau^{(i)}$ at each sample point.
*   **Features:** Feature vectors $\mathbf{f}^{(i)}$ sampled via reflection rays.

**Spatial Contraction:**
To give higher representational capacity to points near the cameras, a spatial contraction function $C(\mathbf{x})$ maps points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ to a sphere of radius 2 [S1]. The function is defined as:
$$
C(\mathbf{x}) = 
\begin{cases} 
\mathbf{x} & \text{if } \|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1 \\
\frac{1}{2 - \|\mathbf{x}\|} \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \text{if } \|\mathbf{x}\| > 1 
\end{cases}
$$
This contracted coordinate is used as input to the NeRF density network [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Camera Ray Sampling:** For each camera ray, sample points $\mathbf{x}^{(i)}$ along the ray at depths $t^{(i)}$.
2.  **Reflection Ray Casting:** For each sample point $\mathbf{x}^{(i)}$ along the camera ray, cast a reflection ray into the NeRF geometry. The direction of this reflection ray is determined by the view direction and the local surface normal estimated from the NeRF density gradient [S1].
3.  **Feature Sampling:** Trace the reflection ray through the NeRF representation to sample properly anti-aliased feature vectors $\mathbf{f}^{(i)}$ from the reflected scene content [S1]. This involves volume rendering along the reflection ray to aggregate features from the reflected geometry.
4.  **Color Decoding:** Use a small, inexpensive MLP to decode the sampled feature vectors $\mathbf{f}^{(i)}$ into reflected color $\mathbf{c}^{(i)}$ [S1].
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Combine the colors $\mathbf{c}^{(i)}$ and weights $w^{(i)}$ from the camera ray samples to produce the final image color $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ using the standard volume rendering equation [S1].

**Volume Rendering Equation:**
The final color $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ is computed as:
$$
\bar{\mathbf{c}} = \sum_i w^{(i)} \mathbf{c}^{(i)}
$$
where $\mathbf{c}^{(i)}$ is the color at the $i$-th sample location, and $w^{(i)}$ is the weight of the $i$-th sample [S1].

The weight $w^{(i)}$ is calculated as:
$$
w^{(i)} = \left( 1 - \exp\left(-\tau^{(i)} \left( t^{(i+1)} - t^{(i)} \right) \right) \right) \exp\left( -\sum_{j < i} \tau^{(j)} \left( t^{(j+1)} - t^{(j)} \right) \right)
$$
where $\tau^{(i)}$ is the volume density and $t^{(i)}$ is the depth of the $i$-th sample [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def nerf_casting_render(camera_origin, camera_direction, nerf_density_network, color_decoder_mlp, num_samples):
    """
    Renders a single camera ray using NeRF-Casting.
    
    Args:
        camera_origin: 3D vector, origin of the camera ray.
        camera_direction: 3D vector, direction of the camera ray.
        nerf_density_network: Function mapping contracted 3D coordinates to volume density.
        color_decoder_mlp: Small MLP mapping feature vectors to color.
        num_samples: Number of samples along the camera ray.
        
    Returns:
        rendered_color: 3D vector, the final color of the ray.
    """
    # 1. Sample points along the camera ray
    # Define depth range [t_min, t_max] based on scene bounds
    t_min = 0.0
    t_max = 10.0 # Example value, depends on scene
    depths = linspace(t_min, t_max, num_samples)
    
    # 2. Evaluate density at sample points
    sample_points = camera_origin + outer_product(depths, camera_direction)
    densities = []
    for point in sample_points:
        # Apply spatial contraction
        contracted_point = spatial_contraction(point)
        # Query NeRF for density
        density = nerf_density_network(contracted_point)
        densities.append(density)
    
    # 3. Compute weights for volume rendering
    weights = compute_weights(densities, depths)
    
    # 4. Cast reflection rays and sample features
    colors = []
    for i, point in enumerate(sample_points):
        # Estimate surface normal from density gradient [G]
        # In practice, this might be approximated or learned
        normal = estimate_normal(point, nerf_density_network)
        
        # Compute reflection direction
        # R = D - 2(D.N)N, where D is view direction (camera_direction)
        # Note: camera_direction points from camera to scene, so view direction for reflection is -camera_direction
        view_direction = -camera_direction
        reflection_direction = view_direction - 2 * dot(view_direction, normal) * normal
        
        # Cast reflection ray and render features [S1]
        # This involves sampling along the reflection ray and volume rendering features
        # For simplicity, assume a function that returns the feature vector from the reflection
        feature = render_reflection_features(reflection_direction, point, nerf_density_network)
        
        # Decode feature to color using small MLP
        color = color_decoder_mlp(feature)
        colors.append(color)
    
    # 5. Volume render the final color
    rendered_color = dot(weights, colors)
    
    return rendered_color

def spatial_contraction(x):
    """
    Maps points in R^3 to a sphere of radius 2.
    """
    norm_x = norm(x)
    if norm_x <= 1:
        return x
    else:
        return (1 / (2 - norm_x)) * (x / norm_x)

def compute_weights(densities, depths):
    """
    Computes the alpha weights for volume rendering.
    """
    weights = []
    num_samples = len(densities)
    for i in range(num_samples):
        t_i = depths[i]
        t_i_plus_1 = depths[i + 1] if i + 1 < num_samples else depths[i] + 0.1 # Handle last sample [G]
        delta_t = t_i_plus_1 - t_i
        
        # Exponential term for transmittance
        transmittance = 1.0
        for j in range(i):
            transmittance *= exp(-densities[j] * (depths[j + 1] - depths[j]))
        
        # Weight calculation
        weight = (1 - exp(-densities[i] * delta_t)) * transmittance
        weights.append(weight)
    
    return weights

def render_reflection_features(reflection_direction, origin, nerf_density_network):
    """
    Casts a reflection ray and renders feature vectors.
    """
    # Sample points along the reflection ray
    # Use a similar sampling strategy as the camera ray
    reflection_depths = linspace(0.0, 5.0, 16) # Example parameters [G]
    reflection_points = origin + outer_product(reflection_depths, reflection_direction)
    
    # Query features from NeRF at reflection points [S1]
    # NeRF typically outputs density and features; here we assume it outputs features for reflection
    features = []
    for point in reflection_points:
        contracted_point = spatial_contraction(point)
        # Assume nerf_density_network also returns features or there is a separate feature network
        # For this pseudocode, we assume a feature query function
        feature = query_reflection_features(contracted_point)
        features.append(feature)
    
    # Volume render the features along the reflection ray
    # Similar to color rendering, but aggregating features
    reflection_weights = compute_weights_for_reflection(reflection_depths) # Simplified [G]
    rendered_feature = dot(reflection_weights, features)
    
    return rendered_feature
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The spatial contraction function involves division by $(2 - \|\mathbf{x}\|)$, which approaches zero as $\|\mathbf{x}\|$ approaches 2. Care must be taken to avoid numerical instability for points near the boundary of the contracted sphere [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The handling of the last sample in the depth range requires a defined $\Delta t$ [G].
*   **Normal Estimation:** The method requires estimating surface normals from the NeRF density gradient. This can be noisy and may require smoothing or regularization [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds the cost of casting and tracing reflection rays for each sample point along the camera ray. However, it avoids the expensive Monte Carlo integration over the entire hemisphere required by inverse rendering methods [S1]. The color decoder MLP is small, reducing the computational burden compared to large MLPs used in Ref-NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The method does not require storing the full environment map in the MLP, reducing memory usage compared to Ref-NeRF [S1].
*   **Performance:** NeRF-Casting outperforms prior methods for view synthesis of scenes containing shiny objects and is the only existing NeRF method that can synthesize photorealistic specular appearance and reflections in real-world scenes while requiring comparable optimization time to current state-of-the-art view synthesis models [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient rendering of specular reflections, consistent reflections of nearby and distant content, lower computational overhead than inverse rendering methods.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the NeRF geometry for reflection ray tracing. Performance may degrade in scenes with complex lighting or transparent objects.

## Background
NeRF-Casting was introduced in the paper "NeRF-Casting: Improved View-Dependent Appearance with Consistent Reflections" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of existing NeRF methods that rely on large computationally-expensive neural networks to model outgoing radiance, which limits optimization and rendering speed [S1]. The method builds on the standard NeRF volume rendering framework [S1].
