# Multi-stage Training Protocol

Also called: Pretrain and Joint Train Strategy, Training Scheme

## Overview
This protocol defines a progressive, multi-stage optimization strategy for inverse rendering models that jointly predict surface properties (albedo, normals, depth) and lighting. It solves the instability issues inherent in end-to-end training of coupled radiance fields and lighting by decoupling the learning process into distinct phases. It sets itself apart from single-stage end-to-end training by ensuring that the Direct Prediction Module produces reasonable initial values before the Lighting Joint Prediction Module is introduced, thereby preventing gradient conflicts and convergence to poor local minima.

## How it works
The method consists of four sequential stages. The inputs are a dataset of multi-view images (typically LDR) and an initialized visible surface volume. The outputs are the trained weights for the Direct Prediction Module ($h_{DP}$), the Lighting Joint Prediction Module, and the Joint Re-prediction Module.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Direct Prediction Module ($h_{DP}$):** A neural network branch predicting albedo ($\tilde{A}$), normals ($\tilde{N}$), and depth ($\tilde{D}$).
*   **Lighting Joint Prediction Module:** A network predicting lighting ($\hat{L}$) conditioned on the outputs of $h_{DP}$.
*   **Joint Re-prediction Module:** A network refining albedo, normals, and depth ($\hat{A}, \hat{N}, \hat{D}$) using the predicted lighting.
*   **Loss Function ($L$):**
    $$ L = \lambda_A L_{albedo} + \lambda_N L_{normal} + \lambda_D L_{depth} + \lambda_L L_{light} + \lambda_{visible} L_{visible} + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg} + \lambda_{rerender} L_{rerender} $$
    Where $\lambda$ terms are hyperparameters balancing the different loss components [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Pretrain Direct Prediction Module:**
    Train only the albedo, normal, and depth branches of $h_{DP}$ using available supervision (e.g., ground truth normals/depth if available, or self-supervised cues). The goal is to ensure $h_{DP}$ produces reasonable geometric and material estimates. The Lighting Joint Prediction Module is not yet active or its weights are not updated.

2.  **Joint Train Direct and Lighting Modules:**
    Activate the Lighting Joint Prediction Module. Jointly train $h_{DP}$ and the Lighting Joint Prediction Module using the full multi-task loss $L$. This stage allows the lighting module to learn from the now-stable outputs of the direct prediction module.

3.  **Train Joint Re-prediction Module:**
    Freeze the weights of the Direct Prediction Module and the Lighting Joint Prediction Module. Train only the Joint Re-prediction Module, which takes the outputs from the previous stages and refines the predictions of albedo, normals, and depth. This step leverages the learned lighting information to correct surface property estimates.

4.  **End-to-End Finetune:**
    Unfreeze all modules. Perform end-to-end fine-tuning of all three modules jointly. The loss is computed on both the final joint prediction outputs $(\hat{A}, \hat{N}, \hat{D}, \hat{L})$ and the intermediate outputs $(\tilde{A}, \tilde{N}, \tilde{D})$ from the Direct Prediction Module. This ensures consistency across all stages and refines the global solution.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def multi_stage_training(model, dataset, hyperparams):
    """
    model: Contains h_DP, LightingJointModule, JointRepredictionModule
    dataset: Multi-view image dataset
    hyperparams: Contains lambda_A, lambda_N, etc.
    """
    
    # Stage 1: Pretrain Direct Prediction Module
    print("Stage 1: Pretrain Direct Prediction Module")
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage1):
        for batch in dataset:
            # Forward pass through h_DP only
            A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde = model.h_DP(batch.images)
            
            # Compute loss for albedo, normal, depth only
            # Note: Lighting loss components are ignored or zeroed
            loss = compute_partial_loss(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde, batch)
            
            # Backward pass for h_DP only
            model.h_DP.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            model.h_DP.step()

    # Stage 2: Joint Train Direct and Lighting Modules
    print("Stage 2: Joint Train Direct and Lighting Modules")
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage2):
        for batch in dataset:
            # Forward pass
            A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde = model.h_DP(batch.images)
            L_hat = model.LightingJointModule(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde)
            
            # Compute full multi-task loss L
            loss = hyperparams.lambda_A * L_albedo(A_tilde, batch) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_N * L_normal(N_tilde, batch) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_D * L_depth(D_tilde, batch) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_L * L_light(L_hat, batch) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_visible * L_visible(batch) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_reg * L_reg(model) + \
                   hyperparams.lambda_rerender * L_rerender(batch)
            
            # Backward pass for h_DP and LightingJointModule
            model.h_DP.zero_grad()
            model.LightingJointModule.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            model.h_DP.step()
            model.LightingJointModule.step()

    # Stage 3: Train Joint Re-prediction Module
    print("Stage 3: Train Joint Re-prediction Module")
    # Freeze weights of first two modules
    model.h_DP.freeze()
    model.LightingJointModule.freeze()
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage3):
        for batch in dataset:
            # Forward pass
            A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde = model.h_DP(batch.images)
            L_hat = model.LightingJointModule(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde)
            A_hat, N_hat, D_hat = model.JointRepredictionModule(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde, L_hat)
            
            # Compute loss for re-prediction outputs
            # Specific loss formulation for re-prediction refinement
            loss = compute_reprediction_loss(A_hat, N_hat, D_hat, batch)
            
            # Backward pass for JointRepredictionModule only
            model.JointRepredictionModule.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            model.JointRepredictionModule.step()

    # Stage 4: End-to-End Finetune
    print("Stage 4: End-to-End Finetune")
    # Unfreeze all modules
    model.h_DP.unfreeze()
    model.LightingJointModule.unfreeze()
    model.JointRepredictionModule.unfreeze()
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage4):
        for batch in dataset:
            # Forward pass
            A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde = model.h_DP(batch.images)
            L_hat = model.LightingJointModule(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde)
            A_hat, N_hat, D_hat = model.JointRepredictionModule(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde, L_hat)
            
            # Compute loss on both intermediate and final outputs
            loss_intermediate = compute_loss(A_tilde, N_tilde, D_tilde, batch)
            loss_final = compute_loss(A_hat, N_hat, D_hat, batch) + compute_lighting_loss(L_hat, batch)
            
            loss = loss_intermediate + loss_final
            
            # Backward pass for all modules
            model.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            model.step()

    return model
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_A, \lambda_N, \lambda_D, \lambda_L, \lambda_{visible}, \lambda_{reg}, \lambda_{rerender}$: Loss weights. Typical values are not specified in the source [S1].
*   Volume resolution: $128^3$ for initialized visible surface volume and predicted VSG lighting [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Precision:** The method relies on the stability of the intermediate outputs. If Stage 1 fails to produce reasonable normals/depth, Stage 2 may diverge.
*   **Boundaries:** Freezing weights in Stage 3 requires careful handling of gradient flow to ensure no accidental updates occur.

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Higher than single-stage training due to four distinct phases. However, each phase is computationally cheaper than a full end-to-end training from scratch, as later stages benefit from good initialization.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to standard end-to-end training, as all modules are active during the final stage. Intermediate gradients must be stored for the backpropagation through all modules in Stage 4.
*   **Strengths:** Improves convergence stability for coupled inverse rendering problems. Allows specialized learning for lighting and surface properties before joint optimization.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage durations and loss weights. More complex to implement than a single training loop.
*   **When to use:** When training inverse rendering models that jointly predict lighting and surface properties, especially when end-to-end training leads to instability or poor lighting estimation.
*   **When not to use:** For simple forward rendering or single-property prediction tasks where joint optimization is not required.

## Background
The multi-stage training protocol was introduced in the context of indoor inverse rendering with 3D spatially-varying lighting. It addresses the challenge of learning HDR lighting from LDR images by progressively building the model's capabilities. The method was proposed by the authors of "Learning Indoor Inverse Rendering with 3D Spatially-Varying Lighting" [S1].
