# Training Configuration and Optimization

Also called: NeRF Training Hyperparameters, Optimization Settings

## Overview
This article defines the hyperparameter configurations and optimization protocols for training Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and its variants. It addresses the problem of converging implicit radiance fields to accurate scene representations by specifying learning rates, sampling strategies, loss weights, and architectural constraints. It differs from sibling articles by focusing on the *training dynamics* (optimizer settings, loss formulation, and regularization) rather than the *network architecture* (MLP depth, encoding functions) or *sampling algorithms* (stratified vs. importance sampling) themselves, though it references them.

## Shared principles
The training process for standard NeRF and its direct descendants relies on minimizing a photometric reconstruction loss between rendered pixels and ground-truth images using gradient-based optimization. The core mechanism involves differentiable volume rendering, where the gradient flows from the pixel loss through the alpha-compositing equation to the MLP weights .

**Optimization Loop**
The standard loop iterates over a fixed number of steps. In each step:
1.  A batch of rays is sampled from training images.
2.  Points are sampled along these rays using stratified sampling within near/far bounds.
3.  The MLP predicts density ($\sigma$) and color ($c$) for each point.
4.  Volume rendering computes the final pixel color $C$ and optionally depth.
5.  Loss is computed and gradients are backpropagated.

**Loss Function**
The primary loss is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered color $C$ and ground truth $C_{gt}$:
$$ L_{rgb} = \| C - C_{gt} \|^2 $$
This is often augmented with regularization terms specific to the variant (e.g., depth loss, semantic loss, or transient sparsity).

**Optimizer**
The Adam optimizer is the standard choice for all variants in this family. Key parameters include the learning rate ($\eta$), weight decay, and learning rate decay schedules (typically step decay or cosine annealing).

## Background
The original NeRF paper  established the baseline training configuration: Adam optimizer with $lr=5 \times 10^{-4}$, 500,000 steps, and stratified sampling. Subsequent works adapted these settings to address specific challenges: depth guidance , semantic stylization , and transient objects .
