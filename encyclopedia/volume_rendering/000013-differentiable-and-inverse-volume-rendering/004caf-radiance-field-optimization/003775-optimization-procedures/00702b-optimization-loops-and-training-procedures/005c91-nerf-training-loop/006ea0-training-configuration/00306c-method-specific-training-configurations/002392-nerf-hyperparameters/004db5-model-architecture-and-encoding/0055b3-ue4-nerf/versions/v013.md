# UE4-NeRF

Also called: UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene

## Overview
UE4-NeRF is a neural rendering system designed for the real-time interactive rendering of large-scale 3D scenes, such as industrial parks and farmlands, by integrating Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) with the Unreal Engine 4 (UE4) rasterization pipeline [S1]. It partitions large environments into sub-NeRFs, representing each block using textured polygonal meshes initialized from regular octahedra, and employs a Level of Detail (LOD) hierarchy to maintain high frame rates at varying observation heights [S1]. Unlike its siblings, which often rely on grid structures or suffer from slow convergence on tilted surfaces, UE4-NeRF uses continuous vertex optimization and multi-resolution hash encoding to achieve real-time performance (up to 43 FPS at 4K) while supporting scene editing and handling semi-transparent objects [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of posed camera images (e.g., from drones) with GPS metadata; a large-scale 3D scene to be partitioned.
*   **Outputs:** A real-time rendered scene within UE4, consisting of textured polygonal meshes and a neural shader that computes color and opacity.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The large scene is divided into $N$ blocks. Each block's training area is slightly larger than the physical scene area to create overlapping borders for smooth transitions [S1].
2.  **Grid and Mesh:**
    *   A $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid is initialized for each block [S1].
    *   Polygonal meshes are created by selecting the center point of each grid cell and its six neighbors (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) [S1].
    *   The mesh geometry is based on regular octahedra with 20 faces (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
    *   Vertex positions are optimized during training [S1].
3.  **Texture Atlas:** Stores feature vectors and opacity information for the mesh vertices [S1].
4.  **Network Weights:**
    *   **Encoder MLP:** 4 layers. Dimensions: $32 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 64$, $64 \times 8$ [S1].
    *   **Decoder MLP:** 3 layers. Dimensions: $17 \times 16$, $16 \times 16$, $16 \times 3$ [S1].
5.  **LOD Hierarchy:** Multiple levels of mesh detail are pre-rendered and stored. Coarser meshes are used for higher observation heights [S1].

**Encoding:**
The method uses multi-resolution hash encoding (from Instant-NGP) for the positional input $p_i$ [S1]. The viewing direction $d_i$ is encoded using Spherical Harmonics (SH) [S1].

**Formulas:**
The ray is defined as $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin and $\mathbf{d} = (\theta, \phi)$ is the viewing direction [S1].
For each ray, the intersection with the polygonal mesh defines the sampling point. The network predicts opacity $\alpha_i$ and a feature vector $\mathbf{M}_i$ from the position $\mathbf{p}_i$, and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ from the feature vector and direction:

$$
E(\text{hash}(\mathbf{p}_i); \theta_E) \rightarrow \mathbf{M}_i, \alpha_i \quad (1)
$$

$$
D(\mathbf{M}_i, \text{SH}(\mathbf{d}_i); \theta_D) \rightarrow \mathbf{c}_i \quad (2)
$$

Where:
*   $E$ is the Encoder MLP with parameters $\theta_E$ [S1].
*   $D$ is the Decoder MLP with parameters $\theta_D$ [S1].
*   $\text{hash}(\cdot)$ is the multi-resolution hash encoding [S1].
*   $\text{SH}(\cdot)$ is the Spherical Harmonics encoding of the direction [S1].
*   $\mathbf{M}_i$ is an 8-dimensional feature vector [S1].
*   $\alpha_i$ is the opacity [S1].
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the final RGB color [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Size:** $128 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   **Octahedron Faces:** 20 total (8 exterior, 12 interior) [S1].
*   **Encoder Layers:** 4 layers, dimensions $[32, 64, 64, 64, 8]$ [S1].
*   **Decoder Layers:** 3 layers, dimensions $[17, 16, 16, 3]$ [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
*   **Hardware:** 1 Nvidia RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Coordinate System:** X-axis East, Y-axis South, Z-axis Up [S1].
*   **Scale:** 100:1 (UE4 units to real-world units) [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Coordinate Transformation:** Apply the UE4 coordinate system (X: East, Y: South, Z: Up) and 100:1 scale conversion to GPS-captured drone imagery [S1].
2.  **Block Partitioning:** Partition the large scene into multiple blocks. Train a coarse model for sub-region segmentation to capture terrain and building outlines. Filter images and use masks to exclude pixels outside the scene area [S1].
3.  **Mesh Initialization:** Initialize a $128 \times 128 \times 128$ grid. Select center points and six neighbors to create polygonal meshes based on regular octahedra [S1].
4.  **Training:**
    *   Implement the Encoder and Decoder networks.
    *   Use multi-resolution hash encoding for acceleration.
    *   Implement computationally intensive portions in CUDA [S1].
    *   Optimize vertex positions continuously during training [S1].
    *   Train for 80,000 epochs per block [S1].
5.  **Shading Mesh Extraction:** Extract coarse polygonal meshes without textures during training. Apply vertex optimization. Encode texture maps (feature vectors and opacity) in network outputs [S1].
6.  **LOD Generation:** Train meshes with varying levels of detail for different observation heights. Coarser meshes are used for higher heights [S1].
7.  **Rendering Integration:**
    *   Implement the Decoder MLP as an HLSL fragment shader in UE4 [S1].
    *   The shader takes feature vectors (from the texture atlas) as input and outputs colors [S1].
    *   Combine pre-rendered polygonal meshes with the UE4 rasterization pipeline [S1].
    *   Use the LOD hierarchy to select the appropriate mesh level based on camera height [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tilted Surfaces:** Mobile-NeRF suffers from slow/unstable convergence on tilted surfaces. UE4-NeRF addresses this by using regular octahedra and continuous vertex optimization [S1].
*   **Transparency:** Mobile-NeRF cannot handle transparent/semi-transparent objects due to opacity binarization. UE4-NeRF stores opacity in texture maps, mitigating this [S1]. However, semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
*   **Pre-rendering Gaps:** Pre-rendering cannot capture rays from all viewing angles, potentially resulting in gaps or holes in the final scene [S1].
*   **Exposure Matching:** Consistent exposure matching with original images is difficult in UE4, causing evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) to underestimate actual reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Memory:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead, which increases with accuracy [S1].
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
# UE4-NeRF Implementation Steps

def ue4_nerf_pipeline(image_data, gps_metadata, scene_bounds):
    """
    Main pipeline for UE4-NeRF.
    """
    # 1. Coordinate Transformation
    # Apply UE4 coordinate system (X: East, Y: South, Z: Up) and 100:1 scale
    transformed_images = []
    for img, gps in zip(image_data, gps_metadata):
        # Convert GPS to UE4 coordinates and apply scale
        ue4_pos = convert_gps_to_ue4(gps, scale_factor=100.0, 
                                     x_axis='east', y_axis='south', z_axis='up')
        transformed_images.append((img, ue4_pos))

    # 2. Block Partitioning
    # Partition scene into blocks with overlapping borders
    blocks = partition_scene(scene_bounds, overlap=True)
    
    trained_blocks = []
    for block_id, block in enumerate(blocks):
        # Filter images associated with this sub-region
        block_images = filter_images_for_block(transformed_images, block)
        
        # Train coarse model for segmentation (optional, for boundary handling)
        coarse_model = train_coarse_segmentation(block_images)
        
        # 3. Mesh Initialization
        # Initialize 128x128x128 grid
        grid_size = 128
        grid = initialize_grid(grid_size)
        
        # Create polygonal meshes from grid centers and neighbors
        # Using regular octahedra (20 faces: 8 exterior, 12 interior)
        mesh = create_octahedral_mesh(grid, num_faces=20)
        
        # 4. Network Definition
        # Encoder: 4-layer MLP (32->64->64->64->8)
        encoder = MLP(layers=[32, 64, 64, 64, 8])
        # Decoder: 3-layer MLP (17->16->16->3)
        # Input: 8 (features) + 9 (SH coefficients for direction) = 17
        decoder = MLP(layers=[17, 16, 16, 3])
        
        # 5. Training
        # Use multi-resolution hash encoding for position
        hash_encoder = MultiResolutionHashEncoder()
        # Use Spherical Harmonics for direction
        sh_encoder = SphericalHarmonicsEncoder(order=3) # 9 coefficients for order 3
        
        # Optimize vertex positions and network weights
        # Train for 80,000 epochs on 1x RTX 3090
        for epoch in range(80000):
            for img, pos in block_images:
                # Sample rays
                rays = sample_rays_from_image(img, pos)
                
                # For each ray, find intersection with mesh
                for ray in rays:
                    intersection = ray_intersect_mesh(ray, mesh)
                    if intersection is not None:
                        p_i = intersection.position
                        d_i = ray.direction
                        
                        # Encode inputs
                        p_encoded = hash_encoder.encode(p_i)
                        d_encoded = sh_encoder.encode(d_i)
                        
                        # Forward pass
                        # E(hash(p_i); theta_E) -> M_i, alpha_i
                        features, alpha = encoder(p_encoded)
                        
                        # D(M_i, SH(d_i); theta_D) -> c_i
                        # Concatenate features (8) and SH (9) -> 17
                        input_to_decoder = concat(features, d_encoded)
                        color = decoder(input_to_decoder)
                        
                        # Compute loss (e.g., MSE against ground truth color)
                        loss = compute_loss(color, img.pixel_color)
                        update_weights(encoder, decoder, mesh.vertices, loss)
        
        # 6. LOD Generation
        # Extract meshes at different levels of detail
        lod_meshes = generate_lod(mesh, levels=3)
        
        # 7. Export for UE4
        # Encode texture maps (features and opacity)
        texture_atlas = encode_texture_atlas(mesh, encoder, decoder)
        
        # Generate HLSL shader code for the decoder
        shader_code = generate_hlsl_shader(decoder)
        
        trained_blocks.append({
            'id': block_id,
            'meshes': lod_meshes,
            'texture_atlas': texture_atlas,
            'shader': shader_code
        })
        
    return trained_blocks

def render_ue4_scene(trained_blocks, camera_pose, resolution):
    """
    Real-time rendering in UE4 using the trained blocks.
    """
    # Determine which blocks are visible based on camera pose
    visible_blocks = get_visible_blocks(trained_blocks, camera_pose)
    
    # Determine LOD level based on camera height
    lod_level = get_lod_level(camera_pose.height)
    
    for block in visible_blocks:
        # Select appropriate LOD mesh
        mesh = block['meshes'][lod_level]
        
        # Bind texture atlas
        bind_texture(block['texture_atlas'])
        
        # Execute HLSL shader for each pixel
        # The shader takes feature vectors from the texture and outputs color
        render_rasterization_pipeline(mesh, block['shader'])
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than prior methods. It is 1000× faster than Mobile-NeRF [S1]. Training time is approximately 40 minutes per block on a single RTX 3090 GPU [S1]. For a full scene with $n$ blocks, the total training time is $1 \text{ hour} + (40 \text{ minutes} \times n/4)$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Rendering large scenes (square kilometers) incurs significant memory overhead. Increasing rendering accuracy further amplifies memory consumption [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves real-time rendering at 4K resolution with up to 43 FPS [S1]. On a full scene at 2K resolution, it achieves 50 FPS; on individual blocks at 2K, it achieves $\ge 70$ FPS [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Real-time interactive rendering of large-scale scenes [S1].
    *   Handles tilted surfaces better than Mobile-NeRF due to octahedral meshes and vertex optimization [S1].
    *   Supports semi-transparent objects (water, glass, vegetation) by storing opacity in texture maps, unlike Mobile-NeRF's binarization [S1].
    *   Enables scene editing in UE4, allowing import of traditional 3D models (.obj, .fbx) with automatic occlusion handling [S1].
    *   Avoids blurriness on natural scenes and renders small objects (e.g., power lines) accurately compared to Mip-NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires CUDA and NVIDIA GPUs [S1].
    *   Semi-transparent objects (e.g., vegetation) increase computational overhead, reducing frame rates [S1].
    *   Pre-rendering may result in gaps or holes if rays from all viewing angles are not captured [S1].
    *   Evaluation metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) may underestimate quality due to exposure matching difficulties in UE4 [S1].
*   **When to use:** When real-time interactive rendering of large-scale, complex scenes (e.g., farmland, construction sites) is required, especially if scene editing or handling of semi-transparent objects is needed.
*   **When not to use:** When hardware is limited to non-NVIDIA GPUs, or when absolute photometric accuracy is critical and exposure matching in UE4 is a blocker.

## Background
UE4-NeRF was introduced in the paper "UE4-NeRF: Neural Radiance Field for Real-Time Rendering of Large-Scale Scene" [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of NeRF in real-time rendering and large-scale scene representation. Prior methods like Mobile-NeRF and Mega-NeRF suffered from slow training times, inability to handle tilted surfaces or transparent objects, and limited rendering resolutions. UE4-NeRF combines the representational power of NeRF with the rasterization pipeline and development tools of Unreal Engine 4, enabling real-time performance and scene editing. It has been evaluated on drone-captured large-scale scenes (Farmland, Construction site, Industrial parks) and the Mega-NeRF public dataset [S1].
