# Adaptive Differentiable Grids for Cryo-ET

Also called: Adaptive density grid optimization, Adaptive density grids based on an octree structure, Adaptive differentiable grids, Coordinate-based representation with adaptive octree

## Overview
Adaptive Differentiable Grids (ADG) is a learning-based representation for cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) reconstruction that replaces the fully connected multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) of traditional neural fields with an adaptive octree structure where each node stores a learnable 3D density grid [S1]. It solves the challenge of reconstructing high-noise, low-signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) cryo-ET data by combining differentiable image formation with regularization terms (Total Variation, boundary consistency, and cross-nodes non-local constraints) to separate noise from signal while preserving fine features [S1]. Unlike coordinate-based neural networks that suffer from long training times and slow rendering, ADG uses differentiable trilinear interpolation for faster optimization and better feature recovery, while the adaptive octree structure accelerates computations by prioritizing regions with complex features [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Tilt-series projection images $b$ (intensity measured by detectors).
    *   Projection geometry (Radon transform operations $A$).
    *   Region of Interest (ROI) bounding box.
    *   Binary mask $M$ excluding rays intersecting fiducial markers or padded gray regions [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   A continuous 3D density field $\hat{x}$ reconstructed within the ROI.
    *   An optimized octree structure defining the spatial distribution of density grids [S1].

### Data Structures

**1. Octree Structure**
*   **Hierarchy:** A hierarchical tree structure where each node represents a 3D sub-volume.
*   **State:** Nodes are classified as `enabled` (active, contains density grid) or `disabled` (empty or outside ROI) [S1].
*   **Update Mechanism:** Nodes can be divided into 8 child octants, merged, or kept unchanged based on a mixed-integer program that minimizes an update loss while keeping the total node count below a fixed limit [S1].

**2. Density Grid ($\mathbf{V}_\sigma$)**
*   **Layout:** Stored within each `enabled` octree node.
*   **Dimensions:** A 3D tensor of size $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$. The sources specify that $N_x = N_y = N_z$ for simplicity, though the general case allows different dimensions [S1].
*   **Content:** Stores learnable volume density values $\sigma$.
*   **Initialization:** Optimized from captured projections during training [S1].

**3. Query Point**
*   **Definition:** A 3D coordinate $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ within the ROI [S1].

### Formulas

**1. Image Formation Model**
The projection intensity $b_i$ for ray $i$ is modeled as:
$$ b_i = A_i x_i + n_i $$
where $A_i$ is the Radon transform along ray $i$, $x_i$ is the vector of 3D density values sampled along the ray, and $n_i$ is noise (assumed Gaussian after preprocessing) [S1].

**2. Density Query via Trilinear Interpolation**
For a query point $\mathbf{p}$, the density $f_\phi(\mathbf{p})$ is obtained by trilinear interpolation from the 8 vertices of the density grid $\mathbf{V}_\sigma$ contained in the octree node covering $\mathbf{p}$:
$$ f_\phi(\mathbf{p}) = \text{trilinear\_interp}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{V}_\sigma) $$
This operation is differentiable, allowing gradients to flow back to the grid values [S1].

**3. Data-Fidelity Loss**
$$ L_{data}(\phi) = \frac{1}{2N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \| M (A f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_i) - b_i) \|_2^2 $$
where $N$ is the batch size (number of sampling points), $M$ is the binary mask excluding fiducial markers, $A$ is the projection operator, $f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_i)$ is the interpolated density, and $b_i$ is the measured intensity [S1].

**4. Total Variation (TV) Loss**
$$ L_{tv} = \sum \| \nabla f_\phi(\mathbf{p}) \|_2 $$
Used as a spatial regularizer to smooth the reconstruction and reduce noise [S1].

**5. Cross-Nodes Non-Local Constraint (CNLC) Loss**
To enforce similarity between density vectors in different nodes $m$ and $n$:
$$ L_{cnlc} = \sum \| \text{softmax}(f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_i^m)^T f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_j^n)) f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_j^n) - f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_i^m) \|_2^2 $$
where $f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_i^m)$ and $f_\phi(\mathbf{p}_j^n)$ are densities sampled from nodes $m$ and $n$ respectively. The softmax operation estimates similarities between density vectors [S1].

**6. Boundary Consistency Loss ($L_{bc}$)**
Minimizes density differences between neighboring grids at their shared boundaries to prevent discontinuity artifacts caused by independent node optimization [S1].

**7. Total Loss**
$$ L_{total} = L_{data} + k_{tv} L_{tv} + k_{cnlc} L_{cnlc} + k_{bc} L_{bc} $$
where $k_{tv}, k_{cnlc}, k_{bc}$ are weighting hyperparameters [S1].

**8. Ray Sampling Strategy**
For each octree node $oc$ crossed by a ray, the number of samples $N_{oc}$ is:
$$ N_{oc} = N_{max} \frac{loc}{doc} $$
where $N_{max}$ is the maximum samples per node, $loc$ is the ray length inside node $oc$, and $doc$ is the diagonal length of node $oc$. Samples are selected using stratified random sampling [S1].

### Parameters
*   $N_x, N_y, N_z$: Grid dimensions per node. Typically $N_x = N_y = N_z$ [S1].
*   $N_{max}$: Hyperparameter for maximum samples per node in ray sampling [S1].
*   $k_{tv}, k_{cnlc}, k_{bc}$: Loss weighting parameters [S1].
*   Maximum Node Limit: Fixed limit for the total number of octree nodes [S1].

import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

class AdaptiveDifferentiableGrid:
    def __init__(self, roi_bounds, initial_grid_size=16, max_nodes=10000):
        """
        Initialize the ADG representation.
        :param roi_bounds: Tuple (min_x, min_y, min_z, max_x, max_y, max_z) defining the ROI.
        :param initial_grid_size: Nx = Ny = Nz for the initial density grids.
        :param max_nodes: Maximum number of octree nodes allowed.
        """
        self.roi_bounds = roi_bounds
        self.initial_grid_size = initial_grid_size
        self.max_nodes = max_nodes
        self.octree = Octree(root_bounds=roi_bounds, grid_size=initial_grid_size)
        self.optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(self.octree.parameters(), lr=1e-3)

    def query_density(self, points):
        """
        Query density at 3D points using trilinear interpolation.
        :param points: Tensor of shape (N, 3) in world coordinates.
        :return: Tensor of shape (N,) containing densities.
        """
        densities = []
        for p in points:
            node = self.octree.find_node(p)
            if node is None or not node.enabled:
                densities.append(0.0) # Outside ROI or disabled node
            else:
                # Normalize point to local grid coordinates [0, 1]
                local_p = self.octree.world_to_local(p, node.bounds)
                # Trilinear interpolation from the node's density grid
                # Assuming node.grid is a tensor of shape (G, G, G)
                grid = node.grid.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0) # Add batch/channel dims for grid_sample
                # grid_sample expects normalized coordinates in [-1, 1]
                norm_p = 2.0 * local_p - 1.0
                norm_p = norm_p.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0) # (1, 1, 1, 3)
                # grid_sample input shape: (N, C, H, W, D) -> (1, 1, G, G, G)
                # output shape: (1, 1, 1, 1)
                val = F.grid_sample(grid, norm_p, mode='trilinear', padding_mode='zeros')
                densities.append(val.squeeze())
        return torch.stack(densities)

    def compute_loss(self, rays, projections, mask):
        """
        Compute the total loss for a batch of rays.
        :param rays: List of ray objects, each containing start, direction, and intersection with octree.
        :param projections: Tensor of measured intensities b.
        :param mask: Binary mask M.

            # Ray sampling: stratified random sampling based on node intersections
            samples = ray.sample_stratified(self.octree, max_samples_per_node=10) # N_max placeholder
            if len(samples) == 0:
                continue
            
            # Query densities at sample points
            points = torch.stack([s.point for s in samples])
            densities = self.query_density(points)
            
            # Accumulate for ray integration (simplified: sum of densities * step size)
            # In practice, A_i x_i involves integrating along the ray
            # Here we assume densities are already weighted by step size or integrated
            all_densities.append(densities)
            all_ray_indices.extend([i] * len(samples))
            
        if not all_densities:
            return torch.tensor(0.0)
            
        all_densities = torch.cat(all_densities)
        
        # Data Fidelity Loss
        # Reconstruct projected values from densities (simplified Radon approximation)
        # In full implementation, A is the differentiable Radon transform
        reconstructed_projections = self.radon_transform(all_densities, rays) 
        data_loss = 0.5 * torch.mean((mask * (reconstructed_projections - projections))**2)
        
        # Regularization Losses
        tv_loss = self.compute_tv_loss(all_densities)
        cnlc_loss = self.compute_cnlc_loss()
        bc_loss = self.compute_boundary_consistency_loss()
        
        # Total Loss
        total_loss = data_loss + 0.1 * tv_loss + 0.01 * cnlc_loss + 0.01 * bc_loss
        return total_loss

    def update_octree(self, projections, mask):
        """
        Update octree structure (divide/merge) based on density variance.
        """
        # 1. Sample densities in each enabled node
        node_variances = {}
        for node in self.octree.enabled_nodes:
            samples = self.sample_node_uniformly(node)
            densities = self.query_density(samples)
            node_variances[node.id] = torch.var(densities)
            
        # 2. Determine updates (divide, merge, keep)
        # Solve mixed-integer program to keep total nodes < max_nodes
        updates = self.solve_mip(node_variances, self.max_nodes)
        
        # 3. Apply updates
        for node_id, action in updates.items():
            if action == 'divide':
                self.octree.divide(node_id)
            elif action == 'merge':
                self.octree.merge(node_id)

    def train_step(self, rays, projections, mask):
        """
        One training iteration.
        """
        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss = self.compute_loss(rays, projections, mask)
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()
        return loss.item()

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **High Noise:** Standard neural fields learn noise as signal or oversmooth. ADG handles this via regularization (TV, CNLC, BC) [S1].
*   **Discontinuity Artifacts:** Independent optimization of octree nodes causes discontinuities at grid edges. Mitigated by $L_{bc}$ [S1].
*   **Fiducial Markers:** Rays intersecting fiducial markers interfere with reconstruction. Mitigated by binary mask $M$ in $L_{data}$ [S1].
*   **Padded Regions:** Gray regions padded by alignment tools (e.g., IMOD) at borders interfere. Excluded by mask $M$ [S1].
*   **Noise Model:** Raw cryo-ET data follows Poisson noise, but preprocessing (motion/intensity correction) allows approximation as Gaussian noise for simplicity [S1].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** Faster than MLP-based neural fields because querying density uses trilinear interpolation ($O(1)$ per point) instead of evaluating a deep network. However, octree updates (mixed-integer programming) add overhead during structure adaptation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Lower than classical tomographic reconstruction techniques as it does not store the entire 3D volume during optimization, only the active octree nodes and their density grids. Memory usage scales with the number of enabled nodes and grid size $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ [S1].
*   **Performance:** Demonstrated on EMPIAR datasets (10643, 10453, 11462) for virus and cell reconstruction. Outperforms SART+TV, Kniesel et al., and NeAT in handling high noise and preserving details [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Faster optimization and rendering than MLP-based fields.
    *   Better detail preservation in complex regions and denoising in uniform regions via adaptive octree.
    *   Flexible integration of regularization priors (TV, CNLC, BC) [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires careful tuning of loss weights ($k_{tv}, k_{cnlc}, k_{bc}$) and hyperparameters ($N_{max}$, grid size).
    *   Complexity of octree update logic (mixed-integer programming) [S1].
    *   Assumes Gaussian noise after preprocessing, which may not capture all noise characteristics [S1].

## Background

*   **Origin:** Proposed by Wang et al. in "Adaptive differentiable grids for cryo-electron tomography reconstruction and denoising" (2023) [S1].
*   **Context:** Addresses limitations of traditional tomographic reconstruction (WFBP, SART) and neural fields (NeAT, Kniesel et al.) in cryo-ET, specifically regarding high noise levels, missing-wedge artifacts, and computational efficiency [S1].
*   **Development:** Builds on recent advances in neural representations (e.g., NeRF, Octree-based fields like ACORN, NeAT) by replacing MLPs with differentiable 3D density grids within an adaptive octree structure [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to cryo-ET reconstruction and denoising of biological samples (viruses, cells) [S1].
