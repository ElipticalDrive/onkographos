# Staged Training

Also called: Staged Pose-NeRF Refinement

## Overview
Staged Training is a two-phase optimization protocol for joint camera pose and neural radiance field (NeRF) reconstruction from sparse, noisy initial poses. It solves the problem of local minima in simultaneous pose and network optimization by decoupling the refinement of camera extrinsics from the high-fidelity learning of scene geometry. It differs from joint training approaches by freezing poses after an initial coarse registration, which prevents the fine network from learning blurry surfaces due to pose exploration [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ images $\{I_i\}$, initial camera poses $\{P_i\}$ (rotation $R_i$, translation $t_i$) with potential noise, and a coarse NeRF network $F_{\theta_c}$ and fine NeRF network $F_{\theta_f}$.
*   **Outputs:** Refined camera poses $\{\hat{P}_i\}$ and optimized radiance fields $F_{\theta_c}, F_{\theta_f}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Pose:** $P_i = [R_i | t_i]$, where $R_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is a rotation matrix and $t_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a translation vector.
*   **NeRF Network:** $F_{\theta}(x, d) \to (\sigma, c)$, mapping 3D position $x$ and viewing direction $d$ to density $\sigma$ and color $c$.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Stage 1: Pose Refinement with Coarse Network**
    *   Initialize the coarse network $F_{\theta_c}$ with random weights or a simple initialization.
    *   Keep the fine network $F_{\theta_f}$ untrained or frozen with random weights.
    *   Optimize the parameters of $F_{\theta_c}$ ($\theta_c$) and the camera poses $P_i$ simultaneously by minimizing the photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ between rendered and input images.
    *   This stage aligns the camera poses to the coarse geometry learned by $F_{\theta_c}$.

2.  **Stage 2: Joint Refinement with Frozen Poses**
    *   Freeze the camera poses $P_i$ to the estimates obtained from Stage 1.
    *   Optimize the parameters of both the coarse network $F_{\theta_c}$ ($\theta_c$) and the fine network $F_{\theta_f}$ ($\theta_f$) simultaneously.
    *   The fine network learns high-frequency details and sharp geometry, benefiting from the now-registered poses and the pre-trained coarse network which provides a stable low-frequency baseline.

**Formulas:**
*   Photometric Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{photo} = \sum_{i} \| I_i - \hat{I}_i \|^2$, where $\hat{I}_i$ is the rendered image for pose $P_i$.
*   Total Loss in Stage 1: $\mathcal{L}_{total}^{(1)} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg}(\theta_c)$, where $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ is a regularization term on network weights [G].
*   Total Loss in Stage 2: $\mathcal{L}_{total}^{(2)} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda_{reg} (\mathcal{L}_{reg}(\theta_c) + \mathcal{L}_{reg}(\theta_f))$ [G].

**Parameters:**
*   Learning rates for $\theta_c$, $\theta_f$, and $P_i$ are typically decayed over time [G].
*   Regularization weight $\lambda_{reg}$ is not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Pose Quality:** If initial poses are too noisy, Stage 1 may fail to converge to a useful alignment. The sources note performance on DTU with 15% initial additive Gaussian noise [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Joint training of poses and both networks (without staging) can lead to blurry surfaces because the fine network explores the pose space while learning geometry [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def staged_training(images, initial_poses, coarse_network, fine_network, num_epochs_stage1, num_epochs_stage2):
    """
    Performs staged training for NeRF with pose refinement.
    
    Args:
        images: List of input images.
        initial_poses: List of initial camera poses (R, t).
        coarse_network: Neural network F_theta_c.
        fine_network: Neural network F_theta_f.
        num_epochs_stage1: Number of epochs for Stage 1.
        num_epochs_stage2: Number of epochs for Stage 2.
        
    Returns:
        refined_poses: List of optimized camera poses.
        coarse_network: Optimized coarse network.
        fine_network: Optimized fine network.
    """
    # Initialize networks
    theta_c = coarse_network.parameters()
    theta_f = fine_network.parameters()
    
    # Initialize poses as trainable variables
    poses = [torch.tensor(p, requires_grad=True) for p in initial_poses]
    
    # Stage 1: Refine poses and coarse network
    optimizer_stage1 = torch.optim.Adam([theta_c] + poses, lr=1e-3)
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage1):
        optimizer_stage1.zero_grad()
        loss = 0.0
        for i, image in enumerate(images):
            # Render image using coarse network and current pose
            rendered_image = render_coarse(coarse_network, poses[i])
            # Compute photometric loss
            loss += torch.mean((rendered_image - image) ** 2)
        
        loss.backward()
        optimizer_stage1.step()
        
    # Stage 2: Freeze poses, train both networks
    # Freeze poses
    for p in poses:
        p.requires_grad = False
        
    optimizer_stage2 = torch.optim.Adam([theta_c, theta_f], lr=1e-4)
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs_stage2):
        optimizer_stage2.zero_grad()
        loss = 0.0
        for i, image in enumerate(images):
            # Render image using coarse and fine networks and frozen pose
            rendered_image = render_fine(coarse_network, fine_network, poses[i])
            # Compute photometric loss
            loss += torch.mean((rendered_image - image) ** 2)
            
        loss.backward()
        optimizer_stage2.step()
        
    return poses, coarse_network, fine_network
```

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Training for Deformable NeRF](21743-two-stage-training-for-deformable-nerf.md)
A training protocol for deformable neural radiance fields that reduces GPU memory consumption and accelerates convergence by decoupling the initialization of the tetrahedral mesh from the high-resolution refinement.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two sequential optimization phases. Stage 1 is generally faster as it only optimizes poses and a coarse network. Stage 2 optimizes two networks but with frozen poses, which can be more stable.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to standard NeRF training, but avoids the memory overhead of storing gradients for poses during the fine network training phase in Stage 2.
*   **Measured Performance:** On the DTU dataset with 3 views and 15% initial additive Gaussian noise, Staged Training achieves a PSNR of 17.58 (18.62 masked), SSIM of 0.71 (0.82 masked), LPIPS of 0.26 (0.13 masked), and Depth Error (DE) of 1.81 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents blurry surfaces by decoupling pose and geometry optimization. Improves PSNR and SSIM compared to joint training of poses with both coarse and fine MLPs [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the transition between stages. Performance depends heavily on the quality of the initial poses.
*   **When to use:** When initial camera poses are sparse and noisy, and joint optimization leads to poor geometry.
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality initial poses are available, or when computational resources are extremely limited for two-stage optimization.

## Background
Staged Training was introduced in the context of "SPARF: Neural Radiance Fields from Sparse and Noisy Poses" [S1]. It addresses the challenges of reconstructing NeRFs from sparse and noisy camera poses, where traditional joint optimization methods struggle with local minima and blurry reconstructions. The method builds on the idea of coarse-to-fine optimization but explicitly stages the pose refinement process.
