# Network Architectures
Also called: NeRF Network Architectures

## Overview
This article details specific modifications to the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) structures used to learn continuous volumetric scene representations $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$. While the standard NeRF architecture [S1] employs two MLPs—a Position MLP ($\Phi_{pos}$) and a Shading MLP ($\Phi_{shade}$)—these variants alter layer depths, input dimensions, activation functions, and output channels to address specific challenges such as dynamic scenes, transparency, mobile efficiency, and semantic consistency. These architectures build on [Network Structure Modifications](20497-network-structure-modifications.md) by defining the precise computational graphs for specialized radiance field learning.

## How it works

### Standard NeRF Baseline
The baseline architecture [S1] serves as the reference point for most modifications. It employs two MLPs:
1.  **Position MLP ($\Phi_{pos}$)**: Takes spatial position $\mathbf{x}$ and its positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ to produce a feature vector $\mathbf{h}$.
2.  **Shading MLP ($\Phi_{shade}$)**: Takes $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ with its encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to predict volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$.

### Dynamic Scene Network Architecture
This architecture [S5] divides dynamic scenes into static and dynamic components, modeled by two separate MLPs to handle time-variant scenes and motion.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Dynamic Network ($F_{\Theta}^{dy}$)**:
    *   Inputs: Positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and time $\gamma(t)$.
    *   Outputs: Color $\mathbf{c}_t$, density $\sigma_t$, scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$, and disocclusion weights $\mathcal{W}_t$.
*   **Static Network ($F_{\Theta}^{st}$)**:
    *   Inputs: Positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$.
    *   Outputs: Color $\mathbf{c}$, density $\sigma$, and blending weight $v$.

**Formulas:**
The networks are defined as [S5]:
$$
(\mathbf{c}_t, \sigma_t, \mathcal{F}_t, \mathcal{W}_t) = F_{\Theta}^{dy}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d}), \gamma(t)) \quad (22)
$$
$$
(\mathbf{c}, \sigma, v) = F_{\Theta}^{st}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d})) \quad (23)
$$

**Steps:**
1.  Perform equidistant sampling to obtain spatial points along rays, rather than hierarchical sampling [S5].
2.  Use the predicted scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$ to establish inter-frame relationships for time consistency [S5].
3.  Jointly optimize camera parameters ($\Delta f, \Delta P$) along with the MLPs $F_{\Theta}^{dy}$ and $F_{\Theta}^{st}$ [S5].

### NeRF Network Architecture with Blend Weight Field
This architecture [S6] extends the standard NeRF to model dynamic human bodies by introducing a blend weight field.

**Data Structures and Parameters:**
*   **Radiance Field Networks ($F_\sigma, F_c$)**: Follow the original NeRF architecture [S6].
*   **Sampling**: Single-level NeRF, sampling 64 points along each camera ray [S6].
*   **Blend Weight Field Network ($F_{\Delta w}$)**:
    *   Structure: Almost identical to $F_\sigma$.
    *   Output Layer: Has 24 channels.
    *   Activation: Applies $\exp(\cdot)$ to the output [S6].
*   **Embeddings**: Appearance code $\mathbf{l}_i$ and blend weight field code $\boldsymbol{\psi}_i$ both have dimensions of 128 [S6].

**Steps:**
1.  Train parameters of $F_\sigma, F_c, F_{\Delta w}, \{\mathbf{l}_i\}$, and $\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_i\}$ jointly over the input video [S6].
2.  Learn neural blend weight fields under novel human poses using the transformed mesh vertices [S6].

### MobileNeRF Architectures (A, F, H)
MobileNeRF [S1] modifies the standard architecture to exploit the polygon rasterization pipeline for mobile efficiency.

**Parameters:**
*   **MLPs A and F**: Use the MLP design from NeRF, but hidden layer sizes are increased from 256 to 384 [S1].
*   **MLP H**: Uses the small MLP from SNeRG, consisting of two hidden layers, each with 16 neurons [S1].
*   **Inference Constraint**: MLPs A and F are not used during inference, allowing for larger training-time computation [S1].

### OccNet (Single-Level NeRF)
OccNet [S4, unofficial] represents a single-level version of NeRF that does not leverage positional encodings.

**Performance Metrics:**
*   **2D Images**: 13.90 PSNR / 0.437 SSIM [S4, unofficial].
*   **Radiance Field**: 20.60 PSNR / 0.849 SSIM [S4, unofficial].
*   **Training Time**: 02:17 (2D) / 100:23 (Radiance Field) [S4, unofficial].
*   **Model Size**: 0.38 (2D) / 0.43 (Radiance Field) [S4, unofficial].

### Residual-NeRF-S
Residual-NeRF-S [S3] is an ablation variant of Residual-NeRF where background and residual NeRFs are merged by simply adding their outputs before activation, instead of using a Mixnet.

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation for Residual-NeRF-S is [S3]:
$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_{bg} + \sigma_{res})_i \delta_i)) (c_{bg+res})_i
$$
where:
$$
c_{bg+res} = S(c'_{bg} + c'_{res})
$$
$$
(c'_{bg}, \sigma_{bg}) = \Phi_{bg}(h(\mathbf{x}, y, z, \theta, \phi))
$$
$$
(c'_{res}, \sigma_{res}) = \Phi_{res}(h(\mathbf{x}, y, z, \theta, \phi))
$$
$$
S(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\mathbf{x}}}
$$
Here, $S(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid activation function applied element-wise to the sum of the background and residual color predictions before the final volume rendering integration [S3].

## Subtopics

### [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md)
MIMO-DONeRF is a method that applies the Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) neural rendering concept to the DONeRF architecture. It modifies the shading network to accept multiple spatial inputs and view directions simultaneously, producing multiple color and density outputs per forward pass. This approach improves inference speed compared to increasing the number of samples in standard DONeRF, while maintaining or improving image quality by allowing the network to accumulate neighbor information through grouped samples. It differs from standard DONeRF by processing $N_p$ samples in parallel rather than sequentially, and from MIMO-NeRF by being specifically adapted to the two-network structure (depth oracle and shading) of DONeRF.

### [Point-NeRF Adaptation for Object Manipulation](16395-point-nerf-adaptation-for-object-manipulation.md)
This method adapts the Point-NeRF architecture to enable 3D scene geometry decomposition and manipulation from 2D images. It solves the problem of editing specific objects within a complex scene by replacing the standard Multi-View Stereo (MVS) point cloud generation with ground-truth dense data during training and using a diffusion model to infer object codes for manipulation. It differs from standard Point-NeRF by integrating a DM-NeRF module to map spatial locations to object codes and modifying the rendering pipeline to support point transformation.

### [Sparse Latent Space for 4D Novel View Synthesis (SLS4D)](18112-sparse-latent-space-for-4d-novel-view-synthesis-sls4d.md)
SLS4D is a method for 4D novel-view synthesis that represents dynamic radiance fields using a learnable sparse latent space, reducing network parameters compared to dense grid discretizations while maintaining high-frequency detail via attention-based interpolation. It differs from dense grid methods (like TiNeuVox) by avoiding the $O(N^3TF)$ space complexity of 4D grids, and from per-image latent code methods by offering better generalization across time through continuous temporal interpolation.

### [Neural Feature Fusion Fields (N3F)](18114-neural-feature-fusion-fields-n3f.md)
Neural Feature Fusion Fields (N3F) is a method that distills dense 2D image features from a pre-trained teacher network into a 3D neural field (student) via differentiable volumetric rendering, enabling 3D-aware feature representation. Unlike Semantic NeRF or Panoptic NeRF which fuse discrete semantic labels, N3F fuses continuous dense feature vectors, allowing for open-world applications without manual labels. It differs from standard feature distillation because the student operates in 3D world space (ensuring spatial consistency) while the teacher operates in 2D image space.

### [CLNeRF Architecture with Embeddings](18159-clnerf-architecture-with-embeddings.md)
CLNeRF is a model architecture modification for NeRF that enables continual learning by incorporating scene changes (appearance and geometry) over time within a single model, preventing model size growth as new scans are added. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like MIMO-DONeRF and SLS4D by using explicit, trainable per-scan embedding vectors rather than latent space compression or multi-instance networks, allowing for efficient updates and low overhead for generative replay.

### [Mutual Information Gradient Shaping](18669-mutual-information-gradient-shaping.md)
Mutual Information Gradient Shaping (MIGS), implemented as JacobiNeRF, is a training regularization technique that shapes the tangent space of a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to encode semantic correlations between scene entities. Unlike first-order approaches that distill 2D features into 3D radiance channels or treat labels as separate output channels, MIGS aligns the Jacobians of correlated entities to maximize their mutual information under random weight perturbations, enabling efficient sparse label propagation and semantic consensus.

### [Multi-Level Neural Scene Graph](19889-multi-level-neural-scene-graph.md)
Multi-Level Neural Scene Graph (NSG) is a scene representation that decomposes an environment into a graph of static, dynamic, and temporal nodes to model urban environments with moving objects and appearance variations. It distinguishes itself from sibling architectures by explicitly separating the radiance field into a static component $\phi$ and a dynamic component $\psi$, conditioned on sequence-level and object-level latent vectors respectively, enabling the disentanglement of scene-dependent appearance from object texture.

### [NAN (Noise-Aware NeRFs)](20279-nan-noise-aware-nerfs.md)
NAN is a burst-denoising method that extends the IBRNet architecture to handle degraded noisy images, such as those captured in low-light conditions, by modifying feature extraction, aggregation, and color blending to be noise-aware. Unlike standard NeRFs that synthesize novel views, NAN fixes an existing view image by leveraging multiple noisy inputs of the same scene. It distinguishes itself from its sibling [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md) and [Point-NeRF Adaptation for Object Manipulation](16395-point-nerf-adaptation-for-object-manipulation.md) by explicitly modeling spatial noise statistics through 3D blending kernels and cross-view variance features, rather than relying solely on single-pixel cross-view consistency.

### [Multiplicative Interaction Module](21736-multiplicative-interaction-module.md)
The Multiplicative Interaction Module (MIM) is a neural component that generates conditional inputs for a NeRF by computing multiplicative interactions between expression, identity, and optional latent codes to improve facial disentanglement and rendering quality. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md) or [Point-NeRF Adaptation for Object Manipulation](16395-point-nerf-adaptation-for-object-manipulation.md) by explicitly modeling higher-order (second and third) multiplicative dependencies between semantic codes rather than relying on linear concatenation or simple additive fusion.

### [Part-Wise NARF (NARFP)](23078-part-wise-narf-narfp.md)
Part-Wise NARF (NARFP) is a neural articulated radiance field method that models an articulated object by training a separate Ray-Traced NeRF (RT-NeRF) for each rigid part, combining their outputs to estimate global density and color. It differs from holistic approaches by explicitly computing rigid transformations via forward kinematics, thereby avoiding implicit transformation issues and part dependency problems inherent in other methods.

### Pyramid NeRF
Pyramid NeRF accomplishes rapid NeRF training and inference through the application of an image pyramid and a strategy based on frequency optimization [S2]. It utilizes a 'low-frequency-first, high-frequency-second' approach, substituting large MLPs with thousands of smaller ones via progressive segmentation [S2]. It introduces a sampling approach that is aware of depth to enhance rendering quality and hasten training convergence [S2]. It enhances training speed by 15x and inference speed by 805x relative to the original NeRF [S2].

### Residual-NeRF-S
Residual-NeRF-S is an ablation variant of Residual-NeRF where background and residual NeRFs are merged by simply adding their outputs before activation, instead of using a Mixnet [S3]. Residual-NeRF-S produces worse depth and view reconstruction compared to Residual-NeRF with Mixnet, especially for partially opaque objects [S3].

### OccNet
OccNet is a single-level version of NeRF that does not leverage positional encodings [S4, unofficial]. It achieves 13.90 PSNR / 0.437 SSIM on 2D Images and 20.60 PSNR / 0.849 SSIM on Radiance Fields, with training times of 02:17 and 100:23 respectively, and model sizes of 0.38 and 0.43 [S4, unofficial].

### Dynamic Scene Network Architecture
This architecture divides dynamic scenes into static and dynamic parts modeled by two separate MLPs [S5]. It uses equidistant sampling to obtain spatial points along rays, instead of hierarchical sampling, and uses predicted scene flow to establish inter-frame relationship for time consistency [S5]. The Dynamic Network $F_{\Theta}^{dy}$ takes positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and time $\gamma(t)$ as inputs, outputting color $\mathbf{c}_t$, density $\sigma_t$, scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$, and disocclusion weights $\mathcal{W}_t$. The Static Network $F_{\Theta}^{st}$ takes positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ as inputs, outputting color $\mathbf{c}$, density $\sigma$, and blending weight $v$ [S5].

### NeRF Network Architecture with Blend Weight Field
This is the network architecture for the radiance field ($F_\sigma$, $F_c$) and blend weight field ($F_{\Delta w}$), following the original NeRF but with specific modifications for blend weights [S6]. It uses single-level NeRF sampling 64 points along each camera ray. The network $F_{\Delta w}$ is almost same as $F_\sigma$, except the final output layer has 24 channels and applies exp(·) to the output. The appearance code dimension is 128 [S6].

## Operating characteristics

**Standard NeRF & Variants:**
*   **Complexity**: The standard architecture [S1] and its direct variants (MobileNeRF A/F) rely on MLPs with hidden layers of 256 or 384 units. The computational cost is dominated by the evaluation of sinusoidal positional encodings and the MLP forward passes for every sample point along the ray.
*   **Performance**: Pyramid NeRF [S2] reports a 15x training speedup and 805x inference speedup compared to the original NeRF. OccNet [S4, unofficial] is significantly faster to train (02:17 vs 100:23 for radiance field) but yields much lower PSNR (20.60 vs typical NeRF >25) due to the lack of positional encodings.
*   **Memory**: MobileNeRF [S1] reduces inference memory by not using MLPs A and F during inference. OccNet [S4, unofficial] has a model size of 0.38/0.43 (units unspecified, likely MB or relative scale).

**Dynamic Architectures:**
*   **Dynamic Scene Network [S5]**: Uses equidistant sampling which may be less efficient than hierarchical sampling for static scenes but simplifies the training pipeline for dynamic scenes. The dual-MLP structure increases parameter count compared to a single NeRF but allows for explicit modeling of motion via scene flow.
*   **Blend Weight Field [S6]**: Adds a separate network $F_{\Delta w}$ with 24 output channels and exponential activation, increasing model size and training time (12 hours for 300 frames on 4 GPUs).

**Specialized Architectures:**
*   **Residual-NeRF-S [S3]**: Simpler merging logic (addition + sigmoid) compared to Mixnet, but results in worse depth and view reconstruction for transparent objects.
*   **Pyramid NeRF [S2]**: Uses progressive segmentation of large MLPs into thousands of smaller ones, trading off representational capacity for speed.

## Background

The standard NeRF architecture [S1] was introduced by Mildenhall et al. (2020) as the baseline for neural radiance fields. Subsequent modifications have addressed specific limitations:
*   **MobileNeRF [S1]** (2023) was developed to enable neural field rendering on mobile architectures by exploiting the polygon rasterization pipeline.
*   **Pyramid NeRF [S2]** (2023) was proposed to accelerate training and inference through frequency optimization and image pyramids.
*   **Residual-NeRF [S3]** (2024) was introduced to improve depth perception and training speed for scenes with transparent objects by leveraging a background prior.
*   **OccNet [S4, unofficial]** (2023) is part of the Factor Fields framework, demonstrating the impact of positional encodings by comparing single-level (no encoding) vs multi-level models.
*   **Dynamic Scene Network [S5]** (2025) addresses dynamic view synthesis by explicitly separating static and dynamic components.
*   **Animatable NeRF [S6]** (2021) introduced blend weight fields to model dynamic human bodies.
