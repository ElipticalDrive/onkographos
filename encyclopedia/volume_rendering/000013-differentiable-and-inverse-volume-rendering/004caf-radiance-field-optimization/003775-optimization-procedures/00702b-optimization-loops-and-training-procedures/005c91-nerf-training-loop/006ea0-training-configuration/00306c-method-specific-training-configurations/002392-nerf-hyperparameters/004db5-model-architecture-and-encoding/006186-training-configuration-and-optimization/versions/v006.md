# Training Configuration and Optimization

Also called: NeRF Training Hyperparameters, Optimization Protocols

## Overview
This article defines the hyperparameter configurations, optimization schedules, and loss function weights required to train Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) models effectively. It addresses the problem of converging the implicit radiance field representation to match observed images by specifying learning rates, batch sizes, sampling strategies, and regularization terms. It sets itself apart from sibling articles by focusing on the *training dynamics* and *hyperparameter tuning* rather than the architectural modifications (e.g., [Residual-NeRF](18113-residual-nerf.md)) or specific application constraints (e.g., [Dynamic and Articulated Scene Modeling](18219-dynamic-and-articulated-scene-modeling.md)).

## Shared principles
The training process for standard NeRF and its variants generally follows a stochastic gradient descent loop using the Adam optimizer . The core mechanism involves minimizing the photometric reconstruction error between rendered pixels and ground-truth images . Key shared principles include:
*   **Optimizer:** Adam is the standard optimizer .
*   **Sampling:** Stratified sampling is used to select points along rays during training to reduce variance .
*   **Loss Function:** The primary loss is typically Mean Squared Error (MSE) or L1 loss between rendered and ground-truth colors .
*   **Learning Rate Scheduling:** A step decay or linear decay schedule is commonly applied to stabilize convergence in later stages .

## Subtopics

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Defines the protocol for DäRF, which integrates depth priors. It differs by adding dropout regularization to positional encoding layers, a separate learning rate for depth scale, and a tuned depth loss weight ($\lambda$) to balance photometric and geometric consistency.

### [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md)
Defines the optimization for text-guided stylization. It differs by freezing density parameters and minimizing directional CLIP loss (cosine similarity) between rendered images and text embeddings, rather than photometric error.

### [NeRF-W Hyperparameter Configuration](14635-nerf-w-hyperparameter-configuration.md)
Defines the protocol for unconstrained photo collections. It differs by using a dual-MLP architecture (static and transient heads) and L1 loss with transient uncertainty regularization to handle dynamic objects and appearance variations.

## Background
The standard NeRF training configuration was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 . The use of Adam optimizer with a specific learning rate schedule ($5 \times 10^{-4}$ decaying to $5 \times 10^{-5}$) became a baseline for subsequent works . Variants like NeRF-W adapted these configurations to handle "in-the-wild" data by introducing transient modeling . CLIPNeRF extended the optimization objective to include semantic guidance from CLIP embeddings .
