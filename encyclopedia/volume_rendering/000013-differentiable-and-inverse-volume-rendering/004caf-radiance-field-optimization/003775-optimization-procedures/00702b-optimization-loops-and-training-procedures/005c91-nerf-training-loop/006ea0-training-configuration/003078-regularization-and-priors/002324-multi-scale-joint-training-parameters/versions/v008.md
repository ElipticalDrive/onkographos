# Multi-scale Joint Training Parameters

Also called: Multi-scale joint training loss weights

## Overview
Multi-scale joint training parameters define the weighting scheme for combining losses across different spatial resolutions or training stages in radiance field optimization. This method addresses the instability of optimizing high-frequency details before coarse geometry is established by balancing mean squared error (MSE) and uncertainty-based losses with specific coefficients [S1]. It is distinct from single-scale training by explicitly decoupling the contribution of fidelity and uncertainty metrics across a hierarchy of network depths or image pyramids [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Per-stage output images, per-stage uncertainty maps, and hyperparameters $\beta_1, \beta_2$.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar total loss value $\mathcal{L}$ for the current training iteration.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Loss Components:** For each stage $i$ (where $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$), the method requires:
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$: The Mean Squared Error between the rendered image and the ground truth image at stage $i$.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$: The uncertainty loss at stage $i$, derived from the network's predicted uncertainty volume.
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $D$: The current number of stages or network growth levels.
    *   $\beta_1$: Weight for the MSE loss.
    *   $\beta_2$: Weight for the uncertainty loss.

**Steps:**
1.  Initialize the total loss $\mathcal{L} = 0$.
2.  Iterate through each stage $i$ from $1$ to $D$:
    a. Compute $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ for the rendered output of stage $i$.
    b. Compute $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ for the uncertainty output of stage $i$.
    c. Accumulate the weighted loss: $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{L} + \beta_1 \mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i + \beta_2 \mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$.
3.  Return $\mathcal{L}$.

**Formulas:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^D \left( \beta_1 \mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i + \beta_2 \mathcal{L}_{unct}^i \right)
$$
Where:
*   $D$ is the current number of stages (also equal to $D-1$ times the network growth count).
*   $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ is the MSE loss of the output image of layer $i$.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ is the uncertainty loss of the output image of layer $i$.
*   $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ are the joint training weights.

**Parameters:**
*   $\beta_1 = 1.0$
*   $\beta_2 = 0.1$
*   [G] The uncertainty loss $\mathcal{L}_{unct}$ typically corresponds to a probabilistic formulation such as $-\log \sigma$ or a similar divergence term depending on the specific implementation of the uncertainty volume, though the exact functional form is not specified in the source [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Stage Count:** The number of stages $D$ is dynamic in Recursive-NeRF, growing as the network densifies. The loss summation must adapt to the current $D$ [S1].
*   **Uncertainty Definition:** The source does not specify the exact mathematical definition of $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ beyond it being an "uncertainty loss" [S1]. Implementers must refer to the specific uncertainty modeling technique used in the parent method (e.g., aleatoric or epistemic uncertainty estimation).

## Subtopics

### Cross-intrinsic Guidance
A coarse-to-fine training scheme that uses different camera intrinsic parameters to construct an image pyramid, enhancing multi-scale feature learning [S2]. It sets itself apart by reconfiguring the intrinsic matrix $K$ for each layer of the pyramid rather than just downsampling the image [S2]. Choose this when multi-view consistency is critical and the scene requires stable gradient-based optimization across scales [S2].

*   **Definition:** Constructs a 4-layer image pyramid with downsampling factors $S = \{2^{k-1} \mid k = 4, 3, 2, 1\}$ by reconfiguring the focal length $f$ and principal point $c$ in the intrinsic matrix $K$ [S2].
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Construct the pyramid with downsampling factors $S = \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ [S2].
    2.  For each layer $s \in S$, reconfigure the intrinsic matrix $K'_s$ as:
        $$
        K'_s = \begin{bmatrix} f/s & 0 & c_x/s \\ 0 & f/s & c_y/s \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
        $$
        [S2]
    3.  Assign matched multi-view settings $M_s = \{M_4, M_3, M_2, M_1\}$ to each layer, where larger downsampling factors use more views for stronger constraints [S2].
    4.  Run only a few thousand iterations per stage for the first three coarse stages ($s=8, 4, 2$) [S2].
    5.  Start fine training ($s=1$) to refine details [S2].
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $S$: Set of downsampling factors $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$. The largest factor $s=8$ is empirically sufficient [S2].
*   **Pitfalls:** Excessively large downsampling factors lead to blurry ground truth, decreasing fidelity and increasing refinement difficulty [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of stages $D$ and the number of pixels in the rendered images [S1]. The cross-intrinsic variant adds overhead for intrinsic matrix reconfiguration and multiple training stages [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the storage of intermediate rendered images and uncertainty maps for all active stages [S1].
*   **Performance:** In Recursive-NeRF, this weighting scheme supports dynamic network growth, allowing efficient resource allocation by focusing uncertainty on complex regions [S1]. In MVGS, it stabilizes multi-view optimization by enforcing constraints at coarser scales first [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents overfitting to high-frequency noise in early training stages; balances fidelity and uncertainty modeling [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$; the cross-intrinsic method requires managing multiple camera setups and image pyramids [S1, S2].
*   **When to use:** When training radiance fields or Gaussian splats that involve multi-scale representations or dynamic network growth [S1, S2].
*   **When not to use:** For single-scale, static networks where multi-view consistency is not a primary concern or where uncertainty modeling is not employed [S1].

## Background
The concept of multi-scale joint training parameters originates from Recursive-NeRF, which introduced a dynamically growing NeRF architecture [S1]. The specific weights $\beta_1=1.0$ and $\beta_2=0.1$ were established to balance MSE and uncertainty losses in this recursive framework [S1]. The related cross-intrinsic guidance scheme was proposed in MVGS (Multi-view-regulated Gaussian Splatting) to address multi-view consistency issues in 3D Gaussian Splatting by leveraging image pyramids and intrinsic parameter adjustments [S2].
