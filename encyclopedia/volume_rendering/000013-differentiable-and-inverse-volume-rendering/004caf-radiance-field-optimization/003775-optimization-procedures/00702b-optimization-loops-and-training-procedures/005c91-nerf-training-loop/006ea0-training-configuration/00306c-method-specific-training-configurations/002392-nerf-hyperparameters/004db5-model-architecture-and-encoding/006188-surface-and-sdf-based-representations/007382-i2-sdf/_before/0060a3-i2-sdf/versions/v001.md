# I2-SDF

Also called: Intrinsic Indoor Scene Reconstruction and Editing via Raytracing in Neural SDFs

## Overview
I2-SDF is a neural implicit framework for complex indoor scenes that jointly recovers underlying shape, radiance, and material fields from multi-view images [S1]. It distinguishes itself by decomposing the radiance field into spatially-varying material and emission components using surface-based, differentiable Monte Carlo raytracing, enabling physically based scene relighting and editing [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF approaches that treat radiance as a monolithic field, I2-SDF employs a two-stage training scheme and error-guided adaptive sampling to resolve ambiguities and improve reconstruction quality on fine-grained small objects [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images of an indoor scene, optionally with geometry priors such as depth and normal maps [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Neural fields for geometry ($F_d$), radiance ($F_c$), emitter semantics ($F_e$), material ($F_a, F_\rho$), and emission ($L[\cdot]$) [S1]. Rendered novel view images and decomposed intrinsic maps [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method uses four primary Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to parameterize the scene factors [S1]:
1.  **Neural SDF Field ($F_d$):** Maps 3D coordinates to Signed Distance Function (SDF) values [S1].
2.  **Neural Radiance Field ($F_c$):** Maps 3D coordinates and view direction to radiance [S1].
3.  **Neural Material Fields ($F_a, F_\rho$):** Model spatially-varying material properties [S1].
4.  **Emission Field ($F_e$ with $L[\cdot]$):** Models emitter semantics and emission [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Casting:** To render a pixel, cast a ray $\mathbf{r}$ from camera position $\mathbf{o}$ through the pixel along view direction $\mathbf{v}$ [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample $M$ points $\mathbf{x}_{ir} = \mathbf{o} + t_{ir}\mathbf{v}$ along the ray [S1].
3.  **SDF and Radiance Prediction:** Feed the sampled points $\mathbf{x}_{ir}$ into $F_d$ and $F_c$ to predict the SDF value $d_{ir}$ and radiance $\hat{c}_{ir}$ [S1].
4.  **Density Transformation:** Transform the SDF value $d(\mathbf{x})$ to volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ using a learnable parameter $\beta$ to control sparsity near the surface [S1]:
    $$
    \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = 
    \begin{cases} 
    \frac{1}{\beta}(1 - \exp(d(\mathbf{x})/\beta)) & \text{if } d(\mathbf{x}) < 0 \\
    \frac{1}{2\beta}\exp(-d(\mathbf{x})/2\beta) & \text{if } d(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0
    \end{cases}
    $$
    where $\beta$ is a learnable parameter [S1].
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Render the pixel color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ via numerical integration [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_{ri} \hat{c}_{ir}
    $$
    where $\hat{c}_{ir} = F_c(\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}_{ir}), \mathbf{v})$, $\alpha_{ri} = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_{ri} \delta_{ri})$, and $T_{ri} = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_{rj})$ [S1].
6.  **Two-Stage Training:**
    *   **Stage 1:** Train geometry ($F_d$), radiance ($F_c$), and emitter semantic ($F_e$) fields [S1].
    *   **Stage 2:** Train material ($F_a, F_\rho$) and emission ($L[\cdot]$) fields. During this stage, $F_d$, $F_c$, and $F_e$ are fixed and detached from gradient descents [S1].
7.  **Adaptive Sampling:** Apply an error-guided adaptive sampling scheme and a novel "bubble loss" to improve reconstruction quality on fine-grained small objects, addressing the issue where low-frequency information (large objects) converges faster than high-frequency information (small objects) [S1].
8.  **Intrinsic Decomposition:** Decompose the radiance field into material and emission fields using surface-based, differentiable Monte Carlo raytracing and emitter semantic segmentations [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\beta$: Learnable parameter controlling sparsity near the surface [S1].
*   $M$: Number of sample points along the ray [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Frequency Mismatch:** Low-frequency information (large objects) converges faster than high-frequency information (small objects), leading to poor reconstruction of fine-grained details without the proposed bubble loss and adaptive sampling [S1].
*   **Training Ambiguities:** The method requires a two-stage scheme to avoid ambiguities between geometry, radiance, and material fields [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method demonstrates superior quality on indoor scene reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and scene editing compared to state-of-the-art baselines [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Handles spatially-varying lighting and achieves indoor relighting with high fidelity; improves reconstruction quality on large-scale indoor scenes with fine-grained small objects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Implicit SDF functions may struggle to fit high-frequency details of thin or small structures without specific losses and sampling strategies [S1].
*   **When to use:** Complex indoor scenes requiring intrinsic decomposition (shape, material, incident radiance) and scene editing applications [S1].

## Background
I2-SDF was introduced in the paper "I2-SDF: Intrinsic Indoor Scene Reconstruction and Editing via Raytracing in Neural SDFs" [S1]. The method addresses the limitation that novel view synthesis alone is insufficient for reconstructing small-scaled and textured objects in complex indoor scenes [S1]. It builds on neural implicit surface representations and differentiable volume rendering [S1].
