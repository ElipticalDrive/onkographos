# Simple-NeRF

Also called: SimpleNeRF

## Overview
Simple-NeRF is a regularization method for sparse-input Neural Radiance Fields that employs two simpler augmented NeRF models (points and views augmentations) to provide depth supervision to the main NeRF, mitigating overfitting and implausible geometries caused by high capacity and sparse views [S1, S2]. It sets itself apart from prior works by obtaining depth supervision through augmented models without requiring pre-training on large datasets or external depth models [S1]. Unlike RegNeRF, which uses depth smoothness priors and may fuse objects at different depths, Simple-NeRF mitigates both floaters and incorrect depth issues by learning in-situ depth priors [S2].

## How it works
Simple-NeRF builds on [DS-NeRF](14428-ds-nerf.md) as its baseline architecture, which uses a coarse and fine MLP for hierarchical sampling [S1, S2]. The method trains two augmented NeRF models in tandem with the main NeRF. These augmented models are used only during training and are discarded at inference [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of $N$ input images (typically 2–4 views) and their corresponding camera poses. Optional sparse depth maps from Structure from Motion (SfM) are used as initial labels [S2].
*   **Outputs:** A trained main NeRF model $F_\theta$ capable of rendering novel views.

### Data Structures and Architecture
The architecture consists of three parallel NeRF models:
1.  **Main Model:** Standard NeRF with coarse MLP $N_1$ and fine MLP $N_2$.
2.  **Smoothing Augmentation (Points Augmentation):** An augmented model with coarse MLP $N_{1s}$ and fine MLP $N_{2s}$.
3.  **Lambertian Augmentation (Views Augmentation):** An augmented model with coarse MLP $N_{1l}$ and fine MLP $N_{2l}$.

**Neural Network Definitions:**
The NeRF predicts view-independent volume density $\sigma_i$ and view-dependent color $c_i$ for a point $\mathbf{p}_i$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{v}$:
$$
\sigma_i, \mathbf{h}_i = N_1(\gamma(\mathbf{p}_i, 0, l_p)); \quad c_i = N_2(\mathbf{h}_i, \gamma(\mathbf{v}, 0, l_v))
$$
where $\mathbf{h}_i$ is a latent feature, $\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2)$ is the positional encoding from frequency $d_1$ to $d_2$, and $l_p, l_v$ are the highest frequencies for position and view [S1, S2].

**Augmentation Modifications:**
*   **Smoothing Augmentation:** Reduces the degree of positional encoding to prevent undesired depth discontinuities and floaters. The coarse MLP $N_{1s}$ receives only low-frequency positional encodings $\gamma(\mathbf{p}_i, 0, l_{ps})$. The remaining high-frequency components $\gamma(\mathbf{p}_i, l_{ps}, l_p)$ are concatenated to the input of the fine MLP $N_{2s}$ along with latent features $\mathbf{h}_i$ and view direction $\mathbf{v}$ [S2]:
    $$
    c_i = N_{2s}(\mathbf{h}_i, \gamma(\mathbf{p}_i, l_{ps}, l_p), \gamma(\mathbf{v}, 0, l_v))
    $$
    This biases the model against sharp depth discontinuities [S1, S2].
*   **Lambertian Augmentation:** Disables view-dependent radiance to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity. The coarse MLP $N_{1l}$ outputs both density and color based on position alone:
    $$
    \tilde{\sigma}_i, \tilde{c}_i = N_{1l}(\gamma(\mathbf{p}_i, 0, l_p))
    $$
    This biases the model against shape-radiance ambiguity in Lambertian regions [S1, S2].

### Training Steps

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the main NeRF ($N_1, N_2$) and the two augmented models ($N_{1s}, N_{2s}, N_{1l}, N_{2l}$) [S2].
2.  **Forward Pass:** For each ray, sample points using coarse stratified sampling and fine dense sampling. Compute density and color for the main model and both augmented models [S1, S2].
3.  **Depth Estimation:** Compute the mean depth $z$ for each pixel from the volume rendering of density and sampled depths [S1, S2]. Let $z_c$ be the coarse depth of the main model, $z_f$ be the fine depth, $z_s$ be the smoothing augmented depth, and $z_l$ be the Lambertian augmented depth [S1, S2].
4.  **Reliability Mask Computation:** Determine which depth estimates are reliable by reprojection consistency [S1, S2].
    *   For a pixel $q$, reproject a $k \times k$ patch around $q$ to the nearest *other* training view using the estimated depths $z_c$ (main) and $z_a$ (augmented, where $z_a \in \{z_s, z_l\}$) [S1].
    *   Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) intensities $e_c$ and $e_a$ between the reprojected patches and the actual training image in that view [S1].
    *   Define a threshold $e_\tau$ [S1].
    *   Compute the ternary mask $m_a$ for the augmented model:
        $$
        m_a = \begin{cases} 
        1 & \text{if } (e_a < e_c) \text{ and } (e_a < e_\tau) \\
        -1 & \text{if } (e_c < e_a) \text{ and } (e_c < e_\tau) \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
        $$
    *   Compute the mask $m_m$ for the main model similarly, comparing $z_c$ against the augmented depth $z_a$ [S2].
    *   *Note:* In specular regions, reprojected patches may mismatch, leading to $m=0$. This results in no supervision for those pixels, preventing incorrect depth estimates but reducing the number of supervised pixels [S1, S2].
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Depth Supervision Loss ($L_{aug}$):** Impose depth supervision using stop-gradient ($\hat{\nabla}$) on the target depth to prevent gradient flow to the teacher model [S2].
        $$
        L_{aug} = \mathbb{1}\{m_a=1\} \odot \|z_m - \hat{\nabla}(z_a)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}\{m_m=1\} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_m) - z_a\|^2
        $$
        Compute $L_s$ for smoothing augmentation and $L_l$ for Lambertian augmentation separately, then sum them: $L_{depth} = L_s + L_l$ [S2].
    *   **Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss ($L_{cfc}$):** Ensure hierarchical sampling consistency between the coarse depth $z_c$ and fine depth $z_f$ of the main model [S2]:
        $$
        L_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}\{m_f=1\} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}\{m_c=1\} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2
        $$
        where $m_f$ and $m_c$ are reliability masks computed for the fine and coarse depths respectively [S2].
    *   **Photometric Loss:** Standard MSE or LPIPS loss between rendered color and ground truth image [S1, S2].
6.  **Optimization:** Update all parameters (main and augmented models) using the total loss:
    $$
    L_{total} = L_{photo} + L_{depth} + L_{cfc}
    $$
    [S2].

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Threshold $e_\tau$:** Used to filter out incorrect depth estimates. Value not explicitly given in sources [S1].
*   **Specular Regions:** In specular regions, reprojected patch intensities may not match, causing masks to be zero. This results in fewer supervised pixels but avoids supervision with incorrect depth estimates [S1, S2].
*   **Single-View Visible Regions:** Transparent floaters may persist in regions visible in only one training view because reliable depth supervision is not available there [S2].
*   **Depth Variance:** The depth supervision loss is imposed only on the mean depth, not on the variance [S2].
*   **Base Model Performance:** The method is limited to cases where the base model achieves reasonable performance but suffers from distortions; it may not help if the base model performance is very poor [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Simple-NeRF queries the coarse MLPs 192 times and fine MLPs 192 times (384 total queries per pixel), whereas standard NeRF queries coarse MLPs 64 times and fine MLPs 192 times (256 total) [S2]. Training takes approximately 1.5 times more time than standard NeRF due to the augmentations on the coarse NeRF [S2]. Disabling the coarse-fine consistency loss ($L_{cfc}$) increases training time by almost 2x because augmentations must be included for the fine NeRF as well [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **LLFF (2 views):** SSIM 0.6501, PSNR 19.57, LPIPS 0.2688. Outperforms RegNeRF (SSIM 0.5712, PSNR 18.52, LPIPS 0.3056) and FreeNeRF (SSIM 0.6322, PSNR 19.52, LPIPS 0.2754) [S1].
    *   **LLFF (4 views):** SSIM 0.7016, PSNR 21.99, LPIPS 0.2633. Outperforms RegNeRF (SSIM 0.6645, PSNR 21.32, LPIPS 0.2794) and FreeNeRF (SSIM 0.6764, PSNR 21.91, LPIPS 0.2848) [S1].
    *   **RealEstate-10K (2 views):** MAE 0.3269, SROCC 0.9215. Outperforms DS-NeRF (MAE 0.7164, SROCC 0.6660) [S2].
    *   **NeRF-LLFF (2 views):** MAE 0.1420, SROCC 0.8480. Outperforms DS-NeRF (MAE 0.2074, SROCC 0.7230) [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates floaters and shape-radiance ambiguity without pre-training. Effective in sparse view settings.
*   **Limitations:** Increased training time and compute overhead. Less effective if the base model is very poor. Transparent floaters may persist in single-view visible regions.

## Background
Simple-NeRF was introduced by Nagabhushan Somraj et al. in 2023 [S1]. It was later expanded as part of the Simple-RF family in 2024 [S2]. The method is designed for few-shot/sparse input NeRF training on datasets like LLFF, using DS-NeRF as the baseline architecture [S1]. It addresses the limitations of prior works that require pre-training on large datasets (e.g., PixelNeRF, IBRNet) or external depth models (e.g., DDP-NeRF, SCADE) [S1]. The approach of using augmented models in tandem with the main radiance field model is closest to the Dual-Student architecture [Ke et al. 2019], but uses complementary abilities rather than identical models [S1, S2].
