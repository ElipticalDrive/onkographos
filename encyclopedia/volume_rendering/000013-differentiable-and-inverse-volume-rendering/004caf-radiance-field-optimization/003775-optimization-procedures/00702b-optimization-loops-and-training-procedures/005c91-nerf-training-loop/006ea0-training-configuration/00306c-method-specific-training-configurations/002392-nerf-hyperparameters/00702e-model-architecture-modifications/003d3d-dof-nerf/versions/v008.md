# DoF-NeRF

Also called: Depth-of-Field Meets Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
DoF-NeRF is a neural rendering framework that extends the standard Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to simulate the depth-of-field (DoF) effects of a physical lens, allowing it to operate on views with different focus configurations and manipulate DoF effects by adjusting virtual aperture and focus parameters [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which assumes a pinhole camera model and all-in-focus inputs, DoF-NeRF explicitly models the aperture and circle of confusion (CoC) to approximate DoF under arbitrary aperture size and focus distance, enabling the reconstruction of all-in-focus scenes from shallow DoF inputs [S1]. It distinguishes itself from related methods like RawNeRF and NeRFocus, which also simulate DoF but assume all-in-focus inputs, by jointly optimizing aperture diameters and focus distances during training to render clear novel views from shallow DoF inputs [S1].

## How it works
DoF-NeRF extends the NeRF architecture by incorporating optical models of geometric optics into the volume rendering process. The method simulates the emergence of DoF in two steps: (1) radiance of spatial points scattering to a particular area, and (2) the center ray accepting the scattered radiance from neighboring rays [S1]. This is achieved by combining optical models with classical volume rendering, using an indicator function to distinguish points based on the CoC diameter and the distance to the ray [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: A set of $N$ training images $\mathcal{I} = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_N\}$ with known camera parameters (generated via COLMAP [S1]). The images may have shallow DoF.
*   **Outputs**: A trained NeRF model $G_\Theta$ and learned aperture parameters $\hat{K}$ and focus distances $\hat{F}$ [S1].

### Data Structures and Parameters
The method introduces two sets of learnable parameters, one for each training image $n \in \{1, \dots, N\}$:
1.  **Aperture Parameter** $K_n$: A scalar representing the aperture size. Defined as $K_n = f \times D$, where $f$ is the focal length and $D$ is the aperture diameter [S1].
2.  **Focus Distance** $F_n$: A scalar representing the distance to the plane of focus for image $n$ [S1].

These parameters are initialized as learnable tensors:
$$ \hat{K} = \text{nn.Parameter}(\text{shape}=(N, 1), \text{requires\_grad}=\text{True}) $$
$$ \hat{F} = \text{nn.Parameter}(\text{shape}=(N, 1), \text{requires\_grad}=\text{True}) $$
[G] In practice, $K$ and $F$ are initialized to small positive values or values estimated from metadata if available, to ensure physical plausibility during early training.

### Formulas
**Circle of Confusion (CoC) Diameter**
The simplified CoC diameter $\delta(h_t)$ for a spatial point at object distance $h_t$ is calculated as [S1]:
$$ \delta(h_t) = K \times \left| \frac{1}{F} - \frac{1}{h_t} \right| $$
where $K$ is the aperture parameter and $F$ is the focus distance [S1].

**Concentration Step**
For a ray $r_p$ passing through pixel $p$, the method first computes the "concentrated" radiance $\hat{C}^s(p)$ by integrating the volume rendering along the ray, weighted by the CoC area [S1]:
$$ \hat{C}^s(p) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_s} K_{volume}(r_p, h_i) c(r_p(h_i), d)}{\pi \delta_c^2(p)/4} $$
where $N_s$ is the number of sample points along the ray, $K_{volume}$ is the volume rendering weight, $c$ is the color, and $\delta_c(p)$ is the CoC radius at the concentration point [S1].

**Scatter Step**
The scattered radiance $\hat{I}_{scatter}(p)$ at pixel $p$ sums contributions from $M$ neighboring pixels $p_j$. A point contributes to pixel $p$ if its CoC diameter is larger than the distance between pixels [S1]:
$$ \hat{I}_{scatter}(p) = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \sum_{i=1}^{N_s} \mathbb{1}_{\delta(h'_i) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2} T'_{ij} \hat{C}(p_j, h'_i) $$
where $\mathbb{1}$ is the indicator function, $T'_{ij}$ is the transmittance, and $\hat{C}(p_j, h'_i)$ is the radiance at neighbor $p_j$ [S1].

**Final Observed Color**
The final color $\hat{I}(p)$ for $P$ rays is computed by scattering the concentrated radiance from neighbors [S1]:
$$ \hat{I}(p) = \sum_{j=1}^{P} \mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2} \hat{C}^s(p_j) $$
[G] The indicator function $\mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2}$ effectively blurs the image by averaging radiance from neighboring pixels whose CoC covers the distance to pixel $p$.

**Loss Function**
The optimization loss $L$ is the photometric error (Mean Squared Error) between synthesized views $\hat{I}$ and observations $I$, summed over all $N$ input images [S1]:
$$ L = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \| I_n - \hat{I}_n \|_2^2 $$

### Algorithm Steps
The training process uses a two-stage optimization to avoid divergence caused by ray gathering in DoF simulation [S1].

**Stage 1: Pinhole Camera Training**
1.  Initialize NeRF model $G_\Theta$, aperture parameters $\hat{K}$, and focus distances $\hat{F}$.
2.  Set aperture parameter $K=0$ for all images, degenerating the model to a linear pinhole camera [S1].
3.  For $N_{pretrain}$ iterations:
    a.  Randomly select rays $\mathbf{r}$ and corresponding ground truth images $I$ [S1].
    b.  Render images $\hat{I}$ using standard volume rendering [S1].
    c.  Compute loss $L$ [S1].
    d.  Update NeRF parameters $\Theta$ [S1].
    [G] During this stage, $\hat{K}$ and $\hat{F}$ are not updated or are fixed to neutral values to establish a coarse 3D geometry [S1].

**Stage 2: Concentrate-and-Scatter Optimization**
1.  For $N_{iter}$ iterations (after $N_{pretrain}$):
    a.  **Patch-based Ray Selection**:
        i.  Define anchors every $N_{anchor}$ pixels on the imaging plane.
        ii. Randomly select one anchor, defining an $N_{patch} \times N_{patch}$ patch [S1].
        iii. Generate rays $\mathbf{r}$ passing through pixels in the selected patch [S1].
    b.  **Concentration**:
        i.  Compute concentrated radiance $\hat{C}^s$ and CoC radius $\delta_c$ for the rays using the current NeRF, $\hat{K}$, and $\hat{F}$ [S1].
    c.  **Scatter**:
        i.  Compute final rendered images $\hat{I}$ using the scatter step [S1].
    d.  **Loss and Update**:
        i.  Compute loss $L$ [S1].
        ii. Update NeRF parameters $\Theta$, aperture parameters $\hat{K}$, and focus distances $\hat{F}$ jointly [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Divergence**: Using random rays for DoF simulation in the early stages can lead to divergence or sub-optimal results due to ray gathering [S1]. The two-stage optimization mitigates this by first establishing geometry with a pinhole model [S1].
*   **Computational Cost**: Simulating DoF for every ray on the entire imaging plane is computationally unacceptable [S1]. The patch-based ray selection method is used as a compromise to reduce cost [S1].
*   **Precision**: The CoC diameter calculation involves division by distance $h_t$. Points at infinity or very close to the camera may require careful handling to avoid numerical instability [G].
*   **Boundary Conditions**: The indicator function $\mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2}$ requires careful handling of pixel boundaries to avoid artifacts when scattering from neighbors near the image edge [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The concentrate-and-scatter rendering is more expensive than standard volume rendering due to the neighbor summation step. The patch-based ray selection reduces the per-iteration cost compared to full-image rendering [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the NeRF model parameters, plus the learnable $K$ and $F$ parameters for each training image. The scatter step may require temporary storage for neighbor radiance values [S1].
*   **Performance**: On the real-world dataset (amiya scene), DoF-NeRF achieves PSNR 28.311 vs NeRF 26.924, SSIM 0.9289 vs 0.9092, and LPIPS 0.1370 vs 0.1633 [S1]. On the synthetic dataset (room scene), DoF-NeRF achieves PSNR 29.443 vs NeRF 26.668, SSIM 0.9135 vs 0.8743, and LPIPS 0.1502 vs 0.1961 [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Explicitly models aperture and focus distance, allowing it to handle shallow DoF inputs and produce better all-in-focus novel view synthesis with less blurriness and more texture details compared to vanilla NeRF [S1]. It is plug-and-play and can be inserted into NeRF-based frameworks [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Requires a two-stage optimization process, increasing training complexity. The scatter step is computationally intensive, necessitating patch-based selection [S1].

## Background
DoF-NeRF was introduced by Zijin Wu, Juewen Peng, and Weicai Zhong in 2022 at the ACM Multimedia conference [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF, which assumes a linear pinhole camera model and all-in-focus inputs, making it unsuitable for real-world images with finite depth-of-field [S1]. The method builds on the principles of geometric optics to simulate the aperture of a lens, allowing for the manipulation of DoF effects and the reconstruction of all-in-focus scenes from shallow DoF inputs [S1]. It differs from RawNeRF and NeRFocus, which also simulate DoF but assume all-in-focus inputs, by optimizing aperture and focus distances during training [S1].
