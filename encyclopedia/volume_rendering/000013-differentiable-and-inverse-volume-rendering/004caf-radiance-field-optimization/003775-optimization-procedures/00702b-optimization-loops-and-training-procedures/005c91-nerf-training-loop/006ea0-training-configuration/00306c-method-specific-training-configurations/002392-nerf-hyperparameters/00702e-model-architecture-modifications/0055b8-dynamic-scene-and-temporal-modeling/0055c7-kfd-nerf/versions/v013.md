# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), weighted by a learnable Kalman gain, rather than relying solely on time-conditioned feature interpolation or rigid deformation priors [S1]. It utilizes a tri-plane spatial representation for the canonical space to enable a shallower observation network and faster convergence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamp $t_i$, and camera parameters.
*   **Outputs:** Deformation field $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ (warping points to canonical space), volume density $\sigma$, and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes ($C = \{P_{xy}, P_{yz}, P_{zx}\}$) encode the canonical space. Each plane has resolution $R \times R$. Features are sampled via bilinear interpolation. Raw coordinate inputs are concatenated with sampled features to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP. Input: $[\mathbf{x}, t_i]$. Output: Mean observation $\mathbf{y}_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_i}$ [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer. Input: $[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_i}, \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}]$. Output: Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Observer Query (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $\mathbf{y}_{t_i}$ and noise terms $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ \mathbf{y}_{t_i}, \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
2.  **Velocity Calculation:** Approximate the velocity $\mathbf{v}_{t_i}$ using the deformation estimates from the two previous frames $t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$ [S1].
    $$ \mathbf{v}_{t_i} = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - \Delta \mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i} $$
    where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
3.  **Prediction:** Calculate the predicted deformation $\hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the local linear motion model. The prediction assumes the deformation at $t_i$ is the deformation at $t_{i-1}$ plus the velocity scaled by time step [S1].
    $$ \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = \Delta \mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot \mathbf{v}_{t_i} $$
    *Note: The source formula in Algorithm 1 shows $\hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = 2\Delta \mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - \Delta \mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}$, which is algebraically equivalent to the velocity-based formulation above [S1].*
4.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ taking noise terms and timestamps [S1].
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_i}, \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
5.  **Deformation Estimation (Update):** Combine the observation $\mathbf{y}_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimate $\hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (\mathbf{y}_{t_i} - \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
6.  **Warping:** Warp all 3D points $\mathbf{x}$ from real space to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$ using the estimated deformation:
    $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i} $$
7.  **Radiance Field Query:** Query the radiance field (using the tri-plane representation and MLPs) with $\mathbf{x}'$ and view direction to obtain density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
8.  **Rendering:** Render the image using standard volume rendering integration [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of five components [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{image}$):** Standard photometric reconstruction loss (e.g., MSE) between rendered and ground truth images [S1].
2.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation and the final estimated deformation [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| \mathbf{y}_{t_i} - \hat{\Delta \mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
3.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** Soft regularization for the initial frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real-world radiance field [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) \Delta \mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
4.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Consistency loss between the proposal network (for occupancy estimation) and the learned model, following Mip-NeRF 360 [2] [S1].
5.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Encourages sparse edges in the tri-plane representation [S1].
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class KFDNeRF:
    def __init__(self, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_layer, tri_plane, radiance_mlp):
        self.observer = observer_mlp
        self.kalman_gain_net = kalman_gain_layer
        self.tri_plane = tri_plane
        self.radiance_mlp = radiance_mlp
        # Store previous frame deformations and noise terms
        self.prev_deformations = {} 
        self.prev_noise_terms = {}

    def get_deformation(self, x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, eps_prev_1, eps_prev_2):
        """
        Calculate deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter logic.
        """
        # 1. Observer: Get current observation and noise terms
        y_ti, eps_ti = self.observer(x, t_i)
        
        # 2. Velocity Calculation (if history exists)
        if t_prev_1 is not None and t_prev_2 is not None:
            dx_prev_1 = self.prev_deformations.get((x, t_prev_1))
            dx_prev_2 = self.prev_deformations.get((x, t_prev_2))
            
            if dx_prev_1 is not None and dx_prev_2 is not None:
                dt_i = t_i - t_prev_1
                # v_ti = (dx_{t_{i-1}} - dx_{t_{i-2}}) / dt_i
                v_ti = (dx_prev_1 - dx_prev_2) / dt_i
                
                # Prediction: dx_hat^- = dx_{t_{i-1}} + dt_i * v_ti
                dx_pred = dx_prev_1 + dt_i * v_ti
            else:
                # Fallback if history incomplete: use observation only or zero prediction
                dx_pred = y_ti 
        else:
            # First frame or insufficient history: prediction relies on observation
            dx_pred = y_ti

        # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
        # Input: eps_ti, eps_prev_1, t_i, t_prev_1
        if t_prev_1 is not None:
            K_ti = self.kalman_gain_net(torch.cat([eps_ti, eps_prev_1, t_i, t_prev_1], dim=-1))
        else:
            # If no previous noise, assume gain is 0 or 1 depending on confidence. 
            # Source implies gain is learned from noise terms. 
            # [G] Default to identity matrix or zero if dimensions mismatch, 
            # but typically K is a scalar or diagonal matrix in this context.
            # Assuming scalar gain for simplicity in this generic implementation.
            K_ti = torch.zeros_like(y_ti) 

        # 4. Update: dx_hat = dx_pred + K_ti * (y_ti - dx_pred)
        dx_estimated = dx_pred + K_ti * (y_ti - dx_pred)
        
        # Store for next steps
        self.prev_deformations[(x, t_i)] = dx_estimated
        self.prev_noise_terms[(x, t_i)] = eps_ti
        
        return dx_estimated, y_ti, eps_ti

    def forward(self, x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, eps_prev_1, eps_prev_2, view_dir):
        # Get deformation
        dx_est, y_ti, eps_ti = self.get_deformation(x, t_i, t_prev_1, t_prev_2, eps_prev_1, eps_prev_2)
        
        # Warp to canonical space
        x_canonical = x + dx_est
        
        # Query Tri-Plane and Radiance MLP
        # [G] Assuming tri_plane.sample returns features based on x_canonical
        features = self.tri_plane.sample(x_canonical)
        
        # Concatenate raw coordinates to handle shift errors
        input_to_radiance = torch.cat([features, x_canonical], dim=-1)
        
        sigma, color = self.radiance_mlp(input_to_radiance, view_dir)
        
        return sigma, color, dx_est, y_ti, eps_ti

    def compute_loss(self, rendered_color, gt_color, dx_est, y_ti, t_i, tri_plane_features):
        # L_image
        l_image = F.mse_loss(rendered_color, gt_color)
        
        # L_kf
        l_kf = torch.mean((y_ti - dx_est)**2)
        
        # L_co (Canonical Observation Loss)
        # Only applies if t_i == 0
        indicator = 1.0 if t_i == 0 else 0.0
        l_co = indicator * torch.mean(dx_est)
        
        # L_prop (Proposal Loss)
        # [G] Placeholder for Mip-NeRF 360 proposal consistency loss
        l_prop = 0.0 
        
        # L_tv (Total Variation Loss on Tri-Planes)
        # Apply to each plane in the tri-plane collection
        l_tv = 0.0
        for plane in tri_plane_features:
            # plane shape: (H, W, C)
            # TV = sum over i,j of ||x_{i,j} - x_{i-1,j}||^2 + ||x_{i,j} - x_{i,j-1}||^2
            diff_y = plane[1:, :, :] - plane[:-1, :, :]
            diff_x = plane[:, 1:, :] - plane[:, :-1, :]
            l_tv += torch.mean(diff_y**2) + torch.mean(diff_x**2)
        
        lambda_tv = 1e-4
        total_loss = l_image + l_kf + l_co + l_prop + lambda_tv * l_tv
        
        return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Frames:** During initial training stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. Observations take precedence in later stages [S1]. The implementation must handle the lack of $t_{i-2}$ data for the first two frames, likely defaulting to observation-only or zero-velocity predictions [S1].
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Concatenating raw coordinates with encoded features is required to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Hard Masking:** Unlike D-NeRF which hard-masks deformation at $t_0$ to zero, KFD-NeRF uses a soft regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{co}$ to allow learning of deformation at $t_0$ while keeping it close to canonical [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to other dynamic NeRF methods. On synthetic data, KFD-NeRF-L takes 3 hours, while KFD-NeRF-S takes 30 minutes [S1]. This is faster than D-NeRF (48 hours) but slower than some voxel-based methods like TiNeuVox-B (40 minutes) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations (e.g., D-NeRF uses 16 MB, KFD-NeRF uses 175 MB for the large variant) [S1].
*   **Performance (Synthetic):** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
*   **Performance (Real):** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate deformation estimation from scene observations and predictions; efficient tri-plane representation allows for a shallower observation MLP; smooth deformation fields are easier to fit with MLPs than high-frequency feature interpolation signals [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on local linear motion assumption, which may struggle with highly non-linear or abrupt motions not captured by the velocity approximation [S1]. Requires chronological training order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Y. Zhan, Z. Li, M. Niu, Z. Zhong, S. Nobuhara, K. Nishino, and Y. Zheng from The University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory [S1]. It was published in 2024 (arXiv:2407.13185v1, July 18, 2024) [S1]. The method draws inspiration from control theory, specifically the Kalman filter, to model the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous dynamic NeRF methods that either disregard motion priors (e.g., DyNeRF, KPlanes) or rely on rigid deformation fields that fail to capture non-rigid motion [S1].
