# NeRF-SR Training Configuration
Also called: NeRF-SR, NeRF Super-Resolution

## Overview
NeRF-SR is a training configuration designed to synthesize high-resolution (HR) novel views from low-resolution (LR) inputs by optimizing a neural radiance field that is explicitly conditioned on the input resolution [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF [3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md] by employing a supersampling strategy that renders at the target HR resolution during training, rather than rendering at LR and upsampling post-hoc [S1]. This approach leverages the 3D consistency of the radiance field to generate plausible HR details without requiring external HR ground truth or pre-trained super-resolution models [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of LR images $\{I_n\}$ with known camera intrinsics $K_n$ and extrinsics $T_n$. The target output is a trained NeRF model capable of rendering HR images.
*   **Outputs:** A trained MLP representing the radiance field $\mathcal{F}_\theta$, optimized to minimize photometric error between rendered HR pixels and ground truth HR images (if available for evaluation) or simply minimizing reconstruction error on the LR inputs rendered at HR scale [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The implementation builds on the standard NeRF data structures [3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md].
*   **Ray Buffer:** Stores rays sampled from the training images.
*   **MLP Models:** Two identical Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are used:
    1.  **Coarse MLP:** Predicts density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ for coarse samples.
    2.  **Fine MLP:** Predicts density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ for fine samples.
*   **Sampling Parameters:**
    *   $N_c = 64$: Number of coarse samples per ray.
    *   $N_f = 64$: Number of fine samples per ray.

**Training Steps:**
1.  **Ray Sampling:** For each training iteration, sample $B$ rays from the training images. The batch size is fixed at $B = 2048$ rays [S1].
2.  **Hierarchical Sampling:**
    *   Sample $N_c$ points along each ray using uniform sampling (or importance sampling based on the coarse MLP's density predictions).
    *   Compute coarse transmittance $T_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ for each sample $i$ [Parent].
    *   Use the coarse density predictions to guide the sampling of $N_f$ points in high-density regions for the fine MLP [Parent].
3.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute the rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for each ray using the differentiable volume rendering integral [Parent]:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
        where $N = N_c + N_f$ (or effectively the sum of contributions from both stages, typically implemented as a weighted sum of coarse and fine outputs) [Parent].
    *   Crucially, the rays are sampled to cover the **HR pixel grid** of the target resolution, not the LR input grid [S1].
4.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered HR color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground truth HR color $C(\mathbf{r})$ [S1]. Note: While the input is LR, the "ground truth" for the HR pixel corresponds to the high-resolution version of the scene, which is approximated by the LR image upsampled or simply by the fact that the model learns to fit the high-frequency content implied by the LR inputs via the supersampling mechanism [S1].
5.  **Optimization:**
    *   Update the MLP parameters $\theta$ using the Adam optimizer [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** Exponentially decayed from $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ to $5 \cdot 10^{-6}$ [S1].
*   **Beta Parameters:** $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$ [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 2048 rays [S1].
*   **Samples:** 64 coarse, 64 fine [S1].
*   **Epochs:**
    *   Blender dataset: 20 epochs [S1].
    *   LLFF dataset: 30 epochs [S1].
*   **Dataset Split:** 100 views for training, 200 images held out for testing (Blender) [S1]. For LLFF, all images are used for training and averaged metrics are reported [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_nerf_sr(model, dataloader, epochs=20, lr_init=5e-4, lr_min=5e-6, 
                  batch_size=2048, n_coarse=64, n_fine=64, beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999):
    optimizer = Adam(model.parameters(), lr=lr_init, betas=(beta1, beta2))
    
    for epoch in range(epochs):
        # Exponential learning rate decay
        current_lr = lr_init * (lr_min / lr_init) ** (epoch / epochs)
        for param_group in optimizer.param_groups:
            param_group['lr'] = current_lr
            
        for batch in dataloader:
            # batch contains rays and corresponding ground truth colors
            # Rays are sampled to cover the HR resolution grid
            rays = batch['rays']
            gt_colors = batch['gt_colors']
            
            # Forward pass: Hierarchical Sampling
            # 1. Coarse sampling
            coarse_samples = sample_rays(rays, n_coarse) 
            coarse_sigma, coarse_color = model(coarse_samples)
            
            # 2. Fine sampling (guided by coarse density)
            fine_samples = importance_sample(rays, coarse_sigma, n_fine)
            fine_sigma, fine_color = model(fine_samples)
            
            # 3. Volume Rendering
            # Combine coarse and fine samples
            all_samples = concatenate(coarse_samples, fine_samples)
            all_sigma = concatenate(coarse_sigma, fine_sigma)
            all_color = concatenate(coarse_color, fine_color)
            
            rendered_colors = volume_render(all_samples, all_sigma, all_color)
            
            # 4. Loss Calculation
            loss = mse_loss(rendered_colors, gt_colors)
            
            # 5. Backpropagation
            optimizer.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            optimizer.step()
```

## Subtopics

### Refinement Network (Ours-Refine)
A post-processing module applied to NeRF-SR outputs to boost texture details and edge sharpness, particularly for real-world datasets like LLFF where supersampling alone is insufficient [S1]. It uses a fixed number of reference patches ($K=8$) with dimensions $64 \times 64$ [S1]. During inference, input images are divided into non-overlapping patches, and each patch is refined using reference patches from the dataset (typically the first image) [S1]. The refinement involves backprojecting pixels to 3D world coordinates using estimated depth and camera parameters, then extracting corresponding patches from reference images [S1]. This stage adds negligible time (~1.3 seconds on a single 1080Ti for a 1008x756 image) compared to the volumetric rendering (~48 seconds) [S1]. It significantly improves LPIPS and PSNR at scale $\times 4$ (PSNR 25.59, SSIM 0.759, LPIPS 0.165) and slightly improves LPIPS at scale $\times 2$ (PSNR 27.34, SSIM 0.842, LPIPS 0.103) on the LLFF dataset [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The training time is comparable to training a standard HR NeRF, as it involves rendering at the target HR resolution [S1]. Inference time is dominated by the volumetric rendering of the MLP; for a 1008x756 image, it takes ~48 seconds on a single 1080Ti [S1]. The optional refinement stage adds ~1.3 seconds [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires memory for two MLPs (coarse and fine) and the ray batch buffer. No explicit voxel grid is stored, unlike grid-based methods.
*   **Performance:** On the Blender dataset, NeRF-SR (Ours-SS) achieves PSNR 26.81, SSIM 0.823, LPIPS 0.145 at scale $\times 2$, and PSNR 24.76, SSIM 0.723, LPIPS 0.292 at scale $\times 4$ [S1]. On the LLFF dataset, it achieves PSNR 27.31, SSIM 0.838, LPIPS 0.139 at scale $\times 2$, and PSNR 25.13, SSIM 0.730, LPIPS 0.244 at scale $\times 4$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Generates photorealistic HR details without external HR data or pre-trained SR models [S1]. Maintains 3D consistency [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not support arbitrary-scale super-resolution (fixed scales like $\times 2, \times 4$) [S1]. Introduces extra computational cost compared to LR rendering [S1].

## Background
NeRF-SR was introduced in the paper "NeRF-SR: High-Quality Neural Radiance Fields using Supersampling" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF, which, while capable of arbitrary resolution rendering, often produces blurry results when upscaling from LR inputs because it is trained on LR data [S1]. The method was developed to provide a pipeline for HR novel view synthesis using mostly LR inputs [S1].
