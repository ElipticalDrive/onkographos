# Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering

Also called: AB Transformer, ABLE-NeRF, Attention-Based Rendering With Learnable Embeddings for Neural Radiance Field, Attention-based Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering (ABLE-NeRF) replaces the physics-based volumetric rendering (VR) equation with a self-attention mechanism to handle view-dependent effects, glossy surfaces, and translucent objects [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which assigns weights based on density $\sigma$ and interval $\delta$, ABLE-NeRF uses Transformer attention to determine point importance, and unlike Ref-NeRF which relies on normal prediction, it avoids the ill-posed nature of predicting normals in an object's interior [S1]. It sets itself apart from other transformer-based methods (e.g., IBRNet, SRT) by applying transformers directly to model the volumetric rendering approach rather than just for feature aggregation or point proposal [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera ray $r(t) = o + td$ (origin $o$, direction $d$), camera pose.
*   **Output:** Predicted RGB color $\mathbf{y}$ for the ray.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Embedding ($v_i$):** Latent vector of dimension $D$. Generated by passing a conic frustum through an embedding block.
*   **Ray Token ($R$):** Latent vector of dimension $D$. Prepended to the sequence of volume embeddings.
*   **Learnable Embeddings (LE):** Static memory tokens of dimension $D$. Stored in latent space to capture scene lighting.
*   **View Token ($V$):** Latent vector of dimension $D$. Derived from camera pose.

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of conic frustum volumes sampled along the ray. Typical value: 96 per network (coarse/fine) [S1].
*   $L$: Number of transformer layers. Typical value: 2 for coarse network, 6 for fine network [S1].
*   $D$: Latent dimensional size. Typical value: 192 [S1].
*   Camera pose encoding: 16-band Fourier features [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting and Sampling:** Cast a camera ray $r(t) = o + td$ into the scene [S1][S2, unofficial]. Sample $N$ conic frustum volumes along the ray using Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) from mip-NeRF [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Volume Embedding:** Pass each conic volume through a volume embedding block consisting of four MLP layers (hidden units $D$, ReLU activation) to generate volume embeddings $v_i$, where $i$ denotes the position along the ray starting from the camera [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Sequence Construction:** Prepend a Ray token $R$ (dimension $D$) to the sequence of volume embeddings to form the input sequence $Z_0 = \{R, v_1, v_2, ..., v_N\}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **AB Transformer (Direct Lighting):**
    *   Apply masked self-attention for volume embeddings. A volume embedding $v_i$ can only attend to itself, the ray token $R$, and other volume embeddings lying in front of it (indices $< i$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Implement masking by setting scaled-dot product attention scores to $-\infty$ in the input of softmax for illegal connections (rear points) [S1].
    *   Do not apply masking to the ray token $R$, allowing it to attend to all volume embeddings [S1].
    *   Update equations for layer $l$:
        $$ v^l_i = \text{Att}(Z^{l-1} \setminus \{v^{l-1}_{i+1}, ..., v^{l-1}_N\}) $$
        $$ R^l = \text{Att}(Z^{l-1}) $$
    *   After the final encoder layer $L$, attach a single MLP classification head to the final ray token $R_L$ to predict the color of the ray [S1].
5.  **Learnable Embeddings (LE) Integration:**
    *   Incorporate Learnable Embeddings (LE) as learnable memory tokens in latent space to store scene information [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Use cross-attention where the LE attends to points sampled during ray casting (volume embeddings) to memorize scene information [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   During inference, map volume embeddings to latent space via Cross Attention with LE [S2, unofficial].
6.  **View-Dependent Decoding (LE Transformer):**
    *   Decode view-dependent effects using a directional view token $V$ comprising the camera pose [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   The view token $V$ is a linear mapping of the camera pose encoded with 16-band Fourier features to the LE dimension [S2, unofficial].
    *   The view token queries the Learnable Embeddings to capture view-dependent lighting [S1][S2, unofficial].
7.  **Final Output:** Separate output into AB Transformer (direct lighting) and LE Transformer (view-dependent lighting) [S2, unofficial]. Apply a fixed mapping function to convert to sRGB for final output [S2, unofficial].

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class AttentionBasedVolumetricRendering(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, D=192, L_coarse=2, L_fine=6, N=96, num_le=32):
        super().__init__()
        self.D = D
        self.N = N
        self.num_le = num_le
        
        # Volume Embedding Block: 4 MLP layers
        self.volume_embedder = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(D, D), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(D, D), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(D, D), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(D, D)
        )
        
        # AB Transformer (Self-Attention with Mask)
        self.ab_transformer = nn.ModuleList([
            TransformerLayer(D, num_heads=4, mask=True) for _ in range(L_coarse)
        ])
        
        # LE Transformer (Cross-Attention)
        self.le_transformer = nn.ModuleList([
            TransformerLayer(D, num_heads=4, mask=False) for _ in range(L_coarse) # Reuse L for simplicity, or define separate
        ])
        
        # Ray Token and LE Initialization
        self.ray_token = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, 1, D))
        self.learnable_embeddings = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, num_le, D))
        
        # Final MLP Head
        self.mlp_head = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(D, D), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(D, 3) # RGB output
        )
        
        # View Token Generator
        self.view_token_proj = nn.Linear(32, D) # 16-band Fourier -> 32 dims -> D

    def forward(self, ray_origins, ray_directions, camera_poses):
        # 1. Sample N conic frustums along the ray
        # Assuming t_vals are pre-computed based on near/far and IPE
        t_vals = get_t_vals(ray_origins, ray_directions, self.N) 
        xyz = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + t_vals.unsqueeze(2) * ray_directions.unsqueeze(1)
        
        # 2. Encode with IPE (Integrated Positional Encoding)
        # Note: IPE encoding is applied to the frustum bounds, resulting in D-dim features
        # For simplicity, assume xyz is already encoded to D dimensions by an external IPE module
        volume_features = encode_with_ipe(xyz) # Shape: (B, N, D)
        
        # 3. Generate Volume Embeddings
        v_i = self.volume_embedder(volume_features) # Shape: (B, N, D)
        
        # 4. Construct Sequence Z0 = {R, v1, ..., vN}
        batch_size = xyz.shape[0]
        R = self.ray_token.expand(batch_size, -1, -1) # Shape: (B, 1, D)
        Z0 = torch.cat([R, v_i], dim=1) # Shape: (B, N+1, D)
        
        # 5. AB Transformer (Self-Attention with Mask)
        # Create causal mask for volume embeddings, but not for Ray Token
        # Mask shape: (N+1, N+1). 
        # Ray token (index 0) attends to all.
        # Volume embedding i attends to 0..i.
        mask = create_attention_mask(N+1)
        
        Z_ab = Z0
        for layer in self.ab_transformer:
            Z_ab = layer(Z_ab, mask=mask)
            
        R_L = Z_ab[:, 0, :] # Extract final Ray Token
        
        # 6. LE Integration and View Token
        # View Token from Camera Pose
        # Camera pose encoding: 16-band Fourier features
        view_token_raw = encode_camera_pose_fourier(camera_poses) # Shape: (B, 32)
        V = self.view_token_proj(view_token_raw) # Shape: (B, D)
        V = V.unsqueeze(1) # Shape: (B, 1, D)
        
        # Cross Attention: LE attends to Volume Embeddings (v_i)
        # LE are static tokens, v_i are the keys/values
        le_input = self.learnable_embeddings.expand(batch_size, -1, -1) # Shape: (B, num_le, D)
        
        # Cross Attention: LE (Query) attends to v_i (Key/Value)
        # Note: The paper says "LE attends to points... to memorize". 
        # This implies LE is Query, v_i is Key/Value.
        # However, standard cross-attention in transformers often uses 
        # the sequence being processed as Query. 
        # Let's follow the description: "LE cross-attend to the embedded volumes"
        
        # We need to implement Cross Attention manually or via nn.MultiheadAttention
        # Q: LE, K: v_i, V: v_i
        le_attn_output = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(
            le_input, v_i, v_i, 
            attn_mask=None # No mask specified for LE cross-attention in text
        )
        
        # Combine LE output with Ray Token output? 
        # The paper says "Separate output into AB Transformer (direct lighting) and LE Transformer (view-dependent lighting)"
        # And "Apply a fixed mapping function to convert to sRGB for final output"
        
        # Final Prediction
        # The paper states: "After the final encoder layer L, attach a single MLP classification head to the final ray token RL to predict the color"
        # This suggests the AB Transformer output R_L is the primary predictor, potentially enhanced by LE.
        # However, it also mentions LE Transformer. 
        # Let's assume R_L is combined with LE information or R_L is the final input to MLP.
        # Given the ambiguity, we use R_L as the primary signal, potentially modified by LE.
        # A common interpretation in such architectures is that LE refines the representation.
        # Let's assume R_L is passed through the MLP head.
        
        color = self.mlp_head(R_L)
        
        # Apply sRGB mapping
        color = srgb_mapping(color)
        
        return color

class TransformerLayer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, D, num_heads=4, mask=False):
        super().__init__()
        self.num_heads = num_heads
        self.mask = mask
        self.self_attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(D, num_heads, batch_first=True)
        self.norm1 = nn.LayerNorm(D)
        self.norm2 = nn.LayerNorm(D)
        self.ffn = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(D, D * 3), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(D * 3, D)
        )
        
    def forward(self, x, mask=None):
        # Self Attention
        attn_output, _ = self.self_attn(x, x, x, attn_mask=mask)
        x = self.norm1(x + attn_output)
        
        # Feed Forward
        ffn_output = self.ffn(x)
        x = self.norm2(x + ffn_output)
        
        return x

def create_attention_mask(N):
    # N is the sequence length (N+1 including Ray Token)
    # Ray token (index 0) attends to all.
    # Volume embedding i (index i+1) attends to 0..i+1.
    mask = torch.triu(torch.ones(N, N), diagonal=1)
    mask = mask.bool()
    # Invert mask for attention (True means mask out)
    # But PyTorch attn_mask expects True to mask out.
    # We want lower triangular including diagonal.
    # So upper triangular (excluding diagonal) should be masked.
    # However, Ray token (0) should attend to all.
    # So row 0 should be all False (no mask).
    # Rows 1..N-1 should have mask for columns > row_index.
    
    # Create a mask where True means "do not attend"
    mask = torch.zeros(N, N, dtype=torch.bool)
    for i in range(1, N):
        mask[i, i+1:] = True
        
    return mask

def encode_camera_pose_fourier(pose, bands=16):
    # Simplified Fourier encoding for camera pose
    # Pose is typically (B, 3) or (B, 4)
    # Apply sin/cos at frequencies 2^0 to 2^(bands-1)
    freq_bands = torch.pow(2, torch.arange(bands, device=pose.device).float())
    # Expand pose to (B, 3, 1) and freq to (1, 1, bands)
    # Result (B, 3, bands)
    encoded = torch.sin(pose.unsqueeze(2) * freq_bands * torch.pi)
    # Concatenate sin and cos? The formula in parent says sin and cos.
    # Here we just use sin for simplicity, or add cos
    encoded = torch.cat([encoded, torch.cos(pose.unsqueeze(2) * freq_bands * torch.pi)], dim=2)
    return encoded.flatten(start_dim=1)

def srgb_mapping(color):
    # Fixed mapping to sRGB
    # Standard sRGB gamma correction
    color = torch.clamp(color, 0, 1)
    return torch.where(color <= 0.0031308, 12.92 * color, 1.055 * torch.pow(color, 1/2.4) - 0.055)

## Subtopics

### [Slot-guided Feature Lifting](21730-slot-guided-feature-lifting.md)
Slot-guided Feature Lifting is a method for learning object-centric radiance fields that encodes scene representations from source views using Slot-Attention, lifts 2D features to 3D to capture fine-grained details, and uses a point-slot mapping to decode 3D representations for rendering color and segmentation masks. It differs from sibling methods like uORF, uOCF, and sVORF by computing only a single radiance field rather than $K$ fields (one per slot), which significantly reduces training time and memory overhead.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** High computational cost due to repeated Attention calculations for AB and LE Transformers, leading to long training times [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing Learnable Embeddings (LE) and volume embeddings for all sampled points.
*   **Measured Performance:** On A100 GPU x8 (rendering on A100 x1): 'mic' train 25h, render 59s, PSNR 36.84; 'drums' train 25h, render 59s, PSNR 26.87; 'materials' train 25h, render 59s, PSNR 35.46 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly reduces 'blurry' glossy surfaces and produces realistic translucent surfaces compared to Ref-NeRF and standard NeRF [S1]. Achieves SOTA results on the Blender dataset [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Long training times due to attention calculations [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering glossy and translucent objects with view-dependent effects is critical.
*   **When not to use:** When training time is a constraint or when the scene does not exhibit complex view-dependent lighting.

## Background
Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering (ABLE-NeRF) was introduced by Zhe Jun Tang, Tat-Jen Cham, and Haiyu Zhao in the paper 'ABLE-NeRF: Attention-Based Rendering with Learnable Embeddings for Neural Radiance Field' (arXiv:2303.13817v1, March 2023) [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of standard NeRF in rendering glossy and transparent surfaces, which often appear murky due to the inherent usage of volumetric rendering (VR) where features are accumulated in the color space [S1]. The method draws inspiration from modern game engines that use Light Probes to store local lighting, incorporating Learnable Embeddings to capture view-dependent effects within the scene [S1].
