# Coarse-to-Fine Model Initialization

Also called: Coarse-to-Fine Initialization, Progressive Scale Training

## Overview
Coarse-to-Fine Model Initialization is a training strategy that initializes a high-resolution (fine) neural radiance field model using the optimized parameters of a lower-resolution (coarse) model [S1]. It shortens training time and improves performance by reusing the coarse deformation MLP weights and interpolating the coarse feature grid into the fine grid [S1]. It employs progressive scale training, doubling the voxel resolution after a set number of iterations [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Optimized weights and feature grids from a trained coarse model; target resolution for the fine model [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Initialized weights and feature grids for the fine model, ready for continued optimization [S1].

**Data Structures**
The method operates on two primary data structures defined in the source [S1]:
1.  **Deformation Feature Grid:** A spatial data structure storing deformation features. The coarse model uses a lower resolution grid, while the fine model uses a higher resolution grid [S1].
2.  **Deformation MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) used to decode deformation features. The architecture is identical for both coarse and fine models [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Train Coarse Model:** Optimize the coarse model (lower resolution) until convergence or a stable state is reached.
2.  **Initialize Fine Model Weights:** Copy the trained network weights from the coarse deformation MLP to the fine deformation MLP. This is valid because the MLP architecture is identical [S1].
3.  **Interpolate Feature Grid:** Initialize the fine model's deformation feature grid by interpolating the optimized feature grid from the coarse model [S1].
4.  **Progressive Scale Training:** Begin training the fine model. Apply progressive scale training, where the voxel resolution is doubled after a set number of training iterations [S1].

**Formulas**
Not given in the sources.

**Parameters**
*   **MLP Architecture (Coarse/Fine Deformation):** 4 fully connected layers, width 64. Input dimensions: position (33), time (11), feature (44) [S1].
*   **MLP Architecture (Color):** 3 fully connected layers, width 128. Input dimensions: position (33), view direction (27), feature (12) [S1].
*   **Activation:** ReLU [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Resolution Mismatch:** The interpolation step assumes a hierarchical relationship between coarse and fine grids. If the resolution scaling is not a power of two or the grid topology differs, standard interpolation may fail [G].
*   **Overfitting:** Training directly at high resolution without coarse initialization can lead to overfitting to noise or artifacts [G].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def initialize_fine_model(coarse_mlp_weights, coarse_feature_grid, initial_resolution, max_resolution, doubling_interval):
    # Step 2: Initialize Fine Model Weights
    # The MLP architecture is identical, so weights are copied directly.
    fine_mlp_weights = coarse_mlp_weights.copy()

    # Step 3: Interpolate Feature Grid
    # Interpolate the coarse feature grid to the initial fine resolution.
    # [G] Standard trilinear or nearest-neighbor grid upsampling operation.
    fine_feature_grid = coarse_feature_grid.interpolate(initial_resolution)

    current_resolution = initial_resolution
    training_iterations = 0

    while current_resolution <= max_resolution:
        # Step 4: Progressive Scale Training
        # Train the model at the current resolution.
        # [G] Standard gradient descent update on the radiance field loss.
        training_iterations += 1

        # Check if it's time to double the resolution
        if training_iterations % doubling_interval == 0 and current_resolution < max_resolution:
            new_resolution = current_resolution * 2
            # Re-initialize the feature grid at the new resolution using interpolation
            fine_feature_grid = fine_feature_grid.interpolate(new_resolution)
            current_resolution = new_resolution

    return fine_mlp_weights, fine_feature_grid
```

## Subtopics

### [Progressive Coarse-to-Fine Training Strategy](8900-progressive-coarse-to-fine-training-strategy.md)
Progressive Coarse-to-Fine Training is a strategy that gradually introduces finer levels of detail during optimization to ensure stable convergence and prevent overfitting. It solves the problem of geometric artifacts and slow convergence that occur when training directly at high resolution by establishing a coherent global structure before fitting high-frequency features. It differs from standard full-resolution training by deferring the fitting of fine features until the global geometry is established, and differs from simple two-stage pipelines by potentially using continuous or multi-stage resolution scaling rather than a single abrupt switch.

### [NeRF Restarting Approach](12980-nerf-restarting-approach.md)
The NeRF Restarting Approach is a training schedule for joint pose and radiance field optimization that eliminates artifacts learned during the pose refinement phase by discarding the neural network weights and retraining from scratch. Unlike staged training, which freezes poses and continues training the existing networks, this method re-initializes both coarse and fine Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) after pose convergence. It is chosen when the initial joint optimization introduces persistent artifacts into the scene representation that persist even after pose stabilization.

### [Overall Training Pipeline](13114-overall-training-pipeline.md)
This article describes the complete training loop for Unconstrained Pose-Prior-Free Neural Radiance Fields (UP-NeRF), which jointly optimizes camera poses, scene geometry, and appearance from unordered, unposed image collections. It integrates a coarse-to-fine hierarchical sampling strategy with a dynamic loss weighting scheme that transitions from feature/depth supervision to RGB reconstruction as training progresses. Unlike standard NeRF pipelines, this method explicitly handles transient artifacts and mirror-symmetry ambiguities through detached network heads and specific scheduling parameters.

### LiDAR Initialization
LiDAR Initialization is an initialization technique using LiDAR point clouds to provide prior information for the scene representation, helping to avoid local minima in optimization [S2]. On Argoverse2, the combination of Hybrid Scene Representation (HSR) and LiDAR Initialization (LI) yields more than 4 times boost in convergence speed compared with NGP [S2]. Without LI, the model cannot converge to NGP's PSNR, and training time is infinite ('Inf.') because it struggles to represent space sparsely [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduces training time significantly compared to training from scratch at high resolution [S1]. The combination of Hybrid Scene Representation (HSR) and LiDAR Initialization (LI) yields more than 4 times boost in convergence speed compared with NGP [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On Argoverse2 sequences, the method achieves significant speed improvements [S2]. For a 600m scene, the proposed method trained within 1 minute exceeds the performance of other methods [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Faster convergence, better geometric stability, avoids local minima [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a hierarchical grid structure; interpolation may introduce artifacts if the coarse model is not well-trained [G].
*   **When to Use:** When training large-scale scenes or dynamic scenes where high-resolution training from scratch is too slow or unstable [S1][S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the scene is small and simple, or when the computational cost of maintaining two models is prohibitive [G].

## Background
The coarse-to-fine initialization strategy was introduced in the context of neural deformable voxel grids for fast optimization of dynamic view synthesis [S1]. It builds on the idea that optimizing a scene at multiple resolutions can lead to more stable and faster convergence [S1].
