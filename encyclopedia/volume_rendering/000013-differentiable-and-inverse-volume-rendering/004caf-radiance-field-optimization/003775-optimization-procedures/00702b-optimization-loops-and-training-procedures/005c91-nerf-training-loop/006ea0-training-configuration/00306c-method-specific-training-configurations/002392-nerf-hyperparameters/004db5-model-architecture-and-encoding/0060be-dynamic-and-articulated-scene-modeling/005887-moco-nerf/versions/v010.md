# MoCo-NeRF

Also called: Motion-Oriented Compositional Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
MoCo-NeRF is a framework for free-viewpoint rendering of monocular human videos that models non-rigid motions (e.g., cloth dynamics) as radiance residual fields rather than spatial offsets, using rigid skeletal transformations as a prior [S1]. It separates the canonical T-pose representation from pose-dependent non-rigid residuals, allowing direct color supervision for both components [S1]. Unlike offset-based strategies that suffer from high computational complexity and misaligned learning objectives, MoCo-NeRF reduces prediction range to the radiance space and establishes direct supervision from ground-truth pixel colors by embedding rigid and non-rigid fields together in the rendering process [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Monocular video frames of a human subject, camera poses, and skeletal joint positions $J$ for each frame.
**Outputs:** Rendered RGB images for novel viewpoints and poses.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Skeletal Weights Volume Decoder:** Optimizes canonical skinning weights $w^c_k$ for $K$ joints [S1].
2.  **Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding (MHE):**
    *   **Single-Subject:** One MHE $\psi_{hash}$ with 16 levels, feature dimension $H=2$ per level [S1].
    *   **Multi-Subject:** One global MHE $\psi_{hash}^G$ (4 levels, $H=2$) and $N_s$ local MHEs $\{\psi_{hash}^i\}$ (16 levels, $H=2$) [S1].
3.  **Learnable Base Code:** $S \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$, randomly initialized [S1].
4.  **Identity Codes:** $\{S_i\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$, learnable vectors for multi-subject ID [S1].
5.  **MLP Decoders:**
    *   $M_{rigid}$: Predicts rigid color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ [S1].
    *   $M_{nr}$: Predicts residuals $\Delta \mathbf{c}, \Delta \sigma$ [S1].
6.  **Projection Matrices:** $W_p, W_Q, W_K, W_V$ for pose-embedded feature generation [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Canonical Transformation:** Transform observation point $\mathbf{x}$ to canonical point $\mathbf{x}_c$ using inverse linear blend skinning [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_c = \sum_{k=1}^K w^o_k (R_k \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_k) $$
    where $w^o_k = \frac{w^c_k(R_k \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K w^c_j(R_j \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_j)}$, $R_k, \mathbf{t}_k$ are rotation and translation of bone $k$, and $w^c_k$ are optimized canonical weights [S1].

2.  **Rigid Branch Feature Query:** Query multiresolution volumetric features $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ from MHE $\psi_{hash}$:
    $$ \mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c} = \psi_{hash}(\mathbf{x}_c) $$
    [S1].

3.  **Rigid Branch Prediction:** Slice the first half ($H$ dimensions) of $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ at each resolution level to compute $\mathbf{f}^{:H}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$. Predict rigid color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ using $M_{rigid}$:
    $$ \mathbf{c}, \sigma = M_{rigid}(\mathbf{f}^{:H}_{\mathbf{x}_c}) $$
    [S1].

4.  **Non-Rigid Branch Feature Preparation:** Use the full feature $\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{x}_c}$. Freeze the first half representing canonical radiance. Denote the remaining optimized half as $\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ [S1].

5.  **Pose-Embedded Implicit Feature Generation:**
    a. Initialize learnable base code $S \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$ [S1].
    b. Apply sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma$ to 23 joint positions $J$:
        $$ \gamma(\mathbf{j}_i) = [\mathbf{j}_i, \sin(2^0 \pi \mathbf{j}_i), \cos(2^0 \pi \mathbf{j}_i), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{j}_i), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{j}_i)] $$
        where $L=10$ frequency bands [S1].
    c. Project encoded joints $\gamma(J) \in \mathbb{R}^{23 \times 63}$ to raw pose-embedded code $\mathbf{P}_{raw}$:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_{raw} = W_p \cdot \gamma(J) $$
        [S1].
    d. Generate query $\mathbf{P}_q$ from $S$, and key $\mathbf{P}_k$, value $\mathbf{P}_v$ from $\mathbf{P}_{raw}$:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_q = W_Q \cdot S, \quad \mathbf{P}_k = W_K \cdot \mathbf{P}_{raw}, \quad \mathbf{P}_v = W_V \cdot \mathbf{P}_{raw} $$
        [S1].
    e. Compute final implicit feature $\mathbf{P}_{nr}$ using cross-attention:
        $$ \mathbf{P}_{nr} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{P}_q \cdot \mathbf{P}_k^\top) \cdot \mathbf{P}_v $$
        [S1].

6.  **Non-Rigid Branch Prediction:** Predict color and density residuals $\Delta \mathbf{c}, \Delta \sigma$ using $M_{nr}$ conditioned on $\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{nr}$:
    $$ \Delta \mathbf{c}, \Delta \sigma = M_{nr}(\mathbf{f}^*_{\mathbf{x}_c}; \mathbf{P}_{nr}) $$
    [S1].

7.  **Compositional Rendering:** For each ray $\mathbf{r}$, query $M$ samples. Compute final RGB $\hat{C}_{final}$ using stratified sampling and volume rendering with compositional density $(\sigma + \Delta \sigma)$ and color $(\mathbf{c} + \Delta \mathbf{c})$:
    $$ \hat{C}_{final}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{m=1}^M T_m (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_m + \Delta \sigma_m)\delta_m)) (\mathbf{c}_m + \Delta \mathbf{c}_m) $$
    where $T_m = \exp(-\sum_{n=1}^{m-1} \sigma_n \delta_n)$ is transmittance, and $\delta_m$ is the distance between adjacent samples [S1].

8.  **Multi-Subject Extension (Optional):** For subject $i$, query global features from $\psi_{hash}^G$ and slice local features from $\psi_{hash}^i$. Concatenate global features, sliced local features, and subject ID code $S_i$ to predict $\mathbf{c}, \sigma$:
    $$ \mathbf{c}, \sigma = M_{rigid}(\psi_{hash}^G(\mathbf{x}_c) \oplus (\psi_{hash}^i(\mathbf{x}_c))^{:H} \oplus S_i) $$
    [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with betas (0.9, 0.99) [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for skeletal weights volume decoder; $5 \times 10^{-3}$ for all other components [S1].
*   **MHE Configuration:** 16 levels with feature dimension $H=2$; global MHE uses 4 levels with $H=2$ [S1].
*   **Sampling:** 6 patches of size $32 \times 32$ per iteration, with 256 samples per ray [S1].
*   **Loss Weight:** $\lambda = 0.2$ for balancing rigid and final loss terms [S1].
*   **Frequency Bands:** $L=10$ for sinusoidal positional encoding [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Spatial offset strategies suffer from high computational complexity because a single spatial offset spans an unbounded range and is unique for every 3D point across all input frames and body poses [S1].
*   Spatial offset strategies suffer from misaligned learning objectives because the learning objective of spatial offsets is not directly aligned with conventional RGB-based supervision [S1].
*   Precision issues may arise from the freezing of the first half of the feature vector in the non-rigid branch; ensure gradients do not flow into frozen parameters [G].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   Training time is significantly reduced compared to offset-based methods. Single-subject training takes 1–2 hours on an RTX 3090 GPU [S1]. Multi-subject training (6 subjects) takes ~2 hours on one RTX 3090 [S1].
*   Comparison: 1.9h vs 38.9h for HumanNeRF (single-subject); ~2h vs ~123h for NHP (multi-subject) [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **Single-Subject (ZJU-MoCap subjects 377, 386, 387, 392, 393, 394; 23 views: 1 train, 22 eval):** PSNR 31.06, SSIM 0.9734, LPIPS* 28.83 [S1].
*   **Multi-Subject (ZJU-MoCap subjects 377, 386, 392):** PSNR 31.97, SSIM 0.9781, LPIPS* 24.54 [S1].
*   Outperforms HumanNeRF, Instant-NVR, GauHuman, NHP, and GHuNeRF in PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS* [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Direct color supervision for non-rigid motions; efficient training; scalable to multi-subject settings [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate skeletal joint positions [S1]. Performance depends on the quality of the canonical T-pose representation [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Free-viewpoint rendering of dynamic human subjects from monocular videos where non-rigid motions (cloth, skin) are significant [S1].
*   When training efficiency is a priority compared to offset-based methods [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Static scenes (use standard NeRF) [S1].
*   Scenes with complex non-human non-rigid motions not modeled by skeletal priors [S1].

## Background
MoCo-NeRF was proposed by J. Kim et al. in 2024 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of spatial offset strategies in dynamic human modeling, which suffer from high computational complexity and misaligned learning objectives [S1]. The method builds on the concept of radiance fields and linear blend skinning [S1]. It is evaluated on the ZJU-MoCap and MonoCap datasets [S1].
