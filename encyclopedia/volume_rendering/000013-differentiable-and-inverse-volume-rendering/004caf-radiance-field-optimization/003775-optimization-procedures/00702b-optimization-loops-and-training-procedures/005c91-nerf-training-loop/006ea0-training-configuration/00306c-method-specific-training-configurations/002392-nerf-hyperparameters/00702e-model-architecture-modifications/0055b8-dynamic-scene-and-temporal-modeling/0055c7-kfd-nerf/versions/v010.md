# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [D-NeRF](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) by replacing time-conditioned feature interpolation with a deformation field guided by a Kalman filter, leveraging the inductive smoothness bias of MLPs for motion representation [S1]. It further distinguishes itself from [TiNeuVox](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) by using a tri-plane spatial representation for the canonical space, enabling a shallower observation MLP and faster convergence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and timestamp $t_i$. Training data consists of multi-view images released in chronological order [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Deformation field $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ mapping real-space points to a canonical space, and radiance/color $c$ in the canonical space [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes encode the canonical space [S1]. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP that takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ as input [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that computes the Kalman gain [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The method estimates the deformation state $d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}$ for each point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t_i$ using a Kalman filter update step.

1.  **Observer Step:** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
    The observer MLP has exactly two layers [S1].

2.  **Prediction Step:** Calculate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using deformations from the previous two frames $t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$ [S1]:
    $$ v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i} $$
    where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    Compute the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the local linear motion model [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}} $$
    [S1].

3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ with noise terms and timestamps [S1]:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
    [S1].

4.  **Update Step:** Combine the observation and prediction to produce the final deformation estimation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
    [S1].

5.  **Warping:** Warp all points in real space to the time-independent canonical space using $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].

6.  **Rendering:** Query the radiance field in the canonical space using the warped points and original direction $\mathbf{d}$ to compute color [S1].

**Training Strategy:**
*   **Chronological Release:** Training images are gradually released in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Balance of Prediction/Observation:** In initial training stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. In later stages, observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details [S1].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
    [S1].

2.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** A soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real-world radiance field [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].

3.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Ensures consistency between the proposal network (used for occupancy estimation via distillation from Mip-NeRF 360 [2]) and the learned model [S1].

4.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation_step(x, t_i, t_prev, t_prev_prev, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman filtering.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D point coordinates (tensor)
        t_i: Current timestamp (float)
        t_prev: Previous timestamp (float)
        t_prev_prev: Timestamp before previous (float)
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP for observation
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer for Kalman gain
        
    Returns:
        dx_hat: Estimated deformation
    """
    # 1. Observer Step for current frame
    y_ti, eps_ti = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # 2. Retrieve previous deformations and noise terms
    # In a training loop, these are stored from previous iterations
    dx_prev, eps_prev = get_previous_state(x, t_prev)
    dx_prev_prev, eps_prev_prev = get_previous_state(x, t_prev_prev)
    
    # 3. Prediction Step
    # Calculate velocity based on local linear motion
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev
    v_ti = (dx_prev - dx_prev_prev) / delta_t
    
    # Predicted deformation
    dx_pred = dx_prev + delta_t * v_ti
    
    # 4. Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Input: noise terms and timestamps
    K_ti = kalman_gain_net(eps_ti, eps_prev, t_i, t_prev)
    
    # 5. Update Step
    dx_hat = dx_pred + K_ti * (y_ti - dx_pred)
    
    return dx_hat

def kfd_nerf_loss(dx_hat, y_ti, tri_planes, t_i, t_0, N, C):
    """
    Computes the total loss for KFD-NeRF.
    """
    # Kalman Loss
    L_kf = mean_squared_error(y_ti, dx_hat)
    
    # Canonical Observation Loss (Soft regularization for t=0)
    indicator = 1.0 if t_i == t_0 else 0.0
    L_co = indicator * mean(dx_hat)
    
    # Total Variation Loss on Tri-Planes
    L_tv = 0.0
    for plane in C:
        # Calculate TV loss for the plane
        # Assuming plane is a tensor of shape [H, W, Channels]
        diff_x = plane[1:, :, :] - plane[:-1, :, :]
        diff_y = plane[:, 1:, :] - plane[:, :-1, :]
        L_tv += mean_squared_error(diff_x) + mean_squared_error(diff_y)
    L_tv = L_tv / len(C)
    
    # Proposal Loss (from Mip-NeRF 360 distillation)
    L_prop = compute_proposal_loss()
    
    # Image Loss (standard NeRF rendering loss)
    L_image = compute_image_loss()
    
    # Total Loss
    lambda_tv = 1e-4
    L_total = L_image + L_kf + L_co + L_prop + lambda_tv * L_tv
    
    return L_total
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Mitigated by concatenating encoded tri-plane features with raw coordinate inputs [S1].
*   **Hard Masking at $t_0$:** Unlike D-NeRF which uses a hard mask to force deformation to 0 at $t_0$, KFD-NeRF uses a soft regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$) to allow learning of deformation at $t_0$ and prevent the canonical space from becoming too complex [S1].
*   **Convergence Balance:** The method relies on the automatic balance between prediction and observation. If the motion dynamics model is inaccurate, the predictor's confidence drops, requiring the observer to compensate [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is comparable to other dynamic NeRF methods. On synthetic data, it takes approximately 30 minutes (KFD-NeRF-S) to 3 hours (KFD-NeRF-L) [S1]. This is significantly faster than D-NeRF (48 hours) and comparable to TiNeuVox-B (40 minutes) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations, allowing for high-resolution canonical space reconstruction [S1]. Parameter size is 175 MB for KFD-NeRF-L, comparable to TiNeuVox-B (50 MB) but larger than D-NeRF (16 MB) [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Accurate deformation estimation by combining observations and predictions [S1].
    *   Efficient tri-plane representation enables fast convergence and high quality [S1].
    *   Shallow observer MLP (2 layers) reduces computational cost [S1].
    *   Inductive smoothness bias of deformation fields makes them easier to fit than high-frequency feature interpolation signals [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion for prediction, which may not hold for highly non-linear motions [S1].
    *   Performance depends on the chronological release of training images to provide priors [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for dynamic scene reconstruction where motion is relatively smooth and can be approximated by local linear motion, and where efficient training and high-quality rendering are required [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Not ideal for scenes with abrupt, non-linear motions that violate the local linear motion assumption [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Y. Zhan, Z. Li, M. Niu, Z. Zhong, S. Nobuhara, K. Nishino, and Y. Zheng from The University of Tokyo, Kyoto University, and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory [S1]. It was published in July 2024 (arXiv:2407.13185v1) [S1]. The method draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states [S1]. It integrates a Kalman filter to optimize state estimations, combining an MLP-based observer and a system-dynamics-based predictor [S1]. The work builds upon previous dynamic NeRF methods like D-NeRF [36] and TiNeuVox [10], addressing their limitations in motion representation and spatial encoding efficiency [S1].
