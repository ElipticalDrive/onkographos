# Unbiased Score Distillation

Also called: USD

## Overview
Unbiased Score Distillation (USD) is a training loss function for radiance fields that rectifies critical bias in traditional Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) by substituting the biased unconditional noise predictions of multi-view diffusion models (e.g., Zero-1-to-3) with the true unconditional noise from a pre-trained 2D diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) [S1]. Unlike prior methods that enforce multi-view consistency via restrictive volume or ray aggregation, USD uses the optimized radiance field itself as a consistency prior, allowing full freedom in camera pose selection during optimization [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:**
  - $\theta$: Parameters of the radiance field (e.g., NeRF).
  - $\phi$: Parameters of the multi-view diffusion model (e.g., Zero-1-to-3), denoted as $\epsilon_\phi$.
  - $\psi$: Parameters of the base 2D diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion), denoted as $\epsilon_\psi$.
  - $c_I$: Input image condition (the source image for 3D generation).
  - $c_P$: Camera pose condition for the view to be synthesized.
  - $t$: Diffusion timestep.
  - $\epsilon$: Standard Gaussian noise.
  - $\omega$: Guidance scale.
- **Outputs:**
  - Gradient $\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{USD}}$ to update radiance field parameters $\theta$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- **Latent $\mathbf{z}_t$**: The noisy latent variable at timestep $t$, computed as $\mathbf{z}_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}\mathbf{z} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\epsilon$, where $\mathbf{z}$ is the clean latent and $\alpha_t$ is from the noise scheduler [S1].
- **Conditions**:
  - $c_I$: Image embedding (e.g., CLIP text/image features).
  - $c_P$: Camera pose embedding.
  - $\emptyset$: Dropped condition (represented as a zero tensor or null pointer depending on implementation, but semantically "no condition") [S1].

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **Identify Bias in Multi-View Diffuser:**
    Standard multi-view diffusers like Zero-1-to-3 are trained to predict noise $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P)$ [S1]. To enable Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), they must predict unconditional noise [S1]. However, Zero-1-to-3 only drops $c_I$ during training, keeping $c_P$ [S1]. During inference, it replaces the tensor after the fully connected layer $f(\cdot)$ with zeros [S1]. Since $f(0) \neq 0$, this does not equate to setting $c_P = \emptyset$, resulting in biased unconditional noise predictions $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_P, \emptyset)$ and $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)$ [S1].

2.  **Rectify Bias via Stable Diffusion Unconditional Noise:**
    The bias is rectified by replacing the multi-view model's unconditional noise with the true unconditional noise from a pre-trained 2D diffusion model $\epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset)$ [S1]. This is valid because Zero-1-to-3 and Stable Diffusion share the same Variational Autoencoder (VAE) architecture, making their latent spaces compatible [S1].

3.  **Simplify Guidance Formula:**
    The standard CFG formula for a model with two conditions ($c_I, c_P$) is:
    $$
    \epsilon_\phi^{\text{CFG}}(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) = \alpha_1[\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) - \epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_P, \emptyset)] + \alpha_2[\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_P, \emptyset) - \epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)] + \epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)
    $$
    [S1].
    To eliminate the biased intermediate term $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_P, \emptyset)$, set guidance scales $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = \omega$ [S1]. This yields Conditional Residual Guidance (CRG):
    $$
    \epsilon_\phi^{\text{CRG}}(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) = \omega[\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) - \epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)] + \epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)
    $$
    [S1].

4.  **Substitute Unconditional Noise:**
    Substitute $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset, \emptyset)$ with $\epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset)$ from Stable Diffusion [S1]. The noise difference term for the loss becomes:
    $$
    \epsilon_\phi^{\text{FG}}(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) - \epsilon = \omega [\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) - \epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset)] + \lambda [\epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset) - \epsilon]
    $$
    [S1].

5.  **Remove Regularization Term ($\lambda = 0$):**
    Standard SDS corresponds to $\lambda = 1$ [S1]. Setting $\lambda = 0$ removes the regularization term $[\epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset) - \epsilon]$, which significantly improves the details of the generated 3D content [S1].

6.  **Compute USD Gradient:**
    The final Unbiased Score Distillation loss gradient is:
    $$
    \nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{USD}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, c_P, \epsilon} \left[ w(t) \left[ \omega (\epsilon_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}_t, c_I, c_P) - \epsilon_{\psi}(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset)) \right] \frac{\partial \mathbf{z}_t}{\partial \theta} \right]
    $$
    [S1].
    Here, $w(t)$ is the standard SDS weighting function (e.g., $\frac{\partial \mathbf{z}_t}{\partial \epsilon}$ or similar scheduler-dependent weight) [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch

def compute_usd_gradient(
    nerf_params, 
    camera_pose, 
    input_image_condition, 
    diffusion_model_phi, 
    diffusion_model_psi, 
    vae, 
    t, 
    omega, 
    w_t
):
    # 1. Render a view from the current NeRF (standard volumetric rendering)
    rendered_rgb = render_ne_view(nerf_params, camera_pose)
    
    # 2. Encode rendered image to latent space
    z_clean = vae.encode(rendered_rgb)
    
    # 3. Add noise to latent
    epsilon = torch.randn_like(z_clean)
    alpha_t = get_alpha(t) 
    sqrt_alpha_t = torch.sqrt(alpha_t)
    sqrt_one_minus_alpha_t = torch.sqrt(1 - alpha_t)
    z_t = sqrt_alpha_t * z_clean + sqrt_one_minus_alpha_t * epsilon
    
    # 4. Predict noise from Multi-View Diffuser (phi) with conditions
    epsilon_phi_cond = diffusion_model_phi(z_t, c_I=input_image_condition, c_P=camera_pose)
    
    # 5. Predict unconditional noise from Base 2D Diffuser (psi)
    epsilon_psi_uncond = diffusion_model_psi(z_t, c_I=None, c_P=None)
    
    # 6. Compute the USD noise difference
    usd_noise_diff = omega * (epsilon_phi_cond - epsilon_psi_uncond)
    
    # 7. Compute the gradient
    # Gradient in latent space: w(t) * usd_noise_diff
    grad_z_t = w_t * usd_noise_diff
    
    # Backpropagate through VAE encode: z_t = sqrt_alpha_t * z_clean + ...
    # d(z_t)/d(z_clean) = sqrt_alpha_t
    grad_z_clean = grad_z_t * sqrt_alpha_t
    
    # Compute gradient w.r.t rendered_rgb
    grad_rendered_rgb = torch.autograd.grad(
        outputs=z_clean, 
        inputs=rendered_rgb, 
        grad_outputs=grad_z_clean, 
        retain_graph=True
    )[0]
    
    # Compute gradient w.r.t nerf_params
    gradient = torch.autograd.grad(
        outputs=rendered_rgb, 
        inputs=nerf_params, 
        grad_outputs=grad_rendered_rgb, 
        retain_graph=False
    )[0]
    
    return gradient
```

**Parameters:**
- $\omega$ (Guidance Scale): Typically $>1$. [S1].
- $\lambda$ (Regularization Weight): Set to $0$. Setting $\lambda=1$ reverts to standard SDS [S1].
- $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$: Set to $\omega$ to simplify the CFG formula [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Bias in Zero-1-to-3:** Zero-1-to-3 does not truly train unconditional noise by dropping both conditions. It only drops $c_I$ and keeps $c_P$. During inference, it replaces the tensor after the fully connected layer $f(\cdot)$ with zeros. Since $f(0) \neq 0$, this is not equivalent to setting $c_P=\emptyset$, leading to biased unconditional noise predictions. USD avoids this by using Stable Diffusion's unconditional noise. [S1].
- **Domain Bias:** The dataset used for fine-tuning multi-view diffusers is often object-centric, which may introduce additional domain bias. USD mitigates this by using the general-purpose Stable Diffusion unconditional noise. [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** On GSO, RTMV, and Objaverse datasets (1,000 random images), SD unconditional noise $\epsilon_\psi(\mathbf{z}_t, \emptyset)$ yields superior denoising results compared to Zero-1-to-3 noise. At T=50 noise steps: PSNR 40.26, SSIM 0.992, LPIPS 0.008. Zero-1-to-3 noise with inference setup $\epsilon_\phi(\mathbf{z}_t, 0)$ yields PSNR 36.13, SSIM 0.979, LPIPS 0.015. [S1].
- **Strengths:** Generates high-quality, detailed 3D content with better geometry fidelity than standard SDS. Maintains full freedom in camera pose selection, unlike methods using volume or ray aggregation. [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires access to both a multi-view diffuser and a base 2D diffusion model. Computationally more expensive than single-model distillation due to the two diffusion model forward passes. [S1].
- **When to Use:** When high-fidelity geometry and texture are required, and camera pose flexibility is important. [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are limited, or when a single diffusion model is available. [S1].

## Background
Unbiased Score Distillation (USD) was introduced in the paper "Optimized View and Geometry Distillation from Multi-view Diffuser" [S1]. The work addresses the limitations of existing methods that use multi-view diffusion models (like Zero-1-to-3) for 3D generation [S1]. It identifies a critical bias in the unconditional noise predictions of these models and proposes a rectification strategy using the unconditional noise from a pre-trained 2D diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) [S1]. The method also leverages the radiance field as a consistency prior to ensure multi-view consistency without restricting camera poses [S1].
