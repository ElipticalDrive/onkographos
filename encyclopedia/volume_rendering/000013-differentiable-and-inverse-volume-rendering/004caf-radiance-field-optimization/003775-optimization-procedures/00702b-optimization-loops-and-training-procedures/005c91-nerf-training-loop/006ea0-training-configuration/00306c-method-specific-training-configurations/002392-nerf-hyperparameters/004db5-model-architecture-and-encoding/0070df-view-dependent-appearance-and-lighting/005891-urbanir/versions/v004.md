# UrbanIR

Also called: Urban Scene Inverse Rendering

## Overview
UrbanIR is a method for inverse rendering of large-scale urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination, inferring shape, albedo, visibility, and sun/sky illumination to enable relighting and night simulation [S1]. It differs from standard NeRFs by learning intrinsic attributes (albedo, normal) independent of lighting, and from other relightable methods like NeRF-OSR or FEGR by requiring only single-illumination RGB video without LiDAR or multiple cameras [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multi-frame video of a scene under single illumination with known camera motion. Defined as $\{I_i, E_i, K_i\}$, where $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is the RGB image, $E_i \in SE(3)$ is the camera pose, and $K_i$ is the camera intrinsic matrix [S1].
*   **Output:** A neural scene model capable of rendering novel viewpoints under novel lighting conditions, including intrinsic maps (albedo, normal, semantics) and density [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene representation is built on Instant-NGP [S1], a spatial hash-based voxel NeRF representation.
*   **Feature Hash Table:** A dense feature hash table representing the scene, providing low memory consumption and high efficiency [S1].
*   **MLP Headers:** Individual Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) headers are used to decode each attribute (albedo, normal, semantics, density) given a queried feature at point $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
*   **Scene Field:** The function $F_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ outputs intrinsic attributes independent of viewing angles and lighting: $F_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$, where $\mathbf{a}$ is diffuse albedo, $\mathbf{n}$ is surface normal, $\mathbf{s}$ is semantic vector, and $\sigma$ is density [S1].
*   **Lighting Model:** A 5-DoF sun model encoding sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$ and azimuth/zenith $\psi_{sun}, \phi_{sun}$; a 3-DoF ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$; and a sky dome model inferring sky texture from viewing direction [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Intrinsic Decomposition:** For each camera ray, accumulate predictions from $F(\cdot; \theta)$ along the ray. Multiple points are sampled, and intrinsics (albedo, normal, semantics) are predicted at query pixels [S1].
2.  **Visibility Estimation:** Compute an estimate $\hat{x}(r)$ of the 3D point being shaded by volume rendering depth (substituting $\hat{t} = \sum w_i t_i$). Estimate visibility $V(\hat{x}, \text{sun})$ by computing transmittance along the ray segment between $\hat{x}$ and the light source using volume rendering [S1].
3.  **Rendering:** Combine intrinsic attributes and visibility with the lighting model $\mathbf{L}$ to produce final RGB color $\mathbf{C}$: $\mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{L}), \mathbf{L})$ [S1].
4.  **Optimization:** Jointly optimize scene parameters $\theta$ and lighting $\mathbf{L}$ using a loss function combining rendering loss and regularization terms [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Alpha-composition weight:**
    $$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    where $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
*   **Volume rendering of intrinsics:**
    $$ \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i $$
    where $\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{N}, \mathbf{S}$ are the rendered albedo, normal, and semantic maps, respectively [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Lighting:** Sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$, azimuth $\psi_{sun}$, zenith $\phi_{sun}$, ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$ [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_1$ for regularization (specific value not given in sources) [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Standard NeRFs bake shading, shadow, and specularity into observed color, making them non-relightable [S1].
*   Modeling visibility with a per-scene optimized MLP head is impractical because the sun position changes during relighting, but training data only has one sun position [S1].
*   Explicit geometry models for shadows may not be consistent with other neural fields [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Uses spatial hash-based voxel representation (Instant-NGP) for low memory consumption and high efficiency in training and rendering [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Designed for large-scale, unbounded outdoor scenes [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Works with single monocular video under single illumination; does not require LiDAR or multiple cameras; enables relighting and night simulation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes single illumination in training data; visibility estimation relies on a simplified sun visibility model [S1].
*   **When to use:** Large-scale urban scene inverse rendering from video.
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with multiple dynamic light sources or when LiDAR data is available and preferred for explicit geometry.

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced by Chih-Hao Lin, Bohan Liu, Yi-Ting Chen, Kuan-Sheng Chen, David Forsyth, Jia-Bin Huang, Anand Bhattad, and Shenlong Wang from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It was published in 3D Vision (3DV) 2025 [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of existing relightable NeRF methods that focus on small bounded indoor environments or require multiple lighting sources/LiDAR [S1].
