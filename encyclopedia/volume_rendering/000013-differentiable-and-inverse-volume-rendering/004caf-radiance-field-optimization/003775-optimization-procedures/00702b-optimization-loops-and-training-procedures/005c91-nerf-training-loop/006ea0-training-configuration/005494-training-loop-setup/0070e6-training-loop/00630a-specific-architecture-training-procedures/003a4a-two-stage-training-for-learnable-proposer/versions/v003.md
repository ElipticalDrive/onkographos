# Two-Stage Training for Learnable Proposer

Also called: LPMix training, Two-stage NeRF training with learnable proposer

## Overview
This method solves the "chicken-and-egg" problem in differentiable volume rendering where training a learnable proposer requires a high-quality fine network to provide useful gradients, but the fine network requires informative samples from a good proposer to converge [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from joint training approaches by decoupling the proposer's initialization from the fine network's optimization, ensuring the proposer learns to mimic a robust heuristic baseline before end-to-end refinement [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Training images and camera poses.
- A scene representation parameterized by three networks: Coarse network ($N_c$), Fine network ($N_f$), and Proposer ($P$).
- A heuristic proposer ($P_h$) that generates samples using a fixed strategy (e.g., uniform or stratified sampling).

**Outputs:**
- Optimized parameters for $N_c$, $N_f$, and $P$.
- Rendered images with reduced variance and improved quality compared to joint training.

**Data Structures:**
- **Proposals:** Sets of 1D coordinates along rays. Let $S_h$ be the set of samples from the heuristic proposer and $S_p$ be the set of samples from the learnable proposer.
- **Losses:**
  - Reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{rec}$ (e.g., MSE between rendered and ground truth colors).
  - Proposal matching loss $\mathcal{L}_{match}$ (L2 distance between matched proposals).

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Stage 1: Heuristic Supervision**
    1.  Train $N_c$ and $N_f$ using the heuristic proposer $P_h$ to generate samples. This is identical to vanilla NeRF training.
    2.  Simultaneously train the learnable proposer $P$.
    3.  For each ray, obtain the set of heuristic samples $S_h$ and the set of learned samples $S_p$.
    4.  Match $S_p$ to $S_h$ in a greedy fashion to minimize computational cost [S1, unofficial].
    5.  Compute $\mathcal{L}_{match} = \sum \|s_h - s_p\|^2$ for matched pairs.
    6.  Update $P$ to minimize $\mathcal{L}_{match}$, effectively making $P$ mimic $P_h$ [S1, unofficial].
    7.  At the end of Stage 1, $N_c$ and $N_f$ are equivalent to those trained in vanilla NeRF, and $P$ has learned to predict similar proposal distributions [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Stage 2: End-to-End Refinement**
    1.  Swap $P_h$ with the learned proposer $P$. The fine network $N_f$ now receives samples from $P$.
    2.  Train the entire system ($N_c, N_f, P$) end-to-end.
    3.  Compute the total loss:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rec}(N_f) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{rec}(N_c) $$
        where $\mathcal{L}_{rec}(N_f)$ is the reconstruction loss of the fine network and $\mathcal{L}_{rec}(N_c)$ is the reconstruction loss of the coarse network [S1, unofficial].
    4.  The coarse network $N_c$ is optimized to minimize its own reconstruction loss while also producing features that enable $P$ to provide informative samples to $N_f$ [S1, unofficial].
    5.  The fine network $N_f$ is optimized to minimize its reconstruction loss based on the samples provided by $P$.

**Parameters:**
- **Training Duration:** The number of SGD steps in Stage 1 equals the number of steps in Stage 2. The total training time is kept constant for fair comparison with vanilla NeRF [S1, unofficial].
- **$\lambda$:** Weight for the coarse network reconstruction loss. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Joint Training Failure:** Training $N_c$, $P$, and $N_f$ jointly from scratch can fail to reach good performance or fail badly (e.g., -5 PSNR on Blender: Ship) due to weak supervision (only final ray color) [S1, unofficial].
- **Greedy Matching:** The greedy matching in Stage 1 is used for speed but may not be globally optimal; however, it is sufficient for mimicking the heuristic proposer [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Importance Prediction
The proposer can predict importance scores for samples to reduce computational burden by querying the fine network only on important samples. This is done by regressing an additional set of scalars using the same mechanism as proposal generation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Total training time is kept constant compared to vanilla NeRF, as the two stages are equal in duration [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing parameters for $N_c$, $N_f$, and $P$, plus intermediate gradients. No significant additional memory overhead compared to vanilla NeRF.
- **Strengths:** Avoids the failure modes of joint training by providing a stable initialization for the proposer.
- **Limitations:** Requires two distinct training phases, which may complicate the training pipeline.
- **When to use:** When joint training of a learnable proposer fails to converge or yields poor results.
- **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and the overhead of two stages is prohibitive, or when a heuristic proposer is sufficient.

## Background
The method was introduced in "NeRF in detail: Learning to sample for view synthesis" [S1, unofficial]. It draws inspiration from two-stage object detectors like Faster R-CNN [S1, unofficial], where a proposal network is trained with full supervision. In NeRF, such supervision is not available, so the heuristic proposer serves as a proxy supervisor in Stage 1.
