# Diffusion-Based Guidance for Radiance Field Optimization

Also called: SDS-like, Score Distillation Sampling, Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) Loss, Stable Diffusion Training Guidance

## Overview
Diffusion-Based Guidance for Radiance Field Optimization uses the score function of a pretrained 2D diffusion model to provide gradient signals that guide the optimization of a differentiable radiance field parametrization $z = g(\theta)$ towards the mode of a conditional probability distribution $p(z_t | y)$ [S1]. It solves the problem of non-photorealistic artifacts and incomplete geometry in 3D scenes by leveraging a strong 2D prior to regularize training from viewpoints distant from the original cameras [S2]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [VCCNF Training Loop](4736-vccnf-training-loop.md) by relying on a frozen diffusion model's noise prediction rather than explicit multi-view consistency losses, and from [MVDream Multi-View Diffusion Guidance](6345-mvdream-multi-view-diffusion-guidance.md) by typically using a single-view diffusion prior without explicit multi-view joint optimization [S1][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A differentiable radiance field parametrization $g(\theta)$ (e.g., NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters), a text prompt $y$, and a diffusion model $\epsilon_\phi$ [S1][S2].
*   **Output:** Updated parameters $\theta$ that minimize the SDS loss [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Radiance Field Parameters ($\theta$):** Varies by representation. For NeRF, $\theta$ includes MLP weights and feature vectors [S1]. For 3D Gaussian Splatting, $\theta$ includes $\mathbf{x}$ (position), $\mathbf{s}$ (scale), $\mathbf{r}$ (rotation), $\mathbf{f}$ (features), and $\alpha$ (opacity) [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Diffusion Model:** A pretrained UNet-based model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 2.1) [S2].
*   **Latents:** If using latent diffusion, rendered images are encoded to latents $z_0$ of shape $[B, 4, 64, 64]$ (for 512x512 input) [S3, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Viewpoint Sampling:** Sample random viewpoints around the center of the scene estimated from input cameras [S2]. In 3D, the rendered image $z_0 = g(\theta)$ is obtained by volume rendering or splatting from these viewpoints [S1].
2.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   If operating in pixel space: Convert the rendered image to the diffusion model's dtype. Interpolate to 512x512 if necessary [S3, unofficial].
    *   If operating in latent space: Encode the rendered image using the diffusion model's VAE encoder to obtain latents $z_0$ [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Noise Addition:**
    *   Sample a timestep $t$ uniformly from a range $[t_{min}, t_{max}]$ (e.g., $[0.02, 0.98]$ of total timesteps) [S3, unofficial].
    *   Sample noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ [S1].
    *   Compute noisy latents/image $z_t$ using the diffusion schedule: $z_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} z_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon$ [S1].
4.  **Noise Prediction:**
    *   Prepare text embeddings for the prompt $y$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   For Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), duplicate the noisy input and timesteps. Concatenate positive (conditional) and negative (unconditional) text embeddings [S3, unofficial].
    *   Pass the inputs to the UNet to predict noise: $\hat{\epsilon}_\phi(z_t, y, t)$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Apply CFG: $\hat{\epsilon}_{cfg} = \hat{\epsilon}_{uncond} + s \cdot (\hat{\epsilon}_{cond} - \hat{\epsilon}_{uncond})$, where $s$ is the guidance scale (default 100) [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Gradient Computation:**
    *   Compute the weighting factor $w = (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   Compute the gradient signal: $\nabla_\theta L_{SDS} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon} [ w \cdot (\hat{\epsilon}_{cfg} - \epsilon) \frac{\partial z_0}{\partial \theta} ]$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Note: The term $\frac{\partial z_0}{\partial \theta}$ is obtained via backpropagation through the differentiable renderer [S1].
    *   Handle numerical instability by clamping NaN gradients to 0: $\text{grad} = \text{torch.nan\_to\_num}(\text{grad})$ [S3, unofficial].
6.  **Update:** Perform a gradient descent step on $\theta$ using the computed loss [S3, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Score Function Relation:** The predicted noise $\epsilon$ is related to the score function $\nabla_{z_t} \log p(z_t | y)$ by:
    $$ \epsilon(z_t, y, t) = -\sigma_t \nabla_{z_t} \log p(z_t | y) $$
    where $\sigma_t$ is the noise standard deviation [S1].
*   **SDS Objective:**
    $$ \nabla_\theta L_{SDS} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon} \left[ \gamma(t) (\epsilon_\phi(z_t, y, t) - \epsilon) \frac{\partial z_0}{\partial \theta} \right] $$
    where $\gamma(t)$ is a weighting function dependent on timestep $t$ [S1].
*   **Reconstruction (for reference):** The one-step denoised estimate $\hat{z}_0$ is:
    $$ \hat{z}_0 = \frac{z_t - \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon_\phi(z_t, y, t)}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}} $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Guidance Scale ($s$):** Multiplier for CFG. Default value in code: 100 [S3, unofficial].
*   **SDS Loss Weight:** Typical value: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S2].
*   **Timestep Range ($t_{range}$):** List $[0.02, 0.98]$ used to compute min/max steps as fractions of total timesteps [S3, unofficial].
*   **Step Ratio:** Float controlling timestep selection. $1.0$ maps to $t_{min}$ (early diffusion), $0.0$ maps to $t_{max}$ (late diffusion) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Diffusion Model:** Stable-Diffusion version 2.1 is commonly used [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **NaN Values:** Gradient computation can produce NaN values due to numerical instability in the diffusion process or guidance scaling. Explicitly clamp NaNs to 0 [S3, unofficial].
*   **Over-saturation and Over-smoothing:** SDS is known to encounter issues such as over-saturation and over-smoothing [S1].
*   **Timing:** SDS loss is often enabled after two-thirds of the total iterations, when the majority of scene structure has stabilized [S2].

Uses a frozen or fine-tuned 2D diffusion inpainting model with a binary mask to condition the diffusion model on visible scene context, ensuring complete geometry and proper pose relative to the environment.

Uses multi-view 2D sketches as spatial priors to define edit regions for localized 3D editing, ensuring edits are confined to sketched regions while preserving unedited parts.

### Context-Translation Training with Segmentation Masking
Passes a segmentation mask to the training process where pixels excluded in the stylization process are masked out, calculating the loss only on pixels outside the mask to avoid changing the appearance of masked pixels [S4]. Employs a background loss $L_{BG}$, defined as the L1 distance between the ground truth pixels of the background in the rendered image and the pixels in the background of the GT image, to prevent unwanted style artifacts in the background [S4].

### Style Adaptation via Diffusion Guidance
Uses a reference image as a condition alongside the text prompt to guide generation, allowing the generated content to more closely resemble the visual characteristics of the reference image [S5].

### Blending Refinement Model
Employs two Instruct-Pix2Pix processes as learned regularization to improve quality by purifying noisy mixup images and aligning them with detailed characteristics, using averaged latent codes for stability [S6]. First pass: uses the mixup image as input and the original image as a condition to preserve structural integrity [S6]. Second pass: uses the resulting image from the first pass as input and the mixup image as the condition to align with detailed characteristics [S6]. Introduces $n_r$ noised latent codes (set to 5) updated independently, computing the average of the latent codes and decoding it back into the image [S6]. Input timestep $t$ is set to 0.6, using 3 diffusion steps [S6].

### Masked-NeRF + DreamFusion
A baseline method that uses per-scene text prompts to guide the generation of masked regions using SDS loss [S7]. Gradually and uniformly decreases the maximum noise steps $t_{max}$ during fitting, until it equals the minimum noise steps $t_{min}$ at the last iteration, to improve quality by leveraging higher noise levels for global structure and lower noise levels for details [S7].

### DeepFloyd IF 2D Guidance
A text-to-image guidance approach using DeepFloyd IF, which features an Imagen-like architecture and a T-5 text encoder for stronger text understanding [S8]. Downscales the input image to 64×64 pixels before passing it to DeepFloyd IF due to its modular cascaded design [S8]. Produces better text-3D alignment than StableDiffusion but yields blurrier assets because of the mandatory downsampling [S8].

### Coherent Score Distillation Sampling
Incorporates fine-grained language features through MaskCLIP and FeatUp to enable open-vocabulary querying [S9]. Introduces Coherent Score Distillation Sampling (CSD) to ensure edits remain consistent across multiple views [S9].

### Latent Distil Module
Maps each edited 2D image to a latent edit code vector to characterize diverse edits and control NeRF-to-NeRF translation [S10]. Extracts a 64-dimensional latent edit code from an edited 2D image using a fixed, pre-trained VAE encoder from Stable Diffusion, passed through a 3-layer MLP network that estimates the mean and variance of a Gaussian distribution from which the code is sampled [S10]. Integrated into GenN2N pipeline for text-driven editing, super-resolution, inpainting, and colorization tasks [S10].

### Post-Optimizing Generated Neural Fields via SDS
Iteratively updates initially generated voxels $V$ by rendering viewpoints from the scene and applying SDS loss on each image independently [S11]. For latent diffusion models, the noise prediction step is applied after encoding $r(V, \kappa)$ to the LDM’s latent space [S11]. Optimizes $L_{SDS}$ with negative guidance using classifier-free guidance, which is equivalent to sampling from $p(x|y)/p(x|y')$ at each denoising step [S11]. The parameter $\kappa$ is sampled uniformly in a 6m² region around the origin of the scene with random rotation about the vertical axis [S11].

### DreamPolisher
Utilizes a ControlNet-based network for texture refinement and ensures consistency between different viewpoints with view-consistent geometric guidance [S12, unofficial].

### Image-to-3D Modification (Zero-1-to-3)
Replaces the pre-trained diffusion model used in SDS loss by Zero-1-to-3 XL for image condition [S12, unofficial]. Adds losses between the input image and the corresponding rendered image in the input view to make generation more consistent with the input image [S12, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** High, due to the iterative noise prediction and backpropagation through the diffusion model for each training step [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High, as it requires storing the diffusion model weights and intermediate latents/gradients [S3, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Effective at removing non-photorealistic artifacts and preserving meaningful image details modeled by the diffusion prior [S2].
*   **Scaling:** Performance scales with the quality and capacity of the pretrained diffusion model [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Strong 2D prior leads to photorealistic textures; works with few input views [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Prone to over-saturation and over-smoothing; computationally expensive; may suffer from "over-saturation" and "over-smoothing" [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-fidelity texture synthesis is required and computational resources are available [S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time performance is critical or when the diffusion model's prior conflicts with the desired scene content [S1].

## Background
Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) was introduced as an optimization objective that uses the score function of pretrained diffusion models to guide the optimization of a differentiable image parametrization [S1]. It has demonstrated remarkable results in Text-to-3D generation [S1]. The method was initially applied to 2D image optimization and later extended to 3D radiance fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1][S2]. Stable Diffusion models, particularly version 2.1, are commonly used as the diffusion prior [S2].
