# Strata-NeRF

Also called: Neural Radiance Fields for Stratified Scenes

## Overview
Strata-NeRF is a neural radiance field method designed to model "stratified scenes"—environments that can be deconstructed into distinct hierarchical levels or tiers, such as the exterior and interior of a building, where structural discontinuities exist between levels [S1]. Unlike standard continuous NeRFs that produce cloudy artifacts and white haziness when trained jointly on multiple hierarchical levels, Strata-NeRF uses Vector Quantized (VQ) latent representations to explicitly capture these sudden changes in scene structure [S1]. It achieves this by conditioning a single radiance field network on discrete latent codes generated from 3D positions, allowing for seamless switching between levels while sharing architectural commonalities to reduce memory usage compared to training independent models per level [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and ray parameters $t$ (distance along ray).
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Codebook ($\mathbf{E}$):** A lookup table of discrete latent vectors.
    *   Size: $N \times D$.
    *   Values: $\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ for $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$.
    *   Typical Values: $N=1024$, $D=48$ [S1].
2.  **Latent Generator Encoder ($\mathcal{E}$):** A small MLP mapping positional encoding to latent space.
    *   Architecture: Two linear layers.
    *   Hidden size: 48 [S1].
3.  **Latent Generator Decoder ($\mathcal{D}$):** A small MLP reconstructing positional encoding from latent space.
    *   Architecture: One linear layer.
    *   Hidden size: 96 [S1].
4.  **Latent Router ($\mathcal{R}$):** An MLP mapping quantized latents to modulation parameters for the Radiance Field.
    *   Architecture: Two linear layers.
    *   Hidden size: 256 [S1].
5.  **Radiance Field ($F_\Theta$):** The base NeRF network (based on Mip-NeRF 360 [S1]).
    *   Conditioning: The first two layers are conditioned by the output of $\mathcal{R}$.

**Step-by-Step Method:**
1.  **Positional Encoding:** For each 3D point $\mathbf{x}$ along a ray, compute the Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE), denoted as $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})$ [S1].
2.  **Latent Generation:** Pass $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})$ through the Latent Generator Encoder $\mathcal{E}$ to obtain a continuous latent vector $\mathbf{z}$ [S1].
3.  **Vector Quantization:** Find the nearest vector $\mathbf{z}_e$ in the codebook $\mathbf{E}$ to $\mathbf{z}$ using Euclidean distance. This discrete code $\mathbf{z}_e$ represents the "level" or "stratum" of the scene at that location [S1].
4.  **Latent Routing:** Pass $\mathbf{z}_e$ through the Latent Router $\mathcal{R}$ (an MLP) to generate modulation parameters [S1].
5.  **Radiance Field Conditioning:** Use the output of the Latent Router to condition the first two layers of the Radiance Field MLP. This allows the network to switch its internal representation based on the discrete level code [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Query the conditioned Radiance Field with $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$. Integrate along the ray using standard volume rendering [G].

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss is a weighted sum of reconstruction, distortion, and VQ losses [S1]:

$$
L_{total} = L_{recon}(\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}, t), \mathbf{c}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \lambda_1 L_{dist}(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{w}) + \lambda_2 L_{vq}
$$

Where:
*   $L_{recon}$ is the mean squared error between rendered color $\mathbf{c}$ and ground truth $\mathbf{c}^*$ [S1].
*   $L_{dist}$ is the distortion loss using normalized ray distances $\mathbf{s}$ and weight vector $\mathbf{w}$ [S1].
*   $L_{vq}$ is the VQ-VAE loss defined as:

$$
L_{vq} = ||\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{y}||_2^2 + ||sg(\mathbf{z}_e) - \mathbf{z}||_2^2 + \beta||\mathbf{z}_e - sg(\mathbf{z})||_2^2
$$

Where:
*   $\mathbf{y}$ is the output of the Latent Generator Decoder $\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{z}_e)$ [S1].
*   $\mathbf{z}$ is the encoder output $\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{x}))$ [S1].
*   $sg$ denotes the stop-gradient operator [S1].
*   $\beta$ is a commitment loss weight [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_1$ (distortion loss weight): 0.01 [S1].
*   $\lambda_2$ (VQ loss weight): 0.1 [S1].
*   $\beta$ (commitment loss weight): 1.0 [S1].
*   Codebook Size: 1024 [S1].
*   Latent Dimension: 48 [S1].
*   Training Iterations: 150,000 [S1].
*   Optimizer: Adam, learning rate $1 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Continuous Nature of Standard NeRFs:** Standard NeRFs (like Mip-NeRF 360) produce unsatisfactory results (cloudy views, white artifacts, haziness) when trained jointly on images from all hierarchical levels because their continuous nature is unsuitable for modeling sudden structural changes [S1]. Strata-NeRF addresses this by using discrete VQ latents [S1].
*   **Precision:** The VQ process involves discrete selection, which can lead to quantization errors. The commitment loss ($\beta$) helps stabilize training by encouraging the encoder to output vectors close to codebook entries [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires 150k iterations [S1]. The additional computational overhead from the Latent Generator and Router is minimal as they are lightweight MLPs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The method introduces significantly fewer parameters than training an independent NeRF model for each level, leading to a significant reduction in memory usage [S1]. The codebook size is fixed at 1024 vectors of dimension 48 [S1].
*   **Performance:** Evaluated on a synthetic Stratified Scenes dataset (simple and complex geometry) and the real-world RealEstate10K dataset, Strata-NeRF demonstrates proficiency in depicting stratified scenes, reducing artifacts, and creating high-fidelity views, surpassing current methods like Mip-NeRF 360 [S1, S2].
*   **Strengths:** Handles discontinuities and sudden changes in scene hierarchy; reduces memory usage compared to multi-model approaches; high-fidelity novel views.
*   **Limitations:** Requires a stratified scene structure; performance on non-stratified scenes is not explicitly detailed beyond being a generalization of NeRF.

## Background
Strata-NeRF was proposed in 2023 by researchers including Ankit Tiwari [S1]. It addresses the limitation of existing NeRF methods in modeling "stratified scenes," which are scenes with multiple hierarchical levels (e.g., exterior and interior of a building) [S1]. The method builds on the Mip-NeRF 360 architecture [S1] and incorporates concepts from Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAE) [S1]. It was evaluated on a newly proposed synthetic dataset of stratified scenes and the RealEstate10K dataset [S1].
