# UV Volumes

Also called: UV Volume Representation

## Overview
UV Volumes is a method for real-time, editable free-view rendering of human performers that decouples smooth 3D geometry/density from high-frequency appearance [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF by encoding smooth density and UV coordinates in a 3D feature volume (the UV volume) while storing detailed appearance in a 2D Neural Texture Stack (NTS), enabling real-time performance and retexturing [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view videos of a human performer, a posed SMPL model parameterized by pose $\theta$, and latent codes $\mathbf{z}$ anchored to SMPL vertices [S1].
**Outputs:** A rendered RGB image for a given camera view [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1. **UV Volumes:** A 3D feature volume generated by a 3D sparse CNN, storing feature vectors $f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}, \theta)$ at 3D points $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
2. **Neural Texture Stack (NTS):** A set of 2D texture representations $\mathcal{E}_k$ for each body part $k$, generated by a CNN texture generator $G$ [S1].
3. **SMPL Model:** Provides the posed mesh structure used for ray sampling bounds [S1].

**Steps:**

1. **Generate UV Volumes:**
   Voxelize the latent codes $\mathbf{z}$ anchored on the SMPL vertices. Input these voxels into a 3D sparse CNN to produce the 3D feature volume (UV volumes) [S1].

2. **Ray Sampling:**
   For a camera ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample $N$ points $\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^N$ along the ray between near and far bounds. The bounds are determined by the intersection of the ray with the posed SMPL model [S1].

3. **Feature Query and Density Prediction:**
   For each sample point $\mathbf{x}_i$, query the feature vector $f(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{z}, \theta)$ from the UV volumes. Predict the volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$ using a shallow MLP $M_\sigma$:
   $$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) = M_\sigma(f(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{z}, \theta)) $$
   where $M_\sigma$ is a shallow MLP [S1].

4. **Volume Rendering of Feature Map:**
   Render the feature volume into a 2D feature map $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{r})$ using standard volume rendering. Calculate the accumulated transmittance $T_i$ and the weighted sum of features:
   $$ T_i = \exp \left( - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{x}_j) \delta_j \right) $$
   $$ \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) \delta_i)) f(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{z}, \theta) $$
   where $\delta_i = \|\mathbf{x}_{i+1} - \mathbf{x}_i\|_2$ is the distance between adjacent sampled points [S1].

5. **UV Image Generation:**
   Decode the feature map $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{r})$ pixel-by-pixel using an MLP $M_{uv}$ to predict part assignments $\hat{\mathcal{P}}$ and UV coordinates $\hat{\mathcal{U}}, \hat{\mathcal{V}}$:
   $$ \left(\hat{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{r}), \hat{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{r}), \hat{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{r})\right) = M_{uv}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{r})) $$
   The part assignments satisfy $\sum_{k=1}^{N_k} \hat{\mathcal{P}}_k(\mathbf{r}) = 1$ [S1].

6. **Generate Neural Texture Stack (NTS):**
   Use a CNN texture generator $G$ conditioned on the pose $\theta$ and one-hot labels $\mathbf{k}$ for each body part to generate the NTS $\mathcal{E}$:
   $$ \mathcal{E}_k = G(\theta, \mathbf{k}) $$
   The body surface is subdivided into $N_k = 24$ parts [S1].

7. **Color Reconstruction:**
   Interpolate texture embeddings from the NTS using the predicted UV coordinates and part assignments. For each pixel, the final color is recovered by querying the NTS at the predicted UV coordinates weighted by the part assignments [S1].

**Parameters:**
* $N_k = 24$: Number of body parts used to subdivide the body surface for the NTS [S1].
* $N$: Number of samples along the ray [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* **SMPL Dependency:** Ray sampling bounds rely on the accuracy of the posed SMPL model [S1].
* **Part Assignment:** The part assignments $\hat{\mathcal{P}}$ must sum to 1 [S1].

## Operating characteristics
* **Performance:** Renders 960 × 540 images at an average of 30 FPS on CMU Panoptic, ZJU Mocap, and H36M datasets [S1].
* **Strengths:** Real-time rendering, editability (retexturing), and better generalization on novel poses and shapes due to the mapping between the parameterized human model and smooth texture coordinates [S1].
* **Limitations:** Relies on the SMPL model for geometry and sampling [S1].

## Background
UV Volumes was introduced to address the high computational costs of real-time free-view human rendering in VR/AR applications [S1]. It builds on the idea of separating smooth 3D properties from high-frequency appearance, leveraging the SMPL model for pose-dependent texture generation [S1].
