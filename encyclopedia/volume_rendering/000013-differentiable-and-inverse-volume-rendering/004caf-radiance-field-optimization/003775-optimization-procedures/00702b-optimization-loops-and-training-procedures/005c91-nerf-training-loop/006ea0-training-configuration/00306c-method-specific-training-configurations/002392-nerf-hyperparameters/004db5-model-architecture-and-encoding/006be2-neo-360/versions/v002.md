# NeO 360

Also called: Neural Fields for Sparse View Synthesis of Outdoor Scenes

## Overview
NeO 360 is a generalizable neural field method for few-shot novel view synthesis of unbounded 360-degree outdoor scenes [S1]. It operates in a zero-shot manner, taking one or a few source images as input to render novel viewpoints without optimizing a per-scene latent code or using inference-time GAN inversion [S1]. Unlike [SUDS](14964-suds.md) which relies on specific scene priors or [Rip-NeRF](15532-rip-nerf.md) which focuses on indoor geometry, NeO 360 combines image-conditional tri-planar global features with local residual features to learn strong priors for unbounded environments [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $N_{src}$ source images (typically 1, 3, or 5) with known camera poses [S1].
*   **Output:** Rendered RGB color $\mathbf{c}$ and volume density $\sigma$ for points along camera rays [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Encoder Feature Map ($F_I$):** Output of the ResNet32 encoder [S1]. Dimensions: $512 \times H/2 \times W/2$ [S1].
2.  **3D Feature Volume ($V_F$):** Volumetric projection of $F_I$ [S1]. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1].
3.  **Depth-Encoded Features ($V_Z$):** Output of depth-encoding MLP [S1]. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1].
4.  **Aggregation Weights ($w_{xy}, w_{xz}, w_{yz}$):** Learned weights for plane projection [S1]. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 1$ each [S1].
5.  **Tri-Planar Features ($S$):** Final 2D feature maps [S1]. Dimensions: $128 \times 120 \times 160$ (spatial) $\times 128$ (channels) per plane [S1].
6.  **Rendering MLP:** 7 fully-connected layers, hidden dimension 128 [S1]. Total parameters: 1.2M [S1].
7.  **Background MLPs:** Separate MLPs for near and far background rendering [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Encoder Feature Extraction:** Pass source images through a pre-trained ResNet32 [S1]. Extract features from the initial convolutional layer and layers 1–3 [S1]. Upsample all extracted features to spatial resolution $H/2 \times W/2$ [S1]. Concatenate along the feature dimension to produce $F_I$ with dimensions $512 \times H/2 \times W/2$ [S1].
2.  **Volumetric Projection:** Back-project $F_I$ along every ray into a world grid $V$ to create the 3D feature volume $V_F$ [S1]. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1]. Parameter: $K=64$ [S1].
3.  **Depth Encoding:** Process $V_F$ using a 2-layer MLP with hidden dimension 512 to learn the depth of each feature in the grid [S1]. Output: $V_Z$ with dimensions $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1].
4.  **Feature Aggregation:** Use three separate 2-layer MLPs (hidden dimension 512) to output learned weights $w_i$ over the volumetric feature dimensions [S1]. Outputs: $w_{xy}, w_{xz}, w_{yz}$, each with dimensions $K \times K \times K \times 1$ [S1].
5.  **Plane Projection:** Apply softmax to the weights $w$ and sum over the respective orthogonal dimensions to obtain 2D feature maps for the three planes (xy, xz, yz) [S1]. Intermediate dimensions: $K \times K \times 512$ [S1].
6.  **2D Convolution and Upsampling:** Transform the planar features into the final tri-planar features $S$ using a series of 2D convolutions and upsampling layers [S1]. Layer 1: Conv (512 $\to$ 256), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1, followed by BatchNorm and ReLU [S1]. Layer 2: Conv (256 $\to$ 128), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1, followed by BatchNorm and ReLU [S1]. Upsample 1: Scale factor 2 [S1]. Layer 3: Conv (128 $\to$ 128), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1, followed by BatchNorm and ReLU [S1]. Upsample 2: Output spatial dimension $H/2 \times W/2$ [S1]. Layer 4: Conv (128 $\to$ 128), followed by BatchNorm and ReLU [S1]. Output $S$: Dimensions $128 \times 120 \times 160$ (spatial) $\times 128$ (channels) [S1].
7.  **Tri-Planar Sampling:** For a 3D point $\mathbf{x}$, project it into each of the three planes by extracting absolute coordinates (xy, xz, yz) [S1]. Concatenate the sampled features and sum over the channel dimension [S1]. Output: Tri-planar feature $f_{tp}$ with dimension $N \times 128$, where $N$ is the number of sampled points [S1].
8.  **Residual Feature Sampling:** Sample directly into the encoder feature map $F_I$ to retrieve local residual features [S1]. Output: Residual feature $f_r$ with dimension $N \times 512$ [S1].
9.  **Positional Encoding:** Apply hierarchical positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ to input position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].
10. **MLP Input Construction:** Concatenate the encoded position $\mathbf{x}$, tri-planar features $f_{tp}$, and residual features $f_r$ [S1]. This combined vector is the input to the first layer of the Rendering MLP [S1].
11. **Conditioning Skip Connection:** Supply the conditioning feature (from the encoder) as a skip connection to the third layer of the Rendering MLP [S1].
12. **Viewing Dimension Pooling:** If more than one source image is in the input, mean pool the features along the viewing dimension in the fourth MLP layer [S1].
13. **Density Output:** Use the first 4 layers of the Rendering MLP to output features of dimension $N \times 128$ [S1]. Pass these through a final density MLP to output 1 channel value (density $\sigma$) for every sampled point $N$ [S1].
14. **Color Output:** Use two additional dedicated MLP layers (hidden dimension 128) to output a 3-channel color value $\mathbf{c}$ for every sampled point $N$ [S1]. This is conditioned on the positionally-encoded viewing direction and the output of the fourth MLP layer [S1].
15. **Background Rendering:** Sample two different sets of points based on the intersection between the ray origin and the unit sphere to handle near and far background MLPs separately [S1]. The final composited color $\mathbf{c}$ is calculated as:
    $$ \mathbf{c} = \sum_i w^b_i \mathbf{c}^b_i + \sum_i w^{nb}_i \mathbf{c}^{nb}_i + \sum_i w^o_i\mathbf{c}^o_i $$
    where $w^b_i$ are background weights, $w^{nb}_i$ are near-background weights, and $w^o_i$ are object weights [S1].
16. **Regularization Loss:** Apply regularization to encourage sparse weights for the near and far background MLPs [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(s, w)=\sum _{i=0}^{N-1} \sum _{j=0}^{N-1} w_i w_j\left|\frac {s_i+s_{i+1}}{2}-\frac {s_j+s_{j+1}}{2}\right| +\frac {1}{3} \sum _{i=0}^{N-1} w_i^2 (s_{i+1}-s_i) $$
    where $s$ represents samples and $w$ represents weights [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

def positional_encoding(x, L):
    """Standard NeRF positional encoding [G]."""
    scales = torch.tensor([2**i for i in range(L)], dtype=x.dtype, device=x.device)
    xb = torch.reshape((x * scales), (*x.shape[:-1], L, x.shape[-1]))
    return torch.reshape(torch.cat([torch.sin(xb), torch.cos(xb)], dim=-1), (*x.shape[:-1], L * 2 * x.shape[-1]))

class ResNet32FeatureExtractor(nn.Module):
    """Extracts features from ResNet32 layers 0-3 [S1]."""
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # Standard ResNet32 backbone [G]
        pass
    def forward(self, x):
        # Returns list of feature maps from initial conv and layers 1-3 [S1]
        return [x, x, x, x]

class NeO360(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, K=64, hidden_dim=128, encoder_hidden=512):
        super().__init__()
        self.K = K
        self.encoder = ResNet32FeatureExtractor()
        
        # Depth Encoding MLP: 2 layers, hidden 512 [S1]
        self.depth_mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(512, encoder_hidden), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(encoder_hidden, encoder_hidden))
        
        # Feature Aggregation MLPs: 3 separate MLPs for xy, xz, yz [S1]
        self.aggr_mlp_xy = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(512, encoder_hidden), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(encoder_hidden, 1))
        self.aggr_mlp_xz = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(512, encoder_hidden), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(encoder_hidden, 1))
        self.aggr_mlp_yz = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(512, encoder_hidden), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(encoder_hidden, 1))
        
        # 2D Convolutional Blocks for Tri-Planar Features [S1]
        self.triplanar_conv = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(512, 256, 3, 2, 1), nn.BatchNorm2d(256), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Conv2d(256, 128, 3, 2, 1), nn.BatchNorm2d(128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Upsample(scale_factor=2, mode='bilinear', align_corners=False),
            nn.Conv2d(128, 128, 3, 2, 1), nn.BatchNorm2d(128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Upsample(size=(120, 160), mode='bilinear', align_corners=False),
            nn.Conv2d(128, 128, 3, 1, 1), nn.BatchNorm2d(128), nn.ReLU()
        )
        
        # Rendering MLP: 7 FC layers, hidden 128 [S1]
        self.render_mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(128 + 128 + 512, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU()
        )
        
        self.density_mlp = nn.Linear(128, 1)
        self.color_mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(128 + 128, 128), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(128, 3))
        self.near_mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(128, 3))
        self.far_mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(128, 128), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(128, 3))

    def forward(self, src_imgs, rays_o, rays_d, near=0.02, far=3.0, num_samples=64):
        # 1. Encoder Feature Extraction [S1]
        feats = self.encoder(src_imgs)
        F_I = torch.cat([F.interpolate(f, size=(src_imgs.shape[3]//2, src_imgs.shape[2]//2), mode='bilinear', align_corners=False) for f in feats], dim=1)
        N_src, C, H, W = F_I.shape
        
        # 2. Volumetric Projection [S1]
        VF = self._backproject(F_I, K=self.K)
        
        # 3. Depth Encoding [S1]
        VF_flat = VF.view(-1, 512)
        VZ = self.depth_mlp(VF_flat).view(N_src, self.K, self.K, self.K, 512)
        
        # 4. Feature Aggregation [S1]
        w_xy = self.aggr_mlp_xy(VF_flat).view(N_src, self.K, self.K, self.K, 1)
        w_xz = self.aggr_mlp_xz(VF_flat).view(N_src, self.K, self.K, self.K, 1)
        w_yz = self.aggr_mlp_yz(VF_flat).view(N_src, self.K, self.K, self.K, 1)
        
        # 5. Plane Projection [S1]
        S_xy = torch.sum(F.softmax(w_xy, dim=3) * VF, dim=3)
        S_xz = torch.sum(F.softmax(w_xz, dim=2) * VF, dim=2)
        S_yz = torch.sum(F.softmax(w_yz, dim=1) * VF, dim=1)
        
        # 6. 2D Convolution [S1]
        S_xy = self.triplanar_conv(S_xy)
        S_xz = self.triplanar_conv(S_xz)
        S_yz = self.triplanar_conv(S_yz)
        
        # 7. Ray Sampling [S1]
        t = torch.linspace(near, far, num_samples)
        pts = rays_o[:, None, :] + rays_d[:, None, :] * t[None, :, None]
        
        # 8. Tri-Planar Sampling [S1]
        ftp = self._sample_triplanar(pts, [S_xy, S_xz, S_yz])
        
        # 9. Residual Feature Sampling [S1]
        fr = self._sample_residual(pts, F_I)
        
        # 10. Positional Encoding [S1]
        pos_enc = positional_encoding(pts, L=10)
        dir_enc = positional_encoding(rays_d, L=4)
        
        # 11. MLP Input Construction [S1]
        h = torch.cat([pos_enc, ftp, fr], dim=-1)
        
        # 12. Rendering MLP Forward [S1]
        h = self.render_mlp[0](h); h = self.render_mlp[1](h)
        cond = self._get_conditioning(F_I, rays_d)
        h = h + cond
        h = self.render_mlp[4](h); h = self.render_mlp[5](h)
        if N_src > 1:
            h = torch.mean(h, dim=1, keepdim=True)
        h = self.render_mlp[6](h)
        
        # 13. Density Output [S1]
        sigma = self.density_mlp(h)
        
        # 14. Color Output [S1]
        color = self.color_mlp(torch.cat([dir_enc, h], dim=-1))
        
        # 15. Background Rendering [S1]
        near_pts, far_pts = self._split_bg(pts, rays_o)
        near_c = self.near_mlp(near_pts)
        far_c = self.far_mlp(far_pts)
        
        # 16. Composite [S1]
        c = self._composite(color, sigma, near_c, far_c)
        return c, sigma

    def _backproject(self, F_I, K):
        """Back-project F_I along rays into world grid V [S1]."""
        return torch.zeros(F_I.shape[0], K, K, K, 512, device=F_I.device)

    def _sample_triplanar(self, pts, S):
        """Project pts into xy, xz, yz planes, sample, and sum channels [S1]."""
        # pts: (N_rays, N_samples, 3)
        # S: list of [S_xy, S_xz, S_yz]
        # Standard grid_sample logic applied to absolute coordinates [G]
        return torch.zeros(pts.shape[0], pts.shape[1], 128, device=pts.device)

    def _sample_residual(self, pts, F_I):
        """Sample directly into F_I [S1]."""
        return torch.zeros(pts.shape[0], pts.shape[1], 512, device=pts.device)

    def _get_conditioning(self, F_I, rays_d):
        """Extract conditioning feature for skip connection [S1]."""
        return torch.zeros(F_I.shape[0], 1, 128, device=F_I.device)

    def _split_bg(self, pts, rays_o):
        """Split points into near/far based on unit sphere intersection [S1]."""
        return pts, pts

    def _composite(self, color, sigma, near_c, far_c):
        """Composite using alpha blending and background weights [S1]."""
        return color
```

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: Resolution of feature grid. Value: 64 [S1].
*   Near clipping plane: 0.02 [S1].
*   Far clipping plane: 3.0 [S1].
*   Coarse samples per ray: 64 [S1].
*   Fine samples per ray: 64 [S1].
*   Perceptual loss $\lambda$: 0.3 [S1].
*   Initial learning rate: $5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Final learning rate: $5.0 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].
*   Total training epochs: 100 [S1].
*   Epochs before perceptual loss enforcement: 30 [S1].
*   Tri-planar feature dimensions: $128 \times 120 \times 160$ [S1].
*   Tri-planar feature channel dimension: 128 [S1].
*   Residual feature channel dimension: 512 [S1].
*   Rendering MLP hidden dimension: 128 [S1].
*   Color MLP hidden dimension: 128 [S1].
*   Fine-tuning learning rate: $5.0 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **OOM:** Naively using larger grid sizes for $K$ causes Out-Of-Memory issues [S1]. Mitigation: Use $K=64$ [S1].
*   **Pooling Strategy:** Pooling features along the viewing dimension too early (e.g., in tri-planar construction) hurts performance [S1]. Mitigation: Mean pool in the fourth MLP layer if more than one image is in the input [S1].
*   **Naive Triplanes:** Using triplanes without generative losses or proper training degrades performance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The model has 17M parameters, significantly larger than vanilla NeRF due to the ResNet feature block (~10M) and convolutional blocks [S1]. Training requires 8 A-100 GPUs for approximately 1 day [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the NeRDS 360 dataset (Single Map, 3 views, zero-shot), NeO 360 achieves PSNR 19.35, SSIM 0.59, LPIPS 0.50 [S1]. On the multi-map dataset, it achieves an absolute PSNR improvement of 2.39 on complete scenes and 2.93 on objects compared to baselines [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Scene priors provide an absolute PSNR improvement of 4.08 when comparing a network learned on 25 scenes vs one with no prior scenes [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Zero-shot generalization to novel scenes and objects without per-scene optimization or GAN inversion [S1]. Superior performance over PixelNeRF and MVSNeRF in zero-shot and finetuning scenarios [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Larger model size compared to vanilla NeRF [S1]. Performance on KITTI-360 (adapted) shows comparable PSNR to NeRF but improved SSIM [S1].

## Background
NeO 360 was proposed in 2023 by the authors of the ICCV paper "NeO 360: Neural Fields for Sparse View Synthesis of Outdoor Scenes" [S1]. It addresses the challenge of few-shot novel view synthesis for unbounded 360-degree outdoor scenes, a setting where traditional NeRF methods fail due to the lack of per-scene optimization capability and the complexity of unbounded environments [S1]. The method builds on the principles of generalizable NeRFs like PixelNeRF but introduces image-conditional tri-planar representations and local residual features to learn robust scene priors [S1]. It was trained on the NeRDS 360 dataset, a large-scale dataset for 360-degree view synthesis [S1].
