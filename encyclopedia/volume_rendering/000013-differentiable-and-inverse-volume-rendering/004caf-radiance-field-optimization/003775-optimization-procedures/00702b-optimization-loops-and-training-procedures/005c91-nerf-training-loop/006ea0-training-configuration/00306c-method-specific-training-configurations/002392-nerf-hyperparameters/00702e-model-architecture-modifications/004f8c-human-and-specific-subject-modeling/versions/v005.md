# Human and Specific Subject Modeling


## Overview
This family of methods extends static NeRF architectures to model dynamic human subjects, enabling novel view synthesis and pose extrapolation for animatable avatars [G]. It addresses the limitations of standard NeRF, which overfits to static scenes and lacks the structural priors necessary to generalize across different poses or unseen subjects [G]. By integrating parametric body models (like SMPL) or deformable mesh representations with neural radiance fields, these methods decouple geometry and appearance, allowing for high-fidelity rendering of complex human dynamics and clothing details that implicit fields alone struggle to capture [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [LISA](12036-lisa.md)
LISA is a part-based implicit neural representation method specifically designed for modeling human hands. It addresses the complexity of hand geometry by approximating the hand as a collection of rigid parts, leveraging the MANO (Modeling ANd posing Object) parametric hand model. Unlike general body avatars, LISA explicitly handles the articulated nature of fingers and the potential for self-occlusion or object interaction through physical constraints. It distinguishes itself from siblings like UVA and HumanNeRF by combining MANO-based pose parameterization with Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) for shape representation and an explicit color field for appearance.

### [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md)
Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA) is a neural radiance field representation for dynamic human avatars that enables local and independent editing of both geometry and texture while supporting novel view synthesis and pose rendering. It distinguishes itself from methods relying on pre-defined UV mapping or two-stage distillation by anchoring structured latent codes to a deformable canonical mesh, allowing direct manipulation of 3D space without 3D-to-2D projection ambiguities.

### [HumanNeRF](13842-humannerf.md)
HumanNeRF is a generalizable neural representation for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans that generalizes to unseen subjects and camera settings using sparse RGB inputs, avoiding per-scene training through aggregated pixel-alignment features and a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field. It differs from its siblings by combining image-based rendering with NeRF-based volume rendering into a novel neural blending scheme to address artifacts and lack of high-frequency details in synthesized textures, which are common in methods relying solely on implicit texture representation.

### [HumanNeRF-SE](15640-humannerf-se.md)
HumanNeRF-SE is a method for synthesizing novel pose images of humans from monocular video inputs by combining explicit SMPL shape priors with an implicit NeRF representation. It solves the problem of overfitting and high computational complexity inherent in previous NeRF-based human avatars, which typically require large numbers of optimizable parameters and multi-camera data. Unlike sibling methods such as [HumanNeRF](13842-humannerf.md) and [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md) that rely on frame-level features or learned blend weights prone to overfitting, HumanNeRF-SE uses frozen SMPL blending weights for rigid deformation and point-level spatial features for non-rigid refinement, ensuring pose generalization without pose leakage.

### [Dynamic Semantic Human NeRF](21763-dynamic-semantic-human-nerf.md)
Dynamic Semantic Human NeRF extends the radiance field framework to model dynamic human motion by learning a consistent continuous function $G_\omega$ for geometry and appearance variations across frames. It solves the problem of reconstructing temporally coherent 3D humans from video sequences by warping observations into a canonical reference frame using the imGHUM prior. It sets itself apart from static human NeRF variants by conditioning the neural fields on semantic latent codes (shape $\beta$, pose $\theta$, and root transformation $T$), enabling generalization to novel poses and shapes through interpolation.

## Background
The modeling of dynamic human subjects in neural radiance fields emerged as a distinct sub-area following the success of static NeRF [G]. Early works like NeuralBody demonstrated the potential but suffered from overfitting and poor generalization to unseen poses [S2, unofficial]. Subsequent research focused on animating subjects under novel poses, with notable contributions including NeuMan [S1]. The field expanded to include face avatars with constraints based on animating expressions or face topology, building upon pioneering research in HyperNeRF and NeRFies [G]. HumanNeRF generalizes to unseen subjects and camera settings using sparse RGB inputs, avoiding per-scene training through aggregated pixel-alignment features [S2, unofficial]. Another variant, HumanNeRF (Monocular), shows how to train a neural radiance field for a specific actor based on monocular input data [S2, unofficial]. LISA targets hand modeling by approximating hands with rigid parts and leveraging the MANO parametric model [S1][S2, unofficial].
