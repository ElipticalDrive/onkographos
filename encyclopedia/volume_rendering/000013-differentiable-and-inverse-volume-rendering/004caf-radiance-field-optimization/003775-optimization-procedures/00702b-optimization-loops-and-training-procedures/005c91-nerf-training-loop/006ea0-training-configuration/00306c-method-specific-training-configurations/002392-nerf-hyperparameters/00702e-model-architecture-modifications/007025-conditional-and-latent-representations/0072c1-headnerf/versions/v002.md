# HeadNerf

Also called: NeRF-based parametric head model

## Overview
HeadNeRF is a NeRF-based parametric head model that integrates neural radiance fields into the parametric representation of the human head to render high-fidelity head images in real-time while supporting direct control over rendering pose and semantic attributes (identity, expression, albedo, illumination) [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF by conditioning the implicit function on low-dimensional latent codes rather than just spatial coordinates and view directions, and it accelerates rendering by combining volumetric rendering with a 2D neural refinement network [S1]. This approach allows for the generation of novel views and semantic editing (e.g., changing expression or identity) without the computational cost of full volumetric integration for the final high-resolution image [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Latent codes: $z_{id}$ (identity), $z_{exp}$ (expression), $z_{alb}$ (albedo), $z_{ill}$ (illumination) [S1].
    *   Camera parameters $P$ (extrinsic and intrinsic matrices) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Final rendered image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 256 \times 256}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Implicit Function $h_\theta$:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that maps encoded spatial positions and latent codes to density and feature vectors [S1].
*   **2D Neural Rendering Module $\pi_\psi$:** A 2D convolutional network that refines low-resolution feature maps into high-resolution images [S1].

**Method Steps:**

1.  **Latent Code Extraction/Definition:**
    The model assumes four independent semantic factors. Identity ($z_{id}$) and expression ($z_{exp}$) control the geometry (density), while albedo ($z_{alb}$) and illumination ($z_{ill}$) control the appearance (color/feature) [S1][S3]. These codes can be extracted from input images or sampled [S1][S3].

2.  **Implicit Function Evaluation:**
    For a 3D point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ sampled along a ray $r(t)$ emitted from the camera center, the implicit function $h_\theta$ predicts the density $\sigma$ and an intermediate high-dimensional feature vector $F(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ [S1].
    
    The input to $h_\theta$ is the concatenation of the positional encoding $\gamma(x)$, the identity code $z_{id}$, and the expression code $z_{exp}$ [S1].
    
    $$ h_\theta : (\gamma(x), z_{id}, z_{exp}) \rightarrow (\sigma, F_{inter}) $$
    
    The albedo $z_{alb}$ and illumination $z_{ill}$ codes are concatenated with $F_{inter}$ to predict the final feature vector $F(x)$ [S1].
    
    $$ F(x) = \text{MLP}_{color}(F_{inter}, z_{alb}, z_{ill}) $$
    
    *Note:* The viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ is explicitly removed from the input to avoid capturing dataset biases and inconsistencies [S1].

3.  **Volumetric Rendering (Low-Resolution):**
    A low-resolution 2D feature map $I_F \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 32 \times 32}$ is obtained by volume rendering along each ray $r$ [S1]. The weight $w(t)$ is defined as:
    
    $$ w(t) = \exp\left(-\int_t^\infty \sigma(r(s)) ds\right) \cdot \sigma(r(t)) $$
    
    The feature map value for ray $r$ is:
    
    $$ I_F(r) = \frac{\int_0^\infty w(t) \cdot F(r(t)) dt}{\int_0^\infty w(t) dt} $$
    
    Since $\int_0^\infty w(t) dt$ represents the total opacity (which is 1 if the ray is fully opaque or normalized), the denominator is often simplified or handled as a normalization factor [S1].

4.  **2D Neural Refinement:**
    The low-resolution feature map $I_F$ is mapped to the final high-resolution image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 256 \times 256}$ using the 2D neural rendering module $\pi_\psi$ [S1].
    
    $$ I = \pi_\psi(I_F) $$
    
    The module $\pi_\psi$ consists of 1x1 Conv2D layers and Leaky ReLU activations [S1]. The resolution is gradually increased through a series of upsampling layers (similar to StyleNeRF) to alleviate multi-view inconsistent artifacts [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Feature vector dimension: 256 [S1].
*   Low-res feature map size: $32 \times 32$ [S1].
*   Final image size: $256 \times 256$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Viewing Direction:** Removing the viewing direction prevents the model from learning view-dependent shading artifacts present in the training data, but it may limit the ability to model complex specularities unless captured via the illumination code [S1].
*   **Multi-view Inconsistency:** The 2D refinement module is specifically designed to handle inconsistencies that arise from the coarse volumetric rendering and the parametric nature of the latent codes [S1].

## Subtopics

### Disentangled Conditional NeRF
Separates shape and appearance conditioning more explicitly using distinct latent spaces for deformation and color, often using separate MLP branches [16793-disentangled-conditional-nerf.md].

### Conditional and Latent Representations
The parent framework describing the general mechanism of conditioning NeRF MLPs on latent vectors $\mathbf{z}$ [15989-conditional-and-latent-representations.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Speed:** Rendering time per frame is reduced from ~5 seconds (standard NeRF) to 25 milliseconds, exceeding 40 fps on modern GPUs [S1].
*   **Quality:** Maintains high fidelity, capturing fine details such as gaps between teeth, wrinkles, and beards [S1].
*   **Control:** Supports direct semantic control over identity, expression, albedo, and illumination [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The method relies on the assumption that head geometry is aligned to a central template (3DMM alignment) [S1]. It is primarily designed for head/face rendering and may not generalize to highly non-rigid articulated body motion without modification [S2, unofficial].

## Background
HeadNeRF was proposed by Huang et al. in 2022 [S1]. It builds upon the success of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [35] and parametric head models (like 3DMM). The work addresses the computational bottleneck of original NeRF rendering by integrating a 2D neural renderer, a strategy previously explored in works like StyleNeRF [16]. It is widely used in applications such as identity recognition, face analysis, and film/game production [S1].
