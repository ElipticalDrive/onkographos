# PBR-NeRF

Also called: Inverse Rendering with Physics-Based Neural Fields, Physics-Based Neural Fields

## Overview
PBR-NeRF is an inverse rendering model that jointly estimates scene geometry, materials, and illumination by extending the NeILF/NeILF++ neural fields framework with two novel physics-based priors: Conservation of Energy and NDF-weighted Specular Losses [S1]. It solves the material-lighting ambiguity inherent in standard NeRF approaches by enforcing physical validity on the estimated BRDF, preventing issues such as specular highlights being "baked in" to the diffuse albedo [S1]. Unlike NeILF/NeILF++, which use a Lambertian prior that assumes perfectly rough, non-metallic materials and suppresses the specular lobe, PBR-NeRF uses physics-based losses that enhance physical validity without sacrificing expressiveness [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics), RGB images, and optionally ground-truth geometry (for evaluation) [G].
*   **Outputs:** Estimated outgoing radiance $L_o(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)$ for novel view synthesis, and spatially varying material fields: albedo $\mathbf{b} \in [0, 1]^3$, metallicness $m \in [0, 1]$, and roughness $r \in [0, 1]$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
The framework consists of three implicit neural networks (MLPs) [S1]:
1.  **NeRF SDF:** Predicts volume density $\sigma$ (geometry) and color $\mathbf{c}$ (estimating outgoing radiance $L_o$) [S1].
2.  **BRDF MLP:** Predicts the Disney BRDF parameters: albedo $\mathbf{b}$, roughness $r$, and metallicness $m$ [S1].
3.  **NeILF MLP:** Predicts incident radiance $L_i(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i)$ [S1].

**Rendering Integral Approximation**
The outgoing radiance is approximated using a fixed set $S_L$ of incident directions $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i$ generated by Fibonacci sampling [S1]:
$$ L_o(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) = \frac{2\pi}{|S_L|} \sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \in S_L} f_r(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) L_i(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i) (\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}) $$
where $|S_L| = 256$ [S1], $\mathbf{n}$ is the surface normal, and $f_r$ is the BRDF.

**BRDF Model**
The model uses a simplified Disney BRDF [11] decomposed into diffuse and specular lobes [S1]:
$$ f_r(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) = f_d(\mathbf{x}) + f_s(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) $$

1.  **Diffuse Lobe:**
    $$ f_d(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1-m(\mathbf{x})}{\pi} \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{x}) $$
2.  **Specular Lobe (Microfacet Model):**
    $$ f_s(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i) = \frac{D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h) F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_o, \boldsymbol{\omega}_h) G(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o, \mathbf{n})}{4(\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}_i)(\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)} $$
    where $\boldsymbol{\omega}_h = \frac{\boldsymbol{\omega}_o + \boldsymbol{\omega}_i}{||\boldsymbol{\omega}_o + \boldsymbol{\omega}_i||}$ is the normalized halfway vector [S1].
3.  **Normal Distribution Function (NDF):**
    Uses a Spherical Gaussian approximation controlled by roughness $r$ [S1]:
    $$ D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h) = \frac{1}{\pi r^4} \exp \left( \frac{2}{r^4}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h \cdot \mathbf{n} - 1) \right) $$

**Physics-Based Losses**
Two novel losses constrain the BRDF [S1]:

1.  **Conservation of Energy Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$):**
    Enforces that reflected radiance weights do not exceed unity [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \max \left( \left( \frac{2\pi}{|S_L|} \sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \in S_L} f_r (\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}) \right) - 1, 0 \right) $$
    This penalizes physically invalid BRDFs that reflect more energy than received [S1].

2.  **NDF-weighted Specular Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}}$):**
    Promotes separation of specular and diffuse lobes by penalizing the diffuse lobe’s magnitude in specular regions [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}} = \frac{1}{|S_L|} \sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \in S_L} \operatorname{softmax} \left( \frac{\operatorname{detach}(D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h))}{T_{\text{spec}}} \right) f_d $$
    where $T_{\text{spec}}$ is a temperature parameter [S1]. The NDF term $D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h)$ is detached from the computation graph to prevent gradient flow through it, ensuring only diffuse contributions update weights [S1].

**Optimization Phases**
Optimization proceeds in three phases [S1]:
1.  **Geometry:** Train only NeRF SDF with geometry loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}}$ [S1].
2.  **Material:** Train NeILF and BRDF MLPs while freezing NeRF SDF with material loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mat}}$ [S1].
3.  **Joint Optimization:** Train all fields with total loss $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{mat}}$ [S1].

The material loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{mat}} = \lambda_{\text{pbr}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pbr}} + \lambda_{\text{ref}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}} + \lambda_{\text{smth}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{smth}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{mat,physics}} $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mat,physics}} = \lambda_{\text{cons}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{spec}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{spec}}$ [S1]. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pbr}}$ is the standard RGB rendering loss, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}}$ is the NeILF++ reflection loss, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{smth}}$ is the BRDF smoothness loss [S1].

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import math

# Constants
NUM_LIGHT_SAMPLES = 256

def fibonacci_sphere(samples=NUM_LIGHT_SAMPLES):
    """Generate incident directions using Fibonacci sampling."""
    points = []
    phi = math.pi * (3.0 - math.sqrt(5.0))
    for i in range(samples):
        y = 1.0 - (i / float(samples - 1)) * 2.0
        radius = math.sqrt(max(1.0 - y * y, 0.0))
        theta = phi * i
        x = math.cos(theta) * radius
        z = math.sin(theta) * radius
        points.append(torch.tensor([x, y, z]))
    return torch.stack(points)

def spherical_gaussian_ndf(roughness, cos_theta_h):
    """Compute NDF D(omega_h) using Spherical Gaussian approximation."""
    r4 = roughness ** 4
    return (1.0 / (math.pi * r4)) * torch.exp((2.0 / r4) * (cos_theta_h - 1.0))

def compute_diffuse_brdf(albedo, metallicness):
    """Compute diffuse lobe f_d."""
    return ((1.0 - metallicness) / math.pi) * albedo

def compute_specular_brdf(roughness, normal, omega_i, omega_o):
    """Compute specular lobe f_s using microfacet model."""
    h = omega_o + omega_i
    h = h / torch.norm(h, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    cos_theta_h = torch.sum(h * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    
    D = spherical_gaussian_ndf(roughness, cos_theta_h)
    
    # Schlick Fresnel approximation [G]
    F0 = 0.04 + (1.0 - 0.04) * torch.clamp(torch.sum(normal * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True), max=1.0)
    cos_theta_o = torch.sum(omega_o * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    F = F0 + (1.0 - F0) * torch.pow(1.0 - cos_theta_o, 5.0)
    
    # Schlick-GGX Geometric Occlusion [G]
    k = (roughness + 1.0) ** 2 / 8.0
    cos_theta_i = torch.sum(omega_i * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    G = 1.0 / (1.0 + k * (1.0 / torch.clamp(cos_theta_i, min=1e-8) + 1.0 / torch.clamp(cos_theta_o, min=1e-8) - 2.0))
    
    denom = 4.0 * torch.clamp(cos_theta_i, min=1e-8) * torch.clamp(cos_theta_o, min=1e-8)
    return (D * F * G) / denom

def compute_brdf(albedo, metallicness, roughness, normal, omega_i, omega_o):
    """Compute total BRDF f_r."""
    f_d = compute_diffuse_brdf(albedo, metallicness)
    f_s = compute_specular_brdf(roughness, normal, omega_i, omega_o)
    return f_d + f_s

def compute_energy_conservation_loss(brdf_values, omega_i, normal, num_samples):
    """Compute Conservation of Energy Loss."""
    cos_i = torch.sum(omega_i * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    sum_weights = (2.0 * math.pi / num_samples) * torch.sum(brdf_values * cos_i, dim=0)
    return torch.relu(sum_weights - 1.0)

def compute_specular_loss(roughness, normal, omega_i, omega_o, albedo, metallicness, num_samples, temp_spec):
    """Compute NDF-weighted Specular Loss."""
    h = omega_o + omega_i
    h = h / torch.norm(h, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    cos_theta_h = torch.sum(h * normal, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    
    D = spherical_gaussian_ndf(roughness, cos_theta_h)
    D_detached = D.detach()
    
    weights = torch.softmax(D_detached / temp_spec, dim=0)
    f_d = compute_diffuse_brdf(albedo, metallicness)
    
    return torch.mean(weights * f_d)

**Parameters**
*   **Batch Size:** 8192 rays [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** 0.002. Fixed for Geometry and Material phases. Decreases by a factor of 5 every 10K iterations during Joint Optimization [S1].
*   **Phase Durations:** Geometry: 5K iterations; Material: 1K iterations; Joint Optimization: 30K iterations [S1].
*   **Loss Weights (NeILF++ Dataset):** $\lambda_{\text{cons}} = 0.01$, $\lambda_{\text{spec}} = 0.5$, $\lambda_{\text{pbr}} = 1$ [S1].
*   **Loss Weights (DTU Dataset):** $\lambda_{\text{cons}} = 0.01$, $\lambda_{\text{spec}} = 0.01$ [S1].
*   **Light Samples:** $|S_L| = 256$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **NDF Detachment:** In the NDF-weighted Specular Loss, the NDF term $D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h)$ must be detached from the computation graph to prevent gradient flow through it during backpropagation [S1].
*   **Lambertian Prior Limitation:** Standard NeILF/NeILF++ use a Lambertian prior that suppresses the specular lobe, causing specular effects to appear in the diffuse albedo [S1].
*   **Energy Conservation:** The Conservation of Energy Loss uses a ReLU-style formulation, allowing reflected irradiance to vary freely as long as it does not exceed incident irradiance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training runtime is 3 to 7.5 hours on a single NVIDIA A6000 [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On the NeILF++ dataset, PBR-NeRF achieves a mean PSNR of 31.27 and SSIM of 87.05 for RGB, outperforming NeILF++ (30.51 PSNR, 86.59 SSIM) [S1]. It outperforms baselines by 1-3 PSNR on average across albedo, metallicness, and roughness estimation [S1].
*   **Strengths:** State-of-the-art material estimation without compromising novel view synthesis quality. Faithful albedo estimates consistent across diverse illuminations [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive due to the three-phase optimization and physics-based losses.

## Background
PBR-NeRF was introduced in the paper "PBR-NeRF: Inverse Rendering with Physics-Based Neural Fields" [S1]. It builds upon the NeILF/NeILF++ neural fields framework [S1] and addresses the limitation of existing NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting approaches that estimate view-dependent appearance without modeling scene materials and illumination [S1]. The method introduces two novel physics-based priors: Conservation of Energy and NDF-weighted Specular Losses, to better constrain the inverse rendering estimation [S1].
