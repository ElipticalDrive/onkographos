# UrbanIR

Also called: Urban Scene Inverse Rendering

## Overview
UrbanIR is a method for the inverse rendering of large-scale urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination, inferring shape, albedo, visibility, and sun/sky illumination to enable relighting and night simulation [S1]. It differs from standard NeRFs by learning intrinsic attributes (albedo, normal) independent of lighting, and from other relightable methods like NeRF-OSR or FEGR by requiring only single-illumination RGB video without LiDAR or multiple cameras [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multi-frame video of a scene under single illumination with known camera motion, defined as $\{I_i, E_i, K_i\}$, where $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is the RGB image, $E_i \in SE(3)$ is the camera pose, and $K_i$ is the camera intrinsic matrix [S1].
*   **Output:** A neural scene model capable of rendering novel viewpoints under novel lighting conditions, including intrinsic maps (albedo, normal, semantics) and density [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene representation is built on Instant-NGP, a spatial hash-based voxel NeRF representation [S1].
*   **Feature Hash Table:** A dense feature hash table representing the scene [S1].
*   **MLP Headers:** Individual MLP headers are used to decode each attribute (albedo, normal, semantics, density) given a queried feature at point $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
*   **Scene Function:** The model learns an intrinsic scene attributes field $F_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma)$, where $\mathbf{a}$ is diffuse albedo, $\mathbf{n}$ is surface normal, $\mathbf{s}$ is semantic vector, and $\sigma$ is density [S1].
*   **Lighting Model:** A 5-DoF sun model encoding sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$ with azimuth $\psi_{sun}$ and zenith $\phi_{sun}$, a 3-DoF ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$, and a sky dome model inferring sky texture from viewing direction [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Intrinsic Decomposition:** For each camera ray $\mathbf{r}$, accumulate predictions from $F(\cdot; \theta)$ along the ray. Multiple points are sampled, and intrinsics (albedo, normal, semantics) are predicted at query pixels [S1].
2.  **Visibility Estimation:** Compute an estimate $\hat{x}(\mathbf{r})$ of the 3D point being shaded by volume rendering depth (substituting $\hat{t} = \sum w_i t_i$ into the depth equation). Estimate visibility $V(\hat{x}, \text{sun})$ by computing the transmittance along the ray segment between $\hat{x}$ and the light source using volume rendering [S1].
3.  **Rendering:** Combine intrinsic attributes and visibility with the lighting model $\mathbf{L}$ to produce final RGB color $\mathbf{C}$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Alpha-composition weight:**
    $$w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$$
    where $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
*   **Volume rendering of intrinsics:**
    $$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i$$
    where $\mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{s}_i$ are the albedo, normal, and semantic vector at sample $i$ [S1].
*   **Final Color:**
    $$\mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{L}), \mathbf{L})$$
    where $\mathbf{L}$ is the lighting model [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Lighting:** Sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$, azimuth $\psi_{sun}$, zenith $\phi_{sun}$, ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$ [S1].
*   **Loss:** Joint optimization of scene $F(\cdot)$ and lighting $\mathbf{L}$ using a loss function $\mathcal{L}_{\text{render}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_1 + \dots$ [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Standard NeRFs bake shading, shadow, and specularity into observed color, making them non-relightable [S1].
*   Modeling visibility with a per-scene optimized MLP head is impractical because the sun position changes during relighting, but training data only has one sun position [S1].

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced by Chih-Hao Lin, Bohan Liu, Yi-Ting Chen, Kuan-Sheng Chen, David Forsyth, Jia-Bin Huang, Anand Bhattad, and Shenlong Wang from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It addresses the limitation of object-centric methods and indoor-focused relightable NeRFs in reconstructing large-scale urban scenes [S1]. Unlike NeRF-OSR which assumes multiple lighting sources, or FEGR/LightSim which require LiDAR or multiple cameras, UrbanIR uses only single illumination RGB video without LiDAR [S1].
