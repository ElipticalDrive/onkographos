# Network Architectures
Also called: NeRF Network Architectures

## Overview
This article details specific modifications to the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) structures used to learn continuous volumetric scene representations $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ [G]. While the standard NeRF architecture [S1] employs two MLPs—a Position MLP ($\Phi_{pos}$) and a Shading MLP ($\Phi_{shade}$)—these variants alter layer depths, input dimensions, activation functions, and output channels to address specific challenges such as dynamic scenes, transparency, mobile efficiency, and semantic consistency [S1, S2, S3, S5, S6].

## How it works

### Standard NeRF Baseline
**Inputs and outputs:** Spatial position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ are input to produce a feature vector $\mathbf{h}$, volume density $\sigma$, and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ [G].
**Data structures:** The architecture consists of a Position MLP $\Phi_{pos}$ and a Shading MLP $\Phi_{shade}$ [G].
**Steps:** 1. Apply positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ to $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ [G]. 2. Pass $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ through $\Phi_{pos}$ to obtain $\mathbf{h}$ [G]. 3. Concatenate $\mathbf{h}$ and $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, then pass through $\Phi_{shade}$ to predict $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [G].
**Formulas:** $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ [G].
**Parameters:** Hidden layer sizes are typically 256 units [G].
**Edge cases:** Not given in the sources.

### Dynamic Scene Network Architecture
**Inputs and outputs:** The dynamic network $F_{\Theta}^{dy}$ takes $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and $\gamma(t)$ to output color $\mathbf{c}_t$, density $\sigma_t$, scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$, and disocclusion weights $\mathcal{W}_t$ [S5]. The static network $F_{\Theta}^{st}$ takes $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to output color $\mathbf{c}$, density $\sigma$, and blending weight $v$ [S5].
**Data structures:** Two separate MLPs model static and dynamic scene components [S5].
**Steps:** 1. Perform equidistant sampling to obtain spatial points along rays instead of hierarchical sampling [S5]. 2. Use the predicted scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$ to establish inter-frame relationships for time consistency [S5]. 3. Jointly optimize camera parameters $\Delta f$ and $\Delta P$ alongside the MLPs [S5].
**Formulas:** $(\mathbf{c}_t, \sigma_t, \mathcal{F}_t, \mathcal{W}_t) = F_{\Theta}^{dy}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d}), \gamma(t))$ [S5]. $(\mathbf{c}, \sigma, v) = F_{\Theta}^{st}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}), \gamma(\mathbf{d}))$ [S5].
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.
**Edge cases:** Equidistant sampling may be less efficient than hierarchical sampling for static scenes [G].

### NeRF Network Architecture with Blend Weight Field
**Inputs and outputs:** The radiance field networks $F_\sigma$ and $F_c$ take $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ to output density and color [S6]. The blend weight field $F_{\Delta w}$ takes pose parameters to output blend weights [S6].
**Data structures:** $F_\sigma$ and $F_c$ follow the original NeRF design [S6]. $F_{\Delta w}$ mirrors $F_\sigma$ but uses a final output layer with 24 channels and applies $\exp(\cdot)$ activation [S6]. Appearance code $\mathbf{l}_i$ and blend weight field code $\boldsymbol{\psi}_i$ are stored as 128-dimensional vectors [S6].
**Steps:** 1. Train $F_\sigma$, $F_c$, $F_{\Delta w}$, $\{\mathbf{l}_i\}$, and $\{\boldsymbol{\psi}_i\}$ jointly over the input video [S6]. 2. Learn neural blend weight fields under novel human poses using transformed mesh vertices [S6].
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources.
**Parameters:** Single-level NeRF sampling with 64 points per camera ray [S6].
**Edge cases:** Not given in the sources.

### MobileNeRF Architectures (A, F, H)
**Inputs and outputs:** Not given in the sources.
**Data structures:** MLPs A and F use the standard NeRF design with hidden layers increased to 384 units [S1]. MLP H uses the SNeRG design with two hidden layers of 16 neurons each [S1].
**Steps:** Not given in the sources.
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources.
**Parameters:** MLPs A and F are excluded during inference to reduce runtime overhead [S1].
**Edge cases:** Hardware constraints require texture sizes to be powers of 2 and at most $4096 \times 4096$ [S1].

### OccNet (Single-Level NeRF)
**Inputs and outputs:** Spatial position $\mathbf{x}$ is input to produce occupancy predictions [S4, unofficial].
**Data structures:** A single-level MLP architecture that omits positional encodings entirely [S4, unofficial].
**Steps:** Not given in the sources.
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources.
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.
**Edge cases:** Performance drops significantly compared to multi-level models that use positional encodings [S4, unofficial].

### Residual-NeRF-S
**Inputs and outputs:** Spatial coordinates $(\mathbf{x}, y, z)$ and spherical angles $(\theta, \phi)$ are input to produce background and residual density/color predictions [S3].
**Data structures:** Background MLP $\Phi_{bg}$ and residual MLP $\Phi_{res}$ [S3].
**Steps:** 1. Train a background NeRF on the static scene [S3]. 2. Train the residual NeRF and merge outputs by adding predictions before activation instead of using a Mixnet [S3].
**Formulas:** $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-(\sigma_{bg} + \sigma_{res})_i \delta_i)) (c_{bg+res})_i$ [S3]. $c_{bg+res} = S(c'_{bg} + c'_{res})$ [S3]. $S(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\mathbf{x}}}$ [S3].
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.
**Edge cases:** Produces worse depth and view reconstruction for partially opaque objects compared to the Mixnet variant [S3].

## Subtopics

### [MIMO-DONeRF](16217-mimo-donerf.md)
Applies the Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) concept to DONeRF by modifying the shading network to accept multiple spatial inputs and view directions simultaneously, producing multiple outputs per forward pass. Choose it when inference speed is critical and neighbor information accumulation is beneficial.

### [Point-NeRF Adaptation for Object Manipulation](16395-point-nerf-adaptation-for-object-manipulation.md)
Adapts Point-NeRF for 3D scene geometry decomposition and manipulation from 2D images by replacing MVS point clouds with ground-truth dense data and integrating a DM-NeRF module. Choose it for editing specific objects within complex scenes.

### [Sparse Latent Space for 4D Novel View Synthesis (SLS4D)](18112-sparse-latent-space-for-4d-novel-view-synthesis-sls4d.md)
Represents dynamic radiance fields using a learnable sparse latent space with attention-based interpolation, avoiding $O(N^3TF)$ grid complexity. Choose it for 4D novel-view synthesis requiring better temporal generalization than per-image latent codes.

### [Neural Feature Fusion Fields (N3F)](18114-neural-feature-fusion-fields-n3f.md)
Distills dense 2D image features from a pre-trained teacher into a 3D neural field via differentiable rendering, fusing continuous feature vectors instead of discrete labels. Choose it for open-world 3D-aware feature representation without manual labels.

### [CLNeRF Architecture with Embeddings](18159-clnerf-architecture-with-embeddings.md)
Enables continual learning by incorporating scene changes over time using explicit, trainable per-scan embedding vectors, preventing model size growth. Choose it for efficient updates and low-overhead generative replay.

### [Mutual Information Gradient Shaping](18669-mutual-information-gradient-shaping.md)
Shapes the tangent space of a NeRF to encode semantic correlations by aligning Jacobians of correlated entities to maximize mutual information under random weight perturbations. Choose it for efficient sparse label propagation and semantic consensus.

### [Multi-Level Neural Scene Graph](19889-multi-level-neural-scene-graph.md)
Decomposes environments into a graph of static, dynamic, and temporal nodes, explicitly separating the radiance field into static $\phi$ and dynamic $\psi$ components conditioned on latent vectors. Choose it for urban environments with moving objects and appearance variations.

### [NAN (Noise-Aware NeRFs)](20279-nan-noise-aware-nerfs.md)
Extends IBRNet to handle degraded noisy images by explicitly modeling spatial noise statistics through 3D blending kernels and cross-view variance features. Choose it for burst-denoising of low-light or noisy captures rather than novel view synthesis.

### [Multiplicative Interaction Module](21736-multiplicative-interaction-module.md)
Generates conditional NeRF inputs by computing multiplicative interactions between expression, identity, and latent codes to model higher-order dependencies. Choose it for facial disentanglement and rendering quality improvement.

### [Part-Wise NARF (NARFP)](23078-part-wise-narf-narfp.md)
Models articulated objects by training a separate RT-NeRF for each rigid part and combining outputs via forward kinematics. Choose it to avoid implicit transformation issues and part dependency in articulated scenes.

### Pyramid NeRF
Accomplishes rapid training and inference through an image pyramid and frequency optimization strategy [S2]. It utilizes a 'low-frequency-first, high-frequency-second' approach, substitutes large MLPs with thousands of smaller ones via progressive segmentation, and introduces depth-aware sampling [S2]. Enhances training speed by 15x and inference speed by 805x relative to the original NeRF [S2].

### Residual-NeRF-S
An ablation variant of Residual-NeRF where background and residual NeRFs are merged by simply adding their outputs before activation, instead of using a Mixnet [S3]. Produces worse depth and view reconstruction compared to Residual-NeRF with Mixnet, especially for partially opaque objects [S3].

### OccNet
A single-level version of NeRF that does not leverage positional encodings [S4, unofficial]. Achieves 13.90 PSNR / 0.437 SSIM on 2D Images and 20.60 PSNR / 0.849 SSIM on Radiance Fields, with training times of 02:17 and 100:23 respectively, and model sizes of 0.38 and 0.43 [S4, unofficial].

### Dynamic Scene Network Architecture
Divides dynamic scenes into static and dynamic parts modeled by two separate MLPs [S5]. Uses equidistant sampling to obtain spatial points along rays, instead of hierarchical sampling, and uses predicted scene flow to establish inter-frame relationship for time consistency [S5]. The Dynamic Network $F_{\Theta}^{dy}$ takes positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and time $\gamma(t)$ as inputs, outputting color $\mathbf{c}_t$, density $\sigma_t$, scene flow $\mathcal{F}_t$, and disocclusion weights $\mathcal{W}_t$. The Static Network $F_{\Theta}^{st}$ takes positional encoding of position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ as inputs, outputting color $\mathbf{c}$, density $\sigma$, and blending weight $v$ [S5].

### NeRF Network Architecture with Blend Weight Field
The network architecture for the radiance field ($F_\sigma$, $F_c$) and blend weight field ($F_{\Delta w}$), following the original NeRF but with specific modifications for blend weights [S6]. Uses single-level NeRF sampling 64 points along each camera ray. The network $F_{\Delta w}$ is almost same as $F_\sigma$, except the final output layer has 24 channels and applies exp(·) to the output. The appearance code dimension is 128 [S6].

## Operating characteristics
**Standard NeRF & Variants:** The standard architecture [S1] and its direct variants (MobileNeRF A/F) rely on MLPs with hidden layers of 256 or 384 units [G]. The computational cost is dominated by the evaluation of sinusoidal positional encodings and the MLP forward passes for every sample point along the ray [G]. Pyramid NeRF [S2] reports a 15x training speedup and 805x inference speedup compared to the original NeRF. OccNet [S4, unofficial] is significantly faster to train (02:17 vs 100:23 for radiance field) but yields much lower PSNR (20.60 vs typical NeRF >25) due to the lack of positional encodings. MobileNeRF [S1] reduces inference memory by not using MLPs A and F during inference. OccNet [S4, unofficial] has a model size of 0.38/0.43 (units unspecified, likely MB or relative scale).

**Dynamic Architectures:** The Dynamic Scene Network [S5] uses equidistant sampling which may be less efficient than hierarchical sampling for static scenes but simplifies the training pipeline for dynamic scenes. The dual-MLP structure increases parameter count compared to a single NeRF but allows for explicit modeling of motion via scene flow [G]. The Blend Weight Field [S6] adds a separate network $F_{\Delta w}$ with 24 output channels and exponential activation, increasing model size and training time (12 hours for 300 frames on 4 GPUs).

**Specialized Architectures:** Residual-NeRF-S [S3] uses simpler merging logic (addition + sigmoid) compared to Mixnet, but results in worse depth and view reconstruction for transparent objects. Pyramid NeRF [S2] uses progressive segmentation of large MLPs into thousands of smaller ones, trading off representational capacity for speed.

## Background
The standard NeRF architecture [S1] was introduced by Mildenhall et al. (2020) as the baseline for neural radiance fields. Subsequent modifications have addressed specific limitations. MobileNeRF [S1] (2023) modifies MLP sizes and omits certain networks during inference to exploit the polygon rasterization pipeline for mobile efficiency. Pyramid NeRF [S2] (2023) was proposed to accelerate training and inference through frequency optimization and image pyramids. Residual-NeRF [S3] (2024) was introduced to improve depth perception and training speed for scenes with transparent objects by leveraging a background prior. OccNet [S4, unofficial] (2023) is part of the Factor Fields framework, demonstrating the impact of positional encodings by comparing single-level (no encoding) vs multi-level models. Dynamic Scene Network [S5] (2025) addresses dynamic view synthesis by explicitly separating static and dynamic components. Animatable NeRF [S6] (2021) introduced blend weight fields to model dynamic human bodies.
