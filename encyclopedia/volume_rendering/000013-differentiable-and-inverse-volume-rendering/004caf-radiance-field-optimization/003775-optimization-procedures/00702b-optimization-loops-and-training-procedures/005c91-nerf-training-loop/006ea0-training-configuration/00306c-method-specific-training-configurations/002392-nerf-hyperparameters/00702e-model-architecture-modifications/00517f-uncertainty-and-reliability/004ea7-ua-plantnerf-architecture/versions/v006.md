# UA-PlantNeRF Architecture

Also called: Dual-Head NeRF

## Overview
UA-PlantNeRF is a unified framework for reconstructing volumetric radiance fields from sparse, unstructured video footage of plants, addressing degraded geometry and the absence of calibrated uncertainty estimates [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by jointly predicting density, color, and decomposed uncertainty (aleatoric and epistemic) via a dual-head architecture with Monte Carlo Dropout, enabling conformal prediction intervals for reliable geometry assessment [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse set of unstructured RGB images (as few as 15 frames selected via VISAR strategy [S1, unofficial]); camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Volumetric radiance field $R$ (density $\sigma$ and color $c$); spatially varying uncertainty field $U$ (epistemic $U_{ep}$ and aleatoric $U_{al}$); conformal prediction intervals [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
*   **Backbone:** Hierarchical NeRF structure [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Positional Encoding:** $L=10$ frequency levels [S1, unofficial].
    *   **MLPs:** Two parallel 8-layer Multi-Layer Perceptrons, each with width 256. Skip connections are inserted at layer 4 [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Head 1 (Geometry/Color):** Predicts density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Head 2 (Aleatoric Uncertainty):** Parallel head predicting log-variance $\log \beta(x, d)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Fine Network:** The second stage of the hierarchical NeRF, augmented with dropout [S1, unofficial].

**Training Objective**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ combines three terms [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{NLL} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} + \lambda_{sem} \mathcal{L}_{sem} $$
*   $\mathcal{L}_{NLL}$: Heteroscedastic Negative Log-Likelihood loss [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$: Mip-NeRF 360 distortion regularizer [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{sem}$: CLIP semantic-consistency term to prevent geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** $\lambda_{reg} = 0.01$, $\lambda_{sem} = 0.1$ [S1, unofficial].

The Negative Log-Likelihood loss for a batch $B$ is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{NLL} = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{r \in B} \left[ \frac{\|\hat{c}(r) - c(r)\|^2}{2\beta(r)} + \frac{1}{2} \log \beta(r) \right] $$
where $\hat{c}(r)$ is the predicted color, $c(r)$ is the ground truth color, and $\beta(r)$ is the variance predicted by the aleatoric head [S1, unofficial].

**Uncertainty Estimation**
*   **Epistemic Uncertainty:** Estimated via Monte Carlo Dropout applied after every hidden layer of the fine network with probability $p=0.1$ [S1, unofficial]. At inference, $K=20$ stochastic passes are performed [S1, unofficial]. The epistemic uncertainty for a ray $r$ is the variance of these predictions [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ U_{ep}(r) = K^{-1} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \|\hat{c}_k - \bar{c}\|^2 $$
    where $\hat{c}_k$ is the prediction of the $k$-th pass and $\bar{c}$ is the mean prediction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Aleatoric Uncertainty:** Derived from the log-variance output $\log \beta$ of the dual head [S1, unofficial]. The aleatoric component $U_{al}(r)$ is computed as the accumulated variance along the ray [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ U_{al}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i \alpha_i \beta(x_i) $$
    where $M$ is the number of samples, $T_i$ and $\alpha_i$ are transmittance and weight terms, and $\beta(x_i)$ is the variance at sample $x_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Total Uncertainty:**
    $$ U_{total}(r) = U_{ep}(r) + U_{al}(r) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

**Conformal Calibration**
To provide distribution-free coverage guarantees, split conformal prediction is used [S1, unofficial]. The calibration set $D_{cal}$ is constructed from held-out plants entirely excluded from training (e.g., 2 of 10 plants in Pheno4D, 4 of 20 in ROSE-X, 3 of 15 in UA-Field) to ensure exchangeability with test plants [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def train_ua_plantnerf(images, poses, config):
    """
    Trains the UA-PlantNeRF model.
    config: dict containing lambda_reg=0.01, lambda_sem=0.1, dropout_p=0.1
    """
    # Initialize Hierarchical NeRF with two 8-layer MLPs (width 256)
    # Head 1: predicts sigma, c
    # Head 2: predicts log_beta (aleatoric variance)
    model = HierarchicalNeRF(num_layers=8, width=256, skip_layer=4, pos_enc_levels=10)
    
    optimizer = Adam(model.parameters())
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs):
        for batch in dataloader:
            rays, gt_colors = batch
            
            # Forward pass through backbone
            sigma, c_pred, log_beta = model.forward(rays)
            beta = exp(log_beta)
            
            # Compute Heteroscedastic NLL Loss
            # L_NLL = mean( ||c_pred - gt||^2 / (2*beta) + 0.5 * log(beta) )
            l_nll = compute_nll_loss(c_pred, gt_colors, beta)
            
            # Compute Distortion Regularization (Mip-NeRF 360)
            l_reg = compute_distortion_regularization(rays, sigma)
            
            # Compute Semantic Consistency (CLIP)
            l_sem = compute_clip_semantic_loss(rays, c_pred, gt_colors)
            
            # Total Loss
            loss = l_nll + config['lambda_reg'] * l_reg + config['lambda_sem'] * l_sem
            
            optimizer.zero_grad()
            loss.backward()
            optimizer.step()

def infer_uncertainty(model, rays, k=20, p=0.1):
    """
    Estimates epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty for given rays.
    """
    model.train() # Enable Dropout
    predictions = []
    
    for _ in range(k):
        # Apply MC Dropout with probability p
        c_k, _, log_beta_k = model.forward(rays, dropout_prob=p)
        predictions.append(c_k)
        
    # Mean prediction
    c_mean = mean(predictions, axis=0)
    
    # Epistemic Uncertainty: Variance of K stochastic passes
    # U_ep = (1/K) * sum( ||c_k - c_mean||^2 )
    u_ep = compute_variance(predictions, c_mean)
    
    # Aleatoric Uncertainty: Accumulated variance from the last pass (or mean beta)
    # U_al = sum( T_i * alpha_i * beta(x_i) )
    # Note: In practice, beta is predicted per sample along the ray
    _, _, log_beta_last = model.forward(rays, dropout_prob=0) # Deterministic for beta estimation
    beta_last = exp(log_beta_last)
    u_al = compute_ray_accumulated_variance(rays, beta_last)
    
    # Total Uncertainty
    u_total = u_ep + u_al
    
    return c_mean, u_ep, u_al, u_total
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is comparable to standard NeRF but slightly higher due to the dual-head and semantic loss. Inference requires $K=20$ forward passes for epistemic uncertainty, increasing inference time by a factor of 20 compared to deterministic NeRF [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing parameters for two MLPs and the variance head. No additional large data structures beyond standard NeRF grids/volumes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides calibrated uncertainty estimates, allowing users to distinguish reliable geometry from artifacts in sparse view scenarios. Effective for plants with occluded leaves [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** High inference latency due to MC Dropout sampling. Performance depends on the quality of the initial frame selection (VISAR) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Sparse view reconstruction of plants where reliability assessment is critical.
*   **When not to use:** Real-time applications requiring fast inference without uncertainty quantification.

## Background
UA-PlantNeRF was introduced to address the specific challenges of 3D plant reconstruction from sparse, unstructured video, where traditional NeRFs suffer from geometry degradation and lack uncertainty metrics [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the VISAR frame selection strategy and integrates concepts from heteroscedastic regression and conformal prediction [S1, unofficial].
