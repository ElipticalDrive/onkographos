# CCNeRF
Also called: Compressible-Composable NeRF, Rank-residual Decomposition NeRF

## Overview
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition of the scene without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field *only* with tensor rank decomposition, preserving the low-rank approximation property required for dynamic size adjustment and arbitrary composition [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 2D images with known camera poses (origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$) used for training; a 3D spatial coordinate $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ for inference.
*   **Output:** Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ at the queried point.

**Data Structures:**
The model structure consists of two main parts [S1]:
1.  **Rank Weights Matrix ($W$):** A matrix storing weights for different feature channels.
2.  **Decomposed Rank Components ($\mathcal{U}$):** A set of components that are either vector-based (CP decomposition) or matrix-based (triple plane variant). The ratio of vector- to matrix-based components is controllable.

For a single scene, the representation is a 4D feature volume described by rank components and a weight matrix. The components are stored as factor matrices/vectors.

**Numbered Steps (Training and Querying):**

1.  **Scene Representation via Rank-Residual Learning:**
    The 3D scene is modeled as a 4D feature volume. To encourage lower ranks to preserve primary information, a rank-residual learning strategy is employed [S1]. This ensures that when the model is truncated, the remaining components retain the most important scene information.

2.  **Querying a 3D Coordinate:**
    To evaluate the field at a specific 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$:
    a. Project the coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ to the decomposed vectors or matrices in $\mathcal{U}$ [S1].
    b. Perform weighted interpolation using the stored rank weights $W$ [S1].
    *Note: The specific mathematical aggregation of the CP and Triple Plane components is not explicitly detailed in the provided text, but it follows the principle of projecting $\mathbf{x}$ into the factor spaces and combining them via the weight matrix.*

3.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Given a ray $r$ originating at $\mathbf{o}$ with direction $\mathbf{d}$, sample points $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \mathbf{d}$ along the ray. Compute density $\sigma_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ for each sample. Render the pixel color $\hat{C}(r)$ using the standard volume rendering equation [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i
    $$
    where:
    *   $T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance.
    *   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity, with $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ being the distance between samples.
    *   $\sigma_i$ is the volume density and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the emitted color at sample $i$.

4.  **Compression (Rank Truncation):**
    Model size is dynamically adjusted by rank truncation. An empirical sort-and-truncate strategy is used to achieve near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank [S1].
    *   **Procedure:** The rank components are sorted based on their contribution (implied by the "sort" in sort-and-truncate) and the least important components are removed.
    *   **Result:** This allows trade-offs between model size and rendering quality without retraining [S1].
    *   **Pitfall:** Standard truncated Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD) does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].

5.  **Composition:**
    Different models are composed by concatenating their rank components along the rank dimension [S1].
    *   **Transformation:** A transformation matrix is recorded for each object to control its position and orientation in the composed scene.
    *   **Implementation:** Since there are no MLP renderers, composition is achieved by simply concatenating the rank components of different objects [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Rank Truncation:** The number of components kept in the decomposition determines the model size and detail level. Lower ranks preserve primary information due to rank-residual learning [S1].
*   **Ratio of Vector- to Matrix-based Components:** Controllable to trade off between model size and performance [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Truncation Optimality:** Standard truncated HOSVD does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].
*   **MLP Dependency:** Unlike TensoRF, CCNeRF avoids MLPs to preserve low-rank approximation properties. If MLPs were used, compression and composition would be prevented [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Querying is faster than MLP-based NeRF as it avoids neural network inference, relying on linear algebra operations (projections and interpolations) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Model size is dynamically adjustable via rank truncation. Compression can mitigate storage cost growth in composed scenes by compressing unimportant objects [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Enables efficient manipulation (compression and composition) without retraining [S1].
    *   No neural networks for rendering, allowing arbitrary composition [S1].
    *   Near-optimal compression without extra optimization [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the assumption that the scene can be well-approximated by low-rank tensors.
    *   Truncated HOSVD is only quasi-optimal [S1].
*   **When to use:** When dynamic LOD control, compression, and composition of 3D scenes are required without the overhead of neural rendering networks [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When the scene has high-frequency details that cannot be captured by low-rank approximations, or when maximum rendering quality is prioritized over manipulation capabilities [S1].

## Background
CCNeRF was introduced by Jiaxiang Tang, Xiaokang Chen, Jingbo Wang, and Gang Zeng from Peking University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong [S1]. The paper "Compressible-Composable NeRF via Rank-residual Decomposition" was published on arXiv in May 2022 (v2 in October 2022) [S1]. It addresses the difficulty of manipulating implicit NeRF representations, which are often restricted by shared renderer networks or suffer from large model sizes [S1]. The method is motivated by the low-rank approximation property of the SVD algorithm and builds on tensor decomposition techniques like Tucker decomposition and CP decomposition [S1].
