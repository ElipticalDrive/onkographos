# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (direct observation via a shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (history-based prediction), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain to handle significant motion and temporal coherence [S1]. Unlike [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) which may rely on feature interpolation, KFD-NeRF uses deformation fields which are smoother and easier to fit with MLPs due to inductive smoothness bias [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and timestamp $t_i \in \mathbb{R}$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Intermediate:** Deformation field $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ mapping real space to canonical space.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Tri-plane Representation:** Three sets of mutually orthogonal 2D planes encoding the canonical space. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
2.  **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1].
3.  **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that computes the Kalman gain [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$

2.  **Prediction Step:**
    *   Retrieve deformation and noise terms from previous frames $t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$:
        $$ d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_{i-1}) $$
        $$ d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-2}} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_{i-2}) $$
    *   Approximate velocity $v_{t_i}$ using the deformation difference between the two previous frames:
        $$ v_{t_i} = (d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}) / \Delta t_i $$
        where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
    *   Calculate the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using local linear motion:
        $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} $$
        Substituting $v_{t_i}$, this simplifies to:
        $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}} $$
        [S1].

3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ with inputs $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and timestamps $t_i, t_{i-1}$ [S1]:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$

4.  **Deformation Estimation:** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$

5.  **Warping and Rendering:** Warp all points in real space to a time-independent canonical space according to $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$. Query the tri-plane representation and density/color networks in the canonical space to render the image [S1].

**Training Strategy:**
*   **Chronological Release:** Training images are gradually released in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Loss Functions:**
    *   **Kalman Loss:** Minimizes error between observation and estimated deformation:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
        [S1].
    *   **Canonical Observation Loss:** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between canonical space and real world:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
        where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
    *   **Total Variation Loss:** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
        where $c$ is a plane from collection $C$ and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].
    *   **Proposal Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{prop}$ ensures consistency between the proposal network and the learned model (distilled from Mip-NeRF 360) [S1].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$
        [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def kfd_nerf_forward(x, d, t, t_prev, t_prev2, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, triplanes):
    """
    x: (N, 3) 3D coordinates
    d: (N, 3) viewing directions
    t: current timestamp
    t_prev: previous timestamp
    t_prev2: timestamp before previous
    observer_mlp: 2-layer MLP returning (deformation, noise_terms)
    kalman_gain_net: Linear layer returning Kalman gain
    triplanes: Tri-plane representation of canonical space
    """
    
    # 1. Observer Step for current frame
    y_t, eps_t = observer_mlp(x, t)
    
    # 2. Prediction Step
    # Get previous deformations
    dx_prev, eps_prev = observer_mlp(x, t_prev)
    dx_prev2, eps_prev2 = observer_mlp(x, t_prev2)
    
    # Calculate velocity
    dt = t - t_prev
    v_t = (dx_prev - dx_prev2) / dt
    
    # Predict deformation
    dx_pred = dx_prev + dt * v_t
    
    # 3. Kalman Gain Calculation
    K_t = kalman_gain_net(eps_t, eps_prev, t, t_prev)
    
    # 4. Deformation Estimation
    dx_est = dx_pred + K_t * (y_t - dx_pred)
    
    # 5. Warping to Canonical Space
    x_canonical = x + dx_est
    
    # 6. Rendering from Canonical Space
    # Query triplanes and density/color networks
    # This part depends on the specific radiance field implementation
    # For KFD-NeRF, features are concatenated with raw coordinates
    features = query_triplanes(x_canonical, triplanes)
    features = concat(features, x_canonical)
    
    sigma, color = radiance_field_network(features, d)
    
    return sigma, color, dx_est, y_t

def compute_losses(sigma, color, image_gt, dx_est, y_t, t, t_prev, t_prev2, 
                   observer_mlp, triplanes, proposal_network, lambda_tv):
    """
    Computes the total loss for KFD-NeRF training.
    """
    # Image Loss (e.g., L1 or MSE)
    L_image = mean_squared_error(color, image_gt)
    
    # Kalman Loss
    N = len(dx_est)
    L_kf = mean_squared_error(y_t, dx_est)
    
    # Canonical Observation Loss (only for t=0)
    if t == 0:
        L_co = mean(dx_est) # 1(t) is 1, else 0
    else:
        L_co = 0.0
        
    # Proposal Loss (from Mip-NeRF 360 distillation)
    # Assuming proposal_network provides density estimates for occupancy
    L_prop = compute_proposal_loss(proposal_network, sigma)
    
    # Total Variation Loss on Triplanes
    # Applied to warped points x_canonical
    L_tv = compute_total_variation_loss(triplanes, x_canonical)
    
    # Total Loss
    L_total = L_image + L_kf + L_co + L_prop + lambda_tv * L_tv
    
    return L_total
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The use of a tri-plane representation significantly reduces memory consumption compared to MLP-based spatial representations (like D-NeRF), allowing for higher resolution in the canonical space [S1]. The observer MLP is shallow (2 layers), reducing computational cost [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
    *   **Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 30 minutes for the small variant (KFD-NeRF-S) and 3 hours for the large variant (KFD-NeRF-L) on the tested datasets, comparable to or faster than methods like D-NeRF (48 hrs) and TiNeuVox-B (40 mins) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate deformation estimation from scene observations and predictions; handles significant motion well; fast convergence due to tri-plane representation; smooth deformation fields easier to fit with MLPs [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on local linear motion assumption, which may not capture complex non-linear motions perfectly; performance depends on the quality of the proposal network for density distillation [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for dynamic scenes with moderate to significant motion where temporal coherence is important and computational efficiency is desired.
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with highly complex, non-linear, or unpredictable motion where local linear approximation fails; scenes requiring extremely high-frequency detail in the feature space rather than deformation [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Y. Zhan, Z. Li, M. Niu, Z. Zhong, S. Nobuhara, K. Nishino, and Y. Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states. The method integrates a Kalman filter to optimize state estimations from both observations and predictions, addressing the limitations of previous dynamic NeRF methods that either disregarded motion priors or relied on feature interpolation which is hard to fit for abrupt radiance changes [S1].
