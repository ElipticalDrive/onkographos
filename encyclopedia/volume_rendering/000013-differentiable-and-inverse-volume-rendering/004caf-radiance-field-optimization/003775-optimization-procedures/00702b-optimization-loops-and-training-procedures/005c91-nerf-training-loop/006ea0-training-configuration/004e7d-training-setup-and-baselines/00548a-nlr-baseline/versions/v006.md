# NLR Baseline

Also called: Neural Lumigraph Representation (NLR)

## Overview
The NLR Baseline establishes a performance reference for novel view synthesis by training a Neural Lumigraph Representation, which models light fields using neural networks to interpolate between input views [S1, unofficial]. It serves as a standard comparison point for meta-learning approaches like MetaNLR, distinguishing itself from direct volume rendering methods (e.g., NeRF) by optimizing a representation that explicitly leverages the structure of input camera poses and views [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images with known camera poses and intrinsics. The implementation used in the baseline sources is accessed from the original NLR authors [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NLR model capable of rendering novel views.

**Data Structures:**
The specific internal data structures of the NLR implementation are not detailed in the source notes; the method is treated as a black-box implementation wrapped by the meta-learning loop [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Load the NLR implementation provided by the original authors [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Training Loop:** Optimize the NLR parameters to minimize the photometric error between rendered images and ground truth training views.
3.  **Rendering:** Sample rays through the trained NLR representation to generate novel views.

**Parameters:**
*   **Resolution:** Training and testing views are processed at 800 × 600 resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Timing:** The baseline reports an iteration time of 2.21 seconds and a rendering time of 0.025 seconds per frame [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   The source notes do not specify precision issues or boundary conditions for the base NLR implementation [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### MetaNLR Baseline
MetaNLR is a variant that wraps the NLR implementation with an outer meta-learning loop using Reptile to learn the initialization of the shape and color networks [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when rapid specialization to new scenes is required, as it optimizes the representation for multiple entire images rather than randomly selected rays, leading to faster convergence despite longer individual iteration times [S1, unofficial]. The meta-learning rate is set to $5 \times 10^{-2}$ [S1, unofficial]. It uses the same training and testing views at 800 × 600 resolution as the base method [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The NLR baseline takes 2.21 seconds per iteration [S1, unofficial]. MetaNLR++ (with shape optimization) takes 7.69 seconds per iteration, while NLR++ (no shape optimization) takes 2.19 seconds [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory/Performance:** MetaNLR++ iterations are longer because they optimize for multiple entire images with significantly larger ray-batch sizes compared to NLR or IDR [S1, unofficial]. However, these methods require significantly fewer iterations to converge [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** MetaNLR variants converge faster in terms of iteration count due to batch optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Higher per-iteration computational cost for meta-learning variants [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The NLR Baseline is derived from the Neural Lumigraph Representation method [3] (cited in [S1, unofficial]). The specific baseline configuration described here was established by the authors of "Fast Training of Neural Lumigraph Representations using Meta Learning" to provide a comparative reference for their MetaNLR++ method [S1, unofficial].
