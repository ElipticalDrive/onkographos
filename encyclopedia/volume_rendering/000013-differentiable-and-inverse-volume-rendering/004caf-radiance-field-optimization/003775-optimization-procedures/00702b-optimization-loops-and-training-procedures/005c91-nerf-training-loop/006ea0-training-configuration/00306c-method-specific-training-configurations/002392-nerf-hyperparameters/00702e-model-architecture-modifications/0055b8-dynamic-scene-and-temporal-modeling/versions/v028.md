# Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, 4D NeRF, Time-conditioned NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling extends the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation to handle time-varying geometry and appearance, solving the problem that standard NeRF assumes a static scene [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that employ complex warping fields, particle systems, or graph structures to model dynamics, this family encompasses a broad range of approaches including direct time conditioning, canonical space deformation, and semantic stratification, with specific methods chosen based on the required balance between temporal coherence, computational efficiency, and editing capabilities [S1].

## Shared principles
The core task of a NeRF network architecture is to learn a continuous volumetric scene representation $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, t) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the spatial position, $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the viewing direction, $t \in \mathbb{R}$ is the time parameter, $\sigma$ is the volume density, and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the view-dependent color [G]. The rendering process integrates these outputs along rays using standard volume rendering equations [G].

## Subtopics

### [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md)
Gear-NeRF is a dynamic scene representation method that stratifies a 4D volumetric scene into multiple "gear" levels based on semantic motion scales, allowing for region-specific spatio-temporal sampling resolution. Unlike unified 4D representations, it uses a series of 4D feature volumes where higher-motion regions are assigned higher gear levels and sampled more densely, improving dynamic novel view synthesis while enabling free-viewpoint object tracking.

### [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)
This method adapts the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) architecture for dynamic scenes by introducing a temporal dimension to the input space. It solves the problem of representing time-varying geometry and appearance, which standard NeRF cannot handle due to its static assumption. Unlike sibling methods that employ complex warping fields, particle systems, or graph structures to model dynamics, this approach serves as a simple baseline by directly conditioning the neural network on time. While it improves reconstruction quality over vanilla NeRF on dynamic data, it is limited by blurry predictions and an inability to reason about complex scene dynamics.

### [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md)
Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis is a method for rendering dynamic scenes by modeling a static scene at a canonical time step and forward warping the entire canonical radiance field to other time steps. It addresses the many-to-one and one-to-many mapping difficulties inherent in dynamic scene reconstruction by employing differentiable forward warping with average splatting and an inpaint network. Unlike backward flow-based methods such as NDVG, this approach leverages a canonical representation that simplifies the learning of trajectories and geometry, achieving state-of-the-art performance on public datasets, particularly for canonical frames.

### [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md)
Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field (DAP-NeRF) is a method for novel view synthesis from monocular video that models scene dynamics using a Lagrangian particle-based representation. It solves the problem of reconstructing dynamic scenes without prior geometric knowledge or multi-view stereo data, which are requirements for related methods like ParticleNeRF. Unlike ParticleNeRF, which encodes the entire scene with particles and requires 20 camera views per frame, DAP-NeRF applies appearance particles only to dynamic elements while maintaining a static grid-based field for the background, enabling efficient linear interpolation and handling single-camera input.

### [KFD-NeRF](15551-kfd-nerf.md)
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (direct observation via a shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (history-based prediction), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain to handle significant motion and temporal coherence. Unlike [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) which may rely on feature interpolation, KFD-NeRF uses deformation fields which are smoother and easier to fit with MLPs due to inductive smoothness bias.

### [SLS4D](16576-sls4d.md)
SLS4D is a method for 4D novel view synthesis that represents dynamic scenes using a sparse, compact, and informative latent feature space. It replaces dense voxel grids or explicit coordinate mappings with learnable latent codes and attention mechanisms, significantly reducing network parameters while capturing global dynamics. It sets itself apart from dense implicit representations (like [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)) and explicit voxel-based dynamic methods (like [V4D](13848-gear-nerf.md)) by decoupling time and space via deformation fields and time slots, allowing it to achieve state-of-the-art results with approximately 6% of the parameters of previous SOTA works.

### [ST-NeRF](18515-st-nerf.md)
ST-NeRF is a layered neural radiance field representation designed for editable free-viewpoint videos of dynamic scenes containing independently moving or articulated objects. It solves the problem of spatial and temporal consistency in dynamic radiance fields by disentangling the positions, deformations, and appearance of distinct scene entities. Unlike monolithic dynamic NeRFs, ST-NeRF represents each dynamic entity as a separate continuous function spanning space and time, enabling specific editing operations such as rescaling, shifting, duplication, removal, and temporal rearrangement.

#### ER-NeRF
- **Definition**: A Conditional NeRF framework for effective, high-accuracy dynamic portrait creation, producing dynamic portrait videos synchronized with audio [S1].
- **Steps**: Utilizes spatial regions’ distinct contributions to steer dynamic head modeling; develops a concise and articulate Tri-Plane Hash Representation; introduces rapid and straightforward Adaptive Pose Encoding to enhance the separation of the head and torso [S1].
- **Pitfalls**: Additional fine-tuning might be necessary to sustain superior rendering outcomes for poses that exceed the scope of the training dataset [S1].
- **Performance**: Attains instantaneous rendering and rapid convergence using a compact model size [S1].

#### NeRF-MS
- **Definition**: A method that diminishes vagueness in visual alterations by implementing a ternary loss to standardize the spread of visual codes across each image [S1].
- **Steps**: Suggests a temporary decomposition unit to more effectively distinguish still scenes from dynamic objects [S1].
- **Pitfalls**: Might encounter difficulties in managing significant sequence discrepancies, like severe alterations in the visual aspect of scenes caused by the activation of lights [S1].
- **Performance**: Trials using both real-life outdoor and artificial datasets reveal that NeRF-MS attains cutting-edge results in multi-sequence scenarios, markedly enhancing rendering efficiency through the reconstruction of 3D-consistent visuals and minimizing ghost effects [S1].

#### DCT-NeRF
- **Definition**: A method that learns smooth and stable trajectories for each point in space in an input video sequence using Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) parameterization, allowing for high-quality reconstruction and consistent motion extrapolation beyond the last available frame [S2, unofficial].
- **Steps**: Parameterize trajectories with the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to model motion; handle temporal occlusions and reflect changes in reflectance over time [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of extending NeRF to dynamic scenes emerged as a natural progression from the original static NeRF formulation [S1]. Early approaches focused on direct time conditioning, while subsequent research introduced canonical space deformations, particle systems, and semantic stratification to handle complex dynamics [S1]. The field has seen significant development with methods like Gear-NeRF, Forward Warping, and KFD-NeRF, each addressing specific limitations such as computational efficiency, temporal coherence, and handling of non-rigid motion [S1]. Recent advancements include latent space representations like SLS4D and layered models like ST-NeRF for editable dynamic scenes [S1][S2, unofficial].
