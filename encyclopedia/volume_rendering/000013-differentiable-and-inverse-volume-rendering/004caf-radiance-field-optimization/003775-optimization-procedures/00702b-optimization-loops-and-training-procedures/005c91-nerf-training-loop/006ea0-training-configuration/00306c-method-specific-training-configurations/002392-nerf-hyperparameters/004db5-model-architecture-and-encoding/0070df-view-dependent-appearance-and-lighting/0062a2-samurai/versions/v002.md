# SAMURAI

Also called: Joint optimization of shape, BRDF, cameras, and illuminations, Shape and Material from Unconstrained Real-World Arbitrary Image Collections

## Overview
SAMURAI is a joint optimization framework that estimates 3D shape, BRDF material properties, per-image illumination, camera poses, and camera intrinsics from unconstrained in-the-wild image collections with varying backgrounds and illuminations [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [UrbanIR](16200-urbanir.md) and [See-NeRF](18115-see-nerf.md) by explicitly decomposing radiance into reflectance and illumination, allowing for relightable assets and handling extreme pose uncertainty through camera multiplexing, whereas siblings often assume known poses or fixed illumination [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A collection of $q$ object images $C_j \in \mathbb{R}^{s_j \times 3}$ with varying resolutions, backgrounds, and illuminations [S1]. Rough camera pose quadrants annotated with 3 binary questions: Left vs. Right, Above vs. Below, and Front vs. Back [S1]. Automatically estimated foreground segmentation masks $M_j \in \{0, 1\}^{s \times 1}$ (e.g., via U2-Net), which may be noisy [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A 3D neural volume $V$ containing BRDF parameters, surface normals, and density; per-image latent illumination vectors; and optimized camera poses and intrinsics. Explicit meshes with BRDF textures can be extracted [S1].

**Data Structures and Layout**
*   **Neural Volume Parameters:** At each point $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the network outputs:
    *   BRDF parameters $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^5$ for the Cook-Torrance model: base color $\mathbf{bc} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, metallic $b_m \in \mathbb{R}^1$, roughness $b_r \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
    *   Unit-length surface normal $\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
    *   Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
*   **Per-Image Latent Illumination:** Vectors $\mathbf{z}_{jl} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ for each image $j$ [S1].
*   **Camera Parameters:** Stored as offsets to initial parameters using a 'look-at' parameterization:
    *   3D look-at vector $\mathbf{r}_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
    *   Scalar up rotation $u_j \in \mathbb{R}[-\pi, \pi]$ [S1].
    *   3D camera position $\mathbf{t}_j \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
    *   Focal length $f_j \in \mathbb{R}$. Stored as compressed vertical offset $\hat{f}_j = f_j / \sqrt{h}$, where $h$ is image height in pixels [S1].
*   **Network Architecture:**
    *   **Fine/Decomposition Network:** MLP with 8 layers of 128 features [S1].
    *   **Density Output:** 1 feature layer [S1].
    *   **Radiance Output:** MLP with a hidden layer for view/appearance conditioned radiance [S1].
    *   **BRDF Decoder:** Compresses main network features to 16 features, then expands to BRDF parameters [S1].
*   **Sampling:** 128 points sampled along the ray in fixed steps. No coarse network is used [S1].

**Algorithm Steps**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize camera poses based on annotated quadrants and an initial Field of View of 53.13 degrees [S1].
    *   Initialize Fourier embedding frequencies with logarithmic spacing, adding Gaussian distributed noise as offsets to prevent axis-aligned artifacts [S1].
    *   Initialize camera multiplex weights $\mathbf{S}_j$ using a memory bank and momentum [S1].

2.  **Ray Casting and Bounds:**
    *   For each image $j$, define the sampling range based on the camera distance to the origin $|\mathbf{o}|$.
    *   Near bound: $|\mathbf{o}| - v$.
    *   Far bound: $|\mathbf{o}| + v$.
    *   $v$ is the sampling radius with a diameter of 1 [S1].

3.  **Forward Pass:**
    *   Cast rays through the volume.
    *   Query the Fine Network for $\sigma, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{n}$.
    *   Compute direct color $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ and BRDF-based color $\hat{\mathbf{c}}$ [S1].
    *   Render mask using volume rendering [S1].

4.  **Loss Calculation:**
    *   Compute individual loss terms (see Formulas).
    *   Apply **Camera Multiplex Weighting**:
        $$ \mathbf{S}_j = \text{softmax}(-\lambda_s \mathbf{L}_{\text{Mask}, ij}) $$
        where $\mathbf{S}_j \in \mathbb{R}^4$ is the weight vector for the 4 multiplex cameras, $\lambda_s$ is a scalar gradually increased during optimization, and $\mathbf{L}_{\text{Mask}, ij}$ is the mask reconstruction loss for camera $i$ and image $j$ [S1].
    *   Re-weigh network loss: $\mathbf{L}_{\text{Network}, j} = \sum_i S_{ji} \mathbf{L}_{\text{Network}, ij}$ [S1].
    *   Apply **Posterior Scaling** (for network optimization only, not camera optimization):
        *   Maintain a circular buffer of ~1000 recent mask and rendered image losses [S1].
        *   Calculate mean $\mu_l$ and standard deviation $\sigma_l$ [S1].
        *   Compute scaling factor: $sp_j = \max(\tanh(\frac{\mu_l - (L_{\text{Mask}, j} + L_{\text{Image}, j})}{\sigma_l}) + 1, 1)$ [S1].
        *   Apply scaling: $\mathbf{L}_{\text{Network}, j} = sp_j \mathbf{L}_{\text{Network}, j}$ [S1].
    *   For camera optimization, use the mean loss without posterior scaling to allow badly initialized poses to recover [S1].

5.  **Optimization Update:**
    *   Update networks using Adam optimizer (LR $10^{-4}$, decay order of magnitude every 300k steps) [S1].
    *   Update cameras using Adam optimizer (LR $3 \times 10^{-3}$, decay order of magnitude every 70k steps) [S1].
    *   Apply optimization scheduling variables $\lambda_b, \lambda_a, \lambda_c$ to fade direct color optimization to BRDF optimization, scale specific losses, and increase image resolution/reduce active multiplex cameras [S1].

**Formulas**

*   **Mask Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{Mask}} = L_{\text{BCE}} + L_{\text{Background}} $$
    where $L_{\text{BCE}}$ is binary cross-entropy and $L_{\text{Background}}$ forces background samples to 0 [S1].

*   **Initialization Loss (Prevents Illumination Local Minima):**
    $$ L_{\text{Init}} = L_{\text{MSE}}(\mathbf{C}_s, \mathbf{bc}) $$
    MSE between input color $\mathbf{C}_s$ and base color $\mathbf{bc}$ [S1].

*   **Network Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{Network}} = \lambda_b L_{\text{Image}}(\mathbf{C}_s, \tilde{\mathbf{c}}) + (1 - \lambda_b) L_{\text{Image}}(\mathbf{C}_s, \hat{\mathbf{c}}) + L_{\text{Mask}} + \lambda_a L_{\text{Init}} + \lambda_{\text{ndir}} L_{\text{ndir}} + \lambda_{\text{Smooth}} L_{\text{Smooth}} + \lambda_{\text{Dec\_Smooth}} L_{\text{Dec\_Smooth}} + \lambda_{\text{Dec\_Sparsity}} L_{\text{Dec\_Sparsity}} $$
    where $L_{\text{Image}}$ is the Chabonnier loss: $\sqrt{(g-p)^2 + 0.001^2}$ [S1].

*   **Decomposition Sparsity Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{Dec\_Sparsity}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i |e_i| $$
    where $N$ is the number of random rays and $e_i$ is the BRDF embedding [S1].

*   **Decomposition Smoothness Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{Dec\_Smooth}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i |f(\theta; e_i) - f(\theta; e_i + \varepsilon)| $$
    where $f(\theta)$ is the BRDF decoder, and $\varepsilon$ is Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.01 [S1].

*   **Camera Look-At Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{lookat}} $$
    L1 loss on the look-at vector $\mathbf{r}_j$ to constrain the camera to look at the object [S1].

*   **Volume Padding Loss:**
    $$ L_{\text{Bounds}} = \max((v - |\mathbf{t}_j|)^2, 0) $$
    Prevents cameras from going too far into the volume bound $v$ [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $\varepsilon$: Standard deviation of Gaussian noise for smoothness loss. Value: 0.01 [S1].
*   Initial Field of View: 53.13 degrees [S1].
*   $\lambda_b$: Scheduling variable for fading direct color optimization to BRDF optimization [S1].
*   $\lambda_a$: Scheduling variable scaling specific losses (e.g., $L_{\text{Init}}$) [S1].
*   $\lambda_c$: Scheduling variable to increase image resolution and reduce active multiplex cameras [S1].
*   $\lambda_s$: Scalar gradually increased during optimization for camera multiplex weighting [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Local Minima:** Camera pose optimization is highly non-convex. Using a single camera per image leads to local minima. The multiplex strategy mitigates this [S1].
*   **Noisy Masks:** Automatic segmentation masks (e.g., U2-Net) can be imperfect. Posterior scaling reduces the influence of images with noisy masks or high reconstruction error [S1].
*   **Axis-Aligned Artifacts:** Standard Fourier embeddings can cause stripes. Adding Gaussian noise to frequencies prevents this [S1].
*   **Illumination/Color Ambiguity:** Without constraints, illumination can fall into local minima (e.g., tinting the object to match illumination). $L_{\text{Init}}$ prevents this by anchoring base color to input colors [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On varying illumination datasets, SAMURAI achieves higher PSNR and SSIM than BARF-A and NeRD when poses are not known, and comparable performance when poses are available. Specifically, on "Poses Not Known," SAMURAI achieves PSNR 23.46 and SSIM 0.90, compared to BARF-A's 16.9 and 0.79 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles unconstrained in-the-wild images with varying backgrounds and illuminations; does not require perfect camera poses or segmentation masks; produces relightable 3D assets; flexible camera parameterization for varying distances [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires manual annotation of rough camera pose quadrants (3-5 minutes per 80-image collection); optimization is complex due to multiplexing and posterior scaling; relies on automatic segmentation which may be noisy [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When reconstructing 3D assets from unstructured, in-the-wild image collections with unknown camera poses and varying lighting, and when relightable outputs are desired [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When high-quality, controlled studio captures with known poses are available (simpler methods suffice); when real-time rendering is required without mesh extraction [S1].

## Background
SAMURAI was proposed by Boss et al. in 2022 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of prior methods like NeRF, which assume fixed camera poses and cannot relight scenes, and Neural-PIL, which requires perfect camera poses and segmentation masks [S1]. It builds on [View-Dependent Appearance and Lighting](18221-view-dependent-appearance-and-lighting.md) by removing the view direction condition from color prediction to prevent spurious correlations, thereby improving synthesis consistency [S1]. It is designed for applications in AR, VR, and games where high-quality 3D assets from casual captures are needed [S1].
