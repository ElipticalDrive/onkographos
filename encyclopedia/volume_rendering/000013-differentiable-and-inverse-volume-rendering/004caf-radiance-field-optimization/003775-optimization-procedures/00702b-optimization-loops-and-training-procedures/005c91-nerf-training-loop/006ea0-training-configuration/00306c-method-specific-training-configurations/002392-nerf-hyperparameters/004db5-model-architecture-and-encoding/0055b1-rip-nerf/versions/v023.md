# Rip-NeRF

Also called: Rip-NeRF25k

## Overview
Rip-NeRF is a hybrid radiance field method that renders high-fidelity, anti-aliased images by combining cone casting with anisotropic 3D Gaussian characterization and Ripmap Encoding [S1]. It solves the aliasing artifacts inherent in discrete point-based sampling by using area-sampling strategies, specifically projecting 3D Gaussians onto Platonic Solid faces to query features via tetra-linear interpolation [S1]. Unlike Zip-NeRF, which uses an effective but computationally expensive multi-sampling mechanism, Rip-NeRF achieves faster training, smaller model size, and higher rendering speeds while maintaining comparable or superior visual quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated multi-view 2D images.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel colors $\mathbf{c}$ and volume density $\sigma$ for each ray, optimized end-to-end.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ripmap Encoding:** A hierarchical feature grid.
    *   **Base Level ($R_{0,0}$):** Learnable parameters. Shape: $H=512, W=512, C=16$ [S1].
    *   **Derived Levels:** Generated from the base level using average pooling, not learnable [S1].
    *   **Structure:** Defined by indices $(i, j)$ for levels in $x$ and $y$ directions [S1].
2.  **Platonic Solid:** Default is an icosahedron with 10 unparalleled faces $\{P_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ [S1].
3.  **3D Gaussian:** Characterizes a conical frustum, parameterized by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].
4.  **Tiny MLP ($F_\theta$):** Takes aggregated features and view direction to predict $\mathbf{c}$ and $\sigma$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Cone Casting and Frustum Division:**
    For each pixel, cast a cone into the scene and divide it into multiple conical frustums [S1]. This avoids discrete point-based sampling of continuous signals [S1].

2.  **3D Gaussian Characterization:**
    Characterize each conical frustum as an anisotropic 3D Gaussian with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S1].

3.  **Platonic Solid Projection:**
    Project the 3D Gaussian onto the faces of the Platonic solid to form 2D Gaussians [S1].
    For each face $P_i$, compute the projected mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{proj}_i$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{proj}_i$:
    $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}^{proj}_i = M_i \boldsymbol{\mu} $$
    $$ \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{proj}_i = M_i \boldsymbol{\Sigma} M_i^T $$
    where $M_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$ is the projection matrix mapping the 3D world coordinate system to the 2D coordinate system of plane $P_i$ [S1].

4.  **Ripmap Encoding Query:**
    Query a feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i$ from the Ripmap Encoding of each plane $P_i$ using tetra-linear interpolation [S1].
    The interpolation position $(p_x, p_y)$ and level $(l_x, l_y)$ are determined by $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{proj}_i$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{proj}_i$ respectively [S1].
    The level indices are derived from the base level $R_{0,0}$ using average pooling [S1]:
    $$ R_{i,j} = \begin{cases} \text{Avg}_{2\times1} R_{i, j-1} & \text{if } j \neq 0 \\ \text{Avg}_{1\times2} R_{i-1, j} & \text{if } i \neq 0 \text{ \& } j = 0 \\ R_{0,0} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $i$ and $j$ are the indices of the levels in the $x$ and $y$ directions [S1].
    A hyper-parameter $w$ (set to 2.0) adjusts how much probability mass of the Gaussian footprint is covered during this query [S1].

5.  **Feature Aggregation:**
    Concatenate all feature vectors $\{\mathbf{f}_i | i = 1, \dots, n\}$ from the corresponding planes $\{P_i\}$ to form the final feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ of the 3D Gaussian [S1].

6.  **Density and Color Prediction:**
    Aggregate the final feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ with the encoded view direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1]. Pass this combined input through the tiny MLP $F_\theta$ to estimate the color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ of the conical frustum [S1].

7.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Render the pixel color by integrating the estimated color and density along the ray using standard volume rendering equations [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Optimize the system end-to-end using a photometric loss between rendered and observed images [S1].
    The loss is scaled by the area of the pixel footprint on the image plane, denoted as "area loss" $L_{area}$ [S1]. This makes the loss area-aware [S1].

**Parameters:**

*   **Hyper-parameter $w$:** 2.0 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Empty Space:** Use a binary occupancy grid (e.g., from NerfAcc) to skip samples in empty areas, improving efficiency [S1].
*   **Precision:** The use of tetra-linear interpolation and average pooling for derived levels ensures consistency across levels [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The method relies on calibrated multi-view images; uncalibrated inputs may require additional structure acquisition steps not detailed in the core method [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** 2.6 hours on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU for 120k iterations [S1]. Rip-NeRF25k (25k iterations) takes 32 minutes (11.76% of Zip-NeRF's 4.5h) [S1].
*   **Model Size:** 160 MB [S1].
*   **GPU Memory:** 20 GB during training [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** 3 FPS [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Outperforms Zip-NeRF in training time (2.6h vs 4.5h), model size (160MB vs 592MB), and rendering speed (3 FPS vs 0.25 FPS) [S1]. Zip-NeRF has slightly better SSIM [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-fidelity anti-aliased rendering, efficient anisotropic area-sampling, compact representation, fast training and rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires calibrated multi-view images [S1]. Designed for bounded objects; performance on unbounded scenes may be less optimal compared to methods like Zip-NeRF [S1].

## Background
Rip-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Rip-NeRF: Anti-aliasing Radiance Fields with Ripmap-Encoded Platonic Solids" [S1]. It builds upon previous work in hybrid representations and area-sampling, such as Mip-NeRF [Barron et al. 2021] and Tri-MipRF [Hu et al. 2023], to address the limitations of discrete sampling in NeRF [Mildenhall et al. 2020]. The method was developed to provide a more efficient alternative to multi-sampling approaches like Zip-NeRF [Barron et al. 2023] while maintaining high rendering quality.
