# ExtremeNeRF

Also called: Few-shot NeRF under varying-illumination, NeRF Extreme

## Overview
ExtremeNeRF is a few-shot view synthesis framework designed to generate novel views from sparse multi-view images captured under unconstrained, varying illumination [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF or illumination-robust methods like NeRF-W and RegNeRF that require dense input views or constrained lighting, ExtremeNeRF achieves robustness by enforcing multi-view albedo consistency through a two-stage intrinsic decomposition pipeline [S1]. It distinguishes itself by using an offline full-image network (FIDNet) to generate pseudo-albedo ground truths for input images, which then guide an online patch-wise network (PIDNet) to decompose synthesized novel view patches, ensuring that the learned geometry and appearance are illumination-invariant [S1].

## How it works
ExtremeNeRF builds on the standard NeRF architecture [Parent](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md), consisting of a coarse and fine MLP that map 3D positions and view directions to volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md]. The method introduces two additional neural networks: FIDNet (Full-image Intrinsic Decomposition Network) and PIDNet (Patch-wise Intrinsic Decomposition Network) [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_i\}$ with known camera poses $(K_i, T_i)$ and intrinsics $K_i$, captured under varying illumination. The input resolution is $300 \times 400$ pixels [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model capable of rendering novel views $\hat{c}(x)$ and depth maps $\hat{d}(x)$ for arbitrary camera poses, along with decomposed albedo $\hat{a}(x)$ and shading $\hat{s}(x)$ components [S1].

### Data Structures
1.  **NeRF MLPs:** Standard coarse and fine networks. The positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2)$ is applied to coordinates and view directions [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].
2.  **FIDNet:** A pre-trained intrinsic decomposition network (specifically IIDWW [S1]) used offline. It takes an input image $I$ and outputs a pseudo-albedo map $a_{pseudo}$ [S1].
3.  **PIDNet:** A convolutional network used during training. It takes a patch of the synthesized color image $\hat{c}(x)$ of size $S_{patch} \times S_{patch}$ (default $32 \times 32$) and outputs local albedo $\hat{a}(x)$ and shading $\hat{s}(x)$ [S1].
4.  **Depth Buffer:** Stores rendered depth $\hat{d}(x)$ for each pixel, computed as:
    $$
    \hat{d}(x) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i t_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i}
    $$
    where $T_i$ is transmittance, $\alpha_i$ is opacity, and $t_i$ is the sampled depth [S1].

### Parameters
*   $S_{patch}$: Patch size for PIDNet input/output. Default: $32 \times 32$ [S1].
*   $re$: Error rate coefficient for the visibility mask. Diminishes toward the end of training [S1].
*   $M_{proj}$: Maximum projection error threshold [S1].
*   Training iterations: 70,000 per scene [S1].
*   Hardware: 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Offline Albedo Extraction (FIDNet):**
    For each input image $I_i$, apply the pre-trained FIDNet to extract the global context-aware pseudo-albedo $a_{pseudo, i}$ [S1]. This step is performed once before training begins [S1].

2.  **NeRF Rendering:**
    For a random ray $r$ sampled from a novel view:
    a. Sample $N$ points $t_i$ along the ray.
    b. Query the NeRF MLPs to obtain volume density $\sigma_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$.
    c. Compute opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ and transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ [S1].
    d. Render the color $\hat{c}(x)$ and depth $\hat{d}(x)$ for the pixel $x$ corresponding to ray $r$ [S1].

3.  **Geometric Alignment (Pixel Correspondence):**
    For a pixel $x$ in the novel view with rendered depth $\hat{d}(x)$, compute the corresponding pixel $x'$ in an input view $I_j$:
    a. Map pixel $x$ to world coordinates $x_w$:
       $$
       x_w = T_j \hat{d}(x) K_j^{-1} \bar{x}
       $$
       where $\bar{x} = [x^T, 1]^T$ is the homogeneous coordinate, $T_j$ is the camera-to-world transformation, and $K_j$ is the intrinsic matrix [S1].
    b. Project $x_w$ to the input view $I_j$ (with pose $T'_j, K'_j$) to find $x'$:
       $$
       x' = (K'_j (T'_j)^{-1} T_j) \hat{d}(x) K_j^{-1} \bar{x}
       $$
       Note: The source formula uses $T$ and $T'$ generically; specifically, if mapping from novel view $j$ to input view $k$, the transformation involves the relative pose [S1].
    c. If $x'$ is outside the field of view of the input image, discard the correspondence [S1].

4.  **Projection Error Calculation:**
    Compute the projection error $E_{proj}(x')$ for the corresponding pixel $x'$:
    $$
    E_{proj}(x') = (\hat{d}(x') - \tilde{d}(x'))^2
    $$
    where $\hat{d}(x')$ is the depth rendered from the novel view at the projected location, and $\tilde{d}(x')$ is the depth projected from the input view's geometry (if available) or derived from the novel view's depth consistency [S1]. *Note: The source defines $\tilde{d}(x')$ as "projected depth, a byproduct of Eq. 7". In the context of few-shot NeRF without ground truth depth, this often refers to consistency checks between adjacent pixels or multi-view consistency. The source explicitly states $E_{proj}$ measures the difference between rendered depth and projected depth.* [S1].

5.  **Visibility Mask Weighting:**
    Calculate the weight $\omega(x)$ to exclude invalid correspondences caused by occlusions or large projection errors:
    $$
    \omega(x) = r_e \left( 1 - \frac{E_{proj}(x')}{M_{proj}} \right)
    $$
    where $r_e$ is the error rate coefficient and $M_{proj}$ is the maximum projection error. $r_e$ decreases as training progresses to rectify inaccurate geometry alignments while persistent occlusions remain [S1].

6.  **Patch-wise Intrinsic Decomposition (PIDNet):**
    Extract a patch $\hat{c}(x)$ of size $S_{patch} \times S_{patch}$ centered at $x$ from the rendered novel view image.
    Feed $\hat{c}(x)$ into PIDNet to obtain the estimated albedo $\hat{a}(x)$ and shading $\hat{s}(x)$ [S1].

7.  **Loss Computation:**
    Compute the total loss $L_{total}$ as a weighted sum of the following components:
    a. **Color Loss:** Mean Squared Error between rendered color and ground truth color $c_{gt}(x)$ for pixels in the training set $S$:
       $$
       L_{color} = \sum_{x \in S} \|\hat{c}(x) - c_{gt}(x)\|_2^2
       $$
       [S1].
    b. **Albedo Consistency Loss ($L_{ac}$):** Enforces that the albedo extracted from the novel view $\hat{a}(x)$ matches the pseudo-albedo $a_{pseudo, j}$ from the corresponding input view pixel $x'$, weighted by $\omega(x)$:
       $$
       L_{ac} = \sum_{x \in P} \omega(x) \|\hat{a}(x) - a_{pseudo, j}(x')\|_2^2
       $$
       where $P$ is the set of all pixels in the novel view [S1].
    c. **Depth Consistency Loss ($L_{dc}$):** Regularizes the projection error between adjacent pixels to prevent smoothing unrelated surfaces due to occlusions:
       $$
       L_{dc} = \sum_{x' \in P'} \sum_{y \in N(x')} (E_{proj}(y) - E_{proj}(x'))^2
       $$
       where $P'$ is the set of pixels in the input view and $N(x')$ are the 4-neighbor adjacent pixels of $x'$ [S1].
    d. **Auxiliary Losses:** Depth smoothness loss $L_{ds}$, edge-preserving loss $L_{edge}$, intrinsic smoothness loss $L_{pid}$, chromaticity consistency loss $L_{chrom}$, and frequency regularization mask [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Update the NeRF MLPs, PIDNet, and any other trainable parameters using gradient descent to minimize $L_{total}$ for 70,000 iterations [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Projection Errors:** Incorrect synthesized depth values lead to large projection errors $E_{proj}$. If $E_{proj}$ is large, the correspondence is likely invalid due to occlusion or floating artifacts. The visibility mask $\omega(x)$ handles this by reducing the weight [S1].
*   **Occlusions:** Direct minimization of $E_{proj}$ can be counterproductive because occlusions smooth unrelated surface depths. The depth consistency loss $L_{dc}$ regularizes the *variation* of projection error between adjacent pixels rather than minimizing the error itself [S1].
*   **Geometry Rectification:** The error rate coefficient $r_e$ diminishes toward the end of training. This allows the model to rectify inaccurate geometry alignments while still respecting persistent occlusions [S1].
*   **FIDNet Substitution:** Replacing FIDNet with other intrinsic decomposition models (e.g., Das et al. 2022) degrades performance, suggesting the specific pre-trained model (IIDWW) is crucial for the pseudo-albedo quality [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** ExtremeNeRF requires approximately 1.5 hours for few-shot view synthesis with 3 input views on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs [S1]. This is significantly faster than other methods like RegNeRF (2.4 hours) and FreeNeRF (2 hours), and much faster than dense-view methods which require 10–20 hours [S1].
*   **Memory:** The method uses standard NeRF memory plus the overhead of FIDNet (offline, pre-computed) and PIDNet (online, patch-wise). PIDNet operates on $32 \times 32$ patches, keeping memory footprint low compared to global intrinsic decomposition [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Works with very few input views (few-shot) [S1].
    *   Robust to unconstrained, varying illumination [S1].
    *   Fast training time compared to baselines [S1].
    *   Produces illumination-robust geometry and appearance [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the quality of the pre-trained FIDNet (IIDWW). Performance degrades if FIDNet is replaced with inferior models [S1].
    *   Performance is sensitive to geometric alignment errors, handled by the visibility mask but still a potential pitfall in highly occluded scenes [S1].
    *   Evaluated on specific datasets (Phototourism F3, NeRF Extreme); generalization to other domains not explicitly detailed [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When input data consists of sparse multi-view images with varying illumination, and fast training is desired.
*   **When Not to Use:** When dense multi-view images are available (other methods may be more accurate), or when illumination is constrained and consistent (standard NeRF suffices).

## Background
ExtremeNeRF was introduced in the paper "Few-Shot Neural Radiance Fields under Unconstrained Illumination" presented at the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence in 2024 [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF and existing illumination-robust methods (like NeRF-W, RegNeRF) that require large numbers of input images. The authors created the "NeRF Extreme" benchmark, the first in-the-wild benchmark for novel view synthesis with varying illumination, to evaluate such methods [S1]. The method builds upon the intrinsic image decomposition literature (Lambertian assumption $\log c = \log a + \log s$) and integrates it with NeRF optimization via a two-stage pipeline [S1].
