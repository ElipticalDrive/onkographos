# DoF-NeRF

Also called: Depth-of-Field Meets Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
DoF-NeRF is a neural rendering framework that extends Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to simulate the depth-of-field (DoF) effects of a physical lens, allowing it to operate on views with different focus configurations and manipulate DoF effects by adjusting virtual aperture and focus parameters [S1]. Unlike vanilla NeRF, which assumes a pinhole camera model and all-in-focus inputs, DoF-NeRF explicitly models the aperture and focus distance, enabling it to handle shallow DoF inputs and produce clear, all-in-focus novel view synthesis with less blurriness and more texture details [S1]. It achieves this by combining optical models with classical volume rendering to approximate the DoF effect under arbitrary aperture size and focus distance configurations [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $I = \{I_1, I_2, ..., I_N\}$ with known camera parameters (generated via COLMAP or similar structure-from-motion tools) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model $G_\Theta$ capable of rendering all-in-focus views, along with optimized learnable aperture parameters $\hat{K}$ and focus distances $\hat{F}$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **NeRF Model ($G_\Theta$):** Standard NeRF MLP architecture taking 3D coordinates and viewing directions as input, outputting color and density [S1].
*   **Learnable Parameters:**
    *   Aperture parameters: $\hat{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}$, one scalar per training image [S1].
    *   Focus distances: $\hat{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 1}$, one scalar per training image [S1].
    *   These are initialized as PyTorch `nn.Parameter` with `requires_grad=True` [S1].

**Formulas and Definitions**
*   **Circle of Confusion (CoC) Diameter:** The diameter $\delta(h_t)$ of the blur circle for a point at object distance $h_t$ is given by:
    $$ \delta(h_t) = K \times \left| \frac{1}{F} - \frac{1}{h_t} \right| $$
    where $K = f \times D$ is the aperture parameter (product of focal length $f$ and aperture diameter $D$), $F$ is the focus distance, and $h_t$ is the distance from the camera center to the spatial point [S1].
*   **Scattered Radiance:** The radiance at a pixel $p$ is computed by summing contributions from neighboring pixels $p_j$. For a concentration point $p$, the scattered radiance $\hat{C}^s(p)$ is:
    $$ \hat{C}^s(p) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_s} K_{volume}(r_p, h_i) c(r_p(h_i), d)}{\pi \delta_c^2(p)/4} $$
    where $K_{volume}$ is the volume rendering weight, $c$ is the color, and $\delta_c(p)$ is the CoC radius at pixel $p$ [S1].
*   **Final Observed Color:** The final color $\hat{I}(p)$ for a ray passing through pixel $p$ is determined by the concentrate-and-scatter model:
    $$ \hat{I}(p) = \sum_{j=1}^{P} \mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2} \hat{C}^s(p_j) $$
    where $\mathbb{1}$ is an indicator function checking if the CoC diameter is larger than the distance to the neighbor, and $P$ is the number of rays in the patch [S1].
*   **Loss Function:** The optimization loss $L$ is the photometric error between synthesized views $\hat{I}$ and observations $I$:
    $$ L = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \| I_n - \hat{I}_n \|_2^2 $$
    [S1]

**Algorithm Steps**
The method employs a two-stage optimization process to reduce complexity and avoid divergence [S1].

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize NeRF model $G_\Theta$ [S1].
    *   Initialize learnable parameters $\hat{K}$ and $\hat{F}$ for $N$ images [S1].

2.  **Stage 1: Pinhole Camera Training (Pre-training):**
    *   Set aperture parameter $K=0$ for all images, degenerating the model to a linear pinhole camera [S1].
    *   For each iteration $i$ from 1 to $N_{pretrain}$:
        *   Sample random rays $r_i$ and corresponding ground truth images $I_i$ [S1].
        *   Render color $\hat{I}_i$ using standard volume rendering [S1].
        *   Compute loss $L = \text{loss}(\hat{I}_i, I_i)$ [S1].
        *   Backpropagate and update NeRF parameters $\Theta$ [S1].
    *   *Note:* During this stage, $\hat{K}$ and $\hat{F}$ are not optimized [S1].

3.  **Stage 2: Joint Optimization with Concentrate-and-Scatter:**
    *   For each iteration $i$ from $N_{pretrain} + 1$ to $N_{iter}$:
        *   **Patch-based Ray Selection:**
            *   Define anchors every $N_{anchor}$ pixels on the imaging plane.
            *   Randomly select one anchor to define an $N_{patch} \times N_{patch}$ patch [S1].
            *   Sample rays $r_i$ passing through pixels in the selected patch [S1].
        *   **Concentration:**
            *   Compute the concentration color $\hat{C}^s$ for the rays using the NeRF model and current parameters [S1].
        *   **CoC Radius Calculation:**
            *   Compute the CoC radius $\delta_c$ for each pixel using the current $\hat{K}$, $\hat{F}$, and estimated depth $h_c$ [S1].
        *   **Scatter:**
            *   Compute the final rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ using the scatter formula [S1].
        *   **Optimization:**
            *   Compute loss $L = \text{loss}(\hat{I}_i, I_i)$ [S1].
            *   Backpropagate and update NeRF parameters $\Theta$, aperture $\hat{K}$, and focus distances $\hat{F}$ jointly [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Divergence:** Using random rays for the concentrate-and-scatter method in the initial stages often leads to divergence or sub-optimal results due to the gathering of rays [S1]. This is mitigated by the two-stage approach [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** Computing rays for the whole imaging plane in each iteration is computationally unacceptable [S1]. Patch-based selection is used as a compromise [S1].
*   **Blurriness:** Vanilla NeRF on shallow DoF inputs tends to generate blurry renderings and miss texture details [S1]. DoF-NeRF addresses this by explicitly modeling aperture and focus [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the real-world dataset (amiya scene), DoF-NeRF achieves PSNR 28.311 vs NeRF 26.924, SSIM 0.9289 vs 0.9092, and LPIPS 0.1370 vs 0.1633 [S1]. On the synthetic dataset (room scene), DoF-NeRF achieves PSNR 29.443 vs NeRF 26.668, SSIM 0.9135 vs 0.8743, and LPIPS 0.1502 vs 0.1961 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Explicit aperture modeling enables NeRF to tackle shallow DoF inputs and benefits all-in-focus novel view synthesis [S1]. It produces photo-realistic all-in-focus novel views with fine-grained details [S1]. It is plug-and-play and can be inserted into NeRF-based frameworks [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The method requires optimizing additional parameters (aperture and focus distance) which increases complexity compared to vanilla NeRF [S1]. It assumes ideal optical systems and neglects ray distortions caused by the lens [S1].
*   **When to use:** When input images have shallow depth-of-field and the goal is to synthesize clear, all-in-focus novel views or to simulate DoF effects with adjustable aperture and focus [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When inputs are already all-in-focus and high fidelity is required without the overhead of DoF simulation, vanilla NeRF may be sufficient [S1].

## Background
DoF-NeRF was introduced by Zijin Wu, Juewen Peng, and Weicai Zhong from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Huawei [S1]. It was presented at the ACM Multimedia Conference (MM '22) in Lisboa, Portugal, on October 10–14, 2022 [S1]. The work addresses the limitation of existing NeRF models that assume a linear pinhole camera model and all-in-focus inputs, which limits their applicability to real-world images with finite depth-of-field [S1]. It builds upon the principles of geometric optics and classical volume rendering [S1].
