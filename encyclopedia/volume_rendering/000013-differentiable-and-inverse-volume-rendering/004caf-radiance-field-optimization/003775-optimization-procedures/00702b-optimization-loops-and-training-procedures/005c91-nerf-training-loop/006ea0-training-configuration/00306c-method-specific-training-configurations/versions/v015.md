# Method-Specific Training Configurations


## Overview
This family defines the hyperparameters, optimization schedules, and architectural constraints specific to individual Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) variants, rather than generic training loop mechanics. It addresses the problem that standard NeRF configurations (e.g., uniform sampling, ReLU activations, MSE loss) often fail to converge or produce artifacts in specialized settings such as unbounded scenes, high-resolution super-resolution, or generative frameworks. It sets itself apart from [Training Loop Setup](1522-training-loop-setup.md) and [Learning Rate Scheduling](2701-learning-rate-scheduling.md) by providing the concrete numerical values and structural modifications required for specific algorithms like Mip-NeRF, DONeRF, and Nerfacto.

## Shared principles
The training configuration for NeRF variants generally involves minimizing a photometric loss between rendered pixel colors $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth images $C_{gt}$ by optimizing neural network parameters [Parent]. The core loop involves ray sampling, point sampling, feature encoding, network forward pass, volume rendering, loss computation, and optimization [Parent].

*   **Volume Rendering Integral:** The accumulated color is computed as:
    $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [Parent].
*   **Optimizer:** Most variants use the Adam optimizer or its variants (AdamW) [S1, S2, S3, S4, S6][S9, unofficial].
*   **Loss Functions:** Photometric losses include Mean Squared Error (MSE), Charbonnier loss, or perceptual losses (LPIPS) [S1, S2, S3, S6]. Generative variants may include GAN losses (R1 regularization) and identity regularizers [S1].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Hyperparameters](3065-nerf-hyperparameters.md)
Defines the baseline configuration for standard NeRF, including network architecture (8-layer MLP, 256/512 units), sampling strategies (64 coarse, 128 fine samples), and optimization schedules (Adam, $5 \times 10^{-4}$ initial LR, 200k–300k iterations). It highlights the sensitivity of NeRF to the balance between coarse and fine sampling densities.

### [Mip-NeRF Optimization and Activation Details](3409-mip-nerf-optimization-and-activation-details.md)
Specifies implementation details for Mip-NeRF and JaxNeRF, focusing on Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE), shifted softplus density activation, and widened sigmoid color activation to prevent optimization failure modes like zero gradients or saturation. It uses a specific learning rate schedule combining warmup and logarithmic annealing.

### [DONeRF](4461-donerf.md)
Replaces standard raymarching with a dual-network design: a depth oracle network and a shading network. The oracle predicts optimal sample locations (2–16 samples per ray) using a classification task on discretized depth bins, while the shading network renders color. Training is sequential: Phase 1 trains the oracle with BCE loss, and Phase 2 trains the shading network with MSE and opacity loss.

### [AR-NeRF Training Configuration](12407-ar-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Extends the pi-GAN framework to learn depth and defocus effects from unposed natural images. It models camera aperture size and focus distance as continuous control parameters during training, enabling variable defocus blur generation. It uses FiLM-SIREN networks and GAN losses (R1, identity).

### [NeRF-SR Training Configuration](15560-nerf-sr-training-configuration.md)
Optimizes a neural radiance field with a supersampling strategy to synthesize high-resolution novel views from low-resolution inputs. It targets high-resolution output during the optimization loop, using 64 coarse and 64 fine samples per ray, and decaying learning rate from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ to $5 \times 10^{-6}$.

### Blender Dataset Simplified Configuration
For the Blender dataset, a simplified configuration is used: one round of sampling, 128 samples for the proposal MLP, 32 samples for the NeRF MLP, a proposal MLP with 4 layers and 256 hidden units, a NeRF MLP with 8 layers and 256 (or 512) hidden units, axis-aligned IPE, MSE loss, and no distortion regularizer [S1].

### LLFF Training Configuration
For the LLFF dataset, the distortion regularizer is not disabled, and Charbonnier loss is used instead of MSE. On LLFF with 256 hidden units, mip-NeRF achieves PSNR 26.93, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.177, Time 2.48 hrs, Params 0.61M; the proposed model achieves PSNR 26.68, SSIM 0.847, LPIPS 0.150, Time 2.39 hrs, Params 0.84M. With 512 hidden units, mip-NeRF achieves PSNR 27.01, SSIM 0.845, LPIPS 0.148, Time 6.15 hrs, Params 2.27M; the proposed model achieves PSNR 26.86, SSIM 0.858, LPIPS 0.128, Time 3.84 hrs, Params 3.23M. The proposed model does not outperform mip-NeRF in PSNR but yields improved SSIM and LPIPS metrics and a significant speedup when the NeRF MLP is large [S1].

### NeUDF Training Configuration
Uses the ADAM optimizer with a learning rate warming up to $2 \times 10^{-4}$ in the first 5k iterations, decreasing to $1 \times 10^{-5}$ by the end. Batch size is 512 random rays sampled from 8 randomly selected camera poses per iteration. Training duration is 400k iterations total, taking 9 hours with mask and 11 hours without mask on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU [S2].

### NeRF-SDP Training Configuration
Uses the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$ for Res-UNet and $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for other parts. Batch size is 500. Hardware is Nvidia GeForce RTX 3090. Total steps are 250,000: the first 170,000 steps optimize auxiliary and rendering losses, and the last 80,000 steps optimize only rendering loss. Feature dimensions are 24 for 2D features and 8 for 3D features. Datasets include LLFF, IBRNet, and Google Scanned [S3].

### Neuralangelo Training Configuration
Samples pixels from images randomly without tracking statistics and errors, using long training iterations (500k total) to reduce stochastics. ROI is assumed to be inside a unit sphere. Inactive hash resolution feature vectors are set to zero. Learning rate is $1 \times 10^{-3}$ with a linear warmup of 5k iterations, decaying by a factor of 10 at 300k and 400k iterations. Optimizer is AdamW with weight decay of $10^{-2}$, with $w_{iek}$ set to 0.1. SDF MLP has one layer; color MLP has four layers. Batch size is 1 for DTU benchmark and 16 for Tanks and Temples dataset [S4].

### GO-NeRF Training Configuration
Optimizes the generation model on a single Nvidia 3090 GPU for a total of 20,000 iterations, with 30% of iterations trained with background augmentation. The scheduler values for $\lambda_S$ and $\lambda_O$ range from 30 to 300. $\lambda_R$ is set to 500 [S5].

### Tri-MipRF Training Configuration
Base mipmap shape $M_{l0}$ is H=512, W=512, C=16. Optimizer is AdamW with weight decay $1 \times 10^{-5}$ and learning rate $2 \times 10^{-3}$, scheduled by MultiStepLR. Tri-Mip encoding learning rate is scaled up 10x relative to the MLP. Loss function is Area loss $L_{area}$, scaling pixel loss by the area of its footprint on the image plane. Implementation uses PyTorch with tiny-cuda-nn extension and nvdiffrast library [S6].

### NeRF† Implementation Configuration
A variant of nerfacto that uses a standard MLP density field instead of HashMLPDensityField. For Blender dataset: $t_n = 2.0$, $t_f = 6.0$. Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, distortion loss, and scene contraction are disabled. Background color is manually set to white. Batch size = 2048 rays. Initial sampler = uniform. Training epochs = $16.5 \times 10^3$. For LLFF dataset: Camera optimizer, appearance embedding, and distortion loss are disabled. Scene contraction uses $\ell_\infty$ norm. Batch size = 1024 rays during training, 2048 during evaluation. Training epochs = $30 \times 10^3$. Leaves scene uses uniform initial proposal sampler. On Mip-NeRF 360, nerfacto† achieves PSNR 24.20, SSIM 0.726, LPIPS 0.247. Compared to nerfacto (HashMLPDensityField), nerfacto† achieves comparable PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360 but is significantly slower [S7].

### DVGO Reference Architecture
Uses Re:NeRF compression method. Hardware is NVIDIA A40 GPU. Software is PyTorch 1.12 [S8].

### Nerfacto Training Configuration
Uses Adam optimizer with a learning rate schedule of $10^{-2} \to 10^{-4}$, trained for 30k iterations by default [S9, unofficial].

## Background
The optimization of Neural Radiance Fields has evolved from the original NeRF implementation [S1] to include specialized configurations for different datasets and architectural variants. Mip-NeRF introduced anti-aliasing and specific activation functions to handle unbounded scenes [S1]. DONeRF addressed computational costs with a dual-network approach [S3]. Subsequent works like NeRF-SR, AR-NeRF, and Neuralangelo have further refined training configurations to handle super-resolution, generative tasks, and high-fidelity surface reconstruction [S1, S2, S4]. The Nerfacto configuration represents a modern baseline for production NeRFs [S9, unofficial].
