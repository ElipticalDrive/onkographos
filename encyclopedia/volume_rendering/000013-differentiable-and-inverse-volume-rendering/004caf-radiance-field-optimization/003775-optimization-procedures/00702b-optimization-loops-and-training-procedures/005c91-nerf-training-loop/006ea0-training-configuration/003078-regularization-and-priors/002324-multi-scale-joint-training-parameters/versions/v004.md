# Multi-scale Joint Training Parameters

Also called: Multi-scale joint training loss weights

## Overview
Multi-scale joint training parameters refer to the specific weighting coefficients and structural configurations used to balance photometric and auxiliary losses across different spatial resolutions or training stages in inverse rendering optimization. This method addresses the instability of gradient-based optimization in multi-view settings by ensuring that coarse-scale features are learned before fine-scale details are refined. It sets itself apart from single-scale training by explicitly decoupling the optimization burden across a hierarchy of scales, preventing the model from getting stuck in local minima caused by high-frequency noise or insufficient initial constraints [S1, S2].

## How it works
The method operates by defining a total loss function that aggregates errors from multiple scales or stages, each weighted by specific hyperparameters. In the context of dynamic growth networks, the loss is computed over $D$ stages, where $D$ corresponds to the current number of sub-networks or growth iterations [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered images from $D$ stages, ground truth images, uncertainty maps for each stage, and hyperparameters $\beta_1, \beta_2$.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar total loss value $\mathcal{L}$ used for backpropagation.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Loss Components:**
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$: Mean Squared Error loss for the output image of stage $i$.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$: Uncertainty loss for stage $i$, derived from the model's predicted uncertainty at that stage.
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $\beta_1$: Weight for the photometric (MSE) term.
    *   $\beta_2$: Weight for the uncertainty regularization term.

**Formulas:**
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^D \left( \beta_1 \mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i + \beta_2 \mathcal{L}_{unct}^i \right)
$$
Where:
*   $D$ is the current number of stages (also equal to $D-1$ times the network growth count).
*   $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ is the MSE loss for the image output of layer/stage $i$.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ is the uncertainty loss for layer/stage $i$.
*   $\beta_1 = 1.0$ and $\beta_2 = 0.1$ are the standard weights [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Initialize the network with $D=1$ (or the initial stage count).
2.  For each training iteration:
    a. Render images from all active stages $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$.
    b. Compute $\mathcal{L}_{MSE}^i$ between the rendered image and ground truth for each stage.
    c. Compute $\mathcal{L}_{unct}^i$ based on the uncertainty predictions for each stage.
    d. Calculate the total loss $\mathcal{L}$ using the weighted sum formula.
    e. Backpropagate $\mathcal{L}$ to update network parameters.
3.  If dynamic growth is triggered (e.g., based on uncertainty clustering), increment $D$ and repeat [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Weight Imbalance:** Incorrectly setting $\beta_2$ too high may cause the optimizer to prioritize uncertainty minimization over photometric accuracy, leading to blurry or structurally incorrect results. Conversely, too low a $\beta_2$ may fail to regularize the model effectively [S1].
*   **Stage Count:** The number of stages $D$ must be managed carefully; excessive stages without proper growth criteria can lead to overfitting or computational waste [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Multi-stage Training Protocol](14692-multi-stage-training-protocol.md)
This protocol structures the optimization of inverse rendering models into sequential phases to ensure stable convergence of coupled components. It addresses the dependency of lighting estimation on accurate geometry and appearance by decoupling the training of Direct Prediction (geometry/appearance) and Lighting Joint Prediction modules. Unlike end-to-end training which may suffer from gradient conflicts or poor initialization, this method progressively introduces complexity, starting with isolated component training before joint optimization and final refinement [14692-multi-stage-training-protocol.md].

### Cross-intrinsic Guidance
A coarse-to-fine training scheme that uses different camera intrinsic parameters (focal length and principal point) to construct an image pyramid, enhancing multi-scale feature learning [S2].

**Definition:**
The method constructs a 4-layer image pyramid by reconfiguring the camera intrinsic matrix $K$ for each layer $s$ in the set of downsampling factors $S$. The modified intrinsic matrix $K'_s$ is defined as [S2]:
$$
K'_s = \begin{bmatrix} f/s & 0 & c_x/s \\ 0 & f/s & c_y/s \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad s \in \mathbf{S}
$$
Where $f$ is the focal length, and $c_x, c_y$ are the principal point coordinates.

**Steps:**
1.  Define the set of downsampling factors $S = \{2^{k-1} \mid k = 4, 3, 2, 1\} = \{8, 4, 2, 1\}$ [S2].
2.  For each layer $s \in S$:
    a. Reconfigure the intrinsic matrix $K$ to $K'_s$ by dividing $f, c_x, c_y$ by $s$.
    b. Assign a matched multi-view setting $M_s$ to the layer. Larger downsampling factors (coarser layers) use more views to provide stronger multi-view constraints [S2].
    c. Run a few thousand iterations for the coarse stages ($s=8, 4, 2$) without fully training the model, treating them as coarse training phases [S2].
    d. Start fine training at $s=1$ (no downsampling) to refine details [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $S$: Set of downsampling factors $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$. The largest factor $s=8$ is empirically sufficient [S2].
*   $s=1$: Indicates no downsampling operation [S2].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Blurry Ground Truth:** Excessively large downsampling factors lead to blurry ground truth images, decreasing the fidelity of reconstructed features (e.g., 3D Gaussians) and increasing the difficulty for later refinement stages [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** In multi-stage protocols, the total training time is the sum of iterations across all stages. Cross-intrinsic guidance adds overhead for intrinsic matrix reconfiguration and multi-view setting management but reduces the number of iterations needed for convergence at fine scales by providing better initial constraints [S1, S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Dynamic growth (Recursive-NeRF) increases memory usage as $D$ grows, as new sub-networks are added. Cross-intrinsic guidance requires storing multiple versions of intrinsic matrices and potentially multiple sets of multi-view settings, but does not inherently increase model parameter count [S1, S2].
*   **Strengths:** Improves convergence stability in multi-view settings; reduces view-dependent noise; ensures coarse features are learned before fine details [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of stage counts and downsampling factors; may introduce complexity in implementation due to dynamic growth or multi-stage logic [S1, S2].
*   **When to use:** When training inverse rendering models with coupled components (geometry, appearance, lighting) or when multi-view consistency is critical and prone to instability [S1, S2, 14692-multi-stage-training-protocol.md].
*   **When not to use:** For simple single-view or low-complexity scenes where standard end-to-end training converges reliably [S1, S2].

## Background
The concept of multi-scale training and dynamic growth in neural radiance fields originated with Recursive-NeRF, which introduced an adaptive dynamic growth strategy based on uncertainty clustering [S1]. This was later extended in methods like MVGS (Multi-view-regulated Gaussian Splatting), which proposed cross-intrinsic guidance to leverage multi-view constraints at different scales [S2]. The multi-stage training protocol further generalizes this by decoupling optimization phases for coupled components [14692-multi-stage-training-protocol.md].
