# FlipNeRF

Also called: Flipped Reflection Rays for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis

## Overview
FlipNeRF is a regularization method for few-shot novel view synthesis that generates auxiliary "flipped reflection rays" from original input rays and estimated surface normals to improve rendering quality and surface normal accuracy with sparse inputs [S1]. Unlike existing methods that focus on depth estimation or require pre-training, FlipNeRF is trained per scene without pre-training and derives rays from estimated normals without heuristic factors, distinguishing it from unseen viewpoint sampling strategies and Ref-NeRF [S1]. It builds on [Model Architecture Modifications](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Original input rays $\mathcal{R}$, ground truth colors $\mathbf{c}^{\text{GT}}$, and a NeRF model (built upon MixNeRF) that outputs mixture density parameters for each sample along a ray.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered colors $\hat{\mathbf{c}}$, estimated surface normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, and flipped reflection rays $\mathcal{R}'$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray:** Defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
*   **Sample:** Along a ray, samples have position $\mathbf{r}(t)$, blending weight $w_i$, and mixture parameters (mean $\mu_i^{\mathbf{c}}$, scale $\beta_i^{\mathbf{c}}$ for RGB).
*   **Bottleneck Feature:** Intermediate feature vector $\mathbf{b}$ from the spatial MLP of NeRF.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Estimate Surface Normal:**
    Calculate the estimated surface normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ for a ray as the weighted sum of blending weights $w_i$ and estimated normal vectors $\mathbf{n}_i$ along the ray [S1]:
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \mathbf{n}_i $$
    The estimated normal vectors $\mathbf{n}_i$ are derived from the gradient of volume density [S1]. Note that $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is not explicitly normalized during training; it is trained to approximate a unit vector naturally [S1].

2.  **Find Peak Blending Weight Sample:**
    Identify the sample index $s$ with the highest blending weight along the original ray $\mathbf{r}$. Calculate the position of this peak sample $\mathbf{p}_s$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_s = \mathbf{o} + t_s \mathbf{d} $$
    where $t_s$ is the distance to the $s$-th sample [S1].

3.  **Derive Flipped Reflection Direction:**
    Calculate the flipped reflection direction $\mathbf{d}'$ using the original direction $\mathbf{d}$ and the estimated normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{d}' = 2(\mathbf{d} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}} - \mathbf{d} $$

4.  **Calculate Flipped Ray Origin:**
    Determine the origin $\mathbf{o}'$ of the flipped reflection ray such that the peak sample of the flipped ray coincides with $\mathbf{p}_s$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{o}' = \mathbf{p}_s - t_s \mathbf{d}' $$

5.  **Construct Flipped Reflection Ray:**
    Define the flipped reflection ray $\mathbf{r}'$ as [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{r}'(t) = \mathbf{o}' + t \mathbf{d}' $$
    Cast $\mathbf{r}'$ toward the identical target pixel as the original input ray $\mathbf{r}$ [S1].

6.  **Apply Masking Strategy:**
    Filter out ineffective flipped rays using a masking function $M(\mathbf{r}')$ based on the angle between the normal and the negative original direction [S1]:
    $$ M(\mathbf{r}') = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \arccos(-(\hat{\mathbf{d}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})) < \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    where $\hat{\mathbf{d}}$ is the normalized original direction, $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the normalized estimated normal, and $\tau$ is the threshold [S1]. Only rays with $M(\mathbf{r}')=1$ are used in subsequent losses [S1].

7.  **Compute Uncertainty-aware Emptiness Loss (UE Loss):**
    Calculate the uncertainty $\rho$ for a ray as the average of the sum of estimated scale parameters $\beta_i^c$ for RGB channels $c \in \{r,g,b\}$ [S1]:
    $$ \rho = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{c}^{\{r,g,b\}} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \beta_i^c $$
    The UE Loss for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is [S1]:
    $$ l_{\text{UE}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \log(1 + \rho \cdot \eta \cdot w_i) $$
    where $\eta$ is a scaling parameter [S1]. This loss is applied to both original rays $\mathcal{R}$ and valid flipped rays $\mathcal{R}'$ [S1].

8.  **Compute Bottleneck Feature Consistency Loss (BFC Loss):**
    Calculate the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between the softmax-normalized bottleneck features $\mathbf{b}$ of the original ray and $\mathbf{b}'$ of the flipped ray [S1]:
    $$ l_{\text{BFC}}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}') = \text{JSD}(\psi(\mathbf{b}), \psi(\mathbf{b}')) $$
    where $\psi(\cdot)$ is the softmax function [S1].

9.  **Compute Orientation Loss:**
    Penalize backward-facing normal vectors using the underlying density gradient normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ (not the predicted normal) [S1]:
    $$ l_{\text{Ori.}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \max(0, \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}})^2 $$

10. **Minimize Total Loss:**
    Train the model by minimizing the total loss [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_\text{Total} = \mathcal{L}_\text{MSE} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_\text{NLL} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_\text{NLL}' + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_\text{UE} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_\text{UE}' + \lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_\text{BFC} + \lambda_6 \mathcal{L}_\text{Ori.} $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_\text{MSE}$ is the mean squared error between rendered and ground truth colors, $\mathcal{L}_\text{NLL}$ and $\mathcal{L}_\text{NLL}'$ are negative log-likelihood losses for original and flipped rays respectively, and $\lambda_i$ are balancing weights [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def flip_nerf_step(rays, gt_colors, model, hyperparams):
    """
    Performs one training step for FlipNeRF.
    
    Args:
        rays: List of Ray objects with origin o, direction d, and target pixel.
        gt_colors: Ground truth colors for the rays.
        model: NeRF model (MixNeRF-based) with methods:
               - render_ray(ray): Returns mixture params (means, scales, weights) and bottleneck features.
               - get_density_gradient(ray): Returns normal vectors n_i for each sample.
        hyperparams: Dict containing thresholds, weights, and optimizer settings.
    
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar loss value.
    """
    mse_loss = 0.0
    nll_loss = 0.0
    nll_prime_loss = 0.0
    ue_loss = 0.0
    ue_prime_loss = 0.0
    bfc_loss = 0.0
    ori_loss = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        # 1. Render original ray
        orig_result = model.render_ray(ray)
        orig_means = orig_result.means
        orig_scales = orig_result.scales
        orig_weights = orig_result.weights
        orig_bottleneck = orig_result.bottleneck_features
        
        # 2. Estimate surface normal for original ray
        # n_i are density gradient normals from model.get_density_gradient
        density_normals = model.get_density_gradient(ray)
        est_normal = sum(w * n for w, n in zip(orig_weights, density_normals))
        
        # 3. Find peak blending weight sample index s
        s = argmax(orig_weights)
        t_s = ray.sample_distances[s]
        p_s = ray.origin + t_s * ray.direction
        
        # 4. Derive flipped direction
        d_prime = 2 * dot(ray.direction, est_normal) * est_normal - ray.direction
        
        # 5. Calculate flipped origin
        o_prime = p_s - t_s * d_prime
        
        # 6. Construct flipped ray
        flipped_ray = Ray(origin=o_prime, direction=d_prime, target_pixel=ray.target_pixel)
        
        # 7. Apply masking
        # theta = arccos(-dot(normalized_d, normalized_n))
        # Note: est_normal is not normalized explicitly in FlipNeRF, but dot product uses it directly
        # The formula uses normalized vectors for the angle check.
        # However, the source formula uses hat{d} and hat{n}.
        # We must normalize est_normal for the mask check.
        est_normal_norm = normalize(est_normal)
        d_hat = normalize(ray.direction)
        angle = acos(dot(-d_hat, est_normal_norm))
        
        if angle < hyperparams.mask_threshold:
            # Ray is valid
            flipped_result = model.render_ray(flipped_ray)
            flipped_means = flipped_result.means
            flipped_scales = flipped_result.scales
            flipped_weights = flipped_result.weights
            flipped_bottleneck = flipped_result.bottleneck_features
            
            # Compute NLL for flipped ray
            nll_prime_loss += compute_nll(flipped_means, flipped_scales, gt_colors[ray.target_pixel])
            
            # Compute UE Loss for flipped ray
            rho_prime = compute_uncertainty(flipped_scales)
            ue_prime_loss += compute_ue_loss(flipped_weights, rho_prime, hyperparams.eta)
            
            # Compute BFC Loss
            bfc_loss += compute_jsd(flipped_bottleneck, orig_bottleneck)
        else:
            # Ray is masked out, do not add flipped losses
            pass
            
        # Compute MSE for original ray
        mse_loss += compute_mse(orig_means, orig_scales, gt_colors[ray.target_pixel])
        
        # Compute NLL for original ray
        nll_loss += compute_nll(orig_means, orig_scales, gt_colors[ray.target_pixel])
        
        # Compute UE Loss for original ray
        rho = compute_uncertainty(orig_scales)
        ue_loss += compute_ue_loss(orig_weights, rho, hyperparams.eta)
        
        # Compute Orientation Loss
        ori_loss += compute_orientation_loss(orig_weights, density_normals, ray.direction)
        
    # Aggregate losses with weights
    total_loss = (
        hyperparams.lambda_mse * mse_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_1 * nll_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_2 * nll_prime_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_3 * ue_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_4 * ue_prime_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_5 * bfc_loss +
        hyperparams.lambda_6 * ori_loss
    )
    
    return total_loss

def compute_uncertainty(scales):
    # rho = 1/3 * sum_c sum_i beta_i^c
    # scales is a tensor of shape (M, 3) for RGB
    return (1.0 / 3.0) * sum(sum(scales[:, c]) for c in range(3))

def compute_ue_loss(weights, rho, eta):
    # l_UE = 1/M * sum_i log(1 + rho * eta * w_i)
    M = len(weights)
    return (1.0 / M) * sum(log(1 + rho * eta * w) for w in weights)

def compute_orientation_loss(weights, normals, direction):
    # l_Ori = sum_i w_i * max(0, n_i . d_hat)^2
    d_hat = normalize(direction)
    return sum(w * max(0, dot(n, d_hat))**2 for w, n in zip(weights, normals))

def compute_jsd(b1, b2):
    # JSD(P || Q) = 0.5 * KL(P || M) + 0.5 * KL(Q || M)
    # where M = 0.5 * (P + Q)
    # P and Q are softmax(b1) and softmax(b2)
    p = softmax(b1)
    q = softmax(b2)
    m = 0.5 * (p + q)
    return 0.5 * kl_divergence(p, m) + 0.5 * kl_divergence(q, m)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Explicit Normalization:** Using explicitly normalized surface normals ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}$) causes training instability and degenerate results (PSNR 16.72) compared to not using explicit normalization (PSNR 22.24) [S1]. The estimated normals are not unit vectors in the initial training stage, with most $\|\hat{\mathbf{n}}\|^2$ far from 1 [S1]. The method relies on regularization and masking to naturally concentrate $\|\hat{\mathbf{n}}\|^2$ close to 1 after training [S1].
*   **Masking Threshold:** Using an inappropriate threshold (e.g., $180^\circ$ with no masking) degrades performance. For example, on the evaluation dataset, $\tau=90^\circ$ achieved PSNR 19.55, SSIM 0.767, LPIPS 0.180, and Average Error 0.101, outperforming no masking ($\tau=180^\circ$) which had PSNR 18.76 [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** FlipNeRF achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, outperforming baselines by a large margin in 3/4-view settings [S1]. Evaluated on Realistic Synthetic 360°, DTU, and LLFF benchmarks [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Reduces floating artifacts, improves surface normal estimation, and does not require pre-training or heuristic factors for unseen view generation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on accurate initial surface normal estimation, which can be challenging in very sparse settings without regularization [S1].
*   **When to use:** Few-shot novel view synthesis with sparse input views [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When dense input views are available, or when pre-training on large datasets is feasible and preferred [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** FlipNeRF was proposed in the paper "FlipNeRF: Flipped Reflection Rays for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis" [S1].
*   **History and Development:** It is built upon MixNeRF, which leverages a mixture model framework [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of existing regularization methods that focus on depth estimation by instead focusing on accurate surface normal reconstruction [S1].
*   **Usage:** Evaluated on Realistic Synthetic 360°, DTU, and LLFF benchmarks [S1].
