# HALO
Also called: HALO (Harnessing Low-frequency Neural Fields), Hi-NeRF, Input-space frequency regularization, LoNeRF, Low-frequency regularized NeRF

## Overview
HALO is a few-shot view synthesis method that prevents overfitting in neural radiance fields by regularizing a high-frequency NeRF (Hi-NeRF) using a low-frequency NeRF (Lo-NeRF) [S2, unofficial]. It leverages the distinct interpolation and extrapolation properties of low- and high-frequency representations to enforce geometric consistency without requiring extra semantic supervision [S2, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods such as [DietNeRF](18409-training-configuration-and-optimization.md) which rely on CLIP-based semantic priors, HALO maintains better lighting consistency and operates purely within the input space [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of $N$ training images with known camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained Hi-NeRF MLP capable of rendering novel views [S2, unofficial].
*   **Implicit Outputs:** A Lo-NeRF MLP and a Ray-based field MLP (optional for dynamic scenes) [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
1.  **Lo-NeRF:** An MLP taking encoded position $\gamma_{Lo}(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma_{Lo}(\mathbf{d})$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Ray-based Field:** An MLP taking ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$. Input is 6D ($\mathbf{o} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$), but has 4 degrees of freedom as $\mathbf{o}$ lies on a predefined sphere and $\mathbf{d}$ is a unit vector [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Hi-NeRF:** An MLP taking encoded position $\gamma_{Hi}(\mathbf{x})$ and direction $\gamma_{Hi}(\mathbf{d})$ [S2, unofficial].

**Positional Encoding**
The input coordinates are mapped to higher dimensions using hierarchical positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ [S2, 13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md, unofficial]. For a scalar input $p$, the encoding is:
$$ \gamma(p) = (\sin(2^0 \pi p), \cos(2^0 \pi p), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi p), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi p)) $$
where $L$ is the number of frequency bands [S2, 13550-model-architecture-and-encoding.md, unofficial].
*   **Lo-P.E.:** Uses a reduced frequency $L_{Lo}$ determined by the tuning criterion [S2, unofficial].
*   **Hi-P.E.:** Uses the default NeRF frequency $L_{Hi} = 10$ [S2, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Tune Lo-NeRF Frequency:**
    *   Render $N$ paired images with a small baseline [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute the averaged frequency domain difference $\sigma$:
        $$ \sigma = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \| \mathcal{F}(I_{ia}) - \mathcal{F}(I_{ib}) \| $$
        where $\mathcal{F}()$ is the Fourier transformation [S2, unofficial].
    *   Reduce the frequency parameter $L$ until $\sigma$ reaches a predefined threshold (25) [S2, unofficial]. This $L$ defines Lo-NeRF.

2.  **Train Lo-NeRF:**
    *   Train the Lo-NeRF using standard volume rendering loss on the training views [S2, unofficial].
    *   Formula for rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ along ray $\mathbf{r}$:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{k=1}^K e^{-\sum_{k_0=1}^{k-1} \sigma_{k_0} \delta_{k_0}} (1 - e^{-\sigma_k \delta_k}) c_k $$
        where $\sigma_k = \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_k))$, $c_k = c(\mathbf{r}(t_k))$, and $\delta_k = t_{k+1} - t_k$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Loss:
        $$ L_{rec} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C_{gt}(\mathbf{r}) \|_2^2 $$
        where $C_{gt}$ is ground truth RGB [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Train Ray-based Field (Static Scenes):**
    *   Freeze Lo-NeRF. Randomly initialize the Ray-based field $F_{ray}$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute depth $D(\mathbf{r}; F_{point})$ by sampling points and accumulating density from the frozen Lo-NeRF [S2, unofficial].
    *   Optimize $F_{ray}$ with loss:
        $$ L_{ray} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}=(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{d})} \| F_{ray}(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}) - D(\mathbf{r}; F_{point}) \|_2^2 $$
        where $\mathbf{o}$ is the ray origin on a predefined sphere and $\mathbf{d}$ is the unit view direction [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Train Hi-NeRF:**
    *   Freeze Lo-NeRF and $F_{ray}$. Randomly initialize Hi-NeRF with $L=10$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Sample points along rays according to the depth predicted by $F_{ray}$ (following the DONeRF strategy) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Optimize Hi-NeRF with total loss:
        $$ L = L_{rec} + \lambda L_{empty} $$
    *   **Empty Space Loss:** Regularizes empty space where depth cannot be inferred from Lo-NeRF [S2, unofficial]. Define accumulated occupancy for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as:
        $$ acc(\mathbf{r}) = \prod_{k=1}^K e^{-\sum_{k_0=1}^{k-1} \sigma_{k_0} \delta_{k_0}} (1 - e^{-\sigma_k \delta_k}) $$
        The empty space loss is:
        $$ L_{empty} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}: acc_{Lo}(\mathbf{r}) < \tau} acc_{Hi}(\mathbf{r}) $$
        where $\tau = 0.01$ is the threshold for determining if space is empty [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **High-Frequency Encoding:** Using high-frequency positional encoding leads to poor interpolation results [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ray-based Blurriness:** Ray-based predictions may suffer from blurry edges, but this is acceptable for rough geometry regularization as Hi-NeRF fine-tunes details [S2, unofficial].
*   **Dynamic Scenes:** Using high-frequency encoding for the time axis introduces temporal flickering motion when dynamic sequence observations are sparse [S2, unofficial].
*   **Empty Space:** The empty space loss relies on the threshold $\tau=0.01$. If Lo-NeRF predicts non-zero occupancy in empty regions, Hi-NeRF may incorrectly fill them [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training involves three sequential stages. The ray-based field training adds overhead compared to vanilla NeRF. On dynamic scenes (TiNeuVox baseline), HALO takes 0.67 GPU/h compared to 0.17 GPU/h for the baseline, but yields significantly higher PSNR (25.687 vs 21.392) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing parameters for Lo-NeRF, Ray-based Field, and Hi-NeRF. The Ray-based field is lightweight as it only predicts a scalar depth [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Does not require extra semantic supervision (unlike DietNeRF); maintains lighting consistency; effective for few-shot settings (4-8 views); applicable to dynamic scenes via time dimension extension [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires three-stage training; sensitive to the frequency tuning threshold; ray-based field may introduce blurriness in regularization [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Few-shot view synthesis (static or dynamic) where semantic priors are unavailable or undesirable [S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Large-scale dense reconstruction (vanilla NeRF suffices); scenarios requiring real-time training with minimal overhead [S2, unofficial].

## Background
HALO (Harnessing Low-Frequency Neural Fields) addresses the overfitting problem in NeRFs under few-shot settings by exploiting the different interpolation and extrapolation properties of low- and high-frequency neural fields [S2, unofficial]. It is related to [DietNeRF](18409-training-configuration-and-optimization.md) but avoids semantic supervision [S2, unofficial]. It builds on [Position Encoding and Embedding](18459-position-encoding-and-embedding.md) principles [S2, unofficial].
