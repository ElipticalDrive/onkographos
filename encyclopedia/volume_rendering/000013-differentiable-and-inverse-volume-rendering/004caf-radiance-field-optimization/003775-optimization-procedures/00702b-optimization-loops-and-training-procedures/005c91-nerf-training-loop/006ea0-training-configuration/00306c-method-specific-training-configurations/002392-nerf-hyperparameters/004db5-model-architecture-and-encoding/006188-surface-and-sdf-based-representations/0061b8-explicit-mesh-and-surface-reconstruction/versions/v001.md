# Explicit Mesh and Surface Reconstruction

Also called: Explicit Mesh Reconstruction, Mesh-based NeRF Distillation

## Overview
This family encompasses methods that convert implicit neural radiance fields (NeRFs) or signed distance fields (SDFs) into explicit polygonal mesh representations or duplex surface structures. It solves the problem of high computational cost and lack of explicit geometric topology in volumetric NeRFs by replacing dense ray sampling with surface intersections or deformable anchor grids. It differs from sibling methods like [I2-SDF](18170-i2-sdf.md) and [SDF-based Volume Rendering with Laplacian CDF](18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md) by prioritizing explicit mesh extraction or duplex surface approximation for real-time rendering, rather than optimizing a continuous SDF field for volume rendering integration.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves predicting a scalar field $f(\mathbf{x})$ that represents the signed distance to the nearest surface (or occupancy) at position $\mathbf{x}$, rather than volume density [S1]. This scalar field is transformed into opacity $\alpha$ or density $\sigma$ for volume rendering integration.

**SDF to Opacity/Density Transformation**
The SDF value $s(\mathbf{x})$ is converted into a probability of occupancy or density using a distribution parameterized by a variance or sparsity term $\beta$ [S1, 18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md].

1.  **Laplacian CDF (Standard SDF):** Used in [SDF-based Volume Rendering with Laplacian CDF](18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md). The opacity $\sigma$ is derived from the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of a Laplacian distribution:
    $$
    \sigma = \begin{cases} 
    \frac{1}{2} \exp\left(\frac{s}{\beta}\right) & \text{if } s \le 0 \\
    1 - \frac{1}{2} \exp\left(-\frac{s}{\beta}\right) & \text{if } s > 0 
    \end{cases}
    $$
    where $\beta$ controls the tightness of the surface representation [18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md].

2.  **Piecewise Exponential (I2-SDF):** Used in [I2-SDF](18170-i2-sdf.md). The density $\sigma$ is defined as:
    $$
    \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = 
    \begin{cases} 
    \frac{1}{\beta}(1 - \exp(d(\mathbf{x})/\beta)) & \text{if } d(\mathbf{x}) < 0 \\
    \frac{1}{2\beta}\exp(-d(\mathbf{x})/2\beta) & \text{if } d(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0 
    \end{cases}
    $$
    where $d(\mathbf{x})$ is the SDF value and $\beta$ is a learnable sparsity parameter [18170-i2-sdf.md].

**Inverse CDF Sampling**
To sample points efficiently near the surface, methods like [SDF-based Volume Rendering with Laplacian CDF](18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md) use inverse CDF sampling. Random numbers $u_i \in [0, 1]$ are generated and transformed to sample distances $t_i$ using the inverse of the CDF $\Phi^{-1}(u_i)$, concentrating samples near the zero-crossing of the SDF [18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md]. Stratification is applied by dividing the unit interval into bins [18174-sdf-based-volume-rendering-with-laplacian-cdf.md].

**Occupancy Fields**
As seen in [UniSurf](#unisurf), an occupancy field acts as an indicator of surface presence. For solid objects, the occupancy at the $i^{th}$ sample corresponds to an indicator of whether the sample is inside the surface [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Deformable Anchor (NeuDA)](18140-neural-deformable-anchor-neuda.md)
NeuDA is a neural implicit surface reconstruction method that replaces learnable feature vectors at voxel vertices with optimized 3D positions (anchors) to adaptively encode local geometry. It solves the limitation of standard voxel grids, where uniformly distributed features fail to capture small structures with complicated topologies, by allowing anchors to move from grid corners to the vicinity of abrupt geometry-changing areas. Unlike Instant-NGP, which uses multi-resolution hash encoding and may suffer from hash collisions, NeuDA uses hierarchical voxel grids with deformable anchors to better exploit spatial context. See [Neural Deformable Anchor (NeuDA)](18140-neural-deformable-anchor-neuda.md).

### [Neural Duplex Radiance Fields (NDRF)](18147-neural-duplex-radiance-fields-ndrf.md)
Neural Duplex Radiance Fields (NDRF) is a method for distilling a pretrained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) into a mesh-based representation that enables real-time view synthesis. It solves the high computational cost of standard NeRF rendering by replacing dense volumetric sampling with a "duplex" interval defined by two ray-surface intersections on a proxy mesh. Unlike standard NeRF which uses a deep MLP for every sample, NDRF uses a shallow convolutional network (CNN) to aggregate local geometric and appearance information from these two points, achieving approximately 10,000 times faster run-time performance while maintaining high-quality appearance. See [Neural Duplex Radiance Fields (NDRF)](18147-neural-duplex-radiance-fields-ndrf.md).

### Explicit Mesh Reconstruction from Neural Fields
An algorithm to extract an explicit 3D mesh representation from the implicit neural radiance field learned by CodeNeRF, allowing for 3D reconstruction from a single input image [S1]. The process feeds the center coordinates of a $256^3$ voxel grid to the CodeNeRF MLP to obtain volume density values $\sigma$ [S1]. Marching cubes is then applied to the volume density values $\sigma$ to find mesh vertices [S1]. Finally, rays are cast along the vertex normals of the generated mesh to associate colors to the vertices [S1].

## Background
The concept of extracting explicit meshes from neural fields was introduced by [CodeNeRF](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccv48922.2021.01271) (2021), which disentangles shape and appearance to allow 3D reconstruction from a single image [S1]. The method leverages texture priors learned by CodeNeRF to complete object shape and texture information not present in the input image [S1]. Subsequent developments like NeuDA and NDRF extended these principles to address specific limitations in spatial encoding efficiency and rendering speed, respectively.
