# GazeNeRF

Also called: GazeNeRF: 3D-Aware Gaze Redirection with Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
GazeNeRF is a 3D-aware gaze redirection method that reformulates the task as a neural volume rendering problem [S1]. It uses a two-stream MLP architecture to separately predict volumetric features for the face and eye regions, allowing for rigid 3D rotation of the eye features to achieve gaze redirection while preserving identity [S1]. It differs from 2D methods (e.g., STED, FAZE) by explicitly modeling the 3D nature of eyeball rotation and face deformation via separate volumetric streams, whereas 2D methods apply weak rotation priors to mixed 2D latent features [S1]. It differs from EyeNeRF by focusing on gaze redirection accuracy and using disentangled feature streams with explicit 3D rotation, whereas EyeNeRF focuses on high-fidelity rendering and relighting quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   3D spatial position $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   View direction $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Gaze label $g \in \mathbb{R}^2$, a 2D vector consisting of pitch and yaw angles of the gaze vector in radians [S1].
    *   Latent codes $z_{sh}, z_{ex}, z_{te}, z_{il} \in \mathbb{R}^k$ representing shape, expression, texture (albedo), and illumination, respectively. These are initialized from 3DMM parameters [S1].
    *   Reference image (for supervision during training) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Rendered 2D images: $I_{wf}$ (whole face), $I_{fw/o}$ (face without eyes), and $I_e$ (eyes only) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Two-stream MLPs:**
    *   $h_{\theta_{fw/o}}$: Face-only stream parameters $\theta_{fw/o}$.
    *   $h_{\theta_{e}}$: Eye stream parameters $\theta_{e}$ [S1].
*   **Volume Feature Maps:**
    *   $F_{fw/o} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 258}$: Low-resolution volume feature map for the face region [S1].
    *   $F_{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 258}$: Low-resolution volume feature map for the eye region [S1].
*   **Rotated Eye Feature Map:**
    *   $F_{e-rot} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 86 \times 3}$: Reshaped version of $F_e$ to allow 3D rotation matrix multiplication [S1].
*   **Merged Feature Map:**
    *   $F_{wf} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 258}$: Merged feature map for the whole face [S1].
*   **Masks:**
    *   $M_{wf}$: Whole face mask.
    *   $M_f$: Face-only mask.
    *   $M_e$: Eyes mask [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Encode Inputs:** Apply positional encoding $\gamma$ to $x$ and $d$. Concatenate $\gamma(x)$ with latent codes $z_{sh}, z_{ex}, z_{te}, z_{il}$ and gaze label $g$ [S1].
2.  **Face Stream Processing:** Pass the encoded inputs through the face-only MLP $h_{\theta_{fw/o}}$ to predict volume density $\sigma$ and feature vector $f$ for the face region (excluding eyes) [S1].
    $$ h_{\theta_{fw/o}} : (\gamma(x), z_{sh}, z_{ex}, z_{te}, z_{il}, g) \rightarrow (\sigma, f) $$
3.  **Eye Stream Processing:** Pass the encoded inputs through the eye MLP $h_{\theta_{e}}$ to predict volume density $\sigma$ and feature vector $f$ for the eye region [S1].
    $$ h_{\theta_{e}} : (\gamma(x), z_{sh}, z_{ex}, z_{te}, z_{il}, g) \rightarrow (\sigma, f) $$
4.  **Volume Rendering:** Use volume rendering on the outputs of both MLPs to generate low-resolution volume feature maps $F_{fw/o}$ and $F_{e}$ with dimensions $\mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 258}$ [S1].
5.  **3D Rotation:** Reshape eye feature maps $F_{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 258}$ to $F_{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 86 \times 3}$. Calculate a 3D rotation matrix $R$ based on the target gaze direction $g$. Apply this rotation matrix to the eye feature map to rigidly rotate the eyeball features:
    $$ F_{e-rot} = R F_{e} $$
    where $R$ is a 3D rotation matrix computed from gaze label $g$ [S1].
6.  **Feature Composition:** Map the rotated eye feature map $F_{e-rot}$ to the segmented eyes region and the face feature map $F_{fw/o}$ to the segmented face-only region. Merge them using element-wise maximum:
    $$ F_{wf} = \max(F_{fw/o}, F_{e-rot}) $$
    [S1].
7.  **Final Rendering:** Render three images using a neural rendering module with shared weights $\theta_{render}$: face-only image $I_{fw/o}$ from $F_{fw/o}$, eyes image $I_e$ from $F_{e-rot}$, and whole face image $I_{wf}$ from $F_{wf}$ [S1].

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with learning rate $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Loss Coefficients:** $\lambda_R = \lambda_P = \lambda_F = \lambda_D = 1$ [S1].
*   **Disentanglement Weights:** $w_{sh} = w_{te} = w_{il} = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ and $w_{ex} = 1.0$ [S1].
*   **Functional Loss Coefficient:** $\lambda_{F_{content}}$ starts at $1 \times 10^{-3}$ and increases by $1 \times 10^{-3}$ after each epoch [S1].
*   **Input Resolution:** Face images resized to $512 \times 512$ pixels [S1].
*   **Feature Map Dimensions:** $64 \times 64 \times 258$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Masking:** The method relies on accurate segmentation masks ($M_{wf}, M_f, M_e$) to ensure each stream generates features corresponding to the correct regions. Incorrect masks can lead to blending artifacts or failure to disentangle eyes from face [S1].
*   **Rotation Matrix:** The rotation matrix $R$ must be correctly computed from the gaze label $g$ (pitch and yaw in radians) to ensure 3D consistency [S1].
*   **Latent Code Initialization:** Latent codes are initialized from 3DMM parameters. Poor initialization may affect convergence or identity preservation [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on the ETH-XGaze dataset. GazeNeRF achieves better results than STED and HeadNeRF in gaze and head redirection errors (degrees). It achieves similar results to HeadNeRF in image quality metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS) [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Explicit 3D awareness of eyeball rotation; disentangled feature streams allow independent control of face deformation and eye rotation; preserves identity better than 2D methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires face parsing models for masks; relies on 3DMM for latent code initialization; computational cost of two-stream MLPs and volume rendering [S1].
*   **When to use it:** When 3D-consistent gaze redirection is required, especially for applications needing high redirection accuracy and identity preservation [S1].
*   **When not:** When high-fidelity relighting is the primary goal (use EyeNeRF instead) or when computational resources are extremely limited and 3D consistency is not critical [S1].

## Background
GazeNeRF was proposed in 2023 by the authors of "GazeNeRF: 3D-Aware Gaze Redirection with Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It builds on the intuition that the face region and eyeballs are separate 3D structures that move in a coordinated yet independent fashion [S1]. It shares similarities with EyeNeRF but focuses on gaze redirection accuracy rather than high-fidelity rendering and relighting quality [S1]. It differs from HeadNeRF, which uses a single MLP conditioned on gaze labels, by using explicit 3D rotation on separated eye features [S1]. It is used for gaze redirection tasks, outperforming 2D methods like STED and FAZE which lack 3D awareness [S1].
