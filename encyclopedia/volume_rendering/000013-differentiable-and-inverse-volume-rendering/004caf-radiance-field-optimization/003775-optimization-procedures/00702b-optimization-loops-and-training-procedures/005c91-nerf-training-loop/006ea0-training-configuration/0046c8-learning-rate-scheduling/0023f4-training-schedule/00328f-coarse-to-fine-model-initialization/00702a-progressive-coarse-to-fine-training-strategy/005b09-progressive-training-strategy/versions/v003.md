# Progressive Training Strategy

Also called: Coarse-to-Fine Training, Progressive Growing, Progressive Growing Strategy, Progressive Growing of GANs, Progressive training (Growing)

## Overview
Progressive training is a method that gradually increases the resolution of the rendered output during optimization to stabilize convergence and prevent geometric artifacts [S2, S3]. It solves the problem of complex loss landscapes and sparse initialization errors that occur when training directly at high resolution [S1, unofficial][S2]. It differs from standard full-resolution training by deferring the fitting of fine features until the global geometry is established [S3], and differs from simple two-stage pipelines by potentially using continuous or multi-stage resolution scaling rather than a single abrupt switch [S1, unofficial][S3].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Training dataset (images or point clouds), total iteration budget $T_{total}$, initial resolution scale $s_{start}$, target resolution scale $s_{end}=1.0$ [S2, S3].
*   **Output:** Trained model parameters (neural network weights or Gaussian attributes) optimized for the target resolution [G].
*   **Resolution Schedule:** A mapping from iteration $t$ to output image dimensions $(H_t, W_t)$ [G].
*   **Model State:** The generator/discriminator architecture or Gaussian point cloud state, which may grow in capacity or remain fixed but render at varying scales [S1, unofficial][S3].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize:** Set current resolution scale $s = s_{start}$ (e.g., 0.3 or low-res dimensions) [S3]. Initialize model parameters [G].
2.  **Iterate:** For each training iteration $t$ from 1 to $T_{total}$:
    a.  **Determine Resolution:** Calculate current scale $s_t$ based on the chosen strategy [S1, unofficial][S3].
        *   **Progressive Growing (StyleNeRF):** Uses three discrete stages [S1, unofficial]. Stage 1 ($t \le T_1$): Render at fixed low resolution. Stage 2 ($T_1 < t \le T_2$): Linearly interpolate resolution from low to high. Stage 3 ($t > T_2$): Render at full target resolution.
        *   **Cosine Scaling (EAGLES):** Uses a continuous schedule [S3]. If $t \le 0.7 \cdot T_{total}$: $s_t = 0.3 + 0.7 \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos(\pi \frac{t}{0.7 T_{total}}))$. If $t > 0.7 \cdot T_{total}$: $s_t = 1.0$.
    b.  **Render:** Generate image at resolution determined by $s_t$ [G].
    c.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate loss against ground truth images at the current resolution [G].
    d.  **Update:** Perform gradient descent to update model parameters [G].
    e.  **Stabilize (Progressive Growing only):** If adding new layers or resolution levels, linearly interpolate the output with the previous low-resolution output to stabilize training due to random initialization of new parameters [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Finalize:** Continue training at full resolution until $T_{total}$ is reached [S1, unofficial][S3].

**Formulas:**
*   **Linear Interpolation (StyleNeRF Stage 2):**
    $$ R_t = R_{low} + (R_{target} - R_{low}) \cdot \frac{t - T_1}{T_2 - T_1} $$
    Where $R_t$ is the output resolution at iteration $t$, $R_{low}$ is the initial low resolution, $R_{target}$ is the target resolution, $T_1$ is the start of Stage 2, and $T_2$ is the end of Stage 2 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Cosine Schedule (EAGLES):**
    $$ s_t = s_{start} + (s_{end} - s_{start}) \cdot \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 - \cos \left( \pi \frac{t}{T_{scale}} \right) \right) $$
    Where $T_{scale} = 0.7 \cdot T_{total}$ [S3].
*   **Output Interpolation (Stabilization):**
    $$ I_{out} = \alpha \cdot I_{new} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot I_{low} $$
    Where $\alpha$ is the interpolation factor corresponding to the progress of layer/resolution addition, computed as $\frac{t - T_1}{T_2 - T_1}$ during Stage 2 [S1, unofficial][G].

**Parameters:**
*   **$T_1$ (StyleNeRF):** Start of Stage 2. Default: 500,000 images [S1, unofficial].
*   **$T_2$ (StyleNeRF):** End of Stage 2. Default: 5,000,000 images [S1, unofficial].
*   **$T_3$ (StyleNeRF):** End of Stage 3. Default: 25,000,000 images [S1, unofficial].
*   **$s_{start}$ (EAGLES):** Initial scale factor. Default: 0.3 [S3].
*   **$T_{scale}$ (EAGLES):** Duration of scaling phase. Default: 70% of total iterations [S3].
*   **Batch Size:** 64 images per batch [S1, unofficial].
*   **Total Iterations (EAGLES):** 30,000 iterations (configurable based on time and memory budget) [S3].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Geometry Failure:** Directly training at the highest resolution causes the model to fail to capture object geometry because the rendering equations are approximations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Floating Artifacts:** Standard full-resolution training forces Gaussians to fit fine features early with sparse SfM initialization, leading to suboptimal optimization and floating artifacts [S3].
*   **Small Datasets:** If the dataset is too small (e.g., MetFaces), progressive training may be unstable; fine-tuning from a pretrained high-resolution model is recommended instead [S1, unofficial].
*   **New Layer Initialization:** Parameters of newly added layers are random; without linear interpolation with low-resolution output, training becomes unstable [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def progressive_training(model, dataset_iterator, total_iterations, strategy="cosine"):
    """
    model: The neural network or Gaussian system to train.
    dataset_iterator: Iterable yielding ground truth images.
    total_iterations: Total number of training steps.
    strategy: "cosine" (EAGLES) or "linear" (StyleNeRF).
    """
    # Standard operations defined inline to avoid external dependencies
    def get_next_batch(iterator, batch_size=64):
        return next(iterator)

    def compute_loss(rendered, ground_truth):
        return ((rendered - ground_truth) ** 2).mean()

    def interpolate_images(img1, img2, alpha):
        return alpha * img1 + (1.0 - alpha) * img2

    def forward_pass(model, resolution):
        # Standard forward pass returning rendered image at given resolution
        return model.render(resolution)

    def update_parameters(model, loss):
        # Standard gradient descent step
        model.step(loss)

    base_height = 256  # Example base resolution
    base_width = 256

    if strategy == "cosine":
        scale_start = 0.3
        scale_end = 1.0
        scale_duration = int(0.7 * total_iterations)

        for t in range(1, total_iterations + 1):
            if t <= scale_duration:
                progress = t / scale_duration
                scale = scale_start + (scale_end - scale_start) * 0.5 * (1 - math.cos(math.pi * progress))
            else:
                scale = scale_end

            current_height = int(base_height * scale)
            current_width = int(base_width * scale)

            gt_images = get_next_batch(dataset_iterator, batch_size=64)
            rendered_images = forward_pass(model, (current_height, current_width))
            loss = compute_loss(rendered_images, gt_images)
            update_parameters(model, loss)

            if t % 175 == 0:
                model.densify()
            if t % 2500 == 0:
                model.reset_opacity()
            if t % 5000 == 0:
                model.prune(0.15)

    elif strategy == "linear":
        T1 = 500000
        T2 = 5000000
        low_res_scale = 0.125  # Corresponds to 32x32 from 256x256
        high_res_scale = 1.0

        for t in range(1, total_iterations + 1):
            if t <= T1:
                scale = low_res_scale
            elif t <= T2:
                progress = (t - T1) / (T2 - T1)
                scale = low_res_scale + (high_res_scale - low_res_scale) * progress
            else:
                scale = high_res_scale

            current_height = int(base_height * scale)
            current_width = int(base_width * scale)

            gt_images = get_next_batch(dataset_iterator, batch_size=64)
            rendered_images = forward_pass(model, (current_height, current_width))

            if t <= T2:
                low_res_rendered = forward_pass(model, (int(base_height * low_res_scale), int(base_width * low_res_scale)))
                final_render = interpolate_images(rendered_images, low_res_rendered, progress)
            else:
                final_render = rendered_images

            loss = compute_loss(final_render, gt_images)
            update_parameters(model, loss)
```

## Subtopics

### Progressive Growing
Starts with a shallow network and fades in layers for higher resolutions. Requires linear interpolation with low-resolution output to stabilize random new parameters. Chosen for speed advantage at low resolutions [S1, unofficial].

### Progressive Up-sampling
Inserts upsample operations during training instead of growing layers. Does not require fading in layers linearly. Works similarly in visual quality but may be slower at low resolutions [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is distributed across resolutions. The majority of iterations (e.g., 70% in EAGLES, or up to $T_2$ in StyleNeRF) occur at lower resolutions, which are computationally cheaper per iteration. However, the total iteration count may be higher to compensate for the coarse phases [S1, unofficial][S3].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Lower resolution rendering requires less memory per iteration. The model architecture size may grow in Progressive Growing, but up-sampling keeps it fixed [S1, unofficial][S3].
*   **Performance:** Prevents failure to capture geometry and reduces floating artifacts compared to direct high-resolution training [S1, unofficial][S3].
*   **Strengths:** Stabilizes optimization in complex loss landscapes; handles sparse SfM initialization better; avoids early fitting to noise/fine details [S3].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful scheduling of resolution transitions; small datasets may not support stable progressive training [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** High-resolution synthesis, scenes with sparse initialization, or when direct training leads to artifacts [S1, unofficial][S3].
*   **When not to use:** Very small datasets where progressive training is unstable, requiring fine-tuning from a pretrained model instead [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The strategy is inspired by Karras et al. (2017) "Progressive Growing of GANs for Improved Quality, Stability, and Variation" [S1, unofficial]. It was adapted for NeRF-based methods (StyleNeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting variants (EAGLES) to address the specific challenges of volume rendering and differentiable rendering, where direct high-resolution training leads to geometric inaccuracies due to approximation errors in the rendering equations [S1, unofficial][S3].
