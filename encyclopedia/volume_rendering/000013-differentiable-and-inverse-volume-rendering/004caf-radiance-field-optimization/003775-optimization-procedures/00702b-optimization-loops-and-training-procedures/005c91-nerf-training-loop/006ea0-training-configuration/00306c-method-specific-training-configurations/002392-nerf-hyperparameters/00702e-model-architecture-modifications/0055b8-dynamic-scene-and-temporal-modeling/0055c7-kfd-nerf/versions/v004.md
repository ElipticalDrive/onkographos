# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (direct observation via a shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (history-based prediction), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain [S1]. Unlike feature-interpolation approaches, it uses deformation fields to warp points to a canonical space, leveraging a tri-plane representation for efficient spatial encoding [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, view direction $\mathbf{d}$, and timestamp $t_i$. Training data consists of multi-view images at discrete timestamps.
*   **Outputs:** Deformation field $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ (warping $\mathbf{x}$ to canonical space), density $\sigma$, and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-Plane Representation:** Three mutually orthogonal 2D planes encode the canonical space. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
*   **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow MLP takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ as input [S1].
*   **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ takes noise terms and timestamps to compute the Kalman gain [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The method estimates the deformation state $d\mathbf{x}_{t_i}$ for each point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t_i$. The process follows these steps:

1.  **Observer Step:** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
2.  **Prediction Step:** Calculate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using deformations from the previous two frames ($t_{i-1}$ and $t_{i-2}$) [S1]:
    $$ v_{t_i} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}}}{\Delta t_i} $$
    Compute the predicted deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using local linear motion [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} = 2d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-1}} - d\mathbf{x}_{t_{i-2}} $$
3.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using a linear layer $f$ that takes noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and timestamps $t_i, t_{i-1}$ as inputs [S1]:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
4.  **Update Step:** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
5.  **Warping and Rendering:** Warp all points in real space to a time-independent canonical space according to $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$. Query the tri-plane representation and color/density networks in the canonical space to render the image [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def kfd_nerf_deformation(x, t_i, t_prev, t_prev_prev, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_net, deform_prev, deform_prev_prev):
    """
    Computes the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman Filter logic.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D point coordinates (tensor)
        t_i: Current timestamp
        t_prev: Previous timestamp
        t_prev_prev: Timestamp before previous
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP for observation
        kalman_gain_net: Linear layer for Kalman gain
        deform_prev: Deformation d_x_{t_{i-1}}
        deform_prev_prev: Deformation d_x_{t_{i-2}}
        
    Returns:
        final_deformation: Estimated deformation d_x_{t_i}
    """
    # Step 1: Observer Step
    # Observer MLP outputs mean observation y and noise-related terms epsilon
    y_t, epsilon_t = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # Step 2: Prediction Step
    # Calculate velocity from previous two frames
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev
    velocity = (deform_prev - deform_prev_prev) / delta_t
    
    # Predict deformation using local linear motion
    # d_x_{t_i}^- = d_x_{t_{i-1}} + delta_t * v_{t_i}
    predicted_deformation = deform_prev + delta_t * velocity
    
    # Step 3: Kalman Gain Calculation
    # Get noise terms from previous step (assumed available from previous iteration)
    # epsilon_prev is epsilon_{t_{i-1}}
    epsilon_prev = get_epsilon_from_prev_step() 
    
    # Compute Kalman gain K_{t_i}
    kalman_gain = kalman_gain_net(epsilon_t, epsilon_prev, t_i, t_prev)
    
    # Step 4: Update Step
    # Combine observation and prediction
    final_deformation = predicted_deformation + kalman_gain * (y_t - predicted_deformation)
    
    return final_deformation
```

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of five components [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv} \mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{image}$):** Standard photometric reconstruction loss [S1].
2.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the estimated deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
3.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real world at $t=0$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise.
4.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Ensures consistency between the proposal network (for occupancy estimation) and the learned model, following Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].
5.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges in the canonical space [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution.

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** 2 layers [S1].
*   **Spatial Representation:** Tri-plane [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Training Strategy:** Images are released in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Early vs. Late Training:** In initial stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. In later stages, observations take precedence to recover precise, fine-grained motion details [S1].
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors; concatenating encoded tri-plane features with raw coordinate inputs mitigates this [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance (Synthetic):** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
*   **Performance (Real):** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 30 minutes for KFD-NeRF-S, 3 hours for KFD-NeRF-L (on Nvidia Real Dynamic Scenes Dataset) [S1].
*   **Parameters:** 175 MB [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Faster convergence and lower memory footprint compared to MLP-based backbones (e.g., D-NeRF) due to the tri-plane representation. Deformation fields are smoother and easier to fit with MLPs than feature interpolation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion for prediction, which may degrade if motion is highly non-linear or abrupt [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It draws inspiration from control theory, modeling the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system. It improves upon previous dynamic NeRF methods like D-NeRF [36] and TiNeuVox [10] by integrating a Kalman filter module to optimize state estimations from both observations and predictions [S1].
