# NeO 360

Also called: Neural Fields for Sparse View Synthesis of Outdoor Scenes

## Overview
NeO 360 is a generalizable neural field method for few-shot novel view synthesis of unbounded 360-degree outdoor scenes [S1]. It operates in a zero-shot manner, taking one or a few source images as input to render novel viewpoints without optimizing a per-scene latent code or using inference-time GAN inversion [S1]. Unlike [SUDS](14964-suds.md) which relies on specific scene priors or [Rip-NeRF](15532-rip-nerf.md) which focuses on indoor geometry, NeO 360 combines image-conditional tri-planar global features with local residual features to learn strong priors for unbounded environments [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $N_{src}$ source images (typically 1, 3, or 5) with known camera poses.
*   **Output:** Rendered RGB color $\mathbf{c}$ and volume density $\sigma$ for points along camera rays.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Encoder Feature Map ($F_I$):** Output of the ResNet32 encoder. Dimensions: $512 \times H/2 \times W/2$.
2.  **3D Feature Volume ($V_F$):** Volumetric projection of $F_I$. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$. Default $K=64$ [S1].
3.  **Depth-Encoded Features ($V_Z$):** Output of depth-encoding MLP. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1].
4.  **Aggregation Weights ($w_{xy}, w_{xz}, w_{yz}$):** Learned weights for plane projection. Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 1$ each [S1].
5.  **Tri-Planar Features ($S$):** Final 2D feature maps. Dimensions: $128 \times 120 \times 160$ (spatial) $\times 128$ (channels) per plane [S1].
6.  **Rendering MLP:** 7 fully-connected layers, hidden dimension 128. Total parameters: 1.2M [S1].
7.  **Background MLPs:** Separate MLPs for near and far background rendering [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Encoder Feature Extraction:**
    Pass source images through a pre-trained ResNet32 [S1]. Extract features from the initial convolutional layer and layers 1–3. Upsample all extracted features to spatial resolution $H/2 \times W/2$. Concatenate along the feature dimension to produce $F_I$ with dimensions $512 \times H/2 \times W/2$ [S1].

2.  **Volumetric Projection:**
    Back-project $F_I$ along every ray into a world grid $V$ to create the 3D feature volume $V_F$.
    *   Dimensions: $K \times K \times K \times 512$.
    *   Parameter: $K=64$ [S1].

3.  **Depth Encoding:**
    Process $V_F$ using a 2-layer MLP with hidden dimension 512 to learn the depth of each feature in the grid.
    *   Output: $V_Z$ with dimensions $K \times K \times K \times 512$ [S1].

4.  **Feature Aggregation:**
    Use three separate 2-layer MLPs (hidden dimension 512) to output learned weights $w_i$ over the volumetric feature dimensions.
    *   Outputs: $w_{xy}, w_{xz}, w_{yz}$, each with dimensions $K \times K \times K \times 1$ [S1].

5.  **Plane Projection:**
    Apply softmax to the weights $w$ and sum over the respective orthogonal dimensions to obtain 2D feature maps for the three planes (xy, xz, yz).
    *   Intermediate dimensions: $K \times K \times 512$ [S1].

6.  **2D Convolution and Upsampling:**
    Transform the planar features into the final tri-planar features $S$ using a series of 2D convolutions and upsampling layers [S1].
    *   **Layer 1:** Conv (512 $\to$ 256), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1. Followed by BatchNorm and ReLU.
    *   **Layer 2:** Conv (256 $\to$ 128), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1. Followed by BatchNorm and ReLU.
    *   **Upsample 1:** Scale factor 2.
    *   **Layer 3:** Conv (128 $\to$ 128), kernel 3, stride 2, padding 1. Followed by BatchNorm and ReLU.
    *   **Upsample 2:** Output spatial dimension $H/2 \times W/2$.
    *   **Layer 4:** Conv (128 $\to$ 128). Followed by BatchNorm and ReLU.
    *   Output $S$: Dimensions $128 \times 120 \times 160$ (spatial) $\times 128$ (channels) [S1].

7.  **Tri-Planar Sampling:**
    For a 3D point $\mathbf{x}$, project it into each of the three planes by extracting absolute coordinates (xy, xz, yz). Concatenate the sampled features and sum over the channel dimension.
    *   Output: Tri-planar feature $f_{tp}$ with dimension $N \times 128$, where $N$ is the number of sampled points [S1].

8.  **Residual Feature Sampling:**
    Sample directly into the encoder feature map $F_I$ to retrieve local residual features.
    *   Output: Residual feature $f_r$ with dimension $N \times 512$ [S1].

9.  **Positional Encoding:**
    Apply hierarchical positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ to input position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].

10. **MLP Input Construction:**
    Concatenate the encoded position $\mathbf{x}$, tri-planar features $f_{tp}$, and residual features $f_r$. This combined vector is the input to the first layer of the Rendering MLP [S1].

11. **Conditioning Skip Connection:**
    Supply the conditioning feature (from the encoder) as a skip connection to the third layer of the Rendering MLP [S1].

12. **Viewing Dimension Pooling:**
    If more than one source image is in the input, mean pool the features along the viewing dimension in the fourth MLP layer. [S1].

13. **Density Output:**
    Use the first 4 layers of the Rendering MLP to output features of dimension $N \times 128$. Pass these through a final density MLP to output 1 channel value (density $\sigma$) for every sampled point $N$ [S1].

14. **Color Output:**
    Use two additional dedicated MLP layers (hidden dimension 128) to output a 3-channel color value $\mathbf{c}$ for every sampled point $N$. This is conditioned on the positionally-encoded viewing direction and the output of the fourth MLP layer [S1].

15. **Background Rendering:**
    Sample two different sets of points based on the intersection between the ray origin and the unit sphere to handle near and far background MLPs separately [S1]. The final composited color $\mathbf{c}$ is calculated as:
    $$ \mathbf{c} = \sum_i w^b_i \mathbf{c}^b_i + \sum_i w^{nb}_i \mathbf{c}^{nb}_i + \sum_i w^o_i\mathbf{c}^o_i $$
    where $w^b_i$ are background weights, $w^{nb}_i$ are near-background weights, and $w^o_i$ are object weights [S1].

16. **Regularization Loss:**
    Apply regularization to encourage sparse weights for the near and far background MLPs [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(s, w)=\sum _{i=0}^{N-1} \sum _{j=0}^{N-1} w_i w_j\left|\frac {s_i+s_{i+1}}{2}-\frac {s_j+s_{j+1}}{2}\right| +\frac {1}{3} \sum _{i=0}^{N-1} w_i^2 (s_{i+1}-s_i) $$
    where $s$ represents samples and $w$ represents weights [S1].

            nn.ReLU(),

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **OOM:** Naively using larger grid sizes for $K$ causes Out-Of-Memory issues. Mitigation: Use $K=64$ [S1].
*   **Pooling Strategy:** Pooling features along the viewing dimension too early (e.g., in tri-planar construction) hurts performance. Mitigation: Mean pool in the fourth MLP layer if more than one image is in the input [S1].
*   **Naive Triplanes:** Using triplanes without generative losses or proper training degrades performance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The model has 17M parameters, significantly larger than vanilla NeRF (1.2M rendering MLP + latent codes) due to the ResNet feature block (~10M) and convolutional blocks [S1]. Training requires 8 A-100 GPUs for approximately 1 day [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the NeRDS 360 dataset (Single Map, 3 views, zero-shot), NeO 360 achieves PSNR 19.35, SSIM 0.59, LPIPS 0.50 [S1]. On the multi-map dataset, it achieves an absolute PSNR improvement of 2.39 on complete scenes and 2.93 on objects compared to baselines [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Scene priors provide an absolute PSNR improvement of 4.08 when comparing a network learned on 25 scenes vs one with no prior scenes [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Zero-shot generalization to novel scenes and objects without per-scene optimization or GAN inversion [S1]. Superior performance over PixelNeRF and MVSNeRF in zero-shot and finetuning scenarios [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Larger model size compared to vanilla NeRF [S1]. Performance on KITTI-360 (adapted) shows comparable PSNR to NeRF but improved SSIM [S1].

## Background
NeO 360 was proposed in 2023 by the authors of the ICCV paper "NeO 360: Neural Fields for Sparse View Synthesis of Outdoor Scenes" [S1]. It addresses the challenge of few-shot novel view synthesis for unbounded 360-degree outdoor scenes, a setting where traditional NeRF methods fail due to the lack of per-scene optimization capability and the complexity of unbounded environments [S1]. The method builds on the principles of generalizable NeRFs like PixelNeRF but introduces image-conditional tri-planar representations and local residual features to learn robust scene priors [S1]. It was trained on the NeRDS 360 dataset, a large-scale dataset for 360-degree view synthesis [S1].
