# SelfNeRF

Also called: SelfNeRF: Fast Training NeRF for Human from Monocular Self-rotating Video

## Overview
SelfNeRF is a novel view synthesis method for human performance that trains from scratch using monocular self-rotating video inputs, achieving high-fidelity results in approximately twenty minutes by using a surface-relative hash encoding to aggregate inter-frame information [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Depth-Guided NeRF Training Configuration](11916-depth-guided-nerf-training-configuration.md) and [CLIPNeRF Training Configuration](12073-clipnerf-training-configuration.md) by relying on a single camera and a specific dynamic human representation that maps current frame geometry to a canonical space, rather than using multi-view inputs or external semantic constraints.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A monocular self-rotating video of a human performer consisting of $N_t$ frames.
*   **Output:** A trained neural radiance field capable of rendering novel views of the performer.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Surface Shape $\{T_t\}$:** A point cloud representing the surface geometry for each frame $t$, where $t = 0, \dots, N_t - 1$. These are recovered using external reconstruction methods (e.g., VideoAvatar or SelfRecon) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Body Mask $\{M_t\}$:** A binary mask for each frame $t$ indicating the human region [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hash Grid:** A multi-resolution hash encoding table storing high-dimensional feature vectors.
*   **NeRF MLP ($F_\omega$):** A multi-layer perceptron with learnable weights $\omega$ that takes encoded features as input and outputs density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $N_t$: Total number of frames in the input monocular video [S1, unofficial].
*   $K$: Number of nearest neighbors used to calculate the surface-relative representation [S1, unofficial].
*   Typical training time: ~20 minutes [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Preprocessing:** For each frame $t$ in the input video, recover the surface shape $T_t$ and body mask $M_t$ using an existing 3D reconstruction method [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Sampling:** For a camera ray $r = o + tv$, sample $N$ points $x_i$ along the ray [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Surface-Relative Representation:** For each sample point $x$ at frame $t$:
    a. Find the $K$-nearest neighbor points on the current frame's point cloud $T_t$ [S1, unofficial].
    b. Calculate the signed distances from $x$ to these $K$ neighbors on $T_t$ [S1, unofficial].
    c. Map these $K$ neighbors and their signed distances to their corresponding points and signed distances in the canonical space (Frame 0, $T_0$) [S1, unofficial]. This forms the surface-relative representation [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Hash Encoding:** Encode the surface-relative representation using multi-resolution hash encoding to produce a high-dimensional feature vector [S1, unofficial].
5.  **NeRF Prediction:** Feed the encoded feature vector into the NeRF MLP $F_\omega$ to regress the density $\sigma(x)$ and color $c(x, v)$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Volume Rendering:** Calculate the pixel color $C(r)$ for ray $r$ using the standard volume rendering equation [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha(x_i) \left( \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha(x_j)) \right) c(x_i, v) $$
    where $\alpha(x_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(x_i) \delta_i)$, $x_i = o + t_i v$, and $\delta_i = u_{i+1} - u_i$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Optimization:** Train the model by minimizing photometric loss and geometric guidance loss [S1, unofficial].

                points_x = sample_points_along_ray(ray, N)
                
                # Step 3: Calculate Surface-Relative Representation
                relative_reprs = []

                    )

                # Step 4: Hash Encoding
                features = hash_encode(relative_reprs)
                
                # Step 5: NeRF Prediction
                densities, colors = nerf_mlp(features, ray.direction)
                
                # Step 6: Volume Rendering

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Reconstruction Quality:** The method relies on external tools (VideoAvatar, SelfRecon) for surface shape recovery. Poor reconstruction quality in the input video will directly degrade the surface-relative representation and final rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Alignment:** The mapping from current frame neighbors to canonical space neighbors assumes consistent alignment between frames. Misalignment can lead to incorrect relative representations [S1, unofficial].
*   **K Selection:** The choice of $K$ affects the granularity of the surface-relative representation. Too small $K$ may miss local geometry; too large $K$ may introduce noise from distant points [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than traditional NeRF methods, converging in approximately 20 minutes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses multi-resolution hash encoding, which is memory-efficient compared to dense voxel grids [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-fidelity results from monocular input; fast training time; effective aggregation of inter-frame information [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires self-rotating video input; dependent on external reconstruction methods for initial surface shape; limited to human performance scenarios [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity novel view synthesis of a human performer is needed from a single camera with limited training time.
*   **When not to use:** For static scenes, multi-view setups, or non-human subjects where the surface-relative representation is not applicable.

## Background
SelfNeRF was proposed by Bo Peng, Jun Hu, Jingtao Zhou, and Juyong Zhang from the University of Science and Technology of China [S1, unofficial]. It was published on arXiv on October 4, 2022 [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the challenge of novel view synthesis for human performance using monocular cameras, which is valuable for applications like sports broadcasts, video conferences, and VR/AR [S1, unofficial]. Previous methods often required multi-view inputs and hours of training, making them impractical for public users [S1, unofficial]. SelfNeRF introduces a surface-relative representation based on multi-resolution hash encoding to speed up training and aggregate inter-frame information [S1, unofficial].
