# Spherical Harmonics NeRF
Also called: NeRF with Spherical Harmonics, SH-NeRF

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics NeRF replaces the view-dependent color prediction of the baseline NeRF architecture with an explicit 2nd-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) illumination model, enabling explicit relighting by varying learnable SH coefficients [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which learns view-dependent appearance implicitly through a neural network, this method separates surface albedo from lighting, allowing for semantic control over illumination [S1].

## How it works
The method modifies the rendering equation to compute color $\mathbf{C}$ as the product of accumulated screen-space albedo $\mathbf{A}$ and an SH-based lighting term evaluated at the accumulated surface normal $\mathbf{N}$ [S1].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Ray samples $\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}$, density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$, transmittance $T(t_i)$, opacity $\alpha$, sample spacing $\delta_i$, and per-image learnable SH coefficients $\mathbf{L}$.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered color $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**Data Structures:**
*   $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 3}$: Per-image learnable SH coefficients. The first dimension (9) corresponds to the 2nd-order SH basis functions, and the second (3) corresponds to RGB channels [S1].
*   $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Accumulated albedo, computed similarly to standard NeRF color but from an albedo MLP [S1].
*   $\mathbf{N} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Accumulated surface normal vector [S1].

**SH Basis Evaluation:**
The SH basis vector $\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{n}) \in \mathbb{R}^9$ is evaluated at the normal $\mathbf{n}$. For 2nd-order SH, the basis functions depend on the components of $\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$ [S1]. The specific analytical forms of the 9 basis functions are standard mathematical constants and are not learned [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Compute Unnormalized Normal:** Calculate the unnormalized normal vector $\mathbf{\hat{N}}$ by summing the gradient of the density with respect to the spatial coordinates, weighted by the volume rendering weights (transmittance and opacity) [S1]:
    $$
    \mathbf{\hat{N}}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}} \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{x}_i} \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i) \right) \odot T(t_i) \alpha(\sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)\delta_i)
    $$
    where $\frac{\partial}{\partial \mathbf{x}_i} \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$ is the gradient of density with respect to the $x, y, z$ components of the ray sample $\mathbf{x}_i$ [S1].

2.  **Normalize Normal:** Normalize $\mathbf{\hat{N}}$ to obtain the unit surface normal $\mathbf{N}$ [S1]:
    $$
    \mathbf{N}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right) = \frac{\mathbf{\hat{N}}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right)}{\left\| \mathbf{\hat{N}}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right) \right\|^2}
    $$
    Note: The source uses the squared norm in the denominator for normalization [S1].

3.  **Evaluate SH Basis:** Compute the SH basis vector $\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{N}) \in \mathbb{R}^9$ using the standard 2nd-order spherical harmonics functions evaluated at $\mathbf{N}$ [S1].

4.  **Compute Lighting:** Multiply the learnable coefficients $\mathbf{L}$ by the basis vector to get the lighting term [S1]:
    $$
    \mathbf{L} \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{N})
    $$
    This results in a vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$ representing the incident lighting color for the given normal [S1].

5.  **Render Color:** Compute the final color by element-wise multiplying the accumulated albedo $\mathbf{A}$ with the lighting term [S1]:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}, \mathbf{L}\right) = \mathbf{A}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right) \odot \mathbf{L} \mathbf{b}\left(\mathbf{N}\left(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{depth}}}\right)\right)
    $$

**Parameters:**
*   $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 3}$: Learnable per-image SH coefficients. These are optimized during training to fit the illumination of the specific image [S1].
*   $N_{\mathrm{depth}}$: Number of samples along the ray [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Shadowing:** The model lacks direct shadow generation, which is a significant limitation for outdoor scenes where shadows are crucial for relighting [S1].
*   **Normal Estimation:** The normal is derived from density gradients. If the density field is noisy or poorly converged, the normal estimates may be inaccurate, leading to incorrect shading [S1].
*   **Lambertian Assumption:** The model accounts for Lambertian effects but does not handle specularities or complex BRDFs beyond diffuse reflection [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def spherical_harmonics_basis_2nd(n):
    """
    Computes the 2nd-order SH basis vector for a normal n.
    n: numpy array of shape (3,)
    Returns: numpy array of shape (9,)
    """
    x, y, z = n[0], n[1], n[2]
    # Standard 2nd-order SH basis functions
    b0 = 0.282095
    b1 = 0.488603 * y
    b2 = 0.488603 * z
    b3 = 0.488603 * x
    b4 = 1.092548 * x * y
    b5 = 0.315392 * (3 * z**2 - 1)
    b6 = 0.546274 * y * z
    b7 = 0.546274 * x * z
    b8 = 0.105783 * (x**2 - y**2)
    return np.array([b0, b1, b2, b3, b4, b5, b6, b7, b8])

def render_sh_nerf(samples, density, transmittance, opacity, albedo, L):
    """
    Renders color using Spherical Harmonics NeRF.
    
    samples: numpy array of shape (N, 3), ray samples x_i
    density: numpy array of shape (N,), sigma(x_i)
    transmittance: numpy array of shape (N,), T(t_i)
    opacity: numpy array of shape (N,), alpha(sigma(x_i) * delta_i)
    albedo: numpy array of shape (3,), accumulated albedo A
    L: numpy array of shape (9, 3), learnable SH coefficients
    
    Returns:
    color: numpy array of shape (3,), rendered color C
    """
    N = len(samples)
    
    # Step 1: Compute unnormalized normal
    # Gradient of density w.r.t spatial coordinates
    # Note: In practice, this requires autograd or finite differences.
    # Here we assume grad_density is precomputed or available.
    # grad_density shape: (N, 3)
    grad_density = compute_density_gradient(samples, density) 
    
    # Weights: T(t_i) * alpha
    weights = transmittance * opacity  # Shape (N,)
    
    # Weighted sum of gradients
    # grad_density[i] is a vector (3,)
    # weights[i] is a scalar
    # Result is sum over i of weights[i] * grad_density[i]
    hat_N = np.sum(weights[:, np.newaxis] * grad_density, axis=0)  # Shape (3,)
    
    # Step 2: Normalize normal
    # Source uses squared norm in denominator
    norm_sq = np.dot(hat_N, hat_N)
    if norm_sq == 0:
        N_vec = np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0]) # Default normal if zero
    else:
        N_vec = hat_N / norm_sq
        
    # Step 3: Evaluate SH basis
    b = spherical_harmonics_basis_2nd(N_vec)  # Shape (9,)
    
    # Step 4: Compute lighting term L @ b
    # L shape (9, 3), b shape (9,)
    # Result shape (3,)
    lighting = L.T @ b
    
    # Step 5: Render color
    color = albedo * lighting
    
    return color

def compute_density_gradient(samples, density):
    """
    Placeholder for computing gradient of density w.r.t spatial coordinates.
    In a real implementation, this would use automatic differentiation.
    """
    # [G] This is a placeholder. In PyTorch/TensorFlow, this would be
    # torch.autograd.grad(density.sum(), samples, create_graph=True)
    # Here we return zeros for demonstration.
    return np.zeros_like(samples)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The rendering step involves a single pass over $N_{\mathrm{depth}}$ samples to accumulate albedo and normal, followed by a constant-time SH evaluation and matrix-vector multiplication. The complexity is $O(N_{\mathrm{depth}})$ per ray [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing per-image SH coefficients $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 3}$, which is negligible compared to the neural network weights [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables explicit relighting by modifying $\mathbf{L}$; separates albedo from lighting; reduces noise in normal and albedo estimation through accumulation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot generate direct shadows; assumes Lambertian surface reflection; performance depends on the quality of the density field for normal estimation [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for indoor scenes or outdoor scenes where shadows are not critical and Lambertian approximation is valid [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Not suitable for outdoor scenes requiring realistic shadows or specular reflections [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by V. Rudnev et al. in "NeRF for Outdoor Scene Relighting" (2022) [S1]. It addresses the lack of semantic meaning and direct lighting control in standard NeRF representations [S1]. The approach builds on the standard NeRF architecture but replaces the view-dependent color MLP with an explicit SH illumination model [S1].
