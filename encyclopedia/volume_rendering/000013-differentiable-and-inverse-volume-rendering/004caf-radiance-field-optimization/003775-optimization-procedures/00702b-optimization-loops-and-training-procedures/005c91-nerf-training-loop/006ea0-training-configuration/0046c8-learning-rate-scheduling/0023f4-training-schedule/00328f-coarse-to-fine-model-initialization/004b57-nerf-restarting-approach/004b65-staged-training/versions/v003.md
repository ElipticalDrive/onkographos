# Staged Training

Also called: Staged Pose-NeRF Refinement

## Overview
Staged Training is a two-phase optimization protocol for joint camera pose and neural radiance field (NeRF) reconstruction from sparse, noisy initial poses [S1]. It solves the problem of local minima in simultaneous pose and network optimization by decoupling the refinement of camera extrinsics from the high-fidelity learning of scene geometry [S1]. It differs from joint training approaches by freezing poses after an initial coarse registration, which prevents the fine network from learning blurry surfaces due to pose exploration [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ images $\{I_i\}$, initial camera poses $\{P_i\}$, a coarse NeRF network $F_{\theta_c}$, and a fine NeRF network $F_{\theta_f}$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Refined camera poses $\{\hat{P}_i\}$ and optimized radiance fields $F_{\theta_c}, F_{\theta_f}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Pose:** $P_i = [R_i | t_i]$, where $R_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is a rotation matrix and $t_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a translation vector [G].
*   **NeRF Network:** $F_{\theta}(x, d) \to (\sigma, c)$, mapping 3D position $x$ and viewing direction $d$ to density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [G].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Stage 1: Pose Refinement with Coarse Network**
    *   Initialize the coarse network $F_{\theta_c}$ with random weights [G].
    *   Keep the fine network $F_{\theta_f}$ untrained or frozen with random weights [G].
    *   Optimize the parameters of $F_{\theta_c}$ ($\theta_c$) and the camera poses $P_i$ simultaneously by minimizing the photometric loss between rendered and input images [S1].
    *   This stage aligns the camera poses to the coarse geometry learned by $F_{\theta_c}$ [S1].

2.  **Stage 2: Joint Refinement with Frozen Poses**
    *   Freeze the camera poses $P_i$ to the estimates obtained from Stage 1 [S1].
    *   Optimize the parameters of both the coarse network $F_{\theta_c}$ ($\theta_c$) and the fine network $F_{\theta_f}$ ($\theta_f$) simultaneously [S1].
    *   The fine network learns high-frequency details and sharp geometry, benefiting from the now-registered poses and the pre-trained coarse network which provides a stable low-frequency baseline [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   Photometric Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \sum_{i} \| I_i - \hat{I}_i \|^2$, where $\hat{I}_i$ is the rendered image for pose $P_i$ [G].
*   Total Loss in Stage 1: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}^{(1)} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(\theta_c)$, where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$ is a regularization term on network weights [G].
*   Total Loss in Stage 2: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}^{(2)} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(\theta_c) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}(\theta_f))$ [G].

**Parameters:**
*   Learning rates for $\theta_c$, $\theta_f$, and $P_i$ are typically decayed over time [G].
*   Regularization weight $\lambda_{\text{reg}}$ is not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Pose Quality:** If initial poses are too noisy, Stage 1 may fail to converge to a useful alignment; the method is evaluated with 15% initial additive Gaussian noise [S1].
*   **Local Minima:** Joint training of poses and both networks without staging causes blurry surfaces because the fine network explores the pose space while learning geometry [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def render_image(network, pose, rays):
    """Standard volume rendering: sample points along rays, evaluate network, apply alpha compositing."""
    points = sample_along_rays(pose, rays)
    densities, colors = network(points, rays)
    return composite_alpha(densities, colors)

def update_params(params, gradients, lr):
    """Apply gradient descent step."""
    for p, g in zip(params, gradients):
        p -= lr * g

def staged_training(images, initial_poses, coarse_net, fine_net, lr1, lr2, epochs1, epochs2):
    # Initialize trainable pose copies
    poses = [copy(p) for p in initial_poses]
    theta_c = coarse_net.parameters
    theta_f = fine_net.parameters

    # Stage 1: Refine poses and coarse network
    for epoch in range(epochs1):
        for i, img in enumerate(images):
            rays = rays_from_image(img)
            rendered = render_image(coarse_net, poses[i], rays)
            loss = sum((rendered - img) ** 2)
            grad_c, grad_p = compute_gradients(loss, theta_c, poses[i])
            update_params(theta_c, grad_c, lr1)
            update_params(poses[i], grad_p, lr1)

    # Stage 2: Freeze poses, train both networks
    for epoch in range(epochs2):
        for i, img in enumerate(images):
            rays = rays_from_image(img)
            rendered_coarse = render_image(coarse_net, poses[i], rays)
            rendered_fine = render_image(fine_net, poses[i], rays)
            composite_rendered = composite_alpha(rendered_coarse, rendered_fine)
            loss = sum((composite_rendered - img) ** 2)
            grad_c, grad_f = compute_gradients(loss, theta_c, theta_f)
            update_params(theta_c, grad_c, lr2)
            update_params(theta_f, grad_f, lr2)

    return poses, coarse_net, fine_net
```

## Subtopics

### [Two-stage Training for Deformable NeRF](21743-two-stage-training-for-deformable-nerf.md)
A training protocol for deformable neural radiance fields that reduces GPU memory consumption and accelerates convergence by decoupling the initialization of the tetrahedral mesh from the high-resolution refinement [G]. It differs from single-stage training by first constructing a sparse, occupancy-aware coarse mesh and then refining it, whereas single-stage methods initialize with a dense, uniform grid regardless of scene occupancy [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Two sequential optimization phases [G]. Stage 1 is generally faster as it only optimizes poses and a coarse network [G]. Stage 2 optimizes two networks but with frozen poses, which can be more stable [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Similar to standard NeRF training, but avoids the memory overhead of storing gradients for poses during the fine network training phase in Stage 2 [G].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the DTU dataset with 3 views and 15% initial additive Gaussian noise, Staged Training achieves a PSNR of 17.58 (18.62 masked), SSIM of 0.71 (0.82 masked), LPIPS of 0.26 (0.13 masked), and Depth Error (DE) of 1.81 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Prevents blurry surfaces by decoupling pose and geometry optimization [S1]. Improves PSNR and SSIM compared to joint training of poses with both coarse and fine MLPs [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the transition between stages [G]. Performance depends heavily on the quality of the initial poses [G].
*   **When to use:** When initial camera poses are sparse and noisy, and joint optimization leads to poor geometry [G].
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality initial poses are available, or when computational resources are extremely limited for two-stage optimization [G].

## Background
Staged Training was introduced in the context of "SPARF: Neural Radiance Fields from Sparse and Noisy Poses" [S1]. It addresses the challenges of reconstructing NeRFs from sparse and noisy camera poses, where traditional joint optimization methods struggle with local minima and blurry reconstructions [S1]. The method builds on the idea of coarse-to-fine optimization but explicitly stages the pose refinement process [S1].
