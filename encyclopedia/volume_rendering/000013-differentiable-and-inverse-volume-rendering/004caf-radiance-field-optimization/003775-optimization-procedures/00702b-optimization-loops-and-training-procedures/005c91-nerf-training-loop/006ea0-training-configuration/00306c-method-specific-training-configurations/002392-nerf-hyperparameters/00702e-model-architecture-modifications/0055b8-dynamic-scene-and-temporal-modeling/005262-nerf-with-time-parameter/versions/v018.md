# NeRF with Time Parameter

Also called: NeRF + time, NeRF with Time Parameter Baseline, Time-NeRF

## Overview
This method adapts the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) architecture for dynamic scenes by introducing a temporal dimension to the input space [S1]. It solves the problem of representing time-varying geometry and appearance, which standard NeRF cannot handle due to its static assumption [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that employ complex warping fields, particle systems, or graph structures to model dynamics, this approach serves as a simple baseline by directly conditioning the neural network on time [S1]. While it improves reconstruction quality over vanilla NeRF on dynamic data, it is limited by blurry predictions and an inability to reason about complex scene dynamics [S1].

## How it works
The method modifies the standard NeRF input pipeline to accept time as a continuous parameter.

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Spatial position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and time parameter $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**Data Structures**
The neural network architecture remains identical to the standard NeRF baseline, except for the expanded input vector. The positional encoding function $\gamma$ is applied to spatial coordinates and time separately.

**Steps**
1.  **Positional Encoding of Time:** Apply the positional encoding function $\gamma_t$ to the time parameter $t$. The specific frequency bands and number of octaves for $\gamma_t$ are not explicitly defined in the sources, but it follows the standard positional encoding scheme used for spatial coordinates [S1].
2.  **Concatenation:** Concatenate the encoded time $\gamma_t(t)$ with the positional encoding of the spatial coordinates $\gamma_x(\mathbf{x})$. The viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ is encoded separately as $\gamma_d(\mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    $$ \mathbf{v} = [\gamma_x(\mathbf{x}), \gamma_t(t)] $$
3.  **Network Forward Pass:** Feed the concatenated vector $\mathbf{v}$ and the direction encoding $\gamma_d(\mathbf{d})$ into the MLP. The network outputs density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
4.  **Rendering:** Perform standard volume rendering integration along rays using the outputs $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [G].
5.  **Training:** Train the model using the configuration reported for NeRF's real forward-facing experiments [S1]. This includes the standard loss functions and optimization hyperparameters used in the original NeRF paper [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Time Encoding:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Training Configuration:** Same as NeRF real forward-facing experiments [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Blurriness:** The method suffers from blurry predictions and lacks detail compared to more advanced dynamic NeRF variants [S1].
*   **Dynamic Reasoning:** It fails to reason about scene dynamics, leading to artifacts when camera poses change significantly across time [S1].
*   **Ghosting:** While improved over vanilla NeRF, it may still exhibit ghosting artifacts if the temporal interpolation is insufficient for rapid motion [S1].

## Subtopics

### Temporal Compression of NeRF Models
A compression strategy for sequences of NeRF models that exploits temporal redundancy by treating the first model as a key-frame and compressing only the deltas between successive models [S2]. It is chosen when storage or transmission bandwidth for a sequence of NeRFs is constrained [S2].
*   **Initialization:** The NeRF for the first frame is trained from scratch [S2].
*   **Subsequent Frames:** Each subsequent NeRF is initialized using the weights of the previously trained frame's NeRF to encourage temporal redundancy and reduce training time [S2].
*   **Delta Calculation:** Compute the delta between the current frame's NeRF weights and the previous frame's NeRF weights [S2].
*   **Compression:** Apply exponent-based quantization and Huffman coding to the calculated deltas [S2].
*   **Restoration:** To restore a model, accumulate the decoded deltas to the reference model (the previous frame's model) [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is reduced for subsequent frames in the temporal compression variant due to warm-start initialization [S2]. The baseline "NeRF + time" training time is comparable to static NeRF but yields lower quality [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The baseline method requires storing one model per time step if not compressed. The compression variant significantly reduces memory by storing only deltas [S2].
*   **Performance:** On KITTI dynamic scenes, "NeRF + time" achieves a PSNR of 24.18, SSIM of 0.677, and LPIPS of 0.425, which is better than vanilla NeRF (PSNR 23.34, SSIM 0.662, LPIPS 0.415) but worse than the proposed Neural Scene Graph method (PSNR 26.66, SSIM 0.806, LPIPS 0.186) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; leverages existing NeRF infrastructure; reduces training time for sequences via warm-start [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Blurry results; poor handling of complex dynamics; significant ghosting compared to graph-based or warping-based methods [S1].
*   **When to use:** As a baseline for comparison; when computational resources for complex dynamic models are unavailable; for compression of NeRF sequences [S1, S2].
*   **When not to use:** When high-fidelity reconstruction of complex dynamic scenes is required; when temporal consistency is critical [S1].

## Background
The "NeRF + time" baseline was introduced in the paper "Neural Scene Graphs for Dynamic Scenes" by Wang et al. (2021) [S1]. It was proposed as a simple modification to demonstrate that simply adding a time parameter is insufficient for high-quality dynamic scene reconstruction, motivating the development of their more complex Neural Scene Graph approach [S1]. The temporal compression strategy for NeRF models was introduced in "Volumetric Video Compression Through Neural-based Representation" by Shi et al. (2024) [S2].
