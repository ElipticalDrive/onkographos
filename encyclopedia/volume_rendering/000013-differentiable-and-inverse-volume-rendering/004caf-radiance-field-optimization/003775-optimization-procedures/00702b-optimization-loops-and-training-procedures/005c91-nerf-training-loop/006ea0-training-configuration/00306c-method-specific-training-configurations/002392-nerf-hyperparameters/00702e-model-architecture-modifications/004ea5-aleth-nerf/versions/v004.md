# Aleth-NeRF

Also called: Aleth-NeRF with Concealing Field Assumption

## Overview
Aleth-NeRF is a NeRF variant that trains on under- and over-exposed images to render normal-light novel views by introducing a 'Concealing Field' assumption, where air particles attenuate emission rather than objects lacking emission [S1]. It differs from RAW-NeRF by taking 8-bit LDR images as inputs, whereas RAW-NeRF requires 16-bit HDR RAW images [S1]. Unlike methods that apply enhancement networks before or after NeRF training, Aleth-NeRF trains end-to-end without pre-processing or post-processing [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Low-light or over-exposed multi-view images (8-bit LDR). Ground truth normal-light images are used for evaluation but not for training supervision in the unsupervised setting described [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Novel views rendered in normal-light conditions [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Density MLP ($F_\sigma$):** Maps 3D location $\mathbf{r}(i)$ to density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ [S1].
2.  **Color MLP ($F_c$):** Maps hidden feature $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
3.  **Local Concealing Field ($\Omega$):** Generated by applying a large kernel convolution layer of size 7 to the output of $F_\sigma$ [S1].
    *   Layout: $\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \text{Conv}_{7} (F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i)))$ [S1].
4.  **Global Concealing Field ($\Theta_G$):** A set of learnable parameters corresponding to camera distance $i$ for all camera rays [S1].
    *   Layout: $\Theta_G(i) \in \mathbb{R}$, constant within a scene, independent of voxels [S1].
    *   Initialization: $\Theta_G(i) = 0.5$ for each camera distance $i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Standard NeRF Rendering:**
    The network uses two MLPs: density MLP $F_\sigma$ maps 3D location $\mathbf{r}(i)$ to density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$, and color MLP $F_c$ maps hidden feature $\mathbf{h}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    The discrete form of standard volume rendering is:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T(\mathbf{r}(i))(1-\exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))\cdot\delta))\cdot\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(i), \mathbf{d}) $$
    $$ T(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(j)) \cdot \delta \right) $$
    where $\delta$ is a constant distance value between adjacent sample points under uniform sampling [S1].

2.  **Compute Local Concealing Field:**
    Calculate local concealing field $\Omega(\mathbf{r}(i))$ by applying a large kernel convolution (size 7) on the density MLP output $F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))$ [S1]:
    $$ \Omega(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \text{Conv}_{\text{size}:7} (F_\sigma(\mathbf{r}(i))) $$
    This establishes spatial relationships and enriches the field with light-related information [S1].

3.  **Compute Concealed Transmittance:**
    Calculate accumulated transmittance $T_{\text{conceal}}(\mathbf{r}(i))$ by multiplying standard transmittance terms with the product of local and global concealing fields along the ray [S1]:
    $$ T_{\text{conceal}}(\mathbf{r}(i)) = \exp \left( -\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(j)) \cdot \delta \right) \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} \Omega(\mathbf{r}(j))\Theta_G(j) $$
    This mimics the process of light suppression [S1].

4.  **Rendering for Training (Low-Light Scenario):**
    *   Generate low-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{adv}}$ by incorporating $T_{\text{conceal}}$ during volume rendering [S1].
    *   Generate normal-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ by removing concealing fields (using original $T$) [S1].

5.  **Rendering for Training (Over-Exposure Scenario):**
    *   Generate very-bright images $\hat{C}_{\text{adv}}$ using original $T$ [S1].
    *   Generate normal-light images $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ by incorporating concealing fields along with $T_{\text{conceal}}$ [S1].

6.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Inverse Tone MSE Loss ($L_{\text{it-mse}}$):** To rectify imbalance where dark pixels contribute minimally and bright pixels dominate, use an inverse tone curve $\Phi$ to re-balance pixel weights [S1].
        $$ \Phi(x) = \frac{1}{3} \left( \sin^{-1}(1 - 2x) - \sin\left(\frac{1 - 2x}{2}\right) \right) $$
        $$ L_{\text{it-mse}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \| \Phi(\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon) - \Phi(C(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon) \|^2 $$
        where $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$ [S1]. Note: In the low-light scenario, $C(\mathbf{r})$ refers to the ground truth normal-light image if available for supervised loss, or the method relies on the unsupervised losses below if strictly unsupervised. The source states "NeRF regression loss is applied to compare predicted adverse-light images with GT adverse-light images" for the concealed rendering, but the inverse tone loss formula uses $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ and $C$. The text implies $L_{\text{it-mse}}$ compares $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$ to $C$ (GT normal light) or potentially $\hat{C}_{\text{adv}}$ to $C_{\text{adv}}$ depending on the specific loss term application. However, Eq 10 explicitly defines $L_{\text{it-mse}}$ with $\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}$. Given the unsupervised nature described for the *additional* losses, $L_{\text{it-mse}}$ likely serves as the primary reconstruction loss against available GTs or is adapted. The source says "Direct apply NeRF’s MSE loss... would result in imbalance... we propose... inverse tone MSE loss". It is applied to the regression task.
    *   **Degree Loss ($L_{\text{de}}$):** Separates the predicted normal-light image from density, controlling enhancement degree [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{de}} = \| \text{avgpool}(\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})) - e \|^2 $$
        where $e = 0.45$ is the enhancement degree hyperparameter [S1].
    *   **Contrast Degree Loss ($L_{\text{co}}$):** Maintains structural similarity and controls contrast between adjacent sampling pixels of predicted normal-light and ground truth adverse-light images [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{co}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \sum_{k \in \{+1, -1\}} \| \hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r}) - \hat{C}_{\text{adv}}(\mathbf{r}+k) \|^2 $$
        Parameter $\eta$ controls contrast degree. In over-exposure scenes, $e \cdot \eta$ is fixed to 0.5 [S1].
    *   **Color Constancy Loss ($L_{\text{cc}}$):** Regularizes color based on the gray-world assumption [S1].
        $$ L_{\text{cc}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r}} \sum_{p,q} (\hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})_p - \hat{C}_{\text{nor}}(\mathbf{r})_q)^2 $$
        where $p, q$ are color channels [S1].

7.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{\text{it-mse}} + \lambda_1 \cdot L_{\text{de}} + \lambda_2 \cdot L_{\text{co}} + \lambda_3 \cdot L_{\text{cc}} $$
    where $\lambda_1 = 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_2 = 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_3 = 10^{-8}$ [S1].

8.  **Optimization:**
    Use Adam optimizer with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and cosine learning rate decay every 2500 iterations [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **MSE Imbalance:** Standard MSE loss causes imbalance where dark pixels contribute minimally and bright pixels dominate. This is solved by using the inverse tone curve $\Phi$ to re-balance pixel weights [S1].
*   **Post-processing vs Pre-processing:** Post-processing NeRF results with enhancement methods ('NeRF + *') underperforms pre-processing ('* + NeRF') because NeRF produces poor-quality dark images that struggle enhancement methods [S1].
*   **Color Imbalance:** Directly removing/adding concealing fields may cause color imbalance, addressed by the color constancy loss [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Aleth-NeRF was introduced by Ziteng Cui, Lin Gu, Xiao Sun, Xianzheng Ma, Yu Qiao, and Tatsuya Harada at The University of Tokyo, Shanghai AI Laboratory, RIKEN AIP, and University of Oxford [S1].
*   **Paper:** "Aleth-NeRF: Illumination Adaptive NeRF with Concealing Field Assumption", published in The Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1].
*   **History:** The method is motivated by the ancient Greek emission theory, which posits that visual perception is formed by visual rays emitted from the eye, and darkness is caused by particles between the object and the eye. This contrasts with the standard NeRF setting where air density is zero [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented using the open-source PyTorch toolbox NeRF-Factory [S1].
