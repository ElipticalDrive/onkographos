# Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering

Also called: AB Transformer, ABLE-NeRF, Attention-Based Rendering With Learnable Embeddings for Neural Radiance Field, Attention-based Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering (implemented as ABLE-NeRF) replaces the physics-based volumetric rendering equation with a self-attention mechanism to handle view-dependent effects, glossy surfaces, and translucent objects [S1][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF by using Transformer attention to determine point weights along a ray rather than fixed density ($\sigma$) and distance ($\delta$) calculations, and from Ref-NeRF by avoiding the ill-posed prediction of interior normals [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Camera ray $r(t) = o + td$ (origin $o$, direction $d$), camera pose.
*   **Output:** Predicted RGB color $\mathbf{y}$ for the ray.

**Data Structures**
1.  **Volume Embedding ($v_i$):** A vector of dimension $D$. Generated by passing a sampled conic frustum through a Volume Embedding block.
2.  **Ray Token ($R$):** A vector of dimension $D$. Prepended to the sequence of volume embeddings.
3.  **Learnable Embeddings (LE):** A set of $K$ static memory tokens, each of dimension $D$. Used to store scene lighting information.
4.  **View Token ($V$):** A vector of dimension $D$. Derived from the camera pose.

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Ray Sampling:** Cast a camera ray $r(t) = o + td$ into the scene. Sample $N$ conic frustum volumes along the ray between near and far boundaries. The positions are encoded using Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) from mip-NeRF [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Volume Embedding:** Pass each conic volume $i$ (where $i=1 \dots N$ is the index from the camera) through a Volume Embedding block consisting of 4 MLP layers with ReLU activations. This generates a sequence of volume embeddings $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_N$, where each $v_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Ray Token Initialization:** Prepend a Ray token $R$ (initialized as a learnable parameter or zero-initialized vector of dimension $D$) to the sequence to form the input set for the first transformer layer:
    $$ Z_0 = \{R, v_1, v_2, \dots, v_N\} $$
    [S1][S2, unofficial]
4.  **AB Transformer (Direct Lighting):** Process the sequence $Z_0$ through $L$ layers of self-attention.
    *   **Masking:** Apply a causal mask to volume embeddings. A volume embedding $v_i$ can only attend to itself, the Ray token $R$, and other volume embeddings $v_j$ where $j < i$ (lying in front of it).
    *   **Implementation:** Set the scaled-dot product attention scores to $-\infty$ for illegal connections (points behind $v_i$) before the softmax operation. No masking is applied to the Ray token $R$, allowing it to attend to all volume embeddings [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   **Update Equations:** For layer $l$ ($1 \dots L$):
        $$ v_i^l = \text{Att}(Z^{l-1} \setminus \{v_{i+1}^{l-1}, \dots, v_N^{l-1}\}) $$
        $$ R^l = \text{Att}(Z^{l-1}) $$
        where $\text{Att}(\cdot)$ denotes the self-attention operation [S1].
    *   After layer $L$, the updated Ray token $R_L$ is passed through a single MLP classification head to predict the direct lighting component [S1].
5.  **Learnable Embeddings (LE) Integration:**
    *   **Cross-Attention:** Use the volume embeddings (or the output of the AB Transformer) as queries and the Learnable Embeddings (LE) as keys/values. The LE tokens attend to the sampled points to memorize scene information (indirect lighting/view-dependent effects) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   **View Token:** Encode the camera pose using 16-band Fourier features and map it linearly to dimension $D$ to create a View Token $V$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **LE Transformer:** Process the sequence containing volume embeddings, LE tokens, and View Token $V$ through an LE Transformer. This separates the view-dependent lighting prediction from the direct lighting [S2, unofficial].
6.  **Color Decoding:** Combine the outputs from the AB Transformer (direct lighting) and LE Transformer (view-dependent lighting). Apply a fixed mapping function to convert the result to sRGB for the final output $\mathbf{y}$ [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $N$: Number of conic frustum volumes sampled along the ray. Typical value: 96 per network (192 total for coarse+fine) [S1].
*   $L$: Number of transformer layers. Typical value: 2 for coarse network, 6 for fine network [S1].
*   $D$: Latent dimensional size of volume embeddings and ray token. Typical value: 192 [S1].
*   Camera pose encoding: 16-band Fourier features [S2, unofficial].
*   Number of LE: 32 for standard Blender dataset, 16 for Shiny Blender dataset [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Masking:** Without masking, the ray is treated as bi-directional, causing the network difficulty in predicting correct surface appearance [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** High computational cost due to repeated Attention calculations for AB and LE Transformers, leading to long training times [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ill-posed Normals:** Unlike Ref-NeRF, this method does not predict normals, avoiding issues with interior point normal prediction [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** High. Training times are significantly longer than standard NeRF due to the Transformer architecture. For example, on an A100 GPU x8, training takes approximately 25 hours per scene [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Increased due to the storage of Learnable Embeddings and the attention mechanism's quadratic complexity with respect to the number of samples $N$ (though masked, the sequence length is $N+1$).
*   **Performance:** Achieves SOTA results on the Blender dataset, surpassing Ref-NeRF in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS [S1]. Specifically, on the 'mic' scene, it achieves a PSNR of 36.84 [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Better handling of glossy and translucent surfaces; captures view-dependent effects via Learnable Embeddings; avoids ill-posed normal prediction [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Long training times; high computational cost [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Attention-Based Volumetric Rendering was introduced by Zhe Jun Tang, Tat-Jen Cham, and Haiyu Zhao in the paper 'ABLE-NeRF: Attention-Based Rendering with Learnable Embeddings for Neural Radiance Field' (arXiv:2303.13817v1, March 2023) [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2023 [S2, unofficial]. The method was developed to address the limitations of standard NeRF in rendering glossy and transparent surfaces, which often appear murky due to the physics-based volumetric rendering equation's inability to capture complex view-dependent effects [S1].
