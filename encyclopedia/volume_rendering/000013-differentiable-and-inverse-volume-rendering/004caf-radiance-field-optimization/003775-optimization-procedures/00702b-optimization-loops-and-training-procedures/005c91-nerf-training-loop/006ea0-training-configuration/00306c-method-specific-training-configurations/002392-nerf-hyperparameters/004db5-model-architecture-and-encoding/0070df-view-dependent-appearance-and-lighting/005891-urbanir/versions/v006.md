# UrbanIR

Also called: Urban Scene Inverse Rendering

## Overview
UrbanIR is a method for the inverse rendering of large-scale urban scenes from a single monocular video under single illumination, inferring shape, albedo, visibility, and sun/sky illumination to enable relighting and night simulation [S1]. It differs from standard NeRFs by learning intrinsic attributes (albedo, normal) independent of lighting, and from other relightable methods like NeRF-OSR or FEGR by requiring only single-illumination RGB video without LiDAR or multiple cameras [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multi-frame video of a scene under single illumination with known camera motion, defined as $\{I_i, E_i, K_i\}$, where $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is the RGB image, $E_i \in SE(3)$ is the camera pose, and $K_i$ is the camera intrinsic matrix [S1].
*   **Output:** A neural scene model capable of rendering novel viewpoints under novel lighting conditions, including albedo, normal, semantic, and density fields, along with inferred sun/sky illumination parameters [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene representation is built on Instant-NGP, a spatial hash-based voxel NeRF representation [S1].
*   **Feature Hash Table:** A dense feature hash table representing the scene [S1].
*   **Decoders:** Individual Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) headers are used to decode each attribute (albedo, normal, semantics, density) given a queried feature at point $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
*   **Scene Field:** The function $F_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ outputs intrinsic attributes independent of viewing angles and lighting: diffuse albedo $\mathbf{a}$, surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, semantic vector $\mathbf{s}$, and density $\sigma$ [S1].
    $$ F_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{n}, \mathbf{s}, \sigma) $$
*   **Lighting Model:** A 5-DoF sun model (sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$, azimuth $\psi_{sun}$, zenith $\phi_{sun}$), a 3-DoF ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$, and a sky dome model inferring sky texture from viewing direction [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Intrinsic Decomposition:** For each camera ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample multiple points along the ray. Query $F_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ to obtain $\mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{n}_i, \mathbf{s}_i, \sigma_i$ at each sample point [S1].
2.  **Volume Rendering of Intrinsics:** Accumulate predictions using alpha-composition weights $w_i$ to produce the final intrinsic maps for albedo $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})$, normal $\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r})$, and semantics $\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{a}_i, \quad \mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{n}_i, \quad \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{s}_i $$
    where the weight $w_i$ is defined as:
    $$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
    and $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
3.  **Visibility Estimation:** To model shadows without per-scene optimized MLP heads (which are impractical for changing sun positions), compute an estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{r})$ of the 3D point being shaded by volume rendering depth (substituting $\hat{t} = \sum w_i t_i$) [S1]. Then, estimate visibility $V(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \text{sun})$ by computing the transmittance along the ray segment between $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and the light source using volume rendering [S1].
4.  **Shading and Rendering:** Combine the intrinsic attributes and shadow visibility with the lighting model $\mathbf{L}$ to produce the final RGB color $\mathbf{C}$:
    $$ \mathbf{C} = \texttt{Shade}(\texttt{Intrinsic}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}), \texttt{Shadow}(F_\theta, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{L}), \mathbf{L}) $$
    [S1].
5.  **Joint Optimization:** Optimize scene parameters $\theta$ and lighting parameters $\mathbf{L}$ jointly using a loss function comprising rendering loss and other regularization terms [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Lighting:** Sun color $\mathbf{L}_{sun}$, azimuth $\psi_{sun}$, zenith $\phi_{sun}$, ambient light $\mathbf{L}_{amb}$ [S1].
*   **Scene:** Learnable parameters $\theta$ for the hash table and MLP headers [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-Relightability of Standard NeRFs:** Standard NeRFs bake shading, shadow, and specularity into observed color, making them non-relightable. UrbanIR solves this by learning intrinsic attributes [S1].
*   **Visibility Modeling:** Modeling visibility with a per-scene optimized MLP head is impractical because the sun position changes during relighting, but training data only has one sun position [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Uses Instant-NGP's spatial hash-based voxel representation, which offers low memory consumption and high efficiency in training and rendering compared to dense voxel grids [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Capable of handling large-scale, unbounded outdoor scenes; requires only single monocular video input; enables realistic relighting and night simulation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Assumes single illumination during training; relies on monocular cues for geometry and lighting inference [S1].

## Background
UrbanIR was introduced by Chih-Hao Lin, Bohan Liu, Yi-Ting Chen, et al. from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It addresses the limitation of existing relightable NeRF methods that focus on small bounded indoor environments or require multiple lighting sources/LiDAR [S1].
