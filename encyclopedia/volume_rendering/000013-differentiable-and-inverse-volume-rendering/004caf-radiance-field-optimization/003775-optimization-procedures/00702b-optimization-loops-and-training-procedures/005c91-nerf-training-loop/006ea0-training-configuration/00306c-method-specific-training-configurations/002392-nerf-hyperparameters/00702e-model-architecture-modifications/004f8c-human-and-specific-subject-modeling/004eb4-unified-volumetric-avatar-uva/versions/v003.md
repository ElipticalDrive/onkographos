# Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)


## Overview
Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA) is a method for representing dynamic human avatars that enables local and independent editing of both geometry and texture while supporting novel view synthesis and novel pose rendering [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF-based approaches by anchoring structured latent codes to a deformable mesh in canonical space, thereby overcoming the spatial ambiguity of discrete code interpolation and the limitations of pre-defined UV mappings which hinder local editing [S1, unofficial]. Unlike two-stage distillation methods that require manual tuning and are restricted to static objects, UVA is end-to-end trainable and jointly optimized with the skinning field from scratch [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Target space query points $\mathbf{x}_t$, pose parameters $\theta_t$, SMPL mesh vertices and topology, and initial LBS weights.
*   **Outputs:** Final pixel color $C(r)$ for a given ray $r$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Deformable Canonical Mesh ($M$):** A mesh in the canonical (T-pose) space.
2.  **Structured Latent Codes:**
    *   $\mathbf{l}_{geo}$: Geometry latent codes anchored to mesh nodes.
    *   $\mathbf{l}_{rgb}$: Appearance (texture) latent codes anchored to mesh nodes.
3.  **Signed Height Indicator ($\tilde{h}$):** A vector containing UV coordinates $(u, v)$ and normalized point-to-surface distance $\bar{d}$.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **LBS Weight Interpolation:** Given a sampled point $\mathbf{x}_t$ in the target (observation) space, determine its Linear Blending Skinning (LBS) weights $\mathbf{w}_b$ by interpolating the weights of its Top-$K$ nearest neighbors (NNs) on the mesh [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{w}_b = \text{Interpolate}(\text{NNs}(\mathbf{x}_t, K), \text{MeshWeights}) $$

2.  **Canonical Space Transformation:** Transform the target point $\mathbf{x}_t$ into the canonical space $\mathbf{x}_c$ by combining the inverse LBS transformation with a neural non-rigid deformation field $\Delta \phi$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{x}_c = \text{LBS}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{w}_b, \theta_t) + \Delta \phi(\mathbf{x}_t) $$
    *Note: The deformation field $\Delta \phi$ is conditioned on the appearance latent codes $\mathbf{l}_{rgb}$ in the source diagram description, though the text emphasizes the combination of skeleton motion and non-rigid field [S1, unofficial].*

3.  **Latent Code Interpolation:** Calculate the latent code for the arbitrary canonical point $\mathbf{x}_c$ through Nearest-Neighbor (NN) interpolation of the structured latent codes anchored on the mesh nodes [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{l}_{geo}(\mathbf{x}_c) = \text{NNInterp}(\mathbf{x}_c, \{\mathbf{l}_{geo}^{\text{node}}\}) $$
    $$ \mathbf{l}_{rgb}(\mathbf{x}_c) = \text{NNInterp}(\mathbf{x}_c, \{\mathbf{l}_{rgb}^{\text{node}}\}) $$

4.  **Signed Height Indicator Calculation:** Compute the local signed height indicator $\tilde{h}$ for $\mathbf{x}_c$. This comprises UV coordinates $(u, v)$ and the point-to-surface distance $\bar{d}$ relative to the anchored mesh [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \tilde{h} = [u, v, \bar{d}] $$
    *Note: $\bar{d}$ tracks mesh deformation, addressing spatial query indistinguishability caused by NN interpolation of discrete codes [S1, unofficial].*

5.  **Density and Base Color Prediction:** Use a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to predict volume density $\sigma$ and base color $\mathbf{c}_0$ given the interpolated latent codes and the signed height indicator [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \sigma, \mathbf{c}_0 = \text{MLP}_{\text{density}}(\mathbf{l}_{geo}(\mathbf{x}_c), \tilde{h}) $$
    $$ \mathbf{c}_0 = \text{MLP}_{\text{color}}(\mathbf{l}_{rgb}(\mathbf{x}_c), \tilde{h}) $$
    *Note: The source diagram [S1, unofficial] shows $\sigma$ derived from $\mathbf{l}_{geo}$ and $\tilde{h}$, and $\mathbf{c}_0$ derived from $\mathbf{l}_{rgb}$ and $\tilde{h}$. The text states "use an MLP to predict volume density $\sigma$ and base color $\mathbf{c}_0$ given the interpolated latent codes and the signed height indicator".*

6.  **Pose-Dependent Shading:** Modulate the base color $\mathbf{c}_0$ with a pose-dependent shading factor $s$ to represent surface illumination in different poses [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{c}_{\text{mod}} = \mathbf{c}_0 \otimes s(\theta_t) $$
    *Note: The exact formula for $s$ is not provided in the text, but it is described as a pose-dependent shading factor replacing view-dependent radiance [S1, unofficial].*

7.  **Volume Rendering:** Integrate the modulated colors along the target ray $r$ to produce the final color $C(r)$ using standard volume rendering integration [S1, unofficial].
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_{\text{mod}, i} $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ and $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$.

**Parameters:**
*   **Top-K:** The number of nearest neighbors used for interpolating LBS weights and latent codes [S1, unofficial]. Typical values are not explicitly stated in the text, but Table 1 in the source lists "Local Tex." and "Local Geo." with values 8, 4, 8, 4, 4 which may correspond to hyperparameters or editing capabilities, but the specific Top-K value is "Not given in the sources."

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spatial Ambiguity:** Using discrete latent codes defined on mesh nodes diffused via nearest-neighbor interpolation leads to spatial query indistinguishability [S1, unofficial]. This is mitigated by the signed height indicator $\tilde{h}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **UV Mapping Limitations:** Unlike methods using pre-defined UV mapping, UVA anchors codes to a mesh, avoiding the difficulty of local texture editing inherent in 3D-to-2D UV mappings [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Experiments on multiple human avatars demonstrate competitive results in novel view synthesis and novel pose rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables local and independent editing of both geometry and texture; end-to-end trainable; jointly optimized with the skinning field from scratch; handles dynamic avatars [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on a deformable mesh; requires Top-K nearest neighbor searches which may be computationally intensive for large meshes; spatial ambiguity requires careful handling via signed height indicators [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When local editing of geometry and texture is required for dynamic human avatars, and end-to-end optimization is preferred over two-stage distillation [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** For static objects where two-stage distillation is sufficient, or when computational resources for nearest-neighbor interpolation are limited [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA) was proposed to address the challenge of editing dynamic avatars, which remains an open question compared to static objects [S1, unofficial]. Previous methods either used pre-defined UV mappings (limiting local editing) or two-stage distillation (requiring manual tuning and limited to static objects) [S1, unofficial]. UVA introduces a novel baseline that encodes geometry and appearance in separate fields anchored to a deformable mesh in canonical space, enabling simple and intuitive manipulation [S1, unofficial]. The method was presented in a paper titled "UVA: Towards Unified Volumetric Avatar for View Synthesis, Pose rendering, Geometry and Texture Editing" [S1, unofficial].
