# FlipNeRF

Also called: Flipped Reflection Rays for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis

## Overview
FlipNeRF is a regularization framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that generates auxiliary "flipped reflection rays" from original input rays and estimated surface normals to improve rendering quality and surface normal accuracy with sparse inputs [S1]. Unlike Ref-NeRF, which reparameterizes the radiance field by replacing the viewing direction with a reflection direction, FlipNeRF generates additional training rays without modifying the original representation, allowing it to leverage reflection geometry as extra supervision [S1]. It distinguishes itself from pre-training methods by optimizing per-scene without large-scale priors, and from heuristic unseen-view sampling strategies by deriving rays deterministically from estimated normals [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of $N$ input images with known camera poses, defining a set of original input rays $\mathcal{R} = \{\mathbf{r}\}$ [S1]. Each ray $\mathbf{r}$ is defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained radiance field model $F_\Theta$ (built upon MixNeRF) that predicts density and color distributions, and synthesized novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method builds upon MixNeRF, which models the color distribution of a ray as a mixture of Gaussians [S1]. For a ray with $M$ samples:
*   Blending weights: $\mathbf{w} = [w_1, \dots, w_M]$, where $w_i$ is the weight of the $i$-th sample [S1].
*   Mixture parameters per sample $i$: Mean color $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i^{\mathbf{c}}$ and scale parameters $\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^c$ for each color channel $c \in \{r, g, b\}$ [S1].
*   Bottleneck features: Intermediate feature vectors $\mathbf{b}$ output by the spatial MLP of the NeRF architecture [S1].
*   Estimated surface normals: $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, computed as the weighted sum of sample normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ along the ray: $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \sum_{i=1}^M w_i \mathbf{n}_i$ [S1]. The sample normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ are derived from the gradient of the volume density $\sigma$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Compute Estimated Surface Normal:**
    For each original input ray $\mathbf{r}$, calculate the estimated surface normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ using the blending weights $w_i$ and the density gradient normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ of the $M$ samples along the ray:
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}} = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \mathbf{n}_i $$
    Note: Explicit normalization of $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is avoided to prevent training instability; the model learns unit-length normals via regularization [S1].

2.  **Identify Peak Blending Weight Sample:**
    Find the index $s$ of the sample with the highest blending weight along the original ray $\mathbf{r}$ [S1]. Calculate the position of this peak sample $\mathbf{p}_s$:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_s = \mathbf{o} + t_s \mathbf{d} $$
    where $t_s$ is the distance to the $s$-th sample [S1].

3.  **Derive Flipped Reflection Direction:**
    Calculate the flipped reflection direction $\mathbf{d}'$ using the original direction $\mathbf{d}$ and the estimated normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$:
    $$ \mathbf{d}' = 2(\mathbf{d} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})\hat{\mathbf{n}} - \mathbf{d} $$
    [S1]

4.  **Calculate Flipped Ray Origin:**
    Determine the origin $\mathbf{o}'$ for the flipped reflection ray such that the peak sample of the flipped ray coincides with $\mathbf{p}_s$:
    $$ \mathbf{o}' = \mathbf{p}_s - t_s \mathbf{d}' $$
    The flipped reflection ray is then defined as $\mathbf{r}'(t) = \mathbf{o}' + t \mathbf{d}'$ [S1].

5.  **Apply Masking Strategy:**
    Filter out ineffective flipped rays using a masking function $M(\mathbf{r}')$ based on the angle between the estimated normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ and the negative original direction $-\hat{\mathbf{d}}$ [S1]. The mask is 1 if the angle is within threshold $\tau$, and 0 otherwise:
    $$ M(\mathbf{r}') = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \arccos(-(\hat{\mathbf{d}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}})) < \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    Typical values for $\tau$ are $90^\circ$ for DTU and Realistic Synthetic 360°, and $30^\circ$ for LLFF [S1]. Only rays with $M(\mathbf{r}') = 1$ are used for regularization losses [S1].

6.  **Compute Losses:**
    Calculate the following loss terms for the batch of original rays $\mathcal{R}$ and valid flipped rays $\mathcal{R}'$:

    *   **MSE Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}$):** Standard pixel-wise reconstruction error between rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth $\mathbf{c}^{\text{GT}}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} || \hat{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{c}^{\text{GT}}(\mathbf{r}) ||_2^2 $$
        [S1]

    *   **Negative Log-Likelihood Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}'$):** NLL for original rays $\mathbf{r}$ and flipped rays $\mathbf{r}'$ respectively, modeling color distributions as mixtures [S1].

    *   **Uncertainty-aware Emptiness Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}'$):** Reduces floating artifacts by penalizing blending weights based on ray uncertainty $\rho$ [S1]. For a ray $\mathbf{r}$:
        $$ l_{\text{UE}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \log (1 + \rho \cdot \eta \cdot w_i) $$
        where $\rho = \frac {1}{3} \sum _{c}^{\{r,g,b\}} \sum _{i=1}^{M} \beta _i^c$ is the uncertainty derived from the scale parameters, and $\eta$ is a scaling factor [S1].

    *   **Orientation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Ori.}}$):** Penalizes backward-facing normal vectors using the underlying density gradient normal $\mathbf{n}_i$ (not the predicted normal) to ensure accurate surface normals:
        $$ l_{\text{Ori.}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \max (0, \mathbf{n}_{i} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}})^2 $$
        [S1]

    *   **Bottleneck Feature Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{BFC}}$):** Encourages consistency between bottleneck features $\mathbf{b}$ of original rays and $\mathbf{b}'$ of flipped rays using Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD), where $\psi$ is the softmax function:
        $$ l_{\text{BFC}}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{r}') = \text{JSD}(\psi (\mathbf {b}), \psi (\mathbf {b}')) $$
        [S1]

7.  **Minimize Total Loss:**
    Update model parameters $\Theta$ by minimizing the weighted sum of all losses:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_\text{Total} = \mathcal{L}_\text{MSE} + \lambda _{1}\mathcal{L}_\text{NLL} + \lambda _{2}\mathcal{L}_\text{NLL}' + \lambda _{3}\mathcal{L}_\text{UE} + \lambda _{4}\mathcal{L}_\text{UE}' + \lambda _{5}\mathcal{L}_\text{BFC} + \lambda _{6}\mathcal{L}_\text{Ori.} $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Masking Threshold $\tau$:** $90^\circ$ for Realistic Synthetic 360° and DTU; $30^\circ$ for LLFF [S1].
*   **Training Duration:** 500 pixel epochs [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 4,096 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with gradient clipping by value at 0.1 and norm at 0.1 [S1].
*   **Learning Rate Annealing:**
    *   Realistic Synthetic 360°: $[10^{-3}, 10^{-5}]$ [S1]
    *   DTU: $[2 \times 10^{-3}, 2 \times 10^{-5}]$ [S1]
    *   LLFF: $[2 \times 10^{-3}, 2 \times 10^{-5}]$ [S1]
*   **Warm-up Steps:** 512 (Real.Syn. 360°), 1024 (DTU), 512 (LLFF) [S1].
*   **Delay Multiplier:** $10^{-2}$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $10^{-3}$ (DTU), $10^{-3}$ (LLFF) [S1].
*   **Loss Balancing Weights ($\lambda$):**
    *   $\lambda_1$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}$): Annealed from $[4.0, 10^{-3}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[0.4, 10^{-4}]$ (DTU), $[0.04, 10^{-5}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_2$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}'$): Annealed from $[0.4, 10^{-4}]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[0.04, 10^{-5}]$ (DTU), $[0.004, 10^{-6}]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_3$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}$): Annealed from $[10^{-4}, 0.1]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[10^{-5}, 0.01]$ (DTU), $[10^{-4}, 0.1]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_4$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{UE}}'$): Annealed from $[10^{-5}, 0.01]$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $[10^{-6}, 0.001]$ (DTU), $[10^{-6}, 0.001]$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_5$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BFC}}$): $0.01$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $0.001$ (DTU), $0.00001$ (LLFF) [S1].
    *   $\lambda_6$ (for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Ori.}}$): $0.1$ (Real.Syn. 360°), $0.01$ (DTU), $0.001$ (LLFF) [S1].
    [S1]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Explicit Normalization:** Using explicitly normalized surface normals ($\hat{\mathbf{n}} / ||\hat{\mathbf{n}}||$) causes training instability and degenerate results (PSNR 16.72 vs 22.24) [S1]. The model relies on regularization to approximate unit vectors naturally [S1].
*   **Initial Normal Quality:** In the initial training stage, estimated normals are not unit vectors ($||\hat{\mathbf{n}}||^2$ far from 1) [S1]. The masking strategy and orientation loss help concentrate $||\hat{\mathbf{n}}||^2$ close to 1 over time [S1].
*   **Ineffective Rays:** Flipped rays that do not satisfy the masking condition (angle $\theta > \tau$) provide misleading training cues and must be masked out [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance on Realistic Synthetic 360°, DTU, and LLFF benchmarks, outperforming baselines by a large margin in 3/4-view settings [S1]. On the evaluation dataset, masking with $\tau=90^\circ$ achieved PSNR 19.55, SSIM 0.767, LPIPS 0.180, and Average Error 0.101, outperforming no masking ($\tau=180^\circ$) which had PSNR 18.76 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for few-shot settings (e.g., 3, 4, 6, 9 views) and scales by adjusting loss weights $\lambda$ to prevent over-regularization as the number of training views increases [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Does not require pre-training on large datasets; does not rely on heuristic factors for unseen view sampling; improves surface normal accuracy which aids depth estimation; reduces floating artifacts via uncertainty-aware regularization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on estimated surface normals derived from density gradients; initial estimates are not unit vectors [S1].
*   **When to use:** Sparse view novel view synthesis where accurate geometry and surface normals are beneficial [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Not given in the sources [S1].

## Background
FlipNeRF was proposed in 2023 by researchers supported by NRF, IITP, and Samsung Electronics [S1]. It is built upon MixNeRF, which leverages mixture density models to represent input rays [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of existing regularization methods that focus on depth estimation or require heuristic sampling factors, by instead focusing on accurate surface normal reconstruction through flipped reflection rays [S1]. It is evaluated on Realistic Synthetic 360°, DTU, and LLFF benchmarks [S1].
