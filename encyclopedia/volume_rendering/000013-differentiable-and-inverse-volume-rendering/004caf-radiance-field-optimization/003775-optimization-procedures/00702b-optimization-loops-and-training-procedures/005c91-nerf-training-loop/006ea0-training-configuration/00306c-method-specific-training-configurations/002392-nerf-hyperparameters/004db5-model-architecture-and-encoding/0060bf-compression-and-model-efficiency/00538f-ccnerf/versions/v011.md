# CCNeRF
Also called: Rank-residual Decomposition NeRF

## Overview
CCNeRF is a neural field representation that enables efficient compression and composition of 3D scenes by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition of the scene without using neural networks for rendering [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that rely on a shared MLP renderer (e.g., NSVF) or hybrid tensor-MLP approaches (e.g., TensoRF), CCNeRF models the radiance field *only* with tensor rank decomposition, preserving the low-rank approximation properties necessary for dynamic size adjustment and arbitrary composition [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 2D images with known camera poses $(o, d)$ and a target rank configuration (vector-based vs. matrix-based components).
*   **Output:** A compressed radiance field representation consisting of factor matrices/vectors, a core tensor (or weight matrix), and per-object transformation matrices. The rendering output is the pixel color $\hat{C}(r)$ for a ray $r$.

**Data Structures:**
The model structure consists of two main parts [S1]:
1.  **Rank Components:** A set of decomposed components that are either vector-based (CP decomposition) or matrix-based (triple plane variant). The ratio of vector- to matrix-based components is controllable.
    *   *Vector-based (CP):* Factor matrices $U^x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times R_{vec}}$, $U^y \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times R_{vec}}$, $U^z \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times R_{vec}}$.
    *   *Matrix-based (Triple Plane):* Factor matrices $U^{xy} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times R_{mat}}$, $U^{xz} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D \times R_{mat}}$, $U^{yz} \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times D \times R_{mat}}$.
2.  **Weight Matrix:** A matrix storing rank weights for different feature channels, denoted as $S$ (core tensor) or a weight matrix mapping the decomposed components to the final feature vector.

**Querying a 3D Coordinate:**
To query a 3D coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$:
1.  Project the coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ to the decomposed vectors or matrices.
2.  Perform weighted interpolation using the stored rank weights [S1].

**Composition:**
Different models are composed by concatenating their rank components along the rank dimension. A transformation matrix is recorded for each object to control its position and orientation [S1].

**Compression:**
Model size is dynamically adjusted by rank truncation. An empirical sort-and-truncate strategy is used to achieve near-optimal low-rank approximation at any targeted rank, allowing trade-off between model size and rendering quality without retraining [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Volume Rendering Equation:**
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_i T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
    where $T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_i)$, $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$, and $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$. Here $\sigma_i$ is density, $c_i$ is color, and $t_i$ is sample position along ray $r$ from origin $o$ with direction $d$ [S1].

*   **Tucker Decomposition:**
    For a 3D tensor $T \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}$:
    $$ T_{i,j,k} = \sum_{p=1}^P \sum_{q=1}^Q \sum_{r=1}^R S_{p,q,r} U^x_{i,p} U^y_{j,q} U^z_{k,r} $$
    where $S$ is the core tensor and $U^x, U^y, U^z$ are factor matrices [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Rank ($R$):** The number of components kept in the decomposition determines the model size and detail level. Lower ranks preserve primary information due to rank-residual learning [S1].
*   **Hybrid Ratio:** The ratio of vector-based (CP) to matrix-based (triple plane) components is controllable to trade off between model size and performance [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Truncation Optimality:** Standard truncated HOSVD does not hold the optimal property of truncated SVD, but yields a quasi-optimal solution sufficient for practical use [S1].
*   **MLP Dependency:** Unlike TensoRF, CCNeRF avoids MLPs in the rendering path to preserve low-rank approximation properties; introducing MLPs would prevent compression or composition [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression:** Model size can be dynamically adjusted by rank truncation without retraining, achieving near-optimal compression [S1].
*   **Composition:** Supports arbitrary composition of different models by concatenating rank components, with no compatibility constraints or retraining required [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves comparable rendering quality to state-of-the-art methods while enabling extra capabilities of compression and composition [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The specific computational complexity of the sort-and-truncate strategy and the exact trade-off curves for the hybrid ratio are not provided in the sources [S1].

## Background
CCNeRF was introduced by Jiaxiang Tang, Xiaokang Chen, Jingbo Wang, and Gang Zeng from Peking University and Chinese University of Hong Kong in 2022 [S1]. The paper addresses the difficulty of manipulating implicit NeRF representations compared to explicit mesh representations. While recent advances in NeRF manipulation are often restricted by shared renderer networks or suffer from large model sizes, CCNeRF circumvents these hurdles by learning a hybrid tensor rank decomposition without neural networks [S1]. It is motivated by the low-rank approximation property of the SVD algorithm and proposes a rank-residual learning strategy to encourage the preservation of primary information in lower ranks [S1].
