# Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, Time-conditioned Radiance Fields, 4D NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling extends the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) architecture to handle time-varying geometry and appearance, solving the limitation that standard NeRF assumes a static scene [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md]. Unlike sibling methods that may use explicit motion fields, particle systems, or forward warping, this family broadly encompasses approaches that condition the radiance field on a temporal coordinate $t$ [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md]. The primary distinction among children lies in how temporal information is integrated: direct input expansion [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md], semantic stratification [13848-gear-nerf.md], canonical field warping [15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md], hybrid static-dynamic decomposition [15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md], or Kalman-filtered deformation [15551-kfd-nerf.md].

## Shared principles
The standard NeRF architecture [Parent](11313-model-architecture-modifications.md) serves as the baseline. Modifications generally fall into **Input Expansion**, adding a temporal coordinate $t$ to the input concatenation [14428-sparse-view-and-few-shot-optimization.md].

The rendering equation is adapted to include time $t$ as a parameter for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md]:

$$
C(\mathbf{r}, t) = \int_{s_n}^{s_f} T(s, t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s), t) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(s), \mathbf{d}, t) ds
$$

where the transmittance $T(s, t)$ is defined as:

$$
T(s, t) = \exp\left(-\int_{s_n}^{s} \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s'), t) ds'\right)
$$

Here, $\mathbf{r}(s) = \mathbf{o} + s\mathbf{d}$ is the ray equation, $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin, and $s_n, s_f$ are near and far clipping planes [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md].

## Subtopics

### [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md)
A framework for free-viewpoint rendering and tracking of dynamic scenes that leverages semantic information to stratify the scene into motion-based 'gears', allowing for region-specific spatio-temporal sampling resolution. It solves the inefficiency of uniform 4D sampling by assigning higher resolution to regions with larger motion scales. Unlike standard NeRF variants that use a single unified volume, Gear-NeRF uses a series of 4D feature volumes, one per gear level, enabling both enhanced dynamic novel view synthesis and free-viewpoint object tracking.

### [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)
This method adapts the static NeRF architecture to handle dynamic scenes by introducing a temporal coordinate $t$ as an additional input to the implicit function. It solves the problem of representing time-varying geometry and appearance, which standard NeRF cannot capture due to its static assumption. Unlike sibling methods that may use explicit motion fields or particle systems, this approach directly extends the input domain of the MLP, serving as a baseline for time-aware rendering.

### [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md)
This method models a static scene at a canonical time step and forward warps the entire canonical field to other time steps. It addresses the many-to-one and one-to-many mapping difficulties inherent in dynamic scene reconstruction by employing differentiable forward warping with average splatting and an inpaint network. Unlike backward flow-based methods such as NDVG, this approach leverages the smooth trajectory field generated by forward warping to achieve superior performance, particularly on canonical frames.

### [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md)
This method models dynamic scenes from monocular video data by combining a static grid-based radiance field with a particle-based dynamic component. It solves the problem of reconstructing time-varying scenes without requiring multi-view stereo data or prior geometric knowledge of moving objects. Unlike ParticleNeRF, which encodes the entire scene with particles and requires multiple camera views per frame, this method applies appearance particles only to dynamic elements and uses a grid-based field for efficient querying via linear interpolation.

### [KFD-NeRF](15551-kfd-nerf.md)
KFD-NeRF models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) or [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), weighted by a learnable Kalman gain. This approach leverages the inductive smoothness bias of deformation fields over feature interpolation, allowing for faster convergence and higher quality reconstruction of non-rigid motions.

## Background
The concept of extending NeRF to dynamic scenes originated with the introduction of time as an input coordinate [14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md]. Subsequent developments introduced more complex mechanisms such as semantic stratification [13848-gear-nerf.md], canonical field warping [15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md], hybrid particle-grid representations [15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md], and Kalman-filtered deformation fields [15551-kfd-nerf.md]. These methods are used in applications requiring novel view synthesis of moving subjects, 4D reconstruction, and free-viewpoint video.
