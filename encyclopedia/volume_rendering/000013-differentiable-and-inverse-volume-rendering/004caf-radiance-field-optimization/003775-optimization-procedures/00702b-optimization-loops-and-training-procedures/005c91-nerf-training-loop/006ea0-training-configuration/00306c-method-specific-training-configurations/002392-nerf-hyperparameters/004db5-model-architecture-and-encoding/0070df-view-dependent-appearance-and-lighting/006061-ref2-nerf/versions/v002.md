# REF2-NeRF

Also called: Reflection and Refraction aware Neural Radiance Field

## Overview
REF2-NeRF is a NeRF-based modeling method for scenes containing transparent objects, specifically glass cases, that models refraction and reflection using elements dependent and independent of the viewer's perspective [S1]. It estimates surfaces where refraction occurs and separates direct and reflected light components [S1]. Unlike methods that assume straight light rays or only handle reflections, REF2-NeRF handles both refraction and reflection in scenes with multiple transparent objects [S1]. It differs from LB-NeRF by introducing a view-independent density field and a view-dependent offset field to estimate refraction surfaces and offsets simultaneously [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Multiple images $\{I_k\}$ ($k = 1, \dots, n$) taken from different viewpoints [S1]. Camera poses must be predetermined [S1].
*   **Output:** Rendered image $C(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$, decomposed into view-independent color $C_{vi}(\mathbf{r})$ and view-dependent color $C_{vd}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].

**Data Structures and Network Architecture**
The method uses four MLPs:
1.  **Glass Network ($\Theta_{gl}$):**
    *   Input: Positional encoded position $\Gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and view direction $\Gamma(\mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    *   Output: Glass density $\sigma_{gl}(\mathbf{x})$ and offset vector $\Delta\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
    *   Architecture: Input layer $[6 \times 256]$, Output layer $[256, 128]$ (implied by diagram description, though text specifies inputs/outputs) [S1].
2.  **Decomposition NeRF ($\Theta_{nf}$):**
    *   Input: Positional encoded adjusted position $\Gamma(\mathbf{x}')$ and view direction $\Gamma(\mathbf{d})$ [S1].
    *   Output: View-independent density $\sigma_{vi}$, view-independent color $\mathbf{c}_{vi} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, view-dependent density $\sigma_{vd}$, and view-dependent feature vector $\mathbf{f}_{vd} \in \mathbb{R}^\theta$ [S1].
    *   Architecture: Input layer $[8 \times 256]$, Output layer $[256, 128]$ [S1].
3.  **Decoder MLP ($\Theta_{dc}$):**
    *   Input: Rendered feature vector $\mathbf{F}_{vd}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].
    *   Output: Reflection color $\mathbf{C}_{vd}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].
4.  **Gate MLP ($\Theta_{gt}$):**
    *   Input: Rendered feature vector $\mathbf{F}_{vd}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].
    *   Output: Blending parameter $\alpha(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].

**Parameters**
*   Feature vector dimension $\theta = 64$ [S1].
*   Regularization weight $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   Sampling points: 128 coarse, 64 fine (hierarchical), 32 glass-density, 32 view-independent density. Total fine model points: 192 [S1].

**Algorithm**

1.  **Positional Encoding:**
    Compute $\Gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and $\Gamma(\mathbf{d})$ using standard positional encoding [S1].

2.  **Glass Network Forward Pass:**
    For a point $\mathbf{x}_i$ sampled along ray $\mathbf{r}$, compute glass density $\sigma_{gl,i}$ and offset $\Delta\mathbf{x}_i$:
    $$ \sigma_{gl,i}, \Delta\mathbf{x}_i = \Theta_{gl}(\Gamma(\mathbf{x}_i), \Gamma(\mathbf{d}_i)) $$
    [S1]

3.  **Refraction Weight Calculation:**
    Calculate the transmittance $T_i$ and weight $w_i$ for each sampling point $i$ using glass density $\sigma_{gl,i}$ and distance $\delta_{gl,i}$ between adjacent points:
    $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_{gl,j} \delta_{gl,j}\right) $$
    $$ w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_{gl,i} \delta_{gl,i})) $$
    [S1]

4.  **Position Adjustment:**
    Compute the adjusted position $\mathbf{x}'_i$ by cumulatively adding the weighted offset vectors along the ray:
    $$ \mathbf{x}'_i = \mathbf{x}_i + \sum_{j=1}^{i} w_j \Delta\mathbf{x}_j $$
    [S1] (Note: The source states "adding the weighted offset cumulatively along the ray" and shows $x' = x + w\Delta x$ in Fig 5, implying cumulative sum for the shift applied to the current point's base position or the final position. The formula $x' = x + w\Delta x$ in Fig 5 likely represents the incremental shift or the final shift for that segment. Given the volume rendering context, the position queried for the NeRF is the shifted position. We define $\mathbf{x}'_i$ as the position used for the NeRF query.)

5.  **Decomposition NeRF Query:**
    For each adjusted point $\mathbf{x}'_i$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}_i$, query the Decomposition NeRF:
    $$ \sigma_{vi,i}, \mathbf{c}_{vi,i}, \sigma_{vd,i}, \mathbf{f}_{vd,i} = \Theta_{nf}(\Gamma(\mathbf{x}'_i), \Gamma(\mathbf{d}_i)) $$
    [S1]

6.  **View-Independent Rendering:**
    Perform volume rendering on the adjusted positions using view-independent density $\sigma_{vi}$ and color $\mathbf{c}_{vi}$:
    $$ C_{vi}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_{vi,i} (1 - \exp(-\sigma_{vi,i} \delta_{vi,i})) \mathbf{c}_{vi,i} $$
    where $T_{vi,i}$ is calculated using $\sigma_{vi}$ similarly to Eq. 4 (using $\sigma_{vi}$ instead of $\sigma_{gl}$):
    $$ T_{vi,i} = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_{vi,j} \delta_{vi,j}\right) $$
    [S1]

7.  **View-Dependent Feature Rendering:**
    Perform volume rendering on adjusted positions $\mathbf{x}'$ and view directions $\mathbf{d}$ using view-dependent density $\sigma_{vd}$ and feature vector $\mathbf{f}_{vd}$:
    $$ \mathbf{F}_{vd}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_{i} (1 - \exp(-\sigma_{vd,i} \delta_{vd,i})) \mathbf{f}_{vd,i} $$
    where $T_i$ is the transmittance calculated using $\sigma_{vd}$:
    $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_{vd,j} \delta_{vd,j}\right) $$
    [S1]

8.  **View-Dependent Color and Blending:**
    Decode the rendered feature vector $\mathbf{F}_{vd}$ into reflection color $\mathbf{C}_{vd}$ and determine blending parameter $\alpha$:
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{vd}(\mathbf{r}) = \Theta_{dc}(\mathbf{F}_{vd}(\mathbf{r})) $$
    $$ \alpha(\mathbf{r}) = \Theta_{gt}(\mathbf{F}_{vd}(\mathbf{r})) $$
    [S1]

9.  **Final Image Composition:**
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = C_{vi}(\mathbf{r}) + \alpha(\mathbf{r}) \mathbf{C}_{vd}(\mathbf{r}) $$
    [S1]

**Training**
*   **Loss Function:**
    $$ L = L_{render} + \epsilon L_{offset} $$
    $$ L_{render} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in R} \|\bar{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r})\|^2 $$
    $$ L_{offset} = \sum_{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}} \|\Delta\mathbf{x}\|^2 $$
    where $\bar{C}(\mathbf{r})$ is the input image color, $R$ is a batch of sampled rays, and $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   **Sampling Strategy:**
    1.  Coarse sampling: 128 points at uniform intervals [S1].
    2.  Fine sampling: 64 points hierarchically sampled based on coarse density [S1].
    3.  Additional points: 32 points sampled using glass density, 32 points sampled using view-independent density [S1].
    4.  Total points for fine model: 192 [S1].
*   **Implementation Details:**
    *   Rays sampled from 1,024 pixels per iteration [S1].
    *   200,000 training iterations [S1].
    *   Hardware: Nvidia RTX 4080 GPU [S1].
    *   Time: ~10 hours per scene [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given in terms of asymptotic complexity. Training takes ~10 hours per scene on an Nvidia RTX 4080 GPU for 200,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given. The method uses four MLPs with specified layer sizes (e.g., $[6 \times 256]$, $[256, 128]$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles both refraction and reflection in scenes with multiple transparent objects (e.g., glass cases) [S1]. Separates view-independent and view-dependent components [S1]. Estimates refraction surfaces and offsets simultaneously [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires predetermined camera poses, which are difficult to acquire in scenes with glass objects [S1]. Relies on a robotic arm for pose acquisition in experiments [S1].
*   **When to use:** Scenes containing glass objects with multiple reflections and refractions [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes without transparent objects or where camera poses are unknown/unavailable [S1].

## Background
REF2-NeRF was proposed in 2024 by authors publishing in the IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS) [S1]. It addresses the challenge of modeling scenes with multiple glass objects, such as objects in a glass showcase, where traditional NeRF methods fail due to multiple reflection and refraction effects [S1]. It builds on LB-NeRF, which simplifies refraction as an offset, but improves upon it by introducing view-independent density and view-dependent offset fields [S1]. It also differs from MS-NeRF, which uses multiple explicit spaces for mirrors, by using a single view-dependent feature field to handle reciprocal reflections from multiple glass plates [S1].
