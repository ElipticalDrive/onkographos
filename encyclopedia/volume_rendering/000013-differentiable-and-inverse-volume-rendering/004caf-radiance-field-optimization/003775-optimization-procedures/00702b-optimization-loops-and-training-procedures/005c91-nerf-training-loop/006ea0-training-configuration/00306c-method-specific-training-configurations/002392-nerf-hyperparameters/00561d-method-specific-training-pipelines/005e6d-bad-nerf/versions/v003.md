# BAD-NeRF

Also called: Bundle Adjusted Deblur Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
BAD-NeRF reconstructs 3D scenes and recovers camera poses from motion-blurred images by explicitly modeling the physical image formation process during the camera's exposure time [S2]. Unlike standard NeRF methods that assume sharp images or fixed camera poses, BAD-NeRF jointly optimizes the Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) parameters and the continuous camera motion trajectory (defined by start and end poses) for each image [S2]. It synthesizes blurred images by rendering a sequence of sharp intermediate views along the interpolated trajectory and averaging them, allowing the network to learn from degraded inputs while recovering accurate pose information [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $M$ motion-blurred images $\{B_m\}_{m=1}^M$, their initial camera poses (typically from COLMAP or ground truth), camera intrinsic parameters $K$, and exposure time $\tau$ [S2].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model $F_\theta$ representing the 3D scene, and refined camera motion trajectories for each image, parameterized by start pose $T_{start}$ and end pose $T_{end}$ [S2].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF ($F_\theta$):** Follows the standard NeRF architecture [S2]. It consists of two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) that take a 3D position $X_w$ and viewing direction as input and output volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $c$ [S2].
*   **Camera Pose Parameterization:** For each image $m$, the motion trajectory is defined by two poses in SE(3): $T_{start} \in SE(3)$ at the beginning of exposure and $T_{end} \in SE(3)$ at the end [S2]. These are represented as 6D vectors corresponding to their Lie algebras [S2].
*   **Ray Sampling:** For each pixel, $n_{pts}$ points are sampled along the ray [S2].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Casting:** For a pixel location $x$ in image $m$, compute the ray direction $d_c$ in the camera coordinate frame using the inverse intrinsic matrix $K^{-1}$ [S2]:
    $$ d_c = K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
    The 3D point $X_w$ in world coordinates is then computed as $X_w = T_c^w \cdot \lambda d_c$, where $\lambda$ is depth and $T_c^w$ is the camera-to-world pose [S2].

2.  **Trajectory Interpolation:** Calculate the virtual camera pose $T_t$ at any time $t \in [0, \tau]$ during the exposure interval using linear interpolation in the Lie algebra of SE(3) [S2]:
    $$ T_t = T_{start} \cdot \exp\left( \frac{t}{\tau} \cdot \log(T_{start}^{-1} \cdot T_{end}) \right) $$
    where $\tau$ is the exposure time [S2].

3.  **Discretization:** Discretize the exposure time into $n$ samples [S2]. The $i$-th virtual pose $T_i$ (for $i = 0, \dots, n-1$) corresponds to time $t_i = \frac{i}{n-1}\tau$ [S2].

4.  **Virtual Sharp Image Rendering:** For each virtual pose $T_i$, render a sharp image $I_i(x)$ using the NeRF $F_\theta$ [S2].
    *   Transform the 3D point $X_w$ from world to camera frame using $T_i$ [S2].
    *   Query $F_\theta$ for color $c_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S2].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1} \sigma_k \delta_k)$ and accumulated color $I_i(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n_{pts}} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i$ [S2].

5.  **Blur Synthesis:** Synthesize the blurred image $B(x)$ by averaging the $n$ virtual sharp images [S2]:
    $$ B(x) \approx \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} I_i(x) $$
    where $n$ is the number of interpolated virtual poses [S2].

6.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the photometric loss $L$ between the synthesized blurred image $B(x)$ and the observed blurred image $B_{obs}(x)$ [S2].

7.  **Gradient Computation and Update:** Optimize NeRF parameters $\theta$, start poses $T_{start}$, and end poses $T_{end}$ by minimizing the loss [S2]. The gradients are computed via the chain rule [S2]:
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta} = \sum_{k=0}^{P-1} \frac{\partial L}{\partial B_k(x)} \cdot \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{\partial B_k(x)}{\partial I_i(x)} \cdot \frac{\partial I_i(x)}{\partial \theta} $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial T_{start}} = \sum_{k=0}^{P-1} \frac{\partial L}{\partial B_k(x)} \cdot \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{\partial B_k(x)}{\partial I_i(x)} \cdot \frac{\partial I_i(x)}{\partial T_{start}} $$
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial T_{end}} = \sum_{k=0}^{P-1} \frac{\partial L}{\partial B_k(x)} \cdot \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{\partial B_k(x)}{\partial I_i(x)} \cdot \frac{\partial I_i(x)}{\partial T_{end}} $$
    where $P$ is the number of pixels (or batch size elements) [S2]. The Jacobians $\frac{\partial I_i(x)}{\partial T_{start}}$ and $\frac{\partial I_i(x)}{\partial T_{end}}$ are derived from the differentiability of the SE(3) interpolation and the NeRF rendering [S2].

8.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using the Adam optimizer [S2].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

def bad_nerf_step(blurry_images, T_start_params, T_end_params, F_theta, K_inv, tau, n_samples, n_pts):
    """
    One training step for BAD-NeRF.
    Inputs:
        blurry_images: Tensor of shape (M, H, W, 3)
        T_start_params: Tensor of shape (M, 6), Lie algebra vectors for start poses
        T_end_params: Tensor of shape (M, 6), Lie algebra vectors for end poses
        F_theta: NeRF MLP module
        K_inv: 3x3 inverse intrinsic matrix
        tau: Exposure time scalar
        n_samples: Number of virtual poses to average (e.g., 7)
        n_pts: Number of points sampled per ray (e.g., 128)
    """
    T_start_params.grad.zero_()
    T_end_params.grad.zero_()
    F_theta.zero_grad()

    M = blurry_images.shape[0]
    total_loss = 0.0

    for m in range(M):
        B_obs = blurry_images[m]
        xi_start = T_start_params[m]
        xi_end = T_end_params[m]

        # Convert Lie algebra vectors to SE(3) matrices using standard exponential map
        T_start_mat = se3_exp(xi_start)
        T_end_mat = se3_exp(xi_end)

        blurred_pred_list = []
        for i in range(n_samples):
            # Interpolate time
            t = (i / (n_samples - 1)) * tau if n_samples > 1 else 0.0

            # SE(3) linear interpolation: T_t = T_start * exp( (t/tau) * log(T_start^-1 * T_end) )
            T_rel = torch.inverse(T_start_mat) @ T_end_mat
            log_T_rel = se3_log(T_rel)
            T_t_mat = T_start_mat @ se3_exp((t / tau) * log_T_rel)

            # Generate ray origins and directions in world space
            # d_c = K_inv @ [x, y, 1]^T, X_w = T_t_mat @ [d_c * depth, 1]^T
            ray_origins, ray_directions = generate_rays(K_inv, T_t_mat)

            # Sample points along rays
            t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, n_pts)
            X_w = ray_origins + t_vals.unsqueeze(-1) * ray_directions

            # Query NeRF for color c and density sigma
            c, sigma = F_theta(X_w, ray_directions)

            # Volume rendering equation
            delta = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
            delta = torch.cat([delta, delta[-1:]], dim=-1)
            transmittance = torch.exp(-torch.cumsum(sigma * delta, dim=-2))
            weights = (1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)) * transmittance
            I_sharp = torch.sum(weights * c, dim=-2)
            blurred_pred_list.append(I_sharp)

        # Blur synthesis: average virtual sharp images
        B_pred = torch.stack(blurred_pred_list).mean(dim=0)

        # Photometric loss
        loss = torch.mean((B_pred - B_obs) ** 2)
        total_loss += loss
        loss.backward(retain_graph=True)

    total_loss /= M
    total_loss.backward()
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $n$: Number of interpolated virtual poses/samples. Typical value: 7 [S2].
*   $\tau$: Exposure time of the captured image [S2].
*   $T_{start}, T_{end}$: Start and end poses, parameterized as 6D vectors (Lie algebra of SE(3)) [S2].
*   Points sampled per ray: 128 [S2].
*   Learning rate for NeRF optimizer: Exponentially decays from $5 \times 10^{-4}$ to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S2].
*   Learning rate for pose optimizer: Exponentially decays from $1 \times 10^{-3}$ to $1 \times 10^{-5}$ [S2].
*   Optimizer: Adam [S2].
*   Training iterations: 200K [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   NeRF assumes infinitesimal exposure time (sharp images), which is violated by motion blur [S2].
*   Accurate camera poses are difficult to obtain from blurred images because they encode motion trajectory information [S2].
*   Directly optimizing $N$ individual camera poses loses ordering information and performs worse than trajectory-based methods (linear or spline) [S2].
*   Linear interpolation is chosen for simplicity and performs comparably to cubic B-Spline for short exposure times, but may be less accurate for complex motions [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Vanilla RS-NeRF](16883-vanilla-rs-nerf.md)
A framework that reconstructs a NeRF from rolling shutter (RS) images by explicitly modeling the camera's motion trajectory during exposure and jointly optimizing NeRF parameters and camera extrinsics for each image row.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Higher than standard NeRF due to the need to render $n$ virtual sharp images per pixel per image and average them [S2]. The complexity scales linearly with the number of samples $n$ [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Evaluated on synthetic datasets from Deblur-NeRF (Cozy2room, Factory, Pool, Tanabata, Trolley) and MBA-VO (ArchViz-low, ArchViz-high) [S2]. Achieves superior performance compared to prior works like BARF and DeblurNeRF in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, especially under severe motion blur [S2].
*   **Hardware:** Tested on NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Physically grounded formulation that recovers sharper results and improves pose accuracy under severe motion blur [S1]. Robust to inaccurate initialized camera poses [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Higher computational complexity compared to methods that fix poses or use compact blur approximations [S1]. Linear interpolation may not capture complex camera motions as well as splines [S2].

## Background
BAD-NeRF was published in November 2022 [S1]. It addresses the challenge of training NeRFs from motion-blurred images, which violate the standard assumption of sharp images and accurate camera poses [S2]. The method builds on the idea of integrating the physical image formation process of motion blur into the NeRF training pipeline [S2]. It differs from BARF and Jeong et al. by optimizing the camera motion trajectory within the exposure time rather than at a single timestamp [S2]. It also differs from DeblurNeRF by jointly estimating trajectories with NeRF parameters instead of relying on fixed poses [S2].
