# Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, 4D NeRF, Temporal NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic Scene and Temporal Modeling extends the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representation to handle time-varying geometry and appearance, solving the problem of novel view synthesis for non-rigid or moving scenes [S1]. Unlike static methods that assume a fixed scene, these approaches introduce a temporal dimension to the input space or model explicit motion fields, allowing the network to condition density and color predictions on specific timestamps [S1]. This family differs from its siblings by explicitly addressing the many-to-one and one-to-many mapping difficulties inherent in dynamic scenes, often through deformation fields, particle systems, or temporal feature interpolation, rather than relying solely on the network's capacity to interpolate scene states as seen in the baseline [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md).

## Shared principles
The core task of a dynamic NeRF network architecture is to learn a continuous volumetric scene representation $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, t) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the spatial position, $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the viewing direction, $t \in \mathbb{R}$ is the time parameter, $\sigma$ is the volume density, and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the view-dependent color [G]. The rendering process integrates these outputs along rays using standard volume rendering equations [G].

To model dynamics, methods generally employ one of the following mechanisms:
1.  **Direct Time Conditioning:** The time parameter $t$ is encoded (e.g., via positional encoding) and concatenated with spatial coordinates or features before being passed to the MLP [S1].
2.  **Deformation Fields:** A deformation network predicts a displacement vector $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ for each point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t$, warping real-space points to a canonical space where a static radiance field is defined [S1].
3.  **Particle/Feature Interpolation:** Scene dynamics are represented using particles or sparse latent codes, with features interpolated over time or space using attention or kernel functions [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md)
Gear-NeRF is a method for free-viewpoint rendering and tracking of dynamic scenes that leverages semantic information from image segmentation models to stratify the scene into motion-based 'gears', allowing for region-specific spatio-temporal sampling resolution. It differs from unified 4D volume approaches by using a series of 4D feature volumes, one per gear level, where higher gears correspond to regions with larger motion scales and thus receive higher-resolution sampling. This motion-aware stratification improves photo-realistic dynamic novel view synthesis while enabling free-viewpoint object tracking as a by-product.

### [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md)
This method adapts the static Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) architecture for dynamic scenes by introducing a temporal dimension to the input space. It solves the problem of representing time-varying geometry and appearance by allowing the network to condition its density and color predictions on a specific timestamp. Unlike advanced dynamic NeRF variants that model explicit motion fields or scene graphs, this approach relies solely on the network's capacity to interpolate or extrapolate scene states through the time parameter, serving as a baseline for temporal modeling.

### [Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis](15541-forward-warping-for-novel-view-synthesis.md)
Forward Warping for Novel View Synthesis is a method for rendering dynamic scenes by modeling a static scene at a canonical time step and forward warping the entire canonical field to other time steps. It addresses the many-to-one and one-to-many mapping difficulties inherent in dynamic scenes using differentiable forward warping with average splatting and an inpaint network. Unlike backward flow methods such as NDVG, this approach achieves superior performance, particularly for canonical frames, by explicitly reconstructing the radiance field at target times through warping.

### [Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field](15550-dynamic-appearance-particle-neural-radiance-field.md)
Dynamic Appearance Particle Neural Radiance Field is a method for novel view synthesis from monocular video that models scene dynamics using a particle-based representation. It solves the problem of reconstructing dynamic scenes without requiring multi-view data or prior geometric knowledge of moving objects. It differs from ParticleNeRF by applying appearance particles only to dynamic elements rather than the entire scene, and by using a grid-based field with linear interpolation for efficient querying instead of radius nearest neighbor searching.

### [KFD-NeRF](15551-kfd-nerf.md)
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly separating motion estimation into an observer branch (shallow MLP) and a predictor branch (linear motion model), fusing them via a learnable Kalman gain, whereas other methods typically rely on direct time-conditioned feature interpolation or rigid/semi-rigid deformation priors.

### [SLS4D](16576-sls4d.md)
SLS4D is a method for 4D novel view synthesis that represents dynamic scenes using a sparse, compact, and informative latent feature space, significantly reducing the number of network parameters compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [TiNeuVox](18515-st-nerf.md) by abandoning dense voxel grids or direct coordinate-based feature lookups in favor of a finite set of globally learnable latent codes interpolated via attention, thereby decoupling spatial and temporal dynamics more efficiently.

### [ST-NeRF](18515-st-nerf.md)
ST-NeRF is a layered neural radiance field representation designed for editable free-viewpoint videos of dynamic scenes. It solves the problem of disentangling spatial and temporal variations in moving objects by assigning each independently moving or articulated entity to a separate layer. Unlike monolithic dynamic NeRFs, ST-NeRF explicitly models space-time coherence through a deformation module and a canonical space radiance module, enabling direct spatial and temporal editing of individual entities.

#### ER-NeRF
ER-NeRF is a Conditional NeRF framework for effective, high-accuracy dynamic portrait creation, producing dynamic portrait videos synchronized with audio [S1]. It utilizes spatial regions’ distinct contributions to steer dynamic head modeling and develops a concise and articulate Tri-Plane Hash Representation [S1]. It introduces rapid and straightforward Adaptive Pose Encoding to enhance the separation of the head and torso [S1]. ER-NeRF attains instantaneous rendering and rapid convergence using a compact model size [S1]. However, additional fine-tuning might be necessary to sustain superior rendering outcomes for poses that exceed the scope of the training dataset [S1].

#### NeRF-MS
NeRF-MS is a method that diminishes vagueness in visual alterations by implementing a ternary loss to standardize the spread of visual codes across each image [S1]. It suggests a temporary decomposition unit to more effectively distinguish still scenes from dynamic objects [S1]. Trials using both real-life outdoor and artificial datasets reveal that NeRF-MS attains cutting-edge results in multi-sequence scenarios, markedly enhancing rendering efficiency through the reconstruction of 3D-consistent visuals and minimizing ghost effects [S1]. The technique might encounter difficulties in managing significant sequence discrepancies, like severe alterations in the visual aspect of scenes caused by the activation of lights [S1].

#### DCT-NeRF
DCT-NeRF is a method that learns smooth and stable trajectories for each point in space in an input video sequence using Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) parameterization, allowing for high-quality reconstruction and consistent motion extrapolation beyond the last available frame [S2, unofficial]. It parameterizes trajectories with the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to model motion [S2, unofficial]. The method handles temporal occlusions and reflects changes in reflectance over time [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of dynamic NeRFs emerged as an extension of the original NeRF architecture [S1]. Early approaches focused on simple time conditioning, while later methods introduced more complex deformation fields, particle systems, and semantic stratification to handle complex dynamics [S1]. The field has seen significant development with the introduction of methods like Gear-NeRF, which leverages semantic segmentation, and KFD-NeRF, which applies Kalman filtering for motion estimation [S1]. These methods are primarily used in applications requiring high-fidelity 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis of dynamic scenes, such as free-viewpoint video, augmented reality, and digital human creation [S1].
