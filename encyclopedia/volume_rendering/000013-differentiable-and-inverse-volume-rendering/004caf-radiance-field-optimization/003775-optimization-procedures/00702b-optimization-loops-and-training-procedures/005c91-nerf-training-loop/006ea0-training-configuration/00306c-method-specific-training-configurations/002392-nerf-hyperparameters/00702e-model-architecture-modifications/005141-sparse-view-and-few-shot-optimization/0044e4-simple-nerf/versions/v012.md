# Simple-NeRF

Also called: SimpleNeRF

## Overview
Simple-NeRF is a regularization method for sparse-input Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that employs two simpler augmented NeRF models to provide depth supervision to the main NeRF, mitigating overfitting and implausible geometries caused by high capacity and sparse views [S1, S2]. It sets itself apart from prior works by obtaining depth supervision through augmented models trained in-situ without requiring pre-training on large datasets or external depth models [S1, S2]. Unlike RegNeRF, which uses depth smoothness priors and can fuse objects at different depths, Simple-NeRF mitigates both floaters and incorrect depth issues by learning in-situ depth priors [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of $N$ input images (typically 2–4 views) and their corresponding camera poses. Optional sparse depth maps from Structure from Motion (SfM) [S2].
*   **Outputs:** A trained main NeRF model used for novel view synthesis. Augmented models are discarded after training [S1, S2].

**Data Structures:**
The architecture consists of four NeRF models trained in tandem:
1.  **Main Model:** Standard NeRF with coarse MLP $N_1$ and fine MLP $N_2$ [S1, S2].
2.  **Smoothing Augmentation (Points Augmentation):** An augmented NeRF with reduced positional encoding capacity [S1, S2].
3.  **Lambertian Augmentation (Views Augmentation):** An augmented NeRF with disabled view-dependent radiance [S1, S2].

**NeRF Preliminaries:**
The NeRF predicts view-independent volume density $\sigma_i$ and view-dependent color $c_i$ for a point $p_i$ and viewing direction $v$ using two MLPs [S1, S2]:
$$
\sigma_i, h_i = N_1(\gamma(p_i, 0, l_p))
$$
$$
c_i = N_2(h_i, \gamma(v, 0, l_v))
$$
where $h_i$ is a latent feature, $\gamma(x, d_1, d_2)$ is the positional encoding from frequency $d_1$ to $d_2$, and $l_p, l_v$ are the highest frequencies for position and view direction respectively. When $d_1=0$, $x$ is concatenated to the positional encoding [S2].

**Augmented Model Designs:**
1.  **Smoothing Augmentation (Points Augmentation):** Reduces the degree of positional encoding to prevent undesired depth discontinuities and floaters [S2]. The coarse MLP $N_{1s}$ receives only low-frequency positional encodings $\gamma(p_i, 0, l_{ps})$. The remaining high-frequency components $\gamma(p_i, l_{ps}, l_p)$ are concatenated to the input of the fine MLP $N_{2s}$ along with latent features $h_i$ and view direction $v$ [S2]:
    $$
    c_i = N_{2s}(h_i, \gamma(p_i, l_{ps}, l_p), \gamma(v, 0, l_v))
    $$
    This biases the model against shape-radiance ambiguity in Lambertian regions [S1, S2].
2.  **Lambertian Augmentation (Views Augmentation):** Disables view-dependent radiance to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity and prevent duplication artifacts [S2]. The coarse MLP $N_{1l}$ predicts both density and color based on position alone [S1]:
    $$
    \tilde{\sigma}_i, \tilde{c}_i = N_{1l}(\gamma(p_i, 0, l_p))
    $$
    The fine MLP $N_{2l}$ is similarly modified to output color based on position [S1].

**Mask Computation:**
To gauge the reliability of depth estimates, the method reprojects pixels using estimated depths onto the nearest training view and compares them with the training image [S1, S2].
For a pixel $q$, reproject a $k \times k$ patch around $q$ to the nearest training view using depths $z_c$ (main coarse) and $z_a$ (augmented coarse) [S1, S2]. Compute Mean Squared Error (MSE) intensities $e_c$ and $e_a$ between the reprojected patches and the training image [S1, S2]. Define a threshold $e_\tau$ [S1].

Compute the ternary mask $m_a$ for the augmented model:
$$
m_a = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } (e_a \le e_c) \text{ and } (e_a \le e_\tau) \\
-1 & \text{if } (e_c < e_a) \text{ and } (e_c \le e_\tau) \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
Similarly, compute mask $m_m$ for the main model [S2].
*   $m=1$: Augmented depth is more reliable.
*   $m=-1$: Main depth is more reliable.
*   $m=0$: Both depths are unreliable (e.g., specular regions) [S1, S2].

**Loss Functions:**
1.  **Depth Supervision Loss ($L_{aug}$):** Imposes depth supervision between the main coarse depth $z_m$ and augmented coarse depth $z_a$ using stop-gradient ($\hat{\nabla}$) on the target depth [S1, S2]:
    $$
    L_{aug} = \mathbb{1}\{m_a=1\} \odot \|z_m - \hat{\nabla}(z_a)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}\{m_m=1\} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_m) - z_a\|^2
    $$
    Separate losses $L_s$ (smoothing) and $L_l$ (Lambertian) are computed and summed [S2].
2.  **Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss ($L_{cfc}$):** Ensures hierarchical sampling consistency between coarse depth $z_c$ and fine depth $z_f$ of the main model [S1, S2]:
    $$
    L_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}\{m_f=1\} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}\{m_c=1\} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2
    $$
    Masks $m_f$ and $m_c$ are computed similarly using fine depths [S2].
3.  **Photometric Loss:** Standard volume rendering photometric loss between rendered color and training image [S1, S2].

**Training Procedure:**
1.  Initialize Main Model ($N_1, N_2$) and Augmented Models ($N_{1s}, N_{2s}, N_{1l}, N_{2l}$) [S2].
2.  For each training step:
    a. Sample rays and points for coarse and fine sampling [S1, S2].
    b. Forward pass through Main, Smoothing, and Lambertian models to obtain densities, colors, and depths [S2].
    c. Compute reprojection MSEs $e_c, e_a$ for coarse depths [S1, S2].
    d. Compute masks $m_a, m_m, m_f, m_c$ [S1, S2].
    e. Compute losses $L_{aug}$ (sum of $L_s, L_l$) and $L_{cfc}$ [S2].
    f. Compute total loss: $L_{total} = L_{color} + \lambda_1 L_{aug} + \lambda_2 L_{cfc}$ [S1, S2].
    g. Backpropagate and update all model parameters [S2].
3.  Inference uses only the Main Model [S1, S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $e_\tau$: Threshold for reprojection MSE. Value: Not explicitly given in sources [S1].
*   $l_{ps}$: Lowest frequency for smoothing augmentation positional encoding. Value: Not explicitly given [S2].
*   $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$: Weights for depth supervision and consistency losses. Value: Not explicitly given [S1, S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Specular Regions:** Reprojected patch intensities may not match, causing masks to be zero. This results in no supervision for those pixels, preventing incorrect depth estimates but reducing the number of supervised pixels [S1, S2].
*   **Single-View Visibility:** Transparent floaters persist in regions visible in only one training view because reliable depth supervision (requiring reprojection to a *different* view) is not available there [S2].
*   **Poor Base Performance:** The method is limited to cases where the base model achieves reasonable performance but suffers from distortions; it may not help if the base model performance is very poor [S2].
*   **Depth Variance:** The depth supervision loss is imposed only on the mean depth, not on the variance [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Simple-NeRF queries coarse MLPs 192 times and fine MLPs 192 times (384 total) per pixel, whereas standard NeRF queries coarse MLPs 64 times and fine MLPs 192 times (256 total) [S2]. Training takes approximately 1.5 times more time than standard NeRF [S2]. Disabling $L_{cfc}$ increases training time by almost 2x due to the inclusion of augmentations for the fine NeRF [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Training augmented models adds compute and memory overhead during training [S2].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **LLFF (2 views):** SSIM 0.6501, PSNR 19.57, LPIPS 0.2688 [S1].
    *   **LLFF (4 views):** SSIM 0.7016, PSNR 21.99, LPIPS 0.2633 [S1].
    *   **RealEstate-10K (2 views):** MAE 0.3269, SROCC 0.9215 [S2].
    *   **NeRF-LLFF (2 views):** MAE 0.1420, SROCC 0.8480 [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates floaters and shape-radiance ambiguity without pre-training or external depth models [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Does not handle transparent floaters in single-view regions well; requires base model to have reasonable initial performance [S2].

## Background
Simple-NeRF was introduced by Nagabhushan Somraj et al. in 2023 [S1, S2]. It is part of the "Simple Radiance Fields" (Simple-RF) family, which applies similar regularization principles to other radiance field representations like TensoRF and ZipNeRF [S2]. The method builds on the DS-NeRF baseline architecture [S1, S2]. It addresses the limitations of generalized sparse NeRFs (which require large pre-training datasets) and prior regularization methods like RegNeRF (which can fuse objects) and FreeNeRF (which anneals frequencies) [S1, S2]. The approach is conceptually closest to the Dual-Student architecture, which trains an identical model in tandem, but uses augmented models with complementary abilities [S1, S2].
