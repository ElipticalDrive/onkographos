# SDF-based Neural Volume Rendering with PBR

Also called: Neural volume rendering based on PBR and neural implicit surfaces, SDF-NeRF with PBR

## Overview
This method integrates Signed Distance Function (SDF) representations for geometry with Physically Based Rendering (PBR) material models to achieve smoother geometric structures and accurate specular reflections, overcoming the coarse detail and foggy artifacts typical of standard NeRF [S1]. Unlike Ref-NeRF, which replaces view-dependent radiance with reflected radiance interpolation, or NeRFReN, which uses an additional MLP for speculars, this approach uses SDF for geometry and PBR for materials to provide interpretable parameters and smoother normals [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Camera poses, pixel information (images), and a set of rays. Each ray is defined by origin $o$ and direction $d$, parameterized as $x = o + td$, where $t$ is incrementally adjusted to sample points along the ray path [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Final pixel color $C(r)$ [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Spatial MLP:** An $N$-layer multilayer perceptron that takes positional encoded coordinates as input. It outputs:
    *   Signed Distance Function value $f(x)$ [S1].
    *   Volume density parameter $\alpha$ (learnable) [S1].
    *   Diffuse color $C_d$ [S1].
    *   *Note:* The source states the MLP outputs the normal vector and volume density $\tau$ derived from the SDF, but the normal is computed via gradient, and density via the CDF formula. The MLP explicitly outputs $f(x)$ and $C_d$ [S1].
*   **BRDF LUT:** A precomputed lookup table indexed by $\cos\theta$ and roughness $\rho$, containing two channels: `brdfLUT.r` and `brdfLUT.g` [S1].
*   **Prefiltered Texture:** A texture or function $f_{prefilteredColor}$ dependent on roughness $\rho$, position $p$, and incident direction $\omega_i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Ray Sampling:** For each pixel, generate a ray $x = o + td$. Sample $N$ points along the ray by incrementing $t$ [S1].
2.  **Positional Encoding:** Apply one round of positional encoding (PE) to the sampled 3D coordinates [S1].
3.  **Spatial MLP Inference:** Feed the encoded points into the spatial MLP. The MLP outputs the SDF value $f(x)$ and the diffuse color $C_d$ at each point [S1].
4.  **Geometry and Density Computation:**
    *   Compute the surface normal $n$ as the gradient of the SDF:
        $$ \text{Normal}(x) = \nabla f(x) $$
        [S1]
    *   Compute the volume density $\tau(x)$ using the SDF value $f(x)$, a learnable parameter $\alpha$, and the cumulative distribution function $\Psi_\beta$ of a zero-mean Laplace distribution with scale parameter $\beta > 0$:
        $$ \tau(x) = \alpha \Psi_\beta(f(x)) $$
        [S1]
    *   *Note:* In VolSDF, $\beta$ is fixed as $\alpha^{-1}$ [S1].
5.  **Diffuse Color Assignment:** Assign the diffuse color $C_d$ directly from the spatial MLP output. This component simulates irradiance with low angular correlation, representing subsurface scattering or air impurities [S1].
6.  **Specular Color Calculation (PBR):**
    *   Calculate the specular term $C_s$ using the Cook-Torrance model and split sum approximation [S1].
    *   The Cook-Torrance specular BRDF is:
        $$ f_{cook-torrance} = \frac{DFG}{4(\omega_0 \cdot n)(\omega_i \cdot n)} $$
        where $D$ is the normal distribution function, $G$ is the geometry function, and $F$ is the Fresnel equation [S1].
    *   Apply the split sum approximation to separate the integration into a prefiltering texture part and a BRDF LUT part:
        $$ L_0(p, \omega_0) \approx \int_{\Omega} L_i(p, \omega_i) \, d\omega_i * \int_{\Omega} f_r(p, \omega_i, \omega_0) \cos \theta d\omega_i $$
        [S1]
    *   The right side (BRDF integration) is approximated using the lookup table:
        $$ \int_{\Omega} f_r \cos\theta d\omega_i \approx F_0 * f(\cos\theta, \rho, \text{brdfLUT}.r) + f(\cos\theta, \rho, \text{brdfLUT}.g) $$
        where $F_0$ is the reflectance at a perpendicular angle [S1].
    *   The prefiltered color $f_{prefilteredColor}$ is computed based on roughness $\rho$, position $p$, and the reflected direction $\omega_i = 2 (\omega_o \cdot n) n - \omega_o$ [S1].
    *   Calculate the final specular color:
        $$ C_s = f_{prefilteredColor} * (F_0 * A + B) $$
        where $A$ and $B$ correspond to the LUT outputs `brdfLUT.r` and `brdfLUT.g` respectively [S1].
7.  **Composite Color:** Compute the total color at the sample point:
    $$ C = C_d + C_s $$
    [S1]
8.  **Volume Rendering:** Render the final pixel color $C(r)$ using the discrete volume rendering equation:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
    where:
    *   $\sigma_i = \tau(x_i)$ is the volume density at sample point $i$ [S1].
    *   $\delta_i$ is the distance between sample point $i$ and $i+1$ along the ray [S1].
    *   $c_i$ is the color at sample point $i$ ($C_d + C_s$) [S1].
    *   $T_i$ is the cumulative transmittance:
        $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
        [S1]

**Parameters**
*   $\alpha$: Learnable parameter controlling the transition between volume and surface density. Large $\alpha$ implies a surface with low transmittance (e.g., metal), while $\alpha \to 0$ implies a homogeneous medium (e.g., fog) [S1].
*   $\beta$: Scale parameter for the Laplace distribution. In VolSDF, it is fixed as $\alpha^{-1}$ [S1].
*   $N$: Number of sample points along the ray. Not given in the sources.
*   $F_0$: Reflectance at a perpendicular angle. Not given in the sources.
*   $\rho$: Surface roughness. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Precision:** The SDF gradient $\nabla f(x)$ requires accurate differentiation. Numerical instability may occur if the MLP outputs are not smooth.
*   **Boundaries:** The definition of SDF relies on the boundary $\partial\Omega$. Points far from the boundary may have ambiguous density interpretations if $\alpha$ is not tuned correctly [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the ray does not intersect the object's implicit surface, the SDF values may be large, leading to low density (high transmittance) if $\alpha$ is small, or high density if $\alpha$ is large. The method assumes the SDF accurately represents the scene geometry [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_sdf_density(sdf_value, alpha, beta):
    """
    Computes volume density from SDF using Laplace CDF.
    Psi_beta is the CDF of zero-mean Laplace with scale beta.
    """
    # Laplace CDF: 0.5 * (1 - sign(x) * exp(-|x|/beta))
    # For x > 0: 1 - 0.5 * exp(-x/beta)
    # For x < 0: 0.5 * exp(x/beta)
    # Simplified: 0.5 * (1 - np.sign(sdf_value) * np.exp(-np.abs(sdf_value) / beta))
    # However, standard implementation often uses:
    # tau = alpha * 0.5 * (1 - np.sign(sdf) * np.exp(-np.abs(sdf) / beta))
    # Note: The source defines tau = alpha * Psi_beta(f(x)).
    # Psi_beta(x) = 0.5 * (1 - sign(x) * exp(-|x|/beta))
    
    sign_val = np.sign(sdf_value)
    exp_term = np.exp(-np.abs(sdf_value) / beta)
    cdf_val = 0.5 * (1 - sign_val * exp_term)
    return alpha * cdf_val

def compute_normal(mlp_output, x):
    """
    Computes normal as gradient of SDF.
    In practice, this is done via automatic differentiation during training.
    For inference/pseudocode, we assume mlp_output contains f(x).
    """
    # Placeholder for gradient computation. 
    # In a real implementation, this is df/dx.
    # [G] Assuming a numerical gradient or autograd engine is available.
    # Here we return a placeholder vector.
    return np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0]) # Placeholder

def compute_diffuse_color(mlp_output):
    """
    Returns diffuse color directly from MLP.
    """
    return mlp_output['diffuse_color']

def compute_specular_color(p, normal, view_dir, roughness, F0, brdf_lut, prefilter_texture):
    """
    Computes specular color using Cook-Torrance and Split Sum Approximation.
    """
    # Reflected direction
    # omega_i = 2 * (omega_o . n) * n - omega_o
    # Note: In the source, omega_o is the view direction (outgoing), 
    # and omega_i is the incident direction for the prefilter.
    # The formula for reflection is: r = 2(n.v)n - v
    dot_nv = np.dot(view_dir, normal)
    reflected_dir = 2 * dot_nv * normal - view_dir
    
    # Prefiltered color
    # f_prefilteredColor depends on roughness, position, and incident direction (reflected)
    f_prefiltered = prefilter_texture(roughness, p, reflected_dir)
    
    # BRDF LUT lookup
    # cos_theta is the dot product of view_dir and normal (assuming hemisphere integration around normal)
    cos_theta = np.clip(dot_nv, 0.0, 1.0)
    
    lut_r = brdf_lut[cos_theta, roughness] # Indexing by cos_theta and roughness
    lut_g = brdf_lut[cos_theta, roughness]
    
    # Specular term: Cs = f_prefilteredColor * (F0 * A + B)
    A = lut_r
    B = lut_g
    
    Cs = f_prefiltered * (F0 * A + B)
    return Cs

def render_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, mlp, brdf_lut, prefilter_texture, alpha, beta, N_samples):
    """
    Renders a single ray using SDF-based Neural Volume Rendering with PBR.
    """
    # 1. Sample points along the ray
    # [G] Assuming a standard sampling strategy, e.g., uniform or stratified.
    # t values are incrementally adjusted.
    t_values = np.linspace(0.0, 10.0, N_samples) # Placeholder range
    points = ray_origin + t_values[:, np.newaxis] * ray_direction
    
    # 2. Positional Encoding
    # [G] Assuming a standard PE function is applied to points.
    encoded_points = positional_encoding(points)
    
    # 3. Spatial MLP Inference
    # mlp_output contains f(x) and diffuse_color
    mlp_outputs = mlp(encoded_points)
    sdf_values = mlp_outputs['sdf']
    diffuse_colors = mlp_outputs['diffuse_color']
    
    # 4. Compute Density and Normal
    densities = np.zeros(N_samples)
    normals = np.zeros((N_samples, 3))
    
    for i in range(N_samples):
        # Compute density
        densities[i] = compute_sdf_density(sdf_values[i], alpha, beta)
        
        # Compute normal (gradient of SDF)
        # [G] In practice, this requires backpropagation or numerical gradient.
        # Here we assume a function that computes grad f(x).
        normals[i] = compute_normal(mlp_outputs, points[i])
        
    # 5. Volume Rendering
    sigma = densities
    delta = np.diff(np.concatenate(([0.0], t_values))) # Distance between samples
    delta = np.append(delta, delta[-1]) # Handle last segment if needed, or adjust loop
    
    # Transmittance
    T = np.zeros(N_samples)
    T[0] = 1.0
    for i in range(1, N_samples):
        T[i] = T[i-1] * np.exp(-sigma[i-1] * delta[i-1])
        
    # Color accumulation
    final_color = np.zeros(3)
    for i in range(N_samples):
        # Compute color at point i
        Cd = diffuse_colors[i]
        Cs = compute_specular_color(points[i], normals[i], ray_direction, roughness=0.5, F0=0.04, brdf_lut=brdf_lut, prefilter_texture=prefilter_texture)
        Ci = Cd + Cs
        
        # Weight
        weight = T[i] * (1 - np.exp(-sigma[i] * delta[i]))
        final_color += weight * Ci
        
    return final_color
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of MLP layers $N$, the number of samples $N$, and the cost of the BRDF LUT lookup and prefiltering. The split sum approximation reduces the complexity of the specular integration from an integral to a lookup.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the MLP weights, the BRDF LUT, and the prefiltered textures.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method achieves better reconstruction quality than prior methods, particularly in scenes with specular reflections, by avoiding foggy artifacts [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Interpretable parameters, smoother normals, accurate specular reflections.
*   **Limitations:** Coarse detail in standard NeRF is overcome, but the method still relies on the smoothness of the MLP for the SDF, which may limit very sharp features if not regularized.
*   **When to use:** When accurate specular reflections and smooth geometry are required, and interpretability of material parameters is important.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and the overhead of PBR calculations and SDF gradients is too high.

## Background
The method originates from the work of Chenyang Lv et al., published in 2024, titled "Volume rendering based on PBR and neural implicit surfaces" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of NeRF, such as coarse detail and foggy artifacts in specular regions, by integrating SDF representations with PBR material models. This approach builds on VolSDF [S1] for geometry and Ref-NeRF [S1] for the concept of using reflected radiance, but replaces the view-dependent radiance MLP with a physically based model.
