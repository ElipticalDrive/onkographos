# UA-PlantNeRF Architecture

Also called: Dual-Head NeRF

## Overview
UA-PlantNeRF is a unified framework for reconstructing volumetric radiance fields from sparse, unstructured video footage of plants, addressing degraded geometry and the absence of calibrated uncertainty estimates [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by jointly predicting density, color, and decomposed aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty via a dual-head architecture trained with a heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood objective [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Uncertainty-aware NeRF](12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md), which focuses on general scene uncertainty, UA-PlantNeRF is specifically tailored for botanical phenotyping, incorporating semantic consistency constraints to prevent geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions and utilizing split conformal prediction for distribution-free coverage guarantees [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse video frames (as few as 15), identified via a frame-selection strategy (VISAR) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Volumetric radiance field $R$, spatially varying uncertainty field $U$, and conformal prediction intervals for each ray.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Backbone:** Two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), each with 8 layers and width 256, featuring skip connections at layer 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Positional Encoding:** Hierarchical encoding with $L=10$ frequency levels, defined as:
    $$
    \gamma(\mathbf{x}, d_1, d_2) = [\mathbf{x}, \sin(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{d_2-1}\pi\mathbf{x})]
    $$
*   **Dual Head:** A parallel network branch predicting the log-variance $\log \beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ for aleatoric uncertainty [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Frame Selection:** Identify maximally informative, non-redundant viewpoints from raw footage using the VISAR strategy (weights $\alpha_1=0.4, \alpha_2=0.3, \alpha_3=0.3$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Network Forward Pass (Training):**
    *   Input positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to the coarse network to obtain latent feature $\mathbf{h}$ and density $\sigma$.
    *   Pass $\mathbf{h}$, $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, and $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ to the fine network.
    *   Apply Monte Carlo Dropout with probability $p=0.1$ after every hidden layer of the fine network [S1, unofficial].
    *   The fine network outputs color $\hat{\mathbf{c}}$ and the parallel head outputs log-variance $\log \beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$.
3.  **Uncertainty Estimation (Inference):**
    *   Perform $K=20$ stochastic passes through the fine network with MC Dropout enabled [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the mean prediction $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ and epistemic uncertainty $U_{ep}(r)$:
        $$
        U_{ep}(r) = K^{-1} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \|\hat{\mathbf{c}}_k - \bar{\mathbf{c}}\|^2
        $$
    *   Compute aleatoric uncertainty $U_{al}(r)$ using the predicted log-variance $\beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) = \exp(\log \beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}))$ and standard volume rendering weights $T_i, \alpha_i$:
        $$
        U_{al}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i \alpha_i \beta(\mathbf{x}_i)
        $$
    *   Compute total uncertainty:
        $$
        U_{total}(r) = U_{ep}(r) + U_{al}(r)
        $$
4.  **Conformal Calibration:** Construct a calibration set $D_{cal}$ from held-out plants (entirely excluded from training, not just held-out views) to ensure exchangeability. Use this set to compute residuals and generate prediction intervals at a user-specified confidence level [S1, unofficial].

**Training Objective:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ combines the heteroscedastic Negative Log-Likelihood ($\mathcal{L}_{NLL}$), distortion regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{reg}$), and semantic consistency ($\mathcal{L}_{sem}$):
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{NLL} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} + \lambda_{sem} \mathcal{L}_{sem}
$$
Where:
*   $\mathcal{L}_{NLL}$ for a batch $B$:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{NLL} = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{r \in B} \left[ \frac{\|\hat{\mathbf{c}}(r) - \mathbf{c}(r)\|^2}{2\beta(r)} + \frac{1}{2} \log \beta(r) \right]
    $$
*   $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ is the Mip-NeRF 360 distortion regularizer [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathcal{L}_{sem}$ is a CLIP semantic-consistency term preventing geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   Dropout probability $p$: 0.1 [S1, unofficial].
*   Stochastic passes $K$: 20 [S1, unofficial].
*   Regularization weights: $\lambda_{reg} = 0.01$, $\lambda_{sem} = 0.1$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Calibration Set Construction:** Calibrating on held-out views of the same plant violates exchangeability assumptions. The calibration set must consist of entirely different plants drawn from the same distribution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion:** Without $\mathcal{L}_{sem}$, geometry may collapse in occluded leaf regions due to lack of visual evidence [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is $K$ times slower than standard NeRF due to $K=20$ stochastic passes for epistemic uncertainty estimation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Increased memory usage due to the dual-head architecture and storage of uncertainty fields [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides calibrated uncertainty estimates, handles sparse views effectively, and maintains geometric integrity in occluded regions via semantic constraints [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful construction of the calibration set to maintain exchangeability; increased computational cost at inference [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Sparse-view plant reconstruction where uncertainty quantification and geometric reliability are critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Dense-view scenarios where standard NeRF suffices, or when computational resources for $K$ stochastic passes are limited [S1, unofficial].

## Background
UA-PlantNeRF was introduced to address the limitations of standard NeRF in the context of 3D plant reconstruction from sparse video frames [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the VISAR frame-selection strategy and integrates concepts from heteroscedastic regression and conformal prediction to provide robust, uncertainty-aware reconstructions [S1, unofficial]. The method is evaluated on benchmarks including Pheno4D, ROSE-X, and a novel UA-Field greenhouse dataset [S1, unofficial].
