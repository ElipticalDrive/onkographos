# UA-PlantNeRF Architecture

Also called: Dual-Head NeRF

## Overview
UA-PlantNeRF is a unified framework for reconstructing volumetric radiance fields from sparse, unstructured video footage of plants, addressing degraded geometry and the absence of calibrated uncertainty estimates [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by jointly predicting density, color, and decomposed aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty through a dual-head architecture trained with a heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood objective [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Uncertainty-aware NeRF](12968-uncertainty-aware-nerf.md), which may rely on different uncertainty modeling strategies, UA-PlantNeRF explicitly integrates conformal prediction for distribution-free coverage guarantees and semantic consistency losses to prevent geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse video frames (as few as 15), camera poses, and intrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** Volumetric radiance field $R$, spatially varying uncertainty field $U$, and conformal prediction intervals [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
The backbone consists of two parallel Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) with the following layout [S1, unofficial]:
*   **Positional Encoding:** Hierarchical NeRF with $L=10$ frequency levels.
*   **Network Architecture:** Two 8-layer MLPs, width 256.
*   **Skip Connections:** Applied at layer 4.
*   **Head 1 (Standard):** Predicts volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$.
*   **Head 2 (Dual/Aleatoric):** Predicts the log-variance $\log \beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ for aleatoric uncertainty [S1, unofficial].

**Training Steps**
1.  **Frame Selection:** Identify maximally informative, non-redundant viewpoints from raw footage using the VISAR strategy (weights $\alpha_1=0.4, \alpha_2=0.3, \alpha_3=0.3$) and temporal non-maximum suppression ($\omega=5$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Forward Pass:** For each ray $r$, sample points $\mathbf{x}_i$ and directions $\mathbf{d}$. Pass through the backbone MLPs.
3.  **Uncertainty Estimation:**
    *   **Aleatoric:** Compute $\log \beta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ from the dual head.
    *   **Epistemic:** Apply Monte Carlo Dropout with probability $p=0.1$ after every hidden layer of the fine network [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the total training loss $\mathcal{L}$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{NLL} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} + \lambda_{sem} \mathcal{L}_{sem} $$
    Where $\lambda_{reg} = 0.01$ and $\lambda_{sem} = 0.1$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Optimization:** Minimize $\mathcal{L}$ using stochastic gradient descent.

**Inference Steps**
1.  **Stochastic Sampling:** Perform $K=20$ stochastic passes through the network with MC Dropout enabled [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Epistemic Uncertainty:** Calculate the variance of the $K$ predictions [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ U_{ep}(r) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \|\hat{\mathbf{c}}_k - \bar{\mathbf{c}}\|^2 $$
    where $\hat{\mathbf{c}}_k$ is the color prediction of the $k$-th pass and $\bar{\mathbf{c}}$ is the mean prediction [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Aleatoric Uncertainty:** Aggregate the heteroscedastic variance along the ray [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ U_{al}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i \alpha_i \beta(\mathbf{x}_i) $$
    where $T_i$ and $\alpha_i$ are the transmittance and weight for sample $i$, and $\beta(\mathbf{x}_i)$ is the variance predicted by the dual head [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Total Uncertainty:** Sum the components [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ U_{total}(r) = U_{ep}(r) + U_{al}(r) $$
5.  **Conformal Prediction:** Apply split conformal prediction using a calibration set $D_{cal}$ constructed from held-out plants (not held-out views of the same plants) to ensure exchangeability [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**
*   **Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):** For a batch $B$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{NLL} = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{r \in B} \left[ \frac{\|\hat{\mathbf{c}}(r) - \mathbf{c}(r)\|^2}{2\beta(r)} + \frac{1}{2} \log \beta(r) \right] $$
    where $\beta(r)$ is the variance predicted by the dual head [S1, unofficial].
*   **Distortion Regularization:** $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ is the Mip-NeRF 360 distortion regularizer [S1, unofficial].
*   **Semantic Consistency:** $\mathcal{L}_{sem}$ is a CLIP-based term preventing geometry collapse in occluded leaf regions [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   Dropout probability $p$: 0.1 [S1, unofficial].
*   Number of stochastic passes $K$: 20 [S1, unofficial].
*   Positional encoding levels $L$: 10 [S1, unofficial].
*   MLP layers: 8 [S1, unofficial].
*   MLP width: 256 [S1, unofficial].
*   Skip connection layer: 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{reg}$: 0.01 [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{sem}$: 0.1 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Calibration Set Construction:** Using held-out views of the same plant for calibration violates exchangeability assumptions, leading to invalid coverage guarantees. Calibration must use entirely held-out plants [S1, unofficial].
*   **Geometry Collapse:** Occluded leaf regions are prone to geometry collapse; the semantic consistency term $\mathcal{L}_{sem}$ is critical here [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sparse Views:** The framework is designed for sparse views (e.g., 15 frames), but performance degrades if frame selection is poor [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Inference is $O(K \cdot N)$ where $K=20$ is the number of stochastic passes and $N$ is the number of samples per ray, due to MC Dropout [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing two parallel MLPs (standard and dual-head) and intermediate dropout masks during training [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides decomposed uncertainty (aleatoric and epistemic) and provable coverage guarantees via conformal prediction; robust to sparse, unstructured input [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Increased inference time due to multiple stochastic passes; requires careful calibration set construction [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Sparse view reconstruction of plants or similar structured natural scenes where uncertainty quantification is critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Dense view scenarios where standard NeRF suffices; real-time applications where $K=20$ passes are prohibitive [S1, unofficial].

## Background
UA-PlantNeRF was introduced to address the limitations of standard NeRF in plant phenotyping, specifically degraded geometry under sparse, unstructured image capture and the lack of uncertainty estimates [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the VISAR frame-selection strategy and integrates concepts from heteroscedastic regression and conformal prediction [S1, unofficial]. Evaluated on benchmarks Pheno4D, ROSE-X, and the novel UA-Field greenhouse dataset [S1, unofficial].
