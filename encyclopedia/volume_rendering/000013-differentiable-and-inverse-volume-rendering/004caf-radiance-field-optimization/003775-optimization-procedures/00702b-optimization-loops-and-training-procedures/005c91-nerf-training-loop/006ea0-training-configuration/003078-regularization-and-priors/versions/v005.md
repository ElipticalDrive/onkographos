# Regularization and Priors
## Overview
Regularization and Priors augment the training objective with auxiliary constraints to enforce physical plausibility, stabilize optimization, and mitigate geometric ambiguities in neural radiance fields. This family addresses issues such as scale ambiguity in material decomposition, instability in dynamically growing networks, and overfitting to noisy observations. It modifies the loss landscape or network outputs directly, contrasting with sibling methods like [Learning Rate Scheduling](2701-learning-rate-scheduling.md) or [Adam Optimisation with Adaptive Scaling](3093-adam-optimisation-with-adaptive-scaling.md) that adjust the optimization trajectory [G].

## Subtopics

### [Multi-scale Joint Training Parameters](2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters.md)
Balances photometric and uncertainty losses across multiple training stages to stabilize dynamically growing networks [2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters.md]. Sets itself apart by tying loss weights to a multi-resolution progression rather than applying static priors. Choose when training adaptive, multi-stage radiance fields.

### [Reflectance and Shading Decomposition Priors](4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors.md)
Enforces physical plausibility by decoupling rendered color into material reflectance and shading [4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors.md]. Solves unstable material properties in novel view synthesis and object insertion. Distinguishes itself by regularizing intermediate decomposition components to mitigate scale ambiguity. Choose when handling complex lighting or inserted objects.

### [Surface Smoothness Regularization](12970-surface-smoothness-regularization.md)
Imposes piece-wise smoothness constraints on rendered depth from unobserved rays to stabilize joint optimization of blur kernels and radiance fields in sparse-view DeRF [12970-surface-smoothness-regularization.md]. Integrates "hidden rays" from estimated blur kernels alongside unseen rays, targeting coupled defocus and geometry uncertainty. Choose for sparse-view deblurring scenarios.

### [Perceptual Distillation Module](13792-perceptual-distillation-module.md)
Transfers texture information from 2D pre-deblurred priors to the implicit 3D radiance field via feature-space matching to address 3D geometric inconsistency in sparse-view deblurring [13792-perceptual-distillation-module.md]. Operates on high-level perceptual features rather than pixel values or geometric derivatives. Choose when deblurring NeRFs trained on sparse views.

### [NeRF Training with Depth Distribution Regularization](21747-nerf-training-with-depth-distribution-regularization.md)
Applies constraints to rendering weights and volume densities to correct inaccurate geometry caused by small camera motion, operating without SfM point clouds [21747-nerf-training-with-depth-distribution-regularization.md]. Designed for minimal camera movement regimes where traditional multi-view stereo fails. Choose when camera motion is small and SfM is unreliable.

### [Neural Field Regularization by Denoising (NEF-RED)](21753-neural-field-regularization-by-denoising-nef-red.md)
Optimizes neural fields for high-resolution 3D reconstruction from sparse-view measurements (e.g., X-ray CT) by integrating a learned regularizer directly into the optimization loop [21753-neural-field-regularization-by-denoising-nef-red.md]. Leverages the RED framework to decouple the conditioning network from optimization, using pre-trained denoisers as implicit priors. Choose for sparse-view tomography or ill-posed inverse reconstruction.

### Weight Annealing
A technique to anneal proposal weights by raising them to a power that increases from 0 to 1 over training steps, encouraging exploration by presenting a wider range of proposal intervals early in training [S1]. The annealed weight $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_n$ at step $n$ (out of $N$ total steps) is computed as:
$$
\hat{\mathbf{w}}_n \propto \hat{\mathbf{w}}^{\frac{bn/N}{(b-1)n/N+1}}
$$
where $b$ is the bias hyperparameter [S1]. In experiments, $b=10$ is used [S1]. Ablating this annealing causes SSIM on the bicycle scene to decrease from 0.687 to 0.679 [S1].

### Laplace Approximation for Neural Networks
Approximates the posterior distribution of network parameters as a multivariate Gaussian centered at the optimal weights $\omega^*$, using the inverse Hessian of the negative log-posterior as the covariance matrix [S2]. The covariance is derived as $\Sigma = -H(\omega^*)^{-1}$ [S2]. Directly applying this to NeRF parameters is impracticable due to high parameter correlation, making accurate covariance estimation challenging, and the expensive sampling overhead [S2].

### Group Shift
A self-supervised regularization technique that shifts the grouping of samples along the ray to assess each point in multiple ways using different groups, imposing consistency on the results [S3]. The head index $k$ of the first group is shifted by $s$ towards the camera, where $s$ is randomly sampled from $\{1, \dots, N_p-1\}$ during training [S3]. Padding samples are added before the front sample to handle indices exceeding the original range ($i' < 1$ or $j' > N$) [S3]. The shifted MIMO function maps shifted group positions to outputs: $f_{\text{MIMO}}^{\text{shift}} : (\mathbb{R}^3)^{N_p} \times \mathbb{S}^2 \rightarrow (\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^+)^{N_p}$, where $(\mathbf{x}_{i'}, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{j'}, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}_{i'}, \dots, \mathbf{c}_{j'}, \sigma_{i'}, \dots, \sigma_{j'})$ [S3]. Choose when implementing MIMO-NeRF to reduce ambiguity in sample grouping.

## Background
Weight annealing was introduced in mip-NeRF 360 to improve proposal network training stability [S1]. Intrinsic decomposition priors (reflectance/shading) were developed to handle lighting variations and object insertion [4624-reflectance-and-shading-decomposition-priors.md]. Uncertainty-aware losses in multi-scale training address the instability of dynamically growing architectures [2967-multi-scale-joint-training-parameters.md]. Bayesian approaches using Laplace approximation have been explored for uncertainty quantification but face scalability issues [S2]. Group shift was proposed in MIMO-NeRF to reduce ambiguity in sample grouping [S3].
