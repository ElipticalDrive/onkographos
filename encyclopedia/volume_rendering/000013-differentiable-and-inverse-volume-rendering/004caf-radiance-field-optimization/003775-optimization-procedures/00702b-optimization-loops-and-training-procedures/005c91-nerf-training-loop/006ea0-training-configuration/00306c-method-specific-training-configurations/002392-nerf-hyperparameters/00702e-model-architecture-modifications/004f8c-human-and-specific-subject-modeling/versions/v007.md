# Human and Specific Subject Modeling


## Overview
This family of methods modifies the standard NeRF architecture to explicitly model human subjects, addressing the challenges of dynamic geometry, non-rigid deformation, and generalization to unseen poses or identities. Unlike monolithic scene representations, these methods integrate parametric human priors (such as SMPL or MANO) or part-based decompositions to constrain the learning space, enabling high-fidelity synthesis of dynamic humans from sparse or monocular inputs.

## Shared principles
The core task of a NeRF network architecture is to learn a continuous volumetric scene representation $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the spatial position, $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the viewing direction, $\sigma$ is the volume density, and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the view-dependent color [G]. The rendering process integrates these outputs along rays using standard volume rendering equations [G].

## Subtopics

### LISA
LISA is a part-based implicit neural representation method specifically designed for modeling human hands [S1][S2, unofficial]. It approximates the complex geometry of hands by decomposing them into a collection of rigid parts, leveraging the MANO (Modeling of the HAND) parametric model [S2, unofficial]. Unlike monolithic NeRF approaches, LISA distinguishes itself by using Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) for shape description and implicitly encoding MANO shape parameters via skeletal posture, while simultaneously learning a color field for realistic appearance [S2, unofficial].

### [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md)
Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA) is a neural representation for dynamic human avatars that enables local and independent editing of both geometry and texture while supporting novel view synthesis and novel pose rendering. It distinguishes itself from UV-mapping-based methods by anchoring structured latent codes to a deformable mesh in canonical space, allowing direct 3D-to-3D manipulation, and from two-stage distillation methods by being end-to-end trainable from scratch.

### HumanNeRF
HumanNeRF is a neural representation for high-fidelity free-view synthesis of dynamic humans that generalizes to unseen subjects and camera settings using sparse RGB inputs, avoiding per-scene training through aggregated pixel-alignment features and a pose-embedded non-rigid deformation field [S2, unofficial]. It differs from per-scene methods like [LISA](12036-lisa.md) by learning a generalized field from a dataset, and from other generalizable methods like [Unified Volumetric Avatar (UVA)](13784-unified-volumetric-avatar-uva.md) by explicitly combining image-based rendering features with a neural radiance field via a novel appearance blending scheme to preserve high-frequency texture details [S2, unofficial].

### [HumanNeRF-SE](15640-humannerf-se.md)
HumanNeRF-SE synthesizes novel pose images of humans from monocular video inputs by combining explicit SMPL shape priors with an implicit NeRF representation. It differs from sibling methods like [HumanNeRF](13842-humannerf.md) and [MonoHuman](13842-humannerf.md) by using frozen SMPL blending weights for rigid deformation and point-level spatial features for non-rigid refinement, thereby avoiding the pose leakage and overfitting associated with frame-level features or learned weight volumes.

### [Dynamic Semantic Human NeRF](21763-dynamic-semantic-human-nerf.md)
Dynamic Semantic Human NeRF extends static radiance field optimization to model dynamic human motion by learning a continuous function $G_\omega$ that maps geometry and appearance variations across frames. It differs from sibling methods by conditioning the neural radiance field on semantically meaningful latent codes from the imGHUM model (shape $\beta$, pose $\theta$, and root transformation $T$), enabling interpolation to novel poses and shapes rather than relying solely on temporal interpolation or explicit mesh tracking.

## Background
The integration of human-specific modeling into NeRFs emerged to overcome the limitations of general scene representations when applied to dynamic, articulated subjects. Early works like NeuMan [S1] demonstrated that leveraging SMPL priors allowed for better generalization to unseen views compared to methods like NeuralBody, which suffered from overfitting to training observations. Subsequent developments focused on generalization to unseen identities (HumanNeRF [S2, unofficial]), efficient local editing (UVA [S1]), and handling monocular inputs with frozen priors to prevent overfitting (HumanNeRF-SE [S1]). More recent approaches incorporate semantic latent codes from models like imGHUM to enable novel pose and shape synthesis (Dynamic Semantic Human NeRF [S1]).
