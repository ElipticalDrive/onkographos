# Frequency-Modulated Position Encoding

Also called: FM-NeRF Encoding, Frequency-Modulated Point Cloud Rendering with Easy Editing

## Overview
Frequency-Modulated Position Encoding is a coordinate encoding scheme used within the context of Frequency-Modulated Point Cloud Rendering, a method that optimizes a sparse point cloud to represent a radiance field. It solves the problem of mapping raw 3D spatial coordinates and viewing directions into a feature space suitable for neural network decoding, specifically addressing the need for fine-grained basis function support for frequency modulation. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF positional encoding by narrowing the encoding interval and using powers of 2 exponents (specifically $2^0, 2^{0.5}, \dots, 2^{L-1}$) rather than powers of 2 frequencies directly, providing a different spectral bias for the optimization of point-based radiance fields.

## How it works
The method operates on a point cloud representation where each point has a 3D position $\mathbf{p}$ and a viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$. The encoding transforms these raw inputs into a high-dimensional vector before being passed to the decoding network (AFNet).

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A raw coordinate or direction vector $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (for position) or $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (for direction).
*   **Output:** An encoded feature vector of dimension $2 \times L \times D$, where $D$ is the dimensionality of the input (3 for both position and direction) and $L$ is the number of frequency bands.

**Data Structures:**
The encoded vector is a concatenation of sine and cosine functions. For a single dimension of the input, the encoding produces $2L$ values. For a 3D input, the total output dimension is $6L$.

**Parameters:**
*   $L$: Number of frequency bands.
    *   For spatial coordinates: $L = 10$ [S1].
    *   For view directions: $L = 2$ [S1].
*   $\pi$: The mathematical constant pi.

**Formula:**
The position encoding function $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ is defined as [S1]:

$$
\gamma (\mathbf {p}) = \left( \sin (2^0 \pi \mathbf {p}), \cos (2^0 \pi \mathbf {p}), \sin (2^{0.5} \pi \mathbf {p}), \cos (2^{0.5} \pi \mathbf {p}), \dots, \sin (2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf {p}), \cos (2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf {p}) \right)
$$

Here, the exponent of the base 2 scales the frequency. Note that the source uses $2^0, 2^{0.5}, \dots, 2^{L-1}$ as the exponents. This implies the frequencies are $2^0, 2^{0.5}, 2^1, 2^{1.5}, \dots$. The notation $2^{L-1}$ in the source text [S1] corresponds to the last term's exponent. Specifically, the sequence of exponents is $k \cdot 0.5$ for $k=0, 1, \dots, 2(L-1)$? No, the source lists $\sin(2^0 \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^0 \pi \mathbf{p}), \sin(2^{0.5} \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^{0.5} \pi \mathbf{p}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{p})$. This implies the exponents are $0, 0.5, 1.0, \dots, L-1$. Let $E_j = j \cdot 0.5$ for $j=0, \dots, 2L-1$? No, the source explicitly writes the last term as $2^{L-1}$. **Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Bleeding Problem:** If the rasterization radius (used to expand points to disks) is too small, points from occluded surfaces and the background can be seen through the front surface [S1].
*   **Edge Expansion:** If the rasterization radius is too large, it leads to inaccurate depth estimation and object edges expand [S1].
*   **Precision:** The use of high-frequency sine/cosine functions can lead to optimization difficulties if the learning rate is not tuned appropriately. The source sets the MLP learning rate to $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and opacity learning rate to $0.01$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The encoding itself is $O(L \cdot D)$ per point. The overall training time is reported as 10 minutes for realistic rendering and 10 hours to almost converge [S1]. One epoch consumes approximately 12.8 seconds on the NeRF-Synthetic dataset [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The input feature dimension to the MLP is 120 (for L=10 pos, L=2 dir) [S1]. The MLP has 5 layers with hidden dimensions 256, 256, 256, 128 [S1].
*   **Performance:** The method achieves higher PSNR/SSIM on NeRF-Synthetic and Tanks and Temples datasets compared to NeRF, CCNeRF, NPBG, NPBG++, and Huang et al. [S1]. Results on ScanNet and DTU are comparable or better [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fast convergence (10 mins for realistic results), easy editing capabilities via point cloud masking [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires preprocessing (denoising/completion loops) [S1]. Sensitive to rasterization radius selection [S1].

## Background
The method originates from the paper "Frequency-Modulated Point Cloud Rendering with Easy Editing" [S1]. It builds on the positional encoding form of NeRF [S1] but narrows the encoding interval. It is used in the context of point-based radiance field optimization, specifically for scenes where easy editing and fast training are desired. It is compared against methods like NPBG, NPBG++, and CCNeRF [S1].
