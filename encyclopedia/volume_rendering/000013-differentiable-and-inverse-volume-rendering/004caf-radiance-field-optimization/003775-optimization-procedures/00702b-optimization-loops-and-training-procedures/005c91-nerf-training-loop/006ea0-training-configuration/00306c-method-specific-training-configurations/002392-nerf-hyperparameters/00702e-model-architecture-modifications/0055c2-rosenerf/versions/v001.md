# RoseNeRF

Also called: Refracting Once is Enough, Refracting once is enough for NeRF

## Overview
RoseNeRF is a method for high-fidelity novel view synthesis of real refractive objects that simplifies complex ray interactions inside transparent media to a single refraction event [S1]. It models refraction by predicting a single refracted ray using a 4D light field representation and a refraction network, avoiding the need for Index of Refraction (IoR) fields or detailed surface reconstructions [S1]. Unlike deformation field methods that predict position offsets for each sampling point, RoseNeRF predicts the refracted ray and uses hierarchical sampling, avoiding the need for point-by-point deformation and additional priors like IoR [S1].

## How it works
RoseNeRF is implemented on top of MS-NeRF [S1]. The method extends the standard NeRF architecture by introducing a Refraction Network (RefNet) to predict refracted rays, a Decoder to fuse features from original and refracted rays, and specific loss functions for regularization [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training images with known camera poses. For each ray, the method requires the 4D light field coordinates of the incident ray and, initially, an estimate of the surface normal [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Synthesized RGB images for novel viewpoints [S1].

### Data Structures and Layout
The training framework consists of the following components [S1]:
1.  **NeRF Spatial MLP:** Takes positional encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ and outputs volume density $\sigma$. Base architecture uses 256 hidden units [S1].
2.  **RefNet (Student Network $\Theta_s$):** A 2-layer MLP with 64 hidden units. Input: 4D light field coordinates $(u, v, s, t)$. Output: 4D refracted ray coordinates $(u_{nr}, v_{nr}, s_{nr}, t_{nr})$ [S1].
3.  **RefNet (Teacher Network $\Theta_t$):** A 3-layer MLP with 128 hidden units. Input: 5D vector $(u, v, s, t, \mathbf{N})$, where $\mathbf{N}$ is the surface normal. Output: 4D refracted ray coordinates $(u_{nr}, v_{nr}, s_{nr}, t_{nr})$ [S1].
4.  **Decoder MLP ($\Theta_D$):** A 2-layer MLP with 256 hidden units and ReLU activations. Input: Densities from original ray $\sigma_i$ and refracted ray $\sigma_{ir}$. Output: Combined density $\sigma_{ic}$ [S1].
5.  **NeRF Directional MLP:** Takes decoded density $\sigma_{ic}$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}$. Outputs color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

### Ray Parameterization
Rays are parameterized using a 4D light field representation by mapping them to intersections with two planes at $Z_0=0$ and $Z_1=1$ [S1]. A ray is defined by 4D coordinates $(u, v, s, t)$ [S1]. The Cartesian direction $\mathbf{d}$ is calculated as [S1]:
$$
\mathbf{d} = (Z_1 - Z_0, u - s, v - t)
$$
The origin of the refracted ray $\mathbf{o}_r$ is established at its intersection with the $Z = z_c$ plane (where $z_c$ is the camera's Z-coordinate) [S1]. The coordinates are determined by [S1]:
$$
\frac{u - x_r}{s - x_r} = \frac{v - y_r}{t - y_r} = \frac{Z_0 - z_r}{Z_1 - z_r}
$$
where $z_r = z_c$ [S1]. The specific choice of the starting point for the refracted ray does not affect the final rendering outcome [S1].

### Training Procedure
The training involves online distillation between a teacher and student RefNet, followed by joint optimization of the NeRF components [S1].

1.  **Initial Phase (Warm-up):** During the first 5,000 iterations, only the density $\sigma$ is queried along the original ray, and the decoder is not used [S1]. In the initial phase of NeRF’s stratified sampling, sample only along the original ray and estimate the surface normal $\mathbf{N}$ using the method from Eq. 5 of the source (standard NeRF normal estimation) [S1].
2.  **Refraction Prediction:** Use the student RefNet $\Theta_s$ to predict the refracted ray $\mathbf{r}_r = \mathbf{o}_r + t\mathbf{d}_r$ from the 4D light field of the incident ray [S1].
3.  **Sampling:**
    *   Query density $\sigma_i$ at sampling points along the original ray using the NeRF Spatial MLP [S1].
    *   Query density $\sigma_{ir}$ at sampling points along the predicted refracted ray using the NeRF Spatial MLP [S1].
    *   Apply importance sampling based on the first stage results to the original ray, while uniformly sampling the refracted ray [S1].
    *   In subsequent stages, both the original and refracted rays undergo independent importance sampling [S1].
4.  **Density Decoding:** Decode the densities of both rays into the complete ray density $\sigma_{ic}$ using the Decoder MLP $\Theta_D$:
    $$
    \{\sigma_i, \sigma_{ir}\} \xrightarrow{\Theta_D} \sigma_{ic}
    $$
    [S1].
5.  **Color Prediction:** Feed $\sigma_{ic}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ into the Directional MLP, inputting the direction last to better output view-dependent RGB color $\mathbf{c}_c$ [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Compute the final pixel color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ via volume rendering [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
    $$
    where $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$, $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$, $\sigma_i$ is volume density, and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is color [S1].

### Loss Functions
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of the baseline NeRF photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{NeRF}$, the online distillation loss $\mathcal{L}_{dist}$, and the refraction loss $\mathcal{L}_{ref}$ [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{dist} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{ref} + \mathcal{L}_{NeRF}
$$
where $\lambda_1 = 0.003$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.003$ [S1].

*   **Online Distillation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{dist}$):** Encourages the density prediction without feeding normal ($\hat{\mathbf{d}}_r$) to match the prediction with normal ($\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{rn}$) [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{dist} = \sum_i (1 - \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ri} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{rni})
    $$
    [S1].
*   **Refraction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{ref}$):** Combines reversible and irreversibility terms to ensure the reversibility of optical paths [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{ref} = \sum_i (1 - \mathbf{d}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_i) + \sum_i (1 - \mathbf{d}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ri})
    $$
    where $\mathbf{d}_i$ is the incident direction and $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ri}$ is the predicted refracted direction [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays per batch [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 200,000 iterations for all experiments [S1].
*   **Positional Encoding:** Highest frequency hyperparameter $L$ (standard NeRF setting) [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam with default parameters [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Floater Artifacts:** NeRF without refraction modeling suffers from floater artifacts (semi-transparent material floating in space) because sampling points are not aligned with the bent ray path [S1].
*   **Loss Importance:** Removing the refraction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{ref}$) disrupts the fitting process because the absence of the correct refracted ray path leads to erroneous sampling points, significantly impeding accurate model training [S1].
*   **Decoder Necessity:** Substituting the specialized decoder with a simple computed average leads to a significant decrease in performance [S1].
*   **Parameterization:** Using the origin and direction of the ray for prediction instead of a 4D light field parameterization worsens accuracy, especially for LPIPS [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** RoseNeRF demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on the Eikonal Fields’ dataset and Shiny dataset without additional information [S1]. It achieves better results on CD and Lab scenes than Ref-NeRF [S1]. It surpasses NeX, which has 500 times more parameters, in terms of detail and achieves competitive results in specular highlights [S1].
*   **Complexity:** The method uses a simple, shallow MLP for refraction prediction, reducing computational load and degrees of freedom compared to point-by-point deformation methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The method assumes a single refraction event, ignoring intermediate ray bending [S1]. This simplification may not capture complex multi-refraction scenarios accurately, though it avoids the cumulative errors of multi-step discretization [S1].

## Background
RoseNeRF was introduced by Xiaoqian Liang et al. in 2024 at the ACM International Conference on Multimedia Retrieval (ICMR) [S1]. The method addresses the challenge of applying NeRF to real-world scenes with refractive elements, where acquiring Index of Refraction (IoR) information is often impractical [S1]. It is heavily inspired by Han et al. [20], who verified the feasibility of reconstructing transparent objects by immersing them in liquid, simplifying modeling by not requiring detailed simulations of light interactions [S1].
