# Tri-MipRF Training Configuration

Also called: Tri-MipRF Training

## Overview
Tri-MipRF Training optimizes a tri-plane based radiance field representation to achieve anti-aliased novel view synthesis by explicitly modeling multi-scale features. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF training by replacing the single-resolution MLP coordinate encoding with a learnable tri-plane mipmap structure, allowing the network to adaptively select the appropriate frequency detail based on the ray's footprint. This approach builds on [Multi-scale and Mip-aware Representations](18780-multi-scale-and-mip-aware-representations.md) by using differentiable texture sampling to approximate cone integrals, addressing the aliasing artifacts common in high-frequency scene reconstruction.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated multi-view 2D images with known camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** A trained Tri-MipRF representation consisting of three learnable tri-planes (XY, YZ, XZ) organized as mipmaps, and a small MLP mapping sampled features to color and density.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-Mip Encoding:** Three 3D tensors representing the tri-planes. Each tri-plane is structured as a mipmap pyramid.
    *   **Base Level ($M_{l0}$):** The highest resolution level. Dimensions are Height $H=512$, Width $W=512$, and Channels $C=16$ [S1].
    *   **Mipmap Levels:** Lower resolution levels are generated by downsampling the base level, following standard texture mipmap generation principles.
*   **MLP:** A small Multi-Layer Perceptron that takes the aggregated features from the tri-plane queries and outputs volume density ($\sigma$) and view-dependent color ($c$).

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** For each pixel in the target image, cast a ray through the scene volume.
2.  **Ray Cone Approximation:** Determine the footprint of the pixel on the image plane to approximate the ray as a cone, following the MipNeRF approach [S1].
3.  **Tri-Plane Query:**
    *   Sample the ray's 3D coordinates against the three tri-planes (XY, YZ, XZ).
    *   Use differentiable bilinear interpolation to query features from the tri-planes.
    *   Select the appropriate mipmap level based on the ray's footprint size to ensure anti-aliasing. The `nvdiffrast` library is used to perform these mipmap queries efficiently [S1].
    *   Aggregate the features from the three planes (e.g., via concatenation or addition) to form a feature vector.
4.  **MLP Forward Pass:** Pass the aggregated feature vector and the viewing direction into the small MLP to predict density and color.
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Perform standard differentiable volume rendering (alpha compositing) along the ray to compute the final pixel color.
6.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the photometric loss between the rendered pixel color and the ground truth image pixel.
7.  **Optimization:** Update the Tri-Mip encoding parameters and MLP weights using gradient descent.

**Formulas:**
*   **Area Loss:** The pixel loss is scaled by the area of its footprint on the image plane, denoted as $L_{area}$ [S1].
    $$ L_{total} = \sum_{p \in \text{pixels}} \text{Area}(p) \cdot \| C_{rendered}(p) - C_{ground\_truth}(p) \|^2 $$
    Where $\text{Area}(p)$ is the footprint area of pixel $p$.

**Parameters:**
*   **Base Mipmap Shape:** $H=512, W=512, C=16$ [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** AdamW [S1].
*   **Weight Decay:** $1 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   **Learning Rate (MLP):** $2 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Learning Rate (Tri-Mip Encoding):** $2 \times 10^{-2}$ (scaled up 10x relative to the MLP) [S1].
*   **Learning Rate Schedule:** MultiStepLR [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 25,000 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The use of `tiny-cuda-nn` and `nvdiffrast` requires careful handling of GPU memory and precision settings to ensure stable gradient flow during mipmap sampling [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The tri-plane representation assumes the scene fits within the defined coordinate space. Out-of-bounds queries must be handled via padding or clamping in the texture sampling operation.

## Subtopics

### [LoD-NeuS](23284-lod-neus.md)
LoD-NeuS is a neural implicit surface reconstruction method that encodes features with a continuous level of detail (LoD) from a tri-plane-based representation to adaptively reconstruct high-fidelity geometry and address aliasing concerns. It builds on [Tri-MipRF Training Configuration](11504-tri-miprf-training-configuration.md) by introducing multi-scale tri-plane position encoding and multi-convolved featurization to approximate ray integrals within a cone, distinguishing itself from standard NeuS by reducing MLP queries while maintaining high-frequency detail.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is dominated by the differentiable texture sampling operations. The use of `tiny-cuda-nn` and `nvdiffrast` optimizes these queries, making them comparable to standard texture lookups.
*   **Memory Complexity:** The memory footprint is determined by the base mipmap size. With $H=512, W=512, C=16$ for three planes, the base memory is $3 \times 16 \times 512 \times 512 \times 4$ bytes $\approx 24$ MB. Mipmap levels add negligible overhead. This is significantly more compact than high-resolution 3D voxel grids.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method is evaluated on the multi-scale Blender dataset, which includes downscaling factors of 2, 4, and 8 to test anti-aliasing capabilities [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient memory usage due to the compact tri-plane representation; effective anti-aliasing through mipmap-based feature selection; fast training due to optimized texture sampling libraries.
*   **Limitations:** The representation is limited to the 2D plane projections, which may struggle with complex 3D structures that do not align well with the XY, YZ, or XZ planes. The choice of base resolution (512x512) is empirical and may not be optimal for all scene scales [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for novel view synthesis tasks where anti-aliasing is critical and memory constraints prevent the use of large 3D voxel grids or high-resolution NeRF MLPs.
*   **When not to use:** Not ideal for scenes with highly complex 3D geometry that cannot be well-approximated by tri-plane projections.

## Background
Tri-MipRF was introduced in the paper "Tri-MipRF: Tri-Mip Representation for Efficient Anti-Aliasing Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. The method was developed to address the aliasing artifacts present in standard NeRF representations, particularly when rendering scenes at multiple resolutions. It builds upon the MipNeRF concept of approximating rays as cones [S1] but replaces the positional encoding with a learnable tri-plane mipmap structure. The implementation relies on PyTorch, `tiny-cuda-nn`, and `nvdiffrast` to achieve efficient differentiable rendering [S1].
