# SpS-NeRF

Also called: SparseSat-NeRF (SpS-NeRF)

## Overview
SpS-NeRF is a variant of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) adapted for novel view synthesis and 3D geometry reconstruction from sparse, single-date satellite imagery [S1]. It solves the problem of reconstructing high-quality Digital Surface Models (DSM) and photorealistic views when only a few (2-3) satellite images are available, a scenario where standard NeRF and its predecessor Sat-NeRF fail due to insufficient geometric constraints [S1]. Unlike learning-based depth completion methods used in indoor scenes, SpS-NeRF leverages traditional Semi-Global Matching (SGM) to generate dense, albeit noisy and incomplete, depth maps which serve as supervision [S1]. It distinguishes itself by incorporating an uncertainty metric derived from SGM cross-correlation maps to guide ray sampling and weight depth losses, allowing the network to refine depth in occluded or textureless areas while maintaining sharp building outlines [S1].

## How it works
SpS-NeRF builds on the architecture of Sat-NeRF [S1]. It omits the modeling of transient objects and solar correction found in Sat-NeRF because it targets synchronous satellite acquisitions where lighting and scene content are consistent across views [S1]. The method jointly optimizes RGB color and depth geometry by integrating dense depth supervision derived from traditional computer vision techniques [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  A set of $N$ satellite images (typically $N=2$ or $N=3$) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics [S1].
2.  Dense depth maps and corresponding cross-correlation confidence maps generated by Semi-Global Matching (SGM) [S1].
    *   The depth maps are computed from images downsampled by a factor of 4, resulting in a Ground Sample Distance (GSD) that is $4\times$ larger than the original image resolution [S1].
    *   The depth maps are incomplete (due to occlusions) and imprecise [S1].
3.  A margin parameter $m$ defining the acceptable deviation from the input depth [S1].

**Outputs:**
1.  Rendered RGB images for novel viewpoints [S1].
2.  A refined, dense Digital Surface Model (DSM) [S1].

### Data Structures
The method relies on the standard NeRF volumetric representation but augments it with external supervision data structures:
*   **SGM Depth Map:** A 2D array $D_{SGM}$ of size $H \times W$ (where $H, W$ are the downsampled image dimensions). Values represent depth in meters. Invalid pixels (occlusions/no texture) are marked with a specific invalid value (e.g., 0 or NaN) [S1].
*   **SGM Correlation Map:** A 2D array $C_{SGM}$ of size $H \times W$. Values represent the cross-correlation similarity score between matched pixels in the stereo pair. Higher values indicate higher confidence [S1].
*   **NeRF Network $F_\Theta$:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) taking encoded position $\mathbf{x}$ and view direction $\mathbf{d}$ as input, outputting volume density $\sigma$ and RGB color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Preprocessing (SGM):**
    For each pair of input satellite images, compute dense depth maps and cross-correlation maps using Semi-Global Matching (SGM) on images downsampled by a factor of 4 [S1].

2.  **Ray Sampling (Guided):**
    For each pixel in a target view, cast a ray $r(\lambda) = o + \lambda d$. Sample $N$ points along the ray within the near/far bounds [S1].
    *   The sampling strategy is "guided" by the input depth and uncertainty [S1].
    *   Calculate the uncertainty weight $w(r)$ for the ray based on the SGM correlation map at the corresponding 2D pixel location [S1].
    *   The uncertainty measure intervenes in three ways:
        1.  As a weight applied to the final depth loss.
        2.  As a threshold to determine whether the depth loss should be activated for a specific sample [S1].
        3.  In guided ray sampling to concentrate samples near the expected depth surface [S1].

3.  **Forward Pass (NeRF):**
    For each sampled point $x_i$ on the ray:
    *   Encode position $\mathbf{x}_i$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ using hierarchical sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ [S1].
    *   Pass encoded features through the MLP $F_\Theta$ to predict volume density $\sigma_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering (RGB):**
    Compute the rendered color $C(r)$ for the ray using standard volume rendering integration [S1]:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where:
    $$ \alpha_i = 1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i} $$
    $$ T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    $$ \delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i $$
    Here, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity of the $i$-th sample, $T_i$ is the transmittance (probability that the ray reaches $x_i$ without being blocked), and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1].

5.  **Depth Estimation:**
    Compute the rendered depth $D(r)$ for the ray. This is typically the expected distance of the samples weighted by their opacity [S1]:
    $$ D(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i t_i $$
    where $t_i$ is the distance of the $i$-th sample from the camera origin [S1].

6.  **Loss Calculation:**
    The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of the RGB loss and the Depth loss [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_d \mathcal{L}_{depth} $$
    
    *   **RGB Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered color $C(r)$ and the ground truth image color $C_{gt}(r)$ [S1].
    
    *   **Depth Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{depth}$):** Formulated similarly to DDPNeRF but using the SGM-derived uncertainty [S1].
        Let $D_{gt}(r)$ be the input depth from SGM at the pixel location.
        The loss is activated only if the predicted depth $D(r)$ is within a margin $m$ of the input depth $D_{gt}(r)$ [S1]:
        $$ \text{Condition: } |D(r) - D_{gt}(r)| < m $$
        If the condition is met, the loss is computed as the absolute difference, weighted by the uncertainty $w(r)$ derived from the SGM correlation map $C_{SGM}$ [S1]:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{depth} = w(r) \cdot |D(r) - D_{gt}(r)| $$
        The weight $w(r)$ is higher for pixels with high correlation confidence (low uncertainty) [S1].
        *Note: The exact formula for $w(r)$ as a function of $C_{SGM}$ is not explicitly defined in the text, but it is described as an "uncertainty measure" based on correlation maps [S1].*

7.  **Optimization:**
    Minimize $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to the network parameters $\Theta$ using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [S1].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Downsample Factor:** 4 (SGM is run on images with $1/4$ resolution) [S1].
*   **Margin ($m$):** Not explicitly given in the text, but referenced as a threshold for depth loss activation (Equation 5) [S1].
*   **Number of Views:** 2 (DFC2v, Dji2v) or 3 (DFC3v, Dji3v) [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Not explicitly named, but standard NeRF optimization uses Adam [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Flat Surface Regularization:** SpS-NeRF lacks regularization on flat surfaces, leading to local irregularities (noise) in the reconstructed DSM [S1]. This is a common problem in NeRF. Adding semantic information is suggested as a potential solution [S1].
*   **SGM Outliers:** SGM is susceptible to outliers. The Ground Truth DSM in the Djibouti dataset was generated using the same SGM method, potentially biasing the evaluation and corrupting the GT with erroneous depth estimations [S1].
*   **Occlusions:** Input depth maps are incomplete due to occlusions. SpS-NeRF handles this by jointly optimizing RGB and depth losses, allowing it to complete missing information, but performance depends on the quality of the SGM correlation map in these regions [S1].
*   **Comparison Bias:** SpS-NeRF is less accurate than full-resolution SGM (SGMscl1) on valid pixels (MAEin) due to lack of regularization, but better in occluded/poorly textured areas (MAEout) [S1].

*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given. Depends on the number of views, samples per ray, and MLP size. Training involves joint optimization of RGB and depth losses.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given. Depends on the resolution of the SGM depth maps and the NeRF MLP size.

*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for sparse views (2-3 images). It outperforms standard NeRF and Sat-NeRF in these scenarios [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Effective for sparse satellite imagery.
    *   Better than SGM in occluded and poorly textured areas (MAEout) [S1].
    *   Produces sharper building outlines than vanilla Sat-NeRF [S1].
    *   Free of SGM outliers [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Lacks regularization on flat surfaces, causing local irregularities [S1].
    *   Less accurate than full-resolution SGM on valid pixels (MAEin) [S1].
    *   Relies on the quality of SGM; if SGM fails (e.g., textureless areas), supervision is weak [S1].

## Background
SpS-NeRF (SparseSat-NeRF) was introduced by Zhang et al. in 2023 [S1]. It is an extension of Sat-NeRF (Marí et al., 2022) and incorporates ideas from DDPNeRF (Roessle et al., 2022) [S1]. The method was developed to address the challenges of 3D reconstruction from sparse satellite imagery, where traditional NeRF variants require many views to produce convincing geometries [S1]. It was tested on the DFC2019 dataset and the Djibouti dataset (obtained via the CNES ISIS framework) [S1]. Numerical computations were performed on the SCAPAD cluster at the Institute de Physique du Globe de Paris [S1].
