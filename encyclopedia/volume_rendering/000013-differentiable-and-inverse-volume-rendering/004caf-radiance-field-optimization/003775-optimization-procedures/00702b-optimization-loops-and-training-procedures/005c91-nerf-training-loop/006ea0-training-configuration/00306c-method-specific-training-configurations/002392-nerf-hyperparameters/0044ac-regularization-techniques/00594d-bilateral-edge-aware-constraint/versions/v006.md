# Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint

Also called: Lbilateral

## Overview
The Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint is a geometric regularization technique for NeRF that preserves sharp structural discontinuities (such as building roofs and shadow transitions) while suppressing high-frequency noise on continuous surfaces [S1]. It differs from standard smoothness constraints by dynamically weighting geometric penalties based on photometric similarity, ensuring that rays with similar colors are enforced to be coplanar while rays with distinct colors are allowed to diverge [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** For a central ray $i$, the method requires its sampled 3D position $P_i$, albedo feature $c_i$, and unit surface normal $n_i$. It also requires $K$ neighboring rays $j \in N_i$, each with their rendered 3D positions $P_j$, albedo features $c_j$, and normals $n_j$.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $L_{bilateral}$ that penalizes deviations from local planarity, weighted by bilateral consistency.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Samples:** Each ray $i$ consists of a set of sampled points along the ray. The method operates on a specific sample point $i$ (typically the depth estimate or a specific integration point).
*   **Neighbor Set $N_i$:** A set of $K$ indices corresponding to neighboring rays in the image plane.

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: Number of neighbors sampled per ray. Value: 4 [S1].
*   $\sigma_d$: Adaptive spatial scale. Computed dynamically per ray [S1].
*   $\sigma_w$: Adaptive photometric scale. Computed dynamically per ray [S1].
*   $\gamma_{bi}$: Weight for the bilateral edge-aware term in the total loss. Selected from $\{0.1, 0.01, 0.001\}$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Neighbor Sampling:** For each central ray $i$, sample $K=4$ neighbors by drawing integer image-plane offsets uniformly from $[-2, 2] \times [-2, 2]$, excluding the zero offset $(0,0)$ [S1].
2.  **Neighbor Rendering:** Render the corresponding neighbor rays to recover their 3D positions $P_j$ and albedo features $c_j$ at the same depth or integration point as $P_i$ [S1].
3.  **Compute Adaptive Scales:**
    *   Compute the spatial scale $\sigma_d$ as the root-mean-square Euclidean distance between $P_i$ and its neighbors $P_j$ [S1]:
        $$ \sigma_d = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| P_i - P_j \|^2} $$
    *   Compute the photometric scale $\sigma_w$ as the root-mean-square difference between the albedo feature $c_i$ and its neighbors $c_j$ [S1]:
        $$ \sigma_w = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| c_i - c_j \|^2} $$
4.  **Calculate Bilateral Weights:** Compute the weight $w_{i,j}$ for each neighbor $j$ using the exponential terms for spatial and photometric distances [S1]:
    $$ w_{i,j} = \exp\left(-\frac{\| P_i - P_j \|^2}{2\sigma_d^2}\right) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\| c_i - c_j \|^2}{2\sigma_w^2}\right) $$
    *   Note: If $\sigma_d$ or $\sigma_w$ is zero (degenerate case), the weight calculation requires a guard to avoid division by zero [G].
5.  **Compute Normalization Factor:** Calculate $l_i$, the average edge length for normalization, defined as the mean distance to neighbors [S1]:
    $$ l_i = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_i} \| P_i - P_j \| $$
6.  **Compute Loss:** Minimize the projected distance of neighboring points onto the tangent plane of the central point. The loss is the sum of squared distances from $P_j$ to the tangent plane at $P_i$ (defined by normal $n_i$), weighted by $w_{i,j}$ and normalized by $l_i$ [S1]:
    $$ L_{bilateral} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{1}{l_i} \sum_{j \in N_i, j \neq i} w_{i,j} \left( (P_i - P_j) \cdot n_i \right)^2 $$
    where $N$ is the total number of central rays processed.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def bilateral_edge_aware_loss(central_ray, neighbor_indices, render_function, K=4):
    """
    Computes the Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint loss.
    
    Args:
        central_ray: Object containing ray origin, direction, and current depth/position P_i, 
                     albedo c_i, and normal n_i.
        neighbor_indices: List of K indices for neighboring rays in the image plane.
        render_function: Function that takes a ray and returns (P, c, n) at the specified depth.
        K: Number of neighbors (default 4).
        
    Returns:
        Scalar loss value.
    """
    P_i = central_ray.position
    c_i = central_ray.albedo
    n_i = central_ray.normal
    
    # Step 1 & 2: Sample and render neighbors
    # Note: In practice, neighbor_indices are derived from image-plane offsets [-2,2]x[-2,2] excluding (0,0)
    neighbors_data = []
    for idx in neighbor_indices:
        # Render neighbor ray to get corresponding 3D point, albedo, and normal
        # The render function must align the depth of the neighbor ray with P_i 
        # or use the same sampling strategy to ensure geometric comparability.
        P_j, c_j, n_j = render_function(idx)
        neighbors_data.append((P_j, c_j))
        
    if len(neighbors_data) == 0:
        return 0.0

    # Step 3: Compute adaptive scales
    # Spatial scale sigma_d
    spatial_diffs_sq = [np.sum((P_i - P_j)**2) for P_j, _ in neighbors_data]
    sigma_d_sq = np.mean(spatial_diffs_sq)
    
    # Photometric scale sigma_w
    photometric_diffs_sq = [np.sum((c_i - c_j)**2) for _, c_j in neighbors_data]
    sigma_w_sq = np.mean(photometric_diffs_sq)
    
    # Guard against zero variance to prevent division by zero
    if sigma_d_sq < 1e-8:
        sigma_d_sq = 1e-8
    if sigma_w_sq < 1e-8:
        sigma_w_sq = 1e-8
        
    sigma_d = np.sqrt(sigma_d_sq)
    sigma_w = np.sqrt(sigma_w_sq)
    
    # Step 4: Calculate bilateral weights
    weights = []
    for P_j, c_j in neighbors_data:
        dist_sq = np.sum((P_i - P_j)**2)
        color_dist_sq = np.sum((c_i - c_j)**2)
        
        w = np.exp(-dist_sq / (2 * sigma_d_sq)) * np.exp(-color_dist_sq / (2 * sigma_w_sq))
        weights.append(w)
        
    # Step 5: Compute normalization factor l_i
    l_i = np.mean([np.sqrt(d_sq) for d_sq in spatial_diffs_sq])
    if l_i < 1e-8:
        l_i = 1e-8
        
    # Step 6: Compute loss
    # Project distance of P_j onto the tangent plane of P_i
    # Distance = |(P_i - P_j) . n_i|
    # Loss term = w * distance^2 / l_i
    loss_sum = 0.0
    for w, (P_j, _) in zip(weights, neighbors_data):
        proj_dist = np.dot(P_i - P_j, n_i)
        loss_sum += w * (proj_dist ** 2) / l_i
        
    return loss_sum
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $K$ is the number of neighbors (typically 4). The rendering of neighbor rays adds significant overhead compared to point-wise losses, as it requires forward passes for $K$ additional rays per central ray [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it only stores temporary neighbor data during the loss computation step.
*   **Strengths:** Effectively preserves sharp geometric boundaries (roofs, walls) that are often blurred by standard L2 smoothness constraints. It is adaptive to local scene statistics [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to the need to render multiple neighbor rays. Sensitive to the choice of $\gamma_{bi}$ and the neighbor sampling range [S1].
*   **When to use:** When reconstructing scenes with distinct geometric structures (e.g., urban environments, DSM from satellite imagery) where boundary preservation is critical [S1].
*   **When not to use:** For scenes with smooth, continuous surfaces where standard smoothness is sufficient, or when computational resources are severely limited [S1].

## Background
The Bilateral Edge-Aware Constraint was introduced in the context of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for Digital Surface Model (DSM) reconstruction from multi-view satellite images [S1]. It was proposed to address the lack of explicit surface connectivity in implicit NeRF representations, where nearby image samples may belong to different structures [S1]. The method builds upon the fusion of NeRF and physical imaging models, using geometric features as external supervision signals to guide the density field learning [S1]. It is part of a broader "Geometric Structure Constraint" framework that also includes a Normal Smoothness Constraint [S1].
