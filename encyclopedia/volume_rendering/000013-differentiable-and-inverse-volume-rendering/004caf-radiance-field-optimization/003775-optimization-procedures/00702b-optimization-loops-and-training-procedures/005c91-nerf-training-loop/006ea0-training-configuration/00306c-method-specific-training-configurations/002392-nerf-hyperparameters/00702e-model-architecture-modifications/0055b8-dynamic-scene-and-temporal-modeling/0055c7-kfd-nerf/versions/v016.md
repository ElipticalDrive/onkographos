# KFD-NeRF

Also called: Kalman Filter Guided Deformation Field NeRF, Kalman Filter Guided Dynamic NeRF

## Overview
KFD-NeRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that models the 4D radiance field as a dynamic system with temporally varying states, estimated using a Kalman filter to combine observations from input data and predictions based on local linear motion equations [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [Gear-NeRF](13848-gear-nerf.md) and [NeRF with Time Parameter](14705-nerf-with-time-parameter.md) by explicitly treating motion estimation as a state estimation problem, using a Kalman gain to weight a shallow MLP-based observer against a physics-based predictor, rather than relying solely on time-conditioned feature interpolation or complex deformation networks [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D point coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, timestamps $t_i$, and previous frame deformation states $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}}, \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Estimated deformation $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ for the current frame, which warps points to a canonical space [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-plane Representation:** The canonical space is encoded using three mutually orthogonal 2D planes [S1]. Features from these planes are concatenated with raw coordinate inputs to handle coordinate shift errors [S1].
*   **Observer MLP:** A two-layer shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1].
*   **Kalman Gain Network:** A linear layer $f$ that computes the Kalman gain [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The method estimates the deformation field $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ for a point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t_i$ using the following steps [S1]:

1.  **Observer Step (Current Frame):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_i$ to obtain the mean observation $y_{t_i}$ and noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}$ [S1].
    $$ y_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_i} = \text{Observer}(\mathbf{x}, t_i) $$
2.  **Observer Step (Previous Frames):** Query the observer MLP with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_{i-1}$ to obtain $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}}$ and $\varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ [S1]. Query with $\mathbf{x}$ and $t_{i-2}$ to obtain $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}$ [S1].
3.  **Velocity Calculation:** Approximate the velocity $v_{t_i}$ using the deformation from the previous two frames [S1]:
    $$ v_{t_i} = (\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}}) / \Delta t_i $$
    where $\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ [S1].
4.  **Prediction Step:** Calculate the predicted deformation state $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using local linear motion, utilizing the previous frame's state and the calculated velocity [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}} + \Delta t_i \cdot v_{t_i} $$
    Substituting the velocity equation, this simplifies to:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} = 2\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-1}} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_{i-2}} $$
5.  **Kalman Gain Calculation:** Compute the Kalman gain $K_{t_i}$ using the linear layer $f$ that takes noise-related terms $\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}$ and timestamps $t_i, t_{i-1}$ as inputs [S1]:
    $$ K_{t_i} = f(\varepsilon_{t_i}, \varepsilon_{t_{i-1}}, t_i, t_{i-1}) $$
6.  **Update Step:** Combine the observation $y_{t_i}$ and the prediction $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}$ using the Kalman gain to produce the final deformation estimation [S1]:
    $$ \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} = \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-} + K_{t_i} (y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}^{-}) $$
7.  **Warping:** Warp all points in real space to a time-independent canonical space according to the estimated deformations $\hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i}$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def estimate_deformation(x, t_i, t_prev, t_prev_prev, observer_mlp, kalman_gain_layer):
    """
    Estimates the deformation for point x at time t_i using Kalman filtering.
    
    Args:
        x: 3D point coordinates.
        t_i: Current timestamp.
        t_prev: Previous timestamp (t_{i-1}).
        t_prev_prev: Timestamp before previous (t_{i-2}).
        observer_mlp: Two-layer MLP returning (observation, noise_terms).
        kalman_gain_layer: Linear layer returning Kalman gain.
        
    Returns:
        estimated_deformation: The final deformation vector dx_hat.
    """
    # Step 1: Observer for current frame
    y_ti, eps_ti = observer_mlp(x, t_i)
    
    # Step 2: Observer for previous frames
    dx_prev, eps_prev = observer_mlp(x, t_prev)
    dx_prev_prev, _ = observer_mlp(x, t_prev_prev)
    
    # Step 3: Velocity calculation
    delta_t = t_i - t_prev
    v_ti = (dx_prev - dx_prev_prev) / delta_t
    
    # Step 4: Prediction
    dx_pred = dx_prev + delta_t * v_ti
    
    # Step 5: Kalman Gain
    K_ti = kalman_gain_layer(eps_ti, eps_prev, t_i, t_prev)
    
    # Step 6: Update
    dx_hat = dx_pred + K_ti * (y_ti - dx_pred)
    
    return dx_hat
```

**Loss Functions:**
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of several components [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{image} + \mathcal{L}_{kf} + \mathcal{L}_{co} + \mathcal{L}_{prop} + \lambda_{tv}\mathcal{L}_{tv} $$

1.  **Kalman Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{kf}$):** Minimizes the error between the observation and the estimated deformation [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{kf} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \| y_{t_i} - \hat{d\mathbf{x}}_{t_i} \|_2^2 $$
2.  **Canonical Observation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{co}$):** Soft regularization for frame $t_0$ to normalize the difference between the canonical space and the real world [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{co} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{N}} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbb{1}(t) d\mathbf{x} $$
    where $\mathbb{1}(t) = 1$ if $t=0$ and $0$ otherwise [S1].
3.  **Total Variation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{tv}$):** Applied to each tri-plane to encourage sparse edges [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{tv}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c, i, j} (\| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i-1,j} \|_2^2 + \| \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j} - \mathbf{x}_c^{i,j-1} \|_2^2) $$
    where $c$ is a plane from the tri-plane collection $C$, and $i, j$ are indices on the plane’s resolution [S1].
4.  **Proposal Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$):** Ensures consistency between the proposal network and the learned model, derived from Mip-NeRF 360 [S1].
5.  **Image Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{image}$):** Standard rendering loss (e.g., MSE) [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Total Variation Weight ($\lambda_{tv}$):** Experimentally chosen value is $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Observer MLP Depth:** Two layers [S1].
*   **Training Strategy:** Gradually release training images in chronological order to ensure previous frames offer ample priors for current frame learning [S1].
*   **Weighting Dynamics:** During initial training stages, predictions primarily influence the process to facilitate convergence for frames with substantial motion. During later training stages, observations take precedence to recover precise and fine-grained motion details [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Coordinate Shift Errors:** Finite resolution tri-planes introduce coordinate shift errors. Mitigated by concatenating encoded tri-plane features with raw coordinate inputs [S1].
*   **Hard Masking:** Unlike D-NeRF, which hard-masks deformation at $t_0$ to zero, KFD-NeRF uses soft regularization to avoid making the canonical space too complex for warping [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance on Synthetic Data:** PSNR 35.73 dB, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.045, Average 0.0131 [S1].
*   **Performance on Real Data:** PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, LPIPS 0.115, Average 0.0389 [S1].
*   **Training Time:** 30 minutes for KFD-NeRF-S and 3 hours for KFD-NeRF-L on the tested datasets, compared to 48 hours for D-NeRF [S1].
*   **Parameter Size:** 175 MB for KFD-NeRF-L [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Faster convergence and lower memory footprint than MLP-based spatial representations due to the tri-plane representation. The Kalman filter mechanism allows for accurate deformation estimation by balancing historical predictions with current observations [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of locally linear motion for the predictor, which may not hold for highly non-linear or chaotic motions [S1].

## Background
KFD-NeRF was introduced by Li, Niu, Zhong, Nobuhara, Nishino, and Zheng in 2024 [S1]. It builds on the idea of modeling 4D radiance fields as dynamic systems, drawing inspiration from control theory and the Kalman filter [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous dynamic NeRF methods that either disregard motion priors or use complex deformation fields that are hard to fit [S1].
