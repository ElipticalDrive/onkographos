# S3IM (Stochastic Structural SIMilarity)

Also called: Multiplex Loss, S3IM loss

## Overview
S3IM is a multiplex regularization term for neural fields that captures nonlocal structural similarity by processing multiple data points (pixels/rays) as a whole set, rather than individually [S1]. It addresses the limitation of point-wise losses (like MSE) which fail to utilize the collective supervision of distant pixels [S1]. Unlike conventional MSE loss, S3IM cannot be expressed as the sum of any other loss computed over individual pixels from the batch [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A minibatch of rays $\mathcal{R}$, ground-truth pixel colors $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$, rendered pixel colors $\hat{\mathcal{C}} = \{\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$, and model parameters $\Theta$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** The S3IM loss value $L_{\mathrm{S3IM}}(\Theta)$ and the total multiplex loss $L_{\mathrm{M}}(\Theta)$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   $\mathcal{R}$: Set of rays in the minibatch with size $B$ (batch size) [S1].
*   $\mathcal{C}, \hat{\mathcal{C}}$: Vectors of color values corresponding to rays in $\mathcal{R}$ [S1].
*   $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}$: Stochastic patch generation function for iteration $m$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $B$: Batch size (number of rays/pixels per minibatch). Typical value: 1024 [S1].
*   $K$: Kernel size for SSIM computation. Typical value: $4 \times 4$ [S1].
*   $s$: Stride size for SSIM sliding window. Typical value: $K$ (non-overlapping) [S1].
*   $M$: Number of stochastic estimations (iterations). Typical value: 10 [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Hyperparameter adjusting the importance of S3IM relative to MSE [S1].
*   $K_1, K_2$: SSIM constants. Typical values: $K_1 = 0.01, K_2 = 0.03$ [S1].
*   $L$: Dynamic range of pixel values. Typical value: 1 for normalized RGB [S1].

**Formulas:**
The total multiplex loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ L_{\mathrm{M}}(\Theta) = \frac{1}{\|\mathcal{R}\|}\sum_{\bm{r}\in\mathcal{R}} l_{\mathrm{MSE}}(\Theta, \bm{r}) + \lambda L_{\mathrm{S3IM}}(\Theta, \mathcal{R}) $$
where $l_{\mathrm{MSE}}$ is the per-pixel MSE and $L_{\mathrm{S3IM}}$ is the S3IM loss [S1].

The S3IM loss is computed as one minus the average of $M$ SSIM estimators over stochastic patches [S1]:
$$ L_{\mathrm{S3IM}}(\Theta, \mathcal{R}) = 1 - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathrm{SSIM}(\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\hat{\mathcal{C}}), \mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\mathcal{C})) $$
where $\mathrm{SSIM}$ is the structural similarity index [S1].

SSIM is defined as the product of luminance, contrast, and structure terms [S1]:
$$ \mathrm{SSIM}(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = l(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) \, c(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) \, s(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) $$
where $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are the flattened vectors of pixel values in the stochastic patches [S1].

The luminance, contrast, and structure comparison terms are [S1]:
$$ l(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{2\mu_a\mu_b + C_1}{\mu_a^2 + \mu_b^2 + C_1} $$
$$ c(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{2\sigma_a\sigma_b + C_2}{\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_b^2 + C_2} $$
$$ s(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{\sigma_{ab} + C_3}{\sigma_a\sigma_b + C_3} $$

Constants for SSIM are [S1]:
$$ C_1 = (K_1 L)^2, \quad C_2 = (K_2 L)^2, \quad C_3 = C_2 / 2 $$

**Steps:**
1. Sample a data minibatch of rays $\mathcal{R}$ from the dataset [S1].
2. Obtain ground-truth pixels $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$ and compute rendered pixels $\hat{\mathcal{C}} = \{\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$ [S1].
3. Loop $m$ from 1 to $M$: initialize stochastic patch generation function $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}$ [S1].
4. Transform rendered pixels into rendered stochastic patch $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\hat{\mathcal{C}})$ and ground-truth pixels into ground-truth stochastic patch $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\mathcal{C})$ [S1].
5. Compute SSIM between $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\hat{\mathcal{C}})$ and $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\mathcal{C})$ using kernel size $K \times K$ and stride $s=K$ [S1].
6. Average the $M$ computed SSIM values to obtain the final S3IM similarity score [S1].
7. Compute S3IM loss as $1 - \text{Average SSIM}$ [S1].
8. Compute total multiplex loss $L_{\mathrm{M}}$ using the formula above [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_s3im_loss(rendered_colors, gt_colors, batch_size, kernel_size, num_iterations, lambda_weight):
    """
    Computes the S3IM multiplex loss.
    Args:
        rendered_colors: Array of shape (batch_size, channels) containing rendered pixel colors.
        gt_colors: Array of shape (batch_size, channels) containing ground truth pixel colors.
        batch_size: Number of rays/pixels in the minibatch (B).
        kernel_size: Size of the local window for SSIM (K).
        num_iterations: Number of stochastic estimations (M).
        lambda_weight: Hyperparameter for S3IM importance (lambda).
    Returns:
        total_loss: The total multiplex loss value.
    """
    channels = len(rendered_colors[0])
    
    # Step 1: Compute standard MSE loss
    mse_sum = 0.0
    for i in range(batch_size):
        for c in range(channels):
            mse_sum += (rendered_colors[i][c] - gt_colors[i][c]) ** 2
    mse_loss = mse_sum / (batch_size * channels)
    
    ssim_sum = 0.0
    grid_side = int(batch_size ** 0.5)
    
    for m in range(num_iterations):
        # Step 3-4: Stochastic patch generation
        # Randomly permute pixel indices to break positional relationships
        indices = list(range(batch_size))
        for i in range(batch_size - 1, 0, -1):
            j = random_integer(0, i)
            indices[i], indices[j] = indices[j], indices[i]
            
        # Reshape permuted pixels into a 2D grid
        grid_rendered = []
        grid_gt = []
        for y in range(grid_side):
            row_r = []
            row_g = []
            for x in range(grid_side):
                idx = y * grid_side + x
                row_r.append(rendered_colors[indices[idx]])
                row_g.append(gt_colors[indices[idx]])
            grid_rendered.append(row_r)
            grid_gt.append(row_g)
            
        # Step 5: Compute SSIM over the stochastic patch with stride s = K
        ssim_window_sum = 0.0
        num_windows = (grid_side // kernel_size) ** 2
        
        for y in range(0, grid_side, kernel_size):
            for x in range(0, grid_side, kernel_size):
                # Extract KxK window and flatten
                a = []
                b = []
                for dy in range(kernel_size):
                    for dx in range(kernel_size):
                        a.append(grid_rendered[y + dy][x + dx])
                        b.append(grid_gt[y + dy][x + dx])
                        
                # Compute local statistics
                n = len(a)
                mu_a = sum(a) / n
                mu_b = sum(b) / n
                var_a = sum((v - mu_a) ** 2 for v in a) / n
                var_b = sum((v - mu_b) ** 2 for v in b) / n
                cov_ab = sum((a[k] - mu_a) * (b[k] - mu_b) for k in range(n)) / n
                
                # Constants
                C1 = (0.01 * 1.0) ** 2
                C2 = (0.03 * 1.0) ** 2
                C3 = C2 / 2.0
                
                # SSIM components
                l = (2 * mu_a * mu_b + C1) / (mu_a ** 2 + mu_b ** 2 + C1)
                c = (2 * (var_a ** 0.5) * (var_b ** 0.5) + C2) / (var_a + var_b + C2)
                s = (cov_ab + C3) / ((var_a ** 0.5) * (var_b ** 0.5) + C3)
                ssim_window_sum += l * c * s
                
        ssim_sum += ssim_window_sum / num_windows
        
    # Step 6-7: Average SSIM and compute S3IM loss
    avg_ssim = ssim_sum / num_iterations
    s3im_loss = 1.0 - avg_ssim
    
    # Step 8: Total multiplex loss
    total_loss = mse_loss + lambda_weight * s3im_loss
    return total_loss
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Stochastic Sampling:** Stochastic sampling breaks positional relationships [S1]. The method accounts for this by treating the sampled pixels as a 'stochastic patch' rather than a spatially coherent image patch [S1].
*   **Stride:** Non-overlapping strides ($s=K$) are used to ensure independent samples within the batch [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** If the batch size $B$ is smaller than the kernel size $K \times K$, the patch generation is undefined [S1]. Typical batch size 1024 and kernel $4 \times 4$ ensures $B \gg K^2$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The S3IM loss requires $M$ iterations of SSIM computation per batch [S1]. Each iteration computes SSIM over $B/K^2$ non-overlapping windows of size $K \times K$, yielding $O(M \cdot B)$ operations per batch [S1]. This is added to the standard MSE computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the batch of rays and rendered pixels [S1]. No additional large data structures are needed beyond the patch vectors [S1].
*   **Performance:** S3IM improves quality metrics significantly, e.g., test MSE loss drops by more than 90% for TensoRF and DVGO over eight novel view synthesis tasks [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Captures nonlocal structural similarity; improves NeRF and neural surface representation quality; demonstrates "unreasonable effectiveness" for difficult tasks [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Adds computational overhead due to $M$ iterations; relies on stochastic sampling which may introduce variance (mitigated by averaging over $M$) [S1].
*   **When to use:** When structural similarity is more important than pixel-wise accuracy; for difficult tasks like novel view synthesis where global structure matters [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and $M$ iterations are prohibitive; when pixel-wise fidelity is the sole objective and structural coherence is irrelevant [S1].

## Background
S3IM was introduced in the paper "S3IM: Stochastic Structural SIMilarity and Its Unreasonable Effectiveness for Neural Fields" [S1]. It is the first multiplex training paradigm for NeRF and relevant neural field methods that processes multiple data points as a whole set instead of independently [S1]. The method was developed to address the failure of point-wise losses to use collective supervision of distant pixels [S1].
