# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Supervised NeRF, Geometric Priors

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of regularization techniques that incorporate geometric constraints—such as depth maps, 3D points, or structural consistency—into the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity and prevent degenerate solutions [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. Unlike methods that rely solely on photometric reconstruction, these approaches enforce geometric plausibility by aligning rendered depth distributions with external priors (e.g., SfM points, monocular estimates) or internal consistency metrics [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md]. This family is distinct from [Diffusion-regularized NeRF](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md) by using explicit geometric targets rather than generative image priors, and from [Volumetric Supervision](11100-volumetric-supervision.md) by focusing on surface/depth alignment rather than volume density constraints.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the augmented loss structure defined by [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md), where the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ combines the photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ with geometric regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{geom}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}} $$

The geometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ is computed by comparing rendered geometric quantities (depth, opacity, or 3D points) against supervision signals. These signals may be:
1.  **Explicit Depth:** Rendered expected depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ compared against ground truth or pseudo-depth $D(\mathbf{r})$ [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md].
2.  **3D Points:** Euclidean distance between backprojected rendered depth and sparse 3D keypoints [S1, 12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md].
3.  **Structural Consistency:** Relative depth ordering or smoothness between neighboring pixels [S1, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md].
4.  **Opacity/Sparsity:** Constraints on the accumulated opacity or density distribution to remove floaters [S1, 11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md].

Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss preventing degenerate solutions (e.g., copying training images) in few-view settings by enforcing foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness. It derives constraints solely from the rendering process and camera frustums, requiring no external depth sensors. Choose this when training on sparse views without reliable depth priors.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured scenes by adapting the sampling range of rays using monocular depth priors and multi-view consistency errors. It guides *sampling locations* rather than acting as a direct loss term, concentrating points around plausible geometry. Choose this when standard uniform sampling fails in textureless regions.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Enforces geometric consistency by adding a depth loss term based on sparse 3D keypoints from Structure-from-Motion (SfM). It minimizes the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and the noisy depth distribution of SfM points. Choose this when sparse SfM keypoints are available and generalization to any scene is required.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A regularization term mitigating geometry error degradation when using dense but inaccurate pseudo-depth maps. It uses a piecewise function (quadratic for small errors, logarithmic for large errors) to reduce sensitivity to outliers compared to standard L2 loss. Choose this when pseudo-depth maps contain significant noise or outliers.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity in rendered depth maps by aligning relative depth relationships of neighboring pixels with external depth priors (e.g., DepthAnythingV2). It focuses on structural consistency (ordering/smoothness) rather than absolute depth matching. Choose this when absolute depth accuracy is less critical than structural coherence.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity by injecting synthetic rays from unobserved viewpoints into the training loop. It explicitly guides optimization toward geometrically plausible solutions by regularizing regions without direct supervision. Choose this to reduce overfitting in sparse-view regimes without external depth data.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit constraints to opacity parameters to enforce geometric compactness and binary opacity values. It solves the problem of "floaters" (translucent artifacts) by encouraging sparsity in empty regions and sharp transitions at surfaces. Choose this to remove floaters and improve structural fidelity.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the Euclidean distance between 3D points backprojected from rendered depth and ground-truth 3D points. It enforces global geometric connections in the world coordinate frame, preventing disjoint local solutions. Choose this for robust camera pose registration in sparse-view regimes where photometric loss fails.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Treats arbitrary rays (e.g., shadow rays) as "ray sensors" to constrain the neural field, extending supervision beyond camera rays. It leverages non-camera rays to provide additional geometric constraints. Choose this when incorporating lighting/shadow information or non-camera viewpoints is beneficial.

### [Depth Refinement and Filtering](14242-depth-refinement-and-filtering.md)
Not given in the sources.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting strategy for dynamic street scenes, distinguishing between static background and dynamic foreground (vehicles). It applies geometry-aware confidence to backgrounds and RGB/SSIM-only metrics to vehicles, improving novel view synthesis quality. Choose this for autonomous driving datasets with unreliable depth for moving objects.

### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
$$ \mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2 $$
where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S2].
$$ L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}\right) \Delta t_k $$
where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2]. The kernel $\epsilon(x)$ integrates to one with a bounded domain [S2]. The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S2].

### Direct Depth Supervision
A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S2]. First, a batch of $N_r$ rays is sampled, then the rendered depth and ground truth depth are compared with different loss functions (MSE or L1) [S2].

### GeoAug
An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The concept of depth supervision in NeRF originated with [Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF)](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md), which introduced sparse 3D point supervision to improve convergence and geometry in sparse-view settings [S1, S3]. Subsequent works expanded this to include monocular depth priors [S1, 7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md], robust loss functions for noisy pseudo-depth [S1, 9368-robust-depth-loss.md], and structural consistency constraints [S1, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md]. The family has been applied to various domains, including few-view synthesis [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md], autonomous driving [S1, 15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md], and general scene reconstruction [S1, 11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md]. Data augmentation techniques like GeoAug also leverage depth regularization to improve robustness [S3].
