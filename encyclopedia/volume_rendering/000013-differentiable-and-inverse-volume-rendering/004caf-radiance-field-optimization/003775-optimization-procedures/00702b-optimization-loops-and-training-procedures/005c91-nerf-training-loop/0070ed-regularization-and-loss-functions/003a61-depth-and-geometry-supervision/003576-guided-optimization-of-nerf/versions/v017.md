# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes by constraining the sampling range of points along camera rays using adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which samples uniformly across a fixed frustum and often converges to geometrically incorrect but radiance-consistent solutions on texture-less surfaces, this method dynamically adjusts the near and far bounds of sampling based on multi-view consistency errors of the depth priors [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md) by not requiring ground-truth depth for optimization, instead using the priors solely to guide the sampling distribution during the standard RGB-only optimization loop [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $N$ input views with RGB images and camera poses.
*   Adapted monocular depth priors $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$ for each view, obtained from a finetuned monocular depth network [S1].
*   Camera intrinsics $K$ and relative poses $T^{i \to j}$ [S1].
*   Hyperparameters: $K=4$ (number of cross-view errors used for error map calculation), $\alpha_l = 0.05$ (lower bound), $\alpha_h = 0.15$ (upper bound), $M$ (number of samples per ray) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized NeRF parameters (implicit radiance field $\mathcal{F}_\theta$) [S1].
*   Final depth map refined by confidence-based plane bilateral filtering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model:** A fully connected network $\mathcal{F}_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \to (c, \sigma)$ mapping 3D location $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $c$ and density $\sigma$ [S1].
*   **Error Map:** A per-pixel scalar map $e_i$ for each view $i$, representing the reprojection error of the depth priors [S1].
*   **Confidence Map:** A per-pixel scalar map $S^i_j$ representing the reliability of the depth prediction at pixel $j$ in view $i$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j$ using the projection function $proj(K, T^{i \to j}, D^i)$ to obtain 2D coordinates $p^{i \to j}$ and projected depth $D^{i \to j}$ [S1]. Sample the ground-truth prior $D^j$ at these coordinates to get $D^{j'}$ [S1]. Calculate the relative reprojection error for each pixel. The error map value $e_i$ for a pixel is the average of the top $K$ minimum cross-view depth projection errors [S1].

2.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ corresponding to a pixel in view $i$, determine the near bound $t_n$ and far bound $t_f$ using the adapted depth prior $D$ at that pixel and the error map value $e$ [S1]:
    $$ t_n = D(1 - \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D(1 + \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    Here, $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ restricts $x$ to the interval $[a, b]$ [S1]. This ensures sampling is concentrated around the prior for low-error pixels and expands for high-error pixels [S1].

3.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly from the bin interval [S1]:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Evaluate the NeRF at these depths to get color $c_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Render the RGB color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and expected depth $D(\mathbf{r})$ for the ray [S1]:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i $$
    $$ D(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) t_i $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ is the distance between adjacent samples, and $T_i = \exp \left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance from $t_n$ to $t_i$ [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    Compute the photometric loss between the rendered RGB $C(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground-truth RGB image. Backpropagate through the volume rendering equation to update the NeRF parameters $\theta$ [S1]. Note that existing correspondence-based optimizations (like CVD) applied to the priors degrade quality and are not used; optimization is driven solely by RGB reconstruction [S1].

6.  **Confidence Score Calculation:**
    After optimization, compute the confidence score $S^i_j$ for each pixel $j$ in view $i$ based on the $L_1$ difference between the ground-truth RGB $C_{gt}^i(j)$ and the rendered RGB $C_{render}^i(j)$, normalized by 255 [S1]:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} || C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) ||_1 $$
    The $L_1$ norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

7.  **Depth Refinement:**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the rendered depth map, weighted by the confidence scores $S^i_j$ [S1]. This step improves depth quality in regions where the rendered RGB images were not accurate [S1].

## Subtopics

### [ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training](7580-enerf-with-guided-sampling-training.md)
Integrates pixel-region and depth-boundary-guided ray sampling into the Efficient NeRF framework to reduce GPU memory consumption and improve sampling efficiency in generalizable NeRF models.

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The method adds computational overhead during the error map calculation phase, which involves projecting depth maps between all pairs of views ($O(N^2)$ projections) and computing reprojection errors [S1]. However, during the optimization loop, the sampling range is adaptive, potentially reducing the number of samples needed to cover the relevant geometry compared to a fixed large frustum, though the number of samples $M$ remains constant per ray [S1]. The memory footprint is similar to standard NeRF, with additional storage for the error maps and confidence maps [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
On the ScanNet dataset, the method outperforms state-of-the-art depth estimation methods in all metrics, including those trained with ground-truth depth supervision (e.g., DeepV2D, DELTAS, Atlas) [S1]. It significantly outperforms original NeRF in view synthesis quality and depth accuracy [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Effectively mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes (e.g., walls) where standard NeRF fails [S1]. Does not require ground-truth depth for optimization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the adapted monocular depth priors; if the priors are poor, the guided sampling may be misleading [S1]. Applying correspondence-based optimization (like CVD) to the priors degrades quality [S1].

**When to Use:**
Use this method when optimizing NeRF for indoor scenes with texture-less regions and when monocular depth priors are available. It is particularly useful when ground-truth depth is not available for supervision [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
Avoid if the scene is rich in texture, where standard NeRF's shape-radiance bias is less problematic [S1]. Avoid if high-quality monocular depth priors cannot be obtained [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in "Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF, which assumes that the correct shape is favored due to smoother surface light fields, an assumption that fails in poorly textured indoor environments [S1]. The work builds on the original NeRF formulation [33] and the concept of shape-radiance ambiguity [61]. It differs from correspondence-based methods like CVD [29], which were found to degrade quality when applied to adapted depth priors [S1].
