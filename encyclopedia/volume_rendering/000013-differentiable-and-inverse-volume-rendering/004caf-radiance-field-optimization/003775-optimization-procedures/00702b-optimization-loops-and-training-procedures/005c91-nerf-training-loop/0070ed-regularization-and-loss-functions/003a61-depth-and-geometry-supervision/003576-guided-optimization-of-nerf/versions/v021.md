# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF optimizes a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) by restricting the sampling of points along camera rays to adaptive ranges derived from monocular depth priors, thereby mitigating shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor scenes [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF training by replacing fixed or sparse-reconstruction-based sampling bounds with error-adapted intervals, and it avoids the quality degradation associated with applying correspondence-based optimization directly to depth priors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $N$ input views with RGB images and camera intrinsics $K$.
*   Adapted monocular depth priors $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$ for each view, obtained from a finetuned monocular depth network [S1].
*   Relative poses $T^{i \rightarrow j}$ between views [S1].
*   Hyperparameters: $K=4$ (number of cross-view errors for consistency), $\alpha_l=0.05$ (lower bound), $\alpha_h=0.15$ (upper bound), $M$ (number of samples per ray) [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ defining the implicit radiance field $F_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (c, \sigma)$ [S1].
*   Refined depth maps for input and novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Error Map $e_i$:** A 2D map for view $i$, where each pixel value represents the average of the $K$ minimum cross-view reprojection errors [S1].
*   **Confidence Map $S^i$:** A 2D map for view $i$, where each pixel value $S^i_j$ is a scalar in $[0, 1]$ derived from RGB reconstruction error [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j$ using camera intrinsics $K$ and relative pose $T^{i \rightarrow j}$ to obtain projected coordinates $p^{i \rightarrow j}$ and depths $D^{i \rightarrow j}$ [S1]:
    $$ p^{i \rightarrow j}, D^{i \rightarrow j} = \text{proj}(K, T^{i \rightarrow j}, D^i) $$
    Sample the depth from view $j$ at these coordinates: $D^{j'} = D^j(p^{i \rightarrow j})$ [S1].
    Calculate the relative reprojection error between $D^j$ and $D^{i \rightarrow j}$ for overlapping pixels. For each pixel in view $i$, collect the errors from all other views, select the $K$ minimum errors, and compute their average to form the error map value $e_i$ for that pixel [S1].

2.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ in view $i$ corresponding to pixel $j$, let $D$ be the depth prior value $D^i(j)$ and $e$ be the error map value $e_i(j)$ [S1]. Compute the near and far bounds $t_n$ and $t_f$ using the clamping function $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ which limits $x$ to $[a, b]$:
    $$ t_n = D(1 - \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D(1 + \text{clamp}(e, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    This ensures sampling is concentrated around the prior for low-error pixels and expands for high-error pixels [S1].

3.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly from the bin interval [S1]:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Query the NeRF $F_\theta$ at positions $\mathbf{x}_i$ along the ray at depths $t_i$ to obtain density $\sigma_i$ and color $c_i$ [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute accumulated transmittance $T_i = \exp \left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1]. Render the RGB color $C(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $D(\mathbf{r})$ for the ray [S1]:
    $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) c_i $$
    $$ D(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) t_i $$

5.  **Compute Confidence Scores:**
    For each pixel $j$ in view $i$, compute the confidence score $S^i_j$ based on the $L_1$ difference between the ground truth RGB $C_{gt}^i(j)$ and the rendered RGB $C_{render}^i(j)$, normalized by 255 [S1]:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} \| C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) \|_1 $$
    The $L_1$ norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

6.  **Depth Refinement (Post-Processing):**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the predicted depth map, weighted by the confidence scores $S^i$, to produce the final refined depth output [S1].

7.  **Optimization Loop:**
    Backpropagate gradients through the volume rendering equations to update the NeRF parameters $\theta$ using the rendered RGB loss against ground truth images [S1].

## Subtopics

### [ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training](7580-enerf-with-guided-sampling-training.md)
ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training is a training procedure that integrates pixel-region and depth-boundary-guided ray sampling into the Efficient Neural Radiance Field (ENeRF) framework. It solves the problem of high GPU memory consumption and inefficient sampling in generalizable NeRF models by using fewer rays per iteration while maintaining or improving rendering performance. It sets itself apart from standard ENeRF by actively guiding sampling locations using adapted monocular depth priors, thereby mitigating shape-radiance ambiguity more effectively than uniform or random sampling strategies.

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The method adds a pre-processing step for error map computation involving cross-view projections, which scales with $O(N^2 \cdot H \cdot W)$ for $N$ views of resolution $H \times W$ [S1]. The training loop itself uses stratified sampling within adaptive bounds, which is computationally similar to standard NeRF but may require fewer samples $M$ to achieve accurate geometry due to the constrained range [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
On the ScanNet dataset, the method outperforms state-of-the-art depth estimation methods in all metrics, including those trained with ground truth depth supervision (e.g., DeepV2D, DELTAS, Atlas) [S1]. It significantly outperforms original NeRF in view synthesis quality and depth accuracy [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Effectively mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor areas (e.g., walls) where standard NeRF fails [S1]. It allows for accurate depth estimation by directly optimizing over RGB images without requiring explicit correspondence-based optimization on the priors, which can degrade quality [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the adapted monocular depth priors. Applying existing correspondence-based neural optimization (like CVD) directly to these priors degrades quality due to unreliable flow correspondences in poorly textured regions [S1].

**When to Use:**
Use for indoor multi-view stereo and depth estimation where texture is sparse and standard NeRF suffers from geometric inaccuracies [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
Avoid if high-quality monocular depth priors are not available or if the scene is richly textured outdoors where standard NeRF geometry is already favored by the smooth surface light field assumption [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed in "Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of standard NeRF, which assumes that correct geometry is favored due to smoother surface light fields, an assumption that fails in poorly textured indoor environments [S1]. The authors observed that NeRF can synthesize high-quality RGB images with incorrect geometry in such areas, leading to depth deviation [S1]. The method builds on the concept of using monocular depth priors but introduces a guided optimization loop that uses these priors to constrain sampling ranges rather than directly optimizing correspondences [S1].
