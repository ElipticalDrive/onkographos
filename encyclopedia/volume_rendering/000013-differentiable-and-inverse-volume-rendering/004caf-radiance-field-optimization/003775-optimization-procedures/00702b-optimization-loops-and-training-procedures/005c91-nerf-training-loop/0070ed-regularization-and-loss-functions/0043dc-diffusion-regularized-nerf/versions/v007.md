# Diffusion-regularized NeRF

Also called: DDM-regularized NeRF, Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training, Diffusion-based Regularization for Neural Radiance Fields, Denoising Diffusion Model Regularization

## Overview
Diffusion-regularized NeRF (often referred to as DiffusioNeRF) is a training procedure that improves the geometric plausibility and generalization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in limited-view scenarios by incorporating a Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM) as a learned prior [S1, S2]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on hand-crafted geometric constraints or normalizing flows, this method directly regularizes the density and color fields by backpropagating the score function (gradient of the log-probability) of RGBD patches learned from synthetic data [S1]. It addresses the under-constrained nature of NeRFs, which often produce artifacts like "floaters" (semi-transparent material in mid-air) when trained on few views [S1].

## How it works
The method operates within the differentiable volume rendering framework [S1, 8582-regnerf.md]. It consists of two phases: training a DDM on synthetic RGBD patches, and using the DDM's score function as a regularization term during NeRF optimization.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images $\mathcal{I}$ with known camera poses and intrinsics; a pre-trained DDM $\epsilon_\theta$; a NeRF representation (e.g., Instant NGP) parameterized by density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\theta$ (density and color fields) [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **NeRF Representation:** The implementation uses Instant NGP, which stores feature encodings of coordinates in multi-scale hash tables within a fixed memory block [S1]. This allows varying spatial resolutions and reduces MLP size [S1].
*   **DDM:** A neural network $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau)$ that estimates noise given a noised input $\mathbf{x}_\tau$ and diffusion time $\tau$ [S1].
*   **RGBD Patches:** Random patches of pixels are rendered from the NeRF. The patch size is typically $48 \times 48$ pixels, enabled by the fast rendering of Instant NGP [S1]. Smaller patches ($24 \times 24$) may be used for specific datasets like LLFF [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Train the DDM (Pre-training):**
    *   Extract RGBD patches from a synthetic dataset (e.g., Hypersim) [S1].
    *   Train the DDM to predict the noise added during the forward diffusion process. The forward process adds Gaussian noise to a data sample $\mathbf{x}_0$:
        $$ \mathbf{x}_\tau = \sqrt{\alpha_\tau} \mathbf{x}_{\tau-1} + \sqrt{\beta_\tau} \epsilon_{\tau-1} $$
        where $\epsilon_{\tau-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and $\alpha_\tau = 1 - \beta_\tau$ [S1].
    *   The reparameterized distribution is:
        $$ q(\mathbf{x}_\tau | \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_\tau; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_\tau} \mathbf{x}_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_\tau)I) $$
        where $\bar{\alpha}_\tau = \prod_{s=0}^\tau \alpha_s$ [S1].
    *   The DDM is trained by minimizing the noise estimation loss:
        $$ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \epsilon} \left[ \frac{\beta_\tau}{2\alpha_\tau (1-\bar{\alpha}_\tau)} || \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta \left( \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_\tau} \mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_\tau} \epsilon, \tau \right) ||^2 \right] $$
        [S1].
    *   Training details: 650,000 steps, batch size 32 [S1].

2.  **NeRF Optimization Loop:**
    For each optimization step:
    1.  **Render RGBD Patches:**
        *   Cast rays through random $48 \times 48$ pixel patches from random camera poses [S1].
        *   Sample points $t_i$ along rays $r(t) = o + td$.
        *   Compute density $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_i))$ and color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t_i), \mathbf{d})$ using the current NeRF [S1].
        *   Compute opacity $\rho(t_i)$ and transmittance $T(t_i)$:
            $$ \rho(t_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_i))(t_{i+1} - t_i)) $$
            $$ T(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \rho(t_j)) $$
            [S1].
        *   Compute rendering weights $w_i = T(t_i)\rho(t_i)$ [S1].
        *   Compute expected color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$:
            $$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) \approx \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t_i), \mathbf{d}) + \left( 1 - \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \right) \mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}} $$
            $$ \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i} $$
            where $\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}$ is white [S1].
        *   *Pitfall Handling:* 25% of the time, use a training pose and sample the RGB component directly from the input training image to help early training stages where NeRF renderings are inaccurate [S1].

    2.  **Compute DDM Score:**
        *   Form the input vector $\mathbf{x}_\tau$ from the rendered RGBD patch [S1].
        *   Compute the diffusion time parameter $\tau$. It smoothly interpolates from 0.1 to 0 during the first 2500 steps of NeRF optimization [S1].
        *   Calculate $\bar{\alpha}_\tau$ using the cosine schedule:
            $$ \bar{\alpha}_\tau = \cos\left(0.5\pi \frac{\tau + 0.008}{1.008}\right) $$
            [S1].
        *   Pass $\mathbf{x}_\tau$ and $\tau$ to the trained DDM to get the noise prediction $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau)$ [S1].
        *   The DDM output is proportional to the negative score function:
            $$ \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau) \propto -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \log p(\mathbf{x}) $$
            [S1].
        *   Normalize the output of $\epsilon_\theta$ to control gradient magnitude [S1].

    3.  **Compute Total Loss Gradient:**
        *   The total loss gradient combines photometric loss, geometric regularizers, and the DDM regularizer:
            $$ \nabla \mathcal{L} = \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{fg}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} + \lambda_{\text{fr}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} + \lambda_{\text{dist}} \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} - \lambda_{\text{DDM}} \epsilon_\theta $$
            [S1].
        *   Alternatively, in simplified formulations focusing only on color:
            $$ \nabla L = \nabla L_p + \lambda_D \epsilon_D $$
            where $L_p = \| \hat{c}(r) - c_g \|^2$ [S2].

    4.  **Backpropagate:**
        *   Backpropagate $\nabla \mathcal{L}$ through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF density and color field parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

### Parameters
*   **$\lambda_{\text{DDM}}$:** Weight for the DDM regularizer. It trades off reconstruction accuracy around thin structures against overall depth smoothness [S1]. Typical value: 0.01 [S2].
*   **$\tau$:** Diffusion time parameter. Scheduled from 0.1 to 0 in the first 2500 steps [S1].
*   **Patch Size:** $48 \times 48$ pixels (standard), $24 \times 24$ pixels (alternative for LLFF) [S1].
*   **Background Color ($\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}$):** White [S1].
*   **NeRF Optimization Steps:** 12,000 steps [S1].
*   **Geometric Regularizer Weights ($\lambda_{\text{fg}}, \lambda_{\text{fr}}, \lambda_{\text{dist}}$):** $\lambda_{\text{dist}}$ is 0 for the first 2500 steps, then linearly increases to $1 \times 10^{-4}$ (DTU) or $1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ (LLFF) by step 8000 [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import numpy as np

# Assume InstantNGP-like NeRF model and Pre-trained DDM are available
class DiffusionRegularizedNeRF:
    def __init__(self, nerf_model, ddm_model, lambda_ddm=0.01, patch_size=48):
        self.nerf = nerf_model
        self.ddm = ddm_model
        self.lambda_ddm = lambda_ddm
        self.patch_size = patch_size
        self.c_bg = torch.tensor([1.0, 1.0, 1.0]) # White background
        
    def render_patch(self, rays_o, rays_d, t_near, t_far, num_samples):
        """
        Renders an RGBD patch for a set of rays.
        Returns: color (C), depth (D), weights (w)
        """
        # Sample points along rays
        t_vals = torch.linspace(t_near, t_far, num_samples)
        # Add noise or stratified sampling if needed
        points = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(0)
        
        # Query NeRF for density and color
        # sigma: [num_rays, num_samples, 1]
        # color: [num_rays, num_samples, 3]
        sigma, color = self.nerf(points)
        
        # Compute opacity and transmittance
        # delta_t: [num_rays, num_samples, 1]
        delta_t = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        delta_t = torch.cat([delta_t, torch.zeros_like(delta_t[:, :1])], dim=1)
        
        rho = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta_t)
        T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - rho + 1e-10, dim=1) # Accumulated transmittance
        # Adjust T: T[i] is transmittance BEFORE sample i
        T_shifted = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=1)
        
        w = T_shifted * rho # Rendering weights
        
        # Expected Color
        C = torch.sum(w * color, dim=1) + (1.0 - torch.sum(w, dim=1)).unsqueeze(1) * self.c_bg
        
        # Expected Depth
        D = torch.sum(w * t_vals, dim=1) / (torch.sum(w, dim=1) + 1e-10)
        
        return C, D, w

    def compute_ddm_score(self, rgb_patch, depth_patch, step, total_steps):
        """
        Computes the DDM score (negative gradient of log probability)
        for the given RGBD patch.
        """
        # Combine RGB and Depth into input x
        # Assuming RGB is [H, W, 3] and Depth is [H, W, 1]
        # Normalize depth if necessary
        x = torch.cat([rgb_patch, depth_patch], dim=-1)
        
        # Compute diffusion time tau
        # Interpolate tau from 0.1 to 0 in first 2500 steps
        if step < 2500:
            tau = 0.1 * (1.0 - step / 2500.0)
        else:
            tau = 0.0
            
        # Compute alpha_bar_tau
        # alpha_bar = cos(0.5 * pi * (tau + 0.008) / 1.008)
        alpha_bar = torch.cos(0.5 * np.pi * (tau + 0.008) / 1.008)
        
        # Get noise prediction from DDM
        # epsilon_theta: [H, W, 4] (RGB + Depth)
        epsilon_theta = self.ddm(x, tau)
        
        # Normalize epsilon_theta to control gradient magnitude [S1]
        # Implementation detail: normalize by L2 norm or similar
        epsilon_theta = epsilon_theta / (epsilon_theta.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True) + 1e-8)
        
        return epsilon_theta

    def train_step(self, rays_o, rays_d, t_near, t_far, num_samples, training_image_rgb=None, use_training_image=False):
        """
        Performs one training step.
        """
        # 1. Render RGBD Patch
        C, D, w = self.render_patch(rays_o, rays_d, t_near, t_far, num_samples)
        
        # Reshape C and D to patch format [H, W, 3] and [H, W, 1]
        # Assuming rays_o/d correspond to a grid of pixels
        rgb_patch = C.view(self.patch_size, self.patch_size, 3)
        depth_patch = D.view(self.patch_size, self.patch_size, 1)
        
        # 2. Handle Early Training / Input Image Sampling
        if use_training_image and training_image_rgb is not None:
            # Sample RGB directly from training image
            rgb_patch = training_image_rgb.view(self.patch_size, self.patch_size, 3)
            
        # 3. Compute DDM Score
        ddm_score = self.compute_ddm_score(rgb_patch, depth_patch, step, total_steps)
        
        # 4. Compute Photometric Loss (L2 between rendered and ground truth if available)
        # Note: In the regularization context, the DDM term is added to the gradient.
        # If ground truth is available for the patch, compute L2 loss.
        # If not, the DDM acts as the primary prior.
        # Here we assume a standard photometric loss component exists.
        # L_photo = || C - C_gt ||^2
        # grad_L_photo = ... (computed via backprop through render_patch)
        
        # 5. Compute Total Gradient
        # The DDM term is subtracted because epsilon_theta is proportional to -grad log p
        # grad_L_total = grad_L_photo - lambda_ddm * grad_L_DDM
        # grad_L_DDM is the gradient of the DDM output w.r.t NeRF params
        
        # Backpropagate DDM score to NeRF
        # ddm_score depends on C and D, which depend on NeRF params
        ddm_score.backward(retain_graph=True)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Early Training Inaccuracy:** NeRF renderings are inaccurate early in training. To mitigate this, 25% of the time, a training pose is used, and the RGB component is sampled directly from the training image [S1].
*   **Over-smoothing:** The DDM regularizer can cause over-smoothing of thin structures [S1].
*   **Hallucination:** The method may hallucinate details in scene regions not visible in any training view, which can negatively impact reconstruction scores [S1].
*   **Patch Size Sensitivity:** A $24 \times 24$ patch size outperforms $48 \times 48$ on the LLFF dataset but underperforms on DTU [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds the cost of rendering RGBD patches and passing them through the DDM to each NeRF optimization step. Instant NGP's fast rendering mitigates the patch rendering cost [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the DDM parameters and the Instant NGP hash tables [S1].
*   **Performance:** Improves geometry quality and generalization to novel views on LLFF and DTU datasets compared to baselines without DDM regularization [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates "floaters" and low-quality geometry in limited-view scenarios; leverages strong priors from synthetic data [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Can over-smooth thin structures; may hallucinate details in occluded regions; requires careful scheduling of $\tau$ and $\lambda_{\text{DDM}}$ [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When training NeRFs with limited input views and seeking improved geometric plausibility [S1, S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** When high-fidelity reconstruction of thin structures is critical and over-smoothing is unacceptable; when computational resources for DDM inference are limited [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed in "DiffusioNeRF: Regularizing Neural Radiance Fields with Denoising Diffusion Models" (CVPR 2023) [S1].
*   **Development:** Builds on the understanding that NeRFs are under-constrained with few views, leading to artifacts [S1]. It leverages the connection between DDM noise estimation and score matching [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented using torch-ngp (Instant NGP) with tiny-cuda-tiny backend [S1]. Evaluated on LLFF and DTU datasets [S1]. Also referenced in generalizable NeRF frameworks trained on DTU [S2].
