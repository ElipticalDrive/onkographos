# Regularization and Loss Functions


## Overview
This family of methods addresses the optimization instability, geometric degradation, and high-frequency detail loss inherent in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training. While standard 3DGS relies on image-based reconstruction losses, these techniques introduce explicit geometric constraints, frequency-domain penalties, and physical priors to stabilize convergence and improve fidelity, particularly under sparse views or high-resolution conditions. They are distinguished from sibling methods like [Pruning and Density Control](16215-pruning-and-density-control.md) (which manages primitive count) and [Optimization and Training Loop](17791-optimization-and-training-loop.md) (which manages the solver schedule) by focusing specifically on the mathematical formulation of the loss landscape and regularization terms applied to the Gaussian parameters.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism across these methods is the augmentation of the standard photometric loss ($L_{rgb}$) with auxiliary terms that enforce specific properties on the Gaussian primitives or the rendered output. These auxiliary losses typically target:
1.  **Geometric Consistency:** Enforcing multi-view consistency of features or normals to prevent geometry collapse when view coverage is limited.
2.  **Frequency Regularization:** Penalizing high-frequency artifacts or encouraging the recovery of fine details that are often blurred by the Gaussian kernel.
3.  **Physical Priors:** Constraining primitive sizes or orientations to align with physical scene structures (e.g., surfaces, edges).

The general optimization objective is formulated as:
$$ L_{total} = L_{rgb} + \sum_i \lambda_i L_{aux}^{(i)} $$
where $L_{rgb}$ is the standard reconstruction loss, $L_{aux}^{(i)}$ are the specific auxiliary losses defined by the variant, and $\lambda_i$ are hyperparameters balancing the terms. The gradients from these losses are backpropagated through the differentiable splatting renderer to update the Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics).

## Subtopics

### Frequency Gaussian Splatting
A method that applies frequency-domain regularization to the rendered 2D image to encourage recovery of high-frequency detail, resolving blurring issues [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the inherent low-pass filtering effect of Gaussian splatting by penalizing discrepancies in the frequency domain between the rendered and ground-truth images.

### Direct Gaussian Primitive Regularization
A regularization method that aligns 2D Gaussian primitives (orientation $R$, scale $S$, position $p$) with latent scene surfaces using multi-view consistency on sampled points [S2]. It overcomes the limitation of point-based consistency by propagating gradients to orientation and scale via a reparameterization-based disk sampling strategy.

**Step**
1.  Sample $K$ points $Z = \{z_k\}$ from the standard Gaussian distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ on each disk-shaped primitive [S2].
2.  Transform these points to the primitive's local space using position $p$, orientation $R$, and scale $S$: $Z = p + LZ'$, where $L = RS$ and $Z' \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ [S2].
3.  Project the transformed 3D points $Z$ to source and target views to obtain 2D positions $q_{src}$ and $q_{tar}$ [S2].
4.  Query Multi-View Stereo (MVS) features $F_{src}(q_{src})$ and $F_{tar}(q_{tar})$ at the projected 2D positions [S2].

**Formula**
Compute point position loss $L_{df}$ via cosine similarity over $K$ points [S2]:
$$ L_{df} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(1 - \frac{F_{src}(q_k^{src}) \cdot F_{tar}(q_k^{tar})}{\|F_{src}(q_k^{src})\| \|F_{tar}(q_k^{tar})\|}\right) $$
Compute normal loss $L_{dn}$ to supervise the normal vector $R[..., 2]$ using rendered normal $n$ [S2]:
$$ L_{dn} = 1 - R[..., 2]^\top n $$
Combine losses for final primitive regularization [S2]:
$$ L_{dr} = L_{df} + L_{dn} $$

**Pitfall**
The last scale value of 2D Gaussian primitives is zero, making it difficult to optimize the normal vector $R[..., 2]$ in the last dimension [S2].

### Geometrically Enhanced Supervision
A supervision technique that incorporates off-the-shelf Multi-View Stereo (MVS) features into Gaussian primitives to prevent geometric degradation under sparse views, where color alone overfits [S2]. It fixes color and feature values during optimization to enforce learning specifically for geometry.

**Step**
1.  Extract feature map $F_i$ from the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) of CLMVSNet for each input image $I_i$ [S2].
2.  Sample both RGB color $c_{ij}$ and MVS feature $f_{ij}$ into each Gaussian primitive at the projected 3D point $p_{ij}$ using the projection function $\pi(\cdot)$: $f_{ij} = F_i(\pi(p_{ij}))$, $c_{ij} = I_i(\pi(p_{ij}))$ [S2].
3.  Fix the values of $f_{ij}$ and $c_{ij}$ during optimization to avoid overfitting and enforce geometric learning [S2].

**Formula**
Rendered feature $\hat{f}$ is computed via alpha compositing over $M$ primitives [S2]:
$$ \hat{f} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} f_m \cdot \alpha_m \prod_{o=1}^{m-1} (1 - \alpha_m) $$
where $\alpha_m$ is opacity. The feature loss is computed as cosine similarity [S2]:
$$ L_{fea} = 1 - \frac{f_{ij} \cdot \hat{f}_{ij}}{\|f_{ij}\| \|\hat{f}_{ij}\|} $$

**Pitfall**
Simultaneous learning of appearance (color) and feature with geometry causes geometric degradation under sparse views [S2].

### DropoutGS
A plug-in approach that combines Random Dropout Regularization (RDR) and Edge-guided Splitting Strategy (ESS) to improve the generalization performance of existing 3DGS methods under sparse views [S3]. It alleviates overfitting by randomly dropping Gaussians during optimization and encourages focus on edge regions for finer details [S3].

**Parameter**
The method is compatible with diverse 3DGS-based methods [S3].

### PrismGS
A physically-grounded regularization framework for large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting that mitigates aliasing artifacts (flickering textures, jagged edges) and optimization instability in high-resolution (e.g., 4K) urban environments by enforcing cross-scale consistency and geometric stability [S4]. It uses pyramidal multi-scale supervision and explicit size regularization to prevent degenerate primitives [S4].

### GSurf Total Loss Optimization
A method that optimizes both Gaussians and MLPs (for SDF and appearance) using a combined loss function [S5]. It initializes Gaussians randomly or from SfM point clouds and adopts densification strategies from 2DGS [S5].

**Step**
1.  Optimize both Gaussians and MLPs using the Adam optimizer on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU [S5].
2.  Initialize 3D Gaussians randomly for OmniObjects3D dataset, or from sparse, noisy Structure-from-Motion (SfM) point clouds for DTU dataset [S5].
3.  Adopt the same densification strategies as those used in 2DGS [S5].

**Formula**
The total loss function is defined as [S5]:
$$ L_{total} = L_{gs} + L_{sdf} + L_{ent} $$

## Background
Regularization in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as the community recognized that standard photometric losses ($L_1$ or SSIM) are insufficient for maintaining geometric integrity, especially in sparse-view scenarios or high-frequency details. Early works like [Frequency Gaussian Splatting] (cited in [S1, unofficial]) and [Direct Gaussian Primitive Regularization] ([S2]) addressed the blurring and geometric degradation by introducing frequency-domain and multi-view consistency losses. [Geometrically Enhanced Supervision] ([S2]) highlighted the conflict between appearance and geometry learning, proposing fixed features to stabilize geometry. [DropoutGS] ([S3]) applied neural network regularization techniques (dropout) to the discrete Gaussian set. [PrismGS] ([S4]) extended this to large-scale urban scenes, addressing aliasing through physical priors. [GSurf] ([S5]) integrated explicit surface representations (SDF) with Gaussian splatting, requiring a composite loss function. These methods collectively shift the training objective from pure image reconstruction to geometry-aware and physically-consistent optimization.
