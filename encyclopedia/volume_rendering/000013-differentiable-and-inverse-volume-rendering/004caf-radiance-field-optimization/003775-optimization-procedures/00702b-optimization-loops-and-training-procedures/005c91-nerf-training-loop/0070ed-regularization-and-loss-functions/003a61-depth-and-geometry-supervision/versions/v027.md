# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Supervised NeRF

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision methods enhance Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) optimization by incorporating explicit geometric constraints, such as depth priors or structural consistency, into the training loop. Unlike standard photometric reconstruction which suffers from shape-radiance ambiguity in sparse-view or textureless settings, these methods enforce that the rendered depth distribution aligns with known geometric structures [S1, S2]. They distinguish themselves from sibling methods like [Diffusion-regularized NeRF](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md) by relying on explicit geometric signals (depth, points, or structural priors) rather than generative image priors, and from [Volumetric Supervision](11100-volumetric-supervision.md) by focusing on the alignment of the ray termination distribution with external geometry rather than just volume density constraints.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the differentiable volume rendering framework where gradients flow from the loss $\mathcal{L}$ back through the rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\hat{\mathbf{D}}(\mathbf{r})$ to the network parameters $\theta$ (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md].

The rendering process computes expected color and depth along rays $r(t) = o + td$ using sample points $t_i$ with weights $w_i = T(t_i)\rho(t_i)$, where $\rho(t_i) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t_i))(t_{i+1} - t_i))$ and $T(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \rho(t_j))$ [S1]. The rendering weights $w_i$ serve as the probability distribution of absorption along the ray, often denoted as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, which is used in distributional geometric losses [S1, S2].

The rendered depth is typically the expected distance along the ray:
$$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i $$
where $w_i = T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i))$ are the rendering weights, $t_i$ are sample depths, and $T_i$ is the accumulated transmittance [S1, S2].

Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the density and color field parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss function designed to prevent degenerate solutions (e.g., "image copying") in few-view settings. It combines photometric loss with three geometric priors: foreground completeness (forcing $\sum w_i = 1$), frustum consistency (penalizing density visible in only one view), and distribution compactness (encouraging weights to cluster around the expected depth). It is chosen when training on sparse views where standard NeRF collapses to memorizing input images.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Optimizes NeRF by restricting sampling to adaptive ranges derived from monocular depth priors. It replaces fixed sampling bounds with error-adapted intervals based on cross-view reprojection errors. It is chosen for poorly textured indoor scenes where standard sampling is inefficient or inaccurate, and it avoids the quality degradation of applying correspondence-based optimization directly to depth priors.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Accelerates optimization and improves quality in sparse-view settings by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints (SfM). It enforces that the ray termination distribution matches surface priors via KL divergence. It is chosen when sparse 3D keypoints are available, as it requires no additional training of external priors and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A geometric regularization term using dense but potentially inaccurate pseudo-depth maps. It uses a piecewise loss that is quadratic for small errors and logarithmic for large errors, making it less sensitive to outliers than standard L2 loss. It is chosen when using pseudo-depth (e.g., from monocular estimators) that may contain significant noise or errors in dynamic regions.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity constraints on rendered depth maps using external dense depth priors (e.g., DepthAnythingV2). It uses a k-nearest neighbor (KNN) approach with a boundary tolerance to ensure that if neighboring pixels in the prior are continuous, the rendered depths maintain similar structural consistency. It is chosen when high-quality dense depth priors are available and structural smoothness is critical.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity by injecting synthetic "novel view" rays into the training loop. It provides additional geometric supervision from virtual viewpoints, reducing overfitting. It is chosen when camera coverage is limited, and it is distinct from depth-supervised methods by generating supervision from virtual viewpoints rather than explicit depth sensors.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit constraints to opacity parameters to enforce geometric compactness and binary opacity values. It solves the problem of "floaters" (translucent artifacts in empty space) by encouraging sparsity in empty regions and sharp transitions at surfaces. It is chosen when the reconstruction suffers from translucent artifacts and no external depth priors are available.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the distance between 3D points backprojected from the NeRF's rendered depth and ground-truth 3D points. It enables robust camera pose registration in sparse-view regimes by enforcing a global geometric connection. It is chosen when precise 3D point correspondences are available and pose refinement is required.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Extends NeRF training by treating "ray sensors" as arbitrary geometric entities (e.g., shadow rays, unseen view rays) rather than just camera rays. It directly supervises radiance along arbitrary paths. It is chosen when supervision signals exist in non-camera-ray forms, such as shadow maps or multi-view consistency constraints from unseen views.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting mechanism that adapts the reliability of geometric and photometric supervision based on scene content (static background vs. dynamic foreground). It decouples confidence metrics for background (geometry/reprojection) and foreground (RGB/SSIM). It is chosen for street view synthesis with dynamic objects where depth map quality varies significantly between static and dynamic elements.

### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
- **Definition**: A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
- **Formula**: $\mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbf{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbf{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2$, where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
- **Definition**: A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S2].
- **Formula**: $L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}) \Delta t_k$, where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2].
- **Parameter**: $\epsilon(x)$: A kernel that integrates to one (a distribution) with a bounded domain parameterized by $\epsilon$ [S2].
- **Pitfall**: The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S2].

### Direct Depth Supervision
- **Definition**: A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S2].
- **Step**: First sample a batch of $N_r$ rays, then compare the rendered depth and ground truth depth with different loss functions (MSE or L1) [S2].

### GeoAug
- **Definition**: An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The concept of depth and geometry supervision in NeRF originated from the observation that standard NeRF optimization is prone to degenerate solutions, such as "image copying" or translucent floaters, especially in sparse-view settings [S1, S2]. Early work, such as DS-NeRF, introduced indirect supervision using KL divergence to align the ray termination distribution with sparse 3D keypoints from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1, S2]. Subsequent methods expanded this to include direct depth supervision from monocular or LiDAR priors, robust losses to handle noisy pseudo-depth, and structural consistency regularizers to enforce smoothness [S2]. More recent approaches, such as Simple-RF, have introduced composite geometric baselines to prevent collapse in few-view scenarios, while others like S-NeRF have introduced confidence-weighted supervision to handle dynamic scenes [S1, 15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md]. The field has evolved from simple depth matching to complex, multi-component geometric regularization strategies that integrate coarse-fine consistency, novel view regularization, and point-based supervision [S1, S2, S3].
