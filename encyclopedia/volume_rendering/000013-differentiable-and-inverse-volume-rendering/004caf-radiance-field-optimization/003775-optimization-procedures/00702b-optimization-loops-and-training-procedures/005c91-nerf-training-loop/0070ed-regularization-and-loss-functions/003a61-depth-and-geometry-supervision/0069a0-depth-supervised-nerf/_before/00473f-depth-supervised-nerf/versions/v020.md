# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality in sparse-view settings by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting to incorrect geometries when given insufficient input views by enforcing that the ray termination distribution matches surface priors derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires no additional training and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds, making it a flexible, "free" supervisory signal [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $\{I_j\}$ with known camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM), and the sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ and visibility flags generated by the SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) that synthesizes novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set of 3D points $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that camera [S1].
*   **Depth Uncertainty:** For each keypoint $x_i$, a scalar $\hat{\sigma}_i$ representing the average reprojection error across all views where $x_i$ was detected [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input training images to estimate camera parameters and generate sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth Estimation:** For each training image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, compute the estimated depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
3.  **Uncertainty Calculation:** Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
4.  **Ray Generation:**
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For each keypoint ray, compute the ray origin in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of the detected keypoint, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
    *   Shoot this ray into the scene [S1].
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Render the color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ for the ray $\mathbf{r}$ using standard NeRF volume rendering [S1, S4].
    *   Sample points $\mathbf{x}_k$ along the ray at distances $t_k$ from the camera origin, within near bound $t_n$ and far bound $t_f$ [S1].
    *   Compute density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}_k)$ and color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_k)$ from the NeRF [S1].
    *   Compute rendering weights $w_k = T_k (1 - \exp(-\sigma_k \delta_k))$, where $T_k = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance and $\delta_k = t_{k+1} - t_k$ [S1, S4].
    *   Rendered depth: $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{k} w_k t_k$ [S1, S4].
    *   Rendered color: $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{k} w_k \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_k)$ [S1].
6.  **Supervision Signal Interpolation:** Since image coordinates are continuous, interpolate the RGB supervision value $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ from the input image $I_j$ for the ray corresponding to the keypoint [S1].
7.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   **Color Loss:** Compute photometric loss between rendered color and interpolated ground truth: $L_{Color} = \|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2$ [S1, S4].
    *   **Depth Loss:** Model the true depth of the first surface as a random variable $D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1]. Compute the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution $h(t)$ and this noisy depth distribution [S1].
        *   Ray termination distribution: $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds)\sigma(t)$ [S1].
        *   Discretized Depth Loss:
            $$ L_{Depth} = \sum_{k} -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
            where $h_k = w_k / \Delta t_k$ is the probability mass at sample $k$, and $\Delta t_k$ is the step size [S1].
        *   *Note:* An alternative MSE loss $L_{MSE} = \|\hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) - D_{ij}\|^2$ is also possible, but KL divergence yields fewer artifacts [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   **Total Loss:** $\mathcal{L} = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth}$, where $\lambda_D$ is a hyper-parameter (typically 1) [S1, S4].
8.  **Optimization:** Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def ds_nerf_training_step(nerf_model, camera_pose, image, keypoints, keypoint_visibility, 
                          near_bound, far_bound, num_samples, lambda_depth=1.0):
    """
    One training step for DS-NeRF.
    
    Args:
        nerf_model: MLP model returning (sigma, color) for 3D points.
        camera_pose: 4x4 transformation matrix from world to camera.
        image: Ground truth RGB image tensor [H, W, 3].
        keypoints: 3D keypoints tensor [K, 3].
        keypoint_visibility: Boolean mask [K] indicating if keypoint is visible in this view.
        near_bound: Float, near clipping plane.
        far_bound: Float, far clipping plane (treated as opaque wall).
        num_samples: Int, number of samples per ray.
        lambda_depth: Float, weight for depth loss.
        
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar loss value.
    """
    device = image.device
    
    # 1. Generate rays for all pixels
    H, W, _ = image.shape
    # Create grid of pixel coordinates [H, W, 2]
    u, v = torch.meshgrid(torch.arange(H), torch.arange(W), indexing='ij')
    u = u.float().to(device)
    v = v.float().to(device)
    
    # Convert pixel coordinates to ray directions in camera space
    # Assuming standard pinhole camera with focal length f = max(H, W) / 2
    # and principal point at center.
    f = max(H, W) / 2.0
    # Ray directions in camera coordinates: [H, W, 3]
    # x = (u - W/2) / f, y = -(v - H/2) / f, z = -1
    dx = (u - W / 2.0) / f
    dy = -(v - H / 2.0) / f
    dz = -torch.ones_like(dx)
    ray_dirs_cam = torch.stack([dx, dy, dz], dim=-1) # [H, W, 3]
    
    # Transform ray origins and directions to world space
    # Ray origins are camera center
    cam_center = camera_pose[:3, 3]
    ray_dirs_world = (camera_pose[:3, :3] @ ray_dirs_cam.permute(2, 0, 1)).permute(1, 2, 0)
    ray_origins_world = cam_center.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).expand(H, W, 3)
    
    # Flatten rays
    ray_origins = ray_origins_world.reshape(-1, 3)
    ray_dirs = ray_dirs_world.reshape(-1, 3)
    
    # 2. Sample points along rays
    # Standard NeRF sampling: uniform + stratified or similar
    # For simplicity, uniform sampling between near and far
    t_vals = torch.linspace(near_bound, far_bound, num_samples).to(device)
    # Add epsilon to avoid zero-length intervals
    t_vals = t_vals + (torch.rand_like(t_vals) - 0.5) * (far_bound - near_bound) / num_samples
    t_vals = torch.sort(t_vals, dim=-1)[0]
    
    # Compute deltas
    deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
    delta_eps = torch.zeros_like(t_vals[:, :1])
    deltas = torch.cat([deltas, delta_eps], dim=-1)
    
    # Midpoints for sampling
    midpoints = (t_vals[:, :-1] + t_vals[:, 1:]) / 2.0
    
    # 3. Query NeRF for density and color
    # Points are ray_origins + ray_dirs * t_vals
    points = ray_origins.unsqueeze(1) + ray_dirs.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    points_flat = points.reshape(-1, 3)
    
    sigma_flat, color_flat = nerf_model(points_flat)
    sigma = sigma_flat.reshape(H, W, num_samples)
    color = color_flat.reshape(H, W, num_samples, 3)
    
    # Ensure non-negative density
    sigma = F.relu(sigma)
    
    # 4. Compute weights and transmittance
    # alpha = 1 - exp(-sigma * delta)
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    
    # Transmittance T_i = prod_{j<i} (1 - alpha_j)
    # T_0 = 1
    weights = alpha * torch.cumprod(torch.ones_like(alpha) - alpha + 1e-10, dim=-1)[..., :-1]
    # Note: torch.cumprod((1-alpha), dim=-1) gives T_{i+1}. 
    # Standard NeRF weight: w_i = alpha_i * T_i
    # Let's compute T explicitly for clarity
    T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha + 1e-10, dim=-1) # T_i is transmittance BEFORE sample i
    # T[:, :, 0] is 1.0 (before first sample)
    # We need T shifted right by 1, with T[:, :, 0] = 1
    T_shifted = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :, :1]), T[:, :, :-1]], dim=-1)
    weights = alpha * T_shifted
    
    # 5. Render Color and Depth
    # Rendered Color
    rendered_color = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=-2)
    
    # Rendered Depth (Expected distance)
    rendered_depth = torch.sum(weights * midpoints, dim=-1)
    
    # 6. Compute Color Loss
    gt_color = image.reshape(-1, 3)
    l_color = torch.mean((rendered_color - gt_color) ** 2)
    
    # 7. Compute Depth Loss for Keypoints
    l_depth = torch.tensor(0.0, device=device)
    num_keypoint_rays = 0
    
    if keypoint_visibility.any():
        # Get visible keypoint indices
        visible_indices = torch.where(keypoint_visibility)[0]
        
        # Project keypoint 3D points to camera space to get depth and 2D coords
        # K_points: [K, 3]
        K_points = keypoints[visible_indices]
        
        # Transform to camera coordinates
        # P_inv is world-to-camera
        P_inv = torch.inverse(camera_pose)
        K_points_cam = (P_inv[:3, :3] @ K_points.t()).t() + P_inv[:3, 3].unsqueeze(0)
        
        # Depth is -z in camera coordinates (assuming z points into scene)
        # Or z if camera looks down -z. Standard COLMAP: z is depth.
        # Let's assume standard OpenGL/COLMAP: camera looks down -Z, depth is positive Z distance?
        # Actually, COLMAP depth is usually the Z coordinate in camera frame if looking down -Z?
        # Let's stick to the source: "taking the re-projected z value as the keypoint's depth"
        # In many conventions, depth = -z_cam. Let's assume depth = -z_cam for standard right-handed camera looking down -Z.
        # However, source says "z value". Let's assume z_cam is the depth if camera looks down +Z.
        # DS-NeRF paper uses COLMAP. COLMAP camera center is origin, looking down -Z.
        # Depth is usually -Z.
        # Let's use depth = -K_points_cam[:, 2]
        depths = -K_points_cam[:, 2]
        
        # Get 2D pixel coordinates of keypoints
        # x = (u - W/2) / f * z + W/2 ? No.
        # u = f * x_cam / z_cam + W/2
        # u = f * (X_cam / -Z_cam) + W/2
        # v = f * (Y_cam / -Z_cam) + H/2
        u_kp = f * (K_points_cam[:, 0] / depths) + W / 2.0
        v_kp = f * (K_points_cam[:, 1] / depths) + H / 2.0
        
        # Get depth uncertainty (average reprojection error)
        # For simplicity, assume a constant uncertainty or pre-computed.
        # Source: "average reprojection error across all views".
        # Here we use a fixed small value or pass it in. Let's assume sigma_hat is passed or computed.
        # For this pseudocode, we assume sigma_hat is a tensor [K]
        sigma_hat = torch.ones_like(depths) * 0.01 # Placeholder for actual reprojection error
        
        # Interpolate GT Color for keypoint rays
        # u_kp, v_kp are continuous.
        gt_color_kp = interpolate_color(image, u_kp, v_kp)
        
        # Generate rays for keypoints
        # Ray origins: camera center
        # Ray directions: from camera center to keypoint
        ray_dirs_kp = K_points_cam / depths.unsqueeze(1) # Normalize? No, direction is vector.
        # Actually, direction is just the vector to the point.
        # But we need to render them.
        # We can reuse the ray marching logic.
        
        # Sample points along keypoint rays
        # We need to sample along the ray from near to far.
        # The ray direction is K_points_cam / |K_points_cam|? 
        # No, the ray goes from camera center through the keypoint.
        # Direction d = K_points_cam / ||K_points_cam||
        # But we sample t from near to far.
        # Points = cam_center + t * d
        # We need to map t to the keypoint depth.
        
        # Let's create rays for keypoints
        ray_origins_kp = cam_center.unsqueeze(0).expand(visible_indices.shape[0], 3)
        ray_dirs_kp = K_points_cam / torch.norm(K_points_cam, dim=1, keepdim=True)
        
        # Sample t values for these rays
        t_vals_kp = torch.linspace(near_bound, far_bound, num_samples).to(device)
        t_vals_kp = t_vals_kp + (torch.rand_like(t_vals_kp) - 0.5) * (far_bound - near_bound) / num_samples
        t_vals_kp = torch.sort(t_vals_kp, dim=-1)[0]
        
        deltas_kp = t_vals_kp[:, 1:] - t_vals_kp[:, :-1]
        delta_eps_kp = torch.zeros_like(t_vals_kp[:, :1])
        deltas_kp = torch.cat([deltas_kp, delta_eps_kp], dim=-1)
        
        midpoints_kp = (t_vals_kp[:, :-1] + t_vals_kp[:, 1:]) / 2.0
        
        points_kp = ray_origins_kp.unsqueeze(1) + ray_dirs_kp.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals_kp.unsqueeze(2)
        points_kp_flat = points_kp.reshape(-1, 3)
        
        sigma_kp_flat, _ = nerf_model(points_kp_flat)
        sigma_kp = sigma_kp_flat.reshape(visible_indices.shape[0], num_samples)
        sigma_kp = F.relu(sigma_kp)
        
        alpha_kp = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma_kp * deltas_kp)
        T_kp = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha_kp + 1e-10, dim=-1)
        T_shifted_kp = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T_kp[:, :, :1]), T_kp[:, :, :-1]], dim=-1)
        weights_kp = alpha_kp * T_shifted_kp
        
        # h(t) = w(t) / dt ? 
        # Source: h_k = w_k / delta_t_k ? 
        # Source formula: L_Depth = sum -log h_k exp(...) delta_t_k
        # h_k is the probability density. h_k = w_k / delta_t_k is an approximation if w_k is mass.
        # Actually, h(t) dt = w. So h(t) = w / dt.
        # The term in exp is -(t - D)^2 / 2 sigma^2.
        
        # Compute Depth Loss
        # D_ij is the estimated depth
        D_ij = depths
        
        # h_k = weights_kp / deltas_kp
        h_k = weights_kp / deltas_kp
        
        # Exponent term
        exponent = -((t_vals_kp[:, :-1] - D_ij.unsqueeze(1)) ** 2) / (2 * sigma_hat.unsqueeze(1) ** 2)
        
        # Log h_k
        log_h_k = torch.log(h_k + 1e-10)
        
        # L_depth = sum_k -log(h_k) * exp(exponent) * delta_t_k
        l_depth_single = -log_h_k * torch.exp(exponent) * deltas_kp
        l_depth = torch.mean(torch.sum(l_depth_single, dim=-1))
        
        num_keypoint_rays = visible_indices.shape[0]
        
    total_loss = l_color + lambda_depth * l_depth
    
    return total_loss

def interpolate_color(image, u, v):
    """
    Interpolate color from image at continuous coordinates u, v.
    """
    # Normalize u, v to [-1, 1] for grid_sample
    # u in [0, W], v in [0, H]
    # grid_sample expects normalized coordinates in [-1, 1]
    # x = 2 * (u - W/2) / W - 1
    # y = 2 * (v - H/2) / H - 1
    u_norm = 2 * (u / (image.shape[1] - 1)) - 1
    v_norm = 2 * (v / (image.shape[0] - 1)) - 1
    
    # grid_sample expects [N, H, W, 2] coordinates
    coords = torch.stack([u_norm, v_norm], dim=-1).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)
    
    # image needs to be [1, C, H, W]
    img_tensor = image.permute(2, 0, 1).unsqueeze(0)
    
    # grid_sample
    sampled = F.grid_sample(img_tensor, coords, align_corners=True)
    
    # Return [N, C]
    return sampled.squeeze().permute(1, 0)
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Near bound $t_n$ and Far bound $t_f$:** Scene bounds. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   **Training iterations:** O(10k - 100k) [S3, unofficial].
*   **Depth loss weight $\lambda_D$:** Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   **Ray allocation:** A portion of total training rays is dedicated to keypoint rays [S1].
*   **Image resolution:** 400 × 300 in experiments [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint location uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different domains (e.g., NeRF Real) [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are 2-3x fewer than standard NeRF to reach the same PSNR [S1]. Wall time per iteration is comparable (~362.4 ms vs ~359.8 ms on RTX A5000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On NeRF Real with 5 input views, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1]. With 2 views, DS-NeRF achieves PSNR 22.6 (100% keypoints) vs NeRF baseline 18.2 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified at lower view counts [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with few views, no additional training required, compatible with other NeRF methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on SfM success; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in "Depth-supervised NeRF: Fewer Views and Faster Training for Free" (CVPR 2022) [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the difficulty of training NeRFs with insufficient input images by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM keypoints [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1]. Compatible with scanned depth sensors and RGB-D reconstruction outputs [S1].
