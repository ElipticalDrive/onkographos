# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Priors, Geometric Constraints

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of regularization techniques that incorporate explicit geometric information—such as depth maps, 3D points, or structural priors—into the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and related radiance fields. These methods solve the ambiguity between shape and appearance (the shape-radiance ambiguity) and mitigate overfitting in sparse-view or textureless scenarios by constraining the density field to align with known geometric structures [S1, S3]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on semantic embeddings [11955-semantic-consistency-loss-clip-based.md] or diffusion priors [7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md], this family explicitly enforces spatial consistency, depth accuracy, or structural continuity [S1, S3].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the augmented loss structure defined by [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md), where the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ combines the photometric reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ with one or more geometric regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ [S1, S3]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{geom}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}} $$

The geometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ typically operates on the rendered depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ or the ray termination distribution $h(t)$, comparing them against a geometric prior $D_{\text{prior}}(\mathbf{r})$ derived from external sources (e.g., monocular estimators, SfM points, or LiDAR) [S1, S3]. The gradients of these terms are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md]. Common strategies include direct pixel-wise depth matching, distribution alignment (KL divergence), structural consistency enforcement, and 3D point proximity minimization [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss designed to prevent degenerate "image copying" solutions in few-view settings by enforcing foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness. It differs from other methods by not requiring external depth maps, instead deriving geometric plausibility directly from the rendered weights and density field properties.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured scenes by dynamically adjusting the stratified sampling bounds along camera rays using adapted monocular depth priors. Unlike [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md), it does not apply a depth loss during optimization but uses depth priors to guide sampling, refining the final depth output based on RGB reconstruction confidence.

### [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md)
Addresses the under-constrained single-view reconstruction problem by initializing geometry via estimated depth maps. It differs from [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md) by explicitly performing a deformable alignment of multiple perspective depth estimates into a canonical panorama before training, rather than applying depth loss directly to raw monocular estimates.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Accelerates optimization and improves quality with sparse views by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution matches surface priors derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) keypoints. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors, it requires only 3D keypoints, making it flexible and independent of training data distribution assumptions.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
Supervises optimization using dense but potentially inaccurate pseudo-depth maps by being less sensitive to outliers than standard L2 loss. It encourages rays to terminate near the pseudo-depth while allowing gradients to vanish for large errors, differing from [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md) by specifically targeting robustness against noise rather than enforcing smoothness.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity constraints on rendered depth maps using external priors (e.g., DepthAnythingV2) by modeling local depth variations between neighboring pixels via KNN. It distinguishes itself by preserving sharp structural edges while smoothing noise, unlike methods that enforce global smoothness.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in sparse data by injecting synthetic rays from novel viewpoints into the optimization loop to provide additional geometric supervision. Unlike methods relying on external priors, it explicitly guides the network toward geometrically plausible solutions by rendering and supervising views not present in the input dataset.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the distance between learned 3D points (backprojected from rendered depth) and ground-truth 3D points, successfully registering poses in sparse-view regimes. Unlike [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md), which applies L1 loss on rendered depth values, it enforces a global geometric connection between training images, preventing disjoint local solutions.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Optimizes neural scene representations by treating arbitrary rays (e.g., shadow rays) as "ray sensors" that record incoming radiance, extending standard NeRF to non-camera rays. It differs from other depth/geometry methods by not requiring explicit geometric labels like depth maps, but instead leveraging physical light transport properties to constrain the density field.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting strategy that adapts supervision based on scene content, distinguishing between static background (using geometry and reprojection confidence) and dynamic foreground (using RGB and SSIM confidence). It addresses the disparity in depth map quality between these regions, unlike standard regularization which applies uniform weights.

#### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
- **Definition**: A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
- **Formula**: $L_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2$, where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

#### Depth Supervision with Planar Architectural Depth Priors
- **Definition**: Incorporates precomputed depth estimates for floor, ceiling, and walls into NeRF training to enforce geometric constraints on textureless planar surfaces [S2].
- **Step**: 1. Compute depth estimates for floor, ceiling, and walls using semantic segmentation. 2. Incorporate these priors into training with MSE and BoundL losses alongside photometric loss. 3. Train until convergence [S2].
- **Parameter**: $\delta$ (BoundL tolerance): 1 mm. $\lambda_{color}$: 10 (bedroom), 1 (living room). $\lambda_{depth}$: 10 (bedroom), 1 (living room) [S2].
- **Number**: RMSE of priors vs ground truth: 2.786 mm (bedroom), 3.201 mm (living room). Converged in 120,000 iterations [S2].
- **Comparison**: Faster convergence than photometric-only (120k vs 200k) and patch-regularization (120k vs 280k) methods due to depth supervision enabling quick identification of empty spaces, concentrating sampling on occupied regions, and providing a stronger optimization signal [S2].

#### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
- **Definition**: A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain the NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior, specifically using KL divergence [S3].
- **Formula**: $L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}) \Delta t_k$, where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S3].
- **Parameter**: $\epsilon(x)$: A kernel that integrates to one (a distribution) with a bounded domain parameterized by $\epsilon$ [S3].
- **Pitfall**: The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S3].

#### Geometry and Opacity Regularization
- **Formula**: Sparsity loss: $L_S = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} O(i)$, where $N$ is number of rays intersecting the 3D box, encouraging sparse opacity to suppress floaters [S4]. Opacity loss: $L_O = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} [O(i) \cdot \log(O(i)) + (1 - O(i)) \cdot \log(1 - O(i))]$, encouraging opacity values to be 0 or 1 to avoid translucent effects [S4].

#### Direct Depth Supervision
- **Definition**: A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S3].
- **Step**: First sample a batch of $N_r$ rays, then compare the rendered depth and ground truth depth with different loss functions (MSE or L1) [S3].

#### GeoAug
- **Definition**: An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S5].

#### Depth Supervision
- **Definition**: A training technique to improve reconstructed geometry by providing depth priors using off-the-shelf depth estimation models, resulting in better geometry and reduced number of floaters [S6, unofficial].
- **Step**: Refit all depth maps against the prior point cloud to the same scale to handle variation between frames [S6, unofficial].
- **Parameter**: `use_depth_loss`: A boolean parameter in OptimizationParams that enables depth supervision during training [S6, unofficial].
- **Layout**: Dataset directory structure for depth priors: `<dataset-root>/depths/<FILENAME>.png`, where FILENAME matches the dataset image name [S6, unofficial].
- **Pitfall**: Depth maps that fail the refitting process against the prior point cloud are discarded and not used for supervision [S6, unofficial].

## Background
The integration of geometric priors into NeRF training originated from the observation that photometric loss alone is insufficient for recovering accurate geometry, especially in sparse-view or textureless regimes [S1, S3]. Early work, such as Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF), introduced indirect supervision by aligning the rendered depth distribution with SfM-derived depth points using KL divergence [S1, S3]. Subsequent methods expanded this to include direct depth supervision from monocular estimators [S3], robust losses to handle noisy pseudo-depth [S1], and structural consistency regularizers to preserve edges [S1]. The field has evolved to include generalized ray supervision for non-camera rays [S1] and confidence-weighted strategies for dynamic scenes [S1]. These methods are widely used in few-view reconstruction, indoor scene modeling, and applications requiring high geometric fidelity [S1, S2, S3].
