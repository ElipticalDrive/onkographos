# S3IM (Stochastic Structural SIMilarity)

Also called: Multiplex Loss, S3IM loss

## Overview
S3IM is a nonlocal multiplex loss function for neural fields that captures structural similarity by processing multiple data points (pixels/rays) as a whole set, rather than individually [S1]. It addresses the limitation of point-wise losses (like MSE) which fail to utilize the collective supervision of distant pixels [S1]. Unlike conventional MSE, S3IM cannot be expressed as the sum of any other loss computed over individual pixels from the batch [S1].

## How it works
S3IM combines a standard pixel-wise MSE loss with a stochastic approximation of the Structural Similarity (SSIM) index computed over randomly sampled groups of pixels [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   $\Theta$: Neural field parameters (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$).
    *   $\mathcal{R}$: A minibatch of rays sampled from the dataset.
    *   $\mathcal{C}$: Ground-truth pixel colors corresponding to $\mathcal{R}$.
    *   $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$: Rendered pixel colors corresponding to $\mathcal{R}$, computed via differentiable volume rendering [S1].
    *   $M$: Number of stochastic estimations (iterations).
    *   $K$: Kernel size for SSIM computation (typically $K \times K$).
    *   $\lambda$: Hyperparameter adjusting the importance of S3IM relative to MSE.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   $L_M(\Theta)$: The total multiplex loss value.
    *   $\nabla_\Theta L_M(\Theta)$: Gradients for updating $\Theta$.

### Data Structures
*   **Minibatch $\mathcal{R}$**: A set of $B$ rays. Typical batch size $B = 1024$ [S1].
*   **Pixel Sets $\mathcal{C}, \hat{\mathcal{C}}$**: Vectors of color values $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ for each ray in $\mathcal{R}$.
*   **Stochastic Patch $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}$**: A subset of pixels from $\mathcal{C}$ and $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ selected for the $m$-th SSIM estimation.

### Parameters
*   $C_1 = (K_1 L)^2$, $C_2 = (K_2 L)^2$, $C_3 = C_2 / 2$: SSIM constants [S1].
*   $K_1 = 0.01$, $K_2 = 0.03$, $L = 1$ (for RGB): Typical values for SSIM constants [S1].
*   $K$: Kernel size. Typical value: $4 \times 4$ [S1].
*   $M$: Number of stochastic repetitions. Default value: 10 [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Weight for S3IM. Not given in the sources.

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Sample Minibatch**: Sample a data minibatch of rays $\mathcal{R}$ from the dataset. Obtain ground-truth pixels $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$ and compute rendered pixels $\hat{\mathcal{C}} = \{\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) | \mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}\}$ [S1].
2.  **Compute MSE**: Calculate the conventional pixel-wise MSE loss:
    $$ L_{\text{MSE}}(\Theta) = \frac{1}{\|\mathcal{R}\|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \|\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})\|^2 $$
    [S1].
3.  **Stochastic SSIM Estimation**: Initialize $L_{\text{S3IM}} = 0$. Loop $m$ from 1 to $M$:
    a.  **Generate Stochastic Patches**: Randomly select pixels from $\mathcal{C}$ and $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ to form rendered stochastic patch $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\hat{\mathcal{C}})$ and ground-truth stochastic patch $\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\mathcal{C})$ [S1]. The sampling treats pixels as a set, breaking positional relationships [S1].
    b.  **Compute SSIM**: Compute the SSIM index between the two patches using kernel size $K \times K$ and stride $s = K$ [S1].
    c.  **Accumulate**: Add the computed SSIM to the sum.
4.  **Average SSIM**: Compute the final S3IM value as the average of the $M$ SSIM estimators:
    $$ \text{S3IM}(\hat{\mathcal{R}}, \mathcal{R}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \text{SSIM}(\mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\hat{\mathcal{C}}), \mathcal{P}^{(m)}(\mathcal{C})) $$
    [S1].
5.  **Compute Total Loss**: Combine MSE and S3IM:
    $$ L_M(\Theta) = L_{\text{MSE}}(\Theta) + \lambda (1 - \text{S3IM}(\hat{\mathcal{R}}, \mathcal{R})) $$
    Note: The source defines $L_{\text{S3IM}} = 1 - \text{S3IM}$ implicitly in Algorithm 1 step 12 [S1]. The range of SSIM is $[-1, 1]$, so $1 - \text{S3IM}$ ranges from $0$ to $2$ [S1].
6.  **Backpropagate**: Compute gradients $\nabla_\Theta L_M(\Theta)$ and update parameters $\Theta$ [S1].

### SSIM Formula Details
The SSIM index between two patches $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is defined as the product of luminance, contrast, and structure terms [S1]:
$$ \text{SSIM}(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = l(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) c(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) s(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) $$
where [S1]:
$$ l(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{2\mu_a \mu_b + C_1}{\mu_a^2 + \mu_b^2 + C_1} $$
$$ c(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{2\sigma_a \sigma_b + C_2}{\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_b^2 + C_2} $$
$$ s(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \frac{\sigma_{ab} + C_3}{\sigma_a \sigma_b + C_3} $$
Here, $\mu_a, \mu_b$ are means, $\sigma_a, \sigma_b$ are standard deviations, and $\sigma_{ab}$ is the covariance computed within a local $K \times K$ window [S1].

### Pseudocode
```python
def s3im_loss(rendered_colors, ground_truth_colors, params, batch_size=1024, kernel_size=4, m_iterations=10, lambda_weight=1.0):
    """
    Computes the S3IM multiplex loss.
    
    Args:
        rendered_colors: Tensor of shape (batch_size, 3) containing rendered pixel colors.
        ground_truth_colors: Tensor of shape (batch_size, 3) containing ground truth pixel colors.
        params: Model parameters (not used directly in loss computation, but needed for gradient flow).
        batch_size: Number of rays in the minibatch.
        kernel_size: Size of the SSIM kernel window (K x K).
        m_iterations: Number of stochastic SSIM estimations (M).
        lambda_weight: Weight for the S3IM term.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar tensor representing the total multiplex loss.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute MSE Loss
    mse_loss = torch.mean((rendered_colors - ground_truth_colors) ** 2)
    
    # Step 2: Initialize S3IM sum
    s3im_sum = 0.0
    
    # Step 3: Stochastic SSIM Estimation
    for m in range(m_iterations):
        # Generate stochastic patches by randomly sampling indices
        # Since pixels are independent in the batch, we sample without replacement
        # or with replacement to form the patch. The source implies random formation.
        # We assume we sample 'kernel_size' pixels to form a 1D patch for SSIM calculation
        # or we treat the batch as a set and sample subsets.
        # The source says "randomly form a rendered patch... and ground-truth patch".
        # To match the "non-overlapping strides" logic for independence, we sample
        # disjoint sets if possible, or just random subsets.
        
        # For simplicity and matching the "stochastic patch" description:
        # We sample a subset of indices for this iteration.
        # Note: The source uses stride s=K and kernel K. In a 1D batch of pixels,
        # this effectively means taking chunks of K pixels. However, since the batch
        # is stochastic, the "positional relationship" is lost.
        # We will implement a simple random sampling of K pixels for the patch.
        
        indices = torch.randperm(batch_size)[:kernel_size]
        
        patch_rendered = rendered_colors[indices]
        patch_ground_truth = ground_truth_colors[indices]
        
        # Compute SSIM for this patch
        ssim_value = compute_ssim(patch_rendered, patch_ground_truth, kernel_size)
        s3im_sum += ssim_value
        
    # Average the SSIM values
    avg_ssim = s3im_sum / m_iterations
    
    # Compute S3IM Loss term (1 - SSIM)
    s3im_loss_term = 1.0 - avg_ssim
    
    # Total Multiplex Loss
    total_loss = mse_loss + lambda_weight * s3im_loss_term
    
    return total_loss

def compute_ssim(patch_a, patch_b, kernel_size):
    """
    Computes SSIM between two patches.
    Assumes patch_a and patch_b are 1D tensors of length kernel_size.
    """
    # Calculate local statistics
    mu_a = torch.mean(patch_a)
    mu_b = torch.mean(patch_b)
    
    sigma_a_sq = torch.var(patch_a, unbiased=False)
    sigma_b_sq = torch.var(patch_b, unbiased=False)
    sigma_ab = torch.mean((patch_a - mu_a) * (patch_b - mu_b))
    
    # Constants
    K1 = 0.01
    K2 = 0.03
    L = 1.0
    C1 = (K1 * L) ** 2
    C2 = (K2 * L) ** 2
    C3 = C2 / 2.0
    
    # Luminance comparison
    l_ab = (2 * mu_a * mu_b + C1) / (mu_a ** 2 + mu_b ** 2 + C1)
    
    # Contrast comparison
    c_ab = (2 * torch.sqrt(sigma_a_sq) * torch.sqrt(sigma_b_sq) + C2) / (sigma_a_sq + sigma_b_sq + C2)
    
    # Structure comparison
    s_ab = (sigma_ab + C3) / (torch.sqrt(sigma_a_sq) * torch.sqrt(sigma_b_sq) + C3)
    
    # SSIM
    ssim = l_ab * c_ab * s_ab
    
    return ssim
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Stochastic Sampling**: Stochastic sampling breaks positional relationships. The method accounts for this by treating sampled pixels as a 'stochastic patch' rather than a spatially coherent image patch [S1].
*   **Stride and Independence**: To ensure independent samples within the batch, non-overlapping strides ($s=K$) are used [S1]. In the context of a random minibatch, this implies that the stochastic patches should not share pixels to maintain the statistical independence assumed by the multiplex loss formulation [S1].
*   **SSIM Range**: The SSIM index ranges from $[-1, 1]$ [S1]. The loss term is $1 - \text{S3IM}$, which ranges from $0$ to $2$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The MSE term is $O(B)$ where $B$ is the batch size [S1]. The S3IM term requires $M$ iterations, each involving SSIM computation on a patch of size $K^2$ (or $K$ pixels in 1D) [S1]. Thus, S3IM is $O(M \cdot K^2)$ per batch [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the minibatch $\mathcal{R}$, $\mathcal{C}$, and $\hat{\mathcal{C}}$ [S1]. No additional large data structures are needed [S1].
*   **Performance**: S3IM improves quality metrics significantly, e.g., test MSE loss drops by more than 90% for TensoRF and DVGO over eight novel view synthesis tasks [S1]. It provides improvements "for nearly free" [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Captures nonlocal structural similarity; improves NeRF and neural surface representation; effective for difficult tasks [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Adds computational overhead due to $M$ iterations [S1]. Relies on stochastic sampling which may introduce variance [S1].
*   **When to Use**: When structural similarity is important and point-wise losses are insufficient [S1].
*   **When Not to Use**: When computational resources are extremely limited and $M$ iterations are prohibitive [S1].

## Background
S3IM was introduced in the paper "S3IM: Stochastic Structural SIMilarity and Its Unreasonable Effectiveness for Neural Fields" [S1]. It is the first nonlocal multiplex training paradigm for NeRF and relevant neural field methods [S1]. The method was designed to address the limitation of point-wise losses in using collective supervision of distant pixels [S1].
