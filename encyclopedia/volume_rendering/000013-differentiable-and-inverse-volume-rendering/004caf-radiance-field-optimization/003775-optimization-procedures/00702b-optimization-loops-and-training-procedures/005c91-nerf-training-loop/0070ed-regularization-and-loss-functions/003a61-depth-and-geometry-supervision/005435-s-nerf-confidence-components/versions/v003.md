# S-NeRF Confidence Components

Also called: S-NeRF Confidence

## Overview
S-NeRF Confidence Components are adaptive weighting mechanisms that modulate the influence of geometric and photometric supervision signals during the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for dynamic street scenes. They solve the problem of inconsistent depth quality between static backgrounds and moving foreground vehicles by selectively applying geometry-aware or appearance-aware confidence metrics. Unlike standard NeRF which treats all pixels equally, or methods using uniform depth priors, S-NeRF distinguishes between background and foreground regions: it employs geometry and reprojection confidence for static backgrounds, while relying solely on RGB and SSIM confidence for dynamic foreground vehicles [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates within the augmented loss structure defined by [Depth and Geometry Supervision](8727-depth-and-geometry-supervision.md). It computes a confidence score for each pixel (or ray) to weight the geometric regularization terms. The implementation differs based on whether the pixel belongs to a static background or a dynamic foreground object.

**Inputs:**
*   Rendered color $\mathbf{C}$ and ground truth color $I$.
*   Rendered expected depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth depth $D(\mathbf{r})$ (e.g., from LiDAR).
*   Structural similarity index $\text{SSIM}(\mathbf{C}, I)$.
*   Reprojection error (computed by projecting 3D points from adjacent frames).
*   Threshold $\tau$ for geometry confidence.

**Outputs:**
*   Confidence weights $w_{\text{bg}}$ for background pixels.
*   Confidence weights $w_{\text{fg}}$ for foreground pixels.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Segmentation Mask:** A binary mask $M$ where $M_{ij}=1$ indicates background and $M_{ij}=0$ indicates foreground. [G] In practice, this is often derived from temporal consistency or object detection.
*   **Geometry Confidence Map:** A scalar field $C_{\text{geo}}$ per pixel.
*   **Reprojection Confidence Map:** A scalar field $C_{\text{rep}}$ per pixel.

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Geometry Confidence ($C_{\text{geo}}$):**
    Calculate the relative depth error between the rendered expected depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground truth depth $D(\mathbf{r})$.
    $$ e_{\text{depth}} = \frac{|\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) - D(\mathbf{r})|}{D(\mathbf{r})} $$
    The geometry confidence is defined as:
    $$ C_{\text{geo}} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } e_{\text{depth}} < \tau \\ 0 & \text{if } e_{\text{depth}} \ge \tau \end{cases} $$
    The threshold $\tau$ is a hyperparameter. A reasonable range is $[10\%, 40\%]$ [S1, unofficial]. The method is not sensitive to the exact value of $\tau$ within this range [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute Reprojection Confidence ($C_{\text{rep}}$):**
    [G] Compute the reprojection error by transforming the 3D point associated with the ray at time $t$ to time $t'$ using the estimated camera pose and depth, then comparing the projected 2D location with the observed pixel. The confidence is typically high if the reprojection error is below a small pixel-distance threshold. [S1, unofficial] implies this module exists but does not provide the explicit formula in the provided notes.

3.  **Compute Background Confidence ($w_{\text{bg}}$):**
    For pixels identified as background ($M_{ij}=1$), combine geometry and reprojection confidence.
    $$ w_{\text{bg}} = C_{\text{geo}} \cdot C_{\text{rep}} $$
    [G] If $C_{\text{rep}}$ is not explicitly defined as binary, it may be a continuous score. The ablation study suggests removing this module drops PSNR by 0.4% [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Foreground Confidence ($w_{\text{fg}}$):**
    For pixels identified as foreground ($M_{ij}=0$), ignore geometry and reprojection confidence because the training depth maps for vehicles are relatively better, reducing the need for strong geometry constraints. Instead, use RGB and SSIM confidence.
    $$ w_{\text{fg}} = \text{Conf}_{\text{RGB}} \cdot \text{Conf}_{\text{SSIM}} $$
    [G] $\text{Conf}_{\text{RGB}}$ is typically derived from the photometric error (e.g., $1 - \text{MSE}$ or similar normalization). $\text{Conf}_{\text{SSIM}}$ is the local SSIM value. Using both yields better LPIPS than using either alone, despite a slight PSNR trade-off [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Apply Weights to Loss:**
    The total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ incorporates these weights into the geometric regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_k$.
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_1 \sum_{\text{bg}} w_{\text{bg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}} + \lambda_2 \sum_{\text{bg}} w_{\text{bg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} + \dots $$
    [G] The specific formulation of how $w_{\text{fg}}$ interacts with the loss is not detailed in the notes, but implies that for foreground, geometric terms are either omitted or weighted by appearance confidence. The loss balance weights $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are fixed hyperparameters [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\tau$: Geometry confidence threshold. Range: $[0.1, 0.4]$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$: Loss balance weights.
    *   Background: $\lambda_1 \in [0, 0.02]$, $\lambda_2 \in [0, 0.02]$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Foreground: $\lambda_1 \in [0.5, 2]$, $\lambda_2 \in [0.015, 0.075]$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Dynamic Background:** If the segmentation mask $M$ is inaccurate, applying background confidence to moving objects may introduce artifacts.
*   **Missing Depth:** If ground truth depth $D(\mathbf{r})$ is missing, $C_{\text{geo}}$ cannot be computed. [G] The method likely falls back to RGB/SSIM confidence or ignores the geometric term for that pixel.

## Subtopics

### [S-NeRF Loss Balance Weights](15157-s-nerf-loss-balance-weights.md)
Fixed hyperparameters ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2$) that scale the depth and smoothness components of the composite loss function. They balance geometric supervision against the primary RGB reconstruction loss. Distinct from the adaptive confidence weights ($w$) computed per pixel.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** Removing the reprojection confidence module drops PSNR by 0.4%. Training without geometry confidence drops PSNR and SSIM by about 0.7% [S1, unofficial].
*   **Foreground Trade-off:** Using only RGB or SSIM confidence for vehicles yields a 1–2% PSNR increase but a 6.6–11% LPIPS decrease compared to using both [S1, unofficial]. Thus, using both is preferred for perceptual quality.
*   **Robustness:** The geometry confidence is not sensitive to the threshold $\tau$ within the range $[10\%, 40\%]$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Complexity:** Adds overhead for computing reprojection errors and segmentation masks.

## Background
S-NeRF (S-NeRF: Neural Radiance Fields for Street Views) was introduced in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the challenges of novel view synthesis in urban street environments, where dynamic objects (vehicles) and varying depth map qualities complicate standard NeRF training. The confidence components were designed to handle the disparity between static background scenes and dynamic foreground vehicles by leveraging different confidence metrics for each [S1, unofficial].
