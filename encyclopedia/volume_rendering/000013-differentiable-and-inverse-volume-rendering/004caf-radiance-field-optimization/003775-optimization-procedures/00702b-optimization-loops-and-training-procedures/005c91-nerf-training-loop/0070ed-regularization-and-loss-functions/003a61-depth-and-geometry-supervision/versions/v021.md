# Depth and Geometry Supervision

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth Priors

## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision refers to a family of regularization techniques that incorporate geometric constraints—such as depth maps, 3D points, or structural consistency—into the training of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity, particularly in sparse-view or poorly textured scenarios. Unlike methods that rely solely on photometric reconstruction or external generative priors, this family explicitly constrains the density field or rendered weights to align with geometric ground truth or physical plausibility [S1, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. It sets itself apart from [Diffusion-regularized NeRF](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf.md) by using explicit geometric signals rather than learned image distributions, and from [Volumetric Supervision](11100-volumetric-supervision.md) by focusing on surface-level or ray-termination constraints rather than volumetric density priors.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate within the augmented loss structure defined by the parent [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md), where the total loss is:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$

The geometric terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ typically operate on the rendered depth $D(\mathbf{r})$, the ray termination distribution $h(t)$, or the accumulated opacity/weights $w_i$. Common strategies include:
1.  **Direct Depth Matching:** Minimizing the distance between rendered expected depth and a reference depth map (e.g., L1, L2, or robust losses) [9368-robust-depth-loss.md, 8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md].
2.  **Distribution Alignment:** Aligning the probability distribution of ray termination weights $w_i$ with a prior depth distribution using KL divergence or similar metrics [8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md, 9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md].
3.  **Structural Consistency:** Enforcing smoothness or continuity in depth maps while preserving edges, often using KNN-based structural constraints [9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md].
4.  **Geometric Plausibility:** Preventing degenerate solutions (e.g., image copying, floaters) by enforcing foreground completeness, frustum consistency, or opacity sparsity [7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md].

Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
A composite loss designed to prevent degenerate solutions (e.g., image copying) in few-view settings. It enforces foreground completeness (sum of weights $\approx 1$), frustum consistency (density in multiple views), and depth distribution compactness. Choose this when training on sparse views without external depth maps.

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in indoor scenes by dynamically adjusting the stratified sampling bounds $[t_n, t_f]$ based on adapted monocular depth priors and multi-view consistency errors. Choose this when you have access to monocular depth priors and want to guide sampling without explicit depth loss during training.

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Accelerates optimization and improves quality with sparse views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints (SfM). It minimizes the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and the noisy depth distribution of keypoints. Choose this when you have sparse 3D keypoints but no dense depth maps.

### [Robust Depth Loss](9368-robust-depth-loss.md)
A regularization term using dense but potentially inaccurate pseudo-depth maps. It uses a piecewise function (quadratic for small errors, logarithmic for large errors) to be less sensitive to outliers than standard L2 loss. Choose this when supervising with noisy pseudo-depth labels.

### [Depth Structural Consistency Regularization](9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md)
Enforces continuity constraints on rendered depth maps using external priors (e.g., DepthAnythingV2). It uses a KNN-based hinge loss on local depth variations to preserve sharp edges while smoothing noise. Choose this when you have high-quality external depth priors and need to preserve structural edges.

### [Novel View Regularization](10806-novel-view-regularization.md)
Mitigates ambiguity in sparse data by injecting synthetic rays from novel viewpoints into the optimization loop to enforce geometric consistency. Choose this when you lack external priors but want to prevent overfitting to sparse training views.

### [Geometry and Opacity Regularization](11192-geometry-and-opacity-regularization.md)
Applies explicit constraints to opacity parameters to enforce geometric compactness (sparsity loss) and binary opacity values (entropy loss), reducing "floaters." Choose this when dealing with translucent artifacts or empty space noise.

### [3D Point Supervision Loss](12693-3d-point-supervision-loss.md)
Minimizes the L1 distance between learned 3D points (backprojected from rendered depth) and ground-truth 3D points. Choose this for pose refinement in sparse-view regimes where depth-only supervision is insufficient.

### [Generalized Ray Supervision](14127-generalized-ray-supervision.md)
Treats arbitrary rays (e.g., shadow rays) as sensors to constrain the density field based on light transport properties, without requiring explicit geometric labels. Choose this when you have physical light transport data (e.g., shadow maps) rather than depth maps.

### [Depth Refinement and Filtering](14242-depth-refinement-and-filtering.md)
Not given in the sources.

### [S-NeRF Confidence Components](15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md)
A selective confidence weighting strategy that decouples background (geometry/reprojection) and foreground (RGB/SSIM) confidence metrics to adapt supervision based on scene content. Choose this for dynamic scenes with mixed static/dynamic elements.

### [Depth Priors and Initialization](15188-depth-priors-and-initialization.md)
Not given in the sources.

### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
*   **Definition:** A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S1].
*   **Formula:** $\mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{\{m_f=1\}} \odot \|z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f)\|^2 + \mathbb{1}_{\{m_c=1\}} \odot \|\hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f\|^2$, where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S1].

### Indirect Depth Supervision (KL Loss)
*   **Definition:** A regularization technique that uses depth priors to constrain NeRF weights by aligning the probability distribution of rendering weights with the depth prior using KL divergence [S2].
*   **Formula:** $L_{KL} = \sum_{i}^{N_r} \sum_{t=t_n}^{t_f} D(r_i) - \epsilon \log w_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D(r_i))^2}{2\hat{\sigma}^2}\right) \Delta t_k$, where $w_k$ are rendering weights, $t_k$ are sampled points, $\Delta t_k$ are distances, $D(r_i)$ is ground truth depth, $\epsilon$ is a kernel parameter, and $\hat{\sigma}$ is a variance parameter [S2].
*   **Parameter:** $\epsilon(x)$: A kernel that integrates to one (a distribution) with a bounded domain parameterized by $\epsilon$ [S2].
*   **Pitfall:** The URF loss (Urban-NeRF) has no open-source implementation, so KL loss is selected as the representative for indirect supervision [S2].

### Direct Depth Supervision
*   **Definition:** A training objective that directly compares the depth rendered by the NeRF with a ground truth depth prior using a pixel-wise loss function [S2].
*   **Step:** First sample a batch of $N_r$ rays, then compare the rendered depth and ground truth depth with different loss functions (MSE or L1) [S2].

### GeoAug
*   **Definition:** An approach that uses data augmentation by rendering new training images with new noisy camera poses using DSNeRF as a baseline and using depth as regularization [S3].

## Background
The concept of using depth priors to regularize NeRF training emerged to address the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in single-view or sparse-view reconstruction [S2]. Early work, such as DS-NeRF, introduced indirect supervision via KL divergence between rendered weight distributions and sparse SfM keypoints [8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md]. Subsequent research expanded to direct depth supervision using dense priors from monocular estimators or LiDAR [S2]. Recent advancements include robust losses for noisy pseudo-depth [9368-robust-depth-loss.md], structural consistency regularization [9371-depth-structural-consistency-regularization.md], and confidence-aware weighting for dynamic scenes [15152-s-nerf-confidence-components.md]. The family is widely used in few-view reconstruction, indoor scene modeling, and dynamic scene reconstruction.
