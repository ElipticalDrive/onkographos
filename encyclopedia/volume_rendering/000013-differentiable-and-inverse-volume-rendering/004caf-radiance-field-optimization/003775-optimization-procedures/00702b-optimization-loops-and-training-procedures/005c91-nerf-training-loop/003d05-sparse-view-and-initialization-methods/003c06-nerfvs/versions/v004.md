# NeRFVS

Also called: Neural Radiance Fields for Free View Synthesis via Geometry Scaffolds

## Overview
NeRFVS is a method for indoor scene free view synthesis (FVS) that utilizes a geometry scaffold generated by off-the-shelf neural reconstruction methods to guide the optimization of a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) [S1]. It addresses the poor extrapolation performance of standard NeRF in sparse-view or low-texture regions by integrating holistic priors, specifically pseudo depth maps and view coverage information, into the training loop [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which suffers from distortions when rendering views significantly different from training views, NeRFVS minimizes per-pixel loss artifacts by enforcing 3D consistent geometry and regularizing density/color variance, distinguishing it from methods like SVS that rely on perceptual losses and suffer from noticeable distortions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A collection of $N$ RGB images $\{I_i\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$.
*   Camera intrinsic parameters $K_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ for each view.
*   Camera poses $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^6$ for each view.
*   A pre-generated geometry scaffold (pseudo depth maps and/or point cloud) derived from an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method using the input images and camera parameters [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A trained NeRF model defined by volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and emitted color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$, capable of rendering novel views with improved extrapolation quality [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) or hash-grid based network (e.g., Instant-NGP compatible) mapping 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density and color [S1].
*   **View Coverage Map:** An approximation $V \in [1, N]^{H \times W \times 1}$ tracking the number of training views covering each pixel region, ignoring emitting directions [S1].

**Formulas:**
1.  **Volume Rendering:** The expected color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is computed as [S1]:
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} w(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt $$
    where $w(\mathbf{r}(t)) = T(t)\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))$ is the weight, $T(t) = \exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds)$ is the accumulated transmittance, and $t_n, t_f$ are near and far bounds [S1].

2.  **Depth Rendering:** The expected depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ is [S1]:
    $$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} w(\mathbf{r}(t)) t dt $$

3.  **Photometric Loss:** The standard L2 loss between rendered color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and ground truth $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{color}}(\mathbf{r}) = \|\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2 $$

4.  **Robust Depth Loss:** To alleviate the influence of scaffold errors (pseudo depth inaccuracies), a robust depth loss is applied. While the exact functional form of Eq. 4 in [S1] is not fully expanded in the provided text, it is described as replacing the standard L2 loss with a robust variant that handles outliers in the pseudo depth map, preventing artifacts such as the disappearance of objects like ceiling lamps [S1]. 5.  **Variance Loss:** To decrease ambiguity in low-texture areas, the variance of the predicted density distribution and color values along each ray is regularized. 6.  **View Coverage Adjustment:** The intensity of the depth and variance losses is dynamically adjusted based on the view coverage map $V$. Regions with low coverage (few-shot regions) receive weaker regularization to avoid over-constraining the geometry where data is scarce, while high-coverage regions receive stronger constraints [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Generate Geometry Scaffold:** Use an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method on the input images $\{I_i\}$ and camera parameters to generate a 3D consistent geometry scaffold (pseudo depth maps) [S1].
2.  **Compute View Coverage:** Generate the view coverage map $V$ by counting the number of input views that observe each pixel/region, approximating the observation density [S1].
3.  **Initialize NeRF:** Initialize the NeRF model (e.g., Instant-NGP) [S1].
4.  **Render and Compute Losses:** For each sampled ray $\mathbf{r}$:
    a. Render color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ using the volume rendering equations [S1].
    b. Compute photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{color}}$ [S1].
    c. Compute robust depth loss against the scaffold's pseudo depth [S1].
    d. Compute variance loss $L_w^{var} + L_{var}$ to regularize density and color distributions [S1].
5.  **Adjust Regularization:** Scale the depth and variance loss terms based on the view coverage value $V$ for the corresponding pixel/ray. Low coverage implies weaker regularization [S1].
6.  **Optimize:** Minimize the total loss (sum of photometric, adjusted robust depth, and adjusted variance losses) using gradient descent to update the NeRF parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_nerfvs(images, poses, intrinsics, scaffold_depths, scaffold_coverage, num_epochs):
    """
    Train NeRFVS using geometry scaffolds and view coverage.
    
    Args:
        images: List of RGB images.
        poses: List of camera poses (R, t).
        intrinsics: Camera intrinsic matrices.
        scaffold_depths: Pre-computed pseudo depth maps from neural reconstruction.
        scaffold_coverage: View coverage map V (H x W), count of views per pixel.
        num_epochs: Number of training iterations.
        
    Returns:
        Trained NeRF model.
    """
    # Initialize NeRF model (e.g., Instant-NGP)
    nerf_model = initialize_nerf()
    
    for epoch in range(num_epochs):
        total_loss = 0
        for image, pose, scaffold_depth, coverage in zip(images, poses, scaffold_depths, scaffold_coverage):
            # Sample rays for this image
            rays = sample_rays(image, pose, intrinsics)
            
            # Render NeRF output
            # ray_colors: (N_rays, 3)
            # ray_depths: (N_rays,)
            # ray_weights: (N_rays, N_samples) - for variance calculation
            # ray_colors_samples: (N_rays, N_samples, 3)
            ray_colors, ray_depths, ray_weights, ray_colors_samples = render_nef(nerf_model, rays)
            
            # 1. Photometric Loss
            gt_colors = get_gt_colors(rays, image)
            loss_color = l2_loss(ray_colors, gt_colors)
            
            # 2. Robust Depth Loss
            # Align scaffold depth with rendered depth
            # Note: Exact robust loss function from Eq. 4 is not fully specified in text
            # Using a placeholder for robust depth loss (e.g., Huber or Truncated L2)
            scaffold_depths_aligned = align_depths(scaffold_depth, rays)
            loss_depth = robust_depth_loss(ray_depths, scaffold_depths_aligned)
            
            # 3. Variance Loss
            # Regularize density (weights) and color distributions
            # L_w_var: Variance of weights along ray
            # L_var: Variance of colors along ray
            loss_var_w = variance_loss(ray_weights)
            loss_var_c = variance_loss(ray_colors_samples)
            loss_variance = loss_var_w + loss_var_c
            
            # 4. View Coverage Adjustment
            # Dynamically adjust regularization strength based on coverage
            # Low coverage -> weaker regularization
            # High coverage -> stronger regularization
            # Note: The text says "weak depth and variance regularization where coverage is low"
            # This implies the loss terms are scaled by a factor derived from coverage.
            # Let's assume a scaling factor alpha(V) that is smaller for low V.
            # [G] Implementer Note: The exact mapping from coverage count V to loss weight 
            # is not provided. A common approach is inverse scaling or a sigmoid.
            # We will use a placeholder function adjust_weight(coverage).
            coverage_weight = adjust_weight(coverage)
            
            # Apply adjustment to depth and variance losses
            # Photometric loss is typically not adjusted or adjusted differently
            adjusted_loss_depth = loss_depth * coverage_weight
            adjusted_loss_variance = loss_variance * coverage_weight
            
            # Total Loss
            batch_loss = loss_color + adjusted_loss_depth + adjusted_loss_variance
            total_loss += batch_loss
            
        # Backpropagate and update
        optimize(nerf_model, total_loss)

    return nerf_model
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Geometry Scaffold Source:** Off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method [S1].
*   **View Coverage Approximation:** Counts number of observations per region, ignoring emitting directions [S1].
*   **Ablation Results (ScanNet Extrapolation):**
    *   Full Model: PSNR 23.714, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.414 [S1].
    *   Without Depth Loss: PSNR 23.187, SSIM 0.821, LPIPS 0.428 [S1].
    *   Without Var Loss: PSNR 23.435, SSIM 0.822, LPIPS 0.431 [S1].
    *   Without Adjustment: PSNR 23.172, SSIM 0.821, LPIPS 0.429 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Inaccurate Scaffold:** If the geometry scaffold is inaccurate, extrapolation performance degrades significantly [S1].
*   **Coverage Approximation:** The view coverage map is an approximation that ignores emitting directions, which may degrade view-dependent appearance [S1]. It also fails in extreme cases like holes and thin objects [S1].
*   **Few-Shot Regions:** Reliance on pseudo depth in few-shot regions may cause poor extrapolation if the geometry is exceptionally poor [S1].
*   **Low-Texture Areas:** Without variance regularization, arbitrary density and color distributions can cause notable artifacts in extrapolation [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. However, the method can be integrated into Instant-NGP, which accelerates training and inference by a large margin without obvious performance degradation [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the ScanNet dataset in an extrapolation setting, the full NeRFVS model achieved a PSNR of 23.714, SSIM of 0.829, and LPIPS of 0.414 [S1]. It outperforms methods like SVS, NerfingMVS, and Dense, which suffer from artifacts or lack 3D consistency [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method is designed for indoor scenes and room-scale free view synthesis [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves extrapolation ability of NeRF-based methods; reduces distortions and floaters; uses 3D consistent geometry scaffolds; robust to scaffold errors via robust depth loss; handles low-texture areas via variance regularization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the geometry scaffold; view coverage approximation ignores emitting directions; may fail in extreme cases like holes and thin objects; poor extrapolation if geometry is exceptionally poor in few-shot regions [S1].
*   **When to use:** Indoor scene free view synthesis where extrapolation beyond training views is required, and a neural reconstruction scaffold is available [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with extreme geometry errors in the scaffold, or where view-dependent appearance is critical and the coverage approximation is insufficient [S1].

## Background
NeRFVS was introduced to address the poor extrapolation performance of NeRF in indoor scenes, particularly in low-texture and few-shot regions [S1]. It builds on the observation that while neural reconstruction methods can recover holistic scene geometry successfully, their synthesized images contain artifacts and are over-smoothed, whereas NeRF has good interpolation but poor extrapolation [S1]. The method was developed to enable free navigation in rooms by equipping NeRF with scene priors from neural reconstruction [S1]. It was evaluated on indoor datasets like ScanNet and compared against methods such as SVS, NerfingMVS, and Dense [S1]. The authors note that NeRFVS can be integrated into Instant-NGP to boost performance [S1].
