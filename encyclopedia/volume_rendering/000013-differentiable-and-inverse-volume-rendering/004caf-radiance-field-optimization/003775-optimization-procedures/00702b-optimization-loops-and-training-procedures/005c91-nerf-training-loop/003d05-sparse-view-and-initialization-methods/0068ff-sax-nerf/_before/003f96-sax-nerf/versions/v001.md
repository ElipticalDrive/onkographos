# SAX-NeRF

Also called: Neural Attenuation Fields, NAF, Nesterov
## Overview
SAX-NeRF is a neural field method for sparse-view Cone-Beam CT (CBCT) reconstruction that models the 3D volume as a continuous attenuation field [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of reconstructing high-quality 3D volumes from a limited number of 2D X-ray projections, addressing sparse-view artifacts like floaters and aliasing [S1, unofficial]. Unlike traditional iterative algorithms, it uses differentiable rendering to optimize neural network parameters directly against projection data [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its siblings [NeRFVS](8667-nerfvs.md) and [ViP-NeRF](8690-vip-nerf.md) by its specific formulation as a baseline for differentiable voxel-based methods, often cited for its high reconstruction quality (PSNR) but prohibitive runtime [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** $K$ 2D X-ray projections (angles and pixel values); camera extrinsics and intrinsics for each view.
**Outputs:** A neural network $\mathcal{N}$ representing the attenuation coefficient field $\mu(\mathbf{x})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

**Data Structures:**
The core data structure is a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) $\mathcal{N}$. The inputs to the MLP are 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, and the output is a scalar attenuation value $\mu$. The specific architecture details (hidden layers, activation functions, encoding) are not explicitly detailed in the provided sources, only that it is a "neural field method" [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Sampling:** For each training image, sample 3D points $\mathbf{x}_i$ along the rays corresponding to the 2D pixels. [G] Typically, points are sampled uniformly or stratified along the ray segment within the volume bounds.
2.  **Network Evaluation:** Query the neural network to get attenuation coefficients: $\mu_i = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_i)$.
3.  **Differentiable Rendering:** Compute the predicted 2D projection intensity $I_{pred}$ by integrating the attenuation along the ray. The sources mention Siddon's method as a differentiable rendering technique used in related baselines (DiffVox) for comparison, but do not explicitly specify the rendering integrator for SAX-NeRF itself, only that it is a neural field method [S1, unofficial]. [G] Standard NeRF-style volume rendering is implied: $I_{pred} = \sum_i T_i (1 - e^{-\mu_i \delta_i})$, where $T_i$ is transmittance and $\delta_i$ is step size.
4.  **Loss Computation:** Compare $I_{pred}$ with the ground truth projection $I_{gt}$ using a loss function, likely Mean Squared Error (MSE) given PSNR reporting [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Optimization:** Update the neural network parameters using gradient descent to minimize the loss.

**Parameters:**
*   Number of views $K$: Evaluated at 5, 10, 20, 30, 60 views [S1, unofficial].
*   Hardware: RTX A6000 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Runtime:** The method is prohibitively slow for real-world deployment [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sparse Views:** Performance degrades as the number of views decreases, though it outperforms traditional methods in PSNR for 30-60 views [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [DIF-Net](19518-dif-net.md)
DIF-Net is a supervised deep learning method for sparse-view Cone-Beam CT (CBCT) reconstruction that formulates the CT volume as a continuous intensity field $g: \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, regressing the intensity value $v$ of an arbitrary 3D point $p$ from $K$ 2D projections $I$. Unlike NeRF-based methods that require per-patient optimization, DIF-Net learns prior knowledge from a large dataset, enabling high-quality reconstruction from extremely sparse views ($\le 10$) without per-scan training. It differs from voxel-based methods by avoiding 3D CNNs for feature decoding, thereby reducing memory requirements and enabling scalable output resolutions without model retraining.

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:** Prohibitively slow. Takes 10.3 hours to reconstruct a volume from 60 input views on an RTX A6000 [S1, unofficial]. This is orders of magnitude slower than traditional methods like Siddon's method or Nesterov, which take no more than 5 minutes [S1, unofficial].
**Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
**Measured Performance:**
*   **PSNR:** Achieves higher PSNR than DiffVox for 30-60 views [S1, unofficial].
*   **Quality:** Produces higher fidelity novel views with fewer stereotypical sparse-view artifacts (floaters, aliasing) compared to previous neural field methods [S1, unofficial].
**Strengths:** High reconstruction quality (PSNR) for moderate sparse-view counts (30-60 views); effective at reducing sparse-view artifacts.
**Limitations:** Extremely slow runtime (10.3h for 60 views), making it unsuitable for real-world clinical deployment compared to faster baselines [S1, unofficial].
**When to use:** When reconstruction quality (PSNR) is prioritized over speed and sufficient time is available for per-scan optimization.
**When not to use:** When real-time or near-real-time reconstruction is required, or when the number of views is very low (where DIF-Net may be preferable) or very high (where traditional methods are faster and comparable).

## Background
SAX-NeRF (also known as Neural Attenuation Fields or NAF) is a neural field method for sparse-view CBCT reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. It is used as a baseline in evaluations of differentiable voxel-based reconstruction methods, such as DiffVox [S1, unofficial]. The method is cited in the context of comparing neural field approaches against traditional iterative algorithms (like Siddon's method, Nesterov) and other neural field methods (like NAF) [S1, unofficial]. The name "SAX-NeRF" appears to be a specific variant or alias used in the DiffVox paper [S1, unofficial], potentially referencing the "Neural Attenuation Fields" (NAF) work by Zha et al. [4] or a specific implementation detail. The sources do not provide the original publication date or authors for "SAX-NeRF" specifically, only citing the DiffVox paper which uses it as a baseline [S1, unofficial].
