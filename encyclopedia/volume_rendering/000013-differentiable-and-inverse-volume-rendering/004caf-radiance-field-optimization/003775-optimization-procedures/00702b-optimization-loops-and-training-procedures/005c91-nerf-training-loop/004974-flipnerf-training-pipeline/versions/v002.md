# FlipNeRF Training Pipeline

Also called: FlipNeRF Ablation Sequence

## Overview
FlipNeRF is a training procedure for few-shot novel view synthesis that improves upon the baseline mip-NeRF representation by introducing flipped reflection rays and consistency-based regularization [S1]. It solves the problem of overfitting and unreliable depth estimation in sparse-view scenarios by enforcing geometric and feature consistency between original and flipped ray paths [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF training which relies solely on photometric loss from observed views, FlipNeRF augments the training loop with reflection-based supervision to regularize the radiance field, achieving higher PSNR and lower uncertainty metrics on synthetic datasets [S1].

## How it works
The method builds on [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) and [Scene Representation and Architecture](11368-scene-representation-and-architecture.md). It modifies the standard volume rendering pipeline by adding a reflection ray branch and specific loss terms.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A set of $N$ input images with known camera poses and intrinsics.
- Output: A trained neural radiance field (MLP and feature grid) capable of rendering novel views.

**Data Structures:**
- **Ray Representation:** Each training step samples a batch of rays. For each ray $r$, a corresponding flipped reflection ray $r'$ is generated. The reflection is computed based on the estimated surface normal at the intersection point of $r$ with the scene geometry.
- **Bottleneck Features:** The network outputs features at a bottleneck layer. Let $f(r)$ be the feature vector for ray $r$ and $f(r')$ for the flipped ray $r'$.

**Numbered Steps:**

1. **Baseline Initialization:** Initialize the model using the standard mip-NeRF representation [S1]. This includes the coarse and fine networks for volume rendering.

2. **Generate Flipped Reflection Rays:** For each sampled ray $r$ during training, compute the intersection point with the estimated geometry. Reflect the ray direction across the surface normal at this point to generate the flipped ray $r'$ [S1].

3. **Apply Uncertainty-aware Loss (LUE):** Compute the Uncertainty-aware Loss (LUE) for both the original ray $r$ and the flipped ray $r'$ [S1]. This loss regularizes the uncertainty predictions of the model, ensuring reliable rendering. The sources indicate that naive application of empirical loss ($L_{Emp}$) degrades performance, whereas LUE consistently improves reliability and reduces Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) [S1].

4. **Apply Bottleneck Feature Consistency Loss (LBFC):** Compute the Bottleneck Feature Consistency Loss (LBFC) using the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) on the bottleneck features of the original and flipped rays [S1]. This encourages the network to produce consistent feature representations for geometrically related rays.

5. **Enforce Feature Consistency:** Explicitly enforce consistency between the bottleneck features of the original ray pair $r$ and the flipped ray pair $r'$ [S1]. This step is critical for performance gains, as shown in the ablation study where adding this consistency significantly improves metrics [S1].

6. **Integrate Orientation Loss (LOri.):** Incorporate the Orientation Loss ($L_{Ori.}$) from Ref-NeRF to refine normal estimation [S1]. Accurate normal estimation is prerequisite for generating meaningful flipped reflection rays. This loss helps the network estimate more accurate normal vectors, which in turn leads to more effective flipped rays and further performance gains [S1].

7. **Optimization Loop:** Update network parameters using an optimizer (typically Adam) by minimizing the combined loss function:
   $$ L_{total} = L_{color} + L_{LUE} + L_{LBFC} + L_{Ori.} $$
   where $L_{color}$ is the standard photometric reconstruction loss [S1].

**Parameters:**
- **Loss Weights:** The specific weights for $L_{LUE}$, $L_{LBFC}$, and $L_{Ori.}$ relative to $L_{color}$ are not explicitly detailed in the provided notes, but their inclusion is mandatory for the full model performance [S1].
- **Hardware/Resolution:** Evaluated on Realistic Synthetic 360° dataset [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Naive Empirical Loss:** Applying existing empirical regularization techniques ($L_{Emp}$) naively without the uncertainty-aware formulation leads to inferior results compared to MixNeRF [S1].
- **Reduced Feature Dimension:** Reducing the feature dimension of the bottleneck ($bd$) reduces the capacity of feature representations and prevents the model from improving robustness [S1].
- **Degenerate Rays:** Without the consistency losses, simply adding flipped rays can lead to overall performance degradation due to scene-by-scene structural variations [S1].

## Subtopics

### FrugalNeRF
FrugalNeRF is a few-shot NeRF framework designed to synthesize novel views from extremely sparse inputs (e.g., 2 views) without relying on learned external priors [S1]. It solves the problems of overfitting and long training times inherent in extreme few-shot scenarios by leveraging weight-sharing voxels across multiple scales and a cross-scale geometric adaptation scheme [S1]. Unlike methods that require complex scheduling or pre-trained models, FrugalNeRF uses reprojection errors to select pseudo-ground truth depth, enabling fast convergence and robust regularization [S1].

### 360FusionNeRF Training Pipeline
A training method for panoramic neural radiance fields using a single 360° RGB-D image, incorporating color, geometric, and semantic consistency losses [S2].
- **Definition:** Optimizes network parameters $\theta$ to synthesize novel views from a single panorama [S2].
- **Steps:**
    1. Input: A single 360° RGB-D panorama [S2].
    2. Generate new training images and depth maps with various virtual camera poses by reprojecting the input panorama [S2].
    3. Optimize network parameters $\theta$ using Adam optimizer [S2].
- **Parameters:**
    - Learning Rate: Initialized to $5 \times 10^{-4}$, exponentially reduced to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S2].
    - Epochs: 200,000 epochs per experiment [S2].
    - Batch Size: 1,400 [S2].
    - Hardware: DGX A100 GPU [S2].
    - Network: $N_c = 64$ and $N_f = 128$ in coarse and refined networks [S2].
- **Comparison:** Unlike OmniNeRF which is estimated from scratch per scene, this method uses geometric and semantic priors to generalize better to unseen views and avoid overfitting [S2]. It introduces Semantic Consistency Loss ($L_{SC}$) computed over a smaller resolution to manage computational cost, minimized every $k$ iterations alongside Color and Geometric losses [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Performance:** The full FlipNeRF model achieves PSNR 19.55, SSIM 0.767, LPIPS 0.180, Average Error 0.101, and NLL 2.56 on the Realistic Synthetic 360° dataset [S1].
- **Comparison:** FlipNeRF renders images from novel views with fewer artifacts and more accurate depth maps than MixNeRF [S1]. It estimates more reliable outputs with lower standard deviation of RGB and depths [S1].
- **Regularization Impact:** The use of LUE consistently achieves more reliable rendering results with much lower NLL compared to MixNeRF [S1].
- **Limitations:** The method relies on accurate normal estimation; poor normal estimation leads to ineffective flipped rays. The performance gain is contingent on the proper application of consistency losses [S1].

## Background
FlipNeRF was proposed in the paper "FlipNeRF: Flipped Reflection Rays for Few-shot Novel View Synthesis" [S1]. It addresses the limitations of existing few-shot NeRF methods like MixNeRF, which model rays with mixtures of distributions but suffer from higher uncertainty and artifacts [S1]. The method builds on the mip-NeRF baseline and incorporates ideas from Ref-NeRF for normal estimation [S1].
