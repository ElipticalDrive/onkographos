# Depth Regularization

Also called: Depth constraints, Depth priors, Depth supervision

## Overview
Depth regularization methods solve the problem of geometric ambiguity and surface hollowness in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing auxiliary loss terms that enforce consistency between rendered depth/disparity maps and external geometric cues, such as monocular depth estimates or sensor data [Parent]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on color fidelity or frequency domain smoothing, this family explicitly targets the 3D position ($\mu$) and opacity ($\alpha$) parameters to recover accurate scene geometry, particularly in sparse-view or ill-posed reconstruction scenarios [Parent].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family compute a differentiable auxiliary loss term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ that is added to the total training loss:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
where $\lambda_{reg}$ is a scalar weight. The core mechanism involves extracting a geometric or spectral property from the rendered output (or the input data) and comparing it against a target or constraint. The gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ flow back through the differentiable rasterizer to update the Gaussian parameters $\theta$ (centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors $c$) [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [PixelSplat Training and Depth Regularization](6420-pixelsplat-training-and-depth-regularization.md)
PixelSplat Training and Depth Regularization is a specific training protocol for the PixelSplat architecture, which reconstructs 3D scenes from image pairs using 3D Gaussian Splatting. It solves the problem of producing high-quality, dense point clouds from generalizable 3D reconstruction by combining a multi-stage visual loss with a geometric depth regularization term. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific use of multiple Gaussians per ray to enable multi-depth estimation and its explicit fine-tuning phase for depth map consistency, whereas [DNGaussian](7399-dngaussian.md) focuses on differentiable normal rendering and [Edge-Aware Disparity Regularization](9140-edge-aware-disparity-regularization.md) focuses on disparity edge preservation [Parent].

### [DNGaussian](7399-dngaussian.md)
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing a two-stage depth regularization process that explicitly manages the trade-off between surface positioning (Hard) and surface completeness (Soft). Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, which lowers PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible regions but allows the optimization to focus strictly on visible geometry.

### [Edge-Aware Disparity Regularization](9140-edge-aware-disparity-regularization.md)
Edge-Aware Disparity Regularization is a technique that encourages local depth smoothness in rendered scenes while preserving depth discontinuities that align with image edges. It solves the problem of noisy or artifact-prone depth maps by weighting the disparity gradient penalty with the inverse of the RGB image gradient, ensuring that smoothness is enforced in flat regions but not across object boundaries. Unlike standard smoothness regularization which penalizes all depth changes equally, this method is edge-preserving, distinguishing it from siblings like [DNGaussian](7399-dngaussian.md) which uses geometric patch-based smoothing.

### 2DGS + LD̂ variant
- [S4] A variant of 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) that incorporates a depth regularization strategy by enabling supervision and gradient flow to depths within the CUDA backend rasterizer, supervised with sensor or monocular depth estimates.

## Background
Depth regularization in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a solution to the "hollowness" and geometric inaccuracies inherent in standard 3DGS, particularly when trained on sparse input views. Standard 3DGS optimizes for photometric consistency ($\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$), which often results in translucent or floating Gaussian structures that do not correspond to real surfaces [S3].

The approach was notably formalized in **DNGaussian** [S1, unofficial][S3], which introduced a two-stage regularization process (Hard and Soft depth) combined with Global-Local Depth Normalization to align Gaussians with monocular depth estimates (e.g., from DPT). This addressed the degradation of geometry in few-shot scenarios. Concurrently, other works like **SparseGS** [S1, unofficial] and **FSGS** [S3] integrated depth priors, often combined with diffusion models or Gaussian unpooling, to stabilize reconstruction from very limited viewpoints (~3 views).

In the broader survey of 3DGS variants, depth regularization is categorized under "Regularization based methods" [S3]. These methods are contrasted with "Generalizability based methods" (like feed-forward models) which rely on learned priors rather than per-scene optimization constraints. The efficacy of depth regularization is noted to diminish with extremely limited views (e.g., single image), leading to a trade-off between geometric quality (high computation, per-scene) and speed/generalization [S3].

Variants also exist for 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS), where depth supervision is enabled directly within the CUDA rasterizer backend to guide planar primitives [S4]. Additionally, earlier sphere-based neural rendering methods (e.g., Pulsar [S2]) utilized opacity-depth energy terms to encourage spheres to align with scene depth, a concept that parallels modern Gaussian depth regularization.
