# DNGaussian

Also called: Depth Normalization Regularized Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Radiance Fields

## Overview
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing explicit depth constraints to correct the geometry degradation caused by insufficient input views [S1]. Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, focusing optimization on visible geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of input images (typically 3 views) with known camera poses, and pre-predicted monocular depth maps for these views generated by a monocular depth estimator (e.g., DPT) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives (centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$) and a Neural Color Renderer, enabling novel view synthesis [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Primitives:** Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
    *   Center $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
    *   Scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (or covariance matrix)
    *   Rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (quaternion)
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   **Neural Color Renderer:**
    *   **Hash Encoder:** Grid-based encoder with 16 levels, resolution range 16 to 512, max size $2^{19}$ [S1].
    *   **MLP:** 5-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron with hidden dimension 64 [S1].
    *   **Inference Optimization:** Intermediate results from the hash encoder are stored; only the last MLP layers are calculated during rendering to merge view direction for acceleration [S1].

**Initialization:**
1.  Initialize a random set of Gaussians using a uniform distribution [S1].
    *   *Note:* Standard 3DGS uses COLMAP point clouds, but DNGaussian uses random initialization because point clouds are unstable in sparse-view situations [S1].

**Optimization Loop:**
The optimization runs for 6,000 iterations [S1]. It consists of two main modules: Color Supervision and Depth Regularization [S1].

1.  **Color Supervision:**
    *   Inherits the standard 3DGS gradient descent and primitive duplication/removal process [S1].
    *   Replaces spherical harmonics with the Neural Color Renderer to predict color for each primitive, preventing overfitting on sparse views [S1].
    *   Loss: $L_{color} = L_1(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda_{LD-SSIM} L_{D-SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)$, where $\hat{I}$ is rendered image and $I$ is ground truth [S1].

2.  **Depth Regularization:**
    *   **Phase 1: Hard Depth Regularization (Iterations 1–1000):**
        *   Freeze scaling $s$ and rotation $q$ [S1].
        *   Apply a large opacity value $\tau = 0.95$ to all Gaussians [S1].
        *   Optimize only center $\mu$ and opacity $\alpha$ [S1].
        *   Render "Hard Depth" $D_{hard}$ dominated by the nearest Gaussians [S1].
        *   Formula:
            $$ D_{hard}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} \tau (1 - \tau)^{i-1} G^{proj}_i(x_p) ||\mu_i - o||_2 $$
            Where $x_p$ is the pixel, $N$ is the set of Gaussians along the ray, $i$ is the ray hit order, $G^{proj}_i(x_p)$ is the projected 2D Gaussian, $\mu_i$ is the center, and $o$ is the camera center [S1].
        *   Loss: $R_{hard} = L_{similar}(D_{hard}, \tilde{D})$ [S1].

    *   **Phase 2: Soft Depth Regularization (Iterations 1001–6000):**
        *   Applied after 1,000 iterations for stability [S1].
        *   Freeze Gaussian centers $\check{\mu}$ to avoid negative influence from center movement [S1].
        *   Optimize opacity $\alpha$ to ensure surface completeness and prevent hollowness [S1].
        *   Render "Soft Depth" $D_{soft}$ using actual opacities [S1].
        *   Formula:
            $$ D_{soft}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} ||\check{\mu}_i - o||_2 \times \tilde{\alpha}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \tilde{\alpha}_j) $$
            Where $\check{\mu}$ is the frozen center, $\tilde{\alpha}$ is the opacity, and the product term represents cumulative transparency [S1].
        *   Loss: $R_{soft} = L_{similar}(D_{soft}, \tilde{D})$ [S1].

3.  **Global-Local Depth Normalization (Applied to both Hard and Soft losses):**
    *   To address the limitation of fixed-scale depth alignment which overlooks small errors, DNGaussian uses Global-Local Depth Normalization [S1].
    *   Let $D_T$ be the rendered depth (Hard or Soft) and $\tilde{D}$ be the monocular depth [S1].
    *   Compute Global Normalized Depth ($D^{GN}$) and Local Normalized Depth ($D^{LN}$) for both rendered and monocular depths [S1].
    *   Total Depth Regularization Loss $R_T$ for $T \in \{hard, soft\}$:
        $$ R_T = L_2(D^{GN}_T, \tilde{D}^{GN}) + \gamma L_2(D^{LN}_T, \tilde{D}^{LN}) $$
        Where $\gamma = 0.1$ is the global loss weight [S1].
    *   $L_{similar}$ is implemented as an L2 loss with an error tolerance to relax the constraint [S1].

4.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{color} + R_{hard} + R_{soft} $$
    [S1]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def train_DNGaussian(input_images, camera_poses, num_iterations=6000, soft_start_iter=1000):
    # 1. Initialization
    gaussians = RandomInitializeGaussians(num_primitives=10000)
    color_renderer = NeuralColorRenderer(levels=16, res_range=(16, 512), max_size=2**19, mlp_layers=5, hidden_dim=64)
    mono_depth_maps = DPT_Predict(input_images)
    tau, gamma = 0.95, 0.1
    optimizer = GradientDescentOptimizer(gaussians + [color_renderer], lr=1e-3)

    for iter in range(1, num_iterations + 1):
        # 2. Color Supervision
        rendered_color = RenderColor(gaussians, color_renderer, camera_poses)
        L_color = L1_Loss(rendered_color, input_images) + Lambda_LDDSSIM(rendered_color, input_images)
        optimizer.zero_grad(); L_color.backward(); optimizer.step()
        # Standard 3DGS duplication/removal of active primitives occurs here [G]

        # 3. Depth Regularization
        if iter <= soft_start_iter:
            # Hard Depth Phase
            freeze_params(gaussians, ['s', 'q'])
            hard_depth = RenderHardDepth(gaussians, tau, camera_poses)
            R_hard = GlobalLocalDepthLoss(hard_depth, mono_depth_maps, gamma)
            optimizer.zero_grad(); R_hard.backward(); optimizer.step()
        else:
            # Soft Depth Phase
            freeze_params(gaussians, ['mu'])
            soft_depth = RenderSoftDepth(gaussians, camera_poses)
            R_soft = GlobalLocalDepthLoss(soft_depth, mono_depth_maps, gamma)
            optimizer.zero_grad(); R_soft.backward(); optimizer.step()

def RenderHardDepth(gaussians, tau, camera_poses):
    depth_map = zeros_like(camera_poses.image_shape)
    for ray in camera_poses.rays:
        sorted_g = SortByDistance(ray.origin, gaussians)
        d = 0.0
        for i, g in enumerate(sorted_g):
            d += tau * ((1 - tau) ** (i - 1)) * EvaluateProjectedGaussian(g, ray.pixel) * distance(g.mu, ray.origin)
        depth_map[ray.pixel] = d
    return depth_map

def RenderSoftDepth(gaussians, camera_poses):
    depth_map = zeros_like(camera_poses.image_shape)
    for ray in camera_poses.rays:
        sorted_g = SortByDistance(ray.origin, gaussians)
        T, d = 1.0, 0.0
        for i, g in enumerate(sorted_g):
            d += distance(g.mu, ray.origin) * g.alpha * T
            T *= (1 - g.alpha)
        depth_map[ray.pixel] = d
    return depth_map

def GlobalLocalDepthLoss(rendered_depth, mono_depth, gamma):
    D_GN, D_LN = NormalizeDepth(rendered_depth)
    D_tilde_GN, D_tilde_LN = NormalizeDepth(mono_depth)
    return L2_Loss(D_GN, D_tilde_GN) + gamma * L2_Loss(D_LN, D_tilde_LN)

# Standard helpers
def RandomInitializeGaussians(num_primitives): return {'mu': uniform_random(num_primitives, 3), 's': uniform_random(num_primitives, 3), 'q': unit_quaternion_random(num_primitives), 'alpha': uniform_random(num_primitives, 1)}
def NeuralColorRenderer(levels, res_range, max_size, mlp_layers, hidden_dim): return {'hash': HashEncoder(levels, res_range, max_size), 'mlp': MLP(mlp_layers, hidden_dim)}
def DPT_Predict(images): return [ForwardPass(DPT_Model, img) for img in images]
def GradientDescentOptimizer(params, lr): return {'params': params, 'lr': lr}
def RenderColor(gaussians, color_renderer, camera_poses): return [Rasterize(gaussians, color_renderer, ray) for ray in camera_poses.rays]
def L1_Loss(a, b): return mean(abs(a - b))
def Lambda_LDDSSIM(a, b): return 0.2 * SSIM_Loss(a, b)
def freeze_params(g, params): [setattr(g, p, g[p].detach()) for p in params]
def SortByDistance(origin, g): return sorted(g, key=lambda x: distance(x.mu, origin))
def EvaluateProjectedGaussian(g, pixel): return exp(-0.5 * (pixel - g.center_proj).T @ g.cov_inv @ (pixel - g.center_proj))
def distance(a, b): return sqrt(sum((a - b)**2))
def NormalizeDepth(depth):
    D_GN = (depth - min(depth)) / (max(depth) - min(depth) + 1e-6)
    D_LN = LocalWindowNormalize(depth)
    return D_GN, D_LN
def L2_Loss(a, b): return mean((a - b)**2)
def zeros_like(tensor): return full_like(tensor, 0.0)
def uniform_random(n, d): return [[random.uniform(0, 1) for _ in range(d)] for _ in range(n)]
def unit_quaternion_random(n): return normalize(uniform_random(n, 4))
def HashEncoder(levels, res_range, max_size): return {'levels': levels, 'res_range': res_range, 'max_size': max_size}
def MLP(layers, dim): return {'layers': layers, 'dim': dim}
def ForwardPass(model, input): return model(input)
def Rasterize(g, c, ray): return BlendColors(g, c, ray)
def SSIM_Loss(a, b): return StructuralSimilarityIndex(a, b)
def LocalWindowNormalize(depth): return LocalMeanStdNormalize(depth)
def full_like(tensor, val): return [[val for _ in row] for row in tensor]
def normalize(v): return v / sqrt(sum(v**2) + 1e-6)
def random: import random; return random
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\tau$ (Hard Depth Opacity): 0.95 [S1].
*   $\gamma$ (Global Loss Weight): 0.1 [S1].
*   Training Iterations: 6,000 [S1].
*   Soft Depth Start Iteration: 1,000 [S1].
*   Neural Color Renderer: Hash encoder (16 levels, 16-512 res, $2^{19}$ max size) + 5-layer MLP (hidden dim 64) [S1].
*   Monocular Depth Estimator: DPT [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sparse View Degradation:** Decreasing input views causes significant geometry degradation in standard 3DGS because scene geometry relies on Gaussian primitive positioning, which becomes incorrectly learned under sparse constraints [S1]. DNGaussian mitigates this via depth regularization.
*   **Overfitting:** Standard spherical harmonics overfit on sparse views; replaced by Neural Color Renderer [S1].
*   **Hollowness:** Without Soft Depth Regularization, surfaces may become semitransparent and cause hollowness [S1].
*   **Initialization Instability:** COLMAP point clouds are unstable in sparse-view situations; random initialization is used instead [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** 25× reduction in training time compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly lower memory overhead compared to NeRF-based methods [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Over 3000× faster rendering speed compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality novel view synthesis from sparse inputs (e.g., 3 views); preserves fine-grained color appearance; efficient training and rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Exposes black background on empty/invisible areas, which can lower PSNR/SSIM metrics for those areas compared to NeRFs that interpolate colors [S1].
*   **When to use:** Few-shot novel view synthesis where training time and rendering speed are critical, and high-quality geometry is needed despite coarse monocular depth supervision [S1].

## Background
DNGaussian was proposed by Xiao Bai et al. in 2024 [S1]. It builds on the success of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and previous depth-regularized sparse-view NeRFs [S1]. The method addresses the geometry degradation issue in 3DGS when trained on sparse views by introducing Hard and Soft Depth Regularization and Global-Local Depth Normalization [S1]. The code is available at https://github.com/Fictionarry/DNGaussian [S1].
