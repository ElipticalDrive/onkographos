# Monocular Depth Estimation Adaptation and Supervision

Also called: MDE Adaptation, Depth-Guided NeRF, Monocular Depth Regularization

## Overview
This family of methods integrates pre-trained Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) networks into NeRF training to resolve the shape-radiance ambiguity and geometric inaccuracies inherent in sparse-view or single-view reconstruction. By supervising the NeRF’s rendered depth with MDE priors, these methods enforce geometric consistency where photometric loss is insufficient. They differ from sibling methods like [NerfingMVS](9518-nerfingmvs.md) which primarily use depth for sampling bounds, and [Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance](11911-depth-guided-nerf-training-via-earth-mover-s-distance.md) which uses distributional losses, by focusing on explicit scale-shift alignment, confidence-weighted supervision, and joint adaptation of the MDE network itself to the target scene.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the following mechanism:
1.  **Depth Prior Generation:** A pre-trained MDE network $G_\phi$ (or $M_\phi$) takes an RGB image $I$ as input and outputs a relative depth map $D^*$. This map lacks absolute scale and shift relative to the NeRF’s coordinate system.
2.  **NeRF Depth Rendering:** The NeRF model $F_\Theta$ renders an absolute depth map $\bar{D}$ for a given view by integrating density $\sigma$ along rays:
    $$ \bar{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i z_i $$
    where $w_i$ are the volume rendering weights and $z_i$ are the sample depths [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Scale-Shift Alignment:** To align the relative MDE depth $D^*$ with the absolute NeRF depth $\bar{D}$, local scale $w$ and shift $q$ parameters are computed. This is typically done via least squares minimization on image patches $P$ to avoid bias from dominant objects:
    $$ \min_{w, q} \sum_{p \in P} \| (w \cdot \text{sg}(D^*(p)) + q) - \bar{D}(p) \|^2 $$
    where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is a stop-gradient operator to prevent gradients from flowing into the MDE network during NeRF updates [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Loss Formulation:** A depth consistency loss is added to the photometric loss. The loss is often weighted by uncertainty or confidence maps to handle MDE errors [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NerfingMVS](9518-nerfingmvs.md)
NerfingMVS uses MDE priors to constrain the ray sampling bounds $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ and guide volume sampling. It differs from other methods by explicitly clamping the ray segment to the depth prior range, effectively restricting the NeRF to search for geometry only within the estimated depth range. This resolves the ambiguity of where to place geometry in empty space. It is chosen when sparse 3D points are available to fine-tune the MDE, creating dense, scene-specific priors.

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance](11911-depth-guided-nerf-training-via-earth-mover-s-distance.md)
This method supervises the *distribution* of ray termination distances using Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) against a monocular depth prior from a diffusion model (DiffDP). Unlike methods that enforce pixel-wise depth consistency, it uses Sinkhorn Divergence to approximate EMD. It distinguishes itself by weighting the supervision by intrinsic uncertainty maps derived from the diffusion process, allowing selective incorporation of prior information. It is chosen for sparse-view settings where global geometric structure is more important than local pixel accuracy.

### [Depth Completion Network NeRF](12001-depth-completion-network-nerf.md)
This approach integrates a Depth Completion Network (DCN) to generate dense depth and uncertainty maps from sparse inputs (e.g., LiDAR or COLMAP points). It differs from NerfingMVS by explicitly modeling depth uncertainty to handle noisy or sparse priors, down-weighting unreliable predictions in the loss function. It is chosen when sparse geometric data is available but incomplete, requiring completion to provide robust supervision.

### [Monocular Depth Regularization on Seen Views](12330-monocular-depth-regularization-on-seen-views.md)
This method regularizes the NeRF by aligning pseudo-depth maps from a pre-trained MDE network with the absolute depth rendered from the NeRF for *seen* input views. It solves the problem of MDE networks outputting relative depth by computing scale and shift parameters locally on image patches. This preserves local depth statistics and reduces computational cost compared to full-image rendering. It sets itself apart from global statistic fitting by avoiding bias from dominant objects.

### [DäRF](12331-d-rf.md)
DäRF combines NeRF with MDE through *online complementary training*. It solves the problem of poor generalization in sparse-view NeRF by injecting MDE's geometric prior into the NeRF representation at both seen and unseen viewpoints, while simultaneously adapting the MDE network to produce multiview-consistent geometry. Unlike methods that only distill depth from a static pretrained MDE, DäRF jointly optimizes both networks to remove MDE ambiguity via patch-wise scale-shift fitting and confidence modeling.

### [NeRF-guided MDE Adaptation](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md)
This method aligns a pre-trained MDE network with the absolute geometry of a specific scene by using a NeRF as a geometric prior. It solves the ambiguity inherent in monocular depth estimates (e.g., convexity/concavity confusion) by providing scene-specific absolute depth priors. Unlike sibling methods that primarily use depth for loss supervision, this method explicitly adapts the MDE weights to the target scene, often jointly optimizing the NeRF and the MDE to mutually reinforce geometric accuracy.

### [Model Variants](13153-model-variants.md)
This family defines three configurations for the Forward Warping Depth (FWD) framework, distinguished by their source of depth supervision and the training status of the MDE module. FWD-U is a fully unsupervised baseline where the depth network is trained from scratch. FWD leverages a pre-trained MVS module fine-tuned end-to-end. FWD-D bypasses the MVS module, using sensor depths as direct inputs. These variants solve the problem of integrating depth priors when the availability of ground-truth depth or pre-trained MDE networks varies.

### [ShadowNeuS](14132-shadowneus.md)
ShadowNeuS reconstructs a neural SDF from single-view images under multiple lighting conditions by supervising *shadow rays* (rays between the light source and the scene) rather than just camera rays. It solves the problem that standard NeRF does not model rays from the scene to the light source, missing shadow information which contains geometry cues via light visibility. Unlike NeRF which uses multi-view cameras, this method uses a single-view camera and shadow rays, making it applicable to real capture setups with limited views.

### [NeRF-Supervision](14729-nerf-supervision.md)
NeRF-Supervision generates dense correspondence datasets to train object-centric visual descriptors using only RGB images and NeRFs. It solves the problem of acquiring ground-truth dense matches for thin, reflective, or ambiguous objects that fail standard MVS pipelines. Unlike MVS which relies on patch-based photometric consistency, NeRF-Supervision uses the NeRF’s density field to probabilistically sample correspondences, handling ambiguity via a "distribution-of-depths" formulation.

#### Monocular Depth Regularization on Unseen Views
- **Definition:** A loss function that supervises NeRF geometry at unseen/novel viewpoints by aligning NeRF-rendered depth with monocular depth estimated from the rendered novel view image [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** For each unseen view $l$ in the set of unseen views $U$: 1. Sample a patch of the novel viewpoint. 2. Render color $\bar{I}_l$ and depth $\bar{D}_l$ from NeRF. 3. Extract monocular depth $\bar{D}^*_l = G_\phi(\bar{I}_l)$ from the rendered color image. 4. Compute scale $w_l$ and shift $q_l$ via least squares between $\bar{D}^*_l$ and $\bar{D}_l$. 5. Calculate loss $L_{unseen}$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** $U$: Set of unseen view images. $P$: Randomly sampled patch of pixels [S1, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Monocular depth from noisy NeRF rendering may be affected by fine-grained artifacts. The MDE model's strong geometric prior is robust against these artifacts, filtering them out [S1, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Unlike previous methods [14, 41, 61, 50] that only impose priors on input viewpoints (limited by self-occlusion/sparsity), this method applies priors to arbitrary viewpoints, reaching every location [S1, unofficial].

#### MDE Adaptation Loss
- **Definition:** A composite loss function for adapting a monocular depth estimator to predict scene-specific absolute geometry by enforcing multi-view consistency and local patch-wise scale-shift fitting [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** Compute the first addend term (scale-shift loss) to force the MDE to adapt towards multiview consistency, reducing its ill-posed nature and aligning its initial global depth prediction with the absolute geometry captured by NeRF. Compute the second addend term (L1 loss with patch-wise scale-shift fitting) to aid the modeling of fine, detailed, local geometry. Optimize the MDE network by minimizing the sum of both terms simultaneously; using only one term performs worse on all metrics than using both in conjunction [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Scale-shift loss weight: Balances global alignment with NeRF geometry. L1 loss weight: Balances local geometric detail preservation via patch-wise fitting. Values not specified in this section but required for optimization [S1, unofficial].

#### Weighted Disparity Alignment
- **Step:** Predict uncalibrated disparity of the source view using a monocular depth estimator: $D_e = D(I_r)$. Define the aligned predicted inverse depth $D_r$ using four parameters $a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3$ and matrices $H, V$: $D_r = a_0 D_e + a_1 \mathbf{1}_{HW} + a_2 H + a_3 V$. Define $H$ and $V$ as $H \times W$ matrices where for pixel $p=(p_x, p_y)$, $H(p)=p_x$ and $V(p)=p_y$. Set weight $w(p)$ to the inverse of the distance between pixel $p$ and the mask centre-of-mass. Perform an additional optimization step to correct $D_r$ to encourage smoothness around the mask, yielding $D_r^{smooth}$ [S2].
- **Parameter:** $N_{depth}$: Number of iterations for supervised training on unmasked pixels before applying depth loss [S2].

## Background
The integration of monocular depth priors into NeRF optimization emerged to address the limitations of sparse-view reconstruction, where photometric loss alone leads to shape-radiance ambiguity and poor geometry. Early works like NerfingMVS [S2] utilized MDE to guide sampling bounds. Subsequent research, such as DäRF [S1, unofficial], introduced joint adaptation of the MDE network to the target scene, recognizing that static priors can propagate errors. Methods like Earth Mover’s Distance guidance [S1, unofficial] and Depth Completion Network integration [S1, unofficial] further refined how depth uncertainty and sparsity are handled. The field has expanded to include shadow-based supervision [S1, unofficial] and correspondence generation for descriptor training [S1, unofficial], demonstrating the versatility of MDE as a geometric regularizer.
