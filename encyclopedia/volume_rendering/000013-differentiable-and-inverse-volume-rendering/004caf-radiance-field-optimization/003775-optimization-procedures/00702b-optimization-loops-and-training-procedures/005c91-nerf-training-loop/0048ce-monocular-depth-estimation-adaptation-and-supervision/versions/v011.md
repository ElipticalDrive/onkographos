# Monocular Depth Estimation Adaptation and Supervision

Also called: MDE-guided NeRF, Depth-Regularized NeRF, Monocular Depth Priors

## Overview
This family of methods integrates pre-trained monocular depth estimation (MDE) networks into the NeRF training loop to resolve the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in sparse-view or single-view reconstruction [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which relies solely on RGB photometric supervision, these methods explicitly guide volume sampling, rendering, or optimization using depth maps extracted from a scene-adapted or static monocular network [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. They distinguish themselves from sibling methods by treating monocular depth not just as a static prior, but as an active supervisory signal that can be adapted to the specific scene geometry (e.g., via fine-tuning or joint optimization) or used to constrain the sampling bounds and loss functions [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the following mechanism:
1. **Depth Prior Extraction:** A pre-trained MDE network $G_\phi$ (e.g., Mannequin Challenge, DiffDP, DPThybrid, Omnidata) is applied to input RGB images $I$ to generate a relative depth map $D^*$ [S1, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
2. **Scale and Shift Alignment:** Since MDE outputs are relative and lack absolute scale, a linear alignment is performed between the MDE depth $D^*$ and the NeRF-rendered depth $\bar{D}$ (or ground truth/sparse points). This is typically done per-image or per-patch using least-squares optimization to find scale $w$ and shift $q$:
   $$ \min_{w, q} \sum_{p} \| (w \cdot \text{sg}(D^*(p)) + q) - \bar{D}(p) \|^2 $$
   where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator to prevent gradients from flowing into the frozen MDE network during NeRF updates [S1, unofficial].
3. **Depth Consistency Loss:** A loss term $L_{depth}$ is added to the total training objective, penalizing the difference between the aligned MDE depth and the NeRF-rendered depth [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
4. **Sampling Guidance:** In some variants, the depth prior is used to clamp the near and far bounds of ray sampling, focusing computation on plausible depths [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NerfingMVS](9518-nerfingmvs.md)
NerfingMVS integrates learning-based monocular depth priors into NeRF optimization to resolve shape-radiance ambiguity in indoor scenes. It distinguishes itself by combining test-time adaptation of a monocular depth estimator (fine-tuned on sparse SfM+MVS points) with a rendering pipeline that clamps ray bounds based on these priors.

### [Depth-Guided NeRF Training via Earth Mover’s Distance](11911-depth-guided-nerf-training-via-earth-mover-s-distance.md)
This method improves geometric accuracy in sparse-view NeRFs by supervising the distribution of ray termination distances using Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) rather than direct depth matching. It uses a diffusion-based depth prior and its intrinsic uncertainty map to selectively guide geometry, replacing standard L2 depth losses with a distributional EMD loss weighted by uncertainty.

### [Depth Completion Network NeRF](12001-depth-completion-network-nerf.md)
This method integrates a guided depth completion network (e.g., MFFNet) into the NeRF loop to recover dense depth from sparse LiDAR measurements. It sets itself apart by using LiDAR-based completion to provide dense, metrically consistent depth supervision, addressing the sparsity of raw LiDAR data in unbounded real-world scenes.

### [Monocular Depth Regularization on Seen Views](12330-monocular-depth-regularization-on-seen-views.md)
This method aligns pseudo-depth maps from a pre-trained MDE network with NeRF-rendered depth for seen input views. It differs from global scale-fitting approaches by computing local scale and shift parameters per image patch, preventing dominant objects from biasing the depth alignment and preserving local geometric details.

### [DäRF](12331-d-rf.md)
DäRF is a framework for robust few-shot NeRF reconstruction that combines NeRF with MDE through online complementary training. It solves ambiguity by jointly optimizing the NeRF and the MDE network at a specific scene, distilling the monocular depth prior to NeRF at both seen and unseen views and finetuning the MDE network with canonical geometry captured by NeRF.

### [NeRF-guided MDE Adaptation](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md)
This method refines a pre-trained MDE network by using a NeRF model as a geometric prior to resolve ambiguities such as convexity/concavity confusion. It distinguishes itself by explicitly adapting the MDE weights to the specific scene's absolute geometry rather than just using depth as a static supervision signal for the NeRF.

### [Model Variants](13153-model-variants.md)
This family defines three specific architectural configurations for the FWD framework, distinguished by their depth supervision strategy and the initialization state of the depth estimation module. They address the trade-off between training stability, convergence speed, and reliance on external geometric priors. FWD-U serves as the unsupervised baseline, FWD provides a supervised baseline with pre-trained geometry, and FWD-D bypasses learned depth estimation entirely by using sensor data.

### [ShadowNeuS](14132-shadowneus.md)
ShadowNeuS reconstructs a neural SDF from single-view images under multiple lighting conditions by supervising shadow rays rather than relying solely on camera rays. It solves the problem that standard NeRF does not model rays from the scene to the light source, missing shadow information which contains critical geometry cues via light visibility.

### [NeRF-Supervision](14729-nerf-supervision.md)
NeRF-Supervision generates dense correspondence datasets to train object-centric visual descriptors using only RGB images and NeRFs. It solves the problem of acquiring ground-truth dense correspondences for thin, reflective, or ambiguous objects where traditional RGB-D sensors and MVS fail, using the NeRF's density field to formulate a "distribution-of-depths" approach.

### [GAINS Stage I: Shape Estimation](17163-gains-stage-i-shape-estimation.md)
GAINS Stage I is the geometry optimization phase of the GAINS pipeline, designed to reconstruct robust 3D shape from sparse RGB images using 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS). It solves novel-view artifacts and geometric collapse by augmenting optimization with monocular depth/normal consistency, local depth ranking, and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from a latent diffusion model.

### Monocular Depth Regularization on Unseen Views
- **Definition:** A loss function that supervises NeRF geometry at unseen/novel viewpoints by aligning NeRF-rendered depth with monocular depth estimated from the rendered novel view image [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** For each unseen view $l$ in the set of unseen views $U$: 1. Sample a patch of the novel viewpoint. 2. Render color $\bar{I}_l$ and depth $\bar{D}_l$ from NeRF. 3. Extract monocular depth $D^*_l = G_\phi(\bar{I}_l)$ from the rendered color image. 4. Compute scale $w_l$ and shift $q_l$ via least squares between $D^*_l$ and $\bar{D}_l$. 5. Calculate loss $L_{unseen}$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** $U$: Set of unseen view images. $P$: Randomly sampled patch of pixels [S1, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** Monocular depth from noisy NeRF rendering may be affected by fine-grained artifacts. However, the MDE model's strong geometric prior is robust against these artifacts, filtering them out [S1, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Unlike previous methods that only impose priors on input viewpoints (limited by self-occlusion/sparsity), this method applies priors to arbitrary viewpoints, reaching every location [S1, unofficial].

### MDE Adaptation Loss
- **Definition:** A composite loss function for adapting a monocular depth estimator to predict scene-specific absolute geometry by enforcing multi-view consistency and local patch-wise scale-shift fitting [S1, unofficial].
- **Step:** Compute the first addend term (scale-shift loss) to force the MDE to adapt towards multiview consistency, reducing its ill-posed nature and aligning its initial global depth prediction with the absolute geometry captured by NeRF [S1, unofficial]. Compute the second addend term (L1 loss with patch-wise scale-shift fitting) to aid the modeling of fine, detailed, local geometry [S1, unofficial]. Optimize the MDE network by minimizing the sum of both terms simultaneously; using only one term performs worse on all metrics than using both in conjunction [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Scale-shift loss weight and L1 loss weight are required for optimization but values are not specified in this section [S1, unofficial].

### [Weighted Disparity Alignment](13957-weighted-disparity-alignment.md)
- **Step:** Predict uncalibrated disparity of the source view using a monocular depth estimator: $D_e = D(I_r)$. Define the aligned predicted inverse depth $D_r$ using four parameters $a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3$ and matrices $H, V$: $D_r = a_0 D_e + a_1 \mathbf{1}_{HW} + a_2 H + a_3 V$. Define $H$ and $V$ as $H \times W$ matrices where for pixel $p=(p_x, p_y)$, $H(p)=p_x$ and $V(p)=p_y$. Set weight $w(p)$ to the inverse of the distance between pixel $p$ and the mask centre-of-mass. Perform an additional optimization step to correct $D_r$ to encourage smoothness around the mask, yielding $D_r^{smooth}$.
- **Parameter:** $N_{depth}$: Number of iterations for supervised training on unmasked pixels before applying depth loss.

### [Depth Alignment for Monocular Cues](16559-depth-alignment-for-monocular-cues.md)
- **Definition:** Aligning monocular depth estimates (range [0,1]) with rendered depth by solving for scale and shift parameters per mini-batch via least-squares optimization.
- **Parameter:** $q$: Shift parameter.
- **Usage:** Used to backproject depth and calculate LDC when using monocular cues from Omnidata model.

## Background
The integration of monocular depth priors into NeRF optimization emerged to address the limitations of sparse-view reconstruction, where photometric consistency alone is insufficient to disambiguate geometry and appearance [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. Early works like [NerfingMVS](9518-nerfingmvs.md) demonstrated that fine-tuning monocular depth estimators on sparse SfM points could guide volume sampling. Subsequent methods such as [DäRF](12331-d-rf.md) and [NeRF-guided MDE Adaptation](12333-nerf-guided-mde-adaptation.md) introduced joint optimization strategies, allowing the MDE network to adapt to the specific scene geometry captured by the NeRF, thereby resolving ambiguities like convexity/concavity confusion [S1, unofficial]. More recent approaches have explored distributional losses like Earth Mover’s Distance to handle the uncertainty in monocular priors [11911-depth-guided-nerf-training-via-earth-mover-s-distance.md], and extended supervision to unseen viewpoints to ensure global geometric coherence [Monocular Depth Regularization on Unseen Views].
