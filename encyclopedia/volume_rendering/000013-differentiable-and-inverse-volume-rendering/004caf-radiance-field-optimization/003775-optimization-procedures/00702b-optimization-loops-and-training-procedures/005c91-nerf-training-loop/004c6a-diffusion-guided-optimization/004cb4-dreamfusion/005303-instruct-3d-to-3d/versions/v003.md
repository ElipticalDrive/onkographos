# Instruct 3D-to-3D

Also called: Instruct 3D-to-3D conversion, Instruct 3D editing

## Overview
Instruct 3D-to-3D is a method for performing 3D scene editing or conversion based on text instructions, building on [DreamFusion](12087-dreamfusion.md) by utilizing a text-to-image diffusion model to guide the optimization of a 3D representation. It solves the problem of modifying an existing 3D scene's geometry and appearance according to natural language prompts without requiring new 3D training data. Unlike standard text-to-3D generation which creates a scene from scratch, this method integrates the source 3D scene as a conditional input to the optimization process, allowing for targeted modifications while preserving unedited parts of the scene.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A source 3D scene (represented as a NeRF or similar radiance field), a text instruction prompt, and a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (specifically InstructPix2Pix [S1]).
- Output: An optimized target 3D scene that reflects the changes described in the text instruction.

**Data Structures:**
- The source scene is represented as a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) or Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) structure, storing density, color, and geometric parameters for each point or voxel.
- The diffusion model maintains its internal latent space and UNet architecture, conditioned on the source image and text prompt.

**Steps:**
1. **Conditioning:** The source 3D scene is rendered into a 2D image from a random camera viewpoint. This image serves as the conditional input (image prompt) to the diffusion model, alongside the text instruction.
2. **Optimization Loop:** The target 3D scene parameters are optimized iteratively using the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss [89].
   - Render an image from the current target 3D scene.
   - Pass the source image and the rendered target image through the diffusion model to compute the SDS loss, which measures the discrepancy between the current render and the desired edit.
   - Backpropagate the gradient from the SDS loss to update the 3D scene parameters (density, color, or Gaussian attributes).
3. **Dynamic Scaling:** To effectively modify scene geometry, the method integrates dynamic scaling. This involves adjusting the scale parameters of the 3D primitives (e.g., Gaussians or voxels) during optimization to allow for expansion or contraction of specific regions as dictated by the text prompt [S1].

**Formulas:**
- The SDS loss is computed as:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{SDS} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon} \left[ w(t) \cdot (\epsilon_\theta(z_t; y, t) - \epsilon) \cdot \frac{\partial x_0}{\partial \theta} \right] $$
  where $z_t$ is the noisy latent, $y$ is the conditional input (source image + text), $\epsilon_\theta$ is the noise prediction from the diffusion model, and $\theta$ represents the 3D scene parameters [S1].
- Dynamic scaling modifies the scale parameter $s$ of 3D primitives, allowing them to adapt their size during the optimization process to match the geometric changes implied by the text [S1].

**Parameters:**
- Text instruction: User-defined string describing the desired edit.
- Diffusion model: InstructPix2Pix [S1].
- Optimization steps: Not given in the sources.
- Learning rate: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **View Consistency:** Since the optimization is driven by 2D diffusion models, there is a risk of view-inconsistent edits if not properly regularized. The use of SDS loss helps mitigate this by encouraging consistency across different viewpoints during training.
- **Geometry Drift:** Without proper constraints, the dynamic scaling might lead to unrealistic geometry changes. Regularization techniques may be needed to preserve the overall structure of the scene.

## Subtopics

### Focaldreamer
Focaldreamer is a variant that initiates with a base shape and editable ellipsoids in focal regions, utilizing rendered normal maps as shape encoding for the text-to-image (T2I) models to guide geometry optimization. It renders the shape with both base and editable textures, unified by a pixelwise discriminative mask, and incorporates several regularizations in addition to the SDS loss to improve editing quality [S1].

### Pix2Vex Reconstructor
Pix2Vex Reconstructor is a network that translates images to a 3D model, based on a pix2pix convolutional and deconvolutional ResNet, outputting vertex offsets from a base model [S2, unofficial]. It predicts offsets from a base model (uniform sphere with predefined topology) rather than absolute coordinates to improve convergence smoothness. It optionally predicts symmetry by splitting the model into four quarters, predicting shifts for one quarter, mirroring it, and predicting shifts for the mirrored quarter until all four are predicted. It also predicts uniform translation and scaling values to reduce offset magnitude and improve stability. Regularization is applied on the 3D mesh considering angles between normals, edge lengths, and curvature. The input is images, with an internal state of a single representative image (from the pix2pix component). The output is $3 \times \#vertices$ values from two fully connected layers, passed through a sigmoid layer, then linearly transformed to range $[-1, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Allows for precise, text-guided editing of existing 3D scenes without requiring new 3D data. The integration of dynamic scaling enables effective geometric modifications.
- **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the underlying diffusion model and the SDS loss, which can sometimes lead to artifacts or view inconsistencies if not properly tuned.
- **When to use:** When you need to edit an existing 3D scene based on text instructions.
- **When not to use:** When generating a 3D scene from scratch without a source scene, or when high-fidelity, view-consistent geometry is critical and diffusion-based artifacts are unacceptable.

## Background
Instruct 3D-to-3D was introduced as a method for realizing 3D-to-3D conversions following text instructions, utilizing InstructPix2Pix [S1]. It builds on the principles of Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) [89], which was originally proposed for text-to-3D generation in DreamFusion. The method was highlighted in a survey on 3D editing based on NeRF and 3DGS [S1].
