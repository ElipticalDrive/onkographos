## Overview
Drone-NeRF is a method for large-scale 3D scene reconstruction from aerial drone imagery, designed to handle varying camera exposure conditions [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by employing a block-parallel training strategy, view frustum sampling for anti-aliasing, joint pose optimization, and appearance embeddings for exposure balancing [S1]. It specifically addresses artifacts caused by vertical face occlusions and shadows in overhead drone views through an expanded bounding box merging process [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of drone-captured images with known or estimated poses and intrinsics [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A merged radiance field representation, rendered RGB images, and depth maps for the entire scene, free from shadow artifacts and exposure discrepancies [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sub-bounding Boxes:** The scene is partitioned into a grid of sub-bounding boxes (sub-blocks) [S1]. Each box contains a trained local NeRF model [S1].
*   **Expanded Sub-bounding Box:** A larger bounding box created around each sub-bounding box to capture projection areas from vertical faces, used to mitigate shadow influence [S1].
*   **MLP Network:** A fully connected network with ReLU activations [S1].
    *   **Structure:** 8 hidden layers with 256 units each, followed by 2 output layers with 128 channels [S1].
    *   **Inputs:** Position encoding, direction encoding, and appearance embeddings [S1].
    *   **Outputs:** Density ($\sigma$) and RGB color ($\mathbf{c}$) [S1].
*   **Position Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 4 [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** Learnable vectors integrated into the MLP to resolve exposure discrepancies [S1].
*   **Hash Coding:** Used in the initial stage of training for rapid query scenarios, combined with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Partitioning:** Divide the large-scale scene into a grid of sub-bounding boxes [S1].
2.  **Block Parallel Training:** Train each sub-block independently [S1].
    *   Initialize the MLP with hash coding and a small fusion MLP for the initial stage to handle high-frequency information efficiently [S1].
    *   Perform hierarchical sampling: 128 coarse samples and 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
    *   Sample 1024 rays per batch [S1].
    *   Optimize the network parameters using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.005 [S1].
    *   Optimize camera poses by back-propagating the loss gradient into the input pose computation, using a camera-optimized learning rate of 0.0005 [S1].
    *   Integrate appearance embeddings into the MLP to balance exposure variations across images [S1].
    *   Train for 5,000 or 30,000 iterations per block to prevent over-fitting [S1].
3.  **Essential Box Selection:** Determine the number of sub-bounding boxes required for merging by calculating intersections between the rays of the rendering area and the sub-bounding boxes [S1]. Use only these essential boxes for rendering to reduce computation [S1].
4.  **Shadow Mitigation via Expansion:**
    *   Create an expanded sub-bounding box larger than the original sub-bounding box to mitigate shadow influence from vertical faces [S1].
    *   Capture RGB values corresponding to projection areas generated by vertical surface ray irradiation from the smaller bounding box using the enlarged sub-bounding box [S1].
    *   Replace shadow occlusion regions in the original rendering with the captured RGB values from the projection areas to eliminate shadow influence [S1].
5.  **Merging and Uniformization:**
    *   Since expanded boxes overlap, uniformize the scene color in overlapping areas [S1].
    *   For depth rendering, employ depths falling within the scope of the previous larger sub-bounding box for merging [S1].
    *   Average depths pertaining to each sub-bounding box within overlapping regions [S1].
    *   Compare depth value averages across overlapping regions to adjust the overall depth for smoother continuity [S1].
6.  **Final Rendering:** Render the overhead view of the drone, combining the processed sub-scenes to produce a final image free from occlusion and shadow artifacts [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is performed in a block-parallel manner, allowing independent training of sub-blocks [S1]. Rendering uses only essential sub-bounding boxes to reduce computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The MLP architecture is relatively compact (8x256 + 2x128) [S1]. Initial stage uses hash coding for efficiency [S1]. Evaluated on an Nvidia 4070ti GPU with 12GB VRAM [S1].

*   **Strengths:** Handles large-scale scenes, mitigates shadow artifacts from vertical faces, balances exposure variations, and improves depth continuity in overlapping regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful selection of sub-bounding boxes and expansion margins. Performance is sensitive to iteration count due to over-fitting risks [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Large-scale aerial survey reconstruction where drone imagery exhibits varying exposure and vertical occlusion artifacts are prominent [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Small-scale scenes where block-parallel processing adds unnecessary complexity, or scenes without significant vertical structures causing shadow artifacts [S1].
