# Mip-NeRF 360

Also called: Unbounded NeRF, mip360

## Overview
Mip-NeRF 360 extends the quality of NeRF to unbounded, 360-degree real-world scenes by combining anti-aliased cone-casting with a non-linear scene contraction parameterization [S1, S2][S5, unofficial]. It solves the problems of aliasing artifacts from multi-scale details and the ambiguity of infinite space, which cause standard NeRF and mip-NeRF to fail or produce "floaters" in unbounded captures [S2][S5, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent [Mip-NeRF](link) by introducing a proposal network trained via online distillation and a distortion regularizer, and from [NeRF-FF](8669-nerf-ff.md) by handling full 360-degree unbounded geometry rather than just forward-facing scenes [S2].

## How it works
Mip-NeRF 360 uses two coupled MLPs: a small **proposal MLP** ($F_p$) and a larger **NeRF MLP** ($F_{\text{NeRF}}$). The proposal MLP predicts only volumetric density to guide sampling, while the NeRF MLP predicts density and color. The method maps infinite 3D space into a bounded domain using a contraction function, applies anti-aliased integrated positional encoding to conical frustums, and uses a distortion loss to regularize the density field [S2][S5, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** Training images with known camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics).
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel colors $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for arbitrary viewpoints.

### Data Structures
1.  **Proposal MLP ($F_p$):**
    *   Input: Encoded position $\gamma(\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}))$.
    *   Output: Scalar density $\tau_p$.
    *   Size: Small capacity (significantly smaller than $F_{\text{NeRF}}$) [S2].
2.  **NeRF MLP ($F_{\text{NeRF}}$):**
    *   Input: Encoded position $\gamma(\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}))$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, and intermediate features from the density head.
    *   Output: Density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.
    *   Size: $\sim 15\times$ the capacity of standard mip-NeRF [S2].
3.  **Sampling Intervals:**
    *   Coarse intervals $\mathbf{t}^c$: Sorted samples from uniform distribution $[t_n, t_f]$ [S2].
    *   Fine intervals $\mathbf{t}^f$: Sorted samples drawn from the histogram defined by $\mathbf{t}^c$ and coarse weights $\mathbf{w}^c$ [S2].

### Scene Contraction
To handle unbounded scenes, 3D coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ are mapped to a bounded unit ball using the contraction function [S5, unofficial]:

$$
\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = 
\begin{cases} 
\mathbf{x} & \|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1 \\
\left(2 - \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|}\right) \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \|\mathbf{x}\| > 1 
\end{cases}
$$

This allocates more MLP capacity to nearby content and compresses distant content [S2].

### Integrated Positional Encoding
Instead of sampling points, Mip-NeRF 360 represents rays as conical frustums with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ [S2]. The positional encoding is integrated over this Gaussian approximation to provide anti-aliasing [S2, S4]:

$$
\gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} \sin(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \\ \cos(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \end{bmatrix} \right\}_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
$$

where $L$ is the number of frequency bands [S2]. High-frequency components are attenuated where the frustum variance is large [S5, unofficial].

### Training Procedure

1.  **Coarse Sampling:** For each training ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample $N_c$ points uniformly from $[t_n, t_f]$ to form $\mathbf{t}^c$ [S2].
2.  **Proposal Network Forward Pass:** Query $F_p$ at contracted positions $\text{contract}(\mathbf{r}(t_i^c))$ to get densities $\tau_p$. Compute coarse alpha weights $w_i^c$ and transmittance [S2]:
    $$
    w_i^c = \left(1 - e^{-\tau_p(t_{i+1}^c - t_i^c)}\right) e^{-\sum_{j < i} \tau_p(t_{j+1}^c - t_j^c)}
    $$
3.  **Fine Sampling:** Sample $N_f$ points $\mathbf{t}^f$ from the histogram defined by $\mathbf{t}^c$ and $\mathbf{w}^c$ using inverse transform sampling [S2].
4.  **NeRF Network Forward Pass:** Query $F_{\text{NeRF}}$ at contracted positions $\text{contract}(\mathbf{r}(t_i^f))$ and directions $\mathbf{d}$ to get densities $\tau$ and colors $\mathbf{c}$. Compute fine alpha weights $w_i^f$ [S2]:
    $$
    w_i^f = \left(1 - e^{-\tau_i(t_{i+1}^f - t_i^f)}\right) e^{-\sum_{j < i} \tau_j(t_{j+1}^f - t_j^f)}
    $$
5.  **Rendering:** Compute the final pixel color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using the fine weights and colors [S2]:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} w_i^f \mathbf{c}_i
    $$
6.  **Loss Computation:** The total loss combines reconstruction loss for coarse and fine passes, and a distortion regularizer [S2][S5, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{10} \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^c), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^f), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{distortion}}
    $$
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is mean squared error (MSE) [S2].
7.  **Online Distillation:** The proposal MLP $F_p$ is not supervised by the image reconstruction loss. Instead, its weights are updated to minimize the difference between its predicted density histogram and the histogram weights generated by the NeRF MLP $F_{\text{NeRF}}$ [S2]. This allows $F_{\text{NeRF}}$ to be large and accurate while $F_p$ efficiently guides sampling [S2].
8.  **Distortion Regularizer:** To prevent "floaters" (spurious geometry) in unbounded scenes, a distortion loss is applied. This loss penalizes the expected distance of the sampled points from the ray origin, weighted by their alpha weights, encouraging density to concentrate on actual surfaces rather than floating artifacts [S2][S5, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Near/Far Planes ($t_n, t_f$):** Define the sampling range along the ray [S2].
*   **Number of Frequencies ($L$):** Standard mip-NeRF value [S2].
*   **Distortion Weight ($\lambda$):** Controls the strength of the floater suppression [S2].
*   **Coarse/Fine Samples ($N_c, N_f$):** Standard mip-NeRF values [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** The contraction function maps infinite space to a bounded ball; care must be taken with numerical stability for very large $\|\mathbf{x}\|$ [S5, unofficial].
*   **Floaters:** Without the distortion regularizer, unbounded scenes suffer from floating geometry artifacts [S2][S5, unofficial].
*   **Surface Accuracy:** The method uses a density representation without explicit surface geometry, resulting in poor surface accuracy compared to SDF-based methods like NeuS [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is $\sim 2\times$ that of mip-NeRF, taking approximately 24–48 hours on the Blender dataset (single A100 GPU) [S1, S2]. The online distillation accelerates training by 300% compared to NeRF in Detail's learned proposer approach [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Total model capacity is $\sim 15\times$ larger than mip-NeRF due to the separate proposal and NeRF MLPs [S2].
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF 360 unbounded dataset, it achieves PSNR = 29.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1]. On NeRF Synthetic (Blender), PSNR = 32.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1]. On LLFF forward-facing scenes, PSNR = 26.4 $\pm$ 0.4 dB [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High quality on unbounded, 360-degree scenes; reduces MSE by 57% compared to mip-NeRF; effective anti-aliasing [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Poor surface accuracy; slow training compared to hash-grid methods like Instant-NGP [S1].
*   **When to use:** When rendering high-quality novel views of unbounded, real-world 360-degree scenes where aliasing and floaters are major issues.
*   **When not to use:** When fast training (<10 minutes) or explicit surface reconstruction is required [S1].

## Background
Mip-NeRF 360 was introduced in November 2021 as an extension of Mip-NeRF to handle unbounded scenes [S2, S3]. It was developed to address the limitations of NeRF and mip-NeRF in real-world captures where the camera rotates 360 degrees and content exists at arbitrary distances [S2]. The method builds on the cone-casting anti-aliasing of mip-NeRF [S2] and introduces scene contraction, online distillation, and distortion regularization [S2][S5, unofficial]. It is widely used as a baseline for unbounded neural rendering research [S4].
