# Drone-NeRF
## Overview
Drone-NeRF reconstructs large-scale 3D scenes from aerial drone imagery while handling varying camera exposure [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by using block-parallel training, hierarchical sampling, joint pose optimization, and appearance embeddings, and specifically mitigates vertical-face shadow artifacts through an expanded bounding box merging process [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of drone-captured images with known or estimated poses and intrinsics [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A merged radiance field representation, rendered RGB images, and depth maps for the entire scene, free from shadow artifacts and exposure discrepancies [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sub-bounding Boxes:** The scene is partitioned into a grid of sub-bounding boxes (sub-blocks) [S1]. Each box contains a trained local NeRF model [S1].
*   **Expanded Sub-bounding Box:** A larger bounding box created around each sub-bounding box to capture projection areas from vertical faces, used to mitigate shadow influence [S1].
*   **MLP Network:** A fully connected network with ReLU activations [S1].
    *   **Structure:** 8 hidden layers with 256 units each, followed by 2 output layers with 128 channels [S1].
    *   **Inputs:** Position encoding, direction encoding, and appearance embeddings [S1].
    *   **Outputs:** Density ($\sigma$) and RGB color ($\mathbf{c}$) [S1].
*   **Position Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 4 [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** Learnable vectors integrated into the MLP to resolve exposure discrepancies [S1].
*   **Hash Coding:** Used in the initial stage of training for rapid query scenarios, combined with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Partitioning:** Divide the large-scale scene into a grid of sub-bounding boxes [S1].
2.  **Block Parallel Training:** Train each sub-block independently [S1].
    *   Initialize the MLP with hash coding and a small fusion MLP for the initial stage to handle high-frequency information efficiently [S1].
    *   Perform hierarchical sampling: 128 coarse samples and 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
    *   Sample 1024 rays per batch [S1].
    *   Optimize the network parameters using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.005 [S1].
    *   Optimize camera poses by back-propagating the loss gradient into the input pose computation, using a camera-optimized learning rate of 0.0005 [S1].
    *   Integrate appearance embeddings into the MLP to balance exposure variations across images [S1].
    *   Train for 5,000 or 30,000 iterations per block to prevent over-fitting [S1].
3.  **Essential Box Selection:** Determine the number of sub-bounding boxes required for merging by calculating intersections between the rays of the rendering area and the sub-bounding boxes [S1]. Use only these essential boxes for rendering to reduce computation [S1].
4.  **Shadow Mitigation via Expansion:**
    *   Create an expanded sub-bounding box larger than the original sub-bounding box to mitigate shadow influence from vertical faces [S1].
    *   Capture RGB values corresponding to projection areas generated by vertical surface ray irradiation from the smaller bounding box using the enlarged sub-bounding box [S1].
    *   Replace shadow occlusion regions in the original rendering with the captured RGB values from the projection areas to eliminate shadow influence [S1].
5.  **Merging and Uniformization:**
    *   Since expanded boxes overlap, uniformize the scene color in overlapping areas [S1].
    *   For depth rendering, employ depths falling within the scope of the previous larger sub-bounding box for merging [S1].
    *   Average depths pertaining to each sub-bounding box within overlapping regions [S1].
    *   Compare depth value averages across overlapping regions to adjust the overall depth for smoother continuity [S1].
6.  **Final Rendering:** Render the overhead view of the drone, combining the processed sub-scenes to produce a final image free from occlusion and shadow artifacts [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def drone_nerf_pipeline(scene_images, poses, intrinsics, box_grid, gt_images):
    # 1. Scene Partitioning [S1]
    sub_boxes = box_grid

    # 2. Block Parallel Training [S1]
    trained_models = {}
    for box in sub_boxes:
        # Initialize MLP: 8 hidden layers (256 units), 2 output layers (128 channels) [S1]
        mlp = init_mlp(hidden_dims=[256]*8, out_dim=128)
        # Initial stage uses hash coding with small fusion MLP [S1]
        mlp.use_hash_coding = True
        # Appearance embeddings for exposure balancing [S1]
        embeddings = np.zeros((len(scene_images), 128))
        # Camera poses for this block
        block_poses = poses[box]

        # Training loop: 5000 or 30000 iterations [S1]
        num_iters = 5000
        for i in range(num_iters):
            # Sample 1024 rays constrained to box [S1]
            rays = generate_rays_constrained(block_poses, intrinsics, box, batch_size=1024)

            # Hierarchical sampling: 128 coarse, 256 fine [S1]
            coarse_pts = stratified_sample(rays, num_samples=128)
            fine_pts = stratified_sample(rays, num_samples=256, density_field=coarse_pts)

            # Forward pass: pos enc (freq 64), dir enc (freq 4), embeddings [S1]
            pos_enc = positional_encoding(coarse_pts.xyz, max_freq=64)
            dir_enc = positional_encoding(coarse_pts.dir, max_freq=4)
            density, rgb = mlp_forward(mlp, pos_enc, dir_enc, embeddings)

            # Volume rendering [G]
            rendered_rgb, rendered_depth = volume_render(density, rgb, coarse_pts)

            # Loss computation [S1]
            loss = mean_squared_error(rendered_rgb, gt_images[box])

            # Back-propagation [S1]
            # Update MLP with Adam (lr=0.005)
            mlp.adam_step(loss, lr=0.005)
            # Optimize poses by back-propagating loss gradient into input pose (lr=0.0005)
            block_poses = optimize_poses(block_poses, loss, lr=0.0005)
            # Update embeddings
            embeddings = embeddings - 0.005 * compute_embedding_grad(loss, embeddings)

        trained_models[box] = {'mlp': mlp, 'poses': block_poses, 'embeddings': embeddings}

    # 3. Essential Box Selection [S1]
    rendering_area = get_rendering_area(scene_images)
    essential_boxes = [b for b in sub_boxes if ray_box_intersection(rendering_area, b)]

    # 4 & 5. Shadow Mitigation, Merging, and Uniformization [S1]
    merged_rgb = None
    merged_depth = None

    for box in essential_boxes:
        model = trained_models[box]

        # Create expanded sub-bounding box larger than previous one [S1]
        expanded_box = expand_box(box, margin_factor=0.1)

        # Render original and expanded boxes
        orig_rgb, orig_depth = render_box(model, box)
        exp_rgb, exp_depth = render_box(model, expanded_box)

        # Replace shadow occlusion regions with captured RGB from projection areas [S1]
        shadow_mask = detect_shadow_regions(orig_rgb, exp_rgb)
        corrected_rgb = np.where(shadow_mask, exp_rgb, orig_rgb)

        # Merge with existing result
        if merged_rgb is None:
            merged_rgb = corrected_rgb
            merged_depth = orig_depth
        else:
            # Handle overlaps
            overlap_mask = compute_overlap(merged_rgb, corrected_rgb)

            # Uniformize scene color in overlapping areas [S1]
            merged_rgb = uniformize_colors(merged_rgb, corrected_rgb, overlap_mask)

            # Depth merging: average depths in overlapping regions [S1]
            merged_depth = merge_depths(merged_depth, orig_depth, overlap_mask)

    return merged_rgb, merged_depth

# Helper functions (standard operations)
def init_mlp(hidden_dims, out_dim):
    return {'weights': [np.random.randn(d, d) for d in hidden_dims], 'out_w': np.random.randn(out_dim, hidden_dims[-1])}

def mlp_forward(mlp, pos_enc, dir_enc, embeddings):
    x = np.concatenate([pos_enc, dir_enc, embeddings], axis=-1)
    for w in mlp['weights']:
        x = np.maximum(0, x @ w)
    return x @ mlp['out_w']

def positional_encoding(x, max_freq):
    scales = np.linspace(1, max_freq, 10)
    return np.concatenate([np.sin(s*x), np.cos(s*x) for s in scales], axis=-1)

def generate_rays_constrained(poses, intrinsics, box, batch_size):
    return {'xyz': np.random.randn(batch_size, 3), 'dir': np.random.randn(batch_size, 3)}

def stratified_sample(rays, num_samples, density_field=None):
    t = np.linspace(0, 1, num_samples)
    return {'xyz': rays['xyz'] + t[:, None] * rays['dir'], 'dir': rays['dir']}

def volume_render(density, rgb, samples):
    delta = 0.1
    alpha = 1.0 - np.exp(-density * delta)
    T = np.ones(len(samples))
    acc_rgb = np.zeros((len(samples), 3))
    acc_depth = 0.0
    for i in range(len(samples)):
        T[i] *= (1.0 - alpha[i])
        acc_rgb[i] += alpha[i] * rgb[i]
        acc_depth += T[i] * samples['xyz'][i]
    return acc_rgb, acc_depth

def mean_squared_error(pred, target):
    return np.mean((pred - target)**2)

def mlp.adam_step(loss, lr):
    pass

def optimize_poses(poses, loss, lr):
    return poses - lr * np.gradient(loss, poses)

def compute_embedding_grad(loss, embeddings):
    return np.gradient(loss, embeddings)

def get_rendering_area(images):
    return np.array([[0, 0], [1, 1]])

def ray_box_intersection(rays, box):
    return True

def expand_box(box, margin_factor):
    return box + margin_factor

def render_box(model, box):
    return np.random.randn(1024, 3), np.random.randn(1024)

def detect_shadow_regions(orig, exp):
    return np.ones_like(orig, dtype=bool)

def compute_overlap(rgb1, rgb2):
    return np.ones_like(rgb1, dtype=bool)

def uniformize_colors(rgb1, rgb2, mask):
    return np.where(mask, (rgb1 + rgb2) / 2, rgb1)

def merge_depths(depth1, depth2, mask):
    return np.where(mask, (depth1 + depth2) / 2, depth1)
```

**Parameters:**
*   **MLP Architecture:** 8 hidden layers of 256 units, 2 output layers of 128 channels [S1].
*   **Position Encoding Highest Frequency:** 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding Highest Frequency:** 4 [S1].
*   **Camera-Optimized Learning Rate:** 0.0005 [S1].
*   **Hierarchical Sampling:** 128 coarse samples, 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays [S1].
*   **Adam Optimizer Initial Learning Rate:** 0.005 [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 5,000 and 30,000 iterations per block [S1].
*   **Initial Stage Optimization:** Uses hash coding with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-fitting:** High iteration counts can lead to over-fitting; the method limits training to 5,000 or 30,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Shadow Artifacts:** Vertical faces of sub-bounding boxes cause occlusions and shadows. This is mitigated by using an expanded sub-bounding box to capture projection areas [S1].
*   **Exposure Discrepancies:** Variations in camera exposure are resolved using appearance embeddings integrated into the MLP [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Drone-NeRF Training](8976-drone-nerf-training.md)
Optimizes large-scale 3D scene reconstruction from aerial imagery by addressing varying camera exposure. It differs from standard NeRF training by using view frustum sampling for anti-aliasing, a pose optimizer for joint pose refinement, and appearance embeddings for exposure balancing.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is performed in a block-parallel manner, allowing independent training of sub-blocks [S1]. Rendering uses only essential sub-bounding boxes to reduce computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The MLP architecture is relatively compact (8x256 + 2x128) [S1]. Initial stage uses hash coding for efficiency [S1]. Evaluated on an Nvidia 4070ti GPU with 12GB VRAM [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Building Scene (Mill 19 dataset), Drone-NeRF achieved an average PSNR of 17.401 (5k iterations), outperforming MipNeRF (16.637) and Instant-NGP (17.179) [S1]. It also outperformed baselines in SSIM and LPIPS metrics for both 5,000 and 30,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles large-scale scenes, mitigates shadow artifacts from vertical faces, balances exposure variations, and improves depth continuity in overlapping regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful selection of sub-bounding boxes and expansion margins. Performance is sensitive to iteration count due to over-fitting risks [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Large-scale aerial survey reconstruction where drone imagery exhibits varying exposure and vertical occlusion artifacts are prominent [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Small-scale scenes where block-parallel processing adds unnecessary complexity, or scenes without significant vertical structures causing shadow artifacts [S1].

## Background
Drone-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Drone-NeRF: Efficient NeRF based 3D scene reconstruction for large-scale drone survey" [S1]. It addresses the challenges of reconstructing large-scale environments from drone-captured images, specifically tackling issues related to exposure variations and shadow artifacts from vertical surfaces [S1]. The method builds on the principles of NeRF but introduces specific optimizations for aerial imagery, including block-parallel training and shadow mitigation techniques [S1].
