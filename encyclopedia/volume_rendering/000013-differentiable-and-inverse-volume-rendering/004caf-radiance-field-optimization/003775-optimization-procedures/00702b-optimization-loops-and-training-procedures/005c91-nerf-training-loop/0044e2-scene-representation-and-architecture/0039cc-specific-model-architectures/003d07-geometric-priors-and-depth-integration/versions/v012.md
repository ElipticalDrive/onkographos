# Geometric Priors and Depth Integration

Also called: Geometric Regularization, Depth-Guided NeRF, SDF-based NeRF

## Overview
Geometric Priors and Depth Integration methods enhance Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by incorporating explicit geometric constraints—such as depth maps, surface normals, or multi-view geometry—into the optimization process. This family solves the inherent ambiguity of sparse-view reconstruction and the "transparent" artifacts of standard NeRF, which relies solely on implicit photometric consistency. Unlike sibling methods that modify the network architecture (e.g., [AdaNeRF](6195-adanerf.md)) or use generative priors (e.g., [DiffusioNeRF](8703-diffusionerf.md)), this family explicitly guides the density field or sampling process using external geometric signals or multi-view consistency constraints.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family build on [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md) but diverge in how the geometric information is integrated into the optimization loop. The shared mechanism involves augmenting the standard photometric loss with geometric terms or modifying the ray sampling process to respect geometric boundaries.

**Geometric Loss Functions:**
Many methods introduce explicit loss terms that penalize deviations from geometric priors. Common forms include:
1.  **Depth Consistency Loss:** Minimizes the difference between the rendered depth (computed as the expected depth along the ray) and a prior depth map $D_{prior}$:
    $$ L_{depth} = \| D_{rendered} - D_{prior} \|_1 $$
2.  **Normal Smoothness:** Encourages surface normals derived from the density field to be smooth or aligned with estimated normals .
3.  **Epipolar Constraints:** Enforces that 3D points reconstructed from matched features in different views lie on the corresponding epipolar lines .

**Depth-Guided Sampling:**
Instead of uniform or hierarchical sampling along the entire ray, these methods use depth priors to concentrate samples near the estimated surface. This reduces computational cost and mitigates the "transparent" artifacts common in standard NeRF when geometry is ambiguous. For example, samples are clustered around the depth value predicted by a monocular depth estimator .

**Signed Distance Fields (SDF):**
Some variants replace the volumetric density $\sigma$ with a Signed Distance Field (SDF). The rendering integral is modified to integrate weights based on the SDF value relative to a truncation distance $tr$, effectively creating a thin shell around the surface rather than a volumetric blob. The weight for a sample at distance $d$ is often computed as:
$$ w = \sigma\left(\frac{d}{tr}\right) \cdot \sigma\left(-\frac{d}{tr}\right) $$
This allows for direct mesh extraction via Marching Cubes .

## Subtopics

### [SfMNeRF](8664-sfmnerf.md)
Integrates Structure-from-Motion (SfM) constraints, including matched feature positions and epipolar geometry, into the training loop. It uses sub-pixel rendering and a pyramidal optimization strategy to handle sparse inputs and avoid local minima.

### [DASNeRF](8668-dasnerf.md)
Uses monocular depth priors (DepthAnythingV2) to guide learning via Depth Ordering Fidelity Loss and Depth Structural Consistency Regularization. It employs dynamic optimal sampling to handle varying depth levels.

### [GeoNeRF](8710-geonerf.md)
Addresses sparse-view ambiguity by modeling geometry through a cascaded cost volume and Transformer-based attention. It extracts view-independent and view-dependent tokens from the cost volume to predict density and color.

### [Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction](11352-neural-rgb-d-surface-reconstruction.md)
Replaces density with a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) for metric 3D reconstruction from RGB-D data. It enforces hard boundaries between occupied and free space, enabling direct mesh extraction.

### [DINER](11354-diner.md)
Reconstructs NeRF from sparse RGB views using predicted dense depth maps to guide feature fusion and sampling. It solves high-disparity issues by leveraging depth priors for input views with large relative distances.

### [Volume Rendering Prior (VRPrior)](14662-volume-rendering-prior-vrprior.md)
Replaces handcrafted UDF-to-density mappings with a learned neural network $f_w$ that incorporates 3D spatial context from neighboring points. It learns the optimal weighting function for alpha blending directly from ground truth geometry.

### [Geometric Structure Constraint](16347-geometric-structure-constraint.md)
Integrates explicit geometric priors (normal smoothness, bilateral edge-aware constraints) directly into the NeRF optimization loop to improve elevation estimates, particularly for satellite imaging conditions.

## Background
The integration of geometric priors into NeRF optimization emerged to address the limitations of implicit volumetric representations, which often produce blurry or incomplete geometry in sparse-view scenarios. Early works like [SfMNeRF](8664-sfmnerf.md) and [DASNeRF](8668-dasnerf.md) focused on using multi-view geometry and monocular depth estimators to regularize the density field. Subsequent methods like [GeoNeRF](8710-geonerf.md) and [DINER](11354-diner.md) explored cost volumes and depth-guided feature fusion for better generalization. The shift towards explicit surface representations, such as TSDF in [Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction](11352-neural-rgb-d-surface-reconstruction.md), allowed for metric reconstruction and mesh extraction. More recent approaches like [VRPrior](14662-volume-rendering-prior-vrprior.md) and [Geometric Structure Constraint](16347-geometric-structure-constraint.md) refine the mapping between geometric fields and radiance, improving robustness and accuracy in complex scenes.
