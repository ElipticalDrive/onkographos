# Dynamic Neural Radiance Fields (TöRF)
Also called: Time-of-Flight Radiance Fields, TöRF

## Overview
TöRF is a dynamic neural radiance field method that reconstructs dynamic scenes by optimizing a neural representation against raw Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensor measurements (phasors) and RGB images, rather than relying on data-driven priors or processed depth maps [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Neural Deformable Voxel Grid (NDVG) by explicitly modeling the physics of ToF imaging to handle weakly-reflecting regions and moving cameras, enabling high-fidelity novel-view synthesis from a potentially moving color camera and C-ToF camera pair [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Synchronized RGB images and raw ToF phasor images (phase shift and amplitude) captured by a moving color camera and a C-ToF camera pair [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A dynamic radiance field capable of rendering novel-view RGB images and synthetic ToF phasor images for arbitrary viewpoints and times [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The method uses two multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs):
1.  **Static Network ($F_{\theta}^{\text{stat}}$):** A 5D function mapping position $\mathbf{x}_t$ and viewing direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_0$ to static density $\sigma^{\text{stat}}(\mathbf{x}_t)$, static radiance $L_s^{\text{stat}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0)$, and static radiant intensity $I_s^{\text{stat}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0)$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Dynamic Network ($F_{\theta}^{\text{dyn}}$):** A 6D function mapping position $\mathbf{x}_t$, viewing direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_0$, and time $\tau$ to dynamic density $\sigma^{\text{dyn}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau)$, dynamic radiance $L_s^{\text{dyn}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0)$, dynamic radiant intensity $I_s^{\text{dyn}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0, \tau)$, and a blending weight $b(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau)$ [S2, unofficial]. Time $\tau$ is not consumed directly but mapped via positional encoding to mitigate spectral bias [S2, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  Sample a ray $\mathbf{x}$ with direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_0$ at time $\tau$.
2.  Query the static network for $\sigma^{\text{stat}}$, $L_s^{\text{stat}}$, and $I_s^{\text{stat}}$ at sampled points $\mathbf{x}_t$.
3.  Query the dynamic network for $\sigma^{\text{dyn}}$, $L_s^{\text{dyn}}$, $I_s^{\text{dyn}}$, and blending weight $b(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau)$ at the same points.
4.  Blend static and dynamic outputs using $b(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau)$ to produce $\sigma^{\text{blend}}$, $L_s^{\text{blend}}$, and $I_s^{\text{blend}}$ [S2, unofficial]. The exact blending formula is Not given in the sources.
5.  Integrate the blended quantities along the ray using the rendering equations to compute RGB and ToF observations.
6.  Minimize a loss function comparing rendered outputs against ground truth RGB and phasor images to optimize network weights [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The method renders two observations along a ray $\mathbf{x}$ with direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_0$ at time $\tau$:
1.  **RGB Rendering:**
    $$L_{\text{RGB}}(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0, \tau) = \int_{t_n}^{t_i} T^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_t, \tau) \sigma^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau) L_s^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0, \tau) dt$$
2.  **ToF Rendering:**
    $$L_{\text{ToF}}(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0, \tau) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \frac{T^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_t, \tau)^2}{\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_t\|^2} \sigma^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \tau) I_s^{\text{blend}}(\mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\omega}_0, \tau) W(2 \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_t\|) dt$$
Where $T^{\text{blend}}$ is the accumulated transmittance, $W(\cdot)$ is a weighting function (e.g., system point spread function), and the inverse-square term accounts for distance attenuation [S2, unofficial]. The integral limits $t_n$, $t_i$, and $t_f$ are Not given in the sources.

**Parameters:**
Typical values and hyperparameters are Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Weak Signals:** Dark objects produce noisy depth in standard ToF processing; TöRF handles this by modeling raw phasors directly, falling back on triangulation cues when phasor magnitudes are small [S2, unofficial].
*   **Moving Cameras:** Unlike static-camera NeRF variants, TöRF supports moving rigs by incorporating time $\tau$ into the dynamic network [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision/Boundaries:** The method does not explicitly define near/far clipping bounds for integration [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Provides superior accuracy compared to NSFF [LNSW21] and space-time neural irradiance fields [XHKK21] [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Reduces the requirement on the number of input views due to strong geometric cues from ToF measurements [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles dynamic scenes with moving cameras; robust to weakly-reflecting surfaces; does not rely on pre-processed depth maps [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a synchronized color camera and C-ToF camera pair; involves optimizing two networks and rendering ToF physics [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use/Not:** Use when raw ToF phasors are available and dynamic scene reconstruction with moving cameras is required; not suitable for monocular RGB-only setups or when specialized ToF hardware is unavailable [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Background
TöRF was introduced by Alldieck et al. in 2021 [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of monocular RGB NeRF methods that rely on data-driven priors for dynamic content reconstruction. By integrating raw ToF sensor measurements, it leverages the single-view geometry capability of ToF sensors to improve reconstruction quality and reduce view requirements [S1, unofficial].
