# Drone-NeRF


## Overview
Drone-NeRF is a method for large-scale 3D scene reconstruction from aerial drone imagery, designed to handle unbounded spaces and varying camera exposure conditions [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF training by employing a block-parallel training strategy, view frustum sampling for anti-aliasing, joint pose optimization, appearance embeddings for exposure balancing, and a spatial contraction function to map unbounded scenes into a bounded domain [S1]. It specifically addresses artifacts caused by vertical face occlusions and shadows in overhead drone views through an expanded bounding box merging process [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of drone-captured images with known or estimated poses, intrinsics, and exposure settings. The scene is divided into sub-bounding boxes (sub-blocks).
*   **Outputs:** A merged radiance field representation, rendered RGB images, and depth maps for the entire scene, free from shadow artifacts and exposure discrepancies.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sub-bounding Boxes:** The scene is partitioned into a grid of sub-bounding boxes. Each box contains a trained local NeRF model.
*   **Expanded Sub-bounding Box:** A larger bounding box created around each sub-bounding box to capture projection areas from vertical faces, used to mitigate shadow influence [S1].
*   **MLP Network:** A fully connected network with ReLU activations.
    *   **Structure:** 8 hidden layers with 256 units each, followed by 2 output layers with 128 channels [S1].
    *   **Inputs:** Position encoding, direction encoding, and appearance embeddings.
    *   **Outputs:** Density ($\sigma$) and RGB color ($\mathbf{c}$).
*   **Position Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding:** Sine/cosine positional encoding with a highest frequency of 4 [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** Learnable vectors integrated into the MLP to resolve exposure discrepancies [S1].
*   **Hash Coding:** Used in the initial stage of training for rapid query scenarios, combined with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Partitioning:** Divide the large-scale scene into a grid of sub-bounding boxes.
2.  **Block Parallel Training:** Train each sub-block independently.
    *   Initialize the MLP with hash coding and a small fusion MLP for the initial stage to handle high-frequency information efficiently [S1].
    *   Perform hierarchical sampling: 128 coarse samples and 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
    *   Sample 1024 rays per batch [S1].
    *   Optimize the network parameters using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.005 [S1].
    *   Optimize camera poses by back-propagating the loss gradient into the input pose computation, using a camera-optimized learning rate of 0.0005 [S1].
    *   Integrate appearance embeddings into the MLP to balance exposure variations across images [S1].
    *   Train for 5,000 or 30,000 iterations per block to prevent over-fitting [S1].
3.  **Essential Box Selection:** Determine the number of sub-bounding boxes required for merging by calculating intersections between the rays of the rendering area and the sub-bounding boxes. Use only these essential boxes for rendering to reduce computation [S1].
4.  **Shadow Mitigation via Expansion:**
    *   Create an expanded sub-bounding box larger than the original sub-bounding box to mitigate shadow influence from vertical faces [S1].
    *   Capture RGB values corresponding to projection areas generated by vertical surface ray irradiation from the smaller bounding box using the enlarged sub-bounding box [S1].
    *   Replace shadow occlusion regions in the original rendering with the captured RGB values from the projection areas to eliminate shadow influence [S1].
5.  **Merging and Uniformization:**
    *   Since expanded boxes overlap, uniformize the scene color in overlapping areas [S1].
    *   For depth rendering, employ depths falling within the scope of the previous larger sub-bounding box for merging [S1].
    *   Average depths pertaining to each sub-bounding box within overlapping regions [S1].
    *   Compare depth value averages across overlapping regions to adjust the overall depth for smoother continuity [S1].
6.  **Final Rendering:** Render the overhead view of the drone, combining the processed sub-scenes to produce a final image free from occlusion and shadow artifacts [S1].

def drone_nrf_training(scene_images, initial_poses, intrinsics):
    # 1. Scene Partitioning
    sub_boxes = partition_scene_into_grid(scene_images)
    
    # 2. Block Parallel Training
    trained_models = {}
    for box in sub_boxes:
        # Initialize MLP with hash coding for initial stage
        mlp = initialize_mlp_with_hash_coding(
            hidden_layers=8, 
            hidden_units=256, 
            output_channels=128
        )
        
        # Initialize appearance embeddings
        appearance_embeddings = initialize_embeddings(len(scene_images))
        
        # Initialize camera poses for this block
        poses = initial_poses[box]
        
        # Training loop
        for iteration in range(5000): # Or 30,000
            # Hierarchical sampling
            coarse_samples = sample_hierarchical(
                rays=generate_rays(poses, intrinsics, box), 
                num_coarse=128, 
                num_fine=256
            )
            
            # Forward pass
            # Inputs: position encoding (freq 64), direction encoding (freq 4), appearance embeddings
            density, color = mlp_forward_pass(
                coarse_samples.positions, 
                coarse_samples.directions, 
                appearance_embeddings,
                pos_freq=64,
                dir_freq=4
            )
            
            # Volume rendering to get RGB and depth
            rendered_rgb, rendered_depth = volume_render(
                density, color, coarse_samples
            )
            
            # Compute loss against ground truth images
            loss = compute_loss(rendered_rgb, ground_truth_images[box])
            
            # Back-propagation
            # 1. Update MLP weights
            mlp.backward(loss)
            
            # 2. Optimize camera poses
            # Back-propagate loss gradient into input pose computation
            pose_gradients = compute_pose_gradients(loss, poses, intrinsics)
            poses = update_poses(poses, pose_gradients, lr=0.0005)
            
            # 3. Update appearance embeddings
            embed_gradients = compute_embedding_gradients(loss, appearance_embeddings)
            appearance_embeddings = update_embeddings(appearance_embeddings, embed_gradients)
            
            # Adam optimizer update for MLP weights (lr=0.005)
            mlp.adam_update(lr=0.005)
            
        trained_models[box] = {
            'mlp': mlp,
            'poses': poses,
            'embeddings': appearance_embeddings
        }
        
    # 3. Essential Box Selection
    rendering_area = get_rendering_area(scene_images)
    essential_boxes = select_essential_boxes(rendering_area, sub_boxes)
    
    # 4. Shadow Mitigation and Merging
    merged_rgb = None
    merged_depth = None
    
    for box in essential_boxes:
        model = trained_models[box]
        
        # Create expanded sub-bounding box
        expanded_box = expand_box(box, margin=SHADOW_MARGIN)
        
        # Capture RGB from expanded box for shadow replacement
        expanded_rgb = render_box(model, expanded_box)
        
        # Render original box
        original_rgb, original_depth = render_box(model, box)
        
        # Replace shadow regions in original_rgb with expanded_rgb values
        shadow_mask = detect_shadow_regions(original_rgb, expanded_rgb)
        corrected_rgb = replace_shadow_regions(original_rgb, expanded_rgb, shadow_mask)
        
        # Merge with existing merged result
        if merged_rgb is None:
            merged_rgb = corrected_rgb
            merged_depth = original_depth
        else:
            # Handle overlaps
            overlap_mask = compute_overlap(merged_rgb, corrected_rgb)
            
            # Uniformize color in overlaps
            merged_rgb = uniformize_colors(merged_rgb, corrected_rgb, overlap_mask)
            
            # Depth merging
            # Average depths in overlapping regions
            merged_depth = merge_depths(merged_depth, original_depth, overlap_mask)
            
    return merged_rgb, merged_depth

def render_box(model, box):
    # Generate rays constrained by the box
    rays = generate_rays_constrained(model['poses'], intrinsics, box)
    
    # Sample rays
    samples = sample_rays(rays, num_coarse=128, num_fine=256)
    
    # Query MLP
    density, color = model['mlp'](
        samples.positions, 
        samples.directions, 
        model['embeddings'],
        pos_freq=64,
        dir_freq=4
    )
    
    # Volume render
    rgb, depth = volume_render(density, color, samples)
    return rgb, depth

*   **Camera-Optimized Learning Rate:** 0.0005 [S1].
*   **Hierarchical Sampling:** 128 coarse samples, 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays [S1].
*   **Adam Optimizer Initial Learning Rate:** 0.005 [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 5,000 and 30,000 iterations per block [S1].
*   **Initial Stage Optimization:** Uses hash coding with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-fitting:** High iteration counts can lead to over-fitting; the method limits training to 5,000 or 30,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Shadow Artifacts:** Vertical faces of sub-bounding boxes cause occlusions and shadows. This is mitigated by using an expanded sub-bounding box to capture projection areas [S1].
*   **Exposure Discrepancies:** Variations in camera exposure are resolved using appearance embeddings integrated into the MLP [S1].
*   **Unbounded Space:** The method uses a spatial contraction function to map unbounded scenes into a bounded domain [S1].

### [Drone-NeRF Training](8976-drone-nerf-training.md)
Optimizes large-scale 3D scene reconstruction from aerial imagery by addressing unbounded space representation and varying camera exposure. It differs from standard NeRF training by using view frustum sampling for anti-aliasing, a pose optimizer for joint pose refinement, appearance embeddings for exposure balancing, and a spatial contraction function to map unbounded scenes into a bounded domain.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is performed in a block-parallel manner, allowing independent training of sub-blocks. Rendering uses only essential sub-bounding boxes to reduce computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The MLP architecture is relatively compact (8x256 + 2x128). Initial stage uses hash coding for efficiency [S1]. Evaluated on an Nvidia 4070ti GPU with 12GB VRAM [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Building Scene (Mill 19 dataset), Drone-NeRF achieved an average PSNR of 17.401 (5k iterations), outperforming MipNeRF (16.637) and Instant-NGP (17.179) [S1]. It also outperformed baselines in SSIM and LPIPS metrics for both 5,000 and 30,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles large-scale scenes, mitigates shadow artifacts from vertical faces, balances exposure variations, and improves depth continuity in overlapping regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful selection of sub-bounding boxes and expansion margins. Performance is sensitive to iteration count due to over-fitting risks [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Large-scale aerial survey reconstruction where drone imagery exhibits varying exposure and vertical occlusion artifacts are prominent [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Small-scale scenes where block-parallel processing adds unnecessary complexity, or scenes without significant vertical structures causing shadow artifacts [S1].

## Background
Drone-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Drone-NeRF: Efficient NeRF based 3D scene reconstruction for large-scale drone survey" [S1]. It addresses the challenges of reconstructing large-scale environments from drone-captured images, specifically tackling issues related to unbounded space, exposure variations, and shadow artifacts from vertical surfaces. The method builds on the principles of NeRF but introduces specific optimizations for aerial imagery, including block-parallel training and shadow mitigation techniques [S1].
