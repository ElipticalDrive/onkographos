# Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction

Also called: Hybrid Scene Representation Network, Neural RGB-D

## Overview
Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction is a method for high-quality, metric 3D reconstruction of room-scale scenes from an RGB-D input sequence. It solves the problem of generating clean, watertight meshes from noisy depth data by replacing the standard density-based volumetric representation of NeRF with a hybrid representation: a truncated signed distance field (TSDF) for geometry and a volumetric radiance field for appearance [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which produces semi-transparent artifacts and lacks metric geometry, this method enforces hard boundaries between occupied and free space, enabling direct mesh extraction via Marching Cubes [S1]. It builds on [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md) by integrating geometric priors directly into the rendering integral rather than as auxiliary losses alone.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** $N$ color frames $I_i$, corresponding aligned depth frames $D_i$, and initial camera poses $T_i$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP parameters, camera poses, and a triangle mesh extracted from the final TSDF.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Shape MLP:** A multi-layer perceptron.
    *   Input: Sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$ of a 3D query point $\mathbf{p}$.
    *   Output: Truncated signed distance value $D_i$.
2.  **Radiance MLP:** A multi-layer perceptron.
    *   Inputs: Sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ of the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, an intermediate feature vector from the Shape MLP, and a per-frame latent corrective code vector $\mathbf{z}_i$.
    *   Output: View-dependent surface radiance $\mathbf{c}_i$.
3.  **Deformation Field:** A 6-layer ReLU MLP acting as a residual correction for pixel locations.
4.  **Camera Poses:** Represented as Euler angles and a translation vector for each frame.
5.  **Scene Scaling:** The scene is scaled such that the truncation region maps to $[-1, 1]$, where positive values are in front of the surface and negative values are behind [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $tr$: Truncation distance. Typical value: $5$ cm [S1].
*   $\lambda_1$: RGB loss weight. Value: $0.1$ [S1].
*   $\lambda_2$: Free-space loss weight. Value: $10$ [S1].
*   $\lambda_3$: Truncation loss weight. Value: $6 \times 10^3$ [S1].
*   $S'_f$: Number of fine samples. Value: $16$ [S1].
*   $S'_c$: Coarse sampling rate. One sample for every $1.5$ cm of ray length on average [S1].
*   Ray length: Must exceed the largest distance in the scene. Typical range: $4$ to $8$ meters [S1].
*   Optimizer: ADAM with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Training iterations: $2 \times 10^5$ [S1].
*   Batch size: $|P_b| = 1024$ randomly chosen rays per iteration [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize camera poses $T_i$ using BundleFusion [S1]. Initialize MLP weights.
2.  **Ray Generation & Refinement:** For a pixel $p$ in batch $b$:
    a. Retrieve the 2D pixel coordinate.
    b. Apply the deformation field MLP to get a 2D shift vector.
    c. Add the shift to the pixel coordinate.
    d. Unproject the corrected 2D coordinate into a 3D ray using the camera pose $T_i$ and intrinsics.
3.  **Coarse Sampling:** Generate $S'_c$ sample points along the ray using stratified sampling.
4.  **Coarse Evaluation:** Evaluate the Shape MLP at these $S'_c$ points to get signed distance values.
5.  **Zero-Crossing Search:** Search for the zero-crossing (where $D_i$ changes sign) in the coarse samples to estimate the surface depth.
    *   *Pitfall:* If the sampling rate $S'_c$ is too low, samples may miss the truncation region, causing the zero-crossing to be missed [S1].
6.  **Fine Sampling:** Generate $S'_f$ sample points clustered around the identified zero-crossing.
7.  **Fine Evaluation:** Evaluate the Shape MLP and Radiance MLP at all $S'_c + S'_f$ samples (concatenating outputs from coarse and fine passes).
8.  **Weight Calculation:** For each sample $i$ with signed distance $D_i$, compute the weight $w_i$:
    $$ w_i = \sigma \left( \frac{D_i}{tr} \right) \cdot \sigma \left( -\frac{D_i}{tr} \right) $$
    where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function. Weights fall to zero as distance from the surface increases. To account for multiple intersections, weights of samples beyond the first truncation region are set to zero [S1].
9.  **Color Integration:** Compute the rendered color $\mathbf{C}$ as a weighted sum:
    $$ \mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i} \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where $K = S'_c + S'_f$ is the total number of samples, and $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the radiance output from the Radiance MLP [S1].
10. **Loss Computation:** Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P})$ for the batch:
    $$ \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{b=0}^{B-1} \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P}) $$
    *   **RGB Loss:** $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} (\mathbf{C}_p - \hat{\mathbf{C}}_p)^2$, where $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_p$ is the observed color [S1].
    *   **Free-Space Loss:** For samples $s$ between the camera origin and the truncation region ($\mathcal{S}_p^{fs}$):
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p^{fs}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p^{fs}} (D_s - tr)^2 $$
        This forces the MLP to predict $tr$ for free space [S1].
    *   **Truncation Loss:** For samples $s$ within the truncation region ($\mathcal{S}_p^{tr}$):
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p^{tr}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p^{tr}} (D_s - \hat{D}_s)^2 $$
        where $\hat{D}_s$ is the signed distance observed by the depth sensor along the optical axis [S1].
11. **Optimization:** Update network parameters $\Theta$, camera poses $T_i$, latent codes, and deformation field using ADAM [S1].
12. **Mesh Extraction:** After training, extract a triangle mesh from the optimized TSDF using Marching Cubes with a spatial resolution of $1$ cm [S1].

        super().__init__()
        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([nn.Linear(input_dim, hidden_dim)] + 
                                    [nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim) for _ in range(4)])
        self.out = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, output_dim)

        h = x

## Subtopics

### [InFusionSurf](12834-infusionsurf.md)
InFusionSurf is a NeRF-style RGB-D 3D surface reconstruction framework that refines depth information to enhance reconstruction quality with minimal impact on optimization time, using per-frame intrinsic refinement and TSDF Fusion prior learning. It addresses camera motion-induced blurs in depth frames by correcting the camera intrinsic matrix per frame and accelerates training by pre-training the geometric prior using classical TSDF Fusion. It builds on [Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction](11352-neural-rgb-d-surface-reconstruction.md) by adding specific intrinsic refinement and a two-stage initialization process.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires $2 \times 10^5$ iterations. Each iteration processes 1024 rays. The two-step sampling (coarse + fine) doubles the forward pass cost compared to single-pass methods.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the MLP size and the number of samples per ray. The deformation field and latent codes add minor overhead.
*   **Measured Performance:** On 10 synthetic scenes, the method achieved a Chamfer $\ell_1$ distance of 0.044, IoU of 0.747, Normal Consistency of 0.918, and F-score of 0.924 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces high-quality, metric 3D reconstructions with hard boundaries, avoiding the semi-transparent artifacts common in density-based NeRFs. Robust to noisy depth data by combining color and depth losses.
*   **Limitations:** Requires RGB-D input. Performance depends on the quality of initial camera poses (initialized via BundleFusion). If coarse sampling is too sparse, the zero-crossing may be missed, leading to reconstruction failure [S1].
*   **When to use:** When metric 3D mesh reconstruction is required from RGB-D sequences, especially in room-scale scenes.
*   **When not to use:** When only novel view synthesis is needed without metric geometry, or when only RGB input is available (use standard NeRF or RGB-D methods with monocular depth estimation).

## Background
Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction was proposed by Dejan Azinović, Ricardo Martin-Brualla, Dan B. Goldman, Matthias Nießner, and Justus Thies in 2022 [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2022. The method addresses the limitations of neural radiance fields (NeRF) which, while excellent for novel view synthesis, do not reconstruct metric and clean meshes. It builds on the idea of implicit surface representations (SDFs) and integrates them with the differentiable rendering framework of NeRF. It is related to concurrent work such as NeuS [S1]. The method is particularly useful for AR/VR applications, teleconferencing, and robotic applications requiring high-fidelity 3D models.
