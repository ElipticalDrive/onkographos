# Incremental Registration

Also called: Incremental CONfidence (ICON) registration, Incremental Image Registration

## Overview
Incremental Registration is a training strategy for NeRF and radiance field optimization that registers camera poses sequentially following the temporal order of video frames [S2]. It solves the problem of non-convex pose optimization and local minima by leveraging the intuition that adjacent camera motions in videos are smooth, initializing new poses close to previous ones [S2]. Unlike standard joint optimization, it builds the dataset $\mathcal{D}$ iteratively, freezing previously registered parameters to prevent catastrophic forgetting and ensure stability [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sequence of unposed images $\{I_1, \dots, I_N\}$ and a scene regressor (e.g., NeRF or Gaussian Splatting model) $\Theta$.
*   **Output:** Optimized radiance field parameters $\Theta$ and camera poses $\{P_1, \dots, P_N\}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Training Buffer:** A set of registered images and their corresponding poses. Initially contains $\{I_1, I_2\}$ with optimized poses $\{P_1, P_2\}$ [S2].
*   **Batch Buffer ($I_{buf}$):** A subset of unregistered images selected for the current registration epoch [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $k$: Iteration interval for adding new frames. Typical values: 1,000 iterations for CO3D/HO3D datasets; 500 iterations for LLFF [S2].
*   Freeze Duration: Iterations to freeze learning rates after registration. Typical values: 100,000 iterations for HO3D/CO3D; 30,000 for LLFF [S2].
*   $s_{inlier}$: Inlier threshold for RANSAC. Typical value: 5,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   Reprojection Error Threshold: 6 pixels [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Jointly optimize NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and poses $\{P_1, P_2\}$ using the first two frames $\{I_1, I_2\}$ [S2].
2.  **Iterative Registration Loop:** Repeat until all $N$ images are registered:
    a.  **Batch Selection:** Select a batch of unregistered images $I_{buf} = \{I_k\}$ [S1, unofficial].
    b.  **Reference Selection:** Select a reference image $I_i$ from the registered set that connects to the most unregistered images [S1, unofficial].
    c.  **Pose Initialization:** For each target image $I_k \in I_{buf}$, initialize its pose $P_k$ using the previous pose $P_{k-1}$ (temporal smoothness) [S2].
    d.  **Coarse Pose Estimation:**
        i.  Pass $I_i$ and $I_k$ to the scene regressor to obtain 3D points $\{X_k\}$ in the global coordinate frame [S1, unofficial].
        ii. Establish 2D-3D correspondences $\{(u_k, X_k)\}$ where $u_k$ are pixel coordinates [S1, unofficial].
        iii. Use RANSAC and Perspective-n-Point (PnP) to estimate $P_k$. The loss function for optimization is:
        $$ \rho(\|d_{i,k} \cdot \nu_{i,k} - (X_i - C_k)\|_2) $$
        where $C_k$ is the camera center, $d_{i,k}$ is a scaling factor, and $\nu_{i,k}$ is the ray direction [S1, unofficial].
        iv. Accept $I_k$ into the training buffer only if the number of inliers exceeds $s_{inlier}$ [S1, unofficial].
    e.  **Training:** Add registered images to the training buffer and train the scene regressor $\Theta$ [S1, unofficial].
    f.  **Freezing:** Freeze learning rates for poses $\{P_i\}$ and $\Theta$ for the specified freeze duration [S2].
    g.  **Interval Check:** If the current iteration count is a multiple of $k$, add the next frame $I_{i+1}$ and initialize $P_{i+1} \approx P_i$ [S2].
3.  **Finalization:**
    a.  Optimize all camera poses using the reprojection loss (Eq. 8 in [S1, unofficial]) to correct accumulated errors [S1, unofficial].
    b.  Fix the seed image pose and optimize all others [S1, unofficial].
    c.  Apply a learning rate decay schedule if necessary [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of frames $N$, but each registration step involves expensive 3D point estimation and RANSAC. Total training time is significantly higher than standard NeRF due to iterative pose optimization [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Scales with the number of registered images and the scene regressor size. No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, except for storing intermediate 3D points during registration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to large initial pose errors by leveraging temporal smoothness; avoids local minima associated with joint optimization of all poses and radiance fields simultaneously [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires temporal order of images; performance degrades if camera motion is not smooth or if there are large gaps between frames [S2]. Accumulated errors in poses may require final refinement [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Video sequences or ordered image collections where camera motion is smooth [S2].
*   **When not to use:** Unordered image collections or scenes with rapid, non-smooth camera motion [S2].

## Background
Incremental Registration was proposed in the ICON (Incremental CONfidence) paper by Wang et al. (2024) [S2]. It addresses the breaking point of 20-degree rotation difference observed in prior work (Wang et al., 2021b) by relying on the intuition that camera motions in videos are smooth [S2]. The method builds on BARF (Blending Arbitrary Reference Frames) settings, using coarse-to-fine positional encoding strategies [S2]. It is used in datasets like CO3D, HO3D, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF360 [S1, unofficial][S2].
