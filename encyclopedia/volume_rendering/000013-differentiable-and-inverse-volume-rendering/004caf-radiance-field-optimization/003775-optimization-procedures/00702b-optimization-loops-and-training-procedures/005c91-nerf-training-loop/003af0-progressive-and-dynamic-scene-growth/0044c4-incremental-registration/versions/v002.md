# Incremental Registration

Also called: Incremental CONfidence (ICON) registration, Incremental Image Registration

## Overview
Incremental Registration is a training strategy for joint pose and radiance field optimization that registers camera frames sequentially following their temporal order, leveraging the intuition that adjacent camera poses in video sequences are spatially close [S2]. It solves the non-convex optimization landscape and local minima inherent in simultaneous pose and NeRF optimization by providing a robust initialization for new frames based on previously registered ones, thereby preventing "overlapping registration" errors where incorrect poses mislead the scene regressor [S2]. Unlike global registration methods that optimize all poses simultaneously, this method builds the trajectory incrementally, freezing previously registered parameters to stabilize the optimization [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sequence of unposed images $\{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_N\}$; a scene regressor $f_{SCR}$ capable of predicting 3D coordinates and features from image pairs; hyperparameters for iteration intervals and freeze durations.
**Outputs:** Optimized NeRF parameters $\Theta$, camera poses $\{P_i\}_{i=1}^N$, and the final radiance field.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Training Buffer:** A set of registered images and their associated poses.
*   **Scene Regressor ($f_{SCR}$):** A neural network (e.g., Transformer-based) that takes two images $(I_{ref}, I_{target})$ and outputs 3D points $\{X\}$ and features $\{G\}$ in a global coordinate frame.
*   **Pose Representation:** Camera poses $P_i$ (extrinsics) and intrinsics.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Seed Initialization:**
    *   Select the first two frames $\{I_1, I_2\}$.
    *   Jointly optimize NeRF parameters $\Theta$ and poses $\{P_1, P_2\}$ for a specified number of iterations [S2].
    *   *Note:* In some implementations (e.g., ZeroGS), a seed image is used to initialize the regressor with fixed identity pose before incremental steps [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Incremental Frame Addition:**
    *   Iterate through the remaining frames $I_i$ in temporal order.
    *   After every $k$ iterations of training the current buffer, add the next frame $I_i$ to the buffer [S2].
    *   Initialize the new pose $P_i$ using the previous pose $P_{i-1}$ (i.e., $P_i \approx P_{i-1}$) [S2].

3.  **Coarse Camera Pose Estimation (for new batch):**
    *   Select a registered reference image $I_{ref}$ that connects to the most unregistered images [S1, unofficial].
    *   Pass $I_{ref}$ and the target image $I_{target}$ (the new frame) to the scene regressor to obtain 3D points $\{X_k\}$ and features [S1, unofficial].
    *   Establish 2D-3D correspondences $\{(u_k, X_k)\}$ where $u_k$ are pixel coordinates [S1, unofficial].
    *   Estimate the initial pose using RANSAC and Perspective-n-Point (PnP) [S1, unofficial].
    *   The optimization minimizes the reprojection error:
        $$ \min_{X_i, C_k} \sum_{i,k} \rho(\|d_{i,k} \cdot \nu_{i,k} - (X_i - C_k)\|_2) $$
        where $C_k$ is the camera center, $d_{i,k}$ is a scaling factor, and $\nu_{i,k}$ is the ray direction [S1, unofficial].
    *   Filter outliers using an inlier threshold $s_{inlier}$ (e.g., 5,000 inliers) and reprojection error threshold (e.g., 6 pixels) [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Training and Freezing:**
    *   Add the newly registered image and its initialized pose to the training buffer.
    *   Train the scene regressor and optimize the new pose.
    *   **Freeze:** Freeze the learning rates for all previously registered poses $\{P_j\}_{j < i}$ and the NeRF parameters $\Theta$ for a duration of $T_{freeze}$ iterations [S2]. This prevents catastrophic forgetting and stabilizes the growing scene representation [S2].
    *   Only optimize the poses registered in the current epoch/batch to improve efficiency [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Convergence and Expansion:**
    *   Repeat steps 2-4 until all $N$ images are registered.
    *   Upon convergence of the incremental phase, expand the buffer or proceed to final refinement [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Final Refinement (Optional):**
    *   Optimize all camera poses jointly using the photometric loss (Eq. 8 in [S1, unofficial]) to correct accumulated errors [S1, unofficial].
    *   Fix the seed image pose and optimize the rest [S1, unofficial].
    *   Apply a learning rate decay schedule if not already applied [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $k$: Iteration interval for adding new frames.
    *   1,000 iterations for CO3D/HO3D datasets [S2].
    *   500 iterations for LLFF dataset [S2].
*   $T_{freeze}$: Duration to freeze learning rates after registration.
    *   100,000 iterations for HO3D and CO3D [S2].
    *   30,000 iterations for LLFF [S2].
*   $s_{inlier}$: Inlier threshold for RANSAC/PnP. Value: 5,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   Reprojection Error Threshold: 6 pixels [S1, unofficial].
*   Finetuning Iterations:
    *   1,000 iterations on LLFF during incremental training [S1, unofficial].
    *   1,500 iterations on Mip-NeRF360 during incremental training [S1, unofficial].
    *   500 iterations for seed initialization [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Local Minima:** Optimizing poses and NeRF jointly is highly non-convex. Incorrect pose initialization can lead to "overlapping registration," where the model re-registers to an already viewed viewpoint [S2]. Incremental initialization mitigates this by using smooth motion priors [S2].
*   **Pose Drift:** Errors accumulate during incremental registration. Final refinement is necessary to correct global consistency [S1, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion:** Avoid sampling rays from occluded regions during training [S2].
*   **Breaking Point:** Without incremental registration, a rotation difference of ~20 degrees in the trajectory can cause optimization failure [S2].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def incremental_registration(images, scene_regressor, k_interval, freeze_duration):
    """
    images: List of image tensors [I1, I2, ..., IN]
    scene_regressor: Function f_SCR(I_ref, I_target) -> (X_3d, G_feat)
    k_interval: int, iterations between adding new frames
    freeze_duration: int, iterations to freeze old poses/NeRF
    """
    N = len(images)
    registered_poses = []
    registered_images = []
    
    # Step 1: Seed Initialization
    # Jointly optimize NeRF and first two poses
    theta, poses_12 = optimize_joint(images[0], images[1], scene_regressor)
    registered_poses.append(poses_12)
    registered_images.append([images[0], images[1]])
    
    current_iter = 0
    
    # Step 2: Incremental Loop
    for i in range(2, N):
        target_image = images[i]
        
        # Select reference image from registered set
        # Heuristic: select reference connecting to most unregistered images
        ref_image = select_best_reference(registered_images, N, i)
        
        # Step 3: Coarse Pose Estimation
        # Get 3D points from regressor
        X_3d, G_feat = scene_regressor(ref_image, target_image)
        
        # Establish 2D-3D correspondences
        # u_k are pixel coordinates of target_image
        correspondences = get_2d_3d_pairs(target_image, X_3d)
        
        # RANSAC + PnP to estimate initial pose P_i
        # Initialize P_i based on previous pose P_{i-1}
        P_prev = registered_poses[-1]
        P_i_init = initialize_pose_from_prev(P_prev, correspondences)
        
        # Step 4: Train and Freeze
        # Add new frame to buffer
        registered_poses.append(P_i_init)
        registered_images.append(target_image)
        
        # Freeze old parameters
        freeze_parameters(registered_poses[:-1], theta, freeze_duration)
        
        # Optimize new pose and update regressor
        theta, P_i = optimize_incremental(
            registered_images, 
            registered_poses, 
            scene_regressor, 
            iterations=k_interval
        )
        
        current_iter += k_interval
        
        # Check for convergence or buffer expansion if necessary
        if not check_convergence(registered_images, registered_poses):
            break
            
    # Step 5: Final Refinement
    # Optimize all poses jointly to correct drift
    theta, all_poses = refine_all_poses(
        registered_images, 
        registered_poses, 
        scene_regressor, 
        fix_seed=True
    )
    
    return theta, all_poses
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of frames $N$, as each frame is processed once. However, the cost per frame depends on the regression and PnP steps. Freezing old parameters reduces the optimization burden per step [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Scales linearly with $N$ as the training buffer grows. The scene regressor memory footprint is constant [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Strengths:** Robust initialization for poses in video sequences; avoids local minima associated with global optimization; efficient as it freezes stable parameters [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires temporal order or a known sequence; susceptible to drift if early frames are misregistered; not suitable for unordered image collections without a pre-defined graph [S2].
*   **When to use:** Video-based reconstruction, datasets with smooth camera motion (e.g., CO3D, HO3D, LLFF) [S2].
*   **When not to use:** Unordered image sets, scenes with large baseline jumps (>20 degrees rotation) between adjacent frames without intermediate steps [S2].

## Background
Incremental Registration was introduced in the context of joint pose and radiance field optimization to address the non-convexity of simultaneous optimization [S2]. It builds on the observation that camera motions in videos are smooth, allowing adjacent poses to be initialized from one another [S2]. The method is closely related to BARF (Blending Arbitrary Reference Frames) but differs in its incremental, frame-by-frame addition strategy rather than frequency-based blending [S2]. It is used in datasets like CO3D, HO3D, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF360 [S1, unofficial][S2].
