# Progressive and Dynamic Scene Growth

Also called: Adaptive Capacity Expansion, Incremental Scene Construction

## Overview
This family of methods addresses the limitations of static scene representations by dynamically expanding the model's capacity or the training dataset during optimization. Unlike static NeRF variants that allocate fixed resources upfront, these approaches grow the representation (via network branches or spatial structures) or the supervision signal (via view synthesis) in response to reconstruction uncertainty, temporal sequence, or active exploration. This enables efficient coarse-to-fine modeling, handles large-scale urban environments, and mitigates artifacts in single-panorama synthesis.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share a core mechanism: the training loop is not a single monolithic optimization over a fixed set of parameters and data. Instead, it consists of alternating phases of **optimization** and **growth/expansion**.

1.  **Evaluation:** The current model state is evaluated to identify regions of high error, uncertainty, or unobserved space.
2.  **Expansion:** Based on the evaluation, the system expands. This expansion can be:
    *   **Structural:** Adding new network branches, blocks, or feature grid regions [S1].
    *   **Data-driven:** Adding new reference views or inpainted ground truth [S1].
    *   **Sequential:** Registering new frames from a video stream .
3.  **Re-optimization:** The expanded system is re-optimized, often with adjusted learning rates or freezing strategies to stabilize the new components while preserving previously learned geometry [S1].

The key differentiator from standard NeRF training is the **conditional trigger** for growth. Standard training uses a fixed schedule; this family uses metrics (uncertainty, inlier count, temporal order) to decide *when* and *where* to grow.

## Subtopics

### [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md)
A training procedure that adaptively increases model capacity by adding network branches in regions of high reconstruction uncertainty. Unlike static architectures, it clusters high-error query points and spawns child networks to refine predictions locally, enabling efficient coarse-to-fine scene representation. It differs from Progressive View Synthesis Training by focusing on spatial density field expansion rather than view synthesis expansion.
*   **When to choose:** When the scene has varying levels of detail and static capacity leads to overfitting in simple regions or underfitting in complex ones.
*   **Link:** [Dynamic Growth Parameters](2968-dynamic-growth-parameters.md)

### [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md)
A training strategy for Panoramic NeRF that improves geometry and semantic coherence in occluded regions by progressively adding reference views to a single input panorama. It addresses "foggy" artifacts and semantic incoherence common in single-panorama synthesis by integrating collaborative RGBD inpainting and an inpainting-and-erasing strategy. It differs from Dynamic Growth Parameters by focusing on view synthesis expansion rather than spatial density field expansion, and from Online Incremental NeRF Optimization by using a pre-defined progressive sampling schedule rather than active agent acquisition [8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md].
*   **When to choose:** When reconstructing scenes from a single panoramic input where occlusions lead to ambiguous geometry.
*   **Link:** [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md)

### [Incremental Registration](11341-incremental-registration.md)
A training strategy that registers camera frames sequentially following their temporal order, leveraging the intuition that camera motions in videos are smooth and adjacent poses are close. It solves the problem of non-convex pose optimization in unposed image sequences by providing a good initialization for camera poses, thereby avoiding local minima and "overlapping registration" errors where incorrect poses mislead the radiance field. It differs from Dynamic Growth Parameters by triggering growth based on temporal sequence rather than uncertainty, and from Online Incremental NeRF Optimization by processing a fixed offline sequence rather than active agent acquisition.
*   **When to choose:** When training on unposed video sequences where global pose optimization is unstable.
*   **Link:** [Incremental Registration](11341-incremental-registration.md)

### [Online Incremental NeRF Optimization](12066-online-incremental-nerf-optimization.md)
Addresses the problem of reconstructing 3D scenes from a continuous stream of incoming camera views, rather than a fixed, pre-collected dataset. It differs from offline methods like DVGO by updating the scene representation dynamically as new observations are acquired, enabling active exploration where the agent plans the next best view based on the current model's uncertainty.
*   **When to choose:** For robotic navigation or real-time SLAM systems where the scene is observed incrementally.
*   **Link:** [Online Incremental NeRF Optimization](12066-online-incremental-nerf-optimization.md)

### CityNeRF
A progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the NeRF model and training set [S1]. It starts by fitting distant views with shallow basic blocks. New blocks are continuously attached as training iterates to adapt to details in far-near views. This effectively activates high-frequency channels in position encoding and unfolds more complex details as training progresses [S1].

## Background
The concept of progressive learning in NeRF was introduced by Xiangli et al. in 2021 with CityNeRF, which proposed synchronously growing the NeRF model and training set to handle large-scale urban environments [S1]. This addressed the limitations of Block-NeRF (Tancik et al., 2022), which decomposed scenes into static blocks [S1]. Dynamic growth parameters were later introduced to handle uncertainty-driven capacity expansion [S1]. Incremental registration methods emerged to handle unposed video sequences by leveraging temporal smoothness . Online incremental optimization extends this to active exploration scenarios [S1].
