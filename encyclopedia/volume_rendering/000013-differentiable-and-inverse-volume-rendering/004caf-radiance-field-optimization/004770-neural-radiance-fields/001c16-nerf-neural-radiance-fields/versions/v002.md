# NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields)

Also called: NeRF: Representing Scenes as Neural Radiance Fields for View Synthesis, Neural Radiance Field

## Overview
NeRF models a 3D scene as a continuous 5D function mapping spatial coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and viewing directions $(\theta, \phi)$ to volume density $\sigma$ and emitted RGB color [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S8, S11, S13, unofficial]. It solves the problem of novel view synthesis by optimizing an implicit neural representation to match observed multi-view images, enabling photorealistic rendering of complex scenes with view-dependent effects like specularities and transparency [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S10, S13, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from [Explicit Radiance Fields](806-explicit-radiance-fields.md) by using a continuous MLP representation rather than discrete grids, and from [NeRF with Softplus Activation and Importance Sampling](3524-nerf-with-softplus-activation-and-importance-sampling.md) by employing a specific hierarchical sampling strategy and asymmetric network architecture [S4, S8, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of $N$ posed 2D images $\{I_i\}$ with known camera poses (rotation $R_i$ and translation $t_i$) and intrinsics (focal length $f$).
**Outputs:** A rendered image $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a novel camera pose, and a trained MLP model.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ray Bundle:** For each pixel $(u, v)$ in an image of height $H$ and width $W$, a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ is defined.
    *   $\mathbf{o} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Camera origin (world coordinates).
    *   $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Unit viewing direction (world coordinates).
2.  **Sample Points:** Along each ray, $N$ points $t_i$ are sampled within $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$.
    *   $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{r}(t_i) \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D spatial coordinate.
    *   $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Viewing direction (shared for all samples on the ray).
3.  **Neural Network (MLP):**
    *   **Positional Encoding:** Maps input $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ or $\mathbb{R}^3$ to $\mathbb{R}^{2L \cdot D}$ using sinusoidal features [S2, S5, S11, S13, unofficial]:
        $$ \gamma(\mathbf{p}) = (\mathbf{p}, \sin(2^0 \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^0 \pi \mathbf{p}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{p})) $$
        *   Position encoding: $L=10$, input dim $3 \to 63$ [S4, S5, unofficial].
        *   Direction encoding: $L=4$, input dim $3 \to 27$ [S4, S5, unofficial].
    *   **Architecture:** An 8-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with 256 hidden units and ReLU activation [S4, S5, S11, unofficial].
        *   **Coarse/Fine Networks:** Two identical MLPs are trained: a coarse network and a fine network [S1, S4, S5, unofficial].
        *   **Asymmetric Input Injection:**
            *   The encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ is fed into the first 4 layers.
            *   A skip connection concatenates the original encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ to the output of layer 4 [S1, S4, S5, unofficial].
            *   Layers 5-7 process the combined features.
            *   Layer 8 outputs the volume density $\sigma$ and an intermediate feature vector $\mathbf{h}$ [S5, S13, unofficial].
            *   The encoded direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ is concatenated with $\mathbf{h}$ and passed through a final layer (or small sub-network) to predict RGB color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1, S2, S5, S13, unofficial].
            *   Density $\sigma$ is predicted *without* the viewing direction to encourage geometric consistency [S1, S2, unofficial].
        *   **Output Activations:**
            *   $\sigma$: ReLU or Softplus (to ensure $\sigma \ge 0$) [S4, S8, unofficial].
            *   $\mathbf{c}$: Sigmoid (to ensure $\mathbf{c} \in [0, 1]$) [S5, S8, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Generation:** For a target image of size $H \times W$, generate rays for each pixel.
    *   Compute normalized image coordinates: $x = (u - W/2)/f$, $y = -(v - H/2)/f$, $z = -1$ [S5, S7, unofficial].
    *   Transform direction to world space: $\mathbf{d}_{world} = R \cdot \mathbf{d}_{cam}$ [S5, S7, unofficial].
    *   Set origin: $\mathbf{o} = t$ [S5, S7, unofficial].
    *   Normalize $\mathbf{d}_{world}$ to unit length [S5, S7, unofficial].

2.  **Coarse Sampling:**
    *   Divide the interval $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ into $N_c$ equal bins (typically $N_c=64$) [S4, S5, unofficial].
    *   **Stratified Sampling:** During training, draw one sample uniformly at random from each bin to break symmetry and improve coverage [S3, S5, S6, unofficial].
        $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U}\left[t_{near} + \frac{i-1}{N_c}(t_{far}-t_{near}), \quad t_{near} + \frac{i}{N_c}(t_{far}-t_{near})\right] $$
    *   If `lindisp` is true, sample linearly in inverse depth ($1/t$) [S3, unofficial].
    *   Add jitter if `perturb` is true [S3, S5, unofficial].

3.  **Coarse Network Forward Pass:**
    *   Apply positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x}_i)$ to each sample point [S5, S8, unofficial].
    *   Pass through the coarse MLP to get raw density $\sigma_i$ and feature $\mathbf{h}_i$ [S5, S13, unofficial].
    *   Concatenate $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ with $\mathbf{h}_i$ and pass through the final layer to get color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S5, S13, unofficial].

4.  **Coarse Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute segment lengths: $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$. For the last segment, use a large value (e.g., $10^{10}$) to ensure the ray terminates [S3, S5, S8, unofficial].
    *   Scale $\delta_i$ by the ray direction norm if directions are not unit length [S3, unofficial].
    *   Compute opacity: $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S5, S6, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance: $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ [S5, S6, unofficial].
    *   Compute weights: $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ [S5, S6, unofficial].
    *   Render coarse color: $\hat{C}_{coarse} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} w_i \mathbf{c}_i$ [S5, S6, unofficial].

5.  **Importance Sampling (Hierarchical Sampling):**
    *   Use the coarse weights $w_i$ to form a probability distribution. Exclude the last sample (which has infinite depth) [S5, unofficial].
    *   Normalize weights to form a PDF: $p_i = w_i / \sum w_i$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Compute the Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) [S5, unofficial].
    *   Draw $N_f$ uniform random numbers $u \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]$ [S5, unofficial].
    *   Use inverse CDF sampling (binary search on CDF) to find new sample locations $t'_j$ in regions of high coarse density [S5, unofficial].
    *   Typically $N_f = 128$ [S4, S5, unofficial].

6.  **Fine Network Forward Pass:**
    *   Combine coarse samples $\{t_i\}$ and fine samples $\{t'_j\}$ into a single set of $N_c + N_f$ points [S6, unofficial].
    *   Sort the points by $t$ [S6, unofficial].
    *   Re-compute segment lengths $\delta$ for the sorted points [S6, unofficial].
    *   Pass all points through the **fine** MLP to get $\sigma'_k$ and $\mathbf{c}'_k$ [S6, unofficial].

7.  **Fine Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute $\alpha'_k$ and $T'_k$ for the fine samples [S6, unofficial].
    *   Render fine color: $\hat{C}_{fine} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_c+N_f} w'_k \mathbf{c}'_k$ [S6, unofficial].

8.  **Loss Computation and Optimization:**
    *   Compute Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered colors and ground truth images $I$ [S2, S4, S13, unofficial].
    *   Total Loss: $\mathcal{L} = \|\hat{C}_{coarse} - I\|^2_2 + \|\hat{C}_{fine} - I\|^2_2$ [S6, unofficial].
    *   Some implementations weight the coarse loss by 0.5 [S4, unofficial].
    *   Optimize using Adam optimizer [S4, S8, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def positional_encoding(input_tensor, num_frequencies, include_input=True):
    """
    Applies sinusoidal positional encoding.
    input_tensor: (..., D)
    num_frequencies: L
    Returns: (..., D * (2*L + include_input))
    """
    freq_bands = 2.0 ** torch.linspace(0, num_frequencies - 1, num_frequencies, device=input_tensor.device)
    # Shape: (..., D, 2*L)
    scaled_inputs = input_tensor.unsqueeze(-1) * freq_bands.unsqueeze(0)
    encoded = torch.cat([torch.sin(scaled_inputs), torch.cos(scaled_inputs)], dim=-1)
    if include_input:
        return torch.cat([input_tensor, encoded], dim=-1)
    return encoded

def nerf_mlp_forward(x_enc, d_enc, model):
    """
    Forward pass through the asymmetric NeRF MLP.
    x_enc: Encoded position (..., 63)
    d_enc: Encoded direction (..., 27)
    model: Contains linear layers for density and color heads.
    """
    # Density backbone (8 layers, skip connection at layer 4)
    h = x_enc
    for i in range(8):
        h = model.density_layers[i](h)
        if i == 3: # Layer 4 (0-indexed 3)
            h = torch.cat([h, x_enc], dim=-1) # Skip connection
        if i < 7:
            h = F.relu(h)
    
    # Output density and feature vector
    sigma = F.relu(model.density_head(h)) # Or Softplus
    feature_vec = model.feature_head(h)   # (..., 256)
    
    # Color head (view-dependent)
    h_color = torch.cat([feature_vec, d_enc], dim=-1)
    rgb = torch.sigmoid(model.color_head(h_color)) # (..., 3)
    
    return rgb, sigma

def volume_render(z_vals, rgb, sigma, rays_d, white_bkgd=False):
    """
    Discrete volume rendering.
    z_vals: (..., N) sample depths
    rgb: (..., N, 3)
    sigma: (..., N) volume density
    rays_d: (..., 3) ray directions
    """
    # Segment lengths
    dists = z_vals[..., 1:] - z_vals[..., :-1]
    dists = torch.cat([dists, torch.tensor([1e10], device=dists.device).expand(dists[..., :1].shape)], dim=-1)
    # Scale by ray direction length
    dists = dists * torch.norm(rays_d[..., None, :], dim=-1)
    
    # Opacity
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-F.relu(sigma) * dists)
    
    # Transmittance (exclusive product)
    transmittance = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([torch.ones_like(alpha[..., :1]), 1.0 - alpha + 1e-10], dim=-1), dim=-1)[..., :-1]
    
    # Weights
    weights = alpha * transmittance
    
    # Composite color
    rgb_map = torch.sum(weights[..., None] * rgb, dim=-2)
    
    # Optional: Composite over white background
    if white_bkgd:
        acc_map = torch.sum(weights, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
        rgb_map = rgb_map + (1.0 - acc_map)
        
    return rgb_map, weights, alpha, transmittance

def hierarchical_sample(z_coarse, weights_coarse, n_fine, perturb=True):
    """
    Importance sampling for fine network.
    z_coarse: (..., N_c)
    weights_coarse: (..., N_c)
    """
    # Use midpoints of coarse segments for sampling
    z_mid = 0.5 * (z_coarse[..., 1:] + z_coarse[..., :-1])
    # Weights for PDF (exclude last sample which has infinite depth)
    weights_mid = weights_coarse[..., 1:-1] + 1e-5
    
    # Normalize to PDF
    pdf = weights_mid / torch.sum(weights_mid, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    cdf = torch.cumsum(pdf, dim=-1)
    cdf = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(cdf[..., :1]), cdf], dim=-1)
    
    # Sample
    u = torch.rand(*cdf.shape[:-1], n_fine, device=cdf.device)
    
    # Inverse CDF (Binary Search)
    inds = torch.searchsorted(cdf, u, right=True)
    below = torch.clamp(inds - 1, min=0)
    above = torch.clamp(inds, max=cdf.shape[-1] - 1)
    inds_g = torch.stack([below, above], dim=-1)
    
    cdf_g = torch.gather(cdf, -1, inds_g.view(*inds_g.shape[:-2], -1)).view(*inds_g.shape)
    z_mid_g = torch.gather(z_mid, -1, inds_g.view(*inds_g.shape[:-2], -1)).view(*inds_g.shape)
    
    denom = cdf_g[..., 1] - cdf_g[..., 0]
    denom = torch.where(denom < 1e-5, torch.ones_like(denom), denom)
    
    z_fine = z_mid_g[..., 0] + (u - cdf_g[..., 0]) / denom * (z_mid_g[..., 1] - z_mid_g[..., 0])
    
    return z_fine

def train_step(rays_o, rays_d, target_rgb, coarse_model, fine_model, near, far, n_coarse=64, n_fine=128):
    """
    Single training step.
    """
    # 1. Coarse Sampling
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., n_coarse, device=rays_o.device)
    z_vals = near * (1. - t_vals) + far * t_vals
    
    if perturb:
        dt = (far - near) / n_coarse
        z_vals += torch.rand_like(z_vals) * dt
        
    # Expand for batch
    z_vals = z_vals.expand(*rays_o.shape[:-1], n_coarse)
    
    # 2. Query Coarse Model
    pts = rays_o[..., None, :] + rays_d[..., None, :] * z_vals[..., None]
    pts_enc = positional_encoding(pts, 10)
    dirs_enc = positional_encoding(rays_d.unsqueeze(1).expand(*pts.shape[:-1], 3), 4)
    
    rgb_coarse, sigma_coarse = nerf_mlp_forward(pts_enc, dirs_enc, coarse_model)
    
    # 3. Render Coarse
    rgb_coarse_map, weights_coarse, _, _ = volume_render(z_vals, rgb_coarse, sigma_coarse, rays_d)
    
    # 4. Hierarchical Sampling
    z_fine = hierarchical_sample(z_vals, weights_coarse, n_fine)
    
    # Combine and sort
    z_combined = torch.cat([z_vals, z_fine], dim=-1)
    z_combined, sort_idx = torch.sort(z_combined, dim=-1)
    
    # Re-query points
    pts_combined = rays_o[..., None, :] + rays_d[..., None, :] * z_combined[..., None]
    pts_combined_enc = positional_encoding(pts_combined, 10)
    dirs_enc_combined = positional_encoding(rays_d.unsqueeze(1).expand(*pts_combined.shape[:-1], 3), 4)
    
    rgb_fine, sigma_fine = nerf_mlp_forward(pts_combined_enc, dirs_enc_combined, fine_model)
    
    # 5. Render Fine
    rgb_fine_map, _, _, _ = volume_render(z_combined, rgb_fine, sigma_fine, rays_d)
    
    # 6. Loss
    loss = F.mse_loss(rgb_coarse_map, target_rgb) + F.mse_loss(rgb_fine_map, target_rgb)
    
    return loss, rgb_fine_map
```

**Parameters:**
*   $N_c$ (Coarse samples): 64 [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   $N_f$ (Fine samples): 128 [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   Positional Encoding Levels: $L=10$ for position, $L=4$ for direction [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   Network: 8 layers, 256 hidden units, skip connection at layer 4 [S4, S5, unofficial].
*   Optimizer: Adam, $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$, initial LR $5 \times 10^{-4}$ decaying to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S4, unofficial].
*   Near/Far planes: Scene-dependent (e.g., 2.0/6.0 for Blender datasets) [S5, S7, unofficial].
*   White Background: Boolean flag to composite over white if transmittance < 1 [S3, S5, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Stability:** Add epsilon ($10^{-10}$) to $(1-\alpha)$ in the cumulative product to prevent NaNs [S3, S8, unofficial].
*   **Last Segment:** The last segment length must be large ($10^{10}$) to ensure the ray terminates and the last sample contributes to the integral [S3, S5, unofficial].
*   **View-Dependent Artifacts:** Leaving viewing directions out of the first 4 layers helps reduce artifacts caused by premature optimization [S11, unofficial].
*   **Density Activation:** Using ReLU for density can cause sharp gradients; Softplus is sometimes used for smoother gradients [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Training Configuration](1203-nerf-training-configuration.md)
Defines the optimization loop, data handling, and regularization strategies required to train a NeRF model. It focuses on the standard MLP architecture and training dynamics, distinguishing itself from [NeuS Training Loss](2535-neus-training-loss.md) by relying on volumetric opacity rather than explicit surface constraints.

### [NeRF-SH](1348-nerf-sh.md)
Replaces direct RGB output with spherical harmonic coefficients, eliminating the need for viewing direction as a network input. This enables efficient storage in a sparse octree (PlenOctree) and faster rendering.

### [Recursive-NeRF](3029-recursive-nerf.md)
Routes query points through a hierarchy of branch networks based on predicted uncertainty, dynamically growing the network structure during training to handle scene complexity.

### [Mip-NeRF](3381-mip-nerf.md)
Renders anti-aliased conical frustums instead of infinitesimal rays to reduce aliasing artifacts, using integrated positional encoding.

### [NeMF (Neural Microflake Field)](3387-nemf-neural-microflake-field.md)
Represents scenes as a distribution of microflakes to recover physical attributes like shape and reflectance under unknown illumination.

### [EgoNeRF](4096-egonerf.md)
Adapts NeRF for large-scale unbounded scenes using a balanced spherical feature grid to maintain uniform ray hit rates [1185-nerf-neural-radiance-fields.md].

### [Intrinsic NeRF](4623-intrinsic-nerf.md)
Decouples geometry, illumination, and material properties without supervised labels, enabling relighting and material editing.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is slow, typically requiring 100k-200k iterations. For example, ~4 hours for a low-res 'lego' scene on a single 2080 Ti (100k iters) and ~20 hours for 200k iters [S3, S4, unofficial]. Rendering is also slow as it requires querying the MLP for every pixel/sample [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The model weights are small (~5MB) [S3, unofficial], but training requires storing gradients for the large MLP and intermediate rendering states for many rays [S4, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On the NeRF-Synthetic benchmark (400x400, 100k iters), it achieves ~32.54 dB PSNR and 0.961 SSIM on the 'Lego' scene [S5, unofficial]. Other scenes: Chair (~33.0 dB PSNR, 0.967 SSIM), Drums (~25.0 dB PSNR, 0.925 SSIM) [S4, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality novel view synthesis, handles view-dependent effects (specularity, transparency), continuous representation allows arbitrary resolution [S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S12, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow training and rendering, requires posed images, struggles with unbounded scenes without parameterization changes (e.g., [Inverted Sphere Parameterization](5573-inverted-sphere-parameterization.md)) [S2, S4, S10, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity novel view synthesis from a small set of posed images is required, and computational resources allow for long training times.
*   **When not to use:** For real-time applications (use [Instant-NGP](link) or [3D Gaussian Splatting](link)), or for unbounded scenes without specific adaptations [S10, S13, unofficial].

## Background
NeRF was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S2, S5, S13, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of implicit neural representations and differentiable volume rendering [S2, S5, S13, unofficial]. The method was developed to address the limitations of discrete 3D representations (like RGB-D volumes) which are memory-intensive and lack high-frequency detail [S12, unofficial]. It has become a foundational method in neural rendering, inspiring numerous variants and extensions [S1, S5, S13, unofficial].
