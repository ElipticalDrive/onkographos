# ZeroRF

Also called: ZeroRF

## Overview
ZeroRF is a per-scene optimization method for sparse-view (specifically 36° or 6-view) 360° reconstruction that integrates a tailored Deep Image Prior into a factorized NeRF representation, specifically TensoRF-VM [8644-tensorf-training-loop.md]. It parametrizes spatial feature grids using untrained deep generator networks driven by frozen standard Gaussian noise, eliminating the need for pretraining, handcrafted regularization, or progressive upsampling. Unlike generalizable NeRFs or methods relying on pretrained priors (e.g., RegNeRF, DietNeRF), ZeroRF avoids data bias and resolution limits, while outperforming non-pretrained priors (e.g., InfoNeRF, FlipNeRF) in robustness and quality on both synthetic and real-world data.

## How it works
**Inputs:** Sparse set of input images (e.g., 6 views), camera poses, and intrinsics.
**Outputs:** A radiance field representation (generated feature volume) and rendered novel views.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Generator Network ($G$):** A deep neural network based on the Stable Diffusion (SD) VAE decoder architecture. It is modified to be smaller in width and depth for speed. Layers are adapted from 2D to 1D/3D as needed for the factorization. It takes frozen Gaussian noise as input.
2.  **Feature Volume ($\mathcal{T}$):** A decomposed tensorial feature volume in TensoRF-VM style. For a 3D tensor $\mathcal{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{I, J, K}$, it is decomposed into vector and matrix factors:
    $$ \mathcal{T} = \sum_{r=1}^{R_1} v_r^1 \circ M_r^{2,3} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_2} v_r^2 \circ M_r^{1,3} + \sum_{r=1}^{R_3} v_r^3 \circ M_r^{1,2} $$
    where $v_r^a$ are vector factors and $M_r^{b,c}$ are matrix factors. These factors are generated by $G$.
3.  **Decoder MLP:** A multi-layer perceptron that maps sampled features to density and color.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the generator network $G$ with random weights. Set the input to $G$ to a frozen standard Gaussian noise sample $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, fixed for the duration of training.
2.  **Feature Generation:** Use $G$ to produce the planes and vectors that form the decomposed tensorial feature volume $\mathcal{T}$ in the TensoRF-VM style.
3.  **Ray Sampling:** For each ray $r$, uniformly sample $N=1024$ points along the ray. Accelerate this process using occupancy pruning and occlusion culling.
4.  **Feature Sampling:** For each sampled point $x_i$ on ray $r$, sample the feature field $F_{x_i}$ from the generated volume $\mathcal{T}$ using linear interpolation (bilinear/trilinear depending on dimension).
5.  **Density Prediction:** Decode $F_{x_i}$ to predict volume density $\sigma_{x_i}$:
    $$ \sigma_{x_i} = \exp(\Theta_\sigma(\text{SiLU}(\Theta_b(F_{x_i})))) $$
    where $\Theta_b$ and $\Theta_\sigma$ are linear layers, and SiLU is the activation function.
6.  **Color Prediction:** Encode the view direction $d$ with Sphere Harmonics (SH). Predict color $c_{x_i}$:
    $$ c_{x_i} = \sigma(\Theta_c(\text{SiLU}(\Theta_b(F_{x_i}) + \Theta_d(\text{SH}(d))))) $$
    where $\Theta_d$ and $\Theta_c$ are linear layers, $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function, and SH denotes Sphere Harmonics encoding. Note: No positional encoding is added to the input of the decoder to prevent leakage of position information outside the deep prior [G].
7.  **Volume Rendering:** Compute the predicted RGB color $\hat{C}(r)$ for ray $r$ using differentiable volume rendering:
    $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
    where $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the volume transmittance, $\sigma_i$ is density, $c_i$ is color, and $\delta_i$ is the ray marching step size.
8.  **Optimization:** Minimize the plain Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between rendered colors and ground truth colors $C(r)$ by optimizing the generator network parameters:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{r \in R} \|\hat{C}(r) - C(r)\|_2^2 $$
    Use the AdamW optimizer with $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.98$, weight decay of 0.2, and a learning rate starting at 0.002 decaying to 0.001 with a cosine schedule. Train for 10k iterations.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
-   **Positional Encoding Leakage:** The decoder must not consume positional encodings. Adding them risks breaking or degrading ZeroRF by leaking position information outside the deep prior [S1].
-   **Numerical Instability:** Methods like FlipNeRF suffer from numerical instabilities on real-world data; ZeroRF avoids this by using a stable deep prior [S1].
-   **Degenerate Input:** ZeroRF is designed for sparse views (e.g., 6 views). Performance on dense views is not the primary focus, though it builds on TensoRF which handles dense views well [S1].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** Training is fast. Full training (10k iterations) takes ~25 minutes on NeRF-Synthetic at 800² resolution. For common resolutions (256² or 320²), it takes ~30 seconds [S1]. Even 1k iterations (2 minutes) outperforms baselines taking 3-6 hours [S1].
-   **Memory Complexity:** During inference, only the radiance field representation (generated feature volume) is stored. The generator network is not stored, resulting in zero overhead compared to the underlying TensoRF factorization [S1].
-   **Performance:** On NeRF-Synthetic 6-view, ZeroRF achieves PSNR 24.73, SSIM 0.889, LPIPS 0.113, outperforming FlipNeRF (PSNR 21.73, SSIM 0.845, LPIPS 0.202) [S1].
-   **Strengths:** No pretraining required, fast training, robust on real-world data (OpenIllumination), high quality on sparse views.
-   **Limitations:** Per-scene optimization (not generalizable to new scenes without retraining). Requires careful tuning of generator architecture size for speed vs. quality trade-off [S1].

## Background
ZeroRF was introduced in 2024 [S1]. It builds on TensoRF [8], which uses tensorial decompositions to expedite training. It also relates to triplane representations (EG3D [6], K-planes [17]) as a special case of TensoRF-VM where vectors are constants. It contrasts with Dictionary Fields (DiF) [9] and Instant-NGP [39] which use different factorizations or hashmaps. ZeroRF addresses the limitations of generalizable NeRFs (data dependency, resolution limits) and per-scene methods with handcrafted priors (InfoNeRF, FreeNeRF, FlipNeRF) which fail on intricate structures or real-world data [S1].
