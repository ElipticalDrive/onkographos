# Light-weight 4D Gaussian Splatting (LGS)

## Overview
Light-weight 4D Gaussian Splatting (LGS) is a method for efficient reconstruction and real-time rendering of dynamic surgical scenes, designed to drastically reduce memory usage while maintaining high visual fidelity [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by integrating three specific compression techniques—Deformation-Aware Pruning (DAP), Gaussian-Attribute Pruning (GAP), and Feature Field Condensation (FFC)—and employs knowledge distillation to transfer quality from an uncompressed teacher model to the compressed student model [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of dynamic scene images (timestamps $t$), an initial set of 3D Gaussians, and a pre-trained, uncompressed "teacher" model [S1].
*   **Output:** A memory-efficient "student" model consisting of pruned Gaussians and a condensed spatial-temporal feature field, capable of real-time rendering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Attributes:** Position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients. GAP reduces the dimensionality of SH coefficients [S1].
2.  **Spatial-Temporal Feature Field:** A 4D voxel grid representing deformation and appearance features. The resolution is fixed to $[16, 16, 16, 25]$ for axes $(x, y, z, t)$ [S1].
3.  **Deformation MLP:** A tiny Multi-Layer Perceptron $F$ used to calculate deformation based on the condensed feature field [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Teacher Model Initialization:** Train an uncompressed 3D Gaussian Splatting model on the dynamic scene data until convergence. This serves as the teacher model [S1].
2.  **Deformation-Aware Pruning (DAP):**
    *   Calculate the redundancy of Gaussians based on their contribution to deformation.
    *   Prune Gaussians that are redundant for deformation calculation.
    *   Parameters: Threshold $h = 0.5$, scaling factor $\beta = 0.1$ [S1].
3.  **Gaussian-Attribute Pruning (GAP):**
    *   Reduce the dimensionality of the spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients for Gaussians in non-crucial areas (simplifying texture/lighting representation).
    *   Parameter: SH dimension threshold $h_{sh} = 2$ [S1].
4.  **Feature Field Condensation (FFC):**
    *   Apply 3D adaptive pooling to each 4D voxel plane of the spatial-temporal feature field to reduce resolution while preserving 4D features [S1].
    *   The condensed feature field $E_{\Phi'}$ is calculated from the original field $E_{\Phi}$ using Eq. 5 below [S1].
5.  **Knowledge Distillation Optimization:**
    *   Initialize the student model with the pruned Gaussians and condensed feature field from steps 2-4.
    *   Optimize the student model by minimizing the total loss $L = L_d + L_r$ [S1].
    *   $L_d$ is the distillation loss between the teacher's rendered images and the student's rendered images.
    *   $L_r$ is the rendering loss between the student's rendered images and the ground truth images [S1].

**Formulas:**

The total loss function is:
$$ L = L_d + L_r $$

The distillation loss $L_d$ is the mean squared error between the teacher's rendered image $\hat{I}_{tch}(t)$ and the student's rendered image $\hat{I}_{stu}(t)$ over $T$ training timestamps:
$$ L_d = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \| \hat{I}_{tch}(t) - \hat{I}_{stu}(t) \|^2 $$

The rendering loss $L_r$ is the mean squared error between the ground truth image $I_{gt}(t)$ and the student's rendered image $\hat{I}_{stu}(t)$:
$$ L_r = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \| I_{gt}(t) - \hat{I}_{stu}(t) \|^2 $$

The Feature Field Condensation (FFC) uses 3D adaptive pooling to compute the condensed feature $E_{\Phi'}$ for a subplane $\Phi$:
$$ E_{\Phi'}(i, j) = \frac{1}{r_{\Phi1} r_{\Phi2}} \sum_{i'=i \cdot r_{\Phi1}}^{(i+1) \cdot r_{\Phi1}} \sum_{j'=j \cdot r_{\Phi2}}^{(j+1) \cdot r_{\Phi2}} E_{\Phi}(i', j') $$
Where:
*   $\Phi \in \{(x, y), (x, z), (y, z), (x, t), (y, t), (z, t)\}$ denotes the subplanes of the spatial-temporal feature field [S1].
*   $r_{\Phi i}$ represents the compression rate on the $\Phi i$ axis [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Without GAP:** The model size increases to 26.30MB and FPS drops to 187.9. GAP reduces Gaussian parameters by 40% and solves issues with repetitive textures [S1].
*   **Precision:** The adaptive pooling in FFC must preserve 4D features to maintain deformation calculation ability via the tiny MLP [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Efficiency:** LGS achieves a model size of 25.00MB on the ENDONERF dataset and 20.40MB on the SCARED dataset [S1]. This is 15x smaller than EndoGaussian (334.5MB) on ENDONERF and 9x smaller on SCARED (184.0MB) [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** LGS achieves 188.3 FPS on ENDONERF and 194.66 FPS on SCARED [S1]. It is 21.8 FPS faster than EndoGaussian and 87.9 FPS faster than EndoGS on ENDONERF [S1].
*   **Quality:** On ENDONERF, LGS achieves SSIM 0.963, PSNR 37.48, and LPIPS 0.068 [S1]. The quality difference compared to GS-based methods is negligible (0.3 lower PSNR and 0.05 lower SSIM) [S1]. On SCARED, LGS outperforms EndoGaussian in SSIM (0.826 vs 0.825) and PSNR (27.05 vs 26.89) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High memory efficiency comparable to NeRF-based methods while maintaining real-time rendering speeds and high visual quality for dynamic surgical scenes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Rendering speed is still lower than pure GS-based methods compared to NeRF-based methods, though LGS bridges this gap significantly [S1].

## Background
LGS was proposed by Liu et al. in the paper "LGS: A Light-Weight 4D Gaussian Splatting for Efficient Surgical Scene Reconstruction" [S1]. It addresses the challenges of rendering efficiency and memory efficiency in endoscopic reconstruction, specifically targeting dynamic surgical scenes [S1]. The method builds upon 3D Gaussian Splatting but introduces specific pruning and condensation techniques tailored for 4D (spatial-temporal) data [S1].
