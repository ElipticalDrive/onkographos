# Polynomial Motion Field

Also called: Hierarchical motion model, Parametric Motion Field, Polynomial warp field

## Overview
The Polynomial Motion Field is a parametric deformation model for dynamic volume rendering that represents temporal motion as a continuous function of time [S1]. It reconstructs 4D scenes from sparse or limited-view measurements by warping a static template volume to arbitrary time points using voxel-wise polynomial coefficients [S1]. Unlike neural deformation fields that map coordinates to displacement via a network, this method explicitly parameterizes the motion trajectory at each voxel, enabling efficient optimization and arbitrary temporal upsampling [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** $N$ sparse or limited-view 2D projections (sinograms) sampled at discrete time points $t_{i=0 \dots N-1}$, and a static template volume $\hat{\sigma}$ [S1].
*   **Output:** A Motion Coefficient Tensor $C$ and the reconstructed dynamic volume $\sigma(x, y, z, t)$ for any $t \in [0, 1]$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Motion Coefficient Tensor $C$:** A tensor of shape $\mathbb{R}^{\beta^3 \times k \times 3}$ [S1].
    *   $\beta^3$: The spatial resolution of the scene (number of voxels) [S1].
    *   $k$: The polynomial order (degree) [S1].
    *   $3$: The three spatial dimensions (x, y, z) [S1].
    *   Each voxel stores $k$ coefficients for each of the 3 spatial displacement components [S1].
*   **Time Samples:** $N$ time points $t_i$ linearly spaced in $[0, 1]$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the Motion Coefficient Tensor $C$ at a base spatial resolution $\alpha$ (where $\alpha < \beta$), resulting in $C_\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{\alpha^3 \times k \times 3}$ [S1].
2.  **Hierarchical Upsampling (Coarse-to-Fine):** Iteratively increase the spatial resolution $\alpha$ (e.g., $2^3 \to 4^3 \to 8^3 \to 16^3$) [S1]. At each step, upsample the current coefficient tensor $C_{\alpha_i}$ to the next resolution $C_{\alpha_{i+1}}$ using linear upsampling $U$:
    $$ C_{\alpha_{i+1}} = U(C_{\alpha_i}) $$
    where $U: \mathbb{R}^{\alpha_i^3} \to \mathbb{R}^{\alpha_{i+1}^3}$ [S1].
3.  **Warp Field Computation:** For a specific time $t_i$, compute the 3D displacement vector $W(C, t_i)$ for all voxels using the polynomial expansion:
    $$ W(C, t_i) = \sum_{j=0}^{k} C_{:, j, :} t_i^j $$
    where $W(C, t_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{\beta^3 \times 3}$ is the warp field at time $t_i$, and $C_{:, j, :}$ denotes the slice of coefficients for the $j$-th polynomial order across all voxels and spatial dimensions [S1].
4.  **Differentiable Grid Sampling:** Warp the template volume $\hat{\sigma}$ using the computed displacement field. The volume at time $t_i$ is obtained via a differentiable grid sampling function $fn$:
    $$ \sigma(x, y, z, t_i) = fn(W(C, t_i), \hat{\sigma}) $$
    This function interpolates the template values at the displaced coordinates [S1].
5.  **Optimization:** Minimize the reconstruction error between the rendered projections of $\sigma(x, y, z, t_i)$ and the measured sinograms [S1]. The optimization encourages the polynomial coefficients to fit a continuous representation of motion, allowing the model to be evaluated at arbitrary time points $t$ not present in the training set [S1].

    num_voxels, k_plus_1, _ = C.shape

    return W

**Parameters:**
*   **Polynomial Order $k$:** The degree of the polynomial. Typically $k=5$ is sufficient for deformable and periodic motion [S1].
*   **Base Resolution $\alpha$:** The initial resolution for coarse-to-fine optimization. Examples include $2^3, 4^3, 8^3, 16^3$ [S1].
*   **Target Resolution $\beta$:** The final spatial resolution of the scene [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Upsampling Accuracy:** The parametric representation constrains the upsampling of the motion field itself to simple trilinear interpolation, which may limit accuracy compared to other interpolation methods [S1].
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Attempting to estimate the warp field directly at the full resolution $\beta^3$ without hierarchical initialization results in poor motion reconstruction [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory Complexity:** The memory usage is dominated by the Motion Coefficient Tensor $C$, which scales as $O(\beta^3 \cdot k \cdot 3)$ [S1]. The method allows optimizing on low-resolution measurements (e.g., $80^3$) and upsampling to high resolutions (e.g., $256^3$) post-optimization, bypassing substantial GPU memory requirements [S1].
*   **Time Complexity:** The warp field computation is $O(\beta^3 \cdot k)$ per time point [S1]. Rendering involves grid sampling, which is typically $O(\beta^3)$ [S1]. The hierarchical optimization adds overhead but improves convergence [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Arbitrary Temporal Resolution:** The polynomial representation allows evaluation at any time $t \in [0, 1]$, enabling arbitrary frame rate upsampling (e.g., from 10 to 90 frames) [S1].
    *   **Memory Efficiency:** Parametric representation reduces memory footprint compared to storing discrete time steps [S1].
    *   **Differentiability:** The method is fully differentiable, allowing gradient-based optimization of the coefficients [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   **Interpolation Constraint:** Trilinear interpolation for upsampling the field itself may not capture complex motion details as well as learned upsamplers [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for dynamic CT reconstruction from sparse/limited views where temporal continuity is expected and memory is a constraint [S1].

## Background
The Polynomial Motion Field was introduced in the context of dynamic CT reconstruction from limited views [S1]. It leverages implicit neural representations and parametric motion fields to warp a template volume to different time values [S1]. The method was proposed to address the challenges of reconstructing objects from limited views, which is traditionally more challenging than from sparse views [S1]. It assumes only that the scene is sparse view sampled, without requiring specialized sampling schemes or phase information [S1]. The hierarchical coarse-to-fine estimation strategy was introduced to overcome poor motion reconstruction at full resolution [S1]. The approach builds on differentiable grid sampling functions [S1].
