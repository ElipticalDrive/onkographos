# Deformation Field Parametrization


## Overview
Deformation Field Parametrization provides a mechanism to model non-rigid, time-varying scenes within the Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) framework by mapping points from a deformed space to a static canonical space [12731-deformation-field-parametrization.md]. It solves the problem of rendering dynamic scenes with complex non-rigid motion, which standard static NeRFs cannot handle, by introducing a learnable displacement field conditioned on pose or time [12731-deformation-field-parametrization.md]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by defining the specific mathematical structure of this mapping (e.g., MLP-based displacement, spline-based fields, or hybrid rigid-residual models) rather than the high-level scene representation [12731-deformation-field-parametrization.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. The core mechanism is the evaluation of the volume rendering equation and the computation of gradients with respect to scene parameters (density, geometry, transfer functions, camera poses) [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

The standard emission-absorption model computes the color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ as [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:

$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\delta_i$ is the step size, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

Differentiation strategies fall into three main categories [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]:
1. **Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation (AD):** Standard backpropagation through the computational graph. This requires storing $O(kn)$ intermediate states (where $n$ is pixels and $k$ is samples), leading to high memory usage [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
2. **Adjoint Method:** Computes gradients by propagating adjoint variables backward through the rendering algorithm, evaluating the chain rule from output to input. This has computational complexity largely constant in the number of parameters, making it preferable for optimizing many parameters like the transfer function [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].
3. **Analytic Inversion / Explicit Gradients:** Uses a-priori knowledge of the DVR operations to invert the order of operations or derive closed-form gradients, reducing memory consumption to $O(n)$ by avoiding the storage of intermediate results [44-radiance-field-optimization.md].

## Subtopics

### [Deformation-Guided Neural Fields](12044-deformation-guided-neural-fields.md)
Deformation-Guided Neural Fields use a deformation field MLP guided by a morphable 3D face model to create a fully 3D face portrait with controllable pose and expression. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF approaches that struggle with moving bodies by leveraging a mesh deformation approach, allowing for interpolation between frames and poses, similar to the paradigm established by Neural Body. See [Deformation-Guided Neural Fields](12044-deformation-guided-neural-fields.md).

### [Neural Deformation Field](12724-neural-deformation-field.md)
The Neural Deformation Field (NDF) is a method for modeling non-rigid deformations in dynamic scenes by predicting a per-point displacement vector $\Delta$ in canonical space, conditioned on pose and geometric attributes. It extends rigid Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) by adding a learned residual, allowing the representation to capture complex, non-rigid motions that LBS alone cannot handle. It is distinguished from sibling methods like Deformation-Guided Neural Fields by its explicit formulation of the deformation as a neural network output added to an inverse rigid transformation, specifically utilizing signed height indicators for conditioning. See [Deformable Neural Radiance Fields (Nerfies)](13428-deformable-neural-radiance-fields-nerfies.md).

### Spline-based Deformation Field
#### definition
- [S1, unofficial] A deformation field parametrization using cubic B-splines with time-modulated weights to map spatial coordinates $(x, y, z)$ at time $t$ to canonical coordinates $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$, enabling reconstruction from sparse projections.

#### step
- [S1, unofficial] Calculate the deformation field from sparse few-projection data recorded at intermediate times $t$ during the dynamic experiment.
- [S1, unofficial] Obtain full field spatio-temporal information by combining the canonical volume and the calculated deformation field.

#### pitfall
- [S1, unofficial] Deformation fields based on canonical models fail when the scene topology changes over time, as they assume topology-preserving motion.

#### comparison
- [S1, unofficial] Unlike methods using general MLP architectures for deformation fields which require complex regularization like elastic energy, this method uses cubic B-splines to enable reconstruction from as few as two projections.

### Local Rotation Estimation via SVD
#### definition
- [S2, unofficial] A technique to estimate the local rotation of each point during deformation to correct view-dependent rendering artifacts caused by large rotations.

#### step
- [S2, unofficial] Estimate location rotation for each point during deformation using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), borrowing the idea from ARAP.
- [S2, unofficial] Interpolate the estimated per-point rotation to the query view direction of the pre-trained radiance field.
- [S2, unofficial] Use the interpolated rotation to redirect the querying ray in the right direction, ensuring consistency between deformed and canonical space representations.

## Background
The concept of using a canonical space with a deformation field to model dynamic scenes was introduced in the context of Neural Radiance Fields. The foundational work on Deformable Neural Radiance Fields (Nerfies) [S1, unofficial] established the use of a neural network to predict displacement vectors conditioned on pose. Concurrently, other approaches explored different parametrizations, such as spline-based fields for tomographic reconstruction [S1, unofficial] and point-based methods with SVD-based rotation estimation for view consistency [S2, unofficial]. The family has evolved to include hybrid approaches combining rigid skinning with neural residuals [S1, unofficial] and methods leveraging 3D Morphable Models for facial dynamics [S1, unofficial].
