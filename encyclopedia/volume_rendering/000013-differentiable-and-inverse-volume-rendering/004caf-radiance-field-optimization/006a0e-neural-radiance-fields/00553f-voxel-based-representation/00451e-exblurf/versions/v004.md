# ExBluRF

Also called: Efficient Radiance Fields for Extreme Motion Blurred Images

## Overview
ExBluRF is a novel view synthesis method designed to reconstruct sharp 3D scenes from extreme motion-blurred images by jointly optimizing voxel-based radiance fields and 6-DOF camera trajectories [S1]. It differs from sibling methods like [FastNeRF](7183-fastnerf.md) or [C-NeRF](7275-c-nerf.md) which focus on static scene acceleration, and from [DeblurNeRF](10878-rendernet.md) (a sibling in the broader deblurring context, though not listed in the immediate sibling tree, it is the primary comparison in [S1]) which uses 2D pixel-wise blur kernels, by formulating motion blur as a trajectory-based accumulation of rays along a Bézier curve [S1]. This approach allows ExBluRF to handle extreme blur with significantly lower memory and computational costs compared to MLP-based deblurring methods, as its memory complexity remains constant relative to the number of blur samples [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A set of $M$ extremely motion-blurred images $\{I_m\}_{m=1}^M$ with known or estimated initial camera poses.
- **Outputs:** A sharp voxel-based radiance field (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) and the optimized 6-DOF camera trajectory parameters for each input image.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Voxel Grid:** Replaces the MLP $f_{NeRF}$ used in standard NeRF. It stores volumetric densities $\sigma$ and colors $\mathbf{c}$ at voxel centers. Direct trilinear interpolation is used to query $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ at any 3D point $\mathbf{X}$ [S1].
2.  **Bézier Curve Control Points:** The camera trajectory for each view is parameterized by control points $\{\hat{\mathbf{p}}_j\}_{j=0}^M$ in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{se}(3)$, where $M$ is the order of the Bézier curve [S1].

**Parameters:**
-   $N$: Number of sampling points (intermediate sub-frames) along the camera trajectory to approximate the motion blur integral. Typical values range from 5 to 31 [S1].
-   $P$: Number of sample points on a single ray during volume rendering [S1].
-   $M$: Order of the Bézier curve. Typical values are 1 (linear) or 3 [S1].
-   $B$: Number of rays processed in a batch [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Trajectory Reparameterization:**
    For each input image, define the initial pose $P_{\tau_o}$ and final pose $P_{\tau_c}$. Reparameterize the 6-DOF motion trajectory using a Bézier curve of order $M$ in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{se}(3)$ [S1]. The pose at normalized time $\tau'_i$ is computed as:
    $$
    \mathbf{p}_{\tau_i} = \sum_{j=0}^{M} \binom{M}{j} (1-\tau'_i)^{M-j} (\tau'_i)^j \cdot \hat{\mathbf{p}}_j
    $$
    where $\tau'_i = \frac{\tau_i - \tau_o}{\tau_c - \tau_o}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_j$ are the learnable control points [S1]. The actual camera pose $P_{\tau_i}$ is obtained by exponentiating $\mathbf{p}_{\tau_i}$ [S1].

2.  **Ray Generation:**
    For each training iteration and each blurry input image:
    a. Define the camera intrinsic matrix $\mathbf{K}$ and extrinsic matrix $\mathbf{R}$ (derived from $P_{\tau_i}$) [S1].
    b. For each pixel $x$ in the image, compute the ray direction $\mathbf{d}_{\tau_i}$ at each of the $N$ intermediate shutter times $\tau_i$:
    $$
    \mathbf{d}_{\tau_i} = \mathbf{R}_{\tau_i} \cdot \mathbf{K}^{-1} [\mathbf{x}]_+
    $$
    where $[\mathbf{x}]_+$ is the homogeneous coordinate of pixel $x$ [S1].
    c. The ray origin is the camera center corresponding to $P_{\tau_i}$ [S1].

3.  **Volume Rendering (Per Ray):**
    For each generated ray $\mathbf{r}_{\tau_i}$, compute the color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}_{\tau_i})$ using the standard volume rendering equation with the voxel-based radiance field [S1]:
    $$
    \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}_{\tau_i}) = \sum_{k=1}^{P} T_k (1 - \exp(-\sigma_k \delta_k)) \mathbf{c}_k
    $$
    where $\sigma_k$ and $\mathbf{c}_k$ are the density and color sampled from the voxel grid at the $k$-th sample point along the ray, $\delta_k$ is the distance between sample points, and $T_k = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the transmittance [S1].

4.  **Blur Accumulation:**
    Compute the blurred color $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_B(\mathbf{x})$ for pixel $x$ by averaging the rendered colors from the $N$ intermediate rays:
    $$
    \hat{\mathbf{C}}_B(\mathbf{x}) \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}_{\tau_i}(\mathbf{x}))
    $$
    This approximates the integral of ray colors accumulated while the shutter is open [S1].

5.  **Optimization:**
    Compute the photo-consistency loss (e.g., $L_1$ or $L_2$) between the estimated blurred image $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_B$ and the input blurry image $I_{blur}$ [S1]. Backpropagate the loss to optimize both the voxel grid parameters ($\sigma, \mathbf{c}$) and the Bézier control points $\{\hat{\mathbf{p}}_j\}$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** Training time is significantly reduced compared to MLP-based methods. On the 'Camellia' dataset, ExBluRF achieves 10x less training time than DeblurNeRF (4.41 hrs vs 27.48 hrs) [S1].
-   **Memory Complexity:** The memory complexity per ray is $O(1)$ because ray marching evaluates sample points sequentially [S1]. In contrast, DeblurNeRF requires $O(P \cdot L)$ memory per ray due to synchronous feed-forwarding of $P$ points through $L$ MLP layers [S1]. ExBluRF's total memory usage is constant with respect to the number of trajectory samples $N$ [S1].
-   **Performance:** On the 'Camellia' scene, ExBluRF achieves a PSNR of 28.83 with 4.31 GB GPU memory, compared to DeblurNeRF's 27.76 PSNR with 13.89 GB memory [S1].
-   **Strengths:** Handles extreme motion blur efficiently; allows large $N$ (e.g., 31 samples) without memory explosion; jointly optimizes geometry and camera pose.
-   **Limitations:** Requires per-scene optimization; sensitive to the order of the Bézier curve (higher orders may overfit, lower orders may underfit complex motion) [S1].
-   **When to use:** When dealing with extreme motion blur where 2D kernel methods fail or are too memory-intensive, and ground truth sharp images are unavailable for supervised pre-training [S1].

## Background
ExBluRF was introduced by Dongwoo Lee, Sunghyun Cho, Joon-Young Lee, Kyoung Mu Lee, et al. in 2023 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous NeRF-based deblurring methods like DeblurNeRF [S1] and BAD-NeRF [S1], which struggled with extreme blur due to high computational costs or inability to model complex 6-DOF trajectories. The method builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering [44-radiance-field-optimization.md] and voxel-based representations [10852-voxel-based-representation.md]. It was validated on the newly proposed ExBlur dataset, which contains multi-view extremely blurred images with ground truth sharp pairs and trajectories [S1].
