# V4D (Voxel for 4D Novel View Synthesis)

## Overview
V4D is a voxel-based architecture for 4D novel view synthesis in dynamic scenes that models the 4D neural radiance field directly from single-view video, eliminating the need for a canonical space or explicit deformation fields [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [D-NeRF](link) and [TiNeuVox](link) by using separate 3D voxel grids for density and texture, combined with a total variation loss and a conditional positional encoding (CPE) module to prevent over-smoothing and over-fitting [S1]. While it requires longer training times than canonical-space methods due to the lack of temporal alignment, it achieves state-of-the-art performance on synthetic dynamic scenes and faster inference than [TiNeuVox](link) by avoiding per-point position offset prediction [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A sequence of single-view images (video) representing a dynamic scene.
*   **Output:** A 4D neural radiance field represented by two 3D voxel grids and associated MLPs, enabling the rendering of novel views at arbitrary times and viewpoints.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Density Volume:** A 3D voxel grid with dimensions $160 \times 160 \times 160 \times 12$. Initialized to zero [S1].
2.  **Texture Volume:** A 3D voxel grid with dimensions $160 \times 160 \times 160 \times 12$. Initialized to zero [S1].
3.  **Basic LUTs:** $M=5$ learnable 3D look-up tables, each with resolution $33 \times 33 \times 33$. One is initialized as the identity LUT; the remaining $M-1$ are initialized to zero [S1].
4.  **Pseudo-surface Depth:** A 3D volume derived from the rendered depth, used to predict spatial-aware weights for LUT refinement [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Sampling:** For a target pixel, generate $N$ sample points $\mathbf{x}_i = (x, y, z)$ along the ray, associated with a time index $t$ and view direction $(\theta, \phi)$. Calculate interval $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ [S1].

2.  **Feature Sampling:**
    *   Sample features $\mathbf{F}_{den}$ from the Density Volume and $\mathbf{F}_{tex}$ from the Texture Volume at $\mathbf{x}_i$ using trilinear interpolation [S1].
    *   Apply Conditional Positional Encoding (CPE) *only* to the texture features $\mathbf{F}_{tex}$ to produce $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{tex}$. Do not apply CPE to $\mathbf{F}_{den}$ to preserve the zero-initialization required for stable training [S1].
    *   The CPE function is defined as:
        $$
        \gamma(\mathbf{p}_v) = \left( \sin\left(2^\pi \mathbf{p}_v + \frac{2\pi}{L-1}t\right), \cos\left(2^\pi \mathbf{p}_v + \frac{2\pi}{L-1}t\right) \right)
        $$
        where $\mathbf{p}_v$ is the sampled feature vector, $L=5$ is the frequency order, and $t$ is the time index [S1].

3.  **Density and Texture Prediction:**
    *   Concatenate the time index $t$, 3D location $(x, y, z)$, view direction $(\theta, \phi)$, and the processed features ($\mathbf{F}_{den}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{tex}$) [S1].
    *   Feed this concatenated vector into a 5-layer MLP to predict volume density $\sigma_i$ [S1].
    *   Feed the same concatenated vector into a separate 5-layer MLP to predict RGB color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].

4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Compute accumulated transmittance $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ [S1].
    *   Render coarse RGB color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for the pixel:
        $$
        \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) \mathbf{c}_i
        $$
    *   Compute pseudo-surface depth $\hat{d}$ for spatial awareness:
        $$
        \hat{d} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) t_i
        $$
        [S1].

5.  **LUTs Refinement (Pixel-level):**
    *   Treat the coarse RGB value $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ as a spatial coordinate in the RGB space.
    *   Perform trilinear interpolation on each of the $M=5$ basic LUTs using this coordinate to obtain interpolated RGB values [S1].
    *   Use the pseudo-surface depth $\hat{d}$ as input to a 10-layer MLP to predict spatial-aware weights $\mathbf{w}_k$ for each basic LUT [S1].
    *   Combine the interpolated RGB values using the predicted weights to produce the refined RGB color [S1].
    *   Repeat this refinement process for $Z=3$ iterations [S1].

6.  **Loss Calculation:**
    *   Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}$:
        $$
        \mathcal{L} = w_1 \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + w_2 \mathcal{L}_{pt\_rgb} + w_3 \mathcal{L}_{bg} + w_4 \mathcal{L}_{TV}
        $$
        [S1].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$: Mean squared error between refined rendered color and ground truth.
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{pt\_rgb}$: Point-wise RGB loss encouraging consistency at sampled points:
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{pt\_rgb} = \frac{1}{R} \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{i=1}^{K} (T_i a_i \hat{C}(r) - C(r))^2
        $$
        where $K$ is the number of queried points, $a_i$ is the termination probability, and $R$ is the batch of training rays [S1].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{bg}$: Background loss encouraging foreground/background separation:
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{bg} = -T_{K+1} \log(T_{K+1}) - (1 - T_{K+1}) \log(1 - T_{K+1})
        $$
        [S1].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{TV}$: Total variation loss applied directly to the Density and Texture voxel grids to prevent over-fitting and maintain geometry. It is *not* applied to the LUTs [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Voxel Resolution:** $160 \times 160 \times 160$ for density/texture volumes; $33 \times 33 \times 33$ for LUTs [S1].
*   **Channel Dimension:** 12 for density and texture volumes [S1].
*   **MLP Depth:** 5 layers for density/texture fields; 10 layers for LUT weight prediction [S1].
*   **Frequency Order ($L$):** 5 for CPE [S1].
*   **Basic LUTs ($M$):** 5 [S1].
*   **Iterations ($Z$):** 3 for LUT refinement [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 250k for synthetic datasets ([23]); 100k for real scene datasets ([21]) [S1].
*   **Learning Rates:** 0.1 for voxel grids; $10^{-3}$ for density/texture MLPs; $10^{-4}$ for LUT MLPs and voxels [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 8,196 rays (first stage); 4,196 rays (second stage) [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** $w_1=1.0, w_2=0.01, w_3=0.001, w_4=0.1$ [S1].
*   **TV Loss Decay:** Exponential decay factor of 0.005 applied to $w_4$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-smoothing:** The total variation loss causes blurring and loss of high-frequency details [S1]. This is mitigated by the CPE module and the LUTs refinement module [S1].
*   **Learning Collapse:** Applying CPE to the density field features breaks the zero-initialization rule, causing learning collapse. CPE must only be applied to texture features [S1].
*   **Spatial Awareness:** Using low-resolution images to predict LUT weights loses 3D spatial awareness. Using pseudo-surface depth via an MLP preserves this awareness [S1].

## Subtopics

### MAV3D
MAV3D synthesizes time-varying 3D scenes directly from text by optimizing a 4D neural radiance field under guidance from a pretrained text-to-video diffusion model [S2]. It differs from V4D by using a hexplane-based 4D representation for efficient spacetime encoding rather than 3D voxel grids, and employs a multi-stage static-to-dynamic training scheme with temporal regularization [S2]. Unlike V4D which requires video input, MAV3D uses a data-free supervision strategy relying only on text and video priors, enabling dynamic 3D content creation without any 4D datasets [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is computationally intensive, requiring longer times (e.g., 6.9 hours on A100 for synthetic datasets) compared to canonical-space methods like [D-NeRF](link) (15.9 hours) or [TiNeuVox](link) (0.35 hours) due to the lack of canonical space adaptation and reliance on TV loss for temporal information [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses two $160^3 \times 12$ voxel grids and 5 $33^3$ LUTs. This is more memory-intensive than implicit MLP-only methods but avoids the overhead of deformation fields [S1].
*   **Inference Speed:** Faster than [TiNeuVox](link) (0.48s vs 1.42s on A100) because it does not use an MLP-based sub-network to predict position offsets for each moment [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM on synthetic dynamic scenes ([23]) and competitive performance on real scenes ([21]), outperforming [D-NeRF](link), [NDVG](link), and [TiNeuVox](link) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** No need for canonical space; plug-and-play LUTs refinement module; high rendering quality.
*   **Limitations:** Longer training time; requires careful tuning of TV loss to balance smoothing and detail preservation.

## Background
V4D was proposed in 2023 by researchers addressing the limitations of MLP-based neural radiance fields in dynamic scenes, specifically the lack of multi-view constraints and the computational cost of global optimization [S1]. It builds on the voxel-based representation family, extending 3D grids to model 4D fields directly from single-view video [S1]. The method was evaluated on 8 synthetic dynamic scene datasets and 4 real scene datasets, demonstrating superior performance over existing methods like [D-NeRF](link) and [TiNeuVox](link) [S1]. The code was made available on GitHub [S1].
