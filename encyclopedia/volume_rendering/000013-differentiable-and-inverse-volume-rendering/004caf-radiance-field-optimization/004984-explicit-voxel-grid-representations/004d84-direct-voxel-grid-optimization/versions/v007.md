# Direct Voxel Grid Optimization

Also called: DVG, DVGO, Direct Voxel Density Optimization, DirectVoxGo

## Overview
Direct Voxel Grid Optimization (DVGO) reconstructs per-scene radiance fields from calibrated images by directly optimizing a dense 3D voxel grid for volume density and a feature voxel grid for color, achieving convergence in less than 15 minutes on a single GPU [S1][S4, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from implicit methods like [Neural Radiance Fields](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) by eliminating the need for lengthy MLP training, and from earlier explicit voxel methods by training the voxel grid directly from scratch without requiring a conversion step from a pre-trained implicit model [S1][S4, unofficial]. Unlike [Plenoxels](806-explicit-radiance-fields.md) which optimizes colors directly in the grid, DVGO uses a shallow MLP to handle view-dependent appearance, balancing speed and quality [S2][S4, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ calibrated images with known camera poses (intrinsic and extrinsic parameters), normalized such that camera centers lie in $[-1, 1]$ [S3][S4, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A rendered novel view image $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for any ray $\mathbf{r}$, and optimized voxel grids representing the scene geometry and appearance [S1, S5].

**Data Structures:**
The representation consists of two primary voxel grids and a shallow MLP:
1.  **Density Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{dens}}$):** Stores raw density values $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$ [S1, S5].
    *   **Layout:** 3D array of size $M \times M \times M$ [S1, S5].
    *   **Values:** Float32 [S1].
    *   **Initialization:** Initialized to yield opacities very close to zero to avoid biasing geometry toward camera near planes [S1][S4, unofficial].
2.  **Feature Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{feat}}$):** Stores feature vectors for the shallow MLP [S1, S5].
    *   **Layout:** 3D array of size $M \times M \times M$ [S1].
    *   **Values:** Float32, with $D$ channels (typically $D=12$) [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   **Initialization:** Zeros or small random values [S1].
3.  **Color Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{color}}$):** Used only in the coarse stage to capture view-invariant color [S3].
    *   **Layout:** 3D array of size $M \times M \times M$ [S3].
    *   **Values:** Float32, 3 channels (RGB) [S3].
4.  **Shallow MLP:**
    *   **Architecture:** 2 hidden layers, 128 channels per layer [S4, unofficial].
    *   **Inputs:** Concatenation of positional encoding of 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, and the feature vector from $V_{\text{feat}}$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   **Output:** View-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1][S4, unofficial].

**Bounding Box (BBox):**
The method automatically finds a BBox tightly enclosing the volume of interest to allocate the voxel grids, ensuring parsimony and scalability [S1][S4, unofficial]. The voxel grid coordinates are mapped to the BBox space [S4, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Grid Resolution:** Coarse stage ($M_c$) is typically $100^3$ [S1][S4, unofficial]. Fine stage ($M_f$) is typically $160^3$ (default) or $256^3$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Sampling:** Coarse step size $\delta_c = 0.5 \times s_c$, where $s_c$ is the voxel size [S4, unofficial]. Fine step size $\delta_f = 0.5 \times s_f$, where $s_f$ is the voxel size [S4, unofficial]. Number of samples $K$ per ray is typically 128 [S1].
*   **Initialization:** $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(c)} = 10^{-6}$ (Initial activated alpha for coarse stage) [S1][S4, unofficial]. $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(f)} = 0.01$ (Initial activated alpha for fine stage) [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Thresholds:** $\tau_c = 10^{-3}$ (Threshold for defining known free space in coarse stage) [S1]. $\tau_f = 10^{-4}$ (Threshold for skipping low-density query points in fine stage) [S1].
*   **Optimization:** Optimizer is Adam [S4, unofficial]. Base learning rate for voxel grids is $0.1$ [S1][S4, unofficial]. Base learning rate for MLP is $10^{-3}$ [S4, unofficial]. Batch size is 8,192 rays [S1][S4, unofficial]. Coarse iterations are 10,000 [S1][S4, unofficial]. Fine iterations are 20,000 [S1][S4, unofficial]. Learning rate schedule uses exponential decay, downscaled by 0.1 at 20,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** $w_{\text{photo}} = 1$ [S1]. $w_{\text{pt\_rgb}}$ is $10^{-1}$ (coarse), $10^{-2}$ (fine) [S1]. $w_{\text{bg}}$ is $10^{-2}$ (coarse), $10^{-3}$ (fine) [S1].
*   **Positional Encoding:** $k_x = 5$ frequencies for position [S1]. $k_d = 4$ frequencies for direction [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The optimization proceeds in two phases: Coarse Geometry Searching and Fine Detail Reconstruction [S3].

1.  **Coarse Geometry Searching:**
    *   **Initialize:** Create $V_{\text{dens}}$ of size $M_c^3$. Initialize raw density values such that the activated alpha is $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(c)} = 10^{-6}$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   **Create Auxiliary Grids:** Initialize $V_{\text{color}}$ (for view-invariant color) and $V_{\text{feat}}$ (zeros) [S3].
    *   **Define Free Space:** Identify points in free space using threshold $\tau_c = 10^{-3}$ and skip query points in this space during optimization [S1].
    *   **Optimize:** For 10,000 iterations:
        *   Sample rays from the training images.
        *   For each ray, sample $K$ points with step size $\delta_c = 0.5 s_c$.
        *   Query $V_{\text{dens}}$ for raw density $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$.
        *   **Post-Activation Interpolation:** Trilinearly interpolate $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$ from the grid, then apply activation (e.g., Softplus or ReLU) to produce opacity $\alpha$ [S1][S4, unofficial]. This allows modeling sharp surfaces within a single grid cell [S1][S4, unofficial].
        *   Query $V_{\text{color}}$ for color $\mathbf{c}$ via trilinear interpolation [S3].
        *   Compute rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using volume rendering quadrature [S1].
        *   Compute loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{coarse}} = w_{\text{photo}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_{\text{pt\_rgb}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{pt\_rgb}} + w_{\text{bg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}$ [S1].
        *   **View-Count Prior:** Apply a lower learning rate to voxels visible to fewer views to avoid redundant voxels allocated for explaining observations from a small number of views [S1][S4, unofficial].
        *   Update parameters using Adam.

2.  **Fine Detail Reconstruction:**
    *   **Scale Grid:** Progressively scale the fine-stage voxel grid resolution (e.g., at checkpoints 1000, 2000, 3000) [S1].
    *   **Initialize Fine Grid:** Create $V_{\text{dens}}$ of size $M_f^3$ (e.g., $160^3$). Initialize raw density values such that activated alpha is $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(f)} = 0.01$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   **Define Unknown Space:** Skip low-density query points where $\sigma < \tau_f = 10^{-4}$ [S1].
    *   **Optimize:** For 20,000 iterations:
        *   Sample rays from the training images.
        *   For each ray, sample $K$ points with step size $\delta_f = 0.5 s_f$.
        *   Query $V_{\text{dens}}$ for raw density $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$.
        *   **Post-Activation Interpolation:** Trilinearly interpolate $\sigma_{\text{raw}}$, then apply activation to produce $\alpha$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
        *   Query $V_{\text{feat}}$ for feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ via trilinear interpolation [S1].
        *   Compute positional encoding for position $\mathbf{x}$ ($k_x=5$) and direction $\mathbf{d}$ ($k_d=4$) [S1].
        *   Pass $\mathbf{f}$, pos($\mathbf{x}$), pos($\mathbf{d}$) through the shallow MLP to get view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ [S4, unofficial].
        *   Compute rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using volume rendering [S1].
        *   Compute loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{fine}}$ [S1].
        *   **View-Count Prior:** Apply lower learning rate to voxels visible to fewer views [S1][S4, unofficial].
        *   Update parameters using Adam.

**Volume Rendering Equation:**
The rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is calculated as [S1, S5]:
$$
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \left( \sum_{i=1}^K T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i \right) + T_{K+1} \mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}
$$
where:
*   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity at sample $i$ [S1, S5].
*   $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance up to sample $i$ [S1, S5].
*   $\sigma_i$ is the density at sample $i$ [S1, S5].
*   $\delta_i$ is the distance to the adjacent sampled point [S1, S5].
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color at sample $i$ [S1, S5].
*   $\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}$ is the background color [S1, S5].

**Loss Function:**
The photometric loss is [S5]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \right\|_2^2
$$
where $\mathcal{R}$ is the set of rays in the mini-batch and $C(\mathbf{r})$ is the observed pixel color [S5].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def positional_encoding(x, k):
    """Standard positional encoding for 3D vector x with k frequencies."""
    # x: (N, 3) -> (N, 3 * 2k + 3)
    freq_bands = [2.0 ** i for i in range(k)]
    pe = []
    for i in range(3):
        pe.append(x[:, i])
        for f in freq_bands:
            pe.append(math.sin(x[:, i] * f))
            pe.append(math.cos(x[:, i] * f))
    return torch.stack(pe, dim=-1)

def trilinear_interpolate(grid, coords):
    """Trilinear interpolation for grid of shape (M, M, M, C) at coords (N, 3) in [-1, 1]."""
    M = grid.shape[0]
    C = grid.shape[3]
    coords_scaled = coords * (M - 1) / 2.0 + (M - 1) / 2.0
    x0, y0, z0 = torch.floor(coords_scaled).long()
    x1, y1, z1 = x0 + 1, y0 + 1, z0 + 1
    x0 = torch.clamp(x0, 0, M-1)
    x1 = torch.clamp(x1, 0, M-1)
    y0 = torch.clamp(y0, 0, M-1)
    y1 = torch.clamp(y1, 0, M-1)
    z0 = torch.clamp(z0, 0, M-1)
    z1 = torch.clamp(z1, 0, M-1)
    
    v000 = grid[x0, y0, z0, :]
    v100 = grid[x1, y0, z0, :]
    v010 = grid[x0, y1, z0, :]
    v110 = grid[x1, y1, z0, :]
    v001 = grid[x0, y0, z1, :]
    v101 = grid[x1, y0, z1, :]
    v011 = grid[x0, y1, z1, :]
    v111 = grid[x1, y1, z1, :]
    
    fx = coords_scaled[..., 0] - x0.float()
    fy = coords_scaled[..., 1] - y0.float()
    fz = coords_scaled[..., 2] - z0.float()
    
    c00 = v000 * (1-fx) + v100 * fx
    c10 = v010 * (1-fx) + v110 * fx
    c01 = v001 * (1-fx) + v101 * fx
    c11 = v011 * (1-fx) + v111 * fx
    
    c0 = c00 * (1-fy) + c10 * fy
    c1 = c01 * (1-fy) + c11 * fy
    
    out = c0 * (1-fz) + c1 * fz
    return out

class ShallowMLP(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d_feat=12, k_x=5, k_d=4):
        super().__init__()
        self.dim_x = 3 * (2 * k_x) + 3
        self.dim_d = 3 * (2 * k_d) + 3
        self.dim_feat = d_feat
        self.dim_in = self.dim_x + self.dim_d + self.dim_feat
        self.fc1 = torch.nn.Linear(self.dim_in, 128)
        self.fc2 = torch.nn.Linear(128, 128)
        self.fc3 = torch.nn.Linear(128, 3)
        
    def forward(self, x, d, feat):
        pe_x = positional_encoding(x, k=k_x)
        pe_d = positional_encoding(d, k=k_d)
        input_vec = torch.cat([pe_x, pe_d, feat], dim=-1)
        h = torch.nn.functional.relu(self.fc1(input_vec))
        h = torch.nn.functional.relu(self.fc2(h))
        color = self.fc3(h)
        return color

def volume_render(rays, V_dens, V_feat, mlp, bg_color, step_size):
    origins = rays[:, 0]
    directions = rays[:, 1]
    N_rays = origins.shape[0]
    K = 128
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0, 1, K).to(rays.device)
    points = origins.unsqueeze(1) + directions.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    
    raw_dens = trilinear_interpolate(V_dens, points)
    sigma = torch.nn.functional.softplus(raw_dens)
    
    deltas = step_size * torch.norm(directions, dim=1, keepdim=True)
    alphas = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    
    one_minus_alpha = 1.0 - alphas
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones(N_rays, 1).to(rays.device), torch.cumprod(one_minus_alpha, dim=1)[:, :-1]], dim=1)
    
    features = trilinear_interpolate(V_feat, points)
    ray_dirs = directions.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, K, -1)
    colors = mlp(points, ray_dirs, features)
    
    weights = T * alphas
    T_last = torch.prod(one_minus_alpha, dim=1)
    color_rendered = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * colors, dim=1) + T_last.unsqueeze(-1) * bg_color
    return color_rendered

def sample_rays_from_poses(poses, batch_size):
    """Sample random rays from camera poses."""
    N = poses.shape[0]
    indices = torch.randint(0, N, (batch_size,))
    origins = poses[indices, :3, 3]
    directions = poses[indices, :3, :3] @ torch.tensor([0, 0, -1.0])
    return torch.stack([origins, directions], dim=1)

def photometric_loss(rendered, target):
    """Mean squared error loss."""
    return torch.mean((rendered - target) ** 2)

def optimize_dvgo(images, poses, V_dens_c, V_feat_c, V_color_c, V_dens_f, V_feat_f, mlp, lr=0.1, batch_size=8192):
    optimizer_c = torch.optim.Adam([V_dens_c, V_color_c, V_feat_c], lr=lr)
    optimizer_f = torch.optim.Adam([V_dens_f, V_feat_f, list(mlp.parameters())], lr=1e-3)
    
    for i in range(10000):
        rays = sample_rays_from_poses(poses, batch_size)
        color_coarse = volume_render(rays, V_dens_c, V_color_c, None, 0.0, 0.5)
        loss = photometric_loss(color_coarse, images)
        optimizer_c.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer_c.step()
        
    for i in range(20000):
        rays = sample_rays_from_poses(poses, batch_size)
        color_fine = volume_render(rays, V_dens_f, V_feat_f, mlp, 0.0, 0.5)
        loss = photometric_loss(color_fine, images)
        optimizer_f.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer_f.step()
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
Direct voxel density optimization is prone to suboptimal geometry solutions where the model allocates 'cloud' in free space to fit photometric loss instead of finding multi-view consistent geometry [S1]. Real-world data introduces uncertainty (inconsistent lighting, SfM error) resulting in multi-view inconsistent and blurrier surfaces, lessening the advantage of post-activation for sharper surfaces [S1]. Using $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(c)} = 10^{-7}$ requires more iterations for the coarse stage of some scenes [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Ref-DVGO](6572-ref-dvgo.md)
Ref-DVGO is a reflection-aware neural rendering method that combines the fast hybrid implicit-explicit representation of Direct Voxel Grid Optimization (DVGO) with the reflection direction reparameterization of Ref-NeRF to achieve high-quality photorealistic novel view synthesis of reflective scenes with reduced training and rendering time. It sets itself apart from DVGO by explicitly modeling specular reflections via a reflection-aware reparameterization and from Ref-NeRF by replacing large spatial MLPs with six distinct voxel grids, significantly reducing GPU memory requirements and training time.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is significantly faster than implicit methods. Convergence is about two orders of magnitude faster than NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses dense voxel grids. Memory usage scales with $M^3$. For $M=160$, this is manageable on modern GPUs [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Training time is ~15 minutes on NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti for $M=160^3$ (31.95 PSNR) [S1]. Training time is ~22 minutes on NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti for $M=256^3$ (32.80 PSNR) [S1]. Rendering speed is ~45x faster than NeRF (0.64s vs 29s per 800x800 image) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Extremely fast convergence from scratch (no pre-training or conversion needed) [S1][S4, unofficial]. High rendering speed [S1]. Sharp surface modeling via post-activation interpolation [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Prone to suboptimal geometry (cloud in free space) if priors are not applied [S1]. Real-world data uncertainty (lighting, SfM error) can lead to blurrier surfaces, reducing the benefit of post-activation [S1]. Not designed for unbounded or forward-facing scenes [S1].
*   **When to use:** When fast per-scene training is required and the scene is bounded and inward-facing [S1].
*   **When not to use:** For unbounded scenes, or when cross-scene generalization is needed without per-scene optimization [S1].

## Background
Direct Voxel Grid Optimization was introduced by Cheng Sun, Min Sun, and Hwann-Tzong Chen in 2022 [S1][S4, unofficial]. It addresses the slow training times of NeRF by directly optimizing explicit voxel grids. The method builds on the idea of explicit volumetric representations but improves upon previous voxel methods by training from scratch and using post-activation interpolation [S1][S4, unofficial]. It is used for novel view synthesis on benchmarks like Syn-NeRF, Syn-NSVF, BlendedMVS, and Tanks and Temples [S1][S4, unofficial].
