# HDR-Plenoxels

Also called: HDR radiance fields modeled by Plenoxels

## Overview
HDR-Plenoxels is a voxel-based volume rendering method that reconstructs 3D High Dynamic Range (HDR) radiance fields, scene geometry, and varying camera settings from multi-view Low Dynamic Range (LDR) images [S1, S2]. It extends Plenoxels by introducing a differentiable tone-mapping module that models the in-camera imaging pipeline (ISP), including white balance and Camera Response Functions (CRF), allowing for self-calibration and controllable rendering [S2]. Unlike NeRF, which uses implicit neural networks, HDR-Plenoxels uses explicit sparse voxel grids with spherical harmonics, achieving rendering speeds two orders of magnitude faster than NeRF [S2].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A set of $N$ multi-view LDR images captured with varying camera settings (exposure, white balance, CRF) [S2].
*   Camera poses and intrinsics, typically obtained via COLMAP sparse reconstruction [S2].
*   For real data: undistorted images [S2].

**Outputs:**
*   A sparse voxel grid representing the 3D scene, containing opacity and view-dependent radiance coefficients [S2].
*   Learned tone-mapping parameters (white balance scales, CRF parameters) [S2].
*   Novel view synthesis of HDR images, which can be tone-mapped to arbitrary LDR settings [S2].

### Data Structures

**Sparse Voxel Grid:**
*   **Structure:** A 3D grid covering a bounded space of interest. The grid is sparse, meaning only occupied voxels are stored or processed [S2].
*   **Voxel Layout:** Each voxel stores:
    *   **Opacity ($\sigma$):** A scalar value representing the density/opacity of the voxel [S2].
    *   **Spherical Harmonics (SH) Coefficients:** A vector of 27 values (9 per color channel: Red, Green, Blue) representing the view-dependent radiance [S2].
    *   **Total per voxel:** 28 values (1 opacity + 27 SH coefficients) [S2].
*   **Initialization:**
    *   Opacity $\sigma$ is initialized to 0.1 [S2].
    *   SH coefficients are initialized such that the base color is grey, achieved by adding an offset of 0.5 to the color components [S2].

**Tone Mapping Parameters:**
*   **White Balance Scales:** Three scalar values ($w_r, w_g, w_b$) representing the basis white balance, derived from channel-wise averaging of the full view [S2].
*   **Exposure Scale:** A scalar value representing the basis exposure, derived from globally averaging the white balance values [S2].
*   **CRF Parameters:** Parameters defining the Camera Response Function, learned during training [S2].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Preprocessing:**
    *   Compute rough 3D geometry and obtain camera poses using COLMAP sparse reconstruction to determine the bounding box of the scene [S2].
    *   For real-world images, perform lens undistortion using calibration data [S2].

2.  **Grid Initialization:**
    *   Initialize a sparse voxel grid within the determined bounding box [S2].
    *   **Synthetic Data Resolution Schedule:** Start at $(128, 128, 64)$ [S2]. Upsample to $(256, 256, 128)$, then $(512, 512, 256)$, and finally $(800, 800, 512)$, with each upsampling occurring after 25,600 iterations [S2].
    *   **Real Data Resolution Schedule:** Start at $(128, 128, 128)$ [S2]. Upsample to $(256, 256, 256)$, then $(750, 600, 300)$ [S2].
    *   Initialize voxel opacity $\sigma = 0.1$ and SH coefficients to represent a grey color (offset 0.5) [S2].

3.  **Coarse-to-Fine Training Loop:**
    *   For each training iteration:
        a.  **Ray Marching (HDR Rendering):**
            *   For each pixel, cast a ray through the scene [S2].
            *   Sample points along the ray.
            *   Interpolate opacity $\sigma$ and SH coefficients from the voxel grid at each sample point [S2].
            *   Compute the HDR radiance $L_{HDR}$ for the viewing direction using the SH basis functions $Y_l(\omega)$ and coefficients $c_l$:
                $$ L_{HDR}(\omega) = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} c_{l,m} Y_{l,m}(\omega) $$
                where $L$ is the degree of SH (typically 2 for 9 coefficients per channel) [S2].
            *   Perform volume rendering to composite the final HDR radiance $C_{HDR}$ for the pixel, accounting for depth and opacity [S2].
        b.  **Differentiable Tone Mapping:**
            *   Apply the tone mapping module to $C_{HDR}$ to produce the synthesized LDR image $C_{LDR}$ [S2].
            *   The tone mapping consists of two stages:
                1.  **White Balance:** Scale the RGB channels by the learned white balance parameters $w_r, w_g, w_b$ [S2].
                2.  **Camera Response Function (CRF):** Apply the learned CRF function $f(\cdot)$ to the white-balanced values [S2].
            *   The final LDR value is computed as:
                $$ C_{LDR} = f(w \odot C_{HDR}) $$
                where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication with white balance scales [S2].
        c.  **Loss Computation:**
            *   Compute the loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the synthesized LDR image $C_{LDR}$ and the ground truth LDR image $C_{GT}$ [S2].
            *   Include Total Variation (TV) loss for opacity $\sigma$ and SH coefficients during the first 3 epochs (before the first upsampling) to stabilize training [S2].
                $$ \mathcal{L}_{TV} = \lambda_{TV,\sigma} \text{TV}(\sigma) + \lambda_{TV,SH} \text{TV}(c_{SH}) $$
                with $\lambda_{TV,\sigma} = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ and $\lambda_{TV,SH} = 1 \cdot 10^{-2}$ [S2].
        d.  **Backpropagation:**
            *   Backpropagate gradients through the tone mapping module to update the SH coefficients, opacity, and tone mapping parameters (white balance, CRF) [S2].
            *   Use the RMSProp optimizer [S2].
        e.  **Grid Management:**
            *   **Upsampling:** Increase grid resolution according to the schedule [S2].
            *   **Pruning:** Remove voxels with opacity below a specified occupancy threshold to reduce computational cost [S2]. The specific threshold value is not given in the sources [S2].

4.  **Controllable Rendering (Post-Training):**
    *   To render at a specific exposure, scale the basis exposure value (globally averaged white balance) [S2].
    *   To render with specific white balance, adjust the red, green, and blue components of the basis white balance (channel-wise averaged) [S2].
    *   To render median exposure, use the median of the minimum and maximum learned exposure values [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np

def evaluate_sh(direction, sh_coeffs):
    """Evaluate degree-2 spherical harmonics for a direction.
    direction: (3,) unit vector. sh_coeffs: (27,) [R0..R8, G0..G8, B0..B8].
    Returns: (3,) radiance vector.
    """
    x, y, z = direction
    pi = np.pi
    # Standard degree-2 SH basis functions [G]
    s0 = 1.0 / np.sqrt(4.0 * pi)
    s1 = np.sqrt(3.0 / (4.0 * pi))
    s2 = np.sqrt(15.0 / (4.0 * pi))
    s3 = np.sqrt(5.0 / (16.0 * pi))
    basis = np.array([s0, -s1*x, s1*y, s1*z, s2*x*y, s2*y*z, 
                      s3*(2*z*z - x*x - y*y), s2*x*z, s2*x*z])
    # Reshape to (3, 9) for channels R, G, B
    coeffs = sh_coeffs.reshape(3, 9)
    radiance = np.zeros(3)
    for c in range(3):
        radiance[c] = np.dot(coeffs[c], basis)
    return radiance

def volume_render_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, grid, opacity_grid, sh_grid, num_samples):
    """Render a single ray through the voxel grid.
    Returns: (3,) HDR radiance, accumulated depth.
    """
    t_min, t_max = grid.bounds(ray_origin, ray_direction)
    t = np.linspace(t_min, t_max, num_samples)
    points = ray_origin + t[:, np.newaxis] * ray_direction
    radiance = np.zeros(3)
    alpha = 1.0
    for i in range(num_samples):
        p = points[i]
        # Trilinear interpolation from sparse grid
        op = grid.interpolate(p, opacity_grid)
        sh = grid.interpolate(p, sh_grid)
        if op < 1e-4:
            continue
        L = evaluate_sh(ray_direction, sh)
        T = np.exp(-op)
        radiance += alpha * (1.0 - T) * L
        alpha *= T
    return radiance, np.mean(t)

def apply_crf(color, crf_params):
    """Apply learned CRF. Uses a parametric power-law approximation [G]."""
    gamma = crf_params.get('gamma', 2.2)
    return np.power(np.clip(color, 0, 1), 1.0 / gamma)

def tone_mapping(hdr_radiance, wb_scales, crf_params):
    """Apply differentiable tone mapping."""
    wb_color = hdr_radiance * wb_scales
    ldr_color = apply_crf(wb_color, crf_params)
    return np.clip(ldr_color, 0, 1)

def compute_tv_loss(params):
    """Compute Total Variation loss."""
    return np.mean(np.abs(np.diff(params, axis=0)))

def prune_voxels(grid, opacity, threshold):
    """Remove voxels with opacity below threshold."""
    mask = opacity >= threshold
    grid.prune(mask)

def upsample_grid(grid, new_resolution):
    """Double grid resolution."""
    return grid.upsample(new_resolution)

def get_next_resolution(schedule, step, interval):
    """Return next resolution from schedule."""
    idx = min(step // interval, len(schedule) - 1)
    return schedule[idx]

def generate_rays(image_shape, camera_pose):
    """Generate camera rays for all pixels."""
    # Standard pinhole camera ray generation [G]
    rays = []
    for u in range(image_shape[1]):
        for v in range(image_shape[0]):
            origin = camera_pose.extrinsics.translation
            direction = camera_pose.extrinsics.rotation @ np.array([u, v, 1.0])
            rays.append((origin, direction))
    return rays

def stack_images(radiance_list, shape):
    """Assemble pixel radiance into image tensor."""
    return np.array(radiance_list).reshape(shape[0], shape[1], 3)

def mse_loss(pred, target):
    """Compute Mean Squared Error."""
    return np.mean((pred - target) ** 2)

class RMSProp:
    def __init__(self, params, lr=0.01, decay=0.9, eps=1e-8):
        self.params = params
        self.lr = lr
        self.decay = decay
        self.eps = eps
        self.cache = {p: np.zeros_like(p) for p in params}
    def step(self):
        for p in self.params:
            self.cache[p] = self.decay * self.cache[p] + (1 - self.decay) * p.grad ** 2
            p.value -= self.lr * p.grad / (np.sqrt(self.cache[p]) + self.eps)

    # Initialize parameters

    sh_coeffs = np.full((grid.size, 27), 0.5) # Grey offset
    wb_scales = np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0])
    crf_params = {'gamma': 2.2} # Learned during training [S2]
    
    optimizer = RMSProp([type('Param', (), {'value': opacity, 'grad': None})(),
                         type('Param', (), {'value': sh_coeffs, 'grad': None})(),
                         type('Param', (), {'value': wb_scales, 'grad': None})(),
                         type('Param', (), {'value': crf_params, 'grad': None})()])
    
    for step in range(total_iterations):
        # Check for upsampling
        if step > 0 and step % iterations_per_step == 0:
            current_res = get_next_resolution(resolution_schedule, step, iterations_per_step)
            grid = upsample_grid(grid, current_res)
            
        # 3. Training Step
        for image, pose in zip(images, poses):
            # Ray generation
            rays = generate_rays(image.shape, pose)
            
            # Forward Pass
            hdr_colors = []
            for origin, direction in rays:
                hdr_c, _ = volume_render_ray(origin, direction, grid, opacity, sh_coeffs, 128)
                hdr_colors.append(hdr_c)
            
            # Tone Mapping
            ldr_colors = []
            for hdr_c in hdr_colors:
                ldr_c = tone_mapping(hdr_c, wb_scales, crf_params)
                ldr_colors.append(ldr_c)
            
            # Loss Computation
            synthesized_image = stack_images(ldr_colors, image.shape)
            loss = mse_loss(synthesized_image, image)
            
            # Add TV loss if in first 3 epochs
            if step < 3 * iterations_per_step:
                tv_loss = compute_tv_loss(opacity) * lambda_tv_sigma + compute_tv_loss(sh_coeffs) * lambda_tv_sh
                loss += tv_loss
            
            # Backpropagation (simulated gradient step)
            optimizer.step()
            
            # Update grid parameters
            opacity = optimizer.params[0].value
            sh_coeffs = optimizer.params[1].value
            wb_scales = optimizer.params[2].value
            crf_params = optimizer.params[3].value
            
            # Pruning
            prune_voxels(grid, opacity, occupancy_threshold)
            
    # Compute basis values for controllable rendering
    basis_exposure = np.mean(wb_scales)
    basis_wb = np.mean(wb_scales) # Channel-wise average of full view
    return grid, wb_scales, crf_params, basis_exposure, basis_wb

def render_novel_view(camera_pose, wb_scale_factor, wb_channel_factors, exposure_factor, 
                      grid, wb_scales, crf_params, basis_exposure, basis_wb):
    """Controllable rendering."""
    # Adjust tone mapping parameters based on controls
    adjusted_wb = basis_wb * wb_scale_factor * wb_channel_factors
    adjusted_exposure = basis_exposure * exposure_factor
    
    # Render HDR
    rays = generate_rays(camera_resolution, camera_pose)
    hdr_colors = [volume_render_ray(o, d, grid, opacity, sh_coeffs, 128)[0] for o, d in rays]
    hdr_image = stack_images(hdr_colors, camera_resolution)
    
    # Apply adjusted tone mapping
    ldr_image = tone_mapping(hdr_image, adjusted_wb, crf_params)
    return ldr_image
```

### Parameters
*   **Occupancy Threshold:** Scalar value for pruning voxels. Value not specified in the sources [S2].
*   **TV Loss Weights:** $\lambda_{TV,\sigma} = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $\lambda_{TV,SH} = 1 \cdot 10^{-2}$ [S2].
*   **Optimizer:** RMSProp [S2].
*   **SH Degree:** 2 (9 coefficients per channel) [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Saturated Regions:** Wide dynamic range scenes cause saturation in LDR training images. HDR-Plenoxels handles this via the differentiable tone mapping module and robust learning of SH coefficients and opacity [S2].
*   **Large Depth in Synthetic Data:** Default concentric sphere grids struggle with large depth in Blender data. Solved by computing rough 3D geometry via COLMAP to determine the proper grid range [S2].
*   **Lens Distortion:** Real images often have lens distortion. Solved by undistorting images via calibration before training [S2].
*   **Training Instability:** SH coefficients can change significantly during training. Mitigated by initializing color to grey (offset 0.5) and opacity to 0.1 [S2].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is tens of minutes [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses sparse voxel grids, which are more memory-efficient than dense grids or dense neural networks for high-resolution scenes [S2].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Two orders of magnitude faster than NeRF [S2].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Fast convergence and rendering [S2].
    *   Handles varying camera settings (exposure, white balance, CRF) without explicit calibration [S2].
    *   Robust to saturated regions and shot noise [S2].
    *   Enables controllable rendering of exposure and white balance [S2].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires COLMAP for initial geometry and poses [S2].
    *   Performance degrades slightly with extreme exposure gaps (e.g., ±6EV), though still outperforms some alternatives [S2].
*   **When to Use:** When fast HDR novel view synthesis is required from multi-view LDR images with varying camera settings, and computational resources are limited compared to NeRF-based methods [S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** When dense COLMAP results are unavailable and cannot be approximated, or when implicit neural representations are strictly required for other reasons [S2].

## Background

*   **Origin:** Introduced by Kim Jun-Seong, Kim Yu-Ji, Moon Ye-Bin, and Tae-Hyun Oh at POSTECH [S2].
*   **Paper:** "HDR-Plenoxels: Self-Calibrating High Dynamic Range Radiance Fields" (arXiv:2208.06787v2, Nov 2022) [S2].
*   **Development:** Extends Plenoxels [53] for HDR radiance fields and adopts the coarse-to-fine training scheme from NSVF [17] [S2]. It addresses the limitations of NeRF in handling HDR and varying camera conditions efficiently [S2].
*   **Usage:** Used for novel view synthesis from multi-view LDR images captured with varying exposure, white balance, and CRF settings. Tested on Blender synthetic datasets and real scenes (Character, Desk, Plant, Coffee, Kitchen) [S2].
