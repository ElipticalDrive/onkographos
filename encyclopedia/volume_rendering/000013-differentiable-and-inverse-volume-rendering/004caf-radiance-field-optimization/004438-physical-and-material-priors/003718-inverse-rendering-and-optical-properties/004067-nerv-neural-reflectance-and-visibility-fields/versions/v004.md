# NeRV (Neural Reflectance and Visibility Fields)

Also called: Neural Reflectance and Visibility Fields

## Overview
NeRV is a differentiable inverse rendering method that reconstructs a 3D scene representation from images under unconstrained, known lighting, enabling view synthesis and relighting with indirect illumination [S1]. It extends the standard emission-absorption NeRF model by representing the scene as a 3D field of oriented particles that absorb and reflect light emitted by external sources, parameterized by neural networks [S1]. Unlike [Neural Radiance Fields](44-radiance-field-optimization.md) which only models direct emission, or [Neural Reflectance Fields](6755-neural-radiosity.md) which is limited to single-point-light direct illumination, NeRV supports arbitrary environment lighting and one-bounce global illumination [S1]. It achieves tractability by introducing a learned neural visibility field that approximates visibility and surface termination depths, reducing light transport simulation complexity from quadratic/cubic to linear/double-spherical integral [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of images of a scene illuminated by unconstrained known lighting, and known camera poses [S1].
**Outputs:** A 3D representation that can be rendered from novel viewpoints under arbitrary lighting conditions, including indirect illumination effects [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method uses three Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) parameterized by weights $\theta$, $\psi$, and $\phi$ [S1]:
1.  **Shape MLP ($MLP_\theta$):**
    *   Input: 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Output: Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ [S1].
2.  **Reflectance MLP ($MLP_\psi$):**
    *   Input: 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Output: Material parameters $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (diffuse albedo) and $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$ (specular roughness) [S1].
3.  **Visibility MLP ($MLP_\phi$):**
    *   Input: 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{S}^2$.
    *   Output: A tuple $(\tilde{V}, \tilde{t}_0)$, where $\tilde{V} \in [0, 1]$ is the estimated visibility and $\tilde{t}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the estimated distance to the first surface intersection (expected termination depth) [S1].

**Surface Normal:**
The surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ is not an MLP output but is computed as the negative normalized gradient of the density $\sigma$ with respect to position $\mathbf{x}$, using automatic differentiation [S1]:
$$
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x}) = -\frac{\nabla_\mathbf{x} \sigma(\mathbf{x})}{\|\nabla_\mathbf{x} \sigma(\mathbf{x})\|}
$$

**Reflectance Function:**
The reflectance $R$ is a standard microfacet BRDF parameterized by diffuse RGB albedo $\mathbf{a}$, scalar specular roughness $\gamma$, and surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ [S1].

**Rendering Equation:**
The total radiance $L(\mathbf{c}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)$ observed from camera center $\mathbf{c}$ in direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_o$ is the sum of direct and indirect lighting components [S1]:

$$
L(\mathbf{c}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) = L_{\text{direct}} + L_{\text{indirect}}
$$

1.  **Direct Lighting ($L_{\text{direct}}$):**
    The integral along the camera ray $\mathbf{x}(t)$, attenuated by the true visibility $V(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{c})$ and the neural visibility approximation $\tilde{V}_\phi$ for the light direction [S1]:
    $$
    L_{\text{direct}} = \int_0^\infty V(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{c}) \sigma(\mathbf{x}(t)) \left[ \int_S \tilde{V}_\phi(\mathbf{x}(t), \boldsymbol{\omega}_i) E(\mathbf{x}(t), -\boldsymbol{\omega}_i) R(\mathbf{x}(t), \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) \, d\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \right] dt
    $$
    Where $E$ is the environment map radiance, $S$ is the sphere of directions, and $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i$ are light directions [S1]. The term $V(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{c})$ is the standard transmittance along the camera ray:
    $$
    V(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{c}) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(\mathbf{x}(s)) \, ds\right)
    $$

2.  **Indirect Lighting ($L_{\text{indirect}}$):**
    Approximated by treating the volume as a hard surface at the expected termination depth $t_0$ of the camera ray [S1]. The outermost integral is replaced by a single point evaluation at $\mathbf{x}(t_0)$:
    $$
    L_{\text{indirect}} = \int_S L(\mathbf{x}(t_0), -\boldsymbol{\omega}_i) R(\mathbf{x}(t_0), \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) \, d\boldsymbol{\omega}_i
    $$
    Here, $L(\mathbf{x}(t_0), -\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)$ is the incident radiance at the surface point, which for one-bounce indirect illumination is approximated using the neural visibility field and environment map [S1]. The termination depth $t_0$ is derived from the camera ray sampling of $\sigma$, not the visibility MLP's prediction for the camera direction, to ensure consistency with the sampled density [S1].

**Training Procedure:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize $MLP_\theta$, $MLP_\psi$, and $MLP_\phi$ [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** For each training step, sample a batch of pixels, their camera rays, and stratified random points along those rays to approximate the integrals [S1].
3.  **Visibility Supervision:** The visibility MLP $MLP_\phi$ is supervised to be consistent with the volume density samples observed during optimization [S1]. Specifically, it learns to predict the visibility and termination depth that would result from raymarching through the current $\sigma$ field [S1].
4.  **Optimization:** Optimize all MLP weights ($\theta, \psi, \phi$) via gradient descent to minimize the error between the rendered color and the observed input image colors [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import random

def stratified_sample(lower, upper, num_samples):
    """Returns num_samples evenly spaced points with stratified jitter in [lower, upper]."""
    step = (upper - lower) / num_samples
    points = []
    for i in range(num_samples):
        points.append(lower + (i + random.random()) * step)
    return points

def sample_sphere(num_samples):
    """Returns num_samples uniformly distributed unit vectors on a sphere."""
    directions = []
    for _ in range(num_samples):
        theta = random.uniform(0, 2 * math.pi)
        cos_phi = random.uniform(-1, 1)
        sin_phi = math.sqrt(1 - cos_phi**2)
        directions.append((sin_phi * math.cos(theta), sin_phi * math.sin(theta), cos_phi))
    return directions

def microfacet_brdf(normal, wi, wo, albedo, roughness):
    """Evaluates a standard microfacet BRDF. Returns reflectance value [G]."""
    # Simplified diffuse approximation for implementation completeness
    return albedo / math.pi

def compute_normal(shape_mlp, x):
    """Computes the negative normalized gradient of density using finite differences."""
    eps = 1e-5
    grad = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    sigma = shape_mlp(x)
    for i in range(3):
        x_plus = list(x)
        x_plus[i] += eps
        sigma_plus = shape_mlp(x_plus)
        grad[i] = (sigma_plus - sigma) / eps
    norm = math.sqrt(sum(g**2 for g in grad))
    if norm < 1e-8:
        return [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]
    return [-g / norm for g in grad]

def render_nev(c, wo, env_map, shape_mlp, reflectance_mlp, visibility_mlp, N_ray, N_light):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using NeRV.
    Args:
        c: 3D vector camera origin
        wo: 3D vector camera direction
        env_map: Function E(x, omega) returning radiance from direction omega at x
        shape_mlp: MLP_theta returning sigma(x)
        reflectance_mlp: MLP_psi returning (albedo a, roughness gamma)
        visibility_mlp: MLP_phi returning (V_tilde, t_tilde)
        N_ray: Number of samples along camera ray
        N_light: Number of samples for light integration
    Returns:
        color: 3D vector (R, G, B)
    """
    # 1. Sample points along camera ray
    t_vals = stratified_sample(0.0, 10.0, N_ray)
    x_pts = [(c[0] + t * wo[0], c[1] + t * wo[1], c[2] + t * wo[2]) for t in t_vals]

    # 2. Evaluate density and compute transmittance/alpha
    sigmas = [shape_mlp(x) for x in x_pts]
    deltas = [t_vals[i+1] - t_vals[i] for i in range(N_ray - 1)]
    deltas.insert(0, 1e-10)

    alphas = [1.0 - math.exp(-s * d) for s, d in zip(sigmas, deltas)]
    transmittances = [1.0]
    for a in alphas[:-1]:
        transmittances.append(transmittances[-1] * (1.0 - a))

    # 3. Direct Lighting Integral
    direct_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    light_dirs = sample_sphere(N_light)

    for i in range(N_ray):
        if alphas[i] < 1e-5:
            continue
        x_i = x_pts[i]
        sigma_i = sigmas[i]
        T_i = transmittances[i]
        alpha_i = alphas[i]

        a_mat, gamma = reflectance_mlp(x_i)
        n = compute_normal(shape_mlp, x_i)

        light_sum = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
        for wi in light_dirs:
            V_tilde, _ = visibility_mlp(x_i, wi)
            E_val = env_map(x_i, (-wi[0], -wi[1], -wi[2]))
            R_val = microfacet_brdf(n, wi, wo, a_mat, gamma)
            for k in range(3):
                light_sum[k] += V_tilde * E_val[k] * R_val[k]

        light_avg = [s / N_light for s in light_sum]
        for k in range(3):
            direct_color[k] += T_i * alpha_i * light_avg[k]

    # 4. Indirect Lighting (One-bounce approximation at expected termination depth)
    num_t0 = sum(t * a for t, a in zip(t_vals, alphas))
    den_t0 = sum(alphas)
    if den_t0 > 1e-8:
        t0 = num_t0 / den_t0
        x_surf = (c[0] + t0 * wo[0], c[1] + t0 * wo[1], c[2] + t0 * wo[2])

        a_s, gamma_s = reflectance_mlp(x_surf)
        n_s = compute_normal(shape_mlp, x_surf)

        indirect_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
        for wi in light_dirs:
            V_tilde, _ = visibility_mlp(x_surf, wi)
            E_val = env_map(x_surf, (-wi[0], -wi[1], -wi[2]))
            R_val = microfacet_brdf(n_s, wi, wo, a_s, gamma_s)
            L_inc = [V_tilde * E_val[k] for k in range(3)]
            for k in range(3):
                indirect_color[k] += L_inc[k] * R_val[k]

        indirect_color = [s / N_light for s in indirect_color]
    else:
        indirect_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

    return [direct_color[k] + indirect_color[k] for k in range(3)]
```

## Subtopics

### [NeRV Evaluation Variants](10273-nerv-evaluation-variants.md)
Inference-time strategies that trade rendering speed for shadow fidelity or material generality, addressing the base method's soft shadows and diffuse-only assumption.

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:** Direct lighting complexity is reduced from quadratic to linear in samples per ray by using neural visibility approximations instead of line integrals [S1]. Indirect lighting complexity is reduced from cubic to a double spherical integral by using hard surface approximations at expected termination depths [S1].
**Memory Complexity:** The method requires storing the weights of three MLPs ($\theta, \psi, \phi$) [S1]. It does not require storing intermediate visibility data structures like BVHs or distance transforms, which are prohibitively expensive to rebuild after each training iteration because the geometry changes constantly during optimization [S1].
**Strengths:** The method supports training with arbitrary environment lighting and one-bounce indirect illumination [S1]. It enables tractable optimization with dynamic geometry changes because visibility is learned via MLPs rather than rebuilt discrete structures [S1]. It simultaneously enables relighting and view synthesis [S1].
**Limitations:** The learned visibility approximation produces soft shadows [S1]. The base formulation assumes diffuse surfaces, though variants exist to handle non-diffuse materials [S1]. Indirect lighting is limited to a one-bounce approximation [S1]. Brute-force light transport simulation through NeRF's volumetric representation is intractable for environment light sources or global illumination without this approximation [S1].

## Background
NeRV was introduced by Pratul P. Srinivasan, Boyang Deng, Xiuming Zhang, Matthew Tancik, Ben Mildenhall, and Jonathan T. Barron in 2021 [S1]. It extends the work on Neural Reflectance Fields, which was limited to single-point-light direct illumination, by introducing a neural visibility field to handle complex lighting and global illumination [S1]. The method addresses the intractability of brute-force light transport simulation in volumetric neural representations by replacing expensive visibility queries with a learned MLP [S1].
