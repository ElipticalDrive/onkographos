# Distance-based Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Mesh-based Gaussian Splatting, Tightly/Loosely-bound Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Distance-based Gaussian Splatting (MeshGS) bridges the gap between large-scale explicit triangle meshes and volumetric Gaussian splats by classifying primitives as tightly-bound or loosely-bound based on their distance to the mesh surface [S1]. It solves the problem of rendering high-fidelity appearance while maintaining geometric consistency with a lightweight mesh, specifically addressing artifacts where strict binding fails to capture severe mesh inaccuracies [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on opacity fields or 2D lifting for segmentation, this method explicitly uses mesh proximity to prune occluded splats and align tightly-bound splats with surface normals [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A decimated triangle mesh $M$ with $K$ triangles (typically $10^3$ to $10^6$ units), a set of training views, and standard 3DGS hyperparameters [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian splats $\mathcal{G}$, classified into tightly-bound ($G_t$) and loosely-bound ($G_l$) subsets, optimized to render images consistent with training views while adhering to the mesh geometry [S1].

**Data Structures**
Each Gaussian primitive $g_i$ is defined by:
*   Center position: $\boldsymbol{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   3D Covariance matrix: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$, parameterized as $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \boldsymbol{R}_i \boldsymbol{S}_i \boldsymbol{S}_i^T \boldsymbol{R}_i^T$, where $\boldsymbol{S}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a scaling vector and $\boldsymbol{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ is a rotation matrix encoded by a quaternion $\boldsymbol{q} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ [S1].
*   Opacity: $o_i \in [0, 1]$ [S1].
*   Color: View-dependent color $\boldsymbol{c}_i$ represented by spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients [S1].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a 3D Gaussian splat at the center $\boldsymbol{m}_j$ of each triangle $\boldsymbol{f}_j$ of the mesh $M$ [S1].
2.  **Occlusion Masking (Pre-training/Per-view):** For a given viewpoint $\boldsymbol{P}_i$, render a depth map $D_i$ from the mesh. Assume the mesh surface is entirely opaque. Mask out any Gaussian splats occluded by the mesh surface in the rendered depth [S1].
3.  **Visibility Assessment:** Assess the visibility of all Gaussian splats across all training views. Identify splats located behind the mesh surface that are never observed by any training view [S1].
4.  **Pruning:** Remove all redundant Gaussian splats situated behind the mesh surface at specific training iterations. The sources specify pruning occurs every 500 training iterations [S1].
5.  **Distance Calculation:** Calculate the Euclidean distance $d_i$ between each Gaussian splat’s center $\boldsymbol{p}_i$ and the corresponding closest mesh face $\boldsymbol{f}_j$ [S1].
6.  **Classification:** Classify Gaussian splats based on the distance threshold $d_{th}$:
    *   Tightly-bound splats ($G_{ti}$): if $d_i < d_{th}$ [S1].
    *   Loosely-bound splats ($G_{li}$): if $d_i > d_{th}$ [S1].
    *   The source sets $d_{th} = 0.01$ [S1].
7.  **Alignment:** Flatten and align the tightly-bound splats ($G_{ti}$) with the mesh surface. This involves refining their orientation to match the mesh normals [S1].
8.  **Rendering:** Render the scene using alpha blending.
    *   Project 3D Gaussians to camera space: $\boldsymbol{p}_{ci} = \boldsymbol{R}_{cw} \boldsymbol{p}_i + \boldsymbol{t}_{cw}$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ic} = \boldsymbol{R}_{cw} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \boldsymbol{R}_{cw}^T$ [S1].
    *   Transform to ray space using affine transformation $\boldsymbol{J}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times3}$: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i = \boldsymbol{J}_i \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ic} \boldsymbol{J}_i^T$ [S1].
    *   Compute 2D Gaussian value: $g_i(\boldsymbol{x}) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}'_i)^T {\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}'_i)}$ [S1].
    *   Compute blending weight: $\alpha_i = o_i g_i(\boldsymbol{x})$ [S1].
    *   Final color: $c = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \boldsymbol{c}_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-\alpha_j)$ [S1].

**Formulas**
*   **3D Gaussian Distribution:**
    $$G_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}) = e^{-\frac {1}{2}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}_{i})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma }_{i}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}_{i})}$$
    Where $\boldsymbol{p}_i$ is the center and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ is the 3D covariance [S1].
*   **2D Gaussian Distribution:**
    $$g_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}) = e^{-\frac {1}{2}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}'_{i})^T {\boldsymbol{\Sigma }'_{{i}}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{p}'_{i})}$$
    Where $\boldsymbol{p}'_i$ is the 2D center and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i$ is the 2D covariance in ray space [S1].
*   **Rendering Equation:**
    $$c = \sum _{i=1}^{n} \boldsymbol{c}_{i}\alpha _{i}\prod _{j=1}^{i-1}(1-\alpha _{j}), \quad \alpha _{i} = o_{i}g_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})$$
    Where $\boldsymbol{c}_i$ is view-dependent color, $o_i$ is opacity, and $g_i(\boldsymbol{x})$ is the 2D Gaussian value [S1].

**Parameters**
*   Distance threshold $d_{th}$: 0.01 [S1].
*   Depth offset: Add 0.01 to the rendered depth $D_i$ when applying occlusion masking [S1].
*   Pruning interval: Every 500 training iterations [S1].
*   Loss weights: $\lambda_{img} = 0.001$, $\lambda_{nc} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{min} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{max} = 10$, $\lambda_{proj} = 50$ [S1].
*   Normal consistency threshold $\rho$: 0.1 [S1].
*   Total iterations: 30,000 [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
def distance_based_gaussian_splatting(mesh, training_views, iterations=30000):
    # 1. Initialization
    # Initialize Gaussian splats at the center of each triangle in the mesh
    gaussians = []
    for triangle in mesh.triangles:
        center = triangle.centroid
        # Initialize standard 3DGS parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH)
        g = Gaussian(center=center)
        gaussians.append(g)
    
    # 2. Training Loop
    for iter in range(1, iterations + 1):
        
        # Pruning step: Remove occluded/redundant splats every 500 iterations
        if iter % 500 == 0:
            for view in training_views:
                # Render depth from mesh
                mesh_depth = render_mesh_depth(mesh, view.camera)
                
                # Mask out splats occluded by mesh (add 0.01 offset to depth)
                for g in gaussians:
                    projected_depth = project_gaussian_depth(g, view.camera)
                    if projected_depth > mesh_depth + 0.01:
                        g.mark_for_removal()
            
            # Remove marked splats
            gaussians = [g for g in gaussians if not g.marked_for_removal()]

        # 3. Distance Calculation and Classification
        for g in gaussians:
            # Calculate distance to closest mesh face
            dist = distance_to_mesh(g.position, mesh)
            if dist < 0.01: # d_th = 0.01
                g.category = TIGHTLY_BOUND
            else:
                g.category = LOOSELY_BOUND

        # 4. Optimization Step
        for view in training_views:
            # Render image using alpha blending
            rendered_image = render_gaussians(gaussians, view.camera)
            
            # Calculate Loss
            # L_img = (1 - lambda_img) * L1(rendered, ground_truth) + lambda_img * SSIM(rendered, ground_truth)
            # L_nc = Normal Consistency Loss for tightly-bound splats
            loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, view.gt_image, gaussians)
            
            # Backpropagate and update Gaussian parameters
            update_gaussians(gaussians, loss)

    return gaussians
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Mesh Artifacts:** Strictly binding all splats to the mesh can suffer rendering performance issues if the mesh has severe artifacts. The loosely-bound classification allows splats further than $d_{th}$ to capture these inaccuracies [S1].
*   **Occlusion Assumption:** The method assumes the mesh surface is entirely opaque. Splats behind the mesh are excluded from rendering [S1].
*   **Redundancy:** Without pruning, initializing splats at every triangle center can lead to a rapid increase in GPU memory usage and undermine rendering quality due to redundant splats in distorted regions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering is $O(N)$ per pixel where $N$ is the number of splats, similar to standard 3DGS. The classification and pruning steps add overhead proportional to the number of splats and training views.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly reduced compared to standard 3DGS which densifies heavily. By initializing only at mesh triangle centers and pruning occluded splats, memory usage is constrained by the mesh complexity ($K$ triangles) rather than scene density [S1].
*   **Performance:** The method maintains geometric quality with a minimal number of triangles ($10^3$ to $10^6$) while achieving high-fidelity rendering [S1]. It outperforms methods like LTM and SuGaR in scenarios requiring lightweight meshes with detailed appearance [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient memory usage, explicit geometric alignment, handles large-scale scenes.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the initial mesh; severe mesh artifacts may require the loosely-bound mechanism to render correctly, potentially introducing slight geometric inconsistency [S1].

## Background
Distance-based Gaussian Splatting, specifically implemented as MeshGS, was introduced by Jaehoon et al. in 2024 [S1]. It builds upon the original 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework [1795-3d-gaussian-splatting.md] and addresses the limitations of existing methods like LTM (Lightweight Triangle Mesh) and SuGaR, which either require excessive triangle counts or fail to capture complex structures with low vertex counts [S1]. The method is designed for high-quality rendering of large-scale scenes where a lightweight mesh representation is desired [S1].
