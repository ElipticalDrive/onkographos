# 7D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 7D Gaussian Representation, 7DGS, 7DGS definition

## Overview
7D Gaussian Splatting (7DGS) is a unified framework for the real-time rendering of dynamic scenes with complex view-dependent effects, representing scene elements as seven-dimensional Gaussians spanning spatial position (3D), time (1D), and viewing direction (3D) [S1]. It solves the problem of simultaneously modeling spatial geometry, temporal dynamics, and view-dependent appearance (such as scattering and anisotropic reflections) in dynamic scenes, a challenge that prior methods like 4DGS and 6DGS addressed in isolation [S1]. Unlike its siblings, 7DGS jointly models all three dimensions in a single coherent framework, using a conditional slicing mechanism to transform 7D Gaussians into time- and view-conditioned 3D Gaussians compatible with existing 3DGS rendering pipelines [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 7D Gaussian primitives, each defined by a mean vector and a covariance matrix in the joint space of position, time, and direction.
*   **Output:** A rendered image for a specific camera pose (view direction) and time instance, generated by projecting the conditioned 3D Gaussians and rasterizing them.

**Data Structures:**
Each scene element is modeled as a 7D Gaussian random variable $X$ jointly encoding spatial ($X_p \in \mathbb{R}^3$), temporal ($X_t \in \mathbb{R}$), and directional ($X_d \in \mathbb{R}^3$) properties [S1]. The distribution is defined as:

$$
X \sim \mathcal{N} \left( \begin{pmatrix} \mu_p \\ \mu_t \\ \mu_d \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma_p & \Sigma_{pt} & \Sigma_{pd} \\ \Sigma_{pt}^\top & \Sigma_t & \Sigma_{td} \\ \Sigma_{pd}^\top & \Sigma_{td}^\top & \Sigma_d \end{pmatrix} \right)
$$

where:
*   $\mu_p, \Sigma_p$: Mean and covariance for spatial coordinates, modeling local geometric shape [S1].
*   $\mu_t, \Sigma_t$: Mean and variance for the temporal coordinate, accounting for dynamic evolution [S1].
*   $\mu_d, \Sigma_d$: Mean and covariance for the viewing direction, capturing view-dependent appearance [S1].
*   $\Sigma_{pt}, \Sigma_{pd}, \Sigma_{td}$: Off-diagonal blocks capturing correlations among spatial, temporal, and directional components, enabling the modeling of complex interdependencies [S1].

The full 7D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is parameterized using Cholesky decomposition to guarantee validity during optimization:

$$
\Sigma = L L^\top
$$

where $L$ is a lower-triangular matrix with positive diagonal entries [S1].

Color representation uses view-dependent spherical harmonics from 3DGS without additional temporal dependencies, as dynamic information is encoded within the Gaussian parameters [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialize 7D Gaussians:** Create a set of 7D Gaussians where each primitive stores $\mu_p, \mu_t, \mu_d, \alpha$ (opacity), and the Cholesky factor $L$ of the covariance matrix $\Sigma$. [S1]
2.  **Conditional Slicing:** For a target rendering time $t$ and view direction $d$, compute the conditioned 3D Gaussian parameters for each primitive. This involves calculating the conditional mean $\mu_{cond}$ and conditional covariance $\Sigma_{cond}$ of the spatial component $X_p$ given $X_t = t$ and $X_d = d$.
    *   Let the joint mean be $\mu = [\mu_p^\top, \mu_t, \mu_d^\top]^\top$ and the joint covariance be $\Sigma$.
    *   Partition $\Sigma$ and $\mu$ into spatial ($s$), temporal ($t$), and directional ($d$) parts.
    *   The conditional mean is:
        $$ \mu_{cond} = \mu_p + \Sigma_{ps} \Sigma_{ss}^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} t - \mu_t \\ d - \mu_d \end{pmatrix} $$
        *(Note: The exact block matrix inversion logic follows standard multivariate Gaussian conditioning. The sources define the slicing mechanism conceptually as transforming 7D to 3D conditioned on time and view [S1]. The specific block algebra is derived from the definition of conditional Gaussian distributions.)*
    *   The conditional covariance is:
        $$ \Sigma_{cond} = \Sigma_p - \Sigma_{ps} \Sigma_{ss}^{-1} \Sigma_{sp} $$
        *(Where $\Sigma_{ss}$ is the sub-matrix for time and direction, and $\Sigma_{ps}$ contains the cross-covariances $\Sigma_{pt}$ and $\Sigma_{pd}$.)*
    *   The conditioned opacity $\alpha_{cond}$ may also be adjusted based on the conditioning, though the source primarily emphasizes the spatial parameters [S1].
3.  **Adaptive Refinement (Optional/Training):** During optimization, use a neural network to predict residuals that dynamically adjust Gaussian parameters to model complex non-rigid deformations and time-varying appearance [S1].
4.  **Render:** Project the conditioned 3D Gaussians ($\mu_{cond}, \Sigma_{cond}, \alpha_{cond}$) onto the image plane using standard 3DGS projection and rasterization. Compute color using spherical harmonics evaluated at the view direction $d$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def cholesky_decompose(cov_matrix):
    """
    Computes the lower-triangular matrix L such that Sigma = L @ L.T.
    Ensures positive diagonal entries.
    """
    L = np.linalg.cholesky(cov_matrix)
    return L

def reconstruct_covariance(L):
    """
    Reconstructs the covariance matrix from its Cholesky factor.
    """
    return L @ L.T

def conditional_slice_7d_to_3d(mean_7d, L_7d, time_val, direction_vec):
    """
    Slices a 7D Gaussian into a 3D Gaussian conditioned on time and direction.
    
    Args:
        mean_7d: Array of shape (7,) containing [mu_p (3), mu_t (1), mu_d (3)]
        L_7d: Lower-triangular Cholesky factor of shape (7, 7)
        time_val: Scalar time value t
        direction_vec: Array of shape (3,) representing view direction d
        
    Returns:
        mu_cond: Array of shape (3,) conditioned spatial mean
        Sigma_cond: Array of shape (3, 3) conditioned spatial covariance
    """
    # Extract components
    mu_p = mean_7d[0:3]
    mu_t = mean_7d[3]
    mu_d = mean_7d[4:7]
    
    # Reconstruct full covariance matrix
    Sigma = reconstruct_covariance(L_7d)
    
    # Partition Sigma into blocks
    # Indices: 0-2 (p), 3 (t), 4-6 (d)
    Sigma_pp = Sigma[0:3, 0:3]
    Sigma_pt = Sigma[0:3, 3:4]
    Sigma_pd = Sigma[0:3, 4:7]
    
    Sigma_tp = Sigma[3:4, 0:3]
    Sigma_tt = Sigma[3:4, 3:4]
    Sigma_td = Sigma[3:4, 4:7]
    
    Sigma_dp = Sigma[4:7, 0:3]
    Sigma_dt = Sigma[4:7, 3:4]
    Sigma_dd = Sigma[4:7, 4:7]
    
    # Construct the joint covariance of the conditioned variables (t, d)
    # and the cross-covariance with p
    # Let z = [t, d]. We want p | z.
    
    # Covariance of z (time and direction)
    Sigma_zz = np.zeros((4, 4))
    Sigma_zz[0, 0] = Sigma_tt
    Sigma_zz[0, 1:] = Sigma_td
    Sigma_zz[1:, 0] = Sigma_dt
    Sigma_zz[1:, 1:] = Sigma_dd
    
    # Cross-covariance between p and z
    Sigma_pz = np.hstack((Sigma_pt, Sigma_pd)) # Shape (3, 4)
    
    # Residual vector for conditioning
    # z - mu_z
    mu_z = np.array([mu_t, *mu_d])
    residual_z = np.array([time_val - mu_t, *(direction_vec - mu_d)])
    
    # Compute conditional mean: mu_p + Sigma_pz @ Sigma_zz^-1 @ (z - mu_z)
    # Solve Sigma_zz @ x = residual_z for x
    try:
        inv_Sigma_zz_residual = np.linalg.solve(Sigma_zz, residual_z)
    except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
        # Fallback for singular matrices, though Cholesky implies positive definite
        inv_Sigma_zz_residual = np.linalg.lstsq(Sigma_zz, residual_z, rcond=None)[0]
        
    mu_cond = mu_p + Sigma_pz @ inv_Sigma_zz_residual
    
    # Compute conditional covariance: Sigma_pp - Sigma_pz @ Sigma_zz^-1 @ Sigma_zp
    Sigma_zp = Sigma_pz.T
    correction = Sigma_pz @ np.linalg.solve(Sigma_zz, Sigma_zp)
    Sigma_cond = Sigma_pp - correction
    
    return mu_cond, Sigma_cond

def render_7dgs(gaussians, time_val, direction_vec, camera_params):
    """
    Renders a frame using 7DGS.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of dicts, each containing 'mean_7d' (7,), 'L_7d' (7,7), 'alpha', 'sh_coeffs'
        time_val: Float, current time
        direction_vec: Array (3,), view direction
        camera_params: Object with projection matrix and intrinsics
        
    Returns:
        image: Rendered image array
    """
    # 1. Slice all 7D Gaussians to 3D
    conditioned_gaussians = []
    for g in gaussians:
        mu_cond, Sigma_cond = conditional_slice_7d_to_3d(
            g['mean_7d'], g['L_7d'], time_val, direction_vec
        )
        # Note: Opacity alpha might also be conditioned, but source focuses on spatial params
        conditioned_gaussians.append({
            'mu': mu_cond,
            'Sigma': Sigma_cond,
            'alpha': g['alpha'],
            'sh_coeffs': g['sh_coeffs']
        })
        
    # 2. Project and Rasterize (Standard 3DGS Pipeline)
    # This part builds on [Gaussian Data Structure](2273-gaussian-data-structure.md)
    # and [3D Gaussian Data Layout](2773-3d-gaussian-data-layout.md)
    image = standard_3dgs_rasterize(conditioned_gaussians, camera_params)
    
    return image
```

**Parameters and Typical Values:**
*   **Spherical Harmonics Degree:** Not specified in sources, but inherits from 3DGS/6DGS [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieved 401 FPS on challenging dynamic scenes with complex view-dependent effects [S1].
*   **Quality:** Outperforms prior methods by up to 7.36 dB in PSNR [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Singular Covariance:** The Cholesky decomposition requires a positive definite covariance matrix. If the optimization leads to a singular or near-singular matrix during the conditioning step (specifically $\Sigma_{zz}$), numerical instability may occur. The pseudocode includes a fallback to least squares, but in practice, regularization or constraints on the diagonal of $L$ are necessary to maintain positive definiteness [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The sources do not specify handling for time or direction values outside the training domain. [G: Typically, clamping or extrapolation is required, but not defined in the source.]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Real-time rendering is achieved, with reported performance of 401 FPS on challenging dynamic scenes [S1]. The conditional slicing operation adds computational overhead compared to static 3DGS, but remains efficient enough for real-time application.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Higher than static 3DGS due to the storage of temporal and directional means, variances, and cross-covariances for each Gaussian. The Cholesky factor $L$ requires storing 28 values (for a 7x7 lower triangular matrix) plus means and opacity, compared to 10-15 values for standard 3DGS.
*   **Strengths:** Unified modeling of space, time, and direction; high PSNR (up to 7.36 dB improvement over prior methods); real-time performance; compatibility with existing 3DGS pipelines.
*   **Limitations:** Increased memory footprint; complexity in optimizing the full 7D covariance matrix; potential numerical instability in conditional slicing if covariance matrices are ill-conditioned.
*   **When to use:** When rendering dynamic scenes with significant view-dependent effects (e.g., specular highlights, scattering) is required, and real-time performance is necessary.
*   **When not to use:** For static scenes (use 3DGS) or scenes where only temporal dynamics or only view-dependent effects are present and extreme efficiency is prioritized over unified modeling.

## Background
7D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Zhongpai Gao, Benjamin Planche, Meng Zheng, Anwesa Choudhuri, Terrence Chen, and Ziyan Wu from United Imaging Intelligence in March 2025 [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous methods: 4DGS, which handles space and time but not view-dependent effects, and 6DGS, which handles space and direction but not temporal dynamics [S1]. The method builds on the foundations of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and 6D Gaussian Splatting (6DGS) [S1]. It has been evaluated on synthetic datasets (D-NeRF) and in-the-wild multi-view videos (Technicolor), as well as a custom dataset (7DGS-PBR) featuring dynamic scenes with complex motions and view-dependent effects [S1].
