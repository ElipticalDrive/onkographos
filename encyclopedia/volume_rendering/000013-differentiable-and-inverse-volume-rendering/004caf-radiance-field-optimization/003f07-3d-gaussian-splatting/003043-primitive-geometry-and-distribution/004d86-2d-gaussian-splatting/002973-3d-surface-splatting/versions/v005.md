# 3D Surface Splatting

Also called: 3DSS

## Overview
3D Surface Splatting (3DSS) is a differentiable surface splatting renderer for physically-based inverse rendering that jointly recovers geometry, materials, and illumination from multi-view images by optimizing an unstructured set of oriented surfel primitives [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of multi-layer surface separation and visibility discontinuity in surface splatting by formulating surface grouping as an interval-merging algorithm over sorted primitives, replacing binary/ternary depth tests with a smooth, coverage-based compositing model where per-layer opacity arises directly from accumulated Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) reconstruction weights [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent [2D Gaussian Splatting](6068-2d-gaussian-splatting.md) by using a surface rendering model rather than a volumetric one (surfels carry no learnable opacity), and from [Flattened Gaussian Ellipsoid Rendering](1667-flattened-gaussian-ellipsoid-rendering.md) by explicitly handling multiple surface layers per pixel through depth-interval merging rather than single-layer rasterization [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** An unstructured set of surfel primitives, each defined by center position $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, tangent vectors $\mathbf{t}_u, \mathbf{t}_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and shading attributes (albedo $\mathbf{a} \in [0, 1]^3$, metallic $m \in [0, 1]$, roughness $r \in [0, 1]$) [S1, unofficial]. Co-optimized HDR environment lighting [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A differentiable 2D image (HDR) and gradients for the surfel parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each surfel primitive stores:
*   $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{t}_u, \mathbf{t}_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Tangent vectors spanning the local tangent plane. These encode orientation and anisotropic scaling [S1, unofficial]. The surface normal is implicitly $\mathbf{n} = (\mathbf{t}_u \times \mathbf{t}_v) / \|\mathbf{t}_u \times \mathbf{t}_v\|$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{a} \in [0, 1]^3$: Albedo [S1, unofficial].
*   $m \in [0, 1]$: Metallic [S1, unofficial].
*   $r \in [0, 1]$: Roughness [S1, unofficial].
*   Derived view-space properties: Depth $d$, symmetric depth extent $\delta$, and depth interval $[d-\delta, d+\delta]$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\delta_{cut}$: Kernel support cutoff threshold. The source uses $\delta_{cut}$ as a generic parameter for the squared distance threshold $\rho^2 \ge \delta_{cut}^2$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **T-matrix Construction:**
    For each surfel, transform $\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{t}_u, \mathbf{t}_v$ to camera space, yielding $\mathbf{c}', \mathbf{t}'_u, \mathbf{t}'_v$ [S1, unofficial]. Construct the $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix $S = [\mathbf{t}'_u \ \mathbf{t}'_v \ \mathbf{0} \ \mathbf{c}']$ mapping local surfel space (unit sphere parameterization) to camera space [S1, unofficial]. Let $P$ be the projection matrix. Compute the T-matrix $T = PS$. This maps points on the unit sphere in local surfel space to clip space [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Bounding Rectangle and Depth Extent:**
    Compute the bounding rectangle of the surfel in screen space using closed-form expressions involving dot products among the rows of $T$ [S1, unofficial]. Compute the symmetric depth extent in view space: $\delta = \sqrt{t'_{u,z}^2 + t'_{v,z}^2} \cdot \delta_{cut}$, where $t'_{u,z}$ and $t'_{v,z}$ are the z-components (view-space depth) of the camera-space tangent vectors [S1, unofficial]. The depth interval is $[d-\delta, d+\delta]$ where $d$ is the view-space depth of $\mathbf{c}'$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Forward Shading (Pre-shading):**
    Shade each surfel before rasterization [S1, unofficial]. Apply forward microfacet shading under the co-optimized HDR environment lighting using split-sum Image-Based Lighting (IBL) [S1, unofficial]. This yields a pre-shaded linear radiance $\mathbf{c}_{pre}$ for each surfel [S1, unofficial]. This step avoids blending directional quantities (like normals) volumetrically [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Tiling and Sorting:**
    Bin surfels into screen-space tiles [S1, unofficial]. Sort the primitives within each tile by their depth interval start $d_{start} = d - \delta$ (closest extent to the camera), not by center depth [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Per-Pixel Rasterization and Surface Layer Grouping:**
    For each pixel in a tile, iterate through the sorted surfels [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Ray-Surfel Intersection:** Represent the pixel's viewing ray as the intersection of two clip-space planes: $\mathbf{h}_u = (-1, 0, 0, u)^T$ and $\mathbf{h}_v = (0, -1, 0, v)^T$, where $(u,v)$ are the pixel coordinates [S1, unofficial]. Map these planes into the surfel's local coordinate system via $T$ to yield vectors $\mathbf{k}$ and $\mathbf{l}$ [S1, unofficial]. Their intersection with the surfel's plane gives local coordinates $(u_{local}, v_{local})$ of the hit point [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Distance and Discard:** Compute squared distance from surfel center: $\rho^2 = u_{local}^2 + v_{local}^2$ [S1, unofficial]. If $\rho^2 \ge \delta_{cut}^2$, discard the surfel for this pixel [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Weight Evaluation:** Compute unnormalized Gaussian reconstruction kernel weight: $w = \exp(-\frac{1}{2} \rho^2)$ [S1, unofficial]. Note: The $1/(2\sigma)$ normalization constant is omitted as weights are used for Shepard normalization later [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Interval Merging (Surface Separation):** Maintain a list of active surface layers for the pixel. Each layer tracks a running sum of weights and attributes [S1, unofficial]. If the current surfel's depth interval $[d-\delta, d+\delta]$ overlaps with the depth interval of the last surfel added to the current active layer, add the surfel to that layer [S1, unofficial]. If it does not overlap, and since primitives are sorted by $d_{start}$, no future primitive can bridge the gap [S1, unofficial]. Start a new surface layer with the current surfel [S1, unofficial]. This exact gap detection requires no look-ahead or deferred merging [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Attribute Accumulation:** For each surfel added to a layer, accumulate its pre-shaded radiance $\mathbf{c}_{pre}$ and weight $w$ to the layer's running sums: $W_{layer} \leftarrow W_{layer} + w$ and $\mathbf{C}_{layer} \leftarrow \mathbf{C}_{layer} + w \cdot \mathbf{c}_{pre}$ [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Compositing:**
    After processing all surfels for a pixel, for each active surface layer $k$: Compute the reconstructed attribute (radiance) for the layer: $\hat{\mathbf{a}}_k = \frac{\sum_{j \in \text{layer } k} w_j \mathbf{c}_{pre, j}}{\sum_{j \in \text{layer } k} w_j}$ [S1, unofficial]. This is a Shepard-type normalization [S1, unofficial]. Compute the coverage-based opacity for the layer: $\alpha_k$ is derived directly from the accumulated EWA weight $W_k$ [S1, unofficial]. Composite layers front-to-back using the over operator: $\mathbf{C}_{out} = \sum_{k} \hat{\mathbf{a}}_k \alpha_k \prod_{j < k} (1 - \alpha_j)$ [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Anti-Aliasing:**
    Apply an object-space MIP filter [S1, unofficial]. This exploits the resampling structure of surface splatting [S1, unofficial]. Use a center-precomputed approximation to amortize the Jacobian evaluation, suppressing sampling aliasing [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def compute_t_matrix(c_prime, t_u_prime, t_v_prime, P):
    """Construct T-matrix T = P * S. S = [t_u_prime, t_v_prime, 0, c_prime] as columns."""
    S = np.zeros((4, 4))
    S[0:3, 0] = t_u_prime
    S[0:3, 1] = t_v_prime
    S[0:3, 3] = c_prime
    return P @ S

def ray_surfel_intersection(T, u, v):
    """Compute local coordinates (u_local, v_local) via plane intersection in local space."""
    T_inv = np.linalg.inv(T)
    h_u = np.array([-1.0, 0.0, 0.0, float(u)])
    h_v = np.array([0.0, -1.0, 0.0, float(v)])
    k = T_inv @ h_u
    l = T_inv @ h_v
    # Intersect planes k and l with z=0 plane: k[0]*x + k[1]*y + k[3] = 0
    det = k[0]*l[1] - k[1]*l[0]
    if abs(det) < 1e-6:
        return 0.0, 0.0
    x = (k[1]*l[3] - k[3]*l[1]) / det
    y = (k[3]*l[0] - k[0]*l[3]) / det
    return x, y

def transform_to_camera(c, t_u, t_v, view_matrix):
    """Apply view transformation to position and tangent vectors."""
    c_h = np.array([*c, 1.0])
    t_u_h = np.array([*t_u, 0.0])
    t_v_h = np.array([*t_v, 0.0])
    c_prime = (view_matrix @ c_h)[:3]
    t_u_prime = (view_matrix @ t_u_h)[:3]
    t_v_prime = (view_matrix @ t_v_h)[:3]
    return c_prime, t_u_prime, t_v_prime

def environment_light(direction, env_map):
    """Sample HDR environment map given a direction vector."""
    # Standard spherical coordinate mapping to env map texture
    theta = math.atan2(direction[0], direction[2])
    phi = math.asin(direction[1] / max(1e-6, math.sqrt(np.dot(direction, direction))))
    u = (theta / (2 * math.pi) + 0.5) % 1.0
    v = (phi / math.pi + 0.5)
    return env_map.sample(u, v)

def forward_shading(albedo, metallic, roughness, normal, env_map):
    """Compute pre-shaded linear radiance using split-sum IBL and microfacet BRDF."""
    # Simplified forward shading: diffuse + specular IBL
    diffuse = albedo * environment_light(-normal, env_map)
    specular = metallic * environment_light(2 * np.dot(normal, np.array([0,0,-1])) * normal - np.array([0,0,-1]), env_map)
    return diffuse + specular

def create_tiles(width, height, tile_size=16):
    """Generate tile bounds."""
    tiles = []
    for y in range(0, height, tile_size):
        for x in range(0, width, tile_size):
            tiles.append({'x_start': x, 'y_start': y, 'x_end': min(x + tile_size, width), 'y_end': min(y + tile_size, height)})
    return tiles

def intersects_tile(surfel, tile):
    """Check if surfel bounding box overlaps tile."""
    return (surfel.bbox_min[0] < tile['x_end'] and surfel.bbox_max[0] > tile['x_start'] and
            surfel.bbox_min[1] < tile['y_end'] and surfel.bbox_max[1] > tile['y_start'])

def rasterize_surfels(surfels, P, view_matrix, env_map, image_width, image_height, delta_cut):
    """Rasterize a set of surfels into an image."""
    image = np.zeros((image_height, image_width, 3))
    
    # 1. Pre-shading and T-matrix construction
    for surfel in surfels:
        c, t_u, t_v, a, m, r = surfel
        c_prime, t_u_prime, t_v_prime = transform_to_camera(c, t_u, t_v, view_matrix)
        surfel.c_prime = c_prime
        surfel.t_u_prime = t_u_prime
        surfel.t_v_prime = t_v_prime
        surfel.T = compute_t_matrix(c_prime, t_u_prime, t_v_prime, P)
        
        t_u_z = t_u_prime[2]
        t_v_z = t_v_prime[2]
        surfel.delta = math.sqrt(t_u_z**2 + t_v_z**2) * delta_cut
        surfel.d = c_prime[2]
        surfel.d_start = surfel.d - surfel.delta
        
        # Compute implicit normal in camera space
        n_prime = np.cross(t_u_prime, t_v_prime)
        n_prime = n_prime / np.linalg.norm(n_prime)
        surfel.n_prime = n_prime
        
        surfel.c_pre = forward_shading(a, m, r, n_prime, env_map)
        
    # 2. Tiling and Sorting
    tiles = create_tiles(image_width, image_height)
    for tile in tiles:
        tile_surfels = [s for s in surfels if intersects_tile(s, tile)]
        tile_surfels.sort(key=lambda s: s.d_start)
        tile.surfels = tile_surfels
        
    # 3. Per-Pixel Rasterization
    for tile in tiles:
        for y in range(tile['y_start'], tile['y_end']):
            for x in range(tile['x_start'], tile['x_end']):
                layers = []
                
                for surfel in tile.surfels:
                    u_local, v_local = ray_surfel_intersection(surfel.T, x, y)
                    rho_sq = u_local**2 + v_local**2
                    
                    if rho_sq >= delta_cut**2:
                        continue
                    
                    w = math.exp(-0.5 * rho_sq)
                    d_start = surfel.d - surfel.delta
                    d_end = surfel.d + surfel.delta
                    
                    added_to_layer = False
                    for layer in layers:
                        if d_start <= layer['max_d_end']:
                            layer['W'] += w
                            layer['C'] += w * surfel.c_pre
                            layer['max_d_end'] = max(layer['max_d_end'], d_end)
                            added_to_layer = True
                            break
                    
                    if not added_to_layer:
                        layers.append({'W': w, 'C': w * surfel.c_pre, 'max_d_end': d_end})
                
                # 4. Compositing
                alpha_accum = 0.0
                final_color = np.zeros(3)
                
                for layer in layers:
                    a_hat = layer['C'] / layer['W'] if layer['W'] > 1e-6 else np.zeros(3)
                    alpha = 1.0 - math.exp(-layer['W'])
                    final_color += a_hat * alpha * (1.0 - alpha_accum)
                    alpha_accum += alpha * (1.0 - alpha_accum)
                
                image[y, x] = final_color
                
    return image
```

## Subtopics

### [3D Surface Splatting Rasterization](4751-3d-surface-splatting-rasterization.md)
The rasterization core of 3DSS, designed to render an unstructured set of oriented surfel primitives into a 2D image while correctly handling self-occlusion at concavities. It solves the problem where single-layer rasterization approaches fail to distinguish between geometrically distinct surface sheets that project onto the same pixel, resulting in holes or translucent artifacts in high-curvature regions like ears or under chins. It sets itself apart from classical A-buffer approaches by restricting summation to surfels within the same depth-interval layer rather than gathering across all depths, and from single-layer ternary depth tests by detecting gaps in the depth-interval chain to support an arbitrary number of surface layers per pixel.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of surfels and pixels, with overhead from sorting and interval merging [S1, unofficial]. The interval merging is efficient as it requires no look-ahead [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of surfels and pixels for storing intermediate layer data [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Handles multiple surface layers per pixel correctly; differentiable visibility; anti-aliased silhouettes; physically-based shading; compatible with mesh reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires pre-shading; interval merging logic is more complex than simple depth testing [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Physically-based inverse rendering where geometry, materials, and illumination need to be recovered jointly, especially for scenes with complex occlusions and multiple surface layers [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When a volumetric representation is preferred or when real-time performance is critical and the overhead of interval merging is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].

## Background
3D Surface Splatting (3DSS) was introduced by Mae Younes in 2026 [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the foundations of Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) surface splatting [Zwicker et al. 2001] and addresses the limitations of prior differentiable surface splatting methods like Differentiable Surface Splatting (DSS) [Yifan et al. 2019] by providing a more robust multi-layer surface separation and a surface rendering model compatible with physically-based inverse rendering [S1, unofficial]. It bridges the gap between point-based inverse rendering and mesh-based workflows by producing an optimized surfel representation that is natively compatible with Screened Poisson Surface Reconstruction [Kazhdan and Hoppe 2013] [S1, unofficial].
