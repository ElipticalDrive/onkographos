# SNS Backward Pass with Intermediate Variable Q

Also called: SNS Gradient Computation, Skew-Normal 2D Gradient Computation

## Overview
The SNS Backward Pass with Intermediate Variable Q computes the gradients of the loss with respect to the 3D Gaussian parameters (mean, skew, rotation, scale, color, opacity) for Skew-Normal Splatting [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of high computational complexity and intricate coupling between rotation $R$ and scale $S$ in standard 3DGS gradient computation by introducing an intermediate variable $Q = ARS$ [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3DGS, which computes gradients w.r.t. 2D image-space variables first, SNS directly evaluates shared auxiliary algebraic terms during the rendering stage to enable efficient closed-form gradient evaluation for 3D parameters in the preprocessing stage [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Loss gradient w.r.t. rendered color $\frac{\partial L}{\partial C}$, rendered color $C$, opacity $a$, 2D Skew-Normal density $SN^{2D}$, pixel coordinate $u$, 2D mean $\mu^{2D}$, intermediate variable $Q$, skew parameter $k$, 2D covariance matrix $\Omega^{2D}$, and the Gaussian component $G_2$.
*   **Outputs:** Gradients $\frac{\partial L}{\partial c}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial o}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial SN^{2D}}$, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial k}$, and $\frac{\partial L}{\partial Q}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$: Intermediate variable matrix.
*   $k \in \mathbb{R}^2$: Skew parameter vector.
*   $\mu^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$: 2D mean vector.
*   $\Omega^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$: 2D covariance matrix.
*   $\Delta = u - \mu^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$: Displacement vector.
*   $m = m^{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^2$: Normalized skew vector.
*   $D \in \mathbb{R}$: Scalar denominator.
*   Auxiliary variables: $c_\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^2$, $M_\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$, $s_\Delta \in \mathbb{R}$, $p_\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^2$ [S1, unofficial].

**Step 1: Compute Intermediate Variable Q and Derived Quantities**
Compute $Q$ from the 3D rotation $R$ and scale $S$ matrices and the projection matrix $A$:
$$ Q = A R S $$
Compute the normalized skew vector $m$:
$$ m = \frac{(Q Q^\top)^{-1} Q k}{\sqrt{1 + k^\top k - k^\top Q^\top (Q Q^\top)^{-1} Q k}} $$
Compute the scalar denominator $D$:
$$ D = \sqrt{1 + k^\top k - k^\top Q^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k} $$
Note: $\Omega^{2D}$ is related to $Q$ and $k$ via the Skew-Normal formulation, but for gradient computation, $(\Omega^{2D})^{-1}$ is treated as a known matrix derived from the forward pass state [S1, unofficial].

**Step 2: Compute Auxiliary Variables**
These variables are computed once per Gaussian per pixel to avoid redundant calculations [S1, unofficial]:
$$ c_\Delta = \Phi \cdot G_2 \cdot (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta $$
$$ M_\Delta = \Phi \cdot G_2 \cdot ((\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta) ((\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta)^\top $$
$$ s_\Delta = \frac{G_2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} (m^\top \Delta)^2 \right) $$
$$ p_\Delta = \frac{G_2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \left( -\frac{1}{2} (m^\top \Delta)^2 \right) \cdot (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} \Delta $$
where $\Phi$ is the skew-normal activation function and $G_2$ is the 2D Gaussian component [S1, unofficial].

**Step 3: Compute Gradients for 2D Image-Space Variables**
Compute the gradient of $SN^{2D}$ with respect to $\mu^{2D}$:
$$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial \mu^{2D}} = c_\Delta - s_\Delta \cdot m $$
Compute the gradient of $SN^{2D}$ with respect to $k$:
$$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial k} = \frac{1}{D} Q^\top (p_\Delta + (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m) - \frac{1}{D^2} (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot k $$
Compute the gradient of $SN^{2D}$ with respect to $Q$:
$$ \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial Q} = (M_\Delta - p_\Delta m^\top - m p_\Delta^\top - (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m m^\top) Q + \frac{1}{D} (p_\Delta + (p_\Delta^\top \Omega^{2D} m) \cdot m) k^\top $$

**Step 4: Chain Rule to 3D Parameters**
The gradients flow from the loss $L$ to the rendered color $C$, then to opacity $a$ and color $c$, and finally to the 3D parameters [S1, unofficial].

Gradient w.r.t. color $c$:
$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial c} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot \frac{\partial C}{\partial c} $$
Gradient w.r.t. opacity $o$:
$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial o} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot \frac{\partial C}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial o} $$
Gradient w.r.t. mean $\mu$:
$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mu} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot \frac{\partial C}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial \mu^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial \mu^{2D}}{\partial \mu} $$
Gradient w.r.t. skew $k$:
$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial k} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot \frac{\partial C}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial k} $$
Gradient w.r.t. $Q$ (which encapsulates $R$ and $S$):
$$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial Q} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial C} \cdot \frac{\partial C}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial SN^{2D}} \cdot \frac{\partial SN^{2D}}{\partial Q} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_sns_gradients(dL_dC, C, a, SN2D, u, mu_2d, Q, k, Omega_2D, G2, Phi):
    """
    Computes gradients for SNS backward pass.
    dL_dC: Gradient of loss w.r.t. rendered color.
    C: Rendered color.
    a: Opacity.
    SN2D: Skew-Normal density value.
    u: Pixel coordinate.
    mu_2d: 2D mean.
    Q: Intermediate variable matrix (2x2).
    k: Skew parameter vector (2).
    Omega_2D: 2D covariance matrix (2x2).
    G2: 2D Gaussian component value.
    Phi: Skew-normal activation value.
    """
    # Step 1: Derived quantities
    Delta = u - mu_2d
    # Compute m
    # Note: (Q Q^T)^-1 Q k requires matrix inversion/multiplication
    QQ_T_inv_Q_k = solve(Q @ Q.T, Q @ k)
    denom_m = sqrt(1 + k.T @ k - k.T @ Q.T @ solve(Q @ Q.T, Q @ k))
    m = QQ_T_inv_Q_k / denom_m
    
    # Compute D
    # Note: Omega_2D_inv_Q_k requires matrix inversion/multiplication
    Omega_2D_inv_Q_k = solve(Omega_2D, Q @ k)
    denom_D = sqrt(1 + k.T @ k - k.T @ Q.T @ Omega_2D_inv_Q_k)
    D = denom_D
    
    # Step 2: Auxiliary variables
    Omega_2D_inv_Delta = solve(Omega_2D, Delta)
    
    c_Delta = Phi * G2 * Omega_2D_inv_Delta
    
    M_Delta = Phi * G2 * outer(Omega_2D_inv_Delta, Omega_2D_inv_Delta)
    
    exp_term = exp(-0.5 * (m.T @ Delta)**2)
    s_Delta = (G2 / sqrt(2 * pi)) * exp_term
    
    p_Delta = (G2 / sqrt(2 * pi)) * exp_term * Omega_2D_inv_Delta
    
    # Step 3: Gradients of SN2D
    # dSN2D_d_mu_2d
    dSN2D_d_mu_2d = c_Delta - s_Delta * m
    
    # dSN2D_d_k
    term1_k = (p_Delta.T @ Omega_2D @ m) * m
    dSN2D_d_k = (1/D) * (Q.T @ (p_Delta + term1_k)) - (1/D**2) * (p_Delta.T @ Omega_2D @ m) * k
    
    # dSN2D_d_Q
    term1_Q = M_Delta - outer(p_Delta, m) - outer(m, p_Delta) - (p_Delta.T @ Omega_2D @ m) * outer(m, m)
    term2_Q = (1/D) * (p_Delta + term1_k)
    dSN2D_d_Q = term1_Q @ Q + outer(term2_Q, k.T) # Note: k^T is row vector, term2_Q is col vector? 
    # Correction based on formula: term2_Q is a vector, k^T is a row vector. 
    # Formula: (1/D)(p_Delta + ...) k^T. This is outer product if term2_Q is col.
    # Let's assume standard matrix mult: term2_Q is 2x1, k^T is 1x2. Result 2x2.
    dSN2D_d_Q = term1_Q @ Q + outer(term2_Q, k) # outer(v, w) = v * w.T
    
    # Step 4: Chain rule to 3D parameters
    # dL/dc
    dL_dc = dL_dC # Assuming dL_dC is already scaled by dC/dc if c is direct param
    
    # dL/do
    # dL/da = dL/dC * dC/da (from alpha compositing)
    dL_da = dL_dC * SN2D # Simplified, actual dC/da depends on transmittance
    dL_do = dL_da * SN2D # da/do = SN2D
    
    # dL/dmu
    # dL/dSN2D = dL/da * a / SN2D (since a = o * SN2D)
    dL_dSN2D = dL_da * a / SN2D
    dL_dmu_2d = dL_dSN2D * dSN2D_d_mu_2d
    # dL/dmu = dL/dmu_2d * dmu_2d/dmu (projection Jacobian)
    dL_dmu = dL_dmu_2d @ dmu_2d_dmu # dmu_2d_dmu is projection Jacobian
    
    # dL/dk
    dL_dk = dL_dSN2D * dSN2D_d_k
    
    # dL/dQ
    dL_dQ = dL_dSN2D * dSN2D_d_Q
    
    return dL_dc, dL_do, dL_dmu, dL_dk, dL_dQ
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The computation of $D$ involves square roots and subtractions, which may lead to numerical instability if $k^\top Q^\top (\Omega^{2D})^{-1} Q k$ is close to $1 + k^\top k$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Matrix Inversion:** Inverting $Q Q^\top$ and $\Omega^{2D}$ can be unstable if these matrices are ill-conditioned. Regularization or pseudo-inversion may be required [G].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If $G_2$ is very small, gradients may vanish. If $SN^{2D}$ is zero, division by $SN^{2D}$ in the chain rule step is undefined [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The auxiliary variable computation is $O(1)$ per Gaussian per pixel. The gradient computations are also $O(1)$ per Gaussian per pixel, dominated by matrix-vector multiplications. This is comparable to standard 3DGS but avoids the complex coupling derivatives of $R$ and $S$ directly [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing auxiliary variables $c_\Delta, M_\Delta, s_\Delta, p_\Delta$ per Gaussian per pixel during the rendering pass, increasing memory usage slightly compared to standard 3DGS [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simplifies the gradient computation for skew parameters and decouples rotation and scale through $Q$, leading to more efficient and stable optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of matrix inversions and square roots. The introduction of $Q$ adds an extra layer of abstraction that must be correctly implemented [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The SNS Backward Pass with Intermediate Variable Q was introduced in the "3D Skew-Normal Splatting" paper [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS gradient computation when dealing with skew-normal distributions, which have more complex parameter dependencies. By introducing $Q = ARS$, the method simplifies the differentiation of the Skew-Normal density function [S1, unofficial].
