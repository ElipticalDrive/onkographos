# Representation and Kernel Modifications

Also called: Kernel Variants, Primitive Representation Extensions

## Overview
Representation and Kernel Modifications extend the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) primitive definition and rendering kernel to address specific limitations in shape modeling, geometric consistency, memory efficiency, and optical effects. While standard 3DGS relies on positive-only, isotropic/anisotropic Gaussian blobs with exponential decay, this family introduces negative color contributions [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md], voxel-based scaffolds for attribute prediction [9186-scaffold-gs.md], tangent-plane densification for geometric smoothness [9187-geogaussian.md], non-exponential decaying kernels [9911-darbf-splatting.md], and spatially-varying blur kernels [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md]. These methods solve issues such as the inability to model complex nonlinear structures (e.g., donuts, thin spokes) [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md, S2], inefficient memory usage in sparse regions [9186-scaffold-gs.md], geometric artifacts during densification [9187-geogaussian.md], and the lack of closed-form integration for non-Gaussian kernels [9911-darbf-splatting.md].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family retain the core differentiable rasterization pipeline of 3DGS but modify one or more of the following components:
1.  **Primitive Attributes:** The tuple defining a Gaussian primitive is extended or reinterpreted. Standard attributes include position $\mathbf{p}$, covariance $\Sigma$, opacity $\alpha$, and color $\mathbf{c}$ (often via Spherical Harmonics). Modifications include allowing negative color values [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md], decoupling attributes from direct storage by mapping voxel features to attributes via neural networks [9186-scaffold-gs.md], or adding learnable oriented normals [S2].
2.  **Kernel Function:** The function $K(\mathbf{x})$ used to weight the contribution of a primitive at a pixel $\mathbf{x}$ is replaced or augmented. Standard 3DGS uses the Gaussian PDF. Alternatives include Decaying Anisotropic Radial Basis Functions (DARBFs) like Half-Cosine or Inverse Multiquadric [9911-darbf-splatting.md], or spatially-varying blur kernels selected from a grid based on sharpness priors [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md].
3.  **Densification and Initialization:** The strategy for adding new primitives is modified to respect geometric constraints (e.g., tangent plane projection [9187-geogaussian.md]) or structural priors (e.g., voxel grid scaffolding [9186-scaffold-gs.md]).
4.  **Integration Shortcut:** Standard 3DGS relies on the closed-form integral of the Gaussian projection. Methods using non-Gaussian kernels [9911-darbf-splatting.md] or oriented surface assumptions [S2] must derive or approximate new integration shortcuts or correction factors to maintain rasterization efficiency.

## Subtopics

### [Negative Gaussian Splatting](9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md)
A variant that extends the primitive representation by incorporating "negative Gaussians"—ellipsoids with negative color values—into the optimization process [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md]. It solves the limitation of standard Gaussian Splatting, which relies on positive-only components to model complex nonlinear structures and high-frequency elements, often requiring a large number of primitives and increasing time complexity [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md]. By allowing positive and negative Gaussians to cancel each other out, NegGS provides greater flexibility in shape representation and color modulation, particularly for rapid color transitions and shadows [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md]. It interprets the interaction between two Gaussians as a "Diff-Gaussian" distribution, defined by the ratio of their probability density functions (PDFs) [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md]. Choose this method when rendering scenes with complex internal structures, thin filaments, or high-frequency color variations that standard positive-only Gaussians struggle to represent efficiently. See [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md].

### [Scaffold-GS](9186-scaffold-gs.md)
A representation modification that replaces the direct parameterization of Gaussian attributes with a voxel-based neural field [9186-scaffold-gs.md]. It solves the issue of inefficient memory usage and poor initialization in sparse regions by decoupling the geometric scaffold (voxels) from the radiance attributes (Gaussians) [9186-scaffold-gs.md]. Unlike standard 3DGS which stores attributes directly per Gaussian, Scaffold-GS determines all Gaussian attributes via interpolated features from a voxel grid and lightweight neural networks, enabling a more compact and structured representation [9186-scaffold-gs.md]. Choose this method when memory efficiency is critical or when the scene has large empty regions where standard densification would waste resources. See [9186-scaffold-gs.md].

### [GeoGaussian](9187-geogaussian.md)
A training optimization and initialization strategy that improves geometric consistency by densifying primitives on the tangent plane of existing Gaussians and enforcing smoothness of geometric properties between neighboring Gaussians [9187-geogaussian.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Negative Gaussian Splatting or Scaffold-GS by focusing specifically on the spatial distribution and geometric coherence of the Gaussian primitives during the densification phase, rather than modifying the kernel function or using scaffold structures [9187-geogaussian.md]. Choose this method when high-fidelity surface reconstruction and geometric smoothness are prioritized over raw photometric accuracy. See [9187-geogaussian.md].

### [DARBF-Splatting](9911-darbf-splatting.md)
A method for differentiable volume rendering that replaces the standard Gaussian reconstruction kernels of 3D Gaussian Splatting with a broader class of non-exponential functions called Decaying Anisotropic Radial Basis Functions (DARBFs) [9911-darbf-splatting.md]. It solves the limitation of splatting methods being restricted to the exponential family by enabling the use of kernels such as half-cosine and inverse multiquadric, which offer faster training convergence and lower memory usage while maintaining comparable visual quality [9911-darbf-splatting.md]. Unlike its siblings, it introduces a scalar correction factor to approximate the closed-form integration shortcut inherent to Gaussians, allowing efficient rasterization of these alternative kernels [9911-darbf-splatting.md]. Choose this method when faster convergence or specific kernel shapes (e.g., finite support) are desired. See [9911-darbf-splatting.md].

### [Grid-based Blur Kernel](14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md)
A method that replaces MLP-generated blur kernels with a learnable, spatially-indexed grid to model spatially-varying blurriness in radiance field optimization [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md]. Unlike siblings that modify primitive geometry or negative contributions, this method explicitly handles defocus blur by assigning one of $N_k$ trainable kernels to pixels based on a sharpness prior, improving reconstruction quality in defocused regions without increasing kernel generation cost [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md]. Choose this method when reconstructing scenes with significant depth-of-field effects or defocus blur. See [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md].

### Gaussian Shell Maps
Combines CNN-based generators with 3DGS to recreate virtual humans with sophisticated details such as clothing and accessories [S1].

### Gaussian Wrapping
A framework that reinterprets 3D Gaussian primitives as oriented probabilistic surface elements representing geometry only within an oriented half-space, allowing derivation of closed-form occupancy, vacancy, and normal fields for watertight mesh extraction [S2]. Model surface points with asymmetric primitives where the Gaussian models density decay on the outward-facing side, while the inward-facing side is considered fully occupied [S2]. Identify geometric gaps where the oriented surface assumption is violated and densify the representation to obtain a watertight shell of oriented primitives [S2]. Introduce a learnable oriented normal at each Gaussian element and define an adapted attenuation formulation to enable closed-form expressions for normal and occupancy fields [S2]. Introduce a novel consistency loss and a dedicated densification strategy to enforce Gaussians to wrap the entire surface by closing geometric holes [S2]. Modify the differentiable rasterizer to output depth as an isosurface of the continuous model [S2]. Use Primal Adaptive Meshing for Region-of-Interest meshing at arbitrary resolution [S2].

## Background
The standard 3D Gaussian Splatting method [Parent] established a baseline for fast, high-quality novel view synthesis using explicit Gaussian primitives. However, limitations in representation (positive-only colors, symmetric blobs) and kernel properties (exponential decay) motivated extensions. Negative Gaussian Splatting [9152-negative-gaussian-splatting.md] and DARBF-Splatting [9911-darbf-splatting.md] address the kernel and color representation limits. Scaffold-GS [9186-scaffold-gs.md] and GeoGaussian [9187-geogaussian.md] address geometric and memory efficiency. Gaussian Wrapping [S2] adapts the primitives for surface reconstruction. Grid-based Blur Kernels [14244-grid-based-blur-kernel.md] address optical defocus. Gaussian Shell Maps [S1] and Gaussian Wrapping [S2] are specifically noted for human/avatar and surface reconstruction applications.
