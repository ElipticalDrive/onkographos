# GeoGaussian


## Overview
GeoGaussian is a training optimization and initialization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that improves geometric consistency by densifying primitives on the tangent plane of existing Gaussians and enforcing smoothness of geometric properties between neighboring Gaussians [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Negative Gaussian Splatting or Scaffold-GS by focusing specifically on the spatial distribution and geometric coherence of the Gaussian primitives during the densification phase, rather than modifying the kernel function or using scaffold structures [S1].

## How it works
GeoGaussian modifies the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting densification process to ensure that new Gaussians are placed in geometrically consistent locations and that their geometric attributes (such as normals) vary smoothly across the surface.

**Inputs:**
- A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{G_i\}$, where each $G_i$ is defined by mean $\mu_i$, covariance $\Sigma_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonics coefficients.
- A set of input images with known camera poses.
- A differentiable rasterizer.

**Outputs:**
- An optimized set of 3D Gaussians with improved geometric structure.

**Data Structures:**
- The standard 3DGS representation is retained. The key modification is in the selection criteria for splitting/cloning Gaussians.

**Steps:**
1. **Gradient Accumulation:** Render images and compute the loss $\mathcal{L}$ (e.g., L1 + D-SSIM). Accumulate gradients for the Gaussian parameters.
2. **Tangent Plane Densification:** Identify Gaussians that require densification (e.g., high gradient magnitude, small projected area). Instead of splitting along arbitrary axes, GeoGaussian projects the split operation onto the tangent plane of the parent Gaussian [S1].
   - Let $n_i$ be the normal of Gaussian $G_i$. The tangent plane is defined by vectors orthogonal to $n_i$.
   - New Gaussians are spawned by perturbing $\mu_i$ within this tangent plane.
3. **Geometric Smoothness Regularization:** Encourage smoothness of geometric properties between neighboring Gaussians [S1].
   - Define a neighbor set $\mathcal{N}_i$ for each Gaussian $G_i$ based on spatial proximity or overlap in the image plane.
   - Compute a regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{smooth}$ that penalizes differences in geometric attributes (e.g., normals or covariance orientations) between $G_i$ and $G_j \in \mathcal{N}_i$.
   - The total loss becomes $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{render} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{smooth}$, where $\lambda$ is a weighting factor.
4. **Optimization:** Update Gaussian parameters using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{total}$.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Pseudocode for GeoGaussian densification step
def geogaussian_densify_step(Gaussians, Gradients, CameraPoses, Images):
    # 1. Identify Gaussians to densify based on standard criteria (e.g., gradient magnitude)
    candidates = [G for G in Gaussians if G.gradient_magnitude > threshold]
    
    new_gaussians = []
    for G in candidates:
        # 2. Densify on the tangent plane
        # Estimate normal n from the covariance matrix or existing geometry
        n = estimate_normal(G.covariance)
        
        # Generate split directions within the tangent plane (orthogonal to n)
        # This ensures new Gaussians lie on the local surface approximation
        tangent_vectors = get_tangent_basis(n)
        
        # Create child Gaussians by perturbing mean along tangent vectors
        for direction in tangent_vectors:
            child_G = copy(G)
            child_G.mean += direction * scale_factor
            child_G.opacity = G.opacity / 2
            new_gaussians.append(child_G)
            
    # 3. Add geometric smoothness regularization to the loss
    # This is typically done during the backward pass, not as a separate forward step
    # L_smooth = sum(||Normal(G_i) - Normal(G_j)||^2) for neighbors i,j
    smoothness_loss = compute_smoothness_loss(Gaussians + new_gaussians)
    
    return new_gaussians, smoothness_loss
```

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda$: Weight for the smoothness regularization. Not given in the sources.
- Threshold: Gradient magnitude threshold for densification. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases:**
- **Degenerate Covariance:** If a Gaussian's covariance is near-singular, normal estimation may be unstable. [G] A small regularization term can be added to the covariance matrix to ensure invertibility.
- **Boundary Conditions:** At scene boundaries, neighbor sets may be sparse, reducing the effectiveness of the smoothness term.

## Subtopics

### [Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking](9773-spatio-temporal-gaussian-tracking.md)
Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking (SGT) is a method for modeling surface dynamics in dynamic scenes by tracking the attribute shift of each Gaussian at time $t$ using a deformation field $D(\mu, t)$, allowing the rendering of deformed Gaussians $G_t$. It sets itself apart from static representations by explicitly parameterizing temporal deformation through a lightweight, decomposed encoding voxel and a set of small MLPs, prioritizing real-time inference speed over large neural network approximations.

### [Progressive training by scales of Gaussians](9871-progressive-training-by-scales-of-gaussians.md)
This method prevents rendering artifacts caused by 3D Gaussians becoming smaller than a single pixel during optimization by enforcing a minimum projected area through covariance regularization. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing a scale-dependent low-pass filter that focuses training on low-frequency details in early stages, ensuring coarse scene structure is learned efficiently before fine details are resolved.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The addition of smoothness regularization increases the computational cost per iteration due to neighbor lookups and normal comparisons. Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard 3DGS, as it does not introduce new large data structures. [G] Neighbor lists may require additional memory if precomputed.
- **Strengths:** Improves geometric consistency and reduces noise in reconstructed surfaces.
- **Limitations:** May slow down convergence due to the additional regularization term. Performance depends on the choice of $\lambda$.
- **When to use:** When geometric accuracy and surface smoothness are critical, such as in architectural reconstruction or detailed object scanning.
- **When not to use:** When real-time training speed is the primary constraint and geometric smoothness is less important than appearance fidelity.

## Background
GeoGaussian was proposed as part of the recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting to address issues with geometric consistency. It is cited alongside other methods like GaussianPro, RadSplat, and Spec-Gaussian in surveys of 3DGS improvements [S1]. The method builds on the idea that standard densification can lead to geometrically inconsistent distributions of Gaussians.
