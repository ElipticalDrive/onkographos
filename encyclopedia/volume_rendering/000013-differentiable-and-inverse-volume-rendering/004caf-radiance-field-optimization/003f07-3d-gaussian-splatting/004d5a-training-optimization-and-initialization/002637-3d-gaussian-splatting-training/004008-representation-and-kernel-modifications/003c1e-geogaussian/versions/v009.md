# GeoGaussian


## Overview
GeoGaussian is a training optimization and initialization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that improves geometric fidelity by modifying the densification process. It addresses the issue of noisy or inconsistent geometry in standard 3DGS by densifying new Gaussians on the tangent plane of existing ones and enforcing smoothness of geometric properties between neighboring Gaussians [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like Scaffold-GS, which uses a scaffold structure for initialization, by focusing on local tangent-space propagation and normal consistency during the growth phase [S1].

## How it works
GeoGaussian modifies the standard 3DGS densification pipeline to ensure that new primitives align with the local surface geometry and maintain smooth geometric transitions.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Current set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{G_i\}$, where each $G_i$ has mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color coefficients.
- Output: Updated set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ with densified primitives.

**Data Structures:**
- Gaussian $G_i$:
  - $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
  - $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix, decomposed into rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{S}_i$ such that $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^T \mathbf{R}_i^T$.
  - $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
  - $\mathbf{c}_i$: Color representation (e.g., SH coefficients).

**Steps:**
1. **Identify Densification Candidates:** Select Gaussians $G_i$ that require densification based on standard criteria (e.g., high gradient magnitude in image space or low opacity) [S1].
2. **Compute Tangent Plane:** For each candidate Gaussian $G_i$, compute the local surface normal $\mathbf{n}_i$. The normal is derived from the covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$. The eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ defines the normal direction $\mathbf{n}_i$ [S1]. The tangent plane is defined as the plane passing through $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ with normal $\mathbf{n}_i$.
3. **Densify on Tangent Plane:** Generate new Gaussians $G_{new}$ by sampling positions on the tangent plane of $G_i$. The positions $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{new}$ are perturbed from $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ within the tangent plane:
   $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{new} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_i + \mathbf{T} \mathbf{u} $$
   where $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 2}$ is a basis matrix for the tangent plane (columns are orthonormal vectors orthogonal to $\mathbf{n}_i$), and $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is a random vector sampled from a distribution (e.g., Gaussian or uniform) scaled by a densification radius [S1].
4. **Initialize New Gaussians:** Initialize the covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{new}$ of the new Gaussians to match the local geometry. A common approach is to set the scale along the normal direction to be small and the scales in the tangent plane to match the parent or a fixed value, ensuring the new Gaussian lies flat on the surface [S1].
5. **Enforce Geometric Smoothness:** During optimization, apply a regularization term to encourage smoothness of geometric properties (such as normals or positions) between neighboring Gaussians. This can be implemented as a loss term:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{smooth} = \sum_{i,j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \| \mathbf{n}_i - \mathbf{n}_j \|^2 $$
   where $\mathcal{N}(i)$ is the set of neighbors of Gaussian $i$ [S1]. This ensures that the geometric properties vary smoothly across the surface.

**Parameters:**
- Densification radius: Controls the spread of new Gaussians on the tangent plane. Not given in the sources.
- Smoothness weight: Weight for the geometric smoothness loss. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Degenerate Covariance:** If the covariance matrix is ill-conditioned, the normal estimation may be unstable. [G] Add a small regularization term to the covariance matrix to ensure numerical stability.
- **Boundary Effects:** Near object boundaries, the tangent plane assumption may break down. [G] Consider using a confidence measure based on the covariance eigenvalues to weight the smoothness term.

## Subtopics

### [Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking](9773-spatio-temporal-gaussian-tracking.md)
Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking (SGT) is a method for modeling surface dynamics in dynamic scenes by tracking the attribute shift of each Gaussian at time $t$ using a deformation field $D(\mu, t)$, allowing the rendering of deformed Gaussians $G_t$. It sets itself apart from static representations by explicitly parameterizing temporal deformation through a lightweight, decomposed encoding voxel and a set of small MLPs, prioritizing real-time inference speed over large neural network approximations.

### [Progressive training by scales of Gaussians](9871-progressive-training-by-scales-of-gaussians.md)
This method prevents rendering artifacts caused by 3D Gaussians becoming smaller than a single pixel during optimization by enforcing a minimum projected area through covariance regularization. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing a scale-dependent low-pass filter that focuses training on low-frequency details in early stages, ensuring coarse scene structure is learned efficiently before fine details are resolved.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Densification adds overhead to the training loop due to tangent plane computation and neighbor search. The smoothness loss requires computing normals for all Gaussians and their neighbors. Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard 3DGS, as it uses the same primitive attributes. Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Improves geometric fidelity and reduces noise in reconstructed surfaces. Ensures smooth transitions between neighboring Gaussians.
- **Limitations:** May require careful tuning of the smoothness weight and densification radius. Computationally more expensive than standard 3DGS due to normal computation and regularization.
- **When to use:** When high-quality geometric reconstruction is required, such as for downstream tasks like mesh extraction or texture mapping.
- **When not to use:** When real-time rendering speed is the primary concern and geometric fidelity is less critical.

## Background
GeoGaussian was proposed in the context of recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting to address the limitations of standard densification strategies that can lead to noisy or inconsistent geometry [S1]. It builds on the idea of using local surface information to guide the placement of new Gaussians, similar to other methods like GaussianPro which considers normal consistency between neighboring views [S1].
