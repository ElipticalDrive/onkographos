# GeoGaussian


## Overview
GeoGaussian is a training optimization and initialization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that improves geometric consistency by densifying primitives on the tangent plane of existing Gaussians and enforcing smoothness of geometric properties between neighbors [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Scaffold-GS, which focuses on sparse scaffolding, and Negative Gaussian Splatting, which handles transparency via negative opacity, by explicitly modeling local surface geometry through tangent-plane propagation and normal consistency constraints [S1].

## How it works
GeoGaussian modifies the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization pipeline [Parent] to address geometric artifacts and poor normal consistency. The method operates on the set of 3D Gaussians $G_i$, each defined by mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$, covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and color $\mathbf{c}_i$.

**Inputs:**
- Initial set of 3D Gaussians $G = \{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$ derived from a point cloud or previous optimization stage.
- Camera poses and images for training.

**Outputs:**
- Optimized set of 3D Gaussians with improved geometric smoothness and densification.

**Data Structures:**
- Each Gaussian $G_i$ retains the standard 3DGS attributes: $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (parameterized by rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ and scale $\mathbf{s}_i$), $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for $\mathbf{c}_i$.
- A neighbor graph or spatial hash grid is used to identify neighboring Gaussians for smoothness regularization.

**Steps:**
1. **Tangent Plane Densification:** When densification is triggered (e.g., based on gradient magnitude or screen-space error), new Gaussians are not spawned randomly in 3D space. Instead, they are generated on the tangent plane of an existing parent Gaussian $G_p$.
   - Compute the normal $\mathbf{n}_p$ of $G_p$ from its covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p$. The normal is the eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p$.
   - Construct an orthonormal basis $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\}$ for the tangent plane orthogonal to $\mathbf{n}_p$.
   - Sample new mean positions $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{new}$ as:
     $$ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{new} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_p + \delta_1 \mathbf{u}_1 + \delta_2 \mathbf{u}_2 $$
     where $\delta_1, \delta_2$ are small random offsets or deterministic samples within a local radius.
   - Initialize the covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{new}$ such that its normal aligns with $\mathbf{n}_p$, ensuring the new Gaussian lies on the local surface geometry.

2. **Geometric Smoothness Regularization:** To encourage smoothness of geometric properties between neighboring Gaussians, a regularization term is added to the loss function.
   - For each pair of neighboring Gaussians $(G_i, G_j)$, compute the difference in their normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ and $\mathbf{n}_j$.
   - The normal $\mathbf{n}_k$ for Gaussian $G_k$ is derived from the eigenvector of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k$ corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue.
   - The smoothness loss $\mathcal{L}_{smooth}$ is defined as:
     $$ \mathcal{L}_{smooth} = \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{N}} \| \mathbf{n}_i - \mathbf{n}_j \|^2 $$
     where $\mathcal{N}$ is the set of neighbor pairs. This term penalizes discontinuities in surface orientation.

3. **Optimization:** The total loss combines the photometric rendering loss $\mathcal{L}_{photo}$ (e.g., L1 or SSIM) and the geometric smoothness loss:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \lambda_{smooth} \mathcal{L}_{smooth} $$
   where $\lambda_{smooth}$ is a weighting hyperparameter. Gradients are backpropagated through the differentiable rasterization pipeline [Parent] to update $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i, \alpha_i, \mathbf{c}_i$.

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_{smooth}$: Weight for geometric smoothness. Not given in the sources.
- Tangent plane sampling radius: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Degenerate Covariance:** If $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$ is near-singular, the normal estimation becomes unstable. A small regularization term may be added to the covariance diagonal [G].
- **Neighbor Definition:** The definition of "neighboring" Gaussians (e.g., based on 3D distance or graph connectivity) affects the smoothness term. Spatial hashing is typically used for efficiency.

## Subtopics

### [Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking](9773-spatio-temporal-gaussian-tracking.md)
Spatio-temporal Gaussian Tracking (SGT) is a method for modeling surface dynamics in dynamic scenes by tracking the attribute shift of each Gaussian at time $t$ using a deformation field $D(\mu, t)$, allowing the rendering of deformed Gaussians $G_t$. It sets itself apart from static representations by explicitly parameterizing temporal deformation through a lightweight, decomposed encoding voxel and a set of small MLPs, prioritizing real-time inference speed over large neural network approximations.

### [Progressive training by scales of Gaussians](9871-progressive-training-by-scales-of-gaussians.md)
This method prevents rendering artifacts caused by 3D Gaussians becoming smaller than a single pixel during optimization by enforcing a minimum projected area through covariance regularization. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing a scale-dependent low-pass filter that focuses training on low-frequency details in early stages, ensuring coarse scene structure is learned efficiently before fine details are resolved.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Densification on the tangent plane adds overhead for normal computation and basis construction per densification step. Smoothness regularization requires neighbor lookups, which can be optimized with spatial hashing. Overall complexity remains comparable to standard 3DGS but with higher constant factors due to geometric computations.
- **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard 3DGS, as no new large data structures are introduced beyond neighbor lists for regularization.
- **Strengths:** Improves geometric consistency and normal estimation, reducing artifacts in regions with complex surface curvature.
- **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the smoothness weight $\lambda_{smooth}$. May slow down training due to neighbor computations.
- **When to use:** When geometric accuracy and smooth surface normals are critical, such as in architectural scenes or objects with smooth surfaces.
- **When not to use:** For scenes with high-frequency noise or where geometric smoothness is not a priority, the overhead may not be justified.

## Background
GeoGaussian was proposed in the paper "GeoGaussian" [41], as cited in [S1]. It is part of the broader effort to improve the geometric fidelity of 3D Gaussian Splatting, building on the differentiable rasterization framework established by the original 3DGS method [Parent]. The method addresses the issue of inconsistent normals and poor surface reconstruction in standard 3DGS by incorporating geometric constraints during training.
