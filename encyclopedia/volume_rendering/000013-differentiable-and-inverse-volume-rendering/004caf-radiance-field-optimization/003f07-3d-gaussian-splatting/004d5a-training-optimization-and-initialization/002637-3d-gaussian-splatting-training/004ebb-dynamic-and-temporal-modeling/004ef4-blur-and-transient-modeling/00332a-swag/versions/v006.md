# SWAG

Also called: Unsupervised Transient Gaussian Splatting, Unsupervised Transient Gaussians

## Overview
SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders [S1][S2, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of reconstructing static geometry in the presence of transient distractors (e.g., moving people, shadows) in casual photo captures, yielding state-of-the-art results on diverse scenes [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on explicit visibility maps or pre-trained feature spaces, SWAG achieves this through unsupervised transient Gaussian training [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
Inputs: A set of uncalibrated or calibrated images of a scene containing transient elements.
Outputs: A set of 3D Gaussians representing the static geometry and appearance.

Data Structures:
- Static Gaussians: Standard 3DGS parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients for color).
- Appearance Embeddings: A learned vector per Gaussian or per view, used to condition appearance.
- Transient Gaussians: A separate set of Gaussians trained to model transient artifacts.

Steps:
1. Initialize 3D Gaussians from the scene using standard 3DGS initialization [S1][S2, unofficial].
2. Learn appearance embeddings for the Gaussians. These embeddings condition the Gaussian's appearance, allowing it to adapt to varying lighting or transient effects [S1][S2, unofficial].
3. Train unsupervised transient Gaussians. These Gaussians are optimized to capture transient occluders and distractors, effectively ignoring them during the reconstruction of the static scene [S1][S2, unofficial].
4. Render the scene by compositing the static Gaussians and transient Gaussians. The transient Gaussians are trained to account for occlusions, allowing the static Gaussians to represent the underlying geometry more accurately [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
Time and memory complexity: Not given in the sources.
Measured performance: Yields state-of-the-art results on diverse photo scenes [S1][S2, unofficial].
Strengths: Robust to transient distractors in casual captures [S1][S2, unofficial].
Limitations: Not given in the sources.
When to use: When reconstructing static geometry from images containing transient elements like moving people or shadows.
When not: When the scene is entirely static and transient modeling is unnecessary.

## Background
Origin: SWAG is described in the context of recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting [S1][S2, unofficial].
History: It is mentioned alongside other methods like Wild-GS and SpotlessSplats as part of the evolution of handling transients in Gaussian Splatting [S1][S2, unofficial].
Usage: Used for high-quality view synthesis and reconstruction in the presence of transients [S1][S2, unofficial].
