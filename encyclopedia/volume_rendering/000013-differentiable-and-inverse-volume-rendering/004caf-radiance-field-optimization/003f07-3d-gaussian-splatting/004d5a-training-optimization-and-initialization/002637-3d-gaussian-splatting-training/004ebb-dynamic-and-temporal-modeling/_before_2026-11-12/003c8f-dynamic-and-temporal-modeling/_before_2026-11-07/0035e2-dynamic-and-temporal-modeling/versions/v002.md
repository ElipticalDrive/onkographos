# Dynamic and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic NeRF, Temporal NeRF, 4D NeRF

## Overview
Dynamic and Temporal Modeling extends static NeRF to reconstruct and render scenes that change over time, solving the problem of non-rigid motion and temporal variation in radiance fields [S1]. It differs from static siblings by treating time as an explicit variable or modeling temporal dependencies, whereas methods like [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) focus on spatial sampling efficiency within a fixed frame.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves augmenting the standard NeRF representation, which maps spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$, to include a temporal component $t$. This allows the radiance field to evolve, enabling the rendering of novel views at arbitrary timestamps. The optimization process must account for temporal consistency, often by conditioning the current state on previous states or by learning a continuous deformation field that maps dynamic observations to a canonical space [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md)
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). It differs from standard NeRF by parametrizing input training data on spherical wavefronts rather than along lines (rays). NeTF is distinguished by its use of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling, and a two-stage training strategy to address sampling bias.

### [Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF](7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md)
A dynamic NeRF training method that uses projected color statistics (mean and variance) of sampled points to guide optimization via implicit point correspondence, replacing temporal deformation models.

### [On-the-Fly Dynamic NeRF Training](7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md)
On-the-fly dynamic NeRF training is a method for reconstructing dynamic scenes by optimizing the radiance field concurrently with the acquisition of training frames, enabling real-time streaming of the event as it unfolds. Unlike standard dynamic NeRFs that require full video sequences for offline 4D reconstruction, this approach minimizes optimization iterations per frame to achieve interactive speeds. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md) and [Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF](7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md) by explicitly formulating the training process as a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) estimation problem, leveraging a first-order Markov assumption to maintain scalability over time.
#### D-NeRF
**Definition**: A method that treats time $t$ as an additional input for system rendering [S1].
**Step**: Controls both camera view and time variables for dynamic rendering [S1].

## Background
Dynamic NeRF methods emerged to address the limitations of static reconstruction in non-rigid scenes. Early approaches, such as D-NeRF, introduced time as an explicit input to the neural network, allowing the model to learn temporal variations in appearance and geometry [S1]. Subsequent work explored deformation fields to map dynamic observations to a canonical space, and more recent methods have focused on real-time streaming and handling complex temporal dependencies through probabilistic models like Hidden Markov Models [S1].
