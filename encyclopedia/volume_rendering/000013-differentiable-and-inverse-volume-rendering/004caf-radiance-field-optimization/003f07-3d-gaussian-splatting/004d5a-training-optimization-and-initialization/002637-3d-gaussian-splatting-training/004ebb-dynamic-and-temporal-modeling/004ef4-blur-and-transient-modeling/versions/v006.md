# Blur and Transient Modeling

Also called: Transient and Blur Handling in 3DGS

## Overview
This family addresses artifacts caused by dynamic elements and optical imperfections in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by explicitly modeling transient occluders and optical blur rather than relying solely on temporal deformation fields [S1]. It sets itself apart from [NeTF](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md) by using Gaussian primitives for transient filtering instead of neural fields, and from [SWAG](6875-swag.md) by focusing on blur modeling techniques [S1].

## Shared principles
The shared principles across all methods in this family are:

1.  **Explicit Artifact Modeling:** Unlike methods that assume a static, sharp scene, these approaches introduce additional parameters or structures to account for non-static or non-ideal capture conditions. This includes modeling transient objects as separate entities or incorporating blur kernels into the rendering pipeline [S1].
2.  **Joint Optimization:** Static scene parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) are optimized jointly with transient or blur-specific parameters. This ensures that the static geometry is not corrupted by dynamic elements or optical blurs [S1].
3.  **Photometric Consistency:** The primary loss function remains photometric (e.g., L1 or LPIPS), but it is applied to the rendered image that includes the effects of transients or blur, ensuring the final output matches the ground truth observations [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md)
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). Unlike [NeRF](52-name.md), which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its specific application to time-of-flight transient data and its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling.

### [SWAG](6875-swag.md)
SWAG is a method for unsupervised transient modeling in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that recovers static scene geometry and appearance by explicitly identifying and ignoring transient occluders. It sets itself apart from siblings like NeTF by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training dedicated transient Gaussians to filter out dynamic elements, rather than modeling transients as a separate neural field.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels on carefully curated datasets but suffers from artifacts such as aliasing and blur in real-world captures [S1]. Recent approaches explicitly incorporated blur modeling during training, employing techniques such as coarse-to-fine kernel optimization [193] and photometric bundle adjustment [325] to address this challenge [S1]. While approximations in GS contribute to efficiency, they also limit the accuracy of illumination estimation and the modeling of challenging objects [S1]. Most existing efforts focus on specific failure modes rather than providing a universal solution [S1].
