# Blur and Transient Modeling

Also called: Dynamic Scene Optimization for 3DGS, Handling Real-World Artifacts in Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Blur and Transient Modeling addresses the degradation of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) quality in real-world captures caused by motion blur, defocus blur, and transient occluders [S1]. Unlike static reconstruction methods, this family explicitly models temporal variations and optical imperfections, distinguishing itself from sibling methods like [D-NeRF](8660-d-nerf.md) which rely on neural deformation fields, and [SWAG](6875-swag.md) which uses appearance embeddings for dynamic scenes [S1].

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of recovering high-fidelity static geometry and appearance from dynamic or imperfect input data. While specific implementations vary, the core challenge addressed is the violation of the static, pinhole-camera assumption inherent in standard 3DGS [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md)
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md]. Unlike [NeRF](52-name.md), which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates [4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md]. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its specific application to time-of-flight transient data and its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling [4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md].

### SWAG
SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to handle dynamic scenes with transient occluders by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings [6875-swag.md]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Neural Transient Field (NeTF) by operating directly in the Gaussian primitive space rather than a neural implicit field, using unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders [6875-swag.md].

Techniques to explicitly incorporate blur modeling during training to address real-world capture issues like motion blur and defocus blur [S1].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting excels on carefully curated datasets but suffers from artifacts such as aliasing and blur in real-world captures [S1]. Recent approaches explicitly incorporated blur modeling during training to address these challenges [S1]. While approximations in GS contribute to efficiency, they limit the accuracy of illumination estimation and the modeling of challenging objects [S1]. Most existing efforts focus on specific failure modes rather than providing a universal solution [S1].
