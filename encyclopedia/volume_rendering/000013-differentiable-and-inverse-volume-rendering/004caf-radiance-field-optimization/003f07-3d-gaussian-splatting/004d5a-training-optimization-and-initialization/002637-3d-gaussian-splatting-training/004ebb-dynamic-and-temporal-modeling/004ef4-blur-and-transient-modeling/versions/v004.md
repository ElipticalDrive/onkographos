# Blur and Transient Modeling

Also called: Transient Gaussian Splatting, Neural Transient Field

## Overview
This family addresses temporal dynamics and optical artifacts in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and related radiance field representations. It solves the problem of reconstructing scenes with moving objects, transient occluders, and optical blurs (motion/defocus) that standard static 3DGS fails to capture [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [D-NeRF](8660-d-nerf.md) by operating directly in the Gaussian primitive space (e.g., SWAG) or using specialized neural transient fields for non-line-of-sight (NLOS) imaging (NeTF), rather than relying solely on continuous deformation fields for general dynamic scenes [S1].

## Shared principles
The parent [Dynamic and Temporal Modeling](9265-dynamic-and-temporal-modeling.md) provides the foundational mechanisms for time-dependent rendering. This family applies those principles to specific failure modes:

1.  **Temporal Parameterization:** Attributes such as position, rotation, opacity, and color are functions of time $t$ [S1, S2, S3].
2.  **Canonical Space Mapping:** Dynamic coordinates are warped to a canonical space via a deformation network or explicit time-conditioned embeddings [S1, S3].
3.  **Rendering with Dynamics:** Rendering integrates time-dependent attributes, either by warping Gaussians to the camera time or querying time-conditioned neural fields [S2, S4].
4.  **Loss Functions:** Optimization minimizes photometric loss, often augmented with temporal smoothness or rigid constraints [S1, S2].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md)
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). Unlike [NeRF](52-name.md), which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its specific application to time-of-flight transient data and its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling.

### [SWAG](6875-swag.md)
SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings to handle dynamic scenes with transient occluders. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like Neural Transient Field (NeTF) by operating directly in the Gaussian primitive space rather than a continuous neural field, using unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders.

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) excels on carefully curated datasets but suffers from artifacts in real-world captures due to blurs such as motion and defocus, as well as dynamic elements [S1]. While standard 3DGS approximations contribute to efficiency, they limit accuracy for challenging objects and illumination estimation [S1]. Recent approaches explicitly incorporated blur modeling during training to address these challenges [S1]. Additionally, specialized methods like NeTF were developed for NLOS imaging to handle transient light transport, while SWAG was introduced to handle transient occluders in Gaussian splatting without explicit segmentation masks [S1][S2, unofficial].
