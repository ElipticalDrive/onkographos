# Vertex-level Pruning in Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Mixed-Primitive Vertex Pruning

## Overview
Vertex-level pruning is a refinement mechanism for mixed-primitive 3D Gaussian Splatting that removes redundant vertices from Gaussian triangles and lines during training, converting them into lower-order primitives (ellipses or lines) to reduce storage and representation complexity [S1, unofficial]. It complements standard primitive-level opacity pruning by addressing geometric redundancy within individual splats, ensuring that primitives do not retain unnecessary vertices when their spatial extent collapses [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A set of mixed-primitive Gaussians, including Gaussian triangles (3 vertices) and Gaussian lines (2 vertices), along with their current vertex positions $\mu$.
- **Output:** The same set of primitives, with some vertices removed and primitives converted to simpler forms (Gaussian ellipses or lines).

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the vertex coordinates $\mu$ of the primitives. A Gaussian triangle is defined by three vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$. A Gaussian line is defined by two vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2$. A Gaussian ellipse is defined by a single center vertex $\mu_1$ and a covariance matrix.

**Parameters:**
- $\omega_{dist}$: The threshold for determining if vertices are "close". Default value: 0.5 [S1, unofficial].
- $\omega_{pear}$: The threshold for determining if Gaussian triangle vertices are collinear. Default value: 0.9 [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
Vertex-level pruning is triggered whenever primitive-level pruning is performed [S1, unofficial]. The process involves three checks:

1.  **Triangle Collapse to Ellipse:** For a Gaussian triangle primitive, calculate the maximum pairwise L2 distance between its three vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$. If this distance is below $\omega_{dist}$, the vertices are considered close. Prune vertices $\mu_2$ and $\mu_3$, and convert the primitive into a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Triangle Collapse to Line:** For a Gaussian triangle primitive, if the vertices are not close (step 1 fails), check for collinearity. Compute the Pearson correlation coefficient from the three vertices. If the absolute value of this coefficient exceeds $\omega_{pear}$, the vertices are nearly collinear. Convert the primitive into a Gaussian line [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Line Collapse to Ellipse:** For a Gaussian line primitive, calculate the L2 distance between its two vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2$. If this distance is below $\omega_{dist}$, the vertices are considered close. Prune vertex $\mu_2$ and reduce the primitive to a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The method does not reduce the representation to only point-centered ellipses because densification (cloning/splitting) is integrated alongside pruning [S1, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise when vertices are extremely close, potentially causing numerical instability in distance calculations.

## Operating characteristics
- **Storage Reduction:** The vertex pruning mechanism reduces the average storage size required to store vertex coordinates by 24% compared to the variant without pruning [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** The method adds a computational overhead during the pruning step, but this is triggered only during primitive-level pruning events, which are relatively infrequent compared to rendering steps.
- **Strengths:** Refines the Gaussian Splatting representation by removing unnecessary vertices, leading to less "messy" representations and better surface reconstruction metrics (e.g., Chamfer distance) [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** The effectiveness depends on the thresholds $\omega_{dist}$ and $\omega_{pear}$. Incorrect values may lead to over-pruning (loss of detail) or under-pruning (inefficiency).

## Background
Vertex-level pruning was introduced in the context of Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting (MP-GS), a method that extends standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by using Gaussian triangles and lines in addition to ellipses [S1, unofficial]. The method was proposed to address the need for refining the representation during learning, particularly in sub-areas where simpler primitives suffice [S1, unofficial].
