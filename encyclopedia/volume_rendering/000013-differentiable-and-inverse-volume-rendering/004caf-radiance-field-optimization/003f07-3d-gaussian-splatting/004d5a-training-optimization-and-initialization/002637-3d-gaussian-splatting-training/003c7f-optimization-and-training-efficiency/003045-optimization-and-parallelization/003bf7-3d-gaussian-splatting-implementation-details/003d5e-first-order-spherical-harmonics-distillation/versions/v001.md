# First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation

Also called: Mobile-GS SH Distillation, SH Knowledge Distillation, SH Order Reduction via Distillation, SH degree reduction, Spherical Harmonics degree reduction

## Overview
First-order Spherical Harmonics (SH) Distillation reduces the parameter count and computational cost of 3D Gaussian Splatting by distilling high-fidelity third-order SH coefficients from a pretrained teacher model into a compact first-order representation for a student model [S2, unofficial][S1]. Unlike standard degree reduction which simply truncates coefficients, this method uses knowledge distillation to preserve rendering quality despite the aggressive reduction from 16 coefficients per color channel to 4 [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [RoofGS](9830-roofgs.md) or [Inverse Multiquadric Splatting](9910-inverse-multiquadric-splatting.md) by specifically targeting the appearance representation's dimensionality rather than the geometric or sorting pipeline [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A pretrained teacher 3D Gaussian Splatting model with third-order SH coefficients ($3 \times 16$ per Gaussian) and a student model initialized with first-order SH coefficients ($3 \times 4$ per Gaussian).
*   **Output:** A student model with optimized first-order SH coefficients that approximate the teacher's directional radiance.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Teacher SH Coefficients:** For each Gaussian $i$, a tensor $\mathbf{C}_i^{\text{tea}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 16}$ representing RGB color coefficients for SH degree 3.
*   **Student SH Coefficients:** For each Gaussian $i$, a tensor $\mathbf{C}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}$ representing RGB color coefficients for SH degree 1.
*   **Depth Buffers:** $\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p^{\text{tea}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p$ for teacher and student rendered depth at pixel $p$.

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$: Stability constant added to teacher depth [S2, unofficial].
*   Target SH Degree: 1 (first-order) [S2, unofficial]. Note: The method framework supports reduction to degree 2 as well, but the specific "First-order" variant targets degree 1 [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the student model's first-order SH coefficients $\mathbf{C}_i$ randomly or by projecting the teacher's coefficients onto the first-order basis [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Rendering:** Render images and depth maps from both the teacher and student models for a batch of training views.
3.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the distillation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dstill}}$ and the scale-invariant depth distillation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}$.
4.  **Optimization:** Update the student model's parameters (primarily the first-order SH coefficients) to minimize the combined loss.

**Formulas:**
The color distillation loss is the mean squared error between the rendered pixel colors of the teacher and student:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{dstill}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \|\mathbf{C}_p^{\text{tea}} - \mathbf{C}_p\|^2
$$
where $P$ is the set of pixels, $\mathbf{C}_p^{\text{tea}}$ is the rendered pixel color from the teacher, and $\mathbf{C}_p$ is the rendered pixel color from the student [S2, unofficial].

The scale-invariant depth distillation loss is defined as:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}(\mathbf{D}, \mathbf{D}^{\text{tea}}) = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{p \in P} \left( \log(\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p) - \log(\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p^{\text{tea}}) \right)^2 - \frac{1}{|P|^2} \left( \sum_{p \in P} \left( \log(\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p) - \log(\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p^{\text{tea}}) \right) \right)^2
$$
where $\hat{\mathbf{D}}_p^{\text{tea}} = \mathbf{D}^{\text{tea}} + \epsilon$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ [S2, unofficial]. The second term subtracts the mean difference to ensure scale invariance, acknowledging that teacher depth may not be strictly reliable [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def first_order_sh_distillation(teacher_model, student_model, dataset, num_iterations):
    """
    Distills third-order SH from teacher to first-order SH in student.
    """
    epsilon = 1e-8
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Sample a batch of views
        views = dataset.sample_batch()
        
        total_loss = 0.0
        
        for view in views:
            # 2. Render from teacher and student
            # teacher_render returns (color_image, depth_image)
            teacher_color, teacher_depth = teacher_model.render(view)
            student_color, student_depth = student_model.render(view)
            
            # 3. Compute Color Distillation Loss
            # MSE between rendered colors
            color_loss = mean_squared_error(teacher_color, student_color)
            
            # 4. Compute Depth Distillation Loss
            # Stabilize teacher depth
            stabilized_teacher_depth = teacher_depth + epsilon
            
            # Log differences
            log_diff = log(stabilized_teacher_depth) - log(student_depth)
            
            # Scale-invariant term: MSE of log diffs minus squared mean of log diffs
            mean_log_diff = mean(log_diff)
            depth_loss = mean(log_diff ** 2) - (mean_log_diff ** 2)
            
            # 5. Combine losses
            # Note: Weights for depth vs color are not specified in sources [S2, unofficial]
            # Assuming equal weight or standard practice if not given.
            # [G] Using a placeholder weight alpha for depth, typically tuned empirically.
            alpha = 1.0 
            batch_loss = color_loss + alpha * depth_loss
            
            total_loss += batch_loss
            
        # 6. Backpropagate and update student model
        total_loss.backward()
        student_model.optimizer.step()
        student_model.optimizer.zero_grad()
        
    return student_model
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Depth Reliability:** The teacher's depth is not always reliable, hence the use of scale-invariant loss rather than strict L1/L2 depth constraints [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The addition of $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ is critical for training stability when taking the logarithm of depth values [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### SH Degree 2 Distillation
A variant that projects third-order SH to second-order ($3 \times 9$ coefficients). It offers a middle ground in compression, reducing parameters by 36% and computation by 35% with a PSNR drop of 0.17 dB [S1]. Choose this when a slight quality retention is preferred over maximum compression. See [Mobile-GS](9830-roofgs.md) for context on degree reduction strategies.

### SH Degree 1 Distillation
The primary method described, projecting to first-order ($3 \times 4$ coefficients). It reduces parameters by 61% and computation by 52% with a PSNR drop of 0.48 dB [S1]. Choose this for mobile/edge deployment where storage and speed are critical. See [Mobile-GS](9830-roofgs.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering complexity is reduced by 52% when moving from degree 3 to degree 1 [S1]. The distillation training time depends on the teacher-student iteration count but is generally faster than full 3DGS training due to the reduced parameter space [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Parameter count is reduced by 61% for the SH component when distilling to first-order [S1].
*   **Performance:** On standard datasets (Mip-NeRF360, Tanks&Temples, Deep Blending), the method achieves a 51.6× overall compression when combined with iterative pruning and Vector Quantization, with a total PSNR drop of 0.73 dB relative to the baseline [S1]. The SH distillation alone contributes a 0.48 dB PSNR drop [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly lowers storage burden and increases rendering speed, making 3DGS viable for mobile devices [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Inevitable quality loss (PSNR drop) compared to third-order SH [S1]. The depth supervision is approximate [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When deploying 3DGS on resource-constrained hardware (mobile, edge) [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When maximum photorealism is required and storage/compute are not constraints.

## Background
First-order Spherical Harmonics Distillation was introduced in the context of **Mobile-GS**, a framework designed for real-time Gaussian Splatting on mobile devices [S2, unofficial]. It addresses the high storage and computational costs of the original 3DGS method, which uses third-order SH to capture view-dependent appearance [S2, unofficial]. While LightGaussian used distillation to reduce SH to second-order [S2, unofficial], Mobile-GS extended this to first-order to achieve higher compression ratios suitable for mobile deployment [S2, unofficial]. The method builds on the concept of knowledge distillation in neural networks, applying it to the parametric representation of Gaussian attributes [S2, unofficial].
