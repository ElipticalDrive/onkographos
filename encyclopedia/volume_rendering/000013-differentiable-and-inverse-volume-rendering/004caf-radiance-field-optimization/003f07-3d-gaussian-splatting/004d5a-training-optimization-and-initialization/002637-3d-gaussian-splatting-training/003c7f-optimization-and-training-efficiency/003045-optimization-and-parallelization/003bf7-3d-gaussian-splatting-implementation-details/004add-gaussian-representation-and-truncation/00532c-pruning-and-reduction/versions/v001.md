# Pruning and Reduction

Also called: Gaussian Pruning, Gaussian Reduction, Vertex Pruning

## Overview
Pruning and Reduction methods remove redundant or low-impact 3D Gaussians from a trained or training model to reduce storage overhead, improve rendering efficiency, or facilitate compression [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike densification which adds complexity, these methods actively reduce the Gaussian count $N$ to a target $K$ or remove primitives based on geometric degeneracy [S1, unofficial][S2]. They are distinguished from sibling articles by their focus on *removal* rather than *representation* (e.g., [Inverse Multiquadric Splatting](9910-inverse-multiquadric-splatting.md) or [Voxel-based Gaussian Splatting](13071-voxel-based-gaussian-splatting.md)), and from [Opacity-Independent Gaussian Truncation](10266-opacity-independent-gaussian-truncation.md) by their use of optimization-aware or structural criteria rather than fixed spatial thresholds [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Shared principles
The family operates on the standard 3D Gaussian representation defined in [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md). The core mechanism involves evaluating a metric for each Gaussian (or primitive) and removing it if the metric indicates redundancy. Common evaluation criteria include:
1.  **Opacity:** Gaussians with opacity $\alpha$ below a threshold are culled [S2].
2.  **Geometric Degeneracy:** Primitives (triangles/lines) are simplified if their vertices are too close or collinear [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Rendering Impact:** The change in rendered pixel color (loss) upon removing a Gaussian is estimated; Gaussians with minimal impact are removed [S2].
4.  **Visibility:** Gaussians occluded by others from all viewpoints are removed [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Vertex-level Pruning in Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting](13983-vertex-level-pruning-in-mixed-primitive-gaussian-splatting.md)
Vertex-level pruning is a refinement mechanism for mixed-primitive Gaussian Splatting that removes redundant vertices from Gaussian triangles and lines during training to reduce storage overhead and representation complexity. Unlike standard 3D Gaussian Splatting which only performs primitive-level opacity pruning, this method dynamically downgrades primitives to simpler forms (ellipses or lines) when their vertex configurations become degenerate, complementing the densification operations that maintain surface detail. See [Vertex-level Pruning in Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting](13983-vertex-level-pruning-in-mixed-primitive-gaussian-splatting.md).

### [SplatQ](14415-splatq.md)
SplatQ is a post-training Gaussian pruning framework that partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent budget concentration, and constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects to jointly select Gaussians under a fixed budget. It differs from global Top-K methods by solving a combinatorial optimization problem within each block rather than ranking all Gaussians globally by independent scores, and it differs from methods that only consider individual deletion errors by modeling pairwise interactions and joint effects via a quadratic objective. See [SplatQ](14415-splatq.md).

### Vertex Pruning
#### definition
- [S1, unofficial] A pruning mechanism designed to support mixed-primitive-based learning, as standard pruning is incompatible with mixed primitives.
#### step
- [S1, unofficial] Prune vertices of the mixed primitives to optimize the representation.

### Gaussian Reduction with Learnable Mask
#### definition
- [S2] A method to reduce the number of Gaussians using a learnable mask during optimization, targeting small-sized kernels for removal.
#### step
- [S2] Apply binary masks M to the opacities and scale attributes of the kernels at every densification step.
- [S2] Use residual vector quantization with L stages of vector quantization for geometric attributes like scale and rotation.
- [S2] Represent view-dependent colors via a grid-based neural field using hash grids for input positions, features, and view direction, fed into an MLP.
#### formula
- [S2] The end-to-end loss is: $L = L_{ren} + \lambda_m L_m + L_r + L_s$, where $L_{ren}$ is L1+SSIM loss, $L_m$ is masking loss ($\frac{1}{N} \sum \sigma(m_n)$), and $L_r, L_s$ are rotation and scale objective functions.
#### parameter
- [S2] $\epsilon$: Masking threshold for the sigmoid function in the learnable mask.
- [S2] $\lambda_m$: Weight for the masking loss term, typically $5 \times 10^{-4}$.

### 3DGS Visibility Pruning
#### definition
- [S2] Application of visibility-based pruning to 3D Gaussian Splatting, using Gaussian centroids as reference points for visibility calculation.
#### step
- [S2] Represent each Gaussian kernel by its centroid position, scaling matrix $S$, and rotation matrix $R$ defining the covariance $\Sigma$.
- [S2] Use the Gaussian centroids as the 3D points $p_i$ in the visibility mask computation algorithm.

## Background
Pruning in Gaussian Splatting evolved from the need to manage the explosive growth of Gaussians during densification. Early methods relied on simple opacity thresholds [S2]. More recent approaches address the limitations of global ranking by introducing block-wise optimization [S1, unofficial] or mixed-primitive structures that require vertex-level simplification [S1, unofficial]. Visibility-based pruning leverages the fact that occluded Gaussians contribute little to the final image, while learnable masks integrate pruning directly into the training loss [S2].
