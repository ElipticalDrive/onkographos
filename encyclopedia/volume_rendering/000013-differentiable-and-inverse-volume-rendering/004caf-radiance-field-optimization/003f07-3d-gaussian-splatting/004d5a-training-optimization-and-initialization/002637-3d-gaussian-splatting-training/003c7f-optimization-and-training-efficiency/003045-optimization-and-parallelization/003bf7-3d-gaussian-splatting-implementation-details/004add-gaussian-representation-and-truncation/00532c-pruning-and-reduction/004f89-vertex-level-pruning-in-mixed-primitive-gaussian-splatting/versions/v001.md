# Vertex-level Pruning in Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Mixed-Primitive Vertex Pruning

## Overview
Vertex-level pruning is a refinement mechanism for mixed-primitive Gaussian splatting that removes redundant vertices from Gaussian triangles and lines during training, complementing standard primitive-level opacity pruning [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by operating on the vertex geometry of mixed primitives (triangles, lines, ellipses) rather than just global primitive existence or opacity thresholds, allowing the representation to dynamically simplify from triangles to lines or ellipses based on local geometric coherence [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A set of mixed-primitive splats, where each primitive is defined by a set of vertices (positions $\mu$) and associated covariance/opacity parameters.
*   **Output:** A refined set of primitives with reduced vertex counts where geometric redundancy is detected.

**Data Structures**
Each mixed primitive maintains a list of vertices. For a Gaussian triangle, this is a list of three vertices $[\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3]$. For a Gaussian line, it is $[\mu_1, \mu_2]$. For a Gaussian ellipse (point-centered), it is $[\mu_1]$.

**Parameters**
*   $\omega_{dist}$: Threshold for vertex proximity. Default: $0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\omega_{pear}$: Threshold for collinearity detection. Default: $0.9$ [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps**
Vertex-level pruning is triggered concurrently with primitive-level pruning operations during the learning procedure [S1, unofficial]. The logic proceeds as follows for each primitive:

1.  **Gaussian Triangle to Ellipse Conversion:**
    If the three vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$ of a Gaussian triangle are "close" to each other, prune $\mu_2$ and $\mu_3$, converting the primitive into a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Condition:** The maximum pairwise L2 distance among the three vertices is less than $\omega_{dist}$:
        $$ \max_{i,j \in \{1,2,3\}, i \neq j} \|\mu_i - \mu_j\|_2 < \omega_{dist} $$

2.  **Gaussian Triangle to Line Conversion:**
    If the vertices are not close (step 1 fails) but are nearly collinear, convert the Gaussian triangle into a Gaussian line [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Condition:** The absolute value of the Pearson correlation coefficient computed from the three vertices exceeds $\omega_{pear}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* The exact formula for computing the Pearson correlation coefficient from three 3D points is not explicitly detailed in the source, but it implies a statistical measure of linear dependence among the coordinates [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Gaussian Line to Ellipse Conversion:**
    If the two vertices $\mu_1, \mu_2$ of a Gaussian line are close to each other, prune $\mu_2$, reducing the primitive to a Gaussian ellipse centered at $\mu_1$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Condition:**
        $$ \|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|_2 < \omega_{dist} $$

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Collinearity Metric:** The source specifies using the Pearson correlation coefficient for collinearity but does not provide the mathematical formulation for applying this metric to a set of 3D points [S1, unofficial]. Implementers must define how to compute a single scalar correlation from three 3D vectors (e.g., via PCA or coordinate projection) [G].
*   **Primitive Conversion:** When converting a triangle to a line, the source does not specify how the new line's orientation and covariance are derived from the original triangle's parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interaction with Densification:** The pruning mechanism is designed to work alongside the original densification (cloning/splitting) mechanisms of Gaussian Splatting. Pruning does not reduce the representation to only point-centered ellipses because densification can re-introduce complexity [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Storage Reduction:** The vertex pruning mechanism reduces the average storage size required to store vertex coordinates by 24% compared to the variant without pruning [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** The source does not provide specific timing benchmarks for the pruning step itself, only qualitative statements that the framework with pruning performs better (in terms of reconstruction quality metrics like Chamfer distance) than the variant without it [S1, unofficial].
*   **Complexity:** The pruning check is $O(1)$ per primitive, as it involves a constant number of distance calculations and a single correlation check for triangles.

## Background
Vertex-level pruning was introduced in the context of Mixed-Primitive Gaussian Splatting (MP-GS), a method that extends standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by using primitives with varying numbers of vertices (triangles, lines, ellipses) for surface reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. The method was proposed to refine the representation by eliminating unnecessary vertices during the learning process, addressing the issue where Gaussian triangles are not necessary in certain sub-areas of an object [S1, unofficial].
