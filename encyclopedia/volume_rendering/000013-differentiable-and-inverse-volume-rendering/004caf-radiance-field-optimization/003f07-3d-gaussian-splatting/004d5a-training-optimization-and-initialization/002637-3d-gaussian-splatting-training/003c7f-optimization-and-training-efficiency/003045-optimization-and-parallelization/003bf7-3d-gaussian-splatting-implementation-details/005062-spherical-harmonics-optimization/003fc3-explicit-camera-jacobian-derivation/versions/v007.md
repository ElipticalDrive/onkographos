# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives the analytical Jacobians of the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline with respect to the camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$ [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of high computational overhead and numerical instability associated with automatic differentiation (AD) when optimizing camera poses in SLAM or tracking applications [S1, unofficial]. By providing minimal Jacobians that match the degrees of freedom of the Lie algebra, it eliminates redundant computations inherent in matrix-based derivatives, enabling efficient gradient descent for camera pose optimization [S1, unofficial]. It is distinct from prior ray-intersection methods which assume fewer Gaussians and do not support the EWA splatting rasterization used in 3DGS [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes the gradients of the rendered image and depth with respect to the camera pose by applying the chain rule through the Gaussian projection and rasterization steps. It uses Lie algebra to ensure the Jacobian dimensions match the 6 degrees of freedom (3 translation, 3 rotation) of the camera pose [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
*   $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$: Camera-to-world pose (translation $t$ and rotation $R$).
*   $\mathcal{G}$: Set of 3D Gaussians, each defined by mean $\mu_W \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance $\Sigma_W \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, opacity $\alpha$, and SH coefficients.
*   Camera intrinsic matrix $K$.
*   Observed image $\bar{I}$ and/or depth $\bar{D}$ (for tracking losses).

**Outputs:**
*   Jacobian matrices $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ required for gradient descent updates.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: Represented as a $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix or decomposed into rotation matrix $R_{CW}$ and translation vector $t_{CW}$.
*   $\mu_C$: Gaussian mean in camera coordinates, $\mu_C = R_{CW} \mu_W + t_{CW}$.
*   $W$: The rotational component of the pose transformation applied to the covariance.
*   $\tau \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$: Infinitesimal generator (Lie algebra element) represented as a 6D vector $[\rho, \theta]^T$, where $\rho$ is translation and $\theta$ is rotation.

**Definitions:**
*   $\mathcal{D}$: Derivative on the manifold (Lie group).
*   $\text{Exp}: \mathfrak{se}(3) \to SE(3)$: Exponential map.
*   $\text{Log}: SE(3) \to \mathfrak{se}(3)$: Logarithmic map.
*   $\times$: Hat operator mapping a 3D vector to a $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrix. For vector $v = [v_x, v_y, v_z]^T$:
    $$
    v^\times = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -v_z & v_y \\ v_z & 0 & -v_x \\ -v_y & v_x & 0 \end{bmatrix}
    $$
*   $W_{:,i}$: The $i$-th column of the rotation matrix $W$.

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**

1.  **Define Manifold Derivative:**
    The derivative of a function $f(T)$ with respect to $T \in SE(3)$ is defined using the limit of the logarithmic map of the perturbed function [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau}
    $$
    where $\circ$ is group composition and $\tau \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Derive Jacobian for Gaussian Mean ($\mu_I$):**
    The mean in image coordinates $\mu_I$ depends on the camera-coordinate mean $\mu_C$. Using the chain rule:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}
    $$
    The term $\frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}$ is derived as follows [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Exp}(\tau) \cdot \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Expanding $\text{Exp}(\tau) \approx I + \tau^\wedge$ for small $\tau = [\rho, \theta]^T$:
    $$
    = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(I + \tau^\wedge) \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Substituting $\tau^\wedge \mu_C = \theta^\times \mu_C + \rho = -\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho$:
    $$
    = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Thus, the full Jacobian for the mean is:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{3 \times 3} & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$

3.  **Derive Jacobian for 2D Covariance ($\Sigma_I$):**
    The 2D covariance $\Sigma_I$ depends on the projection Jacobian $J$, the camera-coordinate mean $\mu_C$, and the rotational component $W$ of the pose [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}
    $$
    The first term uses the mean Jacobian derived in Step 2. The second term requires $\frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}$.

4.  **Derive Jacobian for Rotation Component ($W$):**
    Let $W$ be the rotation matrix part of the pose. The derivative of $W$ with respect to the Lie algebra element $\tau = [\rho, \theta]^T$ is computed by considering the perturbation $\theta^\times$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial W}{\partial \rho} & \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_x} & \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_y} & \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_z} \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Since translation $\rho$ does not affect rotation $W$, $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \rho} = 0$.
    The derivative with respect to rotation components is derived using the property $\frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_i} = e_i^\times W$, where $e_i$ is the $i$-th basis vector [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_x} = e_1^\times W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} W = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -W_{3,1} & -W_{3,2} & -W_{3,3} \\ W_{2,1} & W_{2,2} & W_{2,3} \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Generalizing for all columns $i=1,2,3$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}W}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{:,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Note: The source notation $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}$ implies a block structure where the first column block is zero (translation) and the subsequent blocks are skew-symmetric matrices formed from the columns of $W$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Compute Gradients for Tracking:**
    For photometric tracking, minimize $E_{pho} = \| I(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{I} \|_1$. For geometric tracking, minimize $E_{geo} = \| D(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{D} \|_1$. The depth rasterization is:
    $$
    \mathcal{D}_p = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} z_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
    $$
    where $z_i$ is the distance to $\mu_W$ along the ray [S1, unofficial]. The gradients are backpropagated using the derived Jacobians.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def skew_symmetric(v):
    """Converts a 3D vector to a 3x3 skew-symmetric matrix."""
    return np.array([
        [0, -v[2], v[1]],
        [v[2], 0, -v[0]],
        [-v[1], v[0], 0]
    ])

def compute_mean_jacobian(mu_C):
    """
    Computes D(mu_C) / D(T_CW).
    mu_C: 3D vector, Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
    Returns: 3x6 matrix.
    """
    # [I, -mu_C^x]
    I = np.eye(3)
    mu_C_x = skew_symmetric(mu_C)
    return np.hstack([I, -mu_C_x])

def compute_rotation_jacobian(W):
    """
    Computes D(W) / D(T_CW).
    W: 3x3 rotation matrix component of the pose.
    Returns: 3x6 matrix (flattened or structured as per derivation).
    Based on source: [0, -W_{:,1}^x; 0, -W_{:,2}^x; 0, -W_{:,3}^x]
    This notation in the source likely refers to the derivative of the rotation 
    matrix elements with respect to the 6 Lie algebra parameters.
    Specifically, dW/d_theta_i = e_i^x W.
    """
    # Derivatives w.r.t translation are zero
    dW_d_rho = np.zeros((3, 3))
    
    # Derivatives w.r.t rotation components theta_x, theta_y, theta_z
    # e1 = [1,0,0], e2 = [0,1,0], e3 = [0,0,1]
    e1 = np.array([1, 0, 0])
    e2 = np.array([0, 1, 0])
    e3 = np.array([0, 0, 1])
    
    dW_d_theta_x = skew_symmetric(e1) @ W
    dW_d_theta_y = skew_symmetric(e2) @ W
    dW_d_theta_z = skew_symmetric(e3) @ W
    
    # Concatenate: [dW/d_rho, dW/d_theta_x, dW/d_theta_y, dW/d_theta_z]
    # Each dW/d_theta_i is 3x3. The source groups them.
    # The source formula: [0, -W_{,1}^x; ...] seems to describe the structure 
    # of the derivative of the rotation matrix columns.
    # Let's construct the 3x6 Jacobian for the rotation part if we treat W as a vector?
    # No, W is a matrix. The Jacobian D(W)/D(T) is a tensor or a set of 3x3 matrices.
    # For implementation in gradient descent, we usually vectorize W or handle matrix calculus.
    # The source provides the partials explicitly.
    
    return {
        'dW_d_rho': dW_d_rho,
        'dW_d_theta_x': dW_d_theta_x,
        'dW_d_theta_y': dW_d_theta_y,
        'dW_d_theta_z': dW_d_theta_z
    }

def get_camera_pose_jacobian(mu_C, W):
    """
    Returns the components needed for the chain rule.
    mu_C: 3D vector in camera coords.
    W: 3x3 rotation matrix.
    """
    J_mu = compute_mean_jacobian(mu_C)
    J_W = compute_rotation_jacobian(W)
    return J_mu, J_W
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The limit definition requires careful numerical implementation if not using symbolic differentiation. Using the closed-form skew-symmetric matrices avoids numerical instability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the Gaussian is at the camera center ($\mu_C = 0$), the term $-\mu_C^\times$ becomes zero, simplifying the mean Jacobian to $[I, 0]$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parameterization:** Using the full $4 \times 4$ matrix or quaternion without Lie algebra projection can introduce redundant degrees of freedom or normalization constraints that complicate gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Photometric and Geometric Tracking
This variant applies the explicit Jacobian derivation to the specific task of camera pose tracking within a SLAM framework, optimizing only the current pose $T_{CW}$ without updating the Gaussian map [S1, unofficial]. It minimizes photometric residuals $E_{pho} = \| I(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{I} \|_1$ and/or geometric residuals $E_{geo} = \| D(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{D} \|_1$ [S1, unofficial]. It requires at least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial]. It also optimizes affine brightness parameters to handle exposure variations and penalizes low-opacity or non-edge pixels to improve robustness [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The analytical derivation is $O(1)$ per Gaussian for Jacobian computation, compared to the overhead of automatic differentiation which scales with the depth of the computational graph. This enables real-time performance (e.g., 3.2 FPS monocular, 2.5 FPS RGB-D on Replica dataset) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal, as it avoids storing intermediate AD graphs for the camera pose optimization step.
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates AD overhead, ensures minimal parameterization (6 DOF), and is applicable to the full EWA splatting rasterization used in 3DGS (unlike prior ray-intersection methods) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful implementation of Lie algebra operations. Performance is sensitive to the number of Gaussians if the rasterization step itself is not optimized (though the Jacobian derivation is independent of rasterization speed, the overall loop speed is not) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When optimizing camera poses in 3DGS-based SLAM or tracking systems where computational efficiency and numerical stability are critical.
*   **When not to use:** For offline mapping where the map is updated jointly with poses and AD overhead is amortized or acceptable, or when using representations that do not support EWA splatting.

## Background
The method originates from the "Gaussian Splatting SLAM" paper, which introduced the use of 3D Gaussians as the sole representation for a unified SLAM system [S1, unofficial]. Prior methods for camera pose optimization with 3D Gaussians assumed fewer Gaussians and relied on ray-intersection, making them unsuitable for the dense splatting approach of 3DGS [S1, unofficial]. This derivation provides the first analytical Jacobian of SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians in the context of EWA splatting, enabling efficient online tracking and mapping [S1, unofficial].
