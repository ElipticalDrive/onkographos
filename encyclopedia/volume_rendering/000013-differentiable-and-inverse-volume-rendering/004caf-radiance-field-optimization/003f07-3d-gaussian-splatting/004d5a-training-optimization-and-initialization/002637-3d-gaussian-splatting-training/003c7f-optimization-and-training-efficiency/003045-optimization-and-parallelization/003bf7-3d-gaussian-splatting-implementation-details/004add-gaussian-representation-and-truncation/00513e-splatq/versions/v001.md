# SplatQ

Also called: SplatQ: Post-training Gaussian pruning via QUBO

## Overview
SplatQ is a post-training pruning framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that addresses spatial quality imbalance and ignores joint deletion effects inherent in global Top-K selection methods [S1, unofficial]. It partitions Gaussians into size-limited blocks based on spatial and appearance similarity, allocates retention quotas to prevent budget concentration, and uses a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) model to jointly select Gaussians within each block under a fixed global budget [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that rank Gaussians independently, SplatQ captures pairwise interactions and joint rendering effects to ensure balanced reconstruction quality across the entire scene [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Input:** A pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting model $G$ containing $N$ Gaussians, and a target retention count $K$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Output:** A pruned set of $K$ Gaussians with optimized attributes [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
Each Gaussian $G_i$ is parameterized by:
- Center $\mu_i$
- Scale $s_i$
- Rotation quaternion $r_i$
- Zeroth-order (DC) spherical harmonic coefficients $c_i$
- Higher-order spherical harmonic coefficients $h_i$
- Opacity $o_i$
[S1, unofficial]

The model is represented as $G = \{G_i = (\mu_i, s_i, r_i, c_i, h_i, o_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ [S1, unofficial].

Gaussians are partitioned into blocks $\{C_b\}$, where each block $C_b$ contains at most $M$ members [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
- $N$: Total number of Gaussians in the pretrained model [S1, unofficial].
- $K$: Exact number of Gaussians to retain after pruning [S1, unofficial].
- $M$: Maximum size (number of members) of each block $C_b$ [S1, unofficial].
- $K_b$: Retention quota assigned to block $b$, representing the number of Gaussians to retain within that block [S1, unofficial].
- $R_b$: Response matrix for block $b$, used to estimate individual deletion effects and pairwise interactions [S1, unofficial].

**Steps**

1. **Partitioning**
   Divide the $N$ Gaussians into several size-limited blocks $\{C_b\}$ of at most $M$ members each, based on spatial proximity and appearance similarity [S1, unofficial]. This is achieved using a 3D grid and Morton ordering to efficiently group related Gaussians, avoiding the quadratic cost of all-pairwise comparisons [S1, unofficial].

2. **Opacity Response Modeling**
   For each block $C_b$, compute a response matrix $R_b$ using sampled pixels from multiple views [S1, unofficial]. This matrix estimates the individual deletion effects and pairwise interactions of the Gaussians within the block by observing changes in rendered pixel colors relative to changes in Gaussian opacity [S1, unofficial].

3. **Budget Allocation**
   Assign a retention quota $K_b$ to every block such that $\sum_b K_b = K$ [S1, unofficial]. The initial quota is derived from importance estimates, and the distribution is smoothed to reduce the risk of concentrating the budget in a few blocks, thereby preventing spatial quality imbalance [S1, unofficial].

4. **Quadratic Selection**
   For each block, formulate a QUBO objective incorporating individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties [S1, unofficial]. The objective function uses binary retention variables ($x_i=1$ if retained, $0$ if deleted) to approximate joint deletion effects [S1, unofficial]. Solve the QUBO using Simulated Annealing (SA) or a Coherent Ising Machine (CIM) to determine which $K_b$ block members to retain [S1, unofficial].

5. **Fine-tuning**
   Fine-tune the attributes of the retained Gaussians without changing their count to recover rendering quality [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Spatial Quality Imbalance:** Global ranking methods can lead to situations where the limited budget concentrates in regions with consistently higher scores, leaving other regions with insufficient representational capacity [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ mitigates this via blockwise quota smoothing [S1, unofficial].
- **Joint Deletion Effects:** Independent scoring fails to capture joint rendering effects because multiple Gaussians may jointly support the same pixel or make overlapping contributions [S1, unofficial]. Degradation from removing multiple Gaussians is not strictly additive [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ addresses this by modeling pairwise interactions via a quadratic objective [S1, unofficial].
- **Scalability:** Formulating the optimization problem over the entire model requires $N$ binary variables and an $N \times N$ matrix, which is impractical for millions of Gaussians [S1, unofficial]. SplatQ decomposes this into smaller subproblems per block [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The partitioning step uses 3D grid and Morton ordering to avoid quadratic pairwise comparisons [S1, unofficial]. The QUBO solving step is performed per block, with complexity dependent on block size $M$ and the solver (SA or CIM) [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is reduced by decomposing the global $N \times N$ interaction matrix into smaller block-wise response matrices $R_b$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Ensures balanced reconstruction quality across the scene by controlling regional budget allocation [S1, unofficial]. Captures joint deletion effects and pairwise interactions, leading to better quality under aggressive pruning compared to independent scoring methods [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires additional computational overhead for partitioning, response matrix computation, and QUBO solving [S1, unofficial]. The quality of the pruning depends on the accuracy of the response matrix estimation [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When balanced spatial quality is critical and the scene contains regions with varying importance or density, and when standard global Top-K pruning results in visible artifacts or quality loss in less prominent areas [S1, unofficial].
