# Gaussian Splatting SLAM

Also called: Gaussian Splatting SLAM Tracking and Mapping Optimization, GaussianSplattingSLAM, Monocular Gaussian SLAM, RGB-D SLAM with Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Gaussian Splatting SLAM is a simultaneous localization and mapping method that jointly optimizes camera poses and a 3D Gaussian scene representation from monocular or RGB-D video streams. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [SplaTAM](9193-splatam.md) and [Gaussian-SLAM](9195-gaussian-slam.md) by explicitly enforcing isotropic scale regularization to stabilize localization and mapping, and by utilizing a covisibility-based keyframing strategy for efficient map management.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** A sequence of images $I_t$ and, if available, depth maps $D_t$.
- **Outputs:** Camera poses $\mathbf{T}_t \in SE(3)$ and a set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ representing the scene.

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$:** Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by mean position $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (parameterized by scale $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation $\mathbf{R}_i \in SO(3)$), opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$, and color $c_i$.
- **Keyframes $\mathcal{W}$:** A subset of frames selected based on Gaussian covisibility.

**Parameters:**
- Photometric loss weight $\lambda_{pho} = 0.9$ for RGB-D experiments [S2, unofficial].
- Isotropic regularization weight $\lambda_{iso} = 10$ for both monocular and RGB-D experiments [S2, unofficial].
- Tracking learning rate for Gaussian positions $\boldsymbol{\mu}_W$ is increased by a factor of 10 in monocular settings [S2, unofficial].
- Camera pose learning rates: 0.003 for rotation, 0.001 for translation [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1. **Keyframe Selection:**
   Select new keyframes based on Gaussian covisibility. For two keyframes $i$ and $j$, compute the Intersection of Union (IOU) and Overlap Coefficient (OC) of visible Gaussians $\mathcal{G}^v$:
   $$ IOU_{cov}(i, j) = \frac{|\mathcal{G}_i^v \cap \mathcal{G}_j^v|}{|\mathcal{G}_i^v \cup \mathcal{G}_j^v|} $$
   $$ OC_{cov}(i, j) = \frac{|\mathcal{G}_i \cap \mathcal{G}_j|}{\min(|\mathcal{G}_i^v|, |\mathcal{G}_j^v|)} $$
   A frame is added as a keyframe if covisibility with existing keyframes falls below a threshold [S2, unofficial].

2. **Tracking:**
   For each new frame, optimize the camera pose $\mathbf{T}$ relative to the current map. Minimize the weighted sum of photometric and geometric residuals:
   $$ \min_{\mathbf{T} \subset \mathbf{W} \in SE(3)} \lambda_{pho} E_{pho} + (1 - \lambda_{pho}) E_{geo} $$
   Perform 100 tracking iterations per frame. Terminate early if the magnitude of the pose update is less than $10^{-4}$ [S2, unofficial].

3. **Mapping:**
   Optimize the Gaussian parameters and keyframe poses. Minimize the sum over keyframes $k \in \mathcal{W}$:
   $$ \min_{\forall k \in \mathcal{W}} \sum_{\mathcal{G}, \forall k \in \mathcal{W}} (\lambda_{pho} E_{pho}^k + (1 - \lambda_{pho}) E_{geo}^k) + \lambda_{iso} E_{iso} $$
   Perform 150 iterations for the single-process implementation [S2, unofficial].

4. **Regularization:**
   Apply an extra scale regularization loss $E_{iso}$ to encourage isotropic Gaussians, stabilizing localization and mapping [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of Gaussians and keyframes. Tracking is per-frame, mapping is batched over keyframes.
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of Gaussians.
- **Performance:** On the Replica dataset, GaussianSplattingSLAM achieves PSNR 38.90, SSIM 0.99, and LPIPS 0.07 [S1].
- **Strengths:** High-quality reconstruction, stable localization due to isotropic regularization.
- **Limitations:** Computational cost of optimizing many Gaussians; sensitivity to initialization in monocular settings.

## Background
Gaussian Splatting SLAM was introduced in the paper "Gaussian Splatting SLAM" [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of 3D Gaussian Splatting [11] and adapts them for SLAM by incorporating keyframing and joint optimization. The method addresses challenges in map drift and Gaussian degeneracy through specific regularization and keyframing strategies.
