# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3DGS Learning, Gaussian Splatting Optimization, GaussianTrainer training procedure, Metal-accelerated 3DGS training, Msplat Training Loop & Configuration

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training is a non-deep learning optimization process that adjusts explicit 3D Gaussian parameters to minimize the difference between rendered images and ground truth images, using camera information and initial 3D Gaussian estimates [S2, unofficial]. Unlike NeRF or Plenoxels, which optimize implicit neural fields or voxel grids, 3DGS optimizes a set of explicit geometric primitives (Gaussians) defined by position, rotation, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart by relying on differentiable rasterization of splats rather than volume integration, allowing for real-time rendering and efficient gradient propagation through the projection and alpha-blending steps [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   **Student Data:** Initial 3D Gaussian parameters: center $p_w$, rotation $q$, scale $s$, spherical harmonic coefficients $h$, and opacity $\alpha$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Teacher Data:** Camera information (rotation $R_{cw}$, translation $t_{cw}$, intrinsic matrix $K$ with $f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$) and ground truth image pixel RGB values $\gamma^{gt}_j$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Configuration:** Training steps, learning rates, densification thresholds, and loss weights [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters saved to a file (e.g., PLY format) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Rendered RGB images for evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, L1) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
*   **Gaussian Parameters:** Stored in GPU memory. Each Gaussian $i$ has:
    *   $p_{w,i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D center position [S2, unofficial].
    *   $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation quaternion [S2, unofficial].
    *   $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale vector [S2, unofficial].
    *   $h_i$: Spherical harmonic coefficients for color [S2, unofficial].
    *   $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity [S2, unofficial].
*   **TrainingStats:** Contains `iteration` (int), `splat_count` (int), `ms_per_step` (float) [S1, unofficial].

**Initialization**
1.  Initialize $p_w$ and $h$ from Structure from Motion (SfM) photogrammetry [S2, unofficial].
2.  Initialize $q$, $s$, and $\alpha$ as constants [S2, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* This results in a coarse initial structure with high error in $q, s, \alpha$ [S2, unofficial].

**Loss Function**
The optimization objective is to minimize loss $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to parameters $p_w, q, s, h, \alpha$ [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\underset{p_w, q, s, h, \alpha}{\textrm{argmin}} \quad \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}(\gamma, \gamma_{gt})
$$
The loss is a combination of L1 loss ($\mathcal{L}1$) and D-SSIM loss ($\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$) weighted by $\lambda$ [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}
$$
Typical value for $\lambda$ (ssim_weight) is 0.2 [S1, unofficial].

**Forward Rendering**
For a given camera, the image is rendered by projecting $N$ Gaussians onto the 2D plane.
1.  Project 3D Gaussian $i$ to 2D center $u_i$ and covariance $\Sigma'_i$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  Sort Gaussians by depth [S2, unofficial].
3.  Compute effective opacity $\alpha'_{ij}$ for Gaussian $i$ at pixel $j$:
    $$
    \alpha'_{ij} = g \alpha_i
    $$
    where $g = \exp(-0.5 \omega'_0 d_0^2 - 0.5 \omega'_2 d_1^2 - \omega'_1 d_0 d_1)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   $\omega'$ are upper triangular elements of the inverse 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'^{-1}$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   $d_0, d_1$ are elements of $u_i - x_j$ (vector from pixel center to Gaussian center) [S2, unofficial].
4.  Compute pixel color $\gamma_j$ via alpha blending:
    $$
    \gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha'_{ij} c_i \tau_{ij}
    $$
    where $\tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_{kj})$ is the transmittance [S2, unofficial].
    *   $c_i$ is the color of Gaussian $i$ evaluated from spherical harmonics $h_i$ [S2, unofficial].

**Gradient Computation (Backpropagation)**
Gradients are computed using the chain rule through the 5 forward steps of image generation [S2, unofficial]. The Jacobian for each parameter group is:

1.  **Rotation $q_i$:**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i}
    $$
2.  **Scale $s_i$:**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i}
    $$
3.  **Spherical Harmonics $h_i$:**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i}
    $$
4.  **Opacity $\alpha_i$:**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i}
    $$
5.  **Center $p_{w,i}$:**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{w,i}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}}
    $$

**Intermediate Derivatives**
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{i,j}} = \tau_{i,j}(c_i - \gamma_{i+1,j})$, where $\gamma_{i,j}$ is the color considering only Gaussians $i$ to $N$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} = \tau_{i,j}\alpha'_{i,j}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i} = g$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial \omega'_i} = [-0.5d_0^2, -d_0d_1, -0.5d_1^2]^T g$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial u_i} = [-\omega'_0 d_0 - \omega'_1 d_1, -\omega'_2 d_1 - \omega'_1 d_0]^T g$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\frac{\partial \omega'_i}{\partial \sigma'_i}$ involves the determinant and elements $a,b,c$ of the upper triangular covariance matrix [S2, unofficial].

**Training Loop**
1.  **Camera Selection:** Maintain a shuffled vector of training camera indices. When the iterator reaches the end, reshuffle and reset position before returning the next index [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Iteration:**
    *   Increment `current_step` [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute downscale factor for the current step [S1, unofficial].
    *   Retrieve ground truth image at that resolution [S1, unofficial].
    *   Execute full iteration with SSIM weight [S1, unofficial].
    *   Advance schedulers (e.g., learning rate, SH degree) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Run post-training updates (densification/pruning) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Commit GPU commands [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Evaluation:** For each test camera, render RGB, synchronize GPU, convert to CPU, compute PSNR, SSIM, and L1 loss against ground truth at final resolution, then average across all test cameras [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
def train_3dgs(gaussians, cameras, config):
    # Initialize optimizer with base LR 0.0, relying on per-group overrides
    optimizer = Adam(lr=0.0)
    optimizer.add_param_group({'params': gaussians.q, 'lr': config.lr_q})
    optimizer.add_param_group({'params': gaussians.s, 'lr': config.lr_s})
    optimizer.add_param_group({'params': gaussians.h, 'lr': config.lr_h})
    optimizer.add_param_group({'params': gaussians.alpha, 'lr': config.lr_alpha})
    optimizer.add_param_group({'params': gaussians.p_w, 'lr': config.lr_p_w})

    cam_indices = list(range(len(cameras)))
    shuffle(cam_indices)
    cam_iter = 0

    for step in range(1, config.iterations + 1):
        # Camera selection
        if cam_iter >= len(cam_indices):
            shuffle(cam_indices)
            cam_iter = 0
        cam = cameras[cam_indices[cam_iter]]
        cam_iter += 1

        # Downscale and GT retrieval
        scale = get_downscale_factor(step, config.resolution_schedule, config.num_downscales)
        gt_image = cam.get_image(scale)

        # Forward pass
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, cam)
        
        # Loss computation
        l1_loss = l1_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        ssim_loss = ssim_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        loss = (1 - config.ssim_weight) * l1_loss + config.ssim_weight * ssim_loss

        # Backward pass
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()

        # Update parameters
        optimizer.step()

        # Scheduler updates
        update_sh_degree(step, config.sh_degree, config.sh_degree_interval)
        update_learning_rates(step, config)

        # Densification and Pruning (Adaptive Density Control)
        if step % config.refine_every == 0:
            densify_and_prune(gaussians, step, config.densify_grad_thresh, 
                              config.densify_size_thresh, config.stop_screen_size_at,
                              config.split_screen_size)

        # Checkpointing
        if config.save_every > 0 and step % config.save_every == 0:
            save_gaussians(gaussians, step)

        # Evaluation
        if step % 10 == 0:
            evaluate(gaussians, test_cameras, config)
```

**Parameters**
*   `iterations`: Total number of training steps. Default: 30000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree`: Spherical harmonic degree. Default: 3 [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree_interval`: Steps between increasing SH degree. Default: 1000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `ssim_weight`: Weight applied to SSIM loss. Default: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   `num_downscales`: Number of resolution levels. Default: 2 [S1, unofficial].
*   `resolution_schedule`: Step at which resolution scaling changes. Default: 3000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `refine_every`: Frequency of refinement operations. Default: 100 [S1, unofficial].
*   `warmup_length`: Initial training phase duration. Default: 500 [S1, unofficial].
*   `reset_alpha_every`: Frequency of alpha reset. Default: 30 [S1, unofficial].
*   `densify_grad_thresh`: Gradient threshold for densification. Default: 0.0002 [S1, unofficial].
*   `densify_size_thresh`: Size threshold for densification. Default: 0.01 [S1, unofficial].
*   `stop_screen_size_at`: Step at which screen-size based splitting stops. Default: 4000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `split_screen_size`: Screen size threshold for splitting Gaussians. Default: 0.05 [S1, unofficial].
*   `bg_color`: Background color [R, G, B]. Default: [0.613, 0.010, 0.398] (magenta) [S1, unofficial].
*   `downscale_factor`: Initial dataset downscale factor. Default: 1.0 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   Evaluation requires the dataset to be loaded with `eval_mode=True`; otherwise, it throws a runtime error [S1, unofficial].
*   Arbitrary pose rendering requires exactly 16 elements for the `cam_to_world` matrix; otherwise, it throws a runtime error [S1, unofficial].
*   Precision issues may arise in the calculation of the inverse covariance matrix derivatives [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md)
Optimization of physical volume parameters for 3D Gaussians, including extinction coefficient ($\sigma_t$), albedo ($\omega$), Henyey-Greenstein scattering asymmetry ($g$), and multiple scattering octave weights ($w$).

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training > Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md)
Configures an Adam optimizer with distinct learning rates for each Gaussian parameter group to stabilize training and prevent rapid divergence. It sets up the optimizer with a base learning rate of 0.0, relying entirely on per-group overrides defined in the parameter groups. This approach addresses the varying sensitivity of Gaussian attributes (position, scale, color, opacity) to gradient updates.

### [GES Optimization Strategy](4697-ges-optimization-strategy.md)
The GES Optimization Strategy is a training methodology for Generalized Exponential Splatting (GES) that controls shape density by selectively pruning splats based on shape attributes, thereby eliminating the need for a variable density mechanism. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting training by optimizing the shape parameter $\beta$ alongside position, opacity, and covariance, using a frequency-modulated loss to enhance high-frequency details. It builds on [3D Gaussian Splatting Training](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md) but replaces its density-based densification/pruning with shape-based pruning.

### [WildGaussians](6064-wildgaussians.md)
WildGaussians is a method for "in-the-wild" 3D reconstruction that integrates robust DINO features and a lightweight appearance modeling module within the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework to handle unconstrained photo collections. It sets itself apart from standard 3DGS by explicitly addressing varying illumination, occlusions, and transient objects found in real-world data, while maintaining real-time rendering speeds.

### [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md)
Densification strategies manage the population of Gaussian primitives during the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, addressing the issues of unpredictable model size, redundant primitives, and excessive training duration. Unlike the original 3DGS approach which relies on heuristic splitting and cloning based on position gradients and opacity, this family includes methods that use score-based constructive growth, temporal sensitivity pruning, and groupwise motion distillation to control model complexity and improve rendering efficiency.

### PixelSplat Training and Depth Regularization
Train the model for 300,000 steps using a batch size of 7 [S5]. Use an MSE loss for the first 150,000 iterations [S5]. Supplement the MSE loss with an LPIPS loss with weight 0.05 starting at 150,000 steps [S5]. For each batch element (scene), render 4 target views [S5]. Place 3 Gaussians along each ray to allow gradients for multiple estimated depths during the forward pass [S5]. Determine Gaussian positions by independently sampling from the ray's probability distribution 3 times [S5]. Divide each Gaussian's alpha value by 3 such that $\alpha = \phi_z / 3$, ensuring total opacity of roughly 1 for Gaussians on a ray [S5]. Fine-tune the model for 50,000 steps with a depth regularization loss $L_{reg\_depth}$ to generate point clouds [S5]. For each rendered view, generate a corresponding depth map $D$ [S5]. Compute horizontal depth difference: $D_x^{\Delta}[\mathbf{u}] = D[\mathbf{u}_{x-1}, \mathbf{u}_y] - 2D[\mathbf{u}_x, \mathbf{u}_y] + D[\mathbf{u}_{x+1}, \mathbf{u}_y]$, where $D$ is the depth map and $\mathbf{u}$ represents pixel coordinates $(u_x, u_y)$ [S5]. Compute vertical depth difference: $D_y^{\Delta}[\mathbf{u}] = D[\mathbf{u}_x, \mathbf{u}_{y-1}] - 2D[\mathbf{u}_x, \mathbf{u}_y] + D[\mathbf{u}_x, \mathbf{u}_{y+1}]$ [S5]. VRAM requirement: approximately 80 GB on a single GPU [S5].

### SWAG
A method that extends 3DGS by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders [S6].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The training loop is dominated by the forward rendering and backward pass for each iteration. The number of iterations is typically 30,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians and the number of pixels. The explicit nature of Gaussians avoids the $O(kn)$ intermediate state storage of reverse-mode AD for neural fields, but the sorting and blending steps require significant GPU memory [S5].
*   **Measured Performance:** On an M4 Max, training can be completed in ~90 seconds using a native Metal backend [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Performance scales with the number of Gaussians and the resolution of the rendered images. Downscaling schedules are used to accelerate early training [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering capability, explicit representation, efficient gradient propagation through rasterization [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to initialization (SfM), requires careful tuning of densification parameters, high VRAM usage for large scenes or high resolutions (e.g., 80 GB for PixelSplat) [S5].
*   **When to use:** When real-time rendering is required, or when an explicit 3D representation is needed for downstream tasks (e.g., mesh extraction, editing) [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the scene has complex lighting effects that are difficult to model with spherical harmonics, or when the input data is extremely sparse and SfM initialization is unreliable [S4, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S19, S5]. It builds on the idea of representing scenes as a collection of 3D Gaussians and rendering them using differentiable rasterization [S5]. The training procedure relies on the differentiability of the forward rendering operator and the propagation of gradients through the integration steps, specifically the alpha-blending process [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to address the slow rendering speeds of NeRF and other radiance field methods while maintaining high visual quality [S5]. It has since been extended to various domains, including non-pinhole cameras, sparse views, and in-the-wild captures [S3][S4, unofficial][S6].
