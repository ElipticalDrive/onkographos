# Regularization and Geometry Constraints


## Overview
Regularization and geometry constraints in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) address the inherent ambiguity of representing continuous scenes with discrete, anisotropic primitives, preventing artifacts such as floating geometry, smearing in unseen regions, and poor surface reconstruction. Unlike the base training loop which optimizes for photometric consistency alone, this family introduces explicit penalties on Gaussian properties (scale, opacity) and auxiliary supervisory signals (depth, normals, frequency) to enforce geometric plausibility and structural coherence. It sets itself apart from sibling optimization strategies by focusing on the *quality* and *physical validity* of the resulting radiance field rather than the convergence speed or parameter management.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves augmenting the standard photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}$ with additional terms that constrain the Gaussian parameters $\theta = \{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{s}, \mathbf{r}, \alpha, \mathbf{c}\}$ (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color).

1.  **Depth Regularization**: Enforces consistency between rendered depth and estimated depth maps $D_{est}$. The loss typically minimizes the difference between rendered depth $D_{render}$ and $D_{est}$, often using a combination of $L_1$ and scale-invariant losses to handle depth ambiguity [S3].
2.  **Normal Regularization**: Constrains the orientation of Gaussians to align with surface normals derived from depth maps or priors. This ensures that the Gaussian ellipsoids are tangent to the underlying surface, improving mesh extraction quality.
3.  **Frequency Regularization**: Penalizes low-frequency variations in the scene representation to encourage Gaussians to concentrate in high-frequency detail regions, reducing blurriness [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Visibility and Occlusion Handling**: Uses 2D visibility maps or occlusion-aware likelihoods to prevent Gaussians from being optimized in regions occluded by other geometry, mitigating transient effects and floating artifacts [S1].

## Subtopics

### Wild-GS
A method that aligns pixel appearance features from image triplanes directly to 3D Gaussians and uses depth regularization and visibility maps to mitigate transient effects and constrain geometry [S1]. It specifically targets casual captures where transient objects (people, cars) cause reconstruction artifacts. By leveraging 2D visibility maps, it identifies regions affected by photometric variations and transient occlusions, allowing the optimizer to ignore or down-weight these areas during training. This results in high-fidelity reconstructions of the static scene while suppressing transient distractors.

### Frequency Regularization
Uses frequency regularization to push Gaussians toward high-frequency regions [S2, unofficial]. This technique is particularly useful in scenes with fine details where standard photometric loss might lead to over-smoothing. By penalizing low-frequency components in the Gaussian distribution or the rendered output, the method encourages the splatting process to allocate more Gaussians to complex, high-frequency areas, thereby improving the sharpness of the final render.

### DNGaussian
Uses depth-normal regularizers [S2, unofficial]. This approach combines depth consistency with surface normal alignment to produce more accurate geometric representations. The depth regularizer ensures that the Gaussians lie on the estimated surface, while the normal regularizer ensures that the Gaussians are oriented correctly relative to that surface. This dual constraint significantly improves the quality of extracted meshes compared to using depth alone, as it resolves ambiguities in Gaussian orientation that depth-only supervision cannot address.

### Source Feature Extrapolation
A technique to improve extrapolation capabilities beyond source view frustums by padding source images and adding positional encoding channels for padded regions, preventing smearing artifacts in regions not visible by source images [S3].

#### step
Apply border padding to source images $I^{(i)}$.

#### formula
Create a channel $\Gamma$ containing pixel-wise positional encodings for padded regions: $\Gamma[u,v] = \gamma(u,v)$ if $(u,v)$ is not in $I^{(i)}$, else $0$. Concatenate $I_{pad}$ and $\Gamma$ to form $I'(i) = \text{concatenate}(I_{pad}, \Gamma)$.

#### parameter
$\gamma(\cdot)$ denotes positional encoding with exponential frequencies.

This method modifies the input to the feature extraction network $E$ by padding the source images $I^{(i)}$ with constant values and appending a channel $\Gamma$ that encodes the spatial coordinates of the padded pixels. The positional encoding $\gamma(u,v)$ uses exponential frequencies as proposed in [28]. This allows the network to distinguish between valid image regions and padded regions, preventing the "smearing" of features into empty space when rendering views outside the original camera frustums.

## Background
The challenge of regularizing 3D Gaussian Splatting arises from the fact that standard 3DGS optimizes for photometric consistency without explicit geometric constraints, leading to "floating" Gaussians and poor surface reconstruction. Early works focused on densification strategies to improve detail [Parent], but these did not address geometric validity.

The concept of using depth and normal constraints for Gaussian optimization was explored in works like DNGaussian [S2, unofficial], which demonstrated that aligning Gaussians with surface normals improves mesh quality. Frequency regularization was introduced in FreGS [S2, unofficial] to address blurriness in high-frequency regions.

Wild-GS [S1] emerged from the need to handle casual captures with transient objects, adapting techniques from NeRFs (like visibility maps) to the Gaussian splatting framework. Source Feature Extrapolation, detailed in DINER [S3], addresses the specific issue of extrapolating beyond source views, a common problem in feed-forward and sparse-view Gaussian methods. These methods collectively form a family of techniques that enhance the geometric fidelity of 3DGS beyond simple photometric optimization.
