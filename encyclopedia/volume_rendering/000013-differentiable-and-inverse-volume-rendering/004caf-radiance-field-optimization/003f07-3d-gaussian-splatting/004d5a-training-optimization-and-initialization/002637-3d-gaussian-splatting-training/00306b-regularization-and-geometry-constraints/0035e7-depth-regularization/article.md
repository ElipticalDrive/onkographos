# Depth Regularization

Also called: Depth Constraint, Depth Supervision, Depth Prior

## Overview
Depth Regularization applies a differentiable auxiliary loss term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ to constrain the geometric structure of 3D Gaussian primitives during training, typically by aligning rendered depth or disparity maps with monocular depth estimates or smoothness priors [Parent]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Frequency Regularization](6130-frequency-regularization.md) and [Smoothed 2D Supervision](7213-smoothed-2d-supervision.md) by explicitly targeting the metric depth accuracy and surface completeness, addressing the "hollowness" and geometric degradation common in sparse-view scenarios [S1, unofficial][S4].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family compute a differentiable auxiliary loss term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ that is added to the total training loss:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{reg} \mathcal{L}_{reg} $$
where $\lambda_{reg}$ is a scalar weight. The core mechanism involves extracting a geometric or spectral property from the rendered output (or the input data) and comparing it against a target or constraint. The gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ flow back through the differentiable rasterizer to update the Gaussian parameters $\theta$ (centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors $c$) [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [DNGaussian](7399-dngaussian.md)
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing a two-stage depth regularization process that explicitly manages the trade-off between surface positioning (Hard) and surface completeness (Soft). Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, which lowers PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible regions but allows the optimization to focus strictly on visible geometry.

### [Edge-Aware Disparity Regularization](9140-edge-aware-disparity-regularization.md)
Edge-Aware Disparity Regularization is a geometric constraint used during the training of neural radiance fields to enforce local depth smoothness while preserving sharp depth discontinuities that align with object boundaries in the input imagery. It addresses the issue of noisy or inconsistent depth maps generated by NeRF by weighting the disparity gradient penalty with the intensity gradients of the input image, ensuring that depth edges are not smoothed out where color edges exist. This method builds on [Depth Regularization](7616-depth-regularization.md) by introducing image-guided weighting to the smoothness prior.

## Background
Depth regularization in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a solution to the geometric degradation observed in sparse-view reconstruction [S4]. Standard 3DGS, optimized for dense multi-view inputs, often produces "hollow" or incomplete geometry when trained on few shots [S1, unofficial]. Early approaches like Pulsar utilized sphere-based representations with opacity-depth energy terms to guide geometry [S3]. As the field evolved, methods like DNGaussian introduced explicit depth normalization and two-stage regularization (Hard/Soft) to align Gaussians with monocular depth priors [S1, unofficial]. Other works, such as PixelSplat, integrated depth regularization into feed-forward architectures to refine point cloud generation [S2]. Surveys categorize these as "regularization-based methods," contrasting them with "generalizability-based methods" that rely on learned priors or generative models [S4].
