# Principled Uncertainty Pruning

Also called: Fisher Information-based pruning score, PUP 3D-GS, PUP 3D-GS Pruning Score & Pipeline, Principled Uncertainty Pruning for 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) is a post-hoc method for compressing pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing Gaussians with high uncertainty regarding their contribution to the reconstruction error [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on heuristic visibility scores or opacity thresholds [S1], PUP computes a sensitivity score derived from the Fisher Information matrix of the L2 reconstruction loss, specifically approximating the Hessian with respect to spatial parameters [S1]. It employs a multi-round prune-refine pipeline to achieve high compression ratios (up to 90%) while preserving visual fidelity and foreground details better than single-round pruning or heuristic-based alternatives like LightGaussian [S1].

## How it works
PUP operates as a post-hoc pipeline on a fully converged 3D-GS model, requiring no modifications to the original training loop [S1]. It computes a pruning score for each Gaussian based on its parameter uncertainty and iteratively removes the least certain Gaussians, followed by fine-tuning to recover quality.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A converged 3D-GS model consisting of $N$ Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{\mathcal{G}_1, \dots, \mathcal{G}_N\}$, where each Gaussian $\mathcal{G}_i$ has parameters: mean $\mu_i$, scale $s_i$, rotation $q_i$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color $c_i$. A set of training views $\mathcal{P}_{gt}$ with ground truth images $I_{gt}$.
*   **Output:** A pruned set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ with reduced count $N'$, and a fine-tuned model.

### Data Structures
The method operates on the standard 3D-GS Gaussian representation. For the sensitivity score, only the spatial parameters are considered:
*   **Spatial Parameters Vector $\theta_i$:** A 6-dimensional vector containing the 3D mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and the 3D scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (assuming isotropic or diagonal covariance for the score calculation, or the principal axes if full covariance is used, but the source specifies a $6 \times 6$ matrix implying 3 for mean and 3 for scale) [S1].
*   **Fisher Information Matrix $F_i$:** A $6 \times 6$ block-diagonal matrix approximating the Hessian for Gaussian $i$ [S1].

### Step-by-Step Method

1.  **Convergence Check:** Ensure the 3D-GS model is fully converged. The method assumes the L1 residual $\|I_G - I_{gt}\|_1$ is small [S1].

2.  **Compute Rendering Gradients:** For each training view $\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}$, render the image $I_G(\phi)$ using the current Gaussians. Compute the gradient of the rendered image with respect to the spatial parameters $\theta_i$ of each Gaussian:
    $$ \nabla_{\theta_i} I_G(\phi) $$
    This gradient is a vector of size $3 \times H \times W$ (or flattened to $3HW$), representing how the pixel values change with respect to the mean and scale of Gaussian $i$ [S1].

3.  **Approximate Fisher Information Matrix:** Compute the Fisher Information matrix $F_i$ for each Gaussian $i$ by summing the outer products of the gradients over all training views. The full Hessian of the L2 loss is:
    $$ \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 L_2 = \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_G(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_G(\phi)^T + (I_G(\phi) - I_{gt}) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 I_G(\phi) $$
    Due to convergence, the second-order term $(I_G(\phi) - I_{gt}) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 I_G(\phi)$ vanishes. The Fisher approximation for Gaussian $i$ is:
    $$ F_i = \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\theta_i} I_G(\phi) \nabla_{\theta_i} I_G(\phi)^T $$
    $F_i$ is a $6 \times 6$ matrix [S1].

4.  **Compute Pruning Score:** Calculate the sensitivity score $S_i$ for each Gaussian as the log-determinant of its Fisher Information matrix:
    $$ S_i = \log \det(F_i) $$
    This score represents the entropy of the second-order Laplace approximation of the posterior distribution for that Gaussian's parameters. A higher score indicates higher uncertainty (the parameters can be perturbed more without significantly affecting the loss), making the Gaussian a candidate for pruning [S1].

5.  **Multi-Round Prune-Refine Pipeline:**
    *   **Round 1:**
        *   Sort Gaussians by $S_i$ in descending order.
        *   Prune the top 80% of Gaussians (those with the highest uncertainty scores) [S1].
        *   Fine-tune the remaining 20% of Gaussians for 5,000 optimization steps using the standard 3D-GS loss (L2 + SSIM) to recover quality [S1].
    *   **Round 2:**
        *   Re-compute the Fisher Information matrices and scores $S_i$ for the remaining Gaussians [S1].
        *   Prune the top 50% of the *remaining* Gaussians [S1].
        *   Fine-tune the survivors for another 5,000 steps [S1].
    *   **Total Pruning:** Approximately 90% of the original Gaussians are removed ($1 - 0.2 \times 0.5 = 0.9$) [S1].

        prune_percent_round2: Fraction to prune in round 2 (default 0.5).
        fine_tune_steps: Number of optimization steps per round.
        
    Returns:
        pruned_gaussians: List of remaining Gaussian objects.
    """

            det_F = np.linalg.det(F_i)
            if det_F <= 0:
                score = -np.inf # Should not happen for valid converged models
            else:
                score = np.log(det_F)
            scores.append((score, i))
        
        # Step 3: Sort and Prune
        # Sort by score descending (highest uncertainty first)
        scores.sort(key=lambda x: x[0], reverse=True)
        
        if round_idx == 0:
            num_to_prune = int(len(scores) * prune_percent_round1)
        else:
            num_to_prune = int(len(scores) * prune_percent_round2)

        # to minimize L2/SSIM loss between rendered and ground truth images.
        current_gaussians = fine_tune_gaussians(current_gaussians, training_views, fine_tune_steps)
        
    return current_gaussians

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Pruning Percentages:** 80% in Round 1, 50% in Round 2 [S1].
*   **Fine-tuning Steps:** 5,000 steps per round, 10,000 total [S1].
*   **Patch Size:** The sensitivity score can be computed over image patches (e.g., $2 \times 2$, $4 \times 4$, $8 \times 8$ pixels) to reduce memory and computation. Default is $4 \times 4$ [S1].
*   **Parameters Included:** Only mean ($\mu$) and scale ($s$) are used. Rotation and RGB color parameters are excluded [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Convergence Requirement:** The method assumes the scene is fully converged. If the L1 residual is large, the Fisher approximation (ignoring the second-order term) is inaccurate, and pruning may degrade quality [S1].
*   **Memory Constraints:** Including rotation parameters expands the Fisher matrix to $10 \times 10$, increasing memory usage by $2.78 \times$. This can cause out-of-memory errors on 16GB GPUs (e.g., RTX A4000) [S1].
*   **RGB Parameters:** Using RGB color parameters for the sensitivity score degrades image quality because the score correlates with visibility rather than reconstruction error sensitivity [S1].
*   **Degenerate Gaussians:** Gaussians with zero or near-zero gradients (e.g., occluded or very small) may have singular Fisher matrices. Adding a small regularization term to the diagonal of $F_i$ before computing the determinant is recommended [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The dominant cost is computing the Fisher matrices, which requires rendering gradients for all Gaussians over all training views. This is $O(N \cdot V \cdot HW)$, where $N$ is the number of Gaussians, $V$ is the number of views, and $H, W$ are image dimensions. Fine-tuning adds $O(N' \cdot V \cdot HW \cdot T)$, where $T$ is the number of fine-tuning steps.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory scales linearly with the number of Gaussians $N$, specifically proportional to $N \times 36$ bytes (for the $6 \times 6$ Fisher matrices) [S1]. This fits within 16GB GPUs for typical scenes [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, PUP achieves an average PSNR of 26.67, SSIM of 0.7862, and LPIPS of 0.2719 after 90% pruning, with an average FPS of 204.81 on an RTX A4000 [S1]. It provides a 3.56x average speedup compared to the original 3D-GS model [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Preserves fine details and foreground information better than heuristic methods like LightGaussian [S1]. Achieves higher compression ratios (90%) with less quality degradation than single-round pruning [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a converged model. Post-hoc pruning does not reduce the initial training cost or memory footprint of the original model [S1].

## Background
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) was introduced by Alex Hanson, Allen Tu, Vasu Singla, Mayuka Jayawardhana, Matthias Zwicker, and Tom Goldstein from the University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It was published in the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) in 2025 [S1]. The method addresses the high storage and memory requirements of 3D-GS, which can consist of millions of Gaussians, limiting deployment on resource-constrained devices [S1]. It builds on the Bayesian interpretation of the Hessian of the reconstruction loss and the Fisher Information matrix [S1].
