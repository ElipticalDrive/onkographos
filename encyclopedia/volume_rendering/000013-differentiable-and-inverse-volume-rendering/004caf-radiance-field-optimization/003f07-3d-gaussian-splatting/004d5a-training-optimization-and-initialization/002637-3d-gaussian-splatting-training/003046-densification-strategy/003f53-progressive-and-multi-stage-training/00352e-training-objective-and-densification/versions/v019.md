# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the loss function and densification protocol for **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting), a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface [S1]. It solves the problem of Gaussian splats drifting away from geometry or expanding excessively by introducing surface-aware regularization and projection losses [S1]. It differs from standard 3DGS [22] by splitting the primitive population into **tightly-bound** splats (constrained to the mesh) and **loosely-bound** splats (optimized freely), applying distinct densification and loss strategies to each [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   A set of Gaussian splats $G$, partitioned into tightly-bound splats $G^t$ and loosely-bound splats $G^l$ based on distance to a mesh surface [S1].
    *   A mesh $M$ consisting of faces $F$, where each face $f_j$ has a normal $n_f$ and vertices $v_i$ [S1].
    *   Rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and ground truth image $I_i$ [S1].
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{img}=0.001$, $\lambda_{nc}=0.1$, $\lambda_{min}=0.1$, $\lambda_{max}=10$, $\lambda_{proj}=50$, $\rho=0.1$, distance threshold $d_{th}=0.01$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Updated Gaussian parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) after optimization step.

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Splat $i$**:
    *   $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Center position.
    *   $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3) \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale parameters.
    *   $R_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Rotation matrix.
    *   $n_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Estimated normal vector.
*   **Mesh Face $f_j$**:
    *   Vertices $v_i$.
    *   Normal $n_f$.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Classification**: At every 500 training iterations, classify splats into $G^t$ or $G^l$ based on their distance to the mesh surface. If distance $< d_{th}$ ($0.01$), the splat is tightly-bound ($G^t$); otherwise, it is loosely-bound ($G^l$) [S1].
2.  **Normal Estimation**: For each tightly-bound splat $i \in G^t$, estimate its normal $n_i$ by identifying the minimum scale axis among $S_i$ and applying the rotation matrix $R_i$ to transform it into world coordinates [S1].
3.  **Projection Calculation**: For each $i \in G^t$, project the center $P_i$ to the closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$ [S1]. The projection is calculated as:
    $$ s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j $$
    *(Note: The source formula uses $f_j$ in the dot product and multiplication, implying $f_j$ acts as the surface normal or vector in this specific projection context, though standard projection usually involves the face normal. We follow the source notation exactly)* [S1].
4.  **Loss Computation**: Calculate the four loss components defined below.
5.  **Backpropagation**: Compute gradients of the total loss $L$ with respect to the Gaussian parameters and update them.
6.  **Densification**:
    *   For **loosely-bound splats** $G^l$: Apply the original 3DGS densification strategy (cloning/splitting based on position gradients) [S1].
    *   For **tightly-bound splats** $G^t$: Perform cloning or splitting by sampling points from the corresponding mesh triangle. Initialize new Gaussian splats at these sampled positions [S1].
        *   **Cloning**: Copy additional attributes from the parent splat to the new splat [S1].
        *   **Splitting**: Divide the scale of the new splats by a factor of 1.6 [S1].

### Formulas

**Image Loss** [S1]:
$$ L_{img} = (1-\lambda_{img})L_{1}(\hat{I}_{i},I_{i}) + \lambda_{img}SSIM(\hat{I}_{i}, I_{i}) $$

**Normal Consistency Loss** [S1]:
$$ L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^{t},f \in F} (1 - n_{i} \cdot n_{f}) $$
Where $n_i$ is the splat normal and $n_f$ is the face normal.

**Scale Regularization Loss** [S1]:
$$ L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^{t}}(\lambda_{min}|\text{min}(s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3})| + \lambda_{max}|\text{max}(s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3}) - \rho|) $$
Where $\rho=0.1$ is the threshold for the maximum scale [S1].

**Projection Loss** [S1]:
$$ L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^{t}}\parallel s_{i} - P_{i} \parallel _{2} $$

**Total Loss** [S1]:
$$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj} $$

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_meshgs_loss_and_densify(splats, mesh, images, gt_images, iteration):
    """
    Computes the MeshGS training objective and handles densification.
    """
    # 1. Classification (every 500 iterations)
    if iteration % 500 == 0:
        for splat in splats:
            dist = distance_to_mesh(splat.center, mesh)
            if dist < 0.01:
                splat.type = "tightly_bound"
            else:
                splat.type = "loosely_bound"

    # 2. Loss Calculation
    loss_img = compute_image_loss(images, gt_images)
    
    loss_nc = 0.0
    loss_scale = 0.0
    loss_proj = 0.0
    
    for splat in splats:
        if splat.type == "tightly_bound":
            # Estimate normal from min scale axis and rotation
            min_scale_idx = argmin(splat.scale)
            splat.normal = rotate_vector(get_basis_vector(min_scale_idx), splat.rotation)
            
            # Find closest face and normal
            face, face_normal = find_closest_face_and_normal(splat.center, mesh)
            
            # Normal Consistency
            loss_nc += (1.0 - dot(splat.normal, face_normal))
            
            # Scale Regularization
            min_s = min(splat.scale)
            max_s = max(splat.scale)
            loss_scale += (0.1 * abs(min_s) + 10.0 * abs(max_s - 0.1))
            
            # Projection Loss
            # Note: Source formula uses face vector f_j in projection calculation
            # s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) . f_j) * f_j
            # We assume face_normal is used as f_j for projection onto the plane
            v = face.vertices[0] # Pick a vertex from the face
            projection_offset = dot(splat.center - v, face_normal) * face_normal
            s_i = splat.center + projection_offset
            loss_proj += norm(s_i - splat.center)

    # Total Loss
    lambda_img = 0.001
    lambda_nc = 0.1
    lambda_proj = 50.0
    
    total_loss = loss_img + lambda_nc * loss_nc + loss_scale + lambda_proj * loss_proj
    
    # 3. Densification
    for splat in splats:
        if splat.type == "loosely_bound":
            # Original 3DGS densification (gradient-based)
            densify_3dgs(splat)
        else:
            # Tightly-bound densification (mesh-aligned)
            face, _ = find_closest_face_and_normal(splat.center, mesh)
            sampled_point = sample_point_from_triangle(face)
            
            if should_clone(splat):
                new_splat = clone_splat(splat)
                new_splat.center = sampled_point
                # Copy additional attributes as per source
            elif should_split(splat):
                new_splat = split_splat(splat)
                new_splat.center = sampled_point
                new_splat.scale = new_splat.scale / 1.6
                
    return total_loss
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Projection Ambiguity**: The source formula for $L_{proj}$ uses $f_j$ in the dot product $((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j)$. In standard geometry, projecting to a surface requires the surface normal. If $f_j$ refers to the face normal, the formula projects along the normal. If $f_j$ refers to a tangent vector, the formula is non-standard. The implementation must clarify if $f_j$ is the normal vector [S1].
*   **Scale Threshold**: The scale regularization uses a fixed threshold $\rho=0.1$. If the scene scale is significantly different, this value may need adjustment [S1].
*   **Classification Stability**: The binary classification of splats into $G^t$ and $G^l$ happens every 500 iterations. Rapid movement of splats across the threshold boundary may cause instability [S1].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity**: The additional overhead comes from the normal consistency, scale regularization, and projection loss calculations, which require iterating over $G^t$ and finding closest mesh faces. This is $O(|G^t| \cdot |F|)$ or $O(|G^t| \log |F|)$ with acceleration structures [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the mesh geometry and the classification status of each splat [S1].
*   **Performance**: The method is evaluated on Mip-NeRF360 and Deep Blending datasets, running for 30k iterations [S1]. It aims to improve rendering quality by maintaining tight alignment with the mesh [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Prevents splats from drifting away from the surface; enforces geometric consistency via normal alignment [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Relies on the quality of the input mesh; the fixed threshold $\rho$ may not adapt to varying surface details [S1].

## Background

*   **Origin**: This method is described in "MeshGS: Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting for High-Quality Rendering" [S1].
*   **History**: It extends the original 3D Gaussian Splatting [22] by incorporating explicit mesh geometry, similar to approaches like SuGaR [13] but with a specific focus on adaptive alignment and distinct handling of tightly vs. loosely bound splats [S1].
*   **Usage**: Used for high-quality rendering of large-scale real-world scenes where explicit mesh reconstruction is available or desired [S1].
