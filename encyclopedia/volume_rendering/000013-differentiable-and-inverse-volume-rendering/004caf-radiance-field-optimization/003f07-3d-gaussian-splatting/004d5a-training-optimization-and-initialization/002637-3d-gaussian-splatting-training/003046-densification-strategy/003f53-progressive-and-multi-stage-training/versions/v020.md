# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Progressive Densification, Multi-stage Optimization, Curriculum Learning for Gaussians

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to methodologies that divide the optimization of 3D Gaussian primitives into distinct temporal phases or stages, rather than optimizing a dense point cloud from scratch in a single pass. It solves the problem of poor convergence, instability, and low rendering quality associated with initializing and optimizing a dense set of primitives simultaneously, particularly in complex scenes or when using feed-forward initialization. It sets itself apart from standard single-stage training by decoupling global structure establishment from local detail refinement, or by alternating supervision signals, thereby yielding significantly higher rendering quality and stability [S1, S1, S1, S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares the principle that optimization is not monolithic. Instead, the training process is structured to manage the complexity of the primitive population over time. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Stage Separation:** Training is divided into distinct phases (e.g., coarse-to-fine, initialization-to-fine-tuning, or alternating supervision) where the objective, constraints, or primitive population changes between stages [S1, S1, S1, S1, unofficial].
2.  **Progressive Densification:** Rather than starting with the final target density, the representation begins coarse (sparse points, mesh, or feed-forward predictions) and increases density iteratively based on error metrics or predefined schedules [S1, S1, S1, unofficial].
3.  **Stabilization via Initialization:** Many variants use a preliminary phase (coarse optimization or depth network initialization) to establish a stable baseline (global structure or depth) before applying aggressive differentiable optimization [S1, S1, unofficial][S3].
4.  **Curriculum or Interleaved Supervision:** Some methods alternate between different types of supervision (e.g., voxel vs. slice, spatial vs. temporal) to prevent instability that arises from using a single supervision signal in isolation [S1, unofficial][S2].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
A strategy that divides optimization into distinct stages, initializing coarsely (sparse points or mesh) and densifying iteratively. It solves poor convergence from dense initialization by decoupling global structure from local detail refinement. It differs from siblings by using specific error-based scoring (e.g., DSSIM weighted by area) and re-meshing/re-organization steps during the fine stage. Choose when high-fidelity mesh or point cloud reconstruction from coarse inputs is required.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the loss and densification for MeshGS, aligning primitives with an explicit mesh. It solves Gaussian drift and excessive expansion by introducing surface-aware regularization and splitting primitives into tightly-bound and loosely-bound categories. It differs from standard 3DGS by applying distinct densification (mesh-sampling vs. gradient-based) and loss strategies to each category. Choose when explicit mesh geometry is available and needs to be preserved.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refers to optimizing a pre-initialized set of Gaussians (e.g., from a feed-forward network) to reconstruct a scene. It distinguishes itself by decoupling initial geometry prediction from differentiable optimization, allowing aggressive densification/pruning to remove redundancy. Choose for large-scale environments or when using pretrained models to accelerate convergence and reduce memory usage.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Structures training by alternating between sparse voxel sampling and structured slice-based sampling. It solves unstable global approximation (from voxels alone) and lack of structural continuity (from slices alone). Choose for volumetric data (e.g., MRI) where shear-warp rendering pipelines are used, ensuring both global consistency and local detail.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
- **Definition:** Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].
- **Context:** Used in GaussianPro to handle densification in a fronto-parallel manner, preventing the instability of sudden dense initialization [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
- **Step:** Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs [S2].
- **Context:** Used in adaptive spatio-temporal 3DGS to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail, which can happen if densification decisions are made independently at every iteration [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
- **Definition:** A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
- **Step:** Stage 1: Construct RGB point clouds for each input view, project them to the target view using differentiable point cloud rendering, aggregate point clouds to render RGB images at the target view, and optimize the depth network using photometric loss. Stage 2: Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) [S3].
- **Parameter:** Performance improvement of approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
- **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

## Background
The concept of multi-stage or progressive training in 3D Gaussian Splatting emerged as a response to the instability of optimizing large numbers of primitives from random or sparse initialization. Standard 3DGS [22] uses periodic densification, but this can still lead to artifacts if the initial density is insufficient or if the optimization landscape is rugged.

The **Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy** (linked) adapts techniques from mesh-based rendering to Gaussians, using error metrics to guide densification [S1, unofficial][S2]. **MeshGS** (linked) extends this by incorporating explicit mesh constraints, requiring a specialized training objective to keep Gaussians aligned with geometry [S1, unofficial]. **Fine-tuning** approaches leverage advances in feed-forward neural networks (e.g., Instant3DGS, Zero-123GS) to provide high-quality initializations, shifting the training burden to refinement and redundancy removal [S1, unofficial].

For volumetric data, **Curriculum Learning** (linked) addresses the specific challenge of 3D reconstruction from sparse or structured volumes, where standard image-based supervision is insufficient [S1, unofficial]. In dynamic scenes, **Interleaved Densification** (minor variant) addresses the coupled nature of spatial and temporal changes, ensuring that neither motion nor appearance is neglected [S2]. In multi-view stereo contexts like **FWD-U** (minor variant), two-stage training stabilizes depth estimation, which is critical for accurate 3D reconstruction [S3].

These methods collectively represent a shift from "optimize everything at once" to "structure the optimization process" to handle complexity, scale, and specific geometric constraints.
