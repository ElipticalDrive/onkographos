# Frequency Regularization for Densification

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting with Progressive Frequency Regularization, FreGS

## Overview
Frequency Regularization for Densification (FreGS) addresses the over-reconstruction issue in 3D Gaussian Splatting, where high-variance image regions are covered by a few large Gaussians, leading to blur and artifacts [S1]. Unlike standard 3D-GS which relies solely on pixel-wise L1 loss in the spatial domain, FreGS minimizes the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of the rendered image and the ground truth in the Fourier space [S1]. This approach significantly increases pixel gradients within over-reconstruction regions, enabling a coarse-to-fine densification process guided by progressive frequency annealing [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse point cloud generated by Structure-from-Motion (SfM); ground truth images $I$ for the training views; rendered images $\hat{I}$ from the current Gaussian set.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (covariance $\Sigma$, center $p$, opacity $\alpha$, spherical harmonics coefficients $c$) with densified geometry.

**Data Structures**
*   **3D Gaussian:** Defined by covariance matrix $\Sigma$, center position $p$, opacity $\alpha$, and spherical harmonics coefficients $c$ [S1]. The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is parameterized by a scaling matrix and a rotation matrix for differentiable optimization [S1].
*   **Frequency Spectrum:** Complex-valued arrays representing the amplitude $F(u, v)$ and phase $\angle F(u, v)$ of the image in the Fourier domain [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a set of 3D Gaussians from the sparse point cloud generated by Structure-from-Motion [S1].
2.  **Rendering:** Splat the 3D Gaussians to obtain 2D Gaussians and render the image $\hat{I}$ using standard $\alpha$-blending [S1]. The color $C$ of a pixel is computed by blending $N$ ordered 2D Gaussians overlapping the pixel:
    $$ C = \sum_{i \in N} c_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $c_i$ and $\alpha_i$ are derived from the $i$-th 2D Gaussian's covariance matrix, spherical harmonics coefficients, and opacity [S1].
3.  **Frequency Transformation:** Generate the frequency spectrum $\hat{F}$ of the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the frequency spectrum $F$ of the ground truth $I$ by applying a Fourier transformation [S1].
4.  **Discrepancy Computation:** Compute the discrepancy in amplitude $F(u, v)$ and phase $\angle F(u, v)$ in the Fourier space between the rendered and ground truth spectra [S1].
5.  **Filtering:** Apply a low-pass filter $H_l$ and a dynamic high-pass filter $H_h$ to extract low-to-high frequency components progressively [S1].
6.  **Densification:** Perform Gaussian densification guided by the frequency regularization [S1]:
    *   **Under-reconstruction:** Clone Gaussians in regions with missing geometric features [S1].
    *   **Over-reconstruction:** Divide large Gaussians into multiple smaller Gaussians in large high-variance regions [S1].
7.  **Frequency Annealing:** Use frequency annealing to progressively regularize from low-frequency signals to high-frequency signals, achieving coarse-to-fine Gaussian densification [S1]. Low-frequency signals encode large-scale structures (easier to model), while high-frequency signals encode local details (harder to model) [S1].
8.  **Termination:** Gaussian densification is terminated after the 15,000th iteration, consistent with the standard 3D-GS protocol [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Training Iterations:** 15,000 [S1].
*   **Frequency Annealing Schedule:** Progressive from low to high frequency [S1]. Exact schedule parameters (e.g., start/end frequencies, annealing rate) are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Over-reconstruction:** The primary target of FreGS is the case where high-variance regions are covered by few large Gaussians, causing blur [S1].
*   **Under-reconstruction:** Regions with missing geometric features are handled by cloning [S1].
*   **Precision:** The method relies on Fourier transforms, which may be sensitive to image boundaries and padding [G].
*   **Computational Cost:** Computing Fourier transforms and frequency discrepancies adds overhead to the training loop [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds overhead due to Fourier transforms and frequency discrepancy calculations at each iteration [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing frequency spectra for rendered and ground truth images [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Experiments on benchmarks like Mip-NeRF360 and Tanks-and-Temples show high-quality Gaussian densification and alleviation of over-reconstruction [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly increases pixel gradients in over-reconstruction regions compared to standard L1 loss, leading to better detail recovery [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The specific implementation details of the filters and annealing schedule are not provided, making exact performance scaling difficult to predict [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When standard 3D-GS exhibits over-reconstruction artifacts (blur in high-variance regions) [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are extremely limited, as the frequency domain operations add overhead [G].
