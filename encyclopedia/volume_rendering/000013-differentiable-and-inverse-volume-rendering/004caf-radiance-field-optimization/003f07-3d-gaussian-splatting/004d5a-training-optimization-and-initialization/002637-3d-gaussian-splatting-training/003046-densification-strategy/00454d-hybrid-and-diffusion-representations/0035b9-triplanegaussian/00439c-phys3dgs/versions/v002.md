# Phys3DGS

Also called: Physically-based 3D Gaussian Splatting for Inverse Rendering

## Overview
Phys3DGS is a physically-based inverse rendering method that resolves the "hidden Gaussian" problem, where Gaussians beneath the surface adversely affect pixel color in volume rendering, by employing deferred rendering and a mesh-3DGS hybrid representation [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard relightable 3DGS methods by decoupling geometry estimation from material learning via a two-step training process, and from naive deferred approaches by introducing specific regularizations to stabilize BRDF learning and prevent mesh extraction artifacts [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of multi-view images [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A hybrid scene representation consisting of a mesh and associated 3D Gaussians, with learned PBR parameters (albedo $\mathcal{A}$, metalness $\mathcal{M}$, roughness $\mathcal{R}$, normal $\mathcal{N}$) and baked visibility for real-time relighting [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each 3D Gaussian $i$ in the hybrid representation stores:
*   Center coordinate $\mu_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Opacity $\alpha_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Scale $s_i$ (and 3D covariance $\Sigma_i$ derived from scale and rotation) [S1, unofficial].
*   Spherical Harmonics (SH) parameters for view-dependent color [S1, unofficial].
*   Normal vector $\mathbf{n}_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   BRDF parameters: Albedo $\mathbf{a}_i$, metalness $m_i$, roughness $r_i$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Visibility parameters (SH coefficients for indirect illumination, used during training and baked post-training) [S1, unofficial].

The hybrid representation binds one 3D Gaussian to each triangle of an extracted mesh [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initial Training:** Train a vanilla 3DGS model [4] on the input images [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Opacity Culling:** Remove low-opacity Gaussians from the initial model [S1, unofficial].
3.  **SDF Regularization:** Apply Signed Distance Function (SDF)-based regularization to the remaining Gaussians to flatten them and push their centers toward the implicit surface [S1, unofficial]. This step may cause Gaussians near the surface to grow abnormally large, leading to large triangles in mesh extraction and visibility test interference [S1, unofficial]. Mitigate with **Masked Opacity Regularization** ($L_o$) to constrain opacity near the surface [S1, unofficial]. The exact formula for $L_o$ is not fully provided in the sources [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Mesh Extraction:** Extract a triangular mesh from the regularized 3DGS model [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Hybrid Representation Construction:** Assign one 3D Gaussian to each triangle of the extracted mesh [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Two-Step PBR Training:** Train PBR-related parameters on the hybrid representation using a two-step approach [S1, unofficial]. Step 6a learns geometry and base PBR parameters [S1, unofficial]. Step 6b refines the parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Deferred Rendering Pipeline (Inference/Training):**
For each pixel (ray):
1.  **Estimate Depth:** Calculate the estimated depth $\mathcal{D}$ to the surface by weighted summation of opacity and distance for all Gaussians $i$ intersecting the ray:
    $$ \mathcal{D} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i \alpha_i d_i $$
    where $T_i$ is the transmittance up to Gaussian $i$, $\alpha_i$ is the opacity, and $d_i$ is the distance from the camera center to Gaussian $i$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Estimate Surface Properties:** Calculate the estimated normal $\mathcal{N}$ and BRDF parameters (Albedo $\mathcal{A}$, Metalness $\mathcal{M}$, Roughness $\mathcal{R}$) using the same weighted summation:
    $$ \{\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{R}, \mathcal{N}\} = \sum_{i \in N} T_i \alpha_i \{\mathbf{a}_i, m_i, r_i, \mathbf{n}_i\} $$
    where $\mathbf{a}_i, m_i, r_i, \mathbf{n}_i$ are the per-Gaussian albedo, metalness, roughness, and normal [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Lighting Calculation:**
    *   Perform Fibonacci sampling on incoming light directions $\omega_i$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute Global Illumination $L_{global}(\omega_i)$ using Spherical Harmonics (SH) approximation [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute Local Indirect Illumination $L_{local}(\omega_i)$ using SH parameters stored on each Gaussian, aggregated at the estimated surface point similarly to Eqn. 3 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Determine Visibility $V(\omega_i)$:
        *   *During Training:* Compute visibility via ray tracing using a Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH), detached from back-propagation [S1, unofficial].
        *   *Post-Training (Baking):* Bake the visibility onto each Gaussian and replace the SH coefficients of indirect illumination with the SH coefficients of the baked visibility to reduce model size and boost test-time speed [S1, unofficial].
    *   Combine lighting:
        $$ L(\omega_i) = V(\omega_i) \cdot L_{global}(\omega_i) + L_{local}(\omega_i) $$
4.  **Rendering Equation:** Solve the rendering equation once at the estimated surface point using the estimated BRDF parameters and the computed incoming light $L(\omega_i)$ to obtain the final pixel color [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def phys3dgs_render(ray_origin, ray_direction, gaussians, baked_visibility_map, global_sh_coeffs, use_bvh=False, bvh=None):
    # 1. Filter and sort gaussians by distance to camera
    intersecting = [g for g in gaussians if ray_intersects_gaussian(ray_origin, ray_direction, g)]
    intersecting.sort(key=lambda g: distance(ray_origin, g.mu))
    if not intersecting:
        return [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]  # background_color [G]

    total_transmittance = 1.0
    est_depth = 0.0
    est_albedo = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    est_metalness = 0.0
    est_roughness = 0.0
    est_normal = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

    # 2. Deferred Accumulation: Exact weighted sums per Eqn 2 & 3
    for g in intersecting:
        d_i = distance(ray_origin, g.mu)
        alpha_i = g.alpha
        T_i = total_transmittance

        est_depth += T_i * alpha_i * d_i
        est_albedo = [est_albedo[j] + T_i * alpha_i * g.a[j] for j in range(3)]
        est_metalness += T_i * alpha_i * g.m
        est_roughness += T_i * alpha_i * g.r
        est_normal = [est_normal[j] + T_i * alpha_i * g.n[j] for j in range(3)]

        total_transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha_i)
        if total_transmittance < 1e-4:
            break

    # 3. Lighting Calculation
    light_dirs = fibonacci_sphere(16)
    incoming_light = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    for omega in light_dirs:
        L_global = evaluate_sh(global_sh_coeffs, omega)
        V = get_visibility(omega, est_depth, baked_visibility_map, bvh, use_bvh)
        L_local = aggregate_local_indirect(intersecting, omega)
        L_i = V * L_global + L_local
        incoming_light = [incoming_light[j] + L_i[j] for j in range(3)]
    incoming_light = [x / 16.0 for x in incoming_light]

    # 4. Solve Rendering Equation
    est_normal = normalize(est_normal)
    color = evaluate_pbr_brdf(est_albedo, est_metalness, est_roughness, est_normal, ray_direction, incoming_light)
    return color

def train_phys3dgs(images, initial_gaussians):
    # Step 1: Initial Vanilla 3DGS Training
    gaussians = train_vanilla_3dgs(images, initial_gaussians)  # Standard 3DGS optimization [4]
    
    # Step 2: Opacity Culling
    gaussians = opacity_cull(gaussians)  # Remove low-opacity Gaussians [S1, unofficial]
    
    # Step 3: SDF Regularization + Masked Opacity Regularization
    gaussians = apply_sdf_regularization(gaussians)  # Flatten and push to surface [S1, unofficial]
    gaussians = apply_masked_opacity_regularization(gaussians)  # Constrain scale growth [S1, unofficial]
    
    # Step 4: Mesh Extraction
    mesh = extract_mesh(gaussians)  # Extract from regularized Gaussians [S1, unofficial]
    
    # Step 5: Build Hybrid Representation
    hybrid_gaussians = assign_gaussians_to_mesh(mesh, gaussians)  # 1 Gaussian per triangle [S1, unofficial]
    
    # Step 6: Two-Step PBR Training
    hybrid_gaussians = train_pbr_parameters(hybrid_gaussians, images, mesh)  # Step 6a [S1, unofficial]
    hybrid_gaussians = refine_pbr_parameters(hybrid_gaussians, images, mesh)  # Step 6b [S1, unofficial]
    
    # Post-Training: Bake Visibility
    hybrid_gaussians = bake_visibility(hybrid_gaussians, mesh)  # Replace indirect SH with baked visibility SH [S1, unofficial]
    
    return hybrid_gaussians, mesh

def distance(a, b):
    return ((a[0]-b[0])**2 + (a[1]-b[1])**2 + (a[2]-b[2])**2)**0.5

def fibonacci_sphere(samples=16):
    points = []
    offset = 2.0 / samples
    increment = 3.577709437777094  # pi * (3 - sqrt(5))
    for i in range(samples):
        y = ((i * 1.0) - samples + 1.0) + offset * 0.5
        radius = (1.0 - y * y)**0.5
        theta = increment * i
        points.append([math.cos(theta)*radius, y, math.sin(theta)*radius])
    return points

def evaluate_sh(coeffs, direction):
    # Standard SH evaluation at direction [G]
    return [sum(c * y for c, y in zip(coeffs[j], direction)) for j in range(3)]

def get_visibility(omega, depth, baked_map, bvh, use_bvh):
    if use_bvh:
        return bvh.trace(omega, depth)  # Detached from backprop [S1, unofficial]
    return baked_map.lookup(omega, depth)  # Baked visibility [S1, unofficial]

def aggregate_local_indirect(gaussians, omega):
    # Weighted sum of per-Gaussian indirect SH at omega, analogous to Eqn 3 [S1, unofficial]
    acc = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    for g in gaussians:
        L_g = evaluate_sh(g.sh_indirect, omega)
        acc = [acc[j] + g.alpha * L_g[j] for j in range(3)]
    return acc

def evaluate_pbr_brdf(albedo, metalness, roughness, normal, view_dir, light):
    # Standard PBR BRDF evaluation (e.g., GGX + Schlick Fresnel) [G]
    return [albedo[j] * light[j] for j in range(3)]  # Simplified diffuse for implementation [G]

def normalize(v):
    mag = (v[0]**2 + v[1]**2 + v[2]**2)**0.5
    return [x/mag for x in v] if mag > 1e-6 else [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is more expensive than vanilla 3DGS due to mesh extraction, SDF regularization, and the two-step PBR training process [S1, unofficial]. Inference is real-time capable, comparable to standard 3DGS, as deferred rendering avoids per-Gaussian PBR evaluation and ray tracing is replaced by baked visibility [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Higher than vanilla 3DGS due to the storage of mesh data, additional PBR parameters (albedo, metalness, roughness, normal) per Gaussian, and visibility SH coefficients [S1, unofficial]. Baking visibility reduces the model size at test time by removing the need for dynamic visibility computation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Offers significantly better rendering quality under relighting compared to existing relightable 3DGS methods [S1, unofficial]. Provides better rendering quality with real-time rendering speeds compared to SOTA voxel grid-based inverse rendering methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Solves the hidden Gaussian problem, enables real-time relighting, and enables robust geometry learning via mesh hybridization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to mesh extraction quality, requires careful regularization to prevent abnormal Gaussian scales, and suffers training instability if deferred rendering is applied naively without the proposed two-step approach [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Phys3DGS was proposed by Euntae Choi and Sungjoo Yoo from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Seoul National University, South Korea [S1, unofficial]. It was published on arXiv on September 16, 2024 [S1, unofficial]. The work is motivated by the limitations of existing relightable 3DGS methods, which suffer from quality degradation under novel lighting conditions due to the hidden Gaussian problem [S1, unofficial]. It draws inspiration from SuGaR, which uses a hybrid mesh-Gaussian representation, but differs by demonstrating that simple mesh adoption fails for inverse rendering and proposing a novel deferred learning-based training approach [S1, unofficial].
