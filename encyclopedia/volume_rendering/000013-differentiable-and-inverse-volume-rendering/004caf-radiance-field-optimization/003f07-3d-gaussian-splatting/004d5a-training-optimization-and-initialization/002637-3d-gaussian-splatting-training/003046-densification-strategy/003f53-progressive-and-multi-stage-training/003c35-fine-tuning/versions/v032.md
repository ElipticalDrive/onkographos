# Fine-tuning
Also called: 3DGS Fine-tuning, Gaussian Splatting Fine-tuning

## Overview
Fine-tuning is a training stage in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that refines a pre-initialized set of Gaussian primitives to achieve high-fidelity novel view synthesis. It solves the problem of initializing a scene representation by first predicting a coarse set of primitives via a feed-forward network, then optimizing their geometry and color attributes directly. It sets itself apart from standard 3DGS by leveraging pretrained priors to accelerate convergence and by explicitly enabling the growing and pruning of primitives during the optimization phase to reduce redundancy and memory consumption [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives initialized via a direct feed-forward process. These primitives contain initial geometry (means, covariances/scales, rotations) and color attributes (spherical harmonics coefficients).
*   **Output:** Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives with refined geometry and color attributes, resulting in a scene representation with reduced redundancy and higher fidelity.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the standard 3D Gaussian primitive structure, consisting of:
*   Mean position $\mathbf{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ (parameterized as scale $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and rotation $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^4$)
*   Opacity $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$
*   Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients for color

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Predict a set of 3D Gaussian primitives for initialization via a direct feed-forward process. Both geometry and color attributes are generated by the network [S1].
2.  **Optimization:** Optimize all geometry and color attributes directly, following the vanilla 3DGS optimization pipeline [S1]. This involves differentiable rasterization and gradient descent.
3.  **Densification and Pruning:** Enable the growing (splitting/cloning) and pruning of 3D primitives during fine-tuning. This step is critical because the initial feed-forward Gaussians are often redundant [S1].
4.  **Convergence:** The process continues until convergence, leveraging pretrained priors to accelerate training and achieve photorealism [S1].

**Formulas:**
The optimization follows the vanilla 3DGS loss function, typically combining L1 loss and D-SSIM loss. While the specific loss formula is not explicitly detailed in the provided text, it is stated to follow "vanilla 3DGS" [S1]. The EVolSplat paper mentions a general loss structure involving SSIM and entropy regularization in Equation (11):
$$ \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{ssim} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ssim}} + \lambda_e \mathcal{L}_{\text{entropy}} $$
where $\lambda_{ssim}$ and $\lambda_e$ are weighting factors [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Libraries:** `torchsparse` for 3D sparse convolution and `gsplat` for Gaussian rasterization [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Redundancy:** The initial feed-forward Gaussians are redundant. Without explicit pruning, memory consumption remains high and fidelity may suffer due to over-parameterization [S1].
*   **Convergence Speed:** While pretrained priors accelerate convergence, the method relies on the quality of the initial feed-forward prediction [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# Initialize Gaussians using a feed-forward network
gaussians = feed_forward_predictor(scene_images)

# Configure optimizer
optimizer = Adam(params=gaussians.parameters(), lr=1e-3)

# Training loop
for iteration in range(total_iterations):
    # Sample a random camera view
    camera = sample_camera_view()
    
    # Render image using differentiable rasterization
    rendered_image = gaussian_rasterization(gaussians, camera)
    
    # Compute loss (following vanilla 3DGS)
    # Note: Specific loss weights not provided in source [S1]
    loss = compute_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image)
    
    # Backpropagate and update
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    
    # Enable growing and pruning during fine-tuning
    # This step manages the number of Gaussians to reduce redundancy
    if should_densify(iteration):
        gaussians = densify_and_prune(gaussians, iteration)

return gaussians
```

## Subtopics

### [Exponentially Ascending Gradient Threshold](9684-exponentially-ascending-gradient-threshold.md)
A densification control mechanism that uses an exponentially ascending gradient threshold to regulate splitting and cloning, solving the trade-off between early-stage convergence speed and late-stage overfitting.

### [SplatFormer Training Objective & Refinement](11745-splatformer-training-objective-refinement.md)
A learning-based, feed-forward 3D neural module that refines initial 3DGS representations to enable robust novel view synthesis from out-of-distribution (OOD) camera angles.

### [MCMC-based 3DGS / SGLD for 3DGS](11917-mcmc-based-3dgs-sgld-for-3dgs.md)
Reformulates 3DGS by treating Gaussians as MCMC samples, converting updates into Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) to remove the need for manual densification heuristics.

### [Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting](12318-deblurring-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
A real-time deblurring framework that reconstructs sharp 3D scenes from blurry input images by manipulating covariance matrices and means using a small MLP.

### [Supplementary Point Addition for Sparse Point Clouds](12323-supplementary-point-addition-for-sparse-point-clouds.md)
A densification technique that supplements the initial point cloud with synthetic points to compensate for sparsity in defocus-blurry environments.

### [Gaussian Volume Mask](12710-gaussian-volume-mask.md)
A densification strategy that reduces the number of active Gaussians by applying binary masks to scale and opacity attributes based on a learnable parameter.

### [Augmented Radiance Field](13218-augmented-radiance-field.md)
A post-enhancement framework that improves 3DGS scenes by adding Gaussian kernels with view-dependent opacity to explicitly model specular components.

### [Frequency Regularization for Densification](13484-frequency-regularization-for-densification.md)
Addresses over-reconstruction by minimizing the discrepancy between the frequency spectrum of the rendered image and the ground truth in Fourier space.

### [Visibility-Based Pruning for 3D Gaussian Splatting](14019-visibility-based-pruning-for-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Removes occluded 3D Gaussian kernels during optimization to create a more compact, 'hard' geometry representation.

### [Error-Guided Redistribution](14037-error-guided-redistribution.md)
A family of strategies that refines primitive allocation based on reconstruction error and gradient analysis, rather than relying solely on standard adaptive density control.

### [CoherentGS](14072-coherentgs.md)
A regularized optimization approach that introduces coherency to an explicit 3D Gaussian representation by constraining Gaussian movement in 2D image space.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Accelerated convergence compared to test-time optimization methods due to pretrained priors [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly reduced memory consumption compared to the initial feed-forward state, as redundant Gaussians are pruned [S1].
*   **Performance:** Achieves photorealism on par with or surpassing other methods [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fast convergence, high fidelity, reduced memory footprint through pruning.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the initial feed-forward prediction; requires careful tuning of densification/pruning thresholds to avoid artifacts.
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity synthesis is required and computational resources for initial dense initialization are limited or when leveraging pretrained models is beneficial.
*   **When not to use:** When the initial feed-forward prediction is poor or when real-time inference speed is the primary constraint and pruning overhead is unacceptable.

## Background
Fine-tuning in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the EVolSplat paper, which proposes an efficient volume-based Gaussian Splatting method for urban view synthesis [S1]. The method leverages pretrained weights to accelerate network training and enable faster convergence compared to other test-time optimization methods [S1]. It addresses the issue of redundant initial Gaussians by enabling growing and pruning during the fine-tuning phase [S1].
