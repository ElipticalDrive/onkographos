# Error-Guided Gaussian Reallocation

Also called: EGR

## Overview
Error-Guided Gaussian Reallocation (EGR) is a training-stage module that redistributes a fixed budget of Gaussian primitives toward regions with persistent coarse-scale reconstruction errors, without increasing the total number of Gaussians [S1]. It refines post-densification Gaussian allocation by moving low-contribution (LC) Gaussians to high-error (HE) regions within a fixed Gaussian set, using contribution- and error-aware selection [S1]. Unlike methods using explicit rollback or separate re-correction modules, EGR uses adaptive relocation count, parameter reset, and continued optimization as its stabilization strategy [S1].

## How it works
EGR operates after densification is complete, once the Gaussian count has been fixed [S1]. It identifies paired LC and HE Gaussians based on statistics recomputed from the current model using all scale-1 training cameras [S1]. The process involves identifying candidates, selecting pairs, relocating attributes, and rescaling opacities to preserve transmittance [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** The current set of $N$ Gaussians with parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients, ACO offsets), optimizer states, and the current training iteration count.
*   **Output:** The same set of $N$ Gaussians with updated positions, attributes, and optimizer states for the relocated pairs.

### Data Structures
The Gaussian set is stored as an array of $N$ elements. Each Gaussian $i$ contains:
*   $\mathbf{p}_i$: Position (3D vector).
*   $\mathbf{s}_i$: Scale (3D vector or quaternion/scale representation).
*   $\mathbf{q}_i$: Rotation (Quaternion).
*   $\alpha_i$: Opacity (scalar).
*   $\mathbf{c}_{i,0}$: Base color / DC SH coefficient.
*   $\mathbf{c}_{i,>0}$: Higher-order SH coefficients.
*   $\Delta_{i,s}$: ACO offsets (if applicable).
*   Optimizer state (momentum, variance, etc.).

### Steps

1.  **Compute Blending Weights:** For each view $v \in V$ (where $V$ is the set of scale-1 training cameras), compute the normalized blending weight $\tilde{w}_i(v)$ for each Gaussian $i$.
    $$ \tilde{w}_i(v) = \frac{w_i(v)}{\sum_{i'} w_{i'}(v) + \epsilon} $$
    where $w_i(v)$ is the blending weight of Gaussian $i$ in view $v$, $i'$ indexes projected Gaussians in view $v$, and $\epsilon$ is a small positive constant [S1].

2.  **Compute Contribution Metrics:** Calculate the mean contribution $C_i^{mean}$ and maximum contribution $C_i^{max}$ for each Gaussian over the views $V$.
    $$ C_i^{mean} = \frac{1}{|V|} \sum_{v \in V} \tilde{w}_i(v) $$
    $$ C_i^{max} = \max_{v \in V} \tilde{w}_i(v) $$
    where $|V|$ is the number of views [S1].

3.  **Compute Gradient Magnitude:** Calculate the mean normalized gradient magnitude $G_i^{mean}$ for each Gaussian. First, compute the $L_2$ loss for view $v$:
    $$ L_2(v) = \frac{1}{3H_v W_v} \sum_{h,w,\ell} (\hat{I}_{h,w,\ell}(v) - I_{h,w,\ell}(v))^2 $$
    where $H_v, W_v$ are image height and width, $\hat{I}$ is rendered color, $I$ is ground truth, and $\ell$ is the RGB channel. Then compute the normalized gradient:
    $$ G_i^{mean} = \frac{1}{|V|} \sum_{v \in V} \frac{\|\nabla_{\theta_i} L_2(v)\|}{\sum_{i'} \|\nabla_{\theta'_i} L_2(v)\| + \epsilon} $$
    where $\nabla_{\theta_i} L_2(v)$ is the parameter gradient of Gaussian $i$ from this loss, and the denominator normalizes gradient magnitude within each view [S1].

4.  **Robust Z-Score Normalization:** Apply robust z-score normalization to $C_i^{mean}$, $C_i^{max}$, and $G_i^{mean}$ to reduce sensitivity to extreme values. For a measure $x$ (vector of values for all Gaussians):
    $$ z(x) = \frac{x - \text{median}(x)}{\text{median}(|x - \text{median}(x)|) + \epsilon} $$
    where $\text{median}(x)$ is the median of measure $x$ over all Gaussians [S1].

5.  **Compute LC Score:** Assign a score $s_i^{LC}$ to each Gaussian. High scores indicate low contribution and low gradient magnitude.
    $$ s_i^{LC} = \exp(-z(C_i^{mean}) - z(C_i^{max}) - z(G_i^{mean})) $$
    Gaussians with small normalized mean contribution, max contribution, and gradient magnitude receive high scores [S1].

6.  **Compute HE Score:** Assign a score $s_i^{HE}$ based on the rank of the error-weighted contribution $C_i^{HE}$ (derived from reconstruction error and contribution, as described in the source context for HE evidence) among all Gaussians.
    $$ s_i^{HE} = \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{j=1}^N \mathbb{1}[C_j^{HE} < C_i^{HE}] $$
    where $N$ is the number of Gaussians and $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function. This is a percentile score; a Gaussian with larger $C_i^{HE}$ receives a score closer to one [S1].

7.  **Determine Number of Pairs:** Calculate the effective sample sizes for LC and HE distributions:
    $$ M_{eff}^{LC} = \frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^N (p_j^{LC})^2}, \quad M_{eff}^{HE} = \frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^N (p_j^{HE})^2} $$
    where $p_j^{LC}$ and $p_j^{HE}$ are the probabilities derived from the scores (normalized). The number of relocated pairs $M$ is:
    $$ M = \min(M_{eff}^{LC}, M_{eff}^{HE}) $$
    rounded down [S1].

8.  **Select Pairs:** Select the $M$ Gaussians with the highest $s_i^{LC}$ scores as LC candidates and the $M$ Gaussians with the highest $s_i^{HE}$ scores as HE candidates. Pair them (e.g., by rank order) [S1].

9.  **Relocate:** For each pair $(LC, HE)$:
    *   Relocate the LC Gaussian to the position of the HE Gaussian: $\mathbf{p}_{LC} \leftarrow \mathbf{p}_{HE}$.
    *   Copy the HE Gaussian’s scale, orientation, and base color to the LC Gaussian: $\mathbf{s}_{LC} \leftarrow \mathbf{s}_{HE}$, $\mathbf{q}_{LC} \leftarrow \mathbf{q}_{HE}$, $\mathbf{c}_{LC,0} \leftarrow \mathbf{c}_{HE,0}$.
    *   Reset the relocated Gaussian’s higher-order SH coefficients and ACO offsets to zero: $\mathbf{c}_{LC,>0} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}$, $\Delta_{LC,s} \leftarrow 0$.
    *   Clear the optimizer state of the relocated Gaussian [S1].

10. **Rescale Opacities:** Rescale the opacities of the relocated LC Gaussian and the original HE Gaussian to preserve the original transmittance. This uses the opacity split method from 3DGS-MCMC [21]. Let $\alpha_{LC}$ and $\alpha_{HE}$ be the original opacities. The new opacities $\alpha'_{LC}$ and $\alpha'_{HE}$ are computed such that the combined transmittance remains consistent with the pre-relocation state, accounting for the fact that they now share the same position [S1].

11. **Resume Optimization:** Resume standard gradient-based optimization immediately after relocation. The paired Gaussians retain separate parameters and are independently optimized during subsequent training [S1].

def error_guided_reallocation(gaussians, views, iteration, config):
    """
    Performs Error-Guided Gaussian Reallocation.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: List of Gaussian objects with attributes:
                   pos, scale, rot, opacity, sh_dc, sh_other, aco_offsets, optimizer_state
        views: List of training views (scale-1 cameras)
        iteration: Current training iteration
        config: Configuration dict with thresholds, epsilon, etc.
        
    Returns:
        Updated gaussians list
    """
    N = len(gaussians)
    epsilon = config.epsilon
    V = len(views)
    
    # Step 1: Compute normalized blending weights for all Gaussians in all views
    # w_i(v) is assumed to be computed by the renderer's forward pass
    normalized_weights = np.zeros((N, V))
    for v_idx, view in enumerate(views):
        # Get blending weights w_i(v) for all i in this view
        w = get_blending_weights(gaussians, view) 
        # Normalize
        total_w = np.sum(w) + epsilon
        normalized_weights[:, v_idx] = w / total_w
        
    # Step 2: Compute Contribution Metrics
    C_mean = np.mean(normalized_weights, axis=1) # Mean over views
    C_max = np.max(normalized_weights, axis=1)   # Max over views
    
    # Step 3: Compute Gradient Magnitude
    # Compute L2 loss gradients for each Gaussian in each view
    grad_mags = np.zeros((N, V))
    for v_idx, view in enumerate(views):
        # Get gradients for all Gaussians in this view
        grads = get_gradients(gaussians, view) # Shape (N, param_dim)
        grad_norms = np.linalg.norm(grads, axis=1)
        
        # Normalize by sum of norms in the view
        total_grad = np.sum(grad_norms) + epsilon
        grad_mags[:, v_idx] = grad_norms / total_grad
        
    G_mean = np.mean(grad_mags, axis=1) # Mean over views
    
    # Step 4: Robust Z-Score Normalization
    def robust_z_score(x):
        med = np.median(x)
        mad = np.median(np.abs(x - med)) + epsilon
        return (x - med) / mad
    
    z_C_mean = robust_z_score(C_mean)
    z_C_max = robust_z_score(C_max)
    z_G_mean = robust_z_score(G_mean)
    
    # Step 5: Compute LC Score
    # High score for low contribution/gradient
    s_LC = np.exp(-z_C_mean - z_C_max - z_G_mean)
    
    # Step 6: Compute HE Score
    # Requires C_HE (error-weighted contribution). Assuming this is computed similarly to C_mean but weighted by error.
    # For implementation, we assume get_HE_scores() returns the percentile scores directly or the raw C_HE values.
    # Here we simulate the percentile calculation based on C_HE values.
    C_HE = compute_HE_scores(gaussians, views) # Placeholder for specific HE metric calculation
    # Percentile score: fraction of Gaussians with lower C_HE
    s_HE = np.zeros(N)
    for i in range(N):
        count_lower = np.sum(C_HE < C_HE[i])
        s_HE[i] = count_lower / (N - 1)
        
    # Step 7: Determine Number of Pairs M
    # Effective sample size calculation requires probabilities p_j.
    # Normalize scores to probabilities
    p_LC = s_LC / np.sum(s_LC)
    p_HE = s_HE / np.sum(s_HE)
    
    M_eff_LC = 1.0 / np.sum(p_LC**2)
    M_eff_HE = 1.0 / np.sum(p_HE**2)
    M = int(min(M_eff_LC, M_eff_HE))
    
    if M == 0:
        return gaussians
        
    # Step 8: Select Pairs
    # Get indices of top M LC and top M HE
    idx_LC = np.argsort(s_LC)[-M:] # Indices of highest LC scores
    idx_HE = np.argsort(s_HE)[-M:] # Indices of highest HE scores
    
    # Pair them (e.g., by rank order)
    pairs = list(zip(idx_LC, idx_HE))
    
    # Step 9 & 10: Relocate and Rescale
    for lc_idx, he_idx in pairs:
        lc_g = gaussians[lc_idx]
        he_g = gaussians[he_idx]
        
        # Relocate LC to HE position
        lc_g.pos = he_g.pos.copy()
        
        # Copy scale, orientation, base color
        lc_g.scale = he_g.scale.copy()
        lc_g.rot = he_g.rot.copy()
        lc_g.sh_dc = he_g.sh_dc.copy()
        
        # Reset higher-order SH and ACO
        lc_g.sh_other = np.zeros_like(lc_g.sh_other)
        lc_g.aco_offsets = np.zeros_like(lc_g.aco_offsets)
        
        # Clear optimizer state
        clear_optimizer_state(lc_g.optimizer_state)
        
        # Rescale opacities to preserve transmittance
        # Using 3DGS-MCMC opacity split logic [21]
        alpha_lc_old = lc_g.opacity
        alpha_he_old = he_g.opacity
        
        # Calculate new opacities based on shared position
        # The exact formula from 3DGS-MCMC is referenced here.
        # Typically, if two Gaussians share position, their combined transmittance
        # T = (1-alpha1)(1-alpha2) should be preserved or adjusted.
        # EGR rescales opacities of the paired Gaussians to preserve original transmittance.
        alpha_lc_new, alpha_he_new = rescale_opacities_mcmc(alpha_lc_old, alpha_he_old)
        
        lc_g.opacity = alpha_lc_new
        he_g.opacity = alpha_he_new
        
    return gaussians

### Parameters
*   **Fixed primitive budget:** The total number of Gaussians remains constant during reallocation; no new Gaussians are added [S1].
*   **Reallocation interval:** 1000 iterations [S1].
*   **Reallocation window:** Iterations 16,000 to 25,000 [S1].
*   **Number of reallocation rounds:** 10 [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degenerate Input:** If densification is still active, EGR should not run. It is explicitly disabled during densification (iterations 500-15,000) and only runs after the Gaussian count is fixed [S1].
*   **Precision:** The use of $\epsilon$ in denominators prevents division by zero when contributions or gradients are zero [S1].
*   **Extreme Values:** Robust z-score normalization is used to reduce sensitivity to extreme values in contribution and gradient metrics [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** EGR requires recomputing statistics (contributions, gradients) over all training views for all Gaussians. This is $O(N \cdot |V|)$ per round. With $N$ Gaussians, $|V|$ views, and $R$ rounds, the total overhead is $O(R \cdot N \cdot |V|)$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing intermediate statistics ($C^{mean}, C^{max}, G^{mean}, s^{LC}, s^{HE}$) for all Gaussians, which is $O(N)$.
*   **Measured Performance:** Experiments on MS-GS with three real-scene benchmarks demonstrate improved multi-scale rendering quality, particularly at coarse scales [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method scales with the number of Gaussians and views. The adaptive relocation count $M$ limits the perturbation, making it stable even for large $N$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves coarse-scale rendering quality without increasing the primitive count. Stabilizes training by redirecting primitives to hard regions.
*   **Limitations:** Only applicable after densification is complete. Requires careful tuning of the reallocation window and interval.
*   **When to use:** When coarse-scale reconstruction errors persist after densification and a fixed Gaussian budget is desired.
*   **When not to use:** During densification (iterations 500-15,000) or when increasing the number of Gaussians is preferred over reallocation.

## Background
EGR was introduced in the paper "Adaptive-Scale Color Offset and Error-Guided Gaussian Reallocation for Multi-Scale 3D Gaussian Splatting" (Mathematics 2026, 14, 2713) [S1]. It is part of the ACO-EGR framework, which jointly addresses scale-dependent color mismatch and primitive-allocation imbalance. EGR specifically targets the issue of primitive-allocation imbalance in multi-scale 3DGS, where shared representations require placement criteria that reflect coarse-scale rendering errors [S1].
