# Substitutive LOD with K-wise Moment-Matched Merging

Also called: Cost-aware merging, Cost-increment relocation, Gaussian Hierarchy, Greedy hierarchical LOD, Joint relaxation LOD, Level of Detail (LOD) via Gaussian Merging, Merged Gaussian LOD

## Overview
Substitutive LOD with K-wise moment-matched merging is a technique for reducing the number of 3D Gaussians in a scene by merging groups of $K$ splats into a single representative Gaussian, thereby boosting rendering performance and managing memory constraints [S1, unofficial][S2]. It addresses the problem of excessive Gaussian counts and associated aliasing artifacts by replacing small or redundant Gaussians with larger, merged ones [S2]. Unlike methods that modify Gaussians during reconstruction or prune based on static importance, this method operates on the final set of Gaussians using cost metrics to guide the merging process, allowing for runtime LOD selection [S1, unofficial][S2]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [CoR-GS](1486-cor-gs.md) and [EffGS](3365-effgs.md) by focusing on substitutive merging via moment matching rather than density control or vector quantization [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$, where each Gaussian $g_i$ is defined by its mean $\mu_i$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (or Cholesky factor $R_i$), and opacity/amplitude $\alpha_i$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
**Outputs:** A reduced set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ with $N' < N$ Gaussians, where each output Gaussian is a moment-matched merge of $K$ input Gaussians [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The implementation relies on a spatial partitioning structure (e.g., grid or octree) to identify candidate groups of $K$ Gaussians for merging [S1, unofficial]. Each Gaussian is stored with its parameters:
- $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
- $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix (symmetric positive definite).
- $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity or amplitude.
- Optional: Color coefficients (not detailed in sources for the merging math) [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Parameters:**
- $K$: The number of Gaussians to merge into one. Compression factors of four and above are used in experiments [S1, unofficial].
- Cost Metric: One of L2, Cauchy–Schwarz, gKL, W2, MMD, or L∞ [S1, unofficial].
- Partitioning Algorithm: One of Greedy Hierarchical, Cost-Increment Relocation, or Joint Relaxation [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Spatial Partitioning:** Divide the scene into spatial bins or tiles to group nearby Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
2. **Group Selection:** Identify groups of $K$ Gaussians within each bin that are candidates for merging. The selection is guided by the chosen cost metric and partitioning algorithm [S1, unofficial].
3. **Merging:** For each group of $K$ Gaussians, compute a single merged Gaussian using moment matching. The merged mean $\mu'$ and covariance $\Sigma'$ are computed as the weighted average of the input means and covariances, weighted by their amplitudes $\alpha_i$ [G]. The merged amplitude $\alpha'$ is optimized to minimize the L2 error between the merged Gaussian and the sum of the original Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
4. **Replacement:** Replace the $K$ input Gaussians with the single merged Gaussian in the scene representation [S1, unofficial].
5. **Iteration:** Repeat steps 2–4 until the desired compression factor is reached or no further beneficial merges can be found [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- Merged mean: $\mu' = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i \mu_i}{\sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i}$ [G].
- Merged covariance: $\Sigma' = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i (\Sigma_i + (\mu_i - \mu')(\mu_i - \mu')^T)}{\sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i}$ [G].
- L2-optimal amplitude: $\alpha' = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^K \alpha_i \mathcal{N}(\mu'; \mu_i, \Sigma_i + \Sigma')}{\mathcal{N}(\mu'; \mu', 2\Sigma')}$, where $\mathcal{N}(\mu_a; \mu_b, \Sigma_{sum})$ is the Gaussian PDF evaluated at $\mu_a$ with mean $\mu_b$ and covariance $\Sigma_{sum}$ [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def merge_gaussians(group):
    """Merges a group of K Gaussians into one using moment matching."""
    total_alpha = sum(g.alpha for g in group)
    mu_prime = [sum(g.alpha * g.mu[j] for g in group) / total_alpha for j in range(3)]
    sigma_prime = [[0.0]*3 for _ in range(3)]
    for g in group:
        diff = [g.mu[j] - mu_prime[j] for j in range(3)]
        for r in range(3):
            for c in range(3):
                sigma_prime[r][c] += g.alpha * (g.sigma[r][c] + diff[r] * diff[c])
    for r in range(3):
        for c in range(3):
            sigma_prime[r][c] /= total_alpha
    
    numerator = 0.0
    for g in group:
        sum_cov = [[g.sigma[r][c] + sigma_prime[r][c] for c in range(3)] for r in range(3)]
        numerator += g.alpha * gaussian_pdf(mu_prime, sum_cov)
    denom_cov = [[2 * sigma_prime[r][c] for c in range(3)] for r in range(3)]
    denominator = gaussian_pdf(mu_prime, denom_cov)
    alpha_prime = numerator / denominator
    return (mu_prime, sigma_prime, alpha_prime)

def gaussian_pdf(mu, sigma):
    diff = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    inv = invert_3x3(sigma)
    det = det_3x3(sigma)
    exponent = -0.5 * sum(diff[j] * sum(inv[j][k] * diff[k] for k in range(3)) for j in range(3))
    return (1.0 / ((2 * 3.14159265358979) ** 1.5 * (det ** 0.5))) * (2.718281828459045 ** exponent)

def invert_3x3(M):
    d = det_3x3(M)
    return [[(M[1][1]*M[2][2]-M[1][2]*M[2][1])/d, (M[0][2]*M[2][1]-M[0][1]*M[2][2])/d, (M[0][1]*M[1][2]-M[0][2]*M[1][1])/d],
            [(M[1][2]*M[2][0]-M[1][0]*M[2][2])/d, (M[0][0]*M[2][2]-M[0][2]*M[2][0])/d, (M[0][2]*M[1][0]-M[0][0]*M[1][2])/d],
            [(M[1][0]*M[2][1]-M[1][1]*M[2][0])/d, (M[0][1]*M[2][0]-M[0][0]*M[2][1])/d, (M[0][0]*M[1][1]-M[0][1]*M[1][0])/d]]

def det_3x3(M):
    return M[0][0]*(M[1][1]*M[2][2]-M[1][2]*M[2][1]) - M[0][1]*(M[1][0]*M[2][2]-M[1][2]*M[2][0]) + M[0][2]*(M[1][0]*M[2][1]-M[1][1]*M[2][0])

def substitutive_lod(gaussians, K, metric, algorithm):
    """Performs substitutive LOD by merging Gaussians."""
    bins = {}
    for g in gaussians:
        bin_key = spatial_hash(g.mu)
        bins.setdefault(bin_key, []).append(g)
    
    merged_gaussians = []
    for bin_key, bin_gaussians in bins.items():
        if len(bin_gaussians) >= K:
            groups = select_groups_from_bin(bin_gaussians, K, metric, algorithm)
            for group in groups:
                merged_g = merge_gaussians(group)
                merged_gaussians.append(merged_g)
            remaining = bin_gaussians[len(groups)*K:]
            for g in remaining:
                merged_gaussians.append(g)
        else:
            merged_gaussians.extend(bin_gaussians)
    return merged_gaussians
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Spectral lower bounds have explicit limits and counterexamples where they fail to accurately predict merging quality [S1, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise when computing the L2-optimal amplitude, especially for very small or very large Gaussians [G].
- Boundary effects in spatial partitioning may lead to suboptimal merging if Gaussians near bin boundaries are not considered [G].

## Subtopics

### Greedy Hierarchical LOD
A partitioning algorithm that merges Gaussians hierarchically, similar to the Runnalls algorithm [S1, unofficial].

### Cost-Increment Relocation
A partitioning algorithm that relocates Gaussians to minimize the cost increment of merging. It matches greedy merging within 5.5% at compression factors of four and above [S1, unofficial].

### Joint Relaxation
A partitioning algorithm that relaxes the merging constraints to find a globally optimal solution [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Depends on the partitioning algorithm and the cost metric.
- **Memory Complexity**: Reduces the number of Gaussians by a factor of $K$, thus reducing memory usage by approximately $1/K$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance**: Cost-increment relocation matches greedy merging within 5.5% at compression factors of four and above [S1, unofficial]. Cost-aware merging beats mass-ranked culling with and without amplitude refit on full fits, while culling wins in the median on sparse subsamples [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Effective at reducing aliasing and managing memory footprint, especially on mobile devices [S2].
- **Limitations**: Spectral lower bounds may fail to predict merging quality accurately [S1, unofficial].
- **When to Use**: When memory constraints are tight or rendering performance needs to be boosted without modifying the reconstruction process [S2].
- **When Not to Use**: When high fidelity is required and compression artifacts are unacceptable [G].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of Gaussian splatting for microscopy and scalable interactive web visualization [S1, unofficial]. It builds on earlier work by Yan et al. [24] who combined Gaussians to solve aliasing problems, and Kerbl et al. [18] who introduced a hierarchy of Gaussians [S2]. The technique is part of the broader effort to make 3D Gaussian Splatting more efficient and scalable, particularly for mobile and web-based applications [S2].
