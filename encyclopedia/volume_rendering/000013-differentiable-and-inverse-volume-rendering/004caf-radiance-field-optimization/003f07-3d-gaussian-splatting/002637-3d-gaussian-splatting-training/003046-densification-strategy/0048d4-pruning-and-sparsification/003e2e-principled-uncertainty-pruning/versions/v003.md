# Principled Uncertainty Pruning

Also called: Fisher Information-based pruning score, PUP 3D-GS, PUP 3D-GS Pruning Score & Pipeline, Principled Uncertainty Pruning for 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) is a post-hoc method for compressing pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing redundant Gaussians based on their sensitivity to reconstruction error [S1]. Unlike heuristic approaches that rely on opacity or visibility, PUP computes a principled uncertainty score derived from the Fisher Information matrix of the spatial parameters (mean and scale) [S1]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md) by using a second-order approximation of the loss landscape rather than accumulated rendering weights, enabling higher compression ratios (up to 90%) while preserving fine foreground details [S1].

## How it works
PUP operates on a converged 3D-GS model $\mathcal{G} = \{g_1, \dots, g_N\}$ and a set of training views $\mathcal{P}_{gt}$ with ground truth images $I_{gt}$ [S1]. The method computes a pruning score for each Gaussian, selects the least sensitive ones for removal, and refines the remaining model through a multi-round prune-refine pipeline [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Pretrained set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$. Each Gaussian $g_i$ has parameters: mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, scale $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (or covariance $\Sigma_i$), rotation $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, and color $c_i$ [S1].
    *   Training view poses $\mathcal{P}_{gt}$ and corresponding ground truth images $I_{gt}$ [S1].
    *   Pruning parameters: patch size $K \times K$ (default 4), per-round pruning percentages (80% then 50%), and fine-tuning iterations (5,000 per round) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Pruned set of Gaussians $\mathcal{G}'$ with reduced cardinality [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Gaussian Parameters:** Stored in contiguous GPU buffers. For the sensitivity score, only the 6 spatial parameters (3 for mean, 3 for scale) are used [S1].
*   **Fisher Matrix Block:** For each Gaussian $g_i$, a $6 \times 6$ matrix $F_i$ is computed [S1].
*   **Pruning Score:** A scalar $S_i = \log \det(F_i)$ for each Gaussian [S1].

### Step-by-Step Method

1.  **Compute L2 Loss:** Calculate the total L2 error over all training views [S1]:
    $$ L_2 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \| I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt} \|_2^2 $$
    where $I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)$ is the rendered image at pose $\phi$ [S1].

2.  **Approximate Hessian via Fisher Information:**
    The exact Hessian of the L2 loss is:
    $$ \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 L_2 = \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T + (I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) - I_{gt}) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}}^2 I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) $$
    On a converged model, the residual term $\| I_{\mathcal{G}} - I_{gt} \|$ approaches zero, causing the second-order term to vanish [S1]. The Hessian is approximated by the Fisher Information matrix:
    $$ F \approx \sum_{\phi \in \mathcal{P}_{gt}} \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi) \nabla_{\mathcal{G}} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)^T $$
    This approximation assumes the error follows a Gaussian distribution and relates to the Laplace approximation of the posterior [S1].

3.  **Compute Block-Wise Fisher Matrix:**
    To reduce memory usage and improve accuracy, compute the Fisher matrix block-wise for each Gaussian $g_i$ using only its spatial mean and scale parameters (6 parameters) [S1]. Exclude rotation and color parameters [S1].
    *   Compute gradients $\nabla_{\mu_i} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)$ and $\nabla_{s_i} I_{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)$ for all training views $\phi$ [S1].
    *   Accumulate the outer products to form the $6 \times 6$ matrix $F_i$ for each Gaussian [S1].
    *   Memory complexity is $O(N \times 36)$ bytes, where $N$ is the number of Gaussians [S1].

4.  **Compute Sensitivity Score:**
    For each Gaussian $g_i$, compute the pruning score as the log-determinant of its Fisher block:
    $$ S_i = \log \det(F_i) $$
    This score represents the entropy of the second-order Laplace approximation of the posterior distribution for that Gaussian's spatial parameters [S1]. Lower scores indicate higher uncertainty/redundancy [S1].

5.  **Multi-Round Prune-Refine Pipeline:**
    Execute the following rounds to achieve high compression (e.g., 90%) [S1]:
    *   **Round 1:**
        1.  Sort Gaussians by $S_i$ ascending.
        2.  Remove the bottom 80% of Gaussians [S1].
        3.  Fine-tune the remaining Gaussians for 5,000 iterations using gradient descent on the L2 loss [S1].
    *   **Round 2:**
        1.  Recompute the sensitivity scores $S_i$ for the remaining Gaussians (since parameters changed) [S1].
        2.  Remove the bottom 50% of the *remaining* Gaussians [S1].
        3.  Fine-tune for another 5,000 iterations [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def principled_uncertainty_pruning(Gaussians, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, 
                                   patch_size=4, prune_pct_round1=0.8, 
                                   prune_pct_round2=0.5, fine_tune_iters=5000):
    """
    Performs multi-round pruning of 3D Gaussians using Fisher Information.
    
    Args:
        Gaussians: List of Gaussian objects with attributes .mean, .scale, .render()
        TrainingViews: List of camera poses
        GroundTruthImages: List of target images corresponding to TrainingViews
        patch_size: Size of image patches for gradient computation (default 4)
        prune_pct_round1: Fraction to prune in first round (default 0.8)
        prune_pct_round2: Fraction to prune in second round (default 0.5)
        fine_tune_iters: Number of fine-tuning steps per round (default 5000)
        
    Returns:
        Pruned list of Gaussians
    """
    
    # Round 1
    scores_round1 = compute_fisher_scores(Gaussians, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, patch_size)
    indices_to_keep_round1 = select_top_k(Gaussians, scores_round1, keep_fraction=1.0 - prune_pct_round1)
    Gaussians_round1 = [Gaussians[i] for i in indices_to_keep_round1]
    
    # Fine-tune Round 1
    Gaussians_round1 = fine_tune(Gaussians_round1, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, fine_tune_iters)
    
    # Round 2
    scores_round2 = compute_fisher_scores(Gaussians_round1, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, patch_size)
    indices_to_keep_round2 = select_top_k(Gaussians_round1, scores_round2, keep_fraction=1.0 - prune_pct_round2)
    Gaussians_pruned = [Gaussians_round1[i] for i in indices_to_keep_round2]
    
    # Fine-tune Round 2
    Gaussians_pruned = fine_tune(Gaussians_pruned, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, fine_tune_iters)
    
    return Gaussians_pruned

def compute_fisher_scores(Gaussians, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, patch_size):
    """
    Computes the log-determinant of the 6x6 Fisher Information matrix block 
    for each Gaussian's spatial parameters (mean and scale).
    """
    scores = []
    for g in Gaussians:
        # Initialize 6x6 Fisher matrix
        # Parameters: 3 for mean, 3 for scale
        F = zeros((6, 6))
        
        for view, gt_img in zip(TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages):
            # Render image and compute gradients w.r.t mean and scale
            # Only compute gradients for pixels in the patch or relevant region
            # To save memory, gradients are accumulated over views
            
            # Gradient of rendered image w.r.t Gaussian parameters
            # dI/dmu: shape (H, W, 3)
            # dI/dscale: shape (H, W, 3)
            dI_dmu, dI_dscale = compute_gradients(g, view)
            
            # Flatten gradients for the patch or full image if memory allows
            # Using patch_size to limit computation scope as per source
            # Note: Source mentions computing over image patches. 
            # Implementation detail: Aggregate outer products of flattened gradients.
            
            # Flatten gradients to vectors of size (patch_pixels * 3)
            grad_mu = flatten_gradients(dI_dmu, patch_size)
            grad_scale = flatten_gradients(dI_dscale, patch_size)
            
            # Concatenate to form 6-element vector per pixel? 
            # Source implies block-wise approximation. 
            # Standard Fisher accumulation: sum of outer products of gradients.
            # Since it's a block for ONE Gaussian, we sum over views.
            
            # Construct gradient vector for this view: [grad_mu, grad_scale]
            # Shape: (num_pixels_in_patch * 3, 6) ? 
            # Actually, Fisher is E[grad * grad^T]. 
            # We accumulate F += grad^T @ grad for each view/patch
            
            # Simplified accumulation assuming scalar loss per pixel or aggregated patch loss
            # L2 loss gradient vector for this Gaussian at this view
            grad_vector = concatenate([grad_mu, grad_scale], axis=0) # Shape (K*K*3, 1) ?
            # Outer product: (K*K*3, K*K*3) -> Too large.
            # Source says 6x6 matrix. This implies we treat the Gaussian parameters as the theta.
            # The gradient is w.r.t the 6 parameters.
            # So grad_vector should be shape (6,) aggregated over the patch/views.
            
            # Correct interpretation: 
            # The Fisher matrix F is 6x6.
            # F_ij = sum_over_views ( sum_over_pixels_in_patch ( dL/dtheta_i * dL/dtheta_j ) )
            # Where theta_i are the 6 spatial params.
            
            # Compute scalar gradients of the L2 loss w.r.t the 6 params
            # dL/dmu_x, dL/dmu_y, dL/dmu_z, dL/dscale_x, dL/dscale_y, dL/dscale_z
            dL_dtheta = compute_loss_gradients(g, view, gt_img, patch_size)
            
            # Outer product
            F += outer(dL_dtheta, dL_dtheta)
            
        # Compute log determinant
        det_F = determinant(F)
        if det_F <= 0:
            # Handle numerical instability
            det_F = 1e-6
        score = log(det_F)
        scores.append(score)
        
    return scores

def select_top_k(Gaussians, scores, keep_fraction):
    """
    Returns indices of Gaussians with the highest scores (lowest uncertainty).
    """
    n_keep = int(len(Gaussians) * keep_fraction)
    # Sort indices by score descending
    sorted_indices = argsort(scores, descending=True)
    return sorted_indices[:n_keep]

def fine_tune(Gaussians, TrainingViews, GroundTruthImages, iterations):
    """
    Performs gradient descent on the L2 loss for the given Gaussians.
    """
    for i in range(iterations):
        # Sample a random view or iterate through all
        view_idx = random_choice(len(TrainingViews))
        view = TrainingViews[view_idx]
        gt_img = GroundTruthImages[view_idx]
        
        # Render and compute loss
        rendered_img = render(Gaussians, view)
        loss = mean_squared_error(rendered_img, gt_img)
        
        # Compute gradients w.r.t Gaussian parameters
        grads = compute_gradients(loss, Gaussians)
        
        # Update parameters
        for g, grad in zip(Gaussians, grads):
            g.mean -= learning_rate * grad.mean
            g.scale -= learning_rate * grad.scale
            # Update other params (rotation, color) as needed by 3D-GS optimizer
            
    return Gaussians
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Patch Size:** Default is $4 \times 4$ pixels. $2 \times 2$ and $8 \times 8$ are also viable but $4 \times 4$ yields best quality [S1].
*   **Pruning Percentages:** 80% in Round 1, 50% in Round 2 (cumulative 90%) [S1].
*   **Fine-tuning:** 5,000 iterations per round [S1].
*   **Convergence Assumption:** The method assumes the scene is fully converged. The Fisher approximation relies on a small L1 residual; pruning before convergence degrades accuracy [S1].
*   **Memory Pitfalls:** Including rotation parameters expands the Fisher matrix to $10 \times 10$, increasing memory by $2.78\times$ and causing OOM on 16GB GPUs (e.g., RTX A4000) [S1].
*   **Color Parameters:** Using RGB color parameters for the score degrades quality as it correlates with visibility rather than reconstruction error sensitivity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The Fisher matrix computation scales linearly with the number of Gaussians $N$ and the number of training views. The memory requirement is $O(N \times 36)$ bytes for the $6 \times 6$ matrices [S1].
*   **Performance:** On the Deep Blending playroom scene, PUP reduces Gaussians from 2.65M to 0.265M, increasing FPS from 76.65 to 318.06 (4.15x speed-up) [S1]. Across Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending, it achieves an average 3.56x speed-up after 90% pruning [S1].
*   **Quality:** Outperforms LightGaussian in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS at ~90% pruning [S1]. On Mip-NeRF 360 avg, PSNR 26.67, SSIM 0.7862, LPIPS 0.2719 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High compression ratio (90%), preserves fine details, post-hoc (no training modification needed).
*   **Limitations:** Requires converged model, high memory usage for Fisher matrix computation (though manageable on 16GB+ GPUs), multi-round process increases total time compared to single-pass pruning.

## Background
PUP 3D-GS was introduced by Alex Hanson, Allen Tu, Vasu Singla, Mayuka Jayawardhana, Matthias Zwicker, and Tom Goldstein from the University of Maryland, College Park [S1]. It was published in 2025 at CVPR [S1]. The method builds on the Bayesian interpretation of the Hessian of the reconstruction error, linking Fisher Information to the entropy of the posterior distribution of Gaussian parameters [S1]. It addresses the limitations of heuristic pruning methods that fail to preserve visual fidelity at high compression ratios [S1].
