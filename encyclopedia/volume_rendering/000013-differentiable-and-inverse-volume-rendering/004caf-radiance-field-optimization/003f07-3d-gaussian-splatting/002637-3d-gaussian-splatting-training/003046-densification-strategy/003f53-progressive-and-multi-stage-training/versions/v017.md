# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Multi-stage Optimization, Curriculum Learning for 3DGS, Progressive Densification

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to optimization strategies that decouple the training timeline into distinct phases or stages to improve convergence stability, rendering quality, and geometric consistency in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It solves the problem of poor view synthesis quality and inefficient primitive placement that arises when training starts with uniform, random, or coarse feed-forward initialization [S1, unofficial]. By progressively densifying the point cloud, mesh, or voxel representation based on error metrics or curriculum schedules, it achieves significantly higher rendering quality and faster convergence compared to single-stage training [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Score-based Densification](4705-score-based-densification.md) by explicitly structuring the *timing* and *scope* of densification (e.g., coarse-to-fine, slice-to-voxel, or spatial-temporal interleaving) rather than relying solely on per-step gradient thresholds.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves decoupling the optimization process into sequential or alternating phases where the density, supervision signal, or parameter sensitivity is modified iteratively. Common principles include:

1.  **Phase Decoupling:** Training is split into distinct stages (e.g., coarse initialization followed by fine-tuning, or slice supervision followed by voxel supervision) to stabilize the optimization landscape [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Progressive Densification:** The number or density of primitives increases over time, often triggered by error metrics or fixed iteration intervals, rather than being static [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Curriculum Supervision:** Different types of loss functions or data sampling strategies are introduced in a specific order (e.g., local planar constraints before global volumetric constraints) to guide the learning process [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Interleaved Optimization:** In dynamic scenes, spatial and temporal densification or optimization steps are alternated to prevent the model from neglecting either motion or appearance details [S2].
5.  **Initialization Sensitivity:** These methods often rely on or improve upon specific initialization strategies (e.g., feed-forward predictions, mesh-aligned sampling) to provide a structurally sound starting point for the progressive refinement [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
A multi-stage approach for point-based and mesh-based representations that refines spatial distribution iteratively. It addresses poor initialization by scoring primitives based on rendering error (e.g., DSSIM weighted by pixel coverage) and inserting new primitives in high-error regions. It differs from siblings by explicitly handling mesh degeneracy and using area-weighted scoring to prevent tiny triangles from dominating densification. Choose this when working with mesh-aligned Gaussians or when uniform initialization leads to artifacts.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the loss functions and densification protocols for **MeshGS**, a variant that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It solves geometric inconsistency by partitioning Gaussians into **tightly-bound** (constrained to mesh) and **loosely-bound** (standard optimization) subsets. It differs from siblings by using mesh-aligned point sampling for tightly-bound splats and applying projection, normal consistency, and scale regularization losses. Choose this when explicit mesh geometry is available and geometric fidelity is critical.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refines a pre-initialized set of 3D Gaussian primitives, often from feed-forward predictions, to achieve high-fidelity novel view synthesis. It solves the problem of redundant initialization by actively managing the population through growing and pruning during optimization. It differs from siblings by starting with a structured, pretrained initialization and using entropy regularization to prevent overfitting. Choose this when accelerating convergence or reducing memory consumption compared to training from scratch.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Structures training for volumetric data by progressively introducing supervision signals: first using sparse pixels on arbitrary planes (slice supervision) to establish local geometry, then using dense random voxel sampling for global consistency. It solves the instability caused by random voxel sampling and the lack of global guidance in slice-only supervision. It differs from siblings by leveraging the alignment with the shear-warp rendering pipeline. Choose this for volumetric data (e.g., MRI, Cryosection) where global spatial consistency is required.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial]. It expands the cloud in a fronto-parallel manner, typically guided by a lightweight vision-transformer head that computes a local perceptual error map, densifying only where SSIM falls below a dynamic threshold [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
Alternates between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2]. This is used in adaptive spatio-temporal 3D Gaussian Splatting for scenes with oscillatory motion, ensuring that the optimization landscape balances both spatial geometry and temporal dynamics [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
*   **Stage 1:** Construct RGB point clouds for each input view, project them to the target view using differentiable point cloud rendering, aggregate them, and optimize the depth network using photometric loss between rendered and ground truth target images. This acts as an unsupervised MVS initialization [S3].
*   **Stage 2:** Jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module) with the initialized depth network [S3].
*   **Parameters:** Yields approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

## Background
The concept of progressive training in radiance fields originates from early NeRF works that used curriculum learning to handle high-frequency details [G]. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, multi-stage strategies emerged to address the limitations of single-stage optimization, particularly regarding initialization quality and convergence speed. The Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine strategy and MeshGS variants were developed to handle explicit geometry constraints [S1, unofficial]. Fine-tuning approaches leverage feed-forward networks (e.g., from pretrained models) to provide better initializations [S1, unofficial]. Curriculum learning for volumetric data was introduced to balance local and global constraints in medical and scientific imaging [S1, unofficial]. Interleaved densification was proposed for dynamic scenes to handle spatio-temporal complexity [S2]. The two-stage scheme for FWD-U was introduced to stabilize depth network training in forward-warping novel view synthesis [S3].
