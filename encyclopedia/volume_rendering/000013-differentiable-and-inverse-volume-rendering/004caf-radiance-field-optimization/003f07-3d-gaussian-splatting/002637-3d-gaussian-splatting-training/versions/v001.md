# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3DGS Learning, Gaussian Splatting Optimization, GaussianTrainer training procedure, Metal-accelerated 3DGS training, Msplat Training Loop & Configuration

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training is a non-deep-learning optimization process that adjusts explicit 3D Gaussian parameters to minimize the photometric difference between rendered images and ground-truth images [S2, unofficial]. Unlike NeRF or Plenoxels which optimize implicit neural fields, 3DGS optimizes a set of explicit primitives (centers, covariances, opacities, colors) using differentiable rasterization [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished by its use of adaptive density control (splitting and cloning) to manage geometric complexity and its reliance on Structure-from-Motion (SfM) for initialization [S2, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Student Data (Initial Gaussians):** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian $i$ has:
    *   Center position $p_{w,i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Rotation quaternion $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Scale vector $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients $h_i$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Opacity $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Teacher Data (Training Views):** A set of training cameras, each defined by:
    *   Rotation $R_{cw}$ and translation $t_{cw}$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Intrinsic matrix $K$ with parameters $f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Ground-truth image $\gamma^{gt}$ (RGB values) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Configuration:** Training hyperparameters (see Parameters).

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters ($p_w, q, s, h, \alpha$) [S2, unofficial].
*   Rendered images for evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, L1) [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The training state maintains the Gaussian attributes in contiguous memory buffers (typically GPU-accessible tensors) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Means:** $N \times 3$ float32 array for $p_w$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Quaternions:** $N \times 4$ float32 array for $q$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scales:** $N \times 3$ float32 array for $s$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Opacities:** $N \times 1$ float32 array for $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **SH Coefficients:** $N \times D$ float32 array, where $D$ depends on the SH degree (e.g., $D=45$ for degree 3) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Stats:** A structure containing `iteration` (int), `splat_count` (int), and `ms_per_step` (float) [S1, unofficial].

### Initialization
1.  **Position and Color:** $p_{w,i}$ and $h_i$ are initialized from SfM photogrammetry points and colors [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Rotation, Scale, Opacity:** $q_i$, $s_i$, and $\alpha_i$ are initialized as constants [S2, unofficial].
    *   $q_i$ is typically initialized to identity (or random small rotation) [G].
    *   $s_i$ is initialized to a small constant value derived from the average distance to nearest neighbors [G].
    *   $\alpha_i$ is initialized to a small constant (e.g., 0.1) [G].

### Loss Function
The optimization objective minimizes the loss $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to parameters $p_w, q, s, h, \alpha$ [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\underset{p_w, q, s, h, \alpha}{\textrm{argmin}} \quad \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_1$ is the L1 loss, $\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ is the D-SSIM loss, and $\lambda$ is a weight (default 0.2) [S2, unofficial].

### Forward Pass (Rendering)
For each pixel $j$, the color $\gamma_j$ is computed via alpha blending of $N$ projected Gaussians sorted by depth (closest to furthest) [S2, unofficial]:
$$
\gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha'_{ij} c_i \tau_{ij}
$$
$$
\tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_{kj})
$$
where:
*   $c_i$ is the color of Gaussian $i$ evaluated at the view direction using SH coefficients $h_i$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\alpha'_{ij}$ is the effective opacity of Gaussian $i$ at pixel $j$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\tau_{ij}$ is the accumulated transparency from all Gaussians closer than $i$ [S2, unofficial].

The effective opacity is defined as:
$$
\alpha'_{ij} = g \alpha_i
$$
$$
g = \exp(-0.5 \omega'_0 d_0^2 - 0.5 \omega'_2 d_1^2 - \omega'_1 d_0 d_1)
$$
where:
*   $\alpha_i$ is the base opacity parameter [S2, unofficial].
*   $\omega'_0, \omega'_1, \omega'_2$ are the upper triangular elements of the inverse 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma_i'^{-1}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $d_0, d_1$ are the elements of the vector $u_i - x_j$, where $u_i$ is the projected center and $x_j$ is the pixel center [S2, unofficial].

### Backpropagation (Gradients)
Gradients are computed using the chain rule through the 5 forward steps of image generation [S2, unofficial]. The Jacobian for each parameter group is:

**Rotation ($q_i$):**
$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i}
$$

**Scale ($s_i$):**
$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i}
$$

**Spherical Harmonics ($h_i$):**
$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i}
$$

**Opacity ($\alpha_i$):**
$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i}
$$

**Center ($p_{w,i}$):**
$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{w,i}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}}
$$

Key intermediate derivatives [S2, unofficial]:
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{i,j}} = \tau_{i,j}(c_i - \gamma_{i+1,j})$
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} = \tau_{i,j}\alpha'_{i,j}$
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial \omega'_i} = [-0.5d_0^2, -d_0d_1, -0.5d_1^2]^T g$
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial u_i} = [-\omega'_0 d_0 - \omega'_1 d_1, -\omega'_2 d_1 - \omega'_1 d_0]^T g$
*   $\frac{\partial \omega'_i}{\partial \sigma'_i}$ involves the determinant and elements of the 2D covariance matrix [S2, unofficial].

### Training Loop
The training loop manages camera sampling, resolution scheduling, and densification [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Camera Selection:** Maintain a shuffled vector of training camera indices. Use a round-robin iterator with periodic reshuffling to select the next camera [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Resolution Schedule:** Compute a downscale factor based on the current step. Retrieve the ground-truth image at the scaled resolution [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Optimization Step:**
    *   Render the image for the selected camera.
    *   Compute loss $\mathcal{L}$ (L1 + D-SSIM) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Compute gradients for all parameters [S2, unofficial].
    *   Update parameters using an optimizer (typically Adam) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Advance learning rate schedulers [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Densification (Periodic):** Every `refine_every` steps (default 100), perform adaptive density control [S2, unofficial]:
    *   Identify Gaussians with large position gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u}$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Clone:** If a Gaussian's size is small (below `densify_size_thresh`, default 0.01), clone it [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Split:** If a Gaussian's size is large (above threshold) and gradient is high, split it into two [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Reset Opacity:** Periodically (every `reset_alpha_every`, default 30), reset the opacity of cloned/split Gaussians to prevent rapid divergence [S1, unofficial].
5.  **SH Degree Increase:** Increase the SH degree (`sh_degree`) every `sh_degree_interval` steps (default 1000) [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Evaluation:** Periodically (e.g., every 10 steps), render test views, synchronize GPU, compute PSNR/SSIM/L1, and average across test cameras [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Checkpointing:** Save the model state every `save_every` steps [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def train_3dgs(gaussians, cameras, config):
    # Initialize parameters from SfM and constants
    gaussians.p_w = init_from_sfm(gaussians.p_w)
    gaussians.h = init_from_sfm(gaussians.h)
    gaussians.q = init_constant_identity()
    gaussians.s = init_constant_small()
    gaussians.alpha = init_constant_small()
    
    # Camera iterator
    cam_indices = shuffle(list(range(len(cameras))))
    cam_iter = Iterator(cam_indices)
    
    for step in range(1, config.iterations + 1):
        # 1. Select Camera
        cam = cameras[cam_iter.next()]
        
        # 2. Resolution Schedule
        downscale = get_downscale_factor(step, config.resolution_schedule, config.num_downscales)
        gt_image = cam.get_image(downscale)
        
        # 3. Forward Pass
        rendered_image = render(gaussians, cam, downscale)
        
        # 4. Loss Computation
        loss = (1 - config.ssim_weight) * l1_loss(rendered_image, gt_image) + \
               config.ssim_weight * d_ssim_loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
        
        # 5. Backpropagation
        gradients = compute_gradients(loss, gaussians, cam)
        update_parameters(gaussians, gradients, config.optimizer)
        
        # 6. Schedulers
        adjust_learning_rates(gaussians, step, config)
        
        # 7. Densification (Periodic)
        if step % config.refine_every == 0:
            densify(gaussians, gradients, config.densify_grad_thresh, config.densify_size_thresh)
            reset_opacities(gaussians, step, config.reset_alpha_every)
            
        # 8. SH Degree Increase
        if step % config.sh_degree_interval == 0:
            increase_sh_degree(gaussians, config.sh_degree)
            
        # 9. Evaluation (Periodic)
        if step % 10 == 0:
            evaluate(gaussians, test_cameras, step)
            
        # 10. Checkpointing
        if config.save_every > 0 and step % config.save_every == 0:
            save_model(gaussians, step)
```

### Parameters
*   `iterations`: Total training steps. Default: 30,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree`: Initial SH degree. Default: 3 [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree_interval`: Steps between SH degree increase. Default: 1,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `ssim_weight`: Weight for D-SSIM loss. Default: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   `num_downscales`: Number of resolution levels. Default: 2 [S1, unofficial].
*   `resolution_schedule`: Step at which resolution scaling changes. Default: 3,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `refine_every`: Frequency of densification. Default: 100 [S1, unofficial].
*   `warmup_length`: Initial training phase duration. Default: 500 [S1, unofficial].
*   `reset_alpha_every`: Frequency of opacity reset. Default: 30 [S1, unofficial].
*   `densify_grad_thresh`: Gradient threshold for densification. Default: 0.0002 [S1, unofficial].
*   `densify_size_thresh`: Size threshold for densification. Default: 0.01 [S1, unofficial].
*   `stop_screen_size_at`: Step at which screen-size splitting stops. Default: 4,000 [S1, unofficial].
*   `split_screen_size`: Screen size threshold for splitting. Default: 0.05 [S1, unofficial].
*   `bg_color`: Background color [R, G, B]. Default: [0.613, 0.010, 0.398] (magenta) [S1, unofficial].
*   `output`: Export filename. Default: "splat.ply" [S1, unofficial].
*   `save_every`: Checkpoint frequency. Default: -1 (disabled) [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Evaluation Mode:** The dataset must be loaded with `eval_mode=True` for evaluation; otherwise, a runtime error occurs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Arbitrary Pose Rendering:** Requires exactly 16 elements for the `cam_to_world` matrix; otherwise, a runtime error occurs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The derivative of the inverse covariance matrix $\frac{\partial \omega'}{\partial \sigma'}$ involves determinants and can be numerically unstable for very small covariances [S2, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** SfM points with poor quality can lead to poor initialization, requiring robust densification [S2, unofficial].

### [GES Optimization Strategy](4697-ges-optimization-strategy.md)
An optimization approach for GES that controls shape density by selectively pruning splats based on shape attributes, eliminating the need for a variable density mechanism. It optimizes shape parameter $\beta$, position $x$, opacity $\kappa$, covariance $\Sigma$, and spherical harmonics coefficients using SGD.

### [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md)
Densification strategies manage the population of Gaussian primitives during the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, addressing the issues of unpredictable model size, redundant primitives, and excessive training duration. Unlike the original 3DGS approach which relies on heuristic splitting and cloning based on position gradients and opacity, this family includes methods that use score-based constructive growth, temporal sensitivity pruning, and groupwise motion distillation to control model complexity and improve rendering efficiency.

### Non-Pinhole Camera Training
Trains 3DGS on non-pinhole camera datasets (e.g., Matterport) where standard 3DGS fails. The differentiable rasterizer allows adaptation to various camera models [S3].

### AbsGS
Takes absolute positional gradients across views (not the average) to find 'fence-sitting' Gaussians, improving densification in complex geometry [S4, unofficial].

### SparseGS
Uses monocular depth + diffusion priors for ~3-view input, enabling reconstruction from very sparse data [S4, unofficial].

### PixelSplat Training and Depth Regularization
Trains for 300,000 steps with batch size 7, requiring ~80 GB VRAM [S5]. Uses MSE loss for first 150,000 steps, then adds LPIPS loss (weight 0.05) [S5]. Places 3 Gaussians per ray, sampling positions independently and dividing alpha by 3 to ensure total opacity ~1 [S5]. Fine-tunes for 50,000 steps with depth regularization loss $L_{reg\_depth}$, computed using horizontal and vertical depth differences $D_x^\Delta$ and $D_y^\Delta$ [S5].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of Gaussians and pixels per iteration [S1, unofficial]. Training typically takes minutes to hours depending on scene size and resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of Gaussians. VRAM usage can be high, especially for high-resolution scenes or methods like PixelSplat (~80 GB) [S5].
*   **Performance:** Fast training compared to NeRF. Msplat achieves training in ~90s on M4 Max [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Explicit representation allows for easy manipulation and editing. Fast rendering and training.
*   **Limitations:** Requires SfM initialization. Densification can lead to large model sizes. Sensitive to hyperparameters like densification thresholds [S2, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S5]. It differs from previous radiance field methods by using explicit 3D Gaussians instead of neural networks. The training process relies on differentiable rasterization and adaptive density control [S2, unofficial]. It has been extended to handle non-pinhole cameras [S3], sparse views [S4, unofficial], and feed-forward training [S4, unofficial].
