# Model Compression Pipeline for 3D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 3DGS Model Compression Pipeline, Edge-deployment compression, LightGaussian-derived compression, Compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting Pipeline, Compressed 3DGS, Compressed 3DGS Model, Hardware-oriented 3DGS

## Overview
This method is a compression and rendering pipeline for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that reduces memory footprint and increases rendering speed while maintaining image quality [S1, S2]. It addresses the high memory bandwidth and computational complexity of standard 3DGS, making it suitable for edge deployment, mobile VR/AR, and low-power hardware [S1, S2]. It sets itself apart from its parent [Mini-Splatting](7850-mini-splatting.md) by not imposing a hard limit on the total number of Gaussians via mixed-integer optimization, but rather by iteratively pruning, quantizing attributes, and reducing spherical harmonic (SH) degrees to achieve high compression ratios (up to 51.6×) with minimal quality loss [S1, S2]. It differs from [LightGaussian](link-to-lightgaussian) by using iterative pruning with intermediate fine-tuning, progressive SH-degree reduction, and vector quantization (VQ) on all SH coefficients and colors, rather than only low-salience subsets [S2].

## How it works
The pipeline consists of four main stages: Sensitivity Calculation, Clustering, QA Fine-tuning, and Encoding [S1]. It combines Gaussian pruning, SH distillation, VQ for SH coefficients and colors, and FP16 quantization [S2].

**Inputs:** Trained 3D Gaussian model (positions $\mathbf{p}$, opacities $\alpha$, colors $\mathbf{c}$, covariances $\mathbf{\Sigma}$, SH coefficients $\mathbf{C}$).
**Outputs:** Compressed scene representation (pruned Gaussians, codebook indices, quantized parameters).

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Gaussian Attributes:**
    *   Position: 3D float vector $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Opacity: Scalar $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.
    *   Shape: Covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, parameterized by rotation quaternion $\mathbf{q}$ and scale vector $\mathbf{s}$ [S1].
    *   Color: SH coefficients $\mathbf{C}$ and base color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
2.  **Codebooks:**
    *   **Color Codebook:** Stores quantized SH coefficients and colors. Size varies by scene [S1].
    *   **Shape Codebook:** Stores quantized Gaussian shape parameters ($\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{s}$) and scaling factor $\eta$ [S1].
3.  **Indices:**
    *   Codebook indices replace original attributes for storage [S1].
    *   Morton order layout is used for spatial locality [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Sensitivity Calculation:**
    Calculate sensitivity thresholds $\beta_c$ and $\beta_g$ for SH coefficients and shape parameters, respectively. These thresholds determine which parameters are considered for clustering [S1].

2.  **Iterative Pruning with Intermediate Fine-tuning:**
    Instead of aggressive single-step pruning, the model is pruned progressively [S2].
    *   Prune Gaussians based on opacity and contribution.
    *   Insert fine-tuning between pruning stages to recover visual quality [S2].
    *   Repeat until the target Gaussian count is reached [S2].

3.  **Progressive SH-Degree Reduction via Iterative Distillation:**
    *   Reduce SH degree progressively rather than in one step [S2].
    *   Distill the representation from higher to lower SH degrees, smoothing the quality-compression tradeoff [S2].

4.  **Clustering and Vector Quantization (VQ):**
    *   Apply VQ to **all** SH coefficients and colors, not just low-salience subsets [S2].
    *   Compress SH coefficients and shape parameters into codebooks of predefined sizes [S1].
    *   **Color Clustering:** Cluster SH coefficients and colors into the Color Codebook [S1].
    *   **Gaussian Clustering:** Cluster shape parameters into the Shape Codebook [S1].
    *   The centroid $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_c$ of normalized covariance matrices $\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_i$ with weights $w_i$ is calculated as:
        $$ \hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_c = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i \hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i} $$
        The scaling factor $\eta$ ensures normalization:
        $$ \|\mathbf{s}_c\|_2^2 = \text{Tr}(\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_c) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i \text{Tr}(\hat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i} $$
        [S1]

5.  **QA Fine-tuning:**
    *   Apply quantization-aware fine-tuning to regain information lost during quantization [S1].
    *   This stage takes up 70% of the total compression pipeline time [S1].

6.  **Encoding:**
    *   Apply entropy and run-length encoding [S1].
    *   Use Morton order layout for spatial locality [S1].
    *   Quantize remaining parameters to FP16 [S2].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compress_3dgs(model):
    # model contains positions, opacities, covariances, SH coefficients, colors
    
    # Step 1: Sensitivity Calculation
    beta_c = calculate_sensitivity(model.sh_coeffs)
    beta_g = calculate_sensitivity(model.shape_params)
    
    # Step 2: Iterative Pruning
    current_gaussians = model.gaussians
    while current_gaussians.count > target_count:
        # Prune low-contribution Gaussians
        current_gaussians = prune_gaussians(current_gaussians, threshold=beta_c)
        # Fine-tune to recover quality
        current_gaussians = fine_tune(current_gaussians, model.train_data, lr=0.001)
        
    # Step 3: Progressive SH Reduction
    current_sh_degree = model.sh_degree
    while current_sh_degree > target_sh_degree:
        # Distill from current_sh_degree to current_sh_degree - 1
        current_gaussians = distill_sh(current_gaussians, current_sh_degree, current_sh_degree - 1)
        current_sh_degree -= 1
        # Fine-tune after distillation
        current_gaussians = fine_tune(current_gaussians, model.train_data, lr=0.001)
        
    # Step 4: Clustering and VQ
    # Cluster SH coefficients and colors
    color_codebook, color_indices = cluster_and_quantize(current_gaussians.sh_coeffs, current_gaussians.colors, codebook_size=color_codebook_size)
    
    # Cluster shape parameters
    shape_codebook, shape_indices = cluster_and_quantize(current_gaussians.shape_params, codebook_size=shape_codebook_size)
    
    # Update Gaussians with indices
    current_gaussians.sh_indices = color_indices
    current_gaussians.shape_indices = shape_indices
    
    # Step 5: QA Fine-tuning
    current_gaussians = qa_fine_tune(current_gaussians, model.train_data, codebooks=[color_codebook, shape_codebook])
    
    # Step 6: Encoding
    compressed_data = encode(current_gaussians, color_codebook, shape_codebook, encoding='entropy_run_length_morton')
    
    return compressed_data
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Codebook Sizes:** Predefined sizes for Color and Shape codebooks. Larger codebooks reduce maximum error with minimal memory overhead [S1].
*   **Sensitivity Thresholds ($\beta_c, \beta_g$):** Trade-off between quality and compression rate [S1].
*   **Target Gaussian Count:** Determined by hardware constraints [S2].
*   **Target SH Degree:** Reduced progressively [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Position Compression:** Aggressive compression of Gaussian positions introduces significant error; positions are typically not compressed [S1].
*   **Color Shift:** Subtle color shifts may occur below human perception threshold [S1].
*   **Memory Dominance:** Memory consumption is dominated by coordinates and codebook indices [S1]. Color codebook size varies significantly between scenes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression Ratio:** Up to 31× [S1] or 51.6× [S2] compared to baseline.
*   **Rendering Speedup:** Up to 4× [S1].
*   **Quality Loss:** Indiscernible [S1] or 0.743 dB average PSNR drop [S2].
*   **Memory Bandwidth:** Reduced by ~2× due to compressed data [S1].
*   **Power Consumption:** 0.219 W at 1080p@129 FPS [S2].
*   **Throughput:** 267.5 Mpixels/s [S2].
*   **Energy Efficiency:** 1219 Mpixels/J [S2].
*   **Silicon Area:** 0.66 mm² (TSMC 28 nm, 800 MHz) [S2].
*   **Compression Time:** 5-6 minutes [S1].
*   **Reconstruction Time Increase:** ~10% [S1].
*   **Fine-tuning Time:** 70% of total compression time [S1].
*   **Preprocessing Speedup:** 5× faster with compressed representation [S1].

**Performance on NVIDIA RTX A5000 (1080p):**
*   Bicycle (6.1M Gaussians): Baseline 93/215/321 FPS, Compressed 321 FPS [S1].
*   Bonsai (1.2M Gaussians): Baseline 184/414/502 FPS, Compressed 502 FPS [S1].
*   Average Mip-NeRF360: 4.82ms (uncompressed) vs 1.95ms (compressed) [S1].
*   Average Tanks & Temples/Deep Blending: 5.13ms (uncompressed) vs 2.44ms (compressed) [S1].

**Performance on NVIDIA RTX 3070M (1080p):**

**Strengths:**
*   High compression ratio with minimal quality loss [S1, S2].
*   Suitable for low-power edge devices and mobile VR/AR [S1, S2].

**Limitations:**
*   Cannot aggressively compress Gaussian positions without significant error [S1].
*   Fine-tuning stage is time-consuming (70% of pipeline time) [S1].

## Background
The method is derived from LightGaussian [S2], which combines Gaussian pruning and fine-tuning, SH distillation, VQ for SH coefficients and colors, and FP16 quantization [S2]. It was developed to meet the design target of full-HD rendering at 120 FPS per eye under a sub-0.5 W power budget for edge deployment [S2]. The approach was presented in "Compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting for Accelerated Novel View Synthesis" [S1] and "A 129 FPS Full HD Real-Time Accelerator for 3D Gaussian Splatting" [S2].
