# Explicit Camera Jacobian Derivation

Also called: Analytical Jacobians for 3DGS, Camera Pose Jacobian Derivation (Lie Group), Lie Algebra Jacobian for 3DGS

## Overview
This method derives analytical Jacobians of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussian primitives to enable efficient gradient-based camera tracking in SLAM systems. It solves the problem of high computational overhead associated with automatic differentiation during rasterization, ensuring minimal parameterization using Lie algebra. Unlike prior ray-intersection methods that assume fewer Gaussians, this approach is applicable to the EWA splatting and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera pose $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$, 3D Gaussian parameters (mean $\mu_W$, covariance $\Sigma_W$), projection matrix $K$.
*   **Output:** Jacobians $\frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ and $\frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}}$ required for gradient descent optimization of the camera pose.

**Data Structures:**
*   $T_{CW}$: $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrix representing camera-to-world pose.
*   $\mu_C$: $3 \times 1$ vector representing Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
*   $\Sigma_I$: $2 \times 2$ covariance matrix of the projected Gaussian in image space.
*   $W$: $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix component of $T_{CW}$.
*   $\tau$: $6 \times 1$ vector in Lie algebra $se(3)$.

**Mathematical Definitions:**
The derivative on the manifold is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$
\frac{\mathcal{D}(f(\mathbf{T}))}{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{T}} \triangleq \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Log}(f(\text{Exp}(\tau) \circ \mathbf{T}) \circ f(\mathbf{T})^{-1})}{\tau}
$$
where $\circ$ is group composition, and $\text{Exp}, \text{Log}$ are exponential and logarithmic mappings between Lie algebra $se(3)$ and Lie group $SE(3)$. The skew-symmetric matrix of a vector $\mathbf{v}$ is denoted $\mathbf{v}^\times$.

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**

1.  **Mean Jacobian:**
    The mean in camera coordinates is $\mu_C = W \mu_W + t$, where $t$ is the translation component of $T_{CW}$. Using the chain rule:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \mu_I}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}}
    $$
    The manifold derivative of $\mu_C$ with respect to $T_{CW}$ is derived by perturbing $T_{CW}$ with $\tau = [\rho, \theta]^T$ (translation $\rho$, rotation $\theta$):
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\text{Exp}(\tau) \cdot \mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Expanding $\text{Exp}(\tau) \approx I + \tau^\wedge$:
    $$
    = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{(I + \tau^\wedge)\mu_C - \mu_C}{\tau} = \lim_{\tau \rightarrow 0} \frac{\tau^\wedge \mu_C}{\tau}
    $$
    Substituting $\tau^\wedge \mu_C = \theta^\times \mu_C + \rho = -\mu_C^\times \theta + \rho$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mu_C}{\mathcal{D}T_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\mu_C^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    where $I$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix.

2.  **Covariance Jacobian:**
    The 2D covariance $\Sigma_I$ depends on the projection Jacobian $J$ and the rotation $W$. The chain rule yields:
    $$
    \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial T_{CW}} = \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial J} \frac{\partial J}{\partial \mu_C} \frac{D\mu_C}{DT_{CW}} + \frac{\partial \Sigma_I}{\partial W} \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}
    $$
    The term $\frac{DW}{DT_{CW}}$ requires the derivative of the rotation matrix $W$ with respect to the rotation vector $\theta$. For the $i$-th component $\theta_i$:
    $$
    \frac{\partial W}{\partial \theta_i} = \mathbf{e}_i^\times W
    $$
    where $\mathbf{e}_1=[1,0,0]^T, \mathbf{e}_2=[0,1,0]^T, \mathbf{e}_3=[0,0,1]^T$. Stacking these for all three components:
    $$
    \frac{DW}{DT_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -W_{:,1}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,2}^\times \\ 0 & -W_{:,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Here $W_{:,i}$ is the $i$-th column of $W$. Note that the translation part of the derivative for $W$ is zero since rotation is independent of translation in this parameterization.

3.  **Rotation Matrix Component Jacobian:**
    The Jacobian of the rotation matrix component $\mathbf{u}$ (where $\mathbf{u}$ represents the columns of $W$) with respect to rotation $R_{CW}$ is:
    $$
    \frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbf{u}}{\mathcal{D}R_{CW}} = \begin{bmatrix} -\mathbf{u}_{:,1}^\times & -\mathbf{u}_{:,2}^\times & -\mathbf{u}_{:,3}^\times \end{bmatrix}
    $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def skew_symmetric(v):
    """Converts a 3D vector to its skew-symmetric matrix."""
    return np.array([
        [0, -v[2], v[1]],
        [v[2], 0, -v[0]],
        [-v[1], v[0], 0]
    ])

def compute_mean_jacobian(mu_C):
    """
    Computes D(mu_C) / D(T_CW).
    mu_C: 3D vector, Gaussian mean in camera coordinates.
    Returns: 3x6 matrix.
    """
    I_3 = np.eye(3)
    mu_C_skew = skew_symmetric(mu_C)
    # [I | -mu_C^x]
    return np.hstack([I_3, -mu_C_skew])

def compute_rotation_jacobian(W):
    """
    Computes DW / D(T_CW).
    W: 3x3 rotation matrix.
    Returns: 9x6 matrix (flattened rotation matrix derivative).
    """
    # The derivative is zero w.r.t translation (first 3 cols)
    # And depends on rotation (last 3 cols)
    # DW/d_theta_i = e_i^x W
    # We stack these derivatives for theta_x, theta_y, theta_z
    
    # Columns of W
    w1 = W[:, 0]
    w2 = W[:, 1]
    w3 = W[:, 2]
    
    # Skew symmetric matrices of columns
    w1_skew = skew_symmetric(w1)
    w2_skew = skew_symmetric(w2)
    w3_skew = skew_symmetric(w3)
    
    # Construct the 9x6 matrix
    # First 3 columns are zero (translation part)
    # Next 3 columns are -w_i^x (as rows in the flattened structure)
    # Note: The source formula shows block structure. 
    # The derivative of the 3x3 matrix W w.r.t 3x1 vector theta is a 9x3 matrix.
    # The source combines this with the full SE(3) derivative structure.
    
    # Based on Eq 6: [0, -W_{:,1}^x; 0, -W_{:,2}^x; 0, -W_{:,3}^x]
    # This implies a 9x6 matrix where the first 3 cols are 0, 
    # and the last 3 cols are constructed from the skew matrices of W's columns.
    
    # Let's construct the 9x3 part for rotation
    # Row 1-3: -w1_skew
    # Row 4-6: -w2_skew
    # Row 7-9: -w3_skew
    
    rot_part = np.vstack([-w1_skew, -w2_skew, -w3_skew])
    
    # Full 9x6 matrix
    zero_part = np.zeros((9, 3))
    return np.hstack([zero_part, rot_part])

def compute_camera_jacobian(mu_C, W):
    """
    Computes the full camera pose Jacobian components.
    """
    J_mu = compute_mean_jacobian(mu_C)
    J_W = compute_rotation_jacobian(W)
    return J_mu, J_W
```

**Parameters:**
*   Requires at least 50 iterations of gradient descent per frame for accurate tracking [S1, unofficial].
*   $\mu_C \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Gaussian center.
*   $T_{CW} \in SE(3)$: Camera-to-world pose.
*   $\mathbf{u}$: Rotation matrix component.
*   $\theta$: Rotation vector.
*   $\tau$: Infinitesimal parameter.
*   $\wedge$: Hat operator mapping vector to skew-symmetric matrix.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The limit definition relies on small $\tau$. In implementation, use the analytical closed-form derived above rather than numerical differentiation to avoid precision loss and overhead.
*   **Boundary:** The derivation assumes $T_{CW}$ is a valid rotation matrix. If $W$ drifts from orthogonality during optimization, re-orthonormalization (e.g., via SVD) may be required before computing Jacobians.
*   **Degenerate Input:** If $\mu_C$ is zero or near-zero, the skew-symmetric term vanishes, which is mathematically correct but may lead to singularities if not handled in the broader optimization context.

## Subtopics

### Photometric and Geometric Tracking
Optimizes only the current camera pose without updating the map representation, minimizing photometric and/or geometric residuals. It uses the derived Jacobians to update $T_{CW}$.
*   **Photometric Residual:** $E_{pho} = \| I(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{I} \|_1$, where $I$ renders Gaussians $\mathcal{G}$ from pose $T_{CW}$ and $\bar{I}$ is the observed image.
*   **Geometric Residual:** $E_{geo} = \| D(\mathcal{G}, T_{CW}) - \bar{D} \|_1$, where $D$ is depth rasterization and $\bar{D}$ is observed depth.
*   **Depth Rasterization:** $\mathcal{D}_p = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} z_i \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$, where $z_i$ is the distance to the mean $\mu_W$ of Gaussian $i$ along the camera ray.
*   **Parameters:** Optimizes affine brightness parameters for varying exposure and penalizes non-edge or low-opacity pixels. Uses a weighted combination $\lambda_{pho} E_{pho} + (1-\lambda_{pho}) E_{geo}$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The analytical derivation eliminates the overhead of automatic differentiation, making gradient computation comparable to the rasterization cost itself. This enables real-time tracking (e.g., 3.2 FPS monocular, 2.5 FPS RGB-D on Replica dataset) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal, as it only stores the Jacobian matrices for the current frame's Gaussians.
*   **Strengths:** Computationally efficient, minimal parameterization (6 DOF), applicable to large-scale 3DGS scenes.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate initial pose for convergence in gradient descent. Assumes the Gaussian representation is fixed during tracking (mapping is separate).
*   **When to use:** Real-time SLAM systems using 3D Gaussian Splatting.
*   **When not to use:** Offline mapping where accuracy is prioritized over speed, or when the scene representation is not 3D Gaussians.

## Background
Originated in the context of Gaussian Splatting SLAM, where 3D Gaussians are used as the sole representation for SLAM components including tracking, mapping, and keyframe management. Prior methods for camera pose optimization with 3D Gaussians assumed fewer Gaussians and relied on ray-intersection, which is not applicable to the EWA splatting used in 3DGS. This method provides the first analytical Jacobian for SE(3) camera pose with respect to 3D Gaussians in the splatting context, enabling efficient online tracking [S1, unofficial].
