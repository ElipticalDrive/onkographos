# NeRF++

Also called: Inverted Sphere NeRF, Unbounded NeRF

## Overview
NeRF++ generates novel views for unbounded scenes by separating the scene into a foreground (inside a unit sphere) and a background (outside the sphere) using two separate Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) models [S1]. It solves the parameterization failure of standard NeRF, which assumes the entire scene fits within a bounded volume, leading to either missing background elements or loss of detail due to limited sampling resolution in large-scale scenes [S1, S5]. It sets itself apart from its parent [Radiance-field optimization](44-radiance-field-optimization.md) by explicitly handling infinite depth via radial inversion [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ RGB images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^N$ with known camera poses and intrinsics [S1, S5].
*   **Outputs:** Two NeRF models: $F_{in}$ for the interior volume and $F_{out}$ for the exterior volume, enabling the synthesis of novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** The 3D space is divided by a unit sphere centered at the origin [S1, S5].
    *   **Interior:** Points $\mathbf{x}$ where $\|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1$.
    *   **Exterior:** Points $\mathbf{x}$ where $\|\mathbf{x}\| > 1$.
2.  **NeRF Models:**
    *   $F_{in}: \mathbb{R}^3 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{R}^4$: Maps 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma_{in}$ and color $\mathbf{c}_{in}$ [S3].
    *   $F_{out}: \mathbb{R}^3 \times S^2 \to \mathbb{R}^4$: Maps 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma_{out}$ and color $\mathbf{c}_{out}$ [S3]. Note that $F_{out}$ operates on the *inverted* coordinates of the exterior points [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Definition:** For a pixel $p$ in image $I_i$, define the camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera center and $\mathbf{d}$ is the unit direction vector [S3].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample $N$ points $\{t_n\}_{n=1}^N$ along the ray within the near $t_n$ and far $t_f$ bounds [S6].
    *   Standard NeRF uses uniform sampling with jitter: $t_n \sim U[t_{start} + \frac{n-1}{N}(t_{end}-t_{start}), t_{start} + \frac{n}{N}(t_{end}-t_{start})]$ [S6].
3.  **Point Classification:** For each sample point $\mathbf{x}_n = \mathbf{r}(t_n)$:
    *   If $\|\mathbf{x}_n\| \le 1$, classify as **Interior** [S1].
    *   If $\|\mathbf{x}_n\| > 1$, classify as **Exterior** [S1].
4.  **Coordinate Transformation (Radial Inversion):** For points classified as **Exterior**, apply radial inversion to map them into the unit sphere [S1].
    *   Let $r = \|\mathbf{x}_n\|$.
    *   The inverted coordinate $\mathbf{x}'_n$ is:
        $$ \mathbf{x}'_n = \frac{\mathbf{x}_n}{r^2} = \left( \frac{x}{r^2}, \frac{y}{r^2}, \frac{z}{r^2} \right) $$
    *   This maps $\|\mathbf{x}\|>1$ to $\|\mathbf{x}'\|<1$ [S1][S8, unofficial].
5.  **NeRF Query:**
    *   If **Interior**: Query $F_{in}(\mathbf{x}_n, \mathbf{d})$ to get $(\sigma_n, \mathbf{c}_n)$ [S3].
    *   If **Exterior**: Query $F_{out}(\mathbf{x}'_n, \mathbf{d})$ to get $(\sigma_n, \mathbf{c}_n)$ [S3].
6.  **Volume Rendering Integration:** The ray integral is evaluated in two parts: the segment inside the sphere and the segment outside the sphere [S1].
    *   Let $t'$ be the intersection distance of the ray with the unit sphere (the smaller positive root of $\|\mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}\|^2 = 1$) [G].
    *   The total color $C(\mathbf{r})$ is the sum of the rendered color from the interior part and the exterior part, accounting for transmittance accumulation across the boundary [S1].
    *   $$ C(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t'} T(t) \sigma_{in}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}_{in}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt + \int_{t'}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma_{out}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}_{out}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt $$
    *   Where $T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) ds\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance [S2, S5].
    *   In discrete form with samples $\{t_i\}$:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) \mathbf{c}_i $$
        where $\sigma_i, \mathbf{c}_i$ are selected from $F_{in}$ or $F_{out}$ based on the point classification step [S2].
7.  **Loss Calculation:** Minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered image $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I$ [S2].
    *   $$ \mathcal{L}_{MSE} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \| C(\mathbf{r}) - \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) \|_2^2 $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class MLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_dim=6, hidden_dim=256, out_dim=4):
        super().__init__()
        self.layers = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(in_dim, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, out_dim)
        )
    def forward(self, x):
        return self.layers(x)

class NeRFPlusPlus:
    def __init__(self, num_samples=128, near_plane=0.1, far_plane=100.0):
        self.num_samples = num_samples
        self.near_plane = near_plane
        self.far_plane = far_plane
        self.nerf_in = MLP()
        self.nerf_out = MLP()
        self.optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(
            list(self.nerf_in.parameters()) + list(self.nerf_out.parameters()),
            lr=5e-4
        )

    def radial_inversion(self, x):
        r = torch.norm(x, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
        r = torch.clamp(r, min=1e-6)
        return x / (r ** 2)

    def sample_points(self, ray_o, ray_d, num_samples):
        t_vals = torch.linspace(self.near_plane, self.far_plane, num_samples, device=ray_o.device)
        noise = torch.rand_like(t_vals) * (self.far_plane - self.near_plane) / num_samples
        t_vals = t_vals + noise
        points = ray_o.unsqueeze(1) + ray_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(2)
        return points, t_vals

    def forward(self, ray_o, ray_d, num_samples=None):
        if num_samples is None: num_samples = self.num_samples
        points, t_vals = self.sample_points(ray_o, ray_d, num_samples)
        
        point_dists = torch.norm(points, dim=-1)
        is_exterior = point_dists > 1.0
        
        points_inverted = points.clone()
        points_inverted[is_exterior] = self.radial_inversion(points_inverted[is_exterior])
        
        points_flat = points_inverted.view(-1, 3)
        ray_dir_flat = ray_d.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, num_samples, -1).view(-1, 3)
        
        sigma_in = torch.zeros_like(points_flat[:, 0])
        color_in = torch.zeros_like(points_flat[:, 0:3])
        sigma_out = torch.zeros_like(points_flat[:, 0])
        color_out = torch.zeros_like(points_flat[:, 0:3])
        
        mask_in = ~is_exterior.view(-1)
        if mask_in.any():
            out_in = self.nerf_in(torch.cat([points_flat[mask_in], ray_dir_flat[mask_in]], dim=-1))
            sigma_in[mask_in] = out_in[:, 0]
            color_in[mask_in] = out_in[:, 1:]
            
        mask_out = is_exterior.view(-1)
        if mask_out.any():
            out_out = self.nerf_out(torch.cat([points_flat[mask_out], ray_dir_flat[mask_out]], dim=-1))
            sigma_out[mask_out] = out_out[:, 0]
            color_out[mask_out] = out_out[:, 1:]
            
        deltas = t_vals[:, 1:] - t_vals[:, :-1]
        deltas = torch.cat([deltas, torch.tensor([1e10], device=points.device).expand_as(deltas[:, :1])], dim=-1)
        
        sigma_all = torch.where(is_exterior, sigma_out, sigma_in)
        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma_all * deltas)
        T = torch.cumprod(torch.exp(-sigma_all * deltas), dim=1)
        T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=-1)
        
        weights = alpha * T
        color = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * torch.where(is_exterior, color_out, color_in), dim=1)
        return color

    def train_step(self, ray_o, ray_d, gt_color):
        pred_color = self.forward(ray_o, ray_d)
        loss = torch.mean((pred_color - gt_color) ** 2)
        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()
        return loss.item()
```

**Parameters:**
*   $N$: Number of samples per ray. Not given in the sources.
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. Not given in the sources.
*   Sphere Radius: Typically 1.0 (unit sphere) [S1, S5].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision at Boundary:** Sampling points exactly on the sphere boundary ($\|\mathbf{x}\|=1$) can cause ambiguity in which MLP to query. It is recommended to use a small epsilon or consistently assign boundary points to one domain (e.g., interior) [G].
*   **Inversion Singularity:** Points at the origin ($\mathbf{x}=0$) cannot be inverted. However, the origin is inside the sphere, so it is handled by $F_{in}$ [G].

## Subtopics

### [Inverted Sphere Parameterization](5573-inverted-sphere-parameterization.md)
Inverted Sphere Parameterization is a spatial parameterization scheme that allows Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to model 360° captures of unbounded scenes by separately representing foreground and background content. It solves the parameterization failure of standard NeRF, which assumes the entire scene fits within a bounded volume, leading to either missing background elements or loss of detail due to limited sampling resolution in large-scale scenes. It sets itself apart from its parent [NeRF++](6198-nerf.md) by providing the specific mathematical mapping for the outer volume.

### [DoubleNeRF](7391-doublenerf.md)
DoubleNeRF is a method for synthesizing dynamic scenes by representing them as the superposition of two or more static neural radiance fields, specifically separating a static background from a moving object with a static shape. Unlike [NeRF++](6198-nerf.md), which separates scenes using a spherical boundary, DoubleNeRF separates content semantically (background vs. foreground object) without spherical constraints, and unlike [D-NeRF](5573-d-nerf.md) (not linked in tree but referenced in sources), it does not use time as an input or map canonical space to deformed space, assuming instead that the object's appearance is independent of its position relative to the background.

### [Physically Based Inverse Rendering with Split-Sum Approximation](11361-physically-based-inverse-rendering-with-split-sum-approximat.md)
This method recovers surface materials (albedo, roughness, metallic) and environment lighting from multi-view images by integrating Sur2f with differentiable rendering and a split-sum approximation of the rendering equation. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by decoupling geometry and appearance, allowing seamless transfer of different appearances to different geometries by exchanging the shading network, whereas other methods often couple them.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$ per pixel, where $N$ is the number of rays (pixels) and $S$ is the number of samples per ray [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(M)$, where $M$ is the number of parameters in the two MLPs [G].
*   **Measured Performance:** NeRF++ enables high-quality novel view synthesis for unbounded scenes, which standard NeRF fails to do without masking or truncation [S1, S5].
*   **Strengths:** Handles infinite background; no need for manual masking or background removal; maintains view-dependent effects in the background [G].
*   **When to use:** Unbounded scenes (e.g., outdoor environments, cityscapes) where the background extends to infinity [G].
*   **When not to use:** Bounded indoor scenes where standard NeRF suffices; scenes with strong occlusions that cross the sphere boundary in complex ways [G].

## Background
NeRF++ was introduced by Zhang et al. in October 2020 [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of the original NeRF [S6], which exhibited large computation requirements, difficulty modeling details of objects within a large scene, and vulnerability to variable illumination and transient occluders. The method was adapted to generate novel views for unbounded scenes by separating the scene using a sphere, with the inside containing foreground objects and the outside containing the background, which was reparameterized with radial inversion [S1]. It is a foundational method for unbounded NeRF variants [S1].
