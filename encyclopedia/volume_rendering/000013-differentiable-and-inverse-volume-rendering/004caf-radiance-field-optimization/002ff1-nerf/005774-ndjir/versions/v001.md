# NDJIR
Also called: Neural Direct and Joint Inverse Rendering, NDJIR

## Overview
NDJIR is a method for inverse rendering that jointly decomposes geometry (signed distance function), lights (environment and implicit), and materials (base color, roughness, specular reflectance) by directly addressing the integrals in the rendering equation using volume rendering, avoiding approximations like Spherical Gaussians or split-sum [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by computing the physically-based rendering integral directly in pixel space after volume-rendering spatially varying quantities, rather than approximating the light transport or relying on surface rendering with accurate masks [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [NeRF++](6198-nerf.md) for background modeling.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images with known camera poses, camera locations, and ray directions.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$, decomposed geometry (SDF $s$), materials (base color $\mathbf{c}_b$, roughness $\alpha_r$, specular reflectance $\mathbf{f}_0$), and lights (environment $L_E$, implicit $L_I$, photogrammetric $L_P$, visibility $L_V$).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid Feature:** Dense voxel grid with linear interpolation.
    *   Grid size: 512.
    *   Feature size: 4.
    *   Coordinates: Min $(-1, -1, -1)$, Max $(1, 1, 1)$.
    *   Implementation: CUDA, supports double-backward [S1, unofficial].
*   **MLP Architectures:**
    *   Geometric network $f_G$: 8 layers, skip connection at middle, output dimension $D_G=256$.
    *   Base color $\mathbf{c}_b$ and Photogrammetric light $L_P$: 4 layers, 256 feature size.
    *   Other networks ($\alpha_r, \mathbf{f}_0, L_E, L_V, L_I$): 4 layers, 128 feature size.
    *   Activation: `softplus` with $\beta=100$ for hidden layers, `sigmoid` for outputs (except $L_E, \sigma_{\alpha_r}, \sigma_{\mathbf{f}_0}$ which use `softplus` with $\beta=1$).
*   **Positional Encoding:**
    *   Geometric ($\ell_G$): 6 frequencies.
    *   Environment ($\ell_E$): 6 frequencies.
    *   Visibility ($\ell_V$): 6 frequencies.
    *   Photogrammetric ($\ell_P$): 4 frequencies.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Normalization:** Normalize scene so object is inside a unit sphere at origin. Voxel grid AABB is $[-1, 1]^3$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Sampling:**
    *   Sample 4 camera locations per iteration.
    *   Cast 512 rays per location.
    *   **Inside AABB:** If ray hits AABB, sample 64 points between near/far hits. Upsample 4 times by 16 points (total 64 points from upsampling, effectively denser sampling near surface) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Background:** Use NeRF++ strategy. Sample 32 points per ray starting from far hit of AABB.
    *   **Outside AABB:** If ray does not hit AABB, sample points only for background modeling from distance $\|\text{camera location}\|^2 - 1$ along the ray [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Network Forward Pass:**
    *   Query geometric network $f_G(\mathbf{x})$ to get SDF $s \in \mathbb{R}$ and geometric feature $\mathbf{F}_G \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$.
    *   Compute opacity $\alpha_j$ from adjacent SDF values $s_j, s_{j+1}$ using logistic CDF $\Phi$ with scale $\sigma$:
        $$ \alpha_j = \max \left( \frac{\Phi(s_j, \sigma) - \Phi(s_{j+1}, \sigma)}{\Phi(s_j, \sigma)}, 0 \right) $$
        where $\Phi(s, \sigma) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-s/\sigma}}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$.
    *   Query material networks using $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{F}_G$, and normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ (computed from SDF gradient):
        *   $\mathbf{c}_b(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{F}_G) \in \mathbb{R}^3$
        *   $\alpha_r(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{F}_G, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) \in \mathbb{R}$
        *   $\mathbf{f}_0(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{F}_G, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) \in \mathbb{R}^3$
    *   Query light networks:
        *   $L_E(\mathbf{l}) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Environment)
        *   $L_V(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{l}, \mathbf{F}_G, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Soft Visibility)
        *   $L_I(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{F}_G, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Implicit Illumination)
        *   $L_P(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{F}_G, \hat{\mathbf{n}}, d^{-2}) \in \mathbb{R}$ (Photogrammetric Light, where $d$ is distance) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Volume Rendering of Spatially Varying Quantities:** Compute volume-rendered versions of normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, roughness $\hat{\alpha}_r$, specular reflectance $\hat{\mathbf{f}}_0$, photogrammetric light $\hat{L}_P$, and implicit light $\hat{L}_I$ using the standard volume rendering equation:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
        Apply to each quantity individually. Add $n = 10^{-16}$ to volume-rendered normals $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ before unit-vector normalization to prevent zero-division [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Remapping:**
        *   $\alpha_r \leftarrow \alpha_r^2$
        *   $\mathbf{f}_0 \leftarrow 0.16 \times \mathbf{f}_0^2$
        *   Clip roughness: $\alpha_r \leftarrow \max(\alpha_r, 0.089)$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Integral Computation (Pixel Space):**
    *   Sample 128 incident light directions $\mathbf{l}$ per pixel.
    *   **Diffuse Term:**
        $$ \text{Diffuse} = \frac{\hat{L}_P \mathbf{c}_b}{\pi} \int_{\Omega_+} \left( L_V(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{l}) L_E(\mathbf{l}) (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l}) + \hat{L}_I(\mathbf{x}) \right) d\mathbf{l} $$
        Use Monte Carlo integration with uniform sampling [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Specular Term:**
        $$ \text{Specular} = \hat{L}_P \int_{\Omega_+} \frac{D(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}, \hat{\alpha}_r) G(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}, \hat{\alpha}_r) F(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{f}}_0)}{4(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{v})(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l})} L_V(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{l}) L_E(\mathbf{l}) (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l}) I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) d\mathbf{l} $$
        Use Monte Carlo integration with importance sampling considering roughness $\alpha_r$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Indicator Function:**
        $$ I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l} \ge I) \land (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{v} \ge I) \land \left(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \frac{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}}{\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}\|_2} \ge I\right) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        Threshold $I = 10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Total Color:** $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \text{Diffuse} + \text{Specular}$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{RGB} = \|\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r})\|_1$
    *   $\mathcal{L}_E = (\|\nabla s\|_2 - 1)^2$ (Eikonal constraint) [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{TV} = \sqrt{\Delta f_{i+1,j,k}^2 + \Delta f_{i,j+1,k}^2 + \Delta f_{i,j,k+1}^2}$ (Total Variation on voxel features) [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{c}_b} = \|\mathbf{c}_b(\mathbf{x}) - \mathbf{c}_b(\mathbf{x} + \epsilon_{\mathbf{c}_b})\|_1$ (Base color smoothness) [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\alpha_r} = \frac{\|\alpha_r - r_p\|_1}{\sigma_{\alpha_r}} + \log(\sigma_{\alpha_r})$ (Roughness prior, $r_p=0.5$) [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{f}_0} = \frac{\|\mathbf{f}_0 - 0.04\|_1}{\sigma_{\mathbf{f}_0}} + \log(\sigma_{\mathbf{f}_0})$ (Specular prior, 0.04) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Total Loss:**
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{RGB}}{N} + \lambda_E \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{EMA}}{M} + \lambda_{TV} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{TVMA}}{M} + \lambda_{cb} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{cb} \overline{M}_A}{M} + \lambda_{\alpha_r} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_r \overline{M}_A}}{M} + \lambda_{f_0} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{f_0} \overline{M}_A}{M} $$
        Where $N = \text{batch\_size}(4) \times \text{sampled\_pixels}(512)$, $M = \text{rays\_hitting\_AABB} \times \text{sampled\_points}(128)$, $\overline{M}_A$ is AABB-ray intersection mask, and $\overline{\cdot}$ denotes summation over all dimensions [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Optimization:**
    *   Adam optimizer, weight decay $10^{-3}$.
    *   Learning rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ with warmup for 1.5% of 1500 epochs, then cosine-decay to $5 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Do not propagate gradients through the sampling process [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes approximately 2.8 hours for 49 images and 3.7 hours for 64 images on an A100 GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a dense voxel grid of size $512^3$ with feature size 4, which is memory-intensive. The voxel grid feature is implemented in CUDA [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On Scan 24 of the DTU MVS dataset, NDJIR achieves a PSNR of 28.10 (vs. NeuS 23.98), SSIM of 0.820 (vs. NeuS 0.732), and Chamfer distance of 0.70 (vs. NeuS 1.00) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Directly addresses the rendering equation integral without approximations; jointly optimizes geometry, lights, and materials; produces high-poly meshes without requiring accurate object masks; exports meshes with materials to DCC tools [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Training can fail to bad local minima if implicit illumination is not used; roughness and specular reflectance networks can degenerate to unary/binary values without priors; voxel hash grid features cannot be trained with Eikonal regularization; extracted mesh is rugged and requires post-processing (average filter) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Inverse rendering of real objects in photogrammetric settings where high-quality geometry and material decomposition are required [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are limited due to the high memory and time cost; when approximate methods are sufficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
NDJIR was proposed by researchers from Sony, as indicated by the author affiliations in the source [S1, unofficial]. It was published as a preprint on arXiv in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. The method builds upon the volume rendering framework of NeuS and the background modeling of NeRF++. It addresses the limitations of prior inverse rendering methods that rely on approximations like Spherical Gaussians, split-sum, or co-located light, and surface rendering methods that require accurate object masks [S1, unofficial].
