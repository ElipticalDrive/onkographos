# NDJIR
Also called: Neural Direct and Joint Inverse Rendering, NDJIR

## Overview
NDJIR is an inverse rendering method that jointly decomposes geometry (signed distance function), lighting (environment and implicit), and materials (base color, roughness, specular reflectance) by directly addressing the integrals of the rendering equation using a volume rendering framework [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [DoubleNeRF](7391-doublenerf.md) or [SNeRF](14885-snerf.md) by avoiding approximations such as Spherical Gaussians, split-sum, or pre-integrated lights, instead computing the physically-based rendering integral directly within the volume rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images with known camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Signed Distance Function (SDF) $s$, geometric features $F_G$, base color $c_b$, roughness $\alpha_r$, specular reflectance $f_0$, environment light $L_E$, soft visibility $L_V$, implicit illumination $L_I$, and photogrammetric light $L_P$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** Dense voxel grid with linear interpolation.
    *   Grid Size: $512 \times 512 \times 512$.
    *   Feature Size: 4.
    *   Coordinates: Min $(-1, -1, -1)$, Max $(1, 1, 1)$.
    *   Implementation: CUDA, supports double-backward [S1, unofficial].
*   **MLP Architectures:**
    *   Geometric Network $f_G$: 8 layers, skip connection at middle, output dimension $D_G = 256$.
    *   Base Color $c_b$ and Photogrammetric Light $L_P$: 4 layers, 256 feature size.
    *   Other Networks ($\alpha_r, f_0, L_E, L_V, L_I$): 4 layers, 128 feature size.
    *   Activation: `softplus(β=100)` for hidden layers, `sigmoid` for outputs (except $L_E, \sigma_{\alpha_r}, \sigma_{f_0}$ which use `softplus(β=1)`).
*   **Positional Encoding Frequencies:**
    *   Geometric ($l_G$): 6.
    *   Environment Light ($l_E$): 6.
    *   Soft Visibility ($l_V$): 6.
    *   Photogrammetric Light ($l_P$): 4.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Normalization:** Normalize scene so object is inside a unit sphere at origin. Voxel grid AABB is $[-1, 1]^3$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Casting:**
    *   Sample 4 camera locations per iteration.
    *   Cast 512 rays per location.
    *   **If ray hits AABB:**
        *   Sample 64 points between near and far AABB hits.
        *   Upsample 4 times 16 points (total 64 points) [S1, unofficial].
        *   Compute volume rendering for spatially-varying quantities: roughness $\hat{\alpha}_r$, specular reflectance $\hat{f}_0$, normals $\hat{n}$, photogrammetric light $\hat{L}_P$, and implicit illumination $\hat{L}_I$.
        *   For background modeling, use NeRF++ [6198-nerf.md] and sample 32 points per ray starting from the far hit of AABB.
    *   **If ray does not hit AABB:**
        *   Sample points only for background modeling from location distance $\|camera\_location\|^2 - 1$ along that ray [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Volume Rendering of Quantities:**
    *   Compute opacity $\alpha_j$ from SDF $s_j, s_{j+1}$ and scale $\sigma$:
        $$ \alpha_j = \max \left( \frac{\Phi(s_j, \sigma) - \Phi(s_{j+1}, \sigma)}{\Phi(s_j, \sigma)}, 0 \right) $$
        where $\Phi$ is the CDF of the logistic distribution [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$.
    *   Render quantities $\hat{q}$ (where $q \in \{\alpha_r, f_0, n, L_P, L_I\}$) using:
        $$ \hat{q}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i q_i $$
    *   Add $n = 10^{-16}$ to volume rendered normals $\hat{n}$, then unit-vector normalize to prevent 0-division [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Lighting Model:**
    *   Compute photogrammetric light intensity $L_P(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{n})$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Combine with soft visibility $L_V$, environment map $L_E$, and implicit illumination $L_I$:
        $$ L_{in} = L_V(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{l}) L_E(\mathbf{l}) (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}) + L_I(\mathbf{x}) $$
5.  **Integral Computation (Pixel Space):**
    *   Use Monte Carlo integration with 128 incident lights per pixel. Do not propagate gradients in the sampling process [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Diffuse Term:** Entangle base color $c_b$ and photogrammetric light $\hat{L}_P$. Compute integral:
        $$ C_{diffuse} = \frac{\hat{L}_{pr} c_b}{\pi} \int_{\Omega_+} \left( L_V(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{l}) L_E(\mathbf{l}) (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l}) + \hat{L}_I(\mathbf{x}) \right) d\mathbf{l} $$
        Use uniform sampling [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Specular Term:** Compute integral:
        $$ C_{specular} = \hat{L}_p \int_{\Omega_+} \frac{D(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}, \hat{\alpha}_r) G(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}, \hat{\alpha}_r) F(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{f}}_0)}{4(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{v})(\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l})} L_V(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{l}) L_E(\mathbf{l}) (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l}) I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) d\mathbf{l} $$
        Use importance sampling considering roughness $\alpha_r$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Indicator Function:** Apply $I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}})$ to prevent gradient explosion:
        $$ I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{l} \ge I) \land (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \mathbf{v} \ge I) \land (\hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \frac{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}}{\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}\|_2} \ge I) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
        Threshold $I = 10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Remapping and Clipping:**
    *   Roughness: $\alpha_r \leftarrow \alpha_r^2$.
    *   Specular Reflectance: $f_0 \leftarrow 0.16 \times f_0^2$.
    *   Clip Roughness: $\alpha_r \leftarrow \max(\alpha_r, 0.089)$ to prevent 0-division [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Loss Calculation:**
    *   Compute losses defined below.
    *   Total Loss:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{RGB}}{N} + \lambda_E \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{EMA}}{M} + \lambda_{TV} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{TVMA}}{M} + \lambda_{cb} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{cb} \overline{M}_A}{M} + \lambda_{\alpha_r} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_r \overline{M}_A}}{M} + \lambda_{f_0} \frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{f_0} \overline{M}_A}{M} $$
        Where $\overline{\cdot}$ is summation of all dimensions, $N = 4 \times 512$, $M = (\text{rays hitting AABB}) \times 128$, $M_A$ is AABB-ray intersection mask [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def sigmoid(x):
    return 1.0 / (1.0 + torch.exp(-x))

def softplus(x, beta=100):
    return (1.0 / beta) * torch.log(1.0 + torch.exp(beta * x))

def logistic_cdf(s, sigma):
    # Phi(s, sigma) for logistic distribution
    # Assuming standard logistic CDF: 1 / (1 + exp(-s/sigma))
    # Note: Source uses Phi(s, sigma) in opacity formula. 
    # NeuS typically uses 1 / (1 + exp(-s/sigma)).
    return 1.0 / (1.0 + torch.exp(-s / sigma))

def compute_opacity(s_j, s_j1, sigma):
    phi_j = logistic_cdf(s_j, sigma)
    phi_j1 = logistic_cdf(s_j1, sigma)
    alpha = (phi_j - phi_j1) / phi_j
    return torch.clamp(alpha, min=0.0)

def indicator_function(n_hat, l, v, threshold=1e-8):
    dot_nl = torch.sum(n_hat * l, dim=-1)
    dot_nv = torch.sum(n_hat * v, dim=-1)
    v_plus_l = v + l
    norm_v_plus_l = torch.norm(v_plus_l, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    # Avoid division by zero
    norm_v_plus_l = torch.clamp(norm_v_plus_l, min=1e-8)
    dot_n_vpl = torch.sum(n_hat * (v_plus_l / norm_v_plus_l), dim=-1)
    
    cond1 = dot_nl >= threshold
    cond2 = dot_nv >= threshold
    cond3 = dot_n_vpl >= threshold
    
    return (cond1 & cond2 & cond3).float()

def ndjir_forward(ray_origins, ray_directions, camera_positions, 
                  f_G, c_b, alpha_r_net, f_0_net, L_E_net, L_V_net, L_I_net, L_P_net,
                  voxel_grid, sigma, batch_size=4, rays_per_cam=512, 
                  points_inside=64, points_bg=32, incident_lights=128):
    
    # 1. Ray Casting and Sampling
    # Sample 4 camera locations
    cam_locs = sample_camera_locations(batch_size) # Implementation detail: select from dataset
    
    all_diffuse_colors = []
    all_specular_colors = []
    all_normals = []
    all_sdfs = []
    all_masks = [] # For AABB hit mask
    
    for cam_loc in cam_locs:
        # Cast 512 rays
        rays = cast_rays(cam_loc, rays_per_cam)
        
        diffuse_sum = 0.0
        specular_sum = 0.0
        normal_sum = 0.0
        sdf_sum = 0.0
        mask_sum = 0.0
        
        for ray in rays:
            origin, direction = ray
            hit_aabb = check_aabb_intersection(origin, direction)
            
            if hit_aabb:
                near, far = get_aabb_hits(origin, direction)
                # Sample 64 points between near/far
                t_vals = torch.linspace(near, far, points_inside)
                points = origin + t_vals.unsqueeze(1) * direction.unsqueeze(0)
                
                # Upsample 4 times 16 points (total 64)
                # Note: Source says "upsample 4 times 16 points". 
                # This likely means 4 passes of upsampling or specific stratified sampling.
                # For implementation, we assume we have 64 points sampled.
                # NeuS typically uses stratified sampling.
                
                # Query Voxel Grid for Geometric Feature F_G
                # F_G is dense voxel grid, linear interpolation
                F_G = query_voxel_grid(voxel_grid, points)
                
                # Query MLPs
                s, F_G_out = f_G(points, F_G) # s is SDF, F_G_out is geometric feature
                n_hat = -torch.nn.functional.normalize(torch.autograd.grad(s.sum(), points, create_graph=True)[0], dim=-1)
                
                # Volume Render Quantities
                # Compute opacity
                # Need adjacent SDFs. NeuS uses s_j and s_{j+1}.
                # Here we assume s is computed at each point.
                # Opacity calculation requires adjacent points.
                # Standard NeuS approach:
                alphas = []
                for i in range(len(points) - 1):
                    alpha = compute_opacity(s[i], s[i+1], sigma)
                    alphas.append(alpha)
                alphas.append(0.0) # Last point alpha is 0
                
                # Transmittance
                T = torch.ones_like(alphas)
                for i in range(1, len(alphas)):
                    T[i] = T[i-1] * (1.0 - alphas[i-1])
                
                # Render Spatially Varying Quantities
                # Roughness
                alpha_r_val = alpha_r_net(points, F_G_out, n_hat)
                alpha_r_val = alpha_r_val ** 2 # Remapping
                alpha_r_val = torch.clamp(alpha_r_val, min=0.089) # Clipping
                alpha_r_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * alpha_r_val, dim=0)
                
                # Specular Reflectance
                f_0_val = f_0_net(points, F_G_out, n_hat)
                f_0_val = 0.16 * (f_0_val ** 2) # Remapping
                f_0_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * f_0_val, dim=0)
                
                # Normals
                n_hat_rendered = torch.sum(T * alphas * n_hat, dim=0)
                n_hat_rendered = n_hat_rendered + 1e-16 # Add epsilon
                n_hat_rendered = torch.nn.functional.normalize(n_hat_rendered, dim=-1)
                
                # Photogrammetric Light
                # L_P depends on x, v, F_G, n, d^-1
                # d is distance from camera? Source says d^-1 input.
                d_inv = 1.0 / (torch.norm(points - cam_loc, dim=-1) + 1e-8)
                L_P_val = L_P_net(points, direction, F_G_out, n_hat, d_inv)
                L_P_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * L_P_val, dim=0)
                
                # Implicit Illumination
                L_I_val = L_I_net(points, F_G_out, n_hat)
                L_I_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * L_I_val, dim=0)
                
                # Soft Visibility
                # L_V depends on x, l, F_G, n
                # Note: L_V is sampled at x, but l is light direction.
                # In the integral, L_V(x, l) is used.
                # We need to evaluate L_V for each light direction.
                
                # Background Modeling
                # Sample 32 points from far hit
                bg_t_vals = torch.linspace(far, far + 10.0, points_bg) # Arbitrary far distance
                bg_points = origin + bg_t_vals.unsqueeze(1) * direction.unsqueeze(0)
                # ... similar volume rendering for background color ...
                
                # 2. Integral Computation
                # Sample 128 incident lights
                light_directions = sample_light_directions(incident_lights) # Uniform or Importance
                
                diffuse_color = 0.0
                specular_color = 0.0
                
                for l in light_directions:
                    # Environment Light
                    L_E_val = L_E_net(l)
                    
                    # Soft Visibility
                    # L_V(x, l, F_G, n)
                    # We need to evaluate this at the volume rendered point?
                    # Source says L_V(x, l). x is the point on ray.
                    # But we volume rendered L_V? No, source says "one exception is computing L_V, we apply volume rendering to x".
                    # This implies L_V is not volume rendered, but evaluated at x?
                    # "we first reduce point dimension by volume rendering ... one exception is computing L_V, we apply the volume rendering Eq 1 to x"
                    # This is ambiguous. It likely means L_V is evaluated at the volume rendered position or averaged.
                    # Given L_V is a scalar field, we can evaluate it at the volume rendered point x_hat?
                    # Or is L_V volume rendered? "apply the volume rendering Eq 1 to x" suggests we render L_V.
                    # Let's assume L_V is volume rendered like other quantities.
                    L_V_val = L_V_net(points, l, F_G_out, n_hat)
                    L_V_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * L_V_val, dim=0)
                    
                    # Dot products
                    dot_nl = torch.sum(n_hat_rendered * l, dim=-1)
                    dot_nv = torch.sum(n_hat_rendered * direction, dim=-1)
                    
                    # Indicator Function
                    I = indicator_function(n_hat_rendered, l, direction)
                    
                    # Diffuse Term
                    # (L_pr * cb / pi) * integral(...)
                    # cb is volume rendered? Source says "entangle cb and Lp".
                    # cb is volume rendered c_b_hat.
                    c_b_val = c_b_net(points, F_G_out)
                    c_b_hat = torch.sum(T * alphas * c_b_val, dim=0)
                    
                    # Integrand for diffuse
                    diffuse_integrand = (L_V_hat * L_E_val * dot_nl + L_I_hat)
                    diffuse_color += diffuse_integrand
                    
                    # Specular Term
                    # D, G, F are Filament BRDF functions
                    # D = D(v, l, n, alpha_r)
                    # G = G(v, l, n, alpha_r)
                    # F = F(v, l, f_0)
                    D = compute_D(direction, l, n_hat_rendered, alpha_r_hat)
                    G = compute_G(direction, l, n_hat_rendered, alpha_r_hat)
                    F_brdf = compute_F(direction, l, f_0_hat)
                    
                    denominator = 4.0 * dot_nv * dot_nl
                    # Avoid division by zero
                    denominator = torch.clamp(denominator, min=1e-8)
                    
                    specular_integrand = (D * G * F_brdf / denominator) * L_V_hat * L_E_val * dot_nl * I
                    specular_color += specular_integrand
                
                # Final Colors
                diffuse_color = (L_P_hat * c_b_hat / torch.pi) * diffuse_color
                specular_color = L_P_hat * specular_color
                
                diffuse_sum += diffuse_color
                specular_sum += specular_color
                normal_sum += n_hat_rendered
                sdf_sum += s
                mask_sum += 1.0
            else:
                # Background only
                # Sample points from location distance ||cam_loc||^2 - 1
                dist = torch.norm(cam_loc) ** 2 - 1
                bg_t_vals = torch.linspace(dist, dist + 10.0, points_bg)
                bg_points = origin + bg_t_vals.unsqueeze(1) * direction.unsqueeze(0)
                # ... background rendering ...
                pass
                
        all_diffuse_colors.append(diffuse_sum)
        all_specular_colors.append(specular_sum)
        all_normals.append(normal_sum)
        all_sdfs.append(sdf_sum)
        all_masks.append(mask_sum)
        
    return all_diffuse_colors, all_specular_colors, all_normals, all_sdfs, all_masks

def compute_losses(diffuse_colors, specular_colors, normals, sdfs, masks, gt_colors):
    # Reconstruct render color
    render_colors = diffuse_colors + specular_colors
    
    # L_RGB
    L_RGB = torch.mean(torch.abs(render_colors - gt_colors))
    
    # L_E (Eikonal)
    # Source: L_E = (||s||_2 - 1)^2
    # This is likely applied to the SDF values at sampled points
    L_E = torch.mean((torch.norm(sdfs, dim=-1) - 1.0) ** 2)
    
    # L_TV (Total Variation)
    # Applied to voxel grid features
    # L_TV = sqrt(sum(delta_f^2))
    # Implementation requires accessing voxel grid features and computing differences
    L_TV = compute_total_variation(voxel_grid)
    
    # L_cb (Base Color Prior)
    # L_cb = ||c_b(x) - c_b(x + eps)||_1
    # Perturb x and compute difference
    L_cb = compute_base_color_prior(c_b_net)
    
    # L_alpha_r (Roughness Prior)
    # L_alpha_r = ||alpha_r - r_p||_1 / sigma_alpha_r + log(sigma_alpha_r)
    # r_p = 0.5
    L_alpha_r = compute_roughness_prior(alpha_r_net, sigma_alpha_r_net, r_p=0.5)
    
    # L_f0 (Specular Prior)
    # L_f0 = ||f_0 - 0.04||_1 / sigma_f0 + log(sigma_f0)
    L_f0 = compute_specular_prior(f_0_net, sigma_f0_net, prior=0.04)
    
    # Total Loss
    # N = batch_size * rays_per_cam = 4 * 512
    # M = rays_hitting_AABB * points_inside = (sum of masks) * 128? 
    # Source: M = rays_hitting_AABB * sampled_points(128). 
    # Note: sampled_points(128) refers to incident lights, not volume samples.
    # M is used for normalization of regularization losses.
    # M_A is AABB-ray intersection mask.
    
    N = 4 * 512
    M = torch.sum(masks) * 128 # Approximation
    
    lambda_E = 1e-1
    lambda_TV = 1e-1
    lambda_cb = 1e-1
    lambda_alpha_r = 1e-5
    lambda_f0 = 1e-3
    
    # Note: Source formula for total loss uses overline notation for summation.
    # L_RGB_bar / N
    # L_EMA_bar / M ...
    
    total_loss = (L_RGB / N) + \
                 lambda_E * (L_E / M) + \
                 lambda_TV * (L_TV / M) + \
                 lambda_cb * (L_cb / M) + \
                 lambda_alpha_r * (L_alpha_r / M) + \
                 lambda_f0 * (L_f0 / M)
                 
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma$: Globally trainable scale parameter for logistic CDF.
*   $I$: Threshold for indicator function, $10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_E = 10^{-1}$: Eikonal loss weight.
*   $\lambda_{TV} = 10^{-1}$: Total variation loss weight.
*   $\lambda_{cb} = 10^{-1}$: Base color prior weight.
*   $\lambda_{\alpha_r} = 10^{-5}$: Roughness prior weight.
*   $\lambda_{f_0} = 10^{-3}$: Specular reflectance prior weight.
*   $r_p = 0.5$: Prior roughness value.
*   Learning Rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ with warmup for 1.5% of 1500 epochs, then cosine-decay to $5 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Weight Decay: $10^{-3}$ for Adam [S1, unofficial].
*   Batch Size: 4 camera locations.
*   Rays per Iteration: 512 per camera location.
*   Points Sampled (Inside AABB): 64.
*   Background Points per Ray: 32.
*   Incident Lights per Pixel: 128.
*   $n = 10^{-16}$: Epsilon added to normals.
*   $D_G = 256$: Geometric feature dimension.
*   $\beta = 100$: Softplus parameter for hidden layers.
*   $\beta = 1$: Softplus parameter for output layers of $L_E, \sigma_{\alpha_r}, \sigma_{f_0}$.
*   $l_G = 6, l_E = 6, l_V = 6, l_P = 4$: Positional encoding frequencies.
*   Total Epochs: 1500 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Gradient Explosion:** Negative dot products in the denominator of the specular term (e.g., $\hat{n} \cdot l, \hat{n} \cdot v$) can cause gradient explosion due to saturated dot products increasing the specular term value. Mitigated by the indicator function $I(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{l}, \hat{\mathbf{n}})$ with threshold $I=10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Entanglement:** Failure to entangle $L_P$ and $c_b$ causes training to not successfully start [S1, unofficial].
*   **Local Minima:** Training fails to bad local minima if implicit illumination $L_I$ is not used [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degeneration:** Roughness and specular reflectance networks tend to degenerate to unary (0 or 1) or binary values. Mitigated by priors $L_{\alpha_r}$ and $L_{f_0}$ and using smaller MLPs/increasing incident lights [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Hash Grid:** Voxel hash grid feature cannot be trained when using Eikonal regularization, conjectured due to simultaneous use [S1, unofficial].
*   **Triplane/Triline:** Extracts smoother geometry but causes bad foreground/background separation and worse semantic material separation compared to dense voxel grid [S1, unofficial].
*   **Mesh Ruggedness:** Extracted mesh from voxel grid feature is rugged. Handled by applying simple average filter 2 times as default [S1, unofficial].
*   **Asymmetric Backward:** Asymmetric backward in total variation loss causes baked light on base color. Handled by using symmetric backward [S1, unofficial].
*   **Normal Bias:** Small $n$ added to normals produces bias but is necessary to prevent 0-division at the beginning of training [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 2.8 hours for 49 images and 3.7 hours for 64 images on an A100 GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a dense voxel grid of size $512^3$ with feature size 4, which is memory-intensive.
*   **Performance:** On DTU MVS Scan 24, NDJIR achieves PSNR 28.10 (vs NeuS 23.98), SSIM 0.820 (vs NeuS 0.732), and Chamfer Distance 0.70 (vs NeuS 1.00) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Directly addresses the rendering equation integral without approximations; jointly decomposes geometry, lights, and materials; exports meshes with materials to DCC tools; handles photogrammetric settings with dynamic light masks.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate camera poses; sensitive to initialization; dense voxel grid is memory-heavy; training can fail without implicit illumination or proper priors.
*   **When to Use:** Inverse rendering of real objects in photogrammetric settings where high-fidelity geometry and material decomposition are required.
*   **When Not to Use:** When computational resources are limited (due to dense voxel grid and long training times) or when approximations are acceptable for faster results.

## Background
NDJIR was proposed by Sony researchers (Yama, Narihira) in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the volume rendering framework of NeuS [57] and the background modeling of NeRF++ [6198-nerf.md]. The method addresses the limitations of prior inverse rendering works that rely on approximations like Spherical Gaussians, split-sum, or pre-integrated light, and surface rendering methods that require accurate object masks [S1, unofficial]. It is evaluated on the DTU MVS dataset [29] with 15 scenes, 49/64 images, and 16 LEDs [S1, unofficial].
