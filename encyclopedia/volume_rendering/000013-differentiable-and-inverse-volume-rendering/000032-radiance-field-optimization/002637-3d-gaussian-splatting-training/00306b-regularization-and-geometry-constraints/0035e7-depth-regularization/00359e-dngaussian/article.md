# DNGaussian

Also called: Depth Normalization Regularized Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Radiance Fields

## Overview
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing a two-stage depth regularization process that explicitly manages the trade-off between surface positioning (Hard) and surface completeness (Soft) [S1]. Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, which lowers PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible regions but allows the optimization to focus strictly on visible geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of input images (typically 3 views) with known camera poses and intrinsics; pre-predicted monocular depth maps for these input views generated by a monocular depth estimator (e.g., DPT) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives defined by centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$, and colors $c$ (predicted by a neural renderer), enabling novel view synthesis [S1].

**Data Structures**
The method maintains a list of $N$ Gaussian primitives. Each primitive $i$ stores:
*   $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D center position.
*   $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scaling factors.
*   $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation quaternion.
*   $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
*   Color is not stored as spherical harmonics coefficients but is predicted by a Neural Color Renderer [S1].

**Neural Color Renderer**
To prevent overfitting on sparse views, the method replaces standard spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer [S1]. This consists of:
1.  A hash encoder with 16 levels, resolution range 16 to 512, and max size $2^{19}$ [S1].
2.  A 5-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with a hidden dimension of 64 [S1].
During inference, intermediate results are stored, and only the last MLP layers are calculated to merge view direction for acceleration [S1].

**Parameters**
*   $\tau$ (Hard Depth Opacity): 0.95 [S1].
*   $\gamma$ (Global Loss Weight): 0.1 [S1].
*   Training Iterations: 6,000 [S1].
*   Soft Depth Start Iteration: 1,000 [S1].
*   Monocular Depth Estimator: DPT [S1].

**Algorithm Steps**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize a random set of Gaussians with a uniform distribution. Do not use point clouds from COLMAP or other SfM systems, as they are unstable in sparse-view situations [S1].
2.  **Monocular Depth Prediction:** Use DPT to predict monocular depth maps $\tilde{D}$ for all input views [S1].
3.  **Color Supervision (Standard 3DGS Optimization):**
    *   Render the scene using the differentiable rasterizer.
    *   Compute color loss $L_{color}$ using the Neural Color Renderer.
    *   Apply standard 3DGS gradient descent, including primitive duplication (for active primitives) and removal (for redundant primitives) [S1].
    *   Note: The Neural Color Renderer predicts color $c$ for each primitive based on its center $\mu$ and view direction [S1].
4.  **Hard Depth Regularization (Iterations 1 to 1,000):**
    *   Freeze scaling $s$ and rotation $q$ to reduce negative influence on color reconstruction [S1].
    *   Optimize only center $\mu$ and opacity $\alpha$ [S1].
    *   Render "Hard Depth" $D_{hard}$ by applying a large opacity value $\tau$ to all Gaussians. This ensures the depth is dominated by the nearest Gaussian on each ray [S1].
    *   Calculate Hard Depth Loss $R_{hard}$ using Global-Local Depth Normalization (see Formulas).
5.  **Soft Depth Regularization (Iterations 1,000 to 6,000):**
    *   Freeze Gaussian centers $\check{\mu}$ (the centers optimized during the Hard phase) to avoid negative influence from center movement [S1].
    *   Optimize only opacity $\alpha$ to ensure surface completeness and prevent hollowness [S1].
    *   Render "Soft Depth" $D_{soft}$ using the frozen centers $\check{\mu}$ and current opacities $\alpha$ [S1].
    *   Calculate Soft Depth Loss $R_{soft}$ using Global-Local Depth Normalization.
6.  **Total Loss Calculation:**
    *   Combine losses: $L = L_{color} + R_{hard} + R_{soft}$ [S1].
    *   Backpropagate gradients to update parameters.

**Formulas**

**Hard Depth Rendering:**
$$ D_{hard}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} \tau (1 - \tau)^{i-1} G^{proj}_i(x_p) ||\mu_i - o||_2 $$
Where:
*   $x_p$: Pixel coordinate.
*   $\tau$: Applied opacity (0.95) [S1].
*   $i$: Ray hit order (sorted by depth).
*   $G^{proj}_i(x_p)$: Projected 2D Gaussian contribution at pixel $x_p$ [S1].
*   $\mu_i$: Center of the $i$-th Gaussian [S1].
*   $o$: Camera center [S1].

**Soft Depth Rendering:**
$$ D_{soft}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} ||\check{\mu}_i - o||_2 \times \tilde{\alpha}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \tilde{\alpha}_j) $$
Where:
*   $\check{\mu}_i$: Frozen Gaussian center [S1].
*   $\tilde{\alpha}_i$: Current opacity of the $i$-th Gaussian [S1].
*   The product term $\prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \tilde{\alpha}_j)$ represents the cumulative transparency [S1].

**Depth Normalization and Loss:**
The method uses Global-Local Depth Normalization to refine detailed geometry reshaping [S1].
*   $D^{GN}_T$: Global Normalized Depth for type $T$ (hard or soft).
*   $D^{LN}_T$: Local Normalized Depth for type $T$.
*   $\tilde{D}^{GN}$: Global Normalized Monocular Depth.
*   $\tilde{D}^{LN}$: Local Normalized Monocular Depth.
*   $\gamma$: Global loss weight (0.1) [S1].

The regularization loss $R_T$ for $T \in \{hard, soft\}$ is:
$$ R_T = L_2(D^{GN}_T, \tilde{D}^{GN}) + \gamma L_2(D^{LN}_T, \tilde{D}^{LN}) $$
Where $L_2$ denotes the L2 loss [S1].

The total color loss $L_{color}$ is:
$$ L_{color} = L_1(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda_{LD-SSIM} L_{D-SSIM}(\hat{I}, I) $$
Where $\hat{I}$ is the rendered image, $I$ is the ground truth image, and $L_{D-SSIM}$ is the D-SSIM loss [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 6,000 iterations [S1]. DNGaussian achieves a 25× reduction in training time compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly lower memory overhead compared to NeRF-based methods [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Over 3000× faster rendering speed compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Restores accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision; preserves fine-grained color appearance; efficient training and rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Exposes black background on empty spaces, which can lower PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible areas compared to NeRFs that interpolate colors [S1]. Performance depends on the quality of the monocular depth estimator (DPT) [S1].
*   **When to use:** Sparse-view novel view synthesis (e.g., 3 input views) where accurate geometry is critical and computational efficiency is desired [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Dense-view scenarios where standard 3DGS with spherical harmonics may suffice; scenarios where invisible area color interpolation is required for high PSNR/SSIM metrics [S1].

## Background
DNGaussian was proposed by Xiao Bai et al. in 2024 [S1]. It addresses the degradation of scene geometry in 3D Gaussian Splatting when trained on sparse input views, a problem where Gaussian primitive positioning becomes incorrectly learned [S1]. The method builds on the principles of depth regularization in sparse-view NeRFs but adapts them to the discrete and flexible nature of 3D Gaussian primitives [S1]. It was implemented on the official PyTorch 3D Gaussian Splatting codebase [S1].
