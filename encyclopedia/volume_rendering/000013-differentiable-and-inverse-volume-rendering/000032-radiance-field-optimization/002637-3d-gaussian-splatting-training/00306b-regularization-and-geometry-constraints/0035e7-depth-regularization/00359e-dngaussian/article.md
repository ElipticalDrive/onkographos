# DNGaussian

Also called: Depth Normalization Regularized Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Radiance Fields

## Overview
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing explicit depth constraints that freeze specific Gaussian parameters to guide geometry reconstruction [S1]. Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, which may lower PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible regions but allows the model to focus on visible geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of input images (typically 3 views) with known camera poses, and pre-predicted monocular depth maps for these views generated by a monocular depth estimator (e.g., DPT) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives (centers $\mu$, scales $s$, rotations $q$, opacities $\alpha$, and color features) representing the scene radiance field [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method uses the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting primitive structure but modifies the color representation:
*   **Gaussian Primitive:**
    *   $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D center position.
    *   $s \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scaling factors (3D).
    *   $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation quaternion.
    *   $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity.
    *   **Color:** Instead of spherical harmonics coefficients, the color is predicted by a **Neural Color Renderer** [S1].
*   **Neural Color Renderer:**
    *   **Hash Encoder:** Sparse hash grid with 16 levels, resolution range 16 to 512, max size $2^{19}$ [S1].
    *   **MLP:** 5-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron with hidden dimension 64 [S1].
    *   The renderer takes the hash-encoded features and view direction as input to predict the final color [S1].

**Initialization:**
1.  Initialize a random set of Gaussian primitives using a uniform distribution [S1].
    *   *Note:* Standard 3DGS often initializes with COLMAP point clouds, but DNGaussian uses random initialization because point clouds are unstable in sparse-view situations [S1].

**Optimization Steps:**
The optimization runs for 6,000 iterations [S1]. It consists of two concurrent modules: Color Supervision and Depth Regularization [S1].

1.  **Color Supervision:**
    *   Inherits the standard 3DGS gradient descent and primitive duplication/removal process (densification) [S1].
    *   Replaces spherical harmonics with the Neural Color Renderer to predict color for each primitive [S1].
    *   During inference, intermediate results are stored, and only the last MLP layers are calculated to merge view direction for acceleration [S1].

2.  **Depth Regularization:**
    *   This module applies depth constraints using two phases: Hard Depth Regularization and Soft Depth Regularization [S1].
    *   **Hard Depth Regularization (Iterations 0–1000+):**
        *   Freeze scaling $s$ and rotation $q$ [S1].
        *   Apply a large opacity value $\tau$ to all Gaussians to render a "hard depth" dominated by the nearest Gaussians [S1].
        *   Optimize only center $\mu$ and opacity $\alpha$ to move Gaussians to correct positions [S1].
        *   Render Hard Depth $D_{hard}$:
            $$ D_{hard}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} \tau (1 - \tau)^{i-1} G^{proj}_i(x_p) ||\mu_i - o||_2 $$
            Where:
            *   $x_p$: Pixel coordinate.
            *   $N$: Set of Gaussians along the ray.
            *   $\tau$: Applied opacity (set to 0.95) [S1].
            *   $i$: Ray hit order (sorted by distance).
            *   $G^{proj}_i(x_p)$: Projected 2D Gaussian contribution at pixel $x_p$.
            *   $\mu_i$: Center of the $i$-th Gaussian.
            *   $o$: Camera center [S1].
        *   Calculate Hard Depth Loss $R_{hard}$:
            $$ R_{hard}(P) = L_{similar}(D_{hard}(P), \tilde{D}(P)) $$
            Where $P$ is the target image area and $\tilde{D}$ is the normalized monocular depth [S1].
    *   **Soft Depth Regularization (Starts after 1,000 iterations):**
        *   Applied for stability to ensure surface completeness and prevent hollowness [S1].
        *   Freeze Gaussian centers $\check{\mu}$ (denoted as $\check{\mu}$ in formulas) to avoid negative influence from center movement [S1].
        *   Tune opacity $\alpha$ to ensure surface completeness [S1].
        *   Render Soft Depth $D_{soft}$:
            $$ D_{soft}(x_p) = \sum_{i \in N} ||\check{\mu}_i - o||_2 \times \tilde{\alpha}_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \tilde{\alpha}_j) $$
            Where:
            *   $\check{\mu}$: Frozen Gaussian centers.
            *   $\tilde{\alpha}$: Normalized opacity.
            *   The product term represents cumulative transparency [S1].
        *   Calculate Soft Depth Loss $R_{soft}$:
            $$ R_{soft}(P) = L_{similar}(D_{soft}(P), \tilde{D}(P)) $$

3.  **Global-Local Depth Normalization:**
    *   To refine detailed geometry reshaping and focus on small local depth changes, the method applies Global-Local Depth Normalization to the depth losses [S1].
    *   Previous methods use fixed scales based on large area statistics, which overlook small errors [S1].
    *   The loss $R_T$ (for $T \in \{hard, soft\}$) is calculated as:
        $$ R_T = L_2(D^{GN}_T, \tilde{D}^{GN}) + \gamma L_2(D^{LN}_T, \tilde{D}^{LN}) $$
        Where:
        *   $D^{GN}_T$: Global Normalized Depth.
        *   $\tilde{D}^{GN}$: Global Normalized Monocular Depth.
        *   $D^{LN}_T$: Local Normalized Depth.
        *   $\tilde{D}^{LN}$: Local Normalized Monocular Depth.
        *   $\gamma$: Global loss weight (set to 0.1) [S1].
        *   $L_2$: L2 loss.
        *   $L_{similar}$: Similarity loss, implemented as L2 loss with an error tolerance to relax the constraint [S1].

4.  **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{color} + R_{hard} + R_{soft} $$
    Where:
    $$ L_{color} = L_1(\hat{I}, I) + \lambda_{LD-SSIM} L_{D-SSIM}(\hat{I}, I) $$
    *   $L_1$: L1 loss between rendered image $\hat{I}$ and ground truth $I$.
    *   $L_{D-SSIM}$: D-SSIM loss [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\tau$ (Hard Depth Opacity): 0.95 [S1].
*   $\gamma$ (Global Loss Weight): 0.1 [S1].
*   Training Iterations: 6,000 [S1].
*   Soft Depth Start Iteration: 1,000 [S1].
*   Neural Color Renderer: Hash encoder (16 levels, 16-512 resolution, $2^{19}$ max size) + 5-layer MLP (hidden dim 64) [S1].
*   Monocular Depth Estimator: DPT [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Sparse View Instability:** Decreasing input views causes significant geometry degradation in standard 3DGS because scene geometry relies on Gaussian primitive positioning, which becomes incorrectly learned under sparse constraints [S1]. DNGaussian mitigates this with depth regularization [S1].
*   **Overfitting:** Standard spherical harmonics can overfit on sparse views. DNGaussian uses a Neural Color Renderer to relieve this [S1].
*   **Hollowness:** Without Soft Depth Regularization, surfaces may become semitransparent and cause hollowness [S1].
*   **Invisible Areas:** 3DGS-based methods expose black background on empty spaces, causing lower metrics in PSNR/SSIM for invisible areas compared to NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** DNGaussian achieves a 25× reduction in training time compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Significantly lower memory overhead compared to NeRF-based methods [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Over 3000× faster rendering speed compared to state-of-the-art sparse-view NeRFs [S1].
*   **Performance:** On LLFF, DTU, and Blender datasets, DNGaussian achieves comparable or better results than state-of-the-art methods [S1]. It outperforms baselines in LPIPS score and achieves comparable PSNR, SSIM, and Average Error [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient and high-quality novel view synthesis from sparse inputs; preserves fine-grained color appearance; restores accurate scene geometry [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Lower PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible areas due to black background exposure [S1]. Requires pre-predicted monocular depth maps [S1].
