# Negative Gaussian Splatting


## Overview
Negative Gaussian Splatting (NegGS) is a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that incorporates negative Gaussians (ellipsoids with negative color values) into the scene representation to model high-frequency elements and complex nonlinear structures [S1]. It differs from standard Gaussian Splatting by allowing two Gaussians with identical parameters and inverse colors to reduce each other, providing greater flexibility in shape representation and color modulation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of calibrated images of a scene, camera poses, and an initial point cloud.
*   **Outputs:** A joint family of Gaussians $G_{NeGS}$ representing the 3D scene, optimized to minimize rendering error.

**Data Structures:**
The method extends the standard Gaussian Splatting data structure. Each Gaussian $i$ is defined by a tuple containing:
*   Position $\mathbf{m}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
*   Covariance matrix $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (often parameterized by rotation and scale)
*   Opacity $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
*   Color $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (can be positive or negative)
*   A binary mask $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicating whether the Gaussian is positive ($m_i=1$) or negative ($m_i=0$) [S1].

The rendering pipeline remains identical to standard Gaussian Splatting, but the color accumulation step sums contributions from both positive and negative Gaussians. The binary mask is used to track the sign of the color during optimization without altering the rendering speed [S1].

**Optimization Strategy:**
The optimization operates on a joint family of Gaussians:
$$ G_{NeGS} = G_{GS} \cup G_{-GS} $$
where $G_{GS}$ contains tuples with positive color values $c_i$ and $G_{-GS}$ contains tuples with negative color values $c_i$ [S1].

1.  **Initialization:** The initial number of negative Gaussians is a critical hyperparameter. It is typically set to approximately 20% of the total Gaussians during initialization [S1].
2.  **Gradient Descent:** Standard differentiable rendering gradients are computed with respect to all parameters, including the color signs implicitly handled by the mask [S1].
3.  **Pruning and Densification:**
    *   Gaussians with low opacity are pruned (reduced) [S1].
    *   New Gaussians are introduced into empty 3D regions to dynamically adapt the number of components [S1].
    *   The number of positive and negative components is treated as a hyperparameter, adapted through grid search during cross-validation [S1].

**Mathematical Foundation (Diff-Gaussians):**
The method leverages the concept of "Diff-Gaussians," which are formed by the difference of two standard Gaussians. The optimal coefficient $c_{opt}$ for a Diff-Gaussian distribution formed by two Gaussians $f_{m_0, \Sigma_0}$ and $f_{m_1, \Sigma_1}$ is defined as [S1]:

$$ c_{opt} := \min_x \frac{f_{m_0, \Sigma_0}(x)}{f_{m_1, \Sigma_1}(x)} = \frac{f_{m_0, \Sigma_0}(w)}{f_{m_1, \Sigma_1}(w)} $$

if and only if $w$ satisfies [S1]:

$$ \Sigma_1^{-1}(w - m_1) = \Sigma_0^{-1}(w - m_0) $$

The vector $w$ minimizing the ratio of two Gaussian densities is the argument that minimizes the quadratic function [S1]:

$$ q(x) = x^T A x + 2b^T x $$

where [S1]:
$$ A = \Sigma_1^{-1} - \Sigma_0^{-1} $$
$$ b = \Sigma_0^{-1} m_0 - \Sigma_1^{-1} m_1 $$

Assuming $A$ is positive semi-definite, a necessary and sufficient condition for $w$ is that it be a solution to the linear system derived from the gradient of $q(x)$ [S1].

For diagonal covariance matrices $\Sigma_0 = \text{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2)$ and $\Sigma_1 = \text{diag}(h_1 \sigma_1^2, \dots, h_n \sigma_n^2)$ with $0 < h_i \le 1$, the coordinates $w_i$ of the null density vector are [S1]:
1.  $w_i = \frac{m_{1,i} - h_i m_{0,i}}{1 - h_i}$ if $h_i < 1$
2.  $w_i \in \mathbb{R}$ if $h_i = 1$ and $m_{1,i} = m_{0,i}$

This allows the imposition of a null density on a $k$-dimensional affine space by aligning means and variances [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
# NegGS Optimization Step
# Assumes standard GS rasterization and gradient computation are available
# but extended to support negative colors.

def optimize_neggs(scene_data, initial_points, num_iterations):
    # 1. Initialization
    # Create initial Gaussian tuples
    gaussians = []
    total_gaussians = len(initial_points)
    num_negative = int(0.2 * total_gaussians) # 20% negative Gaussians [S1]
    
    for i, point in enumerate(initial_points):
        g = {
            'position': point.position,
            'covariance': compute_covariance(point.scale, point.rotation),
            'opacity': 1.0,
            'color': point.color,
            'is_negative': False
        }
        gaussians.append(g)
        
    # Assign negative sign to a subset
    negative_indices = random.sample(range(total_gaussians), num_negative)
    for idx in negative_indices:
        gaussians[idx]['is_negative'] = True
        gaussians[idx]['color'] = -gaussians[idx]['color'] # Negative color [S1]

    # 2. Optimization Loop
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # Render current scene
        rendered_images = render_gaussians(gaussians, camera_poses)
        
        # Compute loss against ground truth
        loss = compute_loss(rendered_images, ground_truth_images)
        
        # Compute gradients
        gradients = compute_gradients(loss, gaussians)
        
        # Update parameters
        for i, g in enumerate(gaussians):
            g['position'] -= learning_rate * gradients['position'][i]
            g['opacity'] -= learning_rate * gradients['opacity'][i]
            g['color'] -= learning_rate * gradients['color'][i]
            # Update covariance parameters (scale/rotation)
            update_covariance(g, gradients['covariance'][i])

        # 3. Pruning and Densification
        # Prune low opacity Gaussians [S1]
        gaussians = prune_low_opacity(gaussians, threshold=0.005)
        
        # Densify in empty regions [S1]
        gaussians = densify_empty_regions(gaussians, camera_poses, threshold=0.01)
        
        # Note: The binary mask (is_negative) is preserved during updates [S1]
        
    return gaussians
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Initial Proportion:** The initial number of negative Gaussians is critical. Too few or too many can degrade optimization efficacy [S1].
*   **Precision:** The quadratic minimization for null density requires careful handling of the positive semi-definiteness of matrix $A$ [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If $h_i = 1$ and $m_{1,i} = m_{0,i}$, the solution for $w_i$ is any real number, indicating a direction of invariance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The method fully leverages the original Gaussian Splatting framework implementation, extending it with negative Gaussians tracked via a binary mask, thereby preserving the original GS rendering speed [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF360 dataset, NegGS-30K achieved an average PSNR of 27.39, SSIM of 0.812, and LPIPS of 0.219, compared to GS-30K's 27.21 PSNR, 0.815 SSIM, and 0.214 LPIPS [S1]. On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, NegGS showed slight improvements in PSNR and SSIM over GS [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method requires fewer total Gaussians than standard GS to achieve comparable or better results, indicating better efficiency in representation [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Better modeling of high-frequency elements and complex nonlinear structures; ability to represent rapid color changes [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of the initial proportion of negative Gaussians [S1].
*   **When to use:** When standard Gaussian Splatting fails to capture high-frequency details or complex color variations [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When computational resources are extremely limited and the scene is simple, as the overhead of managing negative components may not be justified [S1].

## Background
Negative Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the paper "NegGS: Negative Gaussian Splatting" [S1]. It is motivated by the observed superiority of Diff-Gaussians over standard Gaussians in covering complex nonlinear structures [S1]. The method was evaluated on NeRF Synthetic, Mip-NeRF360, Tanks and Temples, Deep Blending, and Shiny Blender datasets [S1]. All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU [S1].
