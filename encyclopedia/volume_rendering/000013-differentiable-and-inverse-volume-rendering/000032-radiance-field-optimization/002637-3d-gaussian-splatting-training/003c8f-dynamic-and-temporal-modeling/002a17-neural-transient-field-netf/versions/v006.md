# Neural Transient Field (NeTF)

## Overview
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1]. Unlike NeRF, which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts [S1]. It addresses the challenge of sparse and unbalanced sampling in NLOS reconstruction through Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and a two-stage training strategy [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Sparse measurements of light transients captured by a Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) array on a relay wall. The data is parametrized on spherical wavefronts rather than Cartesian rays [S1].
*   **Output:** A 3D density volume implicitly represented by the MLP, which corresponds to the hidden scene [S1]. The final mesh is extracted via thresholding and Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron that takes spatial coordinates and direction vectors as input and outputs density and potentially other attributes (e.g., albedo) [S1]. The specific network architecture (layer sizes, activation functions) is not detailed in the provided sources [G: Standard MLP structure assumed, e.g., ReLU activations, linear output for density].
*   **Spherical Wavefronts:** The coordinate system for input data, analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates but defined by spherical geometry [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the MLP parameters.
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points on the spherical wavefronts corresponding to the SPAD detection spots [S1].
3.  **MCMC Sampling:** Use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to account for sparse and unbalanced sampling in the transient field [S1]. This enables more reliable volume density estimation [S1].
4.  **Forward Pass:** Query the MLP with sampled coordinates to predict transients/density [S1].
5.  **Loss Calculation:** Calculate the loss function between predicted and measured transients at every measuring spot on the relay wall [S1].
6.  **Optimization:** Update MLP parameters to minimize the loss [S1].
7.  **Two-Stage Training (Optional but recommended for bias):**
    *   **Stage 1:** Conduct training using all initial samples to obtain an initial reconstruction [S1].
    *   **Loss Normalization:** Normalize the calculated loss from Stage 1 to form a probability density function (PDF) [S1].
    *   **Resampling:** Resample detection spots using the PDF, where higher loss corresponds to higher PDF and is more densely sampled [S1].
    *   **Stage 2:** Build a new training dataset from the resampled spots and retrain the network to refine reconstruction [S1].
8.  **Surface Extraction:** Map the implicit volume to surfaces by thresholding the density values followed by Marching Cubes [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Spherical Parametrization:** The input data is parametrized on spherical wavefronts [S1]. The specific mathematical formulation of the spherical coordinate transformation is not provided in the sources [G: Standard spherical coordinates $(r, \theta, \phi)$ likely used, but exact mapping to NLOS physics is not detailed].
*   **Loss Function:** The loss is calculated between predicted and measured transients [S1]. The specific loss metric (e.g., MSE, L1) is not explicitly named, though MAE is used for evaluation [S1].
*   **PDF for Resampling:** $P(i) = \frac{L_i}{\sum_j L_j}$, where $L_i$ is the loss at spot $i$ [G: Derived from "normalize the calculated loss to form a probability density function"].

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Resolution:** Tested on 256x256, 32x32, 16x16, 8x8, and 4x4 spots [S1].
*   **Threshold:** Not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion:** Occluded points may be captured from a very small number of viewpoints, leading to strong reconstruction artifacts if not handled properly [S1]. NeTF handles semi-occlusions better than Phasor Field, DLCT, and F-K [S1].
*   **Sampling Bias:** Undersampling leads to artifacts. Addressed by two-stage training [S1].
*   **Confocal vs. Non-Confocal:** NeTF does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups in its current form [S1].
*   **Noise:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].

## Subtopics

### Two-stage Training
A training strategy to address sampling bias (undersampling) in NLOS reconstruction by resampling detection spots based on reconstruction loss [S1]. It involves an initial training phase, loss calculation, normalization to a PDF, resampling, and retraining [S1]. It recovers details largely missing in one-stage training, such as bunny ears [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Accuracy:** On the Bunny dataset (confocal), NeTF achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.65 cm, compared to 2.38 cm for DLCT and 2.43 cm for F-K [S1]. On the Bunny dataset (non-confocal), NeTF achieved an MAE of 7.45 cm, compared to 3.77 cm for FBP and 7.02 cm for BP [S1].
*   **Quality:** NeTF produces sharper edges for both front and back planes in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field and DLCT/F-K [S1]. NeTF manages to separate different parts of a mesh, whereas DLCT exhibits adhesion between different parts [S1]. NeTF reconstruction incurs much lower noise than SOTA methods, similar to NeRF [S1].
*   **Robustness:** NeTF handles semi-occlusions robustly [S1]. It produces reasonable reconstructions even with very sparse sampling spots (e.g., 8x8) [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups [S1]. Brute-force surface extraction may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].

## Background
NeTF was presented as a novel neural modeling framework for NLOS imaging [S1]. It is analogous to NeRF but uses spherical wavefronts instead of rays [S1]. It uses MCMC to handle sparse sampling [S1]. Tested on synthetic and real NLOS data, including the Bunny, Lucy, Indonesian, Bike, Letter Z, and semi-occluded scenes [S1].
