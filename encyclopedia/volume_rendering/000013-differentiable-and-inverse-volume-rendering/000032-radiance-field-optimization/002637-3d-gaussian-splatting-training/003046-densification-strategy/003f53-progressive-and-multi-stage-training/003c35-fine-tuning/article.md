# Fine-tuning

Also called: 3DGS Fine-tuning, Gaussian Splatting Fine-tuning

## Overview
Fine-tuning is a training phase in 3D Gaussian Splatting that optimizes geometry and color attributes of an initial set of primitives to achieve photorealistic reconstruction [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard initialization-only approaches by enabling dynamic growing and pruning of primitives during optimization, which reduces redundancy and memory consumption while accelerating convergence via pretrained priors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of 3D Gaussian primitives initialized via a direct feed-forward process (geometry and color attributes generated by a pretrained model) [S1].
*   **Output:** Optimized 3D Gaussian primitives with refined geometry, color, and opacity [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Set:** A collection of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_i$, each defined by mean position $\mu_i$, covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (or scale $s_i$ and rotation $q_i$), opacity $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonic coefficients for color [S1, Parent].
*   **Optimizer State:** Adam optimizer state for all learnable parameters (geometry and color) [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Predict a set of 3D Gaussian primitives for initialization via a direct feed-forward process where both geometry and color attributes are generated [S1].
2.  **Optimization Loop:** Optimize all geometry and color attributes directly following vanilla 3DGS [S1].
    *   Render the scene using differentiable rasterization.
    *   Compute loss (e.g., SSIM, L1, Entropy) [S1].
    *   Backpropagate to update parameters using Adam [S1].
3.  **Densification (Growing/Pruning):** Enable growing and pruning of 3D primitives during fine-tuning [S1].
    *   **Pruning:** Remove redundant Gaussians. The number of Gaussians is significantly reduced during this phase as initial feed-forward Gaussians are often redundant [S1].
    *   **Growing:** Split or clone Gaussians based on densification metrics (see Parent) to recover detail.
4.  **Convergence:** Continue until the model achieves photorealism, leveraging pretrained priors for faster convergence compared to test-time optimization methods [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Libraries:** `torchsparse` for 3D sparse convolution, `gsplat` for Gaussian rasterization [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Redundancy:** Initial feed-forward Gaussians may be redundant, necessitating pruning to maintain high fidelity while reducing memory consumption [S1].
*   **Convergence Speed:** Pretrained priors accelerate training; without them, convergence may be slower [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Exponentially Ascending Gradient Threshold](9684-exponentially-ascending-gradient-threshold.md)
A densification control mechanism that uses an exponentially ascending gradient threshold to regulate splitting and cloning, solving the trade-off between early-stage convergence speed and late-stage overfitting.

### [Hard Gaussian Splatting](10590-hard-gaussian-splatting.md)
Identifies "hard Gaussians" causing artifacts and grows them to recover missing details using multi-view significant positional gradients and rendering error analysis.

### [SplatFormer Training Objective & Refinement](11745-splatformer-training-objective-refinement.md)
A learning-based, feed-forward module that refines initial 3DGS representations to enable robust novel view synthesis from out-of-distribution camera angles.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Accelerated convergence compared to standard test-time optimization due to pretrained priors [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced memory consumption compared to the initial feed-forward initialization by pruning redundant primitives [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Achieves photorealism on par with or surpassing other methods; leverages powerful pretrained weights [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the initial feed-forward initialization; redundant initial Gaussians require effective pruning [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When pretrained priors are available and memory efficiency is desired alongside high fidelity [S1].

## Background
Fine-tuning in the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, specifically as implemented in EVolSplat, was introduced to leverage pretrained weights for efficient volume-based Gaussian Splatting for urban view synthesis [S1]. It builds on the vanilla 3DGS optimization framework [S1].
