# Optimization and Densification Strategy

Also called: Modified 3DGS Optimization

## Overview
This method adapts the densification and pruning logic of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to work with ray-splat intersection-based rendering pipelines that lack screen-space gradients [S1]. It replaces screen-space gradient metrics with view-space mean gradients, uses opacity decay instead of reset to prevent over-densification, and employs visibility-score-based pruning [S1]. It is distinct from standard 3DGS by its reliance on geometric scaling for gradient estimation and its integration with hybrid transparency rendering [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Set of 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G}_i$ with means $\mu_i$, covariances $\Sigma_i$, opacities $\alpha_i$, and spherical harmonics coefficients. Camera parameters (view matrix, projection matrix).
*   **Output:** Updated set of Gaussians with modified parameters, including densified and pruned primitives.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Attributes:** Each Gaussian $i$ stores:
    *   $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
    *   $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix (or rotation/scale decomposition).
    *   $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
    *   $c_i$: Color/SH coefficients.
*   **Tile Grid:** Rendered in tiles of size $8 \times 8$ pixels to mitigate divergence in insertion sort for per-pixel core tracking [S1].
*   **Core Tracking:** Maintains the top $K=16$ fragments per pixel with $\alpha > \tau_K = 0.05$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Gradient Computation:**
    Compute the gradient of the splat means $\nabla_{\mu_i} L$ with respect to the loss $L$. Since screen-space gradients are unavailable in ray-splat intersection schemes, scale the gradient by half the distance to the camera in view space [S1]:
    $$
    \nabla_{\mu_i}^{\text{scaled}} = \nabla_{\mu_i} L \cdot \frac{1}{2} d_i
    $$
    where $d_i$ is the Euclidean distance from $\mu_i$ to the camera center in view space [S1].

2.  **Opacity Decay (Prevention of Over-Densification):**
    Every 50 iterations during densification, apply opacity decay to existing Gaussians to prevent over-densification [S1]:
    $$
    \alpha_i \leftarrow \alpha_i \cdot \lambda_o
    $$
    where $\lambda_o = 0.9995$ [S1]. This replaces the opacity reset strategy used in standard 3DGS [S1].

3.  **Densification Decision:**
    Evaluate each Gaussian based on the scaled gradient magnitude $\|\nabla_{\mu_i}^{\text{scaled}}\|$. If the magnitude exceeds a threshold (not specified in sources, typically derived from standard 3DGS heuristics [G]), mark the Gaussian for splitting [S1].
    *   **Splitting:** Replace the parent Gaussian with two children. The children's means are offset along the principal axes of the covariance, scaled by a factor (not specified in sources [G]). The covariance is scaled down (not specified in sources [G]). Opacity is initialized to the parent's opacity divided by 2 [G].

4.  **Pruning:**
    Apply visibility score-based pruning as proposed by Niemeyer et al. [NMR*25] [S1]. Gaussians with low visibility scores (indicating low contribution to the image) are removed [S1].

5.  **Rendering Update:**
    Use a fast CUDA implementation of the 3D filter by Yu et al. [YCH*24] for inversion-free splat evaluation to tackle aliasing [S1]. Use an optimized SSIM implementation [MGK*24] for loss computation [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_o$: Opacity decay multiplier. Value: $0.9995$ [S1].
*   Decay Interval: 50 iterations [S1].
*   Tile Size: $8 \times 8$ pixels [S1].
*   Core Size $K$: 16 [S1].
*   Core Threshold $\tau_K$: 0.05 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Divergence:** Using insertion sort for per-pixel core tracking can cause significant divergence if tile size is too large. Mitigated by reducing tile size to $8 \times 8$ [S1].
*   **Aliasing:** Standard EWA splatting screen-space low-pass filters are not applicable. Must use the 3D filter from Yu et al. [YCH*24] [S1].
*   **Gradient Availability:** Screen-space gradients are not available. Must use view-space scaled gradients [S1].

## Subtopics

### Anisotropy Regularization
A variant that removes needle surgery structural splitting and its associated training calls and parameters, keeping only standard densification and pruning [S2, unofficial]. It introduces log-ratio soft regularization parameters: `lambda_aniso` (default 0.001) and `aniso_ratio_max` (default 5) [S2, unofficial]. This approach is used in volume cloud rendering contexts [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training speedup of $2.1\times$ compared to standard 3DGS [S1]. Rendering is $1.6\times$ faster on average [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Achieves image quality comparable to standard 3DGS but outperformed by Zip-NeRF [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Faster training and rendering, handles aliasing in ray-splat intersection schemes, prevents over-densification via opacity decay [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires custom CUDA implementation for 3D filtering [S1]. Relies on visibility score-based pruning which may have different convergence properties than standard pruning [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When using ray-splat intersection-based rendering pipelines that lack screen-space gradients and require aliasing mitigation [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When standard EWA splatting with screen-space gradients is available, or when maximum image quality is prioritized over training speed (Zip-NeRF outperforms this method) [S1].

## Background
This method was introduced by rbl et al. [KKLD23] (note: citation in source [S1] refers to rbl et al. [KKLD23] as a reference, but the paper itself is "Efficient Perspective‐Correct 3D Gaussian Splatting Using Hybrid Transparency" [S1]). It addresses technical challenges arising from changes to the rendering pipeline, specifically the move to ray-splat intersection-based approaches [S1]. The method adapts densification changes from GOF [YSG*24] and applies visibility score-based pruning from Niemeyer et al. [NMR*25] [S1].
