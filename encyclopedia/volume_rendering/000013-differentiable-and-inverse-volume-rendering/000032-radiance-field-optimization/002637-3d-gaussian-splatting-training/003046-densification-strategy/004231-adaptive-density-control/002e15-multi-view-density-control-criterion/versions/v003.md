# Multi-View Density Control Criterion

Also called: Absolute-gradient split proxy, FastGS criterion

## Overview
The Multi-View Density Control Criterion is a heuristic applied during the teacher training phase of 3D Gaussian Splatting to control the number and distribution of Gaussian primitives [S1, unofficial]. It differs from the standard positional-gradient rule by yielding higher rendering quality with significantly fewer primitives [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The criterion operates during the teacher training phase to manage primitive count [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### DS Threshold (DSTH)
A hyperparameter threshold that controls the densification constraints of Gaussians; increasing it imposes stricter constraints, reducing NGS, rendering quality, and training time [S2, unofficial]. Default value is 30 [S2, unofficial]. Set to 60 for the playroom scene in the DeepBlending dataset [S2, unofficial]. Other scenes use 30 [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
The Multi-View Density Control Criterion yields 0.67 dB and 0.028 LPIPS improvement on average, while using 28% fewer primitives at the same time [S1, unofficial]. In the garden scene, the standard positional-gradient rule produces 240,075 primitives with a PSNR of 26.83 and LPIPS of 0.1303, whereas the multi-view criterion produces 148,658 primitives with a PSNR of 27.06 and LPIPS of 0.1247 [S1, unofficial]. It is applied during the teacher training phase [S1, unofficial].
