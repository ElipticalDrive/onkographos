# GNeRF

Also called: GAN-based Neural Radiance Field without Posed Camera

## Overview
GNeRF is a method for novel view synthesis and camera pose estimation that operates without known camera poses or intrinsic parameters [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of reconstructing a neural radiance field and estimating camera extrinsics simultaneously in complex scenes, including those with low texture or repeated patterns where traditional Structure-from-Motion (SfM) fails [S2]. Unlike sibling methods like [GANeRF](7571-ganerf.md) which may rely on posed initialization or different adversarial strategies, GNeRF uniquely employs a two-phase hybrid optimization scheme: an initial adversarial phase to obtain coarse poses and a radiance field, followed by a regularized photometric refinement phase, interleaved iteratively to escape local minima [S2].

## How it works
GNeRF jointly optimizes a neural radiance field $F_\Theta$ and a set of camera poses $\Phi = \{\phi_1, \dots, \phi_n\}$ for $n$ input images $I = \{I_1, \dots, I_n\}$ [S2]. The method treats camera poses as latent variables and uses a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework combined with an inversion network to bootstrap the optimization [S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $n$ uncalibrated RGB images $I$. No camera poses or intrinsics are provided.
*   **Outputs:** A trained neural radiance field $F_\Theta$ and estimated camera poses $\Phi$.

### Data Structures and Parameters

**Camera Pose Representation:**
To avoid discontinuities in quaternions or Euler angles, poses are represented using continuous embeddings [S2]:
*   **Translation ($t$):** A 3D vector $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ representing the camera position in Euclidean space.
*   **Rotation ($r$):** A 6D vector $r \in \mathbb{R}^6$. Given a rotation matrix $R = [a_1, a_2, a_3] \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, $r$ is formed by dropping the last column ($r = [a_1, a_2]$). The original matrix can be recovered via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization [S2].
*   **Intrinsics:** The method assumes a SIMPLE PINHOLE model with shared intrinsics across all views: focal length $f$, and principal point $(c_x, c_y)$ [S2].

**Neural Radiance Field ($F_\Theta$):**
*   **Input:** 3D location $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $d \in [-\pi, \pi]^2$.
*   **Output:** Emitted color $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma$.
*   **Architecture:** Based on the original NeRF [32] with hierarchical sampling. Both coarse and fine sampling use 64 points. The MLP dimension is increased from 256 to 360 to maintain parameter count parity with the original NeRF while aligning coarse and fine pose spaces [S2].

**Discriminator ($D_\eta$):**
*   **Architecture:** Follows GRAF [S2]. Uses instance normalization over features and spectral normalization over weights [S2].
*   **Input:** Image patches.

**Inversion Network ($E_{\theta_E}$):**
*   **Architecture:** Vision Transformer [S2]. The final layer is modified to output a camera pose (translation and rotation embeddings) [S2].
*   **Input:** Image patches.
*   **Output:** Estimated camera pose.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Set all camera extrinsics to the identity matrix [S2].
    *   Initialize the NeRF model $F_\Theta$ and discriminator $D_\eta$ [S2].
    *   Define a prior pose distribution $P(\phi)$ for random sampling. For Synthetic-NeRF, cameras are uniformly distributed on the upper hemisphere towards the origin. For DTU, cameras are on the upper hemisphere with azimuth $[0, 150]^\circ$ and lookat points from $N(0, 0.012)$ [S2].

2.  **Phase A: Pose-free NeRF Estimation (Adversarial Training):**
    *   **Generator:** Sample a random camera pose $\phi \sim P(\phi)$. Query $F_\Theta$ with $\phi$ to render an image $\hat{I} = G(\phi; F_\Theta)$ using NeRF volume rendering [S2].
    *   **Dynamic Patch Sampling:** Decompose $\hat{I}$ and real images $I$ into patches of fixed size $16 \times 16$ with dynamic scale and random offset [S2].
    *   **Discriminator Update:** Train $D_\eta$ to distinguish real patches $P_d(I)$ from generated patches $P_g(I|\Theta)$ using the adversarial loss $L_A$ [S2]:
        $$ \min_\Theta \max_\eta L_A(\Theta, \eta) = \mathbb{E}_{I \sim P_d}[\log(D(I; \eta))] + \mathbb{E}_{\hat{I} \sim P_g}[\log(1 - D(\hat{I}; \eta))] $$
    *   **Inversion Network Training:** Train $E_{\theta_E}$ to map generated image patches back to their corresponding sampled poses $\phi$ using self-supervised loss $L_E$ [S2]:
        $$ L_E(\theta_E) = \mathbb{E}_{\phi \sim P(\phi)} \| E(G(\phi; F_\Theta); \theta_E) - \phi \|_2^2 $$
    *   Repeat until convergence to obtain a coarse radiance field and initial pose estimates $\Phi^0 = \{ E(I_i; \theta_E) \}_{i=1}^n$ for the real training images [S2].

3.  **Phase B: Regularized Learning Strategy (Photometric Refinement):**
    *   **Static Patch Sampling:** For pose optimization, uniformly sample patches of fixed size $64 \times 64$ across the whole image domain to estimate poses [S2].
    *   **Intrinsic Scaling:** Scale camera intrinsics at the beginning to maximize receptive field, then progressively increase to the original value to focus on fine details [S2].
    *   **Hybrid Loss Optimization:** Refine $F_\Theta$ and $\Phi$ by minimizing the hybrid loss $L_R$ [S2]:
        $$ L_R(\Theta, \Phi) = L_N(\Theta, \Phi) + \lambda \sum_{i=1}^n \| E(I_i; \theta_E) - \phi_i \|_2^2 $$
        where $L_N$ is the photometric reconstruction error:
        $$ L_N(\Theta, \Phi) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \| I_i - \hat{I}_i \|_2^2, \quad \hat{I}_i = G(\phi_i; F_\Theta) $$
        and $\lambda$ is a weighting coefficient [S2].

4.  **Iterative Hybrid Optimization:**
    *   Interleave Phase A and Phase B in the pattern 'A, AB...AB, B' [S2]. This iterative scheme overcomes local minima that arise from directly propagating photometric loss to pose parameters, which is sensitive to low-texture conditions [S2].

### Pseudocode

```python
def GNeRF(images, num_iterations, lambda_weight):
    """
    GNeRF implementation.
    images: List of n RGB images.
    num_iterations: Total optimization steps.
    lambda_weight: Weight for the inversion network penalty in hybrid loss.
    """
    # 1. Initialization
    # Initialize NeRF model F_Theta with standard NeRF architecture (MLP dim 360)
    F_Theta = initialize_NeRF()
    
    # Initialize Discriminator D_Nu following GRAF architecture
    D_Nu = initialize_Discriminator()
    
    # Initialize Inversion Network E_Theta_E using Vision Transformer
    E_Theta_E = initialize_InversionNetwork()
    
    # Initialize camera poses Phi as identity matrices
    Phi = [IdentityPose() for _ in images]
    
    # Define prior pose distribution P(phi) based on dataset (e.g., upper hemisphere)
    PriorPoseDist = get_prior_distribution()
    
    # Set optimizer parameters
    opt_G = RMSprop(lr=0.0005) # Generator (NeRF)
    opt_D = RMSprop(lr=0.0001) # Discriminator
    opt_E = Adam(lr=0.0001)    # Inversion Network
    opt_P = Adam(lr=0.005)     # Camera Poses
    
    # 2. Iterative Optimization Loop
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        
        # --- Phase A: Adversarial Training ---
        # Sample random poses
        random_poses = [PriorPoseDist.sample() for _ in range(batch_size)]
        
        # Render images from random poses
        rendered_images = []
        for pose in random_poses:
            # NeRF volume rendering
            img = render_NeRF(F_Theta, pose)
            rendered_images.append(img)
            
        # Dynamic Patch Sampling (16x16, random offset/scale)
        real_patches = dynamic_patch_sample(images, size=16)
        fake_patches = dynamic_patch_sample(rendered_images, size=16)
        
        # Update Discriminator
        loss_D = adversarial_loss(D_Nu, real_patches, fake_patches)
        D_Nu.update(loss_D, opt_D)
        
        # Update Generator (NeRF) via adversarial loss
        loss_G_adv = adversarial_loss(D_Nu, real_patches, fake_patches, generator=True)
        F_Theta.update(loss_G_adv, opt_G)
        
        # Update Inversion Network (Self-supervised)
        # Map rendered patches back to random poses
        predicted_poses = [E_Theta_E(patch) for patch in fake_patches]
        loss_E = sum(l2_norm(pred - true) for pred, true in zip(predicted_poses, random_poses))
        E_Theta_E.update(loss_E, opt_E)
        
        # --- Phase B: Photometric Refinement ---
        # Static Patch Sampling (64x64, uniform) for pose estimation
        # Note: In practice, this step uses the current Phi to render and compare
        # with real images to compute photometric loss, and uses E to compute pose penalty
        
        # Render current estimates
        rendered_estimates = [render_NeRF(F_Theta, phi) for phi in Phi]
        
        # Photometric Loss
        loss_N = sum(l2_norm(img - est) for img, est in zip(images, rendered_estimates))
        
        # Pose Penalty from Inversion Network
        # Estimate poses from real images using trained E
        estimated_poses_from_E = [E_Theta_E(img) for img in images]
        loss_PosePenalty = sum(l2_norm(est_phi - phi) for est_phi, phi in zip(estimated_poses_from_E, Phi))
        
        # Hybrid Loss
        loss_R = loss_N + lambda_weight * loss_PosePenalty
        
        # Update NeRF and Poses
        F_Theta.update(loss_R, opt_G)
        for i, phi in enumerate(Phi):
            # Update individual pose embeddings
            # Note: Gradients flow through render_NeRF back to Phi[i]
            pass 
        Phi.update(loss_R, opt_P)
        
        # Interleaving logic: 'A, AB...AB, B' pattern handled by loop structure
        # Typically A is run for some steps, then AB interleaved, then final B
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Local Minima:** Directly propagating photometric loss to camera poses is prone to local minima, especially in low-texture scenes. The hybrid iterative scheme is required to mitigate this [S2].
*   **Inversion Network Limitations:** The inversion network accepts limited-size patches, restricting pose accuracy. It may underperform COLMAP on scenes with sufficient reliable keypoints [S2].
*   **Rotation Representation:** Quaternions and Euler angles are avoided due to discontinuities; the 6D embedding is used instead [S2].
*   **Intrinsic Scaling:** Failure to progressively scale intrinsics may hinder convergence on fine details [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is computationally intensive. On a single Nvidia RTX TITAN GPU, training takes approximately 30 hours per scene [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses dynamic patch sampling ($16 \times 16$) for GAN training to reduce memory usage compared to full-image discrimination [S2].
*   **Performance:** On the DTU dataset, GNeRF outperforms COLMAP-based NeRF. On Synthetic-NeRF, it is slightly outperformed by COLMAP-based NeRF but handles challenging scenes (repeated patterns, low texture) better [S1, S2].
*   **Strengths:** Does not require camera pose initialization or masks. Robust to low-texture and repeated pattern scenes where SfM fails [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Pose accuracy is lower than COLMAP on standard scenes with good keypoints. Training time is significantly longer than standard NeRF [S2].

## Background
GNeRF was introduced in 2021 by Meng et al. as a GAN-based framework for unposed neural radiance field reconstruction and camera pose estimation [S1, S2]. It extends the NeRF framework by integrating adversarial training and an inversion network to handle scenarios where camera poses are completely unknown [S2]. It is evaluated on Synthetic-NeRF and DTU datasets [S2].
