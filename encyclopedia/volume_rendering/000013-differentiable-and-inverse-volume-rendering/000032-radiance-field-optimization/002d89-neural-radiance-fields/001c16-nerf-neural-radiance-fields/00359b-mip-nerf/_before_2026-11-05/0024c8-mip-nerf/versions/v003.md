# Mip-NeRF

Also called: Mip-NeRF: A Multiscale Representation for Anti-Aliasing Neural Radiance Fields, multum in parvo NeRF

## Overview
Mip-NeRF extends Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to represent the scene at a continuously-valued scale, rendering anti-aliased conical frustums instead of infinitesimal rays to reduce aliasing artifacts and improve the representation of fine details [S1]. Unlike traditional mipmapping which uses discrete downsampling scales, Mip-NeRF learns a single neural scene model that can be queried at arbitrary scales, addressing the limitation where coordinate-based neural representations could previously only be anti-aliased using expensive supersampling [S1]. It ameliorates aliasing by casting a cone from each pixel, dividing the cone into conical frustums, and computing integrated positional encoding (IPE) features for the volume of each frustum [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** Training images with known camera poses and intrinsics; a neural network representing the scene [S1].
*   **Output:** A continuous volumetric scene representation (MLP) that accepts a 3D Gaussian characterizing a region of space and returns color and density [S1].

**Data Structures**
The core data structure is a multivariate Gaussian approximation of a conical frustum [S1]. It is characterized by:
*   Origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ of the cone axis [S1].
*   Mean distance along the ray $\mu_t$ [S1].
*   Variance along the ray $\sigma_t^2$ [S1].
*   Variance perpendicular to the ray $\sigma_r^2$ [S1].
*   Cone radius at the image plane $\dot{r}$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **Cone Casting:** For each pixel, cast a cone from the camera center of projection $\mathbf{o}$ along direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1]. The radius of the cone at the image plane is parameterized as $\dot{r}$ [S1]. Set $\dot{r}$ to the width of the pixel in world coordinates scaled by $2/\sqrt{12}$, which yields a cone whose section on the image plane has a variance in $x$ and $y$ that matches the variance of the pixel's footprint [S1].
2.  **Frustum Division:** Divide the cone into a series of conical frustums (cones cut perpendicular to their axis) at sampled distances $[t_0, t_1]$ along the ray [S1].
3.  **Gaussian Approximation:** Approximate each conical frustum with a multivariate Gaussian [S1]. Because conical frustums are symmetric around the axis of the cone and assumed circular, the Gaussian is fully characterized by $\mu_t$, $\sigma_t^2$, and $\sigma_r^2$ [S1].
4.  **Compute Gaussian Parameters:** Calculate the mean and variance parameters for the Gaussian approximation of the frustum defined by interval $[t_0, t_1]$ and cone radius $r$ at distance $t$ [S1].
5.  **Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE):** Compute the IPE feature for the Gaussian [S1]. This is the expected positional encoding over the frustum volume, approximated using the Gaussian parameters [S1].
6.  **NeRF Query:** Query the single MLP with the IPE features and the view direction to obtain color and density [S1].
7.  **Volume Rendering:** Perform standard volume rendering integration over the frustums to produce the final pixel color [S1].

**Formulas**

The indicator function $F(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1)$ defines the conical frustum:
$$
F(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1) = \mathbb{1}\left\{ \left( t_0 < \frac{\mathbf{d}^T(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{o})}{\|\mathbf{d}\|_2^2} < t_1 \right) \wedge \left( \frac{\mathbf{d}^T(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{o})}{\|\mathbf{d}\|_2 \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{o}\|_2} > \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + (\dot{r}/\|\mathbf{d}\|_2)^2}} \right) \right\}
$$
where $\mathbb{1}\{\cdot\}$ is an indicator function: $F(\mathbf{x}, \cdot) = 1$ iff $\mathbf{x}$ is within the conical frustum defined by $(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1)$ [S1].

The Integrated Positional Encoding (IPE) $\gamma^*(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1)$ is the expected positional encoding over the frustum:
$$
\gamma^*(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1) = \frac{\int \gamma(\mathbf{x}) F(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1) d\mathbf{x}}{\int F(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}, \dot{r}, t_0, t_1) d\mathbf{x}}
$$
where $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ is the standard NeRF positional encoding [S1].

The Gaussian parameters for a frustum with interval mean $t_\mu$ and interval half-width $t_s$ (where $t_0 = t_\mu - t_s, t_1 = t_\mu + t_s$) and cone radius $r$ at distance $t_\mu$ are:
$$
\mu_t = t_\mu + \frac{2t_\mu t_s^2}{3t_\mu^2 + t_s^2}
$$
$$
\sigma_t^2 = \frac{t_s^2}{3} - \frac{4t_s^4(12t_\mu^2 - t_s^2)}{15(3t_\mu^2 + t_s^2)^2}
$$
$$
\sigma_r^2 = r^2 \left( \frac{t_\mu^2}{4} + \frac{5t_s^2}{12} - \frac{4t_s^4}{15(3t_\mu^2 + t_s^2)} \right) \quad \text{[G]}
$$

**Pseudocode**

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_gaussian_params(t_mu, t_s, r):
    mu_t = t_mu + (2 * t_mu * t_s**2) / (3 * t_mu**2 + t_s**2)
    sigma_t_sq = (t_s**2 / 3) - (4 * t_s**4 * (12 * t_mu**2 - t_s**2)) / (15 * (3 * t_mu**2 + t_s**2)**2)
    sigma_r_sq = r**2 * (t_mu**2 / 4 + 5 * t_s**2 / 12 - 4 * t_s**4 / (15 * (3 * t_mu**2 + t_s**2)))
    return mu_t, sigma_t_sq, sigma_r_sq

def compute_ipe(mu_t, sigma_t_sq, sigma_r_sq, num_freqs=10):
    # Computes expected positional encoding under the Gaussian approximation [G].
    features = []
    for k in range(num_freqs):
        scale = 2**k * np.pi
        features.append(np.sin(scale * mu_t) * np.exp(-0.5 * scale**2 * sigma_t_sq))
        features.append(np.cos(scale * mu_t) * np.exp(-0.5 * scale**2 * sigma_t_sq))
        features.append(np.sin(scale * mu_t) * np.exp(-0.5 * scale**2 * sigma_r_sq))
        features.append(np.cos(scale * mu_t) * np.exp(-0.5 * scale**2 * sigma_r_sq))
    return np.array(features)

def render_pixel(o, d, near, far, num_samples, mlp, r_dot):
    d_norm = np.linalg.norm(d)
    d_unit = d / d_norm
    t_vals = np.linspace(near, far, num_samples + 1)
    
    accumulated_color = np.zeros(3)
    accumulated_density = 0.0
    
    for i in range(num_samples):
        t0 = t_vals[i]
        t1 = t_vals[i+1]
        t_mu = (t0 + t1) / 2.0
        t_s = (t1 - t0) / 2.0
        
        r = r_dot * t_mu / d_norm
        mu_t, sigma_t_sq, sigma_r_sq = compute_gaussian_params(t_mu, t_s, r)
        ipe = compute_ipe(mu_t, sigma_t_sq, sigma_r_sq)
        
        color, density = mlp(ipe, d_unit)
        
        delta_t = t1 - t0
        alpha = 1.0 - np.exp(-density * delta_t)
        weight = alpha * np.exp(-accumulated_density)
        
        accumulated_color += weight * color
        accumulated_density += density * delta_t
        
    return accumulated_color
```

**Parameters**
*   $\dot{r}$: The radius of the cone at the image plane. Set to the width of the pixel in world coordinates scaled by $2/\sqrt{12}$ [S1].
*   Number of samples: Not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Truncated Formula:** The formula for $\sigma_r^2$ is truncated in the source material [S1]. The complete expression is provided in the text and pseudocode [G].
*   **IPE Approximation:** The source states the integral for IPE has no closed form and is approximated by the Gaussian, but the specific approximation function mapping $(\mu_t, \sigma_t^2, \sigma_r^2)$ to IPE features is not fully detailed in the provided text [S1].
*   **Cone Radius:** The calculation of $\dot{r}$ depends on the pixel width in world coordinates, which requires camera intrinsics [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Mip-NeRF is slightly faster than NeRF ($\sim 7\%$) [S1]. It matches the accuracy of brute-force supersampled NeRF on the multiscale dataset while being $22\times$ faster [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Mip-NeRF has half as many parameters as NeRF [S1].
*   **Performance:** Reduces average error rates by $17\%$ on the original NeRF dataset and by $60\%$ on a challenging multiscale variant dataset [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces aliasing artifacts, improves representation of fine details, allows merging of coarse and fine MLPs into a single MLP [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires approximation of conical frustums with Gaussians; exact IPE formula is complex and partially truncated in sources [S1].

## Background
Mip-NeRF was introduced by Barron et al. in 2021 [S1]. It addresses the limitation of NeRF where coordinate-based neural representations could only be anti-aliased using expensive supersampling [S1]. The method extends NeRF to represent the scene at a continuously-valued scale, learning a single neural scene model that can be queried at arbitrary scales [S1].
