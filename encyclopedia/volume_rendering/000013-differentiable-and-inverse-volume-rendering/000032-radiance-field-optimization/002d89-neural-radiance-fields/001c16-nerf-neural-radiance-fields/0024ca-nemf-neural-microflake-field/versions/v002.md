# NeMF (Neural Microflake Field)
Also called: Neural Microflake Field

## Overview
NeMF is a differentiable inverse volume rendering method that represents a scene as a distribution of microflakes at every point in space, enabling the recovery of physical attributes (shape, reflectance, illumination) from multi-view images under unknown natural illumination [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF which models view-dependent radiance, NeMF models light transportation via microflake normals and roughness, allowing it to handle complex geometry and translucent objects better than surface-based methods [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images of a scene, camera poses, and a bounding box defining the scene volume [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A continuous volumetric representation defined by the implicit microflake field $\Phi$, which outputs volume density $\sigma$, albedo $a$, microflake normal $\boldsymbol{\omega}_m$, and roughness $\tau_m$ for any 3D position $\mathbf{p}$ [S1]. The method also outputs optimized Spherical Gaussian (SG) parameters for environment illumination and visibility fields [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The core data structure is the implicit microflake field, implemented via coordinate-based neural networks [S1].
1.  **Geometry Network ($\mathcal{F}_g$):** An MLP that takes position $\mathbf{p}$ and outputs volume density $\sigma$ and microflake normal $\boldsymbol{\omega}_m$, building on the vanilla NeRF structure [S1].
2.  **Appearance Network ($\mathcal{D}_m$):** A U-shaped MLP that encodes the material to a feature vector, applies a sparsity constraint, and decodes it into albedo $a$ and roughness $\tau_m$ [S1].
3.  **Visibility Network ($\mathcal{V}$):** An MLP that overfits visibility SG parameters for each spatial position [S1].
4.  **Illumination Parameters:** A set of 128-dimensional SGs representing infinitely faraway background lights [S1].

**Mathematical Definitions:**
The microflake model assumes space is filled with infinite small flakes [S1]. The probability of normals in direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ on the ellipsoid surface is modeled by the Normal Distribution Function (NDF) [S1]:
$$D(\boldsymbol{\omega}) = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{|S|}(\boldsymbol{\omega}^T S^{-1}\boldsymbol{\omega})^2}$$
where $|S|$ is the determinant of the shape matrix $S$ [S1].

The diffuse phase function is defined as [S1]:
$$f_p^d(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_l) = \frac{1}{\pi \tau(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)} \int_{\Omega} \langle \boldsymbol{\omega}_l, \boldsymbol{\omega} \rangle \langle \boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega} \rangle D(\boldsymbol{\omega}) d\boldsymbol{\omega}$$
where $\langle \cdot \rangle$ is the dot product, $\Omega$ is the hemisphere centered at $\boldsymbol{\omega}_m$, and $\tau(\boldsymbol{\omega})$ is the projected size of the ellipsoid along direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ [S1].

The specular phase function is defined as [S1]:
$$f_p^s(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i, \boldsymbol{\omega}_l) = \frac{D(\boldsymbol{\omega}_h)}{4\tau(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)}$$
where $\boldsymbol{\omega}_h$ is the half-angle between the input light direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_l$ and viewing direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize $\mathcal{F}_g$, $\mathcal{D}_m$, $\mathcal{V}$, and 128-dimensional SG illumination parameters [S1].
2.  **Sampling:** For each ray $\mathbf{r}$, sample discrete positions $\mathbf{p}_k$ along the ray [S1].
3.  **Feature Extraction:** Query $\mathcal{F}_g(\mathbf{p}_k)$ for $\sigma_k$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m,k}$ [S1]. Encode the material at $\mathbf{p}_k$ into a feature vector, apply a sparsity constraint, and query $\mathcal{D}_m$ to decode albedo $a_k$ and roughness $\tau_{m,k}$ [S1]. Query $\mathcal{V}(\mathbf{p}_k)$ for visibility SG parameters [S1].
4.  **Visibility Calculation:** To compute visibility of $\mathbf{p}_k$ w.r.t. light direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_l$, march a ray from $\mathbf{p}_k$ towards the light in the background and calculate opacity $\eta$ using a pre-trained NeRF [S1]. Encode this visibility using SG parameterization [S1].
5.  **Radiance Computation:** Compute diffuse radiance $\nu_d(\mathbf{p}_k)$ by summing the product of environment lighting and visibility: $\nu_d(x) = \sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_l \in \Omega} \{ [V(x, \boldsymbol{\omega}_l) Y(\boldsymbol{\omega}_l)] \otimes [L^{\text{sg}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_l) Y(\boldsymbol{\omega}_l)] \cdot \langle \boldsymbol{\omega}_l \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}_m \rangle \}$ [S1]. Compute specular radiance $\nu_s(\mathbf{p}_k)$ by checking visibility during ray sampling and setting radiance to 0 if the light is invisible [S1]. Total radiance is $\nu^*(\mathbf{p}_k, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i) = \nu_d(\mathbf{p}_k) + \nu_s(\mathbf{p}_k)$ [S1].
6.  **Volume Rendering:** Replace integrals with summations over discrete sampling positions [S1]. Perform importance ray sampling of light directions for calculating radiance, as elaborated in Algorithm 1 of Heitz et al. [22] [S1]. Render image color $C(\mathbf{r})$ via standard volume rendering integration [S1].
7.  **Loss Calculation:** Photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_c = \sum_{\mathbf{r}, I} \|C(\mathbf{r}) - I(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2$ [S1]. Density field loss $\mathcal{L}_\sigma = \sum_k \eta_k (\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \Delta \sigma\|_2^2 + \max(0, \langle \boldsymbol{\omega}_m, \boldsymbol{\omega}_i \rangle))$ [S1]. Latent sparsity loss $\mathcal{L}_z = \sum_j KL(\rho \| \hat{\rho}_j)$ with $\rho=0.05$ [S1]. Smoothness loss $\mathcal{L}_s = \|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}_g(\mathbf{p})) - \mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}_g(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon))\|_1 + \|\mathcal{D}_m(\mathbf{z}) - \mathcal{D}_m(\mathbf{z} + \epsilon)\|_1$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01)$ [S1]. Total loss $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_\sigma + \mathcal{L}_z + \mathcal{L}_s$ [S1].
8.  **Optimization:** Minimize $\mathcal{L}$ via gradient descent [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def train_nerf(scene_data, num_iterations):
    # Initialize networks
    Fg = MLP() # Outputs sigma, omega_m
    Dm = UShapedMLP() # Outputs a, tau_m
    V = MLP() # Outputs visibility SG params
    SG_Lights = [SG(dim=128) for _ in range(128)] # Environment illumination

    # Pre-train NeRF for initial density and visibility
    NeRF = TrainNeRF(scene_data)

    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        total_loss = 0
        for ray in scene_data.rays:
            # 1. Sample points along ray
            points = SamplePoints(ray, num_samples=64)

            # 2. Extract features
            sigma_list = []
            omega_m_list = []
            albedo_list = []
            tau_m_list = []
            visibility_params_list = []

            for p in points:
                sigma, omega_m = Fg(p)
                sigma_list.append(sigma)
                omega_m_list.append(omega_m)

                z = EncodePosition(p)
                a, tau_m = Dm(z)
                albedo_list.append(a)
                tau_m_list.append(tau_m)

                vis_params = V(p)
                visibility_params_list.append(vis_params)

            # 3. Compute Visibility
            visibilities = []
            for p, omega_m in zip(points, omega_m_list):
                opacity = 0.0
                for light_dir in SG_Lights:
                    opacity += NeRF.IntegrateRay(p, light_dir)
                visibilities.append(opacity)

            # 4. Compute Radiance
            radiance_diffuse = 0.0
            for p, omega_m, vis_params in zip(points, omega_m_list, visibility_params_list):
                for light_dir in SG_Lights:
                    vis_term = vis_params * light_dir.Y()
                    light_term = light_dir.L() * light_dir.Y()
                    radiance_diffuse += (vis_term * light_term) * Dot(light_dir, omega_m)

            radiance_specular = 0.0
            for p, omega_m, vis in zip(points, omega_m_list, visibilities):
                if vis > 0:
                    radiance_specular += ComputeSpecularPhase(omega_m, ray.direction)
                else:
                    radiance_specular += 0.0

            total_radiance = radiance_diffuse + radiance_specular

            # 5. Volume Rendering
            rendered_color = 0.0
            T = 1.0
            for i, (p, sigma, radiance) in enumerate(zip(points, sigma_list, total_radiance)):
                alpha = 1.0 - Exp(-sigma * ray.step_size)
                rendered_color += T * alpha * radiance
                T *= (1.0 - alpha)

            # 6. Loss Calculation
            gt_color = scene_data.GetGroundTruthColor(ray)
            loss_c = L2Norm(rendered_color - gt_color)

            delta_sigma = ComputeGradient(sigma_list)
            loss_sigma = Sum([vis * (L2Norm(omega_m - delta_sigma) + Max(0, Dot(omega_m, ray.direction))) for vis, omega_m in zip(visibilities, omega_m_list)])

            loss_z = KL_Divergence(0.05, AverageChannels(z))

            epsilon = RandomGaussian(variance=0.01)
            loss_s = L1Norm(Normalize(Fg(p)) - Normalize(Fg(p + epsilon))) + L1Norm(Dm(z) - Dm(z + epsilon))

            total_loss += loss_c + loss_sigma + loss_z + loss_s

        GradientDescent(total_loss)

# Helper definitions

def UShapedMLP(): return lambda z: (z.mean(), z.std()) # Placeholder for actual network
def SG(dim): return type('SG', (), {'Y': lambda self: 1.0, 'L': lambda self: 1.0})()
def TrainNeRF(data): return type('NeRF', (), {'IntegrateRay': lambda self, p, d: 0.5})()
def SamplePoints(ray, num_samples): return [ray.origin + ray.direction * i for i in range(num_samples)]
def EncodePosition(p): return p
def ComputeSpecularPhase(omega_m, direction): return 1.0
def L2Norm(x): return sum(x**2)**0.5
def ComputeGradient(lst): return [lst[i+1]-lst[i] for i in range(len(lst)-1)]
def KL_Divergence(rho, z): return 0.0
def AverageChannels(z): return z.mean()
def RandomGaussian(variance): return (variance)**0.5
def Normalize(x): return x / (x.norm() + 1e-8)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Normal Estimation:** Removing normal estimation ("w/o normal") bakes ambient lighting into surfaces, yielding significantly worse results (PSNR 15.4516 vs 23.6982) [S1].
*   **Visibility Modeling:** Assuming environment illumination is visible to all points ("w/o vis.") leads to incorrect predictions around occlusion boundaries [S1].
*   **Latent Space:** Mapping points directly to parameters without a latent space ("w/o latent space") results in incorrect material extraction and vaguer edges [S1].
*   **Precision:** The integration in the diffuse phase function requires careful numerical handling. The sources note that integrals are replaced with summations over discrete sampling positions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
Not given in the sources.

**Measured Performance:**
On synthetic Blender scenes (balloon, mic, spot, polyhedron), NeMF achieved PSNR 23.6982, SSIM 0.9387 (Albedo), and PSNR 25.7048 (Relighting), outperforming InvRender, NeRFactor, and PhySG [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Fully relies on volume representation without needing to recover geometry explicitly, allowing it to handle scenes with complex geometry and translucent objects (e.g., clouds) which surface-based methods cannot handle well [S1]. Enables high-quality relighting, material editing, and volume scattering simulation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Surface-based methods (e.g., NeRD, NeRFactor) may be more efficient for simple geometry. NeMF's reliance on inverse volume rendering and visibility modeling adds complexity [S1].

**When to Use:**
Use NeMF when the scene contains complex geometry, translucent objects, or when high-quality relighting and material editing are required under unknown natural illumination [S1].

## Background
NeMF was proposed in the paper "NeMF: Inverse Volume Rendering with Neural Microflake Field" [S1]. It extends the NeRF framework by incorporating a microflake model for light transportation, addressing the limitations of surface-based inverse rendering methods [S1]. The method builds on prior work in inverse rendering, such as InvRender and NeRD, but distinguishes itself by fully utilizing a volume representation [S1].
