# NeRF System Data Preparation

Also called: Blender Dataset Loading, NeRF training data sampling, NeRF validation data sampling, Synthetic NeRF Dataset

## Overview
This method prepares training and validation data for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by loading synthetic scenes (typically Blender datasets) or real-world forward-facing scenes (LLFF), parsing camera metadata, and precomputing ray origins and directions. It differs from general volume rendering by handling specific coordinate conventions (e.g., OpenCV to NeRF's camera space) and background compositing for synthetic data. It builds on [Training Ray Batching](2252-training-ray-batching.md) by providing the structured ray tensors that are subsequently shuffled and sampled.

## How it works
The method supports two primary data formats: Synthetic Blender datasets and Real-world LLFF datasets. The implementation details diverge significantly between these two formats regarding coordinate systems, background handling, and normalization.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Image files (PNG/JPG) and metadata files (`transforms_{split}.json` for Blender, `poses_bounds.npy` for LLFF).
- Configuration parameters: `near`, `far`, `WHITE_BG`, `background_color`.

**Outputs:**
- A tensor of rays with shape `(N, 6)` or `(N, 6)` where the first 3 dimensions are ray origins $(o_x, o_y, o_z)$ and the next 3 are normalized ray directions $(d_x, d_y, d_z)$.
- Target RGB values with shape `(N, 3)`.
- (Optional) Foreground masks with shape `(N, 1)` or `(N, 3)`.

### Data Structures
**Blender Dataset:**
- `transforms_{split}.json`: Contains a list of `frames`. Each frame has `file_path`, `transform_matrix` (4x4), and optionally `foreground_mask`.
- `camera_angle_x`: Horizontal field of view in radians.

**LLFF Dataset:**
- `poses_bounds.npy`: Array of shape `(N, 17)`. The first 12 columns are the flattened 3x4 pose matrix (row-major), followed by `H`, `W`, `focal`. The last 2 columns are `near` and `far` bounds.

### Steps

#### 1. Load Metadata and Compute Focal Length
For Blender datasets, load `transforms_{split}.json`. Extract `camera_angle_x` and compute the focal length $f$:
$$ f = \frac{0.5 \cdot W}{\tan(0.5 \cdot \text{camera\_angle\_x})} $$
[S1, unofficial]

For LLFF datasets, load `poses_bounds.npy`. Reshape the pose data to `(N, 3, 4)` and extract $H, W, f$ from the first frame's bounds. [S1, unofficial]

#### 2. Preprocess Images and Poses
**Blender:**
1. For each frame in the split, load the image and convert to RGBA.
2. Resize to dimensions $(W, H)$.
3. Composite onto a white background if `WHITE_BG` is true:
   $$ \text{rgb} = \text{rgba}_{:3} \cdot \text{rgba}_{3:} + (1 - \text{rgba}_{3:}) $$
   [S1, unofficial]
4. Extract the camera-to-world matrix $C2W$ from the frame's `transform_matrix`.

**LLFF:**
1. Correct the axis convention. The raw colmap poses often use a different coordinate system. Apply the transformation:
   $$ P_{corrected} = [P_{:,1:2}, -P_{:,0:1}, P_{:,2:}] $$
   [S1, unofficial]
2. Append a row $[0, 0, 0, 1]$ to create the 4x4 $C2W$ matrix.
3. Set near and far bounds:
   $$ \text{near} = \min(\text{bounds}) \cdot 0.9 $$
   $$ \text{far} = \max(\text{bounds}) \cdot 1.0 $$
   [S1, unofficial]

#### 3. Generate Ray Origins and Directions
For each camera pose $C2W$, generate rays for every pixel $(i, j)$ in the image grid.

1. Create pixel coordinate grids $i \in [0, W-1]$ and $j \in [0, H-1]$.
2. Compute camera-space directions (OpenCV convention: z-forward, y-down, x-right):
   $$ d_{cam} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{i - C_x}{f} \\ -\frac{j - C_y}{f} \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} $$
   where $C_x = W/2$ and $C_y = H/2$. The negative sign on the y-component flips the image y-down to camera y-up. The z-component is -1 because the camera looks down the negative z-axis in NeRF's convention.
   [S1, unofficial]
3. Transform directions to world space:
   $$ d_{world} = d_{cam} \cdot C2W_{0:3, 0:3}^T $$
   [S1, unofficial]
4. Set ray origins to the camera center:
   $$ o_{world} = C2W_{0:3, 3} $$
   [S1, unofficial]

**LLFF NDC Normalization:**
For LLFF datasets, rays are converted to Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) to handle forward-facing scenes uniformly.
1. Shift ray origins to the near plane:
   $$ t = -\frac{\text{near} + o_{world, z}}{d_{world, z}} $$
   $$ o_{ndc} = o_{world} + t \cdot d_{world} $$
   [S1, unofficial]
2. Project to NDC space:
   $$ o_{ndc, x} = -\frac{2f}{W} \frac{o_{ndc, x}}{o_{ndc, z}} $$
   $$ o_{ndc, y} = -\frac{2f}{H} \frac{o_{ndc, y}}{o_{ndc, z}} $$
   $$ o_{ndc, z} = 1 + \frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{o_{ndc, z}} $$
   $$ d_{ndc, x} = -\frac{2f}{W} \left( \frac{d_{world, x}}{d_{world, z}} - \frac{o_{ndc, x}}{o_{ndc, z}} \right) $$
   $$ d_{ndc, y} = -\frac{2f}{H} \left( \frac{d_{world, y}}{d_{world, z}} - \frac{o_{ndc, y}}{o_{ndc, z}} \right) $$
   $$ d_{ndc, z} = -\frac{2 \cdot \text{near}}{o_{ndc, z}} $$
   [S1, unofficial]

#### 4. Sample Rays for Training
During training, not all rays are used per iteration to save memory and compute.
1. Determine the number of rays to sample (`N_rand`).
2. Select random image indices. If `batch_image_sampling` is enabled, sample `train_num_rays` indices from the dataset; otherwise, sample 1 index.
3. For the selected images, sample random pixel coordinates $(x, y)$.
4. Extract the corresponding ray origins and directions from the precomputed arrays.
5. Normalize the ray directions:
   $$ d_{norm} = \frac{d}{\|d\|_2} $$
   [S2, unofficial]
6. Concatenate origins and normalized directions into a tensor of shape `(N_rand, 6)`.
   [S2, unofficial]

#### 5. Handle Background Color
Set the model's background color based on configuration:
- If `background_color` is 'white', initialize as `torch.ones((3,))`.
- If 'random', initialize with random values.
   [S2, unofficial]

### Pseudocode

```python
import numpy as np
import torch
import json
import os

def load_blender_data(data_dir, split='train', white_bg=True):
    """
    Loads and preprocesses Blender dataset.
    Returns: rays (N, 6), rgbs (N, 3), masks (N, 1) or None
    """
    with open(os.path.join(data_dir, f'transforms_{split}.json'), 'r') as f:
        meta = json.load(f)
    
    W = meta['w']
    H = meta['h']
    focal = 0.5 * W / np.tan(0.5 * meta['camera_angle_x'])
    
    rays_o_list = []
    rays_d_list = []
    rgbs_list = []
    masks_list = []
    
    for frame in meta['frames']:
        # Load image
        img_path = os.path.join(data_dir, frame['file_path'])
        img = np.array(Image.open(img_path))
        if img.shape[2] == 4:
            rgba = img.astype(np.float32) / 255.0
            # Composite on white background
            rgb = rgba[:, :, :3] * rgba[:, :, 3:] + (1 - rgba[:, :, 3:])
            mask = rgba[:, :, 3:]
        else:
            rgb = img.astype(np.float32) / 255.0
            mask = np.ones((H, W, 1))
            rgb = np.concatenate([rgb, np.zeros((H, W, 1))], axis=-1) # Placeholder for mask logic if no alpha
        
        # Get C2W
        c2w = np.array(frame['transform_matrix'])
        
        # Generate rays
        ray_o, ray_d = get_rays_np(H, W, focal, c2w)
        
        rays_o_list.append(ray_o)
        rays_d_list.append(ray_d)
        rgbs_list.append(rgb)
        masks_list.append(mask)
        
    rays_o = np.concatenate(rays_o_list, axis=0)
    rays_d = np.concatenate(rays_d_list, axis=0)
    rgbs = np.concatenate(rgbs_list, axis=0)
    masks = np.concatenate(masks_list, axis=0)
    
    return rays_o, rays_d, rgbs, masks

def get_rays_np(H, W, focal, c2w):
    """
    Generates ray origins and directions for a single image.
    """
    i, j = np.meshgrid(np.arange(W, dtype=np.float32),
                       np.arange(H, dtype=np.float32), indexing="xy")
    cx = W / 2.0
    cy = H / 2.0
    
    # Camera space directions
    dirs = np.stack([
        (i - cx) / focal,
        -(j - cy) / focal,
        -np.ones_like(i)
    ], -1)
    
    # Transform to world space
    ray_d = np.einsum("hwc,kc->hwk", dirs, c2w[:3, :3])
    ray_o = np.broadcast_to(c2w[:3, 3], ray_d.shape)
    
    return ray_o, ray_d

def load_llff_data(data_dir, holdout=8, factor=8):
    """
    Loads and preprocesses LLFF dataset.
    Returns: rays_o_ndc, rays_d_ndc, rgbs, masks
    """
    poses_bounds = np.load(os.path.join(data_dir, 'poses_bounds.npy'))
    N = poses_bounds.shape[0]
    
    # Reshape
    poses = poses_bounds[:, :12].reshape(N, 3, 4)
    bounds = poses_bounds[:, -2:]
    
    # Extract H, W, focal from first frame
    H, W, focal = poses[0, :, 4]
    H /= factor
    W /= factor
    focal /= factor
    
    # Correct axis convention
    poses_np = np.concatenate([
        poses[:, :, 1:2], 
        -poses[:, :, 0:1], 
        poses[:, :, 2:]
    ], axis=2)
    
    # Append homogeneous row
    poses_homo = np.concatenate([
        poses_np, 
        np.zeros((N, 1, 4))
    ], axis=1)
    poses_homo[:, 3, 3] = 1.0
    
    c2ws = poses_homo
    
    # Set near/far
    near = np.min(bounds) * 0.9
    far = np.max(bounds) * 1.0
    
    # Split train/val
    val_ids = list(range(0, len(c2ws), holdout))
    train_ids = [i for i in range(len(c2ws)) if i not in val_ids]
    
    # Generate rays for training set
    rays_o_list = []
    rays_d_list = []
    rgbs_list = []
    
    for i in train_ids:
        c2w = c2ws[i]
        # Get rays in world space
        ray_o, ray_d = get_rays_np(int(H), int(W), focal, c2w)
        
        # Convert to NDC
        ray_o_ndc, ray_d_ndc = ndc_rays(H, W, focal, near, ray_o, ray_d)
        
        rays_o_list.append(ray_o_ndc)
        rays_d_list.append(ray_d_ndc)
        
        # Load image
        img_path = os.path.join(data_dir, f'images_4/{i}.png') # Assuming standard LLFF structure
        img = np.array(Image.open(img_path))
        img = img.astype(np.float32) / 255.0
        rgbs_list.append(img)
        
    rays_o_ndc = np.concatenate(rays_o_list, axis=0)
    rays_d_ndc = np.concatenate(rays_d_list, axis=0)
    rgbs = np.concatenate(rgbs_list, axis=0)
    
    return rays_o_ndc, rays_d_ndc, rgbs

def ndc_rays(H, W, focal, near, ray_o, ray_d):
    """
    Converts rays to NDC space.
    """
    # Shift to near plane
    t = -(near + ray_o[..., 2]) / ray_d[..., 2]
    o0 = ray_o + t[..., np.newaxis] * ray_d
    
    # NDC projection
    o0x = -(2 * focal / W) * o0[..., 0] / o0[..., 2]
    o0y = -(2 * focal / H) * o0[..., 1] / o0[..., 2]
    o0z = 1.0 + 2.0 * near / o0[..., 2]
    
    dx = -(2 * focal / W) * (ray_d[..., 0] / ray_d[..., 2] - o0[..., 0] / o0[..., 2])
    dy = -(2 * focal / H) * (ray_d[..., 1] / ray_d[..., 2] - o0[..., 1] / o0[..., 2])
    dz = -2.0 * near / o0[..., 2]
    
    ray_o_ndc = np.stack([o0x, o0y, o0z], axis=-1)
    ray_d_ndc = np.stack([dx, dy, dz], axis=-1)
    
    return ray_o_ndc, ray_d_ndc
```

### Parameters
- `near`: 2.0 for Blender, computed from bounds for LLFF.
- `far`: 6.0 for Blender, computed from bounds for LLFF.
- `WHITE_BG`: True for Blender, False for LLFF.
- `background_color`: 'white' or 'random'.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Coordinate Conventions:** Blender uses a right-handed coordinate system with Y-up, while NeRF's internal ray generation assumes a specific camera space. The flip in the y-direction of the ray direction is critical.
- **LLFF Axis Correction:** The raw colmap poses for LLFF often have a different axis orientation (e.g., Z-up). The specific permutation `[1, -0, 2]` is required to align with NeRF's convention.
- **NDC Normalization:** Only applicable to forward-facing scenes (LLFF). Applying NDC to non-forward-facing scenes (like Blender) can distort the geometry.
- **Background Compositing:** Synthetic Blender datasets often have transparent backgrounds. Failing to composite onto a white background before training can lead to incorrect color predictions.

## Subtopics

### LLFF Dataset Loading
Loads real-world LLFF dataset scenes, parsing colmap poses from `poses_bounds.npy`, correcting axis conventions, and applying NDC normalization. Unlike Blender data, LLFF data does not use a white background (`WHITE_BG=False`) and requires NDC transformation for uniform sampling. [S1, unofficial]

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot H \cdot W)$ for preprocessing, where $N$ is the number of images. Ray generation is linear in the number of pixels.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot H \cdot W \cdot 6)$ to store all ray origins and directions. This can be significant for high-resolution images.
- **Performance:** Precomputing rays allows for fast training iterations, as ray generation is moved out of the training loop.
- **Strengths:** Handles both synthetic and real-world data formats. Efficient batching via precomputed rays.
- **Limitations:** High memory usage for large datasets. NDC normalization is only suitable for forward-facing scenes.

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020. The data preparation methods described here are based on the official NeRF implementation and subsequent popular open-source repositories like `nerf-from-scratch` and `instant-nsr-pl`. The Blender dataset is a standard synthetic benchmark, while the LLFF dataset is a standard real-world forward-facing benchmark. [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]
