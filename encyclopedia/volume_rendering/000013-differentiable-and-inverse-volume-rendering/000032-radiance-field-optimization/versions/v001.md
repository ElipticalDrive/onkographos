# Radiance-field optimization

Also called: Differentiable volume rendering optimization, Inverse volume rendering, Novel view synthesis via radiance fields

## Overview
Radiance-field optimization is the process of reconstructing a 3D scene representation (a "radiance field") from a set of 2D images by minimizing the photometric error between rendered views and ground-truth observations [S2]. It solves the inverse problem of determining the underlying volumetric or implicit scene properties (density, color, geometry) that produced the input images. What sets it apart from explicit grid-based siblings like [Explicit Radiance Fields](806-explicit-radiance-fields.md) and [Plenoxels](1501-plenoxels.md) is the frequent use of neural networks (typically MLPs) or factorized tensor structures to represent the scene implicitly, allowing for high-fidelity reconstruction of complex geometry and view-dependent appearance without explicit mesh or voxel structures [S2][S3, unofficial]. It builds on [Differentiable and inverse volume rendering](42-differentiable-and-inverse-volume-rendering.md) by treating the scene parameters as differentiable variables updated via gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core objective of minimizing a photometric loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground-truth color $C(\mathbf{r})$ for a batch of rays $\mathcal{R}$:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \right\|_2^2
$$

The optimization loop generally follows these steps:
1.  **Ray Sampling**: Random pixels are selected from training images. Ray origins and directions are computed using camera intrinsics ($K$) and extrinsics ($c2w$).
2.  **Forward Pass**: Points are sampled along rays within near/far bounds. The field model predicts density ($\sigma$) and color ($c$). Differentiable volume rendering computes the final pixel color.
3.  **Loss Computation**: The Mean Squared Error (MSE) is calculated. Additional terms (e.g., Eikonal, TV) may be added.
4.  **Backpropagation**: Gradients are computed and weights are updated via an optimizer (typically Adam).
5.  **Scheduling**: Learning rates and other hyperparameters are adjusted based on the current step or epoch.

### Local Light Field Fusion
Designed for producing photorealistic novel views for well-sampled forward-facing scenes [S5]. It uses a trained 3D convolutional network to directly predict a discretized frustum-sampled RGB$\alpha$ grid (multiplane image or MPI) for each input view, then renders novel views by alpha compositing and blending nearby MPIs into the novel viewpoint [S5]. It does not exceed 64 pixels of disparity between input views [S5]. It can process a small input dataset in under 10 minutes but produces enormous storage requirements (over 15GB for one "Realistic Synthetic" scene) [S5]. It frequently fails to estimate correct geometry in datasets with up to 400-500 pixels of disparity between views and blends between different scene representations, resulting in perceptually-distracting inconsistency [S5].

### Inverse Rendering with Adjoint Derivatives
An optimization method that estimates scene optical properties (position, scale, rotation, cross-section, spherical harmonic coefficients) from images using gradient-based optimization with adjoint derivatives of the forward pass [S6, unofficial]. It starts optimization with a small number of primitives and gradually generates new primitives to add detail [S6, unofficial]. It uses the Bound Adam optimizer and an L1 error, D-SSIM, plus regularization terms for anisotropy and density of small primitives as the loss function [S6, unofficial].

### EndoNeRF
A method for deformable surgical scene reconstruction using a canonical radiance field and a time-dependent displacement field, optimized with stereo depth-cueing and supervision [S7]. Radiance and density at point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $\tau$ are obtained by querying the canonical field $F_\theta$ at the displaced location: $F_\theta(\mathbf{x} + G_\phi(\mathbf{x}, \tau), \mathbf{d})$ [S7]. It uses an MLP architecture with 8 fully connected ReLU layers, each with 256 channels [S7]. It optimizes an entire spatial-temporal field, resulting in significant time and resource consumption [S7].

### Light Field Representation
A technique to generate high-quality light field representation and integrate it into the GPU-based raycasting pipeline to provide novice audiences access to interactive volume renditions [S8].

### NeuRAD
A neural rendering method for Autonomous Driving (AD) with sensor models (camera + LiDAR) [S9, unofficial].

## Background
Radiance-field optimization emerged prominently with the introduction of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) by Mildenhall et al. in 2020/2021, which popularized the use of implicit neural representations for novel view synthesis [S2][S3, S4, unofficial]. Prior to this, methods like Local Light Field Fusion (LLFF) used explicit multiplane images for forward-facing scenes [S5]. The field has since expanded to include explicit grid-based methods like Plenoxels [S1, S3, unofficial] and TensoRF [S1, S3, unofficial], as well as non-MLP representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting [S3, S4, unofficial]. The underlying principle relies on the discrete Volume Rendering Equation (VRE), where radiance is calculated by a single-path summation along the ray trajectory, avoiding the need for full path tracing [S2]. This approach has been applied to various domains including dynamic scenes (Neural Volumes) [S2], medical imaging (EndoNeRF) [S7], and non-line-of-sight imaging (NeTF) [S9, unofficial].
