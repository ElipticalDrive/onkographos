# Spherical Harmonics Evaluation

Also called: SH2RGB, SH Evaluation

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) Evaluation computes the view-dependent RGB color of a 3D Gaussian splat by projecting its stored SH coefficients onto a viewing direction vector [S2, S3, S7, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently representing and querying anisotropic, view-dependent appearance for millions of Gaussian primitives in real-time rendering [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from general SH evaluation by using hardcoded polynomial constants and a fixed degree-3 basis (16 coefficients per color channel) optimized for the specific coordinate system and normalization conventions of 3D Gaussian Splatting, avoiding expensive trigonometric function calls [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** `spherical_harmonics`: A tensor/array of shape $(N, 48)$ containing SH coefficients for $N$ Gaussians. The coefficients are stored grouped by basis function, with each basis function containing 3 values for the R, G, B channels [S3, S7, unofficial].
- **Input:** `dir`: A tensor/array of shape $(N, 3)$ containing normalized viewing direction vectors $(x, y, z)$ from the camera origin to each Gaussian [S7, unofficial].
- **Output:** `color`: A tensor/array of shape $(N, 3)$ containing the computed RGB color values, typically clamped to $[0, 1]$ [S2, S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The SH coefficients are stored as a flat array or tensor of size $N \times 48$. For each Gaussian, the 48 coefficients are arranged by spherical harmonic degree and basis function, interleaved with color channels:
- Indices 0–2: Degree 0 (DC) coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 3–5: Degree 1, basis 1 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 6–8: Degree 1, basis 2 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 9–11: Degree 1, basis 3 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 12–14: Degree 2, basis 1 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 15–17: Degree 2, basis 2 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 18–20: Degree 2, basis 3 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 21–23: Degree 2, basis 4 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 24–26: Degree 2, basis 5 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 27–29: Degree 3, basis 1 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 30–32: Degree 3, basis 2 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 33–35: Degree 3, basis 3 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 36–38: Degree 3, basis 4 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 39–41: Degree 3, basis 5 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 42–44: Degree 3, basis 6 coefficients for R, G, B.
- Indices 45–47: Degree 3, basis 7 coefficients for R, G, B [S3, S7, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Normalize Direction:** Normalize the input direction vector $\mathbf{dir} = (x, y, z)$ to unit length [S1, S3, S7, unofficial].
2. **Extract DC Color:** Initialize output color $\mathbf{C}$ with the DC coefficients at indices 0, 1, 2. The DC basis function is constant: $Y_0^0 = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}} \approx 0.28209479177387814$ [S1, S4, S9, unofficial].
   $$ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{c}_0 \times 0.28209479177387814 $$
3. **Add Degree 1 Terms:** Extract coefficients $\mathbf{c}_1, \mathbf{c}_2, \mathbf{c}_3$ from indices 3–5, 6–8, 9–11. The degree 1 basis functions are proportional to $-y$, $z$, and $-x$. The constant $SH\_C1 = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4\sqrt{\pi}} \approx -0.48860251190291987$ [S1, S7, unofficial].
   $$ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C} - SH\_C1 \times y \times \mathbf{c}_1 + SH\_C1 \times z \times \mathbf{c}_2 - SH\_C1 \times x \times \mathbf{c}_3 $$
4. **Add Degree 2 Terms:** Extract coefficients $\mathbf{c}_4$ through $\mathbf{c}_8$ from indices 12–14, 15–17, 18–20, 21–23, 24–26. Compute squared and mixed terms: $xx=x^2, yy=y^2, zz=z^2, xy=xy, yz=yz, xz=xz$. The constants are [S1, S7, unofficial]:
   - $SH\_C2\_0 = 1.0925484305920792$ (for $xy$)
   - $SH\_C2\_1 = -1.0925484305920792$ (for $yz$)
   - $SH\_C2\_2 = 0.94617469575755997$ (for $2zz - xx - yy$)
   - $SH\_C2\_3 = -1.0925484305920792$ (for $xz$)
   - $SH\_C2\_4 = 0.54627421529603959$ (for $xx - yy$)
   $$ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C} + SH\_C2\_0 \times xy \times \mathbf{c}_4 + SH\_C2\_1 \times yz \times \mathbf{c}_5 + SH\_C2\_2 \times (2.0 \times zz - xx - yy) \times \mathbf{c}_6 + SH\_C2\_3 \times xz \times \mathbf{c}_7 + SH\_C2\_4 \times (xx - yy) \times \mathbf{c}_8 $$
5. **Add Degree 3 Terms:** If `sh_dim` > 27 (i.e., degree 3 is enabled), extract coefficients $\mathbf{c}_9$ through $\mathbf{c}_{15}$ from indices 27–29, 30–32, 33–35, 36–38, 39–41, 42–44, 45–47. The constants and basis polynomials are [S1, S7, unofficial]:
   - $SH\_C3\_0 = 0.59004358992664352$ (for $y(-3xx + yy)$)
   - $SH\_C3\_1 = 2.8906114426405538$ (for $xyz$)
   - $SH\_C3\_2 = 0.45704579946446572$ (for $y(1 - 5zz)$)
   - $SH\_C3\_3 = 0.3731763325901154$ (for $z(5zz - 3)$)
   - $SH\_C3\_4 = 0.45704579946446572$ (for $x(1 - 5zz)$)
   - $SH\_C3\_5 = 1.4453057213202769$ (for $z(xx - yy)$)
   - $SH\_C3\_6 = 0.59004358992664352$ (for $x(-xx + 3yy)$)
   $$ \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{C} + SH\_C3\_0 \times y \times (3.0 \times xx - yy) \times \mathbf{c}_9 + SH\_C3\_1 \times xy \times z \times \mathbf{c}_{10} + SH\_C3\_2 \times y \times (1.0 - 5.0 \times zz) \times \mathbf{c}_{11} + SH\_C3\_3 \times z \times (5.0 \times zz - 3.0) \times \mathbf{c}_{12} + SH\_C3\_4 \times x \times (1.0 - 5.0 \times zz) \times \mathbf{c}_{13} + SH\_C3\_5 \times z \times (xx - yy) \times \mathbf{c}_{14} + SH\_C3\_6 \times x \times (-xx + 3.0 \times yy) \times \mathbf{c}_{15} $$
   Note: The signs in the polynomial terms match the hardcoded constants in the reference implementation [S1, unofficial].
6. **Clamp and Return:** Add 0.5 to the color vector and clamp to $[0.0, 1.0]$ [S3, S7, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def eval_spherical_harmonics(spherical_harmonics, dir, sh_dim=48):
    # Normalize direction vector
    norm = sqrt(dir[:, 0]**2 + dir[:, 1]**2 + dir[:, 2]**2)
    x = dir[:, 0] / norm
    y = dir[:, 1] / norm
    z = dir[:, 2] / norm
    
    # Precompute squared and mixed terms
    xx = x * x
    yy = y * y
    zz = z * z
    xy = x * y
    yz = y * z
    xz = x * z
    
    # Constants
    SH_C0 = 0.28209479177387814
    SH_C1 = -0.48860251190291987
    SH_C2_0 = 1.0925484305920792
    SH_C2_1 = -1.0925484305920792
    SH_C2_2 = 0.94617469575755997
    SH_C2_3 = -1.0925484305920792
    SH_C2_4 = 0.54627421529603959
    SH_C3_0 = 0.59004358992664352
    SH_C3_1 = 2.8906114426405538
    SH_C3_2 = 0.45704579946446572
    SH_C3_3 = 0.3731763325901154
    SH_C3_4 = 0.45704579946446572
    SH_C3_5 = 1.4453057213202769
    SH_C3_6 = 0.59004358992664352
    
    # Extract DC coefficients (indices 0, 1, 2)
    c0 = spherical_harmonics[:, 0:3]
    color = c0 * SH_C0
    
    # Degree 1 coefficients (indices 3-11)
    c1 = spherical_harmonics[:, 3:6]
    c2 = spherical_harmonics[:, 6:9]
    c3 = spherical_harmonics[:, 9:12]
    color = color - SH_C1 * y * c1 + SH_C1 * z * c2 - SH_C1 * x * c3
    
    # Degree 2 coefficients (indices 12-26)
    c4 = spherical_harmonics[:, 12:15]
    c5 = spherical_harmonics[:, 15:18]
    c6 = spherical_harmonics[:, 18:21]
    c7 = spherical_harmonics[:, 21:24]
    c8 = spherical_harmonics[:, 24:27]
    color = color + SH_C2_0 * xy * c4 + SH_C2_1 * yz * c5 + SH_C2_2 * (2.0 * zz - xx - yy) * c6 + SH_C2_3 * xz * c7 + SH_C2_4 * (xx - yy) * c8
    
    # Degree 3 coefficients (indices 27-47)
    if sh_dim > 27:
        c9 = spherical_harmonics[:, 27:30]
        c10 = spherical_harmonics[:, 30:33]
        c11 = spherical_harmonics[:, 33:36]
        c12 = spherical_harmonics[:, 36:39]
        c13 = spherical_harmonics[:, 39:42]
        c14 = spherical_harmonics[:, 42:45]
        c15 = spherical_harmonics[:, 45:48]
        color = color + SH_C3_0 * y * (3.0 * xx - yy) * c9 + SH_C3_1 * xy * z * c10 + SH_C3_2 * y * (1.0 - 5.0 * zz) * c11 + SH_C3_3 * z * (5.0 * zz - 3.0) * c12 + SH_C3_4 * x * (1.0 - 5.0 * zz) * c13 + SH_C3_5 * z * (xx - yy) * c14 + SH_C3_6 * x * (-xx + 3.0 * yy) * c15
        
    # Add bias and clamp to valid color range
    color = color + 0.5
    color = max(0.0, min(1.0, color))
    return color
```

**Parameters:**
- `sh_dim`: The number of SH coefficients used. If > 27, Degree 3 terms are evaluated [S2, unofficial]. Typical value is 48 (degree 3).
- `spherical_harmonics`: Tensor of shape $(N, 48)$ [S3, unofficial].
- `dir`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$ [S7, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Use float32 for performance. Double precision may be needed for very high dynamic range scenes, but standard 3DGS uses float32 [S2, unofficial].
- **Boundaries:** The direction vector must be normalized. If not, the SH evaluation will be incorrect [S1, unofficial].
- **Degenerate Input:** If the direction vector is zero, normalization will fail. Guard against this by checking the norm [G].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of Gaussians. The evaluation involves a fixed number of arithmetic operations per Gaussian (approx. 100-200 FLOPs for degree 3) [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the coefficients and intermediate results. The coefficients are stored as $N \times 48$ floats [S3, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** On modern GPUs, evaluating degree 3 SH for millions of Gaussians can be done in real-time (30+ FPS) using optimized CUDA kernels [S2, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** Scales linearly with the number of Gaussians. Parallelization is efficient due to the independent nature of each Gaussian's evaluation.
- **Strengths:** Efficient, differentiable, and captures view-dependent appearance well up to degree 3.
- **Limitations:** High-degree SH (beyond 3) becomes computationally expensive and may introduce ringing artifacts. The fixed basis functions may not capture very sharp specularities well.
- **When to Use:** Real-time rendering of view-dependent scenes with millions of primitives.
- **When Not to Use:** Scenes with extremely sharp specular highlights that require higher degrees or different representations.

## Background
Spherical Harmonics are a set of orthogonal functions defined on the sphere, commonly used in computer graphics for lighting and view-dependent effects [S4, S9, unofficial]. In 3D Gaussian Splatting, SH coefficients are used to represent the view-dependent color of each Gaussian primitive [S2, unofficial]. The method was introduced in the original 3D Gaussian Splatting paper by Kerbl et al. (2023) [S2, unofficial]. The specific implementation details, such as the hardcoded constants and degree 3 basis, are derived from the reference implementation [S1, unofficial].
