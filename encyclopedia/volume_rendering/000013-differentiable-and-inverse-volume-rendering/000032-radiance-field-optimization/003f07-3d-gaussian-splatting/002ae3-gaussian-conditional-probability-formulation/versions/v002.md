# Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation

Also called: Unnormalized 4D Gaussian probability distribution

## Overview
This method treats the unnormalized 4D Gaussian primitive as a special probability distribution over spatial coordinates $(u, v)$ and time $t$, enabling the factorization of the joint distribution into a temporal marginal and a conditional spatial component [S1, unofficial]. This factorization allows for efficient rendering and optimization by decoupling temporal dynamics from spatial geometry, distinguishing it from static 3D Gaussian Splatting which lacks this probabilistic temporal modeling [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** An unnormalized 4D Gaussian defined by mean $\mu = [\mu_u, \mu_v, \mu_t]^\top$ and covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$.
*   **Output:** The factorized probability density function $p(u, v, t)$ decomposed into $p(t)$ and $p(u, v|t)$, along with the conditional parameters $\mu_{uv|t}$ and $\Sigma_{uv|t}$ required for rendering at a specific time $t$.

**Data Structures:**
The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ is partitioned into blocks corresponding to spatial $(uv)$ and temporal $(t)$ dimensions:
$$
\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{uv} & \Sigma_{uv,t} \\ \Sigma_{t,uv} & \Sigma_t \end{bmatrix}
$$
Where:
*   $\Sigma_{uv} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ is the spatial covariance.
*   $\Sigma_t \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1}$ (scalar) is the temporal variance.
*   $\Sigma_{uv,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 1}$ is the cross-covariance between space and time.
*   $\Sigma_{t,uv} = \Sigma_{uv,t}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 2}$.

**Mathematical Formulation:**
The joint probability density is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$
p(u, v, t) = (2\pi)^{-3/2} \det(\Sigma)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v, t|\mu, \Sigma)
$$
This joint distribution factorizes into a temporal marginal and a conditional spatial distribution [S1, unofficial]:
$$
p(u, v, t) = p(t)p(u, v|t)
$$

1.  **Temporal Marginal $p(t)$:**
    $$
    p(t) = (2\pi)^{-1/2} \det(\Sigma_t)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(t|\mu_t, \Sigma_t)
    $$
    Where $\mu_t$ is the temporal mean and $\Sigma_t$ is the temporal variance.

2.  **Conditional Spatial $p(u, v|t)$:**
    $$
    p(u, v|t) = (2\pi)^{-1} \det(\Sigma_{uv|t})^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v | \mu_{uv|t}, \Sigma_{uv|t})
    $$

3.  **Conditional Covariance (Schur Complement):**
    The conditional covariance $\Sigma_{uv|t}$ is computed using the Schur complement [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \Sigma_{uv|t} = \Sigma_{uv} - \Sigma_{uv,t}\Sigma_t^{-1}\Sigma_{t,uv}
    $$

4.  **Matrix Factorization Proof:**
    To prove the factorization holds for the unnormalized Gaussian, the determinant property $\det(\Sigma) = \det(\Sigma_t) \det(\Sigma_{uv|t})$ is utilized. The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ can be decomposed as [S1, unofficial]:
    $$
    \Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{uv} - \Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ -\Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} & I \end{bmatrix}
    $$
    Note: The middle matrix in the source decomposition contains a zero block for the temporal dimension in the context of the determinant proof structure, effectively isolating the Schur complement for the spatial part relative to the temporal marginal [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def gaussian_conditional_probability(mu, Sigma, t_query):
    """
    Computes the conditional spatial distribution parameters for a 4D Gaussian
    at a specific time t_query.
    
    Args:
        mu: Array [3], mean [mu_u, mu_v, mu_t]
        Sigma: 3x3 Matrix, covariance of [u, v, t]
        t_query: Float, the time instance to condition on
        
    Returns:
        mu_cond: Array [2], conditional mean [mu_u|t, mu_v|t]
        Sigma_cond: 2x2 Matrix, conditional covariance Sigma_uv|t
        p_t: Float, probability density of the temporal marginal at t_query
    """
    # Partition mean
    mu_uv = mu[:2]
    mu_t = mu[2]
    
    # Partition Covariance
    Sigma_uv = Sigma[:2, :2]
    Sigma_uv_t = Sigma[:2, 2]  # 2x1
    Sigma_t_uv = Sigma[2, :2]  # 1x2
    Sigma_t = Sigma[2, 2]      # Scalar
    
    # 1. Compute Temporal Marginal p(t)
    # N(t | mu_t, Sigma_t) = exp(-0.5 * (t - mu_t)^2 / Sigma_t) / sqrt(2*pi*Sigma_t)
    # Note: Sigma_t is a scalar variance
    temporal_diff = t_query - mu_t
    p_t = (1.0 / sqrt(2 * pi * Sigma_t)) * exp(-0.5 * (temporal_diff**2) / Sigma_t)
    
    # 2. Compute Conditional Covariance (Schur Complement)
    # Sigma_uv|t = Sigma_uv - Sigma_uv,t * Sigma_t^-1 * Sigma_t,uv
    # Since Sigma_t is scalar, Sigma_t^-1 is 1/Sigma_t
    Sigma_cond = Sigma_uv - (Sigma_uv_t * (1.0 / Sigma_t) * Sigma_t_uv)
    
    # 3. Compute Conditional Mean
    # mu_uv|t = mu_uv + Sigma_uv,t * Sigma_t^-1 * (t_query - mu_t)
    mu_cond = mu_uv + (Sigma_uv_t * (1.0 / Sigma_t) * temporal_diff)
    
    return mu_cond, Sigma_cond, p_t
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Singularity:** If $\Sigma_t$ is zero or near-zero, the inverse $\Sigma_t^{-1}$ becomes unstable. This implies a static Gaussian with no temporal variance, requiring a limit case handling or regularization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The Schur complement calculation involves subtracting a rank-1 update (or low-rank) matrix from $\Sigma_{uv}$. Numerical stability may be affected if $\Sigma_{uv}$ is ill-conditioned [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### N-Dimensional Gaussian (6DGS/7DGS)
Extends the conditional probability formulation to non-spatial dimensions such as viewing direction or time, modeling a joint distribution over spatial and non-spatial components [S2, unofficial].
*   **6DGS:** Incorporates viewing direction $d$ into a 6D joint distribution $X = [X_p, X_d]^\top \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$, where $X_p$ is spatial and $X_d$ is directional [S2, unofficial]. Conditioning on view $d$ yields conditional covariance $\Sigma_{cond} = \Sigma_p - \Sigma_{pd}\Sigma_d^{-1}\Sigma_{pd}^\top$, conditional mean $\mu_{cond} = \mu_p + \Sigma_{pd}\Sigma_d^{-1}(d-\mu_d)$, and modulated opacity [S2, unofficial].
*   **7DGS:** Adds a temporal dimension $X_t$ to the 6DGS formulation, conditioning on both time and view to produce a dynamic, view-dependent 3D Gaussian [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The factorization allows the temporal component to be evaluated independently of the spatial rendering, potentially reducing the computational overhead of dynamic scenes compared to full 4D volume integration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Enables real-time photorealistic dynamic scene rendering by leveraging the efficient rasterization of the resulting conditional 2D Gaussians at each timestep [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution over time, which may not capture complex, non-Gaussian temporal dynamics or occlusions accurately without additional modeling [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation was introduced in the context of 4D Gaussian Splatting to handle dynamic scenes. It builds on the mathematical properties of multivariate Gaussian distributions to separate temporal and spatial dependencies [S1, unofficial]. The approach was developed to address the limitations of static 3DGS in dynamic environments, providing a differentiable and efficient representation for novel view synthesis of video sequences [S1, unofficial].
