# Frequency Regularization
Also called: FreGS
## Overview
Frequency regularization is a technique used in 3D Gaussian Splatting training to guide the optimization of Gaussian parameters toward high-frequency regions of the scene [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the tendency of standard splatting to produce blurry or low-frequency results by explicitly penalizing the absence of high-frequency content in the rendered output [S1, unofficial]. Unlike geometric constraints that enforce surface alignment, this method operates in the frequency domain to enhance detail and sharpness [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Rendered image $I_{render}$, ground truth image $I_{gt}$, and hyperparameter $\lambda_{freq}$.
*   **Output:** Updated Gaussian parameters $\theta$ via gradient descent.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Rendered Image:** A 2D tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ representing the RGB output of the differentiable rasterizer.
*   **Frequency Domain Representation:** The rendered image is transformed into the frequency domain. While the specific transform is not explicitly defined in the primary source for FreGS, related methods like DWTGS use the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) [S2, unofficial]. For FreGS, the core concept is identifying "high-frequency regions" [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Render:** Compute the rendered image $I_{render}$ from the current Gaussian parameters $\theta$ using the standard differentiable rasterizer.
2.  **Frequency Analysis:** Analyze the frequency content of $I_{render}$. The goal is to identify regions lacking high-frequency information [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Loss:** Calculate a regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{freq}$ that penalizes the model for not capturing high-frequency details. The exact formula is not provided in the sources, but the mechanism involves pushing Gaussians toward areas where high-frequency content is missing or weak [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Combine Loss:** Add the frequency regularization term to the standard photometric loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rgb} + \lambda_{freq} \mathcal{L}_{freq} $$
5.  **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients of $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ with respect to $\theta$ and update the Gaussians.

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{freq}$: Weight for the frequency regularization term. Typical values are not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-sharpening:** Excessive frequency regularization may lead to noise or artifacts if not balanced with the photometric loss.
*   **Precision:** Frequency domain operations may require careful handling of numerical precision to avoid artifacts in the gradient computation.

## Subtopics

### DWTGS
DWTGS is a variant that rethinks frequency regularization specifically for sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting [S2, unofficial]. It applies a Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to the rendered image and penalizes the loss in the wavelet domain [S2, unofficial]. This approach encourages sharper edges compared to standard frequency regularization methods [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when working with sparse input views where edge definition is critical [S2, unofficial].
