# Pruning and Sparsification

Also called: Gaussian Pruning, Sparsification

## Overview
Pruning and Sparsification is the family of methods responsible for removing redundant 3D Gaussians during or after the training of Gaussian Splatting models to reduce memory footprint and improve rendering efficiency. It solves the problem of excessive primitive counts caused by densification strategies, which can lead to "floaters" (noisy, low-opacity primitives) and structural redundancy. Unlike densification methods that add primitives, this family removes them based on criteria such as opacity, accumulated rendering significance, or second-order sensitivity (Fisher Information).

## Shared principles
The family shares the goal of managing the primitive count to match local scene complexity. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Gradient-Based Triggering:** Densification is typically triggered by the magnitude of the 2D positional gradient $\frac{\partial L_v}{\partial \mu_v^k}$ for each Gaussian $g_k$ across training views [S4][S8, unofficial]. A Gaussian becomes a candidate if its average gradient magnitude $\tau_k$ exceeds a threshold $T_{grad}$ [S8, unofficial].
2.  **Split vs. Clone Decision:** For candidates, the action depends on the Gaussian's size relative to the scene extent $e_{scene}$ and a density threshold $P_{dense}$ [S8, unofficial]. If the maximum scaling factor $\max(s_k) > P_{dense} \cdot e_{scene}$, the Gaussian is split into smaller children to resolve over-reconstruction [S8, unofficial]. If $\max(s_k) \le P_{dense} \cdot e_{scene}$, it is cloned to resolve under-reconstruction [S8, unofficial].
3.  **Opacity Management:** Pruning and opacity resets are used to remove redundant "floaters" and stabilize optimization. Gaussians with opacity $\alpha$ below a threshold $\alpha_{min}$ are removed or clamped [S1][S2]. Opacity may be periodically reset to allow re-optimization [S6, unofficial].
4.  **Periodic Execution:** Densification and pruning are performed periodically (e.g., every `refineEvery` steps) rather than at every step to allow the optimizer to stabilize [S1].
5.  **Buffer Management:** Pruned primitives are removed from the active set, often requiring buffer compaction or marking for garbage collection [Parent].

## Subtopics

### [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md)
Significance-Aware Pruning removes redundant 3D Gaussians by evaluating their actual contribution to the rendered image rather than relying solely on opacity or spatial extent. It solves the problem where standard opacity pruning removes large, structurally important Gaussians that have low opacity but high transmittance, and size pruning causes an explosion in Gaussian count as small Gaussians replace large ones. Unlike standard opacity pruning, it spares Gaussians with low opacity if they have high accumulated alpha blending coefficients, thereby optimizing the trade-off between reconstruction quality and scene compactness. See [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md).

### [Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md)
Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) is a post-hoc method for compressing pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models by removing redundant Gaussians based on their sensitivity to reconstruction error. Unlike heuristic approaches that rely on opacity or visibility, PUP computes a principled uncertainty score derived from the Fisher Information matrix of the spatial parameters (mean and scale). It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Significance-Aware Pruning](9676-significance-aware-pruning.md) by using a second-order approximation of the loss landscape rather than accumulated rendering weights, enabling higher compression ratios (up to 90%) while preserving fine foreground details. See [Principled Uncertainty Pruning](9680-principled-uncertainty-pruning.md).

### Depth-Based Gaussian Pruning
A pruning strategy that varies the pruning threshold based on the relative depth (z-axis value) of 3D Gaussians to preserve points at the far plane in forward-facing scenes [S1]. It determines the relative depth for each 3D Gaussian based on its z-axis value and scales the base pruning threshold $t_p$ by a multiplier $w_p$ determined by the relative depth, applying the lowest threshold to the farthest point [S1]. The base threshold $t_p$ is set to $5 \times 10^{-3}$ for real defocus blur datasets and $1 \times 10^{-2}$ for real camera motion blur datasets, with a depth multiplier $w_p$ of 3 [S1].

## Background
Pruning is a core component of the original 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) training pipeline, introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S1]. It was designed to work in tandem with densification to maintain a compact representation. Subsequent works have refined this process: Significance-Aware Pruning was introduced to address the limitations of simple opacity thresholds [S2], while Principled Uncertainty Pruning (PUP) was developed to enable high-compression post-training sparsification using second-order information [S1]. Depth-based pruning was proposed specifically for forward-facing scenes with blur, such as in Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting, to handle sparse far-plane geometry [S1].
