# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the loss functions and densification protocols for **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting), which integrates explicit mesh geometry into the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization pipeline [S1]. It solves the problem of Gaussian primitives drifting away from known surface geometry or expanding uncontrollably, which degrades rendering quality and mesh alignment [S1]. It differs from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting [22] by introducing a projection loss and normal consistency loss to anchor "tightly-bound" splats to a mesh, while retaining the original densification strategy for "loosely-bound" splats to handle occluded or missing geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and ground truth image $I_i$.
*   Set of tightly-bound Gaussian splats $G^t$ and loosely-bound splats $G^l$.
*   Mesh $M$ consisting of faces $F$, where each face $f_j$ has a normal $n_f$ and vertices $v_i$.
*   For each Gaussian $i \in G^t$: center $P_i$, scale parameters $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$, and rotation matrix $R_i$.

**Outputs:**
*   Total loss $L$ used for gradient-based optimization.
*   Updated set of Gaussian splats after densification steps.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Splat:** Contains position $P_i$, scale $S_i$, rotation $R_i$, opacity, and color.
*   **Mesh Face:** Defined by vertices and computed normal.

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{img} = 0.001$ (SSIM weight in image loss) [S1].
*   $\lambda_{nc} = 0.1$ (Normal consistency weight) [S1].
*   $\lambda_{min} = 0.1$ (Min scale regularization weight) [S1].
*   $\lambda_{max} = 10$ (Max scale regularization weight) [S1].
*   $\lambda_{proj} = 50$ (Projection loss weight) [S1].
*   $\rho = 0.1$ (Threshold for maximum scale regularization) [S1].
*   $d_{th} = 0.01$ (Distance threshold to distinguish tightly-bound from loosely-bound splats) [S1].
*   Split scale factor: $1.6$ [S1].
*   Training iterations: $30,000$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Image Loss Calculation:** Compute the difference between the rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and ground truth $I_i$ using a combination of L1 loss and D-SSIM [S1]:
    $$ L_{img} = (1-\lambda_{img})L_{1}(\hat{I}_i, I_i) + \lambda_{img}SSIM(\hat{I}_i, I_i) $$

2.  **Tightly-Bound Splat Normal Estimation:** For each tightly-bound Gaussian $i \in G^t$, estimate its normal $n_i$ in world coordinates [S1]:
    a. Identify the minimum scale axis among $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$.
    b. Apply the rotation matrix $R_i$ to transform this axis into world coordinates.

3.  **Normal Consistency Loss:** Align the estimated normals $n_i$ of tightly-bound splats with the normals $n_f$ of the corresponding mesh faces $f \in F$ [S1]:
    $$ L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^t, f \in F} (1 - n_i \cdot n_f) $$
    *Note: The source sums over all faces $F$, implying a global or nearest-face association is assumed for the dot product calculation.*

4.  **Scale Regularization:** Prevent tightly-bound splats from expanding excessively or becoming too small relative to the mesh surface [S1]:
    $$ L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^t} (\lambda_{min}|\min(s_1, s_2, s_3)| + \lambda_{max}|\max(s_1, s_2, s_3) - \rho|) $$

5.  **Projection Loss:** Ensure tightly-bound splats remain close to the mesh surface [S1]:
    a. For each tightly-bound Gaussian $i$, find the closest mesh face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$.
    b. Calculate the projection of the Gaussian center $P_i$ onto the mesh surface to get point $s_i$. The source provides the formula:
       $$ s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j $$
       *Note: This formula appears to project $P_i$ along the face normal $f_j$ relative to vertex $v_i$, though standard projection usually involves the plane equation. The implementation must follow the source's explicit formula.*
    c. Compute the distance:
       $$ L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \| s_i - P_i \|_2 $$

6.  **Total Loss:** Combine all terms [S1]:
    $$ L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj} $$

7.  **Densification Strategy:**
    *   **Loosely-Bound Splats ($G^l$):** Apply the original 3DGS densification strategy (cloning and splitting based on gradient magnitude and opacity) [S1].
    *   **Tightly-Bound Splats ($G^t$):**
        a. Identify the mesh triangle close to the Gaussian.
        b. **Cloning:** Sample a point from the triangle to initialize the new Gaussian's position. Copy additional attributes (color, opacity, etc.) from the parent [S1].
        c. **Splitting:** Sample a point from the triangle to initialize the new Gaussian's position. Divide the parent's scale by a factor of $1.6$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_loss(rendered_image, gt_image, tightly_bound_gaussians, mesh_faces):
    """
    Computes the total training loss for MeshGS.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Rendered image tensor.
        gt_image: Ground truth image tensor.
        tightly_bound_gaussians: List of Gaussian objects in G^t.
        mesh_faces: List of mesh face objects, each with normal and vertices.
        
    Returns:
        Total loss scalar.
    """
    # 1. Image Loss
    l1_loss = L1Loss(rendered_image, gt_image)
    ssim_loss = SSIMLoss(rendered_image, gt_image)
    lambda_img = 0.001
    l_img = (1 - lambda_img) * l1_loss + lambda_img * ssim_loss
    
    # 2. Normal Consistency Loss
    l_nc = 0.0
    lambda_nc = 0.1
    for gauss in tightly_bound_gaussians:
        # Estimate normal: min scale axis rotated by R
        min_scale_idx = argmin(gauss.scale)
        local_normal = unit_vector(one_hot(min_scale_idx))
        world_normal = rotate_vector(local_normal, gauss.rotation)
        
        # Find corresponding mesh face (implementation detail: nearest face)
        # Assuming get_nearest_face returns a face with normal n_f
        face = get_nearest_face(gauss.position, mesh_faces)
        face_normal = face.normal
        
        # Cosine similarity term
        l_nc += (1 - dot(world_normal, face_normal))
    l_nc *= lambda_nc

    # 3. Scale Regularization Loss
    l_scale = 0.0
    lambda_min = 0.1
    lambda_max = 10
    rho = 0.1
    for gauss in tightly_bound_gaussians:
        scales = gauss.scale # (s1, s2, s3)
        min_s = min(scales)
        max_s = max(scales)
        l_scale += lambda_min * abs(min_s) + lambda_max * abs(max_s - rho)
        
    # 4. Projection Loss
    l_proj = 0.0
    lambda_proj = 50
    for gauss in tightly_bound_gaussians:
        # Find closest face and vertex
        face = get_closest_face(gauss.position, mesh_faces)
        vertex = get_closest_vertex(gauss.position, face.vertices)
        
        # Project center P_i to surface point s_i
        # Formula from source: s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) . f_j) * f_j
        # Note: This projects along the face normal direction relative to vertex
        displacement = dot(gauss.position - vertex, face.normal)
        s_i = gauss.position + displacement * face.normal
        
        l_proj += norm(s_i - gauss.position)
    l_proj *= lambda_proj

    # 5. Total Loss
    total_loss = l_img + l_nc + l_scale + l_proj
    return total_loss

def densify_tightly_bound_gaussians(tightly_bound_gaussians, mesh_faces, threshold=1.6):
    """
    Densifies tightly-bound Gaussians by sampling from mesh triangles.
    """
    new_gaussians = []
    for gauss in tightly_bound_gaussians:
        # Identify close mesh triangle
        face = get_closest_face(gauss.position, mesh_faces)
        
        # Sample point from triangle
        sample_pos = random_sample_triangle(face.vertices)
        
        # Create new Gaussian
        new_gauss = Gaussian()
        new_gauss.position = sample_pos
        
        # Cloning: Copy attributes
        new_gauss.color = gauss.color
        new_gauss.opacity = gauss.opacity
        new_gauss.rotation = gauss.rotation
        
        # Splitting: Scale division
        new_gauss.scale = gauss.scale / threshold
        
        new_gaussians.append(new_gauss)
        
    return new_gaussians
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Projection Formula Ambiguity:** The projection formula $s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j$ projects the point along the face normal $f_j$ offset by the vertex $v_i$. This is not a standard orthogonal projection onto a plane. Implementers must strictly follow this formula as provided in the source [S1].
*   **Normal Estimation:** The normal is derived from the minimum scale axis. If scales are degenerate (e.g., all equal), the axis selection is arbitrary [S1].
*   **Loosely-Bound Handling:** Loosely-bound splats do not use the mesh-aligned losses or densification. They follow standard 3DGS rules, which may lead to inconsistencies if the mesh is incomplete [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The normal consistency and projection losses require iterating over all tightly-bound splats and finding nearest mesh faces/vertices. This adds $O(N \log M)$ or $O(N \cdot M)$ overhead per iteration depending on spatial indexing for mesh queries, where $N$ is the number of tightly-bound splats and $M$ is the number of mesh faces [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the mesh geometry (vertices, faces, normals) in addition to standard Gaussian attributes [S1].
*   **Performance:** Evaluated on Mip-NeRF360 and Deep Blending datasets for 30,000 iterations [S1]. The method aims for high-quality rendering by aligning Gaussians with the mesh, reducing artifacts caused by unanchored primitives [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves geometric fidelity and reduces "flying" artifacts near surfaces by anchoring splats [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the input mesh. If the mesh is noisy or incomplete, the projection and normal losses may introduce errors [S1].

## Background
The method originates from **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting for High-Quality Rendering) [S1]. It builds upon the original 3D Gaussian Splatting [22] by incorporating explicit mesh representations, similar in spirit to SuGaR [13] but with specific adaptations for tightly-bound splats and a distinct loss formulation involving projection and scale regularization [S1]. The approach addresses the limitation of standard 3DGS in ignoring existing 3D geometry during densification [S1].
