# Training Objective and Densification

Also called: MeshGS Training, Tightly/Loosely-bound Training

## Overview
This method defines the loss function and densification strategy for **MeshGS** (Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting), a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface [S1]. It solves the problem of Gaussian splats drifting away from geometry or expanding excessively by introducing surface-aware constraints [S1]. It differs from standard 3DGS [22] by decoupling splats into **tightly-bound** ($G^t$) and **loosely-bound** ($G^l$) sets, applying mesh-specific losses only to $G^t$, and initializing new primitives via mesh sampling rather than gradient-based heuristics alone [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Rendered image $\hat{I}_i$ and ground truth image $I_i$.
- Set of tightly-bound Gaussian splats $G^t$ and loosely-bound splats $G^l$.
- Mesh $M$ consisting of faces $F$, where each face $f_j$ has a normal $n_f$ and vertices $v_i$.
- Scale parameters $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$ and rotation matrix $R_i$ for each Gaussian.

**Outputs:**
- Total loss $L$ for backpropagation.
- Updated set of Gaussian splats via densification.

**Data Structures:**
- **Gaussian Splat:** Position $P_i$, scale $S_i$, rotation $R_i$, opacity, color.
- **Mesh Face:** Vertices $v_i$, normal $n_f$.

**Parameters:**
- $\lambda_{img} = 0.001$ [S1]
- $\lambda_{nc} = 0.1$ [S1]
- $\lambda_{min} = 0.1$ [S1]
- $\lambda_{max} = 10$ [S1]
- $\lambda_{proj} = 50$ [S1]
- $\rho = 0.1$ (scale threshold) [S1]
- Distance threshold $d_{th} = 0.01$ (to distinguish $G^t$ and $G^l$) [S1]
- Training iterations: 30,000 [S1]

**Steps:**

1.  **Image Loss Calculation:**
    Compute the image reconstruction loss using L1 and D-SSIM [S1]:
    $$L_{img} = (1-\lambda_{img})L_{1}(\hat{I}_{i},I_{i}) + \lambda_{img}SSIM(\hat{I}_{i}, I_{i})$$
    Where $\hat{I}_i$ is the rendered image and $I_i$ is the ground truth image [S1].

2.  **Normal Estimation for Tightly-Bound Splats:**
    For each Gaussian $i \in G^t$:
    a. Identify the minimum scale axis among $S_i = (s_1, s_2, s_3)$.
    b. Apply the rotation matrix $R_i$ to transform this axis into world coordinates to estimate the normal $n_i$ [S1].

3.  **Normal Consistency Loss:**
    Compute the alignment between splat normals and mesh face normals [S1]:
    $$L_{nc} = \sum_{i \in G^t, f \in F} (1 - n_i \cdot n_f)$$
    Where $n_i$ is the estimated splat normal and $n_f$ is the face normal of the corresponding mesh face $f$ [S1].

4.  **Scale Regularization Loss:**
    Prevent excessive expansion of tightly-bound splats by regularizing their minimum and maximum scales [S1]:
    $$L_{scale} = \sum_{i \in G^t} (\lambda_{min}|\min(s_1, s_2, s_3)| + \lambda_{max}|\max(s_1, s_2, s_3) - \rho|)$$
    Where $\rho$ is the threshold for the maximum scale [S1].

5.  **Projection Loss:**
    Ensure tightly-bound splats remain close to the mesh surface [S1]:
    a. Project the Gaussian center $P_i$ to the closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$. The projection is calculated as:
       $$s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j$$
       *(Note: The source formula uses $f_j$ as the projection vector/normal in the dot product context, implying projection onto the face plane or edge. The text states "closest point $s_i$ on the mesh surface defined by face $f_j$ and vertex $v_i$". The formula provided in [S1] is $s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) \cdot f_j) \cdot f_j$. This implies projecting $P_i-v_i$ onto the vector $f_j$. If $f_j$ is a face normal, this projects onto the normal line. If $f_j$ is an edge vector, it projects onto the edge. Given the context of "closest point on surface", standard projection onto the plane would use the face normal. However, we must follow the source formula exactly as written [S1].)*
    b. Compute the loss:
       $$L_{proj} = \sum_{i \in G^t} \| s_i - P_i \|_2$$
       This ensures splats do not move too far from the surface [S1].

6.  **Total Loss:**
    Combine all terms [S1]:
    $$L = L_{img} + \lambda_{nc} L_{nc} + L_{scale} + \lambda_{proj} L_{proj}$$

7.  **Densification Strategy:**
    The method distinguishes between tightly-bound ($G^t$) and loosely-bound ($G^l$) splats using a distance threshold $d_{th}=0.01$ [S1].
    - **For Loosely-Bound Splats ($G^l$):** Follow the original 3DGS densification strategy (cloning and splitting based on gradient magnitude and opacity) [S1].
    - **For Tightly-Bound Splats ($G^t$):**
        a. Identify the mesh triangle close to the Gaussian [S1].
        b. Perform point sampling from the triangle to initialize new Gaussian splats [S1].
        c. **Cloning:** Copy additional attributes from the parent Gaussian to the new splat [S1].
        d. **Splitting:** Divide the scale by a factor of 1.6 [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_loss_and_densify(Gaussians, Mesh, Images, Iteration):
    # 1. Separate Tightly and Loosely Bound Splats
    Gt = []
    Gl = []
    for g in Gaussians:
        # Distance threshold d_th = 0.01
        dist = distance_to_mesh(g.position, Mesh)
        if dist < 0.01:
            Gt.append(g)
        else:
            Gl.append(g)

    # 2. Image Loss (Standard 3DGS)
    rendered_img = render(Gaussians, Images.camera)
    L_img = (1 - 0.001) * L1_loss(rendered_img, Images.gt) + 0.001 * SSIM_loss(rendered_img, Images.gt)

    # 3. Tightly-Bound Specific Losses
    L_nc = 0.0
    L_scale = 0.0
    L_proj = 0.0

    for g in Gt:
        # Estimate Normal: Min scale axis rotated by R
        min_scale_idx = argmin(g.scale)
        local_normal = unit_vector(identity_vector(min_scale_idx))
        world_normal = rotate(local_normal, g.rotation)
        n_i = world_normal

        # Find corresponding mesh face f
        f = find_closest_face(g.position, Mesh)
        n_f = f.normal

        # Normal Consistency Loss
        L_nc += (1.0 - dot(n_i, n_f))

        # Scale Regularization
        s_min = min(g.scale)
        s_max = max(g.scale)
        L_scale += (0.1 * abs(s_min) + 10.0 * abs(s_max - 0.1))

        # Projection Loss
        # Project P_i to closest point s_i on mesh surface
        # Source formula: s_i = P_i + ((P_i - v_i) . f_j) * f_j
        # Note: v_i is a vertex of the face, f_j is the face/edge vector used for projection
        v = f.vertices[0] # Pick a vertex from the face
        f_vec = f.normal  # Using face normal as the projection direction based on source formula structure
        # If f_j is meant to be an edge, the source is ambiguous, but we follow the formula structure
        # Assuming f_j is the normal for surface projection consistency
        projection_vector = dot(g.position - v, f_vec) * f_vec
        s_i = g.position + projection_vector
        L_proj += norm(s_i - g.position)

    L_nc = 0.1 * L_nc
    L_scale = L_scale # Weights applied in sum
    L_proj = 50.0 * L_proj

    # 4. Total Loss
    L_total = L_img + L_nc + L_scale + L_proj

    # 5. Densification
    # Loosely-bound: Standard 3DGS strategy
    new_Gl = densify_standard_3dgs(Gl, gradients)

    # Tightly-bound: Mesh-aligned sampling
    new_Gt = []
    for g in Gt:
        # Clone/Split logic based on gradients/opacity
        if should_clone(g):
            # Sample from triangle
            sample_pos = sample_triangle(g.closest_face)
            new_g = copy_gaussian(g)
            new_g.position = sample_pos
            # Copy additional attributes
            new_Gt.append(new_g)
        elif should_split(g):
            # Sample from triangle
            sample_pos = sample_triangle(g.closest_face)
            new_g = copy_gaussian(g)
            new_g.position = sample_pos
            # Divide scale by 1.6
            new_g.scale = g.scale / 1.6
            new_Gt.append(new_g)

    return L_total, new_Gt + new_Gl
```

## Subtopics

### [Budget-Centered Training](9682-budget-centered-training.md)
Initializes and constrains the primitive count to the "knee" of the scene's capacity floor curve ($N^*$), replacing gradient-based pruning with a redundancy map and treating the budget as a hard constraint.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The method adds overhead for mesh intersection and projection calculations for tightly-bound splats at every iteration [S1]. The total training runs for 30,000 iterations [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the mesh geometry and maintaining two sets of splats ($G^t$ and $G^l$) [S1].
- **Performance:** Evaluated on Mip-NeRF360 and Deep Blending datasets [S1]. It achieves high-quality rendering by preventing splats from expanding excessively or drifting from the surface [S1].
- **Strengths:** Aligns Gaussian splats with explicit mesh geometry, improving geometric fidelity [S1].
- **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the extracted mesh (e.g., from LTM [7]) [S1]. The projection formula's interpretation of $f_j$ (face normal vs. edge) may require careful implementation to ensure correct surface projection [S1].

## Background
Originated in **MeshGS: Adaptive Mesh-Aligned Gaussian Splatting for High-Quality Rendering** (2024) [S1]. It builds on the original 3D Gaussian Splatting [22] and integrates mesh reconstruction techniques (like LTM [7]) to bind Gaussians to surfaces. It addresses the limitation of standard 3DGS which overlooks existing 3D geometry during densification [S1].
