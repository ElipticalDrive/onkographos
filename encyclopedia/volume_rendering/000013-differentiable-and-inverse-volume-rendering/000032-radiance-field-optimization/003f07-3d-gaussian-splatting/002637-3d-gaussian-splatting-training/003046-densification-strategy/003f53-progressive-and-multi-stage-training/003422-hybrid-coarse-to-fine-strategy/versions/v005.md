# Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy

Also called: Hybrid coarse-to-fine densification, multi-stage point cloud refinement

## Overview
The Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy is a training methodology for point-based radiance field representations that divides optimization into distinct stages to improve rendering quality over single-stage training [S1]. It addresses the difficulty of converging a dense point cloud from a sparse initialization by progressively refining the spatial distribution of primitives [S1]. Unlike single-stage approaches, it employs a hybrid mechanism that combines coarse structural initialization with fine-grained densification, yielding significantly higher PSNR and SSIM metrics [S1].

## How it works
The strategy operates on a mesh or point cloud representation, progressively densifying it based on rendering error metrics. The implementation details below are derived from the specific Coarse-to-Fine Densification variant described in [S2], which serves as the primary algorithmic realization of this strategy in the context of primitive-based reconstruction.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A downsampled target image (for initialization), initial hyperparameters (iteration bounds, scoring exponents), and a current mesh/point cloud state.
*   **Output:** A refined mesh/point cloud with increased vertex density in high-error regions, ready for subsequent training iterations.

### Data Structures
*   **Mesh:** Defined by a set of vertices $V$ and a set of triangles $T$.
*   **Triangle State:** Each triangle $t$ stores:
    *   $\text{pixels}_t$: The set of pixels covered by the triangle during rasterization.
    *   $\overline{\text{DSSIM}}_t$: The mean per-pixel Structural Dissimilarity (DSSIM) over the covered pixels.
    *   $\text{features}_t$: Vertex features (e.g., color, opacity) averaged or interpolated from parent vertices.

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialization (Coarse Stage):**
    *   Compute Sobel gradient magnitudes on a downsampled version of the target image.
    *   Sample initial vertex positions proportionally to edge strength, applying a small uniform floor to ensure coverage in low-gradient areas.
    *   Create a Delaunay triangulation of the sampled points.
    *   Add boundary vertices to ensure full image coverage.

2.  **Iterative Densification (Fine Stage):**
    *   Iterate through training steps. Densification occurs every 500 iterations, starting at iteration 1,500 and ending at iteration 15,000 [S2].
    *   **Scoring:** For each triangle $t$ in the current mesh:
        *   Calculate the mean DSSIM $\overline{\text{DSSIM}}_t$ for pixels covered by $t$.
        *   Count the number of pixels $|\text{pixels}_t|$.
        *   Exclude triangles with fewer than 3 pixels from scoring [S2].
        *   Compute the score using the formula:
            $$ \text{score}_t = \overline{\text{DSSIM}}_t \cdot |\text{pixels}_t|^\alpha $$
            where $\alpha = 0.75$ is the area-weighting exponent [S2]. This weighting prevents repeated splitting of tiny, high-error triangles and distributes vertices proportionally to spatial impact [S2].
    *   **Selection:** Identify the top-scoring triangles. The number of new vertices to insert is capped at $0.35 \times V$, where $V$ is the current vertex count [S2].
    *   **Insertion:**
        *   Insert centroid vertices for the selected top-scoring triangles.
        *   Initialize the features of new vertices by averaging the features of the parent triangle's vertices [S2].
    *   **Re-triangulation:** Re-run Delaunay triangulation on the full updated vertex set [S2].
    *   **Degeneracy Check:** Detect if the mesh becomes degenerate (e.g., colinear vertices) and re-mesh if necessary, though this is rare in practice [S2].

### Parameters
*   $\alpha = 0.75$: Area-weighting exponent for triangle scoring [S2].
*   Densification Interval: Every 500 iterations [S2].
*   Start Iteration: 1,500 [S2].
*   End Iteration: 15,000 [S2].
*   Max New Vertices: $0.35 \times V$ per step [S2].
*   Minimum Pixels for Scoring: 3 pixels [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degeneracy:** Vertices may become colinear during densification, leading to degenerate triangles. The method includes a check to re-mesh in such cases [S2].
*   **Tiny Triangles:** Triangles with fewer than 3 pixels are excluded from scoring to prevent noise-driven densification [S2].
*   **Precision:** The use of DSSIM requires stable gradient computation; numerical instability in the denominator of the SSIM formula should be handled with a small epsilon constant (standard practice, not explicitly quantified in sources) [G].

## Subtopics

### Coarse-to-Fine Densification
A specific implementation of the hybrid strategy where a sparse mesh is progressively refined by inserting centroid vertices for high-scoring triangles and re-running Delaunay triangulation [S2]. It is chosen when a mesh-based representation is used for primitive initialization. It differs from pure point-cloud densification by maintaining explicit topological connectivity (triangles) during the refinement process [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the Blender dataset, removing the hybrid strategy ('w/o hybrid') reduces PSNR from 30.3 dB to 26.6 dB, SSIM from 0.945 to 0.925, and increases LPIPS from 0.078 to 0.163 [S1].
*   **Complexity:** The densification step involves scoring all triangles and re-running Delaunay triangulation, which is computationally more expensive than simple point insertion but occurs infrequently (every 500 iterations) [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Yields better rendering quality than single-stage training by ensuring a well-distributed initial mesh and targeted refinement [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of densification intervals and scoring parameters; degenerate mesh states may require re-meshing [S2].

## Background
The Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy originates from research into differentiable point-based radiance fields for efficient view synthesis [S1]. It was introduced as a component to validate the effectiveness of multi-stage refinement in point cloud training, contrasting with coordinate-network representations that require ray-marching [S1]. The specific mesh-based densification algorithm was further detailed in work on Neural Harmonic Textures for high-quality primitive-based neural reconstruction [S2].
