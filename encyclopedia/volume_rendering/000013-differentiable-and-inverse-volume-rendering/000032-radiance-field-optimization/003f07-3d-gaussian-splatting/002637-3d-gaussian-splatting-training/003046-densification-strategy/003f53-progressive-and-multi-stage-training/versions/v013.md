# Progressive and Multi-stage Training

Also called: Coarse-to-Fine Training, Curriculum Learning for Gaussians, Two-Stage Optimization

## Overview
Progressive and Multi-stage Training refers to optimization strategies that decouple the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations into distinct temporal phases or hierarchical stages. It solves the problem of optimization instability and incomplete reconstruction that arises when attempting to fit high-frequency details and global structure simultaneously from a sparse or noisy initialization [S1, unofficial]. Unlike single-stage training, which often converges to local minima or suffers from "floaters," these methods establish a coarse global structure first and then progressively densify or refine the representation to capture fine details [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Score-based Densification](4705-score-based-densification.md) by focusing on the temporal scheduling of optimization objectives or structural density rather than just geometric thresholds, and from [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md) by integrating densification and structural changes directly into the primary optimization loop rather than as a post-processing step.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of progressive and multi-stage training involves a temporal or hierarchical decoupling of the optimization process. While specific implementations vary, the shared principles across this family are:

1.  **Temporal Decoupling:** The training process is divided into distinct stages (e.g., Coarse $\rightarrow$ Fine, or Stage 1 $\rightarrow$ Stage 2). Early stages focus on global structure, low-frequency content, or stable initialization, while later stages focus on high-frequency details, local geometry, or refinement [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Progressive Densification:** The density of the Gaussian primitive set is not static. It increases over time, either through explicit splitting/cloning triggered by error metrics in later stages or through the introduction of new primitives in sparse regions [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Curriculum/Schedule:** A schedule dictates when to switch stages or adjust hyperparameters. This may involve switching supervision signals (e.g., from slice-based to voxel-based) or changing the learning rate and densification thresholds [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Stabilization:** The primary goal of the early/coarse stage is to stabilize the optimization landscape, preventing the model from getting stuck in poor local minima caused by random initialization or noisy data [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md)
A methodology that decouples optimization into distinct temporal stages to manage scene complexity. It sets itself apart from [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md) by focusing on the structural density of the primitives themselves rather than the supervision signal, and from [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md) by integrating densification directly into the primary optimization loop. It operates on a set of 3D points or mesh vertices, iteratively inserting new primitives based on error metrics.

### [Training Objective and Densification](7448-training-objective-and-densification.md)
Defines the loss function and densification strategy for **MeshGS**, a variant of 3DGS that aligns primitives with an explicit mesh surface. It solves the problem of Gaussian splats drifting away from geometry by introducing surface-aware constraints. It differs from standard 3DGS by splitting splats into **tightly-bound** ($G^t$) and **loosely-bound** ($G^l$) sets, applying mesh-specific losses only to $G^t$, and using mesh-based sampling for densification of $G^t$.

### [Fine-tuning](9138-fine-tuning.md)
Refers to the optimization phase where a pre-initialized set of 3D Gaussian primitives is refined to achieve photorealistic novel view synthesis. It solves the problem of initializing scene representation efficiently by leveraging pretrained priors or feed-forward predictions. It sets itself apart from standard initialization-only approaches by actively enabling growing and pruning of primitives during the optimization phase, allowing the model to discard redundant initial Gaussians and adapt to scene-specific details.

### [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md)
Addresses the instability and incomplete volumetric reconstruction inherent in optimizing 3D Gaussian representations for shear-warp rendering by decoupling supervision signals over time. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) by explicitly leveraging the structural alignment between 2D slice-based sampling and the shear-warp rendering pipeline, rather than relying solely on geometric densification thresholds.

### Progressive Propagation Strategy
*   **Definition:** Introduces a progressive propagation strategy to expand the Gaussian cloud, ensuring smooth expansion and better coverage [S1, unofficial].
*   **Context:** Used in methods like GaussianPro to manage the growth of the primitive set in a fronto-parallel manner, preventing erratic jumps in density [S1, unofficial].

### Interleaved Densification
*   **Step:** Alternate between spatial and temporal densification every 2 epochs to avoid Gaussians failing to reconstruct either motion or appearance detail [S2].
*   **Context:** Applied in dynamic 3DGS scenarios where both geometry and appearance change over time, ensuring that the model does not neglect temporal consistency for spatial accuracy or vice versa [S2].

### Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U
*   **Definition:** A training strategy that decouples depth network initialization from full model training to improve stability and performance [S3].
*   **Step:**
    1.  Construct RGB point clouds for each input view.
    2.  Project the constructed point clouds to the target view using the differentiable point cloud renderer.
    3.  Aggregate all point clouds from the various input views to render RGB images at the target view.
    4.  Optimize the depth network using a photometric loss between the rendered target views and the ground truth target images.
    5.  Freeze or continue training the initialized depth network and jointly train the entire model (spatial feature network, MLP, renderer, transformer, refinement module).
*   **Parameter:** Performance improvement: approximately 0.4 dB PSNR gain [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Jointly training the depth network and other components from scratch causes instability [S3].

## Background
The concept of progressive training in neural rendering has roots in early NeRF works that used positional encoding schedules and coarse-to-fine optimization [G]. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting, as the representation relies on explicit primitives, the challenge shifts from optimizing continuous fields to managing discrete primitive density and placement. The [Adaptive Density Control](9188-adaptive-density-control.md) method established the baseline for periodic densification, but subsequent works recognized that a single-stage optimization with fixed densification rules could be unstable for complex scenes or dynamic content. The [Hybrid Coarse-to-Fine Strategy](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) and [Curriculum Learning with Slice and Voxel Supervision](12921-curriculum-learning-with-slice-and-voxel-supervision.md) formalized the use of distinct stages to handle structural vs. detail optimization. The [Two-Stage Training Scheme for FWD-U](7173-hybrid-coarse-to-fine-strategy.md) highlights the importance of initializing depth networks separately from the full radiance field model to avoid instability [S3].
