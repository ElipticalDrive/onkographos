# Dynamic and Temporal Modeling

Also called: Dynamic 3DGS, Temporal NeRF, 4D Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
Dynamic and Temporal Modeling extends static radiance field and Gaussian splatting representations to handle non-rigid motion and time-varying scenes, solving the problem of synthesizing novel views from dynamic content captured by sparse or sequential inputs [S1]. It distinguishes itself from static siblings by introducing temporal dimensions into the representation—either through explicit deformation fields, canonical space mappings, or time-conditioned neural networks—allowing for the reconstruction of 4D space-time volumes rather than static 3D geometry [S1].

## Shared principles
The family shares the core objective of mapping spatial coordinates and viewing directions to density/color (or Gaussian parameters) while incorporating a temporal component $t$ [G]. The fundamental challenge is balancing temporal continuity with computational efficiency, often by decomposing the scene into static and dynamic components or by enforcing canonical space constraints to exploit temporal redundancy [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Neural Transient Field (NeTF)](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md)
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [4692-neural-transient-field-netf]. It solves the inverse problem of reconstructing hidden 3D scenes from sparse, unbalanced Time-of-Flight (ToF) measurements captured by Single-Photon Avalanche Diodes (SPADs) [4692-neural-transient-field-netf]. Unlike [NeRF](52-nerf.md), which parametrizes input data along linear rays, NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering in Cartesian space [4692-neural-transient-field-netf]. It is distinguished by its use of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse sampling and a two-stage training strategy to address sampling bias [4692-neural-transient-field-netf].

### SWAG
SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders, yielding state-of-the-art results on diverse photo scenes [6875-swag.md]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like Wild-GS by explicitly modeling transient elements as separate Gaussian entities rather than relying solely on depth regularization or visibility maps to mitigate their effects [6875-swag.md].

### [Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF](7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md)
Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF (OD-NeRF) is an on-the-fly training method for dynamic scenes that replaces explicit temporal deformation fields with a NeRF model conditioned on multi-view projected color statistics [7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on space-time 4D inputs or learned deformation fields which extrapolate poorly to unseen times and require extensive optimization per frame, this approach leverages the relative invariance of projected colors for corresponding 3D points across consecutive frames [7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md]. This implicit correspondence allows the model to effectively utilize radiance fields from previous frames, requiring the minimum number of optimization iterations for each new frame while maintaining interactive training and rendering speeds [7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md].

### [On-the-Fly Dynamic NeRF Training](7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md)
On-the-Fly Dynamic NeRF Training (OD-NeRF) is a method for training dynamic Neural Radiance Fields concurrently with the acquisition of training frames, enabling the streaming of dynamic scenes as they unfold rather than reconstructing them after the event [7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md]. Unlike standard dynamic NeRFs that optimize over full video sequences to reconstruct 4D space-time, this approach treats the radiance field as a hidden state updated incrementally, requiring minimum optimization iterations per frame to achieve interactive speeds [7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md]. It is specifically designed for dynamic scenes captured by multi-view forward-facing cameras [7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md].

### [D-NeRF](8660-d-nerf.md)
D-NeRF is a method for synthesizing novel views at an arbitrary point in time of dynamic scenes with complex non-rigid geometries, optimized from a sparse set of input monocular views without ground-truth geometry or multi-view images [8660-d-nerf.md]. It decomposes the learning process into two modules: a spatial mapping that encodes the scene into a canonical space, and a mapping that transforms this canonical representation into the deformed scene at a particular time [8660-d-nerf.md]. Unlike naive extensions of NeRF that map $(x, y, z, t)$ directly to density and radiance, D-NeRF effectively exploits temporal redundancy by enforcing a common canonical anchor (typically $t=0$), ensuring that the deformation displacement is zero at the canonical time [8660-d-nerf.md]. This approach allows for the rendering of rigid, articulated, and non-rigid motions from a single camera view per time instant [8660-d-nerf.md].

### [Dynamic Object Embedding in Static Scenes](8796-dynamic-object-embedding-in-static-scenes.md)
This method integrates dynamic advertising objects into pre-existing static NeRF scenes by ensuring perspective consistency through spatial alignment and joint rendering with alpha blending [8796-dynamic-object-embedding-in-static-scenes.md]. It distinguishes itself from full-scene dynamic NeRFs (like [D-NeRF](8660-d-nerf.md)) by maintaining a fixed static model for the background and using a lightweight local deformation field only for the dynamic region, reducing parameter count by 95% [8796-dynamic-object-embedding-in-static-scenes.md].

#### Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (Katsumata et al.)
- [S1] An explicit deformation method using Fourier series to fit changes in Gaussian position (inspired by periodic motion) and linear functions to approximate rotation. Other attributes remain unchanged.
- [S1] Dynamic optimization involves optimizing the parameters of the Fourier series and linear function. The number of parameters is independent of time.

#### Temporal 3D Gaussian Splatting
- [S1] A method that introduces a temporal radial basis function to represent temporal opacity, modeling emerging or vanishing content. Polynomial functions model motion and rotation.
- [S1] Spherical harmonics are replaced with features consisting of base color, view-related feature, and time-related feature. The latter two are translated into residual color through an MLP added to the base color.
- [S1] During optimization, new 3D Gaussians are sampled at under-optimized positions based on training error and coarse depth.

#### SWAGS
- [S1] A method that divides the dynamic sequence into different windows based on motion amount, training separate dynamic 3DGS models in different windows with different canonical spaces and deformation fields.
- [S1] The deformation field uses a tunable MLP that focuses more on modeling the dynamic part of the scene.
- [S1] Fine-tuning ensures temporal consistency between windows using overlapping frames to add constraints. The MLP is fixed and only the canonical representation is optimized during fine-tuning.

#### 3DGStream
- [S1] A method that allows online training of 3DGS into dynamic scene reconstruction by modeling the transformation between frames as a neural transformation cache and adaptively adding 3D Gaussians to handle emerging objects.

#### Blur Modeling in 3D GS
- [S2] Techniques to explicitly incorporate blur modeling during training to address real-world capture issues like motion blur and defocus blur.

#### Multi-scale Motion-Compensated Reconstruction
- [S3] A procedure coupled with the two-step DynART/P-DVC algorithm to handle large displacement amplitudes by resolving large-scale features first and progressively enriching with finer details.

## Background
The field originated with methods like D-NeRF, which established the canonical space paradigm for dynamic NeRFs [8660-d-nerf.md]. As 3D Gaussian Splatting gained popularity, researchers adapted it for dynamic scenes, leading to explicit deformation methods like Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (using Fourier series) and Temporal 3D Gaussian Splatting (using polynomial functions for motion and rotation) [S1]. More recent approaches like SWAG focus on handling transient objects, while SWAGS handles complex motion windows within the Gaussian framework [6875-swag.md][S1]. Applications extend to medical fields, such as markless motion reconstruction for infants and monocular endoscopic reconstruction [S1].
