# On-the-Fly Dynamic NeRF Training

Also called: OD-NeRF

## Overview
On-the-fly dynamic NeRF training is a method for reconstructing dynamic scenes by training a neural radiance field concurrently with the acquisition of training frames, enabling the streaming of novel views while the event unfolds [S1]. Unlike standard dynamic NeRFs that require full video sequences for offline 4D reconstruction, this approach estimates the radiance field for the current time step using only the current observations and the previous state, minimizing optimization iterations to achieve interactive speeds [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sequence of multi-view forward-facing images $C_{0:K}$ arriving over time. At step $k$, the inputs are the current images $C_k$ and the radiance field estimate from the previous step $F_{k-1}$.
*   **Outputs:** The updated radiance field function $F_k$ at time $t_k$, which maps spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$, and time $t$ to volume density $\sigma$ and RGB color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The radiance field $F$ is typically represented as a neural network. The specific architecture (e.g., MLP, deformation field) is not detailed in the source notes, but the field must support the standard NeRF input/output signature: $F: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}, t) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ [S1]. The method assumes the use of an occupancy grid $G$ for efficient sampling, with a transition function $P(G_k | G_{k-1})$ to update the grid structure [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The training process is formulated as estimating hidden states in a Hidden Markov Model (HMM), where radiance fields $F$ are hidden states and images $C$ are observations [S1].

1.  **Initialization:** Start with an initial radiance field $F_0$ (or empty state) at $t_0$.
2.  **Iterate for each time step $k = 1, \dots, K$:**
    a.  **Markov Assumption:** Simplify the probability model by assuming conditional independence: $F_k \perp \!\!\! \perp \{C_{0:k-1}, F_{0:k-2}\} \mid \{C_k, F_{k-1}\}$ [S1]. This reduces the estimation to $F_k = \arg\max_{F_k} P(F_k \mid C_k, F_{k-1})$ [S1].
    b.  **Optimization Objective:** Find $F_k$ that minimizes the rendering loss between the rendered image $\hat{C}_k$ and the observed image $C_k$ [S1]:
        $$ F_k = \arg\min_{F_k} \sum_{r \in R} ||\hat{C}_k(r) - C_k(r)||^2_2 $$
        where $R$ is the set of rays [S1].
    c.  **Volume Rendering:** For each ray $r$, compute the rendered color $\hat{C}_k(r)$ by aggregating $N$ points sampled along the ray with interval $\delta_i$:
        $$ \hat{C}(r) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} T_i (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) c_i $$
        where the accumulated transmittance $T_i$ is:
        $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
        Here, $\sigma_i$ and $c_i$ are the density and color predicted by $F_k$ at the $i$-th point [S1].
    d.  **State Transition:** Update the radiance field $F_{k-1}$ to $F_k$ using gradient descent on the loss. The source notes mention a transition function for the occupancy grid $G$ to maintain sampling efficiency, but do not provide the specific update rule for the neural weights [S1].
    e.  **Streaming:** The resulting $F_k$ can immediately render novel views at time $t_k$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Number of samples $N$:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Ray interval $\delta$:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Optimization iterations:** The method requires "minimum optimization iterations per frame" to achieve interactive speed, but the exact number is not specified [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Scalability:** Estimating $F_k$ conditioned on all previous images $C_{0:k}$ is not scalable as $t_k$ increases. The Markov assumption is critical to mitigate this complexity growth [S1].
*   **Camera Setup:** The method is limited to dynamic scenes captured by multi-view forward-facing cameras. It does not support monocular or 360-degree inward-facing setups in its current formulation [S1].

## Subtopics

### Neural Transient Field (NeTF)
Focuses on capturing transient effects (like smoke or fire) using a separate transient field, whereas OD-NeRF focuses on streaming general dynamic scenes via on-the-fly optimization [4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md].

### Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF
Uses projected color guidance to constrain the dynamic reconstruction, potentially offering different stability or accuracy trade-offs compared to the direct on-the-fly optimization of OD-NeRF [7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md].

### D-NeRF
Reconstructs dynamic scenes using a space-time 4D representation or explicit deformation fields trained on full video sequences, rather than streaming them frame-by-frame [8660-d-nerf.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Designed for "interactive speed" by minimizing optimization iterations per frame [S1]. The complexity is linear with respect to the number of frames processed, as it avoids re-optimizing the entire history.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the neural network size and the occupancy grid $G$. The Markov assumption prevents memory growth proportional to the number of past frames $C_{0:k}$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables real-time streaming of dynamic scenes; suitable for social media, VR/AR, and gaming applications [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Limited to multi-view forward-facing cameras; requires careful tuning of optimization steps to balance speed and quality; not suitable for monocular or complex multi-camera inward-facing setups [S1].
*   **When to use:** When the goal is to stream novel views of a dynamic event as it happens, rather than reconstructing a 4D volume after the event.

## Background
On-the-fly dynamic NeRF training was introduced in the paper "OD-NeRF: Efficient Training of On-the-Fly Dynamic Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It addresses the limitation of existing dynamic NeRFs, which typically reconstruct 4D space-time from full video sequences and cannot stream the event while it is happening [S1]. The method leverages recent successes in NeRF acceleration to make on-the-fly training feasible at interactive speeds [S1].
