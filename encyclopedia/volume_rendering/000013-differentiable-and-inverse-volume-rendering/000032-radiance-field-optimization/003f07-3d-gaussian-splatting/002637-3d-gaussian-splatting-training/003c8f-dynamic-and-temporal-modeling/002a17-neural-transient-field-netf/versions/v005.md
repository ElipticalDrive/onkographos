# Neural Transient Field (NeTF)


## Overview
Neural Transient Field (NeTF) is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers a 5D transient function (spatial location, direction, and time) implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1]. Unlike NeRF, which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates [S1]. It is distinguished by its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling, and a two-stage training strategy to address sampling bias [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Sparse measurements of light transport on a relay wall (SPAD spots), characterized by spatial position on the wall, viewing direction, and time-of-flight.
*   **Output:** A 3D density volume implicitly represented by the MLP, which can be mapped to a surface mesh via thresholding and Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron that takes 5D input coordinates (3D spatial position $\mathbf{x}$, 2D viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ on the sphere, and time $t$) and outputs density $\sigma$ and albedo/color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1]. The exact network architecture (layer sizes, activation functions) is not specified in the provided sources [G: Standard MLP structure with ReLU activations is assumed for implementation].
*   **Spherical Coordinates:** Input positions are mapped to spherical coordinates to define the wavefront parametrization [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the MLP parameters.
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points from the sparse NLOS measurements. Due to the sparsity and unbalanced nature of the data, standard sampling may lead to artifacts [S1].
3.  **MCMC Sampling:** Use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to account for sparse and unbalanced sampling in the transient field. This enables more reliable volume density estimation and accurate shape estimation by recovering details caused by occlusions and non-uniform albedo [S1].
4.  **Training:** Train the MLP to minimize the loss between predicted transients and measured transients at the sampling spots [S1].
5.  **Two-Stage Training (Optional but recommended for bias):**
    *   **Stage 1:** Conduct training using all initial samples to obtain an initial reconstruction [S1].
    *   **Loss Calculation:** Calculate the loss function between predicted and measured transients at every measuring spot on the relay wall [S1].
    *   **PDF Formation:** Normalize the calculated loss to form a probability density function (PDF) [S1].
    *   **Resampling:** Resample detection spots using the PDF: higher loss corresponds to higher PDF and is more densely sampled [S1].
    *   **Stage 2:** Build a new training dataset from the resampled spots and retrain the network to refine reconstruction [S1].
6.  **Reconstruction:** Extract the 3D density volume from the trained MLP. Map the volume to surfaces by thresholding the density values followed by Marching Cubes to generate the mesh [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   The NLOS process is formulated under spherical coordinates [S1].
*   The loss function $L$ is calculated between predicted transients $\hat{T}$ and measured transients $T$ at each spot $i$:
    $$ L = \sum_{i} || \hat{T}_i - T_i ||^2 $$
    (Note: The exact loss formulation is not explicitly detailed in the sources, but standard L2 loss is implied by "loss function between predicted and measured transients" [S1].)
*   The PDF for resampling is derived by normalizing the loss $L_i$ at each spot $i$:
    $$ P(i) = \frac{L_i}{\sum_{j} L_j} $$
    [S1]

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Resolution:** Tested on 256x256, 16x16, 8x8, and 4x4 spots on the relay wall [S1].
*   **Thresholding:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion:** Occluded points may be captured from a very small number of viewpoints, leading to strong reconstruction artifacts if not handled properly [S1]. MCMC and two-stage training help mitigate this [S1].
*   **Confocal vs. Non-Confocal:** The current form of NeTF does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups [S1].
*   **Noise:** While NeTF produces lower noise than SOTA methods, brute-force Marching Cubes on the implicit volume may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].

## Subtopics

### Two-stage Training
A training strategy to address sampling bias (undersampling) in NLOS reconstruction by resampling detection spots based on reconstruction loss [S1]. It involves an initial training stage followed by loss-based resampling and a second training stage [S1]. This method recovers fine details (e.g., bunny ears) that are missing in one-stage training and prior art [S1]. It is chosen when the NLOS data is sparse and unevenly distributed, leading to undersampling of certain regions [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1]. The complexity is driven by the MLP training and the MCMC sampling process [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Confocal Bunny Dataset:** NeTF achieved an MAE of 2.65 cm, compared to 2.38 cm for DLCT and 2.43 cm for F-K [S1].
    *   **Non-Confocal Bunny Dataset:** NeTF achieved an MAE of 7.45 cm, compared to 3.77 cm for FBP and 7.02 cm for BP [S1].
    *   **Non-Confocal Z Dataset:** NeTF achieved an MAE of 0.60 cm, compared to 0.46 cm for FBP [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Produces sharper edges for both front and back planes in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field and DLCT/F-K [S1].
    *   Manages to separate different parts of a mesh, whereas DLCT exhibits adhesion between different parts [S1].
    *   Incurs much lower noise than SOTA methods, similar to NeRF [S1].
    *   Robustly handles silhouettes and semi-occlusions [S1].
    *   Performs reasonably well even with very sparse sampling (e.g., 8x8 spots) [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Computationally expensive [S1].
    *   Does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups [S1].
    *   Brute-force Marching Cubes may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality reconstruction with low noise and robust handling of occlusions is required, and computational resources are sufficient.
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time performance is critical or when the setup is strictly confocal/non-confocal and specialized algorithms are available.

## Background
NeTF was introduced as a novel neural modeling framework for NLOS imaging [S1]. It draws inspiration from Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), adapting the concept of implicit neural representation to the 5D transient function in NLOS [S1]. The method was tested on synthetic and real NLOS data, including the Bunny, Lucy, Indonesian, Bike, Letter Z, and semi-occluded scenes [S1]. The work highlights the potential of deep learning for inverse imaging problems, despite the computational costs [S1].
