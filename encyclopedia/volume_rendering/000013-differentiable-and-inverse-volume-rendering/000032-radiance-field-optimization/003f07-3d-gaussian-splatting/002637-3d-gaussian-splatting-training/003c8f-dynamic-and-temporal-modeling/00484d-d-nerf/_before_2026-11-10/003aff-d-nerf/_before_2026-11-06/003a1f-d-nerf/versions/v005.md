# D-NeRF

Also called: Deformable Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
D-NeRF solves the problem of novel view synthesis for dynamic scenes by decomposing the representation into a static canonical space and a learned deformation field [S1, S2]. Unlike methods that treat time as a direct input to a single radiance field or use implicit temporal correspondence, D-NeRF explicitly models non-rigid motion by mapping dynamic points to a canonical template [S1, S2]. This approach sets it apart from siblings like [Neural Transient Field](4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md) which models light transport over time-of-flight, and [Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF](7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md) which uses invariant features without explicit deformation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, and time $t$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
The method uses two Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs):
1.  **Deformation Network ($\mathcal{D}$):** Maps $(\mathbf{x}, t)$ to a displacement vector $\Delta \mathbf{x}$.
2.  **Canonical Radiance Field ($\mathcal{F}$):** Maps the deformed coordinate $\mathbf{x}'$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ to $(\sigma, \mathbf{c})$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Deformation:** For a sampled point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t$, compute the displacement $\Delta \mathbf{x}$ using the deformation network:
    $$ \Delta \mathbf{x} = \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}, t) $$
2.  **Canonical Mapping:** Transform the point to the canonical space $\mathbf{x}'$:
    $$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \Delta \mathbf{x} $$
3.  **Radiance Prediction:** Query the canonical radiance field with $\mathbf{x}'$ and $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$:
    $$ (\sigma, \mathbf{c}) = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{d}) $$
4.  **Rendering:** Integrate $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ along the ray using the standard volume rendering equation [S1, S2].

**Parameters:**
*   Network depth and width: Not given in the sources.
*   Time encoding: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Complex Motion:** The deformation field is difficult to learn when scene motions are complex, leading to blurry results similar to Neural Volumes [S1].
*   **Topology Changes:** The method assumes continuous deformation fields, making it difficult to simulate topological changes which require discontinuities [S2].
*   **Optical Effects:** The deformation process may fail to reconstruct optical effects caused by transparency or specularities when mapping from observation space to canonical space [S2].

## Subtopics

### Canonical Space with Deformation
The core strategy of D-NeRF. It maps dynamic points to a static template via learned displacement. Choose this when explicit motion modeling is required and the scene topology is stable. See [Dynamic and Temporal Modeling](7610-dynamic-and-temporal-modeling.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** Enables efficient learning of dynamic scenes by separating geometry (canonical) from motion (deformation) [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Struggles with complex non-rigid motions and topology changes due to the continuity constraint of the deformation field [S1, S2]. Performance degrades on scenes with significant non-rigid deformation compared to methods that do not rely on canonical space mapping [S1].
*   **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
D-NeRF was proposed by Park et al. in 2021 [S1, S2]. It is a baseline method in dynamic NeRF literature that introduced the canonical space decomposition paradigm [S1]. It treats time $t$ as an additional input to the deformation network, allowing the system to control both camera view and time variables for dynamic rendering [S2].
