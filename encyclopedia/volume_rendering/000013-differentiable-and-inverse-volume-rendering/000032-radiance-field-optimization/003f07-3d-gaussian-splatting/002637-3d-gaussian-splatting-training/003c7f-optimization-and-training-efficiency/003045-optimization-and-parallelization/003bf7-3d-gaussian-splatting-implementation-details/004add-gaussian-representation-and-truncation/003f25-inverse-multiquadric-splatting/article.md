# Inverse Multiquadric Splatting

Also called: 3DIQS, Inverse Multiquadric

## Overview
Inverse Multiquadric Splatting is a differentiable rendering technique that replaces the standard Gaussian radial basis function with an inverse multiquadric function to define the spatial decay of splats. It solves the problem of modeling long-tailed or heavy-tailed distributions in radiance fields, offering an alternative to the standard Gaussian or raised cosine splatting methods. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific algebraic form $1/(ellipse + 1)$ and the application of a fixed correction factor to the covariance matrix.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 2D conic matrix components $(a, b, c)$ representing $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}^{-1}$, pixel offset vector $d = (d.x, d.y)$, and Gaussian opacity $w$ (stored as `con_o.w`).
*   **Output:** Alpha value $\alpha$ for compositing, and gradients with respect to the 2D mean and conic matrix components for backpropagation.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the projected 2D Gaussian representation defined in [Gaussian Representation and Truncation](12855-gaussian-representation-and-truncation.md). The conic matrix is symmetric:
$$ \Sigma'_{2 \times 2}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix} $$
In the implementation, these are accessed as `con_o.x` ($a$), `con_o.y` ($b$), and `con_o.z` ($c$).

**Parameters:**
*   `correctionFactor`: A scalar multiplier applied to the covariance matrices. Value: $1.38$ [S1].
*   `ellipse_threshold_max`: The upper bound for culling. Value: $9.0$ [S1].
*   `alpha_max`: The maximum clamping value for alpha. Value: $0.99$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Covariance Correction:**
    Multiply the 3D covariance matrix $\Sigma$ and the pre-computed 2D covariance matrix $\Sigma'_{2 \times 2}$ by `correctionFactor` ($1.38$) before inverting to form the conic matrix [S1]. This step ensures the inverse multiquadric function matches the desired spatial extent.

2.  **Ellipse Calculation:**
    Compute the quadratic form (ellipse value) using the conic components and pixel offset:
    $$ \text{ellipse} = a \cdot d.x^2 + c \cdot d.y^2 + 2 \cdot b \cdot d.x \cdot d.y $$
    In code: `ellipse = con_o.x * d.x * d.x + con_o.z * d.y * d.y + 2.f * con_o.y * d.x * d.y` [S1].

3.  **Culling:**
    If $\text{ellipse} \le 0$ or $\text{ellipse} \ge 9.0$, skip the splat (continue) [S1]. This limits the computational domain to the significant region of the splat.

4.  **Forward Alpha Computation:**
    Compute the inverse quadric term $G$:
    $$ G = \frac{1}{\text{ellipse} + 1} $$
    Compute the final alpha:
    $$ \alpha = \min(0.99, w \cdot G) $$
    [S1].

5.  **Backward Gradient Computation:**
    Calculate gradients with respect to the inverse quadric term $G$:
    $$ \frac{\partial L}{\partial G} = w \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial \alpha} $$
    [S1].

    Compute the derivative of $G$ with respect to the ellipse components. Let $dL\_dG$ be $\frac{\partial L}{\partial G}$.
    $$ \frac{\partial G}{\partial \text{ellipse}} = \frac{-2}{(\text{ellipse} + 1)^2} $$
    [S1].

    The gradient with respect to the offset components $d.x$ and $d.y$ (which propagate to the 2D mean) is:
    $$ \frac{\partial G}{\partial d.x} = \frac{\partial G}{\partial \text{ellipse}} \cdot (a \cdot d.x + b \cdot d.y) $$
    $$ \frac{\partial G}{\partial d.y} = \frac{\partial G}{\partial \text{ellipse}} \cdot (b \cdot d.x + c \cdot d.y) $$
    [S1].

    The gradient with respect to the conic matrix components $(a, b, c)$ is derived from the chain rule. The source provides specific updates for the 2D covariance matrix gradients via the conic matrix, scaled by `correctionFactor` [S1].
