# 3D Gaussian Splatting Training

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting training in OpenSplat, 3DGS Learning, Gaussian Splatting Optimization, GaussianTrainer training procedure, Metal-accelerated 3DGS training, Msplat Training Loop & Configuration, OpenSplat Training Loop, OpenSplat optimization

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training is a non-deep learning optimization process that adjusts explicit 3D Gaussian parameters to minimize the difference between a rendered image and a ground truth image, using camera information and initial 3D Gaussian estimates [S3, unofficial]. Unlike NeRF-based methods that optimize implicit neural fields, this method optimizes a set of explicit primitives (Gaussians) via differentiable volume rendering [S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its siblings by relying on analytical gradients through the splatting rasterization pipeline rather than backpropagation through a neural network or standard ray-marching [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images with known camera poses (rotation $R_{cw}$, translation $t_{cw}$, intrinsic matrix $K$) and an initial set of 3D Gaussian primitives [S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, spherical harmonic coefficients) and a rendered scene representation [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Each 3D Gaussian $i$ is defined by the following parameters [S3, unofficial]:
*   $p_{w,i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: 3D center position in world coordinates.
*   $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$: Rotation represented as a quaternion.
*   $s_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale (non-uniform scaling along principal axes).
*   $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}$: Opacity.
*   $h_i$: Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients for color/directionality.

**Initialization:**
1.  **Geometry:** Centers $p_w$ and SH coefficients $h$ are initialized from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) photogrammetry points [S3, unofficial].
2.  **Attributes:** Rotation $q$, scale $s$, and opacity $\alpha$ are initialized as constants [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Camera Filtering:** Cameras are filtered by projecting sparse SfM points; a camera is kept only if at least one point is in front of the camera ($z > 0.01$) and within image bounds [S1, unofficial]. If no cameras see any points, training fails [S1, unofficial].

**Optimization Objective:**
The goal is to minimize a loss function $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to the parameters $p_w, q, s, h, \alpha$ [S3, unofficial]:
$$
\underset{p_w, q, s, h, \alpha}{\textrm{argmin}} \quad \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_1$ is the L1 loss, $\mathcal{L}_{D-SSIM}$ is the D-SSIM loss, and $\lambda$ is a weight (typically 0.2) [S3, unofficial].

**Forward Rendering (Image Generation):**
For a given ray, the color $\gamma_j$ of pixel $j$ is computed by alpha-blending $N$ projected Gaussians sorted by depth [S3, unofficial]:
$$
\gamma_j = \sum_{i \in N} \alpha'_{ij} c_i \tau_{ij}, \quad \tau_{ij} = \prod_{k=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_{kj})
$$
where $c_i$ is the color of Gaussian $i$, and $\alpha'_{ij}$ is the effective opacity [S3, unofficial]. The effective opacity is defined as:
$$
\alpha'_{ij} = g \alpha_i
$$
where $g = \exp(-0.5 \omega'_0 d_0^2 - 0.5 \omega'_2 d_1^2 - \omega'_1 d_0 d_1)$, $\omega'$ are the upper triangular elements of the inverse 2D covariance matrix, and $d$ are the elements of $u_i - x_j$ (difference between projected center and pixel coordinate) [S3, unofficial].

**Gradient Computation (Backpropagation):**
Gradients are computed using the chain rule through the 5 forward steps of image generation [S3, unofficial]. The Jacobians for each parameter group are:

1.  **Rotation ($q_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial q_i}
    $$
2.  **Scale ($s_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial \sigma_i} \frac{\partial \sigma_i}{\partial s_i}
    $$
3.  **Spherical Harmonics ($h_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial h_i}
    $$
4.  **Opacity ($\alpha_i$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \alpha_i} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \alpha_i}
    $$
5.  **Center ($p_{w,i}$):**
    $$
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{w,i}} = \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial u_i} \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial p_{w,i}} + \sum_j \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \gamma_j} \frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{ij}} \frac{\partial \alpha'_{ij}}{\partial \sigma'_i} \frac{\partial \sigma'_i}{\partial p_{c,i}} \frac{\partial p_{c,i}}{\partial p_{w,i}}
    $$

Key derivative terms [S3, unofficial]:
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial \alpha'_{i,j}} = \tau_{i,j}(c_i - \gamma_{i+1,j})$
*   $\frac{\partial \gamma_j}{\partial c_i} = \tau_{i,j}\alpha'_{i,j}$
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial \omega'_i} = [-0.5d_0^2, -d_0d_1, -0.5d_1^2]^T g$
*   $\frac{\partial g}{\partial u_i} = [-\omega'_0 d_0 - \omega'_1 d_1, -\omega'_2 d_1 - \omega'_1 d_0]^T g$

**Training Loop:**
The training loop manages camera sampling, resolution scheduling, and densification [S2, unofficial].

```python
# Initialization
device = select_device() # CUDA > MPS > CPU
model = Model(input_data, device)
cameras = filter_cameras(input_data) # Keep cameras seeing SfM points
cam_iter = InfiniteRandomIterator(cameras)
step = 1
if resume_path:
    step = model.load_ply(resume_path) + 1

# Main Loop
for step in range(step, num_iters + 1):
    # 1. Camera Selection
    cam = cam_iter.next()
    
    # 2. Image Pre-fetching (CPU/GPU overlap)
    for k in range(prefetch_target):
        next_cam = cam_iter.peek(k)
        ds_factor = model.get_downscale_factor(step + 1 + k)
        images.request(next_cam, ds_factor)
        
    # 3. Forward Pass
    current_ds = model.get_downscale_factor(step)
    rgb = model.forward(cam, step)
    
    # 4. Loss Computation
    frame = images.acquire(cam, current_ds)
    gt = frame.image
    mask = frame.mask
    loss = model.main_loss(rgb, gt, mask, ssim_weight)
    
    # 5. Backpropagation
    loss.backward()
    
    # 6. Post-Training Updates
    model.after_train(step) # Densification/Pruning
    model.optimizer_step(step)
    model.schedulers_step(step)
    
    # 7. Evaluation/Checkpointing (if applicable)
    if step % save_every == 0:
        model.save_checkpoint(step)
```

**Densification Strategy:**
Periodically (every `refine_every` steps, default 500), the algorithm performs adaptive density control [S3, unofficial]:
1.  Compute gradient magnitudes $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial u}$ for each Gaussian.
2.  For Gaussians with large gradients (indicating high error):
    *   If the Gaussian is small (screen size < `densify_size_thresh`, default 0.01), copy it.
    *   If the Gaussian is large (screen size > `split_screen_size`, default 0.05), split it into two.
3.  Prune Gaussians with low opacity or large screen size [S1, unofficial].
4.  Densification stops at `densify_until` (default `min(15000, num_iters / 2)`) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `num_iters`: Total steps (default 30,000) [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree`: Max SH degree (default 3) [S1, unofficial].
*   `sh_degree_interval`: Steps to increase SH degree (default 1,000) [S1, unofficial].
*   `resolution_schedule`: Steps between doubling resolution (default 3,000) [S1, unofficial].
*   `ssim_weight`: Weight for SSIM loss (default 0.2) [S1, unofficial].
*   `bg_color`: Background color (default magenta [0.613, 0.010, 0.398]) [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **No Cameras:** If all cameras are discarded (no SfM points visible), training throws an error [S1, unofficial].
*   **Arbitrary Poses:** Rendering with arbitrary poses requires a 16-element `cam_to_world` matrix; otherwise, it fails [S2, unofficial].
*   **Evaluation Mode:** Evaluation requires the dataset to be loaded with `eval_mode=True` [S2, unofficial].
*   **CPU Performance:** CPU execution is ~100x slower than GPU [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md)
Optimization of physical volume parameters for 3D Gaussians, including extinction coefficient ($\sigma_t$), albedo ($\omega$), Henyey-Greenstein scattering asymmetry ($g$), and multiple scattering octave weights ($w$).

### [3D Gaussian Splatting Training > Parameter-Specific Learning Rates](4276-3d-gaussian-splatting-training-parameter-specific-learning-r.md)
Configures an Adam optimizer with distinct learning rates for each Gaussian parameter group to stabilize training and prevent rapid divergence. It sets up the optimizer with a base learning rate of 0.0, relying entirely on per-group overrides defined in the parameter groups. This approach addresses the varying sensitivity of Gaussian attributes (position, scale, color, opacity) to gradient updates.

### [GES Optimization Strategy](4697-ges-optimization-strategy.md)
The GES Optimization Strategy is a training methodology for Generalized Exponential Splatting (GES) that controls shape density by selectively pruning splats based on shape attributes, thereby eliminating the need for a variable density mechanism. It distinguishes itself from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting training by optimizing the shape parameter $\beta$ alongside position, opacity, and covariance, using a frequency-modulated loss to enhance high-frequency details. It builds on [3D Gaussian Splatting Training](3761-3d-gaussian-splatting-training.md) but replaces its density-based densification/pruning with shape-based pruning.

### [WildGaussians](6064-wildgaussians.md)
WildGaussians is a method for "in-the-wild" 3D reconstruction that integrates robust DINO features and a lightweight appearance modeling module within the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework to handle unconstrained photo collections. It sets itself apart from standard 3DGS by explicitly addressing varying illumination, occlusions, and transient objects found in real-world data, while maintaining real-time rendering speeds.

### [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md)
This family encompasses the computational strategies for training 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, focusing on gradient computation, distributed memory management, and training pipeline efficiency. It solves the bottlenecks of memory consumption and training speed inherent in differentiable volume rendering of large-scale scenes. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by addressing the underlying computational graph execution (parallelization, distributed rendering) rather than just the optimization objectives or geometric constraints.

### [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md)
Densification strategies dynamically adjust the number and distribution of 3D Gaussian primitives $\mathcal{G}_i$ during the training of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to balance reconstruction fidelity against computational cost. They solve the problem of under-reconstruction in complex geometric or photometric regions (by adding primitives) and over-reconstruction or redundancy in simple regions (by removing or merging primitives), which static initialization cannot address. This family is distinguished from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by its focus on structural topology changes rather than attribute refinement, and from [Optimization and Parallelization](6233-optimization-and-parallelization.md) by its specific logic for primitive lifecycle management.

### [Regularization and Geometry Constraints](6269-regularization-and-geometry-constraints.md)
Regularization and Geometry Constraints in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training address the instability of photometric optimization by enforcing geometric consistency, spectral fidelity, and robustness to transient artifacts. Unlike standard training which minimizes pixel-wise color differences, this family introduces auxiliary losses and preprocessing steps to constrain the learned scene representation, preventing over-smoothing, smearing artifacts, and pose drift. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Physical Parameter Optimization](4111-physical-parameter-optimization.md) by focusing on structural and spectral priors rather than physical light transport models, and from [Densification Strategy](6234-densification-strategy.md) by refining existing Gaussians rather than spawning new ones.

### [Pose-free and Feed-forward Training](6500-pose-free-and-feed-forward-training.md)
This family addresses the dependency on Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipelines and iterative per-scene optimization in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting. It enables reconstruction from uncalibrated images either by bypassing pose estimation via external geometric priors (InstantSplat) or by predicting Gaussian parameters directly in a single forward pass (pixelSplat, AnySplat). Unlike iterative methods, these approaches offer real-time inference or significantly reduced training times by leveraging learned priors or dense correspondence networks.

### [SWAG](6875-swag.md)
SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders, yielding state-of-the-art results on diverse photo scenes. It distinguishes itself from siblings like Wild-GS by explicitly modeling transient elements as separate Gaussian entities rather than relying solely on depth regularization or visibility maps to mitigate their effects.

### [DNGaussian](7399-dngaussian.md)
DNGaussian is a depth-regularized framework for few-shot novel view synthesis that combines Hard and Soft Depth Regularization with Global-Local Depth Normalization to restore accurate scene geometry under coarse monocular depth supervision while preserving fine-grained color appearance. It sets itself apart from standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing spherical harmonics with a Neural Color Renderer to prevent overfitting on sparse views and by introducing explicit depth constraints that freeze specific Gaussian parameters to guide geometry reconstruction. Unlike NeRF-based methods that interpolate colors into invisible areas, DNGaussian exposes black backgrounds on empty spaces, which may lower PSNR/SSIM metrics for invisible regions but allows the model to focus on visible geometry.

### [Base 3DGS Optimization](7549-base-3dgs-optimization.md)
Base 3DGS Optimization initializes the geometry attributes of 3D Gaussian primitives to provide a stable foundation for subsequent shading attribute optimization in editable Gaussian splatting. It differs from standard 3DGS by incorporating an explicit normal attribute $n$ for each Gaussian and optimizing it via a normal consistency loss, while retaining spherical harmonics (SH) for color representation. This initialization accelerates the convergence of editable Gaussian optimization by ensuring accurate scene geometry representation before decomposing color and lighting.

### PixelSplat Training and Depth Regularization
Train the model for 300,000 steps using a batch size of 7 [S6]. Use an MSE loss for the first 150,000 iterations [S6]. Supplement the MSE loss with an LPIPS loss with weight 0.05 starting at 150,000 steps [S6]. For each batch element (scene), render 4 target views [S6]. Place 3 Gaussians along each ray to allow gradients for multiple estimated depths during the forward pass [S6]. Determine Gaussian positions by independently sampling from the ray's probability distribution 3 times [S6]. Divide each Gaussian's alpha value by 3 such that $\alpha = \phi_z / 3$, ensuring total opacity of roughly 1 for Gaussians on a ray [S6]. Fine-tune the model for 50,000 steps with a depth regularization loss $L_{reg\_depth}$ to generate point clouds [S6]. For each rendered view, generate a corresponding depth map $D$ [S6]. Compute horizontal depth difference: $D_x^{\nabla}[\nabla] = D[\nabla_{x-1}, \nabla_y] - 2D[\nabla_x, \nabla_y] + D[\nabla_{x+1}, \nabla_y]$, where $D$ is the depth map and $\nabla$ represents pixel coordinates $(u_x, u_y)$ [S6]. Compute vertical depth difference: $D_y^{\nabla}[\nabla] = D[\nabla_x, \nabla_{y-1}] - 2D[\nabla_x, \nabla_y] + D[\nabla_x, \nabla_{y+1}]$ [S6]. VRAM requirement: approximately 80 GB on a single GPU [S6].

### CLM
A method that removes the GPU memory barrier by moving Gaussian states between CPU and GPU memory during optimization, using a smart caching policy to keep the most frequently updated Gaussians on the GPU [S7, unofficial].

### GS-Net
A 3D neural network that predicts high-dimensional feature vectors for optimized 3D Gaussians, using Q-Render to efficiently render these features into 2D maps for distillation from 2D foundation models [S8, unofficial]. Given optimized 3D Gaussians $\mathcal{G} = \{g_i\}_{i=1}^N$, a 3D neural network predicts a set of $C$-dimensional Gaussian features $\mathcal{F} = \{f_i\}_{i=1}^N$ where $f_i \in \mathbb{R}^C$ [S8, unofficial]. Use Q-Render to transform predicted Gaussian features into rendered feature maps [S8, unofficial]. Train the 3D neural network to minimize the rendering loss between the rendered feature maps and target 2D feature maps extracted from CLIP’s vision encoder [S8, unofficial]. Voxelize 3D Gaussians by sampling their center location $\mu$ to ensure compatibility with typical 3D backbones like Point Transformer v3 (PTv3) and MinkUnet [S8, unofficial]. Perform de-voxelization steps to convert predicted voxel features into predicted Gaussian features $\mathcal{F}$ [S8, unofficial]. Unlike methods that force per-scene feature optimization, GS-Net predicts features in a generalizable manner using a 3D neural network [S8, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training typically runs for 30,000 iterations [S1, unofficial]. GPU acceleration is standard; CPU execution is ~100x slower [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage scales with the number of Gaussians and image resolution. High-resolution training or large scenes can require significant VRAM (e.g., ~80 GB for PixelSplat [S6]).
*   **Performance:** On modern GPUs (e.g., M4 Max), training can complete in ~90 seconds for specific configurations [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Real-time rendering capability post-training, explicit scene representation, fast convergence compared to NeRF [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires initial SfM reconstruction; sensitive to initialization quality; densification can be unstable in textureless regions [S5, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** High-quality novel view synthesis from calibrated image sets.
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with no SfM points, or when pose-free/uncalibrated input is required without additional priors [S5, unofficial].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting training was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the principle of optimizing explicit 3D Gaussian primitives to represent a scene, leveraging differentiable rasterization for fast training and rendering [S3, unofficial]. The method avoids the use of neural networks for the scene representation itself, relying instead on analytical gradients for optimization [S3, unofficial]. It has since spawned numerous variants focusing on densification, regularization, and generalization [S5, unofficial].
