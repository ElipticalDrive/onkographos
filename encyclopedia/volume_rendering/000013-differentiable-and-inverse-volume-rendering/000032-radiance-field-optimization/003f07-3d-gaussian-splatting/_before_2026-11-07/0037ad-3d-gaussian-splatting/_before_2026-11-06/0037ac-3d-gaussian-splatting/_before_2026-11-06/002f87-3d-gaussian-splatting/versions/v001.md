# 3D Gaussian Splatting

Also called: 3DGS, dGS Conditional Slicing, direct Gaussian Splatting conditional slicing

## Overview
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a scene representation method that models a radiance field as a collection of learnable 3D Gaussian primitives, optimizing their parameters via differentiable volume rendering to synthesize novel views [S6]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Neural Radiance Fields](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) and [Plenoxels](5942-plenoxels.md) by using explicit, analytically integrable Gaussian kernels instead of implicit neural networks or voxel grids, enabling real-time rendering speeds and high-fidelity reconstruction [S6].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core objective of minimizing a photometric loss $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground-truth color $C(\mathbf{r})$ for a batch of rays $\mathcal{R}$:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \right\|_2^2
$$

The optimization loop generally follows these steps:
1.  **Ray Sampling**: Random pixels are selected from training images. Ray origins and directions are computed using camera intrinsics ($K$) and extrinsics ($c2w$).
2.  **Forward Pass**: Points are sampled along rays within near/far bounds. The field model predicts density ($\sigma$) and color ($c$). Differentiable volume rendering computes the final pixel color.
3.  **Loss Computation**: The Mean Squared Error (MSE) is calculated. Additional terms (e.g., Eikonal, TV) may be added.
4.  **Backpropagation**: Gradients are computed and weights are updated via an optimizer (typically Adam).
5.  **Scheduling**: Learning rates and other hyperparameters are adjusted based on the current step or epoch.

## Subtopics

### [3DGS Initialization Policy](1398-3dgs-initialization-policy.md)
Defines the standard procedure for seeding 3D Gaussian primitives from sparse input data (SfM points) with specific opacity and scale properties, differing from siblings like [Volumetric Gaussian Splatting (AVIS)](1875-volumetric-gaussian-splatting-avis.md) by using a uniform opacity initialization derived from a power-law function of the total Gaussian count.

### [CoR-GS](1486-cor-gs.md)
Improves sparse-view reconstruction by training two independent Gaussian fields and discarding inaccurate Gaussians based on consistency checks, addressing ambiguity in minimal view overlap scenarios.

### [Gaussian Splatting Volume Rendering](2063-gaussian-splatting-volume-rendering.md)
The differentiable rendering technique that projects 3D Gaussians to 2D screen space and alpha-composites them, capturing view-dependent effects via spherical harmonics.

### [GaussianSet Data Structure](2074-gaussianset-data-structure.md)
The container class storing raw learnable parameters (means, quaternions, scales, opacities, SH coefficients) and computing world-space covariance matrices on demand.

### [Optical Flow Loss for 3D Gaussian Reconstruction](3303-optical-flow-loss-for-3d-gaussian-reconstruction.md)
Incorporates optical flow as a loss term to improve 3D shape reconstruction of static objects from video, resolving concavity/convexity ambiguities at the cost of slight color fidelity degradation.

### [EffGS](3365-effgs.md)
An acceleration framework combining frequency-aware guidance, localized density control, and adaptive primitive scale modulation to maintain high-fidelity reconstruction in complex environments.

### [Rendering and Lighting Pipelines](4135-rendering-and-lighting-pipelines.md)
Defines the differentiable rendering pipelines that convert 3D Gaussian primitives into 2D images using alpha blending or weighted-sum compositing, often augmented with volume-based occlusion.

### [MaskGaussian](4242-maskgaussian.md)
A differentiable pruning method that models Gaussians as probabilistic entities, using masked-rasterization to allow gradients to flow to unsampled (masked-out) Gaussians.

### [Radiative and Tomographic Gaussians](4971-radiative-and-tomographic-gaussians.md)
Adapts 3DGS for X-ray imaging by replacing view-dependent color/opacity with a central density or radiative intensity parameter, enabling 3D density volume retrieval [3040-radiative-gaussian-splatting.md].

### [Density Control and Densification](5337-density-control-and-densification.md)
Manages the population of 3D Gaussian primitives during optimization by dynamically adding, splitting, or removing Gaussians based on gradients and opacity thresholds.

### [Domain-Specific Adaptations](5621-domain-specific-adaptations.md)
Modifies the standard framework for non-standard inputs (e.g., CT/MR volumes) by replacing photometric supervision with voxel-space losses and specialized initialization.

### [Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting](5703-dynamic-3d-gaussian-splatting.md)
Tracks time-varying Gaussian parameters $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t, q_t)$ using local-rigid and ARAP/isometry constraints.

### [Inverse Rendering with Gaussians (GS-IR / GIR)](6153-inverse-rendering-with-gaussians-gs-ir-gir.md)
Uses split-sum approximation to handle indirect light in inverse rendering tasks.

Defines the mathematical shape (mean and covariance) of the Gaussian basis functions, allowing for analytic integration during volume rendering.

### Gaussian Opacity Fields (GOF) Mesh Extraction
A method to extract a 3-D triangulated mesh from a trained Gaussian model without retraining, by building a volumetric density field and extracting an isosurface [S2, unofficial]. It builds a volumetric density field over a voxel grid by splatting each Gaussian with its full anisotropic covariance, truncated at $3\sigma$, defined as:
$$ f(x) = \sum_i \alpha_i \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} (x-\mu_i)^\top \Sigma_i^{-1} (x-\mu_i)\right) $$
The isosurface is extracted using the marching cubes algorithm (specifically `skimage.measure.marching_cubes`), and per-vertex colors are assigned from the DC spherical-harmonics component of the nearest Gaussian via KD-tree lookup [S2, unofficial]. Default voxel grid resolution is 256, and the density threshold is 1.0 [S2, unofficial].

### Substitutive LOD with K-wise Moment-Matched Merging
A substitutive level-of-detail technique that reduces splat count by merging $K$ splats using moment-matching with L2-optimal amplitude, guided by cost metrics [S3, unofficial]. It applies one of three partitioning algorithms: greedy hierarchical (à la Runnalls), cost-increment relocation, or joint relaxation [S3, unofficial]. Cost metrics include L2, Cauchy–Schwarz, gKL, W2, MMD, and L$\infty$ [S3, unofficial]. Cost-increment relocation matches greedy merging within 5.5% at compression factors of four and above, and cost-aware merging beats mass-ranked culling with and without amplitude refit [S3, unofficial]. Spectral lower bounds have explicit limits and counterexamples where they fail to accurately predict merging quality [S3, unofficial].

### Gaussian Conditional Probability Formulation
Treats the unnormalized 4D Gaussian as a special probability distribution over spatial coordinates $(u, v)$ and time $t$, enabling factorization into temporal and conditional spatial components [S4, unofficial]. The joint distribution factorizes as $p(u, v, t) = p(t)p(u, v|t)$ [S4, unofficial]. The joint probability density is $p(u, v, t) = (2\pi)^{-3/2} \det(\Sigma)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v, t|\mu, \Sigma)$ [S4, unofficial]. The temporal marginal is $p(t) = (2\pi)^{-1/2} \det(\Sigma_t)^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(t|\mu_t, \Sigma_t)$, and the conditional spatial is $p(u, v|t) = (2\pi)^{-1} \det(\Sigma_{uv|t})^{-1/2} \mathcal{N}(u, v | \mu_{uv|t}, \Sigma_{uv|t})$ [S4, unofficial]. The conditional covariance is the Schur complement: $\Sigma_{uv|t} = \Sigma_{uv} - \Sigma_{uv,t}\Sigma_t^{-1}\Sigma_{t,uv}$ [S4, unofficial]. The matrix factorization is:
$$ \Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} I & -\Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma_{uv} - \Sigma_{uv,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ -\Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{t,uv} & I \end{bmatrix} $$
[S4, unofficial].

### SuGaR
Post-hoc surface alignment and Poisson meshing [S5, unofficial]. It uses a penalty term to make Gaussians flat and aligned, then performs Poisson reconstruction on the samples [S5, unofficial].

### Epipolar 3D Gaussian Splatting
Incorporates an epipolar transformer to generalize features between two views, pairing this with a probabilistic prediction of Gaussians aligned along camera-to-pixel rays [S6].

### 3D Gaussian Splatting with Transformer-based Parameterization
Tokenizes two to four images and their camera parameters, concatenates them, and passes them through a transformer block whose output tokens are decoded into Gaussian parameters [S6].

### Volume Encoding Gaussians
Adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting for volume data visualization by removing color and opacity attributes for each Gaussian, training only scalar values and enabling interactive transfer function application post-training [S7, unofficial].

### SAGA
A 2D promptable segmentation method lifted to 3D [S5, unofficial].

### Negative Component Splatting
A technique using negative density components in a mixture model to enhance representational power and parameter efficiency, allowing subtraction of color/density to capture complex topologies (e.g., holes) with fewer components [S8]. The rendering equation uses signed weights $w \in \mathbb{R}$ (instead of $w > 0$) in the standard alpha-compositing sum:
$$ C(u) = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i o_i T_i^{2D}(u) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - o_j T_j^{2D}(u)) $$
where $o_i$ is opacity and $T_i^{2D}$ is the 2D component function [S8]. Opacity $o$ is constrained by a tanh function during learning so that $o \in [-1, 1]$, allowing positive and negative components to dynamically change signs while being bounded [S8]. Using squared negative components introduces pairwise interactions between all $K$ components, increasing evaluation complexity to $O(n^2)$, which is significantly slower; therefore, the linear sum with signed weights is used instead [S8].

## Background
3D Gaussian Splatting was introduced by Kerbl et al. in 2023 [S6]. It emerged as a response to the computational bottlenecks of neural radiance fields (NeRFs), offering a way to achieve real-time rendering speeds while maintaining high visual fidelity. The method has since become a foundational technique in differentiable rendering, with numerous extensions addressing dynamic scenes, sparse views, inverse rendering, and domain-specific applications like medical imaging and microscopy [S6].
