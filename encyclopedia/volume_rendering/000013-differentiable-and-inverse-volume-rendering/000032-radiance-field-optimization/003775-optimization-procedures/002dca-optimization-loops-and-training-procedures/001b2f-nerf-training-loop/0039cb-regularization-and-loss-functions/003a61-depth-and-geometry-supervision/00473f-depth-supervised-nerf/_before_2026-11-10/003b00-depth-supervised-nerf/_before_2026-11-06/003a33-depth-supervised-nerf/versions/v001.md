# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF, Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) Training with Keypoint Supervision, Depth-Supervised NeRF Training, NeRF with Keypoint Depth Supervision

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality in sparse-view settings by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints generated during Structure-from-Motion (SfM) preprocessing [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF imposes a loss that forces the rendered ray termination distribution to match the surface priors provided by these keypoints, effectively enforcing that geometry consists of empty space and opaque surfaces [S1]. This approach is complementary to standard NeRF and can be combined with other optimization procedures to reduce overfitting and speed up training by 2-3x [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Input images $I_j$, camera poses $P_j$ (estimated via SfM), sparse 3D keypoints $X$ (estimated via SfM), and near/far clipping bounds $t_n, t_f$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized NeRF model parameters (density $\sigma$ and color $c$).

**Data Structures**
*   **Keypoints:** A set $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that camera.
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each visible keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$.

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera parameters $P_j$ and sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Keypoint Depth:** For each image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each $x_i \in X_j$, project $x_i$ using $P_j$ and take the re-projected z-value as the estimated depth $D_{ij}$ [S1].
3.  **Compute Depth Uncertainty:** Calculate the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
4.  **Define Ray Termination Distribution:** The NeRF renders a ray termination distribution $h_{ij}(t)$, defined as:
    $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left[-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right]\sigma(t) $$
    where $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density at distance $t$ [S1]. The density $\sigma(s)$ must be real-valued and non-negative (e.g., via ReLU or softplus) [S1]. The scene is bounded by $t_n$ and $t_f$, with $t_f$ treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$, making $h(t)$ a valid probability distribution [S1].
5.  **Model True Depth:** Model the true depth of the first surface as a random variable $D_{ij}$ normally distributed around the estimated depth:
    $$ D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i) $$
    [S1].
6.  **Compute Depth Loss:** Minimize the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $\mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$. This is approximated by the following loss term for each keypoint ray:
    $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\log h(t) \exp\left(-\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2}\right) \right] \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\sum_k h_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2}\right) \Delta t_k \right] $$
    where $h_k$ is the discretized weight at sample $t_k$ [S1]. Alternatively, a simple MSE loss can be used, though KL divergence yields slightly improved PSNR [S2, unofficial].
7.  **Generate Keypoint Rays:** For each training iteration, allocate a portion of the total training rays to keypoint rays. Calculate ray origins in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of detected keypoints, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
8.  **Simultaneous Supervision:** Shoot these rays into the scene. Exploit the continuity of image coordinates to interpolate RGB supervision values for the rays corresponding to detected keypoints. Simultaneously supervise RGB (using standard color loss) and depth (using $L_{Depth}$) for these rays within a single ray marching procedure [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def compute_depth_loss(h, t_samples, D_ij, sigma_hat):
    """
    Compute the depth supervision loss for a batch of keypoint rays.
    
    Args:
        h: Tensor of shape (batch_size, num_samples), the ray termination distribution weights h(t).
           Sum of h along dim=1 should be 1.
        t_samples: Tensor of shape (num_samples,), the distances t_k of the samples.
        D_ij: Tensor of shape (batch_size,), the estimated depth for each keypoint ray.
        sigma_hat: Tensor of shape (batch_size,), the depth uncertainty for each keypoint ray.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar, the mean depth loss.
    """
    # Calculate the Gaussian likelihood of the depth estimate given the sample distances
    # exp(-(t - D)^2 / (2 * sigma^2))
    exponent = -((t_samples.unsqueeze(0) - D_ij.unsqueeze(1)) ** 2) / (2 * sigma_hat.unsqueeze(1) ** 2)
    
    # Clip exponent to avoid numerical instability in exp
    exponent = torch.clamp(exponent, min=-100.0)
    
    # Compute the weighted sum: sum_k h_k * exp(...)
    # h is (batch, samples), exponent is (batch, samples)
    weighted_likelihood = torch.sum(h * torch.exp(exponent), dim=1)
    
    # Loss is -log(weighted_likelihood)
    # Add small epsilon for numerical stability in log
    loss_per_ray = -torch.log(weighted_likelihood + 1e-8)
    
    return torch.mean(loss_per_ray)

def train_step_neRF_with_depth(model, rays, images, keypoints_data):
    """
    Single training step combining color and depth supervision.
    
    Args:
        model: The NeRF model.
        rays: Standard training rays (origins, directions).
        images: Ground truth RGB images for the rays' camera views.
        keypoints_data: Dictionary containing:
            - 'rays': Tensor of shape (K, 3), origins of keypoint rays.
            - 'dirs': Tensor of shape (K, 3), directions of keypoint rays.
            - 'D_ij': Tensor of shape (K,), estimated depths.
            - 'sigma_hat': Tensor of shape (K,), depth uncertainties.
            - 'rgb_targets': Tensor of shape (K, 3), interpolated RGB targets.
            
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar loss.
    """
    # 1. Render standard rays for color supervision
    rgb_std, h_std, t_std = model.render(rays.origins, rays.directions)
    color_loss = F.mse_loss(rgb_std, images)
    
    # 2. Render keypoint rays for depth and color supervision
    rgb_kp, h_kp, t_kp = model.render(keypoints_data['rays'], keypoints_data['dirs'])
    
    # Color loss for keypoint rays (using interpolated targets)
    color_loss_kp = F.mse_loss(rgb_kp, keypoints_data['rgb_targets'])
    
    # Depth loss for keypoint rays
    # h_kp is (K, num_samples), t_kp is (num_samples,)
    # Note: t_kp might need to be broadcasted or handled carefully depending on implementation
    # Assuming t_kp is shared across the batch for the sampling strategy
    depth_loss = compute_depth_loss(h_kp, t_kp, keypoints_data['D_ij'], keypoints_data['sigma_hat'])
    
    # 3. Combine losses
    # Weights can be tuned; standard NeRF uses color loss primarily.
    # DS-NeRF paper suggests combining them.
    total_loss = color_loss + color_loss_kp + depth_loss
    
    return total_loss
```

**Parameters**
*   **Near/Far Bounds ($t_n, t_f$):** Define the scene volume. $t_f$ is treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   **Ray Allocation:** A portion of the total training rays is dedicated to keypoint rays [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** Typically $O(10k - 100k)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **SfM Configuration:** COLMAP is run with default settings on the limited training views [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint location uncertainty, which may not hold for depth derived from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Dense Depth:** Dense MVS depth is used only for evaluation, not during training or testing [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are reduced by 2-3x compared to standard NeRF to reach the same PSNR [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as it reuses SfM outputs and interpolates RGB for keypoint rays [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On a single RTX A5000 GPU, a DS-NeRF training iteration takes ~362.4 ms, compared to ~359.8 ms for standard NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified at lower view counts. With 2 views, NeRF fails to match DS-NeRF's performance [S1]. With 5 views, PSNR improves from 13.5 (NeRF) to 20.2 (DS-NeRF) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with fewer views, requires no additional training data or domain-specific priors [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the success of SfM; poor SfM leads to failure. Assumes Gaussian depth uncertainty [S1].

## Background
DS-NeRF was introduced to address the failure mode of NeRF fitting incorrect geometries when given an insufficient number of input views [S1]. It leverages the "free" depth supervision provided by sparse 3D point clouds computed during standard SfM pre-processing steps (e.g., COLMAP) [S1]. The method was demonstrated on datasets including DTU, Redwood, and NeRF Real with 2, 5, or 10 input views [S1]. It is compatible with other NeRF methods and can use depth from scanned depth sensors and RGB-D reconstruction outputs in addition to SfM keypoints [S1].
