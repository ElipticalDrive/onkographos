# Regularization and Loss Functions


## Overview
This family defines the objective functions and constraint mechanisms applied during the optimization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to mitigate under-constrained artifacts, such as "floaters" or image-plane collapse, particularly in few-view or sparse-data scenarios. Unlike sibling methods that focus on sampling strategies or architectural growth, these methods modify the gradient signal or loss landscape. They are distinguished from [DiffNeRF](8572-diffnerf.md) by their approach to gradient computation (finite differences vs. automatic differentiation) and from [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md) by their reliance on external priors (diffusion models, normalizing flows) versus internal ray consistency.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family augment the standard photometric reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ with additional terms $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$ to constrain the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields. The total loss is generally formulated as:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$
where $\lambda_k$ are hyperparameters balancing the regularization strength. The photometric loss is typically the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between rendered colors $\mathbf{C}$ and ground truth images $I$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = || \mathbf{C} - I ||_2^2 $$
The regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ vary by child method but share the goal of enforcing geometric plausibility or statistical priors on the rendered outputs.

## Subtopics

### [Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md)
Addresses severe under-constraint problems in sparse-view NeRFs by regularizing geometry via a Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM). It distinguishes itself by backpropagating the DDM's score function (gradient of the log-probability of RGBD patches) directly to the NeRF parameters, rather than using normalizing flows. It uses patch-based rendering (e.g., $48 \times 48$) and an early training guard where 25% of RGB values are replaced with ground truth for the first 2500 steps.

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
Prevents degenerate solutions (e.g., memorizing training images) in few-view settings using internal ray consistency. It augments photometric loss with three geometric constraints: foreground completeness (sum of weights $\approx 1$), frustum consistency (penalizing density in only one view's frustum), and depth distribution compactness. Unlike diffusion methods, it requires no external models.

### [RegNeRF](8582-regnerf.md)
Improves view synthesis in sparse/inconsistent data by introducing priors via a normalizing flow model and a smoothness prior. It distinguishes itself from [DiffNeRF](8572-diffnerf.md) by approximating gradients via finite differences on patch-based dataloaders rather than automatic differentiation. It constrains the distribution of NeRF outputs to match natural image statistics.

### [Occlusion-Eliminating NeRF Variant](8653-occlusion-eliminating-nerf-variant.md)
Addresses foreground occlusion degradation by decomposing the scene into two MLPs: a Scene MLP for primary subjects and a Background MLP for occluders/background. It uses a cost volume loss and depth bidirectional inconsistency priors to jointly optimize geometry and camera parameters, specifically targeting the restoration of occlusion-free backgrounds.

### AligNeRF Training Objective
Replaces standard per-pixel MSE with a combination of patch-based MSE (for misalignment) and shallow VGG feature-space loss (for high-frequency detail). It uses a weight $w=0.05$ for the high-frequency loss.

## Background
The problem of "floaters" and overfitting in NeRFs, particularly with sparse inputs, was identified early in the field. Regularization techniques evolved from simple smoothness priors [S1] to complex generative priors. RegNeRF introduced normalizing flows [S1]. Diffusion-regularized methods leverage pre-trained DDMs on synthetic data (e.g., Hypersim) to guide optimization [S1]. Geometric baselines focus on intrinsic ray properties [S1]. AligNeRF addresses misalignment artifacts in outdoor scenes [S1].
