# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure for Neural Radiance Fields that incorporates sparse 3D depth supervision from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) keypoints to accelerate convergence and improve quality with fewer input views [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting or failing to converge when given an insufficient number of training images by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution matches the surface priors provided by the sparse point cloud [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires no additional training or category-specific assumptions, making it a flexible, "free" supervisory signal compatible with any NeRF variant [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $I_j$ with known camera poses $P_j$ (estimated via SfM), and the corresponding sparse 3D keypoints $X$ with visibility flags $X_j \subset X$ for each image [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields) and rendered novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set of 3D points $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   **Depth Uncertainty:** For each keypoint $x_i$, a scalar $\hat{\sigma}_i$ representing the average reprojection error across all views where $x_i$ was detected [S1].
*   **Projected Depth:** For a keypoint $x_i$ visible in camera $j$, the depth $D_{ij}$ is the z-coordinate of $x_i$ projected into the camera frame of $P_j$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera parameters and generate sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth Estimation:** For each training image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each $x_i \in X_j$, compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
3.  **Uncertainty Calculation:** Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
4.  **Ray Allocation:** For each training iteration, allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
5.  **Keypoint Ray Generation:** For each allocated keypoint ray corresponding to image $I_j$ and keypoint $x_i$:
    *   Compute the ray origin in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of $x_i$, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
    *   Shoot this ray into the scene using the standard NeRF sampling procedure (sample points $\mathbf{x}_k$ along the ray) [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Render the color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}$ and depth $\hat{D}$ for the ray using volume rendering [S4]. The depth is computed as:
    $$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m-1} w_j s_j $$
    where $w_j = (1 - \exp(-\sigma(\mathbf{x}_j) \Delta s_j)) \exp(-\sum_{k=1}^{j-1} \sigma(\mathbf{x}_k) \Delta s_k)$ and $\Delta s_j = s_{j+1} - s_j$ [S4].
7.  **Color Loss:** Compute the photometric loss for the ray:
    $$ L_{Color} = || \widehat{\mathcal{C}} - \mathbf{I}[u, v] ||_2^2 $$
    where $(u, v)$ are the pixel coordinates of the keypoint [S4].
8.  **Depth Loss:** Compute the depth loss $L_{Depth}$ by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution $h(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution $N(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1].
    *   The ray termination distribution is defined as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp[-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds]\sigma(t)$ [S1].
    *   The loss is approximated over the sampled points $t_k$ along the ray:
        $$ L_{Depth} \approx \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
        where $h_k = h(t_k)$ and $\Delta t_k$ is the step size [S1].
    *   *Note:* An alternative MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ can be used, but KL divergence yields fewer artifacts [S1][S3, unofficial].
9.  **Total Loss:** Combine the losses:
    $$ L = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth} $$
    where $\lambda_D$ is a hyperparameter (typically 1) [S1, S4].
10. **Optimization:** Backpropagate the gradients through the volume rendering process to update the NeRF parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def train_ds_nerf(images, camera_poses, keypoints, uncertainties, num_iterations, lambda_d=1.0):
    """
    images: List of ground truth images I_j
    camera_poses: List of camera projection matrices P_j
    keypoints: List of 3D points X
    uncertainties: List of scalar uncertainties sigma_hat_i for each keypoint
    num_iterations: Number of training steps
    lambda_d: Weight for depth loss
    """
    nerf_model = initialize_nerf()
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Select a batch of rays
        # Allocate a portion of rays to keypoint rays
        batch_rays = []
        keypoint_rays = []
        
        # Standard rays for color supervision
        standard_rays = generate_random_rays(images, camera_poses)
        
        # Keypoint rays for depth supervision
        # Select a subset of keypoints visible in current batch images
        for j, (img, pose) in enumerate(zip(images, camera_poses)):
            visible_keypoints = get_visible_keypoints(keypoints, pose)
            for kp in visible_keypoints:
                # Compute ray direction from image coordinates of kp
                # Set camera axis to -1 in local coordinates
                ray_origin, ray_direction = create_keypoint_ray(kp, pose)
                keypoint_rays.append((ray_origin, ray_direction, kp, img))
        
        # Mix standard and keypoint rays
        batch_rays.extend(standard_rays)
        batch_rays.extend(keypoint_rays)
        
        total_loss = 0.0
        
        for ray in batch_rays:
            if ray.is_keypoint_ray:
                origin, direction, kp, gt_img = ray
                # 2. Render color and depth
                samples = sample_ray(origin, direction, nerf_model)
                rendered_color, rendered_depth, termination_dist = volume_render(samples, nerf_model)
                
                # 3. Color Loss
                # Interpolate RGB from gt_img at keypoint's image coordinates
                gt_color = interpolate_color(gt_img, kp.image_coords)
                color_loss = mse(rendered_color, gt_color)
                
                # 4. Depth Loss (KL Divergence)
                # Get projected depth and uncertainty for this keypoint
                projected_depth = project_depth(kp, pose)
                uncertainty = uncertainties[kp.id]
                
                # Compute KL divergence term for each sample
                depth_loss = 0.0
                for t_k, h_k in zip(samples.distances, termination_dist):
                    # h_k is the probability density h(t_k)
                    # Target distribution N(projected_depth, uncertainty^2)
                    target_prob = gaussian_pdf(t_k, projected_depth, uncertainty)
                    # KL term: -log(h(t)) * target_prob * delta_t
                    # Note: The source formula is E[-log h(t) exp(...)]
                    # This is equivalent to minimizing KL(N || h) if we interpret correctly
                    # Source: L_Depth = E[-log h(t) exp(-(t-D)^2 / 2sigma^2)]
                    depth_loss += -log(h_k) * target_prob * samples.step_size
                
                loss = color_loss + lambda_d * depth_loss
            else:
                origin, direction = ray
                samples = sample_ray(origin, direction, nerf_model)
                rendered_color, _, _ = volume_render(samples, nerf_model)
                gt_color = interpolate_color(gt_img, ray.pixel_coords)
                loss = mse(rendered_color, gt_color)
            
            total_loss += loss
        
        # 5. Backpropagate
        update_nerf(nerf_model, total_loss)
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 [S4].
*   Near/Far bounds ($t_n, t_f$): The scene is bounded by these clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$ [S1].
*   Training Iterations: Typically 10k–100k [S3, unofficial].
*   Image Resolution: 400 × 300 in experiments [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different datasets (e.g., NeRF Real) if combined with DS-NeRF, though DS-NeRF itself generalizes [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are reduced by 2-3x compared to standard NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On a single RTX A5000 GPU, a DS-NeRF training iteration takes ~362.4 ms, compared to ~359.8 ms for standard NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified at lower view counts. With 2 views, NeRF often fails completely, while DS-NeRF succeeds [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with few views, requires no additional training data or category-specific priors, compatible with other NeRF methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of SfM keypoints; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in 2022 [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the difficulty of training NeRFs with insufficient input images, leveraging the "free" depth supervision already available from SfM pipelines like COLMAP [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1].
