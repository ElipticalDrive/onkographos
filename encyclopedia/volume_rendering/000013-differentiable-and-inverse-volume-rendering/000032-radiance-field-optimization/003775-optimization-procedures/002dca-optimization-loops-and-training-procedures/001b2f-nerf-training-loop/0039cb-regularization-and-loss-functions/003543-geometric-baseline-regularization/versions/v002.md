# Geometric Baseline Regularization

Also called: Geometric Baseline, Geometric Loss

## Overview
Geometric Baseline Regularization is a composite loss function designed to prevent degenerate solutions in Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) optimization, particularly in few-view settings where models may collapse to memorizing training images [S1]. It augments the standard photometric reconstruction loss with three geometric constraints: foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on external priors like diffusion models [7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md] or explicit multi-view stereo constraints [8582-regnerf.md], this method relies solely on the internal consistency of the volume rendering process along individual rays [S1].

## How it works
The method computes a total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ by combining the photometric loss with three regularization terms. The optimization updates the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields to minimize this total loss [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   Training images $\mathcal{I} = \{I_1, \dots, I_\mathcal{I}\}$.
    *   Camera poses and intrinsics for each view.
    *   Ray sampling parameters: number of samples $N$, near/far bounds, and sampling strategy (e.g., uniform or importance sampling).
    *   Hyperparameters: $\lambda_{\text{fg}}$, $\lambda_{\text{fr}}$, $\lambda_{\text{dist}}$ (see Parameters).
*   **Outputs**:
    *   Gradients $\nabla_{\sigma, \mathbf{c}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}}$ for backpropagation.

### Data Structures
Standard NeRF ray sampling structures are used. For each ray $\mathbf{r}$, the following derived quantities are computed from the sampled points $t_i$ (depths) and weights $w_i$ (opacity contributions):
*   $t_i$: Depth of the $i$-th sample.
*   $w_i$: Weight of the $i$-th sample, $w_i = \alpha_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$, where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$.
*   $n_i$: Number of training view frustums containing the point at $t_i$.

### Formulas
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{geom}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{fg}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} + \lambda_{\text{fr}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} + \lambda_{\text{dist}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} $$

1.  **Photometric Loss** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$: The L2 difference between rendered colors $\mathbf{C}_i$ and input images $I_i$ [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}(\sigma, \mathbf{c}) = \sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}} ||I_i - \mathbf{C}_i||_2 $$

2.  **Foreground Loss** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}}$: Encourages the sum of weights along a ray to equal unity, ensuring rays are fully absorbed by scene geometry [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{fg}} = \left( 1 - \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \right)^2 $$

3.  **Frustum Loss** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}}$: Penalizes density placed in only one view frustum to prevent "image plane" collapse in few-view scenarios [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{fr}} = \sum_i w_i \mathbf{1}(n_i \le 1) $$
    where $\mathbf{1}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function and $n_i$ is the count of training views whose frustums contain the point at $t_i$.

4.  **Distribution Loss** $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}$: Encourages compact weight distribution along the ray [S1]:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} = \frac{1}{\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})} \left( \sum_{i,j} w_i w_k \left| \frac{t_i + t_{i+1}}{2} - \frac{t_j + t_{j+1}}{2} \right| + \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i=1}^N w_i^2 (t_{i+1} - t_i) \right) $$
    Note: The source text contains a typo in the first summation index ($w_k$ instead of $w_j$). Based on the symmetry of the term and standard formulations of this regularizer [S1], the correct term is $w_i w_j$. The formula is implemented as:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}} = \frac{1}{\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})} \left( \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j \left| \frac{t_i + t_{i+1}}{2} - \frac{t_j + t_{j+1}}{2} \right| + \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i=1}^N w_i^2 (t_{i+1} - t_i) \right) $$

5.  **Expected Depth** $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$: Used to normalize the distribution loss, increasing regularization strength for geometry close to the camera [S1]:
    $$ \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i t_i}{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i} $$
