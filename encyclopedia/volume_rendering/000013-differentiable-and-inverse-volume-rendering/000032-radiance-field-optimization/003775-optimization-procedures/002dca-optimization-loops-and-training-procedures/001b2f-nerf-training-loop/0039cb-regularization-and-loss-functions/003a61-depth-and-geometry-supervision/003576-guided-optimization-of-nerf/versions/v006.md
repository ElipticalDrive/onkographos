# Guided Optimization of NeRF

Also called: Confidence-Based Depth Filtering

## Overview
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for indoor scenes by constraining the sampling of points along camera rays using adapted monocular depth priors [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which samples uniformly between sparse reconstruction bounds, this method calculates adaptive near and far bounds based on multi-view geometric consistency errors, ensuring sampled points are concentrated around plausible geometry [S1]. It further refines the output by computing per-pixel confidence scores from RGB reconstruction errors to weight a plane bilateral filter on the rendered depth map [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** $N$ input RGB images with known camera intrinsics $K$ and poses; a pre-trained monocular depth network (e.g., Mannequin Challenge [S1]) to generate initial depth priors.
**Outputs:** A refined depth map for each input view; a trained NeRF model for novel view synthesis.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Adapted Depth Priors:** $\{D^i\}_{i=1}^N$, where $D^i$ is a 2D depth map for view $i$.
*   **Error Maps:** $\{e_i\}_{i=1}^N$, where $e_i$ is a scalar value per pixel representing the reprojection error.
*   **NeRF Model:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) $\mathcal{F}_\theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c}, \sigma)$ mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $K=4$: Number of minimum cross-view depth projection errors used to calculate the error map value [S1].
*   $\alpha_l = 0.05$: Lower bound for the relative sampling range [S1].
*   $\alpha_h = 0.15$: Higher bound for the relative sampling range [S1].
*   $M$: Number of sample points per ray (standard NeRF default, e.g., 64 or 128) [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Acquire Adapted Depth Priors:**
    Initialize monocular depth estimates for all $N$ input views. Fine-tune the monocular network on the specific scene for 15 epochs to adapt the priors [S1].

2.  **Compute Error Maps:**
    For each view $i$, project its depth map $D^i$ to all other views $j \neq i$ using the camera intrinsics $K$ and relative pose $T^{i \rightarrow j}$:
    $$ p^{i \rightarrow j}, D^{i \rightarrow j} = \text{proj}(K, T^{i \rightarrow j}, D^i) $$
    $$ D^{j'} = D^j(p^{i \rightarrow j}) $$
    where $p^{i \rightarrow j}$ are the 2D coordinates and $D^{i \rightarrow j}$ are the projected depths in view $j$ [S1]. Calculate the relative reprojection error between $D^{j'}$ and $D^j$. For each pixel in view $i$, the error map value $e_i$ is the average of the top $K$ minimum cross-view depth projection errors [S1].

3.  **Calculate Adaptive Sample Ranges:**
    For each ray $\mathbf{r}$ corresponding to a pixel in view $i$, determine the near ($t_n$) and far ($t_f$) bounds using the adapted depth prior $D$ (the depth value at that pixel) and the error map value $e_i$:
    $$ t_n = D(1 - \text{clamp}(e_i, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    $$ t_f = D(1 + \text{clamp}(e_i, \alpha_l, \alpha_h)) $$
    where $\text{clamp}(x, a, b)$ restricts $x$ to the interval $[a, b]$ [S1].

4.  **Stratified Sampling:**
    Partition the interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $M$ bins. For each bin $i \in [1, M]$, sample a point $t_i$ uniformly:
    $$ t_i \sim \mathcal{U} \left[ t_n + \frac{i-1}{M}(t_f - t_n), \,\, t_n + \frac{i}{M}(t_f - t_n) \right] $$
    Query the NeRF $\mathcal{F}_\theta$ at positions $\mathbf{x}_i$ along the ray at distances $t_i$ to obtain color $\mathbf{c}_i$ and density $\sigma_i$ [S1].

5.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Render the color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r})$ for the ray:
    $$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) \mathbf{c}_i $$
    $$ \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} T_i (1 - e^{-\sigma_i \delta_i}) t_i $$
    where $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$, $T_i = \exp \left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j \right)$ is the accumulated transmittance, and $T_1 = 1$ [S1].

6.  **Compute Confidence Scores:**
    Calculate the confidence score $S^i_j$ for pixel $j$ in view $i$ based on the $L_1$ difference between the ground truth RGB image $C_{gt}^i(j)$ and the rendered RGB image $C_{render}^i(j)$, normalized by 255:
    $$ S^i_j = 1 - \frac{1}{3} \| C_{gt}^i(j) - C_{render}^i(j) \|_1 $$
    The $L_1$ norm is taken over the 3 color channels [S1].

7.  **Depth Refinement:**
    Apply plane bilateral filtering to the rendered depth map, weighted by the confidence scores $S^i_j$. This step improves depth quality in regions where the rendered RGB images are inaccurate [S1].

## Subtopics

### ENeRF with Guided Sampling Training
Integrates pixel and depth-guided ray sampling into the Efficient NeRF (ENeRF) framework [S2]. It samples fewer rays per iteration than standard ENeRF, achieving improved performance with lower GPU memory usage [S2]. It uses a total loss $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{mse} + \lambda' \mathcal{L}_{perc}$, where $\mathcal{L}_{mse}$ is the MSE between rendered and ground-truth colors, $\mathcal{L}_{perc}$ is a perceptual loss using image blocks, and $\lambda' = 0.01$ [S2]. It is trained on the DTU dataset for generalization and fine-tuned on novel scenes, tested on Real Forward-facing, NeRF Synthetic, ZJU-MoCap, and digital human datasets [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The method adds overhead for multi-view depth projection and error map calculation during initialization [S1]. During training, the adaptive sampling range calculation is negligible per ray. The guided sampling reduces the search space for geometry, potentially accelerating convergence in texture-less regions [S1]. The ENeRF variant reduces GPU memory usage by sampling fewer rays per iteration [S2].

**Measured Performance:**
On the ScanNet dataset, the method outperforms state-of-the-art depth estimation methods (including DeepV2D, DELTAS, and Atlas) in all metrics, despite those baselines being trained with ground-truth depth supervision [S1]. It significantly outperforms original NeRF in view synthesis quality [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor areas (e.g., walls) where standard NeRF fails [S1]. Produces accurate depth maps and high-quality novel view synthesis [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of adapted monocular depth priors. Applying existing correspondence-based neural optimization (like CVD) to these priors can degrade quality due to unreliable flow correspondences in poorly textured regions [S1].

**When to Use:**
Use for indoor multi-view stereo and depth estimation where texture is sparse and standard NeRF geometry is unreliable [S1].

**When Not to Use:**
Avoid if high-quality monocular depth priors cannot be obtained or if the scene has significant motion blur that reduces NeRF capacity [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed in "Depth-Guided Optimization of Neural Radiance Fields for Indoor Multi-View Stereo" [S1]. It addresses the shape-radiance ambiguity identified in [61], which states that while correct geometry is favored by smooth surface light fields, NeRF can still synthesize plausible RGB images with incorrect geometry in texture-less areas [S1]. The approach builds on the original NeRF formulation [33] and uses a monocular depth network (Mannequin Challenge [23]) adapted via fine-tuning [S1].
