# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF, Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) Training with Keypoint Supervision, Depth-Supervised NeRF Training, NeRF with Keypoint Depth Supervision

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality in sparse-view settings by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints generated during Structure-from-Motion (SfM) preprocessing [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF imposes a loss that forces the rendered ray termination distribution to match the surface priors given by these keypoints, effectively regularizing the density field $\sigma$ without requiring additional training data or domain-specific assumptions [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_j\}$, their camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM), and the resulting sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ with visibility flags [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLPs for density $\sigma$ and color $c$) capable of rendering novel views [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Keypoints:** A set $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ representing 3D points reconstructed by SfM [S1].
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that camera [S1].
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each visible keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and an uncertainty variance $\hat{\sigma}_i^2$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera parameters and generate sparse 3D keypoints [S1].
    *   *Note:* The SfM output is used only during training, not for novel view synthesis [S1].
    *   *Configuration:* Default COLMAP settings are used on the limited training views [S1].

2.  **Compute Keypoint Depths and Uncertainty:**
    *   For each image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$ [S1].
    *   For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].

3.  **Define Ray Termination Distribution:**
    *   The NeRF renders a ray termination distribution $h_{ij}(t)$, defined as:
        $$ h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t) = \exp\left[-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds\right]\sigma(t) $$
        where $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density at distance $t$ from the camera origin [S1].
    *   $h(t)$ is a valid probability distribution because $\int_0^\infty h(t)dt = 1$, assuming $\sigma(s)$ is non-negative and $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$ (enforced by treating the far bound $t_f$ as an opaque wall) [S1].

4.  **Define Supervision Distribution:**
    *   Model the true depth of the first surface encountered by a ray as a random variable $D_{ij}$ normally distributed around the estimated depth with variance $\hat{\sigma}_i^2$:
        $$ D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i^2) $$
    *   The objective is to minimize the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and this noisy depth distribution [S1].

5.  **Compute Losses:**
    *   **Depth Loss ($L_{Depth}$):** Minimize the KL divergence, which simplifies to the following approximation over discrete samples $t_k$:
        $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\sum_k h_k \exp\left(-\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2}\right) \Delta t_k \right] $$
        where $h_k$ is the discrete approximation of $h(t_k)$ [S1].
        *   *Alternative:* An MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ can also be used, yielding similar PSNR improvements, though KL loss yields slightly improved values [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Color Loss ($L_{Color}$):** Standard NeRF reconstruction loss:
        $$ L_{Color} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{r} \in R(P)} \|\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r})\|^2 $$
        where $\hat{C}$ is the rendered color and $C$ is the ground truth [S1].

6.  **Training Loop Optimization:**
    *   To train with both color and depth supervision in a single ray marching step, exploit the continuity of image coordinates [S1].
    *   For each training iteration, allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For these keypoint rays:
        *   Compute ray origins in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of detected keypoints, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
        *   Shoot these rays into the scene and render depth using the standard NeRF sampling procedure [S1].
        *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for these rays from the input images, since image coordinates are continuous [S1].
        *   Simultaneously supervise RGB (via interpolation) and depth (via $L_{Depth}$) for these rays within the single ray marching procedure [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Near/Far Bounds ($t_n, t_f$):** The scene is assumed to lie between these bounds. $t_f$ is treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$ [S1].
*   **Density Activation:** $\sigma(s)$ must be real-valued and non-negative, typically implemented using ReLU or softplus [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** $O(10k - 100k)$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ray Allocation:** A portion of rays is dedicated to keypoint rays [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Poor SfM:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint location uncertainty, which may not hold for depth derived from non-SfM sources (e.g., depth sensors) [S1].
*   **Evaluation Only:** Dense MVS depth is used only for evaluation, not during training or testing [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are comparable to standard NeRF. On a single RTX A5000, a DS-NeRF training iteration takes ~362.4 ms, compared to ~359.8 ms for standard NeRF [S1].
*   **Speedup:** DS-NeRF reaches a target test PSNR in 2-3x fewer training iterations than NeRF [S1]. In a 5-view setup, it achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as SfM keypoints are sparse and pre-computed [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Performance gains are magnified at lower view counts. In a 5-view setup, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR ~13 hours faster [S1]. With 2 views, NeRF fails to match DS-NeRF's performance [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with fewer views, requires no additional training data or domain-specific priors, compatible with other NeRF methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of SfM; fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates. Assumes Gaussian uncertainty for keypoints [S1].
*   **When to use:** Sparse-view scenarios, limited training time, scenes where SfM succeeds.
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with poor SfM reconstruction, or when dense depth maps are available and can be used directly (though DS-NeRF can support other depth sources, its primary design is for SfM keypoints) [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by [S1] (Chen et al., CVPR 2022).
*   **History:** Developed to address the failure mode of NeRF fitting incorrect geometries with insufficient input views. It leverages the "free" depth supervision from SfM keypoints, which are already required for NeRF training [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented for training on DTU, Redwood, and NeRF Real datasets with 2, 5, or 10 input views [S1]. Compatible with other NeRF methods like PixelNeRF and metaNeRF [S1].
