# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints [S1, S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of NeRF overfitting to incorrect geometries when insufficient views are available by enforcing that the rendered ray termination distribution matches surface priors derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF requires no additional training and generalizes to any scene where SfM succeeds, making it a flexible, "free" supervisory signal [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_j\}$, camera poses $\{P_j\}$ (estimated via SfM), and the resulting sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ with visibility flags [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $c$) capable of rendering novel views [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set $X = \{x_1, x_2, \dots\} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ representing 3D coordinates [S1].
*   **Visibility:** For each camera $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from that view [S1].
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera parameters and generate sparse 3D keypoints $\{X\}$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Depth and Uncertainty Calculation:**
    *   For each image $I_j$ with camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$ [S1].
    *   Compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ for each keypoint $x_i \in X_j$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
    *   Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
3.  **Ray Generation and Allocation:**
    *   For each training iteration, allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For each keypoint ray corresponding to $x_i \in X_j$:
        *   Compute the ray origin in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of $x_i$, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
        *   Shoot this ray into the scene [S1].
4.  **Volume Rendering:**
    *   Render the color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ray termination distribution $h(t)$ along the ray $\mathbf{r}$ using the standard NeRF sampling procedure [S1].
    *   The ray termination distribution is defined as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds)$ is the transmittance and $\sigma(t)$ is the differential density [S1].
    *   Note: $h(t)$ is a valid probability distribution over ray distance $t$ such that $\int_0^\infty h(t)dt = 1$, assuming the scene is bounded by near $t_n$ and far $t_f$ bounds, with $t_f$ treated as an opaque wall [S1].
5.  **Color Supervision:**
    *   Interpolate RGB supervision values for the rays corresponding to detected keypoints, exploiting the continuity of image coordinates [S1].
    *   Compute the photometric loss $L_{Color}$ between the rendered color $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{r})$ and the ground truth color $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S1].
6.  **Depth Supervision (KL Divergence Loss):**
    *   Model the true depth of the first surface as a random variable $D_{ij}$ normally distributed around the estimated depth $D_{ij}$ with variance $\hat{\sigma}_i$: $D_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1].
    *   Compute the depth loss $L_{Depth}$ by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the noisy depth distribution [S1]:
        $$ L_{Depth} = \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \left[ -\log h(t) \exp \left( -\frac{(t - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \right] dt $$
    *   Approximated discretely over sample points $t_k$ with step size $\Delta t_k$:
        $$ L_{Depth} \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
        where $h_k = h(t_k)$ [S1].
    *   *Alternative:* Some implementations use a simple MSE loss between rendered depth and sparse depth, though KL divergence yields slightly better results with fewer artifacts [S3, unofficial].
7.  **Total Loss and Update:**
    *   Combine losses: $L = L_{Color} + \lambda_D L_{Depth}$, where $\lambda_D$ is a hyper-parameter balancing color and depth supervision [S1].
    *   Backpropagate gradients through the differentiable volume rendering process to update NeRF parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def train_ds_nerf(images, poses, keypoints, num_iterations, lambda_D):
    """
    images: List of input images.
    poses: Camera poses estimated by SfM.
    keypoints: Sparse 3D keypoints from SfM, including visibility and reprojection errors.
    num_iterations: Total training steps.
    lambda_D: Weight for depth loss.
    """
    nerf_model = initialize_nerf()
    
    for iteration in range(num_iterations):
        # 1. Sample rays
        # Allocate a portion of rays to keypoint rays, rest to random rays
        rays = sample_rays(images, poses, include_keypoint_rays=True)
        
        rendered_colors = []
        rendered_depth_distributions = [] # List of h(t) for each ray
        gt_colors = []
        gt_depths = []
        gt_depth_uncertainties = []
        
        for ray in rays:
            # 2. Volume Rendering
            # Standard NeRF forward pass
            sigma_samples, color_samples, t_samples = nerf_model.ray_march(ray)
            
            # Compute transmittance T(t)
            # T(t) = exp(-sum(sigma * delta_t))
            transmittance = compute_transmittance(sigma_samples)
            
            # Compute ray termination distribution h(t) = T(t) * sigma(t)
            # h(t) is a probability distribution over depth
            h_t = transmittance * sigma_samples
            
            # Rendered color (weighted average)
            c_rendered = sum(h_t * color_samples)
            
            # Store h_t for depth loss calculation
            rendered_depth_distributions.append(h_t)
            rendered_colors.append(c_rendered)
            
            # 3. Supervision
            if ray.is_keypoint_ray:
                # Interpolate GT color from image at ray's 2D projection
                c_gt = interpolate_color(images[ray.camera_id], ray.uv)
                gt_colors.append(c_gt)
                
                # Get depth info from keypoint
                keypoint = ray.keypoint
                d_gt = keypoint.projected_depth
                sigma_hat = keypoint.reprojection_error
                
                gt_depths.append(d_gt)
                gt_depth_uncertainties.append(sigma_hat)
            else:
                # Random rays only use color loss
                c_gt = get_gt_color(images[ray.camera_id], ray.uv)
                gt_colors.append(c_gt)
        
        # 4. Compute Losses
        # Color Loss (MSE)
        color_loss = mean_squared_error(rendered_colors, gt_colors)
        
        # Depth Loss (KL Divergence approximation)
        depth_loss = 0.0
        count = 0
        for i, h_t in enumerate(rendered_depth_distributions):
            if gt_depths[i] is not None: # Only for keypoint rays
                d_gt = gt_depths[i]
                sigma_hat = gt_depth_uncertainties[i]
                t_samples = rendered_depth_distributions[i].t_samples # Assuming stored
                
                # L_Depth = sum_k -log(h_k) * exp(-(t_k - D_ij)^2 / (2 * sigma_hat^2)) * delta_t_k
                term = -log(h_t) * exp(-((t_samples - d_gt)**2) / (2 * sigma_hat**2))
                depth_loss += sum(term * delta_t) # delta_t is step size
                count += 1
        
        if count > 0:
            depth_loss /= count
            
        total_loss = color_loss + lambda_D * depth_loss
        
        # 5. Update
        optimizer.step(total_loss)
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Depth loss weight. Empirically set to 1 in some implementations [S4], or tuned to balance color and depth [S1].
*   Near/Far bounds ($t_n, t_f$): Scene bounds. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall to ensure $\int_0^\infty h(t)dt = 1$ [S1].
*   Training Iterations: Typically 10k–100k [S3, unofficial].
*   Image Resolution: 400 × 300 in original experiments [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **SfM Quality:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint location uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other datasets (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different domains (e.g., NeRF Real) if combined with DS-NeRF, though DS-NeRF itself is domain-agnostic [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has an existing geometry prior handled by its PSV-initialization [S1].
*   **Keypoint Sparseness:** Performance degrades with fewer keypoints, but DS-NeRF still outperforms baseline NeRF even with sparse keypoints (e.g., 20% of available) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are 2-3x fewer than standard NeRF to reach the same PSNR [S1]. Wall-clock time per iteration is comparable (~362.4 ms vs ~359.8 ms on RTX A5000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory overhead compared to standard NeRF, as depth supervision is computed on-the-fly from SfM outputs [S1].
*   **Performance:** On a 5-view setup, DS-NeRF achieves NeRF's peak test PSNR approximately 13 hours faster [S1]. In 2-view settings, DS-NeRF significantly outperforms NeRF, which often fails to converge to high quality [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with few views, compatible with other NeRF methods, requires no additional training data [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the success of SfM; poor SfM leads to failure; assumes Gaussian uncertainty [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Introduced by Deng et al. in 2022 [S1].
*   **History:** Developed to address the difficulty of training NeRFs with insufficient input images by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM pipelines [S1].
*   **Usage:** Applied to LLFF, NeRF Real, Redwood-3dscan, and DTU datasets [S1]. Compatible with depth from scanned sensors and RGB-D outputs [S1].
