# Depth and Geometry Supervision
## Overview
Depth and Geometry Supervision methods accelerate Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) optimization and improve geometric accuracy under sparse-view or single-view conditions by incorporating external depth priors into the training loop [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which relies solely on photometric consistency and often suffers from shape-radiance ambiguity, these methods enforce geometric constraints via adaptive sampling bounds, deformable alignment of monocular estimates, or probabilistic depth losses derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) keypoints [S1].
## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of integrating non-photometric geometric information to constrain the density field $\sigma$ and the ray termination distribution $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds)$ is the transmittance [S1]. Common data structures include adapted depth priors $\{D^i\}$, error maps $e_i$ representing reprojection inconsistencies, and confidence scores $S$ derived from rendering quality [S1]. The core mathematical operation involves modifying the standard volume rendering integral $\int T(t)\sigma(t)c(t)dt$ by either restricting the integration domain $[t_n, t_f]$ based on depth confidence or adding a regularization term $L_{Depth}$ that minimizes the divergence between the rendered distribution and a geometric prior [S1].
## Subtopics

### [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md)
Guided Optimization of NeRF mitigates shape-radiance ambiguity in poorly textured indoor environments by adapting sampling bounds $[t_n, t_f]$ using multi-view consistent depth priors. It sets itself apart from siblings by explicitly constraining optimization to regions likely containing geometry, preventing degenerate shapes. It is chosen when standard sampling fails to recover correct geometry despite accurate RGB reconstruction. See [Guided Optimization of NeRF](7496-guided-optimization-of-nerf.md).

### [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md)
This method addresses the under-constrained single-view NeRF problem by initializing geometry using a 360-degree depth estimation prior. It sets itself apart by handling scale ambiguity and field-of-view limitations through a deformable alignment step before NeRF training, rather than applying depth loss directly to raw estimates. It is chosen for single-view panorama reconstruction. See [Single-view NeRF Training with Depth Map](8679-single-view-nerf-training-with-depth-map.md).

### [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md)
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) accelerates optimization and improves quality under sparse-view conditions by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints generated during SfM preprocessing. Unlike standard NeRF, it enforces geometric constraints by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and a probabilistic depth prior derived from SfM keypoints. It is distinct from Guided Optimization because it does not adjust sampling bounds but directly supervises the density field's output distribution. See [Depth-Supervised NeRF](8687-depth-supervised-nerf.md).

### Zero-level set projection
#### definition
- [S1] Refines inaccurate median depth from a pre-trained NeRF, particularly at grazing view angles, by iteratively projecting points onto the zero level-set of the surface using TSDF values.
#### step
- [S1] For each 3D point p on the viewing ray, perform exactly 4 iterative gradient descent steps along the ray direction using the TSDF predicted value to update the point position.
#### formula
- [S1] $p_{new} = p_{old} + \alpha \times \hat{t}$, where $[\hat{t}, \hat{n}, \hat{f}] = \phi(p)$; $p$ is the current 3D point, $\alpha$ is a small constant step size, $[\hat{t}, \hat{n}, \hat{f}]$ are the tangent, normal, and feature outputs from network $\phi$ evaluated at $p$.
#### parameter
- [S1] $\alpha$: Small constant controlling the step size of the gradient descent update. Value not specified in this section.
#### pitfall
- [S1] Median depth from a pre-trained NeRF becomes inaccurate when the angle between the view direction and the surface is small (grazing angles).
#### usage
- [S1] Applied in NeRFMeshing as a preprocessing step to initialize the mesh geometry before training the SSAN model.

## Background
Originating from the need to resolve the shape-radiance ambiguity inherent in Neural Radiance Fields, these methods were developed to improve geometric fidelity in scenarios where photometric loss alone is insufficient, such as sparse views or textureless regions [S1]. The family includes techniques like Guided Optimization, which uses multi-view consistency [S1], and DS-NeRF, which leverages SfM keypoints [S1]. Zero-level set projection is used in NeRFMeshing to distill NeRFs into accurate meshes [S1].
