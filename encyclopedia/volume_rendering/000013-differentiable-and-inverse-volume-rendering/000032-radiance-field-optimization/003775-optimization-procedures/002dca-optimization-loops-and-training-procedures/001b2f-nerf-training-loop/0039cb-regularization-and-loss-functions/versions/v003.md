# Regularization and Loss Functions


## Overview
Regularization and Loss Functions define the objective landscape for optimizing Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), addressing the severe under-constraint problem where limited input views lead to geometric artifacts like "floaters" or degenerate solutions [S1, 7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md]. This family sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) by focusing on the gradient signals derived from the rendered output rather than the sampling strategy itself, and from [Training Configuration](2332-training-configuration.md) by providing the specific mathematical terms that guide the optimizer [S1, 7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves augmenting the standard photometric reconstruction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$) with additional terms that enforce geometric plausibility, spatial smoothness, or statistical priors. The total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ is generally a weighted sum:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k $$
where $\lambda_k$ are hyperparameters controlling the strength of each regularizer [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md]. The photometric loss is typically the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the rendered color $\mathbf{C}$ and the ground truth image $I$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = || \mathbf{C} - I ||_2^2 $$
Regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_k$ operate on the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ fields or the rendered weights $w_i$ to constrain the solution space [S1, 7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md, 8582-regnerf.md].

## Subtopics

### [Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md)
Addresses under-constrained optimization by regularizing geometry via a Denoising Diffusion Model (DDM) trained on RGBD patches. It differs from [RegNeRF](8582-regnerf.md) by using a DDM score function instead of normalizing flows, and from [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md) by relying on an external generative prior rather than explicit geometric constraints. Choose this for sparse views where floaters are prevalent.

### [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md)
Stabilizes few-view optimization by enforcing foreground completeness, frustum consistency, and depth distribution compactness directly through volume rendering weights. It differs from [Diffusion-Regularized NeRF Training](7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md) by not requiring external models. Choose this when avoiding degenerate single-view solutions is critical.

### [RegNeRF](8582-regnerf.md)
Improves view synthesis from sparse views using a Normalizing Flow prior on output distributions and a Smoothness prior on spatial gradients. It differs from [Geometric Baseline Regularization](7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md) by operating on statistical distributions rather than explicit geometric constraints. Choose this for generalizing to unobserved views.

### [Occlusion-Eliminating NeRF Variant](8653-occlusion-eliminating-nerf-variant.md)
Decouples scene representation into a Scene MLP and Background MLP to explicitly model and remove foreground occlusions. It differs from general regularization methods by structural decomposition rather than loss augmentation. Choose this when training data contains significant foreground occluders.

### AligNeRF Training Objective
Replaces standard per-pixel MSE with a combination of patch-based MSE ($L_{PM}$) for misalignment and shallow VGG feature-space loss ($L_{HF}$) for high-frequency detail [S1]. The formula is $L_{total} = L_{PM} + w \cdot L_{HF}$, with $w$ typically set to 0.05 [S1]. Use this when training data suffers from slight misalignment or motion blur.

## Background
Originating from the need to stabilize NeRF optimization in sparse-view regimes, early methods like RegNeRF introduced normalizing flows to constrain output distributions [S1, 8582-regnerf.md]. Subsequent work, such as Geometric Baseline Regularization, focused on explicit geometric constraints like frustum consistency [7475-geometric-baseline-regularization.md]. More recently, diffusion models have been adapted to provide RGBD priors for geometry regularization [7381-diffusion-regularized-nerf-training.md]. AligNeRF addresses alignment issues in outdoor scenes by modifying the loss function to be robust to misalignment [S1].
