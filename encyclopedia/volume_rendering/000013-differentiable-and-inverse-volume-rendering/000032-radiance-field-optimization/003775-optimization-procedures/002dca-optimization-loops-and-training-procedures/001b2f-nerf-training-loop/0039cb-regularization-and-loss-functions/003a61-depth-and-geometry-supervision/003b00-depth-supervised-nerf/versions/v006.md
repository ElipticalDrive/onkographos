# Depth-Supervised NeRF

Also called: DS-NeRF, Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) Training with Keypoint Supervision, Depth-Supervised NeRF Training, NeRF with Keypoint Depth Supervision

## Overview
Depth-Supervised NeRF (DS-NeRF) is a training procedure that accelerates Neural Radiance Field optimization and improves quality with sparse input views by incorporating depth supervision from sparse 3D keypoints generated during Structure-from-Motion (SfM) preprocessing [S1]. Unlike methods relying on data-driven priors from other scenes or semantic consistency, DS-NeRF enforces geometric plausibility by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered ray termination distribution and the noisy depth distribution of the keypoints, reducing overfitting and speeding up training by 2-3x [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $I_j$ with known camera poses $P_j$ (estimated via SfM/COLMAP), sparse 3D keypoints $X$ with visibility flags, and their reprojection errors.
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model (MLP parameters for density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$) capable of rendering novel views.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Keypoints:** A set $X = \{x_i\}$ where each $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Visibility:** For each image $j$, a subset $X_j \subset X$ of keypoints visible from camera $P_j$.
*   **Depth Estimates:** For each visible keypoint $x_i \in X_j$, a scalar depth $D_{ij}$ and uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$.
*   **Ray Sampling:** Standard NeRF sampling points $t_k$ along rays, with step size $\Delta t_k$.

**Steps:**

1.  **SfM Preprocessing:** Run an SfM solver (e.g., COLMAP) on the input images to estimate camera poses $P_j$ and sparse 3D keypoints $X$ [S1].
2.  **Depth Estimation:** For each image $I_j$ and camera $P_j$, identify visible keypoints $X_j$. For each $x_i \in X_j$, compute the projected depth $D_{ij}$ by projecting $x_i$ with $P_j$ and taking the re-projected z-value [S1].
3.  **Uncertainty Calculation:** Compute the depth uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_i$ for each keypoint $x_i$ as the average reprojection error across all views where the keypoint was detected [S1].
4.  **Ray Generation:**
    *   Allocate a portion of the total training rays to "keypoint rays" [S1].
    *   For each keypoint ray, compute the ray origin in local camera coordinates from the image coordinates of the detected keypoint, setting the camera axis coordinate to -1 [S1].
    *   Shoot this ray into the scene using the standard NeRF sampling procedure (sampling $t_k$ between near bound $t_n$ and far bound $t_f$) [S1].
5.  **Rendering:** Render the color $\mathbf{C}$ and the ray termination distribution $h(t)$ for the generated rays [S1].
    *   The ray termination distribution is defined as $h(t) = T(t)\sigma(t)$, where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_0^t \sigma(s)ds)$ [S1].
    *   Note: $h(t)$ is a valid probability distribution if $\int_0^\infty \sigma(s)ds = \infty$, which is enforced by treating $t_f$ as an opaque wall [S1].
6.  **Color Supervision:** Compute the standard photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Color}}$ between the rendered color $\mathbf{C}$ and the ground truth image color $C(r)$ [S1].
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Color}} = \mathbb{E}_{r \in R(P)} || \hat{C}(r) - C(r) ||_2^2$ [S1].
7.  **Depth Supervision:** Compute the depth loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Depth}}$ by minimizing the KL divergence between the rendered distribution $h_{ij}(t)$ and the Gaussian depth distribution $N(D_{ij}, \hat{\sigma}_i)$ [S1].
    *   The loss is approximated as:
        $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Depth}} \approx \mathbb{E}_{x_i \in X_j} \sum_k -\log h_k \exp \left( -\frac{(t_k - D_{ij})^2}{2\hat{\sigma}_i^2} \right) \Delta t_k $$
        where $h_k = h(t_k)$ and $t_k$ are the sample points along the ray [S1].
    *   *Note:* An MSE loss between rendered depth and $D_{ij}$ is also possible but yields slightly worse results than KL divergence [S2, unofficial].
8.  **RGB Interpolation:** Since keypoint rays correspond to continuous image coordinates, interpolate RGB supervision values from the ground truth image for these rays to allow simultaneous RGB and depth supervision in a single ray marching step [S1].
9.  **Optimization:** Minimize the total loss:
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Color}} + \lambda_D \mathcal{L}_{\text{Depth}} $$
    where $\lambda_D$ is a hyperparameter balancing color and depth supervision [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_D$: Weight for depth loss. Value not given in the sources.
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. $t_f$ is often treated as an opaque wall [S1].
*   Training Iterations: O(10k - 100k) [S2, unofficial].
*   Image Resolution: 400 × 300 (used in experiments) [S1].
*   Activation: $\sigma(s)$ is typically ReLU or softplus [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **SfM Failure:** Optimization fails if SfM produces poor depth estimates or inaccurate depth measurements [S1].
*   **Gaussian Assumption:** The method assumes a Gaussian distribution models keypoint uncertainty, which may not hold for depth from non-SfM sources [S1].
*   **Domain Gap:** Methods pre-trained on other domains (e.g., DTU) may struggle on different datasets (e.g., NeRF Real) due to domain differences, though DS-NeRF itself is domain-agnostic [S1].
*   **MVSNeRF Compatibility:** Adding depth supervision did not improve MVSNeRF because it already has a geometry prior from PSV-initialization [S1].
*   **Evaluation Depth:** Dense MVS depth is used only for evaluation, not training [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training iterations are reduced by 2-3x compared to standard NeRF [S1]. Wall time per iteration is comparable (~362.4 ms vs ~359.8 ms on RTX A5000) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** On NeRF Real with 5 input views, DS-NeRF achieves peak PSNR ~13 hours faster than NeRF [S1]. With 2 views, DS-NeRF significantly outperforms NeRF (PSNR 20.2 vs 13.5) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accelerates training, improves quality with few views, compatible with other NeRF methods, no domain-specific priors required [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on successful SfM, assumes Gaussian depth uncertainty, limited by quality of sparse keypoints [S1].
*   **When to use:** Sparse view synthesis, accelerating NeRF training, scenes where SfM succeeds.
*   **When not to use:** Scenes with poor SfM results, or when using methods with existing strong geometry priors (e.g., MVSNeRF) [S1].

## Background
DS-NeRF was introduced in the paper "Depth-supervised NeRF: Fewer Views and Faster Training for Free" by Wang et al. (CVPR 2022) [S1]. It addresses the failure mode of NeRF fitting incorrect geometries with insufficient input views by leveraging "free" depth supervision from SfM keypoints [S1]. The method is compatible with depth from other sources like RGB-D sensors [S1].
