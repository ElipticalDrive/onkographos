# R2L (Neural Light Field)

Also called: Neural Light Field, R2L network

## Overview
R2L (R2L) is a deep neural light field network designed for efficient novel view synthesis (NVS) by distilling knowledge from a pre-trained NeRF teacher model [S1]. It replaces the standard shallow NeRF MLP with a significantly deeper architecture (depth 88) utilizing extensive residual blocks, trading width for depth under a fixed FLOPs budget to achieve superior rendering quality and 28–31× wall-time speedup [S1]. Unlike methods relying on ground-truth depth or discrete voxel grids, R2L learns a continuous radiance field directly from pseudo-data generated by the teacher, allowing it to handle reflective surfaces where depth-based methods fail [S1].

## How it works
R2L defines the radiance field function $f: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x}$ is a 3D position and $\mathbf{d}$ is the viewing direction [S1]. The method consists of three phases: network initialization, pseudo-data generation, and distillation training with hard-example mining.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Pre-trained NeRF teacher model parameters [S1].
    *   Original captured images (optional, for "Ours-2" variant) [S1].
    *   Camera poses for the original captured images [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   R2L network weights [S1].
    *   Rendered images for novel views [S1].

### Data Structures
The R2L network is a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with specific architectural constraints:
*   **Width ($W$):** 256 (W256D88) or 181 (W181D88) [S1].
*   **Depth ($D$):** 88 layers [S1].
*   **Layers:** The network consists of linear layers interleaved with activation functions. Crucially, it employs **extensive residual blocks** in the internal layers [S1].
*   **Residual Blocks:** Unlike standard NeRF skip connections (which only add input coordinates/directions to the output), R2L uses internal residual connections where the output of a block is added to its input [S1]. Without these residuals, the network is "barely trainable" [S1].
*   **Input/Output Mapping:**
    *   Input vector: $[\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}]$ concatenated [S1].
    *   Output vector: $[\sigma, \mathbf{c}]$ (volume density and view-dependent color) [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Initialize Teacher NeRF:** Train a standard NeRF model on the original captured images. The teacher is trained with a batch size of 1,024, initial learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ (decayed during training), for 200k iterations [S1].
2.  **Generate Pseudo Data:** Sample rays from the pre-trained NeRF teacher to synthesize pseudo training images.
    *   Let $S$ be the number of pseudo images. Default $S = 10,000$ [S1].
    *   Rays are sampled from different images such that they do not share the same origin [S1].
    *   Performance saturates around $S = 5,000$ images; $S=100$ is insufficient and leads to overfitting [S1].
3.  **Initialize R2L Network:** Construct the R2L MLP with width $W \in \{256, 181\}$ and depth $D=88$ [S1]. Initialize weights (standard initialization implied, though specific seed not given) [S1].
4.  **Define Loss Function:** Use the standard photometric loss (e.g., Mean Squared Error) between the rendered color $\mathbf{c}_{pred}$ and the target color $\mathbf{c}_{target}$ [S1].
5.  **Hard-Example Mining:** In each training batch, identify a fraction $r$ of rays with the smallest PSNR (or highest error) relative to the target. Default $r = 0.2$ [S1].
    *   Tested values for $r$: $\{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ [S1].
    *   Rays with high error are prioritized in the gradient update [S1].
6.  **Train R2L:**
    *   **Batch Size:** 98,304 rays per batch [S1].
    *   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
    *   **Learning Rate Schedule:** Same as the teacher NeRF [S1].
    *   **Iterations:** 1,000k iterations [S1].
    *   **Hardware:** 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs [S1].
    *   **Framework:** PyTorch 1.9 [S1].
    *   **Data Source:** Use either pseudo data only ("Ours-1") or pseudo data + original real images ("Ours-2") [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Overfitting with Small Pseudo Data:** Using fewer than 500 pseudo images (e.g., $S=100$) causes the deep network to overfit, saturating test PSNR early while training PSNR rises sharply [S1].
*   **Trainability without Residuals:** Without extensive residual blocks in internal layers, the network is "barely trainable" [S1].
*   **Reflective Surfaces:** Methods relying on ground-truth depth (like DONeRF) fail on reflective surfaces (water, mirrors) because the depth does not correspond to the visual reflection. R2L, not relying on GT depth, handles these well [S1].
*   **Teacher Blurriness:** Blurry artifacts in the teacher NeRF are inherited by R2L, as it learns from teacher-generated data [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** 28–31× faster than NeRF and 2× faster than DONeRF-16-noGT in wall-time [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Storage footprint is 23.7 MB for R2L (W256D88), compared to 2.4 MB for the teacher NeRF [S1]. This is higher than the teacher due to the larger network size, but significantly lower than voxel-based methods like KiloNeRF (38.9 MB) [S1].
*   **FLOPs:** 11.79 M FLOPs for R2L, compared to 303.82 M for the teacher NeRF [S1]. This represents a ~26× reduction in FLOPs [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **NeRF Synthetic Dataset:** 31.87 PSNR (+1.40 over teacher), 0.9950 SSIM, 0.0340 LPIPS [S1].
    *   **DONeRF Synthetic Dataset:** 35.45 PSNR (+2.78 over teacher), 0.047 FLIP [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High speed, high quality (better than teacher when real data is included), robust to reflective surfaces.
*   **Limitations:** Requires a pre-trained NeRF teacher; inherits teacher's blur; higher memory than the teacher NeRF.

## Background
R2L was introduced by H. Wang et al. in the paper "R2L: Distilling Neural Radiance Field to Neural Light Field for Efficient Novel View Synthesis" [S1]. It addresses the computational bottleneck of NeRF by proposing a deep neural light field network trained via distillation. The work highlights the importance of network depth and residual connections for training deep radiance fields, and the necessity of data augmentation via pseudo-samples to prevent overfitting in such deep architectures [S1].
