# NeRF Training Loop

Also called: End-to-End NeRF Training, HashNeRF Training Loop, HoloINR Training Loop, NeRF Optimization Loop, NeRF Training Procedure, Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Training

## Overview
The NeRF Training Loop is the iterative optimization procedure that minimizes the photometric difference between rendered images and ground-truth observations by updating the parameters of a neural radiance field [S1][S5, S6, unofficial][S8][S10, S11, S14, unofficial]. It solves the inverse problem of reconstructing a continuous 3D scene representation from a set of 2D images by treating the radiance field as a differentiable function [S5, S6, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Neural Volume Rendering Training (MSE)](3429-neural-volume-rendering-training-mse.md) by specifically employing stochastic ray sampling to approximate the integral over the entire image plane, rather than rendering full images or using fixed grids, which allows for efficient memory usage during the optimization of high-resolution scenes [S5, S14, unofficial].

## How it works
The method optimizes a coordinate MLP (or SIREN) to map 3D spatial coordinates and viewing directions to volume density and color [S3, S5, unofficial]. The loop iterates over a fixed number of steps or epochs, processing batches of rays sampled from training images [S5, S11, S14, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Training dataset: A collection of $N$ images with known camera poses (extrinsics $C2W$) and intrinsics ($K$) [S2, S5, S14, unofficial].
    *   Model parameters $\theta$: Weights and biases of the neural network(s) [S5, S11, unofficial].
    *   Hyperparameters: Learning rate, batch size ($N_{rand}$), number of samples per ray ($N_{samples}$), near/far bounds [S5, S11, S14, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized model parameters $\theta^*$ [S5, S11, unofficial].
    *   Checkpoints containing model state, optimizer state, and training step/epoch [S8][S10, S11, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Rays:** A ray is defined by its origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$, near bound $t_{near}$, and far bound $t_{far}$ [S5, S14, unofficial].
*   **Pre-computed Ray Buffer:** To accelerate sampling, rays and ground-truth colors are often pre-computed into a flat array `rays_rgb` [S5, S14, unofficial].
    *   **Layout:** Shape `[(N-1)*H*W, 3, 3]` or `[TotalPixels, 3, 3]` [S5, S14, unofficial].
    *   **Axis 0:** Flattened pixel index.
    *   **Axis 1:** Channel type: `0` = Ray Origin $(x,y,z)$, `1` = Ray Direction $(x,y,z)$, `2` = Target RGB Color $(r,g,b)$ [S5, S14, unofficial].
    *   **Axis 2:** Components of the vector.
    *   **Type:** `float32` [S5, S14, unofficial].
*   **Batch Tensor:** During training, a subset of `rays_rgb` is sliced and transposed into a batch tensor `batch` [S14, unofficial].
    *   **Layout:** Shape `[3, N_rand, 3]` [S14, unofficial].
    *   **Axis 0:** Channel type (Origin, Direction, Target) [S14, unofficial].
    *   **Axis 1:** Batch index [S14, unofficial].
    *   **Axis 2:** Components [S14, unofficial].
*   **Checkpoint:** A dictionary storing the training state [S10, S11, unofficial].
    *   **Keys:** `'model'` (or `'coarse'`, `'fine'`), `'optimizer'`, `'epoch'` (or `'step'`) [S10, S11, unofficial].

### Formulas
*   **Ray Sampling (Linear in Depth):**
    $$ t_i = t_{near} + \frac{i}{N_{samples}} (t_{far} - t_{near}), \quad i \in [0, N_{samples}-1] $$
    $$ \mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \mathbf{d} $$
    [S5, S14, unofficial]

*   **Ray Sampling (Linear in Disparity):**
    $$ t_i = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{t_{near}}(1 - \frac{i}{N_{samples}}) + \frac{1}{t_{far}}\frac{i}{N_{samples}}} $$
    [S5, unofficial]

*   **Loss Function (MSE):**
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N_{rand}} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{rand}} \| \hat{C}_j - C_j \|^2 $$
    Where $\hat{C}_j$ is the rendered color and $C_j$ is the ground-truth color for ray $j$ [S14, S17, unofficial].
    Defined as: `tf.reduce_mean(tf.square(x - y))` [S14, unofficial].

*   **PSNR Calculation:**
    $$ \text{PSNR} = -10 \log_{10}(\text{MSE}) $$
    [S14, unofficial]

*   **Learning Rate Decay (Exponential):**
    $$ \text{lr}_{new} = \text{lr}_{initial} \times \text{decay\_rate}^{\frac{\text{global\_step}}{\text{decay\_steps}}} $$
    Where `decay_steps` is often `args.lrate_decay * 1000` [S5, S18, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode implements the standard NeRF training loop with stochastic ray sampling, supporting both single-network and coarse-to-fine architectures [S5, S11, S14, unofficial].

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim
import numpy as np

def initialize_rays_rgb(images, poses, intrinsics, near, far):
    """
    Pre-compute all rays and target colors from training images.
    Args:
        images: Tensor of shape [N, H, W, 3]
        poses: Tensor of shape [N, 4, 4] (C2W matrices)
        intrinsics: Tensor of shape [N, 3, 3] (K matrices)
        near, far: Float bounds
    Returns:
        rays_rgb: Tensor of shape [TotalPixels, 3, 3]
    """
    N, H, W, _ = images.shape
    total_pixels = (N - 1) * H * W # Or N*H*W depending on split
    rays_rgb = np.zeros((total_pixels, 3, 3), dtype=np.float32)
    
    idx = 0
    for i in range(N):
        # Get camera matrix
        c2w = poses[i]
        K = intrinsics[i]
        
        # Generate pixel coordinates
        y, x = np.meshgrid(np.arange(H), np.arange(W), indexing='ij')
        # Convert to homogeneous coordinates
        ones = np.ones_like(x)
        pix_coords = np.stack([x, y, ones], axis=-1) # [H, W, 3]
        
        # Project to camera space
        pix_coords_cam = np.linalg.inv(K) @ pix_coords.reshape(-1, 3).T # [3, H*W]
        
        # Transform to world space
        # p_world = C2W @ [x, y, z, 1]^T
        # We only need direction and origin. 
        # Origin is C2W[:3, 3]
        # Direction is C2W[:3, :3] @ pix_coords_cam
        origins = c2w[:3, 3].reshape(1, 3).repeat(H*W, axis=0)
        directions = (c2w[:3, :3] @ pix_coords_cam).T # [H*W, 3]
        
        # Normalize directions
        directions = directions / np.linalg.norm(directions, axis=1, keepdims=True)
        
        # Target colors
        targets = images[i].reshape(-1, 3)
        
        # Stack into [H*W, 3, 3]: [Origin, Direction, Target]
        batch_data = np.stack([origins, directions, targets], axis=1) # [H*W, 3, 3]
        rays_rgb[idx:idx+H*W] = batch_data
        idx += H*W
        
    return rays_rgb

def train_nerf_loop(model, rays_rgb, optimizer, scheduler, num_iters, batch_size, 
                    near, far, N_importance=0, perturb=0.0, use_batching=True):
    """
    Standard NeRF Training Loop.
    """
    total_rays = rays_rgb.shape[0]
    i_batch = 0
    
    for step in range(num_iters):
        # 1. Ray Sampling
        if use_batching:
            # Slice from pre-computed buffer
            end_batch = min(i_batch + batch_size, total_rays)
            batch_data = rays_rgb[i_batch:end_batch]
            i_batch += batch_size
            
            # Shuffle and reset if exhausted
            if i_batch >= total_rays:
                np.random.shuffle(rays_rgb)
                i_batch = 0
        else:
            # Random sampling from scratch (less efficient)
            # ... implementation omitted for brevity, see S14 ...
            pass

        # Transpose to [3, Batch, 3]: [Origin, Direction, Target]
        batch = batch_data.transpose(1, 0, 2)
        batch_rays = batch[:2] # Origins and Directions
        target_s = batch[2]    # Ground Truth Colors
        
        # 2. Forward Pass
        # Convert to tensors
        rays_o = torch.tensor(batch_rays[0], dtype=torch.float32)
        rays_d = torch.tensor(batch_rays[1], dtype=torch.float32)
        targets = torch.tensor(target_s, dtype=torch.float32)
        
        # Sample points along rays
        # Stratified sampling if perturb > 0
        t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., steps=batch_size * 128) # Example N_samples
        z_vals = near * (1.-t_vals) + far * (t_vals)
        
        if perturb > 0.:
            mids = .5 * (z_vals[..., 1:] + z_vals[..., :-1])
            upper = torch.cat([mids, z_vals[..., -1:]], -1)
            lower = torch.cat([z_vals[..., :1], mids], -1)
            t_rand = torch.rand(z_vals.shape)
            z_vals = lower + (upper - lower) * t_rand
            
        pts = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * z_vals.unsqueeze(2)
        
        # Query Model
        # raw: [Batch, N_samples, 4] (RGB + Density)
        raw = model(pts, rays_d) 
        
        # Volume Rendering (Alpha Compositing)
        # ... (See NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing) for raw2outputs) ...
        # For this loop, we assume a render function that returns rgb and extras
        rgb, extras = render_volume(raw, z_vals, rays_d)
        
        # 3. Loss Computation
        img_loss = torch.mean((rgb - targets) ** 2)
        loss = img_loss
        
        # Coarse-to-Fine Loss (if applicable)
        if N_importance > 0 and 'rgb0' in extras:
            img_loss0 = torch.mean((extras['rgb0'] - targets) ** 2)
            loss = loss + img_loss0
            
        # 4. Backpropagation
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        # 5. Scheduler Update
        if scheduler is not None:
            scheduler.step()
            
        # Logging and Checkpointing
        if step % 100 == 0:
            mse = loss.item()
            psnr = -10.0 * np.log10(mse) / np.log10(10.0)
            print(f"Step {step} | Loss: {mse:.6f} | PSNR: {psnr:.2f}")
            
        if step % 10000 == 0:
            save_checkpoint(model, optimizer, step)

    return model
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Learning Rate (`lr`):** $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S11, unofficial], $0.01$ [S17, unofficial], or defined in config [S8][S9, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size (`N_rand`):** 1024 [S5, S11, unofficial], 2048 [S5, unofficial], or 4096 [S17, unofficial]. Instant-NGP variants may use smaller batches ($2^{10}$) due to memory constraints [S15, unofficial].
*   **Iterations (`n_iters`):** 100,000 [S11, unofficial], 1,000,000 [S14, unofficial], or 250 epochs [S4, unofficial].
*   **Samples per Ray (`N_samples`):** 128 [S17, unofficial], or defined in config [S13, unofficial].
*   **Decay:** Exponential decay with `decay_rate=0.1` and `decay_steps = args.lrate_decay * 1000` [S5, S18, unofficial].
*   **Positional Encoding:** Frequency `multires=10` for position, `multires_views=4` for direction [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **VRAM Overflow:** Rendering full images or large batches can exceed GPU memory. Mitigated by chunking rays (`chunk=args.chunk`) during the forward pass [S5, S14, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Graph Freeing:** Higher-order derivatives (e.g., for logging gradients or Laplacians) must be computed *before* calling `loss.backward()`, as the backward pass frees the autograd graph [S3, unofficial].
*   **Checkpoint Resumption:** When resuming, the learning rate scheduler's last epoch must be set to `start_epoch - 1` to ensure correct scheduling [S9, unofficial]. If a checkpoint is loaded but the step is 0, it is treated as an empty checkpoint and may cause errors [S15, unofficial].
*   **Instability in Dynamic Growth:** Directly growing a randomly initialized sub-network causes density to drop to zero, leading to missing geometry. Grown sub-networks must initialize alpha linear weights to match the parent network [S1].
*   **Empty Checkpoints:** Loading a non-existent or invalid checkpoint should return an error code (e.g., 2 or 3) rather than crashing silently [S15, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md)

Defines the static and dynamic hyperparameters, optimizer settings, and initialization procedures required to optimize a radiance field model. It differs from the loop itself by focusing on numerical values and mathematical schedules.

An adaptive training strategy that expands network capacity based on local scene complexity and prediction uncertainty, clustering high-uncertainty query points to grow parallel network branches.

### [Mip-NeRF 360](3306-mip-nerf-360.md)
Integrates a non-linear scene parameterization, online distillation between two MLPs, and a distortion-based regularizer to handle unbounded, 360-degree scenes, achieving a 57% reduction in MSE compared to standard mip-NeRF.

### [Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md)
Extends Mip-NeRF to unbounded scenes using disparity-based ray parameterization, coordinate contraction, and a proposal network to mitigate "floaters" and "background collapse".

### [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md)
A hierarchical sampling strategy that uses a coarse network to identify opaque regions and a fine network to densely sample those areas, improving high-frequency detail reconstruction.

### [Neural Volume Scene Setup](4168-neural-volume-scene-setup.md)
Constructs a Mitsuba scene containing a volumetric cube and a custom neural radiance field integrator, initializing the density grid and spherical harmonics coefficients.

### [MVSNeRF](6197-mvsnerf.md)
Uses a feedforward neural network to predict the radiance field directly from input images, reducing training time by learning a data prior instead of per-scene optimization.

### [Probability-based Ray Sampling with Quadtree Subdivision](7481-probability-based-ray-sampling-with-quadtree-subdivision.md)
Accelerates training by dynamically adjusting ray distribution through context-based probability sampling and adaptive quadtree subdivision, reducing training time by 15–40%.

### [Dynamic and Temporal Modeling](7610-dynamic-and-temporal-modeling.md)
Extends static NeRF to 4D radiance fields (3D space + time) to model non-rigid motion and temporal evolution.

### [Camera Pose and Parameter Optimization](7611-camera-pose-and-parameter-optimization.md)
Treats camera parameters as differentiable variables within the training loop, allowing joint optimization with the radiance field to handle inaccurate or unknown initial poses.

### [MegaNeRF](8580-meganerf.md)
Divides the environment into distinct regions, each represented by its own MLP network, enabling parallel training and scalable rendering for large-scale scenes.

### [BlockNeRF](8581-blocknerf.md)
Decomposes a large-scale scene into spatial blocks for separate NeRF training, decoupling rendering time from scene size.

### [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md)
Modifications to the core MLP architecture, including network splitting, depth expansion, and convolutional post-processing, to address speed and quality limitations.

### [Regularization and Loss Functions](8586-regularization-and-loss-functions.md)
Defines objective landscape terms (e.g., Eikonal, TV, L1) to address under-constrained optimization and geometric artifacts like floaters.

### [Progressive Inpainting-and-Erasing](8658-progressive-inpainting-and-erasing.md)
A strategy for single-view NeRF that computes a mask of conflicted regions and eliminates them from training to avoid geometry conflicts.

### [Progressive View Synthesis Training](8666-progressive-view-synthesis-training.md)
Reconstructs a 3D scene from a single panorama by progressively adding synthetic reference views to resolve occlusions.

### [NeRFVS](8667-nerfvs.md)
Uses a geometry scaffold from an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method to guide NeRF optimization, reducing distortions in extrapolation.

### [NeRF-FF](8669-nerf-ff.md)
Mitigates degradation from out-of-focus regions by estimating binary image masks to omit blurred pixels during training.

### [L2G-NeRF](8671-l2g-nerf.md)
A bundle-adjusting method that jointly optimizes neural field parameters and camera poses using local and global geometric alignment objectives.

### [KNN-NeRF](8672-knn-nerf.md)
A baseline using sparse 3D point clouds as supervision, assigning the color of the nearest point to rays that do not pass through existing points.

### [Hard Sample Mining](8673-hard-sample-mining.md)
Improves training efficiency by selectively updating the network based on pixel-level error, subsampling a "hard" minibatch.

### [NoPe-NeRF](8705-nope-nerf.md)
Trains NeRF without precomputed camera poses by generating a dense point cloud from monocular depth priors to constrain pose optimization.

Incorporates explicit geometric constraints (monocular depth, SfM keypoints) to accelerate convergence and improve quality in sparse-view scenarios.

### Minor Variants
#### ReconFusion
Uses a Diffusion Prior to constrain unobserved views during the training loop [S19, unofficial].

#### Supersampling Baseline
A baseline method for evaluating NeRF on multiscale data by training on full-resolution images and using brute-force supersampling during evaluation, or training on multiscale data with supersampling during both training and evaluation [S20]. For every pixel, it casts multiple jittered rays sampled uniformly at random through the spatial footprint of the pixel, renders each ray with NeRF, and uses the mean of those rendered values as the predicted color in the loss function [S20]. This baseline performs worse than mip-NeRF and is significantly more expensive during both training and evaluation [S20]. It relies on 'oracle' scale information that does not exist for most datasets [S20].

#### Two-stage training for proposers
A training schedule split into two equal-duration phases to stabilize learning of a trainable proposer network in NeRF-ID [S21, unofficial]. Phase 1 trains the learnt proposer to mimic the heuristic proposer by minimizing the L2 matching loss between their proposal sets. Phase 2 trains all network components end-to-end, resetting the optimizer state and performing a linear warmup for 1,000 SGD steps to reduce shock from switching regimes [S21, unofficial]. While training from scratch sometimes works, two-stage training is consistently effective [S21, unofficial].

#### CityNeRF
A progressive learning paradigm that synchronously grows the NeRF model and training set [S22]. It starts by fitting distant views with shallow basic blocks, continuously attaching new blocks as training iterates to adapt to details in far-near views, effectively activating high-frequency channels in position encoding and unfolding more complex details as training progresses [S22].

#### ViP-NeRF
Introduces visibility priors to supervise density and improve rendering quality under sparse input view conditions (fewer than 50-100 views), preventing artifacts caused by insufficient training data [S23]. It regularizes NeRF with visibility priors by estimating whether each pixel in a main view is visible in a secondary view. This is computed by creating a plane scan volume, warping different depth planes of a secondary view image to the main view, comparing warped planes with the main view to obtain an error map, identifying the minimum error among all planes, and thresholding this error to obtain a visibility prior map used to supervise density during training [S23]. On the RealEstate-10K dataset, ViP-NeRF achieves a PSNR index 3-4 points higher than the latest paper in 2, 3, and 4 input views [S23].

#### Coarse-Fine Consistency Loss
A loss function that enforces consistency between depth estimates of the coarse and fine NeRF models to prevent ineffective hierarchical sampling and blur [S24]. The loss is defined as:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{cfc} = \mathbb{1}_{m_f=1} \odot \| z_c - \hat{\nabla}(z_f) \|^2 + \mathbb{1}_{m_c=1} \odot \| \hat{\nabla}(z_c) - z_f \|^2 $$
where $z_c$ and $z_f$ are coarse and fine depths, and $m_c/m_f$ are reliability masks determined by reprojection [S24].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{iters} \cdot N_{rand} \cdot N_{samples} \cdot D)$, where $N_{iters}$ is the number of iterations, $N_{rand}$ is the batch size (rays per step), $N_{samples}$ is the number of samples per ray, and $D$ is the MLP depth/width [S5, S14, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_{rand} \cdot N_{samples} \cdot D + \text{Model Size})$ [S5, unofficial]. VRAM usage is a primary constraint, often mitigated by chunking rays [S5, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Training typically requires 100,000 to 1,000,000 iterations [S11, S14, unofficial]. For a dataset of 199 images, training for 5 epochs with a batch size of 1024 results in ~39,000 total iterations [S17, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Scaling to large scenes requires decomposition methods like [MegaNeRF](8580-meganerf.md) or [BlockNeRF](8581-blocknerf.md) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Stochastic sampling allows training on high-resolution images without loading full images into memory [S5, S14, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow convergence due to the need for many iterations; sensitive to hyperparameters like learning rate and positional encoding frequencies [S5, S11, unofficial].

## Background
The NeRF Training Loop was introduced in the seminal paper "NeRF: Representing Scenes as Neural Radiance Fields for View Synthesis" by Mildenhall et al. (2020) [S5, S18, unofficial]. It established the paradigm of optimizing a coordinate MLP to represent a 3D scene by minimizing the photometric loss between rendered and observed images. The method has since been extended with various sampling strategies, architectural changes, and regularization techniques, as detailed in the subtopics.
