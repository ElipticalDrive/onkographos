# Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction

Also called: Hybrid Scene Representation Network, Neural RGB-D

## Overview
Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction is a method for high-quality 3D reconstruction of room-scale scenes from an RGB-D input sequence [S1]. It solves the problem of generating metric, clean triangle meshes from color and depth data, addressing the limitation of standard NeRF which produces noisy, semi-transparent geometry [S1]. It sets itself apart by replacing the density-based volumetric representation with a differentiable rendering formulation based on a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF), optimized jointly with camera poses [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** $N$ color frames $I_i$, corresponding aligned depth frames $D_i$, and initial camera poses $T_i$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized MLP parameters $\Theta$, per-frame latent codes, refined camera poses, and a triangle mesh extracted via Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Shape MLP ($F_\sigma$):**
    *   Input: Encoded 3D point $\gamma(\mathbf{p})$.
    *   Output: Truncated signed distance value $D_i$.
    *   Architecture: Multi-layer perceptron [S1].
2.  **Radiance MLP ($F_c$):**
    *   Inputs: Encoded view direction $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, intermediate feature vector from Shape MLP, and per-frame latent corrective code vector [S1].
    *   Output: Surface radiance $\mathbf{c}_i$.
    *   Architecture: Multi-layer perceptron [S1].
3.  **Deformation Field:**
    *   A 6-layer ReLU MLP acting as a residual to pixel location before unprojecting into a 3D ray [S1].
4.  **Camera Poses ($T_i$):**
    *   Represented with Euler angles and a translation vector for every frame [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Truncation distance ($tr$): 5 cm [S1].
*   Loss weights: $\lambda_1 = 0.1$ (RGB), $\lambda_2 = 10$ (Free-space), $\lambda_3 = 6 \times 10^3$ (Truncation) [S1].
*   Fine samples ($S'_f$): 16 [S1].
*   Coarse sampling rate ($S'_c$): One sample for every 1.5 cm of ray length [S1].
*   Ray length: 4 to 8 meters (must exceed largest scene distance) [S1].
*   Optimizer: ADAM, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   Training iterations: $2 \times 10^5$ [S1].
*   Batch size: 1024 randomly chosen rays per iteration [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** Initialize camera poses $T_i$ using BundleFusion [S1].
2.  **Positional Encoding:** Encode query point $\mathbf{p}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ using sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ [S1].
3.  **Ray Generation & Refinement:**
    *   For each pixel, retrieve 2D vector from the deformation field.
    *   Shift the ray origin/direction by this 2D vector.
    *   Transform to world space using camera pose $T_i$ [S1].
4.  **Coarse Sampling:** Generate $S'_c$ sample points on the ray using stratified sampling [S1].
5.  **Zero-Crossing Search:** Evaluate Shape MLP on coarse samples to find the zero-crossing of the signed distance field [S1].
6.  **Fine Sampling:** Generate $S'_f$ sample points around the identified zero-crossing [S1].
7.  **Forward Pass:** Concatenate MLP outputs from coarse and fine samples. Perform forward pass for all $S'_c + S'_f$ samples to get $D_i$ and $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
8.  **Weight Calculation:** Compute weight $w_i$ for each sample:
    $$w_i = \sigma \left( \frac{D_i}{tr} \right) \cdot \sigma \left( -\frac{D_i}{tr} \right)$$
    where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function [S1].
    *   *Pitfall:* If the coarse sampling rate is too low, samples may miss the truncation region, causing the zero-crossing to be missed [S1].
    *   *Edge Case:* Set weights of samples beyond the first truncation region to zero to account for multiple intersections [S1].
9.  **Color Integration:** Compute integrated color $\mathbf{C}$:
    $$\mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i} \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i$$
    where $K = S'_c + S'_f$ [S1].
10. **Loss Computation:** Compute global loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P})$:
    $$\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{b=0}^{B-1} \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P})$$
    *   RGB Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{rgb}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} (\mathbf{C}_p - \hat{\mathbf{C}}_p)^2$ [S1].
    *   Free-space Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{fs}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_p^{fs}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_p^{fs}} (D_s - tr)^2$, where $\mathcal{S}_p^{fs}$ are samples between camera origin and truncation region [S1].
    *   Truncation Loss: $\mathcal{L}_{tr}^b(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{1}{|P_b|} \sum_{p \in P_b} \frac{1}{|S_p^{tr}|} \sum_{s \in S_p^{tr}} (D_s - \hat{D}_s)^2$, where $S_p^{tr}$ are samples within the truncation region and $\hat{D}_s$ is observed signed distance [S1].
11. **Optimization:** Update network parameters $\Theta$, per-frame latent codes, camera poses $T_i$, and deformation field using ADAM [S1].
12. **Mesh Extraction:** At evaluation, use Marching Cubes with spatial resolution of 1 cm to extract mesh from the optimized signed distance function [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of MLP evaluations per ray ($S'_c + S'_f$) and the number of rays per iteration. Training runs for $2 \times 10^5$ iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Stores MLP parameters, per-frame latent codes, and camera poses. No large voxel grids or hash tables are used, unlike grid-based methods [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On 10 synthetic scenes, achieved Chamfer $\ell_1$ distance of 0.044, IoU of 0.747, Normal Consistency of 0.918, and F-score of 0.924 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces high-quality, metric 3D reconstructions with hard boundaries between occupied and free space, avoiding the noisy artifacts and semi-transparent matter common in density-based models like NeRF [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires RGB-D input. If coarse sampling rate is too low, the zero-crossing may be missed [S1].
*   **When to use:** When high-quality, clean mesh geometry is required from RGB-D data, rather than just novel view synthesis [S1].

## Background
Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction was proposed by Dejan Azinović, Ricardo Martin-Brualla, Dan B. Goldman, Matthias Nießner, and Justus Thies [S1]. It was presented at CVPR 2022 [S1]. The method builds on the NeRF framework [Parent] but reformulates the scene representation to use a truncated signed distance field (TSDF) instead of density, specifically tailored for geometry reconstruction from RGB-D sensors like Kinect [S1]. It is related to concurrent work such as NeuS [S1].
