# GIRAFFE

Also called: Compositional Generative Neural Feature Fields, GIRAFFE Scene Composition

## Overview
GIRAFFE represents scenes as a composition of $N$ distinct entities (objects and a background), where each entity possesses its own neural feature field and learnable affine transformation. This architecture enables explicit control over the pose, shape, and appearance of individual entities within a unified generative model. It differs from standard NeRF variants by decoupling scene composition from a single monolithic field, allowing for disentangled generation and manipulation of scene elements [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera pose $\xi$, number of entities $N$, latent shape codes $\mathbf{z}_s^i$, latent appearance codes $\mathbf{z}_a^i$, and transformation parameters $\mathbf{T}_i = \{\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{t}_i, \mathbf{R}_i\}$ for each entity $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.
*   **Outputs:** A synthesized RGB image $\hat{\mathbf{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Feature Field $h_\theta$:** An MLP with 8 layers, hidden dimension 128, and ReLU activations. It takes positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, directional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, and latent codes $\mathbf{z}_s, \mathbf{z}_a$ as input. It outputs density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and a feature vector $\mathbf{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{M_f}$.
    *   For the background entity ($i=N$), the MLP uses half the layers and hidden dimension.
    *   All object feature fields ($i < N$) share weights.
*   **Positional Encoding:**
    *   Position $\mathbf{x}$: $L_x = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 10 = 60$ dimensions.
    *   Direction $\mathbf{d}$: $L_d = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24$ dimensions.
*   **Feature Image $I_V$:** A low-resolution feature map of size $H_V \times W_V \times M_f$ (typically $16 \times 16$).
*   **Transformation Parameters:**
    *   $\mathbf{s}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Scale.
    *   $\mathbf{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Translation.
    *   $\mathbf{R}_i \in SO(3)$: Rotation matrix.
    *   For the background entity $N$, $\mathbf{s}_N$ and $\mathbf{t}_N$ are fixed to span the entire scene and center it at the origin.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Sample Entities and Parameters:**
    *   Sample the number of entities $N \sim p_N$.
    *   For each entity $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, sample latent codes $\mathbf{z}_s^i, \mathbf{z}_a^i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
    *   Sample camera pose $\xi \sim p_\xi$ (uniform distribution over dataset-dependent elevation angles).
    *   Sample transformations $\mathbf{T}_i \sim p_T$ (uniform distribution over valid object transformations).

2.  **Define Transformation Function:**
    *   Define the mapping from object space to scene space $k(\mathbf{x})$ for entity $i$:
        $$ k(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{R}_i \begin{bmatrix} s_{i,1} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & s_{i,2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & s_{i,3} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & s_{i,3} \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}_i $$
        Note: The source uses a $4 \times 4$ matrix representation where the last diagonal element is $s_{i,3}$, effectively scaling the homogeneous coordinate or treating the third dimension with the same scale as the second in the provided matrix structure [S1].

3.  **Evaluate Feature Fields:**
    *   For a query point $\mathbf{x}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ in scene space, compute the canonical coordinates for entity $i$:
        $$ \mathbf{x}' = k_i^{-1}(\mathbf{x}), \quad \mathbf{d}' = k_i^{-1}(\mathbf{d}) $$
    *   Compute density and feature for entity $i$:
        $$ (\sigma_i, \mathbf{f}_i) = h_\theta(\gamma(\mathbf{x}'), \gamma(\mathbf{d}'), \mathbf{z}_s^i, \mathbf{z}_a^i) $$

4.  **Compose Entities (Composition Operator):**
    *   Compute the total density $\sigma$ and the composite feature vector $\mathbf{f}$ at $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ by summing densities and taking the density-weighted mean of features:
        $$ \sigma = \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i $$
        $$ \mathbf{f} = \frac{1}{\sigma} \sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i \mathbf{f}_i $$
    *   If $\sigma = 0$, $\mathbf{f}$ is undefined or zero (implementation detail [G]).

5.  **Volume Render Feature Image:**
    *   For each pixel, cast a ray $\mathbf{d}$ with camera pose $\xi$.
    *   Sample $N_s = 64$ points $\{\mathbf{x}_j\}_{j=1}^{N_s}$ along the ray.
    *   Compute step sizes $\delta_j = \|\mathbf{x}_{j+1} - \mathbf{x}_j\|_2$.
    *   Compute alpha values $\alpha_j = 1 - e^{-\sigma_j \delta_j}$ and transmittance $\tau_j = \prod_{k=1}^{j-1} (1 - \alpha_k)$.
    *   Render the low-resolution feature image $I_V$ at resolution $16 \times 16$:
        $$ \mathbf{f}_{\text{pixel}} = \sum_{j=1}^{N_s} \tau_j \alpha_j \mathbf{f}_j $$

6.  **2D Neural Rendering (Upsampling):**
    *   Apply the neural rendering operator $\pi_\theta^{\text{neural}}$ to map $I_V \in \mathbb{R}^{H_V \times W_V \times M_f}$ to the final RGB image $\hat{\mathbf{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$.
    *   Architecture: A 2D CNN with leaky ReLU activations.
    *   Process: Combine nearest neighbor upsampling with $3 \times 3$ convolutions.
    *   Skip connections: Map the feature image to RGB at every spatial resolution via $3 \times 3$ convolutions and add to the previous output via bilinear upsampling.
    *   Final layer: Apply sigmoid activation to produce RGB values.
