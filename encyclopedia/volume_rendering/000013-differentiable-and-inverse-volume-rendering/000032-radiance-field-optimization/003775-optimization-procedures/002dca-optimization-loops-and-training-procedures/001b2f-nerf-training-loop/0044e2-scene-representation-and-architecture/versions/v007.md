# Scene Representation and Architecture
Also called: Neural Radiance Field Architecture, NeRF Backbone

## Overview
This family defines the neural network structures and spatial decomposition strategies used to approximate the continuous radiance field function $F_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ within the NeRF training loop [Parent]. It solves the problem of representing complex 3D geometry and appearance from 2D images by mapping 6D coordinates (3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and 2D viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$) to 4D output values (scalar density $\sigma$ and 3D color $\mathbf{c}$) using differentiable networks. It sets itself apart from [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) by defining *what* is being queried, and from [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md) by encompassing both the canonical MLP topology and the broader strategies of spatial partitioning and extended topologies.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the mechanism of mapping 6D input coordinates to 4D output values using a differentiable neural network, allowing the radiance field to be optimized end-to-end through the volumetric rendering integral.

**Input Representation:**
Raw coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ are rarely used directly due to the spectral bias of MLPs, which struggle to fit high-frequency details. They are mapped to a higher-dimensional space using positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ [S1, unofficial][S3]. For a coordinate $\mathbf{x}$, the encoding includes the original value and sinusoidal functions at increasing frequencies:
$$
\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = (\sin(2^0 \pi \mathbf{x}), \cos(2^0 \pi \mathbf{x}), \dots, \sin(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{x}), \cos(2^{L-1} \pi \mathbf{x}))
$$
where $L$ is the number of frequency bands [S1, unofficial][S3].

**Network Topology:**
The canonical implementation uses two coupled Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs): a density network $F_\sigma$ and a color network $F_c$ [S1, unofficial][S3].
1.  **Density Network:** Takes the encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ as input. It consists of $L$ hidden layers with ReLU activations and skip connections from the input to every 4th layer to preserve low-frequency information [S1, unofficial][S3]. The output is the density $\sigma$.
2.  **Color Network:** Takes the encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$, the raw viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, and the intermediate feature vector from the density network (usually the output of the last hidden layer before the density head) as input. It predicts the color $\mathbf{c}$ using a final ReLU or sigmoid activation [S1, unofficial][S3].

**Rendering Integration:**
During training, rays are sampled at points $\mathbf{x}_i$ along the ray. The MLPs predict $\sigma_i$ and $\mathbf{c}_i$. The pixel color $C(\mathbf{r})$ is computed via volumetric rendering:
$$
C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad \text{where } \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i), \quad T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
$$
and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1, unofficial][S3].

## Subtopics

### [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md)
Encompasses specific neural network topologies and architectural modifications used to represent radiance fields, extending the canonical two-MLP structure to address limitations in generalization, training speed, dynamic scene modeling, and generative capabilities. Choose when standard MLPs fail to capture high-frequency details or require excessive training time.

### [Scene Representation and Partitioning](9444-scene-representation-and-partitioning.md)
Addresses the scalability limits of monolithic NeRFs by decomposing the 3D scene volume or the training data into manageable sub-regions or components. Choose for large-scale static scenes, distributed training workloads, or dynamic environments requiring spatial or content-based decomposition.

### [Specific Model Architectures and Extensions](10278-specific-model-architectures-and-extensions.md)
Defines specific neural network topologies and input configurations for approximating radiance fields, primarily originating from the NSVF framework. Choose when explicit configuration of feature connections between density and texture networks or specific input encodings (appearance embeddings) are required.

## Background
The canonical two-MLP architecture was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in "NeRF: Representing Scenes as Neural Radiance Fields for View Synthesis" (2020) [S1, unofficial]. It established the standard of using positional encoding and coupled networks for density and color. Subsequent developments in partitioning and extended architectures emerged to address scalability and generalization issues in large-scale and dynamic scenes.
