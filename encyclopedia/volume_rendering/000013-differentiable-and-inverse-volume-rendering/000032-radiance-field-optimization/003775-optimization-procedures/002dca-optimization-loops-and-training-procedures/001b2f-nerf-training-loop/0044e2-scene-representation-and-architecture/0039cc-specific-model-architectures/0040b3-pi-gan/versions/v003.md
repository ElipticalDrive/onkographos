# pi-GAN

Also called: Periodic Implicit Generative Adversarial Networks, π-GAN

## Overview
pi-GAN is a generative adversarial network (GAN) that learns an unsupervised, view-consistent 3D representation of objects from unlabeled 2D images by conditioning a periodic implicit radiance field on a latent noise vector [S1]. It differs from standard NeRF-based optimization methods by treating the radiance field as a generative prior rather than a deterministic scene reconstruction, allowing for the synthesis of novel, high-quality 3D-aware images without explicit 3D supervision [S1]. Unlike GRAF, which concatenates noise codes, pi-GAN uses a StyleGAN-inspired mapping network with Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) to condition the network, and it employs a progressive growing strategy to stabilize training [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A latent noise vector $\mathbf{z} \sim p_z$ (typically from a standard normal distribution) and a camera pose $\xi$ (defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$).
*   **Output:** A synthesized 2D RGB image $I$ rendered from the camera pose $\xi$.

**Data Structures:**
The core representation is a SIREN-based implicit radiance field. The network parameters are divided into:
1.  **SIREN Backbone Parameters:** Weight matrices $\mathbf{W}_i$ and biases $\mathbf{b}_i$ for each layer $i$.
2.  **Mapping Network Parameters:** Weights and biases of a ReLU MLP that maps $\mathbf{z}$ to modulation parameters.
3.  **Modulation Parameters:** Frequencies $\gamma_i$ and phase shifts $\beta_i$ for each layer $i$, generated by the mapping network.
4.  **Head Parameters:** Weights $\mathbf{W}_\sigma, \mathbf{W}_c$ and biases $\mathbf{b}_\sigma, \mathbf{b}_c$ for density and color prediction.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Mapping Network (FiLM Conditioning):**
    The latent vector $\mathbf{z}$ is passed through a mapping network $M_\phi$, which is a standard ReLU MLP. This network outputs the frequency parameters $\gamma_i$ and phase shift parameters $\beta_i$ for every layer $i$ of the SIREN backbone [S1].
    $$
    \{\gamma_i, \beta_i\}_{i=0}^{n-1} = M_\phi(\mathbf{z})
    $$

2.  **SIREN Backbone Evaluation:**
    The 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ is processed through the SIREN backbone $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$. Unlike standard MLPs, each layer $i$ uses a sine activation function modulated by the parameters from Step 1 [S1]:
    $$
    \phi_i(\mathbf{x}_i) = \sin(\gamma_i \cdot (\mathbf{W}_i \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{b}_i) + \beta_i)
    $$
    where $\mathbf{x}_i$ is the input to layer $i$, $\mathbf{W}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{N_i \times M_i}$ is the weight matrix, and $\mathbf{b}_i$ is the bias. The output of the backbone is:
    $$
    \Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \phi_{n-1} \circ \phi_{n-2} \circ \dots \circ \phi_0(\mathbf{x})
    $$

3.  **Density and Color Prediction:**
    The density $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ and color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ are predicted using linear heads applied to the backbone output $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$ and the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1]:
    $$
    \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_\sigma \Phi(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{b}_\sigma
    $$
    $$
    \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) = \mathbf{W}_c \phi_c \left( [\Phi(\mathbf{x}), \mathbf{d}]^T \right) + \mathbf{b}_c
    $$
    Here, $\phi_c$ is typically a ReLU or sigmoid activation, and $[\cdot, \cdot]^T$ denotes concatenation.

4.  **Neural Volume Rendering:**
    Rays are cast from the camera origin $\mathbf{o}$ through each pixel. For a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, samples are taken at depths $t_i$ within the near $t_n$ and far $t_f$ bounds. The pixel color $C(\mathbf{r})$ is computed via standard volumetric rendering [S1]:
    $$
    C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad \text{where } \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i), \quad T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)
    $$
    where $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples $t_i$ and $t_{i+1}$.

5.  **Adversarial Training:**
    The rendered image is fed into a traditional convolutional discriminator $D$. The generator $G$ and discriminator $D$ are trained using the standard GAN minimax objective [S1].

6.  **Progressive Growing:**
    To accelerate training and handle the complexity of 3D GANs, a progressive growing strategy is used. The resolution of the output images and the capacity of the discriminator are increased gradually during training [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def pi_gan_forward(z, camera_pose, mapping_network, siren_backbone, density_head, color_head):
    """
    Generates a 2D image from latent z and camera pose.
    """
    # 1. Map latent to modulation parameters
    # mapping_network is a ReLU MLP
    gamma, beta = mapping_network(z) 
    
    # 2. Evaluate SIREN backbone at sampled 3D points x along rays
    # x shape: (N_samples, 3)
    x_encoded = x # Raw coordinates, no positional encoding in pi-GAN SIREN
    
    # Compute backbone output layer by layer
    h = x_encoded
    for i in range(num_layers):
        # Affine transform
        h = h @ backbone.W[i].T + backbone.b[i]
        # Sine activation with FiLM modulation
        h = torch.sin(gamma[i] * h + beta[i])
    
    phi_x = h
    
    # 3. Predict density and color
    # Density head
    sigma = phi_x @ density_head.W.T + density_head.b
    
    # Color head: concatenate backbone output and viewing direction d
    # d shape: (N_samples, 3)
    concat_input = torch.cat([phi_x, d], dim=-1)
    color = color_head.activation(concat_input @ color_head.W.T + color_head.b)
    
    # 4. Volume Rendering
    # Assume rays, depths, and deltas are pre-computed for the camera pose
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
    T = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha, dim=0) # Cumulative product of transmittance
    # Add 1.0 at the start for T_0
    T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[:, :1]), T[:, :-1]], dim=1)
    
    pixel_color = torch.sum(T * alpha.unsqueeze(-1) * color, dim=0)
    
    return pixel_color

def pi_gan_training_step(real_images, z, camera_pose, generator, discriminator):
    """
    Single training step for pi-GAN.
    """
    # Generate fake images
    fake_images = pi_gan_forward(z, camera_pose, generator.mapping, generator.backbone, 
                                 generator.density_head, generator.color_head)
    
    # Discriminator loss
    d_real = discriminator(real_images)
    d_fake = discriminator(fake_images.detach())
    d_loss = -torch.mean(d_real) + torch.mean(d_fake)
    
    # Generator loss
    d_fake_gen = discriminator(fake_images)
    g_loss = -torch.mean(d_fake_gen)
    
    return d_loss, g_loss
```

**Parameters:**
*   $\mathbf{z}$: Latent noise vector. Dimension not specified in sources, typically 512 or 1024 in StyleGAN-inspired architectures [S1].
*   $\gamma_i$: Frequency parameter for layer $i$. Output by mapping network [S1].
*   $\beta_i$: Phase shift parameter for layer $i$. Output by mapping network [S1].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes for volume rendering [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Local Overfitting:** SIREN networks are prone to local overfitting to the last batch if sufficient coverage of the 3D space is not maintained. This is why pi-GAN does not use a patch-based discriminator (unlike GRAF) [S1].
*   **No Positional Encoding:** Unlike standard NeRF, pi-GAN's SIREN backbone does not use positional encoding on the input coordinates $\mathbf{x}$; it relies on the periodic activation functions and FiLM conditioning to capture high frequencies [S1].

## Subtopics

### GRAF (Generative Radiance Fields)
A predecessor that also uses implicit radiance fields for 3D-aware generation but conditions the MLP via concatenation of noise codes rather than FiLM. GRAF uses a patch-based discriminator [S1].

### StyleGAN
The 2D GAN architecture that inspired pi-GAN's mapping network and progressive growing strategy [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training is computationally intensive due to the need to render images via volume rendering for every batch. Progressive growing helps mitigate this by starting with low resolutions [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The SIREN backbone is memory-efficient compared to voxel-based representations, but the need to store and compute gradients for the entire network during backpropagation through the renderer can be significant [S1].
*   **Performance:** pi-GAN achieves state-of-the-art results for 3D-aware image synthesis on datasets like CelebA, Cats, and CARLA [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces view-consistent images, allows for 3D proxy shape extraction via marching cubes, and does not require 3D supervision [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The extracted 3D proxy shapes are approximations and may not match the quality of methods with explicit shape supervision [S1]. SIREN training can be unstable without careful conditioning and progressive growing [S1].

## Background
pi-GAN was introduced in 2021 by Tero Karras et al. in the paper "pi-GAN: Periodic Implicit Generative Adversarial Networks for 3D-Aware Image Synthesis" [S1]. It builds upon the success of StyleGAN in 2D image generation and the concept of neural radiance fields (NeRF) for 3D representation. The key innovation is the use of periodic activation functions (SIREN) combined with FiLM conditioning to create a generative model that learns a 5D spatio-angular radiance field from 2D images alone [S1].
