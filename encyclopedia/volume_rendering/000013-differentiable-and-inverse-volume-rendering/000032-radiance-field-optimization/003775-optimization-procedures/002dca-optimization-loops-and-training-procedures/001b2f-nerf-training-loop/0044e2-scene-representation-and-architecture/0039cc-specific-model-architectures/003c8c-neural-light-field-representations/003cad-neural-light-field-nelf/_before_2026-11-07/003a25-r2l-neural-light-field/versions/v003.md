# R2L (Neural Light Field)

Also called: Neural Light Field, R2L network

## Overview
R2L (R2L) is a deep neural light field network designed for efficient novel view synthesis (NVS) by distilling knowledge from a pre-trained NeRF teacher model [S1]. It solves the trade-off between rendering quality and computational cost by replacing the standard shallow NeRF MLP with a significantly deeper architecture (depth 88) that utilizes extensive residual blocks [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on ground-truth depth or voxelization, R2L achieves 28–31× wall-time speedup over NeRF with reduced FLOPs while maintaining or exceeding the teacher's rendering quality, particularly on reflective surfaces where depth-based methods fail [S1].

## How it works
R2L learns an implicit radiance field $f_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ using a deep residual network. The core innovation is the architectural shift from width to depth and the use of pseudo-data distillation to prevent overfitting in the deep network [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** 3D position $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$ and emitted color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

### Data Structures
The network is a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with specific structural constraints:
*   **Architecture:** Width $W$ and Depth $D$. Two configurations are cited: W256D88 and W181D88 [S1].
*   **Layers:** The network consists of 88 linear layers (depth 88) [S1].
*   **Residual Blocks:** Extensive residual connections are inserted in the internal layers. The first layer takes $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ as input. Subsequent layers apply linear transformations followed by ReLU activations, with residual connections added to the output of each block [S1].
*   **Output Layer:** The final layer outputs $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ without activation [S1].

### Training Procedure
The training process involves distillation from a pre-trained NeRF teacher and hard-example mining.

1.  **Teacher Initialization:** A standard NeRF model is trained on the original captured images. The original NeRF uses a batch size of 1,024, initial learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$, and trains for 200k iterations [S1].
2.  **Pseudo Data Generation:** Rays are sampled from the pre-trained NeRF teacher to generate pseudo-training images. The number of pseudo images $S$ is typically 10,000 [S1].
3.  **Network Initialization:** The R2L network is initialized with width $W \in \{181, 256\}$ and depth $D=88$ [S1].
4.  **Batch Sampling:** For each training step, a batch of $B = 98,304$ rays is randomly sampled. These rays are sampled from different images to ensure they do not share the same origin [S1].
5.  **Hard-Example Selection:** A fraction $r$ of the rays in the batch are selected based on their prediction error (or PSNR) from the previous iteration. These "hard examples" are prioritized for optimization. The default ratio is $r=0.2$ [S1].
6.  **Forward Pass:** The R2L network predicts $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ for the sampled points.
7.  **Loss Calculation:** The loss is computed between the R2L predictions and the ground-truth colors (from real images) or teacher predictions (from pseudo images). The standard NeRF rendering equation is used for volume integration [S1]:
    $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right), \quad \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i) $$
8.  **Optimization:** The network parameters are updated using the Adam optimizer with the same learning rate schedule as the teacher NeRF. Training runs for 1,000k iterations [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Batch Size:** 98,304 rays per batch [S1].
*   **Hard Example Ratio ($r$):** Fraction of hard examples per batch. Default 0.2. Tested values $\{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ show no significant difference between 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 [S1].
*   **Pseudo Sample Size ($S$):** Number of pseudo images. Default 10,000. Performance saturates around 5,000 images [S1].
*   **Learning Rate:** Same schedule as the teacher NeRF ($5 \times 10^{-4}$ initial, decaying) [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 1,000,000 (1,000k) [S1].
*   **Hardware:** 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs [S1].
*   **Framework:** PyTorch 1.9 [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Overfitting:** The deep network (D88) is prone to overfitting if trained only on the original captured images (e.g., 11 images). Pseudo data is critical to prevent this [S1].
*   **Trainability:** Without residual blocks, the deep network is "barely trainable" [S1].
*   **Reflective Surfaces:** Unlike depth-based methods (e.g., DONeRF), R2L does not rely on ground-truth depth, allowing it to handle reflective surfaces (water, mirrors) correctly [S1].

## Subtopics

### Distillation from NeRF
R2L uses a pre-trained NeRF as a teacher to generate pseudo-data. This allows the deep R2L network to learn from a larger dataset than the original captures, mitigating overfitting. [Link to [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md)]

### Hard-Example Training
A strategy to select a fraction of rays with high reconstruction error in each batch, focusing the optimization on fine-grained details. [Link to [Specific Model Architectures](8585-specific-model-architectures.md)]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The R2L network has significantly fewer FLOPs than NeRF. For W181D88, FLOPs are reduced to ~6.00 M compared to NeRF's 211.42 M (a ~35× reduction) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Storage is 23.7 MB for the R2L network, compared to 2.4 MB for the teacher NeRF (due to the larger number of parameters in the deep network) [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   On NeRF synthetic dataset: PSNR 31.87 (+1.40 over teacher), SSIM 0.9950, LPIPS 0.0340 [S1].
    *   On DONeRF synthetic dataset: PSNR 35.45 (+2.78 over teacher), FLIP 0.047 [S1].
*   **Speed:** 28–31× faster than NeRF and 2× faster than DONeRF-16-noGT on GeForce 2080Ti, Tesla V100, and CPU benchmarks [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High speed, good quality on reflective surfaces, no need for ground-truth depth.
*   **Limitations:** Blurry parts in NeRF results may also appear in R2L results as it learns from the teacher. Requires significant pseudo-data generation.

## Background
R2L was introduced by H. Wang et al. in the paper "R2L: Distilling Neural Radiance Field to Neural Light Field for Efficient Novel View Synthesis" [S1]. It addresses the high computational cost of NeRF by proposing a deep neural light field network. The work builds on the premise that trading width for depth in the MLP architecture, combined with residual connections and abundant pseudo-data, can achieve better performance with lower FLOPs [S1].
