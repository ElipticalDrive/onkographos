# KiloNeRF

Also called: KiloNeRF: Speeding up Neural Radiance Fields with Thousands of Tiny MLPs

## Overview
KiloNeRF is a method that decomposes a large, deep Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) into a set of small, shallow NeRFs arranged in a 3D grid, using a teacher-student training paradigm to distill knowledge from a standard NeRF [S1][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like Mega-NeRF by explicitly optimizing for inference speed through the use of thousands of tiny MLPs and a cached sparse grid for surface localization, achieving acceleration of up to three orders of magnitude compared to the original NeRF [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A pre-trained standard NeRF (the "teacher") representing the scene; camera poses and pixel coordinates for training views.
*   **Output:** A set of lightweight MLPs (the "students") arranged in a 3D grid, and a cached sparse 3D grid structure for surface localization.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **3D Grid of MLPs:** The scene volume is partitioned into a regular 3D grid. Each cell in the grid contains a small, shallow MLP. The sources do not specify the exact grid resolution or the MLP architecture (e.g., number of layers/hidden units) [G: Typically, these are significantly smaller than the original NeRF, e.g., 1-2 hidden layers with 64-128 units, but exact numbers are not given in the sources].
2.  **Cached Sparse 3D Grid:** A data structure used to quickly locate the surface geometry along a ray, allowing the renderer to skip empty space [S3, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Teacher Training:** Train a standard, deep NeRF on the scene data to serve as the teacher model [S1].
2.  **Student Initialization:** Initialize a set of lightweight MLPs arranged in a 3D grid covering the scene volume [S1][S3, unofficial].
3.  **Distillation:** Distill the knowledge from the standard NeRF into the set of lightweight MLPs. This involves training the student MLPs to predict the same density and color as the teacher NeRF for points within their respective grid cells [S1].
4.  **Surface Localization Setup:** Construct a cached sparse 3D grid to approximate the scene geometry, enabling fast ray-surface intersection or empty space skipping [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Rendering:** For a given ray:
    a. Use the cached sparse 3D grid to identify the relevant region of space where the surface lies [S3, unofficial].
    b. Sample points along the ray within this localized region.
    c. Query the specific lightweight MLP corresponding to the grid cell containing each sample point to predict density and color [S1][S2, unofficial].
    d. Perform volume rendering integration using the sampled densities and colors [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The rendering equation remains the standard volume rendering integral, but the density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ are predicted by the student MLP $\mathcal{M}_{cell}$ associated with the grid cell containing the sample point $\mathbf{x}$:
$$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt $$
where $\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \mathcal{M}_{cell}(\mathbf{r}(t))_{\sigma}$ and $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) = \mathcal{M}_{cell}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d})_{\mathbf{c}}$. The specific form of $\mathcal{M}_{cell}$ is not detailed in the sources [G: Typically a small MLP with ReLU activations].

**Parameters:**
*   Grid resolution: Not given in the sources.
*   MLP size: Not given in the sources, but described as "tiny" and "shallow" [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Memory Overhead:** Deploying a large number of MLPs incurs substantial memory overhead [S1].
*   **Dynamic Parameter Loading:** During rendering, a ray passes through different sets of MLPs, each requiring different numbers of samples. This dynamic parameter loading requires special CUDA kernels for proper speedup, as static loading is inefficient [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Teacher-Student Distillation for NeRFs
A training paradigm where a complex model's output is used to supervise a simpler model. KiloNeRF uses this to transfer knowledge from a deep NeRF to shallow MLPs. [Link to Distillation methods if available, otherwise link to Parent](9326-sparse-and-efficient-representations.md).

### Sparse Grids for Ray Tracing
Using a sparse data structure to accelerate ray traversal by skipping empty space. KiloNeRF employs a cached sparse 3D grid for surface localization. [Link to Sparse Radiance Field](8876-sparse-radiance-field-srf-representation.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Up to three orders of magnitude faster than the original NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Substantial memory overhead due to the deployment of thousands of MLPs [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significant inference speedup; efficient representation of local scene details through shallow MLPs.
*   **Limitations:** High memory usage; requires specialized rendering kernels to handle dynamic parameter loading efficiently [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When real-time or near-real-time rendering of large scenes is required, and memory is not the primary constraint.
*   **When not to use:** When memory footprint is critical, or when the scene has global dependencies that shallow local MLPs cannot capture.

## Background
KiloNeRF was introduced by Reiser et al. in their paper "KiloNeRF: Speeding up Neural Radiance Fields with Thousands of Tiny MLPs" presented at ICCV 2021 [S1][S3, unofficial]. It was developed to address the computational inefficiency of standard NeRFs, which sample the entire ray even though only a small fraction of samples contribute to the final image [S1]. The method builds on the principle of spatial decomposition and teacher-student distillation [S1][S2, unofficial].
