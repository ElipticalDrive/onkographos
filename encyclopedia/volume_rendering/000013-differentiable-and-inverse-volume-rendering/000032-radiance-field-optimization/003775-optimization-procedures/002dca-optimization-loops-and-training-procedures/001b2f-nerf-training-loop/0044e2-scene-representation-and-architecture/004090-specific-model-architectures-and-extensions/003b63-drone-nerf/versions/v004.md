# Drone-NeRF


## Overview
Drone-NeRF is a specialized radiance-field optimization method for reconstructing large-scale 3D scenes from aerial drone imagery, addressing challenges such as unbounded space representation, varying camera exposure, and shadow artifacts inherent in overhead views [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF variants by employing a block-parallel training strategy with spatial contraction, appearance embeddings for exposure balancing, and a post-training merging process that explicitly mitigates shadow occlusions from vertical bounding box faces [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of drone-captured RGB images with known or estimated camera poses, intrinsics, and extrinsics.
*   **Outputs:** A merged radiance field model capable of rendering novel views (RGB and depth) of the large-scale scene, free from vertical-face shadow artifacts.

**Data Structures and Layout:**
The core representation is a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.
*   **Position Encoding:** Raw coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ are encoded using sinusoidal positional encoding with a highest frequency of 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding:** Viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ are encoded using sinusoidal positional encoding with a highest frequency of 4 [S1].
*   **Appearance Embeddings:** A learnable embedding vector is concatenated to the input to handle exposure discrepancies between images [S1].
*   **MLP Architecture:** The network consists of fully connected ReLU layers. The main body has 8 hidden layers with 256 hidden units each. The final output head consists of 2 layers with 128 channels [S1].
*   **Initial Stage Optimization:** For rapid query scenarios during early training, a hash coding structure is used in conjunction with a small fusion MLP [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Scene Subdivision:** The large-scale scene is divided into multiple sub-bounding boxes (sub-blocks) [S1].
2.  **Block Parallel Training:** Each sub-block is trained independently [S1].
    *   **Sampling:** Hierarchical sampling is used with 128 coarse samples and 256 fine samples per ray [S1].
    *   **Batching:** 1024 rays are sampled per batch [S1].
    *   **Optimization:** The Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.005 [S1].
    *   **Pose Optimization:** Camera poses are optimized by back-propagating the loss gradient into the input pose computation, using a specific camera-optimized learning rate of 0.0005 [S1].
    *   **Iteration:** Each block is trained for either 5,000 or 30,000 iterations to prevent over-fitting [S1].
3.  **Shadow Mitigation via Expansion:** To address shadows cast by the vertical faces of the sub-bounding boxes during rendering:
    *   An expanded sub-bounding box is created, larger than the original sub-bounding box [S1].
    *   RGB values are captured from the projection areas generated by ray irradiation on the vertical faces of the smaller box, using the model from the expanded box [S1].
    *   These captured RGB values replace the shadow occlusion regions in the original rendering [S1].
4.  **Merging Essential Blocks:**
    *   The number of sub-bounding boxes required for merging is determined by calculating intersections between the rays of the rendering area and the sub-bounding boxes [S1].
    *   Only essential sub-bounding boxes are used for rendering to reduce computation [S1].
    *   The rendering is constrained by a bounding box of identical size to the previously divided one [S1].
5.  **Color and Depth Uniformization:**
    *   **Color:** In overlapping areas of the enlarged sub-bounding boxes, the scene color is uniformized [S1].
    *   **Depth:** For depth rendering, depths falling within the scope of the previous larger sub-bounding box are employed [S1].
    *   **Depth Adjustment:** In overlapping regions, depths from each sub-bounding box are averaged. The overall depth of each sub-bounding box is adjusted by comparing these depth value averages to ensure smoother continuity [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Volumetric Rendering:** The pixel color $C(\mathbf{r})$ for a ray $\mathbf{r}$ is computed as:
    $$
    C(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^N T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i
    $$
    where $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity, $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the transmittance, $\sigma_i$ is the density, $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color, and $\delta_i$ is the distance between samples [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Pose Optimization:** Camera poses are updated by back-propagating the loss gradient $\nabla L$ into the pose parameters $P$:
    $$
    P \leftarrow P - \eta_{pose} \nabla_P L
    $$
    where $\eta_{pose} = 0.0005$ is the camera-optimized learning rate [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **MLP Hidden Layers:** 8 layers of 256 units [S1].
*   **Output Layers:** 2 layers of 128 channels [S1].
*   **Position Encoding Max Frequency:** 64 [S1].
*   **Direction Encoding Max Frequency:** 4 [S1].
*   **Coarse Samples:** 128 per ray [S1].
*   **Fine Samples:** 256 per ray [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 1024 rays [S1].
*   **Adam Learning Rate:** 0.005 [S1].
*   **Camera Pose Learning Rate:** 0.0005 [S1].
*   **Training Iterations:** 5,000 and 30,000 per block [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Over-fitting:** High iteration counts can lead to over-fitting; therefore, training is limited to 5,000 or 30,000 iterations [S1].
*   **Shadow Artifacts:** Vertical faces of sub-bounding boxes create shadows that must be explicitly handled via the expansion and replacement step [S1].
*   **Noise Above Camera:** Excessive noise above the camera's shooting position can interfere with scene construction; the method mitigates this by using bounding boxes to constrain rendering [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the Building Scene (Mill 19 dataset), Drone-NeRF achieves an average PSNR of 17.401 after 5,000 iterations, outperforming MipNeRF (16.637) and Instant-NGP (17.179) [S1]. It also outperforms these baselines in SSIM and LPIPS metrics [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Evaluated on Ubuntu 20.04, Nvidia 4070ti GPU (12GB VRAM), Intel i5-13600KF CPU, 32GB RAM [S1].
*   **Efficiency:** The use of hash coding in the initial stage and the selection of only essential sub-bounding boxes for merging reduce computation requirements [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The method requires a post-training merging step to handle large-scale scenes and explicitly addresses shadow artifacts which are common in drone-captured data [S1].

## Background
Drone-NeRF was introduced to address the specific challenges of 3D scene reconstruction from large-scale drone surveys, such as the Mill 19 dataset [S1]. It builds upon the NeRF framework but adapts it for aerial imagery by incorporating exposure balancing, pose optimization, and shadow mitigation techniques [S1].
