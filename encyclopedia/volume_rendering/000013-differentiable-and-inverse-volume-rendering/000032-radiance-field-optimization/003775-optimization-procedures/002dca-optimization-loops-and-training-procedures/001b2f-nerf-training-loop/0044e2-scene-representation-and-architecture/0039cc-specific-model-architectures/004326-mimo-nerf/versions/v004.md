# MIMO-NeRF

Also called: MIMO-NeRF-distill, MIMO-NeRF-naive

## Overview
MIMO-NeRF is a neural rendering method that accelerates inference by replacing the standard Single-Input Single-Output (SISO) Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with a Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) MLP [S1]. It solves the problem of high computational cost in NeRF by processing groups of $N_p$ samples simultaneously, thereby reducing the number of MLP evaluations (# Run) per ray [S1]. Unlike [AdaNeRF](6195-adanerf.md) which adapts sampling density, or [Sparse and Efficient Representations](9326-sparse-and-efficient-representations.md) which use voxel grids, MIMO-NeRF maintains the original NeRF hyperparameters (sample counts and network width) but groups spatially neighboring samples to share computation [S1]. It is distinguished by its self-supervised training objective, which resolves the ambiguity in density and color inherent in grouped outputs, outperforming naive or distillation-based variants [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
The method takes $N$ samples along a ray. It groups $N_p$ neighboring samples into a single group, creating $N/N_p$ groups total. The MIMO function $f_{\text{MIMO}}$ takes the positions of these $N_p$ samples and the view direction as input, and outputs the RGB colors and volume densities for all $N_p$ samples [S1].

**Data Structures and Layout:**
The network architecture is identical to the baseline NeRF [Parent] except for input/output dimensions [S1].
*   **Input:** A batch of $N_p$ positions $\mathbf{x}_i, \dots, \mathbf{x}_j$ (where $j = i + N_p - 1$) and a single view direction $\mathbf{d}$.
*   **Encoding:** Each position $\mathbf{x}$ is encoded using positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ [Parent]. Since there are $N_p$ positions, the total input dimension for positions is $N_p \times \text{dim}(\gamma(\mathbf{x}))$. The direction $\mathbf{d}$ is encoded as $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$ [S1].
*   **Network:** An 8-layer MLP with ReLU activation and 256 hidden units per layer [S1]. A skip connection incorporates the encoded position into the 5th layer [S1].
*   **Output:** The MLP outputs a vector that is split into density $\sigma$ and color features. The color features are concatenated with $\gamma(\mathbf{d})$, passed through a 1-layer MLP (ReLU, 128 units), and finally a linear layer to produce RGB $\mathbf{c}$ [S1]. This architecture produces $N_p$ densities and $N_p$ colors [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Grouping:** For a ray with $N$ samples, divide them into $N/N_p$ groups of $N_p$ samples each, starting from the sample nearest to the camera [S1].
2.  **Reformulation (Training Only):** To handle the increased input dimensionality without exploding parameter count, the MIMO MLP is reformulated into $M$ parallel SISO-like MLPs with scaling factors $R_m$ [S1].
    *   For $N_p=2$: $M=2$, $R_1=1, R_2=1$.
    *   For $N_p=4$: $M=2$, $R_1=1, R_2=2$.
    *   For $N_p=8$: $M=3$, $R_1=1, R_2=2, R_3=4$ [S1].
    *   Group Shifts (GS) are applied to all reformulated MLPs during training [S1].
3.  **Forward Pass:** Compute $f_{\text{MIMO}}(\mathbf{x}_i, \dots, \mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\mathbf{c}_i, \dots, \mathbf{c}_j, \sigma_i, \dots, \sigma_j)$ [S1].
4.  **Rendering:** Apply standard volume rendering integration using the predicted $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
5.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MIMO}}$ [S1].

**Formulas:**
The MIMO function maps grouped positions and direction to outputs [S1]:
$$
f_{\text{MIMO}} : (\mathbb{R}^3)^{N_p} \times \mathbb{S}^2 \rightarrow (\mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R}^+)^{N_p}, \quad (\mathbf{x}_i, \dots, \mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{d}) \mapsto (\mathbf{c}_i, \dots, \mathbf{c}_j, \sigma_i, \dots, \sigma_j)
$$
where $i \in \{1, 1+N_p, \dots, 1+N-N_p\}$ and $j = i + N_p - 1$ [S1].

The inference cost metric, number of MLP runs per pixel (# Run), is [S1]:
$$
\# \text{Run} = \frac{N_c}{N_p} + \frac{N_c + N_f}{N_p}
$$
where $N_c$ is the number of coarse samples and $N_f$ is the number of fine samples [S1]. In baseline NeRF, $N_p=1$ [S1].

The full training objective is [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MIMO}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{pixel}}^{\text{MIMO}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{3D}}
$$
where $\lambda$ balances the pixel-wise loss and the 3D consistency loss [S1].

The 3D consistency loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{3D}}$ addresses ambiguity in individual point predictions by enforcing consistency between different reformulated MLPs (or views) [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{3D}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{color 3D}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{alpha 3D}}
$$
where "sg" denotes the stop-gradient operation [S1].

Asymmetric weighting $\mu_{mj}^{mi}$ is used to weight the consistency loss, assuming higher scaling factors $R_m$ imply lower ambiguity and higher reliability [S1]:
$$
\mu_{mj}^{mi} = \frac{\sqrt{m_j} \sqrt{R_{\max}}}{\sqrt{R_{m_i}}}
$$
where $R_{\max} = \max_{m \in \{1, \dots, M\}} R_m$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $N_p$: Number of grouped samples. Typical values: 2, 4, 8 [S1].
*   $N_c, N_f$: Number of coarse and fine samples. Blender: $N_c=64, N_f=128$. LLFF: $N_c=64, N_f=64$ [S1].
*   $\lambda$: Weight for 3D consistency loss. Blender: 1. LLFF: 0.4 [S1].
*   $R_m$: Scaling factors for reformulated MLPs (see Reformulation step) [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def mimo_nerf_forward(positions, direction, network_params):
    """
    positions: Tensor of shape (N_p, 3)
    direction: Tensor of shape (3,)
    network_params: Parameters for the 8-layer MLP
    Returns: colors (N_p, 3), densities (N_p,)
    """
    # 1. Positional Encoding
    # Encode each of the N_p positions
    encoded_positions = []
    for x in positions:
        # gamma(x) includes sin/cos at increasing frequencies
        enc = positional_encoding(x) 
        encoded_positions.append(enc)
    
    # Concatenate encoded positions: shape (N_p * dim_enc,)
    x_concat = torch.cat(encoded_positions, dim=0)
    
    # Encode direction
    d_enc = positional_encoding(direction)
    
    # 2. MLP Forward Pass
    # Input: x_concat concatenated with d_enc (or processed via specific MIMO architecture)
    # The architecture is an 8-layer MLP with 256 hidden units, ReLU, skip connection to 5th layer
    output = mlp_forward(x_concat, d_enc, network_params)
    
    # 3. Output Parsing
    # Split output into density and color features
    densities = output[:, 0] # Assuming specific layout or linear head for sigma
    color_features = output[:, 1:]
    
    # Color branch: concatenate with d_enc, pass through 1-layer MLP (128 units), then linear to RGB
    color_input = torch.cat([color_features, d_enc], dim=-1)
    color_head_output = color_mlp_forward(color_input, network_params.color_params)
    colors = linear_layer(color_head_output)
    
    return colors, densities

def train_step(rays, target_images, mimo_network, lambda_3d):
    """
    rays: List of rays, each containing positions (N, 3) and directions (3,)
    target_images: Ground truth RGB images
    mimo_network: The MIMO-NeRF model
    lambda_3d: Weight for 3D consistency loss
    """
    pixel_loss = 0.0
    consistency_loss = 0.0
    
    for ray in rays:
        # Group samples into N_p groups
        # Example: N=128, N_p=2 -> 64 groups
        groups = group_samples(ray.positions, N_p=2)
        
        all_colors = []
        all_densities = []
        
        for group_positions in groups:
            # Forward pass for the group
            colors, densities = mimo_nerf_forward(group_positions, ray.direction, mimo_network.params)
            all_colors.append(colors)
            all_densities.append(densities)
            
        # Stack results
        # Note: In practice, rendering is done per group or the whole ray is processed
        # Here we assume standard volume rendering is applied to the reconstructed ray
        rendered_color = volume_rendering(all_colors, all_densities, ray.depths)
        
        # Pixel Loss
        pixel_loss += mse_loss(rendered_color, ray.target_color)
        
        # 3D Consistency Loss (Self-Supervised)
        # Compare predictions from different reformulated MLPs or augmented views
        # This requires accessing the internal reformulated heads if using distillation/self-supervised mode
        consistency_loss += compute_3d_consistency(all_densities, all_colors, mimo_network.reformulated_heads)
        
    total_loss = pixel_loss + lambda_3d * consistency_loss
    total_loss.backward()
    return total_loss
```
*[G] The pseudocode assumes standard PyTorch-like tensor operations. The `group_samples` function splits the ray's samples into contiguous chunks of size $N_p$. The `volume_rendering` function implements the standard alpha-compositing formula from the Parent article. The `compute_3d_consistency` function implements the asymmetric weighted loss between reformulated heads as described in the source.*

## Subtopics

### MIMO-TensoRF
Application of MIMO-NeRF to the TensoRF architecture [S1]. It improves inference and training speed without deteriorating image quality by selecting appropriate $N_p$ [S1].
*   **Parameters:** $N_p$ is the number of parallel rays/inputs. For Blender, $N_p \le 2$ retains quality. For LLFF, $N_p \le 4$ achieves comparable quality [S1]. Tensor rank parameters $R_\sigma, R_c$ vary (e.g., $R_\sigma=16, R_c=48$) [S1].
*   **Comparison:** MIMO-TensoRF improves I-time and T-time of original TensoRFs with similar image quality when $N_p$ is within the limits mentioned above [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The number of MLP runs (# Run) is reduced by a factor of $N_p$. Specifically, $\# \text{Run} = N_c/N_p + (N_c+N_f)/N_p$ [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Parameter count increases slightly. For $N_p=2$, parameters increase by 1.06x; $N_p=4$, 1.17x; $N_p=8$, 1.39x [S1]. This is because the total dimension of the encoded position $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ increases by $N_p$ times, but the network weights do not scale $N_p$ times [S1].
*   **Performance:** On Blender ($N_p=2$), MIMO-NeRF-self achieves PSNR 31.26, SSIM 0.953, LPIPS 0.054, Inference Time 5.15s, Training Time 5.36h [S1]. On LLFF ($N_p=2$), PSNR 27.70, SSIM 0.870, LPIPS 0.155, Inference Time 4.55s, Training Time 3.97h [S1].
*   **FLOPs:** MIMO-NeRF with $N_p=2$ reduces FLOPs from 303.82 (NeRF) to 160.33. With $N_p=8$, FLOPs drop to 52.72 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Superior speed-quality trade-off compared to NeRF-few (reduced samples) and NeRF-small (reduced width) at comparable FLOPs [S1]. Maintains original NeRF hyperparameters [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Ambiguity in color and density for individual points if not regularized by 3D consistency loss [S1]. Asymmetric weights are disabled for $N_p=2$ because $R_1=R_2=1$ [S1].

## Background
MIMO-NeRF was introduced in the paper "MIMO-NeRF: Fast Neural Rendering with Multi-input Multi-output Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. It addresses the requirement for high-capacity models and extra modules in fast NeRFs by replacing the SISO MLP with a MIMO MLP [S1]. It was evaluated on the Blender and LLFF datasets [S1].
