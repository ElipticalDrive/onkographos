# Specific Model Architectures

Also called: NeRF Architecture Variants, Radiance Field Network Designs

## Overview
This family encompasses modifications to the core Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) architecture of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to address specific limitations such as rendering speed, sparse input quality, geometric ambiguity, and high-frequency detail recovery. While the standard NeRF architecture uses a single MLP to map 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color, these variants introduce structural changes including network splitting, depth expansion, convolutional post-processing, and explicit geometric constraints. They differ from sibling articles on training loops and sampling by focusing on the internal representation and connectivity of the neural network itself.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the fundamental premise of learning an implicit radiance field $f_\Theta: (\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \rightarrow (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$, where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a 3D position, $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a viewing direction, $\sigma$ is volume density, and $\mathbf{c}$ is emitted color. The rendering process remains volume integration along rays:
$$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right), \quad \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i) $$
However, the mapping $f_\Theta$ is altered. Common modifications include:
1.  **Input Fusion:** Integrating auxiliary information (e.g., depth priors, feature maps) into the MLP layers, often via concatenation or additive fusion at each layer [S1, unofficial][S3].
2.  **Output Expansion:** Moving beyond simple RGB output to include feature channels, SDF values, or latent codes that are processed by subsequent networks (e.g., CNNs or secondary MLPs) [S1, unofficial][S3].
3.  **Structural Depth/Width Trade-offs:** Adjusting the number of layers and neurons to balance expressivity and computational cost, often employing residual connections to facilitate training of deeper networks [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Geometric Priors:** Explicitly encoding geometric constraints (e.g., via SDFs or epipolar geometry) into the network's loss function or input representation to resolve ambiguities inherent in implicit representations [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [AdaNeRF](6195-adanerf.md)
AdaNeRF addresses the computational cost of standard NeRF by splitting the network into a lightweight Sampling Network ($N_{sample}$) and a heavier Shading Network ($N_{shade}$). $N_{sample}$ predicts importance weights for fixed sample positions, allowing the system to select only the most significant samples for shading. This enables real-time rendering by reducing the number of queries to the expensive $N_{shade}$. It is chosen when real-time performance is critical and a pre-trained NeRF is available for initialization.

### [SfMNeRF](8664-sfmnerf.md)
SfMNeRF improves NeRF under sparse input conditions by integrating Structure-from-Motion (SfM) constraints. It uses matched SIFT features and epipolar geometry to explicitly constrain 3D geometry, reducing ambiguity. Unlike standard NeRF, it employs a pyramidal training strategy and sub-pixel sampling to handle large pixel motions. It is chosen when input views are sparse and camera poses are known but potentially inaccurate, or when geometric consistency is paramount.

### [DASNeRF](8668-dasnerf.md)
DASNeRF is designed for sparse-view 3D reconstruction using monocular depth priors (specifically DepthAnythingV2). It fuses 5D input vectors (coordinates + ray info) into the MLP and enforces depth ordering and structural consistency. It distinguishes itself by leveraging modern depth estimation to guide learning, making it suitable for scenarios with very limited input views where standard NeRF fails to reconstruct geometry.

### [R2L (Neural Light Field)](8670-r2l-neural-light-field.md)
R2L distills a pre-trained NeRF teacher into a deep Neural Light Field (NeLF) student. It uses an 88-layer MLP with extensive residual blocks and no positional encoding, trading width for depth. It is chosen for efficient novel view synthesis with high quality, particularly on reflective surfaces where depth-dependent methods fail. It relies on hard-example mining during distillation.

### [AligNeRF](8695-alignerf.md)
AligNeRF addresses misalignment from moving objects or calibration errors in high-resolution settings. It combines a convolution-augmented architecture (MLP outputting features, processed by a CNN) with a staged training procedure. It uses patch-based sampling and an alignment-aware loss that searches for the best-matching subpatch in the ground truth. It is chosen when rendering high-resolution images with potential spatial misalignments.

### SinNeRF
SinNeRF uses a depth map to constrain unobserved views via adversarial and perceptual losses, leveraging a specific semantic and geometric regularization to build a semi-supervised learning process for single-image NeRF training [S1, unofficial].

### NeRF in the Wild
NeRF in the Wild adds an Appearance embedding to express different lighting conditions and a Transient embedding to model changing geometry, addressing the lack of robustness to unconstrained photo collections [S1, unofficial].

### StyleNeRF
StyleNeRF is a 3D-aware generative model that merges 3D scene representation with style-based generative models. It uses NeRF to generate low-resolution feature maps and samples them to high resolution to avoid super-computation volume rendering, applying regularization terms to improve 3D consistency [S2].

### CLIP-NeRF
CLIP-NeRF adds a CLIP (text-image pre-trained multimodal model) to provide greater freedom in shape operations and global deformation. It uses a disentangled conditional NeRF architecture where a learned deformation network modulates shape and appearance codes are embedded into volume rendering [S2].

### NeRF-RPN
NeRF-RPN uses a multi-scale Object-NeRF architecture where the scene branch encodes geometry and appearance via a voxel representation of 3D neural volume features, and the object branch encodes each independent object, allowing for 3D bounding box regression without rendering [S2].

### JacobiNeRF
JacobiNeRF synthesizes a NeRF model based on second-order information (Jacobian matrix) to maximize mutual information between highly correlated entities under random scene perturbations. It expresses semantic synergy by aligning the Jacobian matrix of highly related entities [S2].

### DE-NeRF
DE-NeRF decomposes lighting, geometry, and appearance to allow relighting of high-frequency environments and appearance editing. It trains a signed distance field (SDF) function, decomposes the scene into geometric and appearance features (diffuse, roughness, specular), and uses multiple MLPs to predict final color components [S3].

### General Radiance Field Rendering via MLP
This approach uses an MLP that predicts RGB values and volumetric density for a 3D point using its aggregated feature vector $\bar{F}_p$ and viewing direction, rather than raw position $xyz$. It follows NeRF's positional encoding and hierarchical sampling but inputs feature aggregates [S4].

### RefSR-NeRF
RefSR-NeRF is a reference-guided super-resolution framework combining NeRF and RefSR-CNN. It modifies NeRF sampling to patch ray sampling on high-resolution input to get low-resolution images, then fuses feature maps from detail-dominant and degradation-dominant modules to reconstruct high-resolution images [S3].

### RelightingNeRF
RelightingNeRF handles unstructured photographs by changing the model architecture to output SDF values and latent features. The first MLP outputs SDF and latent features; the second MLP takes positions, lighting, viewing directions, and latent features to produce final color, enabling free-viewpoint reconstruction with lighting control [S3].

### DiffusioNeRF
DiffusioNeRF utilizes a denoising diffusion model to learn priors for scene geometry and color. It renders random RGBD patches during training and backpropagates estimated gradients of the log-likelihood to normalize geometry and color, improving quality under sparse input views [S3].

### GeoNeRF
GeoNeRF consists of a geometric morphology reasoner and a renderer. The reasoner builds a cascaded cost volume for source view sequences, and the renderer infers geometric and appearance representations using a Transformer-based attention mechanism and cascaded cost volumes before rendering fine images [S2].

## Background
The original NeRF architecture [S4] introduced the concept of mapping 3D coordinates and viewing directions to density and color using a fully connected MLP with positional encoding. Subsequent research focused on improving this architecture to handle specific challenges:
*   **Efficiency:** AdaNeRF [S1, unofficial] and R2L [S1, unofficial] address the computational bottleneck of dense sampling and deep MLPs.
*   **Sparse Inputs:** SfMNeRF [S1, unofficial], DASNeRF [S1, unofficial], SinNeRF [S1, unofficial], and DiffusioNeRF [S3] introduce external priors (SfM, monocular depth, diffusion) to constrain the implicit field when data is scarce.
*   **Geometric Accuracy:** SfMNeRF [S1, unofficial], DASNeRF [S1, unofficial], DE-NeRF [S3], and RelightingNeRF [S3] incorporate explicit geometric representations (SDF, epipolar constraints) to resolve the ambiguity of density-based representations.
*   **High-Resolution/Detail:** AligNeRF [S1, unofficial], RefSR-NeRF [S3], and StyleNeRF [S2] integrate convolutional layers or generative models to capture high-frequency details that MLPs struggle with.
*   **Generalization/Editing:** CLIP-NeRF [S2], NeRF-RPN [S2], and JacobiNeRF [S2] extend the architecture to support semantic understanding, object-level editing, and multi-modal inputs.

The field has evolved from a single MLP [S4] to complex hybrid architectures involving CNNs, Transformers, and external priors, reflecting the need for NeRF to be robust, efficient, and semantically aware.
