# NeRF-FF

Also called: Focus Frustums, NeRF-FF + iNGP, NeRF-FF + nerfacto, NeRF-FF + splatfacto, Neural Radiance Fields with Focus Frustums

## Overview
NeRF-FF is a plug-in strategy that mitigates the degradation of Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) reconstruction quality caused by out-of-focus (defocus blurred) regions in input images [S1]. It operates by estimating binary image masks that identify in-focus regions, allowing a subsequently trained NeRF model to omit out-of-focus pixels during training [S1]. Unlike methods that use dynamic ray generation or blur kernels (e.g., PDRF, Deblur-NeRF), NeRF-FF relies on static ray assumptions, making it compatible with runtime-optimized NeRF variants like Instant-NGP, nerfacto, and splatfacto [S1].

## How it works
NeRF-FF estimates image masks based on Focus Frustums (FFs), defined as the visible volume in scene space that is in-focus for a given camera view [S1]. The method consists of a preliminary reconstruction phase, edge detection, initial frustum estimation, iterative refinement, and final masked training [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ partially defocus blurred input images $I_i \in [0, 255]^{3 \times W \times H}$ for $i=1 \dots N$, where $W$ and $H$ are image width and height. Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are required for depth reprojection.
*   **Outputs:** A set of binary masks $Mask_i(x,y)$ for each input image, and a trained NeRF model representing the in-focus scene geometry [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Depth Maps:** $D_i \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times H}$, representing the per-pixel depth estimated by the preliminary NeRF model.
*   **Focus Frustum:** For each image $i$, a volume defined by near plane depth $n_i$ and far plane depth $f_i$, clipped by the camera's view frustum.
*   **Refined Focus Frustum:** $RFF_i$, defined by updated near plane $\hat{n}_i$ and far plane $\hat{f}_i$.
*   **Indicator Pixels:** $P_i$, a set of 2D coordinates $(x,y)$ indicating in-focus regions.
*   **Scene Points:** $S$, a set of 3D points sampled from the scene geometry (e.g., high-density points from the preliminary NeRF) [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\tau$: Threshold for the discrete Laplace operator to distinguish in-focus edges. Typical value: 80 [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Hyperparameter controlling the trade-off between scene coverage and reconstruction quality. It defines the minimum fraction of refined frustums that must encompass each sampled scene point. Range: $[0, 1.0]$. Typical value: 0.4 [S1].
    *   $\epsilon = 0$: Omits the Focus Frustum Refinement step.
    *   $\epsilon = 1$: Equivalent to training without NeRF-FF (no masking) [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Preliminary Reconstruction:** Train a preliminary NeRF model (e.g., Instant-NGP) on the blurry input images $I_i$ to obtain per-view depth maps $D_i$ [S1].
    *   *Note:* The preliminary model does not need to produce photorealistic results; adequate geometry estimation is sufficient [S1].

2.  **Edge Detection (Indicator Pixels):** Compute the grayscale version of each input image $I_i$. Apply the discrete Laplace operator $\square$ to detect sharp edges. Identify indicator pixels $P_i$ where the Laplace value exceeds $\tau$:
    $$ P_i = \{ (x, y) \mid \square(I_i)(x, y) > \tau \} $$
    where $\square(I_i)(x, y)$ is the Laplace value at pixel $(x,y)$ [S1].

3.  **Initial Focus Frustum Estimation:** Sample the depth maps $D_i$ at the indicator pixels $P_i$. Perform statistical analysis on the sampled depths per image to estimate the focus depth and range (Depth of Field, DoF). This defines the initial near plane $n_i$ and far plane $f_i$ for the Focus Frustum $FF_i$ [S1].

4.  **Focus Frustum Refinement:** Iteratively expand the near and far planes of the Focus Frustums to ensure scene coverage. The goal is to find updated planes $\hat{n}_i$ and $\hat{f}_i$ such that:
    *   Every spatial position $p \in S$ is encompassed by at least a fraction $\epsilon$ of the refined Focus Frustums $RFF_i$.
    *   The accumulated change in depth $R$ is minimized.
    
    The accumulated change $R$ is calculated considering only frustums whose view frustum encompasses point $p$:
    $$ R = \sum_{i} \mathbb{I}(p \in \text{ViewFrustum}_i) \cdot \text{DepthChange}(FF_i, RFF_i) $$
    *(Note: The exact formulation of DepthChange is not explicitly detailed in the source, but implies the expansion of the interval $[n_i, f_i]$ to $[\hat{n}_i, \hat{f}_i]$)* [S1].

5.  **Mask Generation:** Reproject the dense scene points encompassed by the refined Focus Frustums $RFF_i$ into the respective image space. Generate binary masks $Mask_i(x,y)$ for each image:
    $$ Mask_i(x, y) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } D_i(x, y) \in [\hat{n}_i, \hat{f}_i] \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    Since all pixels in the depth map are visible, other boundaries of the frustum (lateral) do not need explicit consideration for the mask generation [S1].

6.  **Final Training:** Train the subsequent NeRF model using the input images $I_i$, but only consider pixels where $Mask_i(x, y) = 1$. Out-of-focus regions are omitted during the loss calculation [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def nerff_ff(input_images, camera_intrinsics, camera_extrinsics, 
             tau=80, epsilon=0.4, preliminary_iterations=2000, 
             final_iterations=4000):
    """
    NeRF-FF: Focus Frustum Refinement for blurred NeRF training.
    
    Args:
        input_images: List of N blurred images I_i.
        camera_intrinsics: Camera intrinsics for all views.
        camera_extrinsics: Camera extrinsics for all views.
        tau: Threshold for Laplace operator.
        epsilon: Fraction of frustums covering each scene point.
        preliminary_iterations: Iterations for preliminary NeRF.
        final_iterations: Iterations for final NeRF.
        
    Returns:
        trained_nerf: The final trained NeRF model.
    """
    N = len(input_images)
    depth_maps = []
    masks = []
    
    # Step 1: Preliminary Reconstruction
    # Train a preliminary NeRF (e.g., Instant-NGP) on blurred images
    preliminary_nerf = train_neRF(input_images, iterations=preliminary_iterations)
    
    # Generate depth maps from preliminary NeRF inference
    for i in range(N):
        D_i = get_depth_map(preliminary_nerf, input_images[i], 
                            camera_intrinsics[i], camera_extrinsics[i])
        depth_maps.append(D_i)
        
    # Step 2: Edge Detection
    indicator_pixels = []
    for i in range(N):
        # Convert to grayscale
        gray_I_i = to_grayscale(input_images[i])
        # Apply discrete Laplace operator
        laplace_I_i = apply_laplace(gray_I_i)
        # Threshold to find in-focus edges
        P_i = set()
        for (x, y) in all_pixel_coordinates(gray_I_i):
            if laplace_I_i[x, y] > tau:
                P_i.add((x, y))
        indicator_pixels.append(P_i)
        
    # Step 3: Initial Focus Frustum Estimation
    initial_frustums = []
    for i in range(N):
        # Sample depths at indicator pixels
        sampled_depths = [depth_maps[i][x, y] for (x, y) in indicator_pixels[i]]
        
        # Statistical analysis to estimate DoF (near/far planes)
        # Note: Specific statistical method (e.g., mean/std, min/max) 
        # is not detailed in source, assumed to be robust estimation.
        n_i, f_i = estimate_dof(sampled_depths)
        initial_frustums.append({'n': n_i, 'f': f_i})
        
    # Step 4: Focus Frustum Refinement
    # Sample dense scene points S from preliminary NeRF geometry
    S = sample_scene_points(preliminary_nerf, density='high')
    
    refined_frustums = []
    for i in range(N):
        # Initialize refined planes with initial estimates
        n_hat = initial_frustums[i]['n']
        f_hat = initial_frustums[i]['f']
        
        # Iteratively expand planes to satisfy epsilon constraint
        # Minimize accumulated depth change R while ensuring coverage
        # Constraint: Each p in S must be in at least epsilon fraction of frustums
        
        # Optimization loop to find n_hat, f_hat
        # This step is complex and likely involves iterative expansion
        # until the coverage condition is met.
        n_hat, f_hat = refine_frustum(initial_frustums[i], S, epsilon, 
                                      camera_extrinsics[i])
        refined_frustums.append({'n': n_hat, 'f': f_hat})
        
    # Step 5: Mask Generation
    for i in range(N):
        n_hat = refined_frustums[i]['n']
        f_hat = refined_frustums[i]['f']
        mask = np.zeros_like(depth_maps[i], dtype=bool)
        
        for (x, y) in all_pixel_coordinates(depth_maps[i]):
            d = depth_maps[i][x, y]
            # Check if depth is within refined frustum bounds
            if n_hat <= d <= f_hat:
                mask[x, y] = True
            else:
                mask[x, y] = False
        masks.append(mask)
        
    # Step 6: Final Training
    # Train final NeRF using only masked pixels
    final_nerf = train_neRF_masked(input_images, masks, 
                                   iterations=final_iterations)
    
    return final_nerf

def refine_frustum(frustum, scene_points, epsilon, extrinsics):
    """
    Refines the near and far planes of a Focus Frustum.
    Ensures that a fraction 'epsilon' of scene points are covered 
    by at least 'epsilon' fraction of all frustums.
    """
    # This is a simplified representation of the optimization.
    # The source states: "minimizing accumulated depth change R"
    # while ensuring "any spatial position p in S is part of at least 
    # a certain fraction epsilon of the refined Focus Frustums."
    
    # In practice, this involves expanding n_hat and f_hat iteratively.
    # Start with initial n, f.
    n_hat = frustum['n']
    f_hat = frustum['f']
    
    # Expand until coverage condition is met.
    # Note: The exact algorithm for expansion (e.g., binary search, 
    # gradient descent on R) is not provided in the source.
    # We assume an iterative expansion strategy.
    
    while not check_coverage(scene_points, refined_frustums, epsilon):
        # Expand near plane backwards and far plane forwards
        n_hat -= step_size
        f_hat += step_size
        
    return n_hat, f_hat

def check_coverage(scene_points, refined_frustums, epsilon):
    """
    Checks if every point in scene_points is covered by at least 
    epsilon fraction of the refined_frustums.
    """
    coverage_counts = np.zeros(len(scene_points))
    for p in scene_points:
        for frustum in refined_frustums:
            if is_point_in_frustum(p, frustum):
                coverage_counts[scene_points.index(p)] += 1
    
    # Check if all points meet the epsilon threshold
    # Note: The source says "at least a certain fraction epsilon"
    # This implies a global constraint or per-point constraint.
    # Assuming per-point constraint for robustness.
    for count in coverage_counts:
        if count < len(refined_frustums) * epsilon:
            return False
    return True

def is_point_in_frustum(point, frustum):
    """
    Checks if a 3D point is within the depth bounds of the frustum
    and within the camera's view frustum.
    """
    # Project point to camera space to get depth
    depth = project_to_camera_depth(point, frustum['extrinsics'])
    
    # Check depth bounds
    if frustum['n'] <= depth <= frustum['f']:
        # Check if point is within view frustum (lateral bounds)
        # Source notes: "only Focus Frustums with a corresponding view 
        # frustum that encompasses the respective spatial position p"
        # are considered.
        if is_within_view_frustum(point, frustum['extrinsics']):
            return True
    return False
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Low $\tau$:** Causes bad performance because weak signals (texture in out-of-focus regions) are considered in-focus indicators, leading to incorrect initial frustum estimates [S1].
*   **High $\tau$:** Causes slowly decreasing performance as sharp edges in in-focus regions are missed. However, the decline is small because initial frustums are refined [S1].
*   **Fully Blurred Images:** NeRF-FF underperforms on datasets with fully blurred images. The preliminary NeRF produces low-quality dense reconstruction with "floater artifacts" (high optical density volumes with no ground truth counterpart), leading to erroneous depth maps and masks [S1].
*   **Mismatches:** Mismatches between in-focus indicators and the spatial position of scene geometry cause erroneous depth estimates, negatively influencing initial Focus Frustum estimates [S1].
*   **Floater Artifacts:** Even with masking, subsequent reconstruction may suffer from floater artifacts inherent to Instant-NGP, reducing image quality depending on camera position [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is reduced to under 1 minute on end-consumer hardware (NVIDIA RTX 3090) for the full pipeline (preliminary + final NeRF) [S1]. Average training time is 45 seconds, a two orders of magnitude acceleration compared to state-of-the-art methods like PDRF or Deblur-NeRF [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given, but relies on standard NeRF memory usage plus storage for depth maps and masks [S1].
*   **Performance:**
    *   **PSNR:** On par with state-of-the-art methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF) on real-world defocus datasets [S1].
    *   **SSIM:** Consistently outperforms SOTA methods by a fair margin [S1].
    *   **LPIPS:** Trails reference deblurring strategies slightly, but the difference is considered negligible given the speedup [S1].
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible with Instant-NGP, nerfacto, and splatfacto (3D Gaussian Splatting) [S1]. When applied to nerfacto and splatfacto, it improves PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS with negligible impact on training duration [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Not suitable for datasets containing fully blurred images due to floater artifacts and poor preliminary depth estimation [S1].

## Background
NeRF-FF was proposed by T. Wirth et al. in 2024 [S1]. It addresses the limitation that existing NeRF methods for defocus blur often require dynamic ray generation, which is incompatible with static-ray, runtime-optimized NeRF variants like Instant-NGP [S1]. The method leverages the discrete Laplace operator for edge detection and statistical analysis for depth-of-field estimation [S1].
