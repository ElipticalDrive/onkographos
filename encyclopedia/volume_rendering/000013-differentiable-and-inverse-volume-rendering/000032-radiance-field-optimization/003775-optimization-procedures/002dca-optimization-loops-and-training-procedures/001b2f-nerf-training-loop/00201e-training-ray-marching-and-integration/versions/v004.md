# Training Ray Marching and Integration

Also called: CUDA Ray Marching (Training), NeRF Forward Pass, Standard NeRF forward, Training Ray Marching, VolumeRenderer

## Overview
Training Ray Marching and Integration is the differentiable forward pass that synthesizes RGB images from a neural radiance field (NeRF) during optimization. It solves the problem of computing gradients for scene parameters by sampling points along camera rays, querying a neural network for volume density and color, and integrating them via alpha compositing [S1, S2, S4, S9, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from evaluation by using stochastic perturbations, adaptive sampling strategies (e.g., occupancy grid pruning), and chunked processing to manage memory, whereas evaluation disables these for deterministic results [S2, S4, S9, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Ray origins $\mathbf{o} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$, ray directions $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$, near/far planes $\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{f} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, and optional scene bounds (AABB or sphere) [S1, S9, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB image $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, depth map $\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, accumulated transmittance/opacity $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, and intermediate data for backpropagation (e.g., sampled points, weights) [S1, S4, S6, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Density Bitfield:** A packed `uint8` array of shape $[C \cdot H^3 / 8]$ used for fast occupancy grid lookups to prune empty space [S1, S9, unofficial].
*   **Ray Metadata:** An integer tensor of shape $[N, 3]$ storing `(ray_index, point_offset, point_count)` for each ray, allowing sparse storage of sampled points [S1, S9, unofficial].
*   **Sampled Points:** Float tensors of shape $[M, 3]$ for coordinates ($\mathbf{xyzs}$), $[M, 3]$ for directions ($\mathbf{dirs}$), and $[M, 2]$ for deltas ($\mathbf{deltas}$), where $M$ is the total number of samples across all rays [S1, S9, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Ray Bundle Generation:** Compute ray origins and directions in world space from camera parameters (intrinsics, extrinsics) [S8, S4, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Marching (Sampling):**
    *   Initialize counters for active rays [S7, S9, unofficial].
    *   Use a CUDA kernel (`march_rays_train`) to sample points along rays. This step may use:
        *   **Uniform Sampling:** Initial stratified sampling between near and far planes [S9, unofficial].
        *   **Adaptive Sampling:** Upsampling in regions of high density using stratified sampling from the probability density function (PDF) derived from coarse weights [S9, unofficial].
        *   **Pruning:** Skip empty space using an occupancy grid or density bitfield [S1, S8, S9, unofficial].
    *   Perturb sample positions with uniform noise in $[-0.5, 0.5] \cdot \text{step\_size}$ if `perturb=True` [S8, S9, unofficial].
    *   Compute segment lengths (deltas) $\delta_i$ as the distance between consecutive samples. For the last sample, use a large constant ($10^{10}$) to ensure full contribution if opaque [S2, S4, unofficial].
3.  **Neural Network Query:** Query the implicit function (MLP) at sampled points $\mathbf{xyzs}$ and directions $\mathbf{dirs}$ to obtain volume density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S2, S9, unofficial]. Scale densities by `density_scale` if applicable [S9, unofficial].
4.  **Alpha Compositing (Integration):**
    *   Compute segment opacity $\alpha_i$:
        $$ \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i) $$
        [S4, S2, unofficial]
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i$ (probability of ray passing through previous segments):
        $$ T_i = \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
        Implemented as cumulative product with numerical stability epsilon ($10^{-10}$):
        $$ T_i = \text{cumprod}(\max(1 - \alpha + 10^{-10}, 0)) $$
        [S2, S4, unofficial]
    *   Compute weight $w_i$ for each sample:
        $$ w_i = T_i \cdot \alpha_i $$
        [S2, S4, unofficial]
    *   Composite RGB and Depth:
        $$ \mathbf{C} = \sum_i w_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
        $$ \mathbf{D} = \sum_i w_i d_i $$
        [S4, S2, unofficial]
    *   Compute accumulated opacity:
        $$ \mathbf{A} = \sum_i w_i $$
        [S4, unofficial]
    *   Add background color $\mathbf{c}_{bg}$ for rays with incomplete opacity:
        $$ \mathbf{C}_{final} = \mathbf{C} + (1 - \mathbf{A}) \mathbf{c}_{bg} $$
        [S6, S12, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def cumprod_exclusive(tensor: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """Mimics tf.math.cumprod(..., exclusive=True) for the last dimension."""
    c = torch.cumprod(tensor, dim=-1)
    c[..., 0] = 1.0
    return c

def render_volume_density(rbg_field: torch.Tensor, density_field: torch.Tensor, ray_origins: torch.Tensor, depth_values: torch.Tensor) -> tuple:
    """
    Differentiable volume rendering.
    rbg_field: (width, height, num_samples, 4) or (width, height, num_samples, 3) + density
    density_field: (width, height, num_samples)
    """
    # Compute distances between samples
    one_e_10 = torch.tensor([1e10], dtype=ray_origins.dtype, device=ray_origins.device)
    dists = torch.cat((depth_values[..., 1:] - depth_values[..., :-1],
                       one_e_10.expand(depth_values[..., :1].shape)), dim=-1)
    
    # Compute alpha
    alpha = 1. - torch.exp(-density_field * dists)
    
    # Compute weights
    # Add epsilon for numerical stability
    weights = alpha * cumprod_exclusive(1. - alpha + 1e-10)
    
    # Composite RGB
    # rbg_field shape: (width, height, num_samples, 3)
    rgb_map = (weights[..., None] * rbg_field).sum(dim=-2)
    
    # Composite Depth (assuming depth_values is broadcastable or expanded)
    # depth_values shape: (num_samples) -> needs expansion to (width, height, num_samples)
    depth_values_expanded = depth_values[None, None, :]
    depth_map = (weights * depth_values_expanded).sum(dim=-1)
    
    # Accumulated opacity
    acc_map = weights.sum(-1)
    
    return rgb_map, depth_map, acc_map

    xyzs, dirs, deltas, rays = raymarching_cuda_kernel(rays_o, rays_d, nears, fars, perturb=perturb)
    
    # 2. Neural Network Query
    # Flatten points for chunked evaluation
    num_points = xyzs.shape[0]
    num_rays = rays_o.shape[0]
    
    # Initialize output tensors
    rgbs = torch.zeros((num_points, 3), device=xyzs.device)
    sigmas = torch.zeros((num_points,), device=xyzs.device)
    
    # Chunked evaluation to avoid OOM
    for i in range(0, num_points, chunk_size):
        end = min(i + chunk_size, num_points)
        chunk_xyzs = xyzs[i:end]
        chunk_dirs = dirs[i:end]
        
        # Query model
        sigma_chunk, rgb_chunk = model(chunk_xyzs, chunk_dirs)
        
        sigmas[i:end] = sigma_chunk
        rgbs[i:end] = rgb_chunk
        
    # 3. Reshape to ray structure
    # Reconstruct per-ray tensors from flat lists using ray indices
    # This part depends on the specific CUDA kernel output format (rays tensor)
    # Simplified: assume we can gather back to (N, M_max, 3) etc.
    # For the sake of this article, we assume a helper `gather_to_rays` exists
    # which reconstructs the per-ray tensors from the flat CUDA outputs.
    
    # Let's assume we have per-ray tensors now:
    # xyzs_per_ray: (N, M, 3), dirs_per_ray: (N, M, 3), deltas_per_ray: (N, M, 2)
    # sigmas_per_ray: (N, M), rgbs_per_ray: (N, M, 3)
    
    # Note: The actual implementation in [S1, S9, unofficial] does this in CUDA or via complex indexing.
    # Here we show the logical flow.
    
    # 4. Alpha Compositing
    # Compute alpha
    alpha = 1. - torch.exp(-sigmas_per_ray * deltas_per_ray[..., 0]) # deltas[..., 0] is delta_t
    
    # Compute weights
    weights = alpha * cumprod_exclusive(1. - alpha + 1e-10)
    
    # Composite
    rgb_map = (weights[..., None] * rgbs_per_ray).sum(dim=-2)
    depth_map = (weights * deltas_per_ray[..., 0]).sum(dim=-1) # Using delta as depth proxy if z_vals not stored
    acc_map = weights.sum(-1)
    
    # Add background
    # Assuming white background for simplicity, or from config
    bg_color = torch.tensor([1.0, 1.0, 1.0], device=rgb_map.device)
    rgb_map = rgb_map + (1.0 - acc_map[..., None]) * bg_color
    
    return rgb_map, depth_map, acc_map
```

**Parameters:**
*   `chunk_size`: Number of rays/points processed in one forward pass to manage VRAM. Default $64 \times 1024$ [S5, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: Boolean flag to add noise to sample positions. Default `True` for training [S9, unofficial].
*   `density_bitfield`: Packed density grid for pruning [S1, unofficial].
*   `bound`: Spatial bound for ray marching [S9, unofficial].
*   `dt_gamma`: Cone angle parameter for adaptive step sizing [S9, unofficial].
*   `max_steps`: Maximum number of samples per ray [S9, unofficial].
*   `mean_count`: Estimated mean steps to accelerate training; rays are dropped if actual count exceeds this [S9, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Use `float32` for ray marching CUDA kernels to ensure performance and stability [S9, unofficial]. Add epsilon ($10^{-10}$) to $1-\alpha$ to prevent NaNs in log/exp operations [S4, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Handle rays that exit the bounding box early. Use a large delta ($10^{10}$) for the last segment to ensure opaque surfaces are fully rendered [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory:** Large batches of rays can exceed GPU VRAM. Chunking is essential [S1, S4, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Rays with zero length or invalid directions should be handled or filtered [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation](3079-nerf-evaluation-and-metric-calculation.md)
NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation renders a single image from a trained NeRF model at a specified camera pose and computes quantitative performance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) by comparing the rendered output against ground truth data. It differs from training ray marching by disabling stochastic perturbations to ensure deterministic, reproducible results and by often handling background compositing explicitly for fair comparison. It serves as the validation step in the optimization loop, providing the scalar losses and visual quality indicators required for monitoring convergence.

### [NeRF Network Query with Hashed Embeddings](3107-nerf-network-query-with-hashed-embeddings.md)
This method queries a differentiable radiance field by mapping 3D spatial coordinates and view directions into a high-dimensional feature space using a multiresolution hash encoding, then decoding these features into volume density and RGB color via shallow multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs). It solves the problem of efficient, high-frequency detail capture in neural radiance fields by replacing expensive positional encoding with learnable, sparse hash tables that support linear interpolation. It sets itself apart from its siblings by using concatenated MLPs (density followed by color) driven by hash-encoded inputs rather than direct voxel lookups or dense grids, offering a balance between quality and speed.

### [Chunked Network Evaluation](3177-chunked-network-evaluation.md)
Chunked Network Evaluation is a memory-management technique used during the training and rendering of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to avoid out-of-memory (OOM) errors when evaluating the neural network on large batches of 3D points. It solves the problem where the number of samples per ray ($N_{samples}$) multiplied by the number of rays ($N_{rays}$) exceeds the GPU's VRAM capacity for the network's forward pass. It sets itself apart from standard batched evaluation by explicitly partitioning the point cloud into smaller, manageable subsets (chunks) processed sequentially, rather than attempting to process all points in a single tensor operation.

### [NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing)](5124-nerf-rendering-alpha-compositing.md)
NeRF Rendering (Alpha Compositing) is the differentiable volume rendering method that computes the final RGB color and depth for a camera ray by integrating the predicted volume density ($\sigma$) and emitted color ($c$) of sampled points along the ray. It solves the problem of synthesizing novel views from a continuous 5D radiance field by approximating the line integral of light transport using numerical quadrature. What sets it apart from its siblings is its reliance on a neural network (MLP) to query density and color at arbitrary 3D coordinates and view directions, combined with alpha compositing to handle occlusion, whereas variants like [NeRF-style Volume Rendering Integrator](5964-nerf-style-volume-rendering-integrator.md) may use explicit voxel grids or different sampling strategies.

### [NeRF-style Volume Rendering Integrator](5964-nerf-style-volume-rendering-integrator.md)
A simplified NeRF-style integrator that accumulates emission along a ray through a medium, backed by a dense grid instead of a neural network, with no direction-dependent emission. It differs from standard NeRF implementations by using a dense grid for density/emission storage and explicitly handling primal/dual modes for gradient computation in inverse rendering contexts.

### [Active Learning Strategy for NeRF Distillation](7421-active-learning-strategy-for-nerf-distillation.md)
This method addresses the performance gap between teacher and student Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) models during knowledge distillation by actively selecting challenging data samples rather than processing all data uniformly. It distinguishes itself from standard distillation by dynamically identifying and prioritizing difficult camera poses, sample rays, and sample points based on loss metrics, thereby enabling student models to outperform those trained from scratch.

### Training Ray Marching and Integration — CUDA Ray Marching (Training), NeRF Forward Pass, Standard NeRF forward, Training Ray Marching, Training Ray Marching and Integration — NeRF Forward Pass, march_rays_train, run_cuda
A rendering function that uses an occupancy grid estimator to skip empty space during volume rendering, optimizing performance [S10, unofficial]. It takes ray origins, directions, bounds, density bitfield, and parameters like `near_plane`, `render_step_size`, `cone_angle`, and `alpha_thre` [S10, unofficial]. It outputs sampled points, directions, deltas, and ray indices [S1, S9, unofficial].

### Differentiable Volume Rendering Pipeline
Orchestrates rendering by instantiating an implicit volume, a sampling scheme, and a renderer. In `render_images`, it loops over cameras, generates NDC grids, creates ray bundles, passes them through `model.sampler` to generate sample points, then calls `model(ray_bundle)` to evaluate the implicit volume and aggregate results. Extracts rendered features and depth, reshapes to image dimensions, and saves/returns them [S11, unofficial]. The loss is computed as $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{C}_i - C_i)^2$ [S11, unofficial].

### NeRF Forward Pass with Alpha Masking and Two-Stage Shading
Samples points using NDC simulation or standard sampling. Computes distances between samples, handling the last sample with zero distance. If using NDC, adjusts distances by ray norm and normalizes viewdirs. If an alpha mask exists, combines ray validity with the mask. Computes sigma from density features, converts to alpha, weight, and background weight. Computes final RGB map by weighting RGB values by alpha weights. Adds background color for rays with incomplete opacity. Computes depth map as weighted sum of z_vals, adjusted by background weight and ray direction Z component. Clamps depth if NDC Euclidean depth simulation is enabled [S12, unofficial].

### Progressive Strided Ray Sampling
A progressive sampling strategy to stabilize GAN training, starting with large stride ray generation and progressively reducing stride size to cover larger regions initially and focus on local regions later [S13]. In each iteration, it randomly samples two patches: one from the reference view and one from a random unseen view. Obtains geometry pseudo labels via image warping and adds regularization on each patch’s intersection with the corresponding patch’s warped result. As training proceeds into latter stages, decreases stride $s$ to focus on more local regions. Randomly initializes the discriminator after reducing the stride size to help it focus on a fixed resolution and stabilize training [S13]. The $K \times K$ patch $P$ of stride $s$ containing point $(u, v)$ is defined as: $\mathcal{P}(u, v, s)=\left \{(u +sx, v +sy) \mid x, y \in \left \{0, \ldots , {K}\right \}\right \}$ [S13].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot M)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $M$ is the number of samples per ray. Adaptive sampling can reduce $M$ in empty regions [S1, S9, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot M)$ for storing sampled points and intermediate tensors. Chunking reduces peak memory usage to $O(\text{chunk\_size} \cdot M)$ [S1, S4, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On NVIDIA GPUs, CUDA-accelerated ray marching with occupancy grid pruning can achieve real-time training speeds for moderate resolutions [S8, S9, unofficial]. Performance scales with the number of rays and samples per ray [S9, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Differentiable, allowing end-to-end optimization of scene parameters from images. Efficient pruning reduces computation in empty space [S1, S8, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Memory-intensive for high-resolution scenes or large batches. Requires careful tuning of sampling parameters (e.g., `max_steps`, `chunk_size`) [S1, S4, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** During the training phase of NeRFs where gradients are needed.
*   **When Not to Use:** During evaluation/inference where deterministic, reproducible results are required (use [NeRF Evaluation and Metric Calculation](3079-nerf-evaluation-and-metric-calculation.md) instead) [S2, S4, unofficial].

## Background
Originating from the NeRF paper (Mildenhall et al., 2020) [S2, S4, unofficial], training ray marching and integration has become a standard component in neural radiance field optimization. It builds on differentiable volume rendering techniques [S2, S4, unofficial]. Variants like instant-NGP introduced occupancy grid pruning and hash encoding to accelerate training [S1, S8, S9, unofficial]. The method is widely used in 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and inverse rendering tasks [S1, S2, S4, unofficial].
