# MIMO Objective


## Overview
The MIMO objective is the total training loss function for MIMO-NeRF, combining pixel-wise reconstruction errors with a 3D consistency regularization term across multiple reformulated Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF training by explicitly enforcing consistency between different "views" of the same scene generated by parallel MLPs with varying sampling densities, thereby enabling faster rendering without sacrificing quality [S1]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on evaluation metrics like PSNR or SSIM, this objective drives the optimization loop during training [S1].

## How it works
The method trains $M$ reformulated MIMO MLPs. Each MLP $m$ uses a different shift parameter $s$ (or variation reduction configuration) and a specific repetition count $R_m$. The total number of samples ("# Run") across all $M$ MLPs is constrained to not exceed the sample count of a single original SISO (Single-Input Single-Output) MLP [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   $M$: The number of reformulated MIMO MLPs.
*   $\mathbf{C}$: The ground-truth RGB color for the current pixel/view.
*   $\hat{\mathbf{C}}^m$: The predicted RGB color from the $m$-th MIMO MLP.
*   $\mathbf{c}_i^m$: The color of the $i$-th sample along the ray for the $m$-th MLP.
*   $\alpha_i^m$: The alpha (opacity) of the $i$-th sample along the ray for the $m$-th MLP.
*   $N$: The number of samples along the ray.
*   $R_m$: The repetition count (or reliability metric) for the $m$-th MLP.
*   $R_{\max}$: The maximum $R$ value among all $M$ MLPs ($R_{\max} = \max_{m \in \{1, \dots, M\}} R_m$).
*   $\lambda$: Hyperparameter balancing pixel-wise and 3D consistency losses.

**Outputs:**
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MIMO}}$: The scalar total loss value.

### Data Structures
The implementation requires storing the predicted colors and alphas for all $M$ MLPs for every sample point along the ray.
*   `predicted_colors`: Tensor of shape $(M, N, 3)$, where `predicted_colors[m, i]` corresponds to $\mathbf{c}_i^m$.
*   `predicted_alphas`: Tensor of shape $(M, N)$, where `predicted_alphas[m, i]` corresponds to $\alpha_i^m$.
*   `R_values`: Array of size $M$, containing $R_1, \dots, R_M$.

### Formulas
The total loss is defined as [S1]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MIMO}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{pixel}}^{\text{MIMO}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{3D}
$$

1.  **Pixel-wise Loss**: Sum of squared errors for each MLP's final rendered color against the ground truth [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{pixel}}^{\text{MIMO}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \|\hat{\mathbf{C}}^m - \mathbf{C}\|_2^2
    $$

2.  **3D Consistency Loss**: Encourages the $M$ MLPs to produce consistent colors and alphas at the sample level. It consists of color and alpha components [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{3D} = \mathcal{L}_{3D}^{\text{color}} + \mathcal{L}_{3D}^{\text{alpha}}
    $$

    The **Color 3D Consistency Loss** is [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{3D}^{\text{color}} = \sum_{m_1=1}^{M-1} \sum_{m_2=m_1+1}^M \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ \mu_{m_1}^{m_2} \|\mathbf{c}_i^{m_1} - \text{sg}(\mathbf{c}_i^{m_2})\|_2^2 + \mu_{m_2}^{m_1} \|\text{sg}(\mathbf{c}_i^{m_1}) - \mathbf{c}_i^{m_2}\|_2^2 \right]
    $$

    The **Alpha 3D Consistency Loss** is [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{3D}^{\text{alpha}} = \sum_{m_1=1}^{M-1} \sum_{m_2=m_1+1}^M \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ \mu_{m_1}^{m_2} \|\alpha_i^{m_1} - \text{sg}(\alpha_i^{m_2})\|_2^2 + \mu_{m_2}^{m_1} \|\text{sg}(\alpha_i^{m_1}) - \alpha_i^{m_2}\|_2^2 \right]
    $$

    Here, $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes the stop-gradient operation, which prevents gradients from flowing through the target branch in the consistency term [S1].

3.  **Asymmetric Weight**: The weight $\mu_{m_j}^{m_i}$ is defined based on the reliability of the MLPs. MLPs with greater $R_m$ have lower ambiguity and are considered more reliable, thus their influence on the consistency loss is reduced [S1]. The formula provided in the source is [S1]:
    $$
    \mu_{m_j}^{m_i} = \sqrt{m_j} \sqrt{R_{\max}} \sqrt{R_{m_i}}
    $$
    *Note: The source text explicitly provides this formula. However, the accompanying text states that weights are derived from $\sqrt{R_{m_j}} / \sqrt{R_{\max}}$ to reduce the effect of reliable MLPs. The explicit formula in the source contains $\sqrt{m_j}$ which may be a typographical error for $\sqrt{R_{m_j}}$ or similar, but the exact text is quoted above.*

### Parameters
*   $\lambda$: Hyperparameter balancing pixel-wise loss and 3D consistency loss. Typical values are not specified in the sources [S1].
*   $M$: Number of reformulated MLPs.
*   $R_m$: Repetition count for MLP $m$.
*   $R_{\max}$: Maximum $R$ value across all MLPs.

### Pseudocode
```python
def compute_mimo_loss(predicted_colors, predicted_alphas, ground_truth_color, R_values, lambda_weight):
    """
    Computes the MIMO training objective.
    
    Args:
        predicted_colors: Tensor of shape (M, N, 3). Predicted RGB for each sample in each MLP.
        predicted_alphas: Tensor of shape (M, N). Predicted alpha for each sample in each MLP.
        ground_truth_color: Tensor of shape (3). Ground truth RGB for the pixel.
        R_values: List of length M. R_m for each MLP.
        lambda_weight: Scalar. Balances pixel and 3D consistency losses.
        
    Returns:
        total_loss: Scalar. The MIMO objective value.
    """
    M = len(R_values)
    N = predicted_colors.shape[1]
    
    # 1. Pixel-wise Loss
    # Sum of squared errors for each MLP's final rendered color
    # Note: predicted_colors[:, -1, :] is assumed to be the final composited color C_hat^m
    # If predicted_colors contains raw sample colors, they must be composited first.
    # Assuming predicted_colors[:, -1, :] holds the final pixel color C_hat^m:
    pixel_loss = 0.0
    for m in range(M):
        c_hat_m = predicted_colors[m, -1, :]
        pixel_loss += torch.sum((c_hat_m - ground_truth_color) ** 2)
        
    # 2. 3D Consistency Loss
    # Calculate weights mu
    R_max = max(R_values)
    
    # Precompute weights mu_{m_j}^{m_i}
    # Source formula: mu_{m_j}^{m_i} = sqrt(m_j) * sqrt(R_max) * sqrt(R_{m_i})
    # Note: The source text implies weighting by reliability (R), but the formula uses index m_j.
    # We implement the explicit formula given in the source [S1].
    mu = {}
    for m_j in range(M):
        for m_i in range(M):
            # Using 1-based indexing for the formula's m_j, m_i as per source notation
            # In 0-based Python, we map index k to k+1
            mj_val = m_j + 1
            mi_val = m_i + 1
            mu[(m_j, m_i)] = (math.sqrt(mj_val) * math.sqrt(R_max) * math.sqrt(R_values[mi_val - 1]))
            
    l_3d_color = 0.0
    l_3d_alpha = 0.0
    
    # Iterate over all unique pairs (m1, m2) with m1 < m2
    for m1 in range(M - 1):
        for m2 in range(m1 + 1, M):
            # Color consistency
            # Term 1: mu_{m1}^{m2} * ||c_i^{m1} - sg(c_i^{m2})||^2
            # Term 2: mu_{m2}^{m1} * ||sg(c_i^{m1}) - c_i^{m2}||^2
            
            c_m1 = predicted_colors[m1] # Shape (N, 3)
            c_m2 = predicted_colors[m2] # Shape (N, 3)
            
            # Stop gradient on targets
            c_m1_sg = c_m1.detach()
            c_m2_sg = c_m2.detach()
            
            # Compute squared L2 norms element-wise
            # mu_{m1}^{m2} applies to c_m1 as source, c_m2 as target
            w_12 = mu[(m1, m2)]
            diff_12 = c_m1 - c_m2_sg
            term_1 = w_12 * torch.sum(diff_12 ** 2)
            
            # mu_{m2}^{m1} applies to c_m2 as source, c_m1 as target
            w_21 = mu[(m2, m1)]
            diff_21 = c_m2 - c_m1_sg
            term_2 = w_21 * torch.sum(diff_21 ** 2)
            
            l_3d_color += (term_1 + term_2) / N
            
            # Alpha consistency
            a_m1 = predicted_alphas[m1] # Shape (N)
            a_m2 = predicted_alphas[m2] # Shape (N)
            
            a_m1_sg = a_m1.detach()
            a_m2_sg = a_m2.detach()
            
            diff_a_12 = a_m1 - a_m2_sg
            term_a_1 = w_12 * torch.sum(diff_a_12 ** 2)
            
            diff_a_21 = a_m2 - a_m1_sg
            term_a_2 = w_21 * torch.sum(diff_a_21 ** 2)
            
            l_3d_alpha += (term_a_1 + term_a_2) / N
            
    l_3d = l_3d_color + l_3d_alpha
    
    # 3. Total Loss
    total_loss = pixel_loss + lambda_weight * l_3d
    
    return total_loss
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Formula Ambiguity**: The source provides the formula $\mu_{m_j}^{m_i} = \sqrt{m_j} \sqrt{R_{\max}} \sqrt{R_{m_i}}$ but describes the weight as being based on reliability ($R_m$) where higher $R$ reduces ambiguity. The use of the index $m_j$ in the numerator of the source formula is inconsistent with the description of weighting by $R$. Implementers should verify if $\sqrt{m_j}$ is a typo for $\sqrt{R_{m_j}}$ or if the index is intended [S1].
*   **Stop-Gradient**: Forgetting to apply the stop-gradient (`sg`) operation to the target branch in the consistency loss will cause gradients to flow through both branches, potentially destabilizing training or negating the regularization effect [S1].
*   **Sample Alignment**: The 3D consistency loss assumes that samples $i$ across different MLPs correspond to the same 3D coordinate. This requires that the sampling strategy (shift $s$ and repetition $R$) is handled such that sample indices align correctly or are interpolated appropriately [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The 3D consistency loss involves pairwise comparisons between all $M$ MLPs, leading to $O(M^2)$ complexity for the consistency term. The pixel-wise loss is $O(M)$. Total complexity per step is dominated by $O(M^2 \cdot N)$.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing predictions for all $M$ MLPs, $O(M \cdot N)$.
*   **Strengths**: Enables faster rendering by allowing fewer samples per MLP while maintaining quality through consistency regularization. It effectively regularizes the 3D space, which pixel-wise loss alone cannot do [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Increased computational overhead during training due to the pairwise consistency calculations. The choice of $\lambda$ and the weighting scheme $\mu$ are critical and sensitive [S1].
*   **When to use**: When training MIMO-NeRF or similar multi-output NeRF variants where speed-quality trade-offs are managed via sample reduction and consistency.
*   **When not to use**: For standard single-MLP NeRF training, or when evaluation metrics are needed (use sibling methods).

## Background
The MIMO objective was introduced in the paper "MIMO-NeRF: Fast Neural Rendering with Multi-Input Multi-Output Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. The method addresses the need for fast neural rendering by reformulating the NeRF into multiple MLPs with different sampling configurations, ensuring the total computational cost does not exceed that of a standard SISO MLP while improving rendering speed and quality through consistency constraints [S1].
