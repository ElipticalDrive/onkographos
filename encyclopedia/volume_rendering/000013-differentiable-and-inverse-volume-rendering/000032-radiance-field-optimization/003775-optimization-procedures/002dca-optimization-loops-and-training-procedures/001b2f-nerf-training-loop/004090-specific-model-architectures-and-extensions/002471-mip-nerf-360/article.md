# Mip-NeRF 360

Also called: Unbounded Anti-Aliased Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Mip-NeRF 360 extends the quality of neural radiance fields to unbounded scenes where the camera may rotate 360 degrees and content exists at arbitrary distances [S2]. It sets itself apart from its parent [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) and sibling [Mip-NeRF](link-to-mip-nerf) by combining a non-linear scene parameterization (contract mapping) to handle infinite space, anti-aliasing via cone-casting to resolve multi-scale details, and online distillation using a proposal network to maintain training efficiency despite increased model capacity [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Training images with known camera poses and intrinsics; a set of rays $\mathcal{R}$ sampled from these images.
**Outputs:** A trained NeRF MLP ($\Theta_{\text{NeRF}}$) and a proposal MLP ($\Theta_{\text{prop}}$); rendered images $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Conical Frustum:** Defined by mean position $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ along a ray. $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is the center of the interval, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is a diagonal matrix representing the variance (radius) of the cone at that interval.
2.  **Proposal MLP:** An MLP taking 3D coordinates and view direction, outputting scalar density $\tau$. No color output.
3.  **NeRF MLP:** An MLP taking integrated positional encoded coordinates and view direction, outputting density $\tau$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Parameters:**
*   $L$: Number of frequency bands in positional encoding.
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes of the camera.
*   $\alpha$: Weight for coarse loss (typically $1/10$).

**Steps:**

1.  **Scene Parameterization (Contract Mapping):**
    Map 3D scene coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ to a bounded domain $\mathbf{x}'$ to allocate MLP capacity effectively for unbounded scenes. The specific contract mapping function is not detailed in the provided notes, but it ensures that points at infinity are mapped to a finite boundary [S2].

2.  **Coarse Sampling:**
    Sample $N_c$ intervals along the ray. The distances $t^c$ are sorted samples from a uniform distribution between the near and far planes:
    $$ t^c \sim \mathcal{U}[t_n, t_f], \quad t^c = \text{sort}(\{t^c\}) $$
    [S2]

3.  **Proposal Network Evaluation:**
    For each coarse interval $i$, define the conical frustum with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ and covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i$. Compute the integrated positional encoding $\gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i)$:
    $$ \gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} \left[ \sin(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \right] \\ \left[ \cos(2^\ell \boldsymbol{\mu}) \exp\left(-2^{2\ell-1} \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\right) \right] \end{bmatrix} \right\}_{\ell=0}^{L-1} $$
    [S2]
    Query the proposal MLP with $\gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i)$ to get density $\tau_i^{\text{prop}}$.

4.  **Histogram Resampling (Online Distillation):**
    Compute alpha compositing weights $w_i$ for the proposal densities:
    $$ w_i = \left(1 - e^{-\tau_i^{\text{prop}}(t_{i+1}-t_i)}\right)e^{-\sum_{j < i} \tau_j^{\text{prop}}(t_{j+1}-t_j)} $$
    [S2]
    Sample $N_f$ new fine distances $t^f$ from the histogram defined by coarse distances $t^c$ and weights $w^c$ using inverse transform sampling:
    $$ t^f \sim \text{hist}(\mathbf{t}^c, \mathbf{w}^c), \quad \mathbf{t}^f = \text{sort}(\{t^f\}) $$
    [S2]

5.  **Fine Network Evaluation:**
    For each fine interval $i$, compute $\gamma(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i)$ using the fine interval boundaries. Query the NeRF MLP:
    $$ (\tau_i, \mathbf{c}_i) = \text{MLP}(\gamma(\mathbf{r}(T_i)); \Theta_{\text{NeRF}}) $$
    [S2]
    (Note: The view direction is also an input to the MLP but omitted for brevity in the source formula).

6.  **Volume Rendering:**
    Compute the final pixel color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using the fine densities and colors:
    $$ \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}) = \sum w_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    [S2]
    Where $w_i$ are computed using the fine densities $\tau_i$.

7.  **Loss Calculation and Optimization:**
    The total loss combines the reconstruction error of the coarse and fine passes. The proposal MLP is supervised by the histogram weights generated by the NeRF MLP (online distillation), while the NeRF MLP is supervised by the image reconstruction loss.
    $$ \mathcal{L} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \frac{1}{10} \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^c), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^f), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) $$
    [S2]
    Where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is mean squared error and $\mathbf{C}^*$ is the ground truth color.

import numpy as np

def integrated_positional_encoding(mu, sigma_diag, L):
    """
    Computes the integrated positional encoding for a conical frustum.
    mu: array of shape (3,) representing the mean position.
    sigma_diag: array of shape (3,) representing the diagonal of the covariance matrix.
    L: int, number of frequency bands.
    Returns: array of shape (2 * 3 * L,)
    """
    features = []
    for l in range(L):
        freq = 2**l
        # Compute the exponential decay term
        exp_term = np.exp(-0.5 * (freq**2) * sigma_diag) # Note: Source uses 2^(2l-1), which is 0.5 * 4^l. 2^(2l-1) = 2^(2l)/2.
        # The source formula is exp(-2^(2l-1) diag(Sigma)).
        # 2^(2l-1) = 2^(2l) / 2 = (4^l) / 2.
        # Let's stick strictly to the source formula: exp(-2^(2*l - 1) * sigma_diag)
        exp_term = np.exp(-(2**(2*l - 1)) * sigma_diag)
        
        sin_part = np.sin(freq * mu) * exp_term
        cos_part = np.cos(freq * mu) * exp_term
        
        features.append(sin_part)
        features.append(cos_part)
    
    return np.concatenate(features)

def volume_rendering(densities, intervals):
    """
    Computes alpha compositing weights and color.
    densities: array of shape (N,)
    intervals: array of shape (N+1,) defining the boundaries t_i to t_{i+1}
    Returns: color, weights
    """
    N = len(densities)
    # Compute transmittance
    # T_i = exp(-sum_{j=0}^{i-1} tau_j * (t_{j+1} - t_j))
    # We can compute this cumulatively
    
    # Delta distances
    deltas = intervals[1:] - intervals[:-1]
    
    # Alpha values
    alphas = 1.0 - np.exp(-densities * deltas)
    
    # Transmittance
    # T_0 = 1.0
    # T_i = T_{i-1} * (1 - alpha_{i-1})
    transmittance = np.ones(N + 1)
    for i in range(1, N + 1):
        transmittance[i] = transmittance[i-1] * (1.0 - alphas[i-1])
        
    # Weights w_i = T_i * alpha_i
    weights = transmittance[:-1] * alphas
    
    # Color is sum(w_i * c_i). Here we assume colors are passed or returned separately.
    # This function returns weights for the loss calculation.
    return weights, transmittance

def train_mipnerf360(rays, gt_colors, proposal_mlp, nerf_mlp, L, N_coarse, N_fine, t_n, t_f):
    """
    Single training step.
    rays: list of ray objects, each with origin, direction, and camera intrinsics.
    gt_colors: array of ground truth colors for the rays.
    """
    total_loss = 0
    
    for ray, gt_color in zip(rays, gt_colors):
        # 1. Coarse Sampling
        t_coarse = np.sort(np.random.uniform(t_n, t_f, N_coarse))
        
        # 2. Proposal Network Evaluation
        # For each interval, compute mu and sigma
        # mu is the midpoint, sigma depends on ray geometry (focal length, pixel size)
        # This part requires specific ray geometry calculation not fully detailed in notes
        # Assuming helper functions get_mu_sigma(t_intervals, ray)
        intervals_coarse = np.concatenate([[t_n], t_coarse, [t_f]])
        mus_coarse, sigmas_coarse = get_mu_sigma(intervals_coarse, ray)
        
        # Encode and query proposal MLP
        features_coarse = np.array([integrated_positional_encoding(mu, sigma, L) for mu, sigma in zip(mus_coarse, sigmas_coarse)])
        densities_prop = proposal_mlp(features_coarse) # Output scalar density
        
        # 3. Online Distillation Loss for Proposal
        # Compute weights from proposal densities
        weights_prop, _ = volume_rendering(densities_prop, intervals_coarse)
        # The proposal is supervised by these weights (normalized to sum to 1 for histogram)
        # Note: The source says "supervised with the histogram weights generated by the NeRF MLP".
        # In the first pass, we use proposal weights to sample. The loss for proposal is usually
        # KL divergence or MSE between proposal density distribution and NeRF density distribution.
        # The source notes say: "supervised using histogram weights generated by the NeRF MLP".
        # This implies the NeRF MLP must have been evaluated or is being evaluated simultaneously.
        # Standard Mip-NeRF 360 implementation:
        # Loss_prop = MSE(proposal_density, nerf_density) or similar.
        # However, the source explicitly states: "weights produced by the proposal MLP are not supervised using the input image, 
        # but are instead supervised with the histogram weights generated by the NeRF MLP."
        # This implies a joint optimization.
        
        # 4. Fine Sampling
        # Sample from histogram of coarse weights
        t_fine = np.sort(np.random.choice(t_coarse, N_fine, p=weights_prop / weights_prop.sum()))
        intervals_fine = np.concatenate([[t_n], t_fine, [t_f]])
        mus_fine, sigmas_fine = get_mu_sigma(intervals_fine, ray)
        
        # 5. NeRF Network Evaluation
        features_fine = np.array([integrated_positional_encoding(mu, sigma, L) for mu, sigma in zip(mus_fine, sigmas_fine)])
        nerf_output = nerf_mlp(features_fine)
        densities_nerf = nerf_output[:, 0]
        colors_nerf = nerf_output[:, 1:]
        
        # 6. Volume Rendering
        weights_nerf, _ = volume_rendering(densities_nerf, intervals_fine)
        color_pred = np.sum(weights_nerf[:, np.newaxis] * colors_nerf, axis=0)
        
        # 7. Loss Calculation
        # Reconstruction Loss for NeRF
        loss_recon_fine = np.mean((color_pred - gt_color)**2)
        
        # Coarse Reconstruction Loss (weighted by 1/10)
        # Need to render coarse pass with NeRF MLP as well for the loss term
        features_coarse_nerf = np.array([integrated_positional_encoding(mu, sigma, L) for mu, sigma in zip(mus_coarse, sigmas_coarse)])
        nerf_output_coarse = nerf_mlp(features_coarse_nerf)
        densities_nerf_coarse = nerf_output_coarse[:, 0]
        colors_nerf_coarse = nerf_output_coarse[:, 1:]
        weights_nerf_coarse, _ = volume_rendering(densities_nerf_coarse, intervals_coarse)
        color_pred_coarse = np.sum(weights_nerf_coarse[:, np.newaxis] * colors_nerf_coarse, axis=0)
        loss_recon_coarse = np.mean((color_pred_coarse - gt_color)**2)
        
        total_loss += 0.1 * loss_recon_coarse + loss_recon_fine
        
        # Proposal Loss (Online Distillation)
        # The source says proposal is supervised by histogram weights from NeRF.
        # This usually means minimizing the difference between the proposal's density distribution
        # and the NeRF's density distribution.
        # A common implementation is MSE between the normalized densities or weights.
        # Since the source doesn't give the exact formula for the proposal loss, we note it as [G].
        # Typically: Loss_prop = MSE(normalize(densities_prop), normalize(densities_nerf))
        
    return total_loss / len(rays)

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Surface Accuracy:** Mip-NeRF 360 has poor surface accuracy because it uses a density representation without explicit surface geometry [S1].
*   **Precision:** The exponential terms in the integrated positional encoding and volume rendering weights can suffer from precision issues if densities are very large or small.
*   **Unbounded Scenes:** Without the contract mapping, standard NeRF fails to render unbounded scenes correctly due to the infinite extent of the scene [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Zip-NeRF](3030-zip-nerf.md)
Zip-NeRF is a method that integrates scale-aware anti-aliased neural radiance fields with fast grid-based training to achieve lower error rates and faster convergence than prior techniques. It solves the problem of aliasing artifacts in both spatial rendering and the loss function during online distillation, which are particularly prominent when rendering scenes across multiple scales or with sparse sampling. It sets itself apart from its parent [Mip-NeRF 360](3306-mip-nerf-360.md) by replacing the large MLP with an Instant-NGP (iNGP) grid structure and introducing specific anti-aliasing mechanisms (multisampling, downweighting, and interlevel loss) to handle the high-frequency details of grid-based representations.

### [Distortion Loss with Power Transformation](4560-distortion-loss-with-power-transformation.md)
A modification of the mip-NeRF 360 distortion loss that applies a power transformation to metric distance before computing the loss. This curves metric distance to aggressively penalize "floaters" (spurious geometry artifacts) near the camera, which significantly improves the quality of rendered videos. It sets itself apart from standard distortion loss by altering the metric space of the penalty, and from [Gradient Scaling](6887-gradient-scaling.md) by modifying the loss function rather than the backpropagation gradients.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is approximately 2 times that of mip-NeRF [S2]. On the Blender dataset, training takes 24-48 hours (specifically 26.8 hours reported) on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The total model capacity is approximately 15 times larger than mip-NeRF's capacity due to the two MLPs (proposal and NeRF) [S2].
*   **Performance:**
    *   NeRF Synthetic (Blender): PSNR = 32.4 +- 0.4 dB [S1].
    *   Mip-NeRF 360 Unbounded Dataset: PSNR = 29.4 +- 0.4 dB [S1].
    *   LLFF Forward-Facing: PSNR = 26.4 +- 0.4 dB [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Reduces mean squared error by 57% compared to mip-NeRF [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Handles unbounded scenes effectively; reduces aliasing artifacts from multi-scale details; high quality on unbounded and forward-facing scenes [S1, S2].
*   **Limitations:** Poor surface accuracy; slow training compared to Instant-NGP or 3DGS; large model capacity [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When rendering unbounded scenes with 360-degree camera rotation and high-quality anti-aliasing is required, and training time is not the primary constraint.
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time rendering or surface reconstruction accuracy is required.

## Background
Mip-NeRF 360 was introduced as an extension of mip-NeRF to address the challenges of unbounded scenes, where the camera may rotate 360 degrees and content exists at any distance [S2]. It builds on the concept of mip-NeRF, which uses cone-casting to anti-alias radiance fields, and adds a non-linear scene parameterization (contract mapping) to handle infinite space, online distillation with a proposal network for efficient sampling, and a distortion-based regularizer to reduce floaters [S2]. The method was presented in the paper "Mip-NeRF 360: Unbounded Anti-Aliased Neural Radiance Fields" [S2].
