# NeRF-FF

Also called: Focus Frustums, NeRF-FF + iNGP, NeRF-FF + nerfacto, NeRF-FF + splatfacto, Neural Radiance Fields with Focus Frustums

## Overview
NeRF-FF is a plug-in strategy that mitigates the degradation of Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) reconstruction quality caused by out-of-focus (defocus blurred) regions in input images [S1]. It operates by estimating binary image masks that identify in-focus regions using "Focus Frustums"—volumes in scene space corresponding to the depth of field for each camera view [S1]. Unlike methods that employ dynamic ray generation or explicit blur kernel modeling, NeRF-FF generates static masks compatible with runtime-optimized NeRF variants like Instant-NGP, nerfacto, and splatfacto [S1]. This allows the subsequent NeRF model to omit out-of-focus pixels during training, achieving state-of-the-art visual quality with training times reduced by two orders of magnitude [S1].

## How it works
NeRF-FF functions as a preprocessing pipeline that generates per-image binary masks. These masks are applied during the training of a subsequent NeRF model (e.g., Instant-NGP) to restrict optimization to in-focus pixels only [S1]. The method consists of geometry estimation, focus indicator detection, initial frustum estimation, frustum refinement, and mask generation [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ defocus-blurred input images $I_i \in [0, 255]^{3 \times W \times H}$ for $i=1 \dots N$, where $W$ and $H$ are image width and height. Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are required for ray casting.
*   **Outputs:** A set of binary masks $M_i \in \{0, 1\}^{W \times H}$ for each input image $I_i$, where $M_i(x,y)=1$ indicates an in-focus pixel [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Preliminary NeRF Model:** An Instant-NGP instance trained on the raw blurred images to estimate scene geometry.
*   **Depth Maps:** $D_i \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times H}$, representing the distance from the camera center to the nearest surface along each ray, derived from the preliminary NeRF inference [S1].
*   **Indicator Pixels:** A set $P_i$ of 2D coordinates $(x,y)$ identified as sharp edges [S1].
*   **Focus Frustum (FF):** A volume defined by the camera's view frustum clipped by a near plane at depth $n_i$ and a far plane at depth $f_i$. It represents the estimated in-focus volume for image $i$ [S1].
*   **Refined Focus Frustum (RFF):** The updated volume defined by near plane $\hat{n}_i$ and far plane $\hat{f}_i$ after refinement [S1].
*   **Scene Points:** A set $S$ of high-density 3D points sampled from the scene geometry [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\tau$: Threshold for the discrete Laplace operator to distinguish in-focus edges. Typical value: 80 [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Hyperparameter controlling the trade-off between scene coverage and reconstruction quality during frustum refinement. It defines the minimum fraction of refined frustums that must encompass each sampled scene point. Range: $[0, 1.0]$. Typical value: 0.4 [S1].
    *   $\epsilon = 0$: Omits the refinement step.
    *   $\epsilon = 1$: Equivalent to training without NeRF-FF (no masking) [S1].

### Steps

1.  **Preliminary Geometry Estimation:** Train a preliminary Instant-NGP model on the blurred input images $I_i$ for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 2,000 iterations) [S1]. Perform inference to generate dense depth maps $D_i$ for each view [S1]. Note: While the appearance is blurry, the estimated geometry is sufficient for depth estimation [S1].

2.  **Focus Indicator Detection:** Convert each input image $I_i$ to grayscale. Apply the discrete Laplace operator $\square$ to detect sharp edges. Identify indicator pixels $P_i$ where the Laplace response exceeds threshold $\tau$:
    $$ P_i = \{ (x, y) \mid \square(I_i)(x, y) > \tau \} $$
    where $\square(I_i)(x, y)$ is the Laplace value at pixel $(x,y)$ [S1].

3.  **Initial Focus Frustum Estimation:** For each image $i$, sample the depth map $D_i$ at the coordinates in $P_i$. Perform statistical analysis on these sampled depths to estimate the initial near plane $n_i$ and far plane $f_i$ of the Focus Frustum. This defines the initial volume containing the in-focus regions [S1].

4.  **Focus Frustum Refinement:** Iteratively expand the near and far planes of the Focus Frustums to ensure robust scene coverage. The goal is to determine refined planes $\hat{n}_i$ and $\hat{f}_i$ such that:
    *   Every point $p \in S$ (a set of randomly sampled high-density scene points) is encompassed by at least a fraction $\epsilon$ of the refined Focus Frustums.
    *   The accumulated change in depth $R$ is minimized.
    
    The accumulated change $R$ is calculated only for frustums whose view frustums geometrically encompass point $p$ [S1]. The optimization selects $\hat{n}_i, \hat{f}_i$ to minimize $R$ subject to the coverage constraint defined by $\epsilon$ [S1].

5.  **Mask Generation:** Reproject the dense scene points encompassed by the refined Focus Frustums into the respective image space to generate binary masks. For each pixel $(x,y)$ in image $i$, the mask value $M_i(x,y)$ is determined by checking if the depth $D_i(x,y)$ lies within the refined depth range $[\hat{n}_i, \hat{f}_i]$:
    $$ M_i(x,y) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } D_i(x,y) \in [\hat{n}_i, \hat{f}_i] \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
    Since all pixels in the depth map are visible, other frustum boundaries (side clipping) do not need explicit checking for mask generation [S1].

6.  **Subsequent NeRF Training:** Train the final NeRF model (e.g., Instant-NGP, nerfacto, or splatfacto) using the input images $I_i$, but only utilizing pixels where $M_i(x,y) = 1$. Out-of-focus regions are omitted during the loss computation [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Fully Blurred Images:** If input images contain no in-focus regions, the preliminary NeRF reconstruction suffers from "floater artifacts" (volumetric density with no geometric counterpart). This leads to inferior depth maps and erroneous masks. NeRF-FF is not suited for datasets consisting entirely of fully blurred images [S1].
*   **Threshold $\tau$:**
    *   **Low $\tau$:** Causes bad performance because weak signals (texture in out-of-focus regions) are considered in-focus indicators, leading to incorrect depth estimates [S1].
    *   **High $\tau$:** Causes slowly decreasing performance because sharp edges in in-focus regions are missed. However, the decline is small because the initial frustums are refined in the subsequent step [S1].
*   **Dense Reconstruction Quality:** Mismatches between in-focus indicators and the actual spatial position of scene geometry cause erroneous depth estimates, negatively influencing initial Focus Frustum estimates. Low-quality dense reconstruction directly reduces mask quality [S1].
*   **Floater Artifacts:** Even with masking, the subsequent reconstruction may suffer from floater artifacts inherent to Instant-NGP, which can reduce image quality depending on the camera position [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds a preliminary training step and mask generation overhead. However, the subsequent training is accelerated.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing depth maps for all views and the preliminary NeRF model.
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Ma et al. real-world defocus dataset using an NVIDIA RTX 3090, NeRF-FF + Instant-NGP achieves an average training time of 45 seconds (including preliminary training and mask generation). This is a two orders of magnitude acceleration compared to state-of-the-art methods like PDRF, Deblur-NeRF, and DP-NeRF, which can take up to nearly one day [S1].
*   **Quality:** NeRF-FF + iNGP achieves PSNR on par with SOTA methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF) and consistently outperforms them in SSIM by a fair margin. It trails reference deblurring strategies slightly in LPIPS, but the difference is considered negligible given the speedup [S1]. On synthetic datasets with fully blurred images, it underperforms SOTA due to low-quality preliminary reconstruction [S1].
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible with Instant-NGP, nerfacto, and splatfacto (3D Gaussian Splatting). When applied to nerfacto and splatfacto, it improves PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS with negligible impact on training duration [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Extremely fast training times; compatible with static-ray NeRF variants; plug-and-play nature.
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the preliminary NeRF's geometry; fails on fully blurred inputs; sensitive to $\tau$ and $\epsilon$ parameters [S1].

## Background
NeRF-FF was proposed by T. Wirth, A. R., M. B., V. K., A. K., and D. W. F. in the paper "NeRF-FF: a plug-in method to mitigate defocus blur for runtime optimized neural radiance fields" [S1]. The method addresses the limitation of existing defocus mitigation strategies (like PDRF, Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF) which rely on dynamic ray generation and are incompatible with high-performance NeRF variants like Instant-NGP that use static rays and custom CUDA kernels [S1]. The work demonstrates that static-ray variants can achieve comparable or better quality with significantly reduced training times when combined with NeRF-FF's mask-based approach [S1].
