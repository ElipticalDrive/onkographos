# Distortion Loss with Power Transformation

Also called: Power-transformed distortion loss, P-distortion loss

## Overview
A modification of the mip-NeRF 360 distortion loss that applies a power transformation to metric distance before computing the loss. This curves metric distance to aggressively penalize 'floaters' near the camera, which significantly improves the quality of rendered videos [S1]. It sets itself apart from the standard mip-NeRF 360 distortion loss by altering the distance metric to emphasize proximity to the camera origin, addressing the issue where standard distortion loss ignores floaters close to the camera [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   Input: Metric distance $t$ of a sample point from the camera origin.
*   Output: A scalar value representing the transformed distortion loss contribution.

**Data Structures:**
*   No specific data structures are required beyond the scalar distance value and the transformation parameters.

**Parameters:**
*   Multiplier on distortion loss: $0.005$ [S1].
*   Transformation parameters: Scaling factor $s = 10000$ and exponent $\lambda = -0.25$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Take the metric distance $t$ of the sample point.
2.  Scale the metric distance by the factor $s$.
3.  Apply the power transformation $P$ to the scaled distance. The transformation is defined as:
    $$ P(t) = (s \cdot t)^{\lambda} $$
    where $s = 10000$ and $\lambda = -0.25$ [S1].
4.  Compute the distortion loss using the transformed distances $P(t)$ instead of the original metric distances $t$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Precision: The power transformation with a negative exponent can lead to large values for small distances, potentially causing numerical instability if not handled carefully [S1].
*   Boundaries: The transformation is designed to curve metric distance into a normalized space, which helps with resampling and interlevel supervision [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   Time and memory complexity: Not given in the sources.
*   Measured performance: The transformation significantly improves the quality of videos rendered from the model [S1]. It does not significantly improve test-set metrics on the 360 dataset, as floaters do not tend to contribute much to error metrics computed on still images [S1].
*   Scaling: Not given in the sources.
*   Strengths and limitations: The main strength is the aggressive penalization of floaters near the camera, improving video quality [S1]. The limitation is that it does not significantly improve test-set metrics on still images [S1].
*   When to use it and when not: Use it when video quality is important and floaters near the camera are a concern. It may not be necessary if only still image quality is important and test-set metrics are the primary evaluation criterion [S1].

## Background
*   Origin: The method is described in the Zip-NeRF paper [S1].
*   History and development: The power transformation is used to curve metric distance into a normalized space where resampling and interlevel supervision can be performed effectively [S1]. It is also used to curve metric distance within the distortion loss used in mip-NeRF 360 [S1].
*   Where it is used: It is used in the mip-NeRF 360 + iNGP baseline and ablations [S1].
