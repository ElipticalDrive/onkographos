# DoF-NeRF

Also called: Depth-of-Field Meets Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
DoF-NeRF is a neural rendering framework that extends Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to simulate the depth-of-field (DoF) effects of a physical lens, allowing it to operate on and synthesize views with arbitrary focus configurations [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF variants that assume pinhole cameras or all-in-focus inputs, DoF-NeRF explicitly models the aperture and focus distance, enabling the recovery of sharp, all-in-focus 3D representations from shallow DoF inputs and the manipulation of virtual aperture and focus parameters during rendering [S1].

## How it works
DoF-NeRF modifies the volume rendering equation to incorporate geometric optics principles, specifically the Circle of Confusion (CoC), to simulate how light scatters from spatial points across an imaging plane [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\mathcal{I} = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_N\}$ with known camera parameters (obtained via COLMAP) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model $G_\Theta$ and learned per-image aperture parameters $\hat{K}$ and focus distances $\hat{F}$ [S1].

### Data Structures and Parameters
The method introduces two learnable parameters for each of the $N$ training images:
1.  **Aperture Parameter ($K$):** A scalar value $K_i$ for image $i$. In the physical model, $K = f \times D$, where $f$ is focal length and $D$ is aperture diameter [S1].
2.  **Focus Distance ($F$):** A scalar value $F_i$ for image $i$, representing the object distance at which the lens is focused [S1].

These are stored as learnable tensors of shape $(N, 1)$ [S1].

### Formulas and Definitions
The core of DoF-NeRF is the simulation of the **Circle of Confusion (CoC)** diameter $\delta$, which determines the blur size for a point at distance $h_t$ relative to the focus distance $F$ [S1]:

$$
\delta(h_t) = K \times \left| \frac{1}{F} - \frac{1}{h_t} \right|
$$

The emergence of DoF is implemented in two steps: **Concentration** and **Scatter** [S1].

1.  **Concentration:** For a center ray $r_p$ passing through pixel $p$ at distance $h_c$, the method computes a "concentrated" color $\hat{C}^s(p)$. This aggregates radiance from points along the ray, weighted by a volume rendering kernel $K_{volume}$, normalized by the area of the CoC at the focus plane $\delta_c(p)$ [S1]:

$$
\hat{C}^s(p) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_s} K_{volume}(r_p, h_i) c(r_p(h_i), d)}{\pi \delta_c^2(p)/4}
$$

    *   $N_s$: Number of sample points along the ray.
    *   $c(\cdot)$: Color output from the NeRF MLP.
    *   $\delta_c(p)$: CoC diameter at the focus distance for pixel $p$.

2.  **Scatter:** The final observed color $\hat{I}(p)$ for pixel $p$ is computed by summing the concentrated colors from neighboring pixels $p_j$ that fall within the CoC radius of $p$. An indicator function $\mathbb{1}$ ensures only relevant neighbors contribute [S1]:

$$
\hat{I}(p) = \sum_{j=1}^{P} \mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2} \hat{C}^s(p_j)
$$

    *   $P$: Total number of rays (pixels) in the patch.
    *   $\|p - p_j\|$: Euclidean distance between pixel centers.

The optimization loss $L$ is the standard photometric error between the synthesized view $\hat{I}$ and the observed image $I$, summed over all $N$ input images [S1]:

$$
L = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \| I_n - \hat{I}_n \|_2^2
$$

### Algorithm Steps
The training process uses a two-stage optimization to ensure stability [S1]:

**Stage 1: Pinhole Initialization**
1.  Initialize NeRF parameters $\Theta$, and set aperture $K=0$ for all images.
2.  Sample random rays $\mathcal{R}$ from the input images.
3.  Render colors using standard volume rendering (no CoC scattering).
4.  Compute loss $L$ and update $\Theta$.
5.  Repeat until convergence. This stage creates a coarse 3D representation, though foreground/background may be blurred due to shallow DoF inputs [S1].

**Stage 2: Joint Optimization with Concentrate-and-Scatter**
1.  Define anchors on the imaging plane, spaced every $N_{anchor}$ pixels. Each anchor defines the center of an $N_{patch} \times N_{patch}$ patch [S1].
2.  For each iteration:
    a. Randomly select one patch.
    b. Generate rays $\mathcal{R}$ passing through pixels in the selected patch.
    c. **Concentration Step:** For each ray in the patch, compute $\hat{C}^s(p)$ using the current NeRF model, current $K$, and $F$ [S1].
    d. **Scatter Step:** Compute the final pixel colors $\hat{I}(p)$ by aggregating $\hat{C}^s$ from neighboring pixels within the CoC radius [S1].
    e. Compute loss $L$ between $\hat{I}$ and the ground truth image $I$ for the patch [S1].
    f. Perform backpropagation to update NeRF parameters $\Theta$, aperture parameters $\hat{K}$, and focus distances $\hat{F}$ jointly [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Divergence:** Directly optimizing aperture and focus with random rays can lead to divergence or sub-optimal results due to the complexity of the concentrate-and-scatter mechanism [S1]. The two-stage approach (pinhole first) mitigates this [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** Computing scatter for all rays in an image is prohibitively expensive. The patch-based ray selection is a necessary compromise to manage memory and compute time [S1].
*   **CoC Radius Calculation:** The indicator function $\mathbb{1}_{\delta_c(p) > 2 \|p - p_j\|^2}$ requires precise calculation of pixel distances and CoC diameters. Incorrect scaling of $K$ or $F$ can lead to either excessive blur (if $K$ is too large) or no DoF effect (if $K$ is too small) [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The concentrate-and-scatter rendering is more expensive than standard volume rendering due to the neighbor aggregation step. However, the patch-based selection limits the per-iteration cost to a fraction of the image [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the NeRF model, plus $2N$ learnable parameters for $K$ and $F$ [S1].
*   **Performance:** DoF-NeRF outperforms vanilla NeRF on shallow DoF inputs.
    *   **Real-world dataset (amiya scene):** PSNR 28.311 (vs NeRF 26.924), SSIM 0.9289 (vs 0.9092), LPIPS 0.1370 (vs 0.1633) [S1].
    *   **Synthetic dataset (room scene):** PSNR 29.443 (vs NeRF 26.668), SSIM 0.9135 (vs 0.8743), LPIPS 0.1502 (vs 0.1961) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Explicitly models physical optics, allowing manipulation of DoF effects and recovery of all-in-focus scenes from blurry inputs. Plug-and-play with NeRF-based frameworks [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires joint optimization of camera parameters, which can be unstable without the two-stage training. Assumes ideal optical systems (neglects ray distortions) [S1].

## Background
DoF-NeRF was introduced by Zijin Wu, Juewen Peng, and Weicai Zhong from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Huawei [S1]. It was published in the ACM Multimedia Conference (MM '22) in October 2022 [S1]. The work addresses the limitation of existing NeRF variants (like RawNeRF and NeRFocus) which assume all-in-focus inputs, by incorporating geometric optics to handle shallow DoF inputs [S1].
