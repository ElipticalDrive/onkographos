# NeRFVS
Also called: Neural Radiance Fields for Free View Synthesis via Geometry Scaffolds

## Overview
NeRFVS is a method for indoor scene free view synthesis (FVS) that utilizes a geometry scaffold generated by an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method to guide the optimization of a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) [S1]. It solves the problem of poor extrapolation and artifact generation in sparse-view or low-texture regions by introducing holistic priors—specifically pseudo depth maps and view coverage information—to constrain the implicit neural representation [S1]. Unlike standard NeRF, which struggles with novel views significantly different from training views, NeRFVS reduces distortions and floaters by enforcing 3D consistent geometry and regularizing density/color variance [S1]. It differs from siblings like [Sparse NeRF Reconstruction with Geometric and Semantic Initialization](8779-sparse-nerf-reconstruction-with-geometric-and-semantic-initi.md) by specifically targeting the "free navigation" extrapolation problem in indoor scenes using a robust depth loss and dynamic view-coverage-based regularization rather than static semantic or visibility weights [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A collection of $N$ RGB images $\{I_i\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$, camera intrinsic parameters $K_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$, and camera poses $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^6$ [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A trained NeRF model capable of rendering novel views with improved extrapolation quality [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Geometry Scaffold:** A 3D geometric representation (e.g., pseudo depth maps or point cloud) generated by an external neural reconstruction method using the input images and camera parameters [S1].
*   **View Coverage Map:** An approximation tracking the number of training observations per region [S1]. It is represented as $V_i \in [1, N]^{H \times W \times 1}$, where each element stores the count of views covering the corresponding pixel [S1]. This map ignores emitting directions [S1].

**Formulas**
1.  **Volume Rendering:**
    $$ \hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} w(\mathbf{r}(t)) \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t),\mathbf{d}) dt $$
    where $w(\mathbf{r}(t)) = T(t)\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))$, $T(t) = \exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s))ds)$, $t_n$ and $t_f$ are near and far bounds, $\sigma$ is volume density, and $\mathbf{c}$ is emitted color [S1].

2.  **Depth Rendering:**
    $$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} w(\mathbf{r}(t)) t dt $$
    [S1]

3.  **Photometric Loss:**
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{color}}(\mathbf{r}) = \|\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{r}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2 $$
    where $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ is the ground truth color [S1].

4.  **Robust Depth Loss:** The method applies a robust depth loss (referred to as Eq. 4 in the source) to guide optimization [S1]. This loss alleviates the influence of pseudo depth errors from the scaffold, allowing the model to recover accurate geometry (e.g., ceiling lamps) even when the scaffold is inaccurate [S1]. The specific functional form is not explicitly defined in the provided notes, but it is distinguished from standard $L_2$ loss by its robustness to outliers [S1].

5.  **Variance Loss:** The variance loss comprises two terms, $L_w^{var}(\mathbf{r})$ and $L_{var}(\mathbf{r})$ (referred to as Eq. 5 and Eq. 6 in the source), to constrain the density and color distribution along each ray [S1]. This reduces ambiguity in low-texture areas where arbitrary density/color distributions might cause artifacts during extrapolation [S1]. The specific functional forms are not explicitly defined in the provided notes [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **View Coverage Thresholds:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** Not given in the sources [S1].

**Steps**
1.  **Generate Geometry Scaffold:** Use an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method to generate a 3D geometry scaffold from the input images $\{I_i\}$ and camera parameters ($K_i, p_i$) [S1].
2.  **Compute View Coverage:** Generate the view coverage map $V_i$ by tracking the number of input views covering each pixel region. This is an approximation that ignores emitting directions [S1].
3.  **Initialize NeRF:** Initialize a NeRF model (e.g., compatible with instant-NGP) [S1].
4.  **Optimize with Robust Depth Loss:** Optimize the NeRF using the robust depth loss against the pseudo depth from the scaffold. This step alleviates the influence of scaffold errors [S1].
5.  **Apply Variance Regularization:** Regularize the variance of the predicted density distribution and color values along each ray to decrease ambiguity in low-texture areas [S1].
6.  **Dynamic Adjustment:** Dynamically adjust the intensity of the depth and variance losses based on the view coverage map. Regions with low view coverage receive weaker regularization to avoid penalizing valid extrapolation, while well-covered regions receive stronger constraints [S1].
7.  **Iterate:** Repeat optimization until convergence [S1].

            gt_color = get_gt_color(ray)
            gt_depth = get_gt_depth_from_scaffold(ray, scaffold)

            loss_color = l2_loss(color_samples, gt_color)
            loss_depth = robust_depth_loss(depth_samples, gt_depth)
            loss_variance = variance_loss(density_samples, transmittance_samples, color_samples)

            coverage = get_coverage_value(view_coverage, ray['pixel_coords'])
            alpha_depth = adjust_weight(coverage, 'depth')
            alpha_variance = adjust_weight(coverage, 'variance')

            total_loss = loss_color + alpha_depth * loss_depth + alpha_variance * loss_variance
            nerf_model.backward(total_loss)
            nerf_model.step()

    return nerf_model

def sample_rays(images, K, poses):
    """Generate rays for each pixel in each training image."""
    rays = []
    for i in range(len(images)):
        H, W = images[i].shape[:2]
        for r in range(H):
            for c in range(W):
                # [G] Standard ray generation from pixel coordinates, intrinsics, and pose
                rays.append({'pixel_coords': (r, c), 'image_idx': i})
    return rays

def get_gt_color(ray):
    """Return ground truth color for the ray's pixel."""
    return images[ray['image_idx']][ray['pixel_coords'][0]][ray['pixel_coords'][1]]

def get_gt_depth_from_scaffold(ray, scaffold):
    """Return pseudo depth from scaffold for the ray's pixel."""
    return scaffold[ray['image_idx']][ray['pixel_coords'][0]][ray['pixel_coords'][1]]

def l2_loss(pred, gt):
    """Compute mean squared error."""
    return sum((p - g)**2 for p, g in zip(pred, gt)) / len(pred)

def get_coverage_value(coverage_map, pixel_coords):
    """Lookup coverage count for a pixel."""
    return coverage_map[pixel_coords[0]][pixel_coords[1]]

def compute_view_coverage(images, K, poses):
    H, W = images[0].shape[:2]
    coverage_map = [[0 for _ in range(W)] for _ in range(H)]
    for i in range(len(images)):
        depth_map = get_depth_from_scaffold(i, K, poses[i])
        for r in range(H):
            for c in range(W):
                if depth_map[r][c] > 0:
                    coverage_map[r][c] += 1
    return coverage_map

def robust_depth_loss(pred_depth, gt_depth):
    diff = [pred_depth[i] - gt_depth[i] for i in range(len(pred_depth))]
    abs_diff = [abs(d) for d in diff]
    huber = [0.5 * d**2 if a < 1.0 else a - 0.5 for d, a in zip(diff, abs_diff)]
    return sum(huber) / len(huber)

def variance_loss(density, transmittance, color):
    weights = [transmittance[i] * density[i] for i in range(len(transmittance))]
    w_mean = sum(weights) / len(weights)
    loss_w_var = sum((w - w_mean)**2 for w in weights) / len(weights)
    c_mean = [sum(c[j] for c in color) / len(color) for j in range(3)]
    loss_c_var = sum((color[i][j] - c_mean[j])**2 for i in range(len(color)) for j in range(3)) / (len(color) * 3)
    return loss_w_var + loss_c_var

def adjust_weight(coverage, type):
    threshold = 1.0
    return 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-coverage + threshold))

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Inaccurate Scaffold:** Inaccurate geometry scaffolds degrade extrapolation performance [S1]. The robust depth loss helps but cannot fully compensate for exceptionally poor geometry [S1].
*   **View Coverage Approximation:** The view coverage map ignores emitting directions, which may degrade view-dependent appearance [S1]. It also fails in extreme cases like holes and thin objects [S1].
*   **Few-Shot Regions:** Reliance on pseudo depth in few-shot regions may cause poor extrapolation if the geometry is exceptionally poor [S1].
*   **Low-Texture Areas:** Without variance regularization, arbitrary density/color distributions can cause notable artifacts in extrapolation [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1]. Integration with instant-NGP accelerates training and inference by a large margin [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the ScanNet dataset in an extrapolation setting, the full NeRFVS model achieved PSNR 23.714, SSIM 0.829, and LPIPS 0.414 [S1].
    *   Without Depth Loss: PSNR 23.187, SSIM 0.821, LPIPS 0.428 [S1].
    *   Without Var Loss: PSNR 23.435, SSIM 0.822, LPIPS 0.431 [S1].
    *   Without Adjustment: PSNR 23.172, SSIM 0.821, LPIPS 0.429 [S1].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves free navigation and extrapolation in indoor scenes; reduces distortions and floaters; 3D consistent geometry scaffold; robust to scaffold errors via robust depth loss; handles low-texture areas via variance regularization [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the external neural reconstruction method; view coverage approximation ignores emitting directions; fails in extreme cases (holes, thin objects); poor extrapolation if geometry is exceptionally poor [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Indoor scene free view synthesis where extrapolation beyond training views is required, and an off-the-shelf neural reconstruction method is available [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with extremely poor geometry reconstruction; scenes requiring high-fidelity view-dependent appearance where direction-aware coverage is critical; scenes with many holes or thin objects [S1].

## Background
NeRFVS was introduced to address the limitation of standard NeRF in extrapolating to novel views significantly different from training views, particularly in indoor scenes [S1]. It builds on the observation that while neural reconstruction methods can recover holistic scene geometry successfully, their synthesized images often contain artifacts and are over-smoothed [S1]. By combining the geometric priors from neural reconstruction with the photometric fidelity of NeRF, NeRFVS aims to enable high-fidelity free navigation [S1]. The method can be integrated into instant-NGP to boost performance [S1].
