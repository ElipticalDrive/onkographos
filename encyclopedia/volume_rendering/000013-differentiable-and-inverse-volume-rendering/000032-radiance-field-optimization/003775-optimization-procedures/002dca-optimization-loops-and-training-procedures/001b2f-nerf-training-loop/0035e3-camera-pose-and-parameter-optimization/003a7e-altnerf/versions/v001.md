# AltNeRF

Also called: Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization

## Overview
AltNeRF is a framework for creating resilient Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations from monocular videos without relying on known camera poses [S1]. It solves the problem of suboptimal NeRF reconstruction caused by the lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise initial camera poses by using self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SMDE) to provide depth and pose priors [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard NeRF training by employing an alternating algorithm that harmonizes NeRF outputs with SMDE estimates, using error-tolerant regularization to handle the inherent imprecision of the depth priors [S1].

## How it works
AltNeRF comprises two main components: the Scene Prior Module (SPM) and the Scene Representation Module (SRM) [S1]. The SPM estimates initial depth and pose priors, while the SRM refines these poses and learns the 3D scene representation, regularized by the depth priors [S1]. The refined outputs are fed back to the SPM to improve its performance [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A monocular video sequence (frames $I_t$) and camera intrinsics $K$ [S1].
*   **Output:** A NeRF representation $f_n$ (density $\sigma$ and color $c$) and refined camera poses $P$ [S1].

### Data Structures and Layouts

**SPM Depth Network ($f_d$)** [S1]:
*   **Architecture:** Encoder-Decoder.
*   **Encoder:** ResNet-50 with the fully-connected layer removed [S1].
*   **Decoder:** Ten $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (two per scale) with bilinear up-sampling [S1].
*   **Output:** Depth map $D$ [S1].

**SPM Pose Network ($f_p$)** [S1]:
*   **Architecture:** ResNet-34 [S1].
*   **Input:** Pair of images $(I_t, I_s)$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Vector of 9 elements [S1].
    *   First 6 elements: Continuous rotation representation [S1].
    *   Last 3 elements: Translation vector [S1].

**SRM NeRF Network ($f_n$)** [S1]:
*   **Architecture:** Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1].
*   **Structure:** Eight fully-connected layers with skip connections for density output; one linear layer for color [S1].
*   **Input:** 3D point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and unit viewing direction $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Density $\sigma$ and color $c$ [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\alpha$: Weight for L1 term in photometric loss. Value: 0.85 [S1].
*   $\gamma$: Scalar hyper-parameter balancing reconstruction and depth regularization losses. Value: 0.08 for LLFF and CO3D; 0.15 for ScanNet and Captures [S1].
*   $S_r$: Number of SRM iterations per alternation. Value: 50,000 [S1].
*   $S_p$: Number of SPM fine-tuning iterations per alternation. Value: 500 [S1].
*   Alternations: Number of full alternating cycles. Value: 2 [S1].
*   SPM Pretrain LR: Learning rate for pretraining SPM. Value: $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SPM Fine-tune LR: Learning rate for fine-tuning SPM. Value: $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   SRM NeRF LR: Initial learning rate for NeRF learning in SRM. Value: $10^{-3}$, exponentially decays to $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SRM Pose LR: Initial learning rate for pose refinement. Value: $10^{-5}$, linearly increases to $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ after 1,000 iterations, then exponentially decays to $10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Tolerance coefficient for error-tolerant depth regularization [S1]. (Value not given in sources.)

### Step-by-Step Method

1.  **SPM Pretraining:** Pretrain the SPM ($f_d, f_p$) on a large dataset (e.g., NYUv2, VOID) to accumulate prior knowledge for 3D recovery [S1]. The training objective minimizes the final loss $L_{pt}$ [S1]:
    $$ L_{pt} = L_p + L_r + 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times L_s $$
    Where:
    *   $L_p$ is the photometric loss for reconstructing target frame $I_t$ from source $I_s$ using predicted depth and pose:
        $$ L_p(I_t, \hat{I}_t) = (1 - \text{SSIM}(I_t, \hat{I}_t)) + \frac{\alpha}{2} (1 - \alpha) \| I_t - \hat{I}_t \|_1 $$
        where $\alpha = 0.85$ [S1].
    *   $L_s$ is the edge-aware smoothness loss:
        $$ L_s = |\partial_x D| e^{-|\partial_x I|} + |\partial_y D| e^{-|\partial_y I|} $$
        where $\partial_x, \partial_y$ are image gradients [S1].
    *   $L_r$ is the distilling loss using a reference depth $D_r$ from an off-the-shelf estimator (e.g., DPT):
        $$ L_r = 1 - \text{SSIM}(D, D_r) + 0.1 \times (E_r \oplus E / \text{size}(E)) $$
        where $\oplus$ is XOR, $E_r, E$ are occluding boundary maps, and $\text{size}(\cdot)$ returns set size [S1].

2.  **Pose Initialization:** Establish a world coordinate system aligning with the first frame $I_0$ (identity matrix) [S1]. Calculate initial camera poses $P_i$ using the chain rule based on relative transformations $P_{i-1 \to i}$ predicted by SPM:
    $$ P_i = P_{i-1 \to i} \times P_{i-1} $$
    [S1].

3.  **Alternating Algorithm Workflow:** The process consists of $N$ alternating cycles (default $N=2$) [S1]. Let cycle $i$ involve SPM $\Phi_i$ and SRM $\Psi_i$ [S1].

    **Step 3a: SRM Iteration (Scene Representation Module)**
    *   **Input:** Initial pose $P_i$ and depth prior $D_i$ from SPM [S1].
    *   **Pose Refinement:** Initialize residual pose $\Delta P$ to zero. The refined pose is $\hat{P} = \Delta P \times P_i$ [S1].
    *   **Warmup Learning:** Set the learning rate of $\Delta P$ to a low value initially, linearly increasing to the original rate after 1,000 iterations to allow SRM to learn a scene prototype before updating the pose [S1].
    *   **Rendering:** Compute rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ and depth $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ for rays $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$:
        $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(t) c(t) dt $$
        $$ \hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma(t) t dt $$
        where $T(t) = \exp(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(s) ds)$, and $t_n, t_f$ are near and far bounds [S1].
    *   **Loss Calculation:** Minimize $L_{sr}$:
        $$ L_{sr} = L_c + \gamma \cdot L_e $$
        Where:
        *   $L_c$ is the reconstruction loss: $L_c = \| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \|_2$ [S1].
        *   $L_e$ is the error-tolerant depth regularization loss:
            $$ L_e = H\left( \frac{\| \hat{D} - D_i \|_1}{\hat{D} + D_i} - \epsilon, 0 \right) $$
            where $H(\cdot, \cdot)$ is the Huber loss and $\epsilon$ is the tolerance coefficient [S1].
    *   **Multi-view Consistency:** Compute uncertainty $U_t = \| D_t - D_{s \to t} \|_1$ where $D_{s \to t}$ are depth maps warped from nearby source views using current poses [S1]. Incorporate $U_t$ into loss functions ($L_r$ in SPM, $L_e$ in SRM) by weighting them with $\text{Softmin}(U_t)$ to mitigate unreliable estimates [S1].
    *   **Update:** Run for $S_r$ iterations (50,000) [S1].
    *   **Output:** Estimated depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ and improved pose $\hat{P}_{i+1}$ [S1].

    **Step 3b: SPM Fine-tuning (Scene Prior Module)**
    *   **Input:** Refined depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ and pose $\hat{P}_{i+1}$ from SRM [S1].
    *   **Distillation:** Fine-tune the depth network $f_d$ of SPM using the distilling loss $L_r$ (Eq. 6), replacing the reference depth $D_r$ with the rendered depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ [S1].
    *   **Loss:** Minimize $L_{pt}$ (Eq. 7) using the new reference [S1].
    *   **Update:** Run for $S_p$ iterations (500) [S1].
    *   **Output:** Updated depth $D_{i+1}$ and pose $P_{i+1}$ [S1].

4.  **Iteration:** Repeat Step 3 for the specified number of alternations [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Imprecise Priors:** The depth prior from SPM is not precise. Strict alignment with the prior can mislead optimization. AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization ($L_e$) to fit a possible depth range rather than exact values [S1].
*   **Dynamic Objects:** SMDE is susceptible to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances. The distilling strategy in SPM helps mitigate this [S1].
*   **Non-Convex Optimization:** Joint optimization of pose and NeRF is non-convex. Poor initialization can lead to local minima. Using SPM poses as initialization and warmup learning for pose refinement helps converge to the global minimum [S1].
*   **Occlusions:** Multi-view consistency check accounts for occlusions by computing uncertainty and weighting losses with Softmin [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 150K-200K total iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Trains in 4.0-6.4 hours on a single RTX 3090 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Creates resilient NeRF representations from monocular videos without known poses; handles imprecise depth priors via error-tolerant regularization; improves depth and pose estimates iteratively [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the pre-trained SPM; alternating algorithm increases training complexity compared to single-pass methods [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera poses are unknown and only monocular video is available [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When known camera poses are available (standard NeRF or COLMAP-based methods may be more efficient); when real-time inference is required (training time is significant) [S1].

## Background
AltNeRF was proposed in the paper "AltNeRF: Learning Robust Neural Radiance Field via Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization" presented at The Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1]. It builds on the concepts of Self-supervised Monocular Depth Estimation (SMDE) and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of existing NeRF approaches that suffer from suboptimal outcomes due to lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise camera poses [S1]. It extends the BARF approach by introducing depth regularization, improved pose initialization, and warmup learning [S1].
