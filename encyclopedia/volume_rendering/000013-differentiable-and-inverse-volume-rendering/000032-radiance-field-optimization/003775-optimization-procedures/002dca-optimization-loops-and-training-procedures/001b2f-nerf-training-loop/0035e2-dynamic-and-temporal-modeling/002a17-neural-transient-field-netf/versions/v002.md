# Neural Transient Field (NeTF)
## Overview
NeTF is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function across spatial location and direction, implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1]. Unlike NeRF, which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts [S1]. It addresses the challenge of sparse and unbalanced sampling in NLOS by using Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and a two-stage training strategy [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Sparse set of detection spots on a relay wall, time-of-flight measurements (transients) at these spots, and camera geometry [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A 3D density volume implicitly represented by the MLP, which can be converted to a mesh via thresholding and Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron that takes 5D input (3D spatial coordinates + 2D direction on the spherical wavefront) and outputs density and other properties [S1].
*   **Sampling Spots:** A set of 2D coordinates on the relay wall (e.g., $256 \times 256$ grid) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the MLP parameters [S1].
2.  **Spherical Parametrization:** Parametrize the input training data on spherical wavefronts rather than rays [S1].
3.  **MCMC Sampling:** Use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to account for sparse and unbalanced sampling in the transient field [S1]. This enables more reliable volume density estimation [S1].
4.  **Two-Stage Training:**
    *   **Stage 1:** Conduct training using all initial samples to obtain an initial reconstruction [S1].
    *   **Loss Calculation:** Calculate the loss function between the predicted and measured transients at every measuring spot on the relay wall [S1].
    *   **PDF Formation:** Normalize the calculated loss to form a probability density function (PDF) [G].
    *   **Resampling:** Resample detection spots using the PDF: higher loss corresponds to higher PDF and is more densely sampled [S1].
    *   **Stage 2:** Build a new training dataset from the resampled spots and retrain the network to refine reconstruction [S1].
5.  **Reconstruction:** The final reconstruction corresponds to a 3D density volume implicitly represented by the MLP [S1].
6.  **Surface Extraction:** Map the volume to surfaces, e.g., by thresholding followed by Marching Cubes [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   The loss function $L_i$ is calculated between predicted and measured transients at measuring spot $i$ [S1].
*   The resampling probability is $P_i = \frac{L_i}{\sum_j L_j}$ [G].

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Grid:** Typically $256 \times 256$ spots on the relay wall [S1].
*   **Sparse Sampling:** Tested down to $4 \times 4$ spots [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Confocal vs. Non-Confocal:** NeTF does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups in its current form [S1].
*   **Occlusions:** Occlusion can lead to strong reconstruction artifacts if not handled properly; NeTF handles this better than Phasor Field [S1].
*   **Noise:** Brute-force implementation of Marching Cubes may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].
## Subtopics

### Two-stage Training
A training strategy to address sampling bias (undersampling) in NLOS reconstruction by resampling detection spots based on reconstruction loss [S1]. It involves an initial training phase, loss calculation, PDF formation, resampling, and retraining [S1]. It is chosen when dealing with sparse or imbalanced sampling, such as recovering fine details like bunny ears that are missing in one-stage training [S1]. The loss map is visualized on a $256 \times 256$ sampling grid on the relay wall, where red implies higher loss (and thus high PDF in subsequent sampling) and blue implies low loss [S1]. Using two-stage training, the method manages to recover bunny ears largely missing in one-stage training and prior art [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Accuracy:** On the Bunny dataset (confocal), NeTF achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.65 cm, compared to 2.38 cm for DLCT and 2.43 cm for F-K [S1]. On the Bunny dataset (non-confocal), NeTF achieved an MAE of 7.45 cm, compared to 3.77 cm for FBP and 7.02 cm for BP [S1].
*   **Quality:** NeTF produces sharper edges for both front and back planes in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field and DLCT/F-K [S1]. It manages to separate different parts of a mesh, whereas DLCT exhibits adhesion between different parts [S1].
*   **Noise:** NeTF reconstruction incurs much lower noise than State-of-the-Art (SOTA) methods, similar to NeRF [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].
*   **Robustness:** NeTF produces reasonable reconstructions even with very sparse sampling spots (e.g., $8 \times 8$) [S1].
## Background
NeTF was introduced as a novel neural modeling framework for NLOS imaging [S1]. It is analogous to NeRF but uses spherical wavefronts instead of rays [S1]. It was tested on synthetic and real NLOS data, including the Bunny, Lucy, Indonesian, Bike, Letter Z, and semi-occluded scenes [S1].
