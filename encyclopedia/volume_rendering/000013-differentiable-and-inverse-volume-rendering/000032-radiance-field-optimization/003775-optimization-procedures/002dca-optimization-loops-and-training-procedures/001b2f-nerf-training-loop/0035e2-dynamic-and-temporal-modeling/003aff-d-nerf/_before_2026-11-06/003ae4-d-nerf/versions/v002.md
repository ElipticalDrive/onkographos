# D-NeRF

Also called: Dynamic-NeRF, Neural Radiance Fields for Dynamic Scenes

## Overview
D-NeRF is a method for synthesizing novel views at an arbitrary point in time of dynamic scenes with complex non-rigid geometries, optimizing an underlying deformable volumetric function from a sparse set of input monocular views without ground-truth geometry or multi-view images [S1]. It sets itself apart from naive extensions of NeRF (which map $(x, y, z, t)$ directly to density and radiance) by decomposing learning into two modules: a spatial mapping that encodes the scene into a canonical space, and a mapping that transforms this canonical representation into the deformed scene at a particular time, thereby effectively exploiting temporal redundancy [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A sparse set of $T$ RGB images $\{I_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and their corresponding camera pose matrices $\{T_t\}_{t=1}^T$, where $I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ and $T_t \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ is an SE(3) matrix [S1, S2]. The method assumes a single view per time instant [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel color $C'(p, t)$ for a pixel $p$ at time $t$, given a camera ray defined by origin $o$ and direction $d$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The model consists of two deep fully connected networks (MLPs) without convolutional layers [S1]:
1.  **Deformation Network ($\Psi_t$):** Maps scene deformations to a common canonical configuration.
    *   **Input:** Encoded 3D position $x$ and time $t$.
    *   **Output:** Displacement vector $\Delta x \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   **Architecture:** 8-layer MLP with ReLU activations. No non-linearity applied to the output $\Delta x$ [S1].
2.  **Canonical Network ($\Psi_x$):** Regresses volume density and view-dependent RGB color from the canonical configuration.
    *   **Input:** Encoded canonical position $(x + \Delta x)$ and viewing direction $d$.
    *   **Output:** Emitted color $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and volume density $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}$.
    *   **Architecture:** 8-layer MLP with ReLU activations. A final sigmoid non-linearity is applied to $\mathbf{c}$ and $\sigma$ [S1].

**Positional Encoding:**
Raw coordinates are encoded into a higher-dimensional space using $\gamma(\mathbf{p}) = \langle (\sin(2^l \pi \mathbf{p}), \cos(2^l \pi \mathbf{p})) \rangle_{l=0}^{L-1}$, applied independently to each coordinate and camera view component [S1, S2].
*   For spatial coordinates $x$: $L = 10$ [S1, S2].
*   For viewing direction $d$ and time $t$: $L = 4$ [S1, S2].

**Canonical Configuration:**
The canonical scene is defined as the scene at time $t=0$. This enforces that the deformation displacement is zero at $t=0$:
$$ \Psi_t(x, 0) = 0 $$
This anchors all time instances to a common space, interconnecting them [S1].

**Rendering Process:**
For a point $x$ on a camera ray at time $t$ and viewing direction $d$:
1.  Transform the point to canonical space using the deformation network:
    $$ \Delta x = \Psi_t(x, t) $$
    $$ x_{\text{canonical}} = x + \Delta x $$
2.  Query the canonical network with the transformed point and direction:
    $$ [\mathbf{c}, \sigma] = \Psi_x(x_{\text{canonical}}, d) $$

**Volume Rendering:**
The expected color $C(p, t)$ of a pixel $p$ at time $t$ is the integral along the ray segment $[h_n, h_f]$:
$$ C(p, t) = \int_{h_n}^{h_f} T(h, t) \sigma(p(h, t)) \mathbf{c}(p(h, t), d) \, dh $$
where:
*   $p(h, t) = x(h) + \Psi_t(x(h), t)$ is the point on the ray $x(h)$ transformed to canonical space [S1, S2].
*   $T(h, t) = \exp \left( - \int_{h_n}^{h} \sigma(p(s, t)) \, ds \right)$ is the accumulated transmittance [S1, S2].

**Numerical Approximation:**
The integral is approximated via stratified sampling. $N$ samples $\{h_n\}_{n=1}^N$ are drawn from evenly-spaced ray bins in $[h_n, h_f]$ [S1]. The rendered pixel color $C'(p, t)$ is:
$$ C'(p, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} T'(h_n, t) \alpha(h_n, t, \delta_n) \mathbf{c}(p(h_n, t), d) $$
where:
*   $\delta_n = h_{n+1} - h_n$ is the distance between quadrature points [S1].
*   $\alpha(h, t, \delta) = 1 - \exp(-\sigma(p(h, t)) \delta)$ is the opacity [S1].
*   $T'(h_n, t) = \exp \left( - \sum_{m=1}^{n-1} \sigma(p(h_m, t)) \delta_m \right)$ is the discrete transmittance [S1].

**Training:**
Parameters of $\Psi_x$ and $\Psi_t$ are optimized simultaneously by minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between rendered pixels $C'$ and ground-truth pixels $\hat{C}$:
$$ \text{Loss} = \frac{1}{N_s} \sum_{i=1}^{N_s} \| \hat{C}(p_i, t) - C'(p_i, t) \|^2 $$
where $N_s$ is the batch size in rays [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Canonical Time:** $t=0$ [S1].
*   **Positional Encoding Depth:** $L=10$ for $x$, $L=4$ for $d$ and $t$ [S1, S2].
*   **Network Depth:** 8 layers for both $\Psi_x$ and $\Psi_t$ [S1].
*   **Training Images:** $400 \times 400$ resolution [S1].
*   **Iterations:** 800,000 [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** $N_s = 4096$ rays [S1].
*   **Samples per Ray:** 64 [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, exponential decay to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   **Curriculum Learning:** Input images are sorted by timestamp, and images with higher timestamps are incrementally added to improve convergence [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Naive Extension Failure:** Directly mapping $(x, y, z, t)$ to density/radiance does not produce satisfying results because it fails to exploit temporal redundancy [S1].
*   **Single View Per Time:** The method is designed for sparse monocular views (one view per time instant). It does not assume multiple views per time instance [S1].
*   **Canonical Anchor:** The choice of $t=0$ as the canonical configuration is arbitrary but necessary to enforce $\Psi_t(x, 0) = 0$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires 800k iterations. On a single Nvidia GTX 1080, training takes approximately 2 days [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:**
    *   **Hell Warrior Scene:** MSE $31 \times 10^{-4}$, PSNR 23.19, SSIM 0.93, LPIPS 0.08. (Comparison: NeRF MSE $44 \times 10^{-3}$, PSNR 13.52) [S1].
    *   **T-Rex Scene:** MSE $5 \times 10^{-4}$, PSNR 32.80, SSIM 0.98, LPIPS 0.03. (Comparison: NeRF MSE $21 \times 10^{-3}$, PSNR 18.19) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles rigid, articulated, and non-rigid motions. Can synthesize novel views at arbitrary times from sparse monocular inputs without ground-truth geometry [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on a single view per time instant, which may limit geometric consistency compared to multi-view methods. Performance depends heavily on the quality of the canonical space alignment [S1].

## Background
D-NeRF was proposed by Albert Pumarola, Enric Corona, Gerard Pons-Moll, and Francesc Moreno-Noguer from the Institut de Robòtica i Informàtica Industrial (CSIC-UPC) and the Max Planck Institute for Informatics [S1]. It was published in CVPR 2021 (arXiv:2011.13961v1, Nov 27, 2020) [S1]. The method builds on NeRF [26] and generalizes it to handle non-rigid scenes. It differs from T-NeRF, which uses a 6D input $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi, t)$ directly without a canonical mapping [S1]. It is compared against Neural Volumes (NV), Scene Representation Networks (SRN), and Layered Light Field Fusion (LLFF) [S2].
