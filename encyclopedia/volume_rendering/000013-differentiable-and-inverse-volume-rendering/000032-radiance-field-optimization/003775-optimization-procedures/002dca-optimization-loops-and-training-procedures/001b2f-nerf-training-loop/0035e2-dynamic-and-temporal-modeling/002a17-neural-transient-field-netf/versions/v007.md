# Neural Transient Field (NeTF)


## Overview
Neural Transient Field (NeTF) is a neural modeling framework for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) imaging that recovers the 5D transient function (spatial location and direction) implicitly represented by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) [S1]. Unlike NeRF, which parametrizes input training data along lines (rays), NeTF parametrizes input training data on spherical wavefronts, formulating the NLOS process under spherical coordinates analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates [S1]. It is distinguished by its use of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to handle sparse and unbalanced sampling, and a two-stage training strategy to address sampling bias [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Sparse set of detection spots on a relay wall, capturing transient responses (time-of-flight histograms) from hidden scenes. The data is parametrized on spherical wavefronts [S1].
*   **Output:** A 3D density volume implicitly represented by the MLP, which can be mapped to surfaces via thresholding and Marching Cubes [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **MLP:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron that implicitly represents the 5D transient function. The specific architecture (number of layers, hidden units, activation functions) is not detailed in the sources [S1].
*   **Spherical Coordinates:** The input domain for the MLP is defined in spherical coordinates, corresponding to the geometry of the NLOS setup (relay wall positions and viewing directions) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize the MLP to represent the transient field.
2.  **Sampling:** Sample detection spots on the relay wall. Due to sparsity, occluded points may be captured from very few viewpoints [S1].
3.  **Forward Pass:** For each sampled spot, query the MLP with the corresponding spherical coordinates to predict the transient response (density and color/intensity over time) [S1].
4.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the loss function between the predicted transients and the measured transients at every measuring spot on the relay wall [S1].
5.  **MCMC Sampling:** Use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) to account for sparse and unbalanced sampling in the transient field. This enables more reliable volume density estimation and accurate shape estimation by recovering details caused by occlusions and non-uniform albedo [S1].
6.  **Two-Stage Training (Optional but recommended for bias):**
    *   **Stage 1:** Conduct training using all initial samples to obtain an initial reconstruction [S1].
    *   **Loss Normalization:** Calculate the loss at every measuring spot. Normalize the calculated loss to form a probability density function (PDF) [S1].
    *   **Resampling:** Resample detection spots using the PDF. Higher loss corresponds to higher PDF and is more densely sampled [S1].
    *   **Stage 2:** Build a new training dataset from the resampled spots and retrain the network to refine reconstruction [S1].
7.  **Surface Extraction:** Map the implicit 3D density volume to surfaces by thresholding the density values followed by Marching Cubes [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Volume Rendering:** The final reconstruction corresponds to a 3D density volume. While the specific NLOS rendering equation is not explicitly written out, it is analogous to volume rendering under Cartesian coordinates but formulated under spherical coordinates [S1].
*   **Loss Function:** $L = \sum_{i} || \text{PredictedTransient}_i - \text{MeasuredTransient}_i ||^2$ (implied by "loss function between the predicted and measured transients") [S1].
*   **PDF for Resampling:** $P(i) = \frac{L_i}{\sum_j L_j}$, where $L_i$ is the loss at spot $i$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Resolution:** Tested with 256x256, 32x32, 16x16, 8x8, and 4x4 spots on the relay wall [S1].
*   **Thresholding:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion:** Occlusion can lead to strong reconstruction artifacts if not handled properly, as occluded points may be captured from very few viewpoints [S1].
*   **Sampling Bias:** Undersampling leads to missing details (e.g., bunny ears) [S1].
*   **Confocal vs. Non-Confocal:** NeTF does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups in its current form [S1].
*   **Noise:** Brute-force implementation of Marching Cubes may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].

def train_NeTF(measured_transients, initial_spots, num_stages=2):
    # Initialize MLP to represent 5D transient function
    mlp = initialize_MLP()
    
    # Stage 1: Initial Training
    spots = initial_spots

        # Sample spots (potentially using MCMC for better coverage)
        current_spots = sample_spots_mcmc(spots, mlp)
        
        # Forward pass: predict transients
        predicted_transients = []
        for spot in current_spots:
            # Query MLP with spherical coordinates of the spot
            pred = mlp.forward(spot.spherical_coords)
            predicted_transients.append(pred)
        
        # Calculate loss
        losses = calculate_loss(predicted_transients, measured_transients[current_spots])
        
        # Backpropagate
        mlp.backward(losses)
    
    # Stage 2: Resampling and Refinement (if enabled)
    if num_stages > 1:
        # Normalize losses to form PDF
        pdf = normalize_losses(losses)
        
        # Resample detection spots based on PDF
        # Higher loss -> higher PDF -> more densely sampled
        new_spots = resample_spots(initial_spots, pdf)
        
        # Retrain network with new dataset

            current_spots = sample_spots_mcmc(new_spots, mlp)
            predicted_transients = []
            for spot in current_spots:
                pred = mlp.forward(spot.spherical_coords)
                predicted_transients.append(pred)
            
            losses = calculate_loss(predicted_transients, measured_transients[current_spots])
            mlp.backward(losses)
    
    # Surface Extraction

## Subtopics

### Two-stage Training
A training strategy to address sampling bias (undersampling) in NLOS reconstruction by resampling detection spots based on reconstruction loss [S1]. It involves an initial training phase, followed by loss normalization to form a PDF, resampling spots (higher loss = higher PDF = denser sampling), and a second training phase [S1]. It is particularly effective for recovering fine details like bunny ears that are missing in one-stage training [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Accuracy:** On the Bunny dataset (confocal), NeTF achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.65 cm, compared to 2.38 cm for DLCT and 2.43 cm for F-K [S1]. On the Bunny dataset (non-confocal), NeTF achieved an MAE of 7.45 cm, compared to 3.77 cm for FBP and 7.02 cm for BP [S1].
*   **Quality vs. MAE:** MAE does not fully reflect reconstruction quality. NeTF produces sharper edges for both front and back planes in semi-occluded scenes compared to Phasor Field (most sensitive to occlusions) and DLCT/F-K (can only recover one plane at high accuracy) [S1]. NeTF manages to separate different parts of a mesh, whereas DLCT exhibits adhesion between different parts [S1].
*   **Noise:** NeTF reconstruction incurs much lower noise than State-of-the-Art (SOTA) methods, similar to NeRF [S1].
*   **Robustness:** NeTF produces reasonable estimations even with very sparse sampling spots (e.g., 8x8 or 16x16) [S1].
*   **Computational Cost:** Learning-based techniques like NeTF are substantially more computationally expensive than previous optimization techniques [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not separately treat confocal and non-confocal setups [S1]. Brute-force surface extraction may lead to noise on smooth surfaces [S1].

## Background
NeTF was introduced as a novel neural modeling framework for NLOS imaging [S1]. It is analogous to NeRF but adapts the concept to spherical coordinates for NLOS data [S1]. It was tested on synthetic and real NLOS data, including the Bunny, Lucy, Indonesian, Bike, Letter Z, and semi-occluded scenes [S1]. The work was supported by NSFC and STCSM [S1].
