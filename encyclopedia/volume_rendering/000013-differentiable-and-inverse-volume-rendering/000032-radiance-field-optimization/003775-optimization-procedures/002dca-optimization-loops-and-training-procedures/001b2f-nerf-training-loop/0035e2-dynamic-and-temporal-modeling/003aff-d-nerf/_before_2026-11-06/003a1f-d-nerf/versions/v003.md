# D-NeRF

Also called: Deformable Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
D-NeRF is a method for dynamic scene representation that decomposes the scene into a static canonical space and a learnable deformation field [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of novel view synthesis for non-rigidly moving objects by mapping dynamic points to a static template, distinguishing itself from methods that treat time as a direct input to the radiance field without spatial deformation [S1, S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** 3D spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$, viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$, and time $t$.
*   **Outputs:** Volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Data Structures:**
The method utilizes two neural networks:
1.  **Deformation Network ($\mathcal{F}_{def}$):** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that takes spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and time $t$ as input and outputs a displacement vector $\Delta \mathbf{x}$.
2.  **Canonical Radiance Field ($\mathcal{F}_{can}$):** A standard NeRF MLP that takes the deformed spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}'$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ as input to predict density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$.

**Formulas:**
The deformation maps a point $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t$ to the canonical space:
$$ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \mathcal{F}_{def}(\mathbf{x}, t) $$
The radiance field then evaluates the canonical point:
$$ (\sigma, \mathbf{c}) = \mathcal{F}_{can}(\mathbf{x}', \mathbf{d}) $$
Rendering integrates these values along rays, where the ray sampling positions are dynamically warped by the deformation field at each time step [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  Sample points $\mathbf{x}$ along the camera ray for the target time $t$.
2.  Pass $\mathbf{x}$ and $t$ through the deformation network to compute the displacement $\Delta \mathbf{x} = \mathcal{F}_{def}(\mathbf{x}, t)$.
3.  Compute the canonical coordinate $\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \Delta \mathbf{x}$.
4.  Pass $\mathbf{x}'$ and the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ through the canonical radiance field to obtain $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$.
5.  Perform volumetric rendering using standard alpha compositing on the sampled $\sigma$ and $\mathbf{c}$ values [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   Network depth and width: Not given in the sources.
*   Time encoding: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Complex Motion:** The deformation field is difficult to learn when scene motions are complex, leading to blurry results similar to Neural Volumes [S1].
*   **Topological Changes:** The method assumes continuous deformation fields, making it unable to simulate topological changes (e.g., object splitting) which require discontinuities [S2].
*   **Optical Effects:** The mapping from observation space to canonical space may fail to reconstruct optical effects like transparency or specular reflections caused by non-rigid motion [S2].

## Subtopics

### Neural Transient Field (NeTF)
Uses a transient field to model time-varying phenomena like light transport, handling high-frequency temporal changes differently than deformation [4692-neural-transient-field-netf.md].

### Projected Color-Guided Dynamic NeRF
Uses projected color cues for implicit correspondence, avoiding explicit deformation fields [7523-projected-color-guided-dynamic-nerf.md].

### On-the-Fly Dynamic NeRF Training
Processes frames sequentially with a Markov assumption to reduce memory usage, contrasting with D-NeRF's global deformation field [7582-on-the-fly-dynamic-nerf-training.md].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** D-NeRF achieves competitive PSNR and SSIM but suffers from rendering artifacts in complex motions [S1].
*   **Strengths and Limitations:** It effectively handles non-rigid motion by leveraging a static canonical representation. However, it struggles with high-frequency details and complex deformations where the deformation field becomes unstable [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for scenes with smooth, non-rigid motion where a canonical template can be established.
*   **When not:** Unsuitable for scenes with topological changes or extremely complex, discontinuous motions [S1, S2].

## Background
D-NeRF was proposed by Park et al. in 2021 [S1, S2]. It is a baseline method in dynamic NeRF research that introduced the concept of decomposing dynamic scenes into a static canonical space and a deformation field [S1]. It is widely cited as a foundational approach for handling time as an additional input for system rendering while controlling camera view variables [S2].
