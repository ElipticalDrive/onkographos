# TermiNeRF

Also called: Ray Termination Prediction, TermiNeRF Sampling Distribution Learning

## Overview
TermiNeRF is a method for efficient neural rendering that jointly trains a lightweight sampling network alongside a color network to predict the distribution of ideal sampling locations along a viewing ray [S1]. By concentrating samples in regions of high opacity and skipping free space, it significantly reduces the number of color network evaluations required per pixel, achieving rendering times of ≤ 1 second for high-resolution images [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) by predicting a continuous probability distribution of weights directly from ray parameters, rather than relying on a discrete coarse-to-fine hierarchy or spatial subdivision of the image plane [S1, S2].

## How it works
TermiNeRF splits the standard NeRF architecture into two components: a **Sampling Network** that predicts sample importance weights with a single forward pass per ray, and a **Shading (Color) Network** that evaluates only the most significant samples [S1, S2]. The method uses a specific ray parameterization to ensure a bijection between rays and input vectors, avoiding ambiguity in depth prediction [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input**: A viewing ray defined by origin $O$ and direction $d$. The ray is parameterized into a fixed-length segment $[A, B]$ with length $\ell = 4.0$ [S1].
*   **Output**: A set of $N$ normalized weights $\hat{w}_i$ representing the probability density for sampling locations along the ray, and the final rendered color $C$ [S1].

### Data Structures and Layout
**Sampling Network Architecture**:
*   8 hidden layers with 256 units each.
*   Skip connection from the input to the fifth hidden layer [S1].
*   Input: Ray parameters (derived from $O$ and $d$).
*   Output: $N$ raw weights corresponding to $N$ bins along the ray [S1].

**Ray Parameterization**:
To avoid the network solving for arbitrary ray origins, TermiNeRF enforces a constant segment length $\ell = 4.0$ [S1].
*   Segment endpoints $A$ and $B$ are defined such that $|B - A| = \ell$.
*   The direction is $d = B - A$.
*   The midpoint of the segment is the closest point on the line to the scene origin: $O_{mid} = \frac{A+B}{2} = \text{proj}_{AB}(0)$ [S1].
*   Additional samples are placed using **centered-logarithmic sampling** between $A$ and $B$. The sample positions $s_i$ are formed by concatenating two sequences $l$ and $u$:
    $$ l_i = 1 - 2^{\frac{1-i}{N/2-1}}, \quad i \in \{1, ..., N/2 - 1\} $$
    $$ u_j = 2^{\frac{j-N/2}{N/2-1}}, \quad j \in \{1, ..., N/2\} $$
    The sample points are: $\{A, A + s_2 \cdot d, \dots, A + s_{N-1} \cdot d, B\}$ [S1].

### Training Procedure
The training occurs in three phases to ensure stable convergence [S1]:

1.  **Pre-training**: The color network is trained independently using standard NeRF rendering (e.g., 64 coarse + 128 fine samples) until convergence [S1].
2.  **Sampling Network Training**: The sampling network is trained to predict weights that match the distribution generated by the pre-trained color network.
3.  **Joint Fine-tuning**: Both networks are fine-tuned together, alternating updates [S1].

### Ground Truth Generation and Distribution Matching
To train the sampling network, ground truth weights $w_i$ are derived from the fine NeRF network [S1]. For each ray, the fine network is evaluated at $z_i$ (distance from origin), producing 196 tuples $(z_i, w_i)$ for 64+128 samples [S1]. The weight for sample $i$ is:
$$ w_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) (1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)) $$
where $\delta_i = z_{i+1} - z_i$ [S1].

Since the sampling network outputs weights for fixed bins defined by the centered-logarithmic points, and the NeRF weights are at arbitrary $z_i$, a **Distribution Matching** pipeline is required before computing the loss [S1]:

1.  **Max-Resampling**: To preserve peaks and handle uneven bin sizes, weights are assigned to new bins $\hat{b}_j$ delimited by $\hat{z}_j$. The weight $\hat{w}_j$ for bin $j$ is:
    $$ \hat{w}_j = \max \{ w_i \mid \hat{z}_j \le z_i \le \hat{z}_{j+1} \} \cup \{ w'_j, w'_{j+1} \} $$
    where $w'_j$ is the linear interpolation of weights from the old distribution at $\hat{z}_j$ [S1].
2.  **Gaussian Blurring**: To reduce noise in the supervision signal, a Gaussian blur is applied to the resampled weights. The blur window size $K$ covers a Euclidean distance $d$:
    $$ d = \frac{K}{N_{samples}} \cdot \ell $$
    Typical parameters: $K=9$, $\sigma=3.0$ [S1].
3.  **Normalization**: The final ground truth weights are normalized:
    $$ \hat{w}_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_{j=0}^{N} w_j} $$
    [S1].

### Rendering Pipeline
1.  **Ray Discretization**: The viewing ray is parameterized into the fixed segment $[A, B]$ with centered-logarithmic points [S1].
2.  **Sampling Network Evaluation**: The sampling network is evaluated once per ray to predict $N$ weights [S1].
3.  **Stochastic Sampling**: $N_{render}$ points are drawn stochastically from the predicted distribution [S1].
    *   During **joint fine-tuning**, to prevent the sampling network from never seeing unsampled regions, 128 points are drawn from the predicted distribution, and 64 additional equidistant points are sampled along the whole ray between near and far z-values [S1].
4.  **Color Network Evaluation**: The shading network evaluates the color and density $\sigma$ only at the selected points [S1].
5.  **Volumetric Rendering**: The final color is aggregated using standard volume rendering equations [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Uneven Segment Lengths**: Using sphere intersection representations creates uneven segment lengths, increasing computational pressure on the network. This is mitigated by enforcing a constant length segment $\ell$ [S1].
*   **Arbitrary Training Signals**: Training both networks jointly from the start is inefficient because the sampling network receives arbitrary signals from an untrained color network. A pre-trained color network is required for initialization [S1, S2].
*   **Unsampled Regions**: If the sampling network predicts low weights for a region, the color network never sees it, leading to a feedback loop where geometry is never learned. This is handled by adding equidistant samples during joint fine-tuning [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Speed**: TermiNeRF achieves a 14x speedup relative to standard NeRF [S1]. Rendering time is reduced to ≤ 1 second for high-resolution images [S1]. It achieves a 7.32x speedup compared to NeRF with 8+16 samples (32 total forward passes) while maintaining similar PSNR/LPIPS [S1].
*   **Memory**: The sampling network has the same architecture size as a NeRF network (8 hidden layers, 256 units), but the color network is evaluated far fewer times, reducing memory bandwidth pressure during rendering [S1].
*   **Quality**: Achieves similar or higher PSNR/LPIPS than standard NeRF with significantly fewer forward passes [S1].
*   **Fine-tuning**: Scenes can be fine-tuned up to the penultimate step in only three minutes on a GTX 1080 [S1].

## Background
TermiNeRF was introduced by Martin Piala and Ronald Clark from Imperial College London in 2021 [S1]. It addresses the slow rendering times of neural radiance fields by learning a direct mapping from camera rays to locations along the ray that are most likely to influence the pixel's final appearance [S1]. It is cited as a method that splits the NeRF network into a sampling network and a shading network, conditioned on the density of a pretrained NeRF [S2].
