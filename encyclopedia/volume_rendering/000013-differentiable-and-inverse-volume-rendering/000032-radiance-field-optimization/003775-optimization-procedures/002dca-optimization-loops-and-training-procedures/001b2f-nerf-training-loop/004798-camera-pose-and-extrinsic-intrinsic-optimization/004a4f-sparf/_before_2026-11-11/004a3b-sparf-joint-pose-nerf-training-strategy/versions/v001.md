# SPARF Joint Pose-NeRF Training Strategy

Also called: joint pose-NeRF optimization, pose-NeRF training

## Overview
The SPARF Joint Pose-NeRF Training Strategy is a staged optimization procedure that simultaneously refines noisy camera poses and trains a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) from sparse, wide-baseline input images [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard NeRF training by integrating multi-view correspondence and depth consistency losses to guide pose estimation, while employing a coarse-to-fine positional encoding schedule to prevent overfitting during the joint optimization phase [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ training images $\{I_i\}$, initial camera poses $\{\hat{P}_i\}$ (potentially noisy), and depth ranges $[t_n, t_f]$ for each view.
*   **Outputs:** Refined camera poses $\{P_i\}$ and a trained NeRF model (MLP parameters).

**Data Structures**
*   **Camera Pose:** Represented by a world-to-camera transformation matrix $P = [R | t]$.
    *   Translation $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Optimized directly as a 3D embedding vector.
    *   Rotation $R \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Optimized via a 6D embedding vector $r \in \mathbb{R}^6$. The vector $r$ is composed of two 3D vectors $a_1, a_2$, such that $r = [a_1^T, a_2^T]^T$.
*   **NeRF:** Multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with 128 hidden units per layer. Uses softplus activation for volume density $\sigma$ [S1].
*   **Correspondences:** Pre-computed dense correspondences between all image pairs using PDC-Net [S1]. Matches are filtered by a confidence threshold $\gamma = 0.95$ (PR operator) and optionally by cyclic consistency $< 1.5$ pixels [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Depth Sampling:**
    *   LLFF Dataset: Inverse depth space with bounds $1/t_n = 1$ and $1/t_f = 0.05$ [S1].
    *   DTU/Replica Datasets: Metric space bounds $[t_n, t_f]$ [S1].
    *   Noise Expansion: For joint training with noisy poses, the depth range is expanded by factor $\epsilon = 0.3$ to $[(1 - \epsilon)t_n, (1 + \epsilon)t_f]$ [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:**
    *   Multi-view correspondence loss weight $\lambda_c = 10^{-3}$ (default) or $10^{-4}$ (DTU with fixed GT poses) [S1].
    *   Depth consistency loss weight $\lambda_d = 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam [S1].
    *   NeRF parameters: Initial LR $5 \times 10^{-4}$, decaying exponentially to $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
    *   Camera poses: Initial LR $1 \times 10^{-3}$, decaying to $1 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Image Resolution:** Resized to $360 \times 600$ [S1].
*   **Sampling:** 1024 pixel rays sampled per optimization step for photometric loss [S1].

**Rotation Parametrization**
The 6D vector $r = [a_1^T, a_2^T]$ is converted to a rotation matrix $R = [b_1, b_2, b_3]$ using a Gram-Schmidt-like process [S1]:
$$
b_1 = \frac{a_1}{\|a_1\|}
$$
$$
b_2 = \frac{a_2 - (a_2 \cdot b_1)b_1}{\|a_2 - (a_2 \cdot b_1)b_1\|}
$$
$$
b_3 = b_1 \times b_2
$$
$$
R = [b_1, b_2, b_3]
$$
This ensures orthogonality without the discontinuities of quaternions or Euler angles [S1].

**Training Stages**
The training is split into two stages [S1]:

1.  **Stage 1 (Joint Refinement):** Lasts for 30% of total iterations. Poses and the coarse MLP are trained jointly.
    *   **Positional Encoding:** Frequency width linearly adjusted from 40% to 70% of total iterations. For the first 40% of iterations, no positional encodings are applied to 3D points or ray directions [S1].
2.  **Stage 2 (NeRF Fine-tuning):** Lasts for the remaining 70% of iterations. Poses are frozen. Both coarse and fine MLPs are trained (though a single MLP is used for LLFF to save time, as hierarchical sampling yields identical results) [S1].
    *   **Positional Encoding:** Frequency width linearly adjusted from 10% to 50% of total iterations [S1].
    *   **Loss Weighting:** $\lambda_c$ is halved every 10,000 iterations to gradually increase the importance of the photometric loss and correct geometry errors induced by potentially erroneous correspondences [S1]. If poses are fixed from the start, $\lambda_c$ is halved every 10,000 iterations from iteration 0 [S1].

**Iteration Procedure**
At each iteration, the following steps are performed [S1]:
1.  **Photometric Loss:** Sample $x$ random pixels from all training images. Compute the photometric loss between rendered and ground-truth colors.
2.  **Multi-view Correspondence Loss:**
    *   Sample an image pair.
    *   Select 1024 matches from the pre-computed set that satisfy the confidence threshold $\gamma$ and cyclic consistency.
    *   Compute the loss based on the warping error between the source and target views using the current pose estimates.
3.  **Depth Consistency Loss:**
    *   Sample a training view $I_i$ with pose $\hat{P}_i$.
    *   Find the closest other training view $I_j$ based on current pose estimates.
    *   Compute an "unknown" pose $P_{un}$ as an interpolation of $\hat{P}_i$ and $\hat{P}_j$.
    *   Randomly sample 1024 pixels in $I_i$.
    *   Compute the depth consistency loss using $P_{un}$ [S1].

**Pseudocode**

```python
def sparf_joint_pose_nerf_training(images, initial_poses, depth_ranges, total_iterations):
    """
    SPARF Joint Pose-NeRF Training Strategy.
    
    Args:
        images: List of input images.
        initial_poses: List of initial camera poses (noisy).
        depth_ranges: List of depth ranges [tn, tf] for each image.
        total_iterations: Total number of training iterations.
        
    Returns:
        refined_poses: List of optimized camera poses.
        nerf_model: Trained NeRF model.
    """
    # Initialize NeRF model (MLP with 128 hidden units, softplus for density)
    nerf_model = initialize_nerf()
    
    # Initialize pose parameters
    # Translation t in R^3
    translations = [torch.zeros(3, requires_grad=True) for _ in images]
    # Rotation r in R^6 (a1, a2)
    rotations_6d = [torch.zeros(6, requires_grad=True) for _ in images]
    
    # Pre-compute correspondences using PDC-Net
    correspondences = precompute_correspondences(images, threshold=0.95, cyclic_consistency=1.5)
    
    # Optimizers
    optimizer_nerf = Adam(nerf_model.parameters(), lr=5e-4)
    optimizer_poses = Adam(translations + rotations_6d, lr=1e-3)
    
    # LR schedulers (exponential decay to 1e-4)
    scheduler_nerf = ExponentialLR(optimizer_nerf, gamma=...) # Implementation detail for exponential decay
    scheduler_poses = ExponentialLR(optimizer_poses, gamma=...)
    
    stage1_iters = int(0.3 * total_iterations)
    
    for iteration in range(total_iterations):
        # Update learning rates
        scheduler_nerf.step()
        scheduler_poses.step()
        
        # Adjust positional encoding frequency width
        if iteration < stage1_iters:
            # Stage 1: Joint refinement
            # Linearly adjust from 40% to 70% of total iterations
            progress = iteration / total_iterations
            enc_freq_width = 40 + (70 - 40) * (progress / 0.3) if progress <= 0.3 else 70
            # Note: For first 40% of total iterations (progress < 0.4), enc_freq_width is 0
            if progress < 0.4:
                enc_freq_width = 0
        else:
            # Stage 2: Poses frozen, NeRF trained
            # Linearly adjust from 10% to 50% of total iterations
            progress = (iteration - stage1_iters) / (total_iterations - stage1_iters)
            enc_freq_width = 10 + (50 - 10) * progress
            
        nerf_model.set_positional_encoding_width(enc_freq_width)
        
        # Halve lambda_c every 10,000 iterations after poses are frozen (or from start if fixed)
        # Assuming poses are frozen at stage1_iters
        if iteration >= stage1_iters:
            lambda_c = 1e-3 * (0.5 ** (iteration - stage1_iters) // 10000)
        else:
            lambda_c = 1e-3
            
        lambda_d = 1e-3
        
        # 1. Photometric Loss
        # Sample 1024 random pixels from all images
        sampled_pixels = sample_random_pixels(images, count=1024)
        rendered_colors = render_pixels(nerf_model, sampled_pixels, current_poses(translations, rotations_6d))
        loss_photo = compute_photometric_loss(rendered_colors, ground_truth_colors(sampled_pixels))
        
        # 2. Multi-view Correspondence Loss
        # Sample an image pair and 1024 matches
        pair_idx = random_sample_pair(images)
        matches = get_filtered_matches(correspondences, pair_idx, threshold=0.95, cyclic_consistency=1.5)
        selected_matches = random_sample(matches, count=1024)
        loss_corr = compute_correspondence_loss(nerf_model, selected_matches, current_poses(translations, rotations_6d))
        
        # 3. Depth Consistency Loss
        # Sample a view I_i, find closest view I_j, compute interpolated pose P_un
        i_idx = random_sample_image_index(images)
        j_idx = find_closest_view(i_idx, current_poses(translations, rotations_6d))
        P_un = interpolate_pose(current_poses(translations, rotations_6d)[i_idx], 
                                current_poses(translations, rotations_6d)[j_idx])
        # Sample 1024 pixels in I_i
        pixels_i = sample_pixels_in_image(images[i_idx], count=1024)
        loss_depth = compute_depth_consistency_loss(nerf_model, pixels_i, P_un)
        
        # Total Loss
        loss = loss_photo + lambda_c * loss_corr + lambda_d * loss_depth
        
        # Backpropagation
        optimizer_nerf.zero_grad()
        optimizer_poses.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        
        optimizer_nerf.step()
        if iteration < stage1_iters:
            optimizer_poses.step()
            
    # Finalize poses
    refined_poses = [compute_pose_matrix(t, r_6d) for t, r_6d in zip(translations, rotations_6d)]
    return refined_poses, nerf_model

def compute_pose_matrix(t, r_6d):
    """
    Convert 6D rotation vector to rotation matrix using Gram-Schmidt.
    """
    a1 = r_6d[:3]
    a2 = r_6d[3:]
    
    b1 = a1 / torch.norm(a1)
    b2 = a2 - torch.dot(a2, b1) * b1
    b2 = b2 / torch.norm(b2)
    b3 = torch.cross(b1, b2)
    
    R = torch.stack([b1, b2, b3], dim=1)
    return torch.cat([R, t.unsqueeze(1)], dim=1)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Overfitting:** Training longer than necessary with fixed ground-truth poses on few input views leads to overfitting. Limit training to 50K/70K iterations for 3 views, 100K/140K for 6 views, and 150K/200K for 9 views [S1].
*   **Correspondence Errors:** High $\lambda_c$ can cause the NeRF to learn wrong geometry consistent with erroneous correspondences. Halving $\lambda_c$ over time mitigates this [S1].
*   **Depth Range:** Noisy initial poses may require expanded depth ranges ($\epsilon=0.3$) to ensure scene coverage [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is reduced by using a single MLP for LLFF datasets, as hierarchical sampling yields identical results to coarse+fine MLPs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Standard NeRF memory usage plus storage for pre-computed dense correspondences [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Capable of producing realistic novel-view renderings from few wide-baseline input images with noisy camera pose estimates, matching accuracy obtained with perfect poses [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Applicable only to input image collections where each image has visible regions with multi-view correspondence [S1]. Requires pre-computation of dense correspondences (e.g., using PDC-Net) [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera poses are noisy or unavailable, and input views are sparse and wide-baseline [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality ground-truth poses are available and the scene is dense, as standard NeRF training may be sufficient and faster [S1].

## Background
SPARF (Neural Radiance Fields from Sparse and Noisy Poses) was proposed in 2023 [S1]. It builds on prior work such as BARF (Blending ARbitrary RF) for coarse-to-fine positional encoding [S1] and PDC-Net for dense correspondence prediction [S1]. The method addresses the challenge of novel-view synthesis when camera poses are estimated from sparse inputs (e.g., via COLMAP with DSP-SIFT) and are therefore noisy [S1]. It sets a new state of the art on challenging datasets like LLFF, DTU, and Replica [S1].
