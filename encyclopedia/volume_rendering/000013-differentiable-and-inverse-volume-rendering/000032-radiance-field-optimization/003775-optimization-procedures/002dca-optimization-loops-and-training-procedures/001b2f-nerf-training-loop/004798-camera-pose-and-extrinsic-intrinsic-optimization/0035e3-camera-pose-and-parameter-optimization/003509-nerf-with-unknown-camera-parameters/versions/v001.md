# NeRF with Unknown Camera Parameters

Also called: Joint optimisation of NeRF and camera parameters, NeRF with unknown cameras, NeRF--

## Overview
NeRF with Unknown Camera Parameters (NeRF--) is a method that jointly optimises camera parameters along with a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) model on a sparse set of forward-facing views, eliminating the need for pre-estimated camera poses [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which assumes known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, NeRF-- treats camera parameters as trainable variables within a fully differentiable pipeline, enabling joint optimisation by minimising the reconstruction loss [S1, unofficial]. It achieves comparable novel view synthesis (NVS) quality to baseline NeRF (using COLMAP poses) with a perceptual quality metric difference ($\Delta$SSIM and $\Delta$LPIPS) of 0.05, but requires no pre-estimated camera parameters [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ input images $\{I_i\}$ with unknown camera parameters. Ground truth pixel colours are used for loss calculation.
*   **Outputs:** Optimised NeRF model parameters $\Theta$, and camera parameters $\{\pi_i\}$ for each image, where $\pi_i = (\hat{f}, \hat{\phi}_i, \hat{t}_i)$ representing focal length, rotation, and translation respectively.

**Data Structures:**
*   **NeRF Model ($F_\Theta$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) mapping 3D position and viewing direction to colour and density.
    *   **Hidden Layer Dimension:** 128 (reduced from 256 for computation efficiency) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Initialisation:** Kaiming initialisation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Camera Parameters ($\pi_i$):**
    *   $\hat{f}$: Scalar focal length.
    *   $\hat{\phi}_i$: Rotation parameters (axis-angle representation).
    *   $\hat{t}_i$: Translation vector (camera position).
    *   **Initialisation:** All cameras initialised at the origin $(0,0,0)$ looking at the $-z$ direction. Focal length $\hat{f}$ initialised to the image width [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel $p$ on image $I_i$, compute the ray direction $\hat{d}_{i,p}$ using current camera parameter estimates $\hat{\pi}_i = (\hat{f}, \hat{\phi}_i, \hat{t}_i)$. The ray origin is $\hat{o}_i = \hat{t}_i$. The ray is defined as $\hat{r}_{i,p}(h) = \hat{o}_i + h\hat{d}_{i,p}$.
2.  **Ray Direction Calculation:** Calculate $\hat{d}_{i,p}$ using the formula:
    $$ \hat{d}_{i,p} = \hat{R}_i \begin{pmatrix} (u - W/2)/\hat{f} \\ -(v - H/2)/\hat{f} \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} $$
    where $(u, v)$ are the pixel coordinates, $W$ and $H$ are image width and height, and $\hat{R}_i$ is the rotation matrix computed from $\hat{\phi}_i$ using Rodrigues' formula [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Sampling:** Sample $K$ 3D points $\{x_j\}$ along the ray.
4.  **NeRF Evaluation:** Evaluate radiance colours $\{c_j\}$ and density values $\{\sigma_j\}$ for each point $x_j$ from the NeRF model $F_\Theta$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Volume Rendering:** Apply the standard volume rendering equation (Eq. 1 in [S1, unofficial]) to aggregate predicted radiance and densities along the ray to get the pixel colour $\hat{C}_i(p)$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Loss Calculation:** Randomly select $M$ pixels for each input image. Compute the reconstruction loss (e.g., Mean Squared Error) between rendered colours $\hat{C}_i(p)$ and ground-truth colours $C_i(p)$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Optimisation:** Update NeRF parameters $\Theta$, camera poses $\{\hat{\phi}_i, \hat{t}_i\}$, and focal lengths $\{\hat{f}\}$ using gradient descent [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Pixels sampled per image ($M$):** 1024 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Points sampled along each ray ($K$):** 128 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimisers:** Three separate Adam optimisers for NeRF, camera poses, and focal lengths [S1, unofficial].
*   **Initial Learning Rate:** 0.001 for all optimisers [S1, unofficial].
*   **NeRF Learning Rate Decay:** Every 10 epochs multiplied by 0.9954 (stair-cased exponential decay) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pose and Focal Length Learning Rate Decay:** Every 100 epochs multiplied by 0.9 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Duration:** 10,000 epochs unless otherwise specified [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sampling Strategy:** Hierarchical sampling is NOT used [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Local Minima:** Joint optimisation may trap into local minima for certain scenes (e.g., Orchids, Room), resulting in inaccurate intrinsics and worse NVS quality compared to baseline [S1, unofficial].
*   **Intrinsic Accuracy:** The difference between optimised camera focal lengths and COLMAP estimation can be noticeable in these failure cases (e.g., 199.3 and 331.8) [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Blender Forward Facing (BLEFF) dataset and RealEstate10K, NeRF-- achieves comparable NVS quality to baseline NeRF (using COLMAP poses) with $\Delta$SSIM and $\Delta$LPIPS = 0.05 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates the need for pre-estimated camera parameters (e.g., from COLMAP), making it suitable for sparse forward-facing views where pose estimation is difficult or unstable [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Susceptible to local minima, leading to inaccurate camera parameters and degraded NVS quality on specific scenes (Orchids, Room) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When camera parameters are unknown or unreliable, and the scene consists of sparse forward-facing views [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When high-precision camera parameters are required and the scene is prone to local minima traps, or when pre-estimated poses are available and accurate [S1, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was introduced in the paper "NeRF--: Neural Radiance Fields Without Known Camera Parameters" [S1, unofficial].
*   **History and Development:** The authors introduced the Blender Forward Facing (BLEFF) dataset, containing 14 path-traced scenes with rotation and translation perturbations, to evaluate joint optimisation systems [S1, unofficial].
*   **Where it is used:** Used in scenarios requiring novel view synthesis from sparse forward-facing views without prior camera calibration [S1, unofficial].
