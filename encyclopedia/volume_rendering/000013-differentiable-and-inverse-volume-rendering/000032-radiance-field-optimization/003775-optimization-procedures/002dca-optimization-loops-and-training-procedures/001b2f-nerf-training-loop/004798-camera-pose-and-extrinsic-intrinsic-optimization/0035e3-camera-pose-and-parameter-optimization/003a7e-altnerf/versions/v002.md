# AltNeRF

Also called: Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization

## Overview
AltNeRF is a framework for creating resilient Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) representations from monocular videos without relying on known camera poses [S1]. It solves the problem of suboptimal NeRF reconstruction caused by imprecise initial camera poses and lack of explicit 3D supervision by using an alternating algorithm to harmonize NeRF outputs with self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SMDE) priors [S1]. Unlike methods that strictly align rendered depth with depth priors, AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization because the depth prior itself is not precise, allowing it to progressively refine both the scene geometry and camera poses [S1].

## How it works
AltNeRF comprises two components: the Scene Prior Module (SPM) and the Scene Representation Module (SRM) [S1]. The SPM estimates initial depth and pose priors using SMDE, while the SRM refines these poses and learns the 3D scene representation, regularized by the depth priors [S1]. These refined outputs are fed back to the SPM to improve its performance in subsequent iterations [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** Monocular video data (sequence of images $I$) and known camera intrinsics $K$ [S1].
*   **Output:** Refined camera poses $P$, depth maps $D$, and a NeRF model $f_n$ representing the scene [S1].

### Data Structures and Layouts

**Scene Prior Module (SPM)**
The SPM consists of two networks:
1.  **Depth Network ($f_d$):**
    *   **Encoder:** ResNet-50 with the fully-connected layer removed [S1].
    *   **Decoder:** Ten $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (two per scale) with bilinear up-sampling [S1].
    *   **Output:** Depth map $D$ [S1].
2.  **Pose Network ($f_p$):**
    *   **Structure:** ResNet-34 [S1].
    *   **Output:** Vector of 9 elements. The first 6 elements are a continuous rotation representation (axis-angle or similar, as per Zhou et al. 2019 [S1]), and the last 3 elements denote translation $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

**Scene Representation Module (SRM)**
1.  **NeRF Network ($f_n$):**
    *   **Structure:** Eight fully-connected layers with skip connections for density output, and one linear layer for color [S1].
    *   **Function:** $f_n: (p, d) \rightarrow (\sigma, c)$, where $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a 3D point and $d \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a unit viewing direction [S1].

### Parameters
*   $\gamma$: Scalar hyper-parameter balancing reconstruction and depth regularization losses. Value: 0.08 for LLFF and CO3D; 0.15 for ScanNet and Captures [S1].
*   $S_r$: Number of SRM iterations per alternation. Value: 50,000 [S1].
*   $S_p$: Number of SPM fine-tuning iterations per alternation. Value: 500 [S1].
*   Alternations: Number of full alternating cycles. Value: 2 [S1].
*   SPM Pretrain LR: Learning rate for pretraining SPM. Value: $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SPM Fine-tune LR: Learning rate for fine-tuning SPM. Value: $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   SRM NeRF LR: Initial learning rate for NeRF learning in SRM. Value: $10^{-3}$, exponentially decays to $10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   SRM Pose LR: Initial learning rate for pose refinement. Value: $10^{-5}$, linearly increases to $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ after 1,000 iterations, then exponentially decays to $10^{-5}$ [S1].
*   $\alpha$: Weight for L1 term in photometric loss. Value: 0.85 [S1].
*   $\epsilon$: Tolerance coefficient for error-tolerant depth regularization [S1]. (Value not given in the sources.)

### Numbered Steps

**1. SPM Pretraining**
Pretrain the SPM on a large dataset (e.g., NYUv2 and VOID) to accumulate prior knowledge for 3D recovery [S1]. The training objective minimizes:
$$ L_{pt} = L_p + L_r + 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \times L_s $$
where:
*   $L_p$ is the photometric loss for view reconstruction:
    $$ L_p(I_t, \hat{I}_t) = (1 - \text{SSIM}(I_t, \hat{I}_t)) + \frac{\alpha}{2} (1 - \alpha) \| I_t - \hat{I}_t \|_1 $$
    with $\alpha = 0.85$ [S1].
*   $L_r$ is the distilling loss:
    $$ L_r = 1 - \text{SSIM}(D, D_r) + 0.1 \times (E_r \oplus E / \text{size}(E)) $$
    where $D_r$ is a reference depth map from an off-the-shelf estimator (e.g., DPT), $E$ and $E_r$ are occluding boundary maps, and $\oplus$ denotes the XOR operation [S1].
*   $L_s$ is the edge-aware smoothness loss:
    $$ L_s = |\partial_x D| e^{-|\partial_x I|} + |\partial_y D| e^{-|\partial_y I|} $$
    where $\partial_x$ and $\partial_y$ are image gradients [S1].

**2. Pose Initialization**
Establish a world coordinate system aligning with the first frame $I_0$ (identity matrix) [S1]. Calculate initial camera poses $P_i$ using the chain rule:
$$ P_i = P_{i-1 \rightarrow i} \times P_{i-1} $$
where $P_{i-1 \rightarrow i}$ is the relative transformation predicted by $f_p$ [S1]. Use the pose prior $P_0$ from SPM as initialization for SRM [S1].

**3. Alternating Algorithm Workflow**
The algorithm cycles through iterations $i$. Let $\Phi_i$ be the SPM at step $i$ and $\Psi_i$ be the SRM [S1]. The alternation is formulated as:
$$ \Psi_i: (D_i, P_i) \rightarrow (\hat{D}_{i+1}, \hat{P}_{i+1}) $$
$$ \Phi_i: (\hat{D}_{i+1}, \hat{P}_{i+1}) \rightarrow (D_{i+1}, P_{i+1}) $$
[S1].

**3.1. SRM Iteration (Pose Refinement and NeRF Learning)**
SRM takes initial pose $P_i$ and depth prior $D_i$ [S1].
*   **Pose Refinement:** Refine the pose by optimizing a residual pose $\Delta P$. The refined pose is $\hat{P} = \Delta P \times P$ [S1].
*   **Warmup Learning:** Set the learning rate of $\Delta P$ to a low value initially, linearly increasing to the original rate after 1,000 iterations to allow SRM to learn a scene prototype before updating the pose [S1].
*   **Loss Computation:** The loss for SRM is:
    $$ L_{sr} = L_c + \gamma \cdot L_e $$
    where $L_c$ is the NeRF reconstruction loss:
    $$ L_c = \| \hat{C}(r) - C(r) \|_2 $$
    and $L_e$ is the error-tolerant depth regularization loss:
    $$ L_e = H \left( \max \left( \frac{\| \hat{D} - D \|_1}{\hat{D} + D} - \epsilon, 0 \right) \right) $$
    where $H(\cdot)$ is the Huber loss and $\epsilon$ is the tolerance coefficient [S1].
*   **Multi-view Consistency Check:** Compute uncertainty $U_t = \| D_t - D_{s \rightarrow t} \|_1$, where $D_{s \rightarrow t}$ are depth maps warped from nearby source views [S1]. Incorporate this uncertainty into loss functions by weighting them with $\text{Softmin}(\cdot)$ to mitigate unreliable depth estimates [S1].
*   **Update:** Run SRM for $S_r$ iterations. Output estimated depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ and improved pose $\hat{P}_{i+1}$ [S1].

**3.2. SPM Fine-tuning**
Fine-tune SPM for $S_p$ iterations using the distillation loss $L_r$ (Eq. 6 in [S1]), replacing the reference depth $D_r$ with the rendered depth $\hat{D}_{i+1}$ from SRM [S1]. The final loss is $L_{pt}$ (Eq. 7 in [S1]) [S1]. Output updated depth $D_{i+1}$ and pose $P_{i+1}$ [S1].

**4. Repeat**
Repeat steps 3.1 and 3.2 for the specified number of alternations (typically 2) [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def altnerf(video_frames, intrinsics_K):
    """
    Main AltNeRF training loop.
    
    Args:
        video_frames: List of images [I_0, I_1, ..., I_N]
        intrinsics_K: Camera intrinsic matrix K
        
    Returns:
        final_poses: List of refined camera poses
        final_depths: List of refined depth maps
        nerf_model: Trained NeRF model
    """
    # 1. Pretrain SPM
    spm = ScenePriorModule()
    spm.pretrain(dataset="NYUv2+VOID", lr=1e-4)
    
    # 2. Initialize Poses using SPM
    # Set world coordinate system to first frame I_0
    poses = [IdentityMatrix()]
    for i in range(1, len(video_frames)):
        # Predict relative pose from I_{i-1} to I_i
        rel_pose = spm.pose_network(video_frames[i-1], video_frames[i])
        # Chain rule: P_i = P_{i-1->i} * P_{i-1}
        poses.append(rel_pose * poses[i-1])
        
    # 3. Initial Depth Estimation using SPM
    depths = []
    for frame in video_frames:
        depths.append(spm.depth_network(frame))
        
    # 4. Initialize SRM (NeRF)
    nerf_model = SceneRepresentationModule()
    
    # 5. Alternating Optimization
    num_alternations = 2
    sr_iterations = 50000
    sp_iterations = 500
    
    for i in range(num_alternations):
        # --- SRM Step ---
        # Optimize NeRF and Residual Pose Delta_P
        # Loss: L_sr = L_c + gamma * L_e
        # L_c: Photometric reconstruction loss
        # L_e: Error-tolerant depth regularization against current depths[i]
        
        # Warmup strategy for pose LR
        pose_lr_schedule = WarmupSchedule(initial=1e-5, peak=2e-3, warmup_iters=1000)
        nerf_lr_schedule = ExponentialDecay(initial=1e-3, final=1e-4)
        
        for step in range(sr_iterations):
            # Sample rays based on current poses[i] and intrinsics_K
            rays = generate_rays(poses[i], intrinsics_K)
            
            # Render colors and depths
            rendered_colors, rendered_depths = nerf_model.render(rays)
            
            # Compute Photometric Loss L_c
            loss_c = mse_loss(rendered_colors, ground_truth_colors)
            
            # Compute Error-Tolerant Depth Loss L_e
            # L_e = Huber(max((||D_hat - D||_1 / (D_hat + D) - epsilon, 0)))
            # Apply multi-view consistency weighting via Softmin(U_t)
            loss_e = compute_error_tolerant_depth_loss(rendered_depths, depths[i], intrinsics_K, poses[i])
            
            # Total Loss
            loss = loss_c + gamma * loss_e
            
            # Backpropagate and update NeRF and Delta_P
            update(nerf_model, nerf_lr_schedule(step))
            update(residual_pose, pose_lr_schedule(step))
            
        # Apply residual pose to get refined pose
        refined_pose = residual_pose * poses[i]
        refined_depths = rendered_depths
        
        # --- SPM Fine-tuning Step ---
        # Fine-tune SPM depth network using distillation loss
        # Loss: L_pt = L_p + L_r + 1e-3 * L_s
        # L_r uses refined_depths as reference instead of off-the-shelf D_r
        
        for step in range(sp_iterations):
            # Sample pixels from refined_depths and refined_pose
            # Reconstruct views and compute photometric loss L_p
            # Compute distillation loss L_r against refined_depths
            # Compute smoothness loss L_s
            
            loss_pt = compute_spm_loss(refined_depths, refined_pose, video_frames)
            
            # Backpropagate and update SPM depth and pose networks
            update(spm.depth_network, 5e-5)
            update(spm.pose_network, 5e-5)
            
        # Update poses and depths for next iteration
        poses[i] = refined_pose
        depths[i] = refined_depths
        
    return poses, depths, nerf_model
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Initialization Sensitivity:** Joint optimization of camera pose and NeRF representation is highly non-convex and prone to converging to suboptimal solutions if the initial pose is far from the actual pose [S1]. Using the SPM pose prior as initialization helps mitigate this [S1].
*   **Imprecise Priors:** The depth prior from SPM is not precise. Strictly aligning rendered depth with the prior can mislead optimization. AltNeRF uses error-tolerant regularization to fit a possible depth range instead [S1].
*   **Dynamic Objects:** SMDE is susceptible to dynamic objects and view-dependent appearances. The distilling strategy in SPM helps mitigate this [S1].
*   **Uncertainty:** Depth estimates can be unreliable due to occlusions or textureless regions. Multi-view consistency checks are used to measure uncertainty and weight losses accordingly [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training takes 150K-200K total iterations, costing 4.0-6.4 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Creates resilient NeRF representations from monocular videos without known camera poses. The alternating algorithm synergistically boosts SPM and SRM, tolerating imprecise priors [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the quality of the initial SPM estimates. Performance may degrade with highly dynamic scenes or severe view-dependent appearances if not properly mitigated by the distilling strategy [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera poses are unknown and only monocular video data is available.
*   **When not to use:** When precise camera poses are already known (standard NeRF training is more efficient).

## Background
AltNeRF was proposed in the paper "AltNeRF: Learning Robust Neural Radiance Field via Alternating Depth-Pose Optimization" published at the Thirty-Eighth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-24) [S1]. It builds on the concept of self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SMDE) and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [S1]. The method addresses the challenges of existing NeRF approaches that encounter difficulties due to the lack of explicit 3D supervision and imprecise camera poses [S1].
