# Neural Surface Rendering Training (Eikonal & Boundary Regularization)

Also called: Eikonal and Boundary Regularization for Neural Surfaces, Neural SDF Training with Image Reconstruction and Eikonal Regularization

## Overview
This method trains a neural Signed Distance Function (SDF) to render images that match ground truth RGB data while enforcing geometric priors through Eikonal and boundary regularization losses [S1, S2, unofficial]. It differs from standard neural volume rendering training by optimizing an explicit surface representation (SDF) rather than a volumetric density field, using sphere tracing for rendering and random point sampling for regularization [S1, S2, unofficial]. This approach addresses the ambiguity of volumetric representations by enforcing that the gradient of the SDF has unit magnitude (Eikonal equation) and that the SDF values decay appropriately near the surface boundary [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Inputs**: Ground truth images, camera parameters (intrinsics, extrinsics), a 3D bounding box (`bounds`) for sampling, and hyperparameters (learning rate, weights, scheduler settings) [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Outputs**: A trained neural SDF model (MLP) that maps 3D coordinates to signed distance values and optionally color features, capable of rendering images via sphere tracing [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
- **Neural Surface Model**: Consists of a Harmonic Embedding layer for position encoding, an MLP for distance prediction, and an MLP for color prediction [S1, unofficial].
    - `HarmonicEmbedding`: Encodes 3D coordinates using sine/cosine functions at multiple frequencies [S1, unofficial].
    - `MLPWithInputSkips`: A multi-layer perceptron with skip connections from the input embedding to intermediate hidden layers [S1, unofficial].
    - `SDFSurface`: A wrapper module that holds the SDF geometry (e.g., Sphere, Box, Torus) or a learned MLP and outputs distance and color [S1, unofficial].
- **RayBundle**: Contains ray origins and directions for a batch of pixels [S2, unofficial].
- **ImplicitSurface**: Interface providing `get_distance_and_gradient` methods [S2, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps**
1. **Pretraining**: Pretrain the SDF using a sphere loss method to initialize the geometry before image-based training begins [S1, unofficial].
2. **Initialization**: Initialize the optimizer (Adam) and learning rate scheduler [S1, unofficial].
3. **Epoch Loop**: For each epoch, iterate through the training dataloader [S1, unofficial].
4. **Batch Processing**:
    a. Extract the image and camera from the batch and move them to the GPU [S1, unofficial].
    b. Sample random pixel coordinates (`xy_grid`) for ray generation [S1, unofficial].
    c. Generate a ray bundle from the pixel coordinates using the camera parameters [S1, unofficial].
    d. Sample ground truth RGB values at the pixel coordinates from the image [S1, unofficial].
    e. Run the model forward pass with the ray bundle to render the image [S1, unofficial].
    f. Compute the image reconstruction loss (Mean Squared Error) between rendered and ground truth RGB [S1, S2, unofficial].
    g. Sample random points (`eikonal_points`) within the training bounding box [S2, unofficial].
    h. Compute the SDF distance and gradient at these eikonal points [S2, unofficial].
    i. Compute the Eikonal loss based on the gradient magnitude [S2, unofficial].
    j. Compute the boundary loss based on the SDF values at the eikonal points [S2, unofficial].
    k. Compute the total loss as the sum of image, Eikonal, and boundary losses [S2, unofficial].
    l. Perform the optimization step: zero gradients, backward pass, and optimizer step [S1, unofficial].
5. **Scheduler Update**: Update the learning rate scheduler at the end of the epoch [S1, unofficial].
6. **Checkpointing**: Save model and optimizer state every `checkpoint_interval` epochs [S1, unofficial].
7. **Visualization**: Generate and save visualization images every `render_interval` epochs [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**
- **Image Loss**:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{image}} = \mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{c}_{\text{gt}} - \mathbf{c}_{\text{pred}})^2] $$
  where $\mathbf{c}_{\text{gt}}$ is the ground truth RGB and $\mathbf{c}_{\text{pred}}$ is the rendered RGB [S1, S2, unofficial].

- **Eikonal Loss**:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eikonal}} = \lambda_{\text{eik}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\|\nabla d(\mathbf{x})\| - 1]^2 $$
  where $d(\mathbf{x})$ is the SDF value, $\nabla d(\mathbf{x})$ is its gradient, and $\lambda_{\text{eik}}$ is the Eikonal weight [S2, unofficial]. Note: The source code computes `eikonal_loss(eikonal_gradients)` which typically implies minimizing the deviation from unit norm [S2, unofficial].

- **Boundary Loss**:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{boundary}} = \lambda_{\text{inter}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\exp(-100 \cdot |d(\mathbf{x})|)] $$
  where $d(\mathbf{x})$ is the SDF value at random point $\mathbf{x}$, and $\lambda_{\text{inter}}$ is the boundary weight (`inter_weight`) [S2, unofficial].

- **Total Loss**:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{image}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{eikonal}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{boundary}} $$
  [S2, unofficial]

- **Learning Rate Schedule**:
  $$ \eta_{\text{epoch}} = \eta_{\text{initial}} \cdot \gamma^{\frac{\text{epoch}}{\text{step\_size}}} $$
  where $\gamma$ is `cfg.training.lr_scheduler_gamma` and `step_size` is `cfg.training.lr_scheduler_step_size` [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
- `cfg.training.lr`: Initial learning rate for the Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.training.lr_scheduler_gamma`: Decay factor for the exponential learning rate schedule [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.training.lr_scheduler_step_size`: Divisor for the epoch index in the exponent of the learning rate schedule [S1, unofficial].
- `eikonal_weight` (`$\lambda_{\text{eik}}$`): Weight for the Eikonal regularization loss [S2, unofficial].
- `inter_weight` (`$\lambda_{\text{inter}}$`): Weight for the boundary regularization loss [S2, unofficial].
- `bounds`: 3D bounding box defining the region for sampling eikonal points [S2, unofficial].
- `cfg.training.checkpoint_interval`: Number of epochs between model checkpoints [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.training.render_interval`: Number of epochs between visualization renders [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.n_harmonic_functions_xyz`: Number of harmonic functions for position encoding [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.n_layers_distance`, `cfg.n_hidden_neurons_distance`: Architecture parameters for the distance MLP [S1, unofficial].
- `cfg.n_layers_color`, `cfg.n_hidden_neurons_color`: Architecture parameters for the color MLP [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim

# Assume model, optimizer, lr_scheduler, cfg, and dataloader are initialized
# model: NeuralSurface instance
# optimizer: Adam optimizer
# lr_scheduler: ExponentialLR or custom scheduler
# cfg: Configuration object
# dataloader: PyTorch DataLoader

def train_epoch(model, optimizer, lr_scheduler, dataloader, cfg):
    model.train()
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # Iterate through batches
    for iteration, batch in enumerate(dataloader):
        # 1. Extract data and move to device
        image = batch['image'].cuda().unsqueeze(0)  # (1, H, W, 3)
        camera = batch['camera'].cuda()
        
        # 2. Sample random pixels for ray generation
        xy_grid = get_random_pixels_from_image(cfg.training.batch_size, cfg.data.image_size, camera)
        
        # 3. Generate ray bundle
        ray_bundle = get_rays_from_pixels(xy_grid, cfg.data.image_size, camera)
        
        # 4. Sample ground truth RGB
        rgb_gt = sample_images_at_xy(image, xy_grid)
        
        # 5. Render image using sphere tracing
        out = model(ray_bundle)
        rgb_pred = out['color']
        
        # 6. Compute image reconstruction loss (MSE)
        image_loss = torch.mean(torch.square(rgb_gt - rgb_pred))
        
        # 7. Sample random points for regularization
        eikonal_points = get_random_points(cfg.training.batch_size, cfg.training.bounds, "cuda")
        
        # 8. Compute SDF distance and gradient at eikonal points
        eikonal_distances, eikonal_gradients = model.implicit_fn.get_distance_and_gradient(eikonal_points)
        
        # 9. Compute Eikonal loss
        # Assuming eikonal_loss function computes mean squared error from 1.0 for gradient norm
        eikonal_loss_val = eikonal_loss(eikonal_gradients) * cfg.training.eikonal_weight
        
        # 10. Compute boundary loss
        # L_boundary = lambda_inter * E[exp(-100 * |d(x)|)]
        boundary_loss = torch.exp(-1e2 * torch.abs(eikonal_distances)).mean() * cfg.training.inter_weight
        
        # 11. Total loss
        loss = image_loss + boundary_loss + eikonal_loss_val
        
        # 12. Optimization step
        optimizer.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        optimizer.step()
        
        total_loss += loss.item()
        
    # 13. Update learning rate scheduler
    lr_scheduler.step()
    
    return total_loss / len(dataloader)

def train(model, optimizer, lr_scheduler, dataloader, cfg):
    for epoch in range(cfg.training.num_epochs):
        avg_loss = train_epoch(model, optimizer, lr_scheduler, dataloader, cfg)
        print(f"Epoch {epoch}, Loss: {avg_loss}")
        
        # Checkpointing
        if epoch % cfg.training.checkpoint_interval == 0:
            save_checkpoint(model, optimizer, epoch, cfg)
            
        # Visualization
        if epoch % cfg.training.render_interval == 0:
            render_and_save(model, cfg)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- **Precision**: The boundary loss uses an exponential term $\exp(-100 \cdot |d(\mathbf{x})|)$. If the SDF values are not scaled appropriately, this term may underflow or vanish, providing no gradient signal [S2, unofficial].
- **Boundaries**: The random point sampling for Eikonal and boundary losses is confined to a 3D bounding box. If the object extends beyond this box, regularization will be ineffective outside it [S2, unofficial].
- **Sphere Tracing**: The rendering relies on sphere tracing. If the SDF is not sufficiently smooth or the gradient is not close to unit magnitude, sphere tracing may fail to converge or overshoot the surface [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of training iterations and batch size. Each iteration involves a forward pass through the MLP and sphere tracing, which is iterative and depends on the surface complexity [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of parameters in the MLP and the batch size. The model stores weights for the distance and color MLPs [S1, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance**: Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling**: Scales with the number of training images and the resolution of the images. Higher resolution requires more ray sampling and potentially more sphere tracing iterations [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Enforces geometric priors (Eikonal equation) leading to cleaner surfaces compared to volumetric methods. Boundary regularization helps in defining the surface location more precisely [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of regularization weights. Sphere tracing can be slow for complex surfaces. Pretraining is required to initialize the SDF [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use**: When a clean, explicit surface representation is needed, and photorealistic rendering is the goal. Suitable for single-object reconstruction or scenes with well-defined boundaries [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **When not to use**: When the scene has complex transparency or subsurface scattering, as the SDF representation is primarily for opaque surfaces. Also not suitable if real-time rendering is required without optimization [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
This method originates from the field of neural implicit surface reconstruction and differentiable rendering. It builds on the principles of neural volume rendering but adapts them for explicit surface representations using SDFs. The use of Eikonal regularization is inspired by the mathematical property that the gradient of a true SDF has unit magnitude everywhere [S1, S2, unofficial]. The boundary regularization term is designed to encourage the SDF values to decay rapidly away from the surface, further sharpening the surface definition [S2, unofficial]. The implementation details are derived from open-source repositories focusing on neural surface rendering [S1, S2, unofficial].
