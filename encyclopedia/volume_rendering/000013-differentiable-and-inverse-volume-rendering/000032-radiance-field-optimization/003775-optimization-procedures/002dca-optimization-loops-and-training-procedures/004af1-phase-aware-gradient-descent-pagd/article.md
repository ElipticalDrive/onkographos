# Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD)

Also called: Phase-Only Hologram Synthesis via PAGD

## Overview
Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD) is an optimization algorithm designed to encode complex wave fields into phase-only holograms for 3D holographic displays with continuous depth cues [S1]. It solves the problem of ringing artifacts and unnatural blur spreading inherent in amplitude-only or RGB-D holographic synthesis methods by leveraging phase information for supervision [S1]. Unlike amplitude-based approaches that rely on discrete multi-plane supervision, PAGD uses a camera-calibrated learned forward model to propagate the phase-only hologram, enabling accurate reconstruction of tilted wave fields and view-dependent occlusion [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Mesh-represented scene $M$
*   Camera settings $C$
*   Lighting $L$
*   Number of optimization iterations $T$
*   Initial phase hologram $\Phi_{init}$ (typically random or zero) [S1]

**Outputs:**
*   Optimized phase-only hologram $\Phi_{OPT}$ [S1]

**Data Structures:**
*   $\Phi$: Complex-valued phase hologram of size $H \times W$ (SLM resolution).
*   $u_{WR}$: Target complex wave field generated by POS-WRP.
*   $u_{ti}$: Target complex wave field at the $i$-th propagation plane ($z_i$).
*   $u_{ri}$: Reconstructed complex wave field at the $i$-th plane from the current hologram $\Phi$.
*   $s_i$: Complex-valued scale factor for the $i$-th plane.
*   $L(\cdot, \cdot)$: Loss function (MSE).

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialize:** Set $\Phi \leftarrow \Phi_{init}$ [S1].
2.  **Generate Target Wave Field:** Compute the target wave field $u_{WR}$ using the Phase-Only Wave Field Rendering (POS-WRP) method with inputs $M, C, L$ [S1].
3.  **Propagate Targets:** Propagate $u_{WR}$ to three specific depth planes ($z_1, z_2, z_3$) corresponding to near, medium, and far depths to obtain target fields $u_{t1}, u_{t2}, u_{t3}$ [S1].
4.  **Optimization Loop:** For iteration $n$ from 1 to $T$:
    a.  **Reconstruction:** For each of the 3 planes $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
        i.  Propagate the current phase hologram $e^{j\Phi}$ shifted by $z_{WR} + z_i$ using the forward propagation model (e.g., Angular Spectrum Method or a learned surrogate) to obtain the reconstructed field $u_{ri}$ [S1].
    b.  **Scale Factor Calculation:** For each plane $i$, compute the complex-valued scale factor $s_i$ to account for intensity deviations and phase shift invariances. Let $u_r = u_{ri}$ and $u_t = u_{ti}$. The real and imaginary parts of $s_i$ are calculated as [S1]:
        $$
        \text{Re}(s_i) = \frac{\text{Re}(u_r) \odot \text{Re}(u_t) + \text{Im}(u_r) \odot \text{Im}(u_t)}{\text{Re}(u_r)^2 + \text{Im}(u_r)^2}
        $$
        $$
        \text{Im}(s_i) = \frac{-\text{Im}(u_r) \odot \text{Re}(u_t) + \text{Re}(u_r) \odot \text{Im}(u_t)}{\text{Re}(u_r)^2 + \text{Im}(u_r)^2}
        $$
        where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication. This factor addresses the non-uniqueness of phase distribution due to global phase shifts and $2\pi$ pixel-wise shifts [S1].
    c.  **Loss Computation:** Calculate the total loss $E(\Phi)$ by summing the scaled losses for all 3 planes [S1]:
        $$
        E(\Phi) = \sum_{i=1}^3 L(s_i \odot u_{ri}, u_{ti})
        $$
        where the loss function $L$ is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the real and imaginary parts of the scaled reconstructed field and the target field [S1]:
        $$
        L(s \odot u_r, u_t) = \text{MSE}(\text{Re}(s u_r), \text{Re}(u_t)) + \text{MSE}(\text{Im}(s u_r), \text{Im}(u_t))
        $$
    d.  **Backpropagation:** Compute gradients $\nabla E(\Phi)$ and update $\Phi$ using gradient descent (or an optimizer like Adam) [S1].
5.  **Finalize:** Set $\Phi_{OPT} = \Phi$ [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Phase Non-uniqueness:** Phase supervision is challenging because the phase distribution is not unique. Two shift invariances exist: (1) Global shift in phase does not alter the wave field, and (2) Phase value on each pixel can be shifted by $2\pi$ without changing the target field [S1]. The complex-valued scale factor $s$ is introduced to account for these invariances and light source intensity deviations [S1].
*   **Model Mismatch:** Numerical models like Angular Spectrum Method (ASM) struggle to replicate real-world optical imperfections such as SLM non-linearities, aberrations, stray light, and limited diffraction efficiency [S1]. Using a camera-calibrated learned model mitigates this [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Camera-Calibrated Wave Propagation Model Training](12905-camera-calibrated-wave-propagation-model-training.md)
This method trains a learned surrogate model to predict the optical field distribution at target planes given an input phase pattern, effectively replacing analytical propagation models (such as Angular Spectrum Method) within the Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD) optimization loop. It solves the problem of model mismatch caused by real-world optical imperfections, including SLM non-linearities, aberrations, and stray light, which analytical models fail to capture. By calibrating the propagation model against physical hardware measurements, it achieves a 2 dB PSNR improvement over analytical baselines.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The training time for the PAGD optimizer component is approximately 30 seconds per hologram [S1]. The total time for generating one phase-only hologram from a mesh scene is on average 54.96 seconds (24.96 s for POS-WRP and 30 s for PAGD) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Phase-aware supervision offers more accurate reconstruction than amplitude-only supervision for tilted wave fields (e.g., 1° tilt) [S1]. Enforcing complex target supervision on 1-plane yields an improvement of 7 dB in PSNR over 2-plane amplitude-only supervision while being slightly faster, and is 2.39× faster than 5-plane amplitude-only supervision at the same image quality [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Mitigates ringing artifacts and unnatural blur spreading found in amplitude-only or RGB-D methods [S1]. Adheres to the physical two-step hologram process (recording and reconstruction) interconnected through phase information [S1]. Requires training the forward model only once and does not necessitate specific data per viewpoint during training [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Real-time hologram generation remains a goal for future exploration [S1].

## Background
Phase-Aware Gradient Descent (PAGD) was proposed in a 2026 paper published in *Nature Communications* by researchers investigating mesh-represented and learning-empowered hologram synthesis for full 3D holographic displays [S1]. The method addresses limitations of amplitude-based optimization, such as the trade-off between computational complexity and depth cue continuity, and the inability of discrete multi-plane amplitudes to provide realistic defocus blur [S1]. It builds on the physical principles of holography, where phase is critical for reconstructing wave fields that propagate beyond the principal optical axis [S1].
