# Photometric Loss (RawNeRF Variant)

Also called: Color Loss, Gradient-Trick Photometric Loss, Raw-NeRF Loss, RawNeRF Photometric Loss

## Overview
This method is a specialized photometric loss function used to optimize neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and inverse rendering models by enforcing consistency between rendered pixel colors and ground truth images [S1, S2]. It distinguishes itself from standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses by applying a "gradient trick" that treats the radiance estimator as a constant during backpropagation, thereby reducing bias in the gradient estimation for Monte Carlo estimators [S2]. This approach is particularly effective when supervising cache-based predictions against physically-based models [S2].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   $\mathbf{o}$: Ray origin.
    *   $\boldsymbol{\omega}_o$ (or $\mathbf{d}_{\text{out}}$): Ray direction.
    *   $I(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)$: Ground truth pixel color for the ray.
    *   $\hat{L}_i(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$: Predicted radiance estimator (Monte Carlo estimate) parameterized by $\Phi$.
    *   $\hat{L}_i^{\text{cache}}(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$: Cache estimator used for normalization.
    *   $R$: Set of rays in the training batch.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photometric}}$: The scalar loss value for the batch.

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Batch:** A collection of rays, each defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}_o$.
*   **Ground Truth Image:** A tensor of RGB values corresponding to the camera poses of the rays.
*   **Predicted Radiance:** A tensor of RGB values generated by the forward pass of the radiance estimator.
*   **Cache Estimator:** A tensor of RGB values representing the cached radiance estimate, used for variance reduction/normalization.

### Steps
1.  **Compute Difference:** Calculate the difference between the ground truth color $I(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)$ and the predicted radiance estimator $\hat{L}_i(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$.
2.  **Apply Stop Gradient:** Apply the `stopgrad` operator to the difference term. This treats the difference as a constant in the computational graph, preventing gradients from flowing through it during backpropagation.
3.  **Compute Numerator:** Multiply the original difference by the stop-graded difference. This approximates the gradient of the squared error without backpropagating through the stochastic estimator directly.
4.  **Normalize:** Divide the result by the stop-graded cache estimator $\hat{L}_i^{\text{cache}}(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$. This normalizes the loss, reducing bias introduced by the Monte Carlo estimation [S2].
5.  **Estimate Differences:** Use independent secondary samples to estimate the first and second differences in the numerator to ensure unbiased gradient estimation [S2].

### Formula
The standard photometric loss for NeRFs is defined as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) [S1]:
$$
L_{\text{color}} = \sum_{r \in R} \| \hat{C}(r) - C(r) \|^2
$$
where $R$ is the set of rays in the training batch, $\hat{C}(r)$ is the predicted color, and $C(r)$ is the ground truth color [S1].

The RawNeRF variant applies the gradient trick to minimize the expected squared error while correcting for bias [S2]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{photometric}} = \frac{2 (I(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) - \hat{L}_i(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)) \operatorname{stopgrad}(I(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o) - \hat{L}_i(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi))}{\operatorname{stopgrad}(\hat{L}_i^{\text{cache}}(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi))}
$$
where:
*   $I(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o)$ is the ground truth color.
*   $\hat{L}_i(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$ is the radiance estimator.
*   $\Phi$ are the model parameters.
*   $\hat{L}_i^{\text{cache}}(\mathbf{o}, \boldsymbol{\omega}_o; \Phi)$ is the cache estimator used for normalization.
*   $\operatorname{stopgrad}(\cdot)$ is an operator that treats its argument as a constant in the compute graph, preventing gradient flow through it [S2].

### Parameters
*   **stopgrad(·):** An operator that freezes the gradient of its argument. It is applied to the cache estimator and the difference term to ensure correct gradient propagation through the Monte Carlo estimator [S2].
*   **Secondary Samples:** Independent samples used to estimate the differences in the numerator. The number of secondary rays (e.g., 64) can impact noise levels and relative PSNR [S2].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Monte Carlo Noise:** The loss is sensitive to noise in the radiance estimator. Rendering the predicted color image multiple times (e.g., 32 times) and averaging the results can reduce noise [S2].
*   **Bias:** Using the cache estimator for normalization reduces bias in the gradient estimation. Failure to use `stopgrad` on the cache term may reintroduce bias [S2].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the cache estimator is zero or near-zero, division by zero may occur. A small epsilon should be added to the denominator [G].

### Pseudocode
```python
def rawnerf_photometric_loss(ground_truth_color, predicted_radiance, cache_estimator, secondary_samples=None):
    """
    Computes the RawNeRF variant photometric loss.
    
    Args:
        ground_truth_color: Tensor of shape (N, 3) containing ground truth RGB values.
        predicted_radiance: Tensor of shape (N, 3) containing predicted radiance estimates.
        cache_estimator: Tensor of shape (N, 3) containing cache estimates for normalization.
        secondary_samples: Optional tensor for independent secondary samples. Not used in basic implementation.
        
    Returns:
        loss: Scalar tensor representing the photometric loss.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute difference
    diff = ground_truth_color - predicted_radiance
    
    # Step 2: Apply stopgrad to the difference
    # In frameworks like PyTorch, this is done via .detach()
    stopgrad_diff = diff.detach()
    
    # Step 3: Compute numerator: 2 * diff * stopgrad(diff)
    numerator = 2.0 * diff * stopgrad_diff
    
    # Step 4: Normalize by stopgrad(cache_estimator)
    # Add epsilon to prevent division by zero [G]
    epsilon = 1e-8
    stopgrad_cache = cache_estimator.detach()
    denominator = stopgrad_cache + epsilon
    
    # Step 5: Compute loss per ray
    # Note: The formula provided in sources is per-ray. 
    # The final loss is typically the mean over the batch.
    loss_per_ray = numerator / denominator
    
    # Return mean loss over the batch
    return torch.mean(torch.sum(loss_per_ray, dim=1))
```

## Subtopics

### Standard Photometric Loss
The baseline MSE loss used in NeRFs. It is simpler but may suffer from bias in Monte Carlo estimators. Choose this when using deterministic rendering or when bias is not a concern. [Link](7799-photometric-reconstruction-loss.md)

### Depth Supervision
Often combined with photometric loss to improve geometric consistency. It uses MSE on depth maps. [Link](7799-photometric-reconstruction-loss.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of rays in the batch. The additional cost comes from computing the cache estimator and applying the stopgrad operator, which are negligible compared to the forward pass of the radiance estimator.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the gradients and intermediate tensors.
*   **Measured Performance:** Outperforms TensoIR in albedo metrics and relighting by a large margin, despite potential noise in renderings [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces bias in gradient estimation for inverse rendering; effective for cache-based models.
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to Monte Carlo noise; requires careful tuning of secondary samples and averaging [S2].
*   **When to Use:** When optimizing NeRFs or inverse rendering models with stochastic radiance estimators and cache-based predictions.
*   **When Not to Use:** When deterministic rendering is used and bias is not a concern; when computational resources are extremely limited and noise reduction via averaging is not feasible.

## Background
The RawNeRF variant of the photometric loss was introduced to address bias in inverse rendering models that use cache-based predictions [S2]. It builds on the standard NeRF photometric loss [S1] and applies the gradient trick [10] to ensure correct gradient propagation through Monte Carlo estimators. The method is used in physically-based inverse rendering frameworks to supervise NeRF caches against physically-based models [S2].
