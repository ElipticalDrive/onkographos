# L2G-NeRF

Also called: Local-to-Global Registration for Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
L2G-NeRF is a bundle-adjusting Neural Radiance Field method that jointly optimizes neural field parameters and camera poses by combining a local photometric alignment objective with a global geometric alignment objective [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like BARF by computing gradients with respect to local pose perturbations rather than global pose parameters, which allows it to tolerate larger noise perturbations before divergence and offers higher computational efficiency [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $M$ images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^M$ with unknown camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized neural field parameters $\Theta$, optimized camera poses $\{T_i\}_{i=1}^M$, and rendered images.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Pose $T_i$:** Represented as a $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix in $SE(3)$, constructed from rotation $R_i \in SO(3)$ and translation $t_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    $$ T_i = \begin{pmatrix} R_i & t_i \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
*   **Rotation Parameterization:** $R_i$ is generated via the exponential map from the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$:
    $$ R_i = \exp(r_i) $$
    where $r_i$ is an axis-angle vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Ray Coordinates:** For a pixel $j$ in image $i$, the 2D coordinate is $x_j$. The 3D point in camera space is derived from $x_j$ and the intrinsic parameters (assumed known or fixed as per parent principles).

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda$: Multiplier for the global alignment objective.
    *   Synthetic/object-centric scenes: $1 \times 10^2$ [S1].
    *   Forward-facing LLFF scenes: Float between $1 \times 10^2$ and $1 \times 10^5$ [S1].
*   $n_r$: Base rotation noise multiplier. Scales additive rotation noise $r \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_r I)$. Scene-dependent, typically $0.01$ to $0.09$ [S1].
*   $n_t$: Base translation noise multiplier. Scales additive translation noise $t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_t I)$. Scene-dependent, typically $0.3$ to $0.7$ [S1].
*   $n_c$: Convergence schedule factor. Linearly increases noise multipliers by a common factor $\{n_c\}_{c=2}^9$ starting from base values [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    Initialize all camera poses to the identity transformation:
    $$ T_i = I \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, M\} $$
    Perturb initial poses using right multiplication to alter viewing directions and distances. The perturbation is defined by additive noise in the Lie algebra and translation space:
    $$ r \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_r I), \quad t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_t I) $$
    The perturbed pose $T'_i$ is computed as:
    $$ T'_i = T_i \cdot \begin{pmatrix} \exp(r) & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$
    [S1]

2.  **Optimization Loop:**
    For each training step (with increasing noise scale $n_c$):
    
    a. **Ray Sampling:** Generate rays for each pixel $x_j$ in each image $I_i$ using the current camera pose $T_i$.
    
    b. **Local Objective (Photometric Alignment):**
    Compute the rendering error by comparing the rendered color $R(T_i x_j; \Theta)$ with the target image pixel $I_i(x_j)$.
    $$ L_{local} = \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^N \| R(T_i x_j; \Theta) - I_i(x_j) \|^2 $$
    where $N$ is the number of pixels [S1].
    
    c. **Global Objective (Geometric Alignment):**
    Compute the geometric error between the transformed pose $T_{ji}$ (the pose of image $i$ transformed by the relative pose between images $j$ and $i$, or effectively the consistency of the pose graph) and the reference pose $T^*_i$. Note: The source defines the global objective as:
    $$ L_{global} = \lambda \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^N \| T_{ji} x_j - T^*_i x_j \|^2 $$
    where $T_{ji}$ is the transformed pose and $T^*_i$ is the reference pose. In the context of bundle adjustment without ground truth, this term typically enforces consistency across the pose graph or against a coarse initial estimate. The source notes that removing this term ($\lambda=0$) leads to suboptimal poses and ghosting artifacts [S1].
    
    d. **Total Loss:**
    $$ L_{total} = L_{local} + L_{global} $$
    
    e. **Gradient Update:**
    Backpropagate gradients through $L_{total}$ to update $\Theta$ and the pose parameters ($r_i, t_i$). The gradients are computed with respect to the local pose perturbations [S1].

3.  **Convergence Schedule:**
    Linearly increase the noise multipliers $n_r$ and $n_t$ by a common factor $\{n_c\}_{c=2}^9$ starting from base values $n_r=0.01, n_t=0.1$. This annealing strategy helps escape local minima [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** L2G-NeRF is more efficient than BARF because it computes gradients with respect to local pose perturbations rather than global pose parameters [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 Ti, L2G-NeRF takes approximately 4.5 hours for synthetic objects and 8 hours for real-world scenes. In comparison, BARF takes 8 and 10.5 hours respectively [S1].
*   **Robustness:** L2G-NeRF tolerates larger noise perturbations before divergence. It fails to converge at $n_c=8$ ($n_r=0.08, n_t=0.8$), whereas BARF fails at $n_c=4$ ($n_r=0.04, n_t=0.4$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Higher fidelity results than BARF; competitive rendering quality with reference NeRF trained on ground-truth poses; robust to moderate pose misalignment [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Fails to recover camera poses from identity transformation for inward-facing 360° scenes with large rotation displacements [S1]. Large $\lambda$ ($1 \times 10^5$) improves geometric accuracy but reduces robustness, potentially causing divergence (e.g., on the Flower scene) [S1].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for forward-facing LLFF scenes and synthetic object-centric scenes where camera poses are unknown but not extremely misaligned (e.g., not full 360° inward-facing).
*   **When not to use:** Inward-facing 360° scenes with large rotation displacements; scenes requiring very high geometric accuracy where robustness is critical and $\lambda$ cannot be tuned carefully [S1].

## Background
L2G-NeRF (Local-to-Global Registration for Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced to address the challenge of learning neural fields and resolving large camera pose misalignment simultaneously [S1]. It builds on the concept of bundle-adjusting neural radiance fields, extending it by establishing a unified formulation of local-to-global registration. The method demonstrates benefits for both 2D and 3D neural fields [S1]. It was evaluated on standard benchmarks including synthetic objects and the LLFF dataset [S1].
