# ConfEviSurrogate

Also called: Conformal CNN Surrogate with Normal Inverse-Gamma Head

## Overview
ConfEviSurrogate is a CNN-based surrogate model for scientific simulations that provides calibrated uncertainty bounds for its predictions [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the lack of calibrated uncertainty in standard CNN surrogates by replacing the standard output layer with a four-parameter Normal Inverse-Gamma head and applying conformal prediction calibration [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Beta Distribution Prior on Exit Opacity](2274-beta-distribution-prior-on-exit-opacity.md) which focuses on probabilistic priors for exit opacity in volume rendering, or [Uncertainty Prediction Loss](2961-uncertainty-prediction-loss.md) which uses uncertainty to weight data fidelity, ConfEviSurrogate focuses on generating prediction intervals with finite-sample coverage guarantees for full-volume predictions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A spatial feature or simulation parameter set defining the state of the system.
*   **Output:** A full-volume prediction of the simulation output (e.g., density, temperature) along with voxel-level uncertainty estimates (aleatoric and epistemic) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **CNN Architecture:** A convolutional neural network that generates full-volume predictions rather than point queries [S1, unofficial].
*   **Normal Inverse-Gamma Head:** A four-parameter output layer that separates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty [S1, unofficial]. The parameters typically estimate the mean, precision, and shape/scale of the underlying distribution for each voxel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Calibration Set:** A held-out dataset used for conformal prediction calibration [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Training:** Train the CNN surrogate on simulation data. The final layer is modified to output the four parameters of the Normal Inverse-Gamma distribution for each voxel, rather than a single deterministic value [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Forward Pass:** For a given input, the network produces the full-volume prediction and the uncertainty parameters in a single forward pass [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Conformal Calibration:** Apply conformal prediction techniques using a held-out calibration set to adjust the prediction intervals [S1, unofficial]. This step ensures that the prediction intervals have finite-sample coverage guarantees, meaning the true value falls within the interval with a specified probability [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The Normal Inverse-Gamma distribution is defined by four parameters: $\mu$ (mean), $\lambda$ (precision scaling), $\alpha$ (shape), and $\beta$ (scale) [S1, unofficial]. The probability density function for a voxel value $x$ is:
$$
p(x | \mu, \lambda, \alpha, \beta) = \int_0^\infty \mathcal{N}(x | \mu, \lambda \sigma^2) \text{Inv-Gamma}(\sigma^2 | \alpha, \beta) d\sigma^2
$$
where $\mathcal{N}$ is the Normal distribution and $\text{Inv-Gamma}$ is the Inverse-Gamma distribution [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **CNN Hyperparameters:** Not specified in the sources.
*   **Conformal Coverage Level:** Not specified, but typically set to a desired confidence level (e.g., 95%) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Data Scarcity:** Conformal calibration requires a held-out calibration set, which may be unavailable for data-scarce simulations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Full-Volume Prediction:** The CNN architecture generates full-volume predictions, which may be computationally expensive for very large volumes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Uncertainty Calibration:** If the calibration set is not representative of the test data, the coverage guarantees may not hold [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Bayesian Inference Surrogate Framework
This variant trains a surrogate and then applies Bayesian inference to obtain the posterior distribution over parameters conditioned on a user-specified spatial feature [S1, unofficial]. It uses a Fisher Information Matrix prior to accelerate prior probability computation by three orders of magnitude compared to deep ensembles [S1, unofficial]. Posterior characterization requires approximately 1 s of burn-in plus 100–200 sampling steps [S1, unofficial]. It has been validated only on 2D time-varying fields (up to $1024 \times 256 \times 200$) with at most 4 parameters; extension to volumetric data and higher-dimensional parameter spaces remains an open challenge [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Uncertainty estimation takes 0.03–0.36 s per prediction [S1, unofficial]. This is significantly faster than Monte Carlo Dropout (0.83–25.17 s) and deep ensembles (1.60–3.00 s) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not specified in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On three datasets (MPAS-Ocean at $192 \times 96 \times 96$, Nyx at $256^3$, CloverLeaf3D at $64^3$), ConfEviSurrogate achieved the highest voxel-level correlation between predicted uncertainty and true error, outperforming both Monte Carlo Dropout and deep ensembles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides calibrated uncertainty bounds with finite-sample coverage guarantees; fast inference time; separates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a held-out calibration set, which may not be available for data-scarce simulations; generates full-volume predictions which may be memory-intensive for large volumes [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When calibrated uncertainty bounds are critical for scientific simulations and computational efficiency is a concern.
*   **When Not to Use:** When data is scarce and a calibration set cannot be obtained, or when point-wise predictions are sufficient instead of full-volume predictions.

## Background
ConfEviSurrogate was introduced to address the lack of calibrated uncertainty bounds in CNN surrogates for scientific simulations [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of using probabilistic heads (Normal Inverse-Gamma) to model uncertainty and combines it with conformal prediction to provide rigorous coverage guarantees [S1, unofficial]. The method was validated on several large-scale scientific simulation datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in providing accurate and calibrated uncertainty estimates [S1, unofficial].
