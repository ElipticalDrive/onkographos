# Pseudo-depth Supervised Loss

Also called: depth loss, robust depth loss, minimum distance loss, weight variance loss, weight transition loss

## Overview
Pseudo-depth Supervised Loss is a family of regularization and consistency terms used in differentiable volume rendering to constrain the geometry of a neural radiance field (NeRF) using external depth priors. It addresses the problem of geometry error degradation and unreliable depth estimation in texture-less regions by filtering predictions based on confidence scores or by using robust loss functions that are less sensitive to outliers than standard L2 losses [S1][S2, unofficial]. The method encourages the rendered depth to align with pseudo-depth targets (derived from Structure-from-Motion or monocular estimators) while retaining freedom for inaccurate geometry through gradient suppression at large errors [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Ray $r(t) = o + td$ defined by origin $o$ and direction $d$ [S1].
    *   Rendered depth expectation $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ along the camera axis [S1].
    *   Pseudo-depth target $D(\mathbf{r})$ or $D(p)$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Rendered color $\tilde{c}(p)$ and image $I(p)$ [S2, unofficial].
    *   Confidence score $U(p)$ for each pixel [S2, unofficial].
    *   (Optional) Ground truth visibility $\xi_b$, surface normal $n$, and depth $d$ for probabilistic directed distance field (PDDF) variants [S3].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ or $\mathcal{L}_{robust}$, $\mathcal{L}_{render}$, $\mathcal{L}_S$ [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].

### Data Structures
The method relies on standard tensor structures for rays, images, and depth maps. Specific implementations may use:
*   **PDDF Representation:** A set of weights $w_i$ and delta components $d_i$ for $i=1 \dots K$ [S3]. Typically $K=2$ [S3].
*   **Visibility Prediction:** Scalar $\xi$ [S3].

### Steps and Formulas

#### 1. Pseudo-Depth Generation (Preprocessing)
Before computing the loss, pseudo-depth targets are often generated from sparse geometry scaffolds [S4].
1.  Estimate camera poses using Structure-from-Motion (SFM) solvers (e.g., COLMAP) to obtain a sparse point cloud [S4].
2.  Align the camera coordinate system and the sparse point cloud [S4].
3.  Calculate pseudo-depth $D$ by measuring the distance between the sparse point cloud and the camera position [S4].

#### 2. Robust Depth Loss (NeRFVS)
Designed to be less sensitive to outliers than L2 loss, encouraging rays to terminate around pseudo-depth while allowing freedom for inaccurate geometry [S1].

**Formula:**
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{robust}}(\mathbf{r}) = 
\begin{cases} 
0.5\Delta D(\mathbf{r})^{2}, & \text{if } \Delta D(\mathbf{r}) < \beta \\ 
\beta ^ {2} \left(0.5 + \log \left(\frac {\Delta D(\mathbf{r})}{\beta }\right)\right), & \text{otherwise } 
\end{cases}
$$

**Definitions:**
*   $\Delta D(\mathbf{r}) = |\hat{D}(\mathbf{r}) - D(\mathbf{r})|$ is the absolute depth difference [S1]. Note: The source text says "absolute depth difference" but the formula uses $\Delta D$. The text implies $\Delta D$ is the magnitude.
*   $\hat{D}(\mathbf{r})$ is the depth expectation along the camera axis of ray $r(t)$ [S1].
*   $D(\mathbf{r})$ is the distance value from the geometry scaffold [S1].
*   $\beta$ is a constant threshold. Typical value: $0.1$ [S1].

**Properties:**
*   For $\Delta D > \beta$, the loss grows logarithmically, making the gradient decrease to zero as error increases, reducing sensitivity to outliers [S1].
*   The gradient is negligible when $\Delta D$ approaches zero or is very large [S1].

#### 3. Confidence-Filtered Render Loss (Improved NeRF)
Supervises rendered depth against pseudo-depth, filtered by a confidence score to ensure reliability in texture-less regions [S2, unofficial].

**Formula:**
$$
L_{render} = \sum_{p \in I} \lambda_1 \|\tilde{c}(p) - I(p)\|^2 + \lambda_2 \mathbb{I}(U(p) > \delta) |\tilde{d}(p) - D(p)|
$$

**Definitions:**
*   $p$: Pixel index in image $I$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\tilde{c}(p)$: Rendered color at pixel $p$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $I(p)$: Ground truth image color at pixel $p$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\tilde{d}(p)$: Rendered depth at pixel $p$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $D(p)$: Pseudo-depth target at pixel $p$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $U(p)$: Confidence score for pixel $p$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$: Indicator function, returns 1 if condition is true, 0 otherwise [S2, unofficial].
*   $\delta$: Confidence threshold. Typical value: $0.5$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_1$: Weight for color reconstruction. Typical value: $1.0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_2$: Weight for depth loss. Grows linearly from $1e-4$ to $1e-2$ based on training steps [S2, unofficial].

**Total Loss (Multi-scale):**
$$
L = \sum_{l=1}^{3} L_{render}^l + \lambda_3 L_{mvs}
$$
*   $L_{render}^l$: Render loss at level $l$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $L_{mvs}$: Multi-view stereo loss [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_3$: Weight for MVS loss. Typical value: $1.0$ [S2, unofficial].

#### 4. Probabilistic Directed Distance Field (PDDF) Loss
Used in probabilistic shape representations to fit mesh-derived data [S3].

**Components:**
1.  **Minimum Distance Loss ($L_d$):** Trains the highest probability depth component.
    $$
    L_d = \xi |d_{b_{i^*}} - d|
    $$
    *   $i^* = \arg \max_j w_j$ is the maximum likelihood PDDF index [S3].
    *   $d_{b_{i^*}}$ is the depth component corresponding to $i^*$ [S3].
    *   $\xi$ is the visibility prediction [S3].
    *   $d$ is the ground truth depth [S3].

2.  **Visibility Objective ($L_\xi$):** Binary cross entropy.
    $$
    L_\xi = \text{BCE}(\xi, \xi_b)
    $$
    *   $\xi_b$ is the ground truth visibility [S3].

3.  **First-Order Normals Loss ($L_n$):** Matches surface normals via the gradient of the predicted depth.
    $$
    L_n = -\xi |n^T n_{b_{i^*}}(p, v)|
    $$
    *   $n$ is the ground truth surface normal [S3].
    *   $n_{b_{i^*}}(p, v)$ is the normal derived from $\nabla_p d_{b_{i^*}}$ [S3].
    *   $p, v$ define the oriented point [S3].

4.  **Directed Eikonal Regularization ($L_{DE}$):**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{DE}} = \gamma_{\text{E},d} \sum_i \xi \left[ \nabla_i \widehat{d}_i v + 1 \right]^2 + \gamma_{\text{E},\xi} [\nabla_\xi \widehat{\xi} v]^2
    $$
    *   Applied on visibility and each delta component of depth [S3].
    *   $\gamma_{\text{E},d}, \gamma_{\text{E},\xi}$ are hyper-parameters [S3].

5.  **Weight Field Regularization ($L_W$):**
    $$
    L_W = \gamma_V L_V + \gamma_T L_T
    $$
    *   Encourages low entropy PDDF outputs and transition of the maximum likelihood delta component at discontinuities [S3].

    *   **Weight Variance Loss ($L_V$):**
        $$
        L_V = \sum_i w_i
        $$
        *   Encourages low entropy to prevent $i^*$ from switching unnecessarily [S3].

    *   **Weight Transition Loss ($L_T$):**
        $$
        L_T = \max(0, \varepsilon_T - |\nabla_p w_{1n}|)^2
        $$
        *   $\varepsilon_T$ is a hyper-parameter controlling desired transition speed [S3].
        *   $K=2$, so using $w_1$ alone is sufficient [S3].
        *   **Pitfall:** $L_T$ is only applied to oriented points that are surface (S) and tangent (T) data, where a discontinuity is desired [S3].

**Total PDDF Shape-Fitting Loss:**
$$
\mathcal{L}_S = \gamma_d \mathcal{L}_d + \gamma_\xi \mathcal{L}_\xi + \gamma_n \mathcal{L}_n + \mathcal{L}_{\text{DE}} + \mathcal{L}_{W}
$$

#### 5. Combined Loss (UE4-NeRF)
Combines photometric and pseudo-depth losses [S4].
$$
L = L_{rgb} + L_D
$$
*   $L_{rgb}$: Photometric loss [S4].
*   $L_D$: Pseudo-depth loss [S4].

### Pseudocode

```python
def compute_robust_depth_loss(rendered_depth, pseudo_depth, beta=0.1):
    """
    Computes the robust depth loss as defined in NeRFVS [S1].
    """
    # Absolute depth difference
    delta_D = abs(rendered_depth - pseudo_depth)
    
    # Robust loss calculation
    # Case 1: delta_D < beta
    mask_small = delta_D < beta
    loss_small = 0.5 * delta_D[mask_small] ** 2
    
    # Case 2: delta_D >= beta
    mask_large = ~mask_small
    # Avoid log(0) or negative log, though delta_D >= beta > 0
    loss_large = beta**2 * (0.5 + log(delta_D[mask_large] / beta))
    
    # Combine
    loss = zeros_like(delta_D)
    loss[mask_small] = loss_small
    loss[mask_large] = loss_large
    
    return mean(loss)

def compute_confidence_filtered_render_loss(
    rendered_color, 
    gt_image, 
    rendered_depth, 
    pseudo_depth, 
    confidence_map, 
    delta=0.5, 
    lambda_1=1.0, 
    lambda_2=1e-4  # Starts low, grows to 1e-2
):
    """
    Computes the confidence-filtered render loss as defined in Improved NeRF [S2, unofficial].
    """
    # Color loss
    color_loss = lambda_1 * mean((rendered_color - gt_image) ** 2)
    
    # Depth loss with confidence filtering
    # Indicator function: 1 if confidence > delta, else 0
    valid_mask = (confidence_map > delta).float()
    
    depth_diff = abs(rendered_depth - pseudo_depth)
    depth_loss = lambda_2 * mean(valid_mask * depth_diff)
    
    return color_loss + depth_loss

def compute_pddf_loss(
    weights, 
    deltas, 
    visibility_pred, 
    visibility_gt, 
    depth_pred_gt, 
    normal_pred_gt, 
    normal_gt, 
    depth_gt, 
    gamma_E_d, 
    gamma_E_xi, 
    gamma_V, 
    gamma_T, 
    epsilon_T, 
    is_surface_or_tangent_mask
):
    """
    Computes the PDDF shape-fitting loss [S3].
    """
    # 1. Minimum Distance Loss
    # i_star = argmax(weights)
    i_star = argmax(weights, dim=0) # Assuming weights are [K, H, W]
    d_i_star = gather(deltas, i_star) # Select delta component for each pixel
    L_d = mean(visibility_pred * abs(d_i_star - depth_gt))
    
    # 2. Visibility Objective
    L_xi = binary_cross_entropy(visibility_pred, visibility_gt)
    
    # 3. Normals Loss
    # n_i_star derived from gradient of d_i_star
    # Note: Implementation of n_i_star requires gradient computation
    n_i_star = compute_normal_from_depth(d_i_star) 
    L_n = mean(-visibility_pred * abs(dot(normal_gt, n_i_star)))
    
    # 4. Directed Eikonal
    # Gradient of delta components and visibility w.r.t ray direction v
    # Simplified: assume v is provided or implicit in gradient op
    grad_d = gradient(deltas, axis='ray_direction')
    grad_xi = gradient(visibility_pred, axis='ray_direction')
    
    term_d = sum((grad_d * v + 1)**2, dim=0) # Sum over K components
    term_xi = (grad_xi * v)**2
    
    L_DE = gamma_E_d * mean(visibility_pred * term_d) + gamma_E_xi * mean(visibility_pred * term_xi)
    
    # 5. Weight Regularization
    # L_V: Weight variance (sum of weights)
    L_V = mean(sum(weights, dim=0))
    
    # L_T: Weight transition loss
    # Only applied to S and T points
    if is_surface_or_tangent_mask.any():
        # Gradient of w1 w.r.t position p
        grad_w1 = gradient(weights[0], axis='position')
        # |grad_w1|
        abs_grad_w1 = abs(grad_w1)
        # max(0, eps_T - |grad_w1|)^2
        L_T_raw = max(0, epsilon_T - abs_grad_w1)**2
        L_T = mean(L_T_raw[is_surface_or_tangent_mask])
    else:
        L_T = 0.0
        
    L_W = gamma_V * L_V + gamma_T * L_T
    
    # Total
    L_S = gamma_d * L_d + gamma_xi * L_xi + gamma_n * L_n + L_DE + L_W
    
    return L_S
```

### Parameters
*   **$\beta$**: Threshold for robust depth loss. Value: $0.1$ [S1].
*   **$\delta$**: Confidence threshold for filtering pixels. Value: $0.5$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\lambda_1$**: Color loss weight. Value: $1.0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\lambda_2$**: Depth loss weight. Grows linearly from $1e-4$ to $1e-2$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\lambda_3$**: MVS loss weight. Value: $1.0$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\varepsilon_T$**: Transition speed hyper-parameter for PDDF [S3].
*   **$K$**: Number of delta components. Value: $2$ [S3].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Texture-less regions:** Monocular depth prediction is unreliable here. Mitigated by filtering pixels with confidence score $U(p) \le \delta$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outliers:** Large depth errors can degrade extrapolation performance. The robust loss formulation reduces gradient magnitude for large errors, preventing the network from overfitting to inaccurate pseudo-depth [S1].
*   **PDDF Transition:** The weight transition loss $L_T$ must only be applied to surface (S) and tangent (T) points where discontinuities are desired. Applying it elsewhere may cause artifacts [S3].
*   **Coordinate Alignment:** Pseudo-depth generation requires accurate alignment between camera poses and the sparse point cloud from SFM [S4].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Time:** The robust loss and confidence-filtered loss are element-wise operations on depth maps, resulting in $O(N)$ complexity where $N$ is the number of pixels or rays. The PDDF loss involves additional gradient computations and aggregation over $K$ components, but remains linear in the number of samples.
*   **Memory:** Requires storing rendered depth, pseudo-depth, confidence maps, and potentially intermediate gradients for the Eikonal and normal losses. The PDDF representation adds memory for $K$ weight and delta components per sample.

### Measured Performance
*   **NeRFVS:** Uses robust depth loss to improve geometry scaffolds. Specific PSNR/SSIM gains are not detailed in the provided notes, but it addresses geometry error degradation [S1].
*   **Improved NeRF:** Achieves significant improvements in depth metric accuracy on DTU dataset (e.g., Abs err $\downarrow$ 4.29 vs 7.00 for PixelNeRF) and novel-view synthesis metrics (PSNR $\uparrow$ 28.52) [S2, unofficial].
*   **PDDF:** Enables ray-based shape representations with mesh-derived data fitting [S3].

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Robust to outliers in pseudo-depth data [S1].
    *   Filters unreliable depth predictions in texture-less regions [S2, unofficial].
    *   Provides strong geometric priors for NeRF optimization [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   PDDF variant allows for explicit shape fitting and discontinuity handling [S3].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Relies on the quality of pseudo-depth generation (SFM or monocular) [S1][S2, unofficial][S4].
    *   SFM-based pseudo-depth is sparse and may require interpolation or dense estimation [S1, S4].
    *   Monocular depth is inherently ambiguous and requires confidence filtering [S2, unofficial].
    *   PDDF requires careful tuning of transition losses and is limited to specific data types (S, T points) for certain regularizations [S3].

### When to Use
*   Use when pseudo-depth priors are available and noisy/outlier-prone (use Robust Loss) [S1].
*   Use when monocular depth estimates are available but unreliable in certain regions (use Confidence Filtering) [S2, unofficial].
*   Use when explicit shape fitting and discontinuity handling are required (use PDDF Loss) [S3].
*   Use when integrating NeRF with real-time rendering engines like UE4 using sparse geometry [S4].

## Background

*   **NeRFVS:** Proposed the robust depth loss to handle dense but inaccurate depth maps from geometry scaffolds, addressing geometry error degradation in extrapolation [S1].
*   **Improved Neural Radiance Fields:** Introduced confidence-filtered pseudo-depth supervision to mitigate effects of unreliable depth in texture-less regions, using a multi-scale framework [S2, unofficial].
*   **PDDF:** Developed probabilistic directed distance fields for ray-based shape representations, introducing minimum distance, visibility, normals, Eikonal, and weight regularization losses for fitting mesh-derived data [S3].
*   **UE4-NeRF:** Utilized pseudo-depth from SFM sparse point clouds for real-time rendering of large-scale scenes in Unreal Engine 4 [S4].
