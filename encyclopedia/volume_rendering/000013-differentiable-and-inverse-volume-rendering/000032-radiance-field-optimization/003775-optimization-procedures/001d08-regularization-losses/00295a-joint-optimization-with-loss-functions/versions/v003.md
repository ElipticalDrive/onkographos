# Joint Optimization with Loss Functions

Also called: Joint Optimization of Surfel Parameters and HDR Environment Map

## Overview
This method defines a composite objective function for the joint optimization of 3D surface splat (surfel) parameters and an HDR environment map in inverse rendering. It solves the problem of unstable geometry and lighting estimation by combining photometric fidelity with geometric regularization terms that enforce depth consolidation and normal consistency. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its specific focus on multi-layer surfel compositing constraints (inter- and intra-layer depth penalties) and its joint update of material properties alongside the lighting environment [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered image $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$, ground-truth silhouette mask $\mathbf{M}^*$, surfel parameters (positions $\mathbf{c}$, quaternions $\mathbf{q}$, log-scales, albedo, metallic, roughness), and current HDR environment map.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar total loss $\mathcal{L}$ and gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ with respect to all parameters $\theta$.

**Data Structures**
*   **Surfel Attributes:** Each surfel $i$ stores position $\mathbf{c}_i$, normal $\mathbf{n}_i$, compositing weight $w_i$, mean depth $z_i$, and depth variance $\sigma_i^2$.
*   **Neighbor Index:** A spatial data structure (e.g., KD-tree or grid) mapping surfels to their nearest neighbors $\mathcal{N}_i$ with compatible normal orientations. This structure is rebuilt periodically and cached between optimization steps [S1, unofficial].

**Loss Components**

1.  **Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$):** Combines L1 reconstruction and Structural Similarity (SSIM). For HDR targets, both $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and $\mathbf{I}^*$ are transformed to log-space and sRGB before loss computation to prevent gradient dominance by bright pixels [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = (1 - \lambda_{\text{ssim}}) \|\hat{\mathbf{I}} - \mathbf{I}^*\|_1 + \lambda_{\text{ssim}} (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{I}}, \mathbf{I}^*))
    $$
    where $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$ is the SSIM weighting factor.

2.  **Silhouette Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$):** Supervises total accumulated coverage using the residual transmittance $\bar{T}_{K+1}$ (where $K$ is the number of layers). It uses a soft Intersection-over-Union (IoU) metric. This term is disabled once adaptive density control begins [S1, unofficial].
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} = \text{IoU}(1 - \bar{T}_{K+1}, \mathbf{M}^*)
    $$

3.  **Depth Consolidation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$):** Penalizes spurious depth distributions across the $K$ composited surface layers at each pixel $p \in \mathcal{P}$. It consists of two terms:
    *   **Inter-layer term ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}}$):** Penalizes weight allocation across distant layers, driving contributions to a single surface layer.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} = \sum_{i < j} w_i w_j |z_i - z_j|
        $$
    *   **Intra-layer term ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}}$):** Penalizes depth spread within each layer, encouraging coplanarity.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}} = \sum_i w_i^2 \sigma_i^2
        $$
    The total consolidation loss is the mean over all pixels:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{inter-c}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{intra-c}})
    $$

4.  **Normal Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}$):** Penalizes angular deviation between a surfel's normal and its orientation-aware nearest neighbors in 3D space.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}} = \frac{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij} (1 - \mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{n}_j)}{\sum_i \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} w_{ij}}
    $$
    where $w_{ij} = \exp(-\|\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{c}_j\|^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ are distance-dependent weights, and $\sigma$ is derived from local intersample spacing [S1, unofficial].

**Total Loss**
The final objective is a weighted sum:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \lambda_{\text{sil}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} + \lambda_{\text{nc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}
$$
where $\lambda_{\text{sil}}, \lambda_{\text{cons}}, \lambda_{\text{nc}}$ are scalar hyperparameters [S1, unofficial].

**Optimization Steps**
1.  **Initialize:** Set surfel parameters (positions, tangents/quaternions, log-space scales, albedo, metallic, roughness) and HDR environment map. Initialize Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Render:** Compute $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and intermediate compositing values ($w_i, z_i, \sigma_i^2, \bar{T}_{K+1}$) for the current view.
3.  **Preprocess:** If HDR, apply log-space and sRGB transfer to $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ and $\mathbf{I}^*$.
4.  **Compute Losses:**
    *   Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ using L1 and SSIM.
    *   If foreground masks are available and adaptive density control is not active, calculate $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sil}}$.
    *   Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ by summing inter- and intra-layer penalties for all pixels.
    *   Update neighbor indices if periodic rebuild threshold is met. Calculate $\mathcal{L}_{\text{nc}}$ using cached neighbors.
5.  **Aggregate:** Compute $\mathcal{L}$ using the weighted sum formula.
6.  **Backpropagate:** Compute gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ for all parameters $\theta$.
7.  **Update:** Apply Adam optimizer step to update $\theta$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   $\lambda_{\text{ssim}}$: SSIM weight. Value not given in the sources.
*   $\lambda_{\text{sil}}, \lambda_{\text{cons}}, \lambda_{\text{nc}}$: Scalar weights for respective losses. Values not given in the sources.
*   $\sigma$: Bandwidth for normal consistency weights, derived from local intersample spacing. Value not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Gradient Dominance:** In HDR training, bright pixels can dominate gradients. Mitigation: Apply log-space and sRGB transfer to both rendered and ground-truth images before loss computation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Early Training Coverage:** Silhouette loss provides strong coverage signals early on but must be disabled when adaptive density control (densification) begins, as photometric loss and densification take over [S1, unofficial].
*   **Computational Cost:** Neighbor index computation is expensive. Mitigation: Rebuild the spatial data structure periodically and cache it between updates [S1, unofficial].
*   **Oversmoothing:** Screen-space normal regularizers can oversmooth geometry. This method uses 3D geometric normal consistency instead to avoid this [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The photometric and consolidation losses are $O(N)$ with respect to pixels and layers. The normal consistency loss is $O(N \cdot k)$ where $k$ is the number of neighbors, amortized by periodic spatial structure rebuilds.
*   **Strengths:** Enforces geometric thinness and smoothness directly in 3D, preventing spurious depth layers and normal artifacts common in pure photometric optimization. Jointly optimizes lighting and geometry.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful scheduling of loss terms (e.g., disabling silhouette loss). Computationally heavier due to neighbor lookups and multi-layer depth statistics.
*   **When to use:** Object-level inverse rendering where high-fidelity geometry and relighting are required, and ground-truth masks or multi-view consistency is available.

## Background
This method originates from the 3DSS (3D Surface Splatting for Inverse Rendering) framework. It builds on scattered-data approximation principles [Wendland 2004] and integrates depth consolidation ideas from [Barron et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024] adapted for surface layers. It was developed to address the lack of geometric priors in multi-layer compositing and the oversmoothing issues of screen-space normal regularizers [S1, unofficial].
