# Light-SMAE

Also called: Smooth Manifold Auto-Encoder

## Overview
Light-SMAE is a smooth manifold auto-encoder that learns a low-dimensional, differentiable latent representation of high-dynamic-range (HDR) environment maps, serving as a strong regularizer or prior for inverse volume rendering and reflectance decomposition [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from direct optimization of lighting parameters by constraining the solution space to a smooth manifold learned from data, which enables stable gradient-based optimization of illumination in ill-posed inverse problems [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** An HDR environment map $p$, downscaled to $128 \times 256$ resolution.
*   **Output:** A 128-dimensional latent embedding $z_l \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ (via Encoder) or a reconstructed HDR environment map $\hat{p}_0$ (via Decoder).

**Data Structures:**
The architecture consists of an Encoder $E$, a Decoder $G$, and a Discriminator $D$. All are based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) utilizing CoordConv layers.

*   **CoordConv Layer:** A 2D convolution where the input tensor is augmented with coordinate channels (x, y) before convolution.
*   **Conv Layer:** Standard 2D convolution.
*   **ConvT Layer:** Transposed 2D convolution (upsampling).

**Network Architecture (Light-SMAE):**
The specific layer configurations are defined in Table A1 of the source [S1, unofficial].

*   **Encoder $E$:**
    1.  CoordConv: Kernel $3 \times 3$, Stride 1, 8 features, Activation ELU.
    2.  ConvT: Kernel $1 \times 1$, Stride 1, 64 features, Activation ELU.
    3.  CoordConv: Kernel $3 \times 3$, Stride 1, 8 features, Activation ReLU.
    *   *Note:* The source lists these as the initial layers. The full depth is implied by the need to map to $\mathbb{R}^{128}$, but exact subsequent layers for the encoder are not fully enumerated in the provided text beyond the initial structure and the mention of "Table A1" which is not fully transcribed for the encoder's final layers. However, the discriminator and decoder details are explicit.

*   **Decoder $G$:**
    1.  CoordConv: Kernel $3 \times 3$, Stride 1, 8 features, Activation ELU.
    2.  CoordConv: Kernel $4 \times 4$, Stride 2, 21 features, Activation ELU.
    3.  ConvT: Kernel $4 \times 4$, Stride 2, 58 features, Activation ELU.
    4.  ... (Intermediate layers implied by Table A1 structure) ...
    5.  Final Output Layer: Conv $1 \times 1$, Stride 1, 3 features (RGB), Activation $\exp(x - 1)$.
    *   *Padding:* Uses 'same' padding.

*   **Discriminator $D$:**
    1.  CoordConv: Kernel $3 \times 3$, Stride 1, 8 features, Activation ELU.
    2.  Conv: Kernel $4 \times 4$, Stride 2, 21 features, Activation ELU.
    *   *Padding:* No padding.

**Training Procedure:**
The network is trained jointly to minimize a composite loss function. The training involves the following steps:

1.  **Data Preprocessing:**
    *   Load environment maps.
    *   Augment dataset: Rotate each of the 320 environment maps 10 times. Randomly blend two environment maps during training [S1, unofficial].
    *   Downscale inputs to $128 \times 256$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Transform HDR input $x$ to LDR for encoder: $x_{ldr} = \log(1 + x)$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Forward Pass (Encoding):**
    *   Pass $x_{ldr}$ through Encoder $E$ to get latent vector $z$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Forward Pass (Decoding & Reconstruction):**
    *   Pass $z$ through Decoder $G$ to get reconstruction $\hat{p}_{ldr}$.
    *   Transform LDR output to HDR: $\hat{p}_{hdr} = \exp(\hat{p}_{ldr} - 1)$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Latent Interpolation (for Smoothness and Cyclic Loss):**
    *   Randomly sample two latent vectors from the mini-batch: $z_a$ and $z_b$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Sample $m$ linearly interpolated embeddings uniformly spaced between $z_a$ and $z_b$. Let $\alpha_n$ be the interpolation scalar for step $n$, where $n = 1, \dots, m$.
    *   Calculate interpolated latents: $z_n^0 = (1 - \alpha_n) z_a + \alpha_n z_b$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Parameter:* $m = 5$ interpolation steps between each of the batch halves [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Forward Pass (Interpolated Reconstruction and Re-encoding):**
    *   Pass each $z_n^0$ through Decoder $G$ to obtain $\hat{p}_n^0$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Pass each $\hat{p}_n^0$ (transformed to LDR) through Encoder $E$ to obtain re-estimated latents $\hat{z}_n^0$ [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Loss Calculation:**
    The total loss $L$ is a weighted sum of four components [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ L = L_r + \lambda_1 L_a + \lambda_2 L_c + \lambda_3 L_s $$

    *   **Reconstruction Loss ($L_r$):**
        Calculated using the Mean Absolute Log Error (MALE) between the ground truth HDR $x^*$ and the predicted HDR $\hat{x}$ [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_r = |\log(1 + x^*) - \log(1 + \hat{x})| $$
        *(Note: The source defines MALE as the loss metric for reconstruction.)*

    *   **Adversarial Loss ($L_a$):**
        Standard adversarial loss from LSGAN [41] applied to the discriminator $D$ evaluating the interpolated reconstructions $\hat{p}_n^0$ [S1, unofficial].

    *   **Cyclic Loss ($L_c$):**
        L2-loss between the interpolated latents $\{z_n^0\}$ and their re-estimated counterparts $\{\hat{z}_n^0\}$ [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_c = \sum_{n=1}^m \| z_n^0 - \hat{z}_n^0 \|^2 $$

    *   **Smoothness Loss ($L_s$):**
        Imposed on the gradient of the decoder $G$ with respect to the interpolating scalar value $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_s = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{n=1}^m \left( \nabla_\alpha G(z_n^0) \right)^2 $$

7.  **Optimization:**
    *   Update parameters of $E$, $G$, and $D$ using the Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Latent Dimension:** $z_l \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_1 = 0.01$ (Adversarial), $\lambda_2 = 0.0001$ (Cyclic), $\lambda_3 = 0.05$ (Smoothness) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Batch Size:** 24 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Steps:** 4 million steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** Single NVIDIA 1080 TI GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Training Time:** Approximately 5 days [S1, unofficial].

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.optim as optim

# Define the Light-SMAE components based on Table A1 description
class CoordConv(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_channels, out_channels, kernel_size, stride, activation='elu'):
        super().__init__()
        # CoordConv adds coordinate channels to input
        self.conv = nn.Conv2d(in_channels + 2, out_channels, kernel_size, stride, padding=0) # No padding for encoder/discriminator
        self.activation = nn.ELU() if activation == 'elu' else nn.ReLU()
        
    def forward(self, x):
        b, c, h, w = x.shape
        # Generate coordinate grids
        y_coords = torch.linspace(-1, 1, h).view(1, 1, h, 1).expand(b, 1, h, w)
        x_coords = torch.linspace(-1, 1, w).view(1, 1, 1, w).expand(b, 1, h, w)
        # Concatenate coordinates
        x_with_coords = torch.cat([x, y_coords, x_coords], dim=1)
        out = self.conv(x_with_coords)
        return self.activation(out)

class LightSMAE:
    def __init__(self):
        # Encoder: CoordConv(8, elu) -> ConvT(64, elu) -> CoordConv(8, relu)
        # Note: Exact intermediate dimensions for encoder are not fully specified in text, 
        # assuming standard CNN flow to latent space.
        self.encoder = nn.Sequential(
            CoordConv(3, 8, 3, 1, 'elu'), # Input is 3-channel LDR (log(1+x))
            nn.ConvTranspose2d(8, 64, 1, 1), # ConvT 1x1
            nn.ELU(),
            CoordConv(64, 8, 3, 1, 'relu') # Output to latent z
        )
        
        # Decoder: CoordConv(8, elu) -> CoordConv(21, elu) -> ConvT(58, elu) ... -> Conv(3, exp(x-1))
        # Note: Full architecture details for intermediate decoder layers are in Table A1, 
        # which is not fully transcribed. We define the structure conceptually.
        self.decoder = nn.Sequential(
            CoordConv(128, 8, 3, 1, 'elu'), # Input latent z (128 dim)
            # ... intermediate layers mapping 128 -> spatial features ...
            CoordConv(?, 21, 4, 2, 'elu'),
            nn.ConvTranspose2d(21, 58, 4, 2, padding='same'), # ConvT
            nn.ELU(),
            # ... more layers ...
            nn.Conv2d(?, 3, 1, 1, padding='same'), # Final 1x1 conv
            nn.Softplus(beta=1, threshold=20) # Approximation of exp(x-1) for stability, or custom activation
        )
        
        # Discriminator
        self.discriminator = nn.Sequential(
            CoordConv(3, 8, 3, 1, 'elu'),
            nn.Conv2d(8, 21, 4, 2, padding=0), # No padding
            nn.ELU()
        )
        
        self.optimizer = optim.Adam(list(self.encoder.parameters()) + 
                                    list(self.decoder.parameters()) + 
                                    list(self.discriminator.parameters()), 
                                    lr=5e-4)
        
        # Hyperparameters
        self.lambda_a = 0.01
        self.lambda_c = 0.0001
        self.lambda_s = 0.05
        self.m = 5 # Interpolation steps

    def hdr_to_ldr(self, x):
        return torch.log(1 + x)

    def ldr_to_hdr(self, x):
        return torch.exp(x - 1)

    def forward(self, hdr_env_map):
        # 1. Encode
        ldr_input = self.hdr_to_ldr(hdr_env_map)
        z = self.encoder(ldr_input)
        
        # 2. Decode
        ldr_recon = self.decoder(z)
        hdr_recon = self.ldr_to_hdr(ldr_recon)
        
        return z, hdr_recon

    def compute_smoothness_loss(self, z_a, z_b, m):
        """
        Computes Ls: Smoothness loss on the gradient of the decoder w.r.t interpolation scalar alpha.
        """
        # Sample m interpolation steps
        alphas = torch.linspace(0, 1, m + 2)[1:-1] # Exclude 0 and 1 to get m points strictly between? 
        # Source says "m linearly interpolated embeddings ... uniformly spaced between za and zb".
        # Usually this implies alpha in (0, 1). Let's assume m points.
        
        smoothness_loss = 0.0
        for alpha in alphas:
            # Interpolate latent
            z_interp = (1 - alpha) * z_a + alpha * z_b
            z_interp.requires_grad_(True)
            
            # Decode
            recon = self.decoder(z_interp)
            
            # Compute gradient w.r.t alpha
            # Since z_interp depends on alpha, we use chain rule or numerical differentiation.
            # Analytically: d(recon)/d(alpha) = d(recon)/d(z) * d(z)/d(alpha)
            # d(z)/d(alpha) = z_b - z_a
            dz_dalpha = z_b - z_a
            
            # Gradient of recon w.r.t z_interp
            grad_recon_z = torch.autograd.grad(recon.sum(), z_interp, create_graph=True)[0]
            
            # Gradient w.r.t alpha
            grad_recon_alpha = torch.sum(grad_recon_z * dz_dalpha)
            
            smoothness_loss += grad_recon_alpha ** 2
            
        return smoothness_loss / m

    def compute_cyclic_loss(self, z_a, z_b, m):
        """
        Computes Lc: L2 loss between interpolated latents and re-estimated counterparts.
        """
        alphas = torch.linspace(0, 1, m + 2)[1:-1]
        cyclic_loss = 0.0
        
        for alpha in alphas:
            z_interp = (1 - alpha) * z_a + alpha * z_b
            
            # Decode
            ldr_recon = self.decoder(z_interp)
            
            # Re-encode
            ldr_recon_ldr = self.hdr_to_ldr(self.ldr_to_hdr(ldr_recon)) # Identity transform for loss calc
            z_re_est = self.encoder(ldr_recon_ldr)
            
            cyclic_loss += torch.norm(z_interp - z_re_est) ** 2
            
        return cyclic_loss / m

    def train_step(self, batch):
        """
        Single training step.
        """
        self.optimizer.zero_grad()
        
        # Sample two latent vectors from the mini-batch
        # In practice, we encode the batch first to get z_a and z_b
        # For this pseudocode, assume z_a and z_b are sampled from the encoded batch
        # z_a, z_b = sample_latents(batch_encoded_z)
        
        # Placeholder for sampling logic
        z_a = torch.randn(1, 128)
        z_b = torch.randn(1, 128)
        
        # Reconstruction Loss (MALE)
        # Assuming we have ground truth hdr_env_map and reconstructed hdr_recon
        # Lr = |log(1 + x*) - log(1 + x_hat)|
        # This is computed on the whole batch or specific samples.
        # For simplicity, we compute it on the main forward pass of the batch.
        # (Detailed batch reconstruction loss calculation omitted for brevity, follows MALE formula)
        loss_r = 0.0 # Placeholder for batch reconstruction loss
        
        # Adversarial Loss (LSGAN)
        # D evaluates interpolated reconstructions
        # La = standard LSGAN loss
        loss_a = 0.0 # Placeholder for adversarial loss
        
        # Cyclic Loss
        loss_c = self.compute_cyclic_loss(z_a, z_b, self.m)
        
        # Smoothness Loss
        loss_s = self.compute_smoothness_loss(z_a, z_b, self.m)
        
        # Total Loss
        loss = loss_r + self.lambda_a * loss_a + self.lambda_c * loss_c + self.lambda_s * loss_s
        
        loss.backward()
        self.optimizer.step()
        
        return loss.item()
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training requires 4 million steps. On a single NVIDIA 1080 TI GPU, this takes approximately 5 days [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The latent space is 128-dimensional, which is significantly smaller than the $128 \times 256 \times 3$ input resolution, reducing memory requirements for storing lighting parameters during optimization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Provides a strong prior for ill-posed inverse problems (reflectance decomposition) [S1, unofficial].
    *   The smoothness of the manifold enables stable gradient-based optimization [S1, unofficial].
    *   Handles HDR data effectively via specific HDR/LDR transformations and MALE loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires pre-training on a dataset of high-quality environment maps (320 maps augmented) [S1, unofficial].
    *   The problem of jointly estimating 3D shape, materials, and lighting is highly underconstrained; the SMAE acts as a necessary regularizer but does not solve the ill-posedness alone [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When optimizing illumination parameters in inverse rendering or reflectance decomposition tasks where direct optimization is unstable or prone to implausible solutions.
*   **When not to use:** When no prior distribution of lighting is available or when real-time inference of lighting from a single image without pre-trained priors is required.

## Background
Light-SMAE was introduced in the paper "Neural-PIL: Neural Pre-Integrated Lighting for Reflectance Decomposition" [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the concept of Interpolating Auto-Encoders [5] and is designed to learn low-dimensional smooth manifolds for BRDFs and illuminations. The method was developed to address the ill-posedness of jointly estimating 3D shape, materials, and lighting. The Light-SMAE specifically targets environment maps, mapping them to a 128-dimensional latent space, while a companion BRDF-SMAE maps Cook-Torrance BRDF parameters to a 4D latent space [S1, unofficial]. The networks are pre-trained on datasets of real-world BRDFs [10] and environment maps [61], with the Light-SMAE trained on 320 environment maps augmented by rotations and blending [S1, unofficial].
