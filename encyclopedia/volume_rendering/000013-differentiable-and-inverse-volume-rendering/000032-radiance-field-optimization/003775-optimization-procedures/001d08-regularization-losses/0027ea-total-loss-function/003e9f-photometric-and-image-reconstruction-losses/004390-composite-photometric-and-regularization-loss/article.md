# Composite Photometric and Regularization Loss

Also called: Combined Loss Function, L_recon + λ_TV L_TV + λ_smooth L_smooth, Model Optimization with Loss Functions, Optimization Loss, Optimization Loss Functions, Training Losses, Training Objective and Regularization

## Overview
This method defines the composite objective function used to optimize neural scene representations (such as radiance fields, SDFs, or Gaussian splats) by balancing photometric fidelity against geometric and structural regularization terms. It solves the ill-posed nature of inverse rendering by preventing overfitting to noise and encouraging physically plausible scene structures (e.g., surface-aligned densities, smooth deformations, or compact primitives). It differs from simple photometric losses by explicitly incorporating terms for opacity concentration, distortion, normal consistency, and scale compactness, building on the principles of [Photometric and Image Reconstruction Losses](9789-photometric-and-image-reconstruction-losses.md).

## How it works
The total loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a weighted sum of a photometric reconstruction term $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ and one or more regularization terms $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$. The specific components depend on the scene representation (voxels, Gaussians, SDFs) and the application (static, dynamic, inverse rendering).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Rendered image $\mathbf{I}_r$ (or $\tilde{I}_P$, $\hat{I}$) generated by the differentiable renderer.
    *   Ground-truth image $\mathbf{I}_{gt}$ (or $I_P$, $I$).
    *   Scene parameters: densities $\sigma$, colors $C$, positions, scales $s$, opacities $\alpha$, SDF values $S$, or SH coefficients.
    *   Optional: Saturation masks $M_i$, camera projections $\Pi_i$, canonical space grids.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$.
    *   Gradients $\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}$ with respect to scene parameters $\theta$.

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Set $\mathcal{R}$:** A batch of sampled rays $r$.
*   **Voxel Grid $\mathcal{V}$:** 3D grid storing opacity $\sigma$ and color features (e.g., SH coefficients).
*   **Gaussian Set $\mathcal{G}$:** List of primitives, each with position, scale $s_{i,j}$, opacity $\alpha_i$, and rotation.
*   **SDF Field $S$:** Implicit function mapping 3D coordinates to signed distance.

### Formulas and Steps
The general structure is:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}, k}
$$

#### 1. Photometric Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$)
The base term measures the difference between rendered and ground-truth images. Variants include:

*   **L1/L2 Reconstruction:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \| \mathbf{I}_r(r) - \mathbf{I}_{gt}(r) \|_2^2 \quad \text{or} \quad \| \cdot \|_1
    $$
    For HDR/voxel methods with saturation masking [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} M_i(r) \| I_i(\Pi_i(r)) - \mathcal{T}(\hat{C}(r)) \|_2^2
    $$
    where $M_i(r)$ is a saturation mask, $\Pi_i$ is camera projection, and $\mathcal{T}$ is tone mapping.

*   **Multi-Grid Photometric Loss [S2]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \| \mathbf{C}_{\text{Inp}}(r) - \mathbf{C}_{\text{gt}}(r) \|_2^2 + \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \| \mathbf{C}_{\text{Up}}(r) - \mathbf{C}_{\text{gt}}(r) \|_2^2
    $$
    where $\mathbf{C}_{\text{Inp}}$ is rendered from an inpainted grid and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{Up}}$ from an up-sampled grid.

*   **Perceptual Loss [S4]:**
    $$
    L_{\text{rgb}} = \| \tilde{I}_P - I_P \|_2^2 + \| F_{\text{vgg}}(\tilde{I}_P) - F_{\text{vgg}}(I_P) \|_2^2
    $$
    where $F_{\text{vgg}}$ is a VGG feature extractor.

*   **Pyramid/SSIM Loss [S6]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = L_2(\text{pyramid}(\hat{I}), \text{pyramid}(I)) + (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}, I))
    $$

*   **Point-wise Supervision [S2]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}}
    $$
    Directly supervises the color of sampled points, weighted by their contribution to the ray [S2].

#### 2. Regularization Terms
These terms enforce geometric priors. Common variants include:

*   **Total Variation (TV) Loss [S1, S2]:**
    Encourages spatial smoothness of density or features.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{TV}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}, d \in [D]} \sqrt{\Delta_x^2(v,d) + \Delta_y^2(v,d) + \Delta_z^2(v,d) + \epsilon}
    $$
    where $\Delta$ is the difference between successive voxels along axes. Applied to opacity $\sigma$ and SH coefficients separately [S1].

*   **Opacity/Background Entropy Loss [S2]:**
    Encourages densities to concentrate on foreground or background (binary opacity).
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}}
    $$

*   **Distortion Loss [S3]:**
    Penalizes incorrect depth ordering along rays [S3].

*   **Normal Consistency Loss [S3]:**
    Ensures rendered normals are consistent with geometry [S3].

*   **Size/Compactness Regularization [S3, S7]:**
    *   **Triangle Size [S3]:** Encourages larger triangles in triangle splatting.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_s = 2 \| (v_1 - v_0) \times (v_2 - v_0) \|^{-1}
        $$
        where $v_i$ are vertices sampled from the unit sphere scaled by distance $d$.
    *   **Gaussian Volume [S7]:** Penalizes oversized Gaussians.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{vol}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|} \sum_i \prod_{j=1}^3 s_{i,j}
        $$
    *   **Gaussian Opacity [S7]:** Drives redundant Gaussians below prune threshold.
        $$
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{opa}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|} \sum_i \alpha_i
        $$

*   **Eikonal Loss [S6]:**
    Enforces SDF validity (gradient magnitude = 1).
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \| \nabla_x S - 1 \|_2^2
    $$

*   **Flow/Deformation Regularization [S2]:**
    Encourages static points in canonical space.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} = \| V^{\text{flow}}_t \|_1
    $$

*   **Smoothness Loss (CRF) [S1]:**
    Ensures Camera Response Function (CRF) smoothness.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} = \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{e \in [0,1]} g_i''(e)^2
    $$

*   **Scaling Regularization [S5, unofficial]:**
    Prevents Gaussians from becoming long and thin.
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{scaling}}(i) = \begin{cases} |\hat{s}_i|, & \hat{s}_i > \max(T_s, T_r \tilde{s}_i) \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
    $$
    where $\hat{s}_i$ is max scaling, $\tilde{s}_i$ is min scaling, $T_s$ is length threshold, $T_r$ is thinness ratio.

*   **Inpainting Difference Loss [S2]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{vdiff}} = \| V^{\text{R}_{\text{Inp}}}_t - V^{\text{R}_{\text{Up}}}_t \|_1
    $$

#### 3. Composite Loss Examples
*   **HDR-Plenoxels [S1]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \lambda_{\text{TV}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{TV}} + \lambda_{\text{smooth}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}
    $$
*   **Triangle Splatting [S3]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} + \beta_1\mathcal{L}_0 + \beta_2\mathcal{L}_d + \beta_3\mathcal{L}_n + \beta_4\mathcal{L}_s
    $$
*   **DART-3DGS [S7]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda_D)\mathcal{L}_1 + \lambda_D \mathcal{L}_{\text{D-SSIM}} + \lambda_s \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|} \sum_i \prod_{j=1}^3 s_{i,j} + \lambda_o \frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|} \sum_i \alpha_i
    $$
*   **Dynamic Voxel [S2]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_1\mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}} + w_2\mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}} + w_3\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} + w_4\mathcal{L}_{\text{vdiff}} + w_5\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(\sigma_{\text{Can}}) + w_6\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(\text{flow}_t) + w_7\mathcal{L}_{\text{tv}}(D)
    $$
*   **IRON [S6]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = L_2(\text{pyramid}(\hat{I}), \text{pyramid}(I)) + (1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}, I)) + \lambda_1 \| \nabla_x S - 1 \|_2^2 + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{roughness}}
    $$

### Parameters
*   **$\lambda_{\text{TV}}$:** TV weight. $\lambda_{\text{TV}, \sigma} = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $\lambda_{\text{TV}, \text{SH}} = 1 \cdot 10^{-2}$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{smooth}}$:** Smoothness weight. $1 \cdot 10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **$\lambda_D$:** D-SSIM weight. Default $0.2$ [S7].
*   **$\lambda_s, \lambda_o$:** Compactness weights. Default $0.01$ [S7].
*   **$\lambda, \beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \beta_4$:** Weights for LD-SSIM, opacity, distortion, normal, and size terms [S3].
*   **$w_1 \dots w_7$:** Balancing weights for dynamic voxel components [S2].
*   **$T_s, T_r$:** Scaling thresholds [S5, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Saturation:** In LDR/HDR pipelines, failing to mask saturated pixels ($M_i(r)$) leads to incorrect gradients [S1].
*   **Degenerate Primitives:** Without size/compactness regularization, Gaussians or triangles may grow infinitely large or thin, causing artifacts [S3][S5, unofficial][S7].
*   **SDF Validity:** Without Eikonal loss, SDF gradients may not represent true distances, leading to poor normal estimation [S6].
*   **Dynamic Consistency:** In dynamic scenes, lack of flow regularization leads to flickering or inconsistent motion [S2].

## Subtopics

### [MPF Training Objective](10247-mpf-training-objective.md)
The MPF Training Objective is a composite loss function designed to optimize the parameters of a Metric-Phase Field (MPF) network for multi-view image reconstruction. It combines standard photometric supervision with geometric regularizers that exploit rendering weights as soft indicators for near-surface and free-space regions, addressing the lack of ground-truth 3D geometry in this setting. Unlike the original MPF formulation which relies on explicit point cloud supervision, this method adapts the metric-phase decomposition constraints to be implicit and differentiable through the rendering process.

### Scaling and Background Mixing Regularization
This variant augments the photometric loss with a scaling regularization term to prevent Gaussians from growing long and thin, which is critical in monocular settings with limited viewing angles [S5, unofficial]. It also employs background mixing, where a random background color is generated every iteration to mix with the rendered image and ground truth, providing silhouette cues [S5, unofficial]. The scaling loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{scaling}}$ penalizes the max scaling $\hat{s}_i$ only when it exceeds a length threshold $T_s$ and is thin relative to the min scaling $\tilde{s}_i$ by a ratio $T_r$ [S5, unofficial].

### Inverse Rendering Loss Function
Used to optimize neural SDF and material parameters (diffuse albedo, specular albedo, roughness) from multi-view photometric images, this loss combines image fidelity, structural similarity, SDF validity, and material constraints [S6]. It includes an Eikonal loss to enforce SDF gradient magnitude of 1 and a roughness range loss to keep estimated roughness below 0.5 [S6]. It also uses a Gaussian pyramid L2 loss and SSIM loss for image reconstruction [S6].

### Compactness-Regularized Objective
This objective augments the standard 3DGS photometric loss with two lightweight compactness terms: a volume term (product of scale axes) and an opacity term (L1 on opacity) [S7]. The volume term replaces the L1 penalty on scale axes used in 3D-GS-MCMC, penalizing oversized primitives more sharply than their sum [S7]. The opacity term drives redundant Gaussians below the prune threshold, producing a more compact set [S7].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of rays $|\mathcal{R}|$, voxels $|\mathcal{V}|$, or Gaussians $|\mathcal{G}|$. Regularization terms like TV and compactness add overhead proportional to the number of primitives/voxels.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing gradients for all scene parameters. Regularization terms may require temporary buffers for differences or derivatives.
*   **Strengths:** Improves reconstruction quality, reduces artifacts (flickering, noise), enforces geometric priors, and allows training with fewer views or noisy data.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters ($\lambda$ weights). Over-regularization can lead to oversmoothing or loss of fine details.
*   **When to Use:** When photometric loss alone leads to overfitting, artifacts, or physically implausible results. Essential for dynamic scenes, SDFs, and compact Gaussian representations.

## Background
Composite loss functions are fundamental to differentiable rendering and inverse graphics. The concept of combining photometric and regularization terms has evolved from early NeRF works [S3] to specialized methods for dynamic scenes [S2], HDR [S1], SDFs [S6], and Gaussian Splatting [S7]. The specific variants listed above represent adaptations to different scene representations and constraints.
