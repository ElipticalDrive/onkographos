# Total Loss Function

Also called: Combined Loss, Combined Loss Function, Full Training Objective, Total Loss

## Overview
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective function used to optimize differentiable volume rendering and radiance field models end-to-end. It aggregates multiple loss terms—such as photometric reconstruction, geometric consistency, and regularization constraints—to guide the learning of scene representations [S1, S2, S3][S4, unofficial][S6, S8, S9, S10]. It differs from sibling methods by serving as the final summation of configured components, where specific implementations vary based on the underlying representation (e.g., Gaussian Splatting vs. Neural Radiance Fields) and the training stage [10621-overall-training-loss.md]. It builds on [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md) by combining them with primary reconstruction errors [9789-photometric-and-image-reconstruction-losses.md].

## How it works
The total loss is generally computed as a weighted sum of individual loss components. The specific components and weights depend on the radiance field architecture and the desired properties (e.g., surface smoothness, temporal consistency, distractor removal).

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Inputs**: Rendered images $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground truth images $\mathbf{I}^*$, auxiliary outputs (depth, normals, throughput, features), scene parameters (densities, colors, SDFs, Gaussian attributes), and hyperparameters (weights $\lambda, w, \alpha, \beta$).
- **Outputs**: A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ used for gradient-based optimization.

### Data Structures
The loss function itself does not define a persistent data structure but operates on tensors produced by the rendering forward pass. Key tensors include:
- $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}$: Rendered RGB image.
- $\mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*$: Ground truth RGB image.
- $\mathbf{z}$: Latent encoding (if applicable).
- $\mathbf{V}_\alpha$: Volume opacity field.
- $\mathbf{I}_\alpha$: Background image.
- $T_i$: Throughput (transmittance) for sample $i$.
- $m_i$: Binary mask for sample $i$.
- $\nabla f(\mathbf{x})$: Gradient of the SDF field.

### Formulas and Steps
The general form is:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_k w_k \mathcal{L}_k
$$
where $\mathcal{L}_k$ are individual loss terms and $w_k$ are their respective weights.

#### 1. Photometric Reconstruction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}}$)
Minimizes the difference between rendered and ground truth colors.
- **L1 Loss** [S4, unofficial]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |C_i - \hat{C}_i|
  $$
  where $N$ is the number of batch rays, $C_i$ is ground truth color, and $\hat{C}_i$ is rendered color.
- **L2 + SSIM** [S1]: Used for raw quad reconstruction ($\mathcal{L}_q$) and optional color reconstruction ($\mathcal{L}_c$).
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Loss** [S2]:
  $$
  \ell_{\text{rec}} = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*(\mathbf{p}) \right\|^2
  $$
  where $P$ is the number of pixels.

#### 2. Regularization Losses
- **Total Variation (TV) Loss** [S2]: Enforces spatial smoothness on volume opacity $\mathbf{V}_\alpha$.
  $$
  J_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_\alpha)
  $$
- **Beta Prior on Exit Opacity** [S2]:
  $$
  J_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{I}_\alpha)
  $$
- **Eikonal Loss** [S3][S4, unofficial]: Regularizes the SDF field gradient magnitude to 1.
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{i,j=1}^{MN} \|\nabla f(p_{i,j}) - 1\|^2
  $$
  where $M$ is the number of sampled locations on each ray.
- **Curvature Loss** [S3]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{curv}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_i)|
  $$
- **KL Divergence** [S2]: Between latent encoding $\mathbf{z}$ and standard normal distribution.
  $$
  D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1))
  $$

#### 3. Specific Architectural Losses
- **Optical Flow Loss** [S1]: For dynamic scenes.
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_f
  $$
- **Contrastive Loss** [S9]: For generalizable NeRFs.
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{contrast}}
  $$
- **Auxiliary Per-Point RGB Loss** [S6]: Supervises color emission of each query point directly.
- **Background Entropy Loss** [S6]: Enforces background probability concentration.

#### 4. Composite Examples
- **Neural Volumes** [S2]:
  $$
  \ell(\theta) = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*(\mathbf{p}) \right\|^2 + \lambda_{\text{KL}} D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1)) + J_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_\alpha) + J_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{I}_\alpha)
  $$
- **Neuralangelo** [S3]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}} + w_{\text{eik}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} + w_{\text{curv}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{curv}}
  $$
- **Point-NeuS** [S4, unofficial]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L} = (\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + 0.1\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}}) + (\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{\text{usdf}} + \lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{\text{bias}} + \lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{\text{pc}})
  $$
- **Dynamic Gaussian Splatting** [S1]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_q + \mathcal{L}_c + \beta \mathcal{L}_f
  $$
- **ContraNeRF** [S9]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{contrast}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{color}}
  $$
- **DeSplat** [S10]:
  $$
  L = L_{\text{GS}} + \lambda_d \alpha_d + \lambda_{\text{bg}} L_{\text{bg}}
  $$
- **DBARF (Curriculum)** [S8]:
  $$
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}}
  $$

#### 5. Auxiliary AOV Losses (Empty Space & Opacity) [S5, unofficial]
- **Empty Space Loss**: Encourages throughput $T_i$ to be 0 where mask $m_i=0$.
  $$
  L_{\text{empty}} = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|
  $$
- **Opacity Loss**: Encourages throughput $T_i$ to be 1 where mask $m_i=1$.
  $$
  L_{\text{opacity}} = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|
  $$

#### 6. Background Blending [S7, unofficial]
For accurate loss computation when the background is not black, the predicted RGB image is blended with the ground truth background color before computing the loss. This is typically handled by a renderer method `blend_background_for_loss_computation`.

### Parameters and Typical Values
- **$\lambda_{\text{KL}}$**: 0.001 [S2].
- **$\lambda_{\text{tv}}$**: 0.01 [S2].
- **$\lambda_{\text{B}}$**: 0.1 [S2].
- **$\alpha$**: 5 for real-world scenes, 1 for synthetic scenes [S1].
- **$\beta$**: 0.0008 [S1]; $-1e5$ for curriculum schedule [S8].
- **$w_{\text{eik}}$**: 0.1 [S4, unofficial]; varies in Neuralangelo [S3].
- **$w_{\text{curv}}$**: Varies [S3].
- **$\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$**: 1, 1, 0.25 respectively [S4, unofficial].
- **$\lambda_d$**: 0.01 [S10].
- **$\lambda_{\text{bg}}$**: 0.15 [S10]; 0 for appearance-only ablation [S10].
- **$\lambda_s$**: 0.01 for RobustNeRF/On-the-go/Mip-NeRF-360; 0 for Photo Tourism [S10].
- **Batch Size**: 16 [S2].
- **Learning Rate**: $10^{-4}$ for encoder-decoder weights [S2]; $10^{-1}$ for background images [S2]; $10^{-3}$ for per-camera gain/bias [S2].
- **Optimizer**: Adam [S2][S4, unofficial][S10].
- **Training Duration**: ~500,000 iterations [S2]; 60,000 iterations [S1]; 30,000 iterations [S10].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Memory Usage**: Randomly sampling pixels (e.g., 128x128) reduces memory usage during raymarching [S2].
- **Initialization**: Warm-up stages (e.g., 2,000 iterations assuming static scene) help place Gaussians [S1].
- **Background Handling**: If the background is not black, standard RGB loss may be inaccurate without blending [S7, unofficial].
- **Local Locality**: Hash-encoded SDFs suffer from locality problems; numerical gradients or Eikonal loss help distribute updates [9954-numerical-gradient-eikonal-loss.md].

### Empty Space Loss
An AOV loss that encourages rendering throughput to equal 0.0 in regions where a binary object mask is inactive [S5, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|$. Parameters: $w=1.0$ (default), $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ [S5, unofficial].

### Opacity Loss
An AOV loss that encourages rendering throughput to equal 1.0 in regions where a binary object mask is active [S5, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|$. Parameters: $w=1.0$ (default), $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ [S5, unofficial].

### Auxiliary Losses
Includes per-point RGB loss and background entropy loss [S6]. Adding both yields PSNR 35.54, compared to 35.37 (none) or 35.53 (one loss) [S6].

### Background Blending for Loss Computation
Blends predicted RGB with ground truth background color for accurate loss computation when background is not black [S7, unofficial].

### Curriculum Learning Loss
Transitions from depth/photometric supervision to RGB supervision using an exponential schedule [S8]. Formula: $\mathcal {L}_{\text {final}} = 2^{\beta \cdot t} (\mathcal {L}_{\text {depth}} + \mathcal {L}_{\text {photo}}) + (1-2^{\beta \cdot t}) \mathcal {L}_{\text {rgb}}$ with $\beta = -1e5$ [S8].

### Softmax Weighted Volume Rendering
Accumulates colors along a ray weighted by densities after applying softmax to speed up convergence [S9]. Total loss is sum of contrastive and color losses [S9].

### DeSplat Loss Function
Combines standard Gaussian Splatting loss, distractor regularization, and background regularization [S10]. Formula: $L = L_{\text{GS}} + \lambda_d \alpha_d + \lambda_{\text{bg}} L_{\text{bg}}$. Parameters: $\lambda_d=0.01$, $\lambda_{\text{bg}}=0.15$ (or 0 for ablation) [S10].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Depends on the number of samples $N$ and rays. Training can take significant time (e.g., 10 days on a single V100 for 500k iterations [S2]).
- **Memory Complexity**: High memory usage due to storing intermediate states for reverse-mode AD [44-radiance-field-optimization.md]. Sampling strategies (e.g., 128x128 pixels [S2]) mitigate this.
- **Performance**: Varies by scene and method. PSNR improvements are seen with auxiliary losses [S6] and curriculum learning [S8].
- **Strengths**: Flexible combination of photometric, geometric, and regularization terms.
- **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of multiple hyperparameters (weights, learning rates).

## Background
Originated in the context of differentiable volume rendering and neural radiance fields (NeRF). Key developments include Neural Volumes [S2], Neuralangelo [S3], and various Gaussian Splatting optimizations [S1, S10]. It is used in novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, and dynamic scene modeling.
