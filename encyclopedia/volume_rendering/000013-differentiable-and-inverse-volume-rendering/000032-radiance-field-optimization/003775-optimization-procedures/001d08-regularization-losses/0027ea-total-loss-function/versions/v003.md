# Total Loss Function

Also called: Combined Loss, Full Training Objective, Loss function, Total Loss, Training loss

## Overview
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective used to optimize radiance field models (such as Neural Volumes, NeRFs, and Gaussian Splatting) by combining a primary photometric reconstruction error with one or more regularization terms [S1][S2, unofficial][S4][S6][S7]. While the parent article [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md) defines the general structure $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{photo} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg}^{(k)}$, the Total Loss Function article specifies the concrete instantiation of these terms, their weights, and the optimization context for specific radiance field variants. It differs from sibling methods like [Total Variation Regularization](860-total-variation-regularization.md) or [Efficient O(N) Distortion Loss](1051-efficient-o-n-distortion-loss.md) by serving as the aggregate container for all active loss components during a specific training run, rather than a single regularization term itself.

## How it works
The method computes the total loss $\mathcal{L}$ by summing a photometric term (typically L1 or MSE) and weighted regularization terms. The specific components and weights vary significantly depending on the underlying radiance field representation (e.g., neural volume, implicit surface, or Gaussian splatting).

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Rendered image/radiance $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$ or $\mathbf{I}_r$ [S1][S4].
    *   Ground truth image $\mathbf{I}^*$ or $\mathbf{I}_{gt}$ [S1][S4].
    *   Model parameters $\theta$ (network weights, voxel grid values, Gaussian attributes) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Latent encoding $\mathbf{z}$ (if applicable) [S1].
    *   Opacity/Volume field $\mathbf{V}_\alpha$ or SDF field $f$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Auxiliary variables: Throughput $T_i$, Binary mask $m_i$, Visibility map $V_M$ [S3, unofficial][S4].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}$ [S1][S2, unofficial][S4][S6][S7].
    *   Gradients $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$ for the optimizer [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The data structures depend on the specific variant. Common structures include:
*   **Images:** Tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ for RGB [S1][S4].
*   **Latents:** Vector $\mathbf{z}$ [S1].
*   **Fields:** Voxel grid $\mathbf{V}_\alpha$ or MLP parameters [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Masks/Visibility:** Binary or float tensor of shape $(H, W)$ or $(N,)$ for samples [S3, unofficial][S4].

### Formulas and Steps
The total loss is computed as follows. Note that different papers define different sets of regularization terms.

**Variant 1: Neural Volumes (S1)**
Combines pixel reconstruction, KL divergence for latent encoding, total variation on volume opacity, and beta prior on exit opacity.
$$ \ell(\theta) = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*(\mathbf{p}) \right\|^2 + \lambda_{\text{KL}} D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1)) + J_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_\alpha) + J_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{I}_\alpha) $$
*   $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}$: Rendered RGB image.
*   $\mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*$: Ground truth RGB image.
*   $P$: Number of pixels sampled.
*   $\mathbf{z}$: Latent encoding.
*   $D_{\text{KL}}$: KL divergence.
*   $J_{\text{tv}}$: Total variation prior on volume opacity $\mathbf{V}_\alpha$.
*   $J_{\text{B}}$: Beta prior on exit opacity $\mathbf{I}_\alpha$.

**Variant 2: Point-NeuS (S2, unofficial)**
Combines L1 RGB loss, Eikonal loss, and auxiliary losses for uncertain SDF, bias, and point cloud consistency.
$$ \mathcal{L} = (\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + 0.1\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}}) + (\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{\text{usdf}} + \lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{\text{bias}} + \lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{\text{pc}}) $$
Where:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |C_i - \hat{C}_i| $$
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{i,j=1}^{MN} \|\nabla f(p_{i,j}) - 1\|^2 $$
*   $C_i, \hat{C}_i$: Ground truth and rendered colors.
*   $N$: Number of batch rays.
*   $M$: Number of sampled locations on each ray.
*   $f$: SDF field.
*   $\lambda_1=1, \lambda_2=1, \lambda_3=0.25$ [S2, unofficial].

**Variant 3: Gaussian Splatting with Visibility (S4)**
Combines visibility-weighted L1/SSIM, perceptual loss, and regularization for sampling coordinates and visibility map.
$$ L_c = \lambda_1 L_1(V_M \odot I_r, V_M \odot I_{gt}) + \lambda_{\text{SSIM}} L_{\text{SSIM}}(V_M \odot I_r, V_M \odot I_{gt}) + \lambda_{\text{LPIPS}} L_{\text{LPIPS}}(I_r, I_{gt}) $$
$$ L = L_c + \lambda_{\text{sc}} L_{\text{sc}} + \lambda_{\text{vm}} L_{\text{vm}} $$
*   $I_r, I_{gt}$: Rendered and ground truth images.
*   $V_M$: Visibility map.
*   $\odot$: Hadamard product.
*   $\lambda_1=0.8, \lambda_{\text{SSIM}}=0.2, \lambda_{\text{LPIPS}}=0.005, \lambda_{\text{sc}}=0.001, \lambda_{\text{vm}}=0.15$ [S4].

**Variant 4: HybridNeRF (S6)**
Combines photometric loss, Eikonal loss, and interlevel proposal loss.
$$ \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r}) := \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}(\mathbf{r}) + \lambda_{\text{Eik}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Eik}}(\mathbf{r}) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}(\mathbf{r}) $$
*   $\lambda_{\text{Eik}} = 0.01$ [S6].

**Variant 5: Neural Deformable Voxel Grid (S7)**
Combines photometric loss with deformation-related regularizations (point cloud, background, deformation norm, deformation TV).
$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_{\text{ptc}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}} + w_{\text{bg}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}} + w_{d\_norm} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_norm} + w_{d\_tv} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_tv} $$
*   Coarse stage weights: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.1, w_{\text{bg}}=0.01, w_{d\_norm}=0.1, w_{d\_tv}=1$ [S7].
*   Fine stage weights: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.01, w_{\text{bg}}=0.001, w_{d\_norm}=0.01, w_{d\_tv}=1$ [S7].
*   For large motion scenes, $w_{d\_norm}$ is reduced to $0.01$ (coarse) and $0.001$ (fine) [S7].

### Pseudocode
The following pseudocode illustrates the generic structure of computing the total loss, incorporating elements from the cited variants. It assumes a modular design where specific loss components are implemented as functions.

```python
def compute_total_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image, visibility_map, 
                       latent_z, volume_opacity, sdf_field, samples, 
                       config):
    """
    Computes the total loss for radiance field optimization.
    
    Args:
        rendered_image: Tensor of rendered RGB values.
        ground_truth_image: Tensor of ground truth RGB values.
        visibility_map: Tensor of visibility weights (e.g., V_M).
        latent_z: Latent encoding vector (optional).
        volume_opacity: Volume opacity field (optional).
        sdf_field: SDF field function or values (optional).
        samples: Sampled points for regularization (optional).
        config: Dictionary containing loss weights and active terms.
        
    Returns:
        Scalar total loss.
    """
    total_loss = 0.0
    
    # 1. Photometric Loss
    # Supports L1, MSE, or Visibility-Weighted L1/SSIM/LPIPS
    if config.get('use_visibility_weighted'):
        # Variant 3 (S4)
        masked_rendered = rendered_image * visibility_map
        masked_gt = ground_truth_image * visibility_map
        l1_loss = config['lambda_1'] * l1_loss(masked_rendered, masked_gt)
        ssim_loss = config['lambda_SSIM'] * ssim_loss(masked_rendered, masked_gt)
        lpips_loss = config['lambda_LPIPS'] * lpips_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image)
        composite_loss = l1_loss + ssim_loss + lpips_loss
        total_loss += composite_loss
    else:
        # Standard L1 or MSE (Variant 1, 2, 5)
        if config.get('loss_type') == 'mse':
            total_loss += mse_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image)
        else:
            total_loss += l1_loss(rendered_image, ground_truth_image)

    # 2. Regularization Terms (Conditional on config and model type)
    
    # KL Divergence for Latent (Variant 1)
    if config.get('use_kl') and latent_z is not None:
        kl_loss = kl_divergence(latent_z, mean=0, std=1)
        total_loss += config['lambda_KL'] * kl_loss
        
    # Total Variation on Volume Opacity (Variant 1)
    if config.get('use_tv') and volume_opacity is not None:
        tv_loss = total_variation(volume_opacity)
        total_loss += config['lambda_tv'] * tv_loss
        
    # Beta Prior on Exit Opacity (Variant 1)
    if config.get('use_beta_prior') and volume_opacity is not None:
        beta_loss = beta_prior(volume_opacity)
        total_loss += config['lambda_B'] * beta_loss
        
    # Eikonal Loss for SDF (Variant 2, 6)
    if config.get('use_eikonal') and sdf_field is not None and samples is not None:
        # Compute gradient of SDF at sample points
        grad_sdf = compute_gradient(sdf_field, samples)
        eikonal_loss = mean_squared_error(grad_sdf.norm(dim=-1), 1.0)
        total_loss += config['lambda_eik'] * eikonal_loss
        
    # Auxiliary Losses (Variant 2, 5)
    if config.get('use_auxiliary'):
        # Uncertain SDF, Bias, Point Cloud consistency (Variant 2)
        if config.get('use_usdf'):
            total_loss += config['lambda_1'] * uncertain_sdf_loss()
        if config.get('use_bias'):
            total_loss += config['lambda_2'] * bias_loss()
        if config.get('use_pc'):
            total_loss += config['lambda_3'] * point_cloud_loss()
            
        # Per-point RGB, Background Entropy (Variant 5)
        # Note: Specific formulas for these are not fully detailed in sources, 
        # but they are added to the total loss.
        
    # Deformation Regularization (Variant 7)
    if config.get('use_deformation_reg'):
        total_loss += config['w_ptc'] * point_cloud_loss()
        total_loss += config['w_bg'] * background_loss()
        total_loss += config['w_d_norm'] * deformation_norm_loss()
        total_loss += config['w_d_tv'] * deformation_tv_loss()
        
    # Sampling Coordinate and Visibility Map Regularization (Variant 4)
    if config.get('use_sc_reg'):
        total_loss += config['lambda_sc'] * sampling_coord_reg()
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **$\lambda_{\text{KL}}$**: 0.001 [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{tv}}$**: 0.01 [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{B}}$**: 0.1 [S1].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{eik}}$**: 0.01 [S6]; 0.1 coefficient in front of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}}$ in Variant 2 [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$** (Variant 2): 1, 1, 0.25 [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\lambda_1$** (L1 weight, Variant 4): 0.8 [S4].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{SSIM}}$** (Variant 4): 0.2 [S4].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{LPIPS}}$** (Variant 4): 0.005 [S4].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{sc}}$** (Sampling coord reg, Variant 4): 0.001 [S4].
*   **$\lambda_{\text{vm}}$** (Visibility map reg, Variant 4): 0.15 [S4].
*   **$w_{\text{ptc}}, w_{\text{bg}}, w_{d\_norm}, w_{d\_tv}$** (Variant 7 Coarse): 0.1, 0.01, 0.1, 1 [S7].
*   **$w_{\text{ptc}}, w_{\text{bg}}, w_{d\_norm}, w_{d\_tv}$** (Variant 7 Fine): 0.01, 0.001, 0.01, 1 [S7].
*   **Learning Rate**: $10^{-4}$ for encoder-decoder weights [S1]; separate rates for background images ($10^{-1}$) and gain/bias ($10^{-3}$) [S1].

*   **Optimizer**: Adam [S1][S2, unofficial][S4][S5].

*   **Training Iterations**: ~500,000 [S1]; 20,000 for dynamic scenes (S7) [S7].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Memory Usage**: Large batch sizes or high-resolution images can exceed GPU memory. Randomly sampling pixels (e.g., 128x128) is recommended [S1].
*   **Precision**: Step sizes in raymarching should be computed in normalized voxel space $[-1, 1]^3$ to ensure proper saturation at initialization [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input**: Empty regions in the scene should be handled by auxiliary losses like Empty Space Loss (see Subtopics) to prevent artifacts [S3, unofficial].
*   **Large Motion**: In dynamic scenes with large motion, deformation norm regularization weights ($w_{d\_norm}$) should be reduced to avoid over-constraining the deformation [S7].

## Subtopics

### Empty Space Loss
An Arbitrary Output Variable (AOV) loss that encourages rendering throughput to be zero in regions where a binary object mask is inactive [S3, unofficial]. It is computed by retrieving the 'throughput' AOV, converting the binary mask to a float tensor, and calculating the mean absolute difference between throughput and zero, weighted by $(1 - \text{mask})$ and a scalar weight $w$ [S3, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|$ [S3, unofficial]. Typical weight $w=1.0$ [S3, unofficial].

### Opacity Loss
An AOV loss that encourages rendering throughput to be one in regions where a binary object mask is active [S3, unofficial]. It is computed by calculating the mean absolute difference between throughput and one, weighted by the mask $m_i$ and a scalar weight $w$ [S3, unofficial]. Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|$ [S3, unofficial]. Typical weight $w=1.0$ [S3, unofficial].

### Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting
A specific formulation of the total loss used in Gaussian Splatting training that combines L1, SSIM, and LPIPS losses, where L1 and SSIM are weighted by a visibility map $V_M$ [S4]. It also includes regularization terms for sampling coordinates ($L_{sc}$) and visibility map ($L_{vm}$) [S4]. This variant is chosen when high-fidelity perceptual quality and explicit visibility handling are required [S4].

### Auxiliary Losses
Additional loss terms added to the total loss to supervise specific aspects of the rendering, such as per-point RGB emission and background entropy [S5]. Adding both auxiliary losses can improve PSNR (e.g., from 35.37 to 35.54) [S5].

### HybridNeRF Optimization Loss
A total loss formulation for HybridNeRF that combines photometric loss, Eikonal loss, and an interlevel proposal loss [S6]. It is used to train the proposal network efficiently [S6].

### Loss Function Configuration
A configuration of the total loss for dynamic view synthesis using neural deformable voxel grids, which includes photometric loss and four deformation-related regularizations (point cloud, background, deformation norm, deformation TV) [S7]. It uses different weights for coarse and fine training stages, and adjusts deformation norm weights for scenes with large motion [S7].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Depends on the number of rays/samples $N$, batch size, and number of iterations. Training can take significant time (e.g., 10 days on a single V100 for 500k iterations [S1]; 20k iterations for dynamic scenes [S7]).
*   **Memory Complexity**: Scales with batch size and image resolution. Random sampling (128x128) is used to reduce memory usage [S1].
*   **Performance**: The choice of loss components directly impacts reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM) and training stability. For example, adding auxiliary losses improves PSNR [S5].
*   **Strengths**: Flexible framework that can incorporate various regularization terms to address specific artifacts (e.g., floaters, noise, deformation errors).
*   **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of multiple hyperparameters ($\lambda$ weights). Some terms (like KL divergence or perceptual loss) add computational overhead.
*   **When to Use**: When optimizing radiance fields and needing to balance photometric accuracy with geometric or perceptual constraints.
*   **When Not to Use**: When computational resources are extremely limited and only basic photometric loss is feasible, or when the specific regularization terms are not applicable to the scene (e.g., no deformation).

## Background
The concept of combining multiple loss terms for radiance field optimization originated with early neural rendering works. Neural Volumes [S1] introduced a comprehensive training objective including KL divergence and volume priors. Subsequent works like Point-NeuS [S2, unofficial], HybridNeRF [S6], and Gaussian Splatting variants [S4][S7] have adapted and extended this framework to include Eikonal losses, perceptual losses, and deformation regularizations, reflecting the evolving needs of different radiance field representations.
