# Total Loss Function

Also called: Combined Loss, Full Training Objective, Loss function, Total Loss, Training loss

## Overview
The Total Loss Function is the composite objective used to optimize radiance field parameters (such as density, color, SDF, or latent codes) by aggregating multiple regularization and reconstruction terms. It solves the ill-posed nature of inverse volume rendering by balancing photometric fidelity with geometric and structural priers. It sets itself apart from sibling losses by serving as the aggregation mechanism $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ that combines the primary data term $\mathcal{L}_{data}$ with weighted regularization terms $\lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg, k}$, as defined in the parent [Regularization losses](1413-regularization-losses.md).

## How it works
The total loss is computed as a weighted sum of distinct components, each targeting a specific property of the radiance field. The specific components and weights vary by implementation, but the general structure follows the parent's shared principles.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Rendered images $\hat{\mathbf{I}}$, ground truth images $\mathbf{I}^*$, latent codes $\mathbf{z}$, SDF values $f$, gradients $\nabla f$, throughput $T$, binary masks $m$, and model parameters $\theta$.
*   **Outputs:** A scalar loss value $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ used for backpropagation.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Batch Rays:** A set of $N$ rays sampled from training views.
*   **Latent Codes:** Vector $\mathbf{z}$ encoding view-dependent or scene-specific information.
*   **SDF/Opacity Fields:** Discrete or continuous representations of geometry.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Compute Photometric Loss:** Calculate the difference between rendered and ground truth colors. Common metrics include L1 or L2 distance [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Regularization Terms:** Calculate specific penalty terms based on the method's requirements (e.g., KL divergence for latents, Total Variation for smoothness, Eikonal for SDF gradients) [S1][S2, S7, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Auxiliary/Specific Losses:** Add any method-specific losses such as depth consistency, background entropy, or proposal losses [S4, S5, S6].
4.  **Aggregate:** Sum the terms with their respective hyperparameter weights $\lambda_k$.

**Formulas:**
The general form is:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \sum_k \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{reg, k}
$$

Specific instances found in sources:

1.  **Neural Volumes [S1]:**
    $$
    \ell(\theta) = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}(\mathbf{p}) - \mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*(\mathbf{p}) \right\|^2 + \lambda_{\text{KL}} D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{z} \parallel \mathcal{N}(0, 1)) + J_{\text{tv}}(\mathbf{V}_\alpha) + J_{\text{B}}(\mathbf{I}_\alpha)
    $$
    Where $\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{rgb}}$ is the rendered image, $\mathbf{I}_{\text{rgb}}^*$ is ground truth, $\mathbf{z}$ is the latent encoding, $D_{\text{KL}}$ is KL divergence, $J_{\text{tv}}$ is the total variation prior on volume opacity, and $J_{\text{B}}$ is the beta prior on exit opacity.

2.  **Point-NeuS [S2, unofficial]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = (\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} + 0.1\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}}) + (\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{\text{usdf}} + \lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{\text{bias}} + \lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{\text{pc}})
    $$
    Where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rgb}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |C_i - \hat{C}_i|$ is the L1 color loss, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{eik}} = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{i,j=1}^{MN} \|\nabla f(p_{i,j}) - 1\|^2$ is the Eikonal loss.

3.  **HybridNeRF [S5]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}(\mathbf{r}) + \lambda_{\text{Eik}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Eik}}(\mathbf{r}) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}(\mathbf{r})
    $$

4.  **Neural Deformable Voxel Grid [S6]:**
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_{\text{ptc}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}} + w_{\text{bg}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}} + w_{d\_norm} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_norm} + w_{d\_tv} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_tv}
    $$

5.  **GSplat Bilateral Grid [S7, unofficial]:**
    Total variation loss for a tensor $x$ of shape $(B, C, \dots)$:
    $$
    L_{TV} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=2}^{D} \frac{\sum_{j} (x_{..., j+1, ...} - x_{..., j, ...})^2}{N_i}
    $$
    Where $B$ is batch size, $D$ is number of dimensions, and $N_i$ is the number of elements in dimension $i$ excluding batch and channel.

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   $\lambda_{\text{KL}}$: 0.001 [S1].
*   $\lambda_{\text{tv}}$: 0.01 [S1].
*   $\lambda_{\text{B}}$: 0.1 [S1].
*   $\lambda_1$ (uncertain SDF): 1 [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_2$ (bias): 1 [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_3$ (point cloud): 0.25 [S2, unofficial].
*   $\lambda_{\text{Eik}}$: 0.01 [S5].
*   Coarse stage weights [S6]: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.1$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.01$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.1$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$.
*   Fine stage weights [S6]: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.01$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.001$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.01$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$.
*   Learning Rate: $10^{-4}$ for encoder-decoder weights [S1].
*   Batch Size: 16 [S1].
*   Sampling: 128x128 pixels randomly sampled [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Memory Usage:** Full-image photometric loss can be memory-intensive; random pixel sampling (e.g., 128x128) is used to mitigate this [S1].
*   **Step Size:** Raymarching step sizes should be computed in normalized voxel space $[-1, 1]^3$ to ensure the end of the ray is the expected saturation point at initialization [S1].
*   **Large Motion:** For scenes with large motion, deformation norm weights ($w_{d\_norm}$) should be reduced (e.g., to 0.01 coarse, 0.001 fine) to prevent over-regularization [S6].

## Subtopics

### [Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting](4262-composite-image-loss-with-visibility-weighting.md)
The Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting ($L_c$) is a composite objective function used to train radiance field models, specifically SuRFNet, by aligning rendered images with ground-truth references while suppressing errors in occluded or unobserved regions. It sets itself apart from standard photometric losses by explicitly masking the L1 and SSIM terms with a visibility map $V_M$, ensuring that supervision is only applied to pixels corresponding to visible surfaces, while retaining a full-image perceptual loss.

### [Sparse Depth Consistency Loss](7294-sparse-depth-consistency-loss.md)
The Sparse Depth Consistency Loss enforces geometric consistency between the rendered depth maps of a radiance field and sparse depth estimates provided by a Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline. It serves as a regularization term within the total loss function to mitigate floaters and undesired depth discontinuities common in sparse-input settings. It is distinguished from sibling losses like Composite Image Loss with Visibility Weighting by focusing specifically on depth metric alignment rather than photometric color fidelity or visibility-weighted compositing.

### Empty Space Loss
An AOV loss that encourages the rendering throughput to equal 0.0 in regions where a binary object mask is inactive [S3, unofficial].
1. Retrieve the 'throughput' AOV from the dictionary. If missing, return 0.0.
2. Convert the binary mask to a float tensor.
3. Compute the absolute difference between throughput and 0.0.
4. Multiply the difference by $(1.0 - \text{mask})$.
5. Compute the mean over all pixels/samples.
6. Scale by weight.
Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (1 - m_i) \cdot |T_i - 0|$, where $w$ is the weight, $N$ is the number of samples, $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the binary mask value, and $T_i$ is the throughput value [S3, unofficial].
Parameters: `masks` (binary mask array/tensor, values 0 or 1), `weight` (float scalar, default 1.0) [S3, unofficial].

### Opacity Loss
An AOV (Arbitrary Output Variable) loss that encourages the rendering throughput to equal 1.0 in regions where a binary object mask is active [S3, unofficial].
Formula: $L = w \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \cdot |T_i - 1|$, where $w$ is the weight, $N$ is the number of samples, $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the binary mask value, and $T_i$ is the throughput value [S3, unofficial].
Parameters: `masks` (binary mask array/tensor, values 0 or 1), `weight` (float scalar, default 1.0) [S3, unofficial].

### Auxiliary Losses
Adds per-point RGB loss to supervise the color emission of each query point directly, rather than the accumulated color, and background entropy loss to enforce the rendered background probability to concentrate on the background or foreground [S4]. Adding both auxiliary losses yields PSNR 35.54, compared to 35.37 (none) or 35.53 (one loss) [S4].

### HybridNeRF Optimization Loss
The total loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r})$ is the sum of photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$, Eikonal loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Eik}}$ weighted by $\lambda_{\text{Eik}}$, and interlevel proposal loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}$ [S5]. Parameter: $\lambda_{\text{Eik}} = 0.01$ [S5].

### Loss Function Configuration
Overall loss: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} + w_{\text{ptc}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{ptc}} + w_{\text{bg}} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{bg}} + w_{d\_norm} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_norm} + w_{d\_tv} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{d\_tv}$ [S6].
Parameters: Coarse stage weights: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.1$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.01$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.1$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$. Fine stage weights: $w_{\text{ptc}}=0.01$, $w_{\text{bg}}=0.001$, $w_{d\_norm}=0.01$, $w_{d\_tv}=1$. For large motion scenes ('Hell Warrior', 'Jumping Jacks', 'T-Rex'), $w_{d\_norm}$ is set to 0.01 (coarse) and 0.001 (fine) [S6].

### Total Variation Loss
A regularization loss that computes the total variation of the bilateral grid parameters to encourage smoothness in the learned color transformations [S7, unofficial].
Steps: Iterate over all bilateral grids in the model. For each grid, compute the total variation loss using `total_variation_loss` and multiply by `config.bilgrid_tv_loss_mult`. Sum these losses. Compute total variation for a tensor `x` of shape `(B, C, ...)`. For each spatial dimension `i` from 2 to `len(x.shape)`, compute the squared difference between adjacent elements along that dimension. Normalize by the number of elements in the tensor excluding the batch and channel dimensions. Sum over all spatial dimensions and divide by batch size [S7, unofficial].
Formula: The total variation loss for a tensor $x$ is $L_{TV} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=2}^{D} \frac{\sum_{j} (x_{..., j+1, ...} - x_{..., j, ...})^2}{N_i}$, where $B$ is batch size, $D$ is number of dimensions, and $N_i$ is the number of elements in dimension $i$ excluding batch and channel [S7, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training duration varies significantly by scene and method. For example, Neural Volumes training takes about 500,000 iterations, which takes 10 days on a single NVIDIA Tesla V100 [S1]. Direct Voxel Grid Optimization achieves convergence in minutes (e.g., 7.3 minutes for specific configurations) [S4].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is managed by sampling subsets of pixels (e.g., 128x128) rather than full images [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Combines multiple constraints to stabilize training and improve geometry/color fidelity.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of multiple hyperparameters ($\lambda_k$). Incorrect weights can lead to over-smoothing or poor convergence.
*   **When to use:** When optimizing radiance fields for novel view synthesis or reconstruction where both photometric accuracy and geometric consistency are required.
*   **When not to use:** For simple forward rendering without optimization, or when computational resources are extremely limited and only a single loss term is feasible.

## Background
The concept of a total loss function in differentiable volume rendering emerged with the advent of neural radiance fields and neural volumes. Key early works include Neural Volumes [S1] (2019), which introduced a comprehensive training objective combining photometric, latent, and prior losses. Subsequent works like Point-NeuS [S2, unofficial] (2023), HybridNeRF [S5] (2024), and Neural Deformable Voxel Grid [S6] (2022) have refined these objectives to include SDF regularization, deformation constraints, and efficient optimization strategies. The specific auxiliary losses (Empty Space, Opacity) and bilateral grid TV losses are found in modern rendering libraries like GSplat [S7, unofficial] and TLIR [S3, unofficial].
