# NeuS

Also called: Neural Implicit Surface, NeuS

## Overview
NeuS is a differentiable volume rendering method that reconstructs a 3D scene's geometry and appearance by optimizing a neural implicit surface defined by a Signed Distance Function (SDF). It solves the problem of recovering high-fidelity surfaces from multi-view images by replacing the standard opacity-based volume rendering of NeRF with a softplus-based SDF rendering, enabling the use of surface-based regularization [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its sibling [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) by modeling geometry as an SDF rather than a density field, and from [IDR](https://github.com/lioryariv/idr) by using differentiable volume rendering for training stability and higher quality [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Multi-view RGB images with known camera poses.
**Outputs:** A neural implicit surface (SDF) and a color field, allowing for novel view synthesis and mesh extraction.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **SDF MLP:** 8 hidden layers, hidden size 256. Activation: Softplus($\beta=100$). Skip connection from input to the output of the 4th layer.
2.  **Color MLP:** 4 hidden layers, hidden size 256. Inputs: spatial location $p$, view direction $v$, normal vector $n = \nabla f(p)$, and a 256-dimensional feature vector from the SDF MLP.
3.  **Positional Encoding:** Applied to $p$ (6 frequencies) and $v$ (4 frequencies).
4.  **Sampling Points:** Section points $q_i$ and mid-points $p_i$ along the ray.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Network Initialization:** Initialize SDF and Color MLPs. Use weight normalization to stabilize training [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Sampling:**
    *   Sample 64 points uniformly along the ray [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform importance sampling for $k=4$ iterations. In each iteration $i$, add 16 points. Total points: 128 [S1, unofficial].
    *   For the 'w/o mask' setting, sample an additional 32 points outside the bounding sphere to represent the background using a NeRF++ model [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Probability Estimation:** Compute coarse probability estimation in the $i$-th importance sampling iteration using a fixed softplus sharpness parameter $s = 32 \times 2^i$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Alpha and Color Computation:**
    *   For each segment defined by section points $q_i$ and $q_{i+1}$, compute the alpha value $\alpha_i$:
        $$ \alpha_i = \max\left(\frac{\Phi_s(f(q_i)) - \Phi_s(f(q_{i+1}))}{\Phi_s(f(q_i))}, 0\right) $$
        where $\Phi_s$ is the cumulative distribution function of the softplus density [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the color $c_i$ using the color predicted at the mid-point $p_i = o + \frac{t_i + t_{i+1}}{2} v$ [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Rendering:** Composite the color and alpha values along the ray to produce the final pixel color.
6.  **Optimization:** Train the networks using the ADAM optimizer for 300k iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spectral Bias Artifacts:** Random initialization produces axis-aligned artifacts due to the spectral bias of positional encoding. Geometric initialization avoids this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Textureless Regions:** Textureless regions are ambiguous for reconstruction and may fail to be correctly reconstructed (e.g., metal surfaces) [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Rendering a 1600x1200 image takes approximately 320 seconds on an Nvidia 2080Ti GPU in the default volume rendering setting [S1, unofficial].
*   **Optimization:** With sphere tracing (sampling only near surfaces), rendering time drops to ~60 seconds on the same hardware [S1, unofficial].
*   **Convergence:** The error of the local maximum position $\Delta t$ converges to zero faster than the naive solution. Specifically, $\Delta t = O(s^{-2})$ for NeuS versus $\Delta t = O(s^{-1})$ for the naive solution, as the standard deviation $1/s$ of the S-density approaches 0 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality surface reconstruction, differentiable rendering allows for gradient-based optimization of the SDF.
*   **Limitations:** Slow rendering speed in default settings; struggles with textureless or ambiguous regions [S1, unofficial].

## Background
NeuS was introduced by Yen-Chen Lin et al. in the paper "NeuS: Learning Neural Implicit Surfaces by Volume Rendering for Multi-view Reconstruction" [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the NeRF framework but adapts it for implicit surface representation. The method draws architectural inspiration from IDR (Implicit Deformation Reconstruction) [S1, unofficial]. It is used for multi-view reconstruction tasks, particularly on datasets like DTU and BlendedMVS [S1, unofficial].
