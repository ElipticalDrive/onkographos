# Direct Voxel Grid Optimization

Also called: DVG, Direct Voxel Density Optimization, Direct Voxel Grid Optimization Implementation, DirectVoxGo (DVGO)

## Overview
Direct Voxel Grid Optimization (DVGO) is a method for reconstructing per-scene radiance fields from calibrated images by directly optimizing a dense 3D voxel grid for volume density and a feature voxel grid for color, achieving convergence in less than 15 minutes on a single GPU [S1, S4]. It sets itself apart from implicit MLP-based methods like NeRF by eliminating the need for lengthy optimization of continuous functions, and from previous voxel-based methods by training the voxel grid directly from scratch without requiring a conversion step from a pre-trained implicit model [S1, S4]. Unlike its sibling [Voxel Grid Optimization](12241-voxel-grid-optimization.md), which may rely on conversion or simpler representations, DVGO introduces post-activation interpolation and specific priors to enable sharp surface modeling and robust convergence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $N$ calibrated images with known camera poses (intrinsic and extrinsic parameters). The coordinates of camera centers are normalized to the range $[-1, 1]$ [S3].
*   **Outputs:** A rendered novel view image $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for any ray $\mathbf{r}$, and the optimized voxel grids.

**Data Structures:**
The representation consists of two primary voxel grids and a shallow network:
1.  **Density Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{dens}}$):** Stores raw density values $\sigma$.
    *   **Coarse Stage:** Resolution $M(c) = 100^3$ voxels [S1, S4].
    *   **Fine Stage:** Resolution $M(f) = 160^3$ (default) or $256^3$ voxels [S1, S4].
    *   **Layout:** Dense 3D array. Values are queried via trilinear interpolation.
2.  **Feature Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{feat}}$):** Stores feature vectors for the shallow MLP.
    *   **Resolution:** Same as the fine density grid ($M(f)$) [S1].
    *   **Channels:** $D = 12$ channels [S1].
    *   **Layout:** Dense 3D array. Values are queried via trilinear interpolation.
3.  **Color Voxel Grid ($V_{\text{color}}$):** Used only in the coarse stage for view-invariant color.
    *   **Resolution:** Same as coarse density grid ($M(c)$) [S3].
    *   **Channels:** 3 (RGB) [S3].
    *   **Layout:** Dense 3D array. Values are queried via trilinear interpolation.
4.  **Shallow MLP:**
    *   **Architecture:** 2 hidden layers, 128 channels per layer [S4].
    *   **Input:** Concatenation of positional encoding of 3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$.
    *   **Output:** 3-channel color vector $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].

**Positional Encoding:**
*   Position $\mathbf{x}$: $k_x = 5$ frequencies [S1].
*   Direction $\mathbf{d}$: $k_d = 4$ frequencies [S1].

**Bounding Box (BBox):**
The method automatically finds a BBox tightly enclosing the volume of interest to allocate the voxel grids, ensuring parsimony [S1, S4].

**Optimization Phases:**
The optimization is conducted in two phases: Coarse Geometry Searching and Fine Detail Reconstruction [S3].

**Phase 1: Coarse Geometry Searching**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize $V_{\text{dens}}$ to yield opacities very close to zero everywhere to avoid bias toward camera near planes. The initial activated alpha value is $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(c)} = 10^{-6}$ [S1, S4].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points along rays with step size $\delta^{(c)} = 0.5 \times s^{(c)}$, where $s^{(c)}$ is the voxel size of the coarse grid [S4].
3.  **Free Space Handling:** Define known free space using threshold $\tau_c = 10^{-3}$. Skip query points in this space [S1].
4.  **Query:**
    *   Query density $\sigma$ from $V_{\text{dens}}$.
    *   Query color $\mathbf{c}$ from $V_{\text{color}}$ (view-invariant) [S3].
5.  **Rendering:** Compute rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using volume rendering quadrature [S1].
6.  **Loss:** Minimize photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ [S4].
7.  **Update:** Optimize $V_{\text{dens}}$ and $V_{\text{color}}$ for 10,000 iterations using Adam optimizer with base learning rate 0.1 [S1, S4].
8.  **Priors:**
    *   **Low-Density Initialization:** Essential to prevent suboptimal "cloud" allocation in free space [S1].
    *   **View-Count Learning Rate:** Apply a lower learning rate to voxels visible to fewer views to avoid redundant voxels allocated just for explaining observations from a small number of views [S1, S4].

**Phase 2: Fine Detail Reconstruction**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize fine density grid $V_{\text{dens}}^{(f)}$ with $\alpha_{\text{init}}^{(f)} = 0.01$ [S1, S4].
2.  **Sampling:** Sample points along rays with step size $\delta^{(f)} = 0.5 \times s^{(f)}$, where $s^{(f)}$ is the voxel size of the fine grid [S4].
3.  **Unknown Space Handling:** Skip low-density query points in unknown space using threshold $\tau_f = 10^{-4}$ [S1].
4.  **Query:**
    *   Query density $\sigma$ from $V_{\text{dens}}^{(f)}$.
    *   Query features $\mathbf{f}$ from $V_{\text{feat}}$ [S1].
5.  **Color Prediction:** Pass $\mathbf{f}$ and positional encodings of $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ through the shallow MLP to get view-dependent color $\mathbf{c}$ [S1].
6.  **Rendering:** Compute rendered color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ using volume rendering quadrature [S1].
7.  **Loss:** Minimize photometric loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}}$ [S4].
8.  **Update:** Optimize $V_{\text{dens}}^{(f)}$, $V_{\text{feat}}$, and MLP parameters for 20,000 iterations [S1, S4].
    *   **Learning Rates:** Base learning rate for voxel grids is 0.1; for MLP is $10^{-3}$ [S4].
    *   **Schedule:** Exponential learning rate decay, downscaled by 0.1 at 20,000 iterations [S1].
    *   **Progressive Scaling:** The fine-stage voxel grid is progressively scaled at checkpoints 1000, 2000, and 3000 [S1].

**Post-Activation Interpolation:**
A key technique for sharp surface modeling. Instead of interpolating activated opacity or using nearest-neighbor, DVGO trilinearly interpolates the raw density values from the grid, then applies the activation function (e.g., Softplus/ReLU) to produce opacity [S1, S4]. This allows modeling sharp linear surfaces within a single grid cell [S1].

**Volume Rendering Formula:**
For a ray $\mathbf{r}$, $K$ points are sampled between near and far planes. The rendered color is:
$$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \left( \sum_{i=1}^K T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i \right) + T_{K+1} \mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}} $$
where:
*   $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity at point $i$ [S1, S4].
*   $T_i = \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j)$ is the accumulated transmittance [S1, S4].
*   $\sigma_i$ is the density at point $i$ [S1, S4].
*   $\delta_i$ is the distance to the adjacent sampled point [S1, S4].
*   $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color at point $i$ [S1, S4].
*   $\mathbf{c}_{\text{bg}}$ is the background color [S1, S4].

**Loss Function:**
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left\| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \right\|_2^2 $$
where $\mathcal{R}$ is the set of rays in a sampled mini-batch, $\hat{C}$ is rendered color, and $C$ is observed pixel color [S4].
Additional loss weights: $w_{\text{photo}} = 1$, $w_{\text{pt\_rgb}} = 10^{-1}$ (coarse) / $10^{-2}$ (fine), $w_{\text{bg}} = 10^{-2}$ (coarse) / $10^{-3}$ (fine) [S1].

def volume_render(rays, density_grid, feature_grid, color_grid, mlp, near, far, K):
    """
    Render a batch of rays using volume rendering.
    rays: numpy array of shape (B, 3) for origins and (B, 3) for directions
    density_grid: numpy array of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz)
    feature_grid: numpy array of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz, D)
    color_grid: numpy array of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz, 3)
    mlp: function mapping (pos_enc, dir_enc, features) -> color
    near, far: float, near and far plane distances
    K: int, number of samples per ray
    Returns: rendered colors (B, 3), alphas (B, K), transmittances (B, K)
    """
    # Sample points along rays
    t_vals = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, K)
    z_vals = near * (1.0 - t_vals) + far * t_vals
    ray_points = rays[:, :3, np.newaxis, :] + rays[:, 3:, np.newaxis, :] * z_vals[:, :, np.newaxis]
    
    # Query densities
    densities = trilinear_interpolate(density_grid, ray_points)
    
    # Compute opacities
    # delta is distance between adjacent points
    deltas = z_vals[:, 1:] - z_vals[:, :-1]
    deltas = np.concatenate([deltas, np.array([1e10])], axis=1) # Handle last delta
    alphas = 1.0 - np.exp(-densities * deltas)
    
    # Compute transmittances
    transmittances = np.cumprod(1.0 - alphas, axis=1)
    transmittances = np.concatenate([np.ones_like(transmittances[:, :1]), transmittances[:, :-1]], axis=1)
    
    # Query colors
    if feature_grid is not None:
        features = trilinear_interpolate(feature_grid, ray_points)
        pos_enc = positional_encoding(ray_points, 5)
        dir_enc = positional_encoding(rays[:, 3:, np.newaxis, :], 4)
        colors = mlp(pos_enc, dir_enc, features)
    else:
        colors = trilinear_interpolate(color_grid, ray_points)
        
    # Accumulate colors
    weights = alphas * transmittances
    rendered_colors = np.sum(weights[:, :, np.newaxis] * colors, axis=1)
    
    return rendered_colors, alphas, transmittances

## Subtopics

### [Ref-DVGO](6572-ref-dvgo.md)
Ref-DVGO is a reflection-aware neural rendering method that combines the fast hybrid implicit-explicit representation of Direct Voxel Grid Optimization (DVGO) with the reflection direction reparameterization of Ref-NeRF to achieve high-quality photorealistic novel view synthesis of reflective scenes with reduced training and rendering time. It sets itself apart from DVGO by explicitly modeling specular reflections via a reflection-aware reparameterization and from Ref-NeRF by replacing large spatial MLPs with six distinct voxel grids, significantly reducing GPU memory requirements and training time.

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Training Time:** Approximately 15 minutes to train from scratch for a new scene on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti GPU for $M(f)=160^3$ voxels [S1, S4]. For $M(f)=256^3$, it takes approximately 22 minutes [S1]. This is about two orders of magnitude faster than NeRF (10-20 hours) [S1].
*   **Rendering Speed:** Approximately 45x faster than NeRF (0.64 seconds vs 29 seconds per 800x800 image) [S1].
*   **Memory:** The method uses dense voxel grids. The maximum resolution mentioned is 15.6 million voxels (2.5 million per dimension) [S3]. The default fine grid is $160^3$ or $256^3$ [S1, S4].

**Measured Performance:**
*   On Syn-NeRF, Syn-NSVF, BlendedMVS, and Tanks and Temples datasets, DVGO achieves PSNR of 31.95 (160^3) and 32.80 (256^3) [S1].
*   This compares favorably to NeRF (31.01 PSNR, 1-2 days training) [S1].
*   It outperforms MVSNeRF (28.14 PSNR) without requiring cross-scene pre-training [S1].
*   It achieves comparable PSNR to NeuRay-Hi (32.42) in significantly less time (22 mins vs 23 hours) [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Extremely fast convergence; no need for cross-scene pre-training; sharp surface modeling via post-activation interpolation; efficient rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Prone to suboptimal geometry solutions (cloud in free space) if priors are not applied; real-world data uncertainty (lighting, SfM error) can lead to blurrier surfaces, lessening the advantage of post-activation [S1]; not designed for unbounded or forward-facing scenes [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   Use when fast per-scene training is required (minutes vs hours/days).
*   Use for inward-facing scenes with calibrated images [S1, S4].
*   Do not use if cross-scene generalization is needed without retraining, or if the scene is unbounded/forward-facing [S1].

## Background
Direct Voxel Grid Optimization (DVGO) was introduced by Cheng Sun, Min Sun, and Hwann-Tzong Chen in 2022 [S1, S4]. It was presented at CVPR 2022 [S1, S4]. The method addresses the bottleneck of lengthy training times in NeRF and its variants by directly optimizing a voxel grid representation from scratch, avoiding the conversion step required by previous voxel-based methods [S1, S4]. It builds on the idea of explicit volumetric representations but introduces novel techniques for fast convergence and sharp surface modeling [S1].
