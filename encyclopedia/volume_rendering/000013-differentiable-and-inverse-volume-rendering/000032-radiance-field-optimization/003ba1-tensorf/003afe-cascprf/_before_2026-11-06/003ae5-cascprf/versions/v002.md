# CasCPRF

Also called: Cascaded Tensorial Radiance Fields, IRCasTRF

## Overview
CasCPRF is a multi-scale scene representation method that uses a cascaded structure of tensor decompositions to control frequency bands, separating low spatial/high angular frequency content (coarse scale) from high spatial/low angular frequency content (fine scale) for compact reconstruction [S1]. It solves the problem of spectral bias in coordinate-based neural networks by enabling the expansion of high-order radiance field feature volumes across multiple modes and hierarchical levels, facilitating superior reconstruction quality compared to single-scale counterparts within the same model size budget [S1]. It differs from its siblings by explicitly decoupling frequency bands via a binary-tree cascaded tensor structure and integrating a two-stage process: an exploration stage for novel view synthesis and an exploitation stage for physics-based inverse rendering of mesh topology, materials, and lighting [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view images, initial mesh (optional, for exploitation stage), camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered images (exploration), refined mesh with materials, textures, and lighting parameters (exploitation).

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Cascaded Feature Volumes ($\mathcal{V}_\sigma, \mathcal{V}_c$):**
    *   Represented as a sum of rank-one vectors across $D$ cascaded levels.
    *   For level $d$ and rank $r$, the structure consists of vectors $\mathbf{v}_{rx,d}, \mathbf{v}_{ry,d}, \mathbf{v}_{rz,d}$.
    *   The number of rows (rank) for mode $n$ at level $d$ is $I_{n,d} = I_n / 2^{d-1}$, where $I_n$ is the base dimension.
    *   Total dimensions are adaptive, starting at $125^3$ and upsampled to max $250^3$ [S1].
2.  **Color Rendering Network ($MLP_c$):**
    *   Two-layer MLP.
    *   Hidden size: 128 nodes per layer.
    *   Activation: ReLU [S1].
3.  **Environment Map ($M$):**
    *   Tensor of shape $R^{6 \times 512 \times 512}$, representing 6 mip levels of an environment cube map [S1].
4.  **Mesh Parameters (Exploitation Stage):**
    *   Global movement vector $\Delta_m \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Per-vertex deformation vectors $\Delta v_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Material parameters: Diffuse term $b_d$, roughness $r$, metalness $m$.
    *   2D Texture map with fixed vertex-to-texture indices [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize the cascaded structure in a binary-tree style. Set the number of rows for rank-one vectors in mode $n$ and cascaded level $d$ as $I_{n,d} = I_n / 2^{d-1}$ [S1].
    *   Initialize environment map $M$ randomly as 0.5 [S1].
    *   Initialize global movement vector $\Delta_m$ randomly within $[-0.3, 0.3]$ [S1].

2.  **Exploration Stage (Novel View Synthesis):**
    1.  **Sampling:** Sample $N_s$ points per ray along the camera's viewing direction to cast the radiance field feature volume [S1].
    2.  **Grid Sampling:** Grid sample the radiance field feature volume $\mathcal{V}$ to produce density features $F_\sigma$ and color features $F_c$ for each spatial point $x$ [S1].
        $$ \mathcal{V}_\sigma(x) \rightarrow F_\sigma, \quad \mathcal{V}_c(x) \rightarrow F_c $$
    3.  **Feature Factorization:** The volumes are factorized as sums of rank-one vectors across $D$ levels:
        $$ \mathcal{V}_\sigma = \sum_{d=1}^{D} \sum_{r=1}^{R} (\mathbf{v}_{rx,d} \times \mathbf{v}_{ry,d} \times \mathbf{v}_{rz,d}) $$
        $$ \mathcal{V}_c = \sum_{d=1}^{D} \sum_{r=1}^{R_c} (\mathbf{v}_{rx,d} \times \mathbf{v}_{ry,d} \times \mathbf{v}_{rz,d}) $$
        *(Note: Source uses $R$ and $R_c$ for density and color ranks respectively, though notation in Eq 5/6 implies similar structure)* [S1].
    4.  **Radiance Calculation:**
        *   Compute density: $\sigma = \text{ReLU}(F_\sigma)$ [S1].
        *   Compute viewing direction $\omega_o$.
        *   Apply Sinusoidal Positional Encoding (PE) to $\omega_o$:
            $$ \text{PE}(x) = [\sin(2^0 \cdot x), \cos(2^0 \cdot x), \dots, \sin(2^{N_q-1} \cdot x), \cos(2^{N_q-1} \cdot x)] $$
            where $N_q$ is the number of frequency bands [S1].
        *   Compute color: $\mathbf{c} = \text{MLP}_c(F_c, \text{PE}(\omega_o))$ [S1].
    5.  **Volume Rendering:** Render radiance fields into color prediction $C$ using accumulated transmittance $T_i$:
        $$ C(r) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_s} T_i \alpha_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right), \quad \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_j) $$
        where $\delta_j$ is the distance between adjacent sampled points [S1].
    6.  **Loss Calculation:** Minimize Mean-Square Error (MSE) between rendered color $C$ and ground truth $\hat{C}$, plus Total Variation (TV) loss on vectors $\mathbf{v}$ to enforce smoother surfaces:
        $$ L_{explor} = \|C - \hat{C}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{tv} L_{tv}(\mathbf{v}) $$
    7.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using Adam optimizer.
    8.  **Mesh Extraction:** Extract preliminary mesh $M$ from the density volume using the Marching Cubes (MC) algorithm for geometry initialization [S1].

3.  **Exploitation Stage (Physics-based Shading):**
    1.  **Global Alignment (Warm-up):** Learn global movement vector $\Delta_m$ for the first 150 iterations to ensure accurate positioning. $\Delta_m$ is disabled after warm-up [S1].
    2.  **Topology Finetune:** Enable per-vertex deformation vectors $\Delta v_i$ to alter topology. These are derived from gradients [S1].
    3.  **Material & Texturing:**
        *   Learn Disney material parameters: diffuse $b_d$, roughness $r$, metalness $m$.
        *   Constraints: $b_d \in [0, 1]$, $r \ge 0.08$ [S1].
        *   Specular term $b_s$ calculated as: $b_s = (1 - m) \cdot 0.04 + m \cdot b_d$ [S1].
        *   Texture: Use learnable 2D texture parameters initialized from input mesh in Blender with fixed vertex indices [S1].
    4.  **Lighting:**
        *   Use differentiable split-sum approximation for image-based lighting with environment cube map $M$ [S1].
        *   Coarser mip levels are constructed from the base level using a pre-filtering approach to increase roughness [S1].
        *   Light Regularizer $L_{light}$: Penalizes color shifts to assume white lighting:
            $$ L_{light} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i=0}^{3} | \bar{C}_i - \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i=0}^{3} \bar{C}_i | $$
            *(Note: Source formula notation is ambiguous regarding $\bar{C}_i$, likely referring to channel averages or similar)* [S1].
    5.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute $L_1$ norm on tone-mapped colors $\Gamma(\cdot)$ for image reconstruction. Add light and material regularizers [S1].
    6.  **Optimization:** Update parameters using Adam optimizer.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spectral Bias:** The method addresses the difficulty MLPs have in learning high-frequency contents due to rapid eigenvalue spectrum decay in the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) [S1].
*   **Topology Noise:** The preliminary mesh extracted via Marching Cubes may contain high-frequency noise, which is addressed by the topology finetune stage [S1].
*   **Convergence Speed:** Volumetric texture learning can converge slowly and produce overly-smoothed results; CasCPRF avoids this by using fixed 2D texture indices from an input mesh [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Exploration stage takes ~25 minutes for 30k iterations on a single A100 GPU. Exploitation stage takes ~1 hour for 5k iterations on a single A100 GPU [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses a cascaded tensor structure which allows for compact representation. Base grid size starts at $125^3$ and can be upsampled to $250^3$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces superior reconstruction quality compared to single-scale counterparts with the same model size budget. Separates low spatial/high angular frequency (coarse) from high spatial/low angular frequency (fine) content [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires an initial mesh for the exploitation stage (or generation via MC). The exploitation stage is computationally intensive (~1 hour) [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-fidelity novel view synthesis and physics-based inverse rendering (materials, lighting) are required, and computational resources (GPU memory/time) are available for the two-stage process [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** For real-time applications or when only novel view synthesis is needed without material/lighting refinement, simpler NeRF variants may be more efficient [S1].

## Background
CasCPRF (Cascaded Tensorial Radiance Fields), also known as IRCasTRF, was introduced by Wenpeng Xing, Jie Chen, Ka Chun Cheung, and Simon See in 2023 [S1]. It builds upon the success of NeRF's high-frequency Fourier features to overcome spectral bias, proposing a novel multi-scale scene representation using a cascaded structure with disentangled frequency bands [S1]. The method integrates concepts from tensor decomposition, inverse rendering, and physics-based shading, drawing inspiration from works like BACON (band-limited coordinate networks) and PhySG (physics-based shading) [S1]. It was presented at the ACM Multimedia Conference (MM '23) [S1].
