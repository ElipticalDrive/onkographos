# TensoRF

Also called: TensoRF (Tensorial Radiance Field Representation), TensoRF Representation, Tensorial Radiance Field Representation, Tensorial Radiance Fields

## Overview
TensoRF models a radiance field mapping 3D location $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $c$ using factorized tensors for a regular 3D grid [S1][S3, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Neural Radiance Fields](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) by replacing the neural network with a compact tensor decomposition (CP or VM), achieving significantly faster reconstruction times and smaller model sizes while maintaining or exceeding rendering quality [S1][S3, unofficial]. It differs from [Plenoxels](5942-plenoxels.md) by using explicit tensor factorization with global appearance dictionaries rather than fixed spherical harmonics bases, allowing for more flexible and compact representation of view-dependent effects [S1][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs**: Multi-view images with known camera poses ($c2w$), intrinsics ($K$), and near/far bounds.
**Outputs**: Optimized tensor factors for geometry and appearance grids.

**Data Structures**:
The radiance field is split into two grids:
1.  **Geometry Grid** $G_\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K}$: Single-channel, representing volume density directly.
2.  **Appearance Grid** $G_c \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K \times P}$: Multi-channel, with $P$ appearance feature channels.

These grids are factorized using Vector-Matrix (VM) decomposition (or CP decomposition).
*   **Geometry Factors**: For each component $r \in \{1, \dots, R_\sigma\}$:
    *   Vectors: $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^X \in \mathbb{R}^I$, $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Y \in \mathbb{R}^J$, $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Z \in \mathbb{R}^K$.
    *   Matrices: $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{YZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XY} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J}$.
*   **Appearance Factors**: For each component $r \in \{1, \dots, R_c\}$:
    *   Vectors: $\mathbf{v}_{c,r}^X \in \mathbb{R}^I$, $\mathbf{v}_{c,r}^Y \in \mathbb{R}^J$, $\mathbf{v}_{c,r}^Z \in \mathbb{R}^K$.
    *   Matrices: $\mathbf{M}_{c,r}^{YZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{c,r}^{XZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{c,r}^{XY} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J}$.
    *   Feature Vectors: $\mathbf{b}_r \in \mathbb{R}^P$ for $r \in \{1, \dots, 3R_c\}$.
*   **Global Appearance Dictionary**: Matrix $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times 3R_c}$ formed by stacking all $\mathbf{b}_r$ vectors as columns.

**Parameters**:
*   $R_\sigma, R_c$: Number of components for geometry and appearance. Typically $R_\sigma \ll I, J, K$ and $R_c \ll I, J, K$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   $P$: Number of appearance feature channels. Typical value: 27 (corresponding to 3rd-order SH coefficients with RGB channels) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   $Q$: Number of shading points sampled along a ray [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   $N_0$: Initial low-resolution grid dimension for coarse-to-fine reconstruction. Typical value: 128 [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   Optimizer: Adam [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate for tensor factors: 0.02 [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Learning rate for MLP decoder (if used): 0.001 [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   Batch size: 4096 pixel rays [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Method Steps**:

1.  **Coarse-to-Fine Initialization**: Start with a grid resolution $N_0^3$ where $N_0=128$. Initialize tensor factors randomly or via upsampling from a lower resolution.
2.  **Ray Sampling**: Select random pixels from training images. Compute ray origins and directions. Sample $Q$ points along the ray within near/far bounds.
3.  **Feature Evaluation (Trilinear Interpolation)**:
    For a query point $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$, compute the interpolated geometry and appearance features efficiently using the linearity of interpolation on the factors [S1][S3, unofficial].
    
    *Geometry Density*:
    $$ \mathcal{G}_\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{r=1}^{R_\sigma} \sum_{m \in \{X,Y,Z\}} \mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^m(\mathbf{x}) $$
    where $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^X(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^X(x) \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{YZ}(y, z)$, $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^Y(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Y(y) \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XZ}(x, z)$, and $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^Z(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Z(z) \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XY}(x, y)$. Here, $\mathbf{v}(t)$ denotes linear interpolation of the vector at coordinate $t$, and $\mathbf{M}(t_1, t_2)$ denotes bilinear interpolation of the matrix at coordinates $t_1, t_2$ [S1][S3, unofficial].

    *Appearance Features*:
    $$ \mathcal{G}_c(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{B} \left( \bigoplus_{r=1}^{R_c} \left[ \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^X(\mathbf{x}), \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^Y(\mathbf{x}), \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^Z(\mathbf{x}) \right] \right) $$
    where $\bigoplus$ concatenates the scalar interpolated values into a $3R_c$-dimensional vector, and $\mathbf{B}$ is the global appearance dictionary [S1][S3, unofficial].

4.  **Color Prediction**:
    Compute density $\sigma = \mathcal{G}_\sigma(\mathbf{x})$.
    Compute color $c = S(\mathcal{G}_c(\mathbf{x}), \mathbf{d})$, where $S$ is a decoding function.
    *   Option A: Spherical Harmonics (SH) with $P=27$ features [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Option B: Small MLP with two fully connected layers (128 hidden units, ReLU activation) [S1][S3, unofficial].

5.  **Volume Rendering**:
    Compute pixel color $C$ using differentiable volume rendering [S1][S3, unofficial]:
    $$ C = \sum_{q=1}^Q \tau_q (1 - \exp(-\sigma_q \Delta_q)) c_q $$
    $$ \tau_q = \exp\left(-\sum_{p=1}^{q-1} \sigma_p \Delta_p\right) $$
    where $\sigma_q, c_q$ are density and color at sample $q$, and $\Delta_q$ is the step size.

6.  **Loss Computation**:
    Compute MSE loss $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \| \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r}) \|_2^2$.
    Add regularization terms to prevent overfitting and local minima:
    *   L1 norm loss on vector and matrix factors [S1][S3, unofficial].
    *   Total Variation (TV) loss on vector and matrix factors (recommended for datasets with few images or imperfect capture, e.g., LLFF, Tanks and Temples) [S1][S3, unofficial].

7.  **Backpropagation and Update**:
    Update tensor factors using Adam optimizer.
    *   LR for factors: 0.02.
    *   LR for MLP decoder: 0.001 [S1][S3, unofficial].

8.  **Coarse-to-Fine Upsampling**:
    At steps 2000, 3000, 4000, 5500, 7000, upsample the vectors and matrices linearly and bilinearly. The voxel count interpolates between $N_0^3$ and $N^3$ linearly in logarithmic space [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class TensoRF:
    def __init__(self, I, J, K, P, R_sigma, R_c, use_sh=True):
        """
        I, J, K: Grid dimensions
        P: Appearance feature channels (27 for SH)
        R_sigma: Number of geometry components
        R_c: Number of appearance components
        use_sh: If True, use Spherical Harmonics for color decoding. 
                If False, use MLP.
        """
        self.I, self.J, self.K = I, J, K
        self.P = P
        self.R_sigma = R_sigma
        self.R_c = R_c
        
        # Geometry Factors (VM Decomposition)
        # Vectors: (R_sigma, I), (R_sigma, J), (R_sigma, K)
        self.v_sigma_X = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, I))
        self.v_sigma_Y = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, J))
        self.v_sigma_Z = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, K))
        # Matrices: (R_sigma, J, K), (R_sigma, I, K), (R_sigma, I, J)
        self.M_sigma_YZ = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, J, K))
        self.M_sigma_XZ = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, I, K))
        self.M_sigma_XY = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_sigma, I, J))
        
        # Appearance Factors (VM Decomposition)
        # Vectors: (R_c, I), (R_c, J), (R_c, K)
        self.v_c_X = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, I))
        self.v_c_Y = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, J))
        self.v_c_Z = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, K))
        # Matrices: (R_c, J, K), (R_c, I, K), (R_c, I, J)
        self.M_c_YZ = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, J, K))
        self.M_c_XZ = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, I, K))
        self.M_c_XY = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(R_c, I, J))
        
        # Global Appearance Dictionary B: (P, 3*R_c)
        self.B = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(P, 3 * R_c))
        
        # Decoder
        if use_sh:
            self.decoder = None # SH is applied directly in forward pass
            self.use_sh = True
        else:
            # MLP: 2 FC layers, 128 hidden, ReLU
            self.decoder = nn.Sequential(
                nn.Linear(P, 128),
                nn.ReLU(),
                nn.Linear(128, 128),
                nn.ReLU(),
                nn.Linear(128, 3) # RGB
            )
            self.use_sh = False

    def linear_interp(self, vec, coord):
        """Linear interpolation of a 1D vector at coordinate coord."""
        # coord shape: (..., 1)
        # vec shape: (..., N)
        idx = coord.floor().long()
        frac = coord - coord.floor()
        
        idx0 = idx.clamp(0, vec.shape[-1] - 1)
        idx1 = (idx + 1).clamp(0, vec.shape[-1] - 1)
        
        val0 = torch.gather(vec, -1, idx0)
        val1 = torch.gather(vec, -1, idx1)
        
        return val0 * (1 - frac) + val1 * frac

    def bilinear_interp(self, mat, coord_y, coord_z):
        """Bilinear interpolation of a 2D matrix at coordinates (y, z)."""
        # mat shape: (..., H, W)
        # coord_y, coord_z shape: (..., 1)
        
        y_idx = coord_y.floor().long()
        z_idx = coord_z.floor().long()
        y_frac = coord_y - coord_y.floor()
        z_frac = coord_z - coord_z.floor()
        
        y0 = y_idx.clamp(0, mat.shape[-2] - 1)
        y1 = (y_idx + 1).clamp(0, mat.shape[-2] - 1)
        z0 = z_idx.clamp(0, mat.shape[-1] - 1)
        z1 = (z_idx + 1).clamp(0, mat.shape[-1] - 1)
        
        # Gather 4 corners
        # mat[..., y, z]
        v00 = torch.gather(torch.gather(mat, -2, y0.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat)), -1, z0.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat))
        v01 = torch.gather(torch.gather(mat, -2, y0.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat)), -1, z1.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat))
        v10 = torch.gather(torch.gather(mat, -2, y1.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat)), -1, z0.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat))
        v11 = torch.gather(torch.gather(mat, -2, y1.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat)), -1, z1.unsqueeze(-1).expand_as(mat))
        
        # Interpolate
        v0 = v00 * (1 - y_frac) + v10 * y_frac
        v1 = v01 * (1 - y_frac) + v11 * y_frac
        return v0 * (1 - z_frac) + v1 * z_frac

    def forward(self, x, d):
        """
        x: (..., 3) coordinates in [0, 1] or grid bounds
        d: (..., 3) viewing directions
        Returns: sigma (density), c (color)
        """
        x, y, z = x[..., 0:1], x[..., 1:2], x[..., 2:3]
        
        # 1. Compute Geometry Density
        # A_sigma_r^X = v_sigma_X(x) * M_sigma_YZ(y, z)
        v_x = self.linear_interp(self.v_sigma_X, x) # (..., R_sigma)
        M_yz = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_sigma_YZ, y, z) # (..., R_sigma)
        A_sigma_X = v_x * M_yz
        
        # A_sigma_r^Y = v_sigma_Y(y) * M_sigma_XZ(x, z)
        v_y = self.linear_interp(self.v_sigma_Y, y)
        M_xz = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_sigma_XZ, x, z)
        A_sigma_Y = v_y * M_xz
        
        # A_sigma_r^Z = v_sigma_Z(z) * M_sigma_XY(x, y)
        v_z = self.linear_interp(self.v_sigma_Z, z)
        M_xy = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_sigma_XY, x, y)
        A_sigma_Z = v_z * M_xy
        
        # Sum over r and m
        sigma = A_sigma_X.sum(dim=-1) + A_sigma_Y.sum(dim=-1) + A_sigma_Z.sum(dim=-1)
        
        # 2. Compute Appearance Features
        # A_c_r^X, A_c_r^Y, A_c_r^Z
        v_c_x = self.linear_interp(self.v_c_X, x)
        M_c_yz = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_c_YZ, y, z)
        A_c_X = v_c_x * M_c_yz
        
        v_c_y = self.linear_interp(self.v_c_Y, y)
        M_c_xz = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_c_XZ, x, z)
        A_c_Y = v_c_y * M_c_xz
        
        v_c_z = self.linear_interp(self.v_c_Z, z)
        M_c_xy = self.bilinear_interp(self.M_c_XY, x, y)
        A_c_Z = v_c_z * M_c_xy
        
        # Concatenate all A_c values: shape (..., 3*R_c)
        # Order: [A_c_1^X, A_c_1^Y, A_c_1^Z, ..., A_c_Rc^X, A_c_Rc^Y, A_c_Rc^Z]
        A_c_concat = torch.cat([A_c_X, A_c_Y, A_c_Z], dim=-1)
        
        # Multiply by B: (..., 3*R_c) @ (3*R_c, P).T -> (..., P)
        # B is (P, 3*R_c), so we need B.T
        features = torch.matmul(A_c_concat, self.B.T)
        
        # 3. Decode Color
        if self.use_sh:
            # SH decoding with 3rd order (P=27)
            # Standard SH basis evaluation for direction d
            c = sh_decode(features, d)
        else:
            c = self.decoder(features)
            
        return sigma, c

    def render(self, rays_o, rays_d, near, far, Q=128):
        """
        rays_o: (..., 3) origins
        rays_d: (..., 3) directions
        near, far: scalar or (..., 1) bounds
        Q: number of samples
        """
        # Sample points along rays
        t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., Q)
        z_vals = near + t_vals * (far - near)
        points = rays_o.unsqueeze(-2) + rays_d.unsqueeze(-2) * z_vals.unsqueeze(-1)
        
        # Evaluate field
        sigma, c = self.forward(points, rays_d)
        
        # Volume Rendering
        # delta = z_vals[i+1] - z_vals[i]
        deltas = z_vals[..., 1:] - z_vals[..., :-1]
        delta_inf = torch.tensor(1e10).to(z_vals.device)
        deltas = torch.cat([deltas, delta_inf], dim=-1)
        
        # alpha = 1 - exp(-sigma * delta)
        alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * deltas)
        
        # Transmittance
        T = torch.cumprod(torch.exp(-sigma * deltas), dim=-1)
        T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T[..., :1]), T], dim=-1)
        
        # Color = sum(alpha * T * c)
        weights = alpha * T[..., :-1]
        C = torch.sum(weights.unsqueeze(-1) * c, dim=-2)
        
        return C
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**:
*   **Precision**: Trilinear interpolation relies on the linearity of the outer product. Ensure coordinates are normalized to the grid bounds before interpolation [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries**: Clamp interpolation indices to valid grid ranges to avoid out-of-bounds errors [G].
*   **Overfitting**: Use L1 and TV regularization, especially for datasets with few images (e.g., LLFF) or imperfect capture (e.g., Tanks and Temples) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input**: If the bounding box is poorly defined, interpolation may yield incorrect values. Ensure near/far bounds are tight around the scene [G].

## Subtopics

### TensoRF-CP
Uses Canonical Polyadic (CP) decomposition. Simpler than VM, achieves super compact modeling (e.g., <4MB for 500^3 voxels), but lower rendering quality compared to VM [S1][S3, unofficial].

### TensoRF-VM
Uses Vector-Matrix (VM) decomposition. Compresses more parameters per component, achieving significantly better rendering quality than CP with comparable or smaller model sizes [S1][S3, unofficial].

### TensoRF-SH
Uses Spherical Harmonics (3rd order) for the decoding function $S$. Provides a fast, differentiable way to model view-dependent color without an MLP [S1][S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Reconstruction is significantly faster than NeRF. TensoRF-VM-192 (300^3 voxels) takes ~17.4 minutes on Synthetic NeRF, compared to NeRF's ~35 hours [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Model size is compact. TensoRF-CP-384 (500^3 voxels) is <4MB. TensoRF-VM-192 (300^3 voxels) is ~71.8MB [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance**: On Synthetic NeRF, TensoRF-VM-192 achieves PSNR 33.14 and SSIM 0.963 [S1][S3, unofficial]. On NSVF, PSNR 36.52, SSIM 0.982 [S1][S3, unofficial]. On Tanks and Temples, PSNR 28.56, SSIM 0.920 [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Fast reconstruction, compact model size, high quality.
*   **Limitations**: Requires careful tuning of regularization for difficult datasets. Coarse-to-fine upsampling adds complexity to the training loop [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use**: When fast reconstruction and compact storage are priorities, and high-quality novel view synthesis is needed.
*   **When not to use**: When extremely high resolution is required without significant memory increase, or when the scene has complex dynamic effects not captured by static radiance fields [G].

## Background
TensoRF was introduced by Linqi Chen et al. in 2022 [S1][S3, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of representing radiance fields as tensors and factorizing them to reduce memory and computation. It was developed to address the high computational cost and large model size of [Neural Radiance Fields](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) [S1][S3, unofficial].
