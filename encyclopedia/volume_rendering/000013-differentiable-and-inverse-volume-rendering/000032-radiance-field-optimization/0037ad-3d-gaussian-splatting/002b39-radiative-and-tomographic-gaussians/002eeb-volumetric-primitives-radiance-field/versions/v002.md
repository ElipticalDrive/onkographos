# Volumetric Primitives Radiance Field


## Overview
Volumetric Primitives Radiance Field (VPRF) is a differentiable volume rendering integrator designed to optimize and render 3D Gaussian Splatting-like assets using volumetric primitives [S1, unofficial]. It simplifies the radiance field rendering pipeline by considering only primary rays from the camera and accumulating emission based on spherical harmonics within segments [S2, unofficial]. VPRF is tailored for radiance field optimization tasks, whereas its sibling VPPT is designed for general volumetric scattering media [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
VPRF builds on the shared principles of radiative and tomographic Gaussians [Radiative and Tomographic Gaussians](4971-radiative-and-tomographic-gaussians.md). It utilizes the same underlying primitive representation as VPPT but restricts the rendering scope to primary rays for efficient radiance field optimization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera parameters (intrinsics $K$, extrinsics $c2w$), a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, covariance, spherical harmonic coefficients), and training image colors.
*   **Outputs:** Rendered pixel colors $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for the photometric loss calculation.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the 3D Gaussian primitive representation. Each primitive is defined by:
*   Mean position $\mathbf{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   Covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (often parameterized by a rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and scaling vector $\mathbf{s}$).
*   Spherical Harmonic (SH) coefficients representing view-dependent color.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel in the training images, generate a primary ray $\mathbf{r}$ originating from the camera center with direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Primitive Intersection:** Determine which Gaussian primitives intersect the ray $\mathbf{r}$. This involves solving for the intersection of the ray with the implicit surface defined by the Gaussian's covariance [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Segmentation:** For each intersecting primitive, calculate the entry and exit points along the ray to define a segment [S2, unofficial]. The mathematical definition of the Gaussian allows for closed-form calculation of these segments, avoiding stochastic noise [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Emission Accumulation:** Within each segment, accumulate the emission based on the spherical harmonics coefficients and the Gaussian density profile [S2, unofficial]. The transparency and density are computed analytically [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Compositing:** Composite the contributions from all segments along the ray using standard volume rendering integration to produce the final pixel color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** The closed-form calculation of segments and transparency eliminates stochastic noise, improving calculation speed compared to traditional stochastic methods [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Zero noise in rendering; efficient ray tracing for primary rays; suitable for radiance field optimization [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Restricted to primary rays; not designed for general volumetric scattering media (unlike VPPT) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
VPRF is introduced as part of the `volprim` library by Facebook Research [S1, unofficial]. It is described in the context of rendering 3D Gaussian Splatting-like assets, leveraging the volumetric primitives framework to enable differentiable rendering for radiance field optimization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
