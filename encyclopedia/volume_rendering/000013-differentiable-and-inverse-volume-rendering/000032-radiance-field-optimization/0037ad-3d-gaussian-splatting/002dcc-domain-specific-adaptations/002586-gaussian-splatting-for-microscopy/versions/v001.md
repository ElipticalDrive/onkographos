# Gaussian Splatting for Microscopy

Also called: Gaussian Splatting for Microscopy Volumes, nD Gaussian splatting, Gaussian splats

## Overview
Gaussian Splatting for Microscopy decomposes an $n$-dimensional fluorescence microscopy volume into a mixture of oriented, anisotropic Gaussian primitives, enabling interactive web visualization and high compression ratios without voxel grids [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Volumetric Gaussian Splatting (AVIS)](1875-volumetric-gaussian-splatting-avis.md) by targeting scientific data compression and streaming via a fixed-pool optimizer with blind-spot cross-validation, and from [UltraGauss Rendering](5170-ultragauss-rendering.md) by its specific preprocessing pipeline for light-sheet/confocal data and browser-centric delivery [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A preprocessed $n$-dimensional microscopy volume $V: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, typically 3D or 4D (x, y, z, t).
*   **Output:** A set of $K$ Gaussian splats, each defined by position $\mu_k$, amplitude $a_k$, and Cholesky factor $L_k$, stored in a hierarchical scene graph for streaming.

**Data Structures**
Each splat $k$ stores:
*   $\mu_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$: Center position.
*   $a_k \in \mathbb{R}$: Amplitude, stored as a raw value but activated via softplus during optimization to ensure $a_k > 0$. Upper-bounded by configurable $a_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $L_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$: Lower-triangular Cholesky factor of the covariance matrix $\Sigma_k = L_k L_k^\top$. This parameterization stores $D(D+1)/2$ elements (6 for 3D) and guarantees positive-definiteness by construction [S1, unofficial].
*   The diagonal of $L_k$ is parameterized as $\sigma_{min} + \text{softplus}(\cdot)$, where $\sigma_{min} = 1/\sqrt{12}$ voxels (the standard deviation of a uniform voxel), ensuring no splat is narrower than a voxel's own spread [S1, unofficial].

**Preprocessing**
1.  **Registration and Fusion:** Multi-view images (e.g., light-sheet) are registered and fused with intensity equalization [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Deconvolution:** The fused volume is deconvolved (e.g., 10 iterations against the point-spread function) to produce a single fused volume per timepoint [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Normalization:** Source volumes (stored as 16-bit integers or float32) are normalized to $[0, 1]$ using an affine map of the intensity range with dataset-specific outlier clipping [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Clipping Strategy:**
        *   Plain min-max: Kidney, Cells3D, Blastocyst, Tribolium, Mouse heart [S1, unofficial].
        *   1st–99.5th percentiles: OpenCell channels [S1, unofficial].
        *   1st–99.999th percentiles: C. elegans [S1, unofficial].
        *   0th–99.999th percentiles: Sparse Fly brain [S1, unofficial].
        *   0th–99.9th percentiles: Zebrafish, Drosophila [S1, unofficial].
        *   Neuromast channels: Subtract background floors (105.99 and 103.89 grey levels, pinned at build time, clipped at 0), then clip to 0th–99.9th percentiles [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Background Floor:** For benchmarks, no floor suppression is applied (`--floor none`). For compression/timing, automatic floor suppression (`--floor auto`) subtracts a constant pedestal before fitting [S1, unofficial].

**Optimization Steps**
1.  **Seeding:** Initial positions are seeded automatically from gradient-weighted samples of the volume’s edges, complemented by a coarse uniform grid [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Cross-Validation Setup:** To determine the optimal splat count $K$, 5% of voxels are masked and replaced with donut-median neighborhood values [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Fitting:**
    *   Optimize parameters using the Adam optimizer [S1, unofficial].
    *   Loss function: L1 loss between the predicted volume $\hat{V}(x)$ and the input $V(x)$, plus regularization on splat amplitudes and extents [S1, unofficial].
    *   The model prediction is:
        $$ \hat{V}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_k \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \|L_k^{-1} (x - \mu_k)\|^2\right) $$
    *   Periodically, the least informative splats are relocated to regions of high reconstruction residual. This fixed-pool strategy keeps optimizer tensor shapes constant, acting as an analogue to pruning-and-seeding [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Stopping Criterion:** Stop fitting when the held-out PSNR peaks. If the peak is interior or cull-limited (where cull holds count constant), use the peak or the point where cull holds count constant. If a noise floor is estimable (e.g., on fused/deconvolved volumes where held-out peaks are not guaranteed), cap the budget from above to avoid reproducing noise [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Viewer Evaluation:** The viewer uses a truncated Gaussian for rendering to ensure compact support:
    $$ \hat{V}_{viewer}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_k \max\left(0, \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2} \|L_k^{-1} (x - \mu_k)\|^2\right) - \frac{c_T}{1 - c_T}\right) $$
    where $c_T = \exp(-T/2)$ at $T = 2.75$ standard deviations [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Fitting is asymmetric; fitting is expensive (minutes per volume on a single GPU), while viewing is cheap (milliseconds per frame) [S1, unofficial]. Convergence is rapid: at 2,000 iterations, training PSNR is within 0.5 dB of the 20,000-iteration value for 77 of 85 volume-capacity cells [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The representation is compact. Compression ratios range from 6× to 340× (median 99.3×) across 17 volumes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Tribolium embryo nuclei:** 51.4 dB PSNR at 32K splats, 54.9 dB at 512K, 56.0 dB at 2M splats [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Kidney nuclei:** 34.8 dB PSNR at 32K splats, 45.2 dB at 512K splats [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Efficiency:** 16K splats × 500 iterations beats 4K splats × 20,000 iterations by +3.03 dB average across 17 volumes at 10× fewer splat-iterations [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Runtime:** Median 2.4 minutes per volume at 32K budget on NVIDIA RTX 3070 (8 GiB) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Rendering:** On an RTX 3070 at 720p HDR, Gaussian splats maintain 60 FPS (16.7 ms budget) through $10^6$ elements [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Direct GPU rasterization without voxel grids; suitable for streaming where bandwidth and memory are constrained; high compression ratios; interactive web visualization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Structures thinner than the local Gaussian scale (e.g., individual microtubules) blur; the fit plateaus well below the noise ceiling on clean volumes; the representation is for looking, not measuring [S1, unofficial]. Blind-spot cross-validation assumes independent noise; on fused/deconvolved volumes, a held-out peak is not guaranteed [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Interactive web visualization of large n-dimensional microscopy datasets; sharing data via static file hosting; scenarios where bandwidth and client-side memory are constrained [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Quantification, segmentation, or tracking tasks that require precise measurement on source voxels; scenarios where structures are thinner than the Gaussian scale [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Gaussian Splatting for Microscopy is part of the Luxar system, an open-source Python framework for compiling multidimensional scientific data into compact archives for web visualization [S1, unofficial]. It adapts nD Gaussian splatting for microscopy, validating the approach on 17 volumes from 12 datasets spanning spinning-disk, confocal, light-sheet, and structured-illumination modalities [S1, unofficial]. The method uses blind-spot cross-validation to set the number of Gaussians without noise-free references [S1, unofficial]. It complements desktop tools (napari, Fiji) and voxel-pyramid web viewers (Neuroglancer, Viv, Vitessce) by streaming fitted geometry to browsers [S1, unofficial].
