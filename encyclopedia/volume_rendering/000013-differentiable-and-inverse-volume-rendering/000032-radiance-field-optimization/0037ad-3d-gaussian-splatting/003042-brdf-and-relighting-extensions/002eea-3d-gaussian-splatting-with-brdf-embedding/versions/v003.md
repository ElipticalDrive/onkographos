# 3D Gaussian Splatting with BRDF Embedding

Also called: Gao et al. BRDF Embedding, Gao et al. BRDF Method

## Overview
This method extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by embedding physically based rendering (PBR) parameters into each Gaussian primitive, enabling per-point relighting and specular reflection [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard 3DGS, which bakes lighting into spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients and limits performance on reflective surfaces, this approach decouples geometry, material, and lighting [S1][S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from siblings like [Relightable 3D Gaussian](6001-relightable-3d-gaussian.md) by explicitly storing BRDF parameters (albedo, roughness, normals) per Gaussian rather than relying on differentiable shading networks or separate light probes.

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}$ initialized from a SfM point cloud, and a set of input images with known camera poses.
**Outputs:** A rendered image and optimized Gaussian parameters.

**Data Structures:**
Each Gaussian $G_i$ stores the following fields:
1.  **Position:** $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$
2.  **Covariance:** $\Sigma_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ (or rotation/scale decomposition)
3.  **Opacity:** $\alpha_i \in [0, 1]$
4.  **BRDF Parameters:**
    *   Albedo: $\mathbf{c}_{diff, i} \in \mathbb{R}^3$
    *   Roughness: $r_i \in [0, 1]$
    *   Surface Normal: $\mathbf{n}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (unit vector)
5.  **Indirect Lighting:** Spherical Harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{SH, i}$ (typically degree 3, 9 coefficients per RGB channel) [S1][S2, unofficial].
6.  **View Direction:** $\mathbf{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (optional, for view-dependent effects if not fully captured by SH) [G].

**Method Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize Gaussians from COLMAP points. Set initial albedo to mean color, roughness to 0.5, normals to surface estimates or random, and SH coefficients to zero or pre-computed from input views [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Rasterization:** Project Gaussians to screen space. Sort by depth.
3.  **Shading Calculation:** For each pixel covered by a Gaussian, compute the outgoing radiance $L_o$ using a PBR model (e.g., Cook-Torrance) [S1][S2, unofficial]:
    $$ L_o = L_{direct} + L_{indirect} $$
    *   **Direct Lighting ($L_{direct}$):** Reconstructed by evaluating the environment map $\mathcal{E}(\omega_i)$ at the incident light direction $\omega_i$ determined by the view direction and normal [S1][S2, unofficial].
        $$ L_{direct} = \int_{\Omega} f_r(\omega_i, \omega_o) L_i(\omega_i) (\mathbf{n} \cdot \omega_i) d\omega_i $$
        In practice, this is approximated by sampling the environment map or using pre-filtered BRDF LUTs [G].
    *   **Indirect Lighting ($L_{indirect}$):** Modeled using the stored SH coefficients $\mathbf{c}_{SH}$ evaluated at the view direction $\omega_o$ [S1][S2, unofficial]:
        $$ L_{indirect} = \sum_{l=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} c_{SH, l, m} Y_{l, m}(\omega_o) $$
4.  **Compositing:** Alpha-composite the shaded radiance values along the ray, similar to standard 3DGS [S1][S2, unofficial].
5.  **Optimization:** Minimize the difference between rendered and input images using photometric loss (L1 + SSIM). Update all Gaussian parameters, including BRDF and SH coefficients, via gradient descent [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   SH Degree: Typically 3 (9 coefficients per channel) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Roughness Range: $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Degenerate Normals:** If normals are poorly initialized, specular highlights may appear incorrect. [G] Use normal regularization or initial surface fitting.
*   **Precision:** SH coefficients for indirect lighting may require higher precision to avoid banding in complex lighting [G].

## Subtopics

### [3DGS-DR](1471-3dgs-dr.md)
3DGS-DR incorporates classical reflection and shading concepts into the 3DGS framework, addressing the limitation of standard 3DGS's emission-absorption model which bakes lighting into SH coefficients. It enables representation of specular highlights and diffuse reflections, distinguishing it from siblings like GaussianShader and IRGS which may differ in BRDF parameterization or optimization strategies.

### SWAG
SWAG extends 3DGS by conditioning Gaussians on learned appearance embeddings and training unsupervised transient Gaussians to ignore occluders [S2, unofficial]. This yields state-of-the-art results on diverse photo scenes by mitigating transient effects [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Similar to standard 3DGS rasterization, but shading calculation per Gaussian adds overhead due to BRDF evaluation and environment map sampling [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Higher than standard 3DGS due to storage of albedo, roughness, normals, and SH coefficients per Gaussian [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Enables relighting, handles reflective surfaces better than standard 3DGS, physically based material representation [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally more expensive per pixel due to BRDF evaluation; requires careful initialization of normals and roughness [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Scenes with significant specular reflections or when relighting is required [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Simple diffuse scenes where standard 3DGS suffices, or when computational resources are limited [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
Originated from work by Gao et al., who embedded BRDF parameters into 3D Gaussians for per-point physically based rendering [S1][S2, unofficial]. This approach addresses the limitations of standard 3DGS, which relies on primary rays and baked lighting [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is part of the broader trend of integrating classical rendering concepts into differentiable rendering frameworks [S1][S2, unofficial].
