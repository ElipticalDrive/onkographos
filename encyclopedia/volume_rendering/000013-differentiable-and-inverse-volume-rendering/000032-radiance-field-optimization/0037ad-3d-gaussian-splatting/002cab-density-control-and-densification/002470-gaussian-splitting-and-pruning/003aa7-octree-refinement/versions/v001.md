# Octree refinement

Also called: Spatial subdivision refinement

## Overview
Octree refinement is a spatial subdivision technique used to reorder Gaussian splats for progressive rendering, ensuring that sparsely captured areas of a scene are reconstructed earlier than they would be by global contribution metrics alone [S1]. It differs from simple global sorting by enforcing a minimum representation per spatial region, thereby improving background quality at the cost of slight foreground degradation [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}$, each with a pre-computed global contribution score $C_i$ derived from rendering error across training views [S1].
*   **Output:** A refined ordering of Gaussians for progressive loading [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Octree:** A hierarchical spatial partitioning structure. Each node represents a cubic volume. The tree has a configurable maximum depth $D$.
*   **Leaf Node List:** For each leaf node at depth $D$, a list of Gaussians whose centers fall within that node's volume.

**Parameters:**
*   **Octree Depth ($D$):** The maximum depth of the octree. Typical value: 3 [S1].
    *   $D=3$ consistently yields good results across scenes [S1].
    *   Low values ($<3$) have little impact on quality [S1].
    *   High values ($\ge 7$) introduce errors (black blur) and cause convergence back to the base global approach [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Insertion:** Insert all $N$ Gaussians into the octree based on their center positions. Assign each Gaussian to the leaf node corresponding to its spatial location at depth $D$ [S1].
2.  **Selection:** For each leaf node in the octree, identify the subset of Gaussians contained within it. From this subset, select the top $K$ Gaussians with the highest global contribution scores $C_i$. The source specifies using the "top contributing Gaussians per leaf node" [S1]. Implementation notes suggest using the contribution of the 20 highest contributing Gaussians per pixel as a basis for the initial score, but for the octree step, the selection is strictly per leaf [S1].
3.  **Aggregation:** Collect all selected Gaussians from all leaf nodes. If a Gaussian is the top contributor in multiple overlapping regions (unlikely in a strict partition, but possible if centers are near boundaries or if the method implies a soft assignment, though the text implies hard partitioning), it is included once. The resulting set forms the refined ordering [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **High Depth Errors:** Using an octree depth of 7 or larger introduces rendering errors, specifically "black blur," and causes the method to converge back to the base global contribution approach [S1].
*   **Low Depth Impact:** Depth values significantly lower than 3 have little to no impact on quality [S1].
*   **PSNR Loss:** Including the octree refinement results in an average loss in PSNR scores because the foreground (which often contributes more to PSNR) is deprioritized in favor of background coverage [S1]. Perceptually, however, background quality improves [S1].

## Subtopics

### Octree-Based Ordering
An addition to the base contribution metric that uses an octree to force Gaussians to spread out over the scene, ensuring sparsely captured parts are loaded sooner [S1].
*   **Parameter:** Octree depth parameter. A value of 3 consistently yields good results. Values too low or too high result in insufficient attention to the background [S1].
*   **Pitfall:** High depth values (e.g., 7 or larger) introduce errors such as black blur and cause convergence to the base approach. Low depth values have little impact [S1].
*   **Performance:** Including the octree results in an average loss in PSNR scores because the foreground often contributes more to PSNR, but perceptually improves background quality [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Building the octree is $O(N \log M)$ where $N$ is the number of Gaussians and $M$ is the scene extent, assuming a balanced tree. Selection is linear in the number of Gaussians per leaf.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the Gaussians in the octree structure.
*   **Strengths:** Ensures sparsely captured areas are not under-reconstructed; improves perceptual quality of the background; allows for object-level prioritization if manually segmented [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Causes a loss in average PSNR scores; high octree depths introduce rendering artifacts; does not account for viewpoint-specific occlusions (frustum culling is a separate step that occurs after global ordering) [S1].
*   **When to use:** When progressive rendering quality in background/sparse regions is prioritized over peak PSNR metrics.
*   **When not to use:** When peak PSNR is the primary metric or when the scene is uniformly dense.

## Background
Originated in the PRoGS (Progressive Rendering of Gaussian Splats) paper, which introduced spatial subdivision techniques to improve the progressive loading of 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions [S1]. The method was developed to address the issue where global contribution metrics unfairly under-reconstruct sparsely captured parts of a scene [S1].
