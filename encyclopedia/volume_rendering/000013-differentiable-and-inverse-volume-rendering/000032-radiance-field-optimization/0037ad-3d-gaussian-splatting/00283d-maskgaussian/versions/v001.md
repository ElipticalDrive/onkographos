# MaskGaussian

Also called: Masked Gaussian Splatting

## Overview
MaskGaussian is a differentiable pruning method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that models Gaussians as probabilistic entities rather than permanently removing them, utilizing them according to their probability of existence to mitigate sub-optimized reconstruction from deterministic pruning [S1]. It extends Compact3DGS by making pruning probabilistic and introduces masked-rasterization to allow gradients to flow to unsampled (masked-out) Gaussians [S1]. Unlike previous methods that multiply masks with Gaussian attributes (opacity/scale) which results in zero density alpha and prevents gradient updates, MaskGaussian applies masks during blending to allow masked Gaussians to participate in rasterization and receive gradients [S1].

## How it works
MaskGaussian formulates Gaussian pruning based on the probability of existence and samples a mask for each Gaussian to represent its presence or absence according to this probability [S1]. The method ensures that even unsampled Gaussians receive meaningful gradients, guiding the update of their existence probability based on their virtual contribution to the scene [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- Set of $N$ 3D Gaussians $\{G_i\}_{i=1}^N$, where each Gaussian $G_i$ has position, covariance (or scale/orientation), color $\mathbf{c}_i$, and base opacity $\alpha_i$ (derived from scale/orientation) [S1].
- Camera parameters for the current view [S1].
- Learnable mask scores for each Gaussian [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Rendered image color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ for each pixel $\mathbf{x}$ [S1].
- Updated Gaussian attributes and mask scores via backpropagation [S1].
- Pruned set of Gaussians based on sampling results [S1].

### Data Structures
Each Gaussian $i$ maintains:
- **Position**: 3D vector.
- **Covariance**: Defined by scale and rotation (standard 3DGS representation) [S1].
- **Color**: Spherical Harmonics coefficients or RGB vector $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1].
- **Base Opacity/Density**: $\alpha_i$, computed from scale/orientation during splatting [S1].
- **Mask Scores**: Two learnable scalar values, one for "present" and one for "absent" [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1. **Mask Sampling**: For each Gaussian $i$, assign 2 learnable mask scores. Apply Gumbel-Softmax to sample one differentiable category out of the 2 scores, denoted as $\mathcal{M}_i \in \{0, 1\}$ [S1]. This represents the presence (1) or absence (0) of the Gaussian in the current forward pass [S1].

2. **Standard Splatting**: Splat all $N$ Gaussians in the standard way without any interference from the mask $\mathcal{M}_i$ [S1]. Compute the splatted alpha $\alpha_i$ for each Gaussian based on its geometric properties (scale/orientation) and view direction [S1]. This ensures that all Gaussians, regardless of their mask value, have valid alpha values and can pass the alpha-filtering stage [S1].

3. **Masked Rasterization (Forward)**: Pass both present and absent Gaussians through the alpha filter and into masked-rasterization [S1]. Apply the masks $\mathcal{M}_i$ to the transmittance attenuation and color accumulation processes [S1].
    - **Color Accumulation**: Compute the pixel color $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ using the following formula [S1]:
      $$
      \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{M}_i \cdot \mathbf{c}_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot T_i
      $$
      where $\mathbf{c}_i$ is the color of the $i$-th Gaussian, $\alpha_i$ is the splatted density, and $T_i$ is the transmittance before the $i$-th Gaussian [S1].
    - **Transmittance Update**: Update the transmittance $T_i$ for the next Gaussian using the following formula [S1]:
      $$
      T_{i+1} = \mathcal{M}_i \cdot (1 - \alpha_i) \cdot T_i + (1 - \mathcal{M}_i) \cdot T_i
      $$
      If $\mathcal{M}_i = 1$, the Gaussian normally contributes to the color and consumes the transmittance based on its $\alpha_i$ [S1]. If $\mathcal{M}_i = 0$, the Gaussian's contribution to color is masked out, and its transmittance consumption is skipped (i.e., $T_{i+1} = T_i$) [S1].

4. **Gradient Backpropagation (Backward)**: Produce rendered images and allow gradients to backpropagate all the way to the masks $\mathcal{M}_i$, regardless of whether they indicate presence or absence [S1]. The gradient of the loss $L$ with respect to the mask $\mathcal{M}_i$ is given by [S1]:
   $$
   \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathcal{M}_i} = \alpha_i \cdot T_i \cdot \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})} \cdot (\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{b}_{i+1})
   $$
   where $\mathbf{b}_{i+1}$ is the color rendered behind the $i$-th Gaussian, defined as [S1]:
   $$
   \mathbf{b}_{i+1} = \sum_{j=i+1}^N \mathcal{M}_j \cdot \mathbf{c}_j \cdot \alpha_j \cdot T_j
   $$
   This gradient formulation captures the influence between the target color and the masked Gaussian's color, measuring the benefit of using the Gaussian over the background [S1]. It also encompasses $\alpha_i \cdot T_i$, the importance criterion for score-based prunings [S1].

5. **Loss Computation**: Compute the total loss $L$ as [S1]:
   $$
   L = L_{render} + \lambda_m \cdot L_m
   $$
   where $L_{render}$ is the standard rendering loss (e.g., L1 or LPIPS) [S1], and $L_m$ is the squared loss on the average number of Gaussians [S1]:
   $$
   L_m = \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{M}_i \right)^2
   $$
   This term constrains the average number of Gaussians and is weighted by the hyperparameter $\lambda_m$ [S1].

6. **Pruning Procedure**: Sample each Gaussian 10 times [S1]. Remove Gaussians that are never sampled (probability near zero) [S1]. This pruning procedure is applied at every densification step and every 1000 iterations after densification [S1].

import numpy as np

def gumbel_softmax(scores, temperature=1.0):
    """
    Samples a binary mask from two learnable scores using Gumbel-Softmax.
    scores: array of shape (N, 2), where N is the number of Gaussians.
    Returns: binary mask array of shape (N,) with values 0 or 1.
    """
    # Gumbel noise
    u1 = np.random.uniform(0, 1, size=scores.shape)
    u2 = np.random.uniform(0, 1, size=scores.shape)
    g1 = -np.log(-np.log(u1))
    g2 = -np.log(-np.log(u2))
    
    # Gumbel-Softmax logits
    logits = (scores + np.array([g1, g2])) / temperature
    
    # Softmax probabilities
    exp_logits = np.exp(logits - np.max(logits, axis=1, keepdims=True))
    probs = exp_logits / np.sum(exp_logits, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    
    # Sample from categorical distribution
    # In practice, for binary mask, we can sample directly from the probabilities
    # or use the straight-through estimator. Here we assume sampling.
    # For a single sample per Gaussian:
    masks = np.zeros(scores.shape[0])
    for i in range(scores.shape[0]):
        if np.random.rand() < probs[i, 1]: # Assuming index 1 is 'present'
            masks[i] = 1
        else:
            masks[i] = 0
            
    return masks

def compute_mask_gradient(loss, masks, gaussians, camera):
    """
    Computes the gradient of the loss with respect to the mask scores.
    loss: scalar loss value.
    masks: binary array of shape (N,).
    gaussians: list of Gaussian objects.
    camera: camera parameters.
    Returns: gradient array of shape (N, 2) for the two mask scores.
    """
    N = len(gaussians)
    grad_masks = np.zeros((N, 2))
    
    # Re-render to get intermediate values for gradient computation
    # This is a simplified representation; in practice, this is done via autograd
    sorted_indices = sort_gaussians_by_depth(gaussians, camera)
    
    T = 1.0
    # Precompute b_{i+1} for each Gaussian
    b_next = np.zeros((N, 3))
    current_b = np.zeros(3)
    
    # Iterate backwards to compute b_{i+1}
    for idx in reversed(sorted_indices):
        G = gaussians[idx]
        M = masks[idx]
        alpha = compute_splatted_alpha(G, camera)
        
        # b_{i+1} is the color rendered behind the i-th Gaussian
        b_next[idx] = current_b
        
        # Update current_b for the next iteration (which is the previous Gaussian in depth)
        if M == 1:
            current_b += M * G.color * alpha * T

            current_b += 0

        dL_dc = get_loss_gradient_wrt_pixel_color()

### Parameters
- **$\lambda_m$**: Balancing weight for the squared loss $L_m$. Typical values: 0.1 (iterations 19,000-20,000), 0.0005 (0-30,000 iterations), or 0.001 (0-30,000 iterations) [S1].
- **Gumbel-Softmax Temperature**: Not explicitly given in the sources, but typically a small value (e.g., 0.1 or 1.0) is used to balance between discrete sampling and differentiable approximation [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Zero Alpha Filtering**: Previous methods that multiply masks with Gaussian attributes (opacity/scale) result in zero density alpha, causing the Gaussian to be filtered out before rasterization, hindering gradient updates [S1]. MaskGaussian avoids this by applying masks during blending, not to attributes [S1].
- **Outdated Gaussians**: If unsampled Gaussians remain unchanged, they risk becoming outdated, leading to an inaccurate evaluation of their potential contribution when eventually sampled in later iterations [S1]. MaskGaussian mitigates this by allowing gradients to flow to unsampled Gaussians [S1].
- **Fine Gaussians Disadvantage**: Methods that multiply masks with Gaussian scales and opacity may put fine Gaussians at a disadvantage when they receive negative gradients for becoming smaller [S1]. MaskGaussian does not bind mask gradients to scales/opacity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: The method adds a small overhead for mask sampling and masked rasterization, but significantly reduces the number of Gaussians to be processed in later iterations due to pruning [S1].
- **Memory Complexity**: Reduces memory usage by pruning Gaussians, with pruning ratios of 62.4%, 67.7%, and 75.3% on Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending datasets respectively [S1].
- **Measured Performance**:
    - On Mip-NeRF360, MaskGaussian (Ours-α) achieves 27.43 PSNR with 1.205M Gaussians at 384.7 FPS, compared to 3DGS's 27.45 PSNR with 3.204M Gaussians at 187.8 FPS [S1].
    - On Tanks & Temples, MaskGaussian (Ours-α) achieves 23.72 PSNR with 0.590M Gaussians at 558.3 FPS, compared to 3DGS's 23.74 PSNR with 1.825M Gaussians at 254.5 FPS [S1].
    - On Deep Blending, MaskGaussian (Ours-α) achieves 29.69 PSNR with 0.694M Gaussians at 637.1 FPS, compared to 3DGS's 29.53 PSNR with 2.815M Gaussians at 201.3 FPS [S1].
- **Scaling**: The method scales well with the number of Gaussians, as it prunes redundant components, leading to rendering speedups of 2.05x, 2.19x, and 3.16x on Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending datasets respectively [S1].
- **Strengths**: Allows dynamic assessment of Gaussian contribution, mitigates sub-optimized reconstruction from deterministic pruning, and achieves better rendering quality with fewer Gaussians [S1].
- **Limitations**: Requires additional learnable parameters (mask scores) and careful tuning of $\lambda_m$ [S1].

## Background
MaskGaussian was introduced in the paper "MaskGaussian: Adaptive 3D Gaussian Representation from Probabilistic Masks" [S1]. It builds upon Compact3DGS and extends its pruning approach from deterministic to probabilistic [S1]. The method is inspired by the use of masks in other fields, such as removing image spatial area in CNNs, redundant tokens in vision transformers, and LLMs, where making the mask receive gradients simultaneously from active and inactive tokens/patches balances the benefit and cost of mask activation [S1].
