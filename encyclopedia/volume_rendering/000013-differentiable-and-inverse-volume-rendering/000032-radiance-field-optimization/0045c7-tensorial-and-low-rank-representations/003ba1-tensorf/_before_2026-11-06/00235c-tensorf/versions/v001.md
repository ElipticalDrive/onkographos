# TensoRF

Also called: TensoRF (Tensorial Radiance Field Representation), TensoRF Representation, Tensorial Radiance Field Representation, Tensorial Radiance Fields

## Overview
TensoRF models a continuous radiance field mapping 3D location $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to volume density $\sigma$ and view-dependent color $c$ by factorizing the underlying feature grids into compact tensor components [S1][S3, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from [NeRF](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) by replacing the neural network MLP with factorized tensor grids (using CP or VM decomposition), achieving significantly faster reconstruction times and smaller model sizes while maintaining or exceeding rendering quality [S1][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view input images with known camera poses.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized tensor factors representing the radiance field; rendered images via differentiable volume rendering.

**Data Structures:**
The method uses two regular 3D grids, factorized into vectors and matrices:
1.  **Geometry Grid ($G_\sigma$):** A 3D tensor $\mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K}$ storing single-channel volume density values directly [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  **Appearance Grid ($G_c$):** A 4D tensor $\mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K \times P}$ storing $P$ appearance feature channels [S1][S3, unofficial].
3.  **Global Appearance Dictionary ($B$):** A matrix $\mathbb{R}^{P \times 3R_c}$ formed by stacking vectors $\mathbf{b}_r$ as columns [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Factorization (VM Decomposition):**
The geometry grid is decomposed into $R_\sigma$ components. Each component $r$ consists of three terms corresponding to the XYZ axes:
$$ \mathcal{G}_\sigma = \sum_{r=1}^{R_\sigma} \sum_{m \in XYZ} \mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^m $$
where $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^X = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^X \circ \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{YZ}$, $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^Y = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Y \circ \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XZ}$, and $\mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^Z = \mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Z \circ \mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XY}$ [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^X \in \mathbb{R}^I$, $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Y \in \mathbb{R}^J$, $\mathbf{v}_{\sigma,r}^Z \in \mathbb{R}^K$ are vectors.
*   $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{YZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XZ} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times K}$, $\mathbf{M}_{\sigma,r}^{XY} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J}$ are matrices.

The appearance grid is decomposed into $R_c$ components. Each component $r$ uses vectors for the spatial modes and a feature-mode vector $\mathbf{b}_r \in \mathbb{R}^P$:
$$ \mathcal{G}_c = \sum_{r=1}^{R_c} \left( \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^X \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r-2} + \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^Y \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r-1} + \mathcal{A}_{c,r}^Z \circ \mathbf{b}_{3r} \right) $$
where $\mathcal{A}_{c,r}^X = \mathbf{v}_{c,r}^X \circ \mathbf{M}_{c,r}^{YZ}$, etc. [S1][S3, unofficial].
Total parameters: $3R_\sigma + 3R_c$ matrices and $3R_\sigma + 6R_c$ vectors [S1].

**Trilinear Interpolation:**
To evaluate features at continuous coordinates $\mathbf{x}=(x,y,z)$, the method exploits the linearity of interpolation on the factorized components [S1][S3, unofficial]:
$$ \mathcal{A}_r^X(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{v}_r^X(x) \mathbf{M}_r^{YZ}(y, z) $$
where $\mathbf{v}_r^X(x)$ is linearly interpolated along X, and $\mathbf{M}_r^{YZ}(y, z)$ is bilinearly interpolated in the YZ plane [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Rendering Equation:**
Density and color are computed as:
$$ \sigma(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{r=1}^{R_\sigma} \sum_{m \in XYZ} \mathcal{A}_{\sigma,r}^m(\mathbf{x}) $$
$$ \mathcal{G}_c(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{B} \left( \bigoplus_{r=1}^{R_c} [\mathcal{A}_{c,r}^m(\mathbf{x})]_{m \in XYZ} \right) $$
$$ c(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) = S(\mathcal{G}_c(\mathbf{x}), \mathbf{d}) $$
where $\oplus$ concatenates the $3R_c$ scalars into a vector, and $S$ is the decoding function [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Decoding Function $S$:**
*   **Spherical Harmonics (SH):** Uses 3rd-order SH coefficients with RGB channels, corresponding to $P=27$ features [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **MLP:** A small MLP with two fully connected layers (128-channel hidden layers) and ReLU activation [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Optimization Steps:**
1.  Initialize tensor factors on a coarse grid ($N_0^3$ voxels, $N_0=128$) [S1][S3, unofficial].
2.  Optimize per scene via gradient descent (Adam) minimizing L2 rendering loss [S1][S3, unofficial].
3.  Apply regularization: L1 norm loss on factors (adequate for most datasets) or TV loss (for few-image/imperfect capture datasets like LLFF or Tanks & Temples) [S1][S3, unofficial].
4.  Coarse-to-fine reconstruction: Upsample vectors and matrices linearly/bilinearly at steps 2000, 3000, 4000, 5500, 7000. Voxel count interpolates between $N_0^3$ and $N^3$ linearly in logarithmic space [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def linear_interp(val, coord, grid_size):
    """Linear interpolation of a 1D vector at coordinate coord."""
    idx = coord * (grid_size - 1)
    i0 = int(math.floor(idx))
    i1 = min(i0 + 1, grid_size - 1)
    w = idx - i0
    return val[i0] * (1 - w) + val[i1] * w

def bilinear_interp(matrix, y, z, rows, cols):
    """Bilinear interpolation of a 2D matrix at (y, z)."""
    y_idx = y * (rows - 1)
    z_idx = z * (cols - 1)
    
    y0 = int(math.floor(y_idx))
    y1 = min(y0 + 1, rows - 1)
    wy = y_idx - y0
    
    z0 = int(math.floor(z_idx))
    z1 = min(z0 + 1, cols - 1)
    wz = z_idx - z0
    
    v00 = matrix[y0, z0]
    v01 = matrix[y0, z1]
    v10 = matrix[y1, z0]
    v11 = matrix[y1, z1]
    
    top = v00 * (1 - wy) + v01 * wy
    bottom = v10 * (1 - wy) + v11 * wy
    return top * (1 - wz) + bottom * wz

def evaluate_density(x, y, z, factors_sigma):
    """
    Evaluate density sigma at continuous coordinate (x,y,z).
    factors_sigma: list of tuples (v_x, M_yz, v_y, M_xz, v_z, M_xy)
    """
    sigma = 0.0
    for v_x, M_yz, v_y, M_xz, v_z, M_xy in factors_sigma:
        # X mode
        vx_val = linear_interp(v_x, x, len(v_x))
        myz_val = bilinear_interp(M_yz, y, z, M_yz.shape[0], M_yz.shape[1])
        sigma += vx_val * myz_val
        
        # Y mode
        vy_val = linear_interp(v_y, y, len(v_y))
        mxz_val = bilinear_interp(M_xz, x, z, M_xz.shape[0], M_xz.shape[1])
        sigma += vy_val * mxz_val
        
        # Z mode
        vz_val = linear_interp(v_z, z, len(v_z))
        mxy_val = bilinear_interp(M_xy, x, y, M_xy.shape[0], M_xy.shape[1])
        sigma += vz_val * mxy_val
    return sigma

def evaluate_appearance_features(x, y, z, factors_c, B):
    """
    Evaluate appearance feature vector G_c at continuous coordinate (x,y,z).
    factors_c: list of tuples (v_x, M_yz, v_y, M_xz, v_z, M_xy)
    B: Global appearance dictionary matrix (P x 3*R_c)
    """
    # Concatenate all A_c,r,m values
    # Order: r=1..Rc, m=X,Y,Z
    concat_vec = []
    for v_x, M_yz, v_y, M_xz, v_z, M_xy in factors_c:
        # X mode
        vx_val = linear_interp(v_x, x, len(v_x))
        myz_val = bilinear_interp(M_yz, y, z, M_yz.shape[0], M_yz.shape[1])
        concat_vec.append(vx_val * myz_val)
        
        # Y mode
        vy_val = linear_interp(v_y, y, len(v_y))
        mxz_val = bilinear_interp(M_xz, x, z, M_xz.shape[0], M_xz.shape[1])
        concat_vec.append(vy_val * mxz_val)
        
        # Z mode
        vz_val = linear_interp(v_z, z, len(v_z))
        mxy_val = bilinear_interp(M_xy, x, y, M_xy.shape[0], M_xy.shape[1])
        concat_vec.append(vz_val * mxy_val)
        
    # Matrix multiplication: G_c = B @ concat_vec
    # B is P x 3Rc, concat_vec is 3Rc
    features = [0.0] * B.shape[0]
    for i in range(B.shape[0]):
        dot = 0.0
        for j in range(len(concat_vec)):
            dot += B[i, j] * concat_vec[j]
        features[i] = dot
    return features

def render_ray(ray_origin, ray_direction, factors_sigma, factors_c, B, use_sh=True):
    """
    Differentiable volume rendering for a single ray.
    """
    Q = 32 # Number of shading points
    t_near = 0.0
    t_far = 1.0
    delta_t = (t_far - t_near) / Q

        t = t_near + (q + 0.5) * delta_t
        x = ray_origin + ray_direction * t

        weight = transmittance * alpha
        
        color[0] += weight * c[0]
        color[1] += weight * c[1]
        color[2] += weight * c[2]
        
        transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha)
        
        if transmittance < 1e-5:

    return color
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Overfitting:** Tensor factorization can lead to overfitting and local minima, especially with few input images. Regularization (L1 or TV) is critical [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Coarse-to-Fine:** Upsampling must be done carefully to avoid artifacts; linear/bilinear upsampling of factors is used [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Trilinear interpolation of factors is more efficient than interpolating the full grid, but requires correct handling of boundary conditions in interpolation functions [S1][S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### TensoRF-CP
Uses Canonical Polyadic (CP) decomposition. Simpler factorization, super compact modeling (<4MB for largest model), but lower rendering quality than VM [S1][S3, unofficial].

### TensoRF-VM
Uses Vector-Matrix (VM) decomposition. Compresses more parameters per component, achieving significantly better rendering quality and state-of-the-art PSNR with fewer steps [S1][S3, unofficial].

### TensoRF-SH
Uses Spherical Harmonics for the decoding function $S$. Provides view-dependent color without an MLP decoder [S1][S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reconstruction is significantly faster than NeRF. VM models with 300^3 voxels and 192 components take ~17.4 minutes (30k steps) on Synthetic NeRF, compared to NeRF's ~35 hours [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Model size is compact. TensoRF-CP-384 (500^3 voxels) is <4MB. TensoRF-VM-192 (300^3 voxels) is ~71.8MB [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On Synthetic NeRF, TensoRF-VM-192 achieves PSNR 33.14 and SSIM 0.963 [S1][S3, unofficial]. On NSVF, PSNR 36.52 [S1][S3, unofficial]. On Tanks & Temples, PSNR 28.56 [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** Tested on a single Tesla V100 GPU (16GB) [S1][S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast reconstruction, compact model size, high quality.
*   **Limitations:** Requires regular 3D grid, which may be memory-intensive for very large scenes compared to sparse representations. Performance degrades on scenes with very few views without TV regularization [S1][S3, unofficial].

## Background
TensoRF was introduced by Cheng Chen et al. in 2022 [S1][S3, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of representing radiance fields as tensors and factorizing them to reduce parameters, addressing the high computational cost and large model size of [NeRF](4972-neural-radiance-fields.md) [S1][S3, unofficial].
