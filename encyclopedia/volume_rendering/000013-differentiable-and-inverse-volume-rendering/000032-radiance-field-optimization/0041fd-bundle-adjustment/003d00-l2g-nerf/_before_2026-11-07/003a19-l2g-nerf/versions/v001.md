# L2G-NeRF

Also called: Local-to-Global Registration for Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
L2G-NeRF is a bundle-adjusting method that jointly optimizes neural field parameters and camera poses by combining a local photometric alignment objective with a global geometric alignment objective [S1]. It distinguishes itself from global registration methods like BARF by computing gradients with respect to local poses, which is more efficient and tolerates larger noise perturbations before divergence [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** A set of $M$ images $\{I_i\}_{i=1}^M$ with unknown camera poses; initial camera poses $T_i$ (typically identity); hyperparameters $\lambda, n_r, n_t, \{n_c\}$.
*   **Outputs:** Optimized neural field parameters $\Theta$ and optimized camera poses $\{T_i\}_{i=1}^M$.

**Data Structures**
*   **Camera Pose $T_i$:** Represented as a $4 \times 4$ transformation matrix in $SE(3)$, composed of rotation $R \in SO(3)$ and translation $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Pose Parametrization:** The rotation $R$ is generated via the exponential map from the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(3)$: $R = \exp(r)$, where $r \in \mathfrak{so}(3)$. The translation $t$ is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Noise Vectors:** Additive noise $r \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_r I)$ and $t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, n_t I)$ are sampled from normal distributions scaled by multipliers $n_r$ and $n_t$ [S1].

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize all camera poses to the identity transformation: $T_i = I$ for all $i \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ [S1].
2.  **Perturbation:** Perturb the initial poses using right multiplication to alter viewing directions and distances. This involves adding noise sampled from the distributions defined by $n_r$ and $n_t$ to the Lie algebra and translation components respectively [S1].
3.  **Optimization Loop:** Optimize $\Theta$ and $T_i$ by minimizing the combined local and global objectives. The convergence schedule linearly increases noise multipliers by a common factor $\{n_c\}_{c=2}^9$ starting from base values $n_r=0.01$ and $n_t=0.1$ [S1].
4.  **Gradient Computation:** Compute gradients for the local objective (photometric error) and the global objective (geometric alignment) [S1].
5.  **Update:** Update parameters using an optimizer (e.g., Adam) [G].

**Formulas**
*   **Local Objective ($L_{local}$):**
    $$ L_{local} = \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^N \| R(T_i x_j; \Theta) - I_i(x_j) \|^2 $$
    Where $R$ is the NeRF rendering function, $T_i$ is the camera pose, $x_j$ is the pixel coordinate, $I_i$ is the target image, $M$ is the number of images, and $N$ is the number of pixels [S1].

*   **Global Objective ($L_{global}$):**
    $$ L_{global} = \lambda \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^N \| T_{ji} x_j - T^*_i x_j \|^2 $$
    Where $\lambda$ is the global alignment multiplier, $T_{ji}$ is the transformed pose, and $T^*_i$ is the reference pose [S1].

*   **Total Loss:**
    $$ L = L_{local} + L_{global} $$

**Parameters**
*   $\lambda$ (global alignment multiplier): Balances geometric constraints against photometric reconstruction. Set to $1 \times 10^2$ for synthetic/object-centric scenes. For forward-facing LLFF scenes, float between $1 \times 10^2$ and $1 \times 10^5$ [S1].
*   $n_r$ (base rotation noise multiplier): Scales additive rotation noise. Scene-dependent values range from 0.01 to 0.09 [S1].
*   $n_t$ (base translation noise multiplier): Scales additive translation noise. Scene-dependent values range from 0.3 to 0.7 [S1].
*   $\{n_c\}_{c=2}^9$: Common factor for linearly increasing noise multipliers during convergence [S1].

**Pitfalls**
*   **Inward-facing 360° scenes:** Fails to recover camera poses from identity transformation for inward-facing 360° scenes with large rotation displacements [S1].
*   **Large $\lambda$:** A large $\lambda$ (e.g., $1 \times 10^5$) improves geometric accuracy but reduces robustness, potentially causing divergence on specific scenes like the Flower scene [S1].
*   **Random Initialization:** All methods (including L2G-NeRF) fail to converge in cases of random initialization with high noise [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** L2G-NeRF is more efficient than global registration methods like BARF because it computes gradients with respect to local poses rather than global poses [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** On an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 Ti, L2G-NeRF takes approximately 4.5 hours for synthetic objects and 8 hours for real-world scenes. In comparison, BARF takes 8 and 10.5 hours respectively [S1].
*   **Scaling:** L2G-NeRF tolerates larger noise perturbations before divergence compared to BARF (up to $n_c=8$ vs $n_c=4$) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to large camera pose misalignment; efficient gradient computation; competitive rendering quality compared to reference NeRF trained with ground-truth poses [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Fails on inward-facing 360° scenes with large rotation displacements; sensitive to large $\lambda$ values which can cause divergence [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera poses are unknown but initial estimates are close to identity (or can be perturbed from identity), and scenes are not inward-facing 360° with large rotations.
*   **When not to use:** For inward-facing 360° scenes with large rotation displacements; when random initialization is required [S1].

## Background
L2G-NeRF (Local-to-Global Registration for Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced to address the problem of learning neural fields of scenes and resolving large camera pose misalignment simultaneously [S1]. It establishes a unified formulation of bundle-adjusting neural fields, demonstrating benefits for both 2D and 3D neural fields [S1]. The method was evaluated on standard benchmarks including synthetic objects and the LLFF dataset (8 forward-facing real-world scenes) [S1].
