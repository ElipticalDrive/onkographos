# CBARF

Also called: Cascaded Bundle-Adjusting Neural Radiance Fields, Cascaded BA

## Overview
CBARF is a 3D reconstruction model that optimizes imperfect camera poses using a cascaded bundle-adjustment (BA) structure to progressively refine poses and rectify erroneous ones [S1]. It builds on [NeRF Training Loop](970-nerf-training-loop.md) by introducing a coarse-to-fine optimization strategy that biases learning towards initial high values, distinguishing it from standard BA methods that may overfit or fail to converge from large initial errors [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Reference camera poses $P \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}$, which are perturbed to create imperfect poses $\hat{P}$; a set of training images; and a NeRF backbone (specifically the BARF network architecture) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Refined camera poses $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}$ and a trained radiance field [S1].

**Data Structures**
*   **Camera Pose ($P$):** Represented as a $3 \times 4$ matrix $P = [R_p \ t_p]$, where $R_p \in SO(3)$ is the rotation matrix and $t_p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the translation vector. The last row of the standard $4 \times 4$ homogeneous matrix is dropped [S1].
*   **Noise/Refinement Parameters ($n, p$):** Represented as vectors in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{se}(3)$, dimension $n \in \mathbb{R}^6$ [S1].
*   **Transform Matrices ($N, P_r$):** $4 \times 4$ homogeneous transformation matrices derived from the Lie algebra vectors, denoted as $N = [R_n \ t_n]$ and $P_r = [R_r \ t_r]$ [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Noise Coefficient:** $0.35$ for generating initial imperfect poses (average deviation $\approx 30.4^\circ$ rotation, $0.56$ translation) [S1].
*   **Modulation Factors:** $10.0$ (coarse stage), $3.0$ (recursive stage), $1.0$ (fine stage) [S1].
*   **Iterations per Compact BA Module:** $20,000$ iterations for coarse and recursive stages; $200,000$ iterations for the fine stage [S1].
*   **Image Resolution:** $400 \times 400$ pixels during pose optimization (half of the standard $800 \times 800$ training resolution) [S1].

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Simulate Imperfect Poses:** Generate noise $n \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$ from a 6-dimensional normal distribution:
    $$ n = 0.35 \mathcal{N}(0, I) \in \mathbb{R}^6 $$
    Transform $n$ into a camera transform matrix $N = [R_n \ t_n]$. Compose $N$ with the reference pose $P = [R_p \ t_p]$ to obtain the imperfect pose $\hat{P}$:
    $$ \hat{P} = [R_p R_n \ t_p + t_n] $$
    [S1]

2.  **Initialize Cascaded BA:** Structure the optimization into three stages: Coarse, Recursive, and Fine. Use the BARF network as the backbone for each compact BA module [S1].

3.  **Optimize Poses (Per Stage):**
    *   Train camera refinement parameters $p \in \mathfrak{se}(3)$.
    *   Convert $p$ into a transform matrix $P_r = [R_r \ t_r]$.
    *   Compose $P_r$ with the current imperfect pose $\hat{P}$ (or the output of the previous stage) to obtain refined poses $Q$:
        $$ Q = [R_p R_n R_r \ t_p + t_n + t_r] $$
    *   Calculate the learning rate using exponential interpolation with a stage-specific modulation factor $\alpha$. A larger $\alpha$ biases the learning rate towards the initial high value. The effective learning rate $\eta(t)$ at iteration $t$ follows the exponential decay modulated by $\alpha$ [S1].
    *   Run the optimization for the specified iteration count ($20k$ or $200k$) at $400 \times 400$ resolution [S1].

4.  **Handle Incomplete Poses (Optional):** If camera poses are incomplete, randomly drop $10\%$ of poses to form a test set $F$. Assign an initial pose $P_{ini}$ to $F$. Jointly optimize poses for the training set $T$ and test set $F$ during training [S1].

5.  **Evaluation Alignment:** Apply Procrustes analysis to align the optimized poses with the ground-truth poses before calculating evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, rotation/translation error) to remove global coordinate system offsets [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Overfitting:** Standard BA methods may overfit before completing optimization if the initial error is large; CBARF mitigates this via the cascaded structure [S1].
*   **Global Offset:** Optimized poses may have a different coordinate system origin/orientation than ground truth. Procrustes analysis is required for evaluation but does not correct the internal NeRF representation's coordinate alignment if not handled consistently [S1].
*   **Incomplete Poses:** Randomly dropping poses requires joint optimization of training and test poses, which increases computational cost and complexity [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The cascaded structure increases total training time compared to single-stage BA. The coarse and recursive stages each run for 20k iterations, and the fine stage for 200k iterations [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses half resolution ($400 \times 400$) during pose optimization to improve efficiency, reducing memory footprint compared to full-resolution training [S1].
*   **Performance:** Successfully optimizes camera poses with large initial errors (30.4° rotation, 0.56 translation) where BARF fails [S1]. Rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) is comparable to ground-truth pose rendering [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to large initial pose errors; handles incomplete poses; prevents overfitting through coarse-to-fine strategy.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of modulation factors and iteration counts; Procrustes alignment is needed for fair evaluation but adds a post-processing step [S1].

## Background
CBARF was proposed to address the limitations of existing bundle-adjustment methods in NeRF training, which often fail to converge from imperfect camera poses. It builds on the BARF (Blending Activation for Radiance Fields) method, which introduced exponential interpolation for pose optimization [S1]. The method was introduced in a 2024 paper published in the IEEE Transactions on Multimedia [S1].
