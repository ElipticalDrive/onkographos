# Standard Rendering Metrics

Also called: Evaluation Metrics: PSNR and SSIM, FID, KID, LPIPS, MS-SSIM, PSNR, PSNR and SSIM, Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, SSIM, Structural Similarity Index

## Overview
Standard rendering metrics quantify the fidelity of rendered images against ground-truth targets to evaluate the quality of radiance-field optimizations. PSNR provides a cheap, pixel-wise error measurement, while SSIM and MS-SSIM offer structural comparisons; LPIPS provides learned perceptual similarity, and FID/KID evaluate generative distributional quality [S2, unofficial]. These metrics are distinct from geometry metrics (e.g., Chamfer distance) and are primarily used for novel view synthesis evaluation [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Rendered image tensor $\hat{I}$ of shape $[H, W, 3]$ (RGB).
- Target (ground-truth) image tensor $I$ of shape $[H, W, 3]$ (RGB).
- Both tensors must be in the same color space and range (typically $[0, 1]$ or $[0, 255]$).

**Outputs:**
- Scalar PSNR value (in dB).
- Scalar SSIM value (in $[0, 1]$).
- Optional: LPIPS, FID, or KID scores depending on the evaluation suite.

**Data Structures:**
- Images are stored as dense arrays. For PSNR/SSIM, direct pixel access is required. For FID/KID, feature vectors extracted from a pre-trained Inception network are required.

**Steps:**

1. **Preprocessing:** Ensure $\hat{I}$ and $I$ are aligned in resolution and color space. If $\hat{I}$ is a tensor from a deep learning framework, reshape it to $[H, W, 3]$ and convert to a standard numerical format (e.g., NumPy array) [S1, unofficial].
2. **MSE Calculation:** Compute the Mean Squared Error between the predicted and target images:
   $$ MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{I}_i - I_i)^2 $$
   where $N = H \times W \times 3$ is the total number of pixels, and $\hat{I}_i, I_i$ are the scalar values of the $i$-th element (channel-wise) [S1, unofficial].
3. **PSNR Calculation:** Compute the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio:
   $$ PSNR = -10 \log_{10}(MSE) $$
   If the pixel range is $[0, 255]$, the formula is often adjusted to $PSNR = 10 \log_{10}(\frac{MAX_I^2}{MSE})$ where $MAX_I = 255$ [S1, G, unofficial].
4. **SSIM Calculation:** Compute the Structural Similarity Index using a dedicated function `compute_ssim(rgb, target)` [S1, unofficial]. This typically involves sliding windows to compare local structures, luminance, and contrast [S2, unofficial].
5. **Advanced Metrics (Optional):**
   - **LPIPS:** Pass image patches through a pre-trained network (e.g., VGG or AlexNet) and compute the weighted $L_2$ distance between feature activations [S2, unofficial].
   - **FID/KID:** Render crops from the generated images and training images, extract Inception features, and compute the Fréchet distance or Kernel Inception Distance between the feature distributions [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- **SSIM Window Size:** Typically $11 \times 11$ or $3 \times 3$ depending on implementation.
- **LPIPS Network:** VGG or AlexNet backbone.
- **FID/KID Inception Model:** Inception-v3 trained on ImageNet.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Dynamic Range:** Ensure consistent normalization. PSNR is highly sensitive to scaling errors.
- **Black Pixels:** If images contain pure black ($0$) and the range is $[0, 1]$, MSE can be non-zero even for perfect matches if there is slight noise, leading to finite PSNR. [G]
- **Perceptual Insensitivity:** PSNR correlates poorly with human perception; high PSNR does not guarantee high visual quality [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** PSNR and SSIM are $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels. They are computationally cheap [S2, unofficial]. LPIPS and FID/KID require forward passes through deep networks, making them significantly slower ($O(N \cdot C)$ where $C$ is the cost of the network inference).
- **Memory Complexity:** PSNR/SSIM require storing only the image tensors. FID/KID require storing feature vectors for all images in memory or streaming them to compute statistics.
- **Strengths:** PSNR is standard and easy to interpret. SSIM captures structural degradation. LPIPS correlates better with human judgment [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** PSNR is insensitive to perceptual quality [S2, unofficial]. FID/KID are designed for generative models and may not be suitable for deterministic novel view synthesis evaluation without careful crop selection [S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Use PSNR/SSIM for standard benchmarking (e.g., Mip-NeRF 360, Blender datasets) [S2, unofficial]. Use LPIPS for perceptual quality assessment. Use FID/KID when evaluating generative variants or large-scale diversity [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Standard rendering metrics have been adopted from image processing and computer vision communities. PSNR and SSIM are long-standing metrics. In the context of 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRFs, they are used as standard companions to evaluate novel view synthesis quality [S2, unofficial]. Benchmarks like Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, and Deep Blending rely on these metrics for comparison [S2, unofficial].
