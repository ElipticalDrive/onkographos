# Normal-L2 Score Computation
Also called: Masked Normal L2, Normal Error Metric
## Overview
The Normal-L2 score quantifies the geometric fidelity of a predicted surface by measuring the L2 distance between predicted surface normals and reference normals. It serves as a shape-aware alternative to RGB-based metrics like MSE, focusing on shading cues rather than color accuracy [S1]. It is distinct from PSNR and SSIM, which evaluate pixel intensity and structural similarity respectively, by operating on vector fields derived from surface geometry [S1, 2742-psnr-metric-calculation.md, 3650-ssim-metric-calculation.md].
## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Predicted surface normals $N_{pred}$: A tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ containing normalized normal vectors for each pixel.
- Reference surface normals $N_{ref}$: A tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ containing interpolated normal vectors from a ground-truth mesh.
- Predicted mask $M_{pred}$: A binary tensor of shape $(H, W)$ indicating pixels belonging to the predicted surface.
- Ground-truth mask $M_{gt}$: A binary tensor of shape $(H, W)$ indicating pixels belonging to the ground-truth surface.

**Outputs:**
- Normal-L2 score: A scalar value representing the mean squared error of the normal vectors over the valid region.

**Data Structures:**
- Masks are typically boolean or float tensors where 1 indicates surface presence and 0 indicates background.
- Normals are 3D unit vectors.

**Steps:**
1. **Compute Intersection Mask:** Calculate the intersection of the predicted and ground-truth masks to ensure normals are only compared where both surfaces exist.
   $$ M_{valid} = M_{pred} \land M_{gt} $$
   [G] In implementation, this is often done via logical AND or element-wise multiplication followed by a threshold check.
2. **Extract Valid Normals:** Select normal vectors from $N_{pred}$ and $N_{ref}$ where $M_{valid}$ is true.
   $$ N_{pred}^{valid} = \{ n \in N_{pred} \mid M_{valid}(u,v) = 1 \} $$
   $$ N_{ref}^{valid} = \{ n \in N_{ref} \mid M_{valid}(u,v) = 1 \} $$
3. **Compute Squared Differences:** Calculate the squared Euclidean distance between corresponding valid normal vectors.
   $$ D_{uv} = \| N_{pred}^{valid}(u,v) - N_{ref}^{valid}(u,v) \|^2 $$
   $$ D_{uv} = (n_{pred,x} - n_{ref,x})^2 + (n_{pred,y} - n_{ref,y})^2 + (n_{pred,z} - n_{ref,z})^2 $$
4. **Average:** Compute the mean of $D_{uv}$ over all valid pixels.
   $$ \text{Normal-L2} = \frac{1}{|M_{valid}|} \sum_{(u,v) \in M_{valid}} D_{uv} $$

**Parameters:**
- Camera positions: 32 evenly distributed fixed positions using a spherical Fibonacci sequence with radius 4 [S1].
- Render resolution: 512x512 [S1].
- Samples per pixel: 4 [S1].
- Renderer: Mitsuba 2 is used for mesh rendering [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Mask Misalignment:** If masks do not overlap, the denominator becomes zero. The metric is only evaluated on the intersection [S1].
- **Normal Orientation:** Normals must be consistently oriented (e.g., outward-facing) to avoid large errors due to sign flips.
- **Interpolation:** Reference normals are interpolated from the reference mesh, which may introduce artifacts at low resolutions or sharp edges [S1].
- **Numerical Stability:** Normals should be normalized before comparison to ensure unit length.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_normal_l2_score(n_pred, n_ref, mask_pred, mask_gt):
    """
    Compute Normal-L2 score.
    
    Args:
        n_pred: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), predicted normals.
        n_ref: Tensor of shape (H, W, 3), reference normals.
        mask_pred: Tensor of shape (H, W), predicted mask (1.0 for surface, 0.0 otherwise).
        mask_gt: Tensor of shape (H, W), ground-truth mask (1.0 for surface, 0.0 otherwise).
        
    Returns:
        float: Normal-L2 score.
    """
    # Step 1: Compute intersection mask
    # Ensure masks are boolean or float 0/1
    valid_mask = (mask_pred > 0.5) & (mask_gt > 0.5)
    
    # Step 2: Extract valid normals
    # Use boolean indexing to get valid normals
    valid_n_pred = n_pred[valid_mask]
    valid_n_ref = n_ref[valid_mask]
    
    # Check if any valid pixels exist
    if valid_n_pred.numel() == 0:
        return 0.0 # Or raise an error, depending on convention
        
    # Step 3: Compute squared differences
    # Difference vector
    diff = valid_n_pred - valid_n_ref
    # Squared L2 norm per pixel
    squared_diff = torch.sum(diff ** 2, dim=-1)
    
    # Step 4: Average
    normal_l2 = torch.mean(squared_diff)
    
    return normal_l2.item()
```
## Subtopics

### KL Normal Loss
Computes the Kullback-Leibler divergence between a standard normal distribution and a diagonal Gaussian parameterized by the mean and variance of an output embedding tensor. It is used to regularize the latent space of implicit surface models.
- **Definition:** Measures the divergence of the distribution of latent embeddings from a standard normal prior.
- **Steps:**
  1. Compute mean $\mu_d$ and standard deviation $\sigma_d$ along dimension 0 for each dimension $d$ of the output embedding tensor.
  2. Compute variance $\sigma_d^2 = \sigma_d^2$.
  3. Compute KL per dimension: $kl_d = 0.5 (\mu_d^2 + \sigma_d^2 - \ln(\sigma_d^2 + \epsilon) - 1)$.
  4. Sum over all dimensions: $KL = \sum_d kl_d$.
- **Formula:**
  $$ KL = \sum_{d} 0.5 \left( \mu_d^2 + \sigma_d^2 - \ln(\sigma_d^2 + \epsilon) - 1 \right) $$
  where $\mu_d$ is the mean along dimension 0 for dimension $d$, $\sigma_d^2$ is the variance, and $\epsilon = 1e-8$ for numerical stability.
- **When to choose:** Use when regularizing latent embeddings in generative models (e.g., VAEs) rather than evaluating surface geometry.
- **Link:** [KL Normal Loss](2742-kl-normal-loss.md) (Note: No separate article exists, so this is a subtopic entry).

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(H \times W)$ for mask intersection and normal comparison, where $H$ and $W$ are image dimensions. Rendering normals takes additional time depending on the renderer (e.g., Mitsuba 2) [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(H \times W)$ to store normal tensors and masks [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** Evaluated at 512x512 resolution with 4 samples per pixel [S1].
- **Strengths:** Sensitive to surface orientation errors, which are perceptually important for shading [S1]. Complements RGB metrics by focusing on geometry [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires ground-truth mesh normals for comparison, which may not always be available. Sensitive to mask alignment errors [S1].
- **When to use:** When evaluating geometric accuracy of implicit surface reconstructions, especially when shading quality is important [S1].
- **When not to use:** When only RGB fidelity is of interest, or when ground-truth normals are unavailable.
## Background
The Normal-L2 score was introduced in the context of Neural Geometric Level of Detail (NGLOD) for real-time rendering with implicit 3D shapes [S1]. It was used alongside intersection over union (iIoU) to evaluate shape silhouettes and surface normals, as these drive shading and are perceptually important [S1]. The metric is implemented using Mitsuba 2 for mesh rendering [S1].
