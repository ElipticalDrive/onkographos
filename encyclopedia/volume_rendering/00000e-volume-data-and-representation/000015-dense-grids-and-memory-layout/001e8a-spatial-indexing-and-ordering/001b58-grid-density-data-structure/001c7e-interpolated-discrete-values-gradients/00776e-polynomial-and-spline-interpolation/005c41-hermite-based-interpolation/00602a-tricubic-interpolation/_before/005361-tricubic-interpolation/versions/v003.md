# Tricubic Interpolation

Also called: Tricubic Hermite Interpolation

## Overview
Tricubic interpolation is a higher-order method that employs cubic polynomials over a 4x4x4 neighborhood of 64 points to approximate smooth underlying functions with an accuracy of $O(h^4)$, where $h$ is the grid spacing [S1]. It is distinguished from its sibling [Trilinear Interpolation](12781-voxel-data-interpolation-and-sampling.md) by providing $C^1$ continuity, which reduces artifacts in gradient-sensitive tasks like volume rendering, at the cost of increased computational expense due to the larger support and coefficient count [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A dense grid tensor $\mathbf{G}$ of shape $(D_x, D_y, D_z, C)$ containing scalar values and their first-order spatial derivatives (gradients) [S2, unofficial].
- Input: A query point $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
- Output: The interpolated scalar value $f(\mathbf{p})$ and optionally its gradient $\nabla f(\mathbf{p})$.

**Data Structures:**
The grid $\mathbf{G}$ must store not only the value $V$ at each voxel but also the partial derivatives $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial V}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial V}{\partial z}$ [S2, unofficial]. For a single channel, each voxel stores 4 floats: $[V, V_x, V_y, V_z]$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Index Mapping:** Determine the integer indices $(i, j, k)$ of the grid cell containing $\mathbf{p}$ such that $i \le p_x < i+1$, etc. Compute local coordinates $(u, v, w) \in [0, 1]^3$ where $u = p_x - i$, $v = p_y - j$, $w = p_z - k$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Data Gathering:** Retrieve the 64 control points from the 4x4x4 neighborhood surrounding the cell $(i, j, k)$. For each corner $(i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z)$ where $\delta \in \{-1, 0\}$, gather the value and its three partial derivatives [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Basis Evaluation:** Compute the 1D cubic Hermite basis functions for each dimension. The basis functions $H_0(t)$ and $H_1(t)$ map the local coordinate $t \in [0, 1]$ to weights for the value and the derivative, respectively [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Weighted Summation:** Compute the interpolated value using the tensor product of the basis functions and the gathered data.

**Formulas:**
The 1D cubic Hermite basis functions are defined as [S2, unofficial]:
$$ H_0(t) = 2t^3 - 3t^2 + 1 $$
$$ H_1(t) = t^3 - 2t^2 + t $$

The interpolated value $f(u, v, w)$ is calculated as:
$$ f(u, v, w) = \sum_{\delta_x=0}^{1} \sum_{\delta_y=0}^{1} \sum_{\delta_z=0}^{1} \left( H_{\delta_x}(u) H_{\delta_y}(v) H_{\delta_z}(w) \cdot V_{i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z} \right) + \dots $$
*Note: The full expansion includes cross-terms involving the derivatives. A more compact matrix form is often used:*

$$ f(u, v, w) = \mathbf{H}(u)^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{G}_{local} \mathbf{M}^T \mathbf{H}(v) \mathbf{M}^T \mathbf{H}(w) $$
Where $\mathbf{H}(t) = [1, t, t^2, t^3]^T$ is the monomial basis vector, $\mathbf{M}$ is the Hermite basis matrix, and $\mathbf{G}_{local}$ is the 4x4x4 tensor of values and derivatives.

Alternatively, using the explicit scalar expansion for clarity in implementation [S2, unofficial]:
Let $H_0(t)$ and $H_1(t)$ be the basis functions defined above.
Let $V_{ijk}$ be the value at grid point $(i,j,k)$.
Let $Vx_{ijk}, Vy_{ijk}, Vz_{ijk}$ be the partial derivatives.

The contribution of a single corner $(i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z)$ to the final sum is:
$$ \text{Term} = H_{\delta_x}(u) H_{\delta_y}(v) H_{\delta_z}(w) V_{i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z} $$
$$ + H_{\delta_x}(u) H_{\delta_y}(v) H_{\delta_z+1}(w) \Delta w \cdot Vz_{i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z} $$
$$ + H_{\delta_x}(u) H_{\delta_y+1}(v) H_{\delta_z}(w) \Delta v \cdot Vy_{i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z} $$
$$ + H_{\delta_x+1}(u) H_{\delta_y}(v) H_{\delta_z}(w) \Delta u \cdot Vx_{i+\delta_x, j+\delta_y, k+\delta_z} $$
*(Note: The indices for the derivative terms shift by 1 in the corresponding dimension to align with the Hermite basis definition where $H_1$ corresponds to the derivative weight. The $\Delta$ terms are typically 1 if the grid spacing is uniform and incorporated into the derivative storage or basis scaling.)*

**Parameters:**
- Grid spacing $h$: Implicit in the derivative values. If derivatives are not pre-scaled by $h$, they must be multiplied by the local coordinate step size.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Boundary Handling:** Indices outside $[0, D_d-1]$ must be clamped or padded [S1][S2, unofficial].
- **Precision:** The 4x4x4 support requires careful handling of floating-point precision during the accumulation of 64 terms.
- **Derivative Availability:** This method requires pre-computed or analytically available gradients. If only values are available, finite differences must be used to estimate gradients, which may introduce noise [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Grid Cell Collection via Corner Indices
Focuses on retrieving the 8 corner values for trilinear interpolation, which is the lower-order sibling. Choose this when performance is critical and $C^0$ continuity is sufficient. [1518-grid-cell-collection-via-corner-indices.md]

### Piecewise Quadratic Hermite Interpolation
Uses quadratic polynomials, offering $C^1$ continuity with a smaller support than tricubic. Choose this for a balance between smoothness and computational cost. [4001-piecewise-quadratic-hermite-interpolation.md]

### Hermite Interpolation of Scalar Fields
Generalizes the Hermite approach to 1D or 2D, or specific scalar field constraints. Choose this when the data is not strictly volumetric or requires specific boundary conditions. [9450-hermite-interpolation-of-scalar-fields.md]

### B-spline Field Interpolation
Uses B-spline basis functions which have compact support and $C^{k-1}$ continuity for degree $k$. Choose this for smoother results with better numerical stability than Hermite, though it requires more points (e.g., 4x4x4 for cubic B-spline). [11066-b-spline-field-interpolation.md]

### Voxel Data Interpolation and Sampling

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per query, but with a significantly higher constant factor than trilinear due to 64 point accesses and cubic polynomial evaluations.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing gradients alongside values, increasing memory usage by 4x for scalar fields (value + 3 derivatives).
- **Measured Performance:** Studies confirm tricubic's edge in accuracy over trilinear for upsampled 3D volumes, albeit with longer processing times [S1].
- **Strengths:** $C^1$ continuity reduces visual artifacts in gradient-sensitive tasks like volume rendering [S1]. High accuracy $O(h^4)$ for smooth functions [S1].
- **Limitations:** High computational cost. Requires gradient data, which may need to be estimated or pre-computed.
- **When to use:** When $C^1$ continuity is needed for differentiable outputs in visualization [S1].
- **When not to use:** When performance is critical and $C^0$ continuity is sufficient (use trilinear). When gradient data is unavailable and cannot be estimated reliably.

## Background
Tricubic interpolation is a higher-order alternative to trilinear interpolation, developed to address the need for smoother approximations in scientific visualization and medical imaging. It is based on the cubic Hermite spline concept, which uses both function values and derivatives to construct smooth patches. The method has been applied in contexts such as irradiance volume computation for games and medical imaging upsampling [S1][S2, unofficial].
