# Polynomial and Spline Interpolation
## Overview
Polynomial and Spline Interpolation provides continuous scalar or vector fields from discrete grid data by fitting piecewise polynomials or splines to the neighborhood of a query point [S1][S2, unofficial]. It solves the discontinuity and aliasing artifacts inherent in nearest-neighbor sampling by ensuring $C^0$ or higher continuity across cell boundaries, enabling smooth gradient-based shading and accurate feature extraction in volume rendering [S1][S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Nearest Voxel Lookup with Periodic Boundary](1573-nearest-voxel-lookup-with-periodic-boundary.md) by providing differentiability and from [Dense Copy Extraction](3215-dense-copy-extraction.md) by reconstructing values between grid vertices rather than just retrieving them.
## Shared principles
The family operates on a dense grid tensor $\mathbf{G}$ of shape $(D_x, D_y, D_z, C)$, where $D$ are dimensions and $C$ is the channel count (e.g., density, color, or geometry parameters) [S1][S2, unofficial]. For any query point $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the method first maps $\mathbf{p}$ to local cell coordinates $(u, v, w) \in [0, 1]^3$ relative to the enclosing grid cell defined by integer indices $(i, j, k)$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. The interpolated value is computed as a weighted sum of basis functions $B$ applied to the gathered data from the cell's vertices or control points:
$$ f(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{l,m,n} B_{l,m,n}(u,v,w) \cdot \mathbf{G}_{i+l, j+m, k+n} $$
where the indices $l,m,n$ depend on the support size of the specific interpolation kernel (e.g., $0,1$ for trilinear/Hermite, $0..3$ for B-splines) [S1][S2, unofficial]. Boundary conditions (clamping, periodic, or constant) are applied to indices that fall outside the grid domain $[0, D_d-1]$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
## Subtopics

### [Grid Cell Collection via Corner Indices](1518-grid-cell-collection-via-corner-indices.md)
Grid Cell Collection via Corner Indices retrieves the eight vertex values of the grid cell enclosing a query point, serving as the foundational data gathering step for trilinear interpolation in dense voxel grids. It sets itself apart from higher-order methods like [B-spline Field Interpolation](11066-b-spline-field-interpolation.md) by strictly limiting the support to the immediate 8 neighbors of the enclosing cell, and from [VoxelGrid](2159-voxelgrid.md) by focusing on the index computation and gathering logic rather than the storage structure itself. Choose this when implementing standard trilinear interpolation or when the subsequent step requires explicit access to corner values for custom weighting.

### [Piecewise Quadratic Hermite Interpolation](4001-piecewise-quadratic-hermite-interpolation.md)
A method for interpolating scalar fields within a 1D subinterval using two piecewise quadratic Bézier curves to satisfy Hermite data (value and derivative) at both endpoints. It solves the incompatibility of single quadratic interpolation with four constraints (two values, two derivatives) by splitting the interval, providing exact interpolation for normalized fields and better stability for small subdivisions compared to cubic methods. Choose this when $C^0$ continuity with derivative constraints is required in 1D, or as a component in higher-dimensional tensor products where cubic overshoot is undesirable.

### [Hermite Interpolation of Scalar Fields](9450-hermite-interpolation-of-scalar-fields.md)
Hermite interpolation of scalar fields renders scalar-valued functions by storing partial derivatives alongside function values on a regular grid. Unlike standard trilinear interpolation which yields only $C^0$ continuity, this method guarantees a globally $C^1$-continuous result by using Hermite interpolation between samples. It serves as a storage-efficient replacement for normal mapping, as surface normals can be derived directly from the stored gradient data without requiring extra storage. Choose this when gradient-based shading is critical and memory allows storing derivatives per vertex.

### [B-spline Field Interpolation](11066-b-spline-field-interpolation.md)
B-spline Field Interpolation is a method for evaluating a continuous scalar or vector field from a discrete grid of control points using cubic B-spline basis functions. It solves the problem of reconstructing a smooth, differentiable function from sparse samples while maintaining local support, ensuring that the value at any query point depends only on a fixed, small neighborhood of grid vertices. It is distinguished from its siblings, such as [Voxel Data Interpolation and Sampling](12781-voxel-data-interpolation-and-sampling.md) (trilinear) and [Hermite Interpolation of Scalar Fields](9450-hermite-interpolation-of-scalar-fields.md), by providing $C^2$ continuity (continuous second derivatives) and a wider support kernel (4x4x4 points) that blends neighboring weights more smoothly, preventing the "blocky" artifacts of linear interpolation. Choose this for high-quality smooth reconstruction where $C^2$ continuity is beneficial and computational cost of a 64-point kernel is acceptable.

### [Voxel Data Interpolation and Sampling](12781-voxel-data-interpolation-and-sampling.md)
Voxel Data Interpolation and Sampling is the method for retrieving scalar values, gradients, and transformed colors at arbitrary 3D positions within a dense volume grid. It solves the problem of mapping continuous ray-casting coordinates to discrete grid data, supporting various interpolation modes (nearest neighbor, trilinear) and transfer function input configurations. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its role as the fundamental access layer for dense grids, often exposing both interpolated values (`Sample Grid`) and raw integer-indexed values (`Sample Grid Index`). Choose this as the default sampling primitive for standard volume rendering pipelines.

### [Third Order Elimination (Scaling)](13712-third-order-elimination-scaling.md)
Third Order Elimination (Scaling) is a quadratic interpolation method that derives a second-order polynomial from a cubic Hermite basis by scaling the input derivatives to force the third-order coefficient to zero. It solves the problem of approximating a function with derivative data while reducing the polynomial order to avoid the overshoot or instability associated with cubic terms, specifically when the derivatives do not cancel each other out. It is distinguished from [B-spline Field Interpolation](11066-b-spline-field-interpolation.md) by its explicit elimination of the cubic term via scalar multiplication of derivatives, and from [Piecewise Quadratic Hermite Interpolation](4001-piecewise-quadratic-hermite-interpolation.md) by its derivation from a cubic constraint rather than a least-squares fit of derivatives. Choose this when cubic Hermite overshoot is problematic but derivative information is available.

### [Tricubic Interpolation](14963-tricubic-interpolation.md)
Tricubic interpolation is a higher-order method that employs cubic polynomials over a 4x4x4 neighborhood of 64 points to approximate smooth underlying functions with an accuracy of $O(h^4)$, where $h$ is the grid spacing. It provides $C^1$ continuity, reducing artifacts in gradient-sensitive tasks like volume rendering, whereas trilinear interpolation only achieves $C^0$ continuity. It is chosen when differentiable outputs are required for visualization. Choose this for high-fidelity volume rendering where smooth gradients are visually critical and performance permits the 64-tap kernel.

### [NURBS Spline Representation for Measurement Coordinates](16136-nurbs-spline-representation-for-measurement-coordinates.md)
This method represents uncertain measurement coordinates (such as view angles and detector locations) as a NURBS surface whose control points and weights are optimized alongside volume reconstruction. It differs from standard interpolation by treating the geometric parameters of the acquisition as learnable variables within a rational basis function framework, allowing for the correction of measurement noise and uncertainty during the reconstruction process. Choose this when the input geometry or sampling coordinates are uncertain and can be optimized as part of a differentiable rendering pipeline.

### Box Spline Interpolation
**Definition:** A piecewise polynomial interpolation function with finite support, uniquely defined by a direction matrix, used for reconstructing continuous splines from discrete datasets [S1].
**Steps:** Construct the box-spline by taking consecutive directional convolutions along each column direction of the direction matrix $\Xi$ [S1]. For non-Cartesian lattices, apply a change-of-variables to the Cartesian lattice theory [S1].
**Formula:** Recursive definition: $M_{\Xi \cup \{\mathbf{d}\}}(\mathbf{x}) = \int_0^1 M_\Xi(\mathbf{x} - t\mathbf{d}) dt, \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ (Eq 6) [S1].
**Comparison:** Provides higher quality and reasonable performance compared to trilinear or tricubic interpolations commonly used on mobile devices [S1].
**Usage:** Mobile volume rendering, FCC datasets, GPU ray casting [S1].

### Distance Weighting Filter
**Definition:** A spatial interpolation filter that assigns weights based on distance to preserve data structure during grid mapping [S2, unofficial].
**Parameter:** Filter radius determines the spatial extent of the weighting; must be shortened to increase sampling rate [S2, unofficial].
**Pitfall:** Assumes a constant, second-order distribution of weighting terms based on the filter radius; under-samples areas with high data frequency, causing inner and outer shells to disappear where data density varies [S2, unofficial].
**Comparison:** Performs worse than the Gaussian filter for preserving accuracy because it lacks a degree of freedom for adjusting sampling frequency independently of radius [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The family encompasses classical interpolation techniques used in computer graphics and scientific visualization. Trilinear and cubic Hermite interpolation are standard methods for dense grid sampling [S1][S2, unofficial]. B-splines, introduced by de Boor et al., provide $C^2$ continuity and are widely used for smooth surface reconstruction [S1]. Box splines, also introduced by de Boor et al., are multivariate generalizations of uniform B-splines specifically useful for non-Cartesian lattices like Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) datasets, offering high quality on mobile platforms [S1]. Distance weighting filters have been used in A-buffer sampling to preserve data structure, though they suffer from under-sampling in high-frequency regions [S2, unofficial].
