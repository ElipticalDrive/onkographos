# NURBS-based Uncalibrated Imaging Reconstruction

Also called: Spline method

## Overview
This method reconstructs volumetric data from uncalibrated measurements by representing uncertain measurement coordinates (view angles, tilt angles, detector locations) as a NURBS surface, optimizing control points alongside the volume representation [S1, unofficial]. It differs from implicit neural representations by using fixed-degree polynomial splines to model measurement uncertainty, enforcing physical consistency conditions to handle the periodic nature of angular data [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Raw measurement data (projections/sinograms).
*   Initial estimates of measurement coordinates (view angles $\theta$, detector locations $t$, tilt angles).
*   Ground truth or synthetic data for training the reconstruction network.

**Outputs:**
*   Reconstructed volume (via a Unet network $G_\mu$).
*   Optimized NURBS control points and weights defining the measurement coordinate mapping.

**Data Structures:**
*   **NURBS Surface:** Defined by control points $p_{j,k}$ and basis functions.
    *   For 2D CT: Control points map view angle $\theta$ and detector location $t$ to measurement values.
    *   For 3D CT: Control points map tilt angle and two detector location dimensions.
*   **Unet Network:** $G_\mu$ takes measurement coordinates and raw data to predict voxel values.
    *   2D Unet: 1 input channel, 1 output channel, 16 features in first layer.
    *   3D Unet: 1 input channel, 1 output channel, 16 features in first layer, depth of 4 blocks.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Initialize NURBS Control Points:**
    *   Create initial control points based on observed measurement coordinates.
    *   For 2D CT with view angle uncertainty, create additional control points to ensure sufficient support around $0$ and $\pi$ radians.
    *   Let $J$ be the number of view angles and $d_\Theta$ be the NURBS degree in the view angle dimension.
    *   Create $2 d_\Theta K$ additional control points for $J - d_\Theta < j \le J$, where $K$ is the number of detectors.
    *   The new control points are defined by the consistency conditions:
        $$ p_{j+J-1,k} = [\theta_j + \pi, t_k, y([\theta_{e_j}, -t_{e_k}]^T)]^T $$
        $$ p_{j-J-1,k} = [\theta_j - \pi, t_k, y([\theta_{e_j}, -t_{e_k}]^T)]^T $$
        where $-t$ refers to the $t$-th last detector [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Enforce Consistency Conditions:**
    *   The NURBS surface $r_\phi$ must satisfy:
        $$ r_\phi([\theta + \pi, t]^T) = r_\phi([\theta, -t]^T) $$
        $$ r_\phi([\theta - \pi, t]^T) = r_\phi([\theta, -t]^T) $$
    *   This encodes the circular nature of angular data and ensures accuracy in the interval $[0, \pi]$ radians [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Configure NURBS Degrees:**
    *   **2D CT:**
        *   Degree along the measurement coordinate dimension being learned: 18.
        *   Degree along the measurement coordinate dimension not being learned: 2.
    *   **3D CT:**
        *   Degree along the tilt angle dimension: 15.
        *   Degree in the two detector location dimensions: 2.
    *   **3D CT Specific:** Fix basis function weights to one and do not optimize them, converting the NURBS surface to a B-spline surface [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Train Reconstruction Unet:**
    *   Minimize mean-squared error loss using the Adam optimizer.
    *   Train for 100 epochs (one epoch = full pass through training dataset).
    *   **2D CT Data Augmentation:** Random horizontal flips, random vertical flips, random rotations by 90, 180, or 270 degrees.
    *   **3D CT Data Augmentation:** Same as 2D, due to limited training dataset size [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Optimize Parameters:**
    *   Optimize Unet parameters $\mu$ and NURBS control points simultaneously.
    *   **Regularization:** Apply regularization parameter $\lambda$.
        *   For splines (NURBS/B-spline): $\lambda = 0.6$.
        *   For implicit neural representations (for comparison): $\lambda = 0.1$.
    *   **Learning Rates:**
        *   **Neural Network Parameters:**
            *   When splines are used: $5 \times 10^{-3}$.
            *   When implicit neural representations are used: $1 \times 10^{-3}$.
        *   **Input Coordinates (NURBS control points):** $1 \times 10^{-4}$ (for both methods).
    *   **Unet Specific Learning Rates:**
        *   2D Unet batch size: 128, learning rate: $1 \times 10^{-3}$.
        *   3D Unet batch size: 16, learning rate: $1 \times 10^{-3}$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time depends on the Unet architecture and the number of NURBS control points. 2D Unet batch size 128, 3D Unet batch size 16 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the number of control points ($2 d_\Theta K$ additional points for 2D) and the Unet parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Outperforms baseline and implicit neural methods in terms of reconstruction SNR under measurement coordinate uncertainty [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles angular data periodicity via consistency conditions; B-spline simplification in 3D reduces optimization complexity [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful selection of NURBS degrees; data augmentation is critical for 3D due to limited datasets [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated in the paper "Differentiable Uncalibrated Imaging" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of representing measurement uncertainty as a smooth surface (NURBS) to allow gradient-based optimization of both the volume and the measurement parameters.
