# Tricubic B-Spline Interpolation


## Overview
A GPU-accelerated method for sampling 3D volume textures using uniform cubic B-splines, providing smoother results than linear interpolation while being faster than full tricubic convolution [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard tricubic convolution by reducing the number of texture fetches through grouped weight calculations and optimized coordinate mapping, making it suitable for real-time volume rendering where visual fidelity is prioritized over the absolute minimal computational cost of trilinear interpolation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Floating-point 3D coordinate `coord` in texture space $[0, 1]^3$, and volume dimensions `nrOfVoxels` (typically `uResolution`).
- Output: Scalar value (e.g., density or color) at the sampled position.

**Data Structures:**
- The volume is stored in a 2D texture array or similar format accessible via `texture3Dfrom2D`, likely to work around WebGL limitations or optimize memory access [S1, unofficial].
- Standard 32-bit floating-point vectors (`vec3`) are used for coordinates and weights.

**Steps:**
1.  **Coordinate Shift:** Shift the input coordinate from $[0, 1]$ to grid space centered on voxel centers:
    $$ \text{coord\_grid} = \text{coord} \times \text{nrOfVoxels} - 0.5 $$
2.  **Index and Fraction:** Compute the integer base index and the fractional part:
    $$ \text{index} = \lfloor \text{coord\_grid} \rfloor $$
    $$ \text{fraction} = \text{coord\_grid} - \text{index} $$
3.  **One Minus Fraction:**
    $$ \text{one\_frac} = 1.0 - \text{fraction} $$
4.  **B-Spline Weights:** Calculate the four B-spline basis weights for each dimension using the fraction $f$:
    $$ w_0 = \frac{1}{6} (1-f)^3 $$
    $$ w_1 = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{2} f^2 (2-f) $$
    $$ w_2 = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{2} (1-f)^2 (2-(1-f)) $$
    $$ w_3 = \frac{1}{6} f^3 $$
    *Note: The source implementation groups these weights for optimization.*
5.  **Grouped Weights:** Compute grouped weights to reduce multiplications:
    $$ g_0 = w_0 + w_1 $$
    $$ g_1 = w_2 + w_3 $$
6.  **Normalized Sample Coordinates:** Compute the normalized sample coordinates for the two rows of voxels along each axis. Let $\text{mult} = 1.0 / \text{nrOfVoxels}$:
    $$ h_0 = \text{mult} \times \left( \frac{w_1}{g_0} - 0.5 + \text{index} \right) $$
    $$ h_1 = \text{mult} \times \left( \frac{w_3}{g_1} + 1.5 + \text{index} \right) $$
7.  **X-Interpolation (First Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the first Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex000} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(h_0).r $$
    $$ \text{tex100} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_0.y, h_0.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex000} = \text{mix}(\text{tex100}, \text{tex000}, g_0.x) $$
8.  **X-Interpolation (Second Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the second Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex010} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_1.y, h_0.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex110} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_1.y, h_0.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex010} = \text{mix}(\text{tex110}, \text{tex010}, g_0.x) $$
9.  **Y-Interpolation (First Z Plane):** Interpolate the result of the X-passes along Y:
    $$ \text{tex000} = \text{mix}(\text{tex010}, \text{tex000}, g_0.y) $$
10. **X-Interpolation (Third Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the third Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_0.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex101} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_0.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{mix}(\text{tex101}, \text{tex001}, g_0.x) $$
11. **X-Interpolation (Fourth Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the fourth Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex011} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_1.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex111} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(h_1).r $$
    $$ \text{tex011} = \text{mix}(\text{tex111}, \text{tex011}, g_0.x) $$
12. **Y-Interpolation (Second Z Plane):** Interpolate the result of the X-passes along Y for the second set:
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{mix}(\text{tex011}, \text{tex001}, g_0.y) $$
13. **Z-Interpolation:** Final interpolation along Z:
    $$ \text{result} = \text{mix}(\text{tex001}, \text{tex000}, g_0.z) $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def tricubic_b_spline_sample(coord, nr_of_voxels, texture_sampler):
    """
    coord: vec3 in [0, 1]
    nr_of_voxels: vec3 dimensions of the volume
    texture_sampler: function that samples the 3D volume from 2D texture array
    """
    # 1. Shift coordinate
    coord_grid = coord * nr_of_voxels - 0.5
    
    # 2. Index and fraction
    index = floor(coord_grid)
    fraction = coord_grid - index
    one_frac = 1.0 - fraction
    
    # 3. B-Spline Weights
    f = fraction
    w0 = (1.0 / 6.0) * (one_frac ** 3)
    w1 = (2.0 / 3.0) - (0.5 * f ** 2 * (2.0 - f))
    w2 = (2.0 / 3.0) - (0.5 * (one_frac ** 2) * (2.0 - one_frac))
    w3 = (1.0 / 6.0) * (f ** 3)
    
    # 4. Grouped weights
    g0 = w0 + w1
    g1 = w2 + w3
    
    # 5. Normalized sample coordinates
    mult = 1.0 / nr_of_voxels
    h0 = mult * ((w1 / g0) - 0.5 + index)
    h1 = mult * ((w3 / g1) + 1.5 + index)
    
    # 6. X-Interpolation (First Y/Z Plane)
    tex000 = texture_sampler(h0)
    tex100 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h0.y, h0.z))
    tex000 = mix(tex100, tex000, g0.x)
    
    # 7. X-Interpolation (Second Y/Z Plane)
    tex010 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h1.y, h0.z))
    tex110 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h1.y, h0.z))
    tex010 = mix(tex110, tex010, g0.x)
    
    # 8. Y-Interpolation (First Z Plane)
    tex000 = mix(tex010, tex000, g0.y)
    
    # 9. X-Interpolation (Third Y/Z Plane)
    tex001 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h0.y, h1.z))
    tex101 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h0.y, h1.z))
    tex001 = mix(tex101, tex001, g0.x)
    
    # 10. X-Interpolation (Fourth Y/Z Plane)
    tex011 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h1.y, h1.z))
    tex111 = texture_sampler(h1)
    tex011 = mix(tex111, tex011, g0.x)
    
    # 11. Y-Interpolation (Second Z Plane)
    tex001 = mix(tex011, tex001, g0.y)
    
    # 12. Z-Interpolation
    return mix(tex001, tex000, g0.z)
```

**Parameters:**
- `nrOfVoxels`: The dimensions of the 3D volume texture. Typically `uResolution` [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The implementation relies on `texture3Dfrom2D`, implying the volume is stored in a 2D texture array or similar format to work around WebGL limitations or performance constraints [S1, unofficial]. Standard `texture3D` calls are not used.
- Division by zero in weight calculation if $g_0$ or $g_1$ becomes zero. This is unlikely with standard B-spline weights but should be guarded against in robust implementations [G].

## Subtopics

### [High Order Interpolation (Cubic/B-Spline)](14474-high-order-interpolation-cubic-b-spline.md)
High order interpolation methods, such as cubic convolution or B-spline interpolation, provide superior image quality compared to trilinear interpolation, particularly when rendering at large magnification factors where trilinear kernels may produce visible diamond structures or crosses. These methods are chosen when visual fidelity is prioritized over performance, accepting a significant increase in computational cost and memory bandwidth usage.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, but with a higher constant factor than trilinear due to 12 texture fetches and multiple arithmetic operations.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory.
- **Measured Performance:** Faster than full tricubic convolution, but slower than trilinear interpolation [S1, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** Performance scales with the number of samples (pixels/voxels) but is independent of volume size due to texture sampling.
- **Strengths:** Smoother results than linear interpolation; faster than full tricubic convolution.
- **Limitations:** Higher computational cost than trilinear; requires specific texture storage format (2D texture array) [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When visual fidelity is prioritized over performance, particularly at large magnification factors [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When performance is critical and trilinear interpolation is sufficient.

## Background
Originated in the context of WebGL volume rendering, specifically in the `sharevol` library by OKaluza [S1, unofficial]. It is designed to provide a balance between quality and performance for real-time volume visualization in web browsers.
