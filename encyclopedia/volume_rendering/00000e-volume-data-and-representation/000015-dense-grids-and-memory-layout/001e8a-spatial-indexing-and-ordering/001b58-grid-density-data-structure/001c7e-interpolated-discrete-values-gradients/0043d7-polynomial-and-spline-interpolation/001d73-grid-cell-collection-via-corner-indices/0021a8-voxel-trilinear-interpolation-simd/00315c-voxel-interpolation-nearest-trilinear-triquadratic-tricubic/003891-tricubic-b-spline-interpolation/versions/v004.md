# Tricubic B-Spline Interpolation

Also called: GPU Prefilter Cubic B-Spline

## Overview
A GPU-accelerated method for sampling 3D volume textures using uniform cubic B-splines, providing smoother results than linear interpolation while being faster than full tricubic convolution [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard tricubic convolution by reducing the number of texture fetches to eight per sample through grouped weight calculations and optimized coordinate mapping, trading a moderate increase in arithmetic operations for improved smoothness over trilinear interpolation [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Floating-point 3D coordinate `coord` in texture space $[0, 1]^3$, and volume dimensions `nrOfVoxels` (typically `uResolution`) [S2, unofficial].
- Output: Scalar value (e.g., density or color) at the sampled position.

**Data Structures:**
- The volume is stored in a 2D texture array or similar format accessible via `texture3Dfrom2D`, likely to work around WebGL limitations or optimize memory access [S2, unofficial].
- Standard 32-bit floating-point vectors (`vec3`) are used for coordinates and weights [G].

**Steps:**
1. **Coordinate Shift:** Shift the input coordinate from $[0, 1]$ to grid space centered on voxel centers:
   $$ \text{coord\_grid} = \text{coord} \times \text{nrOfVoxels} - 0.5 $$ [S2, unofficial]
2. **Index and Fraction:** Compute the integer base index and the fractional part:
   $$ \text{index} = \lfloor \text{coord\_grid} \rfloor $$
   $$ \text{fraction} = \text{coord\_grid} - \text{index} $$ [S2, unofficial]
3. **One Minus Fraction:**
   $$ \text{one\_frac} = 1.0 - \text{fraction} $$ [S2, unofficial]
4. **B-Spline Weights:** Calculate the four B-spline basis weights for each dimension using the fraction $f$:
   $$ w_0 = \frac{1}{6} (1-f)^3 $$
   $$ w_1 = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{2} f^2 (2-f) $$
   $$ w_2 = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{2} (1-f)^2 (2-(1-f)) $$
   $$ w_3 = \frac{1}{6} f^3 $$ [S1, unofficial]
5. **Grouped Weights:** Compute grouped weights to reduce multiplications:
   $$ g_0 = w_0 + w_1 $$
   $$ g_1 = w_2 + w_3 $$ [S2, unofficial]
6. **Normalized Sample Coordinates:** Compute the normalized sample coordinates for the two rows of voxels along each axis. Let $\text{mult} = 1.0 / \text{nrOfVoxels}$:
   $$ h_0 = \text{mult} \times \left( \frac{w_1}{g_0} - 0.5 + \text{index} \right) $$
   $$ h_1 = \text{mult} \times \left( \frac{w_3}{g_1} + 1.5 + \text{index} \right) $$ [S2, unofficial]
7. **X-Interpolation (First Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the first Y/Z plane:
   $$ \text{tex000} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(h_0).r $$
   $$ \text{tex100} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_0.y, h_0.z)).r $$
   $$ \text{tex000} = \text{mix}(\text{tex100}, \text{tex000}, g_0.x) $$ [S2, unofficial]
8. **X-Interpolation (Second Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the second Y/Z plane:
   $$ \text{tex010} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_1.y, h_0.z)).r $$
   $$ \text{tex110} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_1.y, h_0.z)).r $$
   $$ \text{tex010} = \text{mix}(\text{tex110}, \text{tex010}, g_0.x) $$ [S2, unofficial]
9. **Y-Interpolation (First Z Plane):** Interpolate the result of the X-passes along Y:
   $$ \text{tex000} = \text{mix}(\text{tex010}, \text{tex000}, g_0.y) $$ [S2, unofficial]
10. **X-Interpolation (Third Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the third Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_0.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex101} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_1.x, h_0.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{mix}(\text{tex101}, \text{tex001}, g_0.x) $$ [S2, unofficial]
11. **X-Interpolation (Fourth Y/Z Plane):** Fetch and interpolate along X for the fourth Y/Z plane:
    $$ \text{tex011} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(\text{vec3}(h_0.x, h_1.y, h_1.z)).r $$
    $$ \text{tex111} = \text{texture3Dfrom2D}(h_1).r $$
    $$ \text{tex011} = \text{mix}(\text{tex111}, \text{tex011}, g_0.x) $$ [S2, unofficial]
12. **Y-Interpolation (Second Z Plane):** Interpolate the result of the X-passes along Y for the second set:
    $$ \text{tex001} = \text{mix}(\text{tex011}, \text{tex001}, g_0.y) $$ [S2, unofficial]
13. **Z-Interpolation:** Final interpolation along Z:
    $$ \text{result} = \text{mix}(\text{tex001}, \text{tex000}, g_0.z) $$ [S2, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def tricubic_b_spline_sample(coord, nr_of_voxels, texture_sampler):
    """
    coord: vec3 in [0, 1]
    nr_of_voxels: vec3 dimensions of the volume
    texture_sampler: function that samples the 3D volume from 2D texture array
    """
    # 1. Shift coordinate
    coord_grid = coord * nr_of_voxels - 0.5
    
    # 2. Index and fraction
    index = floor(coord_grid)
    fraction = coord_grid - index
    one_frac = 1.0 - fraction
    
    # 3. B-Spline Weights
    f = fraction
    w0 = (1.0 / 6.0) * (one_frac ** 3)
    w1 = (2.0 / 3.0) - (0.5 * f ** 2 * (2.0 - f))
    w2 = (2.0 / 3.0) - (0.5 * (one_frac ** 2) * (2.0 - one_frac))
    w3 = (1.0 / 6.0) * (f ** 3)
    
    # 4. Grouped weights
    g0 = w0 + w1
    g1 = w2 + w3
    
    # 5. Normalized sample coordinates
    mult = 1.0 / nr_of_voxels
    # Guard against division by zero if g0 or g1 is zero [G]
    h0 = mult * ((w1 / max(g0, 1e-8)) - 0.5 + index)
    h1 = mult * ((w3 / max(g1, 1e-8)) + 1.5 + index)
    
    # 6. X-Interpolation (First Y/Z Plane)
    tex000 = texture_sampler(h0)
    tex100 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h0.y, h0.z))
    tex000 = mix(tex100, tex000, g0.x)
    
    # 7. X-Interpolation (Second Y/Z Plane)
    tex010 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h1.y, h0.z))
    tex110 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h1.y, h0.z))
    tex010 = mix(tex110, tex010, g0.x)
    
    # 8. Y-Interpolation (First Z Plane)
    tex000 = mix(tex010, tex000, g0.y)
    
    # 9. X-Interpolation (Third Y/Z Plane)
    tex001 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h0.y, h1.z))
    tex101 = texture_sampler(vec3(h1.x, h0.y, h1.z))
    tex001 = mix(tex101, tex001, g0.x)
    
    # 10. X-Interpolation (Fourth Y/Z Plane)
    tex011 = texture_sampler(vec3(h0.x, h1.y, h1.z))
    tex111 = texture_sampler(h1)
    tex011 = mix(tex111, tex011, g0.x)
    
    # 11. Y-Interpolation (Second Z Plane)
    tex001 = mix(tex011, tex001, g0.y)
    
    # 12. Z-Interpolation
    return mix(tex001, tex000, g0.z)

def mix(a, b, t):
    return a * (1.0 - t) + b * t

class vec3:
    def __init__(self, x, y, z):
        self.x = x
        self.y = y
        self.z = z
```

**Parameters:**
- `nrOfVoxels`: The dimensions of the 3D volume texture. Typically `uResolution` [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The implementation relies on `texture3Dfrom2D`, implying the volume is stored in a 2D texture array or similar format to work around WebGL limitations or performance constraints [S2, unofficial]. Standard `texture3D` calls are not used.
- Division by zero in weight calculation if $g_0$ or $g_1$ becomes zero. This is unlikely with standard B-spline weights but should be guarded against in robust implementations [G].

## Subtopics

### [High Order Interpolation (Cubic/B-Spline)](14474-high-order-interpolation-cubic-b-spline.md)
High order interpolation methods, such as cubic convolution or B-spline interpolation, provide superior image quality compared to trilinear interpolation, particularly when rendering at large magnification factors where trilinear kernels may produce visible diamond structures or crosses. These methods are chosen when visual fidelity is prioritized over performance, accepting a significant increase in computational cost and memory bandwidth usage. This method builds on [Tricubic B-Spline Interpolation](8246-tricubic-b-spline-interpolation.md) by generalizing the approach to higher-order polynomial kernels.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, with a constant factor determined by eight texture fetches and fixed arithmetic operations [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per sample [G].
- **Measured Performance:** Faster than full tricubic convolution, but slower than trilinear interpolation [S2, unofficial].
- **Scaling:** Performance scales linearly with the number of samples (pixels/voxels) and is independent of volume size due to hardware texture sampling [G].
- **Strengths:** Smoother results than linear interpolation; faster than full tricubic convolution [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires specific texture storage format (2D texture array) to work around WebGL limitations or optimize memory access [S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When smoother results than linear interpolation are needed and the volume is stored in a 2D texture array [S2, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When the volume is stored in a standard 3D texture format or when trilinear interpolation is sufficient [G].
