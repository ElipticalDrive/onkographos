# Second Derivative Computation
Also called: Hessian-based grid computation, Second-order finite difference operators

## Overview
This family computes second-order spatial derivatives (curvature, Laplacian, divergence) from discrete grid data to highlight structural features, boundaries, or physical properties like fluid compressibility. It differs from sibling methods like [Gradient Computation via Finite Differences](1077-gradient-computation-via-finite-differences.md) by operating on the rate of change of the gradient rather than the gradient itself, and from [Analytic Gradient Computation](2167-analytic-gradient-computation.md) by relying on discrete stencil approximations rather than closed-form derivatives of basis functions.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family rely on central difference stencils to approximate second-order derivatives. For a scalar field $f$, the second derivative along an axis $x$ is approximated as:
$$ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} \approx f(x+1) - 2f(x) + f(x-1) $$
in index space (assuming unit voxel spacing). This requires accessing the center voxel and its immediate neighbors along the axis.

For mixed derivatives (e.g., $f_{xy}$), a 4-point stencil is used:
$$ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} \approx \frac{f(x+1, y+1) - f(x-1, y+1) - f(x+1, y-1) + f(x-1, y-1)}{4} $$

The computation iterates over active voxels, constructs a local stencil of neighbors, applies the specific linear combination of values defined by the target operator (Laplacian, Mean Curvature, etc.), and stores the result in a new grid with the same topology. Boundary handling is critical: inactive neighbors or grid edges must be resolved via clamping, zero-padding, or obstacle velocity substitution, depending on the specific child method.

## Subtopics

### [Mean Curvature Grid Computation](3126-mean-curvature-grid-computation.md)
Computes the mean curvature $\kappa = \nabla \cdot (\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})$. It normalizes the gradient, making it sensitive to isosurface shape rather than just density variation. It avoids intermediate grids by computing all derivatives in a single pass. Choose this for isosurface smoothing or feature detection based on surface geometry.

### [Divergence Grid Computation](3151-divergence-grid-computation.md)
Computes $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}$ for a vector field. It differs by operating on vector inputs and handling fluid-solid boundaries by substituting obstacle velocities for inactive cells. Choose this for fluid simulation or compressibility analysis.

### [Laplacian Grid Computation](3160-laplacian-grid-computation.md)
Computes $\nabla^2 f$, the trace of the Hessian. It highlights boundaries and suppresses homogeneous interiors. Choose this for edge enhancement, noise reduction, or solving Poisson equations. It differs from Mean Curvature by not normalizing the gradient.

### [Depth and Image Gradient Regularization](6439-depth-and-image-gradient-regularization.md)
Applies a spatial regularization loss to a depth map, penalizing second-order derivatives (curvature) weighted by the local image gradient magnitude. It differs from standard Laplacian regularization by using adaptive exponential weighting based on image intensity to preserve sharp edges. Choose this for depth map refinement in regions with high texture.

## Background
Second derivative computation is a fundamental operation in scientific visualization and image processing. The Laplacian operator is central to diffusion processes and edge detection [S1]. Mean curvature flow is used in mesh smoothing and surface reconstruction [S1]. Divergence is essential in computational fluid dynamics for enforcing incompressibility [S2]. The regularization technique for depth maps is a more recent application in computer vision for optimizing depth predictions [S1].
