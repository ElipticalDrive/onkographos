# Second Derivative Computation

Also called: Hessian Computation, Second-Order Finite Differences, Laplacian and Curvature Estimation

## Overview
Second Derivative Computation computes the second-order spatial derivatives of a scalar or vector field defined on a grid, enabling the estimation of curvature, divergence, and the Hessian matrix. It solves the problem of quantifying local field acceleration and boundary sharpness, which first-order gradients alone cannot distinguish (e.g., distinguishing a flat region from a linear ramp). It sets itself apart from sibling methods by focusing on the *second-order* operator application (Laplacian, Mean Curvature, Divergence of vector fields) rather than just the gradient vector or magnitude.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a discrete grid representation of a continuous field. The core mechanism is the application of finite difference stencils to approximate partial derivatives.

**Stencils and Neighbors:**
Methods typically use a 6-point (cross) stencil for pure second derivatives ($f_{xx}, f_{yy}, f_{zz}$) or a 27-point (cube) stencil for mixed derivatives ($f_{xy}, f_{xz}, f_{yz}$) and mean curvature.
- **Pure Second Derivative (Central Difference):**
  $$ f_{xx} \approx f(x+1, y, z) - 2f(x, y, z) + f(x-1, y, z) $$
  This formula assumes unit spacing in index space. If physical voxel spacing $\Delta x$ is used, the result is scaled by $1/\Delta x^2$ .
- **Mixed Second Derivative (Central Difference):**
  $$ f_{xy} \approx \frac{f(x+1, y+1, z) - f(x-1, y+1, z) - f(x+1, y-1, z) + f(x-1, y-1, z)}{4} $$
  The multiplier is $0.25$ .

**Boundary Handling:**
Standard implementations assume zero-padding (background value 0) for neighbors outside the active domain . Some methods, like [Laplacian Grid Computation via Finite Differences](8252-laplacian-grid-computation-via-finite-differences.md), use adjoint forward-difference operators which imply specific boundary conditions (first row/col depends on input first row/col) .

**Data Types:**
Single precision (`float`) is standard for real-time simulation .

## Subtopics

### [Mean Curvature Grid Computation](3126-mean-curvature-grid-computation.md)
Computes $\kappa = \nabla \cdot (\nabla f / |\nabla f|)$ by combining first and second derivatives in a single pass. Chosen when surface geometry analysis is required.

### [Divergence Grid Computation](3151-divergence-grid-computation.md)
Computes $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}$ for a vector field $\mathbf{F}$. Chosen for fluid simulation pressure projection to enforce incompressibility.

### [Laplacian Grid Computation](3160-laplacian-grid-computation.md)
Computes $\nabla^2 f$ (trace of Hessian) for a scalar field. Chosen for boundary highlighting and noise suppression.

### [Depth and Image Gradient Regularization](6439-depth-and-image-gradient-regularization.md)
Applies second-order depth curvature weighted by image gradient magnitude. Chosen for view synthesis depth map smoothing.

### [Laplacian Grid Computation via Finite Differences](8252-laplacian-grid-computation-via-finite-differences.md)
Constructs Laplacian as $D^T D$ for inverse problems. Chosen when adjoint structure is required for optimization solvers like GMRES.

### [Eikonal Omega Term Computation](13240-eikonal-omega-term-computation.md)
Computes gradients of Eikonal loss w.r.t. hash grid features using double backprop or second-order kernels. Chosen for neural field training.

### [Second-order Grid Sampler](13559-second-order-grid-sampler.md)
Computes exact Hessian of trilinear interpolation w.r.t. query coordinates and features. Chosen for SDF regularization in neural networks.

## Background
The use of finite differences for second derivatives originates from numerical analysis methods for solving partial differential equations (PDEs) . In computer graphics, the Laplacian operator has been used for smoothing and boundary detection since the early days of volume rendering . The specific application to mean curvature estimation in sparse voxel grids is documented in recent graphics literature . The integration of second-order derivatives into neural field training (Eikonal loss, SDF regularization) is a more recent development driven by the need for sharp surface representations .
