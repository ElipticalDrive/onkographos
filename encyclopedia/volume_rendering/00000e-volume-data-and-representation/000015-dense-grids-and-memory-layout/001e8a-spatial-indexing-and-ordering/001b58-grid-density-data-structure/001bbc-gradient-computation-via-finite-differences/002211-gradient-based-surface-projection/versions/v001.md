# Gradient-based Surface Projection

Also called: Refractive index-only image, Surface projections

## Overview
Gradient-based surface projection visualizes interfaces between regions of different density by projecting through a matrix of gradient magnitude values derived from voxel density [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard volume rendering by treating the volume as a collection of internal surfaces defined by normals and strengths, rather than a continuous medium with absorption and emission [S1, unofficial]. This method allows for the visualization of diffuse transitions and sub-voxel surface accuracy that binary representations cannot achieve [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Scalar volume data $V$ (density values).
*   Pre-computed 3D gradient vectors $N = (N_x, N_y, N_z)$ for each voxel, obtained via finite differences [S1, unofficial].
*   (Optional) Color information associated with the volume [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A 2D image representing the projected surface magnitudes (refractive index-only image) or shaded colored surface projections [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gradient Grid:** A 3D array of vectors. Each element stores $(N_x, N_y, N_z)$, approximated by finite differences of neighboring densities [S1, unofficial].
*   **Magnitude Grid:** A 3D array of floats. Each element stores $|N|$, the L2-norm of the gradient vector at that voxel [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Surface Normal and Strength:**
    For each voxel, the surface normal $N$ is approximated by the finite difference of neighboring densities [S1, unofficial]. The surface strength (magnitude) $|N|$ is calculated as the L2-norm of $N$:
    $$ |N| = \sqrt{N_x^2 + N_y^2 + N_z^2} $$
    [S1, unofficial]

2.  **Project Surface Magnitude (Refractive Index-Only):**
    To generate a refractive index-only image, project directly through the surface magnitude information $|N|$ along the view ray [S1, unofficial].
    *   Since surfaces are defined by fractional values at each voxel, this projection visualizes diffuse transitions [S1, unofficial].
    *   Surfaces may appear to lie at sub-voxel accuracy due to these fractional values [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Shaded Surface Projection (Optional Enhancement):**
    To enhance depth perception, apply 3D lighting cues to the data set [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use the finite difference gradient $N$ as the estimate of the surface normal direction for the lighting model [S1, unofficial].
    *   Light enters the voxel from the back with intensity $I$ and exits with intensity $I'$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Modulate the intensity and color of the light by three voxel-dependent effects: translucency, luminosity, and refraction [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use the direction of the surface normal to modulate the intensity of the transmitted light refracted to the next voxel [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render a shaded colored refractive index image by using color information modulated by the gradient with a shading model in the projection pipeline [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Surfaces may appear to lie at sub-voxel accuracy because they are defined by fractional values at each voxel [S1, unofficial]. This is a feature for visualizing diffuse transitions but may be misleading if precise geometric location is required [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Shaded Surface Projection
An enhancement of depth perception by applying 3D lighting cues to the data set [S1, unofficial]. It modulates transmitted light intensity by translucency, luminosity, and refraction [S1, unofficial]. It uses the finite difference gradient as the surface normal direction for the lighting model, where light enters from the back and is modulated by voxel-dependent effects before being refracted to the next voxel [S1, unofficial]. Choose this when depth perception is critical and computational cost for lighting calculations is acceptable [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of voxels for gradient computation (if not pre-computed) and projection. Shaded projection adds lighting calculation overhead per voxel along the ray.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the gradient vector grid (3 floats per voxel) and potentially the magnitude grid (1 float per voxel) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Visualizes interfaces between regions of different density; allows visualization of diffuse transitions not possible with binary representation; provides sub-voxel accuracy perception [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Does not explicitly model absorption/emission like standard volume rendering; relies on gradient magnitude which may be noisy in flat regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When the goal is to visualize internal interfaces and boundaries within a volume, especially for medical data where tissue boundaries are of interest [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When volumetric transparency and internal structure visibility (e.g., fog, clouds) are more important than sharp surface boundaries [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originated in the context of volumetric rendering of medical data, specifically for stereotactic neurosurgery planning [S1, unofficial]. The method is based on the concept that visualizing interfaces between regions of different density yields sufficient information about the volume [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the use of gradient operators to identify these interfaces [S1, unofficial].
