# Texture Coordinate Conversion

Also called: local2tex

## Overview
Texture Coordinate Conversion maps spatial coordinates (local or world space) to normalized texture coordinates (UVW) suitable for 3D texture sampling in volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of translating geometric positions into the $[0, 1]$ range expected by hardware texture units, while accounting for specific coordinate system conventions such as Y-axis flipping [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [World to UVW Normalization](2229-world-to-uvw-normalization.md) by explicitly handling the Y-axis flip and the 0.5 offset required for center-aligned sampling in this specific implementation context [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $P$: Input spatial coordinate (float3), typically in local or object space.
- $g\_cf3InvVolSize$: Inverse dimensions of the volume grid (float3), precomputed as $(1/N_x, 1/N_y, 1/N_z)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- $uv$: Normalized texture coordinate (float3) in the range $[0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No persistent data structures. The operation is a pure function of the input coordinate and the volume inverse size parameter.

**Steps:**
1. **Scale and Offset:** Multiply the input coordinate $P$ by the inverse volume size $g\_cf3InvVolSize$ and add a constant offset of 0.5 to all components. This maps the coordinate from the grid domain to the normalized $[0, 1]$ range, shifted by half a voxel to align with voxel centers [S1, unofficial].
   $$ uv = P \cdot g\_cf3InvVolSize + 0.5 $$
2. **Flip Y Axis:** Invert the Y component of the resulting texture coordinate. This accounts for the difference between the volume's internal Y-axis orientation (often bottom-up) and the texture coordinate system (often top-down) [S1, unofficial].
   $$ uv.y = 1 - uv.y $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def texture_coordinate_conversion(P: Vector3, inv_vol_size: Vector3) -> Vector3:
    """
    Converts spatial coordinate P to texture coordinates uv.
    
    Args:
        P: Input spatial coordinate (float3).
        inv_vol_size: Inverse volume dimensions (float3), i.e., (1/Nx, 1/Ny, 1/Nz).
        
    Returns:
        uv: Normalized texture coordinate (float3) in [0, 1].
    """
    # Step 1: Scale by inverse volume size and add 0.5 offset
    uv = P * inv_vol_size + 0.5
    
    # Step 2: Flip the Y coordinate
    uv.y = 1.0 - uv.y
    
    return uv
```

**Parameters:**
- $g\_cf3InvVolSize$: Inverse dimensions of the volume. Typical values depend on the volume resolution. For a $256 \times 256 \times 256$ volume, this would be $(1/256, 1/256, 1/256)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Boundary Conditions:** If $P$ is outside the volume bounds, the resulting $uv$ may fall outside $[0, 1]$. Texture filtering or clamping modes determine the behavior (e.g., returning border color or repeating) [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Floating-point precision errors in the multiplication and addition steps may cause slight deviations from exact voxel centers, potentially leading to sampling artifacts at high resolutions [G].
- **Y-Axis Convention:** Failing to flip the Y axis results in vertically inverted volume rendering [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Voxel Center Alignment Offset
Voxel Center Alignment Offset is a variant that explicitly offsets the camera position in texture space by half a unit to align ray origins with voxel centers [S2, unofficial]. It differs from the standard conversion by focusing on the camera position transformation rather than arbitrary spatial points, ensuring that ray casting starts at the center of the first voxel [S2, unofficial]. Choose this when implementing ray marching where the ray origin must be precisely centered in the volume grid. The formula is $\mathbf{p}_{\text{tex}} = \mathbf{p}_{\text{obj}} + (0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$, where $\mathbf{p}_{\text{obj}}$ is the camera position in object space [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- Time: $O(1)$ per coordinate conversion.
- Memory: $O(1)$ auxiliary memory.

**Measured Performance:**
- Not given in the sources.

**Scaling:**
- Linear with respect to the number of coordinate conversions. Independent of volume size, as the inverse size is precomputed [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- Strengths: Simple, fast, and hardware-friendly. Handles the common Y-axis flip and center alignment in one step [S1, unofficial].
- Limitations: Assumes a specific coordinate system convention (Y-flip). Not directly applicable to non-axis-aligned volumes without additional transformation [G].

**When to Use:**
- Use when sampling 3D textures in a volume renderer where the texture coordinate system requires Y-flipping and center alignment [S1, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
- Do not use if the texture coordinate system does not require Y-flipping or if the volume is not axis-aligned [G].

## Background
Originated in the context of dynamic volume raycasting implementations, specifically in the `DynamicVolumeRayCasting` project [S1, unofficial]. The method is part of the shader code (`RayCast.fx`) that handles the mapping from spatial coordinates to texture coordinates for volume data sampling [S1, unofficial].
