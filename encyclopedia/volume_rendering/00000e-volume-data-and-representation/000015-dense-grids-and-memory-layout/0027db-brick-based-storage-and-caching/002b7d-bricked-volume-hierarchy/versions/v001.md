# Bricked Volume Hierarchy
## Overview
A data organization scheme where a volume dataset is partitioned into chunks called bricks across multiple resolution levels. It solves the problem of managing multi-resolution data by decoupling the logical resolution of the data from its spatial geometric subdivision, ensuring that bricks maintain a fixed voxel count regardless of their level in the hierarchy. It is distinct from sibling methods that may tie brick size to spatial extent or use different hierarchical structures like N³-trees.
## Shared principles
The family is defined by the separation of two distinct hierarchies: the data hierarchy and the spatial hierarchy.

1.  **Brick Hierarchy (Data Resolution):** The volume is represented as a hierarchy of resolution levels. A brick is defined as a chunk of voxel data belonging to a specific resolution level. Crucially, the size of a brick in voxels (e.g., $32^3$) is fixed and constant across all resolution levels [S1, unofficial]. This means the brick's logical data size is independent of its spatial extent. The spatial extent of a brick is determined solely by the resolution level it belongs to; higher resolution levels correspond to smaller spatial extents per brick, while lower resolution levels correspond to larger spatial extents [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Residency-Octree Hierarchy (Spatial Subdivision):** In contrast to the fixed-voxel bricks, the spatial subdivision is determined by a residency octree. This octree has multiple subdivision levels where, at each level, each dimension is halved, resulting in eight children per node [S1, unofficial]. The spatial extent of a node in the residency octree is determined by this geometric subdivision, not by the brick's voxel count. This creates a clear distinction between the resolution levels of the volume data and the geometric boundaries of the octree nodes [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Cache Requirement:** To access a brick's data on the GPU, it must be resident in a brick cache. The hierarchy implies that bricks are loaded into this cache as needed for rendering [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept originates from scalable web-based volume rendering of large biomedical datasets, specifically distinguishing between data resolution and spatial subdivision to optimize GPU access [S1, unofficial].
