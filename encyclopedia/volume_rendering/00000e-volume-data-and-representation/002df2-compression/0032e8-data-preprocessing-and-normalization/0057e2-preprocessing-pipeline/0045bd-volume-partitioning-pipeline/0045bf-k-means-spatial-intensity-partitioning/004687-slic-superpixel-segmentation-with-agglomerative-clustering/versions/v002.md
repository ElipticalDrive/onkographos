# SLIC Superpixel Segmentation with Agglomerative Clustering
Also called: Progressive Agglomerative Clustering, SLIC

## Overview
A segmentation method for multivariate volumes that generates SLIC superpixels from multivariate voxels and merges them through progressive agglomerative clustering to accurately segment phases/composites [S1]. It solves the problem of disjointed regions of the same phase being separated by noise or other phases by using global consideration of similarity during merging to label similar disconnected regions as belonging to the same phase [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A multivariate volume dataset where each voxel contains multiple intensity channels [S1].
**Outputs:** A segmented volume where voxels are labeled by their assigned phase or composite [S1].
**Data Structures:** Not given in the sources.
**Steps:**
1. Generate SLIC superpixels from the multivariate voxels [S1].
2. Merge the initial superpixels through several steps of progressive agglomerative clustering [S1].
3. During merging, apply global consideration of similarity to label similar disconnected regions as belonging to the same phase/composite [S1].
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources.
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.
**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:** Disjointed regions of the same phase/composite may be separated by noise or other phases; the method handles this by using global consideration of similarity during merging to label similar disconnected regions as belonging to the same phase [S1].

## Background
Developed for multivariate volume data visualization and analysis [S1]. The method addresses limitations of existing techniques in accurately segmenting multivariate phases/composites in medical and scientific datasets like the MIEC dataset [S1]. The associated software was intended to be open source and freely available, with plans for integration into ParaView and Tomviz [S1].
