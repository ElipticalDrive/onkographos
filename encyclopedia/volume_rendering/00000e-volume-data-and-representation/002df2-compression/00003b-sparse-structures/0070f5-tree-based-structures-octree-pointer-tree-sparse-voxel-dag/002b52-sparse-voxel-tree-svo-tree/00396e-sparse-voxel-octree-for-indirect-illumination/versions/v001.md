# Sparse Voxel Octree for Indirect Illumination
Also called: Hierarchical Voxel Representation, SVOctree

## Overview
This method uses a pre-filtered dynamic sparse voxel octree (SVO) on the GPU to efficiently estimate visibility and integrate incoming indirect energy without involving the actual mesh geometry [S1]. It solves the problem of real-time indirect illumination in complex scenes by replacing dense, low-resolution regular voxel grids with a sparse, hierarchical structure that maintains high precision and supports specular indirect lighting [S1]. Unlike path-tracing or photon mapping, this approach always yields smooth results without noise or temporal discontinuities [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Triangle meshes representing the scene geometry; light source positions and intensities; camera viewing and lighting configuration parameters.
**Outputs:** Indirect illumination contribution per pixel or shading point.

**Data Structures:**
The core data structure is a dynamic sparse voxel octree stored on the GPU [S1].
*   **Root Node:** Represents the entire scene bounding volume.
*   **Internal Nodes:** Each node represents an eighth of its parent's volume. Nodes are associated with a specific bounding box in space [S1].
*   **Leaf Nodes:** Store pre-filtered scene information, including energy intensity, direction, occlusion data, and local normal distribution functions (NDFs) [S1].
*   **Hierarchy:** The tree allows querying filtered scene information with increasing precision by descending the hierarchy [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Real-time Mesh Voxelization:** Convert triangle meshes into a voxel representation using the GPU rasterization pipeline [S1].
2.  **Octree Building and Filtering:** Construct the sparse voxel octree dynamically and apply pre-filtering to aggregate geometry and direct lighting information into the nodes [S1]. This step exploits the GPU rasterization pipeline for efficiency [S1].
3.  **Energy Splatting:** Splat incoming indirect energy from light sources into the octree structure [S1].
4.  **Voxel Cone Tracing:** Use an approximate voxel cone tracing technique to query the pre-filtered octree [S1].
    *   Trace rays/cones from the shading point into the octree.
    *   Descend the tree to find relevant nodes that represent the geometry and lighting in the direction of interest.
    *   Retrieve filtered energy intensity and direction from the leaf nodes [S1].
5.  **Radiance Reconstruction:** Convolve the retrieved energy data (light directions and intensities) with a set of pre-defined Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Functions (BRDFs) to reconstruct the final radiance response [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Scene Resolution:** Chosen suitable to the viewing and lighting configuration to avoid light undersampling or geometric Level-of-Detail (LOD) issues [S1]. The resolution is not fixed by memory constraints of a uniform grid but adapts to the scene complexity and viewing distance [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Light Undersampling:** If the scene resolution is too low for the lighting configuration, light undersampling may occur [S1].
*   **Geometric LOD:** Insufficient resolution may lead to geometric LOD artifacts where thin features are lost or misrepresented [S1].
*   **Dynamic Scenes:** The method relies on real-time rebuilding and filtering, which must be efficient enough to maintain frame rates for highly detailed environments [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Real-time frame rates are achieved even for highly detailed environments, with an almost geometry-independent cost for the lighting calculation after the octree is built [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory occupancy is reduced compared to nested regular voxel grids because it uses sparse storage [S1]. The resolution is not limited by the cost of a diffusion process or uniform grid memory, allowing for higher precision [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces smooth results without noise or temporal discontinuities; supports specular indirect lighting; handles fully dynamic scenes; allows adaptive precision (higher near observer, abstracted farther away) [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires real-time mesh voxelization and octree building/filtering on the GPU; relies on approximate cone tracing which may introduce smoothing artifacts compared to exact ray tracing (though this is a trade-off for speed) [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time indirect illumination in complex, dynamic scenes where smooth results and specular highlights are desired, and where path-tracing noise or photon mapping variance is unacceptable [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When exact geometric precision is required for very thin features that might be averaged out by the pre-filtering, or when offline rendering with unlimited compute time is available [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in the paper "Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel Cone Tracing" [S1]. It derives from the need to produce real-time indirect illumination that is more precise and flexible than previous hierarchical voxel approaches like [KD10], which used nested regular voxel grids and suffered from lack of precision and temporal discontinuities due to low resolution and the cost of diffusion processes [S1]. The approach builds on the concept of sparse voxel octrees [CNLE09, LK10] but adapts them for pre-filtered indirect lighting integration via cone tracing [S1].
