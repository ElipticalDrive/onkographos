# Decomposed Low-Rank Tensor (VM-Decomposition)
Also called: TensoRF VM Decomposition, VM Decomposition, Vector-Matrix Decomposition

## Overview
Decomposed Low-Rank Tensor (VM-Decomposition) is a scene representation method that factorizes high-dimensional density and color tensors into compact vector and matrix factors to reduce memory footprint [S1, unofficial][S2]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like CP Decomposition by relaxing low-rank constraints for two modes of a tensor, effectively acting as a special case of Block Term Decomposition (BTD) [Parent]. Unlike direct storage methods, it stores features decoded by an MLP rather than direct color values [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** 3D spatial coordinates $\vec{x} = (x, y, z)$ and viewing direction $\vec{d}$.
*   **Output:** Density $\sigma$ and color $C$ (or features for color).

**Data Structures:**
The method maintains two primary tensor structures:
1.  **Density Tensor ($T_\sigma$):** Factorized into vectors $v_{\sigma, r}$ and matrices $M_{\sigma, r}$.
2.  **Color Tensor ($T_c$):** Factorized into vectors $v_{c, r}$, matrices $M_{c, r}$, and scalar feature components $b_r$.

**Layout and Dimensions:**
*   $T_\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $T_c \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K \times G}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   $R$: Number of components (rank) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $v_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^I$, $v_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^J$, $v_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $M_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^{J \times K}$, $M_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times K}$, $M_{\sigma, r} \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J}$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $v_{c, r}$ and $M_{c, r}$ have the same shapes as their density counterparts [S1, unofficial].
*   $b_r^X, b_r^Y, b_r^Z \in \mathbb{R}$ are scalar feature components [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Interpolate Density Factors:** For each rank $r \in \{1, \dots, R\}$, compute the interpolated values of the density vectors and matrices at coordinate $\vec{x}$. Let $v_{\sigma, r}^X(x)$, $v_{\sigma, r}^Y(y)$, $v_{\sigma, r}^Z(z)$ be the interpolated vector components, and $M_{\sigma, r}^{YZ}(y,z)$, $M_{\sigma, r}^{XZ}(x,z)$, $M_{\sigma, r}^{XY}(x,y)$ be the interpolated matrix components [S1, unofficial][S2].
2.  **Compute Density:** Sum the outer products of vectors and matrices for each rank [S2]:
    $$ \sigma(\vec{x}) = \sum_{r=1}^{R} \left( v_{\sigma, r}^X(x) \cdot M_{\sigma, r}^{YZ}(y,z) + v_{\sigma, r}^Y(y) \cdot M_{\sigma, r}^{XZ}(x,z) + v_{\sigma, r}^Z(z) \cdot M_{\sigma, r}^{XY}(x,y) \right) $$
    *(Note: The source formula [S2] uses outer product notation $\circ$, which for a vector and matrix implies broadcasting/multiplication along the shared dimension. The explicit expansion above reflects the standard interpretation of VM decomposition terms $v \circ M$ where $v$ is a vector and $M$ is a matrix covering the other two dimensions [S1, unofficial][S2].)*
3.  **Interpolate Color Factors:** Similarly, interpolate $v_{c, r}$ and $M_{c, r}$ at $\vec{x}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Color Features:** Combine interpolated color factors with scalar features $b_r$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ F_c(\vec{x}) = \sum_{r=1}^{R} \left( v_{c, r}^X(x) \cdot M_{c, r}^{YZ}(y,z) \cdot b_r^X + v_{c, r}^Y(y) \cdot M_{c, r}^{XZ}(x,z) \cdot b_r^Y + v_{c, r}^Z(z) \cdot M_{c, r}^{XY}(x,y) \cdot b_r^Z \right) $$
5.  **Decode Color:** Pass the feature vector $F_c(\vec{x})$ and viewing direction $\vec{d}$ through a decoder function $S$ (typically an MLP) to obtain final color [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ C(\vec{x}, \vec{d}) = S(F_c(\vec{x}), \vec{d}) $$

**Parameters:**
*   $R$: Number of components. Typical values are not given in the sources.
*   $I, J, K$: Grid dimensions. Not given in the sources.
*   $G$: Feature axis size for color tensor. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Instability:** When the Gaussian kernel width $\sigma < 0.001$, the 1D kernel $N_{1d}(\sigma)$ is assigned to be a discrete impulse function $\delta$ to avoid numerical instability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Overfitting:** Naively applying low-rank decomposed tensors lacks internal bias, limiting the spectrum of the learned signal and potentially causing suboptimal reconstruction and inaccurate poses during joint camera optimization [S1, unofficial]. A coarse-to-fine training schedule is recommended to limit the spectrum [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpolation:** The tensor values $T_\sigma$ and $T_c$ are defined as component-wise interpolation (e.g., linear) of the stored factors [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Memory:** Significantly reduces memory footprint compared to storing the full tensor $T \in \mathbb{R}^{I \times J \times K}$ by storing only vectors and matrices [S2].
*   **Performance:** Depends excessively on the spatial resolution of the grid [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient storage for large tensors; allows for feature-based color decoding [S2].
*   **Limitations:** Lacks internal bias if not regularized, leading to potential pose optimization issues [S1, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed by Chen et al. in TensoRF (2022) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **History:** It is a scene representation built upon decomposed low-rank tensors [S1, unofficial]. It is distinct from CP-decomposition, which is also considered in TensoRF [S1, unofficial].
*   **Usage:** Used in explicit sparse tensor representations for NeRF-like architectures [S2].
