# Wavelet and Pyramid Transforms
Also called: Multiresolution Decomposition, Wavelet Compression, Pyramid Compression

## Overview
This family of methods compresses volumetric data by transforming it into a frequency or multi-resolution domain, allowing for efficient storage and on-the-fly reconstruction during rendering [S1, unofficial][S2]. They solve the problem of high memory bandwidth and storage requirements inherent in large-scale or time-varying datasets by representing the volume in a domain where coefficients can be pruned, quantized, or entropy-encoded [S1, unofficial]. What sets this family apart from sibling methods like [Sparse structures](5-sparse-structures.md) or [Value compression](6-value-compression.md) is the explicit use of orthogonal, biorthogonal, or Gaussian-based filter banks to create hierarchical representations (wavelets or pyramids) that enable spatial and frequency localization, whereas sparse structures rely on empty-space skipping and value compression relies on scalar quantization without basis transformation [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the mechanism of decomposing the input volume $V$ into a set of coefficients $C$ using a linear transform, followed by encoding and optional decoding for rendering.

1.  **Transform:** The volume is decomposed using a separable 1D filter bank applied along each axis. For a 3D volume, this results in subbands representing different frequency components. In wavelet transforms, these are typically approximation (low-pass) and detail (high-pass) coefficients. In Laplacian pyramids, they are detail residuals between levels of a Gaussian pyramid.
2.  **Hierarchy:** The decomposition is recursive. The approximation subband from level $j$ is further decomposed at level $j+1$, creating a tree-like structure of coefficients. This allows for multi-resolution access, where coarse data is available at the root and fine details are deeper in the tree.
3.  **Quantization and Entropy Encoding:** Coefficients are quantized (lossy) or kept precise (lossless). Entropy coding (e.g., Huffman, Arithmetic, Run-Length) is applied to the quantized or raw coefficients to produce the compressed bitstream.
4.  **Reconstruction:** To render, the necessary coefficients are decoded and the inverse transform is applied. This can be done globally (full reconstruction) or locally (block-wise or point-wise) to support adaptive resolution rendering.

## Subtopics

### [Wavelet-based Volume Rendering](1052-wavelet-based-volume-rendering.md)
Uses orthogonal or biorthogonal wavelet bases (e.g., Haar, Daubechies) for multi-resolution analysis. It distinguishes itself by providing better frequency localization compared to Laplacian pyramids.

### [Laplacian Pyramid Volume Rendering](2261-laplacian-pyramid-volume-rendering.md)
Uses a Gaussian-based decomposition and prediction scheme. It is distinguished by its specific filter structure and on-the-fly reconstruction capabilities using caching.

### [Biorthogonal Wavelet Compression](5381-biorthogonal-wavelet-compression.md)
Partitions volume into blocks transformed independently using biorthogonal filters (e.g., 9/7-tap Daubechies).

### [Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform](6049-discrete-wavelet-packet-transform.md)
A variant that decomposes both detail and approximation coefficients, offering a richer decomposition tree.

### [Blockwise Wavelet Compression](6100-blockwise-wavelet-compression.md)
Splits 3D data into small, equal-sized blocks compressed individually along three axes.

### L2 Norm Wavelet Compression
*   **Step:** [S1, unofficial] Compute coefficients representing the data in the normalized Haar wavelet basis. [S1, unofficial] Sort the coefficients in order of decreasing magnitude to produce the sequence $c_{\sigma}(0), c_{\sigma}(1), \dots, c_{\sigma}(M-1)$ such that $|c_{\sigma}(0)| \ge |c_{\sigma}(1)| \ge \dots \ge |c_{\sigma}(M-1)|$. [S1, unofficial] Find the least $M' < M$ such that the sum of squared discarded coefficients is less than the error tolerance: $\sum_{i=M'}^{M-1} (c_{\sigma}(i))^2 < \epsilon^2$. [S1, unofficial] Alternative thresholding approach: Repeatedly discard all coefficients smaller in magnitude than a threshold, doubling the threshold each iteration until the allowable error level is achieved.
*   **Formula:** [S1, unofficial] L2 error norm for the approximation: $\|f(x) - f_{M'}(x)\|_2^2 = \sum_{i=M'}^{M-1} (c_{\sigma(i)})^2 < \epsilon^2$. This follows from the orthonormality of the basis.
*   **Parameter:** [S1, unofficial] $\epsilon$: User-specified error tolerance. For lossless compression, $\epsilon = 0$. [S1, unofficial] $M'$: Number of retained coefficients after sorting and thresholding.
*   **Number:** [S1, unofficial] Compression ratios demonstrated: 1.22 bits/voxel (bpv), 0.4 bpv, and 0.10 bpv.

### B-spline Wavelet Compression
*   **Definition:** [S2] A preprocessing method using B-spline wavelets to compute wavelet coefficients and positions. Coefficients are arranged with decreasing importance, then compressed with delta encoding, quantization, run-length, and Huffman coding.

### Wavelet Slice Compression (Video Coding Approach)
*   **Definition:** [S2] A compression schema based on wavelets that treats 3D data as separate correlated slices to exploit temporal/spatial coherence amongst these slices, similar to video coding techniques.

### Modified Dyadic Wavelet with Rice Coding
*   **Definition:** [S2] A method using a modified 3-D dyadic wavelet transform tailored to volumetric medical images and an optimized Rice code of very low complexity for scalable lossless coding.

### Wavelet Compression (Haar Basis)
*   **Definition:** [S1, unofficial] Expressing original data using a smaller set of coefficients, with or without loss, using a wavelet basis.
*   **Parameter:** [S1, unofficial] $\epsilon$: User-specified error tolerance. For lossless compression, $\epsilon = 0$.
*   **Usage:** [S1, unofficial] Used in computer graphics based on work by Stollnitz.

### 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform
*   **Definition:** [S1, unofficial] A straightforward extension of the 2D algorithm to 3D data.

## Background
The application of multiresolution decomposition, specifically wavelets, to computer graphics and volume rendering emerged in the late 1990s and early 2000s. Key early works include Stollnitz's application of wavelets to image compression in computer graphics [S1, unofficial], and Lippert et al.'s use of B-spline wavelets for volume data [S2]. Insung et al. and Kim et al. explored block-based Haar wavelet transforms for volumes [S2]. Rodler proposed treating 3D data as correlated slices for wavelet compression [S2]. The Laplacian pyramid approach, originally from [GY95], was adapted for volume rendering to allow on-the-fly reconstruction [S2]. Later works included biorthogonal wavelet compression by [NS01, GWGS02] and hardware-accelerated decoding by Wetekam et al. [S2]. Wu et al. introduced modified dyadic wavelets with Rice coding for medical images [S2].
