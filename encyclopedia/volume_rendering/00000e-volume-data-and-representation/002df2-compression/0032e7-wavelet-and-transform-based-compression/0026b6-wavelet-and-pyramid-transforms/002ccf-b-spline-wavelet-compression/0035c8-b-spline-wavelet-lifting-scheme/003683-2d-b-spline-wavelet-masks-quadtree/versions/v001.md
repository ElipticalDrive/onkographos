# 2D B-spline Wavelet Masks (Quadtree)

Also called: Two-dimensional B-spline wavelet masks

## Overview
This method defines the convolution masks for lifting-based B-spline wavelet transforms on quadtree-organized vertex grids. It solves the problem of generating multiresolution hierarchies for 2D volume data by handling the two distinct types of new vertices introduced during subdivision: midpoints of old edges (blue) and centers of old faces (green). It sets itself apart from standard subdivision wavelets by using B-spline lifting to avoid over- and undershoots, and specifically addresses the data sparsity where values at "blue" positions are not directly available during the wavelet detail calculation, requiring linear interpolation from black vertices [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A 2D grid of scalar values organized in a quadtree structure. Vertices are classified as:
  - **Black**: Original coarse-grid vertices.
  - **Blue**: New vertices at the midpoints of old edges.
  - **Green**: New vertices at the centers of old faces.
- Output: Wavelet coefficients (detail) and approximation coefficients for the next resolution level.

**Data Structures:**
- The grid is implicitly defined by the quadtree hierarchy.
- Masks are 2D convolution kernels. Due to the irregular availability of vertex types, the masks are not uniform across the entire grid but depend on the local neighborhood topology.

**Steps:**
1. **Derive 1D Masks:** Start with the 1D B-spline lifting masks for approximation ($s$-lift) and detail ($w$-lift). Let $a$ be the scaling factor associated with the B-spline order (typically related to the subdivision rule).
2. **Convolve to 2D:** Derive the 2D masks by convolving the 1D masks in the two coordinate directions (horizontal and vertical). This results in separate masks for the different vertex types because the neighborhood structure differs for edge midpoints (blue) and face centers (green).
3. **Apply Masks Simultaneously:**
   - Apply the approximation masks (derived from 1D mask 4) in both directions.
   - Apply the detail masks (derived from 1D mask 6) in both directions.
   - Specifically, the masks corresponding to equations (3) and (4) are applied simultaneously for approximation, and masks (5) and (6) are applied simultaneously for detail [S1, unofficial].
4. **Handle Missing Blue Values:** When applying the 2D detail mask (5), values at the "blue" positions (edge midpoints) are required but do not exist in the input data (only black and green vertices are available or being computed). These missing values are linearly interpolated from the neighboring black vertices. This interpolation modifies the effective coefficients of mask (5) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $a$: Scaling factor for the B-spline lifting scheme. Typical value for linear B-splines is $1/2$ or related to the subdivision weights. Not given in the sources for the exact numerical value of $a$ in the final 2D mask, but implied to be part of the mask structure.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Missing Blue Values:** The primary pitfall is that no values exist at blue positions required for mask (5). These must be linearly interpolated from black vertices, which changes the effective mask (5) [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Linear interpolation introduces approximation errors. The source notes that this view of blue values as linear interpolations is an "approximation" [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### 3D B-spline Wavelet Masks (Octree)
This variant extends the 2D method to 3D octree-organized vertices. It handles three types of new vertices: edge midpoints (blue), face centers (green), and cube centroids (red). The key difference is that no values are available at blue and green vertices during encoding, requiring linear interpolation for blue (from two black vertices) and bilinear interpolation for green (from four black vertices). The masks grow in size and lose some unique structure. For decoding, the s-lift operation adjusts mask (7), where green vertex values are linear interpolations of red neighbors multiplied by $2a$, and blue vertex values are bilinear interpolations of red neighbors multiplied by $4a$. The factor $a$ is renamed to $H$. The final w-lift decoding reduces to an analogous mask (10) [S1, unofficial].

### Lifting Scheme for 3^2 Subdivision (2D)
This variant adjusts the 2D masks for $3^2$ subdivision, where only new vertices at face centers (green) are inserted, with no vertices at edge midpoints (blue). The w-lift operation is executed first using mask (5). The blue/black vertices are assumed to be similarly updated, so blue values remain linear interpolations. This leads to modified masks `w-liftencode(a, b)` (mask 13) and `s-liftencode2(a, b)` (mask 14). The linear `s-liftencode1(a, b)` mask has coefficients $1/4, 1/4$ for black vertices and $1/2$ for green vertices. The decoding mask `s-liftdecode2(a, b)` is derived from mask (3) by renaming factor $a$ to $H$ [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of vertices, as each vertex is processed once per level of the hierarchy. The convolution is local and fixed-size.
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of vertices. The wavelet coefficients are stored alongside or instead of the original data.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Scales with the resolution of the quadtree. Higher levels of detail require more coefficients.
- **Strengths:** Avoids over- and undershoots associated with standard subdivision wavelets. Efficient for hierarchical volume rendering.
- **Limitations:** The interpolation of missing blue values introduces approximation errors. The masks can grow in size, especially in 3D.
- **When to use:** When hierarchical volume representation is needed and topological correctness (no over/undershoots) is critical.
- **When not to use:** When exact reconstruction without interpolation artifacts is required, or when the grid structure does not match the quadtree/octree assumptions.

## Background
The method originates from the work on hierarchical volume representation using subdivision and B-spline wavelets. It was developed to provide a multiresolution hierarchy for volume data that avoids the artifacts of standard wavelet transforms. The specific 2D masks for quadtree organization were derived by convolving 1D B-spline lifting masks. The method has been applied to 2D and 3D volume rendering, as well as surface representation [S1, unofficial].
