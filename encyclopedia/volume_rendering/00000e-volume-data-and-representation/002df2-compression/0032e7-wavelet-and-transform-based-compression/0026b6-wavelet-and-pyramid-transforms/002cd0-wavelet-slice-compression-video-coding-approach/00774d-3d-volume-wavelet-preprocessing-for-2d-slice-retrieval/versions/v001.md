# 3D Volume Wavelet Preprocessing for 2D Slice Retrieval

Also called: 3D Wavelet Compression with Preprocessing for 2D Random Access, Layered Zero Coding with Preprocessing, Preprocessed 3D Wavelet Coding, Wavelet Preprocessing

## Overview
This method is a compression scheme for 3D volumetric data that enables fast random access to individual 2D slices by preprocessing the data before the wavelet transform to group relevant coefficients, avoiding full volume reconstruction [S1]. It solves the problem of inefficient random access to 2D slices by ensuring each subband coefficient corresponds to a specific 2D image, reducing the number of coefficients that must be decoded [S1]. Unlike standard 3D wavelet coding without preprocessing (WOP), which requires decoding a larger set of coefficients for any single slice, this method reduces the number of coefficients to decode by a factor of 2 at level 1, 4 at level 2, and 8 at level 3, resulting in approximately 2x faster decoding time [S1]. It builds on [Wavelet Slice Compression (Video Coding Approach)](5379-wavelet-slice-compression-video-coding-approach.md) by introducing a spatial rearrangement step specifically to isolate slice data within subbands.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   A 3D volumetric data set $G$ of size $S \times R \times C$, where $S$ is the number of slices (planes), $R$ is the number of rows, and $C$ is the number of columns [S1].
*   Data format: Gray-scale images with eight bits per pixel [S1].
*   Slice spacing: Typically 3–10 mm along the z-direction for MRI/CT data [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A compressed bitstream containing encoded wavelet coefficients and markers, allowing for the retrieval and reconstruction of any single 2D slice $p$ (where $1 \le p \le S$) [S1].

### Data Structures
The method operates on the 3D array $G_{pmn}$ where $p=1,\dots,S$, $m=1,\dots,R$, $n=1,\dots,C$ [S1].
*   **Subbands:** The wavelet transform produces subbands at various levels $x$. At level $x$, the subband images have dimensions $R/2^x \times C/4^x$ [S1].
*   **Bitstream:** Contains encoded coefficients for subbands scanned from coarsest resolution (LLL) to finest resolution (HHH). A special character (marker) is inserted into the bitstream after each scanning pass to delimit the coefficients required for a specific slice [S1].

### Parameters
*   **Filters:** Daub4 (Daubechies-4) wavelet transform is applied along the $x$ (row) and $y$ (column) directions. Haar wavelet transform is applied along the $z$ (slice) direction [S1]. Daub4 is chosen for its short filter bank, edge preservation, and lower computational complexity [S1]. Haar is chosen because it performs better for image sets with slice distances greater than 3 mm [S1].
*   **Quantization:** Step size is set to unity (1) to achieve nearly lossless compression [S1]. Note that compression may not be perfectly reversible due to non-integer tap weights and truncation to finite precision [S1].
*   **Transform Levels:** The number of levels $L$ is determined by the volume dimensions.

### Preprocessing Algorithm
Preprocessing rearranges data between planes before the wavelet transform. This ensures that for any two adjacent planes $r$ and $r+1$ (where $r$ is odd), the left half of the plane contains data from one 2D image and the right half contains data from the adjacent 2D image [S1].

**Level 1 Preprocessing ($y=1$):**
1.  Pair adjacent planes $p=1,2$; $p=3,4$; etc.
2.  Divide each plane into $2^1 = 2$ blocks along the column dimension (left half and right half).
3.  Let $a_{ij}$ denote the $j$-th block of plane $i$.
4.  For each pair of planes $(2k-1, 2k)$:
    *   $b_{2k-1, 1} = a_{2k-1, 1}$ (Copy left half of plane $2k-1$ to left half of resultant plane $2k-1$)
    *   $b_{2k-1, 2} = \text{flip}(a_{2k, 1})$ (Flip left half of plane $2k$ and copy to right half of resultant plane $2k-1$)
    *   $b_{2k, 1} = \text{flip}(a_{2k-1, 2})$ (Flip right half of plane $2k-1$ and copy to left half of resultant plane $2k$)
    *   $b_{2k, 2} = a_{2k, 2}$ (Copy right half of plane $2k$ to right half of resultant plane $2k$)
    *   Here, $\text{flip}$ denotes horizontal flipping (reversing the order of columns) [S1].
5.  Result: In resultant planes $b_{rr}$, image data is restricted to specific halves. Image 1 data is in the left half of plane 1; Image 2 data is in the right half of plane 1 and left half of plane 2, etc. [S1].

**General Preprocessing ($y > 1$):**
For subsequent levels, the process generalizes. Let $y$ be the current level.
1.  Pair adjacent planes $p$ and $p+1$ (where $p$ is odd).
2.  Divide the 2D subband images into $2^y$ blocks along the column dimension.
3.  Transfer odd-indexed blocks ($q=1, 3, \dots$) from plane $p$ to plane $r=p$ in the resultant $b_{rr}$.
4.  Transfer even-indexed blocks ($q=2, 4, \dots$) from plane $p+1$ to plane $r=p+1$ in the resultant $b_{rr}$.
5.  Specifically:
    *   $b_{r, 1} = a_{p, 1}$
    *   $b_{r, 2} = a_{p, 3}$
    *   ...
    *   $b_{r, 2^{y-1}} = a_{p, 2^{y-1}}$
    *   $b_{r, 2^{y-1}+1} = a_{p+1, 1}$
    *   ...
    *   $b_{r, 2^y} = a_{p+1, 2^{y-1}}$
    *   (Note: The source describes transferring blocks to specific positions to maintain the separation of image data into distinct halves/quadrants depending on the level. The core principle is that data from specific original slices is mapped to specific spatial regions in the subbands) [S1].

### Encoding
1.  Perform the 3D Wavelet Transform (WT) on the preprocessed data using Daub4 for $x,y$ and Haar for $z$ [S1].
2.  Scan subbands from the coarsest resolution (LLL) to the finest resolution (HHH) [S1].
3.  For a target slice $p$, identify the coefficients required to reconstruct it.
    *   At level $x$, the slices $p$ to $(p + 2^x - 1)$ of the original data map to the $((p + 2^x - 1)/2^x)$-th image of the subband [S1].
    *   The number of coefficients needed from each subband image to reconstruct a single 2D image is $R/2^x \times C/4^x$ [S1].
    *   For an odd-indexed image $p_o$, the coefficients are in the first half of the $((p_o + 1)/2)$-th image of the subbands.
    *   For an even-indexed image $p_e$, the coefficients are in the second half of the $p_e/2$-th image of subbands [S1].
4.  Encode these identified coefficients.
5.  Insert a special marker character into the bitstream after each scanning pass [S1].

### Decoding
1.  User specifies the 2D image index $p$ along the $z$-direction [S1].
2.  Retrieve relevant information from the bitstream using the markers to locate the coefficients for slice $p$ [S1].
3.  Perform Inverse Wavelet Transform (IWT) on the retrieved coefficients [S1].
4.  Apply postprocessing (the reverse of preprocessing) at every level after IWT to reconstruct the original slice [S1].

### Coefficient Identification Formulas
To reconstruct a single 2D image $p$:

1.  **Identify Plane Index $k$:**
    $$
    k = \begin{cases} 
    p/2^x & \text{if } p = a \times 2^x \text{ for } a = 1, 2, \dots \\
    \lfloor p/2^x \rfloor + 1 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    Where $x$ is the transform level, $p$ is the slice index, and $k$ is the index of the subband image plane containing the relevant coefficients [S1].

2.  **Identify Block Number $c$:**
    Divide the $k$-th subband image into $2^x$ equal parts of dimension $R/2^x \times C/4^x$.
    Match index $c$ with $z$, where:
    $$
    z = \begin{cases} 
    2^x & \text{if } p = a \times 2^x \text{ for } a = 1, 2, \dots \\
    p \pmod{2^x} & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    $$
    (Note: The source defines $z$ based on $p$ and $2^x$. The block number $c$ corresponds to the position within the subband plane determined by this mapping) [S1].

### Haar Wavelet Formulas
The Haar transform along the $z$-direction uses the following filters [S1]:

*   **Haar Trend (Approximation):**
    $$ a_m^1 = \frac{(f_{2m-1} + f_{2m})}{\sqrt{2}} $$
*   **Haar Fluctuation (Detail):**
    $$ d_m^1 = \frac{(f_{2m-1} - f_{2m})}{\sqrt{2}} $$
    Where $f$ is the input signal, $a_m^1$ is the trend coefficient at level 1, $d_m^1$ is the fluctuation coefficient at level 1, and $m$ is the index [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** The use of non-integer tap weights (Daub4) and truncation to finite precision means the compression is "nearly lossless" but not perfectly reversible [S1].
*   **Slice Spacing:** The method's performance is sensitive to slice spacing. For small slice spacing (high correlation, e.g., 2 mm), the preprocessing affects the correlation between slices, leading to a slight decrease in PSNR and Compression Ratio compared to WOP. The impact is less prominent for larger slice spacings (3–10 mm) [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The block division assumes dimensions are divisible by powers of 2. If $R$ or $C$ are not divisible by $2^x$, padding or handling of boundary blocks is required, though not explicitly detailed in the source [S1].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Decoding Time:** The method provides approximately 2x improvement in decoding time for slice spacing of 3–10 mm compared to other 3D encoding/2D decoding schemes (WOP) [S1]. Specifically, decoding speedup was measured at ~1.98x across four MRI image sets [S1].
*   **Coefficient Reduction:** The number of coefficients needed to be decoded from each subband image is reduced by a factor of 2 at level 1, 4 at level 2, and 8 at level 3 compared to WOP [S1].
*   **Memory:** Memory complexity is similar to standard 3D wavelet compression, as the entire volume is transformed, but the bitstream can be structured to allow random access to specific coefficients without decoding the entire volume [S1].

### Measured Performance
*   **Hardware/Resolution:** Evaluated on four sets of MRI images with dimensions ranging from $22 \times 64 \times 64$ to $64 \times 128 \times 128$ [S1].
*   **PSNR:** The percentage discrimination in PSNR between WP and WOP is small, ranging from -0.5% to -0.07% across four image sets, indicating WOP is slightly better in PSNR [S1].
*   **Compression Ratio (CR):** WP has a slightly lower CR than WOP due to the preprocessing affecting correlation [S1].

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:** Fast random access to 2D slices; significant reduction in decoding time; good compression ratio for medical imaging data with typical slice spacing [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Slight degradation in PSNR and CR compared to WOP, especially for small slice spacing; nearly lossless rather than lossless due to precision issues [S1].

### When to Use
*   Use when fast random access to individual 2D slices is required from a 3D volume.
*   Suitable for medical imaging (MRI/CT) with slice spacing of 3–10 mm [S1].
*   Not recommended when maximum compression ratio or PSNR is the primary concern and random access speed is less critical [S1].
