# 3D Wavelet-based Encoder with Multi-Component Transformation

Also called: 3D-DWT, Lifting-based 3D wavelet transform, Lifting-Based 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform, JP3D DWT, 3D Wavelet Compression

## Overview
This method compresses 3D volumetric data by applying a lifting-based discrete wavelet transform (DWT) along the component axis (e.g., Y, U, V) to remove inter-component correlations, followed by spatial 2D wavelet decomposition on the resulting subbands [S2]. It differs from pure spatial 3D wavelet approaches by explicitly treating the volume as a multi-component signal (similar to JPEG2000 Part-2) to exploit correlations between color channels or data types before spatial compression [S2].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A 3D volume $\mathbf{S}$ where each voxel consists of $C$ components (e.g., $C=3$ for RGB or YUV) [S2]. The data is typically integer-valued, such as 10-bit medical images [S4, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A compressed bitstream generated by entropy coding the wavelet coefficients using the EBCOT algorithm (JPEG2000 Part-1) [S2][S4, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method operates on the volume as a grid of multi-component samples [S2]. No specific auxiliary data structures (like trees or linked lists) are required for the transform itself; it operates in-place on the voxel arrays [S3].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Multi-Component Transformation (Axial/Component Axis):**
    Apply a 1D lifting-based wavelet transform along the component axis (the dimension indexing the different components of each voxel) [S2].
    *   For the luminance component (Y), use the **Haar** wavelet lifting scheme [S2].
    *   For chrominance components (U, V), use the **5/3** wavelet lifting scheme [S2].
    *   This step decomposes the multi-component vector at each spatial location into low-pass (LL) and high-pass (H) subbands relative to the component axis [S2].
    *   *Note:* The Haar wavelet is preferred for luminance because longer filters (like 5/3) can generate high-frequency energy and ghosting artifacts when inter-component correlations are low or the number of components is small [S2].

2.  **Spatial Decomposition:**
    For each subband frame generated in Step 1 (e.g., LLL, LLH, LH, H), apply a 2D spatial lifting-based wavelet transform [S2].
    *   The transform is applied horizontally and vertically [S3].
    *   The number of decomposition levels can vary per subband (e.g., low-pass subbands may be decomposed deeper than high-pass subbands) [S2].
    *   This results in spatial subbands such as LLL, HLL, LHL, HHL, LLH, HLH, LHH, HHH for a 1-level 3D decomposition [S3].

3.  **Coding:**
    Encode the spatially decomposed wavelet coefficients using the **EBCOT** (Embedded Block Coding with Optimized Truncation) method, as defined in the JPEG2000 Part-1 standard [S2][S4, unofficial].

### Formulas and Lifting Scheme Details
The lifting-based DWT separates a signal into low-pass ($L$) and high-pass ($H$) components using prediction and update steps [S1]. For a 1D signal $\mathbf{S}$ defined on a grid, the process is defined as follows [S1, S3]:

**1. Poly-phase Decomposition:**
Divide the signal into even samples ($\mathbf{S}^{(0)}_0$) and odd samples ($\mathbf{S}^{(0)}_1$) based on poly-phase sub-grids [S1, S3].

**2. Lifting Steps (Iterative for $i = 1$ to $M$):**
The $i$-th prediction and update steps are performed in-place [S1, S3].

*   **Prediction Step (High-pass):**
    $$ \hat{P}_{d,(i)}[\mathbf{S}^{(i-1)}_0] = \left\lfloor K_P \sum_{k=-K_P}^{K_P} c_{P,i,k} \mathbf{S}^{(i-1)}_0[\mathbf{l}_1 - (2k+1)d] \right\rfloor $$
    $$ \mathbf{S}^{(i)}_1[\mathbf{l}_1] = \mathbf{S}^{(i-1)}_1[\mathbf{l}_1] - \hat{P}_{d,(i)}[\mathbf{S}^{(i-1)}_0] $$
    Where $\mathbf{l}_1$ indexes the odd samples, $d$ is the direction vector, $K_P$ is the prediction filter half-length, and $c_{P,i,k}$ are coefficients [S1].

*   **Update Step (Low-pass):**
    $$ \hat{U}_{d,(i)}[\mathbf{S}^{(i)}_1] = \left\lfloor K_U \sum_{k=-K_U}^{K_U} c_{U,i,k} \mathbf{S}^{(i)}_1[\mathbf{l}_0 - (2k+1)d] + 0.5 \right\rfloor $$
    $$ \mathbf{S}^{(i)}_0[\mathbf{l}_0] = \mathbf{S}^{(i-1)}_0[\mathbf{l}_0] + \hat{U}_{d,(i)}[\mathbf{S}^{(i)}_1] $$
    Where $\mathbf{l}_0$ indexes the even samples, $K_U$ is the update filter half-length, and $c_{U,i,k}$ are coefficients [S1].

*   **Integer Arithmetic:**
    The floor operator $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$ and the $+0.5$ offset in the update step ensure integer arithmetic, guaranteeing perfect reversibility for lossless compression [S1, S3]. Floating-point arithmetic is avoided to prevent approximation errors [S1].

**3. Specific Filters:**
*   **Haar (for Y):**
    *   Prediction: $s_x \leftarrow s_x - \lfloor (s_{x-1} + s_{x+1})/2 \rfloor$ [S3].
    *   Update: $s_x \leftarrow s_x + \lfloor (s_{x-1} + s_{x+1} + 2)/4 \rfloor$ [S3].
*   **5/3 (for U, V):**
    *   Uses the standard Cohen-Daubechies-Feauveau (CDF) 5/3 lifting coefficients [S1, S3].

**4. Final Scaling:**
After $M$ lifting steps, the low-pass and high-pass coefficients are scaled by $G_L$ and $G_H$ respectively to preserve energy [S1]:
$$ L_D[\mathbf{l}_0] = G_L \mathbf{S}^{(M)}_0[\mathbf{l}_0] $$
$$ H_D[\mathbf{l}_1] = G_H \mathbf{S}^{(M)}_1[\mathbf{l}_1] $$

```python
def lifting_1d(signal, filter_type='haar'):
    """
    Performs a 1D lifting-based DWT on a 1D array.
    Returns (low_pass, high_pass).
    """
    even = signal[0::2]
    odd = signal[1::2]
    
    if filter_type == 'haar':
        # Prediction: odd <- odd - floor((even_prev + even_next) / 2)
        for i in range(len(odd)):
            prev_e = even[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_e = even[i+1] if i < len(even)-1 else 0
            odd[i] -= (prev_e + next_e) // 2
        # Update: even <- even + floor((odd_prev + odd_next + 2) / 4)
        for i in range(len(even)):
            prev_o = odd[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_o = odd[i+1] if i < len(odd)-1 else 0
            even[i] += (prev_o + next_o + 2) // 4
    elif filter_type == '5/3':
        # Step 1: Predict odd from even
        for i in range(len(odd)):
            prev_e = even[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_e = even[i+1] if i < len(even)-1 else 0
            odd[i] -= (prev_e + next_e) // 2
        # Step 2: Update even from odd
        for i in range(len(even)):
            prev_o = odd[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_o = odd[i+1] if i < len(odd)-1 else 0
            even[i] += (prev_o + next_o + 2) // 4
        # Step 3: Predict odd from updated even
        for i in range(len(odd)):
            prev_e = even[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_e = even[i+1] if i < len(even)-1 else 0
            odd[i] -= (prev_e + next_e) // 2
        # Step 4: Update even from updated odd
        for i in range(len(even)):
            prev_o = odd[i-1] if i > 0 else 0
            next_o = odd[i+1] if i < len(odd)-1 else 0
            even[i] += (prev_o + next_o + 2) // 4
            
    # Reorder: even samples are low-pass, odd samples are high-pass
    return even, odd

def multi_component_transform(volume):
    """
    Applies wavelet transform along the component axis.
    volume shape: (X, Y, Z, C)
    """
    low_pass_components = []
    high_pass_components = []
    
    # Process each spatial location across components
    for x in range(volume.shape[0]):
        for y in range(volume.shape[1]):
            for z in range(volume.shape[2]):
                comp_vector = volume[x, y, z, :]
                # Use Haar for Y (index 0), 5/3 for U,V (indices 1,2)
                f_type = 'haar' if len(comp_vector) == 1 else '5/3'
                lp, hp = lifting_1d(comp_vector, f_type)
                low_pass_components.append(lp)
                high_pass_components.append(hp)
                
    # Reshape back to volume format (simplified conceptual flow)
    # In practice, this is done per voxel vector across C
    return low_pass_components, high_pass_components

def spatial_transform(subband, levels=3):
    """
    Applies 2D spatial wavelet transform to a subband.
    """
    current = subband
    for level in range(levels):
        # Apply 1D DWT along rows
        rows = [lifting_1d(row, '5/3')[0] for row in current]
        current = [row for row in rows]
        # Apply 1D DWT along columns to the low-pass rows
        cols = [lifting_1d(col, '5/3')[0] for col in current]
        current = cols
    return current

def compress_volume(volume):
    # 1. Multi-component transform
    lp_comp, hp_comp = multi_component_transform(volume)
    
    # 2. Spatial transform on subbands
    # Typically, low-pass subbands are transformed more levels than high-pass
    lp_spatial = spatial_transform(lp_comp, levels=3)
    hp_spatial = spatial_transform(hp_comp, levels=1)
    
    # 3. EBCOT Coding per JPEG2000 Part-1 standard [G]
    bitstream = encode_ebcot(lp_spatial) + encode_ebcot(hp_spatial)
    return bitstream
```

### Parameters
*   **Filter Types:** Haar for luminance (Y), 5/3 for chrominance (U, V) [S2].
*   **Decomposition Levels:** Variable per subband. Low-pass subbands (LLL) can be decomposed up to 3 levels, while high-pass subbands (H, LH, etc.) may be decomposed fewer levels or not at all [S2].
*   **Scaling Factors ($G_L, G_H$):** Computed from synthesis wavelet filters to make the transform unitary [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Reversibility:** Floating-point arithmetic introduces approximation errors, making the transform irreversible. Integer calculus with floor operators is mandatory for lossless compression [S1].
*   **Artifact Generation:** Using longer filters (5/3) for luminance when inter-component correlations are low or the number of components is small generates high-frequency energy and causes ghosting artifacts. Haar is preferred for Y to avoid this [S2].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The pseudocode notes boundary handling for the lifting steps (e.g., $s_{x-1}$ when $x=0$). Standard JPEG2000 uses symmetric extension or periodic extension, but the specific method is not detailed in the sources [G].

## Subtopics

### JP3D (JPEG2000 Part-10)
A standard specifically for 3D wavelet compression. It is mentioned as potentially providing better performance than the multi-component approach but was in the Working Draft stage at the time of the cited literature [S2].

### Direction-Adaptive DWT (DA-DWT)
An approach where the DWT direction is adapted to the data structure. It was evaluated for volumetric medical images [S3].

### Block-based Intra-band Prediction
An alternative approach to improve lossless JP3D bitrates evaluated in related studies [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression Performance:** The method achieves significant bitrate reduction compared to traditional approaches. In the Leo sequence experiment (128x80x86 resolution), the depth-plane reduction approach reached 33.08 dB PSNR at 0.63 bpv, compared to 1.25 bpv for the traditional approach [S2].
*   **Efficiency:** For dense data, wavelet methods (especially JPEG2000) are more effective than dictionary-based methods and RLE [S4, unofficial].
*   **Speed:** Decompression speed for wavelet methods is generally slower than BZIP2 or RLE, but BZIP2 offers higher compression ratios for some data types [S4, unofficial].
*   **Scalability:** Supports progressive transmission and multi-resolution representation, allowing lossy-to-lossless scalability [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The method may suffer from inability to access individual slices without decoding all slices in the volumetric image, depending on the specific implementation [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method is based on the lifting scheme for DWT, originally developed for JPEG2000 [S1]. The specific application to 3D volumes with multi-component transformation leverages JPEG2000 Part-2 (multi-component) and Part-1 (EBCOT) standards [S2].
*   **History:** Developed for volumetric medical image compression (CT, MRI) to provide reversible compression, as axial KLT was found to not maximize coding gain compared to axial DWT [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented for multi-resolution compression and progressive transmission of medical data using JPEG2000 and JPIP [S4, unofficial].
