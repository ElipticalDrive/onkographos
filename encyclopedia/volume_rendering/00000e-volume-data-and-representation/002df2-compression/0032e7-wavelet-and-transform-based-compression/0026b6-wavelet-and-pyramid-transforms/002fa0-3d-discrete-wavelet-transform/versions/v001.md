# 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform

Also called: 3D Wavelet Transformation

## Overview
The 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform (3D DWT) extends the 2D wavelet algorithm to volumetric data by applying a separable filter bank along the three spatial axes [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of compressing and representing 3D volume data in a multi-resolution hierarchy, enabling efficient storage and adaptive rendering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its siblings, it operates directly on the 3D grid without prior geometric simplification or specific basis constraints like Haar or L2 norms, serving as the foundational transform for wavelet-based volume compression [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D volume data array $V$ of dimensions $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$.
*   **Output:** A set of wavelet coefficients organized in subbands (approximation and detail) forming a pyramid structure.

**Data Structures:**
The output is typically stored as a hierarchy of subbands. For a single level of decomposition, the volume is split into 8 subbands based on the low-pass (L) and high-pass (H) filtering along each axis:
*   $LLL$: Approximation (Low, Low, Low)
*   $LLH$: Detail in Z (Low, Low, High)
*   $LHL$: Detail in Y (Low, High, Low)
*   $LHH$: Detail in Y and Z (Low, High, High)
*   $HLL$: Detail in X (High, Low, Low)
*   $HLH$: Detail in X and Z (High, Low, High)
*   $HHL$: Detail in X and Y (High, High, Low)
*   $HHH$: Detail in X, Y, and Z (High, High, High)

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Separable Filtering:** Apply 1D wavelet transform along the X-axis, then Y-axis, then Z-axis. This is equivalent to convolving the volume with 3D basis functions formed by the tensor product of 1D wavelet filters.
2.  **Subsampling:** Downsample the filtered data by a factor of 2 along each axis to maintain the critical sampling rate.
3.  **Recursive Decomposition:** Repeat steps 1 and 2 on the approximation subband ($LLL$) until the dimension of the approximation subband becomes small enough (e.g., $16 \times 16 \times 16$) [S1, unofficial].

    return coefficients

**Parameters:**
*   **Levels:** Number of decomposition levels. Typical values depend on volume size; stopping at $16^3$ is suggested [S1, unofficial].
*   **Wavelet Filter:** Choice of wavelet (e.g., Daubechies, Coiflet). Not specified in sources [G].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Boundary Handling:** The sources do not specify boundary extension methods (e.g., symmetric, periodic). This is a critical implementation detail [G].
*   **Non-power-of-2 Dimensions:** The algorithm assumes dimensions are divisible by $2^{\text{levels}}$. Padding may be required [G].

## Subtopics

### [Depth-plane Reduction](6446-depth-plane-reduction.md)
A technique that shifts voxels to reduce depth planes before transformation, concentrating energy in the baseband.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Time complexity is $O(N)$ for $N$ voxels per level, with $O(N \log N)$ total for $O(\log N)$ levels. Memory complexity is $O(N)$ for storage of coefficients.
*   **Performance:** Efficient for compressing smooth volumetric data. High-frequency details are sparsely represented.
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful boundary handling. Not inherently adaptive to geometric features without additional techniques like depth-plane reduction.

## Background
The 3D DWT is a straightforward extension of 2D wavelet algorithms to 3D data [S1, unofficial]. It has been used in computer graphics for image and volume compression, leveraging multiresolution decomposition for efficient representation [S1, unofficial].
