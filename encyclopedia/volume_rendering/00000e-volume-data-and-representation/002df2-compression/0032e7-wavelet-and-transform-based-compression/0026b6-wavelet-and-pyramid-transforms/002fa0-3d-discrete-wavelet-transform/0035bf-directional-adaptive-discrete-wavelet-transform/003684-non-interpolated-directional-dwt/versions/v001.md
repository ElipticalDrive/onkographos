# Non-interpolated Directional DWT

## Overview
Non-interpolated Directional DWT is a variant of the Directional Adaptive Discrete Wavelet Transform (DA-DWT) that performs wavelet decomposition using integer direction vectors for prediction and update operations [S1]. It solves the problem of aligning wavelet basis functions with smooth, non-axis-aligned features in volumetric data (such as CT or MRI scans) without introducing interpolation artifacts or violating causality constraints [S1]. It differs from its parent, Directional Adaptive Discrete Wavelet Transform, by explicitly restricting direction vectors to $\mathbb{Z}^3$ to ensure that no sub-sampling or interpolation is required during the transform steps [S1, 7756-non-interpolated-directional-dwt.md]. Unlike its sibling, Interpolated Directional DWT, which allows fractional coordinates, this method guarantees exact grid alignment, simplifying implementation at the cost of reduced directional flexibility [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Volumetric data grid $\Pi$ with dimensions $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$.
- A predefined discrete set of integer direction vectors $\mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{Z}^3$.
- Filter support limits $K_P$ (prediction) and $K_U$ (update).

**Outputs:**
- Low-pass sub-band coefficients on grid $\Pi_{D0}$.
- High-pass sub-band coefficients on grid $\Pi_{D1}$.

**Data Structures:**
- The grid $\Pi$ is a 3D array of floating-point values.
- Direction vectors $d$ are 3-tuples of integers $(d_x, d_y, d_z)$.
- $\Pi_{D0}$ and $\Pi_{D1}$ are logical partitions of $\Pi$ determined by the chosen direction $d$.

**Constraints on Direction Vector $d$:**
1. $d \in \mathbb{Z}^3$ (integer coordinates only).
2. Depending on the decomposition direction $D$ (H, V, or A), one coordinate of $d$ must be odd to ensure proper sub-sampling parity [S1].
3. The line segment from the origin $(0,0,0)$ to $d$ must not intersect any other integer grid point in $\Pi$. This ensures that $d$ is not linearly dependent with other grid vectors, preventing degenerate alignments [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Select Direction:** Choose a direction vector $d$ from the predefined set $\mathcal{D}$ that optimizes energy compaction (e.g., via rate-distortion optimization) [S1].
2. **Partition Grid:** Divide the input grid $\Pi$ into low-pass grid $\Pi_{D0}$ and high-pass grid $\Pi_{D1}$ based on the parity of the coordinates relative to $d$ [S1].
3. **Apply Prediction:** For each point $l_1 \in \Pi_{D1}$, compute the predicted value using neighbors in $\Pi_{D0}$ shifted by $d$. The constraint ensures causality:
   $$ l_1 - (2k+1)d \in \Pi_{D0}, \quad \forall l_1 \in \Pi_{D1}, \quad k \in [-K_P; K_P[ $$
   The prediction error is stored in the high-pass sub-band [S1].
4. **Apply Update:** For each point $l_0 \in \Pi_{D0}$, update the value using neighbors in $\Pi_{D1}$ shifted by $d$. The constraint is:
   $$ l_0 - (2k+1)d \in \Pi_{D1}, \quad \forall l_0 \in \Pi_{D0}, \quad k \in [-K_U; K_U[ $$
   The updated value is stored in the low-pass sub-band [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Interpolation Avoidance:** The direction vector $d$ must be in $\mathbb{Z}^3$. Using non-integer vectors would require interpolation, which is not supported by this method [S1].
- **Causality:** The constraints on $l_1$ and $l_0$ ensure that the prediction and update operations only depend on previously computed or causally ordered values, allowing for a valid inverse transform [S1].
- **Linear Dependence:** If the line segment from the origin to $d$ intersects other grid points, the direction is invalid because it leads to linearly dependent vectors, which can cause numerical instability or degenerate transforms [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Interpolated Directional DWT](7826-interpolated-directional-dwt.md)
Allows fractional direction vectors ($d \in \mathbb{R}^3$) for better alignment with smooth features, but requires interpolation and may yield negligible compression gains compared to integer-based methods.

### [Segmentation-Driven Directionally Adaptive DWT](7556-segmentation-driven-directionally-adaptive-dwt.md)
Uses segmentation masks to guide direction selection, differing from the exhaustive search or fixed sets used in non-interpolated variants.

### [Block-based DA-DWT](7586-block-based-da-dwt.md)
Applies directional transforms on a block-by-block basis, offering localized adaptivity compared to global direction selection.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The complexity is dominated by the search over the predefined set of direction vectors. For each candidate direction, the transform must be computed to evaluate energy compaction. This makes it more computationally expensive than non-directional DWT [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the input data, low-pass and high-pass sub-bands, and temporary buffers for the transform. No additional interpolation buffers are needed compared to interpolated variants [S1].
- **Strengths:** Guarantees causality without sub-sampling artifacts; avoids interpolation errors; computationally simpler than interpolated directional DWT [S1].
- **Limitations:** Limited directional flexibility due to integer constraint; may not align perfectly with smooth, non-axis-aligned features in medical images (CT/MRI) [S1].
- **When to Use:** Suitable for volumetric data where computational simplicity and exact grid alignment are prioritized over maximum directional adaptivity. Particularly relevant for modalities like CT or MRI where inter-slice sampling pitch is large [S1].

## Background
The Non-interpolated Directional DWT is part of the broader family of Directional Adaptive Discrete Wavelet Transforms (DA-DWT) developed for volumetric medical image compression. It was introduced to improve transform efficiency by leveraging directional wavelets, which can better capture the anisotropic nature of medical data (e.g., CT or MRI) where inter-slice sampling pitch is often larger than intra-slice pitch [S1]. The method builds on the principle that modifying prediction and update functions to accept generic direction vectors can enhance energy compaction, while restricting these vectors to integers ensures practical implementation without interpolation [S1].
