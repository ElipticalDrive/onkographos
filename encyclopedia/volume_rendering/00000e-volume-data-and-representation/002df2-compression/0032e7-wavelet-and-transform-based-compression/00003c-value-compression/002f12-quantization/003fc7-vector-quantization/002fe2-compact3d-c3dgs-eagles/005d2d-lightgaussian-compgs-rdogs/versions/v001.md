# LightGaussian / CompGS / RDOGS

Also called: CompGS, Deng et al, Pruning and Quantization for 3DGS, RDOGS

## Overview
LightGaussian (also known as CompGS) is a compression method for 3D Gaussian Splatting that reduces storage requirements by pruning redundant Gaussians and quantizing parameters using learned codebooks [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from parent methods like Compact3D by explicitly avoiding redundant Gaussian splitting during training via a sliding window mask, rather than relying solely on post-hoc vector quantization of static parameters [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and outputs:**
- Input: A trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (set of Gaussians with position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients, and scale).
- Output: A compressed representation with reduced Gaussian count and quantized parameters.

**Data structures:**
- Gaussian attributes: Position ($\mathbf{p}$), Scale ($\mathbf{s}$), Rotation ($\mathbf{q}$), Opacity ($\alpha$), Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients ($\mathbf{c}$), and potentially albedo/roughness/metallic if relighting is supported [S1, unofficial].
- Codebook: A learned set of vectors used for quantizing SH coefficients and other parameters.

**Steps:**
1. **Pruning via Sliding Window Mask:** During the training process, a sliding window mask is applied to prevent the creation of redundant Gaussians. This mask identifies regions where Gaussian splitting would result in overlapping or unnecessary primitives, effectively reducing the total number of Gaussians ($N$) [S2, unofficial].
2. **Vector Quantization:** The remaining Gaussian parameters (specifically SH coefficients and potentially scale/opacity) are quantized using learned codebooks. Each parameter vector is replaced by the index of the nearest codeword in the codebook [S1, unofficial].
3. **Reconstruction:** The compressed scene is rendered by looking up the quantized parameters from the codebooks using the stored indices.

**Formulas:**
- Quantization error minimization: $\min_{\mathbf{c}_i} \sum_{j} \| \mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{c}_{i_j} \|^2$, where $\mathbf{x}_j$ are the original parameters and $\mathbf{c}_{i_j}$ are the selected codewords [S1, unofficial].
- Storage reduction: The method achieves approximately $10\times$ smaller storage compared to the original 3DGS representation [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Codebook size: Not given in the sources.
- Sliding window size: Not given in the sources.

**Edge cases and pitfalls:**
- Precision loss from quantization may affect high-frequency details.
- The sliding window mask must be tuned to avoid over-pruning essential geometry.

## Operating characteristics
- **Storage:** Achieves approximately $10\times$ smaller storage compared to the original 3DGS representation [S1, unofficial].
- **Quality:** Minimal quality loss is reported [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Significant storage reduction while maintaining visual quality.
- **Limitations:** Requires additional training steps for codebook learning and mask application.

## Background
LightGaussian, also referred to as CompGS, was introduced by Deng et al. [188] as a method to compress 3D Gaussian Splatting representations [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the idea of reducing redundancy in Gaussian splatting by combining pruning during training with post-training quantization. The method is part of a broader effort to make 3DGS more practical for storage and transmission [S1, unofficial].
