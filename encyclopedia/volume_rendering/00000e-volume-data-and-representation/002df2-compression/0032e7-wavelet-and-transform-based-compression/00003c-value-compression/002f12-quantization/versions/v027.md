# Quantization

Also called: Quantization with Matrix

## Overview
Quantization reduces the precision of volume data coefficients or neural field parameters to decrease storage and memory bandwidth requirements. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Predictive and Differential Coding](4393-predictive-and-differential-coding.md) by operating on the amplitude of values rather than their spatial or temporal correlations, and from [Standard Image Compression Formats](4394-standard-image-compression-formats.md) by often employing domain-specific strategies (e.g., per-block extrema or neural field scaling) rather than fixed block-based codecs.

## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of mapping a continuous or high-precision domain to a discrete, lower-precision set of values. This process introduces quantization error, which is managed through bit-allocation, codebook design, or error compensation. The core operation is a mapping function $Q(v)$ that transforms a value $v$ to a quantized index or value $v'$, and an inverse $Q^{-1}(v')$ for reconstruction.

### Lossy Quantization for Coarsened Patches
This method applies quantization to coarsened patches within a bintree hierarchy, where the quantization error $q$ is proportional to the coarsening error $c$ ($q = qc$) [S2]. Quantization levels are restricted to power-of-two multiples of the vertical resolution $\Delta z$ to enable efficient bit-shift implementation [S2]. The shift amount $k$ is calculated as $k = \lceil \log_2(\epsilon_q / \Delta z) \rceil + 1$ if $\epsilon_q \ge \Delta z$, otherwise $0$ [S2]. Vertices are classified as boundary (corners $C$, legs $L$, hypotenuse $H$) or interior $I$; corners are stored at full precision, and quantization is applied to original height values (not residuals) to ensure adjacent patches agree on shared boundaries [S2].

### HSV Colour Sorting
A technique to preserve color quality during DXT1 compression by reordering colors based on HSV values before compression [S3, unofficial]. Its effectiveness depends on which HSV component (H, S, or V) is the most frequent in the voxel data's color set [S3, unofficial].

### Interpolation Symmetry Quantization
A method to improve quantization accuracy by exploiting the symmetry of linear interpolation between segments [S4]. It quantizes both $w$ and $1 - w$ using the scalar quantizer's codebook, keeping the representation with lower error, and stores either $(i, j, w)$ or $(j, i, 1 - w)$ in the output stream [S4].

### Position Lattice Quantization
An attempted method to compress Gaussian positions by quantizing them to a lattice structure and embedding positional constraints into training [S5]. It fails to compress positions without introducing significant rendering error and is identified as a future research direction [S5].

### Post-finetune Refinement
A refinement step using a small number of bits to enhance the reconstruction quality of important voxels, compensating for coarse quantization losses [S6]. Disabling it causes a -1.72 dB $\Delta$PSNR and -0.07 $\Delta$SSIM on the Synthetic-NeRF dataset, confirming its necessity for maintaining rate-distortion performance [S6].

## Background
Quantization is a fundamental component of lossy compression, originating in signal processing and adapted for volume rendering to handle large datasets and neural representations. In volume rendering, it is often applied to transform coefficients [S1] or neural field parameters [S1][S3, unofficial]. The specific application of quantization matrices for 3D DCT coefficients was detailed in the context of neural residual radiance fields for streamable free-viewpoint videos [S1].
