# Wavelet Kernel Selection

Also called: Haar/5x3/9x7/(6, 6) Kernel Comparison, Wavelet Kernel Parameters

## Overview
Wavelet Kernel Selection determines the specific filter coefficients used in the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for volume data compression. It solves the trade-off between lossless reversibility and lossy energy compaction efficiency. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Triadic frequency band decomposition](828-triadic-frequency-band-decomposition.md) and [Laplacian Pyramid](2250-laplacian-pyramid.md) by its reliance on biorthogonal or orthogonal filter banks with specific vanishing moments and support lengths, rather than fixed polynomial or pyramid-based splitting.

## How it works
The method involves selecting one of four standard kernels: Haar, 5x3, 9x7, or (6,6). The selection is driven by the compression mode (lossless vs. lossy) and the statistical properties of the volume data.

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Input**: Compression mode (lossless/lossy), data characteristics (e.g., axial resolution, inter-slice correlation).
- **Output**: A set of lifting coefficients and parameters defining the chosen kernel.

### Data Structures
The kernel is defined by a parameter set. The layout for each kernel is as follows:

**1. 5x3 Kernel (Lossless)**
- `MK` (Max Lifting Steps): 1
- `PK` (Prediction Steps): 1
- `U` (Update Steps): 1
- `G` (Gain): 1
- `L` (Lifting Level): 1
- `H` (Height/Scale): 1
- `k` (Offset): 0
- `cP,i,k` (Prediction Coeffs): -1, 1
- `cU,i,k` (Update Coeffs): 0.5, 0.25

**2. Haar Kernel (Lossless)**
- `MK`: 1
- `PK`: 1
- `U`: 1
- `G`: 1
- `L`: 1
- `H`: 1
- `k`: 0
- `cP,i,k`: -1, 1
- `cU,i,k`: 0, 0.5

**3. 9x7 Kernel (Lossy)**
- `MK`: 2
- `PK`: 1
- `U`: 1
- `G`: 0
- `L`: 0.812893066
- `H`: 1
- `k`: 0, -1
- `cP,i,k`: 1.230174105, -0.052980119, -0.882911076, 0.443506852
- `cU,i,k`: -1, 1

**4. (6,6) Kernel (Lossy)**
- `MK`: 1
- `PK`: 3
- `U`: 3
- `G`: 1
- `L`: 1.306139349
- `H`: 1.140657705
- `k`: 0, -1, -2, 2, -3, 3
- `cP,i,k`: 150/256, 150/512, -25/256, -25/512, 3/256, 3/512
- `cU,i,k`: -1, 1

### Selection Logic and Performance
1. **Lossless Mode**: Select **5x3** or **Haar**.
   - The 5x3 kernel is preferred over Haar due to its longer support length, which provides better energy compaction even along the axial dimension where inter-pixel correlation is lower [S1].
   - The 5x3 kernel uses rational coefficients with dividends as powers of two, allowing perfect reversibility without rounding error accumulation [S1].
2. **Lossy/Near-Lossless Mode**: Select **9x7** or **(6,6)**.
   - Both offer vastly improved performance over 5x3 and Haar, with BD-PSNR improvements up to 2 dB at target bit-rates of 0.50, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and 3.0 bpp [S1].
   - The (6,6) kernel outperforms the 9x7 kernel for 7 out of 11 tested images [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Axial Resolution**: Images with low axial resolution (e.g., CT6, MRI3) have less inter-slice correlation. The bit-rate gain from axial decomposition is smaller for these volumes, but the kernel choice (5x3 vs Haar) remains relevant [S1].
- **Precision**: The 9x7 and (6,6) kernels use floating-point coefficients. Implementations must handle precision carefully to avoid artifacts in near-lossless scenarios [S1].

## Subtopics

### JPEG 2000 5x3 Wavelet Kernel
A biorthogonal wavelet kernel constructed from the Cohen-Daubechies-Feauveau (CDF) 5/3 wavelet. It is the shortest symmetrical biorthogonal CDF wavelet with two vanishing moments. Its synthesis scaling function is a linear B-spline. It is selected by the JPEG committee for lossless compression in JPEG 2000 because all its filters have rational coefficients with dividends as powers of two, enabling perfect reversibility without loss of performance [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Depends on the number of lifting steps (`MK`). 5x3 and Haar are simpler (1 step) than 9x7 (2 steps) and (6,6) (3 steps).
- **Memory Complexity**: Low, as kernels are defined by small constant coefficient arrays.
- **Performance**:
  - **Lossless**: 5x3 > Haar [S1].
  - **Lossy**: (6,6) $\approx$ 9x7 > 5x3 > Haar [S1].
  - The (6,6) kernel offers higher energy compaction efficiency than 9x7 for some datasets [S1].
- **Strengths**: 5x3 is optimal for lossless; 9x7/(6,6) are optimal for lossy.
- **Limitations**: 9x7 and (6,6) are irreversible (lossy). Haar has poor energy compaction compared to 5x3.

## Background
The selection of these kernels is rooted in the JPEG 2000 standard, where the JPEG committee selected the 5x3 kernel for lossless and the 9x7 kernel for lossy compression [S1]. The 5x3 kernel is derived from the CDF 5/3 wavelet, constructed by factorizing a maximally flat Daubechies or Dubuc–Deslaurier half-band filter [S1]. The 9x7 kernel is a variation on the CDF biorthogonal cubic B-spline construction, using the shortest scaling of order four, with vanishing moments divided equally on both analysis and synthesis sides to make the basis functions almost orthogonal [S1]. The (6,6) kernel is an interpolation wavelet kernel proposed as an alternative to 9x7 for higher energy compaction [S1].
