# Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression

Also called: Residual Coding

## Overview
Residual coding is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard predictive coding by deriving residuals from an unquantized reconstruction, ensuring that residuals encoded at all bitrates remain similar, unlike residuals in standard MPEG video codecs [S1]. However, this method does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Original view images, a reconstructed volume (derived from reference views), and a target bitrate or quality threshold.
*   **Output:** Compressed bitstream containing the volume data and residual data.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on a per-view basis. For each view, the residual is a 2D image array of the same dimensions as the original view. The bitstream is structured to allow scalable decoding: first the volume data, then significant parts of the residual bitstream [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Volume Reconstruction (Unquantized):** Construct the volume from reference views. Generate a "direct reconstructed image" for the target view from this volume *without* applying quantization or entropy coding to the volume data itself. This serves as the prediction baseline [S1].
2.  **Residual Calculation:** Calculate the residual for each view as the pixel-wise difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image:
    $$ r(x,y) = I_{original}(x,y) - I_{reconstructed\_unquantized}(x,y) $$
    where $r$ is the residual, $I_{original}$ is the original view, and $I_{reconstructed\_unquantized}$ is the view reconstructed from the volume without quantization [S1].
3.  **Encoding:** Encode the residuals alongside the volume data. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are coded for all bitrates to preserve scalability [S1].
4.  **Decoding:** At the decoder, select the quality of the volume first. Then, decode parts of the residual bitstream until the target bitrate is obtained [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Bitrate Thresholds:** Residuals are employed at bitrates higher than those where view synthesis quality drops below lossless quality by specific thresholds: -0.05 dB, -0.1 dB, or -0.5 dB [S1].
    *   -0.05 dB: For applications demanding good quality of virtual views (e.g., 3D rendering, look-around, free-view point) [S1].
    *   -0.5 dB: For applications where views at original camera positions are significant [S1].

**Pitfalls and Edge Cases:**
*   **View Synthesis Degradation:** Residuals do not greatly improve reconstructed image quality at low bitrates but significantly exacerbate the quality of view synthesis [S1]. View synthesis performance can degrade up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].
*   **Suboptimal Performance at Low Quality:** The highest compression performance cannot be achieved by choosing a low-quality decoded volume. This is because residuals are obtained from the original volume, not directly from reconstructed images at each particular bitrate [S1].
*   **Scalability Constraint:** Because residuals are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction, the residuals encoded at all bitrates are similar [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** At 0.42 bpv with a -0.5 dB acceptable quality criterion, the method achieves the same PSNR (34 dB) as higher bitrate methods (0.68 bpv and 0.56 bpv) but with lower bitrate [S1]. However, subjective quality differs: smoother texture at higher bitrates, sharper edges at lower bitrates [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The method supports scalability in open-loop operation, where the same residuals are coded for all bitrates [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances compression performance (reconstructed image quality) by correcting errors at edges from imperfect volume construction or loss of high frequencies due to bitstream truncation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Deteriorates view synthesis quality, especially at low bitrates [S1]. Highest compression performance is not achievable with low-quality decoded volumes [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When reconstructed image quality is prioritized over view synthesis quality, or when specific bitrate thresholds allow for acceptable view synthesis degradation (-0.05, -0.1, or -0.5 dB) [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When view synthesis quality is critical and cannot tolerate degradation, or at low bitrates where residuals do not significantly improve image quality but worsen view synthesis [S1].

## Background
Residual coding for volumetric compression was investigated in the context of improving compression performance for realistic view synthesis applications [S1]. The technique was proposed to address the limitations of standard volumetric codecs by leveraging the difference between original and unquantized reconstructed images [S1].
