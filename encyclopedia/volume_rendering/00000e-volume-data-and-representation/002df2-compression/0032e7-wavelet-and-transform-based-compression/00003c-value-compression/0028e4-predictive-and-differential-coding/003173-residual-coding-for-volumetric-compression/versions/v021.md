# Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression

Also called: Residual Coding

## Overview
Residual coding is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]. It does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it [S1]. Unlike standard video codec residuals which change with bitrate, these residuals are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction, making them similar across all bitrates [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Original view images $I_{orig}$, and a direct reconstructed volume $V_{vol}$ (constructed from reference views) which has not undergone quantization or entropy coding.
*   **Output:** A bitstream containing the encoded volume data and the encoded residuals $R$.

**Data Structures:**
The residual for each view is a 2D image array of the same dimensions as the original view. The values are signed integers representing the difference $I_{orig} - I_{recon\_direct}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Volume Construction:** Construct the volume using information from reference views. This volume is used to generate a direct reconstruction of each view without any quantization or coding applied to the volume coefficients themselves [S1].
2.  **Residual Calculation:** For each view, calculate the residual $R$ as the difference between the original image $I_{orig}$ and the direct reconstructed image $I_{recon\_direct}$ from the unquantized volume:
    $$ R = I_{orig} - I_{recon\_direct} $$
    [S1]
3.  **Encoding:** Encode the residuals alongside the volume data. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are coded for all bitrates to preserve scalability [S1].
4.  **Decoding:** At the decoder, select the quality of the volume first. Then, decode parts of the residual bitstream until the target bitrate is obtained [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Bitrate Thresholds:** Residuals are employed at bitrates higher than those where view synthesis quality drops below lossless quality by specific thresholds: -0.05 dB, -0.1 dB, or -0.5 dB [S1].
    *   -0.5 dB represents applications where views at original camera positions are significant.
    *   -0.05 dB represents applications where good quality of virtual views is essential (e.g., 3D rendering, look-around, free-view point) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **View Synthesis Degradation:** Residuals significantly exacerbate the quality of view synthesis. The quality of view synthesis is degraded up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].
*   **Low Bitrate Inefficacy:** Residuals do not greatly improve the quality of reconstructed images at low bitrates [S1].
*   **Suboptimal Performance at Low Quality:** The highest compression performance cannot be achieved by choosing the low-quality decoded volume. This is because the residuals are obtained from the original volume, not directly from the reconstructed images at each particular bitrate [S1].
*   **Scalability vs. Optimality:** While open-loop operation preserves scalability (same residuals for all bitrates), it prevents achieving the absolute highest compression performance for specific low-quality targets because the residuals are fixed based on the unquantized reconstruction [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def residual_coding_encoder(original_views, reference_views):
    """
    Encodes volumetric data with residual coding.
    """
    # 1. Construct volume from reference views (assumed existing function)
    volume = construct_volume(reference_views)
    
    encoded_volume = encode_volume(volume) # Encode without quantization/coding for direct recon
    
    residuals = []
    for view in original_views:
        # 2. Direct reconstruction from unquantized volume
        direct_recon = reconstruct_view_from_volume(volume, view.camera_params)
        
        # 3. Calculate residual
        residual = view.image_data - direct_recon
        residuals.append(residual)
        
    # 4. Encode residuals
    encoded_residuals = encode_residuals(residuals)
    
    return encoded_volume, encoded_residuals

def residual_coding_decoder(encoded_volume, encoded_residuals, target_bitrate):
    """
    Decodes volumetric data with residual coding.
    """
    # 1. Decode volume to determine base quality
    volume = decode_volume(encoded_volume)
    
    # 2. Determine how much residual bitrate is available
    volume_bitrate = get_bitrate(encoded_volume)
    available_residual_bitrate = target_bitrate - volume_bitrate
    
    # 3. Decode parts of residual bitstream until target bitrate is obtained
    # Note: The specific logic for "parts of residual bitstream" depends on the 
    # entropy coder's scalability. Here we assume a simplified selection.
    residuals = decode_residuals_selective(encoded_residuals, available_residual_bitrate)
    
    # 4. Reconstruct final views
    final_views = []
    for i, view in enumerate(original_views):
        direct_recon = reconstruct_view_from_volume(volume, view.camera_params)
        # Apply residual to correct errors at edges/high frequencies
        final_image = direct_recon + residuals[i]
        final_views.append(final_image)
        
    return final_views
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The encoding and decoding complexity is dominated by the volume construction and reconstruction steps, plus the entropy coding of the residuals. The residual calculation itself is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels in the view.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the residuals for all views. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are stored for all bitrates, increasing the total bitstream size.
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   At 0.42 bpv with a -0.5 dB acceptable quality criterion, the method achieves the same PSNR (34 dB) as higher bitrate methods (0.68 bpv and 0.56 bpv) but with lower bitrate [S1].
    *   View synthesis performance degradation is up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].
    *   Residuals correct errors at edges caused by imperfect volume construction or loss of high frequencies due to bitstream truncation, providing sharper edges at lower bitrates compared to smoother textures at higher bitrates without residuals [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves reconstructed image quality (PSNR) at higher bitrates; provides scalability in open-loop mode; corrects edge errors.
*   **Limitations:** Degrades view synthesis quality; ineffective at low bitrates for image quality improvement; suboptimal for low-quality targets due to fixed residuals.
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity reconstructed images are required at high bitrates, and view synthesis quality is secondary or can tolerate a 0.1 dB degradation. Useful for applications like 3D rendering where original camera views are critical.
*   **When not to use:** When view synthesis quality is paramount; at low bitrates where residuals provide little benefit; when optimal compression for specific low-quality targets is required.

## Background
Originating from research into color volumetric compression for realistic view synthesis applications, this method was proposed to address the trade-off between reconstructed image quality and view synthesis quality [S1]. It was developed in the context of free-viewpoint video and 3D rendering applications where both original and virtual views are important [S1]. The method highlights the unique behavior of volumetric residuals compared to standard video coding residuals, noting that volumetric residuals are similar across bitrates because they are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction [S1].
