# Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression

Also called: Residual Coding

## Overview
Residual coding is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstruction from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]. It is distinct from standard video codec residuals because it is derived from an unquantized volume reconstruction, meaning the residuals encoded at all bitrates are similar [S1]. Unlike its siblings, it does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Original image data $I_{orig}$, and a direct reconstruction of the volume $I_{vol}$ generated without quantization or entropy coding.
*   **Output:** A bitstream containing the volume data and the encoded residuals.

**Data Structures:**
The residual for each view is a 2D image (or array) of the same dimensions as the original view. The values are floating-point or high-precision integers representing the difference $I_{orig} - I_{vol}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Residuals:** For each view, compute the residual image $R$ as the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]:
    $$ R = I_{orig} - I_{vol} $$
    [S1]
2.  **Encode Residuals:** Encode the residuals alongside the volume data. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are coded for all bitrates to preserve scalability [S1].
3.  **Decode:** At the decoder, select the quality of the volume first, then decode parts of the residual bitstream until the target bitrate is obtained [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Bitrate Thresholds:** Residuals are employed at bitrates higher than those where view synthesis quality drops below lossless quality by specific thresholds: -0.05 dB, -0.1 dB, or -0.5 dB [S1].
    *   -0.5 dB represents applications where views at original camera positions are significant.
    *   -0.05 dB represents applications where good quality of virtual views is essential (e.g., 3D rendering, look-around, free-view point) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   Residuals do not greatly improve reconstructed image quality at low bitrates but significantly exacerbate view synthesis quality [S1].
*   Highest compression performance cannot be achieved by choosing the low-quality decoded volume because residuals are obtained from the original volume, not directly from reconstructed images at each particular bitrate [S1].
*   View synthesis performance can degrade up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

    Encodes the residual between the original image and the direct volume reconstruction.

    without quantization or entropy coding.
    """
    # Step 1: Calculate residual
    # R = I_orig - I_vol
    residual = original_image - volume_reconstruction
    
    # Step 2: Encode residuals

    return residual_bitstream

## Subtopics

### Delta Encoding
Encodes differences between consecutive values or frames. Simpler than residual coding but less effective for complex volumetric structures. [Link](1096-delta-encoding.md)

### Residual Updates in Transform Domain
Applies residual coding in a transformed space (e.g., wavelet domain). Better for preserving high-frequency details but more complex. [Link](5256-residual-updates-in-transform-domain.md)

### Optimal Linear Volumetric Predictor with Truncated Huffman Coding
Uses a linear predictor optimized for volumetric data. More sophisticated prediction than simple difference. [Link](7759-optimal-linear-volumetric-predictor-with-truncated-huffman-c.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of pixels in the view for residual calculation. Encoding/decoding complexity depends on the entropy coder used for the residuals.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the residual image for each view.
*   **Measured Performance:** At 0.42 bpv with a -0.5 dB acceptable quality criterion, the method achieves the same PSNR (34 dB) as higher bitrate methods (0.68 bpv and 0.56 bpv) but with lower bitrate [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances compression performance (reconstructed image quality) at high bitrates. Corrects errors at edges from imperfect volume construction or loss of high frequencies due to bitstream truncation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Deteriorates view synthesis quality (up to 0.1 dB degradation at high bitrate) [S1]. Not effective at low bitrates [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality reconstructed images are required at high bitrates, and view synthesis quality is less critical or can tolerate minor degradation.
*   **When Not to Use:** When view synthesis quality is paramount, or at low bitrates where residuals provide little benefit but still incur overhead.

## Background
Originated in the context of colour volumetric compression for realistic view synthesis applications [S1]. The method was investigated to improve the quality of reconstructed images in volumetric codecs, particularly in scenarios where view synthesis performance was evaluated alongside compression performance [S1].
