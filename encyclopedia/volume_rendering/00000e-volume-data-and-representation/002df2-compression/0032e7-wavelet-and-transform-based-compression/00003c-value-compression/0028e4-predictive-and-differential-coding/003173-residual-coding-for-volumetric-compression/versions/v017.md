# Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression

Also called: Residual Coding

## Overview
Residual coding is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]. It does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it [S1]. Unlike standard video codecs where residuals depend on the quantized output, these residuals are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction, making them consistent across bitrates [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Original image data $I_{orig}$; Direct reconstructed volume data $I_{recon}$ (generated without quantization or coding).
**Outputs:** Encoded volume data; Encoded residual data.

**Data Structures:**
The method separates the bitstream into two components:
1.  **Volume Data:** The primary volumetric representation.
2.  **Residual Data:** The difference between $I_{orig}$ and $I_{recon}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Direct Reconstruction:** Generate $I_{recon}$ from the volume data without applying quantization or entropy coding. This represents the "lossless" reconstruction of the volumetric model itself [S1].
2.  **Residual Calculation:** Calculate the residual $R$ for each view/voxel as the difference between the original image and the direct reconstruction:
    $$ R = I_{orig} - I_{recon} $$
    Because $I_{recon}$ is derived without quantization, $R$ captures errors from imperfect volume construction or loss of high frequencies due to bitstream truncation [S1].
3.  **Encoding:** Encode the residuals alongside the volume data. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are coded for all bitrates to preserve scalability [S1].
4.  **Decoding:**
    *   Select the quality of the volume first.
    *   Decode parts of the residual bitstream until the target bitrate is obtained [S1].
    *   Reconstruct the final image by adding the decoded residuals to the decoded volume data.

**Parameters:**
*   **Bitrate Thresholds:** Residuals are employed at bitrates higher than those where view synthesis quality drops below lossless quality by specific thresholds: -0.05 dB, -0.1 dB, or -0.5 dB [S1].
    *   -0.5 dB represents applications where views at original camera positions are significant.
    *   -0.05 dB represents applications where good quality of virtual views is essential (e.g., 3D rendering, look-around, free-view point) [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Residuals do not greatly improve reconstructed image quality at low bitrates but significantly exacerbate view synthesis quality [S1].
*   Highest compression performance cannot be achieved by choosing the low-quality decoded volume because residuals are obtained from the original volume, not directly from reconstructed images at each particular bitrate [S1].
*   View synthesis performance can degrade up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** At 0.42 bpv with a -0.5 dB acceptable quality criterion, the method achieves the same PSNR (34 dB) as higher bitrate methods (0.68 bpv and 0.56 bpv) but with lower bitrate [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances compression performance; corrects errors at edges caused by imperfect volume construction or bitstream truncation [S1]. Allows flexibility in image size and quality via scalable bitstreams [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Deteriorates view synthesis quality; residuals are similar across all bitrates unlike MPEG video codec residuals [S1].

## Background
This method is described in the context of colour volumetric compression for realistic view synthesis applications [S1]. It addresses the trade-off between reconstructed image quality and view synthesis quality in multi-view volumetric codecs [S1].
