# Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression

Also called: Residual Coding

## Overview
Residual coding is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding [S1]. Unlike standard predictive coding which aims to reduce view synthesis artifacts, this method does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it [S1]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Delta Encoding](1096-delta-encoding.md) by deriving residuals from the unquantized volume reconstruction rather than previous samples, ensuring residuals encoded at all bitrates are similar [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Original image data $I_{orig}$, and a reconstructed volume $V$ (constructed from reference views) which is decoded directly without quantization or entropy coding to produce $I_{recon}$.
*   **Output:** Encoded residuals $R$ alongside the volume data.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Residual Map:** A 2D array or 3D volume of difference values $d(x,y,z) = I_{orig}(x,y,z) - I_{recon}(x,y,z)$. The values are typically integers or fixed-point numbers depending on the precision of $I_{orig}$ and $I_{recon}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Volume Reconstruction (Unquantized):** Construct the volume $V$ from reference view information. Decode or reconstruct the image $I_{recon}$ directly from $V$ without applying quantization or entropy coding to the volume coefficients. This ensures the residual is based on the "true" reconstruction error of the geometry/representation, not the quantization noise [S1].
2.  **Residual Calculation:** For each pixel or voxel, calculate the residual $d$:
    $$ d = I_{orig} - I_{recon} $$
    where $I_{orig}$ is the original image data and $I_{recon}$ is the direct reconstructed image from the volume [S1].
3.  **Encoding:** Encode the residuals $d$ using entropy coding (e.g., Huffman, Arithmetic coding) alongside the volume data [S1]. In open-loop operation, the same residuals are coded for all bitrates to preserve scalability [S1].
4.  **Decoding:**
    *   Select the quality of the volume first.
    *   Decode parts of the residual bitstream until the target bitrate is obtained [S1].
    *   Add the decoded residuals to the reconstructed image to obtain the final output:
        $$ I_{final} = I_{recon} + d_{decoded} $$

**Parameters:**
*   **Bitrate Thresholds:** Residuals are employed at bitrates higher than those where view synthesis quality drops below lossless quality by specific margins: -0.05 dB, -0.1 dB, or -0.5 dB [S1].
    *   -0.5 dB: Applications where views at original camera positions are significant.
    *   -0.05 dB: Applications demanding good quality of virtual views (e.g., 3D rendering, look-around, free-view point) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **View Synthesis Degradation:** Residuals do not greatly improve reconstructed image quality at low bitrates but significantly exacerbate the quality of view synthesis [S1]. View synthesis performance can degrade up to 0.1 dB at high bitrate when residuals are added [S1].
*   **Suboptimal Performance at Low Bitrates:** Highest compression performance cannot be achieved by choosing the low-quality decoded volume because residuals are obtained from the original volume, not directly from reconstructed images at each particular bitrate [S1].
*   **Scalability:** Unlike MPEG standard video codec residuals, residuals encoded at all bitrates are similar because they are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of pixels/voxels for residual calculation and encoding.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the residual map in addition to the volume data.
*   **Performance:** At 0.42 bpv with a -0.5 dB acceptable quality criterion, the method achieves the same PSNR (34 dB) as higher bitrate methods (0.68 bpv and 0.56 bpv) but with lower bitrate [S1]. It corrects errors at edges caused by imperfect volume construction or loss of high frequencies due to bitstream truncation [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enhances subjective quality (sharper edges) and PSNR at specific bitrates; preserves scalability in open-loop operation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Deteriorates view synthesis quality; not optimal for low-bitrate scenarios where view synthesis is critical [S1].

## Background
Originated in research on colour volumetric compression for realistic view synthesis applications [S1]. The method was investigated to balance the trade-off between reconstructed image quality and view synthesis performance in multi-view and volumetric rendering systems.
