# Predictive and Differential Coding

Also called: Differential Pulse-Code Modulation (DPCM), Predictive Coding, Residual Coding

## Overview
Predictive and Differential Coding compresses volumetric data by exploiting spatial, temporal, or transform-domain redundancy. Instead of encoding raw voxel values, the method predicts the current value from previously encoded neighbors or reference frames and encodes only the residual (difference) between the prediction and the actual value [S1, unofficial]. This family is distinct from transform-based methods (like [Wavelet and Transform-based Compression](6882-transform-based-block-coding.md)) because it operates directly on the data domain (or transform coefficients) using local correlation models rather than global frequency decomposition, though it is often used in conjunction with entropy coding. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Vector Quantization](907-vector-quantization.md) by preserving exact sample-to-sample relationships without the block-based distortion inherent to codebook lookups.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the mechanism of decomposing the input volume $V$ into a set of coefficients $C$ using a linear transform, followed by encoding and optional decoding for rendering.

1.  **Transform:** The volume is decomposed using a separable 1D filter bank applied along each axis. For a 3D volume, this results in subbands representing different frequency components. In wavelet transforms, these are typically approximation (low-pass) and detail (high-pass) coefficients. In Laplacian pyramids, they are detail residuals between levels of a Gaussian pyramid.
2.  **Hierarchy:** The decomposition is recursive. The approximation subband from level $j$ is further decomposed at level $j+1$, creating a tree-like structure of coefficients. This allows for multi-resolution access, where coarse data is available at the root and fine details are deeper in the tree.
3.  **Quantization and Entropy Encoding:** Coefficients are quantized (lossy) or kept precise (lossless). Entropy coding (e.g., Huffman, Arithmetic, Run-Length) is applied to the quantized or raw coefficients to produce the compressed bitstream.
4.  **Reconstruction:** To render, the necessary coefficients are decoded and the inverse transform is applied. This can be done globally (full reconstruction) or locally (block-wise or point-wise) to support adaptive resolution rendering.

## Subtopics

### [Delta Encoding](1096-delta-encoding.md)
[Delta Encoding](1096-delta-encoding.md) is a predictive compression method that exploits redundancy in volume data by storing the difference between a predicted value and the actual value. It solves the problem of high storage requirements for volumetric data with high temporal or spatial coherence by transforming the data into residuals that have a smaller dynamic range and a higher probability of being zero. Unlike sibling methods that may use complex linear combinations or transform-domain residuals, standard delta encoding typically relies on simple prediction models such as temporal coherence (previous time step) or spatial progression, making it computationally trivial to implement but limited in random access capabilities.

### [Residual Updates in Transform Domain](5256-residual-updates-in-transform-domain.md)
[Residual Updates in Transform Domain](5256-residual-updates-in-transform-domain.md) is a temporal compression method for volumetric data that operates in the frequency domain rather than the spatial domain. It solves the problem of encoding time-varying volumes by predicting current transform coefficients from the previously reconstructed frame's coefficients, rather than predicting voxel values directly. It sets itself apart from spatial predictors like [Delta Encoding](1096-delta-encoding.md) by concentrating residual energy near regions of surface change in the transform domain, which improves compressibility for subsequent entropy coding.

### [Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression](6518-residual-coding-for-volumetric-compression.md)
[Residual Coding for Volumetric Compression](6518-residual-coding-for-volumetric-compression.md) is a technique that improves the compression performance (reconstructed image quality) of volumetric codecs by encoding the difference between the original image and the direct reconstructed image from the volume without quantization or coding. It does not contribute to view synthesis quality and may deteriorate it. Unlike standard video codecs where residuals depend on the quantized output, these residuals are derived from the unquantized volume reconstruction, making them consistent across bitrates.

### [Optimal Linear Volumetric Predictor with Truncated Huffman Coding](7759-optimal-linear-volumetric-predictor-with-truncated-huffman-c.md)
[Optimal Linear Volumetric Predictor with Truncated Huffman Coding](7759-optimal-linear-volumetric-predictor-with-truncated-huffman-c.md) is a lossless compression algorithm for 3D volume data that removes spatial redundancy by predicting voxel values from previously encoded neighbors and encoding the resulting residuals using a truncated Huffman code. It differs from generic compressors (e.g., gzip, zip) by explicitly modeling spatial coherence via a causal linear predictor, and from other predictive methods by deriving optimal coefficients via the Yule-Walker equations to handle non-isotropic sampling. It achieves approximately 50% compression ratios on medical volume datasets, outperforming standard dictionary-based compressors.

### [Lorenz Predictor with Arithmetic Coding](8070-lorenz-predictor-with-arithmetic-coding.md)
[Lorenz Predictor with Arithmetic Coding](8070-lorenz-predictor-with-arithmetic-coding.md) is a lossless compression method for $n$-dimensional scalar fields that combines an isotropic predictive coding scheme with entropy coding. It solves the problem of compressing large volumetric or spatiotemporal datasets by exploiting data coherence in all dimensions, unlike anisotropic scanline predictors that only use preceding neighbors in a single direction. It is distinguished from its siblings by being the simplest isotropic predictor capable of exactly reconstructing all polynomials of degree less than $n$, and by using arithmetic coding (specifically context-based) to encode the resulting residuals.

### [RDLS-SS-DWT+Pred](11251-rdls-ss-dwt-pred.md)
[RDLS-SS-DWT+Pred](11251-rdls-ss-dwt-pred.md) is a lossless volumetric compression transform that combines the 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) with Reversible Denoising Lifting Steps (RDLS), Step Skipping (SS), and predictive coding. It solves the problem of high entropy in wavelet subbands by applying spatial prediction to the final subbands, significantly improving compression ratios for medical images compared to standard DWT or SS-DWT alone. It differs from its siblings by integrating denoising lifting steps and step skipping into the wavelet decomposition before applying prediction, whereas siblings like [Delta Encoding](1096-delta-encoding.md) or [Lorenz Predictor with Arithmetic Coding](8070-lorenz-predictor-with-arithmetic-coding.md) typically apply prediction directly to raw data or simpler transforms.

### [Closed-Loop DPCM Decompression](11737-closed-loop-dpcm-decompression.md)
[Closed-Loop DPCM Decompression](11737-closed-loop-dpcm-decompression.md) reconstructs seismic trace data from compressed payloads by using a decoder-side predictor that mirrors the encoder's state, ensuring the reconstructed values match the original input exactly (lossless reconstruction of the quantized stream). It differs from open-loop methods by maintaining a synchronized internal state (`prev`) that is updated with every decoded sample, preventing error accumulation. This method is the primary decoder for modern `.sdc` v3+ files, replacing legacy open-loop delta decoding.

### Directional Linear Predictive Coding
- **Definition:** Compresses hologram interference patterns losslessly by exploiting directional correlation using DPCM and linear prediction [S1, unofficial].
- **Steps:**
    1.  Segment interference patterns into blocks [S1, unofficial].
    2.  Scan the pixels in eight directions for each block [S1, unofficial].
    3.  Calculate entropy in each direction [S1, unofficial].
    4.  Determine the direction with minimum entropy [S1, unofficial].
    5.  Encode the difference by DPCM in the determined direction [S1, unofficial].
    6.  Calculate prediction coefficients for the determined direction [S1, unofficial].
    7.  Use DPCM only to determine the prediction direction for the original pixel [S1, unofficial].
    8.  Calculate the difference between the predicted and the original pixel [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** The first process (directional DPCM) achieved a compression rate of 26.7% [S1, unofficial].

### JPEG-LS
- **Definition:** An older JPEG standard variant tested against 3-D/2-D MLZC, featuring seven prediction modes [S2].
- **Comparison:** Provides the best compression for ANGIO data but lacks quality scalability and simultaneous lossy/lossless support in the same codestream [S2].

### Inter-frame Delta Encoding with Haar Wavelets
- **Definition:** Classifying each time step as intra-coded or predictive frame. All frames are encoded with Haar wavelet, but predictive frames are also expressed as delta with respect to previous frames and then encoded [S3].

### Functional Representation (Spherical Ellipsoids)
- **Definition:** An approach similar to inter-frame delta encoding but encoding data with a functional representation based on spherical ellipsoids [S3].

## Background
Predictive coding has been a cornerstone of data compression since the advent of Differential Pulse-Code Modulation (DPCM) in telecommunications. In the context of volume rendering and medical imaging, it was adopted to handle the high redundancy in spatially coherent datasets like MRI and CT scans. Early implementations focused on spatial prediction for static volumes, while later developments extended the concept to temporal domains for time-varying volumes (e.g., cardiac MRI) and transform domains for efficient streaming [S3]. The method is widely used in medical imaging standards (such as JPEG-LS extensions for 3D data) and specialized formats for seismic data and holographic displays [S1, unofficial][S2].
