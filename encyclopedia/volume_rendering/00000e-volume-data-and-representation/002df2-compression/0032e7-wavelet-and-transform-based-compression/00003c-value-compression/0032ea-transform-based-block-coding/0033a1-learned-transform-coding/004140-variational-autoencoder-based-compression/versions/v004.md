# Variational Autoencoder-based Compression

Also called: Autoencoders and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Probabilistic Autoencoder Compression, VAE-based Compression

## Overview
Variational Autoencoder-based Compression uses an encoder-decoder architecture to map high-dimensional volume data into a lower-dimensional latent space, replacing the original grid with a compressed latent code [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from standard autoencoders by imposing a probabilistic structure on the latent space, which enables sampling and interpolation capabilities [S1, unofficial]. This method addresses the problem of storing and transmitting large scientific volumes by balancing reconstruction fidelity with compact representation, serving dual roles in lossy compression and feature learning [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A 3D volume grid $V$ of dimensions $D_x \times D_y \times D_z$.
- Output: A latent code $z$ (and potentially auxiliary parameters) that allows reconstruction of $\hat{V}$ via a decoder.

**Data Structures:**
- **Encoder $E_\theta$**: A neural network (typically 3D convolutions) mapping $V \rightarrow \mu, \sigma$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation vectors of the latent distribution.
- **Latent Space**: A continuous vector space where each point $z$ represents a compressed version of the input.
- **Decoder $D_\phi$**: A neural network mapping $z \rightarrow \hat{V}$.
- **VAE Objective**: The model is trained to maximize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which consists of a reconstruction loss and a KL-divergence regularization term [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Encoding**: The input volume $V$ is passed through the encoder $E_\theta$ to produce the latent parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma$.
2. **Reparameterization**: To enable backpropagation through the stochastic sampling process, a latent vector $z$ is sampled using the reparameterization trick:
   $$ z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) $$
   where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication and $I$ is the identity matrix [S1, unofficial].
3. **Decoding**: The latent vector $z$ is passed through the decoder $D_\phi$ to reconstruct the volume $\hat{V}$.
4. **Training**: The network parameters $\theta$ and $\phi$ are optimized to minimize the loss function:
   $$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta(z|V)}[\log p_\phi(V|z)] - \beta \cdot D_{KL}(q_\theta(z|V) || p(z)) $$
   where $q_\theta(z|V)$ is the approximate posterior, $p_\phi(V|z)$ is the likelihood, $p(z)$ is the prior (typically standard normal), and $\beta$ is a weighting factor [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- **Latent Dimensionality**: Not given in the sources.
- **Batch Size**: Not given in the sources.
- **Learning Rate**: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision**: Standard autoencoders may suffer from decoding bottlenecks if the latent space is too constrained or the decoder is complex [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundaries**: The probabilistic nature requires careful handling of the prior distribution to ensure the latent space remains well-formed for interpolation [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Latent Space Diffusion
A variant that moves the diffusion process into the latent space to address decoding bottlenecks inherent in data-space diffusion methods [S1, unofficial]. Instead of storing latent representations for every frame, it compresses only selected keyframes using a VAE with a hyperprior, then employs a conditional latent diffusion model with factorized space-time attention to interpolate latent representations for non-keyframes [S1, unofficial]. This approach fine-tunes the diffusion process from 1,000 to 32 denoising steps to preserve compression quality [S1, unofficial]. It achieved up to 10× higher compression ratios than SZ3 on S3D combustion data and 4× on E3SM climate data [S1, unofficial]. Decoding throughput reached 61 MB/s on an A100 GPU, which is 200× faster than GCDTC’s data-space diffusion [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- **Compression**: Depends on the encoder network inference time.
- **Decompression**: Depends on the decoder network inference time. Standard autoencoders decode significantly faster than data-space diffusion methods (e.g., 2 seconds vs. 978 seconds for GCDTC) [S1, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
- **Latent Space Diffusion**: Achieved 61 MB/s decoding throughput on an A100 GPU [S1, unofficial].
- **Compression Ratios**: Up to 10× higher than SZ3 on S3D combustion data and 4× on E3SM climate data [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths**: Enables sampling and interpolation due to probabilistic latent structure [S1, unofficial]. Significantly faster decoding than data-space diffusion methods [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: Standard autoencoders may have decoding bottlenecks if not optimized [S1, unofficial]. Requires training data and computational resources for model training.

**When to Use:**
- Use when interactive decoding speed is critical and the volume data has semantic structure that can be captured in a latent space [S1, unofficial].
- Use when interpolation or sampling of the volume data is required [S1, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
- Use when exact lossless reconstruction is required (VAEs are inherently lossy) [S1, unofficial].
- Use when computational resources for training are unavailable [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Autoencoders and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have been explored for scientific data compression prior to the maturation of more recent frameworks [S1, unofficial]. Early works included feature-aware in-situ autoencoders achieving 32× reduction on particle data, GAN in-situ compressors reaching 64:1 on CFD pressure fields, and hierarchical vector-quantized autoencoders achieving 231× compression at 50 dB on climate data [S1, unofficial]. VAEs specifically impose a probabilistic latent structure, enabling sampling and interpolation, which distinguishes them from standard autoencoders [S1, unofficial].
