# Feature-Preserving Volume Data Reduction and Focus+Context Visualization

Also called: Feature-Directed Voxel Repositioning, Grid Space Deformation, Voxel Reposition

## Overview
This method reduces volumetric data size while preserving features of interest by deforming the 3D grid space to magnify important regions and compress unimportant ones, enabling focus+context visualization [S1]. Unlike uniform downsampling which discards information oblivious to data content, or multiresolution methods that require extra data packing or blending to handle cross-resolution boundaries, this method uses continuous deformation to intelligently allocate more samples to important features and sparsely sample unimportant regions, guaranteeing similar resolutions along neighboring grid boundaries and avoiding seams [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Volumetric data set, user-specified color and opacity transfer functions, and a scaling factor $s$ (typically $s > 1.0$) [S1].
*   **Output:** A reduced data set (downsampled voxels) and a deformed grid used for shape recovery at runtime [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The volume is partitioned into a uniform grid $G = \{V, E, C\}$ [S1]:
*   $V = \{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n\}$: Set of vertex positions.
*   $E$: Set of edges connecting vertices.
*   $C$: Set of space cubes (voxels).

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Preprocessing (Optional):** If the input data contains noise, apply a denoising method such as bilateral filtering before computing cube importance to prevent high color contrasts from artificially inflating importance values [S1].

2.  **Importance Assignment:**
    *   Analyze the content of each local region (cube).
    *   Determine the importance of each data cube based on the color gradients of its interior voxels, which depends on the user-defined transfer function [S1].
    *   Assign an importance value to each space cube based on the averaged interior voxel importance values, normalized to $[0, 1]$ [S1]. Let $w_k$ be the importance weight of cube $k$.

3.  **Grid Deformation (Global Optimization):**
    The goal is to find deformed vertex positions $V'$ that minimize distortion while satisfying constraints. The deformation is defined by scaling factors $s'_c$ and rotation matrices $R'_c$ for each cube.

    *   **Edge-based Feature Energy:** Preserve the shape of edges within each cube. For a cube $c$ with edges $E_c$, let $e_{ij} = v_i - v_j$ be the original edge and $e'_{ij} = v'_i - v'_j$ be the deformed edge. The energy is:
        $$ D_f(c) = \sum_{\{i,j\} \in E_c} \| e_{ij} - s'_c R'_c e_{ij} \|^2 \quad (3) $$
        where $s'_c$ is the scaling factor and $R'_c$ is the rotation matrix for cube $c$ [S1].

    *   **Laplacian Smoothing Energy:** Minimize distortion in areas surrounding features of interest by preserving the deformed Laplacian coordinate of each grid vertex. Let $E(i)$ be the neighboring edges and $C(i)$ be the neighboring cubes sharing vertex $v_i$. The Laplacian coordinate is:
        $$ L(v_i) = \frac{1}{|E(i)|} \sum_{\{i,j\} \in E(i)} (v_i - v_j) $$
        The average scaling factor and rotation matrix for vertex $v_i$ are:
        $$ s'_i = \frac{1}{|C(i)|} \sum_{c \in C(i)} s'_c, \quad R'_i = \frac{1}{|C(i)|} \sum_{c \in C(i)} R'_c $$
        The smoothing energy is:
        $$ D_\ell = \sum_{v_i \in V} \| L(v_i) - s'_i R'_i L(v_i) \|^2 \quad (4) $$

    *   **Boundary Constraints:** To retain the original volume boundary, fix coordinates for boundary vertices $\partial V_x, \partial V_y, \partial V_z$:
        $$
        \begin{cases}
        v'_{i,x} = v_{i,x} & \text{if } v_i \in \partial V_x \\
        v'_{i,y} = v_{i,y} & \text{if } v_i \in \partial V_y \\
        v'_{i,z} = v_{i,z} & \text{if } v_i \in \partial V_z
        \end{cases} \quad (5)
        $$

    *   **Edge Flipping Constraint:** Prevent region intersections. Each edge $\{i, j\}$ must satisfy $e'_{ij} \cdot e_{ij} > 0$. If violated, add penalty energy:
        $$ D_e(i, j) = \gamma \| e'_{ij} - \delta e_{ij} \|^2 $$
        Parameters: $\delta = 0.1$ prevents shrinking to zero/negative scale, $\gamma = 10$ is penalty weight [S1].
        *Pitfall:* Flipped edges may become legal if the user changes the transfer function or scaling factor. Constraining them to zero length causes abnormal deformation. Solution: Remove the flipping constraint when $|e'_{ij}| > \delta |e_{ij}|$ (stretching case), as only squeezing flips edges [S1].

    *   **Optimization:**
        1.  Transform the objective function ($D_f + D_\ell + \text{constraints}$) into a linear system $A V_0 = b(V_0)$, where $A$ contains coefficients of unknown vertex positions $V_0$ [S1].
        2.  Solve in the least squares sense as equations > unknowns [S1].
        3.  Solve constrained non-linear objective function iteratively using the Gaussian-Newton method [S1].
        4.  Solve $x, y, z$ coordinates separately due to different boundary constraints [S1].
        5.  Iteratively update $V_0$, $s'_i$, and $R'_c$.
            *   Initialize $s'_i = 1.0$ and $R'_c = I$ (identity) [S1].
            *   Update $R'_c$ and $s'_c$ by placing original/deformed cubes at the origin, computing the transformation matrix, and decomposing via polar decomposition into rotation $R$ and shear $S$. Set $R_c = R$, $s_c = \text{average of diagonal elements in } S$ [S1].
            *   After updating $V_0$, detect edges conflicting with $e'_{ij} \cdot e_{ij} > 0$. Enforce flipped edges to original direction using the penalty energy. Remove constraint if edge is stretched ($|e'_{ij}| > \delta |e_{ij}|$) [S1].

4.  **Distortion Measurement and Magnification Control:**
    Measure cube distortion $\Psi$ incrementally as the volume is magnified. The distortion is the weighted mean squared distance between corresponding vertices of the original cube $\mathbf{c}_k$ and the deformed cube $\mathbf{c}'_k$, transformed to match the original's size and orientation:
    $$ \Psi = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{\mathbf{c}_k \in \mathbb{C}} w_k \| \mathbf{c}_k - (s'_k \mathbf{R}'_k)^{-1} \mathbf{c}'_k \|^2 \quad (9) $$
    where $n$ is the number of cubes, $w_k$ is the importance weight, $\mathbf{c}_k$ is the original cube, $\mathbf{c}'_k$ is the deformed cube, $s'_k$ is its scaling factor, and $\mathbf{R}'_k$ is its rotation matrix [S1].
    Stop the magnification process when $\Psi$ increases rapidly, indicating that less important regions are already fully squeezed and further scaling yields diminishing returns [S1].

5.  **Data Reduction (Downsampling):**
    *   Downsample the deformed volume data set.
    *   Extract voxels via trilinear interpolation from the deformed grid [S1].
    *   The reduced data set stores the resampled values. The deformed grid is stored separately [S1].

6.  **Rendering (Focus+Context):**
    *   Use the deformed grid to locate queried voxels in the reduced data set during rendering (texture lookup) [S1].
    *   This allows the full-size volume to be directly rendered via texture lookup on the GPU without data reconstruction [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def feature_preserving_reduction(volume_data, transfer_function, scaling_factor_s):
    """
    volume_data: 3D array of voxel values
    transfer_function: function mapping voxel value to (color, opacity)
    scaling_factor_s: float > 1.0
    """
    # 1. Preprocessing
    if has_noise(volume_data):
        volume_data = bilateral_filter(volume_data)

    # 2. Partition Grid
    G = partition_uniform_grid(volume_data) # V, E, C
    
    # 3. Compute Importance
    for cube in G.C:
        cube.importance = compute_importance(cube, volume_data, transfer_function)
        
    # 4. Deform Grid
    # Initialize variables
    V_deformed = copy(G.V)
    S_factors = {c: 1.0 for c in G.C}
    R_matrices = {c: identity_matrix() for c in G.C}
    
    # Iterative Optimization (Gaussian-Newton)
    for iteration in range(max_iterations):
        # Compute average S and R for vertices
        for v in G.V:
            neighbors_cubes = get_neighbor_cubes(v, G.C)
            S_factors[v] = mean([S_factors[c] for c in neighbors_cubes])
            R_matrices[v] = mean([R_matrices[c] for c in neighbors_cubes])
            
        # Construct Linear System A * V_deformed = b
        A = build_matrix_A(G, S_factors, R_matrices)
        b = build_vector_b(G, V_deformed, S_factors, R_matrices)
        
        # Solve for V_deformed (x, y, z separately)
        V_deformed = solve_least_squares(A, b)
        
        # Update Cube Transformations
        for cube in G.C:
            # Place original and deformed cubes at origin
            orig_verts = get_cube_vertices(cube, G.V)
            def_verts = get_cube_vertices(cube, V_deformed)
            T = compute_transformation_matrix(orig_verts, def_verts)
            R, S = polar_decomposition(T)
            R_matrices[cube] = R
            S_factors[cube] = mean(diagonal(S))
            
        # Handle Edge Flipping Constraints
        for edge in G.E:
            e_orig = edge.vector(G.V)
            e_def = edge.vector(V_deformed)
            if dot(e_def, e_orig) < 0: # Flipped
                if length(e_def) > 0.1 * length(e_orig): # Stretching
                    remove_constraint(edge)
                else: # Squeezing
                    enforce_original_direction(edge, V_deformed)
                    
        # Check Convergence or Distortion
        distortion = compute_distortion(G, V_deformed, S_factors, R_matrices)
        if distortion_increases_rapidly(distortion):
            break

    # 5. Downsample
    reduced_data = trilinear_resample(volume_data, G, V_deformed)
    
    return reduced_data, V_deformed
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** The method involves solving a large linear system iteratively. The implementation uses the GPU for parallel execution of cube importance determination, objective function minimization, and volumetric raycasting [S1]. Currently, the CPU idles during the deformation process [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** Demonstrated reduction rate of 10:1 while preserving features better than direct downsampling [S1]. Validated on volume data sets from medical applications and scientific simulations [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Continuous space deformation guarantees similar resolutions along neighboring grid boundaries, obviating the need for extra data packing or blending to avoid gaps or handle unmatched data resolutions [S1].
    *   Outputs a simple data format: only a reduced data set and a deformed grid [S1].
    *   Automatically recognizes and visualizes all features of interest simultaneously, unlike lens-based methods that restrict the user to a single focus [S1].
    *   Preserves gaps between features without additional smoothing steps, unlike direct downsampling (jaggy artifacts) or wavelet transforms (overly smooth) [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   If features are distributed everywhere, the deformation fails to magnify them, causing the method to degenerate into pure downsampling [S1].
    *   To preserve space continuity, homogeneous cubes are not shrunk, preventing full utilization of available space for feature preservation [S1].
    *   Local deformation does not guarantee preservation of global structures like straight lines or symmetry [S1].
    *   Multiresolution methods may best preserve features under space constraints but require boundary smoothing that incurs additional space and processing overhead; this method obviates boundary discontinuity handling at the cost of not squeezing all unimportant regions to zero [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Proposed by Yu-Shuen Wang, Chaoli Wang, Tong-Yee Lee, and Kwan-Liu Ma [S1].
*   **History:** The method addresses the challenge of interactive visualization of large volumetric data sets (from $128^3$ to $1024^3$ voxels or larger) [S1]. It is inspired by resizing techniques for polygonal models [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented on GPU for parallel execution. Validated on medical and scientific simulation data [S1].
