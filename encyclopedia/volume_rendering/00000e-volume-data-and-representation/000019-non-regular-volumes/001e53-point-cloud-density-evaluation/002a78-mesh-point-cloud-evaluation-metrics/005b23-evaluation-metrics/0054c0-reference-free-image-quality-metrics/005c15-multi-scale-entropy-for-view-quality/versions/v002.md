# Multi-Scale Entropy for View Quality

Also called: Multi resolution entropy, Wavelet entropy

## Overview
Multi-Scale Entropy (MSE) is a reference-free metric that quantifies the visual information content of a rendered volume image by computing the Shannon entropy of wavelet coefficients across multiple resolution levels [S1]. Unlike standard histogram-based entropy, which ignores spatial correlations between pixels, MSE captures structural information and local detail, making it effective for evaluating the quality of volume rendering views and selecting optimal viewpoints [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A rendered RGB image $X$ with dimensions $W \times H$, encoded as 8 bits per color channel.
**Outputs:** A scalar value $H_W(X)$ representing the multi-scale entropy of the image.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the wavelet decomposition of the image. For each color channel (Red, Green, Blue) and each decomposition level $l$, the algorithm maintains a histogram of wavelet coefficient values.
*   **Wavelet Coefficients ($w_{l,k}$):** The discrete wavelet transform output at level $l$ and index $k$.
*   **Histogram ($p$):** A frequency map where $p(w_{l,k})$ is the relative frequency of coefficient value $k$ at level $l$ for a specific channel.

**Parameters:**
*   **Wavelet Transform:** Haar wavelet transform [S1].
*   **Decomposition Levels ($L$):** Typically 4 levels are sufficient [S1].
*   **Bit Depth:** 8 bits per channel, implying coefficient values are integers in the range $[0, 255]$ (or mapped appropriately for wavelet coefficients, though the source defines $p$ based on relative counts of coefficient values) [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Decomposition:** Apply the Haar wavelet transform to the input RGB image $X$ for $L$ levels. This yields a set of wavelet coefficients $w_{l,k}$ for each color channel, where $l \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ and $k \in \{0, \dots, N_l-1\}$, with $N_l$ being the number of coefficients at level $l$.
2.  **Histogramming:** For each level $l$ and each color channel (R, G, B), compute the histogram of the wavelet coefficients. Calculate the probability $p(w_{l,k})$ for each unique coefficient value $k$ as the relative frequency of that value in the channel at that level.
3.  **Channel Entropy Calculation:** For each level $l$ and channel, calculate the partial entropy contribution using the Shannon entropy formula:
    $$ h_{RGB}(w_{l,k}) = - \sum_{k=0}^{N_l-1} p(w_{l,k}) \log_2 p(w_{l,k}) $$
    where $0 \log_2 0 = 0$ [S1].
4.  **Aggregation:** Sum the entropy contributions across all levels and all three color channels to obtain the total Multi-Scale Entropy:
    $$ H_W(X) = - \sum_{l=1}^{L} \sum_{k=0}^{N_l-1} h_{RGB}(w_{l,k}) $$
    Note: The source formula [S1] writes $\sum_{k=0}^{N_l} h_{RGB}(w_{l,k})$. Given standard histogram definitions, the sum is over the unique values or indices present. The term $h_{RGB}(w_{l,k})$ in the source text is defined as $-p(w_{l,k}) \log p(w_{l,k})$. The outer sum in the source formula (Eq. 2) sums $h_{RGB}$ over $k$. Thus, the total entropy is the sum of the entropies of the histograms at each level and channel.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Zero Probability:** The term $0 \log_2 0$ is defined as 0 to ensure continuity [S1].
*   **Image Size:** Haar wavelet transforms typically require image dimensions to be powers of 2 or require padding. If dimensions are not compatible, padding must be applied [G].
*   **Coefficient Range:** Unlike pixel values (0-255), wavelet coefficients can be negative and have a wider dynamic range. The histogram binning must cover the full range of coefficients to accurately estimate probabilities [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The complexity is dominated by the wavelet transform and histogramming. For an image of size $N$ pixels and $L$ levels, the wavelet transform is $O(N)$. Histogramming is $O(N)$. With $L$ levels and 3 channels, the total time is roughly $O(3 \cdot L \cdot N)$. The source notes that analyzing a sufficient set of views for brute-force selection takes roughly half a minute, implying the metric is computationally expensive for large sets of views [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the image and intermediate wavelet coefficients. $O(N)$ space.
*   **Strengths:** Captures structural information and pixel correlation, which standard entropy misses. It correlates well with human perception of detail and illumination in volume rendering [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive for exhaustive view search. Sensitive to the choice of wavelet and decomposition levels, though Haar and 4 levels are standard [S1].
*   **When to use:** When evaluating the informational content of volume rendering views for automatic viewpoint selection or quality assessment where structural detail is important.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical for every viewpoint evaluation without adaptive search strategies.

## Background
The concept of Multi-Scale Entropy was introduced by Starck et al. to overcome the insensitivity of standard histogram-based entropy to pixel correlation [S1]. It was applied to volume rendering by [S1] (authors of "Representative Views and Paths for Volume Models") to measure the amount of visual information in rendered volume images. The method was validated through user studies showing it approximates the amount of information revealed by illumination [S1].
