# Evaluation Metrics

Also called: LPIPS, PSNR and SSIM Calculation, Rendering Quality Assessment

## Overview
Evaluation Metrics computes quantitative assessments of rendering and reconstruction quality by comparing generated outputs against ground truth data [S2, unofficial][S3][S5, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of objectively measuring fidelity in 3D graphics, covering data-level, image-level, and geometric-level errors [S3]. Unlike its sibling [Evaluation Pipeline](9358-evaluation-pipeline.md), which orchestrates the workflow, this method defines the specific mathematical functions for quality calculation [S2, S5, unofficial]. It differs from [Cloud-to-Mesh Accuracy Metrics](10560-cloud-to-mesh-accuracy-metrics.md) by focusing on image and volume data metrics (PSNR, LPIPS) rather than purely geometric point-cloud distances, though it shares the Chamfer Distance metric for isosurface evaluation [S1, S3].

## How it works
The method calculates three primary metrics: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), and Chamfer Distance (CD) [S3]. PSNR and SSIM assess image-level fidelity, while LPIPS measures perceptual similarity using deep features, and CD measures geometric alignment of isosurfaces [S3][S5, unofficial].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Reconstructed/Rendered image or volume ($I_{rec}$), Ground Truth image or volume ($I_{gt}$). For geometric metrics, two point clouds or meshes representing the reconstructed and ground truth surfaces.
*   **Outputs:** Scalar scores for PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and/or CD.

**Data Structures:**
*   Images: Tensor of shape $(H, W, C)$ or $(B, H, W, C)$, normalized to $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
*   Volumes: 3D grid or implicit representation.
*   Points/Meshes: Set of 3D coordinates or triangle lists.

**Formulas and Steps:**

1.  **PSNR Calculation:**
    Compute the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between $I_{rec}$ and $I_{gt}$:
    $$ MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (I_{rec}(i) - I_{gt}(i))^2 $$
    where $N$ is the total number of pixels/voxels.
    Calculate PSNR:
    $$ PSNR = 10 \cdot \log_{10} \left( \frac{MAX_I^2}{MSE} \right) $$
    where $MAX_I$ is the maximum possible pixel value (typically 1.0 for normalized data) [S3][S5, unofficial]. Higher PSNR indicates superior quality [S3].

2.  **LPIPS Calculation:**
    Extract deep features from both images using a pre-trained convolutional neural network (e.g., AlexNet) [S3]. Let $\phi(I)$ be the feature map.
    Compute the weighted $L_2$ distance between normalized feature maps:
    $$ LPIPS = \sum_{l} w_l \| \frac{\phi(I_{rec})_l - \phi(I_{gt})_l}{\sigma_l} \|^2_2 $$
    where $l$ indexes layers, $w_l$ are learned weights, and $\sigma_l$ are normalization factors [S3]. Lower LPIPS indicates superior quality [S3].

3.  **Chamfer Distance (CD) Calculation:**
    For isosurfaces extracted from decompressed and ground truth volumes, or for mesh/point cloud comparison:
    Sample $N$ points from the reconstructed surface ($P_{rec}$) and $M$ points from the ground truth surface ($P_{gt}$) [S1, S3].
    Compute the minimum distance from each point in one set to the other set using the $L_2$ norm:
    $$ CD = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{p \in P_{rec}} \min_{q \in P_{gt}} \| p - q \|_2^2 + \frac{1}{M} \sum_{q \in P_{gt}} \min_{p \in P_{rec}} \| q - p \|_2^2 $$
    Note: Some implementations use squared CD for stability [S1]. Lower CD indicates superior quality [S3]. For mesh evaluation, points are sampled (e.g., 1M points) and projected to faces [S1]. Reported CD values are often scaled by $10^5$ for readability [S1].

4.  **F1 Score Calculation (for Mesh/Point Cloud):**
    Compute precision and recall based on a distance threshold $\tau$ (typically 0.003) [S1].
    $$ Precision = \frac{|\{p \in P_{rec} : \min_{q \in P_{gt}} \| p - q \|_2 \le \tau\}|}{|P_{rec}|} $$
    $$ Recall = \frac{|\{q \in P_{gt} : \min_{p \in P_{rec}} \| q - p \|_2 \le \tau\}|}{|P_{gt}|} $$
    $$ F1 = 2 \cdot \frac{Precision \cdot Recall}{Precision + Recall} $$
    Higher F1 indicates better shape capture [S1].

5.  **Normal Consistency (NC):**
    Compare surface normals of projected points. Average the dot product of corresponding normals after alignment [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   `channel_axis`: Set to -1 for RGB channels in the last dimension [S2, unofficial].
*   `data_range`: Set to 1.0 for normalized $[0,1]$ image data [S2, unofficial].
*   `F1_threshold`: Set to 0.003 to ensure sensitivity to fine details [S1].
*   `CD_scale`: Factor of $10^5$ for reporting [S1].
*   `network`: AlexNet for LPIPS feature extraction [S3].

## Subtopics

### Real-world Dataset Evaluation
Evaluates methods on real-world scenes from BlendedMVS and Bag of Chips datasets, which provide images and depth maps reconstructed by MVS methods or RGBD cameras [S4]. The BlendedMVS test set consists of the last ten images, and the Bag of Chips test set consists of the last 1/5 of the images [S4]. Performance degrades on real-world data due to varying exposures, rough geometry, and insufficient background for ambient illumination reconstruction [S4].

### Pearson's Correlation Coefficient (PCC)
An image fidelity score used to evaluate the quality of 3D reconstructions and novel 2D views [S5, unofficial]. It measures the linear correlation between the reconstructed and ground truth pixel values [S5, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** PSNR and SSIM are $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of pixels/voxels. LPIPS is dominated by the forward pass of the CNN (AlexNet), which is $O(H \cdot W \cdot C)$ but involves significant constant factors due to multiple layers. CD is $O(N \cdot M)$ for naive point cloud comparison, but can be optimized with spatial indexing (e.g., KD-trees) to near $O(N \log M)$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** PSNR/SSIM require storing the input images. LPIPS requires storing the intermediate feature maps of the CNN, which can be memory-intensive for high-resolution images. CD requires storing the point clouds or meshes.
*   **Strengths:** PSNR and SSIM are standard, easy to compute, and correlate with human perception to some extent. LPIPS correlates better with human perceptual quality than PSNR/SSIM. CD provides a geometric measure independent of rendering artifacts.
*   **Limitations:** PSNR/SSIM do not always correlate well with human perception, especially for textured or high-frequency details. LPIPS requires a pre-trained network and is computationally expensive. CD is sensitive to sampling density and noise. Real-world evaluation is challenging due to dataset-specific artifacts [S4].
*   **When to use:** Use PSNR/SSIM for quick, standard comparisons. Use LPIPS for perceptual quality assessment. Use CD for geometric accuracy of isosurfaces or meshes. Use PCC for linear correlation analysis [S5, unofficial].

## Background
The metrics were introduced in various computer vision and graphics papers. PSNR and SSIM are standard image quality metrics [S5, unofficial]. LPIPS was introduced by Zhang et al. (AlexNet-based) [S3]. Chamfer Distance is a standard geometric metric [S3, S4]. The specific combination and application to volume rendering and 3D reconstruction are detailed in recent works on neural implicit representations and Gaussian splatting [S1][S2, unofficial][S3, S4][S5, unofficial].
