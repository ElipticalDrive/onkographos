# Evaluation Metrics

Also called: Image Quality Metrics, Rendering Quality Metrics

## Overview
Evaluation Metrics quantify the fidelity of rendered images against ground truth references to assess the quality of volume rendering and neural radiance field optimizations. This family aggregates standard computer vision metrics—Mean Squared Error (MSE), Structural Dissimilarity (DSSIM), and Perceptual Loss—to provide a multi-faceted view of rendering accuracy, balancing pixel-level precision with structural and perceptual similarity.

## Shared principles
The evaluation framework relies on three complementary error measures computed between the predicted image $I_{pred}$ and the ground truth image $I_{gt}$.

1.  **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**: Measures pixel-wise intensity difference.
    $$ \text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (I_{pred}(i) - I_{gt}(i))^2 $$
    where $N$ is the total number of pixels. Lower values indicate higher fidelity.

2.  **Structural Dissimilarity (DSSIM)**: Measures structural information loss, derived from the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). The SSIM index for a window $x$ is defined as:
    $$ \text{SSIM}(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)} $$
    where $\mu_x, \mu_y$ are mean intensities, $\sigma_x^2, \sigma_y^2$ are variances, $\sigma_{xy}$ is covariance, and $C_1, C_2$ are stabilization constants. DSSIM is typically computed as $1 - \text{SSIM}$ or averaged over the image, where lower values indicate better structural preservation.

3.  **Perceptual Loss**: Measures high-level feature differences using a pre-trained network. It is defined as a weighted sum of $L_1$ distances between feature maps of specific layers:
    $$ \mathcal{L}_{perc} = \sum_{i} w_i \| \phi_i(I_{pred}) - \phi_i(I_{gt}) \|_1 $$
    where $\phi_i$ represents the feature map output of layer $i$ in a pre-trained network (e.g., VGG), and $w_i$ are weights assigned to each layer [S1]. This metric correlates better with human perception than pixel-wise errors.

## Background
The use of MSE, DSSIM, and Perceptual Loss as a standard evaluation suite for neural rendering was established in the context of Neural Voxel Renderers (NVR). In the seminal work on Neural Voxel Renderers, these three metrics were used to compare the proposed method against alternatives such as Image Translation, Deep Shading, and Single-to-multiview rendering [S1]. The study demonstrated that NVR+ achieved superior performance across all three metrics (MSE: 14.3, DSSIM: 0.012, Perceptual: 8.21) compared to other methods, highlighting the importance of using a combined metric suite to capture different aspects of rendering quality, such as fine detail reproduction and texture accuracy [S1].
