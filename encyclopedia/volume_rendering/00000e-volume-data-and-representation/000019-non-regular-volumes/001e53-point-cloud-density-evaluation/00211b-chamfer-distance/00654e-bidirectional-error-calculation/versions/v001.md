# Bidirectional Error Calculation

Also called: Bidirectional Chamfer Distance, Symmetric Chamfer Distance, Two-Way Chamfer Distance

## Overview
Bidirectional Error Calculation computes a symmetric distance metric between two point clouds by aggregating directed Chamfer distances in both directions. It solves the asymmetry inherent in unidirectional metrics, ensuring that errors in both the source and target sets contribute to the final score. It is distinguished from its siblings by its foundational role as the symmetric aggregation mechanism for Chamfer Distance, whereas [Edge Chamfer Distance](6089-edge-chamfer-distance.md) focuses on specific geometric features, [Pairwise Chamfer Distance via Relative Transform](13046-pairwise-chamfer-distance-via-relative-transform.md) incorporates spatial transformations, and [Chamfer Distance Transform](16562-chamfer-distance-transform.md) and [Vector Distance Transform](16563-vector-distance-transform.md) utilize distance fields for efficiency or vector information.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves computing two directed Chamfer distances and combining them. For two point sets $\mathbf{P}$ and $\mathbf{Q}$, the directed Chamfer distance from $\mathbf{P}$ to $\mathbf{Q}$ is defined as the mean squared Euclidean distance from each point in $\mathbf{P}$ to its nearest neighbor in $\mathbf{Q}$:

$$ d(\mathbf{P} \to \mathbf{Q}) = \frac{1}{|\mathbf{P}|} \sum_{\mathbf{p} \in \mathbf{P}} \min_{\mathbf{q} \in \mathbf{Q}} \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q}\|_2^2 $$

The bidirectional error is typically the sum of the directed distances in both directions:

$$ \text{CD}(\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{Q}) = d(\mathbf{P} \to \mathbf{Q}) + d(\mathbf{Q} \to \mathbf{P}) $$

Some variants, such as those described in [Symmetric Error Calculation](#symmetric-error-calculation), may take the maximum of the two directed errors instead of the sum [S2]. The calculation assumes uniform sampling of the underlying surfaces or structures to ensure statistical validity [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Global Chamfer Distance](9349-global-chamfer-distance.md)
Global Chamfer Distance measures the global geometric accuracy of a reconstructed mesh surface by computing the bidirectional Chamfer Distance between uniformly sampled point sets from the reconstruction and the ground truth. It is distinguished from its siblings by evaluating the entire surface geometry rather than specific features (like edges) or relative transforms, serving as a standard metric for absolute reconstruction error. See [Global Chamfer Distance](9349-global-chamfer-distance.md) for implementation details.

### Surface Alignment Metrics
Metrics for validating point cloud surface alignment against ground truth using Chamfer distance [S1, unofficial]. These metrics include Accuracy (Acc.), Completeness (Com.), and their mean (Over.), all computed with Chamfer distance [S1, unofficial]. Accuracy is typically the mean directed distance from the reconstructed points to the ground truth, while Completeness is the mean directed distance from the ground truth to the reconstructed points [S1, unofficial].

### Symmetric Error Calculation
Computes a bidirectional error metric by treating both original and distorted contents as references and taking the maximum error [S2]. The steps are:
1. Compute error value with original content as reference and distorted content as target [S2].
2. Compute error value with distorted content as reference and original content as target [S2].
3. Return the maximum of the two computed error values [S2].

### DTU Evaluation Metric
Computes mean bidirectional distance between reconstructed and ground truth point clouds, filtered by max distance and observation mask [S3, unofficial]. The steps are:
1. Filter points within bounding box and observation mask [S3, unofficial].
2. Compute nearest neighbor distances in both directions [S3, unofficial].
3. Filter distances by `max_dist` [S3, unofficial].
4. Compute mean for each direction [S3, unofficial].
5. Average the two means [S3, unofficial].

The calculation is expressed as:
```python
mean_d2s = dist_d2s[dist_d2s < max_dist].mean()
mean_s2d = dist_s2d[dist_s2d < max_dist].mean()
over_all = (mean_d2s + mean_s2d) / 2
```

## Background
The concept of bidirectional Chamfer distance originates from the need to evaluate 3D reconstruction quality without bias towards either the ground truth or the prediction. It is widely used in computer vision and graphics for assessing mesh and point cloud reconstruction accuracy. The DTU dataset evaluation protocol popularized the use of Chamfer distance for surface alignment validation [S1, unofficial]. The symmetric variant, taking the maximum of directed errors, is also used in quality assessment methodologies for voxelized point clouds [S2]. The DTU evaluation metric specifically incorporates filtering based on observation masks and maximum distance thresholds to handle sparse and noisy data [S3, unofficial].
