# Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR

Also called: Adaptive Mesh Refinement, Adaptive tetrahedral subdivision, Gradient-based mesh refinement, Prism Splitting, Tetrahedral Subdivision

## Overview
Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR is a training strategy that refines a tetrahedral mesh by subdividing vertices with high color gradients to increase resolution in regions of high-frequency detail, solving the issue of wasted resolution in empty or smooth regions [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its sibling [Recursive Subdivision](1304-recursive-subdivision.md) by relying on gradient-based selection of vertices for refinement rather than a fixed geometric bisection sequence, and from [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) by operating on a volumetric tetrahedral mesh rather than a parametric surface [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Initial tetrahedral mesh (generated via regular grid conversion, fTetWild, TetGen, or Delaunay triangulation of SfM points) [S1, unofficial].
*   Set of training images and camera poses.
*   Target number of tetrahedra or refinement iterations.

**Outputs:**
*   Refined tetrahedral mesh with optimized vertex positions and colors.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Mesh:** Unstructured collection of tetrahedra defined by vertex indices. Each tetrahedron has 4 vertices.
*   **Vertex Data:** Position ($\mathbf{v}$) and Color ($\mathbf{c}$) for each vertex.
*   **Gradient Accumulator:** Absolute color gradient magnitude stored per vertex.

**Parameters:**
*   $\hat{N}$: Number of vertices selected for subdivision. Typically 5% of total vertices [S1, unofficial].
*   Learning rates: Color learning rate ($\eta_c$), Position learning rate ($\eta_p$). Position optimization is unstable; $\eta_p$ must be very low (e.g., $10^{-6}$) if used [S1, unofficial].
*   Regularization strength ($\lambda$) and softplus parameter ($\beta$) for vertex position stability (e.g., $\lambda=10, \beta=100$) [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Generate initial tetrahedral mesh. Initialize vertex colors and positions.
2.  **Epoch Loop:** Repeat until target tetrahedra count is reached:
    1.  **Color Optimization:** Optimize only vertex colors for one epoch (e.g., 800 renderings) [S1, unofficial].
    2.  **Joint Optimization:** Optimize vertex colors and positions jointly for one epoch [S1, unofficial].
    3.  **Gradient Accumulation:** Accumulate absolute color gradients at every vertex without performing optimization steps [S1, unofficial].
    4.  **Vertex Selection:** Identify the $\hat{N}$ vertices with the highest absolute color gradient magnitudes [S1, unofficial].
    5.  **Subdivision:** For each selected vertex, split all incident edges by inserting a new vertex at the midpoint. Split the incident tetrahedra using the newly inserted vertices.
    6.  **Prism Splitting & CSP:** Split the resulting triangular prisms into tetrahedra. Solve the Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) to ensure compatible splits on shared quad faces [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Final Optimization:** Optimize only vertex colors to produce the final result [S1, unofficial].

**Subdivision Geometry:**
When a vertex $\mathbf{a}$ is split, the incident tetrahedra are modified. For a tetrahedron incident to $\mathbf{a}$ with other vertices $\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d}$:
1.  New vertices $\mathbf{b}', \mathbf{c}', \mathbf{d}'$ are created at the midpoints of edges $\mathbf{ab}, \mathbf{ac}, \mathbf{ad}$ respectively.
2.  A new tetrahedron $\{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}', \mathbf{c}', \mathbf{d}'\}$ is formed incident to $\mathbf{a}$.
3.  The remaining volume forms a triangular prism with vertices $\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}, \mathbf{d}$ (base) and $\mathbf{b}', \mathbf{c}', \mathbf{d}'$ (top) [S1, unofficial].

**Prism Splitting and CSP:**
Each triangular prism is split into exactly three tetrahedra. The split direction on the quad faces of the prism is determined by a CSP to ensure compatibility with adjacent prisms [S1, unofficial].
*   **Split Types:** Split edges are categorized as 'Rising' (R) or 'Falling' (F) based on the direction of the tessellation edge relative to the extruded prism face in a counter-clockwise manner [S1, unofficial].
*   **Compatibility:** Two adjacent prisms sharing a quad face are compatible if one uses an R split and the other uses an F split [S1, unofficial].
*   **CSP Constraints:**
    1.  The splits for one prism may not be FFF or RRR [S1, unofficial].
    2.  Two adjacent prisms must split a shared quad face using exactly one F and one R split [S1, unofficial].
*   **Solver:** The CSP is solved using a specialized algorithm (e.g., `FlipSolver.cpp`) [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

def adaptive_subdivision(mesh, images, cameras, target_tets, gradient_percent=0.05):
    """
    Performs adaptive subdivision on a tetrahedral mesh based on color gradients.
    
    Args:
        mesh: Initial tetrahedral mesh object with vertices and cells.
        images: List of training images.
        cameras: List of camera poses corresponding to images.
        target_tets: Target number of tetrahedra.
        gradient_percent: Percentage of vertices to subdivide (default 5%).
        
    Returns:
        Refined mesh with optimized colors and positions.
    """
    # Initialize vertex colors and positions
    mesh.vertices.colors = initialize_colors(mesh.vertices)
    mesh.vertices.positions = initialize_positions(mesh.vertices)
    
    # Hyperparameters
    lr_color = 1e-3  # Example learning rate
    lr_pos = 1e-6    # Must be very low for stability
    lambda_reg = 10
    beta_softplus = 100
    
    while mesh.num_tetrahedra() < target_tets:
        # Epoch 1: Optimize only vertex colors
        optimize_colors(mesh, images, cameras, lr_color)
        
        # Epoch 2: Jointly optimize vertex colors and positions
        optimize_joint(mesh, images, cameras, lr_color, lr_pos, lambda_reg, beta_softplus)
        
        # Epoch 3: Accumulate absolute color gradients without optimization
        accumulate_gradients(mesh, images, cameras)
        
        # Select vertices with highest gradients
        num_to_subdivide = int(gradient_percent * mesh.num_vertices())
        top_vertices = get_top_gradient_vertices(mesh, num_to_subdivide)
        
        # Subdivide incident tetrahedra
        for v in top_vertices:
            split_vertex(mesh, v)
            
        # Resolve prism splits using CSP
        solve_prism_csp(mesh)
        
    # Final optimization: Only vertex colors
    optimize_colors(mesh, images, cameras, lr_color)
    
    return mesh

def split_vertex(mesh, vertex_index):
    """
    Splits a vertex by inserting midpoints on incident edges and splitting incident tetrahedra.
    """
    incident_tets = mesh.get_incident_tetrahedra(vertex_index)
    new_vertices = {}
    
    for tet in incident_tets:
        vertices = tet.get_vertex_indices()
        # Identify the splitting vertex 'a' and others 'b', 'c', 'd'
        a_idx = vertex_index
        b_idx, c_idx, d_idx = [v for v in vertices if v != a_idx]
        
        # Create midpoints if not already created
        edge_ab = tuple(sorted((a_idx, b_idx)))
        edge_ac = tuple(sorted((a_idx, c_idx)))
        edge_ad = tuple(sorted((a_idx, d_idx)))
        
        if edge_ab not in new_vertices:
            new_vertices[edge_ab] = mesh.add_vertex(midpoint(mesh.vertices[a_idx], mesh.vertices[b_idx]))
        if edge_ac not in new_vertices:
            new_vertices[edge_ac] = mesh.add_vertex(midpoint(mesh.vertices[a_idx], mesh.vertices[c_idx]))
        if edge_ad not in new_vertices:
            new_vertices[edge_ad] = mesh.add_vertex(midpoint(mesh.vertices[a_idx], mesh.vertices[d_idx]))
            
        b_prime = new_vertices[edge_ab]
        c_prime = new_vertices[edge_ac]
        d_prime = new_vertices[edge_ad]
        
        # Remove old tetrahedron
        mesh.remove_tetrahedron(tet)
        
        # Add new tetrahedron incident to 'a'
        mesh.add_tetrahedron([a_idx, b_prime, c_prime, d_prime])
        
        # Add prism vertices for later splitting
        # Prism base: b, c, d; Prism top: b', c', d'
        prism = Prism(b_idx, c_idx, d_idx, b_prime, c_prime, d_prime)
        mesh.add_prism(prism)

## Subtopics

### [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md)
Differentiable Mesh Refinement is a training strategy that adjusts the vertex positions and colors of a template mesh to match input photographs, using multi-layer splatting for stability. It solves the problem of fitting a parametric surface to multi-view imagery where single-layer splatting is unstable due to the flexibility of the optimization and the sensitivity of image derivatives. It differs from pose estimation because each vertex depends on fewer pixels, making derivative errors more significant, and from grid-based methods by operating directly on mesh topology rather than occupancy fields.

### [Recursive Subdivision](1304-recursive-subdivision.md)
Recursive Subdivision is a method for generating adaptive, conforming tetrahedral meshes from a regular cubic volume domain. It solves the problem of maintaining mesh conformity (crack-free boundaries) during hierarchical refinement by enforcing a deterministic, state-driven bisection sequence. Unlike octree-based decompositions that require complex stitching to handle resolution discontinuities, this method guarantees that neighboring cells share faces exactly. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) by relying on a fixed geometric subdivision rule rather than gradient-based training, and from [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md) by using a specific 3-step cyclic bisection pattern rather than a uniform 1-to-8 split.

### [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md)
3-root-2 subdivision is a hierarchical volume representation scheme that refines a mesh by doubling the total number of vertices per step, theoretically increasing vertex density by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in each spatial dimension. It solves the problem of coarse granularity in standard octree refinement, which multiplies vertex counts by 8 per step, by providing finer resolution control for multiresolution rendering. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific geometric insertion sequence (centroid, then face centers, then edge midpoints) and its reliance on B-spline wavelets for data value updates.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The subdivision process involves gradient accumulation, vertex selection, and CSP solving. CSP solving and data structure updates can dominate computation time after many iterations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage increases with the number of tetrahedra. The unstructured nature requires storing neighbor relationships and prism data during subdivision [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Color-only optimization achieves a peak PSNR of 33.5 on the Tooth dataset [S1, unofficial]. Joint optimization of colors and positions is unstable and achieves a lower PSNR of approx. 26.9, even with very low learning rates ($10^{-6}$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Adapts resolution to high-frequency details, reducing wasted computation in empty regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Vertex position optimization is unstable, especially as tetrahedra get smaller, increasing the chance of inversion [S1, unofficial]. Subdivision may not sufficiently lower local error, leading to recursive subdivision of the same regions and halting optimization progress [S1, unofficial]. Incompatible splits on shared quad faces can lead to infinitesimally thin holes if CSP is not solved correctly [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When the scene has varying frequency details and empty regions, and when differentiable surface fitting is not suitable.
*   **When Not to Use:** When vertex position optimization stability is critical, or when the mesh becomes too dense for efficient CSP solving.

## Background
DiffTetVR was introduced by Chris Mile et al. in 2026 [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on DiffDVR by Weiss and Westermann [S1, unofficial]. The prism splitting algorithm is based on Erleben et al. [S1, unofficial]. The initial mesh generation can use fTetWild or TetGen [S1, unofficial]. The CSP solver is implemented in `FlipSolver.cpp` [S1, unofficial].
