# Adaptive Volume Subdivision
Also called: Adaptive Octree Subdivision
## Overview
Adaptive Volume Subdivision is a method for managing the resolution of irradiance volumes by using an octree structure to store samples, subdividing only in regions where irradiance changes rapidly and keeping coarse resolution where it changes slowly [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of uniform grids becoming unwieldy for large, complex scenes by allowing variable granularity [S1, unofficial]. It differs from uniform grid approaches by dynamically adjusting voxel size based on scene complexity and irradiance gradients, and from static hierarchical methods by relying on specific heuristics (like scene depth) to drive the subdivision decisions [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A scene geometry, a defined minimum and maximum subdivision level for the octree, and thresholds for subdivision heuristics.
*   **Output:** An octree of irradiance samples where leaf nodes represent voxels of varying sizes, optimized for storage and retrieval of irradiance data.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Octree Node:** Contains a flag indicating if it is a leaf or internal node, and potentially a list of child pointers. Leaf nodes store irradiance samples (e.g., spherical harmonic coefficients).
*   **Voxel:** The spatial unit represented by a leaf node. Its size is determined by its depth in the octree.

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize Octree:** Create the root node of the octree. Define the global minimum and maximum subdivision levels.
2.  **Flag Nodes for Subdivision:** Iterate through the scene or existing samples to determine which voxels require further subdivision. This involves applying one or more heuristics:
    *   **Geometry Check:** Subdivide any voxels that contain scene geometry [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Harmonic Mean of Scene Depth:** Compute the harmonic mean of scene depth at a sample point to determine if subdivision is needed [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Allocate Subnode Tiles:** For each flagged node, allocate child nodes up to the maximum subdivision level, respecting the heuristics.
4.  **Initialize New Nodes:** Initialize the new child nodes with appropriate data structures.
5.  **Sample the Volume:**
    *   Search the octree for the voxel containing the object's centroid or sample point [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use surrounding samples to determine irradiance. This can be done via trilinear interpolation of spherical harmonic coefficients or a weighted sum of surrounding samples weighted by $1/distance$ [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Cull Redundant Samples:** After fully sampling the volume, iterate through the octree to reject redundant samples. If a voxel has been subdivided and its children do not differ significantly from the parent, the children's samples may be culled, and the node may be merged back or marked as non-leaf if appropriate, though the primary action described is culling the redundant child samples [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Harmonic Mean of Scene Depth:**
    $$ HM = \frac{N}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{1}{d_i}} $$
    Where $N$ is the total number of rays fired, and $d_i$ is the distance the $i$-th ray traveled before intersecting scene geometry [S1, unofficial].
*   **Subdivision Condition:** If neighboring irradiance samples are further away than the computed Harmonic Mean ($HM$), their associated voxels should be subdivided [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Min/Max Subdivision Level:** Defines the range of octree depths allowed. Not given in the sources.
*   **Heuristic Thresholds:** Values used to determine if the difference between parent and children, or the scene depth, warrants subdivision. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Infinite Depth:** The harmonic mean is chosen over the arithmetic mean because large depth values (due to infinite depth if no geometry exists in a given direction) would quickly bias the arithmetic mean to a large value [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Geometry Containment:** Determining if a voxel contains scene geometry can be done by sampling scene depth at voxel corners using a cubemap, or by using occlusion queries with the camera at the voxel center [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Harmonic Mean of Scene Depth
A heuristic to determine when subdivision is needed by computing the harmonic mean of scene depth at a sample point; areas with more geometry have more rapidly changing irradiance [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen over the arithmetic mean to avoid bias from infinite depth values [S1, unofficial]. It is implemented by shooting rays from the sample position, computing the harmonic mean of distances to intersection, and using this as an upper bound for sample usefulness; if neighboring samples are further away than this bound, subdivision is triggered [S1, unofficial]. On the GPU, this is done by rendering the scene into a floating-point cubemap with a shader outputting $1/depth$, then reading the cubemap back to find the mean [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Allows finer resolution in areas with quickly changing irradiance and coarser resolution in slowly changing areas, avoiding the unwieldiness of uniform grids for large, complex scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Determining which areas need subdivision is a challenging problem [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When dealing with large, complex scenes where irradiance varies significantly across different regions [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When the scene is simple and uniform, or when the overhead of octree management outweighs the benefits of adaptive resolution [S1, unofficial].
## Background
The method was presented in the GDC 2005 paper "Irradiance Volumes for Games" by Chris Oat [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address the limitations of uniform grids in game engines, specifically for global illumination techniques like irradiance volumes [S1, unofficial].
