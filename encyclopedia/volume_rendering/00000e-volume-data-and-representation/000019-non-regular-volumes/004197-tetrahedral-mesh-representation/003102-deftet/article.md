# DEFTET
Also called: DEFTET (Deformable Tetrahedral Mesh), Deformable Tetrahedral Mesh

## Overview
DEFTET is a differentiable volumetric 3D shape representation that utilizes a deformable tetrahedral mesh for reconstruction, optimizing for both vertex placement (deformation) and binary occupancy of each tetrahedron [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of aliasing (staircase patterns) inherent in discretized voxel grids and the topological rigidity of surface meshes by allowing vertices to move freely within a bounding volume [S1, unofficial]. Unlike voxel grids, DEFTET bypasses aliasing by deforming vertices to align with surfaces, and unlike Differentiable Marching Cubes (DMC), it is not constrained to regular grid edges, providing greater flexibility in representing complex geometry [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A set of observations $I$ (e.g., a point cloud, single image, or multi-view images) and an initial fixed tetrahedral mesh covering a unit cube bounding box [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A deformed tetrahedral mesh defined by vertex positions $v_i$, tetrahedron occupancy labels $O_k$, and optionally per-vertex attributes (RGB color $C_i$, visibility $D_i$) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The representation is a spatial graph defined over a unit cube $[0,1]^3$ [S1, unofficial].
1.  **Vertices:** $N$ vertices $v_i = [x_i, y_i, z_i]^T$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Faces:** $F$ triangular faces $f_s = [v_{as}, v_{bs}, v_{cs}]$ for $s \in \{1, \dots, F\}$, where each face is shared by two neighboring tetrahedra [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Tetrahedra:** $K$ tetrahedra $T_k = [v_{ak}, v_{bk}, v_{ck}, v_{dk}]$ for $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Occupancy:** Binary label $O_k \in \{0, 1\}$ for each tetrahedron $T_k$, dependent on vertex positions $v$ [S1, unofficial].

**Initialization:**
The initial mesh is generated by fully subdividing the unit cube bounding box using QuarTet [S1, unofficial]. This initialization is fixed and serves as the base topology [S1, unofficial].

**Inference Steps:**
1.  **Prediction:** Feed input $I$ into a neural network $h$ to predict vertex offsets $\Delta v_i = [\Delta x_i, \Delta y_i, \Delta z_i]^T$ and occupancy values $O_k$ (or per-vertex visibility $D_i$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Deformation:** Compute deformed vertex positions:
    $$ v'_i = v_i + \Delta v_i $$
3.  **Occupancy Determination:**
    *   *If occupancy is predicted directly:* Threshold $O_k > \text{threshold}$ (e.g., 0.5) [S1, unofficial].
    *   *If visibility is predicted (2D supervision):* Compute tetrahedron occupancy as the maximum of its vertex visibilities:
        $$ O_k = \max(D_{ak}, D_{bk}, D_{ck}, D_{dk}) $$
4.  **Surface Extraction:** Identify the object surface as the set of triangular faces $f_s$ shared by two neighboring tetrahedra $T_{s1}$ and $T_{s2}$ with different occupancy labels ($O_{s1} \neq O_{s2}$) [S1, unofficial]. Internal faces shared by two occupied tetrahedra are discarded [S1, unofficial].

**Differentiable Rendering (for 2D Supervision):**
To enable training from images, DEFTET uses a differentiable renderer [S1, unofficial]:
1.  For each pixel $I_j$, shoot a ray from the camera origin.
2.  Find all hit faces $\{f_{l1}, \dots, f_{lL}\}$ sorted by increasing depth [S1, unofficial].
3.  For each face $f_{lk} = (v_a, v_b, v_c)$, compute barycentric weights $\{w_a, w_b, w_c\}$ relative to the pixel center [S1, unofficial].
4.  Interpolate attributes (visibility $D$ and color $C$) from vertices to the pixel:
    $$ D_j^k = w_a D_a + w_b D_b + w_c D_c $$
    $$ C_j^k = w_a C_a + w_b C_b + w_c C_c $$
5.  Compute face visibility $m_k$ using a volume-rendering-style accumulation, ensuring a face is visible only if all preceding faces are not:
    $$ m_k = \prod_{i=1}^{k-1} (1 - D_j^i) D_j^k $$
6.  Compute final pixel color $R_j$ and total visibility $M_j$:
    $$ M_j = \sum_{k=1}^L m_k $$
    $$ R_j = \sum_{k=1}^L m_k C_j^k $$

**Training Losses:**
The total loss is a weighted sum of reconstruction and regularization terms [S1, unofficial]:
$$ L_{\text{all}}(\theta) = \lambda_{\text{recon}} L_{\text{recon}} + \lambda_{\text{vol}} L_{\text{vol}} + \lambda_{\text{lap}} L_{\text{lap}} + \lambda_{\text{sm}} L_{\text{sm}} + \lambda_{\text{del}} L_{\text{del}} + \lambda_{\text{amips}} L_{\text{amips}} $$

*   **3D Supervision (Occupancy & Surface):**
    *   *Occupancy Loss:* Binary Cross Entropy between predicted $O_k$ and ground truth $\hat{O}_k$ (computed via winding number of the tetrahedron centroid against the ground truth mesh) [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_{\text{occ}}(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K \hat{O}_k \log(O_k) + (1 - \hat{O}_k) \log(1 - O_k) $$
    *   *Surface Loss:* Chamfer distance between sampled points $\mathcal{S}$ from the target surface and predicted faces $\mathcal{F}$ (where $P_s(\mathbf{v})=1$) [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_{\text{surf}}(\theta) = \sum_{p \in \mathcal{S}} \min_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \text{dist}_f(p, f) + \sum_{q \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}} \min_{p \in \mathcal{S}} \text{dist}_p(p, q) $$
    *   *Total 3D Loss:* $L_{\text{recon3D}}(\theta) = L_{\text{occ}} + \lambda_{\text{surf}} L_{\text{surf}}$ [S1, unofficial].

*   **2D Supervision (Image):**
    *   *Reconstruction Loss:* L1 distance between rendered color $R_j$ and ground truth $R_j^{GT}$, plus optional mask loss on visibility $M_j$ [S1, unofficial]. Note: The source text contains a typo in Equation 12, repeating the color term; context implies the second term applies to the mask/visibility [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ L_{\text{recon2D}} = \sum_j |R_j^{GT} - R_j|_1 + \lambda_{\text{mask}} |M_j^{GT} - M_j|_1 $$

*   **Regularization Terms:**
    *   $L_{\text{lap}}$: Laplacian loss penalizing changes in relative positions of neighboring vertices [S1, unofficial].
    *   $L_{\text{del}}$: Delta loss penalizing large deformation magnitudes per vertex [S1, unofficial].
    *   $L_{\text{vol}}$: EquiVolume loss penalizing volume differences per tetrahedron [S1, unofficial].
    *   $L_{\text{amips}}$: AMIPS loss penalizing tetrahedron distortion [S1, unofficial].
    *   $L_{\text{sm}}$: Smoothness loss penalizing normal differences between neighboring faces (used when 3D ground truth is available) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\lambda_{\text{recon}}, \lambda_{\text{vol}}, \lambda_{\text{lap}}, \lambda_{\text{sm}}, \lambda_{\text{del}}, \lambda_{\text{amips}}$: Hyperparameters weighting the loss terms [S1, unofficial].
*   Threshold for occupancy: Optimized on validation set [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Tetrahedra:** The method requires tetrahedra to remain non-degenerate and non-flipped during deformation to maintain validity [S1, unofficial]. Regularization terms ($L_{\text{vol}}, L_{\text{amips}}$) are employed to prevent flipping [S1, unofficial].
*   **Topology:** While DEFTET can represent arbitrary topology, the initial mesh topology is fixed. Complex topological changes require adaptive subdivision or re-meshing [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Achieves high fidelity with a significantly smaller grid size than alternative volumetric approaches (voxels) by deforming vertices to align with surfaces [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths and Limitations:** Unlike DMC, DEFTET vertices are not constrained to grid edges, allowing for more flexible representation of geometric details and avoiding corner cases associated with regular grid topologies [S1, unofficial]. Requires tetrahedra to remain non-degenerate and non-flipped during deformation [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Suitable for 3D reconstruction from noisy point clouds, single images, and multi-view images. Outputs an explicit mesh representation suitable for downstream tasks like physics simulation or artistic editing [S1, unofficial].

## Background
DEFTET was introduced by Jun Gao, Wenzheng Chen, Tommy Xiang, Alec Jacobson, Morgan McGuire, and Sanja Fidler from NVIDIA, University of Toronto, and Vector Institute [S1, unofficial]. The work builds upon the idea of deforming regular grid geometry to represent structures in images, extending it to 3D geometric reasoning [S1, unofficial]. It addresses limitations of previous neural 3D reconstruction methods, including point clouds, voxel grids, surface meshes, and implicit functions, by providing a representation that is simultaneously high-precision, volumetric, and amenable to learning-based neural architectures [S1, unofficial].
