# TetWeave Late-Stage Optimization

Also called: Late-stage refinement, TetWeave final optimization phase

## Overview
TetWeave Late-Stage Optimization is a differentiable refinement phase that adjusts the Signed Distance Field (SDF) values, point positions, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients of an existing tetrahedral mesh without modifying its topological structure [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from earlier stages by decoupling geometry optimization from grid updates, relying instead on multi-view rendering losses (mask, depth, normal) and SDF regularization to polish the reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. This method builds on [Mesh Refinement via Vertex Optimization](989-mesh-refinement-via-vertex-optimization.md) by operating on the final mesh representation derived from the optimized points and SDF.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Target multi-view data: masks ($M$), depth maps ($D$), and normal maps ($N$).
- Current state: vertex positions ($V$), face connectivity ($F$), tetrahedra ($T$), SDF values ($S$) at vertices, and spherical harmonics coefficients ($C_{SH}$).
- Configuration parameters: loss weights, optimization steps, and SH degree.

**Outputs:**
- Refined vertex positions ($V'$), SDF values ($S'$), and SH coefficients ($C_{SH}'$).
- The tetrahedral topology ($T$) remains unchanged.

**Data Structures:**
- `vertices`: Tensor of shape $(V, 3)$, float32.
- `faces`: Tensor of shape $(F, 3)$, int64, defining the surface mesh.
- `tetrahedra`: Tensor of shape $(T, 4)$, int64, defining the volume mesh.
- `sdf`: Tensor of shape $(V,)$, float32, SDF values at vertices.
- `sh_coefficients`: Tensor of shape $(V, K)$, float32, where $K$ is the number of SH coefficients based on the degree.
- `buffers`: Dictionary containing rendered `mask`, `depth`, and `normals_face` tensors.

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialization and Mesh Creation:**
    Create a surface mesh representation from the current vertices and faces. Extract edges from the tetrahedra for regularization purposes.
    ```python
    kal_mesh = SurfaceMesh(vertices, faces)
    edges = get_edges(tetrahedra) # Returns tensor of shape (E, 2)
    ```

2.  **Rendering:**
    Render the surface mesh to the target camera views to obtain buffers.
    ```python
    buffers = render_mesh(
        mesh=kal_mesh,
        cameras=target_cameras,
        resolution=optimization_config.train_res,
        return_types=['mask', 'depth', 'normals_face'],
        white_bg=False
    )
    ```

3.  **Loss Calculation:**
    Compute individual loss components weighted by configuration constants.

    - **Mask Loss:** Mean absolute difference between rendered and target masks.
      $$ L_{mask} = \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum |M_{rendered} - M_{target}| \right) \cdot w_{mask} $$
      Where $w_{mask}$ is `optimization.late_stage.loss.mask_reg`.

    - **Depth Loss:** Root mean square error between rendered and target depth, masked by the target mask. An epsilon $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ is added for numerical stability.
      $$ L_{depth} = \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,j} \left( (D_{rendered} - D_{target}) \cdot M_{target} \right)^2 + \epsilon} \right) \cdot w_{depth} $$
      Where $w_{depth}$ is `optimization.late_stage.loss.depth_reg`.

    - **Normal Loss:** Root mean square error between rendered face normals and target normals, masked by the target mask.
      $$ L_{normal} = \left( \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,j} \left( (N_{rendered} - N_{target}) \cdot M_{target} \right)^2 + \epsilon} \right) \cdot w_{normal} $$
      Where $w_{normal}$ is `optimization.late_stage.loss.normal_reg`.

    - **SDF Regularization Loss:** Computes the SDF regularization loss on the edges (pairs of vertices). The weight is dynamic, decreasing linearly with iteration progress to prioritize geometric alignment early and smoothness later.
      Let $t_{iter} = \frac{iter}{total\_iter}$.
      The dynamic weight $w_{sdf}$ is calculated as:
      $$ w_{sdf} = w_{sdf\_base} - \left( w_{sdf\_base} - \frac{w_{sdf\_base}}{20} \right) \cdot \min(1.0, 4.0 \cdot t_{iter}) $$
      Where $w_{sdf\_base}$ is `optimization.late_stage.loss.sdf_reg`.
      The loss is:
      $$ L_{sdf} = \text{mean}( \text{sdf\_reg\_loss}(S, edges) ) \cdot w_{sdf} $$

4.  **Total Loss and Optimization Step:**
    Sum the losses and perform the backward pass.
    $$ L_{total} = L_{mask} + L_{depth} + L_{normal} + L_{sdf} $$
    Update the optimizers and schedulers for both SDF and SH coefficients.
    ```python
    total_loss.backward()
    optimizer_sdf.step()
    scheduler_sdf.step()
    optimizer_sh.step()
    scheduler_sh.step()
    ```

**Parameters:**
- `optimization.late_stage.loss.mask_reg`: Weight for mask loss. Not given in the sources.
- `optimization.late_stage.loss.depth_reg`: Weight for depth loss. Not given in the sources.
- `optimization.late_stage.loss.normal_reg`: Weight for normal loss. Not given in the sources.
- `optimization.late_stage.loss.sdf_reg`: Base weight for SDF regularization. Not given in the sources.
- `spherical_harmonics.degree`: Degree of the spherical harmonics used for appearance. Not given in the sources.
- `optimization.train_res`: Resolution of the rendered buffers. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Topology Freeze:** The tetrahedral grid topology is not updated during this phase. ODT (Oriented Delaunay Tetrahedralization) loss and fairness loss are not used because the grid structure is static [S1, unofficial].
- **Numerical Stability:** Epsilon ($10^{-8}$) is added to depth and normal losses to prevent division by zero or NaN gradients during the square root operation.
- **Convergence:** The dynamic SDF weight ensures that SDF regularization does not dominate the early stages of refinement where geometric alignment (mask/depth/normal) is critical.

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the differentiable rendering step per iteration. The loss calculation is linear in the number of pixels and edges.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing buffers for all target views (mask, depth, normals) and gradients for vertices, SDF, and SH coefficients.
- **Strengths:** Provides high-fidelity refinement of geometry and appearance without the computational cost and instability of topology changes.
- **Limitations:** Cannot correct topological errors or significantly alter the mesh structure. Performance depends heavily on the quality of the input mesh from previous stages.
- **When to use:** Final polishing stage after the tetrahedral grid has been stabilized.
- **When not to use:** When the mesh topology is incorrect or significantly misaligned with the target geometry.

## Background
TetWeave Late-Stage Optimization is part of the TetWeave pipeline, a method for reconstructing 3D meshes from 2D multi-view images using tetrahedralization and differentiable rendering [S1, unofficial]. It was introduced as part of the broader TetWeave framework to address the need for precise surface alignment after the coarse geometry and topology have been established. The method leverages the differentiability of the rendering process to backpropagate errors from 2D observations to 3D vertex positions, SDF values, and appearance coefficients.
