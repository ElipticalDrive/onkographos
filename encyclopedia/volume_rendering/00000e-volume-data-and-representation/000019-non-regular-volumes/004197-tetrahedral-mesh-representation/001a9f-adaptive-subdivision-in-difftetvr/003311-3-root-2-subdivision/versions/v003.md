# 3-root-2 Subdivision

Also called: 3-subdivision, 3√2 subdivision

## Overview
3-root-2 subdivision is a hierarchical volume representation scheme that refines a mesh by doubling the total number of vertices per step, theoretically increasing vertex density by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in each spatial dimension [S1, unofficial]. It solves the coarse granularity problem of standard octree refinement, which multiplies vertex counts by 8 per step, by providing finer resolution control for multiresolution rendering [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific geometric insertion sequence (centroid, then face centers, then edge midpoints) and its reliance on B-spline wavelets for data value updates [S1, unofficial]. Builds on [Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR](807-adaptive-subdivision-in-difftetvr.md).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A volumetric mesh (typically structured-rectilinear grids or tetrahedral grids) and a scalar field defined on vertices [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A refined mesh with higher vertex density and updated scalar values at all vertices [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
The method operates on a hierarchical structure where each subdivision step generates new vertices [S1, unofficial]. Vertices store 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and scalar values [G]. Connectivity tracks adjacency lists defining which new vertices are adjacent to an old vertex for the averaging step [S1, unofficial]. In structured grids, eight cuboids share a vertex [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps**
The subdivision of a single cuboid element requires three sequential steps to achieve the equivalent refinement of one octree split:

1.  **Step 1 (Centroid):** Insert the centroid of the original cuboid and connect it to all 8 original vertices [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Step 2 (Face Centers):** Insert the centers of the 6 faces of the original cuboid, which form the centroids of the octahedra created in Step 1 [S1, unofficial]. Connect each face center to the vertices of that face [G]. Delete old edges that were not inserted in the previous step (specifically, edges from the original cuboid that are not part of the new connectivity) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Step 3 (Edge Midpoints):** Insert the midpoints of the 12 edges of the original cuboid [S1, unofficial]. Connect each edge midpoint to the vertices of that edge [G]. Delete old edges that were not inserted in the last two steps (specifically, edges inserted in Step 1 and Step 2 that are not part of the final edge-midpoint connectivity) [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas**
The data value update (averaging step) for an old vertex $v$ is defined as:
$$ v_{\text{new}} = \alpha v_{\text{old}} + (1 - \alpha) w $$
Where:
*   $v_{\text{old}}$ is the scalar value at the original vertex [S1, unofficial].
*   $w$ is the centroid (average) of the scalar values of the adjacent new vertices created in the subdivision steps [S1, unofficial].
*   $\alpha$ is a weighting parameter [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   **Alpha ($\alpha$):** The source does not provide a specific value or solution for $\alpha$ in dimensions larger than two ($n=3$) [S1, unofficial]. It states: "we cannot provide a solution for the choice of $\alpha$ in the update rule" for volumetric data [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Structured-Rectilinear Grids:** If the input is a structured rectilinear grid where eight cuboids share a vertex and all cuboids are of equal size, the averaging step does not change the vertex positions regardless of the specific $\alpha$ value [S1, unofficial].
*   **Wavelet Artifacts:** Wavelets based on 3-root-2 subdivision create over- and undershoots [S1, unofficial]. This can cause isosurfaces to vanish or create spurious components when increasing resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Tetrahedral Grids:** For tetrahedral grids, the splitting step is based on bisection of the longest edge, but a full subdivision scheme with an averaging step is not detailed in the source [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode**
```python
class Vertex:
    def __init__(self, coord, value):
        self.coord = coord
        self.value = value
        self.neighbors = []

def apply_3root2_subdivision(mesh, alpha):
    """Applies 3-root-2 subdivision to a mesh of cuboids."""
    new_vertices = []
    for cuboid in mesh.cuboids:
        # Step 1: Insert Cuboid Centroid
        centroid = sum(v.coord for v in cuboid.vertices) / len(cuboid.vertices)
        v1 = Vertex(coord=centroid, value=0.0)
        new_vertices.append(v1)
        for old_v in cuboid.vertices:
            old_v.neighbors.append(v1)
            v1.neighbors.append(old_v)

        # Step 2: Insert Face Centers
        for face in cuboid.faces:
            face_center = sum(v.coord for v in face.vertices) / len(face.vertices)
            v2 = Vertex(coord=face_center, value=0.0)
            new_vertices.append(v2)
            for fv in face.vertices:
                fv.neighbors.append(v2)
                v2.neighbors.append(fv)
        # Source does not specify exact edge removal algorithm for Step 2 connectivity

        # Step 3: Insert Edge Midpoints
        for edge in cuboid.edges:
            midpoint = (edge.v1.coord + edge.v2.coord) / 2.0
            v3 = Vertex(coord=midpoint, value=0.0)
            new_vertices.append(v3)
            edge.v1.neighbors.append(v3)
            edge.v2.neighbors.append(v3)
            v3.neighbors.extend([edge.v1, edge.v2])
        # Source does not specify exact edge removal algorithm for Step 3 connectivity

    # Averaging Step: Update scalar values
    for vertex in mesh.old_vertices:
        adjacent_new = [v for v in vertex.neighbors if v in new_vertices]
        if adjacent_new:
            w = sum(v.value for v in adjacent_new) / len(adjacent_new)
            vertex.value = alpha * vertex.value + (1 - alpha) * w
    return mesh
```

## Subtopics

### Differentiable Mesh Refinement
A training strategy that refines the tetrahedral mesh by subdividing vertices with high color gradients to increase resolution where needed, aiming to solve the issue of wasted resolution in empty regions [S1, unofficial].

### Recursive Subdivision
A general approach to mesh refinement that applies subdivision rules iteratively [G].

### n-variate B-spline Wavelet Lifting Scheme
A lifting scheme that uses narrow filters to limit the influence of wavelet coefficients, addressing the issue that B-spline wavelets normally influence neighbors beyond immediate vicinity [S1, unofficial]. The scheme is developed for $n=2$ (bivariate) and generalized to $n=3$ (trivariate) for 3-root-2 subdivision [S1, unofficial]. It does not consider extraordinary vertices; it is designed for structured grids where eight cubes share a common vertex [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
*   **Scaling:** Each 3-root-2 subdivision step doubles the total number of vertices [S1, unofficial]. Theoretically, this increases vertex count by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in each dimension [S1, unofficial]. Three subdivision steps are equivalent to one octree refinement step (which multiplies vertices by 8) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides finer granularity than octree refinement, allowing the renderer to get closer to a required mesh-element size and potentially render less data for desired quality [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Wavelets based on this scheme create over- and undershoots, which can cause isosurfaces to vanish when increasing resolution [S1, unofficial]. The choice of the averaging parameter $\alpha$ for 3D is not defined [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When finer granularity is needed than octree refinement provides, and the data is structured-rectilinear or tetrahedral [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When isosurface stability is critical and wavelet artifacts (vanishing isosurfaces) are unacceptable, or when a defined $\alpha$ parameter is required for 3D averaging [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of hierarchical volume representation using n-variate B-spline wavelets [S1, unofficial]. It generalizes the 4-subdivision scheme (for $n=2$) to 3-root-2 subdivision for $n=3$ [S1, unofficial]. The name derives from the theoretical factor of $\sqrt{2}$ increase per dimension [S1, unofficial]. The work references prior research in 3D subdivision by MacCracken and Joy (1996) and lifting schemes by Bertram et al. (2001) and Kobbelt (2000) [S1, unofficial].
