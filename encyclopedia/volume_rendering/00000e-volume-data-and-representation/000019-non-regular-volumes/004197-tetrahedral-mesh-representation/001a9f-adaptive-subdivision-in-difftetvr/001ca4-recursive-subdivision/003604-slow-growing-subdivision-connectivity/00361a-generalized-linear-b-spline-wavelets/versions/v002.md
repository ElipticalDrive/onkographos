# Generalized Linear B-Spline Wavelets

Also called: B-spline wavelet transform, Linear B-spline decomposition

## Overview
Generalized Linear B-Spline Wavelets is a wavelet transform designed for progressive compression of volumetric subdivision meshes, specifically those generated by Slow Growing Subdivision (SGS) connectivity [S1]. It solves the problem of representing hierarchical volumetric data with controllable error tolerances by computing wavelet coefficients that capture the difference between predicted cell centers (centroids) and actual inserted vertices [S1]. Unlike the alternative SGS Wavelet Transform, which smooths original vertices using neighbor positions, Generalized Linear B-Spline Wavelets smooths original vertices exclusively through the influence of wavelet coefficients, resulting in superior compression efficiency at low bit rates [S1].

## How it works
The method operates on a multi-tier refinement structure where tier $k$ vertices $v^{(k)}$ are vector-valued (potentially containing physical quantities like pressure or temperature) and tier $k+1$ cell center vertices $v_C^{(k+1)}$ are inserted into cells [S1]. The transform uses a lifting scheme to decompose and reconstruct the mesh hierarchy [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A set of tier $k$ vertices $v_i^{(k)}$ for $i=0 \dots n-1$ belonging to a cell $C$, and the tier $k+1$ vertex $v_C^{(k+1)}$ located at the cell center [S1].
*   **Output:** A wavelet coefficient $\delta^{(k)}$ (vector-valued) and the updated tier $k$ vertices $v^{(k+1)}$ after smoothing [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Vertices:** $v^{(k)}$ and $v_C^{(k+1)}$ are vectors [S1].
*   **Wavelet Coefficients:** $\delta^{(k)}$ are vectors, stored alongside the coarse geometry for progressive encoding [S1].

### Parameters
*   $n$: The number of vertices of the cell $C$ [S1].
*   $m$: The number of edge-connected vertices to a specific vertex in the tier $k+1$ mesh [S1].
*   $\alpha$: A parameter controlling the shape and stability of the wavelet basis functions. For SGS, $\alpha < 0.7$ results in instability and decreased compression efficiency [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

#### Decomposition
1.  **Compute Centroid:** Calculate the centroid of the tier $k$ vertices of cell $C$:
    $$ \text{centroid} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} v_i^{(k)} $$
2.  **Compute Wavelet Coefficient:** Calculate $\delta^{(k)}$ as the difference between the actual tier $k+1$ vertex and the centroid:
    $$ \delta^{(k)} = v_C^{(k+1)} - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} v_i^{(k)} $$
3.  **Smooth Tier $k$ Vertices:** Update the tier $k$ vertices to produce tier $k+1$ vertices $v^{(k+1)}$ [S1]. The smoothing depends on the set $W(v^{(k+1)})$, which is the set of newly computed wavelet coefficients derived from the neighbors of the vertex in the tier $k+1$ connectivity [S1].
    $$ v^{(k+1)} = v^{(k)} - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{\delta \in W(v^{(k+1)})} \delta $$
    *Note: In the source text, the smoothing formula for the B-spline variant is given as subtracting the average of the wavelet coefficients of former edge neighbors [S1]. This contrasts with the SGS wavelet smoothing which uses neighbor positions [S1].*

#### Reconstruction
1.  **Recover Cell Vertex:** Recover the tier $k+1$ cell vertex $v_C^{(k+1)}$ by adding the wavelet coefficient to the centroid of the tier $k$ vertices:
    $$ v_C^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} v_i^{(k)} + \delta^{(k)} $$
2.  **Recover Tier $k$ Vertices:** Reverse the smoothing step [S1]. The tier $k$ vertices are recovered by adding the wavelet coefficients back:
    $$ v^{(k)} = v^{(k+1)} + \frac{1}{m} \sum_{\delta \in W(v^{(k+1)})} \delta $$
    *Note: The reconstruction steps are the inverse of the decomposition steps and reverse their order [S1].*

### Pseudocode
```python
def decompose_linear_b_spline_wavelet(tier_k_vertices, tier_k_plus_1_vertex, neighbor_deltas_list):
    """
    Decomposes a single cell's geometry into wavelet coefficients and smoothed vertices.
    
    Args:
        tier_k_vertices: List of vectors v_i^(k) for i in 0..n-1
        tier_k_plus_1_vertex: Vector v_C^(k+1)
        neighbor_deltas_list: List of lists containing wavelet coefficients from edge-connected neighbors
        
    Returns:
        delta: Wavelet coefficient vector
        smoothed_vertices: List of updated vectors v^(k+1)
    """
    n = len(tier_k_vertices)
    centroid = sum(tier_k_vertices) / n
    delta = tier_k_plus_1_vertex - centroid
    smoothed_vertices = []
    for i, v in enumerate(tier_k_vertices):
        deltas = neighbor_deltas_list[i]
        m = len(deltas)
        avg_delta = sum(deltas) / m if m > 0 else 0  # Guard against empty connectivity [G]
        smoothed_vertices.append(v - avg_delta)
    return delta, smoothed_vertices

def reconstruct_linear_b_spline_wavelet(smoothed_vertices, delta, neighbor_deltas_list):
    """
    Reconstructs the tier k+1 vertex and original tier k vertices.
    
    Args:
        smoothed_vertices: List of smoothed vectors v^(k+1) from decomposition output
        delta: Wavelet coefficient vector
        neighbor_deltas_list: List of lists containing wavelet coefficients from edge-connected neighbors
        
    Returns:
        tier_k_plus_1_vertex: Vector v_C^(k+1)
        original_tier_k_vertices: List of vectors v_i^(k)
    """
    n = len(smoothed_vertices)
    original_vertices = []
    for i, v_smoothed in enumerate(smoothed_vertices):
        deltas = neighbor_deltas_list[i]
        m = len(deltas)
        avg_delta = sum(deltas) / m if m > 0 else 0  # Guard against empty connectivity [G]
        original_vertices.append(v_smoothed + avg_delta)
    centroid = sum(original_vertices) / n
    tier_k_plus_1_vertex = centroid + delta
    return tier_k_plus_1_vertex, original_vertices
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Instability:** For Slow Growing Subdivision, using $\alpha$ values less than 0.7 results in less stable wavelets and decreased compression efficiency [S1].
*   **Connectivity Definition:** The set $E(v^{(k+1)})$ (or $W(v^{(k+1)})$ for wavelets) includes both tier $k$ vertices and new tier $k+1$ vertices inserted according to subdivision rules [S1]. This affects the neighbor count $m$ and the smoothing result [S1].
*   **Vector Components:** Since vertices may contain physical quantities (e.g., pressure), the wavelet coefficients are vector-valued and must be processed component-wise or as a whole vector depending on the quantization strategy [S1].

## Subtopics

### Slow Growing Subdivision Wavelet Transform
A wavelet transform for volumetric subdivision meshes based on slow growing subdivision connectivity rules, using a lifting scheme [S1]. It differs from Generalized Linear B-Spline Wavelets by smoothing original vertices using neighbor positions rather than wavelet coefficients [S1]. The decomposition step computes tier $k+1$ vertices $v^{(k+1)}$ from tier $k$ vertices $v^{(k)}$ using the formula $v^{(k+1)} = \alpha v^{(k)} + \frac{1-\alpha}{m} \sum v$, where the sum is over vertices $v$ in $E(v^{(k+1)})$ (edge-connected vertices in the tier $k+1$ mesh) and $m$ is the count of such vertices [S1]. The parameter $\alpha$ controls the shape and stability of the wavelet basis functions [S1]. The set $E(v^{(k+1)})$ includes both tier $k$ and new tier $k+1$ vertices, affecting $m$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Compression Efficiency:** The Linear B-spline transform produces qualitatively good coarse representations and high-quality trade-off curves [S1]. It outperforms the SGS wavelet transform at small numbers of bits (superior performance at low bit rates) [S1].
*   **Bit Rates:** In tests on a synthetic volumetric ball mesh, SGS compression resulted in 429,344 bits ($\alpha=0.7$) and 454,080 bits ($\alpha=0.9$), while the Linear B-spline transform resulted in 454,848 bits [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relatively low compression rates are observed due to large perturbations introduced into the mesh, which reduce the effectiveness of the SPIHT coder by lowering correlations between parent and child wavelet coefficients [S1].
*   **Stability:** Requires $\alpha \ge 0.7$ for stability in the SGS context [S1].

## Background
The method was presented by Daniel Laney and Valerio Pascucci from Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in the paper "Progressive Compression of Volumetric Subdivision Meshes" [S1]. It is part of a system for progressive compression of volumetric subdivision meshes based on the slow growing refinement algorithm, which couples a wavelet transform with a progressive encoding (SPIHT) to produce progressive bit streams [S1]. The technique generalizes lifted linear B-spline wavelets to the multi-tier refinement structure of slow growing subdivision, inspired by the lifting technique of Sweldons [S1]. It was tested on synthetic data and data from laser indirect-drive fusion simulations [S1].
