# Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR

Also called: Adaptive Mesh Refinement, Adaptive tetrahedral subdivision, Gradient-based mesh refinement, Prism Splitting, Tetrahedral Subdivision

## Overview
Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR is a coarse-to-fine training strategy that refines a tetrahedral mesh by locally subdividing vertices with high accumulated color gradients, aiming to increase resolution in regions of high-frequency detail while avoiding wasted resolution in empty regions [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its sibling [Recursive Subdivision](1304-recursive-subdivision.md) by relying on gradient-based selection of refinement sites rather than a deterministic state-driven bisection sequence, and from [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) by operating directly on tetrahedral topology via prism splitting rather than adjusting parametric surface vertices [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A tetrahedral mesh (initially a regular grid or Delaunay triangulation of SfM points [S1, unofficial]), a set of training images with known camera poses, and a target tetrahedron count.
*   **Output:** A refined tetrahedral mesh with updated vertex positions and colors, optimized to minimize rendering error against the input images [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The mesh is stored as an unstructured collection of tetrahedra [G]. Each tetrahedron is defined by 4 vertex indices [G]. Adjacency information is required to identify incident tetrahedra for a given vertex and to resolve conflicts between adjacent prisms during splitting [S1, unofficial]. The implementation uses a structure similar to `OpenVolumeMesh`, where cells reference vertices and neighbors [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The refinement process is iterative, repeating until a target number of tetrahedra is reached [S1, unofficial]. Each iteration consists of the following phases:

1.  **Color Optimization:** Optimize only vertex colors for one epoch (defined as training with 800 renderings for the Tooth dataset [S1, unofficial]).
2.  **Joint Optimization:** Optimize both vertex colors and positions jointly for one epoch [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Gradient Accumulation:** Accumulate absolute color gradients at every vertex without performing optimization steps for one epoch [S1, unofficial]. Absolute gradients are used as they were found to result in better outcomes compared to other gradient metrics [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Vertex Selection:** Identify the $\hat{N}$ vertices with the highest absolute color gradient magnitudes. In the source implementation, $\hat{N}$ corresponds to the top 5% of vertices [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Edge Splitting:** For each selected vertex $v$, split all incident edges by inserting a new vertex at the midpoint of each edge [S1, unofficial]. Let the selected vertex be $a$ and its neighbors be $b, c, d$. New vertices $b', c', d'$ are created at the midpoints of edges $ab, ac, ad$ respectively:
    $$ b' = \frac{a + b}{2}, \quad c' = \frac{a + c}{2}, \quad d' = \frac{a + d}{2} $$
6.  **Tetrahedron Splitting (Prism Formation):** The incident tetrahedra are split [S1, unofficial]. For a tetrahedron incident to vertex $a$ with other vertices $b, c, d$, the split results in:
    *   A new tetrahedron incident with the splitting vertex $a$: $\{a, b', c', d'\}$.
    *   A truncated pyramid in the form of a triangular prism formed by vertices $b, c, d, b', c', d'$ [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Prism Decomposition:** Each triangular prism is split into exactly three tetrahedra [S1, unofficial]. The split direction of the quad faces of the prism is determined by a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) to ensure compatibility with adjacent prisms [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Final Optimization:** In the final iteration, optimize only vertex colors to produce the final result [S1, unofficial].

**Prism Splitting and CSP:**
When splitting a prism, the quad faces must be split by a diagonal [S1, unofficial]. The split edges are categorized as 'Rising' (R) or 'Falling' (F) based on the direction of the tessellation edge relative to the extruded prism face in a counter-clockwise manner [S1, unofficial]. Two adjacent prisms sharing a quad face are compatible if one uses an R split and the other uses an F split [S1, unofficial].

The CSP constraints are:
1.  The splits for one prism may not be FFF or RRR.
2.  Two adjacent prisms must split a shared quad face using exactly one F and one R split [S1, unofficial].

A specialized algorithm for solving this CSP is implemented in `FlipSolver.cpp` [S1, unofficial]. A lookup table for tessellation patterns based on vertex indices is provided in the source [S1, unofficial].

*   **Learning Rate for Vertex Positions ($\eta_p$):** Must be very low (e.g., $10^{-6}$ or $5 \times 10^{-6}$) to maintain stability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Regularization Strength ($\lambda$):** Used in vertex position optimization (e.g., $\lambda = 10$) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Mesh Inversion:** Optimization of vertex positions is unstable, especially as the mesh becomes more subdivided and tetrahedra get smaller, increasing the chance of inversion [S1, unofficial].
*   **Recursive Subdivision:** Subdivision does not sufficiently lower local error, causing recursive subdivision of the same regions. This eventually halts optimization progress as CSP solving and data structure updates dominate computation time [S1, unofficial].
*   **Incompatible Splits:** If adjacent prisms choose the same split direction (e.g., both R or both F) on a shared face, it leads to infinitesimally thin holes in the tet mesh [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpolation Error:** Prolongated tetrahedra generated during subdivision may have interpolation errors that prevent local error reduction [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md)
Differentiable Mesh Refinement is a training strategy that adjusts the vertex positions and colors of a template mesh to match input photographs, using multi-layer splatting for stability. It solves the problem of fitting a parametric surface to multi-view imagery where single-layer splatting is unstable due to the flexibility of the optimization and the sensitivity of image derivatives. It differs from pose estimation because each vertex depends on fewer pixels, making derivative errors more significant, and from grid-based methods by operating directly on mesh topology rather than occupancy fields.

### [Recursive Subdivision](1304-recursive-subdivision.md)
Recursive Subdivision is a method for generating adaptive, conforming tetrahedral meshes from a regular cubic volume domain. It solves the problem of maintaining mesh conformity (crack-free boundaries) during hierarchical refinement by enforcing a deterministic, state-driven bisection sequence. Unlike octree-based decompositions that require complex stitching to handle resolution discontinuities, this method guarantees that neighboring cells share faces exactly. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) by relying on a fixed geometric subdivision rule rather than gradient-based training, and from [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md) by using a specific 3-step cyclic bisection pattern rather than a uniform 1-to-8 split.

### [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md)
3-root-2 subdivision is a hierarchical volume representation scheme that refines a mesh by doubling the total number of vertices per step, theoretically increasing vertex density by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in each spatial dimension. It solves the problem of coarse granularity in standard octree refinement, which multiplies vertex counts by 8 per step, by providing finer resolution control for multiresolution rendering. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific geometric insertion sequence (centroid, then face centers, then edge midpoints) and its reliance on B-spline wavelets for data value updates.

### Bey's Red-Refinement Tetrahedron Subdivision
Bey's Red-Refinement Tetrahedron Subdivision is a method for dividing a parent tetrahedron into eight child tetrahedra, four outer and four inner, each having one-eighth of the parent's volume [S2, unofficial]. It identifies four unique vertices of the parent tetrahedron: T (top), L (left), R (right), B (back) [S2, unofficial]. It calculates midpoints of the parent vertices: $TL = (T + L) / 2$, $TR = (T + R) / 2$, $TB = (T + B) / 2$, $LB = (L + B) / 2$, $LR = (L + R) / 2$, $RB = (R + B) / 2$ [S2, unofficial]. The eight child tetrahedra are defined as: Top Outer = $\{ T, TL, TR, TB \}$, Top Inner = $\{LB, TB, TR, TL\}$, Left Outer = $\{ L, TL, LB, LR \}$, Left Inner = $\{TR, LR, LB, TL\}$, Right Outer = $\{ R, RB, TR, LR \}$, Right Inner = $\{LB, LR, TR, RB\}$, Back Outer = $\{ B, RB, LB, TB \}$, Back Inner = $\{TR, TB, LB, RB\}$ [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The CSP solving and data structure updates (adding/removing tetrahedra, rebuilding adjacency) become dominant as the mesh refines, potentially halting optimization progress [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Increases linearly with the number of tetrahedra. The mesh structure must store vertex positions, colors, gradients, and adjacency information [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Color-only optimization achieves a peak PSNR of 33.5 on the Tooth dataset. Joint optimization of vertex positions and colors is significantly less stable and achieves a lower PSNR of approx. 26.9, even with very low learning rates [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Allows for higher resolution in detailed regions without increasing the total number of vertices globally, potentially improving rendering quality where it matters most [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Unstable vertex position optimization, risk of mesh inversion, recursive subdivision of same regions, and high computational cost of CSP solving [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When the scene has high-frequency details in specific regions and empty space elsewhere, and when the mesh topology can be managed effectively [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When stability is critical, or when the computational cost of CSP solving and data structure updates is prohibitive. Also not suitable if vertex position optimization is required for high accuracy, as it yields lower PSNR than color-only optimization [S1, unofficial].

## Background
DiffTetVR was introduced in a preprint by Chris Mile et al. in 2026 [S1, unofficial]. The adaptive subdivision strategy builds on the principles of differentiable volume rendering and tetrahedral mesh representation [S1, unofficial]. The prism splitting algorithm is based on work by Erleben et al. [S1, unofficial]. The initial mesh can be generated using regular grids or Delaunay triangulation of SfM points (e.g., from COLMAP) [S1, unofficial]. The method is part of the DiffTetVR framework, which extends DiffDVR [S1, unofficial].
