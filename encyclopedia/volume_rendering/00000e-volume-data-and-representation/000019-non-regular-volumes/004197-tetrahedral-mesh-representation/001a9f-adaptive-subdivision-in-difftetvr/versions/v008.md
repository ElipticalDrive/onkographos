# Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR

Also called: Adaptive Mesh Refinement, Adaptive tetrahedral subdivision, Gradient-based mesh refinement, Prism Splitting, Tetrahedral Subdivision

## Overview
Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR is a training strategy that refines a tetrahedral mesh by locally subdividing vertices with high accumulated color gradients, aiming to increase resolution in regions of high-frequency detail while avoiding wasted resolution in empty regions [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Tetrahedral Regularization](834-tetrahedral-regularization.md) and [DEFTET](6415-deftet.md) by using a coarse-to-fine iterative process driven by rendering error gradients rather than static geometric criteria or fixed basis functions [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) which optimizes triangle meshes, this method operates directly on the tetrahedral volume representation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Initial tetrahedral mesh (generated via regular grid conversion or Delaunay triangulation of SfM points) [S1, unofficial].
- Camera poses and target images for training [S1, unofficial].
- Target number of tetrahedra or subdivision iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Refined tetrahedral mesh with updated vertex positions and colors [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- The mesh is stored as an unstructured set of tetrahedra. Each tetrahedron is defined by 4 vertex indices.
- Vertex attributes include position ($\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) and color ($\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) [S1, unofficial].
- Adjacency information is required to identify incident tetrahedra for a given vertex and to resolve prism splitting constraints [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $\hat{N}$: The number of vertices with the largest gradient magnitudes selected for subdivision. In the source implementation, this is fixed at 5% of the total vertices [S1, unofficial].
- Learning rates: Vertex color learning rate ($lr_c$) and vertex position learning rate ($lr_p$) [S1, unofficial].
- Regularization strength ($\lambda$) and softplus parameter ($\beta$) for vertex position stability [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Initialization:** Generate an initial coarse tetrahedral mesh. This can be done by converting a regular hexahedral grid aligned with the scene bounding box, or by performing Delaunay triangulation on a sparse point cloud from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) algorithms like COLMAP [S1, unofficial]. Libraries such as fTetWild or TetGen can be used for meshing [S1, unofficial].
2. **Epoch Loop:** Repeat the following cycle until the target number of tetrahedra is reached:
    a. **Color Optimization:** Optimize only vertex colors for one epoch (defined as 800 renderings in the source experiments) [S1, unofficial].
    b. **Joint Optimization:** Optimize both vertex colors and positions for one epoch [S1, unofficial].
    c. **Gradient Accumulation:** Accumulate absolute color gradients at every vertex without performing optimization steps for one epoch [S1, unofficial]. Absolute gradients are used because they were found to result in better outcomes compared to other gradient metrics [S1, unofficial].
    d. **Vertex Selection:** Identify the $\hat{N}$ vertices with the highest absolute color gradient magnitudes [S1, unofficial].
    e. **Edge Splitting:** For each selected vertex, split all incident edges by inserting a new vertex at the midpoint of each edge [S1, unofficial].
    f. **Tetrahedron Splitting:** Split the incident tetrahedra using the newly inserted vertices. Splitting a tetrahedron incident to vertex $a$ results in:
        1. A new tetrahedron incident with the splitting vertex $a$.
        2. A truncated pyramid in the form of a triangular prism formed by vertices $b, c, d$ (the other vertices of the original tetrahedron) and $b', c', d'$ (the new midpoints on edges $ab, ac, ad$) [S1, unofficial].
    g. **Prism Splitting (CSP):** Each triangular prism must be split into exactly three tetrahedra. The split direction of the quad faces of the prism is determined by a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) to ensure compatibility with adjacent prisms [S1, unofficial].
        - Split edges are categorized as 'Rising' (R) or 'Falling' (F) based on the direction of the tessellation edge relative to the extruded prism face in a counter-clockwise manner [S1, unofficial].
        - Two adjacent prisms sharing a quad face are compatible if one uses an R split and the other uses an F split [S1, unofficial].
        - CSP Constraints:
            1. The splits for one prism may not be FFF or RRR [S1, unofficial].
            2. Two adjacent prisms must split a shared quad face using exactly one F and one R split [S1, unofficial].
        - A specialized algorithm (e.g., `FlipSolver.cpp`) is used to solve this CSP [S1, unofficial].
3. **Final Optimization:** In the final iteration, optimize only vertex colors to produce the final result [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Vertex Position Instability:** Optimization of vertex positions is unstable, especially as the mesh becomes more subdivided and tetrahedra get smaller, increasing the chance of inversion [S1, unofficial]. With fixed $\beta=100$ and $\lambda=10$, stability is only achieved at very low learning rates ($1e-6$ and $5e-6$) [S1, unofficial].
- **Recursive Subdivision:** Subdivision does not sufficiently lower local error, causing recursive subdivision of the same regions. This eventually halts optimization progress as CSP solving and data structure updates dominate computation time [S1, unofficial].
- **Incompatible Splits:** Incompatible splits on shared quad faces lead to infinitesimally thin holes in the tet mesh. This occurs if adjacent prisms choose the same split direction (e.g., both R or both F) on a shared face [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance Degradation:** After too many optimization epochs, solving the CSPs and updating the data structure (e.g., OpenVolumeMesh) dominates computation time [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md)
Differentiable Mesh Refinement is a technique that optimizes an explicit triangle mesh extracted from a voxel grid by adjusting vertex positions and performing adaptive subdivision or simplification based on rendering error and geometric consistency. It solves the problem of static mesh resolution by dynamically increasing detail in regions with high reconstruction error or complex geometry, while coarsening simple areas to save memory. Unlike sibling methods that rely solely on recursive geometric criteria, this approach uses differentiable rendering to compute gradients from the final image loss, allowing the mesh topology and geometry to be optimized jointly with appearance.

### [Recursive Subdivision](1304-recursive-subdivision.md)
Recursive Subdivision is a method for generating hierarchical tetrahedral meshes from a regular cubic volume domain by recursively bisecting tetrahedra. It solves the problem of maintaining mesh conformity during hierarchical refinement by enforcing a deterministic, state-driven bisection sequence. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Differentiable Mesh Refinement](984-differentiable-mesh-refinement.md) by relying on a fixed geometric subdivision rule rather than gradient-based training, and from [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md) by using a specific 3-step cyclic bisection pattern rather than a uniform split [G].

### [3-root-2 Subdivision](6931-3-root-2-subdivision.md)
3-root-2 subdivision is a hierarchical volume representation scheme that refines a mesh by doubling the total number of vertices per step, theoretically increasing vertex density by a factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in each spatial dimension. It solves the coarse granularity problem of standard octree refinement, which multiplies vertex counts by 8 per step, by providing finer resolution control for multiresolution rendering. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific geometric insertion sequence (centroid, then face centers, then edge midpoints) and its reliance on B-spline wavelets for data value updates. Builds on [Adaptive Subdivision in DiffTetVR](807-adaptive-subdivision-in-difftetvr.md).

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- The complexity is dominated by the rendering process and the CSP solving for prism splitting. As the mesh subdivides, the number of tetrahedra increases, leading to higher memory usage for vertex and element data [S1, unofficial].
- Solving the CSP and updating the data structure (e.g., OpenVolumeMesh) can dominate computation time after many iterations [S1, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
- Color-only optimization achieves a peak PSNR of 33.5 on the Tooth dataset [S1, unofficial].
- Vertex position optimization with fixed $\beta=100$ and $\lambda=10$ is only stable at learning rates $1e-6$ and $5e-6$, achieving a significantly lower PSNR of approx. 26.9 [S1, unofficial].
- Adaptive subdivision results were not satisfactory, with local error not decreasing sufficiently [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths:** Allows for higher resolution in regions of high-frequency detail, potentially improving rendering quality where it matters most [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Unstable vertex position optimization, risk of mesh inversion, recursive subdivision of same regions, and high computational cost for CSP solving [S1, unofficial]. Does not model directional lighting or volume scattering [S1, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
- Use when a coarse-to-fine refinement strategy is needed for tetrahedral volume rendering and gradient-based adaptation is preferred over static refinement [S1, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
- Avoid if vertex position stability is critical or if computational resources are limited due to the high cost of CSP solving and data structure updates [S1, unofficial].
- Avoid if directional lighting or volume scattering effects are required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
**Origin:**
- The method is part of the DiffTetVR framework, which extends differentiable volume rendering to tetrahedral meshes [S1, unofficial].
- The prism splitting algorithm is based on work by Erleben et al. [44] [S1, unofficial].
- Initial mesh generation can use libraries like fTetWild [52] or TetGen [53], or SfM algorithms like COLMAP [50, 51] [S1, unofficial].

**History and Development:**
- The approach was proposed to address the issue of wasted resolution in empty regions of the tetrahedral mesh [S1, unofficial].
- Experiments showed that while adaptive subdivision could increase resolution, it did not significantly improve PSNR compared to color-only optimization and introduced stability issues [S1, unofficial].

**Usage:**
- Initial tetrahedral meshes can be generated using a regular grid aligned with the scene bounding box or Delaunay triangulation of a sparse point cloud from SfM [S1, unofficial].
