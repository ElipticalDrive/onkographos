# Volumetric Mesh Optimization Objective

Also called: Volumetric Mesh Regularization

## Overview
The Volumetric Mesh Optimization Objective is a composite loss function used to regularize the geometry of tetrahedral meshes generated by neural networks, specifically within the Neural Volumetric Mesh Generator (NVMG) framework. It addresses the problem of generating high-quality, non-self-intersecting volumetric meshes from voxel-based representations by penalizing geometric distortions, orientation flips, and surface irregularities. It is distinguished from sibling methods by its explicit inclusion of an orientation preservation term based on the determinant of the tetrahedron transformation matrix and a Laplacian term that differentiates between volume and surface neighborhoods.

## How it works
The objective function combines three primary regularization terms: a smooth term, an orientation term, and a robust projection term (though the smooth term is the primary focus of the optimization objective details provided). The total smooth regularization term $R_{smooth}$ is defined as:

$$
R_{smooth}(V) = \lambda_a R_{edge}(V, F_{tri}) + \lambda_b R_{laplacian}(V, F_{tri}) + \lambda_c R_{normal}(V^s, F_{tri})
$$

where $V$ and $F$ are the vertices and faces of the volumetric mesh, and $V^s$ and $F^s$ are the vertices and faces of the surface mesh. $\lambda_a, \lambda_b, \lambda_c$ are weighting coefficients.

### Edge Regularization
The edge regularization term $R_{edge}$ penalizes deviations in edge lengths to maintain uniformity:

$$
R_{edge}(V, F_{tri}) := \sum_{(i,j) \sim E} \|v_i - v_j\|_2^2
$$

where $E$ is the set of unordered edges in the volumetric mesh, and $v_i, v_j$ are the coordinates of vertices $i$ and $j$.

### Laplacian Regularization
The Laplacian regularization term $R_{laplacian}$ encourages local smoothness by pulling vertices toward the centroid of their neighbors. It distinguishes between volume vertices and surface vertices:

$$
R_{laplacian}(V, F_{tri}) := \alpha \sum_{v_i \in V} \|\delta_i - v_i\|_2^2 + \beta \sum_{v_i^s \in V^s} \|\delta_i^s - v_i^s\|_2^2
$$

where:
*   $\delta_i = \frac{1}{|N_i|} \sum_{j \sim N_i} v_j$ is the average position of the neighbors of volume vertex $i$.
*   $\delta_i^s = \frac{1}{|N_i^s|} \sum_{j \sim N_i^s} v_j$ is the average position of the neighbors of surface vertex $i$.
*   $N_i$ and $N_i^s$ are the neighborhood graphs for volume and surface vertices, respectively, derived from the connectivity of $F$ and $F^s$.
*   The weights are $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 0.5$ [S1, unofficial].

### Normal Consistency
The normal consistency term $R_{normal}$ ensures that adjacent faces on the surface mesh share consistent normals, assuming only two faces share an edge:

$$
R_{normal}(V^s, F_{tri}) = \sum_{f_i^s \sim f_j^s} (1 - \cos(n_i, n_j))
$$

where $f_i^s \sim f_j^s$ denotes that face $i$ and face $j$ on the surface mesh share the same edge, and $n_i, n_j$ are their respective normal vectors.

### Orientation Preservation
The orientation term $R_{orientation}$ prevents tetrahedra from flipping (inverting) by penalizing small or negative determinants of the transformation matrix. It is defined as:

$$
R_{orientation}(V) = \sum_{c,k} l(M_{c,k})
$$

where $c$ indexes the cube in the voxel grid and $k$ indexes the tetrahedron within that cube. The penalty function $l(M_{c,k})$ is:

$$
l(M_{c,k}) = 
\begin{cases} 
-(\det(M_{c,k}) - v_0)^2 \log(\det(M_{c,k})) & \text{if } \det(M_{c,k}) \le v_0 \\
0 & \text{if } \det(M_{c,k}) > v_0
\end{cases}
$$

The matrix $M_{c,k}$ for the $k$-th tetrahedron in the $c$-th cube is a $4 \times 4$ matrix constructed from the homogeneous coordinates of the tetrahedron's vertices:

$$
M_{c,k} = \begin{bmatrix} 
x_{c,k}^0 & y_{c,k}^0 & z_{c,k}^0 & 1 \\ 
x_{c,k}^1 & y_{c,k}^1 & z_{c,k}^1 & 1 \\ 
x_{c,k}^2 & y_{c,k}^2 & z_{c,k}^2 & 1 \\ 
x_{c,k}^3 & y_{c,k}^3 & z_{c,k}^3 & 1 
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $(x_{c,k}^i, y_{c,k}^i, z_{c,k}^i)$ is the coordinate of the $i$-th vertex of the $k$-th tetrahedron. The threshold $v_0$ is set to $0.01$ [S1, unofficial].

### Mesh Quality Metrics
While not part of the loss function itself, mesh quality is evaluated using aspect ratios. For a triangle, the aspect ratio is $\frac{2R_c}{R_i}$, where $R_c$ is the circumradius and $R_i$ is the inradius. For a tetrahedron, it is $\frac{h_{max}}{2\sqrt{6}r}$, where $h_{max}$ is the largest edge length and $r$ is the inradius. A value of 1 indicates a regular cell (equilateral triangle or regular tetrahedron) [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
The following Python-like pseudocode implements the calculation of the smooth regularization terms and the orientation term.

> **Unverified code.** The code below did not pass a test run against a reference implementation, or no test fits it, so it may contain errors.

```python
import numpy as np

def compute_smooth_regularization(V, F, Vs, Fs, lambda_a=1.0, lambda_b=1.0, lambda_c=1.0):
    """
    Computes the smooth regularization term R_smooth.
    
    Args:
        V: numpy array of shape (N, 3), volumetric mesh vertices.
        F: list of lists, volumetric mesh faces (connectivity).
        Vs: numpy array of shape (Ns, 3), surface mesh vertices.
        Fs: list of lists, surface mesh faces.
        lambda_a: weight for edge regularization.
        lambda_b: weight for Laplacian regularization.
        lambda_c: weight for normal consistency.
        
    Returns:
        float: Total smooth regularization loss.
    """
    # 1. Edge Regularization
    # Sum of squared edge lengths
    edge_loss = 0.0
    for face in F:
        # Assuming F contains triangles, iterate over edges of each face
        # Note: This sums each edge twice if not careful, but the formula sums over unordered edges E.
        # To implement strictly over E, one would need an edge set. 
        # Here we approximate by summing over faces, or better, construct E.
        pass 
    
    # Construct unordered edges E from F
    edges = set()
    for face in F:
        for i in range(len(face)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(face)):
                u, v = min(face[i], face[j]), max(face[i], face[j])
                edges.add((u, v))
                
    for u, v in edges:
        diff = V[u] - V[v]
        edge_loss += np.dot(diff, diff)
        
    R_edge = edge_loss

    # 2. Laplacian Regularization
    # Compute neighborhoods for volume vertices
    # N_i is the set of neighbors of vertex i in the volumetric mesh graph F
    N_volume = build_neighbor_graph(V, F)
    
    # Compute neighborhoods for surface vertices
    # N_i^s is the set of neighbors of vertex i in the surface mesh graph Fs
    N_surface = build_neighbor_graph(Vs, Fs)
    
    laplacian_loss = 0.0
    
    # Volume part
    alpha = 1.0
    for i in range(len(V)):
        neighbors = N_volume[i]
        if not neighbors:
            continue
        delta_i = np.mean(V[neighbors], axis=0)
        diff = delta_i - V[i]
        laplacian_loss += alpha * np.dot(diff, diff)
        
    # Surface part
    beta = 0.5
    for i in range(len(Vs)):
        neighbors = N_surface[i]
        if not neighbors:
            continue
        delta_i_s = np.mean(Vs[neighbors], axis=0)
        diff = delta_i_s - Vs[i]
        laplacian_loss += beta * np.dot(diff, diff)
        
    R_laplacian = laplacian_loss

    # 3. Normal Consistency
    # Compute normals for surface faces
    normals = compute_face_normals(Vs, Fs)
    
    # Build adjacency of faces sharing edges
    # This requires identifying which faces share edges
    face_adjacency = build_face_adjacency(Fs)
    
    R_normal = 0.0
    for f_i, f_j in face_adjacency:
        n_i = normals[f_i]
        n_j = normals[f_j]
        # Ensure normals are normalized
        n_i = n_i / (np.linalg.norm(n_i) + 1e-8)
        n_j = n_j / (np.linalg.norm(n_j) + 1e-8)
        cos_val = np.dot(n_i, n_j)
        # Clamp for numerical stability
        cos_val = np.clip(cos_val, -1.0, 1.0)
        R_normal += (1.0 - cos_val)
        
    R_smooth = lambda_a * R_edge + lambda_b * R_laplacian + lambda_c * R_normal
    return R_smooth

def compute_orientation_regularization(V_cubes, v0=0.01):
    """
    Computes the orientation preservation term R_orientation.
    
    Args:
        V_cubes: list of lists of numpy arrays. V_cubes[c][k] is the (4, 3) array of 
                 vertex coordinates for the k-th tetrahedron in the c-th cube.
        v0: threshold for determinant (default 0.01).
        
    Returns:
        float: Total orientation regularization loss.
    """
    R_orientation = 0.0
    
    for c in range(len(V_cubes)):
        for k in range(len(V_cubes[c])):
            verts = V_cubes[c][k] # Shape (4, 3)
            
            # Construct M matrix
            # M is 4x4. Columns 0-2 are x,y,z. Column 3 is 1s.
            M = np.ones((4, 4))
            M[:, 0] = verts[:, 0]
            M[:, 1] = verts[:, 1]
            M[:, 2] = verts[:, 2]
            
            det_M = np.linalg.det(M)
            
            if det_M <= v0:
                # l(M) = -(det(M) - v0)^2 * log(det(M))
                # Note: log is natural logarithm. det(M) must be > 0 for log to be real.
                # If det(M) <= 0, log is undefined. The source implies det(M) > 0 for the log term,
                # but the condition is det(M) <= v0. 
                # If det(M) <= 0, the penalty should likely be infinite or handled separately.
                # However, the source formula uses log(det(M)). 
                # We assume det(M) > 0 in practice for valid tetrahedra, 
                # but if it drops below v0, it is still positive.
                # If det(M) <= 0, this term is undefined. [G] Guard added.
                if det_M <= 0:
                    # Infinite penalty for inverted/degenerate tetrahedra
                    R_orientation += 1e9 
                else:
                    penalty = -((det_M - v0)**2) * np.log(det_M)
                    R_orientation += penalty
            else:
                R_orientation += 0.0
                
    return R_orientation

def build_neighbor_graph(verts, faces):
    """
    Builds a dictionary mapping vertex index to list of neighbor indices.
    """
    N = len(verts)
    neighbors = [[] for _ in range(N)]
    for face in faces:
        for i in range(len(face)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(face)):
                u, v = face[i], face[j]
                if v not in neighbors[u]:
                    neighbors[u].append(v)
                if u not in neighbors[v]:
                    neighbors[v].append(u)
    return neighbors

def compute_face_normals(verts, faces):
    """
    Computes face normals.
    """
    normals = []
    for face in faces:
        v0 = verts[face[0]]
        v1 = verts[face[1]]
        v2 = verts[face[2]]
        e1 = v1 - v0
        e2 = v2 - v0
        normal = np.cross(e1, e2)
        normals.append(normal)
    return normals

def build_face_adjacency(faces):
    """
    Builds a list of pairs of faces that share an edge.
    """
    edge_to_face = {}
    for i, face in enumerate(faces):
        for j in range(len(face)):
            for k in range(j + 1, len(face)):
                u, v = min(face[j], face[k]), max(face[j], face[k])
                edge = (u, v)
                if edge not in edge_to_face:
                    edge_to_face[edge] = []
                edge_to_face[edge].append(i)
                
    adjacency = []
    for edge, face_indices in edge_to_face.items():
        if len(face_indices) == 2:
            adjacency.append((face_indices[0], face_indices[1]))
    return adjacency
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The computation of the smooth term is linear with respect to the number of edges and vertices in the mesh, $O(|E| + |V| + |V^s|)$. The orientation term is linear with respect to the number of tetrahedra, $O(|T|)$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the mesh connectivity ($F, F^s$) and vertex positions ($V, V^s$). The neighbor graphs can be precomputed or computed on the fly.
*   **Performance:** Ablation studies on chair point cloud generation show that higher voxel resolutions improve generation quality. For a 1NN metric with Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD):
    *   $8 \times 8 \times 8$: CD=60.13, EMD=57.41
    *   $16 \times 16 \times 16$: CD=57.89, EMD=54.26
    *   $32 \times 32 \times 32$: CD=56.12, EMD=53.30 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively prevents mesh flipping and self-intersection through the orientation term. The Laplacian term ensures smoothness while respecting surface boundaries.
*   **Limitations:** The network structure may not fit in GPU memory when scaling to higher resolutions (e.g., beyond $32 \times 32 \times 32$) [S1, unofficial]. The choice of sampling strategy (trajectory sampling vs. uniform/Gaussian) also impacts performance, with trajectory sampling distributing query points more efficiently [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Volumetric Mesh Optimization Objective was introduced in the context of the Neural Volumetric Mesh Generator (NVMG) by Wu et al. [S1, unofficial]. It is part of a broader methodology for 3D shape generation and completion using point-voxel diffusion and mesh deformation. The method builds upon the idea of regularizing neural field outputs into explicit mesh structures, addressing defects such as flipping, distortion, and self-intersection common in generated meshes.
