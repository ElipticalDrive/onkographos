# Upsampling and Super-Resolution

Also called: Super-Resolution, Upscaling, Upsampling Layers, Double Resolution, 3D SR Module

## Overview
Upsampling and Super-Resolution methods increase the spatial resolution of volumetric data or rendered outputs, addressing the loss of detail inherent in low-resolution representations or the computational constraints of real-time rendering. This family is distinguished from sibling methods like [VoxelGrid Down Sample](1014-voxelgrid-down-sample.md) and [Mipmap Generation](2602-mipmap-generation.md) by its focus on generating higher-fidelity data from coarser inputs, often leveraging interpolation, learned transformations, or geometric mapping rather than simple hierarchical culling. It differs from [Wavelet-based Volume Rendering](1052-wavelet-based-volume-rendering.md) by typically operating in the spatial domain or using neural networks to reconstruct high-frequency details, whereas wavelet methods rely on frequency-domain decomposition for compression and adaptive rendering.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism of this family involves generating a hierarchy of data representations at different spatial resolutions. Common mathematical and structural operations include:

1.  **Scaling Factors:** Defining an integer or float scale factor $s$ such that the new resolution $N_{new}$ relates to the previous resolution $N_{prev}$ by $N_{new} = s \times N_{prev}$ or $N_{new} / N_{prev} = s$ [S1, unofficial][S5].
2.  **Hierarchical Structures:** Using spatial structures (like Octrees or Bricks) or frequency-domain transforms (like Wavelets) to represent data at multiple scales [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Interpolation and Filtering:** Using bilinear, bicubic, or quadratic spline kernels to estimate values at new grid points or to generate coarser levels from finer ones [S1, S3, unofficial].
4.  **Selection and Culling:** Dynamically choosing which resolution level to render based on error metrics, viewer distance, or hardware constraints [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Cubic Upsampling via Tiling](2342-cubic-upsampling-via-tiling.md)
A method that uses tiling-based cubic kernels to upsample volumetric data. It differs from [3D Image Resize and Crop](2703-3d-image-resize-and-crop.md) by relying on local cubic interpolation within tiles rather than global bilinear resizing with cropping/padding. Choose this when high-frequency continuity is required across tile boundaries without the artifacts of simple bilinear interpolation.

### [3D Image Resize and Crop](2703-3d-image-resize-and-crop.md)
This method prepares 3D volumetric data for super-resolution pipelines by standardizing spatial dimensions and normalizing intensity ranges. It solves the problem of inconsistent input shapes and scales in training datasets by cropping the depth axis to a central region, squaring the lateral dimensions via zero-padding, and resizing to a target resolution. It differs from [Cubic Upsampling via Tiling](2342-cubic-upsampling-via-tiling.md) by using bilinear interpolation and explicit cropping/padding rather than tiling-based cubic kernels, and from [3D Fast Bilateral Solver](6478-3d-fast-bilateral-solver.md) by being a deterministic preprocessing step rather than an optimization-based refinement.

### [3D Fast Bilateral Solver](6478-3d-fast-bilateral-solver.md)
The 3D Fast Bilateral Solver (3D FBS) is a post-processing refinement technique used to upsample low-resolution similarity volumes to high resolution while adapting them to the edges of the underlying raw intensity volume. It solves the problem of visual artifacts in volume rendering caused by the lack of voxel-precise details in coarse feature maps, specifically by restoring fine structures and aligning segmentation borders with intensity gradients. Unlike 2D propagation methods that require complex topology solving and multi-second inference times, the 3D FBS merges features in 3D directly, offering a fast, interactive refinement step (typically < 1 second) that enhances visual fidelity without re-running the feature extraction network.

### Cloud Upscaling
Rendering clouds at a lower resolution and upscaling to screen resolution, leveraging their naturally blurry and fluffy appearance [S1, unofficial]. This approach exploits the perceptual tolerance for lower frequency details in volumetric cloud structures, allowing for significant performance gains by reducing the number of ray-marching steps or voxel evaluations required per frame.

### Upsampling Layer
A layer that expands the spatial dimensions $x$ and $y$ of an input volume $U$ of size $x \times y \times p$ by factors $s_x$ and $s_y$ respectively to generate a volume $V$ of size $(s_x x) \times (s_y y) \times p$ [S2, unofficial]. It contains no trainable parameters and is used in the decoder network to reconstruct the volume of the original size [S2, unofficial]. Typical parameters include scale factors $s_x=2, s_y=2$, a filter size of $2 \times 2$, and a stride of 2 [S2, unofficial].

### Epipolar Overlap Visualization
A visualization step in epipolar transformers where the `valid` mask from sampling data is upsampled to match the context image resolution using `repeat` with factors $r_h=ds$ and $r_w=ds$ [S3, unofficial]. The upsampled valid mask is then blended with context images via `valid = valid * (green + context_images[index]) / 2` and concatenated with the context images [S3, unofficial]. The parameter $ds$ represents the downscale factor for the epipolar transformer, typically an integer greater than 1 [S3, unofficial].

### Pre-processing for Double Resolution
A method to increase resolution by mapping full-resolution voxels to a double-resolution volume and filling gaps to provide more details and accurate voxel positions [S4]. Double resolution allows existing voxels to be located at the middle point between two voxels of the full-resolution volume, correcting for discrete domain positioning errors where non-corresponding pixels might map to the same voxel [S4]. The process involves mapping the voxel at location $(i, j, k)$ of the full-resolution volume to location $(2i-1, 2j-1, 2k-1)$ of the double-resolution volume [S4]. Other voxels are inspected to determine if they are part of the volume or transparent by registering depth maps of references into the double-resolution volume [S4]. The constructed volume is then iteratively refined utilizing brightness information of the reference views, and the color of each voxel is properly selected to render a smooth and realistic image [S4].

### 3D Super-Resolution (SR) Module
Reconstructs the original high-resolution volume from the Implicit Neural Representation (INR) output using deep learning, providing intrinsic denoising and reducing reconstruction artefacts [S5]. It employs a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for feature extraction that incorporates an Attention Mechanism to prioritize significant features, improving volume resolution [S5]. The module learns intricate transformations from low-resolution to high-resolution data spaces, outputting a high-resolution volume with denoised features [S5]. It is trained end-to-end with the INR module using an L1 loss function [S5]. The model used is SRNO [11], and the number of channels in the attention structure is a hyperparameter evaluated during optimization [S5]. Compared to other super-resolution techniques, SRNO produces fewer artefacts, such as ringing and blurring [S5].

## Background
The concept of upscaling volumetric data has evolved from simple interpolation techniques to complex, learning-based super-resolution models. Early methods focused on geometric mapping, such as the "Double Resolution" technique for view synthesis, which addressed discrete positioning errors by inserting voxels at mid-points between existing grid locations [S4]. In the context of real-time rendering, cloud upsampling was proposed to leverage the inherent blurriness of volumetric clouds to render at lower resolutions [S1, unofficial]. With the advent of deep learning, upsampling layers became standard components in autoencoder architectures for compressed volume rendering, expanding spatial dimensions without trainable parameters [S2, unofficial]. More recently, 3D super-resolution modules using CNNs and attention mechanisms have been integrated with Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) to enhance medical volume data, offering denoising capabilities alongside resolution enhancement [S5]. The 3D Fast Bilateral Solver extended 2D edge-aware smoothing to 3D similarity volumes, allowing for fast, interactive refinement of segmentation borders aligned with intensity gradients [S1, unofficial].
