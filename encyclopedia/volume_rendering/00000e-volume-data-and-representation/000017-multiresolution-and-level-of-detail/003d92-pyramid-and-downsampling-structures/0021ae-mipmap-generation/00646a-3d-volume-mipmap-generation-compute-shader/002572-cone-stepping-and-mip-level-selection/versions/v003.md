# Cone Stepping and Mip Level Selection

Also called: Cone Tracing Mip Selection

## Overview
Cone Stepping and Mip Level Selection is the sampling strategy used in Voxel Pre-integration to determine which resolution level of a 3D mipmap to sample at each step along a ray. It solves the problem of aliasing and performance by ensuring that the spatial extent of the sample (the cone diameter) matches the resolution of the texture data, effectively averaging light over the area covered by the cone [S1, S2, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods like Brick LOD Selection by calculating resolution bounds for individual sample positions based on cone geometry rather than determining LOD for entire brick volumes based on distance [S1, unofficial][S4].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Ray origin, ray direction, cone half-angle ($\theta$), current distance from cone origin ($d$), voxel size ($v$), and the 3D mipmap texture.
*   **Outputs:** A sampled color/value from the 3D texture at the appropriate Level of Detail (LOD).

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Texture:** A uniform grid of voxels storing RGBA values, functioning as a 3D bitmap [S2, unofficial].
*   **Mipmap Pyramid:** A set of lower-resolution versions of the 3D texture where each LOD increment $l$ reduces height, width, and depth by a power of two (factor of 2) from the previous level [S2, unofficial]. LOD 0 is the original full-resolution image [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Step along the ray:** Advance along the ray in voxel-size increments (ray-marching) in world space [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Calculate Cone Diameter:** For the current position, calculate the diameter of the cone at that distance [S1, unofficial]:
    $$coneDiameter = 2 \cdot \tan(\theta) \cdot d$$
    Where:
    *   $\theta$ is the cone half-angle.
    *   $d$ is the distance from the cone's start point (origin) to the current sample position.
3.  **Calculate Mip Level:** Determine the appropriate LOD for the 3D texture sample [S1, unofficial]:
    $$mip = \log_2\left(\frac{coneDiameter}{v}\right)$$
    Where:
    *   $v$ is the size of a single voxel in the base level (LOD 0).
    *   The resulting $mip$ value is typically rounded to the nearest integer or clamped to the available mipmap levels [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Sample Texture:** Perform a texture lookup using the calculated LOD [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def sample_voxel_cone(ray_origin, ray_direction, cone_half_angle, voxel_size, texture, max_mip):
    """
    Samples a 3D mipmap texture using cone stepping.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: (float, float, float) Start of the ray.
        ray_direction: (float, float, float) Normalized direction of the ray.
        cone_half_angle: float Half-angle of the cone in radians.
        voxel_size: float Size of a single voxel in world units.
        texture: Object with method 'sample_lod(point, lod)'.
        max_mip: int Maximum available mipmap level.
        
    Returns:
        float or tuple: Sampled color/value.
    """
    # Current position along the ray
    # In a full implementation, this would be inside a loop stepping by voxel_size
    # Here we assume a single step at distance 'step_dist' for demonstration
    step_dist = 1.0 * voxel_size 
    
    # 1. Calculate distance from cone origin
    distance_from_origin = step_dist
    
    # 2. Calculate cone diameter at current position
    cone_diameter = 2.0 * math.tan(cone_half_angle) * distance_from_origin
    
    # 3. Calculate mip level
    # log2(coneDiameter / voxelSize)
    mip_float = math.log2(cone_diameter / voxel_size)
    
    # Clamp mip level to available range [0, max_mip]
    mip_level = int(round(mip_float))
    mip_level = max(0, min(mip_level, max_mip))
    
    # 4. Sample texture at current position with calculated LOD
    # Assuming bilinear/trilinear filtering is active
    sample_point = ray_origin + ray_direction * distance_from_origin
    return texture.sample_lod(sample_point, mip_level)
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Cone Half-Angle ($\theta$):** Determines the spread of the cone. Typical values depend on the scene's lighting setup and desired blur radius [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Size ($v$):** The physical size of a voxel in the base mipmap level [S1, unofficial].
*   **LOD (Level of Detail):** Integer where 0 is the original image. Determines the resolution of the mipmap used for sampling [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision in the logarithm calculation can affect LOD selection [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Ensure the cone diameter does not exceed the texture bounds or the maximum mip level [S2, unofficial].
*   **Aliasing:** Selecting the finest qualifying level defeats memory and large-volume goals by over-resolving. The coarsest level satisfying the condition should be chosen to prevent aliasing artifacts [S4]. Reductions must be band-limited to prevent aliasing, which fabricates structure rather than merely losing it [S4].

## Subtopics

### Mipmap-based Volume Sampling
A technique using low-resolution 3D mipmaps of direct lighting values to sample volumetric color, adjusting LOD based on cone diameter to approximate indirect light [S2, unofficial]. It differs from general cone stepping by explicitly leveraging OpenGL's `textureLod` function with bilinear filters, which yields the average color value at a point in a radius of $2^l$ pixels, where $l$ is the LOD value [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when hardware-accelerated texture filtering is available and bilinear interpolation is acceptable for volume approximation [S2, unofficial].

### Segmented Dataset LOD Management
Modifies the precomputation of levels of detail (LOD) for segmented datasets to preserve label integrity rather than averaging values [S3]. It differs from standard mipmap generation by selecting the most popular (mode) label for each voxel/group instead of computing the average value, and substitutes trilinear filtering with nearest neighbor filtering at runtime to prevent label bleeding [S3]. It is chosen when rendering categorical data (e.g., anatomical labels) where averaging would create invalid intermediate labels [S3].

### Mipmap Level Selection for Volume Rendering
Focuses on the selection criterion for the mipmap level based on the projected pixels per voxel ($p$) [S4]. It differs from cone-diameter-based selection by using the condition $p \le 1$ to select the coarsest (lowest-resolution) mipmap level that satisfies the constraint, rounding up in resolution [S4]. It is chosen when the primary goal is to ensure that one voxel maps to at most one screen pixel to prevent over-sampling and memory waste [S4].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample for LOD calculation and texture lookup, assuming constant-time logarithm and texture access. The overall complexity is proportional to the number of steps in the ray march.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the size of the 3D mipmap pyramid. A full mipmap pyramid for a $N \times N \times N$ texture requires approximately $\frac{4}{3} N^3$ voxels (sum of geometric series $1 + 1/8 + 1/64 + \dots$).
*   **Measured Performance:** Performance is highly dependent on texture cache efficiency. Using the correct LOD minimizes cache misses by accessing the most appropriate resolution [S2, unofficial].
*   **Scaling:** Scales well with resolution as the mipmap pyramid allows efficient sampling at different distances. However, generating and storing the mipmap pyramid increases memory usage [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces aliasing, improves performance by using lower resolutions for distant samples, and simplifies volume sampling by leveraging hardware texture filtering [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires pre-computation of the mipmap pyramid. May introduce blurring if the LOD selection is not accurate [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering large volumetric scenes with varying distances from the viewpoint, and when hardware supports efficient 3D texture sampling with mipmaps [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When memory is extremely constrained and a full mipmap pyramid cannot be stored, or when precise high-frequency details are required at all distances [S4].

## Background
The concept of mipmaps was initially conceived by Lance Williams in his 1983 paper "Pyramidal Parametrics" [S2, unofficial]. The application of mipmaps to voxel cone tracing for volume sampling was proposed by Crassin [S2, unofficial]. The specific cone stepping formula for calculating LOD based on cone diameter is described in the context of voxel-based global illumination [S1, unofficial]. The use of segmented datasets with mode-based LOD selection is discussed in the context of volumetric display of the human musculoskeletal system [S3]. The condition $p \le 1$ for level selection is discussed in the context of volume3D rendering plans [S4].
