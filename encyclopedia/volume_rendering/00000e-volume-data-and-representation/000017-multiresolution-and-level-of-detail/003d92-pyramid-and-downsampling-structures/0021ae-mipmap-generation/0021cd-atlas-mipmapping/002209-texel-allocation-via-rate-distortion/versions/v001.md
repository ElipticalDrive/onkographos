# Texel Allocation via Rate-Distortion

Also called: Atlas Baking, Bake and Texel Allocation, Stage 2, Support-Aware Extent and Rate-Distortion Allocation, Texel Allocation

## Overview
This method optimizes the memory footprint of a neural texture atlas by reducing the spatial extent of surfels to exclude zero-support regions and allocating texel counts per surfel based on visual importance rather than strict Nyquist limits [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the inefficiency of uniform atlas sizing where many texels lie outside the compact kernel support or contribute negligibly to the rendered image [S1, unofficial]. By probing the distortion introduced by downsampling each surfel's tile and weighting it by visibility, the method achieves significant compression with minimal quality loss, outperforming full-extent atlases in both PSNR and file size [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of two main phases: baking the neural representation into a raw texture, and allocating texel sizes using a rate-distortion optimization.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**: A trained hash grid and MLP (from Stage 1); the set of surfels with their positions, orientations, and scales; the accumulated visibility $\alpha_T$ for each surfel across training views.
*   **Outputs**: A shelf-packed, BC7-compressed texture atlas where each surfel occupies a rectangle of variable size.

### Data Structures
*   **Surfel Tile**: A 2D grid of texels representing the neural field output for a single surfel.
*   **Halving Ladder**: For each surfel, a sequence of candidate rectangle sizes $(r_x \times r_y)$, generated by repeatedly halving the dimensions until a minimum side length of 4 is reached, or the surfel is assigned no texture.
*   **Visibility Weight**: A scalar $w_i$ for each surfel $i$, derived from its accumulated $\alpha_T$ visibility.

### Steps

1.  **Support-Aware Extent Reduction**:
    The spatial extent $e$ of each surfel's tile is reduced from 4 to 3. The compact Beta kernel is identically zero past $r = 3\sigma$, so texels beyond this radius are never fetched [S1, unofficial]. This removes approximately 50.5% of texels that lie outside the support, even with a one-texel guard band for bilinear filtering [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Baking**:
    Evaluate the trained hash grid and MLP on each surfel's tile over the kernel support.
    *   Surfel UV coordinates range from $[-3, 3]^2$ at texel centers.
    *   Maximum resolution is $64 \times 64$ texels per surfel.
    *   Computation is performed in FP16 [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Rate-Distortion Probe**:
    For every surfel $i$ and every size $s$ on its halving ladder:
    *   Downsample the full-resolution baked tile to size $s$.
    *   Upsample it back to the original resolution.
    *   Compute the kernel- and opacity-weighted error $D_{i,s}$ between the downsampled-upsampled patch and the full-resolution bake.
    *   Weight this error by the surfel's accumulated visibility $w_i$ to get the importance-weighted distortion $W_{i,s} = w_i \cdot D_{i,s}$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Allocation via Lagrange Multiplier**:
    *   Define a global Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$.
    *   Bisect $\lambda$ to find the value such that the total importance-weighted distortion equals a target distortion $D_{target}$.
    *   The target distortion $D_{target}$ is set to the error introduced by the BC7 codec, ensuring the allocation cost does not exceed the quantizer's inherent loss [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each surfel $i$, select the size $s^*$ that minimizes $W_{i,s} + \lambda \cdot \text{texels}(s)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   If the optimal size is "no texture," the surfel renders from its spherical Voronoi color alone [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Re-baking and Compression**:
    *   Re-evaluate the neural field at the chosen sizes for each surfel.
    *   Shelf-pack the resulting rectangles into a single atlas.
    *   Compress the atlas using BC7 [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Extent $e$**: 3 (reduced from 4) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Minimum Texel Side**: 4 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Max Resolution**: $64 \times 64$ texels per surfel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision**: FP16 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Target Distortion**: BC7 codec error [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision**: FP16 is used for baking, which may introduce quantization noise. However, the BC7 compression also introduces quantization, and the target distortion is set to match this [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries**: The support-aware extent reduction ($e=3$) relies on the Beta kernel being identically zero past $r=3\sigma$. If the kernel is not compact or the support is not correctly calculated, texels may be incorrectly discarded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input**: Surfels with very low visibility may be assigned no texture, rendering from their spherical Voronoi color. This is a valid fallback but may result in visible artifacts if the Voronoi color is not a good approximation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Probing Raw vs. Finetuned Atlas**: Probing the raw bake keeps 45% of texels, while probing the finetuned atlas keeps 60% but costs a second finetune and yields slightly lower quality (0.05 dB less PSNR, 0.002 more LPIPS) for 23% more bytes [S1, unofficial]. The raw bake probe is preferred for efficiency.

## Subtopics

### [Residual Neural Texture Baking](2709-residual-neural-texture-baking.md)
A technique where a neural network (hash grid + MLP) is trained to predict a residual color, which is added to a fixed baseline view-dependent color. After training, the neural network is evaluated over a spatial atlas to bake the residual into a static texture, removing the need for network inference during rendering.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The rate-distortion probe requires evaluating the neural field for each surfel at multiple sizes, which is $O(N \cdot S \cdot R^2)$ where $N$ is the number of surfels, $S$ is the number of sizes on the halving ladder, and $R$ is the max resolution. The bisecting search for $\lambda$ adds a logarithmic factor.
*   **Memory Complexity**: The atlas size is reduced to approximately 45-60% of the full atlas, depending on the probing method [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance**:
    *   **Mip-NeRF 360**: 27.31 PSNR (vs 27.23 full) with 173 MB (vs 388 MB full) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Tanks & Temples**: 24.11 PSNR (vs 24.10) with 132 MB (vs 284 MB) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Deep Blending**: 30.05 PSNR (vs 29.87) with 73 MB (vs 228 MB) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Significant memory reduction with minimal quality loss; outperforms full-extent atlases in PSNR and SSIM [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Requires a pre-trained hash grid and MLP; the rate-distortion probe adds computational overhead during the baking stage [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: When memory bandwidth or storage is a bottleneck, and the scene has varying levels of detail that can be exploited by variable-sized texel allocation.
*   **When Not to Use**: When real-time baking is required, or when the scene is too simple to benefit from variable-sized allocation.

## Background
The method was introduced in "Bake It Till You Make It: Ultrafast Spatial Texture-Atlas Splatting" [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of atlas mipmapping, which addresses undersampling when a distant surfel covers few pixels but keeps its full tile [2639-atlas-mipmapping.md]. The rate-distortion allocation is a novel approach to optimize the atlas size by considering the visual importance of each surfel, rather than just the Nyquist limit [S1, unofficial].
