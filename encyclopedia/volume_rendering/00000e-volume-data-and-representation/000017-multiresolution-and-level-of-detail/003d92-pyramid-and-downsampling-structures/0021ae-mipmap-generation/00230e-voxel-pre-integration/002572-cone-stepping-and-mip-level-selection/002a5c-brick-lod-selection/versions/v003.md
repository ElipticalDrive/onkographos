# Brick LOD Selection

Also called: Choix du niveau de LOD des briques

## Overview
Brick LOD Selection determines the appropriate level of detail for volumetric bricks based on the distance to the viewpoint, aiming to ensure a projected voxel covers exactly one screen pixel [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods by calculating resolution bounds for entire brick volumes rather than individual samples, ensuring all necessary LOD levels within the brick's spatial extent are loaded [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Viewpoint position, brick geometry (bounding box corners) [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A set of LOD levels required for the brick [S1, unofficial].
**Data Structures:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
**Steps:**
1. Assume spherical pixels and circular voxels to simplify resolution determination independent of voxel orientation [S1, unofficial].
2. Calculate ideal voxel size proportional to its distance from the viewpoint [S1, unofficial].
3. Determine a lower bound for brick resolution based on the distance of the farthest corner of the brick to the viewpoint [S1, unofficial].
4. Determine an upper bound for brick resolution based on the distance of the nearest corner of the brick to the viewpoint [S1, unofficial].
5. Load all LOD levels contained within the calculated interval if not already present in storage [S1, unofficial].
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
**Edge cases and pitfalls:** Not given in the sources. [S1, unofficial]
**Pseudocode:**
```python
def select_brick_lod(brick_corners, viewpoint):
    # brick_corners: list of 8 3D vectors in world space
    # viewpoint: 3D vector
    # Step 1 & 2: Ideal voxel size is proportional to distance.
    # Step 3: Lower bound based on farthest corner
    distances = [((c.x - viewpoint.x)**2 + (c.y - viewpoint.y)**2 + (c.z - viewpoint.z)**2)**0.5 for c in brick_corners]
    max_dist = max(distances)
    min_dist = min(distances)
    # The exact formula mapping distance to LOD index is not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
    lod_min = max(0, int(max_dist))
    lod_max = max(0, int(min_dist))
    # Step 5: Load all LODs in interval
    required_lods = range(lod_min, lod_max + 1)
    return required_lods
```

## Subtopics

### Brick Misregistration Fix
Fixes geometric seams caused by multi-LOD bricks fetching boxes snapped to whole voxels of their pyramid level, which were previously treated as owned boxes, causing content shift and stretch [S2, unofficial]. The fetch box covers up to one level voxel more than the brick owns on each face [S2, unofficial]. The fix replaces the use of the fetched box for remap with `chunkOwnedTexBox` [S2, unofficial]. This change is applied as the single source for the remap in all three call sites: `chunkUniformsFor`, `chunkSampleTransform`, and the third implicit site [S2, unofficial]. Without this fix, neighboring bricks snap independently, leading to disagreement about level coordinates at shared faces, creating geometric seams that cannot be removed by brightness adjustments [S2, unofficial]. On the `hoa_heart` pyramid, this moves brick content by up to 3.4/6.3/8.7 common voxels at L2/L3/L4 and corrects stretch of up to 5.9/12.4/23.7 (approx 61 um displacement on an L4 brick) [S2, unofficial]. Single-level plans take an exact path and remain bit-identical to previous behavior [S2, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced to determine brick LOD directly from viewpoint distance, adhering to MIP-mapping constraints where ideal resolution covers exactly one screen pixel [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the need to limit update costs and avoid reloading all data during user navigation, ensuring only necessary LOD levels are loaded into storage [S1, unofficial].
