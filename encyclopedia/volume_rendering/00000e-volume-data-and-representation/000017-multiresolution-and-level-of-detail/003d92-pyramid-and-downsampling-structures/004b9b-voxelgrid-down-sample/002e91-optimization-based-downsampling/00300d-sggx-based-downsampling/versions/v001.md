# SGGX-based Downsampling
Also called: Heitz et al. downsampling

## Overview
SGGX-based Downsampling is a method for reducing the resolution of microflake normal distributions in volume data [S1]. It addresses the loss of visual fidelity that occurs when averaging anisotropic distributions, by preserving the statistical properties of the microflake orientations [S1]. It differs from naive averaging by maintaining the directional coherence of the material, which is critical for accurate appearance in subsequent rendering stages [S1].

## How it works
The method downsamples microflake normal distributions by aggregating them into larger blocks while preserving their statistical moments [S1].

**Inputs:**
- A set of microflake normal distributions $N_i$ within a target block.
- Each distribution is represented by its normal vector $\mathbf{n}_i$ and potentially a spread parameter $\sigma_i$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
- A single representative microflake normal distribution $N_{out}$ for the block [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- The SGGX (Spherical Gaussian Geometry) distribution is defined by a mean direction $\mathbf{m}$ and a covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ [S1].
- For a set of normals $\{\mathbf{n}_i\}$, the aggregated mean $\mathbf{m}$ is the normalized sum of the normals:
  $$ \mathbf{m} = \frac{\sum_i \mathbf{n}_i}{\|\sum_i \mathbf{n}_i\|} $$
- The aggregated covariance $\mathbf{C}$ is computed from the scatter of the normals around the mean [S1]:
  $$ \mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i (\mathbf{n}_i - \mathbf{m})(\mathbf{n}_i - \mathbf{m})^T $$
  where $N$ is the number of normals in the block [S1].

**Steps:**
1. Collect all microflake normals $\mathbf{n}_i$ in the target voxel block [S1].
2. Compute the sum of normals $\mathbf{S} = \sum_i \mathbf{n}_i$ [S1].
3. Normalize $\mathbf{S}$ to obtain the mean direction $\mathbf{m}$ [S1].
4. Compute the covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ using the formula above [S1].
5. Construct the output SGGX distribution using $\mathbf{m}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
- Block size: Not given in the sources.
- Number of normals per block: Not given in the sources.

**Edge cases:**
- If $\|\sum_i \mathbf{n}_i\| \approx 0$, the mean direction is undefined. This occurs when normals are uniformly distributed. In this case, the distribution is isotropic, and $\mathbf{C}$ should reflect uniform spread [G].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
- Time complexity: Linear in the number of normals in the block, $O(N)$, due to the summation and covariance calculation [S1].
- Memory complexity: $O(1)$ per block, storing only the mean and covariance [S1].
- Strengths: Preserves anisotropy better than simple averaging [S1].
- Limitations: Does not account for shadowing effects, which are addressed in subsequent optimization-based methods [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Heitz et al. in 2015 [S1]. It serves as a foundational step for more advanced downsampling techniques that incorporate shadowing [S1].
