# Time-Feature Extraction

Also called: Lipschitz regularity based compression, Lipschitz-based time variation measurement, Time-Feature Extraction via Wavelet Singularity Detection, Time-resolved volume feature extraction, Wavelet probing for time-resolved data

## Overview
Time-Feature Extraction is a method for analyzing time-resolved volume data by identifying regions with strong temporal variation through the measurement of local Lipschitz regularity [S1]. It distinguishes itself from standard temporal compression by operating on the multiresolution wavelet coefficients of individual volumes, treating the time-evolution of specific spatial-frequency components as 1D signals to detect singularities [S1]. This allows for adaptive reconstruction where high-variation regions are preserved with high accuracy while static regions are compressed more aggressively [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A sequence of $N$ volume datasets $V_0, V_1, \dots, V_{N-1}$, each of spatial dimensions $D_x \times D_y \times D_z$.
**Outputs:** A set of Lipschitz exponents $\alpha$ characterizing the temporal regularity of spatial regions, and a compressed representation prioritizing high-variation features.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Sparse Multiresolution Representation:** For each volume $V_t$, a list of wavelet coefficients. Each entry contains:
    *   `scale`: integer $s \in [1, s_{max}]$.
    *   `position`: tuple $(x, y, z)$ indicating the spatial location of the coefficient.
    *   `value`: float64, the wavelet coefficient magnitude.
    *   *Note:* Coefficients below a threshold are discarded to ensure sparsity [S1].
2.  **Time-Evolution Signal:** For a specific spatial-frequency component (identified by scale $s$ and spatial position $(x,y,z)$), a 1D array $C_t$ of length $N$, where $C_t$ is the value of the coefficient at time $t$.

**Parameters:**
*   $s_{max}$: Maximum scale for the wavelet transform. Not given in the sources.
*   Threshold: Minimum coefficient magnitude to be retained. Not given in the sources.
*   Learning rate: For the steepest descent minimization. Not given in the sources.

**Steps:**

1.  **Pre-processing (Spatial Compression):**
    For each time step $t \in [0, N-1]$:
    a. Compute the 3D wavelet transform of volume $V_t$ independently. Do not use information from neighboring volumes [S1].
    b. Store the resulting coefficients in the Sparse Multiresolution Representation.
    c. Discard coefficients with magnitude below the threshold to create a sparse representation [S1].

2.  **Reorganization (Temporal Relation):**
    Iterate through the multiresolution levels starting from the finest scale ($s=1$) to the coarsest ($s=s_{max}$) [S1]:
    a. For each unique spatial position $(x,y,z)$ and scale $s$ that has valid coefficients in at least two time steps:
    b. Construct a 1D signal $C^{(s,x,y,z)}$ of length $N$, where the $t$-th element is the wavelet coefficient value at time $t$ for that specific spatial-frequency component [S1].

3.  **Wavelet Expansion (Temporal Multiresolution):**
    For each generated 1D signal $C^{(s,x,y,z)}$:
    a. Compute a 1D wavelet transform along the time axis [S1].
    b. This creates a multiscale representation of the time evolution for that specific region [S1].

4.  **Singularity Detection and Lipschitz Exponent Calculation:**
    For each time-evolution signal represented in the multiscale domain:
    a. Identify the local maxima lines in the time-scale domain [S1].
    b. Determine the Lipschitz exponent $\alpha$ by analyzing the asymptotic decay of the wavelet coefficients $Wf(s, x)$ as the scale $s$ approaches zero (finer scales) [S1].
    c. The relationship is modeled as $|Wf(s, x)| \approx K \cdot s^\alpha$ [S1].
    d. To find $\alpha$ and the constant $K$, minimize the following energy function over the available scales $s=1$ to $s_{max}$ [S1]:
       $$ E(K, \alpha) = \sum_{s=1}^{s_{max}} (\log(|Wf(s, x)|) - \log(K) - \alpha \cdot \log(s))^2 $$
    e. Solve the minimization problem using a steepest descent algorithm [S1].
       *   Initialize $K$ and $\alpha$ at a random point on the finest scale [S1].
       *   Iteratively update $K$ and $\alpha$ using the first derivative of the energy function [S1].

5.  **Discrimination and Reconstruction Priority:**
    a. Classify regions based on the calculated $\alpha$. High variation (singularities) corresponds to specific $\alpha$ values [S1].
    b. Regions with strong temporal variation (high importance) are marked for high-accuracy reconstruction [S1].
    c. Regions with slight variation are marked for less accurate reconstruction or higher compression [S1].
    d. Restore extracted regions first to improve comprehension and compression ratio [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import numpy as np

def time_feature_extraction(volumes, threshold, max_scale):
    """
    volumes: List of 3D numpy arrays [V_0, V_1, ..., V_{N-1}]
    threshold: Float, minimum coefficient magnitude to keep
    max_scale: Int, maximum wavelet scale
    Returns: List of Lipschitz exponents for each significant time-evolving component
    """
    n_volumes = len(volumes)
    # Step 1: Pre-processing - Compress each volume separately
    sparse_coefficients = [] # List of lists, one per time step
    for t in range(n_volumes):
        # Compute 3D wavelet transform (implementation depends on specific wavelet library)
        # For the purpose of this method description, we assume a function wavelet_3d exists
        coeffs = wavelet_3d_transform(volumes[t], max_scale)
        
        # Filter coefficients based on threshold
        sparse_coeffs = []
        for scale, pos, value in coeffs:
            if abs(value) > threshold:
                sparse_coeffs.append({'scale': scale, 'pos': pos, 'value': value})
        sparse_coefficients.append(sparse_coeffs)

    # Step 2 & 3: Reorganization and Wavelet Expansion
    # We group coefficients by (scale, position) across time
    components = {} # Key: (scale, x, y, z), Value: list of values over time
    
    for t in range(n_volumes):
        for coeff in sparse_coefficients[t]:
            key = (coeff['scale'], *coeff['pos'])
            if key not in components:
                components[key] = []
            components[key].append(coeff['value'])

    lipschitz_results = []

    # Step 4: Singularity Detection
    for key, time_series in components.items():
        # Ensure we have enough data points
        if len(time_series) < 2:
            continue
            
        # Compute 1D wavelet transform along the time axis for this component
        # Returns coefficients for each scale s and time position x
        # Wf(s, x) represents the wavelet coefficient at scale s and time x
        time_wavelet_coeffs = wavelet_1d_transform(time_series, max_scale)
        
        # For each time position x in the 1D signal, determine Lipschitz exponent
        for x in range(len(time_series)):
            # Collect coefficients across scales for this time position
            scale_data = []
            for s in range(1, max_scale + 1):
                # Access coefficient at scale s and position x
                # Note: Implementation detail on how wavelet_1d_transform returns data
                val = time_wavelet_coeffs[s][x]
                if abs(val) > 1e-10: # Avoid log(0)
                    scale_data.append((s, val))
            
            if len(scale_data) < 2:
                continue
                
            # Minimize energy function to find alpha
            # E(K, alpha) = sum( (log(|Wf|) - log(K) - alpha*log(s))^2 )
            alpha = minimize_lipschitz_exponent(scale_data)
            
            if alpha is not None:
                lipschitz_results.append({
                    'scale': key[0],
                    'position': key[1:],
                    'time_position': x,
                    'lipschitz_exponent': alpha
                })

    return lipschitz_results

def minimize_lipschitz_exponent(scale_data):
    """
    scale_data: List of tuples (s, Wf_value)
    Returns: alpha (Lipschitz exponent)
    """
    # Initialize parameters randomly as per source
    K = 1.0
    alpha = 0.5
    learning_rate = 0.01
    iterations = 100
    
    # Steepest descent
    for _ in range(iterations):
        grad_K = 0.0
        grad_alpha = 0.0
        
        for s, wf_val in scale_data:
            log_wf = math.log(abs(wf_val))
            log_s = math.log(s)
            
            # Residual: log(|Wf|) - log(K) - alpha * log(s)
            residual = log_wf - math.log(K) - alpha * log_s
            
            # Derivative of residual w.r.t K: -1/K
            # Derivative of residual w.r.t alpha: -log(s)
            # Derivative of E (sum of residuals^2) w.r.t K: 2 * residual * (-1/K)
            # Derivative of E w.r.t alpha: 2 * residual * (-log(s))
            
            grad_K += 2 * residual * (-1.0 / K)
            grad_alpha += 2 * residual * (-log_s)
            
        # Update parameters
        K -= learning_rate * grad_K
        alpha -= learning_rate * grad_alpha
        
        # Ensure K remains positive
        if K <= 0:
            K = 1e-10
            
    return alpha
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the 3D wavelet transforms of each volume ($O(N \cdot D^3 \log D)$) and the 1D wavelet transforms of the time-evolution signals. The minimization step is iterative and depends on the number of scales and the convergence rate of the steepest descent algorithm.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduced compared to storing the full sequence because each volume is compressed into a sparse multiresolution representation independently [S1]. Memory is saved by not storing coefficients that remain constant or are below threshold [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Allows for parallel processing of volumes (no inter-volume dependency in pre-processing) [S1]. Improves compression ratio and comprehension by prioritizing high-variation regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful selection of thresholds and scales. The steepest descent minimization may be sensitive to initialization [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of compression domain rendering of time-resolved volume data [S1]. It builds upon the theory of wavelet transforms and singularity detection, specifically using the asymptotic decay of wavelet coefficients to determine Lipschitz exponents [S1]. The approach was developed to address the need for extracting and enhancing special features from time series volume data, rather than just processing the entire sequence [S1].
