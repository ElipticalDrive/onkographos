# Cardinal B-spline wavelets

Also called: B-spline Wavelet Construction, B-spline wavelets, B-wavelet, Chui wavelets, Unser wavelets

## Overview
Cardinal B-spline wavelets are a class of semi-orthogonal wavelet bases used in computer graphics and volume rendering for their strict local support, smooth shape, symmetry, and fast decay in the frequency domain [S1, unofficial]. They solve the problem of representing multiresolution data with compactly supported basis functions that allow for linear-time transform algorithms, distinguishing them from general bi-orthogonal wavelets where duals may lack compact support [S1, unofficial]. Unlike orthogonal wavelets, they are semi-orthogonal, enabling the construction of efficient decomposition and reconstruction schemes [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family is built upon the mathematical properties of cardinal B-splines and their derived wavelets. All methods in this family share the following core mechanisms:

**Cardinal B-spline Definition**
The basis functions are cardinal B-splines of order $j$, which are piecewise-polynomial spline functions with equally spaced simple knots [S1, unofficial]. They are defined via a recurrence relation starting from the Haar scaling function (order 1) [S1, unofficial]:

$$
N^1(x) = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } 0 \leq x \leq 1 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

For $j \geq 2$, the recurrence is:

$$
N^j(x) = (N^{j-1} * N^j)(x) = \int_0^1 N^{j-1}(x-t) dt = \frac{x}{j-1} N^{j-1}(x) + \frac{j-x}{j-1} N^{j-1}(x-1)
$$

where $*$ denotes convolution [S1, unofficial]. The support of $N^j(x)$ is bounded by $[0, j]$ [S1, unofficial].

**B-spline Wavelet Construction**
A B-wavelet of order $j$, denoted $\psi^{[j]}(x)$, spans the orthogonal complement between two approximation spaces of scaling functions $\phi^{[j]}$ of different resolutions [S1, unofficial]. It is constructed as a linear combination of scaled scaling functions:

$$
\psi^{[j]}(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{3j-2} q_{j,k} \phi^{[j]}(2x-k)
$$

The coefficients $q_{j,k}$ are given by:

$$
q_{j,k} = \frac{(-1)^k}{2^{j-1}} \sum_{l=0}^j \binom{j}{l} \phi^{[2j]}(k+1-l)
$$

The support of $\psi^{[j]}(x)$ is bounded by $[0, 2j-1]$ [S1, unofficial].

**Properties**
*   **Symmetry:** The wavelets are symmetric for even $j$ and anti-symmetric for odd $j$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Vanishing Moments:** The number of vanishing moments is equal to $j-1$, which makes them attractive for compression applications [S1, unofficial].
*   **Semi-orthogonality:** The bases are semi-orthogonal, meaning they generate an orthogonal Multi-Resolution Analysis (MRA) [S1, unofficial]. This allows for the construction of dual basis functions with compact support (or fast exponential decay), enabling linear time complexity transform algorithms, unlike general bi-orthogonal wavelets where duals may not be compactly supported [S1, unofficial]. The dual functions are typically used for decomposition, while the primal functions are used for reconstruction [S1, unofficial].

### Endpoint-Interpolating B-spline MRA
**Definition**
A multi-resolution analysis on the bounded interval $L^2[0,1]$ using B-splines with multiple knots at the endpoints to handle boundaries without artificial discontinuities [S1, unofficial]. This approach avoids the severe errors introduced by zero-padding or periodization, which can create artificial singularities that wavelets detect as data features [S1, unofficial].

**Construction**
1.  **Knot Sequence:** Define a knot sequence $t_m^j$ on $[0,1]$ with multiple knots at the boundaries:
    $$
    t_m^{-j+1} = t_m^{-j+2} = \dots = t_m^0 = 0
    $$
    $$
    t_m^k = k 2^{-m}, \quad k = 1, \dots, 2^m - 1
    $$
    $$
    t_m^{2^m} = t_m^{2^m+1} = \dots = t_m^{2^m+j-1} = 1
    $$
2.  **Scaling Functions:** Define the scaling functions $B_{mp}^j(x)$ as:
    $$
    B_{mp}^j(x) = N^j(2^m x - p), \quad p = 0, \dots, 2^m - j
    $$
    where $N^j$ is the cardinal B-spline of order $j$ [S1, unofficial]. For indices $p = -j+1, \dots, -1$, $B_{mp}^j$ contains a multiple knot at 0, and for $p = 2^m-j+1, \dots, 2^m-1$, it contains a multiple knot at 1 [S1, unofficial]. These functions span the approximation space $V_m$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameter**
*   $j$: The order of the B-spline (e.g., cubic implies $j=4$) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Cardinal B-spline wavelets were developed independently by Chui and Unser [S1, unofficial]. They are widely used in computer graphics and modelling due to their desirable properties for surface and volume representation [S1, unofficial]. The theory of semi-orthogonal wavelets allows for efficient transform schemes with linear complexity, making them suitable for compression and multiresolution analysis in rendering pipelines [S1, unofficial].
