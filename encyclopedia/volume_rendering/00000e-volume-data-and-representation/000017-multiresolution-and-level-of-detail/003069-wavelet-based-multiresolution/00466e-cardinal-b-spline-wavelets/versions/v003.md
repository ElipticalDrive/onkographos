# Cardinal B-spline wavelets

Also called: B-spline Wavelet Construction, B-spline wavelets, B-wavelet, Chui wavelets, Unser wavelets

## Overview
Cardinal B-spline wavelets are a class of semi-orthogonal wavelet bases used in computer graphics and volume rendering for their strict local support, smooth shape, symmetry, and fast decay in the frequency domain [S1, unofficial]. They differ from orthogonal wavelets by allowing compactly supported dual basis functions, which enables linear-time transform algorithms, and from general bi-orthogonal wavelets by providing a specific, easily constructible semi-orthogonal structure [S1, unofficial]. This family is distinguished by its reliance on B-spline scaling functions to span approximation spaces, enabling efficient multiresolution analysis of volumetric data [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of generating a hierarchy of data representations at different spatial resolutions . This hierarchy is typically constructed using one of two approaches:
1.  **Spatial Hierarchies:** Structures like Octrees or Bricks that partition the volume domain into sub-regions of varying sizes .
2.  **Frequency-Domain Transforms:** Methods like Wavelets that decompose data into coefficients representing different spatial frequencies .

Common mathematical and structural operations include:
*   **Scaling Factors:** Defining a scale factor $s$ such that the new resolution $N_{new}$ relates to the previous resolution $N_{prev}$ by $N_{new} = s \times N_{prev}$ or $N_{new} / N_{prev} = s$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpolation and Filtering:** Using interpolation kernels (e.g., trilinear) or filtering to estimate values at new grid points or to generate coarser levels from finer ones [S1, unofficial].
*   **Selection and Culling:** Dynamically choosing which resolution level to render based on spatial hierarchy (distant or less important regions) and hardware constraints [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [B-spline Wavelet Basis Construction](16466-b-spline-wavelet-basis-construction.md)
This method constructs the semi-orthogonal B-spline wavelet basis of order $j$ used for multiresolution volume data representation. It defines the scaling functions and wavelet functions in both spatial and frequency domains, enabling efficient decomposition and reconstruction of volumetric data. It is distinguished from orthogonal wavelets by its semi-orthogonality, which allows for compactly supported dual bases and linear-phase properties, making it suitable for applications like wavelet splatting where smoothness and vanishing moments are required.

### Endpoint-Interpolating B-spline MRA
A multi-resolution analysis on the bounded interval $L^2[0,1]$ using B-splines with multiple knots at the endpoints to handle boundaries without artificial discontinuities [S1, unofficial]. It defines a knot sequence $t_m^j$ on $[0,1]$ with multiple knots at 0 and 1: $t_m^{-j+1} \dots t_m^0 = 0$ and $t_m^{2^m} \dots t_m^{2^m+j-1} = 1$ [S1, unofficial]. The scaling functions are defined as $B_{mp}^j(x) = N^j(2^m x - p)$ for $p=0, \dots, 2^m-j$, where $N^j$ is the cardinal B-spline of order $j$ [S1, unofficial]. The parameter $j$ determines the order of the B-spline (e.g., cubic implies $j=4$) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Cardinal B-spline wavelets were developed independently by Chui and Unser [S1, unofficial]. They are widely used in computer graphics and modelling [S1, unofficial]. The cardinal B-splines of order $j$ are piecewise-polynomial spline functions with equally spaced simple knots [S1, unofficial]. They are derived from the Haar scaling function (order 1) by self-convolution [S1, unofficial]. The construction of the dual functions is discussed in detail in [13], noting that while the duals do not have compact support, they exhibit fast exponential decay [S1, unofficial]. The number of vanishing moments of B-wavelets is equal to $j-1$, which makes them an attractive choice for compression applications [S1, unofficial].
