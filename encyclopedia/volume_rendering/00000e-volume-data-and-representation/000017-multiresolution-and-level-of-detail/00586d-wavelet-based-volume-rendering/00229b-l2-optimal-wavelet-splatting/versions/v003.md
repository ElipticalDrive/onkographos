# L2-optimal wavelet splatting

Also called: FPS splatting, High-Quality Wavelet Splatting, Volume wavelet rendering, Wavelet Projections for Volume Rendering, Wavelet Splats, Wavelet Splatting, Wavelet-based splatting

## Overview
L2-optimal wavelet splatting is a volume rendering technique that projects volume data by accumulating scaled and translated wavelet basis functions (splats) onto a multiresolution grid [S1, unofficial][S2][S3, unofficial]. It solves the problem of rendering large volume datasets efficiently by performing computations in the wavelet domain without full decompression, while ensuring maximal image quality for a given resolution [S3, unofficial]. It is distinguished from standard wavelet splatting (Method 1) by using least-squares approximation on an adaptive grid, which provides a faster decay in computational complexity with respect to scale and higher quality at coarse resolutions [S2][S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Volume data $f(\mathbf{x})$ of size $M^3$ [S2].
- Viewing direction $\mathbf{s}$ (or spherical coordinates $\alpha, \beta$) [S1, unofficial][S2].
- Rendering screen size $N^2$ [S2].
- Wavelet decomposition level $M$ and scale level $j$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
- B-spline degree $n$ (typically $n=3$ for cubic) [S2].
- Base sampling step $h$ (typically $h=1$) [S2].

**Outputs:**
- Accumulated framebuffer image [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Data Structures:**
- **Wavelet Coefficients:** $d_{Mpqr}^{type}$ for wavelet coefficients at level $m$, position $p,q,r$, and type; $c_{Mpqr}$ for scaling coefficients at level $M$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Precomputed Kernel Table:** $\xi_{h;\theta}(\mathbf{s})$, a table storing the scalar product between the wavelet splat and the dual function for all possible shifts, calculated once per viewing direction [S2].
- **Splat Textures:** 2D textures representing the inverse FFT of Fourier slices for the wavelet basis functions [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Decompose Volume Data:**
    Decompose the volume data $f(\mathbf{x})$ into a linear combination of basis functions $\psi_k(\mathbf{x})$ (wavelets and scaling functions) with coefficients $c(k)$ [S2].
    $$ f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_k c(k)\psi_k(\mathbf{x}) $$
    [S2]

2.  **Compute Wavelet Splats via Fourier Projection Slicing (FPS):**
    The projection of the volume onto a plane $P$ perpendicular to view direction $\mathbf{s}$ is computed using the Fourier slice theorem. The 2D Fourier transform of the projection equals a plane slicing the 3D Fourier transform of the volume parallel to $P$ and intersecting the origin [S1, S3, unofficial].
    
    For B-spline wavelets, the Fourier transform of the basis function is known in closed form. For a B-spline of degree $n$, the Fourier transform is:
    $$ \hat{\beta}_n(\omega) = \left( \frac{\sin(\omega/2)}{\omega/2} \right)^{n+1} $$
    [S2]
    
    The 3D Fourier transform of the volume basis function is the product of the 1D transforms. The splat in the frequency domain is obtained by restricting this 3D transform to the 2D plane defined by the view direction [S1, unofficial][S2].
    
    Apply an inverse FFT to these 2D Fourier slices to obtain the spatial domain wavelet splat textures $\chi_{\theta;j}(\mathbf{y})$ [S1, S3, unofficial].

3.  **Approximate Splats on Multiresolution Grid (L2-Optimal Projection):**
    For a fixed viewing direction $\theta$ and scale $j$, approximate the continuous wavelet splat $\chi_{\theta;j}(\mathbf{y})$ onto a reconstruction grid with sampling step $h_j = 2^j h$ [S2].
    
    The grid coefficients $c_{h_j}(\mathbf{l})$ are calculated using least-squares approximation via the scalar product with the dual function $\tilde{\phi}(\mathbf{y})$ [S2][S3, unofficial]:
    $$ c_{h_j}(\mathbf{l}) = \langle \chi_{\theta;j}(\mathbf{y}), \tilde{\phi}(\mathbf{y}/h_j \mathbf{l}) \rangle h_j $$
    [S2]
    
    To make this efficient, the kernel $\xi_{h;\theta}(\mathbf{s})$ is pre-calculated by convolving the wavelet splat with all possible shifts of the grid basis function [S3, unofficial]. The coefficients are then retrieved via a single table lookup [S2].

4.  **Accumulate into Framebuffer:**
    Map the screen position of the splat texture, weight it by the corresponding wavelet coefficient $c(k)$, and accumulate it into the framebuffer [S1, unofficial].
    
    The intensity at infinity for a view direction $\mathbf{s}$ and position $\mathbf{x}$ is computed as:
    $$ I(\infty, \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{s}) = \sum_m \sum_{p,q,r} d_{Mpqr}^{type} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi_{Mpqr}^{3, type}(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{s}t) dt + \sum_{p,q,r} c_{Mpqr} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi_{Mpqr}^3(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{s}t) dt $$
    [S1, unofficial]
    
    Since basis functions of different iteration levels $m$ differ by dilation, only eight different splats of depth $M$ need to be calculated. All other footprints are derived by subsampling (mipmap-like) [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Interpolate to Full Resolution:**
    Interpolate the projected data from the dual space grid to full-screen resolution $N^2$ in cardinal space using three steps:
    a. Change from dual space to spline space [S2].
    b. Upsample to full resolution using spline interpolation filters [S2].
    c. Change from spline space to cardinal space for visualization [S2].
    [S2][S3, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def l2_optimal_wavelet_splatting(volume_data, view_direction, screen_size, M, N, h=1, n=3):
    """
    Renders volume data using L2-optimal wavelet splatting.
    
    Args:
        volume_data: 3D array of size M^3
        view_direction: 3D vector or spherical coordinates (alpha, beta)
        screen_size: 2D integer (N, N)
        M: Volume resolution dimension
        N: Screen resolution dimension
        h: Base sampling step (default 1)
        n: B-spline degree (default 3)
        
    Returns:
        framebuffer: 2D array of size N x N
    """
    # 1. Decompose volume into wavelet coefficients
    # This step typically involves a 3D wavelet transform
    wavelet_coeffs = decompose_wavelet(volume_data, M)
    
    # 2. Precompute wavelet splats in frequency domain
    # For each wavelet type and decomposition level up to M
    # Only 8 unique splats of depth M are needed; others are subsampled
    splat_frequencies = {}
    for level in range(M):
        for wavelet_type in ['x', 'y', 'z', 'xy', 'xz', 'yz', 'xyz', 'scaling']:
            # Get 3D Fourier transform of the basis function
            basis_ft = get_basis_fourier_transform(level, wavelet_type, n)
            
            # Apply Fourier Slice Theorem: slice the 3D FT along view_direction
            # The slice is a 2D plane in frequency space
            splat_2d_ft = apply_fourier_slice(basis_ft, view_direction)
            
            # Inverse FFT to get spatial splat texture
            splat_texture = inverse_fft2(splat_2d_ft)
            splat_frequencies[(level, wavelet_type)] = splat_texture

    # 3. For each scale j and wavelet coefficient, compute grid coefficients
    framebuffer = zeros(N, N)
    
    # Adaptive sampling step
    for j in range(M): # Scale levels
        h_j = 2**j * h
        
        # Precompute kernel for this view direction and scale if not cached
        # Kernel xi_h_theta(s) = <chi_theta_j(y), phi_tilde(y/h_j * l)>
        # This allows single table lookup for coefficients
        kernel_table = precompute_kernel(view_direction, h_j, n)
        
        # Iterate over wavelet coefficients at this scale
        for (p, q, r), coeff in get_coefficients_at_scale(wavelet_coeffs, j):
            
            # Retrieve grid coefficients via table lookup
            # The shift parameter s depends on the spatial position (p,q,r)
            grid_coeffs = lookup_kernel(kernel_table, get_shift(p, q, r, h_j))
            
            # Map grid coefficients to screen space and accumulate
            # This involves upsampling and interpolation to full resolution
            splat_image = interpolate_to_screen(grid_coeffs, h_j, N)
            
            # Weight by wavelet coefficient and accumulate
            framebuffer += coeff * splat_image

    return framebuffer
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Semiorthogonality:** For B-spline wavelets of order $j > 1$, the function system is semiorthogonal, requiring dual functions for decomposition. Implementations of linear time algorithms may be challenging because additional basis transforms might be required [S1, unofficial].
- **Full Resolution Performance:** At full resolution ($j=0$ or small $j$), the speed-up of Method 2 breaks down because the adaptive grid step $h_j$ becomes small, increasing computation. However, quality remains higher [S2].
- **Approximation Error:** The choice of sampling step $h$ and B-spline degree $n$ affects the approximation error. Cubic B-splines ($n=3$) perform significantly better than linear or Haar wavelets [S2].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The complexity of Method 2 (L2-optimal) decays with a factor of $8^j$ per scale $j$ at small scales, until it reaches a constant dependent on screen size $N^2$ [S2]. The complexity is approximately $Op2 \approx k_3 2^{3j} M^3 + k_7 N^2$ [S2]. In contrast, Method 1 (standard wavelet splatting) decays with only $2^j$ [S2].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for wavelet coefficients and precomputed splat textures. The number of unique splats is limited to eight per depth $M$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Method 2 is up to two orders of magnitude faster than Method 1 at scale $j \approx 4-5$ for large volumes ($M=512, 1024, 2048$) [S2]. At full resolution, Method 2 complexity is 50% higher than Method 1, but yields higher quality [S2].
- **Strengths:** Higher quality at coarse resolutions, full control over approximation error, efficient interactive browsing [S2][S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Higher complexity at full resolution compared to Method 1, requires precomputation of kernels and splats [S2].
- **When to use:** Interactive volume rendering where fast feedback at low resolution is needed, and high quality is required once a viewpoint is chosen [S2][S3, unofficial]. Suitable for medical imaging where physicians need high visualization quality [S3, unofficial].

## Background
L2-optimal wavelet splatting was developed by Stefan Horbelt, Michael Unser, and Martin Vetterli at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology (EPFL) [S2][S3, unofficial]. It extends Gross's method of volume wavelet rendering by computing splats via an orthogonal projection operator using least-squares approximation [S2][S3, unofficial]. The method was published in 1999 [S2] and 1998 [S3, unofficial]. It is used in wavelet-based volume rendering, specifically compared against Method 1 in performance analysis [S2].
