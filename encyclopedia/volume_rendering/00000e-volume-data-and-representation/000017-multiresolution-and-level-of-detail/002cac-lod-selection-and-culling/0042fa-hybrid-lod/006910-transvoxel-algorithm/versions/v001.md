# Transvoxel Algorithm

Also called: Transvoxel, Transition Cell Method

## Overview
The Transvoxel Algorithm is a method for seamlessly stitching together neighboring triangle meshes generated from voxel data at differing resolutions, enabling Level of Detail (LOD) rendering for large voxel-based datasets such as volumetric terrain [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from standard LOD techniques by introducing "transvoxels"—specialized transition cells that bridge the geometric gap between adjacent resolution levels, preventing holes and surface discontinuities that typically occur when marching cubes is applied to voxels of varying sizes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The algorithm operates on voxel data organized into blocks, where each block maintains a consistent logical size but adapts its physical resolution based on its LOD level. The core challenge is that adjacent blocks at different LODs have different voxel counts along shared boundaries, leading to mismatched vertex positions and gaps. The method resolves this through three main components: Modified Marching Cubes, Surface Shifting, and Transvoxel generation.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A volumetric dataset defined by scalar field values (isovalues) at voxel corners. The data is structured into blocks of $16 \times 16 \times 16$ logical voxels. Each block has an associated LOD level $L$, where higher $L$ implies lower resolution.
*   **Output:** A manifold triangle mesh representing the isosurface, with seamless transitions between blocks of different LODs.

### Data Structures
1.  **Voxel Block:** A 3D array of scalar values. The physical dimensions of the block are fixed, but the number of sampling points along each axis depends on the LOD.
2.  **Transvoxel Cell:** A special cell used at LOD boundaries. Unlike a standard voxel cell which has 8 corners, a transvoxel has 13 points:
    *   9 points on the face adjacent to the higher-resolution LOD.
    *   4 points on the face adjacent to the lower-resolution LOD.
    *   The 4 points on the lower-resolution face share the same isovalues as the 4 corners of the higher-resolution face [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Lookup Tables:** Precomputed tables for Modified Marching Cubes and Transvoxel configurations. There are 512 possible configurations for a transvoxel [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **LOD Factor:** Each LOD level reduces the resolution of the volume by a factor of 2 in each dimension [S1, unofficial].
*   **Block Size:** $16 \times 16 \times 16$ logical voxels per block [S1, unofficial].
*   **Transvoxel Space Allocation:** The parameter defining how much of the original voxel space the transvoxel occupies. Eric Lengyel recommends a value of $0.5$ to ensure normal shading and prevent harsh elevation changes [S1, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Block Organization:** Divide the voxel volume into $16 \times 16 \times 16$ blocks. Assign an LOD level to each block based on distance from the viewer or other criteria. Adjacent blocks may differ by at most one LOD level [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Surface Shifting (Vertex Position Correction):**
    *   When generating vertices for a block at a lower resolution, the vertex positions along edges shared with a higher-resolution neighbor must be consistent.
    *   For any vertex on the boundary of a lower-LOD block, do not use the interpolated position from the lower-resolution data alone.
    *   Recursively sample the isovalues along that edge at the higher resolution to determine the precise vertex location [S1, unofficial]. This prevents the terrain from "shifting" vertically when LOD changes, which would otherwise cause objects resting on the terrain to appear to jump [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Transvoxel Identification:**
    *   Identify voxels that lie on the boundary between a higher-resolution block and a lower-resolution block.
    *   Replace any voxel along the lower-resolution boundary with transvoxels. The number of transvoxels required is $1 + N$, where $N$ is the number of sides of the voxel surrounded by higher-resolution LOD [S1, unofficial].
    *   The original voxel space is split: the original voxel retains half its space in the dimension of the boundary, and the remaining half is filled by the transvoxel(s) [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Mesh Generation (Modified Marching Cubes):**
    *   For standard voxels (not at LOD boundaries), use Modified Marching Cubes with Eric Lengyel’s specific lookup tables to fix topological inconsistencies and prevent holes [S1, unofficial].
    *   For transvoxels, use the Transvoxel lookup tables. The process is identical to Modified Marching Cubes: determine the configuration based on the 13 point isovalues, and retrieve the precomputed triangle vertices and connectivity [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Concavity Fixing (Post-Processing):**
    *   After mesh generation, check for concavities at the transition boundaries.
    *   Apply a linear transformation to the vertices at the transition boundary to smooth out concavities [S1, unofficial]. Note: Implementation of this specific fix may vary or be omitted if artifacts are acceptable [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def generate_transvoxel_mesh(blocks):
    """
    blocks: List of Block objects. Each block has:
        - lod_level: int
        - voxels: 3D array of scalar values (size depends on lod_level)
        - position: 3D vector (world space origin of the block)
        - size: 3D vector (physical dimensions of the block)
    """
    mesh = EmptyMesh()
    
    for block in blocks:
        # Determine neighbors to identify boundary voxels
        neighbors = get_adjacent_blocks(block, blocks)
        
        for x in range(block.width):
            for y in range(block.height):
                for z in range(block.depth):
                    voxel = block.voxels[x][y][z]
                    
                    # Check if this voxel is on an LOD boundary
                    is_boundary = is_on_lod_boundary(voxel, block, neighbors)
                    
                    if is_boundary:
                        # Generate transvoxel
                        transvoxel = create_transvoxel(voxel, block, neighbors)
                        # Use transvoxel lookup tables (512 configs)
                        triangles = get_transvoxel_triangles(transvoxel)
                        mesh.add_triangles(triangles)
                    else:
                        # Generate standard voxel using Modified Marching Cubes
                        # Apply Surface Shifting for boundary vertices
                        vertex_positions = get_marching_cubes_vertices(voxel, block)
                        apply_surface_shifting(vertex_positions, block, neighbors)
                        triangles = get_modified_marching_cubes_triangles(voxel, vertex_positions)
                        mesh.add_triangles(triangles)
                        
    # Optional: Fix concavities at transitions
    mesh.apply_concavity_fix()
    return mesh

def create_transvoxel(voxel, block, neighbors):
    """
    Creates a transvoxel structure with 13 points.
    9 points on high-res face, 4 points on low-res face.
    The 4 low-res points share isovalues with the 4 high-res corners.
    """
    # Determine how many sides are surrounded by higher LOD
    num_high_res_sides = count_high_res_neighbors(voxel, neighbors)
    
    # Create 13 points
    # Points 0-8: High-res face (9 points)
    # Points 9-12: Low-res face (4 points)
    # Isovalues for points 9-12 are copied from the 4 corners of the high-res face
    transvoxel = Transvoxel()
    transvoxel.isovalues = compute_isovalues(voxel, block, neighbors)
    transvoxel.geometry = compute_transvoxel_geometry(voxel, block, num_high_res_sides)
    return transvoxel

def apply_surface_shifting(vertex_positions, block, neighbors):
    """
    For vertices on the boundary of a lower-LOD block,
    recurse on the edge to sample isovalues at higher resolution
    to precisely choose the triangle vertex location.
    """
    for vertex in vertex_positions:
        if vertex.is_on_boundary:
            # Find the corresponding edge in the higher-resolution neighbor
            high_res_edge = find_high_res_edge(vertex, block, neighbors)
            # Sample isovalues along the high-res edge
            precise_isovalue = sample_high_res_isovalue(high_res_edge)
            # Recalculate vertex position based on precise_isovalue
            vertex.position = interpolate_position(vertex.edge, precise_isovalue)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Concavities:** Concavities can appear at the transition boundary where transvoxels are created. A linear transformation is recommended to fix this, but it may be difficult to implement correctly [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Surface shifting is critical to prevent terrain shift artifacts. Without it, objects on the terrain may appear to jump when the viewer moves across LOD boundaries [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The algorithm assumes adjacent blocks differ by at most one LOD level. Larger LOD jumps are not supported by the standard transvoxel method [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels. Each voxel is processed once. The lookup table access is $O(1)$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for voxel data. Additional memory for transvoxel structures is proportional to the number of boundary voxels.
*   **Measured Performance:** Real-time rendering of large voxel-based terrain with multiple LOD levels and thousands of trees has been demonstrated [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Seamless LOD transitions without holes; supports destructible terrain; real-time performance.
*   **Limitations:** Concavity artifacts may require additional post-processing; limited to LOD differences of 1; requires precomputed lookup tables.
*   **When to use:** Volumetric terrain rendering where LOD is essential for performance and seamless transitions are required.
*   **When not to use:** Non-voxel data; cases where LOD jumps are large; when concavity artifacts cannot be tolerated and post-processing is not feasible.

## Background
The Transvoxel Algorithm was invented by Eric Lengyel in 2009 and published in 2010 [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to enable Level of Detail rendering for large voxel-based datasets, such as volumetric terrain in video games. The algorithm is implemented in Lengyel's C4 Engine, which demonstrates real-time rendering of large voxel terrains with multiple LOD levels [S1, unofficial].
