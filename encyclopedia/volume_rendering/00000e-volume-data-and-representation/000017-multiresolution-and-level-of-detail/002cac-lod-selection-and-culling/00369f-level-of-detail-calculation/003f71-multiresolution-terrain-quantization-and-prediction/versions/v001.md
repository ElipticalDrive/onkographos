# Multiresolution Terrain Quantization and Prediction

Also called: Patch vertex classification, Quantization shift assignment

## Overview
A method for assigning quantization precision to terrain vertices in a multiresolution hierarchy to minimize bitstream size while maintaining visual fidelity, using specific shift values for different vertex types (Corner, Leg, Hypotenuse, Interior) [S1]. It sets itself apart by deriving quantization shifts from the finest-resolution children to ensure coarsening errors are minimized, and by unifying precision during prediction to prevent boundary artifacts [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A multiresolution terrain mesh defined by patches; a target quantization error $\epsilon_q$; the height difference $\Delta z$ between adjacent vertices.
**Outputs:** A set of quantization shift values $k$ for each vertex in a patch, and a compressed bitstream of residuals.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Vertex Classification:** Each vertex on non-finest levels is classified as:
    *   **Corner (C):** Vertices at the corners of a patch.
    *   **Leg (L):** Vertices on the patch legs (edges).
    *   **Hypotenuse (H):** Vertices on the hypotenuse of the triangular patch.
    *   **Interior (I):** Remaining vertices inside the patch.
*   **Shift Values:** Each patch requires three shift values for decoding:
    *   $k_{L1}, k_{L2}$: Shifts for the two legs.
    *   $k_{HI}$: Shift for the union of Hypotenuse and Interior vertices.
    *   Only $k_{HI}$ is stored explicitly with the patch. $k_{L1}$ and $k_{L2}$ are derived from the $k_{HI}$ values of the patch's children.

**Steps:**

1.  **Classify Vertices:**
    For each patch on non-finest levels, classify vertices into C, L, H, and I [S1].
    *   Corners (C) are left unquantized (full precision, shift $k=0$) [S1].
    *   Boundary vertices are further divided into C, L, and H [S1].

2.  **Determine Quantization Shifts:**
    Calculate the shift $k$ for a vertex based on the target error $\epsilon_q$ and the local height variation $\Delta z$ [S1]:
    $$
    k = \begin{cases} [\log_2 \frac{\epsilon_q}{\Delta z}] + 1 & \text{if } \epsilon_q \geq \Delta z \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \quad (4)
    $$
    *   **Leg (L) Vertices:** An L vertex in a patch may be an L vertex in an adjacent patch on the same level, or an H vertex in a patch one level below. The precision is dictated by the finer resolution children to ensure coarsening errors are no larger [S1]. Thus, the shifts for L vertices are governed by other patches (specifically, they are the $k_{HI}$ shifts of the children) [S1].
    *   **Hypotenuse (H) and Interior (I) Vertices:** Only the two cousin patches forming a diamond must agree on quantization for H vertices. Since they have the same coarsening error, the same shift $k_{HI}$ is used for the union of their H and I vertices [S1].

3.  **Prediction Precision Handling:**
    To avoid unwanted low-order bits on patch boundaries, all vertices involved in a prediction must be reduced to the precision of the vertex being predicted [S1].
    *   Shift out low-order bits where necessary.
    *   Perform the prediction at unified precision.
    *   Add the low-precision residual.
    *   Left shift the result as needed [S1].
    *   **Optimization:** This step is necessary only for Corner (C) and Leg (L) vertices in the first triangle of a patch due to regular connectivity and traversal order from diamond interior to boundaries (low to high precision) [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   $\epsilon_q$: Target quantization error. Typical values depend on the application's visual fidelity requirements [S1].
*   $\Delta z$: Local height difference. Varies per vertex/patch [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision Artifacts:** If prediction does not unify precision, unwanted low-order bits appear on patch boundaries [S1].
*   **Boundary Agreement:** L vertices must agree on quantization with adjacent patches (same level or finer level below). Failure to do so causes discontinuities [S1].
*   **Finest Level:** All vertices on the finest level must be assigned a zero shift for lossless coding [S1].
