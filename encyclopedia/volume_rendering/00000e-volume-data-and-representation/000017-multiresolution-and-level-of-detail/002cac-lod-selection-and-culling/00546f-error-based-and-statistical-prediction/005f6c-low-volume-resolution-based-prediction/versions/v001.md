# Low Volume Resolution-based Prediction

Also called: LVRP

## Overview
Low Volume Resolution-based Prediction (LVRP) estimates high-resolution rendering time by rendering the volume at a significantly lower resolution and applying a linear transformation to the measured time [S1, unofficial]. It exploits the correlation between rendering times at different volume resolutions to optimize rendering workloads [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Histogram Spectra for LOD Selection](11687-histogram-spectra-for-lod-selection.md), which predicts sampling error based on data statistics, LVRP predicts computational cost based on empirical timing measurements at a reduced resolution.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A volume dataset, rendering parameters (transfer function, camera, etc.), and a pre-computed linear model (scaling coefficient and bias).
*   **Output:** Predicted rendering time for the high-resolution volume.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Linear Model:** A pair of floating-point values $(m, c)$ representing the slope and intercept of the linear regression fit between low-resolution time ($t_{low}$) and high-resolution time ($t_{high}$).

**Steps:**
1.  **Downsample:** Reduce the volume resolution to a fixed low resolution. The standard implementation uses a resolution of $64^3$ voxels [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Render:** Render the downsampled volume using the identical rendering parameters intended for the high-resolution prediction. Measure the actual rendering time $t_{low}$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Predict:** Apply the linear scaling coefficient $m$ and bias $c$ to the measured time to predict the high-resolution time $t_{high}$:
    $$ t_{high} = m \cdot t_{low} + c $$
    The coefficients $m$ and $c$ are determined offline by rendering the same scenes at both low and high resolutions with identical parameters and fitting a linear regression model to the time relationship [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Low Resolution:** $64^3$ voxels [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Loss of Fine-Scale Features:** Volume downsampling discards fine-scale structural features that significantly influence raycasting performance. This leads to limited predictive capability, particularly for complex datasets such as cosmology simulations where structural complexity drives performance variability [S1, unofficial].
*   **Complex Timing Behavior:** For datasets with complex timing behavior (e.g., MAESTROeX), the linear correlation may be weaker, resulting in higher Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) compared to simpler datasets [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [ENTIRE](17899-entire.md)
A deep learning-based approach that decouples volume feature extraction from time prediction to handle multiple factors including volume data characteristics, image resolution, camera configuration, and transfer function settings.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The prediction step is $O(1)$ after the initial low-resolution render. The low-resolution render is $O(N_{low})$, where $N_{low}$ is the number of voxels at the low resolution.
*   **Accuracy:** Performance is significantly worse than learning-based methods like ENTIRE for complex datasets. The method fails to capture substantial variability across individual frames, which is common in interactive exploration scenarios [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; requires no training data or complex feature extraction.
*   **Limitations:** Limited predictive capability for complex datasets due to the loss of fine-scale structural features during downsampling [S1, unofficial]. Not suitable for scenarios with high frame-to-frame variability [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Simple optimization tasks where high accuracy is not critical and dataset complexity is low.
*   **When not to use:** Interactive exploration with complex datasets (e.g., cosmology simulations) or when high accuracy is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
LVRP was introduced in the context of optimizing rendering workloads for in situ visualization, specifically within the framework of the ENTIRE project [S1, unofficial]. It serves as a baseline method for comparing against more advanced learning-based prediction models [S1, unofficial].
