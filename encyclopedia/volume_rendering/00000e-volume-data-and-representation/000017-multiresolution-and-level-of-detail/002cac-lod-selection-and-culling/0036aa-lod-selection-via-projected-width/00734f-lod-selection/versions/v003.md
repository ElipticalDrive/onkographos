# LOD Selection

Also called: Gabor Field Level of Detail (LOD), Gabor Fields LOD Culling, Gabor Mixture Prefiltering, Seamless LOD, Spectral LOD

## Overview
This method provides seamless Level-of-Detail (LOD) for volume rendering by prefiltering a mixture of Gabor kernels based on camera parameters and depth [S1]. It selects the minimal set of primitives matching a desired frequency limit, achieved by masking out or culling Gabor kernels that would exceed the rendering bandwidth [S1]. Unlike analytical filtering of Gaussians, which incurs high performance and memory costs, or pruning-based bandlimiting, which degrades quality by misrepresenting high frequencies, Gabor Fields support seamless LOD without these drawbacks [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A mixture of Gabor kernels, each defined by a center $\mu$, scale $s$, orientation (quaternion $q$ or vector $\vec{u}$), frequency $\omega$, and weight $\alpha$ [S1].
*   Camera parameters: field of view $\phi$, resolution $R$ [S1].
*   Depth $d$ of the primitive or bin [S1].
*   Motion parameters: velocity $\vec{v}$ and time $t$ (if applicable) [S1].
*   A fixed attenuation threshold $\tau$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   A subset of Gabor primitives (or bins) that contribute to the final image within the frequency limit [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gabor Kernel:** Contains parameters $\mu, s, q, \omega, \alpha$ [S1].
*   **Bin:** A grouping of Gabor primitives sharing similar orientations and frequencies. Each bin stores the mean frequency $\bar{f}$ and mean orientation $\bar{u}$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Grouping:** Group the Gabor primitives into bins containing different orientations and frequencies [S1].
2.  **Frequency Limit Calculation:** Calculate the angular Nyquist frequency in world units, $f_{\text{limit}}(d)$, for the given depth $d$:
    $$ W_{\text{world}}(d) = 2 d \tan(\phi / 2) $$
    $$ f_{\text{limit}}(d) = \frac{\pi R}{W_{\text{world}}(d)} $$
    where $W_{\text{world}}(d)$ is the visible width at distance $d$ and $R$ is the resolution [S1].
3.  **Attenuation Calculation:** For each bin, calculate the attenuation based on the mean frequency and orientation [S1]. Given motion parameters $(\vec{v}, t)$, calculate the attenuation for the mean frequency and orientation in each bin [S1]. The attenuation for a Gabor kernel with modulation $\vec{\omega}$ is derived as:
    $$ \text{attenuation} = \left| \text{sinc}(m |\vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{d}|) \right| $$
    where $\vec{d}$ is the direction vector representing the view or motion direction, and $m$ is a scaling factor derived from the kernel width [S1]. As vectors become parallel, $|\vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{d}|$ approaches $\|\vec{\omega}\|$, causing destructive interference for small primitives [S1]. When perpendicular, primitives are not attenuated [S1].
4.  **Culling:** Cull bin $i$ if the calculated attenuation for its mean frequency and orientation falls below the fixed threshold $\tau$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def dot(a, b):
    """Compute the dot product of two vectors."""
    return sum(x * y for x, y in zip(a, b))

def sinc(x):
    """Normalized sinc function: sin(pi*x) / (pi*x)."""
    if x == 0:
        return 1.0
    return math.sin(math.pi * x) / (math.pi * x)

def cull_gabor_bins(bins, phi, R, depth, view_dir, m, threshold):
    """
    Cull Gabor bins based on projected width and attenuation.
    
    Args:
        bins: List of bin objects, each with mean_freq (omega) and mean_orientation (u).
        phi: Field of view angle in radians.
        R: Screen resolution.
        depth: Depth to the primitives.
        view_dir: Direction vector (view or motion direction).
        m: Scaling factor related to kernel width.
        threshold: Fixed attenuation threshold tau.
        
    Returns:
        List of bins that are not culled.
    """
    kept_bins = []
    
    for bin in bins:
        # Use mean frequency vector for the bin
        omega = bin.mean_freq
        
        # Calculate attenuation based on mean frequency and orientation [S1]
        attenuation = abs(sinc(m * abs(dot(omega, view_dir))))
        
        # Cull bin if attenuation falls below threshold [S1]
        if attenuation < threshold:
            continue
        kept_bins.append(bin)
            
    return kept_bins
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Fixed Threshold:** The attenuation value below which a bin is culled [S1]. This threshold is independent of the screen-space extent of the mixture; it depends only on camera parameters and depth [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Approximation:** Using peak frequency is an approximation because no closed-form solution exists for the projection of a general Gabor kernel to screen space [S1].
*   **Precision:** Ensure that the calculation of $f_{\text{limit}}(d)$ handles small depths correctly to avoid division by zero or numerical instability [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of bins. Grouping and culling are efficient operations [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it does not require storing additional mipmap levels or volumetric data for LOD [S1].
*   **Performance:** In a stress test with 176 Bunny assets (5,857,280 primitives), the effective count was reduced to 759,164 primitives (x7.7 smaller) using Laplacian decomposition filtering on demand [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Seamless LOD without quality degradation associated with pruning-based methods. No additional storage required compared to Gaussian primitives [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Approximation in frequency projection may lead to suboptimal culling in some cases [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** Gabor Fields were introduced in the paper "Gabor Fields: Orientation-Selective Level-of-Detail for Volume Rendering" [S1].
*   **History:** The method builds on the concept of Gabor noise and fields, leveraging their orientation-selective properties for efficient rendering and LOD [S1].
*   **Usage:** Used in volume rendering for seamless LOD, particularly in scenes with high-frequency details like clouds or complex geometries [S1].
