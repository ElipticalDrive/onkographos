# Haar Wavelet Compression

Also called: 3D Haar Wavelet Compression, 3D Haar Wavelet Compression with Random Access, Ihm and Park method, New Coder, Wavelet-Based 3D Volume Compression with Fast Random Access

## Overview
Haar Wavelet Compression is a lossy compression scheme for 3D volume data designed to achieve high compression ratios while supporting fast random access to individual voxels, enabling interactive visualization of large datasets [S1, unofficial][S2]. It differs from traditional wavelet coders (like SPIHT or zerotree-based methods) by using a block-based data structure with fixed-length codes and spatial coherence flags, avoiding variable-length entropy coding that hinders random access [S2]. It also differs from pure 3D wavelet methods by treating the volume as a sequence of 2D slices, applying temporal prediction to remove inter-slice correlation before performing 2D spatial wavelet decomposition, which reduces computational cost compared to full 3D transforms [S2].

## How it works
The method partitions the volume into $16 \times 16 \times 16$ blocks, subdivides them into $4 \times 4 \times 4$ cells, applies temporal prediction and 2D Haar wavelet transforms, thresholds and quantizes coefficients, and encodes them using a flag-based structure optimized for direct memory access [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
- **Input**: A 3D volume dataset $V$ of size $W \times H \times D$, where voxel values are typically 12-bit integers (range $[0, 2^{12}-1]$) [S1, unofficial].
- **Output**: Compressed bitstream consisting of coefficient values and metadata allowing reconstruction of any voxel without decoding the entire volume [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Data Structures
The compressed data is organized into two main streams and one metadata chunk per block:
1. **`one-byte` array**: Stores quantized coefficients that fit in 1 byte (values $[0, 255]$) [S1, unofficial].
2. **`two-byte` array**: Stores quantized coefficients requiring 2 bytes [S1, unofficial].
3. **Cell Information Chunk**: A $4 \times 4 \times 4$ block of 1-bit flags and offset information [S1, unofficial]. Each bit corresponds to a "cell" (a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ subblock of the original $16 \times 16 \times 16$ block) [S1, unofficial]. If the bit is 1, the cell contains non-zero coefficients; if 0, all coefficients in that cell are zero [S1, unofficial]. Offsets point to the location of the cell's coefficients in the `one-byte` or `two-byte` arrays [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
- **Block Size**: $16 \times 16 \times 16$ voxels [S1, unofficial].
- **Subblock Size**: $4 \times 4 \times 4$ voxels (called "cells") [S1, unofficial].
- **Wavelet**: 2-tap Haar wavelet [S2].
- **Decomposition Levels**: 2 levels [S2].
- **Slice Prediction Distance ($F$)**: $F = 2^k$ for faster processing, where $k$ is chosen based on compression/quality trade-offs [S2].
- **Quantization Range**: Non-zero coefficients are quantized to $[0, 255]$ [S2].

### Steps
1. **Partitioning**: Divide the volume into $16 \times 16 \times 16$ blocks [S1, unofficial]. Within each block, identify $4 \times 4 \times 4$ subblocks (cells) [S1, unofficial].
2. **Temporal Prediction**: Divide the volume into 2D slices along the z-direction [S2]. Apply temporal prediction to remove correlation between slices using forward and backward R-slices (reference slices) to predict I-slices (intra-coded slices) [S2]. The distance between R-slices is $F = 2^k$ [S2].
3. **Spatial Wavelet Transform**: Apply a 2D Haar wavelet transform to each slice to handle spatial redundancy [S2]. The Haar wavelet is chosen for its fast reconstruction speed (2-tap filter) [S2]. Two levels of decomposition are applied [S2].
4. **Thresholding**: Set wavelet coefficients below a certain threshold to zero to increase sparsity [S1, unofficial][S2]. The threshold is determined per-slice to maintain a target Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) [S2]. Coefficients in each slice are sorted in ascending order of magnitude and zeroed until the desired PSNR is reached [S2]. PSNR is calculated using the global maximum of the entire volume [S2]. Global sorting is impractical for large volumes; per-slice sorting is used as a practical approximation [S2].
5. **Quantization**: Quantize the remaining non-zero coefficients to the interval $[0, 255]$ using a uniform scalar quantizer [S2]. Coefficients are grouped into blocks $G$ of at most 64 coefficients [S2]. For each block $G$, determine parameters $A$ and $B$, quantize each coefficient $C$ to a byte $x$, and store $A$ and $B$ as 16-bit integers [S2].
6. **Encoding**: Enumerate cells within each $16 \times 16 \times 16$ block [S1, unofficial]. If a cell contains any non-zero coefficients, tag it with a 1 in the Cell Information Chunk and store its coefficients in the `one-byte` or `two-byte` arrays based on size [S1, unofficial]. If a cell contains only zero coefficients, tag it with 0 [S1, unofficial]. Use flags and offsets in the Cell Information Chunk to map cell indices to addresses in the coefficient arrays [S1, unofficial].
7. **Reconstruction**: Apply the reverse of the decomposition [S1, unofficial]. Each $4 \times 4 \times 4$ subregion of the $16 \times 16 \times 16$ blocks is considered an octree [S1, unofficial]. The root node stores the average of all voxel values in the subregion [S1, unofficial]. The seven detail coefficients in level 1 are used with values from the root to reconstruct the eight voxel values of a corresponding $2 \times 2 \times 2$ subregion [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
- **Forward Haar Transform** (2x2x2 subregion to 8-band filter bank):
  $$ \text{clll} = \frac{c_1 + c_2 + c_3 + c_4 + c_5 + c_6 + c_7 + c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{cllh} = \frac{c_1 + c_2 + c_3 + c_4 - c_5 - c_6 - c_7 - c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{clhl} = \frac{c_1 + c_2 - c_3 - c_4 + c_5 + c_6 - c_7 - c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{clhh} = \frac{c_1 + c_2 - c_3 - c_4 - c_5 - c_6 + c_7 + c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{chll} = \frac{c_1 - c_2 + c_3 - c_4 + c_5 - c_6 + c_7 - c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{chlh} = \frac{c_1 - c_2 + c_3 - c_4 - c_5 + c_6 - c_7 + c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{chhl} = \frac{c_1 - c_2 - c_3 + c_4 + c_5 - c_6 - c_7 + c_8}{8} $$
  $$ \text{chhh} = \frac{c_1 - c_2 - c_3 + c_4 - c_5 + c_6 + c_7 - c_8}{8} $$
  where $c_i$ represents the eight coefficients in a 2x2x2 subregion and $\text{clll}$ represents their average [S1, unofficial].

- **Inverse Haar Transform** (reconstruction):
  $$ c_1 = \text{clll} + \text{cllh} + \text{clhl} + \text{clhh} + \text{chll} + \text{chlh} + \text{chhl} + \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_2 = \text{clll} + \text{cllh} + \text{clhl} + \text{clhh} - \text{chll} - \text{chlh} - \text{chhl} - \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_3 = \text{clll} + \text{cllh} - \text{clhl} - \text{clhh} + \text{chll} + \text{chlh} - \text{chhl} - \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_4 = \text{clll} + \text{cllh} - \text{clhl} - \text{clhh} - \text{chll} - \text{chlh} + \text{chhl} + \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_5 = \text{clll} - \text{cllh} + \text{clhl} - \text{clhh} + \text{chll} - \text{chlh} + \text{chhl} - \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_6 = \text{clll} - \text{cllh} + \text{clhl} - \text{clhh} - \text{chll} + \text{chlh} - \text{chhl} + \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_7 = \text{clll} - \text{cllh} - \text{clhl} + \text{clhh} + \text{chll} - \text{chlh} - \text{chhl} + \text{chhh} $$
  $$ c_8 = \text{clll} - \text{cllh} - \text{clhl} + \text{clhh} - \text{chll} + \text{chlh} + \text{chhl} - \text{chhh} $$
  [S1, unofficial]

- **PSNR**:
  $$ \text{PSNR} = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{\max(x_i)^2}{\text{MSE}} \right) $$
  where $x_i$ is the original image value [S2].

- **Quantization**:
  $$ A = \max_{c \in G}(c), \quad B = \min_{c \in G}(c) $$
  $$ x = \left\lfloor 255 \times \frac{C - B}{A - B} + 0.5 \right\rfloor $$
  $$ \tilde{C} = \left\lfloor \frac{A - B}{255} \times x + 0.5 \right\rfloor + B $$
  where $A$ and $B$ are 16-bit integer parameters, $x$ is the quantization value (byte), $C$ is the original coefficient value, and $\tilde{C}$ is the reconstructed value [S2].

### Pseudocode
```python
def haar_wavelet_compress(volume, target_psnr, k):
    """
    Compress a 3D volume using Haar Wavelet Compression with random access.
    Args:
        volume: 3D list of lists of lists of int, shape (W, H, D), 12-bit values.
        target_psnr: Desired PSNR in dB.
        k: Exponent for slice prediction distance F = 2^k.
    Returns:
        one_byte_stream: list of int (0-255)
        two_byte_stream: list of int
        cell_info: list of dict with 'flags', 'offset_one', 'offset_two'
        block_params: list of dict with 'A', 'B'
    """
    W, H, D = len(volume[0][0]), len(volume[0]), len(volume)
    block_size = 16
    cell_size = 4
    F = 1 << k

    one_byte_stream = []
    two_byte_stream = []
    cell_info = []
    block_params = []

    for z in range(0, D, block_size):
        for y in range(0, H, block_size):
            for x in range(0, W, block_size):
                block = [[volume[z+dz][y+dy][x+dx] for dx in range(block_size)] 
                         for dy in range(block_size) for dz in range(block_size)]
                
                # Temporal prediction is applied slice-by-slice before transform [S2]
                # Simplified here as block-level processing; prediction uses R/I slices [S2]
                
                # 2D Haar Wavelet Transform (2 levels) on each slice
                transformed_block = apply_2d_haar_2_levels(block)
                
                # Thresholding per slice
                thresholded_block = threshold_slices(transformed_block, target_psnr, volume, W, H, D)
                
                # Encoding cells
                cell_flags = []
                current_one_byte_offset = len(one_byte_stream)
                current_two_byte_offset = len(two_byte_stream)
                
                for cz in range(0, block_size, cell_size):
                    for cy in range(0, block_size, cell_size):
                        for cx in range(0, block_size, cell_size):
                            cell = [[thresholded_block[cz+dz][cy+dy][cx+dx] 
                                     for dx in range(cell_size)] 
                                    for dy in range(cell_size) for dz in range(cell_size)]
                            
                            flat_coeffs = [v for row in cell for v in row if v != 0]
                            
                            if flat_coeffs:
                                cell_flags.append(1)
                                # Group into blocks of at most 64 coefficients [S2]
                                for i in range(0, len(flat_coeffs), 64):
                                    group = flat_coeffs[i:i+64]
                                    A = max(group)
                                    B = min(group)
                                    block_params.append({'A': A, 'B': B})
                                    
                                    quantized = []
                                    for C in group:
                                        if A == B:
                                            x = 0
                                        else:
                                            x = int(255 * (C - B) / (A - B) + 0.5)
                                        quantized.append(x)
                                    
                                    one_byte_stream.extend(quantized)
                            else:
                                cell_flags.append(0)
                
                cell_info.append({
                    'flags': cell_flags,
                    'offset_one': current_one_byte_offset,
                    'offset_two': current_two_byte_offset
                })

    return one_byte_stream, two_byte_stream, cell_info, block_params

def apply_2d_haar_2_levels(block):
    """Apply 2D Haar wavelet transform with 2 decomposition levels."""
    # Level 1: Rows
    for z in range(len(block)):
        for y in range(len(block[0])):
            row = block[z][y]
            block[z][y] = haar_1d(row)
    # Level 1: Columns
    for z in range(len(block)):
        for x in range(len(block[0][0])):
            col = [block[z][y][x] for y in range(len(block[0]))]
            col = haar_1d(col)
            for y in range(len(block[0])):
                block[z][y][x] = col[y]
    # Level 2: Rows on approximation subband
    for z in range(len(block)):
        for y in range(len(block[0])):
            row = block[z][y]
            block[z][y] = haar_1d(row)
    # Level 2: Columns on approximation subband
    for z in range(len(block)):
        for x in range(len(block[0][0])):
            col = [block[z][y][x] for y in range(len(block[0]))]
            col = haar_1d(col)
            for y in range(len(block[0])):
                block[z][y][x] = col[y]
    return block

def haar_1d(signal):
    """1D Haar wavelet transform."""
    n = len(signal)
    if n % 2 != 0:
        signal = signal + [signal[-1]]
        n = len(signal)
    approx = [(signal[i] + signal[i+1]) / 2.0 for i in range(0, n, 2)]
    detail = [(signal[i] - signal[i+1]) / 2.0 for i in range(0, n, 2)]
    return approx + detail

def threshold_slices(transformed_block, target_psnr, original_volume, W, H, D):
    """Threshold coefficients per slice to achieve target PSNR."""
    # Global max for PSNR calculation [S2]
    global_max_sq = max(v**2 for row in original_volume for slice in row for v in slice)
    
    for z in range(len(transformed_block)):
        for y in range(len(transformed_block[0])):
            slice_data = transformed_block[z][y]
            flat = [v for row in slice_data for v in row]
            sorted_coeffs = sorted(flat, key=abs)
            
            # Zero out coefficients until target PSNR is met [S2]
            for i in range(len(sorted_coeffs)):
                test_slice = [sorted_coeffs[j] if j >= i else 0 for j in range(len(sorted_coeffs))]
                # Pad to original slice size if needed
                while len(test_slice) < len(flat):
                    test_slice.append(0)
                mse = sum((test_slice[j] - flat[j])**2 for j in range(len(flat))) / len(flat)
                if mse == 0:
                    psnr = float('inf')
                else:
                    psnr = 10 * (global_max_sq / mse)
                if psnr >= target_psnr:
                    break
            
            # Apply threshold to actual slice structure
            idx = 0
            for r in range(len(slice_data)):
                for c in range(len(slice_data[0])):
                    if idx < len(sorted_coeffs) and idx >= i:
                        slice_data[r][c] = sorted_coeffs[idx]
                    else:
                        slice_data[r][c] = 0
                    idx += 1
    return transformed_block
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Global Sorting Impracticality**: Sorting all coefficients globally for thresholding is too slow for large volumes; the method uses per-slice sorting, which is not globally optimal but provides a reasonable solution [S2].
- **Precision**: Original voxel values are 12-bit; averages fall in $[0, 2^{12}]$ and details in $[-(2^{12}-1)/2, (2^{12}-1)/2]$ [S1, unofficial]. Quantization to 8-bit introduces loss, but the uniform scalar quantizer minimizes distortion [S2].
- **Boundaries**: The block-based approach requires volumes to be divisible by block size ($16 \times 16 \times 16$); padding may be needed for non-divisible dimensions [G].
- **Division by Zero**: Quantization assumes $A \neq B$ for non-zero coefficient blocks; a guard is required if a block contains identical values [G].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: The use of the 2-tap Haar wavelet and 2 decomposition levels ensures fast reconstruction; data reconstruction time is imperceptible compared to the time taken by rendering tasks [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Memory Complexity**: The block-based structure with flags allows random access to any voxel by decoding only the relevant cell and its parent nodes, avoiding decoding the entire volume [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Compression Ratio**: Compression ratios of up to 28 were reported with good fidelity [S2]. Other methods like Laplacian Pyramid achieved ~10, and 2D wavelet subband coding achieved 20-60 but lacked fast random access [S2].
- **Strengths**: Fast random access, high compression ratios, interactive visualization support [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Limitations**: Lossy compression; artifacts may appear at high compression factors (e.g., factor 5 showed blocking and contouring artifacts in some comparisons, though Ihm and Park reported good fidelity at factor 28) [S2]. The per-slice thresholding is not globally optimal [S2].

## Background
The method was presented by Insung Ihm and Sanghun Park [S1, unofficial][S2]. It was developed to address the limitations of previous wavelet compression schemes that either supported high compression ratios or fast random access, but not both [S2]. It builds on the idea of treating 3D volume data as a sequence of 2D slices, borrowing techniques from video compression such as temporal prediction [S2]. The use of Haar wavelets was chosen for their computational efficiency [S2].
