# Novel Scene Composition and View Synthesis


## Overview
This method synthesizes novel views and scene compositions by leveraging a decomposed neural scene graph structure [S1]. It handles occlusions from the background and other objects during novel view synthesis by leveraging this decomposed structure [S1]. Unlike NeRF variants that cannot reason about scene dynamics, this method synthesizes shadows and reflections correctly even without changing the viewing direction [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A learned neural scene graph representation of a dynamic scene, which can be learned from manual annotations or noisy tracker outputs [S1].
*   **Output:** Novel view images and composed scenes with new object arrangements or camera poses [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The scene is represented as a neural scene graph where each object is an individual node [S1]. The specific internal layout of the neural fields is defined in the parent article [Neural Scene Graph Representation and Rendering](14688-neural-scene-graph-representation-and-rendering.md) [G].

**Steps:**
1.  **Scene Decomposition:** The scene is decomposed into multiple objects represented as nodes in the scene graph [S1].
2.  **Composition/Sampling:** Novel scene graphs are sampled from existing nodes and edges in the dataset, combined with new transformations sampled on road lanes [S1].
3.  **Collision Avoidance:** The sampling process explicitly forbids collisions between objects during the arrangement phase [S1].
4.  **Rendering:** The novel view is synthesized by rendering the composed graph, leveraging the decomposed structure to handle occlusions from the background and other objects [S1].
5.  **Dynamic Effect Synthesis:** The renderer correctly synthesizes shadows and reflections even when the viewing direction remains unchanged [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Transformation Sampling:** Applied to driveable space or road lanes to generate novel trajectories, such as ego-vehicle motion of approximately 2 meters forward [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Occlusion:** Handled by the graph structure, allowing the renderer to account for background and inter-object occlusions [S1].
*   **Unseen Arrangements:** The method generalizes to translations and arrangements not observed during training [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Probabilistic Scene Graph Generation](14712-probabilistic-scene-graph-generation.md)
Probabilistic Scene Graph Generation (CG3D) is a text-to-3D scene synthesis method that explicitly models semantic relationships and physical interactions between objects using a scene graph interpreted as a probabilistic graphical model. Unlike methods that generate individual objects or extend from a single reference image, CG3D addresses the challenge of multi-object composition by enforcing structural consistency and physical plausibility through a two-stage optimization process.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Strengths:** The method synthesizes shadows and reflections correctly without changing the viewing direction, a capability standard NeRF and time-aware NeRF lack [S1]. It effectively handles occlusions from the background and other objects [S1]. It supports novel scene compositions with new transformations not seen during training [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The quality of novel compositions depends on the sampling of nodes and edges from the dataset [S1].
*   **Performance:** The method is evaluated on sequences from the KITTI data set [S1]. Qualitative results and quantitative evaluations are reported in the source paper (Fig. 10, Tab. 1) [S1].

## Background
This method originates from the "Neural Scene Graphs for Dynamic Scenes" framework, which learns scene graphs from manual annotations or noisy tracker outputs [S1]. It is applied to scenes from the KITTI data set to demonstrate dynamic scene handling and novel compositions [S1]. The approach also extends to 3D object detection by formulating it as inverse rendering and using the scene graph as a forward model [S1].
