# Patch-Grid Composite Loss

Also called: Patch-Grid Loss, Patch-Grid Training Objective

## Overview
The Patch-Grid Composite Loss is a multi-term training objective designed to supervise the neural implicit surface representation in Patch-Grid [Patch-Grid](14449-patch-grid.md). It solves the problem of coordinating multiple localized neural signed distance fields (SDFs) into a globally consistent shape by enforcing surface fidelity, geometric smoothness, and valid Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) merging behavior. It distinguishes itself from standard SDF losses by explicitly including a merge term to constrain the extended zero-level sets of individual patches, ensuring they align correctly at their boundaries for valid CSG operations.

## How it works
The method computes a weighted sum of five distinct loss terms: surface fitting, normal supervision, merge coordination, pseudo-SDF regularization, and Eikonal regularization.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   A set of training points $\mathcal{P}$ sampled from the target surface and its vicinity.
    *   For each point $p_i \in \mathcal{P}$, the ground truth surface position $s_i$ and surface normal $n_i$.
    *   The neural network parameters for the Patch-Grid representation.
*   **Outputs**:
    *   A scalar loss value $L$ used for gradient-based optimization.

### Data Structures
No specific custom data structures are required beyond standard tensors for points, normals, and network outputs. The loss is computed per point and aggregated.

### Formulas
The total loss is defined as:

$$ L = \lambda_{\text{Surf}} L_{\text{Surf}} + \lambda_{\text{Normal}} L_{\text{Normal}} + \lambda_{\text{Merge}} L_{\text{Merge}} + \lambda_{\text{SDF}} L_{\text{SDF}} + \lambda_{\text{Eikonal}} L_{\text{Eikonal}} $$

Where:
*   $L_{\text{Surf}}$: Surface fitting loss. Typically the mean squared error between the predicted SDF value and the ground truth distance (often zero for points on the surface).
*   $L_{\text{Normal}}$: Normal supervision loss. The mean squared error between the normalized gradient of the predicted SDF and the ground truth normal.
*   $L_{\text{Merge}}$: Merge loss. Constrains the extended zero-level sets of patches to ensure validity of CSG operations. It is applied primarily in the leaf nodes of the merge grid where patches overlap or interact.
*   $L_{\text{SDF}}$: Pseudo SDF supervision. Applies regularization close to the geometric surface to prevent offsets in individual patches.
*   $L_{\text{Eikonal}}$: Eikonal loss. Enforces the unit gradient magnitude property of SDFs: $\|\nabla \phi(p)\|_2 \approx 1$.

### Parameters
*   $\lambda_{\text{Merge}}$: Default value is 400. Tested range: 100–1200.
*   $\lambda_{\text{Normal}}$: Default value is 50. Tested range: 12–200.
*   $\lambda_{\text{Surf}}$, $\lambda_{\text{SDF}}$, $\lambda_{\text{Eikonal}}$: Values not explicitly quantified in the primary source text, though their presence is confirmed by ablation studies [S1].

### Pitfalls and Edge Cases
*   **Low $\lambda_{\text{Merge}}$**: Causes artifacts due to insufficient constraint on coordinating the extraneous zero-level sets, leading to invalid CSG merging [S1].
*   **High $\lambda_{\text{Merge}}$**: Causes holes in the surface due to failure in accurately fitting small, localized patches [S1].
*   **Low $\lambda_{\text{Normal}}$**: Leads to less accurate surface fitting and increased artifacts [S1].
*   **High $\lambda_{\text{Normal}}$**: Overwhelms the merge loss, causing similar artifacts to high $\lambda_{\text{Merge}}$ (failure to handle merging of multiple patches) [S1].
*   **Removing $L_{\text{Surf}}$**: Results in large surface offsets and degraded reconstruction quality [S1].
*   **Removing $L_{\text{Normal}}$**: Leads to severe geometric artifacts [S1].
*   **Removing $L_{\text{SDF}}$**: Causes obvious offsets in individual patches and increased Hausdorff distance [S1].
*   **Removing $L_{\text{Eikonal}}$**: Results in a very large Field Error (FE), indicating the learned field does not satisfy the distance field property [S1].

## Subtopics

### Merge Loss
A specialized term ensuring that the zero-level sets of adjacent patches align to support valid CSG operations. Chosen when using octree-based merge grids to combine local SDFs.

### Pseudo SDF Loss
A regularization term applied near the surface to prevent individual patches from drifting or offsetting. Chosen to maintain local consistency in the patch-based representation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of training points and patches. The merge loss adds overhead proportional to the number of points in the leaf nodes of the merge grid, which is a small portion of the shape [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Low, as it does not require storing large auxiliary structures beyond the standard neural network parameters and the merge grid indices.
*   **Measured Performance**: The complete loss achieves the best accuracy across most metrics (CD, HD, F-score, IoU, NC, SPE, FE) compared to ablated versions [S1].
*   **Strengths**: Effectively coordinates multiple patches for CSG validity; prevents severe artifacts from individual patch failures; ensures smooth, unit-gradient SDF fields.
*   **Limitations**: Sensitive to weight balancing; inappropriate weights for $\lambda_{\text{Merge}}$ or $\lambda_{\text{Normal}}$ lead to visible artifacts (holes or merging failures) [S1].
*   **When to use**: When training Patch-Grid representations that rely on CSG operations to combine local neural SDFs.
*   **When not to use**: For single, global SDF representations where patch merging is not required.

## Background
The Patch-Grid Composite Loss was introduced in the paper "Patch-Grid: An Efficient and Feature-Preserving Neural Implicit Surface Representation" by Guying Lin et al. [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of uniform patch volumes in fitting thin tubes and complex features by using localized patch-grids. The loss design was validated through ablation studies showing that each term contributes to specific aspects of reconstruction quality, such as surface offset, normal accuracy, and field smoothness.
