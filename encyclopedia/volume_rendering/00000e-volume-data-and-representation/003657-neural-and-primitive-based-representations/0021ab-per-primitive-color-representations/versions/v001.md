# Per-Primitive Color Representations


## Overview
Per-Primitive Color Representations are mathematical functions used to encode view-dependent color per primitive during rendering [S1, unofficial]. They solve the problem of efficiently storing and evaluating anisotropic or view-dependent appearance for individual primitives (such as surfels or splats) without relying on a global neural network or feature grid lookup for color. What sets them apart from sibling methods like [MLP Neural Fields](3854-mlp-neural-fields.md) or [Hash Grid Neural Fields](3855-hash-grid-neural-fields.md) is that they store explicit parameters directly on the primitive, avoiding hash collisions and the parameter overhead of large texture atlases [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All per-primitive color representations in this family share the following mechanism:
1.  **Primitive-Local Storage**: Each primitive (e.g., a surfel) stores a fixed set of parameters defining its color function. These parameters are independent of the global scene representation's feature tables.
2.  **View-Dependent Evaluation**: The color $c$ for a primitive is computed as a function of the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ (typically a unit vector) and the stored parameters.
3.  **Explicit Parameters**: Unlike implicit fields, the parameters are directly optimized during training (e.g., teacher training and atlas finetune phases) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Mathematical Form**: The color is typically a weighted sum of basis functions or sites, where weights depend on the angle between the viewing direction and the primitive's local geometry or stored sites.

## Subtopics

### Spherical Harmonics (SH)
- **What it is**: A representation using spherical harmonics basis functions to encode view-dependent color.
- **Parameters**: Degree 3, requiring 48 parameters per primitive [S1, unofficial].
- **When to choose**: Standard baseline for view-dependent effects; widely supported but may require more parameters than necessary for simple specularities.
- **Link**: [Spherical Harmonics](3854-mlp-neural-fields.md) (Note: SH is often associated with MLP fields, but here it is used as a per-primitive explicit representation).

### Spherical Betas (SB)
- **What it is**: A representation using spherical beta distributions to model view-dependent color.
- **Parameters**: 2 lobes, requiring 15 parameters per primitive [S1, unofficial].
- **When to choose**: When parameter efficiency is prioritized and the view-dependent effects are relatively simple (e.g., broad specular highlights).
- **Link**: [Spherical Betas](1478-gaussian-head-avatar-gha.md) (Note: SB is a specific variant often used in avatar contexts, but applicable here).

### Soft Spherical Voronoi View-Dependent Color
- **What it is**: A view-dependent color model using a soft spherical Voronoi diagram with $K$ sites on the unit sphere, allowing a single surfel to hold both broad diffuse shifts and sharp highlights [S1, unofficial].
- **Formula**: View-dependent color is $f_{SV}(\mathbf{d}) = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k(\mathbf{d}) c_k$, where weights are $w_k(\mathbf{d}) = \frac{\exp(-\tau_k \|\mathbf{s}_k - \mathbf{d}\|)}{\sum_{k'=1}^K \exp(-\tau_{k'} \|\mathbf{s}_{k'} - \mathbf{d}\|)}$ [S1, unofficial]. The mixture is offset by $b_{SV}=0.5$ and gated by the inner ReLU of Eq. 2 [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameters**: 7 sites, requiring 49 parameters per primitive [S1, unofficial].
- **When to choose**: When high-fidelity view-dependent effects (sharp highlights and diffuse shifts) are needed, and parameter count is less critical than quality.
- **Link**: [Soft Spherical Voronoi](3300-neural-primitive-splatting.md) (Note: SV is the primary method discussed in the source).

## Background
Origin: The Soft Spherical Voronoi model was introduced by Di Sario et al., 2025, in the context of ultrafast spatial texture-atlas splatting [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated during teacher training and atlas finetune phases [S1, unofficial]. The comparison between SH, SB, and SV was conducted on the Mip-NeRF 360 benchmarks, specifically the bonsai scene, to evaluate per-primitive feature ablations [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses limitations of hash grids, such as collisions and parameter averaging, by using explicit per-surfel parameters [S1, unofficial].
