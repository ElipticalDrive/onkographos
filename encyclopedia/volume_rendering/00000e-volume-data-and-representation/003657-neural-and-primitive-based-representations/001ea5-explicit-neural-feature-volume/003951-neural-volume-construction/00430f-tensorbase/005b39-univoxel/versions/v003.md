# UniVoxel

Also called: Unified Voxelization framework

## Overview
UniVoxel is a unified voxelization framework for inverse rendering that encodes a scene into two learnable voxel fields: a Signed Distance Function (SDF) field for geometry and a semantic field for materials and illumination [S1]. It enables efficient trilinear interpolation and volumetric physics-based rendering, jointly modeling geometry, materials, and incident light radiance using local Spherical Gaussians (SG) [S1]. Unlike environment map-based methods that require expensive visibility simulation for every incident direction, UniVoxel avoids this heavy computational burden by integrating illumination directly into the voxel grid [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Multi-view 2D images of a scene [S1].
*   **Outputs:** Learnable voxel embeddings for geometry ($V_{sdf}$) and semantics ($V_{sem}$), from which albedo, roughness, normals, and incident light parameters are derived [S1]. Rendered images via volumetric rendering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **SDF Field ($V_{sdf}$):** A 3D grid of learnable embeddings [S1].
    *   Layout: $\mathbb{R}^{1 \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ [S1].
    *   Dimensions: $N_x, N_y, N_z$ are voxel resolutions [S1].
    *   Content: One scalar value per voxel representing the SDF [S1].
2.  **Semantic Field ($V_{sem}$):** A 3D grid of learnable embeddings [S1].
    *   Layout: $\mathbb{R}^{C \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ [S1].
    *   Dimensions: $C$ is the semantic feature dimension (typically 6) [S1].
    *   Content: $C$-dimensional feature vectors per voxel [S1].
3.  **Lighting Parameters:** Derived from $V_{sem}$ via MLPs, representing Spherical Gaussian lobes [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Voxelization Initialization:** Initialize $V_{sdf} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ and $V_{sem} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times N_x \times N_y \times N_z}$ as learnable parameters [S1].
2.  **Trilinear Interpolation:** For any 3D position $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$, query the SDF value $s(x)$ and semantic features $f(x)$ by trilinear interpolation $\mathcal{F}_{interp}$ over the eight neighboring voxels of $V_{sdf}$ and $V_{sem}$ respectively [S1]:
    $$ s(x) = \mathcal{F}_{interp}(x, V_{sdf}) $$
    $$ f(x) = \mathcal{F}_{interp}(x, V_{sem}) $$
3.  **Geometry Derivation:**
    *   **Opacity:** For a sampled point $x_i$ along a camera ray, calculate opacity $\alpha_i$ using the NeuS formulation [S1]:
        $$ \alpha_i = \max\left(\frac{\sigma(s(x_i)) - \sigma(s(x_{i+1}))}{\sigma(s(x_i))}, 0\right) $$
        where $\sigma(s(x_i)) = (1 + e^{-d_1 s(x_i)})^{-1}$ and $d_1$ is the standard deviation [S1].
    *   **Normal:** Compute surface normal components using central differences with step size $v$ (voxel size) [S1]:
        $$ n_x(x) = \frac{s(x + [v, 0, 0]) - s(x - [v, 0, 0])}{2v} $$
        $n_y(x)$ and $n_z(x)$ are calculated similarly along the y and z axes [S1].
4.  **Material Prediction:** Predict albedo $\kappa(x)$ and roughness $\zeta(x)$ using lightweight MLPs $\Psi_\kappa$ and $\Psi_\zeta$ taking $f(x)$ and position $x$ as inputs [S1]:
    $$ \kappa(x) = \Psi_\kappa(f(x), x) $$
    $$ \zeta(x) = \Psi_\zeta(f(x), x) $$
5.  **Illumination Modeling:**
    *   Represent incident light radiance using local Spherical Gaussians (SG) [S1].
    *   Model SG parameters by learning them from the semantic field voxelization via a lightweight MLP $\Psi_h$ [S1]:
        $$ h(x) = \Psi_h(f(x), x, e_x) $$
        where $e_x$ is a view embedding (dimension $C_v=6$ for NeRD dataset) [S1].
    *   Number of SG lobes $k=16$ [S1].
6.  **Light Visibility:** For incident direction $\omega_i$, compute visibility $v(x, \omega_i)$ via volumetric integration along ray $r_i = x + \omega_i$ [S1]:
    $$ v(x, \omega_i) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{N_l} \alpha_i \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_j) $$
    where $N_l$ is the number of sampled points along the light ray [S1].
7.  **Rendering:** Calculate outgoing radiance $C(x, \omega_o)$ using physics-based rendering [S1]:
    $$ C(x, \omega_o) = \int_{\Omega} L_i(x, \omega_i) f_r(x, \omega_i, \omega_o) (\omega_i \cdot n(x)) d\omega_i $$
    *   Approximate the integral using Fibonacci sampling over the hemisphere with 128 samples [S1].
    *   $L_i(x, \omega_i)$ is the incident light radiance derived from SG parameters [S1].
    *   $f_r$ is the BRDF (e.g., Simplified GGX) using $\kappa(x)$ and $\zeta(x)$ [S1].
8.  **Relighting:** For new illumination, compute light visibility using the method in Step 6 and consider only direct lighting, as the trained incident light field is specific to the training environment [S1]. Apply a 1.5 power correction to roughness to address optimization bias [S1].
9.  **Training:**
    *   **Coarse Stage:** Optimize only the radiance field branch. Voxel resolution $96^3$ [S1].
    *   **Fine Stage:** Optimize the full representation. Voxel resolution $160^3$ [S1].
    *   Use Adam optimizer, batch size 8192 rays, 10k iterations per stage [S1].
    *   Learning rates: 0.001 for MLPs, 0.1 for $V_{sdf}$ and $V_{sem}$ (reduce $V_{sdf}$ to 0.005 in fine stage) [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class UniVoxel:
    def __init__(self, Nx, Ny, Nz, C=6, k=16, Cv=6, d1=100.0):
        """Initialize UniVoxel representation."""
        self.V_sdf = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, Nx, Ny, Nz))
        self.V_sem = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(C, Nx, Ny, Nz))
        self.Psi_kappa = MLP(C + 3, 3, 192, 3)
        self.Psi_zeta = MLP(C + 3, 1, 192, 3)
        self.Psi_h = MLP(C + 3 + Cv, k * 7, 192, 3)
        self.d1 = d1
        # Voxel size depends on scene AABB; assuming [-1,1] bounds for grid_sample
        self.v = 1.0 / max(Nx, Ny, Nz)
        self.k = k
        self.Cv = Cv

    def trilinear(self, grid, coords):
        """Trilinear interpolation for grid [C, 1, H, W, D] at coords [N, 3] in [-1, 1]."""
        coords_norm = coords * 2.0 - 1.0
        coords_norm = coords_norm.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0)
        out = F.grid_sample(grid, coords_norm, mode='trilinear', align_corners=True)
        return out.squeeze(3).squeeze(2)

    def query(self, x):
        """Query SDF and semantic features at position x."""
        s = self.trilinear(self.V_sdf.unsqueeze(1), x)
        f = self.trilinear(self.V_sem.unsqueeze(1), x)
        return s, f

    def compute_normal(self, x):
        """Compute surface normal using central differences on SDF."""
        eps = self.v
        dx = torch.tensor([eps, 0, 0], device=x.device)
        dy = torch.tensor([0, eps, 0], device=x.device)
        dz = torch.tensor([0, 0, eps], device=x.device)
        s_p, _ = self.query(x + dx)
        s_n, _ = self.query(x - dx)
        s_py, _ = self.query(x + dy)
        s_ny, _ = self.query(x - dy)
        s_pz, _ = self.query(x + dz)
        s_nz, _ = self.query(x - dz)
        nx = (s_p - s_n) / (2 * eps)
        ny = (s_py - s_ny) / (2 * eps)
        nz = (s_pz - s_nz) / (2 * eps)
        return F.normalize(torch.stack([nx, ny, nz], dim=-1), dim=-1)

    def predict_materials(self, x, f):
        """Predict albedo and roughness."""
        inp = torch.cat([f, x], dim=-1)
        kappa = torch.sigmoid(self.Psi_kappa(inp))
        zeta = torch.sigmoid(self.Psi_zeta(inp))
        return kappa, zeta

    def predict_lighting(self, x, f, view_embed):
        """Predict SG parameters for incident light."""
        inp = torch.cat([f, x, view_embed], dim=-1)
        sg_flat = self.Psi_h(inp)
        return sg_flat.view(-1, self.k, 7)

    def compute_visibility(self, alpha):
        """Compute light visibility v = 1 - sum(alpha_i * prod(1-alpha_j)) [S1]."""
        # alpha shape: [N]
        prod_term = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha, dim=0)
        # prod(1-alpha_j) for j < i is prod_term[i-1] if i>0 else 1.0
        prod_shifted = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(prod_term[:1]), prod_term[:-1]], dim=0)
        visibility = 1.0 - torch.sum(alpha * prod_shifted, dim=0)
        return visibility

    def render_ray(self, ray_o, ray_d, num_samples):
        """Render a single ray for volume opacity/transmittance."""
        step = self.v / 2.0
        t = torch.arange(num_samples, device=ray_o.device) * step
        x = ray_o.unsqueeze(0) + t.unsqueeze(1) * ray_d.unsqueeze(0)
        s, f = self.query(x)
        sigma = 1.0 / (1.0 + torch.exp(-self.d1 * s))
        sigma_next = 1.0 / (1.0 + torch.exp(-self.d1 * s[1:]))
        alpha = torch.clamp((sigma[:-1] - sigma_next) / sigma[:-1], min=0)
        T = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(alpha[:1]), torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha, dim=0)], dim=0)
        n = self.compute_normal(x)
        kappa, zeta = self.predict_materials(x, f)
        return x, s, f, alpha, T, n, kappa, zeta

    def compute_radiance(self, x, n, kappa, zeta, view_dir, view_embed):
        """Compute outgoing radiance C(x, wo) for a surface point."""
        _, f = self.query(x)
        sg = self.predict_lighting(x, f, view_embed)
        dirs = sg[:, :, :3]
        colors = sg[:, :, 3:6]
        sharp = sg[:, :, 6:7]
        omega_i = fibonacci_hemisphere(128, n)
        dot = torch.sum(omega_i.unsqueeze(1) * dirs.unsqueeze(0), dim=-1)
        L_i = torch.sum(colors.unsqueeze(0) * torch.exp(sharp.unsqueeze(0) * (dot.unsqueeze(-1) - 1)), dim=-1)
        v = self.compute_visibility(torch.ones_like(L_i[:, :1])) # Direct lighting visibility ~1
        f_r = ggx_brdf(kappa, zeta, n, omega_i, view_dir)
        cos = torch.clamp(torch.sum(omega_i * n.unsqueeze(0), dim=-1).unsqueeze(-1), min=0)
        C = torch.sum(L_i * f_r * cos, dim=0) * (2 * torch.pi / 128)
        return C

    def compute_loss(self, C_pred, C_gt, C_rad, x_surf, h_surf, h_perturbed, kappa_pred, zeta_pred, n_pred):
        """Compute total loss L = L_rec + L_smo + L_reg [S1]."""
        # Reconstruction loss
        L_rec = 1.0 * torch.mean((C_pred - C_gt) ** 2) + 1.0 * torch.mean((C_rad - C_gt) ** 2)
        # Smoothness loss (placeholder for surface gradient regularization terms)
        L_smo = 0.002 * torch.mean((n_pred - 0) ** 2) + 0.0005 * torch.mean((kappa_pred - 0) ** 2) + 0.0005 * torch.mean((zeta_pred - 0) ** 2)
        # Illumination regularization
        L_sg = 0.0005 * torch.mean((h_surf - h_perturbed) ** 2)
        L_white = 0.0001 * torch.mean(torch.abs(L_i - torch.mean(L_i, dim=-1, keepdim=True)))
        L_reg = L_sg + L_white
        return L_rec + L_smo + L_reg

class MLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_dim, out_dim, hidden_dim, num_layers):
        super().__init__()
        layers = []
        for _ in range(num_layers):
            layers.append(nn.Linear(in_dim, hidden_dim))
            layers.append(nn.ReLU())
            in_dim = hidden_dim
        layers.append(nn.Linear(hidden_dim, out_dim))
        self.net = nn.Sequential(*layers)
    def forward(self, x):
        return self.net(x)

def fibonacci_hemisphere(N, normal):
    """Generate N points on hemisphere defined by normal using Fibonacci lattice [G]."""
    pts = []
    for i in range(N):
        y = 1.0 - (i / (N - 1)) * 2.0
        r = torch.sqrt(torch.clamp(1.0 - y**2, min=0.0))
        theta = 2.0 * torch.pi * i / 1.61803398875
        pts.append(torch.tensor([torch.cos(theta) * r, y, torch.sin(theta) * r]))
    pts = torch.stack(pts)
    up = torch.tensor([0.0, 1.0, 0.0], device=pts.device)
    if torch.sum(normal * up) > 0.999:
        up = torch.tensor([1.0, 0.0, 0.0], device=pts.device)
    right = torch.cross(normal, up)
    right = right / torch.norm(right)
    forward = torch.cross(right, normal)
    pts = pts[:, 0:1] * right + pts[:, 1:2] * forward + pts[:, 2:3] * normal
    return pts

def ggx_brdf(albedo, roughness, normal, omega_i, view_dir):
    """Simplified GGX BRDF approximation [G]."""
    dot = torch.sum(normal * view_dir.unsqueeze(0), dim=-1).unsqueeze(-1)
    dot = torch.clamp(dot, min=0.0)
    alpha = roughness * roughness
    denom = (dot * dot) * (1.0 - alpha) + alpha
    F = torch.pow(1.0 - dot, 5.0)
    return albedo * (F * (1.0 - alpha) + alpha / denom)
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Voxel Resolution:** $96^3$ (coarse stage), $160^3$ (fine stage) [S1].
*   **Semantic Feature Channels ($C$):** 6 [S1].
*   **SG Lobes ($k$):** 16 [S1].
*   **MLP Architecture:** 3 hidden layers, 192 channels [S1].
*   **Sampling Step Size:** Half of the voxel size [S1].
*   **Incident Light Samples:** 128 (Fibonacci sampling) [S1].
*   **Optimizer:** Adam, batch size 8192 rays, 10k iterations per stage [S1].
*   **Learning Rates:** 0.001 for MLPs, 0.1 for $V_{sdf}$ and $V_{sem}$; $V_{sdf}$ reduced to 0.005 in fine stage [S1].
*   **Loss Weights:** $\lambda_{pbr} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{rad} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{n} = 0.002$, $\lambda_{\kappa} = 0.0005$, $\lambda_{\zeta} = 0.0005$, $\lambda_{sg} = 0.0005$, $\lambda_{white} = 0.0001$ [S1].
*   **View Embedding Dimension ($C_v$):** 6 (for NeRD dataset) [S1].
*   **Roughness Power Correction:** 1.5 (during relighting) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Lighting Ambiguity:** Neural incident light fields can lead to material-lighting ambiguity. Mitigated by SG smoothness and whiteness regularization [S1].
*   **Roughness Bias:** Optimization tends towards higher roughness values. Mitigated by 1.5 power correction during relighting [S1].
*   **Visibility Computation:** Environment map-based methods require heavy visibility simulation. UniVoxel avoids this by using SGs and direct visibility calculation [S1].
*   **Relighting Limitation:** The incident light field from training is not applicable to new illumination. Relighting requires recomputing visibility and considering only direct lighting [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Training time is significantly reduced compared to implicit methods. 18 minutes per scene on MII dataset, compared to 3 hours (TensoIR), 4 hours (Nvdiffrec-mc), and >2 days (NerFactor) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Uses explicit voxel grids. Can be optimized with multi-resolution hash encoding (UniVoxel Hash), which achieves higher relighting quality at the expense of a slight decrease in training speed compared to standard UniVoxel [S1].
*   **Performance:** Outperforms NeRFactor, MII, Nvdiffrec-mc, and TensoIR in most metrics on MII dataset [S1]. Produces accurate reconstructions and relighting, avoiding artifacts like holes and specular issues seen in Nvdiffrec-mc and TensoIR [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Fast optimization, unified modeling of geometry, materials, and illumination, efficient trilinear interpolation, avoids expensive multi-bounce ray tracing [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relighting is limited to direct lighting unless the incident light field is retrained [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When fast inverse rendering and relighting are required, and scenes have complex illumination that can be approximated by Spherical Gaussians [G].
*   **When Not to Use:** When indirect lighting effects are critical and cannot be approximated by direct lighting visibility, or when extremely high-frequency lighting details are present that SGs cannot capture well [G].

## Background
UniVoxel was proposed by S. Wu et al. in 2024 [S1]. It builds on the success of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) in novel view synthesis and adapts it to inverse rendering by learning explicit voxelization of scene representations [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of implicit learning methods and environment map-based illumination modeling, which are computationally expensive and struggle with complex real-world illumination [S1]. It is used on synthetic datasets (MII) and real-world datasets (NeRD, Shiny Blender) [S1].
