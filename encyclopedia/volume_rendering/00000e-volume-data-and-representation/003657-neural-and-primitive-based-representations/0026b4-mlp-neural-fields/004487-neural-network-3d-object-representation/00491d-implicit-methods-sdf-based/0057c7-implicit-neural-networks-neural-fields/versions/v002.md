# Implicit Neural Networks (Neural Fields)
Also called: Implicit Neural Representations, Neural Fields

## Overview
Implicit Neural Networks (Neural Fields) represent continuous measurements as a differentiable function of input coordinates [S1, unofficial]. Unlike discrete grids, they enable gradient-based optimization of uncertain measurement coordinates, such as sensor locations and projection angles, within imaging frameworks like computed tomography [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Query coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ (e.g., 2D or 3D spatial positions).
*   **Output:** A continuous measurement value (e.g., density, occupancy, or SDF) at $\mathbf{x}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Network:** A differentiable function approximator parameterized by weights $\theta$. The exact architecture (depth, width, activation functions) is not specified in the source [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Network weights and biases $\theta$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Initialize network parameters $\theta$. Not given in the sources.
2.  **Forward Evaluation:** Evaluate the network at query coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ to obtain the measurement value $y = f(\mathbf{x}; \theta)$.
3.  **Differentiation:** Compute gradients $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}; \theta)$ via automatic differentiation, leveraging the network's natural differentiability with respect to input coordinates [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Joint Optimization:** Use the computed gradients to jointly optimize the network parameters $\theta$ and uncertain measurement coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ alongside image reconstruction, typically via gradient-based methods [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The representation is defined as:
$$ y = f(\mathbf{x}; \theta) $$
where $y$ is the predicted measurement, $\mathbf{x}$ is the input coordinate, and $\theta$ are the network weights. The key property is the computable gradient:
$$ \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f(\mathbf{x}; \theta) $$
which enables coordinate optimization [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Network Depth/Width:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Activation Function:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Optimization Cost:** Requires more time to optimize compared to spline-based interpolators [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpretability:** Less simple to interpret than splines [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Mixture of Neural Implicit Functions](18011-mixture-of-neural-implicit-functions.md)
A generative variant representing data instances as a weighted sum of basis networks, separating shared structure from instance-specific details. It offers stable modulation compared to HyperNet or FiLM and allows capacity scaling via additional basis functions while keeping inference compact through weighted averaging.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Optimization is slower than spline-based interpolators [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the MLP weight size; not explicitly quantified in the source [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Achieves reconstruction accuracy comparable to differentiable splines for uncalibrated imaging tasks [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Natural differentiability with respect to input coordinates; continuous representation; enables joint optimization of coordinates and network parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slower optimization; reduced interpretability compared to splines [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When continuous representation and gradient-based optimization of uncertain input coordinates are required, such as in uncalibrated imaging or sensor localization [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When fast optimization or high interpretability is prioritized over continuous differentiability; splines are preferred in those cases [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Implicit Neural Networks (Neural Fields) were applied to address uncertainty in measurement coordinates within imaging frameworks, particularly 2D and 3D computed tomography [S1, unofficial]. The approach formulates measurement interpolation at unknown nodes supervised by a forward operator, jointly fitting a measurement representation, optimizing uncertain coordinates, and performing image reconstruction to ensure consistent calibration [S1, unofficial]. In this context, they were compared against differentiable spline interpolators, which perform as well as implicit neural networks while requiring less optimization time and offering simpler interpretability [S1, unofficial].
