# Implicit methods (SDF-based)
Also called: Implicit representation, MLP Implicit Surface Representation, Neural Implicit Surface

## Overview
Implicit methods parameterize scene features (such as density, color, occupancy probability, or Signed Distance Field values) as a continuous function approximated by a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) network [S1]. Unlike explicit methods (voxels, point clouds, meshes) which are discrete and limited in resolution, implicit methods are continuous and accurate at arbitrary resolutions [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves representing the 3D scene using an implicit function $f: \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{R}^k$ for multi-feature outputs) approximated by a neural network [S1]. The input to the network is a 3D coordinate in object space, and the output indicates a property such as occupancy or distance [S1]. This representation is naturally differentiable with respect to input coordinates, allowing for gradient-based optimization [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Implicit Neural Networks (Neural Fields)
Implicit neural networks, also known as neural fields, use implicit neural networks as continuous measurement representations [S2, unofficial]. They are naturally differentiable with respect to input coordinates, allowing gradient-based optimization of measurement coordinates [S2, unofficial]. Compared to splines, implicit neural networks perform as well but require more time to optimize and are less simple to interpret [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Implicit methods emerged as a novel approach to overcome the resolution limits of conventional explicit reconstruction methods (grids of voxels, point clouds, or meshes) [S1]. Early implementations include IM-NET (Chen et al. 2019), which trained networks through deep learning using VAE+GAN to replace traditional reconstruction methods with an implicit surface function decoder for single-view 3D modeling [S1]. Occupancy Networks (Mescheder et al. 2019) further developed this paradigm [S1]. The approach is applied in contexts such as 2D and 3D computed tomography, where it produces improved reconstructions compared to baselines that do not account for uncertain measurement coordinates [S2, unofficial].
