# Layered Latent Motion Conditioning

Also called: Motion Conditioning, fm Mapping

## Overview
Layered Latent Motion Conditioning is a method for embedding explicit motion representations into the latent space of a style-based generative model to control dynamic synthesis, such as facial expressions, while maintaining 3D-awareness and identity consistency. It differs from prior concatenation-based conditioning [13] by injecting motion features into every layer of the synthesis module via summation with style vectors, which accelerates the convergence of motion reconstruction losses.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A low-dimensional motion representation vector $m \in \mathbb{R}^{50}$ [S1].
*   **Input:** A style vector $w$ derived from a noise vector $z$ via a separate mapping network $f_z$ [S1].
*   **Output:** A sequence of conditioned latent vectors passed to the synthesis module layers.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Motion Representation ($m$):** A fixed-size vector of dimension 50 [S1].
*   **Mapping Network ($f_m$):** A ReLU-activated neural network that maps $m$ to the layered latent motion space [S1].
*   **Layered Latent Motion Space:** A concatenation of $N$ latent motion vectors, denoted as $[d_1, \dots, d_N]^T$, where each $d_i \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ [S1].
*   **Style Vector ($w$):** A vector of dimension 512, matching the dimension of $d_i$ [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **$N$:** The number of layers in the synthesis module $G_s$. This determines the depth of the layered latent motion space [S1].

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Embed Motion:** Pass the motion representation $m$ through the mapping network $f_m$ to obtain the layered latent motion vectors:
    $$ [d_1, \dots, d_N]^T = f_m(m) $$
    where $d_i \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ [S1].
2.  **Combine with Style:** Combine the style vector $w$ with each motion vector $d_i$ via element-wise summation to produce the conditioned latent vectors for each layer:
    $$ [w + d_1, \dots, w + d_N]^T $$
    [S1].
3.  **Modulate Synthesis:** Pass the conditioned latent vectors to each corresponding layer of the synthesis module $G_s$ via weight modulation [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def layered_latent_motion_conditioning(m, w, f_m, N):
    """
    Embeds motion representation m into a layered latent space and combines it with style vector w.
    
    Args:
        m: Motion representation vector (R^50).
        w: Style vector (R^512).
        f_m: ReLU mapping network for motion.
        N: Number of layers in the synthesis module.
        
    Returns:
        A list of N conditioned latent vectors for modulation.
    """
    # Step 1: Embed motion into layered latent space
    # f_m outputs a tensor of shape (N, 512)
    d = f_m(m) 
    
    # Initialize list for conditioned vectors
    conditioned_vectors = []
    
    # Step 2 & 3: Combine with style and prepare for modulation
    for i in range(N):
        # Element-wise summation of style vector w and motion vector d[i]
        # w is broadcasted to match the dimension of d[i]
        conditioned_vector = w + d[i]
        conditioned_vectors.append(conditioned_vector)
        
    return conditioned_vectors
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Dimension Mismatch:** The dimension of the style vector $w$ must match the dimension of the latent motion vectors $d_i$ (512 in the source implementation) for summation to be valid [S1].
*   **Layer Count:** The number of motion vectors $N$ must exactly match the number of layers in the synthesis module $G_s$ to ensure proper modulation at every stage [S1].

## Subtopics

### Offline Motion Representation Collection
In the offline step, motion representations $m$ are collected using a regressor $R$ [S1]. This regressor extracts coefficients of a blendshape basis from a statistical head model to provide a low-dimensional motion representation for face dynamics [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Convergence:** This method accelerates the convergence of the motion reconstruction loss $L_{motion}$ compared to prior concatenation strategies [S1].
*   **Generalization:** Unlike methods that train one model per identity [13], this approach generalizes across different identities by using a single generator with motion control [S1].
*   **Consistency:** The method ensures consistency of background, motion features, and subject-specific attributes (lighting, texture, shape, albedo, identity) by incorporating a face parsing net, head regressor, and identity encoder [S1].

## Background
Layered Latent Motion Conditioning was proposed in the context of Controllable Radiance Fields (CoRF) for dynamic face synthesis [S1]. It draws inspiration from GAN-based image inversion and editing work, specifically the use of extended latent spaces (like W+) for editing and expression transfer [S1]. The method was introduced to address the limitation of prior neural implicit head reconstruction methods that required training a separate model for each face identity [S1].
