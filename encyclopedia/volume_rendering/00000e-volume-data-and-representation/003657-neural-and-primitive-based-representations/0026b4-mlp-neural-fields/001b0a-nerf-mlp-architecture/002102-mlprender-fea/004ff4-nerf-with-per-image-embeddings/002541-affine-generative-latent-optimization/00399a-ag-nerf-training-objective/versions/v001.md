# AG-NeRF Training Objective

Also called: Affine Generative Latent Optimization

## Overview
The AG-NeRF training objective defines the loss function and optimization strategy for the Affine Generative Latent Optimization method, which synthesizes novel views of large-scale outdoor scenes by leveraging source image priors. It distinguishes itself from vanilla NeRF [1] and multi-stage methods like BungeeNeRF [7] by using an attention-based feature fusion module to integrate features from a set of $V$ source images, enabling end-to-end training that converges significantly faster (1 day vs. 5 days on RTX 4090) while achieving higher reconstruction quality (approx. 6 dB PSNR gain over BungeeNeRF).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Ray $r$ defined by origin and direction.
    *   3D point coordinates $q_k = (x, y, z)$ and viewing directions $d_k = (\theta, \phi)$ for $N$ sample points along the ray.
    *   A set of $V$ source images and their corresponding camera parameters.
    *   Ground truth color $C(r)$ for the ray.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Rendered color $\tilde{C}(r)$.
    *   Training loss $L$.

**Data Structures**
*   **Feature Vector $f_{att}(q_k)$:** A fused feature vector of dimension $d_{fi} = 35$ associated with 3D point $q_k$, derived from source images via an attention-based feature extraction and fusion module.
*   **MLP1:** A 4-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron with 64 feature dimensions per layer.
*   **MLP2:** A 1-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron with 64 feature dimensions.
*   **Sample Points:** Two sets of points per ray: coarse set of size $N_c = 64$ and fine set of size $N_f = 64$.

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Feature Extraction and Fusion:** For each 3D point $q_k$, extract features from $V$ source images and fuse them into $f_{att}(q_k)$ using an attention mechanism. [S1]
2.  **Positional Encoding:** Map the 3D coordinates $q_k$ and direction $d_k$ to high-dimensional space using positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$. [S1]
3.  **Density and Hidden Feature Prediction:** Concatenate $\gamma(q_k)$ and $f_{att}(q_k)$, then pass through MLP1 to obtain density $\sigma_k$ and hidden feature $h_k$:
    $$ \sigma_k, h_k = \text{MLP1}(\gamma(q_k), f_{att}(q_k)) $$
    [S1]
4.  **Color Prediction:** Concatenate $\gamma(d_k)$ and $h_k$, then pass through MLP2 to obtain color $c_k$:
    $$ c_k = \text{MLP2}(\gamma(d_k), h_k) $$
    [S1]
5.  **Volume Rendering (Coarse and Fine):** Render the predicted color $\tilde{C}(r)$ for both coarse and fine sample sets using the volume rendering equation:
    $$ \tilde{C}(r) = \sum_{k=1}^{N} T_k (1 - \exp(-\sigma_k \delta_k)) c_k $$
    where $\delta_k$ is the distance between adjacent sample points, and $T_k$ is the accumulated transmittance:
    $$ T_k = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right) $$
    [S1]
6.  **Loss Calculation:** Compute the total squared error between the rendered colors ($\tilde{C}_c(r)$ for coarse, $\tilde{C}_f(r)$ for fine) and the ground truth color $C(r)$ over a batch of rays $R$:
    $$ L = \sum_{r \in R} \left( \| C(r) - \tilde{C}_c(r) \|^2 + \| C(r) - \tilde{C}_f(r) \|^2 \right) $$
    [S1]
7.  **Optimization:** Minimize $L$ using the Adam optimizer.

**Parameters**
*   $N_c = 64$: Number of coarse sample points per ray. [S1]
*   $N_f = 64$: Number of fine sample points per ray. [S1]
*   $V = 10$: Number of source images used. [S1]
*   $d_{fi} = 35$: Dimension of the fused feature vector. [S1]
*   MLP1: 4 layers, 64 dimensions. [S1]
*   MLP2: 1 layer, 64 dimensions. [S1]
*   Learning Rate: $10^{-3}$ for the feature extraction network; $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for the MLPs. [S1]
*   Total Iterations: 200,000. [S1]
*   Rays per Iteration: 2,048. [S1]

**Pitfalls**
*   **Source Image Dependency:** The attention-based feature extraction and fusion module fails to function without source image inputs. Removing source images ($V=0$) degrades performance by approximately 11 dB in PSNR compared to the full model. [S1]
*   **Source Image Count:** While increasing source images from 2 to 14 yields about 1.3 dB PSNR gain on the 56 Leonard dataset, the marginal improvement beyond 10 images is tiny. Using more than 10 images increases computational cost without significant quality gains. [S1]
*   **Pooling Alternatives:** Replacing the attention mechanism with average pooling or max pooling results in approximately 1.7 dB and 1.5 dB PSNR decreases, respectively. [S1]

## Subtopics

### [AG-NeRF](8545-ag-nerf.md)
An end-to-end framework for novel view synthesis of large-scale outdoor scenes that handles varying camera altitudes (from drone-level to satellite-level) without requiring a priori camera height or scene scope, reducing training cost compared to multi-stage methods like BungeeNeRF.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Training Time:** AG-NeRF converges in approximately 1 day on a single RTX 4090 GPU, compared to up to 5 days for BungeeNeRF [7]. [S1]
*   **Performance:** On the 56 Leonard and Transamerica datasets, AG-NeRF achieves approximately 6 dB higher PSNR than BungeeNeRF. [S1]
*   **Efficiency:** With only 4,000 iterations (approx. 30 minutes), AG-NeRF achieves competitive performance with BungeeNeRF, demonstrating rapid geometry acquisition due to source image priors. [S1]
*   **Scalability:** The method balances computational efficiency and rendering quality by limiting source images to $V=10$. [S1]
*   **Limitations:** Performance is heavily dependent on the availability and quality of source images; without them, the method reverts to vanilla NeRF performance with significant degradation. [S1]

## Background
AG-NeRF was proposed by Guo, Jingfeng, et al. to address the challenges of rendering large-scale outdoor scenes captured at varying altitudes (drone to satellite). Traditional NeRF-based approaches require extensive training time and struggle with multi-scale scenes. AG-NeRF introduces an attention-guided mechanism to leverage source image priors, enabling end-to-end training that is both faster and more accurate than previous state-of-the-art methods like BungeeNeRF. [S1]
