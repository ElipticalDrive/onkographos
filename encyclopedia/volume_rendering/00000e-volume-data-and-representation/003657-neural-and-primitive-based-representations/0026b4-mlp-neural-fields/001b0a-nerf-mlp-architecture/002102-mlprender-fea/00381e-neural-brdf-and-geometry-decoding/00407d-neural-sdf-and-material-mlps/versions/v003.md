# Neural SDF and Material MLPs

Also called: IRON Pipeline Neural Architectures, IRON SDF and Material Networks, Neural SDF

## Overview
This method defines the specific multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architectures used to decode signed distance functions (SDF) and spatially-varying material properties (diffuse albedo, specular albedo, roughness) from 3D coordinates in neural implicit surface reconstruction pipelines, most notably the IRON framework [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of jointly optimizing geometry and physically-based appearance by separating the decoding of geometric shape from material reflectance into distinct networks [S1]. It is distinguished from single-MLP approaches by explicitly passing high-level geometric features from the geometry network to the material network, which improves training stability and rendering fidelity [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
The method takes a 3D coordinate $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ as the primary input. Depending on the specific decoder, it may also take surface normals $n$, viewing directions, or geometric feature vectors $f$ from the SDF network.
- The **SDF MLP** outputs a scalar signed distance value $s$ and an implicit local geometric feature vector $f_{geo}$ [S1, S2].
- The **Material MLPs** output material parameters: diffuse albedo $\rho_d$, specular albedo $\rho_s$ (or $\kappa$), and roughness $\rho$ (or $\alpha$) [S1, S2, S3].

**Data Structures:**
The core data structure is a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The networks are defined by their depth (number of layers), width (hidden dimension), and skip connections.
- **SDF MLP:** 8 layers, width 256, skip connection at layer 4 [S1, S2].
- **Diffuse Albedo MLP:** 8 layers, width 256, skip connection at layer 4 [S1, S2].
- **Specular Albedo MLP:** 4 layers, width 256 [S1, S2].
- **Roughness MLP:** 4 layers, width 256 [S1, S2].

**Positional Encoding:**
Inputs are encoded using positional encoding to compensate for the spectral bias of MLPs [S1]. The number of frequencies varies by input type and network:
- SDF input $x$: 6 frequencies [S1, S2].
- Diffuse Albedo input $x$: 10 frequencies [S1, S2].
- Diffuse Albedo input $n$ (normal): 4 frequencies [S1, S2].
- Specular Albedo input $x$: 6 frequencies [S1, S2].
- Roughness input $x$: 6 frequencies [S1, S2].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Encode Input:** Apply positional encoding to the 3D coordinate $x$ (and $n$ if applicable) using the specified number of frequencies.
2. **Geometry Decoding (SDF):** Pass the encoded $x$ through the 8-layer SDF MLP. The output is the tuple $(s, f_{geo})$, where $s$ is the SDF value and $f_{geo}$ is the geometric feature vector [S1, S2].
3. **Material Decoding:** Pass the encoded $x$ (and $f_{geo}$, $n$ as required by the specific material decoder) through the respective 4-layer or 8-layer MLPs to obtain material parameters [S1, S2].
4. **Parameterization (vMF Variant):** For the von Mises-Fisher (vMF) BRDF approximation, the material MLP $M$ outputs roughness $\rho \in [0, 1]$, diffuse color $C_d \in [0, 1]$, and specular tint $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ using a softplus activation [S3].
5. **Reflection Direction:** Compute the reflection direction $l_r$ using the view direction $d$ and surface normal $n$:
   $$ l_r = 2(-d \cdot n)n + d $$
   [S3]
6. **BRDF Approximation:** Approximate the BRDF $f(l, -d, x_s)$ as a von Mises-Fisher distribution $G(l, l_r, x_s)$:
   $$ f(l, -d, x_s) \approx G(l, l_r, x_s) = \alpha \exp(\rho(x_s)(l \cdot l_r - 1)) $$
   [S3]

    d: view direction

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Spectral Bias:** Positional encoding is critical to compensate for the spectral bias of MLPs [S1].
- **Initialization:** Geometric initialization is performed before optimization [S2].
- **Activation:** Material parameters ($\rho, C_d, \alpha$) use softplus activation to ensure non-negative outputs [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Geometry and material MLP architecture](14140-geometry-and-material-mlp-architecture.md)
This architecture defines the neural network components for decoding signed distance functions (SDF) and material properties (diffuse albedo, specular coefficients) in neural implicit surface reconstruction. It separates geometry and material decoding into two distinct multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) to improve training stability and rendering fidelity. It is distinguished from single-MLP approaches by its explicit feature vector passing from the geometry network to the material network, allowing the material decoder to access high-level geometric context. See [Geometry and material MLP architecture](14140-geometry-and-material-mlp-architecture.md).

### [Neural diffuse albedo](15344-neural-diffuse-albedo.md)
Neural diffuse albedo is a method for predicting the spatially-varying diffuse reflectance ($\rho_d$) of a surface point using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). It solves the problem of recovering physically-based material properties from inverse rendering pipelines by mapping 3D geometry and local features to albedo values. It is distinguished from sibling material decoders by its specific input composition, which includes surface normals and geometric features, and its higher-frequency positional encoding for coordinates compared to specular or roughness predictors. See [Neural diffuse albedo](15344-neural-diffuse-albedo.md).

### Material Parameterization with vMF BRDF
This variant parameterizes the spatially-varying material of a surface point $x_s$ as roughness $\rho \in [0, 1]$, diffuse color $C_d \in [0, 1]$, and specular tint $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, output by a material MLP network $M$ using softplus activation [S3]. It approximates the BRDF as a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution, defined on the unit sphere with a normalized spherical Gaussian, assuming rotational symmetry with respect to the reflection direction $l_r$ [S3]. The BRDF is computed as $f(l, -d, x_s) \approx \alpha \exp(\rho(x_s)(l \cdot l_r - 1))$ [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Inference is $O(L \cdot W^2)$ per sample, where $L$ is the number of layers and $W$ is the width (256). This is comparable to other MLP-based neural fields.
- **Memory Complexity:** The model size is dominated by the weight matrices. For the SDF MLP: $8 \times 256 \times 256$ weights (plus biases). For material MLPs: $4 \times 256 \times 256$ or $8 \times 256 \times 256$. Total parameters are in the millions, fitting easily in GPU memory.
- **Performance:** The IRON pipeline trains for 100k iterations using 512 randomly sampled pixels per iteration with $\ell_1$ image loss and eikonal regularization loss ($\lambda_1 = 0.1$) [S1].
- **Strengths:** Separating geometry and material decoding improves training stability and allows for more accurate material recovery [S1]. The vMF approximation provides a physically plausible and computationally efficient BRDF model [S3].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful initialization and regularization (eikonal loss) for stable SDF learning [S1, S2]. The vMF approximation assumes rotational symmetry, which may not hold for all anisotropic materials [S3].

## Background
The method originates from the IRON (Inverse Rendering by Optimizing Neural SDFs and Materials) pipeline [S1] and has been adopted in subsequent works like PIR (Photometric Inverse Rendering with Shading Cues Modeling and Surface Reflectance Regularization) [S2] and NeIF (Neural Illumination Fields) [S3]. It builds on the general NeRF MLP architecture [Parent] but specializes in surface-based representations (SDFs) and physically-based material decoding. The use of positional encoding to mitigate spectral bias is a standard technique in neural fields [S1]. The vMF BRDF approximation is based on established computer graphics literature [S3].
