# Neural SDF and Material MLPs

Also called: IRON Pipeline Neural Architectures, IRON SDF and Material Networks

## Overview
This method defines the specific multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architectures used to decode signed distance functions (SDF) and spatially-varying material properties (diffuse albedo, specular albedo, roughness) within neural implicit surface reconstruction pipelines, notably the IRON framework [S1]. It addresses the problem of jointly optimizing geometry and appearance from photometric images by separating the decoding of geometric context from material parameters [S1]. It differs from single-MLP approaches by explicitly passing high-level geometric features from the SDF network to the material networks, thereby improving training stability and rendering fidelity [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
The method takes a 3D spatial location $x$ and, for material decoding, the surface normal $n$ and geometric features $f$ as inputs. It outputs:
1.  A scalar SDF value $S$ and a feature vector $f$ from the geometry network.
2.  Diffuse albedo $\beta$, specular albedo $\kappa$, and roughness $\alpha$ from the respective material networks.

**Data Structures:**
The networks are standard Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). The specific architectures are defined by layer count, width, skip connections, and positional encoding (PE) frequencies.

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Positional Encoding of Input Coordinates:**
    The input 3D location $x$ is encoded using positional encoding. The number of frequencies varies by network:
    *   SDF MLP: 6 frequencies [S1].
    *   Diffuse Albedo MLP: 10 frequencies [S1].
    *   Specular Albedo MLP: 6 frequencies [S1].
    *   Roughness MLP: 6 frequencies [S1].
    *   Surface Normal $n$ (for diffuse MLP): 4 frequencies [S1].

2.  **Geometry Decoding (SDF MLP):**
    *   **Architecture:** 8 layers, width 256.
    *   **Skip Connections:** Added at layer 4 (input to layers 5–8 is the concatenation of the layer 4 output and the original input $x$) [S1].
    *   **Process:** The encoded $x$ is passed through the MLP to produce the SDF value $S$ and the geometric feature vector $f$ [S1].

3.  **Material Decoding:**
    The geometric feature vector $f$ from the SDF MLP is passed as input to the material networks [S1].
    *   **Diffuse Albedo MLP ($\beta_{\Theta_\beta}$):**
        *   **Inputs:** Encoded $x$ (10 freq), surface normal $n$ (4 freq), and feature vector $f$ [S1].
        *   **Architecture:** 8 layers, width 256, skip connection at layer 4 [S1].
        *   **Output:** Diffuse albedo $\beta$ [S1].
    *   **Specular Albedo MLP ($\kappa_{\Theta_\kappa}$):**
        *   **Inputs:** Encoded $x$ (6 freq) and feature vector $f$ [S1].
        *   **Architecture:** 4 layers, width 256 [S1].
        *   **Output:** Specular albedo $\kappa$ [S1].
    *   **Roughness MLP ($\alpha_{\Theta_\alpha}$):**
        *   **Inputs:** Encoded $x$ (6 freq) and feature vector $f$ [S1].
        *   **Architecture:** 4 layers, width 256 [S1].
        *   **Output:** Roughness $\alpha$ [S1].

4.  **BRDF Approximation (vMF Variant):**
    In variants using von Mises-Fisher (vMF) parameterization [S2]:
    *   The material MLP outputs roughness $\rho \in [0, 1]$, diffuse color $C_d \in [0, 1]$, and specular tint $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ using a softplus activation [S2].
    *   The reflection direction $l_r$ is computed from the view direction $d$ and normal $n$:
        $$ l_r = 2(-d \cdot n)n + d $$
    *   The BRDF $f(l, -d, x_s)$ is approximated by a normalized spherical Gaussian (vMF distribution) [S2]:
        $$ f(l, -d, x_s) \approx G(l, l_r, x_s) = \alpha \exp(\rho(x_s)(l \cdot l_r - 1)) $$
    *   Here, $l$ is the light direction, and the distribution is rotationally symmetric with respect to $l_r$ [S2].

**Parameters and Typical Values:**
*   MLP Width: 256 [S1].
*   SDF Layers: 8 [S1].
*   Diffuse Layers: 8 [S1].
*   Specular/Roughness Layers: 4 [S1].
*   SDF PE Frequencies: 6 [S1].
*   Diffuse PE Frequencies (x): 10 [S1].
*   Diffuse PE Frequencies (n): 4 [S1].
*   Specular/Roughness PE Frequencies: 6 [S1].
*   Softplus activation is used for material outputs in the vMF variant [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Spectral Bias:** Positional encoding is required to compensate for the spectral bias of MLPs [S1].
*   **Feature Passing:** The material networks rely on the feature vector $f$ from the SDF network; omitting this link degrades the separation of geometry and material [S1].
*   **Activation:** The choice of activation function (e.g., softplus for vMF outputs) ensures parameters remain within valid physical ranges (e.g., non-negative roughness) [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Geometry and material MLP architecture](14140-geometry-and-material-mlp-architecture.md)
This architecture defines the neural network components for decoding signed distance functions (SDF) and material properties (diffuse albedo, specular coefficients) in neural implicit surface reconstruction. It separates geometry and material decoding into two distinct multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) to improve training stability and rendering fidelity. It is distinguished from single-MLP approaches by its explicit feature vector passing from the geometry network to the material network, allowing the material decoder to access high-level geometric context.

### Material Parameterization with vMF BRDF
This variant parameterizes the spatially-varying material of a surface point $x_s$ as roughness $\rho \in [0, 1]$, diffuse color $C_d \in [0, 1]$, and specular tint $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, output by a material MLP network $M$ using softplus activation: $M: x_s \rightarrow (\rho, C_d, \alpha)$ [S2]. It approximates the BRDF as a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution, assuming rotational symmetry with respect to the reflection direction $l_r$. The BRDF is defined as $f(l, -d, x_s) \approx G(l, l_r, x_s) = \alpha \exp(\rho(x_s)(l \cdot l_r - 1))$, where $l_r = 2(-d \cdot n)n + d$ [S2].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
Not given in the sources. The complexity is dominated by the forward pass of the MLPs, which scales linearly with the number of layers and width.

**Measured Performance:**
Not given in the sources.

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Explicit separation of geometry and material features improves training stability and rendering fidelity compared to single-MLP approaches [S1]. The use of positional encoding mitigates spectral bias [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires careful tuning of positional encoding frequencies and MLP depths [S1]. The vMF approximation assumes rotational symmetry, which may not hold for all anisotropic materials [S2].

**When to Use:**
Use when joint optimization of geometry and appearance is required, and high-fidelity material decoding is needed [S1]. Use the vMF variant when a physically-based, directionally-aware BRDF approximation is desired [S2].

## Background
The method originates from the IRON (Inverse Rendering by Optimizing Neural SDFs and Materials) pipeline [S1]. It was developed to address the challenges of inverse rendering by optimizing neural SDFs and materials from photometric images [S1]. The vMF BRDF approximation is also utilized in Neural Illumination Fields (NeIF) for modeling directional emitted rays and environment occlusions [S2].
