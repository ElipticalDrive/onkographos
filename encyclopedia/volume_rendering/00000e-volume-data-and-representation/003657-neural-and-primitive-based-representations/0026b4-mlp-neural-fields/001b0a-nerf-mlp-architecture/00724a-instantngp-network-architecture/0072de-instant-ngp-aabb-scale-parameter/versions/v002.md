# Instant-NGP AABB Scale Parameter

Also called: AABB scale, aabb_scale parameter, aabb_scale parameter tuning, axis-aligned bounding box scale, bounding box scale factor

## Overview
The `aabb_scale` parameter controls the spatial extent of the axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) used for spatial hashing and grid construction in Instant-NGP [S1]. It scales the scene so that camera positions average 1 unit from the origin when set to 1 [S1]. Unlike standard SfM-MVS pipelines where mesh definition is highly detailed, Instant-NGP models show lower definition but remain comparable when `aabb_scale` is optimized to 8 or 16 [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** The `aabb_scale` integer parameter, typically a power of 2 [S1].
*   **Output:** A scaled coordinate system for the neural field, affecting the spatial hashing grid resolution and the resulting visual quality (noise levels) of the rendered model [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The parameter modifies the scale of the axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) used for spatial hashing and grid construction [S1]. The exact internal data structure layout is defined in [Unbounded Hash-Grid with Negative Integer Mapping](13889-unbounded-hash-grid-with-negative-integer-mapping.md) [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  Determine the `aabb_scale` value based on scene size [S1].
2.  For small synthetic scenes (e.g., original NeRF dataset), set `aabb_scale` to 1 for fastest training [S1].
3.  For larger scenes, set `aabb_scale` to a power of 2 (up to 128) to extend the bounding box [S1].
4.  Upload the transformed JSON file to Instant-NGP [S1].
5.  Visualize model construction to evaluate the resulting model definition and noise levels [S1].
6.  Select a value of 8 or 16 for optimal results [S1].
7.  Avoid values of 1 and 128 due to high negative influence on quality [S1].
8.  Use the built-in tool to transform the voxel representation into a 3D mesh and select the region of interest if further refinement is needed [S1].

**Formulas:**
The scene is scaled such that camera positions average 1 unit from the origin when `aabb_scale` is 1 [S1]. By setting `aabb_scale` to a power of 2 up to a maximum of 128, the NeRF model extends the selection rectangle to a much larger spatial extent [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   `aabb_scale`: Integer. Default is 1. Acceptable values are powers of 2 up to a maximum of 128 [S1].
*   Typical tested values: 1, 8, 16, 128 [S1].
*   Recommended optimal values: 8 or 16 [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Low `aabb_scale` (e.g., 1):** Causes increased noise, with a small base noise detected on each object that can be mitigated by choosing an appropriate value or graphically selecting the object [S1]. Noise increases as `aabb_scale` decreases [S1].
*   **High `aabb_scale` (e.g., 128):** Degrades quality due to high negative influence on the model definition [S1].
*   **Complex Acquisitions:** Datasets with 360-degree acquisition (e.g., Buddha dataset) exhibit higher noise, making artifact elimination complex and sometimes requiring external editing software after mesh export [S1].
*   **Training Speed:** Increasing `aabb_scale` slightly influences training speed [S1].
*   **Computational Stability:** No computational problems were observed across the tested range [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Increasing `aabb_scale` slightly influences training speed [S1]. No computational problems were observed across the tested range [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** On the Buddha dataset (360-degree acquisition), noise was highest, requiring external editing for artifact removal [S1]. For general photogrammetric purposes, `aabb_scale` of 8 or 16 yields optimal results [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The parameter scales linearly with the spatial extent, with 128 extending the selection rectangle significantly compared to 1 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Allows adaptation to various scene sizes and maintains comparable definition to SfM-MVS when optimized [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Suboptimal values (1 for large scenes, 128) cause significant noise or quality loss, and overall definition remains lower than standard SfM-MVS pipelines [S1].
*   **When to use:** Use 1 for small synthetic scenes, and 8 or 16 for larger, real-world scenes [S1].
*   **When not to use:** Do not use 128 for standard scenes due to quality degradation, and avoid 1 for large scenes due to noise [S1].

## Background
The `aabb_scale` parameter is part of the Instant-NGP architecture, which uses neural fields for fast rendering [S1]. It is particularly relevant in the context of integrating Instant-NGP with photogrammetric pipelines like Colmap, where initial camera poses and scene scale are determined [S1]. The impact of this parameter on 3D model quality for photogrammetric purposes has been documented, highlighting trade-offs between noise, resolution, and training speed [S1].
