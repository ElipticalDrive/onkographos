# Cloud Physics: Humidity and Saturation

Also called: Absolute Humidity, Relative Humidity, Saturation, Saturated Mixing Ratio

## Overview
This method defines the thermodynamic state of water vapor within an air parcel to determine saturation, condensation, and evaporation. It solves the problem of identifying when water vapor transitions into liquid water, which is a prerequisite for cloud formation and precipitation in physical cloud models. It differs from [Cloud Physics: Temperature and Buoyancy](19845-cloud-physics-temperature-and-buoyancy.md) by focusing on mass concentration ratios rather than thermal energy and vertical forces, and from [Hybrid Physics-Driven Procedural Model](19848-hybrid-physics-driven-procedural-model.md) by providing the fundamental physical constraints rather than the procedural noise generation.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Atmospheric pressure ($P$), saturated vapor pressure ($e_s$), current absolute humidity (mass of water vapor per mass of dry air).
*   **Outputs:** Saturated mixing ratio ($\omega_s$), relative humidity status (saturated/unsaturated).

**Data Structures:**
No complex data structures are required. The method operates on scalar physical quantities.

**Formulas and Definitions:**
1.  **Absolute Humidity:** Defined as the concentration of water vapor, measured by the mass of water vapor divided by the mass of dry air in a volume at a given temperature [S1].
2.  **Relative Humidity:** Defined as the ratio of the current absolute humidity to the highest possible maximum humidity [S1].
3.  **Saturation Threshold:** At 100% relative humidity, the air is saturated. The threshold for saturation is the saturated mixing ratio ($\omega_s$) [S1].
4.  **Saturated Mixing Ratio Formula:**
    $$ \omega_s = 621.97 \left( \frac{e_s}{P - e_s} \right) $$
    Where:
    *   $\omega_s$ is the saturated mixing ratio.
    *   $e_s$ is the saturated vapor pressure.
    *   $P$ is the atmospheric pressure.
    *   The constant $621.97$ is derived from the ratio of molecular weights of water vapor to dry air [S1].

**Algorithm:**
1.  Calculate the saturated vapor pressure ($e_s$) based on the current temperature of the air parcel.
2.  Retrieve the local atmospheric pressure ($P$).
3.  Compute the saturated mixing ratio $\omega_s$ using the formula above.
4.  Compare the current absolute humidity ($\omega$) against $\omega_s$.
5.  If $\omega > \omega_s$, the air is supersaturated; the excess water vapor condenses into liquid water [S1].
6.  If $\omega \le \omega_s$, the air is unsaturated or saturated; no condensation occurs.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Division by Zero:** If $P = e_s$, the denominator becomes zero. This occurs at the boiling point or extreme low-pressure conditions. In practice, $P$ must always be greater than $e_s$ for liquid water stability [S1].
*   **Precision:** The constant $621.97$ is specific to the units used (typically grams of water per kilogram of dry air, or dimensionless depending on normalization). Ensure unit consistency between $e_s$ and $P$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def calculate_saturation_state(atmospheric_pressure, saturated_vapor_pressure, current_absolute_humidity):
    """
    Determines if air is saturated and calculates the saturation threshold.
    
    Args:
        atmospheric_pressure (float): P, total atmospheric pressure.
        saturated_vapor_pressure (float): es, saturated vapor pressure at current temperature.
        current_absolute_humidity (float): omega, current mass of water vapor per mass of dry air.
        
    Returns:
        dict: Contains saturated_mixing_ratio, is_saturated, and excess_vapor.
    """
    # Guard against division by zero or invalid pressure states
    if atmospheric_pressure <= saturated_vapor_pressure:
        # Physically, liquid water cannot exist if P <= es in this context
        # Treat as fully saturated with undefined excess
        return {
            "saturated_mixing_ratio": float('inf'),
            "is_saturated": True,
            "excess_vapor": 0.0
        }

    # Calculate saturated mixing ratio using the standard formula
    # Constant 621.97 is derived from molecular weight ratios [S1]
    saturated_mixing_ratio = 621.97 * (saturated_vapor_pressure / (atmospheric_pressure - saturated_vapor_pressure))

    # Determine saturation status
    is_saturated = current_absolute_humidity >= saturated_mixing_ratio
    
    # Calculate excess vapor that would condense
    excess_vapor = max(0.0, current_absolute_humidity - saturated_mixing_ratio)

    return {
        "saturated_mixing_ratio": saturated_mixing_ratio,
        "is_saturated": is_saturated,
        "excess_vapor": excess_vapor
    }
```

## Subtopics

### Cloud Physics: Thermodynamics
This variant extends the humidity model by accounting for the energy changes during phase transitions. It defines the latent heat of condensation ($L_c$), which is the heat released when water vapor condenses to liquid water, and the latent heat of evaporation ($L_e$), which is the heat absorbed during evaporation [S1]. It also defines the specific heat of air ($C_p$), the energy required to raise the temperature of air by one degree Celsius [S1]. These parameters are required to compute the temperature changes associated with evaporation and condensation within the air parcel [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
The calculation is $O(1)$ time and $O(1)$ memory per air parcel, as it involves only basic arithmetic operations on scalar values.

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Provides a physically accurate threshold for cloud formation and precipitation. Simple to implement.
*   **Limitations:** Does not account for dynamic temperature changes caused by latent heat release without the companion Thermodynamics model. Requires accurate estimation of $e_s$ from temperature, which is not defined in this article alone.

**When to Use:**
Use this method when implementing the mass conservation aspect of a physical cloud simulation, specifically to determine when water vapor should be converted to liquid water content.

## Background
The concepts of absolute humidity, relative humidity, and saturated mixing ratio are standard meteorological definitions adopted into computer graphics for physically based cloud simulation. The specific formula for saturated mixing ratio using the constant $621.97$ is cited from general atmospheric physics literature referenced in the survey [S1]. The integration of these concepts into GPU-based cloud simulation allows for the generation of realistic cumulus clouds by modeling the condensation process [S1].
