# Procedural Volume Cloud Modeling

Also called: Cloud Density Field Generation, Density Sampling and Weather

## Overview
Procedural Volume Cloud Modeling constructs the volumetric density field of clouds by evaluating mathematical functions and sampling noise textures at arbitrary 3D coordinates, rather than relying on pre-baked voxel grids or static meshes [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of generating infinite-resolution, seamless, and physically plausible cloud structures that can be queried during raymarching [S2, unofficial]. It differs from [Volumetric Cloud Raymarching](1248-volumetric-cloud-raymarching.md) by focusing on the *generation* of the scalar field $f(\mathbf{x})$ rather than the integration of light through it, and from [Atmospheric Height Gradient](4813-atmospheric-height-gradient.md) by incorporating spatially varying weather maps and multi-octave noise erosion rather than a simple vertical falloff [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method evaluates a scalar density field $D(\mathbf{x})$ for a query point $\mathbf{x}$ in world space. The output is a single float value representing optical density, which is typically replicated to RGB for rendering [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
1.  **Cloud Map**: A 2D texture (typically $256 \times 256$ or $512 \times 512$) with 4 channels (RGBA) [S2, unofficial].
    *   **R (Coverage)**: Controls the global presence of clouds (0.0 to 1.0) [S2, unofficial].
    *   **G (Type)**: Defines the cloud classification (0.0 to 1.0) [S2, unofficial].
    *   **B (Erosion)**: Controls the intensity of edge erosion for the specific cloud type [S2, unofficial].
    *   **A (Wetness)**: Controls the absorption/scattering properties associated with precipitation [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Cloud Lut**: A 1D or 2D lookup table defining distribution of cloud types and heights [S2, unofficial].
    *   **R (Density)**: Base density multiplier for the type.
    *   **G (Erosion)**: Additional erosion factor affecting both shape and detail noise [S2, unofficial].
    *   **B (Ambient Occlusion)**: Shadowing factor for the cloud layer.
3.  **Noise Textures**:
    *   **Shape Noise**: Fractal Perlin-Worley noise texture [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Detail Noise**: Fractal Perlin noise texture [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Constraint**: All noise textures must be tileable to support seamless tiling across kilometers [S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Height ($z$)**: Vertical coordinate in world space.
*   **Wind Vector ($\mathbf{w}$)**: 2D or 3D vector controlling temporal displacement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Time ($t$)**: Global simulation time for flow [S2, unofficial].
*   **Noise Scale ($S$)**: Frequency multiplier for base noise (low frequency for large structures) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Erosion Scale ($E$)**: Frequency multiplier for detail noise (high frequency) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Base Height ($z_{base}$)**: Altitude where cloud density begins [S1, unofficial].
*   **Top Height ($z_{top}$)**: Altitude where cloud density ends [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Weather Map Sampling**:
    Sample the Cloud Map using the horizontal world coordinates $(x, y)$ of the query point $\mathbf{x}$. Retrieve Coverage ($C$), Type ($T$), Erosion ($E_{map}$), and Wetness ($W$) [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{Weather} = \text{Sample2D}(\text{CloudMap}, (x, y)) $$

2.  **Cloud Type Mapping**:
    Map the Type value $T$ to a specific cloud layer configuration [S2, unofficial]:
    *   $0.0 \le T < 0.125$: Cirrus (High altitude, thin)
    *   $0.125 \le T < 0.25$: Cirrus + Cumulus (Mixed layer)
    *   $0.25 \le T < 0.5$: Cumulus (Mid altitude, puffy)
    *   $0.5 \le T \le 1.0$: Cumulonimbus (Low to high altitude, complex)
    *   *Note*: For complex types like Cumulonimbus, coverage and erosion are adjusted dynamically based on height: high altitude has low coverage and high erosion; low altitude has high coverage and low erosion [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Height Gradient Attenuation**:
    Calculate vertical falloff to enforce flat bases and rounded tops [S1, unofficial]. Let $h$ be the normalized height within the cloud layer:
    $$ h = \frac{z - z_{base}}{z_{top} - z_{base}} $$
    Apply a vertical remap function $H(h)$. A common implementation uses a smoothstep or polynomial curve to zero out density at the base and taper it at the top [S1, unofficial].
    $$ D_{height} = H(h) $$

4.  **Base Noise Sampling (Shape)**:
    Sample the Shape Noise texture using low-frequency coordinates to generate the basic cloud structure [S2, unofficial]. Displace coordinates by wind and time [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{shape} = (x \cdot S + w_x \cdot t, y \cdot S + w_y \cdot t, z \cdot S_z) $$
    $$ N_{shape} = \text{Sample3D}(\text{ShapeNoise}, \mathbf{p}_{shape}) $$
    The Shape Noise is typically a Fractal Brownian Motion (FBM) of Perlin-Worley noise [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Detail Noise Sampling (Erosion)**:
    Sample the Detail Noise texture using high-frequency coordinates to erode edges and add fine detail [S2, unofficial]. This noise is typically Fractal Perlin noise [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{detail} = (x \cdot E + w_x \cdot t \cdot k, y \cdot E + w_y \cdot t \cdot k, z \cdot E_z) $$
    $$ N_{detail} = \text{Sample3D}(\text{DetailNoise}, \mathbf{p}_{detail}) $$
    The erosion intensity is modulated by the Erosion value from the Cloud Map and Cloud Lut [S2, unofficial].

6.  **Composition and Erosion**:
    Combine the base shape with the erosion. The erosion noise $N_{detail}$ is subtracted or used as a mask to erode $N_{shape}$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ D_{raw} = N_{shape} \cdot (1.0 - \text{ErosionFactor} \cdot N_{detail}) $$
    The ErosionFactor is derived from $E_{map}$ and the Cloud Lut G channel [S2, unofficial].

7.  **Final Density Calculation**:
    Combine all factors. The final density $D(\mathbf{x})$ is:
    $$ D(\mathbf{x}) = C \cdot D_{height} \cdot D_{raw} \cdot \text{LutDensity}(T) $$
    Where $\text{LutDensity}(T)$ is the density multiplier from the Cloud Lut based on the cloud type [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def SampleCloudDensity(x, y, z, time, cloud_map, cloud_lut, shape_noise, detail_noise):
    # 1. Weather Map Sampling
    weather = cloud_map.sample_2d(x, y)
    coverage = weather.r
    cloud_type = weather.g
    erosion_map = weather.b
    wetness = weather.a

    # 2. Cloud Lut Lookup
    lut_entry = cloud_lut.sample_1d(cloud_type)
    lut_density = lut_entry.r
    lut_erosion = lut_entry.g

    # 3. Height Gradient
    # Assuming cloud layer is between z_base and z_top
    height_norm = clamp((z - z_base) / (z_top - z_base), 0.0, 1.0)
    # Flat base, rounded top (e.g., smoothstep or cosine)
    height_falloff = smoothstep(0.0, 0.1, height_norm) * (1.0 - smoothstep(0.8, 1.0, height_norm))
    
    # 4. Base Noise (Shape)
    # Low frequency, Perlin-Worley FBM
    shape_scale = 0.001 # Example low frequency
    wind_offset_shape = vec2(time * 0.1, time * 0.1)
    shape_coord = vec3(x * shape_scale + wind_offset_shape.x, 
                       y * shape_scale + wind_offset_shape.y, 
                       z * shape_scale)
    noise_shape = fractal_perlin_worley(shape_noise, shape_coord, octaves=5)

    # 5. Detail Noise (Erosion)
    # High frequency, Perlin FBM
    erosion_scale = 0.01 # Example high frequency
    wind_offset_detail = vec2(time * 0.2, time * 0.2)
    detail_coord = vec3(x * erosion_scale + wind_offset_detail.x, 
                        y * erosion_scale + wind_offset_detail.y, 
                        z * erosion_scale)
    noise_detail = fractal_perlin(detail_noise, detail_coord, octaves=3)

    # 6. Composition
    # Erosion factor combines map erosion and lut erosion
    erosion_factor = erosion_map + lut_erosion
    # Erode the shape noise with detail noise
    # Note: Specific erosion logic may vary (e.g., min, sub, or mask)
    # Common approach: shape * (1 - erosion * detail)
    density_raw = noise_shape * (1.0 - clamp(erosion_factor, 0.0, 1.0) * noise_detail)

    # 7. Final Density
    final_density = coverage * height_falloff * density_raw * lut_density
    
    return final_density
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Cloud Lut G Channel**: The Erosion channel in the Cloud Lut affects *both* detail noise and shape noise calculations, not just the fine details [S2, unofficial].
*   **Cumulonimbus Height Adjustment**: For Cumulonimbus clouds, static type mapping is insufficient; coverage and erosion must be dynamically adjusted based on the query point's height relative to the cloud base/top [S2, unofficial].
*   **Tiling**: Noise textures must be strictly tileable in X and Y to avoid seams when the camera pans or clouds drift [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision**: Using low coordinates for base noise prevents precision loss over large world distances (kilometers) [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [IsimCloudTexture density evaluation](3530-isimcloudtexture-density-evaluation.md)
IsimCloudTexture density evaluation computes volumetric density by combining geometric primitives (text segments or implicit cloud bodies), fractal Brownian motion (FBM) noise, and depth-based fading. It differs from general procedural gas modeling by focusing on specific cloud structures (text-based or layered bodies) and from simple cloud density sampling by generating the density field procedurally via CUDA kernels rather than sampling pre-baked data. The method outputs a grayscale density value (replicated to RGB) suitable for volume rendering.

### [Procedural Gas Modeling via Turbulence](3551-procedural-gas-modeling-via-turbulence.md)
Procedural Gas Modeling via Turbulence generates volumetric gas density by evaluating a turbulence function and mapping the result through a precomputed power table. It solves the problem of efficiently computing realistic gas density fields for volume rendering by replacing expensive per-sample power function calls with a lookup table. Unlike sibling methods that may rely on 3D textures or distance fields, this approach computes density analytically at any point in space using mathematical shaping functions.

### [Cloud Density Sampling](4806-cloud-density-sampling.md)
Cloud Density Sampling generates the base volumetric density of clouds by combining low-frequency 3D noise textures using a weighted sum (Fractal Brownian Motion, FBM) and remapping the result to control cloud shape and coverage. It differs from its siblings by explicitly separating the generation of the base cloud structure (via low-frequency textures and height gradients) from high-frequency detail (via curl noise displacement and high-frequency textures) and by using a specific channel-weighted FBM from a single RGBA texture sample rather than procedural noise functions. It builds on [Procedural Volume Cloud Modeling](2256-procedural-volume-cloud-modeling.md) by providing the specific evaluation routine for the density field.

### Cumulus Cloud Rendering Configuration
Uses $N = 2$, $M = 32$, and 5 octaves of Perlin noise to simulate enhanced cumulus clouds, with a virtual resolution of $2048^3$ [S3, unofficial]. This configuration is used to encode cloud details for multiple scattering calculations in cloud rendering [S3, unofficial].

### Physical Cloud Model (Cumulus Convection)
A physical model that applies a constant heat source at the base to simulate cumulus convection [S4, unofficial]. Evaporation occurs until air is saturated or all liquid water is evaporated [S4, unofficial]. The saturation mixing ratio is defined by $q_s = A \exp(-\alpha z)$, and the liquid water mixing ratio is $q_l = \max(q - q_s, 0)$ [S4, unofficial]. Parameters include latent heat of vaporization $L_v$, specific heat of air $c_p$, and friction timescale $t_s$ [S4, unofficial]. Boundary conditions set $q_0 = 0.02$ at the bottom and $q_0 = 0.002$ at the top [S4, unofficial]. Coriolis effects are ignored for computational efficiency [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(1)$ per sample, as it involves a fixed number of texture lookups and noise evaluations. However, noise evaluation (especially FBM) is computationally expensive compared to simple linear interpolation [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Low. Requires storing only the Cloud Map, Cloud Lut, and Noise Textures (Shape and Detail). No large voxel grids are needed [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance**: On modern GPUs, procedural noise evaluation can be a bottleneck. For example, generating a $1024^3$ volume with complex noise and shading can result in ~20 FPS without optimization [S3, unofficial]. Using lookup tables (LUTs) for power functions and turbulence can significantly improve performance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Infinite resolution, seamless tiling, low memory footprint, and high artistic control via weather maps and LUTs [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Can appear repetitive if noise parameters are not varied sufficiently; requires careful tuning of noise scales and erosion to avoid "mushy" or "noisy" artifacts; physical accuracy is limited compared to full fluid dynamics simulations [S2, S4, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: Real-time rendering in games and simulations where memory and performance are critical, and artistic control is prioritized over strict physical simulation [S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: When strict physical accuracy of cloud evolution (e.g., convection, precipitation dynamics) is required, or when pre-baked data is available and sufficient [S4, unofficial].

## Background
Procedural cloud modeling has evolved from simple analytic functions to complex noise-based systems. Early methods used analytic shapes or simple height gradients [S2, unofficial]. The introduction of 3D Perlin-Worley noise allowed for more organic, volumetric shapes [S1, unofficial]. Modern implementations, such as those in Unity HDRP, combine these noise techniques with weather maps and LUTs for greater artistic control [S2, unofficial]. Physical models, such as the one by [S4, unofficial], provide a basis for understanding cloud dynamics but are often too expensive for real-time use, leading to hybrid approaches that use procedural noise to approximate physical behaviors [S4, unofficial]. The use of fractal Brownian motion (FBM) and domain warping has become standard for adding detail and turbulence [S1, S5, unofficial].
