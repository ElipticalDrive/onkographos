# Distance to Hypertextures

Also called: Fractal noise deformation, Hypertexture, Hypertexture noise

## Overview
Distance to Hypertextures is a technique for adding geometric relief and procedural detail to surfaces without increasing polygon count, by evaluating procedural texture synthesis within a volumetric region around the object rather than solely on the surface [S1, unofficial]. It deforms implicit surfaces by adding procedural noise to the function's range (value) instead of the domain (coordinates), which enables the use of sphere tracing with safe distance bounds [S2]. This distinguishes it from domain warping methods, which displace input coordinates and often break the Lipschitz continuity required for efficient sphere tracing.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A base Signed Distance Function (SDF) $f(\mathbf{x})$, a query point $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and noise parameters (spectral exponent $\alpha$, number of octaves $n$).
*   **Output:** A deformed SDF value $D(\mathbf{x})$ representing the distance to the hypertextured surface.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Noise Lattice:** An array $\Gamma$ of random unit vectors. For band-limited solid noise, this is typically a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ grid of vectors relative to the query point's integer coordinates [S2].
*   **Base SDF:** A function $f: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying the Lipschitz condition with constant $Lip_f$.

**Formulas:**
1.  **Band-Limited Solid Noise:** The base noise function is defined as a weighted sum of cubic Gaussian approximations centered at lattice points [S2]:
    $$
    \text{noise}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=-1}^{1} \sum_{j=-1}^{1} \sum_{k=-1}^{1} \Gamma(i,j,k) \cdot \phi(\mathbf{x} - (i,j,k))
    $$
    where $\phi$ is the cubic Gaussian approximation (Eq. 36 in [S2]), and $\Gamma(i,j,k)$ are random unit vectors.
2.  **Fractal Noise (Brownian Motion):** To add multi-scale detail, octaves are summed [S2]:
    $$
    \text{noise}_\alpha(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \frac{\text{noise}(2^i \mathbf{x})}{2^{i\alpha}}
    $$
    where $\alpha$ is the spectral exponent.
3.  **Deformed SDF:** The final distance is the base SDF plus the noise [S2]:
    $$
    D(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \text{noise}_\alpha(\mathbf{x})
    $$

**Lipschitz Continuity and Sphere Tracing:**
For sphere tracing to be safe, the resulting function $D(\mathbf{x})$ must be Lipschitz continuous. The Lipschitz constant of the composition is the product of the individual constants [S2]:
$$
Lip_{\text{noise}} = Lip_f \cdot Lip_\Gamma
$$
Given $Lip_f = 3/2$ for the cubic Gaussian approximation and $Lip_\Gamma = 2$ (due to opposing vectors in $\Gamma$), the base noise has $Lip_{\text{noise}} = 3$ [S2].
*   For $1/f$ noise ($\alpha=1$), the Lipschitz constant is the sum of individual octaves, resulting in $Lip_{1/f} = 3$. However, unbounded $1/f$ noise is not Lipschitz; it must be band-limited to a finite number of octaves $n$ to remain valid for sphere tracing [S2].
*   For Brownian motion ($\alpha=2$), the Lipschitz constant is bounded: $Lip_B = 3(2^{1-n} - 1) \approx 6$ [S2].
*   The fractal dimension $D$ of the deformed surface relates to $\alpha$ by $D = 2 + \frac{3 - \alpha}{2}$ for $1 < \alpha < 3$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $\alpha$: Spectral exponent. $\alpha=0$ (white noise), $\alpha=1$ ($1/f$ noise), $\alpha=2$ (Brownian motion) [S2].
*   $n$: Number of octaves. Must be finite for Lipschitz continuity [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Unbounded Noise:** Using infinite octaves for $\alpha=1$ ($1/f$ noise) results in a non-Lipschitz function, making sphere tracing unsafe as the step size cannot be bounded [S2].
*   **Precision:** High-frequency noise requires careful handling of floating-point precision at large coordinates to maintain coherence [S2][S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### On-the-Fly Distance Field & Hypertexture Lookup
A variant where the distance field is derived from a mesh on the fly on the GPU, using the surface vicinity for hypertexture lookups [S3, unofficial]. It aims for high quality by using up to 20 octaves of Perlin noise for shading and complex materials [S3, unofficial]. Performance is limited by complex GPU computations, achieving around 20 FPS for a $1024^3$ volume [S3, unofficial]. In cloud rendering examples, it used $N=2, M=32$ and 5 octaves of Perlin noise for a virtual resolution of $2048^3$ [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** High. Evaluating multiple octaves of solid noise with cubic Gaussian approximations is computationally expensive. On-the-fly variants report ~20 FPS for $1024^3$ volumes due to complex GPU computations [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the implementation. On-the-fly methods may require storing node pools (e.g., 4 MB for $64^3$ entries) and brick pools (e.g., 430 MB) to reference indices [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Adds geometric detail without increasing polygon count; compatible with sphere tracing if band-limited; produces organic, fractal-like details [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive; requires careful parameter tuning to maintain Lipschitz continuity; not suitable for real-time high-resolution rendering without optimization [S3, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high-quality procedural detail is needed on implicit surfaces and performance allows for expensive noise evaluation.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is critical and the scene resolution is very high, or when Lipschitz continuity cannot be guaranteed (e.g., unbounded noise).

## Background
The concept of hypertextures was proposed by Perlin in 1989 [PH89, cited in S1, unofficial][S2]. It was inspired by the idea of evaluating procedural textures within a volumetric region around an object to add relief without geometric cost [S1, unofficial]. The method was later adapted for sphere tracing by Wyvill et al. and others, who formalized the use of band-limited noise to ensure distance bounds [S2]. It is used in procedural volume rendering, cloud simulation, and complex material shading [S3, unofficial].
