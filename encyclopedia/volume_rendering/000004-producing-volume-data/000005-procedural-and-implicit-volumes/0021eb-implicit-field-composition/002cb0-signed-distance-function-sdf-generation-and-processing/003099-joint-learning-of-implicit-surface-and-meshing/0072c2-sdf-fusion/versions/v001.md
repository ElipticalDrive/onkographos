# SDF Fusion

Also called: Yao et al. SDF Fusion

## Overview
SDF Fusion fuses multi-view depth and normal data into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volume and a weighted color volume by raycasting from camera to voxel centers, accumulating signed distances and colors via weighted averaging [S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished by its application in joint learning pipelines where SMPL body priors provide rough features and multi-view inputs provide detailed features, which are fused to jointly learn the SDF for 3D reconstruction [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `vol`: A 3D volume of type `BoundedVolume<SDF_t>` storing the TSDF and weights.
    *   `colorVol`: A 3D volume of type `BoundedVolume<float>` (or `float4`) storing accumulated color.
    *   `depth`: An image of depth values.
    *   `norm`: An image of surface normals.
    *   `T_cw`: A $3 \times 4$ transformation matrix from camera to world coordinates.
    *   `K`: Camera intrinsic matrix.
    *   `img`: An image of RGB colors.
    *   `T_iw`: A $3 \times 4$ transformation matrix from image/second camera to world coordinates.
    *   `Kimg`: Intrinsics for the second camera/image.
    *   `trunc_dist`: The truncation threshold for the SDF.
    *   `max_w`: The maximum allowable weight for an accumulated voxel.
    *   `mincostheta`: The minimum cosine of the angle between the view direction and surface normal.
*   **Outputs:** Updated `vol` and `colorVol`.

**Data Structures:**
*   `SDF_t`: A struct containing:
    *   `d`: A signed distance value (float).
    *   `w`: A weight value (float).
    *   It supports addition (`+=`), clamping in the constructor, and a `LimitWeight` method.
*   `BoundedVolume<T>`: A 3D volume structure accessed via `vol(x,y,z)`.

**Initialization:**
Before fusion, the SDF volume is reset. For every voxel $(x,y,z)$, the value is initialized to:
$$ \text{SDF}_t(\text{trunc\_dist}, 0) $$
This sets the initial signed distance to the truncation limit and the weight to zero [S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Raycasting:** For each voxel $(x,y,z)$ in the bounded volume, cast a ray from the camera center through the voxel center to the depth image.
2.  **Sampling:** Retrieve the depth $d_{obs}$ and normal $\mathbf{n}$ from the depth/normal images at the intersection point. Retrieve the color $c$ from the color image.
3.  **Distance Calculation:** Calculate the signed distance $sd$ from the voxel to the observed surface.
4.  **Visibility Check:** Calculate the cosine of the angle $\theta$ between the view direction $\mathbf{v}$ and the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$:
    $$ \cos\theta = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{n} $$
    Check if $\cos\theta > \text{mincostheta}$.
5.  **Validity Check:** Ensure the calculated distance metric $md$ (mean distance or similar) and weight $w$ are finite.
6.  **Clamping:** If the voxel is within the truncation range and conditions are met, clamp the signed distance:
    $$ sd_{clamped} = \text{clamp}(sd, -\text{trunc\_dist}, \text{trunc\_dist}) $$
7.  **Weight Update:** Create a new SDF value with the clamped distance and a new weight $w$ (derived from the observation confidence or inverse variance):
    $$ \text{SDF}_t_{new} = \text{SDF}_t(sd_{clamped}, w) $$
8.  **Accumulation:** Add the new SDF value to the current volume value:
    $$ \text{SDF}_t_{acc} = \text{SDF}_t_{new} + \text{vol}(x,y,z) $$
    Where addition is component-wise:
    $$ d_{acc} = d_{new} + d_{curr} $$
    $$ w_{acc} = w_{new} + w_{curr} $$
9.  **Weight Limiting:** Limit the accumulated weight to `max_w`:
    $$ w_{final} = \min(w_{acc}, \text{max\_w}) $$
    $$ \text{SDF}_t_{final} = \text{SDF}_t(d_{acc}, w_{final}) $$
10. **Storage:** Store the updated SDF back into the volume:
    $$ \text{vol}(x,y,z) = \text{SDF}_t_{final} $$
11. **Color Fusion:** Update the color volume using weighted averaging of the new color $c$ and existing color, using weights $w$ and $curvol.w$:
    $$ \text{colorVol}(x,y,z) = \frac{w \cdot c + \text{colorVol}(x,y,z) \cdot curvol.w}{w + curvol.w} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def sdf_fuse(vol, color_vol, depth_img, norm_img, T_cw, K, img, T_iw, Kimg, trunc_dist, max_w, mincostheta):
    """
    Fuses depth and normal data into a TSDF volume and a weighted color volume.
    
    Args:
        vol: BoundedVolume<SDF_t> - The SDF volume to update.
        color_vol: BoundedVolume<float> - The color volume to update.
        depth_img: Image<float> - Depth map.
        norm_img: Image<float4> - Normal map.
        T_cw: Mat<float, 3, 4> - Camera to World transformation.
        K: ImageIntrinsics - Camera intrinsics.
        img: Image<uchar3> - Color image.
        T_iw: Mat<float, 3, 4> - Image to World transformation (for second view).
        Kimg: ImageIntrinsics - Intrinsics for second view.
        trunc_dist: float - Truncation distance.
        max_w: float - Maximum weight.
        mincostheta: float - Minimum cosine of viewing angle.
    """
    # Initialize grid and block dimensions for parallel execution
    block_dim = (16, 16)
    grid_dim = (vol.width / block_dim[0], vol.height / block_dim[1])
    
    # Launch kernel for each voxel
    for x in range(0, vol.width, block_dim[0]):
        for y in range(0, vol.height, block_dim[1]):
            for z in range(0, vol.depth, 8): # Assuming 8 threads per block in Z or similar parallelism
                # 1. Raycast to voxel
                voxel_world = vol.index_to_world(x, y, z)
                ray_origin = T_cw.get_origin()
                ray_direction = normalize(voxel_world - ray_origin)
                
                # 2. Intersect with depth image
                # Note: Exact ray-intersection logic depends on depth image representation
                # Assuming we get the intersection point on the surface
                intersection = ray_intersect_depth(ray_origin, ray_direction, depth_img, K)
                
                if intersection is None:
                    continue
                
                # 3. Get surface properties
                depth_val = depth_img.get(intersection.uv)
                normal = norm_img.get(intersection.uv)
                color = img.get(intersection.uv)
                
                # 4. Calculate signed distance
                # sd is the distance from voxel to the observed surface along the ray
                # In many TSDF implementations, this is simply the difference between 
                # the voxel's depth and the observed depth, projected onto the ray.
                # Here we assume 'sd' is computed as the signed distance to the surface plane defined by normal and point.
                surface_point = intersection.point
                sd = dot(voxel_world - surface_point, normal)
                
                # 5. Check truncation
                if abs(sd) > trunc_dist:
                    continue
                
                # 6. Check viewing angle
                view_direction = normalize(ray_origin - surface_point)
                costheta = dot(view_direction, normal)
                
                if costheta <= mincostheta:
                    continue
                
                # 7. Check validity (finite values)
                if not is_finite(sd) or not is_finite(costheta):
                    continue
                
                # 8. Determine weight
                # Weight is typically based on confidence or inverse variance.
                # The notes imply 'w' is derived from the observation.
                # A common choice is w = 1.0 or based on depth confidence.
                # We assume a simple weight assignment for the new observation.
                w = 1.0 
                
                # 9. Get current volume value
                curvol = vol[x, y, z]
                
                # 10. Accumulate SDF
                # New SDF value with clamped distance and new weight
                sdf_new = SDF_t(clamp(sd, -trunc_dist, trunc_dist), w)
                
                # Add to current volume
                sdf_acc = sdf_new + curvol
                
                # Limit weight
                sdf_acc.limit_weight(max_w)
                
                # 11. Store updated SDF
                vol[x, y, z] = sdf_acc
                
                # 12. Update color volume
                # Weighted average of color
                total_w = w + curvol.w
                if total_w > 0:
                    new_color = (w * color + curvol.w * color_vol[x, y, z]) / total_w
                    color_vol[x, y, z] = new_color

def sdf_reset(vol, trunc_dist):
    """
    Resets the SDF volume to the truncation distance with zero weight.
    """
    for x in range(vol.width):
        for y in range(vol.height):
            for z in range(vol.depth):
                vol[x, y, z] = SDF_t(trunc_dist, 0)

class SDF_t:
    def __init__(self, d, w):
        self.d = d
        self.w = w
    
    def __add__(self, other):
        return SDF_t(self.d + other.d, self.w + other.w)
    
    def limit_weight(self, max_w):
        if self.w > max_w:
            self.w = max_w
```

**Parameters:**
*   `trunc_dist`: The truncation range for the SDF. Values outside $[-\text{trunc\_dist}, \text{trunc\_dist}]$ are ignored. Not given in the sources.
*   `max_w`: The maximum weight allowed for a voxel. Not given in the sources.
*   `mincostheta`: The minimum cosine of the angle between the view direction and the surface normal. Used to filter out views with poor visibility. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   If the signed distance $sd$ is further than `trunc_dist` from the surface, the voxel is not updated [S2, unofficial].
*   The update only proceeds if the distance metric $md$ and weight $w$ are finite, and the viewing angle cosine $\cos\theta$ is greater than `mincostheta` [S2, unofficial].
*   Precision issues may arise if the raycasting intersection is inaccurate, leading to incorrect signed distances.
*   The weight accumulation must be limited to `max_w` to prevent numerical instability or dominance of early observations [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of voxels in the volume and the number of views processed. Each voxel is processed once per view.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear with respect to the volume resolution. Requires storage for the SDF volume and color volume.
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently integrates multi-view data into a dense 3D representation. The use of normal information improves surface accuracy. The joint learning with SMPL priors allows for detailed reconstruction of human bodies.
*   **Limitations:** Dependent on the quality of depth and normal inputs. Performance scales with volume resolution. The truncation parameter must be chosen carefully to balance detail and noise.
*   **When to use:** Suitable for real-time or near-real-time 3D reconstruction of dynamic scenes, particularly human avatars, where multi-view data is available.
*   **When not to use:** Not suitable for scenes with large unobserved regions or where high-precision surface normals are unavailable.

## Background
SDF Fusion was introduced by Yao et al. [69] as part of a pipeline for 3D human avatar modeling. It builds on the concept of TSDF fusion but integrates it with neural implicit representations. The method uses SMPL priors as rough features and multi-view inputs as detailed features, fusing them to jointly learn the SDF [S1]. This approach achieves more detailed reconstruction than previous methods like Neural Body, which attached latent codes to the SMPL model [S1]. The method is often implemented on the GPU for real-time performance [S2, unofficial].
