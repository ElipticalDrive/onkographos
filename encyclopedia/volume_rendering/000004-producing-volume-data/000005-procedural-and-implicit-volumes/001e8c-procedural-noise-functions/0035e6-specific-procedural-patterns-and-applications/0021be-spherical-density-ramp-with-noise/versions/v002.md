# Spherical Density Ramp with Noise

Also called: ramp-confined turbulence gas, steam_slab1 confined gas modeling

## Overview
Spherical Density Ramp with Noise is a procedural volume density function that confines a gas (such as steam or smoke) to a specific slab region using a spherical distance ramp and an offset table, simulating steam rising from a source [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its siblings, such as [Worley Noise](1726-worley-noise.md) which creates cellular patterns, or [Local Cloud Flow with FlowMap](2258-local-cloud-flow-with-flowmap.md) which uses explicit vector fields, by relying on a deterministic spatial falloff combined with a discrete random offset table to create organic, rising volumetric structures without requiring complex flow advection [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes the density of a gas at a query point by combining a base turbulence density with a spherical confinement ramp and a vertical exponential falloff.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Point in solid texture space $P_{solid}$, point in world space $P_{world}$, base density $\rho_{base}$, parameter array $\mathbf{p}$, volume structure $V$.
*   **Output:** Updated density $\rho$.
*   **Volume Structure $V$:** Contains `center` (3D vector) and `inv_rad_sq` (vector, where `.y` is $1/r^2$).
*   **Parameters $\mathbf{p}$:**
    *   $\mathbf{p}[1]$: Exponent for the power function shaping the gas [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathbf{p}[2]`: Exponent for the power function [S1, unofficial].
    *   $\mathbf{p}[3]`: Amount of randomness to use in fall off [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **`pow_table`**: Array of size `POW_TABLE_SIZE`. Precomputed values mapping turbulence intensity to density.
2.  **`ramp`**: Array of size `RAMP_SIZE`. Precomputed cosine falloff values.
3.  **`offset`**: Array of size `OFFSET_SIZE`. Precomputed random numbers used to add noise to the ramp-off.

**Precomputation:**
The tables `pow_table`, `ramp`, and `offset` are computed once per image or session.
*   `pow_table[i]` is calculated as:
    $$ \text{pow\_table}[i] = \left( \frac{i}{\text{POW\_TABLE\_SIZE}-1} \cdot \mathbf{p}[1] \cdot 2.0 \right)^{\mathbf{p}[2]} $$
    for $i$ from `POW_TABLE_SIZE-1` down to 0 [S1, unofficial].
*   `ramp[i]` is calculated as:
    $$ \text{ramp}[i] = \frac{\cos\left(\frac{i}{\text{RAMP\_SIZE}-1} \pi\right) + 1.0}{2.0} $$
    for $i$ from 0 to `RAMP_SIZE-1` [S1, unofficial].
*   `offset` contains pseudo-random values [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1.  **Base Turbulence:** Calculate the turbulence value $T$ at $P_{solid}$ using a fast turbulence function (e.g., `fast_turbulence`). Map this to a base density $\rho$ using the `pow_table`:
    $$ \rho = \text{pow\_table}\left[ \left\lfloor T \cdot 0.5 \cdot (\text{POW\_TABLE\_SIZE}-1) \right\rfloor \right] $$
    [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Spherical Confinement:**
    a. Calculate the squared distance from the point to the volume center:
       $$ \text{diff} = P_{world} - V.\text{center} $$
       $$ \text{dist\_sq} = \text{dot}(\text{diff}, \text{diff}) $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    b. Compute the initial density scaling factor:
       $$ \text{density\_max} = \text{dist\_sq} \cdot V.\text{inv\_rad\_sq}.y $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    c. Add noise to the scaling factor using an offset table indexed by the sum of the point's coordinates:
       $$ \text{indx} = \left( \lfloor (P_{world}.x + P_{world}.y + P_{world}.z) \cdot 100 \rfloor \right) \ \& \ (\text{OFFSET\_SIZE} - 1) $$
       $$ \text{density\_max} += \mathbf{p}[3] \cdot \text{offset}[\text{indx}] $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    d. If $\text{density\_max} \ge 0.25$, apply a cosine falloff ramp:
       $$ i = \lfloor (\text{density\_max} - 0.25) \cdot \frac{4}{3} \cdot \text{RAMP\_SIZE} \rfloor $$
       Clamp $i$ to $[0, \text{RAMP\_SIZE}-1]$.
       $$ \text{density\_max} = \text{ramp}[i] $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    e. Multiply the base density by the ramped factor:
       $$ \rho *= \text{density\_max} $$
       [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Vertical Exponential Ramp (Optional/Variant):**
    To simulate rising steam, apply a vertical falloff [S1, unofficial]:
    a. Calculate vertical distance from the volume center:
       $$ \text{dist} = P_{world}.y - V.\text{center}.y $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    b. If $\text{dist} > 0.0$:
       $$ \text{dist} = (\text{dist} + \text{offset}[\text{indx}] \cdot 0.1) \cdot V.\text{inv\_rad}.y $$
       [S1, unofficial].
    c. If $\text{dist} > 0.05$:
       $$ \text{offset2} = (\text{dist} - 0.05) \cdot 1.111111 $$
       $$ \text{offset2} = 1 - \frac{\exp(\text{offset2}) - 1.0}{1.718282} $$
       $$ \text{offset2} *= \mathbf{p}[1] $$
       $$ \rho *= \text{offset2} $$
       [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def spherical_density_ramp_with_noise(p_world, p_solid, density, params, vol, offset_table, ramp_table, pow_table):
    """
    Computes the density of a gas confined to a slab with rising steam effect.
    
    Args:
        p_world: (float, float, float) World space position.
        p_solid: (float, float, float) Solid texture space position (for turbulence).
        density: float Current base density.
        params: list[float] Parameters [p1, p2, p3].
            p1: Exponent for power function / vertical ramp multiplier.
            p2: Exponent for power function.
            p3: Randomness multiplier for fall off.
        vol: dict Volume structure with 'center' (vec3) and 'inv_rad_sq' (vec3).
        offset_table: list[float] Precomputed random offsets.
        ramp_table: list[float] Precomputed cosine ramp values.
        pow_table: list[float] Precomputed power table values.
        
    Returns:
        float: Updated density.
    """
    # 1. Base Turbulence
    # Assuming fast_turbulence is provided by the parent context or a standard noise function
    turb = fast_turbulence(p_solid)
    turb_index = int(turb * 0.5 * (len(pow_table) - 1))
    # Clamp index to table bounds
    turb_index = max(0, min(turb_index, len(pow_table) - 1))
    density = pow_table[turb_index]
    
    # 2. Spherical Confinement
    diff_x = p_world[0] - vol['center'][0]
    diff_y = p_world[1] - vol['center'][1]
    diff_z = p_world[2] - vol['center'][2]
    
    dist_sq = diff_x**2 + diff_y**2 + diff_z**2
    
    density_max = dist_sq * vol['inv_rad_sq'][1]
    
    # Index into offset table
    sum_coords = p_world[0] + p_world[1] + p_world[2]
    indx = int(sum_coords * 100) & (len(offset_table) - 1)
    
    density_max += params[2] * offset_table[indx]
    
    if density_max >= 0.25:
        i = int((density_max - 0.25) * (4.0 / 3.0) * len(ramp_table))
        # Clamp i
        i = max(0, min(i, len(ramp_table) - 1))
        density_max = ramp_table[i]
        
    density *= density_max
    
    # 3. Vertical Exponential Ramp
    dist_y = p_world[1] - vol['center'][1]
    
    if dist_y > 0.0:
        # Reuse indx from previous step or recalculate? 
        # Source uses same indx logic implicitly or assumes it's available.
        # We use the same indx calculated above.
        dist_y = (dist_y + offset_table[indx] * 0.1) * vol['inv_rad_sq'][0] # Assuming inv_rad.y corresponds to vertical scaling
        
        if dist_y > 0.05:
            offset2 = (dist_y - 0.05) * 1.111111
            offset2 = 1.0 - (math.exp(offset2) - 1.0) / 1.718282
            offset2 *= params[0]
            density *= offset2
            
    return density
```

## Subtopics

### [NoiseTexture value evaluation](3448-noisetexture-value-evaluation.md)
NoiseTexture evaluates a volumetric color and density field by combining radial/vertical fading with double-warping via Fractal Brownian Motion (FBM) and sinusoidal noise modulation. It differs from simple procedural noise by applying spatial warping to the noise domain and modulating the result with a sine wave, creating structured, organic patterns suitable for volumetric effects like smoke or clouds.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, assuming the turbulence function is $O(1)$ and table lookups are constant time. Precomputation is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the table size.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for the precomputed tables (`pow_table`, `ramp`, `offset`).
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently confines gas to a specific shape (slab/sphere) and simulates rising behavior with minimal computational cost compared to full fluid simulation or complex flow advection. The use of precomputed tables ensures fast evaluation.
*   **Limitations:** The confinement is rigidly defined by the spherical ramp and vertical exponential function. It lacks the complex, evolving structures of FBM-based clouds or the directional control of flow maps. The "steam" appearance is specific to the chosen parameters and may not generalize to all gas types without tuning.
*   **When to use:** Ideal for simple, localized volumetric effects like steam from a cup, smoke from a chimney, or confined gas clouds where performance is critical and complex turbulence is not required.
*   **When not to use:** Not suitable for large-scale atmospheric phenomena, complex turbulent mixing, or scenes requiring dynamic, evolving gas structures driven by external forces.

## Background
The method originates from David Ebert's work on procedural modeling of gases, fluids, and textures, specifically described in the context of simulating steam rising from a teacup [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address the limitations of basic turbulence functions, which produced gas that was not confined and did not decrease in density as it rose, resulting in unrealistic visuals [S1, unofficial]. The technique combines spherical distance ramps with noise offsets to create a more convincing steam effect [S1, unofficial].
