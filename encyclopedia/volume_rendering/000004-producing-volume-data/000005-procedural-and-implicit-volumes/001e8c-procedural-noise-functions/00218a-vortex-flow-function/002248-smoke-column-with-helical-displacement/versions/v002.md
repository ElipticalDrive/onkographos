# Smoke Column with Helical Displacement

Also called: Helical Smoke Column, Displaced Cylinder Smoke

## Overview
Creates a single column of rising smoke by deforming a vertical cylinder, simulating dispersion, turbulence, and swirling motion [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent [Vortex Flow Function](2538-vortex-flow-function.md) by explicitly combining vertical helical displacement for bending/swirling with height-dependent turbulence and density attenuation, whereas the parent focuses primarily on rotational flow fields [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Point $P = (x, y, z)$ in world space.
- Cylinder axis aligned with the $z$-axis.
- Base radius $R_0$.
- Height $h = z$ (assuming base at $z=0$).
- Time $t$.
- Turbulence scale parameter $k_t$.
- Density decay parameter $k_d$.

**Outputs:**
- Density value $\rho$ at point $P$.
- Displaced position $P'$ (for flow field evaluation).

**Data Structures:**
- No complex data structures; uses scalar values and vector operations.

**Steps:**
1. **Calculate Height:** Determine the vertical position $h$ of the point relative to the smoke column base.
2. **Helical Displacement:** Displace the point horizontally using a vertical helix to simulate bending and swirling. The displacement angle $\theta$ is proportional to height $h$ and time $t$:
   $$ \theta = k_h \cdot h + k_\omega \cdot t $$
   where $k_h$ controls the twist rate and $k_\omega$ controls the rotation speed [S1, unofficial].
   The displaced coordinates $(x', y')$ are:
   $$ x' = x \cos(\theta) - y \sin(\theta) $$
   $$ y' = x \sin(\theta) + y \cos(\theta) $$
   [G] The center of rotation is the cylinder axis $(0,0)$.
3. **Turbulence Increase:** Calculate a turbulence factor $T(h)$ that increases with height to simulate air currents and the transition from smooth to turbulent flow:
   $$ T(h) = k_t \cdot h $$
   [S1, unofficial]
4. **Density Decrease:** Calculate a density attenuation factor $D(h)$ that decreases with height to simulate dispersion:
   $$ D(h) = e^{-k_d \cdot h} $$
   [G] Exponential decay is standard for dispersion; linear decay is also possible but less physically motivated for rising gas [S1, unofficial].
5. **Distance Calculation:** Calculate the radial distance $r'$ of the displaced point from the cylinder axis:
   $$ r' = \sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2} $$
6. **Density Evaluation:** Compute the final density based on the distance from the center, modulated by the height-dependent factors:
   $$ \rho = \max(0, 1 - \frac{r'}{R_0}) \cdot D(h) \cdot (1 + T(h) \cdot \text{noise}(P')) $$
   [G] A simple linear falloff from the center is used as a base shape; noise is added to simulate turbulence. The noise function should be evaluated at the displaced position $P'$ to maintain coherence with the flow.

**Parameters:**
- $k_h$: Twist rate. Typical value: $0.1$ to $1.0$ per unit height.
- $k_\omega$: Rotation speed. Typical value: $0.1$ to $1.0$ per unit time.
- $k_t$: Turbulence scale. Typical value: $0.01$ to $0.1$ per unit height.
- $k_d$: Density decay rate. Typical value: $0.1$ to $0.5$ per unit height.
- $R_0$: Base radius. Depends on scene scale.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** At large heights, floating-point precision may affect the noise evaluation and displacement accuracy.
- **Boundaries:** The density should be clamped to $[0, 1]$.
- **Degenerate Input:** If $R_0 = 0$, the column has no volume.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def smoke_column_density(x, y, z, time, R0, kh, kw, kt, kd, noise_func):
    # Step 1: Height
    h = z
    
    # Step 2: Helical Displacement
    theta = kh * h + kw * time
    cos_theta = cos(theta)
    sin_theta = sin(theta)
    x_prime = x * cos_theta - y * sin_theta
    y_prime = x * sin_theta + y * cos_theta
    
    # Step 3: Turbulence Increase
    turbulence_factor = kt * h
    
    # Step 4: Density Decrease
    density_decay = exp(-kd * h)
    
    # Step 5: Distance Calculation
    r_prime = sqrt(x_prime**2 + y_prime**2)
    
    # Step 6: Density Evaluation
    # Base shape: linear falloff from center
    base_density = max(0.0, 1.0 - r_prime / R0)
    
    # Add turbulence noise
    # Noise is evaluated at the displaced position to follow the flow
    noise_val = noise_func(x_prime, y_prime, z)
    
    # Combine factors
    density = base_density * density_decay * (1.0 + turbulence_factor * noise_val)
    
    return max(0.0, min(1.0, density))
```

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, as it involves basic arithmetic and a noise function evaluation.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, no auxiliary data structures.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement, computationally inexpensive, produces visually plausible smoke columns with minimal parameters.
- **Limitations:** Lacks complex physical interactions (e.g., buoyancy, wind shear) beyond the helical displacement. The turbulence is procedural and may not look realistic for all scenes.
- **When to use:** Quick procedural smoke effects, background elements, or as a base for more complex fluid simulations.
- **When not to use:** When high-fidelity, physically accurate smoke behavior is required.

## Background
Originated in procedural modeling of gases and fluids, as described by David Ebert in "Procedural Modeling, Animation, and Rendering of Gases, Fluids, and Textures" [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the concept of deforming simple geometric primitives (like cylinders) with flow fields and noise to simulate natural phenomena like smoke and steam.
