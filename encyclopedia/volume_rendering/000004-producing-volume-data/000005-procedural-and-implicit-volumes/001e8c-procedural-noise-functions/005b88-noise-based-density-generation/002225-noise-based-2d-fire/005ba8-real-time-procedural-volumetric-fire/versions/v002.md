# Real-Time Procedural Volumetric Fire

Also called: Hardware-Accelerated Procedural Fire, Procedural Volumetric Fire

## Overview
A method for producing real-time procedural volumetric fire using hardware acceleration, Improved Perlin Noise, and a realistic particle simulation to create vibrant volumetric fire with bi-directional environmental macro-interactivity [S1]. It constructs an arbitrarily detailed lattice around a central curve, slices the lattice into quadrilateral planar polygons, and renders them into a unit fire volume [S1]. Unlike sprite systems, it avoids common visual artifacts, and unlike true photo-realistic fire simulation, it avoids high computational costs [S1]. It builds on [Noise-based 2D Fire](2697-noise-based-2d-fire.md) by extending noise-based density generation into a 3D volumetric lattice structure.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A central curve defined by control points, deformed by a particle simulation or pre-scripted movements [S1].
*   A two-dimensional fire profile texture defining color and intensity [S1].
*   Particle simulation parameters: movement, wind, high-energy particle dispersion, and thermal buoyancy [S1].
*   Application-specific frequency for noise detail [S2, unofficial].
*   Perceived burning speed parameter [S1].
*   Unique constant offset for each fire instance [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Rendered quadrilateral planar polygons forming the volumetric fire [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Central Curve:** A four-dimensional curve (three spatial dimensions plus a time/parameter dimension) defining the fire's macro shape [S1].
*   **Lattice:** Constructed around the central curve with four corner splines generated at a distance of $2 \cdot r(t)$ units from the center spline, where $r(t)$ is the radius at parameter $t$ [S1].
*   **Unit Fire Volume:** A normalized texture space representation of the fire cross-section used for rendering [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Deform Central Curve:** Deform the central curve using either a pre-scripted sequence of movements or a realistic particle simulation [S1]. The simulation accounts for movement, wind, high-energy particle dispersion, and thermal buoyancy [S1].
2.  **Construct Lattice:** Generate four corner splines at distance $2 \cdot r(t)$ from the center spline [S1]. Sample the center spline by length to find associated positions on the corner splines, producing both the positions and texture coordinates for each lattice corner [S1].
3.  **Slice Lattice:** Slice the lattice into quadrilateral planar polygons [S1].
4.  **Render Polygons:** Render the polygons with the computed texture coordinates into a unit fire volume [S1].
5.  **Pixel Shader Processing:**
    *   Compute the noise space position $\mathbf{p}_{\text{noise}}$ from the texture coordinates $(u, v)$ using the formula below.
    *   Evaluate four-dimensional Improved Perlin Noise at $\mathbf{p}_{\text{noise}}$ to generate micro fire effects including individual flame shapes, location, and flicker [S1].
    *   Combine the noise output with the two-dimensional fire profile texture to determine the final color and intensity [S1].

**Formulas:**
*   **Corner Spline Distance:** $d = 2 \cdot r(t)$ [S1].
*   **Noise Space Position:**
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{noise}} = (u \cdot S_x, v \cdot S_y - t, 0, t) + \mathbf{O} $$
    Where:
    *   $u, v$ are the texture coordinates in the unit fire volume [S2, unofficial].
    *   $S_x$ and $S_y$ scale the horizontal and vertical axes of noise space by the radius and length, respectively [S2, unofficial].
    *   $S_x = \text{noisescale} \cdot f_{\text{app}} \cdot c_x$ and $S_y = \text{noisescale} \cdot f_{\text{app}} \cdot c_y$, where $f_{\text{app}}$ is the application-specific frequency and $c_x, c_y$ correct each noise function to have a base frequency of one [S2, unofficial].
    *   $t$ is time, serving as the fourth dimension of noise space [S2, unofficial].
    *   The vertical axis is offset by $-t$ to create an upward flow [S2, unofficial].
    *   $\mathbf{O}$ is a unique constant offset added to the noise position for each fire instance [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Perceived Burning Speed:** Passed as a parameter to the pixel shader to control animation tempo [S1].
*   **Application-Specific Frequency:** Multiplied by `noisescale` to adjust for desired levels of detail across different fire types [S2, unofficial].
*   **Unique Constant Offset:** Ensures visual uniqueness between separate fire instances [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Stretching Artifacts:** If noise space is not dynamically scaled according to the fire's radius and length, visual artifacts appear when the fire is enlarged via elongation or radius adjustment [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision Scaling:** The level of detail of the Improved Perlin Noise must be scaled to world coordinates in the pixel shader to eliminate stretching and scaling artifacts [S1].

## Subtopics

### Noise Space Scaling
A method to dynamically scale noise space according to the fire's radius and length to prevent stretching and scaling artifacts when the fire is enlarged [S2, unofficial]. It scales the horizontal and vertical axes of noise space by the radius and length, respectively [S2, unofficial]. It multiplies `noisescale` by an application-specific frequency to adjust for desired levels of detail [S2, unofficial]. It corrects each noise function to have a base frequency of one, making the frequency factor the spatial frequency of micro-level detail in world space [S2, unofficial]. This ensures that enlarging the fire does not stretch the noise effects across rendered frames [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Real-time rendering is achieved through hardware acceleration and efficient lattice construction [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the lattice detail and particle simulation state [S1].
*   **Performance:** Creates visually convincing real-time three-dimensional fire without the artifacts commonly seen in sprite systems or the computational costs associated with true photo-realistic fire simulation and rendering [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Supports bi-directional environmental macro-interactivity, allows content artists to easily modify shape and color via a two-dimensional fire profile texture, and enables dynamic interaction through efficient collision detection on the constructed lattice [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on a particle simulation for macro deformation, which requires tuning for specific environments [S1].

## Background
Originated by A. Fuller, K. Krishnan, K. Mahrous, H. Hamann, and J. Joy from UC Davis [S1]. Presented at I3D 2007 [S2, unofficial]. The method allows content artists to easily modify the shape and color of the fire by manipulating a two-dimensional fire profile texture [S1]. It supports bi-directional interaction between the fire and its environment through efficient collision detection on the constructed lattice [S1].
