# Orthographic Multi-View Voxelization

Also called: 3-Direction Rasterization, 3-View Voxelization, Directional Sheet Buffer Voxelization, Orthographic 3D Texture Rasterization, Voxelization via Orthographic Projection, Worksheet Voxelization

## Overview
Orthographic Multi-View Voxelization rasterizes scene geometry into a 3D texture using three orthographic cameras aligned with the scene's bounding box axes to create a voxelized representation for subsequent volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. A variant approach rasterizes triangles onto three intermediate 2D buffers (sheet buffers) aligned with the X, Y, and Z axes, then synthesizes them into a final 2D 'worksheet' texture representing the 3D volume [S2, unofficial]. It builds on [Multi-view projection](5937-multi-view-projection.md) by explicitly managing the conflict resolution and coordinate mapping for three orthogonal views to ensure 6-connectivity. Unlike [Single 2D Projection Voxelization](3482-single-2d-projection-voxelization.md), which suffers from depth ambiguity and cracks, this method guarantees coverage by taking the union of voxels hit by any of the three projections [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method consists of two primary implementation strategies: the direct 3D texture accumulation approach [S1, unofficial] and the sheet-buffer worksheet synthesis approach [S2, unofficial]. Both rely on the shared principles of [Multi-view projection](5937-multi-view-projection.md).

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Scene geometry (triangle mesh).
*   Scene bounding box: $B_{min} = (x_{min}, y_{min}, z_{min})$, $B_{max} = (x_{max}, y_{max}, z_{max})$.
*   Target voxel resolution: $V = (V_x, V_y, V_z)$.

**Outputs:**
*   **Direct Method:** A 3D texture (or image buffer) of size $V_x \times V_y \times V_z$ storing occupancy or material data [S1, unofficial].
*   **Worksheet Method:** A 2D texture of resolution $2^W \times 2^H$ encoding the discrete voxel space $2^L \times 2^M \times 2^N$ such that $2^L \times 2^M \times 2^N = 2^W \times 2^H \times C$, where $C$ is the number of voxels encoded in a single texel [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures

**1. Direct 3D Texture Layout [S1, unofficial]:**
*   Type: `std::vector<GLfloat>` or GPU `RGBA32F` texture.
*   Size: $4 \times V_x \times V_y \times V_z$ floats.
*   Initialization: All values set to $0.0$ (RGBA(0,0,0,0)).

**2. Worksheet Layout [S2, unofficial]:**
*   Type: 2D Texture.
*   Resolution: $2^W \times 2^H$.
*   Encoding: Each texel stores $C$ voxels. For binary voxelization with 8-bit components, $C=32$ (4 components $\times$ 8 bits).
*   Voxel Access Logic: To access voxel at discrete coordinates $(p_x, p_y, p_z)$:
    1.  Determine patch index: $patch\_z = \lfloor p_z / C \rfloor$.
    2.  Access texel at $(p_x, p_y)$ within the worksheet.
    3.  Determine bit offset within the texel components based on $p_z \pmod C$ and $C$.
    4.  Use a lookup table to extract specific bits from the component [S2, unofficial].

**3. Sheet Buffers [S2, unofficial]:**
*   Three 2D buffers representing parts of the discrete voxel space, one for each axis direction (X, Y, Z). These serve as intermediate storage during rasterization and texelization.

### Parameters
*   **Voxel Grid Scale:** $S = (V_x / R_x, V_y / R_y, V_y / R_z)$, where $R = B_{max} - B_{min}$ is the scene range [S1, unofficial].
*   **Camera Offset:** Extension distance beyond the bounding box, fixed at $0.2f$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Projection Scale:** Frustum expansion factor applied to range, fixed at $1.02f$ (passed as $0.51f$ to half-width) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Default Resolution:** $V = (64, 64, 64)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Rasterization Direction:** Triangles must be rasterized along the axis direction where they have the maximum projection area to avoid degenerate line segments [S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

#### Strategy A: Direct 3D Texture Accumulation [S1, unofficial]

1.  **Calculate Bounding Box:** Compute $B_{min}$ and $B_{max}$ of the scene. Calculate range $R = B_{max} - B_{min}$.
2.  **Initialize Texture:** Clear the 3D texture to RGBA(0,0,0,0). Disable multisampling, depth testing, and face culling. Bind the texture as a read-write image buffer (e.g., `GL_RGBA8` or `RGBA32F`).
3.  **Setup Uniforms:**
    *   Set `boxMin` uniform to $B_{min}$.
    *   Set `scale` uniform to $(V_x/R_x, V_y/R_y, V_z/R_z)$.
    *   Set `viewProject[0]`, `viewProject[1]`, `viewProject[2]` uniforms. These are orthographic projection matrices for the X, Y, and Z axes.
    *   Set frustum bounds: Near plane at $0.1f$, Far plane at $1.2 \times \text{range} + \text{offset}$. The viewing planes are expanded by $1.02 \times$ the scene range [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Rasterization Loop:** For each of the three view directions (X, Y, Z):
    *   Bind the corresponding `viewProject` matrix.
    *   Draw the scene geometry.
    *   In the vertex/fragment shader, transform vertices to voxel space: $v_{voxel} = (v_{world} - B_{min}) \times S$.
    *   Use atomic operations (e.g., `atomicAdd` or `atomicMax`) to write occupancy or material data to the 3D texture at the fragment's voxel coordinates [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Post-Processing:** Generate mipmaps for the filled 3D texture to enable multi-resolution cone tracing [S1, unofficial].

#### Strategy B: Sheet Buffer and Worksheet Synthesis [S2, unofficial]

1.  **Stage 1: Rasterization:**
    *   For each of the three axes (X, Y, Z), create a sheet buffer.
    *   Identify triangles intersecting the current slab.
    *   For each triangle, determine the rasterization direction (X, Y, or Z) that is most parallel to its orientation (maximum projection area) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Rasterize the triangle onto the corresponding sheet buffer.
    *   *Pitfall:* If a triangle is parallel to the rasterization direction, 2D linear interpolation results in a line segment. The choice of direction avoids this [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Stage 2: Texelization:**
    *   Transform voxel positions to 3D volume coordinates immediately.
    *   Use these coordinates to find the correct position in the worksheet.
    *   Add voxels to the worksheet via a blending operation at the corresponding location [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Stage 3: Synthesis:**
    *   Transcode the three directional sheet buffers to the final worksheet.
    *   Transform each element to discrete voxel space.
    *   Encode to the appropriate texel in the worksheet using the access logic defined in Data Structures [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Strategy A: Direct 3D Texture Accumulation
def orthographic_multi_view_voxelization_direct(scene, voxel_size):
    # 1. Calculate Bounding Box
    bb_min, bb_max = scene.calculate_bounding_box()
    range_vec = bb_max - bb_min
    
    # 2. Initialize Texture
    # texture3D is a 3D image buffer of size voxel_size.x * voxel_size.y * voxel_size.z
    texture3D.clear(0.0)
    texture3D.bind_as_image_buffer(access=READ_WRITE)
    
    # 3. Setup Uniforms
    scale = voxel_size / range_vec
    offset = 0.2f
    projection_scale = 1.02f
    
    # Define orthographic matrices for X, Y, Z views
    # Note: Exact matrix construction depends on graphics API, 
    # but conceptually maps world space to [-0.5, 0.5] or [0, 1] voxel space
    view_projects = [
        create_ortho_matrix(bb_min, bb_max, offset, projection_scale, axis='X'),
        create_ortho_matrix(bb_min, bb_max, offset, projection_scale, axis='Y'),
        create_ortho_matrix(bb_min, bb_max, offset, projection_scale, axis='Z')
    ]
    
    # 4. Rasterization Loop
    for vp in view_projects:
        set_uniform("viewProject", vp)
        set_uniform("boxMin", bb_min)
        set_uniform("scale", scale)
        
        # Draw scene with voxelization shader
        # Shader logic:
        # v_voxel = (v_world - boxMin) * scale
        # atomicAdd(texture3D[v_voxel], 1.0) # Or material ID
        scene.draw()
        
    # 5. Post-Processing
    texture3D.generate_mipmaps()
    return texture3D

# Strategy B: Worksheet Synthesis
def orthographic_multi_view_voxelization_worksheet(scene, voxel_dims, texel_voxels):
    # voxel_dims = (2^L, 2^M, 2^N)
    # texel_voxels = C (voxels per texel)
    
    # 1. Stage 1: Rasterization to Sheet Buffers
    sheet_buffers = [create_2d_buffer() for _ in range(3)] # X, Y, Z
    
    for axis_idx in range(3):
        axis = ['X', 'Y', 'Z'][axis_idx]
        for triangle in scene.triangles:
            # Determine rasterization direction
            # Choose axis where triangle has max projection area
            proj_areas = [
                triangle.project_area('X'),
                triangle.project_area('Y'),
                triangle.project_area('Z')
            ]
            best_axis_idx = argmax(proj_areas)
            
            if best_axis_idx == axis_idx:
                rasterize_triangle_to_sheet(triangle, sheet_buffers[axis_idx])
                
    # 2. Stage 2 & 3: Texelization and Synthesis
    worksheet = create_2d_texture(width=2^W, height=2^H)
    
    for axis_idx in range(3):
        sheet = sheet_buffers[axis_idx]
        for voxel in sheet.get_voxels():
            # Transform to discrete voxel space
            px, py, pz = voxel.coords
            
            # Determine patch in worksheet
            patch_z = pz // texel_voxels
            
            # Access texel
            texel = worksheet.get_texel(px, py)
            
            # Encode voxel into texel bits
            bit_offset = (pz % texel_voxels)
            worksheet.set_texel_bits(px, py, bit_offset, voxel.data)
            
    return worksheet
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Degenerate Rasterization:** If a triangle is parallel to the rasterization direction, 2D linear interpolation results in a line segment, causing gaps. Solution: Rasterize along the direction most parallel to the triangle's orientation (maximum projection area) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Hardware Support:** Direct rendering to 3D textures was not supported in consumer graphics hardware of PC platforms at the time of the worksheet method's publication [S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Floating point precision in voxel space conversion can lead to off-by-one errors. Using atomic operations helps mitigate race conditions when multiple fragments map to the same voxel [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Shadow Volume Voxelization](5629-shadow-volume-voxelization.md)
Shadow Volume Voxelization is a rasterization-based technique that generates a 3D shadow volume texture by voxelizing scene geometry into a 3D grid, where each voxel stores a bitmask representing which of its 8 constituent sub-voxels are occluded. It builds on [Orthographic Multi-View Voxelization](3892-orthographic-multi-view-voxelization.md) by replacing the standard occupancy flag with a 3-bit occlusion mask per coarse voxel, enabling multi-resolution shadow representation through mipmaps. Unlike standard binary voxelization, this method preserves partial occlusion information within each voxel, allowing for smoother shadow transitions and hierarchical shadow testing.

### Slicing-based voxelization
Slicing-based voxelization rasterizes the model the same number of times as the resolution of the volume along the z-direction [S2, unofficial]. This feature (rendering to 3D textures) has not been supported in consumer graphics hardware of PC platform at the time of writing [S2, unofficial]. It is significantly slower than the proposed 2D texture method; for the Blade model at $256^3$, it takes ~5 seconds compared to ~100ms for the proposed method [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot V_{proj})$, where $N$ is the number of triangles and $V_{proj}$ is the number of views (3). The worksheet method avoids the $O(V_z)$ complexity of slicing-based methods [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V_x \cdot V_y \cdot V_z)$ for the direct 3D texture method. The worksheet method uses $O(2^W \cdot 2^H)$, which can be more compact if $C > 1$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** For a $256^3$ resolution, the worksheet method takes ~100ms, whereas slicing-based voxelization takes ~5 seconds [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast, hardware-accelerated, supports complex models, ensures 6-connectivity via multi-view union [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires 3 passes (or 3 buffers). Direct 3D texture writes may have hardware limitations. The worksheet method requires complex bit-manipulation logic for encoding/decoding [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time volume rendering, shadow mapping, when high-quality 3D occupancy is needed quickly.
*   **When not to use:** When memory is extremely constrained and $C$ is small, or when hardware does not support image atomics or 3D textures.

## Background
The method originates from the need for fast, hardware-accelerated voxelization for complex models in real-time graphics applications [S2, unofficial]. The direct 3D texture approach is associated with Voxel Cone Tracing implementations [S1, unofficial]. The sheet-buffer worksheet approach was proposed to overcome the lack of 3D texture rendering support in consumer hardware and to provide a more efficient encoding scheme using 2D textures [S2, unofficial].
