# 3D Convolutional Autoencoder for PVH Refinement

Also called: Symmetric Autoencoder, Volumetric Autoencoder

## Overview
This method refines a low-fidelity Probabilistic Visual Hull (PVH) into a high-fidelity PVH of identical dimensions by learning to hallucinate volumetric detail from limited camera viewpoints [S1]. It addresses the limitation of wide-baseline captures where sparse views produce coarse, low-resolution occupancy estimates, enabling high-quality volumetric reconstruction from minimal camera counts [S1]. Unlike correspondence-based methods that fail with wide baselines, this approach treats refinement as a supervised mapping problem, effectively simulating the benefit of additional viewpoints without requiring them during capture [S1].

## How it works
The method employs a symmetric 3D convolutional autoencoder with skip connections to learn a mapping $F$ from a low-camera-count input volume to a high-camera-count target volume [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input ($VL$):** A 4D tensor $\mathbb{R}^{X \times Y \times Z \times 1}$ representing a low-fidelity PVH patch. The single channel encodes the probability of volume occupancy $p(X,Y,Z)$ derived from a small set of camera views [S1].
*   **Output ($VH$):** A 4D tensor $\mathbb{R}^{X \times Y \times Z \times 1}$ representing the refined, high-fidelity PVH of identical dimensions [S1].

### Data Structures and Layout
The network architecture is symmetric with 5 encoder stages and 5 decoder stages [S1].

**Encoder Parameters:**
*   Filter counts ($n_e$): $[64, 64, 128, 128, 256]$
*   Kernel sizes ($k_e$): $[3, 3, 3, 3, 3]$ (all 3D kernels)

**Decoder Parameters:**
*   Filter counts ($n_d$): $[256, 128, 128, 64, 64]$
*   Kernel sizes ($k_d$): $[3, 3, 3, 3, 3]$ (all 3D kernels)

**Skip Connections:**
*   Locations ($s$): $[0, 1, 0, 1, 0]$. A value of 1 indicates a skip connection between the corresponding encoder and decoder layers.
*   Mechanism: Feature maps from the encoder are summed element-wise with the up-convolutional feature maps in the decoder before passing to the next layer [S1].

**Latent Space:**
*   The central fully-connected layer encodes a 100-dimensional latent representation [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Patch Extraction:** The capture volume is divided into densely overlapping 3D sub-volumes (patches). Patches where the sum of occupancy probabilities is zero are ignored for efficiency [S1].
2.  **Encoding:** The input patch $x_i$ is passed through the encoder. This consists of successive 3D convolutional operations with non-linear activation layers, downsampling the spatial dimensions until the central fully-connected layer [S1].
3.  **Latent Representation:** The central fully-connected layer produces a 100-D latent vector [S1].
4.  **Decoding:** The latent vector is decoded using up-convolutional layers. At layers where $s=1$, the corresponding encoder feature maps are added element-wise to the decoder feature maps [S1].
5.  **Output Generation:** The final decoder layer produces the output tensor $VH$ of the same spatial dimensions as the input [S1].
6.  **Training Optimization:** The network weights $\phi$ are updated to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the network output and the ground truth high-fidelity PVH [S1].

### Formulas

**Loss Function:**
The objective is to minimize the MSE across all voxels in the patch:
$$ L(\phi) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} ||F(x_i; \phi) - z_i||_2^2 $$
Where:
*   $N = X \times Y \times Z$ is the number of voxels in the patch.
*   $x_i$ is the input low-fidelity PVH patch.
*   $z_i$ is the ground truth high-fidelity PVH patch.
*   $F(x_i; \phi)$ is the network output.
*   $\phi$ represents the network weights [S1].

**Optimizer:**
Adadelta is used. It restricts the window of accumulated past gradients to a fixed size $w$ to prevent aggressive, radically diminishing learning rates associated with Adagrad [S1].

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Optimizer:** Adadelta [S1].
*   **Epochs:** 10. Convergence is typically small, occurring around 5 to 10 epochs [S1].
*   **Patch Processing:** Patches are processed independently at both training and inference time [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
import math

class ConvAutoencoder:
    def __init__(self, input_shape=(X, Y, Z)):
        """
        Initialize the symmetric 3D convolutional autoencoder.
        input_shape: Tuple (X, Y, Z) defining the voxel dimensions of a patch.
        """
        # Encoder filter counts and kernel sizes
        self.encoder_filters = [64, 64, 128, 128, 256]
        self.encoder_kernels = [3, 3, 3, 3, 3]
        
        # Decoder filter counts and kernel sizes
        self.decoder_filters = [256, 128, 128, 64, 64]
        self.decoder_kernels = [3, 3, 3, 3, 3]
        
        # Skip connection locations (1 means skip connection exists)
        self.skip_connections = [0, 1, 0, 1, 0]
        
        # Latent dimension
        self.latent_dim = 100
        
        # Initialize weights phi (not shown, assumed initialized by framework)
        self.phi = None

    def encode(self, x):
        """
        Encode input PVH patch into latent representation.
        x: Tensor of shape (Batch, X, Y, Z, 1)
        """
        # Stage 1
        h = conv3d(x, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = downsample(h)
        
        # Stage 2 (Skip connection 1)
        h = conv3d(h, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = downsample(h)
        skip_1 = h # Store for skip connection
        
        # Stage 3
        h = conv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = downsample(h)
        
        # Stage 4 (Skip connection 2)
        h = conv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = downsample(h)
        skip_2 = h # Store for skip connection
        
        # Stage 5
        h = conv3d(h, filters=256, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = downsample(h)
        
        # Central Fully Connected Layer
        # Flatten spatial dimensions
        flat = flatten(h)
        latent = fully_connected(flat, units=self.latent_dim, activation='relu')
        
        return latent, skip_1, skip_2

    def decode(self, latent, skip_1, skip_2):
        """
        Decode latent representation to output PVH patch.
        latent: Tensor of shape (Batch, 100)
        skip_1, skip_2: Feature maps from encoder stages 2 and 4
        """
        # Reverse fully connected and reshape
        h = fully_connected(latent, units=256 * (X//8) * (Y//8) * (Z//8)) # Assuming specific downsampling factors
        h = reshape(h, target_shape=(Batch, X//8, Y//8, Z//8, 256))
        
        # Stage 5 (Decoder)
        h = upconv3d(h, filters=256, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        
        # Stage 4 (Decoder) - Skip connection 2
        h = conv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = upconv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        # Add skip connection
        h = elementwise_add(h, skip_2)
        
        # Stage 3 (Decoder)
        h = conv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = upconv3d(h, filters=128, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        
        # Stage 2 (Decoder) - Skip connection 1
        h = conv3d(h, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = upconv3d(h, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        # Add skip connection
        h = elementwise_add(h, skip_1)
        
        # Stage 1 (Decoder)
        h = conv3d(h, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        h = upconv3d(h, filters=64, kernel=3, activation='relu')
        
        # Final Output Layer
        output = conv3d(h, filters=1, kernel=3, activation=None)
        
        return output

    def forward(self, x):
        latent, skip_1, skip_2 = self.encode(x)
        return self.decode(latent, skip_1, skip_2)

    def train_step(self, x, z):
        """
        Perform one training step.
        x: Input low-fidelity PVH patch
        z: Ground truth high-fidelity PVH patch
        """
        # Forward pass
        pred = self.forward(x)
        
        # Calculate MSE Loss
        # N = X * Y * Z
        loss = mean_squared_error(pred, z)
        
        # Backpropagate using Adadelta optimizer
        # Adadelta updates weights phi based on accumulated gradients
        update_weights_adadelta(self.phi, loss, x, z)
        
        return loss

    def infer_patch(self, patch):
        """
        Refine a single patch.
        """
        return self.forward(patch)

    def refine_volume(self, low_fidelity_volume, patch_size, overlap):
        """
        Refine the entire volume by processing densely overlapping patches.
        """
        # Initialize output volume
        high_fidelity_volume = zeros_like(low_fidelity_volume)
        count_volume = zeros_like(low_fidelity_volume)
        
        # Generate densely overlapping patches
        patches = generate_patches(low_fidelity_volume, patch_size, overlap)
        
        for patch in patches:
            # Ignore empty patches
            if sum(patch) == 0:
                continue
                
            refined_patch = self.infer_patch(patch)
            
            # Accumulate results (handling overlap via averaging or simple sum)
            # Note: The source mentions "densely overlapping sub-volumes" but 
            # does not specify the exact blending strategy for inference overlap.
            # Standard practice is averaging.
            high_fidelity_volume += refined_patch
            count_volume += 1
            
        # Average overlapping regions
        # Guard against division by zero
        safe_count = max(count_volume, 1)
        return high_fidelity_volume / safe_count
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Empty Patches:** Patches where the sum of occupancy probabilities is zero are ignored to improve efficiency [S1].
*   **Memory Constraints:** Processing the entire volume at once is intractable due to memory constraints and large receptive fields. Patch-based processing is required [S1].
*   **Vanishing Gradients:** Deeper networks may suffer from vanishing gradients; skip connections help mitigate this [S1].
*   **Detail Loss:** Without skip connections, finer details may be lost or corrupted due to the compact latent feature abstraction [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given, but inference is performed per patch. Training requires 5-10 epochs [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is constrained by the patch size rather than the full volume, making it tractable for large capture volumes [S1].
*   **Performance:** The method allows for high-fidelity reconstruction from minimal camera viewpoints, effectively doubling or more the utility of the camera setup [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles wide-baseline views; reduces cost by requiring fewer cameras; mitigates memory issues via patching [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires training data (pairs of low and high fidelity PVHs); inference quality depends on the training data distribution [S1].
*   **When to use:** When camera count is limited (prosumer scenarios, sports events) or camera placement is restricted [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When high camera counts are available and direct PVH construction is feasible without refinement [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in "Volumetric Performance Capture from Minimal Camera Viewpoints" [S1]. It extends the space of use scenarios for volumetric capture to settings with low camera counts, such as prosumer scenarios or sports events where cost or placement restrictions limit the number of available views [S1]. The approach is inspired by 3D convolutional architectures used for visual content de-noising and up-scaling (super-resolution) [S1].
