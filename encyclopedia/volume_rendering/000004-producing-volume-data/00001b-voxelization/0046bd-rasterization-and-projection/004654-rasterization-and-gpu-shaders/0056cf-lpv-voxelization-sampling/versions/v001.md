# LPV Voxelization Sampling

Also called: Area Lights Voxelization for Light Propagation Volumes, Triangle Voxelization, Voxelization Sampling for Area Lights

## Overview
LPV Voxelization Sampling is a method to improve the simulation of direct illumination from area light sources within the Light Propagation Volumes (LPV) technique by voxelizing the area light source geometry into a voxel volume matching the LPV grid resolution [S1, unofficial]. It generates one Virtual Point Light (VPL) sample for each LPV cell that intersects the light source triangles, providing an optimal distribution of samples that covers all necessary cells, unlike dense sampling which may under-sample or over-sample [S1, unofficial]. This approach reduces the number of VPLs processed per frame compared to dense sampling strategies, offering performance advantages while maintaining plausible lighting [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Area light source mesh (triangles with positions, normals, and optionally texture coordinates/emission directions), LPV grid resolution $(N_x, N_y, N_z)$, LPV bounding box.
*   **Output:** LPV grid updated with spherical harmonic coefficients representing radiance distribution, VPL counter per cell.

**Data Structures:**
*   **LPV Grid:** 3D array of cells. Each cell stores 2-band spherical harmonic coefficients for R, G, B channels (4 coefficients per channel, total 12 floats per cell) and an atomic counter for VPLs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Triangle:** Defined by vertices $A, B, C$ in world space.

**Steps:**

1.  **Projection Selection:** For each triangle of the area light source mesh, project its vertices onto the planes of the six LPV faces. Select the LPV face where the projected triangle has the largest surface area. This optimization ensures that a maximum of 2 LPV cells intersect the triangle projection along the axis perpendicular to the face (z-axis) [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Rasterization Setup:** Render the area light source mesh in a viewport with resolution matching the LPV face resolution. In the Geometry Shader, project the triangle vertices onto the selected LPV face plane [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Triangle Expansion:** To ensure fragments are generated for LPV cells whose centers are partially intersected by the triangle (even if the center is outside the original triangle boundary), expand the triangle outwards. This expansion covers the centers of all potentially intersected fragments [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Initial Intersection Check:** Before processing the expanded triangle, compute the LPV cells along the z-axis that intersect the *initial* (non-expanded) triangle. This is done to avoid injecting radiance into cells that should not receive it due to the expansion covering extra area [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Cell Intersection Test:** For the projected triangle on the selected LPV face, identify the face coordinates $(A0_x, A0_y)$ and depth coordinate $A0_z$. Check only 3 specific cells along the z-axis for intersection with the triangle: $\{(A0_x, A0_y, A0_z-1), (A0_x, A0_y, A0_z), (A0_x, A0_y, A0_z+1)\}$ [S1, unofficial]. Use the test proposed by Akenine-Möller (2001) to check if LPV cells with the same $(A_x, A_y)$ coordinates intersect with the triangle [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Fragment Processing (Fragment Shader):**
    *   For each fragment generated by the rasterizer (representing an intersection area):
        *   Determine the LPV cell coordinates $(A_x, A_y, A_z)$ from the fragment's normalized clipspace coordinates [S1, unofficial].
        *   Create a Virtual Point Light (VPL) with:
            *   Position: Center of the LPV cell.
            *   Direction: Direction of radiance emission (interpolated from triangle vertices if non-homogeneous, or constant if homogeneous) [S1, unofficial].
            *   Radiant Flux: Radiant flux of the triangle (or sampled from texture if textured) [S1, unofficial].
        *   Compute the clamped cosine lobe in terms of zonal spherical harmonics along the negative z-axis, rotated in the direction of radiance emission [S1, unofficial].
        *   Use atomic operations to update the spherical harmonic coefficients and the VPL counter in the LPV cell to avoid concurrency problems [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Spherical Harmonic Update:**
    *   For each VPL injected into a cell, compute its contribution to the spherical harmonic coefficients.
    *   The radiance distribution $D_0$ for all VPLs in an LPV cell is computed as:
        $$ D_0 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N} D_i \cdot C_i $$
        where $D_0$ is the spherical harmonic coefficients approximating radiance distribution in the LPV cell, $N$ is the number of VPLs in the cell, $D_i$ are the coefficients of the clamped cosine lobe for the $i$-th VPL, and $C_i$ is the intensity of the color channel for the $i$-th VPL [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute coefficients for 3 spherical harmonics inside each cell, one for every color channel {R, G, B}. Use 2-band spherical harmonics, resulting in 4 coefficients per color channel [S1, unofficial].
    *   During the voxelization process, update the coefficients without weighting. Store the VPL counter for each cell [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Post-Processing (Compute Shader):**
    *   After the rasterization pass, use a compute shader to weight all spherical harmonic coefficients using the stored VPL counters [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **LPV Resolution:** Must match the resolution of the 3D grid used in the LPV technique [S1, unofficial].
*   **Spherical Harmonics Band:** 2-band spherical harmonics are used [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Under-sampling:** If not every LPV cell that intersects the area light source surface is covered, radiance is not introduced into all necessary cells, resulting in non-plausible, dimly lit scenes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Rasterizer Limitations:** The GPU rasterizer does not generate fragments whose centers are outside the rasterized triangle, even if the triangle partially intersects them. This prevents VPL generation for those areas unless the triangle is expanded [S1, unofficial].
*   **Expansion Artifacts:** The expanded triangle may cover centers of fragments completely outside the triangle. Pre-computation of intersections with the initial triangle is required to avoid injecting radiance into incorrect cells [S1, unofficial].
*   **Concurrency:** Multiple fragment shaders may run in parallel, requiring atomic operations to update LPV cell data [S1, unofficial].

# Pseudocode for LPV Voxelization Sampling
# Assumes standard vector/math operations are available

def lpv_voxelization_sampling(area_light_mesh, lpv_grid, lpv_resolution):
    """
    Voxelize area light source mesh into LPV grid.
    
    Args:
        area_light_mesh: List of triangles, each with vertices A, B, C (world space), 
                         normals, and optionally emission directions/texture coords.
        lpv_grid: 3D array of LPV cells, each storing SH coefficients (12 floats) and VPL counter.
        lpv_resolution: Tuple (Nx, Ny, Nz) defining the LPV grid dimensions.
    """
    
    # Step 1: Iterate over each triangle in the area light mesh
    for triangle in area_light_mesh:
        vertices = [triangle.A, triangle.B, triangle.C]
        
        # Step 2: Project triangle onto all 6 LPV faces and find the face with largest projection area
        projected_faces = []
        for face_normal, face_origin in get_lpv_face_planes():
            projected_vertices = project_onto_plane(vertices, face_normal, face_origin)
            area = compute_triangle_area(projected_vertices)
            projected_faces.append((face_normal, face_origin, projected_vertices, area))
        
        best_face = max(projected_faces, key=lambda x: x[3])
        best_normal, best_origin, best_proj_vertices, _ = best_face
        
        # Step 3: Determine face coordinates (Ax, Ay) and depth (Az) for the projection
        # Map projected vertices to LPV grid indices
        face_coords = [world_to_lpv_face_index(v, best_normal, best_origin, lpv_resolution) for v in best_proj_vertices]
        
        # Find the bounding box of the projected triangle in face coordinates
        min_x, max_x = min(c.x for c in face_coords), max(c.x for c in face_coords)
        min_y, max_y = min(c.y for c in c in face_coords), max(c.y for c in face_coords)
        
        # Step 4: Check initial triangle intersections along z-axis
        # Use Akenine-Moller test to find which cells along z-axis intersect the initial triangle
        initial_intersecting_cells = []
        for x in range(int(min_x), int(max_x) + 1):
            for y in range(int(min_y), int(max_y) + 1):
                # Check cells at z-1, z, z+1 relative to the projected depth
                # The exact z-coordinate depends on the triangle's depth in the LPV volume
                # For simplicity, assume we check a range of z based on triangle depth
                z_min, z_max = get_triangle_z_range(triangle, best_normal, best_origin)
                for z in range(int(z_min), int(z_max) + 1):
                    if akenine_moller_intersection_test(triangle, (x, y, z), best_normal, best_origin):
                        initial_intersecting_cells.append((x, y, z))
        
        # Step 5: Expand triangle for rasterization
        expanded_triangle = expand_triangle(vertices)
        
        # Step 6: Rasterize expanded triangle
        # This step is typically done via GPU rasterization, but we simulate the logic here
        fragments = rasterize_triangle(expanded_triangle, lpv_resolution)
        
        # Step 7: Process each fragment
        for fragment in fragments:
            # Get LPV cell coordinates from fragment position
            cell_coords = world_to_lpv_cell_index(fragment.position, lpv_resolution)
            x, y, z = cell_coords
            
            # Check if this cell was in the initial intersection set to avoid false positives from expansion
            if (x, y, z) not in initial_intersecting_cells:
                continue
            
            # Create VPL
            vpl_position = lpv_cell_center(cell_coords, lpv_resolution)
            vpl_direction = interpolate_emission_direction(triangle, fragment.barycentric_coords)
            vpl_flux = get_triangle_flux(triangle)
            
            # Compute clamped cosine lobe SH coefficients for this VPL
            vpl_sh_coeffs = compute_clamped_cosine_lobe_sh(vpl_direction)
            
            # Get color channel intensities
            color_intensities = get_color_channel_intensities(vpl_flux)
            
            # Update LPV cell with atomic operations
            update_lpv_cell_atomic(lpv_grid, cell_coords, vpl_sh_coeffs, color_intensities)

    # Step 8: Post-processing with compute shader to weight coefficients
    weight_lpv_coefficients(lpv_grid)

def compute_clamped_cosine_lobe_sh(direction):

    Compute 2-band spherical harmonic coefficients for a clamped cosine lobe

    """

    """
    Atomically update the spherical harmonic coefficients and VPL counter in the LPV cell.
    """
    cell = lpv_grid[cell_coords]
    # Atomic add to SH coefficients
    for i in range(4):
        atomic_add(cell.sh_coeffs[i], sh_coeffs[i] * color_intensities[i])
    # Atomic increment VPL counter
    atomic_increment(cell.vpl_counter)

def weight_lpv_coefficients(lpv_grid):
    """
    Weight all spherical harmonic coefficients in the LPV grid using the VPL counters.
    """
    for cell in lpv_grid:
        if cell.vpl_counter > 0:
            for i in range(12): # 4 coefficients * 3 channels
                cell.sh_coeffs[i] /= cell.vpl_counter

## Subtopics

### [Radiance Distribution Approximation for Non-Homogeneous Lights](15820-radiance-distribution-approximation-for-non-homogeneous-ligh.md)
A method to approximate radiance distribution on mesh triangles where emission direction varies across the surface, using clamped cosine lobes distributed over an Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) of the intersection area projection. It handles non-homogeneous emission by interpolating emission directions from triangle vertices and computing a spherical harmonic that approximates the radiance distribution for each intersected LPV cell. Choose this when the area light source has varying emission directions across its surface.

### Textured Area Lights Sampling
A method to store and sample radiant flux information on area light surfaces using texture mapping and mipmapping to approximate the entire radiant flux of an intersection area [S1, unofficial]. It uses texture coordinates at triangle vertices, interpolates them to find the texture coordinate at the AABB center of the intersection area, and applies mipmapping to quickly compute the radiant flux [S1, unofficial]. Choose this when the area light source has varying radiant flux across its surface, represented by a texture map.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of triangles in the area light source mesh and the number of LPV cells intersected by these triangles. The most expensive operation is the atomic update of LPV cell coefficients, which scales with the number of VPLs per cell [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the size of the LPV grid. Each cell stores 12 floats for SH coefficients and an integer for the VPL counter [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On an Nvidia GTX 1660 GPU, an LPV resolution of $64^3$ showed better performance than $32^3$ for almost all test data because atomic operations are less frequent at higher resolutions [S1, unofficial]. Dense sampling performance is poor for a large number of triangles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Optimal distribution of samples covering all intersecting voxels, reducing the number of VPLs processed per frame compared to dense sampling. Efficient use of GPU rasterization for voxelization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires atomic operations which can be a bottleneck. Under-sampling can lead to dimly lit scenes. Expansion of triangles can lead to artifacts if not handled correctly [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When simulating direct illumination from area light sources in real-time global illumination using LPV, especially when performance is a concern and plausible lighting is required.
*   **When Not to Use:** When the area light source has very complex geometry with many small triangles, leading to a high number of VPLs. When high precision in radiance distribution is required and the approximation errors from mipmapping or SH are unacceptable [S1, unofficial].

## Background
LPV Voxelization Sampling was introduced by Lambru, Moldoveanu, Morar, and Asavei in 2022 in the paper "Area Lights Voxelization for Light Propagation Volumes" [S1, unofficial]. It improves upon previous LPV adaptations that lacked a specific sampling strategy for area lights, which often relied on dense sampling without quantitative evaluation [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the LPV technique for real-time global illumination and the voxelization approach proposed by Schwarz and Seidel (2010) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of simulating direct illumination from area light sources, which can reproduce effects such as the light of a TV display, by providing an optimal distribution of VPLs [S1, unofficial].
