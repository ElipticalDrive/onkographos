# Synthetic Aperture Particle Image Velocimetry (SAPIV)


## Overview
SAPIV is a particle volume reconstruction method that applies synthetic aperture processing techniques, originally developed for radar interferometry, to multi-view particle imaging to enhance resolution and accuracy [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard tomographic methods that iterate to fit projections, SAPIV synthesizes a high-resolution "Image of Synthetic Aperture" (IOS) from multiple camera views, allowing for direct particle extraction with fewer cameras than traditional iterative algorithms like MART [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
SAPIV operates by aggregating information from multiple viewing angles into a synthesized 2D representation, which is then sliced and processed to recover 3D particle positions.

**Inputs:**
- A set of $N$ 2D images of particles captured by $N$ calibrated cameras at different viewing angles.
- Calibration data mapping 2D pixel coordinates to 3D world coordinates (specifically the 2D-3D correspondence function).

**Outputs:**
- A 3D voxel grid representing the particle volume, where occupied voxels correspond to particle locations.

**Data Structures:**
- **IOS (Image of Synthetic Aperture):** A 2D image buffer. The sources note that the IOS background has a high noise rate compared to standard Tomo-PIV particle images [S1, unofficial].
- **Voxel Grid:** A 3D array where each voxel stores occupancy or intensity.

**Steps:**

1.  **Calibration:** Establish a 2D-3D position correspondence function. The sources specify using the method presented by Vaish et al. [S1, unofficial]. This function maps 2D pixel coordinates from each camera to 3D spatial coordinates.

2.  **Synthetic Aperture Generation:** Implement the synthetic aperture generation algorithm to create the IOS from the measurements at multiple viewing angles. This step aggregates the multi-view data into a single image representation [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Slicing (Z-Projection):** Center the created IOS map on different planes along the Z-axis of the volume. This effectively slices the synthetic aperture data at various depths [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Particle Extraction:** Apply particle extraction to the IOS. This involves thresholding the IOS image to distinguish particles from the high-noise background [S1, unofficial]. The thresholded peaks correspond to particle locations.

5.  **3D Position Recovery:** Use the 2D-3D correspondence function (from Step 1) to convert the extracted 2D positions in the IOS slices back into 3D coordinates.

6.  **Volume Reconstruction:** Initialize the 3D particle volume by assigning values to voxels corresponding to the recovered 3D positions. The sources describe initializing the volume by assigning each voxel the minimum value of the pixels that correspond to it in the projection images [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- **Number of Cameras:** The method is designed to work with a large number of acquisition cameras. The sources cite a 12-camera CCD system [S1, unofficial].
- **Threshold:** A threshold value is required for particle extraction from the IOS. The optimal value depends on the noise level of the IOS background [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **High Noise:** The IOS background has a high noise rate compared to standard Tomo-PIV images, which may require careful thresholding to avoid false positives [S1, unofficial].
- **Calibration Accuracy:** The method relies heavily on the 2D-3D correspondence function (Vaish et al.). Errors in calibration will directly propagate to 3D position errors.

## Operating characteristics
- **Accuracy:** SAPIV achieves reconstruction quality comparable to MART (Multiplicative Algebraic Reconstruction Technique) but with significantly fewer cameras. Specifically, a 12-camera SAPIV setup matches the quality of a 4-camera MART setup [S1, unofficial].
- **Complexity:** The complexity is dominated by the synthetic aperture generation and the calibration mapping. It avoids the iterative convergence steps of MART, potentially offering faster reconstruction once the IOS is generated.
- **Memory:** Requires storage for the IOS image and the 3D voxel grid.
- **When to use:** Suitable for particle volume reconstruction where high resolution is needed but the number of available cameras is limited compared to traditional tomographic requirements.
- **When not to use:** If the IOS generation algorithm is not available or if the high noise in the IOS background cannot be managed by thresholding.

## Background
SAPIV was proposed by Belden et al. in 2010 [S1, unofficial]. It is inspired by synthetic aperture processing techniques used in interferometry to improve radar image resolution, specifically drawing from the work of Isaksen et al. (2000) and Wilburn et al. (2005) [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to enhance the resolution and accuracy of particle image velocimetry (PIV) volume reconstructions.
