# Conservative Rasterization

Also called: Conservative Rasterization via Geometric Dilatation, Conservative Rasterization via MSAA, GPU Voxelization Conservative Rasterization, Triangle Expansion for Voxelization

## Overview
This method prevents holes in voxelized geometry caused by triangles smaller than the voxel grid resolution by rasterizing all fragments that "touch" the triangle [S1, unofficial]. It achieves this by analytically expanding the triangle in clip space before rasterization, ensuring that any voxel potentially intersected by the triangle is covered [S2, unofficial]. Unlike hardware conservative rasterization (limited to NVIDIA) or Multi-Sample Anti-Aliasing (MSAA) activation [S1, unofficial], this geometric approach is hardware-agnostic but computationally more intensive, requiring a geometry shader for the expansion logic [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A triangle defined by three vertices in clip space (`pos[0..2]`). [S2, unofficial]
*   **Uniforms:**
    *   `halfPixel[3]`: `vec2` array representing half the pixel size in clip space for each of the three possible projection axes. [S2, unofficial]
    *   `viewProject[3]`: Array of 3 `mat4` matrices for view-projection transformations. [S2, unofficial]
    *   `viewProjectInverse[3]`: Array of 3 `mat4` matrices for inverse view-projection transformations. [S2, unofficial]
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Fragment Shader: `FragPos` (world space position), `ProjectPos` (clip space position of the expanded vertex), `BoundingBox` (min/max xy in clip space of the original triangle, enlarged). [S2, unofficial]
    *   Compute Buffer: Atomic counter updates for voxel occupancy. [S2, unofficial]

**Data Structures:**
*   **Edge Planes:** `vec3 edgePlanes[3]`. Each vector $(A, B, C)$ represents the homogeneous plane equation $Ax + By + Cz = 0$ passing through the origin for each triangle edge. [S2, unofficial]
*   **Triangle Plane:** `vec4 trianglePlane`. Represents the plane equation $Ax + By + Cz + D = 0$ containing the original triangle. [S2, unofficial]
*   **Intersection Lines:** `vec3 intersection[3]`. Direction vectors for the lines formed by the intersection of offset edge planes. [S2, unofficial]

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Select Projection Axis:** Determine the dominant axis of the triangle's face normal to select the appropriate projection matrix and half-pixel size. Calculate the normal $N = (pos[1] - pos[0]) \times (pos[2] - pos[0])$. Let $n_x, n_y, n_z$ be the absolute components. If $n_x > n_y$ and $n_x > n_z$, index $= 0$ (project onto YZ plane). Else if $n_y > n_x$ and $n_y > n_z$, index $= 1$ (project onto XZ plane). Else, index $= 2$ (project onto XY plane). [S2, unofficial]
2.  **Calculate Triangle Plane:** Compute the normal $N$ of the triangle in world space (or the space defined by the projection). Normalize it. Calculate $D = -P_0 \cdot N$. [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{trianglePlane} = (N_x, N_y, N_z, D) $$
    *Note: The source code uses `pos[0].xyz` which implies the vertices are in world space or the space where the plane equation is valid for the final voxelization step.* [S2, unofficial]
3.  **Fix Winding Order:** To prevent artifacts with back-facing triangles, ensure counter-clockwise winding relative to the view direction. Check the dot product of the triangle normal with the view direction $(0,0,1)$. If $N \cdot (0,0,1) < 0$, swap vertices 1 and 2. [S2, unofficial]
4.  **Calculate Homogeneous Edge Planes:** For each edge $i$ (connecting vertex $i$ to $i+1$, wrapping around), compute the plane passing through the origin using the cross product of the homogeneous vertex coordinates (xyw components). [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{edgePlanes}[0] = (pos[0].xyw - pos[2].xyw) \times pos[2].xyw $$
    $$ \text{edgePlanes}[1] = (pos[1].xyw - pos[0].xyw) \times pos[0].xyw $$
    $$ \text{edgePlanes}[2] = (pos[2].xyw - pos[1].xyw) \times pos[1].xyw $$
    *Note: In orthographic projection, $w=1$, so this effectively computes the 2D line normal in clip space.* [S2, unofficial]
5.  **Dilate Edge Planes:** Offset the homogeneous planes to expand the triangle. Subtract the projection of the half-pixel size onto the plane's normal from the plane's z-component (distance). [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{edgePlanes}[i].z \leftarrow \text{edgePlanes}[i].z - \text{dot}(\text{halfPixel}[\text{index}], |\text{edgePlanes}[i].xy|) $$
    *Note: $|\text{edgePlanes}[i].xy|$ denotes the absolute value of the x and y components.* [S2, unofficial]
6.  **Calculate Intersection Lines:** Compute the direction vector of the intersection line between two offset planes. The direction is the cross product of their normals. Normalize by dividing by the z-component. [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{intersection}[0] = \text{edgePlanes}[0] \times \text{edgePlanes}[1] $$
    $$ \text{intersection}[1] = \text{edgePlanes}[1] \times \text{edgePlanes}[2] $$
    $$ \text{intersection}[2] = \text{edgePlanes}[2] \times \text{edgePlanes}[0] $$
    $$ \text{intersection}[i].x \leftarrow \text{intersection}[i].x / \text{intersection}[i].z $$
    $$ \text{intersection}[i].y \leftarrow \text{intersection}[i].y / \text{intersection}[i].z $$
    *Note: The z-component of the intersection vector is effectively normalized to 1.* [S2, unofficial]
7.  **Compute Dilated Vertices:** Calculate the new vertices of the dilated triangle. The x and y coordinates are taken from the intersection line vectors. The z coordinate is solved using the original triangle's plane equation: [S2, unofficial]
    $$ z_i = -( \text{intersection}[i].x \cdot \text{trianglePlane}.x + \text{intersection}[i].y \cdot \text{trianglePlane}.y + \text{trianglePlane}.w ) / \text{trianglePlane}.z $$
    $$ \text{pos}[i].xyz = (\text{intersection}[i].x, \text{intersection}[i].y, z_i) $$
    *Pitfall: If $\text{trianglePlane}.z == 0$, the triangle is parallel to the view direction, leading to division by zero. The algorithm should return early or handle this degenerate case.* [S2, unofficial]
8.  **Transform and Output:** Transform the dilated vertices back to world space using the inverse view-projection matrix. Pass the clip-space positions to the fragment shader. [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{voxelPos} = \text{viewProjectInverse}[\text{index}] \times \text{pos}[i] $$
    $$ \text{FragPos} = \text{voxelPos}.xyz $$
    $$ \text{ProjectPos} = \text{pos}[i].xyz $$
    Emit the vertex. [S2, unofficial]
9.  **Fragment Shader Culling:** In the fragment shader, discard fragments outside the bounding box of the original triangle (enlarged by half-pixel) to remove artifacts caused by the expansion. [S2, unofficial]
    $$ \text{if } (\text{ProjectPos}.x < \text{BoundingBox}.x \lor \text{ProjectPos}.y < \text{BoundingBox}.y \lor \text{ProjectPos}.x > \text{BoundingBox}.z \lor \text{ProjectPos}.y > \text{BoundingBox}.w) \text{ discard} $$
    *Note: `BoundingBox` is calculated in the geometry shader as the min/max xy of the original triangle, enlarged by `halfPixel`.* [S2, unofficial]
10. **Voxel Counting:** Map the world space position (`FragPos`) to voxel coordinates and atomically increment the count. [S2, unofficial]
    $$ x = \lfloor (\text{FragPos}.x - \text{boxMin}.x) / \text{step} \rfloor $$
    $$ y = \lfloor (\text{FragPos}.y - \text{boxMin}.y) / \text{step} \rfloor $$
    $$ z = \lfloor (\text{FragPos}.z - \text{boxMin}.z) / \text{step} \rfloor $$
    $$ \text{index} = y \cdot (\text{resolution}.z \cdot \text{resolution}.x) + z \cdot \text{resolution}.x + x $$
    $$ \text{atomicAdd}(\text{cnts}[\text{index}], 1) $$ [S2, unofficial]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def cross(v1, v2):
    return (v1[1]*v2[2] - v1[2]*v2[1], v1[2]*v2[0] - v1[0]*v2[2], v1[0]*v2[1] - v1[1]*v2[0])

def dot(v1, v2):
    return v1[0]*v2[0] + v1[1]*v2[1] + v1[2]*v2[2]

def abs_vec(v):
    return tuple(abs(c) for c in v)

def min_vec(v1, v2):
    return tuple(min(a, b) for a, b in zip(v1, v2))

def max_vec(v1, v2):
    return tuple(max(a, b) for a, b in zip(v1, v2))

def normalize(v):
    length = math.sqrt(dot(v, v))
    return tuple(c / length for c in v)

def select_projection_axis(pos):
    p1 = (pos[1][0]-pos[0][0], pos[1][1]-pos[0][1], pos[1][2]-pos[0][2])
    p2 = (pos[2][0]-pos[0][0], pos[2][1]-pos[0][1], pos[2][2]-pos[0][2])
    face_normal = cross(p1, p2)
    n_dx, n_dy, n_dz = abs(face_normal[0]), abs(face_normal[1]), abs(face_normal[2])
    if n_dx > n_dy and n_dx > n_dz:
        return 0
    elif n_dy > n_dx and n_dy > n_dz:
        return 1
    else:
        return 2

def calc_aabb(pos, half_pixel):
    min_xy = min_vec(pos[0][:2], min_vec(pos[1][:2], pos[2][:2]))
    max_xy = max_vec(pos[0][:2], max_vec(pos[1][:2], pos[2][:2]))
    min_xy = (min_xy[0] - half_pixel[0], min_xy[1] - half_pixel[1])
    max_xy = (max_xy[0] + half_pixel[0], max_xy[1] + half_pixel[1])
    return (min_xy[0], min_xy[1], max_xy[0], max_xy[1])

def conservative_rasterization_expand(pos, half_pixel, view_project_inv, project_index):
    # pos is list of 3 tuples (x, y, z, w)
    # 1. Calculate Triangle Plane
    p1 = (pos[1][0]-pos[0][0], pos[1][1]-pos[0][1], pos[1][2]-pos[0][2])
    p2 = (pos[2][0]-pos[0][0], pos[2][1]-pos[0][1], pos[2][2]-pos[0][2])
    triangle_normal = normalize(cross(p1, p2))
    triangle_plane_w = -dot((pos[0][0], pos[0][1], pos[0][2]), triangle_normal)
    
    # 2. Fix Winding Order
    if dot(triangle_normal, (0.0, 0.0, 1.0)) < 0.0:
        pos[1], pos[2] = pos[2], pos[1]
        
    # Pitfall: Check for degenerate plane
    if abs(triangle_normal[2]) < 1e-6:
        return []
        
    # 3. Calculate Homogeneous Edge Planes
    edge_planes = [None, None, None]
    edge_planes[0] = cross((pos[0][0]-pos[2][0], pos[0][1]-pos[2][1], pos[0][3]-pos[2][3]), (pos[2][0], pos[2][1], pos[2][3]))
    edge_planes[1] = cross((pos[1][0]-pos[0][0], pos[1][1]-pos[0][1], pos[1][3]-pos[0][3]), (pos[0][0], pos[0][1], pos[0][3]))
    edge_planes[2] = cross((pos[2][0]-pos[1][0], pos[2][1]-pos[1][1], pos[2][3]-pos[1][3]), (pos[1][0], pos[1][1], pos[1][3]))
    
    # 4. Dilate Edge Planes
    for i in range(3):
        dilate_dist = dot(half_pixel, abs_vec((edge_planes[i][0], edge_planes[i][1])))
        edge_planes[i] = (edge_planes[i][0], edge_planes[i][1], edge_planes[i][2] - dilate_dist)
        
    # 5. Calculate Intersection Lines
    intersection = [None, None, None]
    intersection[0] = cross(edge_planes[0], edge_planes[1])
    intersection[1] = cross(edge_planes[1], edge_planes[2])
    intersection[2] = cross(edge_planes[2], edge_planes[0])
    
    # Normalize by z-component
    for i in range(3):
        if abs(intersection[i][2]) > 1e-6:
            intersection[i] = (intersection[i][0]/intersection[i][2], intersection[i][1]/intersection[i][2], 1.0)
            
    # 6. Compute Dilated Vertices
    dilated_pos = [None, None, None]
    for i in range(3):
        z_val = -(intersection[i][0] * triangle_normal[0] + intersection[i][1] * triangle_normal[1] + triangle_plane_w) / triangle_normal[2]
        dilated_pos[i] = (intersection[i][0], intersection[i][1], z_val, 1.0)
        
    # 7. Transform to World Space and Prepare Outputs
    outputs = []
    bounding_box = calc_aabb(pos, half_pixel)
    
    for i in range(3):
        # Transform dilated vertex to world space (simplified matrix-vector mult)
        v = dilated_pos[i]
        voxel_pos = (
            view_project_inv[0][0]*v[0] + view_project_inv[0][1]*v[1] + view_project_inv[0][2]*v[2] + view_project_inv[0][3],
            view_project_inv[1][0]*v[0] + view_project_inv[1][1]*v[1] + view_project_inv[1][2]*v[2] + view_project_inv[1][3],
            view_project_inv[2][0]*v[0] + view_project_inv[2][1]*v[1] + view_project_inv[2][2]*v[2] + view_project_inv[2][3]
        )
        outputs.append({
            'FragPos': voxel_pos,
            'ProjectPos': (dilated_pos[i][0], dilated_pos[i][1], dilated_pos[i][2]),
            'BoundingBox': bounding_box
        })
    return outputs

def fragment_shader(FragPos, ProjectPos, BoundingBox, boxMin, step, resolution, cnts):
    if (ProjectPos[0] < BoundingBox[0] or ProjectPos[1] < BoundingBox[1] or 
        ProjectPos[0] > BoundingBox[2] or ProjectPos[1] > BoundingBox[3]):
        return None
        
    x = int((FragPos[0] - boxMin[0]) / step)
    y = int((FragPos[1] - boxMin[1]) / step)
    z = int((FragPos[2] - boxMin[2]) / step)
    
    index = y * (resolution[2] * resolution[0]) + z * resolution[0] + x
    
    # Standard GPU atomic add simulation
    cnts[index] = cnts.get(index, 0) + 1
    
    return (0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The expansion logic is performed per triangle in the geometry shader. It involves a constant number of vector operations (cross products, dot products, normalizations). The fragment shader performs a constant number of comparisons and an atomic add. [S1, S2, unofficial]
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the voxel count buffer (`int cnts[]`) and uniform matrices. [S2, unofficial]
*   **Performance:** The geometric dilatation approach is slower than hardware conservative rasterization (if available) but faster than other software expansion techniques. MSAA activation is described as "not too expensive" and producing few noticeable artifacts, but the geometric dilatation method is more explicit and controllable. [S1, S2, unofficial]
*   **Strengths:** Hardware-agnostic (works on any GPU with geometry shaders), prevents holes effectively for small triangles, explicit control over the dilation amount. [S1, S2, unofficial]
*   **Limitations:** Requires a geometry shader, which can be a bottleneck on some hardware. Computationally more intensive than simple rasterization. Degenerate cases (triangle parallel to view) must be handled. [S2, unofficial]
*   **When to use:** When hardware conservative rasterization is not available (e.g., non-NVIDIA GPUs) and MSAA is insufficient or undesirable. When precise control over voxel coverage is needed. [S1, S2, unofficial]
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and hardware conservative rasterization is available. When the geometry is large and the overhead of the geometry shader is prohibitive. [S1, S2, unofficial]

## Background
This method is a software implementation of conservative rasterization specifically tailored for GPU voxelization. It is used in voxel cone tracing pipelines to ensure complete surface coverage for light injection and opacity voxelization. [S1, unofficial] The technique of geometric dilatation in clip space is detailed in "The Basics of GPU Voxelization" and related blog posts. [S2, unofficial] It addresses the limitation of traditional rasterization, which ignores fragments that do not strictly intersect the triangle's interior, leading to holes in the voxel grid for small or thin triangles. [S1, unofficial]
