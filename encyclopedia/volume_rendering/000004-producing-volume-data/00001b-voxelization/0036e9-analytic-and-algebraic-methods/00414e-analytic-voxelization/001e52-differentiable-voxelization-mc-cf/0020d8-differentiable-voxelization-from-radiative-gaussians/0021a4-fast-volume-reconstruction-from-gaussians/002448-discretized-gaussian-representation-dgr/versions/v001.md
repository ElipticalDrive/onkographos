# Discretized Gaussian Representation (DGR)


## Overview
Discretized Gaussian Representation (DGR) is a framework for direct 3D tomographic volume reconstruction that models the volume as a sum of isotropic Gaussian functions, discretized onto a 3D grid to enable end-to-end differentiable optimization [S1]. It addresses the integration bias and density inconsistency inherent in standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) when applied to CT by ensuring gradient flow through the discretization process, unlike naive rounding methods [S1]. It builds on [Fast Volume Reconstruction from Gaussians](2575-fast-volume-reconstruction-from-gaussians.md) by introducing a "Discretize and Align" mechanism that preserves differentiability while allowing parallel aggregation of Gaussian contributions into a voxel grid.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A set of $n$ Gaussians, each defined by a continuous mean $\mu_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, a covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ (isotropic, diagonal), and a scalar intensity $I_i \in \mathbb{R}^+$.
*   Target volume dimensions $w \times h \times c$.
*   Local region size parameters $w_0, h_0, c_0$ (odd integers).

**Outputs:**
*   Reconstructed voxel grid $V \in \mathbb{R}^{w \times h \times c}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gaussian Parameters:** Stored as tensors of shape $(n, 3)$ for $\mu$, $(n, 3, 3)$ for $\Sigma$ (or $(n, 3)$ for isotropic $\sigma$), and $(n,)$ for $I$.
*   **Volume Grid:** $V$ initialized to zeros.
*   **Local Box Coordinates:** A tensor $B_0$ of shape $(w_0, h_0, c_0, 3)$ containing the 3D coordinates of the cuboid centered at the origin. This is constant for all Gaussians due to translation invariance.

**Parameters:**
*   $w_0, h_0, c_0$: Dimensions of the effective proximity cuboid. Must be odd integers. Typical values depend on the Gaussian spread $\sigma$; the cuboid should cover the significant mass of the Gaussian (e.g., $\pm 3\sigma$). [S1]
*   Isotropic Covariance: $\Sigma_i = \sigma_i^2 I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix. This is chosen because CT tissues, particularly soft tissues, exhibit isotropic attenuation properties [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Gaussian Effect Confinement:** For each Gaussian $i$, define its influence region as a cuboid $B_0$ of size $w_0 \times h_0 \times c_0$ centered on the voxel it primarily affects. The set of 3D coordinates within this cuboid is $B_0 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ [S1].

2.  **Discretize and Align:** To maintain differentiability, do not round $\mu_i$ to the nearest integer. Instead, calculate the contribution at discretized grid positions aligned with the floored integer coordinates of the mean.
    *   Compute the floored mean: $\lfloor \mu_i \rfloor$ (element-wise floor).
    *   Compute the residual offset: $\Delta \mu_i = \mu_i - \lfloor \mu_i \rfloor$.
    *   Define the relative coordinates $B$ for the local box relative to the continuous mean: $B = B_0 - \mu_i$. Note that $B$ is a constant tensor pattern shifted by $\mu_i$.
    *   Transform coordinates to the discretized box $\lfloor B \rfloor$ aligned with the floored mean:
        $$ [B]_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,d} = B_{w_0,h_0,c_0,d} - \Delta \mu_{n,1,1,d} $$
        Here, $\Delta \mu$ is broadcast across the spatial dimensions $w_0, h_0, c_0$. This ensures the Gaussian contribution is evaluated at the correct position on the discretized grid relative to $\lfloor \mu_i \rfloor$ [S1].

3.  **Fast Volume Reconstruction (Compute $D^2$):** Compute the squared Mahalanobis distance $D^2$ for each Gaussian in the local region in parallel.
    *   The covariance matrix $C$ is $\Sigma_i$. For isotropic Gaussians, $C^{-1}$ is diagonal with elements $1/\sigma_i^2$.
    *   Decompose $D^2$ to optimize computation using Einstein summation convention (sum over repeated index $d$):
        $$ D^2 = (B^\top C^{-1} B) - (B^\top C^{-1} \Delta \mu) - (\Delta \mu^\top C^{-1} B) + (\Delta \mu^\top C^{-1} \Delta \mu) $$
        Specifically:
        *   $B^\top C^{-1} B = \sum_d [B]_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,d} C_{n,d,d}^{-1} [B]_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,d}$
        *   $B^\top C^{-1} \Delta \mu = \sum_d [B]_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,d} C_{n,d,d}^{-1} \Delta \mu_{n,1,1,d}$
        *   $\Delta \mu^\top C^{-1} \Delta \mu = \sum_d \Delta \mu_{n,1,1,d} C_{n,d,d}^{-1} \Delta \mu_{n,1,1,d}$
    *   This decomposition reduces time complexity without increasing space complexity, as $B$ (the spatial grid) is reused [S1].

4.  **Compute Contribution:** Calculate the contribution tensor $\Gamma$ for the local region:
    $$ \Gamma_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0} = e^{-\frac{1}{2} D^2_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0}} \cdot I_n $$
    where $I_n$ is broadcast to the spatial dimensions. $\Gamma \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times w_0 \times h_0 \times c_0}$ [S1].

5.  **Aggregation:** Update the volume grid $V$ by adding the contributions $\Gamma$ to the corresponding voxel locations. For each Gaussian $i$ and local coordinate $(x,y,z)$ in the box:
    $$ V_{\lfloor \mu_i \rfloor + (x,y,z)} \leftarrow V_{\lfloor \mu_i \rfloor + (x,y,z)} + \Gamma_{i,x,y,z} $$
    This aggregation is performed in a highly parallelized manner for all Gaussians [S1].

6.  **Optimization:** Project the reconstructed volume $V$ into the measurement domain (e.g., sinogram space via Radon transform or similar projection operator) and compare with measured data. Compute loss and backpropagate gradients to update $\mu_i, \sigma_i, I_i$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def discretized_gaussian_representation(gaussians, volume_shape, box_size):
    """
    Reconstructs a 3D volume from a set of Gaussians using DGR.
    
    Args:
        gaussians: Dict with keys 'mu' (n, 3), 'sigma' (n,), 'I' (n,)
        volume_shape: Tuple (w, h, c)
        box_size: Tuple (w0, h0, c0) - odd integers
        
    Returns:
        V: Reconstructed volume tensor (w, h, c)
    """
    n = gaussians['mu'].shape[0]
    w0, h0, c0 = box_size
    d = 3
    
    # 1. Create constant local box coordinates B0 centered at origin
    # B0 shape: (w0, h0, c0, 3)
    coords = torch.meshgrid(
        torch.arange(w0, dtype=torch.float32) - w0 // 2,
        torch.arange(h0, dtype=torch.float32) - h0 // 2,
        torch.arange(c0, dtype=torch.float32) - c0 // 2,
        indexing='ij'
    )
    B0 = torch.stack(coords, dim=-1) # (w0, h0, c0, 3)
    
    # 2. Extract Gaussian parameters
    mu = gaussians['mu'] # (n, 3)
    sigma = gaussians['sigma'] # (n,)
    I = gaussians['I'] # (n,)
    
    # Covariance inverse for isotropic Gaussians: C_inv = 1/sigma^2
    C_inv = 1.0 / (sigma ** 2) # (n,)
    
    # 3. Discretize and Align
    # Floor the mean
    mu_floor = torch.floor(mu) # (n, 3)
    # Residual delta_mu
    delta_mu = mu - mu_floor # (n, 3)
    
    # Relative coordinates B = B0 - mu
    # B0 is (w0, h0, c0, 3), mu is (n, 3)
    # Broadcast mu to (n, 1, 1, 3)
    B = B0.unsqueeze(0) - mu.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1) # (n, w0, h0, c0, 3)
    
    # Transform to discretized box aligned with floored mean
    # [B] = B - delta_mu
    # delta_mu is (n, 3), broadcast to (n, 1, 1, 3)
    B_disc = B - delta_mu.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1) # (n, w0, h0, c0, 3)
    
    # 4. Fast Volume Reconstruction: Compute D^2
    # D^2 = (B^T C^-1 B) - (B^T C^-1 delta_mu) - (delta_mu^T C^-1 B) + (delta_mu^T C^-1 delta_mu)
    # Using Einstein summation: sum over d (last dim)
    
    # Term 1: B^T C^-1 B
    # B_disc shape: (n, w0, h0, c0, 3)
    # C_inv shape: (n,) -> (n, 1, 1, 1, 1)
    term1 = torch.sum(B_disc ** 2 * C_inv.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1), dim=-1) # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # Term 2: B^T C^-1 delta_mu
    # delta_mu shape: (n, 3) -> (n, 1, 1, 1, 3)
    term2 = torch.sum(B_disc * delta_mu.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1) * C_inv.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1), dim=-1) # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # Term 3: delta_mu^T C^-1 B (same as term 2 for scalar product)
    term3 = term2
    
    # Term 4: delta_mu^T C^-1 delta_mu
    # Sum over d for each n
    term4 = torch.sum(delta_mu ** 2 * C_inv, dim=-1) # (n,)
    
    D2 = term1 - term2 - term3 + term4.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1) # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # 5. Compute Contribution Gamma
    Gamma = torch.exp(-0.5 * D2) * I.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1) # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # 6. Aggregation
    # Initialize volume
    V = torch.zeros(volume_shape, dtype=torch.float32)
    
    # For each Gaussian, add Gamma to the volume at position floor(mu) + local_coords
    # This requires scatter_add or a loop. For dense implementation, we can use scatter_nd if available,
    # or loop over n if n is small. Here we assume a loop for clarity and correctness.
    for i in range(n):
        # Base index
        base_idx = mu_floor[i].long() # (3,)
        
        # Local indices
        local_indices = torch.meshgrid(
            torch.arange(w0),
            torch.arange(h0),
            torch.arange(c0),
            indexing='ij'
        )
        local_indices = torch.stack(local_indices, dim=-1) # (w0, h0, c0, 3)
        
        # Global indices
        global_indices = base_idx.unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0) + local_indices # (w0, h0, c0, 3)
        
        # Extract coordinates
        x = global_indices[:, :, :, 0]
        y = global_indices[:, :, :, 1]
        z = global_indices[:, :, :, 2]
        
        # Clip to volume bounds to avoid out-of-bounds errors
        x = torch.clamp(x, 0, volume_shape[0] - 1)
        y = torch.clamp(y, 0, volume_shape[1] - 1)
        z = torch.clamp(z, 0, volume_shape[2] - 1)
        
        # Add contribution
        V[x, y, z] += Gamma[i, :, :, :]
        
    return V
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-differentiability:** Directly rounding $\mu$ to the nearest integer for indexing makes the process non-differentiable, hindering optimization [S1]. The "Discretize and Align" method avoids this by using the residual $\Delta \mu$ [S1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Gaussians near the volume boundary may have contributions that fall outside the grid. These must be clipped or handled appropriately to avoid out-of-bounds errors [G].
*   **Cuboid Size:** If $w_0, h_0, c_0$ are too small, significant Gaussian mass is truncated, leading to reconstruction artifacts. If too large, computational cost increases [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The Fast Volume Reconstruction technique reduces time complexity by decomposing the Mahalanobis distance calculation and reusing the spatial grid $B$. The exact complexity is not given, but it is described as "highly parallelized" and "minimal overhead" compared to direct computation [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Space complexity is not increased by the decomposition, as $B$ is reused [S1].
*   **Performance:** DGR achieves superior reconstruction quality and runtime performance across various CT reconstruction scenarios compared to traditional DLR and 3DGS [S1].
*   **Strengths:** End-to-end differentiable optimization, efficient parallel aggregation, handles isotropic tissues well [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the assumption of isotropic attenuation, which may not hold for all tissue types or anisotropic structures. The cuboid confinement may lose information from distant Gaussians, though this is mitigated by trainable Gaussians [S1].

## Background
DGR was proposed in 2025 by W. King et al. in the paper "Discretized Gaussian Representation for Tomographic Reconstruction" [S1]. It addresses limitations in existing differentiable volumetric reconstruction methods, particularly the integration bias and density inconsistency of 3D Gaussian Splatting when applied to CT [S1]. The method is designed to be a plug-and-play module that can be integrated into existing 3D Gaussian-based pipelines [S1].
