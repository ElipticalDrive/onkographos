# Dense Volume SDF Generation

Also called: Dense Grid SDF, Mesh-to-Volume Conversion, SDF Voxelization, mesh2volume, meshToDistanceVolume

## Overview
Dense Volume SDF Generation converts a continuous triangle mesh into a dense 3D grid where every voxel stores the exact signed Euclidean distance to the mesh surface [S3]. Unlike sparse narrow-band methods that only store distances near the surface, this method populates the entire bounding volume, making it suitable for full-data access, simulation, and direct marching cubes extraction [S3]. It differs from [Signed Distance Field (SDF) Generation from Scanned Surfaces](854-signed-distance-field-sdf-generation-from-scanned-surfaces.md) by operating on clean B-rep meshes rather than point clouds, and from [SDF-based Binary Voxelization](2402-sdf-based-binary-voxelization.md) by retaining continuous distance values rather than quantizing to binary occupancy [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A triangle mesh defined by a vertex array $V$ (size $N_v \times 3$) and a face array $F$ (size $N_f \times 3$) containing vertex indices [S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A 3D tensor $D$ of shape $(H, W, D)$ containing signed distance values, typically stored in row-major order [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** A 3D array `sdf[H][W][D]` of type `float` [S2, unofficial].
*   **Mesh Data:** `vertices` as `std::vector<Eigen::Vector3f>` or `numpy.ndarray` of floats; `faces` as `std::vector<Eigen::Vector3i>` or `numpy.ndarray` of integers [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `voxelSize`: The physical size of a voxel edge. Often calculated automatically to target a specific voxel count (e.g., $10^7$ voxels) [S3].
*   `origin`: The 3D coordinate of the grid's first voxel corner [S3].
*   `dimensions`: The integer resolution $(H, W, D)$ of the grid [S3].
*   `maxDistSq`: The squared maximum distance to compute. Voxels farther than $\sqrt{maxDistSq}$ are marked as null/empty [S3]. Typical value: $(3 \times \text{voxelSize})^2$ [S3].
*   `signMode`: Algorithm for determining inside/outside. `HoleWindingRule` is robust for holes and self-intersections [S3].
*   `nullOutsideMinMax`: Boolean. If true, voxels outside the bounding box of the mesh are not calculated [S3].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Grid Setup:**
    Calculate the bounding box of the mesh. Determine `voxelSize`, `origin`, and `dimensions` [S3].
    $$ \text{voxelSize} = \frac{\max(\text{extent}_x, \text{extent}_y, \text{extent}_z)}{\text{dim} - 1} $$
    where `dim` is the target resolution [S1, unofficial].
    Initialize the output grid `sdf` with dimensions $(H, W, D)$ [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Parallel Iteration:**
    Iterate over every voxel index $i$ from $0$ to $H \times W \times D - 1$ [S2, unofficial].
    Decode coordinates:
    $$ d = i \pmod D $$
    $$ w = \lfloor i / D \rfloor \pmod W $$
    $$ h = \lfloor i / (W \times D) \rfloor $$

3.  **Voxel Geometry:**
    Calculate the voxel center in world space:
    $$ \text{center} = \text{origin} + (w + 0.5, h + 0.5, d + 0.5) \times \text{voxelSize} $$
    [S2, unofficial][S3].
    Initialize `sdf[h][w][d]` to infinity ($\infty$) [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Distance Calculation:**
    For each triangle face $f$ in the mesh with vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$:
    a. Compute the closest point $P_{closest}$ on the triangle to `center` [S2, unofficial].
    b. Compute Euclidean distance:
    $$ \text{dist} = \| \text{center} - P_{closest} \| $$
    c. If `dist` < `sdf[h][w][d]`, update:
    $$ \text{sdf}[h][w][d] = \text{dist} $$
    [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Sign Determination:**
    Determine if the voxel center is inside or outside the mesh [S2, unofficial].
    a. Cast a ray from `center` to the origin $(0,0,0)$ (or any point known to be outside) [S2, unofficial].
    b. Count intersections with all mesh triangles. Let $N_{intersect}$ be the count of valid intersections where distance $\ge 0$ [S2, unofficial].
    c. If $N_{intersect}$ is odd, the point is inside. Negate the distance:
    $$ \text{sdf}[h][w][d] = -\text{sdf}[h][w][d] $$
    [S2, unofficial].
    *Note:* For robustness with holes, `HoleWindingRule` or Fast Winding Number (FWN) is preferred over simple parity [S3].

6.  **Culling:**
    If `nullOutsideMinMax` is true, set `sdf[h][w][d]` to null/NaN if `dist` > $\sqrt{\text{maxDistSq}}$ [S3].

## Subtopics

### [Cube SDF Grid Construction](4883-cube-sdf-grid-construction.md)
Constructs a discrete Signed Distance Field (SDF) grid for a cube centered in the grid using analytical distance formulas for interior and exterior voxels. It differs from its sibling [Sphere SDF Grid Construction](4895-sphere-sdf-grid-construction.md) by using box-based min/max operations rather than spherical symmetry and Euclidean distance from the center [G].

### [Naive Jumping Farthest Away (JFA)](7621-naive-jumping-farthest-away-jfa.md)
Naive Jumping Farthest Away (JFA) is a GPU-based algorithm for computing the Signed Distance Field (SDF) and the position of the closest surface point for a dense 3D voxel grid. It solves the problem of efficiently propagating distance information from known surface voxels to empty space using a multi-pass approach where the search radius $K$ is halved in each iteration. It differs from its sibling, Cube SDF Grid Construction, by using a logarithmic halving stride strategy rather than a fixed local neighborhood expansion, and from its child, Sequential JFA, by executing the propagation loop in parallel on the GPU rather than sequentially on the CPU.

### [General Mesh SDF and Occupancy Voxelization](10658-general-mesh-sdf-and-occupancy-voxelization.md)
This method converts a solid boundary representation (B-rep) into a regular volumetric occupancy grid and optionally a signed-distance field (SDF). It solves the problem of discretizing continuous CAD geometry into a dense 3D grid suitable for downstream applications such as 3D-print lattice generation, finite element (FE) meshing, and collision queries. Unlike core B-rep operations, this is an application-level capability that relies on tessellated mesh data rather than exact analytical surfaces.

### [SDF Voxel Grid Generation (Brute Force)](15296-sdf-voxel-grid-generation-brute-force.md)
This method generates a dense Signed Distance Field (SDF) by explicitly computing the distance from every voxel in a 3D grid to the nearest surface of an input triangle mesh. It solves the problem of converting a polygonal representation into a continuous scalar field suitable for raymarching or simulation. Unlike hierarchical methods or approximate techniques, this approach guarantees exact distance values for the grid resolution by iterating through all mesh triangles for each voxel, resulting in high accuracy at the cost of quadratic computational complexity relative to the number of voxels and triangles.

### Volumetric Distance Function Computation
A scalar field defined on a voxel grid where empty voxels are populated with the distance from their center to the nearest point on the surface, derived from the initial voxelization [S4, unofficial]. It operates by several 'sweepings' over the volume to efficiently compute distances for all empty voxels based on the surface-intersecting voxels [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_v \cdot N_f)$, where $N_v$ is the number of voxels and $N_f$ is the number of mesh faces [S2, unofficial]. This is quadratic relative to resolution and linear relative to mesh complexity.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_v)$ for the dense grid storage [S3].
*   **Performance:** Suitable for small to medium-sized meshes where performance and memory are not bottlenecks [S3]. Low resolution (e.g., $64^3$) results in poor reconstruction quality; higher resolutions significantly improve results but increase memory usage [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides exact voxel-by-voxel control; ideal for simulation, remeshing, and exporting to machine learning models [S3].
*   **Limitations:** High memory consumption for large volumes; slow for high-resolution grids with complex meshes due to the brute-force triangle intersection checks [S2, unofficial][S3].
*   **When to use:** When full data access is required, or when using marching cubes directly on the volume [S3].
*   **When not to use:** For very large meshes or high-resolution grids where sparse narrow-band methods (like OpenVDB) are more efficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the need to convert continuous parametric objects (like triangle meshes) into digital representations defined on a 3D grid [S1, unofficial]. It is widely used in computer graphics for volume rendering, simulation, and 3D printing. Libraries such as OpenVDB provide narrow-band algorithms, but dense generation is preferred for specific applications like machine learning data preparation or exact geometric analysis [S1, unofficial][S3]. The technique is implemented in various open-source projects, including `Mesh2Volume` and `meshlib` [S1, unofficial][S3].
