# Analytic Tetrahedron Voxelization

Also called: Exact Conservative Voxelization of Tetrahedra, Graph-traversal voxelization, Remeshing-based voxelization, analytic volume sampling of tetrahedra, voxelization of tetrahedra

## Overview
Analytic Tetrahedron Voxelization is a method for robustly intersecting convex polyhedra (specifically tetrahedra) and computing polynomial integrals over the resulting intersection domains, applied primarily to the exact mass-conservative voxelization of tetrahedra for cosmological N-body systems [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard binary voxelization which only stores occupancy, this method computes physically conservative moments (volume, linear, quadratic) such that the sum of voxelized moments equals the analytic moments of the input tetrahedron up to floating-point precision [S2, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Monte Carlo 2D Voxelization](1734-monte-carlo-2d-voxelization.md) by providing deterministic, exact conservation without stochastic noise, and differs from [Analytic Moment Voxelization](3307-analytic-moment-voxelization.md) by focusing specifically on the geometric robustness of tetrahedral clipping via graph traversal rather than general moment approximation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates by converting a tetrahedron into a set of clip planes, identifying voxels that intersect the tetrahedron, clipping the tetrahedron against the voxel boundaries using a graph-based traversal, and integrating polynomial density fields over the resulting clipped polyhedra.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Tetrahedron Vertices:** Four 3D points $v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3$.
*   **Grid Parameters:** Grid dimensions $n = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$, cell sizes $d = (d_x, d_y, d_z)$, and polynomial order $P \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Coordinate System:** The grid covers a specific world space. The method assumes a mapping where world coordinates are converted to grid indices. For a grid covering $[-1, 1]^D$, the step size is $\text{step}_i = 2.0 / N_i$ and index $idx = \lfloor (x + 1) / \text{step}_i \rfloor$ [Parent, 4969-analytic-voxelization.md].

**Outputs:**
*   **Moments Buffer:** An array of pointers `moments[10]` storing the accumulated moments for each voxel in row-major C order. The size is at least $n_x n_y n_z$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Orientation Buffer:** A buffer `orient` of size $(n_x+1)(n_y+1)(n_z+1)$ storing orientation flags for grid points [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The implementation relies on specific structures to manage the planar graph representation of the polyhedra and the grid state [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

1.  **`r3d_plane`**: Defines a clip plane.
    *   `n`: Unit normal vector $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$.
    *   `d`: Signed distance from the origin to the plane.
2.  **`r3d_dest_grid`**: The destination grid container.
    *   `n`: Grid dimensions $(n_x, n_y, n_z)$.
    *   `d`: Cell sizes $(d_x, d_y, d_z)$.
    *   `polyorder`: Integer, 0 (constant/volume), 1 (linear), or 2 (quadratic).
    *   `moments`: Array of `r3d_real*` pointers (size 10). Indices correspond to moments:
        *   0: Volume ($1$)
        *   1-3: Linear ($x, y, z$)
        *   4-9: Quadratic ($x^2, y^2, z^2, xy, xz, yz$)
    *   `orient`: Pointer to `r3d_orientation` buffer.
    *   `bufsz`: Buffer size.
3.  **`r3d_orientation`**: Stores the status of a point relative to clip planes.
    *   `fdist[4]`: Signed distances to the four clip planes.
    *   `fflags`: `unsigned char` bit flags. Bit $f$ is set if the point is outside plane $f$ (distance $> 0$).
4.  **Polyhedron Representation**: Vertices and neighbor indices, without explicit face normals. Normals are derived or implicit in the clipping logic [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Preconditioning and Orientation:**
    Ensure the tetrahedron vertices are oriented such that the volume is positive. If the orientation function `r3du_orient(v0, v1, v2, v3)` returns negative, swap vertices $v_2$ and $v_3$ [S2, unofficial].

2.  **Face Conversion:**
    Convert the vertex representation to a face representation to obtain the four clip planes. Each face $f$ is defined by a unit normal $\mathbf{n}_f$ and signed distance $d_f$ such that $\mathbf{n}_f \cdot \mathbf{x} + d_f \le 0$ for points inside the tetrahedron [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Voxel Search:**
    Identify voxels that potentially intersect the tetrahedron. Two methods are described:
    *   **Brute-Force:** Check every grid point against each tetrahedron face. Scales as $g^3$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Binary Space Partitioning (BSP):** Use a spatial tree to prune empty regions. Scales as $g^2 \log g$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* Brute-force is faster for coarser grids due to BSP overhead; BSP overtakes brute-force at resolution $128^3$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Voxel Classification:**
    For each candidate voxel, determine its relationship to the tetrahedron using the orientation buffer:
    *   Calculate the signed distance of the voxel's 8 corners to the 4 clip planes.
    *   Compute `andcmp` (bitwise AND of all corner flags) and `orcmp` (bitwise OR of all corner flags).
    *   If `andcmp == 0x0f` (all bits set, meaning all corners are inside), the voxel is **Type 1 (Interior)**.
    *   If `orcmp != 0x0f` (some corners are outside), the voxel is **Type 3 (Boundary)** and requires clipping.
    *   If `andcmp == 0x00`, the voxel is outside and ignored [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Clipping (Graph Traversal):**
    For Type 3 voxels, clip the tetrahedron against the voxel boundaries.
    *   Represent the polyhedron as a planar graph with vertices and neighbors [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform a depth-first graph traversal to clip the polyhedron against the voxel's faces.
    *   Insert new vertices in the correct order on the fly. No post-clipping reordering is required [S1, unofficial].
    *   This ensures topological validity and geometric robustness without auxiliary checks or high-precision arithmetic [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Integration (Reduction):**
    Compute the moments of the clipped polyhedron (or the full tetrahedron for Type 1 voxels) and accumulate them into the grid.
    *   For **Type 1 (Interior)** voxels, the voxel is fully contained. The moments are computed analytically for the full cell volume $V = d_x d_y d_z$.
    *   For **Type 3 (Boundary)** voxels, the moments are computed over the clipped polyhedron volume using simplicial decomposition or direct integration over the planar graph [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas for Moment Accumulation

For a voxel fully contained in the tetrahedron (Type 1), with cell size $d_x, d_y, d_z$ and grid index $(i, j, k)$, the moments are calculated as follows [S2, unofficial]:

Let $V = d_x d_y d_z$.

*   **Volume (Order 0):**
    $$ M_0 = V $$

*   **Linear Moments (Order 1):**
    $$ M_1 = V \cdot d_x \cdot (i + 0.5) $$
    $$ M_2 = V \cdot d_y \cdot (j + 0.5) $$
    $$ M_3 = V \cdot d_z \cdot (k + 0.5) $$

*   **Quadratic Moments (Order 2):**
    $$ M_4 = V \cdot \frac{1}{3} d_x^2 (1 + 3i + 3i^2) $$
    $$ M_5 = V \cdot \frac{1}{3} d_y^2 (1 + 3j + 3j^2) $$
    $$ M_6 = V \cdot \frac{1}{3} d_z^2 (1 + 3k + 3k^2) $$
    $$ M_7 = V \cdot \frac{1}{4} d_x d_y (1 + 2i)(1 + 2j) $$
    $$ M_8 = V \cdot \frac{1}{4} d_x d_z (1 + 2i)(1 + 2k) $$
    $$ M_9 = V \cdot \frac{1}{4} d_y d_z (1 + 2j)(1 + 2k) $$

*Note: The formulas above assume the grid origin is at $(0,0,0)$ in index space and the cell centers are at $i+0.5$. If the grid is mapped to $[-1, 1]$, the coordinates must be transformed accordingly.*

For boundary voxels, the integration is performed over the clipped polyhedron. The specific integration routine uses the same planar graph representation as the clipping algorithm [S1, unofficial].

    # Order 0: Volume
    grid.moments[0][voxel.vindex] += V
    
    # Order 1: Linear
    if grid.polyorder >= 1:

        cy = dy * (j + 0.5)
        cz = dz * (k + 0.5)
        grid.moments[1][voxel.vindex] += V * cx
        grid.moments[2][voxel.vindex] += V * cy
        grid.moments[3][voxel.vindex] += V * cz

        cy = dy * (j + 0.5)

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Cancellation Errors:** Shifting each voxel to a local coordinate frame prior to integration can cause cancellation errors, reducing accuracy to a few orders of magnitude below machine precision, even if the reduction process itself is accurate [S1, unofficial].
*   **Geometric Degeneracies:** The method is immune to degeneracies where the tetrahedron is exactly aligned with the grid (e.g., vertices on grid planes), as demonstrated by high accuracy in such tests [S1, unofficial].
*   **Search Method Scaling:** Brute-force search is faster for coarse grids ($< 128^3$) due to BSP overhead. BSP is required for large grids to maintain performance [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Exact General Remeshing Scheme for Conservative Voxelization](3774-exact-general-remeshing-scheme-for-conservative-voxelization.md)
A voxelization method that classifies grid voxels into three types based on their intersection with a tetrahedron, clips boundary voxels using a graph-based traversal, and integrates polynomial density fields over the resulting clipped polyhedra via simplicial decomposition. Choose this when exact conservation of higher-order moments is required for convex polyhedra in cosmological simulations.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Clipping and reduction operations scale roughly quadratically with the grid dimension $g$ ($O(g^2)$), corresponding to the surface area of the tetrahedra in units of squared grid spacing [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Brute-Force Search:** Scales as $O(g^3)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   **BSP Search:** Scales as $O(g^2 \log g)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of voxels $O(n_x n_y n_z)$ for the output buffers. The orientation buffer requires $O((n_x+1)(n_y+1)(n_z+1))$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On a $128^3$ grid with $10^5$ random tetrahedra, the method produces approximately $1.3 \times 10^4$ interior voxels and $1.5 \times 10^4$ boundary voxels per tetrahedron [S1, unofficial]. The clipping operation is 3-4 times faster than López and Hernández (2008), with an overall speed-up of 2x [S1, unofficial].
*   **Accuracy:** The method is physically conservative. Maximum fractional error for volume, linear, and quadratic moments on a $128^3$ grid is approximately $10^{-12}$ in double precision and $10^{-4}$ in single precision [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Exact conservation, geometric robustness against degeneracies, no need for high-precision arithmetic or auxiliary checks.
*   **Limitations:** Complexity of implementation due to graph traversal and integration. Single-precision accuracy is limited by cancellation errors.

## Background
The method was introduced in a paper by an unnamed author (cited as [S1, unofficial]) focusing on conservative voxelization for cosmological N-body systems. It builds on the ideas of Sugihara (1994) for robust polyhedron clipping using planar graph representations. The problem of computing exact intersection volumes and integrals has been studied by Dukowicz and Kodis (1987), Dukowicz et al. (1991), and Grandy (1999).
