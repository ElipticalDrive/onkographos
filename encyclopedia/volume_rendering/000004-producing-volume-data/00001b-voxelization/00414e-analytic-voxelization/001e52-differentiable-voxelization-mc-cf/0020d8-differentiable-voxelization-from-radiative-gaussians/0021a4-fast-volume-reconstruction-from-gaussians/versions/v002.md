# Fast Volume Reconstruction from Gaussians

Also called: Discretized Gaussian Volume Rendering, Fast Volume Reconstruction (FVR), Gaussian Splatting for CT

## Overview
Fast Volume Reconstruction (FVR) is a highly parallelized algorithm that aggregates the contributions of a set of 3D Gaussians into a discrete voxel grid to reconstruct a 3D volume [S2]. It solves the prohibitive memory and time complexity of direct Gaussian-to-voxel mapping by decomposing the Mahalanobis distance calculation into separable 1D operations [S2]. Unlike naive rounding or direct computation, FVR enables efficient, differentiable volume rendering suitable for joint optimization of Gaussian parameters in tomographic reconstruction [S2]. It builds on [Differentiable Voxelization from Radiative Gaussians](2380-differentiable-voxelization-from-radiative-gaussians.md) by providing the specific high-performance kernel used to query the density volume $V$ [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$: Mean positions of $n$ Gaussians. For 3D space, $d=3$ [S2].
- $C \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d \times d}$: Covariance matrices of the Gaussians. The decomposition assumes diagonal covariance, using $C^{-1}_{n,d,d}$ as the inverse variance along dimension $d$ for Gaussian $n$ [S2].
- $I \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$: Intensity (or color/density) of each Gaussian [S2].
- $V_{shape} = (w, h, c)$: Target volume dimensions [S2].

**Outputs:**
- $V \in \mathbb{R}^{w \times h \times c}$: The reconstructed volume tensor, initialized to zeros and accumulated with Gaussian contributions [S2].

**Data Structures:**
- `B'`: A 4D tensor of shape $(w_0, h_0, c_0, d)$ representing the local region shift grid. It is generated via a meshgrid of coordinates ranging from $-\frac{w_0-1}{2}$ to $\frac{w_0-1}{2}$ (and similarly for $h_0, c_0$) [S2].
- `valid_indices`: Boolean mask identifying voxel positions within the volume bounds $[0, w), [0, h), [0, c)$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
- $n$: Number of Gaussians [S2].
- $w_0, h_0, c_0$: Dimensions of the local region used for decomposition. The source notes specify a default `size` of 17, implying $w_0=h_0=c_0=17$ [S2].

**Steps:**
1. **Initialize Volume:** Create $V$ as a zero tensor of shape $(w, h, c)$ [S2].
2. **Initialize Local Shift Grid:** Compute `B'` using a meshgrid for dimensions $w_0, h_0, c_0$ with values from $-\frac{dim-1}{2}$ to $\frac{dim-1}{2}$ [S2].
3. **Compute Fractional Offset:** Calculate $\Delta\mu = \mu - \lfloor\mu\rfloor$ to align the continuous Gaussian means to the discrete voxel grid [S2].
4. **Compute Decomposed Terms:** To avoid the $O(n \cdot w \cdot h \cdot c \cdot d^2)$ complexity of direct Mahalanobis distance, compute the following four terms using summation over dimension $d$:
   - Term 1: $T_1 = B'^T C^{-1} B'$. Computed as $\sum_d B'_{w_0,h_0,c_0,d} \cdot C^{-1}_{n,d,d} \cdot B'_{w_0,h_0,c_0,d}$ [S2].
   - Term 2: $T_2 = B'^T C^{-1} \Delta\mu$. Computed as $\sum_d B'_{w_0,h_0,c_0,d} \cdot C^{-1}_{n,d,d} \cdot \Delta\mu_{n,1,1,d}$ [S2].
   - Term 3: $T_3 = \Delta\mu^T C^{-1} B'$. Computed as $\sum_d \Delta\mu_{n,1,1,d} \cdot C^{-1}_{n,d,d} \cdot B'_{w_0,h_0,c_0,d}$ [S2].
   - Term 4: $T_4 = \Delta\mu^T C^{-1} \Delta\mu$. Computed as $\sum_d \Delta\mu_{n,1,1,d} \cdot C^{-1}_{n,d,d} \cdot \Delta\mu_{n,1,1,d}$ [S2].
5. **Compute Gaussian Contributions:** Calculate the contribution $\Gamma$ for each Gaussian and local shift:
   $$ \Gamma = \exp\left(-(T_1 + T_2 + T_3 + T_4)\right) \cdot I $$
   The source formula omits the standard $1/2$ factor in the exponent, so the implementation follows the explicit expression provided [S2].
6. **Determine Voxel Positions:** Compute the impacted voxel positions $P$:
   $$ P = \lfloor\mu\rfloor + B' $$
   This maps the local grid shifts to absolute voxel coordinates in the volume [S2].
7. **Filter Valid Indices:** Identify `valid` indices where $P$ is within bounds:
   $$ P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,0} \ge 0 \land P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,0} < w $$
   $$ P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,1} \ge 0 \land P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,1} < h $$
   $$ P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,2} \ge 0 \land P_{n,w_0,h_0,c_0,2} < c $$
   [S2].
8. **Accumulate:** Perform a parallel `scatter_add` operation:
   $$ V[P[\text{valid}]] \ += \Gamma[\text{valid}] $$
   [S2].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import torch

def fast_volume_reconstruction(mu, C_inv_diag, intensity, volume_shape, local_size=17):
    """
    Reconstructs a 3D volume from Gaussians using Fast Volume Reconstruction.
    
    Args:
        mu: Tensor of shape (n, 3), Gaussian means.
        C_inv_diag: Tensor of shape (n, 3), inverse diagonal covariance elements.
        intensity: Tensor of shape (n, 1), Gaussian intensities.
        volume_shape: Tuple (w, h, c), target volume dimensions.
        local_size: int, size of the local region w0=h0=c0.
        
    Returns:
        V: Tensor of shape (w, h, c), reconstructed volume.
    """
    n, d = mu.shape
    w, h, c = volume_shape
    
    # 1. Initialize Volume
    V = torch.zeros((w, h, c), device=mu.device, dtype=mu.dtype)
    
    # 2. Initialize Local Shift Grid B'
    half_size = (local_size - 1) / 2.0
    coords = torch.arange(-half_size, half_size + 1, device=mu.device, dtype=mu.dtype)
    # B' shape: (local_size, local_size, local_size, 3)
    B_prime = torch.stack(torch.meshgrid(coords, coords, coords, indexing='ij'), dim=-1)
    
    # 3. Compute Fractional Offset Delta_mu
    mu_floor = torch.floor(mu)
    delta_mu = mu - mu_floor  # Shape: (n, 3)
    
    # 4. Compute Decomposed Terms
    # B_prime: (w0, h0, c0, d)
    # C_inv_diag: (n, d)
    # delta_mu: (n, d)
    
    # Term 1: B'^T C^-1 B' -> sum_d B'[i,j,k,d] * C_inv[n,d] * B'[i,j,k,d]
    term1 = torch.einsum('ijkd,nd->nijk', B_prime, C_inv_diag)
    
    # Term 2: B'^T C^-1 Delta_mu -> sum_d B'[i,j,k,d] * C_inv[n,d] * Delta_mu[n,d]
    term2 = torch.einsum('ijkd,nd->nijk', B_prime, C_inv_diag * delta_mu)
    
    # Term 3: Delta_mu^T C^-1 B' -> sum_d Delta_mu[n,d] * C_inv[n,d] * B'[i,j,k,d]
    term3 = term2.clone()
    
    # Term 4: Delta_mu^T C^-1 Delta_mu -> sum_d Delta_mu[n,d] * C_inv[n,d] * Delta_mu[n,d]
    term4 = torch.sum(delta_mu * C_inv_diag * delta_mu, dim=1, keepdim=True)  # (n, 1)
    term4_exp = term4.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1)  # (n, 1, 1, 1)
    
    # 5. Compute Contributions Gamma
    # Gamma = exp(-(T1 + T2 + T3 + T4)) * I
    exponent = -(term1 + term2 + term3 + term4_exp)
    gamma = torch.exp(exponent) * intensity.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1)  # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # 6. Determine Voxel Positions P
    # P = floor(mu) + B'
    mu_floor_exp = mu_floor.unsqueeze(1).unsqueeze(1)  # (n, 1, 1, 3)
    P = mu_floor_exp + B_prime.unsqueeze(0)  # (n, w0, h0, c0, 3)
    
    # 7. Filter Valid Indices
    valid_x = (P[..., 0] >= 0) & (P[..., 0] < w)
    valid_y = (P[..., 1] >= 0) & (P[..., 1] < h)
    valid_z = (P[..., 2] >= 0) & (P[..., 2] < c)
    valid_mask = valid_x & valid_y & valid_z  # (n, w0, h0, c0)
    
    # 8. Accumulate
    P_flat = P.view(-1, 3)  # (n*w0*h0*c0, 3)
    Gamma_flat = gamma.view(-1)  # (n*w0*h0*c0,)
    Valid_flat = valid_mask.view(-1)  # (n*w0*h0*c0,)
    
    P_valid = P_flat[Valid_flat]
    Gamma_valid = Gamma_flat[Valid_flat]
    
    # Convert (x,y,z) to linear indices
    linear_indices = P_valid[:, 0] + P_valid[:, 1] * w + P_valid[:, 2] * w * h
    
    V_flat = V.view(-1)
    V_flat.scatter_add_(0, linear_indices.long(), Gamma_valid)
    
    return V
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Out of Bounds:** Gaussians near the volume boundary may have contributions falling outside $[0, w), [0, h), [0, c)$ [S2]. These must be filtered out using the `valid` mask to prevent memory access errors [S2].
- **Diagonal Covariance Assumption:** The decomposition relies on $C$ being diagonal (or pre-diagonalized) to compute $C^{-1}_{n,d,d}$ efficiently [S2]. If $C$ is full, the decomposition $B'^T C^{-1} B'$ cannot be simplified to the sum over $d$ without full matrix multiplication, negating the speedup [S2].
- **Precision:** The exponential function can underflow for large Mahalanobis distances [G]. Clamping or using log-space arithmetic may be necessary for stability, though not explicitly detailed in the source [S2].
- **Memory:** Even with decomposition, storing intermediate tensors $T_1, T_2, T_3$ of shape $(n, w_0, h_0, c_0)$ requires significant VRAM if $n$ is large [S2]. The source notes 16.87 GiB for $n=150,000$ and local size 17 [S2].

## Subtopics

### [Discretized Gaussian Representation (DGR)](3244-discretized-gaussian-representation-dgr.md)
A framework that uses FVR as its core voxelization engine, adding a "Discretize and Align" mechanism to preserve differentiability and gradient flow during optimization, unlike naive rounding.

### [Differentiable Voxelization from Radiative Gaussians](2380-differentiable-voxelization-from-radiative-gaussians.md)
The parent method that defines the general differentiable voxelizer $V$ for querying density volumes, which FVR optimizes for speed.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:**
  - Direct Reconstruction: $O(n \cdot w \cdot h \cdot c \cdot d^2)$ [S2].
  - FVR without Decomposition: $O(n \cdot w_0 \cdot h_0 \cdot c_0 \cdot d^2)$ [S2].
  - FVR with Decomposition: $O(w_0 \cdot h_0 \cdot c_0 \cdot d)$ per iteration, as the decomposition reduces the dependency on $n$ in the inner loop [S2].
- **Memory Complexity:**
  - Direct Reconstruction: Estimated 16662.50 GiB VRAM [S2].
  - FVR (with/without decomposition): 16.87 GiB VRAM [S2].
- **Measured Performance:**
  - Reconstructs a $256 \times 256 \times 256$ volume from over 150,000 Gaussians in 0.09 seconds per iteration [S2].
  - Comparison with R2-Gaussian Voxelizer: FVR achieves 7m13s total optimization time vs 8m37s for R2-Gaussian on 50-view Sparse-View CT, with higher PSNR (38.74 vs 38.02) and SSIM (0.960 vs 0.956) [S2].
- **Strengths:** Highly parallelized, significantly reduces memory consumption compared to direct methods, enables joint optimization of all Gaussians without selective view optimization [S2].
- **Limitations:** Requires diagonal covariance matrices for the decomposition speedup. Performance depends on the local region size $w_0, h_0, c_0$; larger sizes increase memory usage [S2].

## Background
Fast Volume Reconstruction was introduced in the context of Discretized Gaussian Representation (DGR) for tomographic reconstruction [S2]. The method was developed to enable efficient, differentiable volume rendering for 3D tomography, allowing for joint optimization of Gaussian parameters [S2]. It is used in public CT datasets such as FIPS, AAPM-Mayo LDCT, and FUMPE [S2].
