# Bounding Volume Optimization

Also called: Adaptive Voxel-Array-Größe, BV Optimisation, Bounding Volume Optimisation, Bounding-Volume-Optimierung, SDF Bounding Volume Culling, Volumen-Größenanpassung

## Overview
Bounding Volume Optimization reduces the computational cost of volume rendering by restricting Signed Distance Field (SDF) evaluations to regions where geometry actually exists, thereby eliminating false positives and redundant calculations [S1, unofficial][S2]. It achieves this by either resizing the voxel array to the minimal bounding box of the data or by using bounding volumes to cull expensive primitive evaluations during raymarching [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike sibling methods that focus on data compression or grid generation, this method specifically targets the traversal and evaluation phase by adapting the active computation domain to the scene's spatial extent [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A discrete voxel grid produced by [Voxelization](12-voxelization.md), a set of geometric primitives (e.g., SDF primitives), and the camera/view parameters.
*   **Output:** An optimized rendering pipeline where SDF evaluations are skipped for non-contributing regions, resulting in reduced frame duration.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Voxel Array:** A 3D texture or buffer. Its dimensions $(W, H, D)$ are adjusted to match the minimal bounding box of the active voxel data, rather than a fixed power-of-two size [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Bounding Volumes:** Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) associated with each geometric primitive or cluster of primitives. These define the spatial extent of expensive SDF evaluations [S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Determine Active Extent:** Analyze the voxel data to find the minimum and maximum coordinates $(x_{min}, y_{min}, z_{min})$ and $(x_{max}, y_{max}, z_{max})$ occupied by non-empty voxels [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Resize Voxel Array:** Allocate or map the voxel texture with dimensions:
    $$ W = x_{max} - x_{min} + 1 $$
    $$ H = y_{max} - y_{min} + 1 $$
    $$ D = z_{max} - z_{min} + 1 $$
    This eliminates unused memory and prevents traversal of empty space [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Bounding Volumes:** For each primitive $i$ in the scene, compute its AABB $B_i$ in world or local space [S2].
4.  **Ray-Primitive Intersection Test:** During raymarching, for each ray step, check if the current ray segment intersects any primitive's bounding volume $B_i$ [S2].
5.  **Conditional SDF Evaluation:**
    *   If the ray does **not** intersect $B_i$, skip the evaluation of the expensive SDF function $S_i(p)$ for that primitive [S2].
    *   If the ray **does** intersect $B_i$, evaluate $S_i(p)$ and update the minimum distance [S2].
6.  **Handle Coordinate Offsets:** If the voxel array is resized, adjust ray origin and direction or texture coordinates to account for the new origin $(x_{min}, y_{min}, z_{min})$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Step Size:** Determines the granularity of the ray march. Smaller steps increase accuracy but require more bounding volume checks [S2].
*   **AABB Tightness:** Tighter bounding volumes reduce false positives in the culling step but may require more complex hierarchy construction [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision errors in AABB intersection tests can lead to incorrect culling or double evaluation [S2].
*   **Degenerate Input:** Empty scenes or scenes with no geometry should be handled by early exit to avoid unnecessary bounding volume checks [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** When resizing the voxel array, ensure that rays near the boundary do not sample out-of-bounds memory [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduces the number of SDF evaluations from $O(N \cdot M)$ to $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of ray steps, $M$ is the total number of primitives, and $K$ is the number of primitives intersecting the ray's bounding volume ($K \ll M$) [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Reduces memory usage by allocating only the necessary voxel array size. For example, a scene fitting in $512 \times 321 \times 512$ voxels uses significantly less memory than a fixed $512^3$ array [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** In a scene with 20 expensive primitives at 720p, enabling BV optimization reduced average frame duration from 41 ms (24 fps) to 21 ms (48 fps) [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Localizes expensive computations to areas with complex geometry, improving cache locality and reducing overall frame time [S2]. Allows separation of game world and entities, enabling voxel models to serve as bounding boxes for rigid bodies [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires additional computation for AABB intersection tests. If the scene is uniformly dense, the overhead of bounding volume checks may outweigh the benefits [S2].
*   **When to Use:** Use when the scene contains sparse geometry or a mix of expensive and cheap primitives. Use when memory bandwidth is a bottleneck due to large, mostly empty voxel arrays [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Avoid for uniformly dense scenes where every voxel contributes to the final image, as the culling overhead is wasted [S2].

## Background
Bounding Volume Optimization originated as a technique to improve the performance of raymarched distance fields by reducing the cost of the map function [S2]. It was explored in the context of CUDA-based raymarching engines to handle scenes with dozens or hundreds of surfaces [S2]. The concept of resizing voxel arrays to fit the data extent was also discussed in the context of voxel-based rendering to eliminate false positives during traversal [S1, unofficial].
