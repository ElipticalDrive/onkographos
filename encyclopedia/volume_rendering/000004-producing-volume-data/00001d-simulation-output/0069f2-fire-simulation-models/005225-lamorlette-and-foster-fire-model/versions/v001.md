# Lamorlette and Foster Fire Model
Also called: Parametric Curve-Based Fire, Shrek Fire Model

## Overview
The Lamorlette and Foster Fire Model is a mixed framework for simulating the macro-movement of fire, originally developed for the production of the motion picture *Shrek* [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of achieving a "holistic" visual appearance for fire, distinguishing it from the atomistic look of standard particle systems or the smoke-suited appearance of texture splats [S1, unofficial]. The method sets itself apart by modeling individual flame elements as parametric space curves that evolve over time, rather than relying solely on discrete point particles or static volumetric fields [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of initial points defining the spine of flame elements.
- Wind field data (velocity and temperature fields) [S1, unofficial].
- Procedural control parameters for placement, intensity, lifespan, shape, color, and size [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A set of rendered particles representing the visible flame (oxidization region) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Parametric Curve:** Defined by a sequence of control points interpolating the flame spine.
- **Particle:** A point sampled near the curve, carrying position, color, and opacity attributes.
- **Implicit Surface:** A cylindrical profile defining the oxidization region boundary [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Curve Initialization:** Model individual flame elements as parametric space curves. Each curve interpolates a set of points defining the spine of the flame [S1, unofficial].
2. **Curve Evolution:** Evolve the curves over time using a combination of physics-based, procedural, and hand-defined wind fields. Physical properties are derived from statistical measurements of natural diffusion flames. The curves are frequently re-sampled to ensure continuity and to model flames generated from moving sources [S1, unofficial].
   - *Wind Dynamics:* The motion is governed by the velocity of the gas rushing into surrounding air and temperature. Drag (shearing forces) causes rotation and mixing, leading to turbulent swirling. Turbulence is exaggerated if gas flows around solid objects, transitioning from smooth flow to chaotic mixing behind the object [S1, unofficial].
3. **Curve Breaking:** Allow curves to break, generating independently evolving flames with a limited lifespan. Engineering observations and stochastic assumptions provide heuristics for this process [S1, unofficial].
4. **Implicit Surface & Sampling:** Build an implicit surface representing the oxidization region (the visible part of the flame) using a cylindrical profile. Sample particles close to this region using a volumetric falloff function [S1, unofficial].
5. **Buoyancy Noise:** Apply procedural noise to the particles in the parameter space of the profile. Animate this noise to follow thermal buoyancy [S1, unofficial].
6. **Turbulence Noise:** Transform particles into the parametric space of the flame’s structural curve. Apply a second level of noise with a Kolmogorov frequency spectrum (derived from Navier-Stokes equations for turbulence) to provide turbulent detail [S1, unofficial].
7. **Rendering:** Render particles using either a volumetric method or a fast painterly method.
   - *Painterly Method:* Simulates a painting-like quality with visible brush strokes using height and opacity maps.
   - *Color Blending:* Adjust particle color based on neighbor properties to allow flame elements to visually merge in a realistic way [S1, unofficial].
8. **Procedural Control:** Apply procedural controls to govern placement, intensity, lifespan, and evolution in shape, color, size, and behavior of the flames [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def lamorlette_foster_fire_model(initial_spine_points, wind_fields, controls):
    # Stage 1: Initialize parametric curves
    curves = []
    for spine_pts in initial_spine_points:
        curve = ParametricCurve.interpolate(spine_pts)
        curves.append(curve)

    # Stage 2-3: Evolution and Breaking Loop
    for time_step in simulation_time:
        new_curves = []
        for curve in curves:
            # Evolve curve using wind fields (velocity, temperature, drag)
            curve.evolve(wind_fields)
            
            # Re-sample for continuity and moving source support
            curve.resample()
            
            # Stochastic breaking heuristic
            if should_break(curve, controls):
                # Generate independent sub-flames with limited lifespan
                sub_flames = curve.break_apart(controls.lifespan)
                new_curves.extend(sub_flames)
            else:
                new_curves.append(curve)
        curves = new_curves

    # Stage 4: Sample particles from oxidization region
    particles = []
    for curve in curves:
        # Define implicit cylindrical surface for oxidization region
        implicit_surface = curve.build_cylindrical_profile()
        
        # Sample particles near the surface with volumetric falloff
        sampled_points = implicit_surface.sample_volumetric_falloff()
        for pt in sampled_points:
            particle = Particle(position=pt)
            particles.append(particle)

    # Stage 5: Apply buoyancy noise in profile parameter space
    for particle in particles:
        noise = procedural_noise(particle.profile_params, time_step)
        noise.animate_thermal_buoyancy()
        particle.position += noise

    # Stage 6: Apply turbulence noise in curve parametric space
    for particle in particles:
        # Transform to structural curve parametric space
        curve_params = particle.transform_to_curve_space()
        
        # Kolmogorov frequency spectrum noise
        turbulence_noise = kolmogorov_noise(curve_params, time_step)
        particle.position += turbulence_noise

    # Stage 7: Render
    # Option A: Volumetric rendering
    # Option B: Painterly rendering
    for particle in particles:
        # Adjust color based on neighbors for visual merging
        neighbor_colors = get_neighbor_colors(particle, particles)
        particle.color = blend_color(particle.color, neighbor_colors)
        
        if use_painterly_method:
            render_painterly(particle, height_map, opacity_map)
        else:
            render_volumetric(particle)

    return rendered_image
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** The re-sampling of curves requires sufficient precision to maintain continuity, especially when modeling flames from moving sources [S1, unofficial].
- **Degenerate Input:** If wind fields are static or zero, the characteristic turbulent swirling may not manifest correctly, as it relies on the interaction between gas velocity and surrounding air [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundary:** The implicit surface definition must correctly bound the oxidization region to avoid sampling particles in non-visible areas [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The model involves multiple stages of curve evolution, particle sampling, and noise application, which can be computationally intensive depending on the number of curves and particles.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Storage is required for curve definitions, particle states, and wind field data.
- **Measured Performance:** The fast painterly method is described as a way to render the image quickly, but specific performance metrics (e.g., frames per second, resolution) are not provided in the sources [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Provides a holistic look for fire, superior to atomistic particle systems and better suited for fire than texture splats [S1, unofficial]. Offers artistic control through procedural parameters [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Relies on heuristics and stochastic assumptions for curve breaking, which may require tuning for different fire types [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When a holistic, production-quality fire appearance is needed, particularly in contexts where artistic control is prioritized over strict physical simulation [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When atomistic particle effects are desired, or when the scene primarily involves smoke or steam where texture splats are more appropriate [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Lamorlette and Foster Fire Model was developed for the motion picture *Shrek* by DreamWorks [S1, unofficial]. It is described as a "very solid mixed framework for macro-movement of fire in a production system" [S1, unofficial]. The model draws on statistical measurements of natural diffusion flames and applies Navier-Stokes equations to turbulence (Kolmogorov frequency spectrum) [S1, unofficial]. It is cited as a key reference for realistic fire rendering in production environments [S1, unofficial].
