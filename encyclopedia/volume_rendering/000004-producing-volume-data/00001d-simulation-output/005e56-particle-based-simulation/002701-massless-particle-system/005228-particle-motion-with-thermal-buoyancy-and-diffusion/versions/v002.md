# Particle Motion with Thermal Buoyancy and Diffusion

Also called: Thermal Buoyancy and Diffusion Model

## Overview
This method defines the kinematic trajectory of massless particles in volumetric fire simulation by combining wind advection, thermal buoyancy, source movement, and diffusion [S1, unofficial][S2]. It solves the problem of realistic macro-scale flame motion by treating particles as tracer elements subject to specific physical forces, building on the [Massless Particle System](3970-massless-particle-system.md) framework [S2]. It is distinguished from pure fluid solvers by its reliance on simplified Navier-Stokes approximations for wind and explicit algebraic formulas for buoyancy, prioritizing real-time performance over strict physical fidelity [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Particle position $x_p$, particle temperature $T_p$, particle age $t_p$, ambient temperature $T_o$, wind field $w(x_p, t)$, source velocity $V_p$, thermal coefficient $\beta$ (or $b$), gravitational acceleration $g$, and kinematic viscosity $\nu$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Outputs:** Updated particle position $x_p(t + \Delta t)$ [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Data Structures:**
Not given in the sources. [G] Standard implementations store particle state in a contiguous array or structure of arrays (SoA) containing position (vec3), temperature (float), and age (float).

**Formulas:**
The primary equation of motion for a particle is [S1, unofficial][S2]:

$$ \frac{dx_p}{dt} = w(x_p, t) + d(T_p) + V_p + c(T_p, t) $$

Source [S1, unofficial] presents the same equation with subtraction signs: $\frac{dx_p}{dt} = w(x_p, t) - d(T_p) - V_p - c(T_p, t)$ [S1, unofficial]. Implementers must verify the coordinate system orientation and sign convention [S1, unofficial][S2].

Where:
*   $w(x_p, t)$ is the arbitrary controlling wind field [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $d(T_p)$ is the motion due to diffusion, modeled as temperature-scaled Brownian motion [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $V_p$ is the motion due to the movement of the fire source [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $c(T_p, t)$ is the motion due to thermal buoyancy [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Thermal Buoyancy:**
Thermal buoyancy is constant over the lifetime of the particle [S1, unofficial][S2]:

$$ c(T_p, t) = -\beta g (T_o - T_p) t_p^2 $$

Source [S1, unofficial] uses the coefficient $b$ and includes a scalar factor $y$: $c(T_p, t) = -b g y (T_o - T_p) t_p^2$ [S1, unofficial]. The term $(T_o - T_p)$ indicates that if $T_p > T_o$ (hot particle), the force direction depends on the sign convention and coordinate system [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Wind Field:**
The wind field $w$ is computed using a reduced form of the Navier-Stokes equations [S2]:

$$ w(u) = \nu \nabla \cdot (\nabla u) - (u \cdot \nabla)u - \nabla p $$

Where:
*   $u$ is a 4D vector $(x_p, t)$ representing spatial dimensions and time [S2].
*   $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity [S2].
*   $p$ is pressure [S2].
*   The term $(u \cdot \nabla)u$ represents convection [S2].
*   The term $\nu \nabla \cdot (\nabla u)$ represents drag/diffusion [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $\beta$ (or $b$): Thermal coefficient scaling buoyancy force [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $g$: Gravitational acceleration constant [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $T_o$: Ambient temperature of the surrounding air [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $t_p$: Age of the particle, calculated as current time minus creation time [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $\nu$: Kinematic viscosity, controlling the rotation and smoothness of the wind field [S2].

**Pitfalls:**
*   The model assumes molecular diffusion is negligible relative to other effects [S2].
*   The gas is assumed to be incompressible [S2].
*   Applying full Navier-Stokes equations would be computationally prohibitive for real-time rendering, hence the reduced form [S2].
*   Precision issues may arise with $t_p^2$ for very old particles if not clamped or handled [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import random

def brownian_motion_3d(scale):
    """Returns a 3D vector with components drawn from a normal distribution N(0, scale)."""
    return (random.gauss(0, scale), random.gauss(0, scale), random.gauss(0, scale))

def update_particle(particle, dt, wind_field_func, source_velocity, ambient_temp, gravity_vec, thermal_coeff):
    # 1. Thermal Buoyancy: c(Tp, t) = -beta * g * (To - Tp) * tp^2
    # S1 uses coefficient b and includes a scalar y factor; S2 uses beta and omits y [S1, unofficial][S2].
    # gravity_vec provides direction (e.g., (0, -9.8, 0)).
    buoyancy = -thermal_coeff * gravity_vec * (ambient_temp - particle.temp) * (particle.age ** 2)

    # 2. Wind Field: w(u) = nu * Laplacian(u) - (u . grad)u - grad(p)
    # u is a 4D vector (x_p, t) [S2].
    # Note: The exact spatial solver for w(u) is not specified in the sources.
    wind = wind_field_func(particle.pos, particle.age)

    # 3. Diffusion: d(Tp) is temperature-scaled Brownian motion [S1, unofficial][S2].
    diffusion = brownian_motion_3d(particle.temp)

    # 4. Source Movement: Vp [S1, unofficial][S2].
    source_motion = source_velocity

    # 5. Total Velocity: dx_p/dt = w + d + Vp + c (S2 convention) or w - d - Vp - c (S1 convention) [S1, unofficial][S2].
    # Implementers should verify coordinate system orientation and sign convention [S1, unofficial][S2].
    total_velocity = wind + diffusion + source_motion + buoyancy

    # 6. Update Position
    particle.pos = tuple(p + v * dt for p, v in zip(particle.pos, total_velocity))

    # 7. Update Age
    particle.age += dt

    return particle
```

## Subtopics

### Physics-Based Macro Flame Animation
This variant focuses on the macro-movement of flame spines using the system of equations from [Kan99] as cited in [S2]. It emphasizes the integration of the thermal buoyancy and diffusion model within a game engine context, leveraging shader software for volumetric rendering [S2]. It distinguishes itself by explicitly linking the particle motion to "flame spines" and bounding volumes composed of hexahedrons, rather than just individual tracer particles [S2]. It is chosen when the visual requirement is large-scale, coherent flame structures rather than fine-grained turbulent details [S2].

### SPH Cloud Parcel Physics
A two-scale model where large particles (parcels) act as physics units and simultaneously contain volumetric texture [S3]. Through decoupling between macro and micro levels, a physics-inspired procedural method is made possible, avoiding expensive physics/value storage for transparent sky regions [S3]. It solves the problem of efficiently simulating large-scale convective cloud dynamics without the computational cost of filling the transparent sky volume with invisible air, which is required by Eulerian CFD methods [S3]. Unlike sibling methods that may require high particle counts for resolution or offline processing, this approach couples a coarse Lagrangian model of air parcels (thermals) with procedural amplification using volumetric noise, ensuring real-time efficiency and scalability to large domains [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G] Standard particle systems scale as $O(N)$ assuming $O(1)$ wind evaluation.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G] Scales as $O(N)$ to store particle states.
*   **Performance:** Designed for real-time rendering. The use of reduced Navier-Stokes equations avoids the computational cost of full fluid simulation [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Computationally efficient; provides physically plausible macro-motion for fire; easy to integrate with existing particle systems. [G]
*   **Limitations:** Lacks fine-scale turbulence details; assumes incompressible flow; accuracy depends on the quality of the wind field approximation. [S2]
*   **When to use:** Real-time volumetric fire rendering in games or interactive applications. [G]
*   **When not to use:** Scientific simulations requiring high-fidelity fluid dynamics or compressible flow effects. [G]

## Background
The method originates from research on realistic real-time rendering of fire in production systems. It was presented by Y. Vanzine and D. Vrajitoru in 2008 [S2] and in an earlier proposal [S1, unofficial]. The model draws inspiration from [FKM* 07] for volumetric rendering integration and uses physics-based controls from [Kan99] for macro-animation [S2]. It is used in game engines and shader-based volumetric rendering pipelines [S2].
