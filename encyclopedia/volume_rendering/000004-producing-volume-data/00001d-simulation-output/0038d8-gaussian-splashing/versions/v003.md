# Gaussian Splashing
    Also called: GSP, Gaussian Splashing (GSP)
    ## Overview
    Gaussian Splashing is a unified framework that combines 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) to enable realistic, interactive simulation and rendering of fluids, deformable bodies, and rigid objects from novel views [S3]. It addresses the limitation of vanilla 3DGS, which simplifies light field composition without well-defined appearance properties, making fluid rendering cumbersome [S3]. Gaussian Splashing distinguishes itself by enriching Gaussian kernels with material parameters (diffusiveness, specularity, roughness, normals) and aligning kernel orientation with surface normals to eliminate spiky noise from rotational deformation [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].
    ## How it works
    Gaussian Splashing treats each Gaussian kernel as a discrete physical particle with volume, allowing direct simulation within the rendering pipeline [S1][S2, unofficial]. The method integrates physically based rendering (PBR) to augment dynamic surface reflections on fluids [S3].
    ### Inputs and Outputs
    *   **Inputs**: Initial 3DGS scene representation (positions, covariances, opacities, colors) [G]; camera poses [G]; scene geometry (meshes for solids) [S3]; physical parameters for PBD (density, viscosity, surface tension) [S3].
    *   **Outputs**: Rendered images from novel viewpoints [S3]; simulated particle states over time [S3].
    ### Data Structures
    The core data structure is the Gaussian kernel, augmented from standard 3DGS with additional fields for physics and material properties [S3]:
    1.  **Position** ($\mathbf{p}$): 3D center of the Gaussian [G].
    2.  **Covariance** ($\Sigma$): Defines the shape and orientation of the Gaussian ellipsoid [G].
    3.  **Opacity** ($\alpha$): Transparency of the kernel [G].
    4.  **Spherical Harmonics** ($\mathbf{c}$): View-dependent color coefficients [G].
    5.  **Normal** ($\mathbf{n}$): Surface normal vector, aligned with the kernel's orientation for solids and approximated for fluids [S3].
    6.  **Material Parameters**: Diffusiveness, specularity, roughness [S1][S2, unofficial].
    7.  **Mass/Volume**: Physical properties for PBD integration [S1][S2, unofficial].
    ### Steps
    1.  **Reconstruction and Disentanglement**: Reconstruct surfaces from input images using 3DGS and disentangle objects that will be actively simulated (solids, fluids) from static background elements [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].
    2.  **Mesh-Based Simulation Framework**: Attach Gaussians to meshes for solid objects to enable texture rendering and physical interaction [S1][S2, unofficial][S3].
    3.  **Normal Alignment and Material Enrichment**:
        *   For **solids**: Augment each Gaussian kernel with a normal vector and align the kernel’s orientation with this surface normal to refine the PBD simulation [S3].
        *   For **fluids**: Enrich Gaussians with material parameters (diffusiveness, specularity, roughness) to handle specular and reflective objects [S1][S2, unofficial].
    4.  **Physics Simulation (PBD)**:
        *   Apply Position-Based Dynamics to update particle positions and velocities [S3].
        *   **Solid Constraint**: Use an anisotropy loss during 3DGS training to cap the stretching ratio, mitigating rendering artifacts induced by rotational deformation [S3].
        *   **Fluid Normal Approximation**:
            *   For fluid kernels near the surface: Approximate the normal based on surface tension [S3].
            *   For scattered fluid droplets: Estimate normal information using a depth volume rendered via the current camera pose [S3].
    5.  **Rendering**: Render the scene using the updated Gaussian kernels and integrate PBR to dynamically synthesize specular shading along with fluid movement [S3].
    6.  **Scene Editing (Optional)**: Augment the system with an image-space segmentation module to select objects of interest from the 3DGS scene, and exploit generative AI to fill missing pixels for physics-based scene editing [S3].
    ### Pseudocode
    ```python
    def gaussian_splashing_init(scene_data):
        """Initialize GSP from input images and scene geometry."""
        gaussians = []
        # 1. Reconstruct 3DGS scene
        gaussians.extend(reconstruct_3dgs(scene_data.images))
        # 2. Disentangle active objects
        solid_meshes = extract_meshes(scene_data.geometry)
        fluid_particles = extract_fluid_particles(scene_data.geometry)
        # 3. Attach Gaussians to meshes for solids
        for mesh in solid_meshes:
            mesh_gaussians = [g for g in gaussians if g.on_mesh(mesh)]
            for g in mesh_gaussians:
                g.normal = compute_surface_normal(mesh, g.position)
                g.covariance = align_covariance_to_normal(g.covariance, g.normal)
        # 4. Initialize fluid Gaussians with material parameters
        for particle in fluid_particles:
            g = create_gaussian(particle.position)
            g.material = {'diffusiveness': 0.5, 'specularity': 0.8, 'roughness': 0.2}
            if is_near_surface(particle.position, fluid_particles):
                g.normal = compute_surface_tension_normal(particle.position, fluid_particles)
            else:
                g.normal = estimate_normal_from_depth(particle.position, scene_data.camera_pose)
            gaussians.append(g)
        return gaussians

    def gaussian_splashing_step(gaussians, dt, camera_pose):
        """Perform one step of PBD simulation and render."""
        # 1. PBD Simulation
        update_pbd(gaussians, dt)
        # 2. Update Normals
        fluid_list = [g for g in gaussians if g.is_fluid]
        for g in gaussians:
            if g.is_fluid:
                if is_near_surface(g.position, fluid_list):
                    g.normal = compute_surface_tension_normal(g.position, fluid_list)
                else:
                    g.normal = estimate_normal_from_depth(g.position, camera_pose)
        # 3. Render
        return render_gaussians_pbr(gaussians, camera_pose)

    def estimate_normal_from_depth(position, camera_pose):
        """Estimate normal for scattered droplets using depth volume."""
        depth_map = render_depth(camera_pose)
        return compute_normal_from_depth_gradient(depth_map, position)

    # Helper definitions
    def reconstruct_3dgs(images): return []  # [G] Standard 3DGS optimization routine
    def extract_meshes(geometry): return []  # [G] Mesh extraction routine
    def extract_fluid_particles(geometry): return []  # [G] Fluid region extraction routine
    def update_pbd(gaussians, dt):
        for g in gaussians:
            g.velocity += dt * compute_forces(g)
            g.position += dt * g.velocity
    def compute_surface_normal(mesh, pos): return mesh.normal_at(pos)
    def align_covariance_to_normal(cov, n): return rotate_matrix(cov, n)
    def create_gaussian(pos): return {'position': pos, 'covariance': identity_matrix(), 'opacity': 1.0, 'normal': [0,0,1], 'is_solid': False, 'is_fluid': True}
    def is_near_surface(pos, particles): return any(distance(pos, p) < SURFACE_THRESHOLD for p in particles)
    def compute_surface_tension_normal(pos, particles): return normalize(sum(p.position - pos for p in particles))
    def render_gaussians_pbr(gaussians, cam): return splat_and_shade(gaussians, cam)
    def render_depth(cam): return depth_buffer_from_gaussians(cam)
    def compute_normal_from_depth_gradient(depth_map, pos): return gradient_to_normal(depth_map, pos)
    ```

    ### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
    *   **Rotational Deformation in Solids**: Vanilla 3DGS suffers from spiky noises due to rotational deformation [S3].
    *   **Fluid Normal Estimation**: For scattered fluid droplets, normal information is not directly available from surface tension [S3].
    *   **Vanilla 3DGS Limitations**: Vanilla 3DGS simplifies light field composition without well-defined appearance properties, making fluid rendering cumbersome [S3].
    ## Operating characteristics
    *   **Time Complexity**: Not given in the sources [S3].
    *   **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources [S3].
    *   **Performance**: Not given in the sources [S3].
    *   **Strengths**: Unified framework for solids, fluids, and deformable bodies; realistic surface reflections on dynamic fluids; eliminates spiky noise from rotational deformation; enables novel view synthesis and physics-based scene editing [S3].
    *   **Limitations**: Relies on PBD, which may not capture complex constitutive models as accurately as SPH or FEM; normal estimation for scattered droplets depends on camera pose and depth rendering quality [S3].
    *   **When to Use**: When realistic interaction between solids and fluids is required in a dynamic scene with novel view synthesis [S3].
    *   **When Not to Use**: When high-fidelity fluid physics (e.g., complex viscosity, turbulence) is more important than visual appearance and novel view synthesis [S3].
    ## Background
    Gaussian Splashing was introduced by Tianjia Shao, Hongzhi Wu, Kun Zhou, Chenfanfu Jiang, and Yin Yang from the University of Utah, Zhejiang University, and UCLA [S3]. The paper was published in CVPR 2025 [S3]. Gaussian Splashing builds upon GaussianShader, which enriches Gaussians with material parameters [S1][S2, unofficial]. It leverages the coherence of 3DGS and PBD to manage rendering, view synthesis, and dynamics in a cohesive manner [S3]. The method addresses the limitations of vanilla 3DGS in handling translucent and specular fluids [S3].
