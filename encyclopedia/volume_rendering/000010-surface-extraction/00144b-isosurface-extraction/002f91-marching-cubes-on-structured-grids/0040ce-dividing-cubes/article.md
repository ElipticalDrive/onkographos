# Dividing Cubes


## Overview
Dividing Cubes is a surface extraction method that replaces the triangular mesh output of Marching Cubes with point-based representations or pixels, primarily used when data points are sufficiently dense that triangles become smaller than a pixel [S1, unofficial]. It eliminates the traditional scan conversion step by subdividing surface voxels into smaller cubes and projecting intensity directly to the viewing plane [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A 3D scalar volume grid; an isovalue threshold.
**Outputs:** A set of points or projected intensity values on the 2D viewing plane.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** Standard 3D array of scalar values.
*   **Subdivision Grid:** A finer grid generated by subdividing active voxels. The subdivision factor is not explicitly defined in the sources, but implies a recursive or fixed subdivision until the small cube size approximates pixel size [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Identify Surface Voxels:** Determine which voxels in the input grid intersect the isosurface using standard Marching Cubes topology logic [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Subdivide:** For each identified surface voxel, subdivide it into smaller cubes [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Topology Transfer:** Instead of generating triangles from the intersection of the small cube and the surface, transfer the cube topology into points [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Intensity Calculation:** Calculate the intensity for the intersection of the small cube and the surface [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Projection:** Project the calculated intensity onto the viewing plane [S2, unofficial].
6.  **Shading:** Form a gradient shaded representation of the object's 3D surface using the projected intensities [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Precision:** The method relies on data points being "very close together" [S1, unofficial]. If data is sparse, the approximation of triangles by points/pixels may fail to represent the surface accurately.
*   **Ambiguities:** The sources note that while Dividing Cubes avoids triangle generation, it does not explicitly resolve the topological ambiguities inherent in Marching Cubes, though it replaces the output format [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The subdivision step likely increases computational cost compared to standard Marching Cubes, but eliminates the rasterization/scan conversion step [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. Subdivision increases the number of primitives (small cubes/points) potentially increasing memory usage for the intermediate representation.
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates the scan conversion step [S2, unofficial]; provides high image quality for complex surfaces [S2, unofficial]; suitable for dense data where triangles are smaller than pixels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires dense data to be effective [S1, unofficial]; pre-processing for data structure preparation (e.g., octrees) may be required [S2, unofficial]; potential for false positives/negatives if data is not dense enough [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When data points are very close together and triangles would be smaller than a pixel [S1, unofficial]; when avoiding scan conversion is desired [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When data is sparse; when a triangular mesh representation is required for further processing (e.g., physics, texturing).

## Background
Dividing Cubes was developed as a variation of the Marching Cubes algorithm [S1, unofficial]. It was introduced to address the computational cost of the scan conversion step in Marching Cubes, particularly in the context of medical data rendering and stereotactic neurosurgery planning [S2, unofficial]. The method is part of the broader family of surface rendering techniques that compete with volume rendering methods like ray casting [S1, unofficial].
