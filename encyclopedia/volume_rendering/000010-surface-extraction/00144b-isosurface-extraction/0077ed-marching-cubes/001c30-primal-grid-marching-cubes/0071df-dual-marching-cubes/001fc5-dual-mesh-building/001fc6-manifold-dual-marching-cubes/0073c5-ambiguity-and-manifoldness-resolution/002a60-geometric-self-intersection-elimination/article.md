# Geometric Self-Intersection Elimination

Also called: Ambiguous face treatment, Ju et al. intersection removal

## Overview
Geometric Self-Intersection Elimination is a post-processing step applied during the connection of dual vertices in adaptive octree constructions to ensure the resulting isosurface is free of polygonal self-intersections [S1, unofficial]. While regular manifold treatments guarantee connectivity without altering mesh topology, this method specifically addresses the geometric validity of the surface in adaptive grids by replacing trivial triangulations with geometrically validated subsets, such as triangle fans, when ambiguity is detected [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Ambiguity and Manifoldness Resolution](22850-ambiguity-and-manifoldness-resolution.md) by extending the ambiguity identification (cases 2B and 3B) to a geometric resolution strategy that prioritizes intersection-free topology over vertex count minimization.

## How it works
The method operates on the dual mesh generated by the Manifold Dual Marching Cubes (MDMC) algorithm. It identifies faces shared between adjacent cells that correspond to ambiguous configurations in the DMC lookup table and resolves potential self-intersections by modifying the triangulation of the dual vertices associated with those faces.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** An adaptive octree structure containing meta-cells; the scalar field values defining the isosurface; the DMC lookup table identifying ambiguous configurations (specifically cases 2B and 3B) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A manifold, self-intersection-free triangular mesh where ambiguous faces are resolved using triangle fans or geometrically validated subsets instead of trivial triangulations [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The method relies on the standard dual mesh representation:
*   **Dual Vertices ($v_c$):** One vertex per cell, located inside the cell boundaries [S1, unofficial].
*   **Edge Vertices ($v_e$):** Vertices positioned on the edges of the grid cells (used in hybrid variants) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Face Vertices ($v_f$):** Vertices located on the faces of the grid cells [S1, unofficial].
*   **Meta-Cell Graph:** Adjacent cells are grouped into meta-cells. The algorithm iterates through the faces of these meta-cells to identify shared ambiguous faces [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Identify Ambiguous Faces:** Iterate through all faces shared between adjacent cells in the adaptive octree. Check if the cell configurations on either side of the face correspond to ambiguous cases 2B or 3B in the Dual Marching Cubes lookup table [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Determine Triangulation Strategy:** For each identified ambiguous face, determine if a trivial triangulation (connecting dual vertices directly) would result in self-intersection. In adaptive constructions, trivial triangulations are often insufficient due to varying resolution levels [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Generate Triangle Fans:** If a trivial triangulation is deemed unsafe or invalid for the specific adaptive configuration, generate a triangle fan originating from the minimum edge $e$ of the ambiguous face [S1, unofficial].
    *   The fan consists of triangles connecting the edge vertex $v_e$ (on edge $e$) to the face vertices $v_f$ (on faces containing $e$) and the dual vertices $v_c$ (inside the adjacent cells) [S1, unofficial].
    *   The size of the fan varies: typically eight or six triangles are created for each minimum edge, depending on the number of adjacent cells and their resolution levels [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Apply Geometric Rules (Hybrid Variant):** To mitigate the excessive polygon count generated by full triangle fans, apply geometric rules from Ju et al. [2006] to substitute large fans with a smaller number of triangles that remain within a valid geometric envelope [S1, unofficial]. This step is optional but recommended for performance [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Connect Meta-Cells:** Finalize the connections between dual vertices across meta-cell boundaries, ensuring that the resolved ambiguous faces are integrated into the global mesh without gaps or overlaps [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Hybrid Primal-Dual Method
A variant that combines primal (vertices on edges) and dual (vertices inside cells) approaches to create intersection-free surfaces in adaptive octrees [S1, unofficial]. It creates a fan of triangles from the minimum edge $e$, connecting vertex $v_e$ (on the edge) to vertices $v_f$ (on faces containing $e$) and dual vertices $v_c$ (inside cells) [S1, unofficial]. A key pitfall is that this method can create an excessive number of polygons per fan (typically eight or six triangles per minimum edge) [S1, unofficial]. To mitigate this, geometric rules from Ju et al. [2006] are used to substitute fans with fewer triangles within a valid envelope [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method adds a small overhead to the meta-cell processing time. In tests on a salt dome volume, processing time increased from 144s to 154s (+10s) for Level 6 trees, and from 161s to 171s for Level 7 trees [S1, unofficial]. The connection step increased by only 2 seconds [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** The method increases the number of vertices and faces in the resulting mesh. For Level 6, vertices increased from 85,220 to 87,138 (+2.2%) and triangles from 41,690 to 44,596 (+7%) [S1, unofficial]. For Level 7, vertices increased from 299,016 to 302,839 (+1.3%) and triangles from 148,188 to 154,248 (+4.1%) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Guarantees a self-intersection-free surface in adaptive constructions, which is critical for visual quality and further processing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Increases mesh size compared to non-resolution methods. The hybrid variant can produce excessive polygons if geometric rules are not applied [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Use when generating isosurfaces from adaptive octrees where geometric validity is paramount and self-intersections are unacceptable. Not suitable when minimal mesh size is the primary constraint and self-intersections can be tolerated or removed by other means.

## Background
The method is based on the work of Ju et al. [2006], who proposed a hybrid primal-dual approach for extracting intersection-free isosurfaces from adaptive octrees [S1, unofficial]. It was implemented and evaluated in the context of extracting salt dome isosurfaces from massive binary volumes, as described in [S1, unofficial]. The method extends the regular ambiguity treatment by addressing the specific challenges of adaptive grids, where trivial triangulations are insufficient to prevent self-intersections [S1, unofficial].
