# Geometric Self-Intersection Elimination

Also called: Ambiguous face treatment, Ju et al. intersection removal

## Overview
Geometric Self-Intersection Elimination is a post-processing geometric treatment applied during the connection of dual vertices in adaptive octree constructions to ensure the resulting isosurface is free of polygon self-intersections [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the topological defects inherent in naive dual triangulations of adaptive grids by replacing trivial triangulations with triangle fans or geometrically validated subsets when ambiguous face configurations are detected [S1, unofficial]. Unlike ambiguity treatments for regular (non-adaptive) constructions, which primarily ensure manifoldness without increasing vertex or face counts, this method explicitly increases mesh complexity to guarantee intersection-free geometry [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** An adaptive octree structure containing meta-cells; a lookup table mapping Dual Marching Cubes configurations (0-255) to ambiguous face directions (or 255 if non-problematic); the current meta-cell being processed [S1, unofficial].
- **Output:** A set of triangles connecting dual vertices and edge vertices that form a self-intersection-free surface patch [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Meta-cell:** A block of octree nodes at a specific level, processed as a unit [S1, unofficial].
- **Dual Vertex ($v_c$):** A vertex located inside a cell, typically at the centroid or computed via QEF [G].
- **Edge Vertex ($v_e$):** A vertex located on an edge of the grid, derived from primal methods [G].
- **Face Vertex ($v_f$):** A vertex located on a face of the grid [G].
- **Configuration Table:** Maps the 256 possible Dual Marching Cubes cases to an ambiguous face direction index [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. **Identify Ambiguous Faces:** Iterate through the faces of adjacent cells within the meta-cell and identify faces that are shared between adjacent cells and have configurations 2B or 3B from the Dual Marching Cubes lookup table [S1, unofficial].
2. **Detect Non-Trivial Edges:** For edges that do not allow for a trivial triangulation due to potential self-intersection identified in Step 1, mark them for special handling [S1, unofficial].
3. **Create Triangle Fans:** Instead of a single trivial triangulation connecting only dual vertices, create a fan of triangles from the minimum edge $e$ [S1, unofficial].
   - Let $v_e$ be the vertex on the edge $e$.
   - Let $v_f$ be the vertices on the faces containing $e$.
   - Let $v_c$ be the dual vertices inside the cells containing $e$.
   - Each triangle in the fan connects $v_e$ to a pair of $(v_f, v_c)$ or similar combinations depending on the specific hybrid rule [S1, unofficial].
4. **Apply Geometric Rules (Hybrid Method):** To reduce the excessive polygon count from full fans, apply geometric rules from Ju et al. [2006] to substitute fans with fewer triangles within a valid envelope [S1, unofficial].
   - If the geometric conditions are met, use a trivial triangulation.
   - Otherwise, use the fan.
   - Fans typically consist of eight or six triangles for each minimum edge [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Hybrid Primal-Dual Method
A hybrid method combining primal (vertices on edges) and dual (vertices inside cells) approaches to create intersection-free surfaces in adaptive octrees [S1, unofficial]. It creates a fan of triangles from the minimum edge $e$, connecting vertex $v_e$ (on the edge) to vertices $v_f$ (on faces containing $e$) and dual vertices $v_c$ (inside cells) [S1, unofficial]. Fans of eight or six triangles are created for each minimum edge [S1, unofficial]. A key pitfall is the excessive number of polygons created per fan; Ju et al. [2006] provide geometric rules to substitute fans with fewer triangles within a valid envelope [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- **Memory:** The method increases the number of vertices and faces in the resulting mesh [S1, unofficial]. For Level 6, vertices increase from 85,220 to 87,138 (+2.2%) and triangles from 41,690 to 44,596 (+7%) [S1, unofficial]. For Level 7, vertices increase from 299,016 to 302,839 (+1.3%) and triangles from 148,188 to 154,248 (+4.1%) [S1, unofficial].
- **Time:** Processing time for meta-cells increases slightly [S1, unofficial]. For Level 6, meta-cell processing time increases from 144s to 154s (+10s) and the connection step increases by 2s [S1, unofficial]. For Level 7, processing time increases from 161s to 171s [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths:** Ensures a self-intersection-free surface, which is critical for volume rendering and simulation [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Increases mesh complexity (vertices and faces) compared to regular ambiguity treatments [S1, unofficial]. The regular ambiguity treatment does not increase vertex or face count but only ensures manifoldness [S1, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
- Use when constructing isosurfaces from adaptive octrees where self-intersections are unacceptable [S1, unofficial].
- Use when the scene requires exact Euclidean distance calculations without propagation errors [S1, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
- Avoid if mesh size is a critical constraint and self-intersections can be tolerated or handled by other means [G].
- Avoid if processing time is extremely limited, as the geometric checks add overhead [G].

## Background
The method is based on the work of Ju et al. [2006], who proposed a hybrid primal-dual approach for adaptive octrees [S1, unofficial]. It was implemented and evaluated in the context of extracting isosurfaces from salt domes [S1, unofficial]. The implementation uses a lookup table for Dual Marching Cubes configurations to identify ambiguous faces [S1, unofficial].
