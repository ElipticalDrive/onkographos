# Combined optimization strategies

Also called: 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-d

## Overview
Combined optimization strategies are four specific combinations of X-face slashing and X-cube loop connection criteria used to optimize the topological and combinatorial properties of the resulting isosurface mesh [S1]. They resolve the ambiguity in how to handle X-faces and X-cubes by prioritizing specific mesh metrics: minimizing triangles ($T$), maximizing triangles, maximizing shells ($S$), or quasi-minimizing shells [S1]. These strategies are applied after the initial X-face identification and are distinct from sibling methods that do not explicitly target these topological invariants [S1].

## How it works
The method operates on the output of the X-cube loop connection criteria, which determines how free loops within X-cubes are connected [Parent]. The strategies define the decision logic for two atomic choices:
1.  **X-face slashing:** How to cut an X-face (which has two possible choices).
2.  **X-cube loop connection:** How to connect remaining free loops within an X-cube.

The notation $\min(W)$ denotes the subset of all meshes $M$ such that the metric $W(m)$ achieves its minimum value for all $m$ in that subset. Similarly, $\max(W)$ denotes the maximum. Where a global maximum or minimum cannot be guaranteed, the terms High and Low are used [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** The state of the X-face graph and the set of X-cubes with free loops.
*   **Output:** A triangulated isosurface mesh with optimized topological properties.

### Data Structures
The implementation maintains the mesh topology variables:
*   $V$: Number of vertices.
*   $T$: Number of triangles.
*   $H$: Number of handles.
*   $S$: Number of shells (connected components of the boundary).
*   $L$: Number of free loops in X-cubes before connection.
*   $s$: Number of loops actually connected.

### Formulas
The relationships between these variables are governed by:
$$ T = 4V + 2L - 4s $$
$$ T = 2V + 4(H - S) $$

From these, it follows that:
$$ 4(H - S) = 2V + 2L - 4s $$

### Steps and Strategies
The four strategies are defined by their optimization goals and the resulting constraints on $L$ and $s$.

**1. Strategy 1-a: Minimize T**
*   **Goal:** Minimize the number of triangles $T$ and minimize $H - S$.
*   **Logic:** Maximize $L$ (the number of free loops) and do not connect any loops ($s = L$).
*   **Derivation:** Substituting $s = L$ into the triangle formula:
    $$ T = 4V + 2L - 4L = 4V - 2L $$
    Maximizing $L$ leads to $\min(T)$.
*   **Topological Consequence:** Since $T = 2V + 4(H - S)$, minimizing $T$ implies minimizing $H - S$. This results in Low($H$) and High($S$) [S1].

**2. Strategy 2-b: Maximize T**
*   **Goal:** Maximize the number of triangles $T$ and maximize $H - S$.
*   **Logic:** Minimize $L$. Since $s \le L$, connecting loops decreases $S$.
*   **Derivation:**
    $$ 4(H - S) = 2V + 2L - 4s \ge 2V - 2L $$
    Minimizing $L$ and connecting loops (lowering $L$ and increasing $s$) leads to High($H - S$).
*   **Topological Consequence:** This results in High($H$) and Low($S$) [S1].

**3. Strategy 3-c: Maximize S**
*   **Goal:** Maximize the number of shells $S$.
*   **Logic:** Do not take any decision that reduces the number of shells if it is possible to avoid it. Criterion $c$ connects parts of the boundary already in the same component.
*   **Derivation:** Connecting parts of the same component does not change $S$ but increases $T$. Since $T = 2V + 4(H - S)$, an increase in $T$ with constant $S$ and $V$ requires an increase in $H$.
*   **Topological Consequence:** This results in High($H$) [S1].

**4. Strategy 4-d: Quasi-minimize S**
*   **Goal:** Tend toward $\min(S)$ with Low($H$).
*   **Logic:** Always connect disconnected equivalence classes.
*   **Limitation:** This strategy may not achieve the global minimum $S$ because ties are resolved randomly. If two slashing choices for an X-face yield the same number of equivalence classes, a random choice is made. This may connect vertices that could have been connected elsewhere, failing to connect vertices that could *only* be connected at that specific X-face. This event is rare but possible [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Global Optimality:** The Max/Min global optimal values for $T$, $H$, or $S$ are reached if and only if the X-face graph has no cycles. In most practical cases, the X-face graph is acyclic, so optimality is achieved [S1].
*   **Tie Situations:** Ties occur when two slashing choices for an X-face fulfill a given criterion equally well.
    *   *Single Magnitude:* If targeting only one metric, a simple random selection can resolve the tie, producing a random mesh from the set of optimal meshes [S1].
    *   *Multiple Magnitudes:* A more robust approach uses an ordered pair of combined criteria to optimize one magnitude while keeping another small [S1].
    *   *Parallel Analysis:* Another approach is to analyze the two candidate paths in parallel [S1].
*   **Internal Vertices:** When using criteria 2 (Maximize T) and 3 (Maximize S), representing the intersection of two or more tunnels may require the introduction of internal vertices [S1].
*   **Triangulation Artifacts:** The implementation triangulates each sheet such that no triangle lies on the faces of the cubes. Triangles on cube faces produce undesirable artifacts when actual data values are used for interpolating vertex positions along grid edges [S1].

    """
    Applies one of the four combined optimization strategies.
    
    Args:
        strategy_id: One of '1-a', '2-b', '3-c', '4-d'.
        x_faces: List of X-face objects, each with possible slashing choices.
        x_cubes: List of X-cube objects, each with free loops.
        
    Returns:
        Triangulated mesh.
    """
    mesh = initialize_mesh()
    
    for x_face in x_faces:
        choices = x_face.get_slashing_choices()
        
        if strategy_id == '1-a':
            # Minimize T: Maximize L, do not connect loops
            # Select choice that maximizes free loops
            choice = select_choice_maximizing_loops(choices)
            x_face.apply_slash(choice)
            
        elif strategy_id == '2-b':
            # Maximize T: Minimize L, connect loops
            # Select choice that minimizes free loops
            choice = select_choice_minimizing_loops(choices)
            x_face.apply_slash(choice)
            
        elif strategy_id == '3-c':
            # Maximize S: Do not reduce shells
            # Connect parts of boundary already in same component
            choice = select_choice_preserving_components(choices)
            x_face.apply_slash(choice)
            
        elif strategy_id == '4-d':
            # Quasi-minimize S: Connect disconnected equivalence classes
            choice = select_choice_connecting_disconnected_classes(choices)
            
            # Handle tie: if choices are equivalent, random selection
            if is_tie(choices):
                choice = random.choice(choices)
                
            x_face.apply_slash(choice)
            
    for x_cube in x_cubes:
        loops = x_cube.get_free_loops()
        
        if strategy_id == '1-a':
            # Do not connect loops (s = L)
            x_cube.keep_loops_unconnected(loops)
            
        elif strategy_id == '2-b':
            # Connect loops to decrease S
            x_cube.connect_loops(loops)
            
        elif strategy_id == '3-c':
            # Connect if it doesn't reduce S (already handled in X-face logic mostly)
            # But X-cube logic follows criterion c
            x_cube.apply_criterion_c(loops)
            
        elif strategy_id == '4-d':
            # Connect disconnected equivalence classes
            x_cube.connect_disconnected_classes(loops)
            
    # Post-processing: Triangulate sheets
    # Ensure no triangle lies on cube faces to avoid interpolation artifacts
    triangulate_sheets(mesh)
    
    return mesh

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** The complexity is determined by the traversal of the X-face graph and X-cubes. The global optimality is guaranteed if the X-face graph is acyclic [S1].
*   **Performance:** The strategies are implemented and tested, with results discussed in the source literature [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Provides explicit control over mesh topology ($T, H, S$). Strategy 1-a is optimal for minimizing triangle count. Strategy 4-d is effective for reducing shell count in most practical cases [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Strategy 4-d is "quasi-optimal" and may fail to achieve the global minimum $S$ due to random tie-breaking [S1]. Strategies 2 and 3 may require internal vertices for complex tunnel intersections [S1]. The other 12 combined decisions (1-b, 1-c, etc.) are not defined as they involve conflicting decisions [S1].
