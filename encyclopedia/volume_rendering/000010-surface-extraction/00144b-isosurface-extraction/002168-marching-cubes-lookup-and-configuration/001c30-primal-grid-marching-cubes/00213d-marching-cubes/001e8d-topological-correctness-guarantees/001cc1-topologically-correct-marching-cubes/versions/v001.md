# Topologically Correct Marching Cubes

## Overview
Topologically Correct Marching Cubes (TMC) is an extension to the standard Marching Cubes algorithm that guarantees the output mesh is homeomorphic to the trilinear interpolant of the input scalar field within each cell [S1]. While standard Marching Cubes may produce topologically incorrect surfaces (e.g., representing a tunnel as two disjoint sheets), TMC ensures the mesh accurately represents small complex features and remains 2-manifold and watertight, provided the isosurface does not intersect the domain boundaries [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A domain containing a scalar value field defined on a grid, and an isovalue [S1].
- **Output:** A polygon mesh (points and polygons) homeomorphic to the trilinear interpolant [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The implementation relies on the standard Marching Cubes lookup tables but augments them with logic to detect and resolve topological ambiguities. The core data structure is the cell cube, defined by 8 vertex values. When a cell configuration is ambiguous (specifically the saddle configurations), TMC inserts additional vertices to resolve the topology [S1].

**Steps:**
1. Iterate over all cells in the domain.
2. Determine the cell configuration based on the sign of the scalar values at the 8 vertices relative to the isovalue.
3. If the configuration is one of the standard non-ambiguous cases, use the standard Marching Cubes triangulation [S1].
4. If the configuration is one of the ambiguous cases (where the trilinear interpolant may contain a tunnel or complex topology), TMC detects this ambiguity [S1].
5. Insert additional vertices within the cell to resolve the complex topology. This involves subdividing the cell or adding vertices at specific locations to ensure the resulting mesh is homeomorphic to the trilinear surface [S1].
6. Generate the triangulation for the resolved cell.
7. Assemble the global mesh. The result is guaranteed to be 2-manifold and watertight if the isosurface does not intersect the domain boundaries [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- If the isosurface intersects the domain boundaries, the guarantee of a 2-manifold and watertight mesh does not hold [S1].
- TMC does not guarantee the recovery of sharp features present in the input value field [S1].
- Standard MC may produce needle or cap-shaped triangles with poor quality; TMC improves topological correctness but mesh quality (aspect ratio) may still be an issue [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Higher than standard Marching Cubes due to the additional checks for ambiguous cases and the computation of additional vertices [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Increases due to the potential insertion of additional vertices within cells [S1].
- **Strengths:** Guarantees topological correctness (homeomorphism to trilinear interpolant), accurately represents small complex features (e.g., tunnels), and ensures 2-manifold and watertight output under boundary conditions [S1].
- **Limitations:** Does not guarantee recovery of sharp features; mesh quality (aspect ratio) may still be poor without additional processing [S1].
- **When to use:** When topological correctness is critical, such as in medical imaging or scientific visualization where tunnels or complex internal structures must be preserved [S1].
- **When not to use:** When sharp feature preservation is required, or when performance is critical and topological errors are acceptable [S1].

## Background
Topologically Correct Marching Cubes is an extension to the classic Marching Cubes algorithm. It was developed to address the topological inaccuracies inherent in the standard lookup-table approach, particularly in ambiguous cell configurations. The method is implemented in the CGAL library as part of its 3D Isosurfacing module [S1].
