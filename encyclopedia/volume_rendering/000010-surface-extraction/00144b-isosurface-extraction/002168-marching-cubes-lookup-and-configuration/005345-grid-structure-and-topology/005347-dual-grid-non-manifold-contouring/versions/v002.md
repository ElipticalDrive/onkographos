# Dual-Grid Non-Manifold Contouring

Also called: Dual-grid approach, Voxel-based contouring

## Overview
Dual-Grid Non-Manifold Contouring extracts non-manifold meshes from segmented volumes containing multiple materials by utilizing a dual grid structure where material attributes are associated with voxel centers rather than cell corners [S1]. Unlike generalized marching cubes, which requires large lookup tables for multi-material cases (e.g., 58 triangulations for three materials), this method avoids lookup tables entirely by generating quadrilateral faces at voxel boundaries and resolving topological ambiguities through a priority-based vertex splitting mechanism [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input**: A 3D volume grid where each voxel $v$ is assigned a discrete material attribute $m(v)$ [S1]. The grid is treated as a dual grid, meaning material values reside at voxel centers [S1].
*   **Output**: A non-manifold mesh composed of quadrilateral faces [S1]. Each face is tagged with the material attributes of the two adjacent voxels [S1].

**Data Structures**
Exact data structure layouts (fields, sizes, bit packing) are not given in the sources [G]. Conceptually, the method requires a material grid storing voxel attributes, a face list storing quadrilateral boundaries with dual material tags, and a vertex map tracking geometric positions alongside their associated material components for splitting [G].

**Algorithm Steps**
1.  **Grid Shift**: Conceptually shift the hexahedral grid by half a cell width in each direction so that material attributes are associated with voxel centers [S1].
2.  **Face Generation**: Iterate over every pair of adjacent grid voxels in a 6-neighborhood (sharing a face) [S1]. For each pair $(v_i, v_j)$ with different material attributes $m(v_i) \neq m(v_j)$, create a quadrilateral face representing their common boundary [S1].
3.  **Tagging**: Tag each generated face with the material attributes of the two adjacent voxels, $m(v_i)$ and $m(v_j)$ [S1].
4.  **Initial Vertex Creation**: For every cell (corresponding to the primal grid cell used in standard Marching Cubes) intersected by a surface component, generate one initial vertex [S1].
5.  **Ambiguity Resolution**: Resolve topological ambiguities (e.g., separating two sheets of the same material) by introducing a priority ordering of material attributes [S1]. Material components of greater priority are merged in ambiguous cases [S1].
6.  **Virtual Movement**: Implement priority merging by virtually moving mesh components toward the material of lower priority [S1]. Since every face is associated with two materials, the direction of this virtual movement is unique [S1].
7.  **Vertex Splitting**: Split vertices incident to faces moving in opposite directions into multiple topological vertices, each associated with an individual mesh component [S1].
8.  **Constraint Check**: Do not perform vertex splitting if at least one polygon contains both components of a split vertex [S1]. This ensures only quadrilaterals are generated, as splitting in such cases would produce non-quadrilateral faces [S1]. These cases occur in regions with three or more adjacent materials [S1].

**Parameters and Edge Cases**
*   **Priority Ordering**: A strict total ordering of material attributes is required to resolve ambiguities [S1]. The choice of priority affects the final topology [S1].
*   **Three or More Materials**: In regions where three or more materials meet, vertex splitting must be skipped if a polygon contains both components of a split vertex to maintain quadrilateral topology [S1].
*   **Precision**: Geometric coordinates are computed at face centers (half-integer offsets) [G]. Floating-point precision issues may arise when determining vertex equality; rounding is necessary [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths**: Handles non-manifold topology naturally; no lookup table required for multi-material cases; generates quadrilateral meshes which are suitable for certain fairing and subdivision techniques [S1].
*   **Limitations**: Requires a priority ordering for ambiguity resolution, which may not always reflect the true physical topology; vertex splitting logic is complex to implement correctly for general cases; may produce gaps if material boundaries are not well-defined [S1].
*   **When to Use**: Segmented volumes with multiple distinct materials where non-manifold topology is expected or desired (e.g., medical imaging with overlapping tissues) [S1].
*   **When Not to Use**: Cases where a strictly manifold topology is required without post-processing, as the union of all extracted meshes defines a non-manifold surface and only the set of meshes enclosing one particular material is guaranteed to be a manifold [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed as an alternative to generalized marching cubes for extracting non-manifold meshes from segmented volumes, particularly in the context of modeling a human heart from MRI data [S1]. Generalized marching cubes, as proposed by [HSSZ97], requires a lookup table with 58 topologically different triangulations for three materials, which becomes intractable for eight materials [S1]. The dual-grid approach, inspired by earlier work on dual grids [Gib98, Nie04], was adopted to avoid constructing such large lookup tables [S1]. The method was presented at EuroVis 2005 [S1].
