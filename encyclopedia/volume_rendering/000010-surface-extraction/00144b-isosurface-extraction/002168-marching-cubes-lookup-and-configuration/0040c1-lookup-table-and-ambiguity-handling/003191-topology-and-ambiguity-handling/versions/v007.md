# Topology and Ambiguity Handling

Also called: Ambiguity Resolution, Topological Consistency

## Overview
This family addresses the topological ambiguities inherent in the standard Marching Cubes lookup table, where specific sign configurations (cases 46, 47, 98, 99, 210, 213, 214, 215) contain saddle points at the cube center [10327-lookup-table-and-ambiguity-handling]. Standard lookup tables resolve these ambiguities using a fixed triangulation, which can produce cracks or holes if adjacent voxels resolve the ambiguity inconsistently [10327-lookup-table-and-ambiguity-handling]. Methods in this family ensure topological consistency and manifold output by either modifying the dual structure [2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes] or decomposing the cube into disjoint patches based on connectivity [5004-marching-cubes-with-disjoint-patch-decomposition].

## Shared principles
The family relies on the precomputed data structures defined in [Lookup Table and Ambiguity Handling](10327-lookup-table-and-ambiguity-handling.md): the `edgeTable` and `triangleTable`. The core problem is that the 8-bit case index $C$ determines the intersection pattern, but for ambiguous cases, the standard table does not guarantee a consistent manifold across voxel boundaries [10327-lookup-table-and-ambiguity-handling]. All methods in this family must ensure that the topology generated within a voxel is compatible with its neighbors, typically by enforcing a consistent rule for resolving the saddle point at the cube center.

## Subtopics

### [Hermite Dual Marching Cubes](2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes.md)
Hermite Dual Marching Cubes combines the topological guarantees of Dual Marching Cubes with feature-preserving capabilities [2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes]. It solves the problem of non-manifold vertices and edges in standard Dual Contouring by leveraging the dual structure of Marching Cubes topology [2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes]. Unlike standard Dual Contouring, it always produces a topological manifold [2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes]. It is chosen when sharp features must be preserved while maintaining strict manifold topology.

### [Marching Cubes with Disjoint Patch Decomposition](5004-marching-cubes-with-disjoint-patch-decomposition.md)
This method resolves ambiguities by decomposing the cube into a graph of 15 points (8 vertices, 6 face centers, 1 cube center) and grouping intersected edges into disjoint, non-overlapping patches using graph-based coloring [5004-marching-cubes-with-dispatch-decomposition]. It sets itself apart from Hermite Dual Marching Cubes by relying on scalar field averaging and connectivity analysis rather than normal vectors or dual-space representations [5004-marching-cubes-with-dispatch-decomposition]. It is chosen when manifold output is required without additional geometric data like normals.

## Background
The topological ambiguity in Marching Cubes was identified by Lorensen and Cline in their original 1987 paper, which introduced the standard lookup table [10327-lookup-table-and-ambiguity-handling]. The ambiguity arises because the isosurface passes through the cube center in specific cases, allowing for multiple valid topological interpretations. Subsequent research, including Dual Marching Cubes by Nielson and various patch decomposition methods, sought to resolve these inconsistencies to ensure watertight meshes [2197-hermite-dual-marching-cubes][5004-marching-cubes-with-dispatch-decomposition].
