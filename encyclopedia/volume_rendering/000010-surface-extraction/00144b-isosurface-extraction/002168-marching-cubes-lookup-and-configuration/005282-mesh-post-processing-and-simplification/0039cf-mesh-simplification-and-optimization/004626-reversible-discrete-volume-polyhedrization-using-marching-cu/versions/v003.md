# Reversible Discrete Volume Polyhedrization using Marching Cubes Simplification

Also called: MC simplification using DPS, Marching Cubes Simplification with Digital Plane Segmentation, RDP-MC, Reversible MC Simplification, Reversible discrete volume polyhedrization using MC simplification

## Overview
This method constructs a reversible and topologically correct polyhedron from a binary 3D discrete volume by simplifying a Marching Cubes (MC) surface using Digital Plane Segmentation (DPS) information [S1]. It reduces the facet count of the MC mesh while strictly preserving the Object Boundary Quantization (OBQ) digitization property, ensuring the final polyhedron reconstructs the original voxel set exactly [S1]. Unlike standard simplification methods that may alter topology or reversibility, this approach restricts reconstruction to the cells defined by the original MC surface, allowing local analysis to guarantee global topological correctness [S1]. It is distinct from [Simplified Marching Cubes](3759-simplified-marching-cubes.md) and [Modified Marching Cubes with Triangle Decimation](3903-modified-marching-cubes-with-triangle-decimation.md) by its explicit use of DPS preimages and linear programming to enforce reversibility constraints during triangle removal [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A binary 3D volume $V$ where $V(x,y,z) \in \{0, 1\}$, and an iso-level $I \in ]0, 1[$.
**Outputs:** A simplified polyhedral mesh $M'$ consisting of coplanar facets (polygons) that is reversible to $V$.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **MC Surface $M$:** Generated by standard Marching Cubes. Consists of vertices $V_{mc}$, triangles $T_{mc}$, and adjacency graphs. Each vertex $v \in V_{mc}$ maps one-to-one to a **surfel** $\{p, q\}$, where $p$ is a voxel in the object ($V(p)=1$) and $q$ is a neighbor voxel in the background ($V(q)=0$). The vertex $v$ lies on the open segment $]pq[$ [S1].
2.  **DPS Labeling:** Each surfel is labeled with a Digital Plane Segment (DPS) identifier. A DPS is a set of voxels $V$ coplanar with parameters $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \in [0, 1]^2 \times [0, 1[$ such that $V \subset P = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 \mid 0 \le \alpha x + \beta y + \gamma + z < 1\}$ [S1].
3.  **Preimage $\mathcal{P}$:** For a DPS, the preimage is the set of valid $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ parameters. For a direction $d \in D = \{(\pm 1, 0, 0), (0, \pm 1, 0), (0, 0, \pm 1)\}$, the directional preimage is the set of planes crossing all segments $[pq[$ for surfels in the segment [S1].
4.  **Triangle Classification:**
    *   **Homogeneous (H):** All three vertices belong to surfels with the same DPS label.
    *   **Non-Homogeneous (NH):** Vertices belong to different DPS labels.
        *   **2-NH:** Vertices belong to exactly 2 distinct DPS labels.
        *   **3-NH:** Vertices belong to exactly 3 distinct DPS labels.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **MC Extraction:** Generate the initial MC surface $M$ from $V$ using iso-level $I \in ]0, 1[$ [S1].
2.  **DPS Decomposition:** Label every surfel of the surface with a DPS and compute its preimage $\mathcal{P}$ [S1].
3.  **Homogeneous Triangle Simplification:**
    *   Identify connected sets $S$ of H-triangles sharing the same DPS label.
    *   Extract an Euclidean plane $P$ from the DPS preimage associated with $S$.
    *   Project all vertices of $S$ onto $P$ along the direction $pq$ of their associated surfels. This preserves reversibility [S1].
    *   Merge connected sets of H-triangles into single coplanar facets [S1].
4.  **Non-Homogeneous Triangle Optimization:**
    *   **Constraint:** The intersection of simplified planes must remain within the MC cell face to ensure reversibility via local analysis [S1].
    *   **Step 4a: Global Simplification:**
        *   Process an unsorted list of all 2-NH triangles.
        *   For each 2-NH triangle, arbitrarily select an edge $e$ to remove. Let $P_1, P_2$ be the preimages of the two associated planes.
        *   Add linear constraints to $P_1$ and $P_2$ to force their intersection to lie inside the MC cell face associated with $e$.
        *   If constraints make a preimage empty, discard constraints and handle removal in Step 4b.
        *   Skip 3-NH triangles in this step [S1].
    *   **Step 4b: Greedy Simplification:**
        *   Arbitrarily choose a remaining 2-NH triangle.
        *   Fix one of its planes $P_1$ using the barycenter of its preimage.
        *   Add constraints to the second plane $P_2$ to remove the triangle if possible.
        *   Propagate to adjacent triangles. If a 3-NH is encountered, skip it until two of its three associated planes are fixed.
        *   If a triangle cannot be removed and no adjacent triangles remain, select a new 2-NH triangle.
        *   Continue until all planes are fixed or no further removal is possible [S1].
5.  **Finalization:**
    *   Displace all vertices to the intersection of their fixed Euclidean plane and their $]pq[$ segment.
    *   Group coplanar triangles into final polygonal facets [S1].

**Edge Removal Conditions (Sufficient Conditions):**
For an edge $e$ on a face $ABCD$ of an MC cell, with associated preimages $P_1, P_2$, the edge can be removed if the intersection $I$ of any $P_1 \in P_1$ and $P_2 \in P_2$ lies inside the square $ABCD$. This is ensured by linear constraints on the parameters of $P_2$ (since $P_1$ is fixed or constrained):
$$x_A < x_I < x_A + 1, \quad y_A < y_I < y_A + 1$$
where $x_I, y_I$ depend linearly on $P_2$'s parameters [S1].

Specific voxel configurations on face $ABCD$ yield specific geometric constraints:
*   **Case 1 (1 object voxel A):** $P_1$ (associated with surfel $\{A,B\}$) crosses segment $CD$, and $P_2$ (associated with $\{A,D\}$) crosses segment $BC$ [S1].
*   **Case 2 (2 object voxels):** No interesting linear constraints are generated [S1].
*   **Case 3 (1 background voxel C):** $P_1$ (associated with $\{D,C\}$) crosses segment $AB$, and $P_2$ (associated with $\{B,C\}$) crosses segment $AD$ [S1].

For 3-NH triangles, if two planes are fixed, the third plane's intersection $I$ must satisfy:
$$x_A < x_I < x_A + 1, \quad y_A < y_I < y_A + 1, \quad z_A < z_I < z_A + 1$$
[ S1]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def reversible_mc_simplification(volume, iso_level):
    # 1. MC Extraction
    # iso_level must be in ]0, 1[ for reversibility
    mesh = marching_cubes(volume, iso_level)
    
    # 2. DPS Decomposition
    # Labels surfels with DPS ID and computes initial preimages P
    surfel_labels, preimages = digital_plane_segmentation(mesh)
    
    # 3. Homogeneous Triangle Simplification
    # Project H-triangles onto their DPS plane and merge facets
    mesh = simplify_homogeneous_triangles(mesh, surfel_labels, preimages)
    
    # 4. Non-Homogeneous Triangle Optimization
    
    # Identify NH triangles, excluding complex MC configurations
    # Configurations with 2+ surface patches (not in {1,2,5,8,9,11}) are skipped
    nh_triangles = get_non_homogeneous_triangles(mesh, surfel_labels)
    valid_nh_triangles = [t for t in nh_triangles if is_simple_mc_config(t)]
    
    # Step 4a: Global Simplification
    # Process all 2-NH triangles to add constraints
    for tri in valid_nh_triangles:
        if tri.type == '2-NH':
            edge = select_edge_to_remove(tri)
            p1, p2 = get_preimages_for_edge(edge, surfel_labels)
            constraints = get_sufficient_constraints(edge.face, p1, p2)
            if not make_preimage_empty(p1, constraints) and not make_preimage_empty(p2, constraints):
                apply_constraints(p1, constraints)
                apply_constraints(p2, constraints)
                mark_edge_removed(tri, edge)
            else:
                # Constraints invalid, handle in greedy step
                pass
        # 3-NH triangles are skipped in global step
    
    # Step 4b: Greedy Simplification
    # Fix planes and propagate constraints
    remaining_nh = [t for t in valid_nh_triangles if not is_removed(t)]
    while remaining_nh:
        tri = remaining_nh.pop(0)
        if tri.type == '2-NH':
            # Fix one plane to barycenter of its preimage
            p1 = fix_plane_to_barycenter(tri.preimage_1)
            # Try to remove triangle by constraining p2
            constraints = get_intersection_constraints(tri.face, p1, tri.preimage_2)
            if constraints_are_valid(constraints):
                apply_constraints(tri.preimage_2, constraints)
                mark_triangle_removed(tri)
                # Propagate to adjacent triangles
                propagate_constraints(tri, remaining_nh)
            else:
                # Cannot remove, try next
                pass
        elif tri.type == '3-NH':
            # Skip until two planes are fixed
            if tri.plane_1_fixed and tri.plane_2_fixed:
                # Add constraints for third plane intersection
                constraints = get_3plane_intersection_constraints(tri.face, tri.plane_1, tri.plane_2, tri.preimage_3)
                if constraints_are_valid(constraints):
                    apply_constraints(tri.preimage_3, constraints)
                    mark_triangle_removed(tri)
    
    # 5. Finalization
    # Displace vertices and merge coplanar facets
    final_mesh = finalize_mesh(mesh, surfel_labels)
    return final_mesh

def get_sufficient_constraints(face, p1, p2):
    # Determine voxel configuration on face ABCD
    voxels = get_voxels_on_face(face)
    if len(voxels) == 1:
        # Case 1 or 3 depending on object/background
        return get_crossing_constraints(voxels, p1, p2)
    elif len(voxels) == 2:
        return [] # No interesting constraints
    else:
        return [] # Fallback

def get_intersection_constraints(face, fixed_plane, mobile_preimage):
    # Linear constraints: x_A < x_I < x_A + 1, y_A < y_I < y_A + 1
    # x_I, y_I are linear functions of mobile_preimage parameters
    return linear_inequality_system(face, fixed_plane, mobile_preimage)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Highly dependent on the size of the DPS preimages. A bound on preimage size ensures bounded costs for DPS decomposition and linear programming solver efficiency [S1]. The linear programming dimension is bounded by 3 to limit computational cost [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing DPS labels, preimages, and adjacency graphs. Large volumes may require rational arithmetic for exact DPS recognition, causing the arithmetical size of internal rational numbers to increase quickly, creating a memory and computational bottleneck [S1].
*   **Performance:** In experiments, the global removal rate is always greater than 75% [S1]. NH triangle removal shows an improvement of at least 30% and up to 60% compared to the initial algorithm [S1].
    *   Pyramid ($4^3$): 512 MC triangles, 342 H, 170 NH. NH removal 62%, Global removal 87% [S1].
    *   Cube ($7^3$): 2024 MC triangles, 1720 H, 304 NH. NH removal 89%, Global removal 98% [S1].
    *   Cube ($10^3$): 3656 MC triangles, 2200 H, 1456 NH. NH removal 37%, Global removal 75% [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Guarantees reversibility (OBQ digitization) and topological correctness. Reduces mesh complexity significantly while preserving the exact voxel set.
*   **Limitations:** Restricted to simple MC configurations (1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 11) for optimization; complex configurations are excluded to avoid overly restrictive constraints [S1]. Visual quality may vary among reversible polygons, though the method prioritizes reversibility over optimal visual fidelity [S1]. Rational arithmetic bottleneck for large volumes [S1].
*   **When to use:** When exact reversibility to the original binary volume is required, such as in medical imaging or geometric modeling where voxel integrity must be preserved.
*   **When not to use:** When visual fidelity is prioritized over reversibility, or when processing very large volumes where rational arithmetic overhead is prohibitive.

## Background
The method was proposed by David Coeurjolly, Florent Dupont, and Isabelle Sivignon from LIRIS, CNRS, France [S1]. It is based on the Object Boundary Quantization (OBQ) model, which establishes that the Marching Cubes surface with an iso-level in $]0, 1[$ is a reversible polyhedrization of a binary object [S1]. The algorithm builds on digital plane segmentation techniques [2, 14, 13] and linear programming for discrete geometry [10, 3, 15]. It addresses the difficulty of exploiting 3D discrete volumes (e.g., from MRI/CT) by providing a simplified, manifold representation that retains the exact topological and geometric information of the original voxel data [S1].
