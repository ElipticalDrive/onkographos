# Reducing Surface Self-Intersections
Also called: Partitioning optimization for Surface Nets, Self-intersection avoidance in Surface Nets

## Overview
This method prevents local self-intersections in triangle meshes generated by Surface Nets by selecting the optimal triangulation for each non-planar quadrilateral. While standard Surface Nets places vertices at voxel centers to produce smoother surfaces than edge-based methods [S1], the resulting quadrilaterals are generally non-planar. This non-planarity, particularly near sharp boundaries or edges, can lead to intersecting triangles if the diagonal partitioning is chosen arbitrarily [S2, unofficial]. This technique resolves that ambiguity by minimizing local surface area.

## How it works
**Inputs:** A mesh of non-planar quadrilaterals generated by the Surface Nets algorithm.
**Outputs:** A triangle mesh where each quadrilateral is partitioned into two triangles without local self-intersections.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Quadrilateral:** Defined by four vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3$ in counter-clockwise order.
*   **Triangle:** Defined by three vertices.

**Steps:**
1.  For each quadrilateral $Q$ in the mesh:
2.  Define the two possible triangulation patterns:
    *   Pattern A: Triangles $(v_0, v_1, v_2)$ and $(v_0, v_2, v_3)$. Diagonal connects $v_0$ and $v_2$.
    *   Pattern B: Triangles $(v_0, v_1, v_3)$ and $(v_1, v_2, v_3)$. Diagonal connects $v_1$ and $v_3$.
3.  Calculate the total surface area for Pattern A ($A_A$) and Pattern B ($A_B$). The area of a triangle with vertices $a, b, c$ is:
    $$ Area(a,b,c) = \frac{1}{2} \| (b-a) \times (c-a) \| $$
    $$ A_A = Area(v_0, v_1, v_2) + Area(v_0, v_2, v_3) $$
    $$ A_B = Area(v_0, v_1, v_3) + Area(v_1, v_2, v_3) $$
4.  Select the pattern with the smaller area:
    *   If $A_A < A_B$, output triangles from Pattern A.
    *   Otherwise, output triangles from Pattern B.
5.  Repeat for all quadrilaterals.

**Parameters:**
*   None. The decision is deterministic based on geometry.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Non-planarity:** Surface Nets quadrilaterals are rarely planar. The choice of diagonal affects the local surface shape significantly [S2, unofficial].
*   **Neighboring Intersections:** When a quadrilateral is highly non-planar (e.g., near an edge), the choice of partitioning for neighboring quadrilaterals can cause triangles to intersect each other [S2, unofficial]. This local minimization helps but does not guarantee global non-intersection.
*   **Performance:** This approach is slightly slower than fixed-diagonal partitioning due to the extra area calculations [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of quadrilaterals. Each quadrilateral requires constant-time area calculations.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory per quadrilateral.
*   **Strengths:** Reduces local self-intersections, which is critical for applications like tetrahedralization and 3D printing [S2, unofficial]. Produces a smoother output surface compared to edge-based vertex placement methods [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not guarantee global non-intersection. Slightly higher computational cost than naive triangulation.
*   **When to use:** When exporting Surface Nets geometry to formats requiring watertight or non-intersecting meshes (e.g., OBJ for 3D printing) [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and self-intersections are tolerable or handled by downstream processes.

## Background
Surface Nets was developed as an extension to Marching Cubes to solve ambiguities and create higher quality output surfaces by placing intersecting vertices in the middle of volume voxels rather than at the edges [S1]. Dual Contouring was later developed as an extension to both Surface Nets and Marching Cubes, retaining a dual vertex within the voxel but interpolating its position using edge crossings and normals to retain sharp features [S1]. The self-intersection reduction technique is applied during the export of Surface Nets geometry, particularly for OBJ file generation [S2, unofficial].
