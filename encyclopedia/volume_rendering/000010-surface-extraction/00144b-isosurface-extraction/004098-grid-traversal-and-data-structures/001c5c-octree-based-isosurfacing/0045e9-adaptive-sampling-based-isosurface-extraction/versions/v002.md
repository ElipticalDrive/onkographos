# Adaptive Sampling-based Isosurface Extraction

Also called: Octree Dual Contouring (OctDC), Octree Marching Cubes (OctMC)

## Overview
Adaptive Sampling-based Isosurface Extraction is a category of methods that construct a mesh by adaptively sampling the implicit field, as opposed to analytic extraction [S1]. These methods rely on octrees as spatial discretization structures to refine resolution near the surface [S1]. They are distinguished from their siblings by their reliance on discrete sampling and topological reconstruction algorithms rather than direct analytic evaluation of the implicit function [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves traversing an octree to identify regions where the implicit field $f(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ intersects the volume [G]. Unlike analytic methods that evaluate the field continuously, these methods sample the field at discrete points to determine topology [G]. The adaptive nature leads to variable resolution across the domain, though evidence indicates that octree adaptivity does not prevent near-surface explosion on curved shapes, causing impractical leaf counts at high depths [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Octree Marching Cubes (OctMC)](1247-octree-based-isosurfacing.md)
Uses the Marching Cubes algorithm on an octree. It is simpler to implement but prone to cracks if the octree is not strictly adapted to the surface topology.

### [Octree Dual Contouring (OctDC)](1247-octree-based-isosurfacing.md)
Uses Dual Contouring on an octree. It produces smoother surfaces and handles topology changes better than OctMC but requires solving a least-squares system for vertex positions.

### [McGrids](1247-octree-based-isosurfacing.md)
A grid-based adaptive method that uses a different spatial discretization strategy than standard octrees, often trading off some adaptivity for simpler data structures.

## Background
The comparison data comes from an evaluation of an analytic extraction method (referred to as 'Ours' or Analytic Marching) which contrasts these sampling-based approaches [S1]. At depth 12 on the Stanford dataset, octree-based extraction became impractical; the Bunny model alone produced 71M leaves, indicating that octree adaptivity does not prevent near-surface explosion on curved shapes [S1]. Runtime and vertex counts for OctMC, OctDC, and McGrids are substantially higher than analytic methods when using the same trained network [S1].
