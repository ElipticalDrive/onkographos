# Valley Surface Extraction via Point Seeding and Convergence

Also called: Valley surface extraction, Mesh-Free Valley Surface Extraction, Mesh-Free Valley Surfaces, Valley Surface Construction

## Overview
Valley Surface Extraction via Point Seeding and Convergence is a mesh-free algorithm that extracts adaptive, non-manifold valley and ridge surfaces from complex-valued 3D scalar fields by decoupling surface extraction from triangulation [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of extracting maximal convexity ridges (valleys) in fields where traditional iso-surface methods fail due to the lack of sign changes across the structure and large variations in function values along the feature [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Valley Cell Construction](9747-valley-cell-construction.md) which decomposes the space into cells, this method grows a connected skeleton directly by seeding points on adaptive ellipsoids around existing surface points and converging them to the valley surface using local polynomial approximations or Newton iteration [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Scattered scalar field data $f(x,y,z)$ (potentially complex-valued, e.g., acoustic pressure) [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A set of points forming the valley surface, connected via a parent-child graph, along with normals and curvature estimates [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
* **Point:** Contains position $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, normal $n \in \mathbb{R}^3$, parent pointer, list of children, and convergence status [S1, unofficial].
* **Local Field Approximation:** A trivariate polynomial $h(x,y,z)$ fitted via Moving Least Squares (MLS) to provide $f$, $\nabla f$, and $H_f$ (Hessian) at any query point [S1, unofficial].
* **Grid:** Uniform grid for fast neighbor lookup, cell size $s_{grid} \approx 2.5 \times \text{avg point distance}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
* $\epsilon$: Convergence threshold, typically $10^{-10}$ to $10^{-4}$ [S1, unofficial].
* $\theta$: Merging threshold, empirically $\approx 0.8 \times \text{distance to parent}$ [S1, unofficial].
* $lod$: Level of Detail, influencing mesh resolution and extraction time [S1, unofficial].
* $r_{seed}$: Initial seed ellipsoid axes length, set to $s_{grid}/4$ for the first seeding step [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1. **Pre-processing:**
    * Construct a uniform grid with cell size $s_{grid} \approx 2.5 \times \text{average point distance}$ [S1, unofficial].
    * Set MLS kernel radius $r_{MLS} \approx s_{grid}$ [S1, unofficial]. Increase $r_{MLS}$ and adjacent cells if point density is low [S1, unofficial].

2. **Initialization (Find First Point):**
    * Iterate over grid cell centroids $x$.
    * Perform Newton iteration to find an initial surface point:
        $$ x_{i+1} = x_i - e_3 \frac{\nabla f(x_i) \cdot e_3}{\lambda_3(x_i)} $$
        where $e_3$ is the eigenvector of maximum curvature and $\lambda_3$ is the corresponding eigenvalue of $H_f$ [S1, unofficial].
    * Compute the ridge normal $n$ using the approximation:
        $$ n_i = \sum_j \frac{\partial e_3}{\partial x_j} e_{3j} + \sum_j \frac{\partial e_{3j}}{\partial x_j} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j} $$
        This requires third derivatives of $f$ (via cubic polynomial approximation or finite differences) [S1, unofficial].
    * If third derivatives are unavailable or unstable, use $e_3$ as a rough normal approximation [S1, unofficial].
    * The first converged point becomes the initial seed $p_{seed}$ and parent for subsequent growth [S1, unofficial].

3. **Region Growing Loop:**
    * While there are surface points $p$ that have not yet seeded neighbors:
        a. **Seeding:** For point $p$ with normal $n_p$ and parent $p_{parent}$:
            * Compute curvature estimates to determine ellipsoid axes $r_1, r_2$ [S1, unofficial].
            * **Curvature Approximation:**
                * Let $e = p - p_{parent}$.
                * Compute $p'$ by mirroring $e$ through $n_{p_{parent}}$ [S1, unofficial].
                * Compute radius $r$ of circle through $p, p_{parent}, p'$:
                    $$ r = \frac{e^T \cdot e}{2.0 \cdot (e^T \cdot n_{p_{parent}})} $$
                * Compute orthogonal edge $e_\perp$ by projecting $n_p \times e$ onto the neighborhood triangle-fan [S1, unofficial].
                * Compute radius $r_\perp$ similarly for $e_\perp$ [S1, unofficial].
                * Set ellipsoid axes $r_1, r_2$ proportional to $r$ and $r_\perp$. Constrain extremal lengths to $s_{grid}$ [S1, unofficial].
            * Define ellipse $E$ in plane orthogonal to $n_p$:
                $$ E(t) = p + R \begin{bmatrix} r_1 \cos(t) \\ r_2 \sin(t) \end{bmatrix} $$
                where $R$ rotates the local frame such that one axis points toward $p_{parent}$ [S1, unofficial].
            * Sample $t \in [0, 2\pi]$ with step $\Delta t = \pi/3$ [S1, unofficial].
            * Create child points at these positions [S1, unofficial].
        b. **Convergence:**
            * For each child point $q$:
                * **Check Boundaries:** If $q$ is on data boundary, $\lambda_3(q)=0$, or $\lambda_2(q)=\lambda_3(q)$ (partial umbilic), stop convergence for this point [S1, unofficial].
                * **Converge:** Use either Analytical Minimum Finding or Constrained Newton Iteration.
                    * *Analytical (Quadratic):* Fit quadratic trivariate polynomial $h(x,y,z) = c \cdot [1, x, y, z, x^2, xy, y^2, yz, z^2, xz]^T$ via MLS [S1, unofficial]. Restrict to line $g(t) = q + t \cdot e_3$ [S1, unofficial]. Find minimum of $(h \circ g)(t)$ analytically [S1, unofficial].
                    * *Newton:*
                        $$ x_{i+1} = x_i - \frac{\nabla f_p}{\|\nabla f_p\|} ((H_f \cdot \nabla f_p) \cdot \nabla f_p) $$
                        where $\nabla f_p$ is projected to the plane of convergence [S1, unofficial].
                * **Convergence Check:** Point converged if:
                    $$ \frac{\nabla f^T \cdot e_3}{\|\nabla f\|} < \epsilon $$
                * **Divergence Handling:** If point diverges or leaves domain, reinitialize to midpoint between $q$ and $p_{parent}$ [S1, unofficial]. Restart iteration with finite-difference normal [S1, unofficial]. Repeat until convergence or distance to parent < minimum [S1, unofficial].
        c. **Merging:**
            * If distance from converged point $q$ to closest existing point $q_{exist}$ is $< \theta$:
                * Merge $q$ into $q_{exist}$.
                * Abandon $q$. Connect $p_{parent}$ to $q_{exist}$'s parent if needed to maintain graph connectivity [S1, unofficial].
        d. **Normal Update:**
            * After all children of $p$ converge, update $n_p$ based on child positions to reflect the mesh normal rather than just the curvature direction [S1, unofficial].

4. **Restart:**
    * If disconnected structures exist, restart the algorithm from step 2 with a new initial point [S1, unofficial].

5. **Non-Manifold Handling (Branching):**
    * At partial umbilic points ($\lambda_2 \approx \lambda_3$) or valley lines ($e_2 > 0, \nabla f \perp e_2$):
        * Restrict Newton iteration to the plane orthogonal to minimal curvature direction $e_1$ [S1, unofficial].
        * If $\lambda_2 \gg 0$ near degenerate lines, extract connector structure by replacing $\lambda_3$ in the initialization equation with $\lambda_2$ to merge crease lines with degenerate lines [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Valley Surface Extraction via Constrained Gradient Descent](10406-valley-surface-extraction-via-constrained-gradient-descent.md)
A method to extract valley surfaces in complex scalar fields by identifying branching locations (partial umbilics) and connecting them using constrained gradient-based surface integration, specifically handling non-manifold regions where standard surface growing fails.

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Higher than grid-based methods due to repeated field evaluations during point convergence [S1, unofficial]. Tracing accurate slope lines increases computation times by a factor of ~5 [S1, unofficial].
* **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the number of surface points [S1, unofficial]. The method maintains a parent-child graph and local polynomial coefficients for each point [S1, unofficial].
* **Measured Performance:** On a 2.16 GHz Intel Core 2 Duo with 4 GB RAM, for an L-shape 137Hz high-res dataset:
    * Analytical quadratic minimum finding: 19s for 2000 points, 25s for 2900 points [S1, unofficial].
    * Constrained Newton iteration: 25s for 2000 points, 31s for 2900 points [S1, unofficial].
    * Low resolution meshes extract faster but may incorrectly merge structures [S1, unofficial].
* **Strengths:** Mesh-free, curvature-adaptive, handles non-manifold structures and branching naturally, decouples extraction from triangulation, suitable for arbitrary neighborhood structures (scattered data) [S1, unofficial].
* **Limitations:** Computation times are generally higher than grid-based methods due to repeated field evaluation; sensitive to noise (reduces surface quality due to inaccurate curvature/normal computations); low resolution can lead to incorrect merging of distinct structures [S1, unofficial].
* **When to use:** Complex-valued scalar fields (e.g., acoustic pressure), scattered data, when non-manifold branching is expected, when mesh-free representation is preferred [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of visualizing complex-valued scalar fields from low-frequency acoustics FEM simulations, specifically to visualize wave nodes and anti-nodes [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of maximal convexity ridges, which classify extrema, crease lines, and crease surfaces based on local curvature properties ($\lambda_{k+1} > 0$ and $\nabla f \cdot e_i = 0$ for $i > k$) [S1, unofficial]. Unlike traditional iso-surface methods (e.g., Marching Cubes) which fail due to lack of sign changes, this approach relies on local properties only, avoiding global extrema detection and streamline computation required by watershed or slope line segmentation [S1, unofficial]. The specific implementation details, including the seeding and convergence steps, are detailed in [S1, unofficial].
