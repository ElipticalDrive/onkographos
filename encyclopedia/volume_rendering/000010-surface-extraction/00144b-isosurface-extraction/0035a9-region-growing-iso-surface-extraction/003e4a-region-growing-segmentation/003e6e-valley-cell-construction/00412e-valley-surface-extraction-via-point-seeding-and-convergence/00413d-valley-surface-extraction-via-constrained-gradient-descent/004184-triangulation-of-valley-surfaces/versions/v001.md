# Triangulation of Valley Surfaces

Also called: Delaunay meshing on valley surfaces, Ribbon triangulation

## Overview
This method converts the point cloud representing a connected surface graph, generated by constrained gradient-based valley surface extraction, into a watertight triangular mesh. It distinguishes itself by handling non-manifold regions bounded by crease and degenerate lines separately from manifold parts, ensuring topological correctness in complex scalar fields where standard surface growing fails [S1, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A point cloud representing a connected surface graph. Each point stores 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and a list of neighboring points in the graph [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A triangular mesh consisting of manifold regions and non-manifold ribbons [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Point:** Contains position vector $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a list of neighbor indices.
*   **Edge:** Defined by two point indices.
*   **Triangle:** Defined by three point indices.
*   **Crease Line:** A sequence of points identified as part of a crease.
*   **Degenerate Line:** A sequence of points identified as part of a degenerate line.
*   **Ribbon:** A pair of ordered point lists (crease line and opposing degenerate line) defining a non-manifold strip [S1, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Manifold Triangulation:**
    *   For each point in the surface graph, identify its neighbors.
    *   Construct local Delaunay triangles for manifold regions using standard Delaunay meshing techniques [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform edge swapping to optimize triangle quality, with the constraint that edges belonging to crease lines must remain as edges in the triangulation [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Identification of Non-Manifold Regions:**
    *   Identify crease points and degenerate points within the surface graph.
    *   For each crease point, find its nearest neighbor in the surface graph that lies on the edge of a triangulated manifold part; this neighbor approximates a point on a degenerate line [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Ribbon Construction:**
    *   Initialize two empty point lists: $L_{crease}$ and $L_{deg}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Starting from a crease point $p_c$ and its opposing degenerate neighbor $p_d$:
        *   Traverse the crease line in both forward and backward directions. Add each visited point to $L_{crease}$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Traverse the opposing degenerate line in both forward and backward directions. Add each visited point to $L_{deg}$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   For each point added to a line, place its neighboring surface points (not on the line) into the appropriate list corresponding to the crease or degenerate line, maintaining the ribbon structure [S1, unofficial].
    *   The pair $(L_{crease}, L_{deg})$ defines a ribbon bounding a non-manifold region [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Ribbon Triangulation:**
    *   Triangulate the ribbon bounded by $L_{crease}$ and $L_{deg}$ using standard ribbon-triangulation techniques [S1, unofficial].
    *   Alternately step along the two line sequences to create triangles.
    *   Apply a quality criterion, such as minimizing triangle edge length, to determine the optimal triangulation configuration [S1, unofficial].
    *   Stop triangle creation when the ribbon ends or when it meets its front in the case of a cyclic ribbon [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Quality Criterion:** Minimal triangle edge length is used to guide ribbon triangulation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Edge Swapping Constraint:** Crease lines must remain as edges in the triangulation [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Cyclic Ribbons:** Triangle creation must stop when the ribbon meets its front [S1, unofficial].
*   **Non-Manifold Regions:** Standard Delaunay triangulation fails in non-manifold regions; these must be handled via ribbon triangulation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Finding the nearest neighbor for degenerate line approximation may be sensitive to point cloud density [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Manifold Triangulation:** Local Delaunay triangulation is typically $O(k \log k)$ per point, where $k$ is the number of neighbors.
*   **Ribbon Triangulation:** Linear in the number of points in the ribbon, $O(n)$, where $n$ is the length of the line sequences.
*   **Memory:** Proportional to the number of points and edges in the surface graph.

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:** Correctly handles non-manifold regions in complex scalar fields, preserving topological features like crease and degenerate lines [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Relies on the accuracy of the preceding valley surface extraction algorithm. The approximation of degenerate lines via nearest neighbors may introduce artifacts if the point cloud is sparse [S1, unofficial].

### When to Use
*   Use when extracting valley surfaces in complex scalar fields where non-manifold regions are present, such as in simulated acoustics for visualizing node regions in standing waves [S1, unofficial].
