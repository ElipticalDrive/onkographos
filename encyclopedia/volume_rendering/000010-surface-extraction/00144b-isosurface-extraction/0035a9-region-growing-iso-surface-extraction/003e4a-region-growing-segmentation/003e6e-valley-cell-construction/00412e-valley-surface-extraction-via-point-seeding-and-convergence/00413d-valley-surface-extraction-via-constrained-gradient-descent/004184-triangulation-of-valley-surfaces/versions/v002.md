# Triangulation of Valley Surfaces

Also called: Delaunay meshing on valley surfaces, Ribbon triangulation

## Overview
This method triangulates the point cloud representing a connected surface graph generated by constrained gradient-based valley surface extraction [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself by handling non-manifold regions bounded by crease and degenerate lines separately from manifold parts, ensuring topological correctness in complex scalar fields where standard surface growing fails [S1, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A point cloud representing a connected surface graph with neighborhood relations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A triangular mesh covering manifold regions and non-manifold ribbons [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Point Cloud:** Contains 3D coordinates and connectivity information for the surface graph [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ribbon Lists:** Two ordered point lists defining a crease line and its opposing degenerate line [S1, unofficial].

### Steps

1.  **Manifold Triangulation:**
    *   Create local Delaunay triangles on manifold surface parts using standard Delaunay meshing techniques [S1, unofficial].
    *   Perform edge swapping to optimize triangle quality, constrained to ensure crease lines remain as edges in the triangulation [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Identification of Non-Manifold Regions:**
    *   Identify opposing crease- and degenerate lines by starting with a crease point and finding its nearest neighbor in the surface graph on the edge of a triangulated part, which approximates a point on a degenerate line [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Ribbon Construction:**
    *   Create two point lists by moving in forward and backward directions along the crease and opposing degenerate line, placing neighboring surface points into the appropriate list for the crease or its opposing line [S1, unofficial].
    *   The pair of lists defines a ribbon bounding the non-manifold region [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Ribbon Triangulation:**
    *   Triangulate the ribbon bound by the two line sequences using standard ribbon-triangulation techniques by alternately stepping along the lines and creating triangles based on a quality criterion such as minimal triangle edge length [S1, unofficial].
    *   Stop triangle creation when the ribbon ends or meets its front in case of a cyclic ribbon [S1, unofficial].

                # Step 4: Ribbon Triangulation
                ribbon_triangles = triangulate_ribbon(crease_line, degenerate_line)
                triangles.extend(ribbon_triangles)

    return triangles

    min_dist = float('inf')
    nearest = None
    for neighbor in crease_point.neighbors:
        if neighbor.is_degenerate:

### Parameters
*   **Quality Criterion:** Minimal triangle edge length is used to guide ribbon triangulation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Edge Swapping Constraint:** Crease lines must remain as edges in the triangulation [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Cyclic Ribbons:** Triangle creation must stop when the ribbon meets its front [S1, unofficial].
*   **Non-Manifold Regions:** Standard Delaunay triangulation fails in non-manifold regions; these must be handled via ribbon triangulation [S1, unofficial].
