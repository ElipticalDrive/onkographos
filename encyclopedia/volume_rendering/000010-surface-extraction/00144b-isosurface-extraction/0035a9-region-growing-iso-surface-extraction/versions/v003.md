# Region-growing Iso-surface Extraction

Also called: Normal consistency constrained marching triangles, Novel region-growing algorithm, Region-growing based iso-surface extraction algorithm

## Overview
Region-growing Iso-surface Extraction is a method that generates high-quality, curvature-adaptive semi-regular meshes while preserving sharp features and extracting all disjoint components of an iso-surface [S1]. It improves upon the original Marching Triangles algorithm by introducing a normal consistency constraint that ensures the intersection of the Delaunay sphere of a new triangle with the iso-surface is a topological disk, thereby providing robustness for complex topologies [S1]. Unlike standard marching cubes which processes cells independently, this method builds the mesh iteratively from seed triangles, ensuring global connectivity and adaptivity to local surface curvature [S1].

## How it works
The algorithm operates on a discretized volume domain. It constructs the iso-surface by growing a mesh front from initialized seeds, using boundary particles to define the surface geometry.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** A volumetric dataset (scalar field) defining the iso-surface; an iso-value threshold.
*   **Output:** A triangular mesh representing the iso-surface, consisting of vertices, edges, and faces, with connectivity information.

### Data Structures
The algorithm relies on the following structures [S1]:
1.  **Boundary Particles:** Points sampled on the iso-surface. Each particle stores its 3D position, normal vector, and classification status (e.g., visited, unvisited, feature).
2.  **Growth Queue:** A priority queue storing candidate triangles or edges for expansion. Prioritization is based on curvature or feature importance.
3.  **Visited Set:** A set or hash map tracking particles or triangles that have already been incorporated into the mesh to prevent duplicates and ensure manifold connectivity.
4.  **Delaunay Triangulation Data:** Structures to maintain the local triangulation of the growing front, allowing for Delaunay sphere intersection checks.

### Algorithm Steps
The pipeline consists of five main steps [S1]:

1.  **Boundary Particle Sampling and Classification:**
    *   Sample points on the iso-surface from the volume data.
    *   Classify each particle based on local geometric properties (e.g., curvature, gradient magnitude) to identify potential feature points or smooth regions.

2.  **Feature Position Extraction:**
    *   Identify and mark sharp features or high-curvature regions. These positions are critical for preserving geometric details in the final mesh [S1].

3.  **Prioritized Seed Initialization:**
    *   Select initial seed triangles from the classified particles.
    *   Seeds are prioritized based on feature importance or curvature, ensuring that critical parts of the surface are extracted first [S1].

4.  **Front Propagation:**
    *   This is the core iterative loop. While the growth queue is not empty:
        *   Extract the highest-priority candidate triangle from the queue.
        *   **Normal Consistency Check:** Verify that the new triangle satisfies the normal consistency constraint. This constraint ensures that the intersection of the Delaunay sphere of the new triangle with the iso-surface is a topological disk [S1]. This check replaces or augments the simple Delaunay face property check used in the original Marching Triangles algorithm, preventing topological errors in complex geometries [S1].
        *   If the check passes, add the triangle to the mesh.
        *   Update the boundary particles: mark newly covered particles as visited and add new candidate triangles/edges to the growth queue.
        *   Prioritize new candidates based on local curvature or feature strength.

5.  **Visited Boundary Particle Identification:**
    *   After propagation, identify any remaining unvisited boundary particles that belong to the iso-surface but were not reached.
    *   If disjoint components exist, initialize new seeds from these particles and repeat the propagation process until all components are extracted [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
def region_growing_iso_surface_extraction(volume, iso_value):
    # Step 1: Boundary particle sampling and classification
    particles = sample_boundary_particles(volume, iso_value)
    classify_particles(particles)

    # Step 2: Feature position extraction
    feature_particles = extract_feature_positions(particles)

    # Step 3: Prioritized seed initialization
    seed_queue = PriorityQueue()
    for particle in feature_particles:
        seed_queue.push(particle, priority=particle.curvature)
    
    # Initialize seeds from the highest priority particles
    seeds = seed_queue.extract_top_k(k=initial_seed_count)
    mesh = Mesh()
    visited_particles = Set()
    
    for seed in seeds:
        triangle = create_initial_triangle(seed)
        if is_valid_triangle(triangle, particles):
            mesh.add_triangle(triangle)
            visited_particles.add(triangle.vertices)
            add_neighbors_to_queue(triangle, particles, seed_queue)

    # Step 4: Front propagation
    while not seed_queue.is_empty():
        candidate = seed_queue.pop()
        
        # Step 4a: Normal Consistency Check
        # Ensure Delaunay sphere intersection with iso-surface is a topological disk
        if not check_normal_consistency(candidate, particles, visited_particles):
            continue
            
        triangle = create_triangle_from_candidate(candidate)
        mesh.add_triangle(triangle)
        visited_particles.add(triangle.vertices)
        
        # Update queue with new neighbors
        new_candidates = get_boundary_neighbors(triangle, particles)
        for new_cand in new_candidates:
            if new_cand not in visited_particles:
                seed_queue.push(new_cand, priority=new_cand.curvature)

    # Step 5: Visited boundary particle identification
    # Check for disjoint components
    unvisited_particles = particles - visited_particles
    for particle in unvisited_particles:
        if particle.is_on_iso_surface():
            new_seed = create_seed_from_particle(particle)
            seed_queue.push(new_seed, priority=new_seed.curvature)
            
            # Re-initialize propagation for new component
            while not seed_queue.is_empty():
                candidate = seed_queue.pop()
                if check_normal_consistency(candidate, particles, visited_particles):
                    triangle = create_triangle_from_candidate(candidate)
                    mesh.add_triangle(triangle)
                    visited_particles.add(triangle.vertices)
                    new_candidates = get_boundary_neighbors(triangle, particles)
                    for new_cand in new_candidates:
                        if new_cand not in visited_particles:
                            seed_queue.push(new_cand, priority=new_cand.curvature)
                            
    return mesh
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Iso-value:** The scalar threshold defining the surface.
*   **Curvature Threshold:** Determines the priority of seeds and the adaptivity of the mesh.
*   **Normal Consistency Threshold:** A tolerance for the normal consistency check to handle numerical precision issues [S1].
*   **Edge Cases:**
    *   **Disjoint Components:** The algorithm explicitly handles multiple disconnected iso-surface components by re-initializing seeds from unvisited particles [S1].
    *   **Sharp Features:** Feature extraction ensures that high-curvature regions are not oversmoothed [S1].
    *   **Complex Topology:** The normal consistency constraint prevents topological errors (e.g., self-intersections, non-manifold edges) that can occur with standard Delaunay checks in complex geometries [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The complexity is dominated by the boundary particle sampling and the front propagation loop. The number of iterations depends on the surface area and the density of the mesh. The normal consistency check adds overhead per triangle but ensures robustness [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for boundary particles, the growth queue, and the resulting mesh. The memory usage scales with the surface area and the complexity of the topology [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Generates high-quality, curvature-adaptive semi-regular meshes; preserves sharp features; robust for complex topologies due to the normal consistency constraint; extracts all disjoint components [S1].
*   **Limitations:** More complex to implement than standard Marching Cubes; requires careful tuning of curvature thresholds and seed initialization; potentially slower due to the additional consistency checks and prioritization [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Suitable for applications requiring high-quality, adaptive meshes from complex volumetric data, such as medical imaging or geometric modeling, where feature preservation and topological robustness are critical [S1].

## Background
The algorithm was proposed by Jian Xi and Ye Duan in 2008 [S1]. It builds upon the original Marching Triangles algorithm, which generates evenly-sized and quasi-equilateral meshes by iteratively joining new triangles to the current region boundary [S1]. The original Marching Triangles algorithm did not adapt well to local surface properties, requiring large numbers of small triangles for surfaces with large curvature variations [S1]. This new algorithm addresses these limitations by introducing curvature-adaptive mesh generation and the novel normal consistency constraint [S1].
