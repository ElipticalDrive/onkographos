# STEVE Hypersurface Generation

Also called: STEVE, STEVE algorithm, Space-Time-Enclosing Volume Extraction

## Overview
STEVE (Space-Time-Enclosing Volume Extraction) is an algorithm for extracting iso-valued hypersurfaces from 4D data sets (e.g., temporal sequences of 3D images), guaranteeing that the resulting hypersurface is free from accidental rifts and fully encloses a region in 4D space [S1, unofficial]. It generates simplex-based manifolds of codimension 1, specifically consisting purely of 3-simplices (tetrahedrons) embedded in 4D space [S1, unofficial]. Unlike methods that extrude 4D data to 5D to construct iso-hypersurfaces, STEVE works directly in 4D to ensure codimension 1 manifolds [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the VESTA framework, using a similar flowchart for mesh features, data segmentation, and ambiguity handling, but extends the vector path logic to four dimensions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A homogeneous, N-cubical (Cartesian) grid in 4D ($x, y, z, t$). Data specifies overall dimensions (bounds and maximum numbers of toxels in $x, y, z, t$ directions) and individual $x, y, z, t$ positions with associated field values for each toxel center [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A set of oriented 3-simplices (tetrahedrons) representing the iso-hypersurface [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Toxel:** A 4D picture element (time-varying voxel) [S1, unofficial].
*   **4D-Cell:** A $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$ neighborhood of toxels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Indexing Scheme:**
    *   **Toxel Site IDs:** 16 IDs (0–15). IDs 0–7 are "past" (time $t$), IDs 8–15 are "future" (time $t+1$) [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Boundary Cube Centers (Support Points):** 32 potential points (IDs 0–31) located at the centers of the 3D faces of the 4D-cell [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Junctures:** 24 potential points of ambiguity (POA) with IDs > 31 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Vector Path Table (Table 2):** A lookup table with 32 rows (Center IDs 0–31) and 18 columns. Columns are grouped by orientation ($\oplus$ or $\ominus$) and path index (1, 2, 3). Each cell contains a directed path from a start Center ID to an end Center ID (e.g., '33 → 0') [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Volume Octant (BVO):** Each of the 16 toxels contributes 4 BVOs based on the orientation of the range vector (positive/negative $x, y, z, t$). Each BVO is represented by a triplet of Toxel Cube Vector (TCV) pairs [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Iso-value:** A scalar threshold. Toxels with field value $\ge$ iso-value are marked active (for enclosure); others are inactive [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpolation:** Linear interpolation is used to reposition support points within bounds defined by range vectors. Higher-dimensional B-Splines are possible [S1, unofficial].
*   **Connectivity Modes:** 'Mixed' (local average-based), global 'disconnect', or global 'connect' [S1, unofficial].
*   **Resolution:** High Resolution (HR) or Low Resolution (LR) decomposition [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Read Input:** Read overall dimensions and individual $x, y, z, t$ positions with field values [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Segment Data:** Compare each toxel's field value to the iso-value. Mark active/inactive toxels [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Identify Transitions:** For each $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$ 4D-cell, identify active-to-inactive transitions. Define a range vector starting at the active toxel center and ending at the inactive neighbor center [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Index 4D-Cell:** Label the 16 toxel site IDs (0–15), 32 boundary cube centers (0–31), and 24 junctures (>31) [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Identify BVOs:** For each of the 16 toxels in the 4D-cell, identify 4 BVOs based on the range vector orientation ($\oplus/\ominus$ in $x, y, z, t$) [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Lookup Vector Paths:** Use the Vector Path Table (Table 2) to determine the 192 possible TCV pairs within the 4D-cell. Map Center IDs and orientations to three directed paths (Path 1, 2, 3) connecting boundary cube centers [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Generate Protomesh:** Concatenate the TCVs to form initial cyclic vector paths.
    *   *Isochronous Segment:* If all 8 "past" toxels are active and all 8 "future" are inactive, generate 6 initial cyclic vector paths from 8 BVOs [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Triangular Strut:* If two BVOs have opposite orientations (e.g., $18\oplus$ and $18\ominus$), their TCVs cancel, resulting in no contribution. Apply indexing and table to yield 5 initial cyclic vector paths [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Resolve Ambiguities:** If a juncture is a Point of Ambiguity (POA) (paths start/end at the same juncture, e.g., juncture 47), use the connectivity diagram to resolve connections.
    *   *Mixed Mode:* Assign the average field value of toxels at positions I, II, III, IV to the POA. If average < iso-value, use 'disconnect' mode; if average > iso-value, use 'connect' mode [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Global Modes:* Enforce global 'disconnect' or 'connect' [S1, unofficial].
9.  **Discard Junctures:** Remove all junctures (point IDs > 31) from the protomesh. This reduces cyclic paths to final tetrahedrons. Each final cyclic vector path represents a triangle embedded in 4D [S1, unofficial].
10. **Inherit Orientations:** Inherit orientations of final vector paths from initial ones ($\oplus$ or $\ominus$) after removal of junctures to ensure consistent 4-normal vector evaluation [S1, unofficial].
11. **Decompose Segments:**
    *   *HR Decomposition:*
        1.  Mark the center of mass point (face center) for each 4-cycle [S1, unofficial].
        2.  Decompose surface cycles into triangles by connecting face centers with corresponding 4-cycle support points [S1, unofficial].
        3.  Collect triangles enclosing a volume, determine the absolute center of the enclosed volume [S1, unofficial].
        4.  Connect face centers with the absolute volume center [S1, unofficial].
        5.  Connect all 4-cycle support points with the absolute volume center to yield 24 properly oriented tetrahedrons [S1, unofficial].
    *   *LR Decomposition:* Replace steps of marking face centers and connecting them with N-cycle decompositions without additional center of mass points. Omit the step connecting face centers to volume center. Yields 12 tetrahedrons [S1, unofficial].
12. **Repeat for Isolated Toxels:** For a single isolated active toxel, process the remaining 15 4D-cells to generate a total of 16 oriented tetrahedrons. The union of these tetrahedrons forms the final STEVE hypersurface for that toxel [S1, unofficial].
13. **Store Output:** Store the generated 3-simplices (tetrahedrons) [S1, unofficial].

        elif connectivity_mode == 'connect':

            # Standard tetrahedron generation
            # 4 final cyclic vector paths -> 4 triangles -> 1 tetrahedron

        tetrahedrons.extend(tetras)
        
    return tetrahedrons

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given, but STEVE uses lesser computational resources than other techniques that generate simplex-based manifolds of codimension 1 [S1, unofficial]. It processes each $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$ 4D-cell independently, similar to VESTA.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not explicitly given. Requires storage for the 4D grid, toxel values, and the resulting tetrahedrons [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Guarantees rift-free, volume-enclosing hypersurfaces in 4D. Works directly in 4D without extrusion to 5D. Handles topological ambiguities explicitly.
*   **Limitations:** Topological ambiguities in 4D (toxels sharing only one common face) require resolution. Using fewer than 12 or 24 tetrahedrons for decomposition introduces stronger directional dependencies [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When extracting iso-hypersurfaces from 4D space-time data (e.g., time-varying CT) where volume enclosure and manifold correctness are critical.
*   **When not to use:** When 3D data is sufficient (use VESTA). When computational resources are extremely limited and approximate results are acceptable.

## Background
STEVE was introduced by B. R. Schlei. A first announcement was made in Ref. [10]. The algorithm is described for the first time in this paper, subject to a pending patent application [1] [S1, unofficial]. It is designed for 4D data sets such as temporal sequences of 3D images from time-varying computed tomography, 3+1D fireball simulations in heavy-ion physics, and ion beam transport simulations [S1, unofficial]. It extends the VESTA algorithm from 3D to 4D, using a similar flowchart and indexing scheme but with 192 vector paths (8 times the 24 of VESTA) to handle the additional dimension [S1, unofficial].
