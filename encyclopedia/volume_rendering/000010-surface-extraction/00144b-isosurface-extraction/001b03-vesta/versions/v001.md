# VESTA
Also called: Marching VESTA, Original VESTA

## Overview
VESTA (Volume-Enclosing Surface exTraction Algorithm) is a surface extraction method that constructs isosurfaces by generating N-cycles from 3-cell configurations, resolving ambiguities at voxel face junctures to prevent surface holes, unlike the original Marching Cubes Algorithm [S1, unofficial]. It is a volume-enclosing surface extraction algorithm that processes voxel face vectors (VFVs) to generate a closed, oriented surface composed of triangles (3-cycles). It handles both isolated voxels and complex connected shapes by establishing connectivity through boundary face centers and junctures [S1, unofficial]. Unlike template-based methods like Marching Cubes, VESTA uses a single building block based on vector decomposition of a single voxel face to resolve 3D cell ambiguities without generating holes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A 3D voxel grid where each voxel has a scalar value (e.g., gray level, temperature). An isovalue $T_f$ (or threshold) defines "active" voxels (value $\ge T_f$) and "inactive" voxels (value $< T_f$).
- **Output:** A triangular mesh (collection of 3-cycles) representing the isosurface. The surface is non-degenerate, closed, and volume-enclosing.

**Data Structures:**
- **Voxel Grid:** 3D array of scalar values.
- **Boundary Face:** A square face shared between an active voxel and an inactive neighbor.
- **Support Point:** The center of a boundary face.
- **Juncture:** The midpoint of an edge of a boundary face (shared by two adjacent boundary faces).
- **Voxel Face Vector (VFV):** A directed vector from a support point (face center) to a juncture (edge midpoint).
- **VESTA Cycle Vector:** A directed vector connecting two support points, representing a segment of the surface cycle.
- **N-Cycle:** A closed loop of VESTA cycle vectors.

**Numbering and Coordinates (Marching 3-Cell):**
- A marching 3-cell is a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ block of voxels.
- Voxel indices $0$ to $7$. Voxel 0 is at $(i, j, k)$.
- Coordinate system $x|y|z$ coincides with range vectors of voxel 0.
- Support points (face centers) and junctures (edge midpoints) are numbered within the 3-cell context.

**Steps:**

1.  **Identify Active Voxels:** Select all voxels with scalar value $\ge T_f$.
2.  **Identify Boundary Faces:** For each active voxel, check its 6 nearest neighbors. If a neighbor is inactive, record the shared face as a boundary face.
3.  **Define Support Points and Junctures:**
    - The center of each recorded boundary face is a support point $S$.
    - The midpoints of the edges of the boundary face are junctures $J$.
4.  **Construct Voxel Face Vectors (VFVs):**
    - For each boundary face, define 4 VFVs connecting the face center $S$ to the 4 junctures $J$ in counterclockwise order relative to the outward normal (or range vector).
    - Let a VFV be $V = J - S$.
5.  **Connect VFVs into Paths:**
    - At each juncture $J$, connect the tip of an incoming VFV from one boundary face to the origin of an outgoing VFV from an adjacent boundary face.
    - Constraint: Only attach the origin of a given VFV to another one's tip.
    - This forms oriented paths between support points.
6.  **Resolve Points of Ambiguity (POAs):**
    - A POA occurs at a juncture where 4 incoming and 4 outgoing VFVs meet (typically where two active voxels share only a single edge).
    - At a POA, choose a successor VFV for each incoming VFV. Two modes exist:
        - **Disconnect Mode:** Select the successor VFV belonging to the *same* voxel as the incoming VFV.
        - **Connect Mode:** Select the successor VFV belonging to the *other* voxel.
    - This choice must be consistent for all POAs in the dataset (globally or locally based on gray-level thresholds).
    - Replace connected VFV pairs with single **VESTA cycle vectors** connecting support points.
7.  **Form Closed Cycles (N-Cycles):**
    - Group VESTA cycle vectors into closed loops (N-cycles).
    - All N-cycles are confined to a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ voxel neighborhood.
    - Valid N-cycle lengths: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12. (Lengths 10, 11 do not exist).
8.  **Decompose N-Cycles into Triangles (3-Cycles):**
    - **Low Resolution (L):** For N-cycles with $N > 3$, insert antiparallel vector pairs to break the cycle into 3-cycles, preserving orientation. No new points are added.
    - **High Resolution (H):** For N-cycles with $N > 3$, introduce an additional point $P_{avg}$ within the cycle. $P_{avg}$ is the average (center of mass) of all support points involved in the N-cycle. Connect $P_{avg}$ to the vertices of the N-cycle to form triangles.
    - If boundary face centers are displaced (see below), recalculate $P_{avg}$ after displacement.
9.  **Displace Support Points (Isosurface Construction):**
    - For isosurface extraction, displace each support point $S$ along its **range vector** $r$.
    - The range vector $r$ originates at the center of the active voxel and ends at the center of the inactive neighbor.
    - The displacement is determined by linear interpolation of the field values at the two voxel centers. Let $V_a$ be the active voxel value and $V_b$ be the inactive voxel value. The intersection parameter $t$ along the edge connecting the voxel centers is $t = (T_f - V_a) / (V_b - V_a)$. The new support point position is $S' = S_{original} + t \cdot r$.
    - Recalculate average points $P_{avg}$ if using High Resolution mode.
10. **Discard Unphysical Triangles:**
    - A triangle is unphysical if at least one of its corner points has a radial and/or temporal value smaller than zero (context-dependent, e.g., in relativistic hydrodynamics) [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def vesta_extract(voxel_grid, isovalue, connectivity_mode='disconnect', resolution_mode='L'):
    """
    voxel_grid: 3D array of scalar values
    isovalue: T_f
    connectivity_mode: 'disconnect', 'connect', or 'mixed'
    resolution_mode: 'L' (low) or 'H' (high)
    """
    active_voxels = []
    boundary_faces = [] # List of (active_voxel_idx, inactive_neighbor_idx, face_id)
    
    # Step 1 & 2: Identify active voxels and boundary faces
    for each voxel v in voxel_grid:
        if v.value >= isovalue:
            active_voxels.append(v)
            for each neighbor n of v:
                if n.value < isovalue:
                    boundary_faces.append((v, n, get_face_id(v, n)))
    
    # Step 3 & 4: Define support points and VFVs
    support_points = {} # Map from face_id to point (initially center)
    vfvs = [] # List of VFVs
    
    for (active_v, inactive_v, face_id) in boundary_faces:
        center = get_face_center(active_v, inactive_v)
        support_points[face_id] = center
        
        # Define 4 junctures (edge midpoints) for this face
        junctures = get_face_edge_midpoints(active_v, inactive_v)
        
        # Define 4 VFVs from center to junctures in CCW order
        for j in junctures:
            vfvs.append(VFV(origin=center, tip=j, face_id=face_id, voxel=active_v))
            
    # Step 5 & 6: Connect VFVs and resolve POAs
    # Build adjacency map from juncture to VFVs
    juncture_to_vfvs = {}
    for v in vfvs:
        if v.tip not in juncture_to_vfvs:
            juncture_to_vfvs[v.tip] = []
        juncture_to_vfvs[v.tip].append(v)
        
    cycle_vectors = []
    visited_vfvs = set()
    
    for j, incoming_vfvs in juncture_to_vfvs.items():
        for incoming_v in incoming_vfvs:
            if incoming_v in visited_vfvs:
                continue
            
            # Determine successor based on connectivity mode
            # Check if this juncture is a POA (4 incoming, 4 outgoing)
            is_poa = len(incoming_vfvs) == 4 and len(juncture_to_vfvs.get(j, [])) == 4
            
            if is_poa:
                if connectivity_mode == 'mixed':
                    # Determine mode locally based on gray levels of surrounding voxels
                    mode = determine_local_mode(j, voxel_grid)
                else:
                    mode = connectivity_mode
                    
                if mode == 'disconnect':
                    successor = select_same_voxel_successor(incoming_v, juncture_to_vfvs[j])
                else: # connect
                    successor = select_other_voxel_successor(incoming_v, juncture_to_vfvs[j])
            else:
                # Trivial connection: connect to the next VFV in the same face or adjacent face
                successor = get_trivial_successor(incoming_v, juncture_to_vfvs[j])
                
            # Create VESTA cycle vector
            cycle_vec = VESTA_Cycle_Vector(origin=incoming_v.origin, tip=successor.tip)
            cycle_vectors.append(cycle_vec)
            visited_vfvs.add(incoming_v)
            visited_vfvs.add(successor)
            
    # Step 7: Form closed cycles
    cycles = form_cycles(cycle_vectors)
    
    # Step 8 & 9: Decompose and Displace
    triangles = []
    for cycle in cycles:
        if len(cycle) == 3:
            # Already a triangle
            triangles.append(cycle)
        else:
            if resolution_mode == 'L':
                # Decompose using antiparallel pairs (no new points)
                triangles.extend(decompose_l(cycle))
            else: # H
                # Calculate average point
                avg_point = sum(cycle.vertices) / len(cycle.vertices)
                # Displace support points along range vectors
                displaced_cycle = displace_cycle(cycle, voxel_grid, isovalue)
                displaced_avg = displace_point(avg_point, voxel_grid, isovalue)
                # Connect displaced_avg to displaced_cycle vertices
                triangles.extend(decompose_h(displaced_cycle, displaced_avg))
                
    # Step 10: Discard unphysical triangles
    valid_triangles = [t for t in triangles if is_physical(t, voxel_grid)]
    
    return valid_triangles
```

**Parameters:**
- **Isovalue ($T_f$):** Threshold for active voxels. Typical value in examples: 139 MeV (for relativistic hydrodynamics) [S1, unofficial].
- **Connectivity Mode:** 'disconnect', 'connect', or 'mixed'. Determines behavior at POAs [S1, unofficial].
- **Resolution Mode:** 'L' (Low, no extra points) or 'H' (High, uses average points) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Points of Ambiguity (POAs):** Occur when two active voxels share only a single edge. Consistent global or local selection is required to prevent holes [S1, unofficial].
- **Corner Contact:** VESTA does not connect voxels sharing only a single point (corner contact) by default. This results in separate surfaces [S1, unofficial].
- **Redundancy:** Original VESTA minimizes redundant support points compared to MCA [S1, unofficial].
- **Precision:** In High Resolution mode, average points should be calculated *after* displacing support points to save computing time and ensure accuracy [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### DICONEX
DICONEX is a 2D algorithm for constructing gap-free contours from binary image data, serving as the 2D analog for VESTA. It collects initial contour vectors (ICVs) separating active and inactive pixels, connects them into closed cycles, and resolves Points of Ambiguity (POAs) where pixels touch at a single point. At a POA, 'disconnect' mode performs a left turn (same pixel), and 'connect' mode performs a right turn (different pixel). It displaces ICV origins to pixel edge midpoints to form final contours [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given in sources, but benchmarks show it is comparable to or faster than MCA for certain datasets. Original VESTA executes faster than marching VESTA and MCA for specific datasets due to fewer support points, though it may require more computing time in some benchmarks due to algorithmic complexity [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Original VESTA processes the entire 3D dataset at once, requiring large memory. Marching VESTA processes $2 \times 2 \times 2$ neighborhoods, reducing memory usage [S1, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:**
    - Benchmark 1 (33 images, 241x81 pixels, 8,000 active voxels): VESTA (original/connect/mixed) produced 3,326 points and 6,406 triangles in ~0.124s. Extended MCA produced 19,218 points in 0.096s [S1, unofficial].
    - Benchmark 2 (200 x-ray microtomography images, 256x256 pixels, 512,603 active voxels, isovalue 135): Original VESTA L: Time 5.34s, Points 932,739, Triangles 1,855,666. MCA L: Time 4.71s, Points 5,566,998, Triangles 1,855,666 [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Generates non-degenerate, closed, volume-enclosing surfaces with no holes and no self-intersection. Resolves 3D cell ambiguities correctly. Not template-based. Reproduces 2D DICONEX contours when intersected with principal planes.
- **Limitations:** Does not connect voxels sharing only a single point (corner contact) by default. Requires consistent handling of POAs. Original version requires significant memory for large datasets.
- **When to use:** When a hole-free, volume-enclosing surface is critical, and template-based ambiguities are a concern. Suitable for scientific visualization of voxel data (e.g., hydrodynamics, tomography).
- **When not to use:** When corner connectivity is required without custom templates. When memory is extremely constrained and marching VESTA's overhead is unacceptable.

## Background
VESTA was introduced in the paper "Volume-Enclosing Surface Extraction" [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the principles of the DICONEX algorithm for 2D data. The method was developed to address the limitations of the original Marching Cubes Algorithm (MCA), particularly the generation of holes and the need for extensive template sets. VESTA uses a single building block (VFVs) instead of templates. It has been applied to computer tomographic scan data and simulation data of relativistic hydrodynamic fireball expansions [S1, unofficial].
