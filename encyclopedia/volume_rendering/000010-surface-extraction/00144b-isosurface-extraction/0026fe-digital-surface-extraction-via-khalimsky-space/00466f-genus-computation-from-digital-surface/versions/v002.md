# Genus Computation from Digital Surface

Also called: Genus from DS, Genus computation via neighbor counting

## Overview
This method computes the genus $g$ of a digital surface $\partial O_\alpha$ derived from a Majority Interpolation (MI) reconstruction. It solves the problem of determining the topological complexity (number of handles) of the extracted isosurface without requiring mesh traversal or global connectivity analysis. It sets itself apart from general mesh-based genus algorithms by leveraging the specific combinatorial properties of the Khalimsky space and the 2-manifold guarantee of the MI reconstruction, allowing for a direct calculation based solely on local neighbor counts of surface points [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A digital surface $\partial O_\alpha$, which is a 2-manifold without boundary, extracted from a scalar field using Majority Interpolation with threshold $\alpha$.
- The set of surface points (surfels) constituting $\partial O_\alpha$.

**Outputs:**
- The genus $g$, an integer topological invariant representing the number of handles of the surface.

**Data Structures:**
- The digital surface is represented as a set of points in the Khalimsky grid graph $G'$.
- Each point $s \in \partial O_\alpha$ maintains a count of its neighbors within the surface set.

**Steps:**
1. Identify the set of surface points $\partial O_\alpha$.
2. For each point $s \in \partial O_\alpha$, count the number of its neighbors that are also in $\partial O_\alpha$. Let this count be $k_s$.
3. Aggregate the counts into three buckets:
   - $|N_3|$: The number of surface points with exactly 3 neighbors.
   - $|N_5|$: The number of surface points with exactly 5 neighbors.
   - $|N_6|$: The number of surface points with exactly 6 neighbors.
   - Note: In the specific Khalimsky-based MI reconstruction, surface points typically have 3, 5, or 6 neighbors. Points with other neighbor counts indicate topological defects or non-manifold configurations, which are excluded from this specific formula [S1, unofficial].
4. Compute the genus using the formula:
   $$ g = 1 + \frac{|N_5| + 2|N_6| - |N_3|}{8} $$
5. Return $g$.

**Parameters:**
- $|N_i|$: The number of surface points in $\partial O_\alpha$ that have exactly $i$ neighbors.
- $g$: The surface genus.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Non-manifold configurations:** The formula assumes $\partial O_\alpha$ is a 2-manifold without boundary. If the MI reconstruction fails to produce a manifold (e.g., due to ambiguous configurations not handled by MI), points with neighbor counts other than 3, 5, or 6 may appear, leading to incorrect results.
- **Precision:** The result $g$ must be an integer. The formula is derived such that the numerator is always divisible by 8 for valid manifolds. Floating-point division should be handled carefully to ensure integer results.
- **Disconnected components:** The formula computes the genus for the entire surface. If the surface consists of multiple disconnected components, the result represents the sum of the genera of all components. To get the genus per component, one must first isolate connected components of $O_\alpha$ and apply the formula to each [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of surface points. Each point is visited once to count neighbors.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the surface points and their neighbor counts.
- **Strengths:** Extremely fast and simple to implement. Does not require mesh traversal, graph traversal, or complex topological data structures. Leverages the mathematical guarantees of the MI reconstruction.
- **Limitations:** Only computes the genus. Does not provide information about the location of topological errors. Assumes the surface is a valid 2-manifold.
- **When to use:** When a quick topological invariant check is needed for MI-reconstructed surfaces.
- **When not to use:** When detailed topological error localization is required (use Topology Verification using Digital Surfaces instead) or when the surface is not guaranteed to be a manifold.

## Background
The method was proposed by Chen and Rong [5] (cited in [S1, unofficial]) as a direct formula for computing the genus of digital surfaces derived from Majority Interpolation. The importance of the MI reconstruction $O_\alpha$ is that its boundary $\partial O_\alpha$ is guaranteed to be a 2-manifold [33, 37], which allows for the application of this specific combinatorial formula. This method is part of the broader effort to provide rigorous topological guarantees for isosurface extraction in digital volumes.
