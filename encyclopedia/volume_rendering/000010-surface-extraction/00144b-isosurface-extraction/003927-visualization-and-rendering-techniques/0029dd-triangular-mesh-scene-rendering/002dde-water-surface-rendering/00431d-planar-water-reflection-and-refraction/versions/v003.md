# Planar Water Reflection and Refraction

Also called: FT-Minecraft Water, WaterRenderer

## Overview
A technique that renders a voxel water surface by generating separate reflection and refraction textures via Framebuffer Objects (FBOs), which are then blended on a water surface shader [S1, unofficial]. It handles both planar ocean water and placed water blocks by separating them into distinct rendering buckets [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Camera position ($P_{eye}$), camera orientation (yaw $\theta$, pitch $\phi$), sea level height ($H_{sea}$), time step ($dt$), water surface geometry (vertices at $y=0$ relative to sea level, i.e., $(x, 0, y)$ in local space), and texture resources (dudv map, normal map) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Two FBO textures (reflection texture, refraction texture) and a final composited water surface image [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **FBOs:** Two Framebuffer Objects, `fbo_reflection` and `fbo_refraction`, each containing a color attachment and a depth attachment [S1, unofficial].
*   **Textures:**
    *   `tex_dudv`: 2D texture, minification filter `GL_LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR`, magnification `GL_LINEAR`, anisotropy up to 8x [S1, unofficial].
    *   `tex_normal`: 2D normal map texture [S1, unofficial].
*   **Clip Planes:** OpenGL clip plane 0 (`GL_CLIP_DISTANCE0`) [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `refractionResolutionScale`: Float, clamped $[0.1, 1.0]$. Scales refraction FBO resolution relative to display [S1, unofficial].
*   `seaLevel`: Float. Y-coordinate of the water plane [S1, unofficial].
*   `dudvTiling`: Float. Tiling frequency of the dudv texture [S1, unofficial].
*   `waterMoveFactor`, `waterMoveFactor2`: Float. Speed factors for wave animation [S1, unofficial].
*   `waveStrength`: Float. Amplitude factor for Gerstner waves [S1, unofficial].
*   `twilightLow`, `twilightHigh`: Float. Sun elevation range $[0.235, 0.315]$ for specular fade band [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Create `fbo_reflection` and `fbo_refraction` [S1, unofficial].
    *   Load `tex_dudv` and `tex_normal`. Configure `tex_dudv` with trilinear filtering and anisotropy $A = \min(8.0, A_{max})$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Initialize wave time $T = 0$ [G].

2.  **Time Update:**
    *   Advance wave time: $T \leftarrow T + dt$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Wrap $T$ to maintain float precision: if $T \ge 2\pi \times 1024$, then $T \leftarrow T \pmod{2\pi \times 1024}$ [S1, unofficial]. This preserves periodicity while preventing precision loss at large time values [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Reflection Pass:**
    *   Bind `fbo_reflection`. Clear color and depth buffers [S1, unofficial].
    *   Enable clip plane 0 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate reflected camera position $P_{ref}$:
        $$ P_{ref}.y = P_{eye}.y - 2.0 \times (P_{eye}.y - H_{sea}) $$
        $$ P_{ref}.x = P_{eye}.x $$
        $$ P_{ref}.z = P_{eye}.z $$
    *   Construct reflected view matrix $V_{ref}$:
        *   Invert camera pitch: $\phi_{ref} = -\phi$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Keep yaw: $\theta_{ref} = \theta$ [S1, unofficial].
        *   Compute front vector $F_{ref}$ from $\theta_{ref}, \phi_{ref}$ [G].
        *   $V_{ref} = \text{lookAt}(P_{ref}, P_{ref} + F_{ref}, (0, 1, 0))$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Set clip plane equation $C_0 = (0, 1, 0, -H_{sea})$. This culls geometry below the water surface [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render sky using a fixed view matrix $V_{sky} = -I$ (identity with inverted axes) and the projection matrix $P$, passing $P_{ref}$ for correct parallax [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render scene geometry using $V_{ref}$ and $P$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Refraction Pass:**
    *   Bind `fbo_refraction`. Clear color and depth buffers [S1, unofficial].
    *   Enable clip plane 0 [S1, unofficial].
    *   Set clip plane equation $C_0 = (0, -1, 0, H_{sea})$. This culls geometry above the water surface, keeping only underwater fragments [S1, unofficial].
    *   Disable shadows, SSAO, and caustics. Caustics are disabled because without shadow occlusion, they would incorrectly appear on terrain not reached by the sun [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render scene geometry using the main camera's view matrix $V_{eye}$ and projection matrix $P$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Water Surface Rendering:**
    *   Bind the water surface shader [S1, unofficial].
    *   Setup uniforms:
        *   Anchor dudv texture coordinates to prevent UV drift at large world coordinates. Snap camera position to the texture tiling period:
            $$ P_{anchor} = \text{floor}(P_{eye} / \text{period}) \times \text{period} $$
            where period is derived from `dudvTiling` [S1, unofficial].
        *   Pass Gerstner wave parameters, anchoring wave calculation to $P_{anchor}$ to maintain float precision [S1, unofficial].
    *   Render the water surface geometry (triangle fan or mesh) using the reflection and refraction textures [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Water Bucket Separation
Separates water faces into two rendering buckets based on Y-coordinate relative to sea level to optimize reflection/refraction passes [S1, unofficial]. Faces at $Y = \text{seaLevel}$ (default 65) are routed to the ocean bucket, which uses the planar reflection/refraction FBOs [S1, unofficial]. All other water faces (side faces of sea-level blocks, placed/spread water, deeper top faces) are routed to the placed bucket, which uses a sky-reflection shader independent of global planes [S1, unofficial]. Planar reflection/refraction passes only apply to ocean water; placed water does not require them [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** The method requires two full scene renders plus one water surface render [S1, unofficial]. The use of clip planes reduces the number of fragments processed in each pass [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple implementation, works well for flat water surfaces, provides realistic reflections and refractions for planar water [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Not suitable for complex, non-planar water surfaces [S1, unofficial]. Precision issues at large world coordinates require UV anchoring [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering flat, horizontal water surfaces in a voxel or low-poly environment [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** For non-planar water, or when reflections/refractions are not required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method is implemented in the `ft_minecraft` voxel engine, a from-scratch project inspired by Minecraft, built as a follow-up to the `ft_vox` project at 42 [S1, unofficial]. The engine emphasizes procedural generation and rendering quality, using OpenGL 4.6 and C++20 [S1, unofficial]. The water rendering technique is part of the engine's feature set, which includes dynamic flow, spreading, and transparent water with reflection/refraction framebuffers [S1, unofficial].
