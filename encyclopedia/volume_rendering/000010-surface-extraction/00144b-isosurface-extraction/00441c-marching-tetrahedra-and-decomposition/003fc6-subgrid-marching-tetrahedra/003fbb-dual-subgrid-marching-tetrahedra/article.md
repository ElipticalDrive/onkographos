# Dual Subgrid Marching Tetrahedra
Also called: dual subgrid method
## Overview
Dual Subgrid Marching Tetrahedra is a surface reconstruction technique that improves the geometric accuracy of the parent [Subgrid Marching Tetrahedra](1737-subgrid-marching-tetrahedra.md) method by decoupling output vertex positions from the grid edges. While primal subgrid marching restricts vertices to lie on the tetrahedral grid edges, this method allows vertices to be placed freely in 3D space by constructing a Poincaré dual mesh. This distinction is critical when a single tetrahedron contains multiple topologically distinct surface components, as it preserves sharp features and thin structures that would otherwise be lost or distorted by edge-constrained interpolation. It differs from [Dual Contouring](link) by associating vertices with primal polygons rather than grid cells, and from [Marching Tetrahedrons](3720-marching-tetrahedrons.md) by utilizing the subgrid connectivity and dual placement strategy.

## How it works
The method operates in two phases: constructing the dual mesh connectivity and computing the vertex coordinates. It assumes the input is the set of closed boundary loops $\Gamma$ generated by the primal subgrid marching tetrahedra algorithm for each tetrahedron in the grid.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input**: A set of closed boundary loops $\Gamma$. Each loop $\Gamma$ is a sequence of $k$ edge intersection points $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and their corresponding unit surface normals $N_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, where $k \ge 3$.
*   **Output**: A triangle mesh representing the isosurface. The mesh consists of dual vertices, dual edges, and dual faces.

### Data Structures
1.  **Primal Loop**: A list of tuples $(x_i, N_i)$ for $i=1 \dots k$.
2.  **Dual Connectivity Map**:
    *   `dual_vertices`: A dictionary mapping each primal polygon (identified by its loop ID) to a dual vertex index.
    *   `dual_edges`: A list of pairs of dual vertex indices.
    *   `dual_faces`: A list of triangles, where each triangle is a triplet of dual vertex indices.
3.  **Vertex Positions**: A list of 3D coordinates for each dual vertex.

### Steps

1.  **Construct Primal Loops**: Generate the closed boundary loops $\Gamma$ for each tetrahedron using the standard subgrid marching tetrahedra connectivity logic [S1]. Do not triangulate these loops yet.

2.  **Build Poincaré Dual Connectivity**:
    *   Create a dual vertex for each primal polygon (loop) generated in Step 1.
    *   Identify interior primal edges (edges shared by two adjacent primal polygons). For each interior primal edge, create a dual edge connecting the two dual vertices corresponding to the adjacent polygons.
    *   Identify interior primal vertices (vertices shared by multiple primal polygons). For each interior primal vertex, create a dual polygon whose vertices are the dual vertices associated with the primal polygons incident to that primal vertex.
    *   Triangulate each dual polygon created in the previous sub-step. This triangulation must not insert any new vertices; it must use only the existing dual vertices [S1].

3.  **Compute Dual Vertex Coordinates**:
    For each dual vertex $v$ associated with a primal polygon (loop) $\Gamma$ containing $k$ points $\{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and normals $\{N_1, \dots, N_k\}$:
    *   Calculate the mean intersection point:
        $$ \bar{x} = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i $$
    *   Solve for the vertex position $p$ that minimizes the regularized Quadratic Error Function (QEF):
        $$ Q_\lambda(p) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k \langle N_i, p - x_i \rangle^2 + \lambda \|p - \bar{x}\|^2 $$
    *   The optimal $p$ is found by solving the linear system derived from setting the gradient of $Q_\lambda(p)$ to zero [S1].

### Formulas

**Quadratic Error Function (QEF)**:
$$ \mathcal{Q}(p) := \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k \langle N_i, p - x_i \rangle^2 $$
where:
*   $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are the edge intersection points along the loop.
*   $N_i$ are the unit normals at $x_i$.
*   $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the candidate vertex position.
*   $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ denotes the dot product.

**Regularized QEF**:
$$ Q_\lambda(p) := \mathcal{Q}(p) + \lambda \|p - \bar{x}\|^2 $$
where:
*   $\bar{x} := \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i$ is the centroid of the intersection points.
*   $\lambda$ is the regularization weight.

**Solution for $p$**:
To minimize $Q_\lambda(p)$, we solve $A p = b$, where:
$$ A = \sum_{i=1}^k N_i N_i^T + \lambda I $$
$$ b = \sum_{i=1}^k (N_i^T x_i) N_i + \lambda \bar{x} $$
Here, $I$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix. Note that the term $\frac{1}{k}$ in the original QEF definition is absorbed into the scaling of the system or can be kept; since it is a constant factor, it does not change the minimizer. The regularization term $\lambda \|p - \bar{x}\|^2$ expands to $\lambda (p^T p - 2 p^T \bar{x} + \bar{x}^T \bar{x})$. The gradient is $2 (\sum N_i N_i^T) p - 2 \sum (N_i^T x_i) N_i + 2 \lambda (p - \bar{x})$. Setting to zero yields the system above.

### Parameters
*   **Regularization weight $\lambda$**: $0.1$ [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Nearly Coplanar Planes**: If the normals $N_i$ are nearly parallel (planes are nearly coplanar), the unregularized QEF matrix $A$ becomes singular or ill-conditioned, potentially placing the minimizer far from the loop and outside the tetrahedron. The regularizer term $\lambda \|p - \bar{x}\|^2$ ensures $A$ is positive definite (assuming $\lambda > 0$), pulling the solution toward the mean intersection point $\bar{x}$ and ensuring a unique minimum [S1].
*   **Normal Orientation**: The input normals $N_i$ do not need to be consistently oriented. The energy term $\langle N_i, p - x_i \rangle^2$ is invariant to sign flips of $N_i$ [S1].
*   **Degenerate Loops**: Loops with $k=1$ or $k=2$ vertices do not arise in this context because a closed boundary loop on a tetrahedral grid must have at least three vertices [S1].
*   **Triangulation**: Dual polygons must be triangulated without adding new vertices (Steiner points). This preserves the direct correspondence between primal features and dual vertices [S1].

```python
import numpy as np

def solve_dual_vertex(intersection_points, normals, lambda_reg=0.1):
    """
    Computes the position of a dual vertex by minimizing the regularized QEF.
    
    Args:
        intersection_points: List of k points (x_i) in R^3.
        normals: List of k unit normals (N_i) in R^3.
        lambda_reg: Regularization weight lambda.
        
    Returns:
        p: Optimal vertex position in R^3.
    """
    k = len(intersection_points)
    if k == 0:
        return np.array([0.0, 0.0, 0.0])
    
    # Calculate mean intersection point x_bar
    x_bar = np.mean(intersection_points, axis=0)
    
    # Initialize matrix A and vector b
    # A = sum(N_i N_i^T) + lambda * I
    # b = sum((N_i . x_i) * N_i) + lambda * x_bar
    A = np.zeros((3, 3))
    b = np.zeros(3)
    
    for i in range(k):
        x_i = np.array(intersection_points[i])
        N_i = np.array(normals[i])
        
        # Outer product N_i * N_i^T
        outer_product = np.outer(N_i, N_i)
        A += outer_product
        
        # Scalar projection (N_i . x_i)
        proj = np.dot(N_i, x_i)
        
        # Add proj * N_i to b
        b += proj * N_i
        
    # Add regularization term to A and b
    A += lambda_reg * np.eye(3)
    b += lambda_reg * x_bar
    
    # Solve linear system A * p = b
    p = np.linalg.solve(A, b)
    return p

def dual_subgrid_marching_tetrahedra(primal_loops):
    """
    Reconstructs the dual mesh from primal loops.
    
    Args:
        primal_loops: List of primal loops. Each loop is a dict/object containing:
            - 'id': Unique identifier for the primal polygon.
            - 'points': List of intersection points (x_i).
            - 'normals': List of normals (N_i).
            - 'vertices': List of primal vertex indices involved in this loop.
            - 'edges': List of primal edge indices involved in this loop.
            
    Returns:
        dual_vertices: List of 3D coordinates.
        dual_faces: List of triangles (triplets of vertex indices).
    """
    # Step 1: Create dual vertices for each primal polygon
    dual_vertex_map = {} # Maps primal_loop_id -> dual_vertex_index
    dual_vertex_positions = []
    
    for loop in primal_loops:
        idx = len(dual_vertex_positions)
        dual_vertex_map[loop['id']] = idx
        
        # Compute position using QEF minimization
        pos = solve_dual_vertex(loop['points'], loop['normals'])
        dual_vertex_positions.append(pos)
        
    # Step 2: Build dual connectivity
    # We need to identify interior primal edges and interior primal vertices.
    # This requires iterating through all primal loops to build adjacency maps.
    
    # Map: primal_edge_index -> list of primal_loop_ids sharing this edge
    edge_to_loops = {}
    for loop in primal_loops:
        for edge_idx in loop['edges']:
            if edge_idx not in edge_to_loops:
                edge_to_loops[edge_idx] = []
            edge_to_loops[edge_idx].append(loop['id'])
            
    # Map: primal_vertex_index -> list of primal_loop_ids sharing this vertex
    vertex_to_loops = {}
    for loop in primal_loops:
        for vert_idx in loop['vertices']:
            if vert_idx not in vertex_to_loops:
                vertex_to_loops[vert_idx] = []
            vertex_to_loops[vert_idx].append(loop['id'])
            
    dual_edges = []
    dual_faces = []
    
    # Create dual edges for interior primal edges
    # An interior edge is shared by exactly two loops in a manifold surface
    for edge_idx, loop_ids in edge_to_loops.items():
        if len(loop_ids) == 2:
            v1_idx = dual_vertex_map[loop_ids[0]]
            v2_idx = dual_vertex_map[loop_ids[1]]
            dual_edges.append((v1_idx, v2_idx))
            
    # Create dual faces for interior primal vertices
    # A dual face is formed by the dual vertices of all loops incident to a primal vertex
    for vert_idx, loop_ids in vertex_to_loops.items():
        if len(loop_ids) < 3:
            continue # Cannot form a polygon with less than 3 vertices
            
        # Get the dual vertex indices for this vertex
        dual_v_indices = [dual_vertex_map[lid] for lid in loop_ids]
        
        # Triangulate the dual polygon defined by dual_v_indices
        # Since the order of loop_ids is not guaranteed to be cyclic,
        # we must determine the correct ordering.
        # In a manifold mesh, the loops around a vertex are ordered cyclically.
        # We can sort them by the angle of their dual vertices relative to the vertex position,
        # or rely on the connectivity from the primal mesh if available.
        # For a general implementation without explicit cyclic order, 
        # we assume the input provides them in a consistent order or we compute convex hull.
        # However, the source states "triangulate... without inserting new vertices".
        # A simple fan triangulation works if the polygon is convex and vertices are ordered.
        
        # Note: The source does not specify the triangulation algorithm for the dual polygon.
        # Standard practice is to fan from the first vertex if the polygon is convex.
        # If the polygon is not convex, a more robust triangulation is needed.
        # Given the constraints, we assume the dual polygon is convex or the order is valid.
        
        if len(dual_v_indices) >= 3:
            # Fan triangulation from the first vertex
            for i in range(1, len(dual_v_indices) - 1):
                face = (dual_v_indices[0], dual_v_indices[i], dual_v_indices[i+1])
                dual_faces.append(face)
                
    return dual_vertex_positions, dual_faces
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: The dominant cost is solving the linear system for each dual vertex. Each system is $3 \times 3$, which is $O(1)$. The connectivity construction requires iterating over primal edges and vertices, which is linear in the number of primal polygons and edges. Overall, the method is linear in the size of the output mesh.
*   **Memory Complexity**: Requires storing the dual connectivity map and the vertex positions. The memory overhead is proportional to the number of primal polygons (which becomes dual vertices) and the number of primal edges/vertices (which become dual edges/faces).
*   **Strengths**:
    *   **Accuracy**: Produces smoother and more accurate surfaces than primal subgrid marching, especially for thin features and sharp creases, because vertices are not constrained to grid edges [S1].
    *   **Feature Preservation**: Capable of preserving topologically distinct features within a single tetrahedron, which primal methods might merge or distort [S1].
    *   **Robustness**: The regularization term ensures a unique solution even in degenerate cases (e.g., parallel normals) [S1].
*   **Limitations**:
    *   **Complexity**: The dual connectivity construction is more complex than primal marching, requiring careful handling of polygon triangulation and adjacency.
    *   **Non-Manifoldness**: If the primal mesh is non-manifold, the dual construction may produce non-manifold dual meshes, requiring additional processing.
    *   **Computational Cost**: Slightly higher per-vertex cost due to QEF minimization compared to simple edge interpolation.

## Background
The method is inspired by dual contouring [Ju et al. 2002] and dual marching [Schaefer and Warren 2004]. It was introduced as part of the Subgrid Marching Tetrahedra framework to address the limitations of primal methods in handling complex topology within grid cells. The key insight is that while dual contouring associates one vertex per cell, dual marching associates a vertex per polygon, making it suitable for subgrid methods where multiple polygons can exist per tetrahedron [S1].
