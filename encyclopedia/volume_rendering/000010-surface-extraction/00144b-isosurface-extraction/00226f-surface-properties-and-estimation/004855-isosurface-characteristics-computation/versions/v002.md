# Isosurface Characteristics Computation

Also called: Integral of Gradient, Mean Gradient, Total Area, Confined Volume, Curvature, Pekar et al. Isosurface Characteristics

## Overview
Isosurface Characteristics Computation is a method for quantifying the geometric and topological properties of an extracted isosurface mesh. It solves the problem of analyzing the structural integrity and complexity of the surface by computing metrics such as the integral of the gradient, mean gradient, total area, confined volume, and curvature [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Marching Cubes Surface Normal Estimation](1005-marching-cubes-surface-normal-estimation.md) and [Marching Cubes Surface Area Estimation](1315-marching-cubes-surface-area-estimation.md) by providing a unified framework for multiple global and local scalar metrics derived from the surface geometry, rather than focusing on a single attribute like normals or area alone.

## How it works
The method operates on the triangular mesh output by an isosurface extraction algorithm (e.g., Marching Cubes) or the underlying scalar field [G]. It computes five primary characteristics:

1.  **Integral of Gradient**: The surface integral of the magnitude of the gradient of the scalar field $\phi$ over the isosurface $S$.
2.  **Mean Gradient**: The average magnitude of the gradient over the surface.
3.  **Total Area**: The sum of the areas of all triangles in the mesh.
4.  **Confined Volume**: The volume enclosed by the isosurface.
5.  **Curvature**: Local curvature measures (mean or Gaussian) at vertices or faces.

### Data Structures
*   **Mesh**: A list of vertices $V$, a list of triangles $T$, and connectivity information.
*   **Scalar Field**: A discrete grid of values $\phi(x,y,z)$ or interpolated values at vertex positions.
*   **Gradient Field**: Pre-computed or on-the-fly gradients $\nabla \phi$ at vertex positions.

### Steps

1.  **Input Preparation**:
    *   Obtain the isosurface mesh $M = (V, T)$ extracted at isovalue $c$.
    *   Ensure the scalar field $\phi$ is available for interpolation or gradient computation at vertex locations.

2.  **Compute Total Area ($A_{total}$)**:
    For each triangle $t_i \in T$ with vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$:
    $$ \vec{e}_1 = v_2 - v_1 $$
    $$ \vec{e}_2 = v_3 - v_1 $$
    $$ A_i = \frac{1}{2} \| \vec{e}_1 \times \vec{e}_2 \| $$
    $$ A_{total} = \sum_{i} A_i $$

3.  **Compute Gradient Magnitudes**:
    For each vertex $v_j \in V$, compute or retrieve the gradient $\nabla \phi(v_j)$. If the field is discrete, use central differences or trilinear interpolation derivatives.
    $$ g_j = \| \nabla \phi(v_j) \| $$

4.  **Compute Integral of Gradient ($I_g$)**:
    Approximate the surface integral by summing the gradient magnitude weighted by the triangle area. For a triangle $t_i$ with vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$ and gradient magnitudes $g_1, g_2, g_3$:
    $$ I_{g,i} = \frac{A_i}{3} (g_1 + g_2 + g_3) $$
    $$ I_g = \sum_{i} I_{g,i} $$

5.  **Compute Mean Gradient ($\bar{g}$)**:
    $$ \bar{g} = \frac{I_g}{A_{total}} $$

6.  **Compute Confined Volume ($V_{conf}$)**:
    Use the divergence theorem or tetrahedral decomposition. For each triangle $t_i$ with vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3$ and origin $O$:
    $$ V_i = \frac{1}{6} (v_1 \cdot (v_2 \times v_3)) $$
    $$ V_{conf} = \sum_{i} V_i $$
    *Note: This assumes a closed manifold. If not closed, the volume is undefined or requires closure [G].*

7.  **Compute Curvature**:
    *   **Mean Curvature ($H$)**: At each vertex, compute the average of the principal curvatures. Using the mesh Laplacian $\Delta v_j$:
        $$ H_j = \frac{1}{2} \| \Delta v_j \| $$
        (Approximation; more accurate methods use local patch fitting [G]).
    *   **Gaussian Curvature ($K$)**: Using angle defects at vertex $v_j$ with neighbors $N_j$:
        $$ K_j = \frac{2\pi - \sum_{k \in N_j} \theta_k}{A_j} $$
        where $\theta_k$ are the angles of the triangles incident to $v_j$ and $A_j$ is the associated area (e.g., Voronoi area).

### Parameters
*   **Isovalue**: The threshold used for extraction.
*   **Gradient Method**: Central differences, finite elements, or analytical derivatives if available.
*   **Curvature Approximation**: Vertex-based vs. face-based.

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Non-manifold Geometry**: Volume computation fails if the mesh is not watertight.
*   **Noise**: High-frequency noise in the scalar field leads to erratic gradient and curvature values. Smoothing may be required [G].
*   **Boundary Effects**: If the isosurface is clipped by the domain boundary, area and volume metrics are incomplete.

## Subtopics

### Hierarchical Surface Segmentation
Hierarchical Surface Segmentation classifies surface regions based on discrete curvature operators to identify topological features [S2]. It combines Boolean operators for mean curvature (convex/concave) and Gaussian curvature (elliptic/hyperbolic) to segment the surface into four types [S2]. The method computes the mean curvature operator to determine convexity/concavity and the Gaussian curvature sign operator to determine elliptic/hyperbolic behavior [S2]. Vertices are classified according to the combination of these results [S2]. This segmentation is used to explicitly localize minima of sulci and maxima of gyri, which serve as seed points for topology graph generation [S2].

### [Volumetric Divergence Metric](12575-volumetric-divergence-metric.md)
The Volumetric Divergence Metric (VDC) estimates the accuracy of isosurfaces extracted by marching algorithms by calculating the total volume between the produced mesh isosurface and the ideal trilinear interpolation isosurface. It sets itself apart from sibling metrics like the Tetrahedron Volumetric Divergence (TVD) Predictor by providing a highly accurate ground truth through recursive subdivision, whereas TVD uses a faster, closed-form geometric approximation.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of triangles, for area and gradient integration. Curvature computation is also $O(N)$ with proper data structures.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(N)$ to store mesh and gradient data.
*   **Strengths**: Provides a comprehensive set of geometric metrics in a single pass. Efficient for large meshes.
*   **Limitations**: Sensitive to mesh quality and noise. Volume computation requires a closed mesh.
*   **When to Use**: When analyzing the structural properties of isosurfaces, such as in medical imaging for tissue characterization or in scientific visualization for feature detection.
*   **When Not to Use**: When only a single metric (e.g., just area) is needed, simpler sibling methods may be more efficient. When the mesh is not closed, volume metrics are invalid.

## Background
The method was proposed by Pekar et al. in 2001 as an efficient approach for computing isosurface characteristics [S1, unofficial]. It builds on earlier strategies by Bajaj et al. [S1, unofficial]. The method is used in volumetric visualization for transfer function specification and feature analysis [S1, unofficial].
