# Boundary Face Center Value Adjustment

Also called: Modifying the center value of a boundary face

## Overview
Boundary Face Center Value Adjustment is a technique to ensure continuity of the isosurface at multiresolution boundaries by modifying the scalar value at the center vertex of a boundary face [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem where the isosurface intersects opposite edges of a face, causing cracks if the high-resolution isoline does not pass through specific intersection points [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by intentionally omitting surface reconnection between adjacent cells of different resolutions, relying instead on sufficient polygon density and local data modification to keep cracks visually negligible [7512-non-reconnected-multiresolution-isosurfacing.md].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A multiresolution scalar field $V$, an isovalue $iso$, and a boundary face $F$ at a resolution transition. The face has four vertices $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ and a center vertex $c$. The face is non-coplanar, meaning the low-resolution and high-resolution surface plots differ at the center cell [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Modified scalar value $v(c)$ at the center vertex $c$ of the boundary face.

**Data Structures:**
The method operates on the scalar values associated with the grid vertices. Specifically, it accesses:
*   $v(c)$: The scalar value at the center vertex of the boundary face.
*   $v(p)$: The scalar value at a specific intersection point $p$ on the high-resolution edge.
*   $v(m')$: The scalar value at the midpoint of the edge connecting the two vertices on the low-resolution side (or derived from geometry as implied by the source figures) [S1, unofficial].
*   $iso$: The global isovalue threshold.

**Steps:**
1.  **Identify Boundary Face:** Locate a face $F$ at a multiresolution boundary where the isosurface intersects the face.
2.  **Calculate Intermediate Value $e$:** Calculate the scalar value at point $p$, denoted as $e$. The source text provides a garbled formula for an intermediate variable $x$ or $e$ involving vertex values, but the core logic relies on comparing $e$ to $0.5$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Apply Adjustment Rule:** Modify $v(c)$ based on the value of $e$ relative to $0.5$ [S1, unofficial]:
    *   If $e > 0.5$:
        $$ e = 2e - 1 $$
        $$ v(c) = iso - e \cdot (m') + v(m') $$
    *   Else ($e \le 0.5$):
        $$ e = 2e $$
        $$ v(c) = iso - e \cdot (m') + v(m') $$
    *   *Note:* The source pseudocode is garbled. The logic adjusts the center value $c$ based on the isovalue $iso$ and the scalar value at $p$. The variable $m'$ appears to represent a scalar value associated with the midpoint geometry, and $v(m')$ is its value [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Handle Opposite Edge Intersection:** If the isoline passes through opposite edges, it may be impossible to adjust $v(c)$ so that the high-resolution isoline passes through both intersection points $p$ and $q$. The implementation adjusts the value so the isoline passes through $p$, guaranteeing coincidence along at least half the face. This may cause hairline cracks on the other half [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Reversibility (Optional):** Before performing any iteration of the forward wavelet transform involving only the high-resolution region, restore the scalar values at the centers of all faces along multiresolution boundaries by setting the scalar value at the center of a face to the average of the scalar values of its four vertices [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def adjust_boundary_face_center(v_center, v_p, v_m_prime, iso):
    """
    Adjusts the scalar value at the center of a boundary face to ensure isosurface continuity.
    
    Args:
        v_center (float): Current scalar value at the center vertex c.
        v_p (float): Scalar value at intersection point p on the high-resolution edge.
        v_m_prime (float): Scalar value at the midpoint m' of the low-resolution side edge.
        iso (float): The isovalue threshold.
        
    Returns:
        float: The new scalar value for the center vertex c.
    """
    # Calculate e based on the scalar value at p.
    # Note: The source text is garbled regarding the exact calculation of e from v_p.
    # We assume e is derived from v_p or is v_p itself for the comparison logic.
    # Based on the structure "e = scalar value at p", we set e = v_p.
    e = v_p
    
    if e > 0.5:
        e = 2 * e - 1
        new_v_center = iso - e * v_m_prime + v_m_prime
    else:
        e = 2 * e
        new_v_center = iso - e * v_m_prime + v_m_prime
        
    return new_v_center

def restore_boundary_face_center(vertices):
    """
    Restores the scalar value at the center of a boundary face to its average.
    Used for reversibility before forward wavelet transform.
    
    Args:
        vertices (list of float): Scalar values of the four vertices of the face.
        
    Returns:
        float: The average scalar value.
    """
    return sum(vertices) / 4.0
```

**Parameters:**
*   $iso$: The isovalue threshold. Typical values depend on the dataset.
*   $e$: Threshold for adjustment logic is $0.5$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Opposite Edge Intersection:** When the isoline intersects opposite edges, it may be impossible to make the high-resolution isoline pass through both intersection points $p$ and $q$. The method prioritizes passing through $p$, which may result in hairline cracks on the other half of the face [S1, unofficial].
*   **Non-coplanar Vertices:** The technique addresses cases where the four vertices at a face are non-coplanar, leading to differences between low and high-resolution surface plots at the center cell [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The change at a particular vertex is a function of only its immediate neighbor and the current isovalue, making the technique efficient [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Minimal additional memory is required as it modifies existing scalar values in place.
*   **Measured Performance:** Experimental results on multiresolution boundary faces intersecting the isosurface show that approximately $4.19\%$ develop cracks [S1, unofficial]. The maximum crack area is $2.84\%$ of the low-resolution boundary face area, and the average crack area is $1.22\%$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficient, trivially reversible, and forces high-resolution edges to coincide with corresponding low-resolution edges [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** May leave hairline cracks on up to $4.19\%$ of intersecting boundary faces, though these are considered visually negligible [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When using non-reconnected multiresolution isosurfacing and visual continuity at boundaries is critical, but reconnection overhead is unacceptable.
*   **When Not to Use:** When strict geometric continuity is required without any potential for hairline cracks, or when the dataset does not have sufficient polygon density to make remaining cracks negligible.

## Background
The technique was introduced in the paper "Seamless Multiresolution Isosurfaces Using Wavelets" [S1, unofficial]. It is part of a broader approach to multiresolution isosurfacing that avoids the tracking overhead of surface reconnection by modifying sparse data values along boundaries [S1, unofficial]. The method relies on the observation that modifying the center value of a boundary face can force the high-resolution isoline to coincide with the low-resolution isoline along at least half the face [S1, unofficial].
