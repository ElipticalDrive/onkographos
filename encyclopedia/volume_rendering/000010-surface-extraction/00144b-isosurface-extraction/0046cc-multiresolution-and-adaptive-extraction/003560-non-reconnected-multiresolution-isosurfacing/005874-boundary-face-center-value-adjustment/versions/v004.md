# Boundary Face Center Value Adjustment

Also called: Modifying the center value of a boundary face

## Overview
Boundary Face Center Value Adjustment is a technique used in multiresolution isosurface extraction to ensure continuity of the isosurface at boundaries where resolution levels change [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem where an isosurface intersects opposite edges of a boundary face, which can cause visual cracks if the high-resolution isoline does not pass through specific intersection points [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by intentionally modifying the underlying scalar data values at boundary face centers rather than post-processing the mesh geometry or reconnecting polygons [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
* Scalar field data $V$ [S1, unofficial].
* Isovalue threshold $iso$ [S1, unofficial].
* Boundary face $F$ at a multiresolution boundary where resolution differs by one level [S1, unofficial].
* Vertices of the face: $v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4$ [S1, unofficial].
* Center vertex $c$ of the face [S1, unofficial].
* Midpoint $m$ of the edge connecting the two vertices on the low-resolution side [S1, unofficial].
* Intersection point $p$ on a high-resolution edge [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
* Updated scalar value $v(c)$ at the center vertex $c$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
* The scalar field is stored in a multiresolution hierarchy (e.g., wavelet tree) [S1, unofficial].
* Boundary faces are identified as those shared between cells of different resolution levels [S1, unofficial].
* Each face has a center vertex $c$ whose scalar value is stored separately or derived [S1, unofficial].
* Exact layouts, fields, sizes, and bit packing are Not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1. Identify boundary faces where the isosurface intersects [S1, unofficial].
2. For each intersecting face, determine if the isoline intersects opposite edges [S1, unofficial].
3. If opposite edges are intersected, calculate the scalar value $e$ at intersection point $p$ on a high-resolution edge [S1, unofficial].
4. Apply the adjustment formula to compute the updated scalar value $v(c)$ at the face center $c$ [S1, unofficial].
5. (Reversible) Before any forward wavelet transform iteration involving only the high-resolution region, restore $v(c)$ to the average of the four face vertices [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
Let $e$ be the scalar value at point $p$ [S1, unofficial].
Let $m'$ be the scalar value at the midpoint $m$ of the edge on the low-resolution side [S1, unofficial].
Let $v(m')$ be the scalar value at $m$ [S1, unofficial].
The update rule for $v(c)$ is:
If $e > 0.5$:
$$ e \leftarrow 2e - 1 $$
$$ v(c) \leftarrow \text{iso} - e \cdot m' + v(m') $$
Else:
$$ e \leftarrow 2e $$
$$ v(c) \leftarrow \text{iso} - e \cdot m' + v(m') $$
The source text contains OCR errors in the formula; the logic adjusts the center value $c$ based on the isovalue $iso$ and the scalar value at $p$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def adjust_boundary_face_center(e, iso, m_prime, v_m_prime):
    """
    Adjusts the scalar value at the center of a boundary face to ensure
    isosurface continuity.
    
    Args:
        e: Scalar value at intersection point p.
        iso: Isovalue threshold.
        m_prime: Scalar value at midpoint m of the low-resolution edge.
        v_m_prime: Scalar value at midpoint m.
        
    Returns:
        New scalar value for the center vertex c.
    """
    if e > 0.5:
        e = 2.0 * e - 1.0
    else:
        e = 2.0 * e
    v_c = iso - e * m_prime + v_m_prime
    return v_c

def restore_boundary_face_center(v, face_vertices):
    """
    Reverses the adjustment by setting the center value to the average 
    of the four vertices.
    """
    v1, v2, v3, v4 = face_vertices
    v_center = (v[v1] + v[v2] + v[v3] + v[v4]) / 4.0
    return v_center
```

**Parameters:**
* Isovalue $iso$: The threshold for surface extraction [S1, unofficial].
* Threshold for $e$: 0.5 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* If the isoline passes through opposite edges, it may be impossible to adjust $v(c)$ so that the high-resolution isoline passes through both intersection points $p$ and $q$ [S1, unofficial]. The implementation adjusts the value so the isoline passes through $p$, guaranteeing coincidence along at least half the face [S1, unofficial]. This may cause hairline cracks on the other half [S1, unofficial].
* Experimental results show approximately 4.19% of multiresolution boundary faces intersecting the isosurface develop these cracks [S1, unofficial]. The maximum crack area is 2.84% of the low-resolution boundary face area, while the average crack area is 1.22% [S1, unofficial].
* The modification is reversible: before performing any iteration of the forward wavelet transform involving only the high-resolution region, restore the scalar values at the centers of all faces along multiresolution boundaries by setting the scalar value at the center of a face to the average of the scalar values of its four vertices [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
* Time Complexity: The change at a particular vertex is a function of only its immediate neighbor and the current isovalue, making the operation efficient [S1, unofficial].
* Memory Complexity: Requires storage for the scalar field and identification of boundary faces [S1, unofficial].
* Strengths: Efficient, local operation; ensures continuity along at least half the face; reversible [S1, unofficial].
* Limitations: May leave hairline cracks on the other half of the face in specific geometric configurations (opposite edge intersections) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
* Origin: The technique is described in "Seamless Multiresolution Isosurfaces Using Wavelets" [S1, unofficial].
* History: It is part of a broader approach to multiresolution isosurfacing that uses wavelets to manage data resolution and addresses the specific issue of cracks at resolution boundaries [S1, unofficial].
* Usage: Used in volume rendering and scientific visualization where seamless isosurfaces are required across multiresolution datasets [S1, unofficial].
