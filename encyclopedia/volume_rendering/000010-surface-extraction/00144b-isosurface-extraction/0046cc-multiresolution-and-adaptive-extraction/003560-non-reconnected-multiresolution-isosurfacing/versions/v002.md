# Non-reconnected Multiresolution Isosurfacing

Also called: Crack Suppression via Polygon Density, deliberate crack omission, non-reconnected multiresolution cells

## Overview
Non-reconnected multiresolution isosurfacing is a surface extraction method that intentionally omits surface reconnection between adjacent cells of different resolutions to avoid tracking overhead, relying on sufficient polygon density to keep cracks visually negligible [S1]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Adaptive Minimum Projected Size Adjustment](7457-adaptive-minimum-projected-size-adjustment.md) and [Adaptive Multiresolution Extraction with Oracle](11830-adaptive-multiresolution-extraction-with-oracle.md) by trading visual perfection for interactive frame rates, avoiding the computational cost of neighbor tracking and surface reconnection required by other multiresolution methods [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A hierarchical cell structure representing the volume data, where each cell has an associated resolution level.
- Output: A set of independent surface elements (triangles) rendered per cell, without connectivity constraints between adjacent cells of different resolutions.

**Data Structures:**
- Cell Hierarchy: A tree or grid structure where each node represents a cell. Each cell contains:
  - `level`: The resolution level of the cell (integer).
  - `geometry`: The surface elements (e.g., triangles) extracted at this resolution.
  - `active`: A boolean flag indicating if the cell is included in the current frame's display.

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Dynamic Level Selection:** For each frame, dynamically select the stopping level in the cell hierarchy display. This selection is based on performance constraints (e.g., target frame rate) and does not enforce connectivity constraints between adjacent cells [S1].
2. **Independent Surface Extraction:** For each active cell, extract the surface elements (e.g., using Marching Cubes or a similar algorithm) independently. No information from neighboring cells is used to adjust the geometry [S1].
3. **Render Surface Elements:** Render the surface elements found at each active cell independently. Do not attempt to reconnect or stitch together surface elements from adjacent cells of different resolutions [S1].
4. **Crack Acceptance:** Accept that visible gaps (cracks) may appear between adjacent cells due to field resolution mismatches. These cracks are considered visually negligible as long as a sufficiently large number of polygons is displayed [S1].

**Formulas:**
- None explicitly provided in the sources. The method relies on the implicit threshold of polygon density.

**Parameters:**
- `sufficient_polygon_threshold`: An implicit threshold where polygon density makes cracks 'hardly visible', justifying the omission of reconnection [S1]. The exact value is not specified but is determined by the visual acceptability of cracks at the current display resolution and viewing distance.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Distant Neighbors:** Adjacent cells may be distant by more than one resolution level in an unconstrained hierarchy. This makes neighbor tracking computationally expensive and slows down display rates [S1].
- **Cracks from Resolution Mismatches:** Cracks may appear due to field resolution mismatches between adjacent cells. Drawing more triangles does not solve this fundamental issue, as the underlying field data is not defined at the same resolution in adjacent cells [S1].
- **Dynamic Surface Changes:** The surface is always changing during the sculpting process, which makes reconnection complex and potentially unstable [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The method avoids the $O(N)$ or higher complexity associated with neighbor tracking and reconnection, leading to faster per-frame processing times. The complexity is dominated by the independent extraction and rendering of triangles in active cells [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Memory usage is proportional to the number of active cells and their associated geometry. No additional memory is required for neighbor relationships or reconnection data structures [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** Applied in the resolution adaptive volume sculpting system's rendering pipeline, it maintains interactive frame rates during dynamic LOD selection [S1]. Specific performance metrics (e.g., FPS, memory usage) are not provided in the sources.
- **Scaling:** The method scales well with the number of cells, as each cell is processed independently. Performance is primarily limited by the total number of triangles rendered and the frame budget [S1].
- **Strengths:**
  - High interactive frame rates due to the absence of reconnection overhead [S1].
  - Simple implementation, as it does not require complex neighbor tracking or reconnection algorithms [S1].
  - Suitable for dynamic environments where the surface is constantly changing [S1].
- **Limitations:**
  - Visible cracks may appear between adjacent cells of different resolutions [S1].
  - Not suitable for applications requiring visually perfect, watertight meshes [S1].
  - Cracks cannot be eliminated by simply increasing polygon density, as they stem from resolution mismatches in the underlying field data [S1].
- **When to Use:** Use this method when interactive frame rates are critical, and minor visual artifacts (cracks) are acceptable. It is particularly useful in dynamic sculpting or visualization systems where the surface changes frequently [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** Do not use this method when visual perfection is required, or when the output needs to be a standard, watertight mesh for further processing (e.g., simulation, export) [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of the "Resolution Adaptive Volume Sculpting" system [S1]. It was developed by Eric Ferley, Marie-Paule Cani, and Jean-Philippe Pernot, as part of their work on interactive volume sculpting tools [S1]. The paper was published in 2001 [S1]. The approach was chosen to balance the need for interactivity with the complexity of multiresolution surface extraction, acknowledging that reconnection would significantly slow down the display rate, especially in an unconstrained hierarchy [S1].
