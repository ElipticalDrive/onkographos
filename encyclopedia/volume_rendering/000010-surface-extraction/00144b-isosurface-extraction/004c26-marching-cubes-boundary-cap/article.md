# Marching Cubes Boundary Cap

Also called: MC boundary cap, box-face crossing cap

## Overview
Marching Cubes Boundary Cap is a post-processing step for the Marching Cubes algorithm that closes open mesh boundaries when the isosurface exits the sample-cube boundary [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of non-watertight meshes (where `IsWatertight` is false) caused by insufficient bounding ranges, ensuring the output is a closed 2-manifold suitable for 3D printing [S1, unofficial]. Unlike the default mitigation where bounding ranges are auto-sized to enclose the set, this method allows users to force a too-small range while still producing a watertight mesh [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- **Input**: A mesh generated by Marching Cubes on a regular grid, scalar field values sampled at grid vertices, the isovalue, and the bounding cube geometry.
- **Output**: A closed 2-manifold mesh where boundary edges on the cube faces are sealed with triangulated caps.

**Data Structures**
- The mesh consists of vertices and triangles.
- Boundary edges are identified as edges belonging to only one triangle.
- Scalar field values are accessed at the 8 vertices of each cube cell.

**Steps**
1. **Identify Boundary Edges**: Iterate through the mesh generated by Marching Cubes. Identify all edges that are part of only one triangle. These edges lie on the boundary of the sample cube where the isosurface exits the domain [S1, unofficial].
2. **Group by Face**: Group the boundary edges by the cube face they lie on. There are 6 faces in a cube. For each face, collect the vertices and edges that form the open boundary contour [S1, unofficial].
3. **Extract Contour Lines**: For each of the 6 cube faces, run a 2D Marching Squares contour algorithm on the face grid to extract the contour lines of the intersection between the isosurface and the cube face. The field values are already sampled on the face vertices from the MC pass [S1, unofficial].
4. **Triangulate Caps**: Triangulate the interior (or exterior, depending on the field sign convention relative to the iso value) region enclosed by the contour lines to create a cap mesh [S1, unofficial]. The triangulation must fill the hole left by the MC mesh on that face.
5. **Weld Vertices**: Weld the vertices of the generated cap triangles to the existing boundary vertices of the MC mesh on that face to form a single closed 2-manifold [S1, unofficial].
6. **Validation**: Assert that the resulting mesh is a closed 2-manifold with outward winding and positive signed volume [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
- **Isovalue**: The threshold for the isosurface. Typical values depend on the scalar field.
- **Cube Bounds**: The bounding box of the sample cube. Must be defined to identify face crossings.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- If the bounding range is auto-sized to enclose the set, the surface remains interior to the cube. The cap method must be a no-op in this case to maintain byte-identical output and performance [S1, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise when determining if a vertex lies exactly on the cube face. Use a small epsilon for comparisons.
- Degenerate input: If the isosurface does not intersect a face, no cap is generated for that face.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of boundary edges and the size of the cube faces. The 2D Marching Squares step is linear with respect to the number of cells on the face.
- **Memory Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of boundary edges, as new triangles are added for the caps.
- **Measured Performance**: In a validation test case with a sphere of radius r=1.8 inside a cube with half-extent 1.6, the uncapped MC mesh had 648 boundary edges and was not watertight. After capping, the mesh was watertight [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Ensures watertightness even with undersized bounding boxes. Suitable for 3D printing.
- **Limitations**: Adds computational overhead. Requires careful handling of the no-op case for auto-sized bounds.
- **When to Use**: When the bounding box is fixed and may be smaller than the isosurface extent, or when watertightness is required regardless of bounding box size.
- **When Not to Use**: When the bounding box is guaranteed to enclose the isosurface, as the cap is a no-op and adds unnecessary complexity.

## Background
Marching Cubes Boundary Cap was developed as a follow-up to Marching Cubes validation (S9) to address issues with non-watertight meshes when the isosurface exits the sample-cube boundary [S1, unofficial]. It was implemented in the `UserBulbMeshExporter` and validated by `MeshValidator` to ensure `SignedVolume > 0` and outward winding [S1, unofficial]. The method was merged into the 'main' branch in August 2026 [S1, unofficial].
