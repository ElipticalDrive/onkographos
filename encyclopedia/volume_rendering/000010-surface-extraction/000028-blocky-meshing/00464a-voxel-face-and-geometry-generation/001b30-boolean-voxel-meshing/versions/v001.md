# Boolean Voxel Meshing

Also called: VoxelGrid to Mesh

## Overview
Boolean Voxel Meshing converts a discrete boolean voxel occupancy grid into a triangular mesh by detecting faces and vertices at voxel boundaries [S1, unofficial]. It differs from interpolation-based methods like Marching Cubes by outputting axis-aligned cube meshes directly from the grid, resulting in simpler but blocky geometry unless voxel resolution is high [S2, unofficial]. It builds on [Blocky meshing](31-blocky-meshing.md) by explicitly generating the vertex and face connectivity required for rendering, rather than just culling or merging blocks.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `occ`: A 3D boolean numpy array of shape `(nx, ny, nz)` representing the voxel occupancy grid. `True` indicates an occupied voxel, `False` indicates empty space.
- `loc`: A 3D vector `(x, y, z)` representing the world-space origin of the voxel grid.
- `scale`: A scalar representing the world-space size of one voxel unit.

**Outputs:**
- `vertices`: A numpy array of shape `(N, 3)` containing the 3D coordinates of the mesh vertices in world space.
- `faces`: A numpy array of shape `(M, 4)` containing the indices of the vertices forming each quad face.

**Data Structures:**
- `occ`: Boolean grid.
- `v`: A boolean grid of shape `(nx+1, ny+1, nz+1)` indicating which vertices are part of the surface.
- `v_idx`: An integer grid of shape `(nx+1, ny+1, nz+1)` mapping vertex grid coordinates to unique vertex indices. Initialized to `-1`.

**Steps:**

1.  **Padding:** Pad the occupancy grid `occ` with a 1-voxel constant boundary of `False` (empty) to handle boundary conditions uniformly.
    $$ \text{occ}_{\text{pad}} = \text{pad}(\text{occ}, \text{pad\_width}=1, \text{mode}=\text{'constant'}, \text{constant\_values}=0) $$
    The shape becomes `(nx+2, ny+2, nz+2)`.

2.  **Face Detection:** Compute boolean masks for faces on the boundaries between occupied and empty voxels for each of the three axes. A face exists if one side is occupied and the other is empty.
    -   **X-axis faces (f1):**
        $$ f1_r = \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[:-1, 1:-1, 1:-1] \land \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:, 1:-1, 1:-1] $$
        $$ f1_l = \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[:-1, 1:-1, 1:-1] \land \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:, 1:-1, 1:-1] $$
        $$ f1 = f1_r \lor f1_l $$
        Shape: `(nx+1, ny, nz)`.
    -   **Y-axis faces (f2):**
        $$ f2_r = \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, :-1, 1:-1] \land \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:, 1:-1] $$
        $$ f2_l = \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, :-1, 1:-1] \land \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:, 1:-1] $$
        $$ f2 = f2_r \lor f2_l $$
        Shape: `(nx, ny+1, nz)`.
    -   **Z-axis faces (f3):**
        $$ f3_r = \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:-1, :-1] \land \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:-1, 1:] $$
        $$ f3_l = \neg \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:-1, :-1] \land \text{occ}_{\text{pad}}[1:-1, 1:-1, 1:] $$
        $$ f3 = f3_r \lor f3_l $$
        Shape: `(nx, ny, nz+1)`.

3.  **Vertex Identification:** Initialize a vertex grid `v` of shape `(nx+1, ny+1, nz+1)` with `False`. Mark vertices as part of the surface if they are corners of any detected face.
    -   For `f1` (X-normal faces):
        $$ v[:, :-1, :-1] \lor= f1 $$
        $$ v[:, :-1, 1:] \lor= f1 $$
        $$ v[:, 1:, :-1] \lor= f1 $$
        $$ v[:, 1:, 1:] \lor= f1 $$
    -   For `f2` (Y-normal faces):
        $$ v[:-1, :, :-1] \lor= f2 $$
        $$ v[:-1, :, 1:] \lor= f2 $$
        $$ v[1:, :, :-1] \lor= f2 $$
        $$ v[1:, :, 1:] \lor= f2 $$
    -   For `f3` (Z-normal faces):
        $$ v[:-1, :-1, :] \lor= f3 $$
        $$ v[:-1, 1:, :] \lor= f3 $$
        $$ v[1:, :-1, :] \lor= f3 $$
        $$ v[1:, 1:, :] \lor= f3 $$

4.  **Vertex Indexing:** Create an index grid `v_idx` of shape `(nx+1, ny+1, nz+1)` initialized to `-1`. Assign unique indices to vertices marked in `v`.
    $$ v_{\text{mask}} = \text{where}(v) $$
    $$ v_{\text{idx}}[v] = \text{arange}(\text{len}(v_{\text{mask}})) $$

5.  **Vertex Coordinate Extraction:** Extract the coordinates of the marked vertices and normalize them to the range `[-0.5, 0.5]` relative to the voxel grid dimensions.
    $$ x, y, z = \text{where}(v) $$
    $$ x_{\text{norm}} = \frac{x}{nx} - 0.5 $$
    $$ y_{\text{norm}} = \frac{y}{ny} - 0.5 $$
    $$ z_{\text{norm}} = \frac{z}{nz} - 0.5 $$
    $$ \text{vertices}_{\text{local}} = \text{stack}([x_{\text{norm}}, y_{\text{norm}}, z_{\text{norm}}], \text{axis}=1) $$

6.  **Face Index Construction:** Extract the grid coordinates of the faces using `np.where` for each direction and construct quad indices. The winding order is consistent for outward-facing normals.
    -   **f1_l (Left, X-min):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{1\_l} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z]] $$
    -   **f1_r (Right, X-max):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{1\_r} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x,y,z+1]] $$
    -   **f2_l (Left, Y-min):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{2\_l} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x,y,z+1]] $$
    -   **f2_r (Right, Y-max):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{2\_r} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z+1], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z]] $$
    -   **f3_l (Left, Z-min):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{3\_l} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y+1,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z]] $$
    -   **f3_r (Right, Z-max):**
        $$ \text{faces}_{3\_r} = [\text{v\_idx}[x,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y,z], \text{v\_idx}[x+1,y+1,z], \text{v\_idx}[x,y+1,z]] $$

7.  **Concatenation:** Combine all face arrays.
    $$ \text{faces} = \text{concatenate}([\text{faces}_{1\_l}, \text{faces}_{1\_r}, \text{faces}_{2\_l}, \text{faces}_{2\_r}, \text{faces}_{3\_l}, \text{faces}_{3\_r}], \text{axis}=0) $$

8.  **World Space Transformation:** Transform local vertices to world space.
    $$ \text{vertices}_{\text{world}} = \text{loc} + \text{scale} \times \text{vertices}_{\text{local}} $$

9.  **Mesh Creation:** Construct the final mesh object using the vertices and faces.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def boolean_voxel_meshing(occ, loc, scale):
    """
    Convert a boolean voxel occupancy grid to a triangular mesh.
    
    Args:
        occ: numpy.ndarray, shape (nx, ny, nz), boolean.
        loc: tuple or list, shape (3,), world space origin.
        scale: float, world space size of one voxel.
        
    Returns:
        vertices: numpy.ndarray, shape (N, 3), world space coordinates.
        faces: numpy.ndarray, shape (M, 4), vertex indices for quads.
    """
    nx, ny, nz = occ.shape
    
    # Step 1: Pad occupancy grid
    # Pad with 1 constant boundary of False (0)
    occ_pad = np.pad(occ, pad_width=1, mode='constant', constant_values=False)
    
    # Step 2: Compute face masks
    # X-axis faces (f1)
    f1_r = occ_pad[:-1, 1:-1, 1:-1] & ~occ_pad[1:, 1:-1, 1:-1]
    f1_l = ~occ_pad[:-1, 1:-1, 1:-1] & occ_pad[1:, 1:-1, 1:-1]
    f1 = f1_r | f1_l
    
    # Y-axis faces (f2)
    f2_r = occ_pad[1:-1, :-1, 1:-1] & ~occ_pad[1:-1, 1:, 1:-1]
    f2_l = ~occ_pad[1:-1, :-1, 1:-1] & occ_pad[1:-1, 1:, 1:-1]
    f2 = f2_r | f2_l
    
    # Z-axis faces (f3)
    f3_r = occ_pad[1:-1, 1:-1, :-1] & ~occ_pad[1:-1, 1:-1, 1:]
    f3_l = ~occ_pad[1:-1, 1:-1, :-1] & occ_pad[1:-1, 1:-1, 1:]
    f3 = f3_r | f3_l
    
    # Step 3: Identify vertices
    # Initialize vertex grid of shape (nx+1, ny+1, nz+1)
    v = np.zeros((nx + 1, ny + 1, nz + 1), dtype=bool)
    
    # Assign f1 to v
    v[:, :-1, :-1] |= f1
    v[:, :-1, 1:] |= f1
    v[:, 1:, :-1] |= f1
    v[:, 1:, 1:] |= f1
    
    # Assign f2 to v
    v[:-1, :, :-1] |= f2
    v[:-1, :, 1:] |= f2
    v[1:, :, :-1] |= f2
    v[1:, :, 1:] |= f2
    
    # Assign f3 to v
    v[:-1, :-1, :] |= f3
    v[:-1, 1:, :] |= f3
    v[1:, :-1, :] |= f3
    v[1:, 1:, :] |= f3
    
    # Step 4: Map vertex indices
    v_idx = np.full((nx + 1, ny + 1, nz + 1), -1, dtype=int)
    v_coords = np.where(v)
    n_vertices = len(v_coords[0])
    v_idx[v] = np.arange(n_vertices)
    
    # Step 5: Extract and normalize vertex coordinates
    v_x, v_y, v_z = v_coords
    v_x_norm = v_x / nx - 0.5
    v_y_norm = v_y / ny - 0.5
    v_z_norm = v_z / nz - 0.5
    
    vertices_local = np.stack([v_x_norm, v_y_norm, v_z_norm], axis=1)
    
    # Step 6: Extract face indices
    # Helper to get indices for a face mask
    def get_face_indices(mask, idx_grid, offset_func):
        x, y, z = np.where(mask)
        # offset_func returns the 4 vertex indices for the quad
        return np.column_stack([
            offset_func(x, y, z, idx_grid)
        ])

    # f1_l: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x,y,z+1], v_idx[x,y+1,z+1], v_idx[x,y+1,z]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f1_l)
    faces_1_l = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x, y, z + 1],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z + 1],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z]
    ])
    
    # f1_r: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x,y+1,z], v_idx[x,y+1,z+1], v_idx[x,y,z+1]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f1_r)
    faces_1_r = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z + 1],
        v_idx[x, y, z + 1]
    ])
    
    # f2_l: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x+1,y,z], v_idx[x+1,y,z+1], v_idx[x,y,z+1]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f2_l)
    faces_2_l = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z + 1],
        v_idx[x, y, z + 1]
    ])
    
    # f2_r: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x,y,z+1], v_idx[x+1,y,z+1], v_idx[x+1,y,z]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f2_r)
    faces_2_r = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x, y, z + 1],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z + 1],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z]
    ])
    
    # f3_l: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x,y+1,z], v_idx[x+1,y+1,z], v_idx[x+1,y,z]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f3_l)
    faces_3_l = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y + 1, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z]
    ])
    
    # f3_r: [v_idx[x,y,z], v_idx[x+1,y,z], v_idx[x+1,y+1,z], v_idx[x,y+1,z]]
    x, y, z = np.where(f3_r)
    faces_3_r = np.column_stack([
        v_idx[x, y, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y, z],
        v_idx[x + 1, y + 1, z],
        v_idx[x, y + 1, z]
    ])
    
    # Step 7: Concatenate faces
    faces = np.concatenate([faces_1_l, faces_1_r, faces_2_l, faces_2_r, faces_3_l, faces_3_r], axis=0)
    
    # Step 8: Transform to world space
    loc_arr = np.array(loc)
    vertices = loc_arr + scale * vertices_local
    
    return vertices, faces
```

**Parameters:**
- `loc`: Default `(0, 0, 0)`.
- `scale`: Default `1.0`.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Empty Grid:** If `occ` is all `False`, `v` will be all `False`, resulting in 0 vertices and 0 faces.
- **Fully Occupied Grid:** If `occ` is all `True`, `v` will be all `False` (since there are no boundaries), resulting in 0 vertices and 0 faces.
- **Precision:** Normalization to `[-0.5, 0.5]` uses floating-point division. Ensure `nx, ny, nz` are non-zero.
- **Boundary Conditions:** The padding step ensures that faces on the outer boundary of the original grid are correctly detected. Without padding, boundary faces would be missed.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels, as each voxel and its neighbors are visited a constant number of times.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the occupancy grid, face masks, vertex grid, and index grid.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement, deterministic, produces a watertight mesh if the input is a valid binary volume, fast for dense grids.
- **Limitations:** Geometry is blocky and lacks smoothness, high memory usage for large grids, does not handle partial voxel occupancy or alpha blending.
- **When to Use:** When a blocky, voxel-accurate representation is acceptable, or as a preprocessing step for further mesh optimization.
- **When Not to Use:** When smooth surfaces are required, or when the grid is very large and memory is constrained.

## Background
Boolean Voxel Meshing is a method for surface extraction from discrete voxel data. It is commonly used in computer graphics for converting voxelized models into triangle meshes for rendering. The method is described in the context of differentiable volumetric rendering pipelines [S1, unofficial].
