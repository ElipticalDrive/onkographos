# Voxel Occupancy Boundary Detection

Also called: Voxel Boundary Detection, Occupancy Grid Boundary Extraction

## Overview
Voxel Occupancy Boundary Detection identifies voxels in a 3D occupancy grid that lie on the interface between occupied and unoccupied space. It solves the problem of converting a discrete boolean volume into a set of boundary indicators required for surface extraction algorithms, such as Boolean Voxel Meshing. It differs from [Octree Traversal for Surface Extraction](2113-octree-traversal-for-surface-extraction.md) by operating on a fixed-resolution grid rather than a hierarchical tree, and from [VoxelGrid Mesh Generation](2714-voxelgrid-mesh-generation.md) by outputting a boolean mask of boundary voxels rather than a mesh directly.

## How it works
**Inputs:** A 3D boolean occupancy grid `occ` of shape $(D, H, W)$, where `True` indicates an occupied voxel and `False` indicates an unoccupied voxel.
**Outputs:** A 3D boolean boundary mask `boundary` of shape $(D, H, W)$, where `True` indicates a voxel is on the boundary.

**Data Structures:**
*   `occ`: 3D array of booleans.
*   `occupied`: 3D array of booleans, representing voxels fully enclosed by occupied neighbors.
*   `unoccupied`: 3D array of booleans, representing voxels fully enclosed by unoccupied neighbors.
*   `boundary`: 3D array of booleans, the result.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Fully Occupied Voxels:** A voxel is considered "fully occupied" (interior) if all 8 of its corner neighbors in the occupancy grid are `True`. This is computed using logical AND across all 8 shifted slices of the grid.
2.  **Compute Fully Unoccupied Voxels:** A voxel is considered "fully unoccupied" (exterior) if all 8 of its corner neighbors are `False`. This is computed using logical OR on the negated neighbors, then negating the result.
3.  **Compute Boundary:** A voxel is on the boundary if it is neither fully occupied nor fully unoccupied.

**Formulas:**
Let $O_{i,j,k}$ be the value of the voxel at index $(i,j,k)$.
The fully occupied mask $I_{i,j,k}$ is:
$$ I_{i,j,k} = \bigwedge_{d \in \{0,1\}, h \in \{0,1\}, w \in \{0,1\}} O_{i+d, j+h, k+w} $$
The fully unoccupied mask $E_{i,j,k}$ is:
$$ E_{i,j,k} = \neg \left( \bigvee_{d \in \{0,1\}, h \in \{0,1\}, w \in \{0,1\}} \neg O_{i+d, j+h, k+w} \right) $$
The boundary mask $B_{i,j,k}$ is:
$$ B_{i,j,k} = \neg I_{i,j,k} \land \neg E_{i,j,k} $$

## Subtopics

### Binary Volume Cell Classification
Binary Volume Cell Classification is a variant used in octree-based surface extraction. It classifies octree leaf cells in a binary volume to determine if an isosurface intersects the cell. It differs from the standard voxel boundary detection by operating on octree cells and explicitly identifying "heterogeneous" cells and "bipolar edges." It is chosen when hierarchical representation is required.
*   **Definition:** Classification of octree leaf cells in a binary volume (values 0 for internal, 1 for external) to determine if an isosurface intersects the cell [S2, unofficial].
*   **Steps:**
    1.  Check the 8 corner voxels of the cell [S2, unofficial].
    2.  If all corner voxels have the same value, classify as homogeneous (non-heterogeneous) [S2, unofficial].
    3.  If at least one corner voxel has a different value than the others, classify as heterogeneous [S2, unofficial].
    4.  For heterogeneous cells, identify bipolar edges: edges where the endpoint voxels have different values (0 and 1) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Pitfalls:** Bipolar edges indicate that the isosurface intersects the edge at least once. This is the primary indicator for surface extraction within the cell [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(D \times H \times W)$, where $D, H, W$ are the dimensions of the grid. The operation involves a constant number of shifts and logical operations per voxel.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(D \times H \times W)$ for the input and output masks. Intermediate masks (`occupied`, `unoccupied`) also require $O(D \times H \times W)$ memory.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Simple, parallelizable, and efficient for dense grids.
*   **Limitations:** Does not handle hierarchical structures. Boundary conditions at the grid edges are ambiguous without padding.
*   **When to use:** When a fixed-resolution occupancy grid is available and a quick boundary mask is needed for meshing.
*   **When not to use:** When the volume is sparse or hierarchical (use Octree Traversal).

## Background
The method is derived from differentiable volumetric rendering frameworks, specifically the `differentiable_volumetric_rendering` repository [S1, unofficial]. It is used to extract surfaces from neural occupancy fields. The concept of checking corner neighbors to determine cell heterogeneity is also found in binary volume surface extraction literature [S2, unofficial].
