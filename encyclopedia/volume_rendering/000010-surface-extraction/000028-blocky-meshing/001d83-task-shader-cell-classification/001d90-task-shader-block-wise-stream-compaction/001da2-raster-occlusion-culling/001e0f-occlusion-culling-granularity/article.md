# Occlusion Culling Granularity

Also called: Block-Based Occlusion Culling Granularity, Occlusion Buffer Sampling Resolution

## Overview
Occlusion Culling Granularity defines the spatial resolution at which the visibility of voxel blocks is determined during the raster occlusion culling phase. It serves as a critical configuration parameter that balances the accuracy of the occlusion test against the computational overhead of the proxy geometry rasterization. This method sets the size of the culling regions (typically aligned with the leaf-level of a reference octree) and determines how the resulting occlusion buffer is sampled, directly influencing aliasing artifacts and shading quality.

## How it works
The method configures the granularity of the occlusion test by defining the dimensions of the culling blocks and the resolution of the sampling grid used to interpret the depth buffer.

**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** The spatial extent of the potentially visible region (PVR) defined by the parent culling stage; the cone aperture angle $\theta$ for shading calculations.
*   **Output:** A binary visibility mask for each culling block, determining whether the block is passed to the extraction stage.

**Data Structures**
*   **Culling Block:** A 3D region of cells. The standard configuration uses a block size of $8 \times 8 \times 8$ cells [S2, unofficial].
*   **Occlusion Buffer:** A 2D depth buffer generated by rasterizing proxy geometry (e.g., bounding boxes) of the potentially visible blocks.
*   **Sampling Grid:** A 2D grid overlaid on the occlusion buffer to determine visibility. Tested resolutions include $2 \times 2$ and $13 \times 13$ samples per block [S3].

**Parameters**
*   **Culling Block Size:** $8 \times 8 \times 8$ cells [S2, unofficial]. This aligns with the leaf-level granularity of the ray-marching reference implementation to ensure fair performance comparison [S2, unofficial].
*   **Extraction Block Size:** $8 \times 8 \times 4$ cells [S2, unofficial].
*   **Task Shader Workgroups:** 2 workgroups are launched per potentially visible region to cover the $8 \times 8 \times 8$ culling block, accounting for the mismatch between culling granularity ($8 \times 8 \times 8$) and extraction granularity ($8 \times 8 \times 4$) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Sampling Resolution:** The number of samples taken from the occlusion buffer per culling block. Values of $2 \times 2$ and $13 \times 13$ are used [S3].

**Steps**
1.  **Define Culling Regions:** Partition the volume into regions of size $8 \times 8 \times 8$ cells [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Launch Task Shaders:** For each potentially visible region, launch two task shader workgroups. Each workgroup processes a subset of the extraction blocks ($8 \times 8 \times 4$) that fit within the $8 \times 8 \times 8$ culling region [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Rasterize Proxy Geometry:** Rasterize the bounding volumes of the blocks in the region to generate the occlusion buffer [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Sample Occlusion Buffer:** Determine the visibility of the block by sampling the occlusion buffer. The resolution of this sampling grid is chosen based on the desired anti-aliasing quality [S3].
    *   For low cone aperture angles ($\theta$), the occlusion extent is small, and lower resolutions (e.g., $2 \times 2$) suffice [S3].
    *   For high cone aperture angles (e.g., $\theta = 85^\circ$), the occlusion extent is large, and higher resolutions (e.g., $13 \times 13$) are required to reduce aliasing [S3].
5.  **Filter Blocks:** Discard blocks determined to be fully occluded by the sampling result.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Aliasing:** Using a low sampling resolution for high $\theta$ values results in significant aliasing artifacts because the cone base covers many texels in the occlusion buffer [S3].
*   **Granularity Mismatch:** The extraction stage operates on $8 \times 8 \times 4$ blocks, while culling is performed on $8 \times 8 \times 8$ regions. This requires launching multiple workgroups per region to ensure all extraction blocks are processed [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Block-Based Occlusion Culling Granularity
A configuration that aligns the culling granularity with the leaf-level of the ray-marching reference octree ($8 \times 8 \times 8$ cells) to ensure a fair performance comparison between the rasterization-based approach and the reference [S2, unofficial][S1]. It requires launching two task shader workgroups per region to accommodate the $8 \times 8 \times 4$ extraction block size [S2, unofficial]. Choose this when benchmarking against ray-marching baselines.

### Occlusion Buffer Sampling Resolution
Defines the density of samples taken from the occlusion buffer to determine block visibility. Higher resolutions (e.g., $13 \times 13$ vs $2 \times 2$) reduce aliasing, particularly for large cone aperture angles ($\theta$) where the occlusion extent covers many texels [S3]. Choose higher resolutions when visual quality is prioritized over the minimal overhead of additional samples.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The cost is dominated by the rasterization of proxy geometry and the sampling of the occlusion buffer. The number of task shader workgroups is fixed at 2 per region regardless of the actual number of blocks, due to the granularity mismatch [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the occlusion buffer. The sampling grid resolution affects cache efficiency but not the primary buffer size.
*   **Strengths:** Aligning with the reference octree leaf level allows for direct, fair comparison with ray-marching methods [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** The fixed granularity ($8 \times 8 \times 8$) may not be optimal for all scene densities. High sampling resolutions are necessary for high $\theta$ values to avoid aliasing, increasing computational cost [S3].
*   **When to use:** When comparing against ray-marching baselines or when the scene structure matches the $8 \times 8 \times 8$ leaf level.
*   **When not to use:** When the scene has significant variation in density that does not align with the fixed block size, or when high $\theta$ shading is required but performance is critical (low sampling resolution may be insufficient).

## Background
The concept of occlusion culling granularity in this context originates from research on efficient direct isosurface rasterization of scalar volumes. The specific configuration of $8 \times 8 \times 8$ culling blocks and $8 \times 8 \times 4$ extraction blocks was introduced to facilitate a fair comparison with a ray-marching reference implementation [S1][S2, unofficial]. The impact of sampling resolution on aliasing in directional occlusion shading models was further detailed in subsequent work on interactive direct volume rendering [S3].
