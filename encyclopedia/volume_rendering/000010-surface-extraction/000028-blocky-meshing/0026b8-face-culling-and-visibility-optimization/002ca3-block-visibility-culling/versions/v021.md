# Block Visibility Culling

Also called: Block Coordinate Change Detection, Block Rendering & Surface Orientation Culling, Camera-relative Block Culling, renderBlock

## Overview
Block Visibility Culling is a coarse-grained optimization technique that determines whether an entire voxel block (chunk) requires mesh regeneration or rendering based on the camera's position and orientation [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of unnecessary geometry updates by skipping index processing when the camera remains within the same logical block and maintains its primary viewing direction [S1, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that operate at the voxel or face level, this method operates at the block level, leveraging the fact that local surface visibility relative to a block does not change if the camera does not cross block boundaries or significantly alter its viewing angle [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method determines block visibility by comparing the current camera state against the previous frame's state. If the camera has not moved to a new block or changed its dominant viewing axis, the block's mesh indices are preserved, avoiding the cost of culling individual faces [S1, unofficial]. If the state changes, the method calculates the surface orientation relative to the camera and updates the visibility flags for the block's faces [S2, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   Camera world position $(P_x, P_y, P_z)$.
    *   Camera rotation angle $\theta$ (in degrees, typically yaw).
    *   Previous frame's camera block coordinates $(B_{x,prev}, B_{y,prev}, B_{z,prev})$ and greatest viewing direction `greatestDir_prev`.
    *   Current frame's camera block coordinates $(B_{x,curr}, B_{y,curr}, B_{z,curr})$.
    *   Current frame's greatest viewing direction `greatestDir_curr`.
    *   Block dimensions: `BLOCK_SIZE` (typically 16 or 32).
    *   Block face visibility flags: `visibleFaces` array or enum containing `YNeg`, `XPos`, `YPos`, `XNeg`, `ZNeg`, `ZPos` [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs**:
    *   Boolean flag indicating if the block's mesh needs regeneration.
    *   Updated `visibleFaces` flags for the block.

### Data Structures
*   **Block Visibility State**: A structure or set containing the camera block coordinates and the dominant viewing direction (e.g., `greatestDir` indicating which axis the camera is primarily facing).
*   **Face Flags**: An enumeration or bitmask array `BlockFace` with 6 entries corresponding to the six faces of a cube [S2, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Compute Camera Block Coordinates**:
    Calculate the integer block coordinates containing the camera by flooring the world position divided by `BLOCK_SIZE` [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ B_x = \lfloor P_x / \text{BLOCK\_SIZE} \rfloor $$
    $$ B_y = \lfloor P_y / \text{BLOCK\_SIZE} \rfloor $$
    $$ B_z = \lfloor P_z / \text{BLOCK\_SIZE} \rfloor $$

2.  **Check for State Change**:
    Compare the current block coordinates $(B_x, B_y, B_z)$ and the greatest viewing direction `greatestDir` with the values from the previous frame [S1, unofficial].
    *   If $B_x = B_{x,prev}$ AND $B_y = B_{y,prev}$ AND $B_z = B_{z,prev}$ AND `greatestDir` = `greatestDir_prev`, skip mesh update and return [S1, unofficial].
    *   Otherwise, proceed to update visibility.

3.  **Calculate Surface Orientation**:
    Determine the vertical position of the camera relative to the block to identify the primary surface orientation [S2, unofficial]:
    *   Calculate block vertical bounds:
        $$ Z_{bottom} = B_z \times \text{BLOCK\_SIZE} $$
        $$ Z_{top} = Z_{bottom} + \text{BLOCK\_SIZE} $$
    *   Determine `surfaceOrientation`:
        *   If $P_z < Z_{bottom}$, `surfaceOrientation` = 1 (looking down at floor).
        *   If $P_z > Z_{top}$, `surfaceOrientation` = 2 (looking up at ceiling).
        *   Otherwise, `surfaceOrientation` = 0 (looking at walls) [S2, unofficial].

4.  **Transform to Camera Space**:
    For each of the four vertical faces (walls), calculate the screen-space coordinates relative to the camera [S2, unofficial]:
    *   Compute rotation components:
        $$ c_s = \cos(\theta \times \pi / 180) $$
        $$ s_n = \sin(\theta \times \pi / 180) $$
    *   For each corner $w \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ of the block's base:
        *   Get world coordinates of the bottom corner $(x_{bottom}, y_{bottom})$.
        *   Translate to camera space:
            $$ x_1 = x_{bottom} - P_x $$
            $$ y_1 = y_{bottom} - P_y $$
        *   If `surfaceOrientation` == 1 (floor), swap $x_1$ and $y_1$ [S2, unofficial].
        *   Project to camera-relative X:
            $$ wallX[w][0] = x_1 \times c_s - y_1 \times s_n $$
        *   (Note: Y projection is handled similarly for screen height, though specific formulas for Y are implied by the context of perspective projection in [S2, unofficial]) [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Update Face Visibility Flags**:
    Based on the camera's position and orientation, determine which faces of the block are potentially visible. A face is marked visible if it is not occluded by the block itself or the camera's position [S2, unofficial].
    *   Iterate through the four wall faces ($w = 0$ to $3$) [S2, unofficial]:
        *   If $w=0$ and `visibleFaces[YNeg]` is false, skip.
        *   If $w=1$ and `visibleFaces[XPos]` is false, skip.
        *   If $w=2$ and `visibleFaces[YPos]` is false, skip.
        *   If $w=3$ and `visibleFaces[XNeg]` is false, skip [S2, unofficial].
    *   The `visibleFaces` flags are updated based on whether the face normal points towards the camera or if the face is on the boundary of the visible volume [S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   `BLOCK_SIZE`: The size of a block in world units. Typical values are 16 or 32 [S2, unofficial].
*   `surfaceScale`: A multiplier for floor/ceiling projection, typically 120 [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision**: Floating-point errors in position comparison may cause flickering if the camera is exactly on a block boundary. Using epsilon comparisons or consistent flooring is recommended [G].
*   **Degenerate Input**: If the camera is exactly at $Z_{bottom}$ or $Z_{top}$, the orientation logic must handle the boundary condition consistently to avoid toggling between floor/ceiling and wall modes [S2, unofficial].
*   **Rotation Ambiguity**: The "greatest viewing direction" must be robustly defined to avoid unnecessary updates when the camera rotates slightly within the same block [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Voxel Face Visibility Check](6723-voxel-face-visibility-check.md)
Determines visibility of individual faces within a block based on geometric relationship with the viewer. It operates at a finer granularity than Block Visibility Culling, which works at the block level. Choose this when fine-grained optimization is needed within a block that is already determined to be visible.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(1)$ per block for the visibility check, as it involves simple coordinate comparisons and trigonometric calculations [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$ per block to store the visibility state [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance**: Significantly reduces mesh generation overhead when the camera is stationary or moving slowly within a block [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Simple to implement, low computational cost, effective for static or slow-moving cameras [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Does not account for occlusion by other blocks or terrain features; only considers camera position and orientation [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: Ideal for voxel engines where blocks are large and camera movement is discrete or slow [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: In scenes with fast-moving cameras or complex occlusion scenarios where face-level culling is necessary [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
Block Visibility Culling originated in early voxel-based rendering systems, particularly in game development contexts where performance on limited hardware was critical [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is commonly used in Minecraft-like engines and other voxel-based games to optimize rendering by reducing the number of triangles submitted to the graphics pipeline [S1, S2, unofficial]. The method is often implemented alongside other culling techniques such as Face Culling and Backface Culling to achieve optimal performance [S1, S2, unofficial].
