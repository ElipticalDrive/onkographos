# Mask Projection Culling

Also called: Mesh Culling via Mask Projection

## Overview
Mask Projection Culling filters mesh vertices and faces by projecting them into multiple camera views and checking if they fall within dilated binary masks, removing geometry not visible in any view [S1, unofficial]. It differs from neighbor-based culling methods like [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by relying on explicit camera visibility rather than local voxel topology, making it suitable for complex, non-voxel-aligned geometry or when occlusion from arbitrary objects must be considered [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A mesh defined by vertices $V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ and faces $F \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 3}$ (indices into $V$).
- Camera parameters: `world_mats` ($4 \times 4$), `scale_mats` ($4 \times 4$), and intrinsics.
- Binary masks: $K$ images of resolution $W \times H$ (typically $1600 \times 1200$) stored as `mask_dir/*.png` [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A filtered mesh containing only vertices and faces visible in at least one camera view.

**Data Structures:**
- `valid`: Boolean tensor of shape $(N,)$ indicating if a vertex projects inside the NDC bounds of *all* cameras.
- `mask`: Boolean tensor of shape $(N,)$ indicating if a vertex projects inside the dilated mask of *all* cameras.
- `face_mask`: Boolean tensor of shape $(M,)$ indicating if all three vertices of a face are valid and masked.

**Steps:**
1. **Load Camera Parameters:** Extract `scale_mats` and `world_mats` from `cameras.npz`. Compute the projection matrix $P = \text{world\_mat} \times \text{scale\_mat}$. Decompose $P$ into intrinsic matrix $K$ and pose matrix $[R|t]$ using `rend_util.load_K_Rt_from_P(None, P)` [S1, unofficial].
2. **Load Masks:** Load binary masks from `mask_dir/*.png`. Hard-code image dimensions $W=1600$, $H=1200$ [S1, unofficial].
3. **Project Vertices:** For each camera $i$:
   - Compute inverse pose $W2C = \text{inverse}(pose_i)$ on GPU [S1, unofficial].
   - Transform vertices to camera space: $P_{cam} = K \times W2C \times V$.
   - Compute pixel coordinates: $pix\_coords = \frac{P_{cam}[0:2, :]}{P_{cam}[2, :] + \epsilon}$, where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by zero [S1, unofficial].
4. **Normalize to NDC:** Convert pixel coordinates to Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) space $[-1, 1]$:
   $$
   pix\_coords_{ndc} = \left( \frac{pix\_coords}{[W-1, H-1]^T} - 0.5 \right) \times 2
   $$
   [S1, unofficial]
5. **Compute Validity Mask:** Determine if points are inside the NDC bounds for all cameras:
   $$
   valid = \left( (pix\_coords_{ndc} > -1) \land (pix\_coords_{ndc} < 1) \right).all(dim=-1).float()
   $$
   [S1, unofficial]
6. **Dilate Masks:** Apply morphological dilation to each binary mask using a disk structuring element of radius 24. Convert to float tensor [S1, unofficial].
7. **Sample Masks:** Sample the dilated masks at the projected pixel coordinates using `F.grid_sample` with `mode='nearest'`, `padding_mode='zeros'`, and `align_corners=True` [S1, unofficial].
8. **Filter Vertices:** A vertex is kept if it is valid (inside NDC) and sampled inside the mask for all cameras:
   $$
   mask = (sampled\_masks > 0.).all(dim=-1).cpu().numpy()
   $$
   [S1, unofficial]
9. **Filter Faces:** A face is kept if all its vertices are in the final mask:
   $$
   face\_mask = mask[mesh.faces].all(axis=1)
   $$
   [S1, unofficial]
10. **Update and Export:** Remove faces not in `face_mask`. Update vertex list to remove unused vertices. Transform remaining vertices back to world space using `scale_mat[0,0]` and `scale_mat[:3,3]` [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $W, H$: Image width and height. Typical: $1600, 1200$ [S1, unofficial].
- Dilation radius: Radius of the disk used for mask dilation. Typical: $24$ [S1, unofficial].
- $\epsilon$: Epsilon for depth division. Typical: $10^{-6}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Division by zero in depth normalization is handled by $\epsilon$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Boundaries:** Points exactly on the NDC boundary ($-1$ or $1$) are excluded by strict inequality checks ($>$ and $<$) [S1, unofficial].
- **Degenerate Input:** If a camera has no associated mask or invalid pose, the projection may fail. The source code uses a try-except block to skip such cases [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### True Back-Face Culling via Group Masking
A back-face culling technique that splits chunk meshes into 6 vertex pool buckets based on face orientation (groups 0-5) [S2, unofficial]. Draw calls are masked based on the camera position relative to the chunk, preventing the draw call from being issued entirely rather than culling in the fragment shader [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when driver overhead must be minimized compared to splitting chunks into 6 sub-meshes [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$, where $N$ is the number of vertices and $K$ is the number of cameras. Projection and sampling are linear in vertices and cameras.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot K)$ for storing intermediate projection masks and sampled values.
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Removes geometry not visible in any view, reducing render load. Works with arbitrary camera positions and complex occlusions.
- **Limitations:** Requires pre-computed masks for all relevant views. Does not handle dynamic occlusion from non-camera objects unless masks are updated.
- **When to use:** When visibility is determined by a fixed set of camera viewpoints and masks are available.
- **When not to use:** When real-time dynamic occlusion from arbitrary geometry is required without pre-computed masks.

## Background
Originated in the context of 2D Gaussian Splatting evaluation and mesh filtering [S1, unofficial]. The technique is used to filter out invisible geometry during the evaluation phase of novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial].
