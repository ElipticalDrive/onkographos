# Extraction of Surface Points (Sobierajski)
Also called: Sobierajski's method, Visible boundary voxel extraction

## Overview
Extraction of Surface Points (Sobierajski) is a direct volume rendering method that accelerates surface visualization by extracting only the visible boundary voxels from volume data, rather than rendering the entire volume or generating a full mesh [S1, unofficial]. It solves the performance bottleneck of direct volume rendering by reducing the number of projected primitives. Unlike [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md) which operates on a generated mesh geometry, this method operates directly on the voxel grid to identify visible surface points before rendering [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Saito's Fast Previewing Algorithm](#saitos-fast-previewing-algorithm) by extracting the full set of potentially visible boundary voxels (approximated by orthographic views) rather than a sparse subset for speed [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method identifies visible boundary voxels by checking visibility against a set of viewing directions, typically the six orthographic views (positive and negative X, Y, Z axes) [S1, unofficial]. It outputs a list of unique boundary voxels with attributes (coordinates, normal vectors) suitable for direct projection into the rendering engine [S1, unofficial].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input**: 3D volume data (scalar field), viewing directions (default: 6 orthographic).
*   **Output**: A list of visible boundary voxels, each containing position and normal vector.

### Data Structures
*   `BoundaryVoxelList`: A list of objects/structs.
    *   `position`: 3D integer grid coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
    *   `normal`: 3D float vector representing the surface normal at the voxel face.
    *   `density`: Original density value (for cutting plane rendering) [S1, unofficial].
*   `VisibilityMask`: A 3D boolean array or hash set indicating if a voxel has been marked as visible from any viewing direction [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Initialize Visibility**: Create a 3D boolean array `is_visible` initialized to `false` for all voxels in the grid.
2.  **Iterate Viewing Directions**: For each of the $N$ viewing directions $V_i$ (typically 6 orthographic directions):
    a.  **Project and Trace**: Project the volume onto a 2D plane perpendicular to $V_i$.
    b.  **Boundary Tracing**: Perform a 2D boundary tracing algorithm on the slices perpendicular to the current axis to identify surface voxels exposed to the viewer [S1, unofficial].
    c.  **Mark Visible**: For each identified boundary voxel $P$, set `is_visible[P] = true`. Store the normal vector corresponding to the face exposed to $V_i$.
3.  **Deduplicate**: Iterate through the grid. If `is_visible[P]` is true, add voxel $P$ and its associated normal(s) to the `BoundaryVoxelList`. Remove duplicates if a voxel is visible from multiple directions (keep all relevant normals or merge them) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Back-face Culling**: For each voxel in the list, calculate the dot product between its surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ and the current viewing direction $\mathbf{v}$:
    $$ \text{dot} = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v} $$
    If $\text{dot} > 0$, the voxel is a back-face relative to the viewer and is excluded from the final render list for that view [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Projection**: Project the remaining visible boundary voxels onto the image plane. To avoid holes between adjacent voxels, project each voxel onto multiple pixels depending on the viewing angle [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Rendering**:
    a.  Shade the projected boundary surface points using lighting conditions [S1, unofficial].
    b.  If cutting planes are used, render voxels intersected by the plane by projecting their original density values onto the image plane [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Holes in Projection**: Adjacent voxels may map to non-adjacent pixels, creating holes. Mitigation: Project each voxel onto multiple pixels based on viewing angle [S1, unofficial].
*   **Duplicate Voxels**: A voxel may be visible from multiple directions. The output list must remove duplicates or handle multiple normals per voxel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Back-face Culling**: Incorrectly calculating the dot product between normal and view direction can lead to rendering back-faces. The condition is $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v} > 0$ for culling [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Saito's Fast Previewing Algorithm
A variant that extracts a subset of boundary voxels according to a uniform distribution to increase rendering speed [S1, unofficial]. It converts extracted surface samples to geometrical primitives (like crosslines perpendicular to the surface normal) and projects them [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when a fast preview is needed rather than a high-quality final render [S1, unofficial].

### Trimmed Voxel Lists
A method that uses 3D points as geometric primitives, projecting exactly one point per visible surface cell to ensure image coverage without subdivision [S2, unofficial]. It differs from Dividing Cubes by avoiding recursive subdivision, achieving coverage by projecting a single point that spans up to three pixels [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Dominated by the 2D boundary tracing on $N$ views. For 6 orthographic views, it is $O(V)$ where $V$ is the number of voxels, but with a small constant factor due to early culling.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(V)$ to store the volume and the visibility mask. The output list size is proportional to the surface area.
*   **Strengths**: Fast direct rendering; simple implementation; supports cutting planes easily [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Approximate visibility (6 views may miss some surfaces); potential for holes if not projected correctly; less accurate than marching cubes for complex surfaces [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use**: Real-time previewing, medical imaging with cutting planes, simple volume visualization [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use**: High-fidelity surface rendering, complex internal structures, non-orthographic views [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Proposed by Sobierajski [116] in the context of medical imaging systems, this method supports flexible manipulation of extracted voxels, such as simultaneous rendering of shaded isosurfaces and cross-sectional slices [S1, unofficial]. It is a direct volume rendering approach that bridges the gap between full volume rendering and surface extraction [S1, unofficial].
