# Extraction of Surface Points (Sobierajski)
Also called: Sobierajski's method, Visible boundary voxel extraction

## Overview
A fast display method for the direct rendering of boundary surfaces by extracting only visible boundary voxels from volume data [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by operating in object space to generate a pre-filtered list of surface points (with attributes like normals) rather than generating faces directly, and by explicitly projecting these points to multiple pixels to prevent holes caused by non-adjacent voxel mapping [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Volume data (density field), viewing directions (typically six orthographic views: $\pm X, \pm Y, \pm Z$) [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A list of unique boundary voxels with attributes (coordinates, normal vectors) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Boundary Voxel List:** A dynamic list storing tuples of `(x, y, z, nx, ny, nz)`.
*   **Visibility Mask:** Implicitly derived from the volume data during slice processing.

**Steps:**
1.  **Visibility Determination:** For each of the chosen viewing directions, determine which boundary voxels are visible. For the standard six orthographic views, this is performed efficiently using a 2D boundary tracing algorithm on slices perpendicular to each coordinate axis [S1, unofficial].
    *   *Note:* The specific 2D boundary tracing algorithm is not detailed in the sources, but it identifies the exposed faces of voxels relative to the viewing direction [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Extraction and Deduplication:** Extract the coordinates of all visible boundary voxels. Remove duplicate elements from the list to ensure each voxel is processed once [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Attribute Assignment:** For each voxel in the deduplicated list, calculate and store necessary rendering attributes, specifically the surface normal vector [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Backface Culling (Voxel Culling):** Reduce the voxel list size for a specific viewing direction by removing back-facing voxels.
    *   Let $\mathbf{n}$ be the surface normal of the voxel.
    *   Let $\mathbf{v}$ be the viewing direction vector (pointing from the eye to the scene, or vice versa depending on convention; the source implies standard backface logic).
    *   If $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v} > 0$ (assuming $\mathbf{v}$ points into the scene and $\mathbf{n}$ points outward, or consistent with standard backface culling where the dot product indicates the face is turned away), the voxel is a back face and is not rendered [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Projection:** Pass the set of surface points to the rendering engine. Project each voxel onto several pixels depending on the viewing direction to avoid holes in the produced image, as adjacent voxels do not necessarily map to adjacent pixels [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Shading and Cutting Planes:** Shade the projected boundary surface points according to lighting conditions. If cutting planes are used, render voxels intersected by the cutting plane by projecting their original density values onto the image plane [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Saito's Fast Previewing Algorithm
A previewing algorithm based on extracting a subset of boundary voxels according to a uniform distribution to increase rendering speed [S1, unofficial]. It differs from the main method by not extracting all visible voxels, but rather a sparse subset [S1, unofficial]. The extracted surface samples are converted to geometrical primitives (like crosslines perpendicular to the surface normal) and projected onto the image plane [S1, unofficial].

### Trimmed Voxel Lists
A surface rendering algorithm that uses 3D points as geometric primitives by projecting exactly one point per visible surface cell to ensure image coverage without subdivision [S2, unofficial]. It differs from methods like Dividing Cubes by avoiding recursive subdivision; it achieves pixel coverage by projecting a single point per cell that can span up to three pixels [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the 2D boundary tracing on slices and the projection step. The number of voxels to be projected is reduced by backface culling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing a list of boundary voxels with attributes. The size is proportional to the surface area of the object in voxels [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast direct rendering; flexible manipulation of extracted voxels (e.g., cutting planes); avoids holes through multi-pixel projection [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Approximate set of visible voxels (six views may miss some); holes can appear if multi-pixel projection is not used; geometric primitives only approximate surfaces [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Fast previewing or direct rendering where speed is prioritized over perfect geometric accuracy [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Proposed by Sobierajski [116] as a fast display method for direct rendering of boundary surfaces [S1, unofficial]. It is cited in the context of medical imaging systems where cutting operations are important [S1, unofficial]. The method is part of the broader class of object-order algorithms and hybrid acceleration methods in volume visualization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
