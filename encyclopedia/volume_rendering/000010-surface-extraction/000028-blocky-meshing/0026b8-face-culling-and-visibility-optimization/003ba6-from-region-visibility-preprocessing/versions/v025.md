# From-Region Visibility Preprocessing

Also called: Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing, Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing

## Overview
From-region visibility preprocessing is a technique that precomputes visibility relationships from specific viewpoints or regions to accelerate rendering by discarding occluded geometry. It differs from neighbor-based culling [923-voxel-block-placement-with-face-culling.md] by relying on global occlusion tests rather than local voxel adjacency, and from view-dependent culling [4569-voxel-backface-culling-and-internal-face-masking.md] by decoupling visibility determination from the runtime camera position.

## How it works
The method identifies triangles or primitives that are occluded from a set of predefined viewpoints (regions) and removes them from the renderable set.

**Inputs:**
- A 3D scene model (typically a triangle mesh).
- A set of predefined viewpoints or regions of interest.

**Outputs:**
- A visibility map or occlusion list indicating which primitives are visible from each region.

**Data Structures:**
- Not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1. Define the set of viewpoints or regions from which visibility is to be computed.
2. For each viewpoint/region, perform visibility tests against the scene geometry.
3. Identify occluded primitives.
4. Store the visibility results for runtime use.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Preprocessing times can be extremely long for large models. For example, Wonka et al. required 9 hours for a model with 8 million triangles [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al. required 33 hours for a model with 6 million triangles [S1, unofficial].
- Early implementations were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Chhugani et al. Visibility and LOD System
A system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy [S1, unofficial]. It is able to interactively render large datasets with less than one pixel of screen-space deviation and correct visibility. However, for a model with 13 million triangles, using a cluster of 16 PCs, the accuracy guarantee costs 128 hours of precomputation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Preprocessing times are significant. Reported times include 9 hours for 8 million triangles [S1, unofficial], 33 hours for 6 million triangles [S1, unofficial], and 128 hours for 13 million triangles [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Measured Performance:** Wonka et al. used 2 processors to overlap visibility computation and rendering at runtime [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Can handle complex occlusion relationships. Chhugani et al. achieve high image accuracy with less than one pixel of screen-space deviation [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Long preprocessing times. Early versions were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When high-quality visibility is required and preprocessing time is acceptable.
- **When not to use:** When real-time preprocessing is required or when the scene is too large for the available hardware.

## Background
The method was developed by Wonka et al. [152] and Durand et al. [41] [S1, unofficial]. Wonka et al. presented a from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm with occluder fusion [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al. presented a from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm that could handle 3D environments [S1, unofficial]. Chhugani et al. developed a system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region [S1, unofficial].
