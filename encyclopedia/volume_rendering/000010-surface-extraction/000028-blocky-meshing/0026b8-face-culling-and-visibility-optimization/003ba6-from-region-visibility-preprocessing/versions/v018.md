# From-Region Visibility Preprocessing

Also called: Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing, Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing

## Overview
From-region visibility preprocessing is a technique that precomputes which parts of a 3D scene are visible from specific regions of the camera space, allowing the renderer to discard occluded geometry during traversal. Unlike the parent method [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) which relies on local voxel neighbor checks, this approach operates on the global geometric structure (typically triangle meshes) to determine visibility from a viewpoint region rather than a single point or local face. It sets itself apart by enabling significant culling of large-scale occluded geometry, though it historically required substantial preprocessing time compared to on-the-fly methods.

## How it works
The method involves constructing a visibility structure that maps regions of the view space to subsets of the scene geometry. While the specific algorithmic details for the core "from-region" logic are not fully detailed in the provided notes, the implementation generally follows these principles based on the cited works:

1.  **Region Definition**: The view space is partitioned into regions (e.g., frusta or grid cells).
2.  **Occluder Fusion**: Geometry is processed to identify occluders. Wonka et al. [S1, unofficial] utilized occluder fusion to simplify the visibility computation.
3.  **Visibility Computation**: For each region, the algorithm determines the subset of triangles that are potentially visible. This often involves projecting geometry or using conservative visibility bounds.
4.  **Storage**: The resulting visibility data (e.g., bitmasks or lists of visible triangles per region) is stored for runtime lookup.

**Parameters and Constraints**:
-   **Dataset Type**: Early implementations were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].
-   **Preprocessing Time**: The method is computationally expensive during the preprocessing phase.
-   **Hardware**: Can be parallelized; Wonka et al. used 2 processors to overlap visibility computation and rendering [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls**:
-   **Preprocessing Overhead**: The method requires long preprocessing times. For a model with 8 million triangles, preprocessing took 9 hours [S1, unofficial]. For a 6 million triangle model, it took 33 hours [S1, unofficial].
-   **Dimensionality Limits**: Initial versions were restricted to 2.5D environments [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Chhugani et al. Visibility and LOD System
A system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy [S1, unofficial]. It guarantees less than one pixel of screen-space deviation and correct visibility. For a model with 13 million triangles, using a cluster of 16 PCs, the accuracy guarantee costs 128 hours of precomputation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity**: Preprocessing time is high, scaling with the complexity of the model (number of triangles) and the number of regions. Reported times range from 9 to 128 hours for models with 6–13 million triangles [S1, unofficial].
-   **Memory Complexity**: Not given in the sources.
-   **Strengths**: Enables interactive rendering of large datasets by reducing the number of triangles processed per frame. Can achieve high image accuracy [S1, unofficial].
-   **Limitations**: Long preprocessing times make it less suitable for dynamic scenes or rapid iteration. Early versions were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].
-   **When to use**: Suitable for static, large-scale datasets where preprocessing time is acceptable and interactive frame rates are required.
-   **When not to use**: Unsuitable for dynamic scenes or when preprocessing time is a bottleneck.

## Background
The concept of from-region visibility preprocessing was introduced to address the limitations of earlier methods like Teller et al., which required long preprocessing times and assumed axis-aligned cells [S1, unofficial]. Wonka et al. [152] presented a from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm with occluder fusion, which was limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al. [41] extended this to handle 3D environments, though it still required significant preprocessing time (33 hours for 6 million triangles) [S1, unofficial]. Chhugani et al. [20] further developed the system to include levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy [S1, unofficial].
