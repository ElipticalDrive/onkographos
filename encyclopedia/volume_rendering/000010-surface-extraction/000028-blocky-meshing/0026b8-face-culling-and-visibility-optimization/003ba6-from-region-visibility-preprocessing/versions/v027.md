# From-Region Visibility Preprocessing

Also called: Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing, Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing

## Overview
From-region visibility preprocessing is a technique that precomputes which geometric primitives are visible from a specific region of the viewing space, allowing for aggressive culling of occluded geometry during rendering [S1, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods like [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md) or [Block Visibility Culling](5334-block-visibility-culling.md) by operating on large-scale scene geometry (often millions of triangles) rather than local voxel occupancy, and by focusing on global occlusion relationships rather than local surface continuity [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method involves precomputing visibility relationships between regions of the scene and the geometry within them. While specific algorithmic steps for the core visibility computation are not detailed in the provided sources, the process is characterized by its heavy preprocessing phase and its ability to handle complex 3D environments [S1, unofficial].

1.  **Region Definition**: The viewing space or scene is partitioned into regions. The specific partitioning strategy (e.g., grid, hierarchical) is not specified in the sources [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Visibility Precomputation**: For each region, the system determines which triangles or geometric primitives are visible. This step involves complex occlusion testing [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Occluder Fusion**: In the Wonka et al. variant, occluder fusion is used to simplify the set of occluding geometry, reducing the complexity of the visibility data [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Runtime Rendering**: At runtime, the visible set for the current camera region is retrieved and rendered [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters and Constraints:**
*   **Preprocessing Time**: Extremely high. Reported times include 9 hours for 8 million triangles [S1, unofficial], 33 hours for 6 million triangles [S1, unofficial], and 128 hours for 13 million triangles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Hardware**: Can utilize multiple processors or clusters (e.g., 16 PCs) to reduce precomputation time [S1, unofficial].
*   **Dataset Type**: Early versions were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial]. Later versions (Durand et al.) handled full 3D environments [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Long Preprocessing**: The primary drawback is the prohibitive preprocessing time for large models [S1, unofficial].
*   **2.5D Limitation**: Initial implementations were restricted to 2.5D datasets, limiting applicability to full 3D scenes [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing
A from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm with occluder fusion [S1, unofficial]. It used 2 processors to overlap visibility computation and rendering at runtime [S1, unofficial]. It required long preprocessing times (9 hours for a model with 8 million triangles) and was limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial]. Later work by Wonka et al. [153] used a from-point approach needing little preprocessing, but only reported results for in-core, 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].

### Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing
A from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm that could handle 3D environments, as opposed to 2.5D [S1, unofficial]. However, the algorithm required long preprocessing times (33 hours for a model with 6 million triangles) [S1, unofficial].

### Chhugani et al. Visibility and LOD System
A system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy [S1, unofficial]. It is able to interactively render large datasets with less than one pixel of screen-space deviation and correct visibility [S1, unofficial]. For a model with 13 million triangles, using a cluster of 16 PCs, the accuracy guarantee costs 128 hours of precomputation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Preprocessing Time**: Very high. Ranges from 9 to 128 hours depending on model size (6M to 13M triangles) and hardware [S1, unofficial].
*   **Runtime Performance**: Enables interactive rendering by reducing the number of triangles sent to the GPU [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory**: Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths**: Can handle large datasets with complex geometry; provides correct visibility and high image accuracy [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Prohibitive preprocessing times; early versions limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use**: When interactive rendering of very large, complex static scenes is required and preprocessing time is acceptable [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use**: For dynamic scenes or when rapid iteration is needed due to long preprocessing times [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The technique was developed in the context of out-of-core visualization of large datasets [S1, unofficial]. Wonka et al. [152] presented the initial from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm with occluder fusion [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al. [41] extended this to 3D environments [S1, unofficial]. Chhugani et al. [20] developed a system incorporating levels of detail [S1, unofficial]. These methods contrast with earlier approaches like Teller et al., which also required long preprocessing times but assumed axis-aligned cells [S1, unofficial].
