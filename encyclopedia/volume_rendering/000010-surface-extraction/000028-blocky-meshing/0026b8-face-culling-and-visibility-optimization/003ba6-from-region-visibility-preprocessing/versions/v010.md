# From-Region Visibility Preprocessing

Also called: Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing, Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing

## Overview
From-region visibility preprocessing is a technique that precomputes the set of visible primitives for specific regions of a scene to accelerate rendering. It solves the problem of expensive per-frame visibility determination for large datasets by shifting the computational load to an offline phase. Unlike [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md), which typically relies on immediate geometric adjacency or simple backface tests, this method uses spatial partitioning and occluder fusion to determine visibility relative to viewing regions, allowing for significant reduction in draw calls at the cost of substantial preprocessing time [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates by partitioning the scene into regions and determining which primitives are visible from within those regions.

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D scene model (typically represented as a mesh or voxelized structure) and a set of viewing regions or camera positions.
*   **Output:** A visibility map or list associating each region with the subset of primitives visible from that region.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Region Grid:** A spatial partitioning structure (e.g., a grid or octree) defining the viewing regions.
*   **Visibility List:** For each region, a list of primitive IDs or indices that are visible.
*   **Occluder Data:** Information about primitives that block visibility, often fused to reduce redundancy.

**Steps:**
1.  **Scene Partitioning:** Divide the scene into manageable regions. The granularity depends on the desired accuracy and preprocessing time [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Visibility Computation:** For each region, determine which primitives are visible. This involves:
    *   **Occluder Fusion:** Combining overlapping occluders to simplify the visibility test [S1, unofficial].
    *   **Ray Casting or Frustum Culling:** Using rays or frustums from the region to test against scene primitives.
3.  **Preprocessing:** Store the computed visibility maps. This step is computationally intensive [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Runtime Rendering:** During rendering, identify the current viewing region and retrieve the precomputed visibility list to render only the necessary primitives [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Region Size:** Smaller regions increase accuracy but increase preprocessing time and memory usage.
*   **Occluder Fusion Threshold:** Determines how aggressively occluders are merged.

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Preprocessing Time:** The method requires significant preprocessing time, which can be prohibitive for large models [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Usage:** Storing visibility maps for many regions can consume large amounts of memory.
*   **Dynamic Scenes:** The method is less suitable for dynamic scenes where the geometry changes frequently, as the visibility maps would need to be recomputed.

## Subtopics

### Chhugani et al. Visibility and LOD System
A system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy. It is able to interactively render large datasets with less than one pixel of screen-space deviation and correct visibility. However, for a model with 13 million triangles, using a cluster of 16 PCs, the accuracy guarantee costs 128 hours of precomputation [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Preprocessing time is high, often taking hours for large models (e.g., 9 hours for 8 million triangles, 33 hours for 6 million triangles) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** High memory usage due to the storage of visibility maps for each region.
*   **Performance:** Enables real-time or near-real-time rendering by reducing the number of primitives processed per frame.
*   **Strengths:** Significant reduction in rendering complexity for static scenes; high accuracy in visibility determination.
*   **Limitations:** Long preprocessing times; limited to static or slowly changing scenes; high memory requirements.
*   **When to Use:** Ideal for large, static datasets where rendering performance is critical and preprocessing time is acceptable.
*   **When Not to Use:** Unsuitable for dynamic scenes or scenarios requiring rapid updates to the visibility data.

## Background
The concept of from-region visibility preprocessing was introduced by Durand et al. [41] and Wonka et al. [152, 153]. Durand et al. presented an algorithm capable of handling 3D environments, while Wonka et al. initially focused on 2.5D datasets with occluder fusion. Later work by Wonka et al. explored a from-point approach with less preprocessing. Chhugani et al. [20] extended the concept by integrating levels of detail. These methods were developed to address the scalability issues of out-of-core visualization for large datasets [S1, unofficial].
