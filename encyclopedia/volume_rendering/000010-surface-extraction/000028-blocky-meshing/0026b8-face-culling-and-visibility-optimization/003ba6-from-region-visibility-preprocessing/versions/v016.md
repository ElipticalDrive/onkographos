# From-Region Visibility Preprocessing

Also called: Durand et al. Visibility Preprocessing, Wonka et al. Visibility Preprocessing

## Overview
From-region visibility preprocessing is a technique that precomputes visibility relationships from specific viewpoints or regions to optimize rendering performance by avoiding the processing of occluded geometry. It differs from standard face culling [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md) by operating at a higher level of abstraction, often involving complex occluder fusion and region-based data structures, which allows for significant speedups in interactive walkthroughs but incurs substantial preprocessing overhead.

## How it works
The method involves constructing a visibility map or culling structure based on the scene geometry relative to predefined regions or viewpoints.

**Inputs:**
*   Scene geometry (typically triangle meshes).
*   Predefined regions or viewpoints from which visibility is computed.

**Outputs:**
*   A visibility structure (e.g., visibility maps, culling volumes) that indicates which parts of the scene are visible from specific regions.

**Data Structures:**
*   The specific layout of the visibility structure is not detailed in the sources, but it typically stores occlusion relationships between scene elements and viewing regions.

**Steps:**
1.  **Region Definition:** Define the regions or viewpoints from which visibility will be precomputed.
2.  **Visibility Computation:** For each region, determine which parts of the scene geometry are visible. This often involves ray casting or spatial partitioning to identify occluders.
3.  **Occluder Fusion:** Combine overlapping occluders to simplify the visibility data and reduce the number of culling primitives [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Structure Construction:** Build the final visibility structure that maps regions to visible/occluded geometry subsets.

**Parameters:**
*   Region size and distribution.
*   Precision of visibility computation.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Preprocessing Time:** The algorithm can require very long preprocessing times, making it less suitable for dynamic scenes or rapid iteration. For example, Wonka et al.'s algorithm required 9 hours for a model with 8 million triangles [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al.'s algorithm required 33 hours for a model with 6 million triangles [S1, unofficial].
*   **Dimensionality Limitations:** Early versions were limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Chhugani et al. Visibility and LOD System
A system that precomputes from-region visibility and levels of detail per region, focusing on image accuracy. It guarantees less than one pixel of screen-space deviation and correct visibility. However, it has high preprocessing costs; for a model with 13 million triangles on a cluster of 16 PCs, precomputation took 128 hours [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Preprocessing Time:** Very high. Reported times range from 9 hours (8 million triangles) to 128 hours (13 million triangles on 16 PCs) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Runtime Performance:** Can enable interactive rendering by reducing the number of triangles processed per frame. Wonka et al. used 2 processors to overlap visibility computation and rendering [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Usage:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** High image accuracy, correct visibility, potential for real-time performance after preprocessing.
*   **Limitations:** Extremely long preprocessing times, limited to static scenes, early versions limited to 2.5D [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Large static scenes where interactive performance is critical and preprocessing time is acceptable.
*   **When Not to Use:** Dynamic scenes, scenes requiring frequent updates, or when preprocessing time is a bottleneck.

## Background
The concept of from-region visibility preprocessing was introduced by Wonka et al. [152] and Durand et al. [41]. Wonka et al. presented a from-region visibility preprocessing algorithm with occluder fusion, initially limited to 2.5D datasets [S1, unofficial]. Durand et al. extended this to handle 3D environments [S1, unofficial]. Later work by Wonka et al. [153] explored a from-point approach with less preprocessing, but only for in-core, 2.5D datasets. Chhugani et al. [20] developed a system incorporating visibility and LOD per region, focusing on image accuracy [S1, unofficial].
