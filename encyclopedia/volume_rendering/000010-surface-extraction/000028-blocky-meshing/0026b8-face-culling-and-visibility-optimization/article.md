# Face Culling and Visibility Optimization

Also called: Interior Polygon Removal, Removing Interior Polygons

## Overview
Face Culling and Visibility Optimization reduces rendering load in voxel-based environments by reconstructing cubes from triangles and removing sides that are adjacent to solid neighbors, thereby avoiding the rendering of hidden geometry [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of naively rendering all 6 faces of every cube, including those hidden by neighbors, which wastes GPU cycles on interior polygons [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent [Blocky meshing](31-blocky-meshing.md) by focusing specifically on the removal of internal faces rather than merging adjacent coplanar faces, and from siblings like [Greedy face merging](8935-greedy-face-merging.md) by operating on a per-face visibility basis rather than geometric expansion.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism involves determining the visibility of each of the six faces of a voxel block by checking the occupancy state of its immediate neighbors in the 3D grid. A face is considered visible if the adjacent voxel in the direction of the face's normal is empty (air/void) or transparent, depending on the specific culling configuration. If a neighbor exists and is opaque, the shared face is internal and is culled. This process is typically performed during mesh generation or as an incremental update when blocks are placed or removed. The optimization relies on the assumption that voxels are axis-aligned cubes and that visibility is determined by local adjacency.

## Subtopics

### [Voxel Block Placement with Face Culling](923-voxel-block-placement-with-face-culling.md)
A dynamic meshing technique that maintains the visibility of voxel chunk faces by toggling shared geometry state during block insertion or removal, rather than regenerating the entire mesh. It differs from its parent by integrating the face visibility state directly into the block data structure's bit fields, allowing for $O(1)$ local updates instead of global reconstruction. It differs from siblings like [Face Coverage Mask Culling](3397-face-coverage-mask-culling.md) by relying on explicit neighbor occupancy checks within a fixed-size chunk rather than pre-computed coverage masks.

### [Face Coverage Mask Culling](3397-face-coverage-mask-culling.md)
A method for removing hidden faces of partial voxel shapes (such as stairs, slabs, or custom geometries) by representing face coverage as a bit mask. It solves the problem where a boolean "solid/empty" flag is insufficient because partial shapes have mixed visible and hidden areas on a single face. Unlike sibling methods that rely on coarse block visibility or vertex packing, this method operates at the sub-face granularity, allowing precise culling of geometry that is occluded by neighbors while preserving exposed portions of the same face.

### [ChunkMesh Vertex Packing and Management](3566-chunkmesh-vertex-packing-and-management.md)
A method for generating and maintaining dynamic meshes in voxel terrain by packing vertex attributes into compact 32-bit integers and managing buffer updates via a free-list of reusable vertex slots. It solves the problem of high memory bandwidth and buffer upload overhead in dynamic voxel worlds by minimizing the data transferred to the GPU per vertex and avoiding frequent reallocation. It builds on [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by providing the concrete data layout and incremental update logic required to implement face culling efficiently in a chunk-based architecture.

### [Voxel Backface Culling and Internal Face Masking](4569-voxel-backface-culling-and-internal-face-masking.md)
A visibility optimization technique for voxel-based rendering that rejects invisible faces by determining front/back orientation in projection space and masking faces shared between neighboring voxels. It differs from standard geometric backface culling by operating on voxel codes to simultaneously handle internal face rejection (faces between two solid voxels) and view-dependent backface culling, allowing for efficient geometry shader-based rendering.

### [Block Visibility Culling](5334-block-visibility-culling.md)
Determines which voxel blocks require mesh updates or rendering based on the camera's position and rotation, avoiding unnecessary geometry generation when the camera has not moved significantly or changed its primary viewing direction. Unlike sibling methods that operate at the fine-grained voxel face level [6723-voxel-face-visibility-check.md] or use coverage masks [3397-face-coverage-mask-culling.md], this method operates at the coarse block level, leveraging camera-relative orientation to cull entire block faces before they are processed into the mesh. It serves as a pre-filter for the parent [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by reducing the set of candidate blocks.

### [Extraction of Surface Points (Sobierajski)](5721-extraction-of-surface-points-sobierajski.md)
A fast display technique for the direct rendering of boundary surfaces by extracting only those boundary voxels visible from a predefined set of viewing directions. It solves the problem of rendering large volume datasets efficiently by reducing the geometric complexity passed to the renderer, avoiding the overhead of full volume raycasting or complex mesh generation. It differs from sibling methods like [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by operating in image space via projection of extracted points rather than constructing a triangle mesh in object space, and from [Saito's Fast Previewing Algorithm](#saitos-fast-previewing-algorithm) by extracting the full visible boundary subset rather than a sparse uniform sample.

### [Mask Projection Culling](6836-mask-projection-culling.md)
A visibility optimization technique that filters mesh vertices and faces by projecting them into multiple camera views and checking if they fall within dilated binary masks, thereby removing geometry not visible in any view. Unlike sibling methods that rely on static voxel occupancy or back-face normals, this method uses explicit camera projections and image-space masks to determine visibility, making it suitable for dynamic scenes or specific view-dependent optimizations. It builds on [Face Culling and Visibility Optimization](3861-face-culling-and-visibility-optimization.md) by replacing neighbor-based checks with projection-based masking.

### [Mesh Culling via 3D Projection and Connected Components](6963-mesh-culling-via-3d-projection-and-connected-components.md)
Culls mesh geometry by projecting vertices into multiple camera views and comparing their depths against reference depth maps to identify and remove unseen or artifact geometry, particularly from unbounded reconstructions like Gaussian Splatting. It distinguishes itself from local face-culling methods (e.g., [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md)) by using global visibility information from multiple viewpoints rather than local voxel adjacency, and from simple backface culling by explicitly handling occlusion and unbounded artifacts. It serves as a post-processing step for surface extraction, ensuring the final mesh represents only the visible, geometrically consistent surface.

### [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md)
A mesh generation optimization that reduces the number of vertices and triangles sent to the GPU by skipping the rendering of voxel faces that are adjacent to existing neighboring voxels. It solves the problem of naively rendering all 6 faces of every cube, including those hidden by neighbors. It is distinguished from its siblings by performing a direct neighbor lookup for each face during mesh creation, rather than using bitwise bulk operations or complex visibility masks [3397-face-coverage-mask-culling.md].

### [Opacity-based Mesh Culling](8759-opacity-based-mesh-culling.md)
Removes geometric primitives from a scene representation based on their contribution to the final image's opacity, rather than their spatial position or adjacency. Unlike sibling methods that rely on voxel occupancy or block visibility, this approach operates on the radiance field's output, discarding triangles that are effectively transparent. It is primarily used to convert neural radiance fields or dense point clouds into lightweight polygonal meshes for real-time rendering, significantly reducing triangle count and storage costs.

### [From-Region Visibility Preprocessing](9048-from-region-visibility-preprocessing.md)
A technique that precomputes which parts of a 3D scene are visible from specific regions of the camera space, allowing for aggressive culling of occluded geometry during rendering. It differs from standard face culling by operating on large geometric clusters or regions rather than individual voxel faces, and it often incorporates occluder fusion to reduce the complexity of the visibility test. Unlike [Face Culling](8735-face-culling.md) which relies on local adjacency, this method solves the problem of global occlusion for large datasets, albeit at the cost of significant preprocessing time.

### Buried Bitmask Optimization
A bitmask used to track which blocks in a chunk are 'buried' (fully surrounded by opaque blocks), allowing the renderer to skip mesh generation for those blocks [S2, unofficial]. The process involves resetting the buried bitmask for the chunk, creating `MappedAdjacentChunks` for the current chunk position to access neighboring chunks, and iterating through all blocks in the chunk (0 to `CHUNK_SIZE`) to determine if each block is buried by calling `block->isBuried(...)` and setting the corresponding flag in the bitmask [S2, unofficial]. If all blocks in the chunk are buried (checked via `buriedBitmask->AreAllBlocksSet()`), the mesh is marked as empty and mesh generation is skipped [S2, unofficial]. When generating the mesh, any block that is air or has its buried flag set in the bitmask is skipped [S2, unofficial].

### Two-pass transparent meshing with opaque separation
A meshing strategy that separates opaque and transparent geometry into two passes to ensure correct rendering order and reduce overdraw, by generating opaque faces between air/transparent blocks and transparent faces only for blocks touching air [S3, unofficial]. The first pass (opaque) generates faces for opaque blocks; if the chunk contains transparent blocks, it tests for opaque blocks (ignoring transparent and air) to generate opaque faces between air and transparent blocks, otherwise it tests for non-air blocks [S3, unofficial]. The second pass (transparent) tests for non-air blocks to mark only transparent blocks, generating faces for all transparent blocks touching air but not adding faces between transparent and solid blocks (since solid blocks already have those faces) [S3, unofficial]. Solid block types are filtered out during the transparent pass by checking if the current block type is transparent before constructing the AO value and adding the block type to the hashmap [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The technique originated with the need to optimize rendering in voxel-based games like Minecraft, where brute-force instantiation of cube models resulted in excessive triangle counts [S1, unofficial]. The initial optimization involved reconstructing cubes from triangles and removing sides adjacent to solid neighbors, reducing the rendering load from 12 triangles per side to 2 triangles per visible side [S1, unofficial]. This approach was implemented by creating a class that generates cubes from triangles and checks for solid neighbors to determine which sides to render [S1, unofficial]. To further optimize, meshes of all cubes were combined into a single big mesh called a 'chunk' [S1, unofficial]. This introduced constraints related to vertex limits (e.g., ~65,000 vertices in Unity) and required handling of neighboring chunks to ensure consistent face culling across chunk boundaries [S1, unofficial].
