# Mesh Culling via 3D Projection and Connected Components

Also called: Mesh Culling via Multi-View Depth Projection, cull with 3d projection

## Overview
This method culls mesh geometry by projecting vertices into multiple camera views and comparing projected depths against reference depth maps to identify and remove artifacts from unbounded reconstructions [S1, unofficial]. It classifies points as seen, unseen, or forecast, retaining only faces where all vertices are consistently visible across a minimum number of cameras [S1, unofficial]. It differs from adjacency-based culling by relying on external viewing geometry rather than local voxel occupancy, making it suitable for post-processing meshes derived from unbounded radiance fields [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `mesh`: A triangle mesh with vertices $V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ and faces $F \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 3}$.
*   `cameras`: A list of $K$ camera poses (world-to-camera matrices $W_i$) and intrinsic parameters ($H, W, f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y$).
*   `depth_maps`: Reference depth maps $D_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ for each camera pose, typically extracted from the source reconstruction or rendered from the mesh itself.
*   `forecast_radius` ($r$): Optional expansion for the "forecast" frustum.
*   `min_views` ($N_{min}$): Minimum number of cameras required to classify a point as "seen" (default 20).
*   `eps`: Depth comparison tolerance (default 0.005).
*   `remove_small_geometry_threshold` ($\alpha$): Minimum area ratio to keep a component (default 0.2).

**Outputs:**
*   `culled_mesh`: A mesh with removed faces and unreferenced vertices.

**Data Structures:**
*   `valid`: Boolean tensor of shape $(N)$, true if vertex is seen in $\ge N_{min}$ cameras.
*   `valid_forecast`: Boolean tensor of shape $(N)$, true if vertex is in the forecast frustum.
*   `face_mask`: Boolean tensor of shape $(M)$, true if all three vertices of a face are valid.

**Steps:**

1.  **Extract Reference Depth Maps:**
    Render forward depth maps for all camera poses using a rasterizer (e.g., PyRender) with flags `OFFSCREEN`, `DEPTH_ONLY`, and `SKIP_CULL_FACES` [S1, unofficial]. Let $D_i$ be the depth map for camera $i$.

2.  **Initialize Masks:**
    Initialize `valid`, `valid_num`, and `valid_forecast` as zero tensors of shape $(N)$ on the target device [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Iterate Cameras:**
    For each camera $i$ with pose $C_i$ (camera-to-world) and intrinsics $K_i$:
    a. **Coordinate Transformation:**
       Convert $C_i$ to world-to-camera $W_i$. Negate the y and z rows of the rotation matrix part of $C_i$ to align with the expected coordinate system [S1, unofficial].
       $$ W_i = C_i^{-1} $$
    b. **Project Vertices:**
       Append ones to $V$ to form homogeneous coordinates $V_h \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 4}$.
       $$ P_{cam} = V_h \cdot W_i^T $$
       Extract the first three rows to get camera-space coordinates $(x_c, y_c, z_c)$ [S1, unofficial].
    c. **Intrinsic Projection:**
       Apply intrinsic matrix $K_i$:
       $$ \begin{bmatrix} u' \\ v' \\ z \end{bmatrix} = K_i \begin{bmatrix} x_c \\ y_c \\ z_c \end{bmatrix} $$
       Compute normalized UV coordinates:
       $$ u = \frac{u'}{z + \epsilon}, \quad v = \frac{v'}{z + \epsilon} $$
       where $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ [S1, unofficial].
    d. **Frustum Check:**
       Determine if points are within the standard frustum:
       $$ \text{in\_frustum} = (0 \le u < W) \land (0 \le v < H) \land (z > 0) $$
       Determine if points are within the expanded forecast frustum (if $r > 0$):
       $$ \text{forecast\_frustum} = (-r \le u < W+r) \land (-r \le v < H+r) \land (z > 0) $$
    e. **Depth Sampling:**
       Reshape $D_i$ to $(1, 1, H, W)$. Normalize UV grid to $[-1, 1]$ for bilinear interpolation. Sample depth at $(u, v)$:
       $$ d_{sample} = \text{GridSample}(D_i, (u, v), \text{padding}=\text{'border'}) $$
    f. **Front-Face Validation:**
       A point is considered "seen" if the sampled depth is positive and the projected depth is closer than or equal to the sampled depth (within tolerance):
       $$ \text{is\_front} = (d_{sample} > 0) \land (z < d_{sample} + \text{eps}) $$
    g. **Update Masks:**
       For points in `in_frustum` and `is_front`:
       $$ \text{valid\_num} \leftarrow \text{valid\_num} + 1 $$
       $$ \text{valid} \leftarrow \text{valid} \lor (\text{valid\_num} \ge N_{min}) $$
       For points in `forecast_frustum`:
       $$ \text{valid\_forecast} \leftarrow \text{valid\_forecast} \lor \text{forecast\_frustum} $$

4.  **Create Face Mask:**
    A face is kept if all its vertices are valid (seen):
    $$ \text{face\_mask} = \text{valid}[F].\text{all}(\text{axis}=1) $$

5.  **Update Mesh:**
    Apply `face_mask` to remove faces. Remove unreferenced vertices [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Optional Connected Component Filtering (Forecast):**
    If `forecast_radius > 0`:
    a. Create a `forecast_mask` from `valid_forecast`.
    b. Compute the Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) of the vertices in `valid_forecast` [S1, unofficial].
    c. Keep only forecast faces within this OBB.
    d. Filter components by area: Keep components where $\text{Area} > \alpha \times \text{GlobalArea}$, or keep only the largest component if `get_largest_components` is True [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def cull_mesh_by_projection(mesh, cameras, depth_maps, forecast_radius=0.0, 
                            min_views=20, eps=0.005, alpha=0.2):
    """
    Cull mesh geometry based on multi-view depth projection.
    
    Args:
        mesh: Object with .vertices (N,3) and .faces (M,3).
        cameras: List of dicts with 'c2w' (4,4), 'H', 'W', 'fx', 'fy', 'cx', 'cy'.
        depth_maps: List of tensors (H, W) for each camera.
        forecast_radius: Expansion for forecast frustum.
        min_views: Minimum cameras to see a point.
        eps: Depth tolerance.
        alpha: Min area ratio for component filtering.
        
    Returns:
        culled_mesh: Mesh object with updated faces/vertices.
    """
    V = mesh.vertices.clone() # (N, 3)
    F = mesh.faces # (M, 3)
    N = V.shape[0]
    K = len(cameras)
    
    # Initialize masks
    valid = torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.bool, device=V.device)
    valid_num = torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.int, device=V.device)
    valid_forecast = torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.bool, device=V.device)
    
    for i, cam in enumerate(cameras):
        # 1. Coordinate Transformation
        c2w = cam['c2w']
        # Negate y and z rows of rotation part for coordinate system alignment
        c2w_aligned = c2w.clone()
        c2w_aligned[:3, 1] *= -1
        c2w_aligned[:3, 2] *= -1
        w2c = torch.inverse(c2w_aligned)
        
        # 2. Project Vertices
        # Homogeneous coordinates
        V_h = torch.cat([V, torch.ones(N, 1, device=V.device)], dim=1) # (N, 4)
        # World to Camera
        P_cam = (w2c @ V_h.T).T[:, :3] # (N, 3) -> (x_c, y_c, z_c)
        
        # 3. Intrinsic Projection
        fx, fy = cam['fx'], cam['fy']
        cx, cy = cam['cx'], cam['cy']
        H, W = cam['H'], cam['W']
        
        u_prime = P_cam[:, 0] * fx + cx * P_cam[:, 2]
        v_prime = P_cam[:, 1] * fy + cy * P_cam[:, 2]
        z = P_cam[:, 2] + 1e-8
        
        u = u_prime / z
        v = v_prime / z
        
        # 4. Frustum Check
        in_frustum = (u >= 0) & (u < W) & (v >= 0) & (v < H) & (z > 0)
        
        forecast_frustum = torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.bool, device=V.device)
        if forecast_radius > 0:
            r = forecast_radius
            forecast_frustum = (u >= -r) & (u < W + r) & (v >= -r) & (v < H + r) & (z > 0)
            
        # 5. Depth Sampling
        # Reshape depth map to (1, 1, H, W)
        D = depth_maps[i].unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0) # (1, 1, H, W)
        
        # Normalize UV to [-1, 1] for grid_sample
        # grid_sample expects (N, H_out, W_out, 2) or similar, here we sample N points
        # Create grid of shape (1, N, 1, 2)
        vgrid = torch.stack([u, v], dim=-1).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(2) # (1, N, 1, 2)
        # Normalize to [-1, 1]
        vgrid_norm = 2.0 * vgrid / torch.tensor([W, H], device=V.device) - 1.0
        
        # Sample depth
        # F.grid_sample(input, grid, mode='bilinear', padding_mode='border', align_corners=True)
        # Input is (1, 1, H, W), Grid is (1, N, 1, 2) -> Output (1, 1, N, 1)
        depth_sample = F.grid_sample(D, vgrid_norm, mode='bilinear', padding_mode='border', align_corners=True)
        depth_sample = depth_sample.squeeze() # (N,)
        
        # 6. Front-Face Validation
        is_front = (depth_sample > 0.0) & (z < depth_sample + eps)
        
        # 7. Update Masks
        # Only update if in frustum and front-facing
        seen_mask = in_frustum & is_front
        valid_num = valid_num + seen_mask.int()
        valid = valid | (valid_num >= min_views)
        
        if forecast_radius > 0:
            valid_forecast = valid_forecast | forecast_frustum
            
    # 8. Create Face Mask
    # Face is valid if all 3 vertices are valid
    face_mask = valid[F].all(dim=1)
    
    # 9. Update Mesh
    # Remove faces
    new_faces = mesh.faces[face_mask]
    
    # Remove unreferenced vertices
    # Create a boolean mask for vertices used in new_faces
    if new_faces.numel() > 0:
        used_verts = torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.bool, device=V.device)
        used_verts[new_faces.flatten()] = True
        new_vertices = V[used_verts]
        
        # Remap face indices
        # Create mapping from old index to new index
        old_to_new = torch.full((N,), -1, dtype=torch.long, device=V.device)
        new_indices = torch.arange(used_verts.sum(), device=V.device)
        old_to_new[used_verts] = new_indices
        remapped_faces = old_to_new[new_faces]
    else:
        new_vertices = torch.empty((0, 3), device=V.device)
        remapped_faces = torch.empty((0, 3), dtype=torch.long, device=V.device)
        
    # 10. Optional Connected Component Filtering
    if forecast_radius > 0:
        # Compute OBB of valid_forecast vertices
        forecast_verts = V[valid_forecast]
        if forecast_verts.numel() > 0:
            # Placeholder for OBB computation. 
            # In practice, this involves PCA or optimization to find the bounding box.
            # For this implementation, we assume a helper function exists or use axis-aligned box as fallback.
            # Here we use Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) as a conservative estimate if OBB is not provided.
            min_pt = forecast_verts.min(dim=0).values
            max_pt = forecast_verts.max(dim=0).values
            
            # Check which faces are within OBB
            # A face is in OBB if all its vertices are in OBB
            forecast_in_obb = valid_forecast.clone() # Simplified: using valid_forecast directly as proxy if OBB logic is complex
            # More accurately:
            # Check if vertices of new_faces are within the OBB defined by forecast_verts
            # This step is non-trivial without a specific OBB library. 
            # We will assume the 'forecast_mask' logic from notes implies keeping components 
            # that are spatially connected to the forecast region.
            
            # Filter by area
            # Calculate area of each face in new_faces
            v0 = new_vertices[remapped_faces[:, 0]]
            v1 = new_vertices[remapped_faces[:, 1]]
            v2 = new_vertices[remapped_faces[:, 2]]
            cross = torch.cross(v1 - v0, v2 - v0)
            areas = 0.5 * torch.norm(cross, dim=1)
            total_area = areas.sum()
            
            if total_area > 0:
                keep_mask = areas >= (alpha * total_area)
                final_faces = remapped_faces[keep_mask]
                final_verts_mask = torch.zeros(new_vertices.shape[0], dtype=torch.bool, device=V.device)
                if final_faces.numel() > 0:
                    final_verts_mask[final_faces.flatten()] = True
                final_vertices = new_vertices[final_verts_mask]
                # Remap again
                old_to_new2 = torch.full((new_vertices.shape[0],), -1, dtype=torch.long, device=V.device)
                new_indices2 = torch.arange(final_verts_mask.sum(), device=V.device)
                old_to_new2[final_verts_mask] = new_indices2
                final_faces = old_to_new2[final_faces]
            else:
                final_vertices = torch.empty((0, 3), device=V.device)
                final_faces = torch.empty((0, 3), dtype=torch.long, device=V.device)
        else:
            final_vertices = torch.empty((0, 3), device=V.device)
            final_faces = torch.empty((0, 3), dtype=torch.long, device=V.device)
    else:
        final_vertices = new_vertices
        final_faces = remapped_faces
        
    # Return new mesh object
    return Mesh(vertices=final_vertices, faces=final_faces)
```

**Parameters:**
*   `points_batch_size`: Maximum number of points processed per batch to limit GPU memory. Default: $5 \times 10^5$ [S1, unofficial].
*   `resolution`: Grid resolution for TSDF/mesh processing. Default: 256 [S1, unofficial].
*   `level_set`: Implicit surface threshold value. Default: 0.0 [S1, unofficial].
*   `remove_small_geometry_threshold`: Minimum face area ratio to keep a mesh component. Default: 0.2 [S1, unofficial].
*   `eps`: Depth comparison tolerance. Default: 0.005 [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Depth comparison uses `eps` to handle floating-point inaccuracies in projection and sampling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Grid sampling uses `padding_mode='border'` to handle points near the image edge [S1, unofficial].
*   **Coordinate Systems:** The rotation matrix rows must be negated to align with the expected coordinate system (e.g., OpenGL vs. COLMAP) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Unbounded Geometry:** This method is specifically designed for unbounded reconstructions where adjacency culling fails [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Mesh Post-Processing (Cluster Filtering)](7002-mesh-post-processing-cluster-filtering.md)
Filters floating artifacts and disconnected components by clustering triangles and retaining the largest connected clusters. Used as a cleanup step after this method.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(K \cdot N)$ for projection and depth sampling, where $K$ is the number of cameras and $N$ is the number of vertices. Face culling is $O(M)$ where $M$ is the number of faces.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for masks and $O(K \cdot H \cdot W)$ for depth maps.
*   **Performance:** Effective for removing artifacts from unbounded Gaussian splatting reconstructions [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles unbounded scenes; removes geometry not visible from any camera; reduces noise.
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate depth maps and camera poses; computationally expensive for large $N$ and $K$; may remove valid geometry if depth maps are noisy [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Post-processing meshes from unbounded radiance fields (e.g., 2D Gaussian Splatting) [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Bounded voxel grids where adjacency culling is sufficient; real-time applications requiring low latency.

## Background
Originated in the context of 2D Gaussian Splatting for geometrically accurate radiance fields (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the challenge of extracting clean meshes from unbounded reconstructions where traditional voxel-based culling is not applicable [S1, unofficial].
