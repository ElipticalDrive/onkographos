# GPU-based Voxelization of 3D Minkowski Sum

Also called: GPU Voxelization Approach, Voxelized Minkowski Sum Computation

## Overview
This method computes the solid and surface voxelization of the Minkowski sum of two arbitrary polyhedra directly on the GPU, bypassing the construction of a complete boundary representation (B-rep) [S1]. It differs from B-rep based algorithms by avoiding complex 3D Boolean operations and high combinatorial complexity, instead leveraging GPU rasterization and depth testing to generate the volumetric representation [S1]. It builds on [Minkowski Sum Boundary Rendering via Primitive Culling](14570-minkowski-sum-boundary-rendering-via-primitive-culling.md) by extending the primitive culling concept to a full volumetric voxelization pipeline.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Inputs:** Two polyhedra $A$ and $B$, defined by their triangle facets $F_A, F_B$, vertices $V_A, V_B$, and edges $E_A, E_B$.
- **Outputs:** A 3D grid of binary voxels representing the solid Minkowski sum $A \oplus B$, and a surface voxelization derived from the exterior boundary.

**Data Structures:**
- **Voxel Grid:** A 3D array of binary values (0 or 1) stored exclusively on the GPU. Each voxel represents a unit volume cell. The grid supports resolutions up to $1024^3$ [S1].
- **Primitive Buffers:** Vertex Buffer Objects (VBOs) containing the generated triangle and quadrilateral primitives [S1].

**Definitions:**
The Minkowski sum of two point sets $A$ and $B$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is defined as [S1]:
$$ A \oplus B = \{a + b \mid a \in A, b \in B\} $$
Equivalently, it can be viewed as sweeping object $A$ along the boundary of $B$ (or vice versa) [S1]:
$$ A \oplus B = \bigcup_{b \in B} A_b = \bigcup_{a \in A} B_a $$
where $A_b$ is the translation of $A$ by vector $b$ [S1].

**Primitives Generation:**
Any facet on the boundary of $A \oplus B$ is generated by one of three operations [S1]:
1. Translating a triangle in $F_A$ by a vector in $V_B$ (Triangle Primitive).
2. Translating a triangle in $F_B$ by a vector in $V_A$ (Triangle Primitive).
3. Sweeping an edge in $E_A$ along an edge in $E_B$ (Quadrilateral Primitive).

The total number of primitives is $|F_A| \times |V_B| + |V_A| \times |F_B|$ triangles and $|E_A| \times |E_B|$ quadrilaterals [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1. **Primitive Culling:**
   Identify and remove surface primitives that lie entirely inside the Minkowski sum and will not contribute to the final boundary. This step significantly reduces the number of primitives to be rendered, lowering memory usage and rendering time [S1]. The criteria for culling are based on geometric propositions from [17] (referenced in [S1]). Note: The specific geometric predicates for culling are not detailed in the provided source text [S1].

2. **Rendering to Depth Textures:**
   Render the remaining surface primitives to depth textures along six orthogonal directions (typically $\pm X, \pm Y, \pm Z$) [S1]. This generates an initial solid voxelization. The graphics pipeline handles partially hidden primitives (those trimmed by others) automatically via depth testing, avoiding explicit intersection computations [S1].

3. **Flood Fill:**
   Employ a fast flood fill algorithm on the voxel grid to identify all outside voxels. This step distinguishes the interior of the Minkowski sum from the exterior, ensuring a watertight solid voxelization without holes [S1].

**Parameters:**
- **Volumetric Resolution:** The algorithm supports high resolutions, specifically tested at $1024^3$ [S1].
- **Primitive Counts:** The number of primitives can reach millions for polyhedra with thousands of triangles, necessitating the culling step [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Memory Usage:** Without culling, rendering millions of primitives can exceed GPU memory limits (e.g., 1 GB limits inputs to a few thousand triangles) [S1].
- **Partial Hiding:** Primitives that are partially hidden by others are handled automatically by the graphics pipeline's depth test, avoiding the need for explicit intersection computation [S1].
- **Holes:** The flood fill step ensures the resulting solid voxelization has no holes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The algorithm avoids the high combinatorial complexity ($O(m^3 n^3)$) of convex-decomposition or convolution approaches for concave objects [S1]. It is at least one order of magnitude faster than existing B-rep based algorithms for computing Minkowski sums of objects with curved surfaces at similar accuracy [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Volumetric data is stored and computed exclusively on the GPU. The culling step significantly reduces memory usage by limiting the number of primitives rendered to depth textures [S1].
- **Strengths:** Easy to implement, avoids complex 3D Boolean operations, supports high volumetric resolution ($1024^3$), and leverages GPU parallelism [S1].
- **Limitations:** The method is specific to polyhedral inputs. The culling criteria rely on external propositions [17] not detailed in the source [S1].
- **When to use:** Motion planning, collision detection, and penetration depth computation [S1].
- **When not to use:** When a precise boundary representation (B-rep) is required instead of a voxelized approximation.

## Background
- **Origin:** The method was presented in 2010 at the ACM Symposium of Solid and Physical Modeling (SPM ’10) by Li and McMain [S1].
- **Development:** It builds on the concept that Minkowski sums can be generated by sweeping one object along the boundary of another [S1]. It addresses the limitations of previous methods like Ghosh's slope diagram approach, which is complex for concave objects [S1].
- **Usage:** The voxelized Minkowski sums are used in applications such as motion planning and penetration depth computation [S1].
