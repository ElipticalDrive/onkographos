# Mask Projection Culling

Also called: Depth-Projected Mesh Culling, Mesh Culling via Mask Projection, point mask culling

## Overview
Mask Projection Culling is a visibility-based mesh filtering technique that removes faces and vertices not consistently observed across multiple camera poses by projecting 3D points into camera space and comparing their depth against rendered depth maps [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of retaining only geometry visible in the training views, effectively removing floating artifacts and background noise from reconstructed meshes [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike sibling methods that rely on backface orientation or connected component analysis, this method explicitly uses binary masks and depth consistency to validate visibility [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A 3D mesh (vertices $V$, faces $F$).
*   A set of $V$ camera poses (world-to-camera matrices $W_i$) and intrinsic matrices $K_i$.
*   Binary masks $M_i$ for each view, indicating valid regions (e.g., foreground).
*   Reference depth maps $D_i$ for each view, rendered from the mesh or ground truth.

**Outputs:**
*   A filtered mesh containing only vertices and faces deemed visible in at least $N$ views.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Camera Parameters:** Extracted from source data (e.g., `cameras.npz`). The projection matrix $P$ is computed as $P = W_{world} \cdot S_{scale}$, where $S_{scale}$ is a scaling matrix [S1, unofficial]. Intrinsics $K$ and pose $W$ are derived from $P$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Masks:** Binary images of fixed dimensions (e.g., $W=1600, H=1200$) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Depth Maps:** Rendered using an offscreen renderer with flags `OFFSCREEN | DEPTH_ONLY | SKIP_CULL_FACES` to ensure accurate depth sampling without rasterization artifacts [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate Transformation:**
    For each vertex $v$ and camera $i$, transform the vertex to camera space. Note that coordinate system conventions (e.g., OpenCV vs. OpenGL) may require flipping axes. Specifically, flip the Y and Z axes of the camera-to-world matrix to match the renderer's convention:
    $$ C2W_{corrected} = C2W \cdot \text{diag}(1, -1, -1, 1) $$
    Then compute the world-to-camera matrix:
    $$ W2C_i = C2W_{corrected}^{-1} $$
    [S2, unofficial]

2.  **Projection:**
    Project the 3D vertex $v_{world}$ to 2D pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ using the intrinsic matrix $K_i$ and the $W2C_i$ matrix:
    $$ p_{cam} = K_i \cdot W2C_i \cdot v_{world} $$
    $$ u = \frac{p_{cam, x}}{p_{cam, z} + \epsilon} $$
    $$ v = \frac{p_{cam, y}}{p_{cam, z} + \epsilon} $$
    where $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by zero [S1, unofficial]. The depth $z_c = p_{cam, z}$ is also retained [S1, S2, unofficial].

3.  **Frustum and Bounds Check:**
    Verify if the projected point lies within the image bounds and in front of the camera:
    $$ \text{in\_frustum} = (0 \le u < W) \land (0 \le v < H) \land (z_c > 0) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    Alternatively, normalize to NDC space $[-1, 1]$:
    $$ u_{ndc} = \frac{u}{W-1} \cdot 2 - 1 $$
    $$ v_{ndc} = \frac{v}{H-1} \cdot 2 - 1 $$
    Validity in NDC:
    $$ \text{valid}_{ndc} = (-1 < u_{ndc} < 1) \land (-1 < v_{ndc} < 1) $$
    [S1, unofficial]

4.  **Mask Sampling:**
    Dilate the binary mask $M_i$ using a morphological operation (e.g., disk of radius 24) to account for projection inaccuracies [S1, unofficial]. Sample the dilated mask at the projected coordinates $(u, v)$ using bilinear or nearest-neighbor interpolation with border padding to handle out-of-bounds queries:
    $$ m_{sampled} = \text{Sample}(M_i^{dilated}, u, v) $$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]
    The point is considered within the mask if $m_{sampled} > 0$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Depth Consistency Check:**
    Sample the reference depth map $D_i$ at $(u, v)$:
    $$ d_{sampled} = \text{Sample}(D_i, u, v) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    Determine if the vertex is in front of the surface (visible) by comparing its depth $z_c$ to the sampled depth $d_{sampled}$ with a tolerance $\epsilon_{depth}$:
    $$ \text{is\_front} = (d_{sampled} > 0) \land (z_c < d_{sampled} + \epsilon_{depth}) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    Typical value for $\epsilon_{depth}$ is 0.005 [S2, unofficial].

6.  **Aggregation:**
    Accumulate visibility counts across all $V$ views. A vertex is valid if it passes both the mask and depth tests in at least $N$ views:
    $$ \text{valid}_v = \sum_{i=1}^{V} (\text{in\_frustum}_i \land \text{valid}_{mask, i} \land \text{is\_front}_i) $$
    $$ \text{keep}_v = (\text{valid}_v \ge N) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    Typical value for $N$ is 20 [S2, unofficial]. Faces are retained only if all their vertices are kept [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Post-Processing:**
    Transform retained vertices back to world space using the scale matrix [S1, unofficial]. Optionally, remove small connected components based on area ratio threshold (e.g., 0.2) [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import numpy as np

def mask_projection_culling(mesh, cameras, masks, depth_maps, eps_depth=0.005, min_views=20):
    """
    Filter mesh vertices and faces based on mask and depth projection consistency.
    
    Args:
        mesh: Object with vertices (N, 3) and faces (M, 3).
        cameras: List of dicts with 'w2c' (4,4) and 'K' (3,3).
        masks: List of tensors (H, W) binary masks.
        depth_maps: List of tensors (H, W) depth maps.
        eps_depth: Depth tolerance.
        min_views: Minimum number of views a vertex must be visible in.
        
    Returns:
        Filtered mesh vertices and faces.
    """
    num_views = len(cameras)
    num_verts = mesh.vertices.shape[0]
    valid_counts = np.zeros(num_verts, dtype=np.int32)
    
    # Batch processing to limit GPU memory
    batch_size = 500000
    num_batches = (num_verts + batch_size - 1) // batch_size
    
    for b in range(num_batches):
        start_idx = b * batch_size
        end_idx = min((b + 1) * batch_size, num_verts)
        batch_verts = mesh.vertices[start_idx:end_idx] # (B, 3)
        
        # Initialize visibility for this batch
        batch_valid = np.zeros(end_idx - start_idx, dtype=bool)
        
        for i in range(num_views):
            w2c = cameras[i]['w2c'] # (4, 4)
            K = cameras[i]['K']     # (3, 3)
            mask = masks[i]         # (H, W)
            depth_map = depth_maps[i] # (H, W)
            
            # Transform vertices to camera space
            # Homogeneous coordinates
            verts_hom = torch.cat([batch_verts, torch.ones(batch_verts.shape[0], 1)], dim=1).cuda() # (B, 4)
            cam_verts = (w2c @ verts_hom.t()).t() # (B, 4)
            
            z_c = cam_verts[:, 2] # (B,)
            
            # Project to 2D
            # u = K[0,0] * x/z + K[0,2]
            # v = K[1,1] * y/z + K[1,2]
            u = (K[0, 0] * cam_verts[:, 0] / (z_c + 1e-6)) + K[0, 2]
            v = (K[1, 1] * cam_verts[:, 1] / (z_c + 1e-6)) + K[1, 2]
            
            # Frustum check
            H, W = mask.shape
            in_frustum = (u >= 0) & (u < W) & (v >= 0) & (v < H) & (z_c > 0)
            
            if not in_frustum.any():
                continue
                
            # Sample Mask and Depth
            # Normalize to [-1, 1] for grid_sample
            u_ndc = (u / (W - 1)) * 2.0 - 1.0
            v_ndc = (v / (H - 1)) * 2.0 - 1.0
            
            # Create grid for sampling
            # grid_sample expects (N, H, W, 2) with coords in [-1, 1]
            # We need to sample at specific points, so we create a grid of shape (1, 1, B, 2)
            # Note: grid_sample expects x (u) and y (v) in specific order depending on implementation
            # Standard PyTorch: x is horizontal (u), y is vertical (v)
            grid = torch.stack([u_ndc, v_ndc], dim=-1).unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).cuda() # (1, 1, B, 2)
            
            # Sample Mask (nearest neighbor, zeros padding)
            # Mask needs to be float and unsqueezed for batch dim
            mask_tensor = mask.float().unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).cuda() # (1, 1, H, W)
            # grid_sample output shape: (1, 1, B, 1)
            sampled_mask = torch.nn.functional.grid_sample(
                mask_tensor, grid, 
                mode='nearest', 
                padding_mode='zeros', 
                align_corners=True
            ).squeeze(-1).squeeze(0).squeeze(0) # (B,)
            
            # Sample Depth (bilinear, border padding)
            depth_tensor = depth_map.float().unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0).cuda() # (1, 1, H, W)
            sampled_depth = torch.nn.functional.grid_sample(
                depth_tensor, grid, 
                mode='bilinear', 
                padding_mode='border', 
                align_corners=True
            ).squeeze(-1).squeeze(0).squeeze(0) # (B,)
            
            # Visibility Tests
            in_mask = sampled_mask > 0.0
            
            # Depth test: vertex must be in front of sampled depth
            # Handle cases where depth is 0 (background)
            is_front = torch.zeros_like(z_c, dtype=torch.bool)
            valid_depth_mask = sampled_depth > 0.0
            is_front[valid_depth_mask] = z_c[valid_depth_mask] < (sampled_depth[valid_depth_mask] + eps_depth)
            # If depth is 0, we assume it's not visible (or handle as per specific logic, here default to False)
            
            view_visible = in_frustum & in_mask & is_front
            
            # Accumulate
            batch_valid = batch_valid | view_visible.cpu().numpy()
            
        # Final filter for batch
        valid_indices = np.where(batch_valid.sum(axis=0) >= min_views)[0]
        valid_counts[start_idx:end_idx][valid_indices] = 1
        
    # Filter vertices
    final_verts_mask = valid_counts > 0
    final_verts = mesh.vertices[final_verts_mask]
    
    # Filter faces (keep if all vertices are valid)
    # Map old vertex indices to new ones
    old_to_new = np.full(mesh.vertices.shape[0], -1, dtype=np.int32)
    old_to_new[final_verts_mask] = np.arange(final_verts_mask.sum())
    
    final_faces = []
    for face in mesh.faces:
        new_indices = old_to_new[face]
        if np.all(new_indices != -1):
            final_faces.append(new_indices)
            
    return final_verts, np.array(final_faces)
```

## Subtopics

### True Back-Face Culling via Group Masking
A technique that splits chunk meshes into 6 vertex pool buckets based on face orientation (groups 0-5) and masks draw calls based on camera position relative to the chunk, preventing the draw call from being issued entirely [S3, unofficial]. Unlike standard back-face culling which discards primitives in the fragment shader, this method avoids driver overhead associated with splitting chunks into 6 sub-meshes by reusing existing vertex pool bucketing [S3, unofficial]. It is chosen when rendering performance is critical and the scene is composed of chunked meshes with known orientations [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot V \cdot B)$, where $N$ is the number of points, $V$ is the number of validation frames, and $B$ is the batch size [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot V)$ for intermediate masks and valid counts [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Depends heavily on the number of views and resolution of depth maps/masks. Batching is required for large meshes to limit GPU memory usage [S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively removes geometry not visible in training views, reducing noise and floating artifacts [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires accurate depth maps and masks; sensitive to coordinate system mismatches and projection errors [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When reconstructing meshes from multi-view images and visibility is defined by the training camera poses [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When real-time visibility is required or when depth information is unavailable [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Mask Projection Culling is used in 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF-based reconstruction pipelines to extract clean meshes from implicit or point-based representations [S1, S2, unofficial]. It was popularized in the context of 2D Gaussian Splatting for high-quality novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial]. The method relies on rendering depth maps and using binary masks to define the foreground region, ensuring that only geometry consistent with the observed data is retained [S1, S2, unofficial].
