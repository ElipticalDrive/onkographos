# CSG Difference
Also called: SDF Difference, Subtraction of SDFs, CSG Subtraction
## Overview
The CSG Difference operation constructs a new Signed Distance Function (SDF) by subtracting one shape from another, effectively carving the first object out of the second. It solves the problem of creating complex geometry with holes or cutouts from simpler primitives. Unlike the standard union (minimum), which merges volumes, this operation removes the volume of the subtractor from the base, ensuring the resulting field represents the solid region of the base that does not overlap with the subtractor.
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Two scalar SDF functions, $S_a(p)$ and $S_b(p)$, evaluated at point $p$. $S_a$ is the subtractor (shape to be removed), and $S_b$ is the base (shape to keep).
- Output: A scalar value $S_{diff}(p)$ representing the distance to the surface of the difference operation.

**Data Structures:**
- No complex data structures are required. The operation is point-wise and stateless. It relies on the scalar values returned by the constituent SDFs.

**Formula:**
The CSG difference is defined as:
$$ S_{diff}(p) = \max(S_b(p), -S_a(p)) $$
Where:
- $S_b(p)$ is the signed distance to the base object at point $p$.
- $S_a(p)$ is the signed distance to the subtractor object at point $p$.
- The negative sign on $S_a(p)$ inverts the inside/outside convention of the subtractor, so that the interior of $A$ becomes "far away" (positive distance) in the max operation, effectively excluding it from the result.

**Steps:**
1. Evaluate the base SDF at the query point $p$: $d_b = S_b(p)$.
2. Evaluate the subtractor SDF at the query point $p$: $d_a = S_a(p)$.
3. Negate the subtractor distance: $neg\_d_a = -d_a$.
4. Return the maximum of the base distance and the negated subtractor distance: $result = \max(d_b, neg\_d_a)$.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def csg_difference(d_a: float, d_b: float) -> float:
    """
    Computes the CSG difference of two SDF values.
    
    Args:
        d_a: Signed distance to the subtractor shape (A).
        d_b: Signed distance to the base shape (B).
        
    Returns:
        Signed distance to the result of B minus A.
    """
    # Invert the subtractor's distance
    neg_d_a = -d_a
    
    # The result is the maximum of the base distance and the inverted subtractor distance
    return max(d_b, neg_d_a)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Near the boundary where $S_b(p) \approx -S_a(p)$, floating-point precision errors can cause artifacts. Ensure epsilon thresholds in ray marching are appropriate for the scale of the scene.
- **Degenerate Input:** If the subtractor completely contains the base, the resulting SDF will be positive everywhere (empty solid). If they do not intersect, the result is simply the base SDF where the subtractor is outside, and the inverted subtractor where it overlaps.
- **Normals:** The normal of the resulting surface is derived from the gradient of the resulting SDF. At the boundary where the subtractor cuts the base, the normal is determined by the base. At the boundary created by the subtractor's interior (the hole), the normal is determined by the subtractor (inverted).

## Subtopics

### [Smooth Subtraction](1840-smooth-subtraction.md)
Smooth Subtraction provides a smooth approximation of the boolean difference operation ($A \setminus B$) for two Signed Distance Functions (SDFs). It blends the subtraction of one shape from another using a smoothing parameter $k$.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per evaluation, requiring two SDF queries and basic arithmetic.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$, no additional storage required.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement, efficient, allows for arbitrary complex shapes to be carved out of others.
- **Limitations:** The resulting SDF is only valid if the input SDFs are valid. Sharp corners at the intersection of the base and subtractor may require careful normal estimation for rendering.
- **When to use:** When you need to create holes, cutouts, or complex boolean combinations in SDF-based rendering.
- **When not to use:** When smooth transitions between shapes are required (use Smooth Subtraction instead).
## Background
The CSG Difference is a fundamental operation in Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) applied to Signed Distance Fields. It is widely used in procedural geometry generation and ray marching shaders to create complex scenes from simple primitives. The method relies on the property that the distance to the union of two shapes is the minimum of their distances, and the distance to the difference is the maximum of the base distance and the negated subtractor distance.
