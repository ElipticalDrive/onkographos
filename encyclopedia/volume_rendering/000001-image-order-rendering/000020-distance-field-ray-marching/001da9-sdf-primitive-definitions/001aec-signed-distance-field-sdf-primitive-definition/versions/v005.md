# Signed Distance Field (SDF) Primitive Definition

Also called: SDF Function, SDF Shapes, Signed Distance Field (SDF), Signed Distance Function (SDF), Signed Distance Function Primitives

## Overview
A Signed Distance Field (SDF) Primitive Definition is an analytical function that computes the shortest Euclidean distance from a query point $\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ to the nearest surface of a geometric primitive, with a sign indicating whether the point is inside (negative), on (zero), or outside (positive) the shape [S2, S5, S6, S7, S8, S14, unofficial]. This method serves as the fundamental building block for distance-field ray marching, replacing explicit mesh intersection tests with conservative distance estimates that guarantee safe step sizes [S1, S7, S14, unofficial]. Unlike [Constructive SDF Modeling (Min Combination)](871-constructive-sdf-modeling-min-combination.md) which combines primitives, or [SDF Domain Manipulation Operators](1730-sdf-domain-manipulation-operators.md) which transform space, primitive definitions provide the exact base geometry [S1, S3, S10, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A query point $\mathbf{p} = (p_x, p_y, p_z)$ in 3D space, and primitive-specific parameters (e.g., radius, center, normal).
- Output: A scalar float $d = \text{SDF}(\mathbf{p})$ representing the signed distance.

**Data Structures:**
Primitives are typically defined in local coordinate space (centered at the origin, aligned with axes) [S5, S12, unofficial]. Common parameter structures include:
- Sphere: radius $r$ (float), center $\mathbf{c}$ (vec3) [S13, unofficial].
- Box: position $\mathbf{p}$ (vec3), half-extents $\mathbf{b}$ (vec3) [S13, unofficial].
- Cylinder: center $\mathbf{p}$ in XZ plane (vec2), radius $r$ (float), half-height $h$ (float) [S13, unofficial].
- Cone: radius $r$ (float), height $h$ (float), center/offset $\mathbf{c}$ (vec3) [S13, unofficial].
- Plane: normal $\mathbf{n}$ (vec3), offset $d_{offset}$ (float), often packed as `vec4 k` where `k.xyz` is normal and `k.w` is offset [S13, unofficial].
- Torus: major radius $R_{out}$ (float), minor radius $R_{in}$ (float) [S13, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1. **Transform (Optional):** If the primitive is not at the origin or axis-aligned, transform the query point $\mathbf{p}$ into the primitive's local space using the inverse of the primitive's world transformation matrix [S10, unofficial]. Let the transformed point be $\mathbf{p}_{local}$.
2. **Evaluate Distance:** Compute the Euclidean distance from $\mathbf{p}_{local}$ to the primitive's surface using the specific analytical formula for the shape.
3. **Apply Sign:** The result is positive if outside, negative if inside, and zero if on the surface [S2, S5, S7, unofficial].
4. **Return:** Return the signed distance value $d$.

**Formulas:**
Let $\|\cdot\|$ denote the Euclidean norm (length). Let $\max(\mathbf{v}, 0)$ and $\min(\mathbf{v}, 0)$ be component-wise maximum and minimum with zero. Let $\text{vmax}(\mathbf{v})$ be the maximum component of vector $\mathbf{v}$ [S5, S12, unofficial].

*   **Sphere** (center $\mathbf{c}$, radius $r$):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{c}\| - r $$
    [S2, S4, S5, S7, S8, S10, S13, unofficial]

*   **Box** (AABB, half-extents $\mathbf{b}$, centered at origin):
    $$ \mathbf{d} = |\mathbf{p}| - \mathbf{b} $$
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \|\max(\mathbf{d}, 0)\| + \text{vmax}(\min(\mathbf{d}, 0)) $$
    [S5, S12, S13, unofficial]
    *Note: A cheaper, overestimated version is $\text{vmax}(|\mathbf{p}| - \mathbf{b})$ [S5, S12, unofficial].*

*   **Plane** (normal $\mathbf{n}$, distance from origin $d_{offset}$):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{n} + d_{offset} $$
    [S5, S13, unofficial]

*   **Cylinder** (infinite, axis along Y, radius $r$):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \|\mathbf{p}_{xz}\| - r $$
    where $\mathbf{p}_{xz} = (p_x, p_z)$ [S13, unofficial].

*   **Cylinder** (capped, radius $r$, half-height $h$):
    $$ d_x = \|\mathbf{p}_{xz}\| - r $$
    $$ d_y = |p_y| - h $$
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \min(\max(d_x, d_y), 0) + \|\max(d_x, d_y, 0)\| $$
    [S13, unofficial]
    *Alternative GLSL implementation:*
    ```python
    d = length(p.xz) - r
    d = max(d, abs(p.y) - height)
    return d
    ```
    [S5, S12, unofficial]

*   **Cone** (axis along Y, radius $r$, height $h$):
    $$ \mathbf{q} = (\|\mathbf{p}_{xz}\|, p_y) $$
    $$ \mathbf{tip} = \mathbf{q} - (0, h) $$
    $$ \mathbf{mantleDir} = \text{normalize}((h, r)) $$
    $$ \text{mantle} = \mathbf{tip} \cdot \mathbf{mantleDir} $$
    $$ d = \max(\text{mantle}, -p_y) $$
    $$ \text{projected} = \mathbf{tip} \cdot (\text{mantleDir.y}, -\text{mantleDir.x}) $$
    If $(q_y > h)$ and $(\text{projected} < 0)$:
        $$ d = \max(d, \|\mathbf{tip}\|) $$
    If $(q_x > r)$ and $(\text{projected} > \|(h, r)\|)$:
        $$ d = \max(d, \|\mathbf{q} - (r, 0)\|) $$
    [S12, S13, unofficial]

*   **Torus** (major radius $R_{out}$, minor radius $R_{in}$):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \|( \|\mathbf{p}_{xz}\| - R_{out}, p_y )\| - R_{in} $$
    [S5, S12, S13, unofficial]

*   **Capsule** (endpoints $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$, radius $r$):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \text{LineSegment}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) - r $$
    where $\text{LineSegment}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \| (\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}) \cdot \text{saturate}(\frac{(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{a})\cdot(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})}{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\|^2} ) + \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{p} \|$ [S5, S12, unofficial].

*   **Generalized Distance Function (GDF)** (approximating polyhedra):
    Uses a set of normalized vectors $\mathbf{v}_i$.
    With exponent $e$ (for rounded edges):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \left( \sum_{i=\text{begin}}^{\text{end}} |\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{v}_i|^e \right)^{1/e} - r $$
    [S5, S12, unofficial]
    Without exponent (sharp edges):
    $$ d(\mathbf{p}) = \max_{i=\text{begin}}^{\text{end}} (|\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{v}_i|) - r $$
    [S5, S12, unofficial]
    *Note: The variable exponent version is slow and may not produce correct distances; specialized implementations are recommended [S12, unofficial].*

**Parameters:**
- **Smoothing Constant ($k$):** Used in smooth minimum blending (e.g., $k=0.2$) [S10, unofficial].
- **Gradient Step Size ($\epsilon$):** Used for normal approximation (e.g., $0.0001$) [S10, unofficial].
- **PHI:** Golden ratio constant $\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}$ used in GDF vectors [S12, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Floating-point precision can affect the accuracy of the distance estimate near the surface [S14, unofficial].
- **Loop Unrolling:** GDF implementations with variable exponents or large loops (e.g., `fIcosahedron`) may fail to unroll on certain drivers (e.g., Nvidia 350.12), causing significant slowdowns [S5, S12, unofficial].
- **Non-Closed Forms:** Not every shape has a closed-form SDF. Arbitrary meshes require approximate SDFs (baked into textures or BVH traversal) [S14, unofficial].
- **Incorrect Bounds:** Some illustrative functions (e.g., `fBlob`) are not correct distance bounds and should not be used for ray marching [S5, S12, unofficial].
- **Discontinuities:** Taking the min/max of two SDFs yields a sharp discontinuity in the derivative, which can affect shading normals if not handled [S10, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def sdf_sphere(p: vec3, c: vec3, r: float) -> float:
    """Returns signed distance from p to sphere with center c and radius r."""
    return length(p - c) - r

def sdf_box(p: vec3, b: vec3) -> float:
    """Returns signed distance from p to AABB centered at origin with half-extents b."""
    d = abs(p) - b
    return length(max(d, vec3(0.0))) + vmax(min(d, vec3(0.0)))

def sdf_torus(p: vec3, R_out: float, R_in: float) -> float:
    """Returns signed distance from p to torus with major radius R_out and minor radius R_in."""
    q = vec2(length(p.xz) - R_out, p.y)
    return length(q) - R_in

def sdf_transform(p: vec3, transform_mat: mat4, sdf_func, params) -> float:
    """Applies inverse transformation to p before evaluating the SDF."""
    # Assuming transform_mat is the world-to-local transformation
    p_local = (inverse(transform_mat) * vec4(p, 1.0)).xyz
    return sdf_func(p_local, params)

def sdf_normal(p: vec3, sdf_func, params, epsilon: float = 0.0001) -> vec3:
    """Approximates the normal at p using finite differences."""
    d = sdf_func(p, params)
    n_x = sdf_func(p + vec3(epsilon, 0, 0), params) - d
    n_y = sdf_func(p + vec3(0, epsilon, 0), params) - d
    n_z = sdf_func(p + vec3(0, 0, epsilon), params) - d
    return normalize(vec3(n_x, n_y, n_z))
```

## Subtopics

### [SDF Primitives & Operations](1075-sdf-primitives-operations.md)
Provides analytical building blocks and combines them via Boolean/smoothing operators. Defines base geometry, unlike domain manipulation.

### [SDF Transformation](1086-sdf-transformation.md)
Applies geometric transformations (rotation, translation, scaling) by manipulating query points. Preserves mathematical integrity, unlike non-linear deformations which require Lipschitz bounds.

### [k-SDF](9530-k-sdf.md)
A representation for real-time view synthesis of fuzzy geometries using $k$ lightweight mesh shells. Binds samples per ray, unlike sorting-free Gaussian splatting.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per primitive evaluation. The cost is dominated by the number of ray marching steps, which depends on scene complexity and desired precision.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for primitive definitions (constants). No mesh storage required.
- **Performance:** Significantly faster than ray-triangle intersection for complex scenes composed of many primitives, as it avoids bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) traversal overhead for simple shapes [S1, S7, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Exact distance guarantees enable robust sphere tracing; easy to combine via CSG; compact representation; supports smooth blending [S1, S7, S10, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Limited to shapes with known analytical SDFs; sharp CSG operations can cause shading artifacts; GDF approximations may be inaccurate or slow [S12, S14, unofficial].
- **When to use:** Real-time rendering of procedural geometry, implicit surfaces, and scenes with high repetition or simple primitives.
- **When not to use:** Arbitrary complex meshes without pre-computed SDFs; scenes requiring exact triangle-level precision for thin geometry where approximations fail [S14, unofficial].

## Background
Signed Distance Functions were popularized in computer graphics by Inigo Quilez's articles on distance functions and ray marching [S1, S6, S10, unofficial]. The mathematical foundation relies on the properties of Euclidean distance and level sets, with roots in computational geometry and level set methods for tracking interfaces [S9, unofficial]. The technique allows for the representation of entire 3D worlds as single mathematical functions, contrasting with traditional mesh-based representations [S8, unofficial].
