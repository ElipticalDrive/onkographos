# Spherical Harmonics

Also called: SH basis, SH coefficients, Spherical Harmonic Representation of Radiance, Spherical Harmonic Volumetric Rendering, Spherical Harmonics (SH) Basis

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) provide a complete, orthonormal basis for representing directional functions defined on the unit sphere $S^2$ [S1, S3, S4, unofficial]. In volume rendering, they solve the problem of efficiently storing and computing integrals over the sphere (such as those in the rendering equation) by converting expensive numerical integration into fast algebraic operations like dot products and matrix-vector multiplications [S1, unofficial]. They differ from their siblings, such as [Spherical Harmonics Approximation](8741-spherical-harmonics-approximation.md) and [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md), by defining the fundamental mathematical basis itself, whereas the siblings focus on specific application pipelines (approximation, PRT) or optimization techniques (degree selection, zonal methods) built upon this basis.

## Shared principles
The SH basis consists of an infinite set of functions $Y_\ell^m(\theta, \phi)$ indexed by degree $\ell \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ and order $m \in \{-\ell, \dots, \ell\}$ [S1, S3, unofficial]. The complex-valued basis functions are defined as [S1, S3, unofficial]:

$$ Y_\ell^m(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi} \frac{(\ell - m)!}{(\ell + m)!}} P_\ell^m(\cos \theta) e^{im\phi} $$

where $P_\ell^m$ are the associated Legendre polynomials [S1, S3, unofficial].

For real-valued rendering applications, the basis is constructed to avoid imaginary numbers for $m \neq 0$ [S3, unofficial]. The real-valued basis $Y_{lm}$ is defined piecewise [S1, S3, unofficial]:

$$
Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi) =
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{2} K_{lm} \cos(m\phi) P_l^m(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m > 0 \\
K_{l0} P_l^0(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m = 0 \\
\sqrt{2} K_{lm} \sin(-m\phi) P_l^{-m}(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m < 0
\end{cases}
$$

where $K_{lm} = \sqrt{\frac{2l + 1}{4\pi} \frac{(l - m)!}{(l + m)!}}$ [S3, unofficial].

A directional signal $f(\omega)$ is represented by a finite set of coefficients $\mathbf{f}$ of size $n = (L+1)^2$, where $L$ is the maximum degree (bandwidth) [S1, Child 10564, unofficial]. The coefficients are computed by projecting the function onto the basis [S1, unofficial]:

$$ f_i = \int_{\Omega} f(\omega) y_i(\omega) d\omega $$

The signal is reconstructed by summing the basis functions weighted by the coefficients [S1, unofficial]:

$$ \tilde{f}(\omega) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i(\omega) $$

The linear index $i$ maps the degree $l$ and order $m$ via $i = l^2 + l + m$ [S1, Child 10564, unofficial].

Convolution of two functions $\tilde{a}(\omega)$ and $\tilde{b}(\omega)$ in the SH domain is performed using a precomputed transfer matrix $C_{ijk}$ [S1, unofficial]:

$$ (a * b)_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} C_{ijk} a_j b_k $$

where $C_{ijk} = \int_{\Omega} y_i(\omega) y_j(\omega) y_k(\omega) d\omega$ [S1, unofficial]. The integral of the product of two approximated functions is the dot product of their coefficient vectors [S1, unofficial]:

$$ \int_{\Omega} \tilde{a}(\omega)\tilde{b}(\omega)d\omega = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} $$

Typical implementations use the lowest 3-5 levels of SH to keep the representation compact [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Spherical Harmonics originated in mathematical physics as a complete basis for functions on the sphere [S1, S3, unofficial]. In computer graphics, they were adopted for Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) to efficiently represent lighting and reflection [S3, S4, unofficial]. They are used in volume rendering to represent the directional distribution of scattered radiance, allowing for efficient rotation and integration [S2]. Recent applications include representing neural radiance fields (NeRFs) via PlenOctrees, where SH basis functions replace standard color representations to enable real-time rendering [S1, unofficial].
