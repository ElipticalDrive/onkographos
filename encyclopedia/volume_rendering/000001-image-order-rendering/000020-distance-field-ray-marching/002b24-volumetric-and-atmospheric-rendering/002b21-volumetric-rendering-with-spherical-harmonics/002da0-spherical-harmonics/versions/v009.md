# Spherical Harmonics

Also called: SH basis, SH coefficients, Spherical Harmonic Representation of Radiance, Spherical Harmonic Volumetric Rendering, Spherical Harmonics (SH) Basis

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics (SH) provide a complete, orthonormal basis for representing directional functions defined on the unit sphere $S^2$ [S1, S3, S4, unofficial]. In volume rendering, they serve as a fixed, non-learnable basis for directional signals, where switching between max degree 3 (SH-16) and 4 (SH-25) yields negligible differences in metrics or visual quality [S1, unofficial]. They differ from their siblings, such as [Spherical Harmonics Approximation](8741-spherical-harmonics-approximation.md) and [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md), by defining the fundamental mathematical basis itself, whereas the siblings focus on specific application pipelines or optimization techniques built upon this basis.

## Shared principles
The SH basis consists of an infinite set of functions $Y_\ell^m(\theta, \phi)$ indexed by degree $\ell \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ and order $m \in \{-\ell, \dots, \ell\}$ [S1, S3, unofficial]. The complex-valued basis functions are defined as [S1, S3, unofficial]:

$$ Y_\ell^m(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi} \frac{(\ell - m)!}{(\ell + m)!}} P_\ell^m(\cos \theta) e^{im\phi} $$

where $P_\ell^m$ are the associated Legendre polynomials [S1, S3, unofficial].

For real-valued rendering applications, the basis is constructed to avoid imaginary numbers for $m \neq 0$ [S3, unofficial]. The real-valued basis $Y_{lm}$ is defined piecewise [S3, unofficial]:

$$
Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi) =
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{2} K_{lm} \cos(m\phi) P_l^m(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m > 0 \\
K_{l0} P_l^0(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m = 0 \\
\sqrt{2} K_{lm} \sin(-m\phi) P_l^{-m}(\cos(\theta)), & \text{if } m < 0
\end{cases}
$$

where $K_{lm} = \sqrt{\frac{2l + 1}{4\pi} \frac{(l - m)!}{(l + m)!}}$ [S3, unofficial]. Source [S1, unofficial] provides an alternative real-valued construction using $Y_\ell^m = \sqrt{2}(-1)^m\text{Im}[Y_\ell^{|m|}]$ for $m < 0$, $Y_\ell^0$ for $m = 0$, and $\sqrt{2}(-1)^m\text{Re}[Y_\ell^m]$ for $m > 0$.

A directional signal $f(\omega)$ is represented by a finite set of coefficients $\mathbf{f}$ of size $n = (L+1)^2$, where $L$ is the maximum degree (bandwidth) [Child 10564]. The coefficients are computed by projecting the function onto the basis [Child 8741]:

$$ f_i = \int_{\Omega} f(\omega) y_i(\omega) d\omega $$

The signal is reconstructed by summing the basis functions weighted by the coefficients [Child 8741]:

$$ \tilde{f}(\omega) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i y_i(\omega) $$

The linear index $i$ maps the degree $l$ and order $m$ via $i = l^2 + l + m$ [Child 10564].

Convolution of two functions $\tilde{a}(\omega)$ and $\tilde{b}(\omega)$ in the SH domain is performed using a precomputed transfer matrix $C_{ijk}$ [Child 8741]:

$$ (a * b)_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} C_{ijk} a_j b_k $$

where $C_{ijk} = \int_{\Omega} y_i(\omega) y_j(\omega) y_k(\omega) d\omega$ [Child 8741]. The integral of the product of two approximated functions is the dot product of their coefficient vectors [Child 8741]:

$$ \int_{\Omega} \tilde{a}(\omega)\tilde{b}(\omega)d\omega = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} $$

Typical implementations use the lowest 3-5 levels of SH to keep the representation compact [S3, unofficial]. SH approximations containing higher frequencies become too large to be practical, and SH functions exhibit 'ringing' artifacts (high-frequency noise) near sharp value changes, especially when many coefficients are used [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Spherical Harmonics Approximation](8741-spherical-harmonics-approximation.md)
Represents a spherical function using a finite SH subset for efficient, differentiable computation. Sets it apart by focusing on projection and reconstruction mechanics with a fixed bandwidth rather than determining optimal bandwidth. Choose it for inverse problems requiring algebraic integration and convolution.

### [Spherical Harmonics Degree Selection](10564-spherical-harmonics-degree-selection.md)
Determines the maximum bandwidth $L$ to balance reconstruction fidelity against memory consumption. Sets it apart by focusing on the capacity limit (bandwidth) rather than the projection or fitting process itself. Choose it when optimizing memory and compute constraints for a given signal's frequency content.

### [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md)
Stores position- and direction-dependent scattered radiance in a 3D grid using SH basis functions. Sets it apart by enabling fast integration via sparse matrix multiplication during rendering. Choose it for efficient multiple scattering in volumes (e.g., hair) where storage compactness and organized memory access are critical.

### H-Basis Encoding
A basis used to encode a hemispherical signal, requiring fewer coefficients to achieve the same quality as 3rd order spherical harmonics [S5, unofficial]. A coordinate system local to the surface is required to orient the hemisphere, commonly utilizing a tangent frame resulting from uv-parameterization [S5, unofficial]. If encoded in textures, the texture needs sufficient resolution to adapt to changes in the underlying tangent space [S5, unofficial]. If two triangles cover the same texel and have significantly different tangents, it leads to a lack of precision in the reconstructed signal [S5, unofficial].

## Background
Spherical Harmonics originated in mathematical physics as a complete basis for functions on the sphere [S1, S3, unofficial]. In computer graphics, they were adopted for Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) to efficiently represent lighting and reflection [S3, S4, unofficial]. They are used in volume rendering to represent the directional distribution of scattered radiance, allowing for efficient rotation and integration [S2]. Recent applications include representing neural radiance fields (NeRFs) via PlenOctrees, where SH basis functions replace standard color representations to enable real-time rendering [S1, unofficial].
