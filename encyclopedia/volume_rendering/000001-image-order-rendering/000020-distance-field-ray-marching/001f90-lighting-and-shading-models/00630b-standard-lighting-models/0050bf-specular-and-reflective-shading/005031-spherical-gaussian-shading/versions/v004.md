# Spherical Gaussian Shading

Also called: SG Shading, Shading Rendering of Spherical Gaussians, Specular BRDF Approximation

## Overview
Spherical Gaussian (SG) Shading is a technique that approximates direct illumination and specular BRDFs using a mixture of Spherical Gaussian basis functions to enable fast, analytic integration and automatic differentiation [S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from Monte Carlo (MC) integration by being less expensive and more robust to roughness variations, though it sacrifices high-frequency detail accuracy [S2, unofficial]. It is primarily used in differentiable rendering and inverse rendering pipelines where lighting and material parameters must be optimized jointly [S2, unofficial][S4].

## How it works
The method operates on a surface point $\mathbf{p}$ with normal $\mathbf{n}$, view direction $\mathbf{v}$, and incident light direction $\mathbf{l}$. It approximates the rendering equation by representing both the incident radiance and the BRDF lobes as sums of Spherical Gaussians.

### Data Structures
1.  **Spherical Gaussian (SG) Kernel**: Defined by four parameters:
    *   $\omega \in S^2$: Input direction.
    *   $\xi \in S^2$: Axis (center) of the lobe.
    *   $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Sharpness (inverse width).
    *   $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^{3+}$: Amplitude (HDR RGB color).
2.  **Lighting Representation**: A mixture of $K$ SGs representing the environment map or spatially variant lights.
    *   Parameters: $\gamma = \{ \xi_{lk}, \lambda_{lk}, \mu_{lk} \}_{k=1}^K$.
    *   Typical $K$: 128 for HDR environment maps, 32 for prediction tasks [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Volumetric SG (VSG) Layout** (if used for spatially varying lighting):
    *   Stored as an 8-channel tensor $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{8 \times X \times Y \times Z}$ [S4].
    *   Each voxel contains:
        *   $\alpha \in [0, 1]$: Opacity.
        *   $c \in \mathbb{R}^3$: HDR RGB intensity (amplitude).
        *   $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Lobe axis.
        *   $\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Sharpness parameter (related to $\lambda$).

### Formulas
The Spherical Gaussian function is defined as [S2, unofficial]:
$$ G(\omega; \xi, \lambda, \mu) = \mu e^{\lambda(\xi \cdot \omega - 1)} $$

**Cosine Foreshortening Approximation**:
The term $|\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}|$ is approximated by a single SG [S2, unofficial]:
$$ |\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}| \approx G_c(\mathbf{l}; \mathbf{n}, 2.133, 1.17) $$

**BRDF Approximation**:
The specular lobe of the BRDF is fitted to a single monochromatic SG, denoted $f_r^{(SG)}$, which is a sum of diffuse and specular lobes [S2, unofficial].

**Rendering Equation (Analytic Integration)**:
The final pixel color $S^{(SG)}$ is computed by integrating the product of the BRDF, incident radiance, and cosine term [S2, unofficial]:
$$ S^{(SG)}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{n}, \omega_o; \theta, \gamma) = \int_{S^2} f_r^{(SG)}(\mathbf{x}, \omega_i, \omega_o; \theta) L_i^{(SG)}(\omega_i; \gamma) G_c(\omega_i) d\omega_i $$
Where $L_i^{(SG)}$ is the incident radiance represented by $K$ SGs [S2, unofficial].

**Point Light Approximation Variant**:
Alternatively, each SG can be rendered as 5 point lights (1 center + 4 sampled around) [S3]. For $k$ point lights with intensity $\mathbf{I}_i$, the shading is [S3]:
$$ \mathbf{S}_{SG}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r}) \cdot \mathbf{L}_i(\mathbf{r})}{|\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{r})| |\mathbf{L}_i(\mathbf{r})|} * \frac{\mathbf{I}_i}{\gamma \cdot \mathbf{Dis}_i(\mathbf{r})^2} $$
Where $\mathbf{L}_i(\mathbf{r})$ is the direction from light to point, $\mathbf{Dis}_i(\mathbf{r})$ is the distance, and $\gamma$ is a hyperparameter controlling luminance [S3].

**Volumetric Radiance Calculation**:
For VSG, incident radiance $R(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{l}, \mathcal{L})$ at point $\mathbf{p}$ in direction $\mathbf{l}$ is calculated via alpha compositing along $N$ equi-spaced points [S4]:
$$ R(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{l}, \mathcal{L}) = \left( \prod_{i=1}^{k-1} (1 - \alpha_i) \right) \sum_{k=1}^{N} \alpha_k G(-\mathbf{l}; c_k, \mu_k, \sigma_k) $$

**Final NeRF Relighting Equation**:
The total image for ray $\mathbf{r}$ combines diffuse, specular, and SG shading [S3]:
$$ I(\mathbf{r}) = I_d(\mathbf{r}) + I_s(\mathbf{r}) + V(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{r})S_{SG}(\mathbf{r}) $$
Where $V$ is shadow visibility and $\mathcal{R}$ is reflectance [S3].

### Pseudocode
```python
def spherical_gaussian_shading(surface_point, normal, view_dir, lighting_params, method="analytic"):
    """
    Compute shading using Spherical Gaussians.
    
    Args:
        surface_point: 3D position p.
        normal: Surface normal n.
        view_dir: View direction v.
        lighting_params: Dictionary containing 'K', 'SGs' (list of {xi, lambda, mu}), 
                         or 'VSG_volume' for volumetric method.
        method: 'analytic' for direct integration, 'point_light' for 5-light approximation.
    
    Returns:
        Color C.
    """
    if method == "analytic":
        # 1. Approximate Cosine Term
        # G_c(l; n, 2.133, 1.17)
        cosine_sg = spherical_gaussian(view_dir, normal, 2.133, 1.17) # Simplified: evaluating at specific direction
        
        # 2. Approximate BRDF Specular Lobe
        # Fit single monochromatic SG to specular lobe based on roughness beta and metalness m
        # fr_SG = diffuse_lobe + specular_lobe_SG
        specular_sg = fit_specular_sg(normal, view_dir, roughness=0.1, metalness=0.5) # Parameters from [S2, unofficial]
        
        # 3. Integrate Incident Radiance
        # L_i(omega) = sum_{k=1}^K G(omega; xi_k, lambda_k, mu_k)
        # The integral of product of SGs has an analytic form [S2, unofficial]
        total_radiance = 0.0
        for sg in lighting_params['SGs']:
            # Analytic integral of G(omega; xi_l, lambda_l, mu_l) * G(omega; xi_s, lambda_s, mu_s)
            # Note: Exact analytic formula for product of two SGs on sphere is complex;
            # sources state it allows "fast, analytic integration" [S2, unofficial] but do not provide the closed form.
            # Implementation note: Use pre-derived analytic integral for SG product.
            contribution = analytic_integral_sg_product(sg, specular_sg)
            total_radiance += contribution
            
        # 4. Combine
        # S_SG = integral( fr_SG * L_i * G_c )
        color = integrate_sg_product(specular_sg, lighting_params['SGs'], cosine_sg)
        return color

    elif method == "point_light":
        # Render each SG as 5 point lights [S3]
        total_shading = 0.0
        gamma = 1.0 # Hyperparameter [S3]
        
        for sg in lighting_params['SGs']:
            # Generate 5 light positions/directions around SG center xi
            lights = generate_point_lights_from_sg(sg)
            
            for light in lights:
                # Calculate direction from surface to light
                l_dir = normalize(light.position - surface_point)
                dist = distance(light.position, surface_point)
                
                # Diffuse term: N . L
                diffuse = max(dot(normal, l_dir), 0.0)
                
                # Inverse square attenuation with hyperparameter gamma
                attenuation = light.intensity / (gamma * dist**2)
                
                total_shading += diffuse * attenuation
                
        return total_shading

def spherical_gaussian(direction, axis, sharpness, amplitude):
    """
    Evaluate a single Spherical Gaussian.
    G(w; xi, lambda, mu) = mu * exp(lambda * (xi . w - 1))
    """
    dot_product = dot(axis, direction)
    return amplitude * exp(sharpness * (dot_product - 1.0))
```

### Parameters
*   **K**: Number of SGs. Typical values: 128 for HDR env maps, 32 for prediction [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\gamma$**: Hyperparameter for luminance control in point-light approximation [S3].
*   **$\beta$**: Surface roughness. SG shading is robust to variations in $\beta$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **$\sigma$**: VSG sharpness. If $\sigma \gg 1$, VSG reduces to RGB$\alpha$ [S4].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **High Frequency Loss**: SGs smooth out high-frequency details and sharp corners [S2, unofficial]. They are not precise enough for real-time scenarios requiring high fidelity without a very large number of coefficients [S1, unofficial].
*   **HDR Lighting**: If the environment is HDR, SGs should be placed far from the scene to assume distant lighting [S3].
*   **VSG Degeneracy**: If $\sigma \gg 1$ in VSG, the representation loses directional lighting capability and becomes a standard RGB$\alpha$ volume [S4].
*   **Coefficient Count**: Accurate reconstruction may require significantly more coefficients than dense pixel maps (e.g., 896 SGs vs 98,304 pixels for an HDR map) [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Final NeRF Relighting Equation
A specific application of SG shading within Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for relighting. It combines diffuse ($I_d$), specular ($I_s$), and SG shading ($S_{SG}$) modulated by shadow visibility ($V$) and reflectance ($\mathcal{R}$) [S3]. It allows for material editing and scene relighting by optimizing SG parameters [S3].

### Volumetric Spherical Gaussian (VSG)
A voxel-based representation for 3D spatially-varying lighting where each voxel acts as a light source parameterized by SG lobes [S4]. It captures view-dependent effects and directional lighting in 3D space, unlike standard RGB$\alpha$ volumes. It is calculated via alpha compositing along rays [S4].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: Faster than Monte Carlo (MC) integration because it uses analytic integration of SG products [S2, unofficial]. The cost scales linearly with the number of SGs $K$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity**: Compact. Requires significantly fewer parameters than dense environment maps (e.g., 896 parameters for K=128 SGs vs 98,304 for a dense HDR map) [S2, unofficial]. VSG adds spatial storage overhead ($8 \times X \times Y \times Z$) [S4].
*   **Performance**: Robust to roughness variations [S2, unofficial]. MC is better for mirror-like objects (low roughness) where high frequencies are preserved, but SG is more efficient for moderate-to-high roughness [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations**: Loses high-frequency details [S2, unofficial]. Not suitable for real-time rendering if high precision is required without large $K$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use**: Differentiable rendering, inverse rendering, optimization of lighting/materials, and scenes with moderate roughness [S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use**: Real-time rendering requiring high-fidelity specular highlights with sharp features, or scenes with very low roughness (mirrors) where MC is superior [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
Spherical Gaussian shading was introduced as a compact approximation for direct illumination in differentiable rendering frameworks, specifically within the DIB-R++ pipeline [S2, unofficial]. It builds on the mathematical properties of Spherical Gaussians to allow analytic integration of the rendering equation, facilitating gradient-based optimization of lighting and materials [S2, unofficial]. It has been applied in various contexts including intrinsic decomposition, relighting in NeRFs [S3], and 3D inverse rendering with spatially varying lighting [S4].
