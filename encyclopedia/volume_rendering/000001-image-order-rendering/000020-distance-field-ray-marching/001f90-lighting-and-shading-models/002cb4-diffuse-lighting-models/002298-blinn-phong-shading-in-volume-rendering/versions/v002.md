# Blinn-Phong Shading in Volume Rendering

Also called: Shaded Volume Rendering (SVR)

## Overview
Blinn-Phong Shading in Volume Rendering is a technique that applies the Blinn-Phong lighting model during the ray-marching process to compute surface normals and lighting contributions for volumetric data, creating the illusion of a solid, shaded object rather than a translucent cloud [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from simple alpha-blending volume rendering by incorporating specular highlights and diffuse shading based on estimated surface normals, providing greater depth cues and visual realism for medical and scientific datasets [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates within a fragment shader pipeline, typically using a full-screen triangle to generate rays for every pixel. It combines ray-box intersection, intensity sampling, normal estimation via finite differences, transfer function mapping, and Blinn-Phong lighting into a front-to-back compositing loop.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs**:
    *   `volumeTexture`: A 3D texture (`texture_3d<f32>`) storing the scalar field data [S1, unofficial].
    *   `tfTexture`: A 1D texture (`texture_1d<f32>`) storing the transfer function (color and opacity mapping) [S1, unofficial].
    *   `Uniforms`: A struct containing the view/projection transform matrix (`transform`), light position (`lightPos`), light color (`lightColour`), lighting coefficients (`diffuse`, `specular`, `ambient`), and transfer function size (`tfSize`) [S1, unofficial].
    *   `coord`: The screen-space pixel coordinate (`vec2<f32>`) from the vertex shader [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output**:
    *   `accColour`: A `vec4<f32>` representing the accumulated RGBA color after compositing all samples along the ray [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
**Uniforms Struct Layout**:
```wgsl
struct Uniforms {
    transform: mat4x4<f32>,
    lightPos: vec3<f32>,
    lightColour: vec3<f32>,
    diffuse: f32,
    specular: f32,
    ambient: f32,
    tfSize: f32,
};
```
*   `transform`: $4 \times 4$ matrix mapping clip space/screen coordinates to volume space [S1, unofficial].
*   `tfSize`: Scalar value representing the number of entries in the transfer function texture [S1, unofficial].

**Intensity Encoding**:
The source volume data is stored in an RG8 format (two 8-bit channels). The scalar intensity is reconstructed from the red ($R$) and green ($G$) channels:
$$ \text{intensity} = \frac{R + G \times 255}{256} $$
This maps the 16-bit integer value represented by the two bytes to a normalized float [S1, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Ray Generation**:
    *   Generate a full-screen triangle using vertex indices to produce clip-space positions $(-1, 3)$, $(-1, -1)$, and $(3, -1)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each fragment, transform the screen coordinate $(coord.xy, 0.0, 1.0)$ by the `transform` matrix to obtain the ray origin $\mathbf{pos}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Transform the vector $(0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 0.0)$ by the `transform` matrix to obtain the ray direction $\mathbf{dir}$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Ray-Box Intersection**:
    *   Compute the entry ($t_{near}$) and exit ($t_{far}$) distances of the ray with the volume's axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) defined by $(0,0,0)$ and $\text{size}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate inverse direction: $\mathbf{inv\_dir} = 1 / \mathbf{dir}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute distances to bounds: $\mathbf{bot} = \mathbf{inv\_dir} \times (\mathbf{bboxMin} - \mathbf{pos})$ and $\mathbf{top} = \mathbf{inv\_dir} \times (\mathbf{bboxMax} - \mathbf{pos})$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Determine intersection intervals: $r_{min} = \min(\mathbf{top}, \mathbf{bot})$ and $r_{max} = \max(\mathbf{top}, \mathbf{bot})$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   $t_{near} = \max(r_{min}.x, r_{min}.y, r_{min}.z)$ and $t_{far} = \min(r_{max}.x, r_{max}.y, r_{max}.z)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Clamp $t_{near}$ to $0$ if negative. If $t_{near} > t_{far}$, the ray misses the volume [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Ray Marching Loop**:
    *   Iterate from $t_{near}$ to $t_{far}$ with a fixed step size (or slice-by-slice as implied by the integer iteration in the source) [S1, unofficial].
    *   For each step, compute the current position $\mathbf{p}$ in volume space [S1, unofficial].
    *   Normalize $\mathbf{p}$ to $[0, 1]$ range to sample `volumeTexture` [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Intensity Sampling**:
    *   Sample the volume texture at the normalized coordinates [S1, unofficial].
    *   Decode the intensity $I$ from the RG8 sample: $I = (\text{sample}.r + \text{sample}.g \times 255) / 256$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Normal Calculation**:
    *   Estimate the surface normal $\mathbf{N}$ using central finite differences [S1, unofficial].
    *   Define a small offset $\epsilon$ (e.g., one voxel or a small fraction of the volume size) [G].
    *   Compute the gradient components:
        $$ N_x = I(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon \hat{x}) - I(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon \hat{x}) $$
        $$ N_y = I(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon \hat{y}) - I(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon \hat{y}) $$
        $$ N_z = I(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon \hat{z}) - I(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon \hat{z}) $$
    *   Normalize the gradient vector: $\mathbf{N} = \frac{(N_x, N_y, N_z)}{\|(N_x, N_y, N_z)\|}$ [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Transfer Function Lookup**:
    *   Map the sampled intensity $I$ to a texture coordinate for the 1D transfer function: $u = I \times \text{tfSize}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Sample `tfTexture` at $u$ to obtain the base color $C_{base}$ and opacity $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Blinn-Phong Lighting**:
    *   Compute the light direction $\mathbf{L}$ from $\mathbf{p}$ to `lightPos` and normalize [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the view direction $\mathbf{V}$ from $\mathbf{p}$ to the camera position and normalize [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute the half vector $\mathbf{H} = \frac{\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{V}}{\|\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{V}\|}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate diffuse term: $D = \max(0.0, \mathbf{N} \cdot \mathbf{L}) \times \text{diffuse}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Calculate specular term: $S = \text{pow}(\max(0.0, \mathbf{N} \cdot \mathbf{H}), \text{shininess}) \times \text{specular}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Combine with ambient term $A = \text{ambient}$:
        $$ C_{lit} = \text{lightColour} \times (C_{base} \times (D + A) + S) $$
    *   Note: The source implementation applies the light color to the entire term: `litColor = uniforms.lightColour * (colour.rgb * (diffuse + ambient) + specular)` [S1, unofficial].

8.  **Front-to-Back Compositing**:
    *   Update the accumulated color `accColour` using alpha blending [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ \alpha_{new} = (1.0 - \text{accColour}.a) \times \alpha $$
        $$ \text{accColour}.rgb += \alpha_{new} \times C_{lit}.rgb $$
        $$ \text{accColour}.a += \alpha_{new} $$
    *   Terminate the loop early if $\text{accColour}.a \ge 0.95$ [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Constants
SHININESS = 10.0
BREAK_THRESHOLD = 0.95
EPSILON = 1.0 / VOLUME_SIZE # [G] Typical offset for finite differences

def render_svr(coord, uniforms, volume_texture, tf_texture):
    # 1. Ray Generation
    # Transform screen coord to volume space origin
    pos = uniforms.transform * vec4(coord.xy, 0.0, 1.0)
    # Transform direction vector
    dir = uniforms.transform * vec4(0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 0.0)
    
    # 2. Ray-Box Intersection
    bbox_min = vec3(0.0)
    bbox_max = vec3(texture_dimensions(volume_texture))
    near, far = ray_box_intersection(bbox_min, bbox_max, pos.xyz, dir.xyz)
    
    if near > far:
        return vec4(0.0)
        
    near = max(0.0, near)
    
    # 3. Initialization
    acc_colour = vec4(0.0)
    step_size = (far - near) / NUM_STEPS # [G] Assuming fixed step count for implementation
    
    for i in range(NUM_STEPS):
        t = near + i * step_size
        p = pos.xyz + t * dir.xyz
        
        # Check bounds
        if not is_inside_volume(p, bbox_max):
            break
            
        # Normalize to [0,1]
        coords = p / bbox_max
        
        # 4. Intensity Sampling
        sample = texture_sample(volume_texture, coords)
        intensity = (sample.r + sample.g * 255.0) / 256.0
        
        # 5. Normal Calculation (Central Differences)
        # [G] Assuming texture sampling supports coordinate offsets
        offset = EPSILON * bbox_max
        p_plus_x = p + vec3(offset.x, 0, 0)
        p_minus_x = p - vec3(offset.x, 0, 0)
        p_plus_y = p + vec3(0, offset.y, 0)
        p_minus_y = p - vec3(0, offset.y, 0)
        p_plus_z = p + vec3(0, 0, offset.z)
        p_minus_z = p - vec3(0, 0, offset.z)
        
        # Sample neighbors
        i_x = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_plus_x / bbox_max))
        i_x_m = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_minus_x / bbox_max))
        i_y = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_plus_y / bbox_max))
        i_y_m = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_minus_y / bbox_max))
        i_z = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_plus_z / bbox_max))
        i_z_m = decode_intensity(texture_sample(volume_texture, p_minus_z / bbox_max))
        
        normal = normalize(vec3(i_x - i_x_m, i_y - i_y_m, i_z - i_z_m))
        
        # 6. Transfer Function Lookup
        tf_index = intensity * uniforms.tf_size
        tf_sample = texture_sample(tf_texture, tf_index)
        base_color = tf_sample.rgb
        alpha = tf_sample.a
        
        # 7. Blinn-Phong Lighting
        light_dir = normalize(uniforms.light_pos - p)
        view_dir = normalize(-p) # Assuming camera at origin in volume space or adjusted
        half_dir = normalize(light_dir + view_dir)
        
        diffuse = max(0.0, dot(normal, light_dir)) * uniforms.diffuse
        specular = pow(max(0.0, dot(normal, half_dir)), SHININESS) * uniforms.specular
        ambient = uniforms.ambient
        
        lit_color = uniforms.light_colour * (base_color * (diffuse + ambient) + specular)
        
        # 8. Compositing
        alpha_contrib = (1.0 - acc_colour.a) * alpha
        acc_colour.rgb += alpha_contrib * lit_color.rgb
        acc_colour.a += alpha_contrib
        
        if acc_colour.a >= BREAK_THRESHOLD:
            break
            
    return acc_colour

def decode_intensity(sample):
    return (sample.r + sample.g * 255.0) / 256.0

def ray_box_intersection(bbox_min, bbox_max, pos, dir):
    inv_dir = 1.0 / dir
    bot = inv_dir * (bbox_min - pos)
    top = inv_dir * (bbox_max - pos)
    r_min = min(top, bot)
    r_max = max(top, bot)
    
    t_near = max(max(r_min.x, r_min.y), r_min.z)
    t_far = min(min(r_max.x, r_max.y), r_max.z)
    
    return t_near, t_far
```

### Parameters
*   `shininess`: Exponent for the specular term. Default value in the reference implementation is $10.0$ [S1, unofficial].
*   `diffuse`, `specular`, `ambient`: Scalar multipliers for the respective lighting components [S1, unofficial].
*   `tfSize`: Size of the 1D transfer function texture [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision**: The intensity decoding relies on 8-bit integer channels packed into a float texture. This limits the dynamic range and may introduce banding in smooth gradients [S1, unofficial].
*   **Normal Estimation**: Finite differences are sensitive to noise in the volume data. Small offsets may lead to noisy normals, while large offsets may blur fine details [G].
*   **Boundary Conditions**: Sampling outside the volume bounds must be handled to avoid undefined behavior. The ray-box intersection ensures marching stays within bounds, but normal estimation requires sampling neighbors, which may fall outside if the sample point is too close to the edge [G].
*   **Early Termination**: The threshold of $0.95$ for alpha accumulation is a heuristic to save computation. It may cause slight underestimation of opacity in very dense regions [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of samples per ray, determined by the ray length and step size. The early termination condition reduces the average case.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$ per pixel for the compositing state, plus the storage for the 3D volume texture and 1D transfer function texture.
*   **Performance**: The method is computationally intensive due to multiple texture samples per step (for normal estimation) and the lighting calculation. The reference implementation runs in a browser using WebGPU, leveraging parallel fragment shader execution [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths**: Provides realistic shading with specular highlights, enhancing depth perception. Simple to implement within a standard ray-marching framework.
*   **Limitations**: Normal estimation via finite differences can be noisy. The RG8 intensity encoding limits precision. Performance may be lower than unshaded volume rendering due to the extra sampling and calculations per step.

## Background
Blinn-Phong Shading in Volume Rendering, referred to as Shaded Volume Rendering (SVR), was implemented in the `ossium` project, a WebGPU-based application for displaying 3D medical volumes (DICOM files) in the browser [S1, unofficial]. The technique adapts the classic Blinn-Phong lighting model, originally developed for polygonal surfaces, to volumetric data by estimating normals from the scalar field gradient [S1, unofficial].
