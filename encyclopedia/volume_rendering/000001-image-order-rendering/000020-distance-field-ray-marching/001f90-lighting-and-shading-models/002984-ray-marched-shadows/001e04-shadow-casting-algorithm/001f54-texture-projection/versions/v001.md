# Texture Projection

Also called: Texture Mapping, Projective Texture Mapping

## Overview
Texture projection maps 2D textures onto geometric and volumetric data by projecting textures through space onto surfaces [S1, unofficial]. It solves the difficulty of wrapping textures around volumetrically defined objects, where the defining surfaces are not explicitly known, by projecting through space rather than relying on surface parameterization [S1, unofficial]. It differs from direct surface wrapping by treating the texture as a light-like projection, allowing it to be handled as a straightforward extension of the shadow casting algorithm [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volumetric dataset (implicit or explicit surface definition).
*   Geometric polygons (optional, embedded in volumetric data).
*   2D texture map.
*   Projection parameters (origin, direction, or projection matrix).

**Outputs:**
*   Textured surface appearance for the volumetric object or embedded polygons.

**Data Structures:**
*   Texture coordinates $(u, v)$ generated per sample point on the surface.
*   Sample point position $\mathbf{p}$ in world or view space.

**Steps:**
1.  **Determine Surface Location:** Identify the location of the defining surface of the volumetric object. This is the primary challenge, as volumetric data does not inherently provide an explicit boundary [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Project Texture Coordinates:** For each point $\mathbf{p}$ on the surface, compute the corresponding 2D texture coordinates $(u, v)$ by projecting $\mathbf{p}$ through the texture space. This is analogous to the shadow casting process, where the texture acts as the light source [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Sample Texture:** Retrieve the color value from the 2D texture map using the computed $(u, v)$ coordinates.
4.  **Composite:** Blend the sampled texture color with the underlying volumetric or geometric shading.

**Formulas:**
Let $\mathbf{p}$ be the 3D point on the surface. The projection to 2D texture coordinates $(u, v)$ is typically defined by a projection matrix $M$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} u' \\ v' \\ w' \end{bmatrix} = M \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
u = \frac{u'}{w'}, \quad v = \frac{v'}{w'}
$$
Where $(u, v)$ are normalized to $[0, 1]$.

**Parameters:**
*   Projection Matrix $M$: Defines the view frustum or orthographic projection for the texture.
*   Texture Resolution: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Surface Ambiguity:** Wrapping textures around volumetrically defined objects is difficult because it requires knowing where their defining surfaces lie [S1, unofficial].
*   **Spectral Mismatch:** Geometric, volumetric, and texture data each exhibit different spectra. Care must be taken when mixing them to avoid visual artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Embedded Polygons:** Mapping textures onto polygons embedded in volumetric datasets is relatively simple but requires handling the intersection of the texture projection with the polygon geometry [S1, unofficial].

    Projects a 2D texture onto a set of 3D surface points.
    
    Args:

        texture_map: 2D array representing the texture.
        projection_matrix: 3x4 or 4x4 matrix for projecting 3D to 2D texture space.
        
    Returns:
        List of colors corresponding to each surface point.
    """
    colors = []
    for point in surface_points:
        # Homogeneous coordinates
        p_homogeneous = [point.x, point.y, point.z, 1.0]
        
        # Project to texture space
        # Note: Exact matrix multiplication details depend on the projection type
        # (orthographic vs perspective). Assuming standard projective transform.
        uvw = multiply_matrix_vector(projection_matrix, p_homogeneous)
        
        # Perspective divide
        if uvw.w == 0:
            # Handle degenerate case (point at infinity or invalid projection)
            continue
            
        u = uvw.u / uvw.w
        v = uvw.v / uvw.w
        
        # Check bounds
        if 0 <= u <= 1 and 0 <= v <= 1:
            # Sample texture
            color = texture_map.sample(u, v)
            colors.append(color)
        else:
            # Point is outside the texture projection area
            colors.append(None) # Or handle as background/transparent
            
    return colors

## Subtopics

### [Triplanar Texture Projection](2303-triplanar-texture-projection.md)
Triplanar texture projection maps a 2D texture onto a 3D surface by applying planar mapping in three perpendicular planes (XY, XZ, YZ) and interpolating the results based on the surface normal's alignment. It solves the problem of texture stretching that occurs with single-axis planar mapping on complex or curved surfaces, such as spheres, by ensuring the texture orientation adapts to the local geometry. Unlike its siblings, it avoids visible seams and stretching by weighting three orthogonal projections, making it suitable for shading complex volumetric surfaces. It builds on [Texture Projection](2002-texture-projection.md).

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Easier to implement than surface wrapping for volumetric data; integrates with shadow casting algorithms [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires determining surface location for volumetric objects; potential artifacts from spectral mismatch between data types [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When rendering mixtures of geometric and volumetric data where explicit surface parameterization is difficult or unavailable [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Texture projection was introduced in the context of rendering mixtures of geometric and volumetric data [S1, unofficial]. The approach is similar to that described by Feibush, Levoy, and Cook [1980] [S1, unofficial]. A good survey of texture mapping algorithms was written by Heckbert [1986] [S1, unofficial]. The technique is part of the broader field of volume rendering, which encompasses both opaque voxel overpainting and semi-transparent voxel blending [S1, unofficial].
