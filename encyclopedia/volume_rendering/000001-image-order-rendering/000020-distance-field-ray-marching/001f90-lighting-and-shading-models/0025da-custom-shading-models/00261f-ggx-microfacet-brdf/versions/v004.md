# GGX Microfacet BRDF

Also called: GGX BRDF, GGX Microfacet Model, GGX Microfacet Specular Model, GGX Shading, Walter et al. Specular

## Overview
The GGX Microfacet BRDF is a specular reflection model that approximates surface roughness using a Gaussian distribution of microfacet normals [S1, unofficial]. It differs from simpler models like Blinn-Phong by providing physically plausible energy conservation and accurate highlight shapes for rough surfaces, making it the standard for high-quality real-time rendering [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, view direction $\mathbf{v}$, light direction $\mathbf{l}$, roughness parameter $\alpha_{rough}$, and base specular reflectance $F_0$.
**Outputs:** Specular reflectance value (scalar or vector).

**Data Structures:**
No complex data structures are required for the core calculation. Inputs are typically stored as:
- `roughness`: float, typically in $[0.0, 1.0]$.
- `F0`: float (dielectric) or vec3 (metallic), typically $0.04$ for dielectrics, material-specific for metals.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Half Vector:** Calculate the half vector $\mathbf{h} = \frac{\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{v}\|}$.
2.  **Compute Alpha:** Square the roughness parameter: $\alpha = \text{roughness}^2$.
3.  **Compute Normal Distribution Function (D):**
    $$ D(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{h}, \alpha) = \frac{\alpha^2}{\pi \left( (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{h})^2 (\alpha^2 - 1) + 1 \right)^2} $$
    Add a small epsilon to the denominator to prevent division by zero [S1, S2, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Geometry Function (G):**
    Use the Schlick-GGX approximation. First compute the helper parameter $k$:
    $$ k = \frac{(\text{roughness} + 1)^2}{8} $$
    Then compute the single-sided geometry terms for view and light:
    $$ G_1(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\max(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}(1 - k) + k, \epsilon)} $$
    $$ G_1(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{l}) = \frac{\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}}{\max(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}(1 - k) + k, \epsilon)} $$
    The total geometry term is $G = G_1(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{v}) \times G_1(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{l})$ [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Compute Fresnel Function (F):**
    Use the Schlick approximation:
    $$ F(\mathbf{l}, \mathbf{h}, F_0) = F_0 + (1 - F_0)(1 - \mathbf{l} \cdot \mathbf{h})^5 $$
6.  **Combine:**
    $$ f_r = \frac{D \cdot G \cdot F}{4 (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}) (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})} $$
    Add epsilon to the denominator to prevent division by zero [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def ggx_microfacet_brdf(n, v, l, roughness, F0):
    # Ensure vectors are normalized
    # n, v, l are assumed normalized
    
    # 1. Half vector
    h = normalize(l + v)
    
    # 2. Alpha
    alpha = roughness * roughness
    
    # 3. Normal Distribution D
    nh = max(dot(n, h), 0.0)
    alpha_sqr = alpha * alpha
    denom = (nh * nh * (alpha_sqr - 1.0) + 1.0)
    # Add epsilon to prevent division by zero
    D = (alpha_sqr) / (PI * denom * denom + 1e-6)
    
    # 4. Geometry Function G
    k = ((roughness + 1.0) ** 2) / 8.0
    nv = max(dot(n, v), 0.0)
    nl = max(dot(n, l), 0.0)
    
    # Single-sided G
    g1_v = nv / max(nv * (1.0 - k) + k, 1e-6)
    g1_l = nl / max(nl * (1.0 - k) + k, 1e-6)
    G = g1_v * g1_l
    
    # 5. Fresnel F
    lh = max(dot(l, h), 0.0)
    F = F0 + (1.0 - F0) * ((1.0 - lh) ** 5)
    
    # 6. Combine
    # Denominator: 4 * (N.V) * (N.L)
    denom_spec = 4.0 * nv * nl
    # Add epsilon to prevent division by zero
    specular = (D * G * F) / max(denom_spec, 1e-4)
    
    return specular
```

**Parameters:**
- `roughness`: Typical value $0.54$ in some implementations [S1, unofficial]. Range $[0.0, 1.0]$.
- `F0`: Typical value $0.14$ in some implementations [S1, unofficial]. For dielectrics, often $0.04$.

**Edge Cases:**
- Division by zero in $D$ and the final BRDF formula is prevented by adding $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ to denominators [S1, S2, unofficial].
- If $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}$ or $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}$ is zero, the BRDF value approaches zero or infinity depending on the limit; the epsilon guard handles this [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Microfacet BRDF with Simplified GGX and Schlick Fresnel](10259-microfacet-brdf-with-simplified-ggx-and-schlick-fresnel.md)
This variant combines the GGX normal distribution with Schlick Fresnel and a simplified Smith geometry function, as used in the NeRV renderer. It differs from standard implementations by incorporating specific algebraic simplifications from the Filament and Unreal Engine renderers to facilitate analytical factorization for Absorption-Based Scene Diffusion (ABSDF) rendering. The primary distinction is the inclusion of the Lambert cosine term $(\mathbf{n} \cdot \boldsymbol{\omega}_i)$ directly within the specular component's structure to simplify downstream mathematical decomposition, resulting in a non-standard separable form.

### Multi-Scattering Energy Compensation for Rough Metals
A method to ensure energy conservation for rough metals by compensating for energy lost in single-scattering GGX specular lobes, using a precomputed directional albedo table [S3, unofficial]. It uses a precomputed $32 \times 32$ directional albedo table $E(\mu, \alpha)$, where $\mu$ is cosine and $\alpha$ is roughness [S3, unofficial]. This ensures white rough metal reflects $\sim 100\%$ at every roughness, correcting the energy loss inherent in single-scattering approximations [S3, unofficial].

### Specular Reflection
Models perfectly smooth surfaces where outgoing radiance is not scattered but mirrors the incident trajectory across the surface normal [S4, unofficial]. The BRDF is defined using Dirac delta functions:
$$ f_{r,m} = \frac{\delta(\cos \theta_i - \cos \theta_r)}{\cos \theta_i} \delta(\phi_i - (\phi_r \pm \pi)) $$
where $\delta$ is the Dirac delta function, $\theta_i, \theta_r$ are incident and reflected polar angles, and $\phi_i, \phi_r$ are azimuthal angles [S4, unofficial]. The pitfall is that the Dirac delta function yields zero for any non-zero parameter mismatch, meaning no light is reflected if the geometric reflection conditions are not perfectly satisfied [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per light per surface point. The calculation involves a few trigonometric operations (dot products, normalization) and power functions.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the standard model. The multi-scattering variant requires $O(1)$ memory access to a $32 \times 32$ texture [S3, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Fast enough for real-time rendering on modern GPUs [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Physically plausible highlights, energy conservation (with proper Fresnel), widely supported in rendering engines.
- **Limitations:** Single-scattering approximation loses energy for very rough metals (addressed by multi-scattering variants) [S3, unofficial]. Assumes isotropic roughness in the base form.

## Background
The GGX distribution was introduced by Walter et al. in "Microfacet Models for Refraction through Rough Surfaces" [S1, unofficial]. It has become the industry standard for specular shading in real-time graphics due to its accurate representation of rough surface highlights [S1, S2, unofficial].
