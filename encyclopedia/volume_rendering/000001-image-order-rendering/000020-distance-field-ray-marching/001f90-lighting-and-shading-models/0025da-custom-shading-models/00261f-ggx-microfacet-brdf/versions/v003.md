# GGX Microfacet BRDF

Also called: GGX BRDF, GGX Microfacet Model, GGX Microfacet Specular Model, GGX Shading, Walter et al. Specular

## Overview
The GGX Microfacet BRDF models specular reflection on rough surfaces by assuming the surface consists of microscopic facets with Gaussian-shaped normal distributions, providing a physically plausible approximation for anisotropic or isotropic roughness [S1, S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from simpler models like Blinn-Phong by using a Normal Distribution Function (NDF) that produces longer specular tails, better matching real-world metallic and dielectric materials, and incorporates a geometry function to account for microfacet shadowing and masking [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (pointing from surface to camera), light direction $\mathbf{l}$ (pointing from surface to light), roughness parameter $\alpha_{rough}$, and base specular reflectance $F_0$.
**Outputs:** Specular reflectance value (scalar or vector).

**Data Structures:**
No complex data structures are required for the core calculation. Inputs are typically passed as floating-point scalars or vectors.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Half Vector:** Calculate the half-vector $\mathbf{h}$ between the view and light directions:
    $$ \mathbf{h} = \frac{\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{l}}{\|\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{l}\|} $$
    *Note: In some implementations, $\mathbf{h}$ is normalized directly from $\mathbf{l} - \mathbf{v}$ if $\mathbf{v}$ is defined as camera-to-surface. Ensure consistent vector definitions.*

2.  **Compute Dot Products:** Calculate the necessary dot products, clamping to zero to handle back-facing components:
    $$ N \cdot H = \max(0.0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{h}) $$
    $$ N \cdot V = \max(0.0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}) $$
    $$ N \cdot L = \max(0.0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}) $$
    $$ L \cdot H = \max(0.0, \mathbf{l} \cdot \mathbf{h}) $$

3.  **Compute Alpha:** Convert roughness to alpha:
    $$ \alpha = \alpha_{rough}^2 $$

4.  **Compute Normal Distribution Function (D):** Using the GGX (Trowbridge-Reitz) distribution:
    $$ D(N, H, \alpha) = \frac{\alpha^2}{\pi \left( (N \cdot H)^2 (\alpha^2 - 1) + 1 \right)^2} $$
    *Pitfall:* Add a small epsilon (e.g., $10^{-6}$) to the denominator to prevent division by zero when $N \cdot H \approx 0$ or $\alpha \approx 0$ [S1, S2, unofficial].

5.  **Compute Geometry Function (G):** Using the Schlick-GGX approximation (Smith function):
    First, compute the geometric parameter $k$:
    $$ k = \frac{(\alpha_{rough} + 1)^2}{8} $$
    Then, compute the single-sided geometry terms for view and light:
    $$ G_1(N, V, k) = \frac{N \cdot V}{\max(N \cdot V (1 - k) + k, \epsilon)} $$
    $$ G_1(N, L, k) = \frac{N \cdot L}{\max(N \cdot L (1 - k) + k, \epsilon)} $$
    The total geometry term is:
    $$ G(N, V, L, \alpha) = G_1(N, V, k) \cdot G_1(N, L, k) $$

6.  **Compute Fresnel Function (F):** Using the Schlick approximation:
    $$ F(L, H, F_0) = F_0 + (1 - F_0)(1 - L \cdot H)^5 $$

7.  **Combine:** Compute the specular BRDF value:
    $$ f_r = \frac{D(N, H, \alpha) \cdot G(N, V, L, \alpha) \cdot F(L, H, F_0)}{4 (N \cdot V) (N \cdot L)} $$
    *Pitfall:* Add epsilon to the denominator $4 (N \cdot V) (N \cdot L)$ to prevent division by zero [S1, S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def ggx_microfacet_brdf(n, v, l, roughness, f0):
    # 1. Half vector
    h = normalize(v + l)
    
    # 2. Dot products
    nh = max(dot(n, h), 0.0)
    nv = max(dot(n, v), 0.0)
    nl = max(dot(n, l), 0.0)
    lh = max(dot(l, h), 0.0)
    
    # 3. Alpha
    alpha = roughness * roughness
    alpha_sqr = alpha * alpha
    
    # 4. D (GGX NDF)
    denom = nh * nh * (alpha_sqr - 1.0) + 1.0
    d = alpha_sqr / (3.14159265359 * denom * denom + 1e-6)
    
    # 5. G (Schlick-GGX Geometry)
    k = (roughness + 1.0) * (roughness + 1.0) / 8.0
    g1_v = nv / max(nv * (1.0 - k) + k, 1e-6)
    g1_l = nl / max(nl * (1.0 - k) + k, 1e-6)
    g = g1_v * g1_l
    
    # 6. F (Schlick Fresnel)
    f = f0 + (1.0 - f0) * pow(1.0 - lh, 5.0)
    
    # 7. Combine
    numerator = d * g * f
    denominator = 4.0 * nv * nl + 1e-4
    specular = numerator / denominator
    
    return specular
```

**Parameters:**
- `roughness`: Surface roughness. Typical value: 0.54 [S1, unofficial].
- `f0`: Base specular reflectance. Typical value: 0.14 for non-metals [S1, unofficial]. For metals, this is derived from the index of refraction.

## Subtopics

### Multi-Scattering Energy Compensation for Rough Metals
A method to ensure energy conservation for rough metals by compensating for energy lost in single-scattering GGX specular lobes, using a precomputed directional albedo table [S3, unofficial]. It uses a precomputed 32x32 directional albedo table $E(\mu, \alpha)$, where $\mu$ is cosine and $\alpha$ is roughness [S3, unofficial]. This ensures white rough metal reflects ~100% at every roughness, correcting the energy loss inherent in single-scattering approximations [S3, unofficial].

### Specular Reflection
Models perfectly smooth surfaces where outgoing radiance is not scattered but mirrors the incident trajectory across the surface normal [S4, unofficial]. The BRDF is defined using Dirac delta functions:
$$ f_{r,m} = \frac{\delta(\cos \theta_i - \cos \theta_r)}{\cos \theta_i} \delta(\phi_i - (\phi_r \pm \pi)) $$
where $\delta$ is the Dirac delta function, $\theta_i, \theta_r$ are incident and reflected polar angles, and $\phi_i, \phi_r$ are azimuthal angles [S4, unofficial]. The BRDF yields zero unless $\theta_r = \theta_i$ and $\phi_r = \phi_i \pm \pi$ [S4, unofficial]. A pitfall is that the Dirac delta function yields zero for any non-zero parameter mismatch, meaning no light is reflected if the geometric reflection conditions are not perfectly satisfied [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per surface point per light. The calculation involves a few trigonometric operations (dot products, normalization, power functions).
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for the core model. Multi-scattering variants require storage for a precomputed albedo table (e.g., 32x32 floats) [S3, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Fast enough for real-time rendering. The Schlick approximation for Fresnel and Geometry is computationally inexpensive compared to exact Fresnel or geometry functions.
- **Strengths:** Physically plausible appearance for rough surfaces; widely supported and understood; good balance between quality and performance.
- **Limitations:** Single-scattering approximation loses energy for very rough metals (addressed by multi-scattering variants) [S3, unofficial]. Does not account for subsurface scattering or thin-film effects without extensions.
- **When to use:** Standard specular shading for rough surfaces in real-time or offline rendering.
- **When not to use:** For perfectly smooth mirrors (use Specular Reflection model) or when high-fidelity energy conservation for rough metals is critical without approximation (use multi-scattering compensation).

## Background
The GGX Microfacet BRDF is based on the work of Walter et al. in "Microfacet Models for Refraction through Rough Surfaces" (2007) [S1, unofficial]. It uses the GGX (Generalized Gaussian) distribution for the normal distribution function, which was shown to provide a better fit to measured data than the Beckmann distribution for many materials. The Schlick approximation for Fresnel and the Schlick-GGX geometry function are commonly paired with it for efficiency [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is widely used in modern real-time engines and voxel-based rendering pipelines [S1, S2, unofficial].
