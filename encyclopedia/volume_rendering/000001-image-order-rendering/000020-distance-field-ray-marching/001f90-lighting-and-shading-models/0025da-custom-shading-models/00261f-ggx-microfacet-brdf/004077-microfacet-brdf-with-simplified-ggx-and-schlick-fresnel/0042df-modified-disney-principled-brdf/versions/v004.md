# Modified Disney Principled BRDF

Also called: ABSDF Base Material, Base Material Model

## Overview
The Modified Disney Principled BRDF is a simplified material model derived from the Disney Principled BRDF [Burley 2012], specifically adapted for use in the ABSDF (Aggregated Bidirectional Surface Distribution Function) factorization framework [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently aggregating complex surface appearances by reducing the original multi-lobe Disney model to a core diffuse-specular structure. It sets itself apart from its parent, the [Microfacet BRDF with Simplified GGX and Schlick Fresnel](10259-microfacet-brdf-with-simplified-ggx-and-schlick-fresnel.md), by explicitly adopting a Lambertian diffuse term and omitting sheen/clearcoat lobes to facilitate analytical integration over parameter distributions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Incident direction $\omega_i$ and outgoing direction $\omega_o$.
- Surface normal $n$.
- Half vector $\omega_h = \frac{\omega_i + \omega_o}{\|\omega_i + \omega_o\|}$.
- Material parameters $\beta_B = (\alpha, \beta_c, \beta_m, \beta_s)$:
  - $\alpha$: Roughness (GGX parameter).
  - $\beta_c$: Base color (diffuse albedo).
  - $\beta_m$: Metallic factor.
  - $\beta_s$: Specular intensity (dielectric reflectance).
- All parameters can be spatially varying.

**Outputs:**
- Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) value $f_{disney}(\omega_i, \omega_o)$.

**Data Structures:**
- The model assumes double-sided surfaces [S1, unofficial].
- Parameters are stored as floating-point values in range $[0, 1]$ where applicable.

**Formulas:**
The total BRDF is the sum of diffuse and specular components:
$$ f_{disney}(\omega_i, \omega_o) = f_d(\omega_i, \omega_o) + f_s(\omega_i, \omega_o) $$

1. **Diffuse Component ($f_d$):**
   Uses a Lambertian model instead of the original empirical retro-reflection model [S1, unofficial].
   $$ f_d(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \frac{(1 - \beta_m)\beta_c}{\pi} $$

2. **Specular Component ($f_s$):**
   Combines metallic and dielectric Fresnel reflection using the microfacet model [S1, unofficial]:
   $$ f_s(\omega_i, \omega_o) = \frac{D(\omega_h; \alpha) G(\omega_i, \omega_o; \alpha)}{4 |n \cdot \omega_o| |n \cdot \omega_i|} \left[ \beta_m F(\omega_h, \omega_o; \beta_c) + (1 - \beta_m) F(\omega_h, \omega_o; \beta_s) \right] $$
   
   Where:
   - $D(\omega_h; \alpha)$ is the Trowbridge-Reitz (GGX) distribution.
   - $G(\omega_i, \omega_o; \alpha)$ is the shadowing-masking function.
   - $F(\omega_h, \omega_o; r_0)$ is the Schlick Fresnel reflectance:
     $$ F(\omega_h, \omega_o; r_0) = r_0 (1 - F_c) + F_c $$
     $$ F_c = (1 - |\omega_h \cdot \omega_o|)^5 $$
   - $r_0$ is the normal incidence reflectance: $\beta_c$ for the metallic lobe, $\beta_s$ for the dielectric lobe.

**Steps:**
1. Compute the half vector $\omega_h$.
2. Calculate the dot products $n \cdot \omega_i$, $n \cdot \omega_o$, and $\omega_h \cdot \omega_o$.
3. Evaluate the Schlick Fresnel term $F_c$ and the full Fresnel $F$ for both metallic ($r_0=\beta_c$) and dielectric ($r_0=\beta_s$) cases.
4. Evaluate the GGX distribution $D$ and shadowing-masking $G$ using $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial].
5. Compute the specular term using the weighted sum of Fresnel terms based on $\beta_m$.
6. Compute the diffuse term using $\beta_c$ and $\beta_m$.
7. Sum $f_d$ and $f_s$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Double-sided assumption:** The model assumes visibility from both sides, ignoring back-face culling [S1, unofficial].
- **Roughness limits:** Standard GGX singularities at $\alpha=0$ or grazing angles must be handled by the underlying $D$ and $G$ implementations.
- **Parameter ranges:** $\beta_c, \beta_m, \beta_s$ are typically in $[0, 1]$. Values outside this range may produce non-physical results.

## Subtopics

### [Specular ABSDF Factorization](10872-specular-absdf-factorization.md)
Specular ABSDF Factorization is a method for computing the aggregated specular response of a surface with varying material parameters (roughness, orientation) by factorizing the integral of the microfacet BRDF over a probability distribution of these parameters. It solves the problem of efficiently evaluating the expectation of a complex microfacet distribution when the roughness $\alpha$ and normal $n$ are not single values but follow a joint probability density function $p_Y(\gamma)$. It sets itself apart from its siblings by explicitly separating the Fresnel mix terms from the microfacet distribution convolution, allowing the shadowing-masking term $G$ to be applied post-convolution due to its smoothness, and supporting orientation-varying parameters via spherical kernel splatting.

### [Phys3DGS BRDF Function](11071-phys3dgs-brdf-function.md)
A physically-based Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) used in 3D Gaussian Splatting for inverse rendering, consisting of a diffuse component and a specular component based on the GGX microfacet model with a correlated Smith masking-shadowing function. It differs from its parent, the Modified Disney Principled BRDF, by employing a specific roughness remapping ($\alpha = r^2$) and a correlated Smith geometry function rather than the separable Schlick-GGX approximation, aiming for higher physical accuracy in the context of Gaussian splatting optimization.

### [Isotropic Disney BRDF](14196-isotropic-disney-brdf.md)
The Isotropic Disney BRDF is a simplified material model based on the Cook-Torrance microfacet framework, designed to model reflectance for both metals and plastics in a unified manner. It is a restricted subset of the full Disney Principled BRDF, optimized for differentiable rendering and optimization tasks where ambiguity from high-dimensional parameter spaces must be reduced. Unlike the full model, it restricts optimization to opaque surfaces with spatially varying diffuse albedo and global constants for roughness, specular albedo, and metalness.

## Operating characteristics
- **Complexity:** The base evaluation is $O(1)$ per sample. Aggregation via ABSDF factorization involves convolutions of Spherical Gaussians, which depend on the number of basis functions used to approximate the parameter distributions.
- **Performance:** Faster than the full Disney BRDF due to the omission of sheen and clearcoat lobes and the use of Lambertian diffuse [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Simple parameterization, analytical tractability for aggregation, wide applicability to standard PBR assets.
- **Limitations:** Lacks detailed surface features like sheen and clearcoat; Lambertian diffuse is less accurate for non-Lambertian materials compared to the original Disney model.

## Background
The Modified Disney Principled BRDF originates from the ABSDF (Efficient Scene Appearance Aggregation for Level-of-Detail Rendering) framework [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to support the widest applicability by targeting the Disney Principled BRDF [Burley 2012], one of the most commonly used models in production. The modifications were made to enable efficient analytical integration for level-of-detail rendering.
