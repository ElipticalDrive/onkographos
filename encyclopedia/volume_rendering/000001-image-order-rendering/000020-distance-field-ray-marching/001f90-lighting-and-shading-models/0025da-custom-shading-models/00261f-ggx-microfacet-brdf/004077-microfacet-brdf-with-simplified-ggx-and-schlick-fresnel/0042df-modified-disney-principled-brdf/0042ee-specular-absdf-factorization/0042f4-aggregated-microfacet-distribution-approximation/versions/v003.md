# Aggregated Microfacet Distribution Approximation

Also called: Beta Distribution Fitting, Roughness Aggregation

## Overview
This method approximates the aggregated microfacet distribution $\hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h)$, which represents the statistical expectation of microfacet distributions over a distribution of roughness values $\alpha$ [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently representing complex, heterogeneous material roughness in a scene by fitting a parametric roughness distribution (Beta) to a weighted sum of two GGX lobes [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its parent, Specular ABSDF Factorization, by providing the specific mathematical mechanism for aggregating roughness statistics into a tractable BRDF approximation [10872-specular-absdf-factorization.md].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Shape parameters $a, b$ of the Beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$ modeling the marginal density $p_A(\alpha)$ of roughness [S1, unofficial].
- The microfacet distribution function $D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha)$, typically GGX [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Weights $m_1, m_2$ and roughness parameters $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ for the two-lobed approximation [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- A 2D lookup table storing the mapping $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2) = M_2(a, b)$ for discrete values of $a$ and $b$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Model the roughness distribution $p_A(\alpha)$ using a Beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$ [S1, unofficial].
2. Define the aggregated microfacet distribution as the expectation:
   $$ \hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h) = \int_0^1 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha) p_A(\alpha) d\alpha $$
   [S1, unofficial]
3. Approximate $\hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h)$ using a weighted sum of two microfacet lobes:
   $$ \hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h) \approx m_1 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha_1) + m_2 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha_2) $$
   where $m_1 + m_2 = 1$ [S1, unofficial].
4. Perform a nonlinear least-square fit to determine the mapping $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2) = M_2(a, b)$ that minimizes the error between the integral and the two-lobe approximation [S1, unofficial].
5. Store the resulting mapping in a 2D table indexed by $a$ and $b$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $a, b$: Shape parameters of the Beta distribution. Typical values are not given in the sources. The parameters must be estimated from asset data [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The roughness $\alpha$ is bounded in $[0, 1]$. Using a Gaussian distribution is invalid because its tails extend beyond this range, causing errors [S1, unofficial].
- The number of lobes $k$ can be extended, but $k=2$ is chosen as the balance between cost and accuracy [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Aggregated Microfacet Distribution via Beta Distribution](10888-aggregated-microfacet-distribution-via-beta-distribution.md)
This method models the aggregated microfacet roughness distribution $p_A(\alpha)$ using a Beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$ to estimate the statistical spread of roughness values $\alpha$ from asset data. It is chosen over Gaussian approximations because the Beta distribution is naturally bounded within the valid $[0, 1]$ range of $\alpha$, preventing the invalid range errors that occur when Gaussian tails exceed these bounds.

### [Aggregated Bidirectional Scattering Distribution Function (ABSDF)](13367-aggregated-bidirectional-scattering-distribution-function-ab.md)
A method for efficient scene appearance aggregation for Level-of-Detail rendering, based on a novel formulation for far-field scene aggregation with the definition of ABSDF (Aggregated Bidirectional Scattering Distribution Function), which captures the aggregated appearance of all surfaces within a volume [13367-aggregated-bidirectional-scattering-distribution-function-ab.md].

### 2-Lobe Microfacet Distribution Fit
A method to fit aggregated microfacet distributions using a 2-lobe weighted sum, compared against ground-truth and single-lobe fits with simple averaged roughness [S1, unofficial]. The 2-lobe fit is overall more accurate and guaranteed to be never worse than simple fits [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources. The fitting process is performed offline to create a lookup table [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the 2D mapping table $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2) = M_2(a, b)$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** More accurate than single-lobe fits with simple averaged roughness [S1, unofficial]. Guaranteed to be never worse than simple fits [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** The fitting process is more tedious than using a truncated Gaussian, although truncated Gaussians can be restricted to the valid range [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When representing heterogeneous material roughness in a scene requires accurate aggregation of microfacet distributions [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When a simpler approximation is sufficient and accuracy is not critical [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the paper "Efficient Scene Appearance Aggregation for Level-of-Detail Rendering" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the need for efficient scene appearance aggregation by approximating the aggregated microfacet distribution, which is the expectation of the microfacet distribution over a roughness distribution [S1, unofficial]. The use of the Beta distribution is motivated by the bounded nature of roughness $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
