# Aggregated Microfacet Distribution Approximation

Also called: Beta Distribution Fitting, Roughness Aggregation

## Overview
This method approximates the aggregated microfacet distribution $\hat{D}_{mic}$, which represents the expectation of microfacet distributions over a distribution of roughness values, by fitting a weighted sum of two GGX lobes [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem that integrating a microfacet distribution over a continuous roughness distribution (such as a beta distribution) has no closed-form solution [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from single-lobe approximations (which use simple averaged roughness) by providing higher accuracy and guaranteeing never to be worse than the simple fit [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Shape parameters $a, b$ of the beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$ modeling the roughness marginal density $p_A(\alpha)$ [S1, unofficial].
- The microfacet distribution function $D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha)$, typically GGX [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Weights $m_1, m_2$ and roughness parameters $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ for the two-lobed approximation [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- A 2D lookup table storing the mapping $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2) = M_2(a, b)$ for various $(a, b)$ pairs [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Model the roughness distribution $p_A(\alpha)$ using a beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$, where $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ [S1, unofficial].
2. Define the aggregated microfacet distribution as the integral:
   $$ \hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h) = \int_0^1 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha) p_A(\alpha) d\alpha $$
   [S1, unofficial]
3. Approximate $\hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h)$ using a weighted sum of two microfacet lobes:
   $$ \hat{D}_{mic}(\omega_h) \approx m_1 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha_1) + m_2 D_{mic}(\omega_h; \alpha_2) $$
   where $m_1 + m_2 = 1$ [S1, unofficial].
4. Perform a nonlinear least-squares fit to determine the mapping $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2) = M_2(a, b)$ that minimizes the error between the integral and the approximation [S1, unofficial].
5. Store the resulting mapping in a 2D table indexed by $a$ and $b$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $a, b$: Shape parameters of the beta distribution. Typical values are not explicitly given in the sources, but they must be estimated from the underlying roughness statistics [S1, unofficial].
- $k=2$: The number of lobes used in the approximation. The source notes that $k=2$ provides a good balance between cost and accuracy, though the method extends to arbitrary $k$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The roughness $\alpha$ is bounded in $[0, 1]$. Gaussian distributions are invalid choices for $p_A(\alpha)$ due to their support [S1, unofficial].
- The fitting process for alternative distributions (e.g., truncated Gaussian) is noted as more tedious [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Aggregated Microfacet Distribution via Beta Distribution](10888-aggregated-microfacet-distribution-via-beta-distribution.md)
A method to represent the roughness distribution $p_A(\alpha)$ using a beta distribution $B(\alpha; a, b)$ to model aggregated microfacet roughness. It is chosen because the beta distribution has the correct support $[0, 1]$ for roughness and is reasonably expressive, unlike Gaussian distributions.

### 2-Lobe Microfacet Distribution Fit
A specific instance of the approximation using $k=2$ lobes. It is compared against ground-truth aggregated distributions and single-lobe fits with simple averaged roughness. It is overall more accurate and guaranteed to be never worse than simple fits [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- Pre-computation: Requires a nonlinear least-squares fit to populate the 2D lookup table. The complexity depends on the resolution of the table and the convergence of the fit [S1, unofficial].
- Runtime: $O(1)$ table lookup for $(m_1, \alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ given $(a, b)$, followed by the evaluation of two microfacet distributions [S1, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
- Not given in the sources.

**Scaling:**
- The method scales with the number of lobes $k$. The source states $k=2$ is a good balance, implying higher $k$ increases accuracy at the cost of performance [S1, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- Strengths: Higher accuracy than single-lobe fits; guaranteed not to be worse than simple fits; handles bounded roughness correctly [S1, unofficial].
- Limitations: Requires pre-computation and storage of a lookup table; the fitting process is non-trivial [S1, unofficial].

**When to use it:**
- When accurate appearance aggregation is needed for varying roughness distributions in level-of-detail rendering [S1, unofficial].

**When not:**
- When computational resources are extremely limited and single-lobe approximation suffices [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the paper "Efficient Scene Appearance Aggregation for Level-of-Detail Rendering" [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the need to efficiently render scenes with aggregated microfacet distributions, particularly in the context of level-of-detail rendering where roughness may vary spatially or temporally [S1, unofficial].
