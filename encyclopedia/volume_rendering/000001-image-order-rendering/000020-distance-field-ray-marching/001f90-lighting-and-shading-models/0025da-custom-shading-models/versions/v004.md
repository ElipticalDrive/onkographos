# Custom Shading Models

Also called: Custom Shading Routines, Per-voxel Shading Callbacks

## Overview
Custom Shading Models allow developers to replace standard volume rendering lookup tables or fixed lighting equations with user-defined logic to compute the color of surface points or voxels during ray marching. This family solves the problem of visual fidelity and artistic control, enabling complex material behaviors (such as metallic reflections, procedural road surfaces, or specific atmospheric effects) that cannot be expressed by simple distance-color blending or standard diffuse models. What sets this family apart is the mechanism of execution: instead of relying on pre-computed tables or rigid mathematical formulas, it employs either callback routines invoked per-voxel or custom initialization of shading tables, trading potential performance for arbitrary computational flexibility [S1].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a surface point $\mathbf{p}$ and its associated geometric data, primarily the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$. The normal is typically derived from the Signed Distance Function (SDF) $f(\mathbf{x})$ using the gradient:
$$ \mathbf{n} = \frac{\nabla f(\mathbf{p})}{\|\nabla f(\mathbf{p})\|} $$
where $\nabla f(\mathbf{p})$ is estimated via finite differences or analytical derivatives [S1, S10, unofficial].

The core shading computation generally follows this structure:
1.  **Geometric Setup:** Determine the view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (from $\mathbf{p}$ to camera) and light direction $\mathbf{l}$ (from $\mathbf{p}$ to light source) [S7, S10, unofficial].
2.  **Occlusion/Ambient Occlusion:** Calculate shadow factors or ambient occlusion values to determine how much light reaches $\mathbf{p}$ from the environment or specific lights [S3, S4, S5, S10, unofficial].
3.  **Reflectance Model:** Compute the diffuse and specular components based on the angle between $\mathbf{n}$, $\mathbf{l}$, and the reflection vector $\mathbf{r}$ [S1, S2, S10, unofficial].
4.  **Color Accumulation:** Combine the base material color with the lighting contributions, often modulated by depth cueing or atmospheric effects [S5, S7, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [GGX Microfacet BRDF](3715-ggx-microfacet-brdf.md)
A specular reflection model that approximates surface scattering using a Gaussian distribution of microfacet normals (GGX) combined with the Schlick Fresnel approximation and a Smith geometry function. It provides a physically plausible long-tail specular response for rough surfaces. Choose this when physically accurate rough specular highlights are required for SDF surfaces.

### [Voxel Shading Pipeline](3898-voxel-shading-pipeline.md)
A per-voxel lighting model that computes illumination for surface points identified by ray marching through a voxel grid. It combines Lambertian diffuse reflection, rim lighting, indirect ambient contribution, and a microfacet-based specular term, modulated by hard shadows and tone mapping. It differs from continuous surface shading by operating on discrete voxel coordinates, often using the voxel face normal directly rather than interpolating across a mesh, and typically employs hard shadows due to the binary nature of voxel occupancy. Choose this for voxel-based engines requiring a complete lighting pipeline including tone mapping.

### [Blinn-Phong with Metallic Reflections](4252-blinn-phong-with-metallic-reflections.md)
A custom shading model for ray-marched signed distance fields (SDFs) that extends the classic Blinn-Phong approximation with environment reflections and emissive contributions to simulate urban night scenes. It distinguishes itself from standard Blinn-Phong by modulating specular intensity, power, and reflection weight based on a material's metallic property, and from GGX microfacet models by using a simplified Schlick-like Fresnel term rather than a full microfacet distribution. Choose this for stylized or semi-realistic metallic materials where full microfacet complexity is unnecessary.

### [Fast Shading for Voxel Engines](5606-fast-shading-for-voxel-engines.md)
Fast Shading for Voxel Engines is a CPU-based lighting algorithm designed for voxel engines that assumes vertical sun rays. It modifies voxel palette indices to simulate lighting by darkening voxels based on the occupancy of their immediate neighbors, effectively simulating shadows cast by adjacent blocks. It sets itself apart from general-purpose shading models by operating directly on discrete palette indices rather than computing continuous radiance, and by relying on a fixed, pre-defined neighborhood kernel rather than ray-marching for occlusion. Choose this for performance-critical voxel engines where lighting is baked or updated on the CPU rather than per-pixel.

### [Gradient-Based Shading](6896-gradient-based-shading.md)
Gradient-Based Shading is a discrete volume rendering (DVR) technique that renders 3D textures using front-to-back accumulation, shading voxels via a pseudo-normal estimated from the volume gradient and a directional light model. It differs from sibling methods like [GGX Microfacet BRDF](3715-ggx-microfacet-brdf.md) or [Blinn-Phong with Metallic Reflections](4252-blinn-phong-with-metallic-reflections.md) by relying on a simple Lambertian diffuse model driven by finite-difference normals rather than complex microfacet distributions or metallic reflections. It is distinct from [Fast Shading for Voxel Engines](5606-fast-shading-for-voxel-engines.md) by its explicit use of a transfer function LUT and continuous/discrete normalization modes within a standard raymarching loop. Choose this for scientific volume visualization where material properties are defined by transfer functions.

### Light Evaluation for SDF Faces
Applies lighting (diffuse, specular, ambient) to a face color based on normal and light direction [S2, unofficial]. It sets the normal's Z component to its absolute value, normalizes the light vector from the sine and cosine of a light angle, and computes the color by adding the specular contribution multiplied by the face alpha to the maximum of the face color RGB and zero. The result is multiplied by a diffuse factor $(1 - \text{dot}(\mathbf{n}, \mathbf{l}) \times \text{_Diffuse})$ and an ambient factor $\text{lerp}(\text{_Ambient}, 1, \mathbf{n}_z^2)$ [S2, unofficial].

### Procedural Road Surface
A procedural surface shader for roads that differentiates between asphalt, curb/shoulder, and terrain based on lateral distance to the road centerline, including emissive lane markings [S3, unofficial]. It calculates the distance $d$ from the road centerline $x$ at a given $z$. If within the road half-width, it sets albedo to dark gray, metal to 0.35, and calculates lane lines using smoothstep for edge lines, center lines, and dashed separators, adding emissive cyan color for lines. Outside the road but within the shoulder width, it sets albedo to dark gray, metal to 0.1, and adds a magenta strip at the road edge. Beyond that, it sets albedo to very dark with no emissive properties [S3, unofficial]. Key parameters include an asphalt metal value of 0.35, lane line color of $\text{vec3}(0.15, 1.0, 1.1) \times 1.3$, edge line offset of $\text{ROAD\_HALF\_WIDTH} - 0.3$, center line offset of 0.18, and dashed line frequency of $p.z \times 0.18$ [S3, unofficial].

### Skylight Approximation
Extending the shading method to multiple light sources to approximate global illumination from skylight (sunlight scattered by atmosphere) [S4, unofficial]. It shades clouds from multiple light sources and stores the resulting particle colors ($i_k$) from all shading iterations. At render time, it evaluates the phase function at each particle once per light to approximate global illumination [S4, unofficial]. This technique is directional and results in shadowing in the clouds as well as anisotropic scattering from multiple light directions and intensities, which is better than a simple ambient contribution. An ambient term is added to compensate for lost scattered light due to the scattering approximation [S4, unofficial].

### Triangle Subdivision Shading
Divides each triangle into four subtriangles by splitting each edge in the middle, then applies either Gouraud or Phong shading algorithm to the sub-triangles [S5]. This process can be iterated several times. The `smooth` argument specifies one plus the number of times to divide the triangles; `smooth=1` means no subdivision [S5]. Using too high a level of smooth can cause rendering quality to deteriorate due to artifacts from polygon filling, exacerbated on devices using anti-aliasing [S5].

## Background
The concept of custom shading in volume rendering originated with the VolPack library, which provided a standard shading model but allowed users to override it via callback routines or custom lookup table initialization [S1]. The VolPack User's Guide details that users could define a callback routine (e.g., `vpSetCallback(vpc, VP_GRAY_SHADE_FUNC, myshader)`) to compute colors per voxel, though this could lead to significant performance degradation [S1]. This flexibility laid the groundwork for modern custom shading models in ray marching, where developers implement complex BRDFs (like GGX), voxel-specific pipelines, and procedural materials directly in shader code or CPU routines.
