# STEVE 4D Normal Vector Computation

Also called: 4D Space-Time Normal Formulation, STEVE 4D Extension, STEVE 4D Normal Vector Computation & Subspace Orientation

## Overview
This method computes the oriented normal vector of a 4D simplex (tetrahedron) defined by four space-time events, providing the geometric foundation for the 4D extension of the STEVE algorithm. It solves the problem of determining consistent surface orientations in relativistic space-time data, which is required for extracting 4D volume boundaries in simulations such as heavy-ion collisions. It differs from standard 3D normal computation by incorporating time as a fourth dimension and handling the metric signature (relativistic vs. non-relativistic) to ensure orientation-preserving construction of higher-dimensional polytopes.

## How it works
**Inputs:** Four 4D events (vertices of a tetrahedron) $x^\mu_1, x^\mu_2, x^\mu_3, x^\mu_4$, where each event is a tuple $(x, y, z, t)$.
**Outputs:** A covariant 4-normal vector $d\sigma_\mu$ and a contravariant 4-normal vector $d\sigma^\mu$.
**Parameters:**
- Metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$: Assumed diagonalized $g_{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(g_{11}, g_{22}, g_{33}, g_{44})$.
  - Relativistic case: $(-1, -1, -1, +1)$ [S1, unofficial].
  - Non-relativistic case: $(+1, +1, +1, +1)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The computation operates on difference vectors derived from the input events. Let the indices $1, 2, 3, 4$ correspond to the spatial coordinates $x, y, z$ and the temporal coordinate $t$ respectively.

**Steps:**

1.  **Compute Edge Differences:**
    Calculate the differences between the vertices relative to the first vertex $x^\mu_1$. For $m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    dx_m &= x_{m+1} - x_1 \\
    dy_m &= y_{m+1} - y_1 \\
    dz_m &= z_{m+1} - z_1 \\
    dt_m &= t_{m+1} - t_1
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    These form the components of the difference vectors $dx^\mu_m$. Specifically, $dx^1_m = dx_m$, $dx^2_m = dy_m$, $dx^3_m = dz_m$, $dx^4_m = dt_m$ [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Compute 3D Determinants of Differences:**
    Calculate the determinant $dS^{ikl}$ for all unique combinations of indices $i, k, l \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$:
    $$
    dS^{ikl} = \det \begin{pmatrix}
    dx_1^i & dx_2^i & dx_3^i \\
    dx_1^k & dx_2^k & dx_3^k \\
    dx_1^l & dx_2^l & dx_3^l
    \end{pmatrix}
    $$
    This represents the signed volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the projections of the difference vectors onto the subspace defined by axes $i, k, l$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Contravariant 4-Normal:**
    The contravariant normal $d\sigma^\mu$ is computed by scaling the determinants with the inverse metric components:
    $$
    d\sigma^\mu = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{dS^{234}}{g_{11}}, \frac{dS^{143}}{g_{22}}, \frac{dS^{124}}{g_{33}}, \frac{dS^{132}}{g_{44}} \right)
    $$
    Note the index permutation in the numerator for the second and fourth components ($143$ and $132$) [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Compute Covariant 4-Normal:**
    The covariant normal $d\sigma_\mu$ is obtained by lowering the index with the metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$:
    $$
    d\sigma_\mu = g_{\mu\nu} d\sigma^\nu = \frac{1}{6} (dS^{2344}, dS^{1433}, dS^{1244}, dS^{1322})
    $$
    Here, the notation $dS^{ijkl}$ implies the determinant where the index $j$ is repeated or modified by the metric scaling factor $g_{jj}$ effectively absorbed into the term. Specifically, the source states this form does not depend on the 4D metric under consideration, implying the metric scaling cancels out in the covariant formulation relative to the contravariant one [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Subspace Orientation Rule (for VESTA N-cycles):**
    When applying the marching variant of VESTA to the eight 3D bounding subspaces of a 4D cell (tesseract), inverse orientations must be chosen for the generated VESTA N-cycles in the $x_0$, $y_1$, $z_0$, and $t_1$ subspaces. This ensures consistent, orientation-preserving construction of higher-dimensional polytopes [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_steve_4d_normal(events, metric_diag):
    """
    Compute 4D normal vectors for a tetrahedron defined by 4 events.
    
    Args:
        events: List of 4 tuples (x, y, z, t).
        metric_diag: List of 4 floats [g11, g22, g33, g44].
        
    Returns:
        d_sigma_cov: Tuple of 4 floats (covariant normal).
        d_sigma_contra: Tuple of 4 floats (contravariant normal).
    """
    # Step 1: Compute differences relative to the first event
    x1, y1, z1, t1 = events[0]
    
    dx = [events[1][0] - x1, events[2][0] - x1, events[3][0] - x1]
    dy = [events[1][1] - y1, events[2][1] - y1, events[3][1] - y1]
    dz = [events[1][2] - z1, events[2][2] - z1, events[3][2] - z1]
    dt = [events[1][3] - t1, events[2][3] - t1, events[3][3] - t1]
    
    # Helper to compute 3x3 determinant
    def det3(a, b, c):
        return (a[0] * (b[1]*c[2] - b[2]*c[1]) -
                a[1] * (b[0]*c[2] - b[2]*c[0]) +
                a[2] * (b[0]*c[1] - b[1]*c[0]))

    # Step 2: Compute determinants dS^{ikl}
    # Indices: 1=x, 2=y, 3=z, 4=t
    # We need specific combinations for the normal components
    
    # dS^{234}: y, z, t components
    dS_234 = det3(dy, dz, dt)
    
    # dS^{143}: x, t, z components
    dS_143 = det3(dx, dt, dz)
    
    # dS^{124}: x, y, t components
    dS_124 = det3(dx, dy, dt)
    
    # dS^{132}: x, z, y components
    dS_132 = det3(dx, dz, dy)
    
    # Step 3: Compute Contravariant Normal
    g11, g22, g33, g44 = metric_diag
    
    d_sigma_contra = (
        dS_234 / g11,
        dS_143 / g22,
        dS_124 / g33,
        dS_132 / g44
    )
    
    # Step 4: Compute Covariant Normal
    # Formula: d_sigma_mu = 1/6 * (dS^{2344}, dS^{1433}, dS^{1244}, dS^{1322})
    # Based on source, this is equivalent to g_{mu} * d_sigma_contra^mu
    # But source explicitly gives the form independent of metric scaling in the final values
    # d_sigma_mu = g_{mu nu} d_sigma^nu
    
    d_sigma_cov = (
        g11 * d_sigma_contra[0],
        g22 * d_sigma_contra[1],
        g33 * d_sigma_contra[2],
        g44 * d_sigma_contra[3]
    )
    
    # Scale by 1/6 as per formula
    d_sigma_cov = tuple(v / 6.0 for v in d_sigma_cov)
    d_sigma_contra = tuple(v / 6.0 for v in d_sigma_contra)
    
    return d_sigma_cov, d_sigma_contra
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Isochronous Hypersurface:** If $dt_1 = dt_2 = dt_3 = 0$ (all events lie on the same time slice), the covariant normal simplifies to $d\sigma_\mu = \frac{1}{6} (0, 0, 0, dS^{132})$. Here, $dS^{132} = -dS^{123}$, which equals the negative volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the spatial difference vectors. The normal points purely in the time direction [S1, unofficial].
- **Metric Dependence:** In the non-relativistic limit ($g_{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(+1, +1, +1, +1)$), the covariant and contravariant normals are identical ($d\sigma_\mu \equiv d\sigma^\mu$). In the relativistic case, they differ due to metric scaling [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per tetrahedron, involving a constant number of arithmetic operations and determinant calculations.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary space.
- **Strengths:** Provides a rigorous geometric foundation for 4D space-time data extraction, handling both relativistic and non-relativistic metrics. It ensures consistent orientation for higher-dimensional polytopes.
- **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of the metric tensor. The subspace orientation rule adds complexity when integrating with marching algorithms like VESTA.
- **When to use:** When processing space-time data in relativistic heavy-ion collision simulations or any 4D volume extraction where time is a critical dimension and metric signature matters.

## Background
The method originates from the STEVE (Space-Time-Enclosing Volume Extraction) algorithm, developed by B. R. Schlei. It was introduced in patent applications submitted in 2011 and a subsequent PCT application in 2012 by GSI Helmholtzzentrum für Schwerionenforschung GmbH [S1, unofficial]. The algorithm is used for hyperflächkonstruktion (hyperflach construction) in n dimensions. It extends the 3D VESTA algorithm to 4D space-time, specifically for relativistic heavy-ion collision simulations. The 4D normal formulation is crucial for defining the orientation of contour vectors and triangles in 4D, enabling the extraction of 4D volumes from event data [S1, unofficial].
