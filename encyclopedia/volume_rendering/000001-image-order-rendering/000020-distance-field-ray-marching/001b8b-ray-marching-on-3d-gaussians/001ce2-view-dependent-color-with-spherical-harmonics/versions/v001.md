# View-dependent color with Spherical Harmonics

Also called: 3D Gaussian Splatting with SH, Rendering with Spherical Harmonics, View-dependent color rendering

## Overview
This method represents the view-dependent color of each 3D Gaussian primitive as a function of the viewing direction using spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients [S1, unofficial][S2]. It solves the problem of storing directional color variation (such as specular highlights and reflections) compactly without requiring per-ray neural network queries [S1, unofficial]. Unlike simple point colors or view-independent representations, this model captures glossy effects by evaluating a fixed SH basis at the viewing direction [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A set of $N$ 3D Gaussians. For each Gaussian $i$, the method requires:
    *   The viewing direction vector $\mathbf{d}_i$, normalized, pointing from the camera center to the Gaussian's mean position [S1, unofficial].
    *   The SH coefficients stored for that Gaussian [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The RGB color $C(\mathbf{d}_i)$ for each Gaussian in the specified viewing direction [S1, S4, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
Each Gaussian stores 48 SH coefficients, organized as 16 coefficients per color channel (Red, Green, Blue) [S1, unofficial]. The coefficients are stored contiguously in memory [S3, unofficial]. The layout is:
*   Indices 0–2: Degree-0 (DC) coefficients for R, G, B respectively [S3, unofficial].
*   Indices 3–47: Remaining coefficients for degrees 1, 2, and 3 [S3, unofficial].

The coefficients are scalars $k_{\ell, c}^m$ corresponding to basis functions $Y_{\ell}^m$, where $\ell$ is the degree, $m$ is the order, and $c \in \{R, G, B\}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Spherical Harmonic Constants**
The following normalization constants are used [S3, unofficial]:
*   $SH\_C0 = 0.28209479177387814$
*   $SH\_C1 = 0.4886025119029199$
*   Degree 2 constants ($SH\_C2_0$ to $SH\_C2_4$): Values not given in the sources.

**Numbered Steps**
1.  **Compute Viewing Direction:** For each Gaussian, compute the normalized direction vector $\mathbf{d} = (x, y, z)$ from the camera center to the Gaussian's mean position [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Initialize Color:** Initialize the RGB color vector $\mathbf{C}$ with the DC component scaled by $SH\_C0$ [S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C} = SH\_C0 \cdot [k_{0,R}^0, k_{0,G}^0, k_{0,B}^0] $$
3.  **Evaluate Degree 1 (if active):** If the SH degree is at least 1, add the contribution from the first-order coefficients (indices 3–12) [S3, unofficial]. The basis functions for degree 1 are linear in $x, y, z$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C} \leftarrow \mathbf{C} - SH\_C1 \cdot (y \cdot [k_{1,R}^{-1}, k_{1,G}^{-1}, k_{1,B}^{-1}] - z \cdot [k_{1,R}^{1}, k_{1,G}^{1}, k_{1,B}^{1}] + x \cdot [k_{1,R}^{0}, k_{1,G}^{0}, k_{1,B}^{0}]) $$
    *Note: The specific mapping of indices $m=-1, 0, 1$ to coefficients 3, 6, 9 and the sign convention follows the implementation in [S3, unofficial].*
4.  **Evaluate Degree 2 (if active):** If the SH degree is at least 2, add the contribution from the second-order coefficients (indices 13–27) [S3, unofficial]. The basis functions involve quadratic terms: $xy, yz, 2z^2-x^2-y^2, xz, x^2-y^2$ [S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C} \leftarrow \mathbf{C} + \sum_{j=0}^{4} SH\_C2_j \cdot B_{2,j}(\mathbf{d}) \cdot \mathbf{k}_{2,j} $$
    Where $B_{2,j}$ are the degree 2 basis polynomials and $\mathbf{k}_{2,j}$ are the corresponding RGB coefficient vectors [S3, unofficial].
5.  **Evaluate Degree 3 (if active):** If the SH degree is at least 3, add the contribution from the third-order coefficients (indices 28–47) [S3, unofficial]. The basis functions involve cubic terms [S3, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C} \leftarrow \mathbf{C} + \sum_{j=0}^{7} SH\_C3_j \cdot B_{3,j}(\mathbf{d}) \cdot \mathbf{k}_{3,j} $$
    *Note: Specific basis polynomials and constants for degree 3 are not fully detailed in the sources [S3, unofficial].*
6.  **Apply Activation:** Apply a ReLU activation and add a bias of 0.5 to the resulting RGB color [S4, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{final} = \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{C}) + 0.5 $$

**Training Schedule**
Optimization begins with only the degree-0 band (base color) active [S1, unofficial]. One further band is unlocked every 1,000 iterations until all four bands (degrees 0–3) are active [S1, unofficial]. This coarse-to-fine schedule allows the base color to settle before higher-frequency view-dependent details are fitted [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Frozen Basis:** The SH basis functions $Y_{\ell}^m(\mathbf{d})$ are fixed polynomials and are never updated during training [S1, unofficial]. Only the coefficients $k$ are learned [S1, unofficial].
*   **Missing Constants:** The specific normalization constants for degree 2 and degree 3 SH are not provided in the sources [S3, unofficial]. Implementers must refer to standard SH definitions or the original 3DGS paper for these values [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Coefficient Layout:** The exact mapping of SH orders ($m$) to coefficient indices must be consistent with the evaluation code. The sources suggest a specific layout used in the reference implementation [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Degree 0 (DC) Color
*   **What it is:** The view-independent base color of the Gaussian, represented by the first 3 coefficients (R, G, B) [S2].
*   **Difference:** Unlike higher degrees, it does not vary with viewing direction [S2].
*   **When to use:** Use when view-dependent effects are not needed or to initialize training [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Link:** [View-dependent color with Spherical Harmonics](1037-view-dependent-color-with-spherical-harmonics.md)

### Degree 1-3 (View-Dependent) Color
*   **What it is:** Higher-order SH coefficients that capture directional variation such as specular highlights and reflections [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Difference:** Captures glossy effects that DC color cannot [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When to use:** Use when photorealistic rendering of shiny surfaces is required [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Link:** [View-dependent color with Spherical Harmonics](1037-view-dependent-color-with-spherical-harmonics.md)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Evaluating SH is a fixed number of polynomial operations per Gaussian, making it very fast at render time [S1, unofficial]. The cost is negligible compared to rasterization [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Each Gaussian stores 48 coefficients (16 per channel) [S1, unofficial]. This is a fixed overhead per Gaussian.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method contributes to the overall 3DGS performance of 134 fps on the Mip-NeRF360 benchmark [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Captures complex view-dependent effects like reflections and highlights [S1, unofficial][S2]. Closed-form evaluation, no neural network query needed [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires storing more data per Gaussian than view-independent color [S1, unofficial]. Higher degrees may introduce artifacts if not trained properly [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When photorealism with specular details is required [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When not:** When memory is extremely constrained or view-independent color is sufficient [S2].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was introduced in the 3D Gaussian Splatting paper by Kerbl et al. (2023) [S1, unofficial].
*   **History:** It repurposes spherical harmonics from irradiance environment maps to represent the color leaving a Gaussian [S1, unofficial].
*   **Usage:** It is a core component of the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline, enabling photorealistic novel view synthesis [S1, unofficial].
