# Motion-Compensated Block Warping

Also called: Inter-frame motion compensation

## Overview
Motion-compensated block warping improves prediction accuracy for moving scenes by inverse-warping voxel-center samples of a target block into the previously reconstructed Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) and resampling at the warped locations using trilinear interpolation [S1]. It sets itself apart from static transformation methods by estimating per-block rigid motion to align temporal frames, thereby reducing residual energy before transform-domain coding [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Target block $b$ at frame $t$, previous reconstructed TSDF block $s_{t-1}^{(b)}$, and the 6-DoF rigid transform $(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})$ for block $b$.
*   **Output:** A motion-compensated predictor block and the encoded residual coefficients.

**Data Structures:**
*   **TSDF Block:** A 3D grid of voxel values.
*   **Motion Side Information:** A 6-DoF rigid transform consisting of translation $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and Euler-angle rotation $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
*   **Sampling Neighborhood:** A stitched $3 \times 3 \times 3$ neighborhood of blocks from $s_{t-1}^{(b)}$ to support interpolation across block boundaries.

**Steps:**
1.  **Inverse-Warp Positions:** For each voxel center position $\mathbf{p}_i$ in the target block $b$ at frame $t$, apply the inverse of the rigid transform $\mathbf{T}(\cdot; \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})$ to map $\mathbf{p}_i$ to a location in the coordinate space of frame $t-1$.
2.  **Assemble Neighborhood:** Construct a $3 \times 3 \times 3$ stitched neighborhood of blocks from the previous frame's TSDF $s_{t-1}^{(b)}$ centered on the corresponding block. This ensures valid sampling support even if warped positions fall into adjacent blocks [S1].
3.  **Trilinear Interpolation:** Evaluate the TSDF value at the warped fractional positions using trilinear interpolation over the assembled neighborhood. Since rigid motion maps positions to fractional locations between voxel centers, interpolation is required [S1].
4.  **Form Predictor:** The resulting interpolated values form the motion-compensated predictor for the current block.
5.  **Encode Residual:** Compute the residual between the current block's TSDF values and the predictor, then encode the transform-domain residual coefficients as in standard TSDF compression [S1].
6.  **Encode Motion:** Encode the per-block rigid transform $(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})$ as motion side information. The decoder applies the same inverse warp to reconstruct the identical predictor before decoding residuals [S1].

**Formulas:**
The rigid transform $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{p}; \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})$ maps a point $\mathbf{p}$ using rotation $\mathbf{r}$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$. The predictor value $\hat{s}$ at a target voxel center $\mathbf{p}_i$ is:
$$ \hat{s}_{t-1}^{(b)}(\mathbf{T}^{-1}(\mathbf{p}_i; \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})) $$
where $\hat{s}_{t-1}^{(b)}$ denotes the trilinearly interpolated TSDF from the previous frame [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Neighborhood Size:** $3 \times 3 \times 3$ blocks [S1].
*   **Motion Representation:** 6-DoF rigid transform (3 for translation, 3 for Euler-angle rotation) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Warped positions may fall outside the target block, requiring the stitched neighborhood to prevent out-of-bounds errors [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If too few reliable samples are available or the $3 \times 3 \times 3$ neighborhood is incomplete, fall back to zero-motion prediction ($\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$) for that block [S1].

## Subtopics

### Per-Block Motion Estimation
Estimates the per-block 6-DoF rigid transform $(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})$ by aligning TSDF samples from frame $t$ with the previous frame $t-1$ using a damped Gauss-Newton procedure [S1]. It is chosen when motion is significant enough to warrant optimization overhead. The objective minimizes squared residuals:
$$ \epsilon_i(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}) = \hat{s}_{t-1}^{(b)}(\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{p}_i; \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})) - s_t^{(b)}(\mathbf{p}_i) $$
$$ \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{r} := \arg \min_{\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{r}} \sum_{i \in \Omega} \epsilon_i(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t})^2 $$
The update step is solved via:
$$ \Delta \mathbf{p} = -(J^\top J + \lambda I)^{-1} J^\top \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} $$
where $J$ is the Jacobian estimated by finite difference and $\lambda$ is a damping factor for stability [S1]. Translation updates are clamped to 1 voxel and rotation updates to 5 degrees per step to prevent divergence. Early termination occurs when the objective decrease falls below a threshold. If the neighborhood is incomplete or samples are unreliable, it falls back to zero motion [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the motion estimation method. Per-block estimation requires iterative Gauss-Newton optimization, which is more expensive than static transforms but reduces residual size.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the $3 \times 3 \times 3$ neighborhood of the previous frame's TSDF blocks for interpolation.
*   **Performance:** Implemented with CUDA acceleration on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly improves prediction for moving objects by compensating for rigid body motion, reducing the energy of residuals to be encoded.
*   **Limitations:** Requires motion estimation overhead and careful handling of block boundaries. Not suitable for non-rigid deformations without extension.
*   **When to use:** When the scene contains rigidly moving objects and temporal redundancy is high.
*   **When not to use:** For static scenes or scenes with complex non-rigid deformations where rigid transforms are insufficient.

## Background
Motion-compensated block warping was introduced in the context of implicit surface compression using discrete cosine transforms and motion compensation [S1]. It builds on sparse TSDF representations and voxel hashing for efficient storage [S1]. The method leverages the continuity of rigid motion to predict future frames from previous reconstructions, a technique adapted from video compression principles to 3D volumetric data.
