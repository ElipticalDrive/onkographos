# MonoSDF

Also called: MonoSDF: Exploring Monocular Geometric Cues for Neural Implicit Surface Reconstruction

## Overview
MonoSDF is a method for neural implicit surface reconstruction that addresses the challenge of reconstructing weak texture areas by incorporating monocular depth and normal constraints [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Monocular Depth Consistency Loss](4668-monocular-depth-consistency-loss.md) to enforce geometric consistency, distinguishing itself from sibling methods by explicitly leveraging monocular geometric cues to stabilize reconstruction in texture-poor regions [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
MonoSDF reconstructs a 3D scene as a neural implicit surface. The core innovation is the integration of monocular geometric priors to guide the optimization of the signed distance function (SDF) and color fields, specifically targeting the failure modes of standard NeRF-based methods in weak texture areas [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
- Monocular images.
- Estimated monocular depth maps.
- Estimated monocular normal maps.

**Outputs:**
- Neural implicit surface representation (SDF).
- Reconstructed RGB images.

**Data Structures:**
- Signed Distance Function (SDF) network: Maps 3D coordinates to distance values.
- Radial field network: Maps 3D coordinates to color/opacity.
- Monocular depth and normal maps: Pre-computed per input image.

**Steps:**
1. **Scene Representation:** Model the scene using an SDF and a radial field, similar to [NeuS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01685) [S1, unofficial].
2. **Volume Rendering:** Render RGB images and depth maps from the SDF and radial fields using volume rendering [S1, unofficial].
3. **Geometric Constraint Application:** Apply constraints using the estimated monocular depth and normal maps [S1, unofficial]. This step is crucial for addressing weak texture area reconstruction [S1, unofficial].
4. **Optimization:** Optimize the network parameters to minimize the difference between rendered and ground truth images, while enforcing consistency with monocular depth and normal cues [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Weight for monocular depth loss: Not given in the sources.
- Weight for monocular normal loss: Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Weak texture areas: Standard methods fail here; MonoSDF uses monocular cues to mitigate this [S1, unofficial].
- Scale ambiguity: Monocular depth is inherently scale-ambiguous; the method relies on the consistency loss to handle this [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### MonoNeRD
MonoNeRD is a monocular 3D target detection framework that achieves dense 3D geometric and occupancy representation by modeling the scene as a signed distance function (SDF) [S2]. It employs volume rendering to recover RGB images and depth maps from the SDF and radial fields [S2]. It is notable as the first time volume rendering has been introduced into the M3D (Monocular 3D Detection) domain, demonstrating the potential for implicit reconstruction of image-based 3D perception [S2]. It demonstrates superiority in handling long-range and occluded objects [S2].

## Operating characteristics
MonoSDF improves reconstruction quality in weak texture areas compared to standard NeRF-based methods [S1, unofficial]. It enables implicit reconstruction of 3D perception from monocular inputs [S2]. Specific time and memory complexity metrics are not given in the sources.

## Background
MonoSDF was introduced in the paper "MonoSDF: Exploring Monocular Geometric Cues for Neural Implicit Surface Reconstruction" at NeurIPS 2022 [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address the challenges faced by NeuS, particularly in weak texture area reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. The method builds on the foundation of NeRF and its extensions, such as NeuS, which use volume rendering for multi-view reconstruction [S1, unofficial]. MonoNeRD, a related method, was introduced in 2023 and represents the first application of volume rendering to monocular 3D detection [S2].
