# Spherical Beta Function

Also called: SB color representation

## Overview
The Spherical Beta (SB) function is a directional appearance representation used in 3D Gaussian splatting to model view-dependent effects [S1, unofficial]. It replaces spherical harmonics (SH) to capture higher-frequency directional variations [S1, unofficial]. It provides a compact, bounded alternative that ensures radiance continuity while enabling sharper directional modeling [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [View-dependent color with Spherical Harmonics](1369-view-dependent-color-with-spherical-harmonics.md) by trading the global basis of SH for localized, asymmetric lobes.

## How it works
The SB function represents the directional intensity or color component of a Gaussian surfel as a sum of $K$ directional lobes [G].

**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** Viewing direction $\omega \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (unit vector), number of lobes $K$, set of principal directions $\{s_k\}_{k=1}^K$, and shape parameters $\{\alpha_k, \beta_k\}_{k=1}^K$ [G].
- **Output:** Scalar value $f_{SB}(\omega)$ representing the directional intensity or color component [G].

**Data Structures:**
For each Gaussian primitive, the view-dependent color is stored as:
- $K$: Integer, number of lobes [G].
- $s_k$: 3D vector, principal direction of the $k$-th lobe, normalized to unit length [G].
- $\alpha_k$: Scalar, shape parameter $> 0$ controlling the "rise" side of the lobe [G].
- $\beta_k$: Scalar, shape parameter $> 0$ controlling the "fall" side of the lobe [G].

**Formula:**
The standard formulation computes the sum of lobe contributions as follows [G]:
$$
f_{SB} (\omega; \alpha, \beta, s) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} (1+s_k \cdot \omega)^{\alpha_k -1} (1-s_k \cdot \omega)^{\beta_k -1}
$$
Where:
- $s_k \cdot \omega$ is the dot product between the principal direction and the viewing direction [G].
- $\alpha_k, \beta_k > 0$ [G].
- The term $(1+s_k \cdot \omega)$ is non-negative because $s_k \cdot \omega \in [-1, 1]$ for unit vectors [G].

**Steps:**
1. Initialize an accumulator to zero [G].
2. For each lobe $k$ from $1$ to $K$ [G]:
3. Compute the dot product $d = s_k \cdot \omega$ [G].
4. Compute the terms $(1+d)^{\alpha_k-1}$ and $(1-d)^{\beta_k-1}$ [G].
5. Multiply the terms and add the result to the accumulator [G].
6. Return the accumulator [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def spherical_beta_function(omega, lobes):
    total = 0.0
    for lobe in lobes:
        s = lobe['s']
        alpha = lobe['alpha']
        beta = lobe['beta']
        dot = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(s, omega))
        term1 = (1.0 + dot) ** (alpha - 1.0)
        term2 = (1.0 - dot) ** (beta - 1.0)
        total += term1 * term2
    return total
```

**Parameters:**
- $K$: Number of lobes. Not given in the sources [G].
- $\alpha_k, \beta_k$: Optimized during training [G]. Typical values are not given in the sources, but they must be $> 0$ [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Boundary Conditions:** The function is well-defined for all $\omega$ on the unit sphere since $1 \pm s_k \cdot \omega \ge 0$ [G].
- **Ill-conditioned Gradients:** Extreme values of $\alpha_k$ or $\beta_k$ generate very sharp and flat regions, causing ill-conditioned gradients [G].

## Subtopics

### [Spherical Betas (SB)](17741-spherical-betas-sb.md)
A directional appearance representation that extends Spherical Gaussians by enabling asymmetric and bounded-support lobes for skewed directional variation. Chosen when SH ringing or SG unbounded support is problematic, as SB provides a compact, bounded alternative that ensures radiance continuity.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(K)$ per evaluation, where $K$ is the number of lobes [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** Stores $K$ directions (3 floats each) and $2K$ shape parameters (2 floats each), totaling $5K$ floats per Gaussian [G].
- **Performance:** In DP-GES, using SB slightly increases FLIP error compared to GES because the function enables higher-frequency view-dependent effects that FLIP interprets as warping error, but it improves view-dependent appearance [S1, unofficial]. Reverting to SH reduces FPS from 472 to 480 and PSNR from 28.11 to 27.72 on MipNeRF360, indicating SB is slightly more expensive but yields higher quality [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Captures higher-frequency view-dependent effects than SH [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Increases the risk of overfitting, as observed in Deformable Beta Splatting (DBS) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The Spherical Beta function was introduced in the context of Gaussian-Enhanced Surfel Rendering to model higher-frequency view-dependent effects [S1, unofficial]. It is used in DP-GES (Depth Peeling for High-Fidelity Gaussian-Enhanced Surfel Rendering) [S1, unofficial].
