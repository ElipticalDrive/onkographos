# Spherical Betas (SB)

Also called: SB color representation

## Overview
Spherical Betas (SB) is a directional appearance representation that extends Spherical Gaussians (SG) by enabling asymmetric and bounded-support lobes to model skewed and sharper directional variation [S1, unofficial]. It is primarily used in neural and implicit representations, such as DP-GES and Deformable Beta Splatting, to replace spherical harmonics (SH) or SG for view-dependent color [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike SH, which suffers from ringing artifacts and high dimensionality, and SG, which has unbounded support requiring truncation, SB provides a compact, bounded alternative that ensures radiance continuity [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** View direction $\hat{V}$, surface normal $\hat{N}$ (for reflection calculation), and primitive parameters (base color, lobe directions, shape parameters).
*   **Output:** View-dependent color $c(\hat{V})$ or directional intensity $f_{SB}(\omega)$.

**Data Structures:**
A 3D Beta primitive stores features that scale linearly with the number of reflection lobes $M$. The feature dimension is $3 + 6M$ [S2]:
*   $c_0$: Diffuse/base color (3 components, e.g., RGB).
*   For each lobe $m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$:
    *   $\hat{R}_m$: Reflection direction (3 components, normalized).
    *   $c_m$: Lobe color (3 components, RGB).
    *   $b_m$: Shininess parameter (1 component, scalar).
    *   *Note:* The source notes a dimension of $3 + 6M$, implying 6 parameters per lobe. The text explicitly lists $\hat{R}_m$ (3), $c_m$ (3), and $b_m$ (1). The 7th parameter per lobe is not explicitly defined in the provided text, but the dimension count is fixed at $6M$ for lobes [S2].

**Formulas:**
The general Spherical Beta function for a direction $\omega$ with $K$ lobes is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ f_{SB} (\omega; \alpha, \beta, s) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} (1+s_k \cdot \omega)^{\alpha_k -1} (1-s_k \cdot \omega)^{\beta_k -1} $$
where $s_k$ is the principal direction, and $\alpha_k, \beta_k > 0$ control the shape [S1, unofficial].

In the context of Deformable Beta Splatting, the appearance is modeled as a sum of a base diffuse color and specular lobes [S2]:
$$ c(\hat{V}) = c_0 + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \mathcal{B}(1 - \hat{R}_m \cdot \hat{V}; b_m) c_m $$
where:
*   $c_0$ is the diffuse/base color.
*   $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of SB lobes.
*   $\hat{R}_m$ is the reflection direction derived from the surface normal and light source direction.
*   $\hat{V}$ is the view direction.
*   $c_m$ is the color of the $m$-th lobe.
*   $b_m$ is the shininess coefficient.
*   $\mathcal{B}$ is the Beta Kernel function. The specific mathematical form of $\mathcal{B}(x; b)$ is not explicitly provided in the source text, but it operates on the input range $[0, 1]$ corresponding to $1 - \hat{R}_m \cdot \hat{V}$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $M$: Number of reflection lobes per primitive. Default value is $M=2$ ($sb=2$) [S2].
*   $b_m$: Shininess parameter. Initialized to zero to make the Beta Kernel initially resemble the Gaussian Kernel [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Ill-conditioned Gradients:** Extreme values of $\alpha_k$ or $\beta_k$ (or $b_m$) generate very sharp and flat regions, causing ill-conditioned gradients and stronger sensitivity to initialization compared to Spherical Gaussians [S1, unofficial].
*   **Truncation Artifacts:** Unlike Spherical Gaussians, which have unbounded support and require truncation (causing radiance discontinuities when view and reflection directions are orthogonal), Spherical Beta is inherently bounded. Its input range is confined to $[0, 1]$, ensuring radiance naturally drops to zero when $\hat{R}_m \cdot \hat{V} = 0$ [S2].

## Subtopics

### Spherical Beta
A directional appearance representation extending Spherical Gaussians (SG) by enabling asymmetric and bounded-support lobes defined as $f_{SB} (\omega; \alpha, \beta, s) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} (1+s_k \cdot \omega)^{\alpha_k -1} (1-s_k \cdot \omega)^{\beta_k -1}$ [S1, unofficial]. It is chosen when sharper and skewed directional variation is needed compared to SG. It is harder to optimize due to sensitivity to initialization and ill-conditioned gradients at extreme shape parameters [S1, unofficial]. See [Spherical Voronoi](12574-spherical-beta-function.md) for a comparison with other directional representations.

### Spherical Beta
A specular lobe modeling technique using learnable bounded Beta kernels to model outgoing reflected radiance, merging ambient and diffuse into a single base color $c_0$ [S2]. It is defined as $c(\hat{V}) = c_0 + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \mathcal{B}(1 - \hat{R}_m \cdot \hat{V}; b_m) c_m$ [S2]. It is chosen for its efficiency (feature dimension $3+6M$) and continuity (no truncation artifacts) compared to Spherical Gaussians [S2]. Default configuration uses $M=2$ lobes per primitive [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Evaluation is $O(M)$ per primitive, where $M$ is the number of lobes. This is more efficient than Spherical Harmonics (SH), which scales with $L^2$ or $L^3$ [S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Feature dimension per primitive is $3 + 6M$ [S2]. For default $M=2$, this is 15 floats per primitive.
*   **Performance:** Fully explicit and differentiable, implemented in CUDA for optimized performance [S2]. Convergence speed varies by scene, with default training at 30,000 iterations [S2].
*   **Strengths:** Bounded support eliminates truncation artifacts and radiance discontinuities at orthogonal view/reflection angles [S2]. More flexible than SG for modeling skewed/sharp lobes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Harder to optimize than SG due to ill-conditioned gradients and sensitivity to initialization [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Spherical Betas were introduced as an extension of Spherical Gaussians to provide asymmetric and bounded-support lobes [S1, unofficial]. They are used in the DP-GES method [S1, unofficial] and Deformable Beta Splatting [S2]. The Deformable Beta Splatting implementation builds upon the 3DGS-MCMC framework and GSplat library [S2].
