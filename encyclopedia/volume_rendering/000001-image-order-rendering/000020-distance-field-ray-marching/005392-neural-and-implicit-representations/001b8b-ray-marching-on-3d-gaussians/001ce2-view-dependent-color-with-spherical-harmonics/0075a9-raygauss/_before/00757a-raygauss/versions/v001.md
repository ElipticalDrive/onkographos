# RayGauss

Also called: Volumetric Gaussian-Based Ray Casting

## Overview
RayGauss is a differentiable volume ray casting algorithm that approximates radiance and density fields using irregularly distributed anisotropic Gaussian primitives to generate photorealistic novel views [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing the rasterization-based splatting pipeline with volume ray casting, thereby eliminating inherent rendering artifacts such as flickering [S1, unofficial]. Unlike grid-based methods (e.g., Instant NGP, TetraNeRF), it adapts efficiently to scene geometry using sparse, irregularly distributed points rather than fixed-resolution structures [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of input images with known camera poses.
**Outputs:** Rendered images for novel viewpoints.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Primitives:** Each primitive $i$ is defined by:
    *   Position $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
    *   Anisotropic Gaussian covariance (shape/orientation).
    *   Scalar opacity/density weight $\alpha_i$.
    *   Radiance parameters: Coefficients for Spherical Harmonics (SH) and/or Spherical Gaussians (SG) to model view-dependent color.
2.  **Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH):** An acceleration structure built from Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs) of the primitives.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Bounding Primitives:**
    Anisotropic Gaussians have unbounded support. To enable BVH construction and reduce evaluation cost, restrict each primitive to the region where its density exceeds a threshold $\tau$.
    $$ \rho_i(\mathbf{x}) = \alpha_i \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)^T \Sigma_i^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_i)\right) > \tau $$
    Compute the AABB for each primitive based on this thresholded region [S1, unofficial].

2.  **BVH Construction:**
    Calculate the AABBs for all primitives. Use a dedicated API (e.g., NVIDIA OptiX) to build the BVH [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Ray Traversal and Integration:**
    For each pixel, cast a ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$. Traverse the BVH to find intersections with primitives.
    Evaluate the discretized radiative transfer equation over successive segments. For a ray passing through $N$ primitives, the transmittance $T(t)$ and accumulated color $C$ are computed by integrating the density $\sigma$ and emitted radiance $L$ along the ray [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) ds\right) $$
    $$ C = \int_0^\infty T(t) \sigma(\mathbf{r}(t)) L(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) dt $$
    In practice, this is approximated by sampling multiple points along the ray segment within each primitive's bounding volume, computing the intersection of these samples with the Gaussian density function, and accumulating the contribution [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Optimization:**
    Optimize primitive parameters (position, covariance, opacity, SH/SG coefficients) using gradient descent. The loss is computed between rendered images and ground-truth images from known viewpoints, similar to NeRF training [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Threshold $\tau$:** Determines the cutoff for bounding boxes. Not given in the sources.
*   **Basis Function:** Anisotropic Gaussian.
*   **Radiance Parameterization:** Combination of Spherical Harmonics and Spherical Gaussians.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Unbounded Support:** Without thresholding, AABBs cannot be computed, and BVH construction fails [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The Gaussian function decays rapidly; sampling density must be sufficient to capture the core of the primitive without aliasing, but sparse enough to maintain performance [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on BVH traversal depth and number of primitives intersected. Generally faster than neural field methods (e.g., Point NeRF) due to lack of MLP evaluation per sample, but potentially slower than rasterized splatting for simple scenes due to ray-tracing overhead [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Linear in the number of primitives. Each primitive stores position, covariance, opacity, and SH/SG coefficients.
*   **Strengths:** Minimal rendering artifacts (no flickering); adaptive resolution via sparse primitives; physically consistent density/radiance modeling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires efficient BVH implementation; potentially higher computational cost per pixel than rasterization for high-resolution views; training time may be longer than splatting due to ray casting integration [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When artifact-free rendering is critical and scene geometry is irregular or sparse; when grid-based methods are too memory-intensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When maximum real-time frame rates are required and artifacts are tolerable (use 3D Gaussian Splatting); when scene is extremely dense and uniform (grid methods may be more efficient) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
RayGauss was presented by researchers at PSL University in Paris, specifically within the cow laboratory at Mines's Paris School [S1, unofficial]. The work is inspired by point cloud-based representations like Point NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting, aiming to combine the quality of point-based methods with the artifact-free nature of volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. It addresses limitations of grid-based methods (resolution limits) and splatting methods (flickering artifacts) [S1, unofficial].
