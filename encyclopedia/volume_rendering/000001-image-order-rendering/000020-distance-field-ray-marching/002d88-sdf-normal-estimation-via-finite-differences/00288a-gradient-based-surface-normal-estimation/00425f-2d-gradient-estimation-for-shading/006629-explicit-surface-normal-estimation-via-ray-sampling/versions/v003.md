# Explicit Surface Normal Estimation via Ray Sampling

Also called: Explicit Normal Estimation, Explicit Surface Normal Estimation, NeRF Normal Estimation via Adjacent Rays, Ray-based Normal Estimation

## Overview
This method estimates surface normals by evaluating the depth of a primary ray and two adjacent rays to construct a local surface plane [S1]. It computes normals by evaluating depth at discrete adjacent rays and forming a plane from the resulting 3D points, rather than differentiating a rendered depth map [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A primary training ray $r$ with origin $o$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1].
- A learned neural radiance field (or similar implicit surface model) capable of returning depth $d(\cdot)$ for any given ray [S1].
- Access to neighboring rays in the image grid [S1].

**Outputs:**
- A unit surface normal vector $\mathbf{n}(r, r_{n1}, r_{n2})$ at the surface point of ray $r$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- Ray representation: Tuple or struct containing origin $o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and direction $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (normalized) [G].
- Depth value: Scalar $d \in \mathbb{R}^+$ [G].
- 3D Point: Vector $p_s \in \mathbb{R}^3$ [G].

**Steps:**
1. **Select Adjacent Rays:** For the current ray $r$, identify two adjacent rays $r_{n1}$ and $r_{n2}$ from the four immediate neighboring pixels in the image grid. To prevent unstable normal calculation, ensure that one neighbor is adjacent along the X-axis and the other along the Y-axis [S1].
2. **Evaluate Depths:** Query the model for the estimated depth values $d(r)$, $d(r_{n1})$, and $d(r_{n2})$ [S1].
3. **Compute 3D Surface Points:** Convert the depth values into 3D coordinates using the ray origins and directions:
   $$ p_s(r) = o + \mathbf{d} \cdot d(r) $$
   $$ p_s(r_{n1}) = o_{n1} + \mathbf{d}_{n1} \cdot d(r_{n1}) $$
   $$ p_s(r_{n2}) = o_{n2} + \mathbf{d}_{n2} \cdot d(r_{n2}) $$
   [S1]
4. **Compute Unnormalized Normal:** Calculate the cross product of the vectors formed by the three surface points:
   $$ \hat{\mathbf{n}}(r, r_{n1}, r_{n2}) = (p_s(r_{n1}) - p_s(r)) \times (p_s(r_{n2}) - p_s(r)) $$
   [S1]
5. **Normalize:** Normalize the resulting vector to obtain the unit normal:
   $$ \mathbf{n}(r, r_{n1}, r_{n2}) = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{n}}(r, r_{n1}, r_{n2})}{\|\hat{\mathbf{n}}(r, r_{n1}, r_{n2})\|} $$
   [S1]

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def estimate_normal(ray_r, ray_n1, ray_n2, depth_model):
    """
    Estimates surface normal using explicit ray sampling.
    
    Args:
        ray_r: Ray object with origin o and direction d for the primary ray.
        ray_n1, ray_n2: Ray objects for two adjacent neighbors (one X-axis, one Y-axis).
        depth_model: Function that takes a ray and returns depth d.
        
    Returns:
        n_hat: Normalized surface normal vector.
    """
    # Step 2: Evaluate depths
    d_r = depth_model(ray_r)
    d_n1 = depth_model(ray_n1)
    d_n2 = depth_model(ray_n2)
    
    # Step 3: Convert depth to 3D surface points
    # p_s(r) = o + d * depth(r)
    p_r = ray_r.o + ray_r.d * d_r
    p_n1 = ray_n1.o + ray_n1.d * d_n1
    p_n2 = ray_n2.o + ray_n2.d * d_n2
    
    # Step 4: Compute unnormalized normal via cross product
    # n_hat = (p_n1 - p_r) x (p_n2 - p_r)
    vec1 = p_n1 - p_r
    vec2 = p_n2 - p_r
    n_hat_unnorm = np.cross(vec1, vec2)
    
    # Step 5: Normalize
    norm = np.linalg.norm(n_hat_unnorm)
    if norm < 1e-8:
        # [G] Return a default normal or handle error; source does not specify fallback.
        return np.array([0.0, 0.0, 1.0]) 
        
    n_hat = n_hat_unnorm / norm
    
    return n_hat
```

**Parameters:**
- Neighbor selection: Random selection from the 4 immediate neighbors, constrained to one X-adjacent and one Y-adjacent pixel [S1].
- Bundle Adjustment: A preprocessing step may be applied to RPC models to improve camera accuracy before normal estimation [S1].
- Learned Camera Adjustment: A per-image shift parameter $q_j$ on the XY-axis may be used to adjust camera accuracy during training [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Unstable Calculation:** Selecting two neighbors along the same axis (e.g., both X-adjacent) can lead to unstable normal calculations. The method explicitly requires one X and one Y neighbor [S1].
- **Degenerate Geometry:** If the three points are collinear, the cross product is zero. The pseudocode includes a guard for this [G].
- **Precision:** The accuracy depends on the precision of the depth estimation and the camera parameters. Bundle adjustment and learned camera adjustments are used to mitigate camera inaccuracies [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Gravity-Aligned Planarity Regularization](19606-gravity-aligned-planarity-regularization.md)
Aligns explicitly estimated normals with the gravity axis to enforce local planarity on flat surfaces. It is chosen over sibling methods when rendering satellite imagery or scenes with strong planar structures to reduce geometric noise.

The method was introduced in the context of "SatGeo-NeRF," a NeRF model for satellite imagery that incorporates geometric regularization [S1]. It builds on the foundational NeRF rendering equations and adapts them to handle the specific challenges of satellite data, such as camera inaccuracies and the need for planar surface consistency [S1].
