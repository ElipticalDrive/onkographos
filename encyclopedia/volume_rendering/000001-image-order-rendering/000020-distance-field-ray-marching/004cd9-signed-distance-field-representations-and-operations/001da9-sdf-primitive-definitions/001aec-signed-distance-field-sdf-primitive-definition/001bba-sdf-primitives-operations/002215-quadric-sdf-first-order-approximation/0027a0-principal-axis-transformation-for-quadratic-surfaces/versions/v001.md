# Principal Axis Transformation for Quadratic Surfaces

Also called: Eigenvalue Analysis of Quadratic Forms, Principal Component Analysis of Quadrics

## Overview
This method diagonalizes the quadratic form of a general quadratic surface to identify its principal axes and curvatures, enabling the classification of the surface type (elliptic, hyperbolic, or parabolic) and the generation of optimal triangulations for rendering or approximation [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from generic SDF primitives by providing the geometric foundation for error-bounded mesh generation on quadratic graphs, specifically addressing the distinct topological and curvature properties of hyperbolic versus elliptic surfaces.

## How it works
**Inputs:** Coefficients of the quadratic function $f(x, y) = c_{20}x^2 + 2c_{11}xy + c_{02}y^2 + c_{10}x + c_{01}y + c_{00}$.
**Outputs:** Eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, eigenvectors $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$, surface classification, and optionally the transformation matrix $H$ for hyperbolic cases.

**Data Structures:**
- Matrix $C$: A $2 \times 2$ symmetric matrix representing the quadratic part.
  $$ C = \begin{pmatrix} c_{20} & c_{11} \\ c_{11} & c_{02} \end{pmatrix} $$
- Eigenvectors $\mathbf{e}_i$: Normalized 2D vectors $(e_{i1}, e_{i2})^T$.
- Eigenvalues $\lambda_i$: Scalar values.

**Steps:**

1.  **Construct Matrix C:** Extract coefficients from the input quadratic function. The linear and constant terms ($c_{10}, c_{01}, c_{00}$) are ignored for the purpose of determining principal axes and curvature classification, as they do not affect the second derivatives [S1, unofficial].
    $$ C = \begin{pmatrix} c_{20} & c_{11} \\ c_{11} & c_{02} \end{pmatrix} $$

2.  **Compute Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors:** Solve the characteristic equation $\det(C - \lambda I) = 0$ for $\lambda$. Let the roots be $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$. Compute the corresponding normalized eigenvectors $\mathbf{e}_1 = (e_{11}, e_{12})^T$ and $\mathbf{e}_2 = (e_{21}, e_{22})^T$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Classify Surface Type:** Calculate the product $P = \lambda_1 \lambda_2$.
    - If $P > 0$: **Elliptic** case.
    - If $P < 0$: **Hyperbolic** case.
    - If $P = 0$: **Parabolic** case (planar if both are zero) [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Construct Transformation Matrix H (Hyperbolic Case Only):**
    If the surface is hyperbolic, construct the transformation matrix $H$ to map the prototype hyperbolic polynomial $h(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = \bar{x}^2 - \bar{y}^2$ to the original function $f$.
    
    *Condition:* Assume $\lambda_1 > 0$ and $\lambda_2 < 0$. If $\lambda_1 < 0$ and $\lambda_2 > 0$, use the quadratic form of $-f$ instead [S1, unofficial].
    
    The matrix $H$ is defined as:
    $$ H = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_1}} e_{11} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{-\lambda_2}} e_{21} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_1}} e_{12} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{-\lambda_2}} e_{22} \end{pmatrix} $$
    
    This satisfies $H^T C H = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Generate Triangulation (Hyperbolic Case):**
    For the hyperbolic case, optimal triangle vertices in the prototype space $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ are:
    $$ v_1 = (\sqrt{\epsilon}, \sqrt{\epsilon})^T $$
    $$ v_2 = (\sqrt{\epsilon}, -\sqrt{\epsilon})^T $$
    $$ v_3 = ((1-\sqrt{5})\sqrt{\epsilon}, 0)^T $$
    where $\epsilon$ is a small error bound parameter [S1, unofficial].
    
    Transform these vertices to the original space using $\mathbf{v} = H \mathbf{v}_{proto}$. The final triangulation is established by transforming two edges according to $H$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
- Quadratic Form: $\mathbf{x}^T C \mathbf{x} = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
- Maximal Edge Error (Hyperbolic): For an edge with endpoints $a$ and $b$ in the prototype space:
  $$ \varepsilon_{\text{ab}} = \frac{1}{4} \left| (a_x - b_x)^2 - (a_y - b_y)^2 \right| $$
  The maximal error for the triangle is located at the midpoint of the edges because the surface has zero curvature along the lines $x+y=0$ and $x-y=0$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** Square roots of eigenvalues require $\lambda > 0$ (or $-\lambda > 0$). Ensure correct sign handling before taking square roots.
- **Degenerate Input:** If $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0$, the surface is a plane. The cluster region can be triangulated by considering only the vertices of its convex boundary polygon [S1, unofficial].
- **Sign Ambiguity:** In the hyperbolic case, if $\lambda_1 < 0$ and $\lambda_2 > 0$, the method requires using $-f$ to maintain the standard prototype form $\bar{x}^2 - \bar{y}^2$ [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Elliptic Case Triangulation](5516-elliptic-case-triangulation.md)
Handles surfaces where $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 > 0$. The graph surface is equivalent to a paraboloid. Optimal triangulation schemes differ from the hyperbolic case due to the uniform sign of curvature. See [Elliptic Case Triangulation](5516-elliptic-case-triangulation.md).

### Hyperbolic Case Triangulation
Handles surfaces where $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 < 0$. Uses the prototype $h(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = \bar{x}^2 - \bar{y}^2$ and places vertices exactly on the surface to minimize error, with maximal error at edge midpoints. See [Hyperbolic Case Triangulation](5516-hyperbolic-case-triangulation.md) (Note: The provided notes describe this in detail under the main text, but the prompt lists it as a child. The details are integrated into the main "How it works" section as it is the primary variant described in the source text for the transformation).

### [Elliptic Case Triangulation](5516-elliptic-case-triangulation.md)
Handles surfaces where $\lambda_1 \lambda_2 = 0$. One principal curvature is zero. If both are zero, the surface is planar. See [Parabolic Case Triangulation](5516-parabolic-case-triangulation.md).

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by eigenvalue decomposition of a $2 \times 2$ matrix, which is $O(1)$.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ for storing coefficients, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors.
- **Strengths:** Provides exact geometric classification and optimal error-bounded triangulation for quadratic surfaces.
- **Limitations:** Only applicable to quadratic surfaces. Requires careful handling of degenerate cases (planar surfaces).
- **When to use:** When rendering or approximating quadratic surfaces with guaranteed error bounds.
- **When not to use:** For non-quadratic surfaces or when approximate SDF values are sufficient without mesh generation.

## Background
Originated in the context of multiresolution modeling for scientific visualization, specifically for the triangulation of graph surfaces of quadratic functions [S1, unofficial]. The method leverages the spectral decomposition of the Hessian matrix (represented by $C$) to align the triangulation with the principal curvatures of the surface.
