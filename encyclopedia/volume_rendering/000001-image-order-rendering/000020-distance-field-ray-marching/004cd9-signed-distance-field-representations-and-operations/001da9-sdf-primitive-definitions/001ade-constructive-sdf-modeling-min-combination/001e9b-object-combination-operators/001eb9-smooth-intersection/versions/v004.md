# Smooth Intersection
Also called: S-Intersection

## Overview
Smooth Intersection is a constructive SDF operator that blends the overlap of two shapes to produce a rounded interior corner, serving as the smooth counterpart to the boolean intersection [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard smooth minimums which approximate unions, this operator approximates intersections by mixing the input distances based on their relative positions within the blending radius [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its sibling [SDF Smooth Union](1817-sdf-smooth-union.md) by its focus on the interior region where both distances are negative, whereas the union focuses on the exterior where at least one distance is positive.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Inputs: Two scalar signed distance values $d_1$ and $d_2$, and a smoothing parameter $k$ (radius).
- Output: A single scalar signed distance value $d$.

**Data Structures:**
None required; the operation is scalar.

**Parameters:**
- $k$: The blending radius or smoothness parameter. It controls the width of the transition zone between the sharp intersection and the smooth blend [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The operator computes an intermediate blend factor $h$ and then mixes the distances [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]:

$$
h = \text{clamp}\left(0.5 - 0.5 \frac{d_2 - d_1}{k}, 0.0, 1.0\right)
$$

$$
d = \text{mix}(d_2, d_1, h) + k \cdot h \cdot (1.0 - h)
$$

Where $\text{mix}(a, b, t) = a \cdot (1 - t) + b \cdot t$ [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def smooth_intersection(d1: float, d2: float, k: float) -> float:
    # Calculate the blend factor h
    # h = clamp(0.5 - 0.5 * (d2 - d1) / k, 0.0, 1.0)
    diff = d2 - d1
    h = 0.5 - 0.5 * diff / k
    if h < 0.0:
        h = 0.0
    elif h > 1.0:
        h = 1.0
    
    # Calculate the mixed distance
    # mix(d2, d1, h) = d2 * (1 - h) + d1 * h
    mixed = d2 * (1.0 - h) + d1 * h
    
    # Add the smoothing term
    # k * h * (1.0 - h)
    smooth_term = k * h * (1.0 - h)
    
    return mixed + smooth_term
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- If $k$ is too large relative to the object size, the geometry may become distorted or invalid [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
- The operator preserves Lipschitz continuity only if the gradients of the two SDFs are orthogonal; deviations greater than 30 degrees or parallel surfaces may cause artifacts [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: $O(1)$ per evaluation.
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(1)$.
- **Strengths**: Produces smooth, continuous transitions for interior corners; differentiable, making it suitable for gradient-based optimization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: May violate Lipschitz continuity if input gradients are not orthogonal, leading to ray marching errors [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
- **When to use**: When a smooth, rounded interior corner is desired between two intersecting volumes.
- **When not to use**: When sharp, precise boolean intersections are required, or when the intersecting surfaces are nearly parallel.

## Background
The method originates from implementations in PyTorch-based sphere tracing libraries, specifically `Torch-Sphere-Tracer` and `Sphere-Tracing-PyTorch3D`, which provide differentiable SDF operations for neural rendering and optimization [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It is part of the broader family of smooth CSG operators designed to maintain gradient continuity for training implicit surfaces.
