# Polyhedral Primitives
## Overview
Polyhedral primitives compute the exact Euclidean signed distance from a query point to the surface of convex polyhedra, enabling precise ray marching and collision detection for shapes with flat faces and sharp edges [G]. They are distinguished from smooth primitives (like spheres) by their piecewise-linear geometry and from implicit surfaces by their reliance on symmetry folding or explicit face/edge projections to resolve the distance analytically [G].

## Subtopics

### [Octahedron Signed Distance Function](2339-octahedron-signed-distance-function.md)
Computes the exact signed distance from a query point to the surface of a regular octahedron centered at the origin. It solves the geometric query required for ray marching or collision detection involving octahedral primitives. It is distinguished from other polyhedral SDFs by its reliance on coordinate permutation to handle the octahedron's symmetry, reducing the problem to a single face evaluation. Choose it when ray marching or collision detection specifically requires octahedral geometry.

### [Hexagonal Prism Signed Distance Function](2348-hexagonal-prism-signed-distance-function.md)
Computes the signed distance from a query point to a hexagonal prism aligned with the Z-axis, defined by a cross-section radius and a half-height. It differs from the [Triangular Prism SDF](2865-triangular-prism-signed-distance-function.md) by utilizing six-fold rotational symmetry to reflect points into a smaller fundamental domain, whereas the triangular prism relies on simpler planar projections. Choose it for modeling hexagonal columns or extruded hexagonal volumes.

### [Triangular Prism Signed Distance Function](2865-triangular-prism-signed-distance-function.md)
Computes the signed distance from a query point to the surface of a triangular prism. It solves the problem of defining a smooth, differentiable boundary for a prism-shaped volume, enabling its use in ray marching and implicit modeling. It is distinguished from other polyhedral primitives by its specific triangular cross-section geometry, defined by an equilateral triangle in the XY plane and extrusion along the Z axis. Choose it for triangular structural elements or extruded triangular profiles.

### [Dodecahedron Modulo](3737-dodecahedron-modulo.md)
The Dodecahedron Modulo method computes the signed distance from a point to the surface of a regular dodecahedron by folding the input space into a fundamental region (a single pentagonal face) using a sequence of reflections and rotations, then returning the distance to that face. It differs from analytical geometric approaches by relying on space-folding symmetries, which allows for a compact, branch-free implementation suitable for GPU ray marching. It serves as a primitive definition within the [Polyhedral Primitives](2529-polyhedral-primitives.md) family, distinct from other polyhedra like the [Octahedron Signed Distance Function](2339-octahedron-signed-distance-function.md) or [Hexagonal Prism Signed Distance Function](2348-hexagonal-prism-signed-distance-function.md) by its specific icosahedral symmetry group operations. Choose it when exact dodecahedral geometry is required in a branch-free shader environment.

## Background
Polyhedral SDFs are a staple of procedural geometry in computer graphics, popularized by the work of Inigo Quilez and others in the context of ray marching [G]. They allow for the creation of complex, hard-surface models using constructive solid geometry (CSG) operations without the need for mesh generation [G].
