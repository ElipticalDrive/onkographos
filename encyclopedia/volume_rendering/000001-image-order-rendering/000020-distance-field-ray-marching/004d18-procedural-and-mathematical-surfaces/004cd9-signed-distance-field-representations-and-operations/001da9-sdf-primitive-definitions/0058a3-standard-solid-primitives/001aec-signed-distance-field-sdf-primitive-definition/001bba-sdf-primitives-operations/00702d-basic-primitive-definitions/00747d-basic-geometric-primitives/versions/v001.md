# Basic Geometric Primitives

Also called: Standard SDF Primitives, Canonical Signed Distance Functions

## Overview
Basic Geometric Primitives provide the foundational signed distance functions (SDFs) for common shapes—specifically the Sphere, Box, and Plane—used in distance-field ray marching and constructive solid geometry (CSG). They differ from complex or derived primitives (such as Capsules or Tori) by offering closed-form, exact Euclidean distance calculations with minimal computational overhead, serving as the atomic building blocks for procedural modeling.

## Shared principles
All primitives in this family adhere to a common computational framework:
1.  **Canonical Placement:** Primitives are defined in a local coordinate system centered at the origin $(0,0,0)$ and axis-aligned where applicable.
2.  **Transformation:** To render a primitive at a different position, rotation, or scale, the query point $p$ is transformed into the primitive's local space (inverse transformation) before evaluation, or the primitive parameters are adjusted accordingly .
3.  **Signed Output:** The function returns a scalar $d \in \mathbb{R}$. $d < 0$ indicates the point is inside the primitive, $d > 0$ indicates it is outside, and $d = 0$ lies on the surface .
4.  **Gradient Availability:** The functions are differentiable almost everywhere, allowing for the computation of surface normals via the gradient $\nabla d$ .

## Subtopics

### [Sphere Signed Distance Function](1237-sphere-signed-distance-function.md)
Computes the exact Euclidean distance to a sphere's surface. It is the fundamental building block for ray marching due to its radial symmetry and is chosen for organic or rounded objects.

### [Signed Box Distance Function](1092-signed-box-distance-function.md)
Computes the exact Euclidean distance to an axis-aligned box. It distinguishes interior from exterior space and correctly calculates distances to edges and corners, unlike cheaper approximations. It is chosen for blocky, architectural, or voxel-like structures.

### [Plane Signed Distance Function](1102-plane-signed-distance-function.md)
Computes the distance to an infinite plane. It is computationally cheaper than curved primitives and serves as a fundamental building block for CSG operations, ground planes, and clipping boundaries.

## Background
The concept of using analytic distance functions for rendering was popularized by Inigo Quilez in his online tutorials . These primitives form the basis of Raymarching, a technique where rays are stepped through space using the distance function as a lower bound on the distance to the scene geometry. The Sphere SDF is particularly notable for enabling adaptive step sizes that guarantee surface intersection without overshooting .
