# Regularization and Gradient Losses

Also called: SDF Regularization, Geometric Consistency Losses

## Overview
This family defines auxiliary loss terms used during the training of Signed Distance Field (SDF) and Directional Distance Field (DDF) networks to enforce geometric priors beyond the primary photometric or depth supervision. While the parent [SDF Training with Eikonal Loss](2553-sdf-training-with-eikonal-loss.md) enforces the unit gradient magnitude constraint ($\|\nabla f(x)\| = 1$), this family addresses other geometric inconsistencies: inter-model agreement between SDF and DDF representations, surface smoothness, and the suppression of "free surfaces" (uncontrollable geometry) in regions behind visible surfaces where supervision is sparse.

## Shared principles
All losses in this family operate on the output of the neural network $f(x)$ (the SDF value) or its derivatives. They do not modify the network architecture but constrain the optimization landscape. Common characteristics include:
1.  **Evaluation Domain:** Losses are computed on sampled points $\mathcal{X}$, which may include points on the surface, points along rays, or sparse point clouds.
2.  **Differentiability:** All terms are differentiable with respect to the network parameters to allow gradient-based optimization.
3.  **Weighting:** Each loss term $\mathcal{L}_i$ is typically scaled by a hyperparameter $\lambda_i$ in the total loss function: $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{primary} + \sum \lambda_i \mathcal{L}_i$.

## Subtopics

### [Surface SDF Loss](14235-surface-sdf-loss.md)
Also called: Track-SDF Regularizer
**What it is:** A consistency loss for Directional Distance Field (DDF) networks that enforces agreement with a co-trained SDF network [14235-surface-sdf-loss].
**Distinction:** Unlike the Eikonal loss which enforces local gradient magnitude, this loss explicitly links two different geometric representations (DDF and SDF) by ensuring that points reconstructed via the DDF lie on the zero-level set of the SDF.
**When to choose:** Use when training a DDF network alongside an SDF network to resolve depth ambiguities or ensure geometric consistency between the two representations.
**Link:** [Surface SDF Loss](14235-surface-sdf-loss.md)

### Sparse Points SDF Loss
**What it is:** A regularization term applied to sparse point cloud data to encourage the SDF to be zero at known surface points.
**Distinction:** It operates on a specific subset of sparse points filtered by a geometric constraint (e.g., inside a unit sphere) and incorporates point weights.
**When to choose:** Use when sparse point cloud data is available and needs to be integrated into the SDF training process.
**Link:** [Geometric Loss Terms](17877-geometric-loss-terms.md) (Note: Specific implementation details are in the minor variant description below).

### SDF Smoothness Loss
**What it is:** A regularization term that penalizes the gradient of the SDF between adjacent sampled points, encouraging geometric smoothness [S2].
**Distinction:** It focuses on the spatial consistency of the SDF gradient field rather than the magnitude of the gradient itself.
**When to choose:** Use to reduce noise or jaggedness in the reconstructed surface, particularly in regions with sparse supervision.
**Link:** [Geometric Loss Terms](17877-geometric-loss-terms.md)

### SparseNeuS Sparseness Regularization
**What it is:** A term that penalizes uncontrollable free surfaces behind visible surfaces to generate compact geometry [S3].
**Distinction:** It uses an exponential penalty based on the absolute SDF value to push non-surface points away from the zero-level set, specifically targeting regions behind visible surfaces.
**When to choose:** Use in sparse-view neural surface reconstruction (e.g., SparseNeuS) to prevent the formation of arbitrary geometry in unobserved regions.
**Link:** [Geometric Loss Terms](17877-geometric-loss-terms.md)

### SDF Rescaling Term for Points Behind Visible Surfaces
**What it is:** A regularization term that encourages SDF values of points behind visible surfaces to be far from zero [S3].
**Distinction:** It uses a linear rescaling of the absolute SDF value to suppress free surfaces, contrasting with the exponential penalty of the Sparseness Regularization.
**When to choose:** Use as an alternative to the Sparseness Regularization when a linear penalty is preferred for controlling free surfaces.
**Link:** [Geometric Loss Terms](17877-geometric-loss-terms.md)

## Background
The need for regularization in SDF training arises from the ill-posed nature of reconstructing surfaces from limited observations (images or sparse points). Without constraints, neural networks can fit noise or generate arbitrary geometry in unobserved regions. The Eikonal loss [12] (covered in the parent article) is the most common gradient-based regularizer. This family extends that by addressing specific failure modes:
1.  **Inter-representation consistency:** When using multiple geometric representations (SDF and DDF), they must agree [14235-surface-sdf-loss].
2.  **Smoothness:** SDFs should vary smoothly in space [S2].
3.  **Compactness:** In sparse-view settings, "free surfaces" (artifacts behind visible geometry) are a major issue [S3].
