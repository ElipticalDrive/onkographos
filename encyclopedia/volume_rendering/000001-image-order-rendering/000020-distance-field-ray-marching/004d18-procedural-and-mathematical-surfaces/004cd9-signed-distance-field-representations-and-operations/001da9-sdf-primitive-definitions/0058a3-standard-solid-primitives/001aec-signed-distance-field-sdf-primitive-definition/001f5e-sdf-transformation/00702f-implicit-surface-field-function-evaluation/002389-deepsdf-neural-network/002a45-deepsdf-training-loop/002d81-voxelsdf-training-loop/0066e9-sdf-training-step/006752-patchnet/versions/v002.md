# PatchNet

Also called: Patch-Based Generalizable Deep Implicit 3D Shape Representations

## Overview
PatchNet represents a 3D object as a collection of learnable surface patches, where each patch models the geometry within a specific radius from its center using a neural implicit function [S1]. It solves the problem of generalizable surface reconstruction by treating patch latent codes and extrinsic parameters (position, orientation, radius) as free variables optimized directly during training (auto-decoding), rather than regressing them from an encoder [S1]. This approach allows for controllable deformation and generalization across object categories, outperforming global implicit representations like DeepSDF and structured approaches like DSIF, particularly when training data is limited [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:** A watertight mesh (for training) or point samples with truncated signed distance field (SDF) values (for test-time optimization) [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A blended SDF field $g_i(x)$ representing the object surface, from which meshes can be extracted via Marching Cubes [S1].

### Data Structures

**Patch Extrinsic Parameters ($e_{i,p}$)**
Each patch $p$ of object $i$ is defined by 7 parameters $e_{i,p} \in \mathbb{R}^7$ [S1]:
*   Radius $r_{i,p} \in \mathbb{R}$: The spherical radius of the patch [S1].
*   Center $c_{i,p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: The 3D position of the patch center [S1].
*   Orientation $\phi_{i,p} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Euler angles defining the rotation of the patch's canonical frame [S1].

**Patch Latent Code ($z_{i,p}$)**
*   $z_{i,p} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$: A vector describing the intrinsic geometry of the patch surface [S1].

**Network Weights ($\theta$)**
*   Parameters of the fully-connected neural network that maps $(z, x)$ to SDF values [S1].

### Initialization

1.  **Preprocessing:** Center the input mesh and fit it tightly into the unit sphere. Sample points mostly close to the surface and compute their truncated signed distance to the object surface, truncating at 0.1 [S1].
2.  **Latent Codes:** Initialize all patch latent codes $z_{i,p}$ to zero [S1].
3.  **Positions:** Initialize patch centers $c_{i,p}$ using greedy farthest point sampling of the object's surface point samples [S1].
4.  **Radii:** Set each patch radius $r_{i,p}$ to the minimum value such that every surface point sample is covered by its closest patch [S1].
5.  **Orientation:** Align the z-axis of the patch coordinate system with the surface normal at the closest point to the patch center [S1].

### Network Architecture
The network $f(x, z, \theta)$ takes a query point $x \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a latent code $z \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ as input and outputs a scalar SDF value [S1].
*   **Structure:** Eight weight-normalized fully-connected layers with 128 output dimensions and ReLU activations [S1].
*   **Concatenation:** The latent code $z$ and query point $x$ are concatenated to the input of the fifth layer [S1].
*   **Output:** The last layer outputs a single scalar, passed through a $\tanh$ activation to bound the SDF value [S1].

### Training Optimization
Training minimizes the total loss $L$ over network weights $\theta$, patch latent codes $\{z_i\}$, and extrinsics $\{e_i\}$ for all objects $i$ [S1]:

$$ \arg\min_{\theta, \{z_i\}, \{e_i\}} \sum_i L(z_i, e_i, \theta) $$

The total loss is defined as [S1]:

$$ L(z_i, e_i, \theta) = L_{recon}(z_i, e_i, \theta) + L_{ext}(e_i) + L_{reg}(z_i) $$

**1. Reconstruction Loss ($L_{recon}$)**
Minimizes the difference between predicted and ground truth SDF values for points within each patch's bounds [S1]:

$$ L_{recon}(z_i, e_i, \theta) = \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} \frac{1}{|S(e_{i,p})|} \sum_{x \in S(e_{i,p})} |f(x, z_{i,p}, \theta) - s(x)| $$

Where:
*   $N_P = 30$ is the number of patches [S1].
*   $S(e_{i,p})$ is the set of normalized point samples within the bounds of patch $p$ [S1].
*   $s(x)$ is the ground truth truncated SDF value [S1].

**2. Extrinsic Loss ($L_{ext}$)**
Guides the placement and orientation of patches [S1]:

$$ L_{ext}(e_i) = L_{sur}(e_i) + L_{cov}(e_i) + L_{rot}(e_i) + L_{scl}(e_i) + L_{var}(e_i) $$

*   **Surface Loss ($L_{sur}$):** Ensures patches stay close to the surface [S1]:
    $$ L_{sur}(e_i) = \omega_{sur} \cdot \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} \max\left( \frac{1}{|O_i|} \sum_{x \in O_i} \|c_{i,p} - x\|^2, t \right) $$
    Where $O_i$ is the set of surface points of object $i$, and $t = 0.06$ is a threshold [S1].

*   **Coverage Loss ($L_{cov}$):** Encourages uncovered surface points $U_i$ to be covered by at least one patch [S1]:
    $$ L_{cov}(e_i) = \omega_{cov} \cdot \frac{1}{|U_i|} \sum_{x \in U_i} \frac{w_{i,p,x}}{\sum_{p} w_{i,p,x}} (\|c_{i,p} - x\|^2 - r_{i,p}) $$
    Where $w_{i,p,x} = \exp\left(-0.5 \cdot \left(\frac{\|c_{i,p} - x\|^2 - r_{i,p}}{\sigma}\right)^2\right)$ with $\sigma = 0.05$ [S1].

*   **Rotation Loss ($L_{rot}$):** Aligns patch orientation with surface normals [S1]:
    $$ L_{rot}(e_i) = \omega_{rot} \cdot \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} (1 - \langle \phi_{i,p} \cdot [0,0,1]^T, n_{i,p} \rangle)^2 $$
    Where $n_{i,p}$ is the surface normal at $o_{i,p} = \arg\min_{x \in O_i} \|x - c_{i,p}\|^2$ [S1].

*   **Scale Loss ($L_{scl}$):** Encourages small patch radii to prevent overlap [S1]:
    $$ L_{scl}(e_i) = \omega_{scl} \cdot \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} r_{i,p}^2 $$

*   **Variance Loss ($L_{var}$):** Encourages similar patch sizes [S1]:
    $$ L_{var}(e_i) = \omega_{var} \cdot \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} (r_{i,p} - m_i)^2 $$
    Where $m_i$ is the mean patch radius of object $i$ [S1].

**3. Regularization ($L_{reg}$)**
L2 regularizer on latent codes assuming a Gaussian prior [S1]:

$$ L_{reg}(z_i) = \omega_{reg} \cdot \frac{1}{N_P} \sum_{p=0}^{N_P-1} \|z_{i,p}\|^2 $$

### Test-Time Optimization
For a new object given point samples and SDF values, optimize for patch latent codes and extrinsics using the same initialization, while keeping network weights $\theta$ fixed [S1].

### Blended Surface Reconstruction
To obtain a smooth SDF field $g_i(x)$ for rendering or meshing, blend overlapping patches using isotropic Gaussian weights [S1]:

$$ g_i(x) = \frac{\sum_{p \in P_{i,x}} w_{i,p,x} f(x, z_{i,p}, \theta)}{\sum_{p \in P_{i,x}} w_{i,p,x}} $$

Where $P_{i,x}$ is the set of patches covering point $x$, and the weight $w_{i,p,x}$ is defined as [S1]:

$$ w_{i,p,x} = \exp\left(-\frac{\|c_{i,p} - x\|^2}{2\sigma}\right) - \exp\left(-\frac{\|c_{i,p} - x\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) $$

With $\sigma = r_{i,p}/3$, ensuring the weight is zero at the patch boundary [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import numpy as np

class PatchNetNetwork(nn.Module):
    """
    Neural network for implicit patch representation.
    Input: x (3,), z (128,)
    Output: scalar SDF
    """
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # 8 weight-normalized FC layers, 128 dims, ReLU
        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(3, 128)), # Layer 0
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 1
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 2
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 3
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 4
            # Layer 5 input is concat(z, x) -> 128 + 3 = 131
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(131, 128)), # Layer 5
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 6
            nn.utils.weight_norm(nn.Linear(128, 128)), # Layer 7
            nn.Linear(128, 1) # Output layer
        ])

    def forward(self, x, z):
        h = x
        for i, layer in enumerate(self.layers):
            if i == 5:
                # Concatenate z and x at the input of the 5th layer (index 5)
                h = torch.cat([z, h], dim=-1)
            h = layer(h)
            if i < 7: # ReLU for all layers except the last
                h = torch.relu(h)
        # Last layer: tanh activation
        return torch.tanh(h)

def farthest_point_sample(points, num_patches):
    """
    Greedy farthest point sampling.
    points: Tensor of shape (N, 3)
    Returns: centers (num_patches, 3)
    """
    N = points.shape[0]
    centers = torch.zeros(num_patches, 3, device=points.device)
    centers[0] = points[0]
    dists = torch.full((num_patches,), float('inf'), device=points.device)
    
    for i in range(1, num_patches):
        # Compute distances from all points to the last added center
        dist_to_last = torch.norm(points.unsqueeze(1) - centers[i-1].unsqueeze(0), dim=2).squeeze(1)
        # Update minimum distances
        dists = torch.minimum(dists, dist_to_last)
        # Pick the farthest point
        idx = torch.argmax(dists)
        centers[i] = points[idx]
    return centers

def preprocess_mesh(mesh):
    """
    Preprocess a watertight mesh: center, fit to unit sphere, sample points, compute truncated SDF.
    Returns: surface_points (N, 3), sdf_values (N,)
    """
    # [G] Standard preprocessing: center mesh, normalize to unit sphere,
    # sample points near surface, compute truncated SDF at truncation=0.1.
    # Placeholder for actual mesh processing pipeline.
    surface_points = torch.randn(3000, 3) # Dummy data
    sdf_values = torch.randn(3000) # Dummy data
    return surface_points, sdf_values

def compute_extrinsic_loss(centers, radii, normals, surface_points, object_normals, params):
    """
    Compute L_ext = L_sur + L_cov + L_rot + L_scl + L_var
    """
    # L_sur: Surface loss
    dists_sur = torch.cdist(centers, surface_points) # (num_patches, N_surf)
    mean_sq_dists = torch.mean(dists_sur**2, dim=1) # (num_patches,)
    L_sur = torch.mean(torch.clamp(mean_sq_dists - params['t'], min=0)) # max(mean_sq, t) logic approximated for gradient flow, but exact max is used in formula
    
    # L_cov: Coverage loss for uncovered points
    dists_cov = torch.cdist(centers, surface_points) # (num_patches, N_surf)
    covered = torch.any(dists_cov**2 <= radii.unsqueeze(1)**2, dim=0) # (N_surf,)
    uncovered_mask = ~covered
    uncovered_points = surface_points[uncovered_mask]
    
    if len(uncovered_points) == 0:
        L_cov = torch.tensor(0.0)
    else:
        dists_uncovered = torch.cdist(centers, uncovered_points) # (num_patches, N_uncovered)
        sq_dists = dists_uncovered**2
        weights = torch.exp(-0.5 * ((sq_dists - radii.unsqueeze(1)) / params['sigma_cov'])**2)
        weight_sums = torch.sum(weights, dim=0) # (N_uncovered,)
        weight_sums = torch.clamp(weight_sums, min=1e-8)
        
        loss_terms = sq_dists - radii.unsqueeze(1)
        weighted_loss = torch.sum(weights * loss_terms, dim=0) / weight_sums
        L_cov = torch.mean(weighted_loss)
        
    # L_rot: Rotation loss
    dot_products = torch.sum(normals * object_normals, dim=1) # (num_patches,)
    L_rot = torch.mean((1 - dot_products)**2)
    
    # L_scl: Scale loss
    L_scl = torch.mean(radii**2)
    
    # L_var: Variance loss
    mean_radius = torch.mean(radii)
    L_var = torch.mean((radii - mean_radius)**2)
    
    total_ext = (params['w_sur'] * L_sur + 
                 params['w_cov'] * L_cov + 
                 params['w_rot'] * L_rot + 
                 params['w_scl'] * L_scl + 
                 params['w_var'] * L_var)
    return total_ext

def compute_reconstruction_loss(network, centers, radii, normals, z_codes, point_samples, sdf_values, params):
    """
    Compute L_recon
    """
    num_patches = z_codes.shape[0]
    total_loss = 0.0
    count = 0
    
    for p in range(num_patches):
        dists = torch.cdist(centers[p:p+1], point_samples) # (1, N)
        mask = dists[0] <= radii[p]
        patch_points = point_samples[mask]
        patch_sdfs = sdf_values[mask]
        
        if len(patch_points) == 0:
            continue
            
        # Transform points to patch-local canonical frame
        local_points = transform_points_to_patch_frame(patch_points, centers[p], normals[p])
        
        z_p = z_codes[p]
        z_batch = z_p.unsqueeze(0).expand(local_points.shape[0], -1)
        
        sdf_pred = network(local_points, z_batch).squeeze(-1)
        
        loss = torch.mean(torch.abs(sdf_pred - patch_sdfs))
        total_loss += loss
        count += 1
        
    if count == 0:
        return torch.tensor(0.0)
    return total_loss / count

def transform_points_to_patch_frame(points, center, normal):
    """
    Transform global points to patch-local canonical frame.
    Aligns z-axis with normal.
    """
    translated = points - center
    R = rotation_matrix_from_normal(normal)
    local_points = torch.matmul(translated, R.T)
    return local_points

def rotation_matrix_from_normal(normal):
    """
    Create a rotation matrix that aligns [0,0,1] with normal.
    """
    z_axis = torch.tensor([0.0, 0.0, 1.0], device=normal.device)
    axis = torch.cross(z_axis, normal)
    angle = torch.acos(torch.clamp(torch.dot(z_axis, normal), -1.0, 1.0))
    
    # Rodrigues' rotation formula
    K = torch.tensor([[0, -axis[2], axis[1]],
                      [axis[2], 0, -axis[0]],
                      [-axis[1], axis[0], 0]], device=normal.device)
    I = torch.eye(3, device=normal.device)
    R = I + torch.sin(angle) * K + (1 - torch.cos(angle)) * torch.matmul(K, K)
    return R

def train_patchnet(meshes, network, optimizer_z, optimizer_e, optimizer_theta, num_epochs=1000):
    """
    Main training loop.
    """
    for epoch in range(num_epochs):
        for obj in meshes:
            surface_points, sdf_values = preprocess_mesh(obj)
            
            if not hasattr(obj, 'patches_initialized'):
                centers, radii, normals = initialize_patches(surface_points)
                obj.centers = centers
                obj.radii = radii
                obj.normals = normals
                obj.z_codes = torch.zeros(30, 128)
                obj.patches_initialized = True
            
            optimizer_z.zero_grad()
            optimizer_e.zero_grad()
            optimizer_theta.zero_grad()
            
            recon_loss = compute_reconstruction_loss(
                network, obj.centers, obj.radii, obj.normals, 
                obj.z_codes, surface_points, sdf_values, params
            )
            
            ext_loss = compute_extrinsic_loss(
                obj.centers, obj.radii, obj.normals, 
                surface_points, obj.normals, params
            )
            
            reg_loss = torch.mean(obj.z_codes**2)
            
            total_loss = recon_loss + ext_loss + reg_loss
            
            total_loss.backward()
            
            optimizer_theta.step()
            optimizer_z.step()
            optimizer_e.step()
            
            if epoch % 200 == 0 and epoch > 0:
                for param_group in optimizer_theta.param_groups:
                    param_group['lr'] *= 0.5
                for param_group in optimizer_z.param_groups:
                    param_group['lr'] *= 0.5
                for param_group in optimizer_e.param_groups:
                    param_group['lr'] *= 0.5

def test_time_optimization(obj, point_samples, sdf_values, network, num_iterations=10000):
    """
    Optimize z and e for a new object with fixed network weights.
    """
    centers, radii, normals = initialize_patches(point_samples)
    z_codes = torch.zeros(30, 128)
    
    optimizer_z = torch.optim.Adam([z_codes], lr=1e-3)
    optimizer_e = torch.optim.Adam([centers, radii, normals], lr=1e-3)
    
    for i in range(num_iterations):
        optimizer_z.zero_grad()
        optimizer_e.zero_grad()
        
        recon_loss = compute_reconstruction_loss(
            network, centers, radii, normals, 
            z_codes, point_samples, sdf_values, params
        )
        
        ext_loss = compute_extrinsic_loss(
            centers, radii, normals, 
            point_samples, normals, params
        )
        
        reg_loss = torch.mean(z_codes**2)
        
        total_loss = recon_loss + ext_loss + reg_loss
        total_loss.backward()
        
        optimizer_z.step()
        optimizer_e.step()
        
        if i % 2000 == 0 and i > 0:
            for param_group in optimizer_z.param_groups:
                param_group['lr'] *= 0.5
            for param_group in optimizer_e.param_groups:
                param_group['lr'] *= 0.5
                
    return z_codes, centers, radii, normals
```

### Parameters
*   **Number of patches ($N_P$):** 30 [S1].
*   **Threshold ($t$):** 0.06 for $L_{sur}$ [S1].
*   **$\sigma$ (Coverage):** 0.05 for $L_{cov}$ [S1].
*   **$\sigma$ (Noise):** Standard deviation of Gaussian noise added to ground truth SDF values during robustness testing; tested values include 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, and 0 [S1].
*   **$\omega_{reg}$:** Linearly increased from 0 to $10^{-4}$ for 400 epochs, then constant [S1].
*   **Learning Rate (Network $\theta$):** Initially $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ [S1].
*   **Learning Rate (Latent/Extrinsics):** Initially $10^{-3}$ [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 64 [S1].
*   **SDF Point Samples per Object:** 3k [S1].
*   **Patch Latent Size:** 128 [S1].
*   **Baseline Patch Latent Size:** 4050 [S1].
*   **ICL-NUIM Specifics:** 800 patches, 50 million SDF samples, extrinsics fixed, 10k iterations, learning rate halved every 2k iterations, 25k SDF samples per iteration [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Wasteful Distribution:** Due to extrinsics initialization and $L_{var}$, all patches tend to have similar sizes, leading to a potentially wasteful distribution of coverage [S1].
*   **Generalization on Extrinsics:** Unlike DSIF, PatchNet does not regress extrinsics, avoiding issues with predicting extrinsics for shapes very different from training data [S1].
*   **Scaling:** For datasets with different scales (e.g., ICL-NUIM), the standard deviation for SDF sample generation must be adjusted (e.g., reduced by a factor of 100) [S1].

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Training Time:** 2 hours on Nvidia V100 GPU for PatchNets ($N_P=10$, size=512) vs 156 hours for the full dataset baseline [S1].
*   **Memory:** The representation is compact, storing only latent codes and extrinsics per object, unlike voxel grids [S1].

### Measured Performance
*   **ShapeNet Test Set:**
    *   Mean IoU: 92.1 (PatchNets) vs 77.4 (DeepSDF) and 76.5 (Baseline) [S1].
    *   Mean Chamfer Distance: 0.044 (PatchNets) vs 0.075 (DeepSDF) and 0.111 (Baseline) [S1].
    *   Mean F-Score: 94.8 (PatchNets) vs 89.9 (DeepSDF) and 80.6 (Baseline) [S1].
*   **Robustness (Reduced Test Set):**
    *   $\sigma=0.1$: IoU 81.2, Chamfer 0.037, F-Score 85.3 [S1].
    *   $\sigma=0.01$: IoU 90.3, Chamfer 0.045, F-Score 94.3 [S1].
    *   $\sigma=0.001$: IoU 91.5, Chamfer 0.047, F-Score 94.4 [S1].
    *   $\sigma=0$: IoU 91.6, Chamfer 0.045, F-Score 94.5 [S1].
*   **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** ~70% improvement in F-score compared to global-patch baseline when evaluating on Dynamic FAUST [S1].

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:** Outperforms DeepSDF, OccupancyNetworks, Structured Implicit Functions, and DSIF on most ShapeNet categories, despite being trained on a smaller subset (~4% of training data) [S1]. Better generalization with few training shapes (1-100 per category) [S1]. Avoids the need for input geometry representation for an encoder [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Patches have similar sizes due to initialization and regularization, which may not be optimal for all geometries [S1].

### When to Use
*   Use for surface reconstruction and generalizable 3D shape representation when training data is limited or when cross-dataset generalization is required [S1].

## Background

### Origin
PatchNet was introduced by Tretschk et al. in the paper "PatchNets: Patch-Based Generalizable Deep Implicit 3D Shape Representations" [S1].

### History and Development
The method builds upon DeepSDF [S1] by introducing a patch-based representation with learnable extrinsics. It was developed to address the limitations of global implicit representations and structured implicit functions, particularly in terms of generalization and handling articulated deformations [S1]. It was compared against concurrent work such as Deep Structured Implicit Functions (DSIF) [S1].

### Usage
PatchNet is used for surface reconstruction and generalizable 3D shape representation on datasets like ShapeNet and Dynamic FAUST [S1].
