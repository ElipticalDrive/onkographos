# Progressive Volume Distillation


## Overview
Progressive Volume Distillation (PVD) is a systematic method for converting trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) models between heterogeneous architectures, including MLPs, sparse tensors, low-rank tensors, and hash tables [S1]. It solves the problem of architectural incompatibility by constructing a unified two-level view of volume representations and performing distillation progressively from shallower to deeper levels [S1]. Unlike methods that distill only between identical architectures, PVD enables any-to-any conversion while maintaining teacher performance, leveraging a block-wise training strategy to accelerate the process significantly [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Input:** A trained teacher model $\phi^*$ (of any architecture: Hash, VM, MLP, or Tensor) and a target student architecture (different from or same as the teacher) [S1].
*   **Output:** A trained student model $\phi_s$ that approximates the teacher's radiance field [S1].

**Data Structures and Unified View**
PVD maps all NeRF architectures to a unified two-level view consisting of a feature extraction stage $\phi_1^*$ and a decoding stage $\phi_2^*$ [S1]. The teacher model is split into two cascaded modules:
$$ \phi^*(x, d) = \phi_2^*(\phi_1^*(x, d)) $$
where $x$ is the 3D coordinate and $d$ is the view direction [S1]. The split depends on the architecture:
1.  **Hybrid Representations:** $\phi_1^*$ is the explicit part (e.g., hash table lookup, tensor grid access) and $\phi_2^*$ is the implicit part (e.g., MLP decoder) [S1].
2.  **Purely Implicit (MLP):** The network is split by depth. $\phi_1^*$ comprises the first $K$ layers, and $\phi_2^*$ comprises the remaining layers. Default $K=4$ [S1].
3.  **Purely Explicit (Plenoxels):** $\phi_2^*$ is set to an identity function, as the explicit grid directly outputs density and color [S1].

**Loss Functions**
The total loss $L$ combines volume-aligned loss, density loss, color loss, RGB loss, and regularization [S1]:
$$ L = \omega_1 L_{v2} + \omega_2 L_{\sigma 2} + \omega_3 L_{c 2} + \omega_4 L_{rgb 2} + \omega_5 L_{reg} $$
where $\|\cdot\|^2$ denotes Mean Squared Error (MSE) [S1].

1.  **Volume-Aligned Loss ($L_{v2}$):** Aligns the intermediate feature outputs of the teacher and student [S1].
    $$ L_{v2} = \| \phi_{1t}^*(x, d) - \phi_{1s}^*(x, d) \|^2 $$
    where $\phi_{1t}^*$ and $\phi_{1s}^*$ are the outputs of the first stage for the teacher and student, respectively [S1].

2.  **Density Loss ($L_{\sigma 2}$):** Matches the density $\sigma$ after clipping to a constrained range $[a, b]$ to ensure numerical stability [S1].
    $$ L_{\sigma 2} = \| \min(\max(\sigma_t, a), b) - \min(\max(\sigma_s, a), b) \|^2 $$
    where $\sigma_t$ and $\sigma_s$ are the teacher and student densities [S1]. The range $[a, b]$ limits values that are physically consistent (transparent or opaque) but numerically unstable [S1].

3.  **Color Loss ($L_{c 2}$):** Matches the color outputs [S1].
    $$ L_{c 2} = \| c_t - c_s \|^2 $$
    where $c_t$ and $c_s$ are the color outputs [S1].

4.  **RGB Loss ($L_{rgb 2}$):** Matches the final rendered RGB values [S1].
    $$ L_{rgb 2} = \| \text{RGB}_t - \text{RGB}_s \|^2 $$

5.  **Regularization ($L_{reg}$):** Applied only to specific explicit or hybrid students [S1].
    *   For **Plenoxels** and **VM-decomposition**: Add $L_1$ sparsity loss and Total Variation (TV) regularization loss [S1].
    *   For other architectures: Not applied (or zero) [S1].

**Parameters**
*   **Loss Weights:** $\omega_1 = 2 \times 10^{-3}$ (volume-aligned), $\omega_2 = 2 \times 10^{-3}$ (density), $\omega_3 = 2 \times 10^{-3}$ (color), $\omega_4 = 1$ (RGB) [S1]. $\omega_5$ is not explicitly given but implied to be active for regularization terms [S1].
*   **Density Range $[a, b]$:** Specific values for $a$ and $b$ are not given in the sources, but the clipping function $\min(\max(\sigma, a), b)$ is mandatory [S1].
*   **Optimization:** Adam Optimizer with initial learning rate 0.02 [S1].
*   **Batch Size:** 4096 rays [S1].
*   **Total Steps:** 20,000 steps [S1].

**Algorithm: Block-wise Distillation**
To accelerate training, PVD uses a block-wise strategy that progressively unfreezes deeper stages of the student network, leveraging the volume-aligned loss to stabilize early training [S1].

1.  **Stage 1 (Steps 1–3,000):**
    *   Forward pass only through the student's first stage $\phi_{1s}^*$ to compute $L_{v2}$ and $L_{\sigma 2}$ (using teacher's $\phi_{1t}^*$ and $\sigma_t$).
    *   Backpropagate gradients to update $\phi_{1s}^*$ [S1].
    *   Note: The teacher's $\phi_{1t}^*$ is frozen and used as a reference [S1].

2.  **Stage 2 (Steps 3,001–8,000):**
    *   Forward pass through student's $\phi_{1s}^*$ and $\phi_{2s}^*$ to compute intermediate outputs.
    *   Compute $L_{v2}$, $L_{\sigma 2}$, $L_{c 2}$, and $L_{reg}$ (if applicable).
    *   Backpropagate gradients to update $\phi_{1s}^*$ and $\phi_{2s}^*$ [S1].

3.  **Stage 3 (Steps 8,001–20,000):**
    *   Forward pass through the full student network $\phi_s^*$ to compute final rendered RGB.
    *   Compute full loss $L$ including $L_{rgb 2}$ [S1].
    *   Backpropagate gradients to update all student parameters [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Numerical Instability:** Optimizing density $\sigma$ directly is unstable. Clipping $\sigma$ to $[a, b]$ is required [S1].
*   **Gradient Saturation:** Applying density loss on $\exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is inefficient due to gradient saturation and high computational cost of exponentials during block-wise implementation [S1].
*   **Architecture Split:** For purely explicit models (Plenoxels), $\phi_2^*$ is identity, so no MLP decoding occurs [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Distillation is significantly faster than training from scratch. An MLP-based NeRF distilled from a hashtable-based Instant-NGP model takes 1.5 hours compared to 25 hours for training from scratch [S1].
*   **Speedup Factor:** 10x to 20x faster than training the original NeRF from scratch [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the student architecture. The method itself does not add significant memory overhead beyond storing the teacher and student models [S1].
*   **Performance:** Maintains teacher performance levels. Conversions between Hash, VM, MLP, and Tensors show little difference in PSNR/SSIM compared to training from scratch or the teacher itself [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Enables any-to-any conversion; accelerates training; improves synthesis quality via density clipping and volume-aligned loss [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a trained teacher model; performance depends on the quality of the teacher [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Experiments performed on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU [S1].

## Background
Progressive Volume Distillation was proposed in the paper "One Is All: Bridging the Gap between Neural Radiance Fields Architectures with Progressive Volume Distillation" [S1]. It addresses the diversity of NeRF architectures (MLP, sparse tensors, low-rank tensors, hash tables) which have different trade-offs in training speed, rendering speed, and geometric manipulability [S1]. Prior methods focused on distilling between models of the same architecture, whereas PVD provides a systematic framework for heterogeneous conversion [S1]. The method was validated on NeRF-Synthetic, LLFF, and TanksAndTemples datasets [S1].
