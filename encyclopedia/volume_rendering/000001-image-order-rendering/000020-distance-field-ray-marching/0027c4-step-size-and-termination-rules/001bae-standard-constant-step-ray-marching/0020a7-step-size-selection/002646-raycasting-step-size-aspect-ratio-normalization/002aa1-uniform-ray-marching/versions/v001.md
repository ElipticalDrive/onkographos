# Uniform Ray Marching

Also called: Ray-to-Sample Generation, Uniform Ray Marching for Visualization

## Overview
Uniform Ray Marching generates 3D sample positions along rays within an Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) using a fixed spacing between samples [S1, S2, unofficial]. It serves as a dense uniform sampler for Line-of-Sight (LOS) integral visualization, replacing coarse sampling to avoid aliasing artifacts such as concentric 'sampling rings' caused by steep gradients near inner radius cuts [S2, unofficial]. It is often used as a fallback strategy in hardware or environments that do not support specialized libraries like nerfacc [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `rays_o`: Tensor of shape `(N, 3)` containing ray origins [S2, unofficial].
*   `rays_d`: Tensor of shape `(N, 3)` containing ray directions [S2, unofficial].
*   `aabb_min`, `aabb_max`: Vectors defining the AABB boundaries [S2, unofficial].
*   `render_step_size`: Scalar float defining the fixed distance between sample points [S2, unofficial].
*   `n_samples`: Integer specifying the number of uniform sub-segments per ray (alternative to `render_step_size`) [S2, unofficial].
*   `r_max_rsun`: Optional float specifying an outer sphere radius in solar radii ($R_{sun}$). If `None`, no outer sphere clipping is applied [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `t_s`: Tensor of shape `(M,)` containing start times for each segment [S2, unofficial].
*   `t_e`: Tensor of shape `(M,)` containing end times for each segment [S2, unofficial].
*   `ray_idx`: Tensor of shape `(M,)` mapping each segment to its parent ray index [S2, unofficial].
*   `valid`: Boolean tensor of shape `(N,)` indicating which original rays had valid intersections [S2, unofficial].
*   Where $M$ is the total number of valid samples across all rays.

**Data Structures:**
*   Rays are represented as separate origin and direction tensors.
*   Intersection times `t_min` and `t_max` are stored as 1D tensors of length $N$.
*   Indices are managed using integer tensors broadcasted to match the maximum number of steps per ray.

**Steps:**

1.  **Ray-AABB Intersection:**
    Compute the intersection of each ray with the AABB to determine the entry (`t_min`) and exit (`t_max`) distances along the ray direction.
    $$ t_{min}, t_{max} = \text{intersect\_ray\_aabb}(rays\_o, rays\_d, aabb\_min, aabb\_max) $$
    [S1, S2, unofficial]

2.  **Initial Validity Mask:**
    Initialize a boolean mask `ok` where rays are potentially valid:
    $$ ok = (t_{max} > t_{min}) $$
    [S2, unofficial]

3.  **Optional Outer Sphere Clipping:**
    If `r_max_rsun` is provided, clip the ray interval to the intersection with a sphere of radius $\rho = r_{max\_rsun} / aabb\_scale$.
    
    Calculate coefficients for the quadratic equation $|o + t d|^2 = \rho^2$:
    $$ A = \sum_{i} (rays\_d_i)^2 $$
    $$ B = 2.0 \sum_{i} (rays\_o_i \cdot rays\_d_i) $$
    $$ C = \sum_{i} (rays\_o_i)^2 - \rho^2 $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Compute the discriminant and handle numerical safety by clamping $A$ to $10^{-20}$ and the discriminant to $0.0$:
    $$ disc = B^2 - 4.0 A C $$
    $$ A_{safe} = \max(A, 10^{-20}) $$
    $$ disc_{safe} = \max(disc, 0.0) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Solve for intersection times $t_{in}$ and $t_{out}$:
    $$ t_{in} = \frac{-B - \sqrt{disc_{safe}}}{2.0 A_{safe}} $$
    $$ t_{out} = \frac{-B + \sqrt{disc_{safe}}}{2.0 A_{safe}} $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Update interval bounds and validity:
    $$ t_{min} = \max(t_{min}, t_{in}) $$
    $$ t_{max} = \min(t_{max}, t_{out}) $$
    $$ hit = (disc \ge 0) $$
    $$ ok = ok \land hit $$
    [S2, unofficial]

4.  **Final Validity Check:**
    Determine final valid rays where the interval is non-empty:
    $$ valid = ok \land (t_{max} > t_{min}) $$
    [S2, unofficial]

5.  **Generate Uniform Segments:**
    There are two common approaches depending on whether `n_samples` or `render_step_size` is the primary control.

    *Approach A: Fixed Number of Samples (`n_samples`)*
    For valid rays, let $a = t_{min}[valid]$ and $b = t_{max}[valid]$.
    Create $n_{samples} + 1$ linearly spaced points in $[0, 1]$:
    $$ u = \text{linspace}(0.0, 1.0, n_{samples} + 1) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Compute edge coordinates (broadcasting $a, b$ to shape $(N_{valid}, 1)$ and $u$ to $(1, n_{samples}+1)$):
    $$ edges = a[:, None] + (b - a)[:, None] \cdot u[None, :] $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Flatten edges into segment starts $t_s$ and ends $t_e$:
    $$ t_s = edges[:, :-1].reshape(-1) $$
    $$ t_e = edges[:, 1:].reshape(-1) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Generate ray indices mapping segments to valid rays:
    $$ ray\_idx = \text{repeat\_interleave}(\text{arange}(N_{valid}), n_{samples}) $$
    [S2, unofficial]

    *Approach B: Fixed Step Size (`render_step_size`)*
    Compute the number of steps per ray:
    $$ n_{steps} = \max(1, \lceil (t_{max} - t_{min}) / render\_step\_size \rceil) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Determine $max\_steps = \max(n_{steps})$.
    Create an index tensor $idx$ of shape $(1, max\_steps)$ containing $[0, 1, \dots, max\_steps-1]$.
    Broadcast $n_{steps}$ to shape $(N, 1)$ and create a boolean mask where $idx < n_{steps}$.
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Compute start times $t_s$ and end times $t_e$:
    $$ t_s = (t_{min} + idx \cdot render\_step\_size) $$
    $$ t_e = \min(t_{min} + (idx + 1) \cdot render\_step\_size, t_{max}) $$
    [S2, unofficial]
    
    Flatten $t_s$ and $t_e$ using the mask.
    Construct $ray\_idx$ by expanding ray indices $[0, N-1]$ to shape $(N, max\_steps)$ and flattening with the mask.
    [S2, unofficial]

6.  **Compute Sample Positions (Post-Processing):**
    If sample positions are required, compute them from the generated $t$ values:
    $$ samples = rays\_o[ray\_idx] + t_s \cdot rays\_d[ray\_idx] $$
    [S1, unofficial]

**Pseudocode (PyTorch-like, Approach A):**

```python
def uniform_ray_marching(rays_o, rays_d, aabb_min, aabb_max, n_samples, r_max_rsun=None, aabb_scale=1.0):
    # 1. Ray-AABB Intersection
    t_min, t_max = ray_aabb_intersect(rays_o, rays_d, aabb_min, aabb_max)
    
    # 2. Initial Validity
    ok = t_max > t_min
    
    # 3. Optional Outer Sphere Clipping
    if r_max_rsun is not None:
        rho = r_max_rsun / aabb_scale
        A = (rays_d * rays_d).sum(dim=-1)
        B = 2.0 * (rays_o * rays_d).sum(dim=-1)
        C = (rays_o * rays_o).sum(dim=-1) - rho * rho
        
        # Numerical safety
        A_safe = torch.clamp(A, min=1e-20)
        disc = B * B - 4.0 * A_safe * C
        disc_safe = torch.clamp(disc, min=0.0)
        
        t_in = (-B - torch.sqrt(disc_safe)) / (2.0 * A_safe)
        t_out = (-B + torch.sqrt(disc_safe)) / (2.0 * A_safe)
        
        t_min = torch.maximum(t_min, t_in)
        t_max = torch.minimum(t_max, t_out)
        
        hit = disc >= 0
        ok = ok & hit
        
    # 4. Final Validity
    valid = ok & (t_max > t_min)
    
    # 5. Generate Uniform Segments for valid rays
    # Filter rays
    valid_rays_o = rays_o[valid]
    valid_t_min = t_min[valid]
    valid_t_max = t_max[valid]
    
    # Create interpolation grid
    u = torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n_samples + 1, device=rays_o.device)
    
    # Broadcast and compute edges
    # valid_t_min shape: (N_valid, 1)
    # u shape: (n_samples + 1,)
    edges = valid_t_min[:, None] + (valid_t_max[:, None] - valid_t_min[:, None]) * u[None, :]
    
    # Flatten
    t_s = edges[:, :-1].reshape(-1)
    t_e = edges[:, 1:].reshape(-1)
    
    # Ray indices
    N_valid = valid.sum()
    ray_idx = torch.arange(N_valid, device=rays_o.device).repeat_interleave(n_samples)
    
    return t_s, t_e, ray_idx, valid
```

## Subtopics

### Oversampled 3D Ray March
An oversampling technique where each fine sample owns the slab it is the midpoint of, clipped to the brick's segment, ensuring slab thicknesses tile $[0, len]$ exactly at LOD (Level of Detail) interfaces [S3, unofficial]. This addresses discontinuities at exit faces caused by previous empty-space skip implementations that probed one stride past each cube's exit face, leaving material in the last sub-stride unprobed [S3, unofficial]. In CPU replicas, this corrected worst-case two-cube split errors ranging from -3.99% to +0.68% [S3, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $S$ is the number of samples per ray. The intersection test is $O(N)$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$ to store the generated sample positions and indices.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; deterministic; avoids aliasing artifacts associated with coarse sampling [S2, unofficial]; serves as a robust fallback when specialized libraries are unavailable [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Fixed step size may lead to over-sampling in empty space or under-sampling in high-frequency regions if not adaptive. The "oversampled" variant requires careful slab ownership logic to prevent discontinuities at LOD boundaries [S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Measured whole-image Total Variation (TV) error improvements of -2.7% (x) and -2.2% (y) in browser-based WebGPU rendering with 64 chunks [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Uniform Ray Marching is a fundamental technique in volume rendering, often implemented as a baseline or fallback method. It is cited in the context of the Jax-Volumetric-Renderer [S1, unofficial] and CoroNeRF_public [S2, unofficial]. Recent implementations in niivue have refined the method to handle multi-LOD streaming and slab tiling correctly, addressing specific artifacts in browser-based visualization [S3, unofficial].
