# Idempotent Eikonal Ray Marching

Also called: Curvature-Bounded Contraction, Fixed-Point Projection for Real-Time Implicit Surface Rendering

## Overview
Idempotent Eikonal Ray Marching is a ray-surface intersection method that formulates intersection as a curvature-accelerated contraction operator on the Eikonal distance field to solve the computational stall of sphere tracing near grazing angles and silhouette edges [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent, Gradient-based Sphere Tracing with Lipschitz Constraints, by selectively activating second-order Halley contraction only within grazing silhouette horizons while maintaining single-query Lipschitz advances in open space, thereby truncating the grazing stall tail by up to 4× [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$; a Signed Distance Function (SDF) $f(\mathbf{x})$ and its derivatives; a fixed-point invariance threshold $\epsilon = 10^{-15}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** The intersection distance $t$ along the ray, or a failure flag if no intersection is found within bounds [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray State:** Current position $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, current distance estimate $t_{curr}$, and previous distance estimate $t_{prev}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Derivatives:** Directional first-order derivative $f'$ and second-order derivative $f''$ evaluated along the ray trajectory [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Initialization:** Set initial step size $t$ based on the initial SDF value $f(\mathbf{o})$. Set $t_{prev} = 0$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Loop Condition:** While the fixed-point invariance condition $||P_{hit}^2 - P_{hit}|| > \epsilon$ is not met, where $P_{hit}$ is the contraction operator [S1, unofficial]:
    a.  **Evaluate Derivatives:** Compute the directional first-order derivative $f'$ and second-order derivative $f''$ at the current position $\mathbf{r}(t)$ along the ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ [S1, unofficial].
    b.  **Determine Contraction Mode:**
        i.  If the ray is in open space (not near a grazing silhouette horizon), execute a single-query Lipschitz advance [S1, unofficial].
        ii. If the ray is within a grazing silhouette horizon, activate second-order Halley contraction [S1, unofficial].
    c.  **Compute Step:**
        i.  *Lipschitz Advance:* Compute step size $\Delta t$ bounded by dynamic Lipschitz safety constraints derived from $f'$ [S1, unofficial].
        ii. *Halley Contraction:* Compute step size $\Delta t$ using a rational Halley formula involving $f$, $f'$, and $f''$, bounded by dynamic Lipschitz safety constraints [S1, unofficial].
    d.  **Update Position:** Update $t_{prev} = t$ and $t = t + \Delta t$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Termination:** Upon entering the root neighborhood, the operator achieves terminal orthogonal projection with fixed-point invariance [S1, unofficial]. Return $t$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Fixed-Point Invariance Condition:**
    $$ ||P_{hit}^2 - P_{hit}|| \le 10^{-15} $$
    where $P_{hit}$ is the contraction operator mapping the current distance estimate to the next [S1, unofficial].
*   **Halley Contraction (Rational):**
    The specific rational Halley formula is not explicitly detailed in the sources, but it utilizes directional first- and second-order derivatives $f'$ and $f''$ along the ray trajectory [S1, unofficial]. It is bounded by dynamic Lipschitz safety constraints [S1, unofficial].
*   **Lipschitz Safety Constraints:**
    The step size is bounded by dynamic Lipschitz constraints derived from the gradient of the SDF [S1, unofficial]. The exact Lipschitz constant calculation is not provided, but it ensures the step does not overshoot the surface [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Fixed-point invariance threshold:** $10^{-15}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Grazing Angles:** Standard sphere tracing suffers from severe iteration inflation (40–60+ steps per ray) and tail-latency spikes near surface horizons because the spatial advance is conservatively bounded by radial distance $\phi(r(t))$, which approaches zero even when the ray intersects geometry far along its directional path [S1, unofficial]. Idempotent Eikonal Ray Marching mitigates this by selectively activating second-order Halley contraction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The fixed-point invariance threshold of $10^{-15}$ ensures high precision in the final projection [S1, unofficial].
*   **Derivative Evaluation:** Accurate evaluation of directional first- and second-order derivatives is critical for the Halley contraction [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Segment Tracing with Local Lipschitz Bounds
A sibling method that uses local Lipschitz bounds for step sizing. It is chosen when local smoothness can be guaranteed, whereas Idempotent Eikonal Ray Marching is preferred for handling grazing angles and silhouette edges with curvature acceleration [S1, unofficial].

### Gradient-based Sphere Tracing with Lipschitz Constraints
The parent method that uses the gradient of the SDF to compute larger, safer step sizes. Idempotent Eikonal Ray Marching extends this by adding second-order Halley contraction for grazing angles [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduces traversal from 55 to 14 steps on silhouette rays, truncating the grazing stall tail by up to 4× [S1, unofficial]. Reduces full-frame mean iterations from 14.6 to 11.6 steps, a 20% reduction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Zero dynamic heap allocation during traversal [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Achieves throughput of 27.0–33.3 MRays/s on multi-threaded CPUs across CSG manifolds and authentic 3D biological cortical meshes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Truncates the grazing stall tail by up to 4× on silhouette rays; provides deterministic frame pacing for latency-critical applications; zero dynamic heap allocation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Classical sphere tracing retains higher raw throughput on simple analytical primitives due to single-query simplicity [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When dealing with implicit surfaces, neural SDFs, and level sets on resource-constrained mobile and XR platforms, especially near grazing angles and silhouette edges [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When rendering simple analytical primitives where classical sphere tracing's single-query simplicity offers higher raw throughput [S1, unofficial].
