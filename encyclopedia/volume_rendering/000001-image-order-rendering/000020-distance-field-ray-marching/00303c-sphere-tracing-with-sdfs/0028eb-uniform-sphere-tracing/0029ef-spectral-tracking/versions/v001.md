# Spectral Tracking

Also called: spectral delta tracking

## Overview
Spectral Tracking is a variance reduction technique for volume rendering that handles per-wavelength optical properties by decomposing the medium's scattering and absorption coefficients into spectral components [S1, unofficial]. It extends the majorant-based sampling of Uniform Sphere Tracing by maintaining separate spectral weights for absorption and scattering events, allowing for unbiased estimation of the spectral radiance contribution [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard delta tracking which treats extinction as a monolithic event, Spectral Tracking distinguishes between absorption and scattering probabilities on a per-wavelength basis, which is critical for accurate color rendering in participating media [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `vsdf`: A volume scene definition object containing:
  - `object`: The volume object container.
  - `object->density_vol`: The density volume field.
  - `object->emission_vol`: The emission volume field (unused in the core sampling loop but part of the structure).
  - `vsdf.scatter`: The single-scattering albedo (ratio of scattering to extinction), typically a `vec3f` or scalar depending on implementation.
- `max_distance`: The maximum distance the ray is allowed to travel within the medium.
- `rng`: The random number generator state.
- `ray`: The input ray defined by origin `o` and direction `d`.

**Outputs:**
- A pair `{ path_length, result }`:
  - `path_length`: The distance traveled along the ray before an event (absorption, scattering, or max distance).
  - `result`: A `vec3f` representing the spectral contribution (transmittance-weighted coefficient ratio).

**Data Structures:**
- `vec3f`: A 3-component floating-point vector representing spectral channels (e.g., RGB).
- `zero3f`: A `vec3f` initialized to `(0, 0, 0)`.
- `max(v)`: The maximum component value of vector `v`.

**Parameters:**
- `max_density`: The global maximum density of the volume, calculated as `density_vol->max_voxel * object->density_mult`.
- `majorant_density`: Set to `max_density`. This serves as the upper bound for the total extinction coefficient across all wavelengths.
- `imax_density`: `1.0f / max_density`.

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    - Set `path_length = 0.0f`.
    - Set `tr = vec3f(1)` (current transmittance).
    - Set `f = vec3f(1)` (final spectral weight accumulator).
    - Calculate `sigma_s = vsdf.scatter * max_density`. This represents the scattering coefficient scaled by the majorant.
    - Calculate `Ps = sigma_s * imax_density`. This is the normalized probability of scattering relative to the majorant.

2.  **Sampling Loop:**
    - **Sample Path Length:** Generate a uniform random number `u` in `[0, 1)`. Update `path_length`:
      $$ \text{path\_length} \leftarrow \text{path\_length} - \ln(1 - u) \times \text{imax\_density} $$
      This samples an exponential distribution with rate `majorant_density`.
    - **Termination Check:** If `path_length >= max_distance`, break the loop.
    - **Position Evaluation:** Calculate the current position:
      $$ \text{current\_pos} = \text{ray}.o + \text{path\_length} \times \text{ray}.d $$
    - **Extinction Evaluation:** Evaluate the total extinction coefficient at `current_pos`:
      $$ \sigma_t = \text{eval\_vpt\_density}(vsdf, \text{current\_pos}) $$
      Note: `eval_vpt_density` returns the total extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ as a `vec3f`.
    - **Component Calculation:**
      - Non-scattering extinction (majorant remainder):
        $$ \sigma_n = \text{majorant\_density} - \sigma_t $$
      - Absorption coefficient:
        $$ \sigma_a = \sigma_t - \sigma_s $$
        *Note: In some implementations, $\sigma_a$ is derived from $\sigma_t$ and the scattering albedo. Here, $\sigma_s$ is fixed to the majorant-scaled scattering.*
    - **Probability Normalization:**
      - Total extinction probability:
        $$ P_t = \sigma_t \times \text{imax\_density} $$
      - Non-interaction probability:
        $$ P_n = \sigma_n \times \text{imax\_density} $$
      - Absorption probability:
        $$ P_a = 1.0 - P_s - P_n $$
        *Note: $P_s$ is the pre-calculated scattering probability. $P_a$ represents the remaining probability mass assigned to absorption.*
    - **Transmittance Update:**
      $$ \text{tr} \leftarrow \text{tr} \times \exp(-\sigma_t \times \text{path\_length}) $$
      *Note: This step applies the transmittance for the sampled distance. However, since the path length was sampled from the majorant distribution, this transmittance update is technically redundant for the *next* step's sampling but is used for the final contribution calculation.*

3.  **Event Selection:**
    - Generate a new uniform random number `u` in `[0, 1)`.
    - **Absorption Check:** If `max(P_a) > u`:
      - Calculate contribution:
        $$ \text{contrib} = \frac{\sigma_a}{\text{majorant\_density} \times P_a} $$
      - Normalize by maximum component:
        $$ \text{contrib} \leftarrow \frac{\text{contrib}}{\max(\text{contrib})} $$
      - Set `vsdf.event = EVENT_ABSORB`.
      - Set `vsdf.density = \sigma_t`.
      - Return `{ path_length, tr * contrib }`.
    - **Scattering Check:** Else if `max(P_t) > u`:
      - Calculate contribution:
        $$ \text{contrib} = \frac{\sigma_s}{\text{majorant\_density} \times P_s} $$
      - Normalize by maximum component:
        $$ \text{contrib} \leftarrow \frac{\text{contrib}}{\max(\text{contrib})} $$
      - Set `vsdf.event = EVENT_SCATTER`.
      - Set `vsdf.density = \sigma_t`.
      - Return `{ path_length, tr * contrib }`.
    - **Non-Interaction (Majorant Step):** Else:
      - Calculate contribution:
        $$ \text{contrib} = \sigma_n $$
      - Normalize by maximum component:
        $$ \text{contrib} \leftarrow \frac{\text{contrib}}{\max(\text{contrib})} $$
      - Update accumulator:
        $$ f \leftarrow f \times \text{contrib} $$
      - Continue to the next iteration of the loop.

4.  **Final Return:**
    - If the loop completes (i.e., `path_length >= max_distance`), return `{ max_distance, f }`.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def eval_spectral_tracking(vsdf, max_distance, rng, ray):
    # Initialization
    object = vsdf.object
    density_vol = object.density_vol
    max_density = density_vol.max_voxel * object.density_mult
    majorant_density = max_density
    imax_density = 1.0 / max_density
    
    path_length = 0.0
    tr = vec3f(1.0)
    f = vec3f(1.0)
    
    # sigma_s is scattering coefficient scaled by majorant
    sigma_s = vsdf.scatter * max_density
    # Ps is normalized scattering probability
    Ps = sigma_s * imax_density
    
    while True:
        # Sample path length from exponential distribution with rate majorant_density
        u1 = rand1f(rng)
        path_length -= log(1.0 - u1) * imax_density
        
        # Check termination
        if path_length >= max_distance:
            break
            
        # Calculate current position
        current_pos = ray.o + path_length * ray.d
        
        # Evaluate total extinction coefficient at current position
        sigma_t = eval_vpt_density(vsdf, current_pos)
        
        # Calculate components
        sigma_n = majorant_density - sigma_t
        sigma_a = sigma_t - sigma_s
        
        # Calculate normalized probabilities
        Pt = sigma_t * imax_density
        Pn = sigma_n * imax_density
        Pa = 1.0 - Ps - Pn
        
        # Update transmittance
        tr *= exp(-sigma_t * path_length)
        
        # Event selection
        u2 = rand1f(rng)
        
        if max(Pa) > u2:
            # Absorption event
            contrib = sigma_a / (majorant_density * Pa)
            contrib /= max(contrib)
            vsdf.event = EVENT_ABSORB
            vsdf.density = sigma_t
            return (path_length, tr * contrib)
            
        elif max(Pt) > u2:
            # Scattering event
            contrib = sigma_s / (majorant_density * Ps)
            contrib /= max(contrib)
            vsdf.event = EVENT_SCATTER
            vsdf.density = sigma_t
            return (path_length, tr * contrib)
            
        else:
            # Non-interaction (majorant step)
            contrib = sigma_n
            contrib /= max(contrib)
            f *= contrib
            
    return (max_distance, f)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Negative Probabilities:** If `sigma_t` exceeds `majorant_density` due to numerical error or incorrect majorant estimation, `sigma_n` becomes negative, leading to invalid probabilities. The majorant must be a strict upper bound.
- **Absorption Probability Calculation:** The formula $P_a = 1.0 - P_s - P_n$ assumes that $P_s + P_n \le 1$. If `sigma_s` is not consistent with `sigma_t` and `sigma_n`, this can lead to negative $P_a$. Specifically, `sigma_a` is calculated as `sigma_t - sigma_s`. If `sigma_t < sigma_s`, `sigma_a` is negative. This implies that `sigma_s` (scattering coefficient) must be less than or equal to `sigma_t` (total extinction) at all points. Since `sigma_s` is fixed to `vsdf.scatter * max_density`, and `sigma_t` varies, this requires `vsdf.scatter` to be the single-scattering albedo ($\sigma_s / \sigma_t$) and `sigma_t` to be consistent. However, the code uses a fixed `sigma_s` derived from the majorant. This is a potential source of error if `sigma_t` drops below the fixed `sigma_s` value.
- **Normalization:** The division by `max(contrib)` ensures that the contribution vector components are $\le 1$. This is crucial for the unbiased estimation when using the maximum component for rejection sampling or weighting.
- **Transmittance Update:** The transmittance `tr` is updated using `sigma_t` and `path_length`. However, `path_length` is sampled from the majorant distribution. The term `exp(-sigma_t * path_length)` is the actual transmittance for the sampled distance. This is correct for the final contribution calculation.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of majorant steps taken before an interaction or termination. The number of steps follows a geometric distribution with mean $1/P_{interaction}$, where $P_{interaction}$ is the probability of an interaction (scattering or absorption) relative to the majorant.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory, excluding the volume data structure.
- **Strengths:** Handles spectral variations in optical properties efficiently. By using a single majorant for all wavelengths, it avoids the need for separate sampling passes for each channel.
- **Limitations:** Requires a valid majorant density. If the majorant is not tight, the algorithm suffers from high variance due to many non-interaction steps. The fixed `sigma_s` assumption may lead to inconsistencies if the scattering albedo varies significantly across the volume.
- **When to use:** When rendering participating media with spectrally varying optical properties and a known upper bound on extinction.
- **When not to use:** When the majorant is very loose compared to the actual extinction, leading to poor performance. Also not suitable if the scattering coefficient is not consistent with the total extinction coefficient across the volume.

## Background
Spectral Tracking is an implementation of spectral delta tracking, a method for sampling participating media with wavelength-dependent properties. The technique was discussed in the context of the Yocto/GL VPT project, where it was identified as a correction to a previous incorrect implementation [S1, unofficial]. The method relies on the principle of majorant density sampling, extended to handle multiple spectral channels by decomposing the extinction into scattering and absorption components [S1, unofficial].
