# Specular Radiance Calculation

Also called: Direct Specular Radiance, Indirect Specular Radiance via Cone Tracing, Specular Lighting

## Overview
Specular radiance calculation determines the view-dependent specular contribution of a surface in real-time volume rendering, a component that cannot be precomputed in a voxel texture due to its dependency on camera position and surface reflection [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by splitting the calculation into indirect specular radiance (computed via cone tracing through the voxel volume) and direct specular radiance (computed via a geometric visibility term), whereas siblings like [Nested Raymarching for Refraction](1957-nested-raymarching-for-refraction.md) focus on transmission or [Real-time Reflection of Clouds](2398-real-time-reflection-of-clouds.md) may use different approximation strategies for reflection [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $I$: Incoming camera direction vector (view direction) [S1, unofficial].
- $N$: Surface normal vector [S1, unofficial].
- $\theta$: Cone width (diameter), determined by surface roughness [S1, unofficial].
- $P$: Surface position (3D fragment position) [S1, unofficial].
- $L$: Light direction vector [S1, unofficial].
- $V$: Visibility factor (occlusion) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- $R_{indirect}$: Indirect specular radiance [S1, unofficial].
- $R_{direct}$: Direct specular radiance [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- No specific complex data structures are required for the calculation itself, but it relies on the output of the [Ray Marching with Reflections](1963-ray-marching-with-reflections.md) step to provide the reflected direction and surface properties. The cone tracing function relies on the voxel texture data structure.

**Steps:**
1.  **Calculate Reflected Direction:** Compute the direction of reflection based on the incident view direction and surface normal.
    $$ \text{Reflect Direction} = \text{normalize}(I - 2.0 \cdot \text{dot}(N, I) \cdot N) $$
    where $I$ is the incoming camera direction and $N$ is the surface normal [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Determine Cone Width:** The cone width $\theta$ is derived from the surface roughness. For a completely smooth surface, the cone diameter is close to $1^\circ$ of the hemisphere. As roughness increases, the diameter increases up to a maximum of $180^\circ$ coverage to emulate light spreading on uneven surfaces [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Calculate Indirect Specular Radiance:** Perform cone tracing from the surface position in the reflected direction to gather indirect specular light from the environment/volume.
    $$ \text{Indirect Specular Radiance} = \text{cone trace}(\text{cone Width}, \text{surfacePosition}, \text{Reflect Direction}) $$
    The `cone trace` function is implemented based on the volume sampling logic described in the source, typically involving iterative distance stepping and voxel sampling with occlusion accumulation [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Calculate Direct Specular Radiance:** Compute the direct specular contribution using a geometry term that depends on the camera's point of view, the surface normal, and the light direction, scaled by PBR shininess.
    $$ \text{direct specular radiance} = \text{visibility} \cdot \max(0, \text{dot}(\text{reflectDir}, \text{light direction})) $$
    where `reflectDir` is the result from Step 1, `light direction` is the vector to the light source, and `visibility` accounts for occlusion [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def calculate_specular_radiance(I, N, P, L, visibility, roughness):
    # Step 1: Calculate Reflected Direction
    # I is the incoming camera direction (view direction)
    # N is the surface normal
    reflect_dir = normalize(I - 2.0 * dot(N, I) * N)
    
    # Step 2: Determine Cone Width based on roughness
    # Smooth surface: ~1 degree cone
    # Rough surface: up to 180 degrees cone
    # This maps roughness to cone diameter theta
    cone_width = map_roughness_to_cone_width(roughness)
    
    # Step 3: Calculate Indirect Specular Radiance via Cone Tracing
    # cone_trace uses the voxel texture, surface position P, reflected direction, and cone width
    indirect_radiance = cone_trace(cone_width, P, reflect_dir)
    
    # Step 4: Calculate Direct Specular Radiance
    # Uses geometry term and visibility
    direct_radiance = visibility * max(0.0, dot(reflect_dir, L))
    
    return indirect_radiance, direct_radiance
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Precision:** The calculation is view-dependent and must be performed in real-time; precomputing it in a voxel texture is not feasible [S1, unofficial].
- **Roughness Mapping:** The cone width must correctly map from 0 to 1 roughness to 1 degree to 180 degrees to accurately simulate surface scattering [S1, unofficial].
- **Visibility:** The visibility term must accurately reflect occlusion to prevent light leaking through geometry [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The indirect specular radiance calculation depends on the cone tracing algorithm, which involves iterative voxel sampling. The complexity is proportional to the number of steps in the cone trace, which is influenced by the cone width and scene density [S1, unofficial]. The direct specular radiance is $O(1)$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires access to the voxel texture for cone tracing. No additional large memory structures are needed for the calculation itself [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Evaluated by timing the overall program and specific functions like rendering and light calculation. Performance varies with voxel texture size and scene complexity [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Provides accurate, view-dependent specular reflections in real-time by leveraging cone tracing for indirect light and geometric terms for direct light [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Computationally expensive due to cone tracing. Accuracy depends on the voxel resolution and the roughness-to-cone-width mapping [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When real-time, view-dependent specular reflections are required in a voxel-based rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When precomputed lighting is sufficient or when performance constraints prohibit real-time cone tracing [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from the evaluation of voxel cone tracing for real-time applications, specifically focusing on the specular radiance component of the lighting model [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of physically based rendering (PBR) and cone tracing techniques for approximating indirect lighting in voxel environments [S1, unofficial].
