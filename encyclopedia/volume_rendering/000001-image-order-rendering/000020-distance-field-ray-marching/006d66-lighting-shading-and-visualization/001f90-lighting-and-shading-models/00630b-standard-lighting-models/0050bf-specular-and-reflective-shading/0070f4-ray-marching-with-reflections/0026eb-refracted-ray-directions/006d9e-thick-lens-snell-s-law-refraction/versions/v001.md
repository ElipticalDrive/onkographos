# Thick Lens (Snell's Law Refraction)
## Overview
Models a thick lens using Snell's law at two spherical interfaces, accounting for lens thickness and curvature [S1, unofficial]. It differs from thin lens approximations by explicitly computing ray propagation through the lens material volume, which is necessary for accurate ray tracing in systems with significant lens thickness or high precision requirements [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Ray origin $o$, ray direction $d$ (normalized), lens radius of curvature $R$, lens thickness $d_{lens}$, lens refractive index $n_{lens}$, external refractive index $n_{ext}$.
- Output: Refracted ray direction after exiting the second interface, or the ray if no intersection occurs.

**Data Structures:**
No complex data structures are required; the method operates on scalar and vector parameters.

**Steps:**
1. **Define Interfaces:** The lens is modeled as two spherical surfaces. The first surface (front) has radius $R_1 = R$. The second surface (back) has radius $R_2 = -R$, centered at a distance $d_{lens}$ along the optical axis from the first center. The centers are oriented such that normals point towards the center of curvature.
2. **Ray-Sphere Intersection (First Surface):**
   - Compute coefficients for the quadratic equation $at^2 + bt + c = 0$:
     - $a = |d|^2$
     - $b = 2(o - c_1) \cdot d$
     - $c_{val} = |o - c_1|^2 - R^2$
     - where $c_1$ is the center of the first sphere.
   - Compute discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac_{val}$.
   - If $\Delta > 0$:
     - $t_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$
     - $t_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$
   - If $\Delta \le 0$ or $t_1 < 0$ or $t_2 < 0$ (virtual intersections), set $t_1, t_2 = \infty$ to ignore [S1, unofficial].
   - Select the smallest positive $t$ as the intersection distance $t_{in}$.
   - Calculate intersection point $p_{in} = o + t_{in}d$.
3. **Compute Normal at First Interface:**
   - The normal $n_1$ at $p_{in}$ is oriented towards the center $c_1$.
   - $n_1 = \text{normalize}(c_1 - p_{in})$ [S1, unofficial].
4. **Refract at First Interface:**
   - Apply Snell's law to compute the refracted direction $d_{refracted}$ inside the lens material using $n_{ext}$, $n_{lens}$, $d$, and $n_1$.
5. **Ray-Sphere Intersection (Second Surface):**
   - Trace the ray from $p_{in}$ with direction $d_{refracted}$ to the second sphere centered at $c_2$ with radius $R_2 = -R$.
   - Compute coefficients:
     - $a = |d_{refracted}|^2$
     - $b = 2(p_{in} - c_2) \cdot d_{refracted}$
     - $c_{val} = |p_{in} - c_2|^2 - R^2$
   - Compute discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac_{val}$.
   - If $\Delta > 0$:
     - $t_1 = \frac{-b + \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$
     - $t_2 = \frac{-b - \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$
   - If $\Delta \le 0$ or $t_1 < 0$ or $t_2 < 0$, set $t_1, t_2 = \infty$ [S1, unofficial].
   - Select the smallest positive $t$ as the exit distance $t_{out}$.
   - Calculate exit point $p_{out} = p_{in} + t_{out}d_{refracted}$.
6. **Compute Normal at Second Interface:**
   - The normal $n_2$ at $p_{out}$ is oriented towards the center $c_2$.
   - $n_2 = \text{normalize}(c_2 - p_{out})$ [S1, unofficial].
7. **Refract at Second Interface:**
   - Apply Snell's law to compute the final refracted direction $d_{final}$ exiting into the external medium using $n_{lens}$, $n_{ext}$, $d_{refracted}$, and $n_2$.

**Formulas:**
- Approximate focal length: $f_{approx} = \frac{1}{(\frac{n_{ext}}{n_{lens}} - 1) (\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2})}$, where $R_1 = R$ and $R_2 = -R$ [S1, unofficial].
- Ray-sphere intersection discriminant: $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac_{val}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $n_{lens}$: Index of refraction of the lens material (float) [S1, unofficial].
- $n_{ext}$: Index of refraction of the external medium (float) [S1, unofficial].
- $R$: Radius of curvature of the lens surfaces (float) [S1, unofficial].
- $d_{lens}$: Thickness of the lens (float) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Virtual intersections ($t < 0$) are set to $\infty$ to be ignored [S1, unofficial].
- Non-intersecting rays ($\Delta \le 0$) have $t_1$ and $t_2$ set to $\infty$ [S1, unofficial].
- Precision issues may arise if the ray is tangent to the surface ($\Delta \approx 0$).

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def refract_vector(d, n, n1, n2):
    """
    Refracts vector d at surface with normal n, going from medium n1 to n2.
    """
    cos_theta1 = -dot(n, d)
    sin_theta1_sq = 1.0 - cos_theta1**2
    sin_theta2_sq = (n1 / n2)**2 * sin_theta1_sq
    
    if sin_theta2_sq > 1.0:
        # Total internal reflection, return reflected vector or handle as needed
        return reflect_vector(d, n)
    
    cos_theta2 = math.sqrt(1.0 - sin_theta2_sq)
    return n2 / n1 * d + (n2 / n1 * cos_theta1 - cos_theta2) * n

def reflect_vector(d, n):
    """
    Reflects vector d at surface with normal n.
    """
    return d - 2 * dot(n, d) * n

def thick_lens_refraction(o, d, R, d_lens, n_lens, n_ext):
    """
    Computes the refracted ray direction after passing through a thick lens.
    """
    # Centers of the two spherical surfaces
    c1 = [0, 0, 0]
    c2 = [0, 0, d_lens]
    
    # First intersection
    a = dot(d, d)
    b = 2 * dot([o[i] - c1[i] for i in range(3)], d)
    c_val = dot([o[i] - c1[i] for i in range(3)], [o[i] - c1[i] for i in range(3)]) - R**2
    
    delta = b**2 - 4 * a * c_val
    
    if delta <= 0:
        return d # No intersection
    
    t1 = (-b + math.sqrt(delta)) / (2 * a)
    t2 = (-b - math.sqrt(delta)) / (2 * a)
    
    t_in = min(t for t in [t1, t2] if t > 0) if any(t > 0 for t in [t1, t2]) else float('inf')
    
    if t_in == float('inf'):
        return d # Virtual intersection
    
    p_in = [o[i] + t_in * d[i] for i in range(3)]
    
    # Normal at first interface (oriented towards center c1)
    n1 = normalize([c1[i] - p_in[i] for i in range(3)])
    
    # Refract into lens
    d_refracted = refract_vector(d, n1, n_ext, n_lens)
    
    # Second intersection
    a = dot(d_refracted, d_refracted)
    b = 2 * dot([p_in[i] - c2[i] for i in range(3)], d_refracted)
    c_val = dot([p_in[i] - c2[i] for i in range(3)], [p_in[i] - c2[i] for i in range(3)]) - R**2
    
    delta = b**2 - 4 * a * c_val
    
    if delta <= 0:
        return d_refracted # No intersection
    
    t1 = (-b + math.sqrt(delta)) / (2 * a)
    t2 = (-b - math.sqrt(delta)) / (2 * a)
    
    t_out = min(t for t in [t1, t2] if t > 0) if any(t > 0 for t in [t1, t2]) else float('inf')
    
    if t_out == float('inf'):
        return d_refracted # Virtual intersection
    
    p_out = [p_in[i] + t_out * d_refracted[i] for i in range(3)]
    
    # Normal at second interface (oriented towards center c2)
    n2 = normalize([c2[i] - p_out[i] for i in range(3)])
    
    # Refract out of lens
    d_final = refract_vector(d_refracted, n2, n_lens, n_ext)
    
    return d_final

def dot(v1, v2):
    return sum(v1[i] * v2[i] for i in range(len(v1)))

def normalize(v):
    mag = math.sqrt(dot(v, v))
    if mag == 0:
        return v
    return [v[i] / mag for i in range(len(v))]
```

### [Porcine Eye Model Construction](21386-porcine-eye-model-construction.md)
The Porcine Eye Model Construction method defines the precise geometric and optical parameters of an ex vivo porcine eye to enable accurate ray tracing for distortion correction in Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) systems. It differs from human eye models by accounting for specific differences in corneal curvature, lens thickness, and gradient refractive index properties inherent to porcine tissue. This model serves as the physical substrate for the Thick Lens (Snell's Law Refraction) method, providing the surface curvatures, thicknesses, and refractive indices required to compute ray paths through the eye's optical components.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Constant time per ray, involving two ray-sphere intersections and two refraction calculations.
- **Memory Complexity:** Constant space, requiring only a few scalar and vector variables.
- **Strengths:** Accurate modeling of thick lenses; handles arbitrary ray angles and positions relative to the lens axis.
- **Limitations:** Computationally more expensive than thin lens approximations; assumes spherical surfaces and homogeneous lens material.
- **When to use:** When high accuracy is required for ray tracing through lenses with significant thickness or when the thin lens approximation is insufficient.
- **When not to use:** For simple optical systems where thin lens approximations are adequate, or for real-time applications with strict performance constraints where the added accuracy is not justified.
## Background
The method is based on the fundamental principles of geometric optics, specifically Snell's law of refraction. It is commonly used in optical design and ray tracing simulations to model the behavior of light passing through thick lenses. The specific implementation details, such as the handling of virtual intersections and the approximation of focal length, are derived from practical considerations in computational optics [S1, unofficial].
