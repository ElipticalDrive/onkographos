# Gouraud Shading

Also called: intensity interpolation shading, smooth shading

## Overview
Gouraud shading is a surface shading technique that computes illumination colors at mesh vertices and linearly interpolates these colors across the resulting primitives to approximate smooth lighting [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by interpolating the final color value rather than the geometric normal vector, as done in [Phong Shading with SDF Normals](1977-phong-shading-with-sdf-normals.md).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A mesh defined by vertices $\mathbf{v}_i$, vertex normals $\mathbf{n}_i$, and a lighting model (typically diffuse) with light direction $\mathbf{l}$ and surface albedo $k_d$.
*   **Output:** A rasterized image with per-pixel colors.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Vertex Attributes:** Each vertex stores a position and a normal vector $\mathbf{n}$.
*   **Interpolation:** Linear interpolation of scalar color values across triangle edges and interior.

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Vertex Normals:** For curved surfaces, calculate the normal for each vertex by averaging the normals of all adjacent faces sharing that vertex [S2, unofficial]. For planar surfaces, the same normal is applied to all defining vertices [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Calculate Vertex Colors:** For each vertex $i$, compute the color $C_i$ using the lighting model. The intensity depends on the angle between the light source direction and the vertex normal vector [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Interpolate Colors:** During rasterization, linearly interpolate the computed vertex colors $C_i$ across the surface of the primitive to determine the color of each pixel [S1].

**Formulas:**
The color at vertex $i$ is calculated as:
$$ C_i = k_d (\mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{l}) $$
Where:
*   $k_d$ is the diffuse reflection coefficient (albedo).
*   $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the normalized vertex normal.
*   $\mathbf{l}$ is the normalized light direction vector.
*   The dot product $\mathbf{n}_i \cdot \mathbf{l}$ represents the cosine of the angle between the normal and the light, determining the fraction of light reflected [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Normal Vector:** A vertex attribute pointing perpendicular to the surface, used to estimate light reflection [S2, unofficial].
*   **Shading Model Flag:** In OpenGL, `glShadeModel(GL_SMOOTH)` enables this method [S2, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Faceted Appearance:** If normals are not averaged for curved surfaces, the discontinuity in slope between faces results in a faceted or gem-like appearance [S2, unofficial].
*   **Missing Highlights:** Because color is interpolated rather than normal, specular highlights that fall within the interior of a triangle may be missed or appear distorted compared to normal-interpolation methods [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Complexity:** Computation is performed per-vertex, making it faster than per-pixel shading methods like Phong shading.
*   **Strengths:** Efficient for real-time rendering; provides a smoother appearance than flat shading [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Cannot accurately represent specular highlights that do not coincide with vertices; color interpolation can introduce brightness artifacts (e.g., bright spots in the middle of dark triangles) [S1].

## Background
Gouraud shading, also known as intensity interpolation shading, was introduced as an approach to render approximate surfaces with smoother color variations than flat shading [S1]. It is implemented in systems such as Smokeview for visualizing fire dynamics simulation data, where it is referred to as smooth shading [S2, unofficial].
