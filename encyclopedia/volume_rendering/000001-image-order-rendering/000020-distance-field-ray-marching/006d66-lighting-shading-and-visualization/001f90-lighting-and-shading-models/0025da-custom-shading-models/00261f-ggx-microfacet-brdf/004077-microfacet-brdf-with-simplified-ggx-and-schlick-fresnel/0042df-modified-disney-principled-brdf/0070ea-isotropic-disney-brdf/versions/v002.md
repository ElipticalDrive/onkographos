# Isotropic Disney BRDF

Also called: Cook-Torrance based Disney BRDF, Disney BRDF Material Model, Simplified Disney BRDF, Unreal Engine 4 BRDF

## Overview
The Isotropic Disney BRDF is a simplified material model based on the Cook–Torrance microfacet framework, designed to model reflectance for both metals and plastics in a unified framework [S2, unofficial]. It is a restricted subset of the full Disney Principled BRDF, optimized for differentiable rendering and optimization tasks where ambiguity from high-dimensional parameter spaces must be reduced [S3]. Unlike the full model, it restricts optimization to opaque surfaces with spatially varying diffuse albedo and global constants for roughness, specular albedo, and metalness [S2, unofficial][S3].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Surface position $\mathbf{x}$, incoming light direction $\mathbf{l}$ (or $\omega_i$), outgoing view direction $\mathbf{v}$ (or $\omega_o$), surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ [S4].
*   **Parameters:**
    *   $c_{\text{base}} \in [0, 1]^3$: Base color / diffuse albedo, spatially varying $a(\mathbf{x})$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
    *   $s \in [0, 1]$: Specular albedo, global constant [S2, unofficial].
    *   $r \in [0, 1]$: Surface roughness (denoted $\beta$ in some sources), global constant [S2, unofficial][S4].
    *   $m \in [0, 1]$: Metalness, global constant [S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** Reflected radiance $L_r(\mathbf{v})$ [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The diffuse albedo $c_{\text{base}}$ is stored as a 3-channel texture or array indexed by $\mathbf{x}$ [S2, unofficial]. The parameters $s$, $r$, and $m$ are scalar constants or global uniforms [S2, unofficial][S4].

**Formulas:**
The BRDF $f_r(\mathbf{l}, \mathbf{v})$ combines a diffuse term and a specular term [S4]:
$$ f_r(\mathbf{l}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{c_{\text{diffuse}}}{\pi} + \frac{D F G}{4(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v})} $$
Where:
$$ c_{\text{diffuse}} = (1 - m) c_{\text{base}} $$
$$ c_{\text{specular}} = (1 - m) 0.08 s + m c_{\text{base}} $$
$$ \alpha = r^2 $$
$$ k = \frac{r + 1}{8} $$
$$ \mathbf{h} = \frac{\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{v}\|} $$
$$ D = \frac{\alpha^2}{\pi \left( (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{h})^2 (\alpha^2 - 1) + 1 \right)^2} $$
$$ G = \left( (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})(1 - k) + k \right) \left( (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v})(1 - k) + k \right) $$
$$ F = c_{\text{specular}} + (1 - c_{\text{specular}})^{2^{-5.55473(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{h}) - 6.98316}} $$

**Steps:**
1. Retrieve base color $c_{\text{base}}$ at $\mathbf{x}$ [S4].
2. Compute half-vector $\mathbf{h} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{l} + \mathbf{v})$ [S4].
3. Compute $\alpha = r^2$ and $k = (r + 1) / 8$ [S4].
4. Evaluate NDF $D$ using $\alpha$ and $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{h}$ [S4].
5. Evaluate geometry term $G$ using $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}$, $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}$, and $k$ [S4].
6. Compute $c_{\text{specular}}$ from $m$, $s$, and $c_{\text{base}}$ [S4].
7. Evaluate Fresnel term $F$ using $c_{\text{specular}}$ and $\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{h}$ [S4].
8. Compute specular BRDF: $f_{\text{spec}} = (D F G) / (4 (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l}) (\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v}))$ [S4].
9. Compute diffuse BRDF: $f_{\text{diff}} = c_{\text{base}} / \pi$ [S4].
10. Return $f_r = f_{\text{diff}} + f_{\text{spec}}$ [S4].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def isotropic_disney_brdf(x, omega_i, omega_o, n, c_base, s, r, m):
    # 1. Retrieve base color
    c_base_val = c_base.sample(x)
    
    # 2. Compute half vector
    h = omega_i + omega_o
    h_len = sqrt(dot(h, h))
    if h_len == 0: return 0.0
    h = h / h_len
    
    # 3. Precompute alpha and k
    alpha = r * r
    k = (r + 1.0) / 8.0
    
    # 4. Evaluate NDF D
    nh = dot(n, h)
    if nh <= 0: return 0.0
    denom = (nh * nh) * (alpha * alpha - 1.0) + 1.0
    D = (alpha * alpha) / (3.141592653589793 * denom * denom)
    
    # 5. Evaluate Geometry G
    nl = dot(n, omega_i)
    nv = dot(n, omega_o)
    if nl <= 0 or nv <= 0: return 0.0
    G = ((nl * (1.0 - k) + k) * (nv * (1.0 - k) + k))
    
    # 6. Compute c_specular
    c_spec = (1.0 - m) * 0.08 * s + m * c_base_val
    
    # 7. Evaluate Fresnel F
    vh = dot(omega_o, h)
    exponent = 2.0 ** (-5.55473 * vh - 6.98316)
    F = c_spec + (1.0 - c_spec) * exponent
    
    # 8. Compute specular term
    f_spec = (D * F * G) / (4.0 * nl * nv)
    
    # 9. Compute diffuse term
    f_diff = c_base_val / 3.141592653589793
    
    # 10. Blend
    return f_diff + f_spec
```

**Parameters:**
*   $c_{\text{base}}$: Base color / diffuse albedo, range $[0, 1]^3$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
*   $s$: Specular albedo, range $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $r$: Roughness (or $\beta$), range $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial][S4].
*   $m$: Metalness, range $[0, 1]$ [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   Backface or grazing angles where $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l} \le 0$ or $\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{v} \le 0$ yield zero contribution [G].
*   The denominator in $D$ approaches $1$ when $\alpha \to 0$ or $\alpha \to 1$, preventing division by zero [G].

## Subtopics

### SG Warping and Approximation for BRDF Evaluation
To evaluate the rendering equation at point $\mathbf{x}$ with normal $\mathbf{n}$ and view direction $\omega_o$, the distribution $D$ is spherically warped and $M$ (Fresnel/shadowing) is approximated by a constant at $\mathbf{x}$ [S1]. The warped distribution is $D_x(\mathbf{h}) = G(\mathbf{h}; \mathbf{n}, \frac{\lambda}{4\mathbf{h} \cdot \omega_o}, \mu)$, and the constant approximation is $M_x(\omega_o, \omega_i) \approx M(\omega_o, 2(\omega_o \cdot \mathbf{n})\mathbf{n} - \omega_i)$ [S1]. The specular BRDF becomes $f_s(\omega_o, \omega_i; \mathbf{x}) = G(\mathbf{h}; \mathbf{n}, \frac{\lambda}{4\mathbf{h} \cdot \omega_o}, M_x \mu)$, combining the warped distribution and constant $M$ into a single SG representation [S1].

## Operating characteristics
Evaluation is a direct formula computation per sample [S4]. The model is used in Monte Carlo shading, requiring $N$ importance-sampled light directions to compute the final pixel color as an average [S2, unofficial]. Strengths include a unified framework for metals and plastics, intuitive parameters, and reduced optimization ambiguity by restricting to opaque surfaces [S2, unofficial][S3]. Limitations include the simplified nature compared to the full Disney Principled BRDF (e.g., missing clearcoat/sheen) and the isotropic specular assumption [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when optimizing material parameters for opaque surfaces in differentiable rendering pipelines [S3].

## Background
The model originates from the Disney Principled BRDF, a widely adopted material model in movies and games that captures versatile appearances with intuitive controls [S3]. The simplified isotropic version was adapted for differentiable rendering contexts, such as DIB-R++ and texture-space differentiable rendering, to facilitate learning and optimization of lighting and material parameters while restricting ambiguity [S2, unofficial][S3].
