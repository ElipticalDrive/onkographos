# Spherical Harmonics Degree Selection

Also called: SH basis selection

## Overview
Spherical Harmonics Degree Selection determines the bandwidth $L$ of the SH basis used to represent directional signals (such as radiance or color) in volume rendering. It solves the trade-off between representation accuracy and computational cost by selecting the minimum degree sufficient to capture the signal's frequency content. It differs from [Spherical Harmonics Approximation](8741-spherical-harmonics-approximation.md) in that it does not compute the coefficients themselves, but rather defines the dimensionality $n = (L+1)^2$ of the coefficient vector $\mathbf{f}$ used by the approximation or storage methods.

## How it works
The method selects an integer bandwidth $L \ge 0$. The selection dictates the number of basis functions and coefficients required.

**Inputs:**
*   Signal characteristics (frequency content, sharpness).
*   Resource constraints (memory budget, compute latency).
*   Target quality metric (e.g., PSNR threshold).

**Outputs:**
*   Integer bandwidth $L$.
*   Total coefficient count $n = (L+1)^2$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Bandwidth ($L$):** Integer.
*   **Coefficient Vector ($\mathbf{f}$):** Array of size $n$. The linear index $i$ maps degree $l$ and order $m$ via $i = l^2 + l + m$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Analyze Signal Frequency:** Determine the highest spatial frequency present in the directional signal. Higher frequencies require higher $L$ to avoid aliasing and ringing artifacts .
2.  **Evaluate Trade-offs:**
    *   **Memory:** Memory usage scales with $n = (L+1)^2$. Higher $L$ significantly increases memory footprint [S1].
    *   **Accuracy:** PSNR generally increases with $L$, but diminishing returns occur. For example, in DVGO density fields on a $256^3$ grid, PSNR peaks at Degree 3 (28.29) and slightly drops at Degree 4 (27.89), while on a $512^3$ grid, it continues to rise from 28.30 (Degree 3) to 29.98 (Degree 4) [S1].
    *   **Compute:** Computational cost for evaluation is not obviously affected by $L$ in some implementations, but storage and transfer matrix operations scale with $n^2$ or $n^3$ [S1].
3.  **Select $L$:** Choose the smallest $L$ that meets the quality requirement. Typical implementations use $L=3$ or $L=4$ (8-25 coefficients) to balance compactness and low-frequency approximation quality . Switching between $L=3$ (SH-16) and $L=4$ (SH-25) may yield negligible visual differences in some contexts [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **$L$ (Bandwidth):** Integer. Common values: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4.
*   **$n$ (Basis Count):** $(L+1)^2$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Ringing:** Using too high a degree for a smooth signal introduces high-frequency noise (ringing) near sharp changes .
*   **Diminishing Returns:** Beyond a certain degree, PSNR gains may plateau or even decrease due to overfitting or numerical instability, as seen in the $256^3$ grid results where Degree 4 underperformed Degree 3 [S1].
*   **Memory Bottleneck:** The quadratic growth of $n$ makes high degrees ($L \ge 5$) impractical for large-scale volume rendering due to memory constraints [S1].

## Subtopics

### SH Degree Truncation
A preprocessing step that reduces the spherical harmonics degree of loaded splats to match a target parameter, lowering memory and compute requirements [S2, unofficial]. It checks if the target `params.sh_degree` differs from the loaded splat's `get_max_sh_degree()` and calls `truncateSHDegree(*splat, params.sh_degree)` to modify the data in-place [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Selection itself is $O(1)$ if $L$ is fixed; if determined by analysis, it depends on the signal analysis cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O((L+1)^2)$ per directional signal.
*   **Performance:** Higher $L$ increases PSNR but requires significantly more memory [S1]. Computational cost for ray casting is not obviously affected by $L$ in some direct volume rendering contexts, but storage and convolution operations scale with $n$ [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Simple, deterministic, provides a clear knob for quality vs. resource trade-off.
*   **Limitations:** Fixed basis cannot adapt to local signal complexity; higher degrees risk ringing artifacts .
*   **When to Use:** When signal frequency content is known or bounded, and memory is constrained.
*   **When Not to Use:** When signal has highly variable frequency content across the domain (consider adaptive methods or SGs [S1]).

## Background
Spherical Harmonics are widely used in computer graphics for representing directional lighting and radiance. The selection of degree is a fundamental parameter in SH-based rendering pipelines. The specific PSNR metrics for DVGO density fields were reported in studies on reducing shape-radiance ambiguity [S1]. The truncation method is used in modern Gaussian Splatting implementations like LichtFeld-Studio [S2, unofficial].
