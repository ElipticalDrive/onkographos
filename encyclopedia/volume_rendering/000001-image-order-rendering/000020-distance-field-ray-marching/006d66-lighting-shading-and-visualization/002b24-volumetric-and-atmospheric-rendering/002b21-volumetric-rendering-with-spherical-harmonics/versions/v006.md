# Volumetric Rendering with Spherical Harmonics

Also called: Volume Rendering with Spherical Harmonics (Forward)

## Overview
Volumetric Rendering with Spherical Harmonics is a direct volume ray casting method that traces rays through a sparse grid represented by spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients, computing color output and optionally log-transmittance for background compositing [S1, unofficial]. It encodes directional color variation into SH coefficients evaluated per sample, allowing view-dependent shading via algebraic projections during compositing [S1, S2, unofficial]. It differs from standard direct volume rendering by replacing scalar transfer function lookups with continuous SH basis evaluation, and from [Neural volume rendering](1576-neural-volume-rendering.md) by using explicit SH basis functions rather than implicit neural fields [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- **Input:** A sparse voxel grid where each active voxel stores a scalar density/opacity proxy and a vector of spherical harmonic coefficients [S1, S2, unofficial]. Camera parameters (origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$) and ray bounds $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Output:** Final pixel color $\mathbf{C}_{out}$ and optionally accumulated log-transmittance for background compositing [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- **Voxel Grid:** A sparse 3D grid. Each active voxel contains:
    - `density`: Scalar value representing extinction coefficient or opacity proxy [S2, unofficial].
    - `sh_coeffs`: Array of $K$ floats, where $K$ depends on the SH degree $L$ ($K = (L+1)^2$) [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **Ray Data:** For $Q$ rays, arrays of origin $\mathbf{o}_i$, direction $\mathbf{d}_i$, and sample positions [S2, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Initialization:** For each pixel/ray $i$, define the ray segment $[t_{near}, t_{far}]$ by intersecting the ray with the volume bounding box [G]. Discretize the segment into $N$ samples (default $N=512$ or $100$) at positions $t_j = t_{near} + j \cdot \Delta t$, where $\Delta t = (t_{far} - t_{near}) / N$ [S2, unofficial]. Sample positions are often parameterized in $[0.05, 0.95]$ to avoid boundary artifacts [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Voxel Interpolation:** At each sample position $\mathbf{p}_j$, perform trilinear interpolation on the voxel grid to retrieve the local density/opacity proxy $\sigma_j$ and the SH coefficient vector $\mathbf{c}_j$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    - Extract opacity proxy: $\sigma_j = \text{interp}(\text{density})$ [S2, unofficial].
    - Extract SH coefficients: $\mathbf{c}_j = \text{interp}(\text{sh\_coeffs})$ [S2, unofficial].
3.  **SH Basis Evaluation:** Compute the SH basis functions $\mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{d}_i)$ for the viewing direction $\mathbf{d}_i$ of the ray [S2, unofficial]. The basis evaluation depends on the chosen degree $L$ (typically 1 or 2) [S3, unofficial].
    - Let $\mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{Y}(\mathbf{d}_i)$ be the vector of basis values [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Color Computation:** Compute the view-dependent color $\mathbf{C}_j$ at sample $j$ by taking the dot product of the interpolated SH coefficients and the basis vector [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}_j = \mathbf{c}_j \cdot \mathbf{y}_i
    $$
    Note: In some implementations, the SH coefficients are reshaped to separate color channels before projection [S4, unofficial].
5.  **Transmittance and Opacity Calculation:** Compute the optical depth and transmittance for each sample [S2, unofficial].
    - Compute step sizes: $\Delta t_j = t_{j+1} - t_j$ (for $j=0 \dots N-2$) [S2, unofficial].
    - Compute weighted optical depth deltas: $\delta_j = -\Delta t_j \cdot \sigma_j$ [S2, unofficial].
    - Compute cumulative transmittance $T_j$ from front to back:
        $$
        T_0 = 1
        $$
        $$
        T_{j+1} = \exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j} \delta_k\right)
        $$
    - Compute per-sample opacity contribution: $\alpha_j = 1 - \exp(\delta_j)$ [S2, unofficial].
6.  **Front-to-Back Compositing:** Composite the samples into the final color [S2, unofficial]:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}_{out} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} T_j \cdot \alpha_j \cdot \mathbf{C}_j
    $$
    $$
    \alpha_{out} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} T_j \cdot \alpha_j
    $$
    Or in iterative form:
    $$
    \mathbf{C}_{acc} \leftarrow \mathbf{C}_{acc} + T_j \cdot \mathbf{C}_j \cdot \alpha_j
    $$
    $$
    T_{j+1} \leftarrow T_j \cdot (1 - \alpha_j)
    $$
7.  **Background Compositing (Optional):** If background rendering is enabled, compute the log-transmittance for the ray and composite the background color using the accumulated transmittance [S1, unofficial].
    - Log-transmittance: $\log T_{final} = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \delta_j$ [S1, unofficial].
    - Composite with background color $\mathbf{C}_{bg}$:
        $$
        \mathbf{C}_{final} = \mathbf{C}_{out} + T_{final} \cdot \mathbf{C}_{bg}
        $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def spherical_harmonics_basis(direction, degree):
    """
    Computes the spherical harmonic basis vector for a given direction.
    direction: (3,) array, normalized viewing direction.
    degree: int, SH degree (0, 1, or 2).
    Returns: (K,) array of basis values, where K = (degree + 1)**2.
    """
    x, y, z = direction
    basis = []
    
    # Degree 0
    basis.append(0.28209479177387814) # 1 / sqrt(4*pi)
    
    if degree >= 1:
        # Degree 1
        basis.append(-0.4886025119029199 * y)
        basis.append(0.4886025119029199 * z)
        basis.append(-0.4886025119029199 * x)
        
    if degree >= 2:
        # Degree 2
        basis.append(1.0925484305920792 * x * y)
        basis.append(-1.0925484305920792 * y * z)
        basis.append(0.31539156525252005 * (3 * z**2 - 1))
        basis.append(-1.0925484305920792 * x * z)
        basis.append(0.5462742152960396 * (x**2 - y**2))
        
    return np.array(basis)

def render_volumetric_sh(voxel_grid, ray_origins, ray_directions, 
                         t_near, t_far, num_samples=512, sh_degree=1, 
                         background_enabled=False, background_colors=None):
    """
    Renders a volume using spherical harmonics for view-dependent color.
    
    Args:
        voxel_grid: Sparse grid object supporting trilinear interpolation.
                    Must support query(pos) -> (density, sh_coeffs).
        ray_origins: (Q, 3) array of ray origins.
        ray_directions: (Q, 3) array of ray directions (normalized).
        t_near: (Q,) array of ray entry parameters.
        t_far: (Q,) array of ray exit parameters.
        num_samples: int, number of samples per ray.
        sh_degree: int, degree of spherical harmonics (0, 1, 2).
        background_enabled: bool, whether to composite with background.
        background_colors: (Q, 3) array of background colors.
        
    Returns:
        colors: (Q, 3) array of final pixel colors.
        transmittances: (Q,) array of final transmittance (if background enabled).
    """
    Q = ray_origins.shape[0]
    K = (sh_degree + 1)**2
    
    # Precompute SH basis for all ray directions
    # Shape: (Q, K)
    ray_sh_bases = np.array([spherical_harmonics_basis(ray_directions[i], sh_degree) for i in range(Q)])
        
    colors = np.zeros((Q, 3))
    transmittances = np.ones(Q)
    
    for i in range(Q):
        # Sample positions along the ray
        t_samples = np.linspace(t_near[i], t_far[i], num_samples + 1)
        # Sample points in world space
        sample_points = ray_origins[i] + t_samples[:, None] * ray_directions[i]
        
        # Interpolate density and SH coefficients at sample points
        # Note: In a real implementation, this is done in bulk or via GPU kernel
        # Here we simulate the interpolation result
        # interp_data shape: (num_samples + 1, 1 + K)
        # Column 0: density/opacity proxy, Columns 1:K+1: sh_coeffs
        interp_data = voxel_grid.interpolate(sample_points)
        
        interp_opacities = interp_data[:, 0]
        interp_sh_coeffs = interp_data[:, 1:]
        
        # Compute SH basis for the ray direction (constant for all samples on the ray)
        ray_basis = ray_sh_bases[i]
        
        # Compute color at each sample
        # Color = dot(sh_coeffs, ray_basis)
        # sh_coeffs shape: (num_samples + 1, K)
        # ray_basis shape: (K,)
        sample_colors = np.dot(interp_sh_coeffs, ray_basis) # Shape: (num_samples + 1, 3)
        
        # Compute optical depth deltas
        deltas = np.diff(t_samples)
        
        # Weighted deltas for transmittance (matches S2: deltas_times_sigmas = - deltas * interp_opacities[:-1])
        deltas_times_sigmas = -deltas * interp_opacities[:-1]
        
        # Cumulative transmittance (matches S2: cumsum prepended with 0)
        cum_weighted_deltas = np.concatenate(([0], np.cumsum(deltas_times_sigmas)))
        T = np.exp(cum_weighted_deltas)
        
        # Per-sample opacity (matches S2: tmp2 = 1 - exp(deltas_times_sigmas))
        alpha = 1 - np.exp(deltas_times_sigmas)
        
        # Front-to-back compositing
        # C_out = sum(T_j * alpha_j * C_j)
        # T_j here is transmittance BEFORE sample j
        # alpha_j is opacity of sample j
        
        contribution = T[:-1] * alpha * sample_colors[:-1]
        colors[i] = np.sum(contribution, axis=0)
        transmittances[i] = T[-1] # Transmittance after last sample
        
    if background_enabled and background_colors is not None:
        colors = colors + transmittances[:, None] * background_colors
        
    return colors, transmittances
```

**Parameters:**
- `num_samples`: Default 512 or 100 [S2, unofficial].
- `sh_degree`: Typically 1 or 2 [S3, unofficial].
- `cuda_n_threads`: Number of CUDA threads per block for ray tracing (macro `TRACE_RAY_CUDA_THREADS`) [S1, unofficial].
- `TRACE_RAY_BG_CUDA_THREADS`: Number of CUDA threads per block for background rendering [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Boundary Artifacts:** Sampling at exact boundaries can cause interpolation errors. Using parameterized ranges like $[0.05, 0.95]$ helps [S2, unofficial].
- **SH Degree Limits:** Higher SH degrees increase memory usage and reduce the maximum number of splats/voxels that can be rendered due to memory constraints (e.g., Degree 2 limits splat count to ~8,000,000 vs ~16,000,000 for Degree 0) [S3, unofficial].
- **Precision:** Floating-point precision in cumulative transmittance calculation can lead to artifacts in deep volumes. Log-transmittance is computed for background compositing [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Spherical Harmonics](5603-spherical-harmonics.md)
Spherical Harmonics (SH) provide an orthonormal basis for representing directional functions defined on the unit sphere $S^2$, enabling the compact storage and efficient manipulation of volumetric radiance distributions. This family of methods solves the problem of expensive numerical integration for directional signals (such as in-scattering or visibility) by projecting them into a coefficient space where operations like rotation and integration become analytic or linear algebraic. It differs from its siblings, such as [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md) and [Spherical Harmonic Volume Shading](5946-spherical-harmonic-volume-shading.md), by serving as the fundamental mathematical representation layer; while the siblings apply this representation to specific shading or transfer pipelines, the core SH family focuses on the basis definition, coefficient projection, and the algebraic properties (like the product convolution) that make these applications feasible.

### [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md)
Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer (VSH-PRT) is a technique for rendering semi-transparent volumetric datasets with dynamic lighting by precomputing the scene's response to illumination. It solves the problem of expensive real-time volume rendering by compressing the 6D Volume Radiance Transfer function into spherical harmonic coefficients stored on a surface mesh, allowing for fast runtime evaluation under arbitrary distant lighting. It sets itself apart from direct volume rendering by decoupling the expensive volume integration from the render loop, and from standard surface PRT by explicitly handling the volumetric scattering process to determine the exit radiance.

### [Spherical Harmonic Volume Shading](5946-spherical-harmonic-volume-shading.md)
Spherical Harmonic Volume Shading is a method for computing diffuse illumination in Fourier Volume Rendering (FVR) by approximating the non-linear shading operator (specifically the $\max(N \cdot L, 0)$ term) using spherical harmonics. It solves the problem of interactive relighting in the frequency domain, where traditional methods require expensive recomputation of 3D Fourier transforms for every change in light direction. Unlike [Volumetric Spherical Harmonics Precomputed Radiance Transfer](5807-volumetric-spherical-harmonics-precomputed-radiance-transfer.md), which precomputes transfer functions for specific geometries, this method operates directly on the volume's gradient-derived normals in the Fourier domain, allowing light direction changes to be handled via simple coefficient updates without re-transforming the volume data.

### [Ray Tracing Backward Pass](5966-ray-tracing-backward-pass.md)
The Ray Tracing Backward Pass computes the gradients of a differentiable volume rendering loss with respect to the scene representation (spherical harmonic coefficients and density) and auxiliary parameters, given the rendered image and ground truth. It serves as the differentiable counterpart to the forward ray casting pass, enabling end-to-end training of volumetric models like Plenoxels. Unlike the forward pass which accumulates color and transmittance, this method propagates error signals backward through the ray integration, handling RGB normalization and optional regularization losses (beta, sparsity).

### [Zonal Method (Bhate and Tokuta)](12221-zonal-method-bhate-and-tokuta.md)
The Zonal Method (Bhate and Tokuta) is a global illumination technique for participating media that extends the classical zonal method by incorporating spherical harmonics to handle anisotropic scattering and surface-volume interactions. Unlike the standard zonal method which assumes isotropic media and ignores surface effects, this method represents the phase function and radiance using spherical harmonics, allowing for the approximation of Mie scattering while maintaining ideal diffuse surfaces. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific use of an extended hemicube technique to compute spherical harmonic form factors and a Gauss-Seidel solver for the coupled volume-surface system.

### Spherical Harmonics Rendering
Renders RGB values by projecting spherical harmonics coefficients along viewing directions, then compositing with weights [S4, unofficial]. Reshapes SH coefficients to separate color channels, computes SH basis components for given directions, multiplies coefficients by components, sums over SH components and adds bias, applies optional activation function to RGB, and composites using RGBRenderer.combine_rgb [S4, unofficial]. The activation function defaults to `nn.Sigmoid()` [S4, unofficial]. A known pitfall in some source implementations is that the code overwrites the RGB tensor in the sum step (`rgb = torch.sum(sh, dim=-1) + 0.5`), discarding the channel multiplication result [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(Q \cdot N \cdot K)$, where $Q$ is the number of rays, $N$ is the number of samples per ray, and $K$ is the number of SH coefficients ($K=(L+1)^2$) [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(V \cdot K)$, where $V$ is the number of active voxels and $K$ is the number of SH coefficients per voxel [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **Measured Performance:** Limits on splat count depend on SH degree: Degree 0 allows ~16,000,000 splats, Degree 1 allows ~11,000,000, and Degree 2 allows ~8,000,000 [S3, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Efficient view-dependent shading without per-sample transfer function lookups; compact representation of directional data [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Memory intensive for high SH degrees; fixed basis may not capture complex directional effects as well as learned bases [S1, S3, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** When view-dependent color effects are important and computational resources allow for the memory overhead of SH coefficients [S3, unofficial].
- **When Not to Use:** When memory is constrained or view-dependent effects are minimal (use Degree 0 or standard scalar transfer functions) [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from CUDA-based volume rendering implementations that use sparse grids represented by spherical harmonic coefficients [S1, unofficial]. It is part of the broader family of spherical harmonic methods for directional data representation [S1, S3, unofficial]. The approach is used in neural volume rendering contexts and real-time rendering applications where view-dependent effects are desired [S1, S3, unofficial].
