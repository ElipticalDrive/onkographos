# Shadow Occlusion via Occlusion Camera

Also called: Light-space shadow maps, Volumetric shadow maps

## Overview
This method uses a dedicated child camera (the "Occlusion Camera") to render a depth map from the light's perspective, allowing scene geometry to block volumetric light beams [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard URP shadows which depend on the light's Shadow Type and global URP shadow settings, this occlusion map operates independently, enabling volumetric shadows even when the light casts no regular geometric shadows [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Volume Ray Casting with Marching](14353-volume-ray-casting-with-marching.md).

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry (filtered by Culling Mask), light parameters (type, position, orientation), and the Occlusion Camera configuration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A depth texture (Occlusion Map) and the final volumetric lighting contribution where shadow density is applied [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures**
*   **Occlusion Map:** A depth texture generated by the Occlusion Camera. Resolution is configurable from 32 to 4096, with a default of 256 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Shadow Coordinates:** Computed at the ray's entry and exit points from the volume, then interpolated along the ray for each marching step [S1, unofficial].

**Steps**
1.  **Render Occlusion Map:** The system adds a lightweight depth-only renderer to the URP Asset's renderer list if not already present [S1, unofficial]. The Occlusion Camera renders a depth-only pass of the scene, respecting the Culling Mask and Near Clip Distance [S1, unofficial].
    *   For **Spot Lights**: The capture projection uses a perspective frustum matching the spot angle [S1, unofficial].
    *   For **Rect/Disc Area Lights**: The capture projection is orthographic [S1, unofficial].
    *   For **Point Lights**: A specific Bake Mode is required:
        *   *Half Sphere*: Captures a 160-degree view in one render, oriented either toward the camera or a fixed direction [S1, unofficial].
        *   *Cubemap One Face Per Frame*: Captures one face of a cubemap per frame [S1, unofficial].
        *   *Cubemap All 6 Faces Per Frame*: Captures all six faces per frame (slowest) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Shadow Coordinates:** In the ray marching shader, shadow coordinates are calculated at the ray's entry point into the volume and at its exit point [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Interpolate:** Shadow coordinates are interpolated between the entry and exit points along the ray for each marching step [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Sample Occlusion:** The shader samples the Occlusion Map texture at the interpolated coordinates to determine shadow density [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Apply Shadow:** The volumetric beam intensity is blended toward the configured Shadow Color (Intensity and Color parameter) based on the sampled density, rather than blending to pure black [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters**
*   **Resolution:** 32 to 4096. Default 256. Higher values are needed for fine patterns like window bars, grilles, or foliage [S1, unofficial].
*   **Intensity and Color:** Defines the color the shadowed part of the beam blends toward [S1, unofficial].
*   **Culling Mask:** Layers that occlude the beam. The TransparentFX layer is excluded by default [S1, unofficial].
*   **Include Transparent:** If enabled, transparent geometry and 2D sprites block light, provided their material writes depth via a DepthOnly pass [S1, unofficial].
*   **Near Clip Distance:** The near plane of the Occlusion Camera. Used when the light is inside a model (e.g., a lamp) [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
*   **Point Light Performance:** Cubemap modes are significantly slower than Half Sphere. All 6 Faces Per Frame is the slowest option [S1, unofficial].
*   **Thin Occluders:** Raymarch Quality controls sampling fineness; thin occluders may appear blurry on lower presets [S1, unofficial].
*   **Transparent Geometry:** Transparent objects only occlude if they explicitly write depth [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Depends on the Occlusion Camera's resolution and the Point Light Bake Mode. Half Sphere is the fastest; Cubemap All 6 Faces Per Frame is the slowest [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Independent of standard shadow settings; allows volumetric shadows on lights that do not cast geometric shadows [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a depth-writing pass from transparent objects for full occlusion; point lights have high performance costs for full coverage [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use/not:** Use when volumetric beams need to be blocked by geometry without relying on standard URP shadow settings. Avoid when point light performance is critical and full cubemap coverage is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This technique is implemented in Unity's Universal Render Pipeline (URP) via third-party volumetric lighting assets (e.g., Kronnect) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the visual artifact where volumetric beams pass through walls and props, creating "glowing cone mesh" appearances without occlusion [S1, unofficial].
