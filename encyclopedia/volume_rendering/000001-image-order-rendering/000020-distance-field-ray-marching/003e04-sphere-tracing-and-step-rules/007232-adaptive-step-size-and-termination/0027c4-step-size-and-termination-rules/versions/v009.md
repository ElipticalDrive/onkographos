# Step Size and Termination Rules

Also called: Ray Marching Parameters and Compositing

## Overview
Step Size and Termination Rules define the iterative logic for advancing a ray through a volume or implicit surface, determining when to stop marching and how far to move per iteration. This family solves the problem of balancing rendering accuracy against computational cost, ensuring that rays do not overshoot surfaces, miss thin details, or loop infinitely. It sets itself apart from [Sphere Tracing](1674-sphere-tracing.md) by encompassing a broader range of strategies beyond the strict Lipschitz-safe leap, including constant steps, optimized heuristics, and early termination based on opacity, while differing from [Standard Constant Step Ray Marching](1080-standard-constant-step-ray-marching.md) by allowing dynamic adjustment of step sizes based on scene context.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and normalized direction $\mathbf{d}$, parameterized by distance $t$. The core loop evaluates a scene function (SDF, density field, or volume texture) at the current position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ to determine the next step size $\Delta t$ and check for termination.

**Termination Conditions:**
1.  **Surface Hit:** The ray has intersected the target geometry. For SDFs, this is typically $|f(\mathbf{p})| < \epsilon$ or $f(\mathbf{p}) \le 0$ . For volumes, this may be a density threshold or opacity accumulation [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Escape/Miss:** The ray has traveled beyond a maximum distance $t_{max}$ or exceeded a maximum iteration count $N_{max}$ .
3.  **Early Termination (Volumes):** Accumulated opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., $1 - \epsilon$), or accumulated transmission falls below a minimum threshold, allowing the ray to stop early as further contributions are negligible [S1, S5, unofficial].

**Step Size Determination:**
The step size $\Delta t$ is computed based on the local scene properties:
*   **SDF-based:** $\Delta t$ is often derived from the SDF value $f(\mathbf{p})$, potentially scaled by a relaxation factor or Lipschitz constant $\lambda$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Volume-based:** $\Delta t$ may be fixed, adaptive based on density gradients, or constrained by minimum/maximum bounds to prevent aliasing or excessive computation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Safety:** Steps must be chosen to ensure the ray does not penetrate the surface or skip over significant volumetric features, often requiring conservative clamping [S4, unofficial].

**Pseudocode Structure:**
```python
def ray_march_loop(ray_origin, ray_direction, scene_func, max_dist, max_steps, epsilon):
    t = 0.0
    for i in range(max_steps):
        if t > max_dist:
            return False, t
        
        p = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        val = scene_func(p)
        
        # Termination: Hit
        if is_hit(val, epsilon):
            return True, t
        
        # Termination: Early Exit (Volume specific)
        if is_early_exit(val, t):
            return False, t # Or handle partial contribution
            
        # Step Size Calculation
        step = compute_step_size(val, t, i)
        
        # Safety Clamp
        step = max(step, min_step)
        step = min(step, max_step)
        
        t += step
        
    return False, t
```

## Subtopics

### [Standard Constant Step Ray Marching](1080-standard-constant-step-ray-marching.md)
Uses a predefined constant step size $\Delta t$ for all iterations. It simplifies implementation but requires careful tuning to avoid aliasing or missed details, as it does not adapt to empty space or local geometry.

### [Sphere Tracing](1674-sphere-tracing.md)
Adapts the step size to the local SDF value ($\Delta t = f(\mathbf{p})$), ensuring convergence without overshooting provided the function satisfies the Lipschitz condition. It is distinguished from constant step methods by its dynamic, geometry-aware stepping.

### [Step Size Optimization](2054-step-size-optimization.md)
Dynamically varies $\Delta t$ per sample using heuristic, error-based, or geometric bounds to balance accuracy and cost. It relaxes the strict Lipschitz safety constraint of sphere tracing in favor of larger steps in safe regions [G].

### [Sphere Tracing Termination and Clipping](2635-sphere-tracing-termination-and-clipping.md)
Explicitly handles edge cases such as oscillation detection and far-plane clipping to guarantee robustness. It distinguishes itself by strictly enforcing convergence thresholds and stability checks (e.g., oscillation tolerance) that standard sphere tracing may lack [G].

### [SDF Scaling and Early Ray Termination for Fast Sphere Tracing](3807-sdf-scaling-and-early-ray-termination-for-fast-sphere-tracin.md)
Uses a two-pass approach: a low-resolution pre-pass with SDF scaling and early termination to generate a coarse distance buffer, followed by a full-resolution pass that initializes rays closer to surfaces. It reduces step counts and memory cost compared to pyramid-based methods.

### [Relaxed Sphere Tracing](9463-relaxed-sphere-tracing.md)
Increases the step size by scaling the conservative SDF value with a relaxation factor $\omega$. It includes a safety check (disjoint sphere test) to fall back to standard steps if the relaxed step might skip an intersection, offering a speedup with guaranteed correctness.

### [Interval Raymarching](13020-interval-raymarching.md)
Evaluates the SDF over an interval using affine arithmetic to enable reliable linear interpolation (secant method). It aims for superlinear convergence ($\phi$ degree) instead of the linear convergence of standard sphere tracing, relying on conservative SDF estimates.

### Adaptive Minimum Step Size
*   **Definition:** Dynamic adjustment of the minimum step size based on distance from the camera and accumulated density to reduce samples in less important areas [S2, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Inside the cloud ($distanceField \le 0$), increase `minStepSize` by `STEP_INSCREASE_OVER_DENSITY` multiplied by the current `sampleDensity`. After advancing the ray, increase `minStepSize` by `STEP_INCREASE_OVER_DISTANCE` multiplied by the `stepSize` used [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** `STEP_INSCREASE_OVER_DENSITY` controls density-based increase; `STEP_INCREASE_OVER_DISTANCE` controls distance-based increase [S2, unofficial].
*   **Comparison:** Increases step size further from the camera and behind dense areas, unlike fixed step sizes [S2, unofficial].

### Cone Step Mapping
*   **Definition:** A space-leaping acceleration technique used in ray marching, often involving cone tracing or relaxed cone step mapping to skip empty space more aggressively than sphere tracing [S3, unofficial].

### Conservative Sphere Tracing Step Sizing
*   **Definition:** A fix for volumetric cloud rendering where sphere tracing takes overly aggressive step sizes, skipping cloud boundaries and leaving circular/scalloped density artifacts [S4, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Clamp the pre-computed 2D organic cell SDF to a conservative maximum of `30000.0 * uWorldScale`. Reduce the sphere tracing step size multiplier from `0.8` to `0.4` in the atmosphere low-res pass. Clamp the empty space skipping step size to a maximum of `2000.0 * worldScale` [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** SDF Max Clamp: `30000.0 * uWorldScale`; Step Size Multiplier: `0.4`; Step Size Max Clamp: `2000.0 * worldScale` [S4, unofficial].
*   **Pitfall:** Uncapped SDF values in empty regions cause bilinear filtering to interpolate extremely large values, and aggressive multipliers cause rays to jump past cloud surfaces [S4, unofficial].

### Early Ray Marching Termination (Threshold)
*   **Definition:** An optimization to stop ray marching when the accumulated transmission falls below a small threshold, saving computation in dense volumes [S5, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Threshold: A small value (e.g., $10^{-3}$ or 0.001) representing the minimum transmission. Below this, the contribution to the final color is considered negligible [S5, unofficial].
*   **Pitfall:** Simply stopping and ignoring the remaining volume contribution is statistically incorrect and introduces bias. It is acceptable for visual rendering but not for high-precision scientific simulation (e.g., thermonuclear engineering) [S5, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of ray marching with variable step sizes originated with the development of implicit surface rendering. Sphere tracing, introduced by John Hart, popularized the use of SDF values as safe step sizes [S3, unofficial]. Subsequent research and practical implementations in volumetric rendering (e.g., Jack Tollenaar's cloud rendering) and game development (e.g., steubengineer's imaginary volumes) expanded these rules to include adaptive stepping, early termination, and conservative clamping to handle complex scenes efficiently [S1, S2, unofficial]. The distinction between strict mathematical convergence (sphere tracing) and visual optimization (early termination, adaptive steps) has become a key area of focus in real-time rendering [S5, unofficial].
