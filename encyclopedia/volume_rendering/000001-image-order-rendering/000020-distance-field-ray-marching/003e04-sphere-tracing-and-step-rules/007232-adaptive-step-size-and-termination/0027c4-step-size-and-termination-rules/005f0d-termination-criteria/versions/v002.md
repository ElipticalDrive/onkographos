# Termination Criteria

Also called: Ray Marching Stop Conditions, Loop Break Conditions

## Overview
Termination criteria define the conditions under which a ray-marching loop halts, ensuring the algorithm returns a valid intersection point or correctly identifies a miss without infinite iteration. It solves the problem of premature termination (missing thin geometry), self-intersection artifacts (due to insufficient epsilon), and excessive computation (tracing through empty space or deep into volumes). It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Sphere Tracing](1674-sphere-tracing.md) by focusing on the general control flow logic applicable to both Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) and volumetric density fields, rather than the specific geometric step-size constraints of sphere tracing or the fixed-step simplicity of constant marching.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and normalized direction $\mathbf{d}$, parameterized by accumulated distance $t$. The core loop evaluates a scene function (SDF, density field, or volume texture) at the current position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ to determine the next step size $\Delta t$ and check for termination.

**Termination Conditions:**
1.  **Surface Hit:** The ray has intersected the target geometry. For SDFs, this is typically $|f(\mathbf{p})| < \epsilon$ or $f(\mathbf{p}) \le 0$. For volumes, this may be a density threshold or opacity accumulation [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Escape/Miss:** The ray has traveled beyond a maximum distance $t_{max}$ or exceeded a maximum iteration count $N_{max}$.
3.  **Early Termination (Volumes):** Accumulated opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., $1 - \epsilon$), or accumulated transmission falls below a minimum threshold, allowing the ray to stop early as further contributions are negligible [S1, unofficial].

**Step Size Determination:**
The step size $\Delta t$ is computed based on the local scene properties:
*   **SDF-based:** $\Delta t$ is often derived from the SDF value $f(\mathbf{p})$, potentially scaled by a relaxation factor or Lipschitz constant $\lambda$ .
*   **Volume-based:** $\Delta t$ may be fixed, adaptive based on density gradients, or constrained by minimum/maximum bounds to prevent aliasing or excessive computation .
*   **Safety:** Steps must be chosen to ensure the ray does not penetrate the surface or skip over significant volumetric features, often requiring conservative clamping.

**Pseudocode Structure:**
```python
def ray_march_loop(ray_origin, ray_direction, scene_func, max_dist, max_steps, epsilon):
    t = 0.0
    for i in range(max_steps):
        if t > max_dist:
            return False, t
        
        p = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        val = scene_func(p)
        
        # Termination: Hit
        if is_hit(val, epsilon):
            return True, t
        
        # Termination: Early Exit (Volume specific)
        if is_early_exit(val, t):
            return False, t # Or handle partial contribution
            
        # Step Size Calculation
        step = compute_step_size(val, t, i)
        
        # Safety Clamp
        step = max(step, min_step)
        step = min(step, max_step)
        
        t += step
        
    return False, t
```

### [Ray Marching with Early Termination](17724-ray-marching-with-early-termination.md)
Ray marching with early termination is a control flow strategy applied to the iterative ray marching loop to halt computation once the ray has interacted with the target scene or exceeded computational limits. It solves the problem of unnecessary processing by breaking the loop when a surface intersection is detected or when the ray has traveled beyond a defined maximum distance. Unlike methods that strictly iterate a fixed number of steps regardless of scene content, this approach dynamically adapts the loop duration based on geometric or volumetric feedback, significantly improving performance for sparse or bounded scenes. See [Ray Marching with Early Termination](17724-ray-marching-with-early-termination.md).

### Early Ray Marching Termination (Threshold)
**Definition:** An optimization to stop ray marching when the accumulated transmission falls below a small threshold, saving computation in dense volumes [S1, unofficial].
**Parameter:** Threshold: A small value (e.g., $10^{-3}$ or 0.001) representing the minimum transmission. Below this, the contribution to the final color is considered negligible [S1, unofficial].
**Pitfall:** Simply stopping and ignoring the remaining volume contribution is statistically incorrect and introduces bias. It is acceptable for visual rendering but not for high-precision scientific simulation (e.g., thermonuclear engineering) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of early termination in ray marching emerged alongside the practical implementation of volume rendering and SDF-based rendering in real-time graphics. As ray marching became a standard technique for rendering implicit surfaces and volumetric data, the computational cost of iterating through empty space or fully opaque regions became a bottleneck. The specific threshold-based early exit for volumes was discussed in community-driven educational resources, highlighting the trade-off between rendering speed and physical accuracy. The geometric termination criteria for sphere tracing, including oscillation detection and grazing resolution, were developed to address numerical instability and visual artifacts like "auras" and self-intersections in SDF rendering.
