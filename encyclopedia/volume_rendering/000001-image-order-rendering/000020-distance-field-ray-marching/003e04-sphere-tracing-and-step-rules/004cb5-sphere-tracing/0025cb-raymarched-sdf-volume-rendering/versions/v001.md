# Raymarched SDF Volume Rendering

Also called: Ray Marching with Signed Distance Fields, SDF Raycasting

## Overview
Raymarched SDF Volume Rendering is a technique to display a 2D projection of a 3D object defined by a mathematical distance function (SDF) by casting rays from the camera and stepping along them until they intersect the surface [S2, unofficial]. It creates an image from equations rather than rasterized geometry, allowing for complex, analytic shapes without explicit mesh data [S1, unofficial]. Unlike traditional Unity Raycasting, which is not available in shaders, this method implements its own ray-intersection logic using distance queries [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A quad covering the screen (positioned at $z=0.32$ or beyond the camera's near clipping plane) [S2, unofficial]. Camera parameters: origin $\mathbf{o}$, basis vectors $\mathbf{r}$ (right), $\mathbf{u}$ (up), $\mathbf{f}$ (forward), and focal length [S2, unofficial]. Scene parameters: object positions, sizes, and material IDs [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A color buffer where each pixel contains the color and alpha of the first surface hit by the ray [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Scene Map:** A function `map(p)` that returns a `vec2` (or `float2`). The x-component is the signed distance to the nearest surface. The y-component is the material ID (e.g., 0.0 for wing, 1.0 for body/antenna) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Vertex Shader Output (`v2f`):**
    *   `uv`: `float2` (TEXCOORD0), texture coordinates from input [S2, unofficial].
    *   `vertex`: `float4` (SV_POSITION), transformed vertex position [S2, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Vertex Shader:** Transform vertex position using model-view-projection matrix and pass texture coordinates to the fragment shader [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{vertex} = \text{mul}(\text{UNITY\_MATRIX\_MVP}, \text{v.vertex}) $$
    $$ \text{uv} = \text{v.texcoord} $$

2.  **Fragment Shader - Ray Setup:** Transform UV coordinates from $[0,1]$ to $[-1,1]$ [S2, unofficial]. Construct the ray direction $\hat{r}$ using camera basis vectors and a focal length $f$ [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{uv}_{\text{ndc}} = \text{uv} \times 2.0 - 1.0 $$
    $$ \hat{r} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{u} \cdot \text{uv}_{\text{ndc}.y} + \mathbf{r} \cdot \text{uv}_{\text{ndc}.x} + \mathbf{f} \cdot f) $$
    Typical focal length: $0.62$ [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Raymarching Loop:** Iterate up to a maximum number of steps $N$ (typically 64 [S2, unofficial] or 130 [S1, unofficial]). Initialize position $\mathbf{p}$ to the camera origin $\mathbf{o}$ [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{for } i = 0 \text{ to } N-1: $$

4.  **Distance Query:** Evaluate the SDF at the current position $\mathbf{p}$ to get distance $d$ [S2, unofficial].
    $$ d = \text{map}(\mathbf{p}).x $$

5.  **Hit Test:** If $d < \epsilon_{\text{hit}}$ (e.g., $0.005$), the ray has hit the surface. Compute color and break [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{if } d < 0.005: \text{color} = \text{getColor}(\mathbf{p}); \text{break} $$

6.  **Far Plane Test:** If $d > \epsilon_{\text{far}}$ (e.g., $400.0$), the ray is too far away. Break [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \text{if } d > 400.0: \text{break} $$

7.  **Step Forward:** Advance position along the ray. A safety factor (e.g., $0.5$) is often applied to prevent tunnelling, especially in non-rigid transforms [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p} \leftarrow \mathbf{p} + \hat{r} \cdot d \cdot \text{safety\_factor} $$

8.  **Color Computation:** If a hit occurred, compute the final color using lighting, fog, and alpha [S2, unofficial].

**Formulas:**

*   **Ray Equation:**
    $$ p(s) = o + s\,\hat{r} $$
    where $p(s)$ is the point on the ray at distance $s$, $o$ is the ray origin, and $\hat{r}$ is the normalized ray direction [S1, unofficial].

*   **Sphere SDF:**
    $$ d_{\text{sphere}} = \lVert \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{c} \rVert - r $$
    where $\mathbf{c}$ is the center and $r$ is the radius [S2, unofficial].

*   **Box SDF:**
    $$ d_{\text{box}} = \lVert \max(\lvert \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{c} \rvert - \mathbf{s}, 0.0) \rVert $$
    where $\mathbf{c}$ is the center and $\mathbf{s}$ is the half-extents (size) [S2, unofficial].

*   **Normal Approximation (Numerical Gradient):**
    Using epsilon $\epsilon = (0.00, 0.02)$ for x and y components respectively [S2, unofficial]:
    $$ \mathbf{n} = \text{normalize} \begin{pmatrix} d(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon_y \mathbf{e}_x) - d(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon_y \mathbf{e}_x) \\ d(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon_x \mathbf{e}_y) - d(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon_x \mathbf{e}_y) \\ d(\mathbf{p} + \epsilon_x \mathbf{e}_z) - d(\mathbf{p} - \epsilon_x \mathbf{e}_z) \end{pmatrix} $$
    *Note: The source code uses `eps.yxx` for x-gradient, `eps.xyx` for y-gradient, and `eps.xxy` for z-gradient, implying $\epsilon_x=0.00$ and $\epsilon_y=0.02$ [S2, unofficial].*

*   **Lighting:**
    $$ \mathbf{L}_{\text{diffuse}} = \mathbf{C}_{\text{light}} \cdot \max(\mathbf{l} \cdot \mathbf{n}, 0.0) $$
    $$ \mathbf{L}_{\text{ambient}} = \mathbf{C}_{\text{ambient}} \times 2.0 $$
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{\text{final}} = (\mathbf{C}_{\text{base}} \cdot (\mathbf{L}_{\text{diffuse}} + \mathbf{L}_{\text{ambient}})) \cdot \mathbf{C}_{\text{base\_color}} $$
    where $\mathbf{l}$ is the normalized light direction [S2, unofficial].

*   **Fog:**
    $$ f = e^{-d_{\text{cam}} \cdot \rho} $$
    $$ \mathbf{C}_{\text{fogged}} = \mathbf{C}_{\text{fog}} (1 - f) + \mathbf{C}_{\text{final}} f $$
    where $d_{\text{cam}} = \lVert \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{o} \rVert$, $\rho = 0.1$ [S2, unofficial].

*   **Alpha (Transparency):**
    $$ \alpha = \max(\mathbf{l} \cdot \mathbf{n}, 0.0) $$
    This makes shadows transparent [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `MARCH_STEPS`: 130 [S1, unofficial] or 64 [S2, unofficial].
*   `Focal Length`: 0.62 [S2, unofficial].
*   `Fog Density`: 0.1 [S2, unofficial].
*   `Hit Threshold`: 0.005 [S2, unofficial].
*   `Far Threshold`: 400.0 [S2, unofficial].
*   `Safety Factor`: 0.5 [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Tunnelling:** In non-rigid transforms (e.g., bending wings), distances are overstated. Rays may tunnel through thin membranes. Solution: Use conservative steps (smaller step size or more iterations) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Clipping:** The quad rendering the volume must be positioned at $z=0.32$ or beyond the camera's near clipping plane (typically 0.3) to be visible [S2, unofficial].
*   **Layer Culling:** Use a separate camera with 'depth only' clear flags for the volume layer, and ensure the main camera excludes that layer from its culling mask to ensure correct backgrounding [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def raymarch_sdf_volume_rendering(camera_origin, camera_right, camera_up, camera_forward, focal_length, scene_map, max_steps=64, hit_threshold=0.005, far_threshold=400.0, safety_factor=0.5):
    """
    Renders a scene defined by an SDF map function.
    
    Args:
        camera_origin: float3, position of the camera.
        camera_right: float3, right vector of the camera.
        camera_up: float3, up vector of the camera.
        camera_forward: float3, forward vector of the camera.
        focal_length: float, focal length for ray direction calculation.
        scene_map: function, takes a float3 position, returns a float2 (distance, material_id).
        max_steps: int, maximum number of ray marching iterations.
        hit_threshold: float, distance below which a hit is considered.
        far_threshold: float, distance above which the ray is considered too far.
        safety_factor: float, multiplier for step size to prevent tunnelling.
        
    Returns:
        float4, the final color and alpha for the pixel.
    """
    
    # 1. Ray Setup
    # Assuming uv is already transformed to [-1, 1]
    # ray_direction = normalize(camera_up * uv.y + camera_right * uv.x + camera_forward * focal_length)
    # For this self-contained example, we assume a generic ray direction is passed or computed externally.
    # Here we define the loop structure.
    
    ray_direction = normalize(camera_right + camera_up + camera_forward) # Placeholder for actual UV-based direction
    position = camera_origin
    
    for i in range(max_steps):
        # 2. Distance Query
        result = scene_map(position)
        distance = result.x
        material_id = result.y
        
        # 3. Hit Test
        if distance < hit_threshold:
            return compute_color(position, material_id, camera_origin)
        
        # 4. Far Plane Test
        if distance > far_threshold:
            return float4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0) # Transparent/Background
        
        # 5. Step Forward
        position = position + ray_direction * distance * safety_factor
        
    return float4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0) # Did not hit

def compute_color(position, material_id, camera_origin):
    """
    Computes lighting, fog, and alpha for a hit point.
    """
    # 1. Normal Approximation
    eps = float2(0.00, 0.02)
    
    # Helper to get distance from scene_map
    def get_dist(p):
        return scene_map(p).x
        
    dx = get_dist(position + float3(eps.y, 0.0, 0.0)) - get_dist(position - float3(eps.y, 0.0, 0.0))
    dy = get_dist(position + float3(0.0, eps.x, 0.0)) - get_dist(position - float3(0.0, eps.x, 0.0))
    dz = get_dist(position + float3(0.0, 0.0, eps.x)) - get_dist(position - float3(0.0, 0.0, eps.x))
    
    normal = normalize(float3(dx, dy, dz))
    
    # 2. Lighting
    light_direction = normalize(float3(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)) # Placeholder for actual light dir
    light_color = float3(1.0, 1.0, 1.0) # Placeholder for actual light color
    ambient_color = float3(0.5, 0.5, 0.5) # Placeholder for actual ambient color
    
    diffuse = light_color * max(dot(light_direction, normal), 0.0)
    ambient = ambient_color * 2.0
    final_light = diffuse + ambient
    
    base_color = float3(0.7, 0.7, 0.7) # Placeholder for material color
    color = base_color * final_light
    
    # 3. Fog
    distance_to_cam = length(position - camera_origin)
    fog_density = 0.1
    fog_color = float3(0.8, 0.8, 0.8)
    fog_factor = exp(-distance_to_cam * fog_density)
    color = fog_color * (1.0 - fog_factor) + color * fog_factor
    
    # 4. Alpha
    alpha = max(dot(light_direction, normal), 0.0)
    
    return float4(color, alpha)
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot M)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $M$ is the maximum number of ray marching steps per pixel.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel, as no geometry buffers are stored; the scene is defined by the SDF function [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Performance depends heavily on the complexity of the SDF function and the number of steps. Conservative stepping (safety factor < 1.0) increases accuracy but requires more steps or smaller steps, potentially reducing performance [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** No mesh generation required; infinite resolution; easy to create complex, analytic shapes; supports boolean operations (union, intersection, subtraction) via min/max functions [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** No direct support for transparency within the volume (only surface transparency via alpha); tunnelling artifacts in non-rigid transforms; limited to scenes that can be efficiently represented by SDFs [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Ideal for procedural geometry, analytic shapes, and scenes where mesh generation is impractical or too expensive [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Not suitable for highly detailed, organic geometry that is better represented by meshes; not suitable for scenes requiring complex volumetric effects (e.g., participating media) without significant extension [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
Raymarched SDF Volume Rendering is a technique popularized in real-time graphics for creating procedural geometry. It is distinct from traditional rasterization and mesh-based ray tracing. The method relies on the mathematical properties of Signed Distance Fields to efficiently determine ray-surface intersections. The technique is often used in demoscene productions and shader art for its ability to render complex shapes with minimal code [S1, S2, unofficial].
