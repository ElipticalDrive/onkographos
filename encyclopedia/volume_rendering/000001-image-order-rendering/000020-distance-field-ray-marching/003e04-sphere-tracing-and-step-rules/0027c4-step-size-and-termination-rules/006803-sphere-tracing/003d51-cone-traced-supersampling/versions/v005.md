# Cone-Traced Supersampling

Also called: Cone-Traced Supersampling With Subpixel Edge Reconstruction, Subpixel Edge Reconstruction (SER)

## Overview
Cone-Traced Supersampling (CTSS) is an efficient spatial antialiasing solution for Signed Distance Field (SDF) rendering that performs supersampling along the traced ray near surfaces with partial visibility [S1, unofficial]. It identifies object contours by evaluating cone intersections within a pixel's view frustum, avoiding the need for casting additional rays per pixel or offline prefiltering [S1, unofficial]. Subpixel Edge Reconstruction (SER) extends CTSS to locate and resolve complex pixels with geometric edges in relatively flat regions that are otherwise undetected by cone intersections [S1, unofficial]. Unlike conventional supersampling, CTSS minimizes shading computations, and unlike post-processing filters, it avoids undesirable blurriness and ghosting [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A ray traced through an SDF volume, the pixel's view frustum geometry, and the SDF function $S(\mathbf{p})$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A final pixel color with reduced aliasing artifacts [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray:** Defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$, parameterized as $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ [G].
*   **Cone:** Defined by the ray direction $\mathbf{d}$, the apex at the ray origin or current marching position, and an angular radius $\theta$ derived from the pixel's solid angle [G].
*   **Visibility State:** A boolean or float indicating if the ray has hit a surface or escaped [G].

**Steps:**
1.  **Cone Intersection Evaluation:** Construct a cone representing the pixel's view frustum at the current ray marching position and evaluate if it intersects the SDF surface to identify surfaces with partial visibility (object contours) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Contour Detection:** Mark the pixel as containing a contour if the cone intersects the surface; otherwise, classify it as flat or fully inside/outside [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Supersampling along the Ray:** For pixels identified as contours, perform supersampling along the traced ray near the surface intersection [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Subpixel Edge Reconstruction (SER):** Detect complex pixels with geometric edges in relatively flat regions that were not identified by the cone intersection step and resolve them [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Specialized Sampling Strategy:** Apply a sampling strategy that minimizes the number of shading computations during supersampling [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Correlation and Aggregation:** Correlate sample visibility to aggregate the samples for the final pixel color [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   Ray march: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ [G].
*   Surface hit condition: $S(\mathbf{p}) < \epsilon$ [S1, unofficial].
*   Visibility aggregation: $V_{\text{final}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} V_i$ [G].

**Parameters:**
*   `max_steps`: Maximum number of ray marching steps. Typical value: 100.
*   `epsilon`: Surface proximity threshold. Typical value: $10^{-3}$.
*   `pixel_frustum.angle_radius`: Angular radius of the pixel's view frustum. Derived from camera FOV and resolution [G].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Complex Pixels in Flat Regions:** Geometric edges in relatively flat regions are otherwise undetected by cone intersections and require SER [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** SDF evaluation near the surface requires high precision to avoid artifacts [G].
*   **Boundaries:** Handling pixels at the edge of the screen or near the camera origin may require special frustum construction [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** CTSS is more efficient than conventional supersampling because it only performs supersampling near contours identified by cone intersections, minimizing shading computations [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Delivers comparable antialiasing quality at significantly lower computational cost than conventional supersampling [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Provides efficient spatial antialiasing without additional rays per pixel, offline prefiltering, or post-processing blurriness and ghosting [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires cone intersection evaluation, which may be complex to implement, and relies on SER for flat regions with edges [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time SDF rendering where antialiasing is required without significant performance loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with no complex edges or when post-processing filters are acceptable and performance is not a concern [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Cone-Traced Supersampling (CTSS) was introduced as an efficient and robust spatial antialiasing solution for SDF rendering [S1, unofficial]. It naturally complements the sphere tracing algorithm and can be easily implemented in existing real-time SDF renderers [S1, unofficial]. The technique was proposed to address the limitations of conventional supersampling and post-processing filters [S1, unofficial].
