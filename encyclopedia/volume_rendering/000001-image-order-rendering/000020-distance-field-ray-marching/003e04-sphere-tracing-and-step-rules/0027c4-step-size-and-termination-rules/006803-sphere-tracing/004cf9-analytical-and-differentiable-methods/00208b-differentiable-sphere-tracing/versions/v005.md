# Differentiable Sphere Tracing

Also called: DIST, Differentiable Sphere Tracing Renderer

## Overview
Differentiable Sphere Tracing (DIST) is a rendering algorithm for implicit Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) represented by neural networks, enabling efficient forward and backward passes for inverse graphics optimization [S1]. It solves the non-differentiability and computational prohibitive cost of naive sphere tracing by optimizing the rendering pipeline for GPU execution, allowing gradients to propagate from 2D image losses directly to the SDF parameters [S1]. It sets itself apart from naive sphere tracing by using a coarse-to-fine approach and a specific gradient approximation to reduce memory consumption, and from [Instant-NSR-PL Forward Pass](2540-instant-nsr-pl-forward-pass.md) by explicitly handling the backward pass gradient derivation for neural SDFs rather than just accumulating forward attributes [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Camera intrinsics $K$ and extrinsics (rotation $R$, translation $c$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
- A neural network $f: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ representing the SDF [S1].
- Optional bounding volume (sphere radius $r_{bound}$ or axis-aligned box) [S2, S4, unofficial].
- Rendering parameters: `num_iterations` (max steps), `convergence_threshold` $\epsilon$ [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- Rendered image (color/depth), surface normals, depth, and opacity [S1][S2, unofficial].
- Gradients with respect to SDF parameters [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- `ray_positions`: Tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ storing the current 3D position of each ray [S1][S3, unofficial].
- `ray_directions`: Tensor of shape $(H, W, 3)$ storing normalized ray directions [S1][S2, unofficial].
- `foreground_masks`: Boolean tensor of shape $(H, W, 1)$ indicating active rays [S1][S3, unofficial].
- `signed_distances`: Tensor of shape $(H, W, 1)$ storing $f(p)$ values [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1. **Ray Initialization:**
   Compute ray origins $c$ and directions $v$ for each pixel $(u, v)$ using camera intrinsics $K$ and extrinsics $R$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
   $$ \text{homo\_calib} = K^{-1} \cdot [u, v, 1]^T $$
   $$ \text{rays} = R^T \cdot \text{homo\_calib} $$
   $$ v = \frac{\text{rays}}{\|\text{rays}\|} $$
   Initialize `ray_positions` to camera center $c$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
   Initialize `foreground_masks` to `True` for all pixels [S1][S3, unofficial].

2. **Bounding Volume Check (Optional):**
   If a spherical bounding radius $r_{bound}$ is provided, clamp rays to the sphere [S2, S3, unofficial].
   $$ a = \|v\|^2, \quad b = 2(v \cdot c), \quad c_{quad} = \|c\|^2 - r_{bound}^2 $$
   $$ \Delta = b^2 - 4ac_{quad} $$
   $$ t = \frac{-b - \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a} $$
   Update positions if hit:
   $$ \text{ray\_positions} = \text{where}(\Delta \ge 0, c + v \cdot t, c) $$
   Update `foreground_masks` to exclude rays outside the bound [S2, S3, unofficial].

3. **Ray Marching Loop:**
   Iterate $n$ from $0$ to `num_iterations` [S1][S3, unofficial]:
   
   a. **SDF Query:**
      Compute signed distances at current positions:
      $$ d^{(n)} = f(\text{ray\_positions}^{(n)}) $$
      Store as `signed_distances` [S1][S3, unofficial].

   b. **Convergence Check:**
      Determine if rays have converged:
      $$ \text{converged} = |d^{(n)}| < \epsilon $$
      where $\epsilon$ is `convergence_threshold` (typical $10^{-3}$ or $5 \cdot 10^{-5}$) [S1][S3, unofficial].

   c. **Position Update:**
      Update positions for non-converged, active rays [S1][S3, unofficial]:
      $$ \text{ray\_positions}^{(n+1)} = \text{ray\_positions}^{(n)} + v \cdot d^{(n)} $$
      Apply mask:
      $$ \text{ray\_positions} = \text{where}(\text{foreground\_masks} \land \neg \text{converged}, \text{ray\_positions}^{(n+1)}, \text{ray\_positions}^{(n)}) $$
      [S1][S3, unofficial].

   d. **Termination:**
      If all rays are either converged or out of foreground (`torch.all(~foreground_masks | converged)`), break loop [S3, unofficial].

4. **Attribute Extraction (Forward Pass):**
   For converged rays, extract surface properties [S1][S4, unofficial]:
   - **Normal:** Compute gradient of SDF w.r.t. position:
     $$ n = \frac{\nabla f(p)}{\|\nabla f(p)\|} $$
     [S1][S4, unofficial]. Note: In implementation, this is often computed via `torch.autograd.grad` during the backward pass or separately [S3, S4, unofficial].
   - **Shading:** Apply lighting model (e.g., point light at camera) [S4, unofficial]:
     $$ L = \frac{2 \max(l \cdot n, 0)}{\|p_{light} - p\|^2} $$
     [S4, unofficial].
   - **Image Composition:** Combine shading with mask [S4, unofficial].

5. **Backward Pass (Gradient Computation):**
   To avoid storing the full computational graph of the neural network queries, DIST uses a gradient approximation [S1][S3, unofficial]:
   
   a. **Detach and Require Grad:**
      Detach `ray_positions` from the graph and enable gradient tracking:
      $$ p_{grad} = \text{ray\_positions.detach}(); \quad p_{grad}.requires\_grad = \text{True} $$
      [S3, unofficial].

   b. **Re-evaluate SDF:**
      Compute $d^{(n)} = f(p_{grad})$ [S3, unofficial].

   c. **Compute Gradients w.r.t. Positions:**
      $$ g_p = \nabla_{p_{grad}} d^{(n)} $$
      [S3, unofficial].

   d. **Compute Output Gradient:**
      Let $g_{out}$ be the gradient of the loss w.r.t. the rendered image/depth. The gradient w.r.t. the SDF output $d^{(n)}$ is approximated by projecting $g_{out}$ onto the ray direction, normalized by the dot product of the SDF gradient and ray direction [S1][S3, unofficial]:
      $$ g_{d} = -\frac{g_{out} \cdot v}{g_p \cdot v} $$
      [S3, unofficial].
      Zero gradients for unconverged points:
      $$ g_{d} = \text{where}(\text{converged}, g_{d}, 0) $$
      [S3, unofficial].

   e. **Propagate to Parameters:**
      Compute gradients w.r.t. SDF network parameters $\theta$:
      $$ \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} = \nabla_\theta d^{(n)} \cdot g_{d} $$
      [S3, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- `num_iterations`: Max marching steps. Typical: 2000 [S3, unofficial].
- `convergence_threshold`: SDF threshold for hit. Typical: $10^{-3}$ [S3, unofficial] or $5 \cdot 10^{-5}$ [S2, unofficial].
- `march_step`: Steps per ray in some implementations. Default: 50 [S2, unofficial].
- `bounding_radius`: Optional sphere radius [S2, S3, unofficial].
- `grid_res`: Dimensions of voxel grid if using grid-based SDF [S4, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** In gradient calculation, clamp `g_p \cdot v` to avoid division by zero. If $>0$, clamp max with $10^{-6}$; if $\le 0$, clamp min with $-10^{-6}$ [S3, unofficial].
- **Non-Intersecting Pixels:** Handle by setting image value to 1 and updating mask iteratively or masking out [S4, unofficial].
- **Normal Normalization:** Add epsilon ($10^{-12}$) to norm to prevent division by zero [S2, unofficial].
- **No Valid Depth:** Raise error if no rays converge [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Instant-NSR-PL Forward Pass](2540-instant-nsr-pl-forward-pass.md)
The Instant-NSR-PL Forward Pass renders scenes by marching rays through a neural SDF and accumulating color, normals, depth, and opacity along each ray. It computes the composite RGB image, surface normals, depth, and opacity by querying a geometry network for SDF values and gradients, computing opacity from the SDF and ray direction, and accumulating results along the ray. It sets itself apart from its siblings by focusing on the forward accumulation of multiple attributes simultaneously, whereas Differentiable Sphere Tracing focuses on the gradient approximation for optimization.

### [Differentiable Silhouette Rendering](4322-differentiable-silhouette-rendering.md)
Differentiable Silhouette Rendering is a technique to render binary silhouettes of 3D shapes represented by SDFs in a way that allows gradient back-propagation to scene parameters. It solves the non-differentiability of standard binary silhouette detection by computing a "soft" silhouette value based on the minimum absolute SDF encountered along a ray. It sets itself apart from its parent [Differentiable Sphere Tracing](2316-differentiable-sphere-tracing.md) by focusing specifically on the silhouette supervision signal rather than full photometric or geometric rendering, and from siblings like [Instant-NSR-PL Forward Pass](2540-instant-nsr-pl-forward-pass.md) by relying on the minimum absolute distance query rather than normal-based edge detection.

### [SDF Direct Diffuse Integrator](4467-sdf-direct-diffuse-integrator.md)
The SDF Direct Diffuse Integrator is a differentiable rendering method that computes direct illumination for scenes defined by SDFs. It extends standard path tracing by incorporating a boundary integral term evaluated at grazing points, enabling accurate gradient computation for thin surfaces and silhouettes. Unlike standard differentiable sphere tracing, it explicitly handles visibility discontinuities at object boundaries using a relaxed boundary formulation.

### [Multi-View Photometric Shape Prediction](5427-multi-view-photometric-shape-prediction.md)
Predicts 3D shape from multiple color images by minimizing cross-view photometric consistency loss without requiring segmentation masks. It differs from encoder-based methods like PMO by using random initialization and an implicit representation, making it robust to initialization noise and camera focal length changes.

### [Camera Pose Optimization via Depth and Silhouette](5667-camera-pose-optimization-via-depth-and-silhouette.md)
This method optimizes an initial camera pose toward a ground truth pose by minimizing gradients computed from 2D depth and binary silhouette observations using a differentiable SDF renderer. It is distinct from sibling methods like [Instant-NSR-PL Forward Pass](2540-instant-nsr-pl-forward-pass.md) which may optimize shape parameters, by focusing specifically on pose recovery given a fixed shape. It differs from [Differentiable Silhouette Rendering](4322-differentiable-silhouette-rendering.md) by explicitly combining silhouette cross-entropy with a scale-invariant depth loss.

### Single-View Depth Shape Completion
Recovers complete 3D shape from a single depth image by optimizing a latent code through a differentiable renderer, requiring no additional training [S1]. It fixes parameters of a pre-trained DeepSDF decoder $f$, initializes latent code $z_0$, renders depth $\tilde{I}_d$ and silhouette $\tilde{I}_s$, and computes loss $\mathcal{L}_d(\mathcal{R}_d(f(z)), I_d) + \mathcal{L}_s(\mathcal{R}_s(f(z)), I_s)$ [S1]. It outperforms DeepSDF because it samples adaptively via differentiable sphere tracing rather than fixed rules [S1].

### Reparameterization for Differentiable SDF Rendering
A method using a reparameterization function $T$ for SDFs that strictly satisfies the boundary requirement $\partial_\pi T(\omega_b, \pi) = \partial_\pi \omega_b$ [S5, unofficial]. It constructs a 3D vector field $\mathbf{V}(x, \pi) := -\frac{\partial_x \phi(x, \pi_0)}{\phi(x, \pi) \|\partial_x \phi(x, \pi_0)\|_2}$ [S5, unofficial]. It computes evaluation distance $t$ using sphere tracing samples: $t := \frac{\sum w^{(i)} t_i}{\sum w^{(i)}}$ [S5, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of pixels and maximum marching steps $N$. Each step requires one neural network forward pass [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to $H \times W \times N$ if storing the full graph, but DIST reduces this to $O(H \times W)$ by detaching positions and re-evaluating SDF for gradients [S1][S3, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Efficient on commodity GPUs. Coarse-to-fine approach and aggressive marching reduce queries compared to naive tracing [S1].
- **Strengths:** Fully differentiable, supports inverse graphics (shape/pose optimization), handles complex implicit geometry [S1][S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Computationally expensive for high-resolution images or deep networks due to repeated SDF queries. Sensitive to convergence threshold [S1][S3, unofficial].
- **When to Use:** Inverse graphics, shape reconstruction from 2D/3D data [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** Real-time rendering where speed is critical and differentiability is not needed [S1].

## Background
Originated by Liu et al. in 2020 with the paper "DIST: Rendering Deep Implicit Signed Distance Function With Differentiable Sphere Tracing" [S1]. It bridges the gap between inverse graphics and neural implicit SDFs, which previously lacked efficient differentiable rendering methods [S1]. The method was implemented in PyTorch and applied to single-view and multi-view 3D shape prediction [S1][S2, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of sphere tracing [Har96] but adapts it for neural networks [S1].
