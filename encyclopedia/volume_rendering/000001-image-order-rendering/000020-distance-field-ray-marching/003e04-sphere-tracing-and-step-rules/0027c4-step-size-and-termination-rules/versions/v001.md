# Step Size and Termination Rules


## Overview
Step Size and Termination Rules define the logic for determining how far a ray advances between samples and when the marching process halts, solving the trade-off between rendering accuracy (avoiding tunneling or missing thin features) and computational performance. This family is distinct from [Standard Constant Step Ray Marching](1080-standard-constant-step-ray-marching.md) by dynamically adjusting step sizes based on local geometry or density, and from [Sphere Tracing](1674-sphere-tracing.md) by often decoupling the step size from strict Lipschitz constraints or applying specific heuristics like early termination and oscillation detection.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism replaces the constant step $\Delta s = \text{const}$ with a function $\Delta s = f(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{d}, \text{context})$, where $\mathbf{p}$ is the current ray position, $\mathbf{d}$ is the normalized ray direction, and context includes previous samples, camera state, or dataset metadata. The general update rule for a ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ is:
$$ \mathbf{p}_{i+1} = \mathbf{p}_i + \Delta s_i \mathbf{d} $$
$$ t_{i+1} = t_i + \Delta s_i $$

Key shared constraints and goals include:
1.  **Safety/Correctness:** The step size must not cause the ray to "overshoot" or "penetrate" geometry or density features that should have been sampled. For SDFs, this often requires $\Delta s \le d(\mathbf{p})$ (Sphere Tracing) or a conservative fraction thereof. For volumes, it requires sufficient sampling density to satisfy the Nyquist criterion or to capture density gradients accurately.
2.  **Efficiency:** The step size should increase in empty or low-gradient regions to reduce sample count.
3.  **Termination:** Step size changes interact with termination rules (e.g., Early Ray Termination). A larger step size might skip the exact intersection point, requiring post-processing refinement (e.g., binary search).

## Subtopics

### [Standard Constant Step Ray Marching](1080-standard-constant-step-ray-marching.md)
Uses a fixed interval $\Delta t$, allowing it to work with any function returning a boolean or scalar field, rather than requiring a strict SDF. Choose when geometry is simple or when working with non-distance fields.

### [Sphere Tracing](1674-sphere-tracing.md)
Dynamically calculates a safe step size equal to the distance to the nearest surface, guaranteeing no overshoot for Lipschitz-continuous SDFs. Choose for robust rendering of complex implicit surfaces.

### [Step Size Optimization](2054-step-size-optimization.md)
Varies $\Delta s$ based on local geometry, density, or global bounds to balance quality and cost. Choose when rendering large volumes or complex scenes where fixed steps are inefficient.

### [Sphere Tracing Termination and Clipping](2635-sphere-tracing-termination-and-clipping.md)
Explicitly handles failure modes like oscillation and far-plane clipping using specific guards (e.g., oscillation detection factor $\alpha$). Choose when rendering noisy SDFs or when strict bounds are required.

### [SDF Scaling and Early Ray Termination for Fast Sphere Tracing](3807-sdf-scaling-and-early-ray-termination-for-fast-sphere-tracin.md)
Uses a low-resolution buffer for a head start and SDF scaling to improve performance. Choose for real-time applications requiring high frame rates.

### Adaptive Minimum Step Size
**Definition:** Dynamic adjustment of the minimum step size based on distance from camera and accumulated density to reduce samples in less important areas [S1, unofficial].
**Step:** Inside the cloud (distanceField <= 0), increase minStepSize by STEP_INSCREASE_OVER_DENSITY multiplied by the current sampleDensity. After advancing the ray, increase minStepSize by STEP_INCREASE_OVER_DISTANCE multiplied by the stepSize used [S1, unofficial].
**Parameter:** STEP_INSCREASE_OVER_DENSITY: Constant controlling how much minStepSize increases per unit of sampleDensity. STEP_INCREASE_OVER_DISTANCE: Constant controlling how much minStepSize increases per unit of stepSize (distance traveled) [S1, unofficial].
**Comparison:** Increases step size further from camera (less detail needed) and behind dense areas (less light contribution), unlike fixed step sizes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originating from early volume rendering techniques, step size adaptation was introduced to handle the computational cost of sampling dense volumetric data, such as clouds, where uniform sampling is inefficient [S1, unofficial]. The concept of adaptive minimum step size was specifically detailed by Jack Tollenaar in the context of volumetric cloud rendering, leveraging precalculated distance fields to skip empty space and dynamically adjusting step sizes based on accumulated density and distance from the camera [S1, unofficial].
