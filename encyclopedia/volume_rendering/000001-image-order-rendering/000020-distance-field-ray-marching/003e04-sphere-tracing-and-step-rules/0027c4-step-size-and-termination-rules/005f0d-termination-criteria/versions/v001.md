# Termination Criteria

Also called: Ray Marching Stop Conditions, Loop Exit Conditions

## Overview
Termination criteria define the conditions under which a ray marching loop halts, ensuring computational efficiency and visual correctness. This family solves the problem of infinite loops and visual artifacts (such as "aura" or "swimming") caused by premature termination or precision errors. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by focusing specifically on the geometric and numerical constraints of the step rule (e.g., SDF distance vs. volume opacity) rather than the step size calculation itself.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and normalized direction $\mathbf{d}$, parameterized by accumulated distance $t$. The core loop evaluates a scene function (SDF, density field, or volume texture) at the current position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ to determine the next step size $\Delta t$ and check for termination.

**Termination Conditions:**
1.  **Surface Hit:** The ray has intersected the target geometry. For SDFs, this is typically $|f(\mathbf{p})| < \epsilon$ or $f(\mathbf{p}) \le 0$. For volumes, this may be a density threshold or opacity accumulation [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Escape/Miss:** The ray has traveled beyond a maximum distance $t_{max}$ or exceeded a maximum iteration count $N_{max}$.
3.  **Early Termination (Volumes):** Accumulated opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., $1 - \epsilon$), or accumulated transmission falls below a minimum threshold, allowing the ray to stop early as further contributions are negligible [S1, unofficial].

**Step Size Determination:**
The step size $\Delta t$ is computed based on the local scene properties:
*   **SDF-based:** $\Delta t$ is often derived from the SDF value $f(\mathbf{p})$, potentially scaled by a relaxation factor or Lipschitz constant $\lambda$ .
*   **Volume-based:** $\Delta t$ may be fixed, adaptive based on density gradients, or constrained by minimum/maximum bounds to prevent aliasing or excessive computation .
*   **Safety:** Steps must be chosen to ensure the ray does not penetrate the surface or skip over significant volumetric features, often requiring conservative clamping.

**Pseudocode Structure:**
```python
def ray_march_loop(ray_origin, ray_direction, scene_func, max_dist, max_steps, epsilon):
    t = 0.0
    for i in range(max_steps):
        if t > max_dist:
            return False, t
        
        p = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        val = scene_func(p)
        
        # Termination: Hit
        if is_hit(val, epsilon):
            return True, t
        
        # Termination: Early Exit (Volume specific)
        if is_early_exit(val, t):
            return False, t # Or handle partial contribution
            
        # Step Size Calculation
        step = compute_step_size(val, t, i)
        
        # Safety Clamp
        step = max(step, min_step)
        step = min(step, max_step)
        
        t += step
        
    return False, t
```

## Subtopics

### [Sphere Tracing Termination and Clipping](2635-sphere-tracing-termination-and-clipping.md)
Sphere Tracing Termination and Clipping defines the conditions under which a ray marching loop in a Signed Distance Field (SDF) terminates, either by finding a surface intersection, determining no intersection exists, or hitting safety limits. It solves the problem of infinite loops and visual artifacts (such as "aura" or "swimming") caused by premature termination or precision errors. It sets itself apart from [Ray Marching with Early Termination](17724-ray-marching-with-early-termination.md) by focusing specifically on the geometric and numerical constraints of sphere tracing (distance-field stepping) rather than general volumetric opacity accumulation. See [Sphere Tracing Termination and Clipping](2635-sphere-tracing-termination-and-clipping.md).

### [Ray Marching with Early Termination](17724-ray-marching-with-early-termination.md)
Ray marching with early termination is a loop control strategy for image-order volume rendering and ray tracing that halts the marching process immediately upon detecting a surface intersection or exceeding a maximum travel distance. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by prioritizing computational efficiency and stability over strict physical accuracy in the final accumulation step, often serving as the foundational termination logic for sphere tracing and standard ray marchers. See [Ray Marching with Early Termination](17724-ray-marching-with-early-termination.md).

### Early Ray Marching Termination (Threshold)
#### definition
An optimization to stop ray marching when the accumulated transmission falls below a small threshold, saving computation in dense volumes [S1, unofficial].
#### parameter
Threshold: A small value (e.g., $10^{-3}$ or 0.001) representing the minimum transmission. Below this, the contribution to the final color is considered negligible [S1, unofficial].
#### pitfall
Simply stopping and ignoring the remaining volume contribution is statistically incorrect and introduces bias. It is acceptable for visual rendering but not for high-precision scientific simulation (e.g., thermonuclear engineering) [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Origin: The concept of early termination in ray marching is rooted in the computational cost of evaluating volumetric data. As noted in "Volume Rendering for Developers," breaking from the loop when transmission drops below a threshold (e.g., $10^{-3}$) is a common optimization because computing samples for the "tail" of the ray contributes negligibly to the output in many cases [S1, unofficial]. However, this practice is explicitly warned against in contexts requiring physical accuracy, such as neutron transport simulation, where the "long tail" of transmission is significant [S1, unofficial]. The specific thresholds and safety checks (like oscillation detection in sphere tracing) were developed to mitigate artifacts like "aura" and "swimming" inherent in discrete sampling of continuous fields [S1, unofficial].
