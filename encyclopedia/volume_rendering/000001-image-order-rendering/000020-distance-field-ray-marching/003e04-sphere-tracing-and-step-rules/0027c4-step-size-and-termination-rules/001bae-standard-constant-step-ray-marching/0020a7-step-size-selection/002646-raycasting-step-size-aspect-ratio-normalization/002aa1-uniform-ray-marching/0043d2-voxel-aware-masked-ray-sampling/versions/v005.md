# Voxel-Aware Masked Ray Sampling

Also called: Hierarchical Sampling for Sparse Feature Volume, Inside-Voxel Sampling, NSVF Hierarchical Ray Sampling with Voxel-Aware Masking, NSVF parallel ray sampling, NSVF voxel intersection sampling, Ray-Voxel Intersection Sampling

## Overview
Voxel-Aware Masked Ray Sampling is a ray sampling strategy for sparse voxel fields that ensures all sampling points lie strictly within intersected voxels, preventing invalid "air" samples common in unconstrained hierarchical sampling [S2, unofficial]. It handles variable numbers of voxel intersections per ray by padding and masking tensors, supporting hierarchical sampling ratios where coarse and fine passes are constrained to valid sparse regions [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard NeRF which uses uniform or stratified sampling along the entire ray, this method restricts integration to occupied sparse voxels, offering significant memory and compute savings for sparse scenes [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origins and directions for a batch of rays.
- Sparse voxel grid structure (defining voxel bounds and IDs).
- Parameters: `chunk_size` (default 4096), `max_hits` (default 60), `raymarching_stepsize` (default 0.01), `fine_num_sample_ratio` (default 0), `inverse_distance_coarse_sampling` (default 'none'), `background_depth` (default 5.0), `fixed_num_samples` (default 0).

**Outputs:**
- `sampled_dists`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, max_steps)` containing distances from camera origin for each sample point.
- `sampled_idx`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, max_steps)` containing voxel IDs for each sample point.
- `sampled_masks`: Boolean tensor indicating valid samples.
- Rendered colors, depths, and other attributes after compositing.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Intersection Outputs Dictionary:**
    -   `min_depth`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays,)`, distance to voxel entry.
    -   `max_depth`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays,)`, distance to voxel exit.
    -   `intersected_voxel_idx`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, max_hits)`, voxel IDs.
    -   `probs`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, max_hits)`, sampling probabilities.
    -   `steps`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays,)`, number of samples per ray.
2.  **All Results Dictionary (Post-processing):**
    -   `missed`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize,)`, boolean mask for rays hitting no voxels.
    -   `colors`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize, 3)`.
    -   `depths`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize,)`.
    -   `normal`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize, 3)` (optional).
    -   `voxel_depth`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize,)` (optional).
    -   `voxel_edges`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize, 3)` (optional).
    -   `feat_n2`: Tensor of shape `(fullsize,)` (optional).

**Steps:**

1.  **Ray-Voxel Intersection Sampling:**
    Identify pairs $P = \{(r, v)\}$ where ray $r$ intersects voxel $v$. For each ray, determine the entry (`min_depth`) and exit (`max_depth`) distances in world space, and the corresponding voxel indices. Calculate sampling probabilities `probs` proportional to the distance within each voxel:
    $$ \text{dists}_i = \max(0, \text{max\_depth}_i - \text{min\_depth}_i) $$
    Mask out invalid voxels (where `pts_idx == -1`) by setting their dists to 0.
    $$ \text{probs}_i = \frac{\text{dists}_i}{\sum_j \text{dists}_j} $$
    Determine the number of steps `steps` for each ray. If `fixed_num_samples > 0`, `steps` is constant. Otherwise:
    $$ \text{steps} = \frac{\sum_i \text{dists}_i}{\text{raymarching\_stepsize}} $$
    If `fine_num_sample_ratio > 0` (hierarchical fine pass), update steps:
    $$ \text{steps} = (\text{intersected\_voxel\_idx} \neq -1).sum(-1).float() \times \text{fine\_num\_sample\_ratio} $$

2.  **Inside-Voxel Sampling:**
    For each intersected voxel $v$ and ray $r$, sample points. Convert world-space distance $d_w$ to sparse-space distance $d_s$ using the ray-voxel exit positions:
    $$ d_s = d_w - (d_w^{(r,v)} - d_s^{(r,v)}) $$
    where $d_w^{(r,v)}$ is the world-space distance of the ray-voxel exit, and $d_s^{(r,v)}$ is the sparse-space distance of the same exit point. Perform uniform coarse sampling and importance sampling in the sparse space to generate $N_c$ coarse and $N_i$ importance samples.

3.  **Parallel Processing and Padding:**
    If the total number of rays `full_size` exceeds `chunk_size` (4096), split rays into chunks. Process each chunk independently.
    -   Find `maxlen`, the maximum number of steps across all chunks.
    -   Pad outputs:
        -   `sampled_idx`: Fill masked/invalid positions with -1.
        -   `sampled_depth`: Fill masked/invalid positions with `MAX_DEPTH`.
        -   `sampled_dists`: Fill masked/invalid positions with 0.0.
    -   Concatenate chunks and truncate to `maxlen`.

4.  **Masking and Truncation:**
    Identify rays that hit the maximum depth boundary:
    $$ \text{sampled\_masks} = (\text{sampled\_depth} == \text{MAX\_DEPTH}) $$
    Calculate the maximum valid steps across all rays:
    $$ \text{num\_max\_steps} = \max_i (\sim \text{sampled\_masks}_i).sum() $$
    Truncate tensors to `num_max_steps`:
    $$ \text{sampled\_dists} = \text{sampled\_dists}[:, : \text{num\_max\_steps}] $$
    $$ \text{sampled\_idx} = \text{sampled\_idx}[:, : \text{num\_max\_steps}] $$

5.  **Compositing and Background Handling:**
    Render colors and depths. Identify missed rays (those with no valid voxel intersections).
    $$ \text{missed} = \text{fill\_in}((\text{fullsize},), \text{hits}, \text{all\_results}['\text{missed}'], 1.0) $$
    Fill missed rays with background values:
    $$ \text{colors} = \text{fill\_in}((\text{fullsize}, 3), \text{hits}, \text{all\_results}['\text{colors}'], 0.0) $$
    $$ \text{depths} = \text{fill\_in}((\text{fullsize},), \text{hits}, \text{all\_results}['\text{depths}'], 0.0) $$
    Add background contribution:
    $$ \text{colors} += \text{missed.unsqueeze}(-1) \times \text{bg\_color} $$
    $$ \text{depths} += \text{missed} \times \text{BG\_DEPTH} $$
    where `bg_color` is derived from `all_results['colors']` and `BG_DEPTH` is `self.field.bg_color.depth`.

def pad_tensor(tensor, max_len, pad_value):
    """
    Pads tensor to max_len along dim 1.
    """

    if current_len < max_len:

## Subtopics

### Sparse Voxel-based Sampling and Rendering
A rendering pipeline that dynamically allocates voxels only where objects and surfaces exist, restricting ray marching and volume integration to occupied regions [S3]. It performs ray-voxel intersection tests to identify sparse voxels traversed by camera rays, samples points within them, and applies volume rendering [S3]. Pitfalls include reduced throughput in high-complexity or noisy scenarios [S3]. Operating frequencies are typically 5 Hz for tracking and 2 Hz for optimization on synthetic datasets [S3].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of intersected voxels per ray and the number of samples per voxel. Parallel processing with chunking (default 4096 rays) helps manage memory and latency.
- **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the number of rays and the maximum number of voxel intersections (`max_hits`, default 60) plus samples per intersection. Padding ensures uniform tensor shapes.
- **Performance:** On a single NVIDIA RTX 3090, per-frame tracking takes ~150-200 ms and joint frame/map optimization takes 450-550 ms [S3]. Typical operating frequencies are 5 Hz for tracking and 2 Hz for optimization on the Replica dataset [S3].
- **Strengths:** Efficient for sparse scenes by ignoring empty space. Prevents invalid sampling outside voxels, which is crucial for sparse feature volumes [S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Performance degrades in highly complex or noisy scenes [S3]. Requires careful handling of variable intersection counts via padding and masking [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Originating from the NSVF (Neural Sparse Voxel Fields) framework [S1, unofficial], this method was developed to address the inefficiency of dense volume rendering in sparse environments. It builds on the concept of sparse voxel grids, where only occupied regions are stored and processed [S3]. The hierarchical sampling strategy ensures that sampling points remain within valid voxels, avoiding the "air" sampling issues of unconstrained methods [S2, unofficial]. It is used in neural implicit reconstruction and rendering pipelines, particularly for dynamic scenes and SLAM systems [S3].
