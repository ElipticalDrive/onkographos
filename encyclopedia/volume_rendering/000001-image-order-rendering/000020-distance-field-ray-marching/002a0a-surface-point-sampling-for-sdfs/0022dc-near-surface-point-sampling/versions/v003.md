# Near-Surface Point Sampling

Also called: Near-surface sampling, perturbed surface sampling

## Overview
Near-Surface Point Sampling generates training data points in the immediate vicinity of a mesh surface by perturbing existing surface samples with Gaussian noise [S1]. It solves the problem of insufficient volumetric coverage near the geometry boundary, which is critical for training neural implicit representations to converge on the correct surface location [S1]. It differs from uniform volume sampling by focusing density on the surface transition region, and from pure surface sampling by providing gradient information for the signed distance field (SDF) values off the zero-level set [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A set of surface sample points $P_{surf} = \{p_1, ..., p_N\}$ on a mesh.
*   A noise standard deviation $\sigma$.

**Outputs:**
*   A set of near-surface sample points $P_{near}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   Points are represented as 3D vectors $(x, y, z)$.

**Parameters:**
*   $\sigma$: Standard deviation of the Gaussian noise. Typical value: $0.01$ [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  For each surface sample point $p_i \in P_{surf}$:
    a.  Generate a random 3D noise vector $\delta_i$ where each component is drawn from a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
    b.  Compute the perturbed point $p'_i = p_i + \delta_i$.
    c.  Add $p'_i$ to the output set $P_{near}$.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def near_surface_point_sampling(surface_points, sigma=0.01):
    """
    Generates near-surface points by perturbing surface points with Gaussian noise.
    
    Args:
        surface_points: numpy array of shape (N, 3) containing surface coordinates.
        sigma: Standard deviation of the Gaussian noise (default 0.01).
        
    Returns:
        numpy array of shape (N, 3) containing perturbed near-surface points.
    """
    # Generate random noise from normal distribution N(0, sigma^2)
    noise = np.random.normal(0, sigma, size=surface_points.shape)
    
    # Perturb surface points
    near_surface_points = surface_points + noise
    
    return near_surface_points
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   If $\sigma$ is too large, points may fall far from the surface, effectively becoming uniform volume samples and losing the benefit of near-surface density [S1].
*   If $\sigma$ is too small, the points may be numerically indistinguishable from the surface, providing little gradient information for the SDF [S1].
*   The method assumes the surface samples are accurate; noise will propagate surface errors into the volume [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of surface samples. Each point requires a constant number of random number generations and additions.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the resulting points.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; effectively increases the density of training points near the surface, which is crucial for SDF regression [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not guarantee coverage of the entire volume; relies on the quality of the initial surface samples [S1].
*   **When to use:** Training neural implicit surfaces where near-surface gradients are critical [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When uniform volumetric coverage is required without surface bias, or when surface samples are unavailable.

## Background
The method was introduced in the context of Neural Geometric Level of Detail (NGLOD) for real-time rendering with implicit 3D shapes [S1]. It is part of a training strategy that distributes points in a 2:2:1 split of surface, near, and uniform samples to optimize the implicit representation [S1]. The approach leverages the fact that perturbing surface points provides valuable gradient information for learning the signed distance field near the zero-level set [S1].
