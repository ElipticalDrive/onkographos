# Near-Surface Point Sampling

Also called: Near-surface sampling, Perturbed surface sampling

## Overview
Near-Surface Point Sampling generates point clouds for implicit surface representation by perturbing existing mesh surface samples with Gaussian noise. It solves the problem of insufficient training data density near the surface boundary, which is critical for learning accurate signed distance fields (SDFs). It is distinguished from its sibling [Surfel Parameterization from Mesh Sampling](4501-surfel-parameterization-from-mesh-sampling.md) by its reliance on random perturbation of existing samples rather than geometric parameterization, and from [Bidirectional Projective Sampling](8824-bidirectional-projective-sampling.md) by its simplicity and lack of view-dependent optimization.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A set of existing surface samples $P_{surf} = \{p_i\}$ from a mesh.
- Noise standard deviation $\sigma$.

**Outputs:**
- A set of near-surface samples $P_{near}$.

**Data Structures:**
- $P_{surf}$: List of 3D vectors $(x, y, z)$.
- $P_{near}$: List of 3D vectors $(x, y, z)$.

**Steps:**
1. Initialize $P_{near}$ as an empty list.
2. For each point $p \in P_{surf}$:
   a. Generate a random noise vector $\epsilon \in \mathbb{R}^3$, where each component $\epsilon_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
   b. Compute the perturbed point $p' = p + \epsilon$.
   c. Append $p'$ to $P_{near}$.
3. Return $P_{near}$.

**Parameters:**
- $\sigma$: Standard deviation of the Gaussian noise. Typical value: $0.01$ [S1].

**Edge Cases:**
- If $P_{surf}$ is empty, $P_{near}$ is empty.
- Precision issues may arise if $\sigma$ is large relative to the mesh scale, causing samples to drift far from the surface.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import numpy as np

def near_surface_point_sampling(surface_samples, sigma=0.01):
    """
    Generate near-surface samples by perturbing surface samples with Gaussian noise.
    
    Args:
        surface_samples: np.ndarray of shape (N, 3), existing surface points.
        sigma: float, standard deviation of the Gaussian noise.
        
    Returns:
        np.ndarray of shape (N, 3), perturbed near-surface points.
    """
    # Generate Gaussian noise with mean 0 and standard deviation sigma
    noise = np.random.normal(0, sigma, size=surface_samples.shape)
    
    # Perturb the surface samples
    near_surface_samples = surface_samples + noise
    
    return near_surface_samples
```

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of surface samples. Each sample requires a constant number of random number generations and additions.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the perturbed samples.
- **Performance:** Very fast, suitable for real-time resampling during training [S1].
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement, effectively increases sample density near the surface without complex geometric calculations.
- **Limitations:** Does not guarantee uniform distribution near the surface; quality depends on the initial surface sample distribution.
- **When to use:** When training implicit surface representations and surface samples are already available.
- **When not to use:** When working directly with volume data or when view-dependent sampling is required.

## Background
Originated in the context of Neural Geometric Level of Detail (NGLOD) for real-time rendering with implicit 3D shapes [S1]. The method is part of a training pipeline that resamples points at every epoch to maintain a balanced distribution of surface, near-surface, and uniform samples [S1].
