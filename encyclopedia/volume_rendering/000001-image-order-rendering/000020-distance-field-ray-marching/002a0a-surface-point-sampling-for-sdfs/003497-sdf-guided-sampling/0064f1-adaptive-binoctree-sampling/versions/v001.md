# Adaptive Binoctree Sampling

Also called: Adaptive fine voxel sampling, Sphere + coarse + fine voxel sampling

## Overview
Adaptive Binoctree Sampling is a ray sampling strategy for optimizing neural implicit Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) from omnidirectional video. It solves the problem of inefficient surface localization in large unbounded scenes by replacing standard importance sampling with a hierarchical guide derived from a pre-built spherical binoctree. Unlike [SDF-guided Sampling](7324-sdf-guided-sampling.md) which may rely on generic distance estimates, this method uses coarse and fine sphoxels to conservatively bound the search space, offering superior accuracy over direct depth supervision methods like [NeuS-facto-D](7324-sdf-guided-sampling.md#sources).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Input depth maps from omnidirectional video.
*   Camera poses and intrinsics.
*   Neural SDF network.

**Outputs:**
*   Set of sample points along a ray for SDF evaluation.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Spherical Binoctree:** A hierarchical structure representing spherical space. Nodes are classified as "coarse" or "fine" based on subdivision criteria.
*   **Sphoxels:** The leaf nodes or active regions of the binoctree, representing volumetric elements in spherical coordinates.

**Steps:**
1.  **Pre-build Binoctree:** Construct a spherical binoctree from the input depth maps to establish coarse spatial bounds for the scene [S1].
2.  **Subdivision:** Subdivide the spherical space based on solid angle measurement and distance from the camera. This creates a spatially variant tree depth, densifying samples near the surface [S1].
3.  **Baseline Sampling:** Initialize ray sampling using sphere sampling as a baseline [S1].
4.  **Coarse Guidance:** Sample points between coarse sphoxels rather than relying solely on sphere bounds. This guides the ray into the relevant geometric region [S1].
5.  **Fine Sampling:** Sample fine sphoxels to locate the surface precisely, replacing the standard importance sampling step [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   Total samples per ray: 128 (kept constant for fairness in ablation studies) [S1].
*   Importance sampling samples per step: 64 (in baseline comparisons) [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   If the initial depth map estimation for coarse voxels is incorrect, it leads to inaccurate guidance for coarse geometry and errors in background regions with nearer initial depths [S1].
*   Mitigation involves dilating and pruning voxels before optimization, but postprocessing may still be required to remove spurious artifacts [S1].

    # Combine samples

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the depth of the binoctree and the number of intersected sphoxels. Pre-building the tree is an offline cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the number of nodes in the binoctree.
*   **Performance:** Yields more significant improvements in surface reconstruction accuracy compared to direct depth supervision (NeuS-facto-D) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Conservatively sets bounds upon depth, relying on other losses to optimize surface location, reducing inaccuracies from noisy depth inputs [S1]. Effective for small-baseline omnidirectional data [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to initial depth map errors; concave region reconstruction may be challenging due to optimization landscape minima [S1]. Requires postprocessing to remove artifacts from incorrect coarse voxel initialization [S1].

## Background
Adaptive Binoctree Sampling was introduced in the OmniSDF paper (2024) for scene reconstruction using omnidirectional signed distance functions. It addresses the challenges of optimizing neural implicit representations for large unbounded scenes from omnidirectional video [S1].
