# Bidirectional Projective Sampling

Also called: Bidirectional projection, Projective sampling with emitter-emitted seed rays

## Overview
Bidirectional Projective Sampling is a boundary sampling method for physics-based differentiable rendering that generates seed rays from both sensors and emitters to initialize a guiding structure, addressing inefficiencies when scenes contain small-area lights at a distance [S1]. It improves upon Zhang et al.'s projective sampling by incorporating rays emitted directly from small-area emitters into the seed ray set during the projection operation, ensuring all successfully projected rays contribute to the guiding structure regardless of emitter size [S1]. Unlike previous methods that rely solely on sensor-spawned rays, which have a near-zero probability of intersecting small-area emitters, this method guarantees intersection by explicitly sampling from the emitter, thereby maximizing contribution to the boundary integral [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry (triangular meshes), emitter definitions (triangular meshes with intensity/distance properties), sensor/camera parameters, differentiating geometry (the shape being optimized or differentiated), and a target number of seed rays $N$.
*   **Outputs:** An initialized guiding structure containing contributions to the boundary integral, ready for importance sampling.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Guiding Structure:** A data structure storing accumulated contributions from projected rays. The specific internal layout is not detailed in the sources, but it supports importance sampling of path segments [S1].
*   **Seed Rays:** Defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}$. Origins remain fixed during projection; directions are determined by the projection result [S1].

**Formulas:**
The method estimates the local formulation of the boundary integral derivative [S1]:
$$
\frac{\partial I}{\partial \pi} = \int_{\partial \mathcal{A}} \int_{S^2} L_i(\mathbf{x}_b, \boldsymbol{\omega}) W_i(\mathbf{x}_b, \boldsymbol{\omega}) \sin \phi \left(\frac{\partial \pi \mathbf{x}_b}{\partial \pi} \cdot \mathbf{n}_b\right) d\boldsymbol{\omega} dl(\mathbf{x}_b) + \int_{\mathcal{A}} \int_{S^1} L_i(\mathbf{x}_b, \phi) W_i(\mathbf{x}_b, \phi) \kappa(\phi) \left(\frac{\partial \pi \mathbf{x}_b}{\partial \pi} \cdot \mathbf{n}_b\right) d\phi dA(\mathbf{x}_b)
$$
Where:
*   $\pi$: Geometric parameters being differentiated.
*   $\mathcal{A}$: The differentiating geometry surface.
*   $\partial \mathcal{A}$: The boundary of the differentiating geometry.
*   $\mathbf{x}_b$: A point on the boundary.
*   $\boldsymbol{\omega}$: Direction in $S^2$.
*   $L_i$: Incident radiance.
*   $W_i$: Weight function.
*   $\phi$: Angle.
*   $\mathbf{n}_b$: Normal at $\mathbf{x}_b$.
*   $\kappa(\phi)$: Curvature-related term.

**Parameters:**
*   **Ray Split Ratio:** 30% of seed rays are emitted by emitters, 70% are spawned from the sensor [S1].
*   **Inverse Rendering Remeshing Interval:** Geometry is intermittently remeshed every 20 iterations [S1].
*   **Inverse Rendering Sample Doubling Interval:** The number of samples per pixel is doubled every 200 iterations [S1].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Assign Rays to Emitters:** Determine the number of rays each emitter must generate based on its intensity and distance from the differentiating shape. Closer and stronger emitters receive more rays [S1].
2.  **Sample Emitter Points:** For each emitter ray, sample a triangle on the emitter proportional to its area, then uniformly sample a point within that triangle to serve as the starting point (origin) for the seed ray [S1].
3.  **Sample Geometry Points:** Determine the endpoint of the seed ray by selecting a triangle on the shape being differentiated based on its area and uniformly sampling a point within that triangle [S1].
4.  **Generate Emitter Rays:** Create seed rays connecting the sampled emitter point to the sampled geometry point [S1].
5.  **Generate Sensor Rays:** Generate seed rays from the sensor side. These rays must directly hit the background plane without intersecting any other geometries as the first ray segment [S1].
6.  **Combine Rays:** Combine the emitter-emitted rays and sensor-spawned rays. Use the specified split ratio (30% emitter, 70% sensor) [S1].
7.  **Reflect Sensor Rays:** For sensor rays that hit the background plane, reflect them. These reflected rays affect the geometry being differentiated as the second ray segment [S1].
8.  **Project Rays:** Project the adjusted seed rays onto the geometric boundaries.
    *   Use JUMP and WALK strategies for triangular meshes to ensure tangency [S1].
    *   Origins remain fixed; directions are determined by the projection results [S1].
    *   Filter contributions: Only projected rays that intersect the scene emitter (small-area light) are kept to contribute to the guiding structure [S1]. Note: For emitter-emitted rays, all successfully projected rays contribute [S1].
9.  **Initialize Guiding Structure:** Compute the locations of the projected rays within the guiding structure and evaluate their contributions using the integrand in eq. (7) (the formula above) to initialize the guiding structure [S1].
10. **Importance Sampling:** Perform importance sampling of path segments from the initialized guiding structure, complete the segments into full paths, and evaluate their contributions to the boundary integral [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

def bidirectional_projective_sampling(scene, differentiating_geometry, num_seed_rays):
    """
    Initializes the guiding structure for differentiable rendering using bidirectional projective sampling.
    
    Args:
        scene: Scene object containing emitters and geometry.
        differentiating_geometry: The mesh/geometry being differentiated.
        num_seed_rays: Total number of seed rays to generate.
        
    Returns:
        guiding_structure: Initialized structure with accumulated contributions.
    """
    # Step 1: Assign rays to emitters based on intensity and distance
    emitter_ray_counts = assign_rays_to_emitters(scene.emitters, differentiating_geometry, num_seed_rays)
    
    emitter_rays = []
    # Step 2 & 3 & 4: Generate emitter-emitted seed rays
    for emitter, count in emitter_ray_counts.items():
        for _ in range(count):
            # Sample triangle on emitter proportional to area
            emitter_triangle = sample_triangle_by_area(emitter.triangles)
            # Uniformly sample point within triangle for origin
            origin = uniform_sample_triangle(emitter_triangle)
            
            # Sample triangle on differentiating geometry based on area
            geo_triangle = sample_triangle_by_area(differentiating_geometry.triangles)
            # Uniformly sample point within triangle for endpoint
            endpoint = uniform_sample_triangle(geo_triangle)
            
            # Generate ray from emitter to geometry
            direction = normalize(endpoint - origin)
            emitter_rays.append(Ray(origin, direction))
            
    # Step 5 & 6: Generate sensor-spawned seed rays and combine
    sensor_ray_count = int(num_seed_rays * 0.7)
    sensor_rays = generate_sensor_rays(scene.camera, scene.geometry, sensor_ray_count)
    
    # Combine rays: 30% emitter, 70% sensor
    # Note: The source specifies a split, implying the total count is fixed.
    # If emitter_rays count doesn't match 30%, adjust or scale.
    # Assuming the assignment in Step 1 ensures the count matches the 30% target.
    all_seed_rays = emitter_rays + sensor_rays
    
    # Step 7: Reflect sensor rays that hit background
    for i, ray in enumerate(all_seed_rays):
        if ray.origin is from_sensor and ray.intersects_background():
            all_seed_rays[i] = reflect_ray(ray, background_plane)
            
    # Step 8: Project rays onto geometric boundaries
    guiding_structure = empty_guiding_structure()
    
    for ray in all_seed_rays:
        # Project using JUMP and WALK strategies for tangency
        projected_ray = project_ray_onto_boundary(ray, differentiating_geometry)
        
        # Filter: Only keep if it intersects the scene emitter (for sensor rays)
        # For emitter rays, all successfully projected rays contribute [S1]
        if ray.origin is from_emitter or projected_ray.intersects_emitter(scene.emitters):
            # Step 9: Compute location and evaluate contribution
            contribution = evaluate_boundary_integral_contribution(
                projected_ray, 
                differentiating_geometry, 
                scene.emitters
            )
            guiding_structure.add(projected_ray.location, contribution)
            
    # Step 10: Importance sampling is performed later using the structure
    return guiding_structure

def assign_rays_to_emitters(emitters, geometry, total_rays):
    """
    Assigns ray counts to emitters based on intensity and distance.
    Closer and stronger emitters get more rays.
    """
    # Implementation detail: [G]
    # Calculate a weight for each emitter: weight = intensity / distance^2
    weights = []
    for emitter in emitters:
        dist = distance_to_geometry(emitter, geometry)
        intensity = emitter.intensity
        weights.append(intensity / (dist * dist))
        
    # Normalize weights to sum to 0.3 (30% of total rays)
    total_weight = sum(weights)
    target_emitter_rays = int(total_rays * 0.3)
    
    counts = []
    remaining = target_emitter_rays
    for i, weight in enumerate(weights):
        # Proportional allocation
        count = int(target_emitter_rays * (weight / total_weight))
        counts.append(count)
        remaining -= count
        
    # Distribute remainder to avoid loss
    for i in range(remaining):
        counts[i % len(counts)] += 1
        
    return dict(zip(emitters, counts))

    Selects a triangle proportional to its area.

    r = random.uniform(0, total_area)
    cumulative = 0
    for t, area in zip(triangles, areas):
        cumulative += area
        if r <= cumulative:
            return t
    return triangles[-1]

def uniform_sample_triangle(triangle):

    Uniformly samples a point within a triangle.

        v = 1 - v

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** The method achieves lower variance gradients in the forward differentiable rendering process and better geometry reconstruction quality in the inverse-rendering results compared to state-of-the-art projective-sampling-based methods [S1].
*   **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics card [S1].
*   **Intersection Queries:** Performed using OptiX [S1].
*   **Scaling:** The number of samples per pixel is doubled every 200 iterations during optimization [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Improves efficiency in scenes with small-area emitters (e.g., mobile phone flashlights) by ensuring rays intersect the emitter, which previous methods struggled with [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The underlying guiding structures for triangle meshes can still suffer from sparse and high-frequency features due to the 1D edge parameterization of the boundary sample space [S1].
*   **When to Use:** Use for differentiable rendering of geometry with small-area lights to improve guiding structure quality for importance sampling [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Not explicitly stated, but less beneficial for scenes with large-area emitters where sensor rays are sufficient [S1].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was proposed as an augmentation of previous projective-sampling-based boundary-sampling methods, specifically improving upon Zhang et al.'s method [S1].
*   **History:** It was developed to address the inefficiency of previous methods in scenes with small-area emitters, where the probability of sensor-spawned rays intersecting the emitter approaches zero as the emitter size approaches a point light [S1].
*   **Usage:** Implemented on the `cuda_ad_rgb` backend from Mitsuba 3 using the Dr. Jit framework for forward/reverse-mode AD and GPU kernel compilation [S1].
