# Multi-pass shaded isosurfaces

Also called: Shaded Isosurfaces via Multi-Pass, Westermann and Ertl

## Overview
Multi-pass shaded isosurfaces is a fast approach to display shaded isosurfaces introduced by Westermann and Ertl [S1, unofficial][S2, book]. It is distinguished from Dachille et al.'s one-pass method, which combines 3D texture hardware interpolation with software shading and classification, by utilizing multiple rasterization passes [S1, unofficial][S2, book].

## How it works
The method is described as a "fast multi-pass approach" [S1, unofficial][S2, book]. Specific algorithmic steps, data structures, formulas, and implementation details are not provided in the sources. Not given in the sources.

## Operating characteristics
Not given in the sources.

## Background
Westermann and Ertl introduced the method alongside other volume rendering techniques that emerged as advanced PC graphics hardware made texture-mapped volume rendering more accessible [S1, unofficial][S2, book]. The sources note that relying on rasterization hardware previously led to significant rendering performance loss, and contrast this approach with Dachille et al.'s one-pass technique and octree-based subdivision methods by Boada et al. and LaMar et al. [S1, unofficial][S2, book].
