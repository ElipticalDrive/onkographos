# Deferred Shading for Isosurfaces

Also called: Curvature Estimation, Deferred Shading with Differential Properties, Differential Surface Properties Computation, Gradient and Hessian Estimation, Image-Space Shading

## Overview
Deferred Shading for Isosurfaces is a multi-pass image-space technique that computes surface geometry and illumination for isosurfaces extracted from scalar volumes by decoupling shading from volume sampling [S1]. It solves the problem of high computational cost and memory bandwidth required for per-pixel lighting in ray-casted volumes by pre-computing differential properties (position, gradient, Hessian, curvature) into off-screen buffers and applying lighting only to visible pixels in a final pass [S1]. It sets itself apart from its siblings, such as [Surface shading for thresholded volumes](1159-surface-shading-for-thresholded-volumes.md), by explicitly computing second-order differential properties (Hessian and principal curvatures) via tri-cubic B-spline filtering in separate rendering passes, rather than relying on immediate gradient calculations or z-buffer slopes [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
The method operates as a pipeline of rendering passes, each writing a specific property to an off-screen pixel buffer (texture) [S1]. The complexity is independent of the grid size, depending only on the image resolution [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Input:** 3D scalar volume texture $V$, camera parameters, isovalue $\tau$.
*   **Intermediate Outputs:**
    *   Position image $\mathbf{p}$ (RGB floating point).
    *   Gradient image $\mathbf{g}$ (RGB floating point).
    *   Hessian image $\mathbf{H}$ (stored in two RGB floating point images).
    *   Curvature images: Principal curvatures $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$ and directions $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$ (stored in two floating point targets) [S1].
*   **Final Output:** Shaded color image [S1].

### Data Structures
*   **Position Image:** Stores the 3D intersection coordinates $(x, y, z)$ in the RGB channels of a floating-point texture [S1].
*   **Gradient Image:** Stores the partial derivatives $(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial z})$ in the RGB channels of a floating-point texture [S1].
*   **Hessian Image:** Stores the six unique second partial derivatives. Due to symmetry ($\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x}$, etc.), the components are stored across two RGB floating-point textures [S1].
*   **Curvature Images:** Store $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$ and direction vectors $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$ in floating-point targets [S1].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **Ray-Casting (Position):**
    *   Perform ray-casting for each pixel to find the first intersection with the isosurface $f(\mathbf{p}) = \tau$.
    *   Store the intersection position $\mathbf{p}$ in the Position Image [S1].
    *   *Note:* The parent article details the ray construction and intersection refinement. This pass assumes a valid hit is found [S1].

2.  **Gradient Computation:**
    *   Compute the gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla f$ in three rendering passes, one for each partial derivative component [S1].
    *   In each pass, evaluate a tri-cubic B-spline convolution sum to compute one first-order partial derivative [S1].
    *   Each pass requires eight texture fetches from the 3D volume texture and three fetches from 1D filter weight textures [S1].
    *   Store the resulting $\mathbf{g}$ in the Gradient Image [S1].

3.  **Hessian Computation:**
    *   Compute the Hessian $\mathbf{H} = \nabla \mathbf{g}$ in six rendering passes, one for each unique second partial derivative [S1].
    *   Each pass uses either eleven or fourteen texture fetches (from volume and 1D weight textures) [S1].
    *   Store the six coefficients in two RGB floating-point images [S1].

4.  **Curvature Computation:**
    *   Compute principal curvature magnitudes $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$ and directions $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$ in a single rendering pass [S1].
    *   Inputs are the Gradient Image $\mathbf{g}$ and Hessian Image $\mathbf{H}$ [S1].
    *   Calculate the shape operator $\mathbf{S}$, defined as the tangent space projection of the normalized Hessian:
        $$ \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{P}^T \frac{\mathbf{H}}{|\mathbf{g}|} \mathbf{P} $$
        where $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{I} - \frac{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^T}{|\mathbf{g}|^2}$ is the projection matrix onto the tangent plane, and $|\mathbf{g}|$ is the magnitude of the gradient [S1].
    *   Let $\mathbf{A}$ be the $2 \times 2$ matrix representation of $\mathbf{S}$ in the tangent basis. Compute the principal curvatures:
        $$ \kappa_{1,2} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \text{trace}(\mathbf{A}) \pm \sqrt{\text{trace}(\mathbf{A})^2 - 4 \det(\mathbf{A})} \right) $$
        [S1]
    *   Compute the principal directions $\mathbf{e}_i$ using the formula:
        $$ \mathbf{e}_i = \kappa_i \mathbf{u} + (\kappa_i + a_{22} - a_{11}) \mathbf{v} $$
        where $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ are the basis vectors of the tangent plane and $a_{11}, a_{22}$ are diagonal elements of $\mathbf{A}$ [S1].
    *   Store $\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2$ in the curvature targets [S1].

5.  **Shading:**
    *   Render the shaded isosurface to the viewport using the property images as inputs [S1].
    *   Apply lighting models (e.g., Blinn-Phong) using the normal vector $\mathbf{n} = \pm \mathbf{g}/|\mathbf{g}|$ derived from the Gradient Image [S1].
    *   Optional effects:
        *   **Curvature Color Mapping:** Map $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$, mean curvature $(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)/2$, or Gaussian curvature $\kappa_1\kappa_2$ to colors via 1D or 2D lookup textures [S1].
        *   **Accessibility Shading:** Darken areas with large negative maximum curvature using a 1D or 2D curvature transfer function [S1].
        *   **Curvature-Aligned Flow Advection:** Project 3D curvature direction vectors to the 2D image plane and advect flow on a per-pixel basis [S1].
        *   **Non-Photorealistic Effects:** Silhouette outlining controlled by curvature, or ridge/valley line depiction specified in the $(\kappa_1, \kappa_2)$ domain [S1].

### Pseudocode

```python
# Pass 1: Ray-Casting (Position)
# Assumes parent method provides intersection position p for each pixel
def pass_ray_casting(volume, camera, isovalue):
    for each pixel (u, v):
        ray = camera.construct_ray(u, v)
        hit = ray_cast_intersection(ray, volume, isovalue)
        if hit.valid:
            position_image[u, v] = hit.position
        else:
            position_image[u, v] = (0, 0, 0) # Or transparent

# Pass 2: Gradient Computation (3 passes)
def pass_gradient(volume, position_image):
    # Compute one component of gradient per pass
    for component in [0, 1, 2]: # x, y, z
        for each pixel (u, v):
            p = position_image[u, v]
            # Evaluate tri-cubic B-spline convolution sum for partial derivative
            # Requires 8 fetches from volume, 3 from 1D weight textures
            grad_val = evaluate_tricubic_partial_derivative(volume, p, component)
            gradient_image[u, v][component] = grad_val

# Pass 3: Hessian Computation (6 passes)
def pass_hessian(volume, position_image):
    # Compute 6 unique second partial derivatives
    hessian_components = [
        (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2), # d2/dx2, d2/dy2, d2/dz2
        (0, 1), (0, 2), (1, 2)  # d2/dxdy, d2/dxdz, d2/dydz
    ]
    for i, (c1, c2) in enumerate(hessian_components):
        for each pixel (u, v):
            p = position_image[u, v]
            # Evaluate tri-cubic B-spline convolution sum for second partial derivative
            # Requires 11 or 14 fetches depending on implementation
            hess_val = evaluate_tricubic_second_partial_derivative(volume, p, c1, c2)
            # Store in two RGB images
            if i < 3:
                hessian_image_1[u, v][i] = hess_val
            else:
                hessian_image_2[u, v][i-3] = hess_val

# Pass 4: Curvature Computation
def pass_curvature(gradient_image, hessian_image_1, hessian_image_2):
    # Combine Hessian components from two images
    H = combine_hessian_images(hessian_image_1, hessian_image_2)
    
    for each pixel (u, v):
        g = gradient_image[u, v]
        mag_g = length(g)
        if mag_g < epsilon:
            curvature_image[u, v] = (0, 0, 0, 0) # No curvature if no gradient
            continue
        
        # Compute projection matrix P
        # P = I - (g * g^T) / |g|^2
        P = identity_matrix(3) - outer_product(g, g) / (mag_g * mag_g)
        
        # Compute Shape Operator S = P^T * (H / |g|) * P
        # Note: H is 3x3, P is 3x3
        H_norm = H / mag_g
        S = transpose(P) @ (H_norm @ P)
        
        # Project S onto tangent basis to get 2x2 matrix A
        # Basis vectors u, v are orthogonal to g
        # This step requires constructing the basis from g
        u, v = construct_tangent_basis(g)
        
        # A_ij = u_i^T S u_j
        A = matrix(2, 2)
        A[0, 0] = dot(u, S @ u)
        A[0, 1] = dot(u, S @ v)
        A[1, 0] = dot(v, S @ u)
        A[1, 1] = dot(v, S @ v)
        
        # Compute eigenvalues (principal curvatures)
        tr_A = A[0, 0] + A[1, 1]
        det_A = A[0, 0] * A[1, 1] - A[0, 1] * A[1, 0]
        discriminant = tr_A * tr_A - 4 * det_A
        
        if discriminant < 0:
            kappa1 = 0
            kappa2 = 0
        else:
            sqrt_disc = sqrt(discriminant)
            kappa1 = 0.5 * (tr_A + sqrt_disc)
            kappa2 = 0.5 * (tr_A - sqrt_disc)
            
        # Compute principal directions in 3D
        # e_i = kappa_i * u + (kappa_i + A[1,1] - A[0,0]) * v
        e1 = kappa1 * u + (kappa1 + A[1, 1] - A[0, 0]) * v
        e2 = kappa2 * u + (kappa2 + A[1, 1] - A[0, 0]) * v
        
        curvature_image[u, v] = (kappa1, kappa2, e1, e2)

# Pass 5: Shading
def pass_shading(position_image, gradient_image, curvature_image, light_positions, light_colors):
    for each pixel (u, v):
        p = position_image[u, v]
        g = gradient_image[u, v]
        kappa1, kappa2, e1, e2 = curvature_image[u, v]
        
        # Normal
        n = normalize(g)
        
        # Lighting (e.g., Blinn-Phong)
        color = ambient_light
        for light in lights:
            L = normalize(light.position - p)
            V = normalize(camera.position - p)
            H = normalize(L + V)
            diff = max(0, dot(n, L))
            spec = pow(max(0, dot(n, H)), shininess)
            color += light.color * (diff * diffuse_coeff + spec * specular_coeff)
            
        # Optional: Curvature-based effects
        # e.g., map kappa1 to color or adjust opacity
        final_color = apply_curvature_mapping(color, kappa1, kappa2)
        
        output_image[u, v] = final_color
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **Filter:** Tri-cubic B-spline convolution [S1].
*   **Texture Fetches:** 8 volume + 3 1D weights for gradient; 11 or 14 for Hessian [S1].
*   **Floating Point Precision:** Used for intermediate images (position, gradient, Hessian, curvature) [S1].
*   **Shading Model:** Blinn-Phong, tone shading [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Zero Gradient:** If $|\mathbf{g}| \approx 0$, the normal and shape operator are undefined. The implementation should handle this, e.g., by setting curvature to zero [S1].
*   **Flow Advection Seams:** Advecting flow along curvature directions may produce visible seams because the orientation of curvature direction vectors is not uniquely defined [S1].
*   **Precision:** High-order derivatives are sensitive to noise in the volume data. Tri-cubic filtering helps but does not eliminate sensitivity [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Surface shading for thresholded volumes](1159-surface-shading-for-thresholded-volumes.md)
Applies lighting to isosurfaces by determining geometry and illumination in image space or via rasterization, distinct from direct volume rendering. It sets itself apart from Deferred Shading for Isosurfaces by avoiding the high memory and computational costs of per-ray cell storage or repeated neighbor-finding, often relying on z-buffer slopes or precomputed gradients rather than deferred multi-pass normal accumulation.

### [Normal-based Contextual Shading](6617-normal-based-contextual-shading.md)
An object-space shading technique applied to isosurfaces to achieve higher visual quality than image-space depth-gradient shading. It solves the problem of inaccurate surface normals in ray-casted volumes by fitting a local surface approximation to the set of closest intersection points, thereby estimating surface inclination more robustly. It sets itself apart from its sibling [Surface shading for thresholded volumes](1159-surface-shading-for-thresholded-volumes.md) by explicitly using a larger voxel neighborhood to fit a function (linear or biquadratic) to the iso-surface points, rather than relying solely on immediate gradient calculations or thresholded binary data.

### Deferred Shading for Volume Rendering
A rendering pipeline used in conjunction with raycasting-based rasterization of elliptical splats [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Independent of grid size; depends on image resolution and number of passes (3 for gradient, 6 for Hessian, 1 for curvature, 1 for shading) [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for multiple floating-point images (position, gradient, Hessian, curvature) [S1].
*   **Performance:** Moderate number of vector and matrix multiplications, solving a quadratic polynomial, and texture instructions per pixel [S1].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality shading and curvature visualization; decoupled shading allows for complex lighting effects; image-space complexity [S1].
*   **Limitations:** High memory bandwidth usage due to multiple passes and large floating-point textures; seams in flow advection [S1].
*   **When to Use:** When high-quality surface shading and curvature analysis are required for large volumes, and memory bandwidth is not the primary bottleneck [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When memory is constrained or real-time performance is critical and simpler shading methods suffice [S1].

## Background
Originated in the paper "Real-Time Ray-Casting and Advanced Shading of Discrete Isosurfaces" by Christoph Schüller, Daniel Weiskopf, and Thomas Ertl (2005) [S1]. The method was developed to provide high-quality shading and differential property visualization for isosurfaces extracted from scalar volumes, leveraging GPU capabilities for deferred shading [S1]. It is used in scientific visualization for analyzing the shape and structure of isosurfaces, such as in medical imaging or fluid dynamics [S1].
