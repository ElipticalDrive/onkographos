# Hybrid Rendering

Also called: Vosh rendering

## Overview
Hybrid rendering combines volume ray marching with surface rasterization to optimize performance and fidelity. It solves the problem of redundant computation in regions where high-fidelity surface geometry is available by restricting volume integration to the space between the camera and the nearest surface intersection [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike pure volume rendering, it leverages mesh depth to terminate ray marching early, and unlike pure surface rendering, it retains volumetric effects (such as subsurface scattering or semi-transparency) in the foreground [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Camera ray $r(t) = o + td$.
*   Voxel grid or neural volume representation (providing density $\sigma$ and color $c$).
*   Surface mesh (providing intersection geometry).
*   Surface shading model (providing diffuse color $c_{d,m}$ and view-dependent features $f_m$).

**Outputs:**
*   Final hybrid color $C_{vosh}$.
*   Diffuse color $C_{vosh,d}$.
*   View-dependent features $F_{vosh}$.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Weights:** Cumulative transmittance weights $w_i$ accumulated during ray marching.
*   **Mesh Weight:** $w_m$, representing the remaining transmittance reaching the surface.
*   **Surface Attributes:** Diffuse color $c_{d,m}$ and view-dependent features $f_m$ at the intersection point.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray-Mesh Intersection:** For each pixel, cast a ray and determine the nearest intersection with the surface mesh. Let $t_{max}$ be the distance to this intersection. If no intersection is found, $t_{max}$ is set to the far clipping plane or volume boundary [S2].
2.  **Volume Ray Marching:** Perform volume rendering (ray marching) along the ray from $t_{min}$ (near clip) to $t_{max}$. Accumulate color and weights using standard volume integration [S1, unofficial][S2].
    *   Let $C_{vol}$ be the accumulated volume color.
    *   Let $W_{vol} = \sum_i w_i$ be the sum of voxel weights (opacity) accumulated up to $t_{max}$.
3.  **Calculate Mesh Weight:** Compute the weight of the surface contribution as the remaining transmittance:
    $$ w_m = 1 - W_{vol} $$
    This represents the portion of light that passed through the volume without being absorbed or scattered [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Surface Contribution:** Retrieve the surface color and features at the intersection point $(t_{max})$.
    *   Diffuse color: $c_{d,m}$
    *   View-dependent features: $f_m$
5.  **Hybrid Compositing:** Combine the volume and surface contributions.
    *   The hybrid diffuse color $C_{vosh,d}$ is a weighted sum of the volume diffuse color and the surface diffuse color:
        $$ C_{vosh,d} = \sum_i w_i c_{d,i} + w_m c_{d,m} $$
        where $c_{d,i}$ is the diffuse color contribution of the $i$-th voxel step [S1, unofficial].
    *   The hybrid view-dependent features $F_{vosh}$ are similarly blended:
        $$ F_{vosh} = \sum_i w_i f_i + w_m f_m $$
        where $f_i$ are the view-dependent features from the volume steps [S1, unofficial].
    *   The final color $C_{vosh}$ is derived from $C_{vosh,d}$ and $F_{vosh}$ using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that models view-dependent effects (e.g., specular highlights):
        $$ C_{vosh} = C_{vosh,d} + \text{MLP}(C_{vosh,d}, F_{vosh}, d) $$
        where $d$ is the view direction [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def hybrid_render(ray_origin, ray_direction, mesh, volume, mlp):
    # Step 1: Find surface intersection
    t_max = mesh.intersect(ray_origin, ray_direction)
    if t_max is None:
        t_max = far_clipping_plane

    # Step 2: Volume Ray Marching from t_min to t_max
    vol_color_diffuse = 0.0
    vol_features = 0.0
    vol_opacity = 0.0
    t = t_min
    dt = step_size
    
    while t < t_max:
        # Sample volume at current position
        density, color_diffuse, features = volume.sample(ray_origin + t * ray_direction)
        
        # Accumulate
        weight = 1.0 - exp(-density * dt)
        vol_opacity += weight * (1.0 - vol_opacity) # Standard alpha compositing weight accumulation
        
        # Additive contribution to color/features
        vol_color_diffuse += weight * (1.0 - vol_opacity) * color_diffuse # Approximation of standard DVR
        vol_features += weight * (1.0 - vol_opacity) * features
        
        t += dt

    # Step 3: Calculate Mesh Weight
    # w_m is the transmittance reaching the surface
    w_m = 1.0 - vol_opacity
    
    # Step 4: Get Surface Attributes
    surface_pos = ray_origin + t_max * ray_direction
    mesh_color_diffuse, mesh_features = mesh.get_attributes(surface_pos)
    
    # Step 5: Hybrid Compositing
    # Blend volume and surface contributions
    final_diffuse = vol_color_diffuse + w_m * mesh_color_diffuse
    final_features = vol_features + w_m * mesh_features
    
    # Apply view-dependent shading via MLP
    final_color = final_diffuse + mlp(final_diffuse, final_features, ray_direction)
    
    return final_color
```

**Parameters:**
*   $t_{min}$: Near clipping plane distance.
*   $t_{max}$: Distance to nearest surface intersection.
*   $dt$: Step size for volume ray marching.
*   MLP: A neural network mapping diffuse color, view-dependent features, and view direction to final color.

**Edge Cases:**
*   **No Intersection:** If the ray does not hit the mesh, $t_{max}$ defaults to the volume boundary, resulting in pure volume rendering [S2].
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision errors at $t_{max}$ may cause gaps or overlaps between volume and surface. [G] Ensure consistent depth bias or epsilon checks when sampling the surface.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_v + N_s)$, where $N_v$ is the number of volume samples (reduced by early termination at $t_{max}$) and $N_s$ is the cost of mesh intersection and shading [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the volume representation (voxel grid or neural network weights) and mesh storage.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces ray marching cost in regions with high-fidelity meshes [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High fidelity in surface regions, efficient rendering of complex scenes with both detailed surfaces and volumetric details [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a valid mesh representation; discontinuities may occur at the boundary between volume and surface if not blended carefully [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Scenes with mixed geometry (e.g., characters with detailed clothing meshes and volumetric hair/fur) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When not to use:** Purely volumetric scenes without surface geometry [S2].

## Background
Hybrid rendering was introduced to leverage the strengths of both volume and surface representations. The specific "Vosh" (Voxel-Mesh Hybrid) formulation was proposed in 2024 for real-time view synthesis, aiming to reduce time costs associated with ray marching by confining it to the space before the surface [S1, unofficial]. The concept of using mesh depth to limit volume integration was also explored in the context of Neural Volumes (2019) to avoid expending representational power on areas already covered by high-fidelity meshes [S2].
