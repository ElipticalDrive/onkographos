# Hybrid Rendering

Also called: Vosh rendering

## Overview
Hybrid rendering combines volume rendering (ray marching) and surface rendering (rasterization) to optimize computational cost and representation fidelity [S1, unofficial][S2]. It restricts ray marching to the region between the viewpoint and a pre-existing mesh surface, using the mesh intersection to terminate volume integration and fill remaining color throughput [S1, unofficial][S2]. This approach is distinct from pure volume or pure surface methods by leveraging the mesh for high-fidelity geometry where available and the volume for complex, occluded, or semi-transparent regions [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A voxel grid (or neural volume representation), a mesh representation (vertices, faces, textures), and a ray origin/direction.
*   **Outputs:** Final color $C_{vosh}$, diffuse color $C_{vosh,d}$, and view-dependent features $F_{vosh}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** Stores per-voxel weights $w_i$ and colors $c_{d,i}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Mesh:** Stores surface geometry and intersection properties (color $c_{d,m}$, features $f_m$) [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray-Mesh Intersection:** For each ray, determine if it intersects the mesh. If it does, record the intersection distance $t_{max}$ [S2].
2.  **Volume Rendering (Ray Marching):** Perform standard volume rendering (ray marching) along the ray.
    *   If the ray intersects the mesh, limit the marching range to $t_{max}$ [S2].
    *   Accumulate voxel weights $w_i$ and colors $c_{d,i}$ for all voxels traversed up to $t_{max}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   If the ray does not intersect the mesh, perform full volume rendering to the far plane [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Mesh Weight Calculation:** Calculate the mesh weight $w_m$ as the remaining transmittance after volume integration [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ w_m = 1 - \sum_i w_i $$
    where $\sum_i w_i$ is the sum of weights of all voxels rendered along the ray [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Color and Feature Composition:** Combine the volume contribution and the mesh contribution [S1, unofficial]:
    *   Diffuse color:
        $$ C_{vosh,d} = \sum_i w_i c_{d,i} + w_m c_{d,m} $$
    *   View-dependent features:
        $$ F_{vosh} = \sum_i w_i f_i + w_m f_m $$
    *   Final color (with optional MLP refinement):
        $$ C_{vosh} = C_{vosh,d} + \text{MLP}(C_{vosh,d}, F_{vosh}, d) $$
    where $c_{d,m}$ and $f_m$ are the diffuse color and view-dependent features at the mesh intersection point, and $d$ is the depth or other positional data [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $t_{max}$: Set to the mesh depth for intersecting rays [S2].
*   MLP: A multi-layer perceptron used to refine the final color based on diffuse color, features, and depth [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Ensure accurate ray-mesh intersection to correctly define $t_{max}$ [S2].
*   **Occlusion:** The volume representation should naturally avoid occluding the mesh in areas where the mesh provides higher fidelity [S2].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the mesh is incomplete, rays may not intersect, falling back to pure volume rendering [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduced compared to full volume rendering because ray marching is truncated at the mesh surface [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for both voxel grid and mesh [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Strengths:** High fidelity for geometrically simple regions (handled by mesh) and complex/semi-transparent regions (handled by volume) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a pre-existing or simultaneously trained mesh [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When to Use:** Real-time view synthesis where both geometry and texture detail are critical [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** Scenes with no clear surface structure or where mesh acquisition is prohibitive [S2].

## Background
Hybrid rendering was introduced in the context of Voxel-Mesh Hybrid Representation for Real-Time View Synthesis [S1, unofficial] and Neural Volumes [S2]. It addresses the limitations of pure volume rendering (high computational cost) and pure mesh rendering (lack of semi-transparency) by combining both [S1, unofficial][S2].
