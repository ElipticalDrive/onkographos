# Hybrid Rendering

Also called: Vosh rendering

## Overview
Hybrid Rendering combines volume rendering (ray marching) and surface rendering (rasterization) to optimize computational cost and visual fidelity [S1, unofficial]. It confines ray marching to the region between the viewpoint and a pre-existing mesh surface, treating the mesh intersection as the termination point for volume integration [S1, unofficial]. This approach leverages the mesh for high-fidelity geometry and texture where available, while using the volume to represent complex details or occlusions in front of the surface [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   A voxel grid representing a semi-transparent volume (density $\sigma$, color $c$).
*   A triangular mesh representing the surface geometry.
*   Camera parameters (origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$).

**Outputs:**
*   Final color $C_{vosh}$, diffuse color $C_{vosh,d}$, and view-dependent features $F_{vosh}$ for each pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Grid:** Standard 3D grid storing per-voxel density and color attributes.
*   **Mesh:** Triangle soup with vertex positions and texture coordinates.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray-Mesh Intersection:** For each pixel, cast a ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$. Intersect the ray with the mesh to find the intersection distance $t_{mesh}$. If no intersection is found, $t_{mesh} = \infty$ [S2].
2.  **Ray Marching:** March along the ray from $t=0$ to $t=t_{mesh}$. Sample the volume density $\sigma$ and color $c$ at each step. Compute the accumulated transmittance $T$ and the accumulated color contribution $C_{vol}$ using standard volume rendering integration [S1, unofficial].
    *   Note: If $t_{mesh} = \infty$, march until the volume boundary or a maximum distance [S2].
3.  **Mesh Sampling:** If $t_{mesh} < \infty$, sample the mesh at the intersection point to obtain the mesh color $c_m$ and diffuse color $c_{d,m}$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Weight Calculation:** Calculate the mesh weight $w_m$ as the remaining transmittance after volume integration:
    $$ w_m = 1 - \sum_i w_i $$
    where $w_i$ are the weights of the sampled voxels [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Color Composition:** Combine the volume contribution and the mesh contribution. The final color $C_{vosh}$ is computed as:
    $$ C_{vosh} = C_{vosh,d} + \text{MLP}(C_{vosh,d}, F_{vosh}, d) $$
    where $C_{vosh,d}$ is the diffuse color component:
    $$ C_{vosh,d} = \sum_i w_i c_{d,i} + w_m c_{d,m} $$
    and $F_{vosh}$ is the view-dependent feature component:
    $$ F_{vosh} = \sum_i w_i f_i + w_m f_m $$
    Here, $c_{d,i}$ and $f_i$ are the diffuse color and view-dependent features of the $i$-th voxel, while $c_{d,m}$ and $f_m$ are the corresponding values from the mesh [S1, unofficial]. The term $\text{MLP}(\cdot)$ represents a multi-layer perceptron that refines the final color based on the diffuse color, view-dependent features, and view direction $d$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **No Mesh Intersection:** If the ray does not intersect the mesh, the volume rendering proceeds normally until the volume boundary [S2].
*   **Mesh Occlusion:** The mesh effectively occludes the volume behind it. The volume representation should not contain density in regions fully covered by the mesh to avoid double-counting [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Reduced compared to full volume rendering because ray marching is truncated at the mesh surface [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for both the voxel grid and the mesh [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High performance for scenes with simple geometry (well-represented by mesh) and complex details (represented by volume) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a pre-existing or simultaneously trained mesh representation [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Hybrid Rendering was introduced in the context of real-time view synthesis, specifically in the "Voxel-Mesh Hybrid Representation" method [S1, unofficial]. It aims to reduce the computational cost of ray marching by leveraging mesh rasterization for surface points [S1, unofficial]. The concept of setting $t_{max}$ to mesh depth for intersecting rays was also discussed in the context of Neural Volumes [S2].
