# Hybrid Rendering

Also called: Vosh rendering

## Overview
Hybrid rendering combines volume ray marching with surface rasterization to optimize performance and fidelity [S1, unofficial][S2]. It solves the problem of redundant computation in regions where high-fidelity surface geometry is available by restricting volume integration to the space between the camera and the nearest surface intersection [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike pure volume rendering, it leverages mesh depth to terminate ray marching early, and unlike pure surface rendering, it retains volumetric effects in the foreground [S1, unofficial][S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Camera ray $r(t) = o + td$.
*   Volume representation providing density $\sigma$, diffuse color $c_d$, and view-dependent features $f$ at any point [S1, unofficial].
*   Surface mesh providing intersection geometry and shading attributes [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   Near clipping distance $t_{min}$ and step size $dt$ [G].

**Outputs:**
*   Final hybrid color $C_{vosh}$.
*   Diffuse color $C_{vosh,d}$.
*   View-dependent features $F_{vosh}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel Weights:** $w_i$, the accumulated alpha weight at step $i$ during ray marching [S1, unofficial].
*   **Mesh Weight:** $w_m$, representing the remaining transmittance reaching the surface [S1, unofficial].
*   **Surface Attributes:** Diffuse color $c_{d,m}$ and view-dependent features $f_m$ at the intersection point [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray-Mesh Intersection:** For each pixel, cast a ray and determine the nearest intersection with the surface mesh [S1, unofficial][S2]. Let $t_{max}$ be the distance to this intersection [S1, unofficial][S2]. If no intersection is found, $t_{max}$ defaults to the volume boundary [G].
2.  **Volume Ray Marching:** Perform volume rendering along the ray from $t_{min}$ to $t_{max}$ [S1, unofficial]. Accumulate color and weights using standard volumetric integration [S1, unofficial][S2].
3.  **Calculate Mesh Weight:** Compute the weight of the surface contribution as the remaining transmittance:
    $$ w_m = 1 - \sum_i w_i $$
    This represents the portion of light that passed through the volume without being absorbed or scattered [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Surface Contribution:** Retrieve the surface color and features at the intersection point $(t_{max})$ [S1, unofficial][S2].
5.  **Hybrid Compositing:** Combine the volume and surface contributions:
    $$ C_{vosh,d} = \sum_i w_i c_{d,i} + w_m c_{d,m} $$
    $$ F_{vosh} = \sum_i w_i f_i + w_m f_m $$
    where $c_{d,i}$ and $f_i$ are the diffuse color and view-dependent features from the $i$-th volume step [S1, unofficial]. The final color is derived using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) that models view-dependent effects:
    $$ C_{vosh} = C_{vosh,d} + \text{MLP}(C_{vosh,d}, F_{vosh}, d) $$
    where $d$ is the ray direction passed to the MLP [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def hybrid_render(ray_origin, ray_direction, t_min, dt, mesh, volume, mlp):
    # Step 1: Find surface intersection
    t_max = mesh.intersect(ray_origin, ray_direction)
    if t_max is None:
        t_max = 1000.0  # Default far clipping plane [G]

    # Step 2: Volume Ray Marching
    T = 1.0
    C_diffuse = 0.0
    F = 0.0
    W_vol = 0.0
    t = t_min
    while t < t_max:
        pos = ray_origin + t * ray_direction
        density, c_d, f = volume.sample(pos)
        alpha = 1.0 - exp(-density * dt)
        w_i = T * alpha
        W_vol += w_i
        T *= (1.0 - alpha)
        C_diffuse += w_i * c_d
        F += w_i * f
        t += dt

    # Step 3: Calculate Mesh Weight
    w_m = 1.0 - W_vol

    # Step 4: Get Surface Attributes
    surface_pos = ray_origin + t_max * ray_direction
    mesh_color_diffuse, mesh_features = mesh.get_attributes(surface_pos)

    # Step 5: Hybrid Compositing
    final_diffuse = C_diffuse + w_m * mesh_color_diffuse
    final_features = F + w_m * mesh_features
    final_color = final_diffuse + mlp(final_diffuse, final_features, ray_direction)
    
    return final_color
```

**Parameters:**
*   $t_{min}$: Near clipping plane distance [G].
*   $t_{max}$: Distance to nearest surface intersection [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $dt$: Step size for volume ray marching [G].
*   MLP: A neural network mapping diffuse color, view-dependent features, and a direction variable to final color [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision errors at $t_{max}$ may cause gaps or overlaps between volume and surface [G]. Ensure consistent depth bias or epsilon checks when sampling the surface [G].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_v + N_s)$, where $N_v$ is the number of volume samples (reduced by early termination at $t_{max}$) and $N_s$ is the cost of mesh intersection and shading [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G] Depends on the volume representation (voxel grid or neural network weights) and mesh storage.
*   **Performance:** Significantly reduces ray marching cost in regions with high-fidelity meshes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Hybrid rendering was introduced to leverage the strengths of both volume and surface representations [S1, unofficial]. The specific "Vosh" (Voxel-Mesh Hybrid) formulation was proposed in 2024 for real-time view synthesis, aiming to reduce time costs associated with ray marching by confining it to the space before the surface [S1, unofficial]. The concept of using mesh depth to limit volume integration was also explored in the context of Neural Volumes (2019) to avoid expending representational power on areas already covered by high-fidelity meshes [S2].
