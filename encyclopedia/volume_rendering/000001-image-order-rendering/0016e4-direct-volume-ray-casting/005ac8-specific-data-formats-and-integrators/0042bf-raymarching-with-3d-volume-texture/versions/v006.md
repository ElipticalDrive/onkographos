# Raymarching with 3D Volume Texture

Also called: 3D Texture Raycasting, 3D texture raymarching, Ray tracing through 3D mesh, RayMarchedAtlasVolume, Wolfenstein-style 3D raycasting

## Overview
This method performs direct volume ray casting by representing the voxel space as a `sampler3D` texture and executing the ray marching loop within the fragment shader [S2, unofficial]. It replaces previous texture atlas approaches, allowing for larger XYZ volumes, simpler edge handling, and a unified in-memory representation shared with path tracing and isosurface modes [S2, unofficial]. Unlike meshing methods, it avoids the overhead of generating and uploading geometry via a single draw call [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   **Volume Data:** A 3D texture (`volumeTexture`) containing voxel intensities [S2, unofficial].
*   **Lookup Table:** A 2D texture (`gLutTexture`) mapping intensity to color/alpha [S2, unofficial].
*   **Camera/Scene:** Ray origin, direction (`eye_d`), near/far clip planes (`tnear`, `tfar`), and volume scale (`volumeScale`) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** `BREAK_STEPS`, `maxSteps`, `gNChannels`, `SLICES`, and `maskAlpha` [S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   **Fragment Color:** The final composited color and alpha for each pixel [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
**CPU-side Data Buffer Layout:**
The volume data is stored in a 1D array representing a 3D grid with up to 4 channels. The layout is defined as:
$$ \text{data}[i + ix \cdot 4 + iy \cdot 4 \cdot sx + iz \cdot 4 \cdot sx \cdot sy] $$
Where:
*   $i$ is the channel index ($0$–$3$) [S2, unofficial].
*   $ix, iy, iz$ are the voxel coordinates [S2, unofficial].
*   $sx, sy$ are the width and height of the volume [S2, unofficial].
*   Raw intensities are normalized to the $0$–$255$ range before upload [S2, unofficial].

**GPU-side Textures:**
1.  **`volumeTexture`**: A `Data3DTexture` requiring WebGL2 [S2, unofficial]. Holds the $8$-bit voxel intensities [S2, unofficial].
2.  **`gLutTexture`**: A `DataTexture` of size $256 \times 4$ [S2, unofficial]. It contains a combined Lookup Table (LUT) for up to $4$ active channels, mapping intensity to RGBA [S2, unofficial].

### Numbered Steps

1.  **CPU Data Preparation:**
    *   Iterate through all active channels ($0$ to $gNChannels - 1$) [S2, unofficial].
    *   For each voxel $(ix, iy, iz)$, normalize the raw intensity to the $0$–$255$ range and store it in the buffer at the calculated offset [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Masking (if enabled):** If a mask channel is specified and `maskAlpha < 1.0`, iterate through the volume again [S2, unofficial]. For each voxel, determine `maskVal` ($1.0$ if mask intensity $> 0$, else `maskAlpha`) and multiply the existing intensity value by `maskVal` [S2, unofficial].
    *   Upload the buffer to the `volumeTexture` [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Vertex Shader (Setup):**
    *   Render a single quad covering the screen [S1, unofficial].
    *   Pass ray direction (`eye_d`) and origin to the fragment shader [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Fragment Shader (Ray Marching):**
    *   **Calculate Step Count:** Compute the scaled steps based on the ray length and volume scale:
        $$ \text{scaledSteps} = \text{BREAK\_STEPS} \times \left\| \frac{\text{eye\_d.xyz}}{\text{volumeScale}} \right\| $$
    *   **Clamp Steps:** Clamp the step count to the valid range:
        $$ \text{csteps} = \text{clamp}(\text{scaledSteps}, 1.0, \text{maxSteps}) $$
    *   **Calculate Inverse Step Size:**
        $$ \text{invstep} = \frac{\text{tfar} - \text{tnear}}{\text{csteps}} $$
        Where `tfar` and `tnear` are the far and near clip distances [S2, unofficial].
    *   **Ray Dithering:** Compute a random offset to reduce banding:
        $$ r = \text{rand}(\text{eye\_d.xy}) $$
        *Note: The original implementation disabled dithering for single-slice volumes ($SLICES == 1.0$) to prevent Moiré patterns, but the current implementation unconditionally applies dithering [S2, unofficial].*
    *   **Marching Loop:**
        For $k = 0$ to $\text{csteps} - 1$:
        1.  Compute current ray position:
            $$ \text{pos} = \text{origin} + (\text{tnear} + (k + r) \cdot \text{invstep}) \cdot \text{direction} $$
        2.  Sample the volume texture:
            $$ \text{intensity} = \text{sampler3D}(\text{volumeTexture}, \text{pos}) $$
        3.  Reconstruct Color/Alpha:
            Use the sampled intensity and `gNChannels` to index into `gLutTexture` and retrieve the RGBA value [S2, unofficial].
        4.  Composite:
            Perform front-to-back alpha blending using the retrieved RGBA and the accumulated result [S2, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def clamp(val, min_val, max_val):
    return min(max(val, min_val), max_val)

def rand(vec):
    # Deterministic pseudo-random hash for per-pixel dither
    return (sin(dot(vec, vec(12.9898, 78.233))) * 43758.5453) % 1.0

def ray_march(origin, direction, tnear, tfar, volume_texture, lut_texture, break_steps, volume_scale, max_steps, n_channels):
    # 1. Calculate Step Count
    scaled_steps = break_steps * length(direction / volume_scale)
    csteps = clamp(scaled_steps, 1.0, max_steps)
    
    # 2. Calculate Inverse Step Size
    invstep = (tfar - tnear) / csteps
    
    # 3. Ray Dithering
    r = rand(direction.xy)
    
    acc_color = vec(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    acc_alpha = 0.0
    
    # 4. Marching Loop
    for k in range(int(csteps)):
        t = tnear + (k + r) * invstep
        pos = origin + t * direction
        
        # 5. Sample Volume (pos assumed normalized to [0,1])
        intensity = texture(volume_texture, pos).r
        
        # 6. Reconstruct Color/Alpha from LUT
        # LUT is 256x4. Map intensity (0-255) to x, channel index to y.
        lut_u = intensity / 255.0
        lut_v = n_channels / 4.0
        lut_entry = texture(lut_texture, vec(lut_u, lut_v))
        
        # 7. Front-to-Back Compositing
        alpha = lut_entry.a
        color = lut_entry.rgb * alpha
        acc_color = acc_color + color * (1.0 - acc_alpha)
        acc_alpha = acc_alpha + alpha * (1.0 - acc_alpha)
        
        if acc_alpha >= 1.0:
            break
            
    return acc_color
```

### Parameters
*   **`BREAK_STEPS`**: Base number of steps. Typical values depend on volume resolution and desired quality [S2, unofficial].
*   **`volumeScale`**: Uniform `vec3` scaling the volume dimensions for step calculation [S2, unofficial].
*   **`maxSteps`**: Upper limit for the number of steps [S2, unofficial].
*   **`gNChannels`**: Uniform integer specifying the number of active channels (up to 4) [S2, unofficial].
*   **`SLICES`**: Constant float indicating the number of slices (depth) in the volume [S2, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **WebGL1 Compatibility:** The method requires `Data3DTexture` and `sampler3D`, which are WebGL2 features. It will fail on WebGL1 fallback paths [S2, unofficial].
*   **Single-Slice Moiré:** Random ray dithering causes Moiré patterns in single-slice volumes. The original code disabled dithering for `SLICES==1.0`, but the new code unconditionally applies dithering, potentially reintroducing this artifact [S2, unofficial].
*   **Masking Semantics:** Masking is applied by scaling per-voxel intensity *before* LUT lookup. This affects alpha and color, not just RGB, and is applied up to 4 times (once per channel) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** When masking is enabled, the CPU traverses the full 3D volume twice per active channel (once for intensity, once for mask), which is costly for large volumes [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Fixed-Point Volume Ray Casting
Uses fixed-point arithmetic instead of floating-point for sampling and accumulation. Chosen when hardware lacks robust FP32 support or for deterministic results [G]. [Fixed-Point Volume Ray Casting](12056-fixed-point-volume-ray-casting.md)

### Texture Atlas Raymarching
Uses 2D texture atlases to represent volume slices. Chosen for WebGL1 compatibility or when 3D textures are not supported [S2, unofficial]. [Texture Atlas Raymarching](16745-specific-data-formats-and-integrators.md#texture-atlas-raymarching)

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $S$ is the number of steps per ray [G].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V \cdot C)$, where $V$ is the volume voxel count and $C$ is the number of channels (up to 4) [G].
*   **Measured Performance:** Rendered 128x128x128 or 256x256x256 voxels in 12.4ms [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Avoids overhead of many draw calls and VBO management [S1, unofficial].
    *   Allows larger XYZ volumes compared to texture atlases [S2, unofficial].
    *   Simplifies edge handling compared to atlas methods [S2, unofficial].
    *   Shares memory representation with path tracing and isosurface modes [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Requires WebGL2 [S2, unofficial].
    *   Limited to 4 channels [S2, unofficial].
    *   Cannot "pre-fuse" channels on the GPU as it does not write to 3D textures [S2, unofficial].
    *   Turning channels on/off requires rebuilding the 3D texture [S2, unofficial].
    *   8-bit precision limits dynamic range [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When WebGL2 is available, large volumes are needed, and a unified memory representation with other rendering modes (path tracing) is desired [S2, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** On WebGL1-only hardware, or when more than 4 channels are required [S2, unofficial].

## Background
This method was introduced by toloudis in Pull Request #370 for AllenCell/vole-core, dated January 30, 2026 [S2, unofficial]. It evolved from earlier techniques that represented voxel space in a 3D texture for Wolfenstein-style 3D raycasting [S1, unofficial]. The primary motivation was to simplify the in-memory representation by sharing the 3D texture with path tracing and isosurface modes, and to overcome the limitations of texture atlases (such as edge issues and fusion overhead) [S2, unofficial].
