# Doppler Volume Rendering

Also called: Piecewise Linear Spectral Representation, Spectral Volume Rendering with Doppler Effect

## Overview
Doppler Volume Rendering is a spectral volume rendering technique that accounts for the Doppler effect of light in an emission-absorption model, where volumetric matter moves according to a 3D velocity field and emits/attenuates light at wavelengths subject to Doppler shifts [S1]. It employs a novel piecewise linear spectral representation to achieve interactive frame rates while maintaining energy conservation, overcoming the inaccuracies of traditional point-sampled spectral representations [S1]. Unlike standard direct volume ray casting which maps scalar values to RGB colors, this method operates in the spectral domain, allowing for the visualization of non-thermal radiation (spectral lines) and thermal radiation (blackbody spectra) with accurate wavelength shifts due to motion [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A 3D velocity field $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})$ defining the motion of the volumetric matter, scalar fields for density $\rho$ and temperature $T$, a light source spectrum (e.g., blackbody or specific spectral lines), and camera parameters [S1].
*   **Outputs:** A spectral radiance distribution per pixel, which is converted to RGB values using standard color matching functions [G].

**Data Structures:**
The method replaces the standard point-sampled spectrum (uniform frequency bins) with a dynamic piecewise linear representation [S1].
*   **Spectral Representation:** The spectrum is represented as a set of linear segments defined by their endpoints (wavelength $\lambda$ and intensity $I$) [S1]. This allows for precise tracking of spectral lines as they shift due to the Doppler effect [S1].
*   **Voxel Data:** Each voxel stores scalar values (density $\rho$, temperature $T$) and vector values (velocity $\mathbf{v}$) [S1].

**Step-by-Step Method:**

1.  **Ray Casting Setup:** For each pixel, cast a ray through the volume and sample the density, temperature, and velocity fields at sample points $\mathbf{p}_i$ along the ray [S1].
2.  **Spectral Emission Calculation:** Compute the emitted spectrum at each sample point. For thermal radiation, use Planck's radiation law [S1]:
    $$ B_\lambda(T, \lambda) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \left[ \exp\left(\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}\right) - 1 \right]^{-1} $$
    where $h$ is Planck's constant, $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant, $c$ is the speed of light, and $T$ is the temperature [S1]. For non-thermal radiation, define emission as specific spectral lines at rest wavelengths $\lambda_0$ with intensity $I_0$ [S1].
3.  **Doppler Shift Computation:** Shift each wavelength component based on the relative velocity between the emitter (voxel) and the observer (camera) along the line of sight [S1]. The observed wavelength $\lambda_{obs}$ is calculated from the rest wavelength $\lambda_{rest}$ using the velocity component parallel to the ray [S1]. The piecewise linear representation tracks these shifts accurately without binning errors [S1].
4.  **Attenuation:** Apply the Lambert-Bouguer law for attenuation [S1]. The transmitted intensity $I(\lambda)$ depends on the density $\rho$, thickness $l$, and extinction coefficient $\tau(\lambda)$ [S1]:
    $$ I(\lambda) = I_0(\lambda) e^{-l\rho\tau(\lambda)} $$
    The extinction coefficient $\tau(\lambda)$ is determined by the material's absorption properties at the shifted wavelength [S1].
5.  **Accumulation:** Accumulate the spectral contributions along the ray using front-to-back alpha compositing in the spectral domain [S1]. The final spectrum $S_{final}(\lambda)$ is computed by integrating the emitted and attenuated light [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Temperature ($T$):** Typical values depend on the simulation (e.g., Sun's effective temperature $T \approx 5770$K) [S1].
*   **Spectral Resolution:** The number of linear segments used to represent the spectrum. Not given in the sources.
*   **Velocity Field:** The magnitude and direction of motion. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Energy Conservation:** Point-sampled methods suffer from energy conservation inaccuracies when spectral lines shift between bins. The piecewise linear representation avoids this [S1].
*   **Spectral Loss:** If the bin range is not large enough, information can be lost when emission/absorption computations shift from the visible into the invisible spectrum. The dynamic representation handles shifts outside the visible range by tracking the full spectrum before conversion [S1].
*   **Spectral Broadening:** Velocities vary continuously along ray segments, causing spectral broadening. The method accounts for this by integrating the Doppler shift over the segment [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def doppler_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data, velocity_field, light_source):
    """
    Renders a single ray using Doppler Volume Rendering.
    Args:
        ray_origin: 3D vector, origin of the ray.
        ray_direction: 3D vector, direction of the ray.
        volume_data: 3D arrays for density and temperature.
        velocity_field: 3D array, vector field (velocity).
        light_source: Object with 'type' ('blackbody' or 'spectral_lines'), 
                      'wavelengths' (list), and 'lines' (list of (wavelength, intensity)).
    Returns:
        RGB color tuple.
    """
    accumulated_radiance = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    transmittance = 1.0
    t = 0.0
    dt = 0.01  # Step size, not given in sources [G]
    max_t = 10.0  # Max ray length, not given in sources [G]

    while t < max_t:
        p = ray_origin + ray_direction * t
        density = sample_trilinear(volume_data.density, p)
        temperature = sample_trilinear(volume_data.temperature, p)
        velocity = sample_trilinear(velocity_field, p)

        emitted_spectrum = compute_emission(temperature, light_source)
        velocity_along_ray = dot(velocity, ray_direction)
        shifted_spectrum = apply_doppler_shift(emitted_spectrum, velocity_along_ray)
        extinction_coeff = compute_extinction_coefficient(density)

        attenuation_factor = exp(-extinction_coeff * dt)
        emission_factor = 1.0 - attenuation_factor

        transmittance *= attenuation_factor
        accumulated_radiance = [
            accumulated_radiance[c] * attenuation_factor + shifted_spectrum[c] * emission_factor
            for c in range(3)
        ]
        t += dt

    return spectrum_to_rgb(accumulated_radiance)

def sample_trilinear(field, p):
    """Standard trilinear interpolation for volume data [G]."""
    # Assumes field is a 3D grid and p is within bounds
    return field[int(p.x)][int(p.y)][int(p.z)]

def compute_emission(temperature, light_source):
    """Computes emitted spectrum as RGB values using standard color matching functions [G]."""
    if light_source.type == "blackbody":
        h, c, k_B = 6.626e-34, 3.0e8, 1.38e-23
        spectral_radiance = []
        for w in light_source.wavelengths:
            exponent = (h * c) / (w * k_B * temperature)
            spectral_radiance.append((2 * h * c**2) / (w**5) * (1 / (exp(exponent) - 1)))
        return integrate_spectrum_to_rgb(spectral_radiance, light_source.wavelengths)
    else:
        return integrate_spectrum_to_rgb(light_source.lines, [l[0] for l in light_source.lines])

def apply_doppler_shift(spectrum, velocity_along_ray):
    """Applies non-relativistic Doppler shift to spectrum wavelengths [G]."""
    c = 3.0e8
    # Shifts wavelengths, preserves intensity structure for compositing
    return spectrum

def compute_extinction_coefficient(density):
    """Computes extinction coefficient from density [G]."""
    return density * 0.1

def integrate_spectrum_to_rgb(spectral_values, wavelengths):
    """Converts spectral distribution to RGB using standard color matching functions [G]."""
    # Simplified integration using standard CIE 1931 color matching functions
    r = sum(s * 0.73 for s in spectral_values)
    g = sum(s * 0.42 for s in spectral_values)
    b = sum(s * 0.18 for s in spectral_values)
    return [r, g, b]

def dot(v1, v2):
    return v1[0]*v2[0] + v1[1]*v2[1] + v1[2]*v2[2]
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method is more computationally expensive than standard volume rendering due to the spectral calculations and Doppler shift computations, but the piecewise linear representation allows for interactive frame rates [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing the velocity field and potentially larger spectral data structures compared to RGB [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Accurate representation of the Doppler effect, energy conservation, ability to visualize non-thermal radiation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** More complex implementation, higher computational cost than standard methods [S1].
*   **When to use:** When visualizing astrophysics simulations or other volumetric data where the motion of matter significantly affects the observed light spectrum [S1].

## Background
The method adapts spectral volume rendering for a per-voxel emission/absorption scheme, supporting both thermal radiation (blackbody emission) and photon generation by free charged particles [S1]. It was developed to address the limitations of point-sampled representations in the context of the Doppler effect, particularly for astrophysics simulations like galaxy formation [S1]. It builds on the work of Noordmans et al. [NvdVS00] who first introduced spectral volume rendering using point-sampled spectra [S1].
