# Extinction-based Ambient Occlusion

Also called: AO via extinction summation, Local AO

## Overview
Extinction-based Ambient Occlusion (AO) approximates the attenuation of diffusely reflected ambient light through local volume occlusion by summing extinction coefficients, avoiding the computational cost of shadow ray tracing [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard Direct Volume Ray Casting (DVR) by replacing the constant ambient term with a spatially varying occlusion factor derived from a local neighborhood integral, and from full global illumination methods by relying on a simplified inverse-square distance attenuation model rather than solving the full rendering equation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D volume dataset containing extinction coefficients $\tau(t)$ and color values $C_{RGB}(t)$ for each voxel $t$. A local neighborhood definition $N(s)$ (typically a sphere or box) around a sample point $s$.
*   **Output:** The final pixel color with ambient occlusion applied, specifically the reflected light term $E_i$ for each sample.

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Summed Area Table (SAT):** A precomputed data structure storing the cumulative sum of extinction coefficients. This allows for $O(1)$ retrieval of the sum of $\tau(t)$ over any axis-aligned rectangular region [S1, unofficial].
*   **Voxel Data:** Standard 3D grid storing $\tau$ and $C_{RGB}$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Precompute SAT:** Construct a 3D Summed Area Table from the volume's extinction coefficients $\tau$. This enables fast aggregation queries [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Sample Volume:** For each sample point $s$ along the viewing ray, determine its local neighborhood $N(s)$ (denoted as $\Omega_A$ in the formula context) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Aggregate Extinction:** Compute the weighted sum of extinction coefficients within $N(s)$. Using the SAT, this is done efficiently. The aggregation accounts for the inverse-square distance attenuation [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Compute Occlusion Factor:** Calculate the local ambient occlusion intensity $I_{AO}(s)$ using the aggregated extinction sum [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Compute Reflected Light:** Combine the occlusion factor with ambient light intensity and voxel color to determine the final reflected light contribution $E_i$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The local ambient occlusion intensity $I_{AO}(s)$ is modeled as:
$$I_{AO}(s) \approx I_A \cdot e^{-\int_{t \in \mathbb{N}(s)} \frac{\tau(t)}{|s-t|^2} dt} \quad (4)$$
Where:
*   $I_{AO}(s)$: Local ambient occlusion intensity at sample $s$.
*   $I_A$: Constant ambient light intensity incident over the boundary $\partial N(s)$.
*   $\tau(t)$: Extinction coefficient at position $t$.
*   $|s-t|$: Euclidean distance from sample $s$ to position $t$.
*   The term $\frac{1}{|s-t|^2}$ accounts for radial distance-based light attenuation [S1, unofficial].

The reflected light term $E_i$ for a voxel is calculated as:
$$E_i = I_{A,L} \cdot e^{-w_{A,L} \sum_{\Omega_{A,L}} \Delta^{\tau_j} \cdot k} \cdot C_{RGB} \quad (8)$$
Where:
*   $E_i$: Reflected light at the voxel.
*   $I_{A,L}$: Ambient or light intensity.
*   $w_{A,L}$: Weight factor. For AO, $w_A = r^{-2}$ (inverse square of the radius of the aggregate query) [S1, unofficial].
*   $\sum_{\Omega_{A,L}} \Delta^{\tau_j} \cdot k$: Aggregated sum of extinction coefficients over the neighborhood $\Omega_A = N(s)$ for AO. $\Delta^{\tau_j}$ represents the extinction contribution, and $k$ is a normal/gradient-dependent local illumination model factor [S1, unofficial].
*   $C_{RGB}$: Voxel color [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_extinction_ao(s, volume, sat, neighborhood_radius):
    """
    Computes extinction-based ambient occlusion for a sample point s.
    
    Args:
        s: 3D coordinates of the sample point.
        volume: 3D array of extinction coefficients tau.
        sat: 3D Summed Area Table of extinction coefficients.
        neighborhood_radius: Radius defining the local neighborhood N(s).
        
    Returns:
        I_AO: The local ambient occlusion intensity factor.
    """
    # Define the bounding box of the neighborhood N(s)
    # Note: The integral in Eq. 4 is approximated by the aggregate query.
    # The SAT allows O(1) retrieval of the sum of tau over a region.
    
    # 1. Get the sum of extinction coefficients in the neighborhood
    # The SAT query returns sum(tau) for the region.
    # We must approximate the integral of tau(t)/|s-t|^2.
    # The source indicates using a weighted sum via the SAT.
    # For a spherical neighborhood, we might approximate with a box or use
    # multiple SAT queries for shells. The source simplifies this to an
    # aggregate query with weight w_A = r^-2.
    
    # Approximation: Sum of tau in neighborhood, weighted by inverse square distance.
    # Since SAT gives sum(tau), and the source mentions w_A = r^-2 for the aggregate query,
    # we apply this weight to the aggregated sum.
    
    # Get sum of tau in the bounding box of the neighborhood
    sum_tau = sat.query_box(s - neighborhood_radius, s + neighborhood_radius)
    
    # Apply the inverse square distance weight as per Eq. 8 and source description
    # w_A = r^-2
    weight = 1.0 / (neighborhood_radius ** 2)
    
    # Weighted sum of extinction
    weighted_extinction_sum = weight * sum_tau
    
    # Constant ambient light intensity (assumed known or passed in)
    I_A = 1.0 # Placeholder for constant ambient intensity
    
    # Compute I_AO using Eq. 4: I_A * exp(-integral)
    # The integral is approximated by the weighted sum
    I_AO = I_A * math.exp(-weighted_extinction_sum)
    
    return I_AO

def compute_reflected_light(s, volume, sat, neighborhood_radius, I_A_L, k):
    """
    Computes the reflected light term E_i with AO.
    
    Args:
        s: 3D coordinates of the sample point.
        volume: 3D array of extinction coefficients and colors.
        sat: 3D Summed Area Table of extinction coefficients.
        neighborhood_radius: Radius for AO neighborhood.
        I_A_L: Ambient or light intensity.
        k: Normal/gradient-dependent local illumination factor.
        
    Returns:
        E_i: Reflected light value.
    """
    # Get the voxel color at s
    C_RGB = volume.get_color(s)
    
    # Compute the AO factor
    I_AO = compute_extinction_ao(s, volume, sat, neighborhood_radius)
    
    # The source Eq. 8 combines I_A,L, the exponential term, and C_RGB.
    # Note: I_AO in Eq. 4 is I_A * exp(-...). 
    # Eq. 8 uses I_A,L * exp(-w * sum) * C_RGB.
    # We can reuse the weighted_extinction_sum from compute_extinction_ao logic.
    
    # Re-calculate weighted sum for clarity in Eq. 8 context
    sum_tau = sat.query_box(s - neighborhood_radius, s + neighborhood_radius)
    weight = 1.0 / (neighborhood_radius ** 2)
    weighted_extinction_sum = weight * sum_tau
    
    # Compute E_i using Eq. 8
    # E_i = I_A,L * exp(-w_A,L * sum(tau) * k) * C_RGB
    # Note: The source writes sum(Delta^tau_j * k). If k is constant per sample,
    # it can be pulled out. If k varies, it must be summed. 
    # The source says "k simply represents a normal, gradient dependent local illumination model factor".
    # Assuming k is evaluated at s for the sample point.
    
    exponent = -weight * sum_tau * k
    E_i = I_A_L * math.exp(exponent) * C_RGB
    
    return E_i
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Neighborhood Radius:** Defines the size of $N(s)$. Typical values are not explicitly given, but the SAT approach allows efficient querying for various radii [S1, unofficial].
*   **Ambient Intensity ($I_A$):** Proportional to the sum of all lights [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The neighborhood $N(s)$ may extend outside the volume. The SAT query should handle out-of-bounds accesses by clamping or returning zero [G].
*   **Precision:** The exponential decay can lead to very small values for highly occluded regions. Standard floating-point precision is assumed sufficient based on the source's performance claims [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If $\tau(t) = 0$ everywhere, $I_{AO}(s) = I_A$, resulting in no occlusion [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The use of the 3D SAT allows the aggregation query to be performed in $O(1)$ time per sample, regardless of the neighborhood size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires $O(N)$ additional memory for the SAT, where $N$ is the number of voxels in the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Aggregate sampling using the SAT is over 45 times faster than individually sampled neighborhoods (0.22s vs 10s) with virtually no difference in image quality [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly faster than shadow ray tracing methods; effective approximation of local occlusion; easy to integrate into existing DVR pipelines.
*   **Limitations:** Approximation of ambient occlusion; not physically accurate for global illumination effects; relies on the assumption of constant ambient light at the neighborhood boundary [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When interactive performance is required and local occlusion effects are desired, but full global illumination is too expensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When physically accurate global illumination or color bleeding (which requires color-aware aggregation) is needed [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Extinction-based AO was introduced as a fast approximation for ambient occlusion in volume rendering, addressing the high cost of shadow ray tracing [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept that light attenuation in a volume is governed by the extinction coefficient [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to provide a useful approximation of the effect of occlusion on diffusely reflected ambient light without the computational burden of full global illumination models [S1, unofficial].
