# Hybrid and Mixed Geometry

Also called: Hybrid ray-volume rendering

## Overview
Hybrid and Mixed Geometry rendering integrates explicit polygonal primitives (isosurfaces, bounding boxes, wireframes) with implicit volumetric data (scalar fields) into a single image [S2]. It solves the problem of visualizing scale, context, or specific structural boundaries relative to internal volumetric structures [S2]. Unlike pure volume rendering, which treats the scene as a continuous medium, or pure surface rendering, which discards internal data, this method uses depth buffering or ray-space compositing to resolve visibility between the two representations [S1][S2]. It is distinct from [Hybrid Ray Tracing](2308-hybrid-ray-tracing.md), which mixes geometry and volume in ray space without depth buffers, and [Hybrid Volume Rendering](15456-hybrid-volume-rendering.md), which primarily uses depth buffers for clipping.

## Shared principles
The method relies on the decomposition of the 3D volume into a set of 2D planar slices [G]. These slices are treated as geometric primitives (typically triangles or quads) that are rasterized by the GPU [G]. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Slice Generation**: The volume is intersected by a series of parallel planes. The intersection of these planes with the volume bounding box defines the polygonal shape of each slice [G].
2.  **Texture Mapping**: Each slice vertex is assigned 3D texture coordinates that map to the volumetric data [G]. The volume data is stored as a 3D texture on the GPU [S2].
3.  **Rasterization and Shading**: The polygons are rasterized. In the fragment shader, the 3D texture is sampled at the interpolated texture coordinates to retrieve the scalar value [G].
4.  **Classification and Blending**: The scalar value is mapped to color and opacity via a transfer function. The fragments are composited using alpha blending (typically `GL_SRC_ALPHA, GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`) [G].
5.  **Sorting**: The slices must be rendered in the correct depth order relative to the camera (back-to-front for standard alpha blending) to ensure correct compositing [G].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Ray Tracing](2308-hybrid-ray-tracing.md)
Hybrid Ray Tracing mixes geometric and volumetric data by casting rays through the ensemble of both, drawing samples of each, and compositing them in depth-sorted order. It solves the problem of rendering scenes containing both explicit polygonal primitives and implicit scalar fields while maintaining correct visibility and anti-aliasing. Unlike sibling methods that convert geometry to volume (3D scan-conversion) or use hardware depth buffers for clipping, this method simultaneously traces rays through volumetric sample arrays and polygonal databases, resampling both at equally spaced intervals to handle mixtures directly in the ray space.

### [Hybrid Volume Rendering](15456-hybrid-volume-rendering.md)
Hybrid volume rendering integrates explicit polygonal geometry (isosurfaces, bounding boxes, wireframes) with implicit volumetric data (scalar fields) into a single image. It solves the problem of visualizing scale, context, or specific structural boundaries (like a grid or skin surface) relative to internal volumetric structures (like bone or fluid density). Unlike pure volume rendering, which treats the scene as a continuous medium, or pure surface rendering, which discards internal data, this method uses depth buffering to resolve visibility between the two representations, allowing geometry to occlude volume or volume to transmit light through geometry.

### [Hybrid Volume Rendering Scheme](16812-hybrid-volume-rendering-scheme.md)
The Hybrid Volume Rendering Scheme is a parallel volume rendering algorithm for unstructured grids that hybridizes object-order and image-order techniques to handle complex block boundaries, dramatic cell size variations, and camera-induced load imbalances. It solves the problem of performance degradation in pure object-order or image-order methods when cell sizes vary significantly or when the camera focuses on specific regions, causing uneven workload distribution. Unlike pure image-order, which suffers from replicated cell loading and unequal cell distribution, and pure object-order, where image compositing becomes a bottleneck, this method adapts its computation based on workload by bounding the amount of computation per phase.

### Cutter Geometry Rendering
Rendering occurs only inside the selected geometric object's volume. If Fire Lights are enabled, only those inside the cutter are rendered. Parameters: `usegizmo` (enable), `gizmo` (object) [S2]. The `Invert Cutter` (`invgizmo`) parameter, when enabled, restricts rendering to the area outside the render cutter; rays not intersecting the cutter are shaded. This is distinct from a cutter with inverted geometry [S2]. Cutter Geometry does not work in Volumetric Geometry mode [S2].

## Background
The concept of hybrid rendering appears in various forms, such as blending OpenGL-rendered volumes with ray-traced non-volume objects, activated by settings like `ray_volume` [S1]. This approach allows for features like shadows or outlines that are not available in standard volume rendering [S1]. However, such hybrid solutions often have limitations, such as requiring image sizes to match the current window size and not functioning in headless (batch) mode [S1]. The specific implementation details vary significantly between the ray-space compositing approach [S2] and the depth-buffer-based clipping approach [S1].
