# Hybrid and Mixed Geometry

Also called: Mixed Geometry Rendering, Hybrid Volume Rendering, Hybrid Ray Tracing

## Overview
Hybrid and Mixed Geometry rendering integrates explicit geometric primitives (polygons, isosurfaces, wireframes) with implicit volumetric data (scalar fields) into a single coherent image . It solves the problem of visualizing the spatial relationship between discrete objects and continuous media, such as bounding boxes around CT scans or wireframes over fluid density fields . It sets itself apart from pure volume ray casting by leveraging hardware depth buffers or explicit sorting to manage occlusion between surfaces and volumes, and from pure surface rendering by handling semi-transparent volumetric media that transmit light from underlying geometry [S1].

## Shared principles
The method relies on the integration of two distinct data representations: explicit geometry and implicit volume. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Dual Representation**: The scene contains both polygonal primitives (with shading and depth) and volumetric data (scalar fields mapped to color/opacity via transfer functions) [S1].
2.  **Depth Resolution**: Visibility between geometry and volume is resolved using depth information. This is typically achieved by rendering opaque geometry first to populate a hardware depth buffer, which then bounds or clips the volume rendering pass .
3.  **Compositing**: The final pixel color is a composite of the geometric contribution and the volumetric contribution. The order of compositing depends on the specific implementation (e.g., volume in front of geometry, or depth-sorted interleaving) [S1].
4.  **Pipeline Integration**: The method bridges standard rasterization pipelines (for geometry) and volume rendering pipelines (ray casting or texture-mapped slicing) [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Hybrid Ray Tracing](2308-hybrid-ray-tracing.md)
Simultaneous ray casting through both volumetric slabs and polygonal intersections, handling occlusion and attenuation within a single ray pass.

### [Mixed Geometry Rendering](3442-mixed-geometry-rendering.md)
Integrates explicit geometric primitives with procedural volumetric data using hardware depth buffers or explicit sorting to manage occlusion.

### [Hybrid Volume Rendering](15456-hybrid-volume-rendering.md)
Allows polygon and volume data to be visualized in a single image, useful for displaying scale, grids, or other polygon-defined objects over, behind, and through volume data.

### Cutter Geometry Rendering
**Definition**: Rendering occurs only inside the selected geometric object's volume. If Fire Lights are enabled, only those inside the cutter are rendered [S1]. Parameters include `usegizmo` (enable) and `gizmo` (object) [S1].
**Parameter**: `Invert Cutter` (`invgizmo`): When enabled, rendering occurs only outside the render cutter. Rays not intersecting the cutter are shaded. This is distinct from a cutter with inverted geometry [S1].
**Pitfall**: Cutter Geom does not work in Volumetric Geometry mode [S1].

## Background
The concept of mixing geometry and volume has evolved from early software-based ray tracers that handled both polygonal environments and voxel data . Early implementations focused on simultaneous ray casting to handle interactions like volumetric attenuation of polygonal highlights . Later, hardware-accelerated approaches leveraged the Z-buffer to separate geometry and volume rendering passes, enabling real-time interaction . Modern implementations often use hybrid pipelines to combine the clarity of explicit surfaces with the richness of volumetric data [S1]. Specific software tools, such as Phoenix for 3ds Max, implement "Cutter Geometry" to restrict volume rendering to specific spatial regions defined by geometric objects [S1].
