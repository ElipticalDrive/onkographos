# Trilinear Interpolation

Also called: 3D Linear Interpolation, Resampling and Interpolation, Trilinear Interpolant, Voxel Trilinear Interpolation

## Overview
Trilinear interpolation estimates the scalar value (e.g., density or opacity) at an arbitrary point within a voxel grid by computing a weighted average of the values at the eight surrounding voxel corners [S1, unofficial][S7, unofficial]. It solves the problem of discretization artifacts inherent in nearest-neighbor interpolation, which produces blocky, aliased images because rays rarely hit voxel centers exactly [S5, unofficial][S13, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from [Frequency Domain Volume Rendering with Sinc Resampling](826-frequency-domain-volume-rendering-with-sinc-resampling.md) by using a compact spatial kernel (8 samples) rather than an infinite sinc function, and from [Fast tri-cubic interpolation](848-fast-tri-cubic-interpolation.md) by offering lower computational cost at the expense of reduced smoothness [S6, unofficial][S13, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
* A 3D scalar field $V$ defined on a regular grid with dimensions $D_x \times D_y \times D_z$ [S2, unofficial].
* A query point $\mathbf{p} = (x, y, z)$ in voxel space [S9, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
* A scalar value $V(\mathbf{p})$.

**Data Structures:**
* The volume data is stored in a 1D array or 3D array with row-major indexing [S8, unofficial]. For a point $(x, y, z)$, the index of the corner $(i, j, k)$ is typically `((k * D_y) + j) * D_x + i` [S8, unofficial].
* The eight corner values are fetched into a temporary structure, often a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ array `data[2][2][2]` or individual variables $f_{000}$ through $f_{111}$ [S1, unofficial][S10, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate Shift:** To align the voxel center with the integer grid coordinates, shift the query point by $-0.5$ in each dimension. This ensures that a sample point exactly in the center of a voxel returns that voxel's value rather than an average of its neighbors [S1, unofficial][S7, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{p}_{\text{lattice}} = \mathbf{p} - 0.5 $$
    [G] If the input coordinates are already normalized to $[0, 1]$ or defined such that voxel centers are at integers, this shift may be omitted or adjusted. However, standard implementations assuming voxel values are stored at integer grid points require this shift [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Index Calculation:** Compute the integer indices of the lower-left corner of the containing voxel using the floor function [S7, unofficial].
    $$ i_0 = \lfloor p_{\text{lattice}, x} \rfloor, \quad j_0 = \lfloor p_{\text{lattice}, y} \rfloor, \quad k_0 = \lfloor p_{\text{lattice}, z} \rfloor $$
    The upper indices $i_1 = i_0 + 1$, etc., must not exceed the grid dimensions minus one [S8, unofficial][S10, unofficial]. If the point lies on the upper boundary, clamp $i_0$ to $D_x - 2$ to ensure $i_1$ is valid [S8, unofficial][S10, unofficial].

3.  **Fractional Weights:** Calculate the fractional offset (weights) within the voxel [S10, unofficial].
    $$ w_x = p_{\text{lattice}, x} - i_0 $$
    $$ w_y = p_{\text{lattice}, y} - j_0 $$
    $$ w_z = p_{\text{lattice}, z} - k_0 $$
    These weights lie in $[0, 1]$. Note that $1 - w_x$ is the weight for the lower corner, and $w_x$ is the weight for the upper corner [G].

4.  **Fetch Corner Values:** Retrieve the scalar values from the eight corners of the voxel defined by $(i_0, j_0, k_0)$ and $(i_1, j_1, k_1)$ [S10, unofficial].
    $$ f_{000} = V(i_0, j_0, k_0), \quad f_{100} = V(i_1, j_0, k_0) $$
    $$ f_{010} = V(i_0, j_1, k_0), \quad f_{110} = V(i_1, j_1, k_0) $$
    $$ f_{001} = V(i_0, j_0, k_1), \quad f_{101} = V(i_1, j_0, k_1) $$
    $$ f_{011} = V(i_0, j_1, k_1), \quad f_{111} = V(i_1, j_1, k_1) $$

5.  **Interpolation:** Perform linear interpolation sequentially along the x, y, and z axes [S4].
    *   **X-axis:** Interpolate pairs along the x-axis for the bottom ($k_0$) and top ($k_1$) faces, and for both $y$ levels [S8, unofficial].
        $$ c_{00} = f_{000}(1 - w_x) + f_{100}w_x $$
        $$ c_{10} = f_{010}(1 - w_x) + f_{110}w_x $$
        $$ c_{01} = f_{001}(1 - w_x) + f_{101}w_x $$
        $$ c_{11} = f_{011}(1 - w_x) + f_{111}w_x $$
    *   **Y-axis:** Interpolate the results from the X-step along the y-axis [S13, unofficial].
        $$ c_0 = c_{00}(1 - w_y) + c_{10}w_y $$
        $$ c_1 = c_{01}(1 - w_y) + c_{11}w_y $$
    *   **Z-axis:** Interpolate the results from the Y-step along the z-axis [S13, unofficial].
        $$ V(\mathbf{p}) = c_0(1 - w_z) + c_1w_z $$

**Formula:**
The expanded form of the interpolation is a weighted sum of the eight corner values:
$$ V(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i \in \{0,1\}} \sum_{j \in \{0,1\}} \sum_{k \in \{0,1\}} w_x^i (1-w_x)^{1-i} w_y^j (1-w_y)^{1-j} w_z^k (1-w_z)^{1-k} f_{ijk} $$
where $f_{ijk}$ corresponds to the value at grid index $(i_0+i, j_0+j, k_0+k)$ [S1, unofficial][S10, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
* `voxel_shift`: Default $0.5$. Determines if voxel values are associated with corners (0) or centers (0.5) [S16].
* Grid assumption: Regular grid with unit lengths in all three cell dimensions, achieved by scaling coordinates [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Boundary Conditions:** If the query point lies exactly on the upper boundary of the grid, the upper neighbor index would be out of bounds. The indices must be clamped such that $i_1 < D_x$ [S8, unofficial][S10, unofficial].
*   **Zero-Dimension Voxels:** If the voxel width is zero ($i1 == i0$), the weight is 0; otherwise, $wx = (x - i0) / (i1 - i0)$ [S10, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Trilinear interpolation involves terms up to cubic order, making it non-linear with respect to the polynomial [S2, unofficial].
*   **Artifacts:** Trilinear interpolation causes C1 discontinuities (false edges or "star patterns") because the triangular pulse reconstruction kernel has infinite extent in the frequency domain with large positive lobes [S6, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def trilinear_interpolation(volume, dims, p):
    """
    volume: 1D array of scalar values, size dims[0]*dims[1]*dims[2]
    dims: tuple (Dx, Dy, Dz)
    p: tuple (x, y, z) in voxel space
    """
    # 1. Shift coordinates
    px = p[0] - 0.5
    py = p[1] - 0.5
    pz = p[2] - 0.5
    
    # 2. Calculate integer indices
    i0 = int(math.floor(px))
    j0 = int(math.floor(py))
    k0 = int(math.floor(pz))
    
    # 3. Clamp indices to valid range
    # i1, j1, k1 are implicitly i0+1, etc.
    # Ensure i0, j0, k0 are within [0, D-2] so that i1, j1, k1 are within [1, D-1]
    i0 = max(0, min(i0, dims[0] - 2))
    j0 = max(0, min(j0, dims[1] - 2))
    k0 = max(0, min(k0, dims[2] - 2))
    
    i1 = i0 + 1
    j1 = j0 + 1
    k1 = k0 + 1
    
    # 4. Calculate fractional weights
    wx = px - i0
    wy = py - j0
    wz = pz - k0
    
    # 5. Fetch corner values
    # Helper to get value from 1D array
    def get_val(x, y, z):
        return volume[(z * dims[1] + y) * dims[0] + x]
        
    f000 = get_val(i0, j0, k0)
    f100 = get_val(i1, j0, k0)
    f010 = get_val(i0, j1, k0)
    f110 = get_val(i1, j1, k0)
    f001 = get_val(i0, j0, k1)
    f101 = get_val(i1, j0, k1)
    f011 = get_val(i0, j1, k1)
    f111 = get_val(i1, j1, k1)
    
    # 6. Interpolate X
    c00 = f000 * (1 - wx) + f100 * wx
    c10 = f010 * (1 - wx) + f110 * wx
    c01 = f001 * (1 - wx) + f101 * wx
    c11 = f011 * (1 - wx) + f111 * wx
    
    # 7. Interpolate Y
    c0 = c00 * (1 - wy) + c10 * wy
    c1 = c01 * (1 - wy) + c11 * wy
    
    # 8. Interpolate Z
    result = c0 * (1 - wz) + c1 * wz
    
    return result
```

## Subtopics

### [Plenoxels Interpolation Methods](935-plenoxels-interpolation-methods.md)
Stores spherical harmonic coefficients and opacity at voxel centers, enabling continuous radiance field approximation for gradient-based optimization. Chosen when differentiable rendering and high effective resolution are required over discrete voxel assumptions.

### [Multi-Texture Trilinear Estimation](1164-multi-texture-trilinear-estimation.md)
Renders scalar field data on rectilinear grids by stacking parallel quads and compositing them, substituting standard trilinear with hybrid bilinear/linear steps. Chosen for non-uniform grid spacing where hardware 3D texture mapping is insufficient or for specific legacy rendering pipelines.

### [Semi-Analytical Volume Rendering with Trilinear Interpolation](1312-semi-analytical-volume-rendering-with-trilinear-interpolatio.md)
Uses trilinear interpolation within hexahedral cells but replaces uniform sampling with analytical extrema detection to eliminate isosurface holes. Chosen for hexahedral meshes where topological correctness of isosurfaces is critical.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per sample, requiring exactly 8 memory fetches and 7 linear interpolations (14 multiplications, 7 additions) [S2, unofficial][S12, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per sample [G].
*   **Performance:** Trilinear interpolation produces softer images than nearest-neighbor but takes approximately 5 times longer to compute [S1, unofficial]. It is fast and easy to implement but does not produce very smooth results compared to higher-order methods [S13, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, computationally efficient, provides smoother results than nearest-neighbor, widely supported by hardware (e.g., GPU texture units) [S12, unofficial][S14].
*   **Limitations:** Exhibits C1 discontinuities (false edges/star patterns) due to the triangular pulse kernel's frequency response [S6, unofficial]. Not suitable for high-quality reconstruction where aliasing must be strictly minimized [S6, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** General-purpose volume rendering, fluid simulation, and real-time applications where performance is critical and moderate visual quality is acceptable [S12, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When high-frequency detail preservation is required (use sinc or cubic), or when rendering rectilinear grids with non-uniform spacing without coordinate transformation [S11, unofficial].

## Background
Trilinear interpolation is a direct extension of bilinear interpolation, defined as the linear interpolation of two bilinear interpolations [S13, unofficial]. It originates from standard numerical analysis techniques for multivariate interpolation on regular grids [G]. In volume rendering, it became a standard technique to mitigate the blocky artifacts of nearest-neighbor sampling [S1, unofficial][S13, unofficial]. The method is discussed in foundational texts on volume rendering and computer graphics, such as those by Shirley et al. [S2, unofficial][S12, unofficial]. It is implemented in various libraries, including OpenVDB, which uses a nested interpolation structure for sparse voxel grids [S1, unofficial].
