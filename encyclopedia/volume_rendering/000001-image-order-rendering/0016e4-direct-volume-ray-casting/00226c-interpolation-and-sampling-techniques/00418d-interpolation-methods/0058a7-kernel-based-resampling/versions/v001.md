# Kernel-based Resampling

Also called: Kernel Interpolation, Convolution-based Interpolation, Filter-based Sampling

## Overview
Kernel-based resampling estimates continuous field values at arbitrary query positions by convolving a discrete voxel grid with a spatially localized weight function (kernel). It solves the problem of reconstructing a smooth, continuous representation from discrete samples for volume rendering, balancing aliasing and blurring through the choice of kernel shape. It differs from [Nearest Neighbor Sampling](11694-nearest-neighbor-sampling.md) by providing continuity and from [Gradient-Based Interpolation by Sample Splatting](9072-gradient-based-interpolation-by-sample-splatting.md) by relying on explicit scalar weights rather than geometric splatting primitives.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the principle that a continuous function $V(\mathbf{p})$ must be approximated from a discrete set of samples $V_i$ located at grid positions $\mathbf{p}_i$. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Query Positioning**: Determining the 3D coordinate $\mathbf{p}$ along the ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ where a value is needed. This may involve converting depth parameters to physical distances or warping coordinates for neural encodings.
2.  **Kernel/Weight Application**: Computing weights $w_i(\mathbf{p})$ based on the distance between $\mathbf{p}$ and the sample centers $\mathbf{p}_i$. The interpolated value is:
    $$
    V(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i} w_i(\mathbf{p}) V_i
    $$
    The choice of kernel (e.g., linear, cubic, inverse distance) determines the spatial extent (number of neighbors) and frequency response (aliasing vs. blurring) .
3.  **Coordinate Transformation**: Mapping world-space or camera-space coordinates to the local coordinate system of the volume data (e.g., normalized $[-1, 1]$ or $[0, 1]$ ranges) before interpolation .

## Subtopics

### [Interpolation Schemes for Volume Rendering](817-interpolation-schemes-for-volume-rendering.md)
Focuses on algorithmic implementations and hardware approximations of kernels, ranging from nearest-neighbor to frequency-domain resampling. It emphasizes the trade-off between visual quality and computational cost in the context of specific rendering pipelines.

### [Lanczos Resampling](11246-lanczos-resampling.md)
Uses a windowed sinc function to reduce aliasing while maintaining sharpness. It is distinguished by its specific kernel formula involving the product of two sinc functions and a window parameter $a$.

### [Interpolation Functions for Resampling](15932-interpolation-functions-for-resampling.md)
Evaluates interpolation based on error characteristics relative to the point-spread function (PSF), focusing on step edge reconstruction and noise sensitivity rather than general frequency response.

## Background
Kernel-based resampling is a fundamental technique in digital signal processing and computer graphics, rooted in the Whittaker–Shannon interpolation formula. In volume rendering, it became standard with the advent of direct volume ray casting to provide smooth visualizations of medical and scientific data. The specific variants like Lanczos resampling were adapted from image processing to handle the high-frequency details in volumetric datasets .
