# General Cubic Spline Interpolation
Also called: B-C Spline Family, General cubic-spline family, Mitchell-Netravali Spline Family

## Overview
General cubic spline interpolation is a family of piecewise cubic reconstruction filters parameterized by coefficients $B$ and $C$, used to sample volume data with higher continuity than linear methods. It distinguishes itself from its siblings by offering a tunable trade-off between interpolation accuracy and smoothing, governed by linear constraints on its parameters [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family defines a symmetric, piecewise cubic reconstruction filter $h(x)$ with support $[-2, 2]$. The function is defined such that $h(x) = h(-x)$ for $x < 0$. For the positive half of the domain, the filter is composed of two cubic polynomials:

$$
h(x) = 
\begin{cases} 
\left(\frac{2}{3} - \frac{B}{2} - C\right)x^3 + (-3 + 2B + C)x^2 + \left(1 - \frac{B}{2}\right) & 0 \le x < 1 \\
\left(\frac{B}{2} - C\right)x^3 + (B + 5C)x^2 + \frac{4B}{3}x + \left(-2B - 8C\right)x + \frac{4B}{3} + 4C & 1 \le x < 2 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

The parameters $B$ and $C$ are free variables that shape the filter. The function is continuous and has a continuous first derivative for all values of $B$ and $C$ [S1, unofficial]. Additionally, the filter always reconstructs constant values correctly regardless of the parameter choices [S1, unofficial].

However, the family does not guarantee interpolation (passing through sample points) or linear reconstruction (reconstructing straight lines accurately) for arbitrary $B$ and $C$ [S1, unofficial]. Specific constraints must be applied to satisfy these conditions:
1.  **Interpolation:** To ensure the function is an interpolating filter, $B$ must be set to $0$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Linear Reconstruction:** To accurately reconstruct a straight line (satisfying condition 6 in signal theoretic background), $B$ must equal $-2C + 1$ [S1, unofficial]. When this condition is met, the derivative at $x=1$ is automatically fixed to $-1/2$ [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The general cubic spline family was described in the context of signal theoretic background for volume rendering and image processing [S1, unofficial]. It is commonly used in image processing and medical volume rendering applications where a balance between sharpness and smoothness is needed [S1, unofficial]. The family includes the cubic B-spline as a specific case where $B=1$ and $C=0$, which serves as an approximating rather than interpolating filter [S1, unofficial].
