# Interpolation Functions for Resampling
Also called: Cubic-spline

## Overview
Interpolation functions for resampling are mathematical kernels used to estimate continuous scalar values from discrete volume samples, primarily for edge reconstruction and shading in volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. They are distinguished from other interpolation methods by their specific focus on the frequency response and artifact generation (such as overshoot or noise sensitivity) relative to the sample distance and point-spread function width [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the principle that a continuous function $V(\mathbf{p})$ must be approximated from a discrete set of samples $V_i$ located at grid positions $\mathbf{p}_i$. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Query Positioning**: Determining the 3D coordinate $\mathbf{p}$ along the ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ where a value is needed. This may involve converting depth parameters to physical distances or warping coordinates for neural encodings.
2.  **Kernel/Weight Application**: Computing weights $w_i(\mathbf{p})$ based on the distance between $\mathbf{p}$ and the sample centers $\mathbf{p}_i$. The interpolated value is:
    $$
    V(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i} w_i(\mathbf{p}) V_i
    $$
    The choice of kernel (e.g., linear, cubic, inverse distance) determines the spatial extent (number of neighbors) and frequency response (aliasing vs. blurring) [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Coordinate Transformation**: Mapping world-space or camera-space coordinates to the local coordinate system of the volume data (e.g., normalized $[-1, 1]$ or $[0, 1]$ ranges) before interpolation.

## Subtopics

### Linear Interpolation
Linear interpolation uses a kernel with a support of two samples. It is less sensitive to noise than cubic-spline but produces larger edge location errors compared to quadratic resampling [S1, unofficial].

### Quadratic Resampling
Quadratic resampling uses a kernel with a support of three samples. It yields the smallest edge location error among the common polynomial splines and is less sensitive to noise in the data compared to linear and cubic-spline functions [S1, unofficial].

### Cubic-Spline Interpolation
Cubic-spline interpolation uses a kernel with a support of four samples. While often considered the best for grey-value reconstruction, it causes overshoot, producing negative values in step responses [S1, unofficial]. This is invalid for MR data due to the complex modulus operator in reconstruction, and it yields the largest edge location error [S1, unofficial].

### Edge location estimation via mean thresholding
#### definition
Estimates the position of a 1D step edge by finding the coordinate where the re-sampled grey-value equals the arithmetic mean of the grey-values on both sides of the step [S1, unofficial].

#### step
1. Re-sample discrete samples across the step using an interpolation function [S1, unofficial].
2. Compute the mean of the grey-values on the low and high sides of the step [S1, unofficial].
3. Locate the coordinate where the re-sampled curve intersects this mean value [S1, unofficial].

#### comparison
Quadratic re-sampling yields the smallest edge location error, linear interpolation performs better than cubic-spline but worse than quadratic, and cubic-spline interpolation yields the largest error [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The analysis of interpolation functions for resampling in the context of medical volume rendering was notably detailed in the work on Iso-Surface Volume Rendering [S1, unofficial]. The research highlights that while cubic-spline is often preferred for image reconstruction, it is suboptimal for edge location accuracy and introduces artifacts like ripples on oblique surfaces when sample distances are large [S1, unofficial]. The error in edge location estimation grows approximately with the third power of the sample distance [S1, unofficial].
