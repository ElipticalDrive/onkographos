# POCS Resampling Filter Design
Also called: POCS filter, Projection on Convex Sets

## Overview
POCS Resampling Filter Design is a method for constructing an optimal resampling filter for frequency domain volume rendering (FVR) that approximates the ideal sinc filter while respecting computational constraints [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of designing a filter that is simultaneously limited in spatial extent (to prevent aliasing from periodic dataset copies) and limited in frequency extent (to reduce computation time), which is theoretically impossible due to the Uncertainty Theorem [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from simple windowed sinc filters by iteratively enforcing hard spatial and frequency domain constraints, and from tri-linear interpolation by providing significantly higher quality with comparable support size [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method computes a discrete 3D resampling filter $h(x,y,z)$ through an iterative projection process that alternates between spatial and frequency domain constraints [S1, unofficial].

**Inputs:**
- Desired spatial support size $N_s$ (e.g., $3 \times 3 \times 3$ or $5 \times 5 \times 5$ samples) [S1, unofficial].
- Desired frequency support size $N_f$ (typically matching $N_s$ or defined by aliasing avoidance criteria) [S1, unofficial].
- Initial window function (Hamming) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- A discrete 3D filter kernel $h$ of size $N_s \times N_s \times N_s$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- The filter is stored as a 3D array of floating-point values [S1, unofficial].
- Intermediate states are stored in spatial and frequency domains [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python

    # 1. Initialize frequency domain filter with Hamming-windowed sinc
    H = hamming_windowed_sinc(N_f)
    
    for k in range(max_iter):
        # 2. Transform to spatial domain (infinite extent)
        h = idft(H)
        
        # 3. Spatial constraint: chop tails outside first periodic copy
        h_trunc = zero_outside_box(h, N_s)
        
        # 4. Transform back to frequency domain
        H_prime = dft(h_trunc)
        
        # 5. Frequency constraint: chop tails outside desired frequency support
        H_new = zero_outside_box(H_prime, N_f)
        
        # 6. Check convergence or continue iteration
        if converged(H, H_new):
            break
        H = H_new
        
    # Final spatial filter
    return idft(H)
```

**Formulas:**
- Initial frequency domain representation: $H_0(u,v,w) = \text{sinc}(u) \cdot \text{sinc}(v) \cdot \text{sinc}(w) \cdot W(u,v,w)$, where $\text{sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}$ and $W$ is the Hamming window [S1, unofficial].
- Spatial constraint enforcement: $h'_k(x,y,z) = h_k(x,y,z)$ if $|x|,|y|,|z| \leq N_s/2$, else $0$ [S1, unofficial].
- Frequency constraint enforcement: $H_{k+1}(u,v,w) = H'_{k+1}(u,v,w)$ if $|u|,|v|,|w| \leq N_f/2$, else $0$ [S1, unofficial].
- Final filter: $h_{\text{final}} = h'_K$ where $K$ is the iteration count [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Filter extent: Commonly $3 \times 3 \times 3$ or $5 \times 5 \times 5$ frequency samples [S1, unofficial].
- Window: Hamming window is used for initialization [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Tri-linear interpolation is not a perfect filter and introduces noticeable artifacts, such as aliasing parts of the skull back into the front of the nose [S1, unofficial].
- The POCS filter mitigates these artifacts significantly compared to tri-linear interpolation [S1, unofficial].
- Larger filters (e.g., $5 \times 5 \times 5$) perform better but are more complex to implement in hardware and take more time to evaluate [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Spatial Premultiplication
A compensation technique for the attenuation of outer regions in the dataset caused by practical interpolation filters, which are approximations of a box function and do not have constant amplitude in the passband [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard resampling by applying a pre-transform correction rather than post-filtering, and should be chosen when passband uniformity is critical for quantitative volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. A pitfall is that premultiplication increases stop band energy because all periodic copies of the spatial dataset are premultiplied with the inverse filter function [S1, unofficial]. See [Frequency Domain Volume Rendering with Sinc Resampling](826-frequency-domain-volume-rendering-with-sinc-resampling.md).

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Depends on the filter extent $N$ and the number of iterations [S1, unofficial]. A $3 \times 3 \times 3$ filter is comparable in extent to tri-linear interpolation but requires more complex evaluation [S1, unofficial]. A $5 \times 5 \times 5$ filter takes more time to evaluate [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the filter size $N^3$ [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance:** The $3 \times 3 \times 3$ POCS filter performs much better than tri-linear interpolation [S1, unofficial]. The $5 \times 5 \times 5$ POCS filter performs even better [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** High quality, reduces aliasing artifacts significantly compared to simple interpolation methods [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** More complex to implement in hardware than tri-linear interpolation [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When high-quality volume rendering is required and computational resources allow for larger filter support [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** When hardware implementation simplicity is critical and tri-linear interpolation is sufficient [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was designed by Malzbender and described in the context of Fourier Volume Rendering (FVR) [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the limitations of the Uncertainty Theorem in signal processing, which states that a function cannot be simultaneously limited in both space and frequency domains [S1, unofficial]. The technique is part of the broader effort to improve the accuracy of frequency domain resampling in volume rendering, moving beyond simple windowed sinc functions to optimized filters that balance spatial and frequency constraints [S1, unofficial].
