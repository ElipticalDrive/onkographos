# Ray Traversal Schemes

Also called: Accumulate Projection, Depth Projection, Max Intensity Projection, Ray Traversal -Accumulate

## Overview
Ray Traversal Schemes define the aggregation logic applied to voxel samples collected along a ray to produce a final pixel value [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. While [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) determines *where* to sample, these schemes determine *how* to combine those samples [S2, unofficial]. They are distinguished from standard front-to-back alpha compositing by their ability to perform non-compositional aggregations (such as Maximum or Average) or to simplify the compositing process for specific visualization goals like X-ray simulation [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the principle that a continuous function $V(\mathbf{p})$ must be approximated from a discrete set of samples $V_i$ located at grid positions $\mathbf{p}_i$. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Query Positioning**: Determining the 3D coordinate $\mathbf{p}$ along the ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ where a value is needed. This may involve converting depth parameters to physical distances or warping coordinates for neural encodings.
2.  **Kernel/Weight Application**: Computing weights $w_i(\mathbf{p})$ based on the distance between $\mathbf{p}$ and the sample centers $\mathbf{p}_i$. The interpolated value is:
    $$
    V(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i} w_i(\mathbf{p}) V_i
    $$
    The choice of kernel (e.g., linear, cubic, inverse distance) determines the spatial extent (number of neighbors) and frequency response (aliasing vs. blurring).
3.  **Coordinate Transformation**: Mapping world-space or camera-space coordinates to the local coordinate system of the volume data (e.g., normalized $[-1, 1]$ or $[0, 1]$ ranges) before interpolation.

## Subtopics

### [Pixel Intensity Evaluation with Scene Aggregates](10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md)
[Pixel Intensity Evaluation with Scene Aggregates](10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md) is a rendering method that integrates radiance over a pixel footprint using aggregated visibility functions to handle inter-voxel correlation and background blending without order-dependent ray marching. It solves the problem of correctly blending scene aggregate contributions with external background objects, which standard volumetric models fail to distinguish when voxels have identical local properties but different spatial correlations. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [X-Ray Volume Rendering](11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md) by explicitly modeling the semi-transparency of geometry primitives via boundary visibility, rather than relying solely on scalar accumulation or extremum selection.

### [X-Ray Volume Rendering](11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md)
[X-Ray Volume Rendering](11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md) is a volume rendering technique that computes the final color by linearly accumulating or averaging sampled voxel intensities along a ray, simulating a transmission view (like a CT scan) where opacity and alpha compositing are ignored. Unlike standard ray casting which uses front-to-back alpha blending to simulate opaque/translucent surfaces, X-Ray rendering sums the raw color values, resulting in a transparent/transmissive appearance where internal structures are visible as a superposition of densities. It differs from its sibling [Pixel Intensity Evaluation with Scene Aggregates](10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md) by specifically targeting the linear integration of voxel values rather than general scene aggregates.

### Voxel Aggregation Methods
These methods focus on probabilistic or differentiable aggregation of voxels along a ray, often for inverse rendering or neural representation tasks [S4, unofficial].

*   **Occupancy Probability (Yan et al.)**: Casts a ray from the pixel, samples all associated voxels, and chooses the one with the highest occupancy probability [S4, unofficial].
*   **Stopping Probability (Tulsiani et al.)**: Computes the probability that a ray stops at a certain distance, allowing rendering of depth maps, color images, and foreground masks [S4, unofficial].
*   **Bidirectional Emission-Absorption (Henzler et al.)**: Introduces a bidirectional emission-absorption (EA) light radiation model for emitting materials, in addition to visual hull (VH) and absorption-only (AO) models [S4, unofficial].
*   **Iterative Accumulation (Lombardi et al.)**: Casts a ray from each pixel and iteratively accumulates color and opacity probabilities. Remaining voxels are discarded when cumulative opacity reaches a maximum value [S4, unofficial].
*   **Surface-Weighted Sum (Jiang et al.)**: Casts a ray from the pixel and locates the surface voxel closest to the camera. The final pixel color is computed as a weighted sum of the surface voxel and its neighboring voxels, with shading based on normals computed from voxel values [S4, unofficial]. Although locating the surface point is not a differentiable operation, the pixel color is differentiable with respect to the voxels around the surface point [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The classification of ray traversal schemes into Intensity, Max, Average, Accumulate, First, and Depth was established in early direct volume rendering literature [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The "Accumulate" scheme, which performs front-to-back compositing, is attributed to Levoy (1988) and is designed to make transparent layers visible [S1, S3, unofficial]. The "Max" scheme, or Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP), is widely used in Magnetic Resonance Angiography [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The "Average" scheme produces an X-ray-like picture by averaging values along the ray [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The "First" scheme extracts iso-surfaces [S1, S3, unofficial]. These schemes form the basis of the compositing stage in the volume rendering pipeline, following ray-tracing/resampling and preceding image pixel output [S2, S3, unofficial].
