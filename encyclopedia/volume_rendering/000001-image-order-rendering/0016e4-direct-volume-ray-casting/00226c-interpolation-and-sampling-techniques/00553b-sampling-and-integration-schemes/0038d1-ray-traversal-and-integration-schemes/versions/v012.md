# Ray Traversal and Integration Schemes

Also called: Ray Marching, Ray Casting, Compositing Loops, Line-of-Sight Integration

## Overview
Ray Traversal and Integration Schemes define the computational framework for evaluating the volume rendering integral by traversing a ray through a volumetric domain, sampling scalar or neural field values, and aggregating these samples into a final pixel color. This family solves the problem of discretizing continuous volumetric data for image-order rendering, distinguishing itself from object-order methods by iterating per-pixel and from pure Monte Carlo methods by often employing deterministic, structured traversal . While [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) provides the fundamental iterative loop structure, this family encompasses diverse aggregation strategies, from standard front-to-back alpha compositing to physical line-of-sight integration and stochastic path tracing [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on the principle that a continuous function $V(\mathbf{p})$ must be approximated from a discrete set of samples $V_i$ located at grid positions $\mathbf{p}_i$. The core mechanism involves:

1.  **Query Positioning**: Determining the 3D coordinate $\mathbf{p}$ along the ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ where a value is needed. This may involve converting depth parameters to physical distances or warping coordinates for neural encodings.
2.  **Kernel/Weight Application**: Computing weights $w_i(\mathbf{p})$ based on the distance between $\mathbf{p}$ and the sample centers $\mathbf{p}_i$. The interpolated value is:
    $$
    V(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i} w_i(\mathbf{p}) V_i
    $$
    The choice of kernel (e.g., linear, cubic, inverse distance) determines the spatial extent (number of neighbors) and frequency response (aliasing vs. blurring).
3.  **Coordinate Transformation**: Mapping world-space or camera-space coordinates to the local coordinate system of the volume data (e.g., normalized $[-1, 1]$ or $[0, 1]$ ranges) before interpolation.

## Subtopics

### [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md)
Ray marching is an iterative numerical algorithm that approximates the volume rendering integral by casting rays from the camera, taking discrete steps along the ray, querying volumetric properties (density, color), and accumulating these samples using alpha compositing. It solves the problem of rendering 3D volumes with real-time performance by avoiding complex geometric intersection tests, instead relying on uniform or adaptive sampling within a defined domain. Unlike [Discrete Ray Tracing](3020-discrete-ray-tracing.md) which traverses voxel grids for binary objects, or [Fixed Step Ray Marching](3136-fixed-step-ray-marching.md) which strictly integrates density, this core method provides the fundamental loop structure for direct volume rendering, often implemented in fragment shaders on the GPU.

### [Image-order Rendering Loop](2938-image-order-rendering-loop.md)
The image-order rendering loop is the fundamental control structure for volume ray casting, iterating over each pixel of the output image to generate a camera ray and invoking a raymarcher to compute the final color. It differs from object-order (rasterization) by being more "CPU-like" in its iteration logic, offering correct perspective projection, single-pass ray casting, and full 32-bit compositing precision without requiring intermediate buffers.

### [Front-to-Back Volume Ray Casting with HSV Shading and SOS/Attenuation/Reflection Filtering](4324-front-to-back-volume-ray-casting-with-hsv-shading-and-sos-at.md)
This method performs direct volume rendering by casting rays from the camera through each pixel into a 3D volume, sampling voxel values along the ray, and compositing them using a front-to-back alpha blending algorithm. It distinguishes itself from standard ray casting by applying strict filtering based on Scalar Output Scalar (SOS), Attenuation, and Reflection values to discard irrelevant samples, and by converting the resulting scalar data into RGB color via an HSV color space mapping. Unlike adaptive step methods, this implementation uses a fixed number of steps with early termination upon opacity saturation or boundary exit.

### [LOS-integration for visualization](4807-los-integration-for-visualization.md)
LOS-integration for visualization computes scalar physical quantities (electron column density, integrated emissivity, or emission-weighted temperature) along camera rays by integrating sampled neural field outputs or ground truth data. It differs from standard volume rendering compositing by performing a direct line-of-sight integral of the physical quantity itself, rather than compositing color and opacity to produce an image. This method is specifically designed for solar physics visualization to avoid artifacts from the saturated chromospheric base by restricting integration to the corona shell.

### [Monte Carlo Path Tracing for Volume Rendering](4980-monte-carlo-path-tracing-for-volume-rendering.md)
Monte Carlo Path Tracing for Volume Rendering is a unified, brute-force, progressive stochastic rendering framework that produces photo-realistic images of volumetric data without relying on pre-computed quantities or auxiliary volumes. It distinguishes itself from traditional ray marching by using Woodcock tracking to efficiently handle light attenuation and shadows, and by integrating depth-of-field and hybrid surface/volumetric scattering through stochastic sampling rather than slice-based approximations.

### [Ray Traversal Schemes](6890-ray-traversal-schemes.md)
Ray Traversal Schemes define the aggregation logic applied to voxel samples collected along a ray to produce a final pixel value. While [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) determines *where* to sample, these schemes determine *how* to combine those samples. They are distinguished from standard front-to-back alpha compositing by their ability to perform non-compositional aggregations (such as Maximum or Average) or to simplify the compositing process for specific visualization goals like X-ray simulation.

### [Ray Casting and Compositing Loop](9826-ray-casting-and-compositing-loop.md)
Ray Casting and Compositing Loop is a single-pass GPU method for direct volume rendering that renders scalar data using front-to-back compositing. It samples scalar values along rays and maps them directly to color and alpha without explicit surface extraction. Unlike IsoSurface Ray Marching, this method does not detect zero crossings or compute normals. It sets itself apart from [Image-order Rendering Loop](2938-image-order-rendering-loop.md) by explicitly managing the ray traversal loop and compositing state within the shader, and from [Monte Carlo Path Tracing for Volume Rendering](4980-monte-carlo-path-tracing-for-volume-rendering.md) by using deterministic, uniform step sizes rather than stochastic sampling.

### [Ray Casting with Ray Subdividing](11392-ray-casting-with-ray-subdividing.md)
Ray Casting with Ray Subdividing is a technique to maintain adequate spatial sampling rates in volume rendering under perspective projection, where rays diverge as they travel deeper into the scene. It addresses the problem of undersampling distant volume slices by dynamically splitting rays when the local ray density falls below a threshold, ensuring that the footprint of each sample remains consistent with the desired resolution. Unlike fixed-step ray marching, this method adapts the integration step size based on geometric divergence rather than local data features.

### [Cone Casting with Inscribed Sphere Sampling](11501-cone-casting-with-inscribed-sphere-sampling.md)
Cone casting with inscribed sphere sampling is a rendering technique that formulates a pixel as a disc on the image plane and emits a cone from the camera's projection center to sample the volume, avoiding the aliasing artifacts of distance-agnostic ray casting. Unlike sibling methods that treat pixels as points or use anisotropic Gaussian distributions for area sampling, this method uses isotropic spheres inscribed within the cone to match pre-filtered feature encodings.

#### Adaptive Segment Length
- **Definition**: A strategy to optimize speed while reducing artifacts by varying segment length based on contribution to ray color [S1, unofficial].
- **Step**: Use smaller segments when the contribution to the ray color is large, and longer segments when the contribution is low [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of ray casting for volume visualization was pioneered by Levoy in 1988, who introduced the front-to-back compositing algorithm and the use of transfer functions to map scalar data to optical properties [G]. Early implementations relied on fixed-step ray marching due to hardware limitations. As GPU architectures evolved, the image-order rendering loop became the standard for real-time volume rendering, allowing complex shading and compositing to be performed in fragment shaders . The introduction of neural radiance fields (NeRF) and subsequent neural volume rendering techniques necessitated new traversal schemes, such as cone casting and inscribed sphere sampling, to handle continuous, implicit representations efficiently [S1, unofficial].
