# Ray Casting with Ray Subdividing

Also called: Adaptive Ray Subdivision, Ray Splitting for Perspective Rendering, Ray Subdividing

## Overview
Ray Casting with Ray Subdividing is a technique to maintain consistent spatial sampling rates in volume rendering under perspective projection, where rays diverge as they travel deeper into the scene [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of undersampling distant volume regions by dynamically splitting rays when the inter-ray distance exceeds the required sampling density [S1, unofficial]. Unlike fixed-step ray marching, this method adapts the integration step size based on view-dependent geometry [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A set of primary rays originating from the camera center, a volume dataset, and a target sampling density (or maximum allowable inter-ray distance).
- Output: A rendered image with correct aliasing characteristics across the depth range.

**Data Structures:**
- Ray Queue: A list of active rays, each storing origin $O$, direction $D$, current depth $t$, and accumulated color/alpha.
- Split Threshold: A scalar value representing the maximum allowed distance between adjacent rays at the current depth.

**Steps:**
1. Initialize the Ray Queue with primary rays from the camera.
2. While the Ray Queue is not empty:
   a. Pop a ray from the queue.
   b. Determine the current inter-ray distance at the ray's current depth $t$. This is typically calculated by projecting the ray's position onto the image plane and comparing it with neighbors, or by estimating the footprint size on the volume slice [S1, unofficial].
   c. If the inter-ray distance is greater than the Split Threshold:
      i. Split the ray into two or more sub-rays. The new origins are offset slightly to cover the same solid angle, or the direction is adjusted to maintain coverage [S1, unofficial].
      ii. Push the sub-rays back into the Ray Queue.
   d. If the inter-ray distance is within the threshold:
      i. Perform a standard ray casting step (sample volume, composite) for the ray.
      ii. Advance the ray's depth $t$.
      iii. If the ray has exited the volume, discard it. Otherwise, push it back into the queue for the next iteration.

**Formulas:**
- Inter-ray distance $d_{ij}$ at depth $t$ for rays $i$ and $j$:
  $$ d_{ij}(t) = \| (O_i + t D_i) - (O_j + t D_j) \| $$
- Split condition:
  $$ d_{ij}(t) > \Delta_{max} $$
  where $\Delta_{max}$ is the maximum allowed sampling interval, derived from the Nyquist frequency of the volume data [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- $\Delta_{max}$: Typically set to the voxel size or half the voxel size to ensure adequate sampling [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- Precision: Floating-point errors in ray origin/position can cause gaps or overlaps when splitting [G].
- Boundary: Rays near the volume boundary may split unnecessarily if the threshold is not adjusted for partial volume effects [G].
- Degenerate Input: Parallel rays (orthographic projection) do not diverge, so subdivision is unnecessary [S2, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def ray_casting_with_subdivision(primary_rays, volume, delta_max):
    ray_queue = list(primary_rays)
    rendered_image = {}
    
    while ray_queue:
        ray = ray_queue.pop(0)
        current_pos = ray.origin + ray.t * ray.direction
        
        # Estimate inter-ray distance at current depth
        # In practice, this requires neighbor information or a global grid
        # For simplicity, we assume a function estimate_inter_ray_distance exists
        inter_ray_dist = estimate_inter_ray_distance(ray, current_pos, ray_queue)
        
        if inter_ray_dist > delta_max:
            # Split the ray
            sub_rays = split_ray(ray, delta_max)
            for sub_ray in sub_rays:
                ray_queue.append(sub_ray)
        else:
            # Perform ray casting step
            color, alpha = cast_ray_step(ray, volume)
            rendered_image[ray.pixel_id] = composite(rendered_image.get(ray.pixel_id, (0,0,0)), color, alpha)
            
            # Advance ray
            ray.t += ray.step_size
            if ray.t < volume.max_depth:
                ray_queue.append(ray)
            else:
                pass # Ray exited volume
```

## Subtopics

### Adaptive Ray Subdivision
Focuses on optimizing the split/merge decisions to minimize computational cost while maintaining quality [S1, unofficial].

### Ray Splitting for Perspective Rendering
Specifically addresses the divergence issue in perspective cameras [S2, unofficial].

### Ray Subdividing
General term for the technique of dividing rays to maintain sampling density [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- Time Complexity: $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $S$ is the average number of splits per ray. $S$ increases with depth and field of view.
- Memory Complexity: $O(N \cdot D)$, where $D$ is the depth of the volume, due to the ray queue.
- Strengths: Maintains consistent image quality across the depth range; avoids aliasing in distant regions [S1, unofficial].
- Limitations: Increased computational cost due to ray splitting; complex implementation for neighbor distance estimation [S2, unofficial].
- When to use: Perspective rendering of large volumes where distant details are important [S2, unofficial].
- When not to use: Orthographic projection or when performance is critical and distant aliasing is acceptable [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Originated as an equivalent approach to EWA splatting for raycasting, aiming to maintain proper sampling rates in distant volume slices [S1, unofficial]. Kreeger et al. proposed improvements for optimal split and merge strategies [S1, unofficial]. It is primarily used in perspective volume rendering to address the sampling density fall-off [S2, unofficial].
