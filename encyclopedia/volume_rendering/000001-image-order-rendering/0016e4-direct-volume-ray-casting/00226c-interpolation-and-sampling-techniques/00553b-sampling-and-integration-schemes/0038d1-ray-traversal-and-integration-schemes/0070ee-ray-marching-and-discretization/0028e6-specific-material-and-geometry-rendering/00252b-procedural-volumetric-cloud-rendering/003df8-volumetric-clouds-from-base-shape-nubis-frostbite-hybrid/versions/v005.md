# Volumetric Clouds from Base Shape (Nubis/Frostbite Hybrid)

Also called: Hillaire 2016 light, Nubis density, Planetary Cloud Layer, Spherical Shell Clouds, Volumetric Clouds as a Spherical Shell, Wrenninge multiple scattering

## Overview
This technique renders volumetric clouds as a spherical shell surrounding a planet, using raymarching between defined inner and outer radii with density derived from 3D noise fields rather than finite 2D textures [S1, unofficial]. It combines Schneider’s density model from Horizon Zero Dawn’s Nubis system with Hillaire 2016 light transport from Frostbite, allowing the cloud layer to conform to any base shape by sampling noise at 3D points in the shading frame [S2, unofficial]. Unlike local volumetrics or ground fog, this method treats clouds as a planetary-scale feature, supporting multiple planets and camera positions inside, outside, or between shells [S1, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Planet center and radius [S2, unofficial].
*   Cloud layer inner and outer altitudes relative to planet radius [S2, unofficial].
*   Noise textures: 128³ Perlin-Worley shape noise and 32³ Worley detail noise [S2, unofficial].
*   Camera position and view direction [S1, unofficial].
*   Sun direction and intensity [S2, unofficial].
*   Atmosphere transmittance data required for correct lighting [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Cloud color and opacity per pixel [S2, unofficial].
*   Transmittance and distance buffers for the camera-centered direction grid [S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures

**CloudLayer Component Parameters:**
*   `inner_radius`: Float. The inner boundary of the cloud shell [S1, unofficial].
*   `outer_radius`: Float. The outer boundary of the cloud shell [S1, unofficial].
*   `coverage`: Float. Authored parameter controlling cloud density [S1, unofficial].
*   `density`: Float. Global density multiplier [S1, unofficial].
*   `wind`: Vector3. Render-space wind direction vector [S2, unofficial].
*   `noise_material`: Asset reference. Contains noise parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Camera-Centred Direction Grid:**
*   Dimensions: 1536 × 768 texels, centered around the base's up vector [S2, unofficial].
*   Resolution: ~0.23° per texel [S2, unofficial].
*   Texel Layout:
    *   `cloud_light`: Vector3. Cloud light contribution including air in front [S2, unofficial].
    *   `transmittance`: Float. Light transmittance through the cloud [S2, unofficial].
    *   `front_distance`: Float. Distance to the front of the cloud shell [S2, unofficial].
    *   `mean_distance`: Float. Mean distance of scattering events [S2, unofficial].
    *   `opacity`: Float. Accumulated opacity [S2, unofficial].

**Shadow Map:**
*   Dimensions: 512² [S2, unofficial].
*   Alignment: Aligned along the sun vector around the camera [S2, unofficial].
*   Reach: Shadows do not extend past the map boundary (e.g., 64 km on Earth, or 4 camera altitudes) [S2, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Ray-Sphere Intersection:** Calculate the entry and exit points of the view ray relative to the cloud shell's inner and outer radii using the shared AtmosphereVolume intersection logic [S1, unofficial]. If the ray misses the shell, the shader early-outs [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Layer Scaling and Lifting:** Scale the cloud layer height relative to the planet's radius [S2, unofficial]. Lift the layer so its bottom is at least 1.25 times the ground's maximum relief to ensure the marcher is unaware of terrain features [S2, unofficial]. For Earth (3.3 km relief), the layer is lifted to 4.1–6.6 km [S2, unofficial]. For a 2 km planet, it is 78–208 m [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Density Calculation (Schneider/Nubis):** Sample the 128³ Perlin-Worley shape noise at the current 3D ray position [S1, unofficial]. Determine cloud cover and type based on thresholds measured directly on the noise values at a "weather scale" [S2, unofficial]. Apply erosion from separate noise fields if available [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Ray Marching:** March the ray through the shell volume using a fixed step size and empty-space skipping inherited from the parent sampling scheme [S1, unofficial]. The marcher does not account for terrain occlusion; a depth-aware march is required to fix clouds wrapping peaks [S2, unofficial].
5.  **Light Transport (Hillaire 2016):** Calculate sun transmittance through the atmosphere using the atmosphere's transmittance march, then through the cloud using progressive shadow samples from the 512² shadow map [S2, unofficial]. Use a dual-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase function for scattering [S2, unofficial]. Implement Wrenninge's multiple-scattering octaves for the cloud interior [S2, unofficial]. Compute lighting using a few in-scattering samples rather than a second full march [S1, unofficial]. Sample the sky's light contribution from the atmosphere's multiple-scattering table [S2, unofficial].
6.  **Integration:** Integrate scattering such that light scattering applies to the light reaching the clouds and the light they send on [S1, unofficial]. Clouds are marched in the same pass as the atmosphere or ordered against it, as clouds sit inside the atmosphere [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Compositing:** The sky shader places clouds over the atmosphere's sky [S2, unofficial]. The page shader adds clouds in front of a fragment to its FOG buffer [S2, unofficial]. The `light()` function reads the shadow map (one fetch) to determine cloud shadows [S2, unofficial].
8.  **Distance Policy:** From orbit, render the cloud layer as a texture on a sphere [S1, unofficial]. From the ground, render as a fly-through volume [S1, unofficial]. The transition must be continuous and measured, not guessed [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas

**Ray-Sphere Intersection:**
Given ray origin $\mathbf{o}$, direction $\mathbf{d}$, sphere center $\mathbf{c}$, and radius $r$:
$$ \mathbf{m} = \mathbf{o} - \mathbf{c} $$
$$ b = \mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{d} $$
$$ c = \mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{m} - r^2 $$
$$ \Delta = b^2 - c $$
If $\Delta < 0$, the ray misses. Otherwise, $t_{entry} = -b - \sqrt{\Delta}$ and $t_{exit} = -b + \sqrt{\Delta}$.

**Beer-Lambert Integration:**
$$ T(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \sigma_t(s) \, ds\right) $$
$$ L = \int_0^t \sigma_s(s) \, T(s) \, ds $$
where $\sigma_t$ is the extinction coefficient, $\sigma_s$ is the scattering coefficient, $T(t)$ is transmittance, and $L$ is accumulated radiance [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode

```python
def render_volumetric_clouds(camera_pos, planet_center, planet_radius,
                             inner_altitude, outer_altitude,
                             shape_noise_tex, detail_noise_tex,
                             sun_direction, atmosphere_data, shadow_map_tex):
    # 1. Allocate camera-centred direction grid (1536x768)
    grid = allocate_2d_array(1536, 768)

    # 2. Compute layer bounds
    max_terrain_relief = compute_max_terrain_height(planet_center)
    layer_thickness = 3000.0  # Typical Earth value [S2, unofficial]
    inner_radius = planet_radius + max_terrain_relief * 1.25
    outer_radius = inner_radius + layer_thickness

    # 3. March grid texels (one invocation per 2x2 block)
    for block in grid.blocks_2x2:
        for texel in block:
            ray_origin = camera_pos
            ray_direction = compute_ray_direction(texel.uv, camera_pos)

            # Ray-sphere intersection
            entry, exit = intersect_sphere_shell(ray_origin, ray_direction,
                                                 planet_center, inner_radius, outer_radius)
            if entry is None:
                continue  # Early-out if ray misses shell

            transmittance = 1.0
            cloud_light = vec3(0.0)
            front_distance = 0.0
            mean_distance = 0.0
            opacity = 0.0
            step_size = compute_fixed_step_size(entry, exit)
            pos = entry

            while pos < exit and transmittance > 0.01:
                # Density calculation (Schneider/Nubis)
                noise_val = sample_3d_noise(shape_noise_tex, pos)
                density = compute_density_from_noise(noise_val)

                if density > 0.0:
                    # Light transport (Hillaire 2016)
                    atm_transmittance = atmosphere_data.get_transmittance(pos, sun_direction)
                    cloud_shadow = sample_shadow_map(shadow_map_tex, pos, sun_direction)
                    phase = dual_lobe_henyey_greenstein_phase(sun_direction, ray_direction)
                    multi_scatter = get_multiple_scattering_octaves(pos)
                    sky_light = atmosphere_data.get_multiple_scattering_table(pos)

                    scattering = density * (atm_transmittance * cloud_shadow * phase + sky_light * multi_scatter)
                    cloud_light += scattering * step_size * transmittance
                    transmittance *= exp(-density * step_size)

                    front_distance = length(pos - ray_origin)
                    mean_distance = (mean_distance * opacity + front_distance * density * step_size) / (opacity + density * step_size)
                    opacity += density * step_size

                pos += ray_direction * step_size

            texel.cloud_light = cloud_light
            texel.transmittance = transmittance
            texel.front_distance = front_distance
            texel.mean_distance = mean_distance
            texel.opacity = opacity

    # 4. Compositing
    for pixel_uv in screen_uv:
        texel = grid.sample_2d(pixel_uv)
        final_color = atmosphere_data.sky_color * texel.transmittance + texel.cloud_light
        fog_buffer.add(final_color, texel.opacity)
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Terrain Occlusion:** The layer is lifted, not composited [S2, unofficial]. The march knows nothing of the terrain, so clouds do not wrap peaks [S2, unofficial]. A depth-aware march is required to fix this [S2, unofficial].
*   **Jagged Edges:** The grid's resolution (0.23° per texel) shows as jagged edges on a small planet seen whole [S2, unofficial].
*   **Shadow Reach:** Shadows do not extend past the shadow map boundary (64 km on Earth, or 4 camera altitudes) [S2, unofficial].
*   **Color Accuracy:** Clouds take the scene's warm sun color, reddened again by the air, resulting in a pinkish-tan appearance [S2, unofficial]. There is no white balance [S2, unofficial]. Fixing this requires setting the scene's light to white, which is a scene change [S2, unofficial].
*   **Wind Direction:** The wind is a render-space vector [S2, unofficial]. On a planet, the noise drifts through the shell away from the pole [S2, unofficial]. It is now aligned with the wind; the old cloud layer drifted against it [S2, unofficial].
*   **Grid Limits:** The froxel grid ends at `ClusterSettings::far` (200 m default) [S1, unofficial]. Planetary clouds are kilometres out, outside the grid by two orders of magnitude [S1, unofficial]. Assuming the grid covers the clouds is a mistake [S1, unofficial].
*   **Atmosphere Dependency:** Clouds lit by a sun that ignores the atmosphere look wrong [S1, unofficial]. The atmosphere component must be implemented first [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   The technique inherits sampling techniques from the parent scheme: 3D noise textures, empty-space skipping, and fixed step size [S1, unofficial].
*   The camera-centered direction grid requires 1536 × 768 texels, with one invocation per 2×2 block [S2, unofficial].
*   Shadow map updates are per-frame [S2, unofficial].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **OneXFly (10 W):** Current implementation cost is 39 ms when switched off, which is the only reason the frame meets the 13.9 ms budget [S1, unofficial]. Direct lighting alone is at twice budget [S1, unofficial].
*   **RTX 3070 Laptop (Power-Saver):**
    *   Forest5cm, steady, 300 frames: Clouds off 19.05 ms, Clouds on 21.21 ms (+2.2 ms) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Earth's pole: 4.8 → 8.1 ms (sky full of clouds) [S2, unofficial].
    *   Earth from 100 km: 5.2 → 11 ms [S2, unofficial].
*   The first version read cloud shadow per fragment, costing +33 ms in the forest [S2, unofficial]. The shadow map optimization removed this cost [S2, unofficial].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Conforms to any base shape; supports multiple planets; efficient lighting via few in-scattering samples and shadow maps; continuous transition between orbit and ground views [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** No terrain occlusion (clouds float above peaks); jagged edges on small planets; limited shadow reach; color accuracy issues due to lack of white balance; high cost on low-power hardware [S1, S2, unofficial].

**When to Use:**
*   Planetary-scale cloud rendering where clouds must follow the curvature of the planet [S1, unofficial].
*   Real-time engines requiring efficient volumetric lighting with shadow maps [S2, unofficial].

**When Not to Use:**
*   Local volumetrics or ground fog (use froxel buffer instead) [S1, unofficial].
*   Scenes requiring precise terrain interaction with clouds (requires depth-aware march) [S2, unofficial].
*   Low-power hardware where the 39 ms overhead is prohibitive [S1, unofficial].

## Background

**Origin:**
The technique is based on Guerrilla's Nubis work for Horizon Zero Dawn (Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015), which models clouds as a spherical shell over the planet [S1, unofficial]. The lighting model is derived from Hillaire 2016 (Frostbite) [S2, unofficial].

**History and Development:**
*   **SIGGRAPH 2015:** Schneider published "The Real-Time Volumetric Cloudscapes of Horizon Zero Dawn," establishing the spherical shell model and Nubis density [S1, unofficial].
*   **Frostbite:** Hillaire 2016 introduced the energy-conserving light transport and dual-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase function [S2, unofficial].
*   **Wrenninge:** Multiple-scattering octaves were adapted for cloud interior lighting [S2, unofficial].
*   **Implementation:** The technique was implemented in the opti-voxel engine (ov::Clouds component), integrated with Godot sky and page shaders, and owned by World with the atmosphere [S2, unofficial].

**Usage:**
Used in the opti-voxel engine, integrated with Godot sky and page shaders [S2, unofficial]. Owned by World with the atmosphere [S2, unofficial].
