# Sphere-Guided Training Limitations
## Overview
Sphere-guided training in neural implicit surface reconstruction exhibits three primary limitations: constrained exploration after initial convergence, amplification of systematic artifacts, and incorrect appearance-geometry coupling when combined with warping methods [S1]. These limitations arise because the method relies on a coarse geometry estimate established early in training, which dictates the sampling distribution and prevents recovery of uncovered regions or correction of initial errors [S1].
## Shared principles
The limitations stem from the fundamental mechanism of sphere-guided ray marching:
1. **Ray-Sphere Intersection Filtering**: Rays are only integrated if they intersect the current sphere cloud; rays missing the cloud are skipped entirely [S1].
2. **Sampling Bias**: The probability of sampling a region is proportional to the density of spheres in that region, causing heavier sampling in regions with many spheres and zero sampling in uncovered regions [S1].
3. **Early Convergence Dependency**: The sphere cloud is optimized jointly with the neural network, meaning the coarse geometry estimated in early training iterations defines the initial sphere distribution and heavily influences the subsequent optimization path [S1].
## Subtopics

### Exploration Limitations
Sphere-guided methods suffer from limited exploration capabilities after the initial convergence phase [S1]. Because rays that do not intersect the sphere cloud are skipped, the method cannot recover uncovered regions such as thin stems after initial convergence [S1]. This results in incomplete reconstruction of thin features that were not well-represented in the early training stages [S1].

### Artifact Amplification
Systematic artifacts present in the initial geometry estimate are not corrected by the sphere guidance mechanism and may be amplified during optimization [S1].

### Appearance-Geometry Coupling Artifacts
When combined with appearance warping methods like NeuralWarp, appearance variations on object bases can be incorrectly baked into the surface geometry [S1]. This artifact may be magnified by the sphere guidance [S1].

## Background
These limitations were identified in "Sphere-Guided Training of Neural Implicit Surfaces" [S1]. The reliance on coarse geometry estimated early in training was highlighted as a main limitation, particularly for thin structures such as Ficus stems and for scenes with complex appearance variations like the Materials scene [S1]. The issues were documented alongside ablation studies comparing the method against NeuS and NSVF variants [S1].
