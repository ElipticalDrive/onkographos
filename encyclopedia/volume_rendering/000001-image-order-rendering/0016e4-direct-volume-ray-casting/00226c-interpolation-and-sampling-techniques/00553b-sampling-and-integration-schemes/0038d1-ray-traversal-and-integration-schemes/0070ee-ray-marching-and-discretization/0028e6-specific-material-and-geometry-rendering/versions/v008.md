# Specific Material and Geometry Rendering

Also called: Material Parameter Setting, DVGO Density and Color Rendering, Procedural Volumetric Cloud Rendering, Mr-Voxel Volume Rendering, Ray-marched Volumetric Fog, Surface and DRR Rendering Modes, Material Percentage Volume Rendering, Volumetric Heat Haze, Volumetric Geometry Rendering

## Overview
Specific Material and Geometry Rendering encompasses the techniques for defining optical properties, managing data structures, and handling specific geometric constraints (such as spherical shells or binary objects) during ray marching. It solves the problem of mapping raw spatial data (voxels, grids, or procedural functions) to physically plausible light interactions and rendering specific material types (clouds, fog, multi-material mixtures) that require specialized traversal or shading logic. It is distinguished from generic ray marching by its focus on the *content* of the volume (material definitions, procedural noise, neural features) and specific *geometric boundaries* (spherical shells, scene intersections) rather than just the integration scheme.

## Shared principles
The family shares the fundamental volume rendering integral, where radiance $L$ is accumulated along a ray $r(t) = o + td$:
$$ L = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma_t(r(t)) L_e(r(t)) e^{-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma_t(r(s)) ds} dt + \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma_s(r(t)) \int_{S^2} f_r(r(t), \omega') L_i(r(t), \omega') d\omega' e^{-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma_t(r(s)) ds} dt $$
where $\sigma_t$ is the extinction coefficient, $\sigma_s$ is the scattering coefficient, $L_e$ is emission, and $f_r$ is the phase function.

Common implementation principles include:
1.  **Discretization:** The integral is approximated via Riemann sums or quadrature, requiring a step size $\Delta l$ or $\delta$.
2.  **Transmittance Accumulation:** Transmittance $T(t) = e^{-\int_{t_n}^{t} \sigma_t(r(s)) ds}$ is updated multiplicatively at each step: $T_{new} = T_{old} \cdot e^{-\sigma_t \Delta l}$.
3.  **Termination:** Marching stops when transmittance drops below a threshold (e.g., $10^{-3}$) or maximum steps are reached, often using Russian Roulette for unbiased termination [S1, unofficial][S3].
4.  **Sampling:** Values are retrieved via interpolation (trilinear for grids, texture sampling for procedural volumes) at sample points.

## Subtopics

### [Procedural Volumetric Cloud Rendering](3480-procedural-volumetric-cloud-rendering.md)
A real-time technique for rendering planetary-scale cloud layers as spherical shells using procedural noise. It differs from local fog methods by handling large-scale geometry (spherical shells) and using multi-octave noise for density shaping. It is chosen when generating dynamic, physically plausible cloudscapes without pre-baked textures.

### [Mr-Voxel Volume Rendering](3874-mr-voxel-volume-rendering.md)
A direct volume ray casting technique using a traditional voxel data structure and explicit ray traversal. It differs from neural methods (like DVGO) by relying on explicit grids and from procedural clouds by its specific implementation of Russian Roulette and single-light approximation. It is chosen for standard voxel-based volumes with explicit geometry.

### [Ray-marched Volumetric Fog](4248-ray-marched-volumetric-fog.md)
Simulates aerial perspective and light shafts by treating fog as a participating medium traced via forward ray-marching. It differs from cloud rendering by using uniform or simple density fields and approximating the integral with a Riemann sum. It is chosen for real-time atmospheric effects and god rays.

### [Material Parameter Setting](5335-material-parameter-setting.md)
Defines the binding between spatial data and optical properties (absorption, scattering, emission). It distinguishes itself through two paradigms: **Structured Object-Based** (using typed fields and texture descriptors) and **Procedural API-Based** (using context handles and integer constants). It is the foundational step for configuring any volume material.

### [DVGO Density and Color Rendering](12839-dvgo-density-and-color-rendering.md)
Synthesizes novel views by combining voxel-based storage with neural networks. It differs from pure voxel methods by supporting continuous, view-dependent shading via an MLP, and from pure NeRF by avoiding $O(N)$ query costs through spatial locality. It is chosen for high-quality novel view synthesis with efficient memory usage.

### Surface and DRR Rendering Modes
Specialized rendering modes for medical or scientific visualization.
*   **Surface Rendering:** Computes color $C(s)$ using the Phong equation based on the volume gradient [S1, unofficial].
*   **DRR Mode:** Sets $C(s) = (0,0,0)$, attenuating only the backlight by accumulated transmittance $t$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **MIP Mode:** Outputs the opacity $\alpha$ of the maximum-opacity interval along the ray [S1, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** A uniform variable controls the active mode [S1, unofficial].

### Material Percentage Volume Rendering
A technique for rendering mixtures of materials (e.g., bone, muscle) by creating separate scalar fields for each material's percentage.
*   **Steps:** Assume scalar field is sampled above Nyquist or low-pass filtered. Create material percentage volumes. Associate color/opacity with each. Composite by linearly combining color and opacity. Use a matte volume for slicing. Transform volume so one axis is perpendicular to the image plane, then project plane-by-plane back-to-front [S2, unofficial].

### Volumetric Heat Haze
Produces the same result as Volumetric Geometry but adds refraction effects.
*   **Definition:** Uses the Surface channel gradient to change ray direction [S3].
*   **Parameter:** `hhfactor` multiplies refraction. 1 approximates normal air heat haze; 0 produces no effect. Larger values needed for smoke/other channels. Only available in Volumetric Heat Haze mode [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Only affects camera view, not shadows. May require increasing Max depth of VRayMtl if intersecting with refraction [S3].

### Volumetric Geometry Rendering
Produces the same visual result as Volumetric mode but uses procedural geometry made of multiple transparent layers.
*   **Definition:** Requires V-Ray. Used for exporting deep images and render elements (normals, velocity, multi matte) not available in standard Volumetric mode [S3].
*   **Pitfall:** Approximate and Approximate+Shadows options for Scattering are not supported. Cutter Geom does not work in this mode [S3].

## Background
The concept of rendering specific materials and geometries evolved from early volume visualization principles. Drebin, Carpenter, and Hanrahan developed techniques for rendering mixtures of materials using material percentage volumes, assuming scalar fields were sampled above the Nyquist frequency [S2, unofficial]. Modern implementations, such as those in GPU volume rendering courses, have expanded this to include surface rendering, DRRs, and MIPs, controlled by fragment shader uniforms [S1, unofficial]. In commercial software like 3ds Max with V-Ray, specific modes like Volumetric Geometry and Heat Haze were introduced to support advanced render elements and refraction effects, requiring specific procedural geometry generation [S3]. More recently, methods like DVGO have integrated neural networks with voxel grids to handle view-dependent material properties efficiently [S1, unofficial].
