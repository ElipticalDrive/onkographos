# Specific Material and Geometry Rendering


## Overview
Specific Material and Geometry Rendering is a family of techniques within direct volume ray casting that addresses the problem of mapping raw spatial data (voxels, grids, or procedural fields) to optical properties and handling specific geometric constraints or material compositions during ray traversal. It solves the disconnect between abstract volume data and physically or artistically plausible light interaction by defining how density, scattering, and emission are sampled and integrated. What sets this family apart from generic ray marching is its focus on the **material definition layer** (how data maps to physics) and **geometric boundary handling** (spherical shells, binary objects, or multi-material mixtures), rather than just the integration scheme itself. It builds on [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) by providing the specific sampling and shading logic required for complex media like clouds, fog, and mixed-density volumes.

## Shared principles
The methods in this family share the fundamental mechanism of evaluating optical properties at sample points along a ray and accumulating radiance. However, they diverge significantly in *how* those properties are derived. The common mathematical core is the volume rendering integral:

$$
C(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t) \sigma_t(t) c(t) dt
$$

where $T(t)$ is transmittance, $\sigma_t$ is extinction, and $c(t)$ is emitted/scattered color. The "Specific Material" aspect refers to the implementation of $\sigma_t(t)$ and $c(t)$:
1.  **Data-to-Property Mapping:** Raw scalar/vector data must be converted to extinction/scattering coefficients. This is done via transfer functions (SDK-based), procedural noise combinations (clouds), or neural network lookups (DVGO).
2.  **Geometric Boundary Definition:** The integration domain is not always a simple box. It may be a spherical shell (clouds), a binary mask (ray tracing binary objects), or a mixture of overlapping scalar fields (material percentages).
3.  **Sampling Strategy:** The choice of sample position (jittered, fixed, sphere-guided) and the method of querying the underlying data (trilinear interpolation, procedural evaluation, or pre-computed optical depth queries) are specific to the material type.

## Subtopics

### [Procedural Volumetric Cloud Rendering](3480-procedural-volumetric-cloud-rendering.md)
Procedural Volumetric Cloud Rendering is a real-time technique that renders planetary-scale cloud layers by ray-marching through a spherical shell volume defined by procedural noise fields. It solves the problem of generating physically plausible, dynamically moving cloudscapes with accurate atmospheric lighting and shadowing without relying on pre-baked textures or finite element meshes. What sets it apart from local volumetrics (like [Ray-marched Volumetric Fog](4248-ray-marched-volumetric-fog.md)) is its treatment of clouds as a global spherical shell, allowing the camera to be inside, below, or above the layer, and its integration of specific atmospheric scattering models (Hillaire 2016/2020) and noise-based shaping (Nubis) to achieve photorealistic scale.

### [Mr-Voxel Volume Rendering](3874-mr-voxel-volume-rendering.md)
Mr-Voxel Volume Rendering is a direct volume ray casting technique implemented within the Mr-Voxel library's `VolumeIntegrator` class. It solves the problem of computing the radiance contribution of a single volumetric region along a ray by marching through the volume, accumulating emitted light and single-scattering contributions from a single light source. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific implementation of Russian Roulette termination based on a fixed probability threshold and its reliance on a pre-computed optical depth (`tau`) query for transmittance calculation, rather than integrating extinction coefficients on the fly.

### [Ray-marched Volumetric Fog](4248-ray-marched-volumetric-fog.md)
Ray-marched volumetric fog simulates light absorption, scattering, and soft shadows by treating fog as a 3D volume of participating media traced via forward ray-marching from the camera to the scene. It solves the problem of rendering aerial perspective and dynamic light shafts (god rays) in real-time applications. Unlike backward ray-marching, this method marches from the camera outward, enabling early-out optimizations when transmittance drops below a threshold.

### [Material Parameter Setting](5335-material-parameter-setting.md)
Material Parameter Setting defines the binding between spatial data (grids, textures, or procedural functions) and optical properties (absorption, scattering, emission) in volume rendering. It solves the problem of how to map raw voxel data to physically or artistically plausible light interaction. It is distinguished by two primary paradigms: a **Structured Object-Based** approach where a material object holds typed fields and texture descriptors, switching between a fast path (grid names) and a programmable path (nodes), and a **Procedural API-Based** approach using context handles and integer constants to define material slots and surface side logic.

### [DVGO Density and Color Rendering](12839-dvgo-density-and-color-rendering.md)
DVGO (Density Volume Grid Optimization) is a method for synthesizing novel views of 3D scenes by combining a 3D voxel grid with a shallow neural network. It solves the problem of efficient volume rendering by storing density and color features in a regular grid, allowing for fast trilinear interpolation during ray marching. Unlike pure neural radiance fields (NeRFs) that query a dense MLP for every sample, DVGO uses the grid to store intermediate representations, significantly reducing memory and compute requirements while maintaining high visual fidelity. It is distinguished from sibling methods like [Mr-Voxel Volume Rendering](3874-mr-voxel-volume-rendering.md) by its specific use of a shifted softplus activation for density and a shallow MLP for view-dependent color, optimized for fast training and inference.

### Surface and DRR Rendering Modes
- [S1, unofficial] Surface rendering mode: $C(s)$ computed according to Phong equation using volume gradient.
- [S1, unofficial] Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs) mode: $C(s) = (0,0,0)$, only backlight is attenuated by accumulated $t$.
- [S1, unofficial] Maximum Intensity Projections (MIPs) mode: Output is the opacity $\alpha$ of the maximum-opacity interval along the ray.
- [S1, unofficial] Uniform variable telling fragment shader which rendering mode to use.

### Material Percentage Volume Rendering
- [S2, unofficial] A technique for rendering volumes with mixtures of materials (e.g., bone, muscle) by creating material percentage volumes and compositing them.
- [S2, unofficial] Assume scalar field was sampled above Nyquist frequency or low-pass filtered. Differentiate materials by scalar value or additional composition info.
- [S2, unofficial] Create material percentage volumes, where each is a scalar field representing one material.
- [S2, unofficial] Associate color and opacity with each material. Obtain composite color and opacity by linearly combining the color and opacity for each material percentage volume.
- [S2, unofficial] Use a matte volume (scalar field 0-1) to slice the volume or perform spatial set operations.
- [S2, unofficial] Transform the volume so one axis is perpendicular to the image plane. Project plane by plane in back-to-front manner and composite to form the final image.

### Volumetric Heat Haze
- [S3] Produces same result as Volumetric Geometry but adds heat haze refraction effect. Requires V-Ray. Uses Surface channel gradient to change ray direction.
- [S3] Heat Haze (hhfactor): Multiplier for refraction based on Surface channel gradient. 1 approximates normal air heat haze from temperature. 0 produces no effect. Larger values needed if using smoke/other channels as source. Only available in Volumetric Heat Haze mode.
- [S3] Heat Haze only affects camera view, not shadows cast through volume. If Heat Haze shader intersects with VRayMtl refraction, Max depth of VRayMtl may need increase.

### Volumetric Geometry Rendering
- [S3] Produces the same visual result as Volumetric mode but uses procedural geometry made of multiple transparent layers. Requires V-Ray. Used for exporting deep images and render elements (normals, velocity, multi matte) not available in standard Volumetric mode.
- [S3] Approximate and Approximate+Shadows options for Scattering in Smoke color window are not supported in this mode.
- [S3] Cutter Geom does not work when Mode is set to Volumetric Geometry.

## Background
The concept of specific material handling in volume rendering evolved from early image-order techniques that treated volume data as a single scalar field [S2, unofficial]. Drebin, Carpenter, and Hanrahan introduced the idea of material percentage volumes to handle mixtures like bone and muscle, allowing composite color and opacity calculation [S2, unofficial]. As rendering moved to GPUs, the need for flexible material parameter setting arose, leading to object-based structures (like the `Material` struct in [S1, unofficial]) and API-based states (like the VP API in [S2, unofficial]). The rise of real-time rendering necessitated procedural approaches for specific geometries like clouds, leading to techniques such as Procedural Volumetric Cloud Rendering [S3]. More recently, hybrid methods like DVGO have emerged, combining grid-based storage with neural networks to optimize the material-property mapping process [S1, unofficial].
