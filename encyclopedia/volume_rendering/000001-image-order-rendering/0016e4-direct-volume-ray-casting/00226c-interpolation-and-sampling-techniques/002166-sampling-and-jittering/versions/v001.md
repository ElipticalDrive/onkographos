# Sampling and Jittering

Also called: Jittered Ray Casting, Jittered Sampling Volume Rendering, RayCaster, Volume Ray Casting with Jittered Sampling and Front-to-Back Blending

## Overview
Sampling and Jittering is a direct volume rendering technique that casts rays from the camera through a 3D voxel grid, samples voxel data with jittered step offsets to reduce banding artifacts, and composites the result using front-to-back alpha blending [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from uniform ray sampling by perturbing sample positions to break up regular grid patterns, and from stochastic methods by using a deterministic, pre-computed jitter texture rather than runtime random number generation [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A 3D voxel texture (`VoxelSampler`), a 2D jitter texture (`JitterTexSampler`), camera parameters (`CameraPosition`, `rayDir`), volume bounds (`VolExtentMin`, `VolExtentMax`), and rendering parameters (`NumSamples`, `RayStepSize`, `JitterTexSize`, `ComputeLighting`, lighting coefficients).
*   **Outputs:** A final color value `FragColor` (vec4) for each fragment.

**Data Structures:**
*   `VoxelSampler`: A 3D texture containing scalar or RGBA voxel data.
*   `JitterTexSampler`: A 2D texture (typically size $N \times N$, e.g., $16 \times 16$ or $32 \times 32$) storing pre-computed jitter offsets. The red channel is used for the offset magnitude [S1, unofficial].
*   `dst`: Accumulator variable, initialized to `(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)`.

**Parameters:**
*   `NumSamples`: Maximum iterations per ray [S1, unofficial].
*   `RayStepSize`: Distance between consecutive sample points [S1, unofficial].
*   `JitterTexSize`: Width/Height of the jitter texture [S1, unofficial].
*   `ComputeLighting`: Boolean flag for shading [S1, unofficial].
*   `VolExtentMin`, `VolExtentMax`: Bounding box of the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   `k_Ambient`: `(0.05, 0.05, 0.05)` [S1, unofficial].
*   `k_Diffuse`: `(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)` [S1, unofficial].
*   `k_Specular`: `(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)` [S1, unofficial].
*   `k_Shininess`: `100.0` [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    Initialize the destination color accumulator:
    $$ \text{dst} = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0) $$
    Calculate the ray direction:
    $$ \text{rayDir} = \text{normalize}(\text{rayPosition} - \text{CameraPosition}) $$
    Calculate the base step vector:
    $$ \text{rayStep} = \text{rayDir} \times \text{RayStepSize} $$

2.  **Ray Marching Loop:**
    Iterate $i$ from $0$ to $\text{NumSamples} - 1$:

    a.  **Jitter Application:**
        Determine the 2D texture coordinate from fragment coordinates modulo `JitterTexSize`:
        $$ u = \text{fragmentCoord.x} \pmod{\text{JitterTexSize}} $$
        $$ v = \text{fragmentCoord.y} \pmod{\text{JitterTexSize}} $$
        Fetch the jitter value from the red channel of the jitter texture:
        $$ \text{jitter\_value} = \text{JitterTexSampler}.sample(u, v).r $$
        Update the ray position:
        $$ \text{rayPosition} \leftarrow \text{rayPosition} + \text{rayStep} \times \text{jitter\_value} $$
        [G] Note: The jitter value typically scales the step size. If the jitter texture stores values in $[0, 1]$, this effectively adds a fractional step offset.

    b.  **Boundary Check:**
        Check if the current `rayPosition` is inside the volume bounds. Using the bounding box defined by `VolExtentMin` and `VolExtentMax`, the point is inside if:
        $$ \text{dot}(\text{test1}, \text{test2}) \ge 3.0 $$
        [S1, unofficial] where `test1` and `test2` are vectors derived from the position relative to the min/max extents (typically checking if the point is within the min and max bounds along all axes). If the point is outside, skip to the next iteration or terminate.

    c.  **Voxel Sampling:**
        Sample the voxel data at `rayPosition`:
        $$ \text{src} = \text{VoxelSampler}.sample(\text{rayPosition}) $$
        Let $\text{src}.rgb$ be the color and $\text{src}.a$ be the opacity.

    d.  **Lighting (Optional):**
        If `ComputeLighting` is true:
        i.  **Gradient Estimation:** Estimate the normal using central differences on the alpha channel ($a$) of the voxel texture. Use offsets `ivec4(1, 0, 0, -1)` and permutations `(yxz, yzx, wyz, ywz, yzw)` to sample neighbors [S1, unofficial].
        ii. **Normal Calculation:**
        $$ \text{normal} = \text{normalize}(\text{sample2} - \text{sample1}) $$
        If `normal` is NaN (e.g., if `sample1` equals `sample2`), skip shading [S1, unofficial].
        iii. **Phong Shading:** Apply Phong shading to `src.rgb` using the shading function:
        $$ \text{halfway} = \text{normalize}(\text{toLight} + \text{toCamera}) $$
        $$ \text{diffuseLight} = \max(\text{dot}(\text{toLight}, \text{normal}), 0.0) $$
        $$ \text{diffuse} = \text{k\_Diffuse} \times \text{src}.rgb \times \text{diffuseLight} $$
        $$ \text{specularLight} = \text{pow}(\max(\text{dot}(\text{halfway}, \text{normal}), 0), \text{k\_Shininess}) $$
        $$ \text{specular} = \text{k\_Specular} \times \text{src}.rgb \times \text{specularLight} $$
        $$ \text{ambient} = \text{k\_Ambient} \times \text{src}.rgb $$
        $$ \text{src}.rgb = \text{ambient} + \text{diffuse} + \text{specular} $$

    e.  **Front-to-Back Blending:**
        Composite the sample into the destination:
        $$ \text{dst} = (1.0 - \text{dst}.a) \times \text{src} + \text{dst} $$

    f.  **Early Ray Termination:**
        If $\text{dst}.a > 0.95$, break the loop [S1, unofficial].

    g.  **Advance Ray:**
        Advance the ray position for the next iteration (if not terminated):
        $$ \text{rayPosition} \leftarrow \text{rayPosition} + \text{rayStep} $$
        Re-evaluate the inside condition for the loop.

3.  **Output:**
    Output `dst` as `FragColor`.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_fragment(fragment_coord, camera_position, ray_dir, ray_step_size, 
                    num_samples, jitter_tex_size, compute_lighting, 
                    vol_extent_min, vol_extent_max, light_position):
    
    # Initialize destination
    dst = vec4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    
    # Calculate ray parameters
    # Note: ray_dir is assumed to be normalized
    ray_step = ray_dir * ray_step_size
    
    # Current ray position starts at the near plane or camera position projected
    # For this implementation, we assume rayPosition is passed or calculated 
    # based on fragment coord and camera projection. 
    # Here we assume rayPosition is the starting point for the loop.
    ray_position = calculate_start_position(fragment_coord, camera_position, ray_dir) 
    
    # Pre-calculate jitter coordinate
    u = fragment_coord.x % jitter_tex_size
    v = fragment_coord.y % jitter_tex_size
    
    for i in range(num_samples):
        # 1. Apply Jitter Offset
        jitter_value = jitter_tex_sampler.sample(u, v).r
        ray_position = ray_position + ray_step * jitter_value
        
        # 2. Check Bounds
        if not is_inside_volume(ray_position, vol_extent_min, vol_extent_max):
            # Advance to next step without sampling
            ray_position = ray_position + ray_step
            continue
            
        # 3. Sample Voxel Data
        src = voxel_sampler.sample(ray_position)
        
        # 4. Lighting (Optional)
        if compute_lighting:
            # Estimate gradient normal
            # Using central differences on alpha channel
            # Offsets: ivec4(1, 0, 0, -1) and permutations (yxz, yzx, wyz, ywz, yzw)
            # This implies sampling neighbors in specific directions
            sample1 = voxel_sampler.sample(ray_position + offset1).a
            sample2 = voxel_sampler.sample(ray_position + offset2).a
            
            normal = normalize(sample2 - sample1)
            
            if isnan(normal):
                # Skip shading if normal is invalid
                pass
            else:
                # Phong Shading
                to_camera = normalize(camera_position - ray_position)
                to_light = normalize(light_position - ray_position)
                
                halfway = normalize(to_light + to_camera)
                diffuse_light = max(dot(to_light, normal), 0.0)
                
                diffuse = vec3(1.0, 1.0, 1.0) * src.rgb * diffuse_light
                specular_light = pow(max(dot(halfway, normal), 0.0), 100.0)
                specular = vec3(1.0, 1.0, 1.0) * src.rgb * specular_light
                ambient = vec3(0.05, 0.05, 0.05) * src.rgb
                
                src.rgb = ambient + diffuse + specular
        
        # 5. Front-to-Back Blending
        dst = (1.0 - dst.a) * src + dst
        
        # 6. Early Ray Termination
        if dst.a > 0.95:
            break
            
        # 7. Advance Ray
        ray_position = ray_position + ray_step
        
    return dst
```

## Subtopics

### [Stochastic Jittering](924-stochastic-jittering.md)
Reduces aliasing by perturbing sample positions with controlled random noise. Chosen when visual quality is prioritized over deterministic reproducibility and runtime RNG is acceptable.

### [Hierarchical Sampling (Inverse CDF Sampling)](973-hierarchical-sampling-inverse-cdf-sampling.md)
Generates fine-grained depth samples based on coarse rendering weights. Chosen for improving density estimation in high-density regions with fewer total samples.

### [Uniform Ray Sampling](1261-uniform-ray-sampling.md)
Generates fixed sample points with uniform distribution. Chosen as a baseline when no transmittance estimator is available or for simplicity.

### [Monte Carlo Volume Rendering](2060-monte-carlo-volume-rendering.md)
Uses random distance sampling and null-collision techniques. Chosen for accurate handling of anisotropic scattering and complex light transport.

### [Random Jitter with Reprojection](2782-random-jitter-with-reprojection.md)
Reduces banding by introducing stochastic jitter and reducing noise via temporal accumulation. Chosen for real-time rendering with temporal coherence.

### [Importance Sampling for SPH Rendering](3624-importance-sampling-for-sph-rendering.md)
Increases performance by sampling particles based on data importance. Chosen for large, unstructured SPH datasets.

### [Ray Casting with Jittered Sampling](4151-ray-casting-with-jittered-sampling.md)
A direct volume rendering technique using jittered sampling offsets to reduce aliasing. Chosen as a deterministic alternative to stochastic jittering.

### [Warp-based Sampling](4298-warp-based-sampling.md)
Optimizes GPU cache performance by reorganizing thread warps. Chosen for large dynamic volumes at low resolutions.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of fragments (pixels) and $S$ is the number of samples per ray (`NumSamples`). Early ray termination can reduce the effective $S$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$ for the voxel texture and $O(J^2)$ for the jitter texture, where $V$ is the volume size and $J$ is the jitter texture size.
*   **Measured Performance:** Performance depends heavily on `NumSamples` and early ray termination efficiency. The use of a pre-computed jitter texture avoids runtime random number generation overhead [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; reduces banding artifacts compared to uniform sampling; deterministic if using a fixed jitter texture; supports shading.
*   **Limitations:** Early ray termination may miss faint structures behind dense ones [S1, unofficial]; gradient calculation may result in NaN if sample values are identical [S1, unofficial]; jitter texture size limits the variety of jitter patterns.
*   **When to Use:** Real-time volume rendering where banding artifacts are visible with uniform sampling and deterministic results are preferred.
*   **When Not to Use:** When accurate light transport simulation is required (use Monte Carlo); when adaptive sampling based on density is needed (use Hierarchical Sampling).

## Background
Sampling and Jittering is a direct volume rendering technique described in the context of the VoxViz project [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of ray casting and front-to-back blending, incorporating jittering to mitigate aliasing. The method is part of the broader family of interpolation and sampling techniques in volume rendering.
