# Stratified Ray Sampling

Also called: Jittered Uniform Sampling, Ray Sampling for Acoustic Volume Rendering, Stratified Sample

## Overview
Stratified Ray Sampling is a technique for distributing sample points along a camera ray in volume rendering by partitioning the ray's depth interval into equal-sized bins and drawing one sample uniformly at random from each bin. It solves the problem of aliasing and clustering inherent in pure uniform random sampling by guaranteeing one sample per spatial segment of the ray. It differs from [Stratified Monte Carlo Radiance Estimate](1373-stratified-monte-carlo-radiance-estimate.md) by focusing specifically on the geometric placement of samples along the ray depth rather than the statistical estimation of radiance integrals, and from [Batch Stratified Sampling](2730-batch-stratified-sampling.md) by operating on individual rays rather than optimizing batch-level variance reduction.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ (3D vector).
*   Ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ (normalized 3D vector).
*   Near depth $t_n$ (scalar).
*   Far depth $t_f$ (scalar).
*   Number of samples $k$ (integer).
*   Perturbation flag `perturb` (boolean, default True).
*   Linear disparity flag `lindisp` (boolean, default False).

**Outputs:**
*   Array of sample depths $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ of size $k$.
*   Array of 3D sample positions $\mathbf{P}$ of size $k \times 3$.

**Data Structures:**
*   `z_vals`: Tensor of shape $(k)$ storing the depth values.
*   `t_rand`: Tensor of shape $(k)$ storing uniform random numbers $u_i \sim U[0, 1]$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Bin Boundary Calculation**:
    Compute the boundaries of the $k$ bins. If `lindisp` is False, the bins are uniform in depth. If `lindisp` is True, the bins are uniform in disparity ($1/t$).
    
    If `lindisp` is False:
    $$ t_i = t_n + i \cdot \frac{t_f - t_n}{k}, \quad \text{for } i = 0, \dots, k $$
    
    If `lindisp` is True:
    $$ \frac{1}{t_i} = \frac{1}{t_n} + i \cdot \left( \frac{1}{t_f} - \frac{1}{t_n} \right) \cdot \frac{1}{k} \implies t_i = \left( \frac{1}{t_n} + i \cdot \frac{1}{k} \left( \frac{1}{t_f} - \frac{1}{t_n} \right) \right)^{-1} $$
    
    *Note: The source [S4, unofficial] defines $t\_vals$ linearly in $[0, 1]$ and maps them to disparity or depth.*

2.  **Random Number Generation**:
    Generate $k$ independent random numbers $u_i \sim U[0, 1]$ for $i = 1, \dots, k$.

3.  **Sample Placement (Stratification)**:
    For each bin $i$ (where $i$ ranges from $1$ to $k$), calculate the sample depth $\tau_i$.
    
    If `perturb` is True:
    The sample is drawn uniformly from within the bin boundaries $[t_{i-1}, t_i]$.
    $$ \tau_i = t_{i-1} + u_i \cdot (t_i - t_{i-1}) $$
    
    If `perturb` is False (Deterministic/Midpoint):
    The sample is placed at the center of the bin.
    $$ \tau_i = \frac{t_{i-1} + t_i}{2} $$
    
    *Note: Source [S5] describes the bin boundaries for index $i$ as $t_n + \frac{i-1}{N}(t_f - t_n)$ and $t_n + \frac{i}{N}(t_f - t_n)$.*

4.  **Position Calculation**:
    Convert the depth samples $\tau_i$ to 3D positions $\mathbf{p}_i$.
    $$ \mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{o} + \tau_i \mathbf{d} $$

**Parameters:**
*   $k$ (or $N$): Number of samples per ray. Typical values: 64 for coarse networks, 128 for fine networks [S4, unofficial][S5].
*   $t_n, t_f$: Near and far clipping planes. Typical values depend on scene scale (e.g., 2.0 to 6.0 for normalized scenes) [S4, unofficial][S5].
*   `lindisp`: Boolean. If True, samples are distributed linearly in disparity space ($1/t$) rather than depth space. This is useful for scenes with large depth variations [S4, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: Boolean. If True, adds uniform noise to break symmetry and reduce aliasing. If False, uses midpoint sampling [S4, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Division by Zero**: When `lindisp` is True, if $t_n$ or $t_f$ is 0, the disparity calculation involves division by zero. Ensure $t_n > 0$.
*   **Precision**: When $t_f - t_n$ is very small, floating-point precision may cause $t_i - t_{i-1}$ to be zero or negative.
*   **Degenerate Input**: If $k=0$, no samples are generated. If $k=1$, the bin is $[t_n, t_f]$ and the sample is either uniform in $[t_n, t_f]$ or at the midpoint.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import numpy as np

def stratified_ray_sampling(o, d, t_n, t_f, k, perturb=True, lindisp=False):
    """
    Generates stratified samples along a ray.
    
    Args:
        o: Ray origin (3,) array.
        d: Ray direction (3,) array.
        t_n: Near depth (scalar).
        t_f: Far depth (scalar).
        k: Number of samples (int).
        perturb: Whether to add uniform noise within bins (bool).
        lindisp: Whether to sample in disparity space (bool).
        
    Returns:
        positions: (k, 3) array of 3D sample positions.
        depths: (k,) array of sample depths.
    """
    if k <= 0:
        return np.empty((0, 3)), np.empty((0,))
    
    # Step 1: Calculate bin boundaries
    # t_vals will store the k+1 boundaries: t_0, t_1, ..., t_k
    if lindisp:
        # Sample linearly in disparity (1/t)
        # 1/t_i = 1/t_n + i/k * (1/t_f - 1/t_n)
        inv_t_n = 1.0 / t_n
        inv_t_f = 1.0 / t_f
        inv_t_vals = inv_t_n + np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, k + 1) * (inv_t_f - inv_t_n)
        t_vals = 1.0 / inv_t_vals
    else:
        # Sample linearly in depth
        t_vals = np.linspace(t_n, t_f, k + 1)
        
    # Step 2: Generate random numbers
    # u_i ~ U[0, 1] for i = 1 to k
    u = np.random.uniform(0.0, 1.0, k)
    
    # Step 3: Calculate sample depths
    if perturb:
        # Draw uniformly from each bin [t_{i-1}, t_i]
        # Bin i corresponds to index i-1 in 0-indexed arrays for boundaries
        # t_vals has length k+1: indices 0 to k
        # Bin i (1-based) is between t_vals[i-1] and t_vals[i]
        lower = t_vals[:-1] # t_0 to t_{k-1}
        upper = t_vals[1:]  # t_1 to t_k
        depths = lower + u * (upper - lower)
    else:
        # Midpoint sampling
        # Center of bin i is (t_{i-1} + t_i) / 2
        mids = 0.5 * (t_vals[:-1] + t_vals[1:])
        depths = mids
        
    # Step 4: Calculate 3D positions
    # p_i = o + depth_i * d
    positions = o + depths[:, np.newaxis] * d
    
    return positions, depths
```

## Subtopics

### [Hierarchical Stratified Sampling](10554-hierarchical-stratified-sampling.md)
Uses stratified sampling for a coarse pass to estimate density, then adaptively refines sampling in high-density regions for a fine pass. Choose when scene complexity varies significantly along rays.

### [Stratified Monte Carlo Radiance Estimate](1373-stratified-monte-carlo-radiance-estimate.md)
Focuses on the statistical estimation of the integral using stratified samples, often combined with importance sampling. Choose when variance reduction in the final pixel color is the primary goal.

### [Batch Stratified Sampling](2730-batch-stratified-sampling.md)
Extends stratification to multiple rays simultaneously to optimize memory access and variance across a batch. Choose when rendering many rays in parallel on a GPU.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity**: $O(k)$ per ray, where $k$ is the number of samples. Generating random numbers and arithmetic operations are linear in $k$.
*   **Memory Complexity**: $O(k)$ per ray to store sample depths and positions.
*   **Performance**: Significantly reduces aliasing artifacts compared to pure uniform sampling [S4, unofficial]. It is computationally cheap and easy to implement.
*   **Strengths**: Simple to implement, unbiased (if perturb=True), effective at reducing high-frequency aliasing.
*   **Limitations**: Does not adapt to scene content; samples are uniformly distributed regardless of where detail or density exists. Can be inefficient in sparse regions [S1].
*   **When to use**: As a baseline sampling strategy, particularly in the coarse stage of hierarchical rendering [S4, unofficial][S5].
*   **When not to use**: In scenes with highly non-uniform density where importance sampling would be more efficient.

## Background
Stratified sampling is a fundamental variance reduction technique in Monte Carlo integration. In the context of volume rendering and neural radiance fields (NeRFs), it was popularized by the original NeRF paper [S4, unofficial] as a standard method for generating sample points along rays. It is used in various forms in acoustic volume rendering [S1], neural surface reconstruction [S3, unofficial], and collaborative NeRF training [S5]. The method ensures that the ray is sampled evenly, preventing the gaps and clusters that can occur with purely random sampling.
