# Stratified Sampling
Also called: Stratified Ray Sampling, Stratified Monte Carlo, Batch Stratified Sampling

## Overview
Stratified sampling is a family of techniques that reduce variance and aliasing in volume rendering by dividing the ray integration interval into discrete bins and sampling one point per bin, ensuring better coverage than pure uniform random sampling . It sets itself apart from [Uniform Ray Sampling](1261-uniform-ray-sampling.md) by replacing fixed intervals or pure randomness with controlled jitter within defined strata, breaking up regular patterns while guaranteeing no gaps in the depth range .

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the core mechanism of partitioning the ray depth interval $[t_n, t_f]$ into $k$ equal-sized bins and generating samples within these bins.

1.  **Bin Definition**: The interval $[t_n, t_f]$ is divided into $k$ bins. The boundaries are defined as:
    $$ t_i = t_n + i \cdot \frac{t_f - t_n}{k}, \quad \text{for } i = 0, \dots, k $$
    

2.  **Sampling**: For each bin $i$ (where $i=1, \dots, k$), a sample $\tau_i$ is drawn.
    *   **Randomized (Unbiased)**: A uniform random number $u_i \sim U[0, 1]$ is generated, and the sample is computed as:
        $$ \tau_i = t_{i-1} + u_i \cdot (t_i - t_{i-1}) $$
        
    *   **Deterministic (Midpoint)**: The center of the bin is used:
        $$ \tau_i = 0.5 \cdot (t_{i-1} + t_i) $$
        

3.  **Position Calculation**: The 3D position $\mathbf{p}_i$ is calculated from the ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{o} + \tau_i \mathbf{d} $$
    

4.  **Variance Reduction**: By ensuring one sample per bin, the method avoids the clustering and gaps inherent in pure uniform sampling, reducing aliasing artifacts .

## Subtopics

### [Stratified Monte Carlo Radiance Estimate](1373-stratified-monte-carlo-radiance-estimate.md)
An unbiased radiance estimation method that reduces variance for degenerate distributions by summing bin contributions weighted by transmittance derivatives. It differs by explicitly computing a radiance estimate $\hat{C}_{\text{IW}}(\mathbf{r})$ rather than just generating points.

### [Batch Stratified Sampling](2730-batch-stratified-sampling.md)
Generates $N_c$ evenly-spaced depth bins and perturbs sample positions within each bin to reduce aliasing in volume rendering. It extends the family by supporting batched ray inputs and normalized coordinate interpolation for efficient GPU processing.

### [Stratified Ray Sampling](3014-stratified-ray-sampling.md)
Generates sample points by dividing the ray interval into equal bins and drawing one random sample per bin. It distinguishes itself by supporting linear disparity sampling (`lindisp`) and is widely used in NeRF-like architectures for coarse network training.

## Background
Stratified sampling is a fundamental technique in Monte Carlo integration, originally developed to improve the convergence rate of numerical integration by reducing variance compared to pure random sampling . In computer graphics, it was adopted to mitigate aliasing in ray tracing and volume rendering, where uniform sampling often produces noisy or structured artifacts . The specific application to neural volume rendering (e.g., NeRF) popularized the stratified ray sampling variant with disparity mapping .
