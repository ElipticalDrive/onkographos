# Stratified Ray Sampling

Also called: Jittered Uniform Sampling, Ray Sampling for Acoustic Volume Rendering, Stratified Sample

## Overview
Stratified Ray Sampling discretizes the integration interval along a camera ray by dividing it into equal-sized bins and drawing one sample uniformly at random from each bin [S1][S4, unofficial][S5]. It is used to approximate acoustic integrals [S1] and to capture scene structure during the coarse training stage of neural radiance fields [S5].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ (3D vector) [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   Ray direction $\mathbf{d}$ (normalized 3D vector) [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   Near plane distance $t_n$ (scalar) [S3, unofficial].
*   Far plane distance $t_f$ (scalar) [S3, unofficial].
*   Number of samples per ray $k$ (integer) [S3, unofficial].
*   Optional: `lindisp` (boolean, whether to sample in disparity space) [S4, unofficial].
*   Optional: `perturb` (boolean, whether to add random jitter) [S4, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Array of sample depths $\tau_i$ for $i=1, \dots, k$ [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   Array of 3D sample positions $\mathbf{p}_i$ for $i=1, \dots, k$ [S1][S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   `z_vals`: A 1D array of size $k$ storing the depth values along the ray [S3, unofficial].
*   `sample_points`: A 2D array of shape $(k, 3)$ storing the Cartesian coordinates of the samples [S3, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Define Interval and Bins:**
    Determine the total length of the integration interval:
    $$ L = t_f - t_n $$
    The boundaries of the $k$ equal-sized bins are defined as:
    $$ t_i = t_n + i \cdot \frac{L}{k}, \quad \text{for } i = 0, \dots, k $$
    [S1][S5].

2.  **Compute Base Depths (Linear or Disparity):**
    If `lindisp` is False (standard depth sampling), compute the midpoints or boundaries linearly [S4, unofficial]. If `lindisp` is True, compute in disparity space ($1/t$) [S4, unofficial].
    
    *Linear Depth:*
    Generate $k$ evenly spaced values between $t_n$ and $t_f$ [S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial]. Let $t_{vals}$ be the boundaries:
    $$ t_{vals}[i] = t_n + i \cdot \frac{t_f - t_n}{k}, \quad i=0,\dots,k $$
    [S4, unofficial].
    
    *Disparity Depth:*
    Compute boundaries in disparity space $d = 1/t$:
    $$ d_{vals}[i] = \frac{1}{t_n} + i \cdot \frac{\frac{1}{t_f} - \frac{1}{t_n}}{k}, \quad i=0,\dots,k $$
    Convert back to depth:
    $$ t_{vals}[i] = \frac{1}{d_{vals}[i]} $$
    [S4, unofficial].

3.  **Generate Stratified Samples:**
    For each bin $i$ from $1$ to $k$:
    *   Identify the bin boundaries: $[t_{i-1}, t_i]$ [S1][S5].
    *   Draw a uniform random number $u_i \sim U[0, 1]$ [S1][S5].
    *   Compute the sample depth $\tau_i$:
        $$ \tau_i = t_{i-1} + u_i \cdot (t_i - t_{i-1}) $$
    
    *Note on Perturbation:* In some implementations (e.g., NeRF), jitter is applied to the midpoints of the bins rather than the boundaries to avoid sampling exactly at the edges, which can cause artifacts if the density function is discontinuous [S4, unofficial]. If `perturb` is True:
    1.  Compute midpoints $m_i = 0.5 \cdot (t_{i-1} + t_i)$ for $i=1, \dots, k-1$ [S4, unofficial].
    2.  Define upper and lower bounds for jitter:
        $$ \text{lower} = [t_0, m_1, \dots, m_{k-1}] $$
        $$ \text{upper} = [m_1, \dots, m_{k-1}, t_k] $$
    3.  Draw random $t_{rand} \sim U[0, 1]$ of size $k$ [S4, unofficial].
    4.  Compute $\tau_i = \text{lower}[i] + t_{rand}[i] \cdot (\text{upper}[i] - \text{lower}[i])$ [S4, unofficial].

4.  **Compute 3D Positions:**
    For each sample depth $\tau_i$, compute the 3D position $\mathbf{p}_i$:
    $$ \mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{o} + \tau_i \mathbf{d} $$
    [S1][S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].

5.  **Render (Integration):**
    The color $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for the ray is approximated by volume rendering integration using the samples [S1]:
    $$ \hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} T_i \alpha_i c_i $$
    where:
    *   $c_i$ is the color contribution at $\mathbf{p}_i$ [S1].
    *   $\sigma_i$ is the density at $\mathbf{p}_i$ [S1].
    *   $\delta_i = t_i - t_{i-1}$ is the distance between bin boundaries [S1]. *Note: In standard NeRF implementations, $\delta_i$ is often approximated as the distance between consecutive samples $\tau_i - \tau_{i-1}$, but the theoretical definition relies on the bin width $L/k$ for unbiased estimation of the integral over the bin* [S1][S4, unofficial].
    *   Opacity: $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S1].
    *   Transmittance: $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math
import random

def stratified_ray_sampling(origin, direction, near, far, num_samples, lindisp=False, perturb=True):
    """
    Generates stratified samples along a single ray.
    
    Args:
        origin: Tuple (x, y, z)
        direction: Tuple (dx, dy, dz)
        near: Float, near plane distance
        far: Float, far plane distance
        num_samples: Int, number of samples per ray (k)
        lindisp: Bool, if True sample in disparity space
        perturb: Bool, if True add uniform jitter within bins
        
    Returns:
        depths: List of floats
        points: List of tuples (x, y, z)
    """
    # Step 1 & 2: Compute bin boundaries
    boundaries = []
    for i in range(num_samples + 1):
        t = i / num_samples
        if lindisp:
            d_near = 1.0 / near
            d_far = 1.0 / far
            d_val = d_near * (1.0 - t) + d_far * t
            boundaries.append(1.0 / d_val)
        else:
            boundaries.append(near * (1.0 - t) + far * t)

    # Step 3: Generate stratified samples
    depths = []
    if perturb:
        # NeRF-style perturbation using midpoints [S4, unofficial]
        mids = [0.5 * (boundaries[i] + boundaries[i+1]) for i in range(num_samples - 1)]
        lower = [boundaries[0]] + mids
        upper = mids + [boundaries[-1]]
        for i in range(num_samples):
            u = random.random()
            depths.append(lower[i] + u * (upper[i] - lower[i]))
    else:
        # Standard stratified sampling without perturbation
        for i in range(num_samples):
            u = random.random()
            depths.append(boundaries[i] + u * (boundaries[i+1] - boundaries[i]))

    # Step 4: Compute 3D positions
    points = []
    for t in depths:
        points.append((origin[0] + t * direction[0],
                       origin[1] + t * direction[1],
                       origin[2] + t * direction[2]))

    return depths, points
```

**Parameters:**
*   `num_samples` ($k$): Typically 64 for coarse networks and 128 for fine networks in hierarchical schemes [S4, unofficial][S5].
*   `near` ($t_n$): Minimum depth. Defined in `cfg.min_depth` [S3, unofficial].
*   `far` ($t_f$): Maximum depth. Defined in `cfg.max_depth` [S3, unofficial].
*   `lindisp`: Boolean. False by default [S4, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: Boolean. True by default in many implementations [S4, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Near Zero Depth:** If $t_n = 0$, disparity sampling ($1/t$) results in division by zero. Ensure $t_n > 0$ for disparity sampling [S4, unofficial].
*   **Bin Width vs Sample Distance:** The opacity calculation $\alpha_i$ uses $\delta_i$. If using stratified sampling, $\delta_i$ should ideally be the bin width $L/k$ to maintain the unbiased nature of the integral over the bin, rather than the distance between random samples $\tau_i - \tau_{i-1}$, which varies [S1]. However, many implementations use sample-to-sample distances for simplicity [S4, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Sampling:** Without perturbation, samples might fall exactly on bin boundaries, which can cause artifacts if the density function is discontinuous at those points [S4, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Hierarchical Stratified Sampling](10554-hierarchical-stratified-sampling.md)
Hierarchical Stratified Sampling divides a camera ray into equally spaced bins, draws uniform samples from each, and adaptively refines regions that contributed to the final color using a probabilistic function. It builds on [Stratified Ray Sampling](3014-stratified-ray-sampling.md) by adding an importance-driven refinement layer to improve reconstruction quality and prevent overfitting.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(k)$ per ray, where $k$ is the number of samples. Generating random numbers and computing positions is linear in the number of samples [S1][S4, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(k)$ per ray to store sample depths and positions [S3, unofficial][S4, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement, unbiased (if random), ensures full coverage of the ray interval [S1][S5].
*   **Limitations:** Does not adapt to scene content; may waste samples in empty space or under-sample in high-frequency regions [S5].
*   **When to Use:** As a baseline sampling strategy, particularly in the coarse stage of hierarchical rendering [S5][S4, unofficial].

## Background
In the context of volume rendering and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), it was popularized by the original NeRF paper and subsequent implementations [S4, unofficial]. It is used in various domains including acoustic volume rendering [S1] and neural surface reconstruction [S3, unofficial]. The method is often employed in the coarse training stage to capture general scene structure before refining with importance sampling [S5].
