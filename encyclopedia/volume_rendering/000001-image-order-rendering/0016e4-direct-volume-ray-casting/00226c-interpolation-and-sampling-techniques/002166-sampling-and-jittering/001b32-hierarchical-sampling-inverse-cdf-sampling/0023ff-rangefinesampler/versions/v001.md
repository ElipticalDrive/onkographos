# RangeFineSampler

Also called: Range-based Fine Sampling

## Overview
RangeFineSampler is a hierarchical sampling technique for direct volume ray casting that refines coarse samples by generating a dense uniform grid around the coarse sample with the highest weight, supplemented by random samples across the full ray extent [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the problem of missing high-frequency details between coarse samples by concentrating computational effort near the most significant signal while maintaining global coverage through random jittering. Unlike [Inverse Opacity Calculation](1099-inverse-opacity-calculation.md) which derives samples from opacity gradients, or [Proposal Network Sampling](1589-proposal-network-sampling.md) which uses learned neural networks to predict sample locations, RangeFineSampler relies on a deterministic geometric expansion around the peak weight index combined with stochastic uniform sampling [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
The method takes coarse sample positions and weights as input and outputs a refined set of sample positions along the ray. It operates on batches of rays, where each ray has a set of coarse z-values (depths) and corresponding weights.

**Inputs:**
- `coarse_z_vals`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, num_coarse_samples)` containing the depth positions of coarse samples.
- `coarse_weights`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, num_coarse_samples)` containing the importance weights (e.g., density or opacity contribution) for each coarse sample.
- `near`: Float, the near plane distance of the ray.
- `far`: Float, the far plane distance of the ray.
- `max_samples`: Integer, the maximum total number of samples allowed per ray.
- `N_samples`: Integer, the number of fine samples to generate around the peak.
- `range`: Float, the total width of the interval around the max weight z-value where uniform samples are placed. Default is 0.5 [S1, unofficial].
- `deterministic`: Boolean, whether to use stratified sampling or pure random jittering.

**Outputs:**
- `z_vals_ret`: Tensor of shape `(num_rays, num_total_samples)` containing the sorted, refined sample positions.

**Data Structures:**
The implementation assumes PyTorch tensors for batched operations. The core logic involves tensor indexing and sorting.

**Steps:**

1.  **Determine Effective Sample Count:**
    Calculate the number of fine samples to generate around the peak. This is bounded by `max_samples` and `N_samples`.
    $$ N_{fine} = \min(\text{max\_samples}, N_{samples}) $$

2.  **Find Peak Weight Index:**
    Identify the index of the coarse sample with the maximum weight for each ray.
    $$ \text{max\_indices} = \text{argmax}_{dim=-1}(\text{coarse\_weights}) $$

3.  **Retrieve Peak Z-Value:**
    Get the depth value corresponding to the peak weight for each ray.
    $$ \text{max\_z\_vals} = \text{coarse\_z\_vals}[\text{arange}(\text{num\_rays}), \text{max\_indices}] $$

4.  **Generate Uniform Samples Around Peak:**
    Create a uniform grid of $N_{fine}$ samples centered around `max_z_vals`. The grid spans an interval of width `range`. The formula distributes points evenly across this interval.
    $$ z_{fine} = \text{max\_z\_vals}[:, \text{None}] - \text{range} + \frac{2 \cdot \text{range}}{N_{fine} - 1} \cdot \text{arange}(N_{fine}) $$
    *Note: If $N_{fine} = 1$, this formula divides by zero. In practice, if $N_{fine}=1$, $z_{fine}$ is simply `max_z_vals`.*

5.  **Apply Stratified Sampling (Optional):**
    If `deterministic` is False, apply stratified jittering to the uniform samples to reduce aliasing.
    - Compute midpoints between adjacent uniform samples:
      $$ \text{mids} = 0.5 \cdot (z_{fine}[:, 1:] + z_{fine}[:, :-1]) $$
    - Define intervals:
      $$ \text{lower} = \text{concat}([z_{fine}[:, :1], \text{mids}], \text{dim}=-1) $$
      $$ \text{upper} = \text{concat}([\text{mids}, z_{fine}[:, -1:]], \text{dim}=-1) $$
    - Generate random jitter:
      $$ t_{rand} = \text{rand}(z_{fine}.shape) $$
    - Jitter the samples:
      $$ z_{fine} = \text{lower} + (\text{upper} - \text{lower}) \cdot t_{rand} $$
    If `deterministic` is True, $z_{fine}$ remains the uniform grid from Step 4.

6.  **Generate Random Samples Across Full Range:**
    Generate $N_{fine}$ random samples uniformly distributed between `near` and `far` for each ray.
    $$ z_{add} = \text{rand}((\text{num\_rays}, N_{fine})) \cdot (\text{far} - \text{near}) + \text{near} $$

7.  **Combine and Sort (Global):**
    Concatenate the original coarse samples with the random samples $z_{add}$ and sort them. This forms the base set of samples for all rays.
    $$ z_{base} = \text{sort}(\text{concat}([\text{coarse\_z\_vals}, z_{add}], \text{dim}=-1), \text{dim}=-1) $$

8.  **Inject Fine Samples for Non-Edge Rays:**
    For rays where the peak index is not at the very beginning (index > 0), concatenate the structured fine samples $z_{fine}$ with the coarse samples and sort. This ensures the high-density region around the peak is preserved and sorted correctly relative to coarse samples.
    $$ \text{mask} = \text{max\_indices} > 0 $$
    $$ z_{ret}[\text{mask}] = \text{sort}(\text{concat}([\text{coarse\_z\_vals}[\text{mask}], z_{fine}[\text{mask}]], \text{dim}=-1), \text{dim}=-1) $$
    For rays where `max_indices == 0`, the sorted combination of coarse and random samples ($z_{base}$) is used, as the peak is at the start and the structured fine samples might not be necessary or might overlap significantly with the coarse start. *Note: The source logic implies that for `max_indices == 0`, the fine samples are not injected in the same way, or the mask logic handles the exclusion. The source explicitly states: "For rays where max_indices > 0...". It does not specify the behavior for `max_indices == 0` other than implying the previous step's result ($z_{base}$) is used or that the concatenation is skipped. Given the sorting step 7 creates $z_{base}$, and step 8 overwrites for `mask`, the default for `~mask` is $z_{base}$.*

9.  **Return Result:**
    The final tensor $z_{ret}$ contains the sorted sample positions.

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch

def range_fine_sampler(coarse_z_vals, coarse_weights, near, far, max_samples, N_samples, range_val, deterministic=True):
    """
    RangeFineSampler implementation.
    
    Args:
        coarse_z_vals: (num_rays, num_coarse) Tensor of coarse sample depths.
        coarse_weights: (num_rays, num_coarse) Tensor of coarse sample weights.
        near: Float, near plane.
        far: Float, far plane.
        max_samples: Int, max total samples.
        N_samples: Int, number of fine samples to generate around peak.
        range_val: Float, width of interval around peak.
        deterministic: Bool, use stratified sampling.
        
    Returns:
        z_vals_ret: (num_rays, num_total) Tensor of sorted sample depths.
    """
    num_rays, num_coarse = coarse_z_vals.shape
    
    # Step 1: Determine effective sample count
    N_fine = min(max_samples, N_samples)
    
    # Step 2: Find peak weight index
    max_indices = torch.argmax(coarse_weights, dim=-1)
    
    # Step 3: Retrieve peak z-values
    # max_indices is (num_rays,), need to index correctly
    arange_rays = torch.arange(num_rays).to(coarse_z_vals.device)
    max_z_vals = coarse_z_vals[arange_rays, max_indices]
    
    # Step 4: Generate uniform samples around peak
    # Handle N_fine = 1 case to avoid division by zero
    if N_fine == 1:
        z_fine = max_z_vals.unsqueeze(1)
    else:
        arange_fine = torch.arange(N_fine).to(coarse_z_vals.device)
        z_fine = max_z_vals.unsqueeze(1) - range_val + (2 * range_val / (N_fine - 1)) * arange_fine.unsqueeze(0)
        
    # Step 5: Stratified sampling
    if not deterministic:
        mids = 0.5 * (z_fine[:, 1:] + z_fine[:, :-1])
        lower = torch.cat([z_fine[:, :1], mids], dim=-1)
        upper = torch.cat([mids, z_fine[:, -1:]], dim=-1)
        t_rand = torch.rand(z_fine.shape, device=coarse_z_vals.device)
        z_fine = lower + (upper - lower) * t_rand
        
    # Step 6: Generate random samples across full range
    z_add = torch.rand((num_rays, N_fine), device=coarse_z_vals.device) * (far - near) + near
    
    # Step 7: Combine coarse and random, sort
    z_base = torch.sort(torch.cat([coarse_z_vals, z_add], dim=-1), dim=-1)[0]
    
    # Step 8: Inject fine samples for rays where max_indices > 0
    mask = max_indices > 0
    z_vals_ret = z_base.clone()
    
    if mask.any():
        # Concatenate coarse and fine for these rays
        z_combined = torch.cat([coarse_z_vals[mask], z_fine[mask]], dim=-1)
        z_vals_ret[mask] = torch.sort(z_combined, dim=-1)[0]
        
    return z_vals_ret
```

**Parameters:**
- `range`: Float, default 0.5. The total width of the interval around the max weight z-value. [S1, unofficial]
- `N_samples`: Integer. Number of fine samples to generate.
- `max_samples`: Integer. Global cap on total samples.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Division by Zero:** If `N_fine` is 1, the uniform spacing formula divides by zero. The implementation must handle this by returning the single peak value.
- **Boundary Conditions:** The random samples `z_add` are strictly within `[near, far]`. The uniform samples `z_fine` are centered on `max_z_vals` with width `range`. If `max_z_vals` is close to `near` or `far`, `z_fine` may extend outside the valid ray bounds. The source does not explicitly clamp `z_fine`, which is a potential pitfall [S1, unofficial].
- **Index 0 Peak:** When `max_indices == 0`, the fine samples are not injected. This may lead to lower resolution near the start of the ray if the peak is at the beginning.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N \log N)$ per ray due to sorting, where $N$ is the total number of samples (coarse + fine + random). The dominant cost is the sort operation.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray to store the sample positions.
- **Performance:** The method is computationally efficient as it avoids neural network inference (unlike Proposal Network Sampling) and complex opacity calculations (unlike Inverse Opacity Calculation). It relies on simple tensor operations.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; concentrates samples where they are most needed (high weight); reduces aliasing via stratified jittering.
- **Limitations:** The fixed `range` parameter may not adapt well to varying scene densities; the lack of clamping for `z_fine` can lead to out-of-bounds samples; the logic for `max_indices == 0` may result in suboptimal sampling at the ray start.

## Background
RangeFineSampler is an implementation detail found in the `vf-nerf` repository, an unofficial project by albertgassol1 [S1, unofficial]. It is not described in a peer-reviewed paper but is part of a codebase that implements vector field neural radiance fields. The method is a heuristic refinement strategy designed to improve visual quality in volume rendering by focusing samples around high-density regions identified by coarse sampling.
