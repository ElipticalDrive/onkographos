# Inverse Opacity Calculation

Also called: inverse opacity sampling, reparameterized volume sampling (RVS) inverse step

## Overview
Inverse Opacity Calculation is a differentiable method for generating importance samples along a ray in volume rendering by inverting the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the rendering contribution [S1, S2]. It solves the problem of finding the ray parameter $t$ corresponding to a target cumulative density value $y$, allowing samples to be drawn directly from transmittance estimates rather than computing expensive integrals [S2]. Unlike standard NeRF hierarchical sampling, which interpolates exponentiated densities (probabilities) and requires auxiliary losses, this method interpolates the density field directly (piecewise linear or constant), offering better numerical stability for end-to-end optimization [S1]. It enables Monte Carlo estimates with fewer ray points and allows gradients to back-propagate through the sampling process to the density field parameters [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
- Near and far clipping planes $t_n, t_f$.
- A set of coarse sample positions $t_1, \dots, t_m$ (where $t_1=t_n, t_m=t_f$) and their corresponding density values $\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(t_j)$ predicted by a neural network.
- A target cumulative opacity value $y \in [0, y_f]$, where $y_f = 1 - \exp(-\int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(s) ds)$ is the total pixel opacity [S1].

**Outputs:**
- A sample position $t$ along the ray such that the cumulative opacity up to $t$ equals $y$.

**Data Structures:**
- **Grid Points:** Array $t$ of size $m+1$, sorted $t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_{m+1}$.
- **Densities:** Array $\sigma$ of size $m+1$, where $\sigma_j = \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(t_j) \ge 0$.
- **Cumulative Integrals:** Precomputed array $I$ of size $m+1$, where $I_j$ is the integral of density from $t_n$ to $t_j$.

**Steps:**

1. **Compute Total Opacity ($y_f$):**
   Calculate the total integral $I_{total} = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(s) ds$ using the chosen approximation (piecewise constant or linear).
   Compute $y_f = 1 - \exp(-I_{total})$ [S1].

2. **Generate Target Values:**
   Generate $k$ i.i.d. uniform random samples $u_i \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 1]$ [S1].
   Compute target cumulative opacity values: $y_{target, i} = y_f \cdot u_i$ [S1].
   *Optional Stratification:* For lower variance, use stratified sampling: $u_i \sim \mathcal{U}[\frac{i-1}{k+1}, \frac{i}{k+1}]$ [S1].

3. **Identify Bin:**
   For each $y_{target}$, find the index $i$ such that the cumulative integral up to $t_i$ is less than or equal to the target integral value $I_{target} = -\ln(1 - y_{target})$.
   Specifically, find $i$ such that $I_i \le I_{target} < I_{i+1}$ [S1].
   *Note:* $I_{target}$ is derived from $y_{target}$ via the inverse of the opacity function $F_r(t) = 1 - \exp(-\int \sigma)$. The equation to solve is $-\ln(1 - y_{target}) = \int_{t_n}^t \sigma_r(s) ds$ [S1].

4. **Solve for $t$ within Bin:**
   Depending on the density approximation, solve for $\Delta t = t - t_i$:

   **Case A: Piecewise Constant Approximation**
   Assume density is constant $\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{t}_i)$ within bin $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$.
   The integral equation is linear:
   $$ I_{target} = I_i + \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{t}_i)(t - t_i) $$
   Solve for $t$:
   $$ t = t_i + \frac{I_{target} - I_i}{\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{t}_i)} $$
   *Pitfall:* If $\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{t}_i) \approx 0$, add a small epsilon $\epsilon$ to the denominator for stability [S1].

   **Case B: Piecewise Linear Approximation**
   Assume density varies linearly between $\sigma_i = \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(t_i)$ and $\sigma_{i+1} = \sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(t_{i+1})$.
   The integral is quadratic in $t$. Let $\Delta t = t - t_i$.
   Formulate the quadratic equation $0 = a\Delta t^2 + b\Delta t + c$ [S1]:
   $$ a = \frac{\sigma_{i+1} - \sigma_i}{2} $$
   $$ b = \sigma_i \times (t_{i+1} - t_i) $$
   $$ c = \left( I_i - I_{target} \right) \times (t_{i+1} - t_i) $$
   *Correction on Source [S1] Formula for c:* The source defines $c$ relative to the sum of previous trapezoids minus $y$. Let's align with the source's explicit coefficients for $y=I_{target}$:
   Source [S1] states: $c = \left( \sum_{j=1}^i \frac{\sigma_j + \sigma_{j-1}}{2} (t_j - t_{j-1}) - y \right) \times (t_{i+1} - t_i)$. Here $y$ is the target integral $I_{target}$. The sum term is exactly $I_i$. So $c = (I_i - I_{target})(t_{i+1} - t_i)$.
   Solve for $\Delta t$:
   $$ \Delta t = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} $$
   Since the integral is monotonically increasing, we take the positive root [S1].
   Then $t = t_i + \Delta t$.

5. **Back-propagation:**
   The solution $t$ is a differentiable function of the densities $\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}$. Gradients flow from the loss through $t$ to the density network parameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import math

def inverse_opacity_calculation(t_vals, sigma_vals, y_targets, approx_type='linear'):
    """
    t_vals: Tensor of shape (M+1,), ray grid points t_1 ... t_{M+1}
    sigma_vals: Tensor of shape (M+1,), density values at grid points
    y_targets: Tensor of shape (K,), target cumulative opacity values y_target
    approx_type: 'constant' or 'linear'
    Returns: Tensor of shape (K,), sample positions t
    """
    device = t_vals.device
    M = len(t_vals) - 1
    
    # 1. Compute Cumulative Integrals I
    # I[i] is integral from t_1 to t_i
    I = torch.zeros_like(t_vals)
    if approx_type == 'constant':
        # Piecewise constant: density is sigma[j] in bin [t_j, t_{j+1}]
        # Integral of bin j: sigma[j] * (t_{j+1} - t_j)
        # I[i] = sum_{j=1}^{i-1} sigma[j] * (t_{j+1} - t_j)
        # Note: Indices in source are 1-based. Here 0-based.
        # Bin j (0-indexed) is [t[j], t[j+1]] with density sigma[j]
        bin_widths = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        bin_integrals = sigma_vals[:-1] * bin_widths
        # Cumulative sum. I[0] = 0. I[1] = bin_integrals[0].
        I[1:] = torch.cumsum(bin_integrals, dim=0)
        
    elif approx_type == 'linear':
        # Piecewise linear: trapezoidal rule
        # Integral of bin j: 0.5 * (sigma[j] + sigma[j+1]) * (t_{j+1} - t_j)
        bin_widths = t_vals[1:] - t_vals[:-1]
        avg_sigma = (sigma_vals[:-1] + sigma_vals[1:]) / 2.0
        bin_integrals = avg_sigma * bin_widths
        I[1:] = torch.cumsum(bin_integrals, dim=0)
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unknown approximation type")

    # 2. Convert y_targets to target integrals I_targets
    # y = 1 - exp(-I) => I = -ln(1 - y)
    # Clamp y to avoid log(0)
    y_clamped = torch.clamp(y_targets, max=1.0 - 1e-6)
    I_targets = -torch.log(1.0 - y_clamped)
    
    # 3. Find bins
    # Find index i such that I[i] <= I_target < I[i+1]
    # searchsorted returns insertion points. 
    # If I_target == I[i], it returns i+1. We want i.
    # We use 'right' to get the index of the first element > I_target.
    # Then bin_index = idx - 1.
    # Handle edge case where I_target >= I[-1] (should be clamped by y_f logic usually, but safety check)
    indices = torch.searchsorted(I, I_targets, side='right')
    # Clamp indices to valid bin range [0, M-1]
    indices = torch.clamp(indices - 1, min=0, max=M - 1)
    
    t_i = t_vals[indices]
    t_ip1 = t_vals[indices + 1]
    I_i = I[indices]
    
    sigma_i = sigma_vals[indices]
    sigma_ip1 = sigma_vals[indices + 1]
    
    dt_bin = t_ip1 - t_i
    
    if approx_type == 'constant':
        # t = t_i + (I_target - I_i) / sigma_i
        # Add epsilon for stability
        eps = 1e-6
        sigma_i_safe = sigma_i + eps
        delta_t = (I_targets - I_i) / sigma_i_safe
        t_samples = t_i + delta_t
        
    elif approx_type == 'linear':
        # Quadratic: 0 = a*delta_t^2 + b*delta_t + c
        # a = (sigma_ip1 - sigma_i) / 2
        # b = sigma_i * dt_bin
        # c = (I_i - I_target) * dt_bin
        
        a = (sigma_ip1 - sigma_i) / 2.0
        b = sigma_i * dt_bin
        c = (I_i - I_targets) * dt_bin
        
        # Solve for delta_t = (-b + sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / 2a
        # Handle a=0 case (constant density within bin)
        zero_a = torch.abs(a) < 1e-6
        delta_t_linear = torch.zeros_like(a)
        
        if not torch.all(zero_a):
            discriminant = b**2 - 4*a*c
            # Clamp discriminant to 0 if negative due to precision
            discriminant = torch.clamp(discriminant, min=0.0)
            delta_t_linear = (-b + torch.sqrt(discriminant)) / (2*a)
            
        delta_t_const = (I_targets - I_i) / (sigma_i + 1e-6)
        
        # Select based on zero_a
        delta_t = torch.where(zero_a, delta_t_const, delta_t_linear)
        t_samples = t_i + delta_t
        
    return t_samples
```

**Parameters:**
- $k$: Number of samples per ray. Typical value is 8 for visible quality without artifacts [S1].
- Learning rate for proposal density network: Starts at $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and decays to $5 \times 10^{-6}$ [S1].
- Learning rate for fine density network: Starts at $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and decays to $5 \times 10^{-5}$ [S1].
- Density output activation: Softplus (ensures non-negative density) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Zero Density:** When $\sigma_{\mathbf{r}}(\hat{t}_i) \approx 0$, the denominator in the piecewise constant inverse formula becomes unstable. Add a small epsilon $\epsilon$ to the denominator [S1].
- **Analytic Inversion:** The opacity function $F_r(t)$ cannot be computed analytically in practice because it requires integrating the density field. It must be approximated using piecewise constant or linear approximations [S1].
- **Boundary Conditions:** Ensure $y_{target}$ is clamped to $[0, 1-\epsilon]$ to avoid numerical issues with $\ln(1-y)$ [S1].

## Operating characteristics
**Time and Memory Complexity:**
- The method requires computing cumulative integrals $O(M)$ and solving a quadratic/linear equation per sample $O(1)$.
- It avoids redundant radiance queries compared to dense uniform grids, potentially reducing computation during training and inference [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
- On the Lego scene (Blender dataset) with $(N_p=16, N_f=32)$, RVS achieves PSNR 29.18 vs NeRF 27.09, SSIM 0.928 vs 0.913, and LPIPS 0.112 vs 0.121, with relative training time 0.37 vs 0.39 [S1].
- On the Lego scene with $(N_p=64, N_f=192)$, RVS achieves PSNR 33.03 vs NeRF 32.69, SSIM 0.964 vs 0.962, and LPIPS 0.047 vs 0.048, with relative training time 0.98 vs 1.0 [S1].
- On the Blender dataset (average) with $(N_p=32, N_f=64)$, RVS achieves PSNR 30.26 vs NeRF 29.49, SSIM 0.939 vs 0.934, and LPIPS 0.082 vs 0.085 [S1].
- On the LLFF dataset (average) with $(N_p=32, N_f=64)$, RVS achieves PSNR 26.01 vs NeRF 26.01 (tie), SSIM 0.796 vs 0.796 (tie), and LPIPS 0.273 vs 0.273 (tie) [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
- **Strengths:** Fully differentiable, allows end-to-end training without auxiliary reconstruction losses, improves reconstruction quality for hierarchical models, reduces variance with stratified sampling [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires careful handling of zero-density bins. The proposal density learned via RVS is significantly different from NeRF's, suggesting that mimicking the fine density is not optimal for the proposal [S1].

**When to Use:**
- Use when training neural radiance fields end-to-end and wanting to avoid auxiliary losses.
- Use when higher quality is needed with fewer samples (e.g., real-time or constrained compute).
- Do not use if the scene has large regions of zero density without proper epsilon handling, as it may cause instability in the constant approximation.

## Background
Inverse Opacity Calculation, often referred to as Reparameterized Volume Sampling (RVS), was introduced by Khrulkhov et al. in 2023 [S1]. It builds on the concept of inverse transform sampling used in NeRF but derives the sampling distribution from the density field directly rather than from a separate proposal network with auxiliary losses. The method leverages the mathematical equivalence between the CDF of the rendering contribution and the transmittance function [S2]. It is used in novel view synthesis and neural radiance field optimization [S1].
