# Temporal Filtering and Jittering

Also called: Jittering, Temporal Filtering, Temporal Supersampling

## Overview
This technique reduces spatial banding artifacts and noise in volume rendering by combining sub-voxel ray jittering with temporal reprojection, effectively increasing perceived resolution without additional raymarching steps [S1, unofficial]. It differs from static spatial filtering by leveraging temporal coherence to smooth noise over time, and from pure spatial jittering by using motion vectors to align previous frame data for blending [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Current frame’s volumetric data (typically stored in a 3D texture), previous frame’s 3D texture, camera motion vectors (or depth/position buffers for reprojection), and a pseudo-random sequence generator (e.g., blue noise or Halton sequence) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** A temporally filtered 3D texture representing the accumulated volumetric light and transmittance [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **3D Texture:** Stores the integrated volumetric data (e.g., in-scattered light and transmittance) for the current and previous frames. The exact layout depends on the rendering API (e.g., OpenGL `GL_TEXTURE_3D`) but must support trilinear interpolation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Motion Vectors:** A 2D texture or buffer storing the displacement of each pixel from the previous frame to the current frame, derived from camera and object transforms [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Depth Jittering:** For each ray in the current frame, calculate a sub-voxel offset using a pseudo-random sequence (such as blue noise or a Halton sequence) [S1, unofficial]. Apply this offset to the ray’s starting position or step size to break up spatial banding artifacts caused by coarse slice resolution [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Ray Marching:** Perform the standard ray marching process using the jittered rays to accumulate volumetric data into a temporary 3D texture for the current frame [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Temporal Reprojection:** Execute a compute pass that reprojects the previous frame’s 3D texture into the current frame’s coordinate space using the camera motion vectors [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Blending:** Blend the current frame’s 3D texture with the reprojected previous frame’s 3D texture. This blending smooths out the noise introduced by jittering and improves the perceived volume resolution [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Final Composition:** (Post-processing step) Sample the filtered 3D texture using hardware trilinear interpolation (`GL_LINEAR`) based on screen coordinates and linear depth converted to 3D texture coordinates $(u, v, w)$ using an exponential depth equation [S1, unofficial]. Add the sampled in-scattered light to the scene color and attenuate the scene color by the sampled transmittance [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Ray Offset:** $P_{jittered} = P_{base} + \Delta P_{random}$, where $\Delta P_{random}$ is the sub-voxel offset derived from the pseudo-random sequence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Reprojection:** $T_{prev\_reprojected}(x, y) = T_{prev}(x - \Delta x, y - \Delta y)$, where $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ are the motion vector components for pixel $(x, y)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Blending:** $T_{final} = \alpha T_{current} + (1 - \alpha) T_{prev\_reprojected}$, where $\alpha$ is a blending factor (value not specified in sources) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Scene Composition:** $C_{out} = C_{scene} \cdot T_{sampled} + L_{in-scattered} \cdot (1 - T_{sampled})$, where $T_{sampled}$ is the transmittance and $L_{in-scattered}$ is the light sampled from the 3D texture [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Random Sequence Type:** Blue noise or Halton sequence [S1, unofficial].
*   **Interpolation Method:** Trilinear interpolation (`GL_LINEAR`) for sampling the 3D texture [S1, unofficial].
*   **Blending Factor:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Motion Vectors:** Incorrect or missing motion vectors will cause ghosting or misalignment during reprojection [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Sub-voxel offsets require sufficient floating-point precision to avoid visible artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Handling the edges of the volume during reprojection may require clamping or extrapolation [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Bicubic Filtering Upscaling
A post-processing technique that smooths noise artifacts from blue noise dithering by calculating a new pixel value using a cubic polynomial over 16 neighboring pixels [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when residual noise patterns remain after temporal filtering and spatial jittering [S2, unofficial]. Implementation involves rendering the raymarched scene into a Frame Buffer Object (FBO), passing the FBO texture as a uniform to a bicubic filtering material, and rendering a fullscreen triangle with that material to output the smoothed scene [S2, unofficial]. It retains details while smoothing noise, particularly at edges and in less dense regions [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Adds a temporal reprojection compute pass and a post-processing blur pass to the standard ray marching cost [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the current and previous frame’s 3D textures, plus motion vectors and FBOs for post-processing [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Performance scales with the resolution of the 3D texture and the complexity of the reprojection and blurring passes [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Improves perceived resolution without extra raymarching steps; effectively hides spatial banding artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires motion vectors for reprojection; may introduce ghosting if motion is not handled correctly; residual noise may still require additional post-processing like bicubic filtering [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When spatial banding and noise are visible due to coarse slice resolution or limited raymarching steps [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When motion vectors are unavailable or unreliable; when real-time performance is critically constrained and the overhead of temporal filtering and post-processing is unacceptable [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Background
This technique is described in the context of volumetric fog rendering using 3D textures in OpenGL [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of stochastic jittering to reduce banding and extends it with temporal reprojection to smooth noise over time [S1, unofficial]. Bicubic filtering upsampling is mentioned as a complementary post-processing step to further reduce noise artifacts in raymarched scenes [S2, unofficial].
