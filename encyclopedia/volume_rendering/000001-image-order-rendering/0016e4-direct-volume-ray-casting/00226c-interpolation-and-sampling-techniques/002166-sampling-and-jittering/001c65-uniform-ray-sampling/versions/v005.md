# Uniform Ray Sampling

Also called: Uniform Sampling, Uniform Point Sampling, Uniform Point Sampling along Rays

## Overview
Uniform Ray Sampling generates sample points along camera rays by uniformly distributing depths between a near plane ($t_n$) and a far plane ($t_f$) [S2][S5, S7, unofficial]. It serves as the baseline discretization method for volume rendering and neural radiance fields (NeRF), assuming that every point along the ray contributes equally to the final image [S2, S10]. Unlike [Stratified Ray Sampling](3014-stratified-ray-sampling.md) or [Importance Sampling for SPH Rendering](3624-importance-sampling-for-sph-rendering.md), which concentrate samples in high-contribution regions, uniform sampling treats all depth intervals equally, making it the simplest to implement but often the least efficient for scenes with large empty spaces or complex density distributions [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Ray origins $\mathbf{o} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (shape $[N, 3]$ for $N$ rays).
*   Ray directions $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (shape $[N, 3]$, typically unit-norm).
*   Near plane distance $t_n$ (scalar or shape $[N]$).
*   Far plane distance $t_f$ (scalar or shape $[N]$).
*   Number of samples per ray $N_s$ (integer).

**Outputs:**
*   Sample positions $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (shape $[N, N_s, 3]$).
*   Sample depths $t_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (shape $[N, N_s]$).
*   Segment lengths $\Delta t_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (shape $[N, N_s]$ or $[N, N_s, 1]$).

**Data Structures:**
The method typically populates a `RayBundle` object or similar structure containing:
*   `origins`: Tensor of ray origins.
*   `directions`: Tensor of ray directions.
*   `sample_points`: Tensor of computed 3D coordinates.
*   `sample_lengths`: Tensor of distances between consecutive samples (or from origin for the first sample).

**Steps:**

1.  **Generate Depth Values:** Create $N_s$ depth values uniformly spaced between $t_n$ and $t_f$.
    $$ t_i = t_n + \frac{i}{N_s - 1} (t_f - t_n), \quad i = 0, 1, \dots, N_s - 1 $$
    *Note: Some implementations use $N_s + 1$ points or specific indexing $i \in [0, N]$ depending on whether endpoints are included [S10]. The standard linear spacing is often generated via `torch.linspace` or equivalent.*

2.  **Broadcast Ray Parameters:** Expand ray origins and directions to match the batch and sample dimensions.
    *   Reshape $\mathbf{o}$ to $[N, 1, 3]$.
    *   Reshape $\mathbf{r}$ to $[N, 1, 3]$.
    *   Reshape $t$ to $[N, N_s, 1]$ or $[1, N_s, 1]$ depending on batching.

3.  **Compute Sample Positions:** Calculate the 3D coordinates for each sample.
    $$ \mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \cdot \mathbf{r} $$
    In tensor operations:
    ```python
    # o: [N, 1, 3], r: [N, 1, 3], t: [N, N_s, 1]
    sample_points = o + t * r
    ```

4.  **Compute Segment Lengths:** Calculate the distance between consecutive samples for integration (e.g., transmittance calculation).
    $$ \Delta t_i = t_{i+1} - t_i $$
    For the last sample, $\Delta t$ is often set to a large value or handled separately in front-to-back rendering [S7, S12, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `n_pts_per_ray` ($N_s$): Number of samples per ray. Typical values: 64 for coarse NeRF passes, 128-256 for fine passes [S14, S11, unofficial].
*   `min_depth` ($t_n$): Near clipping plane. Defined in camera config [S6][S12, unofficial].
*   `max_depth` ($t_f$): Far clipping plane. Defined in camera config [S6][S12, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Empty Space:** Uniform sampling wastes evaluations in empty regions, leading to low efficiency compared to spatial skipping methods [S2].
*   **Large Depth Ranges:** Linear spacing causes excessive samples in the distance and insufficient resolution near the camera in unbounded scenes [S10].
*   **Precision:** When $t_n \approx t_f$, numerical instability may occur. Ensure $t_f > t_n$ [S14, unofficial].
*   **Endpoint Inclusion:** Whether the first and last samples lie exactly on $t_n$ and $t_f$ affects integration accuracy. Some implementations exclude endpoints to avoid double-counting or include them for boundary coverage [S1, unofficial][S10].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def uniform_ray_sampling(origins, directions, near, far, num_samples):
    """
    Generate uniform samples along rays.
    
    Args:
        origins: Tensor of shape (N, 3)
        directions: Tensor of shape (N, 3)
        near: Float or Tensor of shape (N,)
        far: Float or Tensor of shape (N,)
        num_samples: Integer
        
    Returns:
        sample_points: Tensor of shape (N, num_samples, 3)
        sample_lengths: Tensor of shape (N, num_samples, 1)
    """
    # Step 1: Generate linearly spaced depths
    # t_vals shape: (num_samples,)
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, num_samples)
    
    # Map to [near, far]
    # If near/far are scalars, broadcast; if tensors, ensure shape compatibility
    # z_vals shape: (num_samples,) or (N, num_samples) if near/far are per-ray
    if near.dim() == 0:
        z_vals = near * (1.0 - t_vals) + far * t_vals
    else:
        # near/far shape (N,)
        # t_vals shape (num_samples,)
        # Result shape (N, num_samples)
        z_vals = near.unsqueeze(1) * (1.0 - t_vals.unsqueeze(0)) + far.unsqueeze(1) * t_vals.unsqueeze(0)
        
    # Step 2: Broadcast origins and directions
    # origins: (N, 3) -> (N, 1, 3)
    o = origins.unsqueeze(1)
    # directions: (N, 3) -> (N, 1, 3)
    d = directions.unsqueeze(1)
    
    # z_vals: (N, num_samples) -> (N, num_samples, 1)
    z = z_vals.unsqueeze(2)
    
    # Step 3: Compute sample points
    # sample_points: (N, num_samples, 3)
    sample_points = o + d * z
    
    # Step 4: Compute sample lengths (deltas)
    # Calculate differences between consecutive z_vals
    # deltas shape: (N, num_samples - 1)
    deltas = z_vals[:, 1:] - z_vals[:, :-1]
    
    # Append a large value for the last segment or handle in integration loop
    # For simplicity, we return lengths corresponding to intervals
    # If exact segment lengths for each sample point are needed (e.g. for front-to-back),
    # the last delta is often set to a large number or handled by the renderer.
    # Here we return the intervals.
    sample_lengths = torch.cat([deltas, torch.full((deltas.shape[0], 1), 1e10, device=deltas.device)], dim=1)
    
    return sample_points, sample_lengths
```

## Subtopics

### [Stratified Monte Carlo Radiance Estimate](1373-stratified-monte-carlo-radiance-estimate.md)
An unbiased radiance estimation method that reduces variance compared to uniform sampling by dividing the ray interval into strata and sampling one point uniformly at random within each stratum. It ensures coverage of the entire interval, preventing the clustering of samples that can occur with pure random uniform sampling, and is particularly effective for degenerate distributions.

### [Evenly Spaced Sampling with Preprocessed Rays and Depth Indicators](2335-evenly-spaced-sampling-with-preprocessed-rays-and-depth-indi.md)
A method that ensures even spacing along rays within a voxel cell by using preprocessed ray data and a 4-bit depth-sampling indicator stored in pixel opacity. It maintains sampling continuity across cells, differing from basic uniform sampling by relying on shared preprocessed rays and stateful indicators to compute coordinates for trilinear interpolation.

### [Uniform NDC Point Sampling](2395-uniform-ndc-point-sampling.md)
Transforms camera rays into an average camera frame and uniformly samples points within the projected Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) space. It provides linear sampling in disparity from the near plane to infinity, combining benefits of uniform and logarithmic sampling. It is distinct because it requires all samples to lie strictly in the front hemisphere of the average camera frame.

### [Batch Stratified Sampling](2730-batch-stratified-sampling.md)
Generates $N_c$ evenly-spaced depth bins in the interval $[near, far]$ and perturbs sample positions within each bin to reduce aliasing artifacts. It extends uniform sampling by stratifying the depth interval, ensuring more even distribution across the ray's depth range.

### [Stratified Ray Sampling](3014-stratified-ray-sampling.md)
Divides the ray interval $[near, far]$ into $N$ evenly-spaced bins and draws one sample uniformly at random from each bin. Unlike fixed uniform spacing, it introduces controlled randomness within structured intervals to break up regular sampling patterns, improving the quality of training data for volume rendering.

### [Local Sampling](5532-local-sampling.md)
Reduces computational cost by concentrating samples around a known surface representation (e.g., depth texture) rather than distributing them uniformly along the entire ray. It solves the problem of wasted evaluations in empty space, allowing for drastic sample count reduction (down to two per ray) while maintaining quality, unlike uniform sampling which covers the full depth range.

### [Non-linear (Logarithmic) Sampling](5814-non-linear-logarithmic-sampling.md)
Distributes points along a view ray using a logarithmic function to concentrate density near the camera while maintaining coverage over large depth ranges. It addresses the limitation of uniform sampling in large scenes where linear spacing causes excessive evaluations in the distance or insufficient resolution near the viewer.

### Forward Sampling Inside Surface
Samples points forward along the ray from a known surface to get SDF values inside the object, used when latent space is not pretrained [S3, unofficial]. It initializes inside samples based on a grid list derived from the distance to the surface, ensuring samples are placed within the object volume [S3, unofficial].

### Camera Ray Sampling for Translucent Materials
Samples the ray randomly with a small number of samples (as low as one) when the camera ray reaches translucent material after diffuse interactions [S15]. It computes sample points using a formula involving the extinction coefficient $\sigma_t$ and maximum depth $d_{\max}$ to ensure exactly $k_{\max}$ samples are placed, handling small object widths by preventing undersampling [S15].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot N_s)$, where $N$ is the number of rays and $N_s$ is the number of samples per ray. The computation is highly parallelizable on GPUs [S1, S12, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot N_s \cdot 3)$ for storing sample positions, plus $O(N \cdot N_s)$ for depths/lengths [S7, S13, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** On an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, uniform ray sampling for 1M rays on the dragon dataset (fp32, cone=0) takes approximately 2.790ms, with optimizations yielding a 1.06x speedup [S1, unofficial]. For the wdas_cloud dataset (2.6 GB fog), it takes 16.391ms [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement; deterministic (if no jitter); baseline for comparison; works well for small scenes or individual objects where density is uniform [S2, S10].
*   **Limitations:** Lowest efficiency in most cases compared to spatial skipping or PDF-based methods; wastes samples in empty space; poor convergence for large depth ranges [S2].
*   **When to Use:** As a baseline; for small, dense volumes; when no transmittance estimator is available [S2].
*   **When Not to Use:** For large, sparse scenes; when high efficiency is required; when density varies significantly along the ray [S2].

## Background
Uniform sampling is the default raymarching strategy in NeRF-style rendering, placing samples uniformly between the near and far planes to construct feature vectors for the neural network [S10]. It assumes a constant PDF and linearly decaying transmittance, making it the easiest sampling method to implement but often the least efficient [S2]. It has been widely used as the baseline in NeRF [16] and subsequent neural rendering works [S2, S10].
