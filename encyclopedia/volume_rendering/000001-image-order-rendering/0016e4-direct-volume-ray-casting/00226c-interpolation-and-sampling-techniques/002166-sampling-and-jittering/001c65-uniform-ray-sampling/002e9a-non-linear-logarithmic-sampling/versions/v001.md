# Non-linear (Logarithmic) Sampling

Also called: Logarithmic Sampling, Logarithmic Sampling for Volumetric Clouds, Logarithmic depth sampling

## Overview
Non-linear (Logarithmic) Sampling is a sample placement strategy that distributes points along a view ray using a logarithmic function to concentrate density near the camera while maintaining coverage over large depth ranges [S1][S2, unofficial]. It addresses the limitation of uniform sampling in large scenes, where linear spacing causes excessive evaluations in the distance or insufficient resolution near the viewer [S1]. Unlike its sibling [Uniform Ray Sampling](1261-uniform-ray-sampling.md), which treats all depth intervals equally, this method balances the spatial footprint of samples to approximate cubic volumes in view space, preventing visual artifacts such as incorrect lighting during camera rotation [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Minimum depth $d_{min}$, maximum depth $d_{max}$, and the number of samples $N$.
*   **Outputs:** A sequence of $N$ depth values $\tilde{d}_i$ for $i \in [0, N-1]$.

**Data Structures:**
*   A list or array of floating-point numbers representing depth values.

**Parameters:**
*   $d_{min}$: The start of the ray segment (typically camera near plane).
*   $d_{max}$: The end of the ray segment (typically camera far plane).
*   $N$: The total number of samples per ray.

**Formulas:**
The method first generates uniform depth indices $d_i$ and then warps them logarithmically [S1].

1.  **Uniform Depth Generation:**
    $$d_i = d_{min} + \frac{i}{N-1} (d_{max} - d_{min})$$
    where $i$ is the sample index from $0$ to $N-1$.

2.  **Logarithmic Warping:**
    $$\tilde{d}_i = d_{min} + \frac{\log(d_i - d_{min} + 1)}{\log(d_{max} - d_{min} + 1)} \cdot (d_{max} - d_{min})$$
    [S1]

    *Note:* The term $+1$ inside the logarithm ensures numerical stability when $d_i - d_{min}$ is zero or very small [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def generate_logarithmic_samples(d_min, d_max, n_samples):
    """
    Generates n_samples depth values along a ray from d_min to d_max
    using logarithmic non-linearity.
    
    Args:
        d_min (float): Minimum depth (near plane).
        d_max (float): Maximum depth (far plane).
        n_samples (int): Number of samples to generate.
        
    Returns:
        list[float]: List of warped depth values.
    """
    if n_samples <= 0:
        return []
    if d_max <= d_min:
        return [d_min] * n_samples

    samples = []
    depth_range = d_max - d_min
    
    # Precompute the denominator to avoid repeated log calculations
    # Using +1 for stability as per source [S1]
    log_denom = math.log(depth_range + 1)
    
    for i in range(n_samples):
        # Step 1: Calculate uniform depth d_i
        # Linear interpolation between d_min and d_max
        d_i = d_min + (i / (n_samples - 1)) * depth_range
        
        # Step 2: Apply logarithmic warping
        # Formula from [S1]: tilde_d_i = d_min + (log(d_i - d_min + 1) / log(d_max - d_min + 1)) * (d_max - d_min)
        numerator = math.log(d_i - d_min + 1)
        warped_depth = d_min + (numerator / log_denom) * depth_range
        
        samples.append(warped_depth)
        
    return samples
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The addition of $1$ inside the logarithm is critical for preventing $\log(0)$ errors when $d_i = d_{min}$ [S1].
*   **Balance:** If the sampling density along the z-axis is not balanced with the x-y plane coverage, lighting will appear incorrect when the camera rotates, even if the static render looks acceptable [S2, unofficial]. The goal is to keep the frustum covered by a single sample roughly cubic [S2, unofficial].
*   **Under-sampling:** To maintain performance, the number of samples along the z-axis is typically set to approximately 10 times fewer than the number of samples covering the x-y plane area [S2, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Stratified Monte Carlo Radiance Estimate
Uses stratified sampling to reduce variance in radiance estimation. Choose when noise reduction is prioritized over depth-specific density balancing. [1373-stratified-monte-carlo-radiance-estimate.md]

### Evenly Spaced Sampling with Preprocessed Rays and Depth Indicators
Precomputes ray depths to ensure even spacing. Choose when deterministic, uniform coverage is required regardless of depth magnitude. [2335-evenly-spaced-sampling-with-preprocessed-rays-and-depth-indi.md]

### Uniform NDC Point Sampling
Samples in Normalized Device Coordinates. Choose when working with neural radiance fields that assume NDC input, but be aware of perspective distortions away from the average camera frame. [2395-uniform-ndc-point-sampling.md]

### Batch Stratified Sampling
Applies stratification across batches of rays. Choose for parallel rendering pipelines requiring variance reduction. [2730-batch-stratified-sampling.md]

### Stratified Ray Sampling
Stratifies the ray space directly. Choose for general-purpose volume rendering where variance is a primary concern. [3014-stratified-ray-sampling.md]

### Local Sampling
Samples around a specific depth (e.g., ground truth or previous estimate). Choose when the object location is known a priori to maximize efficiency. [5532-local-sampling.md]

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$, where $N$ is the number of samples per ray. The logarithmic calculation is constant time per sample.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the sample positions.
*   **Performance:** In ablation studies, logarithmic sampling increases PSNR by 1.3 dB per doubling of $N$, compared to 1.8 dB for uniform sampling [S1]. While uniform sampling yields higher quality gains per sample, logarithmic sampling provides better quality for a fixed computational budget in large scenes by focusing evaluations where they matter most [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces network evaluations in large depth ranges without losing quality near the camera; prevents visual artifacts during camera rotation by balancing spatial coverage [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Lower PSNR efficiency per sample compared to uniform sampling; requires careful balancing with x-y sampling density to avoid lighting artifacts [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Large scenes with significant depth ranges; volumetric clouds; neural radiance fields where near-field detail is critical.
*   **When not to use:** Small scenes with limited depth range; scenarios where uniform distribution is strictly required for theoretical guarantees.

## Background
Non-linear logarithmic sampling was introduced in the context of compact neural radiance fields, specifically in the DONeRF paper [S1]. It was proposed as an intuitive first step to reduce network evaluations by focusing samples on objects closer to the camera. The technique was further popularized and adapted for real-time volumetric cloud rendering in game engines, where it is described as the "most important optimization for large scenes" to balance sample frustums [S2, unofficial].
