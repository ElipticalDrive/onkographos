# Insane HQ Bokeh Blur

Also called: Iris/Field Blur, Screen-Space Depth of Field / Tilt-Shift Blur, TiltShiftHdrLensBlur, fragBlurInsaneHQ

## Overview
Insane HQ Bokeh Blur is a screen-space post-processing technique that simulates depth-of-field (DoF) or tilt-shift effects using Poisson-disk sampling with a disc kernel [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent, [Separable Weighted Blur (DoF)](21017-separable-weighted-blur-dof.md), by employing a spatially varying kernel weight to control blur radius based on screen position and using a fixed set of Poisson-disk samples for uniform coverage, rather than a separable 1D convolution [S1, unofficial]. It handles stereo rendering and alpha-weighted accumulation to preserve sharp edges in the final image [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A source texture `_MainTex` containing the scene color and alpha. UV coordinates `uv` (normalized 0-1).
*   **Parameters:**
    *   `_BlurSize`: Maximum blur radius multiplier (scalar).
    *   `_BlurArea`: Controls the falloff/gradient strength of the blur weight (scalar).
    *   `_Offsets`: A vector where `.w` is a scale factor for Poisson disk size, `.z` is a minimum sample count floor.
    *   `DiscKernel`: An array of 28 `float3` vectors. Layout: `[x, y, weight]`. Only `x` and `y` are used for offset calculation; `z` is ignored in the standard implementation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A blurred color texture with the computed blur weight `w` stored in the alpha channel.

**Data Structures:**
*   `DiscKernel`: Array of size 28. Each element is a `float3`.
    *   `DiscKernel[l].xy`: Normalized offset coordinates for the l-th sample.
    *   `DiscKernel[l].z`: Precomputed weight (unused in the core sampling loop described in sources) [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Compute Spatial Weight ($w$):**
    Calculate the blur weight based on the normalized UV coordinates and the selected mode (Field or Iris). The weight determines the local blur radius.
    $$ w = \text{clamp}(\text{WeightMode}(uv), 0, \_BlurSize) $$
    Where `WeightMode` is either:
    *   **Field Mode (Tilt-Shift):**
        $$ \text{WeightFieldMode}(uv) = |uv_y \cdot 2 - 1| \cdot \_BlurArea $$
    *   **Iris Mode (DoF):**
        $$ \text{WeightIrisMode}(uv) = \text{dot}(uv \cdot 2 - 1, uv \cdot 2 - 1) \cdot \_BlurArea $$

2.  **Early Exit Optimization:**
    If $w < 10^{-2}$ and the shader API is not DirectX 9, skip sampling and return the center tap immediately. This prevents compilation/runtime crashes on older D3D9 hardware which may fail on early returns or conditional compilation blocks [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Initialize Accumulators:**
    Sample the center pixel:
    $$ \text{centerTap} = \text{tex2D}(\_MainTex, uv) $$
    Initialize the sum with the center tap:
    $$ \text{sum} = \text{centerTap} $$
    Calculate the Poisson scale factor based on the center alpha and offsets:
    $$ \text{poissonScale} = \_MainTex\_TexelSize.xy \cdot w $$
    *(Note: Sources indicate `poissonScale` is multiplied by `centerTap.a * _Offsets.w` in some variants, but the core formula uses `w` derived from the weight function. The scale defines the radius of the disc kernel in texel space)* [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Calculate Sample Count:**
    Determine the normalization divisor. A factor of 0.25 is used to handle high-frequency specular highlights:
    $$ \text{sampleCount} = \max(\text{centerTap}.a \cdot 0.25, \_Offsets.z) $$
    *(Note: In the final normalization step, the denominator is $1.0 + N$ or $1.0 + 2N$ depending on the pass. The `sampleCount` variable here acts as a dynamic floor or accumulator base)* [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Poisson-Disk Sampling Loop:**
    Loop $l$ from 0 to 27 (28 iterations):
    a.  **Compute Sample UV:**
        $$ \text{sampleUV} = uv + \text{DiscKernel}[l].xy \cdot \text{poissonScale} $$
        For stereo rendering, this UV is adjusted for left and right eyes using `UnityStereoScreenSpaceUVAdjust`.
    b.  **Sample Texture:**
        $$ \text{sample0} = \text{tex2D}(\_MainTex, \text{sampleUV}_{\text{left}}) $$
        $$ \text{sample1} = \text{tex2D}(\_MainTex, \text{sampleUV}_{\text{right}}) $$
    c.  **Accumulate:**
        If either sample has non-zero alpha, compute Bokeh weights and accumulate:
        $$ \text{weights} = \text{BokehWeightDisc2}(\text{sample0}, \text{sample1}, \dots, \text{centerTap}) $$
        $$ \text{sum} += \text{sample0} \cdot \text{weights}.x + \text{sample1} \cdot \text{weights}.y $$
        $$ \text{sampleCount} += \text{dot}(\text{weights}, 1) $$
        *(Note: The exact implementation of `BokehWeightDisc2` is not detailed in the provided notes, but it likely weights samples based on their depth/alpha relative to the center to simulate bokeh shape)* [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Normalize and Output:**
    Divide the accumulated RGB sum by the sample count. For HQ passes, the divisor is $1.0 + 2.0 \cdot N$ (where $N=28$); for standard passes, it is $1.0 + N$.
    $$ \text{returnValue}.rgb = \frac{\text{sum}.rgb}{\text{sampleCount}} $$
    Preserve the center alpha:
    $$ \text{returnValue}.a = \text{centerTap}.a $$
    Store the computed blur weight $w$ in the output alpha channel for subsequent passes if needed.
    $$ \text{output} = \text{float4}(\text{returnValue}.rgb, w) $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def insane_hq_bokeh_blur(uv, main_tex, main_tex_texel_size, blur_size, blur_area, offsets, disc_kernel):
    """
    Insane HQ Bokeh Blur implementation.
    
    Args:
        uv: float2, normalized UV coordinates [0, 1].
        main_tex: Texture2D, input scene color/alpha.
        main_tex_texel_size: float2, (1/width, 1/height).
        blur_size: float, max blur radius multiplier.
        blur_area: float, controls falloff strength.
        offsets: float4, .w is Poisson scale factor, .z is min sample count.
        disc_kernel: list of 28 float3, [x, y, weight]. Only xy used.
        
    Returns:
        float4, blurred color with blur weight in alpha.
    """
    
    # 1. Compute Spatial Weight
    # Determine mode. Assuming Iris Mode (DoF) as default for "Bokeh".
    # Field Mode uses abs(uv.y * 2 - 1). Iris Mode uses dot product.
    uv_adjusted = uv * 2.0 - 1.0
    weight_field = abs(uv.y * 2.0 - 1.0) * blur_area
    weight_iris = dot(uv_adjusted, uv_adjusted) * blur_area
    
    # Select mode. The code snippet implies a switch or uniform selection.
    # We'll assume Iris Mode for "Bokeh" unless specified otherwise.
    w_raw = weight_iris 
    
    # Clamp to max blur size
    w = clamp(w_raw, 0.0, blur_size)
    
    # 2. Early Exit Optimization
    # Guard against D3D9 compiler issues with early returns
    if w < 0.01: # 1e-2f
        # In a real shader, this would be #ifndef SHADER_API_D3D9
        # For this generic implementation, we proceed but note the optimization
        pass 
        
    # 3. Sample Center
    center_tap = main_tex.sample(uv)
    
    # 4. Initialize Accumulators
    # Poisson scale: texel size * blur weight
    poisson_scale = main_tex_texel_size * w
    
    # Sample count initialization
    # Factor 0.25 for high-frequency specular highlights
    sample_count = max(center_tap.a * 0.25, offsets.z)
    
    # Sum initialized with center tap
    sum_rgb = center_tap.rgb
    
    # 5. Poisson-Disk Sampling Loop
    num_samples = len(disc_kernel) # 28
    
    for l in range(num_samples):
        # Compute sample UV
        # DiscKernel[l].xy * poisson_scale
        offset = disc_kernel[l].xy * poisson_scale
        sample_uv = uv + offset
        
        # Sample texture
        # Note: Stereo support requires adjusting UVs for left/right eye.
        # Assuming single-pass for this generic implementation.
        sample = main_tex.sample(sample_uv)
        
        # Accumulate
        # If sample has alpha, add to sum.
        # The source mentions BokehWeightDisc2, which is not fully defined.
        # We assume a simple alpha-weighted accumulation for the generic case.
        if sample.a > 0.0:
            sum_rgb += sample.rgb
            sample_count += 1.0 # Simplified accumulation logic based on source notes
            
    # 6. Normalize and Output
    # HQ pass divisor: 1.0 + 2.0 * N
    # Standard pass divisor: 1.0 + N
    # We use HQ divisor as per the method name
    divisor = 1.0 + 2.0 * num_samples
    
    blurred_rgb = sum_rgb / divisor
    
    # Preserve center alpha
    output_alpha = center_tap.a
    
    # Store blur weight in alpha for potential downstream use
    output_alpha = w 
    
    return float4(blurred_rgb, output_alpha)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **D3D9 Compatibility:** The early exit optimization (`if w < 1e-2f`) must be guarded with `#ifndef SHADER_API_D3D9` to prevent compilation or runtime crashes on older DirectX 9 hardware [S1, unofficial].
*   **Disc Kernel Weights:** The `DiscKernel` array contains a `z` component representing a precomputed weight, but the implementation ignores it, using only `x` and `y` for offset calculation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Stereo Rendering:** The method supports stereo rendering by sampling both left and right eye textures. UVs must be adjusted using `UnityStereoScreenSpaceUVAdjust` [S1, unofficial].
*   **Specular Highlights:** The use of `0.25` factor in `sampleCount` calculation helps mitigate artifacts from high-frequency specular highlights [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per pixel, where $N$ is the number of disc samples (28). The complexity is constant per pixel but depends on the resolution via texel size scaling.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per pixel. The kernel is stored in constant memory.
*   **Performance:** The early exit optimization significantly improves performance in regions with low blur weight (sharp focus areas). Stereo rendering doubles the texture fetches.
*   **Strengths:** Simulates realistic bokeh effects using Poisson-disk sampling for uniform coverage. Handles stereo rendering. Preserves sharp edges via alpha weighting.
*   **Limitations:** Screen-space only, so depth-of-field effects are not visible outside the camera's view. Performance is higher than separable blurs due to non-separable kernel sampling.
*   **When to Use:** When realistic depth-of-field or tilt-shift effects are required in post-processing, especially for stereo VR/3D content.
*   **When Not to Use:** When performance is critical and the blur radius is small (separable blurs are faster). When depth information is unavailable or unreliable.

## Background
Insane HQ Bokeh Blur is a screen-space post-processing technique developed for Unity's Image Effects package. It is part of the "Insane" series of blur shaders, which include various optimizations and features for high-quality image processing. The method is designed to simulate depth-of-field effects using Poisson-disk sampling, providing a balance between quality and performance. It is commonly used in Unity projects requiring realistic camera effects.
