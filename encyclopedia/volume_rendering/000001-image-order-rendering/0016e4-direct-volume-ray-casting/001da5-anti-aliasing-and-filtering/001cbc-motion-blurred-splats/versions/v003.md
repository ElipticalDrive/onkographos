# Motion-Blurred Splats

Also called: Motion Blur in Volume Rendering, Temporal Antialiasing Technique for Splatting

## Overview
Motion-blurred splats approximate temporal integration in volume rendering by stretching the spatial footprint of each voxel splat along its projected motion vector, thereby smoothing flickering and scintillation artifacts caused by rapid camera or object movement [S1, unofficial]. Unlike standard splatting which suffers from aliasing during high-speed animation, this method integrates energy over the time domain by distributing the voxel's contribution across the path it traverses, though it does not resolve visibility changes or popping artifacts caused by sorting order variations [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A voxel dataset with associated linear motion vectors for each voxel; a camera view; a frame interval defined by start time $t_{i-1}$ and end time $t_i$.
*   **Output:** A rendered image where moving voxels appear blurred along their motion paths.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Voxel:** Contains spatial position, scalar value (opacity/density), and a 3D linear motion vector $\vec{v}$.
*   **Splat Footprint:** Defined by a radial radius $s$ and a static area $A_1$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Transform Motion Vector:** For each voxel, associate its linear motion vector. Transform this vector into eye space to determine the projected direction and length on the viewing plane [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Determine Footprint Geometry:** For radially symmetric footprint functions, approximate the motion by smearing (stretching) the footprint in the direction of the projected velocity vector [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Construct Motion-Blurred Splat:** Draw the splat between positions $t_{i-1}$ and $t_i$. The original circular footprint is conceptually cut in half and stretched between these positions. This results in a shape composed of:
    *   Two half-circles (A and B) at the endpoints, each with the same radial profile $s$ as the static splat.
    *   A rectangle (C) connecting the two half-circles, with length $l$ (the distance between $t_{i-1}$ and $t_i$) and width $w$ (typically $2s$) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Rotate Footprint:** For arbitrary projected velocity vector directions, rotate the motion-blurred splat geometry to align with the velocity direction, using a rotation scheme similar to Crawfis and Max [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Adjust Opacity for Energy Conservation:** Scale the splat opacity to maintain constant energy contribution. The area of the motion-blurred footprint $A_2$ is calculated as:
    $$ A_2 = A_1 + 2ls $$
    Where:
    *   $A_1$ is the area of the static footprint extent.
    *   $l$ is the distance between $t_{i-1}$ and $t_i$.
    *   $s$ is the radial splat radius [S1, unofficial].
    
    The opacity is scaled by the ratio of the static area to the motion-blurred area:
    $$ \text{opacity}_{\text{new}} = \text{opacity}_{\text{old}} \times \frac{A_1}{A_2} $$
    This ensures the integrated intensity of the motion-blurred splat equals that of a single stationary splat [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Visibility Artifacts:** The algorithm does not address the visibility problem (changing hidden surfaces) or popping artifacts caused by voxel sorting changes; it only addresses integrated energy across the time domain [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The approximation assumes linear motion and constant velocity within the frame interval. Non-linear motion or acceleration is not handled [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Motion Blur via Splat Smearing](1593-motion-blur-via-splat-smearing.md)
A variant that distributes each sample's value evenly across voxels touched by the motion vector, preserving total sum but losing lighting detail and failing to cancel camera motion relative to primitives.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of voxels, similar to standard splatting, with additional overhead for computing projected vectors and rotating footprints.
*   **Memory Complexity:** No significant additional memory required beyond storing motion vectors per voxel.
*   **Performance:** Applied to a 512 x 512 x 76 nerve cell dataset, motion blur dominated the visual result, making the distinction between antialiased and motion-blurred splats less visible for objects with high movement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively reduces flickering and scintillation artifacts in animations with rapid movement or spinning objects [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Does not solve visibility/popping artifacts; approximation may introduce blurring artifacts if motion vectors are inaccurate or if the footprint geometry is complex [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** Ideal for emission or X-ray-like volume models where compositing order is immaterial and temporal smoothness is prioritized over precise geometric accuracy during high-speed motion [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced by Mueller et al. in "Splatting Errors and Antialiasing" (1998) as an extension of splatting techniques to handle motion blur in volume rendering [S1, unofficial]. It builds on earlier work by Max and Crawfis regarding splatting and rotation [S1, unofficial]. The technique addresses the lack of motion blur handling in previous volume rendering systems, which often suffered from temporal aliasing artifacts [S1, unofficial].
