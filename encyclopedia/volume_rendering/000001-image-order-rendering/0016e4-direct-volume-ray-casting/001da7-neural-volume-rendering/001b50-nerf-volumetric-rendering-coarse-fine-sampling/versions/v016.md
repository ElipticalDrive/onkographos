# NeRF Volumetric Rendering (Coarse/Fine Sampling)

Also called: NeRF Coarse-to-Fine Rendering, NeRF Rendering Equation, NeRF Two-Stage Sampling, Standard NeRF, Vanilla NeRF, Volume Rendering (NeRF), Volumetric Rendering (NeRF)

## Overview
NeRF Volumetric Rendering (Coarse/Fine Sampling) is a differentiable volume rendering technique that synthesizes novel views of 3D scenes by querying a neural radiance field along camera rays and accumulating outputs using classical volume rendering [S3][S14, unofficial]. It solves the problem of insufficient sampling in high-frequency regions by employing a two-stage sampling strategy: a coarse network estimates the density distribution to guide a fine network that concentrates samples in high-opacity regions [S12, unofficial][S29]. This method sets itself apart from its siblings, such as [Iterative Ray Marching with Neural Volume Inference](1494-iterative-ray-marching-with-neural-volume-inference.md) which uses learned acceleration structures, and [DVGO Volume Rendering Forward Pass](2363-dvgo-volume-rendering-forward-pass.md) which uses hybrid voxel-MLP representations, by relying on a simple, differentiable stratified resampling of coarse alpha weights without auxiliary acceleration structures or complex data structures [S1, S12, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A batch of rays $\mathbf{r} = (\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d})$ where $\mathbf{o}$ is the origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the direction; near and far plane bounds $t_n, t_f$; number of coarse samples $N_c$ and fine samples $N_f$; boolean flags for `lindisp` (inverse depth sampling) and `perturb` (stratified jittering); and two neural networks: `coarse_model` and `fine_model` [S8][S12, unofficial][S18].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB color $\mathbf{C}_{out}$, accumulated opacity $\alpha_{out}$, depth $\mathbf{D}_{out}$, and optionally disparity maps [S1, S12, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Batch:** Tensor of shape $[R, 8]$ or $[R, 9]$ containing ray origins (3), directions (3), near/far bounds (2), and optionally viewing directions (3) if view-dependent color is used [S8][S12, unofficial].
*   **Sample Positions:** Tensor of shape $[R, N]$ containing depth values $t_i$ along the ray [S9, unofficial].
*   **3D Points:** Tensor of shape $[R, N, 3]$ containing world coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \mathbf{d}$ [S9, unofficial].
*   **Network Output:** Tensor of shape $[R, N, 4]$ containing raw density $\tilde{\sigma}$ (1 channel) and raw color $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ (3 channels) [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Construction:** For each pixel, construct the ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ using the camera pose and intrinsic parameters [S2, S5]. Extract ray origins $\mathbf{o}$, directions $\mathbf{d}$, and near/far bounds $t_n, t_f$ from the ray batch [S8].

2.  **Coarse Sampling:**
    *   Generate $N_c$ linearly spaced values $t_{vals} \in [0, 1]$ [S11, unofficial].
    *   Map to depth bounds:
        *   If `lindisp` is False: $z_{vals} = t_n (1 - t_{vals}) + t_f t_{vals}$ [S8].
        *   If `lindisp` is True: $z_{vals} = \left( \frac{1}{t_n} (1 - t_{vals}) + \frac{1}{t_f} t_{vals} \right)^{-1}$ [S11, unofficial].
    *   If `perturb` is True, apply stratified sampling: compute midpoints $mids = 0.5 (z_{vals}[:, 1:] + z_{vals}[:, :-1])$, define lower bounds $[z_{vals}[:, :1], mids]$ and upper bounds $[mids, z_{vals}[:, -1]]$, and jitter: $z_{vals} = lower + (upper - lower) \cdot \text{rand}()$ [S9, S12, unofficial].
    *   Compute 3D points: $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + z_{vals, i} \mathbf{d}$ [S9, unofficial].

3.  **Coarse Network Query:**
    *   Apply positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ to spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d}$ [S4, S22].
    *   Query `coarse_model` to obtain raw density $\tilde{\sigma}_c$ and raw color $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_c$ [S1, unofficial][S4].
    *   Apply activation functions:
        *   Density: $\sigma_c = \text{softplus}(\tilde{\sigma}_c)$ [S1, S10, unofficial].
        *   Color: $\mathbf{c}_c = \text{sigmoid}(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_c)$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Coarse Rendering:**
    *   Compute segment distances: $\delta_i = z_{vals, i+1} - z_{vals, i}$ for $i < N_c-1$, and $\delta_{N_c-1} = \infty$ (or a large value like $10^{10}$) to handle the last interval [S13, S34, unofficial].
    *   Compute alpha (opacity): $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_{c,i} \delta_i)$ [S9, S34, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance: $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_{c,j} \delta_j\right)$ [S5][S14, unofficial].
    *   Compute weights: $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ [S9, unofficial][S29].
    *   Compute coarse color: $\mathbf{C}_{coarse} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} w_i \mathbf{c}_{c,i}$ [S9, unofficial][S29].
    *   Compute coarse depth: $\mathbf{D}_{coarse} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} w_i z_{vals, i}$ [S5, S28].

5.  **Fine Sampling (Importance Resampling):**
    *   Use the coarse weights $w_i$ (excluding the first sample $i=0$ to avoid bias) as a probability distribution [S29][S38, unofficial].
    *   Draw $N_f$ samples from this distribution using stratified sampling to generate finer depth values $z_{vals}^{fine}$ [S1, unofficial][S29].
    *   **Crucial Implementation Detail:** Detach $z_{vals}^{fine}$ from the computation graph to prevent gradients from flowing back to the coarse network during the fine stage [S29].

6.  **Fine Network Query:**
    *   Compute 3D points for fine samples: $\mathbf{x}_i^{fine} = \mathbf{o} + z_{vals, i}^{fine} \mathbf{d}$ [S9, unofficial].
    *   Apply positional encoding to $\mathbf{x}^{fine}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ [S4, S22].
    *   Query `fine_model` to obtain raw density $\tilde{\sigma}_f$ and raw color $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_f$ [S12, unofficial][S18].
    *   Apply activations: $\sigma_f = \text{softplus}(\tilde{\sigma}_f)$, $\mathbf{c}_f = \text{sigmoid}(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_f)$ [S1, S10, unofficial].

7.  **Fine Rendering:**
    *   Compute distances $\delta_i^{fine}$ between fine samples [S8][S13, unofficial].
    *   Compute alpha: $\alpha_i^{fine} = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_{f,i} \delta_i^{fine})$ [S34, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance: $T_i^{fine} = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_{f,j} \delta_j^{fine}\right)$ [S14, unofficial].
    *   Compute weights: $w_i^{fine} = T_i^{fine} \alpha_i^{fine}$ [S29].
    *   Compute final color: $\mathbf{C}_{out} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} w_i^{fine} \mathbf{c}_{f,i}$ [S31].
    *   Compute final depth: $\mathbf{D}_{out} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} w_i^{fine} z_{vals, i}^{fine}$ [S5].

8.  **Loss Computation:**
    *   Compute photometric loss (MSE) between $\mathbf{C}_{coarse}$ and ground truth $\mathbf{C}_{gt}$, and between $\mathbf{C}_{out}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{gt}$ [S18][S24].
    *   Backpropagate through both stages to update `coarse_model` and `fine_model` parameters [S3][S14, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def positional_encoding(x, include_input=True, log_sampling=True):
    """Standard NeRF positional encoding."""
    if log_sampling:
        scale = 1.0
        freq_bands = 10
        num_bands = freq_bands * 2
    else:
        scale = 1.0
        num_bands = 10
        freq_bands = 5
    
    out = []
    if include_input:
        out.append(x)
    for i in range(num_bands):
        freq = 2**i * scale
        out.append(torch.sin(x * freq))
        out.append(torch.cos(x * freq))
    return torch.cat(out, dim=-1)

def raw2alpha(raw, dists, act_fn=F.softplus):
    """Convert raw density to alpha."""
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-act_fn(raw) * dists)
    return alpha

def sample_finer_z_values(weights, n_samples):
    """
    Draw n_samples from the distribution defined by weights using inverse CDF sampling.
    Computes the CDF of weights[:, 1:], draws uniform samples, and uses inverse transform sampling.
    """
    cdf = torch.cumsum(weights, dim=-1)
    cdf = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(cdf[..., :1]), cdf], dim=-1)
    cdf = cdf / cdf[..., -1:]
    u = torch.rand(weights.shape[0], n_samples, device=weights.device)
    indices = torch.searchsorted(cdf, u, side='right') - 1
    indices = torch.clamp(indices, 0, cdf.shape[-1] - 2)
    cdf_low = cdf.gather(1, indices)
    cdf_high = cdf.gather(1, indices + 1)
    bin_low = torch.arange(cdf.shape[-1] - 1, device=weights.device).float().unsqueeze(0)
    bin_high = bin_low + 1
    t = (u - cdf_low) / (cdf_high - cdf_low + 1e-8)
    z_vals = bin_low + t
    return z_vals

def render(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, n_samples_coarse, n_samples_fine, 
           coarse_model, fine_model, lindisp=False, perturb=1.0, chunk=1024*32):
    """
    Render a batch of rays using coarse and fine sampling.
    rays_o: [R, 3]
    rays_d: [R, 3] (unit vectors)
    near: [R]
    far: [R]
    """
    device = rays_o.device
    R = rays_o.shape[0]
    
    # --- Coarse Stage ---
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., n_samples_coarse, device=device)
    if not lindisp:
        z_vals = near.unsqueeze(-1) * (1. - t_vals) + far.unsqueeze(-1) * t_vals
    else:
        z_vals = 1. / (1. / near.unsqueeze(-1) * (1. - t_vals) + 1. / far.unsqueeze(-1) * t_vals)
        
    if perturb > 0.:
        mids = .5 * (z_vals[..., 1:] + z_vals[..., :-1])
        upper = torch.cat([mids, z_vals[..., -1:]], -1)
        lower = torch.cat([z_vals[..., :1], mids], -1)
        t_rand = torch.rand(z_vals.shape, device=device)
        z_vals = lower + (upper - lower) * t_rand
        
    pts = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * z_vals.unsqueeze(-1)
    viewdirs = rays_d.unsqueeze(1).expand(pts.shape)
    
    pts_enc = positional_encoding(pts.reshape(-1, 3))
    viewdirs_enc = positional_encoding(viewdirs.reshape(-1, 3))
    
    raw_coarse = batchify(coarse_model, pts_enc, viewdirs_enc, chunk)
    raw_coarse = raw_coarse.reshape(R, n_samples_coarse, 4)
    
    sigma_coarse = raw_coarse[..., 3]
    rgb_coarse = raw_coarse[..., :3]
    
    dists = z_vals[..., 1:] - z_vals[..., :-1]
    dists = torch.cat([dists, torch.tensor([1e10], device=device).expand(dists[..., :1].shape)], -1)
    
    alpha_coarse = raw2alpha(sigma_coarse, dists)
    T_coarse = torch.cumprod(1. - alpha_coarse + 1e-10, dim=-1)
    T_coarse = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T_coarse[..., :1]), T_coarse[..., :-1]], -1)
    
    weights_coarse = T_coarse * alpha_coarse
    rgb_coarse_final = (weights_coarse.unsqueeze(-1) * rgb_coarse).sum(dim=-2)
    
    # --- Fine Stage ---
    weights_coarse_detached = weights_coarse.detach()
    z_vals_fine = sample_finer_z_values(weights_coarse_detached[:, 1:], n_samples_fine)
    
    pts_fine = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * z_vals_fine.unsqueeze(-1)
    viewdirs_fine = rays_d.unsqueeze(1).expand(pts_fine.shape)
    
    pts_fine_enc = positional_encoding(pts_fine.reshape(-1, 3))
    viewdirs_fine_enc = positional_encoding(viewdirs_fine.reshape(-1, 3))
    
    raw_fine = batchify(fine_model, pts_fine_enc, viewdirs_fine_enc, chunk)
    raw_fine = raw_fine.reshape(R, n_samples_fine, 4)
    
    sigma_fine = raw_fine[..., 3]
    rgb_fine = raw_fine[..., :3]
    
    dists_fine = z_vals_fine[..., 1:] - z_vals_fine[..., :-1]
    dists_fine = torch.cat([dists_fine, torch.tensor([1e10], device=device).expand(dists_fine[..., :1].shape)], -1)
    
    alpha_fine = raw2alpha(sigma_fine, dists_fine)
    T_fine = torch.cumprod(1. - alpha_fine + 1e-10, dim=-1)
    T_fine = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T_fine[..., :1]), T_fine[..., :-1]], -1)
    
    weights_fine = T_fine * alpha_fine
    rgb_fine_final = (weights_fine.unsqueeze(-1) * rgb_fine).sum(dim=-2)
    
    return rgb_coarse_final, rgb_fine_final

def batchify(model, pts_enc, viewdirs_enc, chunk):
    """Helper to process points in chunks."""
    N = pts_enc.shape[0]
    all_raw = []
    for i in range(0, N, chunk):
        chunk_pts = pts_enc[i:i+chunk]
        chunk_views = viewdirs_enc[i:i+chunk]
        raw = model(chunk_pts, chunk_views)
        all_raw.append(raw)
    return torch.cat(all_raw, dim=0)
```

**Parameters:**
*   $N_c$ (coarse samples): Typically 64 [S34, unofficial].
*   $N_f$ (fine samples): Typically 128 [S34, unofficial].
*   `lindisp`: Boolean, typically False for standard NeRF, True for scenes with large depth ranges [S8].
*   `perturb`: Float, typically 1.0 for training to break symmetry, 0.0 for inference [S9, unofficial].
*   `chunk`: Integer, typically $1024 \times 32$ or similar, to manage GPU memory during MLP evaluation [S16].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The cumulative product for transmittance can underflow to zero, causing all subsequent weights to be zero. Add a small epsilon ($\epsilon = 10^{-10}$) to $(1-\alpha)$ [S33, unofficial][S17].
*   **Boundaries:** The last interval $\delta_{N-1}$ must be handled carefully. Setting it to $\infty$ ensures the last sample contributes if it has non-zero density, effectively treating the ray as extending to infinity [S13, S34, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Flow:** Gradients must not flow from the fine stage back to the coarse model. Detach the fine sample positions $z_{vals}^{fine}$ from the computation graph before querying the fine model [S29].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If near and far bounds are equal or invalid, the sampling range collapses. Ensure $t_n < t_f$ [S8].

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Importance Sampling](1565-nerf-importance-sampling.md)
NeRF Importance Sampling is a two-stage rendering strategy that refines sample distribution by drawing additional points from a probability density function derived from coarse-stage opacity weights. It sets itself apart from siblings like [NerfAcc Rendering Pipeline](1619-nerfacc-rendering-pipeline.md) by relying on simple stratified resampling of alpha weights rather than learned acceleration structures or occupancy grids. Choose it when adaptive sampling improves rendering quality without adding architectural complexity.

### [NerfAcc Rendering Pipeline](1619-nerfacc-rendering-pipeline.md)
NerfAcc is a standalone PyTorch library for efficient volumetric rendering sampling, decoupled from the radiance field representation. It sets itself apart by supporting Occupancy Grids and Proposal Networks to skip empty space and concentrate samples on surfaces. Choose it when training or inference speed is critical and you need a modular acceleration backend.

### [VF-NeRF](3138-vf-nerf.md)
VF-NeRF is a method for rigid registration between two NeRFs when original camera positions are unknown. It sets itself apart by introducing Viewshed Fields to generate informative virtual viewpoints via Normalizing Flows, rather than optimizing for novel view synthesis or training efficiency. Choose it for inter-NeRF alignment and pose estimation tasks.

### [Voxel-based Volume Rendering](3845-voxel-based-volume-rendering.md)
Voxel-based Volume Rendering is a technique that trains a dense or semi-dense voxel representation of a radiance field, where multiple voxels contribute to a single sample. It sets itself apart from sparse or grid-based siblings by using a fixed volumetric grid to approximate the volume integral, providing structural consistency independent of view direction. Choose it when structured spatial priors are needed for opacity and color estimation.

### [Deformable Volume Rendering](16922-deformable-volume-rendering.md)
Deformable Volume Rendering is an extension of standard NeRF rendering that handles dynamic scenes by incorporating time-dependent deformations into the radiance field. It sets itself apart from static rendering methods by modeling non-rigid motion over time. Choose it for novel view synthesis of biological tissues or other non-rigidly moving objects.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources. [G]
*   **Measured Performance:** Training can take over 10 hours on a single GPU [S7].
*   **Scaling:** Performance degrades with increasing scene complexity and resolution due to the need for more samples $N_c, N_f$ to capture high-frequency details [S34, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality novel view synthesis with view-dependent effects; simple implementation; differentiable end-to-end training [S3][S14, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow training [S7].
*   **When to Use:** High-quality offline rendering of static scenes with known camera poses. [G]
*   **When Not to Use:** Real-time applications, dynamic scenes (without extensions), or scenes with unknown camera poses. [G]

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S3][S11, unofficial]. It represents a scene as a continuous volumetric radiance field parameterized by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) [S4, S5]. The method was developed to address the limitations of previous neural rendering approaches, which often struggled with high-frequency details and view-dependent appearance [S3]. The coarse-to-fine sampling strategy was introduced to improve rendering quality by adaptively sampling points in high-density regions [S12, unofficial][S29]. NeRF has become a foundational technique in neural rendering, inspiring numerous variants and acceleration methods [S7][S38, unofficial].
