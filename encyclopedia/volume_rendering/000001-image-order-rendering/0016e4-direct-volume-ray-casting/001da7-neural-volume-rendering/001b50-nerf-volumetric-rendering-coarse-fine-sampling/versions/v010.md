# NeRF Volumetric Rendering (Coarse/Fine Sampling)

Also called: Vanilla NeRF, Standard NeRF, NeRF Two-Stage Sampling, NeRF Coarse-to-Fine Rendering, NeRF Rendering Equation, Volume Rendering (NeRF), Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) Volumetric Rendering, Neural Radiance Field Rendering, coarse-fine volumetric rendering

## Overview
NeRF Volumetric Rendering (Coarse/Fine Sampling) is a differentiable rendering method that synthesizes novel views of 3D scenes by querying a neural radiance field along camera rays and integrating the outputs using classical volume rendering techniques [S3][S14, unofficial]. It solves the problem of insufficient sampling in high-opacity regions, which leads to aliasing and noise, by employing a two-stage sampling strategy: a coarse network samples uniformly (or with stratified sampling) to estimate the density distribution, and a fine network samples more densely around high-density regions identified by the coarse stage to produce a refined estimate [S14, S23, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its siblings, such as [NeRF Importance Sampling](1565-nerf-importance-sampling.md) which refines the distribution via importance sampling, and [NerfAcc Rendering Pipeline](1619-nerfacc-rendering-pipeline.md) which uses learned acceleration structures, by relying on a simple, differentiable two-stage MLP query with uniform/stratified coarse sampling and dense fine sampling without auxiliary acceleration structures [S1, S14, S23, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** A set of training images with known camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics) [S3][S8, unofficial]. For rendering, the input is a camera pose and the trained neural networks (coarse and fine) [S8, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Rendered RGB image, depth map, and accumulation (opacity) map [S6, S10, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray Batch:** A tensor of shape `[R, ...]` containing ray origins `rays_o` `[R, 3]`, directions `rays_d` `[R, 3]`, and near/far bounds `near`, `far` `[R]` or scalars [S6, S9, unofficial].
*   **Neural Network (MLP):** An implicit function $f_\theta$ mapping 5D coordinates (3D position $\mathbf{x}$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$) to raw density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ [S4, S29]. The input is typically processed with positional encoding $\gamma(\mathbf{x})$ [S2, S29].
*   **Sample Points:** A tensor of shape `[R, N]` containing the depth values $t_i$ (or $z\_vals$) along the ray [S7, S9, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Ray Generation:** For each pixel, define the ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$, where $\mathbf{o}$ is the camera origin and $\mathbf{d}$ is the unit direction vector [S2, S15]. Determine the near ($t_n$) and far ($t_f$) bounds of the volume intersection [S6, S8, unofficial].
2.  **Coarse Sampling:** Generate $N_{coarse}$ sample depths $t_i$ linearly spaced between $t_n$ and $t_f$ [S7, S8, unofficial]. If `perturb` > 0, apply stratified sampling: compute midpoints $m_i = 0.5(t_{i+1} + t_i)$, define intervals $[t_i, m_i]$ and $[m_i, t_{i+1}]$, and jitter $t_i$ uniformly within these intervals [S7, S9, unofficial].
3.  **Coarse Network Query:** Compute 3D positions $\mathbf{p}_i = r(t_i)$ [S7, unofficial]. Query the coarse MLP $f_{\theta_{coarse}}$ with $\mathbf{p}_i$ and viewing direction $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain raw density $\sigma_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ [S1, S14, unofficial].
4.  **Coarse Transformation:**
    *   Apply sigmoid to color: $\mathbf{c}_i = \text{sigmoid}(\mathbf{c}_i)$ [S1, S10, unofficial].
    *   Apply softplus or ReLU to density: $\sigma_i = \text{softplus}(\sigma_i)$ or $\text{ReLU}(\sigma_i)$ [S1, S10, unofficial].
    *   Compute segment distances $\delta_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$ for $i=1 \dots N-1$, and append a large value (e.g., $10^{10}$) for the last segment to ensure termination [S10, unofficial][S12].
    *   Compute opacity $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S7, S25, unofficial].
    *   Compute transmittance $T_i = \exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j)$ with $T_1 = 1$ [S13, unofficial][S29].
    *   Compute weights $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ [S7, S23, unofficial].
    *   Compute coarse color $\hat{C}_{coarse} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{coarse}} w_i \mathbf{c}_i$ [S1, S25, unofficial].
5.  **Fine Sampling:**
    *   If $N_{fine} > 0$, generate $N_{fine}$ additional sample depths. These are typically drawn from the probability distribution defined by the coarse weights $w_i$ (excluding the first sample) using stratified sampling [S23, S28, unofficial].
    *   Concatenate coarse and fine sample depths, sort them, and remove duplicates or interleave them [S23, unofficial].
    *   Query the fine MLP $f_{\theta_{fine}}$ with the new sample points and $\mathbf{d}$ to obtain $\sigma_i, \mathbf{c}_i$ [S14, unofficial]. Note: Gradients from the fine stage do not flow back to the coarse network [S23, unofficial].
6.  **Fine Transformation:** Repeat Step 4 using the fine samples to compute $\hat{C}_{fine}$ [S14, unofficial].
7.  **Loss Computation:** Compute the photometric loss (e.g., MSE) between the rendered color ($\hat{C}_{coarse}$ or $\hat{C}_{fine}$) and the ground truth image color [S14, unofficial][S19].
8.  **Rendering (Inference):** For novel view synthesis, repeat steps 2-6 (usually just fine sampling if trained) and composite the final color. If `white_background` is True, composite over white: $C_{final} = \hat{C} + (1 - \sum w_i) \times 1.0$ [S7, S8, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Ray:** $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ [S2]
*   **Opacity:** $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ [S7, unofficial]
*   **Transmittance:** $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ [S13, unofficial]
*   **Weight:** $w_i = T_i \alpha_i$ [S7, unofficial]
*   **Color:** $\hat{C} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \mathbf{c}_i$ [S1, unofficial]
*   **Depth:** $\hat{D} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i t_i$ [S22]

**Parameters:**
*   `n_samples_coarse` ($N_{coarse}$): Number of coarse samples. Default: 64 [S7, S10, unofficial].
*   `n_samples_fine` ($N_{fine}$): Number of fine samples. Default: 0 (if not using two-stage) or 48-192 in original paper [S9, S24, unofficial].
*   `perturb`: Float controlling jitter. 0.0 for no jitter, 1.0 for full jitter. Default: 1.0 [S9, S24, unofficial].
*   `raw_noise_std`: Standard deviation of noise added to raw density for regularization. Default: 1.0 [S10, unofficial].
*   `near`, `far`: Near and far plane distances. For NDC scenes, often 0 and 1 [S27, unofficial].
*   `lindisp`: Boolean. If True, sample in inverse depth space. Default: False [S6, unofficial].
*   `white_background`: Boolean. If True, composite over white. Default: False [S12].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Numerical Stability:** Add a small constant $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ to the exponent in transmittance calculation to prevent underflow/overflow [S13, S24, unofficial].
*   **Last Segment:** The last segment distance $\delta_N$ must be large (e.g., $10^{10}$) to ensure $T_{N+1} \approx 0$, effectively terminating the ray [S10, unofficial][S12].
*   **Gradient Flow:** In two-stage training, detach fine samples from the computation graph to prevent gradients from flowing back to the coarse network [S23, unofficial].
*   **View Direction:** If view-dependent effects are not needed, omit $\mathbf{d}$ from the MLP input [S1, S24, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def raw2alpha(raw, dists, act_fn=F.relu):
    """Convert raw density to opacity."""
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-act_fn(raw) * dists)
    return alpha

def render_rays(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, n_samples_coarse, n_samples_fine, 
                perturb, coarse_model, fine_model, use_viewdirs, white_background):
    """
    Render a batch of rays using coarse/fine sampling.
    rays_o: [R, 3]
    rays_d: [R, 3]
    near: [R] or scalar
    far: [R] or scalar
    """
    R = rays_o.shape[0]
    
    # 1. Generate coarse samples
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., n_samples_coarse + 1, device=rays_o.device)
    if perturb > 0:
        mids = .5 * (t_vals[..., 1:] + t_vals[..., :-1])
        upper = torch.cat([mids, t_vals[..., -1:]], -1)
        lower = torch.cat([t_vals[..., :1], mids], -1)
        t_rand = torch.rand_like(t_vals)
        t_vals = lower + (upper - lower) * t_rand
    else:
        t_vals = torch.cat([t_vals[:-1], t_vals[-1:]], -1) # Keep N samples
        
    # Map to near/far
    z_vals = near * (1.0 - t_vals) + far * t_vals
    
    # 2. Query Coarse Network
    pts = rays_o[:, None, :] + rays_d[:, None, :] * z_vals[..., None] # [R, N_coarse, 3]
    pts_flat = pts.reshape(-1, 3)
    
    if use_viewdirs:
        dirs = rays_d[:, None, :].expand(pts.shape) # [R, N_coarse, 3]
        dirs_flat = dirs.reshape(-1, 3)
        raw_coarse = coarse_model(pts_flat, dirs_flat)
    else:
        raw_coarse = coarse_model(pts_flat)
        
    raw_coarse = raw_coarse.reshape(R, n_samples_coarse, 4)
    rgb_coarse = raw_coarse[..., :3]
    sigma_coarse = raw_coarse[..., 3]
    
    # 3. Transform Coarse
    dists = z_vals[..., 1:] - z_vals[..., :-1]
    dists = torch.cat([dists, torch.tensor([1e10], device=rays_o.device).expand(dists[..., :1].shape)], -1)
    dists = dists * torch.norm(rays_d[:, None, :], dim=-1)
    
    alpha_coarse = raw2alpha(sigma_coarse, dists)
    T_coarse = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha_coarse + 1e-10, dim=-1)
    T_coarse = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T_coarse[..., :1]), T_coarse[..., :-1]], -1)
    weights_coarse = T_coarse * alpha_coarse
    
    rgb_coarse = torch.sigmoid(rgb_coarse)
    rgb_rendered_coarse = torch.sum(weights_coarse[..., None] * rgb_coarse, dim=-2)
    
    # 4. Fine Sampling (if n_samples_fine > 0)
    if n_samples_fine > 0:
        # Sample from coarse weights
        weights_coarse_no_first = weights_coarse[:, 1:]
        pdf_coarse = weights_coarse_no_first / (weights_coarse_no_first.sum(-1, keepdim=True) + 1e-10)
        # Stratified sampling from PDF
        # Note: Implementation of stratified sampling from discrete PDF is complex.
        # Simplified: Use inverse CDF sampling or rely on library.
        # Here we assume a helper `sample_from_pdf` exists or use torch.multinomial with stratification.
        # For completeness, we show the logic:
        cdf_coarse = torch.cumsum(weights_coarse_no_first, dim=-1)
        u = torch.rand(R, n_samples_fine, device=rays_o.device)
        indices = torch.searchsorted(cdf_coarse, u)
        # Interpolate or pick nearest
        z_fine = torch.gather(z_vals[:, 1:], -1, indices)
        
        z_all = torch.cat([z_vals, z_fine], dim=-1)
        z_all, _ = torch.sort(z_all, dim=-1)
        
        # Query Fine Network (detach from coarse)
        pts_fine = rays_o[:, None, :] + rays_d[:, None, :] * z_all[..., None]
        pts_fine_flat = pts_fine.reshape(-1, 3)
        
        if use_viewdirs:
            dirs_fine = rays_d[:, None, :].expand(pts_fine.shape)
            dirs_fine_flat = dirs_fine.reshape(-1, 3)
            raw_fine = fine_model(pts_fine_flat, dirs_fine_flat)
        else:
            raw_fine = fine_model(pts_fine_flat)
            
        raw_fine = raw_fine.reshape(R, z_all.shape[1], 4)
        rgb_fine = raw_fine[..., :3]
        sigma_fine = raw_fine[..., 3]
        
        # Transform Fine
        dists_fine = z_all[..., 1:] - z_all[..., :-1]
        dists_fine = torch.cat([dists_fine, torch.tensor([1e10], device=rays_o.device).expand(dists_fine[..., :1].shape)], -1)
        dists_fine = dists_fine * torch.norm(rays_d[:, None, :], dim=-1)
        
        alpha_fine = raw2alpha(sigma_fine, dists_fine)
        T_fine = torch.cumprod(1.0 - alpha_fine + 1e-10, dim=-1)
        T_fine = torch.cat([torch.ones_like(T_fine[..., :1]), T_fine[..., :-1]], -1)
        weights_fine = T_fine * alpha_fine
        
        rgb_fine = torch.sigmoid(rgb_fine)
        rgb_rendered_fine = torch.sum(weights_fine[..., None] * rgb_fine, dim=-2)
        
        return rgb_rendered_coarse, rgb_rendered_fine
    else:
        return rgb_rendered_coarse, None

```

## Subtopics

### [NeRF Importance Sampling](1565-nerf-importance-sampling.md)
A two-stage strategy that refines the discrete sampling distribution along a ray by drawing additional points from a probability density function (PDF) derived from the coarse stage's opacity weights. It sets itself apart by relying on importance sampling rather than uniform/stratified fine sampling.

### [NerfAcc Rendering Pipeline](1619-nerfacc-rendering-pipeline.md)
A modular, high-performance volumetric rendering method that decouples sampling from the radiance field, supporting Occupancy Grids and Proposal Networks. It sets itself apart by providing a unified API for efficient transmittance estimation and differentiable rendering with acceleration structures.

### [VF-NeRF](3138-vf-nerf.md)
A method for rigid registration between two NeRFs using Viewshed Fields. It sets itself apart by focusing on registration via novel view generation rather than training efficiency or rendering speed.

### [Voxel-based Volume Rendering](3845-voxel-based-volume-rendering.md)
A technique that trains a voxel representation of a radiance field. It sets itself apart by using explicit voxel structures rather than continuous MLP queries for density and color.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(R \cdot (N_{coarse} + N_{fine}))$ where $R$ is the number of rays and $N$ is the number of samples per ray [S7, S14, unofficial]. The MLP query is the dominant cost.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(R \cdot N \cdot D)$ where $D$ is the MLP hidden dimension, for storing activations during backpropagation [S14, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** Training typically takes 10-20 hours on a single GPU for 200k iterations [S8, unofficial]. Rendering a single image takes seconds to minutes depending on resolution and $N$ [S8, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High-quality novel view synthesis, differentiable, simple implementation [S3][S8, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow training and inference due to dense sampling and MLP queries, prone to noise/aliasing with uniform sampling [S1, S23, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When high visual fidelity is required and computational resources are available. When view-dependent effects are important.
*   **When not to use:** When real-time performance is required (use [NerfAcc](1619-nerfacc-rendering-pipeline.md) or [DVGO](2363-dvgo-volume-rendering-forward-pass.md)). When training data is extremely sparse (use [DS-NeRF](5) or other data-efficient variants).

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S3]. It represents a scene as a continuous volumetric radiance field parameterized by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) [S4]. The method was published in ECCV 2020 [S3]. It builds on [Neural volume rendering](1576-neural-volume-rendering.md) by introducing the specific coarse/fine sampling strategy and positional encoding to enable high-fidelity rendering [S14, unofficial][S29].
