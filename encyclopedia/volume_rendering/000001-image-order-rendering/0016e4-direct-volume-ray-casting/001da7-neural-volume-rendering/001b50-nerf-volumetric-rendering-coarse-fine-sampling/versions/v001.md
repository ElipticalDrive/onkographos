# NeRF Volumetric Rendering (Coarse/Fine Sampling)

Also called: Coarse and Fine Sampling, Differentiable Volume Rendering for View Synthesis, Differentiable volume rendering for NeRF, Flat ray-batch volume rendering, Hierarchical Volume Sampling, NeRF Coarse-to-Fine Rendering, NeRF Fine Sampling and Rendering, NeRF Two-Stage Sampling, NeRF Volume Rendering, Standard NeRF Rendering, Two-Stage Sampling, Volume Rendering (NeRF), VolumeRenderer

## Overview
NeRF Volumetric Rendering synthesizes novel views by querying a neural radiance field (a coordinate MLP) along camera rays and accumulating outputs using differentiable volume rendering [S1][S2, S3, unofficial]. It solves the view synthesis problem by optimizing a continuous 5D function (spatial location and viewing direction) to predict volume density and view-dependent color [S1]. Unlike single-stage uniform sampling, this method employs a two-stage hierarchical sampling strategy: a coarse network estimates density to guide finer sampling in high-density regions, reducing aliasing and improving geometric detail [S5, unofficial]. It operates on a flat batch of rays rather than a spatial grid, enabling efficient training on randomly sampled rays [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `rays_o`: Ray origins, tensor of shape $[R, 3]$ [S3, unofficial].
- `rays_d`: Unnormalized ray directions (Blender convention: camera looks down -z), tensor of shape $[R, 3]$ [S3, unofficial].
- `near`, `far`: Near and far clipping plane distances along the ray, scalars or tensors of shape $[R, 1]$ [S2, S5, unofficial].
- `coarse_model`, `fine_model`: Two neural networks with identical architecture but independent weights [S5, unofficial].
- `n_samples`: Number of coarse samples per ray (default 64) [S3, unofficial].
- `fine_sample_size`: Number of fine samples to add (default 128) [S5, unofficial].
- `perturb`: Boolean flag to enable stratified sampling jittering (default True) [S3, unofficial].
- `white_background`: Boolean flag to composite over white background (default True for Blender dataset) [S3, S5, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `rgb`: Rendered color, shape $[R, 3]$ [S3, unofficial].
- `depth`: Weighted average depth, shape $[R]$ [S3, unofficial].
- `acc`: Total accumulated opacity, shape $[R]$ [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- The neural field maps $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi)$ to color $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$. Inputs are positional encoded [S5, unofficial].
- Samples are stored as $z$-values along the ray parameter $t$.

**Steps:**

1.  **Ray Initialization and Coarse Sampling:**
    Generate linearly spaced depths $t_{vals} \in [0, 1]$ of size $n_{samples}$. Map to world space near/far bounds:
    $$ z_{vals} = \text{near} \cdot (1 - t_{vals}) + \text{far} \cdot t_{vals} $$
    Shape: $[R, n_{samples}]$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
    If `perturb` is True, apply stratified sampling:
    - Compute midpoints: $mids = 0.5 \cdot (z_{vals}[:, 1:] + z_{vals}[:, :-1])$ [S3, unofficial].
    - Define intervals: $lower = [z_{vals}[:, 0], mids]$, $upper = [mids, z_{vals}[:, -1]]$ [S3, unofficial].
    - Jitter: $z_{vals} = lower + (upper - lower) \cdot \text{rand}(R, n_{samples})$ [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Coarse Network Query:**
    Compute 3D points: $\mathbf{pts} = \text{rays\_o} + \text{rays\_d} \cdot z_{vals}$ [S2, S3, unofficial].
    Flatten points to $[-1, 3]$ and query `coarse_model` to get raw RGB $\mathbf{c}$ and density $\sigma$ [S3, S5, unofficial].
    Reshape outputs to $[R, n_{samples}, 3]$ and $[R, n_{samples}]$ [S5, unofficial].

3.  **Coarse Rendering (Alpha Compositing):**
    Compute segment distances $\delta$:
    $$ \delta_i = (z_{vals, i+1} - z_{vals, i}) \cdot \|\text{rays\_d}\| $$
    Append a large value ($10^{10}$) for the last segment to ensure termination [S2, unofficial][S4].
    Compute alpha using softplus activation on density (or raw density if not specified, but S2 uses softplus):
    $$ \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\text{softplus}(\sigma_i) \cdot \delta_i) $$
    [S2, unofficial]. Add epsilon ($10^{-10}$) to $(1-\alpha)$ before cumulative product for stability [S3, unofficial].
    Compute transmittance $T$ (exclusive cumulative product):
    $$ T_i = \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_j + \epsilon) $$
    [S3, unofficial]. Compute weights:
    $$ w_i = T_i \cdot \alpha_i $$
    [S3, S5, unofficial].
    Render coarse maps:
    $$ C_{coarse} = \sum w_i \mathbf{c}_i, \quad D_{coarse} = \sum w_i z_{i}, \quad A_{coarse} = \sum w_i $$
    [S5, unofficial].

4.  **Fine Sampling (Hierarchical):**
    Use coarse weights to guide finer sampling. Exclude the first weight (index 0) as it corresponds to the start of the ray [S5, unofficial].
    - Add epsilon ($10^{-5}$) to weights and normalize to form a probability distribution [S5, unofficial].
    - Compute CDF from weights, prepend zero [S5, unofficial].
    - Sample $fine\_sample\_size$ uniform random values $u \in [0, 1]$ [S5, unofficial].
    - Use inverse CDF sampling (searchsorted) to find bin indices and interpolate $z$-values within bins to generate $z_{fine}$ [S5, unofficial].
    - Detach $z_{fine}$ from the computation graph to prevent gradient flow to the coarse network [S5, unofficial].

5.  **Interleaving and Fine Network Query:**
    Concatenate coarse $z_{vals}$ and fine $z_{fine}$, then sort them to interleave samples [S5, unofficial].
    Compute new 3D points $\mathbf{pts}_{all}$ from sorted $z$-values [S5, unofficial].
    Flatten and query `fine_model` to get $\mathbf{c}_{fine}$ and $\sigma_{fine}$ [S5, unofficial].

6.  **Fine Rendering:**
    Recompute distances $\delta$ and alphas $\alpha$ for the sorted samples using the fine network outputs [S5, unofficial].
    Compute new weights $w_i$ and transmittance $T_i$ [S5, unofficial].
    Render final maps:
    $$ C_{final} = \sum w_i \mathbf{c}_i $$
    [S5, unofficial].
    If `white_background` is True, add background contribution:
    $$ C_{final} = C_{final} + (1 - A_{final}) \cdot \mathbf{1} $$
    [S3, S5, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

def softplus(x):
    return nn.functional.softplus(x)

def render_rays(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, coarse_model, fine_model, 
                n_samples=64, fine_sample_size=128, perturb=True, 
                white_background=True, epsilon_alpha=1e-10, epsilon_weight=1e-5):
    """
    Renders rays using NeRF coarse-to-fine sampling.
    
    Args:
        rays_o: [R, 3] Ray origins
        rays_d: [R, 3] Ray directions (unnormalized)
        near: [R, 1] Near plane distances
        far: [R, 1] Far plane distances
        coarse_model: Neural network returning (color, density)
        fine_model: Neural network returning (color, density)
        n_samples: int Number of coarse samples
        fine_sample_size: int Number of fine samples
        perturb: bool Enable stratified sampling
        white_background: bool Composite over white
        epsilon_alpha: float Epsilon for alpha stability
        epsilon_weight: float Epsilon for weight normalization
        
    Returns:
        rgb: [R, 3]
        depth: [R]
        acc: [R]
    """
    R = rays_o.shape[0]
    
    # 1. Coarse Sampling
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n_samples, device=rays_o.device)
    z_vals = near * (1.0 - t_vals) + far * t_vals  # [R, n_samples]
    
    if perturb:
        mids = 0.5 * (z_vals[:, 1:] + z_vals[:, :-1])
        upper = torch.cat([mids, z_vals[:, -1:]], dim=-1)
        lower = torch.cat([z_vals[:, :1], mids], dim=-1)
        random_vals = torch.rand_like(z_vals)
        z_vals = lower + (upper - lower) * random_vals
        
    # 2. Coarse Network Query
    pts_coarse = rays_o[:, None, :] + rays_d[:, None, :] * z_vals[:, :, None]  # [R, n_samples, 3]
    pts_flat = pts_coarse.reshape(-1, 3)
    dirs_flat = rays_d[:, None, :].expand(pts_coarse.shape).reshape(-1, 3)
    
    # Flatten view directions for network input (assuming network takes direction)
    # Note: NeRF usually takes position and direction. 
    # Here we assume the model handles batching or we pass dirs_flat.
    # Simplified: assume model takes (points, directions)
    coarse_out = coarse_model(pts_flat, dirs_flat)
    c_coarse = coarse_out[0].reshape(R, n_samples, 3)
    sigma_coarse = coarse_out[1].reshape(R, n_samples)
    
    # 3. Coarse Rendering
    dists = z_vals[:, 1:] - z_vals[:, :-1]  # [R, n_samples-1]
    dists = torch.cat([dists, torch.full((R, 1), 1e10, device=rays_o.device)], dim=-1) # [R, n_samples]
    dists = dists * torch.norm(rays_d, dim=-1, keepdim=True)  # [R, n_samples]
    
    alpha_coarse = 1.0 - torch.exp(-softplus(sigma_coarse) * dists)
    # Transmittance: exclusive cumprod
    ones = torch.ones_like(alpha_coarse[:, :1])
    alpha_safe = alpha_coarse + epsilon_alpha
    T_coarse = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([ones, 1.0 - alpha_safe[:, :-1] + epsilon_alpha], dim=-1), dim=-1)[:, :-1]
    weights_coarse = T_coarse * alpha_coarse
    
    rgb_coarse = torch.sum(weights_coarse[:, :, None] * c_coarse, dim=1)
    acc_coarse = torch.sum(weights_coarse, dim=1)
    depth_coarse = torch.sum(weights_coarse * z_vals, dim=1)
    
    # 4. Fine Sampling
    # Use weights from index 1 to n_samples-1 (excluding first bin start)
    weights_fine = weights_coarse[:, 1:] + epsilon_weight
    weights_fine = weights_fine / (torch.sum(weights_fine, dim=-1, keepdim=True) + epsilon_weight)
    
    # CDF
    cdf = torch.cumsum(weights_fine, dim=-1)
    cdf = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(cdf[:, :1]), cdf], dim=-1)
    
    # Inverse CDF Sampling
    u = torch.rand(R, fine_sample_size, device=rays_o.device)
    indices = torch.searchsorted(cdf, u, right=True)
    indices = torch.clamp(indices, 0, cdf.shape[-1] - 2)
    
    below = indices
    above = indices + 1
    cdf_below = torch.gather(cdf, 1, below)
    cdf_above = torch.gather(cdf, 1, above)
    z_below = torch.gather(z_vals[:, 1:], 1, below) # Corresponding z bins
    z_above = torch.gather(z_vals[:, 1:], 1, above)
    
    # Linear interpolation
    denom = cdf_above - cdf_below + epsilon_weight
    t = (u - cdf_below) / denom
    z_fine = z_below + t * (z_above - z_below)
    
    # Detach to stop gradient flow to coarse model
    z_fine = z_fine.detach()
    
    # 5. Interleave and Fine Query
    z_all = torch.cat([z_vals, z_fine], dim=-1)
    z_all, indices_sort = torch.sort(z_all, dim=-1)
    
    pts_all = rays_o[:, None, :] + rays_d[:, None, :] * z_all[:, :, None]
    pts_flat_all = pts_all.reshape(-1, 3)
    dirs_flat_all = rays_d[:, None, :].expand(pts_all.shape).reshape(-1, 3)
    
    fine_out = fine_model(pts_flat_all, dirs_flat_all)
    c_fine = fine_out[0].reshape(R, z_all.shape[-1], 3)
    sigma_fine = fine_out[1].reshape(R, z_all.shape[-1])
    
    # 6. Fine Rendering
    dists_all = z_all[:, 1:] - z_all[:, :-1]
    dists_all = torch.cat([dists_all, torch.full((R, 1), 1e10, device=rays_o.device)], dim=-1)
    dists_all = dists_all * torch.norm(rays_d, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    
    alpha_fine = 1.0 - torch.exp(-softplus(sigma_fine) * dists_all)
    ones_all = torch.ones_like(alpha_fine[:, :1])
    alpha_safe_fine = alpha_fine + epsilon_alpha
    T_fine = torch.cumprod(torch.cat([ones_all, 1.0 - alpha_safe_fine[:, :-1] + epsilon_alpha], dim=-1), dim=-1)[:, :-1]
    weights_fine_final = T_fine * alpha_fine
    
    rgb_final = torch.sum(weights_fine_final[:, :, None] * c_fine, dim=1)
    acc_final = torch.sum(weights_fine_final, dim=1)
    depth_final = torch.sum(weights_fine_final * z_all, dim=1)
    
    if white_background:
        rgb_final = rgb_final + (1.0 - acc_final[:, None])
        
    return rgb_final, depth_final, acc_final
```

## Subtopics

### [Hierarchical Sampling (Inverse CDF Sampling)](973-hierarchical-sampling-inverse-cdf-sampling.md)
The specific sampling technique used in the fine stage to draw samples from the probability distribution defined by the coarse weights.

### [Uniform Ray Sampling](1261-uniform-ray-sampling.md)
The baseline sampling method used in the coarse stage before stratification, which NeRF improves upon by adding jitter and hierarchical refinement.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(R \cdot (N_{coarse} + N_{fine}))$ network queries per ray batch, where $R$ is the number of rays. The sorting step adds $O(R \cdot N \log N)$ [S5, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(R \cdot N_{fine})$ to store intermediate tensors for the fine pass [S5, unofficial].
- **Performance:** Significantly higher quality than single-stage uniform sampling, particularly for complex geometry and view-dependent effects, at the cost of roughly double the network queries per ray [S5, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Differentiable end-to-end, allowing optimization from images alone; handles view-dependent appearance via directional encoding; hierarchical sampling efficiently allocates samples to dense regions [S1][S5, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires many network queries (slow rendering without acceleration structures like KD-trees or voxel grids); sensitive to near/far bounds; prone to "floaters" (noise in empty space) if not regularized or if near/far are poorly estimated [S1][S5, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When high-fidelity novel view synthesis is required and training time is acceptable. Not suitable for real-time rendering without further acceleration (e.g., [3D Gaussian Splatting](791-distance-color-blending.md) or voxel-based acceleration).

## Background
NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) was introduced by Mildenhall et al. in 2020 [S1]. It represents a scene as a continuous 5D function mapping spatial coordinates and viewing directions to volume density and emitted color. The method is trained by minimizing the difference between rendered images and ground truth images using gradient descent, leveraging the differentiability of the volume rendering integral [S1]. The coarse-to-fine sampling strategy was introduced to address the inefficiency and aliasing artifacts of uniform sampling, allowing the model to focus computational resources on regions with high density [S5, unofficial].
