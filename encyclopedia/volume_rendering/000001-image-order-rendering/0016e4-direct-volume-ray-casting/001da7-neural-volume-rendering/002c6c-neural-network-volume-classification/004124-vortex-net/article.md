# Vortex-Net
## Overview
Vortex-Net is a method for identifying vortices in volumetric flow data by training a convolutional neural network (CNN) to replicate the classification boundary of the Instantaneous Vorticity Deviation (IVD) criterion [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent [Neural Network Volume Classification](5271-neural-network-volume-classification.md) by focusing on specific physical feature extraction rather than general opacity mapping, and from its sibling [Berenjkoub et al. vortex segmentation](10626-berenjkoub-et-al-vortex-segmentation.md) by relying on labels generated from an existing analytical criterion rather than synthetic ground truth [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Local 3D patches of velocity field data $\mathbf{u} = (u, v, w)$ extracted from the volumetric dataset [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A binary classification for each patch indicating whether it contains a vortex (1) or background (0) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Training Labels:** A binary volume where each voxel is labeled 1 if the IVD criterion identifies it as a vortex, and 0 otherwise [S1, unofficial].
*   **CNN Architecture:** A standard convolutional neural network [S1, unofficial]. Specific architecture details (layers, kernel sizes, activation functions) are not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1.  **Generate Ground Truth Labels:** For a training volume, compute the Instantaneous Vorticity Deviation (IVD) at every voxel and apply the IVD threshold to generate a binary label volume $L_{\text{IVD}}$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Extract Patches:** Extract local 3D velocity patches from the input volume corresponding to the voxel locations in $L_{\text{IVD}}$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Train CNN:** Train the CNN to minimize classification error between its output and $L_{\text{IVD}}$ [S1, unofficial]. The network learns to map local velocity features to the IVD-defined vortex boundary [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Inference:** For new data, extract velocity patches and pass them through the trained CNN to produce a vortex segmentation map [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **IVD Criterion:** The specific mathematical formula for Instantaneous Vorticity Deviation is not provided in the sources [S1, unofficial]. The method relies on replicating the classification boundary defined by this criterion [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Training Volumes:** 3D shockwave-vortex interaction volumes with resolutions $81 \times 81 \times 81$ and $161 \times 81 \times 81$ [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Inherited Bias:** Because training relies on IVD-generated labels, the learned classifier inherits IVD’s definition of vortex boundaries, which limits generalization if IVD's definition is flawed or domain-specific [S1, unofficial].
*   **Threshold Dependency:** While Vortex-Net automates the application of the criterion, it does not solve the threshold selection problem inherent to the IVD criterion itself, unlike methods using synthetic ground truth [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Berenjkoub et al. vortex segmentation](10626-berenjkoub-et-al-vortex-segmentation.md)
A method that trains convolutional neural networks on synthetic cylinder flows with analytically known vortex boundaries to identify vortices in volumetric data, eliminating the need for manual threshold tuning associated with traditional criteria like $\lambda_2$. It sets itself apart from its parent [Vortex-Net](10429-vortex-net.md) by replacing the replication of a specific criterion's boundary with direct supervised learning on ground-truth synthetic data, thereby removing the bottleneck of threshold selection entirely.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On 3D shockwave-vortex interaction volumes ($81 \times 81 \times 81$ and $161 \times 81 \times 81$), Vortex-Net achieved 98–99% precision and 99.5–99.8% recall [S1, unofficial].
*   **Speedup:** 7.4–8.0× speedup over direct IVD computation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** High accuracy in replicating IVD boundaries and significant computational speedup compared to direct criterion evaluation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Generalization is limited to the domain where IVD is valid, and the method inherits biases of the source criterion [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Automating vortex identification with learned classifiers was first explored by Deng et al. [21], who introduced Vortex-Net [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the bottleneck of manual threshold selection in traditional vortex criteria by learning the decision boundary directly from labeled examples generated by an existing criterion (IVD) [S1, unofficial].
