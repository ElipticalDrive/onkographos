# Projection and MIP Techniques

Also called: MIP (Maximum Intensity Projection)

## Overview
Projection and MIP Techniques are a family of volume rendering methods that generate 2D images by aggregating scalar values along viewing rays without using the standard alpha-blending optical model (emission/absorption) [G]. They differ from siblings like [Compositing and Blending](2515-compositing-and-blending.md) by discarding depth cues and opacity accumulation in favor of scalar extrema or integrals [G]. MIP was introduced as a standard technique in medical imaging visualization to overcome the limitations of alpha blending in highlighting high-contrast structures [G]. The technique is widely supported in medical imaging workstations and is a fundamental tool for angiography and bone visualization [G].

## Shared principles
This family builds on the compositing model defined in [Compositing and Output Generation](13463-compositing-and-output-generation.md).

## Subtopics

### [Maximum Intensity Projection](902-maximum-intensity-projection.md)
Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) is a volume rendering technique that projects the maximum scalar value encountered along a viewing ray onto the 2D image plane. Unlike standard alpha blending, MIP ignores intermediate values and opacity accumulation, making it order-independent. It is primarily used in medical imaging to highlight high-intensity structures such as bones in CT scans or contrast-enhanced blood vessels in MRI/CT angiography.

### [Projected Slabs Approximation](1449-projected-slabs-approximation.md)
Projected Slabs Approximation is a technique that approximates perspective volume rendering by rendering slabs using parallel volume rendering hardware and blending the slab images perspectively on the graphics card. It enables acceleration on hardware designed for parallel projection by introducing an analytical error bound.

### [Minimum Intensity Projection](5299-minimum-intensity-projection.md)
Minimum Intensity Projection (MinIP) is a volume rendering technique that projects the lowest intensity value encountered along each viewing ray to the two-dimensional image plane. Unlike standard front-to-back compositing, MinIP discards all but the single minimum sample, effectively highlighting low-density or dark structures such as air-filled spaces, bronchi, or hypodense ducts against brighter surrounding tissue. It serves as the inverse counterpart to Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP).

### [Perspective Approximation via Orthogonal Slices](5871-perspective-approximation-via-orthogonal-slices.md)
This method approximates perspective volume rendering by using view-orthogonal slices instead of the concentric spherical shells required for exact ray casting. It enables interactive frame rates on standard graphics hardware by leveraging optimized texture mapping, trading the geometric precision of spherical sampling for significant performance gains.

### [Projection](7205-projection.md)
Projection is a direct volume rendering technique that visualizes the total accumulated intensity of a 3D volume along a specific axis by summing voxel values. It compresses volumetric data into a 2D representation where pixel brightness corresponds to the line integral of the scalar field along the viewing ray. Unlike [Maximum Intensity Projection](902-maximum-intensity-projection.md), which highlights the highest density structures, projection sums all contributions along the ray, which decreases noise but significantly reduces image contrast due to cumulative background activity.

### [MPI Defocus Rendering](17207-mpi-defocus-rendering.md)
MPI Defocus Rendering synthesizes images with realistic depth-of-field blur and lateral camera translation by compositing a precomputed Multi-Plane Image (MPI) representation. It differs from standard ray casting by operating on discrete disparity planes rather than continuous volumetric samples, allowing for efficient simulation of defocus blur via 2D image-space convolutions. This method is specifically designed for view synthesis from trained neural radiance fields (NeRFs), particularly in low-light conditions where raw sensor data is utilized.

### ProjectionVolumeStyle
A non-composable render style. An example image shows Bronchial tubes rendered with maximum intensity projection (MIP) [S2, unofficial].

### Reverse-Video Rendering
A display mode where bright image pixels are mapped to dark display pixels, and vice versa, often combined with MIP to enhance visualization of dark objects [S3]. It is used to visualize a passive guiding catheter (signal void) embedded in bright surrounding tissue by suppressing the myocardium and blood via MIP through reverse-video [S3].

## Background
MIP was introduced as a standard technique in medical imaging visualization to overcome the limitations of alpha blending in highlighting high-contrast structures [G]. The term "MIP_Mode" is used in interactive visualization libraries to denote this specific rendering mode [S1, unofficial]. The technique is widely supported in medical imaging workstations and is a fundamental tool for angiography and bone visualization [G].
