# Compositing and Blending

Also called: Accumulate, Alternative Compositing Schemes, Blending Modes, First Hit, Front-to-back blending, Maximum Intensity blending, Minimum Intensity blending

## Overview
Compositing and Blending defines the mathematical and algorithmic rules for combining volumetric samples $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ along a ray into a final pixel color, solving the problem of optical integration in direct volume rendering. It sets itself apart from [Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md) by performing iterative accumulation rather than table lookups, and from [Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md) by operating on single-ray sample sequences rather than merging distributed sub-images.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a sequence of samples $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ encountered along a ray, where $c_i$ is the sample color (RGB) and $\alpha_i$ is the sample opacity (A) [G]. The core mechanism involves an accumulator state $(C_{acc}, \alpha_{acc})$ initialized to $(0, 0)$ (or background color/opacity) and updated iteratively for each sample [G].

The standard optical model assumes that light is emitted or scattered by the volume and attenuated by absorption [G]. The standard front-to-back compositing equation updates these accumulators for each sample $i$ from front to back:
$$
C_{acc}^{(i)} = C_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i C_i
$$
$$
\alpha_{acc}^{(i)} = \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i
$$
where $C_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ and $\alpha_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ [G]. The term $(1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)})$ represents the transmittance, i.e., the fraction of light reaching sample $i$ from the camera side [G].

In iterative form using transmittance $T = 1 - \alpha_{acc}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{final} &\leftarrow C_{final} + c_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot T \\
T &\leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_i)
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $T$ is initialized to $1.0$ and represents the accumulated transparency $\prod (1-\alpha_j)$ [G].

The final pixel color is often composited over a background color $C_{bg}$ using $C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}) C_{bg}$ [G].

Early ray termination is a common optimization where the loop breaks if $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., 1.0), as further samples contribute negligibly to the color [G].

```python
def compositing_loop(samples, background_color):
    """
    Standard front-to-back compositing loop shared by the family.
    samples: list of (color: vec3, opacity: float) sorted front-to-back.
    background_color: vec3 for the scene background.
    """
    C_acc = vec3(0.0)
    alpha_acc = 0.0
    
    for c_i, alpha_i in samples:
        transmittance = 1.0 - alpha_acc
        C_acc = C_acc + transmittance * alpha_i * c_i
        alpha_acc = alpha_acc + transmittance * alpha_i
        
        # Early ray termination
        if alpha_acc >= 1.0:
            break
            
    # Composite over background
    return C_acc + (1.0 - alpha_acc) * background_color
```

## Subtopics

### [Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md)
[Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md) replaces iterative accumulation with a single texture fetch from a pre-computed table, solving the performance bottleneck of evaluating the optical integration equation for every sample. It assumes linear data evolution within a slab, allowing the final color and opacity contribution to be determined solely by entry, exit, and middle densities. It sets itself apart from iterative compositing by collapsing multiple samples into a single lookup, and from simplified pre-integration by accounting for color attenuation within the slab.

### [Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md)
[Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md) is a parallel image merging algorithm that combines independently rendered sub-images from multiple processors into a final volume-rendered image. It solves the bottleneck of naive binary compositing, where processors become idle as the tree narrows, by ensuring every processor participates in every stage via a binary-swap tree structure. It sets itself apart from sibling methods by explicitly managing distributed memory communication and from Pre-Integration by operating on already-composited sub-images.

### [Screen-space Post-processing](3621-screen-space-post-processing.md)
[Screen-space Post-processing](3621-screen-space-post-processing.md) applies aspect-ratio preserving UV covering, luminance-based bright passes, color grading, and spatial effects (bloom, blur, vignetting) to a rendered buffer. It differs from ray-casting compositing by operating in screen space on the final buffers rather than during volumetric ray traversal, allowing for complex aesthetic effects without modifying the volume rendering pipeline.

### [Layered SDF Compositing](3641-layered-sdf-compositing.md)
[Layered SDF Compositing](3641-layered-sdf-compositing.md) renders Signed Distance Field (SDF) glyphs by compositing multiple layers (face and outlines) using alpha blending and pre-multiplied alpha handling. It differs from standard volume ray casting by operating on discrete, pre-sampled layers of a 2D texture atlas rather than continuous 3D volume data, allowing for efficient, sharp text rendering.

### [Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md)
[Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md) encompass the mathematical methods used to transform discrete volumetric data into continuous signals and combine them into final pixel colors. It is distinguished by its focus on the underlying signal processing (reconstruction) and the specific mathematical formulation of the accumulation, ranging from standard alpha blending to differentiable training passes.

### [Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md)
[Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md) modifies the standard front-to-back compositing equation to incorporate lighting effects, multi-channel illumination, geometric boundary enhancement, and shadow handling directly into the accumulation step. It sets itself apart from Pre-Integration and Binary Swap by specifically addressing the illumination model within the compositing loop.

### [Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework](6994-layer-centric-volume-rendering-framework.md)
[Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework](6994-layer-centric-volume-rendering-framework.md) decomposes volume visualization into rendering small Regions of Interest (ROIs) as separate layers and compositing them using a rich set of 2D operators. Unlike traditional single-pass ray casting, it treats the final image as a composition of independent renderings, allowing for intuitive 2D-based user interactions and complex material manipulations.

### [MAB Compositing with Gaze-Contingent Blending](8211-mab-compositing-with-gaze-contingent-blending.md)
[MAB Compositing with Gaze-Contingent Blending](8211-mab-compositing-with-gaze-contingent-blending.md) maps framebuffer fragments to a Multi-Anchor Buffer (MAB) to retrieve and blend color values from different resolution levels based on the distance to the user's gaze. It solves the problem of maintaining high frame rates in VR by dynamically adjusting rendering resolution, blending multiple levels simultaneously using smooth-step transitions to prevent artifacts.

### [Multi-view image blending](9831-multi-view-image-blending.md)
[Multi-view image blending](9831-multi-view-image-blending.md) reduces view-dependent artifacts like ghosting by combining multiple pre-rendered images from different camera angles. Unlike single-view texture mapping, it selects and blends at least three textures to approximate volumetric appearance, relying on spatial redundancy across views at the cost of increased memory bandwidth.

### [Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering](10585-dynamic-job-scheduling-for-grid-volume-rendering.md)
[Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering](10585-dynamic-job-scheduling-for-grid-volume-rendering.md) is a distributed rendering strategy that dynamically determines the processing order of sub-volumes based on visibility using obstacle-flags. It solves the inefficiency of static sequential scheduling by dispatching jobs as soon as they become combinable (obscure count 0), even if prior jobs are still being processed by other agents.

### [HardSoftRas Blending](10747-hardsoftras-blending.md)
[HardSoftRas Blending](10747-hardsoftras-blending.md) is a pixel-wise compositing strategy for differentiable rendering that distinguishes between edge pixels and non-edge pixels to balance anti-aliasing with texture sharpness. Unlike SoftRas, which applies weighted blending across all faces, HardSoftRas preserves the most frontal color for non-edge regions while using probabilistic blending for edges, maintaining sharp internal textures.

#### Frame Buffer Copy / Blit
A pass that simply copies the current frame to the output, acting as a simple integration or blit step for the LAO renderer [S3, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Sample frame buffer: `oColor = texture(uFrame, vPosition);` [S3, unofficial].

#### Virtual Dimension Expansion for Compositing
A post-processing step where partial images rendered from blocks of different resolutions are expanded to a common 'virtual dimension' (the highest resolution dimension of the block) before compositing [S4, unofficial]. This allows standard compositing algorithms to work despite varying input resolutions.
*   **Step:** Determine virtual dimensions for each block. Render the block at its assigned resolution (e.g., Medium: Hx/2, Hy/2, Hz/2). Expand the partial image to the virtual dimensions by interpolating or replicating pixels [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Virtual Dimension Selection: The virtual dimension can be set to the dimension of the largest subimage in the scene to minimize padding, or fixed to the global high-resolution block size [S4, unofficial].

#### Voronoi Painter's Algorithm Compositing
A rendering compositing strategy where the scene is rendered part-by-part (one Voronoi cell at a time) and the resulting cell images are composited back-to-front [S5]. It relies on the proof that Voronoi cells admit a partial ordering compatible with occlusion.
*   **Step:** 1. Define partial ordering on Voronoi cells based on distance from camera Q to the Voronoi sites P: $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ iff $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$. 2. Render each Voronoi cell independently. 3. Composite the rendered cell images in the order defined by $<_Q$ (back-to-front) [S5].
*   **Formula:** $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ if and only if $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$ [S5].
*   **Comparison:** Standard Painter's Algorithm fails on cyclic occlusion graphs. Voronoi Painter's is always compatible because the distance-to-camera ordering is a strict partial order [S5].

#### Hybrid Compositing (Radix-k)
Hybrid methods like Radix-k combine direct send and binary swap approaches depending on the problem setting, adaptively running one or the other based on criteria [S6, unofficial].

#### Fabric Compositing
A compositing pass that creates an initial fabric-like texture by combining advected noise fields, silhouette data, and lighting. Pixels not part of the geometry are discarded using the depth buffer [S7, book].
*   **Formula:** Color channels after $k$ iterations:
    $$R(x,y) = \frac{r \cdot f_{\lambda_2}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_1}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y})$$
    $$G(x,y) = \frac{(1-r) \cdot f_{\lambda_1}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_2}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y})$$
    $$B(x,y) = e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y})$$
    Here $f_{\lambda_1}$ and $f_{\lambda_2}$ are the advected fields $p_{\lambda_1}^k$ and $p_{\lambda_2}^k$. $e_{x,y}$ is the silhouette image. $r$ blends between tensor directions. Squaring the denominator emphasizes differences [S7, book].
*   **Parameter:** Blend factor $r$: Scalar factor used to blend between the two chosen tensor directions. Lighting function: A separate lighting function `light` manipulates intensity without affecting the base color. Blinn-Phong shading is mentioned as providing required depth cues [S7, book].

## Background
The concept of compositing in volume rendering originated with the standard front-to-back alpha blending equation, derived from the optical integration model [G]. The VolumePro system popularized support for alternative blending modes such as Minimum Intensity and Maximum Intensity blending alongside standard accumulation [S1, unofficial]. The KTH course materials further categorized these into Accumulate, First Hit, Averaging, and Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP), noting MIP's utility in extracting vessel structures in magnetic resonance angiograms [S2, unofficial]. Hybrid compositing strategies like Radix-k were later developed to combine direct send and binary swap approaches for scalability [S6, unofficial].
