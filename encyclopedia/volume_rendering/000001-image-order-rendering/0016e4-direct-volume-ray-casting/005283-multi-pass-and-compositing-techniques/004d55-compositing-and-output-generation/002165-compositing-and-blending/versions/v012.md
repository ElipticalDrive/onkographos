# Compositing and Blending

Also called: Accumulate, Alternative Compositing Schemes, Blending Modes, First Hit, Maximum Intensity blending, Minimum Intensity blending

## Overview
Compositing and Blending defines the final stage of direct volume ray casting where per-ray sample contributions (color and opacity) are integrated into a 2D pixel value [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of correctly blending semi-transparent, overlapping density fields to produce visually accurate renderings [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a sequence of samples $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ encountered along a ray, where $c_i$ is the sample color (RGB) and $\alpha_i$ is the sample opacity (A) [G]. The core mechanism involves an accumulator state $(C_{acc}, \alpha_{acc})$ initialized to $(0, 0)$ (or background color/opacity) and updated iteratively for each sample [G].

The standard optical model assumes that light is emitted or scattered by the volume and attenuated by absorption [G]. The standard front-to-back compositing equation updates these accumulators for each sample $i$ from front to back:
$$
C_{acc}^{(i)} = C_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i C_i
$$
$$
\alpha_{acc}^{(i)} = \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i
$$
where $C_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ and $\alpha_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ [G]. The term $(1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)})$ represents the transmittance, i.e., the fraction of light reaching sample $i$ from the camera side [G].

In iterative form using transmittance $T = 1 - \alpha_{acc}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{final} &\leftarrow C_{final} + c_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot T \\
T &\leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_i)
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $T$ is initialized to $1.0$ and represents the accumulated transparency $\prod (1-\alpha_j)$ [G].

The final pixel color is often composited over a background color $C_{bg}$ using $C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}) C_{bg}$ [G].

Early ray termination is a common optimization where the loop breaks if $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., 1.0), as further samples contribute negligibly to the color [G].

```python
def compositing_loop(samples, background_color):
    """
    Standard front-to-back compositing loop shared by the family.
    samples: list of (color: vec3, opacity: float) sorted front-to-back.
    background_color: vec3 for the scene background.
    """
    C_acc = vec3(0.0)
    alpha_acc = 0.0
    
    for c_i, alpha_i in samples:
        transmittance = 1.0 - alpha_acc
        C_acc = C_acc + transmittance * alpha_i * c_i
        alpha_acc = alpha_acc + transmittance * alpha_i
        
        # Early ray termination
        if alpha_acc >= 1.0:
            break
            
    # Composite over background
    return C_acc + (1.0 - alpha_acc) * background_color
```

## Subtopics

### [Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md)
Pre-integration lookup replaces iterative per-sample accumulation within a volume slab with a single texture fetch from a pre-computed table. It sets itself apart from standard ray casting by moving the integration step to a pre-computation phase, and from [Simplified Pre-integration](6186-simplified-pre-integration.md) by accounting for color attenuation within the slab. See [Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md).

### [Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md)
Binary Swap Compositing is a parallel image compositing scheme for distributed-memory volume rendering that reduces network congestion by dividing communication between processors over multiple rounds. It ensures every processor participates in all stages of the compositing process by splitting the image plane into two pieces. It is particularly suitable for massively parallel processing. See [Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md).

### [Screen-space Post-processing](3621-screen-space-post-processing.md)
Screen-space post-processing is a full-screen rendering stage that applies aspect-ratio preserving UV covering, luminance-based bright passes, color grading, spatial blurring, and dithering to a scene texture. It differs from direct volume compositing methods by operating entirely in screen space on the final display image rather than during ray traversal. See [Screen-space Post-processing](3621-screen-space-post-processing.md).

### [Layered SDF Compositing](3641-layered-sdf-compositing.md)
Layered SDF Compositing renders Signed Distance Field (SDF) glyphs by compositing multiple pre-multiplied alpha layers (typically a face and one or more outline layers) into a final pixel color. It differs from standard SDF rendering by explicitly managing a fixed number of discrete layers (1, 2, or 4) using specific linear interpolation logic on pre-multiplied colors. See [Layered SDF Compositing](3641-layered-sdf-compositing.md).

### [Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md)
Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques constitute the final stage of direct volume ray casting, where 3D volumetric data is projected into a 2D image by integrating sample contributions along viewing rays. It focuses on the mathematical integration of per-ray sample attributes into a final pixel value. See [Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md).

### [Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md)
Lighting-Specific Compositing integrates illumination models (such as in-scattering, Phong shading, or boundary enhancement) directly into the compositing loop. It differs from standard direct volume ray casting by modifying the color and opacity terms ($c_i, \alpha_i$) before they enter the front-to-back accumulator. See [Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md).

### [Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework](6994-layer-centric-volume-rendering-framework.md)
The Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework constructs the final image by compositing multiple distinct renderings, each treated as an independent layer. It solves the rigidity of traditional single-pass volume rendering by segmenting the workflow into a rendering phase for small regions of interest and a subsequent compositing phase. See [Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework](6994-layer-centric-volume-rendering-framework.md).

### [MAB Compositing with Gaze-Contingent Blending](8211-mab-compositing-with-gaze-contingent-blending.md)
MAB compositing is a gaze-contingent rendering technique that maps framebuffer fragments to a Multi-Anchor Buffer (MAB) to retrieve and blend color values from different resolution levels based on the distance to the center of gaze. Unlike standard fixed-resolution ray casting, it blends multiple resolution levels simultaneously using a smooth-step function. See [MAB Compositing with Gaze-Contingent Blending](8211-mab-compositing-with-gaze-contingent-blending.md).

### [Multi-view image blending](9831-multi-view-image-blending.md)
Multi-view image blending reduces artifacts caused by single-view fading, such as ghosting and transparency variation, by combining a set of images taken from various view angles. It sets itself apart from single-texture approaches by requiring the selection and blending of at least three textures. See [Multi-view image blending](9831-multi-view-image-blending.md).

### [Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering](10585-dynamic-job-scheduling-for-grid-volume-rendering.md)
Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering determines the processing order of sub-volumes based on their real-time visibility rather than a static sequence. By using obstacle-flags to track obscuration, the method allows agents to immediately process newly visible sub-volumes. See [Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering](10585-dynamic-job-scheduling-for-grid-volume-rendering.md).

### [HardSoftRas Blending](10747-hardsoftras-blending.md)
HardSoftRas Blending is a pixel-wise compositing strategy for differentiable rendering that distinguishes between edge pixels (silhouettes and vertical surfaces) and non-edge pixels to balance anti-aliasing with texture sharpness. Unlike SoftRas, which blends all faces in non-edge regions, HardSoftRas retains the most frontal color for non-edges while applying weighted averaging for edges. See [HardSoftRas Blending](10747-hardsoftras-blending.md).

### [Alternative Compositing Methods](22574-alternative-compositing-methods.md)
Alternative compositing methods provide non-integral, statistical projections of volumetric data, serving as specialized visualization tools for specific anatomical or material features. Unlike standard optical compositing which simulates light transport via integration, these methods extract scalar statistics (minimum, maximum, threshold, or average) from the sample sequence along each ray. See [Alternative Compositing Methods](22574-alternative-compositing-methods.md).

### Frame Buffer Copy / Blit
A pass that simply copies the current frame to the output, acting as a simple integration or blit step for the LAO renderer [S3, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Sample frame buffer: `oColor = texture(uFrame, vPosition);` [S3, unofficial].

### Virtual Dimension Expansion for Compositing
A post-processing step where partial images rendered from blocks of different resolutions are expanded to a common 'virtual dimension' (the highest resolution dimension of the block) before compositing. This allows standard compositing algorithms to work despite varying input resolutions [S4, unofficial].
*   **Step:** Determine the virtual dimensions for each block. For a block with original high-res dimensions $(H_x, H_y, H_z)$, the virtual dimensions are $(H_x, H_y, H_z)$ [S4, unofficial]. Render the block at its assigned resolution (e.g., Medium: $H_x/2, H_y/2, H_z/2$). This produces a partial image with actual dimensions smaller than the virtual dimensions [S4, unofficial]. Expand the partial image to the virtual dimensions. This typically involves interpolating or replicating pixels to fill the larger grid, or simply placing the smaller image into a larger buffer with appropriate scaling factors during compositing [S4, unofficial].
*   **Parameter:** Virtual Dimension Selection: The virtual dimension can be set to the dimension of the largest subimage in the scene to minimize padding, or fixed to the global high-resolution block size [S4, unofficial].

### Voronoi Painter's Algorithm Compositing
A rendering compositing strategy where the scene is rendered part-by-part (one Voronoi cell at a time) and the resulting cell images are composited back-to-front to generate the final image. It relies on the proof that Voronoi cells admit a partial ordering compatible with occlusion [S5].
*   **Step:** 1. Define partial ordering on Voronoi cells based on distance from camera $Q$ to the Voronoi sites $P$: $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ iff $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$. 2. Render each Voronoi cell independently. 3. Composite the rendered cell images in the order defined by $<_Q$ (back-to-front) to ensure closer objects occlude further ones [S5].
*   **Formula:** $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ if and only if $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$ [S5].
*   **Comparison:** Standard Painter's Algorithm fails on cyclic occlusion graphs. Voronoi Painter's is always compatible because the distance-to-camera ordering is a strict partial order [S5].

### Hybrid Compositing (Radix-k)
Hybrid methods like Radix-k combine direct send and binary swap approaches depending on the problem setting, adaptively running one or the other based on criteria [S6, unofficial].

### Fabric Compositing
A compositing pass that creates an initial fabric-like texture by combining advected noise fields, silhouette data, and lighting. Pixels not part of the geometry are discarded using the depth buffer [S7, book].
*   **Formula:** Color channels after $k$ iterations:
    $$ R(x,y) = \frac{r \cdot f_{\lambda_2}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_1}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
    $$ G(x,y) = \frac{(1-r) \cdot f_{\lambda_1}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_2}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
    $$ B(x,y) = e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
    Here $f_{\lambda_1}$ and $f_{\lambda_2}$ are the advected fields $p_{\lambda_1}^k$ and $p_{\lambda_2}^k$. $e_{x,y}$ is the silhouette image. $r$ blends between tensor directions. Squaring the denominator emphasizes differences [S7, book].
*   **Parameter:** Blend factor $r$: Scalar factor used to blend between the two chosen tensor directions. Lighting function: A separate lighting function `light` manipulates intensity without affecting the base color of the mesh structure. Blinn-Phong shading is mentioned as providing required depth cues [S7, book].

## Background
The standard front-to-back accumulation model treats volume rendering as an integration of light emission and absorption along rays [S2, unofficial]. Early systems like VolumePro supported multiple blending modes including Front-to-back blending, Minimum Intensity, and Maximum Intensity blending [S1, unofficial]. Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) became widely used for extracting vessel structures in magnetic resonance angiograms [S2, unofficial]. The field has since expanded to include distributed compositing strategies like Binary Swap for scalability [S6, unofficial], gaze-contingent rendering for VR performance [S1, unofficial], and differentiable compositing for neural rendering [S1, unofficial].
