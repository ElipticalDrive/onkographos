# Compositing and Blending

Also called: Accumulate, Alternative Compositing Schemes, Blending Modes, First Hit, Front-to-back blending, Maximum Intensity blending, Minimum Intensity blending

## Overview
Compositing and Blending defines the mathematical and algorithmic rules for combining sample contributions $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ along a ray into a final pixel color. It solves the problem of optical integration, determining how emitted/scattered light and absorption interact to form the visible image. It sets itself apart from its siblings by focusing on the specific accumulation logic (e.g., standard alpha blending, intensity projection, or parallel distribution strategies) rather than the ray traversal or post-processing stages.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a sequence of samples $(c_i, \alpha_i)$ encountered along a ray, where $c_i$ is the sample color (RGB) and $\alpha_i$ is the sample opacity (A) [G]. The core mechanism involves an accumulator state $(C_{acc}, \alpha_{acc})$ initialized to $(0, 0)$ (or background color/opacity) and updated iteratively for each sample [G].

The standard optical model assumes that light is emitted or scattered by the volume and attenuated by absorption [G]. The standard front-to-back compositing equation updates these accumulators for each sample $i$ from front to back:
$$
C_{acc}^{(i)} = C_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i C_i
$$
$$
\alpha_{acc}^{(i)} = \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)}) \alpha_i
$$
where $C_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ and $\alpha_{acc}^{(0)} = 0$ [G]. The term $(1 - \alpha_{acc}^{(i-1)})$ represents the transmittance, i.e., the fraction of light reaching sample $i$ from the camera side [G].

In iterative form using transmittance $T = 1 - \alpha_{acc}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{final} &\leftarrow C_{final} + c_i \cdot \alpha_i \cdot T \\
T &\leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_i)
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $T$ is initialized to $1.0$ and represents the accumulated transparency $\prod (1-\alpha_j)$ [G].

The final pixel color is often composited over a background color $C_{bg}$ using $C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}) C_{bg}$ [G].

Early ray termination is a common optimization where the loop breaks if $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold (e.g., 1.0), as further samples contribute negligibly to the color [G].

```python
def compositing_loop(samples, background_color):
    """
    Standard front-to-back compositing loop shared by the family.
    samples: list of (color: vec3, opacity: float) sorted front-to-back.
    background_color: vec3 for the scene background.
    """
    C_acc = vec3(0.0)
    alpha_acc = 0.0
    
    for c_i, alpha_i in samples:
        transmittance = 1.0 - alpha_acc
        C_acc = C_acc + transmittance * alpha_i * c_i
        alpha_acc = alpha_acc + transmittance * alpha_i
        
        # Early ray termination
        if alpha_acc >= 1.0:
            break
            
    # Composite over background
    return C_acc + (1.0 - alpha_acc) * background_color
```

## Subtopics

### [Pre-Integration Lookup](2854-pre-integration-lookup.md)
Replaces iterative accumulation within a slab with a single texture fetch from a pre-computed table, solving shading inaccuracies from linear interpolation. Choose when high accuracy is needed per-slab without per-sample shading cost.

### [Binary Swap Compositing](3264-binary-swap-compositing.md)
A parallel scheme for distributed-memory rendering that reduces network congestion by dividing communication over multiple rounds. Choose for massively parallel processing to keep all processors busy.

### [Screen-space Post-processing](3621-screen-space-post-processing.md)
Applies aesthetic effects (bloom, vignette, dithering) in screen space on the final image. Choose when visual polish is required without modifying the volume rendering pipeline.

### [Layered SDF Compositing](3641-layered-sdf-compositing.md)
Composites multiple pre-multiplied alpha layers (e.g., text outlines) using specific linear interpolation logic. Choose for rendering crisp, scalable SDF glyphs with anti-aliased edges.

### [Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md)
Focuses on mathematical formulations of sample reconstruction and optical integration. Choose when specific interpolation or integration models (e.g., polynomial) are required beyond standard accumulation.

### [Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md)
Integrates illumination models (in-scattering, shading) directly into the compositing loop. Choose when physically accurate lighting interactions (occlusion, shadowing) are critical.

### [Layer-Centric Volume Rendering Framework](6994-layer-centric-volume-rendering-framework.md)
Composites distinct renderings treated as independent layers using 2D operators. Choose for flexible, interactive visualization where regions of interest are rendered separately.

### [MAB Compositing with Gaze-Contingent Blending](8211-mab-compositing-with-gaze-contingent-blending.md)
Blends multiple resolution levels based on gaze distance to maintain VR frame rates. Choose for immersive VR to prevent cybersickness from performance fluctuations.

### [Multi-view image blending](9831-multi-view-image-blending.md)
Reduces ghosting and transparency variation artifacts by blending images from multiple viewpoints. Choose when single-view fading artifacts are unacceptable and rendering cost can be increased.

### [Dynamic Job-Scheduling for Grid Volume Rendering](10585-dynamic-job-scheduling-for-grid-volume-rendering.md)
Schedules sub-volume rendering based on real-time visibility using obstacle-flags. Choose for grid computing with heterogeneous agents to avoid static scheduling bottlenecks.

### [HardSoftRas Blending](10747-hardsoftras-blending.md)
Distinguishes edge and non-edge pixels to balance anti-aliasing with texture sharpness in differentiable rendering. Choose for differentiable rasterization where silhouette gradients are needed.

### Frame Buffer Copy / Blit
A pass that simply copies the current frame to the output, acting as a simple integration or blit step for the LAO renderer [S3, unofficial].
- **Step:** Sample frame buffer: `oColor = texture(uFrame, vPosition);` [S3, unofficial].

### Virtual Dimension Expansion for Compositing
A post-processing step where partial images rendered from blocks of different resolutions are expanded to a common 'virtual dimension' (the highest resolution dimension of the block) before compositing [S4, unofficial]. This allows standard compositing algorithms to work despite varying input resolutions [S4, unofficial].
- **Step:** Determine the virtual dimensions for each block. For a block with original high-res dimensions $(H_x, H_y, H_z)$, the virtual dimensions are $(H_x, H_y, H_z)$ [S4, unofficial]. Render the block at its assigned resolution (e.g., Medium: $H_x/2, H_y/2, H_z/2$). Expand the partial image to the virtual dimensions by interpolating or replicating pixels to fill the larger grid [S4, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Virtual Dimension Selection: The virtual dimension can be set to the dimension of the largest subimage in the scene to minimize padding, or fixed to the global high-resolution block size [S4, unofficial].

### Voronoi Painter's Algorithm Compositing
A rendering compositing strategy where the scene is rendered part-by-part (one Voronoi cell at a time) and the resulting cell images are composited back-to-front to generate the final image [S5]. It relies on the proof that Voronoi cells admit a partial ordering compatible with occlusion [S5].
- **Step:** 1. Define partial ordering on Voronoi cells based on distance from camera $Q$ to the Voronoi sites $P$: $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ iff $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$. 2. Render each Voronoi cell independently. 3. Composite the rendered cell images in the order defined by $<_Q$ (back-to-front) to ensure closer objects occlude further ones [S5].
- **Formula:** $V_{P'} <_Q V_P$ if and only if $d(P', Q) < d(P, Q)$ [S5].
- **Comparison:** Standard Painter's Algorithm fails on cyclic occlusion graphs. Voronoi Painter's is always compatible because the distance-to-camera ordering is a strict partial order [S5].

### Hybrid Compositing (Radix-k)
Hybrid methods like Radix-k combine direct send and binary swap approaches depending on the problem setting, adaptively running one or the other based on criteria [S6, unofficial].

### Fabric Compositing
A compositing pass that creates an initial fabric-like texture by combining advected noise fields, silhouette data, and lighting [S7, book]. Pixels not part of the geometry are discarded using the depth buffer [S7, book].
- **Formula:** Color channels after $k$ iterations:
  $$ R(x,y) = \frac{r \cdot f_{\lambda_2}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_1}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
  $$ G(x,y) = \frac{(1-r) \cdot f_{\lambda_1}(x,y)}{8 \cdot f_{\lambda_2}^2(x,y)} + e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
  $$ B(x,y) = e_{x,y} + \text{light}(\mathcal{L}_{x,y}) $$
  Here $f_{\lambda_1}$ and $f_{\lambda_2}$ are the advected fields $p_{\lambda_1}^k$ and $p_{\lambda_2}^k$. $e_{x,y}$ is the silhouette image. $r$ blends between tensor directions. Squaring the denominator emphasizes differences [S7, book].
- **Parameter:** Blend factor $r$: Scalar factor used to blend between the two chosen tensor directions [S7, book]. Lighting function: A separate lighting function `light` manipulates intensity without affecting the base color of the mesh structure. Blinn-Phong shading is mentioned as providing required depth cues [S7, book].

## Background
The concept of compositing in volume rendering originated with the standard front-to-back accumulation model, which is mathematically equivalent to solving the rendering equation for participating media under the assumption of emission and absorption [S2, unofficial]. Early systems like VolumePro supported standard alpha blending alongside alternative modes such as Minimum Intensity and Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) [S1, unofficial]. MIP, often used to extract vessel structures in magnetic resonance angiograms, became a standard alternative for highlighting high-density features [S2, unofficial]. The field has since expanded to include distributed compositing strategies (Binary Swap, Dynamic Job-Scheduling) for scalability [S6, unofficial][S1, unofficial], and gaze-contingent techniques for immersive environments [S1, unofficial].
