# Phong Shading with Volume Rendering

Also called: compositeShaderPhong

## Overview
Phong Shading with Volume Rendering is a lighting-specific compositing technique that enhances standard direct volume ray casting by applying Phong illumination models to volume samples. It solves the problem of flat, unshaded volume appearance by computing surface-like shading cues (diffuse and specular highlights) based on gradients derived from the scalar field, thereby improving the perception of internal structure and boundaries. It differs from the standard [Composite Shader](2515-compositing-and-blending.md) by incorporating gradient-based normals and Phong lighting calculations into the color computation before compositing, adding surface-like shading to the volume [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D scalar volume dataset, a transfer function mapping scalar values to RGBA, camera position ($v_{position}$), light direction ($l_{vector}$), and Phong parameters (shininess exponent, specular coefficient).
*   **Output:** A 2D image with accumulated color and opacity.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Ray:** Defined by origin $O$ and direction $D$.
*   **Volume Grid:** Trilinearly interpolated scalar field $V(x,y,z)$.
*   **Accumulators:**
    *   $C_{acc}$: Accumulated color (RGB), initialized to $(0,0,0)$.
    *   $\alpha_{acc}$: Accumulated opacity (float), initialized to $0$.
    *   $C_{bg}$: Background color (RGB), typically $(0,0,0)$.

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Ray-Volume Intersection:** Perform standard Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) intersection to determine the entry parameter $t_0$ and exit parameter $t_1$ along the ray. Calculate the step size (sample length) $s_{len} = (t_1 - t_0) / N_{samples}$, where $N_{samples}$ is the total number of samples along the ray. If no intersection, return background color.
2.  **Initialization:** Set $t = t_0$. Initialize $C_{acc} = (0,0,0)$ and $\alpha_{acc} = 0$.
3.  **Sampling Loop:** Iterate $i$ from $0$ to $N_{samples}-1$ while $t < t_1$ and $\alpha_{acc} < 0.99$ [S1, unofficial]:
    a.  **Sample Scalar Value:** Evaluate the scalar value $v$ at the current ray position $P(t) = O + t \cdot D$ using trilinear interpolation.
    b.  **Transfer Function:** Map $v$ to initial color $c_{init}$ (RGB) and opacity $\alpha_{init}$ (float) using the transfer function.
    c.  **Gradient Computation:** Compute the gradient vector $\nabla V$ at $P(t)$ using central differences or analytical derivatives of the interpolation kernel. Let $\mathbf{n}_{raw} = \nabla V$.
    d.  **Normal Normalization:** If the magnitude $|\mathbf{n}_{raw}| > \epsilon$ (where $\epsilon$ is a small threshold, e.g., $10^{-6}$), compute the normal $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{n}_{raw} / |\mathbf{n}_{raw}|$. Otherwise, set $\mathbf{n} = (0,0,0)$.
    e.  **Lighting Calculation:** If $\mathbf{n} \neq (0,0,0)$:
        i.  Compute the view direction $\mathbf{v} = (O - P(t)) / |O - P(t)|$.
        ii. Compute the light direction $\mathbf{l}$ (normalized).
        iii. Compute the diffuse term: $k_d = \max(0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})$.
        iv. Compute the reflection vector $\mathbf{r} = 2(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})\mathbf{n} - \mathbf{l}$.
        v.  Compute the specular term: $k_s = (\max(0, \mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v}))^{shininess}$.
        vi. Modify the sample color: $c_{lit} = c_{init} \cdot (k_d + k_{ambient}) + \mathbf{k}_s \cdot k_s$, where $k_{ambient}$ is a small constant (e.g., $0.05$) and $\mathbf{k}_s$ is the specular coefficient multiplier [S1, unofficial].
        vii. If $\mathbf{n} = (0,0,0)$, $c_{lit} = c_{init}$.
    f.  **Compositing:** Apply the front-to-back compositing formula using the modified color $c_{lit}$ and the original opacity $\alpha_{init}$:
        $$ \alpha_{new} = \alpha_{init} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
        $$ C_{acc} = C_{acc} + c_{lit} \cdot \alpha_{new} $$
        $$ \alpha_{acc} = \alpha_{acc} + \alpha_{new} $$
    g.  **Early Termination Check:** If $\alpha_{acc} \ge 0.99$, set $\alpha_{acc} = 1.0$ and break the loop [S1, unofficial].
    h.  **Advance Ray:** $t = t + s_{len}$.
4.  **Final Composition:** Compute the final pixel color:
    $$ C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}) \cdot C_{bg} $$

**Formulas:**
*   **Diffuse Term:** $k_d = \max(0, \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})$
*   **Specular Term:** $k_s = (\max(0, \mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{v}))^{shininess}$, where $\mathbf{r} = 2(\mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{l})\mathbf{n} - \mathbf{l}$
*   **Modified Color:** $c_{lit} = c_{init} \cdot (k_d + 0.05) + \mathbf{k}_s \cdot k_s$ [S1, unofficial]
*   **Compositing:**
    $$ \alpha_{new} = \alpha_{init} (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
    $$ C_{acc} \leftarrow C_{acc} + c_{lit} \alpha_{new} $$
    $$ \alpha_{acc} \leftarrow \alpha_{acc} + \alpha_{new} $$

**Parameters:**
*   `shininess`: The specular exponent for the Phong model. Typical values range from 1 to 128, depending on desired glossiness [S1, unofficial].
*   `ks`: The specular coefficient multiplier. Controls the intensity of specular highlights [S1, unofficial].
*   `lightVector`: The direction vector of the light source, normalized [S1, unofficial].
*   `vposition`: The position of the camera/viewpoint, used to compute view vectors [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Zero Gradient:** If the gradient magnitude is zero (e.g., in homogeneous regions), the normal cannot be computed. The method falls back to unlit color $c_{init}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Gradient computation is sensitive to noise in the volume data. Smoothing the volume before gradient calculation may be necessary to avoid noisy shading artifacts.
*   **Boundary:** At the volume boundaries, central differences may require one-sided differences or padding.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def phong_shading_volume_rendering(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data, transfer_function, camera_pos, light_dir, shininess, ks_specular, num_samples):
    # 1. AABB Intersection
    t0, t1 = intersect_aabb(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data.bounding_box)
    if t0 > t1:
        return background_color
    
    step_length = (t1 - t0) / num_samples
    t = t0
    
    # 2. Initialization
    acc_color = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    acc_alpha = 0.0
    ambient_term = 0.05
    
    # 3. Sampling Loop
    for i in range(num_samples):
        if acc_alpha >= 0.99:
            acc_alpha = 1.0
            break
            
        # Current position
        pos = ray_origin + t * ray_direction
        
        # Sample scalar value
        scalar_val = trilinear_interpolate(volume_data, pos)
        
        # Transfer function
        init_color, init_alpha = transfer_function(scalar_val)
        
        # Gradient computation
        grad = compute_gradient(volume_data, pos)
        grad_norm = length(grad)
        
        if grad_norm > 1e-6:
            normal = grad / grad_norm
            
            # View direction
            view_dir = normalize(camera_pos - pos)
            light_dir_norm = normalize(light_dir)
            
            # Diffuse
            diff = max(0.0, dot(normal, light_dir_norm))
            
            # Specular
            reflect_dir = 2.0 * dot(normal, light_dir_norm) * normal - light_dir_norm
            spec = pow(max(0.0, dot(reflect_dir, view_dir)), shininess)
            
            # Modified color
            lit_color = init_color * (diff + ambient_term) + ks_specular * spec
        else:
            lit_color = init_color
            
        # Compositing
        alpha_new = init_alpha * (1.0 - acc_alpha)
        acc_color = acc_color + lit_color * alpha_new
        acc_alpha = acc_alpha + alpha_new
        
        t += step_length
        
    # 4. Final Composition
    final_color = acc_color + (1.0 - acc_alpha) * background_color
    return final_color
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $S$ is the number of samples per ray. The gradient computation adds a constant overhead per sample.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$, where $V$ is the size of the volume dataset, for storing the scalar field. No additional large data structures are required.
*   **Strengths:** Enhances visual clarity by providing depth cues through shading. Simple to implement as it modifies the standard ray casting loop.
*   **Limitations:** Gradient-based normals can be noisy in low-resolution or noisy volumes. Specular highlights may appear inconsistent if the volume does not have well-defined surfaces. Performance is similar to standard ray casting but with slightly higher per-sample cost due to gradient and lighting calculations.
*   **When to use:** When visual clarity and depth perception are prioritized over physical accuracy, and the volume data has sufficient resolution to support meaningful gradient computation.
*   **When not to use:** For highly noisy data without pre-smoothing, or when physical light transport (e.g., subsurface scattering) is required.

## Background
Phong Shading with Volume Rendering is an adaptation of the classic Phong reflection model, originally introduced by Bui Tuong Phong in 1975, to the domain of volume rendering. It was implemented in the context of educational and open-source volume rendering projects, such as the CSE-333 Computer Graphics course project, to demonstrate how surface-like shading can be applied to volumetric data [S1, unofficial]. It builds on the principles of [Lighting-Specific Compositing](5940-lighting-specific-compositing.md) by modifying the color contribution per sample before compositing.
