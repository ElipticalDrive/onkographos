# Training Ray Compositing

Also called: Composite Test Forward Pass, Volume Rendering Composite Forward

## Overview

Training Ray Compositing is a differentiable volume rendering technique that composites per-ray samples of density ($\sigma$), color ($\text{RGB}$), and ambient light into final pixel values while computing gradients for all inputs during the backward pass [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard inference compositing by explicitly supporting gradient propagation to density and color parameters, enabling end-to-end training of neural radiance fields [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Reconstruction and Compositing Techniques](4646-reconstruction-and-compositing-techniques.md) by providing the specific implementation for training with sigma and ambient contributions.

## How it works

**Inputs and Outputs:**
The method takes a batch of rays and their associated samples.
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `sigmas`: Density values, shape $[N]$ or $[B, S]$, where $N$ is total samples and $B$ is batch size [S3, S4, unofficial].
    *   `rgbs`: Sample colors, shape $[N, 3]$ or $[B, S, 3]$ [S3, S4, unofficial].
    *   `deltas`: Step sizes (distances between samples), shape $[N]$ or $[B, S]$ [S3, S4, unofficial].
    *   `ts`: Sample positions (z-values) along the ray, shape $[N]$ or $[B, S]$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   `rays`: Ray metadata mapping rays to sample ranges. In packed layouts, this is a tensor of shape $[N_{rays}, 3]$ containing `[ray_idx, start_idx, num_steps]` for each ray [S3, S7, unofficial]. In unpacked layouts, samples are implicitly grouped by batch index [S1, unofficial].
    *   `background_color`: Background color for transparent regions. Can be a tensor, `'random'` (random noise), or `'last_sample'` (color of the last sample) [S1, unofficial].
    *   `T_threshold`: Transmittance threshold for early ray termination. Default is $10^{-4}$ [S3, S8, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   `rgb`: Final composited RGB color per ray, shape $[N_{rays}, 3]$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   `opacity`: Accumulated alpha per ray, shape $[N_{rays}]$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   `depth`: Weighted average depth per ray, shape $[N_{rays}]$ [S3, unofficial].
    *   `ws`: Per-sample weights, shape $[N]$, required for the backward pass [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Packed Layout:** All rays share a contiguous buffer of samples. `rays` tensor indexes into this buffer. This is memory efficient for sparse sampling [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Unpacked Layout:** Samples are stored in a dense tensor $[B, S, \dots]$. This is simpler but may waste memory if rays have varying sample counts [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:**
    *   Initialize output tensors `rgb`, `opacity`, `depth` to zero for each ray [S3, S7, unofficial].
    *   Initialize transmittance $T = 1.0$ for each ray [S3, S7, unofficial].
    *   If using packed layout, iterate over unique ray indices [S1, S7, unofficial].

2.  **Sample Iteration (Front-to-Back):**
    For each ray $r$, iterate through its samples $s$ from front to back:
    *   **Compute Alpha:** Calculate the opacity contribution of the sample using the density and step size:
        $$ \alpha_s = 1 - e^{-\sigma_s \cdot \delta_s} $$
        where $\sigma_s$ is density and $\delta_s$ is the step size (distance to the next sample) [S2, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Compute Weight:** Calculate the weight $w_s$ for the sample, representing the fraction of light blocked by this sample:
        $$ w_s = \alpha_s \cdot T $$
        where $T$ is the current accumulated transmittance [S2, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Accumulate Color:** Update the accumulated color:
        $$ C_{acc} \leftarrow C_{acc} + w_s \cdot c_s $$
        where $c_s$ is the sample color [S2, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Accumulate Depth:** Update the accumulated depth (for depth estimation):
        $$ D_{acc} \leftarrow D_{acc} + w_s \cdot t_s $$
        where $t_s$ is the sample position [S3, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Accumulate Opacity:** Update the accumulated opacity:
        $$ \alpha_{acc} \leftarrow \alpha_{acc} + w_s $$
        [S3, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Update Transmittance:** Update the transmittance for the next sample:
        $$ T \leftarrow T \cdot (1 - \alpha_s) $$
        [S2, S7, unofficial].
    *   **Store Weight:** Save $w_s$ to the `ws` tensor for backward pass [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Early Termination:** If $T < T_{\text{threshold}}$ (e.g., $10^{-4}$), mark the ray as converged/finished and break the loop [S3, S7, unofficial]. This optimization skips remaining samples for opaque rays.

3.  **Background Compositing:**
    After processing all samples (or terminating early), composite the accumulated color over the background:
    $$ C_{final} = C_{acc} + (1 - \alpha_{acc}) \cdot C_{bg} $$
    where $C_{bg}$ is determined by the `background_color` parameter [S1, S2, unofficial].
    *   If `background_color` is `'random'`, use random noise [S1, unofficial].
    *   If `'last_sample'`, use the color of the last sample [S1, unofficial].
    *   If a tensor is provided, use that value [S1, unofficial].
    *   Note: `'last_sample'` is not implemented for packed samples in some libraries and raises `NotImplementedError` [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Output Assignment:**
    *   Assign $C_{final}$ to `rgb`.
    *   Assign $\alpha_{acc}$ to `opacity`.
    *   Assign $D_{acc}$ to `depth` [S3, unofficial].

**Pseudocode (Packed Layout, CUDA-style):**

```python
def composite_train_forward(sigmas, rgbs, deltas, ts, rays, background_color, T_threshold=1e-4):
    """
    Forward pass for training ray compositing.
    
    Args:
        sigmas: [N] float, densities
        rgbs: [N, 3] float, colors
        deltas: [N] float, step sizes
        ts: [N] float, sample positions
        rays: [N_rays, 3] int, [ray_idx, start_idx, num_steps]
        background_color: tensor [3] or 'random' or 'last_sample'
        T_threshold: float, early termination threshold
        
    Returns:
        rgb: [N_rays, 3]
        opacity: [N_rays]
        depth: [N_rays]
        ws: [N] per-sample weights
    """
    N_rays = rays.shape[0]
    rgb = torch.zeros(N_rays, 3, device=sigmas.device)
    opacity = torch.zeros(N_rays, device=sigmas.device)
    depth = torch.zeros(N_rays, device=sigmas.device)
    ws = torch.zeros(N, device=sigmas.device)
    
    # Handle background color
    if background_color == 'random':
        bg = torch.rand(3, device=sigmas.device)
    elif background_color == 'last_sample':
        # Not supported for packed layout in some implementations [S1, unofficial]
        raise NotImplementedError("last_sample background not supported for packed rays")
    else:
        bg = background_color
        
    for r in range(N_rays):
        ray_idx = rays[r, 0]
        start_idx = rays[r, 1]
        num_steps = rays[r, 2]
        
        if num_steps == 0:
            continue
            
        T = 1.0
        acc_rgb = torch.zeros(3, device=sigmas.device)
        acc_depth = 0.0
        acc_opacity = 0.0
        
        for s in range(start_idx, start_idx + num_steps):
            sigma = sigmas[s]
            delta = deltas[s]
            t = ts[s]
            color = rgbs[s]
            
            # Compute alpha
            alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigma * delta)
            
            # Compute weight
            w = alpha * T
            
            # Accumulate
            acc_rgb += w * color
            acc_depth += w * t
            acc_opacity += w
            
            # Store weight
            ws[s] = w
            
            # Update transmittance
            T *= (1.0 - alpha)
            
            # Early termination
            if T < T_threshold:
                break
                
        # Composite over background
        final_rgb = acc_rgb + (1.0 - acc_opacity) * bg
        
        rgb[ray_idx] = final_rgb
        opacity[ray_idx] = acc_opacity
        depth[ray_idx] = acc_depth
        
    return rgb, opacity, depth, ws
```

**Pseudocode (Unpacked Layout, PyTorch-style):**

```python
def composite_train_forward_unpacked(sigmas, rgbs, deltas, ts, background_color, T_threshold=1e-4):
    """
    Forward pass for training ray compositing (unpacked).
    
    Args:
        sigmas: [B, S] float
        rgbs: [B, S, 3] float
        deltas: [B, S] float
        ts: [B, S] float
        background_color: tensor [3] or 'random'
        T_threshold: float
        
    Returns:
        rgb: [B, 3]
        opacity: [B]
        depth: [B]
        ws: [B, S]
    """
    B, S = sigmas.shape
    ws = torch.zeros(B, S, device=sigmas.device)
    
    # Compute alpha
    alpha = 1.0 - torch.exp(-sigmas * deltas)
    
    # Compute transmittance product
    # T_i = product_{j=0}^{i-1} (1 - alpha_j)
    # Prepend 1.0 for the first sample
    one_minus_alpha = 1.0 - alpha + 1e-10 # eps for stability
    accum_prod = torch.cat([torch.ones(B, 1, device=sigmas.device), 
                            torch.cumprod(one_minus_alpha[:, :-1], dim=1)], dim=1)
    
    # Compute weights
    w = alpha * accum_prod
    ws = w
    
    # Accumulate RGB
    rgb = torch.sum(w.unsqueeze(-1) * rgbs, dim=1)
    
    # Accumulate Depth
    depth = torch.sum(w * ts, dim=1)
    
    # Accumulate Opacity
    opacity = torch.sum(w, dim=1)
    
    # Background
    if background_color == 'random':
        bg = torch.rand(3, device=sigmas.device)
    else:
        bg = background_color
        
    final_rgb = rgb + (1.0 - opacity.unsqueeze(-1)) * bg
    
    return final_rgb, opacity, depth, ws
```

## Subtopics

### [Training Ray Compositing (Sigma)](3868-training-ray-compositing-sigma.md)
Training Ray Compositing (Sigma) is a differentiable volume rendering technique that composites per-ray samples of density ($\sigma$), color ($\text{RGB}$), and ambient light into final pixel values while computing gradients for all inputs during the backward pass. It differs from standard inference compositing by explicitly supporting gradient propagation to density and color parameters, enabling end-to-end training of neural radiance fields. It builds on [Training Ray Compositing](3785-training-ray-compositing.md) by providing the specific implementation for training with sigma and ambient contributions.

### [Ray Functions](10623-ray-functions.md)
Ray functions determine the final color and opacity contribution of a ray passing through a volume by aggregating sample values along the ray path. While standard compositing accumulates opacity and color, alternative ray functions such as Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP), average value, and distance-to-threshold provide different visualizations where the result is a scalar value or distance rather than an accumulated color. The alpha compositing ray function is distinct because its result is not a scalar value or distance that can be represented on the ray profile, but rather an accumulated color and alpha weight.

### [Multi-View Color Blending](12853-multi-view-color-blending.md)
Multi-View Color Blending fuses colors from $N$ reference views into query points using view-dependent weights derived from feature similarity and viewing directions. It differs from standard alpha compositing by explicitly aggregating multi-view information before density and color prediction, addressing view-dependent appearance and occlusion in generalizable neural radiance fields.

### [Segment Coalescing Compositing](15558-segment-coalescing-compositing.md)
Segment Coalescing Compositing is a method for aggregating per-sample contributions (RGB, alpha, and depth) into final pixel values during direct volume ray casting. It solves the problem of efficiently mapping scattered ray samples to pixel-space outputs by using indexed reduction operations. It differs from standard sequential accumulation by explicitly grouping samples by `ray_id` and reducing them via sum, allowing for flexible handling of variable-length rays and background blending.

### Multi-Layer Compositing with Gradient Backpropagation
Multi-Layer Compositing with Gradient Backpropagation caps the number of composited layers at $c_{max} = 16$ per pixel to bound memory for backward pass statistics [S9, unofficial]. The forward pass performs depth-interval grouping, MIP-filtered kernel evaluation, Shepard normalization, and front-to-back compositing fused into a single kernel invocation per tile [S9, unofficial]. The backward pass first replays forward gap detection to reconstruct per-layer totals $(C_c, \alpha_c)$ for $c=1$ to $c_{max}$, then retraverses primitives to assign each to its layer and decompose per-layer gradients into per-surfel gradients through normalized accumulation using atomic operations [S9, unofficial]. Concurrent writes from different pixels during gradient decomposition are handled by atomic operations [S9, unofficial].

### Partial Image Source Signature Integration
Partial Image Source Signature Integration is a post-processing step that combines pre-computed ray buffer data (absorption/color) with a source signature vector to compute final emerging intensities, using OpenMP parallelization [S10, unofficial]. It retrieves control portals for the buffer and intensity arrays, determines pixel and channel counts, and iterates over all pixels in parallel [S10, unofficial]. For each pixel and channel, it computes the new intensity as: $\text{intensity} = \text{emission} + \text{buffer} \cdot \text{source\_sig[channel]}$ [S10, unofficial]. The buffer stores color or absorption values per pixel/channel, and the intensities store the final emerging intensity [S10, unofficial].

### Shaded Compositing
Shaded Compositing modifies the compositing ray function to include shading by computing ambient, diffuse, and specular illumination at each sample using estimated volume gradients [S11, book]. The shaded compositing equation is:
$$ I(t_{near}, \vec{\omega}) = \sum_{t = t_{near}}^{t \leq t_{far}} \alpha(t)(I_a + I_d + I_s) \prod_{t' = t_{near}}^{t' < t_{far}}(1 - a(t')) $$
where $I_a, I_d, I_s$ are ambient, diffuse, specular illumination computed using estimated volume gradient as normal, and $\alpha(t)$ is light reflected per unit length [S11, book]. This requires estimating gradients at many locations within the volume for each pixel, making 3D gradient estimation more suitable than 2D [S11, book].

### Kernel-based Compositing
Kernel-based Compositing is a compositing method using interpolation kernels where color and opacity are accumulated based on kernel weights [S12, unofficial]. The color accumulation is $c = c_s \cdot \alpha_s \cdot (1 - \alpha)$, and the opacity update is $\alpha = \alpha_s \cdot (1 - \alpha) + \alpha$, where $c_s$ is sample color, $\alpha_s$ is sample opacity, and $\alpha$ is accumulated opacity [S12, unofficial].

### Mesh/Volume Depth Compositing
Mesh/Volume Depth Compositing is a compositing strategy ensuring meshes and volumes occlude each other correctly, supporting per-segment opacity [S13, unofficial].

### Multi-channel Volume Rendering with GL_MAX
Multi-channel Volume Rendering with GL_MAX switches blend factors to $(GL\_ONE, GL\_ONE)$ and optionally enables GL_MAX blend equation to compute per-component max for multiple visible channels [S14, unofficial]. After drawing all visible channels, it restores blend factors to $(GL\_SRC\_ALPHA, GL\_ONE\_MINUS\_SRC\_ALPHA)$ and blend equation to GL\_FUNC\_ADD [S14, unofficial]. The parameter `self._use_gl_max` (bool, default True) controls this, with probing at init to handle drivers that reject GL_MAX (e.g., ANGLE on Windows) [S14, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics

*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{\text{eff\_samples}})$ per ray in the worst case, where $N_{\text{eff\_samples}}$ is the number of valid samples per ray [S3, unofficial]. Early termination reduces the average cost significantly for opaque rays [S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing per-sample weights `ws` required for the backward pass, where $N$ is the total number of samples [S3, unofficial].
*   **Hardware:** Typically implemented as CUDA kernels for GPU acceleration, with thread blocks of size 256 and grid size $\lceil N_{\text{rays}} / 256 \rceil$ [S3, S7, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Differentiable, enabling end-to-end training of neural radiance fields; efficient early termination; supports both packed and unpacked layouts [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Gradient for depth is not propagated to sigmas [S4, S8, unofficial]; `'last_sample'` background color is not implemented for packed samples [S1, unofficial]; requires careful handling of numerical stability (e.g., eps in transmittance product) [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** During the training phase of neural radiance fields or differentiable volume rendering where gradients with respect to density and color are needed [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** During inference/rendering where gradients are not needed, standard compositing is sufficient and faster [S1, unofficial].

## Background

Training Ray Compositing originates from the need to train Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and related implicit neural representations efficiently. The method was popularized by libraries such as `nerfacc` and implementations in `sdfstudio` [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the foundational volume rendering equation by Kajiya [G] and the differentiable rendering techniques developed for NeRFs [G]. The specific CUDA kernel implementations for forward and backward passes are found in various open-source projects like `IDDR-NGP` [S8, unofficial] and `ngp_pl` [S3, unofficial].
