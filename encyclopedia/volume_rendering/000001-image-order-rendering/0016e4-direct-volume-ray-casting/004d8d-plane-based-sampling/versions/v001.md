# Plane-Based Sampling

Also called: Plan-Based Sampling Method, Plane-Based Sampling for Ray Casting

## Overview
Plane-Based Sampling is a method to improve the traditional Ray Casting Algorithm (RCA) for fast reconstruction of 3D biomedical models from sequential images [S1]. It calculates the optical properties of all sampling points based on the intersection points when a ray travels through an equidistant parallel plan cluster of the volume dataset, rather than interpolating within every voxel individually [S1]. It reduces the number of arithmetic operations per sampling point compared to traditional trilinear interpolation, improving rendering speed by over three times while maintaining image quality [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D volume dataset defined by a grid of voxels; a camera position and viewing direction defining rays for each pixel; a transfer function mapping voxel values to optical properties (color $\mathbf{c}$, opacity $\alpha$); a desired sampling interval $\Delta t_{sample}$.
*   **Output:** A 2D image with composited pixel colors and opacities.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Data:** A 3D array of scalar values $V_{ijk}$ representing voxel intensities.
*   **Plane Cluster:** A set of $K$ equidistant parallel planes defined by their normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ and spacing $d_{plane}$. In the context of the source, these planes are aligned with the volume axes (orthometric axes) [S1].
*   **Intersection Points:** For a given ray, a list of 3D coordinates $\mathbf{p}_{int, k}$ where the ray intersects the planes in the cluster.

**Parameters:**
*   **Plane Spacing ($d_{plane}$):** The distance between adjacent parallel planes. The method requires this distance to not far surpass the sampling interval $\Delta t_{sample}$ to guarantee image density [S1].
*   **Sampling Interval ($\Delta t_{sample}$):** The desired distance between sampling points along the ray.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** For each pixel, cast a ray $r(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ through the volume from back to front [S1].
2.  **Plane Intersection:** Identify the group of equidistant parallel planes in the volume data field that the ray travels through [S1]. Calculate the intersection points $\mathbf{p}_{int, k}$ where the ray intersects these planes.
3.  **Property Calculation:** For sampling points located between adjacent intersection points, calculate their optical properties (color and opacity) using the formula of definite proportion and separated points, based on the properties of the intersection points [S1].
    *   Let $\mathbf{p}_{int, k}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{int, k+1}$ be two adjacent intersection points on the ray.
    *   Let $\mathbf{p}_{sample}$ be a sampling point between them.
    *   The optical properties at $\mathbf{p}_{sample}$ are interpolated from the properties at $\mathbf{p}_{int, k}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{int, k+1}$.
    *   The source cites "formula of definite proportion and separated points" but does not provide the explicit mathematical formula for this interpolation in the provided text [S1]. [G: Standard linear interpolation is implied by "definite proportion" in this context: $P(\mathbf{p}_{sample}) = P(\mathbf{p}_{int, k}) + \frac{dist(\mathbf{p}_{int, k}, \mathbf{p}_{sample})}{dist(\mathbf{p}_{int, k}, \mathbf{p}_{int, k+1})} (P(\mathbf{p}_{int, k+1}) - P(\mathbf{p}_{int, k}))$].
4.  **Compositing:** Composite the calculated optical properties into the output pixel color using the front-to-back volume rendering equation [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   If the distance between adjacent parallel planes is significantly larger than the desired sampling interval, the method fails to guarantee image density, potentially leading to aliasing or loss of detail [S1].
*   The source does not specify how to handle the case where a ray does not intersect any planes (e.g., if the plane cluster is sparse or the ray is parallel to the planes). [G: In practice, fallback to standard sampling or ensure plane coverage is dense enough].
*   The exact formula for "definite proportion and separated points" is not provided in the source [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method reduces the number of arithmetic operations per sampling point compared to traditional trilinear interpolation. Traditional RCA involves 17 additions and 16 multiplications per sampling point (including voxel location) [S1]. The plane-based method is described as faster, improving rendering speed by over three times [S1]. The exact complexity in terms of operations is not explicitly stated [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Measured Performance:** Improves rendering speed by over three times compared with the conventional algorithm while maintaining image quality [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Faster rendering speed due to reduced arithmetic operations per sampling point [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Image density is not guaranteed if the plane spacing is too large relative to the sampling interval [S1]. The method relies on the assumption that properties can be accurately interpolated between intersection points, which may not hold for highly heterogeneous volumes.

## Background
*   **Origin:** The method was proposed in a paper titled "Plane-Based Sampling for Ray Casting Algorithm in Sequential Medical Images" [S1].
*   **History:** It was developed to improve the traditional Ray Casting Algorithm (RCA) for fast reconstruction of 3D biomedical models from sequential images [S1]. The work cites Qian et al. [11] as having previously replaced sampling points with intersection points when rays travel through three groups of parallel planes along three orthometric axes, but notes that this earlier approach could not guarantee image density when the distance between adjacent parallel planes far surpassed the sampling interval [S1].
*   **Usage:** Primarily used in the context of biomedical image visualization from sequential 2D images [S1].
