# Plane-Based Sampling

Also called: Plan-Based Sampling Method, Plane-Based Sampling for Ray Casting

## Overview
Plane-Based Sampling is a ray casting optimization technique that replaces per-voxel trilinear interpolation with a two-stage process: calculating ray-plane intersections and then interpolating between those intersection points using definite proportions [S1]. It solves the high computational cost of traditional Ray Casting Algorithms (RCA) by reducing the number of arithmetic operations required to determine optical properties at sampling points [S1]. Unlike standard methods that sample within every voxel individually, this method leverages the geometry of equidistant parallel planes to guarantee image density while significantly accelerating rendering speed [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D volume dataset (discrete samples), a ray defined by origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$, and a sampling interval $\Delta s$ [S1].
*   **Output:** The accumulated color and opacity for each pixel along the ray [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on the geometric structure of the volume data, specifically treating it as a cluster of equidistant parallel planes [S1]. No additional complex data structures beyond the standard volume array are required [S1].

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** Cast a ray through the volume from back to front for each output image pixel [S1].
2.  **Plane Identification:** Identify the group of equidistant parallel planes in the volume data field that the ray travels through [S1].
3.  **Intersection Calculation:** Calculate the intersection points where the ray transacts with these parallel planes [S1].
4.  **Proportional Interpolation:** For sampling points located between adjacent intersection points, calculate their optical properties (color and opacity) using the formula of definite proportion and separated points, based on the properties of the intersection points [S1].

**Formulas:**
The traditional RCA requires 17 additions and 16 multiplications per sampling point to locate the voxel and perform trilinear interpolation [S1]. The Plane-Based Sampling method replaces this with proportional interpolation between intersection points [S1]. If a sampling point $S$ lies between two adjacent intersection points $I_a$ and $I_b$ with optical properties $(\mathbf{c}_a, \alpha_a)$ and $(\mathbf{c}_b, \alpha_b)$, its properties are calculated as:
$$ \mathbf{c}_S = \mathbf{c}_a + \frac{d(S, I_a)}{d(I_a, I_b)} (\mathbf{c}_b - \mathbf{c}_a) $$
$$ \alpha_S = \alpha_a + \frac{d(S, I_a)}{d(I_a, I_b)} (\alpha_b - \alpha_a) $$
where $d(\cdot, \cdot)$ denotes the Euclidean distance along the ray [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Sampling Interval ($\Delta s$):** The distance between adjacent parallel planes [S1]. The method requires this distance to not far surpass the desired sampling interval to guarantee image density [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Plane Spacing:** If the distance between adjacent parallel planes is significantly larger than the desired sampling interval, the method fails to guarantee image density, potentially leading to aliasing or loss of detail [S1].
*   **Precision:** The method relies on accurate intersection calculations, and standard floating-point precision issues in ray-plane intersection may apply [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def plane_based_sampling(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data, plane_spacing, num_planes):
    """
    ray_origin: 3D vector (x, y, z)
    ray_direction: 3D vector (x, y, z), normalized
    volume_data: 3D array with bounds.min/max and interpolate(point) method
    plane_spacing: float, distance between adjacent parallel planes
    num_planes: int, number of parallel planes in the cluster
    Returns: accumulated_color, accumulated_opacity
    """
    # Step 1: Cast ray from back to front
    t_min, t_max = compute_ray_box_intersection(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data)
    if t_min > t_max:
        return (0.0, 0.0, 0.0), 0.0

    accumulated_color = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    accumulated_opacity = 0.0
    front_opacity = 1.0

    # Step 2 & 3: Identify parallel planes and calculate intersections
    # Assuming planes are aligned with the Z-axis
    plane_normal = (0.0, 0.0, 1.0)
    intersection_points = []

    for i in range(num_planes):
        plane_origin_z = i * plane_spacing
        plane_origin = (0.0, 0.0, plane_origin_z)
        denom = dot(plane_normal, ray_direction)
        if abs(denom) > 1e-8:
            t_intersect = dot(plane_normal, (plane_origin - ray_origin)) / denom
            if t_min <= t_intersect <= t_max:
                point = ray_origin + t_intersect * ray_direction
                color, opacity = volume_data.interpolate(point)
                intersection_points.append({'t': t_intersect, 'color': color, 'opacity': opacity})

    # Sort intersection points from back to front (decreasing t)
    intersection_points.sort(key=lambda k: k['t'], reverse=True)

    # Step 4: Interpolate between intersection points
    for i in range(len(intersection_points) - 1):
        p1 = intersection_points[i]
        p2 = intersection_points[i+1]
        dist_segment = p2['t'] - p1['t']
        if dist_segment <= 0:
            continue

        # Determine sampling points within this segment based on plane_spacing
        num_samples = max(1, int(dist_segment / plane_spacing))

        for j in range(num_samples):
            ratio = j / num_samples
            # Definite proportion interpolation
            color_sample = lerp_vec3(p1['color'], p2['color'], ratio)
            opacity_sample = lerp_scalar(p1['opacity'], p2['opacity'], ratio)

            # Front-to-back compositing
            alpha = opacity_sample
            color = color_sample
            accumulated_color = (
                accumulated_color[0] + front_opacity * color[0] * alpha,
                accumulated_color[1] + front_opacity * color[1] * alpha,
                accumulated_color[2] + front_opacity * color[2] * alpha
            )
            accumulated_opacity = accumulated_opacity + front_opacity * alpha
            front_opacity = front_opacity * (1.0 - alpha)

            if accumulated_opacity >= 1.0:
                break
        if accumulated_opacity >= 1.0:
            break

    return accumulated_color, accumulated_opacity

def dot(v1, v2):
    return v1[0]*v2[0] + v1[1]*v2[1] + v1[2]*v2[2]

def lerp_vec3(a, b, t):
    return (a[0] + t*(b[0]-a[0]), a[1] + t*(b[1]-a[1]), a[2] + t*(b[2]-a[2]))

def lerp_scalar(a, b, t):
    return a + t*(b-a)

def compute_ray_box_intersection(origin, direction, volume_data):
    t_min, t_max = 0.0, float('inf')
    for i in range(3):
        if direction[i] == 0:
            if origin[i] < volume_data.bounds.min[i] or origin[i] > volume_data.bounds.max[i]:
                return float('inf'), 0.0
        else:
            t1 = (volume_data.bounds.min[i] - origin[i]) / direction[i]
            t2 = (volume_data.bounds.max[i] - origin[i]) / direction[i]
            t_min = max(t_min, min(t1, t2))
            t_max = min(t_max, max(t1, t2))
    return t_min, t_max
```

## Subtopics

### Traditional Ray Casting Algorithm (RCA)
The baseline method that Plane-Based Sampling improves upon [S1]. It performs trilinear interpolation for every sampling point, requiring 17 additions and 16 multiplications per point [S1].

### Qian et al. Method
A related approach that replaces sampling points with intersection points along three groups of parallel planes along orthometric axes [S1]. However, it cannot guarantee image density when the distance between adjacent parallel planes far surpasses the sampling interval [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The method reduces the number of arithmetic operations per sampling point compared to traditional trilinear interpolation [S1]. The traditional RCA takes 17 additions and 16 multiplications per point [S1]. The Plane-Based Sampling method is described as "faster" and improves rendering speed by over three times [S1]. The exact operation count is not given [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1]. The method does not appear to require additional large data structures beyond the volume data itself [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:** In the context of fast reconstruction of 3D biomedical models from sequential images, the method improves rendering speed by over three times compared with the conventional algorithm while maintaining image quality [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Significantly faster rendering speed; reduces computational load by using fewer intersection points and proportional division; maintains image quality [S1].
*   **Limitations:** If the distance between adjacent parallel planes is significantly larger than the desired sampling interval, the method fails to guarantee image density, potentially leading to aliasing or loss of detail [S1].
*   **When to use:** When fast reconstruction of 3D biomedical models from sequential images is required and the volume data can be effectively represented by equidistant parallel planes [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When high image density is critical and the plane spacing cannot be kept close to the sampling interval, as this leads to aliasing [S1].

## Background
The method was proposed to improve the traditional Ray Casting Algorithm (RCA) for the fast reconstruction of three-dimensional biomedical models from sequential images [S1]. It addresses the high proportion of time spent on sampling optical properties (about 39% of rendering time) in traditional RCA [S1]. The work cites Qian et al. [11] as prior art that used intersection points along three groups of parallel planes but failed to guarantee image density under certain conditions [S1].
