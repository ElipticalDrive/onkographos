# Tonemapping and Gamma Correction

Also called: Color Clamping, Exposure and Gamma Correction, Final image processing, Gamma Correction & PPM Output, PPM Writer

## Overview
This method processes linear radiance values into a displayable sRGB image by applying exposure control, chroma preservation, black point correction, and gamma encoding [S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of high dynamic range data exceeding display limits and the visual artifacts introduced by non-linear gamma encoding, such as shadow haze and desaturation of bright colors [S2, unofficial]. It differs from [ACES Filmic Tone Mapping](4228-aces-filmic-tone-mapping.md) by using a simpler exponential compression and explicit post-processing steps for chroma and black level rather than a single parametric curve [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Linear radiance color $C_{in}$ (vec3, range $[0, \infty)$), samples per pixel $S$ (float) [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial].
**Outputs:** 8-bit sRGB color $C_{out}$ (vec3, range $[0, 255]$) [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `col`: vec3 floating point color.
- `lum`: float luminance.

**Steps:**

1.  **Exposure and Compression:**
    Apply exponential tonemapping to compress HDR values [S2, unofficial].
    $$ C_{temp} = 1 - \exp(-C_{in} \times 1.35) $$
    [S2, unofficial]

2.  **Chroma Preservation:**
    Exponential tonemapping desaturates bright colors by compressing the dominant channel hardest [S2, unofficial]. Restore saturation by mixing the original color with a luminance-only version [S2, unofficial].
    Calculate luminance using standard coefficients:
    $$ L = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B $$
    Mix with factor $k=1.32$:
    $$ C_{temp} = \text{clamp}(\text{mix}(\vec{3}(L), C_{temp}, 1.32), 0.0, 1.0) $$
    [S2, unofficial]

3.  **Black Point Correction:**
    Gamma lifting ($x^{1/2.2}$) amplifies near-black values, causing visible haze in shadows (e.g., water appearing teal instead of blue-violet) [S3, unofficial]. Subtract channel-specific black points to restore deep shadow purity [S3, unofficial].
    $$ C_{temp} = \text{max}(C_{temp} - \vec{3}(0.0175, 0.0160, 0.0060), \vec{3}(0.0)) $$
    [S3, unofficial]

4.  **Gamma Correction and Scaling:**
    Convert linear light to sRGB gamma space and scale to 8-bit integer range [S1, unofficial].
    $$ C_{out} = \text{clamp}\left(255 \sqrt{\frac{C_{temp}}{S}}, 0, 255\right) $$
    Note: The square root $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is used here as the gamma encoding step, corresponding to a gamma of 2.0, followed by casting to integer. $S$ is `samplesPerPixel` [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def tonemap_and_gamma(col: tuple, samples_per_pixel: float) -> tuple:
    # 1. Exposure and Compression
    col = tuple(1.0 - math.exp(-c * 1.35) for c in col)
    
    # 2. Chroma Preservation
    lum = 0.2126 * col[0] + 0.7152 * col[1] + 0.0722 * col[2]
    col = tuple(max(0.0, min(1.0, lum * (1.0 - 1.32) + c * 1.32)) for c in col)
    
    # 3. Black Point Correction
    black_point = (0.0175, 0.0160, 0.0060)
    col = tuple(max(c - bp, 0.0) for c, bp in zip(col, black_point))
    
    # 4. Gamma Correction and Scaling
    scaled = tuple(c / samples_per_pixel for c in col)
    gamma_corrected = tuple(math.sqrt(s) for s in scaled)
    final = tuple(max(0.0, min(255.0, g * 255.0)) for g in gamma_corrected)
    
    return final
```

**Parameters:**
- Exposure factor: $1.35$ [S2, unofficial]
- Chroma mix factor: $1.32$ [S2, unofficial]
- Black point vector: $(0.0175, 0.0160, 0.0060)$ for $(R, G, B)$ [S3, unofficial]
- Gamma exponent: $0.5$ (square root) [S1, unofficial]

**Edge Cases:**
- If $S=0$, division by zero occurs. Ensure $S \ge 1$ [G].
- If $C_{in}$ is negative, clamp to 0 before processing [G].
- The black point subtraction assumes linear input to the gamma step; applying it after gamma lifting would be incorrect [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Channel-wise Black Point Correction
A post-processing step to correct color purity in shadows [S3, unofficial]. Gamma lifting ($pow(col, 1/2.2)$) lifts near-black values, causing visible haze [S3, unofficial]. This step subtracts a channel-specific black point to restore deep shadows [S3, unofficial]. Without this, renders may carry roughly three times too much green compared to reference, resulting in washed-out teal instead of deep blue-violet water [S3, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Black point subtraction vector: `vec3(0.0175, 0.0160, 0.0060)` for Red, Green, Blue channels respectively [S3, unofficial].
- **Code:** `col = max(col - vec3(0.0175, 0.0160, 0.0060), vec3(0.0));` [S3, unofficial]
- **Source:** [S3, unofficial]

### Chroma-Preserving Tonemapping
A post-processing step applied after tonemapping to restore saturation to bright colors that were desaturated by exponential tonemapping [S2, unofficial]. It mixes the original color with a luminance-only version, weighted to preserve chroma [S2, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** Chroma mix factor: $1.32$ [S2, unofficial].
- **Code:**
```glsl
float lum = dot(col, vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722));
col = clamp(mix(vec3(lum), col, 1.32), 0.0, 1.0);
```
- **Source:** [S2, unofficial]

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel. The operations are basic arithmetic and vector dot products [G].
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory [G].
- **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
- **Scaling:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Simple to implement; effectively handles HDR compression and common gamma artifacts (shadow haze, desaturation) [S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Less perceptually uniform than ACES Filmic; relies on fixed constants which may not suit all scenes [G].
- **When to use:** When a lightweight, fixed-parameter tonemapping pipeline is sufficient and ACES is not required [G].
- **When not to use:** When high dynamic range fidelity and color accuracy across diverse lighting conditions are critical [G].

## Background
This method originates from practical implementations in educational raytracing and shader-based rendering projects [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. The specific combination of exponential tonemapping, chroma preservation, and channel-wise black point correction was documented in unofficial repositories focusing on Manim animations and Go-based raytracers [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial][S3, unofficial]. The black point correction addresses the specific issue of gamma lifting near-black values, which was identified through palette analysis comparing renders to reference images [S3, unofficial].
