# Extinction-based Shadow and Scattering Approximation

Also called: Cone approximation via SAT, Extinction SAT queries

## Overview
This method approximates directional shadows and light scattering in volume rendering by computing the extinction of light intensity for voxels using a 3D Summed Area Table (SAT) [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of efficiently calculating soft shadows and ambient occlusion (AO) without propagating illumination from outside the volume or performing expensive per-sample shadow ray tracing [S1, unofficial]. Unlike half-angle slicing [Sibling: Half-angle slicing] or explicit sampling [Sibling: Explicit Shadow Rays], it handles any light type and multiple sources by covering the shadow cone with axis-aligned cuboids [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Volume dataset $V$, transfer function $T$, light sources (position, type, color), cone angle $\Psi$, sampling frequency, growth rate, and weighting function $h$.
*   **Output:** Light cache texture containing shadow and AO terms, applied during ray casting to modulate sample color.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **3D Summed Area Table (SAT):** A 3D texture where each voxel stores the cumulative sum of extinction coefficients $\sigma$ from the origin $(0,0,0)$ to that voxel. Constructed once per transfer function or light source change [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Light Cache Texture:** Stores precomputed shadow and AO terms. Updated every frame if lights move or TF changes, or once if static [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **SAT Construction:** Compute the 3D SAT of extinction coefficients $\sigma$ from the volume data. Time complexity depends on volume size $N^3$ (e.g., 0.311s for $256^3$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Primary Axis Selection:** For each light source, define a primary axis for sampling. The primary axis is the coordinate axis (X, Y, or Z) with the smallest angle to the vector pointing to the light source [S1, unofficial]. This ensures the best coverage of the cone with axis-aligned cuboids.
3.  **Cone Sampling:** Define sampling points along the primary axis using a user-defined sampling frequency and growth rate. The growth rate controls the change of frequency over distance, allowing coarser sampling further away [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Cuboid Derivation:** For each sampling point, derive a set of axis-aligned cuboids that approximate the shadow cone. The cuboids are derived from the primary axis sampling and the projection of the query cone onto the primary-secondary axis planes [S1, unofficial].
5.  **SAT Lookup:** Perform SAT lookups to sum extinction coefficients for the derived cuboids. The sum represents the total extinction along the cone path [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Weighting:** Apply a weighting function $h$ to the cuboid sums to strengthen or weaken the shadow effect [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Light Cache Update:** Store the computed shadow and AO terms in the light cache texture. This avoids duplicate queries during ray casting [S1, unofficial].
8.  **Ray Casting:** During the standard direct volume ray casting pass, apply illumination terms using one additional texture lookup per sample from the light cache [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
*   **Cone Angle:** $\Psi$ defines the angular spread of the shadow cone. Typical values: 1.0 to 5.0 degrees [S1, unofficial].
*   **Extinction Sum:** For a cuboid $C$, the extinction $E_C$ is obtained via SAT lookup:
    $$ E_C = \text{SAT}(C_{max}) - \text{SAT}(C_{min}) $$
    where $C_{max}$ and $C_{min}$ are the corner coordinates of the cuboid in the SAT space [S1, unofficial].
*   **Shadow Term:** The final shadow term $S$ for a sample is a weighted sum of cuboid extinctions:
    $$ S = \sum_{k} h_k \cdot E_{C_k} $$
    where $h_k$ is the weight for cuboid $k$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Cone Angle ($\Psi$):** 1.0–5.0 degrees [S1, unofficial].
*   **Sampling Frequency:** Typically a few dozen lookups per cone and voxel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Growth Rate:** User-defined, controls sampling density decay with distance [S1, unofficial].
*   **Weighting Function ($h$):** User-defined, modulates shadow strength [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Cuboid Artifacts:** Using very few cone samples (e.g., 12) results in clearly visible cuboid artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Light Source Position:** The method trivially handles light sources within or on the border of the dataset without additional effort, unlike propagation methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Transfer Function Changes:** The SAT and light cache must be recomputed whenever the transfer function changes, as extinction coefficients depend on the TF [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** SAT construction is $O(N^3)$ where $N$ is the volume dimension. Light cache computation is $O(N^3 \cdot K)$ where $K$ is the number of cuboids per voxel. Ray casting adds $O(1)$ per sample for light cache lookup.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the SAT ($N^3$) and the light cache ($N^3$).
*   **Performance:** On a Mac Pro 2.4GHz dual-Xeon with NVIDIA GeForce GTX 285, SAT construction for $256^3$ takes 0.311s. AO computation for $256^3$ with 3 shells takes 0.0307s, compared to 3.0818s for explicit sampling. Shadow computation for $64^3$ with 50 samples takes 0.0079s/frame [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Fast, flexible, handles multiple light sources and any light type, handles internal light sources trivially, low cost per frame after precomputation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires recomputation if TF or light position changes, cuboid artifacts if sampling is too low, limited to directional shadows and AO [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Interactive volume rendering with dynamic transfer functions or light sources, where soft shadows and ambient occlusion are desired [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When high-quality, physically accurate shadowing is required, or when the volume is static and precomputation is not feasible [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in "Extinction-based Shading and Illumination in GPU Volume Ray-Casting" by UZH researchers [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to provide interactive performance for volume rendering with complex illumination, addressing the limitations of previous methods like half-angle slicing [Sibling: Half-angle slicing] and explicit shadow ray tracing [Sibling: Explicit Shadow Rays]. The technique leverages the efficiency of Summed Area Tables for fast volume queries [S1, unofficial].
