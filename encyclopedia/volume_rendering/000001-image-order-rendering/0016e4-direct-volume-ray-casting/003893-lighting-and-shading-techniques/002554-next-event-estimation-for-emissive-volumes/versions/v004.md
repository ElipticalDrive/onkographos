# Next Event Estimation for Emissive Volumes

Also called: Emissive Media Rendering, Emissive Volume Rendering with Next Event Estimation (NEE), NEE for Emissive Volumes

## Overview
Next Event Estimation (NEE) for emissive volumes is a technique to reduce noise in path-traced volume rendering by directly sampling light sources (emissive media) rather than relying solely on random walk scattering events. It solves the problem of high variance when emissive volumes are small or distant relative to the camera. It differs from standard volume ray casting by introducing a separate sampling branch for light sources, managed via Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) to combine the direct light estimate with the standard volume integration.

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
*   Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\vec{\omega}$ (from camera or previous scattering event).
*   Volume scalar field $V(\mathbf{x})$ defining density/emission.
*   Emissive volume geometry (bounding shape or mesh) to determine ray intersection bounds.
*   Emission coefficient function $L_e(\mathbf{x}, \vec{\omega})$.
*   Absorption coefficient $\sigma_a(\mathbf{x})$ and scattering coefficient $\sigma_s(\mathbf{x})$ (if participating media).

**Outputs:**
*   Radiance contribution $L_{nee}$ from the emissive source.
*   MIS weight for combining with other estimators.

### Data Structures
*   **Ray Bounds:** Two scalar values $t_{entry}$ and $t_{exit}$ representing the distance from the ray origin to the entry and exit points of the emissive volume along the ray direction. These are computed via ray-shape intersection tests [S1, unofficial].
*   **PDF Storage:**
    *   $p(\vec{x})$: The standard volume PDF, typically uniform along the ray segment or based on distance sampling.
    *   $p(\vec{\omega})$: The solid angle PDF of the light source as seen from the shading point.

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Ray-Shape Intersection:**
    Cast a ray from the current position $\mathbf{x}$ towards the emissive volume. Determine the entry distance $t_{entry}$ and exit distance $t_{exit}$ along the ray direction $\vec{\omega}_l$ pointing to the light [S1, unofficial]. If no intersection, return 0.

2.  **Sample a Point on the Light:**
    Sample a point $\mathbf{x}_l$ on the emissive volume surface or within the volume. In the context of volume NEE, this often involves sampling a point along the ray segment inside the volume or on its boundary, depending on the specific implementation of "emissive media." The source notes imply tracking entry/exit points to calculate the exact directional PDF [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Solid Angle PDF:**
    Calculate the probability density $p(\vec{\omega})$ of the sampled direction $\vec{\omega}_l$ from the shading point $\mathbf{x}$. This is the solid angle PDF.

4.  **Convert to Volume PDF (MIS Weighting):**
    To combine with the standard volume estimator, the solid angle PDF must be converted to a volume PDF $p(\vec{x})$. The correct conversion involves the distance $r$ from $\mathbf{x}$ to $\mathbf{x}_l$.
    
    The MIS weight is computed as:
    $$ \mathrm{MIS}(r^{-2}p(\vec\omega), p(\vec x)) $$
    where $r^{-2}p(\vec\omega)$ represents the volume PDF derived from the solid angle PDF [S1, unofficial].
    
    *Note:* Simply dividing $p(\vec{\omega})$ by $r^2$ is insufficient if not handled consistently with the emission evaluation. The term $r^{-2}p(\vec{\omega})dV$ is a non-normalisable PDF because the integral $\int_V r^{-2} p(\vec{\omega}) \mathrm{dV}$ does not converge to 1 in the standard manner [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Evaluate Emission:**
    Evaluate the emission $L_e$ at the sampled point $\mathbf{x}_l$. Crucially, the emission evaluation must be consistent with the direction sampling used in the MIS calculation. The simplest consistent method is to evaluate the emission along the entire ray segment from $\mathbf{x}$ to $\mathbf{x}_l$, accounting for attenuation [S1, unofficial].

6.  **Compute Radiance Contribution:**
    The radiance contribution is:
    $$ L_{nee} = \frac{L_e(\mathbf{x}_l) \cdot \cos(\theta) \cdot T(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_l)}{p(\vec{x})} $$
    Where $T(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_l)$ is the transmittance from $\mathbf{x}$ to $\mathbf{x}_l$, and $p(\vec{x})$ is the volume PDF used for weighting. The term $p(\vec{x})$ in the denominator is effectively $r^{-2}p(\vec{\omega})$ adjusted for the volume element [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Combine with MIS:**
    Apply the MIS weight (e.g., balance heuristic) to combine $L_{nee}$ with the standard volume scattering estimator $L_{vol}$:
    $$ L_{final} = L_{nee} \cdot w_{nee} + L_{vol} \cdot w_{vol} $$

### Pseudocode

```python
def next_event_estimation_emissive_volume(ray_origin, ray_direction, volume_data, light_geometry):
    # Step 1: Intersect ray with emissive volume geometry
    hit = ray_intersect(ray_origin, ray_direction, light_geometry)
    if not hit:
        return 0.0

    t_entry = hit.t_min
    t_exit = hit.t_max
    
    # Sample a point on the light (simplified: sample midpoint or specific distribution)
    # In practice, this involves sampling a point x_l on the volume/mesh
    x_l = sample_light_point(light_geometry)
    
    # Vector from shading point to light sample
    l_vec = x_l - ray_origin
    r = length(l_vec)
    if r < epsilon:
        return 0.0
    
    omega_l = normalize(l_vec)
    
    # Step 2: Compute Solid Angle PDF p(omega)
    p_omega = light_geometry.pdf(omega_l, x_l)
    
    # Step 3: Convert to Volume PDF for MIS
    # The volume PDF equivalent is r^-2 * p(omega)
    # Note: This is non-normalizable, so we use it directly in the MIS ratio
    p_vol_equivalent = (1.0 / (r * r)) * p_omega
    
    # Step 4: Evaluate Emission at x_l
    # Consistent evaluation: integrate emission along the segment or evaluate at x_l
    # Assuming point evaluation for simplicity, but source suggests segment evaluation
    L_e = volume_data.emission(x_l, omega_l)
    
    # Step 5: Compute Transmittance from ray_origin to x_l
    T = compute_transmittance(ray_origin, x_l, volume_data)
    
    # Step 6: Compute Radiance Contribution
    # Using the balance heuristic, we need p(x) for the volume estimator too.
    # Assuming standard volume PDF p_vol_standard is available from the main loop
    # For this NEE branch, the estimator is:
    # L_nee = L_e * T / p_vol_equivalent
    
    # However, MIS requires both PDFs.
    # Let p_vol_standard be the PDF of the current volume sampling strategy
    p_vol_standard = volume_data.pdf(ray_origin, x_l)
    
    # Avoid division by zero
    if p_vol_standard < epsilon or p_vol_equivalent < epsilon:
        return 0.0
        
    # MIS Weight (Balance Heuristic)
    w_nee = (p_vol_equivalent * p_vol_equivalent) / (p_vol_equivalent * p_vol_equivalent + p_vol_standard * p_vol_standard)
    # Note: The source notes MIS(r^-2 p(omega), p(x)). 
    # The actual weight calculation depends on the specific MIS strategy.
    # Standard balance heuristic: w1 = f1(x) / (f1(x) + f2(x)) where f1, f2 are unnormalized densities.
    
    # Radiance contribution
    L_nee = (L_e * T) / p_vol_equivalent
    
    return L_nee * w_nee
```

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Distance $r$:** Must be strictly positive to avoid division by zero.
*   **Non-normalizable PDF:** The term $r^{-2}p(\vec{\omega})$ is not a normalized PDF. Integrating it over the volume does not yield 1. This must be handled correctly in the MIS framework by using unnormalized densities in the weight calculation [S1, unofficial].
*   **Consistency:** The emission evaluation must match the sampling distribution. If the PDF is derived from solid angle, the emission should be evaluated consistently, potentially requiring integration along the ray segment [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision issues may arise when $r$ is very large or very small.

## Operating characteristics

*   **Performance:** The implementation using NEE is slower than not using NEE. In `llvm_*` modes, it is approximately 5x slower. In `cuda_*` modes, it is approximately 1.5x slower [S1, unofficial].
*   **Noise Reduction:** NEE provides a noticeable reduction in noise for the same sample count, particularly for small or distant emissive sources [S1, unofficial].
*   **Complexity:** Integrating the volume PDF over the ray length is significantly slower (20x-40x) due to required ray casts and raises OptiX warnings in CUDA modes. Therefore, direct PDF conversion and MIS weighting is preferred over integration [S1, unofficial].
*   **Platform Differences:** There are discrepancies between `llvm_*` (Embree) and `cuda_*` (OptiX) modes regarding ray entry/exit points, leading to different results. The implementation currently gives correct results primarily for `cuda_*` modes [S1, unofficial].

## Background

*   **Origin:** This method was implemented as a feature in Mitsuba 3, specifically in the `feature/emissive-media` branch. The discussion and implementation details are documented in GitHub Pull Request #678 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Development:** The implementation was contributed by Microno95 and reviewed by dvicini. It addresses the challenge of correctly converting solid angle PDFs to volume PDFs for MIS in the context of emissive volumes [S1, unofficial].
