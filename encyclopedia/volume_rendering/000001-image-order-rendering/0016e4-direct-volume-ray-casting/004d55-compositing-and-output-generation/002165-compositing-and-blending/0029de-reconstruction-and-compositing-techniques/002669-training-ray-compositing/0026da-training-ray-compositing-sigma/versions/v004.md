# Training Ray Compositing (Sigma)

Also called: Ray Compositing for Training (Sigma + Ambient)

## Overview
Training Ray Compositing (Sigma) composites ray samples (RGB and sigma) into final image colors, alpha weights, and depth using volume rendering, specifically designed to support gradient computation for training [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard inference compositing by explicitly computing gradients for density (`sigmas`), color (`rgbs`), and ambient light contributions, enabling end-to-end optimization of the underlying neural fields [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Training Ray Compositing](3785-training-ray-compositing.md) by providing the differentiable forward pass required for backpropagation.

## How it works
The method performs differentiable alpha compositing along rays. It accumulates transparency and weighted color/depth contributions while simultaneously computing gradients for the input density and color fields.

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
- `sigmas`: Float tensor of shape $[M,]$, representing volume density at $M$ sample points [S1, unofficial].
- `rgbs`: Float tensor of shape $[M, 3]$, representing view-dependent color at $M$ sample points [S1, unofficial].
- `ambient`: Float tensor of shape $[M,]$, representing ambient light contribution at $M$ sample points [S1, unofficial].
- `deltas`: Float tensor of shape $[M, 2]$, representing step sizes (distances) between samples [S1, unofficial].
- `rays`: Integer tensor of shape $[N, 3]$, containing ray indices or identifiers mapping samples to $N$ rays [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `weights_sum`: Float tensor of shape $[N,]$, output alpha channel (accumulated weight) [S1, unofficial].
- `ambient_sum`: Float tensor of shape $[N,]$, output ambient contribution [S1, unofficial].
- `depth`: Float tensor of shape $[N,]$, output depth [S1, unofficial].
- `image`: Float tensor of shape $[N, 3]$, output RGB color [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
The implementation relies on contiguous CUDA tensors. The input tensors `sigmas`, `rgbs`, `ambient`, `deltas`, and `rays` must be contiguous in memory [S1, unofficial]. The output tensors are allocated with size $N$ (number of rays) or $N \times 3$ for color [S1, unofficial].

### Algorithm Steps
1.  **Validation**: Ensure `sigmas`, `rgbs`, and `ambient` are contiguous tensors [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Dimensions**: Retrieve $M = \text{sigmas.shape}[0]$ and $N = \text{rays.shape}[0]$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Allocation**: Allocate output tensors `weights_sum` $[N]$, `ambient_sum` $[N]$, `depth` $[N]$, and `image` $[N, 3]$ initialized to zero [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Forward Kernel**: Call the CUDA backend kernel `_backend.composite_rays_train_sigma_forward` with inputs `sigmas`, `rgbs`, `ambient`, `deltas`, `rays`, and outputs `weights_sum`, `ambient_sum`, `depth`, `image`. This kernel performs the volume rendering integration [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Save for Backward**: Save `sigmas`, `rgbs`, `ambient`, `deltas`, `rays`, `weights_sum`, `ambient_sum`, `depth`, `image`, and dimensions $M, N, T_{\text{thresh}}$ for the backward pass [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Backward Kernel (Optional/Implicit)**: In the backward pass, ensure gradient tensors `grad_weights_sum`, `grad_ambient_sum`, and `grad_image` are contiguous [S1, unofficial]. Retrieve saved tensors. Initialize `grad_sigmas`, `grad_rgbs`, `grad_ambient` to zeros. Call the backward kernel `_backend.composite_rays_train_sigma_backward` to compute gradients [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Ambient Gradient**: Call CUDA backend kernel `_backend.composite_rays_ambient_sigma` to handle ambient light gradient propagation [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
The forward pass computes the standard volume rendering integral for each ray $r$:

$$
C_r = \sum_{i=1}^{M_r} T_i \alpha_i c_i
$$

$$
D_r = \sum_{i=1}^{M_r} T_i \alpha_i d_i
$$

$$
A_r = \sum_{i=1}^{M_r} T_i \alpha_i a_i
$$

$$
W_r = \sum_{i=1}^{M_r} T_i \alpha_i
$$

Where:
- $M_r$ is the number of samples on ray $r$.
- $T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \sigma_j \delta_j\right)$ is the accumulated transmittance up to sample $i$.
- $\alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_i \delta_i)$ is the opacity of sample $i$.
- $c_i$ is the RGB color $\text{rgbs}_i$.
- $d_i$ is the depth contribution (typically the distance of sample $i$).
- $a_i$ is the ambient light $\text{ambient}_i$.
- $\sigma_i$ is the density $\text{sigmas}_i$.
- $\delta_i$ is the step size from $\text{deltas}_i$.

The backward pass computes gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \sigma_i}$, $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial c_i}$, and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial a_i}$ using the chain rule, propagating gradients from the loss $\mathcal{L}$ through $C_r, D_r, A_r, W_r$ [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
- $T_{\text{thresh}}$: Float, default $10^{-4}$. Transparency threshold. Rays with accumulated transparency below this value are considered fully opaque and can be skipped or terminated [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
- **Gradient for Depth**: The gradient for depth is not propagated to `sigmas` [S1, unofficial]. This is a known limitation/pitfall where depth errors do not directly influence density optimization [S1, unofficial].
- **Contiguity**: Non-contiguous tensors will cause errors in the CUDA kernel calls [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Training Ray Compositing (Backward)](3893-training-ray-compositing-backward.md)
Computes gradients of the volume rendering loss with respect to input density and color parameters during the backward pass. It is the backward counterpart to this method.

### [Ray Compositing for Training (Uncertainty)](3909-ray-compositing-for-training-uncertainty.md)
Composites rays for training including uncertainty estimation, computing gradients for sigmas, RGBs, ambient, and uncertainty.

### [Gradient Scaling by Distance Squared](8937-gradient-scaling-by-distance-squared.md)
Scales gradients of field outputs based on the squared distance of samples from the camera to improve training stability for distant objects. It is a specific variant of this method.

### [Ray Compositing for Training (Uncertainty)](3909-ray-compositing-for-training-uncertainty.md)
Composites rays for training with triplane-based ambient lighting (auditory and eye-specific), computing gradients for sigmas, RGBs, and various ambient components.

### CUDA Ray Compositing
A GPU-accelerated kernel that performs alpha compositing of pre-sampled RGB and sigma values along rays to produce final image and depth.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity**: Linear with respect to the number of samples $M$ and rays $N$, $O(M + N)$, as each sample is processed once per ray [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity**: $O(M + N)$ for storing input tensors and outputs [S1, unofficial].
- **Performance**: Implemented as a CUDA kernel for GPU acceleration, providing significant speedup over CPU-based compositing [S2, unofficial].
- **Strengths**: Differentiable, supports ambient lighting, efficient GPU implementation [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations**: Gradient for depth is not propagated to sigmas, which may limit depth-aware optimization [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Training Ray Compositing (Sigma) is part of the differentiable volume rendering pipeline used in neural radiance fields (NeRF) and related methods. It enables the optimization of neural field parameters by providing gradients for density and color. The method is implemented in the ER-NeRF repository [S1, unofficial] and is used in training scenarios where ambient lighting and uncertainty are modeled.
