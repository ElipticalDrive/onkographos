# Eye Align


## Overview
Eye Align is a sample placement strategy for volume raycasting where the first sample point inside the volume is calculated relative to the eye position, creating a sampling pattern across pixels that forms concentric lines [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Entry Align](855-entry-align.md) by aligning samples to the viewer's perspective rather than the volume entry boundary, which can introduce specific aliasing artifacts at the ray endpoints [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `entryPoint`: 3D vector, entry point of the ray into the volume (in texture coordinates) [S1, unofficial].
*   `exitPoint`: 3D vector, exit point of the ray from the volume (in texture coordinates) [S1, unofficial].
*   `eyePos`: 3D vector, position of the camera/eye (in texture coordinates) [S1, unofficial].
*   `dim`: 3D vector, dimensions of the volume grid [S1, unofficial].
*   `samplingRate`: float, number of samples per unit length in texture space [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   `tStart`: float, the parameter value for the first sample point inside the volume [S1, unofficial].
*   `tIncr`: float, the step increment between consecutive samples [S1, unofficial].
*   `tEnd`: float, the total length of the ray within the volume [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
Standard floating-point vectors and scalars [G].

**Steps:**
1. Calculate the ray vector in texture coordinates:
   $$ \vec{ray} = \text{exitPoint} - \text{entryPoint} $$
2. Calculate the total number of samples based on ray length, volume dimensions, and sampling rate:
   $$ \text{samples} = \|\vec{ray} \odot \text{dim}\| \times \text{samplingRate} $$
   where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication, and $\|\cdot\|$ is the Euclidean norm [S1, unofficial].
3. Calculate the total ray length:
   $$ t_{\text{End}} = \|\vec{ray}\| $$
4. Calculate the step increment:
   $$ t_{\text{Incr}} = \frac{t_{\text{End}}}{\text{samples}} $$
5. Calculate the distance from the eye to the entry point to determine the offset for the first sample:
   $$ \text{numSteps} = \frac{\|\text{entryPoint} - \text{eyePos}\|}{t_{\text{Incr}}} $$
6. Calculate the start parameter for the first sample inside the volume by removing the fractional part of the steps from the eye:
   $$ t_{\text{Start}} = (1 - \text{fract}(\text{numSteps})) \times t_{\text{Incr}} $$
   where $\text{fract}(x) = x - \lfloor x \rfloor$ [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
import math

def eye_align(entryPoint, exitPoint, eyePos, dim, samplingRate):
    # 1. Ray vector in texture coordinates
    ray = (exitPoint[0] - entryPoint[0],
           exitPoint[1] - entryPoint[1],
           exitPoint[2] - entryPoint[2])
    
    # 2. Total number of samples (element-wise multiply then Euclidean norm)
    ray_scaled = (ray[0] * dim[0], ray[1] * dim[1], ray[2] * dim[2])
    samples = (ray_scaled[0]**2 + ray_scaled[1]**2 + ray_scaled[2]**2)**0.5 * samplingRate
    
    # 3. Total ray length
    tEnd = (ray[0]**2 + ray[1]**2 + ray[2]**2)**0.5
    
    # 4. Step increment
    if samples == 0:
        return 0.0, 0.0, 0.0 # Guard against division by zero [G]
    tIncr = tEnd / samples
    
    # 5. Distance from eye to entry in terms of steps
    dist_eye_entry = ((entryPoint[0] - eyePos[0])**2 + 
                      (entryPoint[1] - eyePos[1])**2 + 
                      (entryPoint[2] - eyePos[2])**2)**0.5
    numSteps = dist_eye_entry / tIncr
    
    # 6. Start parameter
    frac = numSteps - math.floor(numSteps) # fract(x) = x - floor(x)
    tStart = (1.0 - frac) * tIncr
    
    return tStart, tIncr, tEnd
```

**Parameters:**
*   `samplingRate`: Not given in the sources. [G]

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Endpoint Estimation Error:** The Riemann sum mid-point estimate used in conjunction with this sampling strategy tends to over- or underestimate contributions at both the first and last sample points, visually indicated by overshooting artifacts [S1, unofficial].
*   **Zero Ray Length:** If `entryPoint` equals `exitPoint`, `tEnd` and `tIncr` become zero, leading to division by zero in steps 4 and 5. This should be handled by skipping rendering or returning default values [G].

## Subtopics

### [Entry Align](855-entry-align.md)
A sample placement strategy for fixed-step volume raycasting that shifts the first sample point half a step away from the volume entry boundary to align with the Riemann sum midpoint estimate. Unlike Eye Align, which aligns samples relative to the eye position, Entry Align aligns the sampling grid relative to the volume geometry, eliminating the boundary estimation error at the entry point. Choose it when minimizing endpoint artifacts is prioritized over viewer-aligned sampling patterns.
