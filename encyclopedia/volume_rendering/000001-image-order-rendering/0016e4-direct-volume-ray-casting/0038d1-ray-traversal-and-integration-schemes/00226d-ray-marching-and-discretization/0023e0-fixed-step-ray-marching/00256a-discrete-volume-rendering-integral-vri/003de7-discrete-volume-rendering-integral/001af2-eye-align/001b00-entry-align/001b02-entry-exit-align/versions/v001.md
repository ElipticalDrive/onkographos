# Entry Exit Align

Also called: Entry Exit Align Corner, Entry Exit Align Corner Sampling, Recommended Sampling Strategy

## Overview
Entry Exit Align is a sample placement strategy for fixed-length volume raycasting that aligns sample points with both the ray entry and exit points to minimize boundary artifacts [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Entry Align](855-entry-align.md), which only aligns the first sample, Entry Exit Align ensures that neither the first nor the last sample under- or overestimates the integral by adjusting the step size per ray [S1, unofficial]. It is recommended as the best practice for volume raycasting due to zero performance impact and minimal errors [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   `entryPoint`: The 3D coordinate where the ray enters the volume, given in texture coordinates.
*   `exitPoint`: The 3D coordinate where the ray exits the volume, given in texture coordinates.
*   `samplingRate`: The desired number of samples per unit volume dimension (typically 1.0 for one sample per voxel, but can be higher for supersampling).
*   `dim`: The dimensions of the volume texture (width, height, depth).

**Outputs:**
*   `tStart`: The starting parameter along the ray for the first sample.
*   `tIncr`: The step increment (distance between samples) along the ray.
*   `tEnd`: The total length of the ray vector.

**Data Structures:**
*   `vec3`: A 3D vector type holding `x`, `y`, `z` components.
*   `float`: Standard floating-point number.

**Algorithm:**
The method calculates the number of samples required to cover the ray length while ensuring the first sample is half a step inside the entry and the last sample is half a step inside the exit. To handle corner rays where the length is smaller than the standard step size, it uses a ceiling function on a dimension-adjusted length to ensure at least one sample is placed at the midpoint of such short rays [S1, unofficial].

1.  Calculate the ray vector in texture coordinates:
    $$ \text{ray} = \text{exitPoint} - \text{entryPoint} $$
2.  Calculate the number of samples. This uses the ceiling function applied to the scaled length of the ray against the volume dimensions minus one. This adjustment ensures that rays shorter than the step size (e.g., corner rays) still receive a sample [S1, unofficial]:
    $$ \text{samples} = \lceil \text{samplingRate} \times \|\text{ray} \times (\text{dim} - \vec{1})\| \rceil $$
    Where $\vec{1}$ is a vector $(1, 1, 1)$ and $\times$ denotes component-wise multiplication.
3.  Calculate the total ray length:
    $$ t_{\text{End}} = \|\text{ray}\| $$
4.  Calculate the step increment by dividing the total length by the number of samples:
    $$ t_{\text{Incr}} = \frac{t_{\text{End}}}{\text{samples}} $$
5.  Set the start parameter to half a step from the entry point:
    $$ t_{\text{Start}} = 0.5 \times t_{\text{Incr}} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def entry_exit_align(entryPoint, exitPoint, samplingRate, dim):
    """
    Calculates sampling parameters for Entry Exit Align Corner strategy.
    
    Args:
        entryPoint (vec3): Entry point in texture coordinates.
        exitPoint (vec3): Exit point in texture coordinates.
        samplingRate (float): Samples per unit dimension.
        dim (vec3): Volume dimensions (width, height, depth).
        
    Returns:
        tStart (float): Start parameter for first sample.
        tIncr (float): Step increment.
        tEnd (float): Total ray length.
    """
    # 1. Ray vector in texture coordinates
    ray = exitPoint - entryPoint
    
    # 2. Number of samples
    # Adjust dimensions by subtracting 1 to handle corner cases where ray length < step size
    # This ensures ceil() returns at least 1 for very short rays
    adjusted_dim = dim - vec3(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)
    scaled_ray = ray * adjusted_dim
    samples = ceil(samplingRate * length(scaled_ray))
    
    # Ensure at least one sample is taken
    if samples < 1:
        samples = 1
        
    # 3. Total ray length
    tEnd = length(ray)
    
    # 4. Step increment
    tIncr = tEnd / samples
    
    # 5. Start parameter (half a step from entry)
    tStart = 0.5 * tIncr
    
    return tStart, tIncr, tEnd
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Corner Rays:** Standard strategies may exclude contributions at corners where the ray length is smaller than the step size. This method explicitly includes these by adjusting the step size to the ray length and placing a sample at the midpoint [S1, unofficial].
*   **Pre-integrated Rendering:** Implementations using pre-integrated volume rendering reduced to two dimensions require all rays to use the same step length. Consequently, they cannot use this per-ray adaptive step size and must choose a strategy that under- or overestimates the integral [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The use of `ceil` and dimension adjustment ensures robustness for degenerate rays (length near zero), preventing division by zero or zero samples [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of samples per ray. The calculation of parameters is $O(1)$ per ray.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ auxiliary memory per ray.
*   **Performance:** Zero performance impact compared to simpler strategies [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates stepping artifacts and gaps at the bounding box. It does not suffer from over- or underestimation of first/last samples [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires per-ray calculation of step size, which may be incompatible with fixed-step pre-integrated rendering techniques [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Recommended as the best practice for general volume raycasting [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When using pre-integrated volume rendering in 2D where a uniform step size across all rays is mandatory [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method was introduced in the paper "Volume Raycasting Sampling Revisited" [S1, unofficial]. It builds upon the "Eye Align" and "Entry Align" strategies, addressing their respective deficiencies regarding boundary estimation. The authors note that while errors due to sample placement can be significant for data at the borders, they are often marginal in real-world use cases with higher resolution volumes. However, artifacts can appear when zooming in on borders, justifying the recommendation of this method [S1, unofficial]. The concepts were developed and evaluated in the Inviwo framework [S1, unofficial].
