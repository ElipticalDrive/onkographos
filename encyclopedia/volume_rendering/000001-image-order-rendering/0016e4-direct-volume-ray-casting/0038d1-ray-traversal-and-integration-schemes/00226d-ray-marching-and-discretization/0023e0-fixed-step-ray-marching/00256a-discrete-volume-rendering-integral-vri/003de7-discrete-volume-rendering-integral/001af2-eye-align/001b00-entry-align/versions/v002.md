# Entry Align


## Overview
Entry Align is a sample placement strategy for fixed-step volume raycasting that shifts the first sample point half a step away from the volume entry boundary to align with the Riemann sum midpoint estimate [S1, unofficial]. Unlike its sibling [Eye Align](849-eye-align.md), which aligns samples relative to the eye position, Entry Align aligns the sampling grid relative to the volume geometry, eliminating the boundary estimation error at the entry point [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `entryPoint`: The 3D position where the ray enters the volume, in texture coordinates [S1, unofficial].
- `exitPoint`: The 3D position where the ray exits the volume, in texture coordinates [S1, unofficial].
- `dim`: The dimensions of the volume texture (width, height, depth) [S1, unofficial].
- `samplingRate`: The number of samples per unit length in texture space [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `tStart`: The starting parameter for the first sample along the ray [S1, unofficial].
- `tIncr`: The step increment between consecutive samples [S1, unofficial].
- `tEnd`: The total length of the ray in texture coordinates [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
- `ray`: A 3D vector representing the direction and length of the ray in texture space [S1, unofficial].
- `samples`: A scalar representing the total number of samples to be taken [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Calculate the ray vector in texture coordinates:
   $$ \text{ray} = \text{exitPoint} - \text{entryPoint} $$
2. Calculate the total number of samples required based on the ray length and sampling rate:
   $$ \text{samples} = \|\text{ray} \times \text{dim}\| \times \text{samplingRate} $$
   The multiplication `ray * dim` scales the texture coordinate ray to voxel indices [G]. The function `length()` computes the Euclidean norm of the scaled vector [G].
3. Calculate the total length of the ray in texture coordinates:
   $$ t_{\text{End}} = \|\text{ray}\| $$
4. Calculate the step increment (distance between samples in parameter space):
   $$ t_{\text{Incr}} = \frac{t_{\text{End}}}{\text{samples}} $$
5. Set the start parameter to half a step from the entry point:
   $$ t_{\text{Start}} = 0.5 \times t_{\text{Incr}} $$

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def entry_align(entry_point, exit_point, dim, sampling_rate):
    # 1. Calculate ray vector in texture coordinates
    ray = exit_point - entry_point
    
    # 2. Calculate total number of samples
    # ray * dim scales texture coordinates to voxel indices [G]
    # length() computes the Euclidean norm [G]
    samples = length(ray * dim) * sampling_rate
    
    # 3. Calculate total ray length in texture coordinates
    t_end = length(ray)
    
    # 4. Calculate step increment
    t_incr = t_end / samples
    
    # 5. Set start parameter to half a step from entry
    t_start = 0.5 * t_incr
    
    return t_start, t_incr, t_end
```

**Parameters:**
- `samplingRate`: Determines the density of samples. Typical values depend on the desired visual quality and performance constraints. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Back Boundary Error:** While Entry Align corrects the estimation error at the entry point, the last sample may still under- or overestimate the contribution at the exit boundary, depending on how close the final sample is to the back side of the volume [S1, unofficial]. This occurs because the step size is fixed based on the entry alignment, and the exit point is not necessarily aligned with a sample midpoint [G].

## Subtopics

### [Entry Exit Align](856-entry-exit-align.md)
A variant that aligns both the first and last samples to minimize boundary artifacts at entry and exit by adjusting the step size per ray. Recommended as best practice for zero performance impact and minimal errors.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **Strengths:** Eliminates the boundary estimation error at the entry point, which is a common artifact in naive fixed-step raycasting [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Does not correct the boundary error at the exit point [S1, unofficial]. The fixed step size may lead to suboptimal sampling density if the ray length varies significantly across pixels, though the step size is calculated per ray [G].
- **When to use:** Not given in the sources.
- **When not to use:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
Entry Align was introduced to address Riemann sum midpoint estimation errors where samples over- or underestimate contributions at boundaries [S1, unofficial]. It shifts samples half a step to align with the entry point, similar to techniques described in "Real-Time Volume Graphics" by Engel et al. [S1, unofficial].
