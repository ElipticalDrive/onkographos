# Ray Traversal and Integration Schemes

Also called: Ray Marching, Ray Casting Loop, LOS Integration, Monte Carlo Volume Rendering, Multi-layer Ray Casting, MLS Ray Casting

## Overview
Ray Traversal and Integration Schemes define the computational strategy for aggregating scalar voxel values along a viewing ray into a final pixel color and opacity, ranging from deterministic fixed-step integration to stochastic path tracing and multi-layer compositing [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. Unlike standard front-to-back ray casting which strictly integrates the emission-absorption model, these schemes may prioritize specific visual features (such as maximum intensity), handle irregular data distributions via Moving Least Squares (MLS), or manage complex Volume-of-Interest (VOI) interactions through pre-computed layer buffers [S2, S4, unofficial]. They are distinguished from [Neural volume rendering](1576-neural-volume-rendering.md) by their reliance on explicit geometric or grid-based sampling rather than implicit neural field representations, and from [Temporal Accumulation (Closest Hit)](2080-temporal-accumulation-closest-hit.md) by their primary focus on single-pass spatial integration rather than temporal consistency [S1, abstract only, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All methods in this family operate on a ray segment $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$ defined by the intersection of the camera ray with the volume domain (typically an axis-aligned bounding box) [S1, S8, S16, unofficial]. For each sample point $\mathbf{p}_i$ along the ray, a scalar value $d_i$ is obtained via interpolation (typically trilinear) from the voxel grid or spatial data structure [S1, S2, S3, S4, unofficial]. This scalar is mapped to optical properties (color $C_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$) via a transfer function $T(d_i) \rightarrow (C_i, \alpha_i)$ [S2, S2789]. The opacity is often corrected for the step size $\Delta t$ to account for discretization error, using the relation $\alpha'_i = 1 - (1 - \alpha_i)^{\Delta t}$ or similar approximations [S11, S32, unofficial]. The core distinction among the subtopics lies in the aggregation function $A(\{(C_i, \alpha_i)\}_{i=1}^N)$ that combines these samples into the final pixel color $C_{final}$ and opacity $\alpha_{final}$, and in the method used to determine the sample points $\mathbf{p}_i$ [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md)
An iterative technique that approximates the volume rendering integral by casting rays and accumulating optical properties at discrete sample points. Unlike fixed-step traversal methods, iterative variants may advance rays using variable step sizes guided by distance estimators, enabling efficient handling of sparse, implicit, or procedural data. It processes the image pixel-by-pixel in a fragment shader, ensuring view-independent quality and supporting early ray termination.

### [Image-order Rendering Loop](2938-image-order-rendering-loop.md)
The primary execution structure for direct volume ray casting, iterating over each pixel to generate a ray and compute its final color contribution. It solves the problem of mapping 3D volumetric data to a 2D screen by decoupling pixel processing, allowing for flexible shading modes and adaptive sampling. Unlike object-order methods (rasterization), this approach offers correct perspective projection, single-pass ray casting, and full 32-bit compositing precision without the need for multiple passes or early-z testing.

### [Front-to-Back Volume Ray Casting with HSV Shading and SOS/Attenuation/Reflection Filtering](4324-front-to-back-volume-ray-casting-with-hsv-shading-and-sos-at.md)
Performs direct volume rendering by casting rays and compositing results front-to-back using alpha blending. It distinguishes itself by mapping multi-component volume values (Scalar Output Scalar, Attenuation, Reflection) to an HSV color space for shading, rather than using standard gradient-based Phong lighting or direct RGBA transfer functions. It employs specific thresholding filters on the SOS, Attenuation, and Reflection components to discard irrelevant samples before color conversion.

### [LOS-integration for visualization](4807-los-integration-for-visualization.md)
Computes physical scalar quantities (electron column density, integrated emissivity, or emission-weighted temperature) along camera rays by integrating sampled field values. It differs from standard direct volume ray casting by targeting specific astrophysical diagnostics rather than generic RGBA color compositing, utilizing a neural network or ground truth field to predict local densities and temperatures, and mapping them to emissivity via a lookup table (LUT) or learned field before integration. It is designed for visualizing solar corona structures where physical consistency and unit conversion are critical.

### [Monte Carlo Path Tracing for Volume Rendering](4980-monte-carlo-path-tracing-for-volume-rendering.md)
A stochastic method that simulates light transport within participating media by randomly sampling scattering events along a ray, rather than discretizing the ray into fixed steps. Unlike deterministic ray marching methods that integrate over equidistant intervals, this approach uses distance sampling to locate the next interaction point, allowing for efficient handling of sparse or highly heterogeneous volumes where large empty regions can be skipped. It distinguishes itself from its siblings by replacing the fixed-step compositing loop with a probabilistic path generation loop, accumulating results over multiple frames to reduce noise.

### [Ray Traversal Schemes](6890-ray-traversal-schemes.md)
Defines how scalar voxel values along a ray are aggregated into a single pixel color and opacity, ranging from simple statistical projections to full volumetric compositing. Unlike standard front-to-back ray casting which integrates light transport, these schemes often prioritize specific visual features (such as maximum intensity or surface proximity) or differentiability for inverse graphics. They are distinguished from [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) by their focus on the aggregation logic rather than the sampling interval, and from [Monte Carlo Path Tracing for Volume Rendering](4980-monte-carlo-path-tracing-for-volume-rendering.md) by their deterministic, single-pass nature.

### [Multi-layer Volume Ray Casting](9441-multi-layer-volume-ray-casting.md)
A GPU-accelerated rendering method that handles complex volume-of-interest (VOI) interactions, including clipping, embedding, and CSG operations, by encoding spatial overlaps into a pre-generated ray-layer buffer. Unlike standard ray casting which processes a single continuous segment or requires multiple passes for layered objects, this method iterates through non-overlapping ray segments stored in the buffer, applying material-specific transfer functions and priorities per segment within a single rendering pass. It sets itself apart from polygon-assisted ray casting by storing only the Z-coordinate of layer starts rather than full 3D texture coordinates, reducing memory usage and ensuring bricking compatibility.

### [Ray Casting and Compositing Loop](9826-ray-casting-and-compositing-loop.md)
Performs direct volume rendering by integrating radiative energy along rays using Moving Least Squares (MLS) approximation for scalar field reconstruction and precomputed integration tables for optical accumulation. It distinguishes itself from standard voxel-based ray casting by handling irregular or sparse data distributions without requiring a regular grid, relying on local neighborhood queries to define the basis functions.

#### Spherical Volume Rendering with Adaptive Ray Casting
- **Definition**: A method for expressing ocean scalar fields in a 3D digital Earth environment using a minimum bounding volume based on spherical mosaic as proxy geometry and adaptive sampling [S1, abstract only, unofficial].
- **Steps**: Construct a minimum bounding volume based on spherical mosaic as the proxy geometry; apply the depth texture of the seabed terrain to determine the position of sampling points in the spatial interpolation process; propose an adaptive sampling step algorithm based on heterogeneous depth distribution and data change rate to improve ray-casting efficiency; apply a nonlinear color-mapping enhancement scheme based on dataset skewness characteristics; design a transparency transfer function to realize volume rendering and local feature structure extraction [S1, abstract only, unofficial].

## Background
The foundational principles of ray traversal and integration in volume rendering were established in the early 1990s. The shear-warp factorization algorithm, introduced by Levoy in 1990, provided a fast method for rendering rotated 3D volumes by decomposing the viewing transform into shear and warp operations [S1, abstract only, unofficial]. This approach was later adapted for deskewed lightsheet data and other specialized imaging modalities [S1, abstract only, unofficial]. The front-to-back compositing equation, which forms the basis of most deterministic ray marching implementations, was formalized by Williams et al. [S1, S13, unofficial]. Subsequent developments included the integration of Monte Carlo methods for participating media [S1, abstract only, unofficial], the use of Moving Least Squares for unstructured data [S1, abstract only, unofficial], and the development of multi-layer buffering techniques for complex VOI interactions [S1, abstract only, unofficial]. Recent advancements have focused on adaptive sampling for spherical domains [S1, abstract only, unofficial] and the integration of neural networks for physical property estimation in astrophysical visualization [S1, abstract only, unofficial].
