# Ray Traversal Schemes

Also called: Accumulate Projection, Depth Projection, Max Intensity Projection, Ray Traversal -Accumulate

## Overview
Ray traversal schemes define the aggregation logic applied to the optical properties (color and opacity) sampled along a ray to produce the final pixel value [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. While the parent article [Ray Traversal and Integration Schemes](8341-ray-traversal-and-integration-schemes.md) establishes the standard front-to-back compositing loop, these schemes offer alternative accumulation strategies such as maximum intensity projection, averaging, or first-hit detection [S2, unofficial]. They are distinguished from sibling schemes like [Ray Marching and Discretization](2788-ray-marching-and-discretization.md) by their focus on the *compositing rule* rather than the sampling geometry, and from [Image-order Rendering Loop](2938-image-order-rendering-loop.md) by their specific mathematical reduction of the 1D ray integral [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares the input data structure defined in the parent: a sequence of samples $(C_i, \alpha_i)$ retrieved from the volume at positions $t_i$ along the ray segment $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$ [S5, S10, unofficial]. The core distinction lies in the reduction function $R$ that maps the sequence of samples to a single pixel color $C_{pixel}$ and potentially an auxiliary value (e.g., depth or accumulated opacity) [S2, unofficial].

Common reduction types include:
1.  **Accumulation (Compositing)**: The standard front-to-back integration defined in the parent, where transparency is modeled physically [S1, S13, unofficial].
2.  **Extremum**: Selecting the maximum or minimum scalar value along the ray, ignoring opacity for the final color selection but often using it for visibility [S2, unofficial].
3.  **Averaging**: Computing the arithmetic mean of scalar values, effectively projecting density without occlusion [S2, unofficial].
4.  **First Hit**: Identifying the first sample that exceeds a threshold, typically used for surface extraction [S1, unofficial].

All schemes operate on the discretized ray samples generated by the parent's sampling stage [S5, S10, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Pixel Intensity Evaluation with Scene Aggregates](10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md)
This method replaces sequential ray marching with a precomputed visibility aggregation model to solve the problem of blending scene aggregates with external backgrounds while handling inter-voxel correlation [10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md]. Unlike standard schemes that process samples sequentially, it evaluates pixel intensity by integrating radiance over the pixel footprint using Aggregated Visibility Functions (AVF) [10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md]. It is chosen when accurate silhouette reconstruction and background blending are required without per-ray traversal overhead [10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md]. See [Pixel Intensity Evaluation with Scene Aggregates](10890-pixel-intensity-evaluation-with-scene-aggregates.md).

### [X-Ray Volume Rendering](11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md)
This scheme computes a linear accumulation of voxel intensity values along a ray, producing an image similar to a projectional X-ray or CT scan [11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md]. Unlike standard direct volume rendering which uses alpha compositing to simulate transparency and occlusion, this method sums the sampled values to highlight dense structures without depth-based occlusion [11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md]. It differs from its sibling [Average](6890-ray-traversal-schemes.md) rendering by explicitly normalizing the sum by the total number of steps to maintain intensity invariance relative to step size, whereas Average typically divides by the count of *valid* or *non-background* samples [11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md]. See [X-Ray Volume Rendering](11336-x-ray-volume-rendering.md).

### Intensity
Extracts the scalar value of the first sample that meets a classification threshold, effectively isolating iso-surfaces [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is used when the goal is to visualize specific internal structures (e.g., bone or skin) rather than the entire volume [S1, unofficial].

### Max (Maximum Intensity Projection)
Projects the maximum intensity value encountered along the entire ray length [S2, S3, unofficial]. It is particularly useful for visualizing high-contrast structures like blood vessels in Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA) or CT angiography, as it preserves the brightest voxels regardless of depth [S2, S3, unofficial].

### Average
Produces an X-ray-like picture by averaging the scalar values along the ray [S2, S3, unofficial]. This method removes depth cues and occlusion, providing a global view of density distribution [S2, unofficial].

### Accumulate
Accumulates opacity while compositing colors to make transparent layers visible [S2, S3, unofficial]. This is the standard direct volume rendering approach described in the parent article, utilizing the front-to-back compositing equation [S1, S13, unofficial].

### First
Extracts the iso-surface closest to the camera by finding the first sample that exceeds an opacity threshold [S1, unofficial]. It is used for surface extraction and visualization of specific boundaries [S1, unofficial].

### Depth
Records the distance $t$ along the ray where the primary visual event (e.g., first hit, max intensity, or accumulated opacity threshold) occurs [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is used for depth mapping and spatial analysis [S1, S2, unofficial].

#### Voxel Aggregation Methods
These variants focus on probabilistic or differentiable aggregation for neural rendering contexts [S4, unofficial].
*   **Yan et al.**: Casts a ray and selects the voxel with the highest occupancy probability [S4, unofficial].
*   **Tulsiani et al.**: Computes the probability that a ray stops at a certain distance, enabling depth map and color image rendering [S4, unofficial].
*   **Henzler et al.**: Introduces bidirectional emission-absorption (EA) light radiation models alongside visual hull (VH) and absorption-only (AO) models [S4, unofficial].
*   **Lombardi et al.**: Iteratively accumulates color and opacity probabilities, discarding remaining voxels when cumulative opacity reaches a maximum [S4, unofficial].
*   **Jiang et al.**: Locates the surface voxel closest to the camera for Signed Distance Functions (SDFs), computing color as a weighted sum of the surface and neighboring voxels, with shading based on local normals [S4, unofficial]. Note that locating the surface point is not differentiable, though the pixel color is differentiable with respect to surrounding voxels [S4, unofficial].

## Background
The classification of ray traversal schemes was formalized in early direct volume rendering literature [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. The "Accumulate" scheme is attributed to Levoy (1988) as the standard for volumetric compositing [S1, S2, S3, unofficial]. Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP) and Average projection were established as standard alternatives for specific medical imaging applications, such as angiography and X-ray simulation [S2, S3, unofficial]. Recent developments in differentiable rendering have expanded these schemes to include probabilistic aggregation and neural voxel representations [S4, unofficial].
