# Ray Traversal Schemes

Also called: Accumulate Projection, Depth Projection, Max Intensity Projection, Ray Traversal -Accumulate

## Overview
Ray traversal schemes define the aggregation logic applied to sample values along a ray to produce a single pixel color, ranging from standard compositing to specialized projections [S1, S2, unofficial]. Unlike standard front-to-back compositing which integrates opacity and color, these schemes may prioritize maximum intensity, average density, or specific depth cues, offering distinct visual interpretations of the volume data [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Shared principles
All schemes operate on the discretized ray segment $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$ defined in [Direct volume ray casting](19-direct-volume-ray-casting.md). For each sample $i$ along the ray, the scheme retrieves the classified color $C_i$ and opacity $\alpha_i$ (or scalar intensity $I_i$) derived from the transfer function [S1, S2, unofficial]. The schemes differ solely in the accumulation function $F$ that maps the sequence of sample attributes $\{(C_i, \alpha_i)\}$ or $\{I_i\}$ to the final pixel value. No shared mathematical compositing formula exists across all children, as some (like MIP) discard opacity entirely, while others (like Accumulate) use the standard over operator.

## Subtopics

### Max Intensity Projection (MIP)
MIP projects the maximum intensity value found along the ray, ignoring opacity and color accumulation [S1, S2, unofficial]. It is primarily used for visualizing high-density structures such as blood vessels in Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA) [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Distinct from siblings:** Discards opacity and color blending; outputs a single scalar intensity mapped to color.
*   **When to choose:** When the goal is to highlight the brightest or most dense structures regardless of occlusion.
*   **Link:** [Max Intensity Projection](6305-projection-and-mip-techniques.md)

### Accumulate Projection
Accumulate projects the standard front-to-back compositing result, accumulating opacity and color to make transparent layers visible [S1, S2, unofficial]. This is the standard volume rendering output described in [Direct volume ray casting](19-direct-volume-ray-casting.md).
*   **Distinct from siblings:** Integrates both color and opacity using the over operator, preserving depth cues and transparency.
*   **When to choose:** For general-purpose volume visualization where internal structure and transparency are important.
*   **Link:** [Direct Volume Rendering](1184-direct-volume-rendering.md)

### Average Projection
Average projects the mean of the scalar values (or intensities) along the ray, producing an X-ray-like image [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Distinct from siblings:** Averages scalar values rather than integrating optical properties; does not account for opacity attenuation.
*   **When to choose:** When a global density overview is required, similar to a medical X-ray.
*   **Link:** [Projection and MIP Techniques](6305-projection-and-mip-techniques.md)

### Depth Projection
Depth projection outputs the distance $t$ to the first sample that meets a specific criterion (e.g., first non-zero opacity or intensity threshold) [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Distinct from siblings:** Outputs a depth value rather than a color/intensity; often used for segmentation or distance mapping.
*   **When to choose:** When surface location or depth information is the primary data of interest.
*   **Link:** [Surface Rendering Pipeline](2614-surface-rendering-pipeline.md)

### First Hit Projection
First hit outputs the color and opacity of the very first sample along the ray that is non-transparent [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Distinct from siblings:** Stops accumulation at the first visible sample; no blending of subsequent layers.
*   **When to choose:** For efficient surface rendering or when only the outermost layer is of interest.
*   **Link:** [Surface Rendering Pipeline](2614-surface-rendering-pipeline.md)

### Intensity Projection
Intensity projects the raw scalar value of the sample at a specific depth or the maximum intensity, depending on implementation context, often used as a base for other schemes [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Distinct from siblings:** May refer to simple scalar projection without compositing logic.
*   **When to choose:** For basic data inspection.
*   **Link:** [Projection and MIP Techniques](6305-projection-and-mip-techniques.md)

## Background
The classification of ray traversal schemes into categories like Intensity, Max, Average, Accumulate, First, and Depth was established in early volume rendering literature, notably by Levoy in 1988 [S1, S2, unofficial]. These schemes form the foundational aggregation steps in the volume rendering pipeline, preceding the final image pixel generation [S1, S2, unofficial].
