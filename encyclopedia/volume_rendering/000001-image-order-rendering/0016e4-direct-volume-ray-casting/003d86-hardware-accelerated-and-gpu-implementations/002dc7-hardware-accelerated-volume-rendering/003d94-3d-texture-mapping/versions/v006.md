# 3D Texture Mapping
Also called: 3D Texture Volume Rendering, Rendering Slices with 3D Texture Mapping, Texture-mapped volume rendering (3D texture), Voltx Method, Volume Rendering with Shading via Three-Dimensional Texture Maps, Volume Texturing
## Overview
3D Texture Mapping renders a volumetric dataset by storing it as a single 3D texture in GPU memory and resampling it using hardware-accelerated trilinear interpolation on planar polygons [S1, unofficial][S2, unofficial]. It solves the performance bottleneck of software ray casting by offloading interpolation and sampling to the graphics hardware's texture engine [S2, unofficial]. Unlike 2D texture stack approaches, it allows arbitrarily small slice distances and avoids popping artifacts caused by texture stack switching [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs and outputs**: Inputs are the volume dataset (dimensions $n_x, n_y, n_z$, voxel spacing $\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$) and viewing parameters (rotation matrix $R$) [S2, unofficial]. The output is the rendered image.
**Data structures**: The volume is stored in a 3D texture of resolution $(N_x, N_y, N_z)$, where $N_x, N_y, N_z$ are the smallest powers of two greater than or equal to $n_x, n_y, n_z$ [S2, unofficial].
**Steps**:
1. Compute the bounding cube side length $d = \sqrt{L_x^2 + L_y^2 + L_z^2}$, where $L_x = N_x \Delta x$, $L_y = N_y \Delta y$, $L_z = N_z \Delta z$ [S2, unofficial].
2. Define the texture coordinate mapping for the volume's original orientation: $s = (x + \frac{1}{2}n_x\Delta x)/(N_x\Delta x)$, $t = (y + \frac{1}{2}n_y\Delta y)/(N_y\Delta y)$, $r = (z + \frac{1}{2}n_z\Delta z)/(N_z\Delta z)$ [S2, unofficial].
3. For arbitrary viewing angles, keep the bounding cube stationary in screen space and rotate the texture-space coordinates of the slicing planes' corners using the inverse rotation matrix $R^{-1}$ [S2, unofficial].
4. Render the volume by applying the 3D texture to parallel planes (squares in screen-space), building a stack of slabs drawn back-to-front [S2, unofficial].
5. Composite the interpolated textures in depth order [S1, unofficial].
**Formulas**: 
Bounding cube side length: $d = \sqrt{L_x^2 + L_y^2 + L_z^2}$ [S2, unofficial].
Scaled dimensions: $L_x = N_x \Delta x$, $L_y = N_y \Delta y$, $L_z = N_z \Delta z$ [S2, unofficial].
Texture coordinate mapping: $s = (x + \frac{1}{2}n_x\Delta x)/(N_x\Delta x)$, $t = (y + \frac{1}{2}n_y\Delta y)/(N_y\Delta y)$, $r = (z + \frac{1}{2}n_z\Delta z)/(N_z\Delta z)$ [S2, unofficial].
Transformation for arbitrary angles: $(s, t, r) = (x, y, z) R^{-1} D^{-1} S T = (x, y, z) D^{-1} R^{-1} S T$, where $D$ is a uniform scale by $d$, $S$ is a non-uniform scale by $(d/L_x, d/L_y, d/L_z)$, $T$ is a translation, and $R$ is the client's rotation matrix [S2, unofficial].
**Parameters**: Texture map resolution $(N_x, N_y, N_z)$ and bounding cube corners $(\pm d, \pm d, \pm d)$ [S2, unofficial].
**Edge cases and pitfalls**: Non-uniform scaling $S$ does not commute with rotation. The correct order of operations on texture coordinates is rotation, then scaling, to avoid shearing distortion [S2, unofficial]. Planar regions outside the volume must be handled to prevent rendering artifacts [S2, unofficial].
## Subtopics

### [Hardware-Accelerated Volume Rendering via 3D Texturing](2799-hardware-accelerated-volume-rendering-via-3d-texturing.md)
Renders volumetric datasets by storing them as 3D textures and resampling via hardware-accelerated trilinear interpolation on planar polygons. Sets it apart by performing trilinear interpolation followed by classification and compositing, avoiding the final warp step of Shear-Warp. Choose for perspective projections requiring consistent performance and higher image quality.

### [3D Pixel Texture Classification](6055-3d-pixel-texture-classification.md)
Applies multi-dimensional transfer functions during direct volume ray casting by encoding classification parameters into a 3D texture lookup. Sets it apart by explicitly incorporating boundary information (via the second derivative) into the hardware interpolation pipeline for precise material boundary control. Choose when high-dimensional transfer functions are needed without complex shader logic.

### [Hardware Shading via Pixel Texture](6056-hardware-shading-via-pixel-texture.md)
Integrates surface shading into hardware-accelerated volume rendering by encoding quantized surface normals into a 2D pixel texture. Sets it apart by using nearest-neighbor interpolation on a dedicated 2D texture to retrieve lighting information, avoiding redundancy and truncation errors of standard 3D normal interpolation. Choose when geometric shading is required for texture-memory based rendering.

### Texture Mapping Volume Rendering Optimization
Reduces texture-mapped polygons to decrease the number of samples taken along rays, achieving faster rendering speeds [S3, book]. Choose when texture download rates are a bottleneck or interactive performance is prioritized over maximum fidelity [S3, book].

## Background
The first implementation was devised by Cabral et al. and ran on SGI Reality Engine workstations [S1, unofficial]. VanGelder and Kim introduced a fast technique to pre-shade the volume on the fly before slicing and compositing [S1, unofficial]. Meißner et al. enabled direct diffuse illumination for semi-transparent volume rendering, though multiple rasterization passes caused performance loss [S1, unofficial]. Dachille et al. proposed a one-pass approach to mitigate this [S1, unofficial].
