# Near Plane Clipping Correction

Also called: Camera Inside Volume Handling, Inside-Volume Volume Rendering via Backface Raycasting, Near Clipping Plane Hole Filling

## Overview
Near Plane Clipping Correction is a technique for rendering volumetric data when the camera viewpoint is located inside the volume bounds, a scenario where standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume, creating holes in the ray entry data [S1, S2, unofficial]. It extends the image-based ray setup by intersecting rays with explicit polygonal geometry to correctly determine entry points and clip internal surfaces, allowing for hybrid rendering of isosurfaces and direct volume rendering [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** Camera position in world coordinates, volume bounding box (defined by axes and sizes), RayData texture (precomputed ray entry points and marching distances), near clipping plane geometry.
*   **Output:** Corrected ray origins and entry distances for pixels where the camera is inside the volume or the near plane clips the front faces.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Volume Box Matrix ($M_{vol}$):** A $4 \times 4$ matrix defining the local coordinate system of the volume.
    *   Columns 0-2: `vec4(axis_direction * length, 0)` for x, y, and z axes respectively.
    *   Column 3: `vec4(tx, ty, tz, 1)` representing the box position.
*   **Inverse Volume Box Matrix ($M_{inv}$):** The inverse of $M_{vol}$, used to transform world coordinates to the local unit cube $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$.
*   **RayData Texture:** A texture storing ray entry positions and depths. Cleared to zero before rendering.
*   **AABB Structure:**
    ```glsl
    struct AABB {
        vec3 min; // (0,0,0)
        vec3 max; // (1,1,1)
    };
    struct Ray {
        vec3 origin;
        vec3 dir;
    };
    ```

**Numbered Steps:**

1.  **Coordinate System Transformation:**
    *   Construct $M_{vol}$ using the volume box axes and their lengths. The last column is the box position $(tx, ty, tz, 1)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute $M_{inv} = M_{vol}^{-1}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Transform the camera position from world coordinates to the local unit cube coordinate system:
        $$ \mathbf{p}_{local} = M_{inv} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_{cam} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
        The resulting $\mathbf{p}_{local}$ is passed to the shader as `LOCAL_CAM_POS` [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Marking Clipped Regions (Back-face Rasterization):**
    *   Clear the RayData texture to zero [S2, unofficial].
    *   Render only the **back faces** of the volume bounding box (vertices at $(0,0,0)$ to $(1,1,1)$ in the local system) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Pass the interpolated local unit texture coordinates (`vLocalUnitTexCoord`) to the shader [S1, unofficial].
    *   In the fragment shader, write a **negative color value** into the **green channel** of the RayData texture for these pixels [S2, unofficial]. This marks pixels where back faces were rendered but front faces were clipped by the near plane [S2, unofficial].

3.  **Rasterization Sequence for Depth Correction (Optional/Alternative Approach):**
    *   *Note: [S3, unofficial] describes a multi-pass rasterization approach to fill holes using depth buffer information.*
    *   **Step 3.1:** Disable depth buffer, enable color buffer. Rasterize the entire near clipping plane [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Step 3.2:** Enable depth buffer, disable color buffer. Rasterize the nearest back faces of active bricks [S3, unofficial].
    *   **Step 3.3:** Enable depth buffer, enable color buffer. Rasterize the nearest front faces of active bricks [S3, unofficial].

4.  **Shader Correction for Marked Pixels:**
    *   Identify pixels where the green channel is negative (indicating clipped front faces) [S2, unofficial].
    *   For these pixels, explicitly initialize the ray entry position to the texture-space position of the corresponding point on the near plane [S2, unofficial].
    *   Correct the depth value read from the RayData texture by subtracting the distance from the eye to the corresponding point on the near plane [S2, unofficial].

5.  **Ray Setup and Marching Start Position:**
    *   Define the ray origin as `LOCAL_CAM_POS` [S1, unofficial].
    *   Define the ray direction as the normalized vector from the camera to the back-face vertex coordinate:
        $$ \mathbf{r}_{dir} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{v}_{LocalUnitTexCoord} - \mathbf{LOCAL\_CAM\_POS}) $$
    *   Calculate the entry distance $t$ to the unit AABB $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$ using the slab method [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ \mathbf{r}_{InvDir} = \frac{1.0}{\mathbf{r}_{dir}} $$
        $$ \mathbf{t}_{Minima} = (\mathbf{b}_{min} - \mathbf{r}_{origin}) \cdot \mathbf{r}_{InvDir} $$
        $$ \mathbf{t}_{Maxima} = (\mathbf{b}_{max} - \mathbf{r}_{origin}) \cdot \mathbf{r}_{InvDir} $$
        $$ \mathbf{t}_{Entries} = \min(\mathbf{t}_{Minima}, \mathbf{t}_{Maxima}) $$
        $$ t_{MaxEntry} = \max(\max(\mathbf{t}_{Entries}.xy), \max(\mathbf{t}_{Entries}.yz)) $$
        *(Note: The source code uses `vec2(tEntries.st)` and `vec2(tEntries.pp)` which corresponds to `xy` and `yz` components in GLSL, effectively finding the maximum of the minimums to determine the first real entry value)* [S1, unofficial].
    *   Determine the start position for the raymarching loop by mixing between the camera position and the front-face intersection point based on the sign of $t$ [S1, unofficial]:
        $$ \mathbf{p}_{front} = \mathbf{r}_{origin} + \mathbf{r}_{dir} \cdot t $$
        $$ \mathbf{useFrontCoord} = 0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \text{sign}(t) $$
        $$ \mathbf{startPos} = \text{mix}(\mathbf{LOCAL\_CAM\_POS}, \mathbf{p}_{front}, \mathbf{useFrontCoord}) $$
        If $t > 0$ (camera outside), use the front face intersection; if $t \le 0$ (camera inside), use the camera position [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
# GLSL-like pseudocode for the fragment shader

struct AABB {
    vec3 min;
    vec3 max;
};

struct Ray {
    vec3 origin;
    vec3 dir;
};

// Helper: Get entry distance to unit AABB
float getUnitAABBEntry(Ray r) {
    AABB b;
    b.min = vec3(0.0);
    b.max = vec3(1.0);
    
    vec3 rInvDir = vec3(1.0) / r.dir;
    vec3 tMinima = (b.min - r.origin) * rInvDir;
    vec3 tMaxima = (b.max - r.origin) * rInvDir;
    
    vec3 tEntries = min(tMinima, tMaxima);
    
    // Find first real entry value
    // Note: Source uses .st and .pp (which is .yz in GLSL)
    vec2 tMaxEntryCandidates = max(vec2(tEntries.x, tEntries.y), vec2(tEntries.y, tEntries.z));
    float tMaxEntry = max(tMaxEntryCandidates.x, tMaxEntryCandidates.y);
    
    return tMaxEntry;
}

// Helper: Determine closer position based on t sign
vec3 getCloserPos(vec3 camera, vec3 frontFaceIntersection, float t) {
    float useFrontCoord = 0.5 + 0.5 * sign(t);
    return mix(camera, frontFaceIntersection, useFrontCoord);
}

void main() {
    // Inputs
    vec3 LOCAL_CAM_POS; // Uniform: Camera position in local unit cube
    vec3 vLocalUnitTexCoord; // Varying: Back-face interpolated coordinate
    
    // Ray Setup
    Ray r;
    r.origin = LOCAL_CAM_POS;
    r.dir = normalize(vLocalUnitTexCoord - LOCAL_CAM_POS);
    
    // Calculate entry distance to unit AABB
    float t = getUnitAABBEntry(r);
    
    // Front face intersection point in local coordinates
    vec3 frontFaceLocalUnitTexCoord = r.origin + r.dir * t;
    
    // Determine start position
    vec3 startPos = getCloserPos(LOCAL_CAM_POS, frontFaceLocalUnitTexCoord, t);
    
    // Correction for clipped regions (if green channel is negative in RayData)
    // If marked, override startPos with near plane position and correct depth
    // This logic is typically handled by checking the RayData texture's green channel
    // For the core ray setup, startPos is used as the beginning of the march
    
    vec3 start = startPos;
    vec3 end = vLocalUnitTexCoord;
    
    // Raymarching loop would follow here
    // ...
}
```

**Parameters with Typical Values:**
*   **Volume Box:** Defined by axes and lengths. The local coordinate system maps to $(0,0,0)-(1,1,1)$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **RayData Texture:** Cleared to zero before rendering [S2, unofficial].
*   **Green Channel Marker:** Negative value used to indicate clipped front faces [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Inside Volume:** Standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces, creating holes in the ray entry data [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** The slab method for AABB intersection must handle cases where ray direction components are zero or near-zero to avoid division by zero or precision issues [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If the camera is exactly on the boundary of the volume, the sign of $t$ determines the behavior. The `mix` function with `sign(t)` handles this by preferring the camera position if $t \le 0$ [S1, unofficial].

This method enables volume ray casting when the camera viewpoint is located inside the volume (e.g., virtual colonoscopy), a scenario where standard bounding-box ray setups fail because the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume, creating holes in the ray entry data. It extends the image-based ray setup by intersecting rays with explicit polygonal geometry to correctly determine entry points and clip internal surfaces, allowing for hybrid rendering of isosurfaces and direct volume rendering.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The correction adds a constant number of rasterization passes (back faces, near plane, front faces) and a simple shader calculation per pixel. The complexity is dominated by the raymarching loop, which is unchanged.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires the RayData texture to store entry points and depths, plus additional buffers for the multi-pass rasterization if the [S3, unofficial] approach is used.
*   **Measured Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Scaling:** Scales with the resolution of the RayData texture and the number of samples in the raymarching loop.
*   **Strengths:** Correctly handles camera positions inside the volume and near-plane clipping artifacts, enabling immersive viewing experiences like virtual colonoscopy [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires explicit rasterization of the volume bounding box and near clipping plane. May introduce artifacts if the near plane is very close to the camera or if the volume geometry is complex [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When the camera viewpoint is inside the volume or when the near clipping plane intersects the front faces of the volume [S1, S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the camera is always outside the volume and the near plane does not intersect the front faces [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Background
*   **Origin:** The technique is described in the context of GPU-based volume raycasting, particularly for handling fluid volumes and immersive viewpoints [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **History and Development:** The method is detailed in "Real-Time Simulation and Rendering of 3D Fluids" (GPU Gems 3) and "Advanced Illumination Techniques for GPU-Based Volume Raycasting" [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Where it is Used:** Virtual colonoscopy, immersive fluid visualization, and any application requiring camera movement inside volumetric data [S1, S3, unofficial].
