# Volume Ray Casting with RayData Texture

Also called: Deferred Volume Rendering, GS Raymarch Pass, Ray-marching pixel shader, Raymarching Pass, Volume Ray Marching, Volume Ray-Casting (Simple), Volume Raycasting Pass

## Overview
This method renders a 3D volume texture by executing a geometry shader (or pre-pass) to generate ray entry/exit bounds and a fragment shader to perform front-to-back ray marching and compositing [S1, S2, unofficial]. It solves the problem of determining ray intersection points and marching distances for volume rendering without performing expensive ray-cube intersection calculations in the final rendering pass [S2, S3, unofficial]. It differs from [Bounding Box Raymarching](2071-bounding-box-raymarching.md) by explicitly storing ray entry coordinates and traversal distances in a dedicated "RayData" texture (or separate front/back position textures) during a pre-pass, allowing the final pass to simply sample these values rather than computing them from geometry [S2, S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A 3D texture containing scalar volume data (normalized to [0,1]) [S3, unofficial]. A camera view matrix and projection matrix [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A 2D render target containing the final composited color and opacity [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Volume Texture (`mVolume`):** A 3D texture of dimensions $(W, H, D)$, format `SurfaceFormat.Single` (float) or `DXGI_FORMAT_R16G16B16A16_FLOAT` for high precision [S1, S3, unofficial]. Data is loaded from RAW files (8-bit or 16-bit) and scaled by dividing by `byte.MaxValue` (255) or `ushort.MaxValue` [S3, unofficial].
2.  **RayData Texture (`mFarNearTex` / `mFront` / `mBack`):**
    *   Format: `DXGI_FORMAT_R16G16B16A16_FLOAT` [S1, unofficial].
    *   In the [S2, unofficial] variant: A single texture where RGB stores the texture-space entry point and A stores the marching distance (depth through volume) [S2, unofficial].
    *   In the [S3, unofficial] variant: Two separate 2D textures, `mFront` and `mBack`, storing the world/texture-space positions of the front and back faces of the bounding cube [S3, unofficial].
3.  **Constant Buffer (`mCBperFrame`):** Contains view/projection matrices and frame-specific parameters [S1, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Volume Loading:** Load scalar data into a 3D texture. Fit a bounding box/cube from $[0,0,0]$ to $[1,1,1]$ to the volume space [S3, unofficial].

2.  **Pre-pass (Ray Data Generation):**
    *   *Variant A (RayData Texture [S2, unofficial]):*
        1.  Clear `mFarNearTex` to zero.
        2.  Draw back faces of the simulation volume (six quads) with a shader. Output the distance from the eye to the fragment (view space) into the Alpha channel [S2, unofficial].
        3.  Draw front faces with a shader. Output texture-space coordinates into the RGB channel. Use **subtractive blending** to compute depth:
            $$OutputColor.rgb = SourceColor.rgb$$
            $$OutputColor.a = DestinationColor.a - SourceColor.a$$
            This results in the Alpha channel storing the distance between front and back faces (marching distance) [S2, unofficial].
    *   *Variant B (Front/Back Textures [S3, unofficial]):*
        1.  Clear `mFront` to Black.
        2.  Draw front-facing triangles of the cube to `mFront`, storing pixel positions [S3, unofficial].
        3.  Draw back-facing triangles of the cube to `mBack`, storing pixel positions [S3, unofficial].

3.  **Rendering Pass (Ray Marching):**
    1.  Bind the volume texture sampler (`m_pGenSampler`) with trilinear filtering and clamped addressing [S1, unofficial].
    2.  Bind the Geometry Shader (if generating rays on-the-fly) and the Ray Casting Pixel Shader [S1, unofficial].
    3.  Bind the RayData texture (or `mFront`/`mBack`) and the Volume Texture as shader resources [S1, S3, unofficial].
    4.  Execute a single draw call (e.g., `Draw(1, 0)` for a full-screen quad or geometry) [S1, unofficial].

    **Pixel Shader Logic (per pixel):**
    1.  **Retrieve Ray Info:**
        *   *Variant A:* Sample `RayData` texture. Entry point $P_{entry}$ is in RGB. Marching distance $L$ is in A [S2, unofficial].
        *   *Variant B:* Calculate projective texture coordinates from input position:
            $$texC.x = 0.5 \times \frac{input.pos.x}{input.pos.w} + 0.5$$
            $$texC.y = -0.5 \times \frac{input.pos.y}{input.pos.w} + 0.5$$
            Sample `mFront` and `mBack` using $texC$ to get $P_{front}$ and $P_{back}$ [S3, unofficial].
    2.  **Calculate Ray Direction:**
        *   *Variant A:* $Dir = normalize(P_{entry} - Eye)$ (both in texture space) [S2, unofficial].
        *   *Variant B:* $Dir = normalize(P_{back} - P_{front})$ [S3, unofficial].
    3.  **Initialize:**
        *   Current position $Pos = P_{entry}$ (or $P_{front}$) [S2, S3, unofficial].
        *   Destination color $Dst = (0, 0, 0, 0)$ [S3, unofficial].
        *   Step size $StepSize$ (typically half a voxel) [S2, unofficial].
        *   Step vector $Step = Dir \times StepSize$ [S3, unofficial].
    4.  **Marching Loop:**
        For $i = 0$ to $Iterations$:
        1.  Sample volume scalar value $V$ at $Pos$ using `tex3Dlod` [S3, unofficial].
        2.  Map scalar to source color/alpha. Example: $Src.a = V \times 0.5$ (for transparency), $Src.rgb = V$ [S3, unofficial].
        3.  **Front-to-Back Blending:**
            $$Src.rgb \times= Src.a$$
            $$Dst = (1.0 - Dst.a) \times Src + Dst$$
            $$Dst.a += Src.a \times (1.0 - Dst.a)$$
        4.  **Early Termination:** If $Dst.a \ge 0.95$ (or $0.99$), break loop [S2, S3, unofficial].
        5.  **Advance Position:** $Pos.xyz += Step$ [S3, unofficial].
        6.  **Boundary Check:** If $Pos.x > 1.0$ OR $Pos.y > 1.0$ OR $Pos.z > 1.0$, break loop [S3, unofficial].

4.  **Cleanup:** Unbind shader resources [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Step Size:** Equal to half a voxel [S2, unofficial].
*   **Iterations:** Proportional to marching distance. Not explicitly defined as a constant in [S3, unofficial], but loop condition is `i < Iterations` [S3, unofficial].
*   **Opacity Threshold:** 0.95 [S3, unofficial] or 0.99 [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Camera Inside Volume:** If the camera is inside the fluid, the near plane clips front faces, leading to incorrect entry points [S2, unofficial].
    *   *Fix:* Mark pixels where back faces were rendered but front faces were not (by writing negative green channel during back-face pass). In the shader, if green < 0, initialize $Pos$ to the near plane position and subtract the near plane distance from the depth [S2, unofficial].
*   **Scene Occlusion:** Rays may march through volume parts occluded by scene geometry [S2, unofficial].
    *   *Fix:* Compare front-face distance to scene depth. If front-face distance > scene distance, output a large negative red value in the RayData pre-pass to ensure no samples are taken [S2, unofficial].
*   **Banding:** Integral number of equally spaced samples causes banding artifacts [S2, unofficial].
    *   *Fix:* Take one additional sample. Weight its contribution by $d / sampleWidth$, where $d$ is the difference between scene distance and total distance traveled, and $sampleWidth$ is the step size [S2, unofficial].
*   **Hardware Requirements:** Requires Shader Model 3.0 and support for RGBA16F textures [S3, unofficial].
*   **Compiler Warnings:** Older DirectX SDK fxc compilers may return error exit codes on warnings (X4121). Fix by checking only for "error" string in exit code logic [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Scene Depth Clipping for Volume Occlusion
Modifies ray marching to stop when rays hit scene geometry or are occluded by it, preventing rendering of volume parts behind obstacles [S2, unofficial]. It calculates scene distance by reverse-projecting scene depth to view space [S2, unofficial]. The ray marching limit is the minimum of the back-face distance (from RayData) and the scene distance [S2, unofficial]. If the front face is occluded, a large negative red value is written to RayData to skip sampling [S2, unofficial]. Pitfall: If the depth buffer cannot be read as a texture (e.g., MSAA in D3D10), scene distance must be computed in the main scene pass using multiple render targets [S2, unofficial].

### Near Plane Clipping Correction
Handles cases where the camera is inside the fluid volume and the near plane clips the front faces, causing incorrect ray entry points [S2, unofficial]. It marks clipped regions by writing a negative value to the green channel during the back-face pre-pass [S2, unofficial]. In the shader, pixels with negative green are identified, and the ray entry position is explicitly initialized to the near plane's texture-space position, with the depth corrected by subtracting the eye-to-near-plane distance [S2, unofficial].

### Banding Suppression via Weighted Sampling
Suppresses banding artifacts caused by integral numbers of equally spaced samples, especially with rapidly changing depth values [S2, unofficial]. It takes one additional sample beyond the calculated number. The additional sample's contribution is weighted by $d / sampleWidth$, where $d$ is the difference between the scene distance and the total distance traveled by the ray at the last sample, and $sampleWidth$ is the typical step size [S2, unofficial]. Pitfall: Banding may remain with high-frequency density variations; solutions include jittering or higher-order filters [S2, unofficial]. Hellgate: London used trilinear jittered sampling at twice per voxel [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \times M)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $M$ is the number of samples per ray (proportional to volume thickness) [S2, S3, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(W \times H)$ for the RayData/Position textures plus $O(W_{vol} \times H_{vol} \times D_{vol})$ for the volume texture [S1, S3, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Fast due to pre-computed ray bounds and early termination [S2, unofficial]. Requires Shader Model 3.0 hardware [S3, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Simple implementation; efficient early termination; handles occlusion and near-plane clipping with extensions [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Banding artifacts; requires high-precision render targets (RGBA16F) [S2, S3, unofficial]; performance degrades with thick volumes or high opacity [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** Real-time fluid rendering where performance is critical and volume density is relatively uniform [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** High-frequency density variations requiring high sampling rates; hardware lacking RGBA16F support [S2, S3, unofficial].

## Background
Originated in real-time fluid simulation rendering, specifically described in *GPU Gems 3* (Scharsach 2005) and implemented in games like *Hellgate: London* [S2, unofficial]. The technique was also independently documented in XNA-based tutorials (Kyle Hayward, 2009) [S3, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of deferred shading by pre-computing ray parameters in a geometry/fragment shader pass [S1, S2, unofficial].
