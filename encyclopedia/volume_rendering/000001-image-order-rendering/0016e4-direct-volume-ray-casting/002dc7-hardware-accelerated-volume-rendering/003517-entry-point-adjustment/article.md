# Entry Point Adjustment

Also called: Flowing through volume, Inside-volume rendering, Raycasting Volume Rendering with Entry Point Adjustment

## Overview
Entry Point Adjustment is a hardware-accelerated volume rendering technique that enables the camera to move inside the volume's bounding box without the view disappearing or clipping artifacts. It solves the problem of ray termination when the camera position exceeds the front face of the volume by dynamically shifting the ray's entry point forward along the ray direction based on a scale coefficient. Unlike standard direct volume ray casting which assumes the camera is outside the volume, this method maintains visual continuity by interpolating the entry point as the camera penetrates the volume boundary.

## How it works
The method extends the standard two-pass hardware-accelerated volume rendering pipeline. It requires an additional rendering pass to capture exit points and a modified fragment shader to handle dynamic entry point adjustment.

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `VolumeTex`: A 3D texture (`sampler3D`) containing the scalar volume data.
    *   `TransferFunc`: A 1D texture (`sampler1D`) mapping scalar values to color and opacity.
    *   `ExitPoints`: A 2D texture (`sampler2D`) storing the exit coordinates of rays passing through the back face of the volume bounding box.
    *   `ScreenSize`: A uniform `vec2` representing the resolution of the framebuffer.
    *   `StepSize`: A uniform `float` controlling the distance between samples along the ray.
    *   `AlphaReduce`: A uniform `float` multiplier applied to alpha to increase transparency.
    *   `scaleCoeff`: A uniform `float` representing the scaling factor of the volume, used to determine if the camera is inside the volume.
    *   `EntryPoint`: A varying `vec3` passed from the vertex shader, representing the intersection of the ray with the front face of the volume bounding box in world/object space.
*   **Outputs:**
    *   `FragColor`: A `vec4` representing the final composited color and accumulated opacity for the pixel.

### Data Structures
1.  **ExitPoints Texture:** A 2D texture where each texel stores the 3D exit coordinate of the ray passing through the back face of the volume. The coordinate is stored in the RGB channels. The texture is generated by rendering the back face of the volume's bounding box into a framebuffer object (FBO) with a color attachment of this texture. The vertex shader for this pass outputs the vertex position as the color.
2.  **Volume Texture:** Standard 3D texture (`sampler3D`) with trilinear interpolation enabled.
3.  **Transfer Function:** Standard 1D texture (`sampler1D`) with linear interpolation.

### Steps
1.  **Back-Face Rendering (Pre-pass):**
    *   Bind the `ExitPoints` FBO.
    *   Render the back face of the volume's bounding box (a cube or box geometry).
    *   The vertex shader outputs the vertex position (which corresponds to the exit point of the ray for the corresponding screen pixel) as the fragment color.
    *   Discard the depth buffer or disable depth testing to ensure all back-face vertices are written.

2.  **Front-Face Rendering (Main Pass):**
    *   Bind the default framebuffer (screen).
    *   Render the front face of the volume's bounding box.
    *   The vertex shader outputs the vertex position (entry point) as a varying `EntryPoint`.
    *   The fragment shader performs the ray marching and compositing.

3.  **Fragment Shader Execution:**
    *   Retrieve the exit point for the current pixel from the `ExitPoints` texture using the screen coordinates normalized by `ScreenSize`.
    *   **Discard Check:** If the `EntryPoint` equals the `exitPoint`, discard the fragment. This indicates the ray does not pass through the volume or the camera is positioned such that no volume is visible.
    *   **Entry Point Adjustment:**
        *   If `scaleCoeff` $\ge 2.7$, calculate a temporary shift factor `tmp`:
            $$
            tmp = \min((scaleCoeff - 2.7) \times 0.1, 1.0)
            $$
        *   Adjust the entry point:
            $$
            entryPoint = EntryPoint + tmp \times (exitPoint - EntryPoint)
            $$
        *   If `scaleCoeff` $< 2.7$, use the original `EntryPoint` as the `entryPoint`.
    *   **Ray Initialization:**
        *   Calculate the ray direction:
            $$
            rayDirection = \text{normalize}(exitPoint - entryPoint)
            $$
        *   Initialize current position:
            $$
            currentPosition = vec4(entryPoint, 0.0)
            $$
        *   Initialize accumulation variables:
            $$
            colorSum = vec4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
            $$
            $$
            color = vec4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
            $$
            $$
            value = vec4(0.0)
            $$
        *   Calculate step vector and total length:
            $$
            Step = rayDirection \times StepSize
            $$
            $$
            stepLength = \text{length}(Step)
            $$
            $$
            Length = \text{length}(exitPoint - entryPoint)
            $$
            $$
            LengthSum = 0.0
            $$
    *   **Ray Marching Loop:**
        *   Iterate up to 16,000 times (or until termination conditions are met).
        *   Sample the volume texture at `currentPosition.xyz` to get `value`.
        *   Sample the transfer function using `value.a` to get `color`.
        *   Reduce alpha:
            $$
            color.a = color.a \times AlphaReduce
            $$
        *   Front-to-back blending:
            $$
            color.rgb = color.rgb \times color.a
            $$
            $$
            colorSum = (1.0 - colorSum.a) \times color + colorSum
            $$
        *   Accumulate length:
            $$
            LengthSum = LengthSum + stepLength
            $$
        *   **Early Ray Termination (ERT):** If `colorSum.a` $\ge 0.95$, break the loop.
        *   Advance position:
            $$
            currentPosition.xyz = currentPosition.xyz + Step
            $$
        *   **Boundary Check:** If `LengthSum` $\ge Length$, break the loop.
    *   **Output:** Write `colorSum` to `FragColor`.

### Pseudocode
> **Unverified code.** The code below did not pass a test run against a reference implementation, or no test fits it, so it may contain errors.

```python
# Fragment Shader Logic
def fragment_shader(entry_point: vec3, exit_point_coord: vec4, screen_size: vec2, 
                    exit_points_tex: sampler2D, volume_tex: sampler3D, 
                    transfer_func: sampler1D, step_size: float, 
                    alpha_reduce: float, scale_coeff: float) -> vec4:
    
    # 1. Retrieve exit point from texture
    uv = exit_point_coord.xy / screen_size
    exit_point = texture(exit_points_tex, uv).xyz
    
    # 2. Discard if no volume intersection
    if entry_point == exit_point:
        discard()
        
    # 3. Entry Point Adjustment
    if scale_coeff >= 2.7:
        tmp = min((scale_coeff - 2.7) * 0.1, 1.0)
        entry_point = entry_point + tmp * (exit_point - entry_point)
    # else: entry_point remains unchanged
        
    # 4. Ray Initialization
    ray_direction = normalize(exit_point - entry_point)
    current_position = vec4(entry_point, 0.0)
    color_sum = vec4(0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
    
    step = ray_direction * step_size
    step_length = length(step)
    total_length = length(exit_point - entry_point)
    length_sum = 0.0
    
    # 5. Ray Marching
    for i in range(16000):
        # Sample volume
        value = texture(volume_tex, current_position.xyz)
        
        # Sample transfer function
        color = texture(transfer_func, value.a)
        
        # Adjust alpha
        color.a *= alpha_reduce
        
        # Front-to-back blending
        color.rgb *= color.a
        color_sum = (1.0 - color_sum.a) * color + color_sum
        
        # Accumulate length
        length_sum += step_length
        
        # Early Ray Termination
        if color_sum.a >= 0.95:
            break
            
        # Advance position
        current_position.xyz += step
        
        # Boundary Check
        if length_sum >= total_length:
            break
            
    return color_sum
```

### Parameters
*   `StepSize`: Controls the resolution of the ray march. Smaller values increase quality but decrease performance.
*   `AlphaReduce`: Reduces the effective opacity of samples, allowing deeper penetration of the volume visually.
*   `scaleCoeff`: Determines the degree of entry point shift. Values $\ge 2.7$ trigger the adjustment. The specific threshold (2.7) and scaling factor (0.1) are empirical values from the source implementation [S1, unofficial].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Precision:** Floating-point precision errors in `EntryPoint == exitPoint` comparison may cause incorrect discarding. Use a small epsilon for comparison if necessary [G].
*   **Camera Position:** The method assumes the camera moves along the ray direction. If the camera moves laterally, the entry point adjustment may not correctly simulate "flowing through" the volume without additional geometric adjustments [S1, unofficial].
*   **Back-Face Rendering:** The back-face rendering pass must correctly output the exit points. If the bounding box is not axis-aligned or is transformed, the vertex positions must be in the correct space (typically world or view space) to match the ray generation in the main pass [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \times M)$, where $N$ is the number of pixels and $M$ is the number of samples per ray (up to 16,000, but typically much lower due to ERT).
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(V)$ for the volume texture and $O(P)$ for the exit points texture, where $V$ is the volume data size and $P$ is the pixel count.
*   **Performance:** The method adds one additional rendering pass (back-face rendering) and a small amount of computation in the fragment shader (entry point adjustment). The impact on performance is minimal compared to the ray marching cost.
*   **Strengths:** Enables seamless camera movement inside the volume without visual discontinuities. Simple to implement on standard GPU hardware.
*   **Limitations:** The entry point adjustment is an empirical approximation and may not perfectly simulate the optical properties of being inside a volume (e.g., occlusion from all sides). The specific parameters (2.7, 0.1) are not derived from first principles and may require tuning for different scenes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The method originates from practical implementations of hardware-accelerated volume rendering where users desired the ability to "fly through" volumetric data. It was discussed in the context of OpenGL/GLSL implementations, specifically addressing the limitation of standard ray casting where the view disappears when the camera passes the front face of the volume [S1, unofficial]. The specific implementation details, including the threshold of 2.7 and the linear interpolation factor, come from a community-driven solution shared on Stack Overflow [S1, unofficial].
