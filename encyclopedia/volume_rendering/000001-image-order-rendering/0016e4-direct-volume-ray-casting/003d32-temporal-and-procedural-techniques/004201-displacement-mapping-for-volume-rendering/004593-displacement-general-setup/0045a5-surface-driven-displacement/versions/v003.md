# Surface Driven Displacement

Also called: Monochrome Texture Displacement

## Overview
Surface Driven Displacement shifts fluid points along the normal of their projection onto the isosurface of a defined Surface channel, using a monochrome texture map to determine displacement magnitude [S1]. Unlike Gradient-driven displacement, this method ensures points above and below the surface are displaced in the same direction, producing results more similar to displaced solid geometry [S1]. It is distinct from [Vector Displacement](11532-vector-displacement.md), which uses a colored vector texture [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
1. Volumetric simulation data containing a defined "Surface" channel [S1].
2. A monochrome texture map [S1].

**Outputs:**
1. Displaced volumetric geometry suitable for rendering [S1].

**Data Structures:**
* **Surface Channel:** A scalar field within the simulation that defines the fluid's boundary for displacement [S1].
* **Monochrome Texture:** A texture where the displacement magnitude is encoded in the brightness at each coordinate [S1].
* Exact data structure layouts and bit packing are not given in the sources.

**Steps:**
1. For a given point $P$ in the fluid volume, find its projection $P_{proj}$ onto the isosurface of the Surface channel [S1].
2. Determine the surface normal $\mathbf{N}$ at $P_{proj}$ [S1].
3. Sample the monochrome texture at $P_{proj}$ to obtain the displacement magnitude $D$, which corresponds to the texture's brightness at that location [S1].
4. Shift the original point $P$ along $\mathbf{N}$ by distance $D$ to get the new position $P'$:
   $$ P' = P + D \cdot \mathbf{N} $$

**Parameters:**
* **Surface Channel Selection:** The user selects which scalar field defines the surface (e.g., Liquid, Smoke) [S1].
* **Texture Mapping:** The monochrome texture must be mapped to the simulation domain [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* **Performance:** This method is slower than Gradient-driven displacement because it requires finding the projection onto the isosurface and evaluating the normal at that projection point, rather than evaluating the gradient at the current point [S1].
* **Direction Consistency:** Unlike gradient-driven methods, this method ensures that points above and below the surface are displaced in the same direction relative to the surface normal, which is critical for smoke and fire simulations to maintain visual coherence [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Surface Channel Definition](12817-surface-channel-definition.md)
Specifies which data channel defines the fluid's surface geometry and the isosurface threshold. It is distinct from Gradient-driven displacement because it relies on a specific channel's scalar value rather than the gradient of the density field.

## Operating characteristics
* **Time Complexity:** Slower than Gradient-driven displacement due to the additional computation required to find the surface projection and normal [S1].
* **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the Surface channel data and the monochrome texture map [S1].
* **Strengths:** Produces displacement results more similar to displaced solid geometry surfaces; ensures consistent displacement direction for points above and below the surface, making it suitable for smoke and fire simulations [S1].
* **Limitations:** Computationally more expensive than Gradient-driven displacement [S1].
* **When to use:** When simulating smoke or fire where consistent surface-normal-based displacement is desired [S1].
* **When not to use:** When performance is critical and the directional variation of Gradient-driven displacement is acceptable [S1].

## Background
Surface Driven Displacement is a rendering technique implemented in Phoenix for 3ds Max [S1]. It is designed to handle fluid simulations like smoke and fire, where standard gradient-based displacement might produce visually inconsistent results due to varying local gradients [S1]. The method relies on a named simulation channel to define a clear boundary for displacement operations [S1].
