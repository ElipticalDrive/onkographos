# ROI-based High-Resolution Rendering
Also called: Multi-resolution volume rendering approach, Two-pass resolution rendering
## Overview
ROI-based High-Resolution Rendering is a rendering strategy to handle prohibitively large datasets by first rendering the entire volume at reduced resolution, then refining a user-selected region of interest (ROI) at full resolution [S1]. It addresses memory and compute constraints when full-resolution rendering exceeds hardware limits, such as tiled cryo-imaging datasets exceeding 60 GB [S1].
## How it works
**Inputs:**
1. A large volumetric dataset (e.g., tiled cryo-imaging data) [S1].
2. A user-defined Region of Interest (ROI) within the volume [S1].

**Outputs:**
1. A low-resolution visualization of the entire volume [S1].
2. A high-resolution visualization of the ROI [S1].

**Data Structures:**
Not given in the sources. [S1]

**Steps:**
1. Render the entire volume at reduced resolution [S1].
2. The user indicates a smaller ROI [S1].
3. Produce a high-resolution volume rendering for the selected ROI [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Region-Based Progressive Rendering](20668-region-based-progressive-rendering.md)
Region-based progressive rendering is a transmission and rendering strategy for foveated volume data that prioritizes a user-defined Region of Interest (ROI) by reconstructing and displaying it at high resolution first, then iteratively adding peripheral layers at decreasing resolutions. It differs from coarse-to-fine schemes by avoiding the interference of low-resolution coefficients on the high-resolution ROI, resulting in a clearer initial view of the area of interest at the cost of a longer initial wait time.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time and Memory Complexity:** Reduces initial memory and compute requirements by deferring high-resolution sampling to a smaller sub-volume [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** Designed for datasets exceeding 60 GB, such as tiled cryo-imaging data with a 4×5 tiling configuration and 40μm section thickness [S1].
- **Strengths:** Enables visualization of data sizes that are prohibitively large for full-resolution rendering on current graphics processors [S1].
- **Limitations:** Requires user interaction to define the ROI [S1].
- **When to Use:** When the dataset size exceeds available GPU memory and the user has a specific area of interest [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** Not given in the sources. [S1]

## Background
The method was developed to address the challenge of efficient handling of large data sets in volume visualization, specifically for color cryo-imaging data [S1]. It was introduced in the context of visualizing adult and embryonic mouse data sets, where tiled acquisition generates data volumes larger than 60 GB [S1]. The approach is part of a broader set of enhanced volume rendering techniques that include feature detectors and optical transfer functions (OTFs) for highlighting anatomical features based on color properties and gradients [S1].
