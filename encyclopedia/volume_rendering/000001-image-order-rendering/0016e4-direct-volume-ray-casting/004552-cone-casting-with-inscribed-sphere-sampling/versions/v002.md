# Cone Casting with Inscribed Sphere Sampling

Also called: Cone casting, MipNeRF Cone Sampling, Tri-MipRF Cone Casting

## Overview
Cone Casting with Inscribed Sphere Sampling is a rendering technique that formulates a pixel as a disc on the image plane and emits a cone from the camera's projection center to sample the volume, avoiding the aliasing artifacts of distance-agnostic ray casting [S1]. Unlike methods that sample a cone with anisotropic multivariate Gaussians [S1], this method uses spheres inscribed within the cone, which aligns with isotropic pre-filtering strategies in the underlying feature encoding [S1]. It is primarily used in conjunction with Tri-Mip encoding to achieve anti-aliased, high-fidelity renderings of neural radiance fields [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Camera parameters: optical center $\mathbf{o}$, focal length $f$, pixel width $\Delta x$ and height $\Delta y$ in world coordinates [S1].
*   Scene bounds: Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) of the 3D space [S1].
*   Tri-Mip encoding: Three 2D mipmap maps $\mathcal{M}$ (XY, YZ, XZ planes) [S1].
*   MLP: A shallow neural network with weights $\Theta$ [S1].
*   Sampling parameters: Base radius parameter $\beta$ (denoted as $\baseradius$ in source), near plane $t_n$, far plane $t_f$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
*   Final pixel color $C_{out}$ [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tri-Mip Maps:** Three 2D arrays storing pre-filtered 3D features. Each map corresponds to a projection plane (XY, YZ, XZ) and contains multiple mipmap levels [S1].
*   **Binary Occupancy Grid:** A coarse grid marking empty vs. non-empty space to skip samples [S1].
*   **Sphere Set $S$:** A list of spheres, each defined by center $\mathbf{x}$ and radius $r$ [S1].

**Steps:**

1.  **Pixel Disc Formulation:**
    Calculate the radius $\tilde{r}$ of the pixel disc on the image plane using the pixel dimensions in world coordinates:
    $$ \tilde{r} = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta x \cdot \Delta y}{\pi}} $$
    [S1]

2.  **Cone Emission:**
    Define the cone $C$ originating from the camera optical center $\mathbf{o}$. The central axis direction $\mathbf{d}$ is calculated from the optical center to the pixel center $\mathbf{p}_o$:
    $$ \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{p}_o - \mathbf{o} $$
    The central axis of the cone is parameterized as:
    $$ \mathbf{a}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t \mathbf{d} $$
    [S1]

3.  **Sampling Distance Selection:**
    Determine a set of distances $t_i$ along the central axis. These distances are typically uniformly sampled between the camera's near plane $t_n$ and far plane $t_f$, or between the intersections of the cone's central axis with the scene's AABB [S1].
    *   *Optimization:* Use the binary occupancy grid to skip distances $t_i$ where the sphere at that distance falls entirely within empty space [S1].

4.  **Sphere Parameterization:**
    For each distance $t_i$, calculate the sphere center $\mathbf{x}_i$ and inscribed radius $r_i$. The center lies on the central axis:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{o} + t_i \mathbf{d} $$
    The radius $r_i$ is calculated to ensure the sphere is inscribed within the cone at that distance. Let $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2$ denote the Euclidean norm. The formula is:
    $$ r_i = \frac{\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{o}\|_2 \cdot f \cdot \beta}{\|\mathbf{d}\|_2 \cdot \sqrt{\left(\sqrt{\|\mathbf{d}\|_2^2 - f^2} - \beta\right)^2 + f^2}} $$
    where $\beta$ is the base radius parameter [S1].

5.  **Featurization (Tri-Mip Encoding):**
    For each sphere $S_i(\mathbf{x}_i, r_i)$, extract a feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i$. This is done by orthogonally projecting the sphere onto the three Tri-Mip planes (XY, YZ, XZ). The sampling level (mipmap index) for each plane is determined by the sphere's occupied area on that plane, effectively performing area-based pre-filtering [S1].

6.  **Density and Color Prediction:**
    Feed the feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i$ and the view direction $\mathbf{d}$ into the tiny MLP to predict the density $\tau_i$ and color $\mathbf{c}_i$ for the sphere:
    $$ [\tau_i, \mathbf{c}_i] = \text{MLP}(\mathbf{f}_i, \mathbf{d}; \Theta) $$
    [S1]

7.  **Volume Rendering Integration:**
    Integrate the density and color of the sampled spheres using front-to-back volume rendering. The final color $C_{out}$ is computed as:
    $$ C_{out} = \sum_{i} T_i \left(1 - \exp(-\tau_i (t_{i+1} - t_i))\right) \mathbf{c}_i $$
    where the transmittance $T_i$ is:
    $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{k < i} \tau_k (t_{k+1} - t_k)\right) $$
    [S1]

**Pseudocode:**

```python
import math

def cone_casting_pixel(o, d, pixel_width_world, pixel_height_world, 
                       focal_length, base_radius, tri_mip_maps, mlp, 
                       scene_aabb, occupancy_grid, num_samples):
    """
    Renders a single pixel using Cone Casting with Inscribed Sphere Sampling.
    
    Args:
        o: Camera optical center (3D vector).
        d: Ray direction vector (3D vector, not necessarily normalized).
        pixel_width_world: Width of the pixel in world units.
        pixel_height_world: Height of the pixel in world units.
        focal_length: Camera focal length f.
        base_radius: Base radius parameter beta.
        tri_mip_maps: Dictionary containing 3 mipmap maps for XY, YZ, XZ planes.
        mlp: Neural network function mapping (features, view_dir) -> (density, color).
        scene_aabb: Axis-Aligned Bounding Box of the scene.
        occupancy_grid: Binary grid for empty space skipping.
        num_samples: Number of sampling points along the axis.
        
    Returns:
        final_color: 3D vector representing the pixel color.
    """
    
    # Step 1: Calculate pixel disc radius
    # Note: Source formula is r_tilde = sqrt(dx * dy / pi)
    pixel_area = pixel_width_world * pixel_height_world
    pixel_disc_radius = math.sqrt(pixel_area / math.pi)
    
    # Step 2: Define cone axis
    # Central axis a(t) = o + t * d
    # d is already defined as p_o - o
    
    # Step 3: Determine sampling distances
    # Intersect central axis with AABB to get t_start and t_end
    t_start, t_end = intersect_axis_with_aabb(o, d, scene_aabb)
    
    # Uniformly sample distances between t_start and t_end
    # If occupancy grid is used, filter these t values
    t_values = []
    for i in range(num_samples + 1):
        t = t_start + (t_end - t_start) * (i / num_samples)
        # Check occupancy for sphere at this t
        if not is_empty_in_occupancy_grid(o, d, t, base_radius, focal_length, occupancy_grid):
            t_values.append(t)
            
    # Ensure we have at least two points for integration
    if len(t_values) < 2:
        return [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]

    # Step 4 & 5 & 6: Iterate and accumulate
    final_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    transmittance = 1.0
    
    # Pre-calculate norms to avoid repeated computation
    d_norm = math.sqrt(sum(x**2 for x in d))
    d_norm_sq = d_norm ** 2
    
    # Term inside the square root in the denominator of radius formula
    # sqrt(d_norm^2 - f^2)
    # Note: If d_norm < f, this term is imaginary. In standard pinhole cameras, 
    # the ray direction magnitude relates to focal length. 
    # The source formula uses ||d||_2. We assume valid camera geometry.
    inner_sqrt_arg = d_norm_sq - (focal_length ** 2)
    if inner_sqrt_arg < 0:
        inner_sqrt_arg = 0 # Guard against numerical error or invalid geometry
    sqrt_term = math.sqrt(inner_sqrt_arg)
    
    denominator_base = math.sqrt((sqrt_term - base_radius)**2 + (focal_length ** 2))
    
    for i in range(len(t_values) - 1):
        t_curr = t_values[i]
        t_next = t_values[i+1]
        dt = t_next - t_curr
        
        # Calculate sphere center x_i
        # x_i = o + t_curr * d
        x_i = [o[j] + t_curr * d[j] for j in range(3)]
        
        # Calculate sphere radius r_i
        # r_i = (||x_i - o|| * f * beta) / (||d|| * denominator_base)
        # ||x_i - o|| is simply t_curr * ||d||
        dist_from_origin = t_curr * d_norm
        
        numerator = dist_from_origin * focal_length * base_radius
        denominator = d_norm * denominator_base
        r_i = numerator / denominator
        
        # Featurize sphere: Project to Tri-Mip planes and sample
        # This requires implementing the specific Tri-Mip sampling logic
        # which involves determining the mipmap level based on occupied area.
        # For the purpose of this method description, we assume a function exists.
        features = featurize_sphere_trimap(x_i, r_i, tri_mip_maps)
        
        # Predict density and color
        density, color = mlp(features, d)
        
        # Volume rendering integration
        # alpha = 1 - exp(-density * dt)
        alpha = 1.0 - math.exp(-density * dt)
        
        # Contribution
        contribution = [color[j] * alpha * transmittance for j in range(3)]
        final_color = [final_color[j] + contribution[j] for j in range(3)]
        
        # Update transmittance
        transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha)
        
        # Early termination if fully opaque
        if transmittance < 1e-4:
            break
            
    return final_color

def intersect_axis_with_aabb(o, d, aabb):
    """
    Helper to find t_start and t_end where the ray o + t*d intersects the AABB.
    Returns (t_min, t_max).
    """
    # Standard ray-AABB intersection algorithm
    t_min = -math.inf
    t_max = math.inf
    
    for i in range(3):
        if d[i] == 0:
            if o[i] < aabb.min[i] or o[i] > aabb.max[i]:
                return (math.inf, -math.inf) # No intersection
        else:
            t1 = (aabb.min[i] - o[i]) / d[i]
            t2 = (aabb.max[i] - o[i]) / d[i]
            t_enter = min(t1, t2)
            t_exit = max(t1, t2)
            t_min = max(t_min, t_enter)
            t_max = min(t_max, t_exit)
            
    if t_min > t_max:
        return (math.inf, -math.inf)
        
    return (t_min, t_max)

def is_empty_in_occupancy_grid(o, d, t, base_radius, focal_length, grid):
    """
    Check if the sphere at distance t is in empty space.
    """
    # Calculate sphere center and radius to query the grid
    d_norm = math.sqrt(sum(x**2 for x in d))
    x_i = [o[j] + t * d[j] for j in range(3)]
    
    d_norm_sq = d_norm ** 2
    inner_sqrt_arg = d_norm_sq - (focal_length ** 2)
    if inner_sqrt_arg < 0: inner_sqrt_arg = 0
    sqrt_term = math.sqrt(inner_sqrt_arg)
    denominator_base = math.sqrt((sqrt_term - base_radius)**2 + (focal_length ** 2))
    
    dist_from_origin = t * d_norm
    numerator = dist_from_origin * focal_length * base_radius
    denominator = d_norm * denominator_base
    r_i = numerator / denominator

    # If the entire sphere is in empty cells, return True
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of sampling points $N$ along the ray, plus the cost of MLP evaluation and Tri-Mip lookups per sample [S1]. The use of the binary occupancy grid reduces the effective number of samples by skipping empty regions [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Dominated by the storage of the three Tri-Mip maps (3 2D arrays) and the shallow MLP weights [S1]. This is significantly more compact than full 3D volume representations [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Produces anti-aliased, high-fidelity renderings in both close-up and distant views by explicitly modeling the pixel area via cone casting and inscribed spheres [S1]. It converges faster than methods using hash encoding due to the compact Tri-Mip representation [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires calibrated multi-view images for reconstruction [S1]. The method is specific to the Tri-Mip encoding; it does not directly apply to other neural representations like standard NeRF or Instant-NGP without modification [S1]. The radius calculation assumes a specific camera model (pinhole) and geometry [S1].
*   **When to use:** When rendering neural radiance fields with Tri-Mip encoding and anti-aliasing is required without the computational overhead of super-sampling [S1].
*   **When not to use:** For standard ray-casting on dense voxel grids where pre-filtering is handled differently, or when the underlying representation is not Tri-Mip based [S1].

## Background
Cone Casting with Inscribed Sphere Sampling was introduced in the paper "Tri-MipRF: Tri-Mip Representation for Efficient Anti-Aliasing Neural Radiance Fields" [S1]. The method addresses the flaw in previous neural rendering methods (like NeRF [S1]) that formulate pixels as single points, leading to blurriness in close-up views and aliasing in distant views [S1]. It differs from MipNeRF [S1], which uses multivariate Gaussians for cone sampling, by using inscribed spheres to align with the isotropic pre-filtering of the Tri-Mip encoding [S1]. The technique is part of the Tri-MipRF framework, which uses a compact 2D representation (Tri-Mip) for 3D content [S1].
