# Loosely Nested Grids

Also called: Bottom-up hierarchical grid construction, Cazals et al. hierarchical grid

## Overview
Loosely Nested Grids (LNG) is a hierarchical grid building method proposed by Cazals et al. [S1][S2, unofficial] designed to handle very complex scenes by organizing primitives into a structure that allows for efficient traversal of empty spaces [S1]. Unlike top-down adaptive subdivision methods, LNG employs a bottom-up construction approach via filtering and clustering steps [S1][S2, unofficial]. This method builds upon the concept of hierarchical grids with macrocells by structuring the hierarchy through local grid clusters rather than a single global recursive subdivision [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of geometric primitives defining the scene.
**Outputs:** A hierarchical grid data structure, viewed as a loosely nested recursive grid.

**Data Structures:**
The structure is a hierarchy of grids. Each level consists of clusters of primitives. Each cluster is associated with a local grid. The hierarchy connects these local grids, allowing traversal to skip empty regions. The exact bit-packing or memory layout of the "local grid" is not specified in the sources, other than it acting as a bounding box or occupancy structure for the cluster [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The construction follows a four-step bottom-up process [S1][S2, unofficial]:

1.  **Organize Primitives:** Divide the scene primitives into subsets of similar size. This initial partitioning ensures balanced workloads for subsequent clustering [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Cluster Neighbors:** For each group of primitives identified in Step 1, group neighboring primitives into clusters. This step effectively filters and clusters spatially proximate objects [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Construct Local Grids:** For each cluster formed in Step 2, construct a local grid. This grid serves as a bounding volume or occupancy map for the primitives within that cluster [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Construct Hierarchy:** Build a hierarchy of these local grids. This step links the local grids into a coherent structure that supports ray traversal, enabling the skipping of empty space [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Build Cost:** The method has a greater build cost compared to general bottom-up strategies [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Complexity:** While designed for complex scenes, the multi-step clustering and hierarchy construction may introduce overhead for simpler scenes [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Build Cost:** Higher than general bottom-up strategies [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Traversal Efficiency:** Designed to quickly traverse empty spaces in very complex scenes [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Scalability:** Suitable for very complex scenes due to the hierarchical organization [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Bottom-up construction avoids the pitfalls of top-down adaptive subdivision; effective for complex scenes with dense regions [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Higher build cost; specific implementation details for clustering and grid resolution are not provided in the sources [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background
Loosely Nested Grids were proposed by Cazals et al. in 1995 [S1][S2, unofficial]. The method was introduced as an alternative to top-down adaptive subdivision techniques, emphasizing a bottom-up construction approach through filtering and clustering [S1][S2, unofficial]. It is often cited alongside the work of Klimaszewski and Sederberg [KS97], who proposed a similar adaptive grid data structure using a bottom-up approach [S1][S2, unofficial].
