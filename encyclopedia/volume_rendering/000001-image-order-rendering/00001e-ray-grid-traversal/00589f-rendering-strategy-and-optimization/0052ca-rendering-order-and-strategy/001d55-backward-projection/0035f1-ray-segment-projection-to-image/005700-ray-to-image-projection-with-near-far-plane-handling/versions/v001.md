# Ray-to-Image Projection with Near/Far Plane Handling


## Overview
This method projects 3D camera rays into 2D image space to determine their intersection with the image frame boundaries, computing bounding segments $(t_{min}, t_{max})$ and pixel coordinates $(xy_{min}, xy_{max})$ along with a validity flag [S1, unofficial]. It specifically handles near/far clipping planes and degenerate camera origins (where the ray origin is at the camera position or on the zero-depth plane) [S1, unofficial]. It differs from [Pixel-Weighted Line Integration](8503-pixel-weighted-line-integration.md) by explicitly computing the geometric bounding box of the ray segment within the image plane rather than integrating weights directly, providing the necessary bounds for subsequent rasterization or sampling [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- `origins`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$, ray origins in world coordinates.
- `directions`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3)$, ray directions in world coordinates.
- `extrinsics`: Tensor of shape $(N, 4, 4)$, world-to-camera transformation matrices.
- `intrinsics`: Tensor of shape $(N, 3, 3)$, camera intrinsic matrices (normalized such that principal point is at image center and coordinates are in $[0, 1]$ range) [S1, unofficial].
- `near`: Optional float or tensor specifying the near clipping plane distance. If `None`, projection is computed at zero depth [S1, unofficial].
- `far`: Optional float or tensor specifying the far clipping plane distance. If `None`, projection is computed at infinite depth [S1, unofficial].
- `epsilon`: Float threshold for detecting zero-depth origins (default $10^{-6}$) [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
- `t_min`: Tensor of shape $(N,)$, parameter $t$ at the entry point of the ray into the image frame.
- `t_max`: Tensor of shape $(N,)$, parameter $t$ at the exit point of the ray from the image frame.
- `xy_min`: Tensor of shape $(N, 2)$, 2D coordinates $(x, y)$ at $t_{min}$.
- `xy_max`: Tensor of shape $(N, 2)$, 2D coordinates $(x, y)$ at $t_{max}$.
- `overlaps_image`: Boolean tensor of shape $(N,)$, true if the ray segment intersects the image frame.

**Data Structures:**
- The method uses boolean masks to handle different validity combinations of min/max projections.
- Coordinates are normalized to $[0, 1]$ range, with the principal point at the center $(0.5, 0.5)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Transform to Camera Space:**
    Compute the world-to-camera transformation matrix $M = \text{extrinsics}^{-1}$ [S1, unofficial]. Transform origins and directions:
    $$
    O_{cam} = M_{0:3, 0:3} O_{world} + M_{0:3, 3}
    $$
    $$
    D_{cam} = M_{0:3, 0:3} D_{world}
    $$
    In implementation, this is done via einsum multiplication of the inverse extrinsics and slicing to 3D [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Handle Degenerate Origins:**
    Check if the origin is at the camera position or on the zero-depth plane [S1, unofficial].
    - If $\|O_{cam}\| < \epsilon$: Replace the origin with the direction vector for projection purposes (effectively treating the ray as starting from the center of projection) [S1, unofficial]. Mark these rays with a specific mask `mask_at_camera` [S1, unofficial].
    - If the origin is on the zero-depth plane but not at the camera (depth $\approx 0$ but not zero vector): Mark as invalid (`valid=False`) [S1, unofficial]. Use mask `mask_depth_zero` [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Compute Near/Far Projections:**
    - **Near Plane:** If `near` is not `None`, compute the intersection point $P_{near} = O_{cam} + \text{near} \cdot D_{cam}$. If `near` is `None`, compute projection at $t=0$ (origin) [S1, unofficial].
    - **Far Plane:** If `far` is not `None`, compute the intersection point $P_{far} = O_{cam} + \text{far} \cdot D_{cam}$. If `far` is `None`, compute projection at $t = \infty$ by passing directions as points with $t = \infty$ [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Intersect with Image Boundaries:**
    For each projected point (near and far), compute intersections with the image frame boundaries $x=0, x=1, y=0, y=1$ [S1, unofficial].
    - Use a helper function `_intersect_image_coordinate` to find the parameter $t$ where the ray crosses each boundary [S1, unofficial].
    - Determine $t_{min}$ and $t_{max}$ by taking the minimum and maximum of the valid intersection parameters across all four boundaries for the near and far segments [S1, unofficial].
    - Compute corresponding 2D coordinates $xy_{min}$ and $xy_{max}$ by evaluating the ray equation at $t_{min}$ and $t_{max}$ and projecting to 2D [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Assign Validity and Bounds:**
    Iterate over the 4 validity combinations of min/max projections using `itertools.product([True, False], [True, False])` [S1, unofficial].
    - Construct boolean masks for each combination (e.g., min valid and max valid, min invalid and max valid, etc.) [S1, unofficial].
    - Assign `t_min`, `t_max`, `xy_min`, `xy_max`, and `overlaps_image` based on the active mask [S1, unofficial].
    - A ray overlaps the image if its bounding segment intersects the $[0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ square [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Near-edge coordinate proxy for grazing views
A deterministic least-squares 2D line proxy used to compute a stable near-edge coordinate for clipping planes in grazing camera views, where perspective projection of 3D planes is nonlinear [S2, unofficial]. It is chosen when camera placement is close to edge-on (e.g., $1.5^\circ$ from edge-on) to ensure numerical stability [S2, unofficial]. The proxy line $l$ is computed as $l = \text{pinv}(P)^\top [n; -\tau]$, where $P$ is the camera projection matrix, $n$ is the plane normal, and $\tau$ is the plane offset [S2, unofficial]. The sign of $l$ is oriented so that the reference foreground adjacent to the cut lies at signed distance $s < 0$, independent of the stored plane orientation [S2, unofficial]. This method avoids the instability caused by the nonlinear projection of general 3D planes under perspective [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear in the number of rays $N$, dominated by matrix multiplications and boundary intersection checks [S1, unofficial].
- **Memory Complexity:** Linear in $N$, storing intermediate tensors for origins, directions, and projections [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Handles degenerate camera origins robustly; provides explicit bounding boxes for efficient rasterization; stable for grazing views with the least-squares proxy [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Requires normalized intrinsics and centered principal point; performance may degrade if rays are nearly parallel to image boundaries without the proxy [S1, S2, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When precise ray-to-image mapping is required for volume rendering or Gaussian splatting, especially with near/far clipping and degenerate origins [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** For simple orthographic projections where near/far clipping is not needed, or when computational resources are extremely limited and approximate methods suffice.

## Background
The method originates from the NoPoSplat project, which focuses on neural rendering without pose supervision [S1, unofficial]. It is used in the context of image-order rendering for volume rendering, specifically in ray-grid traversal strategies [S1, unofficial]. The near-edge coordinate proxy is introduced in XClipGS for exact half-space clipping in medical volume Gaussian splatting, addressing stability issues in grazing views [S2, unofficial].
