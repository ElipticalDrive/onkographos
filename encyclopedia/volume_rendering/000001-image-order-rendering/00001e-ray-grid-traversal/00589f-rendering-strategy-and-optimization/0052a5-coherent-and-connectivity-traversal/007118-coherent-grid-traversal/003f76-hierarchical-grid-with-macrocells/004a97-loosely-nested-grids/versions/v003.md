# Loosely Nested Grids

Also called: Bottom-up hierarchical grid construction, Cazals et al. hierarchical grid

## Overview
Loosely Nested Grids is a hierarchical grid building method designed to handle very complex scenes by organizing primitives into a structure that allows efficient traversal of empty spaces [S1]. Unlike top-down adaptive subdivision methods, it employs a bottom-up construction approach via filtering and clustering steps [S1]. It builds on [Hierarchical Grid with Macrocells](10019-hierarchical-grid-with-macrocells.md) by structuring the hierarchy through local grid clusters rather than a single global recursive subdivision [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of geometric primitives defining the scene [S1].
**Outputs:** A hierarchical grid data structure, viewed as a loosely nested recursive grid [S1].

**Data Structures:**
The structure consists of a hierarchy of grids where each level contains clusters of primitives, and each cluster is associated with a local grid [S1]. The hierarchy connects these local grids to enable traversal that skips empty regions [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
The construction follows a four-step bottom-up process [S1][S2, unofficial]:

1.  **Organize Primitives:** Divide the scene primitives into subsets of similar size [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Cluster Neighbors:** For each group of primitives identified in Step 1, group neighboring primitives into clusters [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Construct Local Grids:** For each cluster formed in Step 2, construct a local grid [S1][S2, unofficial].
4.  **Construct Hierarchy:** Build a hierarchy of these local grids [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Build Cost:** The method has a greater build cost compared to general bottom-up strategies [S1].
*   **Complexity:** While designed for complex scenes, the multi-step clustering and hierarchy construction may introduce overhead for simpler scenes [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Build Cost:** Higher than general bottom-up strategies [S1].
*   **Traversal Efficiency:** Designed to quickly traverse empty spaces in very complex scenes [S1].
*   **Scalability:** Suitable for very complex scenes due to the hierarchical organization [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Bottom-up construction avoids the pitfalls of top-down adaptive subdivision; effective for complex scenes with dense regions [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Higher build cost; specific implementation details for clustering and grid resolution are not provided in the sources [S1].

## Background
Loosely Nested Grids were proposed by Cazals et al. [S1]. The method was introduced as an alternative to top-down adaptive subdivision techniques, emphasizing a bottom-up construction approach through filtering and clustering [S1]. It is often cited alongside the work of Klimaszewski and Sederberg [S1], who proposed a similar adaptive grid data structure using a bottom-up approach [S1].
