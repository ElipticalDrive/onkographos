# Jiang et al. Acceleration Structure
Also called: JRZ*16 method
## Overview
The Jiang et al. acceleration structure [JRZ*16] is a hierarchical data structure designed to efficiently locate active cells within a volumetric dataset for both direct and indirect visualization approaches [S1]. It enables rendering of active cells via ray marching, significantly reducing the computational cost associated with traversing inactive background regions [S1]. Unlike pure image-order methods, it is classified as a hybrid object-order and image-order approach, meaning its performance and structure are dependent on the rendering resolution [S1].
## How it works
Inputs: A scalar volume dataset and a target rendering resolution.
Outputs: An acceleration structure enabling efficient ray traversal to identify active cells.

The method constructs a hierarchy that allows the renderer to skip inactive regions. While the specific bit-packing and traversal algorithm details are not provided in the source text [S1], the structure operates by identifying active cells that contribute to the final image. The renderer uses this structure to march rays only through relevant volume segments [S1].

Key characteristics derived from the source:
1. The structure supports both direct (e.g., ray casting) and indirect visualization techniques [S1].
2. It relies on ray marching to render the identified active cells [S1].
3. It is resolution-dependent, linking it to hybrid object-order/image-order paradigms [S1].

## Subtopics

### IsoBAS Acceleration Structure
An acceleration structure introduced by Liu et al. [LCD15] for faster identification of active cells with less memory overhead than previous methods [S1]. It is achieved through CPU-based sorting of volume slices to rasterize primitives in a front-to-back order, increasing fragment discard rates [S1]. It is also considered a hybrid object-order and image-order approach dependent on rendering resolution [S1].

Limitations: Performance is dependent on rendering resolution due to its hybrid nature [S1].
When to use: When efficient ray marching through active volume regions is required for both direct and indirect visualization [S1].
When not: When resolution-independent performance is critical or when the overhead of resolution-dependent structure building is prohibitive [S1].
## Background
Origin: Proposed by Jiang et al. [JRZ*16] [S1].
History: It is part of a lineage of hybrid approaches including Liu et al.'s earlier work [LCD09, LCD15] [S1]. These methods aim to improve upon pure ray casting by integrating object-order benefits (active cell identification) with image-order rendering (ray marching) [S1].
Usage: Used in real-time rendering of scalar volumes, specifically for efficient direct isosurface rasterization and general volume visualization [S1].
