# Jiang et al. Acceleration Structure
Also called: JRZ*16 method

## Overview
The Jiang et al. acceleration structure [S1] is a hierarchical data structure designed to efficiently locate active cells for both direct and indirect volume visualization approaches, rendering these active cells via ray marching [S1]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [IsoBAS](1663-hierarchical-spatial-enumeration-optimization.md) by supporting both direct and indirect visualization paradigms within a unified framework, whereas IsoBAS focuses primarily on faster identification with lower memory overhead [S1]. As a hybrid object-order and image-order approach, its performance is dependent on the rendering resolution [S1].

## How it works
The method builds upon the shared principles of hierarchical descent and ascent defined in [Hierarchical Data Structures](15869-hierarchical-data-structures.md).

**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A scalar volume dataset and a rendering resolution.
*   **Output:** An acceleration structure enabling efficient traversal to active cells for ray marching.

2.  **Traversal:** Perform hierarchical traversal starting from the root. For each node, determine the child octant intersected by the ray using the standard bit-based index calculation:
    $$
    k = 4 \cdot \mathbb{I}(p_x \ge c_x) + 2 \cdot \mathbb{I}(p_y \ge c_y) + 1 \cdot \mathbb{I}(p_z \ge c_z)

    where $\mathbf{c}$ is the node center, $s$ is the half-extent, and $\mathbf{p}$ is the ray position [S1][G].
3.  **Active Cell Identification:** Skip empty or uniform subtrees to locate active cells [S1]. The structure allows for efficient location of these cells for both direct (e.g., isosurface rasterization) and indirect (e.g., ray casting) visualization [S1].
4.  **Rendering:** Once active cells are located, render them using ray marching [S1].

## Subtopics

### IsoBAS Acceleration Structure
An acceleration structure introduced by Liu et al. [LCD15] for faster identification of active cells with less memory overhead than previous methods [S1]. It is a CPU-based sorting approach that rasterizes primitives in front-to-back order to increase fragment discard rates [S1]. It is considered a hybrid object-order and image-order approach, dependent on rendering resolution [S1].

## Operating characteristics
The method is a hybrid object-order and image-order approach, meaning its performance and efficiency are dependent on the rendering resolution [S1]. Specific time and memory complexity metrics, measured performance data, or hardware scaling characteristics are not given in the sources [S1].

## Background
The Jiang et al. acceleration structure was proposed by Jiang et al. [JRZ*16] as an efficient method for locating active cells for both direct and indirect visualization approaches [S1]. It is part of a lineage of hybrid approaches that includes Liu et al.'s IsoBAS [LCD15] and earlier methods [LCD09], all of which rely on rendering resolution [S1]. The work is associated with research in efficient direct isosurface rasterization of scalar volumes [S1].
