# Jiang et al. Acceleration Structure
Also called: JRZ*16 method

## Overview
The Jiang et al. acceleration structure [S1] is a hybrid object-order and image-order method designed to efficiently locate active cells for both direct and indirect volume visualization [S1]. It differs from pure image-order ray casting by leveraging a hierarchical structure to accelerate active cell identification, while remaining dependent on rendering resolution like other hybrid approaches [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Scalar volume data and rendering parameters [S1].
**Outputs:** Rendered image with active cells computed via ray marching [S1].

The method builds on [Hierarchical Data Structures](15869-hierarchical-data-structures.md) [G]. Traversal begins at the root and determines intersected children by comparing the ray position to the node center [G].
1. **Structure Construction:** Build an acceleration structure from the volume data to identify active cells [S1].
2. **Ray Traversal:** For each pixel, cast a ray into the volume and traverse the hierarchy starting from the root [G].
3. **Active Cell Identification:** Use the structure to efficiently locate active cells intersected by the ray [S1].
4. **Ray Marching:** Once active cells are located, perform ray marching within those regions to compute the final color [S1].

### IsoBAS Acceleration Structure
An acceleration structure introduced by Liu et al. [LCD15] for faster identification of active cells with less memory overhead than previous methods [S1]. It is chosen when memory efficiency and rapid active cell identification are prioritized over other hierarchical variants.

## Background
The method was proposed by Jiang et al. [JRZ*16] as part of research into efficient volume rendering techniques [S1]. It is situated in the context of hybrid rendering approaches that combine object-order preprocessing with image-order rendering [S1].
