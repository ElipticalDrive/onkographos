# Frustum and Visibility Culling

Also called: Frustum Culling, Visibility Culling, Frustum-Occlusion Culling

## Overview
Frustum and Visibility Culling methods optimize volume rendering by identifying and discarding geometry that does not contribute to the final image, thereby reducing computational load [S1, unofficial]. This family distinguishes itself from sibling traversal methods by decoupling visibility determination from the core sampling loop, often serving as a pre-pass or integrated filter to limit the working set of voxels, splats, or bricks [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Octree and Tree Traversal](4134-octree-and-tree-traversal.md) which focuses on hierarchical descent, these methods focus on spatial containment relative to the camera [G].

## Subtopics

### [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md)
This method renders hierarchical shell and solid volumetric structures by traversing a bounding-sphere tree and adaptively controlling the frame rate through dynamic adjustment of minimum splat sizes. It distinguishes itself from standard octree traversal by replacing fixed-resolution voxel sampling with screen-space splat rendering, where the granularity of the visual output is directly tied to performance feedback. Unlike sibling methods that rely on geometric bounding boxes for culling, this approach uses bounding spheres and hierarchical normal cones to optimize visibility tests for point-based primitives. Choose when adaptive performance control for point-based primitives is needed. See [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md).

### [Frustum Culling Integration](8798-frustum-culling-integration.md)
Frustum Culling Integration is a visibility optimization technique applied during the progressive loading and rendering of 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes. It filters out splats that lie outside the user's current viewing frustum to prioritize the transmission and rendering of visible geometry. It differs from its sibling [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md) by focusing on spatial visibility rather than frame-rate stability, and from [Frustum-Occlusion Culling for Octree Traversal](12324-frustum-occlusion-culling-for-octree-traversal.md) by typically operating on a pre-ordered list of splats rather than a hierarchical tree structure during the culling phase. Choose for optimizing transmission and rendering of visible Gaussian splats. See [Frustum Culling Integration](8798-frustum-culling-integration.md).

### [Frustum-Occlusion Culling for Octree Traversal](12324-frustum-occlusion-culling-for-octree-traversal.md)
This method identifies a set of visible voxels by traversing an octree hierarchy and culling nodes that do not intersect the camera's view frustum or whose projected screen area is smaller than a single pixel. It serves as a visibility pre-pass for voxel rasterization, automatically determining the Level of Detail (LOD) for rendering. Unlike standard ray-marching which samples volume density, this approach outputs a list of 3D bounding boxes (voxels) to be rasterized as geometry using hardware instancing. It is distinct from [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md) by using explicit frustum intersection tests rather than frame-rate driven depth limits, and from [Frustum Culling Integration](8798-frustum-culling-integration.md) by specifically targeting octree structures for voxel-based rendering pipelines. Choose when targeting octree structures for voxel-based rendering pipelines. See [Frustum-Occlusion Culling for Octree Traversal](12324-frustum-occlusion-culling-for-octree-traversal.md).

### [Frustum Culling for Octree Nodes](17935-frustum-culling-for-octree-nodes.md)
Frustum culling for octree nodes is a hierarchical visibility test that discards entire octree subtrees if their axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) lies completely outside the view frustum. It solves the problem of efficiently determining which parts of a sparse volumetric or point-cloud structure are visible, avoiding per-element processing for millions of points with a single bounding box test. Unlike sibling methods that may integrate occlusion or frame-rate control, this method focuses purely on geometric containment within the six clipping planes, serving as the primary "existence" decision before traversal begins. Choose as a primary existence decision before traversal begins. See [Frustum Culling for Octree Nodes](17935-frustum-culling-for-octree-nodes.md).

### Ray-Guided Volume Rendering
Visibility is determined during ray traversal, providing implicit view frustum culling without extra steps [S1, unofficial]. The method provides implicit occlusion culling without extra steps or occlusion buffers [S1, unofficial]. Rays determine the working set directly by writing out a list of bricks they require (intersect) in front-to-back order [S1, unofficial]. It uses modern OpenGL extensions such as GL_ARB_shader_storage_buffer_object [S1, unofficial]. Introduced by Crassin et al. in 2009 [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of culling visible geometry is fundamental to computer graphics [G]. In the context of volume rendering, early approaches relied on explicit pre-passes to determine the "working set" of bricks or voxels [S1, unofficial]. As hardware capabilities evolved, particularly with the advent of shader storage buffer objects, methods like Ray-Guided Volume Rendering integrated culling directly into the rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial]. The specific techniques described in the subtopics represent specialized applications of these principles to different data structures (octrees, splats, bounding-sphere trees) and rendering paradigms (rasterization, ray-casting) [G].
