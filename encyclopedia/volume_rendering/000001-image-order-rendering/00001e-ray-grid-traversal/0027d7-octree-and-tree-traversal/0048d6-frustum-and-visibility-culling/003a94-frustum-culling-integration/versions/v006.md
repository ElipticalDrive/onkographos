# Frustum Culling Integration

Also called: Base+Frustum culling

## Overview
Frustum Culling Integration is a visibility optimization technique applied during the progressive loading and rendering of 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes. It filters out splats that lie outside the user's current viewing frustum to prioritize the transmission and rendering of visible geometry [S1]. It differs from its sibling [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md) by focusing on spatial visibility rather than frame-rate stability, and from [Frustum-Occlusion Culling for Octree Traversal](12324-frustum-occlusion-culling-for-octree-traversal.md) by typically operating on a pre-ordered list of splats rather than a hierarchical tree structure during the culling phase [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A list of 3D Gaussians (splats) with their mean positions $\mathbf{\mu}$, covariance matrices $\Sigma$, and screen-space projections; the current camera view matrix $V$ and projection matrix $P$; a frustum margin parameter $\alpha$.
*   **Output:** A filtered subset of splats that are considered visible (inside the frustum).

**Data Structures:**
*   **Splat List:** An array of Gaussian objects. Each Gaussian contains:
    *   $\mathbf{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Mean position.
    *   $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$: Covariance matrix (or rotation/scale parameters).
    *   $\mathbf{p}_{screen} \in \mathbb{R}^2$: Pre-computed or on-the-fly projected center coordinates.
    *   $\sigma_x, \sigma_y$: Screen-space standard deviations (derived from $\Sigma$ and $V$).

**Steps:**
1.  **Global Ordering (Pre-requisite):** As per the source, frustum culling is applied *after* a global ordering of splats has been determined (e.g., by depth or contribution) [S1]. This means the culling step does not re-order the list but filters it.
2.  **Frustum Margin Application:** Define the viewing frustum boundaries (left, right, bottom, top, near, far). Expand the near, far, left, right, top, and bottom planes by a margin factor $\alpha$ [S1].
    *   Typical value for $\alpha$: $30\%$ (i.e., boundaries are increased by 30%) [S1].
3.  **Projection and Filtering:** For each Gaussian $i$ in the ordered list:
    *   Project the mean $\mathbf{\mu}_i$ into clip space using $V$ and $P$.
    *   Convert to normalized device coordinates (NDC).
    *   Check if the projected center lies within the expanded frustum boundaries.
    *   **Note:** The source specifies filtering based on the projection of splats onto the image plane [S1]. It acknowledges that Gaussians cover areas, so a mean outside the frustum might still contribute, but the method filters based on the center's position relative to the margin-expanded frustum [S1].
4.  **Selection:** Retain only those splats where the projected center is inside the expanded frustum.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def frustum_cull_splats(splats, view_matrix, proj_matrix, margin_factor=0.3):
    """
    Filters a list of splats based on the viewing frustum with a margin.
    
    Args:
        splats: List of Gaussian objects with 'mean' (vec3) and 'covariance' (mat3).
        view_matrix: 4x4 view matrix.
        proj_matrix: 4x4 projection matrix.
        margin_factor: Percentage to expand frustum boundaries (e.g., 0.3 for 30%).
        
    Returns:
        List of splats inside the expanded frustum.
    """
    visible_splats = []
    
    # Compute combined projection matrix
    # Note: In practice, this involves transposing for row-major vs column-major conventions.
    # We assume standard OpenGL/DirectX conventions where V and P are column-major.
    vp_matrix = proj_matrix @ view_matrix
    
    for gaussian in splats:
        # 1. Project mean to clip space
        # Homogeneous coordinate
        pos_homogeneous = vec4(gaussian.mean, 1.0)
        clip_pos = vp_matrix @ pos_homogeneous
        
        # 2. Perspective division to get NDC
        if clip_pos.w <= 0.0:
            continue # Behind camera or invalid
            
        ndc_x = clip_pos.x / clip_pos.w
        ndc_y = clip_pos.y / clip_pos.w
        ndc_z = clip_pos.z / clip_pos.w
        
        # 3. Check against expanded frustum boundaries
        # Standard NDC frustum is [-1, 1] for x, y and [0, 1] or [-1, 1] for z depending on API.
        # Assuming OpenGL NDC: x,y in [-1,1], z in [-1,1].
        # Expanding boundaries by margin_factor means:
        # min_bound = -1 * (1 - margin_factor)
        # max_bound = 1 * (1 + margin_factor)
        # However, the source says "increasing the frustum boundaries by 30%".
        # This usually implies expanding the visible region.
        
        margin = margin_factor
        x_min = -1.0 * (1.0 - margin)
        x_max = 1.0 * (1.0 + margin)
        y_min = -1.0 * (1.0 - margin)
        y_max = 1.0 * (1.0 + margin)
        
        # Z-check is often omitted in simple 2D frustum culling for splats if depth is handled by ordering,
        # but strictly, it should be checked. The source focuses on "projecting onto image plane".
        # We check x and y primarily as per "filter splats that fall outside it" on the image plane.
        
        if x_min <= ndc_x <= x_max and y_min <= ndc_y <= y_max:
            visible_splats.append(gaussian)
            
    return visible_splats
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion Ignorance:** Frustum culling occurs after global ordering and does not account for occlusions from the current viewpoint [S1]. A splat may be inside the frustum but occluded by a foreground splat.
*   **Small Background Splats:** If more than 10% of splats are visible within the frustum, small background splats may still not be selected for high-quality loading because the culling happens after the initial priority list is formed [S1].
*   **Gaussian Extent:** Since Gaussians have spatial extent, a splat with a mean outside the frustum might still contribute to the image. The method uses a margin to mitigate this, but it is an approximation [S1].

## Subtopics

### In-frustum prioritization
A technique to fine-tune the ordering of splats based on the user's initial pose, selecting a percentage of splats from within the viewing frustum to allow for rapid movement while focusing on visible content [S1]. It involves projecting all splats onto the image plane and filtering those outside the frustum [S1]. A typical parameter is the frustum margin, set to 30% to increase frustum boundaries [S1]. This variant is chosen when user-specific view information is available to improve perceived quality during camera movement [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of splats, as each splat must be projected and tested against the frustum [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ to store the splat list and the filtered result [S1].
*   **Performance:** The complete approach (octree + frustum culling) performs better when user-specific view information is available [S1]. However, if >10% of splats are visible, the benefit is reduced for background details [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Reduces the number of splats processed/rendered by excluding clearly invisible geometry [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Does not handle occlusion; relies on pre-computed ordering; margin parameter requires tuning [S1].
*   **When to use:** When user view direction is known and the scene has significant geometry outside the current view [S1].
*   **When not to use:** When occlusion is the primary bottleneck or when the view is fully contained within the scene (high visibility percentage) [S1].

## Background
The method originates from the PRoGS (Progressive Rendering of Gaussian Splats) paper [S1]. It was developed to address the bandwidth and rendering costs of progressive 3D Gaussian Splatting by leveraging user view information to prioritize visible splats [S1]. The approach builds on the base progressive rendering method by adding a visibility filter [S1].
