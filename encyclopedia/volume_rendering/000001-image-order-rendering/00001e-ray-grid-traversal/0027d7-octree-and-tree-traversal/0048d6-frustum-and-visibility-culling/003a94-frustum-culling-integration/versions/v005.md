# Frustum Culling Integration

Also called: Base+Frustum culling

## Overview
Frustum Culling Integration is a visibility optimization technique applied during the progressive loading of Gaussian splats, specifically after global contribution-based ordering has been established [S1]. It filters out splats that lie outside the current camera's viewing frustum to prioritize the transmission and rendering of visible geometry [S1]. Unlike its sibling [Bounding-Sphere Tree Traversal with Frame-Rate Control](7227-bounding-sphere-tree-traversal-with-frame-rate-control.md) which manages frame budgets, or [Frustum-Occlusion Culling for Octree Traversal](12324-frustum-occlusion-culling-for-octree-traversal.md) which integrates visibility into the traversal structure, this method operates as a post-ordering filter [S1]. It is distinct because it does not account for depth-based occlusions from the current viewpoint, relying instead on the pre-computed global importance ranking [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- A list of Gaussian splats, pre-sorted by global contribution (e.g., based on training view importance) [S1].
- The current camera projection matrix and view frustum parameters [S1].
- A frustum margin parameter [S1].

**Outputs:**
- A filtered subset of splats that are considered "visible" for the current frame [S1].

**Data Structures:**
- The splat list is typically organized in chunks or segments, often derived from a compressed `.ply` format where minimum and maximum splat indices are stored to allow dequantization upon loading [S1].
- Each splat is defined by its mean position $\mathbf{\mu}$ and covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ (or scale/rotation/quaternion parameters) [S1].

**Steps:**
1. **Global Ordering:** First, determine the global ordering of all splats based on their contribution to the scene (e.g., using the contribution of the highest contributing Gaussians per pixel as a proxy) [S1]. This step is independent of the current viewpoint [S1].
2. **Projection:** Project the mean position $\mathbf{\mu}_i$ of each splat $i$ onto the 2D image plane using the current camera projection matrix $P$ [S1].
   $$ \mathbf{p}_i = P \cdot \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{\mu}_i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
   Normalize $\mathbf{p}_i$ to obtain normalized device coordinates (NDC) $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ [S1].
3. **Frustum Filtering:** Check if the projected splat falls within the viewing frustum boundaries $[x_{min}, x_{max}] \times [y_{min}, y_{max}]$ in NDC space [S1].
   - To account for the fact that Gaussians cover areas rather than points, apply a margin to the frustum boundaries [S1].
   - The effective boundaries are expanded by a margin percentage $m$ (typically 30%) [S1]:
     $$ x'_{min} = x_{min} - m \cdot (x_{max} - x_{min}) $$
     $$ x'_{max} = x_{max} + m \cdot (x_{max} - x_{min}) $$
     (Similarly for $y$) [S1].
   - A splat is considered inside if $x'_{min} \le x_i \le x'_{max}$ and $y'_{min} \le y_i \le y'_{max}$ [S1].
4. **Selection:** Select a percentage of the splats that pass the filter, prioritizing those with higher global contribution scores [S1]. This allows for rapid movement while focusing on visible content [S1].

**Parameters:**
- **Frustum Margin:** The percentage by which frustum boundaries are increased to include partially visible splats. Typical value: 30% [S1].
- **Selection Percentage:** The proportion of in-frustum splats to load. Not explicitly fixed, but described as "a percentage" to allow rapid movement [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Occlusion Ignorance:** Since culling occurs after global ordering, it does not account for occlusions from the current viewpoint [S1]. Splats that are globally important but occluded in the current view may still be loaded if they are in-frustum [S1].
- **Background Noise:** If more than 10% of all splats are visible within the current frustum, small background splats may still be selected despite being low priority, because the global order is fixed [S1].
- **Mean vs. Extent:** The check is based on the splat mean. A splat with a mean outside the frustum might still partially overlap the view, hence the margin [S1].

## Subtopics

### In-frustum prioritization
A technique to fine-tune the initial ordering based on the user's initial pose, selecting a percentage of splats from within the viewing frustum to allow rapid movement [S1]. It involves projecting all splats onto the image plane and filtering those that fall outside the frustum [S1]. It uses a frustum margin (typically 30%) to account for the area coverage of Gaussians [S1].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of splats for projection and filtering $O(N)$ [S1].
- **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the chunking strategy; typically loads only in-frustum splats, reducing memory bandwidth [S1].
- **Performance:** Improves foreground quality marginally when user-specific view information is available [S1]. However, it performs worse than octree-only approaches when user-specific knowledge is absent, as it cannot adapt to the current viewpoint's occlusion structure [S1].
- **Limitations:** Fails to reduce load for occluded objects; if >10% of splats are in-frustum, background noise persists [S1].
- **When to Use:** When the user's initial pose is known and rapid movement is prioritized over perfect occlusion culling [S1].

## Background
Originated in the context of progressive rendering of 3D Gaussian Splats (3DGS) [S1]. The method is part of the PRoGS (Progressive Rendering of Gaussian Splats) framework [S1]. It builds on the base approach of global contribution ordering and is often combined with octree structures for better scene representation without user-specific knowledge [S1]. The approach was adapted from techniques used in compression and chunking of compressed `.ply` files [S1].
