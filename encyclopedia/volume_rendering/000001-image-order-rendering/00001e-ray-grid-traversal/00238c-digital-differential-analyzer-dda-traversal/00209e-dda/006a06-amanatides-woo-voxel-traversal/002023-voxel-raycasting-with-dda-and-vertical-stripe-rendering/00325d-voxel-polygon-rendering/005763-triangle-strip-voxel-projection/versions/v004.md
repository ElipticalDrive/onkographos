# Triangle Strip Voxel Projection

Also called: Hardware Accelerated Voxel Rendering, Structure Rendering Algorithm

## Overview
A hardware-accelerated rendering method for voxel landscapes that replaces software span rendering with triangle strips [S1, unofficial]. It projects voxels onto the screen in columns and constructs geometry based on the visibility of adjacent columns to minimize polygon count and utilize hardware interpolation [S1, unofficial]. Unlike software span rendering which draws spans immediately, this method buffers column projections to construct triangle strips, reducing draw calls and leveraging hardware interpolation for texture coordinates, lightmap colors, and heights [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Voxel Polygon Rendering](6747-voxel-polygon-rendering.md) by replacing immediate span drawing with batched strip construction [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A voxel map representing a landscape; a camera view; a configurable step size for column sampling (default 32 pixels) [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A sequence of OpenGL draw calls using triangle strips [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The algorithm processes the landscape by scanning along depth lines (columns) rather than processing individual rays [S1, unofficial]. For each sampled column, voxels are projected onto the screen, but spans are not drawn immediately; the algorithm waits until the next column is projected [S1, unofficial]. The algorithm maintains state for two adjacent columns: $A$ (left-most point of the current strip portion) and $B$ (right-most point of the next column) [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Scan Columns:** Iterate through the landscape depth lines [S1, unofficial]. Sample columns at intervals defined by the step size (default 32 pixels) [S1, unofficial]. Smaller steps generate more polygons [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Project Voxels:** For each sampled column, project the voxels onto the screen [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Evaluate Visibility:** Determine the visibility status of column $A$ (previous column) and column $B$ (current column) [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Construct Geometry:** Based on the visibility of $A$ and $B$, execute one of four cases [S1, unofficial]:
    -   **Case 1 ($A$ not visible, $B$ visible):** Start a new triangle strip [S1, unofficial]. Send vertices of columns $A$ then $B$ to the GL [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Case 2 ($A$ visible, $B$ visible):** Continue the currently active triangle strip [S1, unofficial]. Send only vertices of column $B$ to the GL [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Case 3 ($A$ visible, $B$ not visible):** Send coordinates of column $B$ to the GL and stop the current triangle strip [S1, unofficial].
    -   **Case 4 ($A$ not visible, $B$ not visible):** Usually do nothing [S1, unofficial]. Handle a special sub-case for holes [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Handle Holes (Case 4 Sub-case):** If a triangle strip was started or finished at column $A$ during the previous pass, draw a single triangle to close the gap [S1, unofficial]. Check the condition:
    $$ (Y_1 > pY_1) \text{ and } (Y_2 > pY_2) $$
    Where $Y_1, Y_2$ and $pY_1, pY_2$ are Y-coordinates from the projected spans [S1, unofficial]. If true, draw the triangle [S1, unofficial]. This approximation may draw triangles that are not quite visible but is more efficient than performing line intersections [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
-   **Holes:** In Case 4, if a triangle strip was started or finished at the previous column during the previous pass, a single triangle must be drawn to close the gap [S1, unofficial].
-   **Mesh Density:** The triangle mesh can become too dense at the bottom of valleys [S1, unofficial]. A span reduction technique is ideally needed to mitigate this [S1, unofficial].
-   **Approximation:** The hole-closing condition may draw triangles that are not quite visible, but this is accepted for efficiency over line intersections [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
-   **Time Complexity:** Highly efficient due to batching draw calls into strips, though exact complexity depends on column count and visibility transitions [S1, unofficial].
-   **Memory Complexity:** Low, as it processes columns sequentially and streams vertices to the graphics pipeline without storing the full mesh [S1, unofficial].
-   **Measured Performance:** In the terraVoxGL demo scene, it rendered 8664 triangles (across 4 passes) in 925 strips [S1, unofficial].
-   **Strengths:** Extremely efficient due to triangle strip usage; leverages hardware interpolation for texture coordinates, lightmap colors, and heights; reduces draw calls compared to software span rendering [S1, unofficial].
-   **Limitations:** Can produce dense meshes in valleys; the described texturing scheme (4 single passes with alpha coefficients) is inefficient [S1, unofficial].
-   **When to use:** When rendering voxel landscapes on hardware that supports triangle strips and interpolation, and when software span rendering is too slow [S1, unofficial].
-   **When not to use:** When high-quality procedural shading is required (the described texturing is a simple emulation of software) or when valley density is a critical issue without span reduction [S1, unofficial].

## Background
-   **Origin:** Developed by an anonymous author for the "Realtime Voxel Landscape Engines" series, specifically Part 6 on Hardware Acceleration [S1, unofficial].
-   **History:** Presented on Flipcode in February 2000 as a response to the slowness of software span rendering for voxel landscapes [S1, unofficial]. It was implemented in the terraVoxGL demo using OpenGL [S1, unofficial].
-   **Usage:** Used in the terraVoxGL demo to accelerate voxel landscape rendering by utilizing the video card as a "huge interpolating machine" [S1, unofficial].
