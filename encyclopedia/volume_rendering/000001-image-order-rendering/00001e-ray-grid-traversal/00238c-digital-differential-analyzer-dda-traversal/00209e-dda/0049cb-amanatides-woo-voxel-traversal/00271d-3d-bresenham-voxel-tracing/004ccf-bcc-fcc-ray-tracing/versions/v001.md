# BCC/FCC Ray Tracing

Also called: BCC/FCC Ray Tracing and Object Representation, Ray-tracing in BCC and FCC grids, Smooth Object Surface representation

## Overview
BCC/FCC Ray Tracing is a volume rendering and object representation method that samples 3-D data on Body Centered Cubic (BCC) or Face Centered Cubic (FCC) grids instead of standard cubic grids [S1]. It solves topological defects inherent in cubic grids—specifically, the possibility of a ray passing through an object surface without intersecting any voxel—by utilizing grids with superior topological properties that guarantee closed surfaces and robust discrete curve/surface definitions [S1]. Unlike cubic grid methods such as [3D Bresenham Voxel Tracing](3949-3d-bresenham-voxel-tracing.md) or [Integer-based Amanatides and Woo Voxel Traversal](4246-integer-based-amanatides-and-woo-voxel-traversal.md), this approach defines voxels as Voronoi cells (Truncated Octahedra for BCC, Rhombic Dodecahedra for FCC) and traces rays by minimizing the cross-sectional area of the ray beam within these polyhedral structures [S1].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  **Volume Data:** A 3-D signal sampled on a BCC or FCC grid. The data is typically resampled from a standard cubic grid using a 3-D cubic B-Spline interpolator [S1].
2.  **Smooth Surface Coefficients:** A signed integer coefficient for each voxel in the transition region, representing the sub-voxel distance between the voxel center and the object boundary. Values range from -128 to 127, where positive values indicate interior voxels and negative values indicate exterior voxels [S1].
3.  **Ray Definition:** An origin point $P$ and a direction vector $\vec{PQ}$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
1.  **Rendered Image:** A 2-D pixel array with lighting and antialiasing [S1].
2.  **Traced Ray Path:** A sequence of connected voxels representing the light beam [S1].

### Data Structures

**1. Grid Topology**
The grid is defined by its Voronoi cells. Connectivity is strictly defined by common faces:
*   Two voxels are connected if they share a common face.
*   Two faces are connected if they share a common edge.
*   Two edges are connected if they share a common vertex.
*   Intersection at lower dimensions ($m < n-1$) does not imply connectivity [S1].

**2. Smooth Object Surface Representation**
*   **Object Surface:** The set of faces between object voxels and non-object voxels. Due to grid properties, every face in the object surface has exactly one face connected to each of its edges, ensuring the surface is always closed [S1].
*   **Smooth Surface Coefficients:** A signed integer $c \in [-128, 127]$ assigned to each voxel in contact with the object surface. These coefficients are computed using an edge detector operator based on geometric moments to estimate sub-voxel distances [S1].

**3. Directional Regions**
All possible ray directions are partitioned into regions corresponding to the yard directions perpendicular to the faces of the Voronoi polyhedron.
*   **BCC Grid:** 24 directional regions.
*   **FCC Grid:** 20 directional regions [S1].
*   Each region is defined by a basis of three vectors $\{\vec{A}, \vec{B}, \vec{C}\}$ corresponding to faces of the Voronoi polyhedron sharing a common vertex. These vectors span a triangular pyramid with its vertex at the polyhedron center [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

**Step 1: Compute Smooth Surface Coefficients**
Apply an edge detector operator based on geometric moments to the volume data. This estimates the sub-voxel distance between the voxel center and the object boundary.
*   Result: Signed integer coefficients $c \in [-128, 127]$.
*   $c > 0$: Interior voxel.
*   $c < 0$: Exterior voxel [S1].

**Step 2: Determine Directional Region**
Given the ray direction vector $\vec{d}$, identify which of the 24 (BCC) or 20 (FCC) directional regions it falls into. This is analogous to finding the octant in Bresenham's algorithm but uses the yard directions perpendicular to the Voronoi faces [S1].

**Step 3: Decompose Ray Vector**
Decompose the line vector $\vec{PQ}$ components $\{n_a, n_b, n_c\}$ in the basis $\{\vec{A}, \vec{B}, \vec{C}\}$ of the identified region.
*   Geometrically, this means that to go from $P$ to $Q$, $n_a$ steps must be performed in the $\vec{A}$ direction, $n_b$ in $\vec{B}$, and $n_c$ in $\vec{C}$ [S1].

**Step 4: Trace the Ray (Minimize Rhombus Area)**
Trace the line by advancing from one voxel to the next, minimizing the area of the rhombus $R$ (the rhombic base of the prism containing all line beam points).
1.  Project the grid points onto a plane $N$ orthogonal to the line $\vec{PQ}$. The projection consists of a set of 2-D grids shifted by a constant vector [S1].
2.  At each step, select the next voxel such that the area of the rhombus $R$ formed by the current and next beam points is minimized.
3.  Advance using only integer sums [S1].

### Parameters and Edge Cases
*   **Coefficient Range:** Signed integers from -128 to 127 [S1].
*   **Directional Regions:** 24 for BCC, 20 for FCC [S1].
*   **Precision:** The algorithm uses only integer sums for advancement, avoiding floating-point accumulation errors during traversal [S1].
*   **Degenerate Input:** The method assumes the ray direction is not parallel to any basis vector face normal, which would make the decomposition ambiguous. The partitioning into regions handles this by assigning directions to specific pyramids [S1].
*   **Resampling:** The input volume must be resampled to the BCC/FCC grid. The source uses a 3-D cubic B-Spline interpolator, which duplicates the number of voxels compared to the original cubic grid [S1].

## Operating characteristics

**Time and Memory Complexity:**
*   **Time:** The ray tracing step uses only integer sums, making it computationally efficient, similar to Bresenham's algorithm [S1].
*   **Memory:** The method reduces information by representing objects only by their external surfaces and smooth coefficients, potentially reducing memory usage compared to full volume storage [S1].

**Measured Performance:**
*   **Hardware:** PC-Pentium 166MHz, Linux 2.0.18 [S1].
*   **Scene:** Hip joint CT reconstruction, 100x100x100 voxels, resampled to BCC.
*   **Output:** 256x256 pixels.
*   **Time:** 3 seconds for ray tracing, lighting (one light source), and antialiasing [S1].

**Strengths and Limitations:**
*   **Strengths:** Eliminates topological problems (rays passing through surfaces without touching) common in cubic grids. Allows robust definition of discrete curves and surfaces. Enables sub-voxel deformation by changing coefficients. Reduces aliasing effects [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires resampling from cubic to BCC/FCC, which increases voxel count (duplicates voxels). The complexity of the basis decomposition and rhombus minimization may be higher than simple cubic DDA for very simple scenes [S1].

**When to Use:**
*   When topological correctness is critical (e.g., medical imaging where missing a surface is unacceptable).
*   When sub-voxel surface representation is needed for smooth rendering or collision detection.
*   When computational resources allow for the overhead of resampling and basis decomposition.

**When Not to Use:**
*   For simple scenes where cubic grid topological defects are negligible or handled by thicker rays.
*   When real-time performance on very low-end hardware is required and the overhead of BCC/FCC processing is prohibitive.

## Background

**Origin:**
The method was introduced by Luis Ibáñez, Chafia Hamitouche, and Christian Roux from ENST-Bretagne, France [S1].

**History and Development:**
The paper describes a ray-tracing and object description method for objects sampled in BCC and FCC grids, which are well-known in crystallography. The use of these grids is motivated by their ability to reduce the density of samples needed to represent a signal without information loss (satisfying the Shannon theorem optimally with BCC) and their better topological properties compared to the cubic grid [S1].

**Usage:**
The method is used as a ray casting engine in a direct volume rendering program for medical imaging, specifically for hip joint CT reconstruction data [S1].
