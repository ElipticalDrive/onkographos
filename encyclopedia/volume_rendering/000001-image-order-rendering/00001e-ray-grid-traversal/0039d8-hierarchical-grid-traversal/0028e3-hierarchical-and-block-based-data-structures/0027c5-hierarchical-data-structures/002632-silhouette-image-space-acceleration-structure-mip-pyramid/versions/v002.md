# Silhouette Image Space Acceleration Structure (Mip Pyramid)

Also called: Hierarchical Voxelization Acceleration Structure

## Overview
This method accelerates voxelization culling by constructing a hierarchical mip pyramid over silhouette images, inspired by the hierarchical z-buffer [S1]. It solves the problem of efficiently determining whether a voxel intersects the visual hull surface defined by multiple camera views without sampling every point within the voxel volume [S1]. It is distinguished from sibling methods by operating in image space rather than world space, using conservative min/max bounds over 2x2 texel blocks to guarantee no small features are missed and no holes are created in the resulting voxelization [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- $N$ silhouette images, one per camera view $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$.
- A voxel $v$ defined by its 8 corners in Normalized Device Coordinate (NDC) space.

**Outputs:**
- A conservative bound on the silhouette value $\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{p})$ for all $\mathbf{p} \in v$.

**Data Structures:**
- **Mip Pyramid:** For each silhouette image $i$, a hierarchy of textures (pyramid levels).
- **Level $k$ Texels:** Each texel at level $k$ stores two values: the minimum and maximum of the 4 corresponding texels at level $k+1$ (the next lower level, i.e., higher resolution). Level 0 is the highest resolution (original smoothed silhouette image).

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Pyramid Construction:**
    Generate $n$ image pyramids using a GPU compute kernel launched immediately after silhouette image smoothing [S1]. For each level $k > 0$, and for each texel position $(u, v)$, compute:
    $$
    \text{min}_{k}(u,v) = \min(\text{min}_{k+1}(2u, 2v), \text{min}_{k+1}(2u+1, 2v), \text{min}_{k+1}(2u, 2v+1), \text{min}_{k+1}(2u+1, 2v+1))
    $$
    $$
    \text{max}_{k}(u,v) = \max(\text{max}_{k+1}(2u, 2v), \text{max}_{k+1}(2u+1, 2v), \text{max}_{k+1}(2u, 2v+1), \text{max}_{k+1}(2u+1, 2v+1))
    $$
    [S1]

2.  **Voxel Projection and Bounding Box:**
    For a voxel $v$, project its 8 NDC space corners into the silhouette image space of camera $i$. Compute the axis-aligned bounding rectangle (AABB) defined by:
    $$
    \text{minX} = \min(\text{projected } x \text{ coordinates})
    $$
    $$
    \text{minY} = \min(\text{projected } y \text{ coordinates})
    $$
    $$
    \text{maxX} = \max(\text{projected } x \text{ coordinates})
    $$
    $$
    \text{maxY} = \max(\text{projected } y \text{ coordinates})
    $$
    [S1]

3.  **Level Selection:**
    Determine the sampling level $L$ in the mip pyramid:
    $$
    L = \lfloor \log_2(\max(\text{maxX} - \text{minX}, \text{maxY} - \text{minY})) \rfloor
    $$
    [S1]
    At this level $L$, the bounding rectangle covers at most a $2 \times 2$ block of texels.

4.  **Sampling:**
    Sample the texels at the corners of the AABB in the pyramid at level $L$. Let these sampled values be $(\text{min}_c, \text{max}_c)$ for corner $c \in \{\text{top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right}\}$.

5.  **Conservative Bound Calculation:**
    Compute the bound for silhouette image $i$:
    $$
    \text{bound}_i(v) = \min_c(\text{min}_c) \quad \text{and} \quad \text{upper}_i(v) = \max_c(\text{max}_c)
    $$
    [S1]
    The value $\mathcal{I}_i(\mathcal{C}_i(\mathbf{p}))$ for any point $\mathbf{p}$ within voxel $v$ is bounded by these values.

6.  **Global Voxel Bound:**
    Combine bounds across all $N$ cameras to determine if the voxel intersects the visual hull. The conservative bound on the silhouette value $\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{p})$ is derived by taking the product of the minimums (and similarly maximums) over all images [S1]:
    $$
    \text{GlobalMin}(v) = \prod_{i=0}^{N-1} \text{bound}_i(v)
    $$
    $$
    \text{GlobalMax}(v) = \prod_{i=0}^{N-1} \text{upper}_i(v)
    $$
    [S1]
    If $\text{GlobalMin}(v) > 0.5$ (or appropriate threshold), the voxel is outside the visual hull and can be culled. If $\text{GlobalMax}(v) < 0.5$, it is inside. Otherwise, further processing is required.

**Pitfalls:**
- **Conservatism:** The bounds are conservative to ensure no small features are missed and no holes are created in voxelization [S1]. This may lead to overestimation of occupancy in some cases, requiring finer resolution or additional checks.
- **Precision:** Floating-point precision in projection and log2 calculation must be handled carefully to avoid incorrect level selection.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Pyramid construction is $O(N \cdot W \cdot H)$ where $W, H$ are image dimensions. Voxel query is $O(1)$ after projection, involving a few texture lookups.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot W \cdot H)$ for storing the mip pyramids (approximately $4/3$ of the base image size per camera due to mip levels).
- **Performance:** Greatly reduces computation and data accesses needed by the oracle kernel for culling decisions [S1].
- **Strengths:** Conservative bounds prevent holes in voxelization; efficient culling via image-space hierarchy.
- **Limitations:** Requires pre-computed silhouette images; performance depends on number of cameras and image resolution.

## Background
The method was introduced in "Real-time high-resolution sparse voxelization with application to image-based modeling" [S1]. It is inspired by the hierarchical z-buffer [Greene et al. 1993] and used to accelerate voxelization in image-based modeling applications.
