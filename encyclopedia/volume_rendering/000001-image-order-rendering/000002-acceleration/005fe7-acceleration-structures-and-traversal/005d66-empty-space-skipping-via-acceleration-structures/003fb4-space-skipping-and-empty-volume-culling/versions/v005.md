# Space Skipping and Empty Volume Culling
## Overview
Space Skipping and Empty Volume Culling is a family of acceleration techniques in volume rendering that reduces computational load by identifying and bypassing regions of the volume or ray path that do not contribute to the final image [G]. It encompasses strategies ranging from geometric bounding volumes and visibility masks to parallel segment lists, distinguishing itself from [Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control](1791-early-termination-and-probabilistic-path-control.md) by focusing on pre-computed or geometrically derived exclusion zones rather than dynamic ray state termination [G].

## Subtopics

### [Parallel Ray Casting with Segment Lists](2565-parallel-ray-casting-with-segment-lists.md)
Parallel Ray Casting with Segment Lists is a distributed-memory and shared-memory parallel volume rendering algorithm that accelerates ray casting by precomputing lists of occupied volume segments to skip empty space [G]. It distributes image rows across MPI ranks and OpenMP threads, calculating sampling points based on a derived step length [G]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [SparseLeap](5152-sparseleap.md) by using explicit segment lists derived from volume geometry rather than sparse voxel octree traversal or occupancy misses, and from [Visibility Culling](6226-visibility-culling.md) by operating at the ray-segment level rather than tile or view-frustum level [G]. Choose it when rendering large, block-structured volumes on distributed systems. [Parallel Ray Casting with Segment Lists](2565-parallel-ray-casting-with-segment-lists.md).

### [Visibility Culling](6226-visibility-culling.md)
Visibility Culling is a family of acceleration techniques in volume rendering that reduces computational load by identifying and excluding primitives, voxels, or ray segments that do not contribute to the final image [G]. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Bounding Volume Empty-Space Culling](17140-bounding-volume-empty-space-culling.md) by focusing on optical or geometric visibility relative to the camera, rather than purely on geometric emptiness, and from [Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control](1791-early-termination-and-probabilistic-path-control.md) by often operating on pre-computed or screen-space visibility masks rather than just dynamic ray state [G]. Choose it when fragment-level occlusion or screen-space coverage can be leveraged. [Visibility Culling](6226-visibility-culling.md).

### [Bounding Volume Empty-Space Culling](17140-bounding-volume-empty-space-culling.md)
Bounding Volume Empty-Space Culling restricts volume reconstruction computation to the "effective" reconstruction area by calculating an initial bounding volume and slicing it according to the viewing direction, using these slices as a depth buffer to guide reconstruction. It sets itself apart from sibling methods like [Hierarchical Bounding Box Tree for Empty-Space Skipping](8182-hierarchical-bounding-box-tree-for-empty-space-skipping.md) by using a simple geometric approximation (truncated shapes) rather than a hierarchical tree structure, and from [Visibility Culling](6226-visibility-culling.md) by focusing on the validity of the reconstruction domain itself rather than fragment-level occlusion. Choose it for parallel beam reconstruction pipelines where a geometric bounding volume can serve as a depth buffer. [Bounding Volume Empty-Space Culling](17140-bounding-volume-empty-space-culling.md).

### Parsimonious Ray Sampling and Fused Rendering
A rendering optimization that infers ray-BBox intersection to sample query points only within the scene bounding box, applicable only to bounded scenes [S1, unofficial]. It fuses approximately ten point-wise operations for the forward and backward pass of the density-to-alpha function conversion [S1, unofficial]. In the volume rendering accumulation, it halts tracking a ray once the accumulated transmittance falls below $10^{-3}$ [S1, unofficial].

### Surface Localization Acceleration
Acceleration methods that localize the surface interface and concentrate samples only in relevant regions, effectively skipping empty space, because only 10-20% of evaluated samples have non-zero density [S2]. It refines the sampling strategy to focus solely on the localized surface [S2]. Standard volumetric rendering is inefficient because it samples the entire ray; surface localization reduces the number of required MLP evaluations by skipping empty space [S2]. Choose it when rendering neural radiance fields or implicit representations where surface geometry occupies a small fraction of 3D space [S2].

### Per-ray Volume Culling
Optimization to avoid visiting every volume at every step [S3, unofficial]. Previously, the march visited every volume at every step just to ask cell_index whether it was inside, and almost always was not [S3, unofficial]. The new method builds an active list up front, so a step only visits volumes whose t-range contains it [S3, unofficial]. It differs from the previous approach which visited every volume at every step [S3, unofficial]. The new approach is pure work elimination and produces byte-identical output [S3, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of skipping empty space in volume rendering dates back to the early days of ray casting, where the primary goal was to avoid sampling voxels with zero density [G]. Early methods relied on simple bounding boxes or grid-based occupancy flags [G]. The rise of neural radiance fields (NeRF) and implicit representations has renewed interest in these techniques, leading to methods like parsimonious ray sampling and surface localization acceleration that integrate culling directly into the learning and rendering pipeline [S1, unofficial][S2].
