# Ray Marching with Dithering and Early Termination

Also called: Dithered Ray Marching with Early Termination

## Overview
This method performs volume rendering by marching rays through a volume texture, applying shading and compositing in a single pass, while using dithering to reduce aliasing artifacts and early termination to improve performance [S1, unofficial]. It is distinguished from its siblings by its specific implementation of front-to-back alpha blending with a fixed early termination threshold and its reliance on screen-space dithering to mitigate undersampling artifacts, rather than probabilistic path control or empty-space skipping [S1, unofficial]. Unlike [Russian Roulette](1371-russian-roulette.md), it is a deterministic early termination method that introduces bias by ignoring the ray tail beyond the opacity threshold [1019-early-ray-termination.md].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume texture (`voltex`): 3D texture containing scalar density/intensity values.
*   Transfer function texture (`tftex`): 1D or 2D texture mapping intensity to RGBA color.
*   Camera parameters: `start` (ray origin), `end` (ray end point), `screen_width`, `screen_height`.
*   Rendering parameters: `nsamples` (number of steps), `SHADING_THRES` (opacity threshold for shading), `DELTA` (gradient step size), `early_term_thresh` (opacity threshold for termination).

**Outputs:**
*   Fragment color (`outcolor`): The final RGBA color for the pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   `pos`: Current ray position in world space (vec3).
*   `delta`: Step vector (vec3).
*   `color`: Current sample color (vec4).
*   `outcolor`: Accumulated color (vec4).
*   `N`: Surface normal (vec3).
*   `L`: Light vector (vec3).
*   `V`: View vector (vec3).

**Steps:**

1.  **Initialize Ray:**
    Calculate normalized screen coordinates:
    $$ \text{norm\_coord} = \frac{\text{gl\_FragCoord.st}}{\text{vec2}(\text{screen\_width}, \text{screen\_height})} $$
    Calculate ray direction and length:
    $$ \text{direction} = \text{end} - \text{start} $$
    $$ \text{len} = \|\text{direction}\| $$
    Calculate step size:
    $$ \text{stepsize} = \frac{\text{len}}{\text{nsamples}} $$
    Normalize direction and calculate delta vector:
    $$ \text{direction} = \frac{\text{direction}}{\|\text{direction}\|} $$
    $$ \text{delta} = \text{direction} \times \text{stepsize} $$

2.  **Apply Dithering:**
    To hide staircase aliasing from undersampling, perturb the starting position:
    $$ \text{pos} = \text{start} + \text{delta} \times \text{rand}() $$
    Where $\text{rand}()$ generates a random number in $[0, 1)$ [S1, unofficial].

3.  **Initialize Accumulators:**
    $$ \text{pos} = \text{start} $$
    $$ \text{outcolor} = \text{vec4}(0.0) $$
    $$ \text{color} = \text{vec4}(0.0) $$
    $$ f\_max\_i = 0 $$

4.  **Marching Loop:**
    Loop $i$ from $0$ to $\text{nsamples}$ while $\text{len} > 0$:
    a.  **Advance Position:**
        $$ \text{pos} = \text{pos} + \text{delta} $$
        $$ \text{len} = \text{len} - \text{stepsize} $$
    b.  **Sample Volume:**
        $$ \text{intensity} = \text{texture}(\text{voltex}, \text{pos}).r $$
    c.  **Transfer Function:**
        $$ \text{color} = \text{texture}(\text{tftex}, \text{intensity}) $$
    d.  **Shading (Conditional):**
        If $\text{color}.a > \text{SHADING\_THRES}$ AND $\text{shading\_mode} \neq 3$ AND $\text{nsamples} > 500$:
        i.  Compute normal using central differences:
            $$ \mathbf{N}_{local} = \text{gradient\_central\_diff}(\text{voltex}, \text{pos}, \text{DELTA}) $$
        ii. Transform to world space:
            $$ \mathbf{N} = \text{normalize}(\text{normalmatrix} \times \text{vec3}(\mathbf{N}_{local})) $$
        iii. Calculate light vector $\mathbf{L}$ and view vector $\mathbf{V}$ in world space.
        iv. Apply shading based on mode:
            *   Mode 0: Blinn-Phong.
            *   Mode 1: Blinn-Phong + Edge Enhancement.
            *   Mode 2: Toon shading.
            *   Mode 3: No shading [S1, unofficial].
    e.  **Compositing:**
        Apply compositing based on mode:
        *   Mode 0: Front-to-Back Alpha Blending.
        *   Mode 1: Maximum Intensity Projection (MIP).
        *   Mode 2: Minimum Intensity Projection (MIDA) [S1, unofficial].
    f.  **Early Termination:**
        If $\text{outcolor}.a > \text{early\_term\_thresh}$, terminate loop [S1, unofficial].

5.  **Return Result:**
    Return $\text{outcolor}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   `SHADING_THRES`: 0.10 [S1, unofficial].
*   `DELTA`: 0.005 [S1, unofficial].
*   `early_term_thresh`: 0.95 [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Dithering reduces geometric aliasing (woodgrain/staircase) but does not prevent aliasing in the transfer function or shading calculations [S1, unofficial].
*   Early termination introduces bias by discarding the remaining ray contribution, which can lead to darkening in translucent regions if the threshold is too high [1019-early-ray-termination.md].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of samples per ray. Early termination reduces the average number of steps, but worst-case remains $N$.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ per pixel for accumulators, plus storage for volume and transfer function textures.
*   **Performance:** Improves rendering speed by skipping shading calculations for low-opacity samples and terminating rays that are sufficiently opaque. The specific implementation in [S1, unofficial] requires `nsamples > 500` to enable shading, which may impact performance on lower sample counts.
*   **Strengths:** Simple to implement in a fragment shader; effective at reducing aliasing artifacts from undersampling; reduces computational cost for opaque regions.
*   **Limitations:** Deterministic early termination is biased; dithering does not fix all aliasing types; shading is only applied if opacity exceeds a threshold, potentially missing subtle details in low-opacity regions.

## Background
This method is described in the context of a GPU-based volume rendering implementation using OpenGL and Qt [S1, unofficial]. It leverages Shader Model 3.0 features to perform ray marching within the fragment shader, extending earlier techniques by integrating dynamic branching for early termination and dithering for aliasing reduction [S1, unofficial]. The specific codebase is part of the `qvrc` project [S1, unofficial].
