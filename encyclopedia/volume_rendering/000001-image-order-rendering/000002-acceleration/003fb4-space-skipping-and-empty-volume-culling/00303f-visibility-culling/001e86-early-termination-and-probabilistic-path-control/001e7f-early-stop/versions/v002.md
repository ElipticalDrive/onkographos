# Early Stop

Also called: Early stop+0, Early stop+1

## Overview
Early Stop (ES) is an optimization for sphere tracing procedural volumes that limits the evaluation of noise frequencies ($f_i$) by breaking the summation loop as soon as the current position is determined to be outside the bounding volume of the current octave [S1]. It solves the problem of unnecessary computational cost when higher-frequency noise terms cannot possibly influence the isosurface intersection because their bounding volumes do not contain the isovalue [S1]. Unlike [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md) which skips entire ray segments, ES reduces the per-step cost by skipping function evaluations within a single step, trading a potential increase in total step count for fewer evaluations per step [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
- Isosurface value $v$.
- Procedural noise function defined by octaves $f_i$, where $i$ ranges from $0$ to $n$.
- Bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) where each level $i$ has an upper bound function $\text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$ and optionally a lower bound $\text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$.
- Parameter $k$: the number of additional octaves to compute beyond the minimum required index $i_{min}$.

**Outputs:**
- Intersection point $\mathbf{p}$ and distance $t$ along the ray.

**Data Structures:**
- **BVH Bounds:** For each octave $i$, the bounding volume is represented by inclusion functions. The upper bound $\text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$ provides a value such that $f_i(\mathbf{p}) \le \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$. The lower bound $\text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$ satisfies $f_i(\mathbf{p}) \ge \text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$. These bounds are typically computed using interval arithmetic or Taylor inclusion functions [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Initialize:** Set current ray position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o}$, accumulated distance $t = 0$, and maximum step limit [G].
2. **Ray Marching Loop:** While $\mathbf{p}$ has not converged to the isosurface within a tolerance $\epsilon$:
    a. **Find Minimum Index:** Determine $i_{\min}$, the first octave index where the position lies outside the bounding volume: $i_{\min} = \min\{i \mid \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p}) < v\}$ [S1]. If all bounds remain above $v$, set $i_{\min} = n$ [G].
    b. **Summation with Early Stop:** Compute the accumulated sum $S$ starting from $i_{\min}$ up to $i_{\min} + k$. Break the octave loop immediately after computing $f_{i_{\min}+k}$ [S1].
    c. **Step Size Calculation:** Calculate the safe step size $\Delta t$ using the accumulated sum and the derivative bounds of the computed octaves. Using fewer octaves (smaller $k$) typically yields smaller step sizes because the bounding volume of the truncated sum is tighter or the derivative estimate is less accurate [S1].
    d. **Advance Ray:** Update $\mathbf{p} \leftarrow \mathbf{p} + \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{d}$ and $t \leftarrow t + \Delta t$.
3. **Convergence Check:** If $\Delta t < \epsilon$, return $\mathbf{p}$ and $t$ [G].

**Formulas:**
- **Minimum Index Condition:**
  $$ i_{\min} = \min\{i \mid \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p}) < v\} $$
  [S1]
- **Alternative Condition (Inside Volume):**
  When inside the volume and searching for the isosurface, $i_{\min}$ can equivalently be defined using the lower bound:
  $$ \text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p}) > v $$
  [S1]
- **Summation Limit:**
  The octave loop breaks after computing:
  $$ f_{i_{\min}+k} $$
  [S1]

**Parameters:**
- $v$: The isovalue for the isosurface [S1].
- $k$: The number of additional functions to compute beyond $i_{\min}$. Typical value is $1$ (Early stop+1) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Early Stop+0 ($k=0$):** Computing only $f_{i_{\min}}$ causes slow convergence. The ray steps safely to the outer bounding volume and repeats, massively increasing the total number of steps, especially for grazing rays (rays passing close to but not entering a bounding volume) [S1].
- **Step Size Reduction:** Stopping early reduces the number of $f_i$ computed but may result in smaller step sizes, potentially increasing the total number of steps required to converge. This could raise the final cost if the overhead of extra steps outweighs the savings in function evaluations [S1].
- **Grazing Rays:** ES+0 is particularly bad for grazing rays. ES+1 drastically diminishes this performance loss by considering one extra level of the BVH, making slow convergence rare [S1].
- **Precision:** The bounds $\text{BVH}_{i+}$ and $\text{BVH}_{i-}$ must be tight enough to be useful. Loose bounds may cause premature termination or failure to detect intersections [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def early_stop_sphere_trace(ray_origin, ray_direction, isovalue, bvhs, noise_func, k=1, tolerance=1e-4, max_steps=1000):
    """
    Perform sphere tracing with Early Stop optimization.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: Initial ray position.
        ray_direction: Normalized ray direction.
        isovalue: The target isovalue v.
        bvhs: List of BVH bound objects. bvhs[i].upper_bound(p) returns BVH_i+(p).
        noise_func: Callable noise_func(i, p) returning f_i(p).
        k: Number of additional octaves to compute beyond i_min.
        tolerance: Convergence threshold.
        max_steps: Safety limit for ray marching iterations.
        
    Returns:
        Intersection point and distance, or None if no intersection.
    """
    p = ray_origin
    t = 0.0
    
    for _ in range(max_steps):
        # 1. Find i_min: first octave where upper bound drops below isovalue
        i_min = len(bvhs)
        for i in range(len(bvhs)):
            if bvhs[i].upper_bound(p) < isovalue:
                i_min = i
                break
                
        # 2. Compute sum from i_min to i_min + k
        accumulated_sum = 0.0
        upper_bounds = []
        for i in range(i_min, min(i_min + k + 1, len(bvhs))):
            accumulated_sum += noise_func(i, p)
            upper_bounds.append(bvhs[i].upper_bound(p))
            
        # 3. Calculate step size using standard sphere tracing logic with bounds [G]
        # Step size is the minimum distance to any bounding surface minus accumulated sum
        min_dist = float('inf')
        for ub in upper_bounds:
            dist = ub - accumulated_sum
            if dist < min_dist:
                min_dist = dist
                
        # Clamp to a safe minimum to prevent zero/negative steps
        step_size = max(min_dist, 1e-6)
        
        # 4. Advance ray
        p_new = p + step_size * ray_direction
        t += step_size
        
        # 5. Convergence check
        if step_size < tolerance:
            return p_new, t
            
        p = p_new
        
    return None, None
```

## Subtopics

### [Early Stop + k](1938-early-stop-k.md)
Early Stop + k (ES+k) is an optimization for sphere tracing procedural volumes that prevents unnecessary intermediate convergence checks during BVH traversal by computing $k$ additional octaves per traversal step. It builds on [Early Stop](1790-early-stop.md) by trading a small increase in per-step function evaluations for a significant reduction in the total number of BVH traversal steps. Unlike standard Early Stop (ES+0), which may converge too slowly and incur high step counts, ES+k maintains a balance between step reduction and computational overhead, with ES+1 generally offering the best performance across diverse scenes.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The cost per step is reduced by skipping $f_i$ evaluations for $i > i_{min} + k$. However, the total number of steps may increase due to smaller step sizes when $k$ is small [S1]. The total computation cost depends on the number of $f_i$ computed rather than just the number of steps [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** ES+1 provides results very similar to full computation (computing all $f_i$) but with significantly lower cost. ES+0 causes massive performance loss due to slow convergence [S1]. ES+k with $k>1$ provides diminishing returns compared to $k=1$ [S1].
- **Strengths:** Reduces per-step computational cost by avoiding unnecessary noise function evaluations. Effective for procedural volumes with hierarchical bounding structures.
- **Limitations:** May increase the total number of steps if step sizes become too small. Not suitable for $k=0$ due to grazing ray issues.
- **When to Use:** When rendering procedural volumetric noise with sphere tracing, especially in large and detailed scenes where noise evaluation is expensive.
- **When Not to Use:** When the overhead of BVH bound computation outweighs the savings from skipping noise evaluations, or when the noise function does not have well-defined bounding volumes.

## Background
Early Stop was introduced in the context of fast sphere tracing of procedural volumetric noise for very large and detailed scenes [S1]. It leverages the hierarchical nature of bounding volume hierarchies (BVH) used in sphere tracing to prune the computation of noise octaves. The method addresses the inefficiency of computing all noise frequencies even when higher frequencies cannot affect the isosurface intersection due to their bounding volumes [S1].
