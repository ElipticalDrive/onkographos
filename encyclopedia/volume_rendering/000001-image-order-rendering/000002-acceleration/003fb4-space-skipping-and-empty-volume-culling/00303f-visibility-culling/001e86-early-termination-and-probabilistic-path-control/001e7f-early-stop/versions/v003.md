# Early Stop

Also called: Early stop+0, Early stop+1

## Overview
Early Stop (ES) is an optimization for sphere tracing procedural volumes that limits the evaluation of noise frequencies ($f_i$) by breaking the summation loop as soon as the current position is determined to be outside the bounding volume of the current octave [S1]. It solves the problem of unnecessary computational cost when higher-frequency noise terms cannot possibly influence the isosurface intersection because their bounding volumes do not contain the isovalue [S1]. Unlike [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md) which skips entire ray segments, ES reduces the per-step cost by skipping function evaluations within a single step, trading a potential increase in total step count for fewer evaluations per step [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
- Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ [G].
- Isosurface value $v$ [S1].
- Procedural noise function defined by octaves $f_i$, where $i$ ranges from $0$ to $n$ [S1].
- Bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) where each level $i$ has an upper bound function $\text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$ and optionally a lower bound $\text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$ [S1].
- Parameter $k$: the number of additional octaves to compute beyond the minimum required index $i_{\min}$ [S1].

**Outputs:**
- Intersection point $\mathbf{p}$ and distance $t$ along the ray [G].

**Data Structures:**
- **BVH Bounds:** For each octave $i$, the bounding volume is represented by inclusion functions [S1]. The upper bound $\text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$ provides a value such that $f_i(\mathbf{p}) \le \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p})$ [S1]. The lower bound $\text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$ satisfies $f_i(\mathbf{p}) \ge \text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p})$ [S1]. These bounds are typically computed using interval arithmetic or Taylor inclusion functions [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**
1. **Initialize:** Set current ray position $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o}$, accumulated distance $t = 0$, and maximum step limit [G].
2. **Ray Marching Loop:** While $\mathbf{p}$ has not converged to the isosurface within a tolerance $\epsilon$:
    a. **Find Minimum Index:** Determine $i_{\min}$, the first octave index where the position lies outside the bounding volume: $i_{\min} = \min\{i \mid \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p}) < v\}$ [S1]. If all bounds remain above $v$, set $i_{\min} = n$ [G].
    b. **Summation with Early Stop:** Compute the accumulated sum $S$ starting from $i_{\min}$ up to $i_{\min} + k$. Break the octave loop immediately after computing $f_{i_{\min}+k}$ [S1].
    c. **Step Size Calculation:** Calculate the safe step size $\Delta t$ using the accumulated sum and the derivative bounds of the computed octaves [G]. Using fewer octaves (smaller $k$) typically yields smaller step sizes because the bounding volume of the truncated sum is tighter or the derivative estimate is less accurate [S1].
    d. **Advance Ray:** Update $\mathbf{p} \leftarrow \mathbf{p} + \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{d}$ and $t \leftarrow t + \Delta t$ [G].
3. **Convergence Check:** If $\Delta t < \epsilon$, return $\mathbf{p}$ and $t$ [G].

**Formulas:**
- **Minimum Index Condition:**
  $$ i_{\min} = \min\{i \mid \text{BVH}_{i+}(\mathbf{p}) < v\} $$
  [S1]
- **Alternative Condition (Inside Volume):**
  When inside the volume and searching for the isosurface, $i_{\min}$ can equivalently be defined using the lower bound:
  $$ \text{BVH}_{i-}(\mathbf{p}) > v $$
  [S1]
- **Summation Limit:**
  The octave loop breaks after computing:
  $$ f_{i_{\min}+k} $$
  [S1]

**Parameters:**
- $v$: The isovalue for the isosurface [S1].
- $k$: The number of additional functions to compute beyond $i_{\min}$. Typical value is $1$ (Early stop+1) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Early Stop+0 ($k=0$):** Computing only $f_{i_{\min}}$ causes slow convergence [S1]. The ray steps safely to the outer bounding volume and repeats, massively increasing the total number of steps, especially for grazing rays (rays passing close to but not entering a bounding volume) [S1].
- **Step Size Reduction:** Stopping early reduces the number of $f_i$ computed but may result in smaller step sizes, potentially increasing the total number of steps required to converge [S1]. This could raise the final cost if the overhead of extra steps outweighs the savings in function evaluations [S1].
- **Grazing Rays:** ES+0 is particularly bad for grazing rays [S1]. ES+1 drastically diminishes this performance loss by considering one extra level of the BVH, making slow convergence rare [S1].
- **Precision:** The bounds $\text{BVH}_{i+}$ and $\text{BVH}_{i-}$ must be tight enough to be useful [S1]. Loose bounds may cause premature termination or failure to detect intersections [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def early_stop_sphere_trace(ray_origin, ray_direction, isovalue, bvhs, noise_func, k=1, tolerance=1e-4, max_steps=1000):
    """
    Perform sphere tracing with Early Stop optimization.
    
    Args:
        ray_origin: Initial ray position.
        ray_direction: Normalized ray direction.
        isovalue: The target isovalue v.
        bvhs: List of BVH bound objects. bvhs[i].upper_bound(p) returns BVH_i+(p).
        noise_func: Callable noise_func(i, p) returning f_i(p).
        k: Number of additional octaves to compute beyond i_min.
        tolerance: Convergence threshold.
        max_steps: Safety limit for ray marching iterations.
        
    Returns:
        Intersection point and distance, or None if no intersection.
    """
    p = ray_origin
    t = 0.0
    
    for _ in range(max_steps):
        # 1. Find i_min: first octave where upper bound drops below isovalue
        i_min = len(bvhs)
        for i in range(len(bvhs)):
            if bvhs[i].upper_bound(p) < isovalue:
                i_min = i
                break
                
        # 2. Compute sum from i_min to i_min + k
        accumulated_sum = 0.0
        step_candidates = []
        for i in range(i_min, min(i_min + k + 1, len(bvhs))):
            accumulated_sum += noise_func(i, p)
            step_candidates.append(bvhs[i].upper_bound(p) - accumulated_sum)
            
        # 3. Calculate step size using standard sphere tracing logic [G]
        step_size = max(min(step_candidates), 1e-6)
        
        # 4. Advance ray
        p_new = p + step_size * ray_direction
        t += step_size
        
        # 5. Convergence check
        if step_size < tolerance:
            return p_new, t
            
        p = p_new
        
    return None, None
```

## Subtopics

### [Early Stop + k](1938-early-stop-k.md)
Early Stop + k (ES+k) is an optimization for sphere tracing procedural volumes that prevents unnecessary intermediate convergence checks during BVH traversal by computing $k$ additional octaves per traversal step. It builds on [Early Stop](1790-early-stop.md) by trading a small increase in per-step function evaluations for a significant reduction in the total number of BVH traversal steps. Unlike standard Early Stop (ES+0), which may converge too slowly and incur high step counts, ES+k maintains a balance between step reduction and computational overhead, with ES+1 generally offering the best performance across diverse scenes.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** The cost per step is reduced by skipping $f_i$ evaluations for $i > i_{\min} + k$ [S1]. However, the total number of steps may increase due to smaller step sizes when $k$ is small [S1]. The total computation cost depends on the number of $f_i$ computed rather than just the number of steps [S1].
- **Measured Performance:** ES+1 provides results very similar to full computation (computing all $f_i$) but with significantly lower cost [S1]. ES+0 causes massive performance loss due to slow convergence [S1]. ES+k with $k>1$ provides diminishing returns compared to $k=1$ [S1].
- **Strengths:** Reduces per-step computational cost by avoiding unnecessary noise function evaluations [S1]. Effective for procedural volumes with hierarchical bounding structures [S1].
- **Limitations:** May increase the total number of steps if step sizes become too small [S1]. Not suitable for $k=0$ due to grazing ray issues [S1].
- **When to Use:** When rendering procedural volumetric noise with sphere tracing, especially in large and detailed scenes where noise evaluation is expensive [S1].
- **When Not to Use:** When the overhead of BVH bound computation outweighs the savings from skipping noise evaluations, or when the noise function does not have well-defined bounding volumes [G].

## Background
Early Stop was introduced in the context of fast sphere tracing of procedural volumetric noise for very large and detailed scenes [S1]. It leverages the hierarchical nature of bounding volume hierarchies (BVH) used in sphere tracing to prune the computation of noise octaves [S1]. The method addresses the inefficiency of computing all noise frequencies even when higher frequencies cannot affect the isosurface intersection due to their bounding volumes [S1].
