# Early Ray Termination
Also called: ERT, opacity culling, transmittance cutoff

## Overview
Early Ray Termination (ERT) accelerates volume rendering by halting the integration loop along a ray when the accumulated transmittance drops below a specified threshold, indicating that the ray has become effectively opaque and subsequent samples contribute negligibly to the final pixel color [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial]. It differs from sibling methods like [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md), which bypasses empty regions entirely, by actively processing the volume until opacity accumulates, and from [Adaptive Sampling and Resolution](1412-adaptive-sampling-and-resolution.md), which varies step size, by keeping the step size fixed but reducing the number of evaluated samples [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial].

## Shared principles
ERT modifies the standard front-to-back compositing loop by introducing a termination condition based on accumulated transmittance $T$. The standard loop is:
$$ C_{acc} = C_{acc} + C_{sample} \cdot \alpha_{sample} \cdot T $$
$$ T = T \cdot (1 - \alpha_{sample}) $$
where $C_{sample}$ is the sample color, $\alpha_{sample}$ is the sample opacity, and $T$ is the accumulated transmittance initialized to 1.0 [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial].

In ERT, the loop continues only while $T > \tau$, where $\tau$ is a user-defined threshold (typically close to 0, e.g., 0.05 or 0.01, representing the minimum transmittance required for visibility) [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial]. When $T \le \tau$, the loop breaks, and the current $C_{acc}$ is returned as the pixel color [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial]. This optimization is most effective in volumes with large transparent regions or high-opacity objects that occlude the background [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Conservative Early Ray Termination](1949-conservative-early-ray-termination.md)
An optimization for scan conversion that prunes cell processing by determining if a ray is sufficiently opaque before rasterization, using an ERT-table and subscreen aggregation to avoid per-pixel overhead.

### [Early Ray Termination with Binary Mask](6641-early-ray-termination-with-binary-mask.md)
Combines front-to-back compositing with a binary mask to skip fully transparent regions by jumping directly to opaque voxels, terminating when accumulated transmittance drops below a threshold.

### [Hybrid Surface-Volume Rendering with Masking and Early Ray Termination](8155-hybrid-surface-volume-rendering-with-masking-and-early-ray-t.md)
A method combining volume and surface rendering that uses ERT for the volume part and masking for the surface part to reduce computation.

## Background
Early Ray Termination is a fundamental acceleration technique in image-order volume rendering, originating from the need to optimize the computationally expensive front-to-back compositing process [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial]. It is widely used in medical imaging, scientific visualization, and real-time rendering engines where performance is critical [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial]. The technique is often combined with other acceleration methods such as empty-space skipping and adaptive sampling to achieve further performance gains [S1, S2, S3, unofficial][S4][S5, S6, unofficial].
