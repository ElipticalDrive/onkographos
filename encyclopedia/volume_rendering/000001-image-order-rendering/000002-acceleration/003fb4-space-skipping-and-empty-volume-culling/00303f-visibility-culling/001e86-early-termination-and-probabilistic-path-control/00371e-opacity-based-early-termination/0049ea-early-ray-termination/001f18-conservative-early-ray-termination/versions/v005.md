# Conservative Early Ray Termination

Also called: ERT in scan conversion phase, ERT-table scheme

## Overview
Conservative Early Ray Termination (CERT) is an optimization that prunes unnecessary scan conversion of geometric cells by integrating visibility checking based on partially accumulated opacity and subscreen-based comparison [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard Early Ray Termination by operating during the scan conversion phase rather than the final compositing phase, using an ERT-table to avoid per-pixel overhead [S1, unofficial]. Unlike exact ERT, CERT checks a sufficient but not necessary condition for termination, trading potential speedup for significantly lower maintenance cost to enable parallel distributed execution [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Early Ray Termination](4675-early-ray-termination.md) and contrasts with [Early Termination for Gaussian Splatting](4764-early-termination-for-gaussian-splatting.md), [Early Ray Termination with Binary Mask](6641-early-ray-termination-with-binary-mask.md), and [Hybrid Surface-Volume Rendering with Masking and Early Ray Termination](8155-hybrid-surface-volume-rendering-with-masking-and-early-ray-t.md).

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume data represented as unstructured cells (e.g., tetrahedra) with vertex attributes [S1, unofficial].
*   Camera viewpoint and screen resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   An opacity threshold for early termination [S1, unofficial].
*   A subdivision of the screen into subscreens [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A rendered image [S1, unofficial].
*   An internal binary decision for each cell indicating whether to skip its scan conversion [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ray-Segment List:** For each pixel, a list of ray segments intersecting the pixel, ordered by depth [S1, unofficial]. Partial compositions generate new segments (e.g., $s_{ij}$ from $s_i$ and $s_j$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **ERT-Table:** A lookup table where each entry corresponds to a subscreen $S$ rather than a single pixel or cell projection area [S1, unofficial]. Each entry contains a one-bit flag indicating whether conditions C1 and C2 are satisfied for that subscreen, and a `max_term_Z` value representing the maximum depth among all subscreen's per-pixel front-most partially accumulated ray-segments whose opacity exceeds the threshold [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  Subdivide the screen into subscreens [S1, unofficial].
2.  For each subscreen $S$, maintain an ERT-table entry containing a one-bit flag and a `max_term_Z` value [S1, unofficial].
3.  For each cell $c$ to be rendered:
    a. Compute the projection area $R$ of cell $c$ on the screen [S1, unofficial].
    b. Check the corresponding ERT-table entries for the subscreens covering $R$ [S1, unofficial].
    c. If the one-bit flag is true for a subscreen, compare its `max_term_Z` with `nearest`, the depth value of the nearest vertex in cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
    d. If `max_term_Z` < `nearest`, terminate the scan conversion of cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
    e. Otherwise, proceed with standard scan conversion for cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  During scan conversion, maintain the ray-segment lists for pixels [S1, unofficial]. When a partial composition yields a ray segment with opacity exceeding the threshold, update the ERT-table entry for the subscreen containing that pixel by setting the flag to true and updating `max_term_Z` to the maximum of its current value and the segment's depth [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas and Conditions:**
The conditions to neglect scan conversion of cell $c$ are defined as [S1, unofficial]:
*   **C1:** There exists a nonempty ray-segment list for all pixels corresponding to the projection area $R$ for cell $c$.
*   **C2:** There exists a partially composed ray segment whose opacity is sufficiently high in each of these ray-segment lists.
*   **C3:** At least one of such ray-segments in each of the lists is located in front of the cell $c$.

CERT approximates these conditions by aggregating them per subscreen [S1, unofficial]. The termination condition is [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \text{max\_term\_Z} < \text{nearest} $$
Where $\text{max\_term\_Z}$ is the maximum depth value among all subscreen's per-pixel front-most partially accumulated ray-segments whose opacity exceeds the threshold, and $\text{nearest}$ is the depth value of the nearest vertex in the cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Opacity Threshold:** The value above which a ray segment is considered sufficiently high [S1, unofficial]. In the source evaluation, this was set to 0.9 [S1, unofficial].
*   **Subscreen Size:** Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Per-pixel Overhead:** A naive implementation checking conditions C1-C3 per cell projection area incurs significant overhead due to per-pixel searches on ray-segment lists [S1, unofficial]. The ERT-table alleviates this by aggregating status per subscreen [S1, unofficial].
*   **Conservatism:** CERT checks a sufficient, though not necessary, condition, which may reduce the effect of ERT but significantly reduces the cost to maintain the ERT-table information [S1, unofficial].
*   **Partial Composition Dependency:** The efficiency of CERT relies on partial composition increasing the opacity of ray segments to exceed the threshold more frequently [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Weak Sharing of ERT Information
A technique in parallel implementations where Working Nodes (WNs) exchange their ERT-table information to increase the accuracy/completeness of termination decisions across the system [S1, unofficial]. If WN$_a$ terminates scan conversion due to ERT, WN$_b$ (holding cells behind WN$_a$) can also terminate based on shared information [S1, unofficial]. The sharing frequency is set far beneath the frequency of ERT information updates at each WN to avoid interprocessor communication overhead [S1, unofficial]. This reduced scan conversion time by at most 43% (Viewpoint A) and 50% (Viewpoint B), with effective parallel performance improvements of 7% and 30% respectively [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The overhead of maintaining the ERT-table is $O(1)$ per subscreen per update, significantly less than per-pixel checks [S1, unofficial]. The scan conversion time is reduced by skipping cells [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires an ERT-table of size proportional to the number of subscreens, plus ray-segment lists per pixel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On a dataset of 307,565 tetrahedra, CERT provided a 3.86x speedup in scan conversion time in sequential execution using a 300×100 screen resolution [S1, unofficial]. In parallel execution, weak sharing of ERT information yielded up to 30% effective performance improvement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Applicable in the scan conversion phase; low overhead due to subscreen aggregation; effective in parallel distributed systems with weak sharing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Conservative approximation may miss some termination opportunities; effectiveness depends on the opacity threshold and data distribution; requires partial composition to be effective [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When rendering large unstructured volumes with significant opacity, especially in parallel environments where scan conversion is a bottleneck [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the volume is largely transparent or when the overhead of maintaining the ERT-table outweighs the savings from skipped cells [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Conservative Early Ray Termination (CERT) was introduced in the context of Cell-Projection Parallel Volume Rendering (CP-PVR) [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address load imbalance and inefficiency in parallel volume rendering by applying ERT concepts during the scan conversion phase, which was previously not feasible due to the lack of accumulated opacity information [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated on segmented human aorta simulation data consisting of 307,565 unstructured tetrahedral cells and 62,475 vertices [S1, unofficial].
