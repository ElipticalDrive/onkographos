# Conservative Early Ray Termination

Also called: ERT in scan conversion phase, ERT-table scheme

## Overview
Conservative Early Ray Termination (CERT) is an optimization for volume rendering that prunes unnecessary scan conversion of geometric cells by integrating visibility checking based on partially accumulated opacity and subscreen-based comparison [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from standard Early Ray Termination (ERT) by operating during the **scan conversion phase** rather than the final compositing phase, using an **ERT-table** to avoid per-pixel overhead [S1, unofficial]. Unlike exact ERT, CERT checks a sufficient but not necessary condition for termination, trading some potential speedup for significantly lower maintenance cost, making it applicable to parallel distributed systems [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume data represented as unstructured cells (e.g., tetrahedra) with vertex attributes (e.g., opacity, scalar values) [S1, unofficial].
*   Camera viewpoint and screen resolution [S1, unofficial].
*   A threshold for "sufficiently high" opacity (typically 0.9 in evaluations) [S1, unofficial].
*   Screen subdivision into subscreens [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A rendered image.
*   A binary decision for each cell: whether to skip its scan conversion.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Ray-Segment List:** For each pixel, a list of ray segments intersecting the pixel, ordered by depth. During scan conversion, partial compositions generate new segments (e.g., $s_{ij}$ from $s_i$ and $s_j$) [S1, unofficial].
2.  **ERT-Table:** A lookup table where each entry corresponds to a **subscreen** $S$ (not a single pixel or cell projection area) [S1, unofficial]. Each entry contains:
    *   A **one-bit flag** indicating whether conditions C1 and C2 are satisfied for that subscreen [S1, unofficial].
    *   A **`max_term_Z`** value: the maximum depth (rearmost depth) among all pixels in the subscreen whose front-most partially accumulated ray segment has opacity exceeding the threshold [S1, unofficial].

**Numbered Steps:**
1.  **Subdivide Screen:** Divide the rendering screen into a grid of subscreens [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Initialize ERT-Table:** For each subscreen $S$, initialize the one-bit flag to false and `max_term_Z` to $-\infty$ (or a value indicating no valid segment) [G].
3.  **Scan Conversion Loop:** For each cell $c$ to be rendered:
    a. Compute the projection area $R$ of cell $c$ on the screen [S1, unofficial].
    b. Identify the subscreens $S$ that intersect with $R$ [S1, unofficial].
    c. For each relevant subscreen $S$, check the ERT-table entry:
        i. Check if the one-bit flag is set (indicating C1 and C2 are satisfied for that subscreen) [S1, unofficial].
        ii. If the flag is set, retrieve `max_term_Z` for subscreen $S$ [S1, unofficial].
        iii. Determine `nearest`, the depth value of the nearest vertex of cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
        iv. **Termination Check:** If `max_term_Z` < `nearest`, terminate the scan conversion of cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
    d. If the termination check fails (or flag is not set), proceed with standard scan conversion for cell $c$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Update ERT-Table:** During the scan conversion of cells, maintain the ray-segment lists for pixels. When a partial composition results in a ray segment with opacity $> \text{threshold}$:
    a. Identify the pixel $p$ and its subscreen $S$ [S1, unofficial].
    b. If this is the front-most such segment for pixel $p$, update the ERT-table entry for $S$:
        i. Set the one-bit flag to true [S1, unofficial].
        ii. Update `max_term_Z` for $S$ to $\max(\text{current\_max\_term\_Z}, \text{depth of segment})$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas and Conditions:**
The conditions to neglect scan conversion of cell $c$ are defined as [S1, unofficial]:
*   **C1:** There exists a nonempty ray-segment list for all pixels corresponding to the projection area $R$ for cell $c$.
*   **C2:** There exists a partially composed ray segment whose opacity is sufficiently high (exceeds threshold) in each of these ray-segment lists.
*   **C3:** At least one of such ray-segments in each of the lists is located in front of the cell $c$.

CERT approximates these conditions by aggregating them per subscreen. The termination condition is [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \text{max\_term\_Z} < \text{nearest} $$
Where:
*   $\text{max\_term\_Z}$ is the maximum depth value among all subscreen's per-pixel front-most partially accumulated ray-segments whose opacity exceeds the threshold.
*   $\text{nearest}$ is the depth value of the nearest vertex in the cell $c$.

**Parameters:**
*   **Opacity Threshold:** The value above which a ray segment is considered "sufficiently high." In the source evaluation, this was set to **0.9** [S1, unofficial]. The exact value is not theoretically derived but chosen empirically [S1, unofficial].
*   **Subscreen Size:** Not specified in the sources. The choice affects the granularity of the ERT-table and the overhead of maintaining it [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Per-pixel Overhead:** A naive implementation checking conditions C1-C3 per cell projection area incurs significant overhead due to per-pixel searches on ray-segment lists [S1, unofficial]. The ERT-table alleviates this by aggregating status per subscreen [S1, unofficial].
*   **Conservatism:** CERT checks a sufficient, though not necessary, condition. This means it may miss opportunities for early termination (reducing speedup) but ensures correctness and lower cost [S1, unofficial].
*   **Partial Composition Dependency:** The efficiency of CERT relies on partial composition increasing the opacity of ray segments, making it more likely to exceed the threshold [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Weak Sharing of ERT Information
A technique in parallel implementations where Working Nodes (WNs) exchange their ERT-table information to increase the accuracy/completeness of termination decisions across the system [S1, unofficial]. If WN$_a$ terminates scan conversion due to ERT, WN$_b$ (holding cells behind WN$_a$) can also terminate based on shared information [S1, unofficial]. The sharing frequency is set far beneath the frequency of ERT information updates at each WN to avoid interprocessor communication overhead [S1, unofficial]. This reduced scan conversion time by at most 43% (Viewpoint A) and 50% (Viewpoint B), with effective parallel performance improvements of 7% and 30% respectively [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The overhead of maintaining the ERT-table is $O(1)$ per subscreen per update, significantly less than per-pixel checks [S1, unofficial]. The scan conversion time is reduced by skipping cells [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires an ERT-table of size proportional to the number of subscreens, plus ray-segment lists per pixel [S1, unofficial].
*   **Measured Performance:** On a dataset of 307,565 tetrahedra, CERT provided a **3.86x speedup** in scan conversion time in sequential execution (screen resolution 300x100) [S1, unofficial]. In parallel execution, weak sharing of ERT information yielded up to 30% effective performance improvement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Applicable in the scan conversion phase; low overhead due to subscreen aggregation; effective in parallel distributed systems with weak sharing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Conservative approximation may miss some termination opportunities; effectiveness depends on the opacity threshold and data distribution; requires partial composition to be effective [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When rendering large unstructured volumes with significant opacity, especially in parallel environments where scan conversion is a bottleneck [S1, unofficial].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the volume is largely transparent (low opacity) or when the overhead of maintaining the ERT-table outweighs the savings from skipped cells [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Conservative Early Ray Termination (CERT) was introduced in the context of Cell-Projection Parallel Volume Rendering (CP-PVR) [S1, unofficial]. It was developed to address load imbalance and inefficiency in parallel volume rendering by applying ERT concepts during the scan conversion phase, which was previously not feasible due to the lack of accumulated opacity information [S1, unofficial]. The method was evaluated on segmented human aorta simulation data [S1, unofficial].
