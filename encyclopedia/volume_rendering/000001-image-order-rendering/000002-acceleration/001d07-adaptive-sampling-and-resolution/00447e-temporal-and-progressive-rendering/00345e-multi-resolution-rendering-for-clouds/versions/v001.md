# Multi-Resolution Rendering for Clouds

Also called: Low-resolution first rendering for clouds, Progressive resolution cloud rendering

## Overview
Multi-resolution rendering for clouds is an acceleration technique that limits the total number of volume samples by initially rendering the scene at a low resolution to identify regions requiring detailed sampling, followed by higher-resolution passes in those specific areas [S1, unofficial]. This approach distinguishes itself from sibling methods like [Resolution Adaptation](3063-resolution-adaptation.md) by decoupling the coarse visibility/structure estimation from the fine detail accumulation, rather than dynamically adjusting step sizes or sampling probabilities per ray based on local error metrics. It addresses the high computational cost of volumetric cloud rendering by avoiding expensive high-resolution queries in empty or uniform sky regions.

## How it works
The method operates in two distinct phases: a coarse pass and a refinement pass.

**Inputs:**
- Cloud volume data (density field $\rho$, scattering properties).
- Camera parameters (view matrix, projection matrix).
- Target output resolution $R_{high}$ and initial low resolution $R_{low}$ (where $R_{low} \ll R_{high}$).

**Outputs:**
- Final accumulated color buffer $C_{final}$.

**Data Structures:**
- $C_{low}$: Color buffer at resolution $R_{low} \times R_{low}$.
- $C_{high}$: Color buffer at resolution $R_{high} \times R_{high}$, initialized to zero.
- $M_{mask}$: Binary mask at resolution $R_{high} \times R_{high}$ indicating regions to refine.

**Steps:**

1.  **Coarse Pass:** Render the cloud volume at resolution $R_{low}$. For each pixel $(u, v)$ in the low-res grid, cast a ray and perform standard volume integration (e.g., ray marching) to compute color $C_{low}(u, v)$. This pass uses a reduced number of samples or a lower-resolution density field to execute quickly [S1, unofficial].

2.  **Upsampling and Masking:** Upsample $C_{low}$ to the resolution $R_{high} \times R_{high}$ using bilinear or nearest-neighbor interpolation to create a base color estimate $C_{base}$. Simultaneously, generate a mask $M_{mask}$ to identify pixels that require refinement. A pixel is marked for refinement if:
    - The low-resolution color indicates significant density (e.g., $\rho_{avg} > \epsilon$).
    - The gradient of the low-resolution color exceeds a threshold, indicating edges or complex structures.
    - The low-resolution pass failed to converge or has high variance.
    [S1, unofficial]

3.  **Refinement Pass:** For each pixel $(i, j)$ in the high-resolution grid:
    - If $M_{mask}(i, j)$ is false, set $C_{high}(i, j) = C_{base}(i, j)$.
    - If $M_{mask}(i, j)$ is true, cast a ray at high resolution and perform volume integration with a higher sample count or finer step size. Compute the refined color $C_{refined}(i, j)$.
    - Combine the base and refined colors. A simple accumulation is:
      $$ C_{high}(i, j) = C_{refined}(i, j) $$
      Alternatively, the low-res result can serve as a bias or initial guess, but the primary goal is to replace the low-fidelity estimate with high-fidelity data only where necessary [S1, unofficial].

4.  **Final Output:** The buffer $C_{high}$ is the final rendered image.

**Parameters:**
- $R_{low}$: Typically $1/4$ to $1/16$ of $R_{high}$ in each dimension.
- $\epsilon$: Density threshold for mask generation. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases:**
- **Temporal Consistency:** Rapid changes in resolution masks between frames can cause flickering. The source notes that temporal accumulation is difficult to apply to clouds due to velocity vectors, implying that this method may require careful temporal filtering or is best suited for static or slowly moving scenes [S1, unofficial].
- **Aliasing:** Upsampling $C_{low}$ may introduce artifacts if the low-res pass did not adequately sample high-frequency details.

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{low} \cdot S_{low} + N_{refine} \cdot S_{high})$, where $N_{low}$ is the number of low-res pixels, $S_{low}$ is samples per low-res ray, $N_{refine}$ is the number of pixels marked for refinement, and $S_{high}$ is samples per high-res ray. Since $N_{refine} \ll N_{high}$ and $S_{low} \ll S_{high}$, this is significantly faster than full high-resolution rendering.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for two color buffers and a mask at high resolution.
- **Strengths:** Reduces total sample count by avoiding high-resolution queries in empty or uniform regions.
- **Limitations:** Requires two passes. Temporal stability is challenging. The source author did not implement this, so exact performance metrics are not provided [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This technique was proposed as a potential optimization for efficient volumetric cloud rendering. The author of the source considered it but did not implement it, suspecting it could be very effective. The idea is to use a low-resolution pass to guide a higher-resolution pass, limiting the number of expensive samples [S1, unofficial].
