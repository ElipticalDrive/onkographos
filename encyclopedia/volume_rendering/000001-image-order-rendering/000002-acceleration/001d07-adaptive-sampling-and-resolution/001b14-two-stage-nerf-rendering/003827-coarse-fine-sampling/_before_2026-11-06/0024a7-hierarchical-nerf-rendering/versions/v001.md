# Hierarchical NeRF Rendering

Also called: Coarse-Fine Rendering, Hierarchical Rendering Pipeline, Two-Pass NeRF

## Overview
Hierarchical NeRF Rendering is a two-pass volume rendering technique that improves sampling efficiency by first estimating scene density with a coarse pass, then using those estimates to importance-sample a fine pass [S1, S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from uniform ray marching by adaptively concentrating samples in high-density regions identified by the coarse network, thereby reducing computational waste in empty space while maintaining high-frequency detail [S1, S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** Ray origins $\mathbf{o}$, ray directions $\mathbf{d}$, near/far clipping planes $t_{near}, t_{far}$, number of coarse samples $N_c$, number of fine samples $N_f$, perturbation factor $\epsilon$, and chunk size for memory management.
**Outputs:** Final RGB color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$, accumulated opacity $\alpha(\mathbf{r})$, depth, and auxiliary coarse RGB for loss calculation.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Rays:** Tensor of shape $(N_{rays}, 3)$ for origins and directions.
*   **Coarse Samples:** $t_{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_c}$, $\mathbf{pts}_{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_c \times 3}$, $\mathbf{rgb}_{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_c \times 3}$, $\sigma_{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_c \times 1}$.
*   **Fine Samples:** $t_{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_f}$, $\mathbf{pts}_{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_f \times 3}$, $\mathbf{rgb}_{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_f \times 3}$, $\sigma_{f} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times N_f \times 1}$.
*   **Merged Samples:** $t_{all} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{rays} \times (N_c + N_f)}$.

**Parameters:**
*   $N_c$: Number of coarse samples. Default: 64 [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   $N_f$: Number of fine samples. Default: 128 [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   $\epsilon$: Perturbation for stratified sampling. Default: 1.0 (on) [S2, unofficial].
*   $chunk$: Batch size for model inference to avoid Out-Of-Memory (OOM). Default: 1024 points [S1, unofficial] or $1024 \times 32$ [S2, unofficial].
*   $netchunk$: Max points per network forward pass. Default: $1024 \times 64$ [S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**

1.  **Coarse Sampling:** Generate $N_c$ samples uniformly along the ray between $t_{near}$ and $t_{far}$. If $\epsilon > 0$, apply stratified sampling by adding noise $\mathcal{U}(0, \epsilon)$ to the sample positions [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ t_{c, i} = t_{near} + \frac{i + u}{N_c} (t_{far} - t_{near}), \quad u \sim \mathcal{U}(0, \epsilon) $$
    Compute 3D points: $\mathbf{pts}_{c} = \mathbf{o} + t_{c} \cdot \mathbf{d}$.

2.  **Coarse Evaluation:** Evaluate the coarse neural network $F_{\Theta_c}$ on $\mathbf{pts}_{c}$ in chunks of size $chunk$ to obtain color $\mathbf{rgb}_{c}$ and density $\sigma_{c}$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{rgb}_{c}, \sigma_{c} = F_{\Theta_c}(\mathbf{pts}_{c}, \mathbf{d}) $$

3.  **Coarse Volume Rendering:** Compute weights $w_{c, i}$ and accumulated color $\mathbf{C}_c$, depth $D_c$, and opacity $\alpha_c$ using standard volume rendering integration [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ \alpha_{c, i} = 1 - \exp(-\sigma_{c, i} \delta_{c, i}) $$
    $$ w_{c, i} = \alpha_{c, i} \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha_{c, j}) $$
    $$ \mathbf{C}_c = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} w_{c, i} \mathbf{rgb}_{c, i} $$
    Where $\delta_{c, i} = t_{c, i+1} - t_{c, i}$ (with $\delta_{c, N_c} = t_{far} - t_{c, N_c}$).

4.  **Fine Sampling (Importance Sampling):** Use the coarse weights $w_{c}$ as a probability distribution to sample $N_f$ additional points. This is done using inverse CDF sampling based on the cumulative sum of $w_{c}$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
    $$ t_{f} = \text{inverse\_cdf}(w_{c}, N_f) $$
    Compute 3D points: $\mathbf{pts}_{f} = \mathbf{o} + t_{f} \cdot \mathbf{d}$.

5.  **Fine Evaluation:** Evaluate the fine neural network $F_{\Theta_f}$ on $\mathbf{pts}_{f}$ in chunks to obtain $\mathbf{rgb}_{f}$ and $\sigma_{f}$ [S1, S2, unofficial].

6.  **Merge and Sort:** Concatenate coarse and fine depth values $t_{c}$ and $t_{f}$, then sort them in ascending order to form $t_{all}$ [S1, unofficial].
    $$ t_{all} = \text{sort}(\text{concat}(t_{c}, t_{f})) $$
    Compute corresponding merged points $\mathbf{pts}_{all}$ using $\mathbf{o}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ [S1, unofficial].

7.  **Fine Volume Rendering:** Evaluate the fine network on $\mathbf{pts}_{all}$ (if not already done for $t_f$) and render the final image using the merged samples [S1, unofficial].
    $$ \mathbf{C} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_c + N_f} w_{all, i} \mathbf{rgb}_{all, i} $$
    The coarse RGB $\mathbf{C}_c$ is attached to the output for auxiliary loss calculation [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def render_rays(model, rays_o, rays_d, near, far, N_c, N_f, perturb, white_bg, chunk):
    """
    Renders rays using hierarchical sampling.
    rays_o: (N_rays, 3)
    rays_d: (N_rays, 3)
    """
    # 1. Coarse Sampling
    # Generate uniform stratified samples
    t_c = stratified_sample(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, N_c, perturb)
    pts_c = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_c.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # 2. Coarse Evaluation
    rgb_c, sigma_c = evaluate_model_chunks(model.forward_coarse, pts_c, rays_d, chunk)
    
    # 3. Coarse Volume Rendering
    # Compute deltas
    deltas = torch.cat([t_c[:, 1:] - t_c[:, :-1], 
                        torch.tensor([far - t_c[:, -1]]).to(t_c.device)], dim=-1)
    
    # Compute alphas and weights
    alpha_c = 1 - torch.exp(-sigma_c * deltas)
    transmittance_c = torch.cumprod(1 - alpha_c + 1e-10, dim=1)
    weights_c = alpha_c * transmittance_c[:, :-1]
    
    # Render coarse color
    rgb_coarse = torch.sum(weights_c.unsqueeze(-1) * rgb_c, dim=1)
    
    # 4. Fine Sampling (Importance Sampling)
    # Sample from coarse weights
    t_f = importance_sample(t_c, weights_c.detach(), N_f, perturb)
    pts_f = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_f.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # 5. Fine Evaluation
    rgb_f, sigma_f = evaluate_model_chunks(model.forward_fine, pts_f, rays_d, chunk)
    
    # 6. Merge and Sort
    t_all = torch.sort(torch.cat([t_c, t_f], dim=-1), dim=-1).values
    pts_all = rays_o.unsqueeze(1) + rays_d.unsqueeze(1) * t_all.unsqueeze(-1)
    
    # Re-evaluate fine model on all merged points if necessary, 
    # or assume pts_f covers the new points and pts_c covers old.
    # In practice, we often re-evaluate the fine model on pts_all 
    # or merge rgb_c/sigma_c with rgb_f/sigma_f. 
    # Based on S1: "Evaluate fine model in chunks... on pts_flat2"
    rgb_all, sigma_all = evaluate_model_chunks(model.forward_fine, pts_all, rays_d, chunk)
    
    # 7. Fine Volume Rendering
    deltas_all = torch.cat([t_all[:, 1:] - t_all[:, :-1], 
                            torch.tensor([far - t_all[:, -1]]).to(t_all.device)], dim=-1)
    
    alpha_all = 1 - torch.exp(-sigma_all * deltas_all)
    transmittance_all = torch.cumprod(1 - alpha_all + 1e-10, dim=1)
    weights_all = alpha_all * transmittance_all[:, :-1]
    
    rgb_final = torch.sum(weights_all.unsqueeze(-1) * rgb_all, dim=1)
    
    return {
        "rgb_map": rgb_final,
        "rgb_coarse": rgb_coarse,
        "depth": torch.sum(weights_all.unsqueeze(-1) * t_all, dim=1),
        "acc": torch.sum(weights_all, dim=1)
    }

def stratified_sample(rays_o, rays_d, near, far, N, perturb):
    """Generate N stratified samples between near and far."""
    t_vals = torch.linspace(0., 1., N + 1)
    t_vals = near + (far - near) * t_vals
    # Add noise for stratification
    if perturb > 0:
        mids = 0.5 * (t_vals[..., 1:] + t_vals[..., :-1])
        upper = torch.cat([mids, t_vals[..., -1:]], -1)
        lower = torch.cat([t_vals[..., :1], mids], -1)
        t_rand = torch.rand(t_vals.shape).to(t_vals.device) * perturb
        t_vals = lower + (upper - lower) * t_rand
    return t_vals

def importance_sample(t_c, weights, N_f, perturb):
    """Sample N_f points based on cumulative weights."""
    # Compute cumulative sum of weights
    cumsum = torch.cumsum(weights, dim=-1)
    # Normalize to [0, 1]
    cumsum = cumsum / cumsum[..., -1:]
    
    # Generate uniform random numbers
    u = torch.rand(cumsum.shape[0], N_f).to(cumsum.device)
    if perturb > 0:
        u = u * (1. - 1e-5) + 1e-5
    
    # Inverse CDF lookup
    # Find indices where u falls in the cumulative sum
    indices = torch.searchsorted(cumsum, u, side='right')
    # Clamp indices
    indices = torch.clamp(indices, 0, N_c - 1)
    
    # Interpolate or select t_vals at indices
    # Simple selection:
    t_f = torch.gather(t_c, 1, indices)
    return t_f

def evaluate_model_chunks(model_fn, pts, dirs, chunk):
    """Evaluate model in chunks to manage memory."""
    N = pts.shape[0]
    rgb_list = []
    sigma_list = []
    
    for i in range(0, N, chunk):
        pts_chunk = pts[i:i+chunk]
        dirs_chunk = dirs[i:i+chunk]
        rgb, sigma = model_fn(pts_chunk, dirs_chunk)
        rgb_list.append(rgb)
        sigma_list.append(sigma)
        
    return torch.cat(rgb_list, dim=0), torch.cat(sigma_list, dim=0)
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** When computing transmittance, use `1e-10` epsilon to avoid `log(0)` or `exp(-inf)` issues in dense regions [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Boundaries:** Ensure $\delta$ for the last sample uses $t_{far}$, not a non-existent next sample [S1, unofficial].
*   **Degenerate Input:** If $N_c=0$, importance sampling fails. Ensure $N_c \ge 1$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory:** Large $N_c$ and $N_f$ combined with high-resolution rays can exceed GPU memory. Use `chunk` size to limit simultaneous evaluations [S1, S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N_{rays} \cdot (N_c + N_f))$ per ray. The coarse pass is $O(N_{rays} \cdot N_c)$ and the fine pass is $O(N_{rays} \cdot (N_c + N_f))$ due to merging and re-evaluation.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_{rays} \cdot (N_c + N_f))$ for storing merged samples and intermediate tensors.
*   **Measured Performance:** With $N_c=64, N_f=128$, it provides a significant quality improvement over uniform sampling with $N=128$ while being computationally cheaper than uniform sampling with $N=256$ [S1, S2, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Efficiently allocates samples to high-density regions; reduces noise in empty space; simple to implement as a drop-in replacement for uniform sampling.
*   **Limitations:** Requires two network evaluations (coarse and fine) or a single network evaluated twice; sorting merged samples adds overhead; may still undersample if the coarse pass misses thin structures.
*   **When to Use:** Standard NeRF training and rendering where quality is prioritized over raw speed.
*   **When Not to Use:** Real-time applications requiring strict frame budgets without coarse-fine trade-off tuning; scenes with extremely thin structures that the coarse pass might miss entirely.

## Background
Hierarchical NeRF Rendering was introduced in the original NeRF paper (Mildenhall et al., 2020) as a core component of the training pipeline [S1, S2, unofficial]. It builds on the concept of importance sampling in volume rendering, adapting it to the learned density field. The method has become a standard baseline for NeRF implementations, with variations in coarse/fine sample counts and network architectures [S1, S2, unofficial].
