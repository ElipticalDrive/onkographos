# Multiresolution Atlas Buffer (MAB)

Also called: VR DVR acceleration wrapper, bracket approach for VR raycasting, gaze-dependent resolution DVR

## Overview
The Multiresolution Atlas Buffer (MAB) is an acceleration wrapper for Direct Volume Rendering (DVR) in Virtual Reality (VR) that reduces ray density adaptively based on peripheral vision and Head-Mounted Display (HMD) lens distortion, then reconstructs the full-resolution image via compositing [S1]. It differs from standard foveated rendering by not requiring high-speed eye-tracking hardware, relying instead on the fixed nature of HMD lenses and the known non-uniform resolution of the human visual system [S1]. Unlike [Level-of-detail (LOD) methods](1007-level-of-detail-lod-methods.md) which often modify the geometry or volume data structure itself, MAB operates as a "bracket" around existing raycasting implementations, modifying ray construction and post-traversal compositing without altering the core traversal logic [S1].

## How it works
The method consists of two main phases: adaptive ray density reduction (the "opening brace") and multi-resolution compositing (the "closing brace") [S1].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Volume dataset and transfer function (for the underlying raycaster).
    *   Current HMD frame buffer dimensions $(W, H)$ [S1].
    *   Gaze center position in screen space $(u_g, v_g)$ [S1].
    *   Current frame timing metrics (to adjust resolution for the next frame) [S1].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Final high-resolution image for the HMD [S1].

### Data Structures
**Multiresolution Atlas Buffer (MAB):**
*   A 2D texture or buffer partitioned into multiple non-overlapping regions [S1].
*   Each region corresponds to the entire frame buffer at a specific resolution level $L_i$.
*   Resolution levels $L_0, L_1, \dots, L_n$ are ordered to minimize the overall framebuffer size [S1].
*   The scaling ratio between subsequent levels is arbitrary and not necessarily a power of two [S1].
*   Regions with no active rays (black areas) are discarded before ray traversal to save computation [S1].

### Mapping Functions
A bidirectional mapping between the MAB space and the HMD Frame Buffer (FB) space is mandatory [S1].

1.  **Forward Mapping (Ray Construction):**
    *   Determines which MAB regions receive rays.
    *   Assigns every MAB fragment a unique position in the framebuffer and a blending factor for the final composite [S1].
2.  **Inverse Mapping (Compositing):**
    *   Assigns each framebuffer fragment several MAB fragments and blending factors [S1].
    *   Computed using two functions for each framebuffer fragment at screen coordinates $(u, v)$:
        *   $f_a(u, v)$: Takes the distance to the center of gaze $d = \sqrt{(u-u_g)^2 + (v-v_g)^2}$ and outputs a set of blending factors $\{ \alpha_i \}$ for the MAB levels [S1].
        *   $f_b(u, v)$: Takes the screen-space coordinates $(u, v)$ and outputs a set of corresponding coordinates $\{ (u_i, v_i) \}$ in the respective MAB regions [S1].

### Algorithm Steps

1.  **Initialization (Per Frame):**
    *   Evaluate frame timings from the previous frame [S1].
    *   Adjust rendering parameters (e.g., number of resolution levels, scaling ratios) for the next subsampling stage to maintain a consistent refresh rate [S1].

2.  **Ray Construction (Opening Brace):**
    *   Iterate through the MAB regions.
    *   For each region at resolution level $L_i$, generate primary rays corresponding to the pixels in that region.
    *   The density of rays is determined by the resolution of the region; peripheral regions have lower resolution (fewer rays) [S1].
    *   Forward rays to the existing DVR raycaster [S1].

3.  **Ray Traversal:**
    *   The underlying raycaster traverses the volume for each generated ray and stores the resulting color/opacity into the corresponding MAB region [S1].

4.  **Compositing (Closing Brace):**
    *   Render a full-screen primitive covering the entire output HMD framebuffer [S1].
    *   For each fragment generated by the primitive at screen coordinates $(u, v)$:
        *   Compute gaze distance $d$ from $(u, v)$ to gaze center $(u_g, v_g)$ [S1].
        *   Compute blending factors $\{ \alpha_i \} = f_a(d)$ [S1].
        *   Compute MAB coordinates $\{ (u_i, v_i) \} = f_b(u, v)$ [S1].
        *   Initialize output color $C_{out} = 0$.
        *   For each level $i$ where $\alpha_i > 0$:
            *   Sample color $C_i$ from the MAB at coordinates $(u_i, v_i)$ [S1].
            *   Accumulate: $C_{out} = C_{out} + \alpha_i \cdot C_i$ [S1].
        *   Write $C_{out}$ to the HMD framebuffer [S1].

```python
def mab_rendering_loop(volume_data, transfer_function, hmd_width, hmd_height, gaze_center):
    """
    Main loop for MAB rendering.
    """
    # 1. Determine Resolution Levels
    # Levels are ordered to minimize total framebuffer size.
    # Ratios between levels are arbitrary (not necessarily powers of 2).
    levels = compute_resolution_levels(hmd_width, hmd_height)
    
    # Initialize MAB buffer with regions for each level
    mab_buffer = create_multiresolution_buffer(levels)
    
    while rendering_active:
        # 2. Adaptive Parameter Adjustment
        # Evaluate frame timings from previous frame
        frame_time = get_current_frame_time()
        target_frame_time = 1.0 / 90.0  # Example: 90 Hz target
        levels = adjust_resolution_levels(levels, frame_time, target_frame_time)
        
        # 3. Opening Brace: Adaptive Ray Density Reduction
        # Clear MAB buffer
        clear_buffer(mab_buffer)
        
        for level in levels:
            # Generate rays for this resolution level
            # Only rays corresponding to non-black (active) regions are generated
            rays = generate_rays_for_region(level, hmd_width, hmd_height)
            
            # Traverse rays using existing DVR raycaster
            for ray in rays:
                color = raycast_volume(volume_data, transfer_function, ray)
                # Store result in the corresponding MAB region
                store_in_mab(mab_buffer, level, ray.screen_coords, color)
        
        # 4. Closing Brace: Compositing
        # Render full-screen primitive to reconstruct final image
        for y in range(hmd_height):
            for x in range(hmd_width):
                screen_coords = (x, y)
                
                # Compute distance to gaze center
                dx = x - gaze_center.x
                dy = y - gaze_center.y
                gaze_dist = sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy)
                
                # Compute blending factors and MAB coordinates
                blending_factors = f_a(gaze_dist)
                mab_coords = f_b(screen_coords)
                
                composite_color = 0.0
                for i, factor in enumerate(blending_factors):
                    if factor > 0:
                        # Lookup color in MAB
                        mab_color = sample_mab(mab_buffer, mab_coords[i])
                        composite_color += factor * mab_color
                
                write_to_hmd_framebuffer(screen_coords, composite_color)
```

### Parameters and Typical Values
*   **HMD Update Rate:** At least 90 Hz, with a tendency toward 120 Hz or more [S1].
*   **HMD Resolution:** Around 2880 x 1600 pixels [S1].
*   **Resolution Scaling:** Arbitrary ratios between levels; not restricted to powers of two [S1].
*   **Gaze Model:** Center of gaze is assumed to be the center of the screen or a fixed point relative to the HMD, as rapid eye movements are not tracked [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Eye Tracking Absence:** The method does not account for rapid eye movements because HMD lenses are fixed and provide only a slight angle with the highest projected resolution [S1]. This may lead to visual artifacts if the user's gaze deviates significantly from the assumed center during fast saccades.
*   **Blending Artifacts:** The quality of the final image depends on the smoothness of $f_a$ and $f_b$. Discontinuities in blending factors or coordinate mapping can cause visible seams or popping [S1].
*   **Precision:** The bidirectional mapping must be precise to avoid misalignment between the low-resolution rays and the high-resolution composite [S1].

## Subtopics

### FAVR (Foveated Adaptive Volume Rendering)
The predecessor to MAB, which also considers HMD lens distortion but was less generalized as a wrapper [S1].

### MIP-Maps
MAB is conceptually similar to MIP-maps but allows arbitrary scaling ratios between levels, whereas MIP-maps typically use fixed power-of-two ratios [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The ray traversal cost is reduced proportional to the number of rays generated in the MAB, which is less than the full HMD resolution. The compositing step is $O(W \cdot H)$, where $W, H$ are the HMD dimensions.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the MAB, which is the sum of the areas of all resolution levels. This is typically less than the full HMD framebuffer area due to the ordering of levels to minimize size [S1].
*   **Performance:** Designed to guarantee stable high refresh rates (90-120 Hz) on VR hardware, making DVR feasible for high-resolution HMDs (e.g., 2880 x 1600) [S1].
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Minimal integration effort ("bracket" approach) [S1].
    *   Adapts to hardware performance in real-time [S1].
    *   Leverages HMD lens distortion and HVS properties without expensive eye-tracking [S1].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Does not handle rapid eye movements (saccades) [S1].
    *   Potential for visual artifacts at resolution boundaries if blending is not smooth [S1].
*   **When to Use:** VR setups with HMDs where DVR is performance-bound and stable high refresh rates are critical to avoid cyber-sickness [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** Desktop VR without HMDs, or scenarios requiring precise foveated rendering based on actual eye-tracking data [S1].

## Background
The Multiresolution Atlas Buffer (MAB) method was introduced by Jens Krüger and Andre Waschk in the WSCG 2022 Proceedings (CSRN 3201) [S1]. It builds upon the Foveated Adaptive Volume Rendering (FAVR) method by Waschk and Krüger (2020) and earlier gaze-dependent rendering works [S1]. The method addresses the challenge of rendering high-resolution volumes in VR, where the close proximity of the display and high refresh rate requirements (to avoid cyber-sickness) make standard DVR computationally expensive [S1]. By exploiting the non-uniform resolution of the human visual system and the fixed nature of HMD lenses, MAB reduces the number of primary rays while maintaining visual quality in the central field of view [S1].
