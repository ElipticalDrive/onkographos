# Adaptive Sampling (Sparse Grid)

Also called: Sparse Grid Adaptive Sampling

## Overview
Adaptive Sampling (Sparse Grid) is a rendering optimization that exploits coherence in projections—often introduced by sensing device blurring—to reduce computational overhead by casting a sparse grid of rays (less than one per pixel) and adaptively increasing ray density in regions of high image complexity [S1, unofficial]. It differs from uniform sampling by prioritizing high-frequency details and from pure brute-force methods by achieving savings of more than two orders of magnitude for many datasets [S1, unofficial]. It is particularly well-suited for scan-conversion methods where polygons are filtered prior to ray tracing [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A volumetric dataset or scene geometry; an initial sparse grid resolution; a target display resolution [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A rendered image formed by interpolation and resampling of sample colors [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sample Array:** A non-uniform array storing colors at specific ray-casting locations [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Initialize Sparse Grid:** Cast rays from an initial sparse grid configuration. The initial sampling rate is less than one ray per pixel [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Form Initial Image:** Interpolate the resulting non-uniform array of sample colors and resample them at the target display resolution to form a crude image [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Assess Complexity:** Identify regions of high image complexity that require higher sampling density [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Adaptive Refinement:** Cast additional rays in the identified high-complexity regions [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Update Sample Array:** Add the colors from the new rays to the existing sample array [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Re-render:** Repeat the interpolation and resampling steps to generate a successively more refined image [S1, unofficial].
7.  **Iterate:** Continue steps 3-6 to generate a sequence of images with gradually improving quality [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   **Initial Sampling Rate:** Must be low enough to be "sparse" (less than one ray per pixel) but high enough to avoid missing small features if integrated with other techniques [S1, unofficial].
*   **Refinement Threshold:** The specific criteria for determining "high complexity" are Not given in the sources.

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Missing Small Polygons:** When integrated with adaptive supersampling in a hybrid ray tracer, there is a risk of missing small polygons if the initial sampling rate is too low [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def adaptive_sparse_grid_rendering(scene, initial_grid_res, target_res, max_iterations):
    sample_array = []
    # 1. Initialize: cast rays from initial sparse grid (<1 ray per pixel)
    for x in range(0, target_res[0], initial_grid_res[0]):
        for y in range(0, target_res[1], initial_grid_res[1]):
            sample_array.append({"pos": (x, y), "color": scene.sample((x, y))})
            
    for _ in range(max_iterations):
        # 2. Form image: interpolate and resample non-uniform samples to display resolution
        image = interpolate_and_resample(sample_array, target_res)
        
        # 3. Assess complexity: identify regions of high image complexity
        high_complexity_regions = []
        for sample in sample_array:
            if image.variance(sample["pos"]) > THRESHOLD:
                high_complexity_regions.append(sample["pos"])
                
        if not high_complexity_regions:
            break
            
        # 4 & 5. Adaptive refinement: cast rays and add colors to sample array
        for pos in high_complexity_regions:
            sample_array.append({"pos": pos, "color": scene.sample(pos)})
            
    # 6 & 7. Final output
    return interpolate_and_resample(sample_array, target_res)
```
*Note: `scene.sample`, `interpolate_and_resample`, and `image.variance` represent standard ray-scene intersection, spatial interpolation/resampling, and local variance metrics respectively [G].*

## Subtopics

### Adaptive Point Insertion Strategy
A strategy that intelligently prioritizes topologically critical regions to optimize computational resources, improving topological fidelity compared to uniform sampling [S2]. It starts from an $8^3$ grid and expands to a total of $50^3$ points (including sample points and projections) using the adaptive approach [S2]. Reconstruction quality correlates directly with the proportion of adaptively inserted samples; a higher percentage of points added via quality-driven criteria yields superior topological fidelity [S2]. This method is particularly effective for high-genus models, preserving intricate details that conventional methods (like Marching Cubes or RFTA) may miss [S2].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The cost of ray tracing volumetric data using a hierarchical enumeration grows linearly with the size of the image, logarithmically with the length of each ray, and linearly with the depth complexity of the data [S1, unofficial]. Adaptive termination reduces dependence on depth complexity [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the size of the sample array, which grows with the number of adaptive rays cast [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Savings of more than two orders of magnitude over brute-force rendering methods have been obtained for many datasets [S1, unofficial]. Crude images can be obtained in seconds, with high-quality images in less than a minute [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Significant performance gains; good for data with coherence (e.g., from sensing devices); allows progressive refinement [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Risk of missing small features if initial sampling is too low [S1, unofficial].

## Background
This method was introduced by Marc Levoy in 1988 as an optimization for rendering volumetric data [S1, unofficial]. It takes advantage of coherence in projections, which is often present in data acquired from sensing devices where the acquisition process introduces considerable blurring [S1, unofficial]. The method was implemented in C on a Sun 4/280 with 32MB of main memory [S1, unofficial].
