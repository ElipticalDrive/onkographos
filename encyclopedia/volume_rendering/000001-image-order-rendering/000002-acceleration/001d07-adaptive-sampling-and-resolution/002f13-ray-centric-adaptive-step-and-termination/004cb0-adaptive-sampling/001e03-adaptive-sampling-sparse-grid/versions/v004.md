# Adaptive Sampling (Sparse Grid)
Also called: Sparse Grid Adaptive Sampling
## Overview
Adaptive Sampling (Sparse Grid) is a rendering optimization that exploits coherence in volumetric projections by casting a sparse grid of rays (fewer than one per pixel) and adaptively increasing ray density in regions of high image complexity [S1, unofficial]. It differs from its parent [Adaptive Sampling](1385-adaptive-sampling.md) by explicitly using a sparse initial grid structure to reduce bricking overhead, and from [Adaptive Sampling with Relative Standard Error](4545-adaptive-sampling-with-relative-standard-error.md) by relying on spatial complexity heuristics rather than statistical error bounds for refinement.
## How it works
**Inputs:** Volumetric dataset; display resolution; initial sparse grid configuration.
**Outputs:** Final rendered image; intermediate sequence of refined images.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Sample Array:** A non-uniform array storing color values at specific ray intersection points. Layout: List of `(x, y, color)` tuples, where `x, y` are pixel coordinates and `color` is the accumulated radiance.
*   **Refinement Map:** Implicit or explicit map tracking ray density per pixel region to identify high-complexity areas.

**Steps:**
1.  **Initial Sparse Casting:** Cast a sparse grid of rays through the volume. The density is less than one ray per pixel on average [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Interpolation:** Form an initial image from the non-uniform array of sample colors by interpolating the sparse samples to the full display resolution [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Complexity Analysis:** Identify regions of high image complexity (e.g., high frequency, sharp edges) in the interpolated image.
4.  **Adaptive Refinement:** Cast additional rays in identified high-complexity regions. Add the resulting colors to the sample array [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Resampling:** Repeat the interpolation and resampling steps to integrate new samples into the image [S1, unofficial].
6.  **Iteration:** Repeat steps 3-5 to generate a sequence of successively more refined images until convergence or time limit [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def adaptive_sparse_grid_render(volume, display_res, initial_density):
    # Step 1: Initial sparse casting
    sample_array = []
    for x, y in sparse_grid_coords(display_res, initial_density):
        color = cast_ray(volume, x, y)
        sample_array.append((x, y, color))
    
    # Step 2: Initial interpolation
    image = interpolate_and_resample(sample_array, display_res)
    
    # Step 4-6: Adaptive refinement loop
    while not converged(image):
        # Identify high complexity regions
        complex_regions = find_high_complexity_regions(image)
        
        # Cast more rays in complex regions
        for region in complex_regions:
            for x, y in dense_grid_coords(region, display_res):
                # Check if sample already exists to avoid redundancy
                if (x, y) not in sample_array:
                    color = cast_ray(volume, x, y)
                    sample_array.append((x, y, color))
        
        # Resample to update image
        image = interpolate_and_resample(sample_array, display_res)
        
    return image
```

**Parameters:**
*   `initial_density`: Ratio of rays to pixels. Must be $< 1$ [S1, unofficial].
*   `convergence`: Threshold for image quality or maximum iterations.

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Small Polygons:** If integrated with adaptive supersampling in a hybrid ray tracer, missing small polygons occurs if the initial sampling rate is too low [S1, unofficial].
*   **Blurring:** Method is particularly effective for data acquired from sensing devices where acquisition induces considerable blurring, as coherence is higher [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:** The cost of ray tracing volumetric data using hierarchical enumeration grows linearly with image size, logarithmically with ray length, and linearly with depth complexity [S1, unofficial]. Adaptive termination reduces dependence on depth complexity, becoming nearly linear with image size for opaque environments [S1, unofficial].
**Memory Complexity:** Linear with the number of samples stored in the array.
**Performance:** Savings of more than two orders of magnitude over brute-force methods have been obtained for many datasets [S1, unofficial]. Crude images can be obtained in seconds, with high quality in less than a minute [S1, unofficial].
**Strengths:** Exploits coherence; allows progressive refinement; reduces bricking overhead.
**Limitations:** Risk of missing small features if initial sampling is too sparse; interpolation artifacts may occur in low-coherence regions.
## Background
Originated from Michael Levoy's work on rendering mixtures of geometric and volumetric data (1988) [S1, unofficial]. The method was developed to optimize ray tracing by leveraging coherence in projections, particularly for data from sensing devices. It is well-suited for scan-conversion methods where polygons are filtered prior to ray tracing [S1, unofficial].
