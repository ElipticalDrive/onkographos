# Coarse-to-Fine Hierarchical Sampling

Also called: Hierarchical Sampling, Two-Stage Sampling, Proposal Sampling

## Overview
Coarse-to-Fine Hierarchical Sampling is a strategy that uses a lightweight "coarse" network to estimate the opacity distribution along a ray, then uses that estimate to guide a denser, "fine" sampling pass. It solves the problem of inefficient uniform sampling by concentrating computational effort in regions of high opacity while skipping free space . It sets itself apart from uniform sampling by clustering additional samples near opaque regions identified by the coarse pass, and from single-network variants like [Mip-NeRF 360 Proposal Sampling](5408-mip-nerf-360-proposal-sampling.md) by maintaining two distinct MLPs with separate parameters .

## Shared principles
The family shares the mechanism of decoupling the estimation of the sampling distribution from the final color/density prediction.

1.  **Coarse Estimation:** A coarse network $\text{MLP}_{coarse}$ is queried at $N_C$ uniformly (or stratified) spaced depths $t_c$ along the ray $[\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{d}]$. It outputs raw densities $\sigma_c$ and colors $\mathbf{c}_c$.
2.  **Weight Computation:** Alpha compositing is applied to the coarse outputs to compute weights $w_c$:
    $$ \alpha_i = 1 - \exp(-\hat{\sigma}_i \delta_i) $$
    $$ T_i = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \hat{\sigma}_j \delta_j\right) $$
    $$ w_i = \alpha_i T_i $$
    where $\delta_i$ is the segment length and $\hat{\sigma}$ may include noise .
3.  **Hierarchical Sampling:** $N_F$ new depths $t_f$ are sampled from the distribution defined by $w_c$. This is typically done via inverse transform sampling using the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of $w_c$ .
4.  **Gradient Cut:** The coarse samples $t_c$ and weights $w_c$ are detached from the computation graph before hierarchical sampling to prevent gradients from flowing back through the non-differentiable sampling step to the coarse network .
5.  **Fine Rendering:** The coarse and fine depths are concatenated, sorted, and queried by $\text{MLP}_{fine}$. The final color is rendered using alpha compositing on the fine samples .

## Subtopics

### [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md)
The canonical implementation using stratified uniform coarse sampling and inverse transform sampling for the fine pass. It uses a fixed heuristic for the PDF construction.

### [TermiNeRF](8828-terminerf.md)
Replaces the heuristic coarse pass with a learned **Sampling Network** that predicts weights for bins along a parameterized ray segment. It uses centered-logarithmic sampling and max-resampling to align ground truth weights from a pre-trained color network.

### [NeRF-ID](8867-nerf-id.md)
Replaces the heuristic coarse-to-fine sampling with a differentiable **Proposer** module (e.g., Transformer, Pool, or MLPMix) that takes coarse features and positions as input to directly output fine sample locations, enabling end-to-end training.

## Background
Originated in [NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) (Mildenhall et al., 2020) to improve detail learning without proportional computational cost . It established the standard two-MLP architecture for neural radiance fields. Subsequent works like [TermiNeRF](8828-terminerf.md) and [NeRF-ID](8867-nerf-id.md) refined the sampling distribution estimation from heuristic to learned.
