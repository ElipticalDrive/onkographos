# Coarse-to-Fine Hierarchical Sampling

Also called: Hierarchical Sampling, Two-Stage Sampling

## Overview
Coarse-to-Fine Hierarchical Sampling is a strategy that decouples the estimation of a scene's opacity distribution from the final color prediction to improve rendering quality and computational efficiency [S1]. It solves the problem of sparse uniform sampling missing high-frequency details by first querying a coarse network to identify opaque regions, then clustering a denser set of fine samples around those regions [S1]. Unlike standard uniform sampling, which distributes points evenly across the entire ray segment, this method concentrates computational effort where the scene geometry is present, allowing the fine network to learn detailed textures without the prohibitive cost of uniformly dense sampling [S1].

## Shared principles
The core mechanism shared by all children is the deviation from a fixed, uniform sampling strategy [G]. Instead of casting a constant number of rays or samples per pixel/ray, the method computes a metric (such as gradient magnitude, rendering error, or temporal difference) and uses it to modulate the sampling rate or resolution [G].

Mathematically, this often involves a control loop:
1. **Metric Evaluation:** Compute a scalar value $M$ representing local complexity or error [G].
2. **Adaptation:** Adjust a parameter $P$ (step size $\Delta t$, resolution $R$, or sampling probability $w$) based on $M$ [G].
3. **Rendering/Training:** Execute the volume integral or loss computation with the adjusted parameter [G].

Common adaptation laws include:
* **Step Size Adaptation:** $\Delta t \propto \frac{1}{M}$ or $\Delta t = \min(\Delta t_{max}, \max(\Delta t_{min}, \frac{k}{M}))$ [G].
* **Resolution Control:** $R_{new} = R_{old} + \alpha \cdot (t_{target} - t_{actual})$ or threshold-based switching [G].
* **Probability Weighting:** $w_i \propto M_i$ for importance sampling [G].

All methods in this family aim to maintain visual fidelity while reducing the total number of volume queries [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md)
Uses a coarse MLP to estimate weights for hierarchical sampling, generating fine samples via inverse transform sampling on the coarse CDF. Chosen when standard NeRF quality is needed with better detail capture than uniform sampling.

### [TermiNeRF](8828-terminerf.md)
Uses a single lightweight sampling network evaluation per ray to predict a full weight distribution, enabling rendering speeds up to 14x faster than standard NeRF. Chosen when inference speed is critical and a dedicated sampling network can be trained.

### [NeRF-ID](8867-nerf-id.md)
Replaces heuristic coarse-to-fine sampling with a differentiable 'proposer' module, enabling end-to-end training of the entire system. Chosen when end-to-end differentiability of the sampling process is required to improve view synthesis quality.

## Background
The concept of hierarchical sampling in neural rendering was popularized by [Mildenhall et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08934), who introduced the two-stage process to mitigate aliasing and improve detail in NeRF. Prior to this, uniform sampling was the standard, often requiring hundreds of samples per ray to achieve acceptable quality. The method allows for efficient allocation of computational resources to opaque regions.

Recent developments have focused on making the sampling process more adaptive and generalizable. For instance, [What You See is What You GAN](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr52733.2024.02148) [S1] proposes a generalizable learned sampler conditioned on cheap low-resolution information to enable full-resolution rendering during training, replacing dense uniform sampling. This sampler is a scene-conditional proposal network that generalizes across scenes, inspired by density estimators, instead of being category-specific or optimized per-scene [S1]. Unlike acceleration structures like octrees which are limited to coarse or category-specific acceleration, this sampling-based method generalizes to every new scene [S1]. The sampler operates on a per-ray basis to adaptively accelerate rendering [S1] and allows the method to operate with just 20 samples per ray, compared to existing 3D GANs which must use at least 96 samples per ray [S1]. The approach uses up to 5 times fewer depth samples than dense sampling methods [S1].
