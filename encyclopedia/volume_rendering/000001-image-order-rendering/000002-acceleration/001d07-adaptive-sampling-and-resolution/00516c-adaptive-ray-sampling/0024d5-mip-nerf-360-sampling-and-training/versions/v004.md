# Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training

Also called: Mip-NeRF 360, Unbounded Anti-Aliased Neural Radiance Fields

## Overview
Mip-NeRF 360 extends the mip-NeRF framework to handle unbounded, outdoor scenes by introducing a disparity-based ray parameterization and a proposal network training strategy. It solves the problems of "floaters" (disconnected dense regions) and "background collapse" (distant surfaces modeled as nearby semi-transparent clouds) common in unbounded NeRFs [S1]. It sets itself apart from standard NeRFs by using linear-in-disparity sampling and from its siblings in adaptive sampling by using an asymmetric proposal loss to guide coarse-to-fine sampling without requiring explicit scene geometry [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A set of training rays $\mathcal{R}$, where each ray $\mathbf{r}$ has a near plane $t_n$ and far plane $t_f$, and a ground truth color $\mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})$.
**Outputs:** A rendered image $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$ for each ray.

**Data Structures:**
1.  **Proposal MLP ($\Theta_{prop}$):** A small Multi-Layer Perceptron.
    *   Input: Normalized ray distance $s \in [0, 1]$ and ray direction.
    *   Output: Volumetric density $\hat{\sigma}$.
    *   Note: Does not predict color.
2.  **NeRF MLP ($\Theta_{NeRF}$):** A larger Multi-Layer Perceptron.
    *   Input: Normalized ray distance $s \in [0, 1]$ and ray direction.
    *   Output: Volumetric density $\sigma$ and color $\mathbf{c}$ (RGB).
3.  **Ray Samples:**
    *   Discrete set of normalized distances $\mathbf{s} = \{s_1, \dots, s_K\}$ sorted in $[0, 1]$.
    *   Corresponding Euclidean distances $\mathbf{t} = \{t_1, \dots, t_K\}$ derived via inverse mapping.
    *   Weights $\mathbf{w}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ representing the contribution of each interval.

**Step 1: Scene and Ray Parameterization**
To handle unbounded scenes and improve anti-aliasing, rays are parameterized by normalized distance $s \in [0, 1]$ rather than Euclidean distance $t$. An invertible mapping is defined using a scalar function $g(\cdot)$ [S1]:

$$
s \triangleq \frac{g(t) - g(t_n)}{g(t_f) - g(t_n)}, \quad t \triangleq g^{-1}(s \cdot g(t_f) + (1-s) \cdot g(t_n))
$$

The function $g(x)$ is typically set to $1/x$ to produce ray samples distributed linearly in disparity (inverse depth). Setting $g(x) = \log(x)$ yields logarithmic spacing [S1].

Coordinates $\mathbf{x}$ are contracted to a ball of radius 2 to handle unbounded space [S1]:

$$
\text{contract}(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{cases} 
\mathbf{x} & \text{if } \|\mathbf{x}\| \le 1 \\
\left( 2 - \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} \right) \frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|} & \text{if } \|\mathbf{x}\| > 1
\end{cases}
$$

When transforming Gaussian intervals $(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$ through a coordinate transformation $f$, the linear approximation is used [S1]:

$$
f(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = (f(\boldsymbol{\mu}), \mathbf{J}_f(\boldsymbol{\mu}) \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{J}_f(\boldsymbol{\mu})^T)
$$

where $\mathbf{J}_f(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ is the Jacobian of $f$ at $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ [S1].

**Step 2: Coarse Sampling and Proposal Network**
1.  Sample $K_c$ points uniformly in $s$-space (linear in disparity).
2.  Convert $s$ to $t$ using $g(x)=1/x$.
3.  Evaluate Proposal MLP $\Theta_{prop}$ at these points to get densities $\hat{\sigma}$.
4.  Compute proposal weights $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ using standard volume rendering integration formulas based on $\hat{\sigma}$ and the interval lengths in $t$-space.

**Step 3: Fine Sampling and NeRF Network**
1.  Use proposal weights $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ to sample $K_f$ points non-uniformly in $s$-space, concentrating samples where proposal mass is high.
2.  Evaluate NeRF MLP $\Theta_{NeRF}$ at these points to get densities $\sigma$ and colors $\mathbf{c}$.
3.  Compute NeRF weights $\mathbf{w}$ and render color $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r})$.

**Step 4: Proposal Loss Calculation**
The proposal MLP is trained to bound the NeRF MLP's weights. Define a function that computes the sum of proposal weights overlapping an interval $T_i$ associated with NeRF weight $w_i$ [S1]:

$$
\text{bound}(\hat{\mathbf{t}}, \hat{\mathbf{w}}, T_i) = \sum_{j: T_i \cap \hat{T}_j \neq \emptyset} \hat{w}_j
$$

The proposal loss penalizes surplus NeRF mass that exceeds this bound [S1]:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}(\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{w}, \hat{\mathbf{t}}, \hat{\mathbf{w}}) = \sum_i \frac{1}{w_i} \max(0, w_i - \text{bound}(\hat{\mathbf{t}}, \hat{\mathbf{w}}, T_i))^2
$$

This loss is asymmetric; it only penalizes underestimation by the proposal. The division by $w_i$ ensures well-behaved optimization gradients [S1]. Because $\mathbf{t}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ are sorted, this sum is computed efficiently using summed-area tables [S1].

**Step 5: Joint Training**
1.  Apply a stop-gradient on the NeRF MLP's outputs $\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{w}$ when computing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}}$ so the NeRF leads and the proposal follows [S1].
2.  Compute the total loss as a weighted sum of reconstruction losses for coarse and fine rays [S1]:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \left( \frac{1}{10} \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^c), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{r}, \mathbf{t}^f), \mathbf{C}^*(\mathbf{r})) + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{prop}} \right)
$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is mean squared error, $\mathbf{t}^c$ and $\mathbf{t}^f$ are coarse and fine sample sets, and $\lambda$ is a weighting factor for the proposal loss (value not given in sources) [S1].

**Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The mapping $g(x)=1/x$ requires careful handling of $t_n$ near zero to avoid division by zero or numerical instability.
*   **Boundaries:** The contraction function maps infinity to a finite radius; ensure coordinate transformations respect the Jacobian scaling.
*   **Asymmetry:** The proposal loss does not penalize overestimation. The proposal weights will form an upper envelope over NeRF weights.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Dominated by two MLP evaluations per ray (proposal and NeRF) and the resampling step. The proposal loss computation is $O(K)$ due to summed-area tables [S1].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storing parameters for two MLPs. No explicit voxel grid or hash table is used [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles unbounded scenes effectively; reduces floaters and background collapse; anti-aliased rendering via mip-NeRF's interval-based approach; efficient sampling via proposal network [S1].
*   **Limitations:** Requires joint training of two networks; the proposal network must be small enough to be tractable but large enough to bound the NeRF [S1].
*   **When to use:** Unbounded, outdoor scenes with large depth ranges where standard NeRFs suffer from floaters or background collapse [S1].

## Background
Mip-NeRF 360 was introduced by Barron et al. in 2022 [S1]. It builds on the original mip-NeRF work by extending it to unbounded scenes. The method addresses specific artifacts like floaters and background collapse that arise in unbounded settings due to the ill-posed nature of the reconstruction problem [S1].
