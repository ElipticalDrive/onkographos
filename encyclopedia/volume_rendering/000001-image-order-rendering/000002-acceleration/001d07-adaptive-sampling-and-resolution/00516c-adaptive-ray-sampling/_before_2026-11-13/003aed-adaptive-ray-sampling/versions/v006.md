# Adaptive Ray Sampling


## Overview
Adaptive ray sampling refers to a family of training procedures in neural radiance fields (NeRF) that replace uniform ray sampling with strategies that concentrate computational resources on regions of high geometric or photometric complexity. It solves the inefficiency of uniform sampling, which wastes evaluations on empty space or smooth regions, by dynamically adjusting sample locations or ray selection based on density, error, depth priors, or spatial partitions. This family is distinguished from [Training Ray Marching and Integration](2199-training-ray-marching-and-integration.md) by focusing on *where* to sample rather than *how* to integrate, and from [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) by encompassing broader strategies including error-based mining, depth-guided selection, and superpixel partitioning, not just hierarchical point clustering.

## Shared principles
All methods in this family share the goal of reducing the number of MLP evaluations required to achieve a target rendering quality by avoiding uniform distribution along rays or across the image plane. They generally operate within the training loop, using intermediate signals (density, loss, depth estimates, or error maps) to inform the next iteration's sampling distribution. Common mechanisms include:
1.  **Non-Uniform Distribution:** Replacing uniform spacing $t \sim U[t_n, t_f]$ with distributions biased toward surfaces (e.g., inverse transform sampling from density, truncated normal from depth priors).
2.  **Hierarchical/Two-Stage Evaluation:** Using a lightweight "coarse" or "proposal" network to estimate importance, then sampling densely only in high-importance regions for a "fine" network evaluation.
3.  **Spatial/Contextual Weighting:** Adjusting the probability of casting rays based on 2D image properties (color variance, superpixel error) or 3D spatial partitions (quadtree nodes, occupancy grids).
4.  **Differentiability Constraints:** In methods where sampling locations are learned (e.g., proposers), the sampling step is often non-differentiable, requiring stop-gradient operations or specific architectural designs (like the proposer in NeRF-ID) to allow end-to-end training.

## Subtopics

### [Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md)
Optimizes radiance fields for unbounded scenes using a disparity-based ray parameterization, a coordinate contraction function, and a coarse-to-fine training loop with a proposal network. It mitigates artifacts such as floaters and background collapse by regularizing the density distribution and ensuring efficient sampling through online distillation. The method differs from standard NeRF by using linear-in-disparity sampling and handles unbounded scenes via coordinate contraction.

### [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md)
A hierarchical sampling strategy that improves the learning of high-frequency details in neural radiance fields by allocating computational resources adaptively along rays. It solves the problem of uniform sampling being inefficient for sparse or complex geometry by using a coarse network to identify opaque regions and a fine network to densely sample those specific areas. Unlike standard uniform sampling which distributes points evenly across the entire ray segment, this method clusters additional samples near the object boundaries identified by the coarse pass, thereby improving detail reconstruction without a proportional increase in coarse sampling cost.

### [Probability-based Ray Sampling with Quadtree Subdivision](7481-probability-based-ray-sampling-with-quadtree-subdivision.md)
This method accelerates radiance field training by dynamically adjusting ray distribution through context-based probability sampling and adaptive quadtree subdivision. It differs from sibling methods like [Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md) and [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) by using rendering error to decide node subdivision into four children rather than just adjusting point counts per fixed block, allowing the number of leaf nodes to change during training.

### [Hard Sample Mining](8673-hard-sample-mining.md)
Hard Sample Mining (HSM) is an optimization procedure that replaces uniform or density-based ray sampling with a strategy that ranks point samples by their propagated pixel loss and subsamples a "hard" minibatch for gradient updates. It differs from sibling methods like [Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md) or [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) by explicitly using the scalar error signal (pixel loss) to determine sample importance, rather than relying solely on geometric priors like density or transmittance. This allows the optimizer to focus computational resources on regions where the model is currently performing poorly, accelerating convergence and reducing memory usage.

### [TermiNeRF](8828-terminerf.md)
TermiNeRF is a method for efficient neural rendering that jointly trains a lightweight sampling network alongside a color network to predict the distribution of ideal sampling locations along a viewing ray. By concentrating samples where opacity is high and skipping free space, it significantly reduces the number of color network evaluations required per pixel, achieving rendering speeds up to 14x faster than standard NeRF while maintaining comparable image quality. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by using a single network evaluation per ray to predict a full weight distribution, conditioned on the density of a pre-trained NeRF, without requiring explicit depth maps or occupancy grids.

### [Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling](8866-pixel-region-and-depth-guided-ray-sampling.md)
Pixel Region and Depth Guided Ray Sampling is a strategy that increases sampling probability in pixel areas exhibiting significant color and depth variation, reducing redundant rays in flat regions to accelerate network convergence and improve geometry perception. Unlike conventional NeRF which uses uniform distribution, this method samples varying ray densities based on depth boundaries and pixel regions to simultaneously accelerate convergence and improve geometry perception. It is distinct from sibling methods like [Mip-NeRF 360 Sampling and Training](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md) which focuses on cone tracing for anti-aliasing, and [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) which focuses on hierarchical point sampling along a single ray; instead, this method operates at the pixel level to select *which* rays to cast based on 2D image priors and estimated depth.

### [NeRF-ID](8867-nerf-id.md)
NeRF-ID (NeRF in detail) is a differentiable ray sampling method that replaces the heuristic coarse-to-fine sampling of vanilla NeRF with a learnable "proposer" module. It solves the problem of suboptimal sample placement by enabling end-to-end training of the coarse network, proposer, and fine network jointly, allowing the system to learn where to concentrate samples for maximum rendering quality. Unlike sibling methods that rely on handcrafted probability density functions derived from coarse densities, NeRF-ID uses a neural network to directly predict fine sample locations from coarse features.

### [Two-Stage Sampling with Occupancy Grid and Nonlinear Function](9146-two-stage-sampling-with-occupancy-grid-and-nonlinear-functio.md)
This method addresses the inefficiency of uniform sampling in neural implicit surface reconstruction by combining spatial acceleration with density-aware sampling. It solves the problem of wasting computation on empty space and scattering samples randomly within occupied regions. Unlike [NeuS](8828-terminerf.md) which uses inverse transform sampling that scatters points randomly near the surface, and [Instant-NGP](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md) which uses uniform sampling within occupied grid cells, this method uses an Occupancy Grid to skip empty regions and a nonlinear piecewise exponential function to bias samples toward the surface within occupied regions.

### [Truncated Depth-Based Sampling](10613-truncated-depth-based-sampling.md)
Truncated Depth-Based Sampling (TDBS) is a ray sampling strategy for Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) that utilizes monocular depth priors to concentrate samples near estimated surface locations, thereby improving convergence speed and pose estimation accuracy in joint optimization scenarios. Unlike sibling methods that may use depth for supervision or coarse-to-fine alternation, TDBS relies exclusively on truncated normal distributions centered on depth priors for sampling throughout the training process.

### [Superpixel-guide Efficient Sampling (SES)](11252-superpixel-guide-efficient-sampling-ses.md)
Superpixel-guide Efficient Sampling (SES) is a training strategy for neural radiance fields that uses SLIC superpixels as the smallest processing units to guide ray sampling, addressing slow convergence and scattered sampling in high-resolution NeRF training by focusing computation on complex regions. Unlike sibling methods such as [Probability-based Ray Sampling with Quadtree Subdivision](7481-probability-based-ray-sampling-with-quadtree-subdivision.md) which use fixed geometric partitions, SES uses adaptive superpixels that can be re-segmented based on rendering error, resolving inadequate partitioning issues and reducing computational overhead per instance.

### [Surface-Based Sampling](11356-surface-based-sampling.md)
Surface-Based Sampling is a ray sampling strategy that restricts 3D sample points to a narrow region surrounding the estimated surface of an object, rather than sampling uniformly throughout the entire volume or free space. It solves the problem of quality degradation caused by wasting samples in irrelevant free space or occluded regions, which is common in standard NeRF. Unlike standard NeRF, it leverages external depth information to concentrate computational resources where the radiance field is most active.

#### Error Map Importance Sampling
- **Step:** If `opt.error_map_size` is set, compute sampling weights: `sample_weight = error_map + 2 * error_map.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)`. Sample ray indices using multinomial distribution: `var.ray_idx_coarse = torch.multinomial(sample_weight, opt.nerf.n_rays//batch_size, replacement=False)`. Decode 1D sampled indices to 2D grid coordinates: `inds_x = ray_idx // error_map_size`, `inds_y = ray_idx % error_map_size` [S1, unofficial].
- **Parameter:** `error_map_size`: int, defines the resolution of the 2D error map grid [S1, unofficial].
- **Pitfall:** The integer division operator `//` triggers a PyTorch 1.10+ deprecation warning; use `torch.div` or `torch.floor_divide` instead [S1, unofficial].
- **Comparison:** Combines 1/3 importance sampling based on error map with 2/3 uniform random sampling to prevent overfitting to local error spikes [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Adaptive ray sampling emerged as a critical optimization in NeRF training to address the computational bottleneck of evaluating large MLPs for every pixel. Early works like [Coarse-to-Fine NeRF](3406-coarse-to-fine-nerf.md) introduced hierarchical sampling to handle high-frequency details. Subsequent research diversified into disparity-based parameterization for unbounded scenes ([Mip-NeRF 360](3393-mip-nerf-360-sampling-and-training.md)), error-driven mining ([Hard Sample Mining](8673-hard-sample-mining.md)), and depth-guided strategies ([TermiNeRF](8828-terminerf.md), [TDBS](10613-truncated-depth-based-sampling.md)). The family continues to evolve with learnable proposers ([NeRF-ID](8867-nerf-id.md)) and spatial partitioning methods ([SES](11252-superpixel-guide-efficient-sampling-ses.md), [Quadtree](7481-probability-based-ray-sampling-with-quadtree-subdivision.md)).
