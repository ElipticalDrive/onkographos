# Early Termination for Gaussian Splatting

Also called: Early Termination (ET)

## Overview
Early Termination (ET) is an acceleration mechanism for Gaussian Splatting that selectively omits computations for Gaussians with negligible impact on the final rendered output [S1]. It leverages physically grounded transmittance to achieve a decay pattern similar to alpha-blending, allowing the rendering process to halt when the remaining contribution falls below a threshold [S1]. Unlike sort-free Gaussian Splatting methods (e.g., LC-WSR) which lack early termination due to their design, ET enables significant speedups comparable to vanilla 3DGS without requiring an explicit Gaussian order [S1].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A camera ray, a set of valid Gaussians intersected by the ray ($N^{\text{val}}$), and a termination threshold $\epsilon$ [S1].
*   **Output:** The final pixel color and the number of Gaussians actually rendered ($N^{\text{rendered}}$) [S1].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Valid Gaussians:** A list of Gaussians intersected by the ray, ordered front-to-back along the ray [G].
*   **Transmittance:** A scalar value representing the accumulated opacity along the ray [G].

**Algorithm:**
```python
def early_termination(ray, gaussians, epsilon):
    # Sort Gaussians by depth along the ray [G]
    gaussians.sort(key=lambda g: g.depth_along_ray)
    accumulated_color = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    transmittance = 1.0
    rendered_count = 0
    for g in gaussians:
        alpha = g.opacity
        color = g.color
        contribution = color * transmittance * alpha
        accumulated_color += contribution
        transmittance *= (1.0 - alpha)
        rendered_count += 1
        if transmittance < epsilon:
            break
    return accumulated_color, rendered_count
```

**Formulas:**
*   **Termination Ratio:** The efficiency of early termination is measured by the ratio of skipped computations:
    $$r^{\text{ET}} = 1 - \frac{N^{\text{rendered}}}{N^{\text{val}}}$$
    where $N^{\text{rendered}}$ is the number of Gaussians contributed to the final color, and $N^{\text{val}}$ is the total number of interacted valid Gaussians along the ray [S1].
*   **Cell-Level Transmittance:** In cell-based variants, the transmittance $T_{\text{cell}}^n$ decays towards zero during front-to-back rendering [S1].

**Parameters:**
*   **Threshold ($\epsilon$):** The predefined threshold for early termination. For standard ET, this is maintained at the same value as used in vanilla 3DGS [S1]. For Cell-Based ET, it is an empirical threshold constrained by the opacity parameter $\sigma_{n,k} \in [0, 1]$ [S1]. The exact numerical value is not given in the sources [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** The transmittance must decay sufficiently to trigger termination. If the opacity parameters are not constrained to physically plausible ranges (e.g., $[0, 1]$), the transmittance may not decay correctly, leading to artifacts or failure to terminate [S1].
*   **Order Independence:** In sort-free methods, the lack of strict front-to-back ordering can affect the accuracy of transmittance decay [S1]. The method relies on physically grounded transmittance to eliminate transparency artifacts and enable early termination without explicit sorting [S1]. The locally order-independent property of the transmittance forms the cornerstone for the elimination of popping artifacts [S1].

## Subtopics

### Early Termination for Cell Based 3DGS
A rendering optimization that halts the front-to-back blending process at the cell level once the cell-level transmittance decays below a predefined threshold $\epsilon$, eliminating transparency artifacts and accelerating rendering [S1]. Unlike prior Weighted Splatting/Rendering (WSR) methods, this formulation constrains the opacity parameter $\sigma_{n,k}$ to the physically plausible range of $[0, 1]$, allowing the empirical threshold to be directly transferred and facilitating more effective pruning and regularization [S1]. The cell-level transmittance $T_{\text{cell}}^n$ is the key innovation that eliminates transparency artifacts and enables early termination [S1].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Performance:** On the Mip-NeRF360 dataset, enabling Early Termination results in 155 FPS, compared to 212 FPS when disabled (w/o ET) [S1].
*   **Quality:** The PSNR with ET enabled is 27.66, compared to 26.60 when disabled [S1].
*   **Comparison:** Duplex-GS achieves termination results comparable to vanilla 3DGS without requiring an explicit Gaussian order, whereas sort-free methods like LC-WSR do not support early termination [S1].
*   **Limitations:** The efficiency of cell search rasterization is contingent upon the degree of coherence between the visible regions of a cell and its associated Gaussians [S1]. Discrepancies between spatial intervals of cells and emitted neural Gaussians can introduce redundant computations [S1].

## Background
Early Termination for Gaussian Splatting was introduced in the context of Duplex-GS, a proxy-guided weighted blending method for real-time order-independent Gaussian Splatting [S1]. The method addresses the limitations of prior sort-free Gaussian Splatting methods, which lacked early termination capabilities [S1]. By restoring explicit physical meaning to the opacity parameter and ensuring physically grounded transmittance, the method enables efficient acceleration while maintaining competitive visual quality [S1].
