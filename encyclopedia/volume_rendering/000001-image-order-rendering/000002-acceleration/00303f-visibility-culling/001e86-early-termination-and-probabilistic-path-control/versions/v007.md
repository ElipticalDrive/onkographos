# Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control

Also called: Early Ray Termination (ERT), Russian Roulette (RR), Adaptive Termination

## Overview
Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control are acceleration families that reduce computational cost by halting the ray-marching integration loop when the remaining contribution to the pixel color becomes negligible. They differ from sibling methods like [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md) (which skips geometric voids) and [Bounding Volume Culling](1239-bounding-volume-culling.md) (which limits geometric extent) by making decisions based on the accumulated optical properties (opacity/transmittance) of the volume itself. Deterministic variants introduce bias by ignoring the ray tail, while probabilistic variants maintain statistical unbiasedness by scaling surviving paths.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism modifies the standard front-to-back compositing loop defined in [Acceleration](18-acceleration.md):
$$ C_{acc} = C_{acc} + C_{sample} \cdot \alpha_{sample} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
$$ \alpha_{acc} = \alpha_{acc} + \alpha_{sample} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
by inserting a termination condition after each sample (or at specific intervals). The ray must be traversed in front-to-back order to ensure accumulated opacity correctly represents occlusion [1019-early-ray-termination.md][S2, unofficial].

Two primary strategies exist:
1.  **Deterministic Threshold:** The loop breaks when accumulated opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$ (e.g., 0.95) or transmittance $T = 1 - \alpha_{acc}$ drops below $\epsilon$. This ignores the ray tail, introducing systematic bias [1019-early-ray-termination.md].
2.  **Probabilistic Survival (Russian Roulette):** A random number $\xi \in [0, 1]$ is compared against a survival probability $p_{surv}$ derived from the current path weight or transmittance. If the path is terminated, its contribution is scaled by $1/p_{surv}$ to maintain an unbiased estimator [1371-russian-roulette.md].

## Subtopics

### [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md)
Skips regions of zero/negligible density using acceleration structures. Sets itself apart by physically jumping over empty space rather than terminating based on accumulated opacity.

### [Bounding Volume Culling](1239-bounding-volume-culling.md)
Terminates rays based on geometric distance from the eye. Sets itself apart by using deterministic geometric bounds rather than material properties.

### [Russian Roulette](1371-russian-roulette.md)
Probabilistic termination that scales weights to maintain unbiasedness. Sets itself apart from deterministic ERT by avoiding bias.

### [Early Stop](1790-early-stop.md)
Limits procedural noise evaluations based on BVH bounds. Sets itself apart by optimizing function evaluation counts for sphere tracing rather than sample counts.

### [Conservative Early Ray Termination](1949-conservative-early-ray-termination.md)
Prunes cells during scan conversion using an ERT-table. Sets itself apart by operating in the rasterization phase with subscreen aggregation.

### [Ray Marching with Dithering and Early Termination](3565-ray-marching-with-dithering-and-early-termination.md)
GPU-based ray marching with dithering and transmittance thresholds. Sets itself apart by integrating dithering to mitigate aliasing alongside deterministic termination.

### [Early Ray Termination with Binary Mask](6641-early-ray-termination-with-binary-mask.md)
Uses a binary mask to jump to opaque voxels. Sets itself apart by skipping empty space evaluation entirely via bit manipulation.

### [Opacity-based Early Termination](7920-opacity-based-early-termination.md)
General family of threshold checks on accumulated opacity. Sets itself apart as the broad category encompassing deterministic ERT and probabilistic variants.

### Uncertainty-based Early Termination
- **definition**: A mechanism that allows a query coordinate to be finalised early (not processed further) when its predicted uncertainty is less than a certain threshold, avoiding unnecessary calculations [S1].
- **implementation**: In Recursive-NeRF, a network predicts uncertainty $\delta$ for each query. If $\delta$ is low, the point exits to the output network; otherwise, it is clustered and sent to child branches [S1].

### Recursive Rendering
- **definition**: During training, sample points can exit at multiple stages. During inferencing, points exit only once at a specific stage. Points found earlier to be reliable immediately exit and are rendered. Which branch is taken depends on clustering results [S1].
- **implementation**: Uncertain points in the current stage are clustered and fed to different child-branches with the same structure in the next stage [S1].

### Early Ray Termination via Depth Buffer
- **definition**: A technique to skip rendering fragments for rays that are already saturated (opaque) by testing against the depth buffer before executing the fragment shader for the brick [S2, unofficial].
- **step**:
    1. Traverse the volume in front-to-back order to enable early termination [S2, unofficial].
    2. Before rendering a brick, execute a fragment shader program while drawing a solid bounding box around the brick with back face culling switched on. This shader writes the maximal depth value in the depth buffer for saturated rays [S2, unofficial].
    3. When slicing the brick texture, the early z-test prevents fragment operations for rays where the depth test fails (indicating saturation), reducing load on rasterization and fragment shader bottlenecks [S2, unofficial].
- **formula**: Color update: $C_{i+1} = (1 - A_i) \cdot \alpha_i \cdot c_i + C_i$. Opacity update: $A_{i+1} = (1 - A_i) \cdot \alpha_i + A_i$. Where $C, A$ are current ray color/opacity, $c, \alpha$ are sample color/opacity, and $i$ is the sample index. A ray is saturated when $A_i \approx 1$ [S2, unofficial].
- **pitfall**: Early ray termination is performed only once per brick, not per octree node or sample, because the overhead of changing fragment shaders and performing the test would annihilate benefits [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Early Ray Termination (ERT) is a standard optimization in volume rendering, often implemented via depth buffer tests in GPU rasterization pipelines [S2, unofficial]. Russian Roulette is a fundamental technique in path tracing and volumetric rendering to handle low-weight paths without bias [1371-russian-roulette.md]. Uncertainty-based termination and recursive rendering were introduced in Recursive-NeRF to optimize neural volume rendering by skipping low-uncertainty queries [S1].
