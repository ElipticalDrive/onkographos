# Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control

Also called: Early Ray Termination (ERT), Russian Roulette (RR), Early Stop

## Overview
Early Termination and Probabilistic Path Control are acceleration techniques that reduce the computational cost of volume rendering by halting the ray-marching integration loop before it reaches the end of the volume or the maximum sample count. They solve the problem of redundant sampling in regions where the accumulated opacity is already high (occlusion) or the remaining contribution is negligible. This family is distinguished from [Empty-space skipping](1002-empty-space-skipping.md) and [Bounding Volume Culling](1239-bounding-volume-culling.md) by terminating rays based on accumulated material properties (opacity/transmittance) or probabilistic weights rather than geometric emptiness or bounding box extents. It builds on [Acceleration](18-acceleration.md) by modifying the compositing loop's termination condition.

## Shared principles
The core mechanism shared by all methods in this family is the modification of the standard front-to-back compositing loop to include a termination condition. The standard loop updates accumulated color $C_{acc}$ and opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ as:
$$ C_{acc} = C_{acc} + C_{sample} \cdot \alpha_{sample} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
$$ \alpha_{acc} = \alpha_{acc} + \alpha_{sample} \cdot (1 - \alpha_{acc}) $$
[Parent](18-acceleration.md).

Methods in this family introduce a check after each sample (or at specific intervals) to determine if the loop should break. The decision is based on one of two criteria:
1.  **Deterministic Threshold:** The accumulated opacity $\alpha_{acc}$ exceeds a predefined threshold $\tau$ (e.g., 0.95 or 1.0), or the transmittance $T = 1 - \alpha_{acc}$ drops below a small epsilon $\epsilon$. This introduces systematic bias by ignoring the ray tail [1019-early-ray-termination.md].
2.  **Probabilistic Survival:** A random number is compared against a survival probability derived from the current path weight or transmittance. If the path is terminated, its contribution is scaled by the inverse of the survival probability to maintain an unbiased estimator [1371-russian-roulette.md].

All methods require the ray to be traversed in front-to-back order to ensure that accumulated opacity correctly represents the occlusion of subsequent samples [1019-early-ray-termination.md][S2, unofficial].

#### Uncertainty-based Early Termination

### definition
- [S1] A mechanism that allows a query coordinate to be finalised early (not processed further) when its predicted uncertainty is less than a certain threshold, avoiding unnecessary calculations.

#### Recursive Rendering

### definition
- [S1] During training, sample points can exit at multiple stages. During inferencing, points exit only once at a specific stage. Points found earlier to be reliable immediately exit and are rendered. Which branch is taken depends on clustering results.

#### Early Ray Termination via Depth Buffer

### definition
- [S2, unofficial] A technique to skip rendering fragments for rays that are already saturated (opaque) by testing against the depth buffer before executing the fragment shader for the brick.

### step
- [S2, unofficial] Traverse the volume in front-to-back order to enable early termination.
- [S2, unofficial] Before rendering a brick, execute a fragment shader program while drawing a solid bounding box around the brick with back face culling switched on. This shader writes the maximal depth value in the depth buffer for saturated rays.
- [S2, unofficial] When slicing the brick texture, the early z-test prevents fragment operations for rays where the depth test fails (indicating saturation), reducing load on rasterization and fragment shader bottlenecks.

### formula
- [S2, unofficial] Color update: $C_{i+1} = (1 - A_i) \cdot \alpha_i \cdot c_i + C_i$. Opacity update: $A_{i+1} = (1 - A_i) \cdot \alpha_i + A_i$. Where $C, A$ are current ray color/opacity, $c, \alpha$ are sample color/opacity, and $i$ is the sample index. A ray is saturated when $A_i \approx 1$.

### pitfall
- [S2, unofficial] Early ray termination is performed only once per brick, not per octree node or sample, because the overhead of changing fragment shaders and performing the test would annihilate benefits.

## Background
Early Ray Termination (ERT) is a standard optimization in volume rendering, particularly in GPU-based implementations, to reduce the number of fragment shader executions [S2, unofficial]. The concept of using probabilistic termination (Russian Roulette) originates from path tracing in computer graphics to handle infinite light paths, later adapted for volumetric integration to maintain statistical unbiasedness when truncating paths [S1][S2, unofficial][S3]. Uncertainty-based early termination and recursive rendering are more recent developments in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), where neural networks predict uncertainty to decide if a sample point has converged [S1].
