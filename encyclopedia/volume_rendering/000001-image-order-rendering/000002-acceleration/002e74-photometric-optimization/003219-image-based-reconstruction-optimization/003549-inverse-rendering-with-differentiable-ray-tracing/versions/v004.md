# Inverse Rendering with Differentiable Ray Tracing

Also called: Differentiable Rendering Optimization, Gradient-based Inverse Rendering, Robust Point Light Source Estimation Using Differentiable Rendering

## Overview
Inverse rendering recovers latent scene parameters—such as camera pose, light placement, and surface color—that produce a given target photograph by formulating the problem as an unconstrained optimization task using gradients from the rendering pipeline [S1]. This method solves the problem of estimating 3D illumination (specifically punctual light source position and intensity) and material properties by minimizing the photometric error between a rendered image and an observed RGBD image [S2, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from alternatives like [Inverse Rendering with Shape Prior](7506-inverse-rendering-with-shape-prior.md) by relying on a completely differentiable renderer based on the Blinn-Phong model that explicitly includes cast shadows, thereby increasing robustness against imperfect reflectance estimation [S2, unofficial]. Unlike methods that use discrete candidate light sources or explicit shadow detection, it performs continuous optimization of light position and renders a shadow map at each iteration [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Scene geometry $G = (X, N, C)$, where $X$ is a 3D point cloud, $N$ is the surface normals, and $C$ is the camera pose; material properties $M = (k_d, k_s, \alpha)$ (diffuse reflectance, specular reflectance, shininess); and an observed target image $I^*$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Optimized lighting parameters $L = (L_i, I_{L,i})$, where $L_i$ is the 3D location and $I_{L,i}$ is the scalar intensity of the punctual light source [S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Geometry $G$:** Represented as an oriented point cloud. $X$ contains 3D coordinates for each point. $N$ contains the corresponding normal vectors. $C$ contains camera pose parameters [S2, unofficial].
*   **Lighting $L$:** Parametrized by a 3D vector for position $L_i$ and a scalar for intensity $I_{L,i}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   **Image $I$:** A tensor of shape $H \times W \times C$, normalized to $[0, 1]$ [S1].

**Illumination Model:**
The total image intensity $I$ at a point $x$ is the sum of ambient, diffuse, and specular terms:
$$ I = I_a + I_d + I_s $$
[S2, unofficial]

1.  **Ambient Term:** Calculated as the product of diffuse reflectance $k_d$ and ambient illumination $L_a$:
    $$ I_a = k_d \times L_a $$
    [S2, unofficial]

2.  **Diffuse Term:** Proportional to the dot product of the surface normal $N$ and the light vector $(L_i - X)$, scaled by the shadow term $S_i$, diffuse reflectance $k_d$, and light intensity $I_{L,i}$:
    $$ I_d = k_d \times S_i \times (L_i - X)^t \times N \times I_{L,i} $$
    [S2, unofficial]

3.  **Specular Term:** A factor of the scalar product between the normal $N$ and the halfway vector $H$ (between the viewer $C-X$ and the light source $L_i-X$), scaled by specular reflectance $k_s$, shininess $\alpha$, shadow term $S_i$, and light intensity $I_{L,i}$:
    $$ I_s = k_s \times S_i \times (H^t N)^\alpha \times I_{L,i} $$
    [S2, unofficial]

4.  **Shadow Term $S_i$:** Equals 1 when the light illuminates the point $X$ and 0 otherwise [S2, unofficial].

**Optimization Objective:**
The problem minimizes the squared L2 norm of the difference between the rendered image $I(G, M, L)$ and the observed image $I^*$ with respect to the lighting parameters $L$:
$$ \hat{L} = \arg \min_L E = \arg \min_L \|I(G, M, L) - I^*\|_2^2 $$
[S2, unofficial]

The mean squared error (MSE) loss used is:
$$ L(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (I_i(\theta) - T_i)^2 $$
where $I(\theta) \in [0, 1]^{H \times W \times C}$ is the rendered image, $T \in [0, 1]^{H \times W \times C}$ is the target image, and $N = H \cdot W \cdot C$ is the total number of pixel-channel values [S1].

**Algorithm:**
The optimization uses the Adam optimizer [S1]. The update step for parameters $\theta$ (which may include light position, intensity, or material properties) is:
$$ \hat{m}_t = \frac{m_t}{1 - \beta_1^t}, \quad \hat{v}_t = \frac{v_t}{1 - \beta_2^t} $$
$$ \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t + \epsilon}} $$
where $m_t, v_t$ are biased first and second moment estimates, and $\eta, \beta_1, \beta_2, \epsilon$ are optimizer hyperparameters [S1].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def inverse_rendering_optimization(geometry, materials, target_image, max_iterations, loss_tolerance):
    """
    Optimizes lighting parameters (and optionally materials) to match a target image.
    
    Args:
        geometry: Tuple (X, N, C) representing point cloud, normals, camera pose.
        materials: Tuple (kd, ks, alpha) representing diffuse, specular reflectance, shininess.
        target_image: Tensor of shape (H, W, C) normalized to [0, 1].
        max_iterations: Integer Nmax.
        loss_tolerance: Float epsilon for convergence.
        
    Returns:
        Optimized lighting parameters L.
    """
    # Initialize lighting parameters randomly or with a prior
    L = initialize_lighting_parameters()
    
    # Adam optimizer state
    m = 0
    v = 0
    t = 0
    
    converged = False
    iter_count = 0
    
    while not converged and iter_count < max_iterations:
        # 1. Forward Pass: Render image with current parameters
        # The renderer computes I = I_a + I_d + I_s using the Blinn-Phong model
        # and differentiable ray tracing to compute shadows.
        rendered_image = render(geometry, materials, L)
        
        # 2. Compute Loss
        # MSE loss over all pixels and channels
        loss = mean_squared_error(rendered_image, target_image)
        
        # 3. Compute Gradients
        # Gradients are propagated through the differentiable renderer
        # to compute d(loss)/d(L) and optionally d(loss)/d(materials)
        gradients = compute_gradients(loss, L, geometry, materials)
        
        # 4. Update Parameters using Adam
        t += 1
        m = beta1 * m + (1 - beta1) * gradients
        v = beta2 * v + (1 - beta2) * (gradients ** 2)
        
        m_hat = m / (1 - beta1 ** t)
        v_hat = v / (1 - beta2 ** t)
        
        L = L - learning_rate * m_hat / (sqrt(v_hat) + epsilon)
        
        # Optional: Project material parameters back to [0, 1] if optimizing materials
        # materials = project_to_rgb_cube(materials)
        
        # 5. Check Convergence
        if loss < loss_tolerance:
            converged = True
            
        iter_count += 1
        
    return L
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Adam Hyperparameters:** $\eta, \beta_1, \beta_2, \epsilon$ are used consistently across tasks. Specific values are not listed in the sources but were selected for stable, monotone convergence [S1].
*   **Diffuse Reflectance ($k_d$):** Coefficient for diffuse term [S2, unofficial].
*   **Specular Reflectance ($k_s$):** Coefficient for specular term [S2, unofficial].
*   **Shininess ($\alpha$):** Exponent for specular term [S2, unofficial].
*   **Ambient Illumination ($L_a$):** Constant ambient light term [S2, unofficial].
*   **Finite Difference Perturbation ($h$):** Used for verification, $h = 1 \times 10^{-5}$ relative to parameter value ($\Delta \theta_i = h \cdot |\theta_i|$ for $\theta_i \neq 0$, and $\Delta \theta_i = h$ for $\theta_i = 0$) [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Imperfect Reflectance:** The method handles imperfect reflectance estimation by including cast shadows, which increases robustness compared to methods that do not use shadows or explicitly separate diffuse/specular components [S2, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Verification:** Gradient approximation error $E_{grad}$ is measured as $\frac{\|\nabla_{AD} L - \nabla_{FD} L\|_2}{\|\nabla_{FD} L\|_2 + \epsilon}$, where $\nabla_{AD}$ is the AD gradient and $\nabla_{FD}$ is the finite-difference gradient with $h=10^{-5}$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ [S1].
*   **Material Bounds:** When optimizing material properties (RGB colors), a Euclidean projection onto the RGB cube $[0, 1]^3$ is applied after every iteration to ensure valid colors [S1].

## Subtopics

### Image-based Parameter Fitting
Extracts imaging parameters (illumination, camera positions, sea colors) from observed images by iteratively optimizing lighting parameters using the differentiable renderer [S3]. It reconstructs surface normal and roughness first, then optimizes lighting parameters (color of sky light, color of sunlight, direction of sunlight) incrementally using gradient propagation [S3]. In experiments, optimization ran for 400 iterations using Adam, reducing loss to $10^{-6}$ and converging to ground truth values [S3].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The computational cost scales with the number of operations in the forward pass rather than with the number of parameters, making it substantially more efficient for high-dimensional optimization problems [S1].
*   **Measured Performance:**
    *   **Camera Calibration:** MSE loss fell from >5700 to <100 within 150 iterations. Final position error 0.008 ± 0.002 units (0.20 ± 0.05% relative error). Converged in 147 ± 12 iterations over 10 runs [S1].
    *   **Light Estimation:** Converged in <500 iterations. Position error <2% of ground truth. Intensity error <5% of true value [S1].
    *   **Material Estimation:** Final MSE < 1e-4 in RGB space. Initial RGB error 0.43 ± 0.12 reduced to 0.004 ± 0.001 at convergence (within 50 iterations) [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Robust to imperfect reflectance estimation due to shadow inclusion; does not require explicit separation of specular/diffuse components or image segmentation [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires a differentiable renderer; performance depends on the quality of the initial geometry and material estimates [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to Use:** When estimating light source position/intensity from a single RGBD image with known geometry and materials [S2, unofficial]. When recovering camera pose or material colors from a target photograph [S1].
*   **When Not to Use:** When the scene geometry is unknown and cannot be estimated beforehand, or when real-time performance is critical and differentiable ray tracing is too slow [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Inverse rendering was introduced as a method to recover latent scene parameters (camera pose, light placement, surface color) that produce a given target photograph by formulating the problem as an unconstrained optimization task using gradients from the rendering pipeline [S1]. The specific application of robust point light source estimation using differentiable rendering with cast shadows was proposed to address the common practical case of imperfect reflectance estimation [S2, unofficial]. The method has been applied to mixed reality applications, such as inserting virtual objects consistently lit into real video or photographs [S2, unofficial]. It has also been used in dynamic ocean inverse modeling to extract imaging parameters like illumination and sea colors [S3].
