# Differentiable Uncalibrated Imaging Framework

Also called: Differentiable Imaging Framework, Differentiable Uncalibrated Imaging, Joint Calibration and Reconstruction, Joint optimization of measurement coordinates and representation, Uncalibrated imaging with implicit representations

## Overview
The Differentiable Uncalibrated Imaging Framework addresses the problem of image reconstruction when the measurement coordinates (e.g., sensor locations, projection angles) are uncertain or the system is out of calibration [S1, unofficial][S2]. It formulates the problem as measurement interpolation at unknown nodes, supervised by the forward operator, by jointly optimizing a continuous measurement representation and the uncertain coordinates [S1, unofficial][S2]. Unlike methods that fine-tune a neural network for unknown coordinates or train on a family of operators, this framework reevaluates measurements via a continuous representation for a single example, avoiding the need for fine-tuning or handling distributional tradeoffs [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Differentiable Inverse Graphics Framework](7617-differentiable-inverse-graphics-framework.md) by introducing a specific mechanism for handling coordinate uncertainty through joint optimization of representation parameters and input coordinates.

## How it works
The method models the measurement space as a continuous function that maps measurement coordinates to sampled measurements. It leverages the natural differentiability of implicit neural networks (neural fields) or differentiable splines with respect to their input coordinates to enable gradient-based optimization [S1, unofficial][S2].

### Inputs and Outputs
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Observed measurements $y \in \mathbb{R}^M$ sampled at assumed measurement coordinates $\mu = (\mu_1, \dots, \mu_M) \in \Omega^M$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Assumed measurement coordinates $\mu$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   A differentiable reconstruction method $G_\mu: \mathbb{R}^M \to \mathbb{R}^N$ designed using $\mu$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   A forward operator $A_\mu$ parameterized by $\mu$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:**
    *   Optimized measurement coordinates $\hat{\mu} \in \Omega^M$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Optimized representation parameters $\hat{\phi}$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Reconstructed image/object $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$ [S1, unofficial].

### Data Structures
*   **Measurement Representation $r_\phi(\cdot)$:** A continuous function parameterized by $\phi$. Two implementations are described:
    1.  **Implicit Neural Network (Neural Field):** Comprises a Fourier feature mapping layer followed by standard fully-connected layers [S1, unofficial].
    2.  **Differentiable Spline (NURBS):** Non-uniform Rational Basis Splines using locally supported basis functions, with learnable control points and weights [S1, unofficial].
*   **Reconstruction Method $G_\mu$:** A differentiable operator. In the cited experiments, this is a 2D Unet for 2D CT or a 3D Unet for 3D CT, trained on pseudoinverse initial estimates [S1, unofficial].

### Formulas
The observed measurements $y$ follow the model:
$$ y = A_{\tilde{\mu}}(x) + \eta $$
where $A_{\tilde{\mu}}$ is the discrete forward operator parameterized by the true unknown coordinates $\tilde{\mu}$, $x$ is the object, and $\eta$ is noise [S1, unofficial].

The measurement representation $r_\phi(\cdot)$ is optimized to satisfy:
$$ r_\phi(\omega) \approx y(\omega) $$
where $\omega \in \Omega$ is a measurement coordinate [S1, unofficial]. For a batch of coordinates $\omega = (\omega_1, \dots, \omega_Q) \in \Omega^Q$, the batch evaluation is:
$$ R_\phi(\omega) = [r_\phi(\omega_1), \dots, r_\phi(\omega_Q)]^T \approx [y(\omega_1), \dots, y(\omega_Q)]^T $$
where $R_\phi(\omega) \in \mathbb{R}^Q$ [S1, unofficial].

The joint optimization objective minimizes two losses:
1.  **Fitting Loss:** Ensures the representation matches the observed measurements at the optimized coordinates $\nu$:
    $$ L_{\text{fitting}}(\nu, \phi) = \| y - R_\phi(\nu) \|_2^2 $$
    where $\nu \in \Omega^M$ are the input coordinates being optimized [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Consistency Loss:** Ensures the representation is consistent with the reconstruction method designed for the *assumed* coordinates $\mu$:
    $$ L_{\text{consistency}}(\phi) = \| R_\phi(\mu) - A_\mu G_\mu(R_\phi(\mu)) \|_2^2 $$
    where $G_\mu$ is the reconstruction method and $A_\mu$ is the forward operator based on $\mu$ [S1, unofficial].

The complete objective is:
$$ \hat{\nu}, \hat{\phi} = \arg \min_{\nu \in \Omega^M, \phi \in \Phi} L_{\text{fitting}}(\nu, \phi) + \lambda L_{\text{consistency}}(\phi) $$
where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is a tunable weight [S1, unofficial].

The final reconstruction is:
$$ \hat{x} = G_\mu(R_{\hat{\phi}}(\hat{\mu})) $$
Note that the reconstruction uses the fixed assumed coordinates $\mu$ for $G_\mu$ and $A_\mu$, even though the observations are re-evaluated at the optimized coordinates $\hat{\mu}$ [S1, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   $\mu$: Assumed measurement coordinates (e.g., view angles, detector locations) [S1, unofficial].
*   $\lambda$: Weight for the consistency loss [S1, unofficial].
*   $\phi$: Parameters of the measurement representation (neural network weights or spline control points/weights) [S1, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Initialize:** Set the input coordinates $\nu$ to the assumed measurement coordinates $\mu$, as they are close to the true unknown coordinates $\tilde{\mu}$ [S1, unofficial]. Initialize representation parameters $\phi$ [G].
2.  **Optimize:** Minimize the joint objective (8) with respect to $\nu$ and $\phi$ using gradient-based optimization (e.g., Adam) [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute $L_{\text{fitting}}$ by evaluating $R_\phi(\nu)$ and comparing to $y$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Compute $L_{\text{consistency}}$ by evaluating $R_\phi(\mu)$, passing it through $G_\mu$ and $A_\mu$, and comparing to $R_\phi(\mu)$ [S1, unofficial].
    *   Use automatic differentiation to compute gradients with respect to $\nu$ and $\phi$ [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Reconstruct:** After optimization, obtain the final estimate $\hat{x}$ using the optimized coordinates $\hat{\mu}$ (the optimized $\nu$) and parameters $\hat{\phi}$:
    $$ \hat{x} = G_\mu(R_{\hat{\phi}}(\hat{\mu})) $$
    [S1, unofficial].

### Pseudocode
```python
def differentiable_uncalibrated_imaging(y, mu, G_mu, A_mu, lambda_weight, num_iterations):
    """
    y: Observed measurements, shape (M,)
    mu: Assumed measurement coordinates, shape (M, D) where D is dimension of coordinate space
    G_mu: Differentiable reconstruction method designed for mu
    A_mu: Forward operator parameterized by mu
    lambda_weight: Weight for consistency loss
    num_iterations: Number of optimization steps
    """
    # Initialize representation parameters phi [G]
    phi = initialize_representation_parameters()
    
    # Initialize input coordinates nu to assumed coordinates mu
    nu = mu.clone()
    nu.requires_grad = True
    
    # Representation parameters also require gradients
    phi.requires_grad = True
    
    for i in range(num_iterations):
        # Zero gradients
        nu.grad = None
        phi.grad = None
        
        # 1. Compute Fitting Loss
        # R_phi(nu) evaluates the continuous representation at the current optimized coordinates nu
        R_nu = evaluate_representation(phi, nu) # Shape (M,)
        loss_fitting = torch.norm(y - R_nu) ** 2
        
        # 2. Compute Consistency Loss
        # R_phi(mu) evaluates the representation at the FIXED assumed coordinates mu
        R_mu = evaluate_representation(phi, mu) # Shape (M,)
        
        # Reconstruct using the method designed for mu
        # G_mu takes measurements and outputs image x
        x_recon = G_mu(R_mu)
        
        # Forward operator A_mu acts on the image x to produce simulated measurements
        # A_mu is fixed to mu, so it does not need to be rebuilt
        y_simulated = A_mu(x_recon)
        
        loss_consistency = torch.norm(R_mu - y_simulated) ** 2
        
        # 3. Total Loss
        loss = loss_fitting + lambda_weight * loss_consistency
        
        # 4. Backpropagation
        loss.backward()
        
        # 5. Update parameters and coordinates
        # Use an optimizer like Adam
        optimizer = torch.optim.Adam([nu, phi], lr=0.01)
        optimizer.step()
        
    # Final reconstruction
    # Use the optimized coordinates nu (now hat_mu) and optimized phi
    R_hat_mu = evaluate_representation(phi, nu)
    x_final = G_mu(R_hat_mu)
    
    return x_final, nu, phi
```

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Consistency Loss with Updating Coordinates:** Using the continually updating learned coordinates $\nu$ in the consistency loss $L_{\text{consistency}}$ requires rebuilding the forward operator $A_\nu$ after each iteration. This is time-consuming if the operator is large or complex [S1, unofficial]. The framework avoids this by using the fixed assumed coordinates $\mu$ for the consistency loss [S1, unofficial].
*   **Initialization:** Initializing $\nu$ to $\mu$ is critical because $\mu$ is assumed to be close to the true unknown coordinates $\tilde{\mu}$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Reconstruction Method Training:** The reconstruction method $G_\mu$ must be trained on data generated with the assumed coordinates $\mu$ [S1, unofficial]. If the measurement noise level in the training data does not match the actual measurement noise, performance may degrade [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### Implicit Neural Networks (Neural Fields)
A type of measurement representation using Fourier feature mapping and fully-connected layers. Chosen for their natural differentiability and ability to model complex measurement spaces [S1, unofficial].

### Differentiable Splines (NURBS)
An alternative representation using Non-uniform Rational Basis Splines. Chosen for comparable performance to neural networks, faster optimization time, and well-understood properties [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** The optimization involves gradient-based updates for both representation parameters and input coordinates. The use of fixed $\mu$ in the consistency loss avoids the cost of rebuilding the forward operator $A_\mu$ at each step, which would be required if $\nu$ were used [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the size of the representation (neural network or spline) and the reconstruction method $G_\mu$ [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Applied to 2D and 3D computed tomography, producing improved reconstructions compared to baselines that do not account for the lack of calibration [S2]. The framework allows reuse of pre-trained neural network reconstructions without retraining for new coordinates [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles measurement coordinate uncertainty; does not require fine-tuning or training on a family of operators; allows reuse of existing reconstruction methods [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires the forward operator and reconstruction method to be differentiable; performance depends on the quality of the initial assumption $\mu$ [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The framework was proposed by Gupta, Kothari, Debarnot, and Dokmanić from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign and the University of Basel [S1, unofficial]. It was published as an arXiv preprint in November 2022 (v3 in December 2023) [S1, unofficial]. The work addresses limitations in existing methods that either fine-tune neural networks for unknown coordinates or train on a family of operators, which induce tradeoffs and require extensive dataset generation [S1, unofficial].
