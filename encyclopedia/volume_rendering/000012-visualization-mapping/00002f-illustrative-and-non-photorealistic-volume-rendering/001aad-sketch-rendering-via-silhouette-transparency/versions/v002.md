# Sketch Rendering via Silhouette Transparency

Also called: Volume Sketching, Volumetric Sketch Lines

## Overview
This method creates a sketch-like appearance by rendering volume features as lines or edges, making non-silhouette regions transparent and applying shadows based on the original volume's opacity [S1][S2, unofficial]. It differs from standard illustrative rendering by aggressively modulating opacity based on silhouette strength to abstract continuous volumetric data into discrete, sketch-like outlines, rather than preserving the full volumetric density structure [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume scalar field and view direction $V_{dir}$ [S2, unofficial].
*   Light direction $L_{dir}$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Sketch color (e.g., white or black) [S2, unofficial].
*   Original volume opacity $\alpha_{orig}$ computed from the transfer function and scalar values [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Parameters: $k_{sc}$ (silhouette center), $k_{ss}$ (silhouette spread), $k_{se}$ (silhouette exponent) [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   Final fragment color and opacity [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   Fragment shader state holding the normalized gradient $\nabla f_n$ (a 3D vector) derived from the volume sample [S2, unofficial].
*   Original volume opacity $\alpha_{orig}$ (a scalar) stored per sample for shadow calculation [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1.  **Compute Silhouette Enhancement Factor ($E_{sil}$):** Calculate the alignment between the view direction and the volume gradient normal $\nabla f_n$ [S2, unofficial]. Features facing the viewer have low silhouette strength [S2, unofficial]. The exact formula for $E_{sil}$ is not explicitly provided in the sources, but the parameters $k_{sc}$, $k_{ss}$, $k_{se}$ suggest a Gaussian-like or power-law falloff centered on the silhouette edge [G]. A standard implementation is:
    $$ E_{sil} = \exp\left( -k_{ss} \cdot (\max(0, \nabla f_n \cdot V_{dir}) - k_{sc})^2 \right)^{k_{se}} $$
2.  **Determine Sketch Opacity:** Map $E_{sil}$ to the final opacity $\alpha_{final}$ [S2, unofficial]. Regions with high silhouette enhancement (edges) remain opaque, while non-silhouette regions (interiors) become transparent [S2, unofficial].
    $$ \alpha_{final} = E_{sil} $$
3.  **Set Sketch Color:** The base color of the fragment is set to the specified sketch color (e.g., white or black) [S2, unofficial].
4.  **Calculate Shadows:** Compute the shadow term $S$ using the **original** volume opacity $\alpha_{orig}$, not the sketch opacity [S1][S2, unofficial]. This typically involves ray marching or pre-integration of $\alpha_{orig}$ along the light direction [G].
5.  **Composite Final Color:** Modulate the sketch color and opacity by the shadow term $S$ [G].
    $$ C_{final} = C_{sketch} \cdot S $$
    $$ \alpha_{final} = \alpha_{final} \cdot S $$

**Parameters:**
*   $k_{sc} = 0.9$ (Silhouette center) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   $k_{ss} = 50$ (Silhouette spread) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   $k_{se} = 0.25$ (Silhouette exponent) [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases:**
*   Not given in the sources.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def sketch_render(gradient_normal, view_dir, light_dir, alpha_orig, k_sc, k_ss, k_se, sketch_color):
    # 1. Compute silhouette enhancement factor
    dot_product = max(0.0, dot(gradient_normal, view_dir))
    E_sil = exp(-k_ss * (dot_product - k_sc)**2)**k_se

    # 2. Determine sketch opacity
    alpha_final = E_sil

    # 3. Calculate shadows based on original volume opacity
    # Shadow integration along light direction [G]
    S = integrate_opacity(alpha_orig, light_dir)

    # 4. Composite final color and opacity
    C_final = sketch_color * S
    alpha_final = alpha_final * S
    return C_final, alpha_final
```

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time and Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Effectively emphasizes feature orientation and creates clear, stylized outlines of complex internal structures, such as the liver honeycomb and kidney interior [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Not given in the sources.
*   **When to use:** When a sketch-like appearance is desired by making non-volumetric silhouettes transparent and shading them based on original volume opacity [S2, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** Not given in the sources.

## Background
The technique originates from research on non-photorealistic rendering of volume models [S1][S2, unofficial]. It was developed to visualize complex internal structures in medical volumes by abstracting them into sketch-like forms that highlight boundaries and orientation [S1][S2, unofficial].
