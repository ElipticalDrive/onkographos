# Volume Illustration Tone Shading

Also called: Gooch tone shading for volumes, Modified Gooch tone shading for volume, Modified Gooch tone shading for volume models, Tone illumination model, Volumetric tone shading

## Overview
Volume Illustration Tone Shading is a non-photorealistic illumination model that enhances the perception of 3D shape in volume rendering by modulating color temperature based on the orientation of volume samples relative to a light source [S3, S4, unofficial]. It assigns warm colors (yellows/reds) to regions facing the light and cool colors (blues) to receding regions, creating an illustrative effect suggestive of warm directional illumination like sunlight [S1, S2]. Unlike standard Gooch shading which uses the full signed dot product for illumination, this method modifies the illuminated object contribution to be zero at orientations orthogonal to the light vector, matching familiar diffuse illumination models more closely [S3, S4, unofficial]. It builds on [Illumination and Tone Shading Models](3857-illumination-and-tone-shading-models.md) by combining gaseous illumination with directed tone shading [S3, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Inputs:** Volume scalar field $f$, transfer function $T(v)$ mapping scalar value $v$ to base color and opacity, normalized volume sample gradient $\nabla f_n$ at each sample location, light direction unit vectors $L_i$ for $i = 1 \dots N_L$, gaseous illumination color $I_G$, and illuminated object color contribution $I_i$ derived from the transfer function and gaseous illumination [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   **Outputs:** Final fragment color $c$ [S3, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Gradient Field:** A 3D vector field storing $\nabla f_n$ for each voxel, typically computed via central differences during ray marching or rasterization [S2][S3, unofficial].
*   **Light Vectors:** Array of normalized 3D vectors $L_i$ [S3, unofficial].
*   **Parameters:** Scalars $k_{ta}, k_{td}, k_{ty}, k_{tb}$ [S3, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The final color $c$ is a weighted sum of gaseous illumination and the total tone and directed shading from all light sources [S3, S4, unofficial]:

$$ c = k_{ta} I_G + \sum_{i=1}^{N_L} (I_t + k_{td} I_o) $$

Where:
*   $k_{ta}$ controls the amount of gaseous illumination $I_G$ included [S3, unofficial].
*   $N_L$ is the number of light sources [S3, unofficial].
*   $k_{td}$ controls the amount of directed illumination included [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   $I_t$ is the tone contribution to volume sample color [S3, unofficial].
*   $I_o$ is the illuminated object color contribution [S3, unofficial].

**Tone Contribution ($I_t$):**
For a single light source with direction $L$, the tone contribution interpolates between a warm color $c_w$ and a cool color $c_c$ based on the signed dot product between the normalized gradient $\nabla f_n$ and $L$ [S3, S4, unofficial]:

$$ I_t = \left( \frac{1.0 + \nabla f_n \cdot L}{2} \right) c_w + \left( 1 - \frac{1.0 + \nabla f_n \cdot L}{2} \right) c_c $$

Where:
*   $c_w = (k_{ty}, k_{ty}, 0)$ is the warm tone color vector [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   $c_c = (0, 0, k_{tb})$ is the cool tone color vector [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   $k_{ty}$ controls yellow intensity [S3, unofficial].
*   $k_{tb}$ controls blue intensity [S3, unofficial].

**Directed Illumination Component ($I_o$):**
Unlike standard Gooch shading, the illuminated object contribution is calculated using only the positive dot product, becoming zero at orientations orthogonal to the light vector [S3, S4, unofficial]:

$$ I_o = \begin{cases} I_i (\nabla f_n \cdot L) & \text{if } \nabla f_n \cdot L > 0 \\ 0 & \text{if } \nabla f_n \cdot L \le 0 \end{cases} $$

Where $I_i$ is the illuminated object color contribution [S3, S4, unofficial]. The original paper contains a typo defining $I_o$ with an extra $k_{td}$ factor, which would cause double-scaling when combined with the main summation; the corrected definition above matches the intended control and the pseudocode [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def volume_illustration_tone_shading(sample_value, gradient, transfer_function, light_vectors, k_ta, k_td, k_ty, k_tb):
    # I_G and I_i are base colors from transfer function, possibly modulated by ambient light [G]
    base_color = transfer_function(sample_value)
    I_G = base_color
    I_i = base_color

    c = k_ta * I_G
    for L in light_vectors:
        d = dot(gradient, L)
        w = (1.0 + d) / 2.0
        c_w = vec3(k_ty, k_ty, 0.0)
        c_c = vec3(0.0, 0.0, k_tb)
        I_t = w * c_w + (1.0 - w) * c_c
        if d > 0:
            I_o = I_i * d
        else:
            I_o = vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
        c += I_t + k_td * I_o
    return c
```

**Parameters:**
*   $k_{ta}$: Gaseous illumination weight. Typical value: 1.0 [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   $k_{td}$: Directed illumination weight. Typical value: 0.6 [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   $k_{ty}$: Warm tone yellow intensity. Typical value: 0.3 for medical volumes, 0.15 for iron protein [S3, unofficial].
*   $k_{tb}$: Cool tone blue intensity. Typical value: 0.3 for medical volumes, 0.15 for iron protein [S3, unofficial].
*   $N_L$: Number of lights. Recommended: A few (e.g., 1-3) [S3, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Multiple Lights:** Using more than a few light sources is likely to result in confusing images because humans are not used to interpreting complex illumination from many lights [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   **Orthogonal Gradients:** The directed contribution $I_o$ becomes zero when $\nabla f_n \cdot L = 0$, ensuring no illumination from surfaces orthogonal to the light, matching diffuse models [S3, S4, unofficial].
*   **Gradient Noise:** Noisy gradients can cause flickering tone shading; smoothing or filtering the gradient field may be required [G].

## Subtopics

### [Gooch Shading](1670-gooch-shading.md)
Gooch Shading is a non-photorealistic illumination model for volume rendering that maps surface orientation relative to a light source to a warm-to-cool color gradient to convey shape. It is distinguished from standard Gooch shading by its specific integration with on-the-fly central difference normal estimation in rasterization pipelines, ensuring consistent visual output across different extraction methods (rasterization vs. ray-marching). Choose it when consistent normal estimation across rendering backends is required.

### [Cool to Warm Shading](13943-cool-to-warm-shading.md)
Cool to warm shading is a tone-based illumination model that enhances 3D shape perception by mapping surface orientation to a color gradient, assigning warmer colors to regions facing the reference direction and cooler colors to receding regions. Unlike physically based models like Phong, which may obscure shape information in shadowed areas, this method preserves orientation cues across the visible surface. It is a standard technique in technical illustration and volume rendered illustrations. Choose it when preserving orientation cues in shadowed or back-facing regions is prioritized.

*   **Strengths:** Enhances shape perception and depth cues effectively; preserves basic tissue colors while adding illustrative shading; works well with medical and scientific data [S3, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce confusing results with too many lights; relies on accurate gradient estimation; the illustrative style may not be suitable for all scientific visualization tasks requiring photorealism [S3, S4, unofficial].

*   **When Not to Use:** When photorealistic rendering is required or when the scene contains complex lighting interactions that cannot be approximated by simple directional lights [S3, unofficial].

## Background
Volume Illustration Tone Shading is based on the Gooch tone shading model proposed by Gooch et al. in 1998 [S3, S4, unofficial]. It was adapted for volume rendering by Wilson, Lum, and Ma in their work on "Volume Illustration: Non-Photorealistic Rendering of Volume Models" (2000/2001) [S2][S3, unofficial]. The method was applied to medical volumes (liver/kidney CT) and scientific data (iron protein, tomato MRI) to demonstrate its effectiveness in revealing internal structures and enhancing 3D shape perception [S3, unofficial].
