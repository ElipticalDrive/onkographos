# Vignette Effect
Also called: Post-processing Vignette, Vignetting Post-Processing

## Overview
The Vignette Effect darkens the corners and edges of a rendered image relative to the center by multiplying the color by a factor derived from the pixel's normalized UV coordinates [S1, unofficial]. It is applied as a post-processing step to the final color buffer, distinct from volumetric illumination models like [Vignette Shading](1830-vignette-shading.md) which modulate lighting based on ray alignment within the volume [G].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs**
- Input: Final rendered color buffer (RGB or RGBA), image dimensions ($W \times H$).
- Output: Vignette-modified color buffer.

**Data Structures**
- UV Coordinates: Normalized coordinates $(u, v)$ where $u \in [0, 1]$ and $v \in [0, 1]$, representing the pixel's position in the image plane [S1, unofficial].

**Steps**
1. For each pixel, calculate normalized UV coordinates:
   $$ u = \frac{x}{W}, \quad v = \frac{y}{H} $$
   where $x$ and $y$ are pixel coordinates.
2. Compute the vignette factor. Two formulations exist in the sources:

   *Formulation A (Polynomial/Root-based) [S1, unofficial]:*
   $$ \text{vignette\_factor} = 0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \left( 16 \cdot u \cdot v \cdot (1-u) \cdot (1-v) \right)^{0.25} $$
   The final color is multiplied by this factor [S1, unofficial].

   *Formulation B (Power-based with Fringe) [S2, unofficial]:*
   Let $pos_{2d}$ be the 2D position vector from the image center, and $fr$ be a fringe radius parameter [G].
   $$ \text{vignetting} = \min\left(\frac{\|pos_{2d}\|}{fr}, 1.0\right) $$
   $$ \text{vignetting} = \left( 1.0 - \text{vignetting}^{20.0} \right)^{2.0} $$
   The color is mixed with a background color weighted by this factor:
   $$ \text{color}_{out}.xyz = \text{mix}(\text{vignetting\_background} \cdot \text{color}_w, \text{color}_{xyz}, \text{vignetting}) $$
   where $\text{color}_w$ is the alpha channel and $\text{color}_{xyz}$ is the RGB channels [S2, unofficial].

**Parameters**
- $fr$: Fringe radius. Not given in the sources.
- Exponent (20.0 in Formulation B): Controls the steepness of the vignette. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls**
- Precision: The term $u \cdot v \cdot (1-u) \cdot (1-v)$ in Formulation A is zero at the edges and center, potentially causing floating-point underflow or exact zero results if not handled, though the root operation is stable [G].
- Boundaries: Formulation A naturally goes to 0.5 at the corners (since $u,v \to 0$ or $1$), while Formulation B can go to 0 depending on the exponent and $fr$ [G].
- Degenerate Input: If $W$ or $H$ is 0, UV calculation fails. [G] Ensure $W, H > 0$.

**Pseudocode**
```python
import math

def apply_vignette(color_buffer, width, height, fringe_radius=1.0, vignette_background=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)):
    """
    Applies vignette effect. color_buffer is a 2D list of [r, g, b, a] floats in [0, 1].
    Returns a tuple of (result_a, result_b) implementing Formulation A and B.
    """
    h = len(color_buffer)
    w = len(color_buffer[0])
    center_x = width / 2.0
    center_y = height / 2.0
    result_a = [[None] * w for _ in range(h)]
    result_b = [[None] * w for _ in range(h)]

    for y in range(h):
        for x in range(w):
            pixel = color_buffer[y][x]
            r, g, b = pixel[0], pixel[1], pixel[2]
            a = pixel[3] if len(pixel) > 3 else 1.0

            # Normalized UV coordinates
            u = x / width
            v = y / height

            # Formulation A [S1, unofficial]
            term_a = 16.0 * u * v * (1.0 - u) * (1.0 - v)
            term_a = max(term_a, 0.0)
            factor_a = 0.5 + 0.5 * math.pow(term_a, 0.25)
            result_a[y][x] = [r * factor_a, g * factor_a, b * factor_a, a]

            # Formulation B [S2, unofficial]
            pos_x = (x - center_x) / width
            pos_y = (y - center_y) / height
            pos_2d_len = math.sqrt(pos_x * pos_x + pos_y * pos_y)
            vignetting = min(pos_2d_len / fringe_radius, 1.0)
            vignetting = math.pow(1.0 - math.pow(vignetting, 20.0), 2.0)

            # Linear mix: mix(a, b, t) = a * (1.0 - t) + b * t
            bg_r, bg_g, bg_b = vignette_background
            out_r = (bg_r * a) * (1.0 - vignetting) + r * vignetting
            out_g = (bg_g * a) * (1.0 - vignetting) + g * vignetting
            out_b = (bg_b * a) * (1.0 - vignetting) + b * vignetting
            result_b[y][x] = [out_r, out_g, out_b, a]

    return result_a, result_b
```

## Background
The vignette effect is a common post-processing technique in computer graphics and photography [G]. The specific polynomial formulation using $16 \cdot u \cdot v \cdot (1-u) \cdot (1-v)$ is cited from [S1, unofficial]. The power-based formulation with a fringe radius is cited from [S2, unofficial].
