# Lit Sphere Shading

Also called: Lit sphere map shading, Sphere map shading

## Overview
Lit sphere shading is a non-photorealistic rendering technique that captures artistic lighting styles by using an image of a sphere shaded in a desired style as a lookup table [S1][S2, unofficial]. It represents the shading of an object as a function of eye-space normal orientation, allowing the sphere to act as a proxy object for illumination [S1][S2, unofficial]. Unlike photometric models that calculate light transport, this method maps normal vectors directly to pre-computed artistic colors, providing view-dependent lighting that rotates with the camera [S1][S2, unofficial].

## How it works

### Inputs and Outputs
**Inputs:**
1.  **Lit Sphere Map:** A square texture containing the artistic shading style [S1][S2, unofficial].
2.  **Eye-space Normal:** The normal vector $\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$ of the volume fragment in eye space [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
1.  **Fragment Color:** The final color sampled from the lit sphere map based on the normal's orientation [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Data Structures
**Lit Sphere Map:**
*   **Format:** Square texture (e.g., $N \times N$ pixels) [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Content:** Represents the shading of a sphere under orthographic projection [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Layout:**
    *   The center of the texture corresponds to normal vectors parallel to the viewing direction ($n_x=0, n_y=0$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   The circumference of the inscribed disk corresponds to normal vectors orthogonal to the viewing direction ($n_x^2 + n_y^2 = 1$) [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Texels outside the inscribed disk are never accessed [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   The $n_z$ component is ignored during indexing [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Steps
1.  **Acquire Lit Sphere Map:** Render or obtain an image of a sphere under orthographic projection shaded in the target artistic style [S1][S2, unofficial]. This image serves as the lit sphere map [S2, unofficial].
2.  **Compute Eye-space Normal:** For each fragment, determine the eye-space normal vector $\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y, n_z)$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
3.  **Map to Texture Coordinates:** Convert the $n_x$ and $n_y$ components, which are in the range $[-1, 1]$, to texture coordinates $(u, v)$ in the range $[0, 1]$ [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Formula:
        $$ u = \frac{n_x + 1}{2} $$
        $$ v = \frac{n_y + 1}{2} $$
4.  **Sample Color:** Index the lit sphere map using $(u, v)$ to retrieve the fragment color [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Parameters
*   **Light Source Position:** Treated as a headlight, rotating with the camera (view-dependent lighting) [S1]. This is the desired setup in volume visualization [S1].

### Edge Cases and Pitfalls
*   **Front/Back Face Ambiguity:** The $n_z$ component is ignored during indexing, meaning lighting does not distinguish between front and back faces [S1][S2, unofficial]. This is intentional because the gradient direction in the volume serving as the normal might be flipped depending on data values at a material boundary [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Texture Boundary:** Texels outside the inscribed disk are never accessed [S1][S2, unofficial]. Implementers should ensure texture coordinates are clamped or that the texture is sized to accommodate the full disk without interpolation artifacts at the boundary.

### Pseudocode

```python
def lit_sphere_shading(normal_eye_space, lit_sphere_map):
    """
    Shading technique using a lit sphere map.
    
    Args:
        normal_eye_space: Tuple (nx, ny, nz) representing the eye-space normal.
        lit_sphere_map: 2D array or texture representing the lit sphere map.
        
    Returns:
        Color value sampled from the lit sphere map.
    """
    nx, ny, nz = normal_eye_space
    
    # Convert nx, ny from [-1, 1] to [0, 1]
    u = (nx + 1.0) / 2.0
    v = (ny + 1.0) / 2.0
    
    # Clamp coordinates to handle potential floating point errors
    u = max(0.0, min(1.0, u))
    v = max(0.0, min(1.0, v))
    
    # Sample the lit sphere map
    # Note: nz is ignored as per the method definition
    color = lit_sphere_map.sample(u, v)
    
    return color
```

## Subtopics

### Lit Sphere Map Extraction
*   **What it is:** A method for creating lit sphere maps from existing artwork or non-spherical regions.
*   **Differentiation:** Allows artists to extract shading styles from 2D images rather than rendering 3D spheres.
*   **When to choose:** When a specific artistic style from an existing image is desired.
*   **Link:** [Lit Sphere Map Extraction](link-to-extraction-article)

## Operating characteristics

### Time and Memory Complexity
*   **Time:** $O(1)$ per fragment for shading, as it involves simple arithmetic and a texture lookup [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Memory:** $O(N^2)$ for the lit sphere map texture, where $N$ is the texture resolution [S1][S2, unofficial].

### Strengths and Limitations
*   **Strengths:**
    *   Captures artistic lighting styles effectively [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   View-dependent lighting effects appear regular and rotate with the camera [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Images of illuminated spheres are relatively easy to obtain [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Extraction process allows building a large database of styles with little effort [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:**
    *   Fails to capture complex aspects of realistic illumination [S1][S2, unofficial].
    *   Does not distinguish between front and back faces [S1][S2, unofficial].

### When to Use
*   Use when the goal is to represent the general shading style of an object in an illustrative or non-photorealistic manner [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Use when view-dependent lighting is desired without complex light transport calculations [S1][S2, unofficial].

### When Not to Use
*   Do not use when realistic illumination is required [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   Do not use when front/back face shading distinction is critical [S1][S2, unofficial].

## Background

### Origin
*   The method was presented by Sloan et al. in [SMGG01] for non-photorealistic rendering of polygonal models [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   It was adapted for volume rendering in "Style Transfer Functions for Illustrative Volume Rendering" [S1][S2, unofficial].

### History and Development
*   Sloan et al. described a method for extracting lit sphere maps from non-spherical regions in artwork, providing an interactive tool for rapid extraction [S1][S2, unofficial].
*   The technique is well-suited for illustrative volume rendering due to its simplicity and ability to capture artistic styles [S1][S2, unofficial].
