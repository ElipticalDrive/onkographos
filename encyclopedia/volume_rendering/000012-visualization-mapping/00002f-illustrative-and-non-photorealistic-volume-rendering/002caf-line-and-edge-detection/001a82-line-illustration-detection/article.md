# Line Illustration Detection

Also called: Analytical and pixel-based line detection, Line detection in NPR

## Overview
Line Illustration Detection identifies and renders lines within volumetric data to enhance perceptual clarity through non-photorealistic edge representation [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by focusing specifically on line primitives rather than silhouettes or face-based highlights, categorizing approaches into analytical (vector-oriented, resolution-independent) and pixel-based (image-space, G-buffer dependent) techniques [S1, unofficial].

## Shared principles
The family shares the fundamental goal of enhancing perceptual clarity through non-photorealistic edge representation [G]. Common mechanisms include:
1. **Edge Detection:** Identifying boundaries based on local volumetric properties (gradients, Laplacian of Gaussian) or image-space properties (depth, normal discontinuities) .
2. **Line Rendering:** Converting detected edges into line primitives (strips, segments) or stylized strokes .

## Subtopics

### Analytical Line Detection
Analytical approaches are vector-oriented, resolution-independent, and transformable without artifacts, but require explicit analytical representations of the volume or isosurfaces [S1, unofficial]. These methods are generally more general and flexible than pixel-based alternatives [S1, unofficial].

### Pixel-based Line Detection
Pixel-based methods, such as those utilizing G-buffers, are faster and simpler to implement than analytical methods, particularly when explicit analytical representations are not available [S1, unofficial]. They rely on image-space processing operators and can reuse G-buffers without recalculation [S1, unofficial].

### Principal Direction Line Texture
A technique for visualizing volume isosurfaces by advecting particles along principal curvature directions to create line textures that communicate surface shape [S1, unofficial]. It is based on the premise that lines in the principal directions of curvature communicate surface shape better than lines in other directions [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Calculate the set of principal directions and principal curvatures in the volume [S1, unofficial].
2. Place an evenly distributed set of tiny opaque particles on the isosurfaces of interest [S1, unofficial].
3. Advect the particles through the vector field defined by the principal directions [S1, unofficial].
4. Treat the paths of the particles defined by this advection as a 3D line texture [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
- Particle distribution: evenly distributed on isosurfaces [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
- Algorithmic efficiency and artistic line quality must be addressed for NPR applicability [S1, unofficial].
- Showing line direction alone is not necessarily sufficient for an aesthetically pleasing rendering [S1, unofficial].

### Copper Plate Etching Simulation
A technique simulating copper plate etching by intersecting a scene with closely-spaced, equidistant, parallel planes to detect surface curves [S1, unofficial]. It modulates the final line style using G-buffers containing geometric properties such as colour and depth [S1, unofficial].

**Steps:**
1. Intersect the scene with a set of closely-spaced, equidistant, parallel planes [S1, unofficial].
2. Use a modified ray tracer to detect the surface curves resulting from these intersections [S1, unofficial].
3. Use G-buffers containing geometric properties such as colour and depth to modulate the final line style [S1, unofficial].

## Background
The concept of line illustration in volume visualization has roots in traditional printmaking, specifically copper plate etching, which was used for centuries in scientific publications before modern photographic reproduction [S1, unofficial]. Leister simulated this process by intersecting scenes with parallel planes and using a modified ray tracer [S1, unofficial].

In the context of digital volume visualization, Interrante introduced a novel line illustration technique for visualizing volume isosurfaces in 1997 [S1, unofficial]. This work focused on using principal directions and curvatures to advect particles, creating viewpoint-independent line textures that could visualize multiple isosurfaces simultaneously [S1, unofficial].

The distinction between analytical and pixel-based methods is well-documented in surveys of NPR techniques. Analytical methods are noted for their flexibility and lack of resolution dependence, while pixel-based methods (like those by Saito and Takahashi) are favored for their speed and simplicity when analytical models are unavailable [S1, unofficial]. Hertzmann provided a survey of image-space outline generation algorithms, noting that simple outlines can be generated by extracting edges from depth maps [S1, unofficial].
