# Volume Painting

Also called: Artistic brush-stroke volume rendering, Non-photorealistic volume rendering with brush strokes

## Overview
Volume Painting is a non-photorealistic volume rendering technique that visualizes 3D volume datasets with artistic brush-strokes controlled by user-defined directional texture maps [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of generating painterly, stylized 3D imagery by combining volumetric ray casting with vector fields derived from texture maps to simulate traditional painting strokes [S1, unofficial]. Unlike general volume rendering which produces photorealistic results, Volume Painting produces artistic effects by using noise and vector fields to define stroke direction and width [S1, unofficial]. It builds on [Illustrative and Non-Photorealistic Rendering Styles](19663-illustrative-and-non-photorealistic-styles.md) by focusing specifically on brush-stroke simulation in 3D space.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A triangular mesh model (created in Alias Maya or Topmod), a user-defined directional texture map (vector field), and camera parameters [S1, unofficial].
*   **Output:** A 2D image with artistic brush-stroke effects [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The volume dataset is a 3D voxel array. The file structure contains four components per voxel [S1, unofficial]:
1.  **Voxel Index:** Identifier for the voxel.
2.  **Signed Distance Field (SDF):** A float value. Positive values indicate outside the surface, negative values indicate inside, and zero indicates the boundary. The absolute value is the distance to the closest surface point [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Color:** RGB value derived from UV texture coordinates [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Directional Information:** Vector data derived from UV texture coordinates [S1, unofficial].

**Voxelization (Preprocessing):**
1.  Generate the SDF for the input mesh. The sign is determined by the dot product of the surface normal and the vector from the voxel center to the closest surface point [S1, unofficial].
2.  Assign color and directional values to each voxel by accessing UV texture coordinates based on the quadratic equation for triangles [S1, unofficial].
3.  Convert the SDF to density information using a transfer function [S1, unofficial].

**Volume Ray Casting and Surface Estimation:**
1.  Emit rays from the camera through the voxel block [S1, unofficial].
2.  Instead of standard compositing, use the **bisection method** to find the surface boundary. Divide the distance between temporary exterior (positive SDF) and interior (negative SDF) intersection points in half. Place the surface point between them until the distance is less than a given threshold [S1, unofficial].
3.  Estimate the real boundary by adding the voxel density to the value of Perlin noise applied with Line Integral Convolution (LIC) to create a new value [S1, unofficial].
4.  Check if the new value is within defined threshold parameters to determine the surface point $\tilde{p}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Gradient Estimation:**
Two methods are described for estimating the gradient vector $\vec{N} = [N_x, N_y, N_z]$ [S1, unofficial]:

*   **Central Difference Method:**
    Calculate the gradient from the voxel density function $f(x,y,z)$ [S1, unofficial]:
    $$N_x = f(x-1, y, z) - f(x+1, y, z)$$
    $$N_y = f(x, y-1, z) - f(x, y+1, z)$$
    $$N_z = f(x, y, z-1) - f(x, y, z+1)$$
    This method requires the derivative of voxel densities to exist [S1, unofficial].

*   **Geo-Dome Method:**
    Position a Geo-Dome (spherical geometry with unit-length vertices) at the surface point [S1, unofficial]. Calculate the gradient $\nabla f$ as:
    $$\nabla f \approx \sum_{i=0}^{K} a_i \vec{v}_i$$
    where $\vec{v}_i$ are the Geo-Dome vertices and $a_i = 1$ if $f(\vec{v}_i + p) \ge 0$ (outside) and $a_i = 0$ otherwise (inside). Sum the outside vectors and subtract the inside vectors. Normalize the result. Rotate the Geo-Dome around a random axis with random magnitude to remove artifacts caused by limited vertex count [S1, unofficial].

**Brush-Stroke Noise Generation:**
1.  Normalize the surface point $P$ to get $h(p) = \text{normalize}(P(wp))$, where $w$ is the frequency [S1, unofficial].
2.  Compute the noise value $g(p)$:
    $$g(p) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N} h(p_i)$$
    where $P()$ is the Perlin noise function, $p$ is the surface point, $p_i$ are sample positions along the brush-stroke direction $\vec{v}$, and $N$ is the number of samples [S1, unofficial].

**Opacity Calculation:**
The opacity $\alpha$ is computed based on the desired structure [S1, unofficial]:
1.  **Solid Internal Structure:**
    $$\alpha = g(p) + f(p)$$
2.  **Empty Inside:**
    $$\alpha = g(p) + |f(p) - a|$$
    where $a$ is an arbitrary real number that changes the thickness of the painted area, and $f(p)$ normalizes the signed distance field between 0 and 1 [S1, unofficial].
The surface point $\tilde{p}$ is estimated by determining if $\alpha$ is larger than a given threshold [S1, unofficial].

**Illumination and Shading:**
1.  Convolve the color information of the surface point $\tilde{p}$ along the brush-stroke direction $\vec{v}$ to get the diffuse color $C(\tilde{p})$ [S1, unofficial].
2.  Apply the Phong illumination model [S1, unofficial]:
    $$I = k_a C(\tilde{p}) + \sum_{i=1}^{n} [k_d C(\tilde{p})(\vec{N} \cdot \vec{L}_i) + k_s O_s (\vec{R}_i \cdot \vec{V}_n)]$$
    where $I$ is the illumination color, $k_a, k_d, k_s$ are ambient, diffuse, and specular coefficients, $\vec{L}_i$ are light vectors, $\vec{R}_i$ are reflection vectors, $\vec{V}_n$ is the view vector, and $O_s$ is the specular exponent [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def volume_painting(camera_rays, volume_dataset, texture_map, threshold):
    """
    Renders a volume dataset with artistic brush strokes.
    
    Args:
        camera_rays: List of rays from camera.
        volume_dataset: 3D voxel array with SDF, color, direction.
        texture_map: User-defined directional texture map.
        threshold: Threshold for surface boundary detection.
        
    Returns:
        Image buffer.
    """
    image = create_empty_image()
    
    for ray in camera_rays:
        # 1. Volume Ray Casting with Bisection
        # Find initial exterior (pos_sdf) and interior (neg_sdf) points
        pos_sdf, neg_sdf = find_ray_voxel_intersections(ray, volume_dataset)
        
        # Bisection to find precise boundary
        while abs(pos_sdf - neg_sdf) > threshold:
            mid = (pos_sdf + neg_sdf) / 2.0
            sdf_val = sample_sdf(ray, mid, volume_dataset)
            if sdf_val > 0:
                pos_sdf = mid
            else:
                neg_sdf = mid
        
        surface_point = (pos_sdf + neg_sdf) / 2.0
        sdf_val = sample_sdf(ray, surface_point, volume_dataset)
        
        # 2. Gradient Estimation (Geo-Dome Method preferred for robustness)
        gradient = estimate_gradient_geodome(surface_point, volume_dataset)
        
        # 3. Brush-Stroke Noise Generation
        # Get brush direction from texture map at surface point
        brush_direction = get_direction_from_texture(surface_point, texture_map)
        
        # Sample Perlin noise along brush direction
        noise_samples = []
        for i in range(N_samples):
            sample_pos = surface_point + i * brush_direction
            # Normalize position scaled by frequency w
            h_p = normalize(sample_pos * frequency)
            noise_val = perlin_noise(h_p)
            noise_samples.append(noise_val)
        
        g_p = sum(noise_samples) / len(noise_samples)
        
        # 4. Opacity Calculation
        # Normalize SDF between 0 and 1
        f_p = normalize_sdf(sdf_val)
        
        # Choose structure type (Solid or Empty)
        # For this example, assuming Solid Internal Structure
        alpha = g_p + f_p
        
        # 5. Surface Point Estimation
        if alpha > threshold:
            # 6. Illumination and Shading
            # Convolve color along brush direction
            color = convolve_color_along_direction(surface_point, brush_direction, volume_dataset)
            
            # Phong shading
            light_contrib = 0.0
            for light in scene_lights:
                L = normalize(light.position - surface_point)
                R = reflect(-L, gradient)
                V = normalize(camera.position - surface_point)
                light_contrib += kd * color * dot(gradient, L) + ks * specular_exponent * dot(R, V)
            
            illumination = ka * color + light_contrib
            
            # Composite into image
            image.set_pixel(ray.pixel_index, illumination)
        else:
            image.set_pixel(ray.pixel_index, background_color)
            
    return image
```

**Parameters:**
*   **Voxel Array Size:** 30x30x30, 50x50x50, or the maximum size allowed by physical memory [S1, unofficial].
*   **Threshold parameters:** Used to determine if the calculated new value defines the surface boundary [S1, unofficial].
*   **Frequency of Perlin noise:** Adjusts the width of the brush-stroke [S1, unofficial].
*   **Vector field:** Obtained from a user-defined texture map, controls the direction and style of the brush-stroke [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Gradient Estimation:** The Central Difference Method fails if the derivative of voxel densities does not exist. The Geo-Dome method is used as an alternative but may require rotation to remove artifacts from limited vertex count [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Usage:** Larger voxel sizes generate higher quality images but require large amounts of physical memory and longer processing times [S1, unofficial].
*   **Standard Compositing:** The method explicitly avoids standard compositing ($C_{out} = C_{in} + (1 - \alpha_{in})C_i$) which uses early termination at $\alpha_{in} = 0.9999$, in favor of the bisection method for boundary detection [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not explicitly given, but processing time increases with voxel array size [S1, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Proportional to the voxel array size. Limited by physical memory of the processing computer [S1, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Allows intuitive adjustment of brush-stroke direction via texture maps; simulates artistic painterly effects in 3D space; can be animated by simulating the vector field [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Requires significant memory for large voxel arrays; relies on user-defined texture maps for style control; not photorealistic [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When artistic, non-photorealistic, brush-stroke style visualization of 3D volume data is desired [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When photorealistic rendering is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Volume Painting was proposed by Ebert and Rheingans [13] (Note: The source text attributes the method to the author of the thesis, citing Ebert and Rheingans as related work for LIC, but the thesis itself presents the Volume Painting method). The technique was developed as part of a master's thesis at Texas A&M University [S1, unofficial]. It is used in the field of non-photorealistic rendering (NPR) to create artistic visualizations of volume data, such as implicit objects and medical models [S1, unofficial].
