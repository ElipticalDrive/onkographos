# Silhouette Edges

Also called: Gradient-based silhouette detection, View-dependent edge rendering

## Overview
Silhouette Edges is a volumetric gradient-based edge detection method that emphasizes features within a density volume by rendering surface normals perpendicular to the view direction as opaque black lines [S1, unofficial]. It distinguishes itself from sibling methods by operating directly on the volume gradient during ray casting, limiting edge visibility to a specific density range to prevent the "thick line" artifact common in kernel density estimates, whereas [LOG-based Silhouette Detection](6322-log-based-silhouette-detection.md) relies on local orientation gradients and [Contour Rendering](8471-contour-rendering.md) typically isolates specific isosurfaces [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Volume density field $\rho(\mathbf{x})$.
*   Gradient field $\nabla \rho(\mathbf{x})$.
*   View direction vector $\mathbf{v}$ (normalized).
*   Emphasis range $[\rho_{min}, \rho_{max}]$.
*   Silhouette threshold $\tau$ (dot product threshold).

**Outputs:**
*   Final color buffer with black silhouette edges overlaid on the volume rendering.

**Data Structures:**
*   Standard ray marching state: current position $\mathbf{x}$, accumulated alpha $\alpha$, accumulated color $C$.
*   Sampled gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla \rho(\mathbf{x})$.

**Steps:**
1.  Initialize ray marching parameters. Set accumulated alpha $\alpha = 0$ and color $C = (0,0,0,0)$.
2.  For each sample point $\mathbf{x}$ along the ray:
    a.  Sample the density $\rho$ and gradient $\mathbf{g}$ at $\mathbf{x}$.
    b.  Calculate the dot product $d = \mathbf{g} \cdot \mathbf{v}$.
    c.  Check if the sample is within the emphasis range: $\rho_{min} \le \rho \le \rho_{max}$.
    d.  Check if the sample is a silhouette candidate: $d < \tau$.
    e.  If both conditions are met, render the sample as fully opaque black. This overrides the standard volume accumulation for this sample, effectively drawing a black line where the gradient is perpendicular to the view and density is in the target range.
    f.  Otherwise, perform standard front-to-back alpha blending using the sampled density and opacity transfer function.
    g.  Update accumulated alpha $\alpha$.
    h.  Stop if $\alpha \ge 0.96$ or the ray exits the volume [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\tau$: Predefined threshold for the dot product. Not given in the sources.
*   $\rho_{min}, \rho_{max}$: Range of density values for emphasis. Not given in the sources.
*   $\alpha_{stop}$: Threshold for stopping ray marching. Typically $0.96$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pitfalls:**
*   Rendering all silhouette edges without density filtering leads to very thick edge lines that obscure most of the volume because kernel density estimation results in no completely solid surfaces [S1, unofficial].
*   Precision issues may arise if the gradient magnitude is near zero, making the dot product unstable. [G] Normalize the gradient before dot product calculation.

## Subtopics

### Environment Map Silhouette Darkening
A hybrid method by Gooch et al. that illustrates silhouette edges by darkening vertices based on a dot product threshold, with width varying by curvature [S2]. It uses an environment map to illustrate silhouette edges and darkens vertices that are below a low dot product threshold [S2]. It achieves a stylistic effect with silhouette width varying with curvature [S2]. This method is object-space based, contrasting with the volume-space approach of the main Silhouette Edges method.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Linear with respect to the number of samples per ray, as it requires one gradient evaluation and dot product check per sample.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the volume density and gradient fields.
*   **Performance:** Fast, as it integrates edge detection into the standard ray casting loop without requiring additional passes or complex post-processing [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Effectively emphasizes features within the volume by outlining regions of interest (defined by the density range) [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Can produce thick, obscuring lines if the density range is not carefully chosen or if the volume lacks sharp density transitions [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When emphasizing specific density ranges in a volumetric dataset, such as particle systems or medical volumes, without isolating a single isosurface.
*   **When not to use:** When thin, precise edge lines are required, or when the volume has no clear density-based features to emphasize.

## Background
The method was introduced in "Illustrative Rendering of Particle Systems" [S1, unofficial]. It is designed to enhance the visualization of particle systems and other volumetric data by adding illustrative edge cues. The approach builds on standard ray casting techniques but adds a conditional opaque black rendering step based on gradient-view alignment and density thresholds.
