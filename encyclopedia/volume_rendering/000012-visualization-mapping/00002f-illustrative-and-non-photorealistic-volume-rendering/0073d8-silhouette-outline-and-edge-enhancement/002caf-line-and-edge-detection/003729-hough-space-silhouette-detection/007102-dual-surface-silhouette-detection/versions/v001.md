# Dual Surface Silhouette Detection
Also called: Duality Map Silhouette Detection
## Overview
Dual Surface Silhouette Detection is a geometric method for identifying object silhouettes by transforming surface geometry into a dual space, where silhouette detection is reduced to the intersection of the viewplane with the dual surface [S1]. Unlike Gauss maps, which are ideally suited for orthographic projections but require additional searching under perspective projections, dual surfaces provide a general approach applicable to both projection types [S1]. It builds on [Hough Space Silhouette Detection](7957-hough-space-silhouette-detection.md) by utilizing duality maps to simplify the visibility computation.
## How it works
**Inputs:** A 3D surface mesh (defined by vertices and faces) and a camera configuration (viewpoint and viewplane).
**Outputs:** A set of 2D silhouette points on the viewplane.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on a duality map that transforms geometric primitives from primal space (3D) to dual space.
- **Dual Surface:** The representation of the input surface in dual space. For a surface defined by points or edges, the dual space contains corresponding dual points or dual planes.
- **Viewpoint Dual Plane:** The representation of the camera's viewpoint in dual space.

**Steps:**
1. **Transform to Dual Space:** Apply a duality map to the input surface geometry. This transforms the primal surface (vertices/edges) into a dual surface [S1].
2. **Intersect with Viewplane:** Compute the intersection of the viewplane with the dual surface. This intersection yields a set of silhouette points [S1].
3. **Visibility Testing (Optional):** For efficient rendering, hierarchical visibility methods can be applied. Testing for silhouette visibility is reduced to searching hierarchies on the dual planes, achieving performance logarithmic in the number of edges [S1].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def dual_surface_silhouette_detection(surface, camera):
    """
    Detect silhouettes using dual surface intersection.
    
    Args:
        surface: A 3D mesh defined by vertices and edges.
        camera: A camera object with a viewpoint and viewplane.
        
    Returns:
        silhouette_points: A list of 2D points on the viewplane.
    """
    # Step 1: Transform surface into dual space using a duality map
    # The duality map transforms primal points/edges into dual points/planes
    dual_surface = apply_duality_map(surface)
    
    # Step 2: Intersect the viewplane with the dual surface
    # The intersection yields the silhouette points
    silhouette_points = intersect_viewplane_with_dual_surface(camera.viewplane, dual_surface)
    
    return silhouette_points
```

- **Duality Map:** The specific mathematical transformation used. Not given in the sources.
- **Hierarchical Structure:** If used, typically an octree or BSP tree for acceleration [S1].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Perspective vs. Orthographic:** While Gauss maps require additional searching for perspective projections, dual surfaces handle both generally. However, the complexity of the dual surface intersection may vary with projection type [S1].
- **Precision:** Intersection calculations in dual space may suffer from numerical precision issues if the duality map involves large coordinate ranges [G].

## Subtopics

### Edge/Point Dual Silhouette Extraction
A method by Pop et al. that derives silhouettes by intersecting point or edge duals with the viewpoint dual plane [S1]. It is a specific instantiation of the dual surface approach where the dual elements are discrete points or edges rather than continuous surfaces [S1]. Choose this variant when working with discrete geometric primitives or when a simplified dual representation is sufficient. It is linked to [Dual Surface Silhouette Detection](52-dual-surface-silhouette-detection.md).

## Operating characteristics
**Time Complexity:** When hierarchical visibility methods (such as octrees or BSP trees) are used, silhouette visibility testing achieves performance logarithmic in the number of edges [S1].
**Memory Complexity:** Depends on the storage of the dual surface and any hierarchical structures. Not given in the sources.
**Strengths:** General applicability to both orthographic and perspective projections; efficient visibility testing via hierarchies [S1].
**Limitations:** Requires transformation to dual space; complexity of intersection may be higher than image-space methods for very dense meshes [S1].
**When to Use:** When geometric precision is required and the scene allows for efficient hierarchical culling.
**When Not to Use:** For real-time applications with extremely high polygon counts where image-space methods might be faster [S1].

## Background
The method was introduced by Hertzmann and Zorin [HZ00] using the notion of a dual surface to detect silhouettes [S1]. A similar approach was employed by Pop et al. [PDB*01], deriving silhouettes by intersecting point (or edge) duals with the viewpoint dual plane [S1]. The technique is part of the broader field of non-photorealistic rendering (NPR) and edge enhancement, where accurate silhouette detection is crucial for illustrative rendering [S1].
