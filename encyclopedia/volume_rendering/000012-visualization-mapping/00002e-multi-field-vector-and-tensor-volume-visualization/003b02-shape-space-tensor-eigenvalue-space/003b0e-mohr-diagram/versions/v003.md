# Mohr Diagram

Also called: Mohr's Circle

## Overview
The Mohr Diagram is a visualization technique for stress tensors that plots normal stress against shear stress to represent the most important invariants [S1, book]. It sets itself apart from scalar volume rendering by explicitly mapping the relationship between the three eigenvalues of a symmetric tensor to geometric circles, allowing for the inspection of maximum shear stress and the transition between tensile and compressive forces [S1, book]. It builds on [Shape Space (Tensor Eigenvalue Space)](8885-shape-space-tensor-eigenvalue-space.md) by projecting tensor invariants onto a 2D plane [S1, book].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A symmetric second-order tensor field. The three eigenvalues of each tensor are extracted [S1, book].
*   **Output:** A 2D plot where the horizontal axis represents normal stress and the vertical axis represents shear stress [S1, book].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Tensor:** A $3 \times 3$ symmetric matrix [S1, book].
*   **Eigenvalues:** A tuple $(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3)$ representing the principal stresses [S1, book].
*   **Mohr Circle:** Defined by a center $C$ and radius $R$ [S1, book].

**Formulas:**
For a tensor with eigenvalues $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3$, three Mohr circles are constructed between the eigenvalues [S1, book]:
1.  **Outer Circle:** Connects the maximum and minimum eigenvalues [S1, book].
    *   Center: $C_{outer} = \frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_3}{2}$
    *   Radius: $R_{outer} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}$
2.  **Middle Circle:** Connects the maximum and middle eigenvalues [S1, book].
    *   Center: $C_{mid1} = \frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}{2}$
    *   Radius: $R_{mid1} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2}$
3.  **Inner Circle:** Connects the middle and minimum eigenvalues [S1, book].
    *   Center: $C_{mid2} = \frac{\sigma_2 + \sigma_3}{2}$
    *   Radius: $R_{mid2} = \frac{\sigma_2 - \sigma_3}{2}$
The shaded region between the circles represents all possible combinations of normal and shear forces for a given cutting plane [S1, book]. A point within this region corresponds to the orientation of the plane’s normal [S1, book].

**Parameters:**
*   **Sign Convention:** Negative eigenvalues are interpreted as compressive forces (volume reduction); positive eigenvalues as tensile forces (volume expansion) [S1, book]. Some application areas use the reverse sign convention [S1, book].
*   **Clutter Reduction:** To equalize circle distribution and reduce clutter around the origin (high shear and tensile/compressive transitions), the diagram is divided into three separate sub-diagrams based on anisotropic behavior (isotropic, linear, planar) [S1, book]. Semi-circles are drawn in context regions to achieve a compact visualization [S1, book].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_mohr_circles(eigenvalues):
    sigma1, sigma2, sigma3 = eigenvalues
    return [
        {"center": (sigma1 + sigma3) / 2.0, "radius": (sigma1 - sigma3) / 2.0, "type": "outer"},
        {"center": (sigma1 + sigma2) / 2.0, "radius": (sigma1 - sigma2) / 2.0, "type": "mid1"},
        {"center": (sigma2 + sigma3) / 2.0, "radius": (sigma2 - sigma3) / 2.0, "type": "mid2"}
    ]

def classify_tensor(eigenvalues):
    sigma1, sigma2, sigma3 = eigenvalues
    # Categorization thresholds not given in the sources.
    if abs(sigma1 - sigma3) < 1e-6:
        return "isotropic"
    elif abs(sigma2 - sigma3) < abs(sigma1 - sigma2):
        return "planar"
    else:
        return "linear"
```

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Isotropic Tensors:** If $\sigma_1 \approx \sigma_2 \approx \sigma_3$, the circles collapse to a single point [S1, book].
*   **Precision:** Numerical stability in eigenvalue decomposition is critical for accurate circle placement [G].

## Subtopics

### Mohr Diagram (Extended)
An extension of the standard Mohr diagram designed to reduce clutter and analyze the relationship between three eigenvalues and their relative strength [S1, book]. It categorizes tensors by anisotropic behavior (isotropic, linear, or planar) and divides the diagram into three separate sub-diagrams to equalize circle distribution [S1, book]. Tensors with similar eigenvalue behavior are clustered using Euclidean distance in shape space as the similarity measure, and the number of occurrences in each cluster is encoded by color [S1, book]. Semi-circles are drawn in context regions to achieve a compact visualization without losing information [S1, book].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, book].
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources [S1, book].
*   **Strengths:** Provides immediate visual insight into maximum shear stress (radius of the outer circle) and the balance between tensile and compressive forces (position on the x-axis) [S1, book].
*   **Limitations:** Best used after a selection has been specified in other diagram views, as it is not suitable for inspecting large, unselected datasets due to potential clutter [S1, book].
*   **Scaling:** The extended version scales better for dense datasets by using clustering and separation by anisotropy [S1, book].

## Background
The Mohr diagram originates from the analysis of stress tensors, where it represents the most important invariants [S1, book]. It is based on the classical Mohr's circle method from continuum mechanics [10] (cited in [S1, book]). The specific visualization techniques described, including the extended diagram with clustering and anisotropy-based separation, were proposed by A. Kratz, B. Meyer, and I. Hotz in 2011 [S1, book].
