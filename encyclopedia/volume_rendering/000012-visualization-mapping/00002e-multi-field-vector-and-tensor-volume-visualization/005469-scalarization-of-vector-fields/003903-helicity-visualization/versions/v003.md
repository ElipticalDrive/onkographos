# Helicity Visualization
Also called: Helicity Calculation
## Overview
Helicity Visualization is a scalarization technique for vector fields that maps the dot product of velocity and vorticity to opacity and color to identify vortices [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from siblings like [Metric Scalar Field Rendering from Vector Fields](14065-metric-scalar-field-rendering-from-vector-fields.md) by explicitly leveraging the alignment between flow direction and local rotation (curl) to highlight swirling features, whereas metric methods often rely on magnitude or strain [S2].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A 3D vector field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z)$ representing velocity.
- Output: A volume rendering where opacity and color are determined by the helicity scalar field.

**Data Structures:**
- The velocity field $\mathbf{F}$ is stored as a 3D grid of vectors.
- The helicity field $h$ is a 3D grid of scalars, computed per voxel.

**Steps:**
1. **Compute Vorticity:** Calculate the curl of the velocity field, $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$, at each grid location. This represents the local rotation or vorticity [S2].
2. **Compute Helicity:** Calculate the dot product of the velocity vector $\mathbf{F}$ and the vorticity vector $\nabla \times \mathbf{F}$ at each point [S1, unofficial][S2]:
   $$ h(\mathbf{x}) = (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})) \cdot \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) $$
   This scalar value represents the curl in the direction of the velocity [S2].
3. **Map Magnitude to Opacity:** Map the magnitude $|h|$ to opacity $\alpha$. Large magnitudes (indicating strong vortices) are mapped to high opacity, while small magnitudes are mapped to near transparency [S1, unofficial].
4. **Map Sign to Color:** Map the signed value of $h$ to color:
   - Extreme negative values: Cyan [S1, unofficial].
   - Extreme positive values: Magenta [S1, unofficial].
   - Values near zero: Yellow [S1, unofficial].

### [Perturbation Velocity Visualization](8398-perturbation-velocity-visualization.md)
Isolates flow disturbances by removing bulk free stream flow, mapping perturbation magnitude to opacity. Chosen when background flow obscures features of interest.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by the computation of the curl (gradient) and dot product, which is $O(N)$ where $N$ is the number of voxels.
- **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the velocity field, vorticity field, and helicity field, $O(N)$.
- **Strengths:** Effectively highlights vortices and swirling features by exploiting the alignment of velocity and vorticity [S1, unofficial][S2].
- **Limitations:** Requires accurate computation of derivatives (curl), which can be sensitive to noise in the velocity field. The sign of helicity indicates rotation direction, but the visualization maps magnitude to opacity, potentially losing directional information in the alpha channel [S1, unofficial].
- **When to use:** When the goal is to identify and visualize vortices in a fluid flow field.
- **When not to use:** When the flow is irrotational (vorticity is zero) or when the alignment between velocity and vorticity is not the feature of interest.

## Background
Helicity visualization was introduced in the context of Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) volume rendering to identify vortices [S1, unofficial]. The concept of helicity as the dot product of velocity and vorticity is a standard definition in fluid dynamics [S2]. The specific visualization technique using color mapping (cyan/magenta/yellow) and opacity mapping based on magnitude was demonstrated in early NASA research on volume rendering for CFD [S1, unofficial]. Later work integrated helicity into multi-dimensional transfer functions for interactive 3D flow visualization [S2].
