# Diffusion Tensor Principal Eigenvector Display

Also called: principal eigenvector display, rank 1 tensor display

## Overview
This method simplifies the diffusion tensor model to display only the principal eigenvector for each voxel, representing local connectivity and anisotropy [S1, unofficial]. It reduces full tensor information to a dominant directional component suitable for direct rendering or tracking, differing from full tensor glyphs like [Tensor Glyphs](12146-tensor-glyphs.md) by focusing exclusively on the primary diffusion direction [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:** A diffusion tensor field $T(\vec{r})$ over a 3D volume, where each voxel stores a symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrix [S1, unofficial][G].
**Outputs:** A derived vector field $\vec{v}(\vec{r})$ per voxel for rendering or tracking [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Input Tensor:** Stored as 6 unique components per voxel or a $3 \times 3$ matrix [G].
*   **Eigen Decomposition:** Yields eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \lambda_3 \ge 0$ and orthogonal eigenvectors $\vec{e}_1, \vec{e}_2, \vec{e}_3$ [G].
*   **Output Vector:** A 3D array of shape $(N_x, N_y, N_z, 3)$ [G].

**Steps:**
1.  **Eigen Decomposition:** For each voxel, compute eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ and eigenvectors $\vec{e}_i$ of $T$ [G].
2.  **Select Principal Component:** Identify $\vec{e}_1$ corresponding to the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_1$, representing the direction of maximum diffusion [S1, unofficial].
3.  **Compute Scalar Modulator:** Calculate a scalar measure $f(\vec{r})$ to weight the vector magnitude, such as $\lambda_1$ or the trace $\sum_{i=1}^3 \lambda_i$ [S1, unofficial].
4.  **Construct Vector Field:** Compute the display vector $\vec{v}(\vec{r}) = f(\vec{r})\vec{e}_1(\vec{r})$, treating the tensor as a rank-1 tensor aligned with $\vec{e}_1$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formulas:**
The diffusion tensor decomposes as $T = (\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)T_1 + (\lambda_2 - \lambda_3)T_2 + \lambda_3 T_3$, where $T_i = \vec{e}_i \vec{e}_i^T$ [S1, unofficial]. For display, $\vec{v}(\vec{r}) = f(\vec{r})\vec{e}_1(\vec{r})$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_principal_eigenvector_display(tensor_field):
    """
    Computes the principal eigenvector display vector field from a diffusion tensor field.
    Args:
        tensor_field: A 3D array of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz, 6) representing symmetric diffusion tensors.
    Returns:
        vector_field: A 3D array of shape (Nx, Ny, Nz, 3) containing the display vectors.
    """
    Nx, Ny, Nz = tensor_field.shape[:3]
    vector_field = [[[[0.0, 0.0, 0.0] for _ in range(Nz)] for _ in range(Ny)] for _ in range(Nx)]
    
    for x in range(Nx):
        for y in range(Ny):
            for z in range(Nz):
                # Extract tensor T at voxel (x,y,z) from 6 components [Txx, Tyy, Tzz, Txy, Txz, Tyz]
                c = tensor_field[x][y][z]
                T = [[c[0], c[3], c[4]],
                     [c[3], c[1], c[5]],
                     [c[4], c[5], c[2]]]
                
                # Eigen decomposition step [G]
                eigenvalues, eigenvectors = eigendecompose_3x3_symmetric(T)
                
                # Sort eigenvalues and eigenvectors in descending order
                indices = sorted(range(3), key=lambda i: eigenvalues[i], reverse=True)
                eigenvalues = [eigenvalues[i] for i in indices]
                eigenvectors = [[eigenvectors[i][j] for j in range(3)] for i in indices]
                
                # Principal eigenvector e1
                e1 = eigenvectors[0]
                
                # Scalar measure f(r) (e.g., trace)
                f = sum(eigenvalues)
                
                # Construct display vector
                v = [f * e1[0], f * e1[1], f * e1[2]]
                
                vector_field[x][y][z] = v
                
    return vector_field
```

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Degenerate Tensors:** In regions where fibre tracts meet or cross, the tensor may be rank 2 or 3. The principal eigenvector display cannot optimally handle these points, as it assumes a single dominant direction [S1, unofficial].
*   **Precision:** Numerical stability in eigen decomposition should be ensured, particularly for near-isotropic tensors where eigenvalues are close [G].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** At volume boundaries, tensor estimation may be unreliable, leading to noisy eigenvectors [G].

## Subtopics

### Eigenvector Color Maps
A visualization technique that maps the spatial direction of the principal eigenvector directly to RGB colors [S2, unofficial]. The X, Y, and Z components of the principal eigenvector $\vec{e}_1$ are mapped to the Red, Green, and Blue color channels, respectively [S2, unofficial]. The saturation of the resulting RGB color is modulated by an anisotropic metric (e.g., Fractional Anisotropy, or CL/CP/CS metrics) to indicate the strength of anisotropy [S2, unofficial]. This is useful for displaying spatial patterns of coherence, as the principal eigenvector aligns with coherent fibers [S2, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Computationally efficient compared to full tensor rendering (e.g., ellipsoids). It simplifies the visual clutter of tensor data and is a direct prerequisite for streamline tractography [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Loses information about the shape of diffusion (e.g., planar vs. linear anisotropy) and cannot resolve crossing fibers [S1, unofficial]. Structural boundaries may be harder to distinguish compared to anatomical or MIP images [S1, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When the primary goal is to visualize connectivity or dominant flow directions, or as an intermediate step for tracking algorithms [G].
*   **When not to use:** When detailed microstructural information (shape of the diffusion ellipsoid) or crossing fiber resolution is required [G].

## Background
This method is used in interactive visualization tools for MRI vector and tensor fields [S1, unofficial]. It simplifies the ellipsoid model to displaying the principal eigenvector for each voxel to address connectivity questions [S1, unofficial].
