# Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC)
## Overview
The Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC) is a scalar quantity describing the diffusion along a specific direction vector $\mathbf{v}$, derived directly from the diffusion tensor $\mathbf{D}$ [S1, unofficial]. Unlike rotationally invariant metrics such as Mean Diffusivity (MD) or Fractional Anisotropy (FA) that summarize the entire tensor, ADC is direction-dependent, quantifying diffusion intensity along a chosen axis [S1, unofficial]. It provides a direct tensor-based scalar for directional analysis, distinct from shape-domain metrics like linear or planar certainty [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
1. $\mathbf{D}$: A $3 \times 3$ symmetric diffusion tensor at a specific voxel location [G].
2. $\mathbf{v}$: A direction vector along which the diffusion is to be measured [S1, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
1. $a_{\mathbf{v}}$: A scalar value representing the Apparent Diffusion Coefficient along $\mathbf{v}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
The diffusion tensor $\mathbf{D}$ is stored as a $3 \times 3$ matrix [G]. Due to symmetry, only 6 unique values are required, but the formula operates on the full matrix representation [G]:
$$
\mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix}
D_{xx} & D_{xy} & D_{xz} \\
D_{yx} & D_{yy} & D_{yz} \\
D_{zx} & D_{zy} & D_{zz}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
where $D_{ij} = D_{ji}$. The direction vector $\mathbf{v}$ is a 3-element vector:
$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
v_x \\
v_y \\
v_z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Steps:**
1. Normalize the direction vector $\mathbf{v}$ to unit length [S1, unofficial].
2. Compute the quadratic form $a_{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{D} \mathbf{v}$ [S1, unofficial].

**Formula:**
The ADC along direction $\mathbf{v}$ is defined as:
$$
a_{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{D} \mathbf{v}
$$
Expanding this into component form:
$$
a_{\mathbf{v}} = D_{xx}v_x^2 + D_{yy}v_y^2 + D_{zz}v_z^2 + 2D_{xy}v_xv_y + 2D_{xz}v_xv_z + 2D_{yz}v_yv_z
$$

**Parameters:**
* $\mathbf{v}$: A normalized direction vector [S1, unofficial].
* $\mathbf{D}$: The diffusion tensor [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
* **Critical Points:** If the diffusion tensor $\mathbf{D}$ has two equal eigenvalues, it is a critical point and the eigenvectors are not uniquely defined [S1, unofficial]. The ADC calculation remains numerically stable, but the choice of $\mathbf{v}$ becomes sensitive to noise if derived from eigenvectors at these points [S1, unofficial].
* **Normalization:** The formula assumes $\mathbf{v}$ is a unit vector. If $\mathbf{v}$ is not normalized, the result scales with the square of the vector's magnitude, leading to incorrect physical interpretations [G].
* **Tensor Validity:** The tensor $\mathbf{D}$ must be positive semi-definite. In practice, noise in DTI acquisition can lead to non-positive-definite tensors, which may result in negative ADC values for certain directions. Clamping or tensor regularization may be required before computation [G].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_adc(tensor_D, direction_v):
    # tensor_D is a 3x3 matrix (list of lists or array)
    # direction_v is a 3-element vector
    vx, vy, vz = direction_v
    norm_sq = vx*vx + vy*vy + vz*vz
    if norm_sq == 0.0:
        return 0.0
    inv_norm = 1.0 / (norm_sq ** 0.5)
    vx, vy, vz = vx * inv_norm, vy * inv_norm, vz * inv_norm
    Dxx, Dxy, Dxz = tensor_D[0]
    Dyx, Dyy, Dyz = tensor_D[1]
    Dzx, Dzy, Dzz = tensor_D[2]
    return (Dxx * vx*vx + Dyy * vy*vy + Dzz * vz*vz +
            2.0 * Dxy * vx * vy + 2.0 * Dxz * vx * vz + 2.0 * Dyz * vy * vz)
```

## Subtopics

### [Fractional Anisotropy (FA)](12299-fractional-anisotropy-fa.md)
FA is a scalar metric quantifying the degree of anisotropy in a voxel based solely on the diffusion tensor's eigenvalues. It distinguishes directional structures from isotropic environments where Mean Diffusivity is insufficient. Unlike higher-order anisotropy measures, FA is rotationally invariant but ignores higher-order tensor components, making it noise-sensitive for single-tensor data. Choose FA when a single rotationally invariant scalar is needed to quantify anisotropy magnitude, rather than directional diffusion values.

## Background
The Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC) is defined directly from the diffusion tensor $\mathbf{D}$ [S1, unofficial]. Basser et al. [BP96] introduced several rotationally invariant anisotropy metrics, such as fractional anisotropy, which measure the degree of anisotropy in a given voxel [S1, unofficial]. The diffusion tensor can be interpreted as an ellipsoid describing the shape to which water diffuses from a given point in a given time [S1, unofficial].
