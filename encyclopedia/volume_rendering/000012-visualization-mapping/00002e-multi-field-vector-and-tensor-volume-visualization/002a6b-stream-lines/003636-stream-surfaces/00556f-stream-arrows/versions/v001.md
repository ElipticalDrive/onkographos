# Stream Arrows

Also called: Texture Mapping of Arrows

## Overview
Stream arrows are a technique for visualizing integral surfaces by texture mapping arrows onto the surface geometry to convey the local direction of the flow [S1]. This method builds on [Stream Surfaces](7696-stream-surfaces.md) by adding directional cues to the surface representation, solving the problem of ascertaining flow orientation in areas of substantial directional change where streamlines curl around each other. Unlike [Stream-Balls](7700-stream-balls.md) which use discrete glyphs, stream arrows provide a continuous directional field across the surface.

## How it works
**Inputs:** A triangulated stream surface mesh and a vector field.
**Outputs:** A rendered image of the surface with arrow textures.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Stream Surface:** A mesh defined by vertices and connectivity, generated by advancing particles from seed points.
*   **Texture Coordinates:** UV coordinates mapped to the surface vertices.
*   **Arrow Texture:** A 2D image containing an arrow graphic, typically with a transparent background.

**Steps:**
1.  **Surface Generation:** Generate the stream surface using particle propagation. The surface is formed on-the-fly using a greedy approach [S1]. Divergence and convergence of particles in the advancing front are treated using a simple distance criterion that inserts and removes particles to ensure a balanced resolution of the surface [S1].
2.  **Texture Mapping:** Map arrow textures onto the stream surface [S1]. The texture coordinates are assigned to the surface vertices such that the arrow orientation aligns with the local vector field direction at each point.
3.  **Rendering:** Render the textured surface. The arrows are drawn according to the texture mapping, providing a visual indication of the flow direction across the integral surface.

**Parameters:**
*   **Arrow Size:** Not specified in the sources.
*   **Texture Resolution:** Not specified in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Distortion:** The original method does not address stretching and divergence of the surface, which distorts the parameterization and results in very large or small arrows [S1]. This can lead to visual clutter or unreadable arrows in regions of high shear or expansion.

## Subtopics

### Hierarchical Tiling of Texture Space
A refinement of stream arrows using a regular, hierarchical tiling of texture space to adjust for surface stretching and divergence [S1]. It applies a regular, hierarchical tiling of texture space and adjusts the arrow textures based on the tiling to compensate for distortion [S1]. This addresses the pitfall of the original method where surface stretching results in distorted arrow sizes.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
*   **Strengths:** Provides continuous directional cues on integral surfaces, enhancing the understanding of flow direction.
*   **Limitations:** High visual complexity of the resulting surfaces makes stream arrows rarely used in integral surface visualization from CFD data [S1]. The original method suffers from distortion due to surface stretching and divergence [S1].
*   **When to use:** When continuous directional information on an integral surface is required and the surface geometry is relatively uniform.
*   **When not to use:** When the surface exhibits significant stretching or divergence, leading to distorted arrows, or when visual clarity is prioritized over continuous directional cues.

## Background
Stream arrows were introduced by Löffelmann [26] as an advanced visualization of integral surfaces, with the goal of conveying the local direction of the flow [S1]. The same authors subsequently addressed the issue of distortion by proposing a regular, hierarchical tiling of texture space that results in adjusted arrows [25]. Despite these improvements, stream arrows are rarely used in integral surface visualization from CFD data due to the high visual complexity of the resulting surfaces [S1].
