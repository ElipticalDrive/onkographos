# LIC on Stream Surfaces
Also called: Line Integral Convolution on Stream Surfaces

## Overview
LIC on Stream Surfaces is a visualization technique that superimposes a texture generated by the Line Integral Convolution (LIC) algorithm onto stream surfaces to resolve the lack of directional information inherent in standard surface rendering [S1, unofficial]. It differs from standard stream surfaces by providing dense, local flow direction cues across the entire surface area, whereas siblings like [Stream-Balls](7700-stream-balls.md) or [Hairs](12773-hairs.md) rely on discrete geometric primitives or point-based markers that may fail to convey continuous flow coherence in regions of substantial directional change.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
1. A steady-state 3D vector field $\mathbf{V}(x, y, z)$.
2. A set of seed points defining the boundary of the stream surface.
3. A scalar noise texture $N(u, v)$, typically high-frequency white noise.

**Outputs:**
A rendered image of the stream surface with LIC texture applied, revealing flow direction.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Stream Surface Mesh:** A triangle mesh representing the iso-contour surface of the integrated flow field. Vertices are stored as 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$.
*   **Noise Texture:** A 2D array of scalar values representing the noise field.

**Steps:**
1.  **Construct Stream Surface:** Integrate the vector field $\mathbf{V}$ from seed points to generate the stream surface geometry. This involves solving $\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{V}(\mathbf{x})$ for adjacent streamlines and connecting them to form a surface [S1, unofficial].
2.  **Project to Screen:** Render the stream surface mesh to a screen-space buffer. For each pixel $(x_s, y_s)$ covered by the surface, determine the corresponding 3D position $\mathbf{p}$ on the surface and the surface normal $\mathbf{n}$.
3.  **Define Integration Path:** For each pixel, define a streamline path in the vector field that lies on or near the surface. The path direction is determined by the projection of the vector field onto the surface tangent plane.
4.  **Compute LIC Value:** For each pixel, integrate the noise value along the streamline path. The LIC value $L(x_s, y_s)$ is computed as:
    $$ L(x_s, y_s) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} N(u_i, v_i) $$
    where $K$ is the number of integration steps, and $(u_i, v_i)$ are the 2D coordinates in the noise texture corresponding to the $i$-th point on the streamline. The noise texture coordinates are mapped from the 3D streamline positions, often using a parameterization of the surface or a fixed 2D domain if the surface is planar or can be unfolded [S1, unofficial].
5.  **Composite:** Combine the LIC value with the surface color or shading. The texture reveals the direction of flow along the surface.

**Parameters:**
*   $K$: Number of integration steps. Typical values depend on the resolution and desired texture density. Not given in the sources.
*   Noise Texture Size: Resolution of the noise field. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Occlusion:** Stream surfaces can occlude themselves, requiring careful sorting or semi-transparent rendering, which is computationally expensive [S1, unofficial].
*   **Direction Ambiguity:** Standard LIC does not inherently encode flow direction (positive/negative). Additional techniques or asymmetric noise may be required to distinguish forward from backward flow [S1, unofficial].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** The flow must enter or exit the volume boundaries for the stream surface technique to work properly. Artificial boundaries may need to be placed to rectify this [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Optimized LIC](7862-optimized-lic.md)
Accelerates the LIC computation by reusing convolution results for adjacent pixels along the same streamline, reducing redundant integrations.

### [Dye-Advection LIC](7865-dye-advection-lic.md)
Extends LIC to 3D by simulating the advection of colored dye, allowing dynamic injection of flow features and directional correspondence.

### [Line Integral Convolution (LIC)](7940-line-integral-convolution-lic.md)
The foundational texture-based method that maps noise onto vector fields by integrating along streamlines.

### [Fabric-like Visualization of Tensor Field Data](10568-fabric-like-visualization-of-tensor-field-data.md)
A technique for tensor fields using reaction-diffusion noise, distinct from LIC's white noise approach.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Depends on the number of pixels and the length of the streamline integration. Optimized variants reduce this by reusing computations.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Requires storage for the stream surface mesh and the noise texture.
*   **Strengths:** Provides dense, continuous flow direction information on complex surfaces.
*   **Limitations:** Computationally intensive for high-resolution surfaces; occlusion handling is difficult; does not inherently encode speed or bidirectional flow without modifications.
*   **When to Use:** When visualizing steady-state flows on complex surfaces where directional coherence is critical.
*   **When Not to Use:** When real-time performance is required for large datasets, or when bidirectional flow information is essential without additional processing.

## Background
The technique was proposed by Interrante and others as a solution to the lack of direction on stream surfaces, building on the work of Van Wijk who constructed stream surfaces from iso-contours of integrated flow functions [S1, unofficial]. It leverages the Line Integral Convolution algorithm, originally developed by Cabral and Leedom, to texture the surfaces with flow-aligned patterns.
