# LIC on Stream Surfaces
Also called: Line Integral Convolution on Stream Surfaces

## Overview
LIC on Stream Surfaces is a visualization technique that superimposes a texture or pattern generated by the Line Integral Convolution (LIC) algorithm onto stream surfaces to solve the lack of directional information inherent in standard surface rendering [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Optimized LIC](7862-optimized-lic.md)
Accelerates LIC by reusing convolution results for adjacent pixels along the same streamline, differing from standard LIC by computing long field line segments once and incrementally updating the convolution integral rather than recomputing independently. Choose it when rendering performance is critical.

### [Dye-Advection LIC](7865-dye-advection-lic.md)
Extends LIC to 3D by simulating dye advection, differing from standard LIC by allowing dynamic injection of colored dye at arbitrary locations to visualize local flow features and wavefronts. Choose it when directional correspondence and local feature tracking are required.

### [Line Integral Convolution (LIC)](7940-line-integral-convolution-lic.md)
Maps noise onto vector fields by integrating along streamlines, differing from streamline-based methods by densely displaying direction information across the entire field without requiring user-defined seed points. Choose it for dense, global flow direction visualization.

### [Fabric-like Visualization of Tensor Field Data](10568-fabric-like-visualization-of-tensor-field-data.md)
Visualizes symmetric second-order tensor fields on surfaces using eigendirections as thread-like structures, differing from LIC by using smooth reaction-diffusion noise to allow compositing two distinct eigenvector textures. Choose it for tensor field visualization on complex geometries without explicit surface parameterization.

## Background
The technique was proposed by Interrante and others as a solution to the lack of direction on stream surfaces, building on the work of Van Wijk who constructed stream surfaces from iso-contours of integrated flow functions [S1, unofficial].
