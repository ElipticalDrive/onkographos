# Stream Lines

Also called: Integral Curve, Stream Lines and Ribbons Rendering

## Overview
Stream lines are a local point tracking algorithm that visualizes vector fields by computing curves tangent to the field at every point, approximating the path of an infinitesimal massless particle [S1, S3, S6, unofficial]. Unlike [Glyph-based Vector/Tensor Visualization](10535-glyph-based-vector-tensor-visualization.md), which displays discrete vectors at fixed grid locations, stream lines reveal continuous flow topology and connectivity [S9, unofficial]. They differ from [Stream Surfaces](7696-stream-surfaces.md) by representing individual trajectories rather than surfaces formed by families of lines [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   Vector field data $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})$ defined on a 3D grid or texture [S5, S8, unofficial].
*   Seed points $\mathbf{x}_0$ defining the starting locations of the lines [S1, S6, unofficial].
*   Integration parameters: step size $\Delta t$, maximum integration steps or length, and termination thresholds [S1, S5, S6, unofficial].

**Outputs:**
*   A set of polylines (sequences of vertices) representing the trajectories [S6, S8, unofficial].
*   Optional visual attributes: color mapped to velocity magnitude [S2, S7, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Vector Field:** Stored as a 3D texture or array. For GPU implementations, a `cudaArray` with `cudaCreateChannelDesc<float4>()` is bound to a 3D texture object with linear filtering and clamp addressing [S8, unofficial]. The fourth component often stores magnitude or is unused [S8, unofficial].
*   **Seeds:** An array of 3D coordinates (float3) [S6, S8, unofficial].
*   **Output Buffers:**
    *   `outVertices`: Array of `float3` positions. Each streamline occupies `maxSteps / geometrySampling + 1` vertices [S6, unofficial].
    *   `outVertexColors`: Array of `float3` colors, aligned with vertices [S6, unofficial].
    *   `outComputedSteps`: Array of `uint` tracking the number of valid steps per seed [S6, unofficial].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Initialization:** For each seed point $i$, initialize the current position $\mathbf{p}$ in double precision for accuracy, and write the initial position to the output buffer [S6, unofficial]. Set the integration step counter $k=0$ [S6, unofficial].
2.  **Integration Loop:** Iterate while $k < \text{maxSteps}$:
    a.  **Boundary Check:** If $\mathbf{p}$ is outside the volume bounds (e.g., $x < 0$ or $x > \text{width}$), terminate the streamline for this seed [S6, S8, unofficial].
    b.  **Vector Lookup:** Sample the vector field $\mathbf{v}$ at $\mathbf{p}$ using trilinear interpolation [S5, S8, unofficial].
    c.  **Termination Check:** If $|\mathbf{v}| < \text{threshold}$ (e.g., minimum speed), terminate [S1, S5, unofficial].
    d.  **Position Update:** Compute the new position $\mathbf{p}_{new}$ using an integrator (Euler or RK4) [S6, S8, unofficial].
    e.  **Geometry Sampling:** If the current integration step is a multiple of `geometrySampling`, write $\mathbf{p}_{new}$ to `outVertices` and the magnitude-based color to `outVertexColors` [S6, S8, unofficial]. This reduces memory usage by emitting fewer vertices than integration steps [S6, unofficial].
    f.  **Update:** Set $\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{p}_{new}$, increment $k$ [S6, unofficial].
3.  **Finalization:** Store the total computed steps for each seed in `outComputedSteps` [S6, unofficial].

**Formulas:**

The fundamental ordinary differential equation for a streamline in a steady field is [S1, S3, unofficial]:
$$ \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r}(t)) $$
With initial condition $\mathbf{r}(t_0) = \mathbf{x}_0$ [S3, unofficial].

**Euler Integration** (First-order, used for time-averaged fields like PCA data) [S1, unofficial]:
$$ \mathbf{r}(t + \Delta t) = \mathbf{r}(t) + \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r}(t)) $$
The step $\Delta t$ is typically chosen to match the minimum voxel dimension (e.g., 0.5–1 mm) [S1, unofficial].

**Runge-Kutta 4th Order (RK4)** (Higher accuracy, used in CUDA implementations) [S8, unofficial]:
$$ \mathbf{r}_{new} = \mathbf{r} + \frac{\Delta t}{6} (k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4) $$
Where:
$$ k_1 = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r}) $$
$$ k_2 = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r} + \frac{\Delta t}{2} k_1) $$
$$ k_3 = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r} + \frac{\Delta t}{2} k_2) $$
$$ k_4 = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r} + \Delta t k_3) $$
RK4 requires four texture lookups per step [S8, unofficial].

**Trilinear Interpolation** (Piecewise) [S5, unofficial]:
For a point $\mathbf{x}=(x,y,z)$ in a cell defined by corners $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ to $(x_i+dx, y_i+dy, z_i+dz)$, the value is:
$$ \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j=1}^{8} b_j \mathbf{v}_j $$
The weights $b_j$ are calculated as:
$$ b_1 = \frac{(x_i+dx-x)(y_i+dy-y)(z_i+dz-z)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_2 = \frac{(x-x_i)(y_i+dy-y)(z_i+dz-z)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_3 = \frac{(x-x_i)(y-y_i)(z_i+dz-z)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_4 = \frac{(x_i+dx-x)(y-y_i)(z_i+dz-z)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_5 = \frac{(x_i+dx-x)(y_i+dy-y)(z-z_i)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_6 = \frac{(x-x_i)(y_i+dy-y)(z-z_i)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_7 = \frac{(x-x_i)(y-y_i)(z-z_i)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$
$$ b_8 = \frac{(x_i+dx-x)(y-y_i)(z-z_i)}{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz} $$

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def compute_streamlines(seeds, vector_field, max_steps, dt, geometry_sampling, volume_bounds):
    """
    seeds: list of (x, y, z) tuples
    vector_field: function v(x, y, z) returning (vx, vy, vz)
    volume_bounds: (min_x, min_y, min_z, max_x, max_y, max_z)
    """
    vertices = []
    colors = []
    steps_count = []
    
    min_x, min_y, min_z, max_x, max_y, max_z = volume_bounds
    
    for seed in seeds:
        # Use double precision for integration accuracy
        x, y, z = float(seed[0]), float(seed[1]), float(seed[2])
        
        # Store initial vertex
        vertices.append((x, y, z))
        colors.append(magnitude_to_color(vector_field(x, y, z)))
        
        current_steps = 1
        geometry_step = geometry_sampling
        
        for step in range(1, max_steps):
            # Boundary check
            if x < min_x or x > max_x or \
               y < min_y or y > max_y or \
               z < min_z or z > max_z:
                break
            
            # Get vector
            vx, vy, vz = vector_field(x, y, z)
            mag = (vx**2 + vy**2 + vz**2)**0.5
            
            # Termination check (low speed)
            if mag < 1e-6: # Placeholder threshold
                break
            
            # Euler Integration
            x_new = x + dt * vx
            y_new = y + dt * vy
            z_new = z + dt * vz
            
            x, y, z = x_new, y_new, z_new
            
            # Geometry sampling
            geometry_step -= 1
            if geometry_step == 0:
                vertices.append((x, y, z))
                colors.append(magnitude_to_color(vector_field(x, y, z)))
                current_steps += 1
                geometry_step = geometry_sampling
        
        steps_count.append(current_steps)
        
    return vertices, colors, steps_count

def magnitude_to_color(mag):
    # Placeholder for transfer function lookup
    return (mag, 0, 0) 
```

**Parameters:**
*   `dt` (Step size): Typically equal to the minimum voxel dimension (0.5–1 mm) [S1, unofficial].
*   `maxSteps`: Maximum number of integration steps. In ParaView, `MaximumStreamlineLength` is often set to $6\pi$ [S2, unofficial].
*   `geometrySampling`: Integer specifying integration steps per output vertex. Default 1 (every step); higher values save GPU memory [S6, unofficial].
*   `SeedType`: "Point Cloud" (spherical distribution, radius ~2.2, count ~300) or "Line Source" (high resolution line) [S2, unofficial].
*   `TubeRadius`: If rendered as tubes, typical value 0.03 [S2, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Precision:** Integration should be performed in double precision to prevent drift, but results can be stored in single precision to save memory [S6, unofficial].
*   **Self-Intersection:** In steady fields, streamlines do not cross except at cycles. Termination may occur if a streamline re-enters a previously visited cell [S5, unofficial].
*   **Distorted Coordinates:** If the volume is distorted (e.g., horseshoe shape), the vector field must be transformed from computation space (C-space) to physical space (P-space) using the Jacobian of the coordinate transformation to ensure correct traces [S4, unofficial].
*   **Time-Varying Fields:** Streamlines are defined for steady fields. For time-varying fields, these are technically pathlines, which may self-intersect and are not tangential to the instantaneous field [S3, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Adaptive Stream Line Seeding](5604-adaptive-stream-line-seeding.md)
Adaptive Stream Line Seeding refines the density of stream lines in regions of high flow divergence (turbulence, vortices) while keeping them sparse in laminar areas. It iteratively subdivides neighboring stream lines when the distance between them exceeds a threshold, creating denser surface representations where complexity is highest.

### [Directional Textured Tracelines](6965-directional-textured-tracelines.md)
This method enhances standard 1D stream lines by overlaying an animated 1D texture (e.g., zebra pattern) that moves along the line in the direction of flow. This resolves the ambiguity of direction inherent in static lines.

### [Stream Surfaces](7696-stream-surfaces.md)
Stream surfaces stitch adjacent stream lines together to form a continuous geometric surface. This provides topological separation of the flow volume and helps ascertain overall flow direction in regions where lines curl around each other.

### [Higher Order Tensor (HOT) Lines](14000-higher-order-tensor-hot-lines.md)
HOT Lines visualize vector and tensor fields of arbitrary order by integrating trajectories that follow local extrema of a function on the unit sphere. This allows for multiple distinct trajectories per voxel, solving issues in fiber crossings where second-order tensors fail.

### Flow Geometry Bricks Rendering
A rendering approach for flow visualization using geometry bricks that hold range information of the visible region. Streamlines are stored in a single vertex buffer as a line strip, and arrows in an indexed buffer. Opaque geometry is rendered before the semi-transparent volume using depth testing to accurately mix geometry and volume information [S4, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S)$, where $N$ is the number of seeds and $S$ is the number of integration steps per streamline. RK4 is 4x more expensive per step than Euler due to multiple texture lookups [S8, unofficial].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N \cdot S / \text{geometrySampling})$ for output buffers. GPU implementations use `geometrySampling` to trade precision for memory [S6, unofficial].
*   **Performance:** Real-time visualization is achievable on GPUs using CUDA and texture lookups for interpolation [S8, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Intuitive representation of flow direction and connectivity; effective for identifying vortices and stagnation points [S6, S9, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Sensitive to seed placement; may clutter the visualization if too dense; does not show flow magnitude as clearly as color-mapped glyphs without additional encoding [S6, S7, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When understanding the global topology and connectivity of a steady vector field is required [S9, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** For time-varying fields (use pathlines); for fields with extremely high complexity where line density becomes unreadable (use stream surfaces or adaptive seeding) [S3, S1, unofficial].

## Background
Stream lines were introduced as a fundamental technique for visualizing vector fields, originating from the mathematical definition of integral curves [S3, unofficial]. They are widely used in fluid dynamics (blood flow, aerodynamics) and diffusion tensor imaging (neural connectivity) [S1, unofficial]. Modern implementations leverage GPU acceleration (CUDA) for real-time rendering of large datasets [S6, S8, unofficial].
