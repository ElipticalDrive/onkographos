# Integral Surface

Also called: Path Surface, Rake, Stream Surface

## Overview
An integral surface generalizes the concept of a stream line or path line to a two-dimensional manifold in space-time, generated by sweeping a seeding curve through a vector field. It solves the problem of visualizing coherent structures in time-varying flows (as a path surface) or steady flows (as a stream surface) by connecting a continuous set of seed points rather than discrete locations. It is distinct from single-line tracing by maintaining topological connectivity across the seeding curve, and from streak surfaces by integrating from the initial time $t_0$ rather than tracking particles emitted continuously up to time $t$.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A univariate seeding curve $c: I \subseteq \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n$, where $I$ is the parameter domain (typically $[0,1]$).
- Input: A velocity field $v(x, t)$ defined on $\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}$.
- Output: A parametric surface $S(s, t)$ representing the integral surface.

**Data Structures:**
The surface is represented as a parametric map $S(s, t)$ where $s$ indexes the seeding curve and $t$ indexes time. No explicit mesh is required for the definition; implementation typically discretizes $s$ and $t$ into a grid of points.

**Steps:**
1. Define the seeding curve $c(s)$ for $s \in I$.
2. For each parameter pair $(s, t)$ where $t \ge t_0$:
   a. Initialize the position $x_0 = c(s)$.
   b. Integrate the ordinary differential equation (ODE) describing the particle trajectory starting from $x_0$ at time $t_0$ up to time $t$.
   c. The position at time $t$ is given by the integral formula below.

**Formulas:**
For a time-varying velocity field $v(x, \tau)$, the integral surface $S(s, t)$ is defined as [S1, unofficial]:

$$
S(s, t) = c(s) + \int_{t_0}^{t} v(\tau, S(s, \tau)) \, d\tau
$$

Where:
- $S(s, t)$ is the position on the surface at seeding parameter $s$ and time $t$.
- $c(s)$ is the initial position on the seeding curve (rake).
- $v(\tau, x)$ is the velocity vector at time $\tau$ and position $x$.
- $t_0$ is the initial time.

In the specific case of a **steady flow** (where $v$ does not depend on time), this reduces to a stream surface, where the integration is along the spatial streamlines originating from $c(s)$.

**Parameters:**
- $t_0$: Initial time. Typical value: $0$.
- $t$: Final time. Depends on the simulation duration.
- $s$: Seeding parameter. Range $[0, 1]$ or normalized length of the curve.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Self-Intersection:** As the surface evolves, lines on the surface for fixed $t$ (timelines) may no longer be tangential to the flow field and may self-intersect, leading to visual artifacts or topological ambiguities [S1, unofficial].
- **Higher-Dimensional Generalization:** If the seeding geometry is higher-dimensional (e.g., a curve in 3D space swept over time), the definition generalizes to path volumes and time surfaces [S1, unofficial].
- **Streaklines vs. Pathlines:** Do not confuse with streak surfaces. Streak surfaces track particles emitted continuously from $c(s)$ up to time $t$, whereas integral surfaces (path surfaces) track particles emitted at a single initial time $t_0$ from all points on $c(s)$ [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_integral_surface(c, v, t0, t_end, s_samples, t_samples):
    """
    Computes the integral surface (path surface) for a time-varying flow.
    
    Args:
        c: Function mapping s -> initial position x0.
        v: Function mapping (x, tau) -> velocity vector.
        t0: Initial time.
        t_end: Final time.
        s_samples: List of s values in the domain I.
        t_samples: List of t values from t0 to t_end.
        
    Returns:
        Surface points: List of (s, t, x, y, z) tuples.
    """
    surface_points = []
    
    for s in s_samples:
        # 1. Get initial position from seeding curve
        x0 = c(s)
        
        # 2. Integrate trajectory for this seed point
        # Using a simple Euler method for demonstration; RK4 is preferred for accuracy
        x = x0
        t = t0
        dt = (t_end - t0) / (len(t_samples) - 1)
        
        for t in t_samples:
            # Add current point to surface
            surface_points.append((s, t, x[0], x[1], x[2]))
            
            # Compute velocity at current position and time
            vel = v(x, t)
            
            # Update position (Euler step)
            x = x + vel * dt
            
    return surface_points
```

## Subtopics

### [Magnetosphere Field Line Tracing](12012-magnetosphere-field-line-tracing.md)
This method traces a single magnetospheric magnetic field line starting from an ionospheric footpoint using a fixed-step Euler integration along the normalized magnetic field vector. It solves the problem of visualizing the topology of the Earth's magnetic field within the magnetosphere by connecting ionospheric regions to the magnetopause boundary. It is distinct from general stream line tracing by its specific reliance on an analytical dipole field model and its geometric termination conditions (ionosphere and magnetopause surfaces) rather than a uniform domain. It builds on [Integral Surface](11268-integral-surface.md) by providing the specific vector field evaluation and boundary logic for magnetospheric contexts.

### [Particle Traces on 2D Surfaces](12772-particle-traces-on-2d-surfaces.md)
Particle traces on 2D surfaces represent the magnitude and direction of a vector field on arbitrary 2D surfaces by drawing long, evenly spaced particle trace lines composed of contrasting color segments. Unlike random seed placement which does not guarantee coverage, this method uses hierarchical seeding to ensure a nearly constant density of lines across the surface, resolving directional ambiguity in still images via sawtooth color maps. It builds on [Integral Surface](11268-integral-surface.md) by focusing specifically on arbitrary 2D surfaces rather than just contour surfaces.

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** $O(N_s \cdot N_t \cdot N_{steps})$, where $N_s$ is the number of seeding points, $N_t$ is the number of time steps, and $N_{steps}$ is the number of integration steps per trajectory.
- **Memory Complexity:** $O(N_s \cdot N_t)$ to store the surface points.
- **Strengths:** Provides a continuous, topologically connected visualization of flow structures; effective for identifying coherent structures in time-varying data.
- **Limitations:** Computationally expensive for large domains; prone to self-intersection in complex flows; requires careful handling of seeding curve discretization.

## Background
The concept of integral surfaces, also known as path surfaces or rakes, originates from the mathematical theory of dynamical systems and fluid dynamics, specifically in the context of Lagrangian coherent structures. The formal definition provided in [S1, unofficial] generalizes the classical stream line and path line concepts to higher-dimensional seeding geometries. It is widely used in scientific visualization to analyze time-varying vector fields, such as weather patterns or fluid dynamics simulations.
