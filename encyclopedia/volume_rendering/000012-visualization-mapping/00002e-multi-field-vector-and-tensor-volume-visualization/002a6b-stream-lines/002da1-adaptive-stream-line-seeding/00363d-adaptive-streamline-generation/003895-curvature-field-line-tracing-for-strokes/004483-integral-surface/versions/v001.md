# Integral Surface

Also called: Path Surface, Rake, Stream Surface

## Overview
An integral surface (also known as a path surface or rake) is a geometric construct used to visualize time-varying vector fields by sweeping a seeding curve along the flow. It generalizes the concept of a stream line (steady flow) to time-dependent scenarios, where the surface represents the locus of particles released continuously from a one-dimensional seeding curve $c(s)$. Unlike streaklines, which track particles that have *ever* passed through a point, integral surfaces track particles released at specific times from the entire curve, forming a "path volume" in higher-dimensional generalizations. It is distinct from [Stream Lines](52-stream-lines.md) in its handling of temporal evolution and from [Streak Surfaces](53-streak-surface.md) in its definition of particle release history.

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A time-varying velocity field $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, t)$, a univariate seeding curve $c: I \subseteq \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n$, and a time interval $[t_0, t]$.
- Output: A parametric surface $S(s, t)$ defined over the seed parameter $s$ and time $t$.

**Data Structures:**
The surface is represented as a parametric map. For implementation, this is typically discretized into a grid of points $(s_i, t_j)$, where $s_i$ samples the curve $c$ and $t_j$ samples the integration time.

**Method:**
The position of the surface at seed parameter $s$ and time $t$ is computed by integrating the velocity field along the trajectory of a particle released from $c(s)$ at time $t_0$.

1.  **Initialize:** Define the seeding curve $c(s)$.
2.  **Integrate:** For each seed point $s$ and target time $t$, compute the trajectory $\mathbf{x}(\tau)$ for $\tau \in [t_0, t]$ by solving the ordinary differential equation:
    $$ \frac{d\mathbf{x}}{d\tau} = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}(\tau), \tau) $$
    with initial condition $\mathbf{x}(t_0) = c(s)$.
3.  **Compute Position:** The surface point $S(s, t)$ is the final position $\mathbf{x}(t)$.

**Formula:**
The integral surface is defined as [S1, unofficial]:
$$ S(s, t) = c(s) + \int_{t_0}^{t} \mathbf{v}(\tau, S(s, \tau)) \, d\tau $$
Where:
- $S(s, t)$ is the position on the surface.
- $c(s)$ is the position on the seeding curve (rake).
- $\mathbf{v}(\tau, \mathbf{x})$ is the velocity field at time $\tau$ and position $\mathbf{x}$.
- The integral represents the displacement of the particle from its release point.

**Parameters:**
- $t_0$: Start time of integration.
- $t$: End time of integration.
- $I$: Domain of the seeding curve parameter $s$.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Self-Intersection:** As noted in [S1, unofficial], lines on an integral surface for fixed $t$ are timelines. In complex flows, these lines may self-intersect or become non-tangential to the flow field if the definition is generalized improperly.
- **Higher Dimensions:** If the seeding geometry is higher-dimensional (e.g., a surface instead of a curve), the result generalizes to a path volume or time surface.
- **Steady Flow:** In steady flow ($\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})$), the integral surface reduces to a stream surface, where the surface is everywhere tangent to the velocity field.

**Pseudocode:**
```python
def compute_integral_surface(velocity_field, seeding_curve, t0, t, num_seeds, num_times):
    """
    Computes an integral surface by integrating trajectories from a seeding curve.
    
    Args:
        velocity_field: Function v(x, t) returning velocity vector.
        seeding_curve: Function c(s) returning position for seed parameter s.
        t0: Start time.
        t: End time.
        num_seeds: Number of samples along the seeding curve.
        num_times: Number of time steps for integration.
        
    Returns:
        surface_points: Array of shape (num_seeds, num_times, 3) containing positions.
    """
    # Discretize the seeding curve parameter s
    s_values = linspace(0, 1, num_seeds)
    # Discretize the time axis
    t_values = linspace(t0, t, num_times)
    
    surface_points = zeros((num_seeds, num_times, 3))
    
    for i, s in enumerate(s_values):
        # Initial position is on the seeding curve
        current_pos = seeding_curve(s)
        current_time = t0
        
        # Store initial position
        surface_points[i, 0, :] = current_pos
        
        # Integrate forward in time
        for j in range(1, num_times):
            dt = t_values[j] - t_values[j-1]
            
            # Use a numerical integrator (e.g., RK4) to advance the particle
            # Note: The integral form in the definition implies solving the ODE
            # dx/dt = v(x, t)
            current_pos = integrate_step(velocity_field, current_pos, current_time, dt)
            
            current_time = t_values[j]
            surface_points[i, j, :] = current_pos
            
    return surface_points

def integrate_step(velocity_field, x, t, dt):
    """
    Simple Euler step for integration. For production, use RK4.
    """
    v = velocity_field(x, t)
    return x + v * dt
```

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
- **Complexity:** Time complexity is $O(N_s \cdot N_t \cdot N_{steps})$, where $N_s$ is the number of seed points, $N_t$ is the number of time steps, and $N_{steps}$ is the number of sub-steps per integration step. Memory complexity is $O(N_s \cdot N_t)$.
- **Performance:** Performance depends heavily on the smoothness of the velocity field and the required accuracy of the integrator. Adaptive step sizes can reduce $N_{steps}$ for smooth regions.
- **Strengths:** Provides a continuous visualization of flow history from a line source. Useful for identifying coherent structures originating from specific boundaries.
- **Limitations:** Can become visually cluttered in complex flows. Self-intersections may occur, making interpretation difficult. Not suitable for steady flow where stream surfaces are more appropriate and simpler.

## Background
The concept of integral surfaces, also referred to as path surfaces or rakes, is discussed in the context of multi-field visualization and time-varying flow fields. It is defined in [S1, unofficial] as a generalization of stream lines to time-dependent scenarios. The term "rake" refers to the seeding curve $c(s)$ from which particles are released. The definition distinguishes integral surfaces from streak surfaces, which track particles that have passed through a point at any time in the past.
