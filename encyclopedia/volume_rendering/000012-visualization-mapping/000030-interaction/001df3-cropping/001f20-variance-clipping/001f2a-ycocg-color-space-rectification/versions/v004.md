# YCoCg Color Space Rectification

Also called: Color Space Selection for History Rectification, YCrCb Rectification

## Overview
YCoCg Color Space Rectification is a history rectification method used in temporal anti-aliasing (TAA) for volumetric rendering that transforms reprojected historical color data into a luminance-chrominance color space (specifically YCoCg or YCrCb) before applying bounding-box clipping [S1, unofficial]. It solves the problem of temporal color bleeding artifacts caused by high correlation between RGB channels, which leads to poorly aligned bounding boxes and stale color information persisting across frames [S1, unofficial]. It sets itself apart from its parent method, [Variance Clipping](1970-variance-clipping.md), by operating on a fixed geometric bounding box oriented along the luminance axis rather than a statistical distribution, and from its sibling [AABB Clamping](2019-aabb-clamping.md) by explicitly decoupling luminance and chrominance to reduce chroma-related ghosting [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: Reprojected historical color sample $h_{reprojected}$ (RGB vector), current frame neighborhood samples (RGB vectors).
- Output: Rectified historical color sample $h_{clipped}$ (RGB vector).

**Data Structures:**
- Color vectors are 3-element arrays/tuples.
- YCoCg space: $[Y, Co, Cg]^T$.
- YCrCb space: $[Y, Cr, Cb]^T$.

**Steps:**
1. **Transform to Luminance-Chrominance Space:** Convert the reprojected historical color $h_{reprojected}$ and the current frame neighborhood samples from RGB to the chosen color space (YCoCg or YCrCb).
2. **Define Bounding Box:** Determine the minimum and maximum values for each component (Y, Co, Cg or Y, Cr, Cb) across the current frame's neighborhood samples. This creates an Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) in the transformed space.
3. **Clamp History:** Clamp each component of the transformed historical sample $h_{reprojected}$ to the corresponding min/max bounds of the AABB.
4. **Transform Back to RGB:** Convert the clamped historical sample back to RGB space to produce $h_{clipped}$.

**Formulas:**

*RGB to YCoCg Transform:*
$$
\begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Co \\ Cg \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{4} & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{4} \\ \frac{1}{2} & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \\ -\frac{1}{4} & \frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{4} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix}
$$

*Inverse YCoCg to RGB Transform:*
$$
\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Co \\ Cg \end{bmatrix}
$$

*RGB to YCrCb Transform:*
$$
\begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Cr \\ Cb \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.299 & 0.587 & 0.114 \\ -0.5 & -0.4187 & 0.0813 \\ -0.1687 & -0.3313 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix}
$$

*Inverse YCrCb to RGB Transform:*
$$
\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1.402 & 0 \\ 1 & -0.714 & -0.344 \\ 1 & 0 & 1.772 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Cr \\ Cb \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Parameters:**
- Color Space Selection: YCoCg or YCrCb. YCoCg is preferred for better decorrelation of luminance and chrominance [S1, unofficial].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- **Color-Related Artifacts in RGB:** Performing history rectification directly in RGB space causes the AABB to be poorly aligned with the actual distribution of color samples because RGB channels are highly correlated, leading to ghosting artifacts [S1, unofficial].
- **Temporal Color Bleeding:** In RGB space, abrupt color changes (e.g., a light turning from red to yellow) cause stale color information to bleed into subsequent frames, taking many frames to converge [S1, unofficial].
- **Luminance Artifacts:** While YCoCg and YCrCb reduce chrominance bleeding, they can increase luminance artifacts. For example, when color shifts from green to blue, stale luminance information in the green channel may bleed into the next frame, causing excess luminance around the light source [S1, unofficial].

**Pseudocode:**

```python
def rectify_history_ycocg(h_reprojected_rgb, neighborhood_samples_rgb):
    """
    Rectifies historical color using YCoCg color space rectification.
    
    Args:
        h_reprojected_rgb: Tuple (R, G, B) of the reprojected historical sample.
        neighborhood_samples_rgb: List of tuples [(R, G, B), ...] of current frame neighborhood samples.
        
    Returns:
        Tuple (R, G, B) of the rectified historical sample.
    """
    
    # Step 1: Transform historical sample to YCoCg
    h_y, h_co, h_cg = rgb_to_ycocg(h_reprojected_rgb)
    
    # Step 2: Transform neighborhood samples to YCoCg and find bounds
    # Initialize bounds with extreme values
    y_min, y_max = float('inf'), float('-inf')
    co_min, co_max = float('inf'), float('-inf')
    cg_min, cg_max = float('inf'), float('-inf')
    
    for sample_rgb in neighborhood_samples_rgb:
        y, co, cg = rgb_to_ycocg(sample_rgb)
        
        # Update bounds
        if y < y_min: y_min = y
        if y > y_max: y_max = y
        if co < co_min: co_min = co
        if co > co_max: co_max = co
        if cg < cg_min: cg_min = cg
        if cg > cg_max: cg_max = cg
        
    # Step 3: Clamp historical sample components to bounds
    y_clamped = max(y_min, min(y_max, h_y))
    co_clamped = max(co_min, min(co_max, h_co))
    cg_clamped = max(cg_min, min(cg_max, h_cg))
    
    # Step 4: Transform back to RGB
    h_clipped_rgb = ycocg_to_rgb(y_clamped, co_clamped, cg_clamped)
    
    return h_clipped_rgb

def rgb_to_ycocg(rgb):
    r, g, b = rgb
    y = 0.25 * r + 0.5 * g + 0.25 * b
    co = 0.5 * r + 0.0 * g - 0.5 * b
    cg = -0.25 * r + 0.5 * g - 0.25 * b
    return (y, co, cg)

def ycocg_to_rgb(ycocg):
    y, co, cg = ycocg
    r = 1.0 * y + 1.0 * co - 1.0 * cg
    g = 1.0 * y + 0.0 * co + 1.0 * cg
    b = 1.0 * y - 1.0 * co - 1.0 * cg
    return (r, g, b)
```

## Subtopics

### [AABB Clamping](2019-aabb-clamping.md)
AABB Clamping is a history rectification method that individually limits each channel of the reprojected historical color to the minimum and maximum values of the current frame's neighborhood samples. It builds on [YCoCg Color Space Rectification](1971-ycocg-color-space-rectification.md), which transforms colors into a luminance-chrominance space before applying rectification [G]. What sets it apart from its siblings and nearest alternatives is its use of a simple Axis-Aligned Bounding Box (AABB) approximation constructed from per-channel minimum and maximum values, whereas variants like Variance Clipping use statistical distributions to define bounds.

### Color Space Conversion for TAA
Color Space Conversion for TAA is a variant that involves transforming volumetric samples into a specific color space (RGB, YCoCg, or YCrCb) before blending, then transforming back to RGB for storage [S1, unofficial]. It is evaluated as a general approach to handling color space selection in TAA for volumetric rendering, with YCoCg and YCrCb shown to reduce chrominance artifacts compared to RGB [S1, unofficial].

## Operating characteristics
- **Time Complexity:** O(N) where N is the number of neighborhood samples, due to the linear transformation and min/max operations.
- **Memory Complexity:** O(1) additional memory per sample for storing transformed values and bounds.
- **Measured Performance:** In tests involving abrupt color changes (e.g., spot light changing from red to yellow), YCoCg and YCrCb significantly reduced temporal color bleeding artifacts compared to RGB, as measured by SSIM scores over time [S1, unofficial]. However, these color spaces introduced increased luminance artifacts during specific color transitions (e.g., green to blue) [S1, unofficial].
- **Strengths:** Reduces color-related ghosting and chrominance bleeding by decoupling luminance and chrominance. YCoCg provides better decorrelation than YCrCb [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Can introduce luminance artifacts when chrominance changes rapidly. Requires additional transform operations.
- **When to Use:** When temporal color bleeding is a prominent issue, especially with high-contrast color changes.
- **When Not to Use:** When luminance stability is critical during rapid color shifts, or when computational overhead of color space transforms is prohibitive.

## Background
YCoCg Color Space Rectification originated in the context of temporal anti-aliasing for real-time froxel-based volumetric rendering. It was explored as an improvement over RGB-space history rectification to mitigate color-related artifacts. The method leverages the properties of luminance-chrominance color spaces, specifically YCoCg and YCrCb, to align the history rectification bounding box with the direction of luma, which typically has high local contrast [S1, unofficial]. The approach was evaluated in a study on advances in volumetric temporal anti-aliasing, comparing RGB, YCoCg, and YCrCb color spaces in terms of artifact reduction and convergence speed [S1, unofficial].
