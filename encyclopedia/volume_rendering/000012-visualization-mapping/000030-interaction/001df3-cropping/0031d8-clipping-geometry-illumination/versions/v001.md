# Clipping Geometry Illumination

Also called: Clipping Surface Shading

## Overview
Clipping geometry illumination is a shading technique used during volume rendering to provide realistic visual cues at the boundaries of clipping geometry. While standard volume rendering relies on the volume's internal gradient for shading, this method substitutes the surface normal of the clipping geometry at the intersection point to determine lighting. This distinguishes it from sibling methods like [Clipping plane for volume suppression](887-clipping-plane-for-volume-suppression.md) or [Variance Clipping](1970-variance-clipping.md), which focus primarily on opacity/transparency transitions or statistical thresholds, by explicitly addressing the visual discontinuity and lack of surface definition that occurs when a volume is cut.

## How it works
**Inputs:**
1.  Ray origin $\mathbf{o}$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$.
2.  Volume dataset $V$ with associated gradient field $\nabla V$.
3.  Clipping geometry definition (e.g., a set of planes or a mesh) with defined surface normals $\mathbf{n}_{clip}$.
4.  Lighting parameters (light direction $\mathbf{l}$, view direction $\mathbf{v}$, material properties).

**Outputs:**
1.  Final color $C$ for the pixel.

**Data Structures:**
*   **Clipping Geometry:** Defined by implicit functions (e.g., plane equations $ax+by+cz+d=0$) or explicit meshes. For plane-based clipping (common in cropping contexts), the normal $\mathbf{n}_{clip}$ is constant for each plane.
*   **Volume Gradient:** Precomputed or computed on-the-fly using finite differences (e.g., central differences) to approximate $\nabla V$.

**Steps:**
1.  **Ray Casting:** Cast a ray through the volume.
2.  **Integration Loop:** For each sample point $\mathbf{p}$ along the ray:
    a.  Sample volume value $v = V(\mathbf{p})$.
    b.  Compute volume gradient $\mathbf{g} = \nabla V(\mathbf{p})$ [G: typically using central differences for smoothness].
    c.  **Clipping Check:** Determine if $\mathbf{p}$ lies on the boundary of the clipping geometry.
        *   If $\mathbf{p}$ is inside the clipping volume (transparent region), skip shading or return 0 opacity.
        *   If $\mathbf{p}$ is outside, proceed.
    d.  **Normal Selection:**
        *   Check if the current sample point $\mathbf{p}$ is within a small distance $\epsilon$ of the clipping geometry boundary.
        *   If yes, set shading normal $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{n}_{clip}$ (normalized surface normal of the clipping geometry at $\mathbf{p}$).
        *   If no, set shading normal $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{g}$ (normalized volume gradient).
    e.  **Illumination:** Compute lighting model (e.g., Phong or Blinn-Phong) using $\mathbf{n}$, $\mathbf{l}$, and $\mathbf{v}$.
    f.  **Compositing:** Accumulate color and opacity using front-to-back or back-to-front compositing.

**Formula:**
The shading normal $\mathbf{n}_{shade}$ at point $\mathbf{p}$ is:
$$
\mathbf{n}_{shade} = \begin{cases} 
\frac{\mathbf{n}_{clip}}{\|\mathbf{n}_{clip}\|} & \text{if } \text{dist}(\mathbf{p}, \text{ClipBoundary}) < \delta \\
\frac{\nabla V(\mathbf{p})}{\|\nabla V(\mathbf{p})\|} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
Where $\delta$ is a small threshold distance defining the "boundary zone" [S1, unofficial].

**Parameters:**
*   $\delta$: Boundary thickness. Not given in the sources. [G: Typical values range from 1 to 5 voxels depending on resolution].

**Edge Cases:**
*   **Gradient Magnitude Zero:** If $\|\nabla V\| \approx 0$, the volume gradient is undefined. Use a default normal or fallback to clipping normal if near boundary [G].
*   **Degenerate Clipping Geometry:** If clipping geometry has no well-defined normal (e.g., sharp edges), interpolation or averaging of adjacent face normals may be required [G].

## Subtopics

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(N)$ per ray, where $N$ is the number of samples. The clipping check adds a constant overhead per sample.
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(1)$ additional memory per ray for clipping geometry checks, assuming implicit clipping planes. If explicit meshes are used, spatial acceleration structures (BVH) may be needed, increasing memory.
*   **Strengths:** Provides clear visual distinction between volume interior and clipped exterior; enhances depth perception at cut surfaces.
*   **Limitations:** Requires definition of clipping geometry normals; potential for visual artifacts if the boundary zone $\delta$ is poorly chosen (e.g., halo effects).
*   **When to use:** When realistic surface shading at cut planes is desired to improve interpretability of internal structures.
*   **When not to use:** When performance is critical and clipping boundaries are not visually significant, or when clipping geometry is complex and expensive to intersect.

## Background
Clipping geometry illumination was described by Weiskopf et al. in TVCG 2003 [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the visual discontinuity that arises when parts of a volume are made transparent via clipping geometry, ensuring that the boundary of the clipping geometry contributes to the shading model rather than relying solely on the volume's internal gradients.
