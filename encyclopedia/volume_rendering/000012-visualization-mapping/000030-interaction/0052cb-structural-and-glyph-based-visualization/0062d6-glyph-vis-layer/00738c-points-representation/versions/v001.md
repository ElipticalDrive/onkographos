# Points representation
Also called: Point-based volume rendering
## Overview
Points representation renders a dataset as a collection of points by first extracting its surface mesh [S1, S2]. It serves as a lightweight alternative to solid surface rendering, allowing users to avoid translucent geometries during interactive exploration while reserving them for final presentations [S1, S2]. It is distinguished from [Surface](18767-surface.md) by rendering only discrete vertices rather than filled polygons, and from [Wireframe](18768-wireframe.md) by omitting edge connectivity information.
## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
- Input: A structured or unstructured dataset containing geometric cells and associated point coordinates.
- Output: A rendered image composed of point sprites or markers at the extracted surface vertices.

**Data Structures:**
The method relies on an extracted surface mesh. The specific internal layout of the mesh (e.g., VTK cell arrays, point coordinates) is not detailed in the sources, but the logical structure consists of a set of unique vertices defining the boundary of the data [S1, S2].

**Steps:**
1. Extract the surface mesh from the input dataset. This involves identifying the boundary cells or surfaces that define the extent of the data [S1, S2].
2. Render the extracted mesh as a collection of points. Each vertex of the surface mesh is drawn as an individual point marker [S1, S2].

**Coloring:**
Users can apply coloring to the points using one of two strategies [S1, S2]:
1. **Solid Color:** Assign a single uniform color to all points, similar to the [Outline](18769-outline.md) representation.
2. **Scalar Coloring (Pseudocoloring):** Map a selected data array from the dataset to a color lookup table, applying the resulting color to each point based on its associated scalar value [S1, S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
- The method is strictly dependent on the successful extraction of a surface mesh. If the dataset lacks a well-defined surface or if extraction fails, the representation cannot be generated.
- As a point-based visualization, it does not convey surface continuity or curvature as effectively as solid surface rendering.

## Operating characteristics
Time and memory complexity: Not given in the sources.
Measured performance: Not given in the sources.
Scaling: Not given in the sources.
Strengths and limitations: The primary strength is performance during exploration, as it avoids the computational cost of rendering translucent geometries [S1, S2]. It is less suitable for detailed surface analysis compared to solid surface or wireframe representations.
When to use it and when not: Use it for rapid exploration of large datasets where surface topology is sufficient for understanding. Do not use it when surface continuity, shading, or detailed geometric features are required for the final output.

## Background
**Subtopics:**
- [Surface](18767-surface.md): Renders the extracted mesh as solid polygons. Choose when surface continuity and shading are needed.
- [Wireframe](18768-wireframe.md): Renders the extracted mesh as lines connecting vertices. Choose when cell boundaries and connectivity are important.
- [Feature Edges](18770-feature-edges.md): A subset of Wireframe highlighting prominent edges (sharp angles or boundaries). Choose when specific geometric features need emphasis.

**Background:**
The Points representation is a standard visualization mode in ParaView, introduced in version 5.8.1 and maintained in version 6.2.0 [S1, S2]. It is part of a family of surface-based representations that rely on mesh extraction [S1, S2].
