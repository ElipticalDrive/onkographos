# Frequency Distribution-based Transfer Function
Also called: TF considering frequency distribution
## Overview
This method maps voxel values to colors and transparencies based on the frequency distribution of the scalar field to express subtle spatial differences in narrow-range data [S1, unofficial]. It addresses the difficulty of visualizing subtle differences in datasets with narrow distribution ranges and small spatial differences [S1, unofficial].
## How it works
**Inputs:** A scalar volume dataset [S1, unofficial].
**Outputs:** A transfer function mapping scalar values to color and transparency [S1, unofficial].
**Data Structures:**
1. **Histogram:** An array storing the frequency count for each scalar value range [S1, unofficial].
2. **Coloring Probability Map:** An array storing the probability weight for each frequency range [S1, unofficial].
**Steps:**
1. **Count Frequency Distribution:** Count the frequency distribution of the scalar field [S1, unofficial].
2. **Calculate Voxel Ratios and Coloring Probabilities:** Calculate the voxel ratios and the coloring probabilities of the frequency ranges [S1, unofficial]. Not given in the sources.
3. **Map Values:** Map the voxel values to colors and transparencies according to the coloring probabilities [S1, unofficial].
**Formulas:** Not given in the sources.
**Parameters:** Not given in the sources.
**Pitfalls:** Narrow distribution ranges and small spatial differences of the scalar field make perspective expression of subtle differences difficult [S1, unofficial].
## Subtopics
[Multi-dimensional Transfer Function](1217-multi-dimensional-transfer-function.md): Multi-dimensional transfer functions (MTFs) are a data-centric classification approach that maps voxel attributes to color and opacity using two-dimensional histograms of feature space pairs [S1, unofficial]. Unlike single-dimensional transfer functions that rely solely on scalar intensity, MTFs leverage additional features (such as gradient magnitude or derivatives) to better convey boundaries and subtle spatial differences in volume data [S1, unofficial]. This method sets itself apart by explicitly visualizing the joint distribution of features, allowing users to identify and isolate specific material properties that may be indistinguishable in 1D histograms [S1, unofficial].
## Operating characteristics
- **Strengths:** Improves the perspective expression of subtle differences in spatial distribution, particularly for shallow lakes or datasets with narrow scalar ranges [S1, unofficial].
- **Limitations:** Narrow distribution ranges and small spatial differences of the scalar field make perspective expression of subtle differences difficult [S1, unofficial].
- **Complexity:** Not given in the sources.
- **When to use:** Datasets with narrow scalar ranges where standard threshold-based transfer functions fail to reveal subtle spatial variations [S1, unofficial].
- **When not to use:** Not given in the sources.
## Background
Proposed by Wangjun He, Alan Yuan, Gu Xianyong, and Zhenliang Liao in November 2021 to explore the distribution and diffusion of pollutants in lakes [S1, unofficial]. The method addresses the difficulty of visualizing subtle differences in lake water quality models using standard volume rendering [S1, unofficial].
