# Diffusion Models (DDPMs)
Also called: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

## Overview
Diffusion Models (DDPMs) generate data by learning to reverse a gradual noising process, iteratively denoising from pure noise to a data distribution sample [S1, unofficial][S2]. In the context of volume rendering, they are applied to volume generation, Transfer Function (TF) design, and visualization synthesis [S1, unofficial]. They set themselves apart from GAN-based siblings (e.g., [Opacity GAN](7664-opacity-gan.md)) by their iterative refinement nature, which naturally handles the multimodal distributions common in scientific data [S1, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs and Outputs:**
*   **Input:** A noise sample $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ and a conditioning signal $\mathbf{c}$ (e.g., class label, text prompt, or coarse volume) [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Output:** A generated sample $\mathbf{x}_0$ representing the target data (e.g., a volume grid, transfer function parameters, or rendered image) [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Data Structures:**
The model parameterizes the reverse process using a neural network $\epsilon_\theta$. The network typically takes the noisy sample $\mathbf{x}_t$, the time step $t$, and the condition $\mathbf{c}$ as inputs [S1, unofficial][S2].

**Algorithm:**
The method consists of a forward process (data noising) and a reverse process (denoising).

1.  **Forward Process (Fixed):**
    The data $\mathbf{x}_0$ is gradually corrupted by adding Gaussian noise over $T$ steps. The transition kernel $q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_{t-1})$ is defined as:
    $$ q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I}) $$
    where $\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ is the variance schedule [S2]. This allows sampling $\mathbf{x}_t$ directly from $\mathbf{x}_0$:
    $$ \mathbf{x}_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{x}_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}) $$
    where $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{i=1}^t \alpha_i$ [S2].

2.  **Training (Reverse Process Parameterization):**
    The model learns to predict the noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ added at step $t$. The loss function is the mean squared error between the predicted noise and the actual noise:
    $$ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \| \boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) \|^2 \right] $$
    where $\epsilon_\theta$ is the neural network [S1, unofficial][S2].

3.  **Sampling (Reverse Process):**
    Starting from $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, the model iteratively denoises from $t=T$ down to $t=0$. The reverse transition $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_t)$ is approximated as:
    $$ p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} | \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)) $$
    The mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta$ is computed from the predicted noise $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$ [S2].

**Parameters:**
*   $T$: Total number of diffusion steps. Typical values range from 100 to 1000 [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   $\beta_t$: Variance schedule. Common choices include linear or cosine schedules [S2].

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Computational Cost:** The iterative nature requires $T$ network evaluations per sample, making it slower than single-pass methods like GANs [S1, unofficial].
*   **Mode Collapse:** While less prone than GANs, poor variance schedules or insufficient training can lead to incomplete coverage of the data distribution [S1, unofficial].

## Subtopics

### [Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) for 3D](12787-latent-diffusion-models-ldm-for-3d.md)
Performs diffusion in a compressed latent space rather than directly in the high-dimensional 3D representation space, significantly improving computational efficiency and output quality. It sets itself apart from standard DDPMs by decoupling the high-dimensional data representation from the diffusion process, allowing the use of powerful 2D diffusion priors adapted for 3D latent structures.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** $O(T \cdot N)$, where $T$ is the number of diffusion steps and $N$ is the cost of one neural network evaluation [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Memory Complexity:** $O(N)$ for storing the model parameters and intermediate states [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **Performance:** DDPMs produce high-quality samples with stable training compared to GANs [S2]. However, they are computationally expensive during inference due to the iterative denoising process [S1, unofficial].
*   **Strengths:** Handles multimodal distributions well; stable training; high fidelity [S1, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow inference speed; requires careful tuning of the variance schedule [S1, unofficial][S2].
*   **When to use:** When high-fidelity generation is required and computational budget allows for iterative refinement [S1, unofficial].
*   **When not to use:** When real-time inference is required [S1, unofficial].

## Background
Diffusion models entered volume visualization after Wang and Han’s survey [147]. They have garnered significant attention in 3D editing and generation tasks [S2]. Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) further leveraged pre-trained models like Stable Diffusion to improve efficiency [S2].
