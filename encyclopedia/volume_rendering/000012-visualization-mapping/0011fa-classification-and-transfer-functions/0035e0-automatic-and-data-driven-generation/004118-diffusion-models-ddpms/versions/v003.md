# Diffusion Models (DDPMs)
Also called: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

## Overview
Diffusion Models (DDPMs) are generative models that transform a sample from a noise distribution into a data distribution by learning to reverse a gradual noising process [S1][S2, unofficial][S3]. In the context of volume rendering, they are applied to volume generation, transfer function (TF) design, and visualization synthesis [S2, unofficial]. They are distinguished from sibling methods like GANs and Gaussian Mixture Models by their iterative refinement process, which naturally handles the multimodal distributions common in scientific data [S2, unofficial].

## How it works
**Inputs:**
*   $z_0$: The target data sample (e.g., a latent representation of a volume or transfer function).
*   $y$: Conditioning information (e.g., text prompts, low-resolution images, or other guidance signals) [S1].
*   $T$: The total number of diffusion timesteps.
*   $\{\alpha_t\}_{t=1}^T$: A predetermined noising schedule defining the variance added at each step.

**Outputs:**
*   A generated sample $z_0$ drawn from the learned data distribution [S1][S2, unofficial].

**Data Structures:**
*   **Noising Schedule:** A sequence of coefficients $\bar{\alpha}_t$ (cumulative product of $\alpha_t$) and $\sigma_t$ (noise scale at step $t$). These are typically precomputed constants.
*   **Denoising Network ($\epsilon_\phi$):** A neural network (often a U-Net) parameterized by $\phi$ that predicts the noise component $\epsilon$ given a noisy input $z_t$, condition $y$, and timestep $t$ [S1].

**Algorithm Steps:**

1.  **Forward Process (Training Data Preparation):**
    For a clean data sample $z_0$ and a randomly sampled timestep $t \sim U(0, T)$, compute the noisy latent $z_t$ using the closed-form reparameterization [S1]:
    $$
    z_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} z_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon
    $$
    where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is Gaussian noise [S1].

2.  **Training Objective:**
    Train the denoising network $\epsilon_\phi(z_t, y, t)$ to predict the noise $\epsilon$ used in the forward process. The loss function is the weighted mean squared error [S1]:
    $$
    \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim U(0,T), \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)} \left[ \gamma(t) \| \epsilon_\phi(z_t, y, t) - \epsilon \|_2^2 \right]
    $$
    where $\gamma(t)$ is a weighting function (often constant or dependent on the schedule) [S1].

3.  **Sampling (Reverse Process):**
    To generate data, start with pure Gaussian noise $z_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and iteratively denoise from $t=T$ down to $t=0$ [S1][S2, unofficial]. At each step $t$, compute the predicted noise $\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\phi(z_t, y, t)$ and update to $z_{t-1}$ using the ancestral sampling formula [S1]:
    $$
    z_{t-1} = \sqrt{\alpha_t} \left( \frac{z_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \hat{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \right) + \sigma_t \epsilon'
    $$
    where $\epsilon' \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ is new Gaussian noise added at each step to maintain stochasticity [S1]. Note: The term inside the parenthesis simplifies to the estimate of the clean data $z_0$ derived from $z_t$ and $\hat{\epsilon}$.

**Parameters:**
*   $T$: Total timesteps. Not given in the sources.
*   $\gamma(t)$: Loss weighting. Not given in the sources.
*   $\alpha_t, \bar{\alpha}_t, \sigma_t$: Schedule parameters. Not given in the sources.

**Edge Cases and Pitfalls:**
*   **Conditioning Mismatch:** The network requires $y$ (conditioning). If $y$ is missing or malformed, the prediction $\epsilon_\phi$ may be arbitrary [S1].
*   **Stochasticity:** The addition of $\epsilon'$ in the sampling step means results are not deterministic for the same seed unless the noise sequence is fixed [S1].

## Subtopics

### [Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) for 3D](12787-latent-diffusion-models-ldm-for-3d.md)
Performs diffusion in a compressed latent space using a pretrained VAE, improving computational efficiency and allowing the use of 2D diffusion priors for 3D structures.

## Operating characteristics
*   **Time Complexity:** Sampling is iterative, requiring $T$ forward passes through the denoising network. Not given in the sources.
*   **Memory Complexity:** Depends on the resolution of $z_t$ and the size of the denoising network. Latent Diffusion reduces this by operating in a lower-dimensional space [S1].
*   **Strengths:** Handles multimodal distributions effectively; produces high-quality samples; flexible conditioning [S2, unofficial].
*   **Limitations:** Slow sampling due to iterative nature; requires significant training data and compute [S2, unofficial].
*   **When to use:** When generating complex, multimodal volume data or transfer functions where GANs might suffer from mode collapse [S2, unofficial].

## Background
Diffusion models were introduced as generative models that transform noise to data via a fixed forward process and a learned reverse process [S1, S3]. They entered volume visualization following surveys by Wang and Han, who noted their iterative refinement capabilities [S2, unofficial]. Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) further improved efficiency by projecting data into a latent space using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) before diffusion [S1, S3].
